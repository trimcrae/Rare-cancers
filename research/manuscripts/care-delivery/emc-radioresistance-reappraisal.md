# The EMC radioresistance contradiction, tested — and why BED is not what resolves it

> ⛔ **NOTHING HERE ASSERTS EFFICACY, SAFETY, A THERAPEUTIC WINDOW OR CLINICAL READINESS.** Every
> estimate below is an observational association from cohorts in which radiotherapy was allocated
> **by indication**. No randomised trial of radiotherapy in EMC exists, and none of this is
> treatment advice.

**One home for every number: [`emc-rt-bed-reappraisal.json`](../../modalities/emc-rt-bed-reappraisal.json),
generated by [`emc_rt_bed_reappraisal.py`](../../modalities/emc_rt_bed_reappraisal.py).** Regenerate
with `python3 research/modalities/emc_rt_bed_reappraisal.py`; `--check` fails on drift. The prose
here quotes that artifact and computes nothing.

## 0 · What was asked, and what came back

[`emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md` §3.7](../program/emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md#37--the-radioresistance-reappraisal)
registered a contradiction — a national registry finding no radiotherapy effect on local
recurrence against a single-centre series finding 100 % versus 63 % ten-year local control — and
proposed that a **BED meta-regression with α/β free** would resolve it.

**It does not, and it cannot.** Four instruments were run, and three of them return negatives:

| instrument | question | answer |
|---|---|---|
| **A · consistency** | Do the series actually disagree? | **No.** Cochran Q = 2.015, df = 2, **p = 0.37**, I² ≈ 0.007. All three point the same way. |
| **B · BED** | Did the null series deliver a lower dose? | **No, at every α/β tested.** The null series' BED range *contains* the positive series' range and its maximum is the higher of the two. |
| **C · identifiability** | Can α/β be fitted at all? | **No.** Fraction size is perfectly separated from treatment setting; **zero** EMC exposures vary fractionation within a setting. |
| **D · bias** | Which way does confounding run? | **Against radiotherapy, in both series.** Removing the one imbalance the registry measured moves its own estimate from HR 0.50 to **HR 0.33** — *toward* the positive series. |

**Verdict: the question RESOLVES, and not by dose.** What was described as a contradiction between
two findings is a contradiction between two *p-values* attached to two estimates that do not
significantly differ. What survives as genuinely open is the **magnitude** of the association, and
that is a precision problem, not a conflict.

## 1 · Instrument A — test the contradiction before explaining it

Three EMC series report an association between radiotherapy and **local recurrence**. Put on one
log scale (`consistency.studies`):

| series | design, dx years | contrast | effect (RT vs no RT) | 95 % CI | weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Masunaga 2025** (PMID 40885991, PMC12398172) `[FT]` | Japanese national registry, n = 134 localized surgical, 2002–2022 | univariable Cox HR | **0.50** | 0.11 – 2.25 | 52 % |
| **Bishop 2019** (PMID 31436747) `[API]` | MD Anderson, n = 41 consecutive localized, 1990–2016 | multivariable Cox HR, *inverted* from the published surgery-alone HR 12.7 (1.4–115.3) | **0.079** | 0.0087 – 0.71 | 25 % |
| **Paioli 2021** (PMID 32572850) `[API/secondary]` | Italian Sarcoma Group, 3 centres, molecularly-confirmed localized, subgroup n = 27 | crude odds ratio from 1/10 vs 7/17 | **0.159** | 0.016 – 1.55 | 23 % |

**Cochran's Q = 2.015 on 2 df, p = 0.365, I² = 0.007.** There is no evidence in the published
record that these three series estimate different things.

The pairwise comparison the memo's framing actually rests on — registry against MD Anderson —
gives **z = 1.36, p = 0.175**. Two estimates that both favour radiotherapy, one of which crossed
0.05 and one of which did not. *The difference between significant and non-significant is not
itself significant*, and that is the whole of the "contradiction".

⚠ **What the pooled value is and is not.** The artifact emits a fixed-effect pooled estimate of
**0.244 (95 % CI 0.082 – 0.728)** with `is_headline: false`, and it must stay that way. Inverse-
variance combination on the log scale is a **third** combination method, distinct from both of the
two named in [`POLICY-evidence.md` §2](../../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md) — neither the crude-
proportion/Wilson method nor the DerSimonian–Laird model in `research/meta/meta-analysis.mjs`
combines hazard ratios. It is used here to **test agreement**, not to produce an effect estimate:
the inputs mix an unadjusted Cox HR, a multivariable Cox HR and a crude odds ratio, and every one
of them is confounded by indication.

### 1a · The POLICY-compliant pooled proportion, separately

Where integer counts exist in **non-overlapping** populations — a Japanese national registry and an
Italian three-centre series — POLICY-evidence §2.2 applies directly
(`consistency.pooled_proportions_policy_evidence_2_2`):

- **with radiotherapy: 3/34 = 8.8 % (95 % CI 3.0 – 23.0 %)**
- **without radiotherapy: 21/127 = 16.5 % (95 % CI 11.1 – 24.0 %)**

Crude, mixed follow-up, unadjusted, **not** a causal effect. Per §2.2 the honest heterogeneity
signal is the range rather than an I²: the RT-arm rate spans 8.3 – 10.0 % across the two cohorts
and the no-RT arm spans 12.7 – 41.2 %. That wide no-RT spread is real and is compatible with the
Italian surgery-alone group being enriched for higher-risk cases.

**Two series are excluded, and the reasons are different:**

- **Bishop 2019** reports 10-year Kaplan–Meier local control only. POLICY-evidence §2.4 forbids
  merging time-anchored survival into a pooled proportion. It contributes its hazard ratio to
  instrument A and nothing to the proportion.
- **Drilon 2008** (PMID 18951519) is excluded on a **measured discordance**, not on availability.
  Two published reviews read the same primary in opposite directions: Masunaga 2025 reports it as
  *"no difference … (41 vs. 35 %, p = 0.79)"* with the radiotherapy arm at 41 %, while the 2020
  state-of-the-art review (PMID 32967265) reports the same series as showing *"a trend of a better
  local recurrence rate in patients treated with surgery combined with radiotherapy"*. The primary
  is not in the open-access subset and the Europe PMC `fullTextXML` endpoint returns 404, so the
  direction of that series' radiotherapy effect is **unresolved here** and is not used.
  ⭐ *Two reviews disagreeing about the direction of a third paper's result is itself a finding, and
  belongs in any write-up of this lane.*

### 1b · Precision, which is the real problem

`precision.registry`: the registry's local-recurrence analysis rests on **16 events** with 24 of
134 patients irradiated. Under Schoenfeld's log-rank relation that gives:

- **power 18.5 %** to reach p < 0.05 at the registry's *own* point estimate (HR 0.50);
- **power 97.4 %** at MD Anderson's point estimate (HR 0.079).

So *"no association was found"* is a statement about power at moderate effect sizes. The registry
**is** mildly informative against an effect as large as MD Anderson's — its CI lower bound of 0.11
sits just above 0.079 — and is entirely uninformative against a real, moderate benefit.

## 2 · Instrument B — BED, with the assumption in the open

BED = D·(1 + d/(α/β)). **The α/β assumption is not hidden in a constant**: every exposure is
computed across the sweep **1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10** (`alpha_beta_sweep`), and both BED and EQD2 are
emitted at each (`bed_exposures[*].bed_gy`, `.eqd2_gy`). ⚠ *§3.7 of the memo carried "α/β = 10
assumed", and a single assumed value is exactly what makes a BED argument unfalsifiable.*

**The doses the two contradicting series actually delivered** — this is the load-bearing retrieval,
and it is what the memo did not have:

- **Masunaga 2025**, verbatim `[FT]`: *"neoadjuvant radiotherapy was administered at a total dose of
  **40–50 Gy in 2 Gy fractions** in four patients, and adjuvant radiotherapy at a total dose of
  **50–66 Gy in 2 Gy fractions** in 20 patients."*
- **Bishop 2019**, read in the Remiszewski 2025 review `[secondary]`: *"56 % (n = 23) received
  preoperative radiotherapy with a **median dose of 50 Gy (range, 50–50.4 Gy)**, 24 % (n = 10)
  received postoperative radiotherapy with a **median dose of 60 Gy (range, 60–65 Gy)**, and 20 %
  (n = 8) underwent surgery alone."* The review does not state a fraction size, so **both 1.8 and
  2.0 Gy are carried** rather than one being chosen; the conclusion is identical either way.

**Result (`series_bed_comparison`):**

| α/β | registry BED range (Gy) | MD Anderson BED range (Gy) | registry range contains MDACC's? | registry max − MDACC max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 93.3 – 154.0 | 110.0 – 151.7 | yes | +2.3 |
| 2 | 80.0 – 132.0 | 95.0 – 130.0 | yes | +2.0 |
| 3 | 66.7 – 110.0 | 80.0 – 108.3 | yes | +1.7 |
| 4 | 60.0 – 99.0 | 72.5 – 97.5 | yes | +1.5 |
| 5 | 56.0 – 92.4 | 68.0 – 91.0 | yes | +1.4 |
| 10 | 48.0 – 79.2 | 59.0 – 78.0 | yes | +1.2 |

**The series that found nothing delivered the wider distribution, and the higher maximum, at every
α/β.** A dose explanation of the contradiction would require the null series to have *out-dosed*
the positive one and still found nothing, which is not a dose-response account of anything.
**BED is refuted as the explanation, on data rather than on assumption.**

⚠ Both series publish arm-level dose **ranges**, not per-patient doses. This is therefore a
comparison of ranges and not the regression the memo asked for — and no EMC series publishes the
per-patient dose-and-outcome table that regression would need.

### 2a · The lesion-level exposures

`bed_exposures` also carries every EMC exposure with a total dose, a fraction size **and** a
per-lesion outcome. There are five, all n = 1:

| exposure | dose | setting | local outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Improta 2020, PMC7731621 `[FT]` | 50 Gy / 25 fx | preoperative, recurrent primary | **complete pathological response** |
| SABR case 2025, PMC12659415 `[FT]` | 60 Gy / 25 fx (2.4 Gy) | neoadjuvant, primary | **local relapse** (after surgery too — not attributable to RT alone) |
| same patient | 40 Gy / 5 fx | metastasis-directed SABR | **complete response, no relapse** |
| same patient | 35 Gy / 5 fx | metastasis-directed SABR (bowel constraint) | complete/major response, no relapse |
| HDR-ISBT 2022, PMC9044308 `[FT]` | 30 Gy / 2 fx | metastasis-directed, ×3 sites | **long-term local control at all three** |

A 50 Gy conventional course producing a **complete pathological response** in EMC is on its own
awkward for the "EMC is radioresistant" premise the whole lane inherits.

⛔ **The one α/β an EMC paper has ever stated is an assumption, not a measurement.** The
brachytherapy report computes its own BED as *"142 Gy, α/β = 4"* and contrasts it with 24 Gy for a
single 8 Gy fraction. It chose 4; it did not estimate it.

## 3 · Instrument C — α/β is not estimable, and that is a closure

`alpha_beta_identifiability`. BED admits α/β **only** through the dose per fraction d. Identifying
it requires exposures that differ in d while being comparable in everything else. In the EMC
record:

- curative-intent primary-site exposures: **d ∈ {1.8, 2.0, 2.4} Gy**
- metastasis-directed exposures: **d ∈ {7.0, 8.0, 15.0} Gy**
- **perfectly separated. Overlap: none. Exposures with a within-setting fractionation contrast: 0.**

So fraction size is aliased with treatment setting, target volume and tumour burden. **Any α/β
fitted to these data would be estimating "ablative treatment of small oligometastatic nodules
controls them better than adjuvant treatment of a bulky operative bed"** — true of every histology,
and silent about EMC.

⭐ **The one suggestive contrast, reported as a hypothesis and not as evidence.** Within a *single*
patient (PMC12659415): neoadjuvant 60 Gy / 25 fx to the primary was followed by local relapse,
while 40 Gy / 5 fx and 35 Gy / 5 fx to metastatic nodules gave durable control. At **α/β = 10**
those three have almost identical BED (74.4 / 72.0 / 84.0 Gy); at **α/β = 3** they separate
sharply (108.0 / 146.7 / 116.7 Gy). A **low α/β** — a late-responding, fractionation-sensitive
tumour, which is what the chondrosarcoma literature generally assumes — is the only value at which
BED orders these outcomes correctly. **n = 1, and confounded by everything that differs between a
bulky operated primary and a sub-centimetre nodule.** It is a reason to look, not a result.

**Consequence for the memo:** *"fit local control against BED with α/β free"* **cannot be
executed** on the existing record. That is a **definitional closure of the method**, not a gap more
literature retrieval would fill. The memo's line *"Nobody has an α/β estimate for EMC"* is correct,
and this analysis adds why: nobody *can* have one from published data.

## 4 · Instrument D — the confounding runs one way, and it is the wrong way for the memo's story

The registry measured its own indication bias (`indication_bias`), verbatim `[FT]`: *"Of the 24
patients who received (neo)adjuvant radiotherapy, **10 (41.7 %) had R1 or R2 surgical margins**,
whereas only **20 (18.2 %) of the 110** patients who did not receive (neo)adjuvant radiotherapy had
R1 or R2 surgical margins."* The same paper puts the margin hazard ratio for local recurrence at
**4.76**.

Under a **null** radiotherapy effect, that imbalance alone produces an expected hazard ratio of
**1.524** against radiotherapy. Dividing the observed HR through it:

**HR 0.50 → margin-corrected HR ≈ 0.328.**

**The measured confounding hides benefit; it does not manufacture it.** And the same logic applies
in the opposite series: MD Anderson *omitted* radiotherapy in 8 patients, and omission is itself an
indication (small, superficial, cleanly excised tumours), which biases that series toward finding
**no** benefit as well. **Both series are biased in the same direction**, which is precisely why
they cannot be read as an optimistic design against a pessimistic one.

E-values (`indication_bias.e_values`), risk-ratio scale, local-recurrence risk 16/134 = 11.9 % so
the HR≈RR approximation is stated rather than buried:

| estimate | E-value |
|---|---|
| registry, observed HR 0.50 | **3.41** |
| registry, margin-corrected HR 0.33 | **5.55** |
| MD Anderson, HR 0.079 | **24.9** |
| MD Anderson, CI bound HR 0.714 | **2.15** |

An unmeasured confounder would need an association of roughly that strength with **both**
radiotherapy receipt and local recurrence, over and above the measured margin imbalance, to explain
each estimate away.

**Assumptions, stated:** proportional and multiplicative hazards; the margin hazard ratio is
transportable across arms; exactly **one** measured imbalance is removed and no other.

## 5 · What this is, honestly

**Resolved:** the contradiction, as a contradiction. It does not survive a heterogeneity test, BED
does not explain it, and the direction of confounding is the same in both series.

**Not resolved, and not resolvable from published data:** the **magnitude**. Every available
estimate is an association under allocation-by-indication whose measured bias runs against
radiotherapy, so each is a lower bound of unknown tightness. Nothing here establishes causation and
nothing here is a recommendation. The pragmatic stance already recorded in
[`emc-clinical-registry.json`](../../data/emc-clinical-registry.json) → `evidenceQuestions` →
`rt-localized-emc` — selective, not routine, radiotherapy — is untouched by this analysis; what
changes is the *reason* given for the disagreement in that entry.

**What would change it:** a per-patient dose-and-outcome table from any EMC series (none of the four
cohorts publishes one), or a single cohort in which fraction size varied within one treatment
setting, which would make α/β identifiable for the first time.

⛔ **This REFUTES the framing in [`emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md` §3.7](../program/emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md#37--the-radioresistance-reappraisal); it does not confirm it.**
Three specific sentences in that memo are overtaken: the shortlist row calling the lane *"resolvable
with a BED regression"*, the claim that *"the registries cannot see dose"* (the largest one states
its doses in full), and the α/β = 10 assumption, which is retired in favour of an explicit sweep.
Those re-grades are **routed, not applied** — this pass computes and does not edit another
document's canonical section. Ready-to-apply, anchors verified verbatim:
[`emc-rt-adaptive-lanes-map-edits.json`](./emc-rt-adaptive-lanes-map-edits.json) → E1, E3, E4.

**The paper this is for.** A short methodological correspondence: *"The apparent conflict over
radiotherapy in extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is a power artefact, not a dose effect."* One
sentence for the field's record: **three EMC series that are read as contradicting each other agree
(Q p = 0.37); the series reported as showing no benefit delivered the higher biologically effective
dose; and α/β for EMC cannot be estimated from any published data because fractionation is
perfectly confounded with treatment setting.** What is missing: any randomised evidence, any
per-patient dose table, and any within-setting fractionation contrast.
