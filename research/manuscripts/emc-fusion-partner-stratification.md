# Fusion-variant stratification in EMC (EWSR1::NR4A3 vs TAF15::NR4A3) — a partner-stratified pooled synthesis

**Preprint draft · no wet lab · no compute · $0.** **Lane:**
[`emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md` §3.2](./emc-unexplored-treatment-lanes.md#32--fusion-variant-stratification)
(ranked #2 of twelve).

**Every number below has one home:** [`emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json`](./emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json),
generated by [`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](./emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py). This document states the
finding and the argument; it does not re-derive the arithmetic, and **a number typed here that disagrees with

## Abstract

**Background.** Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is defined by an *NR4A3* rearrangement whose 5′
partner is *EWSR1* in most cases and *TAF15* in a minority. Four separate literatures — objective response to
antiangiogenic tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (TKIs), outcome by partner, partner prevalence, and a transcriptional
mechanism — each report a difference between the two variants. None of the four had been combined with any
other, and no source pools the response data at all.

**Methods.** Every published EMC report giving an outcome stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner was identified and
pooled under a single pre-existing, binding evidence contract: crude denominator-weighted proportions with
Wilson score 95 % intervals, explicit integer `{events, denom}` required, non-overlapping populations only, and
the smaller cohort held out wherever overlap could not be excluded. Heterogeneity is reported as the range of
per-cohort rates. A random-effects model was deliberately **not** fitted. Fisher exact p-values are reported as
post-hoc descriptive statistics and license nothing.

**Results.** Exactly **two** published reports give objective response by partner: a sunitinib named-use series
(PMID 24703573) and the pazopanib phase 2 trial NCT02066285 (PMID 31331701). **No TAF15::NR4A3 patient has ever
been reported to respond to an antiangiogenic TKI** — 0 of 3 to 0 of 5 patients, depending on how the overlap
between the two reports resolves. The 95 % Wilson upper bound on the TAF15 response rate is **43.4 % to
56.1 %**, which sits **above** the comparator arm's point estimate in both analyses (37.0 % and 21.1 %).
Post-hoc Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.155 pooled, 1.0 for the trial alone. In the only cohort publishing outcome
event counts by partner (Agaram 2014, PMID 24746215), disease-specific death is 3/7 (42.9 %) with TAF15 versus
1/16 (6.2 %) with EWSR1 — while **distant recurrence runs the opposite way to the review literature's claim**
(6/16 EWSR1 vs 2/7 TAF15). The two larger series to test the partner as a prognostic factor both fall short of
significance once tumour size is in the model. Pooling four independent molecular series gives TAF15::NR4A3 in
**28/154 = 18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned EMC — **below** the ≈27 % the field routinely
quotes from a single referral-centre cohort.

**Conclusions.** The published record supports a **consistent direction and no established magnitude**. The
*NR4A3* 5′ partner is a **candidate — not established — stratification variable**, and the data cannot exclude
a TAF15 response rate equal to the EWSR1 one. The concrete, zero-patient-cost ask is that the partner be
reported alongside response in every EMC systemic-therapy report, and that three existing paywalled tables be
published in partner-stratified form.

## 1 · Introduction

### 1.1 · The disease, the drug class and the variable

EMC is an ultra-rare soft-tissue sarcoma, accounting for under 1 % of soft-tissue sarcomas, and it is
molecularly defined: *"EMCs are translocation sarcomas and harbor in > 90% of the cases an NR4A3
rearrangement"* [12]. The 5′ partner is *EWSR1* in the large majority, *TAF15* in a minority, and *TCF12*,
*TFG* or an unidentified partner in a small residue [7,8,9,10]. The clinical shape of the disease is the
reason a stratification variable would matter: *"Although often associated with prolonged survival, EMC have
high rates of distant recurrences and disease-associated death"*, and *"Anthracycline-based chemotherapy,
which is the first-line regimen used in STS, has low activity in this sarcoma subtype"* [12,4].

The one systemic class with reproducible activity in EMC is the antiangiogenic TKIs. Sunitinib produced
responses in a named-use series [1] after an index two-patient report [2], and pazopanib was taken into a
dedicated single-arm phase 2 trial, NCT02066285, which reported 4 objective responses among 22 evaluable
patients [3]. Both reports remark, in passing and without pooling, that the responders carried EWSR1::NR4A3 and
that the TAF15::NR4A3 patients did not respond.

### 1.2 · Four literatures that had never been combined

The claim "the fusion partner matters" is currently supported in four places that do not talk to each other:

| line of evidence | what it reports | what it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| **TKI response** | responders are EWSR1, non-responders include every TAF15 patient [1,3,4,5,6] | never states a denominator in the primary text; never pooled |
| **Outcome by partner** | TAF15 associated with worse disease-specific survival [7,8,9] | only one cohort publishes event counts; two publish p-values only |
| **Partner prevalence** | TAF15 in roughly one EMC in five [7,8,9,10] | never pooled across series; the field quotes one cohort |
| **Mechanism** | an axon-guidance switch separates the two variants [11] | is not a clinical endpoint, and shares a consortium with the trials |

Each is repeated in review after review, usually flatly and usually without a denominator. The result is a
belief that is stronger in the field's prose than in the field's data, and it has never been possible to say by
how much, because nobody had put the counts in one place.

### 1.3 · What this synthesis adds

Pooling changes the conclusion's **strength**, not its **direction**. It also surfaces four things that no
individual source says out loud: a response contrast whose confidence interval overlaps its own comparator; a
metastasis signal that runs backwards in the only cohort with counts; a prognostic effect that does not survive
adjustment for tumour size in either of the two larger series; and a prevalence figure materially below the one
the field repeats. A fifth item is an attribution correction to the field's most-quoted caveat (§3.8).

## 2 · Methods

### 2.1 · The binding evidence contract

The statistical method here was **not selected for this analysis**. It is the repository's standing evidence
contract, [`systems/POLICY-evidence.md`](../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md), which predates this work and is
enforced in continuous integration by `scripts/validate-registry.mjs`. Adopting a pre-committed method removes
the degree of freedom that matters most in a synthesis this small: the choice of estimator after seeing the
counts.

Sections that bind here:

- **§1.3 — primary versus secondary provenance.** `sourceId` is the document a number was actually **read in**.
  A number read out of a review carries `provenance: "secondary"` and a `primaryRef` naming the original study.
  Identifiers are never invented for a primary source that was not itself fetched.
- **§2.1 — what may be pooled.** Molecularly typed EMC; **explicit integer** `{events, denom}` (never counts
  back-derived from a published percentage); an outcome that is not the inclusion criterion; a non-overlapping
  population.
- **§2.2 — the estimator.** Crude denominator-weighted proportion p̂ = Σevents / Σdenom, with a **Wilson score**
  95 % interval on the summed counts. Heterogeneity is reported as the **range of per-cohort rates**.
- **§2.3 — double-counting.** Mutually exclusive strata within a study; where two populations may overlap, the
  **smaller** cohort is held out with `contextReason: "population-overlap"`.
- **§2.4 — estimand discipline.** Event-rate metrics are pooled as **crude during-follow-up proportions**,
  explicitly labelled as mixed-follow-up and uncensored. Time-anchored survival figures are never merged.

### 2.2 · Estimator and interval, and why Wilson

Every stratum in this analysis is small and several sit **exactly at zero events**. The normal-approximation
interval collapses to a degenerate point at 0/n and would misrepresent the evidence in the single place where
the evidence is most easily overstated. The Wilson score interval is well behaved at small n and at the
boundaries, and §2.2 of the contract names it by construction. Arithmetic:
[`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](./emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py) → `wilson()`.

### 2.3 · Inclusion, exclusion, and the overlap rule

Reports were assessed for whether they give an outcome **stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner** with integer counts
on both sides of the stratification. Cohorts that report a partner-stratified result without counts are carried
as **context** — visible with their own numbers and citation, excluded from any pooled figure — exactly as the
contract requires. The full inclusion table is §3.2.

The consequential exclusion is the sunitinib series. It cannot be shown non-overlapping with the pazopanib
trial, so §2.3 holds the smaller cohort out of the headline. Because that single decision moves the
denominator, **both analyses are reported**: the POLICY-conformant primary (trial alone) and a maximal-
information secondary (both cohorts, valid only if no patient appears in both), with the bounds that follow.

### 2.4 · Significance testing, declared as post-hoc

Fisher exact two-sided p-values are reported for each contrast. They are **post-hoc and descriptive only**:
not prespecified, not performed in any source report, not corrected for the several endpoints on this page,
and **not used to license any claim in this document**. They are printed because a reader will compute them
otherwise, and printing them with their status attached is more honest than withholding them.

### 2.5 · Methods deliberately not used, and why

- **Random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) pooling.** This repository owns a random-effects pooler
  ([`research/meta/meta-analysis.mjs`](../meta/meta-analysis.mjs)) and it is the wrong instrument here. With
  two to nineteen patients and zero to ten events per stratum, a between-study variance is not estimable; a τ²
  from these counts would be an artefact, and quoting a random-effects interval would manufacture precision the
  data cannot support.
- **I².** Not computed, for the same reason. The honest heterogeneity signal at this scale is the **range of
  per-cohort rates**, printed beside every pooled figure.
- **Hazard ratios, survival models, Kaplan–Meier.** No individual patient data exist for any cohort here, and
  the two cohorts that ran time-to-event analyses publish p-values without event counts. No time-to-event
  quantity is estimated anywhere in this work.
- **Back-derived counts.** Where a source gives only a percentage, no `{events, denom}` is inferred. Rounding
  invents data, and §2.1(2) forbids it.

### 2.6 · Retrieval

The development environment's egress proxy refuses CONNECT to Europe PMC, NCBI and ClinicalTrials.gov
(verified this session: curl exit 56, HTTP 000). Every source was therefore read from the repository's own
`literature-cache` branch, populated by GitHub Actions runs of `.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`. Cache
slugs used, the verbatim sentence each count was read from, and the documents that are **not** retrievable are
recorded in the artifact's `retrieval_provenance` block and in each citation's `verification_note`. Full
accounting: §8.

## 3 · Results

### 3.1 · Objective response to an antiangiogenic TKI, by partner

Across every published EMC report that gives an objective-response outcome by *NR4A3* partner — there are
exactly **two** — **no TAF15::NR4A3 patient has ever been reported to respond to an antiangiogenic TKI.**

| analysis | TAF15 arm | comparator arm | TAF15 95 % upper bound | Fisher (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (pazopanib trial alone, POLICY-conformant) | 0/3 | 4/19 = 21.1 % | **56.1 %** | 1.0 |
| **Secondary** (both cohorts, assumes independence) | 0/5 | 10/27 = 37.0 % | **43.4 %** | 0.155 |

**The honest statement is a direction, not a difference.** The direction is consistent across two cohorts, two
drugs and five years of accrual (July 2011 – January 2017). The magnitude is not established: in **both**
analyses the TAF15 arm's 95 % upper bound lies **above the comparator arm's own point estimate**, so the data
cannot exclude a TAF15 response rate equal to the EWSR1 one. Anyone who has written "TAF15 tumours do not
respond to antiangiogenics" has written a claim the evidence does not carry — and that includes reviews that
state it flatly.

Two further features of the primary analysis matter and are easy to lose:

- **The comparator arm is `non-TAF15`, not verified EWSR1.** All four pazopanib responders are reported as
  EWSR1::NR4A3, but no accessible source gives the trial's full partner distribution, so the 19 comparator
  patients may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped *NR4A3*-rearranged cases. Labelling that arm "EWSR1" would be
  a fabrication of the trial's own composition.
- **A sensitivity analysis on the one unevaluable patient does not rescue anything.** If the patient who died
  before the primary analysis carried TAF15, the strata become 0/2 and 4/20; the TAF15 upper bound rises to
  65.8 % and the contrast weakens. No claim here depends on which way that resolves
  (`analyses.A_tki_objective_response.sensitivity_analyses`).

**The overlap that holds back the headline.** The sunitinib series and the pazopanib trial cannot be shown
non-overlapping, and the risk is specific rather than theoretical. Istituto Nazionale Tumori Milan ran the
series and was a trial site under the same senior investigator; the trial's entry criterion —
*NR4A3*-translocated advanced EMC with RECIST progression in the previous six months — is satisfied **by
definition** by the series' two progressors, who are exactly its two TAF15 patients; and neither report states
whether prior antiangiogenic therapy was permitted. So the smaller cohort is held out of the headline, and both
analyses are reported. **Worst case, the world's entire TAF15 antiangiogenic experience is three patients.**

### 3.3 · Outcome by partner — and a direction that reverses

One cohort — Agaram 2014, MSKCC, 26 consecutive cases with follow-up on all of them — publishes outcome event
counts by partner. Twenty-three of its patients carry an assigned EWSR1 or TAF15 partner.

| endpoint | TAF15::NR4A3 | EWSR1::NR4A3 | Fisher (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|---|
| disease-specific death | **3/7 = 42.9 %** (95 % CI 15.8–75.0) | **1/16 = 6.2 %** (95 % CI 1.1–28.3) | 0.0672 |
| local recurrence | 2/7 = 28.6 % (8.2–64.1) | 1/16 = 6.2 % (1.1–28.3) | 0.2095 |
| **distant recurrence** | **2/7 = 28.6 %** (8.2–64.1) | **6/16 = 37.5 %** (18.5–61.4) | 1.0 |

⭐ **The metastasis signal runs backwards in the one cohort that publishes counts.** Distant recurrence is
higher in the **EWSR1** arm — the opposite direction to the claim repeated across the review literature
(PMC12398172, PMC12376927, PMC7563993 and PMC9131214 all state lower metastasis or better metastasis-free
survival with EWSR1). **No published source states this reversal.**

It is not, however, a refutation, and this document does not present it as one. The TAF15 arm's mean follow-up
is about half the EWSR1 arm's (21.7 vs 43.3 months), which is enough to produce the reversal on its own —
these are crude during-follow-up proportions with no censoring. The same truncation makes the **death** excess
**harder** to observe, not easier, which is why the mortality contrast survives it while the recurrence
contrast does not. And the one cohort that treated metastasis as a **time-to-event** endpoint (Paioli 2021,
n = 67) trends the other way and does not reach significance (DMFS p = 0.09). Crude proportions and DMFS are
different estimands and the two readings are not in contradiction — **but neither establishes the metastasis
claim the reviews make.**

### 3.4 · The partner is not independent of tumour size

Two series, both larger than any cohort that could be pooled here, tested the partner as a prognostic factor in
a multivariable model. **Neither found it independent.**

- **Huang 2023** (PMID 36948401, n = 58, Taiwan): TAF15::NR4A3 shortens disease-specific survival on
  univariate analysis and **loses significance on multivariable analysis**. Only size > 10 cm (P = .004) and
  metastasis at presentation (P = .032) remain independent — and TAF15::NR4A3 is itself associated with
  size > 10 cm (78 %, P = .025).
- **Paioli 2021** (PMID 32572850, n = 67, Italian Sarcoma Group): NR4A3-EWS shows only a **trend** for DFS
  (p = 0.08) and DMFS (p = 0.09), in the same analysis where **size reaches p = 0.004**.

Neither publishes per-partner event counts, so neither can be pooled. The pattern they share is the important
part: in both, the variable that carries the prognosis is **tumour size**, and the partner is associated with
size. **The partner may be a marker for a big tumour rather than for a biology.**

### 3.5 · Partner prevalence — how many patients this would touch

Pooling four independent molecular series — MSKCC (USA), Taiwanese institutions, Czech Republic and the Italian
Sarcoma Group, with no shared authors and no shared referral network between them:

| partner | pooled count | share of partner-assigned EMC | 95 % CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWSR1::NR4A3 | 120/154 | **77.9 %** | 70.7–83.7 |
| **TAF15::NR4A3** | **28/154** | **18.2 %** | **12.9–25.0** |
| TCF12::NR4A3 | 5/154 | 3.2 % | 1.4–7.4 |
| TFG::NR4A3 | 1/154 | 0.6 % | 0.1–3.6 |

Per-cohort TAF15 share ranges 15.8 – 29.2 % (spread 13.4 points). That is the population any
partner-stratified decision would move: large enough for the question to matter, small enough to explain why
every series' TAF15 arm is 2–10 patients.

⚠ **The pooled figure is below the ≈27 % the field usually quotes.** That figure comes from the single
referral-centre cohort (Agaram 2014) that is the high outlier of the four (29.2 % of assigned cases, against
15.8–18.2 % elsewhere) — and a tertiary sarcoma referral centre is exactly the setting where morphologically
unusual, high-grade, hard-to-classify tumours are over-represented, which is what TAF15::NR4A3 tends to be. The
two excluded congress abstracts bracket the pooled estimate rather than contradicting it (7 of 26
partner-assigned in Valencia; 1 of 9 in Pilsen/Znojmo).

### 3.6 · Mechanism, and what a third expression cohort does and does not add

Brenca 2019 [11] profiled 7 EWSR1-NR4A3 and 5 TAF15-NR4A3 EMC and reported an axon-guidance switch: class 4–6
semaphorins and pro-tumorigenic guidance cues higher in TAF15, growth-inhibitory class 3 semaphorins higher in
EWSR1, recapitulated in cells engineered to express either chimera. Verbatim: *"SEMA3C, SEMA3G, and RELN were
more expressed in EWSR1 tumors"*, and, from a chromatin affinity purification experiment, *"the ability of
NR4A3 to recognize the SEMA3C target region was retained by the EWSR1-NR4A3 chimera but was impaired by
TAF15-NR4A3"*.

⛔ **A third EMC expression cohort replicates SEMA3C, and it does not touch the clinical claim.** GSE28866
(3SEQ) is the only $0-reachable deposit carrying EMC libraries alongside normal tissue; in it SEMA3C reads
**1.8× normal tissue and 1.7× other sarcomas** across 4 EMC libraries, on a platform sharing no probe design
with the two arrays this repository had already read. One home:
[`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json`](../modalities/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json) → `per_gene.values`; reading:
[`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md`](./gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md).

**That is evidence about the pathway, not about the stratification, and the distinction is not a formality:**

1. **GSE28866 carries no fusion-partner annotation at all**, so it cannot produce a partner contrast. Brenca's
   finding is a *difference between two partner groups*; a deposit with no partner calls cannot replicate,
   refute or weaken it.
2. **A partner-blind EMC cohort being SEMA3C-high is what an ~78 % EWSR1 population would look like under
   Brenca's own model**, so the observation is *consistent* with the mechanism and carries no information about
   the contrast.
3. **n = 4 EMC libraries, medians of per-peak medians of read density, transcript only.** No interval, no test,
   no protein, no localisation.
4. **The clinical claim in this document is about objective response and survival**, and no expression deposit
   of any size can move it.

The honest one-line statement is therefore: *the mechanistic axis Brenca described is detectable in a third EMC
cohort on a third technology; the clinical stratification claim is exactly where it was.* Borrowing strength
from the deposit for the clinical claim would be the error this section exists to prevent.

⚠ **And Brenca 2019 is not an independent replication of the clinical correlation.** It shares its senior
investigators and consortium (Milan/Aviano) with both TKI reports. It is the same investigators explaining
their own clinical observation, which is a legitimate and common thing to do and is not the same thing as an
outside group reproducing it.

### 3.7 · The only matched model pair reads against the hypothesis, not with it

Bangerter [12] established the only matched EWSR1/TAF15 patient-derived model pair in existence —
USZ20-EMC1 (EWSR1::NR4A3) and USZ22-EMC2 (TAF15::NR4A3). **The authors' finding is
partner-INDEPENDENCE.** Verbatim: *"Both models independent of the NR4A3 fusion partner showed high
sensitivity to carfilzomib"*, and *"Similar drug responses were seen in both models, independent of the fusion
partner from NR4A3."*

⚠ **Read the screen's shape before reading its result, because it is easy to overstate and this document
overstated it in draft.** The 40-drug panel was **not** run on the pair. Verbatim: *"A medium throughput drug
screen using 40 drugs was conducted with USZ20-EMC1 at passage 5."* Only three agents — carfilzomib,
doxorubicin and venetoclax — went forward to a **validation** step that used both models, and it is that step,
not the 40-drug screen, that the partner-independence sentences describe. The correction does not weaken the
reading, because the comparison was always the validation; it changes how wide the comparison was. Registered
in Appendix A, and corrected in the artifact in the same commit.

The only partner-differential reported is a scoring-threshold one: the carfilzomib + venetoclax and
carfilzomib + doxorubicin combinations scored **synergistic** in the EWSR1 model and **additive** in the TAF15
model, with one model per arm and no statistical claim attached. **Neither model was tested against an
antiangiogenic TKI.**

This does not refute the clinical correlation — it cannot, since it never tested the drug class in which the
correlation was observed. What it does is **bound** the biomarker's scope: there is no evidence that the
*NR4A3* partner predicts drug response in general, and the one experiment positioned to look found the
opposite. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: this repository's own lane memo recorded that "even the carfilzomib synergy
splits by partner", which states the reverse of the primary source; see Appendix A.*

### 3.8 · An attribution correction: whose hedge, and when

The investigators who reported the correlation do not believe the fusion causes it:

> **"Even in EMCS the fusion-protein is unlikely to be related to sunitinib sensitivity"**

⚠ **The quote is from the 2012 two-case report (PMID 23058004), not the 2014 series (PMID 24703573) it is
usually attributed to — including in this repository's own lane memo.** Verified against the cached full text
of both. The 2014 abstract does not contain it, and says instead that *"genotype/phenotype analyses support a
correlation between response and EWSR1-NR4A3 fusion"*.

This changes what the hedge **is**. Written in 2012, when the authors had two EWSR1-positive responders and no
TAF15 patient at all, it is their **prior** — reasoning by analogy to alveolar soft part sarcoma, another
translocation sarcoma in which sunitinib activity is not linked to the fusion protein — rather than a
retrospective caveat added once the partner correlation appeared. **That makes it stronger context, not
weaker**, and it belongs in the abstract of any paper on this question.

Three later observations are consistent with reading the partner as a **surrogate for something downstream**
rather than as a mechanism, and all three are above: the prognostic effect is absorbed by tumour size in the
two largest series (§3.4), Paioli 2021 cannot reach significance on it at all (§3.4), and the matched ex vivo
pair shows partner-independent drug response for the one class it tested (§3.7).

## 4 · Limitations

This section is written to survive an unsympathetic reader. Every item below is a reason to hold the
conclusion loosely, and several of them are reasons the conclusion could be wrong outright.

**4.1 · Every cohort is small, and that is structural rather than remediable.** EMC is ultra-rare. The largest
partner-stratified series anywhere in this synthesis has 67 patients, the largest **poolable** outcome cohort
has 23, and **every TAF15 arm carrying an actual outcome is 2–7 patients.** The only larger TAF15 groups
(Huang 2023's 9, Paioli 2021's 10) publish no event counts at all. The intervals printed here are wide because
the evidence is thin, and they are printed at that width deliberately. No analytic choice can make an ultra-rare
disease's cohorts larger.

**4.2 · This is not a meta-analysis and must not be described as one.** What is done here is **crude
denominator-weighted pooling of counts from non-overlapping cohorts, with Wilson score intervals**. There is
**no** random-effects model, **no** between-study variance, **no** I², **no** funnel plot and **no** formal
heterogeneity test — and that is a deliberate methodological decision recorded in §2.5, not an omission. With
two to nineteen patients and zero to ten events per stratum, τ² is not estimable and a random-effects interval
would be an artefact of the estimator rather than a property of the evidence. Consequently the pooled figures
here are **weighted descriptive summaries**, not meta-analytic estimates, and they carry none of the inferential
guarantees a reader may associate with the word "pooled". Where only one cohort contributes — the entire
outcome-by-partner analysis — the "pool" is a **single-cohort Wilson interval**, stated as such rather than
dressed as a synthesis of several.

**4.3 · Partner assignment is not uniformly molecularly confirmed across reports.** The four prevalence series
did not use one assay, and **two of them do not state, in any source reachable here, which assay assigned the
5′ partner in which case.** Agaram 2014 typed by FISH with RT-PCR junction typing; Huang 2023 describes a
FISH-typed cohort; Lenz 2023 reports only that molecular typing succeeded in 12 of 17 cases; Paioli 2021 reports
only that the diagnoses were molecularly confirmed. A method that is not stated cannot be assumed to be the
same method. Each series also carries a
*NR4A3*-rearranged-but-partner-unassigned residue (2, 1, 1 and 5 cases respectively) which is excluded from both
numerator and denominator, because the series do not describe those residues comparably. **That exclusion
assumes the unassigned cases are missing at random with respect to partner, and no report supports that
assumption.** If variant partners are systematically harder to type — which is the direction a break-apart FISH
workflow would produce — the pooled TAF15 share of 18.2 % is an **underestimate**, and the effect is largest in
the cohort with the biggest residue. Two further consequences: a "TAF15" case in one series and a "TAF15" case
in another are not guaranteed to have been ascertained by the same criterion, and the two congress abstracts
excluded here were excluded partly because their residues cannot be interpreted at all.

**4.4 · The two TKI response strata rest on secondary provenance.** Neither primary full text is open access.
The **per-arm denominators** for both the sunitinib series and the pazopanib trial were read from reviews and
from an independent group's reading of the series, not from the trial or series reports themselves; the
primaries are recorded as `primaryRef` and the strata carry `provenance: "secondary"` under §1.3 of the
contract. Corroboration comes from three sources including two independent groups (Ghent; University of
Michigan), which is why the numbers are used at all — but a reader should know that **the counts on which the
headline rests have not been read in the documents that generated them.**

**4.5 · The overlap between the two TKI cohorts is unresolved, and it is not a small effect.** It is the reason
the headline denominator is a **range (3 to 5)** rather than a number. If the sunitinib series' two TAF15
patients re-enrolled on the pazopanib trial, the world's entire TAF15 antiangiogenic experience is three
patients. The primary analysis assumes the worst case, so the conclusion is stated at both ends and does not
change between them — but the uncertainty is real and only a paywalled table can close it.

**4.6 · The comparator arm is not verified EWSR1.** In the primary analysis it is *non-TAF15* (§3.1). Treating
it as an EWSR1 arm would attribute to EWSR1::NR4A3 a response rate that may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped
cases.

**4.7 · Crude proportions, mixed follow-up, no censoring.** The outcome figures in §3.3 are during-follow-up
proportions from a cohort whose two arms differ roughly two-fold in mean follow-up (43.3 vs 21.7 months). They
are not survival estimates, they are not adjusted, and they are not comparable to the DFS/DMFS figures in §3.4,
which are a different estimand entirely. No hazard ratio, survival curve or individual prognosis is derived
anywhere in this work, and none should be derived from it.

**4.8 · The Fisher p-values are post-hoc.** They were not prespecified, are not performed in any source report,
are not corrected for the several endpoints reported here, and license nothing. The 0.0672 in §3.3 in particular
must not be read as a near-significant finding; with 23 patients and four events it is a description of a small
table.

**4.9 · Publication and referral bias run in a direction that favours the hypothesis.** A perfect response
split is more publishable than a mixed one, and a two-patient report of a striking response is what began this
literature. The one cohort with the highest TAF15 share is a tertiary referral centre. Both biases inflate the
apparent contrast, and neither can be corrected here.

**4.10 · The mechanism is not independent of the clinical observation.** Brenca 2019 shares its consortium with
both TKI reports (§3.6). A mechanism proposed by the same investigators who reported the correlation is
context, not corroboration.

**4.11 · Data vintage.** The pooled cohorts describe patients diagnosed between 1989 and 2017 and treated
across an era in which EMC systemic options changed materially — antiangiogenic TKIs are themselves largely a
post-2011 development in this disease. Per §4 of the evidence contract, older outcome data most likely
understate the outlook of a patient diagnosed today and should be read as a conservative floor. No number here
has been adjusted upward to compensate, because inventing optimism is as dishonest as inventing pessimism.

**4.12 · No treatment recommendation is made, and none follows.** ⛔ Nothing in this document recommends,
supports or opposes giving or withholding sunitinib, pazopanib or any other agent to any patient or patient
group. It asserts no efficacy, no safety, no therapeutic window and no clinical readiness for any agent, and it
is not a basis for a clinical decision. The population-level question it addresses — *what does the published
record actually support?* — is not the individual question a clinician faces.

## 5 · What this synthesis claims, and what it does not

**Claims.**

- That four independent lines converge on the *NR4A3* 5′ partner as a **candidate** stratification variable in
  EMC, and that this is the first time they have been combined under a single stated method.
- That the pooled objective-response evidence is **0 of 3–5 TAF15::NR4A3 patients**, with a 95 % upper bound of
  43.4–56.1 % — **directionally consistent and statistically inconclusive**, a distinction no existing review
  makes.
- That the prognostic literature contains a **reversal nobody has reported**: distant recurrence is higher in
  the EWSR1 arm of the only cohort publishing partner-stratified event counts, while the review literature
  states the opposite.
- That the partner's prognostic effect **does not survive adjustment for tumour size** in either of the two
  larger series to test it (Huang 2023, Paioli 2021), in both of which size is the significant variable.
- That TAF15::NR4A3 affects **18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned EMC — **below** the ≈27 % the
  field routinely quotes from a single referral-centre series.
- That the field's own hedge is **misattributed**: the sentence quoted as the sunitinib series' caveat is from
  the 2012 two-case report, written before any TAF15 patient existed.
- A concrete, zero-patient-cost ask of the field, stated in §6.

**Does not claim.**

- **Not** that TAF15::NR4A3 predicts non-response to antiangiogenic TKIs. Zero of five is not a rate.
- **Not** that EWSR1::NR4A3 patients should receive, or TAF15::NR4A3 patients should be denied, sunitinib,
  pazopanib or any other agent. No treatment recommendation follows from this analysis, and the comparator arm
  of the trial is **non-TAF15**, not verified EWSR1.
- **Not** that the fusion partner is mechanistically responsible for the response difference. The primary
  authors say the opposite, the mechanism paper shares their consortium, and the one matched model pair shows
  partner-independent drug response.
- **Not** that TAF15::NR4A3 is an independent prognostic factor. Huang 2023 says it is not once size is
  adjusted for, and Paioli 2021 does not reach significance at all.
- **Not** a survival model, a hazard ratio, or any individual prognosis. These are crude during-follow-up
  proportions with mixed follow-up and no censoring.
- **Not** a meta-analysis with heterogeneity modelling (§4.2).

**Endpoint, one sentence, for [`systems/views/L3-publications.md`](../../systems/views/L3-publications.md):**
*the NR4A3 5′ fusion partner is a candidate — not established — stratification variable in EMC, whose entire
published antiangiogenic-response evidence base is three to five TAF15 patients, and the field should start
reporting it.*

## 6 · What would falsify this, and what the field could do about it

**What would kill it** (full list in the artifact under `what_could_kill_this`):

1. **One published TAF15::NR4A3 objective response on any antiangiogenic TKI.** With a denominator of 3–5, a
   single response moves the point estimate to 20–33 % and the contrast disappears. This is the shortest
   falsifier in the paper and it is one case report away.
2. The Lancet Oncol 2019 full text showing that the pazopanib trial's non-TAF15 arm contains non-EWSR1
   partners, or that a TAF15 patient sat outside the evaluable 22.
3. Confirmation that the sunitinib series' TAF15 patients re-enrolled on the pazopanib trial, collapsing the
   world's TAF15 experience to three patients.
4. A partner-stratified reanalysis of any registry (SEER, the Japanese national registry, the US Sarcoma
   Collaborative) showing no survival separation once size and stage are adjusted for — the direction Huang
   2023 already points.

**The ask.** Report the *NR4A3* partner alongside response in every EMC systemic-therapy report, and publish
the partner-stratified counts from the trials and series that already hold them. **Three paywalled tables would
more than double the world's poolable evidence on this question at zero new patient cost:**

| document | what it holds | why it is not reachable |
|---|---|---|
| Stacchiotti 2019, *Lancet Oncol* (PMID 31331701) | the pazopanib trial's full partner distribution and prior-therapy table | paywalled; `isOpenAccess N`, `inEPMC N` |
| Huang 2023, *Mod Pathol* (PMID 36948401) | per-partner survival event counts, n = 58 | paywalled |
| Paioli 2021, *Ann Surg Oncol* (PMID 32572850) | per-partner relapse and metastasis counts behind the DFS/DMFS p-values, n = 67 | paywalled; `isOpenAccess N`, `inEPMC N` |

`NCT02066285`'s eligibility text — which would say whether prior antiangiogenic therapy was permitted, and so
whether the overlap in §3.1 is possible — is absent from the cached ClinicalTrials.gov v2 field set.

## 7 · Data, code and reproducibility

**All data underlying this synthesis are published data.** No patient-level data were obtained, held or
analysed; no individual patient record was accessed; and no new patient data of any kind were generated. Every
count comes from a peer-reviewed report or a congress abstract, and each is traceable to a resolvable
identifier (PMID, PMCID, DOI or NCT).

| item | path | role |
|---|---|---|
| pooled artifact | [`emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json`](./emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json) | **the one home of every number in this paper** — cohorts, strata, citations, analyses, provenance |
| generator | [`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](./emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py) | stdlib-only; the counts are in its `COHORTS` table and every derived figure is computed from them |
| evidence contract | [`systems/POLICY-evidence.md`](../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md) | the binding method, with its CI validator `scripts/validate-registry.mjs` |
| clinical registry | [`research/data/emc-clinical-registry.json`](../data/emc-clinical-registry.json) | the repository's cited EMC registry; overlaps this work in citations only |
| third expression cohort | [`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json`](../modalities/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json) | SEMA3C read in §3.6; **not** used for any clinical claim |

**Reproduction.** One command, no dependencies beyond the Python standard library, no network:

```
python3 research/manuscripts/emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py
```

**Verified this session:** re-running the generator on this checkout reproduces the committed artifact
**byte-for-byte apart from its `_generated_utc` stamp**. Changing a count means changing it in the generator's
`COHORTS` table and regenerating — the artifact carries `_do_not_hand_edit` for that reason, and a figure typed
into this document that disagrees with it is the defect.

**Licensing.** Each citation entry records the source's licence and it is respected; the raw-text
`literature-cache` branch is never republished.

## 8 · Provenance and retrieval

The development sandbox's egress proxy returns 403 on CONNECT to Europe PMC, NCBI and ClinicalTrials.gov
(verified this session: curl exit 56, HTTP 000). Every source was read from the repository's own
`literature-cache` branch, populated by GitHub Actions runs of `.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`. Cache
slugs used, the verbatim sentence each count came from, and the documents that are **not** retrievable are
recorded in the artifact's `retrieval_provenance` block and in each citation's `verification_note`.

Two facts a reader should be able to check without trusting this document:

- **Every count in §3 is accompanied in the artifact by the verbatim sentence it was read from**, together with
  the cache path and the HTTP status of the fetch that produced it.
- **The three documents that would resolve the open questions are named, with the specific reason each is
  unreachable** (§6). An absent reading is recorded as an absent reading, never as a reading of absence.
