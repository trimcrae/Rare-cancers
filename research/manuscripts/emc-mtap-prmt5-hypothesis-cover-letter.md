**Re:** Submission — *"PRMT5 and the MTAP locus in extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma: two rationales tested against the available public data, neither supported"*

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript above for consideration in *Genes, Chromosomes and Cancer*. Extraskeletal
myxoid chondrosarcoma is an ultra-rare translocation sarcoma driven by an *NR4A3* fusion, no
clinically validated agent directly targets that driver, and nothing indexed pairs the PRMT5
methylosome with this histology. The paper takes the two independent rationales that would put that
target class in front of the disease, one transferring from other EWSR1-fusion sarcomas and one
resting on selection at the *MTAP* locus, and tests each against the only public data able to
address it: the two archival expression series containing this histology, a public sarcoma CRISPR
dependency panel, and a sequence analysis of where PRMT5's reported substrate motif falls in the
fusion protein.

**Neither rationale is supported, and that is what the paper reports.** The result a reader is most
likely to use is the *MTAP* negative, and it is now argued on the right instrument. Homozygous 9p21
deletion is a subset event, so a difference of group means cannot test it; the manuscript therefore
reads every tumour. Five of ten EMC tumours on one platform do sit below every comparator for
*MTAP*, which is the pattern the rationale predicts and which no group statistic shows. None of the
five carries the low *CDKN2A* that 9p21 co-deletion requires: all five sit at or above the median of
their own array, the tumour with the lowest *MTAP* reading carries the highest *CDKN2A* in the arm,
two further 9p21 genes agree, and neither array quality nor the two-colour reference pool explains
the tail. No tumour of sixteen is deletion-consistent, which bounds the frequency at 17% rather than
excluding the event, and the manuscript says which single stain would close the question.

For the fusion-class rationale the honest report is that this data cannot test it. *PRMT5* reads
higher in EMC on both platforms and first of the readable PRMT family on both, and that reading
clears no conventional threshold once the number of genes examined is corrected for. The corrected
value is also a property of the family chosen and ranges across three orders of magnitude, which the
manuscript reports as a sensitivity rather than quoting one point from it.

The fit with the journal is the fusion. Readers of *Genes, Chromosomes and Cancer* are the audience
for EWSR1::NR4A3, EWSR1::ATF1 and EWSR1::FLI1 biology, and the paper's one durable positive
observation is at that level: the N-terminal segment every EWSR1 fusion retains carries none of
PRMT5's eleven reported motif sites in EWSR1. The same section withdraws a claim an earlier draft
made from the same analysis, that two diseases' commonest junctions retain a matching number of
sites, because four sites cluster within twenty residues and the next lies 143 residues away, so any
breakpoint across a 142-residue window returns four. That is a correction the paper makes against
itself and it is reported at full length.

**Related manuscripts, shared data and what distinguishes this one.** Two further manuscripts from
me are in preparation for this journal and both re-analyse deposits this one uses, so I set out the
overlap rather than leaving an editor to discover it. `nr4a3-fusion-transcriptional-output` reads
the same two GEO series, GSE24369 and GSE4303, through the same committed expression artifact and
the same analysis code, and asks a different question: what the *NR4A3* fusion's transcriptional
output looks like across a target panel, together with a confound audit of the EMC expression
contrast. `emc-atr-collaborator-package` concerns the ATR/replication-stress axis in the same
disease and also draws on GSE4303. The three share sixteen EMC tumours, one reduction and one code
base; they differ in the gene panel read and in the hypothesis tested, and no result, figure or
table is duplicated between them. If the editors would prefer these consolidated into a single paper
on the EMC expression record, reporting every panel read against it with the PRMT5 and *MTAP*
readings as sections, I would rather do that than divide one re-analysis into three submissions, and
I will follow the editors' preference. No version of any of them has been published or is under
consideration at another journal.

The study is entirely computational. No experiment was performed in an EMC cell, no drug was given
to any cell or animal, no reagent was made, and no wet-laboratory work of any kind was carried out.
Every number re-analyses public archival deposits or public reference sequence, and no new human or
animal data were generated, so no ethics approval was required. The manuscript asserts no efficacy,
selectivity, safety, therapeutic window or clinical readiness for any agent in any disease, and
makes none by implication.

Three limitations are structural rather than fixable, and the manuscript states them as such rather
than leaving them to a referee. On the 16-tumour series, disease class is perfectly collinear with
GEO submission block, with the two-colour reference pool and with within-study platform assignment,
so a permutation that relabels those samples is not exchangeable and that platform is reported as a
consistency check rather than as replication. Every primary reading rests on a single probe per gene
per platform. And no EMC cell line carrying the fusion exists in any public dependency dataset, so
no dependency evidence for this axis in this disease can be generated computationally at all.

I intend to deposit the manuscript as a preprint on bioRxiv, consistent with the journal's preprint
policy, and will link the preprint to the published version. I am the sole author, an unaffiliated
independent researcher with no institutional address; no ORCID accompanies this submission. An AI
research assistant was used in the analysis and drafting, which is disclosed in the manuscript's
Methods and Declarations, including what I personally re-derived and re-read; no AI tool is an
author, and I take full responsibility for the content.

I have no competing interests and received no funding.

Should it help the editors, appropriate reviewer expertise would include sarcoma molecular
