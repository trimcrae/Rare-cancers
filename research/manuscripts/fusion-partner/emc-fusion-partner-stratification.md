## Abstract

**Background.** Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is defined by an *NR4A3* rearrangement whose 5′
partner is *EWSR1* in most cases and *TAF15* in a minority. Reports on objective response to antiangiogenic
tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (TKIs), on outcome by partner, on partner prevalence and on a transcriptional
mechanism each describe a difference between the two variants, usually without stating the denominators the
difference rests on.

**Methods.** This is a descriptive synthesis of the EMC reports in this repository's citation set that give an
outcome stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner. No database was queried and no screening flow was run (§2.3a), so
nothing here is a census of the literature. Counts are crude denominator-weighted proportions with Wilson
score 95 % intervals on explicit integer `{events, denom}` pairs, non-overlapping populations only, following
a pre-existing evidence contract. **Every quantitative input was checked against a retained source, and the
inputs that could not be verified were removed rather than carried** (§8).

**Results.** **Response.** Three reports give objective response by partner. In the pazopanib phase 2 trial
NCT02066285, no response is recorded among 3 reported *TAF15::NR4A3* patients against 4 of 19 among the
remaining evaluable patients — a difference of recorded proportions of 21.1 percentage points whose
uncertainty is very wide (TAF15 Wilson upper bound 56.1 %, which lies above the comparator's own point
estimate). In the sunitinib named-use series, no response is recorded among 2 *TAF15::NR4A3* patients against
6 of 8; a secondary source reports that all eight clinical-benefit patients carried *EWSR1::NR4A3*. Combining
the two series is a **conditional descriptive mixture** of different drugs, settings and eligibility, valid
only if no patient appears in both; it gives 0/5 against 10/27, a 37.0-point difference with a TAF15 upper
bound of 43.4 %. **A comparison confined to treated patients cannot establish that the partner modifies
treatment benefit, at any denominator.** **Outcome.** One cohort's per-partner outcome event counts are
source-verified: Agaram 2014 records disease-specific death in 3/7 *TAF15* against 1/16 *EWSR1* patients, with
one further *EWSR1* patient dead of unknown cause, local recurrence 2/7 against 1/16, and distant recurrence
2/7 against 6/16, over arms whose mean observation windows differ about two-fold. **The two-cohort pooled
prognostic magnitude previously reported here is withdrawn**, because the Huang 2023 Table 1 cells it rested
on are not verified by any retained original (§8). Four further series are reported separately at their own
scope. **Prevalence.** Across four molecular series with source-verified partner counts, *TAF15::NR4A3* is
recorded in **28/154 = 18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned EMC.

**Conclusions.** What this synthesis contributes is a transparent accounting of a small, selected evidence
base: the denominators, the recorded events, the uncertain partner assignments, the overlap assumptions and
the source limitations, stated together in one place. It does **not** establish an independent prognostic
effect of the partner, equivalence of recurrence or metastasis between partners, a predictive treatment
interaction, or that tumour size explains a partner association. The *NR4A3* 5′ partner remains a
**candidate — not established — stratification variable**. The concrete, zero-patient-cost ask is that the
partner be reported alongside response and outcome in every EMC systemic-therapy and outcome report.

## 1 · Introduction

### 1.1 · The disease, the drug class and the variable

EMC is an ultra-rare soft-tissue sarcoma, and it is
molecularly defined: *"EMCs are translocation sarcomas and harbor in > 90% of the cases an NR4A3
rearrangement"* [12]. The 5′ partner is *EWSR1* in the large majority, *TAF15* in a minority, and *TCF12*,
*TFG* or an unidentified partner in a small residue [7,8,9,10]. The clinical shape of the disease is the
reason a stratification variable would matter: *"Although often associated with prolonged survival, EMC have
high rates of distant recurrences and disease-associated death"*, and *"Anthracycline-based chemotherapy,
which is the first-line regimen used in STS, has low activity in this sarcoma subtype"* [12,4].

The one systemic class with reproducible activity in EMC is the antiangiogenic TKIs. Sunitinib produced
responses in a named-use series [1] after an index two-patient report [2], and pazopanib was taken into a
dedicated single-arm phase 2 trial, NCT02066285, which reported 4 objective responses among 22 evaluable
patients [3]. Both reports remark, in passing and without pooling, that the responders carried EWSR1::NR4A3 and
that the TAF15::NR4A3 patients did not respond.

### 1.2 · What the four lines of evidence report, and what they leave unstated

The claim "the fusion partner matters" is currently supported in four places that mostly do not cite each
other:

| line of evidence | what it reports | what it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| **TKI response** | responders are reported as EWSR1, and the reported TAF15 patients did not respond [1,3,4,5,6] | no source computes an interval on either stratum, or reports how imprecise the difference is |
| **Outcome by partner** | TAF15 associated with worse disease-specific survival [7,8,9] | of the four series that test the partner against outcome, only one publishes per-partner event counts that a retained source verifies (§3.3); one publishes p-values only, one is an unresolved-overlap MSK series reporting no OS association by subtype, and one series' outcome table is not verifiable here (§8). None is adjusted in a way this synthesis can inspect |
| **Partner prevalence** | TAF15 in roughly one EMC in five [7,8,9,10] | not pooled across series, so the share is stated as a point figure and no interval exists for it |
| **Mechanism** | an axon-guidance switch separates the two variants [11] | is not a clinical endpoint, and shares a consortium with the trials |

⚠ **What is missing is not the counts — it is the accounting.** The per-arm denominators are stated in the
review literature, both cohorts' in a single paragraph of [5]: *"the two cases with progressive disease
harbored a TAF15-NR4A3 translocation"* for the sunitinib series, and *"Of the three patients with TAF15 as
translocation partner, none showed a response"* for the pazopanib trial. This paper read its own denominators
from exactly those reviews (§4.4), so it is in no position to say the field never stated them. What no source
does is put them on one page with their uncertainty, their overlap assumptions and their source limitations
attached. The result is a belief stated more firmly in the field's prose than the underlying counts support.

⚠ **And these literatures are not as separate as an earlier version of this section claimed.** The 2020
disease review [4] already brings mechanism, morphology, targets and clinical partner response together, and
the 2025 review [15] connects morphology, partner and therapy again. Agaram's own abstract already reports
1/16 against 3/7 died-of-disease, so a crude partner difference existed in the literature before any
calculation here.

### 1.3 · What this synthesis adds

It adds one thing, and the claim is deliberately narrow: **a transparent numerical accounting of the reports
this synthesis actually examined.** For each stratum it states the integer events, the denominator, the
population that denominator describes, the source sentence it was read from, and what the source does not
say. Four consequences of doing that are not stated out loud by any individual source. First, the response
difference is imprecise enough that the comparator arm's own response proportion sits inside the TAF15 arm's
interval. Second, the partner comparisons available in treated cohorts are comparisons within treated
patients, which cannot establish a treatment interaction however large they are. Third, only one cohort's
per-partner outcome counts survive source verification, so the prognostic contrast is a single series'
recorded events rather than a pooled magnitude. Fourth, prevalence has an **interval** where the literature
carries point figures.

⚠ **It also keeps two questions apart that the literature blurs.**

| question | what the examined record supports | what is missing |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) PROGNOSIS by partner** | one source-verified series' recorded event counts, plus four other series described at their own scope (§3.3) | a second verifiable event-count cohort, per-arm follow-up, event ordering, and any adjusted analysis whose specification can be inspected (§3.4) |
| **(b) TREATMENT RESPONSE by partner** | recorded response proportions with wide uncertainty: 0 of 3 in the trial, 0 of 5 under a conditional mixture (§3.1) | a treatment comparator. A partner comparison among treated patients cannot separate prognosis from prediction |

**(a) and (b) are different endpoints in different populations, and neither moves the other.**

## 2 · Methods

### 2.1 · The binding evidence contract

The statistical method here was **not selected for this analysis**. It is the repository's standing evidence
contract, [`systems/POLICY-evidence.md`](../../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md), which predates this work and is
enforced in continuous integration by `scripts/validate-registry.mjs`. Adopting a pre-committed method removes
the degree of freedom that matters most in a synthesis this small: the choice of estimator after seeing the
counts.

Sections that bind here:

- **§1.3 — primary versus secondary provenance.** `sourceId` is the document a number was actually **read in**.
  A number read out of a review carries `provenance: "secondary"` and a `primaryRef` naming the original study.
  Identifiers are never invented for a primary source that was not itself fetched.
- **§2.1 — what may be pooled.** Molecularly typed EMC; **explicit integer** `{events, denom}` (never counts
  back-derived from a published percentage); an outcome that is not the inclusion criterion; a non-overlapping
  population.
- **§2.2 — the estimator.** Crude denominator-weighted proportion p̂ = Σevents / Σdenom, with a **Wilson score**
  95 % interval on the summed counts. Heterogeneity is reported as the **range of per-cohort rates**.
- **§2.3 — double-counting.** Mutually exclusive strata within a study; where two populations may overlap, the
  **smaller** cohort is held out with `contextReason: "population-overlap"`.
- **§2.4 — estimand discipline.** Event-rate metrics are pooled as **crude during-follow-up proportions**,
  explicitly labelled as mixed-follow-up and uncensored. Time-anchored survival figures are never merged.

### 2.2 · Estimator and interval, and why Wilson

Every stratum in this analysis is small and several sit **exactly at zero events**. The normal-approximation
interval collapses to a degenerate point at 0/n and would misrepresent the evidence in the single place where
the evidence is most easily overstated. The Wilson score interval is well behaved at small n and at the
boundaries, and §2.2 of the contract names it by construction. Arithmetic:
[`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](../emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py) → `wilson()`.

### 2.3 · Inclusion, exclusion, and the overlap rule

Reports were assessed for whether they give an outcome **stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner** with integer counts
on both sides of the stratification. Cohorts that report a partner-stratified result without counts are carried
as **context** — visible with their own numbers and citation, excluded from any pooled figure — exactly as the
contract requires. The full inclusion table is §3.2.

The consequential decision is the sunitinib series. The **primary analysis is the pazopanib trial alone**, and
the reason is stated as a design judgement rather than as an arithmetic rule: it is the larger, prospective,
single-protocol cohort, and it is the one whose eligibility, drug and setting are uniform. The sunitinib
series enters only a **conditional descriptive mixture** that assumes the two cohorts contain distinct
patients (§3.1). Two arms sharing the label *non-TAF15* is necessary for that mixture and is not sufficient to
make two studies poolable: the drugs, the settings, the eligibility and the comparator response proportions
differ, so the combined figure is a sample-weighted mixture under the selected study weights and not a
demonstrated drug-class response proportion.

⛔ **2.3a · No systematic search strategy was run, and every claim in this paper that depends on
exhaustiveness is bounded by that fact.** The candidate literature was assembled from this repository's
existing EMC citation set — the review articles already cited here [4,5,15], the reports they cite, and the
reports that cite them — read against the `literature-cache` branch populated by
[`.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`](../../../.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml) (§2.6). **No
database was queried with a registered search string across a defined date range, no PRISMA-style screening
flow was run, and no second reader checked the inclusion decisions.**

Every count, every "only", and every statement about what the field has or has not done therefore reads
**within the set of reports this synthesis examined**, and none is a census. Two statements are most at risk
of being read as a census, and both are stated in their bounded form where they appear rather than only here:
that **no source puts these counts on one page with their uncertainty and limits** (§1.2), and that **no
response is recorded for any TAF15::NR4A3 patient in the reports examined** (Abstract, §3.1). ⚠ *Superseded,
retained: an earlier version of this paragraph named specific superlatives — "**Two** cohorts publish EMC
outcome event counts by NR4A3 partner", "the only two cohorts that publish event counts by partner", "the
largest series to test metastasis by partner directly" — as the claims it bounded. Those sentences have been
removed from the paper along with the pooled analysis they described (§3.3), so the list is no longer a
description of this document.* The paper behaves throughout as though a single further report could change
these figures, which is why §6 asks for exactly that.

### 2.4 · Significance testing, declared as post-hoc

Fisher exact two-sided p-values are reported for each contrast. They are **post-hoc and descriptive only**:
not prespecified, not performed in any source report, not corrected for the several endpoints on this page,
and **not used to license any claim in this document**. They are printed because a reader will compute them
otherwise, and printing them with their status attached is more honest than withholding them.

### 2.5 · Methods deliberately not used, and why

- **Random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) pooling.** This repository owns a random-effects pooler
  ([`research/meta/meta-analysis.mjs`](../../meta/meta-analysis.mjs)) and it is not used here. ⚠ **The reason
  is a judgement about interpretability, not a mathematical impossibility, and an earlier version of this
  paragraph said otherwise.** With few studies and sparse counts a between-study variance estimate is
  unstable and a τ² from these numbers would be difficult to interpret, so quoting a random-effects interval
  would suggest precision this evidence base does not have. A repository pooling policy is also not by itself
  a scientific justification for a pooling decision; where a decision matters it is argued in the section
  that makes it.
- **I².** Not computed, for the same reason. The honest heterogeneity signal at this scale is the **range of
  per-cohort rates**, printed beside every figure pooled over more than one cohort — §3.1 for the conditional
  response mixture and §3.5 for prevalence — and computed in the artifact's `heterogeneity` blocks,
  which are the one home of every one of these spreads.
- **Hazard ratios, survival models, Kaplan–Meier.** ⚠ **The binding reason, stated correctly:** harmonised
  individual-level time-to-event information — per-patient follow-up time, event times and censoring — is not
  available for these cohorts. That is not the same as saying no individual patient records exist: this
  synthesis reads published per-patient case tables where sources provide them (§3.3), and several of the
  sources here do. What is missing is the time-to-event structure a survival model needs, in a form that
  could be harmonised across studies. No time-to-event quantity is estimated anywhere in this work.
- **Back-derived counts.** Where a source gives only a percentage, no `{events, denom}` is inferred. Rounding
  invents data, and §2.1(2) forbids it.

### 2.6 · Retrieval

The development environment's egress proxy refuses CONNECT to Europe PMC, NCBI and ClinicalTrials.gov
(verified this session: curl exit 56, HTTP 000). Every source was therefore read from the repository's own
`literature-cache` branch, populated by GitHub Actions runs of `.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`. Cache
slugs used, the verbatim sentence each count was read from, and the documents that are **not** retrievable are
recorded in the artifact's `retrieval_provenance` block and in each citation's `verification_note`. Full
accounting: §8.

## 3 · Results

### 3.1 · Objective response to an antiangiogenic TKI, by partner

**No response is recorded for any *TAF15::NR4A3* patient** in the three EMC reports **this synthesis
examined** that give an objective-response outcome by *NR4A3* partner. One is the primary analysis; one enters
a conditional mixture only; one is contained in that series and is excluded from both (§3.2). ⚠ *No systematic
search was run (§2.3a), so this is a statement about the reports examined here, not about the literature.*

| analysis | TAF15 arm | comparator arm | difference of recorded proportions | TAF15 95 % upper bound | Fisher (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (pazopanib trial alone) | 0/3 = 0.0 % | 4/19 = 21.1 % | **21.1 pts** | **56.1 %** | 1.0 |
| **Conditional mixture** (both series, assumes distinct patients) | 0/5 = 0.0 % | 10/27 = 37.0 % — per cohort **21.1 %** (pazopanib, 4/19) and **75.0 %** (sunitinib, 6/8), spread **53.9 pts** | **37.0 pts** | **43.4 %** | 0.155 |

⭐ **These are differences of recorded response proportions, and they are imprecise.** ⚠ *Superseded,
retained: "a zero-event arm yields no magnitude at any denominator", and "zero of five is not a rate".* Both
statements are **mathematically false and are withdrawn.** Zero responses among three or five patients is an
observed proportion with a very wide uncertainty interval, not a missing denominator. The real limits are
different and are stated here instead: in both analyses the TAF15 arm's 95 % upper bound lies **above the
comparator arm's own point estimate**, so the data are compatible with a TAF15 response proportion equal to
the comparator's; the intervals printed are marginal intervals on each arm, and **overlapping marginal
intervals are not a test or an interval for the difference**; and the comparator is `non-TAF15` in both
analyses, not verified EWSR1 (§4.6), so no figure here is a response proportion *in EWSR1::NR4A3 patients*.

⛔ **And a comparison confined to treated patients cannot establish a predictive treatment interaction, at any
denominator.** Every patient in every arm above received an antiangiogenic TKI. A difference between partner
groups among treated patients is consistent with the partner predicting benefit from the drug and equally
consistent with the partner marking a worse-behaving tumour that would have done worse untreated. Separating
those requires a treatment comparator and a treatment-by-partner analysis, or a carefully bounded alternative
design. None exists in this record. Anyone who has written "TAF15 tumours do not respond to antiangiogenics"
has written a claim the evidence does not carry, and that includes reviews that state it flatly.

**Three further features of these strata matter and are easy to lose:**

- **The pazopanib analysis populations are only partly reconstructed.** The trial's primary report
  distinguishes **26 patients treated, 23 eligible for the modified intention-to-treat analysis, and 22
  evaluable** after a death. The held reviews place three TAF15 patients in the trial and report that none
  responded; they do not give a partner-by-analysis-population flow. **The 0/3 versus 4/19 table is therefore
  conditional on all three TAF15 patients sitting inside the 22 evaluable**, and the three other exclusions
  between 26 and 22 are unaccounted for in every source held here. No alternative partner assignment is
  invented for them; what is not reported is marked as not reported. If the one patient who died before the
  analysis carried TAF15, the strata become 0/2 and 4/20 and the TAF15 upper bound rises to 65.8 %, outside
  the 43.4–56.1 % range quoted above — so that range is itself conditional.
- ⭐ **The sunitinib series' eight non-progressing patients are reported as EWSR1 by a secondary source, and
  an earlier version of this paper said no source classified them.** ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "No source held
  here states the partner of the two stable-disease patients."* That was **false**. Davis 2017 states that the
  eight patients with clinical benefit — the six partial responses **and** the two stable diseases — harboured
  *EWSR1-NR4A3*. The correct record is **eight EWSR1 cases as reported by a secondary source**, not eight
  untyped cases. This does not make the classification a primary patient-level verification: the 2014 primary
  report is held here as an **abstract**, not a full text, and the statement is an account of another group's
  cohort. ⛔ **It does not transfer to the pazopanib trial**, whose partner distribution no retained source
  gives. The arm keeps the label `non-TAF15` for a different and now explicitly stated reason: that is the
  label under which it is combined with the pazopanib remainder in the conditional mixture, where both arms
  must mean the same thing.
- **Neither comparator arm is verified EWSR1 at trial level.** All four pazopanib responders are reported as
  *EWSR1::NR4A3*, but no accessible source gives the trial's full partner distribution, so the 19 comparator
  patients may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped *NR4A3*-rearranged cases.

**Why the trial is the primary analysis, and what the overlap evidence actually says.** Istituto Nazionale
Tumori Milan ran the sunitinib series and was a trial site under the same senior investigator, so patient
reuse is a real question. ⭐ **The retained registry record bears on it and is now applied.** NCT02066285's
ClinicalTrials.gov v2 record excludes *"Patients who have received previous antiangiogenic agents"*; sunitinib
is an antiangiogenic agent, so the trial's own written eligibility runs **against** these patients having
re-enrolled. That is protocol evidence, not an enrolment audit: it cannot establish patient-level
independence by itself, and protocol deviations are not excluded. ⛔ *Superseded, retained: the claim that the
trial's RECIST-progression-within-six-months entry criterion is satisfied "by definition" by the series' two
progressors.* Progression recorded in an earlier series does not establish RECIST progression in the six
months before a later enrolment. The trial is the primary analysis because it is the larger prospective
cohort under one protocol, and the mixture is reported separately because it assumes something the record
cannot confirm.

### 3.3 · Outcome by partner — one source-verified series, and four reported beside it

⛔ **The two-cohort pooled prognostic magnitude previously reported in this section is withdrawn.**
⚠ *Superseded, retained: "Pooled over 73 patients, disease-specific death is 7/15 = 46.7 % (95 % CI
24.8–69.9) with TAF15::NR4A3 against 6/58 = 10.3 % (95 % CI 4.8–20.8) — a 36.3-point gap, post-hoc Fisher
p = 0.0034."* Every figure in that sentence depended on cells transcribed from Huang 2023's Table 1, and no
retained original verifies them: the holdings for that paper are an abstract record and a manifest recording
HTTP 403 on the publisher's full-text and PDF routes, re-inspected on 2026-09-08 and confirmed to be anti-bot

patients.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **disease-specific death** | **3/7 = 42.9 %** (95 % CI 15.8–75.0) | **1/16 = 6.2 %** (95 % CI 1.1–28.3) | 36.6 pts | 0.0672 |
| local recurrence | 2/7 = 28.6 % (8.2–64.1) | 1/16 = 6.2 % (1.1–28.3) | 22.3 pts | 0.2095 |
| distant recurrence | 2/7 = 28.6 % (8.2–64.1) | 6/16 = 37.5 % (18.5–61.4) | −8.9 pts | 1.0 |

*These are crude proportions of recorded events over unequal, uncensored observation windows in a single
referral-centre series. They are not survival probabilities at a common horizon, not adjusted, and not a
prognosis. The intervals are marginal intervals on each arm, not intervals on the difference, and the Fisher
values are post-hoc and descriptive (§4.8).*

⚠ **One further *EWSR1* patient died of an unknown cause, and is kept distinct.** Agaram's case 3 is recorded
as dead of unknown cause. It is not counted as a disease-specific death, and it is equally not evidence that
the patient survived without one, so the comparator denominator above is **not** a set of patients all known
to have avoided a disease-specific death. Any reading of that arm carries this unresolved case.

⚠ **The arms' observation windows differ and the direction of the resulting bias cannot be signed.** Mean
follow-up is 43.3 months in the EWSR1 arm and 21.7 in the TAF15 arm. ⛔ *Superseded, retained: the claim that
shorter TAF15 follow-up biases against that arm accruing deaths, so the death excess is observed despite
shorter observation.* In an uncensored during-follow-up analysis follow-up **ends at death**, so the TAF15
arm's shorter window is partly produced by its own deaths; and death also precludes a later recorded
recurrence. Neither effect can be signed from final counts alone without event ordering, and no censored
analysis in the source would separate them. The honest statement is that the windows differ, that the
difference is not independent of the endpoints being compared, and that this is a limitation of the display
rather than a correction that can be applied to it.

⚠ **The distant-recurrence row is a recorded tally under the source's own label.** Agaram reports "distant
recurrence" without stating whether a patient metastatic at presentation sat inside its denominator, so the
cell cannot be read as incident metastasis risk.

**Four further series bear on the same question and are reported here at their own scope, not merged.**

- **Huang 2023** (n = 58, Taiwan), at abstract scope only: 46 *EWSR1*, 9 *TAF15*, 2 *TCF12* and 1
  unidentified partner; *TAF15::NR4A3* significantly associated with size > 10 cm (78 %, P = .025); size
  > 10 cm, moderate-to-severe nuclear pleomorphism, metastasis at presentation, *TAF15::NR4A3* and
  administration of chemotherapy each associated with shorter univariate disease-specific survival; and only
  size > 10 cm (P = .004) and metastasis at presentation (P = .032) remaining prognostically independent. See
  §3.4 for what that does and does not establish.
- **Paioli 2021** (n = 67), at abstract scope only: partner trends for DFS (p = 0.08) and DMFS (p = 0.09),
  tumour size p = 0.004 for DMFS, median follow-up 55 months. The adjustment structure is not stated in the
  abstract and the full text is closed (§6), so this is not a second adjusted test.
- **Suemitsu 2025** [17] (n = 18, MSK): 14 *EWSR1*, 2 *TAF15*, 1 *TCF12*, 1 *FUS*, and no statistically
  significant correlation between overall survival and fusion subtype. With two TAF15 patients that is a
  series failing to establish an association on its own endpoint, not evidence against one.
- **Sjögren 2003** [16] (nine patients, ten tumours): recorded tumour-related deaths are **0/3 with TAF15 against
  1/5 with EWSR1** — the comparator arm is not zero, because *EWSR1* case 7 records a tumour-related death at
  ten years. The denominators are **patients**, not the ten tumours of the paper's title: cases 6-I and 6-II
  are two metastases from one patient and are counted once. Four of the nine entered on an already-published
  fusion transcript, follow-up differs per patient, and the source's own discussion reports no apparent
  correlation with prognosis while asking for longer follow-up and larger series. ⛔ **This is descriptive
  context with sampling and follow-up limits. It is not evidence of equivalence, and it is not pooled.**

⛔ **No Huang-dependent sensitivity is computed.** A conditional addition of the Sjögren counts to the
withdrawn Huang inputs would have been **7/18 against 7/63**, not the 7/18 against 6/63 an earlier round
proposed — that proposal omitted the one *EWSR1* tumour-related death the source records. Neither figure is
carried here, because both depend on inputs this synthesis has withdrawn. The corrected arithmetic is stated
only so the earlier, wrong pair is not quoted from the record.

⛔ **What none of this establishes.** Not an independent prognostic effect of the partner; not equivalence of
local recurrence or metastasis between partners; not a survival difference at a common horizon; not a signed
direction of follow-up bias; and nothing at all about response to treatment. Non-significant Fisher values on
these counts do not establish a null: at these denominators, clinically substantial differences in either
direction remain compatible with the recorded events.

### 3.4 · What the reported adjusted analyses do and do not establish

Two series tested the partner against tumour size, and neither is external to this synthesis: Huang 2023
contributes to the prevalence pool of §3.5, and Paioli 2021 likewise. **Neither found the partner to reach
conventional significance for prognosis in the analysis it reports.**

- **Huang 2023** (PMID 36948401, n = 58, Taiwan): the abstract reports a multivariable analysis of
  disease-specific survival in which only size > 10 cm (P = .004) and metastasis at presentation (P = .032)
  remained prognostically independent, and reports *TAF15::NR4A3* significantly associated with size > 10 cm
  (78 %, P = .025).
- **Paioli 2021** (PMID 32572850, n = 67, Italian Sarcoma Group): the partner shows only trends for DFS
  (p = 0.08) and DMFS (p = 0.09), in an analysis where size reaches p = 0.004. ⚠ *Whether that analysis was
  adjusted is not recorded in any source held here, and its size p-value concerns **distant metastasis-free
  survival** — a different endpoint from Huang's disease-specific survival. The two p-values cannot be
  contrasted to infer that one variable carries prognosis and the other does not.*

⛔ **That the partner term did not remain conventionally significant does not establish that an independent
association is absent, that any crude association is "real" and then "explained", or that tumour size
confounds the partner.** ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "the partner is standing in for size", "the crude partner
effect on death is real and now measured", and the description of this analysis as the finding's "defeater".*
Those readings are withdrawn, and so are the words *defeater*, *confounded* and *absorbed* wherever this
paper applied them to the partner. The reasons are specific. No partner coefficient, confidence interval,
event count, model specification or missing-data rule is available in any retained source, so attenuation and
precision cannot be assessed. The abstract names five univariate candidate predictors, and at the death count
a series of this size implies, model instability is a material concern. And an association between size and
partner does not make size a confounder: tumour size may be a consequence of fusion-driven biology, or lie on
a causal path between the partner and the outcome, in which case adjusting for it removes part of the effect
being estimated. Conversely, a significant partner term in some future adjusted analysis would not turn an
observational association into evidence of biology.

⚠ **What survives is a narrower statement.** In the analyses these two series report, **an independent
prognostic association of the fusion partner was not established**; imprecision and mediation both remain
open. The 78 % size figure is Huang's own reported association within its cohort and is attributed to that
cohort, not to TAF15 EMC generally. Reopening a stronger claim in either direction requires the actual model
specification, the partner coefficient and its uncertainty, the event count, the missing-data handling and a
defensible causal interpretation; significance thresholds alone will not supply it.

size, the cases in which molecular characterisation succeeded, the cases assigned a named partner, and the
cases followed for outcome. The prevalence figures below are computed over **named-partner assigned cases

does, and nothing here supports that step. The Wilson interval is conditional on a binomial sampling model
over the pooled counts: it does not account for study selection, differential molecular ascertainment or
between-study variation, and it cannot supply representative-population precision. ⚠ **Nor does this synthesis
sign the direction of the missing-case bias, and an earlier version did.** If variant partners are
systematically harder to type the pooled TAF15 share would be an underestimate; but a break-apart workflow
could equally fail to assign other rare partners, which would push the other way, and referral enrichment has
no established direction without a specified selection mechanism.

⚠ **The pooled interval contains the share this document's own cited sources state.** The disease review [4]
and the introduction of the model paper [12] both put the TAF15 share at about 20 %, which falls **inside**

over four series, and shows where the individual cohorts fall within it. Agaram 2014 is the highest of the
four (29.2 % of assigned cases, against 15.8–18.2 % elsewhere), and a tertiary sarcoma referral centre is a
setting where morphologically unusual, high-grade tumours are over-represented — which is what
*TAF15::NR4A3* tends to be. The two excluded congress abstracts bracket the pooled estimate (7 of 26
partner-assigned in Valencia; 1 of 9 in Pilsen/Znojmo), and Sjögren 2003 sits above it at 3/9 = 33.3 % at
patient level. Sjögren is held out of this denominator because four of its nine patients entered on an
already-published fusion transcript, so its partner share is partly an entry criterion; **that exclusion moves
the estimate away from the highest published shares rather than toward them**, and is named here for that
reason. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: the description of that exclusion as "outcome-is-the-inclusion-criterion".*
Selection on cytogenetic material and prior publication is selection on **partner ascertainment**, not on the
mortality outcome this synthesis contrasts, and the same rule is applied to every series in the table.

### 3.6 · Mechanism, and what a third expression cohort does and does not add

Brenca 2019 [11] profiled 7 *EWSR1-NR4A3* and 5 *TAF15-NR4A3* EMC tumours and reported an axon-guidance
switch: class 4–6 semaphorins and pro-tumorigenic guidance cues higher in TAF15, growth-inhibitory class 3
semaphorins higher in EWSR1. Verbatim: *"SEMA3C, SEMA3G, and RELN were more expressed in EWSR1 tumors"*, and,
from a chromatin affinity purification experiment, *"the ability of NR4A3 to recognize the SEMA3C target
region was retained by the EWSR1-NR4A3 chimera but was impaired by TAF15-NR4A3"*.

⚠ **Two different kinds of result are reported in that paper and this synthesis keeps them apart.** The
seven-versus-five comparison is partner-associated profiling of human tumours. The recapitulation is an
effect produced by engineered constructs expressed in the paper's own cell systems, with independent
biological replicates, in which the two chimeric proteins were present at **different abundance** despite
similar mRNA. Effects in those systems can support a mechanism there; they are not a comparison at equal
protein abundance, and they are not a clinical treatment interaction. The paper's header points to a 2021
correction whose content and figures were not separately inspected here, so no claim is made about the
erratum.

**A third EMC expression cohort carries the same gene, and it does not touch the clinical claim.** GSE28866
(3SEQ) is the only openly reachable deposit carrying EMC libraries alongside normal tissue. In it, SEMA3C
reads **about 1.8× the normal-tissue median and 1.7× the other-sarcoma median across 4 EMC libraries**.
⛔ **Those ratios are ratios of the deposited, transformed scores, not fold-changes in transcript
abundance.** The series' own design description states that read counts were normalised to depth and sample
mean and then **square-root transformed** to reduce the influence of outliers, so the deposited values are
compressed relative to abundance and a ratio computed on them is a ratio on that compressed scale. **No
inverse transform is applied here and none should be inferred**: the deposit is used only for the narrow
statement that SEMA3C is higher in these EMC libraries than in the comparison groups on the deposited scale.
The normal comparison groups are 10 fetal and 17 adult libraries. One home:
[`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json`](../../modalities/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json) → `per_gene.values`;
reading: [`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md`](../fusion-output/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md).

**That is an orthogonal, partner-blind descriptive observation, not a replication of the partner switch:**

1. **GSE28866 carries no fusion-partner annotation**, so it cannot produce a partner contrast. Brenca's
   finding is a *difference between two partner groups*; a deposit with no partner calls can neither replicate
   nor refute it.
2. **Its four EMC libraries have no linked partner or outcome annotation**, and inferring that they carry the
   partner composition of some other series would be unwarranted.
3. **n = 4 EMC libraries, medians of per-peak medians of read density, transcript only, unmatched tissue
   groups.** No interval, no test, no protein, no localisation.
4. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "no expression deposit of any size can move the clinical claim."* That is too
   broad. An expression cohort with **linked clinical outcomes and partner annotation** could inform a
   clinical association; what this particular deposit cannot do is inform one, because it has neither.

⚠ **And Brenca 2019 is not an independent replication of the clinical correlation.** It shares its senior
investigators and consortium (Milan/Aviano) with both TKI reports. Shared investigators do not negate an
experiment, but they mean this is the same group explaining its own clinical observation, and it should be
distinguished from external replication.

### 3.7 · The one model pair, and what two patients can bound

Bangerter [12] established two patient-derived EMC models — USZ20-EMC1 (*EWSR1::NR4A3*) and USZ22-EMC2
(*TAF15::NR4A3*). ⚠ **They are two models from two different patients, not an isogenic or randomised partner
contrast.** The retained patient information distinguishes a long-treated recurrent EWSR1 tumour from a newly
diagnosed TAF15 tumour, and the models differ in other alterations as well as in partner. In that paper
"matched" describes each model's correspondence with its own native tumour, not matching of patients across
partners.

**The authors report partner-independent responses.** Verbatim: *"Both models independent of the NR4A3 fusion
partner showed high sensitivity to carfilzomib"*, and *"Similar drug responses were seen in both models,
independent of the fusion partner from NR4A3."*

⚠ **Read the screen's shape before its result.** The 40-drug panel was **not** run on the pair. Verbatim:
*"A medium throughput drug screen using 40 drugs was conducted with USZ20-EMC1 at passage 5."* Only three
agents — carfilzomib, doxorubicin and venetoclax — went forward to a **validation** step using both models,
and it is that step, not the screen, the partner-independence sentences describe. Those three are not one
pharmacological class, so similar responses to them bound the observation rather than demonstrating
partner-independent drug responsiveness in general.

The only partner-differential reported is a scoring-threshold one: the carfilzomib + venetoclax and
carfilzomib + doxorubicin combinations scored **synergistic** in the EWSR1 model and **additive** in the TAF15
model, with one model per arm and no statistical claim attached.

⚠ *Superseded, retained: "Neither model was tested against an antiangiogenic TKI."* The retained text does
not enumerate the 40-compound inventory, which it refers to a figure not held here, so that blanket statement
cannot be checked. What can be said is narrower and is what is said now: **the three agents validated in both
models are named, and none of them is an antiangiogenic TKI.** So this work cannot confirm or refute the
clinical correlation — it did not test that drug class in both models — and what it does is **bound** the
biomarker's scope: two models, three validated agents, no evidence that the partner predicts drug response in
general.

### 3.8 · A dated sequence of published hypotheses, and one attribution correction

In 2012, reporting two responding patients, the investigators wrote:

> **"Even in EMCS the fusion-protein is unlikely to be related to sunitinib sensitivity"**

⚠ **That sentence is from the 2012 two-case report (PMID 23058004), not the 2014 series (PMID 24703573) this
repository's own lane memo attached it to.** ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "Verified against the cached full text
of both."* The retained 2014 record is an **abstract**, not a full text. The attribution to the 2012 report
stands on the retained 2012 full text, in which the sentence occurs; all that can be said of the 2014 record
is that its abstract does not contain it and says instead that *"genotype/phenotype analyses support a
correlation between response and EWSR1-NR4A3 fusion"*. The correction is to an internal memo of this
repository, and it is stated as one.

**What the 2012 sentence is.** Written when the authors had two EWSR1-positive responders and no TAF15
patient at all, it is their **prior**, reasoning by analogy to alveolar soft part sarcoma — another
translocation sarcoma in which sunitinib activity is not linked to the fusion protein. It is dated context.

⛔ **It is not a statement of what these investigators now believe, and an earlier version of this paper
treated it as one.** ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "The investigators who reported the correlation do not believe
the fusion causes it."* The same group's Brenca 2019 discussion proposes that the fusion-dependent
axon-guidance changes **may affect receptor signalling and sunitinib response** — a later, explicitly
fusion-mediated hypothesis. The record is therefore a sequence: a 2012 prior that the fusion protein is
unlikely to be the direct mediator, and a 2019 hypothesis that the fusion acts on response through a
downstream pathway.

⚠ **And "downstream" is not the opposite of "fusion mechanism".** An effect acting through a pathway the
fusion drives is still a fusion-mediated effect; a drug need not bind the fusion protein for the fusion to
influence response. Three later observations belong beside the 2012 prior at their own scope, none of them
establishing the absence of a fusion-mediated mechanism: Huang 2023's reported multivariable analysis, in
which the partner term did not remain conventionally significant (§3.4); Paioli 2021, which reaches only
trends (§3.4); and Bangerter 2022's two models, in which three validated agents produced similar responses
(§3.7).

## 4 · Limitations

This section is written to survive an unsympathetic reader. Every item below is a reason to hold the
conclusion loosely, and several of them are reasons it could be wrong outright.

**4.1 · Every cohort is small, and that is structural rather than remediable.** EMC is ultra-rare. The largest
partner-stratified series here has 67 patients, the source-verified outcome series has 23 patients across its
two arms, and every TAF15 arm carrying an actual outcome is 2–7 patients. The intervals printed here are wide
because the evidence is thin, and they are printed at that width deliberately.

**4.1a · Nothing here is adjusted, and the record cannot separate the competing explanations.** The recorded
outcome proportions in §3.3 are unadjusted for size, stage, era, treatment or grade. Huang 2023's own
univariate list names five factors — *"Size >10 cm, moderate-to-severe nuclear pleomorphism, metastasis at
presentation, TAF15::NR4A3 fusion, and the administration of chemotherapy"* — of which this synthesis
stratifies by partner only the fusion. Nuclear pleomorphism is tested by partner nowhere here, although
Agaram 2014 is titled for that very association; chemotherapy allocation by partner is published by neither
outcome series. The partner may be standing in for grade, size or treatment, and this record cannot separate
them — which is a statement about what is unknown, not a finding that any of them explains the partner
(§3.4).

**4.2 · This is not a meta-analysis and must not be described as one.** What is done here is **crude
denominator-weighted pooling of counts from non-overlapping cohorts, with Wilson score intervals**. There is
no random-effects model, no between-study variance, no I², no funnel plot and no formal heterogeneity test.
The pooled figures are **weighted descriptive summaries**, not meta-analytic estimates, and they carry none of
the inferential guarantees a reader may associate with the word "pooled". Every Wilson bound is computed on
the **summed** counts under a common-binomial approximation that treats the contributing patients as one
sample, so between-cohort variation enters the point estimate through the denominator weights and enters the
interval not at all, and no prediction-interval reading is available. Where only one cohort contributes — the
entire response analysis, and now the outcome analysis as well — the figure is a **single-cohort Wilson
interval**, stated as such.

**4.3 · Partner assignment is not uniformly molecularly confirmed across reports.** The four prevalence series
did not use one assay, and two of them do not state, in any source reachable here, which assay assigned the
5′ partner in which case. Agaram 2014 typed by FISH with RT-PCR junction typing; Huang 2023 describes a
FISH-confirmed cohort; Lenz 2023 reports that molecular testing succeeded in 12 of 17 cases; Paioli 2021
reports only that the diagnoses were molecularly confirmed. A method that is not stated cannot be assumed to
be the same method, so a "TAF15" case in one series and a "TAF15" case in another are not guaranteed to have
been ascertained by the same criterion. Each series also carries a *NR4A3*-rearranged-but-partner-unassigned
residue, excluded from both numerator and denominator because the series do not describe those residues
comparably (§3.5). **That exclusion conditions the estimate on assignment, and no report supports any
assumption about which cases go unassigned or in which direction that biases the share** (§3.5).

**4.4 · The two response strata rest on secondary provenance.** Neither primary full text is open access. The
per-arm denominators for both the sunitinib series and the pazopanib trial were read from reviews and from an
independent group's reading of the series, not from the trial or series reports themselves; the primaries are
recorded as `primaryRef` and the strata carry `provenance: "secondary"`. Corroboration comes from three
sources including two independent groups (Ghent; University of Michigan), which is why the numbers are used
at all — but **the counts on which the response section rests have not been read in the documents that
generated them.** The same applies to the classification of the eight sunitinib clinical-benefit patients as
EWSR1 (§3.1): reported by a secondary source, not verified at patient level.

**4.5 · The relationship between the two response cohorts is argued, not audited.** The retained registry
record excludes patients who have received previous antiangiogenic agents, which runs against the sunitinib
series' patients having re-enrolled on the trial (§3.1). That is protocol evidence and not an enrolment
audit: registries publish eligibility criteria, not enrolment decisions, and protocol deviations are not
excluded. The primary reports remain unread here, so no patient-level check exists in either direction, and
the conditional mixture in §3.1 is reported as conditional for that reason.

**4.6 · The response comparator arms are labelled `non-TAF15`, and the label is doing specific work.** In the
trial, the comparator is the reported non-TAF15 remainder; treating it as an EWSR1 arm would attribute to
*EWSR1::NR4A3* a proportion that may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped cases, since no source gives the
trial's partner distribution. In the sunitinib series, a secondary source does report all eight
clinical-benefit patients as EWSR1 (§3.1), and the arm still carries the `non-TAF15` label — because that is
the label under which it is combined with the trial remainder in the conditional mixture, where both arms must
mean the same thing. **A comparison of a TAF15 arm against a verified EWSR1 arm at trial level does not exist
anywhere in this synthesis and is not claimed.** No count changes under either labelling.

**4.7 · Crude proportions, mixed follow-up, no censoring.** The outcome figures in §3.3 are during-follow-up
proportions in one series whose arms differ about two-fold in mean follow-up (43.3 vs 21.7 months). They are
not survival estimates, they are not adjusted, and they are not comparable to the DFS/DMFS figures in §3.4,
which are a different estimand. **No hazard ratio, survival curve or individual prognosis is derived anywhere
in this work, and none should be derived from it.**

**4.7a · One count set was withdrawn during final review, and its absence is the largest gap in this paper.**
Huang 2023's Table 1 supplied a second, larger set of per-partner outcome counts between 2026-08-08 and
2026-09-08. No retained original verifies them: what this repository holds for that paper is a Europe PMC
abstract record and a manifest recording HTTP 403 on the publisher's full-text and PDF routes, re-inspected
on 2026-09-08 and found to be anti-bot interstitials rather than the article. A human narrative of a PDF read
is not a retained original, and a manifest of a denied route is not the denied document. **The counts, their
denominators, the two hazard ratios, the mean-size comparison and the quoted Discussion sentence are
withdrawn from this synthesis** and are quarantined in the artifact so the removal is auditable (§8). The
abstract-supported partner counts and the reported model result remain, at abstract scope (§3.4, §3.5).
⚠ *This is scoped absence, not a finding that the published counts are wrong.*

**4.8 · The Fisher p-values are post-hoc.** They were not prespecified, are not performed in any source
report, are not corrected for the several endpoints reported here, and license nothing. A non-significant
value among them establishes no null and no equivalence.

**4.9 · Publication and referral bias run in a direction that favours the hypothesis.** A perfect response
split is more publishable than a mixed one, and a two-patient report of a striking response is what began this
literature. The cohort carrying the recorded outcome contrast is consultation material rather than a
population sample: Agaram 2014 is a tertiary sarcoma referral centre and is also the prevalence high outlier
(§3.5), so selection toward morphologically unusual — that is, TAF15-like — tumours plausibly inflates the arm
whose events carry that contrast. Neither bias can be corrected here.

**4.10 · The mechanism is not independent of the clinical observation.** Brenca 2019 shares its consortium
with both TKI reports (§3.6). A mechanism proposed by the same investigators who reported the correlation is
context, not corroboration.

**4.11 · Data vintage is unrecorded for the outcome series.** Agaram 2014 publishes a follow-up range (2–99
months) and no accrual window. Per the evidence contract, vintage is marked **absent** rather than inferred
from a publication year. What the sources do state are two *treatment* windows, neither of them a diagnosis
window: Paioli 2021, which enters the prevalence pool and not the outcome section, was surgically treated
1989–2016, and the pazopanib trial enrolled 24 Jun 2014 – 17 Jan 2017. The synthesis therefore spans an era in
which EMC systemic options changed materially, and no number here has been adjusted to compensate.

**4.12 · No treatment recommendation is made, and none follows.** ⛔ Nothing in this document recommends,
supports or opposes giving or withholding sunitinib, pazopanib or any other agent to any patient or patient
group. It asserts no efficacy, no safety, no therapeutic window and no clinical readiness, and it is not a
basis for a clinical decision.

## 5 · What this synthesis claims, and what it does not

**Claims.**

- That the reports bearing on the *NR4A3* 5′ partner have been given a **transparent numerical accounting**
  under a single stated method: every stratum's integer events, its denominator, the population that
  denominator describes, the source sentence it was read from, and what the source does not say. ⚠ *That is a
  narrower claim than an earlier version made, and the change is deliberate.* The four lines of evidence had
  been combined before: the 2020 disease review [4] already brings mechanism, morphology, targets and clinical
  partner response together, and the 2025 review [15] connects morphology, partner and therapy again. Agaram's
  own abstract already gave 1/16 against 3/7 died-of-disease. The selection here is non-systematic (§2.3a), so
  nothing in this paper is a census of the field.
- That the recorded objective-response evidence is **0 of 3 TAF15::NR4A3 patients in the trial, and 0 of 5
  under a conditional mixture**, with differences of recorded proportions of 21.1 and 37.0 percentage points
  whose uncertainty is wide enough that the comparator's own point estimate lies inside the TAF15 arm's
  interval (95 % upper bound 43.4–56.1 %, itself conditional on the evaluability assumption of §3.1).
- That a **partner comparison among treated patients cannot establish a predictive treatment interaction**,
  at any denominator, because it has no untreated comparator.
- That **one cohort's per-partner outcome event counts are source-verified**: Agaram 2014 records
  disease-specific death 3/7 with TAF15 against 1/16 with EWSR1, with one further EWSR1 patient dead of
  unknown cause; local recurrence 2/7 against 1/16; distant recurrence 2/7 against 6/16, over arms whose mean
  observation windows differ about two-fold. These are recorded counts in one referral series, stated with
  their limits and never as a prognosis.
- That the previously reported **two-cohort pooled prognostic magnitude is withdrawn**, because the Huang 2023
  Table 1 cells it rested on are not verified by any retained original (§4.7a, §8). This is scoped absence and
  not a finding that the published counts are wrong.
- That in the analyses Huang 2023 and Paioli 2021 report, **an independent prognostic association of the
  partner was not established** — which is not the same as showing that none exists, and does not establish
  that tumour size explains a partner association (§3.4).
- That *TAF15::NR4A3* is recorded in **18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned EMC across four
  series whose partner counts are source-verified — a share among assigned patients in selected series, not an
  estimate for all EMC (§3.5).
- That **this repository's own lane memo** misattributed the 2012 hedge to the 2014 report, and that the
  published record on mechanism is a **dated sequence of hypotheses** rather than a statement of current
  investigator belief (§3.8).
- A concrete, zero-patient-cost ask of the field, stated in §6.

**Does not claim.**

- **Not** that *TAF15::NR4A3* predicts non-response to antiangiogenic TKIs. The recorded proportions are
  imprecise, and a treated-only comparison cannot establish prediction.
- **Not** that EWSR1::NR4A3 patients should receive, or TAF15::NR4A3 patients should be denied, sunitinib,
  pazopanib or any other agent. No treatment recommendation follows.
- **Not** that the fusion partner is mechanistically responsible for the response difference — and equally
  **not** that it is excluded as a mechanism (§3.8).
- **Not** that *TAF15::NR4A3* is an independent prognostic factor, and **not** that it is not one. The
  reported models do not establish either, and the recorded proportions in §3.3 are crude counts in one
  series, not a prognosis.
- **Not** that recurrence or metastasis is equivalent between partners. Non-significant descriptive contrasts
  at these denominators leave substantial differences in either direction open.
- **Not** that tumour size confounds, absorbs or explains a partner association (§3.4).
- **Not** a refutation of the 2020 review's cautious description of a metastasis-free-survival trend. That is
  a different endpoint, and a non-significant crude tally from different cohorts cannot refute it. ⚠ *A
  specifically attributed criticism of the stronger phrase "insensitivity to antiangiogenic treatment", which
  Jacobs 2021 [5] does use, is defensible and is made in §3.1; not every review makes the same claim.*
- **Not** a survival model, a hazard ratio, or any individual prognosis.
- **Not** a meta-analysis with heterogeneity modelling (§4.2).

**Endpoint for [`systems/views/L3-publications.md`](../../../systems/views/L3-publications.md):**
*The NR4A3 5′ fusion partner is a candidate — not established — stratification variable in EMC. One
source-verified series records more disease-specific deaths in its TAF15 arm; the reported adjusted analyses
do not establish an independent partner effect in either direction; and the entire examined
antiangiogenic-response evidence base is three to five TAF15 patients with no recorded responses, which is a
difference of proportions too imprecise to act on. The field should start reporting the partner alongside
response and outcome rather than acting on it.*

## 6 · What would reduce the uncertainty, and what the field could do about it

⚠ **This section replaces a "what would kill this" list, and the replacement is a correction rather than a
restyling.** The old list mis-stated how a new patient enters an estimate, printed one arithmetic convention
as though it were a biological robustness threshold, asserted that a single further cohort could not overturn
the result, and rested several entries on quantities now withdrawn. What follows states what is actually
uncertain.

⚠ **How a new patient enters.** A newly published **unique** responding TAF15 patient adds to the numerator
**and** the denominator: 0/3 becomes 1/4 (25.0 %) and 0/5 becomes 1/6 (16.7 %). ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "a
single response moves the point estimate to 20–33 %."* Those figures described changing an **existing**
patient's recorded outcome, which is a different operation. Neither operation makes a descriptive difference
disappear by itself.

⛔ **Zero-death reconciliation thresholds are arithmetic conventions, not biological robustness.**
⚠ *Superseded, retained: "Reconciling the two would take 19 further TAF15 patients with zero deaths — a total
TAF15 denominator of 34", and "one such cohort would not overturn it."* On the same counts, comparing a point
estimate against a comparator's marginal Wilson upper bound, against its point estimate, or asking when
marginal intervals first overlap give three different answers; they are three criteria, not three
measurements. A real further cohort would also contribute comparator patients and its own follow-up and
sampling design rather than leaving one arm fixed. The unqualified claim that one cohort could not overturn
the result is **withdrawn**, and no such threshold is computed anywhere in this work.

**Observations that would reduce the actual uncertainty, in no ranked order:**

1. The pazopanib trial's **full partner distribution** and its partner-by-analysis-population flow across the
   26 treated, 23 modified-ITT and 22 evaluable patients, including which population the four non-evaluable
   patients left and why.
2. **Huang 2023's Table 1 original**, which would restore or refute a second source-verified set of
   per-partner outcome counts together with their follow-up categories, endpoint definitions and
   cause-of-death handling.
3. **Per-partner relapse and metastasis event counts** behind Paioli 2021's DFS/DMFS p-values, and the
   adjustment structure of the analysis that produced them.
4. Any partner-stratified report publishing **per-arm observation windows and event ordering**, which is what
   would let a censored or competing-risk reading replace crude during-follow-up proportions.
5. Any adjusted partner analysis reporting the **partner coefficient, its uncertainty, the event count and
   the model specification**. Adjusted significance or non-significance without those does not settle whether
   an independent association exists in either direction.
6. **Any published TAF15::NR4A3 response or non-response on an antiangiogenic TKI, reported with its
   partner.** This is the observation the field is asked for below, and the one that most directly narrows
   the response proportions.

**The ask.** Report the *NR4A3* partner alongside response **and** outcome in every EMC systemic-therapy and
outcome report, and publish the partner-stratified counts from the trials and series that already hold them.
**Three documents remain unread here and would materially extend the poolable evidence at zero new patient
cost:**

| document | what it holds | why it is not readable here |
|---|---|---|
| Stacchiotti 2019, *Lancet Oncol* (PMID 31331701) | the pazopanib trial's full partner distribution and prior-therapy table | closed access: `oa_status: closed`, zero open locations recorded, and of the listed institutional handles four are metadata-only while the fifth is unread behind an anti-bot challenge rather than measured empty |
| Paioli 2021, *Ann Surg Oncol* (PMID 32572850) | per-partner relapse and metastasis counts behind the DFS/DMFS p-values, n = 67 | closed access: the institutional repository records state that no file is attached |
| Huang 2023, *Mod Pathol* (PMID 36948401), Table 1 and full text | the per-partner outcome counts, follow-up categories and endpoint definitions withdrawn from this synthesis in §4.7a | designated free by the publisher (`oa_status: bronze`) but returning HTTP 403 to every automated fetch behind an anti-bot challenge. ⚠ A paywall needs an author or an institution; a bot block needs a reader with a browser and a retained original |

✅ **`NCT02066285`'s eligibility text is readable and has been applied** (§3.1, §4.5): ClinicalTrials.gov v2,
Exclusion Criteria, verbatim *"Patients who have received previous antiangiogenic agents"*. It is the
protocol's rule and not a patient-level audit, so it bears on the overlap question without settling it.

## 7 · Data, code and reproducibility

**All data underlying this synthesis are published data.** ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "no individual patient
record was accessed" and "no individual patient data exist for any cohort."* Both were wrong as written: this
synthesis reads **published per-patient case tables** where sources provide them (§3.3), which is how the
Sjögren counts and several partner assignments were established. The accurate statement is: **only published
records were used; no new or non-public patient data were obtained, held or generated; and harmonised
individual-level time-to-event data were unavailable for every cohort here.** Every count comes from a
peer-reviewed report or a congress abstract, and each is traceable to a resolvable identifier (PMID, PMCID,
DOI or NCT).

| item | path | role |
|---|---|---|
| pooled artifact | [`emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json`](./emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json) | **the one home of every number in this paper** — cohorts, strata, citations, analyses, provenance |
| correction register | [`emc-fusion-partner-correction-register.md`](./emc-fusion-partner-correction-register.md) | every superseded statement this repository has made about partner stratification, and what replaced it. It corrects no PUBLISHED record — nothing here has appeared under an external identifier — and it is held outside this paper so the paper does not depend on it to be read correctly. |

**Reproduction.** One command, no dependencies beyond the Python standard library, no network:

```
python3 research/manuscripts/emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py
```

**Verified this session:** re-running the generator on this checkout reproduces the committed artifact
**byte-for-byte apart from its `_generated_utc` stamp**. Changing a count means changing it in the generator's
`COHORTS` table and regenerating — the artifact carries `_do_not_hand_edit` for that reason, and a figure typed
into this document that disagrees with it is the defect.

**The artifact has a verify mode**, which re-derives the document **in memory**, compares it against the
committed file, writes nothing and exits non-zero on any difference:

```
python3 research/manuscripts/emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py --check
```

It runs in continuous integration on every push, and
`research/manuscripts/tests/test_emc_fusion_partner_pooling_check.py` perturbs the real committed artifact on
disk — a pooled event count, a Wilson bound, a per-cohort stratum — and asserts that `--check` refuses each
**and writes nothing**. That last assertion is the point: a check that regenerates its own reference would
return 0 precisely because it had just made the file match.

**Licensing.** Each citation entry records the source's licence and it is respected; the raw-text
`literature-cache` branch is never republished.

## 8 · Provenance and retrieval

The development sandbox's egress proxy refuses CONNECT to Europe PMC, NCBI and ClinicalTrials.gov, so every
source used here was read from this repository's own `literature-cache` branch, populated by GitHub Actions
runs of [`.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`](../../../.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml). Cache
slugs used, the verbatim sentence each count was read from, and the documents that are **not** readable are
recorded in the artifact's `retrieval_provenance` block and in each citation's `verification_note`.

⛔ **One count set was withdrawn on 2026-09-08 because no retained original verifies it.** Huang 2023's
Table 1 counts entered this synthesis on 2026-08-08 as a human's reading of the published PDF. What this
repository actually holds for PMID 36948401 is a Europe PMC core record carrying the abstract, and a manifest
recording **HTTP 403** on the publisher's full-text and PDF routes. Those route files were re-inspected on
2026-09-08 and contain anti-bot interstitial pages, not the article. **A manifest of a denied route is not the
denied document, and a narrative of a PDF read is not a retained original.** The affected quantities — the
per-partner outcome cells, the 53-followed and 42/8 denominators, the two hazard ratios, the mean-size
comparison, the printed EWSR1 size cell and the quoted Discussion sentence — are removed from the active
synthesis and quarantined in the artifact under `cohorts[huang-2023-outcome].withdrawn_2026_09_08`, so the
removal is auditable and nothing recomputes from them. **What remains from that paper is what its abstract
states**: the 58-case series, the 46/9/2/1 partner counts, the 78 % size association with P = .025, the five
named univariate factors, and that only size > 10 cm (P = .004) and metastasis at presentation (P = .032)
remained prognostically independent. ⚠ *This is scoped absence, not a claim that the published counts are
false. It reverses if an authentic original is supplied.*

✅ **One source hold was closed on 2026-09-08 from bytes this repository already held.** The Lenz 2023 primary
abstract (PMID 36563884) was located in the `literature-cache` branch, in the retained Europe PMC search
record `literature/emc-partner-events/epmc_search_taf15_fulltext.txt`. No retrieval was performed. It states
verbatim that *"Molecular testing was successfully performed in 12/17 cases"* and that *"Molecular assays
revealed 8 EWSR1::NR4A3 positive tumors (67%), 2 TAF15::NR4A3 positive tumors (17%), 1 TCF12::NR4A3 positive
tumor (8%) and 1 NR4A3 positive tumor (8%) in which no other gene alteration was identified."* Those are the
integers §3.5 pools, and they also correct that series' ascertainment flow: 17 in the series, 12
characterised, 11 with a named partner, and **five cases with no successful molecular result** that an
earlier version of this paper omitted. This is separate from, and additional to, the bibliographic repair of
that citation.

**Three facts a reader should be able to check without trusting this document:**

- **The artifact does not carry a machine-checkable per-count fetch record, and this paper says so rather than
  implying otherwise.** Counted against the committed artifact: of its citation records, most carry a
  `verification_note` as prose, and four of those name a cache slug and an HTTP status inside that prose. No
  record carries `cache_path` or `http_status` as a field. What exists artifact-wide is
  `retrieval_provenance.cache_slugs_used`, a flat list of the cache slugs this synthesis read from, which does
  not bind a slug to a count. Every count named here is corroborated by the verbatim quotations printed in
  §3, but building a per-count fetch record is separate work that is not done here.
- **The documents that would resolve the remaining open questions are named, with the specific reason each is
  unreadable here** (§6). An absent reading is recorded as an absent reading, never as a reading of absence,
  and never as a claim that a source does not exist.
- **Items that moved between "unreachable" and "resolved" are kept in both states.** The artifact's
  `resolved_2026_08_08` block records what was retrieved and what it turned out to be; the withdrawal above
  records what was removed and why. A retrieval failure that is later fixed, and an input that is later
  withdrawn, are both facts about the same source and neither is silently deleted.
