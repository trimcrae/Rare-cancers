## Abstract

**Background.** Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is defined by an *NR4A3* rearrangement whose 5′
partner is *EWSR1* in most cases and *TAF15* in a minority. Four separate literatures — objective response to
antiangiogenic tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (TKIs), outcome by partner, partner prevalence, and a transcriptional
mechanism — each report a difference between the two variants. None of the four had been combined with any
other, and no source pools the response data at all.

**Methods.** The EMC reports giving an outcome stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner that this repository's
citation set contains — no database was queried and no screening flow was run (§2.3a) — were pooled under a single pre-existing, binding evidence contract: crude denominator-weighted proportions with
Wilson score 95 % intervals, explicit integer `{events, denom}` required, non-overlapping populations only, and
the smaller cohort held out wherever overlap could not be excluded. Heterogeneity is reported as the range of
per-cohort rates. A random-effects model was deliberately **not** fitted. Fisher exact p-values are reported as
post-hoc descriptive statistics and license nothing.

**Results.** **Three** reports give objective response by partner and **one enters the primary pool**: the
pazopanib phase 2 trial NCT02066285 (PMID 31331701). The sunitinib named-use series (PMID 24703573) cannot be
shown distinct from that trial, so it is held out of the primary analysis for population overlap and enters
only the secondary analysis that assumes independence; the two-case index report (PMID 23058004) is contained
in that series and is excluded from both — neither for lacking partner data (§3.2). **No TAF15::NR4A3 patient
was found to have been reported responding to an antiangiogenic TKI in the sources this synthesis examined**
(§2.3a — no systematic search was run, so this is not a claim that no such report exists) — 0 of 3 in the
primary analysis, 0 of 5 if the two reports are independent. The 95 % Wilson upper bound on the TAF15 response rate is **43.4 % to
56.1 %**, which sits **above** the comparator arm's point estimate in both analyses (37.0 % and 21.1 %).
Post-hoc Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.155 pooled, 1.0 for the trial alone. **On prognosis the picture is
different, because two cohorts now publish outcome event counts by partner** (Agaram 2014, PMID 24746215; and
Huang 2023, PMID 36948401, whose Table 1 was read from the published PDF on 2026-08-08). Pooled over 73
patients, disease-specific death is **7/15 = 46.7 % (95 % CI 24.8–69.9)** with TAF15::NR4A3 against
**6/58 = 10.3 % (95 % CI 4.8–20.8)** with EWSR1::NR4A3 — a 36.3-point gap, post-hoc Fisher p = 0.0034, and the
first magnitude this contrast has ever had. ⛔ **It arrives with its own defeater, from the larger cohort's own
authors:** adjusted for tumour size and metastasis at presentation, TAF15::NR4A3 is **not** an independent
predictor of disease-specific survival, and 78 % of TAF15 tumours were > 10 cm. Local recurrence
(26.7 % vs 22.4 %) and distant metastasis (33.3 % vs 24.1 %) show no material separation. Pooling four
independent molecular series gives TAF15::NR4A3 in **28/154 = 18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned
EMC — **below** the ≈27 % the field routinely quotes from a single referral-centre cohort.

**Conclusions.** The two halves of the question are now in different states, and conflating them is the error
this paper most wants to prevent. **Prognosis** has a crude magnitude and a published defeater in the same
table: the partner tracks disease-specific death and is not independent of tumour size. **Treatment response**
has neither, and cannot: the entire TAF15::NR4A3 antiangiogenic experience is three to five patients with zero
reported responses, and a zero-event arm yields no magnitude at any denominator. The *NR4A3* 5′ partner is a
**candidate — not established — stratification variable**, and the data cannot exclude a TAF15 response rate
equal to the EWSR1 one. The concrete, zero-patient-cost ask is that the partner be reported alongside response
in every EMC systemic-therapy report, and that the two remaining unreachable tables be published in
partner-stratified form.

## 1 · Introduction

### 1.1 · The disease, the drug class and the variable

EMC is an ultra-rare soft-tissue sarcoma, and it is
molecularly defined: *"EMCs are translocation sarcomas and harbor in > 90% of the cases an NR4A3
rearrangement"* [12]. The 5′ partner is *EWSR1* in the large majority, *TAF15* in a minority, and *TCF12*,
*TFG* or an unidentified partner in a small residue [7,8,9,10]. The clinical shape of the disease is the
reason a stratification variable would matter: *"Although often associated with prolonged survival, EMC have
high rates of distant recurrences and disease-associated death"*, and *"Anthracycline-based chemotherapy,
which is the first-line regimen used in STS, has low activity in this sarcoma subtype"* [12,4].

The one systemic class with reproducible activity in EMC is the antiangiogenic TKIs. Sunitinib produced
responses in a named-use series [1] after an index two-patient report [2], and pazopanib was taken into a
dedicated single-arm phase 2 trial, NCT02066285, which reported 4 objective responses among 22 evaluable
patients [3]. Both reports remark, in passing and without pooling, that the responders carried EWSR1::NR4A3 and
that the TAF15::NR4A3 patients did not respond.

### 1.2 · Four literatures that had never been combined

The claim "the fusion partner matters" is currently supported in four places that do not talk to each other:

| line of evidence | what it reports | what it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| **TKI response** | responders are EWSR1, non-responders include every TAF15 patient [1,3,4,5,6] | never states a denominator in the primary text; never pooled |
| **Outcome by partner** | TAF15 associated with worse disease-specific survival [7,8,9] | **four** series test the partner against outcome and only **two** publish per-partner event counts; of the other two, one publishes p-values only and one is an unresolved-overlap MSK series reporting no OS association by subtype (§3.2). Neither event-count cohort is adjusted; **the one series that ran a multivariable model lost the partner to tumour size, and a second reaches no significance on the partner in an analysis where size does** |
| **Partner prevalence** | TAF15 in roughly one EMC in five [7,8,9,10] | never pooled across series; the field quotes one cohort |
| **Mechanism** | an axon-guidance switch separates the two variants [11] | is not a clinical endpoint, and shares a consortium with the trials |

Each is repeated in review after review, usually flatly and usually without a denominator. The result is a
belief that is stronger in the field's prose than in the field's data, and it has never been possible to say by
how much, because nobody had put the counts in one place.

### 1.3 · What this synthesis adds

Pooling changes the conclusion's **strength**, not its **direction**. It also surfaces five things that no
individual source says out loud: a response contrast whose confidence interval overlaps its own comparator; the
first pooled magnitude for the prognostic contrast, immediately beside the multivariable analysis that defeats
it; a metastasis claim that **neither** cohort with counts establishes, in **either** direction; a prevalence
figure materially below the one the field repeats; and an attribution correction to the field's most-quoted
caveat (§3.8).

⚠ **And it makes explicit a separation the literature blurs.** This paper asks two questions, and as of
2026-08-08 they are in different states:

| question | what the record now supports | what is missing |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) PROGNOSIS by partner** | a **crude magnitude** across two cohorts and 73 patients (§3.3) | any evidence that the effect is independent of tumour size — the one series that tested it says it is not (§3.4) |
| **(b) TREATMENT RESPONSE by partner** | a **direction only**: 0 of 3–5 TAF15 patients (§3.1) | a non-zero denominator of events. **A zero-event arm yields no magnitude at any denominator**, so no amount of re-reading the existing reports closes this |

**(a) moving does not move (b).** Huang 2023 is a pathology series with a survival analysis; it reports no
antiangiogenic therapy at all, and the only systemic treatment it records is chemotherapy, as a prognostic
covariate rather than a response endpoint. §3.1 is unchanged word for word by the 2026-08-08 integration.

## 2 · Methods

### 2.1 · The binding evidence contract

The statistical method here was **not selected for this analysis**. It is the repository's standing evidence
contract, [`systems/POLICY-evidence.md`](../../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md), which predates this work and is
enforced in continuous integration by `scripts/validate-registry.mjs`. Adopting a pre-committed method removes
the degree of freedom that matters most in a synthesis this small: the choice of estimator after seeing the
counts.

Sections that bind here:

- **§1.3 — primary versus secondary provenance.** `sourceId` is the document a number was actually **read in**.
  A number read out of a review carries `provenance: "secondary"` and a `primaryRef` naming the original study.
  Identifiers are never invented for a primary source that was not itself fetched.
- **§2.1 — what may be pooled.** Molecularly typed EMC; **explicit integer** `{events, denom}` (never counts
  back-derived from a published percentage); an outcome that is not the inclusion criterion; a non-overlapping
  population.
- **§2.2 — the estimator.** Crude denominator-weighted proportion p̂ = Σevents / Σdenom, with a **Wilson score**
  95 % interval on the summed counts. Heterogeneity is reported as the **range of per-cohort rates**.
- **§2.3 — double-counting.** Mutually exclusive strata within a study; where two populations may overlap, the
  **smaller** cohort is held out with `contextReason: "population-overlap"`.
- **§2.4 — estimand discipline.** Event-rate metrics are pooled as **crude during-follow-up proportions**,
  explicitly labelled as mixed-follow-up and uncensored. Time-anchored survival figures are never merged.

### 2.2 · Estimator and interval, and why Wilson

Every stratum in this analysis is small and several sit **exactly at zero events**. The normal-approximation
interval collapses to a degenerate point at 0/n and would misrepresent the evidence in the single place where
the evidence is most easily overstated. The Wilson score interval is well behaved at small n and at the
boundaries, and §2.2 of the contract names it by construction. Arithmetic:
[`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](../emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py) → `wilson()`.

### 2.3 · Inclusion, exclusion, and the overlap rule

Reports were assessed for whether they give an outcome **stratified by *NR4A3* 5′ partner** with integer counts
on both sides of the stratification. Cohorts that report a partner-stratified result without counts are carried
as **context** — visible with their own numbers and citation, excluded from any pooled figure — exactly as the
contract requires. The full inclusion table is §3.2.

The consequential exclusion is the sunitinib series. It cannot be shown non-overlapping with the pazopanib
trial, so §2.3 holds the smaller cohort out of the headline. Because that single decision moves the
denominator, **both analyses are reported**: the POLICY-conformant primary (trial alone) and a maximal-
information secondary (both cohorts, valid only if no patient appears in both), with the bounds that follow.

⛔ **2.3a · No systematic search strategy was run, and the two claims in this paper that depend on
exhaustiveness are bounded by that fact.** The candidate literature was assembled from this repository's
existing EMC citation set — the review articles already cited here [4,5,15], the reports they cite, and the
reports that cite them — read against the `literature-cache` branch populated by
[`.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`](../../../.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml) (§2.6). **No
database (PubMed, Europe PMC, Embase) was queried with a registered search string across a defined date range,
no PRISMA-style screening flow was run, and no second reader checked the inclusion decisions.** Two sentences
in this document are stated more absolutely than that process can support and are qualified here rather than
softened where they are quoted, because a reader who meets only §3.1 or the Abstract should not have to find
this paragraph to know the limit: **"no source pools the response data at all"** (Abstract, §1.2) is a
statement about the reviews and series already in this repository's citation set, not a claim that no such
pooling exists anywhere in the unindexed literature; and **"no TAF15::NR4A3 patient has ever been reported to
respond"** — which the Abstract and §3.1 now state in that bounded form directly rather than relying on a
reader reaching this paragraph — means *no such report was found in the sources this synthesis examined*,
which is exactly why §6's falsifier #1 treats a single counter-report as sufficient to overturn it — the paper
already behaves as if this could be wrong, and this paragraph is why. Caught by a blind adversarial seat,
2026-08-26.

### 2.4 · Significance testing, declared as post-hoc

Fisher exact two-sided p-values are reported for each contrast. They are **post-hoc and descriptive only**:
not prespecified, not performed in any source report, not corrected for the several endpoints on this page,
and **not used to license any claim in this document**. They are printed because a reader will compute them
otherwise, and printing them with their status attached is more honest than withholding them.

### 2.5 · Methods deliberately not used, and why

- **Random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) pooling.** This repository owns a random-effects pooler
  ([`research/meta/meta-analysis.mjs`](../../meta/meta-analysis.mjs)) and it is the wrong instrument here. Across
  the TKI-response and outcome-by-partner strata (§3.1, §3.3) the per-stratum denominator runs from three
  (TAF15, primary TKI analysis) to fifty-eight (EWSR1, pooled outcome) and the event count from zero (TAF15
  response, either analysis) to fourteen (EWSR1 distant metastasis); a between-study variance is not estimable
  at that scale, a τ² from these counts would be an artefact, and quoting a random-effects interval would
  manufacture precision the data cannot support. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "two to nineteen patients and zero to
  ten events per stratum." That range described the TKI-response strata alone, before Huang 2023's counts
  (§3.3) were pooled on 2026-08-08 and widened the outcome-by-partner denominators past nineteen. Caught by a
  blind adversarial seat, 2026-08-26.*
- **I².** Not computed, for the same reason. The honest heterogeneity signal at this scale is the **range of
  per-cohort rates**, printed beside every pooled figure.
- **Hazard ratios, survival models, Kaplan–Meier.** No individual patient data exist for any cohort here, and
  the two cohorts that ran time-to-event analyses publish p-values without event counts. No time-to-event
  quantity is estimated anywhere in this work.
- **Back-derived counts.** Where a source gives only a percentage, no `{events, denom}` is inferred. Rounding
  invents data, and §2.1(2) forbids it.

### 2.6 · Retrieval

The development environment's egress proxy refuses CONNECT to Europe PMC, NCBI and ClinicalTrials.gov
(verified this session: curl exit 56, HTTP 000). Every source was therefore read from the repository's own
`literature-cache` branch, populated by GitHub Actions runs of `.github/workflows/fetch-literature.yml`. Cache
slugs used, the verbatim sentence each count was read from, and the documents that are **not** retrievable are
recorded in the artifact's `retrieval_provenance` block and in each citation's `verification_note`. Full
accounting: §8.

## 3 · Results

### 3.1 · Objective response to an antiangiogenic TKI, by partner

Across every published EMC report **this synthesis examined** that gives an objective-response outcome by
*NR4A3* partner — **three**, of which **one enters the primary pool**, one is held out of it for population
overlap and contributes to the secondary analysis only, and one is contained in that held-out series and
excluded from both (§3.2) — **no TAF15::NR4A3 patient is reported to respond to an antiangiogenic TKI in any
of them.** ⚠ *No systematic search was run (§2.3a), so "every published report" here means every report in
this repository's citation set, not every report that exists.*

| analysis | TAF15 arm | comparator arm | TAF15 95 % upper bound | Fisher (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (pazopanib trial alone, POLICY-conformant) | 0/3 | 4/19 = 21.1 % | **56.1 %** | 1.0 |
| **Secondary** (both cohorts, assumes independence) | 0/5 | 10/27 = 37.0 % | **43.4 %** | 0.155 |

**The honest statement is a direction, not a difference.** The direction is consistent across two cohorts, two
drugs and five years of accrual (July 2011 – January 2017). The magnitude is not established: in **both**
analyses the TAF15 arm's 95 % upper bound lies **above the comparator arm's own point estimate**, so the data
cannot exclude a TAF15 response rate equal to the EWSR1 one. Anyone who has written "TAF15 tumours do not
respond to antiangiogenics" has written a claim the evidence does not carry — and that includes reviews that
state it flatly.

Two further features of the primary analysis matter and are easy to lose:

- **The comparator arm is `non-TAF15`, not verified EWSR1.** All four pazopanib responders are reported as
  EWSR1::NR4A3, but no accessible source gives the trial's full partner distribution, so the 19 comparator
  patients may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped *NR4A3*-rearranged cases. Labelling that arm "EWSR1" would be
  a fabrication of the trial's own composition.
- **A sensitivity analysis on the one unevaluable patient does not rescue anything.** If the patient who died
  before the primary analysis carried TAF15, the strata become 0/2 and 4/20; the TAF15 upper bound rises to
  65.8 % and the contrast weakens. No claim here depends on which way that resolves
  (`analyses.A_tki_objective_response.sensitivity_analyses`).

**The overlap that holds back the headline.** The sunitinib series and the pazopanib trial cannot be shown
non-overlapping, and the risk is specific rather than theoretical. Istituto Nazionale Tumori Milan ran the
series and was a trial site under the same senior investigator; the trial's entry criterion —
*NR4A3*-translocated advanced EMC with RECIST progression in the previous six months — is satisfied **by
definition** by the series' two progressors, who are exactly its two TAF15 patients; and neither report states
whether prior antiangiogenic therapy was permitted. So the smaller cohort is held out of the headline, and both
analyses are reported. **Worst case, the world's entire TAF15 antiangiogenic experience is three patients.**

### 3.3 · Outcome by partner — a magnitude, and the defeater it arrives with

**Two** cohorts publish EMC outcome event counts by *NR4A3* partner. Agaram 2014 (MSKCC, 26 consecutive cases,
23 partner-assigned with follow-up) was the only one until 2026-08-08, when Huang 2023's Table 1 was read from
the published PDF and added 50 partner-assigned patients with follow-up from 15 Taiwanese institutions
(§8). The two share no authors, no institutions and no continent, so §2.3 permits the pool.

| endpoint | TAF15::NR4A3 | EWSR1::NR4A3 | gap | Fisher (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **disease-specific death** | **7/15 = 46.7 %** (95 % CI 24.8–69.9) | **6/58 = 10.3 %** (95 % CI 4.8–20.8) | **36.3 pts** | **0.0034** |
| local recurrence | 4/15 = 26.7 % (10.9–52.0) | 13/58 = 22.4 % (13.6–34.7) | 4.3 pts | 0.7389 |
| distant metastasis after presentation | 5/15 = 33.3 % (15.2–58.3) | 14/58 = 24.1 % (15.0–36.5) | 9.2 pts | 0.5163 |

Per cohort, on death, both point the same way: 3/7 = 42.9 % vs 1/16 = 6.2 % (Agaram) and 4/8 = 50.0 % vs
5/42 = 11.9 % (Huang). **This is the first magnitude the prognostic contrast has ever had** — before Huang's
table was read, the "pool" was a single-cohort Wilson interval and this document said so.

⛔ **AND IT MUST NEVER BE QUOTED WITHOUT THE ANALYSIS THAT DEFEATS IT, WHICH IS IN THE SAME PAPER.** These are
**crude, unadjusted, during-follow-up proportions**. Huang 2023's own multivariable model for disease-specific
survival retains only **size > 10 cm (P = .004, HR 30.60)** and **metastasis at presentation (P = .032,
HR 8.14)**; **TAF15::NR4A3 loses significance under adjustment**. The same table shows the mechanism: **78 % of
TAF15 tumours were > 10 cm** against 12/46 of EWSR1 ones (P = .025), and mean size was 13.7 ± 6.2 cm vs
7.3 ± 4.7 cm (P = .024). In the authors' own words, the TAF15 survival difference *"might be partly
attributable to the predominance of large tumors > 10 cm in TAF15-rearranged EMCs."* So the honest reading is:
**the crude partner effect on death is real and now measured; the independent partner effect is not
established, and the one series positioned to test it says the partner is standing in for size.**

⚠ **The metastasis reversal does not survive the second cohort — and what replaces it is a null, not the
reviews' claim.** *Superseded, retained: "the metastasis signal runs backwards in the one cohort that publishes
counts … the opposite direction to the claim repeated across the review literature."* Agaram alone has distant
recurrence 6/16 = 37.5 % in EWSR1 against 2/7 = 28.6 % in TAF15 — EWSR1 higher, the reversal. **Huang runs the
other way** (8/42 = 19.0 % EWSR1 vs 3/8 = 37.5 % TAF15 for metastasis developing after presentation), i.e. in
the reviews' own direction. Pooled, the gap is 9.2 points with overlapping intervals and post-hoc p = 0.5163.
⭐ **What survives is the negative, and it is stronger than the reversal was:** the largest series to test
metastasis by partner *directly* reports **P = .728** on its own three-way table, so the metastasis claim attributed to a
single review (PMC7563993 alone — the only actual review among the four PMCIDs previously cited for it; the
other three were **misattributed rather than fabricated** and have been removed, Appendix A11) **is not established by either cohort, in either
direction**. ⚠ *That review's verbatim sentence is not held in this repository, so the attribution is by
record and not by quotation — the standard §8 asserts for every count is not met for this one claim, and it
rests on n = 1 rather than on a literature.* A single-cohort reversal quoted as a finding was always one cohort away from being overturned; a
two-cohort null does not depend on which cohort you happen to hold. Paioli 2021, the only cohort treating
metastasis as a **time-to-event** endpoint, agrees that nothing is established: DMFS p = 0.09.

⚠ **Two estimand caveats, both of which bound the table above.** First, the metastasis row is matched on
**labels, not on a published definition**: Agaram reports "distant recurrence", Huang partitions metastasis
into present-at-presentation and developed-later, and neither states whether a patient metastatic at
presentation sat inside its recurrence denominator. This document pools Agaram's recurrence with Huang's
*after-presentation* cell and reports Huang's *any-metastasis* figure (4/8 vs 16/42) within-cohort only, so the
reading can be checked at either. Death and local recurrence carry no such ambiguity — both cohorts report them
as cells of a complete partition. Second, **follow-up is unequal and, in one cohort, unstated**: Agaram's arms
differ about two-fold in mean follow-up (21.7 vs 43.3 months, biasing *against* the TAF15 arm accruing events)
and Huang publishes no per-arm follow-up at all.

### 3.4 · The partner is not independent of tumour size

Two series, both larger than any cohort that could be pooled here, tested the partner as a prognostic factor
against tumour size. **Neither found the partner to carry the prognosis.** ⚠ *They did not test it the same
way, and this paper previously said they did.* Huang 2023 reports an explicit **multivariable** model in which
the partner loses significance — that is the analysis §3.3, §4.1a and §5 mean by "the one series that ran that
model". For Paioli 2021 this repository holds only what its abstract reports: the partner reaches no
conventional significance while size does, in an analysis whose adjustment structure **is not stated in any
record held here**. Its full text is closed (§6), so whether that contrast was adjusted is UNKNOWN rather than
established, and it is not counted as a second multivariable result. *Superseded, retained: "Two series …
tested the partner as a prognostic factor in a multivariable model."*

- **Huang 2023** (PMID 36948401, n = 58, Taiwan): TAF15::NR4A3 shortens disease-specific survival on
  univariate analysis and **loses significance on multivariable analysis**. Only size > 10 cm
  (P = .004, **HR 30.60**) and metastasis at presentation (P = .032, **HR 8.14**) remain independent — and
  TAF15::NR4A3 is itself associated with size > 10 cm (78 %, P = .025) and with mean size (13.7 vs 7.3 cm,
  P = .024).
- **Paioli 2021** (PMID 32572850, n = 67, Italian Sarcoma Group): NR4A3-EWS shows only a **trend** for DFS
  (p = 0.08) and DMFS (p = 0.09), in the same analysis where **size reaches p = 0.004**. ⚠ *Whether that
  analysis was multivariable is not recorded anywhere in this repository — the abstract does not say and the
  full text is closed. This is the third independent series failing to establish the partner, not a second
  size-adjusted one.*

Huang 2023's event counts are now pooled in §3.3; Paioli 2021 publishes none and cannot be. That difference
does not soften the point they share, it sharpens it: **the cohort that supplies most of the pooled prognostic
magnitude is the same cohort that publishes the analysis saying that magnitude is confounded.** In both series
the variable reaching significance is **tumour size** and the partner is not, and in the one series that
adjusted, the partner is associated with size. **The partner
may be a marker for a big tumour rather than for a biology**, and nothing in the published record separates
those two readings.

⚠ **One arithmetic inconsistency in Huang's size row, recorded rather than resolved.** The paper prints the
EWSR1 arm's > 10 cm cell as *12/46 (28 %)*, and 12/46 is 26.1 %. One of those three printed numbers is wrong;
a denominator of 43 gives 27.9 % and a numerator of 13 gives 28.3 %, and §2.1(2) forbids back-deriving a count
from a percentage to close the gap. The integers are recorded as printed and the discrepancy with them
(`cohorts[huang-2023-outcome].size_covariate`). The TAF15 side has no such problem: 7/9 = 77.8 %, printed as
78 %, and independently corroborated verbatim in the Europe PMC abstract record. **No figure in this document
depends on the EWSR1 size cell.**

### 3.5 · Partner prevalence — how many patients this would touch

Pooling four independent molecular series — MSKCC (USA), Taiwanese institutions, Czech Republic and the Italian
Sarcoma Group, with no shared authors and no shared referral network between them:

| partner | pooled count | share of partner-assigned EMC | 95 % CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWSR1::NR4A3 | 120/154 | **77.9 %** | 70.7–83.7 |
| **TAF15::NR4A3** | **28/154** | **18.2 %** | **12.9–25.0** |
| TCF12::NR4A3 | 5/154 | 3.2 % | 1.4–7.4 |
| TFG::NR4A3 | 1/154 | 0.6 % | 0.1–3.6 |

Per-cohort TAF15 share ranges 15.8 – 29.2 % (spread 13.4 points). That is the population any
partner-stratified decision would move: large enough for the question to matter, small enough to explain why
every series' TAF15 arm is 2–10 patients.

⚠ **The pooled figure is below the ≈27 % the field usually quotes.** That figure comes from the single
referral-centre cohort (Agaram 2014) that is the high outlier of the four (29.2 % of assigned cases, against
15.8–18.2 % elsewhere) — and a tertiary sarcoma referral centre is exactly the setting where morphologically
unusual, high-grade, hard-to-classify tumours are over-represented, which is what TAF15::NR4A3 tends to be. The
two excluded congress abstracts bracket the pooled estimate rather than contradicting it (7 of 26
partner-assigned in Valencia; 1 of 9 in Pilsen/Znojmo).

### 3.6 · Mechanism, and what a third expression cohort does and does not add

Brenca 2019 [11] profiled 7 EWSR1-NR4A3 and 5 TAF15-NR4A3 EMC and reported an axon-guidance switch: class 4–6
semaphorins and pro-tumorigenic guidance cues higher in TAF15, growth-inhibitory class 3 semaphorins higher in
EWSR1, recapitulated in cells engineered to express either chimera. Verbatim: *"SEMA3C, SEMA3G, and RELN were
more expressed in EWSR1 tumors"*, and, from a chromatin affinity purification experiment, *"the ability of
NR4A3 to recognize the SEMA3C target region was retained by the EWSR1-NR4A3 chimera but was impaired by
TAF15-NR4A3"*.

⛔ **A third EMC expression cohort replicates SEMA3C, and it does not touch the clinical claim.** GSE28866
(3SEQ) is the only $0-reachable deposit carrying EMC libraries alongside normal tissue; in it SEMA3C reads
**1.8× normal tissue and 1.7× other sarcomas** across 4 EMC libraries, on a platform sharing no probe design
with the two arrays this repository had already read. One home:
[`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json`](../../modalities/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json) → `per_gene.values`; reading:
[`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md`](../fusion-output/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal-reading.md).

**That is evidence about the pathway, not about the stratification, and the distinction is not a formality:**

1. **GSE28866 carries no fusion-partner annotation at all**, so it cannot produce a partner contrast. Brenca's
   finding is a *difference between two partner groups*; a deposit with no partner calls cannot replicate,
   refute or weaken it.
2. **A partner-blind EMC cohort being SEMA3C-high is what an ~78 % EWSR1 population would look like under
   Brenca's own model**, so the observation is *consistent* with the mechanism and carries no information about
   the contrast.
3. **n = 4 EMC libraries, medians of per-peak medians of read density, transcript only.** No interval, no test,
   no protein, no localisation.
4. **The clinical claim in this document is about objective response and survival**, and no expression deposit
   of any size can move it.

The honest one-line statement is therefore: *the mechanistic axis Brenca described is detectable in a third EMC
cohort on a third technology; the clinical stratification claim is exactly where it was.* Borrowing strength
from the deposit for the clinical claim would be the error this section exists to prevent.

⚠ **And Brenca 2019 is not an independent replication of the clinical correlation.** It shares its senior
investigators and consortium (Milan/Aviano) with both TKI reports. It is the same investigators explaining
their own clinical observation, which is a legitimate and common thing to do and is not the same thing as an
outside group reproducing it.

### 3.7 · The only matched model pair reads against the hypothesis, not with it

Bangerter [12] established the only matched EWSR1/TAF15 patient-derived model pair in existence —
USZ20-EMC1 (EWSR1::NR4A3) and USZ22-EMC2 (TAF15::NR4A3). **The authors' finding is
partner-INDEPENDENCE.** Verbatim: *"Both models independent of the NR4A3 fusion partner showed high
sensitivity to carfilzomib"*, and *"Similar drug responses were seen in both models, independent of the fusion
partner from NR4A3."*

⚠ **Read the screen's shape before reading its result, because it is easy to overstate and this document
overstated it in draft.** The 40-drug panel was **not** run on the pair. Verbatim: *"A medium throughput drug
screen using 40 drugs was conducted with USZ20-EMC1 at passage 5."* Only three agents — carfilzomib,
doxorubicin and venetoclax — went forward to a **validation** step that used both models, and it is that step,
not the 40-drug screen, that the partner-independence sentences describe. The correction does not weaken the
reading, because the comparison was always the validation; it changes how wide the comparison was. Registered
in Appendix A, and corrected in the artifact in the same commit.

The only partner-differential reported is a scoring-threshold one: the carfilzomib + venetoclax and
carfilzomib + doxorubicin combinations scored **synergistic** in the EWSR1 model and **additive** in the TAF15
model, with one model per arm and no statistical claim attached. **Neither model was tested against an
antiangiogenic TKI.**

This does not refute the clinical correlation — it cannot, since it never tested the drug class in which the
correlation was observed. What it does is **bound** the biomarker's scope: there is no evidence that the
*NR4A3* partner predicts drug response in general, and the one experiment positioned to look found the
opposite. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: this repository's own lane memo recorded that "even the carfilzomib synergy
splits by partner", which states the reverse of the primary source; see Appendix A.*

### 3.8 · An attribution correction: whose hedge, and when

The investigators who reported the correlation do not believe the fusion causes it:

> **"Even in EMCS the fusion-protein is unlikely to be related to sunitinib sensitivity"**

⚠ **The quote is from the 2012 two-case report (PMID 23058004), not the 2014 series (PMID 24703573) it is
usually attributed to — including in this repository's own lane memo.** Verified against the cached full text
of both. The 2014 abstract does not contain it, and says instead that *"genotype/phenotype analyses support a
correlation between response and EWSR1-NR4A3 fusion"*.

This changes what the hedge **is**. Written in 2012, when the authors had two EWSR1-positive responders and no
TAF15 patient at all, it is their **prior** — reasoning by analogy to alveolar soft part sarcoma, another
translocation sarcoma in which sunitinib activity is not linked to the fusion protein — rather than a
retrospective caveat added once the partner correlation appeared. **That makes it stronger context, not
weaker**, and it belongs in the abstract of any paper on this question.

Three later observations are consistent with reading the partner as a **surrogate for something downstream**
rather than as a mechanism, and all three are above: the prognostic effect is absorbed by tumour size in the
two largest series (§3.4), Paioli 2021 cannot reach significance on it at all (§3.4), and the matched ex vivo
pair shows partner-independent drug response for the one class it tested (§3.7).

## 4 · Limitations

This section is written to survive an unsympathetic reader. Every item below is a reason to hold the
conclusion loosely, and several of them are reasons the conclusion could be wrong outright.

**4.1 · Every cohort is small, and that is structural rather than remediable.** EMC is ultra-rare. The largest
partner-stratified series anywhere in this synthesis has 67 patients, the pooled outcome analysis rests on
73, and **every TAF15 arm carrying an actual outcome is 2–8 patients** — the pooled TAF15 outcome arm is 15.
The intervals printed here are wide because the evidence is thin, and they are printed at that width
deliberately: the pooled TAF15 mortality interval spans 24.8–69.9 %, which is a range, not a prognosis. No
analytic choice can make an ultra-rare disease's cohorts larger. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "the largest
**poolable** outcome cohort has 23 … The only larger TAF15 groups (Huang 2023's 9, Paioli 2021's 10) publish no
event counts at all." Huang 2023's event counts were read from the published PDF on 2026-08-08 and are pooled;
Paioli 2021's clause stands.*

**4.1a · The prognostic magnitude is crude and its own larger cohort says it is confounded.** This is stated
here as well as in §3.3 and §3.4 because it is the single most quotable number in the paper and the single
easiest to quote without its defeater. Adjusted for tumour size and metastasis at presentation, the partner is
**not** an independent predictor of disease-specific survival in the one series that ran that model, and 78 %
of that series' TAF15 tumours were > 10 cm. Nothing here is adjusted for size, stage, era or treatment.

**4.2 · This is not a meta-analysis and must not be described as one.** What is done here is **crude
denominator-weighted pooling of counts from non-overlapping cohorts, with Wilson score intervals**. There is
**no** random-effects model, **no** between-study variance, **no** I², **no** funnel plot and **no** formal
heterogeneity test — and that is a deliberate methodological decision recorded in §2.5, not an omission. The
per-stratum patient and event counts that make τ² inestimable are stated once, in §2.5, rather than repeated
here. Consequently the pooled figures
here are **weighted descriptive summaries**, not meta-analytic estimates, and they carry none of the inferential
guarantees a reader may associate with the word "pooled". Where only one cohort contributes — the entire
TKI-response analysis — the "pool" is a **single-cohort Wilson interval**, stated as such rather than dressed as
a synthesis of several. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "Where only one cohort contributes — the entire
outcome-by-partner analysis — …". The outcome analysis became a genuine two-cohort pool on 2026-08-08; the
response analysis did not, and it is the one the sentence now names.*

**4.3 · Partner assignment is not uniformly molecularly confirmed across reports.** The four prevalence series
did not use one assay, and **two of them do not state, in any source reachable here, which assay assigned the
5′ partner in which case.** Agaram 2014 typed by FISH with RT-PCR junction typing; Huang 2023 describes a
FISH-typed cohort; Lenz 2023 reports only that molecular typing succeeded in 12 of 17 cases; Paioli 2021 reports
only that the diagnoses were molecularly confirmed. A method that is not stated cannot be assumed to be the
same method. Each series also carries a
*NR4A3*-rearranged-but-partner-unassigned residue (2, 1, 1 and 5 cases respectively) which is excluded from both
numerator and denominator, because the series do not describe those residues comparably. **That exclusion
assumes the unassigned cases are missing at random with respect to partner, and no report supports that
assumption.** If variant partners are systematically harder to type — which is the direction a break-apart FISH
workflow would produce — the pooled TAF15 share of 18.2 % is an **underestimate**, and the effect is largest in
the cohort with the biggest residue. Two further consequences: a "TAF15" case in one series and a "TAF15" case
in another are not guaranteed to have been ascertained by the same criterion, and the two congress abstracts
excluded here were excluded partly because their residues cannot be interpreted at all.

**4.4 · The two TKI response strata rest on secondary provenance.** Neither primary full text is open access.
The **per-arm denominators** for both the sunitinib series and the pazopanib trial were read from reviews and
from an independent group's reading of the series, not from the trial or series reports themselves; the
primaries are recorded as `primaryRef` and the strata carry `provenance: "secondary"` under §1.3 of the
contract. Corroboration comes from three sources including two independent groups (Ghent; University of
Michigan), which is why the numbers are used at all — but a reader should know that **the counts on which the
headline rests have not been read in the documents that generated them.**

**4.5 · The overlap between the two TKI cohorts is unresolved, and it is not a small effect.** It is the reason
the headline denominator is a **range (3 to 5)** rather than a number. If the sunitinib series' two TAF15
patients re-enrolled on the pazopanib trial, the world's entire TAF15 antiangiogenic experience is three
patients. The primary analysis assumes the worst case, so the conclusion is stated at both ends and does not
change between them — but the uncertainty is real. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "and only a paywalled table can
close it."* A **public registry** carries the answer and it was retrieved on 2026-08-08: NCT02066285's
exclusion criteria read *"Patients who have received previous antiangiogenic agents"*, corroborated on the EU
Clinical Trials Register (§6). Sunitinib is an antiangiogenic agent, so the series' patients were ineligible
for the trial. **This document has deliberately NOT yet acted on that**, for three reasons stated in §6: it is
the protocol's rule and not a patient-level audit, it changes which denominator is primary rather than whether
the TAF15 arm has any events, and it is a change to a **pooling decision**, which belongs in the generator's
`COHORTS` table rather than in prose. The 3-to-5 range therefore still stands here, and the sentence above is
now a statement about what is *unapplied*, not about what is *unknowable*.

**4.6 · The comparator arm is not verified EWSR1.** In the primary analysis it is *non-TAF15* (§3.1). Treating
it as an EWSR1 arm would attribute to EWSR1::NR4A3 a response rate that may include *FUS*, *TCF12* or untyped
cases.

**4.7 · Crude proportions, mixed follow-up, no censoring — and one cohort does not publish its follow-up at
all.** The outcome figures in §3.3 are during-follow-up proportions pooled over two cohorts: one whose arms
differ roughly two-fold in mean follow-up (43.3 vs 21.7 months) and one that publishes no per-arm follow-up,
so the same check cannot be run on it in either direction. Huang 2023 also reports outcome only for the 53 of
58 patients with follow-up available, and the five without are in neither numerator nor denominator. The
figures are not survival estimates, they are not adjusted, and they are not comparable to the DFS/DMFS figures
in §3.4, which are a different estimand entirely. The two hazard ratios quoted anywhere in this document
(HR 30.60 for size, HR 8.14 for metastasis at presentation) are **Huang 2023's own published values, reported
because they are the defeater** — no hazard ratio, survival curve or individual prognosis is derived anywhere
in this work, and none should be derived from it.

**4.7a · One count set was read by a human, not by a fetcher, and that provenance is different.** Every other
count on this page came from a document in the repository's `literature-cache` branch with its HTTP status
recorded. Huang 2023's Table 1 counts came from a person reading the published PDF, because the publisher's
edge returns 403 to automation while designating the same PDF free (§8). The identity of the paper behind
those counts was machine-verified against the Europe PMC core record; the counts themselves were not, and
cannot be until the block lifts. A reader who wants to check them must open the PDF.

**4.8 · The Fisher p-values are post-hoc.** They were not prespecified, are not performed in any source report,
are not corrected for the several endpoints reported here, and license nothing. **The single most quotable
number in this document, p = 0.0034 (§3.3, the pooled disease-specific-death contrast), is this section's real
subject and must not be read as inferential**: it is a post-hoc description of a 7-versus-6-event table across
two cohorts, not a prespecified test, and §4.1a states its confound. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "The 0.0672 in
§3.3 in particular must not be read as a near-significant finding; with 23 patients and four events it is a
description of a small table." That sentence described Agaram 2014 alone, before Huang 2023's counts were
pooled on 2026-08-08. The pooled §3.3 analysis no longer contains 0.0672 anywhere — 0.0034 replaced it — and
this caveat had been left pointing at a number the paper no longer states, leaving the actual headline
uncaveated. Caught by a blind adversarial seat, 2026-08-26.*

**4.9 · Publication and referral bias run in a direction that favours the hypothesis.** A perfect response
split is more publishable than a mixed one, and a two-patient report of a striking response is what began this
literature. The one cohort with the highest TAF15 share is a tertiary referral centre. Both biases inflate the
apparent contrast, and neither can be corrected here.

**4.10 · The mechanism is not independent of the clinical observation.** Brenca 2019 shares its consortium with
both TKI reports (§3.6). A mechanism proposed by the same investigators who reported the correlation is
context, not corroboration.

**4.11 · Data vintage.** The pooled cohorts describe patients diagnosed between 1989 and 2017 and treated
across an era in which EMC systemic options changed materially — antiangiogenic TKIs are themselves largely a
post-2011 development in this disease. Per §4 of the evidence contract, older outcome data most likely
understate the outlook of a patient diagnosed today and should be read as a conservative floor. No number here
has been adjusted upward to compensate, because inventing optimism is as dishonest as inventing pessimism.

**4.12 · No treatment recommendation is made, and none follows.** ⛔ Nothing in this document recommends,
supports or opposes giving or withholding sunitinib, pazopanib or any other agent to any patient or patient
group. It asserts no efficacy, no safety, no therapeutic window and no clinical readiness for any agent, and it
is not a basis for a clinical decision. The population-level question it addresses — *what does the published
record actually support?* — is not the individual question a clinician faces.

## 5 · What this synthesis claims, and what it does not

**Claims.**

- That four literatures bearing on the *NR4A3* 5′ partner have been combined under a single stated method for
  the first time — **not** that they converge as four *independent* lines. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "four
  independent lines converge ... a candidate stratification variable."* That overstated the mechanism and TKI
  lines' independence from each other (§4.10: they share the Milan/Aviano consortium and, per §3.1, a senior
  investigator with a trial site) and from the prevalence line (no independent-evidence claim was ever made
  for it, so it adds nothing to a count of *converging* lines). What remains after removing the non-independent
  and non-evidential lines is **one** — prognosis (§3.3) — and §3.4 shows it is the confounded one. The
  *NR4A3* 5′ partner is still a **candidate** stratification variable; it is a candidate on the strength of one
  crude, confounded prognostic contrast and a directionally consistent but statistically inconclusive treatment
  signal, not on convergent independent evidence. Caught by a blind adversarial seat, 2026-08-26.
- That the pooled objective-response evidence is **0 of 3–5 TAF15::NR4A3 patients**, with a 95 % upper bound of
  43.4–56.1 % — **directionally consistent and statistically inconclusive**, a distinction no existing review
  makes.
- That the **crude prognostic contrast now has a magnitude**: pooled over the two cohorts publishing
  partner-stratified event counts, disease-specific death is **7/15 = 46.7 % (95 % CI 24.8–69.9)** with TAF15
  against **6/58 = 10.3 % (4.8–20.8)** with EWSR1 — stated **only** together with the claim below, never alone.
- That the partner's prognostic effect **does not survive adjustment for tumour size** in either of the two
  series to test it (Huang 2023, Paioli 2021), in both of which size is the significant variable — so the
  magnitude above is a **crude, confounded** quantity that the larger of its own two cohorts declines to
  attribute to the partner.
- That the review literature's **metastasis claim is not established by either cohort with counts, in either
  direction**: the two disagree on sign, the pooled gap is 9.2 points with overlapping intervals, and the
  largest series to test it directly reports P = .728. ⚠ *Superseded, retained: "That the prognostic literature
  contains a **reversal nobody has reported**: distant recurrence is higher in the EWSR1 arm of the only cohort
  publishing partner-stratified event counts, while the review literature states the opposite." — Appendix A7.*
- That TAF15::NR4A3 affects **18.2 % (95 % CI 12.9–25.0)** of partner-assigned EMC — **below** the ≈27 % the
  field routinely quotes from a single referral-centre series.
- That the field's own hedge is **misattributed**: the sentence quoted as the sunitinib series' caveat is from
  the 2012 two-case report, written before any TAF15 patient existed.
- A concrete, zero-patient-cost ask of the field, stated in §6.

**Does not claim.**

- **Not** that TAF15::NR4A3 predicts non-response to antiangiogenic TKIs. Zero of five is not a rate.
- **Not** that EWSR1::NR4A3 patients should receive, or TAF15::NR4A3 patients should be denied, sunitinib,
  pazopanib or any other agent. No treatment recommendation follows from this analysis, and the comparator arm
  of the trial is **non-TAF15**, not verified EWSR1.
- **Not** that the fusion partner is mechanistically responsible for the response difference. The primary
  authors say the opposite, the mechanism paper shares their consortium, and the one matched model pair shows
  partner-independent drug response.
- **Not** that TAF15::NR4A3 is an independent prognostic factor. Huang 2023 says it is not once size is
  adjusted for, and Paioli 2021 does not reach significance at all. **The 46.7 % vs 10.3 % contrast is a crude
  proportion and is not a prognosis, an adjusted effect, or a reason to treat two patients differently.**
- **Not** that the prognostic result says anything about treatment response. They are different endpoints in
  different populations: the prognosis pool is 73 surgically managed patients from two pathology series, and
  the response question is 3–5 patients given an antiangiogenic TKI for advanced disease. **(a) moving in
  §3.3 leaves (b) exactly where it was**, and a zero-event arm yields no magnitude at any denominator.
- **Not** a survival model, a hazard ratio, or any individual prognosis. These are crude during-follow-up
  proportions with mixed follow-up and no censoring.
- **Not** a meta-analysis with heterogeneity modelling (§4.2).

**Endpoint, one sentence, for [`systems/views/L3-publications.md`](../../../systems/views/L3-publications.md):**
*the NR4A3 5′ fusion partner is a candidate — not established — stratification variable in EMC: it tracks
disease-specific death across the only two cohorts that publish event counts by partner and is not independent
of tumour size in either series that adjusted for it, while its entire published antiangiogenic-response
evidence base is three to five TAF15 patients with no events — so the field should start reporting the partner
rather than acting on it.*

## 6 · What would falsify this, and what the field could do about it

**What would kill it** (full list in the artifact under `what_could_kill_this`):

1. **One published TAF15::NR4A3 objective response on any antiangiogenic TKI.** With a denominator of 3–5, a
   single response moves the point estimate to 20–33 % and the contrast disappears. This is the shortest
   falsifier in the paper and it is one case report away.
2. The Lancet Oncol 2019 full text showing that the pazopanib trial's non-TAF15 arm contains non-EWSR1
   partners, or that a TAF15 patient sat outside the evaluable 22.
3. Confirmation that the sunitinib series' TAF15 patients re-enrolled on the pazopanib trial, collapsing the
   world's TAF15 experience to three patients.
4. A partner-stratified reanalysis of any registry (SEER, the Japanese national registry, the US Sarcoma
   Collaborative) showing no survival separation once size and stage are adjusted for — the direction Huang
   2023 already points.
5. A **third** outcome cohort with per-partner event counts in which TAF15 mortality is not elevated. The
   pooled crude death contrast rests on two TAF15 arms of 7 and 8 patients; a third of similar size
   disagreeing would put the pooled point estimate inside the comparator arm's interval.
6. Any **size-adjusted** partner analysis in which the partner *does* remain independent. That would overturn
   the defeater rather than the effect, and it is the one result that would turn §3.3's crude magnitude into a
   claim about biology instead of a claim about tumour size.

**The ask.** Report the *NR4A3* partner alongside response in every EMC systemic-therapy report, and publish
the partner-stratified counts from the trials and series that already hold them. **Two documents remain
unreachable and would materially extend the world's poolable evidence at zero new patient cost:**

| document | what it holds | why it is not reachable |
|---|---|---|
| Stacchiotti 2019, *Lancet Oncol* (PMID 31331701) | the pazopanib trial's full partner distribution and prior-therapy table | genuinely closed — `oa_status: closed`, zero OA locations in Unpaywall, `any_repository_has_fulltext: false` in OpenAlex; every listed institutional handle is metadata-only |
| Paioli 2021, *Ann Surg Oncol* (PMID 32572850) | per-partner relapse and metastasis counts behind the DFS/DMFS p-values, n = 67 | genuinely closed — same profile; the IRIS Bologna and Florence Research records state in terms that no file is attached |

✅ **Huang 2023 (PMID 36948401) is off this list as of 2026-08-08, and the reason it was on it was wrong.** It
was filed "paywalled". It is **bronze open access**: Unpaywall and OpenAlex independently designate a free
publisher-hosted *published-version* PDF (`oa_status: bronze`, `oa_date: 2023-03-21`), and what blocked every
automated fetch was an **anti-bot challenge returning HTTP 403**, not a subscription. The remedies differ — a

## 7 · Data, code and reproducibility

**All data underlying this synthesis are published data.** No patient-level data were obtained, held or
analysed; no individual patient record was accessed; and no new patient data of any kind were generated. Every
count comes from a peer-reviewed report or a congress abstract, and each is traceable to a resolvable
identifier (PMID, PMCID, DOI or NCT).

| item | path | role |
|---|---|---|
| pooled artifact | [`emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json`](./emc-fusion-partner-pooling.json) | **the one home of every number in this paper** — cohorts, strata, citations, analyses, provenance |
| generator | [`emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py`](../emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py) | stdlib-only; the counts are in its `COHORTS` table and every derived figure is computed from them |
| evidence contract | [`systems/POLICY-evidence.md`](../../../systems/POLICY-evidence.md) | the binding method, with its CI validator `scripts/validate-registry.mjs` |
| clinical registry | [`research/data/emc-clinical-registry.json`](../../data/emc-clinical-registry.json) | the repository's cited EMC registry; overlaps this work in citations only |
| third expression cohort | [`gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json`](../../modalities/gse28866-tumour-vs-normal.json) | SEMA3C read in §3.6; **not** used for any clinical claim |

**Reproduction.** One command, no dependencies beyond the Python standard library, no network:

```
python3 research/manuscripts/emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py
```

**Verified this session:** re-running the generator on this checkout reproduces the committed artifact
**byte-for-byte apart from its `_generated_utc` stamp**. Changing a count means changing it in the generator's
`COHORTS` table and regenerating — the artifact carries `_do_not_hand_edit` for that reason, and a figure typed
into this document that disagrees with it is the defect.

⛔ **And until 2026-08-08 that `_do_not_hand_edit` banner was unenforced, which is worth stating in the paper
rather than only in a commit message.** The generator parsed no arguments: `--check` was accepted by the shell,
ignored by the script, the artifact was regenerated and overwritten whatever you passed, and the process exited
0 — so **a hand edit to a pooled clinical event count would have persisted with nothing able to detect it**, in
an artifact that is nothing but clinical event counts. It now has a real verify mode that re-derives **in
memory**, compares against the committed file, writes nothing and exits non-zero on any difference:

```
python3 research/manuscripts/emc_fusion_partner_pooling.py --check
```

It runs in CI on every push, and — because a guard that no-ops into the behaviour it replaced produces no
symptom — `research/manuscripts/tests/test_emc_fusion_partner_pooling_check.py` perturbs the **real committed
artifact on disk** (a pooled event count, a Wilson bound, a per-cohort stratum, the tumour-size defeater, and
the `_do_not_hand_edit` note itself) and asserts the real `--check` refuses each **and writes nothing**. That
last assertion is what separates the repair from the defect: a `--check` that regenerates its own reference
returns 0 precisely because it has just made the file match.

**Licensing.** Each citation entry records the source's licence and it is respected; the raw-text
`literature-cache` branch is never republished.
