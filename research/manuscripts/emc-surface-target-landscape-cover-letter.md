**Re:** Submission of an Article — *"Surface-antigen prioritisation in extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma: a lineage-surrogate ranking tested against three tumour-tissue cohorts"*

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript above for consideration as an Article in the *British Journal of Cancer*.
Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma is a rare sarcoma driven by a nuclear fusion transcription
factor, so cell-surface antigens offer a delivery axis gated differently from driver-directed
routes; but the disease's surface-antigen expression has never been systematically mapped, and
prioritisation for it has therefore run on lineage surrogates. The manuscript reports a two-stage
study: a 2,826-gene surfaceome ranked across a translocation-sarcoma cell-line class with a
normal-tissue prior, and then the test of those priorities in three EMC tumour-tissue cohorts. The
priorities did not transfer. None of the eight surrogate-selective antigens was concordantly
elevated on both arrays, two were concordantly lower, none of the eleven therapeutic addresses named
by candidate surface-directed routes was concordantly elevated, and the one antigen elevated on both
arrays sat below the normal-organ median on the only cohort able to measure that.

The fit with the journal is that this is a negative result about a method the field uses routinely.
Surrogate-based target lists are how surface-antigen programmes for rare tumours are built when the
tumour itself has no data, and here the surrogate's negatives transferred while its positives did
not, which is the asymmetry that matters to anyone about to commit reagent development to such a
list. The manuscript also carries an amendment record: a cell line this programme had treated as its
one real EMC line is recorded as not carrying the fusion, and every use of it is reclassified rather
than quietly dropped.

The study is entirely computational. It analyses public gene-expression deposits and public
annotation resources only, involved no wet-laboratory work, no human participants, no identifiable
data and no patient-level records, and required no ethics approval. Nothing here asserts that any
antigen is a validated target in this disease, that any agent is safe or effective in it, that a
therapeutic window exists, or that any route is ready for clinical use.

The honest principal limitation is the one the Limitations section names as binding: every quantity
reported is transcript abundance, while every address discussed is a protein or glycan question, so
a high reading is a reason to stain rather than an antigen call; and the tumour cohorts are small
archival bulk deposits on decade-old platforms, at n = 6, n = 10 and n = 4, with the normal-tissue
exposure axis resting on medians of four libraries against six visceral organ types.

I intend to deposit the manuscript as a preprint on bioRxiv, consistent with the journal's preprint
policy, and will link the preprint to the published version. I am the sole author, an unaffiliated
independent researcher with no institutional address; no ORCID accompanies this submission. Analysis
code, data processing and drafting were carried out with substantial AI assistance under my
direction, which is disclosed in the manuscript; no AI tool is an author, and I take full
responsibility for the content.

The work is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. I have no
competing interests and received no funding.

Should it help the editors, appropriate reviewer expertise would include tumour-antigen and
cell-surface target discovery, sarcoma translational biology, and cross-platform expression
analysis in small archival cohorts.

Thank you for considering this manuscript.
