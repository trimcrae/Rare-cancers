Microenvironment / B-cell interpretation
I read the initial CD47/SIRPa plan, all three dated amendments, processed-marker values and code, raw counting code and source-linked mapping diagnostics. The processed release has 9,500 gene rows; MS4A1, CD79A and PTPRC are absent. Therefore the frozen minimum-three-B-marker and PTPRC-dependent contrast cannot run on that processed release. This is a valid plan stop and is not evidence that those transcripts or cells are absent in EMC. The unverified reconstruction of LASSO-derived risk classes cannot substitute for actual patient outcomes.

The worker appropriately recognized raw data remain accessible and appended a raw-processing plan, instead of calling the assay unmeasured. I challenged whether 20.72% exact-probe mapping from the first Si01 prefix was enough to dismiss raw data. It was not. The worker tested both published HWT2.0 and official HWT2.1 designs, confirmed all prespecified marker probes are present, considered forward/reverse and half-probe mapping, and tested a prefix from all twelve libraries. Exact matching varies from 6.62% to 69.18% across these prefixes. The alternate manifest does not solve the issue. Many unmatched reads have mismatching probe halves or no matching half, rather than merely one sequencing error.

These are implementation diagnostics on prefixes, not expression measurements or a complete evaluation of all reads. The 75% threshold is the worker's frozen allocation gate, not an externally validated assay-quality cutoff. Failing it supports pausing this unvalidated estimator, not declaring the raw measurements inherently unsuitable. Raw-count reconstruction remains pending accessible analysis and would need an independently validated processing approach, per-sample QC, all twelve full libraries and sensitivity to probe/quality effects. No subset of better-mapping EMC samples may replace the complete cohort. A biological or prognostic claim also needs observed outcome labels and independent tissue evidence.

Concrete feasible follow-up, without guaranteeing a paper
A bounded count-to-published-rank diagnostic can determine whether raw reconstruction is feasible. Before seeing the proposed full-library results, I advised against spending a full 2.7 GB stream solely to create an unverified reconstructed-risk B/PTPRC contrast. Across twelve samples, B-marker/PTPRC correlation is expected from shared immune content and does not disprove a B-specific prognostic association. A nonsignificant residual cannot establish absence of B specificity; PTPRC subtraction itself is not a validated cell-fraction measure. The original B associations already fail FDR, and the exact patient risk/outcome crosswalk is unavailable. Successful reconstruction would therefore provide methodological feasibility, not a sufficient new disease contribution. A genuine reopening would need observed patient group/outcome linkage and an independently justified discriminating comparison, or meaningful cellular localization evidence. The raw source remains pending accessible analysis, rather than unsuitable. Public CD47/SIRPa aggregates and newly retrieved old pharmacology curves do not themselves supply novel biology.
