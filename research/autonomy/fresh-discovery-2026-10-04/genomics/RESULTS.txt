GENOMICS PILOT RESULT — 2026-10-04
Decision: SHELVE THE STANDALONE PAPER. No new useful EMC finding cleared the bar.

Question and added analysis
The frozen plan asked whether a clinical sequencing release reveals recurrent secondary cell-cycle lesions beyond the defining fusion, with greater genomic disruption. We actually extracted and examined all 15 current EMC-labelled specimens (13 distinct patient IDs), every released mutation/CNA/SV for those samples, and all 15,770 SV entries for NR4A3/CHN/TEC in symbols and annotation. This last adversarial check avoids excluding a record whose two endpoint symbols say EWSR1 but whose event description says EWSR1:NR4A3. It added no patient. All seven selected cell-cycle genes occur in all three represented assay panels.

Observed, unpromoted measurements
The 11 mutation rows represent 10 distinct genes, each in only one donor. SMAD2's apparent duplicate occurs in two specimens of one donor. Among TP53, RB1, CDKN2A/B, CDK4, CCND1 and MDM2, only TP53 was altered: a frameshift in P-0054159, and deep-deletion calls in two specimens of P-0011067. Thus two donors, not three independent events (2/13; descriptive Wilson95% 0.0433–0.4223, conditioned on this clinical-sequencing sample).
Eight patients have an NR4A3 event/endpoint description in this release; the other five remain histologically labelled, with no inference that absence of a detected rearrangement disproves EMC. The TP53-deletion donor lacks released NR4A3 support; the frameshift donor has event-description support despite its endpoint-symbol mismatch. This does not license changing its source annotations or excluding the donor.
Twelve specimens pass FACETS QC; three fail. Only the TP53-deletion donor has QC-passing affected samples, making a cell-cycle-versus-FGA comparison uninformative. Two WGD-positive specimens have no NR4A3 event support in this release; this cannot establish WGD prevalence in authenticated EMC. TMB remains low (0–1.75543 mutations/Mb) and is not a new discovery. Missense variants were retained as literal variants, not automatically called drivers or actionable lesions. No survival association was estimated.

Why the paper is shelved
Suemitsu2025 already found TP53 in two patients and linked secondary alterations to outcomes. This pilot found no additional recurrent selected gene, and no independent validation of that prognostic claim. The historical MSK2022 release shares ten of these thirteen patients, while its P-0022485 is absent from the current API clinical lookup; absence is not evidence of reclassification. Three current patients are not in that older release. The two releases therefore contain fourteen distinct public patient IDs, not twenty-four independent observations.
The Sept30 repository HLA analysis already used the exact same 15-sample/13-patient MSK50K cohort. Calling this a new source would be wrong. The present cell-cycle question is distinct, but the underlying source is reused and the result adds too little beyond published secondary-genomics work. Complete broader genomic coverage was not achieved and remains recorded below; shelving does not falsely convert those gaps to exclusions.

Scientific reopening condition
A genuinely independent, fusion-authenticated cohort with a repeated non-TP53 secondary lesion and interpretable measurement/clinical context could reopen this question. For a prognostic claim, crosswalk to Suemitsu2025 and common time-origin/biopsy timing would be essential. No current result justifies drafting; completing inventories or adding narrower caveats would not rescue this standalone contribution.
