Pilot 1: macrophages and CD47/SIRPa
The Dancsok2020 primary source includes six EMC cases. Printed Figure2 measurements
show CD68 median0cells/mm2 (n5), CD163 median5cells/mm2 (n5), and adjusted mean
CD68:TIL ratio0.3 (n4). The two microarray cores per case are technical subsamples.
Thus relative M2 predominance does not establish high absolute macrophage density.
Figure4 shows heterogeneous CD47 staining and only a minority of EMC cases with
SIRPa-positive macrophages, but its EMC per-assay denominator is not printed.
We did not invent exact categorical counts or infer paired-cell relationships.
Supplement tables provide clinical aggregates (four cases with clinical data,
all four grade2; three outcome-evaluable), not a per-case checkpoint matrix.
All published EMC aggregates and the relevant figures were inspected. This is
useful contrary context, but the underlying measurements are already published.
Chaiboonchoe2026 already explicitly discusses EMC immune heterogeneity. Neither
retelling the protein results nor juxtaposing unmatched cohorts is new enough.

Pilot 2: is the reported favorable-risk B-cell signal B-cell-specific?
The prespecified contrast used MS4A1/CD79A/CD79B/CD37 relative to PTPRC with
CD3D/CD3E/CD3G and LST1/AIF1/FCER1G controls. We evaluated all twelve columns
of the released9500-gene TempO-Seq log2CPM matrix. MS4A1,CD79A,PTPRC,CD3G,LST1
and AIF1 are absent from that processed file; the frozen panel-coverage rule
failed, so no substituted-marker test or prognostic association was calculated.
The missing rows are not zero counts, absent expression or assay target absence.
The actual assay manifest contains every prespecified gene. The paper describes
zero/duplicate/low-expression filtering and9909retained genes; releasedS9 has9500.
No inference about causes of this discrepancy was made.

Raw follow-through
PRJNA1357027 contains twelve public50nt single-end TempO-Seq libraries matching
all twelve Si sample identifiers. Full FASTQs total about2.705GB compressed;
streaming would avoid disk growth. Official HWT2.0 manifest has22537probes.
Before full counting, the frozen exact-match implementation gate required75%
assignment or investigation. The first10000reads of EVERY library were inspected
as a measurement diagnostic, not as completed biological analysis.
Exact unique full50nt assignment ranged6.62–69.18%, all below the allocation
threshold. All matched reads were forward; all120000inspected reads were50nt.
The official v2.1 manifest shares22533sequences withv2.0 and did not improve
assignment. In Si01, only10of159unmatched reads among the first200were one
mismatch from a v2.0probe; many were23–25mismatches away. Across libraries,
2.15–14.53%of inspected reads matched different probes' first/last25nt halves.
These diagnostics do not establish the cause of unassigned reads and do not
show that the original investigators' aligned counts or biological claim are
invalid.75%is our prospective allocation threshold, not a validated assay rule.
They leave our exact-match method insufficiently validated for the intended
cell-composition comparison. No full12-library count stream was launched.

Interpretation and remaining value
No B-cell versus global-leukocyte result was established, either positive or
negative. The public paper's B-cell-risk contrasts were already nominal only
and did not survive its36-comparisonFDR; its mIF consists of two selected tumors,
not20independent patients. This pilot does not overturn that published biology.
Patient-specific risk labels/outcomes were not authenticated. A three-gene
reconstructed score would be exploratory; no inference used it. No G/B or Time
metadata field was treated as validated survival/censor information.

Reopening: a validated same-assay count/alignment pipeline or released unfiltered
probe counts, specimen-specific risk/outcome labels, and enough marker signal
to assess the original fixed contrast across all twelve cases. A new result
would still need all relevant EMC source evaluation and independent challenge.
Obtaining counts alone would not establish scientific value or revive a paper.
