Round 2 secondary-genomics discovery plan — 2026-10-04
Recorded before new case-level genomic values. Prior round files are immutable.

Question
Do the published potentially actionable IDH2, ARID1A and NTRK3 events in EMC recur across independent, molecularly supported EMC patients, and does the actual variant/fusion evidence support the biological interpretation assigned to them? A secondary exploratory question is whether complete public EMC genomic profiles reveal any other repeated, plausible non-TP53 lesion with evidence sufficient to change disease-specific research priorities.

Prior art examined before this plan
The GENIE ultra-rare sarcoma study (DOI10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-23-0876; PMC11874058) reports14 labelled EMC and one each of IDH2 mutation, ARID1A mutation and NTRK3 fusion. The overlapping GENIEv12 histology-agnostic analysis (DOI10.3389/fonc.2022.1079909) already reports one EMC with an NTRK fusion, uses source histology labels and oncogenic/likely-oncogenic OncoKB calls, and does not establish efficacy from a genomic call. These are published observations, not new hypotheses generated by our patient-level results. Pan-TRK IHC false-positive EMC and fusion-negative EMC controls are relevant primary evidence to retrieve; expression cannot substitute for a functional fusion.
Round1 already evaluated all15MSK50K specimens/13donors and older11MSK donors, with ten overlapping IDs. It found no new recurrent lesion among seven selected cell-cycle genes. All-gene source rows were saved and can be reused without new downloads. Suemitsu2025 already reports TP53 recurrence and an SGA-survival association. Foundation75 prior work recovered source identity but did not answer this biological question.

Exact first pilot and analysis choices
Retrieve the actual primary full text and supplements for GENIE ultra-rare and histology-agnostic studies, determine the release(s), then retrieve every accessible labelled EMC specimen and all released events, panel metadata and donor IDs. Query historical EMC aliases and defining NR4A3/EWS-TEC/CHN rearrangements beyond labels when the source permits. Preserve each source's original histology and event text. Do not change a diagnosis because a fusion call is absent. Distinguish NR4A3-supported, labelled with fusion unmeasured/undetected, and source-conflicting identity. Do not classify NTRK rearrangements as oncogenic unless partner/orientation/domain or explicit source annotation supports that interpretation.
Extract all eligible EMC records from Foundation75 and Davis2017 primary measurements, then harmonize literal events across these, GENIE and reused MSK. Include other published cohorts discovered by source/citation tracing, staging any unresolved access or patient crosswalk explicitly. All genes/events remain in the source tables, not just favorable ones. SNV/indel, copy number, gene fusion and IHC are different measurements.
Count independent donors, not repeated specimens, calls or releases. Explicit matching identifiers establish overlap; unresolved cross-institution or published-series overlap prevents independent validation claims. Denominators must reflect gene and event-class assay coverage; missing records are not proven negative measurements. No pooled prevalence across heterogeneous referral series. Report source-specific counts and Wilson95% intervals only when a valid measured donor denominator exists; descriptive intervals do not estimate unselected EMC prevalence. No gene-significance screen, survival model or clinical efficacy inference in this pilot. Any new gene-wide association requires a dated amendment and a defensible comparison, not opportunistic p-values.

Potential scientific addition and its strongest alternatives
A reproducible secondary actionable lesion in NR4A3-supported, independent EMC could motivate a specific biomarker or mechanistic experiment. Conversely, an exact event-level reason that an influential reported target is not biologically interpretable as claimed could challenge that claim, provided it is more than a source-inventory cleanup. Alternative explanations include one nonindependent patient repeated across releases, variant-of-uncertain-significance inflation, assay-specific missingness, incomplete rearrangement ascertainment and unresolved sarcoma classification.

Defeat and reassessment
No repeat independent event, no interpretable functional alteration, or prior publication already establishing the same useful inference defeats a standalone paper. Inability to authenticate a published case is uncertainty, not proof of misdiagnosis or a negative result. A literature count or expanded inventory does not clear the bar. A promising signal triggers the most likely disproof: exact source row, alternative diagnosis support, donor overlap, panel ascertainment and every identifiable contrary EMC observation. Suitable accessible pending evidence blocks promotion. At the pilot checkpoint explicitly continue, perform a bounded decisive follow-up, or shelve, preserving unfinished coverage and specifying reopening evidence.
