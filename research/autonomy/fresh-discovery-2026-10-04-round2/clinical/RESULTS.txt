Completed patient-level calculation
All 23 patients in Oliveira2000 Table2 were evaluated. The source's three patients
with metastatic presentation (3,6,15) were excluded, leaving 20 initially
nonmetastatic patients. Twelve were alive, observed and metastasis-free at five
years. Two had first metastases during years 5–10 (case11 at5.5y, case2 at9y), and
six were still observed metastasis-free at ten years. The conditional five-year
metastasis estimate is 21.43% (Greenwood log-log 95%CI 5.55–63.86%). Changing the
ordering of the event/censor tie at5.5y changes the estimate to22.08%. The first
five-year estimate was31.93% (15.72–57.90%). No before/after significance test or
claim of declining hazard was made. Deaths in the eligible source rows occurred
after metastasis, so no competing death changes the metastasis CIF in these rows.

This is a small historical Mayo series, 1968–1996, with five referred patients;
referral-entry dates are unavailable. Delayed-entry/selection bias and variable
surveillance cannot be corrected. The model assumes independent censoring. Source
diagnosis is pathological and predates routine NR4A3 confirmation. No surveillance
schedule, treatment efficacy, dormancy mechanism or general patient risk is inferred.
Fifteen cases overlap the later McGrory report according to Oliveira's actual
methods; these must not be counted as independent evidence.

Why this does not support a paper
Late relapse is already an explicit original-study conclusion. Drilon2008 Table2
already reports distant recurrence-free survival71% at5y and58% at10y, implying
18.31% conditional NET recurrence over the next5y. Ogura2012 prints89% and61%,
implying31.46%. Those re-expressions have no valid uncertainty from the two printed
percentages alone and are not real-world competing-risk estimates. Drilon starts
the clock at wide local excision; Mayo timing is from diagnosis; Ogura's original
methods remain unavailable despite public abstract/tables. The new computation
does not refine a clinically consequential prediction beyond these known results.
An independent worker checked all23 rows and recomputed the result, agreeing to shelve.

Discordance checked rather than explained away
Minami2020's primary abstract reports all10 extracompartmental-invasion patients
developed postoperative distant metastasis in35 operated EMC patients. Ashburner
reports16/44 multicompartmental tumors and p=1.000 for metastasis association.
These may be different measurements. Minami full methods were blocked with403;
the exact ETI definition and its correspondence to the MRI compartment definition
remain unverified. P-values from differently defined features are not a comparative
effect test. No reversal or validation claim is advanced. This is a specific
unfinished question requiring source definitions and complete outcome crosswalks.

Kandoussi2024-online/2025-print provides another30 EMC patient rows; all were
inspected. It records ever-metastasis and final follow-up, not first-metastasis
times. Only17 have pretreatment imaging, and the imaging table is aggregated by
site. Its data are available upon request; outreach is not authorized. It cannot
complete either proposed comparison. Differences among imaging denominators must
be resolved from full methods rather than silently borrowing all30 as MRI cases.

Reopening conditions
Conditional risk: sufficient independent EMC individual follow-up with explicit
time origins, referral entry, first events and competing death, plus a clinically
useful contrast beyond known persistent late relapse. MRI: authenticated individual
pretreatment enhancement/compartment definitions, initial metastatic status and
complete subsequent outcomes. Current gaps are in COVERAGE.txt. No manuscript,
external scientific record or publication state was changed.
