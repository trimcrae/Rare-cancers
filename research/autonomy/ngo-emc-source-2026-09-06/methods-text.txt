Supplementary Materials and Methods 61 
Patient samples 62 
Sample selection 63 
Cases with pathological diagnosis of epithelioid sarcoma (EpS) or extracranial 64 
extrarenal rhabdoid tumor (EERT) (age >18 years) between January 1998 and January 65 
2022 were retrieved from the Department of Pathology of Institut Gustave Roussy, 66 
Villejuif, France. All cases were histologically reviewed by two soft tissue 67 
pathologists (C.N. and J-M. C.) and classified by histological subtype (distal, 68 
proximal or hybrid EpS) according to the reference consensus guidelines 69 
(Supplementary Table S1) [1]. Final pathological diagnoses and clinico-pathological 70 
characteristics of the patients are summarized in Supplementary Table S2. Archived 71 
frozen and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples were used according to 72 
the French legal framework of MR-004 reference methodology set up by the French 73 
Data Protection Authority (Number I14112002202020 Health Data Hub, available at 74 
https://www.health-data-hub.fr/). Tumor samples were collected regardless of tumor 75 
status (untreated or treated primary tumors, recurrences, or metastases), with a 76 
preference given to untreated primary tumors whenever available. 77 
An additional external cohort of 12 EpS and 1,041 selected soft tissue tumors’ bulk 78 
RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) data was provided by Centre Lé on Bé rard, Lyon, 79 
France. 80 
For single-cell RNA-sequencing analysis  81 
Seven patients among the selected cases signed a preoperative written informed 82 
consent to allow the use of tumor residual samples for single-cell RNA-sequencing 83 
(scRNA-seq) analysis.    84 
Bulk RNA sequencing  85 
Sample preparation and paired-end sequencing 86 
Total RNAs were extracted from FFPE tissue sections with tumor purity > 40%, using 87 
a FormaPure RNA kit (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA). The Ambion DNase I 88 
(Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was used to remove DNA. RNA 89 
quantification was assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher 90 

PAGE 4
4 
 
Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA) measurement and RNA quality using 91 
TapeStation system with Hs RNA screen tape (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). 92 
Samples with sufficient RNA quantity (> 0.5 μg) and quality [percentage of RNA 93 
fragments > 200 nucleotides (DV200) > 30%] were considered suitable for 94 
sequencing. Individual libraries were prepared using 100 ng of total RNA and TruSeq 95 
RNA Exome kits (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Sequencing was performed 96 
(paired-end, 2 ×  75 cycles) using NextSeq 500/550 high output kits on a NextSeq 500 97 
machine, or the NovaSeq 6000 SP/S1 reagent kit on a NovaSeq 6000 machine 98 
(Illumina). The mean number of reads per sample was approximately 80 million. 99 
Bioinformatics and data analysis 100 
Alignments were performed using Spliced Transcript Alignment to a Reference 101 
(STAR) version 2.6.1 (available at https://github.com/alexdobin/STAR) on the hg19 102 
version of the human reference genome. Numbers of duplicate reads were assessed 103 
using Picard version 2.23.9 (available at https://broadinstitute.github.io/picard/). 104 
Samples with a number of unique reads less than 8 million were discarded from 105 
further analyses. Fusion transcripts were called by STAR-Fusion version 1.10.1 and 106 
Arriba version 2.4.0 (available at https://github.com/suhrig/arriba). Expression values 107 
were extracted using the Salmon v1.4.0 tool with University of California, Santa Cruz 108 
Genome Reference Consortium Human Build 37 (GRCh37) reference. All samples 109 
were normalized together using a variance stabilizing transformation with the DESeq2 110 
R package version 1.34.0 (available at 111 
https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/DESeq2.html). 112 
Consensus hierarchical clustering using the Ward.D2 was performed on the 113 
transcriptomic data of 33 EpS and 3 EERT samples using the top 25% most variably 114 
expressed genes. 115 
Differential gene expression analysis was performed with the DESeq2 R package. The 116 
significance was given by adjusted P-value (Padj) < 0.05 and log2 fold change 117 
(log2FC) > 1. Functional enrichment analysis was performed using ToppFun from the 118 
ToppGene suite [2]. Enriched gene sets from Gene Ontology Biological Process (GO 119 
BP) with Padj < 0.05 were selected. 120 
Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed using the normalized 121 
expression by median of ratios from DESeq2 with the hallmark gene set collection 122

Whole-exome sequencing (WES) 129 
Sample preparation and sequencing 130 
WES was performed on 17 tumor samples and 16 tumor/normal sample pairs with 131 
tumor purity > 40%.  132 
For 14 tumor/normal pairs and 2 tumor samples, sequence capture and exome 133 
sequencing were performed by the Sequencing Platform at Institut Curie, Unit of 134 
Genetics (Paris, France). Briefly, genomic DNA was isolated from fresh frozen tissue 135 
by standard proteinase K digestion and phenol/chloroform extraction. Then, the 150–136 
200 bp DNA fragments library was analyzed with SureSelectXT HS kit and 67.3 Mb 137 
CRE v2 probes (Agilent). DNA fragments were barcoded, captured, and enriched by 138 
polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with SureSelect Post-Capture Primer Mix Agilent 139 
and P5 & P7 primers Illumina (P5: 5'-AATGATACGGCGACCACCGA-3'; P7: 5'-140 
CAAGCAGAAGACGGCATACGAGAT-3'), before quantifying and qualifying using 141 
the TapeStation system. PCR was performed with an initial denaturation at 98° C for 2 142 
min, followed by 9 cycles of 98° C for 30 s, 60° C for 30 s, and 72° C for 1 min, and a 143 
final extension at 72° C for 10 min. Fragments were then sequenced by NextSeq500 144 
paired-end 2 × 100 bp (Illumina). Mean sequencing depths of 200× for somatic and 145 
100×  for germline were reached.  146

exome sequencing were performed by IntegraGen SA (Evry, France). DNA was 148 
extracted from the FFPE tissue sample using the QIAamp Diagnostic Sample 149 
Preparation (DSP) DNA FFPE Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), according to 150 
the manufacturer’s instructions. The protocol followed by IntegraGen has been 151 
described elsewhere [3]. In brief, genomic DNA was captured using Twist Human 152 
Core Exome Enrichment System (Twist Bioscience, South San Francisco, CA, USA) 153

Bioinformatics and data analysis 166 
Sample reads were aligned following Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) best practice 167 
with bwa version 0.7.17-r1188. Targeted regions were sequenced to an average depth 168 
of 150×, with 99% of the regions covered by ≥ 1×, 98% covered by ≥ 4×, and 95% 169 
covered by ≥ 20×. 170 
For the detection of somatic single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) and base insertions or 171 
deletions (indels), we used GATK Mutect2. We applied the following filtering criteria 172 
for somatic mutation calling: alternative read count < 9. The following filtering 173 
criteria were applied for germline variant calling: alternative read count <1. The 174 
variants were annotated using VEP (available at http://www.ensembl.org/vep) and 175 
SNPeff (available at http://pcingola.github.io/SnpEff/). Only frame-shift 176 
deletions/insertions, in-frame insertions/deletions, missense mutations, nonsense 177 
mutations and splice-site variants were selected.  178 
For copy-number variation (CNV) analysis, we used CNVkit version 0.9.9 (available 179 
at https://github.com/etal/cnvkit). A homozygous deletion was considered when 180 
estimated log2 copy ratio was below 1. Intermediate estimated log2 copy ratios 181 
between -1 and -0.25 were considered as heterozygous deletions.  182 
Tumor mutational burden (TMB) per Mb was assessed in samples with matched 183 
normal samples using the maftools R package version 2.10.05 and only non-184 
synonymous variants with functional impact. 185

DNA methylation 186 
Sample preparation and sequencing 187 
Thirty-seven DNA samples were hybridized on the Infinium MethylationEPIC array 188 
(Illumina) according to the manufacturer’s instructions at the Genomics and 189 
Proteomics Core Facility of the German Cancer Research Center (DKFZ) Heidelberg. 190 
Both fresh frozen and FFPE samples with tumor purity > 50% were included. DNA 191 
from frozen samples was extracted by standard proteinase K digestion and 192 
phenol/chloroform. Following deparaffinization, DNA from FFPE samples was 193 
extracted with the Maxwell® 16FFPE Plus LEV DNA Kit (Promega, Fitchburg, 194 
Wisconsin, USA) and a Maxwell DNA extractor (Promega). 195 
Bioinformatics and data analysis 196 
CNV analysis was performed using the ‘conumee' R package in Bioconductor 197 
(available at http://bioconductor.org/packages/conumee/) from .IDAT files. In brief, 198 
the sum of the raw fluorescence signals from probes targeting both the methylated and 199 
unmethylated alleles of a CpG site was calculated, then the intensity values were 200 
log2-transformed and referenced to the diploid genome. This copy-number ratio was 201 
then plotted in a graph according to chromosomal location. Homozygous deletion was 202 
considered when log2 ratios of targeting probes were below 1. Intermediate log2 203 
ratios between -1 and -0.25 were considered as heterozygous deletions. A low-level 204 
copy number gain was defined as a log 2 ratio > 0.25 and an amplification as log 2 205 
ratio > 0.4. Evaluation of arm-level copy number alteration was based on visual 206 
assessment of DNA methylation CNV plots. 207 
Fluorescence hybridization in situ (FISH) 208 
Sections (4 μm thick) were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded 209 
series of ethanol solutions. For the detection of SMARCB1 deletion, ZytoLight® 210 
SPEC SMARCB1/22q12 Dual Color Probe was used (ZytoVision GmbH, 211 
Bremerhaven, Germany). We followed the procedure as recommended by the 212 
manufacturers and slides were viewed under a fluorescent microscope equipped with 213 
multi‐bandpass filters to visualize colors simultaneously.  214

To predict sample tumor fraction and copy number counts, ichorCNA analysis was 228 
applied (available at https://github.com/broadinstitute/ichorCNA). IchorCNA uses a 229

simultaneously segment the genome, predict large-scale copy number alterations, and 231 
estimate the tumor fraction of an ultra-low-pass whole genome sequencing sample. 232 
Immunohistochemistry analysis 233 
Immunohistochemical staining was performed on 3 μm-thick sections from FFPE 234 
tumor samples for antibodies to the following proteins: switch/sucrose non-235 
fermentable-related matrix associated actin dependent regulator of chromatin 236 
subfamily B member 1 (SMARCB1), CD3, CD8, CD68, CD163, CD21, CD23 and 237 
desmoglein 2 (DSG2). The antibodies and conditions used for immunohistochemistry 238 
are summarized in Supplementary Table S10.  239 
The density of tumor-infiltrating immune cells was evaluated on whole-tissue sections 240 
with a semi-quantitative method, as follows: 0: 0%; 1: 1%-25%; 2: 26%-50%; 3: > 241 
50%, in the core of the tumor or intratumoral area and in the invasive margin or 242 
peritumoral area. The density of tumor-infiltrating immune cells was subsequently 243 
divided into high or low based on the median score as a cut-off for each individual 244 
immune cell. 245

Visium Spatial transcriptomics assay 251 
Sample and library preparation 252 
Representative FFPE tumor blocks from four EpS patients with DV200 > 30% were 253 
selected for the Visium Spatial transcriptomics assay (10x Genomics, Pleasanton, CA, 254 
USA) to provide optimal coverage of the 6.5 ×  6.5 mm capture area. Using the human 255 
whole transcriptome probe set, spatial transcriptomics was performed with 10x 256 
Genomics Visium technology according to the Visium Spatial Gene expression for 257 
FFPE samples protocol described previously [7]. Generated Visium libraries were 258 
pooled and loaded in a single small paired-end Illumina flow cell. Sequencing was 259 
performed on NovaSeq PE 50 at a sequencing depth of 50,000 read pairs per spot 260 
covered with tissue using 10x Genomics recommended run parameters. 261 
Bioinformatics and data analysis 262 
Space Ranger (10x Genomics) was used to perform the demultiplexing of Visium-263 
prepared raw base call files generated by Illumina sequencers into FASTQ files using 264 
Space Ranger version 2.0.0 mkfastq. Next, Space Ranger count was used to perform 265 
tissue and fiducial alignment from the microscope slide image and barcode/ Unique 266 
Molecular Identifier (UMI) counting to generate feature-barcode matrices that were 267 
further used for downstream analyses.  268 
Downstream analyses relied on the Seurat R package (version 4.3, available at 269 
https://satijalab.org/seurat/). We calculated the average expression levels of selected 270 
genes subtracted by the aggregated expression of control gene sets using the 271 
AddModuleScore function from Seurat. 272

Bioinformatics and data analysis 285 
Raw Binary Base Call (BCL) files were demultiplexed and converted to Fastq format 286 
using bcl2fastq (version 2.20.0.422 from Illumina). Read quality control was 287 
performed using fastQC (version 0.11.9) and assignment to the expected genome 288 
species was evaluated with fastq-screen (version 0.14.0). Reads were pseudo-mapped 289 
to the Ensembl reference transcriptome v99 corresponding to the homo sapiens 290 
GRCH38 build with kallisto (version 0.46.2, available at 291 
https://github.com/pachterlab/kallisto) using its 'bus' subcommand and parameters 292 
corresponding to the 10x Chromium 5' scRNA-Seq v2 chemistry. The index was 293 
made with the kb-python (version 0.24.4) wrapper of kallisto. Barcode correction 294 
using a whitelist provided by the manufacturer (10x Genomics) and gene-based reads 295 
quantification were performed with BUStools (version 0.40.0, available at 296 
https://github.com/BUStools/bustools). 297 
ScRNA-Seq data were analyzed using the Seurat R package (version 4.0.4). Samples 298 
stemming from different patients were combined by merging the raw count matrices 299 
of individual Seurat objects. The SCTransform normalization method [10] was used 300 
to normalize, scale, and select 2,000 highly variable genes. Pearson residuals from 301 
this regression were used for dimension reduction by principal component analysis 302 
(PCA). The number of PCA dimensions to keep for further analysis was evaluated by 303 
assessing a range of reduced PCA spaces using 3 to 50 dimensions, with a step of 2. 304 
For each generated PCA space, Louvain clustering of cells was performed using a 305 
range of values for the resolution parameter from 0.1 to 1.2 with a step of 0.1. The 306

11 
 
optimal space was manually evaluated as the combination of kept dimensions and 307 
clustering resolution resolving the best structure (clusters homogeneity and 308 
compactness) in a uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP). 309 
Additionally, we used the clustree method (version 0.4.3) to assess if the selected 310 
optimal space corresponded to a relatively stable position in the clustering results 311 
tested for these dimensions/resolution combinations.  312 
The integration of samples stemming from different patients was performed by using 313 
the Harmony algorithm (https://github.com/immunogenomics/harmony) [11]. 314 
Datasets were merged using the merge() function from Seurat (version 4.0.4), and the 315 
SCTransform normalization method was used to normalize, scale and select 2,000 316 
highly variable genes. The reduced PCA spaces were used as input for the 317 
HarmonyMatrix() function implemented in Harmony package (version 0.1.0) where 318 
the batch effect was regressed. The batch-corrected shared space output by Harmony 319 
was used for clustering.  320 
We used CerebroApp (version 1.3.1, available at 321 
https://github.com/romanhaa/cerebroApp/), a cell report browser which allows users 322 
to interactively visualize various parts of single-cell transcriptomics analyses to 323 
identify the most expressed genes in each cell subpopulation to compute pathway 324 
enrichment on marker genes (based on the Enrichr application programming 325 
interface) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) [12]. 326 
Inference of single cells’ CNV was performed using the InferCNV tool. Raw gene 327 
expression data were first extracted from each patient's Seurat object. For each 328 
sample, normal reference cells were selected based on the expression of immune cells 329 
and/or cancer-associated fibroblasts canonical markers and tumor cells were grouped 330 
according to annotated Louvain clusters as defined above. The cutoff for the 331 
minimum average read count per gene was set to 0.1, as recommended for 10x 332 
Genomics’ data. The 'cluster_by_groups' setting was used to perform separate 333 
clustering for each cluster as defined in the cell annotations file. All other options 334 
were set to their default values. Each CNV was annotated as a gain or a loss to a p- or 335 
q-arm using the GRCh38 reference genome. We also applied InferCNV on the 336 
merged dataset with the same parameters, a down-sampling of 800 cells per cell type 337 
and using all the non-tumor cells as reference. 338

To infer cell-cell communication between identified tumor and immune cell clusters, 341 
we used CellphoneDB (available at https://github.com/Teichlab/cellphonedb), a 342

Assessment of scRNA-Seq-derived signatures’ specificity and prognostic value 349 
CIBERSORTx 350 
To deconvolve scRNA-Seq EpS malignant cell subclusters identified in EpS scRNA-351 
seq data from EpS bulk RNA-seq, CIBERSORTx (https://cibersortx.standford.edu) 352 
was used to derive a signature matrix from scRNA-seq data. Using the Seurat subset 353 
function, 500 or 1,000 cells were extracted from each cluster from the scRNA-seq 354 
integrated dataset. Cluster-labeled cells served to obtain a single-cell reference matrix 355 
(scREF-matrix) used as an input on the CIBERSORTx online server using the 356 
“Custom” option. Default values for replicates (n = 5), sampling (0.5), and minimum 357 
expression (0.0) were used. Additional options for kappa (999), q-value (0.01), and 358 
number of barcode genes (300–500) were kept at default values. The imputation of 359 
cell subtype fractions defined on the scREF-matrix was performed on counts per 360 
million (CPM) values from EpS bulk RNA-seq data using the ‘Impute Cell Fractions’ 361 
function with the ‘Custom’ option and ran in absolute mode. A total of 500 362 
permutations was performed to test for statistical significance. 363 
Gene signature scores calculation for specificity and prognostic assessment 364 
The top 100 upregulated genes within each malignant cell subcluster from the 365 
scRNA-seq integrated dataset, defined by Seurat graph-based clustering using the K-366 
nearest neighbor method, were selected to define cluster-specific gene signatures. A 367 
score for each cluster-specific signature was then calculated from the Institut Gustave 368 
Roussy EpS bulk RNA-seq dataset and an external cohort of 1,041 samples from 32 369 

PAGE 13
13 
 
soft tissue tumor subtypes using the arithmetic mean of Z-scores of each gene 370 
expression expressed in variance stabilizing transformed raw counts. Samples with a 371 
number of unique reads less than 10 million were discarded from this analysis. 372 
First, to assess the specificity of scRNA-Seq derived cluster-specific gene signatures 373 
for EpS, an analysis of variance (ANOVA) test was performed to compare the 374 
signature values between EpS transcriptomic subtypes, EERT and all other subtypes 375 
of soft tissue tumors. The significance test was set at P < 0.05.  376 
To further evaluate the prognostic value of scRNA-Seq-derived cluster-specific gene 377 
signatures, the IGR EpS bulk RNA-seq dataset was segregated into ‘High’ and ‘Low’ 378 
signature scores using the optimal cutpoint method. We then performed a survival 379 
analysis for patients from whom the tumor samples were collected to compare ‘High’ 380 
versus ‘Low’ signature scores using the Kaplan-Meier model and tested for 381 
significance with the log-rank test. Overall survival was defined as the time from the 382 
EpS diagnosis until death from disease. Metastasis-free survival was defined as the 383 
time from the EpS diagnosis until the occurrence of distant metastasis or death from 384 
disease. Subsequently, we validated our findings on an external validation cohort of 385 
RNA-Seq data from 12 EpS samples. 386 
Statistical analyses 387 
Unless otherwise specified in the specific methods section, statistical data analysis 388 
was performed using PRISM 9 (GraphPad Software Inc., CA, USA) on the raw data. 389 
Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Fisher’s exact test for 390 
categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous (non-categorical) 391 
variables. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. 392
