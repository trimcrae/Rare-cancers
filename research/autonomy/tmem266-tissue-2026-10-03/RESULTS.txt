TMEM266 within published EMC tissue: targeted follow-up
3 October 2026 America/New_York / 4 October UTC

Answer
Yes, there is published tissue-level evidence for TMEM266-targeted RNA in
pathology-reviewed EMC FFPE areas, in addition to the earlier bulk datasets.
The official assay probe exactly matches a spliced TMEM266 region. We have
not located that RNA in morphologically identified malignant cells or
established an intact protein-coding transcript or surface protein.

Demonstrated finding (two-sentence candidate)
A new analysis of the published 12-specimen EMC FFPE expression table found
TMEM266-targeted RNA signal, and sequence verification shows that the matching
manufacturer v2.0 probe spans the TMEM266 exon 10-11 splice junction outside
the identified antisense-overlap region. This strengthens the tissue RNA
lead, but the scraped tissue areas contain unspecified cell mixtures and
the measured junction is shared by coding and alternative transcripts.

The workbook contains one exact TMEM266 row19836, probeTMEM266_14670,
Entrez123591, ENSG00000169758, listed transcriptENST00000388942.
Probe: GTGGTGACCATACACACAGCTGGCTCTGGGACACCGCTGTCACTGCTGGG (50nt).
Its reverse complement exactly equals the joined hg38 intervals
chr15:[76202254,76202264) + [76203740,76203780), respectively the final10nt
of canonical exon10 and first40nt of exon11. Coordinates here are 0-based,
half-open; the literal manufacturer span15:76202255-76203780:- is consistent
with 1-based inclusive boundaries and antisense probe orientation.
This target is distinct from shared antisense exon[76175558,76175674).
The exact assay batch/manifest revision is unreported, so matching the named
v2.0 assay to this official v2.0 design remains a qualification. Exact design
matching does not replace an assay-specificity experiment or exclude every
possible genomic off-target. It supports the interpretation of a spliced
TMEM266 region in tissue, not direct sequencing of an entire endogenous RNA.

The same junction occurs in GENCODEv26 canonical ENST00000388942.7 and
alternative ENST00000484722.5, ENST00000561302.5 and ENST00000558249.1.
Current Ensembl annotation also retains this ambiguity and adds further
models. Do not interpret the manufacturer's single transcript identifier as
isoform-specific detection. isoform-context-results.json records every
queried model and its junction compatibility, with versioned coordinates.

Independent supporting analysis: one patient-derived culture
Davila2016 DOI10.1186/s12864-016-3161-9, source cultureV1-34 / GSM2113301,
is labelled EMC in source TableS4 and EWSR1-NR4A3 in TableS6. Original
methods describe patient-derived residual-tumor cultures; this is source-
authenticated provenance, not a fresh STR/culture-purity validation.

recount3 G026 assigns1333 and1694 aligned-base coverage sums to TMEM266.

All other11 union intervals have zero coverage, including the116bp shared
antisense exon. Thus this particular antisense-overlap explanation does not
account for the culture signal. Each complete range query transferred less
than24KB; no entire BigWig or raw sequencing file was downloaded.

These are four UNION-exon regions, not four canonical exons. In canonical
ENST00000388942.7/.9, exons1-8 have zero coverage in both runs, exon9 has0/83,
exon10 has113/63 and exon11 has279/573. The upstream extension of union
interval12 is compatible with a retained-intron transcript; the distal last
interval is an alternative terminal exon absent from the canonical model.
The sparse downstream signal strengthens TMEM266-locus attribution but
cannot phase exons into a complete RNA, establish mapping uniqueness or
infer a particular alternative isoform. Full coding RNA remains unproven,
not disproved. Do not call alternative-isoform predominance or an NMD
discovery. Culture coverage does not localize RNA in intact patient tissue.

Supporting context and rejected overinterpretations
1. Restricting the prior Hofvander dataset to explicitly deep-thigh specimens
   leaves4EMCs(11.01-28.88TPM), all above4LGFMS and5MLPS comparators. A
   superficial upper-arm EMC has32.20TPM. This weakens a simple anatomical-
   site explanation; it does not exclude microscopic muscle admixture.
   ASCATpurity is unavailable for all9EMCs. Existing normal-muscle array
   values overlap EMC, so tumor-specific expression is not established.
2. All8 engineered fusion-fibroblast libraries have TMEM266-assigned counts
   (14-109;1.03-9.01CPM within this matrix). These are engineered tBJ/ER
   fibroblasts, not patient EMC; without a fusion-negative control no fusion-
   induction claim is possible. This does not answer tissue localization.
3. Brenca2019 described tumor-rich RNA samples (>70%tumor nuclei), but its
   accessible selected-gene supplementary tables do not include TMEM266.
   Their missing row is not a negative measurement. No complete processed
   patient table was recovered under bounded public access.
4. No qualifying public EMC single-cell/spatial/RNA-ISH/microdissected
   TMEM266 measurement was found in the inspected sources. The epithelioid
   sarcoma and CIVO spatial studies were different histologies; pediatric
   atlas access/EMC identity could not be established. This is a bounded
   search result, not proof that no such material exists anywhere.
5. The original FFPE paper's limited multiplex imaging did not assay TMEM266;
   it cannot localize this target. H&E is not a TMEM266 RNA measurement.
6. Previous Lund datasets have unresolved patient overlap. Do not pool their
   sample counts as independent patients or claim all cohorts fully independent.

Why this changes the next investigation
The lead now has a specific tissue RNA target region and model-locus support,
making localization in EMC cells worth testing. A discriminating next
measurement would pair TMEM266 RNA localization with EMC pathology/fusion
identity and muscle/stromal controls, and separately test upstream coding
regions plus protein. A junction-only assay cannot answer whether a surface
target is made. No wet-lab, outreach or publication action was undertaken.
