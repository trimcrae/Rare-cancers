A separate [reproducible check](coordinator_tie_check.py) shows why a no-ties extremum cannot automatically cover true ties. With two A events before B's sole censor, tied A events give U=2/3, V=2/9 and Q=2; strict ordering gives U=5/6, V=17/36 and Q=25/17. The difference concerns the ordinary tied-event variance. It does not refute the published theorem, which excludes ties. This is a regression case for any proposed relaxation, not an empirical clinical result or a standalone discovery claim.

Published signed-Z extrema also do not generally identify the exact smallest attained Q when the Z hull crosses zero. The hull gives a conservative zero lower bound; discrete feasible values need not attain zero. Any shortcut must preserve this distinction, aggregate-constraint feasibility and actual nonemptiness.
