## Five reader explanations

**Question.** Could an EMC gene-fusion join supply a shared vaccine target, and which missing evidence prevents judging whether that approach is feasible?

**Approach.** The study reconstructed joined RNA, predicted whether its protein fragments could bind immune-display proteins, checked matches to normal proteins, and calculated coverage under different prediction and population assumptions. It then organized ten biological, measurement, material-access and clinical-development limits.

**Main finding.** Five of27 declared exon joins preserved the reading frame and yielded174 fragments. The initial classI screen returned11 distinct binders, four strong. A normal-protein isoform check removed one binder while retaining all four strong binders. Estimated coverage changed sharply with screen settings:27.4% on ten alleles and30.4% on34, with wider threshold changes yielding0% to72.6%. These are predictions, not patient presentation rates.

**Main limitations.** Tumour display, useful abundance, immune recognition, cross-reactivity, safety and benefit were not measured. The lead differs from a normal peptide by one residue, with an unresolved anchor convention. The screen is below its decoy expectation; that does not prove individual calls false. Population dependence bounds do not quantify predictor uncertainty. Bulk and very limited spatial data cannot establish a general cold-versus-excluded tumour phenotype.

**Next step.** Confirm the patient/model RNA sequence and test actual tumour processing and presentation, abundance and immune recognition under the stated controls before inferring vaccine feasibility. Resolving material access and tumour immune context remains separate from obtaining better predictions.
