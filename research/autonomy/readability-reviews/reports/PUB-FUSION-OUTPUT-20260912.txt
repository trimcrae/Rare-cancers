## Five reader explanations

**Question.** Do genes claimed to be targets of NR4A3 fusion proteins show more expression in EMC than a similarly sized arbitrary gene set would, and can that expression establish what the fusion drives?

**Approach.** The study typed published mechanistic evidence, analysed the small available expression cohorts separately, and calibrated gene-set scores against random sets of the same size. It examined comparator choice and other expression confounds, motif counts, and available surrogate binding/accessibility data.

**Main finding.** The pooled set of three fusion-assay genes plus16 native NR4A3 targets reached39% and88% of the two random-set thresholds and exceeded neither; the three genes alone did not meet the four-gene scoring floor. A known EMC expression signature exceeded both thresholds. SEMA3C depended on the comparator, PPARG reused its discovery cohort, and ENO3 survived the expression sensitivity checks but was a pre-designated control rather than an independent discovery.

**Main limitations.** The cohorts contain4,6 and10 EMC cases and have heterogeneous comparators. Expression association does not establish fusion causation. Native or paralogue binding in another disease does not measure fusion occupancy in EMC. The available fusion chromatin study measured accessibility in HEK293T cells; the targeted overlap failed the geometry-preserving null. Two nominal occupancy hits did not survive correction; the across-build selection makes the nominal combined calculation indicative. The bounded search also found an unresolved targeted SRA dataset, not a ready fourth whole-transcriptome cohort.

**Next step.** Obtain fusion occupancy and a controlled fusion perturbation with RNA readout in a verified EMC model. Distinguish these experiments from deeper native NR4A3 data in another disease, the completed targeted accessibility comparison, and any broader future per-arm reanalysis.
