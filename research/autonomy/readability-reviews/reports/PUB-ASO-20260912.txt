## Five reader explanations, independently written

**Question:** Can short molecules designed to bind the RNA join in an NR4A3 fusion avoid matching RNA from the normal parent genes?

**Approach:** The study designed 190 molecules across 38 modeled fusion junctions and screened their sequences against several kinds of RNA and the genome. It separately checked partial pairing through the molecule's central DNA segment and compared the results with artificial junctions.

**Main finding:** At the adopted ten-base cutoff, 87 designs, or 45.8%, matched a mature normal parent. Choosing among designs gave one without such a match for 35 of 38 junctions. But the answer changed with the cutoff, and artificial exon joins had a similar rate, leaving an EMC-specific excess unresolved.

**Main limitations:** Sequence pairing is not measured RNA cleavage or therapeutic selectivity. The ten-base cutoff is a convention; some searches were incomplete, modified chemistry is not captured by the temperature model, and the reported cell-model junctions have not been matched at nucleotide resolution. Delivery, potency, safety and a therapeutic window were not measured.

**Next step:** Establish the actual model's RNA junction before choosing a design. Any subsequent evaluation would need the stated controls and measurements that separate fusion knockdown from normal-parent effects, with enough precision to interpret the result. The paper itself reports no laboratory work.

These explanations are recoverable from the full proposal. Their recoverability does not erase ASO-R1: the abstract alone still asks the nonspecialist to decode the decisive measurement.
