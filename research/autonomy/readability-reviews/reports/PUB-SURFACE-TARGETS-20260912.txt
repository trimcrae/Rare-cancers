## Five questions, answered from the proposals

1. **What is the question?** Can RNA measurements in this rare cancer identify a gene worth examining directly in tumor tissue, and does the answer change when different sarcomas or sequencing years are used for comparison?

2. **What did the authors do?** They tested a previously fixed list of 11 genes using nine EMC tumors and 45 tumors from three other sarcoma types. They compared how often EMC had the higher RNA measurement, repeated comparisons within the same sequencing year, and tested sensitivity to removing patients, cancer types or years. A second dataset provided six EMC and 17 low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma biopsies for a separate comparison. A previously studied RNA marker, CHRNA6, was a control outside the 11-gene selection rule. Normal-tissue and protein observations supplied separate context.

3. **What was the main finding?** CSPG4 alone met the fixed selection rule. It had higher RNA ordering against low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma in both datasets. But the positive combined result depended on the comparison: removing 2019 reversed the within-year summary, and the overall comparison with dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans was not positive. The finding supports a specific tissue follow-up question rather than a general claim that CSPG4 distinguishes EMC from all sarcomas.

4. **What are the main limitations?** Only four distinct EMC patients contributed to the combined within-year comparisons. Sequencing year incompletely represents processing differences. The cohorts are small convenience samples with remaining uncertainty about historical overlap and array lesion stage. Bulk RNA mixes cancer-cell and other-cell signals, and normal-tissue observations do not establish safety. RNA measurements cannot establish accessible protein on cancer cells, diagnostic accuracy or treatment benefit. The fixed threshold organizes follow-up; it is not a clinically validated cutoff.

5. **What should happen next?** Examine CSPG4 protein directly in EMC tissue to determine whether it is located in malignant cells and whether it is accessible, across relevant specimen and technical variation. Diagnostic performance and treatment safety or benefit would require further distinct evidence. No claim that those requirements are already satisfied is made.

The abstract itself supports these five answers without requiring a reader to understand the formula, software versions or archive records. Gene names serve as identifiers rather than unexplained mechanistic arguments. Long sarcoma names remain necessary identifiers and are written out. Technical detail follows in Methods and the SI.

A first naive inline-code scan accidentally spanned a fenced block; a fence-aware comparison confirmed both inline code and fenced blocks are unchanged.

The abstract removes repeated rounded LGFMS values 0.966 and 0.933 and the DFSP value 0.467. Their corresponding full-precision results remain in the body (0.96581, 0.93333 and 0.46667), and the qualitative results remain in the abstract. The array A=1.000 result likewise remains in Results. The abstract's 45 comparison tumors is the retained total 14+13+18, not an added cohort. The detailed pair-score formula and individual matched-contrast denominator remain in Methods/SI. This is relocation of detail, not removal of an unfavorable result.

I specifically checked preservation of the following distinctions:

- Nine primary EMC overall versus three per matched histology and four unique matched patients; matched comparisons do not imply nine matched patients.
- The rule was fixed before the investigator inspected new values, not publicly preregistered and not prospective specimen collection.
- All three rule conditions, the 0.70 threshold and >0.50 direction tests remain unchanged. Year deletion is an interpretation qualifier; the text does not invent a different pass rule to remove CSPG4's original pass.
- CSPG4's positive pointwise intervals do not negate the 2019-deletion reversal. Conditional bootstrap limitations and the degenerate MLPS interval remain explicit.
- Negative or discordant panel findings remain visible, including PRAME, L1CAM, ALPP and cross-platform reversals. CHRNA6 remains outside the candidate pass count.
- The DFSP result is consequential contrary context, although outside the primary rule. Revised diagnoses remain partly expression-informed and not independent confirmation.
- Bulk RNA does not locate protein in malignant cells. HPA normal localization, missing or uncertain stains, antibody ambiguity and the colon-versus-EMC overlap are retained; record counts are not promoted to independent donors.
- Five negative PRAME protein specimens are neither a paired RNA/protein experiment nor proof of universal protein absence or absent peptide-HLA presentation.
- Historical reproducibility numbers and protocols are preserved, with the same-host replay and computational-versus-biological distinction intact. No archival scientific decision is rewritten.

## Readability judgment and remaining boundary

Paragraphs now tell a sequence: why RNA is useful but limited, how comparisons were defined, which result survived the fixed rule, what weakened that result, and which tissue observation would answer the next question. Methods preserve rigor while defining the score and named procedures at first use. The SI is necessarily technical, but its introductory definitions and section organization explain how its records relate to the main claim.

For example, the former opening phrase “comparative molecular characterization” becomes the concrete statement that CSPG4 is worth examining directly in EMC tissue and immediately gives the qualifiers. “Bulk tissue admixture” becomes an explanation that tissue mixes signals from malignant and nonmalignant cells. “Pointwise” intervals are explained as describing one estimate rather than simultaneous coverage of all estimates. These changes improve comprehension without increasing the strength of the conclusions.
