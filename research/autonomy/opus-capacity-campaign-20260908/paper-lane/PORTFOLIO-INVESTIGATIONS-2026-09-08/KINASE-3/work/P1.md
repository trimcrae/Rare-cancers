# PUB-KINASE-LEADS — portfolio investigation

Baseline `git rev-parse HEAD` at start of work: `b6baa1b7cf919a352dc0f61d0bf0c2ab354ae2b4`
(2026-09-09T00:01:16Z). Writes are confined to this directory. No `git add`/`commit`/`push`,
no `preflight.sh`, no subagent, no worktree, no repo copy, no network call.

## 1 · The question

**Every expression argument behind the four kinase leads is of the form "moves the same way in EMC
on both array platforms". How often does a randomly drawn gene do that, at the same strength — and
where do the lead genes sit against that negative?**

This is a target-attribution question with an appropriate negative, not an audit. It is answerable
from committed data alone.

## 2 · Paper-level merit

`PUB-KINASE-LEADS` would claim that four kinase-directed observations specific to EMC exist and were
never followed up. Three of the four already survive only as *transcript* corroboration
(`systems/graph/routes.json` grades RT-RET, RT-SGK1, RT-ALK-HIT), and the corroboration is always
argued the same way: concordant direction across GSE24369/GPL6244 and GSE4303/GPL3290. Two-platform
concordance is the load-bearing inference of the paper's whole computational arm.

That inference has never been given a false-positive rate. `research/modalities/emc-expression-panels.json`
supplies a per-platform genome-wide null (`platforms.<file>.genome_wide_null`) and per-platform random
background draws (`background_reads`, 3000 genes each, unfiltered sampling frame), and
`S53-HYPOXIA-BACKGROUND-READ` (2026-09-02) used the background at *panel* level to refute the
hypoxia/PPARγ separation. **Nothing has ever used it at the level of the joint, two-platform statement
the leads actually make.** Patient relevance is indirect but real: EMC has no targeted agent, and RET
is the lead on which the field's "clinically targetable" language rests (PMID 28423517's rendering of
PMID 24703573). A referee will ask what the concordance is worth. The paper currently has no answer.

## 3 · The exact evidence gap, and what makes it different from completed and held work

| Panel-versus-background null (hypoxia vs PPARγ) | `ndrg1-panel-attribution.json`; S53, 2026-09-02 | **Panel scores**, one series at a time. Refuted a *separation between two panels*; says nothing about a single gene's cross-platform concordance. |
| Grading of the four leads against their primary records | `emc-kinase-leads-source-verification.md`; campaign report `P4-PUB-KINASE-LEADS-paper-step.md` | Verification of prose against records. **No computation, no null.** P4 explicitly did not recompute anything. |

The unfinished input is therefore precise: **an empirical joint null over genes readable on both
series, and the placement of each lead gene in it.**

## 4 · The bounded step taken, and what it found

Two read-only scripts in this directory, both run, both exit 0
(`checks/01-concordance-null/`, `checks/02-sensitivity-curated-frame/`).

**Primary frame** — the 431 genes drawn into *both* platforms' random background samples. Welch t
(EMC − comparator) recomputed per gene from the committed per-sample z values. A gene's joint
statistic is (direction agreement) AND (min |t| across the two platforms); its joint empirical p is
the fraction of null genes that are concordant and at least that strong on their weaker platform.

### ⭐ Result 1 — direction agreement alone is close to worthless

**281 of 431 randomly drawn genes (65.2%) already move the same way in EMC on both platforms.**
Sensitivity frame (the 1981 curated genes placed on both platforms): **65.8%**. The two frames — one
random, one deliberately enriched for genes somebody already cared about — agree to 0.6 points.

Concordance is not near 50% because the two series share the same EMC arm identity and have
correlated comparator arms; whatever the cause, **"higher in EMC on both platforms" is a statement
two out of three random genes can make**, and no lead is entitled to it as evidence without the
strength term. ⚠ Roughly half of that baseline is now attributable, as an upper bound, to sample
composition — see the composition-adjusted supplement below.

> ⚠ **Composition-adjusted supplement (lane `EXPR-COMPOSITION`, 2026-09-09; re-derived by lane
> `KINASE-3`, 0 mismatches over 433 artifact leaves).** Holding a frozen 20-marker tumour/stroma
> composition score constant, the primary-frame direction concordance falls **0.6521 → 0.5668**
> (removing 56.1 % of the excess over 0.5) and the secondary frame **0.6368 → 0.5981** (28.3 %);
> cross-platform Pearson r falls 0.405 → 0.251 and 0.447 → 0.281. The unadjusted figures above are
> retained, not replaced: they remain the correct description of the raw contrast. The residual
> **survives** — against a label-permutation null recomputed *with* the adjustment in place,
> p = 0.022 (primary) and p = 0.0085 (secondary), versus the unadjusted p ≤ 0.0005 — so this is an
> attenuation, **not a refutation**. ⛔ Composition and biology are **not separable here even in
> principle**: in a mesenchymal tumour the stromal programme is partly the tumour's own, so
> adjusting for the score removes some genuine EMC biology too. **0.561 is therefore an UPPER BOUND
> on the composition share, not an estimate of it, and the surviving p values are conservative.**

### ⭐ Result 3 — three things the paper does not currently say

1. **The negative arm of the RET route is better supported than the positive arm.** GFRA2, which the
   route cites as evidence that the receptor's activating module is *depleted*, is the most extreme
   gene in this whole set on the joint scale (p = 0.039) — more extreme than RET itself. The route's
   own hedge is its strongest measurement.
2. **The instrument's ceiling is measured, and it is low.** `NR4A3` — the gene fused in essentially
   100% of these tumours — reaches only p = 0.156. At n_EMC = 6 and 10 on two decade-old archival
   array platforms, **this instrument cannot cleanly resolve even the disease-defining driver.** No
   single-gene transcript reading here can carry a target-attribution claim, in either direction.
   That is a bound on the whole expression arm, not on RET alone.
3. **NDRG1's headline number changes meaning under calibration.** The endpoint's supporting prose
   leans on NDRG1 at the "98th percentile"; that is an *abundance* percentile within an array. Its
   **contrast** placement is signed percentile 88.7 / 85.2 and joint p = 0.088 — about one gene in
   eleven. Both numbers are true and they are not the same statement.

**Honest direction of the result: this is a calibration, and it cuts both ways.** It does not refute
RET or NDRG1 — a 1-in-13 gene is still an unusual gene — and it does not rescue SGK1, ROS1 or the Ku
subunits, which are indistinguishable from random. What it removes is the *unqualified* use of
"concordant on both platforms" anywhere in the paper.

⚠ **The calibration is itself calibrated.** Under the composition adjustment above, NDRG1 — lead 4's
substrate and the largest consistent attenuation among the leads — loses most of its association on
both platforms (r +0.398 → +0.150 on GPL6244, +0.504 → +0.324 on GPL3290), while NR4A3's association
**strengthens** on both (+0.540 → +0.610; +0.373 → +0.474). Neither reading is a refutation of the
joint p values in Result 2, which are computed on the unadjusted statistic and stand as reported;
they say the unadjusted placement of NDRG1 is the less robust of the two to a composition proxy.

## 5 · Artifact · validation · provenance · limitations · stop condition

**Artifact.** `two-platform-concordance-null.json` (this directory) + the two scripts that produce
and stress it. Every field is machine-readable and each lead carries its own joint p and, where a
platform read is missing, an explicit absent-reading note rather than a zero.

**Validation / baseline.** The recomputed Welch t was checked against the statistic the source
artifact computed independently genome-wide (`genome_wide_null.placed_wanted_genes`): **338 shared
genes on GPL6244, max |Δt| = 0.002; 408 shared genes on GPL3290, max |Δt| = 0.001** — i.e. rounding.
The null frame is on the same scale as the placements it is used to judge. Second baseline: the
biased curated frame reproduces the headline within 0.6 points and the lead ordering exactly.

**Provenance.** Sole input `research/modalities/emc-expression-panels.json` (`generated_utc`
2026-08-29T12:51:32+00:00), committed, read at HEAD `b6baa1b7`. Background seeds
`20260829:GSE24369_series_matrix.txt.gz` and `20260829:GSE4303-GPL3290_series_matrix.txt.gz`, both
recorded in the artifact; sampling frame is every symbol any probe maps to, unfiltered, panel members
not removed (the artifact records 338 of 3000 draws overlap the panels, and says so on its face).
No network access, no new data, no GPU, no paid API.

**Limitations.**
- The joint frame is **431 genes**, the intersection of two independent 3000-gene draws from frames
  of 18697 and 14928. A p of 0.039 is 17 genes; a p of 0.074 is 32. Resolution below ~0.01 does not
  exist here. The curated frame (1981) is larger but enriched, so it bounds rather than replaces.
- **No multiple-testing correction, by design** — the null is empirical and the reads were specified
  in advance. Fourteen genes were placed; that is not adjusted for.
- Every value is **transcript abundance**. It is silent on activation, phosphorylation, protein and
  drug response. It cannot address lead 1's "activated", lead 4's IHC, or any IC50.
- Both series are **bulk archival tissue** in a matrix-dominated tumour; tumour content is not
  deconvolved, and the two series have **different comparator arms** (LGFMS/desmoid/fibrosarcoma vs
  DFSP/GIST), which is itself a candidate cause of the 65% baseline concordance and is not separated
  here. ⚠ **Partly addressed, 2026-09-09.** Lane `EXPR-COMPOSITION` held a frozen 20-marker
  tumour/stroma score constant: the primary-frame concordance falls to 0.5668, i.e. **at most 56.1 %**
  of the excess over 0.5 is carried by that proxy, with the residual still surviving at p = 0.022.
  The marker score is **not** a measured cell fraction and no deconvolution was performed, so this
  bounds the confound rather than removing it.
- The comparator-count discrepancy for GSE24369 flagged by P4 (memo says 36, artifact says 29) is
  **unresolved**; this analysis uses the artifact's own `comparator_gsms` and reports n_comparator=29,
  the number the source computations used. It does not adjudicate the memo.
- Nothing here establishes efficacy, safety, selectivity, a therapeutic window, or clinical readiness
  for any agent in any disease, and nothing here is advice about a patient.

**⛔ Bounded no-go inside this lane, with its named dependency.** Lead 4's SGK1 observation is the one
lead whose primary claim carries a denominator (10/10 IHC, PMID 16756948), and the only retrievable
open thread on whether it reproduced in a human model is **PMID 22592656 — paywalled, no PMCID, a
CLOSED route (backlog B9)**. It was **not** retried, proxied or inferred. The consequence for the
paper is stated rather than worked around: whether SGK1 protein reappeared in the same group's human
model is **UNKNOWN**, and the transcript arm computed above cannot substitute for it — SGK1 is
discordant across platforms (joint p = 0.383) and a transcript reading is not a readout of kinase
activity in any case.

**Stop condition (set before running).** Stop once the joint null is built on a frame whose
statistic is validated against the artifact's own genome-wide null, every lead gene is placed or
explicitly recorded as unreadable, and one independent frame either reproduces or breaks the headline
— or stop immediately if the recomputed t failed to reproduce the artifact's t, in which case the
null would be uninterpretable and the finding would be that discrepancy. **Met, on the first branch.**
