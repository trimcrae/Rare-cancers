Four quantities a reader needs are therefore not recoverable and are not present in the abstract: how many samples the limited set contained, how many were positive, which of the three named assays produced the word *activated*, and whether tumour cellularity was controlled (`research/manuscripts/dependency/emc-kinase-leads-source-verification.md:60-79`).

Two later records bound how much weight the sentence can carry. The originating group, four years on and with seven shared authors, wrote that *"the molecular target of sunitinib in EMC is unknown"*, in the same paper whose introduction restates their own RET observation (PMID 29937513, PMC6073125, full text read). The authors did not treat their observation as identifying a target. Separately, a next-generation-sequencing report renders the observation as *"analysis of receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) activity demonstrated elevated expression and activation of RET, a known target of sunitinib"* and elsewhere calls the receptor *"clinically targetable"*, while its own measurement is transcriptome sequencing — mRNA abundance, which is silent on activation (PMID 28423517, PMC5400622, full text read). The qualifier *a limited set of samples* does not survive into that paraphrase. **The demonstrated drift is at one identifier. The total downstream weight is UNKNOWN**: no cited-by instrument is reachable, and the only measured quantity is that a title/abstract co-mention search over PubMed pairs this disease with RET or sunitinib in five records in total, the newest from 2018 (`:148-166`). A co-mention search is not a citation count.

In this repository's own reading of two public expression series, RET transcript is higher in tumours of this disease than in comparator sarcomas on both platforms (+0.4568 SD, 6 versus 29 samples; +1.1067 SD, 10 versus 6), while the GFRα co-receptor and GDNF-family ligand panels are lower on both (co-receptors −0.2989 SD, t = −5.08; −1.2914 SD, t = −4.981) (`research/modalities/census-route-expression-grading.json`). These are transcript-abundance associations on small samples, uncorrected for multiple testing; they neither corroborate nor refute an activation state, which no measurement in the open record addresses.

#### 2.2 A screen hit whose own hit list reassigns it, and an instrument statement that was wrong

A patient-derived cell line of this disease was established and screened against 221 anticancer agents; the report states that three candidates *"demonstrated low IC50 values"* (PMID 40580361, DOI 10.1007/s13577-025-01250-7). **Access state: abstract only** — no PMCID, checked 2026-08-27. The IC50 values themselves, the assay, the concentration range and any comparator line are outside the abstract and are UNKNOWN; *low* is the source's own unquantified word.

Read against this repository's curated extract of the Broad Drug Repurposing Hub, two of the three hits carry the same curated mechanism, a pan-HDAC one (`research/modalities/nr4a3-repurpose-candidates.json`; table at `emc-kinase-leads-source-verification.md:327-336`). The lead had been carried as a single-agent kinase observation; its own hit list contains a within-screen class replication that the single-agent framing could not show. In a public CRISPR panel, neither kinase named on the third agent's curated target list is a dependency in a single one of 91 screened sarcoma lines (mean gene effects −0.040 and +0.112, fraction dependent 0.000 for both), while a class I HDAC is a dependency in 82.4% of those lines — and in 87.8% of everything else, so that class is not selective for this tissue class either (`research/modalities/depmap-sarcoma-dependency.json`). A gene-effect score is a knockout phenotype and an inhibitor's IC50 is not; the line the screen ran on is absent from that panel; none of this establishes what the hit acted on.

**We record here a correction to a statement this programme itself propagated.** Until 2026-08-29 the route grade, its generated view, the census artifact's prose and the memo that verified this lead all asserted that both named kinases have no probe on either expression platform, and therefore that the arrays could never attribute the hit. The artifact that owns those reads states otherwise: one kinase is readable on the Gene ST platform and not on the two-colour platform, the other is readable on **both**, and the three-gene target group reaches full coverage on the Gene ST platform and emitted a score there — lower in tumours of this disease than in comparator sarcomas by 0.2188 SD (t = −2.055) (`research/modalities/emc-expression-panels.json`; `research/modalities/census-route-expression-grading.json`). Only the two-colour platform emits no score, at 2 of 3 genes readable, below the panel's coverage floor. The false sentence was hand-typed prose sitting in the same generated file as the per-gene table contradicting it, and it propagated by copy. **The correction does not turn a null into a positive**: where the kinase is readable it sits below the array median in this disease and level with the comparators. The lead moves from *we cannot see it* to *we can see it on one platform and it is unremarkable there.*

Unlike the other three observations, this one is approximately one year old. It belongs in this series not because the field left it alone, but because its own published hit list points away from the reading under which it was raised.

#### 2.3 An interaction curated on the driver protein, measured once in a tissue this disease is not

The curated evidence connecting a DNA-damage-response kinase to the nuclear receptor that this disease's fusion retains is a single UniProt annotation resting on one primary source. That source measured a complex by co-immunoprecipitation from vascular smooth muscle cell lysates and showed the receptor to be a substrate phosphorylated *"in the N-terminal domain"*, with phosphorylation associated with post-transcriptional stabilisation through prevented ubiquitination (PMID 25852083, DOI 10.1093/cvr/cvv126). **Access state: abstract only** — no PMCID; the body is unreachable. The system is *"Cultured human aortic SMC"*; the words *fusion*, *EWSR1* and *sarcoma* do not appear. The protein measured is wild-type. No residue is recoverable: the abstract localises the site only to a domain, and no body text was obtainable on either of two instruments.

**We correct a characterisation this programme applied to this lead.** The lead was graded as *single-source*. That is true of the **annotation** and false of the **axis**: four primary papers stand on it, and this repository's own memo listed all four on 2026-08-07, two days before the grade was written (`research/manuscripts/dependency/emc-dnapk-nr4a3-lane-assessment.md`; verification at `emc-kinase-leads-source-verification.md:236-256`). Two facts that a *single-source* framing hides then become visible, and both cut against the lead. First, the two smooth-muscle papers **disagree on mechanism**: the 2015 report describes stabilisation through prevented ubiquitination, while an independent group in pulmonary artery smooth muscle reports that the kinase *"affected proliferation by regulating NOR1 protein synthesis"* (PMID 36114572, PMC9479248). A degradation brake and a synthesis effect are different claims about where in a protein's lifecycle a kinase acts, and neither paper adjudicates the other. Second, the two family-level papers are about the receptor **family**, not this paralogue: one states that *"NR4A proteins interact with the DNA-PK catalytic subunit"* (PMID 21979916, PMC3197202 — identifier resolves, full-text body returns empty, so this record is read at abstract level), and the other locates the binding function in a pocket of the DNA-binding domain that all three paralogues share (PMID 30784586, PMC6381605). Nothing in this axis is specific to the paralogue this disease's fusion carries.

The dependency prior is unfavourable and incomplete. Both partner subunits of the same heterotrimer are dependencies in 100% of the **91 screened** sarcoma lines of the public CRISPR panel, with mean gene effects of −1.324 and −1.766. The catalytic subunit itself returned no reading: it carries no column in the release's gene-effect table, measured on two instruments rather than assumed, and its absence is an instrument gap, **not a reading of absence** (`emc-kinase-leads-source-verification.md:190-215`). **We further record a denominator correction.** Both dependency-citing grades in this series originally attributed these figures to 176 sarcoma lines. 176 is the number of sarcoma models in that release; only 91 of them carry CRISPR gene-effect data, and every per-gene row of the source artifact records 91. The percentages and gene effects are unchanged — they were always computed on the screened subset — but the denominator overstated the evidence base by almost double, and the identical error had already been found and corrected elsewhere in this programme before these grades were written (`:376-400`).

#### 2.4 A twenty-year-old series that is unworked rather than refuted, and a substrate reading that is not an activity reading

The oldest of the four observations is also the only one whose published claim carries a denominator. Poulin and colleagues report that *"immunohistochemistry of 10 EMC tumors positive for EWS/NOR1 showed that all of them over-express the SGK1 protein in contrast to non-neoplastic cells in the same biopsies and various other sarcoma types"* (PMID 16756948, DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2006.05.134). **Access state: abstract only** — `isOpenAccess: false`, no PMCID; Europe PMC serves no full-text XML. The comparison is stated to have been made within the same biopsy sections, which is a stronger design than a comparator cohort processed elsewhere. Nothing an immunohistochemistry result is normally graded on is retrievable: antibody clone, vendor and dilution; scoring system and positivity threshold; blinding and reader count; which non-neoplastic cells and how abundant; how many other sarcoma types and at what n; and whether the ten tumours were consecutive or selected (`research/manuscripts/dependency/emc-sgk1-lane-assessment.md:56-97`). The defensible statement is therefore narrower than *10 of 10 with an internal negative control*, and it is the narrower one that this paper makes. The causal arm of the same report is a tetracycline-regulated **rat** chondrogenic line assayed by differential display; nothing in the paper shows the kinase to be required by a cell of this disease.

**The silence is measured, and it is a silence of neglect rather than of refutation.** Forward-citation retrieval returns ten citing records in twenty years, and not one is a follow-up of this kinase in this disease. The field's most recent comprehensive review of the disease, fourteen years later, still lists the kinase among candidates and adds nothing: *"…which have been demonstrated to be transcriptional targets of the oncogenic fusion protein"* (PMID 32967265, PMC7563993, open access, full text read) (`:105-137`). One thread is retrievable and unresolved: the same group repeated the target-gene identification after replacing the rat system with *"an in vitro human cellular model"*, and **the abstract names no gene** (PMID 22592656). Whether the kinase reappeared in a human model is UNKNOWN, and the barrier is a paywall (`:139-160`).

Read by a different modality, the transcript does not corroborate the antibody series. The kinase transcript is **discordant** across the two platforms — 0.1807 SD lower than comparators on one, 0.6156 SD higher on the other. Its canonical substrate is **concordantly higher on both**, by 0.4228 and 0.6998 SD, at the 98.1st array percentile on the larger series (`research/modalities/census-route-expression-grading.json`). **That substrate number is transcript abundance and is not a readout of kinase activity, and we state this as a limitation of the reading rather than as a hedge.** Every published mechanism linking this kinase to this substrate is a phosphorylation of the substrate protein; in one published system, removing the kinase left substrate expression unchanged while its phosphorylation fell (PMID 25200670), and one perturbation moves abundance up while moving phosphorylation down (PMID 19682504). Three kinases have each been shown individually sufficient to carry the substrate's phospho signal in some human cell background (PMIDs 15461589, 31461270, 23581296). An attempt within this programme to attribute the substrate's elevation to a co-elevated transcriptional programme was **withdrawn on 2026-09-02** after the deciding null was recomputed; the elevation is attributed to no programme, and no fraction of it can be assigned to this kinase (`systems/graph/routes.json:6370`; `systems/graph/artifacts.json`). Whether the substrate is phosphorylated at all in this disease is unknown: no such measurement exists for this disease, for any sarcoma, or for any fusion-positive cell.

### Limitations

This paper reports what was measured and what was not. It makes no claim that any kinase named here is a therapeutic target in this disease, that any inhibitor would have activity against it, or that any of these four observations is clinically actionable; it names no agent as a treatment, and it reports drug names only as they appear in the published records it examines. There is no wet laboratory behind this work and no experiment was performed for it: every statement is either a quotation from a retrieved record or a computation over data already public. **Three of the four primary sources are readable only at abstract level** — PMID 24703573, PMID 40580361 and PMID 25852083 have no PubMed Central record, and PMID 16756948 is not open access; PMC3197202 resolves but returns an empty body. Paywalls were respected and not circumvented, so where a denominator, an IC50, a phosphosite or a gene list sits behind one, this paper records it as unknown rather than inferring it. **A missing measurement is unknown, not zero:** citation volume behind three of the four observations is unknown, because no cited-by instrument was available, and the co-mention searches reported here bound a keyword-reachable literature and are not citation counts. The expression readings are transcript abundance on two small public series (6 versus 29 and 10 versus 6 samples), uncorrected for multiple testing, from bulk tumour, and cannot speak to protein level, phosphorylation, or paracrine supply from stroma; a discordance between platforms is reported as a discordance and not resolved. The dependency figures are knockout phenotypes in 91 screened sarcoma lines of a public CRISPR panel, **none of which is a line of this disease**; the one model labelled as such is recorded as not fusion-positive per its curated record and carries no CRISPR data. Where a subunit returned no column, that is an instrument gap and not a finding of non-dependency. Three of the four observations were re-graded during this work and the corrections are reported above as findings rather than smoothed away, including two that were this programme's own errors: a platform-readability statement that was false and propagated by copy, and a dependency denominator that overstated its evidence base by almost double. Finally, the interval since publication differs sharply across the four: three have stood for eleven to twenty years, and one is approximately one year old and is included for a different reason.
