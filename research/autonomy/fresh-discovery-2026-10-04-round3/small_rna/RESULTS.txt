Decision: shelve the standalone paper. No new coordinated mature DLK1–DIO3 miRNA phenotype in EMC has been demonstrated.

Direct EMC miRNA evidence does exist. The ten-case Sápi study measured four selected miRNAs in tumors chosen for reduced SMARCB1 staining. Only one of those markers belongs to the proposed domain, and the public abstract does not identify the marker-level result for each patient. Separate 2013 and 2014 meeting reports describe miRNA profiling of sixteen EMC patients and selected miRNA associations; they likely reuse cases and expose no patient-level domain matrix. These are important prior observations, not findings newly established by this campaign.

The source and sample gate therefore did not support a quantitative domain pilot. All 77 samples in the broader NCI miRNA series were checked at the metadata level, and the original and current S-MED labels were inspected. Neither supplies an authenticated EMC comparison set. Unspecific sarcoma or chondrosarcoma labels remain identity gaps, not negative EMC results. The assigned repository trace extends this evaluation and is recorded separately in the final handoff.

No differential-expression test, domain score, survival model or assay effect estimate was run. Computing such a result from selected published miRNAs or unverified tumor labels would not answer the frozen question. The new arithmetic is limited to source metadata checks: 77 NCI profiles collapse to 73 names after removing four explicitly labelled replicate profiles, and current S-MED header spans sum to 312 despite different published/FAQ corpus counts. These checks are not disease discoveries.

The separate low-DLK1 mRNA result is preserved as contrary evidence to a broad target rationale, but it neither proves nor disproves the mature-miRNA phenotype. No loss-of-imprinting, compensatory-expression, dependency or efficacy explanation is inferred. Finding no accessible matrix in this pass also says nothing about whether the domain is expressed in EMC.

Scientific value: general EMC miRNA signatures and possible prognostic associations already have explicit prior art. A worthwhile addition would need a reproducible, coordinated domain result in authenticated EMC, appropriate myxoid and neural differentiation comparisons, and an interpretation that changes the next biological experiment. A repackaged signature, an analysis of the lone selected miR-381 marker, or failure of our untested hypothesis would not meet that standard.

Reopening requires an actual mature-miRNA measurement matrix or complete published domain panel with EMC specimen identities, all relevant conditions, comparator identities, normalization and technical-replicate information. The sixteen-case underlying data are a specific reopening opportunity if released or located. Shared-author cohorts require an overlap crosswalk before independent validation is claimed. Any reopened analysis must freeze the exact locus annotation, mature arms, sample rules, controls and uncertainty before examining relevant values. All suitable public EMC measurements would then require evaluation, including unfavorable cases.

Promising but unfinished: the underlying sixteen-patient EMC small-RNA profiles may permit a meaningful question if the actual measurements and sample linkage become accessible. At present this is a data-dependent possibility, not a surviving demonstrated finding. No manuscript drafting or external action was undertaken.
