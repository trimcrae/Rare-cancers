Independent review, 2026-10-04. Decision: SHELVE a standalone EMC biological or treatment-response claim from the reviewed sources. This is a source-identity and measurement-scope review, not a new disease finding.

I visually inspected the original retained poster, March slide 18, June slides 13 and 17, and September swimmer image. File inspection occurred at 15:55–15:56 America/New_York, outside the daily restricted period. I independently read the registry and dated report text. No image values were digitized and no response percentage, treatment duration, survival quantity or efficacy estimate was calculated.

1. The source discrepancy is real. The ESMO poster from 17–21 October 2025 has patient 102-001 on the top row of Figure 4, explicitly labelled rectal cancer, with data as of 1 October 2025. March 2026 slide 18 labels that same top-row identifier myxoid chondrosarcoma. June 2026 slide 13 explicitly labels 102-001 extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma, soft tissue, with 1.6 mg/kg escalated to 3.2 mg/kg and an asterisk denoting remaining in study. These labels align visually with the identifiers; the discrepancy is not explained by flattened text or a one-row transcription shift. The inspected sources do not explain whether this is an annotation correction, a diagnostic revision, an identifier error or another cause.

2. Later disease-assessment categories exist and must not be erased. June slide 17 and the updated September swimmer both mark SD on the 102-001 row. The June slide cites a response table dated 14 March 2026; the September release is dated 28 September 2026 and states a 1 September 2026 cutoff and investigator assessment. The September image has patient IDs but no histology column, so its EMC attribution depends on the earlier June crosswalk and inherits the unresolved discrepancy. The defensible description is that later sponsor reports identify 102-001 as EMC and report stable-disease categories. This does not authenticate a new EMC efficacy or mechanistic result, and stable disease alone cannot establish treatment effect in an uncontrolled potentially indolent malignancy.

3. The other myxoid label is not a second verified EMC patient. ESMO and March identify 101-001 as myxoid liposarcoma; the June table uses the less specific myxoid sarcoma. No fusion or pathology evidence resolves it as EMC. Do not count it as an additional EMC observation or silently discard its ambiguous later label.

4. The trial namespace supports reuse across versions, not independent cases. The ESMO poster explicitly names NCT06260774; the registry identifies TTX-MC138-002, a TransCode-sponsored phase 1/2 dose-escalation/expansion study with 16 enrolled participants. The March and June phase 1a sources and September release use the same drug and overlapping patient IDs. No evidence of a new independent patient-ID namespace appears. The registry contains no posted results or individual donor-to-histology crosswalk. It cannot resolve the discordant diagnosis.

5. No EMC-specific pharmacodynamic measurement survives in the reviewed source set. March slides 15–16 assign the phase 0 delivery/target-engagement example to metastatic breast cancer; its reported blood miR-10b reduction and PET/MRI delivery cannot be transferred to EMC. Thyroglobulin observations belong to thyroid-cancer patient 103-003. Aggregate pharmacokinetics, exposure and cohort response categories do not measure EMC miR-10b inhibition. The June EMC-labelled row supplies exposure and later categorical disease assessments, but no EMC tissue miR-10b assay, fusion authentication, measured pretreatment growth trajectory or lesion-level serial record.

Reopening requires a public authoritative correction or authenticated case crosswalk, plus informative EMC-specific measurements and baseline context. Even corrected labels alone would not make these already reported categories new biological knowledge. A worthwhile analysis would still need an explicit new question, relevant comparator, uncertainty, complete EMC coverage and independent scientific-value review. No outreach is authorized or performed.
