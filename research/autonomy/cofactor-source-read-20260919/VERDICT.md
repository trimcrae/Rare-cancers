The useful question is whether a defined factor is required to maintain the fusion's expression or activity. The present EMC expression inputs cannot answer that causal question. Retain the idea as an evidence requirement within the existing fusion-output work; do not nominate RUNX2, create another route or write a paper from this analogy.

Mendes et al.'s retrieved **preprint** combines RUNX2 knockdown and xenograft experiments with prior PAX3::FOXO1 occupancy data and reciprocal expression perturbations. Fusion suppression lowers RUNX2; fusion occupancy is reported at a RUNX2 enhancer. RUNX2 suppression lowers fusion RNA/protein, but this reverse direction lacks demonstrated direct regulatory binding or a reported rescue establishing mediation in the inspected main text. Growth effects therefore do not by themselves prove that fusion loss mediates them. CADD522 has partially discordant effects and acknowledged metabolic off-target possibilities. This supports a mechanistic lead in the studied RMS models, not an EMC dependency or an established selective drug mechanism. Main-text figures 2–6 and Discussion support this bounded reading; linked supplements were not independently inspected. [Primary preprint, DOI 10.1101/2025.07.21.665972](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.07.21.665972).

For EMC, first specify the causal arrows: fusion-to-factor regulation, factor-to-fusion regulation, and the relevant phenotype. A persuasive reciprocal-loop claim would need evidence that distinguishes each arrow from a general change in cell state or viability. Perturbation with suitable controls, temporal ordering, and rescue or equivalent mediation evidence could do this; coexpression alone cannot. Direct occupancy would address physical regulation but would still need functional interpretation. These are claim-specific requirements, not a demand that every computational result must have wet-lab validation.

The retained RT-FUSION-OUTPUT records separate expression portability from fusion causality and identify the unresolved perturbation/occupancy evidence. RT-TXN-CDK addresses a broader transcriptional dependency; RT-PARTNER-STRAT addresses differences between fusion partners. The present source adds a useful reciprocal-regulation question within the first route, rather than a demonstrated new therapeutic route distinct from all three. No current EMC cofactor is established by this reading, and no universal claim about the literature's absence is made.

Reopen for a named EMC candidate with attributable fusion-specific perturbation or regulatory evidence, or a precise source-grounded question that available measurements can discriminate. Reading a relevant newly identified source is sufficient to reconsider admission; new experiments are not the only reopening input. Do not screen expression correlations merely to produce a candidate with a preferred story.
