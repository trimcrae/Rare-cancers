## Why the scientific consequence is substantive

The article explicitly acknowledges overfitting and absence of external survival validation. Criticizing those again would be redundant. The remaining asserted external support is different: lineage specificity and cross-platform baseline comparability. These are used to support interpretation of the proposed three-gene panel and appear in results/conclusions, with the H1FX specificity finding also in the abstract. The audit addresses what that remaining evidence establishes.

First, the cited GSE6481 source describes105 soft-tissue tumor samples and lists ten diagnoses, including19 myxoid liposarcomas, without an EMC group. The published study instead describes19 EMC and128 cartilaginous comparators; S6B carries total147 but labels19 EMC and59 comparators. The figure visually confirms these conflicting labels. The exact source-based ceiling is that the cited accession and published group descriptions do not identify a reproducible EMC lineage comparison. It is NOT established that the authors analyzed myxoid liposarcoma as EMC, used the cited accession, or generated incorrect underlying expression values. A typo/different dataset remains plausible. The19-count coincidence is not evidence of misclassification.

Second, S6C explicitly uses separate within-dataset z-scores before comparing the cohorts. For each nonconstant gene, z(a*x+b)=z(x) for positive a. Consequently, the analyzed values cannot identify cohort-specific location or positive-scale differences removed by that preprocessing. The Mann–Whitney results can describe standardized-distribution differences; they cannot establish the original baseline comparability, assay agreement or normalization validity claimed. Standardization does not force identical distribution shapes or P=1. Non-significance is also not an equivalence test. This limitation follows from the stated method, independently of the unavailable MI-ONCOSEQ matrix, and does not imply that the two platforms are actually discordant.

The single coherent contribution is therefore an evidence boundary: the published external analyses do not presently establish the two particular validation claims attributed to them. This is more consequential than collecting unrelated errors, while narrower than invalidating the exploratory panel or its internal biological observations.

The GSE24369 complement issue is secondary. Two pooled muscle references and benign/intermediate lesions mean the complement is not36 other sarcomas. This warrants precise sample selection, but no material change in any expression contrast has been demonstrated. It should not be inflated into a third independent major result.

## Evidence, novelty and remaining input

The novelty is article-specific scientific clarification, not a new standardization theorem or proof that rare-cancer models overfit. It helps readers distinguish expression detection, histology association, cross-platform comparability and prognostic validation; those are different evidentiary claims. It does not establish a clinical harm, treatment consequence or diagnostic performance difference.

Independent full-source GEO verification is required for the accession facts; its source worker report was pending at assignment, although the recovered full metadata already supports the105-sample description. Exact sample IDs, actual accession/version, probe/gene mapping, external expression values and S6 analysis code remain needed to resolve the underlying lineage analysis. The supplementary schema and root audit establish their absence from the inspected packet, not universal unavailability.

The precise clarification packet should request those reproducibility inputs and invite correction of the cohort descriptions and interpretation of S6C. A response could change the source-discrepancy portion of a comment. It would not make separate z-scoring a test of removed location/scale differences. No misconduct inference is warranted.

The standardization argument needs a short algebraic derivation, not a simulation campaign.
