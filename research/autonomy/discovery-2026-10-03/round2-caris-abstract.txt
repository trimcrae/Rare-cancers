 Background: 
 Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is a rare sarcoma defined by NR4A3 fusions, most often with EWSR1 and less commonly with partners such as TAF15, TCF12, TFG, PGR, and SMARCA2. Although EMC may appear indolent initially, nearly half of patients develop metastases, and some experience early recurrence or death. Morphologic grading provides limited prognostic information, and the impact of non-EWSR1 fusions remains uncertain. These observations highlight the need to characterize secondary genetic alterations that may underlie the clinical heterogeneity of EMC. 



 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 Design: 
 DNA (592-gene or whole exome) and RNA (whole transcriptome) sequencing were performed on patient tumor samples submitted to Caris Life Sciences. NR4A3 fusion positive samples were identified from RNA transcripts. Non-mesenchymal neoplasms were excluded. Only pathogenic or likely pathogenic mutations were considered when determining gene mutation frequencies. Genes with a copy number below an assay-dependent threshold were called deleted. Fisher’s Exact test was used to determine statistical significance. 



 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 Results: 
 In this retrospective study of 33 NR4A3 fusion-positive cases, the median age was 59 years (36-83), and 76% were male. Primary sites were trunk (n=14), lower extremity (n=13), upper extremity (n=2), and other (n=4). Most patients had advanced disease: 79% were stage IV, several had locally advanced or recurrent disease, and few had no evidence of metastasis. Specimens included 18 metastases, 11 primaries, and 4 local recurrences. Fusion partners were EWSR1 (n=20), TAF15 (n=9), TCF12 (n=2), SMARCA2 (n=1), and PGR (n=1). Secondary alterations were identified in 13 of 25 informative cases (52%), more frequent in non-EWSR1 (9/12) than EWSR1 tumors (4/13, p=0.027). Concurrent mutations were enriched in non-EWSR1 tumors (6/13 vs 1/16, p=0.026). Only FBXW7 mutations were recurrent (n=2); SMARCB1, EZH2, PTPRD, FANCL, and WRN were each mutated in single patients. PTEN and ERCC2 deletions were each seen in 3 tumors and CDKN2B in 1. Tumor mutational burden was low: 9 tumors had <2 mut/Mb, 18 had 2-4, and 2 had >4. Loss of heterozygosity varied (median 2, range 0-25), with one tumor showing high-level LOH (≥16). 



 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 Conclusion: 
 Secondary alterations are frequent in advanced-stage EMC and enriched in non-EWSR1 partners. This study outlines the genomic profile of higher-stage EMC and suggests that such alterations may contribute to its heterogeneity. Larger, stage-balanced cohorts are needed to define their prognostic impact.
