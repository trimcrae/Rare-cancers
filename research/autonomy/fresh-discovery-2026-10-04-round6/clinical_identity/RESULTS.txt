R6 CLINICAL FAPI IDENTITY GATE — 2026-10-04

Decision: SHELVE the standalone EMC imaging paper for this bounded lane. No new disease finding was demonstrated, and no authentic EMC scan was identified in the evaluated portions of these five sources. That is not a claim that public EMC FAPI scans do not exist: several individual subtype and access gaps remain unresolved.

The frozen PLAN.json specified the worthwhile potential finding before new outcome values: an authenticated within-patient/within-lesion FAPI–FDG difference or independently verified additional lesion, with protocol, treatment interval and donor overlap resolved. None of the evaluated releases supplies the necessary EMC identity. No numerical EMC effect, significance test, model, or figure was produced. Broad sarcoma uptake, reader agreement, and time-point results are already the original authors' observations; reproducing them would not satisfy the new-knowledge bar.

Actual source evaluations:
1. Naeimi et al., J Nucl Med2023;64:618–622, DOI10.2967/jnumed.122.264941 / PMC11927082. The entire publicly indexed main article and all14 Table1 categories were read. The table accounts for43 patients, including two labelled only sarcoma; each underwent FAPI46 imaging at10min,1hour,3hours. No individual sarcoma subtype/ID or case-linked FDG value is exposed there. The two illustrated patients are esophageal and pancreatic cancers. Keep both sarcoma identities and all three conditions unresolved. Table1, rather than the internally inconsistent prose category summary, supplies the43-person count. Original-byte article retrieval failed through the attempted public routes; the retained source is a dated primary-indexed-text receipt, not an original XML/PDF.
2. Hirmas et al., J Nucl Med2023;64:711–716, DOI10.2967/jnumed.122.264689 / PMID36581374. The actual primary abstract and source-location metadata were evaluated:324 patients,131 sarcoma,237 pairedFDG across all entities, Essen Oct2018–Oct2021. Main/supplemental individual histologies were not obtained; ordinary publisher requests were403. No extrapolation from the broad sarcoma averages to EMC is permissible. This source remains incomplete.
3. Hirmas et al., J Nucl Med2024;65:372–378, DOI10.2967/jnumed.123.266652 / PMID38331453. Original primary Methods explicitly excludes sarcoma, pancreatic cancer and pleural mesothelioma because those cohorts were or would be reported separately. The analyzed115-patient cohort therefore cannot supply EMC evidence. This resolves its eligibility rather than inferring exclusion from an abstract or keyword search.
4. Mei et al., J Nucl Med2023;64:1043–1048, DOI10.2967/jnumed.122.265245 / PMID37230530. All six pages of the original version-of-record PDF were evaluated. Fifty patients from Essen/Bologna include ten generic sarcomas; Table1 gives aggregate demographics, Table5 gives broad entities, and Figure2D gives a generic sarcoma patient with diverticulosis. No EMC-specific subtype or scan crosswalk is supplied. Fifteen observers are readers, not donors. FAPI46 acquisition ranges10–48min; nine sarcoma patients had diagnosticCT. The written guide, four teaching cases and one test case described in the article were not acquired; their identities, overlap and eligibility remain pending, rather than presumed non-EMC.
5. Ma et al., Holistic Integrative Oncology2026;5:74, DOI10.1007/s44178-026-00295-4. All76 individual rows in TablesS1/S2 and every main Table1 histology were evaluated, including malignant and nonmetastatic liver conditions. No EMC label appears. S1:4 is female59 with generic pleomorphic sarcoma, without molecular confirmation or a more specific diagnosis; keep unresolved. The article's11-sarcoma/37-lesion grouping is not transparently reproduced by the histology labels: ten sarcoma/SFT/GIST/chordoma-labelled rows total32 lesions, and the neuroendocrine row adds one patient/five lesions. That arithmetic correspondence is an inference about a source discrepancy, not a corrected classification or new EMC result. Exact source labels and both scan counts were retained unchanged.

Donor independence: Essen papers derive from overlapping registry periods and some use the same ethics approvals; no patient crosswalk justifies pooling them. Naeimi's Heidelberg cases may overlap prior Heidelberg reports. Verified reuse of Gu2022 shows the liver study shares Fudan Shanghai Cancer Center, IRB2004216-25 and a recruitment period overlapping May2020–May2021; no case-level donor independence can be asserted. No study counts were added to estimate unique donors.

Value and limits: no new useful EMC phenotype survives this source gate. The unresolved identities and unavailable source portions prevent a public-data absence claim and would block manuscript advancement. The liver classification discrepancy is not an EMC discovery, and a broad sarcoma comparison cannot substitute for EMC. No inference is made about tumour-cell FAP binding, treatment efficacy, dosimetry, safety, or an EMC therapeutic window.

Reopening conditions: authentic EMC-to-scan/lesion mappings with actual FAPI/FDG values or verified detection outcomes, all relevant conditions, source protocol and overlap resolution; then a fresh prior-art/value assessment and independent challenge before any disease claim. A named cohort alone is insufficient. No unexecuted promising biological pilot is being presented as a finding.

Reproducibility: evaluate_eligibility.py reproduces all76 liver rows, both supplement tables, all14 time-point categories, six-page interobserver source and explicit Hirmas2024 exclusion. eligibility-results.json preserves original labels, both scan-condition counts, source hashes and overlap evidence. The optional --gu-source path is an unchanged R5 input; no old packet was edited. No denied tissue/glycan/WGS/HLA-review scope was read or retried.
