## Finalists and strongest alternatives

**Rank 4 — Trial discoverability.** New result: retrieval failures attributable to molecular eligibility versus disease naming, measured on a frozen, explicitly bounded corpus and held-out diagnoses. User: registry/search developers and sarcoma referral teams. [Existing EMC manuscript](../../manuscripts/care-delivery/emc-trial-reachability.md) supplies candidate cases, but the broad search was truncated and actual free-text EMC search was not tested. Disease-level plausibility is not patient eligibility.

Strong prior work: [TrialGPT, Nature Communications 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-53081-z) already evaluates retrieval, criterion matching and ranking. Another LLM matcher is not a new contribution. Public registry records and eligibility text are obtainable in principle and existing snapshots are committed; a complete current corpus and independent reference labels have not been acquired. Decisive validation compares condition-name, synonym/full-text and molecular-rule retrieval against blinded source-cited labels, retaining hard negatives and uncertainty. Fatal limitation: no credible denominator, no independent labels, or no useful improvement over ordinary search. Estimated one bounded pilot before deciding whether several benchmark cycles are warranted; adjudication costs are additional and not assumed solved.

**Rank 3 — Response recoverability.** New result: distinguish extraction failures from genuinely unrecoverable endpoint definitions and determine which interpretations change after respecting arm denominators, unevaluable patients, confirmation and SD duration. User: evidence synthesists and trial reporters. [Existing corpus/manuscript](../../manuscripts/endpoint/response-endpoint-indolent-tumours.md) supplies 552 arms, but selected complete tables cannot represent all trials; the 2,851-record screen needs independent extraction validation.

[Primary trial NCT04009681](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04009681) distinguishes disease control from clinical benefit with a duration requirement. The parent task's independent challenge additionally identifies FDA guidance explaining why stable disease can reflect natural history; this memo does not infer that DCR is superior. Obtainable inputs: saved registry records and linked protocol/publication material, preserving access failures. Decisive test: prespecified source-document reconstruction including extractor-negative records, independently reconciling denominator, time rule and arm identity. Fatal limitation: a missing-field count with no consequential interpretation or reproducible recovery advance. Estimated one or two pilot cycles before a larger audit. Its attainable validation is an advantage, but does not by itself outrank a successfully validated disease-specific atlas.

**Rank 1 — Therapeutic-address atlas.** New result: reproducible versus cohort-specific target evidence, with explicit normal-tissue limitations, to guide what an experimental group should measure. [Dulken et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38447752/) is strong CHRNA6 tissue prior art; the parent task's source check identifies GSE24369 as its discovery cohort, so that array is not independent validation of the marker. [Completed readiness audit](../../modalities/expression-validation-readiness.md) establishes missing prerequisites in supplied caches, not assay absence. Identified GEO records are available; compatible full matrices, annotation and patient independence are not all established. Decisive analysis preserves all compatible probes and separates cohorts and prespecified contrasts. Fatal limitation: no independent compatible cohort or therapeutic conclusions inferred from RNA alone. Several retrieval/reanalysis cycles are likely; tissue protein validation is outside the present program. This evidence gap makes readiness provisional; the scientific rank remains first because the proposed result directly changes an EMC target-validation decision.

**Rank 2 — External fusion-design benchmark.** New question: do prespecified sequence/design criteria predict measured knockdown and parent sparing across external fusion targets and assay conditions? User: oligonucleotide experimentalists selecting designs. This is a new question-first alternative identified in the parent task's independent biology challenge, not an extension justified by the convenience of existing ASO code. That challenge identifies [PFRED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7822268/), [ASOG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12493213/) and [fusion siRNA optimization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4672813/) as prior art defeating generic enumeration novelty. Public papers are identified; a usable comparable dataset is not verified. Decisive validation requires held-out fusion targets, explicit chemistry/assay strata, simple baselines, negative results and separate knockdown/parent-sparing endpoints. Do not pool ASO and siRNA outcomes as interchangeable. Fatal limitation: unrecoverable or selectively reported paired outcomes. Initial data-feasibility assessment would be bounded; a meaningful benchmark would require several analysis cycles and independent verification. Its rank reflects potential contribution, not readiness.

## New questions and novelty checks

Question-first alternatives beyond the current drafts include the external outcome-calibrated fusion-design benchmark, response decision-error analysis, and a molecular-versus-histology retrieval test. These are proposed contributions, not discoveries. For clinical reconstruction, a decision-error calibration under known ground truth could be considered with explicitly labelled synthetic data; the parent challenge identifies existing IPDfromKM simulation benchmarks, so generic digitization-error simulation is insufficient.

[CAMERA, Wu and Smyth](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458527/) already addresses competitive gene-set testing with inter-gene correlation, limiting novelty of size-matched random sets. [ESCAPE-seq, Nature Genetics 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02268-1) provides experimental peptide–HLA evidence for driver mutations and fusions, raising the standard for the neoantigen reserve. Its engineered assay does not establish endogenous EMC presentation. Exact EMC junction/allele overlap and usable source data remain unverified.
