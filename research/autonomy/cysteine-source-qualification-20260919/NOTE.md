# What a cysteine reactivity reference can test

The next step in AUT-016 needs a more precise outcome than “confirmed unreactive cysteines.” A measured low-reactivity reference can challenge a predictor of relative reactivity under the same assay. It is not a universal nonreactive site, and an unobserved peptide is not a measured negative.

Weerapana et al. compare IA-probe labeling at several concentrations. Figure 1 includes measured high- and low-ratio peptide examples; Figure 2 contrasts low-ratio catalytic cysteines with other cysteines having higher ratios. Both groups are observed labeled peptides. Figure 5 distinguishes active and inactive designed hydrolases, which tests catalytic function in that design experiment rather than universal resistance to covalent reaction. These statements are supported by the primary figure captions reproduced in [PubMed, PMID 21085121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21085121/) and the paper's [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09472).

The [CysDB primary publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37119813/) separately describes identification, intrinsic reactivity and ligandability. An identification-only entry or lack of competition by one compound must not be substituted for the relative-reactivity outcome. We did not inspect its full published methods or numeric tables in this checkpoint.

For this project, an admissible future reference would retain the measured ratio or a source-defined reactivity category, probe and concentration pair, biological preparation, residue/peptide identity, replicate information and missing-value rules. A compound-competition endpoint would answer a different question. Sites without a quantitative observation stay missing. A matched external test would also need a fixed mapping from structural inputs to that outcome, with calibration separated from evaluation; it cannot set its cutoff on the same positive control and call the result validation. These are our requirements for a prospective test, not claims that this checkpoint has assembled such a dataset.

The existing S56 result remains adverse: removing competitors opened the reach corridor, but the size-matched null showed that the opening did not demonstrate useful selection. No criterion was fitted, no reach analysis rerun, and no NR4A engagement or selectivity claim changed. The older IC3 accession-coverage read is a separate result and does not answer generic assay qualification.

Keep the covalent route blocked and defer the generic calibration campaign. Reopen for a specified prediction task with compatible structural inputs and a quantified, assay-defined reference that can test it. Public datasets can be useful; new experiments are not the only possible reopening evidence. A generic collection of presumed negatives would not resolve the current route question.
