## Judgment and exact contribution

Yes: the two analyses support one coherent, narrow empirical diagnostic-methods report. Its question is whether apparent robustness survives explicit control of training support and score-summary definitions. In a fixed public sarcoma dataset, equalizing class-specific training budgets reduces the supplier-versus-sample Brier difference from 0.0803 to 0.0211; separately, reference-fitted score sharpening increases threshold assignments while a discordant zero-vote case and class weighting expose limitations of favorable aggregate losses. A combined short report is more coherent than separate papers for partitions, temperature scaling, macro loss and provenance recovery.

## General facts versus observations

- General mathematical/statistical facts: positive temperature transformation preserves within-profile class ordering; numerical thresholds on distinct score scales need not be equivalent; proper losses need not move together; changing class weights changes the target distribution; a zero target vote makes unsmoothed log loss unbounded. These are explanatory premises, not newly discovered results.
- Dataset-specific observations: size of the matched-support attenuation; 44 to 115 threshold assignments with one discordance; profile-weighted versus equal-class NLL changes; epsilon-dependent sign of the smoothed NLL change; and sample 262 becoming an above-threshold discordance. These are the measured contribution. The epsilon result is appropriately framed as a sensitivity to representation of the observed zero-vote failure, not as a general discovery about classifiers.
- Neither supplier associations nor classifier concordance establish clinical accuracy or biological discovery. The draft retains these boundaries. Its 820/821 discrepancy, array/patient distinction and absence of E-MTAB-9875 predictions are properly preserved.
