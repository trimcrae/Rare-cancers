## Independent plain-language account

**Question.** Does a public cancer-data export still attach each reported rearrangement to the sample named in the original study workbook?

**Approach.** The authors compared every rearrangement row in the two fixed files, keeping the workbook's order and tracking its sample ID, worksheet row and two gene names. They then checked the mapping with a separate program reading the original workbook. They also tested what happens when someone uses the original identifiers to retrieve records for one sarcoma subtype. Gene-name differences were investigated separately.

**Main finding.** The export identifiers follow event position plus three instead of the original sample identifiers. Of 3,771 event records, 3,770 carry a different identifier; the original records belong to 3,182 source IDs. Two events from one source sample can therefore appear to come from two export samples. Searching this export with the 75 original EMC IDs retrieves 28 events from other source samples and misses all 76 events belonging to the EMC group. A recovery resource restores the workbook identifiers while preserving the export's other fields.

**Main limitations.** This is evidence about the relationship between fixed data files. It does not verify the original diagnoses, biological fusions, germline/somatic origin, a live website, patient outcomes or downstream papers. Both extraction programs use the same reader family. The recovery follows the demonstrated source-order correspondence, not a recovered original curation script. Three gene-text discrepancies remain unresolved, and the other twelve have current naming support rather than independent biological validation.

**Next step.** Give maintainers the reproducible mapping for reconciliation and verify any resulting corrected export. Do not treat the offline derivative as an already approved or deployed replacement.
