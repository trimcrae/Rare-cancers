# EMC junction catalogue

This catalogue identifies the ASO sequences that score best in a stated comparison
with normal parent-gene RNA. It covers every junction in our four fixed-format
design tables. It preserves the evidence for each junction and every tied choice.

**Start with [the sequence catalogue](CATALOGUE.md).** The complete numerical data
are in [all-designs.tsv](results/all-designs.tsv). These are our computational
designs, generated by placing a 16-base window at five positions across each
reference junction and taking its reverse complement. No oligos were synthesized.

## What the complete comparison establishes

There are 43 target junctions and 215 designs. A separate annotation-error control
adds one junction and five designs. Of the target junctions, five match deposited
fusion sequences, one has a reconstruction from reported coordinates, and one has
a reconstruction from an engineered-construct description. The remaining 36 exact
reference joins are hypothetical. A published fusion name or exon label alone does
not establish their exact sequence.

The normal comparison now includes 85 records representing 78 distinct sequences
across seven parent genes. It preserves the earlier six-gene corpus and adds eight
PGR records, including seven curated RefSeq accession versions. Every design is
compared with the same corpus, including records from genes other than its parents.

Compared with one archived canonical transcript for each of the same seven genes,
the expanded isoform set reveals longer gap-spanning matches for 23 of 215 designs.
It changes the set of best positions under that measure for nine of 43 junctions.
The final comparison yields one choice for 27 junctions and tied choices for 16.
No design has a complete 16-base match within this corpus.

For example, the centred TCF12 exon-5/NR4A3 exon-3 design has a longest match of
seven bases in the canonical-transcript comparison and eleven in the expanded
comparison. Moving the binding position by one base gives an eight-base longest
match in the expanded comparison. That shifted design also has greater full-window
sequence difference than the other position tied on the primary measure. Its
sequence is `GGCATATCCATCAGAT` (5′ to 3′).

Adding PGR is a separate change in gene coverage: it increases the longest match
for 50 designs relative to the frozen expanded six-gene corpus. This result is not
an isoform-only effect. Both comparisons remain visible in the design table.

## How a choice is made

1. Find each design's longest continuous exact match to normal RNA that covers
   all six proposed DNA-gap positions. Prefer the design with the shortest of
   these maximum match lengths.
2. Among tied positions, prefer the one whose closest normal 16-base window has
   the most mismatches. This searches every complete window, not just those found
   by the first measure.
3. Keep all remaining ties. Also retain positions that offer a competing trade-off
   between the two measures, and all five original designs.

This ordering was specified in [the analysis plan](analysis-plan.json) before the
complete run. No biological cutoff or weighted score was fitted to the results.
The main table retains the primary ties even when the second measure breaks them.
Ten junctions have competing alternatives, showing that the priority assigned to
the two measures can matter. The file label `pareto_alternative` means that no
other position is at least as good on both measures and better on one.

## Scope and interpretation

“Best” means best by this declared sequence comparison among five positions with
one proposed 5-LNA/6-DNA/5-LNA architecture. The normal corpus covers seven genes,
not the human transcriptome. It contains reference RNAs, not measurements of their
expression in particular tissues. This comparison does not include unspliced RNA,
bulges, gapped alignments, accessibility, delivery or experimental cleavage.
Neither the metric ordering nor the proposed chemistry has been biologically
validated. A zero gap-run value would mean no complete exact six-base gap match,
not no complementarity. Absence of a complete 16-base match does not establish
selectivity. Earlier genome/transcriptome screens remain separately dated and are
not used as ranking gates here.

The method is not new: sequence enumeration and splice-variant-aware off-target
searching are established, for example in
[PFRED](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0238753). The contribution is an
inspectable EMC catalogue connecting exact reference sequences, source evidence,
binding position and normal-parent matches. Our unpublished design tables and
their extension form one project. The source crosswalk and its unresolved cases
are retained in the [accepted junction evidence](../2026-09-26/evidence/results/junction-evidence.tsv).
In particular, USZ20 is a reference reconstruction rather than a patient RNA
consensus, and USZ22 remains unresolved.

## Reproduction and data definitions

Run `python analyze_catalogue.py` from any directory. It uses only Python's standard
library and the saved inputs listed in [input-manifest.json](input-manifest.json).
It writes only this candidate's `results` directory. Use `--output DIRECTORY` to
write to another location. Run `python verify_catalogue.py` for the independent
window-by-window checks and boundary tests. This does not rerun unrelated old
analyses. The analysis itself checks all 40 prior expanded comparisons and all
190 primary-atlas baseline values.

`capture_pgr.py` records the small online retrieval separately and refuses to replace
an existing capture. Network access is unnecessary for reproduction. Its captured
URLs, times, response hashes and accession selection are under `sources/`.

- [Junction table](results/junction-catalogue.tsv): exact 30+30-base reference
  context, coordinate conventions, evidence classes, source URLs, unresolved
  ambiguity, tied choices and competing positions. Historical unversioned Ensembl
  labels remain explicitly unversioned; the pinned sequence hashes identify the
  actual inputs.
- [Design table](results/all-designs.tsv): all five positions per junction, ASO
  and target sequences, the paired corpus comparisons, both ranking measures,
  primary/final choice flags and trade-offs. `donor_bases` describes the forward
  target RNA window. All sequences use the DNA alphabet, with T standing for U.
- [Longest-match locations](results/gap-match-locations.tsv) and
  [closest full windows](results/nearest-normal-windows.tsv): every tied site and
  accession alias. Starts are zero-based in forward mature RNA; mismatch positions
  are one-based within the aligned target window.
- [Normal reference manifest](results/normal-reference-manifest.tsv) and
  [FASTA](results/normal-reference-transcripts.fasta): all included reference records
  and sequence hashes. Identical sequences retain separate aliases but count once
  in the distinct-sequence total.
- [Numerical summary](results/summary.json) and
  [independent verification](results/verification.json): counts and actual checks.

This is a research dataset, not a new manuscript or a submission-readiness claim.
