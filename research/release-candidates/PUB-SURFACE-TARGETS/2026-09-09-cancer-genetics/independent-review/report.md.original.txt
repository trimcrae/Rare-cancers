## Scientific assessment and continuity

The central claim remains modest: within a fixed 11-gene universe and specified histologies, observed CSPG4 RNA ordering motivates assessment of malignant-cell protein localization. The title, abstract, conclusions, highlights and cover letter retain the small matched cohort, comparator dependence and sequencing-year fragility. They do not claim diagnostic validation, malignant-cell surface protein, therapeutic efficacy, normal sparing, a universal EMC marker, or a new validated target. The revised title's use of enrichment is bounded by the explicitly defined rank estimand, not presented as a fold change.

The limitations that most restrict this claim remain prominent and substantive:

- Nine RNA-sequencing EMC and six array biopsies are small convenience samples; the three matched comparisons each use three EMC and their union is only four unique EMC patients.
- Removing 2019 reverses the matched CSPG4 composite to approximately 0.433. The observed overall matched result therefore cannot establish batch robustness.
- The marginal DFSP contrast is nonpositive, approximately 0.467, which defeats an interpretation of general sarcoma specificity.
- The bootstrap is conditional on observed histology/year strata, with fixed singleton cells and potentially narrow or degenerate intervals. It does not supply population-level precision or rescue the year reversal.
- Historical independence is incomplete, bulk-cell origin and protein accessibility are unmeasured, and the separate normal-expression sources cannot establish a therapeutic window.

These are major limits on generalization, but they do not contradict the narrowly worded descriptive finding. I would not require a new wet-lab campaign or a rerun of unchanged analyses simply to make this report scientifically admissible. A journal may nevertheless decide that the limited contribution needs more evidence to meet its significance threshold.
