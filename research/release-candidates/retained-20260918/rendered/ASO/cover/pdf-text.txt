Please consider "Junction and register choices in a computational catalogue of NR4A3 fusion gapmer 
designs" as a Full-length research article in Computational Biology and Chemistry.
This computational catalogue asks how exact RNA-junction and register choices change the sequence 
matches relevant to designing NR4A3 fusion gapmers. It records 190 designs across 38 modelled 
junctions. A one-base register change produces an eleven-base normal-NR4A3 match through the 
DNA gap; a different acceptor requires a different sequence. These are concrete design distinctions, 
not measurements of biological selectivity.
The adopted ten-base criterion classifies 87 designs as matching mature parent RNA, but artificial 
exon joins meet the same criterion at a similar rate. We retain this negative control and the cutoff 
sensitivity without claiming an EMC-specific liability excess. Most modelled junctions are unreported 
in patients. The released sequence records support conditional design inspection; no cleavage, potency, 
delivery, safety or therapeutic window has been measured.
The article fits the journal through its computational design and analysis of nucleic-acid interactions. 
The manuscript includes the full author declarations. The research was self-funded, with no external

interest.
