The development of a fistula between the gastric conduit and airway after transthoracic oesophagectomy for oesophageal cancer is a rare complication seen in only 0.8% to 3.9% of cases. However, death from septic shock and/or respiratory failure occurs in up to 57% of patients [ ].
Risk factors for neo-oesophageal-airway fistula (NEAF) include intraoperative tracheal injury, postoperative mediastinitis, tumour recurrence, radiation necrosis, erosion injury and ischaemia of the gastric tube [ , , ]. The fistula connects the airway to the stomach, which has been pulled up into the thorax and reconstructed to replace the oesophagus. Gastric juices and contents flow into the tracheobronchial tree through the fistula, causing various symptoms such as coughing, chest pain, recurrent pneumonia, sepsis, life-threatening haemoptysis and/or respiratory failure. Upfront surgery (S) is the best option for rapidly closing the fistula but carries a mortality rate of up to 32% [ ]. The nonoperative management (NOM) of NEAF relies on drainage, antibiotic therapy and endoscopic procedures including stent implantation to cover 1 or both fistulous orifices. Fistula closure may be most durable with covered metallic stents and when the fistula is at the level of the bronchial stem [ ]. Despite the high recurrence rate of up to 39%, stenting may provide sufficient time to prepare the patient for surgery [ ]. Thus, 3 treatment strategies exist: NOM only, S and NOM followed by surgery (NOM + S). Data on mid- and long-term outcomes with these 3 strategies are needed to determine whether one is superior to the others.
To obtain such data, we conducted a systematic literature review and a meta-analysis of studies in patients with NEAF. One-year survival was the primary outcome chosen to compare NOM, upfront surgery and NOM + S. The secondary outcomes were resumption of an oral diet and fistula recurrence. Finally, we assessed whether fistula location was associated with 1-year survival.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
This systematic review and meta-analysis were carried out according to the Meta-analysis of ObservatiOnal Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) statement [ ]. One author (Thibaud Bertrand) identified the articles, used the abstracts or full texts to select articles that had none of the exclusion criteria and extracted the data from the selected articles. Doubtful cases were discussed with another author (Olaf Mercier) and selected or excluded by consensus. Both Thibaud Bertrand and Olaf Mercier are thoracic surgeons.

## Search strategy
A computerized literature search was performed to identify relevant studies in the PubMed, EMBASE and Web of Science databases, from the inception of each database to July 2021. The free text search terms were (‘airway gastric fistula’ OR ‘tracheobronchial fistula’ OR ‘gastrotracheal fistula’ OR ‘esophagobronchial fistula’ OR ‘bronchial gastric fistula’ OR ‘thoracogastric airway fistula’ OR ‘tracheo gastric fistula’ OR ‘tracheoesophageal fistula’ OR ‘respiratory digestive fistula’ OR ‘digestive system fistula’ OR ‘digestive tract fistula’ OR ‘respiratory system fistula’ OR ‘gastrointestinal fistula’ OR ‘respiratory digestive system fistula’ OR ‘gastric fistula’) AND (oesophagectomy OR esophagectomies OR ‘oesophagus surgery’ OR ‘oesophagus resection’ OR ‘gastrointestinal surgery’ OR ‘upper gastrointestinal surgery’ OR ‘digestive system surgery’) AND (‘oesophageal cancer’ OR ‘oesophageal neoplasm’ OR ‘oesophagus carcinoma’ OR ‘upper gastrointestinal cancer’ OR ‘gastrointestinal neoplasm’ OR ‘respiratory cancer’ OR ‘digestive cancer’ OR ‘gastric cancer’) NOT (‘bariatric surgery’ OR ‘gastric bypass’ OR ‘weight loss surgery’ OR ‘obesity surgery’ OR ‘stomach bypass surgery’ OR ‘bariatric procedure’). Initial selection of papers was done by the first author. For eliminating a paper, the validation of a second reviewer was mandatory. The references of each accepted articles were also searched manually by the first author. We did not contact authors to request further information.

## Study selection criteria
Figure  depicts the selection process. Each article retrieved using the search terms was screened based on the title and abstract. Exclusion criteria were as follows: abstract only, reviews, editorials and letters to editors; malignant NEAF; absence of oesophagectomy and esophagogastric anastomosis; fewer than 5 patients; no information on follow-up duration; and NEAF not the main focus (i.e. no detailed description of the causes, treatment and outcomes of NEAF). If >1 study from the same institution was found, we included only the study with the largest number of patients. Only studies published after 2000 were included.
 

## Data extraction and study outcomes
The following were extracted from each included article: general data including the first author, year of publication, country, study period, study design, sample size, age and sex distribution; primary tumour histology; main symptoms of NEAF, time from oesophagectomy to diagnosis and NEAF location; whether tracheal self-expandable metallic stent implantation was performed as part of NOM; and 1-year mortality, resumption of oral intake and fistula recurrence. We assessed only those variables reported in at least 40% of the articles. NOM was defined as the exclusive use of medical, endoscopic or interventional treatment. S was defined as the exclusive use of surgical treatment. NOM + S was defined as a two-step strategy with NOM treatment in bridge to S treatment.
The primary outcome was 1-year post-treatment mortality. The secondary outcomes were resumption of an oral diet and fistula recurrence. To assess outcomes, we used only data from individual patients or subgroups meeting our inclusion criteria. Since in the relevant articles detailed data was present, we were able to provide the information needed for patients involved in our meta-analysis. We assessed the quality of each study using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Case Series Results ( , ) [ ].

## Statistical analysis
We used both a fixed-effects model and a random-effects model to compute summary proportions. Due to the considerable data heterogeneity, double arcsine transformation of proportions was performed to stabilize the variance [ ]. The data are presented as forest plots with point estimates and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Both random-effects and fixed-effects summaries are reported. For the results, only the more conservative statistical approach (the random-effect estimator) was used for making inferences. Funnel plot analysis and the Egger’s test were used to assess publication bias. To assess the role of 1 study in the global result, influence analysis was performed by calculating pooled estimates omitting 1 study at a time. All calculations were performed using the R program version 4.2.2 (package Meta v7.0.0).

## RESULTS

## General characteristics of studies and patients
Of 177 identified studies, 17 were selected for inclusion as shown in the study flowchart (Fig.  ). All 17 studies used a retrospective design. The PICO classification is provided in , . They were all case series or little retrospective cohort associated with a low level of evidence. Eight studies were carried out in China, 3 in Germany and 1 in each of the following countries: France, Japan, Sweden, Switzerland, the Netherlands and the USA.
A total of 316 patients were included in the 17 selected studies. For 3 studies, we excluded a total of 14 patients, who did not meet our inclusion criteria: 3 patients studied by Li et al. [ ] had tumour recurrence as the main cause of fistula formation, 4 patients studied by Elbe et al. [ ] did not have primary oesophageal cancer and 7 patients studied by Rosskopfova et al. [ ] did not have oesophagectomy for oesophageal cancer. This left a total of 302 patients for our study.
Table  reports the general characteristics of the 17 studies. Of the 302 patients, 181 (7 studies) had NOM, 23 (2 studies) upfront surgery and 98 (8 studies) NOM + S. Mean patient age ranged from 55.9 to 65.1 years and the mean proportion of men from 3% to 52%. The primary type of oesophageal tumour was reported for 75 patients and was adenocarcinoma in 40 patients, squamous-cell carcinoma in 31 patients and other types in 4 patients (Table  ).
 

## Clinical presentation of the fistula
The presenting symptoms were described in only 9 articles (191/302 patients). Cough triggered by oral intake or by lying down was the most common presenting symptom, with 191 patients (Table  ). The other most common symptoms in order of decreasing frequency were pneumonia, dyspnoea, fever and need for reintubation.
 

## Diagnostic modalities and time from oesophagectomy to diagnosis
The diagnostic procedures comprised various combinations of bronchoscopy (83% of patients), gastroscopy (63%) and computed tomography (60%). The mean time from oesophagectomy to fistula diagnosis was reported in 9 articles and varied from 0.30 ± 0.10 to 13.75 ± 7.16 months.

## Fistula location
Location of the fistulous orifice in the airway was reported for 15 studies with 248 patients. The trachea was affected in 136 patients, including 43 with carinal involvement. Of the remaining 112 fistulas involving 1 main bronchus, it was specified that 64 involved the right main bronchus and 51 the left main bronchus.

## Treatments and outcomes
Fistula location dichotomized as involving the trachea vs a main bronchus did not differ across therapeutic strategies (Table  and Fig.  A). NOM was used in 181 (59.9%) patients, of whom 77 resumed an oral diet and 18 experienced fistula recurrence. self-expandable metallic stent implantation within the tracheobronchial tree was the most common NOM modality, with 143 patients. The other modalities were silicon stenting, imbricated airway stenting, oesophageal stenting, double airway and oesophageal stenting, drainage and sealant application.
 Upfront surgery was performed in 23 patients. Fistula repair was with autologous fascia-lata tissue, pericardial flap, perforator-based intercostal-artery muscle flap, or colonic tissue. Of the 23 patients, 7 resumed an oral diet and 2 experienced fistula recurrence.
NOM + S consistently involved stent implantation as the NOM modality. Among the 98 patients treated by NOM + S, 45 resumed an oral diet and 21 experienced fistula recurrence.

## Associations of treatment strategy with mortality, oral diet and fistula recurrence
Figure  B is the forest plot of mortality after fistula treatment which was reported in all 17 papers. Overall, the mean 1-year mortality was 53% (95% CI, 36–69%) and differs significantly according to the treatment strategy (P value of the interaction test, 0.0343, random effects model): mortality was lower with NOM + S (33%; 95% CI: 17–48%) than with NOM (68%; 95% CI: 39–97%) or upfront surgery (67%; 95% CI: 36–98%).
Figure  C is the forest plot for resumption of an oral diet. Overall, the mean proportion of patients who resumed an oral diet was 50% (95% CI, 37–63%). The proportion did not differ significantly across treatment strategies (P value of the interaction test, 0.52, random effects model): for NOM + S, 45% [95% CI: 29–61%]; for NOM, 53% [95% CI: 30–77%] and for surgery alone, 64% [95% CI: 35–92%].
A summary table of main results of the meta-analyses of our 3 main end-points is proposed in , .
Table  reports data on fistula recurrence, which were available for 16 of the 17 studies. The considerable variability in the proportion of patients with fistula recurrence (none in 5 studies and up to 60% in the remaining studies) did not allow a meaningful meta-analysis.
 

## Publication bias and sensitivity analysis
The possibility of publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots for 2 end-points: death and proportion of patients who resumed an oral diet. Visual inspection of these plots did not revealed large asymmetry. However, if the linear regression test of Egger for funnel plot asymmetry revealed no asymmetry for the proportion of patients who resumed an oral diet (t = 1.15, df = 14, P = 0.2684), it suggested some asymmetry for proportion of deaths (t = −3.20, df = 15, P = 0.0060) (Fig.  ).
 Finally, we carried out a sensitivity analysis through influential analysis, using the random effect model whose results are provided in , . We did not find any particular studies generating significant heterogeneity in the pooled estimates.

## DISCUSSION
In this systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 observational cohort studies conducted between 2006 and 2021, 1-year mortality after the treatment of NEAF varied significantly according to the treatment strategy. Thus, 1-year mortality was halved with NOM + S compared to NOM or surgery alone. Neither fistula recurrence not resumption of an oral diet differed significantly across the 3 treatment strategies.
Our pooled data indicated similar 1-year mortality with NOM and with upfront surgery. In contrast, in their review of 24 studies (89 patients, 19 case reports), Li et al. found a longer median survival with upfront surgery than with NOM [ ]. However, some patients in the surgery group initially received non-surgical treatments: no NOM + S group was individualized. Moreover, only 23 of the 89 patients underwent surgical repair.
Our data seem to support NOM + S, with NOM being used to optimize patient health before performing surgical fistula repair. However, upfront surgery may have been required as a rescue procedure in critically ill patients. This would have resulted in worse outcomes with surgery only. Also, NOM alone may have been used in some patients who were too ill to undergo surgery. Again, this would have worsened outcomes in the NOM group. Without knowledge of the criteria used to select patients to each treatment strategy, no firm conclusions can be drawn.
Among NOM modalities, the most often used was endoscopic stenting. Caution is in order with double gastric and tracheal stenting, which can increase the size of the fistula [ ]. Moreover, stenting may promote fistula formation: of 6 patients with anastomotic leakage treated by oesophageal fistula in 1 study, 5 developed a fistula to the airway [ ]. To limit stent-induced injury, we suggest stenting of the airway only, combined with mediastinal drainage, followed by stent removal during surgical fistula repair.
Upfront surgery may be the best option in patients with partial or total gastric-conduit necrosis, a large fistula, uncontrolled mediastinitis, or life-threatening consequences of the fistula. However, surgery requires high catecholamine doses, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation and prolonged postoperative endotracheal mechanical ventilation. Moreover, nutritional rehabilitation must be delayed after surgery for NEAF. All these factors may delay fistula healing. Thus, surgery should be viewed as a rescue procedure reserved for critically ill patients.
Previous NOM allows surgery in a patient whose health status has improved. Procedures of greater complexity can be used, and lasting fistula healing is more likely to occur. NOM before surgery should include weaning off endotracheal mechanical ventilation, correction of nutritional deficiencies and the treatment of inflammation and infection. Antibiotic therapy is generally required to treat airway, pulmonary and mediastinal infection. The importance of sepsis control has been highlighted previously [ ]. Weaning off the endotracheal tube has been reported to promote airway healing [ ]. Nutrition should be provided either enterally through a jejunostomy or gastrostomy or parenterally.
Neither the resumption of an oral diet nor fistula recurrence differed significantly across treatment strategies in our study. However, the small number of events may have limited our ability to detect significant differences. Moreover, definitions varied across studies.
Many surgical procedures for NEAF repair have been described. To date, the optimal procedure is unclear. Nevertheless, use of a perforator flap has major advantages including provision of an adequate blood supply, tension-free posterior airway wall closure, material-free airway repair and resistance to digestive secretions. The versatility and plasticity of perforator flaps allow any fistula site to be reached. Moreover, the flap fills the mediastinal space with no risk of airway or digestive-tract compression.
A major strength of our study is the use of a quality checklist. In addition, we included only studies with at least 5 instances of this rare event, done in high-volume centers with greater experience. Our statistical analysis was done to a high standard. We excluded 14 patients from 3 studies to limit population heterogeneity. The large sample size of 302 patients improves the reliability of the data compared to earlier reports.
Our meta-analysis must be interpreted with great caution due to the clinical material upon which it was built. Besides the fact that this meta-analysis was not registered in the PROSPERO registry, the 17 studies included in the meta-analysis all have a retrospective observational design, are monocentric and have highly variable sample sizes, often low. Furthermore, they report research works conducted over a period of 20 years, during which various elements of medical or surgical management may have changed. Finally, some publications do not report data on important points such as primary tumour histology, tumour stage, tumour location, neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatments, surgical approach for oesophagectomy, fistula formation mechanism, time elapsed between oesophagectomy and fistula diagnosis, ventilatory support modalities, nutritional status, or the presence or absence of infection. From a statistical point of view, the direct consequence of these facts is significant inter-study heterogeneity reflected by an I2 statistic always higher than 50%. Therefore, since we couldn't find reasons explaining this heterogeneity, the average effect of the treatment proposed by the meta-analysis should only be considered indicative.

## CONCLUSION
Methodologically, it is essential in future studies concerning this rare condition to better report predictive to identify the situations in which each treatment strategy is likely to be most appropriate. Therefore, we propose a NEAF reporting format ( , ) to allow better comparability of studies, and the possibility of combining them in a conclusive meta-analysis. Clinically, the results of the meta-analysis suggest that NOM to prepare the patient before surgery may provide the best outcomes compared to each of the 2 treatments prescribed individually, but unless the reasons for inter-study heterogeneity are well understood, the effect of the treatment on new subjects will be hard to predict.
