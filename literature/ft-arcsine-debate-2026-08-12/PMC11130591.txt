## Purpose
The increase in thyroid cancer incidence has inevitably led to an increase in thyroid cancer surgeries. This meta-regression analysis aimed to determine if the rate of post-thyroidectomy complications changes by year.

## Materials and Methods
PubMed and Embase databases were used to perform a systematic literature search of studies published from January 1, 2005, using the keywords “thyroidectomy” and “complication.” A meta-regression was performed for post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia and bleeding.

## Results
This meta-analysis included 25 studies involving 927751 individuals. Through the years of publications in this study, there was no significant difference in the proportion of post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia and bleeding (p=0.9978, 0.6393).

## Conclusion
Although the number of thyroid surgeries has recently increased, the incidence of post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia and bleeding did not significantly increase.

## INTRODUCTION
The incidence of thyroid cancer increases yearly. According to the annual cancer statistics report in South Korea, only 12649 patients were diagnosed with thyroid cancer in 2000. By 2019, this number had quadrupled to 30676. The increase in thyroid cancer incidence has various causes, the leading ones being the early diagnosis of thyroid cancer and the development of high-resolution ultrasound diagnostics. 
The increase in thyroid cancer incidence inevitably led to the rising number of thyroid cancer surgery cases. From 2008 to 2021, in the Thyroid Cancer Center of Gangnam Severance Hospital, the number of thyroid cancer surgeries increased from 601 to 2861. As the number of thyroid cancer surgeries increases, the importance of complications after thyroid surgery also becomes greater. The increasing number of surgical cases may also lead to increased post-thyroidectomy complications, which may be the basis for negative opinions about the rise in thyroid cancer surgeries.
Complications after thyroidectomy include hemorrhage, hypocalcemia, and recurrent laryngeal nerve injury. Complications in thyroid surgery are not common, especially when experienced surgeons perform the surgeries. However, even if the complication rate is low, complications of thyroidectomy should not be overlooked as they reduce the patient’s quality of life and are sometimes life-threatening. This meta-regression analysis aimed to determine if the rate of post-thyroidectomy complications changes by year.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
Our systematic review was based on the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) statement.

## Search strategy
PubMed and Embase databases were used to perform a systematic literature search of studies published from January 1, 2005 to December 31, 2022, using the following keywords: “thyroidectomy” and “complication.” Two authors (Lee JS and Lee YS) conducted an independent selection process. Any discrepancy was resolved through discussion.

## Selection criteria
The following were included in this meta-analysis: 1) studies about post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia (permanent) or bleeding; 2) prospective or retrospective original articles; 3) articles published in English; 4) studies where all patients underwent thyroidectomy (unrestricted in surgical procedures) with or without lymph node dissection; 5) studies where all patients with post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia required periodic calcium supplementation; and 6) studies where all patients with post-thyroidectomy hemorrhage required reoperation. The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) reviews, letters to the editor, commentaries, and editorials; irretrievable articles; animal studies; and articles whose full text was not in the English language; 2) studies including patients with only parathyroid surgery; 3) studies including patients with a limited method of surgery; and 4) studies not including neither post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia nor bleeding.

## Data extraction and quality assessment
The two authors extracted relevant data from the included articles using the standardized form. Author, year of publication, country and region, research design, number of cases, incidence, and the corresponding data were recorded independently.

## Statistical analysis
Pooled proportions with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using the fixed- and random-effects models. Heterogeneity among studies was identified visually using a forest plot and quantified statistically using the I2 index. In the forest plot, the common effect model meant the fixed-effect model. Publication bias was evaluated using Egger’s regression test and/or a funnel plot. In addition, a meta-regression was performed to see the trend of each complication according to the year of publication. All statistical analyses were performed using the R package, version 4.1.3 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). p-value<0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. The meta-analysis was performed by applying the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation considering the bias due to the low proportion. Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation is a way to provide stability at more extreme proportion values than basic arcsine transformation. 

## RESULTS

## Study characteristics
Following systematic retrieval from the databases, 10969 studies were initially included in this systematic review. Next, the titles and abstracts were scanned carefully, and 10709 reviews, case reports, letters, and irrelevant studies were excluded. The full texts of the remaining 260 articles were carefully evaluated, and 25 studies that met the inclusion criteria were included in this meta-analysis (927751 patients). Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia occurred in 11603 of 179773 patients, and post-thyroidectomy hemorrhage was observed in 9091 of 751489 patients. ## DISCUSSION
Post-thyroidectomy complications are infrequent, especially when an experienced surgeon performs the procedure. Complications include hemorrhage, hypocalcemia, and recurrent laryngeal nerve injury. Nevertheless, the potential for significant morbidity is present due to the anatomical feature of the thyroidectomy operation site. In this study, the analysis was conducted focusing on hypocalcemia and bleeding.
This study’s meta-regression analysis showed no increase in post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia and bleeding through the years. Through the years of publications in this study, there was no significant difference in the proportion of post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia and bleeding (p=0.9978, 0.6393). These can be explained by several reasons except the development of surgical skills of surgeons with the increase in the number of surgeries. One reason could be the use of new surgical instruments. The thyroid gland is highly vascularized. Thus, achieving precise hemostasis is essential to avoid serious complications, especially post-thyroidectomy bleeding. Technological innovation allowed surgeons to use energy devices to improve the methods of dissection and coagulation. This made the surgical procedure simple regarding the adequacy of hemostasis and reduction of operative times. These positive aspects of energy devices are thought to have played a role in preventing complications despite the increased number of thyroid cancer surgeries.
The change in guidelines for surgical extent may also be a reason for no increase in complications. In the 2015 ATA guideline, hemithyroidectomy was widely recommended than the bilateral thyroidectomy compared to the previous guideline. The extent of prophylactic central compartment lymph node dissection was also changed from bilateral to unilateral in the 2009 ATA guideline. Moreover, the ratio of papillary thyroid microcarcinoma (PTMC, tumor size <1 cm) in newly diagnosed patients has increased, and this shift may be due to the increasing use of neck ultrasonography and early diagnosis. The combination of change in guidelines and increase in PTMC could be a reason of this study’s results.
The emergence of minimally invasive surgery could be another reason. Conventional thyroidectomy has been a standard of care for thyroid cancer; however, various minimally invasive approaches have been developed for cosmetic purposes. Contemporary minimally invasive surgical techniques include the following: robotic trans-axillary thyroidectomy, bilateral axillo-breast approach endoscopic thyroidectomy, bilateral axillo-breast approach robotic thyroidectomy, transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy via vestibular approach, and transoral robotic thyroidectomy. These advanced surgical procedures are safe alternatives to conventional thyroidectomy, with the advantage of avoiding a disfiguring scar on the neck. 
This study had some limitations. First, this study only analyzed hypocalcemia and bleeding among the post-thyroidectomy complications. A meta-analysis of recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy is also needed in future studies. Second, patient characteristics, such as age and sex ratio, were not fully included due to the limitation of the reference studies. Additional meta-regression was performed on the age and female ratio to correct the heterogeneity between studies, but no relevance was found. Therefore, it seems necessary to collect data in a further study to find the cause of heterogeneity. Finally, the number of articles included in the meta-analysis of this study was relatively small. A large-scale analysis including several articles is needed in an additional study.
In conclusion, although the number of thyroid surgeries has recently increased, post-thyroidectomy hypocalcemia did not significantly increase. Moreover, post-thyroidectomy bleeding significantly decreased in meta-regression analysis. The increase in the number of thyroid surgeries did not cause an increase in postoperative complications.
