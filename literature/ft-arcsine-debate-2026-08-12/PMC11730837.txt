## Abstract

## Background:
To determine the efficacy of 4-phenylbutyrate (4-PB) or sodium 4-phenylbutyrate (SPB) in treating diseases caused by genetic mutations.

## Methods:
We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and EMBASE for studies of patients with genetic mutations treated with 4-PB or SPB. All data were tested using RStudio software.

## Results:
4-PB or SPB corrected the “functional” production of mutant genes (0.88 [95% confidence interval {CI}: 0.73–1.00], P = .45, I2 = 0%), restored mRNA transcription of mutant genes (0.50 [95% CI: 0.18–0.82], P = .13, I² = 47%), and improved symptoms (0.89 [95% CI: 0.78–1.00], P = .99, I² = 0%) and biochemical laboratory values (1.00 [95% CI: 0.89–1.00], P = .11, I² = 33%) in patients with inherited genetic diseases.

## Conclusion:
4-PB or SPB can be used to treat genetic diseases. However, they must be validated in high-quality randomized controlled trials before clinical use.

Keywords: 4-PB, efficacy, misfolded proteins, mutation, SPB

## 1. Introduction
Genetic mutations result in misfolded, unfolded, and incomplete folded proteins that mislocate or aggregate in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). This phenomenon includes the ER stress response.[ , ] Misfolded proteins remain in the ER and are degraded and cleared by ER-associated degradation pathways to maintain a stable intracellular environment, preventing proteins from reaching the cell surface.[ ] A misfolded protein is not transported (ER retention) and cannot be transported to its functional area (desired site of action).[ ] The ER’s quality control system ensures that only correctly folded proteins reach their final destinations. The protein is misfolded, unable to acquire or maintain its bioactive conformation.[ – ]
The pharmacological chaperone 4-phenylbutyrate (4-PB) acts as a chaperone to rescue mutant proteins; it helps proteins fold and acquire natural or near-natural conformation to restore their function. This agent improves the plasma membrane expression of proteins affected by genetic diseases.[ – ] For example, detecting 4-phenylbutyric acid (4-PBA) in cell experiments restored the correct intracellular localization of dysferlin missense mutations in 25 of 64 patients.[ ]
In summary, after reading many research articles, it has been found that 4-PB has the potential to treat inherited gene mutation diseases. Therefore, this meta-analysis aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of 4-PB-related drugs in treating inherited gene mutation diseases.

## 2. Methods
This meta-analysis was registered with PROSPERO (ID: CRD42023403003). The meta-analysis was performed per the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) Statement 2020 guidelines.[ ]

## 2.1. Literature retrieval
We reviewed the results of in vivo human trials evaluating the efficacy of 4-PB or SPB in patients with inherited genetic mutations. Literature searches were conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and EMBASE. At the same time, we searched the regional database CNKI and found no clinical trial literature related to oral 4-PBA-related drugs for the treatment of patients with gene mutations. Using medical subject headings (MeSH) and the keywords: “4-phenylbutyric acid,” “buphenyl,” “NaPB,” “sodium phenylbutyrate,” “4-phenylbutyrate,” “ammonaps,” “mutation,” or “variation.” We performed advanced searches of terms for drugs (4-PBA, buphenyl, NaPB, sodium phenylbutyrate, 4-PB, ammonaps) and mutation/variation combinations.
We also reviewed reference lists of retrieved articles and reviews to identify other trials that met the primary and secondary outcome indicators. Because 4-PB is converted into 4-phenylacetate (4-PA) in the liver,[ ] the effect of 4-PB might be mediated by 4-PA; therefore, studies of 4-PA were included.

## 2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria
The studies were imported into NoteExpress literature management software for de-duplicating. We read the titles, abstracts, and full texts to determine that the studies met the selection criteria.
Inclusion criteria were as follows: all cases were diagnosed as inherited genetic mutation diseases; interventions must include the administration of oral 4-PB/4-PA or SPB therapy. Exclusion criteria were as follows: duplicate articles; in vitro and non-human experiments; systematic reviews and traditional reviews; inability to extract data; poor quality of articles and unknown outcomes.

## 2.3. Data extraction and quality evaluation
Two researchers extracted and evaluated the quality of the retrieved literature in a mutually blind manner, cross-checked the data, and drew a table to extract data, including first author, publication year, study design, patient characteristics, drug use, and outcome evaluation. In cases of disagreement, a consensus was reached by discussion or consultation with a third party. Finally, 12 observational studies and 4 experimental studies were included.[ – ] See the meta-included studies 1 to 16 of Supplemental Files 1–16, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O135, which illustrates studies that were included. No randomized controlled trials met the primary outcome and were treated with 4-PB or SPB; therefore, we only performed the meta-analysis on other studies.
Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) is a commonly used quality assessment tool for case-control studies and cohort studies (retrospective cohort study, prospective cohort study) in observational studies. The quality evaluation of the NOS is divided into 8 scoring item standards in 3 significant modules (comparability, selection, and outcome). The NOS “star system” for semi-quantitative principles is rated 9 stars (9 points).[ ] The quality score of the 12 included articles was estimated according to the NOS.[ ] The quality evaluation scores are displayed in Table .
 According to the research quality evaluation checklist formulated by Kmet et al,[ ] we evaluated the quality of the included studies (“Yes” = 2, “part” = 1, “No” = 0 [not applicable]; total score = [“yes” quantity × 2 + “part” quantity × 1]/[28–“not applicable” quantity × 2] × 100).[ ] The quality scores of the included experimental studies is shown in Table .
 

## 2.4. Data analysis
RStudio software was used to perform a meta-analysis of the effectiveness of 4-PB or SPB in treating patients with inherited genetic mutations. Statistical analysis was performed using meta-analysis management software (RStudio). See the analytic code of Supplemental File 17, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/O136, which illustrates the meta-analysis process by RStudio. The I2 test analyzed the heterogeneity of the included results. I2 describes the percentage of variability attributable to heterogeneity. Studies with I2 < 25% had no heterogeneity, those with I2 between 25% and 50% had low heterogeneity, and those with I2 between 50% and 75% had moderate heterogeneity. If I2 <50% or P > .05, the studies had no heterogeneity, and the fixed-effect model was selected for the combined analysis. Otherwise, a random effect model was used for the combined analysis. Egger’s linear regression was used to determine whether there was publication bias. Because the funnel plot results from visual inspection, there is a subjective bias. Egger’s test is quantitative to determine whether there is publication bias and whether the results are accurate and reliable.
A meta-analysis of a single proportion requires that the distribution of a single rate should obey normal distribution as much as possible. The R language meta package metaprop function provides five sample rate estimation methods: “PRAW” (Raw, i.e., untransformed proportions), “PLN” (log transformation), “PLOGIT” (Logit transformation), “PAS” (Arcsine transformation), and “PFT” (Freeman-Tukey Double arcsine transformation). In this paper, 5 methods were transformed for each outcome (Table ), and the method closest to the normal distribution was selected according to the test results. See Table , which illustrates each outcome indicator’s sample rate estimation method.
 

## 2.5. Sensitivity analysis
RStudio software was used for sensitivity analysis to determine whether the analysis results were robust. One study at a time was excluded to determine whether a single study caused heterogeneity in the group, and the remaining studies (n–1) were combined for meta-analysis. By observing the changes in the combined results, the results of the meta-analysis were evaluated to determine whether there were significant changes in the results.

## 2.6. Retrieval results
The literature search ended on February 1, 2023. A total of 2512 articles were retrieved from PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and EMBASE by manual and computer retrieval. After the duplicate studies were excluded using NoteExpress software, 975 studies remained. Based on the title and abstract, 296 articles that did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded, and 679 articles remained. Finally, after reading the full text, 16 studies remained. The flow diagram of literature screening is shown in Figure .
 

## 2.7. Basic characteristics of the included studies
We included 2512 articles, and 16 studies and 79 subjects were obtained after screening and exclusion. Fifty-seven patients with inherited genetic mutations were treated with SPB, 19 with inherited genetic mutations were treated with 4-PB, and 3 with 4-PA. Follow-up periods ranged from several months to several years. The serum biochemical prognostic indexes of Byler’s disease were evaluated using liver function tests. Nitrogen homeostasis was measured as a proxy for ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency (OTCD). Serum biochemical prognostic indexes of sickle cell anemia were assessed using the F-reticulocyte percentage. Plasma ammonia was used as an index of citrullinemia type 1. Hemoglobin concentration was used as an index of Cooley’s anemia. In addition, 4-PB, a maximum tolerated dose of 20 g, was defined based on minimal adverse effects, safety, and statistically significant induction of chloride transport (maximum at day 3), as reported in investigational literature. There is no added benefit to taking 30 g daily and accumulating phenylacetate in the plasma.[ ]
Many studies have reported that 4-PB and SPB have few side effects. Most patients reported no severe side effects in this meta-analysis; most reported side effects were relatively mild and confined to the gastrointestinal tract. Only 1 patient reported jaundice, most likely related to the patient’s portal hypertension.[ ] Collins et al[ ] reported erythroblastosis, spinal cord compression in 1 patient and deep vein thrombosis in another. In all patients, the drug was administered gastrointestinal. The essential characteristics of the included studies are shown in Table .
 

## 3. Meta-analysis
Mutations usually have an adverse effect, and a mutant DNA stop signal cannot be transcribed to produce mRNA, or the mutant gene is retained in the cell (such as the ER). It cannot have a stable structure/conformation in its functional position after abnormal transport, ultimately leading to a defect of the mutant gene mRNA or protein.[ ] The mutant gene results in reduced mRNA synthesis and the abnormal transport cannot reach the functional site to form a “functional” (protein) production. In addition, genetic central dogma suggests that genetic information is transcribed from DNA into mRNA and translated from mRNA into protein.
For example, ABCB11 (PFIC2) and ATP8B1 (PFIC2) mutation/deficiency induces decreased BSEP protein expression and function; that is, mislocalization and intracellular retention of BSEP.[ ] mRNA expression and plasma membrane trafficking are the mechanisms that increase the cell surface expression of 2 types of mutant BSEP (ABCB11, ATP8B1).[ ] OTCD is a disorder of ornithine transaminase (OTC) activity caused by mutations/deletions in the OTC gene. Severe β-thalassemia is also known as Cooley’s anemia. β-Thalassemia is caused by mutation/deletion of the β-globin gene, which leads to disorders of β-globin gene mRNA transcription and β-globin peptide chain synthesis. Citrullinemia type I is an argininosuccinate synthase (ASS) deficiency caused by mutations/functional defects in the ASS gene. The inherited glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) defects result from mutations in the X-linked gene PIGA that result in decreased transcription (mRNA) of PIGM and a decrease in cell-surface GPI.[ ] Sickle cell anemia is the formation of single-gene DNA sequence variation/mutation/defect in the β-peptide chain of hemoglobin. Alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency is caused by a homozygous mutation of alpha-1-antitrypsin (a1AT) deficiency, which is mislocalized into hepatocytes rather than correctly localized into blood and body fluids.[ ] Urea cycle disorders (UCDs) include OTC deficiency and ASS deficiency.
Therefore, this meta-analysis of studies on the effect of 4-PB or SPB in patients with inherited genetic mutations was divided into 2 primary and 2 secondary outcomes. The 2 primary outcomes were “functional” production and mRNA transcription. The secondary outcomes were clinical symptoms and serum biochemical tests. Our collected data for 4 outcome indicators: “functional” production, mRNA transcription, clinical symptoms, and serum biochemical tests.

## 3.1. Meta-analysis of 4-PB or SPB for “functional” production of mutant genes
The “functional” production of mutant genes in patients with inherited genetic mutations before and after 4-PB/4-PA or SPB treatment was analyzed in 8 studies. 4-PB/4-PA or SPB corrected the “functional” production of mutant genes. The type of effect size was using the PRAW measure. The recovery rate of “functional” production was (0.88 [95% CI: 0.73–1.00], P = .45, I2 ＝ 0%). Figure A shows the forest plot. There was no heterogeneity between studies in the “functional” production of the mutant genes, and a fixed effects model was used.
 

## 3.1.1. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
All effect sizes were within the 95% confidence interval (95% CI: 73%–100%) of the combined effect size after excluding a single study. This finding indicates that the meta-analysis results are not likely to change significantly with the number of studies. Therefore, the stability and reliability of the studies are good, and there is no need to eliminate some included studies. The sensitivity analysis is shown in Figure B. Egger’s linear regression test showed that P = .9300, P > .05, suggesting no publication bias. The results are shown in Figure C.

## 3.2. Meta-analysis of mRNA transcription of mutant genes by 4-PB or SPB
Four studies analyzed the mRNA of mutant genes in patients with inherited genetic mutations before and after 4-PB/4-PA or SPB treatment. Either 4-PB/4-PA or SPB corrected mRNA transcription in the mutant genes. This primary outcome’s type of effect size was using the PAS measure. The recovery rate of mutant genes mRNA transcription was (0.50 [95% CI: 0.18–0.82], P = .13, I² = 47%). Figure A shows the forest plot. The testing outcome with the fixed/random effects model is shown in a forest plot.
 

## 3.2.1. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
All effect sizes were within the 95% CI (18%–82%) of the combined effect size after excluding the single included study. The sensitivity analysis is shown in Figure B. Egger’s linear regression test showed that P = 1.0000, P > .05, suggesting no publication bias. Egger’s linear regression is shown in Figure C.

## 3.3. Meta-analysis of 4-PB or SPB alleviating symptoms with inherited genetic mutations
Nine studies analyzed the effect of clinical symptoms in patients with inherited genetic mutations before and after 4-PB or SPB treatment. 4-PB and SPB alleviated symptoms associated with inherited mutations. The remission rate of clinical symptoms in patients with inherited mutations was 0.89 (95% CI: 0.78–1.00, P = .99, I² = 0%). Figure A shows the forest plot. This secondary outcome’s type of effect size was using the PLN measure.
 

## 3.3.1. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
All effect sizes were within the 95% CI (78%–100%) of the combined effect size after excluding the single included study. The sensitivity analysis is shown in Figure B. Egger’s linear regression test, P = .0278, P < .05, suggests publication bias. Egger’s linear regression is shown in Figure C. Different types of studies and large differences in the total number of subjects caused publication bias. Only 1 of the 9 articles had a clinical symptom remission rate that was not 100% in the included studies. The trim-and-fill method was used to evaluate the impact of publication bias on the results. Figure D shows the funnel plot after the trim-and-fill method, funnel plot after the trim-and-fill method is relatively symmetrical.

## 3.4. Meta-analysis of 4-PB or SPB improved serum biochemical tests in patients with inherited genetic mutations
Thirteen studies analyzed the effect of improved serum biochemical tests in patients with inherited genetic mutations before and after 4-PB or SPB treatment. 4-PB or SPB improved serum biochemical tests in patients with inherited genetic mutations. This secondary outcome’s type of effect size was using the PFT measure. The improvement rate of serum biochemical tests in patients with inherited genetic mutations was 1.00 (95% CI: 0.89–1.00, P = .11, I² = 33%). Figure A displays the forest plot.
 

## 3.4.1. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
All effect sizes were within the 95% CI (89%–100%) of the combined effect size after excluding a single included study. The Egger’s linear regression test showed that P = .1967, P > .05, suggesting no publication bias. Sensitivity analysis is shown in Figure B, and Egger’s linear regression test is shown in Figure C.

## 4. Discussion
This meta-analysis publication interval is extensive and covers several countries. Sixteen studies on 4-PB/4-PA or SPB intervention in patients with inherited genetic mutations were included. Information and data were extracted and combined for analysis. The meta-analysis heterogeneity of all outcomes was within the acceptable range. 4-PB/4-PA and SPB are safe and effective in treating patients with inherited genetic mutations, providing a basis for treating patients with inherited genetic mutations. Moreover, 4-PBA was mostly applied to children and found to have fewer side effects in the included research literature. Given that many genetic disorders manifest early in life, the results of this study are generalizable to pediatric populations with genetic mutation diseases.
The limitations of this meta-analysis are as follows. Due to the limited clinical trial studies, the relevant randomized controlled trial literature could not be retrieved. So, the quality of the included studies needs to be improved. Most included studies were observational and did not strictly follow the principle of randomized double-blinding. Furthermore, the sample sizes were small. Large sample, multi-center, randomized, double-blinded trials are needed to validate the safety and efficacy of 4-PB/4-PA or SPB in patients with gene mutations. Only 8 mutants were included in this meta-analysis; all 8 mutations belong to genetic mutation diseases of the same species. Because we could not retrieve more useful literature. The study included inherited genetic mutation diseases: PFIC, UCD, OTCD, sickle cell anemia, Cooley’s anemia, and citrullinemia type 1. ABCB11 was the most common mutation included in the study.

## 5. Conclusion
SPB (SPB/NaPB) is the salt form of 4-PB, and 4-PA is the liver metabolite of 4-PB. 4-PBA has been approved for treating UCD to eliminate hyperammonemia,[ , ] activate fetal hemoglobin transcription, and treat sickle cell anemia and β-thalassemia.[ ] Although 4-PB/4-PA and SPB are rarely used in clinical practice in patients with inherited genetic mutations, many cell and animal studies confirmed their efficacy. This evidence-based medicine meta-analysis confirmed its therapeutic prospects. Table describes the results of the meta-analysis of 4-PB/4-PA, SPB in treating genetic mutation diseases. Although meta-analysis occupies the top of the evidence pyramid and is the highest level of scientific evidence, large clinical trials are needed to demonstrate its safety and efficacy. A deeper understanding of 4-PB/4-PA or SPB will provide an evidence basis for the clinical treatment of harmful inherited diseases.
