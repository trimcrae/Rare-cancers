## Abstract

## Aims
Several mechanisms have been identified in the aetiopathogenesis of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). Among these, coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) may play a key pathophysiological role. We performed a systematic review and meta‐analysis to investigate the prevalence, echocardiographic correlates, and prognostic implications of CMD in patients with HFpEF.

## Methods and results
A systematic search for articles up to 1 May 2023 was performed. The primary aim was to assess the prevalence of CMD. Secondary aims were to compare key echocardiographic parameters (E/e′ ratio, left atrial volume index [LAVi], and left ventricular mass index [LVMi]), clinical outcomes [death and hospitalization for heart failure (HF)], and prevalence of atrial fibrillation (AF) between patients with and without CMD. Meta‐regressions according to baseline patient characteristics and study features were performed to explore potential heterogeneity sources. We identified 14 observational studies, enrolling 1138 patients with HFpEF. The overall prevalence of CMD was 58%. Compared with patients without CMD, patients with HFpEF and CMD had larger LAVi [mean difference (MD) 3.85 confidence interval (CI) 1.19–6.5, P < 0.01)], higher E/e′ ratio (MD 2.76 CI 1.54–3.97; P < 0.01), higher prevalence of AF (odds ratio 1.61 CI 1.04–2.48, P = 0.03) and higher risk of death or hospitalization for HF [hazard ratio 3.19, CI 1.04–9.57, P = 0.04].

## Conclusions
CMD is present in little more than half of the patients with HFpEF and is associated with echocardiographic evidence of more severe diastolic dysfunction and a higher prevalence of AF, doubling the risk of death or HF hospitalization.

## Discussion
The main findings are summarized as follows: (a) 51% of patients with HFpEF have impaired CFR; (b) patients who have reduced CFR have a more severe diastolic impairment (i.e. a higher E/e′ ratio and LAVi); (c) patients with HFpEF and CMD have a higher prevalence of developing AF; (d) reduced CFR in patients with HFpEF is associated with a doubling of the hazard of death or hospitalization for HF compared with HFpEF patients with normal CFR (Central illustration).
As HFpEF is characterized by a heterogeneous pathophysiology and clinical presentation, CMD itself is a heterogeneous disease that can be divided into two different endotypes: structural and functional CMD. The former is caused by inward remodelling of coronary arterioles or capillary rarefaction and is characterized by reduced CFR in response to a non‐endothelium‐dependent vasodilator. The latter is caused by endothelial dysfunction with impaired vasodilation in medium‐ and large‐sized arterioles when myocardial oxygen demands increase and is characterized by limited vasodilatory response to intracoronary acetylcholine.
The prevalence of structural or functional CMD or both in HFpEF patients was 58%, whereas the pooled prevalence of structural CMD (i.e. impaired CFR) only was 50%, suggesting that structural microvascular dysfunction is the primary type of CMD in HFpEF patients. Compared with the invasive measurement of CFR, non‐invasive methods showed greater heterogeneity. This finding could be the result of the inherent diversity among the analysed non‐invasive methods (i.e. echocardiogram, PET, and MRI), as well as their dependence on adequate imaging windows, which are frequently suboptimal in the setting of HFpEF due to concomitant lung disease and obesity.
In 2013, Paulus and Tschope proposed a new paradigm according to which CMD constitutes the pathophysiological link between co‐morbidities‐related pro‐inflammatory state and myocardial remodelling and dysfunction, resulting in HFpEF. The natural consequence of CMD is the occurrence of areas of chronic ischaemia and fibrosis. Several studies have proposed that repeated subclinical episodes of micro‐ischaemia cause focal areas of fibrosis, ultimately promoting diastolic dysfunction. , At the same time, as most of the coronary flow occurs during diastole, impaired lusitropy and diastolic dysfunction may attenuate coronary flow, leading to impaired myocardial perfusion. Hence, as the coronary flow is strictly dependent on myocardial relaxation, it is challenging to ascertain the causality between ischaemia and diastolic dysfunction. Taqueti et al. attempted to solve this dilemma by demonstrating that CMD predicted an increased risk of HFpEF hospitalization, apparently as the ‘primum movens’ in the pathophysiological process. In summary, the pathophysiological mechanisms behind CMD‐related HFpEF probably lie on three pillars, feeding each other in a vicious cycle: inflammatory‐mediated reduction of NO bioavailability, structural changes in the microcirculatory bed (i.e. capillary rarefaction and fibrosis), and diastolic dysfunction.

## Coronary microvascular dysfunction and echocardiographic variables
We analysed echocardiographic variables that represent indirect indicators of elevated left ventricular filling pressures and are included in two clinically validated score‐based algorithms (H2FPEF and HFA‐PEFF), both of which are recommended by the ESC guidelines. An increased E/e′ ratio represents the most robust marker of LV filling pressure, as it is less, but still, influenced by other variables, such as age, sex, changes in volume, or AF. Again, an increased LAVi is a marker of chronic elevation of left atrial pressure and an indirect surrogate of LV filling pressure, showing a close association with other echocardiographic indices of LV diastolic function. Pathologic LVMi was associated in previous studies with increased LV stiffness and diastolic dysfunction, presenting with low sensitivity (26%), as confirmed by various clinical trials that revealed its absence in more than one‐third of patients. According to our results, the presence of CMD seems to be associated with greater diastolic dysfunction in terms of higher E/e′ ratio and LAVi values, but with similar LVMi values. It is likely that interstitial fibrosis in patients with CMD‐HFpEF increases myocardial stiffness, but does not increase LVMi, because CMD usually does not lead to increased left ventricular hypertrophy. We hypothesized that the combination of increased E/e′ ratio and LAVi, together with a LVMi in the range of normality could be the echocardiographic phenotypic presentation of CMD‐HFpEF. Incidentally, this echocardiographic phenotype, along with a higher prevalence of AF, resembles the HFpEF subtype characterized by atrial myopathy, an entity described in the recent ESC guidelines. Therefore, it can be assumed that CMD represents the ‘common soil’ between atrial dysfunction and HFpEF. More advanced imaging strategies such as deformation imaging and cardiac magnetic resonance might allow to investigate more in depth the functional and structural abnormalities underlying this pathological entity.

## Coronary microvascular dysfunction and clinical outcome
Our study revealed that the CMD‐HFpEF endotype is associated with a worse clinical prognosis than the no‐CMD one. To date, the prognostic impact of CMD in this subset of patients has not been clearly assessed, with conflicting results across various studies, all of which were severely limited by a small sample size. Interestingly, ‘all‐cause‐deaths’ were considered in the definition of the composite outcome, together with hospitalization for HF, highlighting the multi‐organ impairment frequently seen in HFpEF patients. , , Shah et al. found that HFpEF patients with CMD had a higher urinary albumin‐to‐creatinine ratio and a lower reactive hyperemia index, which are markers of systemic endothelial dysfunction. At the meta‐regression analysis, the prevalence of beta‐blocker use was found to correlate negatively with the incidence of the clinical endpoint, suggesting that their use might exert a favourable effect in patients with CMD‐HFpEF. However, due to the observational nature of the included studies, this result should be considered only hypothesis‐generating. To date, only two RCTs have evaluated the effect of beta‐blockers in patients with HFpEF, showing overall neutral results. , Nevertheless, the adoption of broad inclusion criteria (LVEF > 40–45%), not accounting for the presence of severe diastolic dysfunction and CMD, may have influenced the accuracy of the results.
In conclusion, in HFpEF, CMD is present in little more than half of the patients and is associated with increased E/e′ and LAVI, a higher prevalence of AF, and higher risk of death or HF hospitalization. Systemic inflammation and CMD may be a target for novel interventions in HFpEF.

## Limitations
The present study has several limitations. First, our meta‐analysis was based on observational studies, five of which are only retrospective, with inherent limitations (e.g. un‐blinded outcome measures, participant selection, and inadequate control group). Hence, the association between CMD and both echocardiographic variables and clinical outcomes cannot be determined to be causative. Second, the statistical method used for the composite clinical endpoint analysis provided an approximation of patient‐level time‐to‐event data. Third, the reliability of the pooled results was weakened by the high variability of the definitions and thresholds adopted for the diagnosis of CMD across the included studies, as confirmed by the moderate to severe heterogeneity reported in the analyses. Of note, CMD should be understood as a continuum, so the use of a cut‐off appears to be a simplification, and the diagnosis of certainty derives from an endo‐myocardial biopsy. Fourth, the included studies differed in their definitions of HFpEF, possibly affecting the pooled results. Fifth, among the echocardiographic variables, we did not include the global longitudinal strain, because it was not provided by a sufficient number of studies to allow a pooled analysis to be performed.

## Conclusions
Evidence of CMD, defined as impaired CFR, can identify a phenotype of HFpEF with poorer prognosis and distinct echocardiographic features of myocardial dysfunction. Future RCTs, restricted to a phenotype‐selected HFpEF population, could represent the key to identifying effective treatments in this subset of patients, tested so far in the broad population of ‘garden variety’ HFpEF.
