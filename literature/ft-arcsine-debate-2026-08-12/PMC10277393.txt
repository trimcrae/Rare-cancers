## Abstract

## Aim
To estimate the effect size of distance education on the readmission of patients with heart failure.

## Design
This study was a systematic review and meta‐analysis.

## Method
Both Persian and English interventional studies focused on investigating the effectiveness of any form of distance education interventions on the readmission of patients with heart failure were retrieved from the main databases: Embase, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, SID, and Google Scholar. Two independent teams screened the articles for eligibility. The Cochrane Risk of bias tool was implemented to evaluate the studies' quality. A random‐effects model was applied to pool the effect sizes, I
2 was calculated to examine heterogeneity, and Meta‐regression was used to investigate the source of heterogeneity. The proposal was registered in the PROSPERO database (no. CRD42020187453).

## Results
Articles 8836 were retrieved, and 11 articles were selected. Nine studies investigated the effect of distance education on readmission with <12‐month follow‐up (RR: 0.78 [95% CI 0.67–0.92]) and the I
2 of 0.00%; and four studies examined the effect of distance intervention on readmission with 12‐month or more follow‐up (RR: 0.89 [95% CI 0.73–1.09]) and the I
2 of 71.59%.

Keywords: distance education, heart failure, meta‐analysis, readmission, systematic review

1.

## INTRODUCTION
Heart failure is a highly prevalent cardiovascular disease, which leads to an increased mortality rate, successive hospitalization, and mental health problems. Furthermore, patients with heart failure are vulnerable to low quality of life, physical dysfunction, and consequent an increased disease burden (Bekelman et al.,  ; Bytyçi & Bajraktari,  ; Desai & Stevenson,  ; Zambroski et al.,  ). The American Heart Association (AHA) estimated that the disease will affect over eight million people over the age of 18 by 2030, given a 46% increase in the prevalence rate between 2012 and 2030. As a result, the percentage of the total population with heart failure will increase from 2.4% in 2012 to 3% in 2030 (Virani et al.,  ).
Despite the availability of specific pharmaceutical treatments and care support, the hospital readmission of patients with heart failure is very high (Aranda et al.,  ; Ross et al.,  ), which imposes challenges on patients, their families, and the healthcare systems. While the 30‐day hospital readmission for patients with heart failure is 17%–27%, it is estimated that half of the patients are re‐admitted to the hospital in the 6 months after discharge (Toback & Clark,  ). Readmission is a key and objective outcome to assess the results of healthcare services. It provides essential information about the care and discharge plans' limitations and patients' special unmet needs (Abdi et al.,  ). Readmissions are more likely to decrease when provided care promotes patient self‐care.
Studies indicated the effectiveness of patient education in improving the patient's awareness of the disease and promoting self‐care behaviours. Ultimately, self‐care behaviours may improve patients' performance, increase their quality of life, and reduce the readmission rate (Köberich et al.,  ).
With the advancement of technology and internet accessibility, patient education can occur remotely in electronic educational platforms provided for distance learning (Win,  ; Win et al.,  ). Distance education, also known as distance learning, is a type of education in which the teacher and the learner are geographically distant. The education process is done through an electronic or print medium in this educational method (Pandza & Masic,  ). Recently, network technologies have been applied to provide synchronous and asynchronous teaching activities. Distance learning has been acknowledged for its advantage in minimizing face‐to‐face education barriers and making patients focus on receiving educational content based on their capabilities, facilities, and requirements.
Given that patient education in clinical settings is usually conducted quickly within crowded and noisy situations, people may not learn effectively. Therefore, education based on e‐learning can help eliminate such barriers to keep patients focused on receiving the information. As a result, patients would always have access to such information, and they may refer to the materials repeatedly to remember or review the information (Movahedi et al.,  ; Sweeney & Chiriboga,  ).
Investigating the effect of distance education on heart failure hospital readmission resulted in contradictory findings. For example, Negarandeh et al. ( ) reported that telemonitoring and education positively impacted the self‐care behaviours of patients diagnosed with heart failure following hospital discharge, but there was no reduction in re‐hospitalization. However, Bento and Brofman ( ) reported a decreased frequency of hospitalizations with telemonitoring and nursing consultation. Despite the studies regarding the effectiveness of distance education on readmission of patients with heart failure, there is no systematic review and meta‐analysis study to pool the findings of those studies and provide more robust evidence accordingly. Therefore, this systematic review and meta‐analysis's main objective was to estimate the effectiveness of distance education on the readmission of patients with heart failure.

2.

## METHODS
The present systematic review and meta‐analysis study was conducted and reported using the PRISMA guideline. In addition, the research proposal was registered in the PROSPERO database on 05 July 2020 (No. CRD42020187453).
2.1.

## Study selection
All Persian and English interventional studies investigating the effectiveness of any form of distance education interventions on the readmission of patients with heart failure that were published until December 31, 2020, were included. Following inclusion criteria were employed: (1) experimental studies, (2) distance education interventions, (3) diagnosis of heart failure among the target community, and (4) studies that reported re‐admission as an outcome. No restriction of the gender of participants or age cut‐offs was considered. On the other hand, the present study did not include case reports and letters to the editor.
Endnote data management software was used to organize selected studies and review duplicated articles. Two independent reviewers (S.R., Z.Y.) reviewed the articles' titles and abstracts to exclude unrelated records from the evaluation process. Then, the full text of the remaining studies was examined by two independents reviewers (S.R., Z.Y.), and unrelated articles were excluded. In case of any disagreement, it was resolved upon a third researcher's discussion and judgement (R.N.).

2.2.

## Data sources and search strategy
The search strategy was selected based on PICO. It was determined as follows: p (patients with “heart failure”), I (performing any forms of distance education interventions), C (non‐implementation of distance education intervention), and O (readmission). English articles were also looked up within Embase, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases using appropriate keywords. Besides, Persian articles were looked up from the Google Scholar and Scientific Information Database (www.sid.ir).
The researcher selected these keywords: heart failure, congestive heart failure, myocardial failure, cardiac failure, readmission, hospital readmission, hospitalization, online learning, online education, distance education, health technology, educational website, remote health intervention, m‐Health, mobile health technology, web‐based intervention, telehealth, telephone, mobile phone, mobile health, distance counselling, health education, internet, short message, network, mobile.

2.3.

## Assessment of study risk of bias
At this stage, the quality of the selected papers was examined by two distinct evaluators (S.R., Z.Y.). The results were then discussed in a joint meeting, and in cases of any disagreement, the discussion would continue until an ultimate agreement was reached. Cochrane Risk of bias was implemented to evaluate the quality of articles. This scale is used to assess the extent of bias within interventional studies. For this purpose, the following biases were assessed by this scale: selection, performance, attrition, reporting, and other biases. These biases were then evaluated against the three measures of high, low, and unclear (Higgins et al.,  ).

2.4.

## Data extraction
The required data were collected from selected articles using a checklist including the following items: the name of the first author, the year of publication, quality assessment of the study, sample size, the setting of the study (country), the information of samples (age & gender), the interventions for each group, outcome variables, the duration of intervention, the duration of the patient follow‐up process, readmission rate, and risk ratio.

2.5.

## Data analysis
SPSS software was used for data analysis, and STATA software version 16 was used to perform the meta‐analysis. Besides, I
2 was measured to investigate the heterogeneity. A random‐effects model was chosen for the present study (Borenstein et al.,  ). Moreover, the probability of publication bias was determined using Funnel Plot and Egger statistical test. Finally, provided that the Freeman‐Tukey double arcsine transformation method can prevent unacceptable confidence intervals regarding the ratios which are close to 0 and 1, it was implemented to calculate readmission ratios (Nyaga et al.,  ). The source of heterogeneity was investigated using meta‐regression or subgroup analysis based on the studies' characteristics, including the duration of intervention and follow‐up periods. The weight of each study was based on the inverse of the overall study variance (that is, 1/variance).

3.

## RESULTS
The search process applied for the study selection is summarized in Figure  . In aggregate, 8836 articles were retrieved. Then, irrelevant studies were removed after reviewing the titles; and 41 articles remained after reviewing the abstracts. Moreover, two studies were excluded because the full text was published in other languages (Grebennikova et al., , in Russian; Mainardi et al., , in Italian). Another study (Slater et al., 2008) was excluded because it was not designed as a randomized controlled trial. Finally, according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the present research, the full texts of 38 articles were reviewed. Among them, 11 articles were selected to extract and analyse the data. One of the articles was examined separately because it compared two distance learning interventions with routine interventions. Publication bias was examined using Funnel Plot Egger statistical test, and the results indicated no respective bias (Figure  ).
 3.1.

## Study characteristics
All the selected studies (n = 11) were in English and were published by the end of 2020. These studies were randomized clinical trials; respective details are presented in Table  . A total number of 5682 patients participated in these studies, given that 2804 were assigned to the intervention groups (mean age of 67.43) and 2878 were assigned to the control groups (mean age of 68.64). The pooled estimation of RR for included studies was calculated based on different follow‐up times, that is, <12 and 12 months or more.
 The meta‐analysis of Nine studies that investigated the effect of distance intervention with <12‐month follow‐up (Bento & Brofman,  ; Chen et al.,  ; Negarandeh et al.,  ; Nouryan et al.,  ; Riegel et al.,  , ; Woodend et al.,  ; Yanicelli et al.,  ) revealed a pooled effect size of 0.78 (95% CI 0.67–0.92) and also a heterogeneity coefficient of 0.00%. Respective distance interventions in these studies included sending educational text messages (SMS), installing a mobile educational application, and controlling and educating patients through telephone calls and video sessions (Figure  ).
 Besides, a meta‐analysis of four studies that examined the effect of distance intervention 12‐month or more follow‐up (Ferrante et al.,  ; Galinier et al.,  ; Kalter‐Leibovici et al.,  ; Woodend et al.,  ) showed a pooled effect size of 0.89 (95% CI 0.73–1.09) and also a heterogeneity coefficient of 71.59%. Respective distance interventions in these studies included: providing educational intervention through telephone calls or video sessions and distributing booklets among the participants (Figure  ).
 The source of heterogeneity was investigated using meta‐regression or subgroup analysis based on the studies' characteristics, but we could not find any significant factor related to the heterogeneity. The pooled effect sizes were less than one in both follow‐up periods. Nevertheless, the confidence interval for studies that evaluated the effect of distance intervention for 12 months or more follow‐ups includes 1, which means interventions utilized in these studies were not effective on the readmission rate.

4.

## DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
4.1.

## Discussion
The present systematic review and meta‐analysis aimed to investigate the impact of a distance education intervention on the readmission of patients with heart failure. The review included 12 interventions conducted on 5682 participants in nine countries. Accordingly, a variety of distance learning interventions were performed. Five studies provided the materials through phone calls, two studies used video sessions, one study provided booklets, another study was conducted by sending educational messages (SMS), one other study provided the contents through the combination of educational package and telephone communication, one study focused on telephone training or video sessions upon the patient's choice, and finally, a study was performed through installing an educational application on patients' smartphones.
The findings of the selected studies were synthesized using meta‐analysis, and the pool estimation of the effect size of these interventions was also obtained. The results of analyses for <12‐month follow‐up periods show the value of RR less than one, and their confidence interval was not embraced 1; consequently, it shows that the interventions used in these studies were associated with an effective risk reduction.
Nonetheless, analyses for 12‐month or more follow‐ups displayed a value of RR less than one. However, their confidence interval was (0.73, 1.09), which shows that the intervention did not have a significant effect. According to the results, the impact of educational interventions decreases after 12 months. Therefore, it is necessary to repeat Distance education interventions periodically for patients.
Inglis et al. ( ) conducted a meta‐analysis study to investigate the effectiveness of structured telephone support or non‐invasive home telemonitoring on patients with heart failure. As one of the main variables, they should measure the readmission of patients due to heart failure. The study findings indicated that Non‐invasive telemonitoring reduced heart failure‐related hospitalizations (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.60–0.83) and Structured telephone support reduced heart failure‐related hospitalizations (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.77–0.93) (Inglis et al.,  ).
Among numerous meta‐analysis studies on patients with heart failure, no study investigates the effect of various distance educational interventions on patients' readmission. The results of a seminal systematic review by Krishna et al. ( ) that included 25 studies from 13 countries indicated that mobile phones could be applied as an interventional method of distance education in patients, such as by using voice services or sending messages. This may lead to improving self‐management behaviours and encouraging patients to adhere to the proposed treatment plan, which is also highly effective in achieving health standards (Krishna et al.,  ). Moreover, two recent meta‐analyses studies on the effect of sending short text messages to patients with chronic diseases showed that the transfer of information through distance education had become a prevalent method in public health.
Thakkar et al. ( ) conducted a meta‐analysis of 16 randomized clinical trials to evaluate the effectiveness of mobile phone text messaging in chronic disease medication adherence. The median intervention duration was 12 weeks, and self‐report was the most commonly used method to assess medication adherence. The point estimate showed an intervention effect (odds ratio, 1.68; 95% CI, 1.18–2.39). Therefore they concluded that text messaging improved medication adherence significantly (Thakkar et al.,  ). Also, a study by Sarabi et al. ( ) was conducted to address whether text message reminders effectively improved patients' adherence to medication. The authors used five electronic databases: PubMed, Embase, PsycINFO, CINAHL, and the Cochrane central register of controlled trials to conduct a systematic literature search. The results showed that text messaging interventions had improved patients' medication adherence rates (Sarabi et al.,  ).
Readmission is recognized as one of heart failure's most common complications, which imposes high health costs on patients and their families. Patients with insufficient knowledge about their disease and inappropriate skills to conduct necessary self‐care behaviours are more likely to experience readmission. Therefore, educational interventions might help reduce readmission by improving patients' knowledge (Golden,  ).
The selected studies in the present systematic review were based on 1–18 months of intervention periods and 3–48 months of patient follow‐up. Although it was expected to observe more significant effects of distance education on the readmission of patients with an increase in the duration of interventions and follow‐up, this hypothesis did not accept by the current study findings.
The distinctive feature of the present study is the provision of a pooled estimate of the effectiveness of distance education interventions on readmission among patients with heart failure. There are some limitations in the study. First, only studies published in either Persian or English were included in this systematic review. Second, some studies have co‐occurrence of daily monitoring of patients' vital signs (Telemonitoring) with distance education intervention. The last limitation was that the difference in spelling forms of some words, like hospitalization in the American and British, was not considered in the search strategy. Also, because two studies received poor scores from the risk of biased examination, this issue should be considered in interpreting the results.

4.2.

## Conclusion
The findings of this systematic review and meta‐analysis showed that distance education interventions could be beneficial in reducing the readmission of patients for at least 1 year. However, the results should be interpreted cautiously due to the risk of bias and high heterogeneity.
Meanwhile, with the high prevalence of heart failure worldwide, distance education interventions, along with other treatments, may help reduce the readmission of patients with heart failure. Therefore, it is recommended to consider distance education as one of the nursing interventions because it is reasonable in the present millennium. It should be noted that all the selected studies have compared distance education with routine treatment. As a result, it is suggested to conduct further studies to obtain more information about the efficiency and cost‐effectiveness of distance education interventions compared to other conventional methods such as face‐to‐face education.
