## ABSTRACT

## Background
People receiving dialysis treatment are at higher risk of sarcopenia. This review aimed to determine the global prevalence of sarcopenia in people on peritoneal dialysis and hemodialysis. We investigated whether the prevalence of sarcopenia differs based on assessment criteria, timing of assessment, and measurement tools used and explored the risk factors for sarcopenia in people on dialysis.

## Methods
This review followed JBI and PRISMA guidelines and included studies assessing sarcopenia in adults aged 18 and older with chronic kidney disease undergoing dialysis. Five databases were searched from inception to November 2022. The JBI SUMARI software was used to perform the meta‐analysis. Publication bias and risk factor analysis were performed using STATA (Version 18).

## Results
A meta‐analysis of 62 studies (15,382 participants) found the global prevalence of sarcopenia to be 30.1% (95% CI: 25.6%–39.9%) for hemodialysis and 20.5% (95% CI: 15.1%–26.4%) for peritoneal dialysis. Sarcopenia prevalence ranged between 23.1% and 30.3% in HD and between 6.1% and 26.9% in PD, based on the assessment criteria. Post‐dialysis sarcopenia prevalence was higher (33%) than pre‐dialysis (24.2%) in hemodialysis patients. Measuring muscle mass after dialysis using dual‐energy X‐ray assessment yielded a lower prevalence of sarcopenia (22.5%) than bioimpedance analysis or spectroscopy (33%). Risk factors in the HD population included age, sex, diabetes, inflammation markers, nutritional indices, and dialysis vintage, although heterogeneity between studies was high.

## Conclusion
This study showed a high prevalence of sarcopenia among the dialysis population and identified many risk factors, emphasizing the need for early identification and intervention and standardized assessments.

## Methodological Quality of Included Studies
The methodological quality of the included studies ranged from 12 to 16 (average 13) out of a possible 16 points. All studies reported on the criteria for inclusion, provided detailed descriptions of the study setting, and measured the exposure and outcomes in a valid and reliable manner using objective standard criteria for the measurement of the condition, as well as appropriate statistical analysis. Areas of concern include the identification of confounding factors and the strategies used to address them, with only 14 studies (23%) reported on these criteria.

## Prevalence by Different Timing of Assessment
The timing of the LMM assessment was analyzed only in the HD group. To assess the effect of the timing of LMM assessment, studies using BIA/BIS were categorized into pre‐ and post‐dialysis. A total of 10 studies (n = 5177) investigated the LMM pre‐dialysis with a sarcopenia prevalence of 24.2% (CI: 14.8%–35%, I
2: 97.8%) (see Figure  ). When the same instrument was used post‐dialysis (14 studies, n = 3502), the prevalence of sarcopenia was 33% (CI: 24.7%–41.7%, I

## Risk Factors Associated With Sarcopenia
The association between risk factors and sarcopenia in HD patients was explored in 21 studies. A pooled OR was calculated only for the HD group due to the small number of PD studies. The results demonstrated a significant association with sarcopenia for the following factors: older age, sex, diabetes, high c‐reactive protein, low body mass index, low albumin, and low calf circumference. However, there was a high heterogeneity among studies (I
2: 67%–95.6%). Longer time on dialysis (dialysis vintage) was the only significant risk factor for sarcopenia in the HD population (OR: 1.06, CI: 1.03–1.08), with lower heterogeneity (I

## Publication Bias/Sensitivity Analysis
The funnel plots showed that the studies were evenly distributed, suggesting no evidence of publication bias (Figures  and ). Egger's tests for HD studies (p = 0.12) and PD studies (p = 0.27) showed no significant publication bias (p > 0.05). The robustness of the meta‐analysis was tested by sequentially removing studies and changing the effect models. There was no significant difference between the adjusted values and the original values of the total pooled estimates.

## Discussion
This review investigated the prevalence of sarcopenia in people receiving different dialysis modalities and highlighted variations in prevalence based on different assessment criteria and the timing of assessment. We found that people receiving HD have a higher prevalence of sarcopenia compared to PD. Using AWGS criteria to determine sarcopenia in patients on dialysis yielded a higher prevalence compared to EWGSOP criteria. This review further investigated the timing of LMM measurement and found that measuring LMM post‐dialysis presented a higher prevalence than pre‐dialysis. When LMM was measured post‐dialysis, those using BIA/BIS presented a higher prevalence of sarcopenia than those using DXA. While several factors have been reported as risk factors associated with sarcopenia, the overall results were inconclusive due to the high heterogeneity among studies.
This review found that the prevalence of sarcopenia is high in people receiving dialysis and higher in those undergoing HD than in those undergoing PD. Therefore, routine screening for sarcopenia across the dialysis population is warranted in the clinical setting due to the high prevalence demonstrated in this review, particularly for those receiving HD who experience sarcopenia at a younger age [ ]. The choice of assessment criteria however remains less clear as they are not developed specifically for the CKD population [ , ]. The EWGSOP [ ] and AWGS [ ] criteria were initially developed for the general elderly population and did not consider sarcopenia resulting from chronic disease. Despite this, they remain the most used assessment criteria for sarcopenia in patients on dialysis.
The choice of assessment criteria, however, remains less clear as they are not developed specifically for the CKD population [ , ]. The EWGSOP [ ] and AWGS [ ] criteria were both updated in 2019 and developed for the general elderly population and did not consider sarcopenia resulting from chronic disease. Despite this, they remain the most used assessment criteria for sarcopenia in patients on dialysis.
Our study demonstrated a higher prevalence of sarcopenia using AWGS criteria compared to the European criteria. This difference in prevalence is likely explained by the AWGS criteria's use of a higher cutoff for handgrip strength (HGS) in both males and females and slightly higher muscle mass cutoffs for females when indexing for height compared to other criteria [ , ]. Furthermore, the AWGS criteria call for different cutoffs for muscle mass depending on the use of DXA or BIA and specify that muscle mass should be indexed for height (kg/m2) [ ]. However, individual studies comparing the various criteria in patients on HD routinely found a higher prevalence of sarcopenia using the European criteria [ , ]. Therefore, while the assessment criteria may influence the prevalence of sarcopenia, other factors, including timing and measurement tools used for muscle measurements, may also play a role.
In contrast to the existing literature, which identified a higher prevalence of sarcopenia when LMM was measured by DXA [ , ], our review found that studies on the HD population using DXA reported a lower prevalence of sarcopenia compared to those using BIA/BIS. This discrepancy may be related to differences in subgroup analysis, with previous studies grouping PD and HD together (despite clinical differences between these groups) and not considering the timing of measurements. In this review, we included only studies focusing on HD and compared measurements taken exclusively after a dialysis session. The discrepancy could also be influenced by ethnicity as most studies included in our review were from Asian countries. East Asians tend to have lower muscle mass and higher fat percentage compared to Europeans for the same BMI [ ]. Consequently, the difference between BIA‐measured LMM and DXA could be more pronounced [ ]. This finding highlights the need for population‐specific calibration of body composition measurement tools to ensure accuracy and reliability across diverse ethnic groups. Furthermore, the potential impact of other factors such as diet, physical activity levels, and genetic predispositions should be explored to better understand and address these discrepancies.
Although DXA is considered the gold standard for estimating muscle mass, it is not always practical in the clinical setting due to its invasive nature and associated labor costs [ ]. Therefore, BIA and BIS are more commonly used and are well supported by the literature and current guidelines. In dialysis patients, BIA has shown good correlation and agreement with DXA in assessing body composition [ ]. Furthermore, lean body mass index assessed via BIA has been found to be a good surrogate for DXA in elderly dialysis patients [ ]. The 2020 Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) guidelines for nutrition in CKD recommend the use of BIA in patients on dialysis [ ].
However, questions remain regarding the timing of measurements. In this study, a higher prevalence of sarcopenia was found when BIA/BIS measures were performed post‐dialysis compared to pre‐dialysis, potentially due to changes in fluid status. Wang et al. reported that nearly half of all patients with sarcopenia were missed when using pre‐dialysis estimation of body stores compared to post‐dialysis (13.8% vs. 23%) [ ], suggesting that for the best estimation of muscle mass and measurement consistency, BIA should be taken in a dry state, preferably 15–20 min after the second weekly dialysis session [ , ]. Future studies should assess the feasibility of this method as it may not always be practical due to patients' ability or willingness to stay after treatment and staff availability.
In this study, we aimed to identify associations for sarcopenia in dialysis patients. However, the high heterogeneity and small number of studies investigating each potential risk factor limited the interpretation and application to the clinical setting. Surprisingly, both male and female genders were identified as risk factors for sarcopenia across different studies. However, for females, despite an OR approaching 6, this result should be interpreted with caution due to the high heterogeneity and a sample size of only three studies. Older age was strongly associated with sarcopenia, based on data from 11 studies. This aligns with the well‐documented age‐related decline in muscle mass and function, exacerbated by the physiological stress of HD. Dialysis vintage, representing the duration of time on dialysis, was the only factor with relatively low heterogeneity. Diabetes and elevated CRP were also linked to sarcopenia. This finding suggests that prolonged dialysis contributes to sarcopenia, likely due to cumulative catabolic effects, chronic inflammation, and nutrient losses associated with the procedure. Low albumin levels, indicative of poor nutritional status and/or inflammation (commonly found in patients on dialysis), were another significant factor. While the results highlight important associations, the high heterogeneity limits the generalizability of the findings. Variations in study design, participant characteristics, diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia, and measurement methods contribute to this variability. Standardizing these parameters in future research is essential to strengthen evidence and guide clinical practice. Furthermore, few studies reported risk factors for sarcopenia in patients on PD; thus, no pooled OR could be performed for PD patients given the low number of studies. Additional caution should be taken when applying results to PD patients.
4.1

## Strengths and Limitations
Our study had several limitations. Firstly, most of the included studies were cohort or observational, so causation cannot be determined from these associations. Secondly, the considerable variation in assessment criteria for sarcopenia and the use of different muscle measurement tools may limit the overall results. Despite using pooled OR to determine the association, high heterogeneity between studies restricted the number of meaningful results. Thirdly, many studies were conducted in Asian countries, so these findings may not be generalizable to other parts of the world.
The strengths of this review include its status as the largest review of studies examining the prevalence of sarcopenia in CKD patients undergoing dialysis. While the prevalence may be influenced by factors such as gender and age, this analysis was beyond the scope of the review. The inclusion of various assessment criteria and measurement tools provides better insight into clinically applicable and currently used measurements. This is the first review to examine pooled ORs for association with sarcopenia in dialysis patients, aiming to offer insight into patients at risk of sarcopenia.

4.2

## Clinical Implications
Given the discrepancies in sarcopenia prevalence when applying different diagnostic criteria, the AWGS criteria appear to be more appropriate for dialysis cohorts, particularly in Asian populations. The higher sensitivity of AWGS, reflected in stricter cutoffs for muscle mass and strength, allows for earlier identification of sarcopenia, which is crucial in preventing further decline in physical function in dialysis patients. Conversely, the EWGSOP criteria may remain applicable in Western population, given their alignment with regional practices and validated cutoffs. Clinicians should tailor their selection of diagnostic criteria based on the demographic and clinical contexts of their patients, while advocating for modification to both AWGS and EWGSOP guidelines to better address the specific needs of the CKD patients.

5

## Conclusion
In our systematic review, we have shown that sarcopenia is an issue affecting 20.5% of PD patients and 30.1% of HD patients, highlighting it as a significant issue among patients on dialysis. The large variability in criteria used, muscle measurement tools, and timing affect the strength of results and the ability to define risk factors and associations. There is a need for additional research to assess the practicality of various assessment tools and the role of other measurements or predictors of low muscle mass, strength, and sarcopenia. Global research would benefit from the development of a single criterion to be applied to dialysis patients and standardize screening tools for this population. Further research is required in regions such as Australasia and North America, where minimal studies have been conducted to ascertain the prevalence of sarcopenia.
