## Abstract

## Background
Burns are a significant cause of disability and death. Nonfatal burns have drastic impacts on survivors’ lives. This study examines the epidemiological characteristics and prognostic factors of burn injuries of hospitalized patients in China.

## Methods
Literature search was conducted in online databases and study selection was based on precise eligibility criteria. Meta-analyses of proportions were performed to estimate the relative incidence rates of burn injuries between 1980 and 2022 by age, total body surface area (TBSA), cause, and season. Odds ratios (ORs) were pooled and metaregression analyses were performed to seek prognostic factors for mortality.

## Results
23 studies (465,433 patients; 33% females [95%CI 30, 35]) were included. Incidence rates were higher for age groups 0–4 and 30–45 years. Incidence was higher in summer (29% [95%CI 27, 30]) and lower in autumn (21% [95%CI 20, 22]). The incidences of mild (TBSA 0–10%), moderate (TBSA 10–30%), severe (TBSA 30–50%), and very severe (TBSA > 50%) burns were 52% [95%CI 45, 59] 27% [95% CI 23, 32], 9% [95%CI 6, 13], and 8% [95%CI 6, 10], respectively. Scald (50% [95%CI 43, 57]) and flame (27% [95%CI 19, 37]) were the main sources of burns. The mortality rate was 3% [95%CI 2, 3]. Age (OR 2.283 [95% CI 1.441, 3.125]), TBSA (2.266 [95% CI 1.708, 2.824), burn severity (OR 1.064 [95% CI 1.025, 1.103]), and inhalation injury (OR 6.670 [95% CI 3.032, 11.308]), and flame burns (metaregression coefficient: 0.128 [95% CI 0.029, 0.227]; p = 0.015) were associated with higher mortality.

## Conclusions
In China, burn injuries remain a serious health concern. Young children (0–4 years of age) constitute a special risk group for safety measures. Burns due to flame are found to be more fatal than other causes.

## Background
Burns are injuries mainly caused by heat, but may also occur due to radiation, electricity, chemicals, or friction. A burn can be a scald burn if caused by hot liquid, flame burn if caused by fire exposure, and contact burn if caused by hot solids. Burns are a major cause of disability and a significant cause of death. Although, the age-standardized incidence of burns is decreasing globally, the number of new burn cases is increasing [ ]. Worldwide, approximately 180,000 individuals die from burns each year, the vast majority in low- and middle-income countries. Non-fatal burns lead to morbidity with prolonged hospitalization, disfigurement, and disability [ ]. Mortality is higher in patients with larger burnt surface area [ , ]. Serious morbidity is also frequent in patients with burn injuries. Burns render an individual prone to infections [ ] and double the risk of pneumonia [ ]. Severely burnt patients are at an increased risk of gut [ ] and renal [ ] dysfunction. The prognosis of burn patients with diabetes is also poor [ ]. Comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease also significantly affect the mortality rates in burn patients [ ].
In some countries, such as Bulgaria, Czech Republic, Iran, Israel, and Oman, there is an increasing trend, whereas in some other countries, such as Austria, Germany, Japan, South Korea, Sweden, and the USA, there is a decreasing trend in the incidence of burn injuries. Moreover, in developed countries, there is a decreasing trend in the incidence of severe burns and duration of hospitalization due to burn injuries [ ]. Lower socio-economic status has also been found to be associated with higher burn incidence rates [ ] and poor outcomes of burn treatment [ ]. The incidence of burn injuries is twofold in males than in females. Burn survivors have a poor quality of life that persists in the long run and, in many cases, lifelong [ , ]. Burns not only cause physical injuries, but victims also have emotional and psychological ailments. Disabilities, scarring, and disfiguring resulting from burns of moderate to severe degree strongly affect an individual’s life [ ]. Serious burns also incur much higher monetary costs than those with other health conditions [ ].
Advancements in critical care have improved the prognosis of burn patients. More pertinent aspects include the improvements in fluid resuscitation protocols, burn excision and closure techniques, infection prevention and control, nutritional support, and metabolic modulation [ ]. Although the survival of burn patients has improved with the improvements in healthcare systems, the prognosis of burn patients with severe injuries remains poor. Prognostic models based on mortality as an outcome measure usually focus on age, total body surface area of burn, and inhalation injury as the most important prognostic factors [ , ].
Along with China's decreasing population growth rate in recent years, its working population has also declined [ ]. Moreover, more people live in urban than in rural areas in China now [ ]. The scenario may indicate that the incidence rates of burn injuries and mortality have also changed over time. So far, there is no repository in China to inform age-standardized, and population-adjusted incidence rates of burn injuries. However, several studies have been published to report on the epidemiological features of burn cases in China. A synthesis of the data of hospitalized burn patients can provide estimates of the extent, trend, and prognosis of burn epidemiology. The aim of the present study was to conduct a systematic review of this area for the identification of studies that reported the characteristics of burn patients who attended a burn center or were hospitalized for a burn injury to perform meta-analyses of relative incidence rates with regard to age, body surface area, cause, and season and to seek prognostic factors affecting mortality of patients with burn injuries.

## Methods

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Studies were included in this meta-analysis if they reported (a) the epidemiological data of patients with burn injuries; (b) the incidence rates of burn injuries according to demographic, causative, periodic, and severity categorization; and (c) prognostic factors affecting the mortality of burn patients. Studies were excluded from the meta-analysis if they reported the outcomes of burn patients with a particular type of source such as burns due to a chemical or explosion or reported the outcomes of burn patients focusing on infectious, renal, gastroenterological, hematological, or other complications.

## Literature search
The literature search was conducted in Google Scholar and PubMed online search engines using relevant keywords. The main search strategy utilized “Burn” AND “injuries” AND “epidemiology” OR “incidence” OR “prevalence” OR “characteristics” and “China” key terms. The bibliographic sections of included studies and other related research articles were also screened. The literature search encompassed original research articles published in English before August 2024. Two reviewers conducted the literature search independently and then combined their outputs. During the study selection stage, these reviewers decided on inclusions and exclusions with mutual consultations.

## Data extraction, synthesis, and analysis
Demographic information, burn characteristics, burn source and severity, annual and seasonal data, study design and conduct features, and outcome data were extracted from the research articles of the selected studies and were organized on datasheets for synthesis. To achieve relative incidence rates of burn injuries by age, season, total body surface area (TBSA), and burn cause, meta-analyses of proportions were performed using binomial data reported by the included studies. Freeman Tukey’s double arcsine transformation of proportions was incorporated for variance stabilization using the exact binomial method in these meta-analyses. Burn severity was categorized as mild (TBSA 0–10%), moderate (TBSA 10–30%), severe (TBSA 30–50%), and very severe (TBSA > 50%) burns.
To examine the associations between burn injury and prognostic factors identified by the individual studies, the odds ratios reported by these studies were pooled using the Dersimon–Liard method. In addition, metaregression analyses were performed to evaluate the associations between mortality rate and several independent variables including study sample size, percentage of females, percentage of children, cause of burn, TBSA, burn severity, percentage of inhalation injuries, and hospital stay. All analyses were performed with Stata software (Stata Corporation, College Station, Texas, USA).

## Results
Over a thousand studies were identified during abstract screening of which 54 full-text articles were scrutinized for study eligibility assessment which led to the selection of 23 studies [ – ] for inclusion (Fig.  ). All studies were retrospective in design and reported the data of 465,433 patients who attended a medical institution or were hospitalized for burn injuries between 1980 and 2022. The proportion of females in these studies was 33% [95% CI 30, 35]. The proportion of children in studies that reported the outcomes of different age groups was 29% [95% CI 27, 32]. Important characteristics of the included studies are presented in Table S1.
 In general, the burn incidence rates were higher for age groups 0–4 years, and 30–45 years in comparison with other age groups (Fig.  ; Table S2). The incidence of burn injuries was higher during the summer months (29% [95% CI 27, 30]) followed by the winter (26% [95% CI 24, 27]), spring (25% [95% CI 24, 26]), and autumn (21% [95% CI 20, 22]) (Figure S1). Several studies reported trends in burn injuries through the years (Figure S2). Among these, five studies [ , , , , ] observed increasing trends between 1980 and 2010. Qian et al. [ ] also found an increasing trend between 2010 and 2016. However, one study observed a decreasing trend during 2000 and 2008 [ ]. Two smaller studies observed decreasing trends in burn injuries between 2015 and 2020 [ , ].
 Mild burns accounted for 52% [95% CI 45, 59] of the cases followed by moderate (27% [95% CI 23, 32]), severe (9% [95% CI 6, 13]), and very severe (8% [95% CI 6, 10]) burns (Figure S3). Inhalation injuries occurred in 8% [95% CI 4, 14] of the patients. Scald and flame were the main sources which accounted for 50% [95% CI 43, 57] and 27% [95% CI 19, 37] of the sources of burn injuries, respectively. Electrical and chemical sources accounted for 8% [95% CI 6, 10] and 4% [95% CI 3, 5] of the cases, respectively (Figure S4).
The mortality rate was 3% [95% CI 2, 3] (Figure S5). Pooling of the odds ratios of included studies led to the identification of several risk factors. Age was a significant risk factor for mortality after burn injury (OR 2.283 [95% CI 1.441, 3.125]). Sex was not associated with mortality (OR 2.950 [95% CI − 0.177, 6.078]). Higher TBSA (2.266 [95% CI 1.708, 2.824), burn severity (OR 1.064 [95% CI 1.025, 1.103]), and inhalation injuries (OR 6.670 [95% CI 3.032, 11.308]) were also significantly associated with higher mortality after burn injury. Whereas scald burns were associated with significantly lower mortality (OR 0.616 [95% CI 0.317, 0.914]), flame burns were associated with higher mortality (OR 1.441 [95% CI − 1.143, 4.025]) (Fig.  ).
 Several other risk factors for mortality were also identified by individual studies. Wang et al. [ ] reported that folk remedies (OR 1.88 [95% CI 1.25, 2.83] or no treatment (OR 1.44 [95% CI 0.93, 2.22] compared to cleaning and dressing were associated with higher mortality. Zheng et al. [ ] reported that explosions compared to other sources were also associated with higher mortality (OR 146.7 [95% CI 53.94, 398.87]). Chen et al. [ ] reported that residing in a city (OR 0.03 [95% CI 0.003, 0.28]) or a town (OR 0.15 [95% CI 0.03, 0.84) compared to residing in a rural area, and university (OR 0.43 [95% CI 0.06, 0.69]) or secondary (OR 0.59 [95% CI 0.13, 0.11]) education compared to primary education or less were associated with lower mortality. Moreover, health education (OR 0.13 [95% CI 0.03, 0.53]), and safety measures (OR 0.03 [95% CI 0.01, 0.29]) were also associated with lower mortality.
In metaregression analyses, the age (metaregression coefficient (MC): 0.095 [95% CI 0.001, 0.189]; p = 0.048), percentage of patients with flame burns (MC: 0.128 [95% CI 0.029, 0.227]; p = 0.015), percentage of patients with severe burns (MC: 0.186 [95% CI 0.063, 0.310]; p = 0.006), percentage of patients with very severe burns (MC: 0.263 [95% CI 0.131, 0.395]; p = 0.001), and longer hospital stay (MC: 0.204 [95% CI 0.075, 0.332]; p = 0.006) were positively associated with mortality, whereas the percentage of children (MC: − 0.066 [95% CI − 0.138, 0.005]; p = 0.064), the percentage of cases with scald burns (MC: − 0.101 [95% CI − 0.199, − 0.004]; p = 0.042), and the percentage of patients with mild burns (MC: − 0.115 [95% CI − 0.211, − 0.020]; p = 0.022)) were inversely associated with mortality (Table  ).
 

## Discussion
This meta-analysis of over 465,000 Chinese burn patients found that young children (0–4 years of age) and adults (30–45 years of age) were the most vulnerable among all age groups. However, the mortality rate may be lower in children than in adults. Almost half of this population had scalded burns. The incidence of burn injuries was higher during summer (29%) and lower in autumn (21%). Severe burns and longer hospitalization are found to be associated with higher mortality rates. Whereas flame burns were associated with higher risk, scald burns posed a lower risk of mortality.
We have found that young children of less than 4 years of age are more vulnerable to burn injuries. Similar reports are also found for some other countries, such as the USA [ ], Spain [ ], and Turkey [ ]. It is thought that the exhibition of more exploratory behaviors by young children may pose them to the risk of burn injuries. Moreover, occupational mothers may have to leave their children at home which devoid them of the necessary care they need to remain safe while performing activities [ ]. Thus, this age group demands better care. Safety measures such as the placement of safety fences beside bedside stoves have remained successful in reducing burn injuries. Moreover, a better education level of parents can also provide better protection to children as it has been reported that even secondary in comparison with primary or no education is associated with a lower mortality rate after burn injury [ ].
Overall, an increasing trend in the incidence of burn injuries between 1980 and 2000 is evident from data extracted from included studies [ , ]. Some studies have also reported an increasing trend in burn injuries between 2000 and 2010 [ , , ]. During 2010 and 2020, data show variable findings with one study showing an increasing trend [ ] but two smaller studies showing a decrease in the number of burns [ , ]. Outside this meta-analysis, Zhu et al. [ ] reported that between 2007 and 2016, the incidence rate of burns due to “guo lian kang” (stove connected to bed) scalds decreased. Zhang et al. [ ] have found an increasing trend in the incidence of hydrofluoric acid burns between 2004 and 2013. Sun et al. [ ] found an increasing trend in burn injuries due to electrical sources between 2000 and 2009. Wang et al. [ ] found that whereas there is a decrease in the incidence of burn injuries from flames, there was a slightly increasing trend in the incidence of injuries from electrical sources between 2000 and 2008.
The demographic structure of China has changed dramatically over the last 30 years. China’s population growth rate has decreased from 0.57% in the last decade to 0.53% in recent years. The number of newborns has decreased from 18 million in the last decade to 12 million in the year 2020. China's working population (ages 16–59 years) has also decreased by 40 million since 2010 [ ]. After 2011, more people live in urban than in rural areas in China [ ]. The fertility rate which was 2.6 in the 1980s has decreased to 1.16 in 2020 which is now comparable to the fertility rates of the USA and Australia. Japan has an even further lower fertility rate of 1.3. [ ]. South Korea has also observed a record low birth rate in recent years. In 2020, first time in history, South Korea had a lower natality rate than mortality rate [ ]. These demographic changes are supposed to be associated with decreased rates of burns. However, precise data are not yet available for China in the literature. According to a study of global burn incidence rates, 12% of the world's new burn cases were recorded in China In 2019. However, China had the most significant reduction in the number of deaths due to burns between 1990 and 2019 which is in contrast to India, where a 12% increase in burn mortality has been observed during this period [ ].
The incidence of burns may be affected by several factors. A lower socioeconomic status has been associated with an increased risk of burn injuries [ , ]. A review article identified several factors associated with an increased risk of burns. These included low income, larger family size, single parents, low literacy, lower education status, joblessness, poor living conditions, no ownership of property, and crowding [ ]. Education is one important factor in preventing burns. Preventive efforts such as the use of smoke detectors, and controlled heat or hot water have reduced the incidence rates of burns in developed countries [ ]. As power use is being transformed from fossil fuels to electrical sources, there is a need to take appropriate measures for the safety of appliances, installations, wiring, and repair.
We have found that whereas flame burns were associated with higher risk, scald burns were associated with lower risk of mortality. A couple of studies that could not be included in the pooled analysis of odds ratios have also reported similar outcomes. One study found that non-flame burns in comparison with flame burns were associated with a lower risk of mortality (OR 0.17 [0.137, 0.21]) [ ]. Another study found that the odds of mortality were lower with scald burns in comparison with flame burns (OR 0.213 [0.14, 0.325]) [ ]. Flame burns are found to constitute up to 92% of the sources responsible for fatalities due to burns [ ]. Bang et al. [ ] have reported a mortality rate of 6.4% in 3680 Kuwaiti patients of which over 92% sustained flame burns. In another study of 7866 Pakistani burn patients with a mortality rate of 23.2%, the flame burn was the major source of injury among those who died [ ]. In a Brazilian study of about 400 patients, ICU, and hospital mortality rates were 33% and 34%, respectively. Flame was the cause of burn injuries in 77% of the patients [ ]. These data also suggest that there is an urgent need to shift from fossil fuel-based energy utilization to electrical sources with safer technologies.
In the present study, female burn patients constituted about 33% of the population. We found no significant association between the mortality due to burn injuries and sex. However, there were variabilities in the outcomes of the individual studies in this regard. Male sex was found to be associated with higher mortality in a large database study [ ]. Xu et al. [ ] also found male sex to be associated with higher mortality after burn injuries. On the other hand, Chen et al. [ ] and Wang et al. [ ] did not find any significant difference between the sexes in mortality due to burn injuries. In 2019, 23% of all global deaths due to burns occurred in India, and 73% of victims were females [ ]. In some Mideastern countries, the incidence of burn injuries is found almost equal in both males and females [ , ] and domestic accidents are more common in such places. Domestic accidents are identified as a risk factor for mortality in a study of 3680 burn patients in which 41% suffered from cooking gas accidents [ ].
Most hospitalized burn patients have inhalation injuries. We have found an incidence rate of approximately 8% for inhalation injuries in the overall population but the incidence was much higher (up to 70%) in severe cases [ ]. Inhalation injury is a major cause of death in burn patients [ ]. Fire is accompanied by huge amounts of smoke and if the cause of fire is any gaseous material it can further exaggerate the amount of gas to be inhaled by those who are trapped in such an incident. Toxic fumes, particles, and a combination of several compounds such as formaldehyde, polyurethane, and other asphyxiants manifest a severe insult to soft tissues in the upper respiratory tract that often leave irreversible damage [ ]. In our meta-analysis of pooled odds ratios as well as in metaregression, inhalation injury was significantly associated with higher mortality. Among the included studies of this meta-analysis, Chen et al. [ ] reported that 70% of patients who died had inhalation injuries.
Among the foremost limitations of the present study, the observance of high statistical heterogeneity in meta-analyses is an important consideration, because factors such as age, source, severity, and season could not explain high heterogeneity. However, it should be noted that we have used I2 as an index to estimate heterogeneity which calculates the proportion of variance that cannot be explained by sampling error alone to evaluate inconsistency in the outcomes between included studies. Hospitalized burn patients inherently represent a heterogenous group because of their varying socioeconomic backgrounds, localities, occupational characteristics, and many other factors. Moreover, the retrospective design of the included studies might have also exaggerated the pre-existing heterogeneity. Another constraint was the less availability of yearwise data, especially for the 2010–2025 period due to which the current trend in the incidence rates remained obscured. Our meta-analysis of incidence rates for various age groups may have slightly exaggerated estimates, because different authors of the included studies reported varying age groups, and we relied on the best available synthesis of these data.

## Conclusions
In China, whereas the incidence of burn injuries increased between 1980 and 2000, the current state of incidence is not as clear. Young children (0–4 years of age) are more vulnerable to burns and deserve special attention. The vast majority of burn injuries were due to scald and flame sources. Severe burns and longer hospitalization are found to be associated with higher mortality rates. The incidence of burn injuries was higher during summer. Flame burns are found to be more fatal than other causes which demand shifting energy sources from fossil fuels to renewable energy and increased use of safer technologies.
