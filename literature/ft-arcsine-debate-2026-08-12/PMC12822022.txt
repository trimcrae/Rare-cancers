## Background
Adhering to 24-hour movement guidelines, encompassing physical activity, sedentary behavior, and sleep, is crucial for health. This study aimed to estimate the prevalence of (non)adherence to these guidelines in the Chinese population.

## Method
Four electronic databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and CNKI) were searched for quantitative studies published in Chinese and English between June 2016 and October 2025. Studies were included if they reported overall (non)adherence to the 24-hour movement guidelines in Chinese participants. The prevalence data from multiple studies were pooled using a random-effects model. Subgroup analyses were conducted to determine differences in prevalence across populations, regions, survey years, female percentage, and assessment methods for physical activity and sleep. Additionally, meta-regression analyses were performed to identify the sources of heterogeneity.

## Results
The meta-analysis included 68 studies, encompassing 832,027 participants. The overall pooled prevalence of meeting all three 24-hour movement guidelines was 7.6% (95% CI: 6.2%, 9.0%). Adults showed the highest adherence (17.9%, 95% CI: 11.4%, 25.4%), followed by preschoolers (13.3%, 95% CI: 11.3%, 15.4%), while children and adolescents (5.1%, 95% CI: 4.1%, 6.2%) and older adults (1.7%, 95% CI: 1.5%, 2.0%) demonstrated markedly lower adherence. Furthermore, 13.0% (95% CI: 11.0%, 14.9%) of the population met none of the guidelines, with older adults exhibiting the highest non-adherence (17.4%, 95% CI: 6.3%, 32.4%).

## Conclusion
This meta-analysis underscores the low adherence to 24-movement guidelines among Chinese populations. These findings highlight the urgent need for targeted interventions to improve 24-hour movement behaviors across all age groups.

## Introduction
China faces various public health challenges that have been influenced by sociodemographic and lifestyle shifts since the country initiated economic reforms four decades ago [ ]. These changes have resulted in increased prevalence of overweight, obesity, and chronic noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), which are causing a strain on the national health system [ , ]. In recognition of these trends, the Chinese government introduced the Healthy China 2030 Initiative in 2016. This initiative emphasises the promotion of healthy lifestyles and behavioral modifications as key modifiable determinants of health [ ]. To translate this vision into concrete actions, the subsequent Healthy China Action (2019–2030) was launched in 2019, which specifically advocates for increased physical activity (PA), reduced sedentary behavior (SB), and adequate sleep [ ].
PA, SB, and sleep are interrelated components of a 24-hour behavioral cycle, which range from complete rest to vigorous activity [ , ]. A scoping review has found that the distribution of these movement behaviors within 24 h affects physical, mental, and developmental health across the lifespan [ ]. In line with this integrated paradigm, countries have developed 24-hour movement guidelines for specific age groups [ – ]. It is recommended that preschool-aged children engage in at least 180 min of PA (including 60 min of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity [MVPA]), limit recreational screen time (ST) to 1 h or less, and sleep for 10 to 13 h [ ]. Recommendations for children and adolescents are at least 60 min of MVPA each day, less than 2 h of recreational ST, and adequate sleep (9 to 11 h for 5 to 13 years, 8 to 10 h for 14 to 17 years) [ ]. Adults aged 18 to 64 years should engage in at least 150 min of MVPA a week, less than 8 h of SB a day, and 7 to 9 h of sleep each night [ ]. The recommendation for MVPA and sedentary time is the same for younger adults and older adults aged 65 years and above. The recommended sleep duration for older adults is 7 to 8 h per night [ ].
Adherence to all three components of the 24-hour movement guidelines has been consistently associated with physical, psychosocial, and cognitive benefits, surpassing the benefits linked to partial or non-adherence [ ]. Therefore, understanding the extent to which populations comply with these guidelines are an important research focus in public health and behavioral epidemiology. The 2017 Physical Activity and Fitness in China—The Youth Study (PAFCTYS) reported that only around 5.0% of Chinese children and adolescents met 24-hour movement guidelines [ ]. A recent scoping review, which synthesized evidence on 24-hour movement behaviors in Chinese populations found that the proportion of individuals meeting all three guidelines varied across age groups. It ranged from 2.9% to 16.4% in preschoolers, 0.3% to 26.1% in children and adolescents, 15.1% to 27.0% in adults, and 1.61% in older adults [ ]. The review did not include a meta-analytic synthesis to estimate pooled prevalence rates across studies.
Synthesizing findings across individual studies is a prerequisite for establishing a robust evidence base. This, in turn, could help shape future research priorities, direct national health surveillance initiatives, and develop targeted early intervention strategies. Therefore, the aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to assess the prevalence of full, partial, or non-adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines among the Chinese population.

## Method
This systematic review and meta-analysis were registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under Registration number CRD42024574774. It followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines published in 2020 [ ]. The PRISMA checklist is available in Supplementary Table 1.

## Eligibility criteria
Studies were included based on the following Participants, Intervention, Comparison, Outcomes, and Study (PICOS) criteria [ , ] :

Participants: apparently healthy preschoolers, children, adolescents, adults, and older adults.
Outcome: adherence to overall 24-hour movement guidelines and/or failure to meet any of the individual movement behavior recommendations.
 Study design: no restrictions were applied, except for excluding systematic reviews, meta-analyses, qualitative, and case studies. The search was limited to peer-reviewed articles published in English or Chinese.

The exclusion criteria were as follows:Studies exclusively involving participants who were overweight, obese, or diagnosed with physical or mental disorders.
Intervention studies involving only non-active individuals at baseline.
Studies published before April 2016, as the first 24-hour movement guidelines for children and youth were released in that month [ ].
Studies based on the same dataset to avoid duplication.
Gray literature.
Qualitative and case studies. Only the most comprehensive study was included when multiple papers used the same dataset. 

## Information sources and search strategy
Two researchers (ZZ and YFX) systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), and Cochrane Library, focusing on studies published between 16 June 2016 and 24 October 2025. The search strategy was based on the PICOS criteria, using combinations of the following keywords:

“movement behavio*” OR “24-h*”
“physical activit*” OR sport* OR exercise
sedentary OR sitting OR “screen time” OR screentime
sleep
“guideline*” OR “recommendation*”

The search strategy was tailored to each database by adapting the search terms and applying database-specific filters. The complete search strategy is presented in the Supplementary Table 2. Additionally, the reference lists from the included studies and previous reviews were checked to ensure that no eligible studies were missed.

## Selection process
Duplicate studies were removed using EndNote 21 after the initial search. Two researchers (ZZ and YFX) then screened all titles and abstracts independently to identify potential studies for a full-text review. A third researcher (ZCC) was consulted when there were disagreements. The full text of the studies was then assessed by ZZ and YFX to confirm eligibility.

## Data collection process
For the included studies, one researcher (ZZ) extracted the relevant data, and another (YFX) verified the information for accuracy. The extracted data included: author(s), publication year, percentage of females, mean age, study design, regions, survey year, sample size, and the measurement methods for PA, SB, and sleep. In cases where both self-reported and device-based measures were present, the device-based measures were prioritized. For longitudinal studies, only baseline data were used.

## Risk of bias assessment
To prevent bias in the assessment of methodological quality, the authors’ identities, affiliations, dates, and sources were concealed. Two researchers (ZZ and RHL) independently evaluated the methodological quality of the studies, and discrepancies were resolved by a third researcher (SK). The Quality Assessment Tool for Observational Cohort and Cross-sectional Studies [ ], which includes 14 items for longitudinal studies (11 items applicable to observational and cross-sectional studies), was used. These items assess elements such as the research question, study population, justification of sample size, exposure and outcome measures, blinding, and statistical analyses. A detailed list of quality assessment questions is provided in Supplementary Table 3. Each item is scored as yes (1 point), no (0 points), not reported, or not applicable. Scores ranged from 0 to 14 for longitudinal studies and 0 to 11 for cross-sectional studies. Based on these scores, studies were classified as high-quality (longitudinal: >9 points; cross-sectional: >7 points), medium-quality (longitudinal: 4–9 points; cross-sectional: 3–7 points), or low-quality (longitudinal: <4 points; cross-sectional: <3 points).

## Outcome measures
The primary outcome of this review is the (non)adherence to all three of the 24-hour movement guidelines. The secondary outcome was the adherence to two or one of the 24-hour movement guidelines.

## Synthesis methods
The metaprop procedure [ ] in the Stata 18.0 software (Stata Corp, College Station, TX, USA) was used to perform the meta-analysis. The DerSimonian and Laird method was applied to pool the prevalence of multiple studies [ ]. The 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) for prevalence were established using the exact or Clopper-Pearson method [ ], and the results were normalized using the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation before calculating the pooled prevalence [ ]. The random-effects model was selected due to substantial population stratification differences among included studies and significant heterogeneity in results, which necessitated accounting for inter-study variability [ ]. The degree of heterogeneity in the pooled prevalence was assessed using the I2 statistic and Cochran’s Q test. Heterogeneity levels were categorized as low (< 25%), moderate (25–75%) or high (> 75%), respectively [ ]. Publication bias was examined using funnel plots and Egger’s linear regression test. A p-value < 0.05 in Egger’s test was considered indicative of significant publication bias. To explore potential sources of heterogeneity, univariate meta-regression analyses were conducted using the following continuous moderators: mean age, female percentage, survey year, study quality score, sample size, and published year. Each moderator was tested separately in a random-effects meta-regression model to examine its association with the pooled prevalence estimates. Subgroup analyses were conducted according to age group (preschoolers, children and adolescents, adults, and older adults), survey time (before COVID-19, after COVID-19), and geographic regions (Central China, Eastern China, Northern China, Northeast China, Northwest China, Southern China, Southwest China, Muti-regions).

## Results

## Study selection
The study selection process is illustrated in the PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. ). A total of 4,332 records were identified through database searching, and an additional two records were identified through previous reviews’ reference lists [ ]. After removing duplicates and irrelevant items, 2,806 records remained for screening. During the screening phase, 2,806 records were assessed, and 109 full-text articles were evaluated for eligibility. Of these, 26 articles were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria, seven studies had overlapping data with other studies, and eight studies did not provide data about (non)adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines. Ultimately, 68 studies were included in the meta-analysis.

 

## Study characteristics
This systematic review and meta-analysis included 68 studies published between 2016 and 2025, comprising a total of 832,027 participants aged 3 to 80 years. The analysis incorporated 60 cross-sectional studies [ , – ] and eight longitudinal studies [ – ]. Population distribution included 17,815 preschoolers [ , , , , – , , , ], 72,4885 children and adolescents [ , – , , , – , , – , , , , , , – , – , – ], 80,631 adults [ , , , , – , , , , , , , ], and 8,696 older adults [ , ]. Regarding measurement methods, 13 studies utilized accelerometers for PA assessment [ , , , , , , , , , , , , ], while two studies used accelerometers for SB [ , ], and 5 for sleep duration [ , , , , ]. Temporally, 24 studies were conducted before COVID-19 pandemic [ , – , , , , , , , , , , , , , , ], 42 after [ , , – , , , , – , – , – , – , ], and one study conducted three cross-sectional surveys in 2019, 2020, and 2021 [ ]. One report didn’t specify the date of the survey [ ]. See details in Supplementary Table 3.

## Risk of bias in studies and reporting biases
The results of the methodological quality assessment are presented in Supplementary Table 4. Specifically, 48.5% (n = 33) of the studies were considered high-quality, and 51.5% (n = 35) were considered medium-quality. Reporting for sample description criteria (Items 1–4) was generally adequate across studies. However, justification of sample size (Item 5) was provided in only three studies (4.4%). While the vast majority of studies (86.8%, n = 59) employed a cross-sectional design, only 13.2% (n = 9) conducted prospective baseline assessments (Item 7). Most studies provided clear definitions of independent and dependent variables (Items 8, 9, and 11), whereas repeated measurements (Item 10) were implemented in only four studies (5.9%). None of the studies reported the use of blinding procedures (Item 12), and only seven studies (10.3%) documented participant attrition during follow-up (Item 13). In statistical analyses, 65 studies (95.6%) appropriately adjusted for relevant covariates, while the remaining three (4.4%) did not incorporate such adjustments.

## Adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines
The meta-analysis revealed a critically low overall prevalence of meeting all three 24-hour movement guidelines among the Chinese population, at 7.6% (95% CI: 6.2%, 9.0%). The prevalence of meeting two guidelines is 32.6% (95% CI: 29.9%, 35.4%), and 40.9% (95% CI: 38.5%, 43.3%) met only one guideline. The analyses exhibited considerable heterogeneity, as indicated by I² statistics of 99.82%, 99.78%, and 99.68% for meeting all three, two, and one guideline(s), respectively (Supplementary Figs. 1–3).
The funnel plots did not reveal substantial asymmetry for any of the outcomes (Supplementary Figs. 4–6). Egger’s regression tests found no statistical evidence of publication bias (Meeting all three: p = 0.0795; Meeting two: p = 0.1028; Meeting one: p = 0.2217). Furthermore, leave-one-out sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of the pooled prevalence estimates, demonstrating that the sequential omission of any single study did not significantly alter the overall results (Supplementary Fig. 7).
As shown in Fig.  , the prevalence of meeting all three guidelines differed significantly across population subgroups (Qb = 287.40, p < 0.001). The highest prevalence was observed among adults (17.9%, 95% CI: 11.4%, 25.4%), followed by preschoolers (13.3%, 95% CI: 11.3%, 15.4%). The prevalence was substantially lower among children and adolescents (5.1%, 95% CI: 4.1%, 6.2%) and was lowest in older adults (1.7%, 95% CI: 1.5%, 2.0%). 
No statistically significant regional difference was identified (Qb = 13.64, p = 0.06). The prevalence was highest in studies from Southwest China (13.9%, 95% CI: 4.7%, 26.7%), followed by multi-region studies (10.1%, 95% CI: 7.2%, 13.5%). The lowest prevalence was reported in Eastern China (4.9%, 95% CI: 3.5%, 6.5%).
A clear temporal trend was observed in relation to the COVID-19 pandemic (Qb = 8.95, p < 0.01). The prevalence was significantly higher in studies conducted after the COVID-19 pandemic (9.7%, 95% CI: 7.7%, 11.9%) compared to those before (5.4%, 95% CI: 3.6%, 7.4%).
No statistically significant differences in prevalence were found between studies using objective versus subjective measurement methods for MVPA (Qb = 0.02, p = 0.89) or for sleep duration (Qb = 0.08, p = 0.78). Similarly, no significant difference was observed based on female participant percentage (Qb = 1.66, p = 0.20).
Meta-regression analysis identified that the proportion of female participants was positively associated with the prevalence of meeting all three guidelines (β = 1.67, p = 0.00) (Table  ). Sample size demonstrated a small but statistically significant negative association with meeting all three guidelines (β = 0.00, p = 0.01). None of the other covariates demonstrated statistically significant associations with the outcome, including mean age (β = 0.00, p = 0.06), survey time (β = 0.01, p = 0.19), sample size (β = 0.00, p = 0.21), publication quality (β = 0.01, p = 0.78), or published year (β = 0.03, p = 0.14).

## (Non) adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines
The pooled prevalence of meeting none of the 24-hour movement guidelines was 13.0% (95% CI: 11.0%, 14.9%) (Supplementary Fig. 8). Considerable heterogeneity was observed across studies (I² = 99.77%, p < 0.001). Funnel plot (Supplementary Fig. 9), Egger’s test (p = 0.3697), and leave-one-out sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Fig. 10) indicated no substantial publication bias or influence from individual studies on the pooled estimate.
As shown in Fig.  , subgroup analysis revealed significant differences across population groups (Qb = 36.98, p < 0.001). Older adults demonstrated the highest prevalence of meeting none of the guidelines (17.4%, 95% CI: 6.3%, 32.4%), followed by children and adolescents (16.5%, 95% CI: 14.2%, 18.8%), preschoolers (7.6%, 95% CI: 5.2%, 10.5%), while adults showed the lowest prevalence (6.3%, 95% CI: 3.8%, 9.4%).
In contrast, no statistically significant differences were identified across geographical regions (Qb = 8.33, p = 0.30), survey periods in relation to COVID-19 (Qb = 0.54, p = 0.46), female participant percentage (Qb = 0.69, p = 0.41), or measurement methods for either MVPA (Qb = 0.38, p = 0.54) or sleep duration (Qb = 0.02, p = 0.90).

 

Meta-regression analysis (Table  ) identified that the proportion of female participants was a significant negative predictor of meeting none of the guidelines (β = −1.32, p = 0.04), indicating that studies with higher proportions of female participants reported lower prevalence of non-adherence. None of the other covariates demonstrated statistically significant associations with the outcome, including mean age (β = 0.00, p = 0.40), survey time (β = 0.01, p = 0.24), sample size (β = 0.00, p = 0.21), publication quality (β = 0.02, p = 0.50), or published year (β = −0.04, p = 0.11).

 

## Discussion

## Main findings
This systematic review and meta-analysis examined the prevalence of meeting the 24-hour movement guidelines in China, incorporating data from 68 studies with a total sample size of 832,027 participants. The analysis revealed that only 7.6% of individuals met all three 24-hour movement guidelines. Particularly, we found that in the early stages of life, only 13.3% of preschoolers and 5.1% of children and adolescents met all three guidelines. Our results also showed that only 13.0% met none of the guidelines.

## Comparison with existing literature
Low adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines is a pervasive global public health challenge, and the rates observed in Chinese youth are consistent with this international trend. For instance, according to a meta-analysis by Tapia-Serrano on the prevalence of meeting 24-hour movement guidelines in 23 countries worldwide, 7.12% of youth met all three guidelines, and 19.21% met none of the three recommendations [ ]. However, this is based on data from before 2020. The latest comprehensive evidence reveals that the prevalence of meeting all three guidelines in this group ranges from 0% to 53.3%, and most studies report adherence below 10% [ ].
In contrast to youth, adults showed a significant increase in adherence to all three guidelines. Our findings for this group are consistent with what Huang et al. (2025) found in their review [ ], with one exception that we excluded the study that exclusively involved only college students with overweight or obesity [ ]. When compared internationally, the prevalence of meeting all three guidelines in Chinese adults was lower than in the United States [ ], Slovenia [ ], Brazil [ ], and Chile [ ], but higher than in Canada [ ], South Korea [ ], Thailand [ ], and Latin American countries [ ].
Regarding older adults, only two studies were identified in this review, which were from the China Health and Nutrition Survey (CHNS) [ ] and Health Surveillance of Hubei China (HSHC) [ ] cohort. The reported adherence to all three guidelines was only 1.6% and 1.8%, which falls within the lower bound of global estimates for this population (1.6%−20.0%) [ ].

## Subgroup variations and potential explanations
Generally, adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines is low due to a complex interplay of demographic, socioeconomic, environmental, and behavioral factors [ ]. These include the natural decline in PA with increasing age, the high prevalence of ST in urban and technology-saturated environments, and the displacement of active time by academic pressures and screen use. Socioeconomic disadvantages, certain family dynamics, and environmental barriers like air pollution further exacerbate the problem, while psychological well-being and supportive settings are consistently linked to better adherence [ ].
These factors are prominent in youth. Childhood and adolescence are foundational for establishing lifelong health behaviors and metabolic patterns [ , ]. The intense academic pressures, excessive educational ST, and delayed bedtimes prevalent in this demographic likely create a “triple jeopardy” scenario [ , ], where the failure in one behavior (e.g., sleep loss due to homework) exacerbates another (e.g., increased ST and reduced PA) [ , ].
In addition to age, this review analyzed variations by gender. Although subgroup analysis failed to detect significant differences between groups with higher (≥ 50%) and lower (< 50%) female percentages, meta-regression revealed a consistent pattern: a higher proportion of female participants was associated with increased overall guideline adherence and a lower prevalence of meeting none of the recommendations. This finding appears to contrast with numerous previous studies reporting lower PA levels among females [ – ]. This seeming paradox, however, demonstrates the conceptual and practical advantage of a holistic 24-hour movement behavior paradigm compared to examining behaviors in isolation. Females generally exhibit higher levels of health consciousness and are more likely to engage in preventive health behaviors [ , ], including optimizing sleep duration [ ] and limiting recreational screen time [ ], even if their MVPA levels are often lower than males [ , ]. Additionally, females typically demonstrate better sleep hygiene practices and are more aware of the importance of adequate sleep duration, which is a critical component of the 24-hour guidelines [ ].
Finally, this study analyzed trends relative to the COVID-19 pandemic. We divided the data collection period into before and after the pandemic. Our findings contrast with previous reports by revealing an improvement in adherence to all three guidelines after the COVID-19 pandemic [ , ], with the prevalence increasing from 5.4% to 9.7%. This positive shift may be attributable to the nationwide implementation of health policies such as the “Healthy China 2030” initiative [ ]. However, a more critical finding is the persistently high prevalence of meeting none of the guidelines. We observed only a marginal and non-significant decrease, from 13.8% before the pandemic to 12.3% afterward. This indicates that a substantial vulnerable subpopulation remains structurally trapped in an unhealthy behavioral pattern. Pandemic-induced confinements, facility closures, social disruption, and the shift to online education may have exacerbated their SB, excessive ST, and sleep dysregulation, thereby reinforcing a state of complete non-adherence [ ].

## Public health and policy implications
The low prevalence rates found in this review carry significant implications for public health concerns and policymakers. This early-life deficit in healthy movement behaviors establishes a trajectory of elevated chronic disease risk that may persist for a lifetime [ ]. Conversely, the finding that college years reported the highest adherence among all life stages suggests an opportunity. Interventions during this period may be related to the maintenance of healthy behaviors throughout adulthood [ ].
Globally, China confronts socioeconomic pressures common to other countries experiencing rapid population ageing [ ]. What distinguishes China’s demographic challenge is the unprecedented scale of its elderly cohort, which is already the largest worldwide [ ]. China’s ageing burdens will increase further as the ‘second baby boomers’ (those born between 1962 and 1975) start to enter retirement in 2022 [ ]. The low adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines observed in this review threatens to significantly increase this burden, making the current lack of foundational research on the movement behaviors of older adults a major public health liability. PA, SB, and sleep are integral to the healthy aging process [ – ], with each component demonstrating significant associations with physiological resilience and functional independence in older adults. This therefore underscores the urgent need for targeted research and intervention in China’s rapidly expanding older population.
Furthermore, the results related to gender have direct policy implications. Emerging evidence suggests that the health benefits derived from 24-hour movement behaviors differ by gender. For instance, a prospective study demonstrated that although the maximal survival benefit was achieved at the same volume of activity, women experienced a substantially greater reduction in all-cause mortality (24%) compared to men (18%) [ ]. This indicates that for any given dose of PA, women may derive proportionately greater health benefits. Consequently, future guidelines and public health policies should integrate these sex-specific considerations. Such compelling evidence of enhanced benefit can also be leveraged to motivate currently inactive women, particularly those who perceive time as a primary barrier, by demonstrating a potentially greater return on investment for their effort.
In recent years, the number of policies related to PA in China has been increasing annually [ ]. The release of Healthy China Action (2019–2030) [ ] and Chinese Physical Activity Guidelines [ ] has established quantifiable assessment standards for multiple populations, which are consistent with international practices [ ]. However, as pointed out by Chen et al., there remain obvious gaps in China’s policies regarding SB and Sleep [ ]. The Healthy China Action (2019–2030) [ ] and Chinese Physical Activity Guidelines [ ] only mentioned reducing SB and set advocacy-based targets for sleep duration among different populations, rather than specific guidelines.
The COVID-19 pandemic offers a critical case study underscoring the necessity of addressing these policy gaps. While public health guidelines and policies provide an essential framework, macro-level social events, like the pandemic, go beyond their intended scope, creating acute behavioral challenges that require immediate strategic adjustments [ ]. Specifically, pandemic-related lockdowns and restrictions led to significant global reductions in PA and widespread increases in SB and disrupted sleep. Given the low baseline adherence found in this review, these societal disruptions likely amplified existing chronic disease risk factors in the Chinese population. This demonstrates that effective health policy must not only address baseline behaviors but also possess the resilience and data capture mechanisms to respond to sudden, widespread behavioral shifts.
Two key actions may be warranted. First, considering the significant public health impact of SB and sleep [ , ], their inclusion in national surveillance systems should be strongly advocated. Second, policymakers and public health stakeholders in China could look to international precedents and consider either formulating or adopting the 24-hour movement guidelines. These measures will help achieve the goal of “comprehensively controlling health risk factors” proposed in the Healthy China Action (2019–2030) [ ].

## Strengths and limitations
This systematic review sheds light on compliance with recommended levels of movement among different age categories in China. It draws data from 68 studies involving more than 800,000 subjects, which provides a relatively robust estimate based on population-supporting health policies in the country. The inclusion of age-stratified analyses has enabled a detailed examination of adherence patterns throughout the life course, revealing critical population disparities that should be prioritized in targeted interventions.
However, it should be acknowledged that clear heterogeneity is attributable to variations in demographic variations across studies, such as age distribution, geographical region, and survey period, as confirmed by subgroup and meta-regression analyses, which undermines the accuracy of pooled estimates. Notably, the measurement method (objective vs. subjective) was not identified as a significant heterogeneity source for adherence, enhancing the reliability of the pooled estimates despite diverse assessment tools. However, the predominant reliance on self-reported measures still poses a potential risk for measurement bias. Furthermore, the lack of sufficient longitudinal studies restricts trend analysis. These studies would be beneficial in the future as more adherence patterns are captured over time. Therefore, although this review adds further understanding regarding how the Chinese population adheres to movement guidelines, more research should be conducted with more uniform measures over extended periods and more device-related measures.

## Conclusion
This systematic review and meta-analysis found a critically low adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines in China (7.6%) and demonstrated a U-shaped pattern of risk across the lifespan. Children, adolescents, and older adults demonstrate particularly poor adherence, while significant geographical variations highlight regional disparities. Temporal trends show policy impacts are often behavior-specific and vulnerable to external disruptions. Future strategies must adopt an integrated, life-course approach that addresses vulnerable populations while accounting for regional diversity.
This systematic review and meta-analysis highlight the need for interventions tailored to age-specific requirements, particularly for adolescents and older adults, who demonstrated the lowest adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines. In subsequent investigations, standardized approaches must be emphasized to ensure greater comparability and create sustainable public health interventions. Therefore, we can conclude that even though general adherence to 24-hour movement guidelines is relatively low in China, there is a possibility that this rate will increase owing to growing awareness.
