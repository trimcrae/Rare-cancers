## Background
Long-term noninvasive positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy is an effective, albeit complex, intervention for obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) and/or chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure (CHRF) in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). PAP uptake and use is often reported in studies designed for other purposes. Systematic review methods were used to summarise acceptance of and adherence with PAP therapy.

## Methods
A systematic search for studies that reported the proportion of patients that either declined or accepted PAP therapy and/or objective use of newly prescribed PAP therapy in patients with COPD was conducted according to a predefined protocol (PROSPERO CRD42021259262). Meta-analysis and meta-regression techniques were used to establish summary effect estimates and explore heterogeneity.

## Results
On average, PAP therapy was declined or discontinued by 14% (95% CI 10–19) of participants, often within 6 weeks of initiation among studies reporting repeated measures. The pooled median adherence was 6.3 h·day−1 (95% CI 5.9–6.6). While meta-regression found higher acceptance (p=0.03) and shorter use per day (p<0.01) for the indication of OSA versus CHRF, this explained only some heterogeneity in the summary effect estimates. Summary of study-level variables associated with acceptance or adherence highlighted limitations in the available literature.

## Conclusions
An anticipated six out of seven patients with COPD newly prescribed PAP therapy will be successful using therapy; however, heterogeneity was substantial among studies, and only partly explained by indication for use. Prospective studies to explore barriers to use and detailed reporting of acceptance and adherence in studies on PAP therapy in patients with COPD are needed.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common chronic respiratory disease, affecting an estimated 10.3% of the global population [ ], with a rising prevalence [ ]. The symptoms and systemic manifestations of COPD result in substantial disability and mortality worldwide [ , ]. Exacerbations of COPD (ECOPD) are a leading cause of hospitalisations and mortality in developed countries [ ] and interventions that decrease ECOPD benefit patients, providers and healthcare systems.
Conditions that affect ventilation and/or work of breathing during sleep are associated with poor health outcomes in patients with COPD. Patients with COPD and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), a disease in which there is repetitive narrowing or collapse of the upper airway during sleep, experience increased hospitalisations [ – ], longer length of stay in hospital [ ] and increased risk of readmission [ , ]. Mortality is similarly increased in patients with COPD and OSA compared to patients with COPD who do not have OSA [ ]. End-stage ventilatory failure also confers a poor prognosis. Patients with COPD and chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure (CHRF) have a high mortality [ , ], with respiratory failure being a common cause of death [ ].
Positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy is a complex long-term therapy that improves health outcomes in patients with airway compromise or ventilatory failure and COPD. When used to treat patients with CHRF secondary to COPD, noninvasive ventilation (NIV) improved short-term health-related quality of life and prolonged survival in stable patients with CHRF secondary to COPD [ ]. After an ECOPD, initiation of NIV prolonged admission-free survival in patients with awake hypercapnia [ ]. In patients with OSA comorbid with COPD, patients who both accept and adhere to PAP therapy experienced fewer ECOPD, fewer hospitalisations for ECOPD and longer survival when compared to patients who do not adhere to therapy [ – ]. These studies support the conclusion that acceptance of and adherence with PAP therapy are necessary for patients to experience health benefits from this complex intervention.
Little is currently known about how to implement PAP therapy successfully in patients with COPD to maximise the proportion of patients who can both accept and adhere to therapy [ ]. While the evidence surrounding PAP therapy is often evaluated specific to indication for therapy, the patient experience becoming established on newly prescribed PAP therapy may vary among patient populations, clinical settings, clinical team supports and regional healthcare models. Additionally, within the literature and within clinical practice, there may be overlapping indications for therapy or incompletely characterised patients with COPD who are prescribed PAP therapy, particularly in obese patients with severe COPD. Summarising the acceptance and adherence from a variety of populations and study designs will provide a more nuanced understanding of success implementing PAP therapy in a variety of research and clinical contexts. This systematic review utilises descriptive, meta-analytic and meta-regression techniques to evaluate the available literature for what is known about the success and challenges of implementing PAP therapy in patients with COPD.

## Methods
The protocol was registered on 13 July 2021 in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration number CRD42021259262) [ ] and updated on 17 April 2023, while piloting the selection process. A detailed protocol was published on 3 July 2023 [ ]. The results are reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2020 statement [ ]. The methods were developed based on methodological frameworks outlined in the Cochrane Handbook of Systematic Reviews [ ].
Studies meeting the following inclusion criteria were eligible for inclusion: 1) primary research studies reporting on randomised interventional trials or observational studies; 2) studies of adults with COPD newly prescribed PAP therapy for long-term use at home; and either 3) data available on either acceptance of PAP therapy and/or 4) data available on adherence with PAP therapy using an objective measure such as device counter or machine download. Long-term PAP therapy was defined as the provision of noninvasive PAP through a mask for >7 days at home. Studies were not required to include a comparator group. Studies meeting the following criteria were excluded from the review: 1) studies in which >20% of the COPD cohort had neuromuscular or other comorbid lung diseases; 2) studies including patients with a tracheostomy; 3) studies that included patients being re-started on PAP therapy in more than a few patients; and 4) pre-specified acceptance or adherence for study inclusion or prior to reporting on the primary outcome measures. Sham PAP interventions were excluded from the primary outcome assessment. Studies that had loss to follow-up of ≥5% of the study population were excluded from contributing data to acceptance; however, these studies were retained for adherence. Crossover trials were included if measures of acceptance or adherence were provided after a trial of one PAP therapy setting/mode prior to transitioning to another. Studies with discrete groups that varied by prespecified clinically relevant covariates or by reporting of primary outcomes were separated into arms within the study, labelled A–D and synthesised separately [ ].
Acceptance of PAP therapy within 1 year was included within this review by any definition used within research studies, whether it was an affirmation of acceptance or discontinuation. Measures of acceptance prior to study enrolment were summarised but not included in the summary effect estimate.
Adherence to therapy was measured from a device counter or machine download and defined as a measure of use per unit time. All methods of reporting adherence were included in this study, including proportions and continuous measures.
For studies that reported repeated measures for acceptance or adherence, the closest measure not exceeding 1 year was used in the pooled summary estimates. If not explicitly stated, the timing of the primary outcome measure was inferred to be when similar clinical data were obtained in follow-up. Where unclear, the median/mean follow-up of patients was used as the timing of the primary outcome measure.
A systematic search was executed by an expert searcher/health librarian (S. Campbell) on the following databases: PROSPERO, OVID Medline, OVID Embase, Wiley Cochrane Library (CDSR and Central), EBSCO CINAHL, APA PsychInfo, Proquest Dissertations and Theses Global, and SCOPUS using controlled vocabulary (e.g., MeSH and Emtree) and key words representing the concepts “COPD” and “CPAP” and “patient adherence”. No language or publication limits were applied. Databases were searched from inception to 18 August 2022. Detailed search strategies are available in supplementary material S1. The search results were exported into Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation Ltd., Melbourne, Australia).
The grey literature search was executed by one author (C.R. Laratta). Forward and reverse citation checks were conducted of included articles and the publication lists of senior authors on the included studies were reviewed. Indexes of conference abstracts were hand searched from the American Thoracic Society, Canadian Thoracic Society, European Respiratory Society, American College of Chest Physicians, Associated Professional Sleep Societies, Canadian Sleep Society and the World Sleep Society for 5 years (2018–2023). A Google Scholar search was also conducted.

## Study selection and data extraction
After removing duplicate citations, the title screen was completed by a single author (C.R. Laratta) as the number of citations retrieved was >1000. Screening by title, abstract and full-text review and reasons for exclusion were documented in Covidence. Screening of studies was performed independently and in duplicate (C.R. Laratta and/or L. Moore), with a title and abstract screen followed by a full-text review with reasons for exclusion documented. The inter-rater reliability for the full text review was assessed by Cohen's Kappa and found to be 0.70. Discrepancies between reviewers was resolved by consensus.
Data were extracted by a single author (either S.W. Kirkland or C.R. Laratta) and included study design, funding, conflicts of interest, inclusion and exclusion criteria, study setting, PAP therapy implementation, follow-up details, composition of the multidisciplinary team, reported indication for therapy, and PAP therapy type. Data that were pooled within the systematic review were confirmed by a second author (either S.W. Kirkland or C.R. Laratta).

## Quality rating
The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for cohort studies [ ] was adapted to allow for an evaluation of selection bias and comparability without a comparator group based upon the reporting and characterisation of the indication for PAP therapy (selection), study population (age, sex) and spirometry (forced expiratory volume in 1 (FEV1)) (comparability). Detailed risk of bias criteria are available in supplementary material S2. Quality assessment of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) was completed with the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool version 2.0 [ ]. The risk of bias assessment applied to each primary outcome separately independently and in duplicate (C.R. Laratta and/or L. Moore).

## Statistical analysis
In the meta-analysis of acceptance, the Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation [ ] for variance stabilisation was employed as many of the studies reported zero proportions. An inverse-variance weighted random-effects meta-analysis was then performed using the restricted maximum-likelihood estimator to generate a pooled proportion. Post-estimation back-transformation was performed before summarising the study-level and pooled measures in forest plots. For the meta-analysis of adherence, since continuous measures of adherence were often skewed and reported as medians and interquartile ranges in a majority of studies, the confidence distribution method was used to convert means (and standard deviations or 95% confidence intervals) to asymptotic medians and variances in the R package metamedian. Then an overall median estimate of adherence time was generated by pooling study-level medians using a random-effects model with quantile estimation [ ].
Heterogeneity among the studies was explored with a descriptive synthesis including an effect direction plot [ , ] and sample-size weighted bubble plots. The effect direction plot is organised by variables identified within the World Health Organization (WHO) framework to address adherence to chronic medical therapies [ ]. This framework categorises variables associated with the use of chronic medical interventions as factors associated with disease severity, factors associated with therapy implementation, delivery or support, social or economic factors, patient-related factors or societal or health system factors. This framework was adapted in order to apply to PAP therapy. The sample size weighted bubble plots summarise acceptance or adherence over time with the size of the bubbles weighted by the sample size.
Heterogeneity among studies was also explored with quantitative synthesis that included stratification by prespecified variables. Of the variables that were pre-specified in the study protocol [ ], five were selected for the meta-regression prior to the data extraction being competed and were chosen from pre-specified variables of interest that were reported sufficiently among studies. These five variables were as follows: 1) indication for PAP therapy; 2) hypercapnia; 3) categorical grading of the severity of COPD by FEV1; 4) type of PAP therapy; and 5) pressure intensity of NIV. Detailed definitions of these variables are available in supplementary material S3. Indication for therapy was designated post hoc as the indication that was appropriate for ≥70% of each study cohort. For both outcomes, two-sided z-statistics were computed to test for subgroup differences in the stratified analyses. Univariable and multivariable random-effects linear meta-regression models were fitted to quantify the associations between both outcomes and the prespecified characteristics. Additional analyses explored included tests for outliers and influential studies as well as sensitivity analyses (comparative fixed-effects models and random-effects models stratified by publication type and study design).
All statistical analyses were performed using Stata release 18.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA) and R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) in the RStudio 2023.12.1 environment (Posit PBC, Boston, MA, USA). Hypothesis testing was conducted at a two-tailed alpha level of 5%.

## Results

## Study flow
The database search identified 2200 citations. After excluding duplicates, 1801 citations were reviewed for inclusion. The title screen resulted in the removal of 544 citations, with the subsequent exclusion of 1069 citations during title and abstract screening. A full-text review was performed on 188 citations, following which 45 were included ( ). The grey literature search resulted in a further 196 citations undergoing full-text review, following which 42 citations were included.
 

## Critical review
Of the 87 included studies ( ), 76 were journal articles, 10 were abstracts [ – ] and one was a thesis report [ ]. Other than 22 parallel group RCTs [ – ] and five crossover RCTs [ – ], the studies were observational in design. 14 studies were multicentre [ , – , , , , , , , , – ]. Sources of the recruited participants were clinical research units, hospitals, respirology clinics, home mechanical ventilation (HMV) programmes, polysomnography (PSG) laboratories or respiratory homecare companies. One study sourced patients from a cloud-based system for PAP adherence [ ]. Of the included studies, 24% had an objective specific to acceptance or adherence [ , , , , , – , , – ].
 There were 110 arms of data from the included studies, with each arm varying by relevant clinical variables such as the populations for which acceptance and adherence were reported, indication for PAP therapy, location of the PAP titration, mode/settings of NIV or the supports provided after PAP therapy was recommended. Demographic data were available for 91% of participants; however, missing data predominantly applied to patients who passed away or patients that did not accept or adhere to therapy. For the study population included in this review, body mass index was consistently reported, with only 10% of studies not reporting this characteristic [ , , , , – ]. Characterisation of a measure of COPD severity classification using FEV1 or Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) stage was less consistently reported, with 20% of included studies not reporting this characteristic [ , , , , – , , , , – ]. Few studies reported other markers of COPD disease severity such as frequency of ECOPD or use of long-term oxygen therapy. OSA was not routinely reported, particularly in studies when CHRF was the indication for therapy, with 42% of included studies reporting use of sleep diagnostic testing to evaluate for OSA within their study protocol. Pressure settings prescribed for PAP therapy were reported in 52% of the included studies. Baseline symptom burden or change in symptoms with PAP therapy were not consistently available due to variability in assessment and reporting among studies.
The description of programme supports provided during the implementation of PAP therapy was either insufficiently detailed to allow for the extraction of relevant information or did not vary significantly on relevant variables to allow for comparison across studies. The overall reporting of variables identified a priori that may impact the success with implementation of PAP therapy but were either infrequently reported or had limited variability are summarised in supplementary material S4, table S4. Titrations of PAP therapy were performed in hospitals, ventilation units or during PSG, with rare titrations within a home environment [ , ]. Of the included studies, 29% reported involving multidisciplinary team members in the follow-up of patients. 18% of studies had clinical follow-up provided by clinicians who were nurses [ , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , ], 3% nurse practitioners [ , , ], 10 % respiratory therapists [ , , , – , , , ], 2 % technicians or technologists [ , ], 3 % psychologists [ , , ], 1 % physical therapists [ ], or 1 % speech therapists [ ]. The involvement of physicians with specialities other than pulmonologists or ventilation/sleep specialists was reported in one study [ ]. The timing of initial follow-up was often within 1 –3  months, with a few studies reporting the initial follow-up within 2  weeks [ , , , ], between 4 –6  months [ , , , , , ], annual follow-up [ , , ] and 10 % of included studies reporting unclear timing of follow-up [ , , – , , , , ]. While several studies reported having home [ , , ] or virtual [ , , , ] assessments, this level of detail was often not available.

## Acceptance of PAP therapy
70 arms from 62 studies, involving 3532 patients were included for the analysis of acceptance. Measures of acceptance reported in the included studies were the proportion of patients who declined or discontinued therapy, rather than the proportion of patients who could use therapy. The definitions reported are summarised in supplementary material S4, table S1. 57 arms from 52 studies reported acceptance within 1 year, involving 2737 participants, median sample size 23 (interquartile range (IQR) 14, 54), median age 64 years. Summary estimates of the average pooled proportion of patients with COPD who declined or discontinued PAP therapy within 1 year was 14% (95% CI 10–19), with significant heterogeneity among studies ( ).
 A stratified meta-analysis followed by a linear meta-regression explored heterogeneity in the summary effect estimate. On stratified analysis, three pre-specified subgroup analyses demonstrated a departure of the summary effect estimates from their comparator ( ). When a backward elimination procedure was applied to the linear meta-regression, indication for therapy was the only significant covariate, where fewer patients declined or discontinued therapy when the indication for treatment was OSA comorbid with COPD rather than CHRF secondary to COPD (stratified pooled mean proportion 6%, 95% CI 1–14 versus 17.0%, 95% CI 11.0–24.0; unadjusted β=−0.32, 95% CI −0.64–−0.01, p-value=0.04). Details of the linear meta-regression results are available in the supplementary material S4, table S2. The heterogeneity remained high in the stratified analysis of indication for therapy (I2=94.1% versus 88.6%, respectively).
 The timing of the acceptance measure was not anticipated to explain heterogeneity of the summary effect estimate as using quantitative and qualitative synthesis, a lack of acceptance of PAP therapy was found to occur shortly after the prescription of therapy. In post hoc meta-regression analysis with time as the explanatory variable, acceptance was not dependent on the time of reporting the measure (see supplementary material S4, figure S1). Five studies collecting repeated measures of PAP acceptance within 6 months of the initial prescription reported the majority of those who declined or discontinued therapy did so at the time of initiation or within 6 weeks [ , , , , ], with another study supporting this at its earliest measure reported of 3 months [ ].
Acceptance prior to research participation was reported in four prospective studies as the proportion of participants who declined to undergo titration at the time of being approached for enrolment in a study providing CPAP [ ] or NIV [ , , ]. When synthesised, 7% (95% CI 3–12) of patients approached for participation in prospective research declined to participate due to the use of CPAP or NIV in the study ( ). These data suggest that the acceptance data for prospective studies may underestimate the proportion who decline or discontinue therapy by 7% (95% CI 3–12) unless participants are asked for this information when enrolled.

## Adherence with PAP therapy
77 arms from 60 studies, involving 10 992 patients, contributed data to adherence. The median sample size was 31 (IQR 15, 58). The units and definitions of adherence were reported as either a continuous measure (hours of use per day) or as a proportion. Detailed summaries of the definitions used is in supplementary material S4, table S1. Use in hours·day−1 was reported in 66 arms of 51 studies, involving 2838 participants with a median sample size of 20 (IQR 11, 47) and a median age 65 years. The median pooled adherence was 6.3 h·day−1 (95% CI 5.9–6.6), with significant heterogeneity among studies ( ).
A stratified meta-analysis followed by a linear meta-regression explored heterogeneity in the summary effect estimate. The stratified meta-analysis demonstrated departure of the summary effect estimates in three variables ( ). Using backward elimination, the final model of the linear meta-regression analysis was a univariate model of indication for therapy, where use of PAP therapy was less in patients with OSA comorbid with COPD rather than CHRF secondary to COPD (pooled median use 5.54 h·day−1, 95% CI 5.03–6.05 versus 6.99 h·day−1, 95% CI 6.46–7.52; unadjusted β=−1.43, 95% CI −2.09– −0.77, p-value=0.004). A detailed summary of the full adjusted model and the model using backward elimination is in supplementary material S4, table S3. Heterogeneity remained high in the stratified analysis (I2=99.9% versus 99.9%, respectively). For studies with an indication for therapy in the “other” category, the pooled median use of PAP therapy is unlikely clinically meaningful even though it was significant as these studies either lacked a clearly defined indication for therapy or had indications for therapy such as eucapnic COPD or high healthcare utilisation that are not clearly associated with literature demonstrating improved health outcomes with use.
The timing of the reported adherence measure may contribute to heterogeneity in the summary effect estimate. In a post hoc analysis, adherence measures at 6 and 12 months were more homogeneous than measures obtained within 1 month or >12 months. A sample size-weighted bubble plot is available in supplementary material S4, figure S2. When this finding was further explored through descriptive analysis of studies with repeated measures, the time to achieve greatest adherence varied among studies, with some studies reporting stable adherence over time [ , , , ], increasing use between 6–12 weeks followed by stability [ , ] or increasing use between 3 months and 1 year [ ]. Despite time contributing to the heterogeneity, the confidence intervals in the beta estimates for adherence at all time intervals remained wide, indicating that time is insufficient to explain all of the variation in adherence. At 6 months, the beta estimate was 1.67 (95% CI 0.50–2.83) and at 12 months the beta estimate was 1.24 (95% CI 0.12–2.36) in the linear meta-regression model with time as the independent variable.

## Study-level variables associated with acceptance of or adherence with PAP therapy
An effect direction plot summarises associations between study-level variables and acceptance or adherence ( ). This is accompanied by a figure summarising relevant variables and the strength of evidence associating these with the primary outcomes (see supplementary materials S4, figure S3). The available evidence is limited to a few categories of variables that may be impactful after adapting the WHO framework of barriers to adherence to chronic medical therapies [ ]. The majority of associations were reported in narrative form or assessed via unadjusted comparisons. Analyses that accounted for some confounders were reported in studies designed to evaluate aspects of PAP therapy implementation on acceptance or adherence, such as location of titration [ ], NIV settings [ ], use of telemedicine [ ], addressing comorbid insomnia [ ] and timing of the NIV implementation after ECOPD [ ].
 

## Sensitivity analyses, including assessments for outliers and influential studies
For adherence, evidence of small sample size effects was observed; however, this was not the case for acceptance. The leave-one-out meta-analysis indicated that no single study disproportionately influenced the results for either outcome. Additionally, sensitivity analyses evaluating variations based on the type of model used, study design and publication rigour demonstrated that the pooled estimates remained robust across these factors.

## Methodological quality of the included studies
Of the 62 observational studies included in this systematic review, 59 did not have a comparison arm. The majority of studies were of good quality. Detailed risk of bias assessments for acceptance and adherence are shown in supplementary materials S4, figures S4 and S5. For both primary outcomes, poor quality scores were commonly due to either a lack of clarity in the indication for therapy or limited characterisation of study participants who did not tolerate or adhere to PAP therapy. Studies also received lower quality scores if prior PAP use was not explicitly excluded or if adherence measures were obtained at different time intervals with only a single-point estimate of adherence reported. Given that the primary objective was not to report acceptance or adherence for most included studies, study reporting may have been at times incomplete. For example, few studies reported the exact number of participants who contributed data to the adherence measure; however, in a few studies with detailed reporting the number of participants contributing data varied across follow-up intervals [ , ]. Adherence data for patients who died or were lost to follow-up during the study were also inconsistently reported.

## Discussion
To experience the health benefits of PAP therapy, patients with COPD must accept and use the therapy as prescribed. This systematic review summarises what is known about acceptance of and adherence to PAP therapy among patients with COPD. The premise of pooling acceptance and adherence across indications for PAP therapy acknowledges the challenges in assessing for and applying current clinical case definitions for CHRF and/or OSA in patients with COPD. Pooling of these data also allows a more complete understanding of variables beyond indication to therapy that may account for why some studies have high success establishing patients on therapy while others do not demonstrate the same success.
This study demonstrates that patients with COPD are often successful in starting and adhering to PAP therapy. Six of seven patients with COPD who are prescribed PAP therapy for any indication will accept therapy. While acceptance was higher in patients with OSA than CHRF, systematic review methods to explore heterogeneity among studies did not clarify why acceptance was low in some studies and high in others. Similarly, adherence to PAP therapy was found to be high in patients with COPD, with a pooled median usage of 6.2 h·day−1. While patients with OSA had a shorter daily usage than patients with CHRF, the adherence was still acceptable (5.5 h·day−1). The timing of the adherence measure may contribute to heterogeneity among studies for adherence; however, systematic review methods did not clarify differences in adherence among studies. The lack of clarity in these results is partly due to key limitations in reporting, including limited characterisation of study populations for disease severity of CHRF, OSA or COPD, variable descriptions or limited reporting of the indication for therapy, and limited reporting of factors specific to programme implementation or health systems that vary among studies and health systems.
This review also contributes a detailed descriptive summary of study-level variables that are associated with acceptance or adherence with newly prescribed PAP therapy in patients with COPD; however, other than a few well-designed studies, most variables were identified in unadjusted analyses and pertained to a limited number of factors such as device intolerance, which was nonspecific and may encompass a broad spectrum of concerns related to mask fit or mask tolerance, device settings or ventilator dyssynchrony.
The study was performed with methodological rigour including using a registered protocol, employing a comprehensive search strategy to avoid publication bias and sophisticated analytic strategies. The study design was carefully structured to exclude studies that select for accepting or adherent populations in order to minimise selection bias, increasing confidence in the summary effect estimates. A strength of this review was the synthesis of acceptance and adherence data for PAP therapy in adult patients that is generalisable to all patients with COPD, irrespective of disease severity, indication for PAP therapy or mode of PAP therapy. By doing so, synthesis techniques, meta-analysis and meta-regression explore the entire body of available literature to summarise what is known about successes or barriers to implementing therapy.
Limitations of this systematic review and meta-analysis include that acceptance of and/or adherence with PAP therapy were not the primary outcome of included studies and reporting was often not detailed. Stanchina
et al. [ ] report that death during the study period was associated with low adherence, highlighting how a lack of reporting for this population could introduce bias in the summary estimate. Despite efforts to limit selection bias, some studies required that patients attend a follow-up appointment after a pre-specified period or have a complete dataset for retrospective studies, which may introduce bias. Where it was possible to ascertain that acceptance or adherence were pre-specified prior to reporting acceptance or adherence, these studies were excluded, so this is anticipated to affect a minority of included studies. Acceptance estimates for prospective studies may also be underestimated by approximately 7% for those studies that did not record lack of acceptance of PAP therapy as the reason for declining to participate in a study, a limitation which is a novel finding of this systematic review. That said, after being enrolled in a study, the studies were only included in the acceptance estimate if they had <5% loss of participants during follow-up in order to avoid introducing bias in the summary effect estimate. Key demographics may not be represented in the available literature as missing demographics were predominantly participants who died, were lost to follow-up or who declined or did not adhere to therapy. Adherence was often provided as a point estimate rather than reported with the number of patients contributing data and the timing at which the adherence was obtained. Finally, as many included studies recruited patients from sleep programmes or HMV programmes, the acceptance and adherence observed may not be generalisable to other settings. While the larger number of studies included in this meta-analysis may also contribute to heterogeneity, these other factors are likely also contributory.
The summarised variables that have been associated with acceptance or adherence to PAP therapy in patients with COPD align with research in more heterogenous populations with longitudinal follow-up [ ]. Similar to in patients with restrictive or neuromuscular disorders, home NIV titration does not seem to impact treatment adherence [ ]. Of note, there are likely many variables that have not yet been explored that impact the acceptance and adherence in patients with COPD, such as mental health and other comorbidities, as these have been shown to be relevant in other populations that require PAP therapy [ ].
Further research to improve the implementation of PAP therapy in patients with COPD requires improved reporting of acceptance and adherence and the integration of prospective tools into study designs that measure or address barriers to implementation. Future studies would benefit from detailed reporting of relevant clinical variables that classify patients into working case definitions for CHRF or OSA. Comprehensive data collection and reporting of acceptance and adherence would include cloud-based or remote monitoring of acceptance and adherence as well as reporting of standardised units of measurement. Detailed reporting of demographics, clinical characteristics, acceptance of and adherence with PAP therapy for patients that are unsuccessful with implementation or who pass away after a new PAP start would inform PAP implementation. Tools for use in prospective research that capture reasons for a lack of acceptance or adherence could be incorporated into study designs and reported. These tools could also facilitate exploration of the impact of a broad scope of variables within the WHO framework on success with implementation. Finally, detailed reporting of programme structure and supports, particularly for programmes that have high acceptance and adherence among their patients, would inform programme implementation in regions where success with implementation is not as high.

## Conclusion
An anticipated six out of seven patients with COPD prescribed PAP therapy successfully initiate treatment and achieve clinically meaningful daily use. Significant heterogeneity was identified among studies for both acceptance and adherence, only some of which were explained by the indication for therapy. Barriers to the use of PAP therapy that contribute to the observed heterogeneity are not yet fully understood. Detailed reporting of programme structure and teams that achieved high acceptance and adherence would be beneficial to aid in programme design. Research dedicated to exploring barriers to the use of PAP therapy in patients with COPD would lead to a greater understanding of how to support patients with COPD when they initiate PAP therapy.

## Points for clinical practice
Six of seven patients with COPD accept PAP therapy, and use is substantial with a pooled median adherence to PAP therapy of 6.3 h·day−1.
Patients with COPD and a sleep-related breathing disorder prescribed PAP therapy have a higher acceptance and fewer hours of use per day than patients with COPD and CHRF; however, indication for therapy only partly explains heterogeneity among the study estimates for acceptance or adherence.
The high heterogeneity among study estimates for acceptance and adherence is not yet understood, with the majority of available research in the areas of PAP implementation, delivery or support and/or factors associated with disease severity for COPD, CHRF or OSA.
Further research is needed to better understand indicators of success with PAP therapy implementation in patients with COPD in order to inform policy, clinical programmes and patient care.
