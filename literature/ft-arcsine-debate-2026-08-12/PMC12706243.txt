## Abstract

## Introduction
Sleep problems are an escalating global health concern, with prevalence estimates ranging from 8.3% to 45%. Physicians are disproportionately affected, with rates around 44% compared with 36% in the general population. In Bangladesh, reported rates range from 32% to 58%, with physicians being particularly vulnerable. Poor sleep among physicians is strongly linked to burnout, medical errors and increased mental health risks. Despite these serious implications, existing evidence from Bangladesh remains fragmented and inconsistent, limiting its utility for health policy and workforce interventions. This review therefore seeks to generate reliable pooled prevalence estimates and identify key determinants of sleep problems among Bangladeshi physicians.

## Methods and analysis
The research team will search the PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, EMBASE, PsycInfo, ProQuest Medical, CINAHL, Google Scholar and BanglaJOL electronic and regional databases following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidelines for published studies from inception until 1 August 2025, using truncated and phrase-searched keywords, relevant medical subject headings and citation chaining from grey literature. Observational cross-sectional studies published within the predefined timeframe, using validated assessment tools, and published in English or other major international languages will be prioritised for inclusion. Review papers, case reports, case series, intervention studies, commentaries, preprints, meeting abstracts, protocols, unpublished articles and letters will be excluded. Two independent reviewers will screen the retrieved papers using the Rayyan web-based application, with any disagreements resolved by a third reviewer. Quantitative estimates of sleep problems, including prevalence, duration, quality and associated risk factors among Bangladeshi physicians will be extracted. A narrative synthesis and meta-analysis will be performed to assess the pooled prevalence using a random effects meta-analysis model. Forest and funnel plots will be generated for visualisation. Heterogeneity will be assessed using the I2 statistic, with sensitivity or subgroup analysis conducted as required. The quality of included studies will be evaluated using Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal tools for observational study designs. All statistical analysis will be conducted using Jamovi V2.7.6, R V4.3.2 ‘meta’ packages and GraphPad Prism V9.0.2.

## Ethics and dissemination
This review will synthesise evidence from existing published literature. While completing the findings, the findings will be submitted to an international peer-reviewed journal and disseminated through conferences, policy forums and stakeholders’ networks to inform future research and interventions.

## STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY
This review will follow established methodological guidelines, adhering to their respective checklists.
Inter-rater reliability between independent reviewers will be evaluated using percentage agreement.
Cross-sectional and web-based surveys will be considered to enhance the breadth of evidence synthesis.
Methodological heterogeneity across studies may limit comparability and generalisability of the findings.
Reliance on self-reported measures of sleep problems in included studies may introduce recall bias.

## Introduction
 Sleep is a fundamental human behaviour that plays a crucial role in maintaining physiological homeostasis and supporting both physical and mental health. Trajectories of sleep problems, a major global health concern, include a range of complaints (eg, poor sleep quality, difficulties in initiating or maintaining sleep, early morning awakening, short or long sleep duration, excessive daytime sleepiness and insomnia, etc). Recent evidence suggests that the prevalence of sleep problems has been increased globally over time. For instance, a 4.3% increase in the prevalence of insomnia has been reported in a global literature review-based studies over a period of 25 years (11.9%–16.2% from 2000 to 2010). A range of epidemiological studies indicates that sleep disturbances are highly prevalent among the general population, with reported prevalence estimates varying widely from 8.3% to 45% globally. Sleep disturbances are associated with numbers of negative health and societal outcomes including mental disorder, metabolic health, cardiovascular outcome and increased risk of mortality. In addition, poor sleep quality is also associated with fatigue, reduced quality of life, psychological impairments, low mood and decreased productivity. Additionally, insomnia has also been linked to a 2.5–4.5-fold increased risk of accidents and may negatively affect the immune system, leading to adverse health outcomes. Both high-income countries and low- and middle-income countries had observed a higher prevalence of poor sleep quality, 29.8% (28.2%–31.5%), 16.6% (4%–40%) across the general population, respectively.
The medical profession is considered highly stressful, including academic stress, extensive study period, work-life imbalance, long working hours, night shifts and roster-based duties. , Comparing with the general population, physicians faced a high risk of fatigue and burn-out due to the nature of the profession and appeared to be vulnerable to inadequate sleep. The prevalence of poor sleep quality among physicians in China is 39.2%1, in Kuwait 78.8%, in Bangladesh 51%, in Brazil 5.74%, in Ethiopia 58.9% and in Turkey 57%. A systematic review and meta-analysis reported that the prevalence of sleep problems among physicians was 44%, compared with the general population at 36%. A comparative illustrative figure of previously reported global and regional prevalence of sleep problems among healthcare professionals is presented in as illustrative background. A recent study in Bangladesh reported that 46.2% medical students experienced inadequate sleep (only 4–6 hours per night), and 57.8% reported alterations in sleep cycles after the initiation of their undergraduate degree, while similar findings were reported from the Bangladeshi physician community. , Other studies from Bangladesh reported the pooled prevalence of sleep problems among the general population to be 45% (32%–58%), while a higher prevalence of 58.4% was reported among university students. Poor sleep is a common phenomenon among Bangladeshi physicians, which could result in negative personal health outcomes, poor-quality patient care, increased medical errors, academic life, and this mostly affects their overall mental health. Although few studies had explored the sleep quality and associated factors in Bangladesh, the prevalence varied greatly across studies, ranging from 18.6% to 55.1%. In addition, numerous sociodemographic, environmental, psychological and lifestyle factors including working environment, economic condition, gender differences, professional category, professional conflict and lifestyle factors (like smoking, stress, etc) are influencing the overall sleep disorder. Global studies reported work schedule (shift work), chronic symptoms, working characteristics, lack of social support, anxiety, depression, frontline work nature, insomnia history, sedentary lifestyle and exposure to infection were the potential triggering factors for poor sleep quality among the healthcare professionals, particularly across the physicians. These triggering factors, along with inadequate sleeping trajectories, were associated with a substantially increased risk of developing future mental health problems, at least four times higher (OR=3.74; 2.76 to 5.07) compared with individuals without sleep disturbances. 
 Sleep disturbances among physicians are a critical occupational but less studied, health concern linked to cognitive impairment and compromised patient safety. Although several studies in Bangladesh have investigated this issue, their findings vary widely due to differences in study design, populations and assessment tools, limiting interpretability and representativeness. This systematic review and meta-analysis protocol aims to describe the methodologies for synthesising existing evidence to provide an overall estimate of sleep disturbance among Bangladeshi physicians while also exploring heterogeneity across studies. Rather than focusing solely on pooled prevalence, the analysis will identify consistent risk factors and contextual influences such as work schedules and institutional settings that contribute to poor sleep. By clarifying evidence patterns and methodological gaps, this study will further inform future research strategies and guide policymakers in developing targeted, context-specific interventions to improve physicians’ well-being and patient safety in Bangladesh.

## Aim and research question
The aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis will be to synthesise available evidence on the prevalence, duration and quality of sleep problems, as well as their associated risk factors, among physicians in Bangladesh. The review will explore contextual determinants including sociodemographic, professional and institutional determinants that may influence sleep health in this population. Guided by the PICOT framework (Population, Intervention/Exposure, Condition/Comparator, Outcome and Timeframe), the research question will be ‘What is the prevalence and pattern of sleep problems, and what are the associated risk factors among physicians of Bangladesh?’. The question will aim to synthesise existing evidence on the pooled prevalence of sleep problems among Bangladeshi physicians and identify key determinants that influence their trajectories of sleep quality.

## Methods

## Study design
This study will be a systematic review and meta-analysis in design, adhering to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) reporting guidelines, and the Meta-analysis of observational studies in Epidemiology for observational studies. It will systematically explore, search and synthesise existing literature on the prevalence of sleep problems and its associated risk factors among Bangladeshi physicians. The study protocol has already been registered in PROSPERO. 

## Eligibility criteria
The study will include all empirical studies including (1) available full texts, (2) published from inception to 1 August 2025, ensuring comprehensive coverage of both recent and earlier research capturing a substantial wide range. All relevant studies (3) published in English or other major languages (French, Japanese, Spanish, Chinese, German) reporting sleep problems among Bangladeshi physicians will be considered for inclusion. Two authors are fluent in the included languages, and any remaining articles will be translated collaboratively by the team or via professional services to minimise language bias and capture relevant evidence from non-English-speaking countries. Observational studies including cross-sectional and web-based surveys will be included in this study, (4) focused on sleep problems (eg, insomnia, sleep disorders, sleep quality, sleep disturbances and sleep duration) across the physicians. In addition, (5) studies reporting on sleep outcomes and associated factors, excluding those centred primarily on other diseases, except when assessing co-occurring anxiety or depression.

## Information sources
The information sources for this study will include a comprehensive and advanced search strategies of several electronic databases including Medline (via PubMed), Scopus (via Elsevier), Web of Science (via Clarivate Analytics), EMBASE (via Elsevier), PsycInfo (via APA/Ovid), ProQuest Medical (via ProQuest), CINAHL (via EBSCOhost) and Google Scholar (via Google) to identify relevant literature. In addition, grey literature and regional databases like Bangladesh Journals Online (BanglaJOL via INASP) will also be explored. Reference lists of included studies and relevant systematic reviews will be reviewed to identify additional studies through citation chaining technique (via snowballing). The search strategy will incorporate key search terms related to both exposure and outcome, and database-specific filters will further be applied to refine and optimise the final search results.

## Search strategy
Prior to performing the systematic search on selected electronic and regional databases, initial searches will be conducted integrating medical subject headings (MeSH) to identify relevant keywords related to the research problem. Furthermore, a comprehensive search strategy will be developed in consultation with an expert systematic reviewer, which will be adapted for the selected bibliographic databases and combined with MeSH terms. In view of the two key concepts of (population+sleep-related outcomes), including ‘physicians’ plus ‘sleep disturbance’ or ‘sleep problems’ or ‘sleep quality’ or ‘sleep duration’ or ‘excessive daytime sleepiness’ or ‘sleep disorder’ or ‘sleep habit’ or ‘sleep hygiene’ related terms and synonyms will be identified and cross-checked with a second reviewer (systematic review expert) for their adequacy and coverage of the topic. The tentative search strategy for electronic databases is illustrated in and other predefined databases summarising the key search strategies ( ). To ensure retrieval of all relevant articles, Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) will be used to combine pre-specified keywords based on the key concepts, ensuring a comprehensive search of the literature.
 

## Condition/domain being studied
The conditions being studied include sleep problems (eg, sleep duration, sleep quality, daytime sleepiness) and associated risk factors among physicians of Bangladesh.

## Population/participants (P)
The review will include studies from all ethnicities and genders across Bangladesh, encompassing medical and dental doctors (postgraduate medical doctors or students, registered physicians, practising physicians, residents and non-resident doctors), physicians (clinical and public health specialists, general practitioners and intern doctors). Both public and private sector physicians will be included in this study irrespective of urban, rural community and subject specialisation.

## Exposure (E)
Being physicians with inadequate sleep and having any risk factors related to sleep problems (sociodemographic, psychosocial, psychological, academic/work-related and lifestyle factors).

## Comparator(s)/control (C)
There will be no comparison group. Where applicable, comparisons between subgroups (eg, gender, professional type (eg, interns, residents, practising physicians, consultants), specialisation, physicians’ stage (eg, junior doctors, senior doctors), work shift, population group, data collection period, instruments used, daytime sleepiness, socioeconomic status).

## Context (C)
Understanding prevalence, duration and quality of sleep problems. In addition, associated risk factors and the role of institutional support mechanisms in mitigating sleep problems among Bangladeshi physicians.

## Timeframe (T)
No restriction on publication year; all available observational cross-sectional prevalence studies from inception up to 1 August 2025 will be included.

## Exclusion criteria

## For the study to be excluded
Review papers, case reports and case series, interventional studies, study protocols, books, chapters, preprints, meeting abstracts, preprints, meeting abstracts, commentaries and letters or editorials.
Not providing sufficient quantitative estimate to aggregate prevalence of sleep problems.
Studies that reported results for physicians with non-physicians (general population) in the same group, without specifying a subgroup analysis.
Full-text inaccessible studies.
Unpublished studies.
Could not undergo a full review due to the unavailability of their complete texts.
Studies published in languages other than English, French, Japanese, Spanish, Chinese or German, or studies for which full texts could not be translated by the review team, will be excluded.
Studies not using any appropriate measure for assessing level of sleep problem, such as Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), Athens Insomnia Scale (AIS) for insomnia, two-item Sleep Condition Indicator, the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) and Stanford Sleepiness Scale (SSS) for daytime sleepiness.

## For the population/ participants to be excluded
Non-physicians.
Physicians outside Bangladesh.
Studies focusing exclusively on caregivers or family members of patients rather than physicians.
Studies on paediatric or adolescent populations not enrolled as healthcare trainees, and university students with voluntary engagement in healthcare services.
Mixed-population studies without sub-specifying physicians in Bangladesh.

## Outcomes and measures
The anticipated outcome of this study would be to report the prevalence of sleep problems among physicians. The specific measures would be to (1) report the pooled mean duration of sleep per night, (2) determine the prevalence of poor sleep quality as measured by the index, (3) determine the prevalence of excessive daytime sleepiness, (4) investigate age, gender and country as covariates/factors of sleep quality and excessive daytime sleepiness among Physicians. Eligible instruments for assessing poor sleep quality include the PSQI or modified Bangla PSQI, where a global score >5 indicates poor sleep quality ; the ISI, scored 0–28, with standardised cut-off ≥8 indicating subthreshold and ≥15 clinical insomnia and the AIS, scored 0–24, with ≥6 suggesting insomnia. The ESS, ranging from 0 to 24, classifies scores ≥10 as excessive daytime sleepiness, while the SSS, rated on a 7-point scale, considers ≥3–4 as excessive sleepiness. According to validated instruments, sleep quality will be categorised as good or poor, and where applicable, severity will be further classified into mild, moderate or severe sleep problems based on the scoring thresholds of each tool (eg, PSQI, ISI, AIS, ESS, SSS). However, if a study reports multiple instruments, a predefined hierarchy (ISI→PSQI→AIS→ESS→SSS→others) will be used to extract data from the highest-priority tool based on clinical relevance and measurement robustness. In addition to estimating the pooled prevalence of sleep problems, this review will also meta-analyse the risk factors associated with sleep problems. Effect measures such as ORs, relative risks (RRs), HRs or correlation coefficients (rs), along with their corresponding 95% CIs, will be extracted from the included studies. Where possible, data will be pooled across studies, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be performed further based on the type of risk factor (eg, sociodemographic, psychosocial, lifestyle, institutional factors).

## Patient and public involvement
This present systematic review and meta-analysis protocol does not directly involve any individual patients or public agencies.

## Study records

## Data management
EndNote V.21.0 reference management software (Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA) will be used to manage references retrieved from the database searches. Search results will be merged, and duplicates will be removed. Manually identified articles will also be added to the EndNote library. After de-duplication, the remaining records will be exported to the web-based application ‘Rayyan QCRI’ for title and abstracts screening, as well as reviewer collaboration. Citation abstracts and full-text articles will be uploaded to Rayyan to facilitate system screening.

## Study selection process
Study selection will follow the PRISMA flow diagram to summarise inclusion and exclusion ( ). Two independent reviewers (MIZ and IA) will screen titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria, with inter-rater agreement assessed; ≥80% agreement will be considered acceptable. Disagreements will be resolved by another reviewer (ATS). Full texts of potentially eligible articles will then be assessed independently by two reviewers (MIZ and IA), with discrepancies resolved through discussion with a third reviewer (ATS). Reasons for exclusion at each stage will be documented.
 

## Data extraction
Data extraction will be performed independently by the first author using a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet (Microsoft Corporation, Washington, USA) and cross-checked by the second (MIZ) and third (IA) author. A standardised form will capture study characteristics including author name, publication year, study design, sample size, sampling method, population demographics and assessment tools with their defined cut-off scores. The primary focus will be on extracting data related to the prevalence, duration and quality of sleep. Furthermore, information regarding the associated risk factors will be collected, covering sociodemographic, academia, psychosocial, psychological and lifestyle domains (eg, stress, burnout, peer pressure, financial hardship, social stigma, physical activity, substance use, smartphone use and relationship conflict). Contextual factors such as socioeconomic status, gender and institutional support systems will also be recorded where available. Finally, data on mental health support mechanisms (eg, counselling, peer support, institutional programmes, online services, wellness and voluntary initiatives) will be extracted to explore their role in mitigating sleep-related problems and associated psychological outcomes.

## Statistical analysis
For statistical analysis, both quantitative and qualitative (meta-narrative analysis) data will be extracted and analysed. The prevalence of sleep problems will be extracted from each study in this review to derive a pooled effect estimate with the inverse variance method, which will be involved in calculating the weighted average using SE. To stabilise variances, particularly for proportions near 0 or 1, the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation (Transform→Pool→Back-transform→pooled prevalence in the original 0–1 scale) will be applied before pooling to account for between-study heterogeneity. A meta-regression test will be adopted to assess if covariates like age, sample size and quality score of the study are affecting the pooled prevalence. Furthermore, studies reporting ORs or RRs will be converted to a common effect size, log OR (ln(OR)), for consistency and easy interpretability. Studies reporting median scores will be converted to means and SD for better comparability across the included studies to ascertain methodological rigor. The results will be combined using a random effects meta-analysis model (REML) using Jamovi V2.7.6, R statistical package V4.3.2 in-built meta-packages and GraphPad Prism V9.0.2. This model will be adopted as it not only considers sampling errors and possible heterogeneity across the studies, but also the true effect sizes of the individual studies. 
Furthermore, only if appropriate, a subgroup meta-analysis will also be conducted to explore the source of heterogeneity across age, gender, geographic location, timeframe, socioeconomic or demographic context. This subgroup analysis will allow a deeper understanding of the observed heterogeneity and assess effect modifications, ensuring more precise estimates and targeted interpretations. The estimated pooled prevalence will be presented in forest plots with 95% CIs. Funnel plots will be used to visually assess publication bias using Egger’s test (when ≥10 studies to meta-analyse). For the probable publication bias, the fill and trim method will be adopted (when substantial to high heterogeneity (I² ≥50%) or visual funnel plot asymmetry will be present), considering small-study effects. Risk factors will be narratively synthesised and where possible, quantitative estimates will be analysed using inverse variance model to aggregate the findings. Moreover, we will also plan to perform sensitivity analyses using the Jackknife method (eg, excluding lower-quality studies or those with a higher risk of bias (RoB)) to identify potential sources of heterogeneity and assess the robustness of the study results.

## Assessment of heterogeneity
Based on the extracted available data, a heterogeneity analysis will be performed. The χ2 test (test level: α=0.05) and I2 will be used to test heterogeneity. If p˃0.05, there is no heterogeneity among the studies that will be considered. If p˂0.05, there is heterogeneity among the included studies. I2 represents the level of heterogeneity across the studies: if I2 is between 0%–40% (might not be important), 30%–60% (may represent moderate heterogeneity), 50%–90% (may represent substantial heterogeneity) and 75%–100% (considerable heterogeneity). 

## Sensitivity analysis
Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the influence of individual studies on the overall pooled estimates, by sequentially excluding one study at each time. Additional sensitivity analyses will be performed based on sample size, methodological quality, choice of statistical model (fixed-effect vs random-effects) and missing data. Furthermore, studies rated as low quality will be excluded, and the remaining data will be re-pooled and meta-analysed to evaluate the consistency and stability of the main results.

## Missing data handling
For outcome-related data including total sample size, prevalence or effect estimates (CI) that will be missing from included studies, corresponding authors will be contacted via email. If no response is received, these studies will be excluded from meta-analysis but included in the narrative synthesis. For limited missing quantitative dispersion data (eg, SD, CI), established conversion formulas by Hozo et al. will be used to estimate the required statistics from available summary measures (eg, median, range or IQR). All decisions regarding missing data handling, exclusion or narrative inclusion, along with their potential impact, will be clearly reported in the discussion section of the final review.

## RoB assessment
The RoB and methodological quality will be independently assessed by two reviewers using the validated Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) tool for prevalence studies, with a third reviewer as adjudicator. Inter-rater reliability will be reported using both Cohen’s kappa and percentage agreement. The tool will evaluate (1) external validity, based on selection and non-response biases and (2) internal validity, based on measurement bias and bias related to the analysis. In addition, JBI critical appraisal checklist for analytical cross-sectional studies will be used to assess study quality. The JBI checklists consist of eight items, rated on a four-point Likert scale (ie, ‘Yes (1 point)’, ‘No (0 point)’, ‘Unclear’ and ‘Not applicable’). The items cover appropriateness of the sample frame; recruitment procedure; adequacy of the sample size; description of subjects and setting; description of the identified sample; validity of the methods used to screen for sleep problems; reliability of the screening methods; adequacy of statistical analyses; confounding factors and response rate. For scoring, 1 point will be assigned for each ‘yes’ response. Studies scoring (6–8) points will be considered as high-quality with low RoB, those scoring (4–5) points as moderate quality, and those scoring (≤3) points as low-quality with weak methodology and high RoB.

## Strategy for data synthesis
Based on the prior keywords, a search conducted in PubMed on 1 August 2025, yielded 497 relevant studies related to sleep problems and associated factors. Data synthesis will involve the extraction and pooling of key outcome variables from the included studies. Meta-analysis will be conducted using R studio V.4.3.2. Both the fixed-effect model (FEM) and random-effect model (REM) will be applied based on a consistent threshold or cut-off value, underlying assumption and study variance (τ²). To obtain the pooled estimates, the FEM will be applied when heterogeneity is negligible (I² <50%), assuming a common underlying effect. While an REM will be applied if heterogeneity is substantial (I² ≥50%), with the between-study variance component (τ²) estimated via restricted maximum likelihood (REML). If the studies are too heterogeneous to be pooled, a descriptive analysis will be performed instead. For the associated factors, ORs will be extracted and, when sufficient quantitative data are available, pooled using FEM or REM. If only a limited number of studies report comparable quantitative data, a narrative synthesis will be conducted instead of meta-regression.

## The status and timeline of the study
The systematic review is currently ongoing and in its preliminary stage (protocol development and literature searching). The entire review process commenced on 1 August 2025 and is expected to be completed by 30 January 2026. This timeline includes all stages of the review literature search, registration, screening, data extraction, quality assessment, data synthesis, meta-analysis and manuscript preparation. Each database will be searched up to 1 August 2025, while any amendments or deviations from this protocol will be fully documented, reported and justified in the final review manuscript.

## Discussion
This systematic review will provide an overview of evidence from observational prevalence studies regarding the overall pooled prevalence of sleep problems and qualitatively synthesise the associated factors triggering sleep quality among physicians of Bangladesh from all diversity. Beyond estimating the overall prevalence, this review may help to identify underlying factors contributing to poor sleep quality and address knowledge gaps to guide future interventions. Prior studies from Bangladesh reported higher prevalence of poor sleep quality among the healthcare physicians during the COVID-19 pandemic compared with the general population (45% vs 51%) assessed by PSQI and ISI-7 instrument, the burden was depicted in as illustrative background. Moreover, poor sleep quality was significantly associated with mental health outcomes, including suicidality, anxiety and depression. Studies revealed that sleep disturbances are higher in healthcare sectors associated with female physicians, nurses and shifting work. Moreover, among 50.41% healthcare physicians were found to have daytime sleepiness, daytime dysfunction 22.7% (1–2 times/week), with 73% having sleep duration of <7 hours . Poor sleep quality and short sleep duration (<7 hours) were strongly associated with professional burnout, with an estimated 12.46-fold higher risk among the vulnerable group. However, the attributable proportion of burnout linked to inadequate sleep was 25%–73% among physicians and 7%–29% among nurses, underscoring the substantial contribution of sleep deprivation to burnout in both groups. 
 However, where discrepancies exist among studies, potential sources of heterogeneity such as study design, methodology, profession and sample characteristics can be explored through subgroup or sensitivity analyses. Applying rigorous methods to extract and synthesise data from observational studies will provide a robust basis for drawing conclusions that cannot be derived from individual studies alone, ultimately contributing to a clearer understanding and comparison of sleep problem trajectories across this population.

## Strengths and limitations
To our knowledge, to date, this will be the first systematic review and meta-analysis aiming to synthesise and critically appraise evidence from cross-sectional observational studies reporting the prevalence, its trajectories and associated risk factors ( ). This study will strictly follow the Cochrane Handbook’s strict methodology, methodological guidelines and corresponding checklists in each phase of the study. Moreover, a key strength of the study will be the use of three independent reviewers, with substantial inter-rater agreement ensured (Cohen’s kappa ≥0.60; percentage agreement ≥80%). A potential limitation of this study would be the limited number of studies, different assessment tools and cut-off values used across the studies, which may limit the generalisability of the pooled estimates. In addition, missing quantitative dispersion data will be estimated using appropriate statistical methods, which may introduce some bias. The study will rely on meta-narrative synthesis of risk factors without conducting a meta-regression, which may limit the quantitative assessment of associations. Additionally, excluding qualitative studies from the synthesis may limit the comprehensiveness of the findings.
 

## Ethics and dissemination
The study will synthesise evidence gathered from published studies. As it will not involve primary data, ethical approval will not be required. Whether or not this review will ultimately get published, we intend to publish this review protocol separately to serve as a reference for future-related studies and to facilitate the dissemination and advancement of research methods in this healthcare field. After completion of the review, the findings will be shared through national and international peer-reviewed journals, conferences, webinars and another relevant platform for broader community engagement. Furthermore, concise summaries of the findings of the study will be shared with policy-makers, stakeholders, practitioners and institutions to guide practice, scale up future research and support intervention planning.
