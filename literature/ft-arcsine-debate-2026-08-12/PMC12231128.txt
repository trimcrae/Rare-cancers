## Abstract

## Aims
The role of sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) in patients with cardiac amyloidosis (CA) is controversial. However, they have shown encouraging results in several clinical settings, including heart failure, myocardial infarction, chronic kidney disease, and various forms of restrictive cardiomyopathy. The current study aims to evaluate the tolerability and efficacy of SGLT2i in patients with CA.

## Methods and results
PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Embase were scanned for eligible articles up to 28th of March 2025. Safety endpoints included the cumulative prevalence of adverse events (AEs) and drug discontinuation (DD) in the SGLT2i-group. Efficacy endpoints were the pooled risk ratio (RR) of all-cause death (ACD) and hospitalization due to worsening heart failure (WHF) between treatment- and control-groups, as well as the difference between mean change of N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels in both treatment- and control-groups. Thirteen observational studies, encompassing 19 227 patients, were included in the meta-analysis. Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors use in patients with CA resulted to be tolerable, as demonstrated by a low absolute cumulative prevalence of both AEs [8%; 95% confidence interval (CI) 2–17, nine studies, 603 patients] and DD (4%; 95% CI: 1–7, nine studies, 603 patients). Furthermore, its use was associated with a reduction in the risk of ACD (RR 0.59; 95% CI: 0.48–0.72) and NT-proBNP levels (median difference: −525.54; 95% CI: −718.09 to −332.98), despite no significant association with WHF was noted.

## Conclusion
The administration of SGLT2i proved to be well tolerated in patients with CA. Randomized controlled trials are urgently needed to confirm the prognostic improvement associated with their use in this clinical setting.

## Introduction
Cardiac amyloidosis (CA) is increasingly recognized as a significant cause of heart failure (HF) and is associated with poor clinical outcomes. Current guidelines highlight the growing role of targeted therapies based on histotype. Still, their widespread use is limited by high costs, the need for genetic testing, restricted applicability to patients with comorbidities or contraindications, as well as the non-negligible number of adverse events (AEs). , Notably, a recent analysis from the USA showed that systematic screening for transthyretin amyloid CA in elderly patients with HF and preserved ejection fraction is not cost-effective, primarily due to the substantial financial burden associated with tafamidis therapy. In contrast, sodium-glucose co-transporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) may represent a potentially more accessible treatment option, if proven effective in this subset of patients. , Specifically, in patients with HF with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction, SGLT2i demonstrated an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio approximately eight times lower than that observed for tafamidis in patients with transthyretin amyloid CA. , Recent evidence suggests that SGLT2i exerts favourable effects in acute myocardial infarction, acute HF, and other restrictive cardiomyopathies, which share pathophysiological similarities with CA, providing a rationale for exploring their use in CA patients. , Data on their safety and efficacy in CA remain inconsistent and limited to observational studies, with underpowered sample sizes, especially for rare events. , The current work aimed to perform a study-level meta-analysis of the tolerability and impact of SGLT2i use in CA.

## Methods

## Research strategy
This meta-analysis was performed according to the Meta-analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines and the current PRISMA guidelines for meta-analyses. , The study protocol was registered within the PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews with the following ID: CRD42025632733. We searched for relevant articles published in PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Embase up to 28th of March 2025, using the following keywords: ‘SGLT2i’, ‘Sodium-Glucose Co-transporter 2 Inhibitors’, ‘empagliflozin’, ‘dapagliflozin’, ‘canagliflozin’, ‘sotagliflozin’, and ‘amyloid*’ ( , ). The reference MOOSE flowchart is provided in the ( , ). Inclusion and exclusion criteria are fully reported in , . Data were extracted by two authors (R.L. and S.E.) using a standardized worksheet. A revised version of the MINORS scale was used to assess study quality (see , and ).

## Study endpoints
Safety endpoints included the cumulative prevalence of AEs and drug discontinuation (DD) in the SGLT2i-group. Primary efficacy endpoints were the pooled risk ratio (RR) of all-cause death (ACD) and hospitalization due to worsening heart failure (WHF) between treatment- and control-groups. The secondary efficacy endpoint was the difference between the mean change of N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels in treatment- and control-groups. If data were not directly available as mean values and standard deviations, conversion was performed, using the method described by Hozo et al., which provides distribution-free approximations suitable for skewed data. Furthermore, if data were reported only visually through graphs, they were extracted using the web tool WebPlotDigitizer. When data could not be numerically derived from individual events since they were already presented as measures of association [e.g. odds ratio (OR), hazard ratio (HR)], conversion formulas were applied. , Data at the longest available follow-up were extracted. For inferential purposes, a random-effects model was used (restricted maximum likelihood). Risk ratio with 95% confidence intervals (CI) was used for categorical variables and mean difference (MD) for continuous variables. P-values <0.05 (two-tailed) were considered statistically significant. The prevalence of AEs and DD was pooled with a random-effects model (restricted maximum likelihood) after the Freeman–Tukey Double Arcsine transformation to stabilize the variances. Leave-one-out analysis was performed to evaluate the influence of removing individual studies on the pooled effect size. Heterogeneity across studies was assessed with Cochrane's Q method and I2 testing. Publication bias was assessed using funnel plot visual examination and Egger’s test. The meta-analysis was performed using Stata 18 (64 bit; StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

## Results
Thirteen observational studies were included in the meta-analysis, with 19 227 patients involved. , , The mean age was 76.9 (±3.7) years, and the mean follow-up time was 27.5 (±20.3) months. More details about the characteristics of the included studies are provided in .
 

## Safety endpoints
Data on the safety profile of SGLT2i were provided across nine studies, encompassing a total of 603 patients. Since all studies reported AEs and DDs exclusively in SGLT2i-treated patients, these outcomes were analysed only as single-arm data, thus precluding the conduct of a formal comparative meta-analysis.

## Adverse events
Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors therapy proved to be well tolerated in CA, with only 48 of 603 patients experiencing AEs, yielding a negligible cumulative prevalence of 8% (95% CI 2–17%; I2 = 88.22%; nine studies, 603 patients) ( ). Additional pooled analyses were performed for three separate AEs, namely: urogenital symptoms (US), hypotension (HT), and acute kidney injury (AKI). Urogenital symptoms emerged as the most frequent, with a pooled prevalence of 2%, followed by HT (0.7%) and AKI (0.5%) (see , ). A further detailed description of each AE subgroup and heterogeneity assessment were provided in , 
 

## Drug discontinuation
Most patients enrolled remained adherent to SGLT2i-treatment, with DD reported in only 29 out of 603 cases, corresponding to a cumulative prevalence of 4% (95% CI: 1–7, I2 = 39%, nine studies, 603 patients) ( ).
The most commonly cited AEs leading to therapy interruption were: US (e.g. urinary tract infections, dysuria, or local irritation), 48%; constipation, 10.3%; reduction in glomerular filtration rate 10.3%; volume depletion-related symptoms (e.g. dizziness or orthostatic HT), 6.9%; skin reactions 3.4%; intolerance due to dyspnoea, 3.4%. When reported, these events resolved following either discontinuation or temporary suspension of the therapy.

## Efficacy endpoints
Data on the prognostic impact of SGLT2i in patients with CA, in terms of ACD and WHF, were reported by 10 and 9 studies, respectively. Of these, two studies (i.e. Sivamurugan et al. and Byer et al.) did not provide event numbers for each treatment group, but reported instead data as OR and HR, respectively. , The transformation formulas were used to calculate RR, relying on the incidence of the events in the untreated group, as extrapolated from the works of Porcari et al. and Schwegel et al. , From the calculated RRs, the number of events per group were estimated, knowing the total number of treated and untreated patients. Therefore, these two studies were formally incorporated into the meta-analysis. However, to mitigate the risk of bias due to indirect estimation of events, they were excluded from the primary efficacy pooled analyses and included instead in supplementary efficacy analyses.

## All-cause death
The risk of ACD was significantly reduced in the SGLT2i-treated group (RR 0.59; 95% CI 0.48–0.72, P < 0.001; I2 = 64.88%, eight studies, 16 549 patients) ( ). This result was also confirmed in the comprehensive supplementary analysis, including the two studies in which the RR was derived indirectly, as described above (RR 0.57, 95% CI 0.50–0.66; I² = 64.04%, 10 studies, 19.061) (see , ).
 

## Worsening heart failure
No significant difference was found between the two groups in terms of WHF (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.42–1.78, P = 0.68; I2 = 78.14%, six studies, 5149 patients), ( ). This result was also confirmed in the comprehensive supplementary analysis, including the two studies in which the RR was derived indirectly, as described above (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.64–1.17, P = 0.34; I2 = 90.68%, nine studies, 18 885 patients) (see , ).

## N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide
A significant reduction in NT-proBNP levels was found in the treatment group compared to the control group (MD: −525.54; 95% CI: −718.09 to −332.98, I2 = 35.56%, four studies, 463 patients) ( ). When the pre-post correlation coefficient for NT-proBNP was not available, it was estimated using the within-subject correlation formula, based on the pre-, post-, and follow-up change variances reported by Dobner et al. Notably, NT-proBNP data , , were not directly provided in three studies and were extracted from graphs using the web tool WebPlotDigitizer. , , , 

## Publication bias and leave-one-out sensitivity analyses
The visual assessment of the funnel plot was not reliable due to the number of included studies less than 10 for each endpoint, although Egger's test showed no publication bias for any of the endpoints (see , , , , , , and ). In leave-one-out sensitivity analyses, the exclusion of Schwegel et al. revealed a significant association between SGLT2i-treatment and reduced WHF events (see , ). Conversely, the removal of any of the studies did not affect the significance of overall results for the other outcomes (see , , , , , and ).

## Discussion
The key findings of the current meta-analysis, which encompasses 19 227 patients from 13 observational studies, can be summarized as follows:
The use of SGLT2i in patients with CA was well tolerated, as evidenced by the low occurrence of AEs (i.e. 8%) and DD (i.e. 4%).
The use of SGLT2i in patients with CA was associated with a greater reduction in NT-proBNP levels from baseline, compared to the control group.
In patients with CA, SGLT2i use was associated with a reduction in ACD, without a corresponding benefit on WHF events, as compared with control.
To the best of our knowledge, the present study represents the first meta-analysis specifically assessing the tolerability profile and the most comprehensive in evaluating the prognostic impact of SGLT2i therapy in patients with CA. A recent meta-analysis addressing the same topic, albeit based on a smaller number of studies and focused exclusively on efficacy outcomes, also reported a protective effect of SGLT2i against ventricular tachycardia and atrial fibrillation in this clinical context, thereby providing additional support to our findings. However, owing to the observational design of the included studies, our results should be interpreted as associations rather than causal effects.

## Safety and tolerability
The safety and tolerability of SGLT2i in the context of CA represents a critical aspect of their therapeutic potential. In our study, the low occurrence of side effects of this drug class is consistent with their well-characterized safety profile in other cardiac and renal diseases. , , Notably, the cumulative prevalence of USS was numerically comparable to that observed in randomized clinical trials evaluating SGLT2i in patients without CA but with HF and preserved ejection fraction (i.e. 2% vs. 1.4% in EMPEROR-Preserved and 1% in DELIVER). Additionally, concerning the prevalence of HT and AKI, our findings indicate that these AE were less frequently reported than in the aforementioned trials (e.g. HT: 0.7% vs. 9.5% in EMPEROR-Preserved and 7.8% in DELIVER; AKI: 0.5% vs. 3.3% in EMPEROR-Preserved and 3.4% in DELIVER). , 
The evaluation of SGLT2i safety and tolerability in this specific clinical context was strongly needed, as the multi-organ involvement of the disease and the advanced age of patients involved would theoretically place them at significant risk of AEs, to the extent that the current expert consensus recommends avoiding beta-blockers and renin–angiotensin system inhibitors. , Indeed, it is generally assumed that the autonomic dysfunction associated with amyloidosis could exacerbate HT and rhythm disturbances associated with such therapies. Conversely, our work does not raise any concerns about the safety and tolerability of this class of drugs. Nevertheless, the observational design of the included studies renders our results hypothesis-generating rather than hypothesis-testing. A direct comparison between SGLT2i and placebo within a randomized controlled trial, adequately powered to assess even rare AEs, is urgently needed to confirm these findings.

## All-cause death and worsening heart failure events
A notable finding from this analysis is the reduction in ACD observed in patients treated with SGLT2i as compared with control, despite no significant association with a reduction in WHF events. Cardiovascular mortality accounts for approximately two-thirds of all deaths among patients with CA, predominantly due to WHF and sudden cardiac death. , Cardiac injury in patients with CA is attributable not only to fibrillar deposition, resulting in mechanical compression, impaired diastolic dysfunction, and elevated intra-cardiac filling pressures but also to the direct cytotoxic effects exerted by soluble monomers and oligomers of amyloid. Therefore, the mechanism underlying the potential mortality benefit of SGLT2i may be multifactorial and wide-ranging, involving both the direct cardioprotective and anti-arrhythmic effects of SGLT2i and their ability to modulate systemic factors such as glucose metabolism, fluid balance, nephroprotection, and effective decongestion. Furthermore, SGLT2 inhibition exerts beneficial effects on myocardial metabolism by promoting a shift towards energy-saving substrates (i.e. ketones and autophagy), which may enhance cellular repair processes and mitigate the cytotoxic effects of amyloid-induced damage to myocardial cells. This is further supported by the reduction in NT-proBNP levels, a well-known marker of mortality in this clinical setting. , , A recent analysis, consistent with our findings, showed that SGLT2i were safe and associated with reduced mortality, without significantly affecting decompensation events, also in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, another form of restrictive cardiomyopathy. From a pathophysiological perspective, SGLT2i have been shown to enhance diastolic relaxation in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy by reducing cytoplasmic calcium overload. , To date, no studies have specifically investigated the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of SGLT2i in individuals with CA. Collectively, the emerging evidence supporting the safety and prognostic benefits of SGLT2i should also encourage further research into their specific mechanisms of action in this clinical context, to enable more precise patient selection for therapy. The absence of a detectable effect of SGLT2i on the incidence of WHF events may be attributable, instead, to several factors.
First, unlike mortality, hospitalization for HF represents a more heterogeneous and potentially observer-dependent endpoint, susceptible to reporting bias. Patients treated with SGLT2i may have undergone a closer follow-up or more meticulous data collection, potentially resulting in a higher event capture compared to control subjects, thereby underestimating the therapeutic benefit of SGLT2i on this outcome.
Secondly, mortality may act as a confounding factor in the occurrence of WHF events. Consequently, the higher incidence of mortality observed in the control group may represent a competing risk, potentially masking the true incidence of WHF events and thus rendering it comparable to that observed in patients treated with SGLT2i. The exclusion of the study by Schwegel et al. in the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis yielded a statistically significant association in favour of SGLT2i concerning WHF events. Notably, this study included the oldest patient population, in whom the competing risk of death may have been particularly prominent, potentially attenuating the treatment effect on WHF. 
Thirdly, it cannot be excluded that SGLT2i therapy in patients with CA may confer a selective benefit on mortality while failing to significantly reduce episodes of decompensation. A recent meta-analysis assessing the efficacy of SGLT2i specifically in patients with transthyretin CA reported an association between SGLT2i use and a reduction in WHF events. However, this observed effect appeared to be primarily driven by a study available only in abstract form and, therefore, characterized by a high risk of bias. To date, longitudinal data on the disease progression of CA remain limited. Future prospective studies, incorporating robust methodological approaches to minimize potential biases, are urgently warranted to elucidate the impact of SGLT2i on the natural history of the disease.

## Strength and limitations
The main strength of the current study lies in its comprehensive synthesis of the current evidence regarding the tolerability and efficacy of SGLT2i in patients with CA. Although hypothesis-generating in nature, these findings provide an essential foundation for the design of adequately powered trials aimed at evaluating clinically relevant outcomes. However, our meta-analysis has certain limitations. Firstly, the inclusion of observational studies allows for the identification of associative relationships, precluding definitive conclusions regarding causality. Secondly, the safety analysis is descriptive rather than comparative, as it is limited to prevalence estimates among patients treated with SGLT2i, owing to the unavailability of corresponding data in the control group. Therefore, although our findings appear promising, a comprehensive safety assessment through comparative analysis remains warranted. Thirdly, in only six of the included studies, SGLT2i were administered as an adjunct to disease-modifying targeted therapies, whereas for the remaining studies such information was not reported. Further evidence, preferably deriving from adequately powered randomized controlled trials, is needed to evaluate the incremental prognostic benefit of SGLT2i in this clinical context. Fourthly, for six of the included studies, the duration of clinical follow-up was <2 years. To assess the long-term effects, additional data from studies with extended follow-up periods are strongly required.

## Conclusion
In patients with CA, the administration of SGLT2i proved to be well-tolerated and associated with a significant reduction in the risk of ACD. However, future randomized controlled trials are urgently required to validate the prognostic benefits associated with their use in this clinical setting.
