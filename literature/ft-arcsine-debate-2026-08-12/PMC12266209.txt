## Abstract

## Introduction
Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) is a rare but severe complication of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE); however, the overall patient and renal survival rates of patients with SLE-associated TMA (SLE-TMA) remain controversial. This study aimed to evaluate the prognosis of and identify potential prognostic factors in patients with SLE-TMA.

## Methods
Literature search was performed in MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trial, CINAHL, PsycINFO, Scopus, CNKI, and Sinomed for studies published before December 2023. Studies that encompassed no less than 5 patients with SLE-TMA and followed well-recognized diagnostic criteria were included, whereas studies with a median follow-up < 6 months or that provided incomplete outcomes were excluded.

## Results
Fourteen eligible studies encompassing a total of 446 patients with SLE-TMA were included in the meta-analysis. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient survival rates were 83% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.65–0.96), 81% (95% CI: 0.59–0.96), and 77% (95% CI: 0.52–0.95), respectively. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year renal survival rates were 85% (95% CI: 0.81–0.89), 75% (95% CI: 0.63–0.85), and 56% (95% CI: 0.31–0.79). The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year renal relative risks in patients with SLE-TMA compared with patients with SLE without TMA were 4.01 (95% CI: 2.10–7.65), 2.53 (95% CI: 1.83–3.51), and 2.58 (95% CI: 2.01–3.29). Univariate Cox regression indicated that high baseline serum creatinine (SCr) levels, high renal chronicity index and acute hemodialysis were independent prognostic factors of renal survival.

## Conclusion
Patients with SLE-TMA exhibit lower long-term patient and renal survival rates. High baseline SCr levels, high renal chronicity index or a need for acute hemodialysis at baseline are associated with poorer renal outcomes.

Keywords: pooled survival rate, prognostic factor, systemic lupus erythematosus, thrombotic microangiopathy

TMA is an independent prognostic factor of SLE and results in higher mortality and higher risk of developing end-stage renal disease. Among patients with SLE, the prevalence of TMA is approximately 1% ; however, a previous study based on lupus nephritis (LN) kidney biopsy suggested that TMA manifested in 24.3% of patients with LN. TMA can result from various etiologies, including thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS), drug-induced, infections, antiphospholipid syndrome, systemic sclerosis, and malignant hypertension. Specifically, TTP is characterized by ADAMTS13 enzyme deficiency, often accompanied by more pronounced thrombocytopenia and milder renal abnormalities ; whereas aHUS, characterized by abnormalities in the complement alternative pathway, is frequently associated with severe renal impairment. Nevertheless, the etiology remains elusive in a substantial proportion of patients. 
However, the outcome of patients with SLE-TMA remains unclear. Previous cohort studies have yielded varying survival rates for patients with SLE-TMA, with the 5-year patient survival rate ranging from 33.9% to 100%, , and 5-year renal survival ranging from 13.0% to 77.3%. , The pronounced disparities could be because of the differing diagnostic criteria either according to renal biopsy results or combined clinical symptoms of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and organ damage. Limited sample size, different demographic characteristics, different treatment strategies, as well as the various and possibly unclear pathologies further augmented the heterogeneity. To enhance the monitoring and management of SLE-TMA, it is crucial to identify potential prognostic indicators. This study is a systematic review and meta-analysis that aimed to integrate patient and renal survival in observational studies and review the potential prognostic factors of SLE-TMA.

## Methods
The meta-analysis and systematic review were conducted based on recommendations of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta Analyses (PRISMA) Statement protocol and was registered on PROSPERO (CRD42024461175).

## Literature Search
Literature search was performed in MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trial (CENTRAL), CINAHL, PsycINFO, Scopus, CNKI, and Sinomed for studies published before December 6, 2023, using the following search terms in the title or abstract, or as MESH terms: (systemic lupus erythematosus OR lupus) AND (thrombotic microangiopathy OR thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura OR haemolytic uraemic syndrome OR haemolytic uremic syndrome OR hemolytic uraemic syndrome OR hemolytic uremic syndrome).

## Study Selection
Studies were eligible if the satisfied the following conditions: (i) Patients with SLE were diagnosed according to the 1982 American Rheumatism Association revised criteria, the 1997 American College of Rheumatology revised criteria, the 2012 Systemic International Collaborating Clinics criteria, or the 2019 European League Against Rheumatism/American College of Rheumatology classification criteria ; (ii) TMA was diagnosed according to renal biopsy results or combined clinical symptoms of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, with or without presentation of organ damage of kidney or nervous system, without other apparent causes or precipitants ; (iii) they were original articles including case series, case-control or cohort studies; (iv) they comprised no less than 5 patients with SLE-TMA diagnosed by well-recognized criteria; and (v) they provided either 1-year patient or 1-year renal survival, or sufficient data to calculate the survival rates. Studies were excluded if they satisfied had the following conditions: (i) they were correspondence, conference abstracts, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, expert opinion, or consensus statement; (ii) the median or mean follow-up durations were < 6 months. There were no restrictions on language and region. For studies from the same center with overlapping time span, the study with the longest observational time span or the largest sample size was included. Two reviewers (GYX and ZZY) independently assessed the studies for eligibility. Discrepancies were discussed or resolved by consulting a third reviewer (LMT).

## Data Extraction and Quality Assessment
Study characteristics that were extracted included first author, year of publication, region of study conduct, data source, time span, number of patients, study design, and median or mean follow-up time. Baseline data of patients included biological sex, race, age at SLE-TMA diagnosis, duration of SLE, disease subgroup and diagnostic criteria for SLE (LN) and TMA (renal TMA, TTP, aHUS), presence of thrombocytopenia, presence of neurological symptoms, with or without control, and treatment regime. Prognostic data included 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year patient survival and renal survival rates (or data in the form of Kaplan-Meier curves, tables, or narrative descriptions), relative risk, and prognostic factors. The quality of the studies was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (http://www.ohri.ca/programs/clinical_epidemiology/oxford.asp).

## Statistical Analysis
One-, 3-, and 5-year pooled patient and renal survival rates were assessed. Before conducting the meta-analysis, we tested the normality of the raw rates and those transformed using the following 4 different methods: “PLN” (log transformation), “PLOGIT” (logit transformation), “PAS” (inverse sine transformation), and “PFT” (Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation). Based on the normality test results, the method most closely approximating a normal distribution, namely PFT, was selected to improve comparability and stabilize variance across studies. Heterogeneity among studies was assessed using the inconsistency index (I2) statistic, which quantifies the degree of variation across studies. An I2 value > 50% indicated substantial heterogeneity, and a random-effects model was employed in these cases. If I2 < 50%, a fixed-effects model was applied. Subgroups were categorized based on the following factors: publication time (before or after 2016), language of publication (English or Chinese), study design (retrospective cohort or case series), sample size (< or > 28), race (Asian, others), disease subtype (LN, renal TMA, TTP, or aHUS), diagnostic criteria (clinical syndrome or renal biopsy), treatment strategy (with or without plasma exchange), and study quality (Newcastle-Ottawa Scale score > or < 6). Publication bias was assessed through visual inspection of funnel plot symmetry. When the number of included studies exceeded 5, Egger's regression test and Begg's rank correlation test were applied to statistically evaluate asymmetry. Egger's test assesses the presence of small-study effects by examining the relationship between study size and effect estimates, whereas Begg's test uses rank correlation to detect potential bias. , A significant result (P < 0.05) in either test suggests the possible presence of publication bias. If either test indicated significant asymmetry, the trim-and-fill analysis was considered to adjust for potential publication bias and reestimate the pooled effect size. All analyses were performed using R statistical software (v4.3.2) meta or meta for package. A 2-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Literature Search
The literature search yielded a total of 11,438 articles (4299 from Embase, 2845 from Web of Science, 2152 from Scopus, 1458 from Pubmed, 136 from CINAHL, 18 from CENTRAL, 4 from PsycINFO, 230 from CNKI, and 296 from Sinomed), of which 6438 remained after removal of duplicate records and records other than journal article. After title and abstract screening, 6366 records unrelated to SLE-TMA or included < 5 patients with SLE-TMA were excluded. Correspondence, conference abstracts, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, expert opinion or consensus statement were also excluded at this step. Seventy-two records entered full text screening and 58 articles were removed because of incomplete data, unspecified diagnosis, or repeated population. Finally, 14 records were included in the final meta-analysis. , , , , , , , , , , , , , A flowchart of record screening is illustrated in .
 

## Characteristics of Included Studies
In , we summarize the essential characteristics of studies included in the study. Four hundred forty-six patients with SLE-TMA from China, Mexico, Brazil, France, and the USA were involved. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale quality score ranged from 5 to 9 ( ).
 

## Pooled Patient and Renal Survival
A total of 8, 6, and 5 studies reported 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient survival rates, respectively. One-, 3-, and 5-year renal survival rates were reported in 10, 9, and 5 studies, respectively. One-, 3-, and 5-year renal relative risks were reported in 6, 5, and 3 studies, respectively. Analysis of patient survival relative risk was not conducted because of the limited number of eligible studies. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient survival rates were 83% (95% CI: 0.65–0.96), 81% (95% CI, 0.59–0.96) and 77% (95% CI, 0.52–0.95), respectively. The pooled 1-, 3- and 5-year renal survival rates were 85% (95% CI: 0.81–0.89), 75% (95% CI: 0.63–0.85), and 56% (95% CI: 0.31–0.79), respectively. The pooled 1-, 3,- and 5-year renal relative risks were 4.01 (95% CI: 2.10–7.65), 2.53 (95% CI: 1.83–3.51), and 2.58 (95% CI: 2.01–3.29), respectively ( ).
 

## Sensitivity Analysis and Publication Bias
Heterogeneity was evident in 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient survival with I2 of 84%, 81%, and 79%, respectively. Leave-one-out analysis demonstrated that W. C. Chen's study accounted for the heterogeneity in 3-year (I2 = 34% vs. I2 = 81%) and 5-year (I2 = 38% vs. I2 = 79%) patient survival ( b and c). Subsequent subgroup analysis was conducted to explain the source of heterogeneity of 1-year patient survival, which revealed that whether TMA was diagnosed according to clinical syndrome or renal biopsy explains the heterogeneity ( , ). I2 in 3-, and 5-year renal survival were 72% and 89%, respectively. Leave-one-out analysis showed that the heterogeneity in 5-year renal survival mainly resulted from F. L. Strufaldi's study ( f), whereas subgroup analysis indicated that race explained the 3-year renal survival ( ). Sensitivity analysis indicated that the 1-year pooled renal survival rate and all renal relative risks remained stable, with no single study exclusion altering the significance of the pooled survival rates ( d, g, h, and i). Funnel plots were employed to assess publication bias in the meta-analysis. As shown in , the funnel plot shapes exhibited no evident asymmetry. In addition, both Begg’s and Egger’s tests verified the absence of publication bias (P > 0.05).
 

## Prognostic Factors of Patients With SLE-TMA
The prognostic factors identified in various studies exhibited considerable variation. As for patient survival, a concurrent diagnosis of TMA with SLE or the presence of positive anti–ribonucleic protein antibodies were significant predictors of mortality. In terms of renal survival, clinical manifestations correlated with poorer renal outcomes included a need for acute hemodialysis at admission, , acute heart failure, glomerulosclerosis, thrombocytopenia, and elevated SLE disease activity index scores. Laboratory indicators included high baseline SCr levels, elevated renal chronicity index, , low platelets, low hemoglobin levels, the presence of both C4d deposition and decreased serum factor H, and deposition of pauci-immune complex among patients treated with steroids and immunosuppressants. With respect to treatment, the use of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors served as a protective factor, whereas double filtration plasma plasmapheresis significantly improved 2-year renal survival rate in patients requiring initial renal replacement treatment ( ).
 

## Discussion
TMA is a rare and severe complication of SLE. This meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the pooled survival and renal survival rates of patients with SLE-TMA and to assess the prognostic factors reported across individual studies. Despite the scarcity of studies meeting rigorous eligibility criteria and the presence of heterogeneity, this investigation represents the first comprehensive assessment of the prognosis of patients with SLE-TMA. The meta-analysis showed that the pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year patient survival rates were 83%, 81%, and 77%, respectively. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year renal survival rates were 85%, 75%, and 56%, respectively. The pooled 1-, 3-, and 5-year renal relative risks were 4.01, 2.53, and 2.58, respectively. Heterogeneity was explained by different diagnostic criteria of TMA in patient survival, and patient race in renal survival. In 1-year patient survival, patients diagnosed according to clinical symptoms exhibited worse outcomes than patients diagnosed solely based on renal biopsy, likely because systemic symptoms indicate a more severe condition than isolated renal impairment. In 3-year and 5-year patient survival, heterogeneity was explained by the only study that made the diagnosis based on symptoms rather than renal biopsy. As for renal survival, 3-year renal survival rate of Han Chinese patients exceeds patients of other races, whereas heterogeneity in the 5-year renal survival rate was mainly driven by the only study where patients' race differed. The pooled renal survival rate in Han Chinese patients with SLE-TMA correlated with another study not included in the analysis, and might be attributed to differences in genetic predisposition.
Although the prevalence of TMA among patients with SLE is relatively lower than other complications, , , it is recognized as one of the most frequent causes of mortality. One-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of patients with SLE were 98.3%, 96.9%, and 95.7%, respectively, far exceeding the survival rate of patients with SLE-TMA. Ten-year survival of patients with LN was 95.6%, whereas 10-year renal survival rate was 89.9%. Compared with patients with non-TMA LN, patients with LN-TMA have poorer response to traditional therapies and worse prognosis. Notably, previous studies have shown that treatment responses and outcomes of patients with SLE-TTP were comparable or even better than those with primary immune-mediated TTP, suggesting that the prognosis of patients with SLE-TMA does not represent a simple composite of these disease entities. In contrast, TMA has traditionally been regarded as an acute and transient condition. However, this meta-analysis uncovered a persistent decline in the 3- and 5-year survival and renal survival rates among patients with TMA, despite a reduced relative risk compared with patients with non-TMA SLE. These findings highlight the necessity for extended surveillance of patients with TMA to improve long-term prognosis.
To elucidate the elevation and heterogeneity of mortality rate in SLE-TMA, we intended to further analyze the prognostic factors of survival and renal survival. However, only 6 of the studies identified prognostic factors for patients with SLE-TMA, and few of the reported prognostic factors were overlapping. The scarcity of studies precluded a meta-analysis of pooled hazard ratios for these factors, and a systematic review was performed instead.
Prognostic factors for renal survival, including high baseline SCr levels, elevated renal acute index or chronicity index, glomerulosclerosis, and the need for acute hemodialysis at baseline reflect severe renal impairment. Acute heart failure could result in renal hypoperfusion, whereas C4d deposition coupled with decreased serum complement factor H could lead to inflammation and injury of the endothelium, directly causing renal injury. Elevated SLE disease activity index indicates more active SLE, whereas thrombocytopenia and anemia denote severeness of TMA. Prognostic factors for patient survival include anti–ribonucleic protein antibodies, which also indicate higher overall mortality in patients with SLE, and a concurrent diagnosis of TMA with SLE, which implies that SLE has progressed to a more advanced stage before appropriate treatment is initiated. The majority of the aforementioned prognostic factors result from delayed diagnosis and treatment. Another large multicenter study revealed that baseline organ damage and renal involvement were associated with increased mortality, which supported our conclusion that severe renal impairment and delayed treatment are critical prognostic factors in SLE-TMA. Therefore, it is suggested that laboratory evidence should be promptly sought, including the presence of schistocytes on peripheral blood smear examination, anemia, reduced plasma haptoglobin levels, elevated lactate dehydrogenase, and a negative result of Coombs test. In addition, it is recommended to test for ADAMTS13 activity, anti-ADAMTS13 antibodies, and antiphospholipid antibodies promptly. Exclusion of typical hemolytic uremic syndrome via fecal culture and serum Shiga toxin assays, alongside detection of mutations in complement regulatory genes and anti–complement factor H antibodies are also crucial for the diagnosis of aHUS. While awaiting test results, risk stratification of TTP with PLASMIC score should be conducted, and plasma exchange and glucocorticoid treatment should be initiated for patients with moderate to high risk. 
The recommended management of SLE-TMA varies in patients with different etiologies. For patients with ADAMTS13 activity < 10%, that is, patients with SLE-TTP, the treatment regimen includes glucocorticoids and rituximab, which improves patient survival but not renal survival. For patients with normal ADAMTS13 activity and positive aPLs, that is, patients with antiphospholipid syndrome, anticoagulation should be included in therapy. For patients who do not fit into the above 2 categories and exhibit complement-mediated etiologies, such as mutations in complement regulatory genes or existence of complement factor H antibodies, that is, patients with aHUS, eculizumab is recommended. Eculizumab has been proven to improve renal survival and overall outcome of patients with SLE-TMA. , In this meta-analysis, the use of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors also improves renal survival, possibly via its protective effect against cardiovascular complications and proteinuria, and could be optimal for patients with systemic sclerosis or malignant hypertension-related SLE-TMA. As a therapeutic regimen recommended across a spectrum of etiologies including TTP, aHUS, and antiphospholipid syndrome, plasmapheresis exhibits controversial effect in previous studies, , , , possibly because of the single-center experience and small sample size. Likewise, double filtration plasma plasmapheresis turns out to be effective in improving renal outcomes of patients with SLE-TMA requiring initial renal replacement treatment; however, significance is not achieved across the entire cohort, indicating its greater efficacy for those with severe renal impairment. A large-scale multicenter randomized clinical trial is warranted to test the utility of plasmapheresis or double filtration plasma plasmapheresis in SLE-TMA.
The study has several limitations. First, despite 14 eligible studies included in the study, not all studies provide data for all 3 fields including patient survival, renal survival, and relative risk. Because of the low prevalence of SLE-TMA, some subgroups included a limited number of studies or patients, which could introduce potential bias and limit the generalizability of our findings. However, because of the rarity of SLE-TMA, our study includes as many patients with SLE-TMA as possible. Because of the varying definitions of renal survival among the included studies, we defined renal survival based on the presence of study-reported renal survival, including increase in SCr, need for dialysis, and end-stage renal disease. These variations may introduce heterogeneity in the pooled analysis. Second, though we have provided a unified explanation for the heterogeneity observed in our analyses, with the TMA diagnostic criteria explaining heterogeneity in patient survival rates and race accounting for heterogeneity in renal survival rates, we acknowledge that the number of studies and sample size included in each subgroup analysis was small. This limitation raised the possibility that the observed subgroup effects might be due to chance and warrants further validation using larger cohorts. Third, although 6 studies reported prognostic factors specifically for SLE-TMA, there was minimal overlap among the reported factors, precluding a meta-analysis of hazard ratios. Subsequent investigations are necessary to validate the prognostic factors reported in the extant studies.
In conclusion, patients with SLE-TMA exhibit a progressive decline of patient and renal survival in the long-term. High renal chronicity index, high baseline SCr levels, and a need for acute hemodialysis were identified as independent prognostic factors for SLE-TMA. Prompt diagnosis and risk stratification based on identified risk factors are crucial to improve the survival and renal outcome of patients with SLE-TMA. It is essential for future studies to further discuss the effectiveness of present therapeutic strategies.
