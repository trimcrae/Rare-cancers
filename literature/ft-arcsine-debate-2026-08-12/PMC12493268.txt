## Abstract

## Purpose
To synthesize and critically appraise the literature on long-term outcomes of proximal osteotomies of the first metatarsal (M1) to treat HV without inflammatory disease or degenerative arthritis and to assess the long-term HV recurrence rates of studies with a minimum follow-up of 5 years.

## Methods
This systematic review conforms to the PRISMA guidelines. The authors conducted a search using PubMed, Embase®, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials databases. Studies that report outcomes of proximal osteotomies of the M1 for non-inflammatory and non-degenerative HV at a minimum follow-up of 5 years were included. We found four eligible studies comprising four datasets, and all assessed proximal osteotomies with a mean follow-up that ranged from 8 to 18 years.

## Results
The systematic search returned 7,918 records, of which 2,693 were duplicates, leaving 5,225 for screening. Of these, four studies were included, covering a total of 158 feet with a mean follow-up that ranged from 8 to 18 years. The pooled HVA following proximal osteotomies was 20.4° (CI: 11.3°–29.4°; I2 = 95%), pooled IMA was 8.8° (CI: 3.3°–14.2°; I2 = 98%), and pooled AOFAS was 82.1 (CI: 62.4–101.8; I2 = 97%).

## Conclusions
At a minimum follow-up of 8 years following proximal osteotomies of M1, the HVA was 20.4° and the IMA was 8.8°. Furthermore, the recurrence rates considering the various thresholds of HVA were 12% having >30°, 73% having >20°, and 13% if recurrence is a >10° increase in HVA.

## Level of evidence
Meta-analysis, level IV.

## Introduction
Hallux valgus (HV) is one of the most common forefoot pathologies and mostly causes pain, but less often could lead to impaired gait, poor balance, and an increased fall risk in older adults ( , ). For symptomatic HV, operative correction is the standard, as it is more effective compared to conservative measures ( ).
Over the years, a multitude of surgical techniques have been described, including distal, shaft, and proximal osteotomies of the first metatarsal (M1), first metatarsophalangeal joint resections, soft tissue procedures, as well as first tarsometatarsal (TMT1) and metatarsophalangeal (MTP1) fusion ( ). Recurrence is a frequent concern expressed by patients before surgery, which may occur in the long term from poor results of former surgical techniques ( ). There are numerous studies published on the outcomes of distal and shaft osteotomies, and a recent systematic review found a recurrence rate of 64% using a >15° threshold for the former ( ). To the authors’ knowledge, no systematic review has evaluated the outcomes of proximal osteotomies, which are less often used but could be an appropriate alternative.
The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to synthesize and critically appraise the literature on long-term outcomes of proximal osteotomies of the M1 to treat HV without inflammatory disease or degenerative arthritis, and to assess the long-term HV recurrence rates of studies with a minimum follow-up of 5 years.

## Methods
The protocol for this systematic review was submitted to PROSPERO before commencement (registration number: CRD42022298199) and conforms to the principles outlined in the handbook of the Cochrane Collaboration ( ), along with the guidelines established by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) ( ).

## Search strategy
The authors conducted a structured electronic literature search on 23 January 2024 using the PubMed, Embase®, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials databases, applying the keywords ‘HV’ and ‘surgery' OR 'surgical' OR 'surgeries’ and ‘long-term or long term’ (Appendix I (see section on given at the end of the article)). The search was limited to articles published between 2000 and 2024. After removal of duplicate records, each of two researchers (ML, LN) screened the titles and abstracts to determine suitability for the review using the following predefined eligibility criteria:

## Inclusion criteria

-Studies that report outcomes of proximal osteotomies of the first metatarsal (M1) for non-inflammatory and non-degenerative HV at a minimum follow-up of 5 years.

## Exclusion criteria

-Studies with a minimum follow-up <5 years.
-Studies on HV with inflammatory disease or degenerative arthritis (hallux rigidus).
-Narrative or systematic reviews, case reports, expert opinions, editorials, or letters to editors.
-Articles published in languages other than English, French, German, Italian, Spanish, or Dutch.
-Articles published before the year 2000.

## Study selection
Studies that met the eligibility criteria during title and abstract screening underwent full-text screening by two researchers (ML, LN). Any disagreement was first discussed between the researchers, and if required, a third researcher (FVR) resolved it. The reference lists of studies for full-text review were searched, and a fellowship-trained foot and ankle surgeon (PB) was consulted to identify further relevant studies that may not have been captured by the database searches.

## Data extraction and quality assessment
Data extraction was performed by two researchers (ML, LN) independently, and their results were compared to ensure accuracy. Where there was disagreement in the documented value, the true value was ascertained by simultaneous review of the data in question by both researchers. The following data were extracted from the included studies: author(s), journal, year of publication, level of evidence, country where the study was performed, conflicts of interest, and funding declaration. Patient characteristics were retrieved, including number of patients in each group, sex, and age. Indications for surgery and techniques, as well as pre- and post-operative angles and clinical scores were extracted including hallux valgus angle (HVA), intermetatarsal angle (IMA), and American Orthopaedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) score. Finally, all recurrences and complications were documented. Methodological quality of the eligible studies was assessed by two researchers (ML, LN) according to the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) checklist to appraise the reporting quality (ten items). Where there was disagreement between the researchers, consensus was achieved by discussion and review.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was performed by one of the researchers (LN). When available in the original articles, outcomes were tabulated: continuous outcomes were reported as means, standard deviations, and ranges, while categorical outcomes were reported as proportions. Heterogeneity was evaluated by visual inspection of forest plots and using the I2 statistic and its connected χ2 test to provide a measure of the degree of inconsistency across studies. Pooled estimates of raw means and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using a random-effects model framework. Pooled estimates of proportions and their 95% CIs were calculated via Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation using inverse-variance weighting within a random-effects model framework. P-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.1.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Austria) with the meta package.

## Results
The systematic search returned 7,918 records, of which 2,693 were duplicates, leaving 5,225 for screening. A total of 5,052 studies were excluded by examining their titles and/or abstracts, and a further 169 studies were excluded after full-text review: 48 had a follow-up <5 years, 104 were on treatments other than proximal osteotomy, and 17 were not relevant. This left four eligible studies ( , , , ), with a total of 158 feet ( , ). The four studies comprised four datasets, and all assessed proximal osteotomies with a mean follow-up that ranged from 8 to 18 years.
 

## Quality assessment
All four eligible studies were case series. Clear inclusion criteria were insufficient in one of the four studies ( ), and statistical analyses were insufficient in another one of the four studies ( ) ( ). Of the four studies, one case series scored high for risk of bias ( ).
 

## Recurrence rate
The four datasets reported recurrence of HV considering three different thresholds ( ). Due to heterogeneity in reporting of recurrence, pooling was not possible. One study ( ) reported that four patients (11%) developed a recurrence (>10° increase in HVA), though all were asymptomatic. One study ( ) reported 37 cases (73%) of recurrence (HVA ≥20°), none of which required revision. Finally, two articles ( , ) considered the highest thresholds for recurrence (HVA >30°) and found two cases of recurrence each (4 and 8%), all requiring revision surgery.
 

## Hallux valgus angle
Of the four included datasets, all reported pre- and post-operative HVA ( ). The pooled HVA following proximal osteotomies was 20.4° (CI: 11.3°–29.4°; I2 = 95%) ( ).
 

## Intermetatarsal angle
Of the four included datasets, all reported pre- and post-operative IMA ( ). The pooled IMA following proximal osteotomies was 8.8° (CI: 3.3°–14.2°; I2 = 98%) ( ).
 

## AOFAS
Of the four included datasets, two reported pre- and post-operative AOFAS while one reported only post-operative outcomes ( and Table 2). The pooled AOFAS following proximal osteotomies was 82.1 (CI: 62.4–101.8; I2 = 97%).
 

## Revision rates
The revision rate was 0–8% following proximal osteotomies ( ). Two articles reported that no further surgery was required, while the other two reported two revision surgeries each, all due to HV recurrence (HVA >30°).

## Discussion
This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to synthesize and critically appraise the literature on long-term outcomes of proximal osteotomies of the M1 to treat non-inflammatory and non-degenerative HV. We found four eligible studies, with a mean follow-up that ranged from 8 to 18 years. At a minimum follow-up of 8 years following proximal osteotomies of M1, the HVA was 20.4° and the IMA was 8.8°. There was considerable heterogeneity in all reported outcomes.
The first proximal osteotomy procedure for the treatment of HV was described in 1992 by Mann et al. (Repair of HV with a distal soft-tissue procedure and proximal metatarsal osteotomy) and required fixation using a screw or pin, with soft tissue release. In the study by Mann et al., the 107 operated patients underwent follow-up at a minimum of 2 years and found minimal rates of recurrence (2%). Furthermore, studies by Biz et al. revealed good clinical outcomes following HV correction with additional Reverdin-Isham and Akin percutaneous osteotomies ( ), as well as with minimally invasive intramedullary nail devices ( ). In the present systematic review, four different types of osteotomy were performed, but all patients included underwent a soft tissue release.
In the present meta-analysis on proximal osteotomies, we found a mean post-operative HVA of 20.4°. This angle was greater than expected and exceeds the threshold of 15° to be considered recurrent HV. Several hypotheses can be raised concerning these results: first, patients may only have a moderate recurrence with few symptoms and no revision required; second, patients may be disappointed with the first procedure and not consider a revision because of this bad experience already undergone; or third, revisions were performed at another centre. It is impossible to provide retrospective clarification of this situation with the present study, and it is likely that a mix of these different hypotheses may have occurred for patients with recurrences. In a recent systematic review on distal osteotomies for treatment of HV, a mean HVA of 15° was found. This could explain why proximal osteotomies are less often used, and why fewer studies have been published on this technique, as the rates of recurrence might be slightly higher. On the other hand, according to the findings of the present meta-analysis, the IMA was approximately the same as for distal osteotomies, which is in disagreement with the previous meta-analysis by Tsikopoulos et al. ( ), which found a better IMA result for proximal osteotomies. However, this conclusion was based on shorter-term follow-up studies.

## Limitations
The results of the present review should be interpreted with the following limitations in mind. First, there was high heterogeneity among outcomes of proximal procedures and post-operative HVA, IMA, and AOFAS, making it difficult to compare outcomes. This heterogeneity was, however, consistent with those previously reported in similar studies ( , ) and might be due to the lack of consensus to define recurrence in HV; some of the authors use HVA thresholds (20°, 30°, or >10° increase in HVA in this study), while others only consider symptomatic recurrence or those requiring revision. As the most common threshold to define HV is an HVA greater than 20°, we would recommend using this same threshold to define recurrence in future studies in order to minimise heterogeneity. Finally, due to the limited number of studies on the long-term outcomes of proximal osteotomies, the CIs are often large, which could make it difficult to draw firm conclusions.

## Conclusion
At a minimum follow-up of 8 years following proximal osteotomies of M1, the HVA was 20.4° and the IMA was 8.8°. Furthermore, the recurrence rates considering the various thresholds of HVA were 12% having >30°, 73% having >20°, and 13% having a >10° increase in HVA. These findings could help surgeons and patients set realistic expectations in terms of recurrence rates at longer-term follow-up.
