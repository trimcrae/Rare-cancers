## ABSTRACT

## Objective
Aortic stenosis (AS) may complicate cardiogenic shock (CS) or acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) in a small but significant number of patients. In such cases, urgent treatment is necessary to prevent mortality and adverse outcomes. Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) and balloon aortic valvuloplasty (BAV) represent the main treatment options, however, limited studies have compared them directly. Therefore, this systematic review and meta‐analysis aims to synthesize available studies directly comparing urgent TAVI and BAV, in respect to reported outcomes.

## Methods
We systematically searched MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science for studies comparing outcomes in patients with CS or ADHF undergoing urgent TAVI or BAV. Followingly, a meta‐analysis of all included studies was performed.

## Results
A total of six studies, including 21,020 patients (10,597 patients undergoing TAVI and 10,423 patients undergoing BAV) were analyzed. Regarding in‐hospital mortality, it was found to be significantly lower in patients undergoing urgent TAVI, compared to BAV (risk ratio [RR]: 0.53; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.32−0.87]. Moreover, urgent TAVI was also associated with significantly lower all‐cause mortality at 30‐days follow up (RR 0.51, 95% CI: 0.31−0.84).

## Conclusion
In this meta‐analysis of observational, retrospective studies including patients undergoing urgent transcatheter interventions for acute AS, TAVI is associated with significantly reduced in‐hospital and 30‐days mortality, in comparison to BAV. More larger studies, reporting extended follow‐up and adverse events' rates, are needed to safely conclude regarding the benefit of urgent interventions in the acute care.

Keywords: aortic stenosis, balloon aortic valvuloplasty, cardiogenic shock, heart failure, transcatheter aortic valve implantation

## Introduction
Aortic stenosis (AS) is one of the most common valvular heart diseases (VHD), having an estimated worldwide prevalence of 3.4%, which it is further increased in the elderly, and therefore holding important treatment implications, considering the aging population [ ]. AS and VHD in general are generally presented in stable patients. However, they can also present in the context of cardiogenic shock (CS) and acute hemodynamic instability, complicating their management whilst further promoting left ventricular dysfunction and adverse outcomes. Large studies investigating acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) cohorts have shown variable rates of VHD in CS and ADHF, ranging from 11% to 36%, with more than 20% of such diagnoses being related to aortic disease [ , , , ]. Without treatment, the survival of such patients is dreadful, reaching up to 70% [ ].
Before the era of transcatheter intervention, the only invasive solution for the management of AS in the setting of CS was surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR). Early experience with SAVR showed acceptable, for this high‐risk population, operation‐related mortality and adverse events [ , ]. More recently, transcatheter options became available and therefore investigated in patients with CS. Early studies with balloon aortic valvuloplasty (BAV) showed feasibility but reported suboptimal mortality rates [ ], contrary to transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), that showed more favorable results, leading to its suggestion by most expert consensus statements in this setting [ , ]. However, studies directly comparing the two interventions are limited, as most performed investigations are single‐arm reports [ ]. Therefore, the aim of this systematic review and meta‐analysis is to analyze all available studies directly comparing TAVI and BAV in CS or ADHF patients in regard to mortality events.

2.

## Methods
2.1.

## Data Source and Search Strategy
A systematic literature search was performed in three electronic databases (MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science) from their inception to March 4, 2025 for studies comparing urgent TAVI and BAV in patients with CS or ADHF, in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses (PRISMA) statement [ ]. The search strategy included relevant terms, among which “transcatheter aortic valve implantation,” “transcatheter aortic valve replacement,” “balloon aortic valvuloplasty,” “cardiogenic shock,” and “acute heart failure,” which were modified per each database requirements. Potentially eligible articles not identified by algorithm search were assessed from the references of relevant articles through the “snowball” approach.

2.2.

## Study Selection
Studies were considered eligible if they met the following criteria: (1) human subjects aged ≥ 18 years undergoing urgent TAVI or BAV for severe AS; (2) two‐arm studies comparing the two interventions, (3) events for at least one outcome were reported for both arms. Studies involving single‐arm studies, conference abstracts, case reports, editorials, reviews, and expert opinions, were excluded from the present analysis.

2.3.

## Data Extraction
Two investigators (K.A. and N.P.) independently screened all titles and abstracts and manually searched the full text versions of all relevant studies that fulfilled the inclusion criteria. The reference lists of the retrieved articles were also independently reviewed. Any discrepancies between the two investigators were resolved by a third investigator (K.D.). Data extracted from the eligible studies included study and baseline characteristics as well as reported patient events.

2.4.

## Statistical Analysis
A meta‐analysis of the reported outcomes was performed by assessing the risk ratios (RR) alongside their respective 95% confidence intervals (CI). A fixed and random effect model was applied in all analyses and is presented in the respective forest plots. The degree of heterogeneity among studies was measured using I². For statistical significance, considering the low number of studies, a fixed‐effects meta‐analysis was used if the heterogeneity was low, that is, if I² values were lower than 50%, while in cases of greater heterogeneity, a random effects model was employed. Moreover, pooled mortality rates for both TAVI and BAV cohorts at all times of follow‐up were calculated using the Freeman‐Tukey double arcsine transformation to stabilize variance, while 95% CI were estimated using the Wilson score method. Significance was defined by p values less than 0.05. Publication bias was assessed with the analysis of the respective funnel plots. Finally, a sensitivity analysis with the method of “leave‐one‐out” was performed for all reported outcomes. All meta‐analyses were generated using the R software (version 4.3.3).

2.5.

## Risk of Bias Assessment
For the assessment of the quality of included studies, the Risk of Bias in Non‐Randomized Studies of Interventions (ROBINS‐I) tool was used to determine any risk of bias [ ]. This tool examined three domains of bias (pre‐intervention, at intervention, post‐intervention) and based on the results, the included studies are categorized in (1) low risk of bias; (2) moderate risk of bias; (3) serious risk of bias and (4) critical risk of bias. The results of the bias assessment can be found in the supporting material (Supporting Information Material, Supporting Information S1: Figure  ).

3.

## Results
The search yielded a total of 385 records. After duplicate removal, 225 studies were reviewed, according to the PRISMA guidelines (Figure  and Supporting Information Material, Supporting Information S1: Table  ). Following title/abstract and full text review, six studies met the inclusion criteria [ , , , , , ]. Two studies included in the full‐text review were ultimately excluded, as one did not report event rates [ ] and the other [ ] raised concerns for potential overlap of the reported cohort and outcomes with an already included study [ ]. A total of 21,020 patients were analyzed (weighted mean age of 79.2 years old), with 10,597 patients undergoing urgent TAVI and 10,423 undergoing urgent BAV. Their baseline characteristics are shown in Table  . Overall, all patients had severe AS, with 61.5% of the population being male. In respect to cardiovascular comorbidities, 69.2% of patients had coronary artery disease, 81.3% hypertension and 54.0% atrial fibrillation. Moreover, 43.7% of patients had diabetes, while 43.6% had chronic renal disease, 27.3% peripheral vascular disease and 29.9% chronic lung disease.
 In hospital pooled rates for all‐cause mortality were 10.4 (95% CI: 3.5%−20.0%) and 21.7% (95% CI: 7.2%−41.0%) for TAVI and BAV, respectively, while the RR was found significantly reduced in the TAVI, compared to the BAV cohort (RR: 0.53; 95% CI: 0.32−0.87) (Figure  ). In studies reporting 30‐day follow‐up, pooled mortality rates were 11.8 (95% CI: 0.9%−30.1%) and 27.6% (95% CI: 14.3%−41.8%), with a significant reduction being also noted in the pooled risk ratio (RR 0.51, 95% CI: 0.31−0.84) (Figure  ). Combining all available studies, all‐cause mortality rates were 11.0 (95% CI: 3.6%−21.1%) and 25.6% (95% CI: 14.4%−38.5%) for TAVI and BAV respectively, while the RR was consistently found significantly reduced in patients undergoing TAVI (RR: 0.54; 95% CI: 0.38−0.77) (Figure  ). Regarding adverse events, these were only reported in two studies [ , ] with largely different effect sizes, therefore a meta‐analysis was not feasible, as one study accounted for over 99% of the analysis weight. Finally, the sensitivity analysis revealed no significant study effect in the overall analysis (Supporting Information Material, Supporting Information S1: Figure  ), while the funnel plots show moderate risk for publication bias (Supporting Information Material, Supporting Information S1: Figure  ). According to the ROBINS‐I criteria, the risk of bias was low (Supporting Information Material, Supporting Information S1: Figure  ).
 
4.

## Discussion
Our analysis of observational, retrospective studies shows that in patients presenting with CS or ADHF complicated by AS, TAVI is associated with significantly reduced mortality events both in hospital and in 30‐days follow up, compared to BAV. These findings expand the evidence for the use of TAVI over BAV in urgent situations, showing that in direct comparison, TAVI reduces mortality by approximately 50%.
The benefit of urgent TAVI found in this analysis is in line with larger, single‐arm studies, providing prognostic information in separate cohorts. In specific, the most recent meta‐analysis by Kuhne et al. [ ], including a total of 17 studies (seven reporting outcomes for TAVI, 12 for BAV) and 2811 patients (2428 TAVI and 329 BAV) showed a pooled in‐hospital mortality of 11% for TAVI (95% CI: 0.06−0.18) and 40% (95% CI: 0.28−0.54) for BAV, as well as a pooled 30‐day mortality of 19% (95% CI: 0.17−0.20) for TAVI and 39% (95% CI: 0.32−0.46) for BAV. As in our analysis, mortality was reduced in the emergent TAVI studies, while the 50% risk reduction observed in our cohort is similar to the reduction found by Kuhne et al. (19 vs. 39%), even though employing a different cohort. Therefore, especially considering that mortality with urgent TAVI is the highest in the first 30 days, with the following period having comparable mortality to elective, high‐risk TAVI [ ], the aforementioned results further support the suggestion by consensus document of TAVI over BAV in emergent situation, when TAVI is readily available [ , ]. Despite further studies, especially randomized, could be difficult to be performed in this high‐risk cohort, future investigations are particularly needed, to provide more evidence potentially leading to the recommendation of TAVI by guidelines.
A low number of the included studies report outcomes beyond mortality. In specific, the study by Llah et al. [ ], which used the National Inpatient Sample, reported that myocardial infarction is significantly increased in BAV compared to TAVI (12.3 vs. 32.4%; p < 0.001), while urgent TAVI increases the risk for stroke (6.2 vs. 3.4%; p = 0.02) and new pacemaker implantation (11.9 vs. 6.0%; p < 0.001). Other outcomes, such as vascular complications and bleeding events, were similar between groups. Similar results were also reported for stroke and major vascular complications in a smaller study, with no effect on stroke. Interestingly, the meta‐analysis by Kuhne et al. [ ], despite not being able to provide a statistical comparison between the two interventions, showed a numerical increase of stroke (4% vs. 1%) and vascular complications (8% vs. 3%) in TAVI patients. It should be noted, however, that probably not procedural adverse events, but multiorgan failure, blunted cardiac output, and systemic hypoperfusion mostly contribute to the early mortality identified in this patient cohort [ ]. However, considering the limited results, as well as the high‐risk profile of these individuals, investigators should evaluate whether there is an intervention‐related increase in adverse events with either TAVI or BAV, to better guide procedural planning (i.e., use of cerebral protection devices) and further improve outcomes.
There is an absence of guidelines addressing the use of such interventions in the urgent setting. However, expert consensus documents provide more clarity in this matter. More specifically, an expert document by Chioncel et al. [ ] regarding acute heart failure and VHD recommends to select TAVI in patients with severe AS presenting with CS, along with pharmacotherapy and mechanical circulatory support, when needed. BAV is reserved only as a bridge to TAVI or surgery, in centers where TAVI is not available. Similar recommendations are made by an expert document regarding the interventional management of left‐sided VHD [ ]. Despite expert opinions and the evidence thus far support the use of urgent TAVI in such cases, there are still many unanswered questions in the literature regarding its optimal implementation. To begin with, most studies, either single or double arm, do not use the same definition for CS, while there is a lack of employing universal criteria, such as the SCAI shock criteria [ ]. Future studies need to employ widely accepted diagnostic criteria and categorization for CS, to limit between‐study heterogeneity. Moreover, TAVI offers a destination therapy, while BAV is mostly considered as a bridge to either surgery or TAVI. Therefore, considering lifetime management of AS, any differences among the types of valves used (balloon‐ or self‐expandable) should be investigated, aiming to address their effect in short‐term hemodynamics in this population as well as the risk for future bioprosthetic valve dysfunction. Finally, the timing of intervening is not well‐established in TAVI cohorts. Evidence from urgent BAV studies indicate that delaying treatment (> 48 h from CS onset) may adversely impact survival [ ], thus supporting prompt treatment of patients presenting with this phenotype. Therefore, despite there is a clear benefit on mortality with the use of urgent TAVI, the optimal timing, valve and procedural characteristics, patient phenotype and setting (expert CS center) [ ] should be further determined, toward establishing these interventions in the acute setting.
Our study has certain limitations. First, all included studies were retrospective observational and therefore bear the respective limitations of their design. Importantly, due to their observational design, there is the possibility of patient selection bias in the included reports, which cannot be statistically compensated in the absence of randomized, large‐scale studies. Second, the indication for urgent TAVI or BAV in each trial has differences, as each trial defined the urgent intervention indication differently; therefore, resulting in possible heterogeneity. Moreover, the small number of included studies and the low number of events in some of the studies may limit the efficacy of large‐sample approximations and the generalizability of these findings, highlighting the need for further, large scale studies. Finally, as only two studies reported adverse outcomes, their large differences limited our ability to report further event estimates.

5.

## Conclusion
This systematic review and meta‐analysis of observational, retrospective studies shows that urgent TAVI, in patient with severe AS and CS or ADHF, is associated with significantly enhanced survival both in‐hospital and at 30‐days follow up. Further larger, prospective and randomized studies directly comparing TAVI and BAV and reporting outcomes beyond mortality, as well as providing longer‐term follow up, are necessary toward establishing indications of transcatheter treatment in the acute cardiovascular care.
