## Introduction
Dolutegravir (DTG) + lamivudine (3TC) demonstrated high rates of virologic suppression (VS) and low rates of virologic failure (VF), discontinuation, and drug resistance in randomized trials. Real-world evidence can support treatment effectiveness, safety, and tolerability in clinical practice and aid in treatment decisions.

## Methods
A systematic literature review (SLR) was conducted to identify studies using DTG + 3TC (January 2013–March 2024). Studies were screened to include observational studies reporting 48- or 96-week on-treatment VS, VF, or discontinuation outcomes; proportions of individuals with each outcome at each time point were estimated using random- and common-effects models.

## Results
Of 249 SLR-identified publications, 43 reported consistently defined outcomes of interest at comparable time points, representing 1480 individuals naive to antiretroviral therapy (ART) and 12,234 individuals with prior ART experience. At weeks 48 and 96, respectively, estimated proportions (95% CIs; random-effects model) with on-treatment VS were high (naive to ART, 0.964 [0.945–0.979] and 0.902 [0.816–0.966]; prior ART, 0.966 [0.950–0.980] and 0.971 [0.946–0.990]), with low estimated proportions experiencing VF (naive to ART, 0.001 [0.000–0.013] and 0.001 [0.000–0.008]; prior ART, 0.009 [0.005–0.015] and 0.015 [0.007–0.024]) and discontinuations for any reason (naive to ART, 0.052 [0.019–0.097] and 0.130 [0.084–0.183]; prior ART, 0.067 [0.042–0.098] and 0.084 [0.047–0.130]). Across identified studies (> 44,000 unique individuals), those reporting resistance outcomes at VF/blip (regardless of emergence) detected integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) mutations in 0 of 2346 individuals naive to ART and 0.02% (4/20,060) of individuals with prior ART experience (S147G, R263K, G118R + E138K, T66A + G118R + E138K); additionally, N155H was reported in an individual using DTG + 3TC with unknown baseline ART status.

## Conclusion
Overall treatment outcomes in real-world settings confirm the efficacy, tolerability, and high barrier to resistance seen in phase 3 trials across diverse populations, including those naive to ART or with prior ART experience.

## Introduction
Regional and international guidelines currently recommend dolutegravir (DTG) combined with lamivudine (3TC), or DTG + 3TC (single- or multi-tablet combination), as a first-line antiretroviral therapy (ART) for individuals naive to ART or as a suppressed-switch option for individuals with prior ART experience living with HIV-1 [ – ]. In randomized controlled trials (RCTs), DTG + 3TC demonstrated high antiviral efficacy and durability. As first-line therapy in participants naive to ART, DTG + 3TC demonstrated non-inferiority to DTG-based 3-drug regimens at 24 weeks in the phase 4 D2ARLING trial (vs pooled DTG + tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine [TDF/FTC] and DTG + TDF/3TC) [ ] and at 144 weeks in the phase 3 pooled GEMINI-1/-2 clinical trials (vs DTG + TDF/FTC) [ ]. In participants with prior ART experience and virologic suppression (VS), switching to DTG + 3TC was non-inferior to continuing or switching to bictegravir/FTC/tenofovir alafenamide (BIC/FTC/TAF) at 48 weeks in the phase 4 DYAD and PASO DOBLE trials, respectively [ , ]; continuing a stable 3- or 4-drug current ART regimen at 48 weeks in the phase 3 SALSA [ ] and phase 4 DOLAM [ ] trials; and continuing a stable 3- or 4-drug TAF-based regimen at 144 weeks in the phase 3 TANGO trial [ ]. Furthermore, the phase 2a VOLVER trial and SOLAR-3D trial suggest that historical M184V/I does not affect DTG/3TC (single-tablet combination) efficacy in stable-switch settings through 48 or 144 weeks, respectively [ , ], consistent with results from a meta-analysis of RCTs and real-world data demonstrating that historical M184V/I had a minimal impact on incidence of study-defined virologic failure (VF) at weeks 24, 48, and 96 [ ].
Real-world evidence (RWE) can supplement RCT results and reassure clinicians that DTG + 3TC is an effective and tolerable treatment option across diverse populations encountered in clinical practice. Observational data are becoming increasingly available that address knowledge gaps regarding longitudinal DTG + 3TC effectiveness, including use in populations that are historically underrepresented in RCTs, such as those with an unknown or positive history of VF, historical or current resistance-associated mutations (RAMs), or certain comorbidities.
A previous meta-analysis estimated the effectiveness and tolerability of DTG + 3TC in real-world settings using systematic literature review (SLR)-identified studies published between January 2013 and August 2021 [ ]. Week 48 meta-analysis-estimated proportions across populations naive to ART and with prior ART experience, respectively, were high for VS (on-treatment VS, 1.000 and 0.987; Snapshot VS, 0.854 and 0.864) and low for VF (0.000 and 0.012) and discontinuations (0.136 and 0.119) [ ]. In this SLR and meta-analysis, our objective is to expand those findings to include more recent (through March 2024) RWE studies with data from larger, more geographically and characteristically diverse populations, including a larger number of individuals naive to ART, individuals with current or historical resistance mutations against DTG and/or 3TC, and individuals with prior ART experience with viremia at switch.

## Methods

## Systematic Literature Review
Two searches were performed for the SLR: one on November 4, 2022, and the other on March 6, 2024, to identify studies published from January 2013 to November 2022 and from January 2022 to March 2024, respectively. The SLR was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [ ]. MEDLINE® and MEDLINE® In-Process (via PubMed®), Embase®, and Cochrane databases were searched to identify RWE publications reporting DTG + 3TC use in people living with HIV from non-interventional studies conducted in routine clinical practice. Additionally, 21 HIV- or AIDS-related regional or international conference materials were manually searched to identify RWE studies from 2016 to 2024. Detailed search strategies are described in Table S1.
Publication titles and abstracts were initially screened by two independent reviewers to exclude duplicates, non-observational studies, case studies/reports, and studies with fewer than 10 individuals using DTG + 3TC. Discrepancies between reviewers were resolved by a third independent reviewer. Relevant study population and outcome data were extracted by one writer and reviewed by a senior writer.
To avoid counting overlapping populations, only the publication with the largest sample size for that cohort was included and accounted for in the total population size. The publication with the most recent data or largest population size was chosen to represent that population or cohort as the “lead” study.

## Meta-Analysis
All SLR-identified studies were screened for meta-analysis inclusion by two independent reviewers. Studies reporting at least one outcome of interest for individuals using DTG + 3TC were included. Outcomes of interest for those initiating or switching to DTG + 3TC were on-treatment VS, study-defined VF, and discontinuations for any reason at weeks 48 or 96.
Single-arm analyses were performed using both random-effects and common-effects (or fixed-effects) models to produce point estimates and 95% CIs for proportions of individuals experiencing each outcome at each time point. Studies reporting multiple outcomes were included in analyses corresponding to each outcome. Data were pooled from studies according to ART status at DTG + 3TC start (naive to ART or prior ART), endpoint (VS, VF, or discontinuation), and time point (week 48 or 96). Virologic outcomes had a base-case and sensitivity analysis performed at each time point. Base-case analyses pooled data from all studies (inclusive of all outcome definitions) and sensitivity analyses pooled data from studies using the same definitions (VS, viral load < 50 copies/mL; VF, one or two consecutive viral loads ≥ 50 copies/mL) to assess the impact of more restrictive definitions on overall estimates.
Data sets were transformed using Freeman–Tukey double-arcsine transformations and meta-analyses were performed using inverse-variance methods. Additional sensitivity analyses were performed using arcsine square root-transformed data sets. Meta-analyses were conducted using R (version 4.0.3 or greater; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) via the meta and metafor packages [ , ]. Forest plots were constructed to report the effect size and 95% CI for each study and for pooled outcome results.
Heterogeneity was assessed using the consistency statistic I2 according to the equation I2 = ([Q − df]/Q) × 100, where Q is the chi-squared statistic and df is its degrees of freedom. Thresholds of I2 estimated whether heterogeneity levels may be unimportant (0–40%), moderate (30–60%), substantial (50–90%), or considerable (75–100%). The results section reports estimates using the random-effects model, given the moderate-to-considerable degree of between-study heterogeneity present in most cases; however, both random- and common-effects model results are presented in figures and tables. Publication bias for each outcome was assessed by measuring the funnel plot asymmetry of sample size vs estimated effect size using Egger’s test [ ].

## RAMs at VF/Blip Among all SLR-Identified Publications
Cases of RAMs detected at VF or blip at any time point were assessed among all SLR-identified studies reporting resistance-testing results in individuals initiating or switching to DTG + 3TC. Analysis time point, treatment-emergence status, and type of mutation are summarized, with any potential overlap recorded. Mutations were interpreted and classified according to the Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database [ , ].

This article is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

## Results

## Systematic Literature Review
The SLR identified 249 publications reporting DTG + 3TC use for treatment of HIV in real-world settings (Fig.  ). These publications represent 191 studies, 84 discrete cohorts, and 44,436 unique individuals (naive to ART, n = 2346; prior ART, n = 20,060; unspecified/pooled, n = 22,030). 

## Meta-Analysis

## Included Studies and Baseline Characteristics
Among the 249 SLR-identified publications, 43 publications (n = 13,714 individuals) reported outcomes of interest at weeks 48 and/or 96 and were included in the meta-analysis (Fig.  ; Table S2) [ – ]. Outcome data from 15 lead studies with populations naive to ART (n = 1480 individuals) and 27 lead studies with populations with prior ART experience (n = 12,234 individuals) were used in meta-analysis calculations. Baseline characteristics of populations from studies included in the meta-analysis are summarized in Table  [ – ]. 
Among studies reporting baseline viral load categories for individuals naive to ART, approximately 32.6% (396/1214) of unique individuals had viral loads ≥ 100,000 copies/mL [ – , , , , , , ] and 24.1% (93/386) had viral loads ≥ 500,000 copies/mL [ , , , , ]. Among studies reporting RAMs before DTG + 3TC initiation, 1.3% (12/916) of unique individuals had transmitted drug resistance at baseline; of them, 3 (< 1.0%) individuals had the 3TC RAM M184V and 3 (< 1.0%) had integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) RAMs (G163K, n = 1; unspecified, n = 2) [ , , , , , , ].
Among studies reporting baseline viral load for individuals with prior ART experience, 2.6% (318/12,124) of unique individuals included in at least one meta-analysis calculation at week 48 [ , – , – , – , – ] and 3.4% (174/5104) included at week 96 [ , , – , , , , , , ] had a detectable (and/or ≥ 50 copies/mL) viral load at time of switch. Across nine studies reporting historical VF at baseline or using historical VF as an exclusion criterion, 8.6% (573/6654) of unique individuals had historical VF [ , , , , , , , , ]; among these studies, 63 individuals had viral loads ≥ 50 copies/mL at switch [ , ]. Twenty individuals experienced VF with an INSTI regimen [ ]. Individuals with pre-switch INSTI RAMs (n = 11) were reported, with two studies reporting specific RAMs (G118R, E138K + G163R, and S147G, n = 1 each; H51H/D/N/Y, n unspecified) [ , , , ]. Reported pre-switch nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor RAMs included M184V/I (n = 262) [ , , , , , , , , ].

## Virologic Suppression
Among the 43 studies included in the meta-analysis, 38 reported VS outcomes at either time point in populations naive to ART and/or with prior ART experience (Tables S3 [ – , – , ] and S4 [ , , – , – , – ]). Definitions of VS included having a viral load of either < 50 copies/mL (28 studies), < 40 copies/mL (2 studies), or ≤ 20 copies/mL (4 studies); 4 studies used another definition and/or included more than one criterion.
Among individuals naive to ART, initiating treatment with DTG + 3TC resulted in high meta-analysis-estimated proportions achieving on-treatment VS, inclusive of all VS definitions, at weeks 48 and 96 (Fig.  a) [ – , – , – , ]. Specifically, estimated proportions with on-treatment VS were 0.964 (95% CI 0.945–0.979) at week 48 (pooled from 13 studies; n = 862 individuals) and 0.902 (95% CI 0.816–0.966) at week 96 (pooled from 4 studies; n = 385 individuals). 
Among individuals with prior ART experience, switching to DTG + 3TC also resulted in high meta-analysis-estimated proportions with on-treatment VS, inclusive of all VS definitions, at both time points (Fig.  b) [ , , , , , – , – , – ]. Specifically, estimated proportions with on-treatment VS were 0.966 (95% CI 0.950–0.980) at week 48 (pooled from 24 studies; n = 7130 individuals) and 0.971 (95% CI 0.946–0.990) at week 96 (pooled from 9 studies; n = 3192 individuals).

## Virologic Failure
Among the 43 studies included in the meta-analysis, 37 reported VF outcomes at either time point in populations naive to ART and/or with prior ART experience (Tables S3 [ – , – , ] and S4 [ , , – , – , – ]). Definitions of VF included having one or two consecutive viral loads ≥ 50 copies/mL (13 studies; 1 reported separate outcomes for a second definition [≥ 200 copies/mL; not included in meta-analysis]; 1 required first achieving an undetectable viral load; 2 included alternative criterion) and/or one or two consecutive viral loads of either ≥ 20 copies/mL (1 study), ≥ 40 copies/mL (2 studies), ≥ 200 copies/mL (6 studies; 1 specified that the consecutive viral loads be within a 90-day epoch; 1 had additional criterion), or ≥ 1000 copies/mL (5 studies; 1 specifying “persistent” viral load ≥ 1000 copies/mL after ≥ 6 months of ART); additionally, 8 studies did not define VF criteria, and 2 studies used investigator’s discretion.
Among individuals naive to ART, treatment with DTG + 3TC resulted in low meta-analysis-estimated proportions experiencing VF, inclusive of all VF definitions (Fig.  a) [ – , – , – , – , ]. Specifically, estimated proportions experiencing VF were 0.001 (95% CI 0.000–0.013) at week 48 (pooled from 13 studies; n = 906 individuals) and 0.001 (95% CI 0.000–0.008) at week 96 (pooled from 5 studies; n = 629 individuals). Of note, an insufficient number of studies defined VF using the sensitivity analysis VF definition (one or two consecutive viral loads ≥ 50 copies/mL) per time point; thus, VF sensitivity analyses were not performed for populations naive to ART. 
Among individuals with prior ART experience, the meta-analysis-estimated proportions experiencing VF, inclusive of all VF definitions (Fig.  b), were 0.009 (95% CI 0.005–0.015) at week 48 (pooled from 25 studies; n = 8988 individuals) and 0.015 (95% CI 0.007–0.024) at week 96 (pooled from 12 studies; n = 4181 individuals) [ , , – , – , – , – ].

## Discontinuations
Among the 43 studies included in the meta-analysis, discontinuations for any reason were reported by 13 studies in populations naive to ART (n = 1140 individuals) and 19 studies in populations with prior ART experience (n = 6632 individuals) through week 48 and by 3 studies (n = 222 individuals) and 7 studies (n = 2148 individuals), respectively, through week 96 (Table S5) [ – , , – , , , , , , – , – ]. Meta-analysis-estimated proportions discontinuing for any reason through weeks 48 and 96 were 0.052 (95% CI 0.019–0.097) and 0.130 (95% CI 0.084–0.183) [ – , , – , – , ], respectively, for individuals naive to ART (Fig.  a) and 0.067 (95% CI 0.042–0.098) and 0.084 (95% CI 0.047–0.130), respectively, for individuals with prior ART experience (Fig.  b) [ , , – , , , , , , – , – ]. Across all lead studies reporting reasons for discontinuation, 2.5% (218/8606) of individuals had discontinuations attributed to adverse events or toxicities (treatment-unrelated deaths excluded; n = 25 studies) [ , , , , , – , , , , – , , – ], and 1.0% (14/1364) of individuals had discontinuations specifically due to drug-related adverse events (n = 11 studies) [ , , – , , , , ]. 

## Sensitivity Analyses and Bias Assessments
Sensitivity analyses using data from studies sharing the most common virologic outcome definitions (VS, viral load < 50 copies/mL; VF, one or two consecutive viral loads ≥ 50 copies/mL) were consistent with base-case analyses that pooled all data across all virologic outcome definitions, indicating that data could be pooled to maximize n values without affecting estimates (Figs. S1 and S2) [ , , – , – , , , – , – , , – , – , , , ]. Results for all outcomes were similar to corresponding additional sets of sensitivity meta-analyses using arcsine square root-transformed data (Tables S6 and S7).
Overall, funnel plots showed little to no asymmetry, suggesting limited evidence of publication bias across analyses, including sensitivity analyses (Figs. S3, S4, and S5). Egger’s linear regression test showed no evidence of publication bias across analyses [ ].

## RAMs at VF/Blip Among all SLR-Identified Publications
The SLR identified 249 publications representing 84 cohorts and 44,436 unique individuals; of the studies that reported resistance outcomes, no individuals naive to ART (n = 2346) had any RAMs reported at VF/blip and 0.02% (4/20,060) of individuals with prior ART experience had INSTI RAMs reported at VF/blip (Table  ) [ , , – ]: S147G at week 18 [ ], R263K at week 144 [ ], G118R + E138K at month 21 [ ], and T66A + G118R + E138K (time of VF was reported as “months later” after switching to DTG + 3TC) [ ]. One study reported one case of treatment-emergent N155H after an unspecified duration of using DTG + 3TC [ ]; however, their ART status before using DTG + 3TC was not reported. Non-INSTI RAMs at VF across all SLR studies reporting resistance outcomes are summarized in Table S8 [ , , , , , – ]. Treatment-emergent M184V/I was reported in seven individuals with prior ART experience and four individuals with non-reported baseline ART status (one study had potential cohort overlap). No RAMs were reported at virologic blip. 

## Discussion
Our SLR identified RWE studies representing more than 44,000 individuals using DTG + 3TC in real-world clinical settings and updates a previously published SLR [ ] to include studies published through March 2024. The meta-analysis of the SLR provides estimated proportions of individuals with virologic and tolerability outcomes at weeks 48 and 96 using data from a larger (32 additional studies with 10,541 more individuals [naive to ART, + 1328; prior ART, + 9213]), more global, and more diverse data set, including data from populations with baseline or historical drug resistance to DTG or 3TC and populations with prior ART experience who had viremia at switch. Real-world evidence is critical to support clinicians with real-world treatment decisions; using data gathered from routine clinical practice, outside of the controlled environment of clinical trials, enriches our understanding of treatment outcomes in people living with HIV across diverse demographics, behaviors, and comorbidities.
The meta-analysis-estimated high proportions of individuals with VS and low proportions of individuals with VF are consistent with outcomes from phase 3 RCTs evaluating efficacy of DTG + 3TC. In participants naive to ART from the pooled GEMINI-1/-2 trials, proportions with per-protocol VS were 0.929 at week 48 and 0.880 at week 96, and proportions meeting confirmed virologic withdrawal criteria were 0.008 at week 48 and 0.015 at week 96 [ ]. In comparison, our estimated proportions of individuals naive to ART with on-treatment VS were 0.964 at week 48 and 0.902 at week 96; estimated proportions with VF were 0.001 at both time points. In participants with prior ART experience and VS at switch from TANGO and SALSA [ , ], the proportions with per-protocol VS at week 48 were 0.940 and 0.964, respectively, compared with our on-treatment VS estimate of 0.966; at week 96, the proportion of participants from TANGO with per-protocol VS was 0.874, compared with our estimated proportion of 0.971. No participants met confirmed virologic withdrawal criteria at either time point in TANGO or SALSA. In suppressed-switch populations, VF may be a more clinically significant outcome compared with VS because it indicates loss of previously achieved, potentially stable, virologic control; however, incidence of VF was also low in our estimated proportions, which included individuals who were not suppressed at switch (week 48, 0.009; week 96, 0.015). Therefore, this meta-analysis demonstrates the high effectiveness of DTG + 3TC in real-world settings and supports results from RCTs.
Our meta-analysis estimated high VS and low VF rates in individuals switching to DTG + 3TC, which included data from 262 individuals with historical M184V/I. A meta-analysis of RWE and RCTs in populations switching to DTG + 3TC estimated similarly low rates of VF between those with and without pre-switch M184V/I mutations through 96 weeks [ ]. The SOLAR-3D trial reported that similar proportions of participants receiving DTG + 3TC with and without pre-switch M184V/I had HIV-1 RNA ≥ 50 copies/mL at week 144 by Snapshot (0.040 [2/50] and 0.060 [3/50], respectively) [ ]. Furthermore, participants from the phase 2a VOLVER trial switching to DTG/3TC with suspected or confirmed 3TC RAMs had high proportions with Snapshot VS (0.901 [109/121] with HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) at week 48 [ ]. Most importantly, no cases of treatment-emergent INSTI mutations were reported at VF among these three studies [ – ]. An SLR analysis of RWE and RCTs using DTG + 3TC (n = 47,350 people naive to ART and with prior ART) found that INSTI mutations at VF were rare (< 0.05%) overall, and zero people with pre-existing M184V/I developed INSTI RAMs at VF [ ]. Together, these data suggest that DTG + 3TC in the presence of M184V/I does not represent DTG functional monotherapy.
Because of the low toxicity levels of each component individually [ , ], DTG + 3TC was expected to be well tolerated. Overall proportions of participants discontinuing DTG + 3TC treatment in RCTs were 0.092 and 0.145 from GEMINI-1/-2 at weeks 48 and week 96, respectively [ , ]; 0.073 and 0.111 from TANGO at weeks 48 and 96, respectively [ , ]; and 0.057 from SALSA at week 48 [ ]. Discontinuation estimates from our meta-analysis at weeks 48 and 96 (naive to ART, 0.052 and 0.130, respectively; prior ART, 0.067 and 0.084, respectively) were consistent with RCT proportions at corresponding time points. Total discontinuations should be interpreted with caution, as reasons can be unrelated to treatment tolerance (e.g., lost to follow-up, insurance changes); however, a low (0.025) proportion of discontinuations from our lead studies were reported to be due to adverse events or toxicities.
Currently, DTG + 3TC is recommended as a first-line therapy or switch option for people living with HIV-1 who are naive to ART or have prior ART experience, respectively [ – ]. Another recommended therapy for both people naive to ART and with prior ART experience is BIC/FTC/TAF, where BIC is a different second-generation INSTI. Treatment outcomes from direct comparisons of BIC/FTC/TAF and DTG/3TC continue to emerge from RCTs. In the prospective RUMBA study, 97.5% of participants switched to DTG/3TC (79/81) and 97.5% switched to or continuing BIC/FTC/TAF (39/40) maintained on-treatment VS (HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) at week 48 [ , ]. In the phase 4 DYAD study, 85.2% (127/149) of participants switched to DTG/3TC (from BIC/FTC/TAF) had Snapshot VS, compared with 80.8% (59/73) continuing BIC/FTC/TAF, at week 48 [ ]. In the larger phase 4 PASO DOBLE study, where both treatment groups were switched from a previous regimen, 93.1% (258/277) of participants switched to DTG/3TC and 89.9% switched to BIC/FTC/TAF (248/276) had Snapshot VS at week 48 [ ]. Overall, both regimens were equally effective at maintaining VS in RCTs, with similarly low proportions of participants experiencing confirmed VF/virologic withdrawal criteria [ , , ]. Across these RCTs, only one INSTI RAM (with unknown treatment emergence) was reported at VF (from the DYAD study: DTG/3TC, n = 0; BIC/FTC/TAF, n = 1 [G140G/S]) [ , ]. Discontinuations due to drug-related adverse events were low among DTG/3TC and BIC/FTC/TAF treatment groups (0% to 4.0%) [ , ]. Results support the high efficacy and high barrier to resistance of recommended first-line therapies with a second-generation INSTI, as well as an overall good tolerability profile for both regimens.
Our SLR results identified 191 studies reporting DTG + 3TC use in > 44,000 individuals, demonstrating an abundance of RWE since the initial US approval of DTG in 2013 and DTG/3TC in 2019 [ , ]. Larger studies (N > 100 individuals) comparing DTG + 3TC and BIC/FTC/TAF treatment outcomes found overall similar VS and VF rates at time points from 24 weeks to approximately 2 years between the regimens [ , , , , , , , , ]. A recent SLR and meta-analysis for BIC/FTC/TAF RWE including 792 individuals naive to ART and 6732 individuals with prior ART experience showed high VS and low discontinuation estimates at weeks 24 and 48, confirming RCT results [ ]. Estimates and 95% CIs for VS and discontinuation outcomes overlapped at the week 48 time point between the BIC/FTC/TAF meta-analysis and our DTG + 3TC meta-analysis, both of which included large data sets (DTG + 3TC: naive to ART, n = 1480 individuals [15 lead studies]; prior ART, n = 12,234 individuals [27 lead studies]; BIC/FTC/TAF: naive to ART, n = 792 individuals [4 studies]; prior ART, n = 6732 individuals [11 studies]); however, VF estimates and RAMs detected at VF were not reported by the BIC/FTC/TAF meta-analysis for a full comparison of outcomes. Overall, the first-line recommended regimens of DTG + 3TC and BIC/FTC/TAF have similar and high efficacy in RCT settings in both populations naive to ART and with prior ART experience; furthermore, the performance of each regimen has been consistent between real-world and RCT settings.
Across all DTG + 3TC SLR-identified studies, there was an overall low incidence of reported RAMs at VF, supporting the high barrier to resistance of DTG + 3TC in real-world settings. A previous review of RWE reported a low incidence of treatment-emergent RAMs at VF on DTG + 3TC (0.1% [2/3558]; both in individuals with prior ART experience) [ ]. Across all our SLR-identified studies, those that reported resistance outcomes found that only five individuals had INSTI RAMs detected at VF (naive to ART, 0/2346; prior ART, 0.02% [4/20,060]; unknown ART status, n = 1); moreover, ≥ 0.07% (≥ 13/20,060) of individuals with prior ART experience had non-INSTI treatment-emergent RAMs at VF (an additional four individuals with unknown baseline ART status and potential study overlap had non-INSTI treatment-emergent RAMs at VF). In phase 3 RCTs, only one treatment-emergent RAM was reported across participants receiving DTG + 3TC: no RAMs were reported at VF through week 196 in TANGO [ ] or week 48 in SALSA [ ], and one participant who did not meet confirmed virologic withdrawal criteria developed M184V + R263R/K mutations through week 144 across both DTG-based regimens in GEMINI-1/-2 [ ].
Our study has several strengths. First, using a one-arm meta-analysis is an established technique to analyze single-arm, observational studies that provides more accurate estimates than individual studies alone by taking study-setting heterogeneity into account. Second, pooling all studies increased sample sizes without affecting estimates; of note, estimated proportions with VS included studies with more stringent definitions (viral load < 20 or < 40 copies/mL) than those included in sensitivity analyses (viral load < 50 copies/mL). Third, multiple sensitivity analyses were performed to ensure that estimates were robust. Fourth, individuals were from diverse populations representing various age groups (including ages ≥ 50 years), continents, and RAM statuses; additionally, populations naive to ART included individuals with very high baseline viral loads (≥ 500,000 copies/mL) and populations with prior ART experience included individuals with previous VF. Fifth, even though adherence to ART was not regularly measured in these observational studies, analyses from outcomes obtained in real-world clinical settings include individuals with varying degrees of adherence. Finally, the rarity of treatment-emergent RAMs (especially INSTI RAMs) extended across all SLR-identified studies reported here provides reassurance and highlights the barrier to resistance inherent to DTG + 3TC in individuals with diverse levels of compliance to treatment. Overall, our results support that DTG + 3TC is an effective and well-tolerated antiretroviral regimen, with robust results outside of controlled clinical trial environments in populations naive to ART and with prior ART experience.
Our study also has several limitations, though most are typical of single-arm, non-comparative meta-analyses. First, outcomes of interest were limited to reported data only and were not consistently reported across publications. Second, because test results for genotypic resistance were not available for all individuals experiencing VF, incidence of treatment-emergent resistance could not be fully described; however, guidelines recommend drug-resistance testing at VF [ – ], and it is reasonable to expect that any RAMs detected at VF would have been reported in the original studies. Third, RAMs detected at VF could only be confirmed as treatment emergent if individuals had pre-switch RAMs reported. Fourth, meta-analysis-estimated proportions for tolerability used discontinuations for any reason and were not performed to distinguish those related to treatment (e.g., adverse events) from those not related to treatment (e.g., lost to follow-up), which may oversimplify the interpretation of these data. Finally, even if it was high for the main analysis, the capacity to detect publication bias via Egger’s test was potentially lacking for sensitivity analyses pooling data from fewer than 10 studies (e.g., week 96 outcomes).

## Conclusion
Overall, our data show that DTG + 3TC is an effective and well-tolerated regimen in real-world clinical practice, with high proportions of VS and low proportions of VF that are consistent with virologic outcomes from RCTs, while representing a broader spectrum of demographics, behaviors, and comorbidities. Our meta-analysis, which includes data from more than 13,000 individuals using DTG + 3TC with diverse characteristics (including high baseline viral loads, previous VF, and/or RAMs) in real-world settings, continues to support DTG/3TC as an effective therapy for individuals naive to ART and with prior ART experience. The SLR-identified publications represented > 44,000 individuals, where only 5 cases of INSTI RAMs conferring resistance to DTG (4 individuals with prior ART and 1 individual using DTG + 3TC with unknown baseline ART status) were reported at VF among included studies reporting resistance outcomes (including 1 case of a secondary INSTI mutation), highlighting the high barrier to resistance of the regimen.
