https://orcid.org/0009-0005-4930-4333YeYing ShiYa https://orcid.org/0009-0008-1610-0790DengTiantian https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5135-822XGuCan Against this backdrop, age-related diseases such as sarcopenia have become a core challenge. This geriatric syndrome, characterised by progressive decline in skeletal muscle mass, strength and function, has received widespread attention due to its high prevalence and serious consequences. A systematic review incorporating 263 studies indicates that the prevalence of sarcopenia among the global older adult population ranges from approximately 10% to 27%. This condition is closely associated with adverse health outcomes such as chronic diseases, falls, disability, increased hospitalisation rates and higher mortality, imposing a significant public health and societal burden.
Therefore, increasing attention has been focused on probable sarcopenia, which is defined as an early screening stage of sarcopenia characterised primarily by a decline in muscle strength. Accordingly, the most recent consensus statements from the European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People 2 (EWGSOP2) and the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia 2 (AWGS2) both emphasise that a key strategy for addressing sarcopenia is early prevention, highlighting the importance of early screening and intervention for probable sarcopenia. At present, epidemiological studies conducted internationally report a notably high prevalence. For example, a survey of older adults in southern Brazil by Paludo et al. reported a prevalence of probable sarcopenia of 19.2% ; a study by Tsekoura et al. in western Greece found a prevalence of 25.4% among older adults ; Pérez-Sousa et al. reported a prevalence of 46.5% in a Colombian population ; and a cross-sectional study conducted in China by Yao et al. reported a prevalence as high as 48.1%. 
However, current epidemiological evidence on probable sarcopenia presents contradictory findings, with striking discrepancies in prevalence estimates across studies. First, even among studies conducted within the same country and applying identical diagnostic criteria, prevalence estimates can vary several-fold. For instance, a national study based on the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study reported a prevalence of probable sarcopenia of 38.5% using the AWGS2 criteria, whereas another cross-sectional study in China applying the same criteria reported a substantially lower prevalence of only 11.1%. Second, the influence of diagnostic criteria on prevalence estimates has also been inconsistent across studies from different countries. Among community-dwelling older adults in Korea, prevalence estimates based on the EWGSOP2 and AWGS2 criteria were similar (23.5% vs 23.7%) ; however, in Chinese populations, estimates differed markedly, with 21% reported using the EWGSOP2 criteria compared with 38.5% when applying the AWGS2 criteria. 
Furthermore, studies on risk factors for probable sarcopenia have also yielded inconsistent results. Contradictory trends are apparent even for established demographic factors such as age. A longitudinal study in Uppsala, Sweden, reported a prevalence as high as 73% among older adults aged 85–89 years, suggesting sharply elevated risk in the oldest old. By contrast, a study in Colombia observed a prevalence of approximately 37% in adults aged 60–79 years, which then declined to 24.8% in those over 80. Similarly, the associations between body mass index (BMI) and probable sarcopenia lack consensus. While Curtis et al identified higher BMI as a risk factor among community-dwelling older adults in England, Chalermsri et al reported a protective effect of higher BMI in an Asian community sample. Inconsistency extends to modifiable lifestyle factors as well. A study in Chile found that consuming fewer than two daily servings of fruit and vegetables was associated with a 4.28-fold higher risk of low muscle strength ; however, a community-based study in Iran observed no significant association after multivariable adjustment. 
Taken together, current evidence on the prevalence and risk factors of probable sarcopenia resembles scattered and at times contradictory ‘fragments’ rather than a unified ‘knowledge map’. This inconsistency in evidence presents a threefold challenge. First, due to the lack of integrated analysis based on uniform diagnostic criteria, reliable global and regional prevalence estimates have not been established, which limits the accuracy and comparability of disease burden estimates. Second, conflicting results in identifying relevant risk factors make it difficult to determine priority targets for prevention and intervention. Furthermore, existing studies lack systematic assessment and interpretation of outcome heterogeneity, hindering deeper understanding of the potential true distribution patterns and impact mechanisms of sarcopenia. Therefore, a systematic review and meta-analysis is urgently needed to quantitatively integrate existing global evidence and further explore the sources of heterogeneity.
To address these gaps, the study aims to achieve three core objectives through a systematic review and meta-analysis. First, comprehensively estimate the combined prevalence of probable sarcopenia among older adults globally and across different regions. Second, conduct a comprehensive analysis of the risk factors for probable sarcopenia. Third, assess and explore sources of heterogeneity in prevalence and risk factor outcomes, including diagnostic criteria, measurement tools and demographic characteristics. The results of this study will consolidate the evidence base on the prevalence and risk factors of probable sarcopenia, which is expected to provide a basis for the development of precise screening and prevention strategies according to different population characteristics, lay a foundation for the establishment of a standardised diagnosis and evaluation system in the future, and provide a reliable evidence-based medical reference for scholars in related fields.

## Methods and analysis

## Study design and registration
This protocol will follow the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2020 statement and the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for prevalence reviews. It was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251153142) prior to commencing searches. As a review of aggregate published data, ethics approval is not required. Any protocol amendments will be documented in PROSPERO and reported transparently in the final review.

## Eligibility criteria
Studies will be included if they meet the following criteria, structured according to the PECOS framework. 

## Inclusion criteria

## P-Population
The included subjects were ≥60 years old, or the mean age of the subjects was ≥60 years old. The threshold of 60 years was chosen as it aligns with international definitions of older age and is widely used in sarcopenia research, despite variations across regions (eg, ≥50 years in some African countries, ≥65 years in Western nations, ≥75 years in Japan). 

## E-Exposure
The exposure factor was probable sarcopenia. Probable sarcopenia was defined as low muscle strength based on international consensus guidelines (EWGSOP2, AWGS2). According to the EWGSOP2 standard, grip strength: men <27 kg, women <16 kg; Chair stand test: >15 s for five rises. According to the AWGS2 criteria, Grip strength: men <28 kg and women <18 kg; Chair stand test: ≥12 s for five rises.

## C-Comparator
For research question 2 (risk factors), the comparator group will comprise older adults without probable sarcopenia, defined as those with normal muscle strength according to the same diagnostic criteria used to identify low muscle strength in each study. No explicit comparator was required for research question 1 (prevalence of probable sarcopenia) or research question 3 (heterogeneity analysis).

## O-Outcomes
Primary outcomes: Prevalence of probable sarcopenia reported as a percentage, proportion (numerator/denominator), or raw data enabling calculation. Secondary outcomes: Measures of association between potential risk factors and probable sarcopenia, including ORs, risk ratios or HRs with corresponding 95% CIs, or sufficient data to derive these estimates.

## S-study design
Cross-sectional studies, cohort studies and case–control studies. Randomised controlled trials will be eligible only if they report relevant baseline data prior to randomisation; such data will be used exclusively for prevalence estimation and for cross-sectional risk factor analyses, and intervention effects will not be considered.

## Exclusion criteria
Studies recruiting exclusively from disease-specific clinical settings, or examining probable sarcopenia concomitant with specific diseases (eg, cancer, cachexia, chronic kidney disease requiring dialysis, neurological diseases).
Non-original research (eg, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, scoping reviews, narrative reviews, editorials, commentaries, opinion articles, letters), conference abstracts, grey literature (eg, dissertations, unpublished reports), or case reports with a very small sample size.
Postintervention data from trials (only pre-randomisation baseline data are eligible), or duplicate publications (the less comprehensive version will be excluded).
Study protocols, ongoing studies or publications with insufficient data for extraction even after consulting the authors.

## Search strategy
We will perform a comprehensive literature search across multiple sources. 11 international and Chinese databases will be searched: Web of Science [Web of Science Core Collection], PubMed, MEDLINE [via Web of Science], Embase [Ovid], Scopus [Elsevier], Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials [Cochrane Library], CINAHL [EBSCOhost], Chinese Biomedical Literature Database [SinoMed platform], Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure [CNKI platform], WANFANG Database [WanFang platform] and Chinese Science and Technology Periodical Database [VIP platform]. Two trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP) will be searched for ongoing or unpublished studies. We will search for grey literature using Google Scholar. Retrieval and screening will be limited to the first 200 records, sorted by relevance, as this approach captures the vast majority of eligible studies while maintaining feasibility. Reference lists of included studies will be manually checked, and citation tracking will be conducted to ensure complete coverage. To ensure comprehensive global coverage and minimise language and regional bias, we will not apply language restrictions.
In consultation with an experienced medical librarian, we developed the full search strategies (encompassing both controlled vocabulary and free-text terms) for all databases, trial registries and websites. These strategies were subsequently peer-reviewed in accordance with the PRISMA-S checklist. The complete strategies, including any applied filters and limits, are detailed in the Supplementary Material. All searches will be run from the inception of each source up to January 2026.

## Study selection
The study selection process will consist of three stages: searching and deduplication, preliminary screening and full-text assessment, which will be completed by April 2026. During the searching and deduplication stage, two reviewers (YY and YS) will independently conduct searches in each database according to the predefined search strategies, then will compare the number of records retrieved from each database. If there is any discrepancy, a third member of the research team will verify the search. All retrieved records will be imported into EndNote to remove duplicates and then will be transferred to Rayyan for screening. 
During the preliminary screening stage, eligibility criteria will be piloted on a sample of records to ensure consistency between reviewers. Two independent reviewers (YY and YS) will then screen all records by title, abstract and keywords based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria described above. During the full-text assessment stage, two reviewers will independently evaluate the full text of potentially eligible articles to determine their suitability. Disagreements at both screening stages will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, consultation with a third reviewer (TD). Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be documented. Following the PRISMA 2020 recommendations, we will prepare a flow diagram to illustrate the study selection process, including the total number of records identified, screened, assessed for eligibility and included in the review, as well as specific reasons for exclusion at each stage. ## Data extraction
Two reviewers (YY and YS) will independently extract data using a standardised extraction form, with discrepancies resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer (TD). The extraction form will be piloted on a subset of eligible studies to ensure clarity, completeness and consistency before full implementation. Extracted information will include study characteristics (first author, publication year, country, design, and sample size); participant characteristics (age, sex, and residential setting); diagnostic criteria and measurement tools used to define probable sarcopenia; data on prevalence; effect estimates with corresponding 95% CIs; and variables adjusted for in multivariable analyses. For non-English and non-Chinese records that pass the initial screening, our translation and data extraction process will proceed in two stages. First, titles and abstracts will be translated using reliable machine translation software (eg, Google Translate, DeepL) for initial eligibility screening. Second, for any study deemed potentially eligible, the full text will be professionally translated. All critical data for extraction, such as prevalence estimates, effect measures and population characteristics, will be reviewed and verified against this professionally translated text to ensure accuracy.
The extraction process will be iterative, with updates made as new studies are included. Missing data will be addressed using appropriate imputation methods when feasible. Studies with substantial missingness will be excluded if the data cannot be reliably recovered. Data extraction is expected to be completed by July 2026.

## Risk of bias assessment
Two reviewers will independently assess the methodological quality and risk of bias of all included studies using the JBI Critical Appraisal Tools. Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or, when necessary, consultation with a third reviewer. Cross-sectional studies will be evaluated using the JBI Checklist for Analytical Cross-sectional Studies, while cohort and case–control studies will be appraised using their respective JBI checklists. Each checklist item will be rated as ‘yes’, ‘no’, ‘unclear’ or ‘not applicable’. Studies fulfilling a majority of the applicable criteria will be considered of acceptable methodological quality. The results of these assessments will be presented in supplementary materials and visualised using risk-of-bias plots. Sensitivity analyses excluding studies with a high risk of bias will be conducted to test the robustness of the pooled estimates. Risk-of-bias results will be summarised narratively and visualised using traffic-light plots. The final risk-of-bias graph summarising these assessments will be generated and completed by the end of September 2026.

## Data synthesis
Data synthesis will comprise both narrative synthesis and quantitative meta-analysis. Given the expected methodological and clinical heterogeneity in probable sarcopenia research, quantitative pooling will be conducted only where studies are sufficiently comparable; otherwise, structured narrative synthesis will be provided.

## Narrative synthesis
A descriptive synthesis will summarise the key features and methodological characteristics of the included studies. Guided by the integrative review principles of Whittemore and Knafl, this process will provide a comprehensive overview of study characteristics, including diagnostic criteria for probable sarcopenia, measurement tools for muscle strength, population demographics and geographical distribution. Summary tables will be developed to highlight methodological diversity and variations in prevalence estimates across studies, as well as to describe reported risk and protective factors. This descriptive synthesis will help identify data gaps, evaluate comparability across studies and inform the feasibility and strategy of the subsequent meta-analysis.

## Quantitative synthesis
If studies are sufficiently homogeneous in population, definition and outcomes, we will perform a meta-analysis. To avoid generating a single, ambiguous global estimate, we will not pool prevalence data across different diagnostic frameworks. Separate meta-analyses will be conducted according to major diagnostic frameworks (EWGSOP2 and AWGS2). All meta-analyses will employ a random-effects model to account for inherent between-study heterogeneity. Prevalence will be pooled as proportions with 95% CIs. For prevalence estimates approaching 0% or 100%, the Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation will be applied to stabilise variances. Results will be visualised using forest plots. 

## Assessment and investigation of heterogeneity
Statistical heterogeneity will be assessed using Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic. To explore potential sources of heterogeneity, we have pre-specified subgroup analyses (where data permit) based on the following factors: diagnostic criteria (ie, EWGSOP2 and AWGS2); muscle strength assessment methods (eg, handgrip strength vs. five-time chair stand test); and key population characteristics, such as geographic region, age, sex and clinical setting. If a sufficient number of studies are available, meta-regression will be performed to examine the influence of study-level characteristics (eg, mean age, sex distribution, study setting, risk-of-bias score) on the pooled estimates. These analyses are exploratory in nature, aimed at describing heterogeneity rather than establishing causal explanations. If substantial heterogeneity persists and quantitative pooling is deemed inappropriate, results will be presented through narrative synthesis.

## Risk factor analysis and interpretation
As the included studies are expected to be predominantly cross-sectional in design, analyses of risk and protective factors will be limited to the interpretation of associations rather than causal inference. Where available, multivariable-adjusted effect estimates (eg, ORs, risk ratios) will be preferentially extracted for synthesis. Furthermore, we will document and categorise the key confounding variables adjusted for in each study (such as age, sex, BMI and comorbidities). This approach will allow us to qualitatively assess whether observed associations remain consistent across studies that have controlled for similar sets of core confounders, thereby adding a layer of interpretative strength beyond the quantitative pooling of estimates. Findings will be interpreted cautiously, considering the limitations inherent in observational data.

## Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the robustness of the review findings. For pooled prevalence estimates and overall associations, analyses will be repeated after excluding studies judged to have a high risk of bias. For risk factor associations, we will perform additional sensitivity analyses to examine the influence of individual studies on summary estimates, to compare results from studies using different sets of adjusted confounders (eg, minimally adjusted vs fully adjusted models), and to assess the stability of associations across major diagnostic criteria subgroups (EWGSOP2 and AWGS2). Publication bias will be evaluated using funnel plots, and for meta-analyses including ≥10 studies, Egger’s regression and Begg’s tests will be applied. When publication bias is suspected, the trim-and-fill method will be used to estimate its potential effect on the pooled outcomes.

## Certainty of the evidence
We will exploratively apply the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations (GRADE) approach to assess the overall certainty of the evidence for the pooled prevalence estimates, acknowledging that this framework has not yet been formally established for prevalence meta-analyses. As an adapted application, the initial certainty will be rated as low, in accordance with GRADE’s default for evidence from observational studies. We will then downgrade the certainty based on five domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. The final certainty will be classified as high, moderate, low or very low. This assessment should be interpreted as a transparent, provisional evaluation rather than a definitive GRADE judgement, and the results will be presented with appropriate caution. The overall evidence synthesis and certainty grading process is expected to be completed by the end of December 2026.

## Statistical analysis and software
All statistical analyses will be conducted using STATA V.18.0 , and Review Manager (RevMan) V.5.4 will be used for generating forest plots, funnel plots and other graphical outputs.

## Patient and public involvement
Patients and the public will not be involved in this research.

## Ethics and dissemination
Ethical approval was not required for this protocol. The results of the final review will be disseminated in a peer-reviewed journal.

## Discussion
This study aims to provide the first comprehensive assessment of the prevalence, risk factors and sources of heterogeneity of probable sarcopenia among older adults globally through systematic review and meta-analysis. Most of the existing studies focus on the established sarcopenia, and relatively little attention is paid to the ‘probable sarcopenia’ stage, which is an early sign of muscle strength decline. In addition, there is obvious fragmentation of previous evidence, with significant differences in geographical coverage, diagnostic criteria, measurement tools and population selection among different studies, leading to huge fluctuations in prevalence estimates and conflicting conclusions on risk factors. This situation seriously hinders the accurate assessment of the disease burden at this stage and makes the development of primary prevention strategies lack a solid evidence base. Therefore, a global comprehensive assessment plays a key role in achieving early intervention and reducing the overall disease burden of sarcopenia.
Compared with previous studies, the innovation of this research is reflected at multiple levels. First, by moving our focus to the critical window of intervention of probable sarcopenia, we aim to establish the first benchmark data for its global disease burden and fill the core gaps in the existing evidence system. Second, we no longer regard heterogeneity between studies as a mere statistical obstacle. Instead, through prespecified subgroup analyses and meta-regressions, we proactively quantify the impact of factors such as differing diagnostic criteria, measurement tools and geographical regions on prevalence rates. This transforms heterogeneity into information bearing both methodological and public health significance. This strategy not only enhances the interpretability of results but also provides an evidence base for future decisions concerning diagnostic framework selection and reporting standards. In addition, the systematic identification of modifiable risk and protective factors will directly point out the targets for precise prevention and health promotion at the community level. Overall, by establishing a standardised framework for evidence synthesis, this study will enhance the interpretability of current findings on probable sarcopenia and will provide a methodological reference for future population-based research and prevention strategies.
In order to ensure the robustness and reliability of the study results, this study adheres strictly to methodological norms during the design phase. The design is based on the JBI methodology and is reported in accordance with the PRISMA-P 2020 statement. The research questions are clearly defined based on the PECOS framework. Literature screening, data extraction and quality evaluation are conducted through a standardised, independent two-person process to ensure the transparency and reproducibility of the methods. Additionally, by removing language restrictions and implementing a translation protocol, we aim to minimise language and regional biases and enhance the global representativeness of the evidence.
Nonetheless, several potential limitations are anticipated. The study will be subject to inherent methodological heterogeneity and design limitations. The primary challenge will be the inherent variability across included studies in terms of diagnostic criteria, measurement tools and population characteristics, which is expected to lead to substantial heterogeneity. To address this, the study will apply stratified meta-analyses, meta-regression and sensitivity analyses based on diagnostic frameworks and geographic regions and will employ narrative synthesis where quantitative pooling is inappropriate, thereby systematically interpreting rather than simply eliminating heterogeneity. Second, most included studies are expected to be cross-sectional, so analyses of risk factors will identify associations rather than causal relationships. Despite this limitation, synthesising multivariable-adjusted associations and performing sensitivity analyses will partially mitigate this limitation and provide important insights for early intervention strategies and future longitudinal studies. Third, operational definitions of ‘probable sarcopenia’ vary across studies, primarily between the two major diagnostic frameworks (EWGSOP2 and AWGS2). To address this challenge, we prespecified and conducted separate meta-analyses for each framework.
At the level of the evidence base, there are potential representativeness and quality biases. Although an exhaustive search without language restrictions will be conducted, studies published in less accessible sources may still be missed. Consequently, results will primarily serve to characterise the distribution and patterns of available evidence rather than provide precise prevalence estimates for all regions. Moreover, given the potential variability in study quality, publication bias will be systematically addressed using JBI tools for quality appraisal and appropriate statistical methods. It is anticipated that, through these rigorous and transparent methodological approaches, the study will systematically identify and address limitations, thereby ensuring that its contribution to the global evidence base on probable sarcopenia remains valid and valuable.
