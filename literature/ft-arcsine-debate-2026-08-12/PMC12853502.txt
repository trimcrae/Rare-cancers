## Abstract

## Introduction
Hearing loss is a global health problem. It is not only an impairment of hearing but also has debilitating effects on the social, emotional and cognitive domains. It also imposes costs on a country’s healthcare sector, which must first be identified to prevent the prevalence of the impairment. In a low- and middle-income country like Nepal, there is no published systematic review on the prevalence of hearing loss. We aim to perform a systematic review and meta-analysis on the national prevalence of hearing loss in Nepal across all age groups.

## Methods and analysis
We will report our findings in line with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidelines. We will search the MEDLINE (PubMed) and Nepal Journals Online databases for publications in Nepali and English. Google Scholar will also be used to broaden the search scope. We will also search grey literature, like government reports. Studies that report the prevalence of hearing loss in Nepal will be included. Titles and abstracts of the studies will be independently screened by both reviewers (PB and SA) using Rayyan software. Full-text articles that pass the initial screening will also be independently reviewed by both reviewers. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion at all stages. If necessary, a third reviewer will be invited, and the original author will be contacted where appropriate (e.g., when data are missing in the original study). We will perform a narrative synthesis of the study results. Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic will be calculated to assess heterogeneity. I² values of 25%, 50% and 75% will be interpreted as indicating low, moderate and high heterogeneity, respectively. We will conduct a meta-analysis using a random-effects model, which accounts for both within-study and between-study variability. We will perform a sensitivity analysis to examine whether a single study disproportionately contributes to heterogeneity. Potential publication bias will be evaluated using both visual and statistical approaches (Funnel plot and Egger’s test). In addition, if funnel plot asymmetry is observed, trim-and-fill analysis will be conducted. Statistical tests will be performed in R (V.4.3.1) using RStudio (V.2023.06.1+524) with the metafor package for meta-analysis.

## Ethics and dissemination
Ethics approval is not required for this protocol. This review and meta-analysis are based entirely on secondary data; no primary data will be collected. Our findings will be useful to quantify the national prevalence of hearing impairment in Nepal. They may be useful for developing intervention policies and furthering research agendas for government agencies and hearing-health researchers alike. The results will be presented at conferences, published in peer-reviewed journals and shared with relevant policy-making bodies.

## STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY
The findings of this study will be reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidelines.
This systematic review will investigate Nepali and English-language publications and also explore grey literature, including government data not yet published in scholarly journals.
The number of prevalence studies conducted across different age ranges and settings, such as schools, communities and hospitals, may be limited in Nepal.
Due to the limitations in e-governance, the availability of unpublished government reports may be restricted.

## Introduction
 More than one in five people (1.57 billion) globally have hearing loss. It is projected to reach 2.5 billion by 2050. In addition to auditory deprivation and reduced quality of life, hearing loss is associated with cognitive decline and social isolation as direct consequences. There are also financial costs for countries, including healthcare expenditures, public awareness efforts and years lost to disability, including time spent in childcare and the restructuring of accessible spaces for people with hearing loss. If left untreated, children with hearing loss experience impaired language development. With distorted auditory input, they may also develop speech articulation difficulties. Early intervention mitigates the risk of such speech and language disorders. For adults, quality of life can be improved when hearing loss is detected, and an appropriate intervention strategy is implemented. However, in the Nepali context, there is a lack of prevalence data across age groups despite it being a significant public health concern. 
This systematic review aims to estimate the prevalence of hearing loss across different age groups at a national level in Nepal. It is based on studies that have estimated prevalence across different years, populations and settings. Therefore, this review provides a unified approach and data on the prevalence and severity of hearing loss across all age groups in Nepal. We not only review academic journals but also grey literature, particularly government reports. By taking this approach, this review helps practitioners, public health workers, organisations working in hearing care and policymakers in making evidence-based policies, plans and decisions.

## Objectives
Our primary aim is to review the epidemiological studies of hearing loss in Nepal to estimate and establish the prevalence of hearing loss in the Nepali population across all age groups. The objectives of this review are to:
Assess the degree (severity) and type of hearing loss in individuals of all age groups.
Assess any associated comorbidities.
Determine the factors associated with hearing loss, like locality (rural vs urban), sociodemographic variables (socioeconomic status, gender differences) and study setting (hospital, community, school, university).
It is noteworthy that the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2019 study lacked prevalence data from low-income countries such as Nepal. This review will therefore position Nepal’s hearing health alongside the findings from the GBD 2019 study and inform future GBD studies. Therefore, we will also align our age-group categorisation with that of the GBD 2019 study, using the same definition of hearing loss as in the GBD study.

## Methods and analysis

## Registration
This review protocol is registered with PROSPERO (International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews). Any future changes will be documented as amendments to the protocol in PROSPERO.

## Start and end date
The study is anticipated to begin on 01 February and conclude on 30 April 2026, including screening, selection, analysis and write-up. This is a feasible time period given the small number of expected studies.

## Patient and public involvement
This review will be based entirely on secondary data from published studies; there will be no patient or public involvement.

## Eligibility criteria
Study design: we will include cross-sectional studies and exclude non-cross-sectional studies, such as case reports and longitudinal studies (except baseline data), with only baseline point prevalence estimates from longitudinal studies extracted and included in the analysis.
Participants: we will include cross-sectional studies of the general population across all age groups (from newborns to older adults). We will exclude studies conducted on populations with specific diseases (e.g., middle ear disease like otitis media) and occupational hazards (e.g., traffic police, factory workers).
Setting: studies conducted in a hospital setting on patients with specific diseases, such as otitis media, will be excluded.
Language: articles published in English and reports published in Nepali and English will be considered. Nepali, the reviewers’ first language, will be reviewed without translation. Academic and scientific publications in other regional languages of Nepal (e.g., Newari) are uncommon and therefore will not be considered in this review.
Journals: we will include articles published in PubMed-indexed journals. Nepal-based journals indexed in NepJOL (Nepal Journals Online) and government surveys and reports will also be included if available.
Primary outcome: we will include studies that report the prevalence of hearing loss as period prevalence, point prevalence or other relevant epidemiological measures. Studies that do not clearly or explicitly report prevalence estimates will be excluded.
Duplicate publication: for articles with duplicate use of the same data, only the most recent publication will be included.

## Search strategy
We will search MEDLINE (via PubMed) for potentially relevant studies using the inclusion and exclusion criteria described above. Keywords will be searched for in the title and abstract or as MeSH terms using the following exact syntax:
(“Hearing Loss”[MeSH Terms] OR “hearing impairment”[Title/Abstract] OR “deafness”[Title/Abstract] OR “hearing loss”[Title/Abstract])
AND
(“Prevalence”[MeSH Terms] OR “prevalence”[Title/Abstract] OR “epidemiology”[Title/Abstract] OR “burden”[Title/Abstract] OR “frequency”[Title/Abstract])
AND
(“Nepal”[MeSH Terms] OR “Nepal”[Title/Abstract])
We will adapt the aforementioned search query for Google Scholar and inspect twice (Shariff et al reported that search on Google Scholar returns twice the number of papers that PubMed search returns.) as many papers as we find in PubMed, thereby expanding the search scope. However, other databases that require subscription fees will not be searched.
The following syntax will be used for a query on Google Scholar:
(intitle: “hearing loss” OR intitle: “hearing impairment” OR intitle: deafness) AND (prevalence OR epidemiology OR burden OR frequency) AND Nepal
To reduce publication bias and ensure comprehensiveness, we will also search grey literature. We will manually search the websites of various government agencies in Nepal (e.g., the Ministry of Health and Population and the Central Bureau of Statistics) to identify reports on health and disease prevalence. If no reports are available online, we will email the Information Officers at the respective ministries and bureaus and request that they provide these reports. If no response is received within 2 weeks, the documents will be considered inaccessible.
We will also expand our search to a Nepal-specific repository—NepJOL.
The project will begin in early February 2026. Studies published till the end of January will be included in this review.

## Primary outcome
The primary outcome of this review is the prevalence of hearing loss in Nepal, estimated across all included studies. Hearing loss will be defined in accordance with the GBD 2019 methodology as a pure-tone average (PTA) >20 dB HL in the better-hearing ear, calculated across standard audiometric frequencies (0.5, 1, 2 and 4 kHz). Prevalence estimates will be reported overall and, where data permit, stratified by GBD-aligned age groups to enable direct comparison with global estimates and to serve as a data source for future GBD studies.

## Secondary outcome
Secondary outcomes will include the severity of hearing loss (mild, moderate, moderately severe, severe and profound) classified using GBD 2019 study, and type of hearing loss (conductive, sensorineural or mixed), where reported, along with comorbidities, age distribution, locality (urban vs rural), sociodemographic characteristics (e.g., socioeconomic status and gender) and associations between hearing loss prevalence and study setting (hospital, community, school or university). Where data permit, exploratory analyses will additionally describe minimal hearing loss (PTA: 15–25 dB HL) and extended high-frequency hearing loss (>20 dB HL above 8 kHz) to characterise early-stage hearing loss, which is increasingly recognised as clinically meaningful; these will be reported separately and not pooled with GBD-aligned prevalence estimates. Diagnostic criteria will be extracted and coded, and subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be conducted to account for variability in diagnostic criteria across studies.

## Selection process
All records retrieved from the database and grey literature searches will be imported into Rayyan, a systematic review software, and duplicates will be removed prior to screening. Title and abstract screening will be conducted independently by two reviewers (PB and SA) against the predefined eligibility criteria (inclusion and exclusion criteria). Rayyan’s blinding feature will be used during this phase to minimise selection bias. Studies deemed potentially eligible will undergo full-text screening, which will again be performed independently by both reviewers (PB and SA). Rayyan will be used to identify and track conflicts at each screening stage.
Disagreements at any stage (title/abstract or full text) will first be resolved through discussion and consensus between the two reviewers. If consensus cannot be reached, a third independent reviewer with expertise in audiology and/or public health will adjudicate. When necessary (e.g., when eligibility is unclear or information is missing), the original study authors will be contacted for clarification. Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be systematically documented and reported. The study selection process will be summarised using a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols flow diagram.

## Data collection process
The first reviewer (PB) will extract relevant data from each study and enter it into a table in a Google Sheet shared among the reviewers. The data will include the study’s first author’s name, year of data collection, publication year, prevalence of hearing loss, participant characteristics (mean age, age range, male to female ratio, socioeconomic status), study setting (hospital, community, school, university), sample size, locality (rural, urban), tests administered to assess hearing loss, type and severity of hearing loss, and any reported comorbidities. A data dictionary defining all extracted variables and coding decisions will be included in the appendix to ensure reproducibility. The second reviewer (SA) will review the extracted information for discrepancies or errors. Should any disagreement arise between the reviewers, a consensus will be reached through discussion. We will first pilot this process with four papers (two earliest and two most recent by publication date) to ensure consistency, clarity and efficiency. If any issues are identified, we will refine the process and document them with appropriate justification. We will contact the original author if there is missing data. If there is no response from the authors or no data are available, we will use median imputation. Missing numeric values will be imputed using median values from comparable studies. Comparable studies are those conducted on a similar population (within ±5 years of age) using the same methods, that is, in the same setting (school, community or hospital) and following the same diagnostic criteria.

## Data management
We will store data both locally and in the cloud. A copy of the articles, data and the analysis script/code will be stored locally on a hard drive at the NCCS office on project completion. Another copy will be stored on Google Drive and shared between the two reviewers. A third copy of the data and analysis script will be stored on a GitHub repository, and version control will be maintained. We will archive the repository and make it public at the end of the project.

## Risk of bias (quality) assessment
We will adapt Joanna Briggs Institute’s Checklist for Prevalence Studies and Risk of Bias Tool for Prevalence Studies to evaluate all included studies for bias and quality. Two reviewers will independently assess the quality and bias. Inter-rater agreement will be calculated using Cohen’s Kappa.

## Strategy for data synthesis and analysis
We will perform a narrative or descriptive synthesis of the included studies. The relevant information will be summarised and presented in text and tables. We will conduct a meta-analysis using a random-effects model, which accounts for both within-study and between-study variability and is appropriate when clinical or methodological heterogeneity is expected. Statistical heterogeneity will be assessed using Cochran’s Q test (p<0.10) and the I² statistic, with values of approximately 25%, 50% and 75% interpreted as low, moderate and high heterogeneity, respectively. If studies use different diagnostic criteria and there are at least 10 studies per criterion, diagnostic criteria will also be included as a moderator in the random-effects model (a commonly cited rule of thumb ); subgroup analyses will be conducted when fewer studies are available.
For pooled analyses, prevalence proportions will be stabilised using the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation, and pooled prevalence estimates will be calculated using a random-effects model. Pooled estimates will be back-transformed to the original proportion scale for interpretation. A priori subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore sources of heterogeneity across studies, including age (young vs old), sex, locality (urban vs rural), study setting (community, school, hospital), type of hearing loss (conductive, sensorineural, mixed), degree of hearing loss (e.g., mild vs moderate) and diagnostic criteria used to define hearing loss.
If substantial heterogeneity is observed (I²≥75%), subgroup analyses will be emphasised to identify potential sources of variability. Forest plots will be generated to display individual study estimates and pooled prevalence estimates with 95% CIs. We will perform a sensitivity analysis to assess whether a single study disproportionately contributes to heterogeneity by iteratively excluding it one at a time, following Cochrane guidelines. Potential publication bias will be evaluated using both visual and statistical approaches. Funnel plots will be used to assess the symmetry of effect size distributions, and Egger’s regression test will be applied to test for funnel plot asymmetry formally. A p value below 0.05 will be considered indicative of potential publication bias. In accordance with Cochrane guidelines, assessment of publication bias will be conducted only when more than 10 studies are available. In addition, if funnel plot asymmetry is observed, we will apply the trim-and-fill method to estimate the potential influence of small-study effects on the pooled prevalence estimates. 
All statistical analyses will be conducted in R (V.4.3.1) using RStudio (V.2023.06.1+524). Meta-analyses will be conducted using the metafor package, which allows computation of standardised effect sizes, modelling of between-study heterogeneity and generation of forest and funnel plots.

## Limitations of the study
Despite efforts to collect data from grey literature, including government agencies, data availability remains limited because Nepal’s government websites are frequently not updated and responses to information requests are delayed. It may result in incomplete data collection from the grey literature. We anticipate heterogeneity among studies, particularly in their settings (hospital vs community) and target populations (children vs adults), which may limit the comparability of the findings. In addition, there may be only a small number of studies in the subgroup analyses, thereby limiting their statistical power. Furthermore, excluding hospital-based studies involving clinical populations (e.g., those with middle ear diseases) may introduce selection bias, potentially underestimating or overestimating the prevalence of hearing loss in the general population. Finally, if the available literature is disproportionately dominated by one locality, for example, if most studies are conducted in urban areas, then it poses a challenge to the generalisability of our findings to rural and semi-urban areas.

This study will use published data. No ethics approval is necessary.
We plan to present the findings of this review and meta-analysis at relevant national and international conferences. Written manuscripts will be submitted to international peer-reviewed journals for publication. We will also prepare a policy report based on the project’s findings and share it with the relevant government authorities and the WHO.
