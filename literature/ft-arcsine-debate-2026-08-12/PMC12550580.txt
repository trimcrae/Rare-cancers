## Abstract

## Background
Tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality, yet it remains an undertreated entity. Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) has emerged as a promising therapeutic option, with multiple novel systems demonstrating early safety and efficacy. The objective of this study is to systematically review and synthesize the clinical outcomes of transcatheter heart valve systems.

## Methods
A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane databases was conducted from database inception through March 31, 2025. Eligible studies included adult patients undergoing TTVR for severe or torrential TR, reporting at least one clinical outcome. Pooled event rates were calculated using a random-effects model. Subgroup analyses were performed by valve type, and meta-regression explored potential sources of heterogeneity.

## Results
Twenty studies comprising 1017 patients were included. The mean age was 73 ± 9 years, and 71.2% were female. The weighted in-hospital and 30-day mortality were 1.37 and 2.49%, respectively. New permanent pacemaker implantation occurred in 7.98%, with highest rates observed in EVOQUE recipients (11.64%). Only 3% of patients had greater than or equal to severe TR at follow-up, and only 12% remained in New York Heart Association functional class III/IV. Subgroup analysis revealed lower in-hospital and 30-day mortality with EVOQUE but higher permanent pacemaker implantation compared to other valves.

## Conclusions
TTVR is a feasible and safe intervention for patients with severe TR, providing significant functional improvement and TR reduction. Robust, longer-term randomized controlled trials with standardized outcome reporting and clinical endpoints are urgently needed to define durable clinical benefit and guide optimal device selection for patients with severe TR.

Keywords: Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement, Tricuspid regurgitation, Tricuspid valve outcome

## Highlights
•This meta-analysis pooled data from 20 studies including 1017 patients undergoing transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) across 8 different device platforms.
•TTVR was associated with low in-hospital (1.37%) and 30-day (2.49%) mortality rates, with significant reduction in tricuspid regurgitation and improvement in New York Heart Association functional class and Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire-12 scores.
•Device-specific differences were observed: the EVOQUE valve showed excellent procedural safety but a higher rate of new pacemaker implantation, likely due to its subannular anchoring design.
•Exploratory analyses highlighted that older age was associated with an increased pacemaker risk, and female sex was associated with persistent symptoms, suggesting patient-level factors influence post-TTVR outcomes.

## Introduction
Tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is a significant source of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, often underdiagnosed and undertreated in its early stages. Severe TR leads to substantial hemodynamic compromise—characterized by right-sided volume overload, reduced forward cardiac output, and systemic venous congestion—resulting in decreased cardiac reserve, impaired functional status, frequent hospitalizations, and progressive dysfunction of vital organs such as the liver and kidneys. , 
Historically, surgical management of TR has been underutilized due to high perioperative risks and the frequent presence of comorbidities in affected patients. As a result, many patients have been managed conservatively, despite accumulating evidence demonstrating poor long-term outcomes associated with untreated severe TR. In recent years, the emergence of transcatheter therapies has revolutionized the management of valvular heart disease, and TR has now become a target of intense clinical and technological innovation.
The Food and Drug Administration recently approved the EVOQUE transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) system (Edwards Lifesciences), marking a pivotal milestone in the treatment landscape of TR. In addition to EVOQUE, several novel TTVR platforms have emerged, demonstrating encouraging results in early feasibility and compassionate use studies. These include the LuX-valve (Ningbo Jenscare Biotechnology, Ningbo, China), the NaviGate valve (NaviGate Cardiac Structures Inc, Lake Forest, California), the MonarQ valve (InQB8 Medical Technologies, Suzhou, China), the Topaz valve (MicroPort CardioFlow Medtech, Shanghai, China), among others. , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , These devices vary in design, anchoring mechanisms, and delivery systems, yet provide a safe and effective transcatheter solution to an otherwise undertreated disease.
Given the growing number of available devices and the evolving body of clinical evidence, there is a need to synthesize current outcomes data to guide future research and inform clinical decision-making. In this meta-analysis, we aim to comprehensively pool and analyze the existing literature on outcomes following TTVR with various platforms, focusing on procedural success, hemodynamic improvement, safety, and survival.

## Methods
This study was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis guidelines. , Institutional review board approval was not required as the study was entirely based on previously conducted studies.

## Search Strategy
We conducted a systematic review by searching the MEDLINE/PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane databases for studies using the keywords [“tricuspid”] and [“transcatheter valve replacement”]. Detailed search strategies for each database and the data extraction template are provided in the . The search covered publications from the inception of each database till the end of March 2025. The reference lists of all included studies were reviewed to identify eligible studies.

## Eligibility Criteria
The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) Studies that had patients undergoing TTVR for the treatment of severe, torrential, or massive TR, (2) studies including adult patients (≥18 years), (3) studies with a minimum follow-up duration of hospital discharge or 30 days, (4) studies that reported at least one clinical outcome, including residual TR ≥ 3+, New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III/IV, in-hospital mortality, 30-day mortality, or new permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation, and (5) study designs including prospective or retrospective observational cohorts, case series, or post hoc analyses of clinical registries.
The studies were excluded if (1) they reported outcomes for surgical tricuspid valve repair or replacement, (2) they reported nontricuspid valve–targeted therapies, (3) they included mixed valvular interventions (combined mitral and tricuspid) unless separate outcome data for TTVR is available, (4) the study reported <5 patients, and (5) they did not report any outcome of interest.

## Data Extraction and Outcomes
Two authors independently screened studies for inclusion, with discrepancies resolved by consensus. Datapoints were collected based on a predefined extraction template, including first author, year of publication, study design, patient numbers, type of valve used, baseline characteristics (e.g., age, sex, comorbidities, preprocedural TR severity, and NYHA functional class), follow-up duration, and the outcomes of interest (residual TR severity >3+, NYHA functional class III-IV, in-hospital mortality, 30-day mortality, and incidence of new PPM placement).
Primary endpoints were 30-day mortality and TR severity >3+ at follow-up. Secondary endpoints included in-hospital mortality, new PPM, NYHA III/IV functional class, changes in tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), right ventricular fractional area change (RV-FAC), Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-12), major bleeding, and thrombotic events at follow-up.

## Quality Assessment
The risk of bias for included studies was assessed using the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized studies of Interventions tool. This tool evaluates the methodological quality and potential bias across domains including confounding, selection of participants, classification of interventions, deviation from intended interventions, missing data, measurement of outcomes, and selective reporting. Two reviewers independently conducted the assessment based on the predefined criteria, with discrepancies resolved through discussion.

## Statistical Analysis
For the single-arm design, proportions were pooled using a random effects model with 95% CIs. To address for variance instability associated with pooling proportions, particularly in presence of extreme event rates (0% or 100%), the Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation was applied. Pooled estimates were obtained under a random-effects model and subsequently back transformed to the original proportion scale for interpretation. For dichotomous outcomes, risk ratios and 95% CIs were calculated using the Mantel–Haenszel random-effects model, and a p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed with the I2 statistic, with an I2 value greater than 50% considered indicative for substantial heterogeneity.
Subgroup analyses were conducted based on the valve type to provide device-specific outcome estimates. Differences between valve subgroups were assessed using a chi-squared test for subgroup differences (Q-test), with statistical significance defined as a two-sided p value <0.05. A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate the influence of individual studies on the pooled effect size and assess for potential sources of heterogeneity. For each outcome, a funnel plot was generated to visually assess the possibility of publication bias. In addition, random-effects meta-regression analyses were conducted to explore the impact of potential confounding variables, that is, mean age, proportion of female sex, and history of left sided heart surgery. All statistical analysis was conducted using R software (version 4.4.2; R Core Team, 2014; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The “meta”, “dmetar”, and “metafor” R packages were used for meta-analysis.

## Results
Following a comprehensive search that initially identified 115 studies, we screened and excluded those lacking clinical outcome data, resulting in 35 studies for full-text review. After excluding 15 case reports, 20 eligible studies were included in this meta-analysis, encompassing a total of 1017 patients who underwent TTVR ( ). All included studies were nonrandomized observational studies. The included studies evaluated multiple TTVR systems: 7 studies on the LuX-valve, 3 on the LuX-Valve Plus, 5 on the EVOQUE valve, and 1 study each on the CardioValve, NaviGate, MonarQ, Topaz, and Trisol transcatheter heart valve (THV) systems. , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , The weighted mean age was 73 ± 9 years, and 71.2% (n = 716) of patients were female. Atrial fibrillation was reported in 87.8% (n = 816) of patients. The weighted mean left ventricular ejection fraction was 56% ± 16%. At baseline, 97.0% (n = 977) of patients had TR graded as greater than or equal to severe and 85.6% (n = 871) were classified as NYHA functional class III or IV ( ).
 

## Mortality Outcomes
The weighted in-hospital mortality rate was 1.37% (95% CI: 0.18%–3.26%, I2 = 16.4%). Among valve platforms, the LuX-valve (transapical approach) demonstrated the highest in-hospital mortality at 3.43% (95% CI: 0.37%–8.34%), followed by the LuX Plus at 1.49% (95% CI: 0.00%–5.81%), and the EVOQUE platform at 0% (95% CI: 0%–1.17%). Notably, the NaviGate platform reported an in-hospital mortality rate of 10%; however, this result was derived from a compassionate use study ( and ).
 The pooled 30-day mortality rate across platforms was 2.49% (95% CI: 0.67%–5.05%, I2 = 45.9%). The highest 30-day mortality was observed with the NaviGate platform (22%), followed by CardioValve (10%), Topaz (9%), LuX (3.77%), EVOQUE (1.66%), and LuX Plus (1.49%) ( and ). Funnel plot analysis indicated a consistent effect across studies without major asymmetry ( ).
 

## New Permanent Pacemaker Implantation
The incidence of new PPM implantation varied across devices. The pooled new PPM rate was 3.99% (95% CI: 1.44%–7.37%, I2 = 68.5%). The highest incidence was observed with the EVOQUE valve at 11.64% (95% CI: 8.69%–14.89%), followed by the LuX Plus at 4.75% (95% CI: 0.21%–12.78%) and the LuX valve at 0.00% (95% CI: 0.00%–0.70%) ( and ). Subgroup analysis revealed a significant difference between platforms (p < 0.001). In the compassionate use studies, CardioValve and Trisol each reported a PPM rate of 10%, Topaz 9.09%, and NaviGate 6.67%. Among the 7 patients who received the MonarQ valve, no PPM implantation was required during follow-up. Funnel plots revealed no significant asymmetry, suggesting a low risk of publication bias ( ).
 

## Tricuspid Regurgitation at Follow-Up
Significant reductions in TR severity were reported across all platforms. By the last follow-up, only 3% of patients (95% CI: 2%–5%, I2 = 0.0%) had residual TR graded as greater than or equal to severe. No statistically significant differences were noted between different TTVR devices ( and ).
 

## NYHA Functional Class and KCCQ-12 Improvement
All included studies demonstrated significant improvement in NYHA functional class by the last follow-up. The pooled incidence of NYHA III/IV after TTVR was 12.7% (95% CI: 8.57%–17.15%, I2 = 61.1%). Improvements from NYHA class III/IV to I/II were observed across devices, with comparable rates: EVOQUE (14.25%), LuX (14.2%), LuX Plus (11.25%), CardioValve (15%), and NaviGate (27.78%). No significant difference between subgroups was observed (p = 0.44) ( and ). KCCQ-12 also showed significant improvement across pooled analysis (22 points, 95% CI: [15-29], I2 = 98.6%) ( and )
 

## Bleeding and Thrombotic Events
Pooled estimate of thrombotic and bleeding events was 1.66% (95% CI: 0.00-4.90, I2 = 67.3%) and 8.93% (95% CI: 4.23-14.81, I2 = 79.5%), respectively. The highest rate of thrombotic events was noted with the EVOQUE valve (6.77% [95% CI: 0.04-20.67]) with no statistical differences when comparing to other THV systems. When comparing bleeding events, there were significant statistical difference with the highest bleeding events noted with the NaviGate and EVOQUE THV systems ( and and ).
 

## RV Function
Pooled estimate for changes in RV-FAC and TAPSE showed a change in RV-FAC by -1.6% (95% CI: −5.06-1.88, I2 = 99.6%) and −0.8 cm (95% CI: −2.6-1.0, I2 = 98.5%), respectively. Patients undergoing TTVR with the EVOQUE experienced the most significant reduction in RV-FAC and TAPSE compared to other THV systems (p < 0.001 and p = 0.009, respectively) ( and and ).
 

## Sensitivity Analysis
Meta-regression analyses identified that previous left heart surgery was associated with a lower incidence of new PPM implantation (β = −0.3628; 95% CI: −0.6758 to −0.0497; p = 0.0231), whereas a higher proportion of female patients was associated with increased rates of NYHA class III/IV symptoms (β = 0.4509; 95% CI: 0.0443 to 0.8575; p = 0.0297). Mean age was significantly associated with higher PPM implantation rates (β = 0.0203; 95% CI: 0.0145 to 0.0261; p < 0.0001), fully explaining between-study heterogeneity. No significant associations were found between these covariates and 30-day or in-hospital mortality ( ).
Leave-one-out analyses showed that the omission of Lu et al reduced heterogeneity for in-hospital mortality (I2 from 16.4 to 0%), whereas the omission of other studies did not result in any notable changes to the pooled estimates or heterogeneity for any other outcome ( ).

## Discussion
The present meta-analysis demonstrates that TTVR is associated with high procedural success and meaningful clinical improvement in patients with severe TR. Across 20 included studies encompassing 1017 patients, the weighted in-hospital mortality was low at 1.37% (95% CI: 0.18%–3.26%) and the 30-day mortality remained favorable at 2.49% (95% CI: 0.67%–5.05%). New PPM implantation occurred in 7.98% (95% CI: 5.38%–11.00%) of patients, although rates varied depending on valve platform and anchoring mechanisms. At follow-up, significant clinical improvement was observed, with only 12.7% of patients remaining in NYHA class III/IV, along with a significant increase in KCCQ scores and a reduction in RV-FAC and TAPSE. In addition, residual TR severity was markedly reduced, with only 3% (95% CI: 2%–5%) of patients exhibiting greater than or equal to severe TR.
The Food and Drug Administration approved the EVOQUE TTVR system based on promising early results from the TRISCEND II trial's Breakthrough Pathway Cohort, which showed improvements in patient-reported outcomes like KCCQ scores and 6-minute walk distance. However, the trial did not assess hard endpoints such as mortality or hospitalizations, prompting concerns about the long-term clinical relevance of these early benefits. As a result, Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services required extended outcome reporting under a Coverage with Evidence Development framework. This reflects broader challenges in defining success for TTVR, including limited understanding of TR’s natural history in less severe cases, the complex symptom profile driven by venous congestion, and the questionable validity of commonly used subjective outcomes in this patient population.
In this meta-analysis, mortality outcomes differed notably based on device design and access route. The weighted in-hospital mortality across platforms was 1.37% (95% CI: 0.18%–3.26%), but varied by system. The LuX valve demonstrated the highest in-hospital mortality compared to THV systems at 3.43% (95% CI: 0.37%–8.34%), likely influenced by its transapical surgical approach, which is inherently more invasive. In contrast, the LuX-Plus, a transjugular evolution of the system, demonstrated a lower in-hospital mortality of 1.49% (95% CI: 0.00%–5.81%). The EVOQUE platform, delivered via transfemoral or transjugular access, reported a weighted in-hospital mortality of 0% (95% CI: 0%–1.17%), reflecting the advantages of a fully percutaneous approach, greater operator experience, and iterative device improvements. , NaviGate, an early-generation platform using a larger transatrial delivery, showed a high in-hospital mortality of 10%, likely related to patient complexity and early feasibility settings. 
At 30 days, the overall weighted mortality was 2.49% (95% CI: 0.67%–5.05%), with the highest rates observed for NaviGate (22%) and CardioValve (10%), compared to 1.66% for EVOQUE and 1.49% for LuX-Plus. These findings underscore the importance of less invasive access strategies and newer-generation designs in improving early survival after TTVR.
PPM implantation also varied across systems. EVOQUE had the highest rate of new PPM implantation at 11.64% (95% CI: 8.69%–14.89%). This likely reflects the EVOQUE system’s subannular anchoring, which, while providing stability, increases the risk of atrioventricular conduction disturbance. In contrast, the LuX-Plus demonstrated a lower PPM rate of 4.75% (95% CI: 0.21%–12.78%), and the original LuX valve had a near 0% PPM rate, likely owing to its septal anchoring mechanism which minimizes pressure on the conduction system. Intermediate PPM rates were observed for CardioValve, Topaz, and Trisol, although interpretation remains limited by small sample sizes and early study designs.
The pooled thrombotic event rate following TTVR was 1.66% (95% CI: 0.01–4.90), whereas bleeding events occurred at a higher rate of 8.93% (95% CI: 4.23–14.81). These findings demonstrate a generally low risk of thrombosis across TTVR platforms. However, bleeding remains a significant concern due to the advanced disease, frailty, and coagulopathy common in this patient population. , 
Most patients undergoing TTVR have atrial fibrillation and are on chronic anticoagulation. Managing anticoagulation around the procedure is complex, as stopping it may reduce bleeding risk but increase the risk of hypo-attenuated leaflet thickening, valve thrombosis, and strokes, whereas early resumption may heighten bleeding complications. Individualized strategies balancing these risks are essential but still evolving.
The highest thrombotic rate was seen with the EVOQUE valve at 6.77%, although differences between devices were not statistically significant, possibly reflecting early device design and patients’ comorbidities. , Bleeding was notably higher with the NaviGate and EVOQUE systems, likely due to larger transatrial and transfemoral access routes and more complex implantation techniques, which may contribute to increased procedural bleeding. , , 
Functional improvement and TR reduction were consistently observed across all TTVR platforms. At follow-up, only 3% of patients had residual TR graded as severe or greater (95% CI: 2%–5%), with no significant differences between valve types. These findings confirm that orthotopic TTVR achieves effective and durable TR reduction, even in patients with complex anatomy and advanced right heart remodeling. Correspondingly, NYHA functional class shifted toward class I/II in most patients, and KCCQ-12 scores improved significantly, reinforcing the link between TR reduction, functional status, and quality of life. , 
Changes in RV function after TTVR show a small but statistically nonsignificant decline. The mean change in RV-FAC was –1.6% (95% CI: –5.06 to 1.88), and the mean change in TAPSE was –0.8 cm (95% CI: –2.6 to 1.0), with significant heterogeneity across studies (I2 > 98%). The most pronounced reduction in RV function was observed with the EVOQUE valve, which was statistically significant compared to other THV systems. This phenomenon is well documented in transcatheter tricuspid interventions. , Chronic severe TR often leads to pseudo-preserved RV function due to volume unloading, but following valve implantation, the acute increase in afterload unmasks subclinical RV dysfunction, resulting in an apparent decrease in FAC and TAPSE. , , The EVOQUE valve’s larger frame and complete leaflet coaptation may lead to a more complete elimination of TR, causing a greater increase in RV afterload and thus a more notable decline in RV function. , , , , Clinically, despite this imaging-based decline, patients often report functional improvement as reflected in NYHA class and KCCQ scores, suggesting that RV remodeling and adaptation continue to evolve over time.
In exploratory meta-regression analyses, history of previous left heart surgery was associated with a lower incidence of new PPM implantation. This finding may reflect a selection of patients with already remodeled conduction systems less susceptible to additional injury, or more cautious procedural techniques adopted by operators in surgically altered anatomies. In contrast, mean age was significantly associated with higher rates of new PPM implantation, fully accounting for the observed between-study heterogeneity. This likely reflects the intrinsic vulnerability of the conduction system in elderly patients, who have a higher baseline risk of atrioventricular conduction abnormalities even before device implantation. Furthermore, a higher proportion of female patients was associated with increased rates of persistent NYHA class III/IV symptoms at follow-up. This may be partially explained by known sex-based differences in cardiac remodeling and RV-pulmonary vascular coupling, which could limit full symptomatic recovery even after technically successful valve implantation. These findings underscore the need for individualized patient selection and highlight the importance of considering baseline conduction status and demographic factors when anticipating recovery trajectories after TTVR.

## Limitations
This meta-analysis has several important limitations. Most included studies were small, nonrandomized, and conducted in early feasibility or compassionate use settings, which introduces selection bias and limits generalizability. Considerable heterogeneity was present across studies in terms of patient populations, device platforms, delivery routes, procedural techniques, and outcome definitions, which complicates cross-platform comparisons and may contribute to unmeasured confounding. In addition, follow-up was short in most reports, often limited to 30 days, restricting our ability to assess long-term device durability, RV remodeling, and late complications such as structural valve degeneration or thrombosis.
Although our analysis stratified results by device, the sample sizes for newer platforms such as Trisol, Topaz, and CardioValve were small, yielding imprecise estimates with wide CIs. Furthermore, although formal assessments suggested minimal publication bias, the possibility of selective outcome reporting remains—particularly given the reliance on registry data and the underreporting of unsuccessful or complicated cases outside of clinical trials. The rapid pace of device evolution and procedural refinement also means that early-generation outcomes may not reflect current or future performance.
Finally, this field continues to face major challenges in endpoint definition. The heavy reliance on patient-reported outcomes and surrogate measures, combined with a lack of standardized, validated endpoints for TR, complicates the interpretation of clinical benefit. As a result, our findings should be considered hypothesis-generating. Robust, longer-term randomized controlled trials with standardized outcome reporting and mechanistic endpoints are urgently needed to define durable clinical benefit and guide optimal device selection for patients with severe TR.

## Conclusion
Overall, the results underscore that TTVR provides substantial clinical benefits by achieving effective TR elimination and symptomatic improvement with acceptable safety profiles. Nevertheless, variations in mortality and PPM rates between valve platforms highlight the importance of individualized device selection, considering anatomical factors, comorbidities, and procedural risk. As second-generation systems continue to evolve and commercial experience expands, long-term outcomes regarding valve durability, RV function, and lifetime management strategies will be critical areas for future research.
