## Abstract

## Background
Local injection of darvadstrocel, a suspension of expanded adipose‐derived allogenic mesenchymal stem cells, has been used for treatment‐refractory perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease (CD).

## Objective
This study aimed to investigate efficacy and safety of darvadstrocel for complex perianal fistulas in CD.

## Methods
A systematic search was conducted through April 2024 in relevant databases for observational studies evaluating darvadstrocel. A random‐effects meta‐analysis model was used to calculate the pooled effect sizes (proportions or incidence rates [IRs]) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of effectiveness and safety outcomes. The risk of bias was evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Tool. The I
2 value assessed heterogeneity. Sensitivity and subgroup analyses were also conducted.

## Results
Twelve studies were included with 595 patients. The pooled rate of patients achieving clinical remission, defined as fistula healing, was 68.1% at month 6 (95% CI 63.4–72.7) and 77.2% (95% CI 70.1–83.8) at month 12. Combined remission, defined as clinical remission and absence of collections >2 cm confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging, was reported in 60.6% and in 69.7% of patients at months 6 and 12, respectively. The rate of patients with treatment failure, defined as no clinical remission at the last follow‐up (mean 18.7 months; SD 9.9), was 34.5%. Failure rate was independent of follow‐up time (p = 0.85). For effectiveness outcomes, between‐study heterogeneity was negligible. Subgroup analysis indicated that none of the covariates modified the treatment effect. Pooled IRs per 100 patient‐years of adverse events (AE), serious AEs, perianal abscesses, and reoperations were 19.6, 3.2, 16.9 and 7.1, respectively.

## Conclusion
Evidence from observational studies supports the efficacy and safety of darvadstrocel for complex perianal fistulas in CD. Studies have reported high fistula healing rates that can be sustained long‐term in most patients, with negligible between‐study heterogeneity, as well as a favorable safety profile.

## DISCUSSION
Mesenchymal stem cells have potent anti‐inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, and the delivery of stem cells directly to perianal fistula tracts can increase cell numbers locally to promote fistula healing. The present study is the most comprehensive systematic review and meta‐analysis of observational studies evaluating the outcomes of darvadstrocel (a suspension of ASCs) for complex perianal fistulas in patients with CD. The meta‐analysis performed confirmed the consistent effectiveness and safety of locally administered darvadstrocel for perianal fistulizing CD and provided a broader perspective that may enable patients and clinicians to make more informed treatment decisions.
The patient populations of the studies included in the review had a long duration of CD and were highly refractory to treatments. Most patients did not have fistula healing despite combined treatment with an anti‐TNF and conservative surgery, which is the standard of care for perianal fistulizing CD. This is in line with the characteristics of patients included in the ADMIRE‐CD trial with a duration of CD of 12 years and with 78% of patients exposed to anti‐TNF. In this treatment refractory population, the studies included in the review showed that darvadstrocel, a suspension of allogeneic ASCs administered as a single local injection, has a high short‐term efficacy that can be sustained over the long term in most patients. The pooled clinical remission rate at month six indicated that more than two‐thirds of patients achieved fistula healing at this time point. Fistula healing rates further increased over time with more than 3 in 4 patients achieving fistula healing at month 12. In the ACCENT II clinical trial, infliximab induction followed by maintenance therapy to patients who responded to induction showed that only 23% of patients (i.e., 36% of patients who responded to induction [64%]) had fistula healing at week 54. However, since the studies in the review adopted an add‐on design in which baseline treatments such as biologics and immunosuppressants were continued, the reported efficacy results of darvadstrocel for perianal fistulas should be considered as obtained in combination with immunomodulatory therapy.
Fistula closure was confirmed in most patients within a short period of time (mean of 6 weeks), which was expected given that the surgical cell delivery technique involves closure of the internal fistula opening. Remarkably, fistula healing rates were not associated with the length of follow‐up of the studies, indicating that when fistula healing is achieved, it is maintained over time. This is in line with the results of the retrospective chart review study (INSPECT) conducted in patients included in the ADMIRE‐CD trial, which showed that after a follow‐up of up to 156 weeks darvadstrocel can lead to sustained fistula healing. The pooled combined remission (defined as clinical remission and absence of collections >2 cm confirmed by MRI) rates at months 6 and 12 in this meta‐analysis are somewhat higher than those reported in the darvadstrocel arm of the ADMIRE‐CD trial, which uses a similar definition of combined remission, at these time points. , In observational studies, physicians had greater freedom to escalate the dose of biologics or to use concomitant treatments that are prohibited in trials, which may partly explain the higher clinical remission rates observed in the real‐world setting.
An appreciable degree of between‐study heterogeneity is common in meta‐analyses of real‐life studies, which is to be expected given that the populations, definitions, and methods used often vary from study to study. However, fistula healing rates at month 6 ranged over a very short interval from 57.1% (exactly the clinical remission rate reported in the darvadstrocel arm of the ADMIRE‐CD study) to 69.7%. Similarly, the combined remission rate at month 6 ranged from 50% (equal to the combined remission rate reported in the darvadstrocel arm of the ADMIRE‐CD study) to 60%. One explanation for this extreme between‐study homogeneity is that, although the studies were conducted in a clinical setting, in almost all cases the minimally invasive surgical technique described in the ADMIRE‐CD study was followed, and that the amount of cells injected and their distribution were performed according to the manufacturers' instructions.
Recently reported results from the ADMIRE‐CD II RCT (a replica of ADMIRE‐CD study), which showed non‐significant but quantitative differences in the primary endpoint (combined remission at week 24) for darvadstrocel versus control, raise questions about the efficacy of the treatment. The placebo combined remission rate at 24 weeks (46.3%) in ADMIRE‐CD II was higher than expected, and this may be attributed to the minimally invasive surgical techniques employed before administration of placebo, including vigorous fistula tract curettage and closure of internal openings, as well as the permitted use of ongoing medical therapies. Given the differences in efficacy results from the ADMIRE‐CD RCTs, we believe that the consistently high clinical and combined remission rates reported in the observational studies included in this meta‐analysis may help therapeutic decision making. However, none of the observational studies included in our review had a control group evaluating the outcomes of curettage/closure alone, and they cannot replace the efficacy outcomes reported in RCTs.
In the present review, darvadstrocel showed an acceptable safety profile, consistent with the safety profiles from ADMIRE‐CD RCT reporting at week 52 similar rates of AEs and SAEs for darvadstrocel versus control and reassured in the extended follow‐up through 104 weeks showing no new safety signals. As in the darvadstrocel development program, the most common treatment emergent AE was perianal abscesses, which for some authors could be considered as a treatment failure. Complications often associated with surgery, such as fecal incontinence, have not been reported. There is a concern regarding tumorigenicity and ectopic tissue formation that might occur with long‐term stem cell therapy. There were no cases of treatment‐related tumor development in the studies included in the review. These findings are in accordance with previous studies in which local injections of mesenchymal stem cells (autologous or allogeneic) were reported to have a favorable safety profile. A recent systematic review including both RCTs and observational studies reassures the efficacy and safety of darvadstrocel in patients with complex perianal fistulizing CD. This review only includes 3 of the 12 studies analyzed in our meta‐analysis and was limited to reporting descriptive results.
The strengths of the present review process include the assessment of eligible studies for causes and heterogeneity and risk of bias using gold‐standard methods. Importantly, the very small heterogeneity between studies and the narrow margins in the fistula healing rates of all studies included in this meta‐analysis prevent the use of subgroup analyses or meta‐regression analyses to account for between‐study heterogeneity. However, subgroup analyses have been performed to provide estimates of treatment effect in clinically relevant subgroups of patients, demonstrating that the efficacy of darvadstrocel is consistent across different types of patients. To minimize the risk of publication bias, we performed comprehensive electronic searches and allowed inclusion of studies published as abstracts that increased the precision of this meta‐analysis.
This meta‐analysis has several limitations including those associated with observational studies. Some studies were small, retrospective, and/or non‐comparative and were susceptible to bias. In addition, the power to detect bias may be affected by the limited number of studies making Egger's analysis inappropriate for analysis, and by the inclusion of small studies with few patients. Most of the studies included in our systematic review did not include data on longer‐term follow‐up (i.e., beyond 1 year) even though the sustained fistula healing is an important therapeutic goal. There is a lack of standardized definition of AEs and SAEs, which could explain the considerable between‐study heterogeneity for safety outcomes. Another limitation of this review is that all studies are from Europe, Israel and Japan, with a lack of representation from other important geographical areas.
In conclusion, the results of this meta‐analysis of observational studies confirm the effectiveness (with very small heterogeneity between studies) of darvadstrocel as a single injection delivered by a minimally invasive surgical technique in achieving healing of complex perianal fistulas refractory to standard treatment in patients with CD. The acceptable safety data presented here support the positive long‐term benefit‐risk profile of darvadstrocel. These real‐world effectiveness and safety results provide a broad perspective that can be used to guide treatment decisions in the management of patients with perianal fistulizing CD who have not responded to conventional medical and surgical therapies.
