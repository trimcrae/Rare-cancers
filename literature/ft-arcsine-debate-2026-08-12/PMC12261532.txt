## Background
China implemented the Priority Review Program (PRP) to accelerate the approval of innovative drugs with significant clinical value. This study explores whether drugs approved through the PRP have more significant clinical benefits by comparing differences in approval time, efficacy, and safety between drugs approved through the priority and non-priority review pathways.

## Methods
In this observational comparative analysis, we conducted an analysis of innovative cancer drugs approved by the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) of the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) between January 1, 2015, and December 31, 2024. Data were systematically collected and evaluated on NDA/BLA review times, efficacy outcomes (overall survival [OS], progression-free survival [PFS], and overall response rate [ORR]), and safety outcomes (Grade ≥ 3 adverse events [AEs] and treatment-related serious adverse events [SAEs]). A meta-analysis was conducted to assess the hazard ratios for PFS, ORR, and the risk ratios for Grade ≥ 3 AEs and treatment-related SAEs. Additionally, subgroup analyses were conducted to assess the impact of the 2020 policy changes on the PRP.

## Results
The NMPA approved 49 innovative cancer drugs for 69 indications, of which 47 indications were subject to priority review and 22 to non-priority review. Priority-reviewed indications significantly shortened NDA/BLA review times (263.5 vs. 352 days, p < 0.001). However, no significant differences in efficacy were observed between priority and non-priority-reviewed indications in terms of median PFS (9.87 vs. 8.95 months, p = 0.875), median OS (10.85 vs. 20.85 months, p = 0.240), the hazard ratio of PFS (0.46 vs. 0.50, p = 0.608), and ORR (60.8% vs. 67.6%, p = 0.915; pooled ORR: 54% vs. 53.7%, p = 0.986). In contrast, priority-reviewed indications were associated with higher risks of Grade ≥ 3 AEs (56.33% vs. 57.35%, p = 0.732; risk ratio: 1.68 vs. 1.12, p = 0.017) and treatment-related SAEs (14.46% vs. 24.55%, p = 0.087; risk ratio: 1.82 vs. 1.39, p = 0.021) compared to non-priority-reviewed indications.

## Conclusions
Although the PRP expedites patient access to innovative drugs, evidence remains insufficient to demonstrate significant additional benefits in efficacy and safety.

## Background
The development of novel therapeutics is a complex and lengthy process [ ], often requiring substantial time and financial investment [ ]. For patients with severe or life-threatening diseases, current treatment options are frequently insufficient to meet their needs [ ]. Globally, regulatory agencies are actively exploring strategies to accelerate the development and approval of new drugs [ ]. For example, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) have implemented expedited approval programs aimed at reducing review times and accelerating patient access to potential new therapies [ – ].
Compared with other countries, China has been slower in drug development and approval, which has limited drug accessibility and innovation [ – ]. To address these issues, the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) introduced a Priority Review Pathway (PRP) in 2015 [ – ]. The 2020 revision of the Drug Registration Regulation further expanded PRP eligibility, shortened the review timeline to 130 working days, and enhanced interaction between the review center and applicants [ ]. Between the 11th and 13th Five-Year Plans, innovative drug research was declared a national strategic priority, leading to rapid progress in domestic immuno-oncology R&D and a surge in related publications [ ]. Concurrently, China aligned increasingly with international standards, resulting in a growing number of domestically developed innovative drugs completing pivotal clinical trials and gaining approval in China [ ]. Cancer remains the second leading cause of death worldwide and imposes a heavy economic burden on health systems [ – ]. In China, the cancer burden is especially pronounced, with a cancer profile reflecting the dual challenges faced by both developed and developing regions [ ]. Compared with the United States and the United Kingdom, China has higher cancer mortality and disability-adjusted life year rates, highlighting the urgent need for more effective drugs and innovative therapies [ ].
The primary objective of the PRP was to accelerate drug market authorization and address delays in drug approval and registration backlog. Over time, this program has evolved into an innovative pathway centered on “clinical value and therapeutic advantage”. However, existing studies have primarily focused on the impact of the PRP on approval times, with a lack of systematic evaluation of the efficacy and safety of the drugs approved through this pathway [ – ]. Therefore, this study aimed to compare innovative anticancer drugs approved under the priority and non-priority review programs in terms of approval time, efficacy, and safety. The findings will provide critical evidence for further optimizing the drug approval system in China to better meet patient needs.

## Methods

## Data sources
We searched the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) website to identify all new cancer drug applications approved by the NMPA in China from January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2024, and determined their specific approved indications. Our study focused exclusively on Class 1 chemical and biological products, excluding traditional Chinese medicines, vaccines, gene therapies and supportive therapies (e.g. treatments for cancer-related complications). Class 1 drugs are defined as new active substances or molecular entities that have not yet received marketing approval either domestically or internationally.

## Data extraction
Given that cancer drugs often have multiple indications with varying evidence of efficacy and clinical benefit, all indications (including original and supplemental) were included for analysis [ ]. The characteristics of each indication were extracted, including the year of approval, market authorization status, cancer type and site, indication, lines of therapy, trial design, clinical trial phase, and primary efficacy endpoints. Market authorization categories were assigned based on the CDE registration acceptance numbers: those prefixed with “C” were classified as domestically developed drugs, while those prefixed with “J” were classified as imported drugs. For all original indications, we collected the NDA/BLA (New Drug Application/Biologics License Application) review times from the Insight Database. Two researchers (QL and DY) independently extracted these data, and any discrepancies were resolved by a third researcher (HG).

## Identification of pivotal clinical trials
We identified all pivotal clinical trials supporting each cancer indication from the NMPA review reports, where these trials were explicitly described. We then used each trial’s registration number to search PubMed for the most recent pre-approval peer-reviewed publications and to obtain international conference abstracts (e.g. American Society of Clinical Oncology annual meeting), thereby supplementing the pivotal trial data. For randomized controlled trials (RCTs), we extracted data on hazard ratios (HRs) and median survival times for both overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). For single-arm trials, we extracted the overall response rate (ORR). For solid tumors is typically referred to as objective response rate, assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST v1.1), which includes both partial and complete responses [ ]. To assess the safety between PR and non-PR indications, we extracted the treatment-emergent serious adverse events (SAEs) and Grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs). Data inclusion criteria prioritized pivotal clinical trial results evaluated by an independent review committee (IRC) [ ]. PFS gains and OS gains were defined as the difference in median PFS and median OS, respectively, between the experimental and control arms in randomized controlled trials. Following previous studies, we considered OS gains of ≥ 2.5 months and PFS gains of ≥ 3 months for all cancer types to be clinically meaningful [ ].

## Statistical analysis
We conducted a descriptive analysis comparing the characteristics of priority-reviewed and non- priority-reviewed indications. Categorical variables were presented as numbers, and continuous variables as medians (interquartile ranges, IQR). Fisher’s exact test was employed to compare categorical variables. For continuous variables not following a normal distribution, the Mann-Whitney U test was applied; otherwise, a T-test was used. NDA/BLA review time (defined as the duration from NDA/BLA submission to NDA/BLA approval) was compared between PR and non-PR cancer drugs using Kaplan-Meier survival curves, log-rank tests, and Cox proportional hazards models, consistent with previous studies [ – ].
A meta-analysis was performed to summarize primary efficacy endpoints and safety outcomes, including the hazard ratio (HR) for PFS and OS in RCTs, ORR in single-arm trials, relative risk of treatment-related SAEs and Grade ≥ 3 AEs in RCTs, and incidence rates of treatment-related SAEs and Grade ≥ 3 AEs in single-arm trials. Confidence intervals for proportions (ORR, and rates of treatment-related SAEs and Grade ≥ 3 AEs in single-arm trials) were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson exact method, and variance stabilization achieved through Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation. Differences between HR and ORR for priority and nonpriority review indications were compared using the Cochran’s Q test. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic, with I² ≥50% indicating significant heterogeneity, in such case, random effects models were applied. while fixed effects models were used otherwise.
Following the implementation of the new Provisions for Drug Registration, we conducted a subgroup analysis to assess changes in approval time, efficacy, and safety following the policy’s enactment. All statistical analyses were conducted using STATA 17.0, with a p-value of < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Characteristics of innovative drugs and pivotal clinical trials
From January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2024, the NMPA approved 49 innovative cancer drugs for 69 indications, Of these drugs, 13 received supplemental approvals, and none were approved between 2015 and 2017. (Figure S1). The highest number of drugs was approved in 2021, with 18 new drugs covering 21 indications (30.43%) (Figure S2 and Table  ). Of the 69 indications, the NMPA granted the PR to 47 (68.12%) indications. Lung cancer (25 indications [36.24%]) is the most common cancer type, followed by lymphoma (14 indications [20.29%]) (Table  ). The proportion of original anticancer indications was significantly higher among priority-reviewed indications than non-priority-reviewed indications (38 of 47 [80.9%] vs. 11 of 22 [50.0%]; p = 0.012; Table  ). There are no significant differences between priority and non-priority-reviewed indications regarding market authorization, cancer type, or cancer site. The indications for second-line and beyond treatments (40 of 47 [85.11] vs. 12 of 22 [54.55], p = 0.014) are more likely to be granted priority review (Table  ). Among the 69 Class 1 innovative drug indications approved in China, 65 (94.20%) were domestically developed, and of these, more than half (43/65 [66.15%]) were granted priority review. Clinical evidence supporting priority-reviewed indications is typically derived from Phase II (65.95% vs. 27.28%, p = 0.006) single-arm trials (72.34% vs. 31.82%, p = 0.003) (Table  ). 

## NDA/BLA review times
We found that for all original indications, the NDA/BLA review time for priority-reviewed indications was significantly shorter than for non-priority-reviewed indications (263.5[195,305] vs. 352[297,482], p <0.001; HR = 2.40[1.20,4.80], Log rank p = 0.011) (Fig.  ). The NDA/BLA review time for cancer drugs has been further shortened following the release of the new policy, with the review time for priority review indications reduced to 197 days (Fig.  ).

 

 

## Efficacy
Among the 69 approved drug indications, 41 (59.41%) were approved primarily based on ORR, 18 (26.09%) on PFS, 5 (7.25%) on OS, and 2 (2.9%) on PFS/OS (Table  ). ORR as a primary clinical endpoint in pivotal clinical trials, is more likely to be granted priority review (70.2% vs. 36.36%, p = 0.002) (Table  ). Compared to non-priority-reviewed indications, priority-reviewed indications had fewer enrolled patients (183 vs. 355, p = 0.053), but the difference was not statistically significant (Table  ).

Compared to non-priority-reviewed indications, priority-reviewed indications had a longer median PFS (9.87 vs. 8.95, p = 0.875), but shorter median OS (10.85 vs. 20.85, p = 0.240) and ORR (60.8 vs. 67.6, p = 0.915), although no significant differences were observed (Table  ). Similarly, the results from pooled analysis showed that no significant difference for HR for OS (pooled HR: 0.73 vs. 0.69, p = 0.551)、HR for PFS (pooled HR:0.46 vs. 0.50, p = 0.608) and ORR (pooled estimate: 54% vs. 53.7%, p = 0.986) (Table  and Figure S3). Among the 11 priority-reviewed indications, 9 showed clinical significance, while 11 out of 14 non-priority-reviewed indications showed clinical significance. Subgroup analysis revealed that, following the release of the new policy, there were no significant differences between priority-reviewed and non-priority-reviewed indications (Table S1). 

## Safety
In single-arm trials, the incidence of Grade ≥3 AEs was significantly higher in priority-reviewed indications compared to non-priority-reviewed indications (36.23% vs 22.70%, p = 0.029), while there was no significant difference in the incidence of treatment-related SAEs (17.38% vs 8.69%, p = 0.056) (Table ). Pooled analysis revealed that priority-reviewed indications had significantly higher rates of Grade ≥3 AEs (37.9% vs 21.6%, p = 0.001) and treatment-related SAEs (18.6% vs 8.6%, p < 0.01) compared to non-priority-reviewed indications (Table and Figure S4).
In randomized controlled trials, no significant differences were observed between priority-reviewed and non-priority-reviewed indications regarding the incidence of Grade ≥3 AEs (56.33% vs 57.35%, p = 0.732) and treatment-related SAEs (14.46% vs 24.55%, p=0.087) (Table ). Pooled risk ratio analysis indicated that priority-reviewed indications had a higher risk of Grade≥3 AEs (1.68 vs 1.12, p=0.017) and treatment-related SAEs (1.82 vs 1.39,p=0.021), with statistically significant differences when compared to non-priority-reviewed indications (Table and Figure S4). The results of the subgroup analysis were consistent with the overall findings (Table S2). 

## Discussion
To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically examine the differences in NDA/BLA review times and clinical outcomes between priority-reviewed and non-priority-reviewed drugs approved by the NMPA in China. The findings demonstrate that the priority review process significantly reduced NDA/BLA review times. Although priority-reviewed drugs did not show significant efficacy differences, they were associated with a higher risk of Grade≥3 AEs and treatment-related SAEs compared to non-priority-reviewed drugs.
Consistent with previous studies, the priority review process has accelerated the market entry of clinically valuable innovative cancer drugs, thereby improving patient accessibility [ , , , ]. Our findings indicate that the NDA/BLA review time for priority-reviewed drugs was reduced by 88.5 days compared to non-priority-reviewed drugs. Following the 2020 revision of the Drug Registration Regulation, the CDE assigns priority review resources, reducing the standard review timeline from 200 to 130 working days. For urgently needed orphan drugs already approved overseas but not yet in China, the review period is further shortened to 70 working days. During the technical review phase, applicants may submit supplementary materials upon request without extending the overall timeline, and explicit milestones are defined for each review step, all of which enhance procedural efficiency. These reforms support the original objectives of the PRP—namely, alleviating registration backlogs, increasing approval efficiency, and expediting market access. Nevertheless, compared with the FDA’s median accelerated approval review time of 179 days, China’s review timelines still lag behind [ ].
Our study shows that the efficacy of priority-reviewed and non-priority-reviewed drugs is comparable, which aligns with the findings of Luo et al. regarding the efficacy assessment of breakthrough and conditionally approved drugs [ , , ]. This suggests that cancer drugs approved through accelerated review pathways in China have not demonstrated significant improvements in clinical efficacy. Currently, neither the NMPA, the FDA, nor the EMA place stringent requirements on comparative effectiveness data when granting accelerated approval [ ].
OS remains the “gold-standard” endpoint for evaluating anticancer efficacy, but demonstrating an OS benefit typically requires large sample sizes, prolonged follow-up, and mature data, which delays patient access to new therapies [ – ]. To accelerate review, regulatory agencies increasingly rely on surrogate endpoints such as PFS and ORR; however, only about 20% of drugs approved on the basis of these surrogates subsequently confirm an OS benefit in confirmatory trials [ ]. In our study, pivotal trials supporting priority review were predominantly single-arm studies using ORR as the primary endpoint. An ORR improvement does not necessarily translate into a survival advantage. Durvalumab represents one notable international example: in 2017, it received approval for previously treated locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma based on ORR data from a single-arm trial, but subsequent studies failed to demonstrate an OS benefit and showed a shorter PFS compared with chemotherapy, leading to withdrawal of this indication in the United States in 2021 [ ]. Although China has not approved this indication, vigilance is warranted. This is not an isolated case—nine accelerated-approval indications were rescinded in the US in 2021, six of which involved immunotherapies for solid tumors [ ]. Therefore, we call for enhanced international communication and collaboration, continuous monitoring of label updates and ongoing clinical trial results across jurisdictions, and the grounding of clinical practice in the most robust evidence to ensure that patients receive truly effective treatments [ , ].
Safety is a crucial component in evaluating the clinical benefits of cancer therapies. However, existing safety assessments remain somewhat constrained, primarily due to the reliance on single-arm trials for most priority-reviewed drugs, which limits the robustness of safety data [ ]. Our study indicates that priority-reviewed drugs are associated with a significantly higher risk of adverse events compared to non-priority-reviewed drugs. This may reflect the urgent clinical needs these therapies address, often with limited alternative treatment options. In contrast, studies of breakthrough therapies have not demonstrated significant differences in safety profiles between breakthrough and non-breakthrough drugs [ , ]. This discrepancy may stem from the inclusion of all indications in our study, including supplementary indications. Additionally, the safety data analyzed were derived from individual pivotal trials, rather than from pooled analyses of multiple trials. Future research should aim to increase sample sizes and incorporate data from randomized controlled trials and real-world evidence to provide a more comprehensive safety evaluation, thus supporting more informed regulatory decision-making.
We found that imported drugs approved as Class 1 innovative agents in China were extremely rare (only four indications in our cohort). Because Class 1 status denotes drug that has not previously been marketed domestically or internationally, this suggests that international companies seldom choose China as their initial launch market. Conversely, approvals of innovative oncology drugs in China have been predominantly concentrated in second-line and later-line settings. As domestic R&D advances, innovative “global-first” agents are increasingly being evaluated in earlier indications (first-line, adjuvant, neoadjuvant) to meet unmet clinical needs [ ]. Chinese pharmaceutical companies have demonstrated substantial innovation by developing novel PD-1 antibodies, small-molecule targeted therapies, and cell-based approaches [ ]. However, international uptake remains limited because the pivotal trials supporting approval were almost exclusively conducted within China in predominantly Chinese patient populations. Such single-population trials rarely fulfill the FDA’s multi-regional confirmatory evidence requirement, hindering global adoption [ , ]. To enhance regulatory acceptability and facilitate earlier international launches, future pivotal trials should include multi-regional cohorts.
This study has several limitations. First, our analysis was restricted to cancer drugs, limiting the broader applicability of our findings to other therapeutic areas. Second, we combined different cancer types in the efficacy and safety analyses, which may introduce considerable heterogeneity in the effect sizes. Ideally, a stratified analysis based on cancer types would provide more precise comparisons, but the available data did not support such subgroup analyses. Furthermore, some drug evaluation reports did not disclose approval dates, preventing accurate calculation of NDA/BLA review times; thus, we relied on data from the Insight database. Lastly, it is important to acknowledge that regulatory decisions are informed by a comprehensive risk-benefit assessment, which not only considers clinical outcomes such as ORR or PFS but also takes into account the specific clinical context of each drug.

## Conclusions
The implementation of the priority review policy in China has significantly expedited the market approval of innovative cancer drugs, thereby enhancing patient accessibility. Our study demonstrates that, although there is no significant difference in efficacy between priority-reviewed and non-priority-reviewed drugs, the former may be associated with a higher incidence of adverse reactions. These findings suggest that while the priority review process can accelerate the market entry of potentially life-saving therapies, further confirmation of their efficacy and safety is required. Such evidence is essential to broaden the inclusion of clinically beneficial drugs in the priority review pathway and facilitate their expedited approval.
