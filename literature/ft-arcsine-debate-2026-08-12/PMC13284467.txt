## OBJECTIVE
An obstetrical fistula is an abnormal communication between the vagina and adjacent tubular structures, usually the bladder and rectum. If timely and effective treatment is not given, women with a fistula face lifetime embarrassment, isolation, social stigmatization, and marital separation. Although early detection and management of obstetrical fistula often result in improved treatment outcomes, most women do not have access to care due to either the cost, stigma, or lack of awareness. This study aimed to determine the pooled prevalence and predictors of healthcare seeking for obstetrical fistula in sub-Saharan Africa.

## DATA SOURCE
As this is a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol, the primary data sources will be electronic databases and grey literature. We will systematically search PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar to retrieve eligible primary studies.

## STUDY ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA
Per our protocol, we apply the CoCoPop (Condition, Context, and Population) mnemonic framework:•Inclusion Criteria: Observational studies (cross-sectional, case-control, cohort) investigating healthcare seeking for obstetric fistula and its determinants; conducted in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) countries; published in the English language between 2015 and 2025.
•Exclusion Criteria: Studies conducted outside of SSA, studies lacking an abstract or full text, qualitative studies, conference summaries, and studies where the primary outcome variable is not formal clinical healthcare-seeking behavior.

## METHODS
This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. PubMed, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and gray literature were used to search for eligible articles. The quality of the studies was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal checklist. All studies on obstetrical fistula care seeking conducted in English from 2015 to 2025 and in sub-Saharan Africa were included. The required data were extracted from each article using Microsoft Excel 2016 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA) and exported to Stata (version 16; StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX). Forest plot and I2 statistics were performed for heterogeneity. A funnel plot and Egger regression test were performed to check for publication bias. Subgroup analysis and meta-regression analysis were performed to identify potential sources of bias.

## RESULTS
Because this manuscript is a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol, there are no empirical results to report at this stage. The protocol establishes our plan to extract, transform, and pool data using STATA version 17 to determine the pooled magnitude and effect sizes (OR/RR) of predictors once the full review is completed.

## CONCLUSION
As a protocol, final study conclusions are not yet available. However, this upcoming systematic review and meta-analysis is designed to provide a comprehensive, pooled magnitude of healthcare-seeking behavior and its multi-level socio-ecological predictors. The final findings will provide robust empirical evidence intended to guide policy makers, program implementers, and healthcare providers in optimizing maternal health interventions across sub-Saharan Africa.

## AJOG Global Reports at a Glance

## Why was this study conducted?
Untreated obstetric fistula causes profound physical and psychosocial trauma for millions of women in sub-Saharan Africa, yet timely healthcare-seeking rates remain low, and a comprehensive, region-wide synthesis of the specific barriers and multi-level determinants influencing care utilization is currently lacking.

## Key findings
This manuscript details a rigorous systematic review and meta-analysis protocol designed to map out electronic databases (2015–2025) and establish a standardized framework for pooling the prevalence and socio-ecological predictors of formal health facility utilization among affected women.

## What does this add to what is known?
By providing a pre-registered, transparent methodology that differentiates population-based from institution-based barriers and accounts for zero-event studies, this protocol lays the groundwork to deliver high-quality, aggregated empirical data essential for refining digital health and maternal health systems transformation across sub-Saharan Africa.

## Introduction
An obstetrical fistula is an abnormal communication between the vagina and adjacent tubular structures, usually the bladder and rectum. This abnormal communication leads to continuous leakage of urine or feces through the vagina, which has pervasive consequences on the psychological, social, physical, and economic well-being of women. If timely and effective treatment is not given, women with a fistula face lifetime embarrassment, isolation, social stigmatization, and marital separation. , Furthermore, they are prone to develop chronic vaginal and urinary tract infections, renal failure, pelvic inflammatory disease, and amenorrhea. 
An estimated 50,000 to 100,000 reproductive-age women worldwide develop obstetrical fistula annually. The problem is higher in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) and Asia, with 2 to 3 million women suffering from untreated obstetrical fistula. The lifetime prevalence of vaginal fistula in SSA is 3 cases per 1000 women of reproductive age. , 
Although surgical closure is the most effective intervention to treat vaginal fistula, for many high-burden countries, access to skilled professionals capable of repairing fistula remains limited, and very few hospitals are providing the service. In addition, most fistula cases are from impoverished and marginalized segments of the population, and treatment seeking is likely hindered by psychosocial, economic, and other contextual factors. 
Studies and crude estimates have suggested that timely treatment seeking for fistula is generally low and as high as 2 million fistula cases remain untreated globally. , , The magnitude of obstetrical fistula and the problems of healthcare-seeking behavior are high in the sub-Saharan countries because of factors, including lack of social/family/support, lack of information about fistula and its treatment, shortage of health workers, unaffordable treatment cost, societal male dominance, and loss of dignity and self-worth. , , 
Quantitative studies with multiple countries and pooling of primary studies were limited because a vaginal fistula is a rare event in a statistical sense and recruiting adequate cases through community-based surveys is usually impractical. This study aimed to determine the pooled prevalence of healthcare seeking and the determinants using primary quantitative studies conducted in SSA.

## Materials and methods

## Systematic review question
What is the pooled prevalence of healthcare seeking among women with obstetrical fistula? What are the factors that affect the healthcare-seeking behavior of women with an obstetrical fistula?

## Search strategy and data sources
Electronic databases (PubMed, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and gray literature) were systematically searched online to retrieve related articles using Medical Subject Headings and key terms. The literature search technique was performed using title/abstract/key words. We searched the primary studies using the following key terms: “obstetric fistula” OR “vaginal fistula” OR “vesicovaginal fistula” OR “genital fistula” AND “healthcare seeking” OR “health seeking” OR “treatment seeking” OR “help seeking” OR “care seeking” AND “sub-Saharan Africa” list of individual countries in the region. A sample search strategy for the PubMed database is presented in .
The protocol for this systematic review and meta-analysis is registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number: CRD420251127679; available at https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420251127679).

## Eligibility criteria
For this review, the CoCoPop mnemonic (condition, context, and population) approach was used. All observational studies (cross-sectional, case-control, prospective, and retrospective cohort studies) on fistula treatment seeking and associated factors conducted in the SSA countries were included. These studies should be in the English language and published from 2015 to 2025. Studies conducted outside of SSA, studies with no abstract and full text, studies not in the English language, studies published before 2015, studies on fistula but the outcome variable is not about care-seeking behavior, and qualitative studies were excluded. This timeframe was selected because it aligns with the United Nations’ adoption of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, which integrates fistula treatment into several Sustainable Development Goals. Furthermore, it coincides with the 2016 launch of the standardized fistula surgery training initiative of the International Federation of Gynaecology and Obstetrics. , The conference summaries were excluded because of the lack of sufficient information for quality evaluation or data extraction.
The outcome variable, healthcare seeking, is defined as follows: the comprehensive process initiated by a woman upon the recognition of symptoms, leading to the pursuit and utilization of formal medical services (medical or surgical interventions) within formal health institutions (public or private clinics, hospitals, or specialized fistula centers). To ensure a focused analysis of clinical care pathways, interventions or consultations provided by traditional healers or informal community practitioners were excluded from this definition.

## Data extraction and management
Our systematic search was performed across all identified databases. Eligible studies were imported to EndNote (version 9), and duplicated studies, studies conducted before 2015, and close but unrelated articles based on their title and abstract were excluded. Of note, 3 independent reviewers performed the abstract and full-text review. If there was disagreement between the 2 reviewers, a discussion was performed to reach a consensus. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement for reporting systematic review and meta-analysis was used to summarize the selection procedures of the articles. The pooled estimates will be computed for any determinant reported by at least 3 independent studies using comparable metrics.
We categorized studies into population- and institution-based settings to ensure a robust synthesis of evidence. This classification is reasoned out as institution-based studies are inherently limited to care-seeking women who have already reached out to facilities and as population-based studies allow for the inclusion of non–care-seeking women. In addition, we anticipate significant statistical heterogeneity between these 2 settings. Subgrouping by setting allows us to identify whether the reported barriers are universal or specific to certain stages of the care-seeking journey.
Finally, the data extraction sheet were prepared to collect information on the names of the authors, countries, year of publication, study setting (population based vs institution based), study design, sample size, study participants, magnitude of healthcare seeking, identified factors, and adjusted effect sizes. Transformation was made on outlier estimates to adjust the effect of these estimates on the pooled results.

## Analysis of determinants and effect size pooling
We used a structured meta-analytical approach to assess the factors influencing healthcare seeking for obstetrical fistula. Determinants were categorized using a social-ecological framework to ensure clinical and social relevance, including individual level, household level, health system level, and sociocultural level.
Pooled estimates (odds ratio [OR] or risk ratio [RR]) were computed for each determinant where at least 3 studies provided sufficient data. To account for anticipated clinical and methodological heterogeneity, a random-effects model was used as the default. Heterogeneity was quantified using the I2 statistic. If I2>75%, we refrained from pooling and instead provided a narrative synthesis or conducted a meta-regression to explore the sources of variance.
In addition, we prioritized the extraction of the most-adjusted estimate from each study. If adjustment sets vary significantly, we performed subgroup analyses comparing minimally adjusted vs fully adjusted models to determine whether the effect size remains robust. If only unadjusted (crude) ORs were available for a specific determinant, they were analyzed separately from adjusted estimates to avoid mixing biased and unbiased data.

## Quality assessment
The Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) checklist for analytical cross-sectional studies was used for studies investigating the determinants of healthcare-seeking behavior, whereas the JBI checklist for prevalence studies was used for frequency data. The checklist is freely available at https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal and is presented in . Studies with scores of ≥5 in the JBI criteria were considered to be of good quality. Each study was independently assessed by 2 reviewers, with a third reviewer resolving any discrepancies. Finally, the PRISMA statement was used for reporting of systematic review and meta-analysis results.

## Statistical analysis
The required data were extracted from the eligible studies using Microsoft Excel 2016 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA) and exported to the Stata software (version 17; StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX) for analysis. The articles were summarized by tables and a forest plot. The magnitude of the outcome variable for each study was pooled using a fixed-effects model if no significant heterogeneity existed. If not, a random-effects model was used. Statistical heterogeneity was examined subjectively using a forest plot and examined objectively using the Cochran Q test statistics. The presence of publication bias was checked using funnel plots and Begg statistical tests. However, these formal assessments were performed in accordance with Cochrane recommendations if a minimum of 10 studies were included in a specific meta-analysis. If fewer than 10 studies were available, publication bias was discussed qualitatively, focusing on the likelihood of “file drawer” effects and the exclusion of gray literature. For any tests performed, a P value of <.10 was considered indicative of significant asymmetry, and the results were interpreted with caution given the expected clinical heterogeneity in the fistula populations. In addition, subgroup analysis was performed based on countries, publication year, and study setting. As the outcome variable, care seeking for a gynecologic fistula, is a rare event, we used the Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation to stabilize variance and include studies with zero-event studies or sparse data. 

## Ethics and dissemination
The review was conducted based on published articles and did not require ethical approval. We registered our topic on PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251127679). The results will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and may be presented at conferences.

## Patient and public involvement
None.

## Discussion
This systematic review and meta-analysis provided the pooled magnitude of healthcare seeking for obstetrical fistula and its predictors. The findings will provide empirical evidence for policymakers, program implementers, healthcare providers, and future researchers.

## Strengths and limitations of the study
This study included studies conducted in multiple countries in the region, which may provide stronger evidence than single-country studies. The proposed systematic review and meta-analysis followed the PRISMA guidelines. The quality of eligible studies was assessed using the JBI critical appraisal checklist. However, the study excluded studies conducted in languages other than English and qualitative studies.

## Conclusion
As a protocol, final study conclusions are not yet available. However, this upcoming systematic review and meta-analysis is designed to provide a comprehensive, pooled magnitude of healthcare-seeking behavior and its multi-level socio-ecological predictors. The final findings will provide robust empirical evidence intended to guide policy makers, program implementers, and healthcare providers in optimizing maternal health interventions across sub-Saharan Africa.
