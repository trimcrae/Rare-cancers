## Abstract

## Background:
Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a highly heterogeneous disease group. Ibrutinib's monotherapy or combination therapy is effective in relapsed/refractory (R/R) DLBCL. However, the treatment response in R/R DLBCL varies from 15% to 90% with different regimens, and the tolerance remains controversial.

## Areas of Uncertainty:
The efficacy and safety of ibrutinib monotherapy or combination therapy in patients with R/R DLBCL remain uncertain.

## Data Sources:
The PubMed, CBM, MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, and Embase databases were searched from their inception to July 2021.

## Therapeutic Advances:
The total complete remission rate (CRR) and overall response rate in R/R DLBCL patients treated with ibrutinib were 26% and 49%, respectively. The CRR of ibrutinib combination therapy was significantly higher than the ibrutinib monotherapy (45% vs. 19%). Moreover, the CRR of patients was 40% in double expressing lymphoma, 35% in central nervous system lymphoma, and 33% in nongerminal center B-cell–like (non-GCB) DLBCL, which was higher than the 8% in those with the GCB subtype. The pooled median PFS and overall survival were 5.57 and 10.17 months, respectively. GCB-DLBCL had the worst overall survival (5.1 months). Nevertheless, we found that combination regimens had no survival advantage compared with monotherapy (P > 0.05), indicating that combination therapy was only a transitional treatment and bridge for chimeric antigen receptor T cells or other treatments. Moreover, 12% of patients on ibrutinib combination therapy had ≥grade 3 adverse events compared with 9% on ibrutinib monotherapy.

## Conclusions:
Ibrutinib monotherapy or combination therapy was safe and effective in treating R/R DLBCL with tolerable adverse reactions.

## INTRODUCTION
Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a group of highly heterogeneous diseases, accounting for approximately 40% of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL). Rituximab, in combination with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP), remains the first-line standard regimen for newly diagnosed DLBCL patients with a 5-year overall survival (OS) of 60%–70%. , However, 30%–40% of patients relapse after initial treatment, and approximately 10%–15% of patients progress to refractory disease. Moreover, the overall response rate (ORR) of second-line therapy is < 40%, even high-dose chemotherapy followed by autologous stem cell transplantation (HDC-ASCT) , is given, and limited further options are available for these patients. Therefore, it is urgently necessary to develop novel therapeutic strategies for patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) DLBCL. , 
Activation of the nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) pathway is essential in the progression and drug resistance in nongerminal center B-cell (non-GCB) type DLBCL. Because activated B-cell DLBCL (ABC-DLBCL) relies on chronic active B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling, which involves Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK), spleen tyrosine kinase, and PI3K kinases that activate NF-κB downstream, furthermore, these kinase activities are more significant in R/R ABC-DLBCL. , The BTK is a crucial enzyme in the BCR signaling pathway and a tyrosine kinase in the Tec family, which plays an important role in the maturation, activation, proliferation, and survival of B-cell lymphoma. Ibrutinib is an irreversible small-molecule BTK inhibitor, which can destroy the signaling components from BCR to NF-κB by inhibiting the autophosphorylation of BTK and can effectively inhibit the proliferation of B cells and induce their apoptosis. Ibrutinib is approved by the Food and Drug Administration for chronic lymphocytic leukemia and mantle cell leukemia because of its favorable efficacy and good tolerance. In addition, many mature B-cell malignancies are highly sensitive to kinase inhibitors that disrupt BCR signaling. There are some reports that the monotherapy or combination therapy of ibrutinib is effective in R/R DLBCL, especially in the ABC subtype. However, the treatment response in R/R DLBCL ranges from 15% to 90% with different treatment regimens, and the tolerance remains controversial. Therefore, we conducted this meta-analysis to explore the efficacy and safety of ibrutinib in R/R DLBCL patients, providing an evidence-based basis for its clinical application and prompting clinical trials of ibrutinib in R/R DLBCL.

## METHODS

## Search strategies
Recurrent DLBCL was defined as lymphoma that relapsed after obtaining complete remission (CR) from initial chemotherapy, while refractory lymphoma could be diagnosed if any of the following conditions were met: (1) tumor shrinkage <50% or disease progression after 4 cycles of standard regimens of chemotherapy; (2) CR was achieved by standard chemotherapy, while relapse occurred within 6 months; (3) 2 or more times of recurrence after CR; and (4) recurrence after ASCT. This study involved a systematic review and meta-analysis of the benefits of ibrutinib therapy in R/R DLBCL patients. Relevant clinical studies were identified by a systematic search of PubMed, CBM, MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, and Embase databases from their inception to July 2021. The retrieval strategy adopted the subject words combined with free words. The search terms included chemotherapy-refractory, refractory, relapsed, chemotherapy refractory, relapse, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL), and ibrutinib (see Table, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/AJT/A179). To ensure comprehensive data collection, the included literature was traced back, and secondary literature retrieval was performed.

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Inclusion criteria were set as follows: (1) published studies of ibrutinib treatment in R/R DLBCL; (2) all DLBCL patients involved were relapsed or refractory, regardless of whether hematopoietic stem cell transplantation was performed, and these patients were independent of gender, age, nationality, and disease stage; and (3) the efficacy of ibrutinib in R/R DLBCL was evaluated.
Exclusion criteria were set as follows: (1) non-English literature; (2) duplicate published studies; (3) incomplete or inconsistent original data; (4) studies consisting of < 10 patients; (5) and abstracts, reviews, conference reports, case reports, cell experiments, and animal experiments were also excluded.

## Outcome measures
Outcome indicators were as follows: OS, ORR, progress free survival (PFS), complete remission rate (CRR), partial remission rate, and rate of serious adverse reactions of patients. ORR was the sum of CRR and partial remission rate. Serious adverse events (AEs) were defined as those occurring during ibrutinib-targeted therapy according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE 4.0), and the patients with grade ≥3 AEs were counted.

## Literature screening, data extraction, and quality evaluation
For any missed information, the author should be contacted as far as possible to obtain the literature. Data extraction mainly included the first author, publication date, study design type, number of patients, gender, age, disease type, drug combination, outcome indicators, and AEs. A methodological index for nonrandomized studies was used to evaluate the quality of the included studies. 

## Statistical analysis
Stata 14.0 software was used for statistical analysis, and the Q test and I2 test were used to evaluate the heterogeneity of the included literature. According to the results of the heterogeneity test, the corresponding statistical effect model was selected. P > 0.1 or I2 < 50% indicated homogeneity of the studies, and a fixed-effects model was used for analysis. In contrast, P < 0.1 or I2 > 50% indicated heterogeneity between studies, and the random-effects model was used for analysis. Subgroup analysis was performed according to the possible related factors. In addition, we searched for sources of heterogeneity through a sensitivity analysis that excluded references one by one. If heterogeneity could not be eliminated after grouping, a systematic evaluation or a joint analysis using a random-effects model was considered. The original data were normalized by the double arcsine method. The final results of the meta-analysis were reduced using the formula P = [sin (tp/2)]2. A funnel plot was used to evaluate the publication bias, and P < 0.05 was defined as the existence of publication bias.

## RESULTS

## Identification of eligible literature
According to the retrieval strategy, 179 studies were preliminarily retrieved. According to inclusion and exclusion criteria, 17 articles were finally included in this study. Figure depicts a detailed flowchart of the selection process. Tables and show the characteristics of included studies.
 

## Total treatment response rate
A total of 17 studies were included for analysis, and 4 of the 514 patients included were lost to follow-up and could not be evaluated for response. The mean age was 58–71 years old. Single-group meta-analysis was conducted on CRR and ORR of ibrutinib in patients with R/R DLBCL, and I2 of the heterogeneity test was 72.02% and 74.77% (Figures and ), respectively, indicating statistical heterogeneity. Therefore, the random-effects model was used for combined analysis. The results showed that ibrutinib-treated R/R DLBCL had a weighted combined CRR of 26% (95% CI, 0.19–0.34) and ORR of 49% (95% CI, 0.40–0.58).
 

## Subgroup analysis
To explore the potential factors affecting the treatment effects of ibrutinib, we divided the enrolled patients into 4 subgroups according to the disease type, including GCB DLBCL, non-GCB DLBCL, DEL, and central nervous system lymphoma (CNSL); treatment methods; treatment lines; and age. The results showed the CRR was 40% (95% CI, 0.22–0.60), 35% (95% CI, 0.18–0.53), and 33% (95% CI, 0.14–0.55) in patients with DEL, CNSL, and non-GCB subtypes, respectively, which was higher than in patients with the GCB subtype (8%, 95% CI, 0.02–0.16). It was worth noting that the CRR in CNSL patients was similar to those of the non-GCB subtype, confirming the fact that most CNSL patients belonged to the non-GCB subtype of DLBCL.
The CRR of ibrutinib-based combination therapies was as follows: 45% in ibrutinib + rituximab, 53% in ibrutinib + HD-MTX (methotrexate) + R, 86% in ibrutinib + DA-TDEEi-R (liposomal doxorubicin, dexamethasone, ibrutinib, and rituximab), 31% in ibrutinib + bendamustine + R, 55% in ibrutinib + R-ICE (ifosfamide, etoposide, and carboplatin), and 29% in ibrutinib + lenalidomide + R, – which was significantly higher than the ibrutinib monotherapy (19%). However, the CRR of the ibrutinib + immune checkpoint inhibitor – was only 20%, similar to that of the ibrutinib monotherapy. The number of previous treatment lines and age did not affect the treatment response of patients (Table ).

## Survival analysis
Moreover, 16 studies were finally included, and 1 article did not report PFS in patients treated with ibrutinib monotherapy. The results showed that I2 = 99.7%, P = 0.000, with significant heterogeneity. Using the random-effects model, the median PFS was 5.57 months (95% CI, 4.61–6.52). However, combination regimens had no PFS advantage compared with monotherapy (7.85 vs. 4.78 months, P = 0.053) (Figure A). Among subtypes of DLBCL, the PFS of CNSL, DEL, and non-GCB was 7.98 months (95% CI, 5.17–10.79), 4.88 months (95% CI, 3.92–5.84), and 3.97 months (95% CI, 2.48–5.46), respectively. In contrast, GCB DLBCL had the worst PFS of 3.10 months (95% CI, 2.11–4.09) (Figure B).
 Five – , , of the 17 studies did not provide OS data. Therefore, a total of 12 studies were included. There was a statistically significant difference in heterogeneity among studies (I2 = 99.7%, P = 0.000) (Figure ). The random-effects model was used for meta-analysis, and the median OS was 10.17 months (95% CI, 8.48–11.85, P = 0.210) (Figure A). Compared with other disease types, GCB-DLBCL had the worst OS of 5.11 months (95% CI, 3.56–6.67) (Figure B).
 

## Incidence of serious AEs
The patients with grade ≥3 AEs were assessed and counted as a percentage of the total number of patients, as shown in Figures and . Most of the studies were followed for more than 1 year. The most common adverse reactions of ibrutinib in the studies were infection and neutropenia. Ibrutinib-related grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 12% of patients with combination treatments compared with 9% in patients treated with ibrutinib monotherapy.
 Notably, patients treated with ibrutinib combination therapy were more likely to have atrial fibrillation (16%) than those with ibrutinib monotherapy (1%). In the study of Sauter et al, 1 patient withdrew because of grade 3 atrial fibrillation. The presence of atrial fibrillation is considered a disturbing adverse reaction. In addition, ibrutinib also increases the risk of clinically relevant bleeding events, which presents unique challenges for managing these patients. In general, ibrutinib was safe and effective in the treatment of R/R DLBCL, although there might be more adverse reactions in combination therapy.

## Sensitivity analysis
The included studies were further subjected to the sensitivity analysis, and changes in effect indicators were observed after removing the included studies one by one, as well as differences in heterogeneity and confidence intervals compared with the original model. The results showed that none of the studies significantly affected the combined results, suggesting that the results of this meta-analysis were stable and reliable. At the same time, to exclude the source of methodological heterogeneity, we switched to the fixed-effects model and found that there was no significant difference between the random-effects model and the fixed-effects model, indicating that the type of method combining effect values was not the cause of heterogeneity.

## Publication bias
Figure shows the funnel plot results of ORR, PFS, and OS in R/R DLBCL patients with ibrutinib treatment, and the distribution of each study point was roughly symmetric. Begg test evaluated the publication bias of the included studies. The results of CRR, PFS, and OS were P = 0.59, 0.488, and 0.661, respectively, indicating no significant publication bias (P > 0.05). However, the existence of publication bias could not be ruled out because the funnel plot looked asymmetric, which might be because of the limited number of studies, resulting in inadequate test effectiveness.
 

## DISCUSSION
Although the therapeutic efficacy of DLBCL has been greatly improved with the emergence of novel anticancer drugs and therapies, R/R DLBCL is still a blood tumor with high morbidity and mortality. Before the application of molecular targeted therapy, less than half of patients are treated with HDC-HSCT because of individual differences and multiple factors. Patients with DLBCL who have relapsed within 1 year after HDC-HSCT have a poor prognosis, with an OS of 5.9 months. , Recently, CD19-targeting chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell therapies (tisagenlecleucel and axicabtagene ciloleucel) have been approved for third-line therapy of R/R DLBCL in the United States and Europe. The best ORR and CRR of the tisagenlecleucel treatment are 60% and 32%, respectively. The median follow-up is 14.1 months, and PFS and OS are 3 and 10.7 months, respectively. Axicabtagene ciloleucel has an ORR of 83%, a CRR of 58%, a PFS of 5.9 months, and an OS of more than 2 years. In addition, polatuzumab vedotin is recently a promising antibody conjugate in R/R DLBCL. Polatuzumab vedotin in combination with bendamustine and rituximab significantly increases the CR rate from 17.5% to 40.0% (P = 0.026), prolongs PFS from 3.7 to 9.5 months (P < 0.001), prolongs OS from 4.7 to 12.4 months (P = 0.002), and reduces the risk of death by 58%. However, ASCT and CAR-T–cell therapies offer patients the opportunity for a lasting response. Many patients are not eligible for ASCT and/or CAR-T–cell therapy or are not available for antibody conjugates. In recent years, the emergence of new drugs, such as BTK inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors, has shown efficacy in patients with R/R DLBCL, providing patients with more treatment options. However, the timing and indications of these new therapies also present challenges to physicians.
It is well known that non-GCB DLBCL has a low response to standard chemotherapy and a poor prognosis. Therefore, the addition of new targeted drugs to the classical regimen aims to improve the prognosis of non-GCB DLBCL. Furthermore, by using LymphGen, ABC-DLBCL is divided into 4 gene subtypes (MCD, BN2, N1, and A53), namely GCB-DLBCL (EZB-MYC+, EZB-MYC−, ST2, and BN2). Wilson et al. have found that young patients with MCD DLBCL who receive ibrutinib plus R-CHOP have 3-year event-free survival (EFS) and OS of 100%, while patients treated with R-CHOP alone have significantly lower EFS and OS, which are 48% (P =0.01) and 69.6% (P =0.032), respectively. They have also found that MCD involves exodurative lymphomas (including those in the testes, breasts, adrenal glands, ovaries, and uterus) at a significantly higher frequency than BN2 or N1 (P = 0.004). MCD tumors mainly participate in the BCR pathway by acquiring CD79B and MYD88 mutations, while N1 mutations preferentially target CD79A, multiple negative regulators of proximal BCR signaling, and downstream mediators of NF-KB activation. Both subtypes have acquired mutations that promote BCR signaling and BTK dependence, explaining the treatment response. The study by Schmitz R et al has confirmed the above findings. The ORR of ibrutinib alone in the ABC subtype is 40%, and the PFS is 5.5 months, while the ORR in the GCB subtype is only 5%. Besides, clinical results associated with MYD88 and CD79B or CD79A mutations that respond to ibrutinib exist only in ABC-DLBCL. In a meta-analysis of 8 monotherapies of R/R DLBCL, the combined ORR is 30%, and CRR is 11%–12%. 
Because ibrutinib monotherapy has limited activity in R/R DLBCL, combination therapy might be preferred. A phase 1/2 study shows that ibrutinib, lenalidomide, and rituximab combined therapy for R/R non-GCB DLBCL has an ORR and CRR of 55% and 30%, respectively, and the PFS and OS are 5 and 17 months, respectively. Consistent with our conclusion, the treatment response of the combination therapy is significantly increased. For ibrutinib+rituximab-based therapy, the combined ORR and CRR in our study were 62% and 45%, respectively. The PFS was 7.85 months (95% CI, 5.59–10.12), and the median OS was 10.17 months (95% CI, 8.48–11.85). Ibrutinib in combination with R therapy for R/R DLBCL appears to be promising compared with the CRR (7%) of conventional therapy reported in large international retrospective studies. However, our study showed that there was no statistically significant difference in PFS and OS between ibrutinib combination therapy and monotherapy (P > 0.05). Similarly, a study shows that ibrutinib + R-GDP (rituximab in combination with gemcitabine, dexamethasone, and cisplatin) has no advantage over R-GDP (ORR: 28.6% vs. 50.1%), and the study is terminated because of severe infection events after the addition of ibrutinib. These results indicate that the combination therapy is only a transitional treatment, and its significance is to provide a bridge for CAR-T–cell therapy or other treatments. Among disease types, CNSL had a CRR of 35% and the longest PFS (7.98 months, 95% CI, 5.17–10.79, P = 0.003) and OS (12.35 months, 95% CI, 3.74–20.96, P = 0.024), while the CRR of GCB-DLBCL was only 8%, PFS was 3.10 months (95% CI, 2.11–4.09), and OS was 5.11 months (95% CI, 3.56–6.67). Goldwirt et al have shown that ibrutinib can quickly cross the blood–brain barrier and thus effectively treat CNSL, supporting this conclusion.
The most common adverse reactions during treatment were neutropenia and infection, and the combination therapy was more likely to produce adverse reactions than ibrutinib monotherapy. Meta-analysis has confirmed that ibrutinib treatment of DLBCL has good safety, and our results showed that ibrutinib treatment of R/R DLBCL had a high proportion of ≥grade 3 AEs. On the one hand, it might be related to the poor selection effect of ibrutinib on BTK and the off-target effect caused by it. Recently, the off-target effect of ibrutinib has been determined by independent methods, and learning more about ibrutinib's effects on different target proteins may pave the way for wider clinical use of the drug. On the other hand, most patients with R/R DLBCL are elderly. A randomized phase 3 trial of ibrutinib + R-CHOP in patients with non-GCB DLBCL shows that in patients aged ≥60 years, ibrutinib + R-CHOP is associated with increased toxicity, resulting in worse EFS, PFS, and OS. Therefore, we should pay more attention to it in clinical application.
In this meta-analysis, a random-effects model was employed to combine the data to mitigate the impact of intergroup heterogeneity. Nevertheless, several limitations exist in this study. Firstly, the combined meta-analysis results exhibited a high degree of heterogeneity. This might be associated with the disparities in treatment regimens, whether patients had previously received ASCT, the disease stage, the patient's age, and the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group score. These factors could, in turn, influence treatment decisions and prognosis, and further affect response and survival outcomes. However, it is challenging to obtain complete relevant data for subgroup analysis. Secondly, as the single-arm trial lacked a control group, the comparison between ibrutinib and other treatments was conducted based on inconsistent baseline population data, which led to significant fluctuations. Last but not least, the funnel plots for PFS and OS are asymmetrical. Specifically, there is asymmetry with a cluster of studies demonstrating larger effects. This is highly suggestive of publication bias. Large randomized controlled studies are expected to further verify these results.

## CONCLUSIONS
Taken together, this single-arm meta-analysis suggested that ibrutinib combination treatment achieved higher treatment response, especially in DLBCL subtypes, including CNSL, DEL, and non-GCB R/R DLBCL, supplying more treatment chances, such as CAR-T–cell infusions, autologous or allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. Meanwhile, more severe SEs should be noted in ibrutinib combination therapy. Collectively, multicenter randomized controlled studies are expected to further confirm these results in R/R DLBCL.
