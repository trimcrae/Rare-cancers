## Abstract
Orofacial pain (OFP) affecting the jaw, mouth, or facial area, is a prevalent public health issue globally with a significant burden in low-and middle-income countries (LMICs) due to limited oral health services. The objective of this study is to synthesize evidence on the prevalence of OFP among adults in LMICs and highlight its implications for oral health services and management. This systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. A comprehensive search was conducted across multiple bibliographic databases to identify observational studies reporting the prevalence of OFP in LMICs. Study quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist. A random effects model was employed for meta-analysis, and heterogeneity was quantified using the l2 statistic. Twelve cross-sectional studies were included in the analysis. The pooled prevalence of toothache, a key component of OFP, was estimated at 23.3%. Significant heterogeneity was observed (l2= 99.5%), reflecting the multifaceted nature of OFP and its determinants. The variability in prevalence was influenced by geographical, socio-economic, and health system factors. The findings highlight the significant burden of OFP in LMICs, emphasizing the critical need for improved oral health services and targeted interventions. This study underscores the importance of prioritizing OFP in health policies to enhance management and treatment strategies in resource-constrained settings. Further research is needed to address existing knowledge gaps and develop effective strategies to mitigate OFP’s impact.
Keywords: Low- and middle-income countries, meta-analysis, orofacial pain, systematic review

## INTRODUCTION
Orofacial pain (OFP) encompasses any discomfort or pain situated in the jaw, mouth, or facial area, including the temporal region. To facilitate its identification, the International Headache Society launched the first edition of an international classification focusing on the characteristics of these disorders rather than their location.[ , , , ] OFP is recognized as a prevalent condition impacting a significant segment of the global population. Defined by the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP), OFP includes various conditions such as temporomandibular disorders, trigeminal neuralgia, and dental pain. Its prevalence demonstrates considerable variation across different regions, with some estimates indicating rates as high as 22.7%.[ , ]
In low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), home to over 80% of the global population, there exists a pronounced gap in oral health services, exacerbating the challenge of managing OFP. These countries face a high burden of oral diseases, notably untreated dental caries and periodontal disease, affecting a vast number of individuals.[ , ] Despite the recognized prevalence of OFP and its significant impact, data on its epidemiology in LMICs remain sparse. Previous research efforts have explored the prevalence of OFP across various global populations.[ , , ] However, there is a critical need for a consolidated understanding of OFP’s prevalence within LMICs.
This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to fill the existing knowledge gap by investigating the prevalence of OFP among adults in LMICs. By synthesizing available evidence, this study endeavors to provide a clearer picture of OFP’s epidemiology on a global scale, thereby aiding policymakers and health professionals in these regions to develop more informed strategies. The current scarcity of evidence regarding OFP in LMICs presents a significant barrier to enhancing the management and treatment of this condition, underscoring the importance of this research initiative.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
This systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines[ ] and was registered with the National Institute for Health Research PROSPERO, International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (CRD42021273522). A PRISMA checklist has been provided as supplementary information [ ].

## Search strategy
A comprehensive search was conducted on March 2022 by three authors (PRC-U, RBKU, RB) across several bibliographic databases including PubMed, Lilacs, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Scielo, PsycINFO, and African Index Medicus, to identify studies reporting the prevalence of OFP. The search spanned up to a specified date (March 28, 2022), with no restrictions on publication time, age, race, and gender. The articles in languages other than English were excluded.
The search strategy was built around four conceptual blocks: (1) pain-related terms (e.g., OFP, neuralgias, chronic pain, paroxysmal pain, neurotic pain, dental pain), (2) adult demographics (e.g., men, women, adults), (3) geographical terms targeting LMICs (e.g., Africa, Asia, Central America, Latin America, and South America), and (4) epidemiological measures (e.g., prevalence, incidence). The specific search query used to identify potentially eligible papers is presented in . Relevant studies were imported into Rayyan.
 

## Study selection
Eligibility criteria were set to include observational population-based studies (case-control, cross-sectional, retrospective, and prospective cohort studies) from LMICs reporting the prevalence of OFP among adults. Exclusions were made for case studies, book chapters, conference abstracts, and dissertations.
After de-duplication, three authors (SC, AM, RBKU) independently screened studies based on abstracts and then full texts (other authors) according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Discrepancies were resolved through group discussion or a fourth evaluator (AC) when necessary. LMICs were defined as countries with a gross national income per capita less than $12, 535, where low-income economies are defined as those with a gross national income per capita calculated using the World Bank Atlas, of $1036 or less in 2019; and middle-income economies are those with a gross national income per capita between $1036 and $12,535.
Disease definition: IASP defines OFP as “a frequent form of pain perceived in the face and/or oral cavity. It may be caused by diseases or disorders of regional structures, dysfunction of the nervous system, or through referral from distant sources.”

## Quality assessment
The Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal checklist for prevalence studies[ ] was used independently by two authors (SC and AM) to assess study quality. The checklist’s nine items were rated as yes, no, unclear, or not applicable, with overall risk of bias deemed low, medium, or high based on the number of criteria met. The kappa statistic was calculated to determine inter-examiner agreement on the risk of bias assessment.

## Data extraction process and data items
Two independent reviewers (SC and AM) used a standardized form for data extraction, noting each study’s aim, design, participant setting, sample size, recruitment strategy, inclusion and exclusion criteria, data collection timeframe, pilot testing, measurement reliability and validity, dependent variable definitions, participant inclusion, attrition rates, main findings, and other relevant notes.

## Statistical analysis
Data were organized into evidence tables and analyzed using a meta-analysis based on a double arcsine transformation approach,[ ] employing a random effects model to account for geographic and population discrepancies.[ , ] The analysis was conducted in R version 3.4.1 using the “meta” package,[ ] with results presented as percentage prevalence and 95% CIs.[ ] Heterogeneity was evaluated using the I2 index, Cochrane’s Q statistic, and χ2 test, defining high heterogeneity as I2 >50%. A priori sensitivity analyses were conducted through subgroup analysis to explore the impact of bias risk and OFP type on overall estimates. Meta-regression was used to investigate the influence of the female/male ratio, latitude, longitude, and study sample size on the results.

## RESULTS

## Study selection
The search strategy, employing specific search engines and MeSH terms, initially identified 8,429 titles. After deduplication and screening titles and abstracts, 124 articles were selected for full-text review. Any disagreements were resolved through consensus based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Ultimately, 13 articles were found to meet the inclusion criteria; however, for the purposes of this systematic review, 12 studies were included, and 8 of those, reporting on OFP prevalence in LMICs, were eligible for meta-analysis. The degree of inter-rater agreement between reviewers (Cohen’s kappa statistics = 1) was high. A PRISMA flow diagram is shown in .
 

## Study characteristics
The included studies consisted of 12 cross-sectional studies. These studies reported data from Brazil (seven studies: Cavalheiro et al.,[ ] Bastos et al.,[ ] Ruivo et al.,[ ] Lacerda et al.,[ ] de Pinho et al.,[ ] Peres et al.,[ ] Constante et al.[ ]), Iran (two studies: Momeni et al.,[ ] Kakoei et al.[ ]), Nepal (one study: Manandhar et al.[ ]), Ethiopia (one study: Zebenigus et al.[ ]), and Turkey (one study: Boluk et al.[ ]). All data collection methods included utilizing questionnaires. Sample sizes ranged significantly, from 400 in the smallest study[ ] to 54,367 in the largest.[ ]
Among the findings, one study specifically reported on overall OFP prevalence being 54.75%.[ ] The prevalence of toothache ranged from 14%[ ] to 55.1%.[ ] On the other hand, prevalence of headache ranged from 44.9%[ ] to 85.4%.[ ] None of the included studies provided incidence data.

## Risk of bias
The risk of bias for each included study was evaluated using the JBI tool, yielding an inter-rater reliability coefficient of 0.818 (95% CI: 0.705–0.932). This high level of agreement between raters indicates a reliable assessment process. Out of the studies assessed, six were determined to have a low risk of bias, six exhibited a medium risk, and one was categorized as having a high risk of bias. The primary sources of bias were identified in the domains of condition identification, statistical analysis, and the coverage of data analysis.

## Pooled estimates

## Total prevalence
The analysis of OFP types across studies showed varied reports, with eight studies providing data on toothache,[ , , , , , , , ] two on headache,[ , ] one on trigeminal neuralgia,[ ] and one on general OFP.[ ] Given the clinical distinctions among these types of pain, the meta-analysis focused solely on toothache, as the other categories did not meet the minimum required number of studies for a pooled estimate.
The pooled prevalence estimate for toothache was determined to be 23.3% (95% CI: 15.2–32.6), indicating that a significant proportion of the studied populations experience this condition. However, there was substantial heterogeneity among the studies (I2 = 99.5%), suggesting wide variability in study outcomes [ ].
 

## Additional analysis
Further analyses explored the influence of continuous variables such as latitude, longitude, and the female/male ratio on the pooled prevalence of toothache. These variables did not significantly affect the prevalence estimates, with latitude (estimate = 0.00, P = 0.8517), longitude (estimate = 0.00, P = 0.2629), and the female/male ratio (estimate = −0.08, P = 0.6918) all showing no substantial impact. Additionally, the Egger test for publication bias was conducted, resulting in no evidence of bias (intercept = 0.0756, P = 0.1824), further validating the robustness of the pooled prevalence findings.

## DISCUSSION
The global prevalence of OFP represents a significant public health concern, with a particularly pronounced impact in LMICs. This condition, which affects the face and mouth region, can result from a variety of causes including dental issues, musculoskeletal disorders, neurological conditions, and psychological stressors. The primary aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to consolidate, analyze, and synthesize available research on the prevalence and incidence of OFP among adults in LMICs, with an emphasis on demographic factors such as age and gender, as well as psychological aspects like depression and anxiety.
OFP is influenced by a complex interplay of factors. Psychological stress, for instance, has been identified as a key contributor, with stress, anxiety, and depression potentially intensifying both the perception and severity of pain.[ ] These findings suggest a significant link between mental health and the experience of OFP, emphasizing the need for a multidisciplinary approach to management and treatment in LMIC settings.
The review uncovered a significant variance in the prevalence of OFP across different LMICs, highlighting the role of socio-economic factors, access to health care, and cultural perceptions of pain. Gender differences were also evident, with a higher prevalence of OFP reported among women. This disparity could be attributed to hormonal influences, differences in pain sensitivity, and a greater likelihood of women seeking medical attention for pain issues.[ , ] Furthermore, age was identified as a critical factor, with younger adults more frequently reporting OFP, a trend that may reflect lifestyle choices, dental health practices, and the long-term effects of chronic conditions.[ , ]
This study’s comprehensive search strategy, spanning multiple databases and including literature in various languages, ensures an exhaustive review of OFP prevalence in LMICs. However, the limited focus on incidence and the predominantly cross-sectional design of the studies reviewed restrict our understanding of the condition’s dynamics over time. The reliability of these findings is directly tied to the methodological quality of the included studies, underscoring the importance of a thorough evaluation of each study’s design and execution.[ ]
OFP significantly affects quality of life, hindering daily activities and overall well-being. This review calls for increased research efforts in LMICs, particularly longitudinal studies that can shed light on causality and the effectiveness of interventions. Future studies should integrate quality of life assessments to fully capture the impact of OFP and develop targeted interventions.[ ] Standardizing research methodologies and ensuring comprehensive reporting are essential for enhancing the comparability of studies and facilitating global OFP management strategies.

## CONCLUSION
This systematic review and meta-analysis have provided a comprehensive overview of the global burden of OFP in adults residing in LMICs. By synthesizing evidence from various studies, this research sheds light on the significant prevalence of OFP and its considerable variation across LMICs. The findings underscore the critical gap in oral health services in these regions, which exacerbates the challenge of managing OFP effectively. Policymakers and health professionals in these countries are urged to prioritize OFP within their health agendas, aiming to develop and implement informed, effective interventions that can alleviate the suffering of millions and foster healthier populations.
