INSERM U1059 SAINBIOSE Laboratory, Saint-Etienne University Hospital, Saint-Etienne University, Saint-Etienne, France Correlation has been well established between adverse outcomes and MVP complicated by increasing degrees of mitral regurgitation (MR) and the associated remodelling of the left ventricular (LV). In the absence of MR and LV dysfunction, MVP is generally considered a benign condition. Nevertheless, since the earliest descriptions of the disease, a small but clinically important subgroup of patients has been recognized as being at increased risk for malignant ventricular arrhythmias (VAs) and sudden cardiac death (SCD). 
The estimated annual incidence of SCD in unselected MVP cohorts is ∼0.14% but can reach up to 7% in autopsy studies of young individuals. , Mitral valve prolapse with frequent and/or severe VA in the absence of other established arrhythmogenic substrates is now referred to as arrhythmic MVP (AMVP). Despite advances in imaging modalities and the identification of phenotypic risk features, the overall benign nature of AMVP and its low annual incidence of SCD make it challenging to study and difficult to risk-stratify on an individual basis. 
For patients with AMVP who survive a life-threatening VA, current guidelines endorse implantation of an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) for secondary prevention. , However, information remains limited regarding the incidence rate of recurrent VAs after ICD implantation, as well as the phenotypic characteristics of these recurrences. To address this gap, the present pooled analysis aimed to quantify the incidence of appropriate ICD therapies and to characterize the nature of recurrent arrhythmic events in AMVP patients who received an ICD following a first malignant arrhythmic presentation.

## Methods

## Data sources and search
The literature search was performed in PubMed/MEDLINE and EMBASE databases until January 2025 to identify studies evaluating outcomes in AMVP patients implanted with an ICD for secondary prevention. Two investigators (K.B. and K.K.) independently screened studies using the following Medical Subject Headings: ‘mitral valve prolapse’, ‘arrhythmic’, ‘sudden cardiac death’, ‘ventricular arrhythmia’, ‘defibrillator’, and ‘recurrence’. The search was supplemented by hand-searching bibliographies of retrieved articles and relevant review papers (PROSPERO CRD42024588610). A detailed search methodology is presented in . Cochrane collaboration guidelines were followed, and results were reported according to recommendations outlined in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses document ( ). In accordance with our institutional policies, systematic reviews and meta-analyses do not require Institutional Review Board approval.

## Study selection
Titles and abstracts of studies retrieved through the search strategy and additional sources were independently screened by two investigators (K.B. and K.K.) to identify eligible studies. Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (i) studies involving patients diagnosed with AMVP who had received an ICD for secondary prevention, (ii) explicitly reporting the incidence rate of appropriate ICD therapy during follow-up, and (iii) with a minimum follow-up duration of 1 year. Studies involving primarily surgically corrected MVP (as well as those investigating ablation outcomes in AMVP) were excluded from our analysis due to the lack of specific data on ICD follow-up and arrhythmic recurrence. For each study, we included only patients with AMVP who received an ICD for secondary prevention after documented malignant VA. When subgroup details were not fully available, corresponding authors were contacted to obtain clarifications and complementary data.
Pre-clinical or animal-based studies, abstracts, editorials, reviews, case reports, or case series with fewer than three patients were excluded to minimize reporting bias and small-study effects. The full texts of all potentially eligible studies were retrieved and independently reviewed by two investigators. Any disagreements regarding study eligibility were resolved through discussion with a third investigator (K.V.) to ensure consensus. Authors were contacted whenever insufficient aggregate data were reported.

## Data extraction and quality assessment
A structured data collection form was used for extraction of the baseline characteristics of the study populations and outcomes of interest. Two investigators (K.B. and K.K.) independently extracted data from studies meeting inclusion criteria, including patient demographics, study design, follow-up duration, number/type of appropriate ICD therapies, and types of arrhythmic recurrence. Any disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer (K.V.). The quality of included studies was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS). 

## Outcome measures
The primary outcome was the incidence rate of appropriate ICD therapies in patients with AMVP who received ICDs for secondary prevention. Appropriate ICD therapies were defined as device-detected and treated VAs, including ventricular fibrillation (VF) or ventricular tachycardia (VT), as confirmed through stored electrograms or adjudication at the study level. Secondary outcomes included the proportion of recurrent VAs categorized by type, distinguishing between polymorphic VAs (e.g. VF or polymorphic VT) and monomorphic VAs (e.g. monomorphic VT). To supplement missing or incomplete outcome data, authors of the included studies were contacted when needed.

## Statistical analysis
To conduct pooled analyses of appropriate ICD therapies, the number of events (appropriate ICD therapies) were extracted, as well as the total person-years at risk for each study. When studies reported follow-up duration as median with interquartile range, authors were contacted or these values were converted to mean and standard deviation using previously validated estimation methods for meta-analyses of continuous data. The Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation was applied to stabilize variance across studies. Pooled estimates and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using a DerSimonian and Laird random-effects model to account for between-study variability. Studies were weighted by the inverse of their variance to ensure greater influence of more precise estimates. , Results were subsequently back-transformed and expressed as proportions or incidence rates per 100 person-years, as appropriate.
Between-study heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic, with values >50% considered indicative of moderate-to-high heterogeneity. Sensitivity analyses were performed by excluding studies with a moderate or high risk of bias based on the NOS, as well as by removing potential outliers using a leave-one-out approach. Given the potential limitations of the Freeman–Tukey double arcsine transformation in small-sample contexts, an additional sensitivity analysis using a single arcsine transformation was conducted to assess the robustness of the pooled estimates.
Publication bias and small-study effects were evaluated using funnel plot inspection and Egger's regression test for asymmetry. For summary estimates, P values <0.05 (two-tailed) were considered statistically significant. Data analysis was performed using R statistical software (version 4.2.3) with the ‘Meta’ and ‘Metafor’ packages.

## Results
A total of 9 studies, including 166 patients with AMVP and an ICD for secondary prevention were included in the analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram showing the number and reasons for exclusion of publications from the originally retrieved citations is outlined in Figure . A summary of the main characteristics of the included studies is presented in Table . The studies consisted of five multicentre cohorts and four single-centre cohorts. The pooled cohort had a weighted mean age of 41.8 ± 15.2 years, and a weighted proportion of female patients of 55.8% (95% CI, 47.7–63.6).
 Over a weighted mean follow-up of 4.9 ± 3.1 year, 34.9% (95% CI: 28.1–42.5) of the patients experienced a malignant VA recurrence requiring ICD therapy. The pooled incidence of appropriate ICD therapies was 8.1 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 5.6–10.5), displaying minor heterogeneity between studies (I2: 11.7%) (Figure ). Among arrhythmic recurrences, VF or polymorphic VT accounted for more the half of the cases (53.8%, 95% CI: 44.3–63.0%), while the remaining cases were monomorphic VT ( ). Among 67 ventricular arrhythmic episodes reported in 7 studies with available data, 65 required shock therapy, corresponding to a shock proportion of 97.0% (95% CI: 89.6–99.6%), while only 2 episodes (3.0%) were treated with anti-tachycardia pacing alone.
 

## Risk of bias assessment and sensitivity analyses
Based on the NOS assessment, two studies were rated as having a moderate risk of bias regarding outcome reporting, while seven demonstrated good reporting quality and a low risk of bias. Sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the primary findings: excluding the most extreme study yielded a pooled incidence rate of 7.6 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 5.4–9.8; I2 = 0%), and restricting the analysis to the seven studies with low risk of bias produced similar results (pooled incidence rate 7.4 per 100 person-years, 95% CI: 5.2–9.6; I2 = 0%). Likewise, applying a simple arcsine transformation resulted in a consistent pooled estimate (7.6 per 100 person-years, 95% CI: 5.5–9.8; I2 = 12.3%) (Figure ).
 Publication bias was evaluated using a funnel plot and Egger's regression test, which did not reveal significant asymmetry (Z = 0.892, P = 0.372), suggesting a low likelihood of small-study effects or reporting bias (see , ).

## Discussion
To the best of our knowledge, this pooled analysis provides the first comprehensive assessment of the incidence rate of appropriate ICD therapies and type of VA recurrence in AMVP patients with ICD implanted for secondary prevention. These findings reveal a substantial burden of recurrent VA following the index arrhythmic event, with an estimated incidence rate of appropriate ICD therapies of 8 per 100 person-years. Notably, more than half of these recurrences present as a polymorphic VA (VF or polymorphic VT).
Interestingly, the incidence rate observed in the setting of secondary prevention for AMVP patients appears higher than that reported in other established arrhythmogenic conditions, including Brugada syndrome (∼3.0 per 100 person-years), idiopathic VF (∼2.5 per 100 person-years), or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (∼4.4 per 100 person-years). However, this risk remains lower than the incidence rate observed in secondary prevention settings for patients with arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (∼18.3 per 100 person-years). Overall, these findings reinforce the notion that, despite its low incidence, malignant AMVP is a high-risk condition, marked by a high risk of arrhythmia recurrence in patients who have previously presented with a malignant event. Given the yet suboptimal identification of patients with MVP at risk of malignant arrhythmia, the data presented encourage research efforts for more accurate and earlier identification of this subgroup of patients at risk. , 
Furthermore, VA recurrences occurred slightly more frequently as polymorphic rhythms (54% of VF/polymorphic VT) than as organized monomorphic VT. Although previous catheter ablation studies in MVP patients have explored arrhythmic substrates and ablation outcomes in AMVP individuals with frequent premature ventricular contractions, VT, or VF, no large-scale cohorts have yet reported the prevalence or distribution of specific VA phenotypes within AMVP populations. Beyond regions of maximal mechanical stress such as the papillary muscles, it has been demonstrated that in MVP patients with mitral annular disjunction (MAD), the mitral annulus or MAD region may harbour low-voltage myocardial tissue that serves as an arrhythmogenic substrate. However, these studies did not distinguish between electrophysiological substrates underlying different VA phenotypes. , Notably, a study by the Mayo Clinic investigated arrhythmic substrates in patients with bileaflet MVP, comparing those presenting with VF with those with other forms of VA. The findings suggested that VF was associated with arrhythmias originating from the Purkinje system, despite no identifiable structural explanation for this Purkinje abnormality in bileaflet MVP. It remains unclear whether VF presentations represent a different disease stage or a distinct phenotypic variant. 
Of note, our findings are aligned with those of the recent large multicentre SAVE-MVP study by Sabbag et al., which represents the most detailed phenotypic characterization of VAs in AMVP to date. In their cohort of 225 patients, VF emerged as the dominant arrhythmic phenotype (65%), while sustained monomorphic VTs were less frequent. These proportions are close to the distribution observed in our pooled analysis, reinforcing the concept that AMVP encompasses a spectrum of malignant arrhythmic manifestations rather than a single uniform phenotype. Importantly, Sabbag et al. also identified distinct clinical and imaging correlates of sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia, including older age, family history of SCD, syncope, and the presence of myocardial fibrosis on late gadolinium enhancement, suggesting that patients who present with organized monomorphic arrhythmias may represent a more structurally remodelled AMVP substrate.

## Limitations
Although this meta-analysis includes nearly all currently available studies reporting outcomes in AMVP patients with ICDs for secondary prevention, the overall sample size remains modest. This reflects the rarity of the condition and the limited number of cohorts specifically reporting on ICD outcomes in this population. While the pooled data offer meaningful insights into arrhythmic recurrence risk, future larger, prospective, and multicentre registries are warranted to confirm these findings and to better inform clinical decision-making. Unfortunately, access to individual patient-level data was not available, limiting our ability to adjust for interventions such as antiarrhythmic therapy or mitral valve surgery. Furthermore, while the studies by Aabel et al., Figliozzi et al., and Bumgarner et al. included predominantly patients who received ICDs for secondary prevention following SCD, a minority of patients in these cohorts underwent device implantation after haemodynamically unstable VT. However, despite these differences in inclusion criteria, all studies consistently reported high rates of VA reccurence during follow-up, with low heterogeneity across studies. Rather than drawing strong causal inferences, this descriptive analysis was intended to characterize the clinical trajectory of AMVP patients in secondary prevention and to capture both the risk and phenotype of recurrent VAs.

## Conclusions
Arrhythmic MVP patients receiving an ICD for secondary prevention remain at high risk of recurrent VAs during follow-up, reflecting the persistent arrhythmic burden associated with this condition. Notably, more than half of these recurrences present as VF or polymorphic VT, underscoring the malignant nature of the underlying arrhythmogenic substrate. Although these findings require confirmation in larger, prospective studies, they highlight the need for refined risk stratification tools and sustained longitudinal surveillance in this high-risk population.
