## Background
COVID-19 is a thrombogenic condition. Deep venous thrombosis prevalence was reported as high as 85% in critically ill patients when systematic screening (SS) with ultrasound (US) was adopted. Notwithstanding this, the exact value of SS is yet to be established. Hence, we aimed to systematically examine the odds of mechanical ventilation (MV) associated with DVTs detected by SS.

## Methods
Studies exploring DVT prevalence via US SS in critically ill COVID-19 patients were searched in PubMed and EMBASE, and Cochrane Library. We summarized each group's MV frequency utilizing proportion meta-analysis with the random-effects model and double arcsine transformation. Furthermore, we computed the odds of MV in the DVT group.

## Results
Four studies, including 313 patients (DVT=174). MV proportion was increased in the DVT group, 0.65 (95% CI 0.27-0.96, I2 95%) than no DVT group, 0.44 (95% CI 0.06-0.85, I294%). MV odds were higher in the DVT group OR 2.94 (95% CI 1.30-6.66, I2 28%).

## Conclusion
We found higher MV odds in critically ill COVID-19 patients with DVT detected by US SS. This may indicate a value for SS, at least in critically ill patients. Nevertheless, this exploratory analysis is limited by the small number of included studies, significant data heterogeneity, and absence of adjustment. Thus, the findings are to be confirmed via prospective cohorts where DVT events (symptomatic, asymptomatic, and distal) are monitored for propagation (to proximal DVT or pulmonary embolism) and mortality rates.
