ChandragiriPranay Sai TayalAnshula MittalSaurabh MadanNeha Kawatra TiwariPawan HaddaVijay MohanAnant MadanKaran ] Both benign and neoplastic pathologies can present as abnormal lymphadenopathy and/or a mediastinal mass.[ ] A significant proportion of patients (up to 48%) may have reactive lymphadenopathy, where no specific pathology is identifiable in tissue samples.[ ] In Western Hemisphere countries, the most common cause of mediastinal lymphadenopathy may be malignancy, especially lung cancer. However, in Asia and Southeast Asian countries, granulomatous diseases like tuberculosis and sarcoidosis are common.[ ] Early and accurate diagnosis requires sufficient tissue to be obtained for pathological, genetic and immunological assessments.
The current evidence-based guidelines recommend endosonographic strategies as the preferred initial approach for the staging of lung cancer.[ ] The limited amount of tissue retrieved by endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) might be inadequate for a confident diagnosis of various other tumours and benign mediastinal diseases, wherein a histopathological rather than a cytological sample may be ideal.[ ]
The diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA in non-small-cell lung cancer is approximately 89%, while for sarcoidosis, it is lower, approximately 79%.[ ] A meta-analysis on the diagnostic utility of EBUS-TBNA for lymphoma reported a pooled diagnostic sensitivity of 66.2%.[ ] Many patients may require invasive testing in the setting of a non-diagnostic EBUS-TBNA.[ ] Hence, there remains significant scope for improvement in the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA.[ ]
Recently, a new strategy for mediastinal lymph node sampling has been described: the endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TMC). With this technique, a thin (usually 1.1 mm) cryoprobe is introduced into a mediastinal node under the guidance of EBUS, and cryo-biopsies are obtained. A limited number of studies have described the utility of EBUS-TMC. EBUS-TMC may provide a greater quantity of tissue than needle aspiration alone, with minimal risk of complications.[ ] It was also observed that the additive information provided by the EBUS-TMC increases the diagnostic yield when combined with EBUS-TBNA.[ ] In this systematic review, we summarize and analyse the various studies describing the utility and safety of EBUS-TMC.

## METHODS

## Search strategy and initial review
The authors performed a systematic search of two databases: PubMed and EMBASE, to identify relevant studies on EBUS-TMC (from 2010 to Dec 2022). The following free text search terms were used: (ebus OR ebus-tbna OR ebus tbna OR tbna OR endobronchial ultrasound OR endobronchial ultrasonography OR endobronchial ultrasound-guided OR endobronchial ultrasound-guided) AND (cryobiopsy OR cryo). The authors also reviewed their personal files to identify relevant studies. All the retrieved references were imported into reference management software (EndNote). Duplicate references were removed. References were screened through titles and abstracts. Full texts of the relevant articles were obtained. The reference lists of the extracted studies were also reviewed to identify any missed studies. The following types of studies were excluded: (a) studies that did not report the utilization of either EBUS-TBNA or cryotherapy; (b) studies describing the use of standard EBUS-TBNA for mediastinal lesions; (c) studies describing cryobiopsy of lung parenchyma or endobronchial lesions; (d) studies not in the English language; and (e) review articles, editorials and letters without any case description. Any disagreement between the authors was resolved after mutual discussion. Three authors (P.C., A.T. and K.M.) screened the studies and made the selections. P.C. and K.M. extracted the data which was entered into a Microsoft Excel worksheet for subsequent analysis. The systematic review methodology is summarized in .
 

## Data abstraction
Data from the finally selected studies were abstracted on a data extraction form. The following information was retrieved after a thorough review of the full text: (a) author, (b) year, (c) number of patients, (d) sex, (e) number of centres involved, (f) operator experience, (g) EBUS bronchoscope type and procedure approach, (h) EBUS-TBNA needle gauge, (i) cryoprobe size, (j) cryoprobe activation time, (k) anaesthesia, (l) airway conduit, (m) most frequently sampled stations, (n) method used for creating cryoprobe entry, (o) number of EBUS-TBNA passes taken, (p) number of TMC samples taken, (q) duration of the procedure, (r) complications, (s) rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE), (t) nodal size, (u) sample adequacy, (v) diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA and (x) diagnostic yield of cryobiopsy.

## Assessment of study quality
Study quality was evaluated with the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS) and the Cochrane tool for randomized studies. The MINORS tool consists of a checklist of eight items developed for non-comparative studies and four additional items for comparative studies. Items on the MINORS tool are scored as 0 (not reported), 1 (reported but inadequate) and 2 (reported and adequate), resulting in a total score of 16 for non-comparative studies and 24 for comparative studies.[ ] The Revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2) was used to assess the risk of bias for the randomized control trials (RCTs). This consists of five domains through which bias might be introduced into the result. The domain names are direct descriptions of the causes of bias addressed in the domain. The tool includes algorithms that map responses to signalling questions onto a proposed risk-of-bias judgement for each domain. The possible risk-of-bias judgements are as follows: (1) Low risk of bias; (2) Some concerns; and (3) High risk of bias.[ ] Each study was independently reviewed by two authors (P.C. and K.M.), after which the scores were compared and decided on final scores during a consensus meeting.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using the STATA statistical analysis software (StataCorp. 2017. Stata Statistical Software: Release 15. College Station, TX: StataCorp LLC.) and RevMan (Review Manager [Computer program]). A proportional meta-analysis was performed using the random effects model to calculate the pooled diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TMC. In the random effects model, the observed difference between the proportions is not entirely attributed to errors in sampling. The pooled estimate describes the mean of the distribution of the estimated parameters. The diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA/EBUS-TMC was calculated for the final selected studies for meta-analysis. Proportions with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for individual studies. The data were subsequently pooled to estimate a summary proportion, with 95% CI representing the diagnostic yield of each procedure. Using Metaprop, 95% of CIs were calculated using the score statistic and the exact binomial method. This method incorporates the Freeman–Tukey double-arcsine transformation of proportions. The within-study variability is modelled with the binomial distribution using this method. We also performed a meta-analysis to compare the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TMC using the risk difference method.

## Heterogeneity assessment
The impact of heterogeneity on the pooled estimates of the diagnostic yields and the comparative diagnostic yield was assessed using the I2 (I-Square) test Cochran Q statistic.[ ] I2 is a statistical tool to evaluate the impact of unobserved heterogeneity. It describes the percentage of total variation seen across studies attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance. An I2 value of ≥50% indicates significant heterogeneity. For the Cochran Q statistic, a P value of <0.1 is significant for the presence of statistical heterogeneity.

## Assessment of publication bias
The funnel plot (Egger test) was used to assess for publication bias (statistically significant publication bias when P < 0.1)[ ].

## RESULTS
The initial search yielded 228 study results. No previously published systematic review or meta-analysis on the subject was found. After initial screening, 14 studies (including 869 patients) describing the utilization of EBUS-TBNA or EBUS-TMC in mediastinal lymphadenopathy were identified [ ]. During the phase of manuscript writing, we identified a new qualitative systematic review on EBUS-TMC; however, a meta-analysis was not performed by the authors of that paper.
Of the 14 studies, two were case series, which included fewer than five patients,[ ] five were case series that included five or more patients,[ ] five were single-patient case reports and two were randomized controlled studies.[ ] Out of these studies, cases series and RCTs (with more than five patients) which described the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TMC (including 545 patients) were included in the meta-analysis for calculation of the pooled summary statistics for diagnostic yield of EBUS-TMC and EBUS-TBNA. One case series was excluded from the meta-analysis as it did not state the exact statistical details of the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TMC.[ ] Another was excluded as it included only ROSE-negative subjects who underwent TMC.[ ] All studies were prospective in nature. One case series and both the RCTs were multicentric[ ] and the rest were single-centre studies. ## Technical considerations
All EBUS-TMC procedures were performed by experienced proceduralists. The most commonly used EBUS-TBNA needles were 21G and 22G.[ ] In three studies, 19G needle was used.[ ] A 1.1 mm cryoprobe (ERBE, Germany) was utilized in all studies, except one (Gershman et al.),[ ] where a 1.7 mm cryoprobe was also used in 9 of the 27 patients who underwent TMC. General anaesthesia (GA) along with an airway conduit (laryngeal mask airway) was used for the procedure in two studies (none of the RCTs used GA).[ ] The most frequently sampled lymph nodes were subcarinal (station 7) and right lower paratracheal (station 4R). Methods used for Cryoprobe introduction through the mucosal wall were (a) direct introduction through the needle puncture site (the most used method), or (b) a high-frequency needle knife (used in both RCTs). In one study, EBUS-TBNA needle sheath advancement and laser were also used.[ ] The cryoprobe activation time was 3–4 s. ROSE was performed routinely in two studies[ ] and on selected patients in another study.[ ]

## Meta-analysis

## Diagnostic yield
A pooled analysis was performed from two case series and both RCTs which reported the diagnostic yields of both EBUS-TBNA and EBUS-TMC. The pooled diagnostic yield for EBUS-TBNA was 81% (95% CI, 77%-85%; random effect model; ). The pooled diagnostic yield for EBUS-TMC was 92% (95% CI, 89%–95%; random effect model [ ]. The risk difference in yield was 11% (95% CI, 6%–15%, I2 = 0%) when EBUS-TMC was compared with EBUS-TBNA favouring EBUS-TMC over EBUS-TBNA [ ]. There was no evidence of publication bias on the funnel plot [ ]. Disease-specific yields are shown in .
 

## Safety and procedure-related complications
Minor bleeding was the only complication reported in the case reports (one out of five reports) and case series (four of the six case series). Among the RCTs, Zhang et al. reported bleeding in 87.8% and 84.7% of cases in the EBUS-TMC first and EBUS-TBNA first group, respectively. They encountered pneumothorax in 1% of cases in each group. Pneumomediastinum was seen in 1% of cases in EBUS-TMC first group. Ye Fan et al. reported bleeding in 14% and 11% of cases in EBUS-TBNA plus TMC and EBUS-TBNA alone groups, respectively. They also reported that pneumothorax and pneumomediastinum were seen in 1% of cases in the EBUS-TBNA plus TMC group; however, there was a higher rate of pneumothorax (3%) in EBUS-TBNA alone group. There were no reports of major bleeding.

## Case reports
The data from five single-patient case reports are summarized in .[ ] One case report described the use of GA and rigid bronchoscope as a conduit to perform the procedure. In the remaining (four of the five case reports), GA was not used. Two case reports described the use of a high-frequency needle knife to create an opening for inserting the cryoprobe into the lesion.[ ] In one case report, a 21G needle sheath was inserted into the lesion after the puncture to obtain the cryobiopsy sample. The only complication reported was minor bleeding encountered in one case report.[ ]

## DISCUSSION
In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we found that EBUS-TMC is a safe modality, that adds to the diagnostic yield of standard EBUS-TBNA. As a standalone tool, the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TMC was superior to standard EBUS-TBNA. The safety profile of EBUS-TMC is comparable with EBUS-TBNA, as the only frequently reported complication in both procedures was minor bleeding. Rarely, pneumothorax and pneumomediastinum were also encountered in a minority of patients studied.
EBUS-TBNA is the standard approach for diagnosing mediastinal pathologies, and the diagnostic yield is excellent for the diagnosis of malignant mediastinal lymph node involvement.[ ] However, its diagnostic yield may be variable in mediastinal pathologies including lymphoma. In comparison with histology, cytological samples may be less likely to identify noncaseating granulomatous inflammation, and diagnostic differentiation from other entities is more challenging.[ ] With this background, there is a need for newer sampling approaches to improve the yield of EBUS-TBNA.
In 2020, Zhang et al. reported the first case in which an EBUS-TMC was performed to diagnose a seminoma in a 17-year-old patient.[ ] They reported the use of a high-frequency needle knife to create an entry for the cryoprobe into the lesion.[ ] Subsequently, other authors have described the utilization of the EBUS-TBNA needle insertion point as the entry point for a 1.1 mm cryoprobe into the lesion, to perform TMC, and this is the most utilized method in clinical practice.[ ] A modification of the technique was also reported, wherein the cryoprobe was passed through the TBNA needle sheath to perform TMC.[ ] In one of the case reports, a high-frequency needle knife was used to make an incision in the oesophageal wall to access the lesion and to obtain cryobiopsy, demonstrating that this modality may be extended to endoscopic ultrasound and fine needle aspiration procedures.[ ]
Topical anaesthesia and sedation are important aspects of EBUS-TBNA to optimize patient comfort. Most of the studies reviewed utilized moderate to deep sedation for the procedure. Operators adequately experienced in EBUS-TBNA and cryobiopsy may quickly familiarize themselves with EBUS-TMC procedures. A vascular perforation due to inadvertent freezing/extraction may be associated with serious consequences. General anaesthesia and a supraglottic airway (LMA) may be preferable, especially with smaller lymph nodes. No anaesthesia-related complications were reported in studies where general anaesthesia was used for TMC.[ ]
The most important question regarding the use of EBUS-TMC is the patient population, wherein this modality is likely to be most useful. In the studies included in this meta-analysis, the improvement in diagnostic yield with TMC was prominent for lymphoma, sarcoidosis and benign conditions. The maximum impact in the meta-analysis is from the two RCTs, and both the RCTs showed low yield for benign lesions with EBUS-TBNA (53.2% and 67%), including sarcoidosis, compared to studies and meta-analyses published in the past (order of 79%–83%) and this could have skewed the yield in favour of cryobiopsy or a combined procedure. In the context of sarcoidosis, the addition of endobronchial biopsy (EBB) and transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) significantly increases the yield of the procedure and that may negate the need for a novel procedure like cryobiopsy. Another modality to obtain larger samples from mediastinal lymph nodes is intranodal forceps biopsy (IFB). However, the sample acquisition rate is variable and inconsistent, and the specimen quality is low as crush artefacts are common and forceps malfunction can occur. Agrawal et al. reported a pooled diagnostic yield of 92% of EBUS-TBNA in combination with EBUS-IFB, with subgroup analyses showing an improved yield for patients with suspected sarcoidosis and lymphoma.[ ] However, in our personal experience, samples are obtained with IFB inconsistently, and this modality requires further research. The use of larger EBUS needles has also not translated into a consistently improved diagnostic yield.[ ]
Generally, EBUS-TMC is a safe modality without any major complications as far as the data is available in the existing literature.[ ] We observed that minimal bleeding, pneumothorax and pneumomediastinum (non-life threatening) can be potential complications during the procedure. This warrants caution and careful observation in the procedural and in the immediate post-procedural period, to immediately identify any adverse events. There is a need for further studies to analyse the safety aspects and long-term complications.
Our study is the first meta-analysis to compare the diagnostic yield of EBUS-TMC with EBUS-TBNA. A recently published qualitative systematic review on EBUS-TMC including seven studies (555 patients) had reported the diagnostic yields of EBUS-TMC and EBUS-TBNA (92% vs 80%).[ ] Our comprehensive systematic review methodology identified nearly twice as many studies (n = 14). In our paper, we have additionally performed a rigorous assessment of study quality (separate tools used for case series and RCTs).
There remain many unanswered questions regarding EBUS-TMC. Another question which has not been addressed by the previous studies is the technical failure rate of EBUS-TMC. Usually, a single lymph node or station is sampled during TMC. The safety and impact on the diagnostic yield of multistation TMC remain to be explored. The role of EBUS-TMC in the staging of lung cancer is not clear. The other concern is the cost and the requirements of additional equipment for setting up a facility for EBUS-TMC.

## CONCLUSION
The result of this meta-analysis demonstrates that EBUS-TMC is a safe and promising modality for diagnosing mediastinal pathologies. The indications of EBUS-TMC may evolve further.
