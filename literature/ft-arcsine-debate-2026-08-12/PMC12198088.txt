## Abstract

## Background
Myanmar is estimated to have one of the highest cervical cancer incidence and mortality rates in Asia. There have been limited screening and treatment services for cervical cancer in Myanmar. In this context, to support cervical cancer elimination, understanding the current burden of cervical cancer in the country is crucial. This review aims to synthesize the evidence on the epidemiology of cervical cancer in Myanmar.

## Methods
We used the Joanna Briggs Institute approach for systematic review of observational epidemiological studies to conduct a comprehensive review of burden of human papillomavirus (HPV), precancerous cervical lesions, key co-factors and cervical cancer in Myanmar. Embase, Global Health, Medline, and local databases were searched to May 31, 2024.

## Results
Regional population-based cancer registries from Nay Pyi Taw and Yangon reported age-standardized incidence rates of cervical cancer of 19.3 (in 2018) and 14.1 (during 2013–2017) per 100 000 females, respectively. Meta-analyses were conducted to estimate HPV prevalence among the general female population (18–65 years) as 4% [95% Confidence Interval (CI): 3–5%] in the community-based and 11% [95% CI: (4–21%)] in outpatient clinic screening. Among HIV-positive women, HPV prevalence was reported at 30.1%. The most common HPV types in HPV-positive cervical cancers were HPV 16 (60–85.7%) and HPV 18 (14.8–20%). The prevalence of key co-factors included HIV infection (0.7% in the general female population and 8.3% in female sex workers), tobacco use in females (smoking: 8.4% and smokeless: 24.1%), high parity (4.99 births per married woman) and early aged pregnancy (33 births per 1000 adolescent females).

## Conclusions
The cervical cancer incidence rates reported from two regional cancer registries in Myanmar are high relative to those reported in countries across Asia. An estimated one in nine to one in 25 women in Myanmar is infected with HPV, which is broadly concordant with regional estimates. A high prevalence of key co-factors for cervical cancer, combined with limited screening and treatment services, may increase the risk of progression to invasive cancers among HPV infected women. Consequently, Myanmar is likely to bear a substantial burden of cervical cancer.

## Introduction

Cervical cancer is the fourth most diagnosed cancer type and the fourth leading cause of cancer death in women worldwide [ ]. There are inequities in the burden of cervical cancer worldwide with about 90% of new cases and deaths occurring in low and middle-income countries (LMICs) [ ]. Almost all cervical cancer is caused by persistent human papillomavirus (HPV) infection, which is influenced by several important co-factors, including HIV infection, tobacco smoking, use of oral contraceptive pills, multiparity and early age at first full-term pregnancy [ – ]. Periodic HPV screening and treatment of precancerous cervical lesions effectively prevent progression of persistent HPV infection into cancer [ ]. In 2018, the World Health Organization (WHO) called for a global action to eliminate cervical cancer and, in 2020, WHO launched the global strategy for cervical cancer elimination which supports increased HPV vaccination, cervical cancer screening coverage and treatment access [ ].

Myanmar is estimated to have one of the highest cervical cancer incidence and mortality rates in Asia, with an age-standardized incidence rate (ASR) of 21.4 and a mortality rate of 13.4 per 100 000 females, according to GLOBOCAN 2022 [ ]. Myanmar has a population of 51.14 million with diverse ethnicities residing in different geographical settings [ ]. Most of the population (70%) lives in rural areas, where there are barriers to accessing healthcare services [ – ]. For cervical cancer prevention, Myanmar had made some progress in recent years towards reaching the WHO cervical cancer elimination targets, including the adoption of HPV vaccination into the national immunization scheme and initiation of an HPV-based screening program in limited regions, with an expansion plan nationwide [ – ]. However, cervical screening rates remained low with only 4% of eligible women ever-screened in 2019, as compared to other regional countries, such as Thailand (67%), Malaysia (47%), Vietnam (25%) and Cambodia (17%), according to WHO [ ]. Limited availability and accessibility of cancer screening, treatment and overall healthcare services in Myanmar has also been documented [ , , ]. In this context, Myanmar needs to prioritise its cervical cancer elimination strategies to achieve the targets by 2030, and thus, it is crucial to understand the current burden of cervical cancer. This study aims to systematically review and synthesize available evidence for cervical cancer and to describe its key co-factors in Myanmar.

## Methods

We used the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) approach for systematic review of observational epidemiological studies reporting on cervical cancer and its associated risk factors [ ]. All steps of the review were predefined and documented according to JBI methodological guidance. The protocol was developed as a part of the PhD dissertation, but not registered. We developed the search strategies based on the research questions, the inclusion and exclusion criteria. We conducted searches in Embase, Global Health, Medline via Ovid Platform and the local databases in Myanmar, up to 31st May 2024, with no restriction of language. (search strategies and terms outlined in A1 to A4 in the appendix). We used the JBI tool to assess the quality of included studies and reported the outcomes of assessment (see A 6 in the appendix). We extracted relevant data using a predesignated template from each study and conducted meta-analyses using random effects with Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation to calculate the pooled prevalence estimates of HPV infection and cervical precancerous lesions using RStudio (version 2024.12.1) [ ].

## Results
We identified 21 full text reports on the prevalence of HPV infection and cervical precancerous lesions in the female population, nine nationally representative reports on key co-factors, and seven reports describing incidence and mortality of cervical cancer at population level in Myanmar (see A5 in the appendix for detailed selection process and included studies).

## Prevalence of HPV Infection and Cervical Precancerous Lesions Among the Female Population in Myanmar

Five studies, with an overall total sample size of 49,986 sexually active women aged 18 to 65 years, reported the proportion of infection with any HPV genotype among the general female population in the community-based screening setting (Table  ). The overall pooled prevalence estimate of HPV infection in the community screening was 4% [95% CI (3 − 5%)], with heterogeneity (I2 = 55.7%) (Fig.  ). Three studies were conducted in females with gynaecological symptoms i.e., abnormal vaginal discharge, attending an outpatient clinic setting and reported higher pooled HPV prevalence at 11% [95% CI (4–21%)], I2 = 90.9% (Table  ; Fig.  ). A single study reported the prevalence of HPV infection among HIV positive women as 30.1% (40/133) in Insein, Yangon region in 2019 (Table A 5 − 3). The pooled prevalence estimate of high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions among the general female population was 1% [95% CI (0- 1%)] with heterogeneity (I2 = 0.01%) (Fig.  ). The prevalence of HPV infection in females with histologically confirmed invasive cervical carcinomas was reported in six studies conducted between 2007 and 2016 (Table A 5 − 4) and the pooled prevalence estimate was 75% [95% CI 65% -85%)] with heterogeneity (I2 = 74.9%) (Fig.  ). The most prevalent HPV genotype in invasive cervical cancer was HPV 16 (ranged between 60 − 85.7% of invasive cancers that were HPV positive), followed by HPV 18 (ranged between 14.8 − 20%) (Table A 5 − 4). The other prevalent high-risk HPV genotypes in invasive cancers were HPV 31, 35, 45, 52 and 58.
 

## Prevalence of Key Co-Factors of Cervical Cancer Among the Female Population in Myanmar

## HIV Infection
In 2022, the estimated HIV prevalence among adult women was 0.7%, accounting for about 40% of the HIV infected population in Myanmar [ ]. Approximately 3,400 new cases of HIV-infected women are reported annually in Myanmar, and HIV prevalence among female sex workers in Myanmar was among the highest in the Asia-Pacific region at 8.3% [ , ]. Despite a gradual improvement in access to anti-retroviral therapy in recent decades, there were still gaps in diagnosis and treatment with about 74% of adults and children with HIV receiving anti-retroviral therapy in 2022 [ ].

## Tobacco Smoking Among Women
The prevalence of current tobacco smoking among women aged 15–64 years was 7.8% in 2009 and increased to 8.4% in 2014. It was higher among older aged groups, with 13% prevalence in women aged 45–54 and 21.4% prevalence in those aged 55–64, respectively [ , ]. The Myanmar Demographic and Health Survey reported a significant difference among regions with higher rates of smoking tobacco among women in Kayin State (17.2%) and Rakhine State (12.7%) in 2016 [ ]. Smokeless tobacco is the predominant form of tobacco used among women in Myanmar, with a prevalence of 16.1% in 2009, which increased to 24.1% in 2014 [ , , ].

## Hormonal Contraceptive Use
In Myanmar, 52% of currently married women use family planning methods, of which 14% use hormonal oral contraceptive pills [ ]. It increased from 11.5% in 2010 to 14% in 2015-16, with a higher prevalence of oral contraceptive use among women in urban areas, compared to those in rural areas (18.1% vs. 12.3%) [ , , ].

## Multiparity and Early Age at First Full Term Pregnancy
The number of full-term pregnancies is inferred from the total and marital fertility rates, and early age at first pregnancy from the adolescent birth rates (15–19 years). There has been a decline in fertility in all women from 6.1 to 2.5 children per woman between 1965 and 2014 [ ]. In 2014, the total fertility rate was slightly higher in rural areas (2.4), compared to urban areas (1.9) and varied with geography and socio-economic status, with the highest in Chin State (5.0) and the lowest in Yangon region (1.8). However, in married women, the fertility rate was 4.99 births per woman in 2014 [ ]. The adolescent birth rate increased from 16.9 to 33 per adolescent females between 2007 and 2014 [ , ].

## Incidence and Mortality of Cervical Cancer in Myanmar
The Yangon Population-Based Cancer Registry (PBCR) collected new cancer cases among approximately 7.3 million people from 2018 to 2020. Since 2013, the Nay Pyi Taw PBCR has collected new cancer cases and deaths in about 1.16 million people [ , ]. Nay Pyi Taw PBCR reported that cervical cancer constituted 6.8% (n = 406) of all cancer cases and 13% of all female cancers during 2013–2017. The age-standardized incidence rate of cervical cancer was 14.1 per 100 000 women, which ranked second only to breast cancer (ASR 22.4 per 100 000 women) in Nay Pyi Taw [ ]. In Yangon, cervical cancer comprised 6.7% (n = 895) of all cancers and 12.7% of all female cancers in 2018. The age-standardized incidence rate of cervical cancer in Yangon was reported to be 19.3 per 100 000 women, which ranked as the third most commonly diagnosed cancer in females, after breast cancer (ASR 32.7 per 100000) and lung cancer (ASR 21.7 per 100 000 women) [ ]. Regarding data validity, the percentages of cervical cancer cases that were registered with morphological verification were 90.2% in Nay Pyi Taw during 2013–2017 and 65% in Yangon PBCR in 2018. The percentages of cases for which the only information came from a death certificate were 9.85% in Nay Pyi Taw during 2013–2017 and 24% in Yangon PBCR in 2018 [ – ]. In terms of mortality, the proportion of all female cancer deaths attributed to cervical cancer were 10.3% in the Nay Pyi Taw PBCR (2013–2017) and 10.28% in the Yangon PBCR (2018), respectively [ , ]. A community death survey, which used automated verbal autopsy method in 42 townships in Myanmar, reported the cause-specific mortality fraction of 2% for cervical cancer among females who died in the community during 2018–2019 [ ].

## Discussion
We found that HPV prevalence in the general female population in Myanmar is estimated to range between one in nine women in clinic-based screening to one in 25 women in community-based setting. This range is broadly comparable to findings from previous regional studies, including approximately one in seven women in Southeast Asia and one in 11 women in Asia reported by Bruni et at. (2010), and approximately one in 16 and one in 13 women, respectively, as reported by De Sanjose et al. (2007) [ , ]. The interpretation of the estimate in our review should be made with caution due to some limitations of studies included. Firstly, the studies in our review included older women (mean age of 40 years) which may have resulted in a lower prevalence. Worldwide, the prevalence of HPV is the highest in women younger than 25 or 35 years and declines in middle age, especially in 35–44 years age groups [ , ]. Secondly, the use of convenience sampling methods in all studies limits their representativeness. Thirdly, four out of seven studies used the signal amplification careHPV tests which have lower sensitivity and specificity for HPV using vaginal samples rather than polymerase chain reaction-based DNA assays, and may have reduced the reported prevalence [ ]. We found that HPV positivity is substantially higher in high-risk population, with one in three HIV-positive women infected with HPV indicating that future studies need to explore the burden of HPV infection among high-risk populations in Myanmar [ ]. The prevalence of high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion among general population is about 1%, comparable to those reported in other LMICs, such as South Africa (1.8%) and Vietnam (0.1-2%) [ , ]. The pooled prevalence of HPV in invasive cervical cancer in our review (75%) is lower than that reported in a regional study in 2011 (89.9% worldwide and 89.7% in Eastern Asia) [ ]. Several factors may explain the lower positivity, including older age of patients, misclassification of lower uterine-segment endometrial as endo-cervical primaries and failure of detection of integrated HPV DNA due to assays or biological specimen issues [ – ]. The most common HPV types in HPV positive invasive cervical cancers in Myanmar were HPV 16 and 18, consistent with the reported global and regional prevalences of HPV types [ ].
The likelihood of Myanmar having a high burden of cervical cancer in Asia, could be driven, in part, by the higher prevalence of key co-factors including HIV infection, smoking tobacco and smokeless tobacco use, marital parity and early age pregnancies, compared to other countries in the region. The estimated HIV prevalence of 0.7% among the general female population in Myanmar, remains high among Asia-Pacific countries, compared to the regional estimate of 0.2–0.3% of adults aged 15–49 year in Asia-Pacific, according to UNAIDS in 2022 [ ]. The HIV epidemic was initially considered an epidemic in the general population and is now still concentrated among high-risk population in Myanmar, i.e., men who have sex with men, female sex workers and people with intravenous drug use [ , , , ]. Myanmar has a high prevalence of tobacco smoking among women. In 2014, approximately one in 12 women aged 15–64 years reported smoking, a rate higher than the global average (one in 16 women, in 2015) and the regional average of Southeast Asia (one in 42 women, in 2015) [ ]. Furthermore, the use of smokeless tobacco is substantially higher in Myanmar (one in four women, in 2014) than globally (one in 29 women, in 2020) and in Southeast Asia (one in nine women, in 2020) [ ]. Regarding parity, married women in Myanmar have high parity, despite of overall low fertility in all women. This discrepancy is attributable to exceptionally high percentage of women who never marry in Myanmar (approximately one in seven women at ages 40–44, one of the highest in Asia) [ , ]. In terms of early age at full term pregnancy, Myanmar has seen a rise in the adolescent birth rate, which doubled from 17 in 2007 to 33 per 1000 women in 2014, and is significantly higher than the regional rates for South Asia (26 births per 1000 adolescent girls) and East Asia (7 per 1000 adolescent girls) [ ].
The incidence rates of cervical cancer reported from two regional PBCRs in Myanmar are also high, compared to regional countries. The ASRs incidence of cervical cancer − 19.3 per 100 000 females in Yangon (2018) and 14.1 in Nay Pyi Taw (2013–2017) - were higher than most of the PBCRs in regional countries. For comparison, the ASRs were10.4 in Penang, Malaysia (2008–2010); 11.7 in Khon Kaen (2008–2012) and 12.2 in Bangkok (2008–2010), Thailand; 14.1 in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam (2009–2012); 15.3 in Manila, the Philippines (2008–2012); and 10.4 in Mumbai and 17.3 in Chennai, India (2008–2012) per 100 000 females, respectively [ ]. Both Yangon and Nay Pyi Taw PBCRs reported cervical cancer as the second and third most common cancer in females, accounting for approximately one out of eight female cancers. In terms of mortality, the completeness of death registration in Myanmar is still low, with only 57% of all deaths registered in Myanmar in 2017, according to WHO [ ]. Therefore, the maturity of PBCR data collection, incompleteness, quality of death registration and certification need to be considered when interpreting mortality data in Myanmar. In addition, the two registries cover only 17% of total population and may not be representative at national level. In terms of data validity, the accuracy of cervical cancer diagnosis initially reported by both registries are equivalent with that of other PBCRs in the regions or similar resource settings in Central, East, and South Asia, based on reporting in 2014 [ ]. However, cancer registries in Myanmar need to be strengthened by more years of consistent and sustained collection and reporting of cancer statistics to monitor the burden of cervical cancer over time. The complete and quality death registration is also required to accurately estimate the mortality of cervical cancer and, thus, strengthening the vital registration system is critical in Myanmar.
We identified the gaps of cervical cancer related data in Myanmar. Myanmar needs more granular data for HPV prevalence in high-risk population such as HIV-infected females, female sex workers and populations in wider geographic regions to comprehensively understand the disparity of cervical cancer burden. We highlighted the need for improvement of PBCRs for quality cancer data collection over time and the importance of strengthening civil registration and health information systems to monitor the disease burden in Myanmar. The summary estimates and findings in this review are dependent on the quality of included studies. Most of the studies and reports included in this review were conducted before 2022 and may not reflect the recent changes in risk factors, socio-economic conditions, health service access and provision in Myanmar after 2022. Further studies should also focus on estimating the disease trends over time and the effectiveness of country-specific strategies for cervical cancer elimination in the context of the high burden of the disease in Myanmar.
In conclusion, HPV prevalence among the general female population in Myanmar is broadly concordant with the regional range. Limited availability and access to screening and pre-cancer treatment services may contribute to delayed detection and an increased risk of progression to invasive cancers among HPV infected women. The risk is further exacerbated by the high prevalence of key co-factors– such as HIV prevalence, tobacco use, early age at first pregnancy, high parity– which exceeds regional averages. As a result, Myanmar is likely to have a substantial burden of cervical cancer within the Asian region. The timely and comprehensive implementation of cervical cancer elimination strategies across the country is critical to reducing this burden and accelerating towards elimination of cervical cancer in Myanmar.
