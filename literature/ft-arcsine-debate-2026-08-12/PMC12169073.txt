## ABSTRACT

## Background
Children admitted to the paediatric intensive care unit who require mechanical ventilation are at increased risk for endotracheal tube complications. Increased endotracheal tubes complications can lead to increased length of stay and subsequent increased health care costs.

## Aim
To estimate the incidence of endotracheal tube‐associated infections and other complications in mechanically ventilated children.

## Study Design
Systematic review and meta‐analysis of observational studies. The electronic databases and search machines PubMed, Embase and Cumulative Index of Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL) were searched for articles published 2012 to 2023. We included cohort studies and randomized controlled trials set in paediatric intensive care that reported endotracheal tube‐associated complications (infection, accidental removal, pressure injuries and blockage). Study eligibility assessment, data extraction and critical appraisal were undertaken by pairs of reviewers. Pooled estimates of complications were generated using random effects meta‐analysis. Study quality was assessed using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme tool for randomized controlled trials and cohort studies.

## Results
We included data from 34 studies (12 RCTs and 22 cohort studies; n = 50 359 patients; 232 123 ventilator days). There was substantial study heterogeneity. The primary outcome was endotracheal tube‐associated infection: ventilator‐associated pneumonia, ventilator‐associated events and/or ventilator‐associated tracheobronchitis. Ventilator‐associated pneumonia was the most common endotracheal tube‐associated complication (incidence rate 13.5/1000 ventilator days; 95% confidence interval [CI] 8.2, 22.2) with higher rates in low‐ and middle‐income countries than high‐income economies (incidence rate difference 11.9%; 95% CI 10.2, 13.7). Accidental extubation was the second most common complication (7.4/1000 ventilator days; 95% CI 5.1, 10.7) followed by endotracheal tube‐associated pressure injuries (5.4/1000 ventilator days; 95% CI 1.7, 16.6) and endotracheal tube blockage (5.0/1000 ventilator days; 95% CI 1.4, 17.6).

## Conclusions
Endotracheal tube‐associated complications remain prevalent and contribute avoidable harm. The continued high incidence of ventilator‐associated pneumonia highlights further efforts are needed to reduce burden, especially in lower‐ and middle‐ income countries.

## Relevance to Clinical Practice
Awareness of endotracheal tube‐associated complications will promote initiatives to reduce complications and prevent patient harm.

## Study Characteristics
Table  outlines characteristics of included studies. Included studies were prospective (n = 17; n = 28 570 patients) or retrospective (n = 5; n = 16 346 patients) cohort studies or RCTs (n = 12; n = 5443 patients) published between 2012 and 2023. The studies were conducted in South America (n = 8, 23%), Europe (n = 7, 21%), North America (n = 6, 17%), Asia (n = 7, 21%), Africa (n = 2, 6%), Oceania (n = 1, 3%) and multi‐national (n = 3, 9%). Studies involved 50 359 patients from 18 countries, with a median sample size of 86 (IQR 62, 604.5). Studies were predominately single site (n = 27; 79%).

## Quality Assessment
Study quality and risk of bias was highly variable in the cohort studies (Table  ). In the RCTs, the main risk of bias was associated with a lack of clinician and participant blinding. In trials where blinding had no effect on the control group outcomes a modified version of the CASP tool was used that did not assess blinding. No studies were found to have high suspicion of methodological flaws. Three studies (2 cohort and one randomized control trial) answered affirmatively to all questions in the tool [ , , ]. Studies with small sample sizes (< 20) were excluded from being rated as strong [ ]. Four studies were limited by small sample size [ , , , ]. The funnel plot (refer Supporting Information  ) showed a substantial scatter of study estimates, suggesting heterogeneity and potential small study effects.

## Primary Outcome—ETT‐Associated Infections
The pooled unadjusted incidence of VAP was 13.5/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 8.2, 22.2; I
2 = 97.9%, p = 0.000; Figure  ). Two studies reported VAE with incidence rates of 26.5/1000 Ventilator days (95% CI 17.8, 39.5) [ ] and 5.7/1000 Ventilator days (95% CI 0.9, 274.5) [ ] respectively. Heterogeneity of infection definition limited data aggregation for VAP, VAE and VAT. VAP was defined using Centres for Disease Control and Prevention definitions [ ], the CPIS [ , ] or author defined [ ]. VAP was reported in 26 studies involving 41 669 patients (refer Table  ).

## Subgroup Analyses—Primary Outcome VAP

Studies that reported an average participant ETT dwell ≤ four days had a pooled VAP incidence rate of 7.7/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 1.3,46.0; I
2 = 94.9%, p = 0.000) which was reduced when compared with studies reporting an average ETT dwell of > 4 days (14.5/1000 ventilator days; 95% CI 8.7, 24.4; I
2 = 97.9%, p = 0.000) (refer Supporting Information  ). Studies from low‐ to middle‐ income countries and high‐income countries had relative incidence of 20.7/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 12.9, 33.4; I
2 = 93.7%, p = 0.000) and 7.8/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 2.5, 24.8; I
2 = 97.0%, p = 0.000; incidence rate difference 11.9%; 95% CI 10.2, 13.7) (refer Supporting Information  ). Prospective study design also impacted incidence rate with 17.7/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 10.3, 30.4; I
2 = 98.0%, p = 0.000) compared to 2.6/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 1.5, 4.3; I
2 = 77.6%, p = 0.004) in retrospective studies (refer Supporting Information  ). Study participant numbers had an inverse effect on incidence rates with studies with > 100 participants having a pooled incidence of 6.8/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 3.6, 12.9; I
2 = 98.5%, p = 0.000) in and studies with < 100 participants having a pooled incidence of 25.6/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 17.8, 36.7; I
2 = 80.7%, p = 0.000) (refer Supporting Information  ). A sensitivity analysis excluding the Karakaya study for using the 1998 CDC definition of VAP altered the overall VAP incidence to 13.04/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 7.78, 21.53; I
2 = 97.9%). Studies with a weak CASP rating had a pooled VAP incidence of 16.63/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 7.84–35.28; I
2 = 94.6%, p = 0.001), those with a moderate rating had an incidence of 12.56/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 6.09–25.89; I
2 = 98.2%, p = 0.001) and studies with a strong rating had a lower incidence of 6.67/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 1.58–28.12; I
2 = 98.8%, p < 0.001) (see Supporting Information  ).

## Secondary Outcomes—ETT‐Associated Complications
Accidental extubation was the second most frequent complication following VAP (7.4/1000 ventilator days; 95% CI 5.1, 10.7; I
2 = 87%, p = 0.000; Figure  ). Incidence rates of ETT blockage [ , ] were 5.0/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 1.4, 17.6; I
2 = 50.2%, p = 0.157) and pressure injury [ , ] was 5.4/1000 ventilator days (95% CI 17, 16.6; I
2 = 76.2%, p = 0.015). A single study reported post extubation stridor [ ] with 13 patients (18%) developing the complication across 213 ventilator days. Two studies reported subglottic stenosis [ , ] with a total of 17 cases out of 175 participants (10%), total ventilator days were not included in published data.

## Discussion
In this systematic review we confirm ETT‐associated infections and complications remain common. VAP was most common complication (13.5/1000 ventilator days) followed by accidental extubation (7.4/1000 ventilator days) and ETT blockage (5.0/1000 ventilator days). Although differences in that incidence rates were observed and studies with larger sample sizes (> 100 participants), conducted in high‐income countries or involving short‐term endotracheal intubation (≤ 4 days), the wide and overlapping 95% confidence intervals highlight substantial uncertainty, limiting our ability to determine if differences are statistically or clinically meaningful.
Our findings are consistent with existing evidence which demonstrates VAP in paediatric intensive care exerts a considerable global health burden [ ]. VAP is a significant source of intensive care acquired mortality (attributable mortality 3%–17%) and, in survivors, years lost to disability [ , , , ]. With incidence rates largely unchanged over the past decade, a review of VAP prevention bundles and their implementation is needed. In paediatrics, current VAP prevention recommendations are based on low‐quality evidence [ , ] highlighting the importance of reliable clinical trial data to strengthen intervention recommendations. International studies suggest that multimodal prevention strategies developed by multidisciplinary stakeholders with executive buy‐in and a structured business plan may reduce risk [ , ]. Given the substantial hospital costs associated with VAP (mean attributable cost 51 157 USD per case) [ ], large scale implementation of proven infection control strategies in children should be expedient to prevent harm.
In addition to infectious complications, we found a relatively high incidence of other complications including accidental extubation, blockage and pressure injury. Rates were consistent with published reports (accidental extubation 0.11–2.27/100 ventilator days [ ]; endotracheal‐related pressure injury 0.2%–72.6% [ ] of patients) with the exception of tube blockage where there is limited published data. Such complications typically require additional intervention, increasing the risk of new morbidities secondary to intensive care treatment [ , ]. With increasing focus on prevention and health care costs [ ], multimodal strategies are needed to reduce high complication rates. The use of standardized clinical guidelines combined with education/training may help improve clinician uptake of evidence‐based practices, while additional high‐quality research, and clinical improvement initiatives are needed.
5.1

## Strengths and Limitations
This systematic review provides comprehensive data on the incidence of ETT‐associated complications using rigorous methods, including analysis of high‐quality studies and a random‐effects model to address heterogeneity. We mitigated publication bias through a pre‐registered protocol, a broad search strategy, funnel plot analysis and outreach to authors for additional data.
However, limitations remain. We did not include outcomes related to mechanical ventilation parameters (e.g., lung impedance, atelectasis or long‐term tracheal stenosis) and instead focused on ETT‐related complications during dwell to inform nurse‐led interventions. Data quality was variable, with many studies lacking ventilator‐day reporting, leading to the exclusion of 27 studies because of insufficient data despite contacting 44 authors. Some studies aggregated demographic data from mixed populations, preventing subgroup analysis.
Inconsistent definitions for VAP, VAE and VAT may have introduced misclassification bias, affecting pooled estimates. Wide confidence intervals for some outcomes reflect imprecision, especially in smaller studies. Subgroup analyses did not substantially reduce heterogeneity, likely because of variations in study populations. Further the risk differences (e.g., for VAP in low‐ and middle‐income countries versus high‐income countries) should be interpreted cautiously because of wide, overlapping confidence intervals, which may affect the precision and generalizability of the estimate. Selection bias may have occurred from excluding studies published in non‐English languages because of translation limitations.
Future research should prioritize the development of standardized definitions and comprehensive data reporting to improve precision and comparability. Additionally, complications such as ETT cuff dysfunction, tube kinking and airway injury were underreported, highlighting the need for more robust surveillance of these potentially harmful outcomes.

5.2

## Implications and Recommendations for Practice
This review highlights the incidence of ETT‐associated infections and other complications. The variation in VAP definition highlights the challenges associated with diagnosing ventilator‐associated infections consistently with current surveillance definitions receiving criticism for their complexity and difficult clinical application (i.e., combining clinical, radiological and or microbiological data [ , , ]). Work to reduce the subjective nature of diagnosis led to the new VAE definition however, evidence has suggested this variable is less sensitive than VAP. This lower sensitivity may lead to underreporting, delayed recognition or missed opportunities for intervention.
While our review did not directly compare VAE and VAP definitions, the observed data suggest that VAE criteria may underestimate pulmonary complication rates, particularly when subtle clinical signs (e.g., chest x‐ray changes or sputum cultures) do not meet VAE thresholds. Our findings align with prior research indicating that VAE may not fully capture the complexity of complications in critically ill children. Further research is needed to enhance detection and improve outcomes, potentially by modifying VAE criteria or combining it with VAP‐based assessments. The limitations of imprecision in this review highlights the need for more robust, standardized reporting of complications to enhance future meta‐analysis.

6

## Conclusions
The findings of this review underscore the high burden of ETT‐associated complications but should be interpreted within the context of high heterogeneity and imprecision. Renewed efforts are required to improve consistency in definitions, data reporting and prevention strategies to strengthen the quality of evidence. This review suggests that VAP incidence is associated with increased duration of mechanical ventilation and is more prevalent in low‐ and middle‐ income countries. The current results suggest a need for renewed efforts to reduce VAP and other complications of ETTs. Additionally, the current definitions for VAP are inconsistent and problematic, highlighting the need for new, easily implementable clinical definitions, a challenge well‐recognized globally.
