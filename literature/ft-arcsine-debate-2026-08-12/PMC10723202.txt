## Background:
The prevalence of allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) in Indian asthmatic patients remains unknown. We systematically reviewed the literature for estimating the prevalence of Aspergillus sensitization (AS) and ABPA in Indian subjects with bronchial asthma.

## Methods:
We searched the PubMed and Embase databases for studies from India reporting the prevalence of AS or ABPA in at least 50 asthmatics. The primary outcome of our study was to assess the prevalence of ABPA. The secondary outcomes were to evaluate the prevalence of AS in asthma and ABPA in Aspergillus-sensitized asthma. We pooled the prevalence estimates using a random effects model and examined the factors influencing the prevalence using multivariate meta-regression.

## Results:
Of the 8,383 records retrieved, 34 studies with 14,580 asthmatics met the inclusion criteria. All the studies were from tertiary centers. The pooled prevalence of ABPA in asthmatics (26 studies; 5,554 asthmatics) was 16.2% [95% confidence interval (CI), 12.5–20.4]. The pooled prevalence of AS in asthma (29 studies; 13,405 asthmatics) was 30.9% (95% CI, 25.3–36.6), while the prevalence of ABPA in AS (20 studies; 1,493 asthmatics) was 48.2% (95% CI, 39.6–56.8). Meta-regression identified studies published after 2009 (OR 1.14; 95% CI, 1.02–1.28) and studies with severe asthmatics (OR 1.12; 95% CI, 1.00–1.26) as the only factors associated with higher ABPA prevalence.

## Conclusions:
There is a high prevalence of ABPA in Indian asthmatic subjects at tertiary centers, underscoring the need for screening all asthmatic subjects in special asthma and chest clinics for ABPA.

## INTRODUCTION
Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) is a complex lung disorder caused by immunological reactions mounted against Aspergillus fumigatus.[ ] Usually complicating the course of asthma and cystic fibrosis patients,[ ] ABPA frequently manifests with poorly controlled asthma, hemoptysis, and recurrent pulmonary opacities.[ ] Repeated episodes of exacerbations characterize the natural history of ABPA. If untreated, ABPA can culminate in irreversible lung damage and bronchiectasis.[ ]
India has a high burden of ABPA.[ ] It would be of great interest to clinicians if the prevalence of ABPA is known in asthmatic patients. Unfortunately, the prevalence of ABPA in asthma remains unclear, including that in India.[ ] The knowledge of prevalence is essential as it could guide screening policies for ABPA in asthma or chest clinics. Herein, we systematically reviewed the literature for estimating the prevalence of Aspergillus sensitization (AS) and ABPA in Indian subjects with bronchial asthma.

## METHODS
We systematically searched the published literature to evaluate the prevalence of AS and ABPA among Indian subjects with bronchial asthma. We registered the review protocol (PROSPERO database; crd.york.ac.uk; CRD42022352448) and report the results per the Meta-analysis of observational studies in epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines.[ ] An institute ethics committee approval was not required.

## Search strategy
We (RA, VM, and ISS) searched the PubMed and the Embase databases (since inception without language restrictions) and identified the relevant studies using the following search strategy: “allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis” OR “ABPA” OR “allergic bronchopulmonary mycosis” OR “fungal sensitisation” OR “fungal sensitization” OR “fungal allergy” OR “mold allergy” OR “mold allergy” OR “fungal asthma” OR “Aspergillus sensitisation” OR “Aspergillus sensitization” OR “Aspergillus hypersensitivity.” We also hand-searched the following journals: Indian journal of chest disease and allied sciences, Lung India, and the Indian journal of allergy, asthma, and immunology. We supplemented the search by reviewing our files and the references of the articles included.

## Initial review of studies
The search results were imported into a citation manager (Endnote X9.3.3, Clarivate Analytics, NY, USA). We initially screened the articles by title and abstract and then retrieved the full text of each citation. Three authors (RA, VM, and ISS) independently assessed the articles to assess eligibility for inclusion. Any disagreement was resolved by discussion.

## Study selection
We included studies conducted in India enrolling at least 50 asthmatics irrespective of the study setting and reporting the occurrence of AS or ABPA in asthmatic subjects. AS was defined as the presence of an immediate cutaneous hyper-reactivity to A. fumigatus antigens or raised A. fumigatus-specific IgE. For ABPA, we recorded the criteria used by the different authors.
We excluded: (1) abstracts, editorials, review articles, and case reports; (2) studies describing the occurrence of AS or ABPA in subjects with cystic fibrosis; and (3) studies not reporting the number of asthmatics screened.

## Data extraction
The following information was extracted: (1) citation details and the state from where the study was published; (2) study design (prospective or retrospective); (3) age, sex, duration, and asthma severity; (4) age and sex of ABPA subjects; (5) method (intradermal injection, skin prick test, or A.fumigatus-specific IgE estimation) and the antigen (commercial or in-house) used for diagnosing AS; (6) details of the criteria used for identifying ABPA; and (7) radiological subtype of ABPA (no bronchiectasis, bronchiectasis, or high-attenuation mucus). When studies provided data for both skin tests and A. fumigatus-IgE estimation, we used the higher prevalence reported by either of the methods for diagnosing AS.

## Quality assessment of the studies
We used the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) for cross-sectional studies for assessing the study quality.[ ] Based on the study selection (score 0 to 5), comparability (score 0 to 2), and outcome (0 to 3), we assigned an overall NOS score ranging from 0 to 10. The study was further rated as fair or good when the score was <8 or ≥8, respectively.

## Primary outcome
To investigate the prevalence of ABPA in subjects with bronchial asthma. We also assessed the factors influencing the prevalence of ABPA using multivariate logistic meta-regression.

## Secondary outcomes
To evaluate the prevalence of AS in individuals with bronchial asthma and the prevalence of ABPA in Aspergillus-sensitized asthma.

## Pooled effect
We calculated the prevalence of AS and ABPA in the individual studies by computing the proportion (number of subjects with AS or ABPA divided by the total number of asthmatics) with 95% confidence intervals (CI).[ ] The proportions were first turned into a quantity using the Freeman-Tukey variant of the arcsine square root transformed proportion.[ ] We subsequently calculated the pooled proportion (back transform of the weighted mean of the transformed proportions) using the DerSimonian-Laird random effects model.[ ] We used the statistical package StatsDirect (version 3.3.5; StatsDirect Ltd; Cheshire, UK [http://www.statsdirect.com]) to perform the meta-analysis.

## Heterogeneity and publication bias
We reported significant statistical heterogeneity if the Higgins inconsistency index (I2) was ≥50%.[ ] The presence of publication bias was assessed by constructing a funnel plot with proportion in the horizontal axis plotted against the standard error of proportion in the vertical axis. We also used three statistical tests, namely, Egger's, Harbord-Egger's, and Begg-Majumdar, to evaluate publication bias.[ ]

## Subgroup (univariate) analysis and meta-regression
To evaluate the heterogeneity of ABPA prevalence reported in varying studies, we analyzed the following subgroups: (1) year of publication (before the year 2010 versus 2010-till date); we chose this year because the immunological investigations became widely available commercially in India; (2) study quality (fair versus good); (3) study design (prospective versus retrospective); (4) the geographic zone/region from where the study was published (north, south, east, and west);[ ] and (5) asthma severity (any versus severe). Further, we performed a multivariate meta-regression (using an unrestricted maximum likelihood random effects model) adjusting for the abovementioned subgroups. We used Stata (version 16.0, Stata Corp, College Station, TX) for conducting the meta-regression and reported the results as odds ratio (OR) with 95% CI.

## RESULTS
We found 8,383 records from database searches and 10 from other sources and finally included 34 articles [ ].[ ] All the studies were conducted at tertiary care centers and included 14,580 asthmatics [ ]. Twenty-nine studies were prospective,[ ] while five were retrospective.[ ] Most studies were conducted on adult asthmatics; four included children with asthma.[ ] Nine were conducted on subjects with severe asthma,[ ] while the remaining included a mixed population of asthmatics. Twenty-eight studies were published from the north zone of the country, while two studies each were published from the east,[ ] west,[ ] and south zones.[ ] The median (interquartile range) NOS score was 8 (8-9). Eight and 26 studies were of fair[ ] and good[ ] quality, respectively [File S1, ].
 

## Primary outcome (prevalence of ABPA in asthma)
Twenty-six studies (5,554 asthmatic subjects) reported the prevalence of ABPA in asthmatic subjects. The most widely used criteria for diagnosing ABPA was that of the Rosenberg-Patterson (n = 19); three studies used the ISHAM-ABPA working group criteria, while four studies did not specify the criteria. The studies using the Patterson criteria variably utilized between five and eight criteria for diagnosing ABPA. The prevalence of ABPA in asthma varied from 3% to 41% [ ], with a pooled prevalence of 16.2% (95% CI, 12.5–20.4).
 

## Subgroup analysis and meta-regression
We performed a univariate analysis by calculating the pooled prevalence of ABPA in the various subgroups [ ]. The pooled prevalence was significantly higher in the following groups: published after 2009, good quality, published from north India, and in studies involving subjects with severe asthma. On multivariate meta-regression, after adjusting for other covariates, only studies published after the year 2009 (OR 1.14; 95% CI, 1.02–1.28) and studies involving severe asthmatic subjects (OR 1.12; 95% CI, 1.00–1.26) significantly influenced the reported ABPA prevalence [ ].
 

## Heterogeneity and publication bias
There was significant heterogeneity for the primary outcome, with an I2 value of 93.6%. The funnel plots showed evidence of publication bias [ ]. The statistical tests also revealed the presence of publication bias by two of the three methods (Begg-Mazumdar: Kendall's tau = 0.305, P = 0.03; Egger: bias = −5.45, P = 0.0006; Harbord-Egger: bias = 1.76, P = 0.44).
 

## Secondary outcomes
Twenty-nine studies (13,405 asthmatics) provided data for calculating the prevalence of AS in asthma.[ ] Eighteen studies used intradermal testing,[ ] ten used skin prick test,[ ] one study used A. fumigatus-specific IgE estimation,[ ] and one study provided data for AS using both intradermal test and specific IgE estimation.[ ] Fifteen studies used commercial A. fumigatus antigens,[ ] 10 studies used in-house A. fumigatus extracts,[ ] while three studies did not provide the antigen data.[ ]
The prevalence of AS in asthma [ ] ranged from 3% to 51%, with a pooled prevalence of 30.9% (95% CI, 25.3–36.6). The prevalence of ABPA in AS [ ] varied from 17% to 90%, with the pooled prevalence being 48.2% (95% CI, 39.6–56.8).
 

## Heterogeneity and publication bias
There was significant heterogeneity for the secondary outcomes (AS in asthma, ABPA in AS) with an I2 value of 97.2% and 90.3%. The funnel plots showed evidence of publication bias [File S1, Figures S1, and S2], but the statistical tests showed no evidence of publication bias for the outcomes of AS in asthma (Begg-Mazumdar: Kendall's tau = 0.151, P = 0.26; Egger: bias = −1.57, P = 0.52; Harbord-Egger: bias = −2.88, P = 0.06) and ABPA in AS (Begg-Mazumdar: Kendall's tau = 0.063, P = 0.73; Egger: bias = 0.112, P = 0.96; Harbord-Egger: bias = −0.139, P = 0.93).

## DISCUSSION
We found a high prevalence of ABPA (16%) in Indian subjects with bronchial asthma. The prevalence of ABPA was even higher in asthmatic subjects with AS (48%). While the year of publication (2010 onwards), good study quality (NOS ≥8), the geographic locale of the study site, and asthma severity were associated with higher prevalence on univariate analysis, only the asthma severity and the publication year were associated with higher prevalence on multivariate meta-regression. We have previously published a systematic review on the prevalence of ABPA in asthma.[ ] However, the previous analysis included only 21 studies from across the globe, while the current review includes 34 studies from India alone. Further, in the current study, we have performed multivariate analyses to evaluate specific factors affecting the prevalence of ABPA in Indian asthmatic patients.
The prevalence of ABPA in the current analysis ranged from 3% to 41%. While the wide range could reflect geographic variability, most (77%) studies were published from north India. Hence, more studies are needed from other parts of India to understand the impact of geography on ABPA prevalence. Asthma severity is another crucial factor that can affect ABPA prevalence. The prevalence of ABPA was highest in subjects admitted for severe acute asthma.[ ] Although asthma severity significantly affected ABPA prevalence in the current study, it is essential to remember that ABPA can complicate asthma of any severity.[ ] The year of publication was the other key factor that affected ABPA prevalence. The detection of ABPA has dramatically increased lately due to the widespread availability of immunological tests and is the probable cause for the publication year being significant on multivariate analysis. The occurrence of AS in the present study ranged between 3% and 51%. The detection of AS is vital in asthma as it is the first step in the development of ABPA.[ ] The importance of AS is further emphasized by the high prevalence of ABPA in patients with Aspergillus-sensitized asthma (48% in the current study). The presence of AS in asthma, even without ABPA, may be associated with poor asthma outcomes.[ ]
What are the clinical implications of the current study? ABPA is recognized as a crucial treatable trait in patients with bronchiectasis.[ ] Our results suggest that ABPA falls into a disease category that meets the criteria for implementing a systematic screening program for early detection.[ ] The disease has a high prevalence (16% in the current study) in asthma clinics. Early detection and treatment can reduce ABPA exacerbations, the progression of bronchiectasis, and improve the quality of life. Notably, the currently recommended screening tool for ABPA in asthmatics is a simple blood test (A. fumigatus-specific IgE), with a sensitivity and specificity of 99% and 72%, respectively, making it an ideal test.[ ]
Our study has limitations. The major limitation is the methodological and statistical heterogeneity due to the use of different immunological tests and diagnostic criteria. While most studies used the Patterson criteria for diagnosing ABPA, the studies used varying components (5 to 8) as the exact number is not clearly specified in the criteria. We have previously shown that the diagnostic performance of the Patterson criteria varies depending on the number of components that are used for making a diagnosis.[ ] Also, there were very few studies from the east, west, and south zones. Further, all the studies were reported from referral centers. Hence, the reported ABPA prevalence may not represent the population prevalence. For future research, we suggest that studies use uniform methodology, A. fumigatus-specific IgE (rather than skin test) for detecting AS, and the recently proposed modified ISHAM-ABPA working group criteria for diagnosing ABPA.[ ] The strengths of our study include an exhaustive literature search and predefined criteria for study assessment and statistical analysis. We used the random effects model to partially offset the statistical heterogeneity and performed multivariate meta-regression to explore the causes of heterogeneity.
In conclusion, we found a high occurrence of AS and ABPA in Indian subjects with bronchial asthma. This high prevalence underscores the need for screening all asthmatic patients in asthma and chest clinics for AS and ABPA.
