## Abstract

## Background
The evolution of neoadjuvant therapies for resectable head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) has accelerated with the advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors. While platinum-based induction chemotherapy (ICT) shows modest benefits, emerging neoadjuvant immunotherapy has demonstrated unprecedented pathological response rates (PRR) in early-phase trials. However, significant controversy persists regarding the survival benefits and optimal integration of these strategies into multimodality paradigms. This umbrella review synthesizes meta-analytical evidence to evaluate whether neoadjuvant immunotherapy or chemotherapy confers clinically meaningful advantages in surgical outcomes and long-term survival for resectable HNSCC.

## Methods
We conducted an umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses (SRMAs) evaluating neoadjuvant immunotherapy or chemotherapy in resectable HNSCC (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library; January 2016–March 2025, no language restriction). Critical endpoints included pathological complete response (PCR), major pathological response (MPR), disease control rate (DCR), objective response rate (ORR), treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS). Methodological rigor was assessed using AMSTAR-2 (A Measurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews 2), and GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation) with sensitivity analyses performed for corrected covered area (CCA). The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 for Abstracts (PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts) checklist was followed.

## Results
A total of 10 systematic reviews with meta-analyses were included, with a cumulative patient population of 15,871. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy achieved PCR rates of 26.7–30.1%. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy yielded the highest downstaging rate of 93.3% (95% confidence interval [CI], 68.1–99.8%) and ORR of 57.0% (95%CI, 44.0–72.0%). Neoadjuvant immunotherapy showed 1-year OS of 84.0–89.7%, though 3-year OS converged to 78.9% (95%CI, 63.2–89.1%). ICT showed marginal benefits in locoregional control (hazard ratio [HR], 0.59; 95%CI, 0.42–0.85) but had limited impact on overall survival (HR, 1.16; 95%CI, 1.07–1.26). However, these efficacy benefits must be balanced against a significant risk of Grade 3–4 TRAEs of 9.7–35.0%.

## Conclusions
Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy offers superior pathological and early survival outcomes in resectable HNSCC, supporting its integration into treatment algorithms. However, the lack of high-quality evidence regarding this treatment limits definitive conclusions. Further trials are needed to validate long-term benefits and guide biomarker-driven patient selection.

## Background
Resectable head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) presents a therapeutic challenge, where neoadjuvant strategies aim to improve surgical outcomes and survival [ – ]. While platinum-based induction chemotherapy (ICT) has historically been explored [ ], its benefits remain debated, with modest survival gains and no clear impact on locoregional control.

Recent immunotherapy advances, particularly with anti-PD-(L)1 agents, have transformed neoadjuvant treatment paradigms. Presurgical nivolumab in resectable oral cancer has shown an objective response rate (ORR) of 33.0% [ ]. An emerging Phase 3 study published after our search cutoff (KEYNOTE-689) confirms survival benefits for perioperative pembrolizumab, with an event-free survival (EFS) benefit (hazard ratio [HR], 0.73; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.58–0.92) and 3.0% higher pathological complete response (PCR) rate in pembrolizumab group with major pathological response (MPR) improvement of 9.3% [ ]. However, comparative evidence on regimen efficacy remains inconsistent. Yu et al. reported superior ORR with neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy versus immunotherapy alone (64.0% vs. 12.0%; P = 0.002) [ ], whereas Wang et al. found no ORR difference between neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy and immunotherapy (57.0% vs. 42.0%; P = 0.12) [ ]. These discrepancies underscore a fragmented evidence base.

Critically, existing systematic reviews with meta-analyses (SRMAs) suffer from three key limitations: high heterogeneity in included study designs and patient cohorts, insufficient direct comparison of multimodal strategies (e.g., immunotherapy vs. neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy vs. neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy), and inadequate assessment of long-term survival outcomes such as 3-year overall survival (OS). This umbrella review addresses these gaps by synthesizing meta-analytical evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and single-arm studies to evaluate neoadjuvant immunotherapy versus chemotherapy in resectable HNSCC. To ensure methodological rigor, we prospectively registered the protocol (PROSPERO: CRD420251035540), adhered to PRISMA 2020 guidelines, and employed A Measurement Tool to Assess systematic Reviews 2 (AMSTAR-2), Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) and evidence-grading frameworks to appraise meta-analyses. By stratifying outcomes by treatment modality and assessing evidence quality, this work establishes a hierarchy of therapeutic efficacy and safety to guide clinical decision-making.

## Methods

## Protocol and reporting
The protocol of this umbrella review was prospectively defined and registered on the PROSPERO website (ID: CRD420251035540) (https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/) [ ]. This review is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement and a completed PRISMA checklist is provided (Additional file 1) [ ].

## Study search and selection
We performed a comprehensive systematic literature search without restriction on language of publication. Four key electronic databases including PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were searched from 2016 to March 2025. Moreover, we additionally screened the reference lists of included studies to avoid potential omissions. The search strategy combined terms associated with Neoadjuvant Therapy, Head and Neck Neoplasms, and HNSCC, and systematic review or meta-analysis (full search strategy is available in Additional file 2: Table S1). This umbrella review focused on neoadjuvant immunotherapy (primarily anti-PD-(L)1 agents) and chemotherapy. Broader regimens were included to capture the evolving evidence base.
Three reviewers (FYF, QWM, and CCH) independently screened studies according to predefined inclusion criteria based on the population, intervention, comparison, outcome, and study design (PICOS) strategy (Table  ). Discrepancies were resolved by consensus.

 Exclusion criteria:
Reviews not involving neoadjuvant immunotherapy or chemotherapy.
Meta-analyses evaluating immunotherapies not including PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.
Laboratory or animal studies.
Systematic reviews without quantitative synthesis.
Meta-analyses containing fewer than 3 included studies.

## Data extraction
Three reviewers (FYF, QWM, and CCH) screened the titles and abstracts, assigning unique identification numbers to all the included articles. Two reviewers (FYF and QWM) independently extracted data using a predesigned form. Extracted data included study characteristics (authors, publication year, sample size), pooled effect estimates (odds ratios [OR], hazard ratios [HR], and proportions), heterogeneity (I2, Cochran’s Q P-value), and publication bias assessments (Egger’s test, funnel plots, when reported).
Subgroup findings (e.g., HPV status, tumor location, treatment modality) were also documented. Any discrepancies were resolved by discussion with a third reviewer (LYZ). Missing data were sought via author contact or transformed using Freeman-Tukey methods [ , ], prioritizing individual participant data.

## Methodological quality appraisal and evidence grading
The methodological quality of SRMAs was assessed using AMSTAR-2 [ ], with final ratings categorized as high, moderate, low, or critically low. For the body of evidence addressing key outcomes from pairwise meta-analyses, we applied the GRADE framework [ ] to evaluate the certainty of evidence, which was graded as high, moderate, low, or very low. This assessment considered factors including risk of bias, imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness, and publication bias. In parallel, the strength of evidence from pairwise meta-analyses was also classified as convincing (class 1), highly suggestive (class 2), suggestive (class 3), or weak (class 4) based on pre-specified criteria [ , ]: statistical significance, sample size, heterogeneity, small-study effects, and excess significance bias (Additional file 2: Table S2). Small-study effects were assessed via Egger’s regression [ ], and excess significance bias was evaluated by comparing observed versus expected statistically significant results [ ].

## Sensitivity analysis
To account for overlap of primary studies across SRMAs, we constructed a co-citation matrix and calculated the corrected covered area (CCA). Overlap was categorized into four tiers (slight to very high)(Additional file 2: Table S3) [ ]. In cases of very high overlap (≥ 15.0%), prioritization was based on (a) AMSTAR-2 quality rating, (b) recency of publication, (c) comprehensiveness of effect estimates, and (d) number of included studies or participants. Lower-priority reviews were excluded in sensitivity analyses to assess robustness [ ].

## Statistical analysis
We summarized effect sizes (OR, HR, pooled proportions) as reported in eligible SRMAs. All HRs are presented as experimental arm vs. control arm. HR < 1.00 favors the experimental arm, indicating a reduction in the hazard of the event (e.g., OS). Conversely, an HR > 1.00 favors the control arm. To ensure consistency, numerical formatting was standardized (percentages to one decimal place, OR/HR to two decimals). We did not conduct de novo meta-analyses; instead, results were tabulated and synthesized narratively. All data handling, statistical summaries, and figure generation were performed using R 4.4.2. Additionally, REVMAN version 5.4 was utilized to verify the statistical outputs reported in the included meta-analyses.

## Results

## Selection and characteristics of the included meta-analyses
We retrieved a total of 1217 records from databases and registers (Fig.  ). After removing duplicates (n = 263), the title and abstract of 954 records were screened against including criteria, and 938 records were excluded. The full-text analysis was conducted on the remaining 16 records, 6 records were excluded, and no additional record was added through citation searching of reference lists. The list of excluded studies with reasons for exclusion is detailed in Additional file 2: Table S4. Ultimately, 10 systematic reviews with meta-analyses were included for data extraction and further analysis (Additional file 2: Table S5). The descriptive characteristics of included meta‐analyses under 5 major outcome domains (pathological efficacy, clinical efficacy and disease control, surgical outcomes, safety and long‐term survival and prognosis) are summarized in Additional file 2: Table S6 and Additional file 2: Table S7.
 Outcomes were unevenly distributed across five domains: safety (≥ Grade 3 treatment-related adverse events [TRAE]; n = 6), pathological efficacy (PCR; n = 5), clinical efficacy and disease control (disease control rate [DCR]; n = 4), long-term survival and prognosis (progression-free survival [PFS]; n = 3), and surgical outcomes (surgical delayed rate, n = 3). In total, 143 meta-analytical associations were included in this umbrella review. More detailed data are presented in Additional file 2: Table S8 and Additional file 2: Table S9.

## Methodological quality assessment
One meta‐analyses (10.0%) demonstrated high methodological confidence [ ], 7 (70.0%) low quality, and 2 (20.0%) critically low (Additional file 2: Table S10) [ , ]. Critical limitations included absent a priori protocols (n = 6) and unanalyzed publication bias (n = 5). Non-critical issues involved incomplete rationale for study design inclusions (n = 1).

## HNSCC-related evidence from single-arm meta-analyses

## Pathological efficacy
Neoadjuvant immunotherapy demonstrated PCR rates of 2.9–15.3% in three trials [ , , ], with Cao et al. reporting 37.0% (95%CI, 33.0%–42.0%; P = 0.39) [ ] in Additional file 2: Table S8 and Table  . MPR rate ranged from 9.7 to 30.7% (P < 0.05), and pathological response rate (PRR) was 55.0% (95%CI, 48.0%–63.0%) in one study [ ]. Subgroup analyses showed significant PCR variation by regimen (P < 0.00001) [ ] in Additional file 2: Table S8: neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy achieved 26.7–30.1% vs 1.4–3.0% for monotherapy. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy yielded superior MPR rate of 75.7% (95%CI, 58.2–89.6%), partial pathological response (PPR) rate of 97.0% (95%CI, 77.2–100.0%), and clinical-to-pathological downstaging rate of 93.3% (95%CI, 68.1–99.8%). No significant heterogeneity was observed in PRR across regimens (P = 0.21).

 

## Clinical efficacy and disease control
ORR to neoadjuvant immunotherapy were 39.0% (95%CI, 29.0–54.0%) [ ], with a DCR of 88.3% (95%CI, 78.1–95.7%) [ ]. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy showed the highest ORR of 61.0% (95%CI, 42.0–79.0%). Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy showed higher ORR of 57.0% (95%CI, 44.0–72.0%) and highest DCR, whereas anti-PD-(L)1 + anti-KIR-2 had the lowest efficacy (ORR, 11.0%; 95%CI, 4.0–30.0%) (Additional file 2: Table S8).

## Surgical outcomes and safety
The surgical complications rate was 0.4% (95%CI, 0.0–1.7%; P = 0.37), and the surgical delay rate ranged from 0.0 to 2.8%. There was no significant difference in the treatment regimens for surgical complications and surgical delays (Table  ). Yu et al. reported that all Grade TRAEs after neoadjuvant immunotherapy were as high as 82.6% (95%CI, 65.8–94.5%) [ ], but ≥ Grade 3 TRAEs accounted for only 9.7% (95%CI, 3.3–18.8%). The results for ≥ Grade 3 TRAEs in the remaining studies were relatively consistent, ranging from 8.4 to 35.0%, and there was no significant difference in treatment regimens (Additional file 2: Table S8 and Table  ).

 

## Long-term survival and prognosis
Neoadjuvant immunotherapy yielded 1-year OS of 89.7% (95%CI, 72.4–96.6%), decreasing to 78.9% (95%CI, 63.2–89.1%) at 3 years. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy combinations achieved the highest 1-year disease-free survival (DFS) of 89.8% (95%CI, 81.4–94.7%), with robust 1-year PFS and 1-year DFS rates across regimens (Table  ).

## HNSCC-related evidence from pairwise meta-analyses
Three pairwise meta-analyses [ , , ] (20 associations) compared ICT with surgery alone in resectable HNSCC (Additional file 2: Table S9). No associations met criteria for strong evidence; 50.0% (n = 10) were classified as weak and 50.0% (n = 10) nonsignificant. Significant heterogeneity (I2 > 50.0%) was observed in 55.0% (n = 11) of analyses (Additional file 2: Table S7). For clinical efficacy and disease control, 40.0% (n = 8) of studies assessing ICT demonstrated improvement in locoregional control (HR, 0.59; 95%CI, 0.42–0.85), but increased distant recurrence risk (HR, 1.16; 95%CI, 1.06–1.26). Among long-term survival analyses (45.0%, n = 9), two indicated reduced OS with ICT: HR, 1.16; 95%CI, 1.07–1.26 and HR, 1.30; 95%CI, 1.16–1.46 for laryngeal surgery subgroups (Table  ).

 

## Subgroup findings
To further investigate potential heterogeneity, subgroup analyses were performed based on HPV status and tumor location, with the subgroup outcomes for treatment method discussed in preceding sections.
Subgroup analyses stratified by HPV status and tumor location revealed no significant heterogeneity in outcomes for neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Neither pathological efficacy (PCR, MPR, PPR, clinical-to-pathological downstaging rate), clinical efficacy (ORR, DCR), nor surgical safety (surgical complications, surgical delays, TRAEs) differed by HPV status (P > 0.05) or tumor location (P > 0.05)(Additional file 2: Tables S11). In contrast, ICT demonstrated differential survival outcomes by anatomical site. While no tumor location-based heterogeneity was observed in death (P = 0.38), significant variations emerged in OS and DFS (P < 0.05)(Additional file 2: Table S11). For OS, ICT was associated with significantly reduced survival in laryngeal cancers (HR, 1.39; 95%CI, 1.10–1.77), with no significant detriment in hypopharyngeal (HR, 1.12; 95%CI, 0.91–1.38) or oral cavity tumors. Conversely, DFS improvements were most pronounced in laryngeal cancers (HR, 0.91; 95%CI, 0.88–0.95), whereas hypopharyngeal cancers showed inferior DFS (HR, 1.35; 95%CI, 1.03–1.78).

## Sensitivity analysis
Based on the CCA analysis, we identified 7 studies with very-high overlap (Additional file 2: Table S12). After reprioritizing studies across different outcomes and indicators, all data from Masarwy2021 were excluded. The remaining studies underwent partial exclusions as well. Post-sensitivity analysis evaluation demonstrated considerable robustness in most findings (Additional file 2: Table S13). However, notable divergence emerged regarding ORR across therapeutic regimens.
Before excluding studies with very-high overlap, neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy demonstrated the highest ORR of 61.0% (95%CI, 42.0–79.0%). Following sensitivity analysis, neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy showed the highest ORR of 57.0% (95%CI, 44.0–72.0%), while neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy yielded an ORR of 56.0% (95%CI, 21.1–87.8%).
This reversal in treatment efficacy ranking suggests no substantial difference between the two regimens (evidenced by overlapping confidence intervals). Nevertheless, both regimens outperformed other therapies in ORR outcomes after accounting for study overlap bias.

## Discussion
This umbrella review demonstrates that for HNSCC, neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy achieved PCR rate of 26.7–30.1%, neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy optimized downstaging rate of 93.3% and PPR of 97.0%, at the cost of Grade 3–4 TRAEs of 29.0%. Critically, HPV-negative patients show attenuated 3-year OS (64.0% vs. 100.0% in HPV-positive), while anatomical stratification reveals that the DFS benefit from ICT was confined to laryngeal tumors (HR, 0.91) but inferior DFS in hypopharyngeal cancers (HR, 1.35). These findings define evidence-based therapeutic hierarchies and highlight unmet needs in high-risk cohorts requiring benefit-risk optimization.

## Main findings from single-arm meta-analyses
Single-arm meta-analyses [ , , , ] supported that neoadjuvant immunotherapy improves PCR, clinical efficacy, surgical feasibility, and long-term survival in resectable HNSCC. Comparative analyses [ , ] revealed significant heterogeneity in PCR by treatment regimens (I2 = 75.8–91.8%).
Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy achieved superior PCR rates (26.7–30.1%) versus anti-PD-(L)1 alone (1.4–3.0%), potentially through chemotherapy-induced immunogenic cell death, which enhances tumor antigen release, dendritic cell activation, and T-cell priming [ – ]. Notably, although conventional ICT achieves an ORR of 55.0–65.0%, this has consistently failed to translate into survival benefits [ , ]. In contrast, our study demonstrates that neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy not only yields a PCR rate of 30.1% but also delivers a 1-year DFS rate of 89.8%, suggesting its potential to overcome the limitations of traditional therapies.
All Grade TRAEs occurred in 82.6% (95%CI, 65.8–94.5%) of patients, with ≥ Grade 3 events in 9.7% (95%CI, 3.3–18.8%) and minimal surgery delays (0.0–2.8%). Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 therapy had the lowest ≥ Grade 3 TRAEs of 9.4% (95%CI, 0.9–25.4%), versus 27.0% (95%CI, 14.0–53.0%) for dual checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-(L)1 + anti-CTLA-4), reflecting CTLA-4’s broad immune activation [ ]. Dual regimens warrant restricted use pending risk–benefit refinement.
In this umbrella review, particular attention was paid to patients’ long-term survival and prognosis. By converting data from different indicators and redrawing the actual data in the form of proportion, we reanalyzed the major studies of these meta-analyses. The observed decline in OS from the 1-year rates (84.0–90.0%) to the 3-year rate of 78.9% warrants careful consideration. While direct comparison between the 1-year [ ] and 3-year data [ ] points is limited by their derivation from distinct study cohorts, this pattern nonetheless suggests potential challenges in sustaining the initial therapeutic benefit over the longer term. The CheckMate 358 study [ ] provides compelling evidence, demonstrating a 3-year OS difference of 100.0% for HPV-positive patients versus 64.0% for HPV-negative patients. This dramatic divergence strongly suggests that the overall 3-year OS rate (79.0%) reported likely reflects a weighted average heavily influenced by the poorer prognosis associated with HPV-negative disease within the analyzed cohort. HPV-negative HNSCC is biologically distinct, often characterized by higher mutational burden but also greater aggressiveness and intrinsic resistance mechanisms [ ]. Besides, while neoadjuvant immunotherapy can induce potent anti-tumor immune responses, the development of acquired resistance mechanisms over time is a recognized challenge. Tumors may evolve to evade immune surveillance through various pathways, such as alterations in antigen presentation, upregulation of immunosuppressive checkpoints (beyond the initial target, e.g., PD-1, CTLA-4), or fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [ ]. This could lead to disease progression in patients who initially responded.
Subgroup analysis of treatment methods indicated that the optimal postoperative survival outcomes were achieved when neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy was used, with a 1-year DFS rate as high as 89.8% (95%CI, 81.4–94.7%). This was consistent with the previous study [ ] reporting a 1-year DFS rate of 95.8% (95%CI, 73.9–99.4%). This might be attributed to early systemic therapy potentially reducing the risk of distant metastasis and possibly enhancing OS.

## Main findings from pairwise meta-analyses
The main focus of the pairwise meta-analyses was to evaluate the therapeutic effect of ICT as a preoperative adjuvant treatment for HNSCC. However, we did not observe high suggestive evidence, with only 10 associations showing weak evidence and the remaining associations not being statistically significant. The reason for the weak evidence in 30.0% of cases was attributed to insufficient sample size (number of participants < 1000). Although our results were not sufficient to strongly support our research findings, we still divided the weak evidence into three main categories: (i) clinical efficacy and disease control, (ii) surgical outcomes, and (iii) long-term survival and prognosis.
No significant differences were observed in local recurrence, locoregional control, or laryngeal preservation. However, in terms of distant metastasis, it was demonstrated that ICT was beneficial for patients with locally advanced and resectable laryngeal cancer, with an 11.7% lower rate of distant metastasis [ ]. However, Lacas B et al. reported a HR for distant metastasis of 0.76 (95%CI, 0.66–0.88) [ ], indicating a 4.1% reduction at 5 years, while no significant difference was observed in locoregional control (HR, 1.07; 95%CI, 0.99–1.15). It may be due to the fact that ICT, as a systemic therapy, can eliminate occult micrometastases that are present at diagnosis but not detected by imaging [ ]. In contrast, local recurrence is primarily influenced by subsequent local treatments such as surgery, and if local treatment is sufficiently effective, the additional benefit of ICT may be limited [ ].
Gao et al. reported that patients receiving ICT did not offer an improved OS in HNSCC (HR, 1.16; 95%CI, 1.07–1.26) [ ]. However, this detrimental effect was not universally observed. Ashley et al. found no significant OS difference between treatments (HR, 0.96; 95%CI, 0.83–1.10) [ ], possibly due to differences in sample size: Pei Gao’s study included 674 patients, while Ashley Lau’s study included 2746 patients. Notably, the largest study in the meta-analysis was nominally statistically significant. Therefore, more large-scale RCTs and/or extensive meta-analyses are required to further confirm these findings.
The KEYNOTE-689 trial (post-dating our search) confirmed EFS benefits with perioperative pembrolizumab while reinforcing critical observations from our analysis. Hypopharyngeal tumors showed unfavorable EFS (HR, 2.55), echoing our reported site-specific survival heterogeneity (hypopharyngeal DFS HR, 1.35 vs. laryngeal HR, 0.91). The 10.7% local progression rate in the pembrolizumab arm further underscores resistance mechanisms in high-risk subgroups. These findings affirm site-dependent efficacy, urging biomarker-driven dual-modality strategies for resistant anatomic subtypes.

## Strengths
Firstly, one of our strengths is that we comprehensively summarized the current evidence by reviewing previous meta-analyses on the associations between various health outcomes and neoadjuvant immunotherapy or chemotherapy for resectable HNSCC. Secondly, our protocol was registered in PROSPERO, ensuring transparency and robustness of planned analyses and results. We used the established AMSTAR-2 tools and evidence-grading standards to assess methodological quality and evidence classification. By integrating fragmented evidence into a hierarchy of efficacy and safety, our study informed guideline-making and trial design, highlighting the need for practical RCTs comparing modern neoadjuvant regimens.

## Limitations
Our umbrella review design, which exclusively incorporated SRMAs, introduces inherent methodological constraints. By relying on pre-aggregated evidence, we inevitably excluded recent high-quality primary studies (e.g., KEYNOTE-689) published after the search dates of included SRMAs. Our conclusions are consequently constrained by the methodological choices, analytical biases (e.g., publication bias, selective outcome reporting), and limitations of the source SRMAs, wherein errors may cascade into this review. Moreover, to maintain completeness, certain SRMAs with lower methodological confidence (e.g., Chen et al., 2023) were retained. Their influence was evaluated using CCA and sensitivity analyses, which confirmed that their inclusion did not materially affect the overall conclusions. This approach ensures methodological transparency and supports the robustness of our findings. Limited patient numbers for key endpoints, especially in immunotherapy subgroups and rare histologic subtypes, along with a lack of large-scale RCTs that directly compare modern neoadjuvant regimens, restrict the generalizability of the conclusions.

## Conclusions
Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + chemotherapy achieves superior PCR of 26.7–30.1% and 1-year DFS (89.8%) with manageable toxicity (≥ Grade 3 TRAEs: 9.7–35.0%), supporting its role in micrometastatic eradication. Neoadjuvant anti-PD-(L)1 + radiotherapy optimizes surgical downstaging but requires validation in borderline-resectable cases.
