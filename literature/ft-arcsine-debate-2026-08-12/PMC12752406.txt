## Background
Globally, about 1.9 million stillbirths occurred at the end of 2021. This scenario has been worst in Sub-Saharan African countries, which contribute about 47% of the total stillbirths. In Ethiopia, though there was a previous study on the rate of stillbirths and its associated factors, it only explored the effect of antenatal care visits and previous stillbirth history. Yet, the meta-effects of other contributing factors such as antepartum hemorrhage, meconium-staining, labor duration, body weight, hypertension, prematurity, and premature rupture of membranes have not been investigated. Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to estimate the rate of stillbirths and identify its associated factors in Ethiopia.

## Methods
PubMed, HINARI, Science Direct, and other sources including Google Scholar and African Journals Online were searched using different search engines. Critical appraisal was done using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI 2020) checklist. The heterogeneity among studies was tested using I-squared statistics. Publication bias was tested using a funnel plot visual inspection and Begg’s test. The results were presented using forest plots and tables. The random effect model was fitted to estimate the pooled rate of stillbirths and odds ratio of factors with a 95% confidence interval. Finally, statistically significant association was declared at a P-value of < 0.05.

## Results
A total of 28 studies with 18,454 study participants were included. The pooled rate of stillbirths in Ethiopia was found to be 71.2 (95% CI: 58.3, 96.7) per 1000 births. In subgroup analysis, the rate of stillbirth ranged from 53.4 per 1000 births in the Amhara region to 105.5 per 1000 births in the Oromia region. Antepartum hemorrhage (OR: 4.59, 95% CI: 3.58, 5.89), congenital abnormality (OR: 6.06, 95% CI: 4.21, 8.71), previous history of stillbirth (OR: 4.91, 95%CI: 2.42, 9.97), prolonged labor (OR: 3.22, 95% CI: 2.61, 3.98), low body weight (OR: 4.84, 95% CI: 3.92, 5.98), meconium-stained amniotic fluid (OR: 5.18, 95% CI: 3.72, 7.20), obstructed labor (OR: 2.40, 95% CI: 1.78, 3.23), not using partograph (OR: 4.03, 95% CI: 2.01, 8.08), preeclampsia (OR: 2.28, 95% CI: 1.71, 3.04), preterm delivery (OR: 5.11, 95% CI: 3.48, 7.50) and premature rupture of membranes(OR: 2.64, 95% CI: 1.47, 4.77) were factors associated with a higher odds of stillbirths. Whereas, having four or more antenatal care visits (OR: 0.37, 95% CI: 0.29, 0.46) was associated with a lower risk of stillbirths.

## Conclusions
This systematic review and meta-analysis concludes that the rate of stillbirth remains high in Ethiopia. Therefore, pregnant mothers should be encouraged to utilize maternal and child healthcare services. Furthermore, all concerned stakeholders should consider the aforementioned factors in healthcare provision to reduce stillbirths in Ethiopia.

## Background
Stillbirth is one of the neglected global public health issues. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), a baby who dies after 28 weeks of gestational age(during or before birth) is classified as a stillbirth [ ]. The stillbirth rate is computed by dividing the number of stillborn by the total births and multiplying it by 1000 [ ]. Globally, around 1.9 million (90% confidence interval:1.8, 2.0) stillbirths occurred in 2021, which implies that one stillbirth happened every 17 s [ ].
Stillbirths have an extensive psychological impact on the mothers and families; such as depression, anxiety, and other psychological symptoms as well as stigma and taboo [ , ]. According to the most recent United Nations Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation (UN IGME), an estimated 13.9 stillbirths per 1,000 births occurred globally in 2021, which was eightfold higher in low-income countries than in high-income countries [ ]. In sub-Saharan African countries, about 21.0 stillbirths per 1,000 births occurred in 2021 [ ]. The Sub-Saharan Africa and Southern Asia regions hold the burden that contribute to about 77 percent of global stillbirths [ ].
About 45 percent of all stillbirths have occurred during the intrapartum period [ ]. The common causes are hypoxia, placental obstruction, and other delivery and labor-related complications and many of these can be prevented by improving the quality of maternal and child health services [ ]. Considering the importance of the problem, in 2014, the WHO launched an initiative “Every Newborn Action Plan (ENAP), which calls for all countries to achieve 12 or fewer stillbirth rates per 1000 total births by 2030 [ ]. Full achievement of ENAP targets could save 2.6 million births from being stillbirths by 2030 [ ]. However, if the current trends continue, about 15.9 million stillbirths have been projected between now and 2030 [ ]. Hence, Sub-Saharan African countries, including Ethiopia have to get in fast trucks to achieve the ENAP targets.
In Ethiopia, previous studies revealed that the rate of stillbirths varies across regions with the highest rate in the Harari region (14.5 stillbirths per 100 births) [ ] to the lowest rate in the Amhara region (2.3 stillbirths per 100 births) [ ]. Moreover, several fragmented primary studies also confirmed that there was substantial heterogeneity in the rate of stillbirths across regions and different factors were associated with stillbirths [ – ]. Furthermore, mixed results were reported on the association between stillbirths and some of the factors. For instance, factors such as premature rupture of membranes(PROM) [ , , ], pregnancy-induced hypertension [ , , , ], partograph utilization [ , , , ], preterm delivery [ , , , ], duration of labor [ , , , , ], fetal weight [ , , ] and previous history of stillbirth [ , , , , ] were widely reported as factors associated with stillbirths. However, other previous studies demonstrated that PROM [ , , , , ], pregnancy-induced hypertension [ , , , ], previous history [ , ], preterm delivery [ , , ], fetal weight [ ], duration of labor [ ]and partograph utilization [ ] have no effect on stillbirths. Hence, estimating the aggregated odds ratio for these factors using systematic review and meta-analysis (SRMA) study is important to determine the true effect sizes and resolve such inconsistencies. In Ethiopia, though there was a previous SRMA study on the rate of stillbirths and its associated factors [ ], it only included eleven primary studies and examined the pooled effects of antenatal care visits and previous stillbirth history and yet, the meta-effects of other contributing factors such as antepartum hemorrhage, meconium-staining, labor duration, fetal weight, pregnancy-induced hypertension, prematurity, partograph utilization, and PROM have not been investigated. Therefore, this SRMA aimed to estimate the rate of stillbirths and identify its associated factors in Ethiopia. The findings of this study could help policymakers or the government to develop a comprehensive strategic plan for the identified factors at the national level.

## Methods

## Study protocol and registration
This systematic review and meta-analysis was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO), which is available with the registration number https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD42023468030.

## Search strategies
This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Statement (PRISMA2020) guideline (Additional Table 1) [ ]. PubMed, HINARI, Science Direct, and other sources including Google Scholar and African Journals Online were searched to obtain relevant articles. The following terms and phrases such as “stillbirth”, “fetal mortality”, “fetal death”, “fetal demise”, “adverse pregnancy outcome”, “perinatal death”, “perinatal mortality”, “perinatal demise”, “predictors”, “associated factors”, “risk factors”, “determinants” and “Ethiopia” were the main key search terms used in this meta-analysis. The Boolean search operators such as “AND” and “OR” were used separately and in combination during database searching (Additional Table 2).

## Eligibility criteria
The inclusion criteria were: 1) a study conducted in Ethiopia, 2) observational studies (cross-sectional, case-control and cohort) were included, 3) Studies that report the rate of stillbirth and/or associated factors, or determinant factors and 4) we included studies available at the electronic source from 2015/1/1–2023/8/29 to estimate the up to date/level of post ENAP initiative stillbirth rate in Ethiopia. On the other hand, studies that didn’t report the event or number of stillbirths, citations without abstract and/or full-text, anonymous reports, editorial reports, and qualitative studies were excluded from the analysis.

## Data extraction
After extensive browsing, all articles were exported to EndnoteX7 to identify and remove duplication. Four authors (GF, ZA, AM, and HG) extracted the data using a standardized form. We extracted the author’s name, publication year, event of stillbirth, rate of stillbirth, study region, study design, and predictor with its odd ratios from each primary study.

## Quality assessment/critical appraisal
The Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI 2020) Checklist for cross-sectional studies and case-control studies was used to assess the quality of the studies [ ]. Two authors (MG and AA) evaluated the qualities of the primary studies. Any discrepancy between the two authors was handled by taking the mean score. The tool has Yes, No, Unclear, and Not Applicable options: “1” is given for “Yes” and “0” is given for other options. The scores were summed and changed to percentages. Studies with >50% quality scores were included in this meta-analysis. Finally, 28 studies that received a quality score of >50% were included in this meta-analysis (Additional Table 3).

## Outcome measurement
The first outcome of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to estimate the rate of stillbirth in Ethiopia. The rate of stillbirth was calculated by dividing the number of babies born with no signs of life at 28 weeks or more of gestation by total births and multiplying it by 1000. The second outcome was to identify the predictors of stillbirths in Ethiopia. Accordingly, the odd ratio of predictors with its 95% confidence intervals (CI) was extracted from the original studies to compute the pooled odd ratio of factors associated with stillbirths.

## Statistical analysis
We entered data using Microsoft Excel 2013 and then exported to R software version 4.1.3 for further analysis. A meta-package for proportion was run using the number of stillbirths and total births to estimate the pooled rate of stillbirths. The random effect model was fitted to estimate the pooled rate of stillbirths and odds ratio of factors with a 95% confidence interval. Finally, statistically significant association was declared at a P-value of < 0.05. Heterogeneity was checked using the I-square [ ]. We used double arcsine-based transformation to stabilize the variance of each study’s proportion. Subgroup analysis was done with the evidence of heterogeneity. Univariate meta-regression analysis was done to identify the possible source of heterogeneity considering the sample size and publication year. Sensitivity analysis was done by omitting individual studies to detect the contribution of each study to the final estimate of stillbirths. Funnel plot visual inspection was done to identify publication bias. Finally, Begg’s test was done to assess any significant publication bias. Further, the trim-and-fill analysis was done to correct the effect of publication bias. The results were presented using forest plots and tables.

## Results

## Searching results and characteristics of studies
A total of 1257 studies were identified from PubMed, HINARI, Science Direct, Google Scholar, and African Journals Online. Of these, 345 articles were from PubMed, 464 articles were from HINARI, 383 articles were from Science Direct, and the rest 65 studies were identified from Google Scholar and African Journals online. From these studies, firstly, a total of 382 studies were excluded due to duplication. Secondly, from the remaining 875 studies, a total of 823 studies were excluded as not being relevant to the study after reviewing its title and abstract. Thirdly, the rest 52 studies were assessed by reviewing the full text. After full text review, at total of 24 studies were excluded as they didn’t report the outcome interest (7 studies), fail to retrieve the full text (4 studies), low quality appraisal score (1 study), unpublished thesis (1 study), old literature (7 studies), stillbirth rate reported but wrongly computed (2 studies) and redundancy using EDHS 2016 data set (2 studies). Finally, a total of 28 studies were fulfilled the inclusion criteria and incorporated into the final meta-analysis [ – ] (Fig. ). Nine of the studies were conducted using a case-control design and 16 were done using a cross-sectional study design. These studies were done from different parts of Ethiopia (Amhara, Oromia, SNNPR (South Nation, Nationalities and People Region), Tigray, Somali and Harari regions, and Addis Ababa) (Table ).

 

 

## The pooled rate of stillbirths in Ethiopia
A total of 19 studies were used to estimate the pooled rate of stillbirth [ – , , – , – , – ]. Accordingly, the pooled rate of stillbirth in Ethiopia was found to be 71.2 (95% CI: 58.3, 96.7) per 1000 births using a random effect model. Heterogeneity between studies was identified (I2 = 93%, P-value < 0.01(Fig. ). Hence, subgroup analysis was conducted based on the regions. Accordingly, the rate of stillbirths varied across regions in Ethiopia, ranging from 53.4 per 1000 births in the Amhara region to 105.5 per 1000 births in the Oromia (Fig. ). Moreover, Meta-regression analysis was done to identify the possible source heterogeneity using the publication years and sample size. None of these factors were statistically significant (Table ). Finally, sensitivity analysis was done. In sensitivity analysis, nearly all studies have equal contributions to the pooled estimate of stillbirth rate in Ethiopia (Fig. ). 

 

## Publication bias
Funnel plot visual inspection was done to identify publication bias. Upon this, asymmetric distribution was detected (Fig.  ). The Begg’s test also shows a statistically significant publication bias with Z-value = −2.27, and P-value = 0.023. Further, trim and fill analysis was done to detect the impact of publication bias. After filling ten studies, the bias-corrected estimate was found to be 24.9 (95%CI: 8.7; 48.5) per 1000 births (Fig.  ).

 

 

## Meta-analysis of factors associated with stillbirths in Ethiopia
A total of 18 studies were used to compute the pooled odds ratio of factors associated with stillbirth [ , – , – ]. Accordingly, the likelihood of stillbirth was reduced by 53% (OR: 0.37, 95% CI: 0.29, 0.46) among mothers who had four or more antenatal care visits (ANC) as compared to mothers who didn’t have antenatal care visits. The odds of stillbirth were 4.59 times (OR: 4.59, 95% CI: 3.58, 5.89) higher among mothers who developed antepartum hemorrhage (APH) as compared to their counterparts. The likelihood of stillbirth was 6.06 times (OR: 6.06, 95% CI: 4.21, 8.71) higher among fetuses with congenital abnormality as compared to fetuses without congenital abnormality. The likelihood of stillbirth in the next pregnancy was 4.91 times (OR: 4.91, 95%CI: 2.42, 9.97) higher among mothers who have a previous history of stillbirth as compared to their counterparts. Prolonged labor (labor duration >18 h) was associated with higher odds of stillbirth. Such that, the odds of stillbirth were 3.22 times (OR: 3.22, 95% CI: 2.61, 3.98) higher among mothers with prolonged labor as compared to their counterparts. The odds of stillbirth were 4.84 times (OR: 4.84, 95% CI: 3.92, 5.98) higher among fetuses whose birth weight is < 2.500 g as compared to fetuses whose birth weight is 2.500 g or more. The odds of stillbirth were 5.18 times greater (OR: 5.18, 95% CI; 3.72, 7.20) among mothers with meconium-stained amniotic fluid than their counterparts. The likelihood of having stillbirth was 2.40 times higher (OR: 2.40, 95% CI: 1.78, 3.23) among mother with obstructed labor as compared to their counterparts. The odds of stillbirth were 4.03 times higher (OR: 4.03, 95% CI: 2.01, 8.08) among mothers who didn’t follow using partograph during labor than their counterparts. The likelihood of stillbirth was 2.28 times higher (OR: 2.28, 95% CI: 1.71, 3.04) among mothers with pregnancy-induced hypertension as compared to mothers with no pregnancy-induced hypertension. The odds of having stillbirth were 5.11 times (OR: 5.11, 95% CI: 3.48, 7.50) higher among mothers with preterm delivery than their counterparts. The likelihood of having stillbirth was 2.64 times higher (OR: 2.64, 95% CI: 1.47, 4.77) among mothers who have premature rupture of membranes as compared to their counterparts (Table ).

 

## Discussion
This systematic review and meta-analysis discloses the pooled rate of stillbirth in Ethiopia, and further, identify the factors associated with it. Accordingly, the pooled rate of stillbirth in Ethiopia is found to be 71.2 (95% CI: 58.3, 96.7) per 1000 births. The finding is higher than the rate of stillbirth reported by a multi-country study conducted in Malawi (20.3 per 1000 births), Zimbabwe (34.7 per 1000 births), and Kenya (38.8 per 1000 births) [ ]. The possible explanation for discrepancy might be the variation in the implementation and utilization of maternal and child health services in Ethiopia. The fact that maternal and child health service utilization in Ethiopia was lower as compared to other African countries [ ]. Moreover, in subgroup analysis, the rate of stillbirths across regions ranged from 54.3 per 1000 births in the Amhara region to 105.5per 1000 births in the Oromia region. The possible justification for the disparities might be the variation in access, utilization, and quality of maternal and child healthcare services across regions.
According to this meta-analysis, the likelihood of having stillbirth was lower among mothers who have four or more ANC visits as compared to mothers who do not have ANC visits. The finding is supported by previous systematic review and meta-analysis results [ – ]. This can be explained as ANC visits are crucial for screening mothers with risk and preventing stillbirth. However, a study from Angola has demonstrated that ANC visits have no effect on stillbirths [ ]. The possible explanation for the discrepancy might be that Angola has endorsed the Maternal and Child Health (MCH) Handbook program [ ]. The program provides a handbook for all mothers, which has basic information about maternal and child health. Hence, the handbook can boost the awareness of mothers on high-risk pregnancies and their precautions regardless of antenatal care visits.
The likelihood of stillbirth in the next pregnancy was higher among mothers who have a previous history of stillbirth as compared to their counterparts. The finding is supported by previous studies conducted elsewhere [ , , , – ]. This can be due to recurrent genetic exposure of the fetus to hereditary chromosomal abnormality. However, the finding is contrary to the study conducted in Kenya that prior stillbirth has no association with the succeeding stillbirth [ ]. The possible annotation for the discrepancy might be the study in Kenya included only mothers from urban residences. Hence, the mothers may have better follow-up and those mothers with high-risk pregnancies can be managed before complications.
The odds of stillbirth are higher among mothers with prolonged labor or/obstructed labor as compared to their counterparts. The finding is consistence with a previous systematic review and meta-analysis [ ]. This could be the fact that obstructed labor can prolong the labor and cause perinatal asphyxia. This can raise the risk of stillbirth.
Mothers who are experiencing premature rupture of membranes (PROM) during the current pregnancy are associated with higher odds of stillbirth as compared to their counterparts. The finding is supported by a prior study conducted elsewhere [ , ]. This can be explained as mothers with PROM are prone to ascending uterine infection (i.e. chorioamnionitis) which can contribute to stillbirth. However, a study from Tanzania found that PROM was associated with a reduced risk of stillbirth [ ]. The discrepancy might be that the study in Tanzania was conducted in a large medical center, hence healthcare professionals can take aggressive obstetric care to prevent stillbirths.
This meta-analysis revealed that being low body weight was associated with a higher odds of stillbirth. The finding is consistent with previous meta-analyses conducted in sub-Saharan Africa [ ]. This could be the fact that low birth weight fetuses are more likely to be intrauterine retarded, immature, and possibly have limited calorie stores and immature organ systems. This can increase the likelihood of stillbirth.
This meta-analysis revealed that the likelihood of stillbirth was higher among mothers with pre-eclampsia as compared to their counterparts. The finding is supported by previous studies conducted elsewhere [ , , , – ]. It is a fact that pregnancy-induced hypertension can cause perinatal asphyxia [ ]. However, studies from England [ ] and Japan [ ] demonstrated that pre-eclampsia was associated with a reduced risk of stillbirth. The possible explanation for the discrepancy might be that the developed nations have better access to health facilities for the care of pregnant mothers. Hence, mothers with high-risk pregnancies can be followed in special care and the fetus can be delivered early due to maternal reasons before the occurrence of obstetric complications.
This meta-analysis revealed that the likelihood of stillbirth was higher among fetuses with congenital abnormality as compared to fetuses without congenital abnormality. The result is consistent with the study done elsewhere [ , , ]. This could be fetus with congenital anomalies (i.e. organs such as heart, kidney, brain, lung, and others) could face different organ system dysfunctions, which can attribute to stillbirth.
In this study, the odds of stillbirth are higher among mothers who didn’t follow using partograph during labor than mothers who followed using partograph. The finding is contrary to a study conducted in South Africa that partograph utilization has no effect on birth outcomes [ ]. The possible annotation for the discrepancy might be South Africa introduced a portable continuous wave Doppler(Umbiflow™) technology for fetal health evaluation even in low-resource health facilities, which may overwhelm the importance of partograph [ ]. However, the finding is supported by previous meta-analysis studies [ , ]. This could be partograph utilization during labor and delivery is important to follow the progress of labor, the well-being of fetuses, and for early identifying obstetrics complications. Hence, it helps healthcare professionals to make medical decisions and take preventive measure.
The odds of stillbirth were higher among mothers with antepartum hemorrhage during the index pregnancy as compared to their counterparts. The finding is supported by previous studies conducted elsewhere [ , , ]. Antepartum hemorrhage is bleeding from the birth canal after the 24th week of gestational age [ ]. Thus, it can reduce blood flow from the placenta to the fetus, this further compromises blood flow to vital organs such as kidney, brain, heart, and lung and later, can end with multi-organ dysfunction. This can increase the risk of stillbirths.
The odds of stillbirth are higher among mothers with preterm delivery than their counterparts. The finding is contrary to a study conducted in Kenya that prematurity has no effect on stillbirth [ ]. However, this study was conducted among Kenyan mothers who were comprised under the WACh (Mobile Solutions for Women’s and Children’s Health) program, a two-way communication between women and healthcare professionals. Hence, mothers with high-risk pregnancies may call early to health facilities. On the other side, the finding is consistent with studies conducted elsewhere [ , , , – ]. The possible elucidation can be that preterm babies have immature body systems and limited energy storage, such that, they are prone to develop multi-organ dysfunction with minimal physiological disturbance. Thus, preterm babies are more vulnerable to stillbirth.
In this meta-analysis, stillbirth was predicted by meconium-stained amniotic fluid. Thus, the odds of stillbirth were higher among mother with meconium-stained amniotic fluid during the index pregnancy as compared to their counterparts. The finding is supported by a previous study conducted in Tanzania [ ]. This could be explained as meconium-stained amniotic fluid most likely occurs in fetuses with perinatal asphyxia. This increases the risk of stillbirth. In general, researchers, program implementers, and policymakers should consider these factors in health care provision.

## Strength and limitations
This systematic review and meta-analysis provides the aggregated estimate of stillbirth rates in Ethiopia in the sustainable development goal era (SDGs). However, this study is not free from limitation, as the primary studies included were only from six regions, hence, some other regions may not be represented. Moreover, majority of the primary studies were conducted based on cross-sectional data, which can’t establish the cause-and-effect relationship. Furthermore, some variables related to stillbirth were excluded from the analysis because they were reported only in one primary article.

## Conclusions
This systematic review and meta-analysis concludes that the rate of stillbirthremains high in Ethiopia. Antepartum hemorrhage, congenital abnormality, previous history of stillbirth, prolonged labor, low body weight, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, obstructed labor, not using partograph, preeclampsia, preterm delivery and premature rupture of membranes are factors associated with a higher odds of stillbirths. Whereas, having four or more antenatal care visits was associated with a lower risk of stillbirths. Therefore, pregnant mothers should be encouraged to utilize maternal and child healthcare services. Furthermore, all concerned stakeholders should consider the aforementioned factors in healthcare provision to reduce stillbirths in Ethiopia.
