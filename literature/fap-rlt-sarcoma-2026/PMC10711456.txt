## Summary
Hardly any new tracers attracted more attention in nuclear medicine in the last couple of years than radiolabeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (FAPi’s). Molecules targeting cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) or disease-associated fibroblasts in benign disorders (DAFs) gave rise to a new class of radiopharmaceuticals widely applicable for imaging and with the desired use as therapeutic compounds. Despite displaying benefits in diagnostic sensitivity over FDG, most FAP-targeting compounds in today’s clinical routine continue to lack therapeutic utility due to short tumor retention. In this study, we evaluated 3BP-3940, specifically designed for achieving prolonged tumor retention and remarkably low uptake in healthy tissues. We herein present the automated manufacturing of gallium-68 (Ga-68) and lutetium-177 (Lu-177)-labeled 3BP-3940, their respective in vitro stability, validation of an automated production process, and validation of an analytical HPLC method for quality control. Finally, we give a first insight into the clinical utility of the two compounds.

## Introduction
After the exceptional success of radiopharmaceuticals targeting tumor-associated antigens, such as somatostatin receptors (SSTRs) and prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA), the quest for additional potent targets sparked a lot of attention in the nuclear medicine community. Apart from looking for targets expressed in just one particular cancer, the most interesting and challenging attempt remains the identification of a versatile target present in a variety of malignancies with high expression in or close to tumors and negligible expression in healthy tissues. In this context, the fibroblast activation protein (FAP) was identified as an excellent target being a membrane-bound protease with limited expression in normal adult tissues, but high expression in the tumor microenvironment of many solid cancers as well as on the cell membrane of sarcomas due to its presence on cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). , , , , , 
Guided by the huge success of PSMA, a large group of small molecule-based radiopharmaceuticals targeting FAP evolved over the last years. Various structures were considered and tested, but the most prominent and so far most clinically used compounds originated from glycine-2-cyanopyrrolidine-based FAPis, originating from the most potent inhibitor (S)—N-(2-(2-cyano-4,4-difluoropyrrolidin-1-yl)-2-oxoethyl)quinoline-4-carboxamide (UAMC1110), developed by the group of Pieter van der Veken. , , , , , Most of these small molecule compounds showed promising results when used for the imaging of a variety of cancers and other diseases, but only a few displayed sufficient tumor retention over a longer period to allow for therapeutic applications as well. , , , , , The most promising attempt among the clinical proof-of-concept data published to date appears to be the homodimer approach by Moon et al. leading to longer tumor retention. This benefit must be balanced though against the overall increased physiological uptake of this class of compounds. , , , 
Another approach, which has so far drawn less attention, is the use of peptide-based structures targeting FAP. These are known to often offer certain advantages, such as allowing easier structural modifications for optimization toward higher binding affinity and selectivity as well as improved stability, especially when cyclized. The first and so far, most promising example of such a cyclic peptide-based structure is FAP-2286. Equipped with the chelator 2,2′,2″,2‴-(1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane-1,4,7,10-tetrayl)tetraacetic acid (DOTA), it is capable of complexing a variety of radionuclides, such as Ga-68 for imaging and Lu-177 for therapy and therefore provides ideal prerequisites for use as a theranostic radiopharmaceutical. Excellent preclinical results confirmed the strong potential of this new peptide-based compound. First-in-human investigations conducted by Baum et al. demonstrated prolonged retention of the compound in advanced metastatic cancers with a residence time of up to 10 days when labeled with Lu-177. In addition, Ga-68-labeled FAP-2286 has the potential to outperform FDG in certain indications. Currently, the LuMIERE Phase 1/2 study is evaluating the therapeutic potential of [177Lu]Lu-FAP-2286 in patients with advanced solid tumors. Previous and ongoing activities demonstrate the outstanding potential of cyclic peptide-based FAP-targeting compounds as diagnostic and therapeutic agents which are applicable to a variety of diseases and cancer types and have opened a gate toward new developments of even more potent peptides with potentially better properties, for example, higher stability, longer tumor residence time, or accelerated blood clearance. With the new compound 3BP-3940 ( ) we herein present a new peptide which we expect to show some of the above-mentioned properties.
 

## Results

## Labeling process optimization, validation, and quality control of [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940
Radiolabeling of 3BP-3940 () with Ga-68 was performed in an automated process using a mini-All-in-One cassette-based module (Trasis, , , and ). The module was placed in an H700 hot cell with the reaction taking place in a class C environment and the formulation in a closed class A isolator. Process optimization revealed an ideal radiolabeling temperature of 85°C. Increasing the temperature to 90°C was found to result in increased side product formation, whereas below 85°C a significant drop in labeling yield was observed. This side product is believed to be the oxidized thioether which is a quiet common reaction for thioether under the presented conditions. Labeling in sodium acetate buffer (0.7 M, pH 5.5,2 mL) provided decay-corrected radiochemical yields (RCY’s) of ≥90% within 20 min using 50 μg of 3BP-3940 in a total of 7 mL labeling volume. Decreasing the precursor amount reduced the yield as well. For the automated process the Ge/Ga-generator was evaluated automatically using the syringe connected in position E using an optimal flow of 1 mL/min. The eluate which was collected in syringe 3 was subsequently transferred into the brown/orange reaction vial shown in the middle of . From port D the peptide dissolved in the buffer is transferred to the reactor using syringe 3 which is done by applying overpressure to vial D and making a spontaneous connection to the reactor vial. After the heating time, the product is again collected by syringe 3 and the liquid is transferred to the waste vial passing the HPLB+ cartridge in position 10 trapping all the labeled molecule. A rinse of the reactor is also performed. Therefore syringe 3 draws NaCl 0.9% from port 6 and pushes it into the reactor. After a few seconds, the syringe again transfers this liquid again to the waste vial by passing the cartridge. The next step is the elution of the cartridge. Therefore syringe 3 draws 0.5 mL ethanol from port B and pushes it threw the cartridge while the system is connected to the final product vial. Eventually, the cartridge lets go of the labeled product, and after syringe 3 rinses the cartridge again with 10 mL of NaCl 0.9% formulation takes place while passing the sterile filter. For robust production, the above-mentioned ideal parameters were used for process validation (n = 3) with starting activities between 590 and 628 MBq. Main focus during validation was set on two specifications: radiochemical purity of >95% (by radio-HPLC) and RCY of >80%. An example of one analytical radio-HPLC chromatogram is given in , demonstrating a radiochemical purity (RCP) ≥97%. The average RCP of the validation cycles was 97.1 ± 1.4 and the average yield 94.8 ± 4.5% indicating a process that can be operated within the desired parameters. For the subsequent routine productions of [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940, a mean RCP of 97.7 ± 0.9% at the end of synthesis was observed (n = 25). The automated synthesis yielded activities of the final drug product between 308 MBq and 987 MBq (decay corrected RCY = 93.1 ± 5.7%) starting from activities between 1242 MBq and 625 MBq. The molar activity ranged between 9 and 29 MBq/nmol.
 Further quality control parameters of the compound included dose activity, visual control, pH, half-life determination and gamma spectrometry for nuclide identification, radiochemical purity (dual TLC system and HPLC), sterile filter integrity, and endotoxin test. An overview of specifications and quality control results is given in . All values were within the specified range and gave access to a drug product of adequate quality.
 Radiochemical purity verification via analytical HPLC revealed one additional radioactive side product with a retention time of tR = 5.3 min (drug product tR = 6.4 min) ( ). With the analytical method used an efficient baseline separation of the drug and side product was possible as confirmed during HPLC method validation, allowing the determination of a typical formation rate of the side product between 2% and 4%.

## Labeling process optimization, validation, and quality control of [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940
Radiolabeling with Lu-177 was performed with the same set-up and in the same environment as described in the previous section. For labeling a 0.1 M ascorbic acid solution containing gentisic acid (1.2 mL, pH 4.5, 30 mg gentisic acid) and the same reaction temperature of 85°C for 30 min was found most suitable. Using 37–50 μg of precursor per GBq -provided the highest RCY results. Precursor amounts below and above this range did not provide any improvement. No further purification was required prior to formulation to meet the specifications for RCP and RCY (see ), which was confirmed by process validation (n = 3) using the above-mentioned parameters and 50 μg/GBq of 3BP-3940. An example of one analytical radio-HPLC chromatogram is depicted in . The average RCP of the process validation runs was 97.5 ± 1.4% and the average yield 94.4 ± 1.6% indicating a process that can be operated within the desired parameters. For the automated Lu-process, the V-shaped Lu-vial is connected to two different cannulas, a short one to position 1 and a long one connected to the orange/brown reactor. The process then is initiated by syringe 3 applying pressure to vial A which transfers the volume in vial A to the reactor. Afterward, syringe 3 draws the peptide dissolved in the buffer from position B and subsequently transfers it to vial A. After the heating syringe 3 draws 5 mL of NaCl 0.9% from port C and subsequently collects all liquid from the reactor to dilute it in syringe 3. The mixture is then transferred to the final product vial while passing the sterile filter. Thereafter syringe 3 rinses the reactor three times with 4 mL of NaCl 0.9% and transfers the liquid of each rinse to the final product vial. During subsequent routine productions of [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940 a mean RCP of 96.8 ± 1.2% and a mean RCY of 91.5 ± 9.1% at the end of synthesis were observed (n = 14). The automated synthesis used starting activities between 5.7 and 35 GBq.
Further specifications of the drug product included radioactivity, visual control, pH, half–life determination for nuclide identification, radiochemical purity (dual TLC system and HPLC), sterile filter integrity, and endotoxin test. An overview of the specifications and results from quality control is given in . All results were within the specified range and gave access to drug products of sufficient quality.
A similar side product is observed when performing labeling with Lu-177 compared to the side product observed after Ga-68 labeling ( ). An acceptable separation of the main and side product on analytical HPLC was demonstrated (tR = 5.5 min and tR = 6.3 min).

## In vitro stability
The stability of the drug products was investigated without further processing by incubation in human serum (HS) and saline (NaCl) at 37°C. Analysis was performed using TLC. Both drug products showed no significant release of the radiometal over the course of two half-lives of the corresponding isotopes ( ) with a complex stability of >90% under both conditions. However, the TLC results can only indicate release of radiometal. Radiolytic decomposition of the molecules by means of analytical HPLC was only investigated over a period of 2 h after formulation, resulting in an RCP >95%.
 

## HPLC method validation for [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940
For working with an initially unknown molecule, an efficient and robust method for quality control with HPLC must be developed and validated early on. Separation of the drug product from the side product(s) was also part of method development and optimization. The International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) guideline Q2 and the EANM guideline on validation was used as a basis for experimental planning and method optimization. Measurements with [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 for the determination of linearity, accuracy, precision, reproducibility, and specificity as well as lower limit of detection (LLOD) and lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) were performed using [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 and [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 for UV and radiodetection, respectively. Linearity, specificity, LOQ, LOD, and carry-over for the radiodetector for [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 were determined in addition.

## Linearity (UV detection)
Seven different concentrations of [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 (0.5–10 μg/mL) framing the expected value of 1.3 μg/mL were injected in triplicate ( ). Relative standard deviations (RSD) < 2% were found for the peak areas obtained, and R2 of the regression line was above 0.998, consistent with ICH Q2 requirements.
 

## Accuracy (UV detection)
Twelve different measurements were made at four different concentration levels of [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 all in the range of 50%–150% of the expected concentration of 1.30 μg/mL. Due to the absence of a certified reference material, the accuracy samples and the calibration samples were prepared independently from the same synthesis batch. The average accuracy was 99.5% with an RSD of 0.42%, also in alignment with the requirements in the ICH guideline.

## Precision (UV detection)
Three measurements of [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 at 80%, 100%, and 120% of the expected concentration were conducted. The retention times and peak areas were measured (n = 3) and compared. The obtained RSD for all measurements was below 1% and therefore in line with guidelines.

## Specificity (UV-detection)
The retention time of the unlabeled peptide 3BP-3940 was compared with [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940. Co-injection of both molecules at a concentration of 5 ng/μL each resulted in baseline separation with tR of 5.9 min for 3BP-3940 and 6.2 min for [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 ( ). These retention times were confirmed by the individual injection of the two compounds.

## Lower limit of detection and lower limit of quantification (UV detector)
Different methods for the determination of LLOD and LLOQ are available according to guidelines: visual inspection, signal-to-noise measurement, or calculation via the calibration curve. In this example, calculation via the calibration curve was chosen. For  = 0.631 mAU was used, which is the standard deviation of the calibration curve and S = 20.168 which is the slope of the curve. LLOD and LLOQ were determined using the following equations:

## Linearity (radioactivity detection)
Nine different radioactivities of Ga-68 between 0.1 MBq and 1.0 MBq prepared by the dilution of high activities were injected to determine the linearity of the radiodetector. Triplicates were not performed as the reproducible preparation of radioactivity concentrations was not feasible. As a result, no RSD was calculated but a R2 value above 0.998, consistent with the required norms according to ICH Q2, was obtained.

## Accuracy (radioactivity detection)
Nine different measurements were made at nine different Ga-68 radioactivity concentration levels in the range of 75%–368% including the expected average activity concentration of 49 MBq/mL (decay correction was performed with regard to the injection time point). The average recovery was 100.5% with an RSD of 0.5% being in alignment with the ICH guideline. It is noteworthy that there was no access to a calibrated source or a second HPLC. Therefore, the true value was determined by the linearity including decay correction.

## Precision (radioactivity detector)
Instead of the typical injection scheme of three different samples with three repetitions, eight samples with different Ga-68 concentrations were measured due to the inherent difficulties involved in reproducing exact radioactivity concentrations. The mean recovery of all measurements had an RSD value below 1% and can therefore be seen in compliance with the ICH guideline.

## Specificity (radioactivity detector)
The retention time of the unlabeled peptide 3BP-3940 was compared with [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940. Co-injection of both molecules at a concentration of 5 ng/μL each resulted in baseline separation with tR of 5.9 min for 3BP-3940 and 6.4 min for [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 ( ). These retention times were confirmed by the individual injection of the two compounds.

## Carry-over
Following 12 consecutive measurements of [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 no noteworthy carry-over was observed in either the UV or radio-detector channel. No quantifiable transfer of any radioactive species to the next run allowed to omit additional column rinsing procedures ( ).

## Patient investigation
For diagnostic purposes, the patient received 150 MBq [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 30 min prior to PET/CT imaging. The administered amount of 3BP-3940 was 17 μg. The presented image was acquired 60 min after injection. There were no adverse or clinically detectable pharmacologic effects and no changes in vital signs.
For therapeutic purpose, the administered amount of 3BP-3940 was 388 μg with an administered activity of 9.7 GBq. There were no adverse or clinically detectable pharmacologic effects for this patient and no significant changes in vital signs or in the results of laboratory studies, concerning immediate adverse events, were observed. Visual analysis of posttherapy whole-body scans and SPECT/CT scans demonstrated significant uptake and retention of [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940 in tumor lesions upon delayed imaging (15.3 h after injection) The initial biodistribution of the radiopharmaceutical was identical to that of the pretherapeutic [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940, as can be seen in .
 

## Discussion
Introducing new radiolabeled compounds for clinical investigations demands a detailed analysis of their quality for the safe administration of the final product to patients. It is essential that the manufacturing of the drug product is reproducible and that only qualified equipment and methods are used to ensure compliance to applicable regulations aiming at protecting patient safety. Nevertheless, special attention must be also given to safe working conditions of operators in the context of handling radioactivity. Establishing an automated manufacturing process rather than a manual one has been shown to be advantageous to keep radiation exposure of operators to a minimum and further provide a reproducible production process. , The manufacturing of both drug products [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 and [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940 was successfully implemented on an automated synthesis module, equipped with cGMP-compliant software and sterile GMP-grade single-use materials, in a shielded hot cell providing a Class C environment during radiolabeling. Dispensing of both drug products was performed in a Class A isolator via a sterile filter into a closed sterile vial. Quality control prior to injection included a filter integrity test and a test on bacterial endotoxins as well as other methods to determine the suitability of the drug products for human use.
For quality control of the drug products the tests mentioned in were performed. However, since there is no suitable monograph, a dedicated validation of the HPLC methods used was carried out, based on ICH guideline Q2 and the guideline on radiopharmaceutical preparations. Validation results covering linearity, accuracy, precision, intermediate precision, specificity, LLOQ and LLOD for the UV detection and linearity, precision, specificity, LLOQ, LLOD and carry-over for the radio detection are given in . The results indicate that the used method is valid and allows for correct results. Since only one HPLC instrument was used for quality control, there was no need for the investigation of intermediate precision between multiple instruments but only between different analysts and days. Further attempts to improve the validation and to confirm full ICH compliance may include more detailed specificity measurements by including further impurities. Additionally, consistent impurities should be characterized.
 The complex stability of both drug products was investigated using TLC. In each case, no more than 10% metal was released over two half-lives of the corresponding nuclide ( ). HPLC measurements were performed to determine the stability of the drug products 2 h after end of synthesis. There were no noticeable changes in RCP for both compounds with values >95%. Investigations into the longer-term stability of the 177Lu drug product and potentially further optimization of the formulation may be required for centralized manufacturing and distribution of [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940.
Following quality control, both drug products were administered to a 53-year-old patient with moderately differentiated ductal pancreatic adenocarcinoma with hepatic metastases. First, the patient was examined with [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 PET/CT and then underwent therapy with [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940 ( ). The SPECT/CT shows a very favorable biodistribution of the compound 18 h after the injection of the radiotherapeutic compound. The residence time in the target tissue appears to be very longer eventually giving it advantages in comparison to known small molecule FAPi derivatives. , , However, this conclusion needs to be proven in further patients. A precise analysis of these data is beyond the scope of this work and will be presented elsewhere.

## Limitations of the study
A major limitation is that validation processes for HPLC systems with UV detectors are described in the guidelines, but HPLC systems with radioactivity detectors are not mentioned. Since the complete situation is not fully covered by the published position articles, the validation is adapted to the guidelines, but structured to the best of our knowledge and practice. This becomes particularly evident when measuring accuracy and precision. However, we hope that we can further contribute to standardization with our suggestions.

## Method details
3BP-3940 precursor was produced in GMP grade by piCHEM (Austria). The reference standard [natGa]Ga-3BP-3940 was produced by 3B Pharmaceuticals GmbH (Germany). Analytical data for both compounds are provided in the Supplementary. Both were provided free-of-charge by 3B Pharmaceuticals GmbH (Germany). Usually, a 1 mg/mL stock solution prepared with TraceSelect-water (Sigma-Aldrich, United States) was used for labeling. All chemicals were of the highest chemical grade available. Solvents for high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) were obtained as HPLC grade from Merck (Germany). Reagents, materials, as well as low-bioburden plastic cassettes were provided by Trasis (Belgium). The test for bacterial endotoxins was performed using a Nextgen PTS (Charles River, United States) device with suitable single-use consumables. Radioactivity of the drug products was determined using an ISOMED2010 (NUVIA Instruments, Germany) dose calibrator.
Ga-68 was eluted from a 68Ge/68Ga-generator (GalliaPharm, Eckert & Ziegler Radiopharma GmbH, Germany) and used without further purification. Radiolabeling was performed utilizing 2.0 mL of 0.7 M ammonium acetate buffer at pH 5.5 and 50 μg of precursor at 85°C with an All-In-One mini synthesis module (Trasis, Belgium) equipped with a disposable cassette. Subsequently, the reaction mixture was passed through an Oasis HLB Plus cartridge preconditioned with ethanol (2 mL) and water (5 mL) to remove free Ga-68. Elution of the product was performed with 0.5 mL ethanol. The product was then diluted with 10 mL of saline and passed through a sterile filter (Sterifix, B. Braun SE, Germany).
[177Lu]LuCl3 n.c.a. (EndolucinBeta) was obtained from ITM Isotope Technologies Munich (Germany) in different activities amounts dissolved in 0.04 M HCl (40 GBq/mL) and used without further purification. Radiolabeling was performed utilizing 2.0 mL of 0.15 M ascorbic acid buffer at pH 4.5 and 37–50 μg precursor per GBq Lu-177 at 85°C with an All-In-One mini synthesis module (Trasis, Belgium) equipped with a disposable cassette. No further purification of the product was needed, and formulation took place via dilution with 15 mL of saline and passing through a sterile filter (Sterifix, B. Braun SE, Germany).
The pH was controlled at the start and after labeling by disposable pH-Stripes (Merck, Germany). For reaction control, radio-TLC (TLC Silica gel 60 F254 Merck, Germany) with (1) citrate buffer pH 4 and (2) a 1:1 mixture of 1.0 M ammonium acetate buffer and MeOH (v/v) as mobile phase were used.
Analytical radio-HPLC was performed using an HPLC system (Agilent, Infinity 1200, 100 μL injection loop, 20 μL injection for quality control) equipped with a Ramona∗ radioactivity detector (Elysia-Raytest, Belgium) and an external BGO scintillator flow cell (300 μL, Elysia-Raytest, Belgium). The following column was used: VDSpher PUR 150 C18-E 5 μm, 100 × 4.0 mm VDS Optilab, Germany. The mobile phases were acetonitrile (A) and water (B), each containing 0.1% TFA. The gradient with a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min started at 5% of A and increased to 45% of A within 10 min. TLCs were analyzed with a miniGita TLC scanner (Elysia-Raytest, Belgium) and the analysis software Gina (Elysia-Raytest, Belgium) for data interpretation.
Stability studies were performed in human serum (HS), saline and final product formulation in triplicate at 37°C. HS (human male AB plasma, USA origin) was obtained from Sigma Aldrich, United States; saline was purchased from B. Braun, Germany. The final procedure used 100 μL of the formulated product solution (20 MBq) added to 1 mL of either HS or saline. Radio-TLC was carried out under the analysis conditions previously mentioned.
The medical use was conducted in accordance with §13 2b, German Medicinal Products Act. All patients had histologically confirmed malignancies and received oncologic treatment before. Additional criteria for patient selection were willingness to give informed consent, sufficient renal function, and no known allergies to any of the excipients. After injection of [68Ga]Ga-3BP-3940 (150 MBq) in 10 mL NaCl PET/CT images (Biograph Vision 600 Edge) were acquired from the vertex to the proximal femora after an uptake time of 70 min. A low-dose CT scan (130 keV, 30 mAs, CareDose; reconstructed with a soft-tissue kernel to a slice thickness of 5 mm) was used for attenuation correction and anatomical mapping of the tracer. The SPECT/CT images were acquired 18 h after injection of [177Lu]Lu-3BP-3940 (9.7 GBq) on a Siemens Symbia SPECT/CT. Further images were obtained, but are beyond the scope of this publication.

## Quantification and statistical analysis
For quantification of HPLC signals the Gina Star Software (Elysia-Raytest) was used and manual integration of the signals was performed. For data interpretation Excel (Microsoft) was used. All replication levels are given in the paper and are at least n = 3 (the number of measurements) expt values of the patient investigation (n = 1). For statistical analyses, the sample mean, standard deviation, and linear regressions were used and calculated using Excel.
