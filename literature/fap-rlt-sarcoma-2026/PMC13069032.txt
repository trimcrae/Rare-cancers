## Background
Sarcomas comprise a heterogeneous group of aggressive mesenchymal malignancies with limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis, underscoring the need for novel treatment strategies. Fibroblast activation protein (FAP), which is highly expressed on sarcoma-associated fibroblasts within the tumor microenvironment, has emerged as a promising target for precision radioligand therapy. To evaluate this approach, we employed a patient-derived xenograft (PDX) sarcoma mouse model to investigate [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 radioligand therapy. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed longitudinally in vivo using [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging and subsequently corroborated by pathological analyses.

## Results
The radiosynthesis of both [18F]F-FAPI-74 (n = 8) and [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 (n = 3) achieved high radiochemical purity (> 95%), confirming their suitability for subsequent applications. Both tracers showed similar biodistribution patterns with predominant renal clearance. MicroPET imaging demonstrated clear [18F]F-FAPI-74 uptake in PDX sarcomas (SUVmean = 0.60 ± 0.12), and microSPECT imaging revealed detectable [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 tumor uptake (SUVmean = 0.20 ± 0.18). In the in vivo evaluation of therapeutic efficacy, the combination therapy ([177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 + pazopanib) demonstrated the most pronounced antitumor effect, suppressing tumor growth by approximately six-fold compared with the control group, without inducing body weight loss or overt toxicity. Longitudinal [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging revealed markedly decreased tracer uptake in the combination group (SUVmean = 0.68 ± 0.20) compared with the control (SUVmean = 1.42 ± 0.47) and pazopanib monotherapy (SUVmean = 1.22 ± 0.11) groups. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed reduced FAP expression (− 38% versus control), decreased Ki-67 proliferation index (− 70%), and elevated cleaved caspase-3 levels (~ 2-fold increase) in radioligand-treated tumors, indicating enhanced apoptosis and an improved therapeutic response.

## Conclusions
[177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 demonstrated significant therapeutic potential against sarcoma in our preclinical model, with an apparent enhancement when combined with pazopanib. Additionally, [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging proved valuable as a non-invasive radiotracer for treatment monitoring. These findings provide strong preclinical evidence supporting the advancement of FAP-targeted radioligand therapy in combination with pazopanib into clinical trials.

## Introduction
Sarcomas constitute a rare and histologically diverse group of malignant tumors of mesenchymal origin, comprising more than 50 recognized subtypes arising from either bone (e.g., osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma) or soft tissues (e.g., liposarcoma and leiomyosarcoma) [ ]. This pronounced histological heterogeneity, compounded by their relative rarity, presents substantial challenges for accurate diagnosis and the development of rational, effective therapies. Contemporary management strategies rely primarily on surgical resection, radiotherapy, and systemic chemotherapy; however, clinical outcomes remain unsatisfactory, particularly for patients with advanced or relapsed disease [ ].
While the therapeutic landscape has begun to evolve with the gradual emergence of targeted therapies and immunotherapies, conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy continues to predominate across most subtypes. In this setting of limited therapeutic options, the multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) pazopanib [ ] has received regulatory approval for advanced or metastatic soft tissue sarcoma following disease progression after standard chemotherapy [ ]. Pazopanib exerts its effects primarily by inhibiting vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR)-1, -2, and − 3; platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) receptors α and β; and the stem cell factor receptor (c-KIT). This inhibition disrupts downstream proliferative signaling, and pazopanib has demonstrated notable antitumor efficacy in patients with sarcoma [ ]. However, even with these advances, patient survival remains highly variable and depends largely on histological subtype, disease stage, and individual treatment responsiveness [ ].
In response, contemporary oncological research has increasingly focused on the tumor microenvironment as a reservoir of novel therapeutic targets, with particular emphasis on cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and their central role in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance [ , ]. Accordingly, fibroblast activation protein (FAP), a serine protease highly expressed on activated CAFs, has emerged as an attractive candidate. Importantly, FAP expression in sarcomas extends beyond stromal fibroblasts to include tumor cells themselves and other non-stromal cellular components [ ], thereby providing a strong rationale for FAP-directed therapeutic approaches. This mechanistic understanding has catalyzed the development of radioligand therapy using FAP inhibitor-based radioligand therapy using therapeutic radionuclides, most notably [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286, which has shown both therapeutic and diagnostic potential across multiple cancer types [ ], including sarcomas [ , ]. Promising preclinical and early clinical data suggest that [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 treatment can impair tumor growth and prolong overall survival [ , ].
However, clinical responses in patients with sarcoma have thus far been limited and heterogeneous, underscoring the need for improved strategies to realize the full therapeutic potential of FAP-targeted approaches. Accordingly, the therapeutic efficacy of [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 monotherapy may be limited by several factors, including heterogeneous FAP expression patterns within individual sarcomas [ , ] and intrinsic cellular radioresistance mechanisms [ ]. To overcome these hurdles, combination therapeutic approaches targeting complementary biological pathways represent a promising strategy for improving treatment outcomes . In this context, pazopanib represents a rational combination candidate. Beyond its established antiangiogenic properties, pazopanib has been reported to exert additional antiproliferative effects that may modulate the tumor microenvironment. These effects include alterations in tumor perfusion and hypoxia, suppression of pro-survival and proliferative signaling pathways, and potential impairment of DNA damage checkpoint signaling, all of which are implicated in resistance against radiotherapy [ – ]. Through these mechanisms, pazopanib may influence cellular responses that would otherwise limit the therapeutic impact of targeted radionuclide therapy. This conceptual framework is supported by our preliminary investigations [ ], which suggested synergistic therapeutic activity between a FAPI radioligand and pazopanib in a sarcoma patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model.
Parallel to therapeutic development, successful clinical translation of FAP-targeted approaches requires robust, non-invasive imaging methodologies for accurate treatment response monitoring and patient stratification. Compared to FAPI-02 and FAPI-04, FAPI-74 demonstrates superior imaging resolution and higher tumor-to-background contrast with minimal mucosal uptake, while offering exceptional clinical flexibility through its NOTA chelator, which enables both large-scale [18F]F-AIF production and rapid, decentralized ambient-temperature [68Ga]Ga cold-kit labeling [ , ]. These favorable characteristics make [18F]F-FAPI-74 particularly well-suited for high-contrast molecular imaging of FAP-rich malignancies like sarcomas, enabling precise in vivo assessment of FAP expression dynamics and reliable monitoring of therapeutic response.
Building upon these insights and driven by the need to develop more effective sarcoma therapeutics, this study was designed to evaluate the antitumor activity of [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286, either monotherapy or in combination with pazopanib, in a preclinical sarcoma model. We concurrently performed longitudinal [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging to assess treatment response and correlated imaging findings with post-treatment immunohistochemical results.

## Materials and methods

## PDX model
All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (IACUC approval numbers: 2022031803 and 2022121508) and were conducted in accordance with institutional and national animal welfare guidelines. In brief, animal preparation procedures were performed as previously described by Lin et al. [ ], and CAF expression within tumor tissues was confirmed by Western blot analysis (Figure ). Sarcoma tissues obtained from the same patient with radiation-induced sarcoma (RIS) were washed with phosphate-buffered saline, sectioned into small fragments, and subcutaneously implanted into the upper right flank of six-week-old male SCID mice (25–30 g). Animals were randomized immediately after tumor tissue implantation. All animal experiments were conducted at the Laboratory Animal Center of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital. Upon arrival at the facility, animals were acclimatized prior to the initiation of experimental procedures. Standard laboratory chow (Altromin 1324) and water were provided ad libitum. Tumor size was monitored three times weekly, and mice became eligible for imaging and treatment once tumor volumes reached 50–75 mm3. A tumor volume exceeding 2000 mm3 was defined as a humane endpoint.

## Experimental workflow
The study workflow is presented in Fig.  . In brief, tumor-bearing mice were randomly assigned to four experimental groups: vehicle control, pazopanib monotherapy, [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 monotherapy ([177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286), and combination therapy ([177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 + pazopanib). Pazopanib was obtained from Novartis Co. Ltd. (Basel, Switzerland). Tumor volume and body weight were measured five times per week throughout the treatment period. Therapeutic efficacy was evaluated using longitudinal [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging before treatment initiation and at defined intervals during the study. To allow comparison of tumor growth between the control and pazopanib groups, all animals were scheduled for euthanasia on day 15 after radioligand treatment, and tumor tissues were collected for immunohistochemical analysis.

 

## Animal treatment
The treatment dose of the [177Lu]Lu-labelled FAPI compounds is a critical determinant of efficacy. Based on our previous study [ ] and a prior report by Wang et al. [ ], a dose of 7.4 MBq was insufficient to achieve therapeutic efficacy in solid tumors [ ]. Therefore, we selected a higher dose to maximize antitumor efficacy while preserving normal physiological function in mice. [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 monotherapy consisted of intravenous administration of 20 MBq via tail vein injection on days 3 and 11 following baseline imaging. Pazopanib monotherapy involved oral administration of 150 mg/kg daily for 14 consecutive days. Combination therapy incorporated both treatment regimens as specified above. Therapeutic efficacy was monitored through serial measurements of tumor dimensions and body weight. Tumor volumes (mm3) were calculated using the formula: 0.5 × width2 × length, where width represents the shorter diameter and length the longer diameter.

## [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 radiochemistry
FAPI-2286 was purchased from MedChemExpress (Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA; catalog HY-147057). [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 synthesis was performed using previously established protocols with minor modifications [ ]. In brief, FAPI-2286 (10 mM in DMSO), sodium acetate buffer (0.4 M, pH 5.5), 20% ascorbic acid solution, and [177Lu]LuCl3 (Isotopia Molecular Imaging Ltd, Petah Tikva, Israel) were combined in a reaction vial and incubated at 95 °C for 60 min. The crude product was purified using a C18 Sep-Pak cartridge (Waters, Boston, MA, USA; catalog WAT023501), preconditioned with ethanol (10 mL) and double-distilled water (10 mL), and eluted with an acetonitrile/water mixture (1:1, v/v). Radiochemical purity was determined by radio-thin layer chromatography using an AR2000 imaging scanner (Eckert & Ziegler Medical, Berlin, Germany) with instant thin layer chromatography plates (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) and 0.5 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 5.0) as the mobile phase. Only preparations with radiochemical purity exceeding 90% were used for subsequent experiments.

## [18F]F-FAPI-74 radiochemistry
[18F]F-FAPI-74 radiolabeling was performed according to Naka et al. [ ] with minor procedural modifications. Briefly, [18F]F-fluoride (740 MBq) was passed through a PS-HCO3 cartridge (Macherey-Nagel GmbH & Co. KG, Düren, Germany; catalog number 730691) to capture fluoride ions. Trapped fluoride was subsequently eluted into a reaction vial using 0.5 mL sodium acetate buffer containing 30 µL of 2 mM AlCl3. FAPI-74 precursor (~ 67 µg) prepared in ultrapure water/NaOAc/DMSO (20:180:400, v/v/v) was added, and fluorination proceeded at 95 °C. The reaction mixture was purified using a C18 Sep-Pak cartridge (Waters; catalog number WAT023501). Radiochemical purity and labeling yield were determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) with a C18 column (Luna C18, 4.6 × 150 mm, 5 μm; Phenomenex, Torrance, CA, USA). The mobile phase consisted of 50 mM H3PO4/acetonitrile (9:1, v/v) at 0.6 mL/min flow rate. Radioactivity and UV absorbance (254 nm) were monitored using a radio-detector (B-FC-100; Bioscan, Poway, CA, USA) and UV detector (Waters), respectively.

## In vivo PET/CT and SPECT/CT imaging
To evaluate the utility of [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET for monitoring [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 therapy and predicting therapeutic response in RIS, we assessed its correlation with [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 SPECT imaging acquired at closely matched, though not identical, time points. The distribution and intensity of tracer uptake were broadly comparable between [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET and [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 SPECT. Animals in the [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 treatment group underwent both imaging modalities. For PET imaging, mice received 5.4 MBq [18F]F-FAPI-74 via tail vein injection followed by a 30-min uptake period and 15-min acquisition using a nanoScan PET/CT scanner (Mediso, Budapest, Hungary). SPECT/CT imaging was performed 24 h after administration of 21.6 MBq [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 using a nanoScan SPECT/CT scanner (Mediso) with 30-min acquisition time. All animals were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane and maintained at 32 °C using heated airflow during imaging sessions.
Therapeutic efficacy was assessed through longitudinal [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET/CT imaging performed before and after treatment in all experimental groups. The researcher was blinded to the [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET images of the mice during image analysis. The imaging protocol (5.4 MBq tracer dose, 30-min uptake period, and 15-min scan duration) remained consistent across all sessions. Both SPECT and PET images were reconstructed using 3D ordered-subsets expectation maximization with CT-based attenuation correction. Tumor regions of interest (ROIs) were defined using PMOD software, version 4.0 (PMOD Technologies GmbH, Fällanden, Switzerland) through semi-automatic segmentation with thresholds set at 50% of maximum radioactivity from manually contoured regions. This threshold was selected based on commonly used percentage-of-maximum approaches for PET-based tumor delineation, as previously described in the literature [ , ]. Using the threshold, the ROIs were delineated to precisely encompass the whole tumor (Figure S2). Tumor radioactivity was quantified as mean standardized uptake values (SUVmean) to compare [18F]F-FAPI-74 and [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 uptake before and/or after treatment.

## Immunohistochemistry
At the conclusion of imaging, animals were sacrificed and tumors excised for immunohistochemistry. Tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, sectioned at 10 μm thickness, and stored at − 80 °C. Immunohistochemical staining followed standard DAB-based protocols. In brief, endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with a commercial H2O2 inhibitor, followed by 10-min protein blocking. After three 5-min PBS washes, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: anti-FAP (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA; catalog number ab53066; 1:100), anti-caspase-3 (Cell Signaling Technology, Inc., Danvers, MA, USA; catalog number 9662; 1:200), or anti-Ki-67 (clone 30 − 9; ready-to-use; Roche Tissue Diagnostics, Tucson, AZ, USA; catalog number 790–4286). Sections were then incubated with an HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature. Signal detection was accomplished with an HRP/DAB IHC Detection Kit (Abcam; catalog ab64261). Stained sections were imaged with a Zeiss Axio Imager M2 microscope (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG, Jena, Germany). Images were captured using identical exposure settings at 10× magnification. The detailed procedure for quantifying IHC images was previously described by Crowe et al. [ ] using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). Briefly, color deconvolution was applied to separate hematoxylin and DAB signals using the H DAB option in the Color Deconvolution menu. The DAB channel (Color 2), representing target protein signal, was selected for further analysis. Automatic thresholding was then applied to generate a binary image of positively stained regions. Protein expression was quantified as the percentage of DAB-positive area relative to the total analyzed tissue area using the Measure function, and results were expressed as the percentage of positive staining area (% area).

## Statistical analysis
Continuous data are expressed as means ± standard deviations (SDs). Statistical comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by post hoc multiple comparison tests. Analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism, version 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), with all tests two-sided at a 5% level of significance.

## Results

## Radiopharmaceutical synthesis and quality control
[18F]F-FAPI-74 radiolabeling achieved an activity yield of 12.3 ± 6.6% (non-decay corrected, n = 8) with a total synthesis time of approximately 60 min. Radio-HPLC analysis confirmed radiochemical purity exceeding 99%. The UV absorbance of the FAPI-74 reference standard (Fig.  A) and the radioactive signal of [18F]F-FAPI-74 (Fig.  B) showed identical retention times, confirming successful radiolabeling and high product purity. [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 synthesis followed established protocols, achieving radiochemical purity exceeding 95% as determined by radio-thin-layer chromatography (Fig.  ). The comparable purity profiles of both radiopharmaceuticals validated their suitability for in vivo imaging and therapeutic applications.

 

 

## Radiopharmaceutical biodistribution and in vivo tumor targeting
Both radiopharmaceuticals exhibited similar biodistribution patterns, with predominant retention in kidneys and bladder, consistent with urinary clearance as the primary elimination pathway. MicroPET imaging demonstrated substantial [18F]F-FAPI-74 accumulation in PDX sarcomas located on the upper right flank following intravenous administration (SUVmean = 0.60 ± 0.12, n = 10; Fig.  A). MicroSPECT imaging at 24 h post-injection revealed comparable [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 tumor uptake (SUVmean = 0.20 ± 0.18, n = 3; Fig.  B).

 

## Therapeutic efficacy and treatment response monitoring
Notably, combination therapy produced the greatest antitumor efficacy, resulting in approximately six-fold suppression of sarcoma growth compared with controls (Fig.  A). No significant changes in body weight were observed across treatment groups at any time point (Fig.  B), and no overt treatment-related toxicities were detected. [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging was performed longitudinally to monitor treatment-associated changes in FAP-related tracer uptake (Fig.  A), and additional PET/CT images are provided in Figure S3.

 

 

Pre-treatment images showed uniform radiotracer distribution throughout sarcoma tissue, whereas post-treatment scans revealed radioactivity predominantly at tumor periphery, suggesting central necrosis or reduced perfusion in larger tumors. Baseline tumor [18F]F-FAPI-74 SUVmean was 0.60 ± 0.12 (n = 10) across all experimental groups. At day 18 post-treatment, the combination therapy group demonstrated significantly reduced tumor radiotracer uptake (SUVmean = 0.68 ± 0.2; n = 4) compared to the control (SUVmean = 1.57 ± 0.39, p < 0.05; n = 4) and pazopanib monotherapy (SUVmean = 1.22 ± 0.11, p < 0.05; n = 4) groups. In contrast, the reduction in uptake was not statistically significant when compared to the [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 monotherapy (SUVmean = 1.19 ± 0.49, p > 0.05; n = 3) group (Fig.  B). In addition, the number of deaths during the study, relative to the total number of animals in each group, was as follows: control (2/7), pazopanib (2/6), [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 (2/5), and combination treatment (1/6).

## Immunohistochemical validation of treatment response
Upon completion of in vivo imaging studies, animals were euthanized and tumor tissues collected for immunohistochemical analysis. Distinct expression patterns were observed across treatment groups (Fig.  and Figure S4). FAP expression remained high in both the control group (FAP-positive area: 35.28 ± 6.08%) and the pazopanib monotherapy group (FAP-positive area: 39.07 ± 2.42%). In contrast, tumors from animals treated with [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286, either as monotherapy or in combination with pazopanib, exhibited markedly reduced FAP immunoreactivity, with decreases of approximately 25% in the monotherapy group (FAP-positive area: 26.32 ± 4.82%) and 38% in the combination group (FAP-positive area: 21.87 ± 5.39%), indicating a stronger therapeutic effect with the combination regimen. These findings were further supported by proliferation and apoptosis markers. Ki-67 staining revealed a pronounced reduction in proliferative activity, with nearly 70% lower levels in both [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 groups compared with control and pazopanib monotherapy. Conversely, cleaved caspase 3 expression was significantly elevated, reaching roughly two-fold higher levels in radioligand treated tumors, thereby confirming enhanced apoptotic activity.

 

## Discussion
Management of sarcoma remains a considerable clinical challenge, particularly in relapsed or metastatic disease where surgery, standard chemotherapy, and radiation therapy rarely achieve durable control [ ]. In clinical practice, pazopanib is primarily indicated for advanced non-adipocytic soft tissue sarcomas, with the most significant clinical benefits observed in leiomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma, and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS). Furthermore, our study highlights the potential of [18F]F-FAPI-74 and [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 in managing RIS. Predominantly manifesting as UPS or angiosarcoma, RIS arises within a markedly fibrotic microenvironment. These subtypes are expected to exhibit intense FAP expression due to the high density of activated CAFs.
While radioligand therapy targeting FAP on CAFs using 177Lu-labeled FAPI compounds has demonstrated efficacy across multiple malignancies in preclinical and clinical studies [ ], investigations in sarcoma remain sparse despite abundant CAF infiltration within the tumor microenvironment. Crane et al. [ ] reported that most sarcoma specimens exhibited FAP expression in both stromal and tumor (non-stromal) cells, albeit with considerable heterogeneity. Consistent with these findings, our RIS sample demonstrated high FAP expression in the stromal compartment and heterogeneous but appreciable FAP expression in tumor cells (Figure S4). These results suggest that [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 may effectively target both stromal CAFs and FAP-expressing tumor cells. These stromal elements are particularly abundant in sarcoma and actively promote tumor growth, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance [ ]. Unlike conventional cytotoxic agents that target proliferating malignant cells, FAPI-based radioligand therapy disrupts the supportive tumor microenvironment, potentially reducing recurrence risk and circumventing established chemoresistance mechanisms [ ]. Notably, this approach may prove particularly valuable in sarcomas, which frequently exhibit heterogeneous architecture and poor vascularization that limits conventional drug penetration.
The combination of [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 with pazopanib produced the most pronounced tumor suppression in our study, suggesting synergistic interactions between targeted radiotherapy and antiangiogenic inhibition. Small-molecule VEGFR2 inhibitors, such as pazopanib [ ], have been reported to increase tumor perfusion through vascular normalization, promote tumor stroma remodeling, and thereby enhance the delivery of concomitant therapies [ ]. The addition of [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 may further potentiate these effects by delivering localized radiation to the tumor microenvironment, thus inducing direct cytotoxicity as well as potential bystander effects on neighboring cells. This proposed mechanistic interaction is supported by emerging clinical evidence suggesting synergistic activity between antiangiogenic agents and radioligand therapies in breast and thyroid malignancies [ , ]. Our current immunohistochemical findings provide additional mechanistic validation, revealing increased caspase-3 expression and reduced Ki-67 staining in combination-treated tumors, consistent with enhanced apoptotic signaling and growth arrest. While [177Lu]Lu-FAPI-2286 monotherapy also demonstrated antitumor activity, its therapeutic impact remained inferior to the combination approach, reinforcing the clinical importance of simultaneously targeting both stromal and angiogenic pathways within the sarcoma microenvironment.
These promising findings should be interpreted in light of several limitations. First, the potential for additive toxicities must be considered. Pazopanib is associated with known adverse effects, including hepatotoxicity, hypertension, and fatigue, which could be compounded by the myelosuppressive effects of 177Lu β-radiation [ ]. Although no major adverse events or significant weight loss were observed during our short-term evaluation, the long-term safety profile and potential cumulative toxicities remain to be determined and warrant further investigation. Second, the interaction between antiangiogenic therapy and radionuclide delivery is inherently complex. While vascular normalization may enhance therapeutic delivery, excessive vascular disruption could conversely impair tumor perfusion and reduce radiotracer uptake, particularly in the absence of optimized treatment sequencing [ ]. As a result, decreases in [18F]F-FAPI-74 uptake may not solely reflect true downregulation of FAP expression or loss of FAP-expressing cells, but may also be influenced by perfusion-related effects. This pharmacodynamic interplay highlights the need for future studies to refine the timing and dosing strategies of each therapeutic component. From a translational perspective, this combined modality may have potential as an adjuvant to conventional therapies or as a salvage option in treatment-resistant disease. However, careful consideration of long-term safety is especially important in pediatric and young adult populations, who represent a substantial proportion of patients with sarcoma. In these groups, radiation-induced injury to developing skeletal and soft tissues remains a theoretical concern [ ], underscoring the importance of rigorous dosimetry, patient selection, and long-term follow-up in future clinical investigations.
Notwithstanding these caveats, our data provide evidence for the therapeutic potential of integrated FAPI-based theranostic approaches combined with antiangiogenic therapy in sarcoma management. The demonstrated utility of [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging for quantifying FAP expression and monitoring treatment response establishes a foundation for precision medicine approaches incorporating patient stratification and individualized treatment algorithms [ ]. It should be noted that both diagnostic and therapeutic FAP-targeting ligands may interact with overlapping functional binding sites of the FAP protein [ ], raising the theoretical possibility of transient target occupancy effects following radioligand therapy. However, therapeutic agents such as [177Lu]Lu-FAP-2286 are characterized by prolonged – yet finite – tumor retention, and follow-up FAPI imaging performed after an appropriate interval is expected to minimize residual target occupancy. In the present study, follow-up [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging was conducted at a delayed interval relative to radioligand administration, a timing strategy intended to mitigate potential confounding due to ligand competition and to more accurately reflect therapy-associated changes in FAP expression.
Translation of these findings to clinical practice will require validation in larger, more diverse preclinical models. Larger tumor-bearing models may provide more robust assessment of radionuclide dosimetry, spatial dose distribution, and potential off-target toxicity. In addition, evaluation across diverse sarcoma subtypes with heterogeneous FAP expression patterns will be important to establish the generalizability of FAP-targeted theranostic strategies. Furthermore, the use of syngeneic, immunocompetent models represents an important future direction. Given the potential for radionuclide therapy to induce immunogenic cell death and modulate antitumor immune responses, such models would enable investigation of immune-mediated effects that cannot be captured in immunodeficient xenograft systems.

## Conclusion
This preclinical study demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of [177Lu]Lu-FAP-2286 in a sarcoma model, with antitumor activity observed both as monotherapy and in combination with pazopanib. While [177Lu]Lu-FAP-2286 alone was sufficient to significantly inhibit tumor growth, the combination regimen showed a trend toward improved tumor growth control under the experimental conditions evaluated. Notably, [18F]F-FAPI-74 PET imaging proved to be a valuable noninvasive tool for assessing FAP-targeted tracer uptake and monitoring treatment-associated changes over time. Tumor growth inhibition observed in the combination group was accompanied by corresponding changes in FAP-related imaging signals and immunohistochemical markers, supporting the feasibility of FAPI-based theranostic approaches in this setting. Collectively, these findings demonstrate the potential clinical relevance of FAP-targeted radioligand therapy for sarcoma and provide a rationale for further investigation. Future studies should focus on optimizing dose levels, treatment sequencing, and scheduling, as well as conducting comprehensive safety assessments, to better define the therapeutic contribution of combination regimens and to facilitate translation toward early-phase clinical studies.
