## Abstract

## Background
Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are pivotal stromal components in the tumor microenvironment (TME) of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), influencing tumor progression, metastasis, and immunotherapy resistance. However, a comprehensive bibliometric overview of CAFs in HCC is lacking.

## Methods
A systematic search of the Web of Science Core Collection from January 1, 2004, to June 15, 2025 yielded 646 eligible publications. Bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer and CiteSpace mapped publication trends, international collaboration, institutional contributions, author networks, journal impact, and research hotspots.

## Results
A total of 646 articles were analyzed, spanning 282 journals and 2684 keywords. From 2004 to June 15, 2025, there was a significant increase in publications. China led in publication numbers, followed by the US and Japan. Renowned institutions include Sun Yat Sen University, Fudan University, and Zhejiang University. Notable researchers are Fan Jia, Giannelli Gianluigi, and Zhou Jian. The journal with the highest publication and citation frequency is Cancer Research. The main research focuses are the effects of CAFs on HCC cell proliferation, angiogenesis, invasion, metastasis, immunosuppression, and drug resistance, as well as the mechanisms of CAFs in HCC immunotherapy and the progress in targeted CAF research.

## Conclusion
CAFs research in HCC has transitioned from descriptive studies to precision therapeutic targeting. Future directions involve dissecting CAFs plasticity, leveraging single-cell multiomics, and optimizing combination therapies to overcome TME-mediated resistance.

## Introduction
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a highly lethal and aggressive tumor that commonly occurs in patients with chronic liver disease, but it may also develop in the absence of chronic liver disease [ ]. According to the 2020 Global Cancer Statistics, HCC ranks sixth globally in terms of new cancer cases and is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths [ ]. Risk factors vary from case to case and region to region, but the main factors include alcoholic cirrhosis, diabetes mellitus, hepatitis B, hepatitis C and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis [ ].The treatment of HCC primarily relies on methods such as surgical resection and liver transplantation. However, these therapeutic approaches are often limited by the advanced stage of the disease at the time of diagnosis. Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) serves as a crucial therapeutic option, particularly vital in the early stages of liver cancer. Nevertheless, most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, leading to poor prognosis. This highlights the existence of gaps in current research on hepatocellular carcinoma, and requires further inquiries in the future.
Bibliometrics is a discipline that employs quantitative and statistical methods to analyze the production and dissemination of scholarly literature. It involves the collection, organization, and analysis of data such as citation counts, co-authoring patterns, and publication locations [ ]. Bibliometrics can effectively track the dissemination and influence of research over time, helping researchers grasp hotspots and trends in academic research, identify emerging fields, and to facilitate academic collaboration [ ].
As fundamental research on cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in HCC continues to advance, a bibliometric analysis of this field can provide guidance for clinical issues and enhance understanding of research trends. Therefore, conducting a bibliometric analysis on the role of CAFs in HCC is essential. Such an analysis can reveal the current state of research, thematic areas, and future trends in the field of liver cancer CAFs. It can help experts and newcomers broaden their research horizons, discover new research topics, and clarify future research directions.

## Materials and methods

## Database and search strategy
We picked the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) as the database. To guarantee the reliability and accuracy of the results, we adopted ‘cancer-associated fibroblasts’ as the keyword and optimized the search method by integrating the insights from previous studies [ , ]. The detailed search strategy is described below: TS=(“liver cancer” OR “Liver Neoplasm” OR “liver tumors” OR “Hepatocellular Cancers” OR “Hepatoma” OR “Liver Carcinoma” OR “Hepatic tumor” OR “Hepatic cancer” OR “Cancer of Liver” OR “cancer of the liver” OR “hepatic neoplasms” OR “Neoplasm of liver” OR “liver and intrahepatic bile duct carcinoma” OR “liver and intrahepatic biliary tract cancer” OR “Tumor of liver” OR “liver malignant tumors” OR “Neoplasm of the liver” OR “Hepatocellular Carcinoma”)) AND TS=(“cancer-associated fibroblast*” OR “tumour-associated fibroblast*” OR “tumor-associated fibroblast*” OR “tumor associated fibroblast*” OR “tumour associated fibroblast*” OR “cancer associated fibroblast*” OR “tumor-related fibroblast*” OR “tumor related fibroblast*” OR “carcinoma-associated fibroblast*” OR “carcinoma associated fibroblast*” OR “tumor associate fibroblast*” OR “cancer-associated myofibroblast*” OR “cancer associated myofibroblast*” OR “tumor-associated myofibroblast*” OR “tumor associated myofibroblast*” OR “tumour-associated myofibroblast*” OR “tomour associated myofibroblast*” OR “tumour associate fibroblast*” OR “cancer associate fibroblast*” OR “carcinoma associate fibroblast*” OR “cancer-related fibroblast*” OR “cancer related fibroblast*” OR “tumour-related fibroblast*” OR “tumour related fibroblast*” OR “carcinoma-related fibroblast*” OR “carcinoma related fibroblast*”).The study spans a period of more than 20 years, from January 1, 2004 to June 15, 2025. The articles included in the study were categorized as dissertations and review articles, and all were written in English. To minimize systematic bias that may result from database updates, we conducted a exhaustive search and filtering. In addition, we took vigorous measures to deal with possible overlapping publications. During the data collection process, we processed the initial data exported from the WOSCC database using EndNote’s duplicate removal function. By matching key information such as title, author, journal name, year of publication and DOI, the software was able to precisely detect and remove duplicates, ensuring that no duplicates from databases or different search strategies made it into the subsequent analysis. Subsequently, during the data cleaning and screening stages, two researchers checked the de-duplicated literature manually, including titles, abstracts, and full texts, against preset inclusion and exclusion criteria to assure that there were no duplicate entries. Ultimately, the manuscript was completed on 29/7/2025.

## Data analysis
We created flowcharts in Microsoft Word 2019. For bibliometric analysis, we visualized international collaboration between countries through the online bibliometrics website (https://bibliometric.com/) and conducted bibliometric analysis of institutions, authors, journals, and keywords using VOSviewer 1.6.19 and Citespace 6.2R4, with a focus on co-authorship, co-occurrence and co-citation patterns. We merged overlapping items into unified elements, manually corrected spelling errors, and cleaned the data before exporting for subsequent analysis.

## Analysis of annual highly cited articles
Annual highly cited articles play a crucial role in bibliometrics. These articles usually represent research results with significant scientific contributions and wide academic influence in specific fields. They not only reveal the hot issues and cutting-edge trends in the research field, but also provide important theoretical foundations and experimental methods for subsequent research. From the Fig.  , it can be discovered that the research field of CAFs and HCC presents the following characteristics: the research theme has gradually deepened from the early focus on the role of CAFs in the tumor microenvironment to the relationship between CAFs and immunosuppression, immunotherapy efficacy, tumor stem cell properties, treatment resistance and metastasis mechanisms. The research hotspots focus on the study of the mechanisms by which CAFs promote immunosuppression, maintenance of tumor stem cell properties, and therapeutic resistance through signaling pathways such as STAT3 and FAP. The research methodology has gradually introduced cutting-edge technologies such as single-cell sequencing and exosome studies from traditional cell biology and molecular biology methods to deeply analyze the heterogeneity and functions of CAFs. The research horizon extends from the interaction between tumor cells and CAFs alone to the entire tumor microenvironment, including the combined effects of immune cells (e.g., NK cells), cytokines (e.g., CCL2), and hypoxic stress.
 

## Discussion

## General distribution

## Analysis of the number of publications (Figure S1-S2)
From 2004 to 2025, the number of publications in the field of CAFs and HCC research has shown an obvious stage-by-stage growth characteristic. 2004 to 2014 was a period of slow growth, with an average of less than 20 publications per year, and then entered a period of rapid growth after 2015, with an average of annual publications climbing rapidly, reaching a peak of 109 publications in 2023. This growth pattern is in line with the typical law of research hotspot formation, i.e., slow growth in the initial exploration stage, and entering a rapid expansion period as the research value is widely recognized. The driving factors behind include: [ ] Increasing global burden of liver cancer: according to WHO data, liver cancer is the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths in the world, with high incidence rates especially in Asia and Africa, and the clinical needs are urgently driving basic research investment [ , ]. As an important component of the tumor microenvironment, CAFs are involved in many key aspects of tumor proliferation, metastasis, drug resistance, etc., and have become an emerging hotspot in tumor research [ , ]. In addition, the study of visible mechanisms in keyword burst maps has become a hot topic of recent research [ ]. The popularization of high-throughput technologies, such as single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and proteomics, has made the study of CAFs go from the traditional pathological level to the molecular mechanism level, which has greatly improved the efficiency of research output [ – ].

## Situation of countries/regions and institutions (Figure S3, S4, S5 and Table S1)
In the field of tumor microenvironment research, China, the US, Germany, Japan, and South Korea are the top countries in terms of publication output. China has emerged as the most influential country in this field since 2021 due to its outstanding volume of publications, a development whose underlying reasons warrant careful consideration. In 2021, China released the Chinese expert consensus on conversion therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma (2021 edition) [ ]. Given the poor prognosis of advanced HCC and the limitations of existing treatment modalities, this consensus has spurred Chinese scholars to continue exploring novel therapeutic targets and approaches for HCC. Additionally, medical journals with significant international influence and recognition, such as Hepatology, provide a platform for showcasing China’s scientific achievements [ , ]. These publications attract more high-quality research submissions, enhancing China’s academic reputation in the field. Concurrently, the increasing number of high-quality papers published by Chinese researchers in internationally renowned journals further solidifies China’s leading position in this domain [ , ]. Meanwhile, frequent cooperation between China and the US, as well as China and Europe, shows that transnational cooperation is a key driver for research development in this field, facilitating the sharing of research resources and accelerating the clinical translation and application of research findings.
At the institutional level, Chinese universities like Sun Yat-sen University, Fudan University, and Zhejiang University lead in article publications in this field, showing China’s research strength. However, American universities such as Columbia University (21.41) and the University of Hong Kong (15.62) have higher average citation frequencies, indicating that while Chinese universities excel in quantity, they need to improve quality. International cooperation helps to increase the impact of theses. For example, Harvard University and Johns Hopkins University have significantly increased the citation frequency and academic influence of their papers through cooperation with Chinese universities.

## Authors analysis (Figure S6 and Table S2)
From the perspective of author level, scholars such as Fan Jia, Gianluigi Giannelli, and Zhou Jian are at the top of the list in terms of the number of publications and citation frequency, which indicates that they have a high academic influence in the field. At the same time, these high-impact authors have formed several close collaborative sub-networks among themselves, especially the most significant transnational collaborations between China, the United States, and China and Europe. The reasons behind this phenomenon may include: (1) Cross-national cooperation can realize the complementarity of technology, samples, and data, and enhance the depth and breadth of research – ]. (2) International high-level journals are more inclined to receive research results from multicenter and interdisciplinary cooperation, which motivates authors to carry out collaborative research [ , , ].

## Journals analysis (Figure S7 and Table S3)
In this field, research results are mainly published in renowned journals like Frontiers in Immunology, Frontiers in Oncology, Cancers, International Journal of Molecular Sciences, Hepatology, and Journal of Experimental & Clinical Cancer Research. However, journal impact factors don’t always reflect research quality. For example, Frontiers in Immunology has the most articles [ ] but an average citation frequency of only 0.64, suggesting a “quantity over quality” issue. In contrast, high-impact journals like Hepatology, Journal of Hepatology, and Nature Communications have fewer articles but higher average citation frequencies, indicating that high-quality research is still relatively rare.

## Keywords co-occurrence, clusters, timeline, and bursts (Figs.  , , and )
CiteSpace analysis revealed that the research field of CAFs and HCC showed a dynamic development trend between 2004 and 2025. Keyword co-occurrence and clustering analyses (Figs. and ) showed that CAFs play a central role in the tumor microenvironment (TME), especially promoting tumor progression and metastasis through interactions with immune cells (e.g., regulatory T cells, tumor-associated fibroblasts), growth factors (e.g., TGF-β), and signaling pathways (e.g., epithelial-mesenchymal transition, EMT). For example, Zhou et al. first found that HCC cells deliver miR-21 to hepatic star-like cells via exosomes, inhibit their PTEN expression and activate PDK1/AKT signaling, thereby converting them into CAFs; these neonatal CAFs then secrete VEGF, MMP2/9, bFGF and TGF-β, which facilitates angiogenesis and accelerates tumor growth, while serum exosome miR-21 level is positively correlated with the degree of CAFs activation and microvessel density in patients [ ]. On this basis, Song’s team further elucidated that CLCF1 secreted by CAFs induces HCC cells to upregulate CXCL6 and TGF-β, which autocrinally enhance tumor stemness and paracrinely recruit and polarize tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) to the tumor-promoting “N2” phenotype; CXCL6/TGF-β then activates ERK1/2 signaling in CAF to promote the release of more CLCF1, forming a positive feedback loop that accelerates the progression of HCC, and the blockade of CLCF1 or its downstream ERK pathway significantly suppresses the tumor [ ]. Meanwhile, Chen et al. revealed that CAFs induced tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to polarize to the M2 type and up-regulated PAI-1 expression by secreting CXCL12, and PAI-1-mediated epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) significantly enhanced the proliferation and invasion of HCC cells [ ]. The latest Lv study, on the other hand, pointed out that secretion of IL-6 by CAFs stimulated HCC cells to autocrine IGF-1, which promoted tumor cell proliferation and conferred resistance to sorafenib via the IGF-1R/STAT3/IRS-1/AKT-ERK axis, whereas NT157, an IGF-1R inhibitor, reversed CAF-mediated tumor-promoting and drug-resistant effects, suggesting that co-targeting the CAF- IGF-1 axis may be a novel strategy to improve the therapeutic efficacy of HCC [ ]. Additionally, it has been shown that CAFs synergistically drive tumor metastasis through multiple molecular axes.Liu et al. found that CAFs hypersecrete chemokine CXCL11, activate the circUBAP2/miR-4756/IFIT1/3 signaling axis in tumor cells, up-regulate IL-1β and IL-17 expression which significantly enhanced HCC cell migration and increased the number of metastatic foci in an in situ model [ ] Xu et al. reported that CAFs released CCL5 in large quantities, which inhibited the ubiquitination degradation of HIF1α and maintained its normoxic stability after binding to its receptor, and then upregulated ZEB1 and induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition, which ultimately facilitated HCC lung metastasis [ ]. Furthermore, Mai et al. demonstrated that HCC cells with high In addition, Mai et al. showed that high expression of ITGB4 in HCC cells facilitates lactate production, lactate recruitment and activation of CAFs, forming a hypoxic microenvironment. ITGB4 enhances glycolysis through AKT/HK2 and amplifies CAFs feedback via TGF-β/Smads, and inhibition of ITGB4 reduces lactate secretion, reverses CAFs activation, and significantly decreases the metastatic load of tumors [ ].The timeline graph (Fig. ) clearly demonstrates the evolution of research focus: early studies (2004–2016) focused on basic mechanisms, such as gene expression and functions of CAFs. For example, Lin and Chuang were the first to report that different HCC cell lines could differentially induce CAFs to up-regulate oncogenes such as APLN, CCL2, CCL26, FGF1/2, IL-6, LOXL2, PDGFA, PGK1, and VEGFA, although the expression of the rest of the genes was affected by CAF subpopulation-specific effects despite the generalized elevation of PGK1 and generalized decrease of PDGFA. This suggests that single-gene blockade is difficult to become a universal strategy [ ] The same team found in a follow-up study that CAFs, when counteracting HCC cells, consistently elevated the expression of CCL2, CCL26, IL-6, and LOXL2, and enhanced tumor proliferation, migration, and invasion, confirming that CAFs and HCC cells have bidirectional and partially overlapping characteristics in gene regulation [ ]. Cheng et al. further revealed that CAFs activate STAT3 signaling through secretion of IL-6, inducing dendritic cells to highly express the immunosuppressive enzyme IDO, thus upregulating the Treg-related gene network and achieving tumor immune escape; blocking IL-6, STAT3, or IDO could reverse this immunosuppressive program [ ]. More recent studies (2017–2025) are more focused on translational applications, such as combination therapies, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), and pan-cancer analysis. For instance, Gao et al. identified FAP⁺CAF interacting with naïve T cells through CXCL12-CXCR4 axis by single-cell transcriptome analysis of 43 tumors and 14 paracancerous tissues of 14 HCC patients, suggesting a CAF-mediated immune checkpoint inhibitor resistance mechanism; meanwhile, it was found that NDUFA4L2 highly expressed CAF was positively correlated to tumor progression, while HSPA1B highly expressed central memory T cells had tumor suppression potential [ ]. At the same time, it was found that CAF with high NDUFA4L2 expression was positively correlated with tumor progression, while central memory T cells with high HSPA1B expression had tumor suppressor potential [ ]. Yu et al. identified four CAFs subgroups in HCC by integrating public scRNA-seq and TCGA large-sample data and constructed a risk score based on the six CAF-related prognostic genes screened from three of these subgroups, which was independent of clinicopathological features and predicted the patient’s prognosis and response to immunotherapy [ ]. Xie et al. combined the data from 27 HCC monocytes and 374 TCGA-LIHC, and found that there was extensive communication between CAFs and HP⁺ tumor cells, Neu_AIF1⁺ neutrophils, and tumor endothelial cells, and that ErbB and GnRH signaling pathways were commonly activated in CAFs and other stromal cells, suggesting that CAFs remodels the immune microenvironment and promotes tumor development. critical role in remodeling the immune microenvironment and facilitating tumor heterogeneity [ ].In HCC research, the interaction between CAFs and tumor-associated macrophages has become a focus of attention in pan-cancer analysis. For example, Qiao et al. completed pan-cancer analysis using TCGA, GTEx, TIMER2 and other databases, and found that purine receptor P2RX4 was highly expressed in a variety of tumors, including HCC, and its level was positively correlated with the infiltration of CAFs, suggesting that P2RX4 could be used as a cross-tumor poor prognostic biomarker [ ]. Zhang’s team integrated scRNA-seq, ATAC-seq and methylation data and confirmed that the fibronectin (FN1) secreted by tumor-associated macrophages and CAFs activates the transcription factor JUN through the TNFRSF11B/SMAD3 axis, which in turn upregulates the downstream key gene of Hippo pathway, KIF13A, and thus facilitates the metastasis of HCC; this FN1-JUN-Hippo signaling axis also holds true in pan-cancer data. This FN1-JUN-Hippo signaling axis also holds true in pan-cancer data, suggesting that the CAFs-mediated metastasis mechanism is cross-tumor generalizable [ ].The emergence map (Fig. ) identifies key research turning points, such as EMT (2017–2019) and TGF-β (2018–2019) becoming research hotspots, followed by the rapid emergence of immune infiltration (2022–2023) and single-cell sequencing (2023–2025), reflecting the field’s shift toward precision medicine and immunotherapy. The factors driving the above research trends mainly include [ ] poor prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma and limited efficacy of existing treatments (e.g., anti-PD-1 therapy), prompting researchers to deeply explore the mechanism of CAFs-mediated remodeling of the tumor microenvironment in search of new therapeutic targets [ ]. The development of high-throughput technologies (e.g., single-cell RNA sequencing) has enabled researchers to deeply analyze the heterogeneity of CAFs and their spatial distribution and function in the tumor microenvironment, which has promoted a boom in the study of CAFs subtypes and their functions. Notably, the keyword “biliary tract cancer” appears in Fig. . The outbreak period for biliary tract cancer was from 2017 to 2019, with an intensity of 2.71. Although this intensity is relatively low, its emergence still warrants attention. Possible explanations include: In 2017, research indicated that mutations in certain genes—such as NF2, TERT promoter, and TP53—may simultaneously trigger the coexistence of two types of cancer cells [ , ]. Additionally, beyond inducing hepatocellular carcinoma, the hepatitis B virus (HBV) can also trigger cholangiocarcinoma. This further underscored the indispensable role of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in treating HBV-associated cholangiocarcinoma [ ]. These research findings likely sparked academic interest in biliary tract cancer, leading to an increase in related publications and consequently revealing a citation surge in the bibliometric analysis.

## Analysis of annual highly cited articles (Figure )
Phase I (2004–2013): basic research and preliminary discovery phase. During this interval, investigations centered on elucidating the fundamental biology of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), with particular emphasis on their impact on the tumor microenvironment (TME) and consequent promotion of tumor progression. Yang and colleagues provided seminal evidence that α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA)-positive CAFs are intimately linked to HCC aggressiveness; conditioned medium from α-SMA-expressing fibroblasts markedly enhanced HCC cell migration, and α-SMA levels in CAFs correlated positively with metastasis and advanced clinical stage [ ]. Lin and Chuang subsequently demonstrated that HCC cell lines exert heterogeneous effects on pro-tumorigenic gene expression within CAFs, implying that single-gene targeting strategies may be insufficient for broad-spectrum HCC therapy [ ]. Jia et al. delineated a paracrine circuit whereby HCC-derived CAFs secrete hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) to accelerate malignant cell proliferation [ ], whereas Li et al. revealed that CAF-derived prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) and indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO) synergistically impair natural killer (NK) cell cytotoxicity, thereby fostering an immunosuppressive niche conducive to tumor progression [ ]. Collectively, these early studies provided direct experimental evidence that CAFs are active drivers of HCC oncogenesis, operating through multifaceted paracrine and immunomodulatory mechanisms. Nevertheless, the inherent limitations of small sample sizes, predominant reliance on in vitro assays and murine xenograft models, and insufficient characterization of CAF heterogeneity may constrain the translational relevance of these findings to human HCC.
Phase II (2014–2016): comprehensive mechanism exploration phase. Studies in this phase began to delve into the specific mechanisms of CAFs in the tumor microenvironment, especially how they affect the function of immune cells through different signaling pathways. Liu et al.‘s study found that CAFs are able to secrete a variety of chemokines such as CCL2, CCL5, CCL7 and CXCL16, which promote the migration and invasion of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells through activation of the Hedgehog (Hh) and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) signaling pathways, which promote the migration and invasion of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells, and thus promote the metastasis of HCC [ ]. Lau et al. showed that CAFs regulate the plasticity of tumor-initiating cells in HCC and influence their malignant progression by activating the c-Met/FRA1/HEY1 signaling pathway through the secretion of hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) [ ].Yang et al. found that a subgroup of CAFs, which is, fibroblast activating protein (FAP)-expressing CAFs, was able to induce an inflammatory phenotype and promote the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) through the STAT3-CCL2 signaling pathway, thereby exerting an immunosuppressive effect in the tumor microenvironment and promoting tumor growth [ ].Ding et al. demonstrated that conditioned medium derived from MRC-5 fibroblasts (MRC-5-CM) elicits an atypical epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells, accompanied by activation of the integrin/FAK signaling axis and up-regulation of matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP2), thereby enhancing HCC cell migration and invasion. Concomitantly, MRC-5-CM exerts heterogeneous effects on the cell-cycle distribution and apoptosis of distinct HCC cell lines [ ].Song et al. reported that tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase-1 (TIMP-1) drives the transdifferentiation of hepatic stellate cells into cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and suppresses HCC apoptosis via activation of the SDF-1/CXCR4/PI3K/AKT cascade, ultimately promoting tumor progression [ ]. Kim et al. observed that CCN2, epithelial membrane antigen (EMA), and fibroblast activation protein (FAP) are markedly elevated in fibrotic HCC stroma. Notably, EMA-positive neoplastic epithelial cells are spatially juxtaposed to FAP-expressing CAFs, indicating potential tumor epithelium-stromal crosstalk that facilitates CAF activation and underpins the aggressive phenotype of HCC [ ].Collectively, these investigations provide multifaceted insights into the mechanistic roles of CAFs within the tumor microenvironment, delineating how CAFs modulate neoplastic cell behavior and immune cell function through diverse signaling networks. A broad methodological repertoire-including in vitro cellular assays, pre-clinical animal models, and human tissue-based analyses-enhances the robustness and translational relevance of the findings. Nevertheless, several limitations remain. First, most studies have focused on CAF biology in a single tumor entity, leaving the generalizability of these mechanisms across other malignancies largely unexplored. Second, while molecular and cellular mechanisms have been extensively interrogated, the spatiotemporal dynamics of CAFs in the in vivo tumor microenvironment and their complex interactions with additional stromal and immune constituents remain insufficiently characterized.
Phase III (2017–2019): Immuno-oncology phase. During this period, research interest pivoted toward the role of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in tumor immunotherapy, driven by recognition of their pleiotropic biological functions and pivotal position within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Tsuchiya et al. emphasized that the pronounced inter-and intra-tumoral heterogeneity of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) necessitates a multidimensional therapeutic approach that incorporates components of the TME, including CAFs. While underscoring CAF-tumor cell crosstalk as a promising avenue for precision oncology, their work remained primarily descriptive, with limited mechanistic dissection of CAF-specific signaling pathways [ ]. Yin and colleagues advanced the concept of CAF heterogeneity, delineating its prognostic significance and therapeutic potential in HCC progression. They proposed that CAFs facilitate tumor invasion and metastasis via diverse paracrine circuits; however, dynamic CAF remodeling within the immune landscape and their reciprocal interactions with immune effector cells remain incompletely characterized [ ].Qu et al. interrogated CAF-mediated therapeutic resistance from a TME-centric perspective, demonstrating that CAFs can modulate treatment response through non-cell-autonomous mechanisms, thereby offering novel strategies to overcome drug resistance. Nevertheless, a granular analysis of CAF-driven resistance specifically to immune checkpoint blockade is lacking [ ]. Sun et al. highlighted the emerging role of exosomes in HCC biology and proposed their cargo as non-invasive biomarkers, providing a conceptual framework for dissecting CAF-immune cell communication [ ]. Complementary studies by Yin and Tahmasebi Birgani et al. further underscored the TME as a central determinant of HCC progression, reinforcing the rationale for CAF-targeted interventions [ , ]. Future investigations should prioritize four critical areas: [ ] mechanistic elucidation of how CAFs sculpt the immune milieu via secreted factors and/or direct cell-cell contact; [ ] systematic mapping of CAF heterogeneity across distinct tumor contexts; [ ] delineation of CAF-specific mechanisms underpinning immunotherapy resistance; and [ ] development of CAF-directed, precision therapeutic strategies to enhance the efficacy of current immunotherapeutic regimens.
Phase IV (2020–2024): Therapeutic-strategy phase. Current research has shifted toward devising innovative treatment paradigms and elucidating the intricate complexity of the tumor microenvironment (TME). A central theme is the therapeutic targeting of cancer stem cells (CSCs) and the disclosure of how the TME dictates resistance and metastasis. Meng et al. reported that chromobox 2 (CBX2) endows HCC cells with CSC-like properties by promoting cell-cycle progression, proliferation, invasion, metastasis, wound healing, and radioresistance. High co-expression of CBX2 and CEP55 was associated with superior response to immune-checkpoint inhibitors, positioning these molecules as candidate therapeutic targets [ ]. Sipos and Műzes emphasized the reciprocal crosstalk between CSCs and a pro-tumorigenic inflammatory milieu, demonstrating that CSCs not only evade immune surveillance but also reprogram immune cells to foster metastasis, chemoresistance, and tumorigenesis [ ].Nallasamy et al. showed that the TME sustains CSC traits via paracrine networks of growth factors, cytokines, chemokines, miRNAs, and exosomes, thereby driving progression and metastasis [ ]. Loh and Ma specifically delineated how cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) generate a CSC-supportive niche through dysregulated paracrine signaling and aberrant extracellular-matrix remodeling [ ].Collectively, these studies underscore the imperative to deepen mechanistic understanding of TME-CSC interactions and to develop rational combination strategies-integrating CSC-directed agents with immunotherapeutics-to overcome resistance and prevent metastatic relapse.

## Frontier topics

## Origins and heterogeneity of CAFs
CAF form a highly heterogeneous group of cells that may be closely associated with their broad origin (Fig. ). NFs located in tissues are the core source of CAFs. They are converted to CAFs when activated by cancer cells or other elements of the TME [ , ].Various growth factors, cytokines, chemokines, and key molecules linked to cell signaling, such as TGF-β, fibroblast growth factor (FGF), PDGF, Sonic hedgehog (Shh), and interleukin-6 (IL-6), induce the transformation of NFs into CAFs through the activation of relevant signaling pathways [ ]. Stellate-type cells are specialized tissue-resident fibroblasts analogous to NFs that can be triggered and converted into CAFs upon specific conditions. Based on growing evidence, quiescent pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs) and hepatic stellate cells gain a CAF phenotype and function accordingly in the reaction to TGF-β, IL-1, PDGF, and other stimuli [ – ]. Bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BM-MSCs) provide a third, highly plastic lineage. In gastric tumors, Quante et al. documented that ≥ 20% of intratumoral CAFs originate from BM-MSCs in a TGF-β/SDF-1-dependent fashion [ ]. Comparable data in breast cancer implicate the myeloid zinc finger 1/TGF-β1 axis, whereas HOXA9-driven TGF-β2 transcription governs MSC-to-CAF conversion in ovarian carcinoma [ , ]. The core role of this pathway was emphasized by the inhibition of BM-MSCs pro-tumor activity and expression of classical CAF markers by TGF-β inhibitors [ , ]. The primary source of CAF also includes a range of mature and differentiated cells. Epithelial cells differentiate into CAFs via EMT [ , ], while endothelial cells differentiate via endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition [ , ]. Other findings indicate that smooth muscle cells, adipocytes and pericytes can also be converted into CAFs [ – ]; however, the exact origin of CAFs has not been fully elucidated due to the common stem cell origin of some cell types and few exclusive markers. Although techniques such as genetic traces and fluorescent tagging have made the study of cell phenotypes and type transformations easier and more reliable [ , ], the exact biological origin of CAFs still needs to be explored.
 

## Functional heterogeneity of CAFs
CAF heterogeneity is featured by distinctive divergence in phenotype and function associated with tumor type, tumor stage, and other characteristics (Fig. ) [ , ]. The diverse potential sources of CAFs may explain somewhat their heterogeneity. Besides, the flexibility of CAFs and the stimulation of varied signaling pathways leading to shifts in CAF phenotype and function may be another crucial cause of CAF heterogeneity [ ].There is increasing evidence that CAF can facilitate liver tumorigenesis, immune escape, metastasis and proliferation through exosome secretion, ECM remodeling and cytokine secretion [ , – ].Bioinformatics analysis showed that KLF2 expression was higher in fibroblasts and lower in HCC progression related to liver fibrosis and cirrhosis [ ].In addition, Song et al. demonstrated that serum levels of cardiotrophic factor-like cytokine factor 1 (CLCF1), a primary CAF-derived cytokine in patients with HCC, were strongly correlated with tumor lymph node metastasis, cirrhosis and poor prognosis [ ]. Recently, work has documented the existence of several CAF-secreted cytokines in hepatocellular carcinoma, including C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL-2), IL-8,IL-6, and C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12). It was further demonstrated that these cytokines inhibit CD8 + T cells and facilitate macrophage polarity to the M2 phenotype [ , ]. A preclinical mouse model study demonstrated that CAF-derived CCL5 depresses ubiquitination and degradation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF1α). Upregulation of the expression of genes located in the downstream of the HIF1α pathway, such as zinc finger enhancer binding protein 1 (ZEB1), has reportedly induced EMT and facilitated HCC lung metastasis [ ].A recently research clarified that CAF-derived exosomes could facilitate HCC malignant phenotypic transformation and immune escape. Shang et al. indicated that in hypoxic conditions, exosomes secreted by CAFs facilitated HCC cell proliferation, invasion, and immune escape, and also exhibited a high level of circHIF1A [ ].In addition, multiple microRNAs in CAF-derived exosomes have been described to regulate tumorigenesis. For example, the CAF-secreted exosome miR-92a-3p has been demonstrated to bind to the AXIN1/β-catenin axis in vivo, thereby fostering tumor development and stemness [ ].Jing et al. demonstrated that the CAF subpopulation, F5-CAF, facilitated the stemness of HCC cells in vitro [ ].Furthermore, it was shown that CAFs regulate the stemness properties of tumor cells as a way to strengthen disease progression in HCC [ – ]. Gene expression profiles of HCC patient samples demonstrated a remarkable positive association between CAFs with high levels of placental growth factor (PlGF) and neoangiogenesis; therefore, it was concluded that the existence of such cells in the TME of HCC suggests a poor prognosis [ ]. Concordant with these findings, cell-based studies have shown that hepatocellular CAFs are capable of facilitating VM formation by an upregulation of the expression of VM-associated genes (MMP2 and EphA2) [ ].
 

## Mechanism research of CAF regulating HCC immunotherapy
Current studies know that immune cells in TME, such as TAN, CAF, cytotoxic CD8 + T cells, TAM and NAN cells, either alone or in conjunction, plays critical roles in tumor progression and immunoresistance (Fig.  ).
For example, IL-6 secreted by CAFs can promote the activation of PD-L1-positive TAN by means of the JAK/STAT3 pathway, thereby damaging the function of T cells and triggering immunoresistance in HCC [ ]. Additionally, CLCF1 secreted by CAFs can overregulate the expression of CXCL6 and TGF-β by AKT/STAT3 signaling pathway. With xenograft mouse models of HCC, CLCF1 was demonstrated to promote TAN polarization to an “N2” phenotype, which aggravates HCC [ ]. CD36 + CAF-derived macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) was proved to reinforce the accumulation of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which exerted their effects in an IL-6/STAT3 activation-dependent manner. STAT3 activation-dependent immunosuppression and tumor stemness. By targeting CD36 in a mouse model of HCC potentiated the efficacy of anti-PD-1 therapy, which was correlated with reductions in Tregs and MDSC as well as increases in IFN-γ + and granzyme B (GzmB) + CD8 + T cells [ , ]. Similarly, it has been reported that CD36 + CAFs may upregulate PD-1 expression through the release of inhibitory cytokines, thereby inducing CTL depletion and contributing to poor clinical outcomes in HCC patients [ ].Earlier multi-omics-based work has shown that CAFs can interplay with SPP1 + macrophages to facilitate tumor immune barrier (TIB) formation; this may in part explain the reduction in CD8 + T-cell infiltration related to immunotherapy in non-responders. Targeting SPP1 suppressed TIB formation, diminished CAF infiltration, and boosted cytotoxic T-cell infiltration, thereby improving the efficacy of targeting PD-1 in a mouse model of HCC [ ]. Using single-cell and spatial analyses, accumulation of SPP1 + macrophages and cancer stem cells spurred by hypoxic conditions may contribute to immune resistance and poorer prognosis in HCC [ ]. Furthermore, in vitro and in vivo work has indicated that IL34 + CAFs play a key role in immunomodulation in the form fostering Treg infiltration, depressing CD8 + T-cell cytotoxicity by markedly diminishing GzmB generation, and eventually facilitating the growth and metastasis of HCC [ ].The impact of CAFs stretches to other types of immunotherapies. In this regard, former work has demonstrated that FAP expression in CAFs positively associated with the expression of PD-L1, 2, PD-1, and CTLA4 in HCC cells, indicating that CAFs may influence the prognosis of HCC patients by modulating cellular immune [ ]. Luo and colleagues revealed that tumor-infiltrating CAFs triggered the Forkhead box Q1 (FOXQ1)/N-myc downstream regulated gene 1 (NDRG1) axis in HCC cells to promote the initiation of HCC, and that the FOXQ1/NDRG1 axis could be fed back into the assembly of infiltrating CAFs [ ]. In addition, in sclerosing hepatocellular carcinoma (SHCC), insulin-like growth factor 2 (IGF2) secreted by tumor cells can activate CAFs through the NF-κb signaling pathway [ ].CAF-derived exosomes also impact the actions of immune cells and cancer cells through a variety of mechanisms. For example, high expression of circHIF1A in exosomes secreted by CAFs under stress treatment (hypoxia) regulates the proliferation of CD8 + T cells; they also interplay with the Hu antigen R (HuR) to up-regulate PD-L1 in HCC cells, implying that inhibition of circHIF1A in CAF-derived exosomes may potentially reverse resistance to PD-1/PD-L1 therapy [ ]. ECM plays a key role in regulating TME and immune-mediated tumor regression in solid cancers [ – ]. In HCC, CAF secreted TGF-β1 enhances Sema3C expression by activating AP1 signaling, which facilitates ECM contraction and activates HSC. mechanistically, Sema3C binds to neuropilins 1 (NRP1) and integrin β 1 (ITGB1) in HSC to stimulate IL-6 release and upregulate HMGCR expression by triggering the activation of downstream NF-kB signaling [ ]. Spatial transcriptomic analysis of clinical samples from advanced HCC patients receiving neoadjuvant treatment with cabozantinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and navulizumab demonstrated that with increased collagen type I α 1 (COL1A1) and collagen type III α 1 (COL3A1), matrix and reactive remodeling assembles CAF and triggers immune rejection, leading to immune cell exhaustion. Put differently, immunotherapeutic agents in combination with CAF inhibitors may improve treatment efficacy in patients with HCC [ ]. Taken together, these findings enhance the understanding of how CAFs affect the efficacy of HCC immunotherapy and provide a foundation for follow-up studies to detect additional mechanisms of CAFs in HCC TME.

## Current status of targeted CAF therapy
Therapeutic approaches targeting CAF are currently being developed in both laboratory and clinical settings. Relevant treatment strategies are outlined below, as shown in Fig.  .
 

## Biological agent therapeutic treatment
Currently, multiple researches have discussed the role of TGF-β in modulating immunosuppressive properties in HCC TME as well as strategies to counteract such immunosuppressive effects [ – ]. For example, YM101 (Y101D) is a bispecific antibody (BsAb) that binds to both TGF-β and PD-L1, and Yi and his team have demonstrated in preclinical studies that YM101 facilitates the infiltration of lymphocytes and dendritic cells, as well as enhances anti-tumor immune responsiveness [ , ]. Notably, YM101 has entered the clinical phase; a phase I/II trial (CTR20230372) is in progress to evaluate the security and efficiency of YM101 in combination with bevacizumab as a first-line treatment for advanced HCC. Other second-generation TGF-β/PD-L1 BsAbs, such as SHR-1701, BPB-101, and JS201, have also entered the clinical phase for the treatment of advanced solid tumors. For instance, a phase I clinical trial (NCT03710265) showed that SHR-1701 showed promising antitumor effects and a manageable safety profile in HCC, biliary tract cancer (BTC) or gastric cancer (GC) patients [ ].Other BsAbs targeting FAP have been successfully developed recently; simlukafusp alfa (FAP-IL2v) is in a phase I clinical trial (NCT02627274) in metastatic and aggressive solid tumors [ ]. A Phase I trial (NCT04958239) is underway to test the effectiveness of BI-765,179, a BsAb who targets FAP and CD137, both alone and in conjunction with ezabenlimab, in the treatment of patients with advanced solid carcinomas. In addition, OMTX705 is a new antibody-drug coupling (ADC) comprising a humanized anti-FAP monoclonal antibody (mAb) and a microtubule inhibitor. A Phase I clinical trial (NCT05547321) is ongoing to test the effectiveness of OMTX705 as monotherapy and in conjunction with pembrolizumab in the treatment of advanced solid tumors. Currently, although clinical trials provide limited insight into the efficacy of CAF biologics as therapeutic agents for HCC, their therapeutic potential can be discerned based on data from preclinical studies.

## Chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell-based anticancer therapies and NK cell therapies
Chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T)cell-based anticancer therapies have displayed potential in different kinds of carcinomas, including HCC. Bispecific CAR-T cells targeting FAP and GPC3 have shown promising antitumor effectiveness in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models of HCC [ ]. Fourth-generation CAR-T cells targeting Nectin4/FAP expressing IL-7 and CCL19 or IL-12 have been tested in a phase I clinical trial (NCT03932565) to evaluate their capability to eradicate residual cancer cells to the maximum extent possible and to prevent recurrence in patients with aggressive malignant solid neoplasms.NK cell therapies have also been utilized as an immunotherapeutic modality for HCC and other cancers [ , ]. A phase I/II clinical trial (NCT06026657) was initiated in 2023 to assess the efficacy and safety of TGF-β imprinted NK cells in conjunction with gemcitabine +/- nacilimumab in the treatment of metastatic breast cancer.

## Small molecule inhibitors
Currently, small-molecule inhibitors targeting effector molecules of CAFs, such as fibroblast growth factor receptor 4 (FGFR4) and TGF-β, are undergoing clinical trials. Taking nisotocitinib (also known as BLU-554) as an example, it is a potent FGFR4 selective inhibitor. In a Phase Ib/II clinical trial (NCT04194801), researchers evaluated the safety and antitumor efficacy of nisogatinib in patients with locally advanced or metastatic hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Results from July 2020 to December 2021 showed that among 4 patients treated with nisoldipine plus sugemalimab (CS1001), the objective response rate (ORR) reached 50% and the disease control rate (DCR) was 100% [ ]. Another FGFR4 inhibitor, H3B-6527, is currently undergoing a Phase I clinical trial (NCT02834780) for patients with advanced HCC or intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC). H3B-6527 has been reported to be well tolerated and has demonstrated preliminary evidence of clinical activity. Specifically, two patients achieved partial response (PR), while seven others maintained disease stability for over five months [ ]. Additionally, small-molecule inhibitors targeting TGF-β have also entered clinical trial phases. In an open-label Phase II clinical trial (NCT01246986), Kelley et al. found that among patients with advanced HCC who had not previously received systemic therapy, treatment with galunisertib (a TGF-β inhibitor) resulted in a median time to progression (TTP) of 4.1 months (95% confidence interval: 2.8–6.5), and a median overall survival (OS) of 18.8 months (95% CI: 14.8–24.8) [ ]. In addition to known drugs, other small-molecule inhibitors targeting TGF-β, such as YL-13,027 and GFH018, have also been developed. Ongoing clinical trials (NCT03869632 and NCT05051241) are evaluating the safety and pharmacokinetic properties of these drugs in treating patients with advanced solid tumors. Dr. Guo and his team reported that GFH018 demonstrated favorable safety and moderate efficacy in treating patients with advanced solid tumors [ ]. Talabostat (also known as PT-100, Val-boroPro, and BXCL701) is a small-molecule inhibitor that targets both DPP4 and FAP [ ]. Currently, a Phase II basket study (NCT04171219) is evaluating the efficacy of talabostat in combination with pembrolizumab for the treatment of advanced solid tumors; however, the results of this study have not yet been published.

## Radiotherapy
Radionuclides like 177Lu, 225Ac and 153Sm are routinely used in the treatment of cancer patients. Past studies have shown that radionuclide drug coupling compounds (RDCs), particularly FAP-targeted ligands with radionuclides, can inhibit tumor progression in mice [ – ]. Preclinical evaluation of 177Lu-FAP-2286 was reported to display antitumor activity in a FAP-expressing HEK293 mouse model and in xenografts derived from sarcoma patients with minimal weight loss [ ]. A phase I/II clinical trial of 177 Lu-FAP-2286 (NCT04939610) demonstrated a promising safety profile and long-term preservation of the radioactive peptide in multiple types of cancers [ ]. Furthermore, in a tumor mouse model, 177 Lu-LNC1004 in conjunction with anti-PD-L1 was proven to reprogram TME and facilitate anti-tumor immunity by enhancing CD8 + T-cell activation, M1 macrophage cells counts, and T-cell receptor diversity [ ].

## Vaccinotherapy
The efficacy of FAP-targeted DNA vaccines in collaboration with other tumor vaccines, chemotherapies, or immunotherapies to improve treatment effectiveness in cancer patients has been studied before [ , ]. As an example, a new chimeric DNA vaccine targeting and surviving human FAP was found to decrease the count of immunosuppressive cells, facilitate lymphocyte infiltration, and remodel TME in a manner that facilitates anti-tumor immunity, and a potential therapeutic utility has been reported by combining a FAP/surviving co-targeted DNA vaccine with low-dose gemcitabine in a Panc02 mouse model of pancreatic carcinoma [ ].To date, very limited progress has been made in the field of CAF-targeted anti-HCC vaccine development. Regrettably, despite the above discussion, most of the described CAF-targeted therapeutic modalities are still in the early clinical and preclinical stages [ ].
Bibliometric analysis is an important method for studying the trajectory of a subject area and identifying key research directions. But it must be recognized that this method is not flawless. First, depending on the WOSCC database to search for bibliometric information may result in relevant data being missed by other databases. Second, disregarding the most recent publications from 2025 may not capture the up-to-date research hotspots and trends more comprehensively. Third, despite double-checking measures, duplicate or inaccurate entries still need to be manually corrected. Fourth, the inherent limitations of bibliometric algorithms tend to ignore the contributions of emerging researchers, and information biases between different measurement software can lead to mixed results. Finally, the exclusion of “articles” and “reviews” from the inclusion criteria may lead to bias, but the overall bias is manageable due to the small number of exclusions.
In recent years, research on hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) has attracted much attention, and the significant growth in the number of related papers confirms their increasing importance. In this study, we systematically sorted out the countries, top institutions and researchers involved in HCC and CAF research worldwide. It has become a hot research topic to deeply investigate the tumor-promoting mechanism of CAF, to develop precise targeted therapies and to overcome TME-mediated drug resistance. The long-term prognosis of patients receiving targeted therapies may become a key direction for future studies. Researchers in this field can take this opportunity to fully grasp the development and cutting-edge direction of this field.
