## Visual Abstract
 

## Key Points

In human renal mesangial cells stimulated with endothelin-1, atrasentan reduced mesangial cell proliferation and matrix expansion.
Atrasentan reduced kidney injury in two preclinical models of IgA nephropathy.
These findings support the therapeutic potential of atrasentan to treat IgA nephropathy; clinical studies of atrasentan are ongoing.

## Background
IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is the leading cause of primary GN and carries a high risk of progression to ESKD. Endothelin-1 type A (ETA) receptor activation may be a key driver of proteinuria, inflammation, and fibrosis in patients with kidney diseases. Atrasentan, a highly potent and highly selective antagonist of the ETA receptor, demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful proteinuria reduction of 36.1% (95% confidence interval, 26.4% to 44.6%; P < 0.001) relative to placebo, at week 36 in the ongoing Atrasentan in Patients With IgA Nephropathy (ALIGN) phase 3 trial. Based on these data, atrasentan is indicated to reduce proteinuria in adults with primary IgAN at risk of rapid disease progression.

## Methods
We explored the biologic effects and changes in gene transcription with atrasentan in cultured human renal mesangial cells, grouped ddY mouse model of IgAN, and Wistar rats injected with anti-Thy1.1 (CD90) antibody to induce mesangial injury and mesangioproliferative GN. The effect of atrasentan on proteinuria and kidney gene transcriptome was evaluated in both animal models, and histologic and morphometric assessments of rat kidneys were conducted.

## Results
In separate transcriptomics analyses of all three models, atrasentan reversed gene expression changes related to cell proliferation, proinflammatory, and profibrotic signatures. In human renal mesangial cells stimulated with endothelin-1, atrasentan reduced mesangial cell proliferation and matrix expansion. After 7 days, atrasentan treatment in the mesangioproliferative GN rat model diminished renal mesangial hypercellularity and matrix expansion, decreased glomerular and tubulointerstitial structural alterations, and significantly reduced proteinuria. In grouped ddY mice, atrasentan treatment for 4 days significantly reduced mean albuminuria by >60%.

## Conclusions
The acute mechanistic effects of atrasentan demonstrated in vitro and in vivo support the therapeutic potential of atrasentan in IgAN. The ongoing Atrasentan in Patients With Proteinuric Glomerular Diseases (AFFINITY) and Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Crossover Study of Atrasentan in Subjects With IgA Nephropathy (ASSIST) phase 2 trials, and the ALIGN phase 3 trial in patients with IgAN will further evaluate the translation of these mechanistic effects to the clinical efficacy and safety of atrasentan.

## In Vitro and In Vivo Transcriptomic Changes with Atrasentan Treatment on Cell Proliferation, Proinflammation, and Profibrotic Pathways

## Cultured HRMCs
Atrasentan caused concentration-dependent changes in gene expression in HRMCs stimulated by 4 nM ET-1 (Figure , A and B). Atrasentan treatment was strongly and negatively correlated with ET-1 stimulation of gene transcription (Figure C, Pearson correlation r=−0.79), suggesting that atrasentan directly reverses ET-1–driven transcriptomic changes in HRMCs.
 ET-1 stimulation significantly increased the transcription of gene sets such as G2M checkpoint and E2F targets (involved in cell proliferation); TNFα signaling, IL2/STAT5, IL6/Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription signaling, and complement (proinflammatory genes); as well as epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (profibrotic genes). For all gene sets studied, atrasentan reversed ET-1–induced transcriptional changes (Figure D).
To infer potential primary transcriptomic changes in HRMC mediated by atrasentan, we analyzed the DEGs induced by atrasentan (1 and 25 nM) on untreated HRMC using only the statistical significance threshold of adjusted P value < 0.05 with no threshold set on the log-fold change. We observed 27 DEGs in the low concentration atrasentan (1 nM) and 60 DEGs in the high concentration of atrasentan (25 nM). Eight genes (PTGFR, GNG11, C1R, trio rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor, FAT1, SORT1, GCNT4, aggrecan) showed robust change in both concentrations (Supplemental Figures 1 and 2). The minimal overlap in the number of DEGs across the two concentrations could be a result of (1) technical noise and sequencing variance; (2) nonspecific effects of atrasentan on untreated HRMC; or (3) endogenous baseline activation of the ET-1 receptor in cultured HRMC; thus, the DEGs reflect the effects of ET-1 receptor inhibition by atrasentan.
Alluding to these questions and to infer potential biologic function, we then performed over-representation analysis (ORA) using the 27 DEGs as a gene set. The ORA showed these genes mediated by low concentration atrasentan are likely involved in focal adhesion, phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein kinase B signaling, and integrin/extracellular matrix signaling pathways (Supplemental Figures 3 and 4). To unmask the potential primary effects further, we ask the question whether the 27 DEG signature can be found in the ET-1 reversal gene signature of atrasentan. We observed that 14 genes of the 27 DEGs demonstrated a reversal pattern (i.e., genes that are upregulated by ET-1 were downregulated by atrasentan and vice versa) in HRMCs treated with 25 nM atrasentan in the presence of 4 nM ET-1 (Supplemental Figure 5). Further pathway enrichment analysis of this reversal 14 gene set demonstrates functional enrichment for focal adhesion, cytoskeleton, and syndecan and basigin interactions (Supplemental Figures 6 and 7).
Taken together, the transcriptomics analysis suggests that the primary action of atrasentan in mesangial cells is indeed the inhibition of the ET-1 receptor and that this inhibition of potential low grade baseline activation of ET-1 receptor in absence of exogenous ET-1 stimulation could affect focal adhesion, phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein kinase B signaling, and integrin/extracellular matrix signaling pathways.

## gddY Mice
Atrasentan altered gene transcription in a dose-dependent manner (Figure , A–C) and reversed trends in gene expression observed in patients with IgAN by downregulating key genes mediating proliferative, inflammatory, and fibrotic signaling pathways, as well as ETA receptor target genes (Figure D), including PDGF, NF kappa-light chain-enhancer of activated B cells, IL-6, and TNF (Figure E).
 

## Anti-Thy1.1 Rat Model of MPGN
Anti-Thy1.1–induced kidney injury led to the upregulation of 2331 kidney genes and downregulation of 1441 genes (Figure A), while atrasentan treatment led to downregulated expression of 1033 genes and upregulated expression of 504 genes compared with anti-Thy1.1 treatment, for a total of 1537 DEGs (log-fold change of >0.58 or <−0.58; Padj ≤ 0.05; Figure B). Atrasentan treatment generally reversed gene expression induced by anti-Thy1.1; 1132 genes upregulated by anti-Thy1.1 were downregulated by atrasentan, and the transcription of 514 genes downregulated by anti-Thy1.1 were upregulated by atrasentan (Figure C). Atrasentan also significantly reduced the failed repair signature score, which indicates the presence of proinflammatory and profibrotic proximal tubule genes that can prevent recovery from AKI (Figure D). Anti-Thy1.1 upregulated gene networks were associated with cell proliferation, inflammation, and fibrosis, consistent with the hallmark gene sets dysregulated in the glomeruli of patients with IgAN, whereas downregulated genes were involved in metabolic processes such as oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid metabolism. Atrasentan treatment downregulated the intrarenal proliferative, inflammatory, and fibrotic transcriptional networks and upregulated the metabolic networks (Figure E).
 

## Biologic Effects of Atrasentan In Vivo

## gddY Mice
To determine the effects of different atrasentan doses on kidney function, proteinuria was assessed as UACR at baseline before atrasentan administration and on the fourth day of atrasentan administration. At baseline, mean (±SEM) UACR was 293.1±16 mg/g in gddY mice overall, with no differences between the treatment groups (Figure A). This degree of UACR indicates substantial proteinuria; for comparison, healthy bagg albino mice (appropriate murine control strain) have almost undetectable levels of UACR through 2 years. On day 4 of the study, atrasentan 10, 20, and 30 mg/kg reduced UACR by 28%±44%, 62%±8%, and 63%±6%, respectively (Figure B).
 

## Anti-Thy1.1 Rat Model of MPGN
Vehicle-treated rats injected with anti-Thy1.1 had marked increases in UPCR compared with healthy rats. Atrasentan treatment reduced proteinuria compared with vehicle in anti-Thy1.1 rats over the 8-day study (Figure A). One day after the anti-Thy1.1 injection, proteinuria increased by 19.97±1.82 mg/mg in vehicle-treated rats, 3.23±1.27 mg/mg in atrasentan-treated rats, and 0.70±0.11 mg/mg in control rats (no anti-Thy1.1 injection). Atrasentan consistently reduced proteinuria throughout the study, although, on day 7, the effect failed to achieve statistical significance (P = 0.0577 versus vehicle).
 All rats in the study had similar body weights at baseline and study completion (Table ). Significant increases in mean kidney weight were observed in vehicle-treated anti-Thy1.1 rats relative to healthy controls (P = 0.0115), whereas the mean kidney weight in atrasentan-treated rats was significantly less than that in vehicle-treated animals (P = 0.0333; Figure B). When adjusted for body weight, treatment-group differences remained significant (P = 0.0159; Figure B). Kidney weight increases were associated with increases in mesangial proliferation, matrix expansion, and accompanying inflammation in anti-Thy1.1 rats treated with vehicle, whereas significant reductions in histologic signs of kidney injury, including mesangial hypercellularity and matrix expansion, glomerular adhesions, segmental mesangiolysis, and glomerulosclerosis, were observed in atrasentan-treated animals (Figure C and Supplemental Table 3). Mean Glomerular and Tubular Injury scores were significantly greater in vehicle-treated anti-Thy1.1 rats than in both healthy controls and atrasentan-treated rats (Figure D).
 Although bulk RNA sequencing in vivo limits mesangial cell-specific interpretations, and cross-species integration presents additional challenges to interpretation, the results of the gene set enrichment analysis indicate that atrasentan consistently downregulates proliferative, proinflammatory, and profibrotic pathways across all three models (human mesangial cells, gddY mice, and anti-Thy1.1 rats), thus supporting the robustness of these findings.

## Discussion
The results of preclinical studies described herein show that atrasentan has a consistent mechanistic benefit at the transcriptomic, cellular, and systemic levels in cell culture and animal models of proteinuric kidney diseases. In analyses of genes expressed in ET-1–stimulated HRMCs, gddY mouse models of IgAN, and anti-Thy1.1 Wistar rat models of MPGN, atrasentan normalized the transcription of cell proliferative, proinflammatory, and profibrotic genes. In HRMCs, atrasentan blocked mesangial cell activation and proliferation caused by exogenous ET-1. The observation that atrasentan prevents HRMC hyperproliferation in response to IgA-IC from patients with IgAN suggests that the autocrine action of endogenously produced ET-1 on ETA receptors contributes to mesangial cell activation by pathogenic IgA-IC. These results support the role of ET-1 in mediating mesangial cell activation and proliferation in IgAN. It is interesting to note that while atrasentan inhibited IL-6 production and mesangial cell proliferation with similar IC50 values after 48 hours, a higher atrasentan concentration was needed to inhibit the same level of cell proliferation but not IL-6 secretion at 72 hours. As ET-1–mediated mesangial cell proliferation is a result of multiple and complex signaling pathway interplay as evident by the transcriptomic analysis in this study, and cytokines may act on different points of the mesangial cell cycles, whether IL-6 contributes to earlier phase of the cell cycle progression and the overall ET-1-mediated cell proliferation in mesangial cells requires further investigation. Furthermore, ET-1 plays an important role in kidney injury in rodent models of IgAN. Atrasentan treatment attenuated kidney dysfunction by decreasing glomerular and tubulointerstitial structural alterations in anti-Thy1.1 rats and reduced proteinuria in both mouse and rat models. In addition, atrasentan may have reduced proteinuria in both animal models by modulating renal hemodynamic and inflammation mechanisms. While the disease models used in this study have low variability, and therefore relatively low sample sizes are sufficient to generate robust results, findings from the animal studies should nonetheless be interpreted in light of the small sample sizes, particularly in the gddY mouse study.
In IgAN, decreased proteinuria is the strongest predictor of clinical outcomes. In gddY mice, atrasentan reduced albuminuria by >60% after only 4 days of treatment at clinically relevant plasma exposures, whereas anti-Thy1.1 rat models of MPGN treated with atrasentan had near-normal proteinuria levels throughout the study. These findings are consistent with results from clinical studies. Significant, near-maximal reductions in UACR were observed within 7 days of atrasentan initiation in patients with diabetic kidney disease. Within 3 hours of starting intravenous administration of the selective ETA antagonist BQ123, peak reductions in proteinuria of >30% were observed in patients with nondiabetic CKD. The results of the present preclinical studies show that atrasentan rapidly reduces albuminuria.
Whether the mechanistic effects of atrasentan reported herein translate into clinical benefits beyond IgAN is currently under investigation in the Atrasentan in Patients With Proteinuric Glomerular Diseases (AFFINITY, NCT04573920) phase 2, open-label, basket study designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of atrasentan in patients with proteinuric glomerular disease who are at risk of progressive loss of kidney function, including IgAN, FSGS, Alport syndrome, and diabetic kidney disease treated with RASi and an SGLT2i. The primary objective of Atrasentan in Patients With Proteinuric Glomerular Diseases is to evaluate the effect of atrasentan on proteinuria. In an analysis of patients in the IgAN cohort treated for 1 year, atrasentan was generally well-tolerated with no treatment-related serious adverse events; at week 52, 24-hour UPCR was reduced by 45.4% from baseline (95% confidence interval, 24.0 to 60.7). 
ALIGN (NCT04573478) is an ongoing phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial in patients with IgAN at risk of progressive loss of kidney function who will be randomly assigned (1:1) to atrasentan 0.75 mg or placebo in addition to maximally tolerated RASi therapy for 132 weeks. An exploratory stratum enroled participants also receiving a stable dose of SGLT2is. The primary objective is to evaluate the effect of atrasentan versus placebo on proteinuria as measured by UPCR at week 36. Secondary and tertiary objectives include evaluation of eGFR to week 136 and safety and tolerability. Interim results from the first 270 randomized patients show that atrasentan significantly reduced the 24-hour UPCR by 36.1% at 36 weeks compared with placebo (P < 0.001), with reductions observed as early as 6 weeks. 
The ongoing results of these trials will confirm the safety and efficacy of atrasentan in IgAN as well as other proteinuric glomerular diseases. In addition, the ongoing Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Crossover Study of Atrasentan in Subjects With IgA Nephropathy (ASSIST) randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover study of atrasentan in IgAN will further evaluate the safety and efficacy of atrasentan versus placebo in patients with IgAN while on background therapy with an SGLT2i.
Recently, sparsentan received full approval for IgAN in the United States, but access is restricted under the risk evaluation and mitigation strategies program because of the potential for liver and embryo-fetal toxicity. Although targeted release-budesonide has also received full approval to treat IgAN, patients can experience adverse events common to systemic corticosteroid use, and the current recommended duration of therapy is 9 months. These limitations leave an ongoing need for effective treatments with a favorable safety and tolerability profile that will reduce proteinuria and slow kidney disease progression. In ALIGN, the most common adverse reactions seen in the 36 week Interim Analysis data from the first 270 patients treated (≥5%) were peripheral edema (10% and 7% for atrasentan and placebo, respectively) and anemia (6% and 1% for atrasentan and placebo, respectively). Atrasentan has demonstrated a favorable hepatic safety profile across clinical trials involving more than 5000 patients with IgAN and diabetic nephropathy, with no clinically meaningful serious liver toxicity observed.
Mechanistic studies in cultured human cells and animal models reported herein have demonstrated that atrasentan effectively reduces mesangial cell proliferation, matrix expansion, histologic evidence of glomerular damage; reverses transcription of genes that promote cell proliferation and inflammatory and fibrotic processes; reduces proteinuria; and ameliorates kidney injury. These results support the clinical effects of atrasentan observed to date in IgAN and its ongoing investigation in other proteinuric kidney diseases.
