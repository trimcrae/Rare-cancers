## Abstract

## Background
Radiolabeled FAP-targeting sdAb 4AH29 was developed previously, showing potent therapeutic efficacy. This head-to-head study compares sdAb 4AH29 and peptide FAP-2286, generally considered as most clinically advanced, for their in vitro and in vivo tumor retention, resulting dosimetry, and therapeutic efficacy in relevant rodent tumor models.

## Results
In vitro, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 showed sustained cell-associated activity until 48 h, whereas it dropped to baseline within the first hours for [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286. In vivo, in U87-MG, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 accumulated better at the tumor at early time points but from 24 h was significantly less retained than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29, leading to a threefold higher absorbed dose to the tumor for [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29. Compared to U87-MG, absolute tumor uptake for both tracers in HT-29 was decreased, in line with lower FAP expression of this model. Of note, significant improved tumor retention was observed for [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 over time, resulting in a twofold higher tumor absorbed dose. The transfected HEK-hFAP model showed an enhanced tumor uptake with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 at all assessed time points, displaying a 4.8 more elevated absorbed dose to tumor than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29. Treatment with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 or [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 significantly delayed U87-MG tumor development compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 43 days post inoculation. Survival was improved after administration of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, whereas it was a trend with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29. In HT-29, no significant differences in tumor control were observed at day 29 before first animal reached humane endpoint. Nevertheless, enhanced survival was achieved with a single shot of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 at 120 kBq.

## Conclusions
These preclinical results underline the potential benefit of radiolabeled 4AH29 over FAP-2286 due to its enhanced tumor retention and more pronounced anti-tumor effects warranting clinical investigation.

## Background
Fibroblast activation protein alpha (FAP) is a pan-tumor marker present in stromal tissue, allowing the targeting of broad cancer indications, such as breast, lung, pancreatic and esophageal tumors [ ]. FAP is overexpressed on cancer associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and can also be detected at the surface of certain tumors like sarcoma and glioblastoma, while displaying limited expression on healthy tissues [ ]. Meta-analysis revealed that high FAP expression was associated with increased risk of metastasis [ ].
Radioligand therapy (RT) relies on the delivery of radioactive isotopes by a vector targeting the tumor. This promising approach aimed to preserve healthy tissues, while inducing cell death in primary tumors and distant metastasis. Strategies include the use of alpha emitters, such as 225Ac or beta-emitters, such as 177Lu, bound to monoclonal antibodies, fragments thereof, synthetic proteins, peptides and small molecules [ ]. In addition, administration of an imaging radioligand permits the visualization of tumors, while optimizing the selection of patients more likely to benefit from this approach. FAP-targeting using FAP enzyme inhibitors (FAPI) showed exciting results regarding patient imaging, as seen with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAPI-04 or [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-FAPI-46 [ ]. Those FAPI PET tracers exhibited superiority compared to conventional [18F]FDG to detect primary and metastatic lesions in gastrointestinal cancers, breast and ovarian cancers [ ]. Other advantages of FAPI over [18F]FDG includes higher lesion to background ratio, fast renal clearance and no reliance on blood glucose [ ]. Preclinical evaluation of FAPI such as [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 showed promising results with one single administration in high FAP+ cancer models, including FAP-overexpressing HEK-293 or patient-derived sarcoma [ ]. In addition, first-in-human evaluation of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, considered now the most clinically advanced therapeutic FAP-targeting compound, was well tolerated with manageable side effects in a broad range of cancers [ ]. Nevertheless, the perceived limited tumor retention of radiolabeled FAP-2286 likely impacts its efficacy to achieve a positive clinical outcome. Single-domain antibodies (sdAbs) are promising candidate for RT due to their prolonged tumor retention and rapid blood clearance [ ]. We previously developed 4AH29, a sdAb targeting mouse/human FAP without impairing FAP dimerization and binding outside its catalytic pocket with a high affinity and specificity. In rodents, 4AH29 radiolabeled with 225Ac or 131I showed dose-response therapeutic efficiency and improved survival in tumor-bearing mice [ ]. Importantly, in immunocompetent tumor-bearing mice, 4AH29 radiolabeled with 225Ac synergized with anti PD-L1 checkpoint blockade [ ].
Here we compare head-to-head sdAb 4AH29 and peptide FAP-2286 for their in vitro and in vivo tumor retention, resulting dosimetry, and therapeutic efficacy in various models. The use of cancer cell lines displaying a wide range of FAP levels enables an in-depth preclinical evaluation that may more closely reflects the clinical heterogeneity observed in patients.

## Materials and methods
A detailed description of materials and methods can be found in the supplementary materials.

## Animal models
Female Crl: NU(NCr)-Foxn1nu mice (Charles River, Massachusetts, USA) were injected subcutaneously (s.c.) into the neck with 2 × 106 U87-MG cells, 10 × 106 HT-29 cells or 5 × 106 HEK-hFAP cells. Tumor dimensions were measured using a digital caliper (length x width x width / 2). Animal studies were approved by the ethical committee of the Vrije Universiteit Brussel (ECD 24-272-04, 24-272-25 and 24-272-34). Due to the tendency of HT-29 to develop necrosis, humane endpoint was set at 1500 mm3, whereas it was set at 2000 mm3 with U87-MG tumors.

## Radiolabeling
Radiolabeling procedures for sdAb 4AH29, peptide FAP-2286, and small molecule FAPI-46 are described in details in the dedicated supplementary data section. Briefly, sdAb 4AH29 was radiolabeled with 111In, 177Lu, and 225Ac at a selected pH for 30 min at 50 °C, then purified using NAP5 SEC, formulated in a chosen formulation mixture, and filtered through a 0.22 μm filter. FAPI-46 and FAP-2286 were radiolabeled with 111In and 177Lu at a selected pH for 10 min at 100 °C, then purified with a SepPak cartridge, formulated in a specific formulation mixture, and filtered through a 0.22 μm filter.

## In vitro cellular retention
75’000 U87-MG and 25’000 TC-1-hFAP cells were plated one day prior the experiment in 24 well plates. As illustrated on Fig.  , on the day of the experiment, cells were incubated for 1 h at 4 °C or at 37 °C, then medium was removed and replaced with 125 nM of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (0.18 kBq, 86.49 ng, 6.25 pmol), [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (0.14 kBq, 9.19 ng, 6.25 pmol) or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46 (0.14 kBq, 5.55 ng, 6.25 pmol) in 200 µL of medium per well. After 1 h incubation at 4–37 °C, the wells were washed twice and replaced with fresh medium. Baseline at t = 0 was collected to capture the maximal bound fraction of each compound attached to the cells, followed by collection at 1 h, 4 h at 4 °C and 1 h, 4 h, 24 h and 48 h at 37 °C. Briefly, the unbound fraction is defined as the radioactive fraction collected in the supernatant post incubation, whereas the bound fraction is defined as the radioactive fraction associated to cells, and collected by disrupting the cells with NaOH 1 M.
 

## Long-term biodistribution and dosimetry
U87-MG tumor-bearing athymic nude mice (n = 4) were i.v. injected with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (3.84 ± 0.09 MBq, 1 nmol, 13.8 µg), [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (3.92 ± 0.04 MBq, 1 nmol, 1.47 µg) or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46 (3.79 ± 0.08 MBq, 1 nmol, 0.89 µg). HT-29 tumor-bearing athymic nude mice (n = 4) were i.v. injected with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (3.49 ± 0.05 MBq, 1 nmol, 13.8 µg) or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (3.15 ± 0.09 MBq, 1 nmol, 1.47 µg). HEK-hFAP tumor-bearing athymic nude mice (n = 4) were i.v. injected with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (3.30 ± 0.04 MBq, 1 nmol, 13.8 µg) or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (3.17 ± 0.07 MBq, 1 nmol, 1.47 µg). Mice were sacrificed at different time points until 168 h post injection, after which animals were dissected. Isolated organs and tissues were weighed and counted for radioactivity using a γ-counter (PerkinElmer) for ex vivo analysis and dosimetry calculations. The results are presented as percentage of injected activity per gram (%IA/g). Blood and kidney clearance half-lives were derived from fitted blood and kidney uptake values over time. ## Therapeutic efficacy
Treatment efficacy was assessed in U87-MG tumor-bearing athymic nude mice (n = 10) i.v. injected with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (63.26 ± 1.12 MBq, 1 nmol, 13.8 µg), [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (62.46 ± 0.38 MBq, 1 nmol, 1.47 µg) or [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 (65.28 ± 0.36 kBq, 0.25 nmol, 3.42 µg). A single dose of the indicated treatment or vehicle control was administered i.v at an average U87-MG tumor size of 50 mm3. Evaluation of therapeutic efficacy in HT-29 tumor-bearing athymic nude mice (n = 10) was done by i.v injecting [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (62.93 ± 0.55 MBq, 1 nmol, 13.8 µg), [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (62 ± 0.79 MBq, 1 nmol, 1.47 µg), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 (63.32 ± 1.37 kBq, 0.25 nmol, 3.42 µg), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 (126.24 ± 2.27 kBq, 0.5 nmol, 6.84 µg). A single dose of the indicated treatment or vehicle control was administered i.v at an average HT-29-tumor size of 100 mm3. Animals developing tumor necrosis in the ongoing study or those who died of non-health-related reasons were excluded from the study. Humane endpoints were reached if the body weight loss was more than 20% or when the tumor volume reached > 2000 mm³ with U87-MG or > 1500 mm3 with HT-29 tumor model.

## Statistical analysis
Values were tested for normality. When data was not following a normal distribution, a log transformation was applied, if the mathematical correction led to a Gaussian distribution, one-way ANOVA (Tukey correction for multiple comparisons) or two-way ANOVA (Tukey correction for multiple comparisons) were applied. Therapy studies were analyzed via Kaplan-Meier curves using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test. Adjusted p’ values were calculated using the Holm-Bonferroni correction. Analyses and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10. The results are presented as mean ± SEM, tests are significant when α ≤ 0.05, p-value ≤ 0.05 (*p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; ****p < 0.0001).

## Results

## In vitro tumor retention
Tumor retention was assessed in vitro over time using U87-MG, which endogenously express FAP and TC-1-hFAP transduced to overexpress the target. U87-MG cell line expressed 8 times less FAP compared to TC-1-hFAP (Supplementary Fig. 1). 4AH29 and FAP-2286 were evaluated along with FAPI-46, commonly used as a diagnostic rather than as a therapeutic compound. Of note, both 4AH29 [ ] and FAP-2286 [ ] previously reported excellent binding affinities to human FAP, however displaying distinct binding sites, outside and in the catalytic pocket, respectively. The cell lines were either metabolically slowed down at 4 °C or maintained at 37 °C allowing physiological processes to take place (Fig. ). [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46 showed higher bound fractions than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 at 4 °C in U87-MG (Fig. A) and TC-1-hFAP (Fig. B), at baseline (t = 0) with FAP-2286 and up to 4 h with FAPI-46. Of note, at 37 °C, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 exhibited an excellent retention on U87-MG and TC-1-hFAP, whereas the bound fractions of FAPI molecules decreased rapidly (Fig. C-D). 177Lu-labeled 4AH29 maintained a bound fraction at 47%, 24 h on U87-MG compared to 5.99% with FAP-2286 and 10.79% with FAPI-46 (Fig. C). Due to increased FAP binding sites resulting from cell line proliferation, higher bound fractions were measured from 48 h with U87-MG and 24 h with TC-1-hFAP, in line with their different growth rate. In addition, at baseline, the raw γ-counts revealed both on U87-MG (Fig. E) and TC-1-hFAP (Fig. F) a higher deposition of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46. However, the total γ-counts were higher with the radiolabeled sdAb compared to FAPI molecules from 1 h to 48 h in U87-MG (Fig. E), and at 24 h post incubation on TC-1-hFAP (Fig. F). Along the same line, when radiolabeled with 111In, 4AH29 also showed enhanced cellular retention at 37 °C in contrast to FAP-2286 and FAPI-46 (Supplementary Fig. 2A-F). In vitro, the superior retention of 4AH29 did not translate into greater reduction in cancer cell viability compared to FAPI compounds (Supplementary Fig. 3).
 

## Biodistribution
To evaluate the in vivo tumor retention, resulting dosimetry, and clearance, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46 were assessed in tumor-bearing mice. In view of the clinical use of FAPI-46 mostly limited to diagnostic, this compound was assessed as a FAPI reference and baseline only in U87-MG tumor model. Three rodent tumor models were used: (1) U87-MG, endogenously expressing FAP; (2) HEK-hFAP transduced to overexpress FAP; and (3) HT-29 a cancer line negative for FAP but actively recruiting mouse FAP+ cell to form a stromal compartment. Evaluation by flow cytometry confirmed the different degrees of FAP expression with absence of FAP on HT-29 and 400 folds higher expression on HEK-hFAP than U87-MG (Fig.  A). To measure FAP expression on HT-29, mice were engrafted and collected at different volumes for immunohistochemistry analysis. It showed that HT-29 recruited FAP+ cells, which were heterogeneous within the tested samples and negatively correlated with the volume of the tumors (Fig.  B-C). Similar negative correlation between target level and tumor size was seen with U87-MG samples, however, exhibiting higher FAP expression than HT-29 in small tumors (Fig.  B). Blood clearance followed a two-phase decay for all evaluated tracers, with fast T1/2 values within the first 1 h post administration for all three compounds, and slow T1/2 ranging 2–5 h, as shown in Supplementary Table 1. Kidney clearance followed a one phase decay in all three cases, resulting in T1/2 of 11.05 h for radiolabeled FAPI-46, compared to 28.67 and 21.25 h for radiolabeled 4AH29 and FAP-2286. Volume of distribution calculated 84.39, 40.32 and 16.77 mL for radiolabeled 4AH29, FAPI-46 and FAP-2286, respectively.
 Interestingly, upon administration of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46, distinct levels of tumor retention were observed. Indeed, in U87-MG tumor-bearing mice, FAP-2286 led to a significantly higher tumor uptake than 4AH29 1 h post injection, whereas it was a trend with FAPI-46 (Fig. D). Of note, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 exhibited enhanced tumor uptake from 24 h until 168 h, highlighting an improved long-term retention. Resulting dosimetry showed threefold higher tumor uptake with radiolabeled 4AH29 than FAP-2286 (Table ). On the other hand, in HT-29 model, both tested compounds showed similar uptake 1 h post injection (Fig. E). A better retention in HT-29 was monitored with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 from 6 h to 72 h, while reaching baseline at 168 h. Dosimetry calculated a twofold increase in tumor uptake between 4AH29 and FAP-2286 (Table ). In contrast, HEK-hFAP cells displayed an improved tumor uptake with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 at all assessed time points (Fig. F), leading to a 4.8 fold higher tumor uptake (Table ). Early time points were in line with the reported %IA/g of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 in patient-derived sarcoma [ ] but lower than other studies in HEK-hFAP [ , ].
 Different kidney clearance patterns were observed between [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAPI-46, coinciding with the inherent pharmacokinetic properties of a radiolabeled sdAb [ ], peptide [ ] and small molecule [ ], respectively (Fig. A-C). Indeed, kidney uptake was the highest upon 4AH29 administration showing similar renal excretion in all the models used: 9.26%IA/g in U87-MG (Fig. A), 9.31%IA/g in HT-29 (Supplementary Fig. 4A) and 9.21%IA/g in HEK-hFAP (Supplementary Fig. 4C) 1 h post injection. In the case of FAP-2286, the most elevated organs were the kidneys with 4.12%IA/g, 4.01%IA/g, and 3.60%IA/g at 1 h in U87-MG (Fig. B), HT-29 (Supplementary Fig. 4B), and HEK-hFAP (Supplementary Fig. 4D), respectively. On the other hand, FAPI-46 displayed lower kidney uptake than 4AH29 or FAP-2286, while showing the highest uptake in multiple healthy organs, such as skin and bone (Fig. C). In addition, with all tested compounds, limited uptake was detected in healthy organs expressing low level of mouse FAP as reported [ ] in skin, lymph node, uterus, ovaries, joint and bone (Fig. A-C). In addition, on-target, off-tumor uptake is in part due to FAP shedding circulating in blood [ ]. All values obtained in long term biodistribution studies in U87-MG, HT-29 and HEK-hFAP are reported in Supplementary Tables 2–8.
 

## Therapeutic efficacy in U87-MG tumors
To compare the therapeutic efficacy of 4AH29 and FAP-2286, mice were engrafted with U87-MG and treatment was initiated when tumors reached an average of 50 mm3 (Fig. A). Administration of the radiolabeled compounds was performed as one single-shot with 60 MBq/nmol of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 as previously described [ ] or 60 MBq/nmol [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and 1000-fold [ ] lower 225Ac activity with 60 kBq of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 to allow comparison with 177Lu counterparts. No acute toxicity or significant weight loss were observed upon the injection of the different treatments until sacrifice, when reaching 2000 mm3. 177Lu and 225Ac-labeled 4AH29 displayed a better tumor control at day 43 than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 (Fig. B-C). Treatment with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 only tended to prolong survival (median survival 51 days) compared to vehicle group (median survival 47 days). On the other hand, radiolabeled 4AH29 significantly improved survival compared to vehicle control (Fig. D), however it was more pronounced with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 (median survival 77 days) than [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 (median survival 55.5 days). In addition, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 only tended to increase survival compared to [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, whereas it was significant with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29. Tumor growth in all animals are showed in Supplementary Fig. 5A-C, where one mouse administered with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 showed a maximum of 220 mm3, then controlled the tumor until the end of monitoring (Supplementary Fig. 5D).
 

## Therapeutic efficiency in HT-29 tumors
In addition to U87-MG model, therapeutic potential of 4AH29 and FAP-2286 was assessed in HT-29 tumors. One shot of 60 MBq/nmol of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and 60 or 120 kBq of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 were administered when the tumor average reached 100 mm3 (Fig.  A). HT-29 constituted a challenging model, attributable to limited and heterogeneous FAP expression. No significant differences were observed in tumor control, only a trend with 120 kBq of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 at day 29 before the first animal reached humane endpoint (Fig.  B-C). No acute toxicity or significant weight loss was observed until sacrifice at 1500 mm3. Improved survival with a median survival of 80 days was achieved upon treatment with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 at 120 kBq (Fig.  D). [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and 60 kBq of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 did not impact survival with a respective median survival of 46, 43 and 46 days (Fig.  D) similar to the vehicle group. Individual tumor kinetics are shown in Supplementary Fig. 6.
 

## Discussion
FAP is a pan-tumor target, predominantly expressed on CAFs in primary tumors and distant metastases, while displaying low to undetectable expression in healthy tissues. Diverse molecules have been developed, such as FAP inhibitors, which already showed very exciting imaging data in various cancers, for instance in lung and breast cancers [ ]. FAPI superior imaging capacity compared to conventional [18F]FDG [ ], may pave the way for improving detection and patient stratification most likely to benefit from an anti-FAP therapy. FAP-2286 is considered the frontrunner in therapeutic clinical development; however, its limited tumor retention may affect its effectiveness in patients. In this study, we compared head-to-head two FAP-targeting compounds: 4AH29 and FAP-2286 in terms of the tumor retention, biodistribution in models displaying a wide range of FAP expression, resulting dosimetry and therapeutic efficacy.
 In vitro assessment of tumor retention of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 on U87-MG and TC-1-hFAP, showed at baseline a bound fraction of 89% with FAP-2286, whereas it drastically dropped at 12% after 1 h at 37 °C. On the other hand, 4AH29 displayed an enhanced retention over time with a limited decrease of 10% in U87-MG and 17% in TC-1-hFAP after 1 h incubation at 37 °C. These in vitro results highlighted 4AH29’s superior retention capacity compared to FAP-2286. No head-to-head was conducted to track internalization of both compounds, thus, it is unknown if FAP-2286 binding on U87-MG and TC-1-hFAP triggers endocytosis, followed by secretion or if it dissociates from the cell surface. However, using another cell line, Zboralski et al. showed that tracking of fluorescently labeled FAP-2286 in HEK-hFAP cells led to cellular internalization [ ]. Of note, the improved retention observed with radiolabeled 4AH29 did not translate into a discernible difference in cell viability compared with FAP-2286. Considering that 177Lu is a β-emitter that induces mainly single-strand DNA breaks, it is plausible that DNA repair mechanisms activated following 177Lu irradiation are sufficient to prevent the onset of cell death, at least in this artificial in vitro setting. Nevertheless, further research is needed to fully characterize the effects of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, and eventually of their 225Ac-labeled variants on in vitro cell viability. In this context, an additional approach to explore this question would be to assess early markers of radiation-induced damage. For example, γH2AX quantification of DNA double-strand breaks may provide a more sensitive readout for evaluating the biological impact of radiolabeled 4AH29 and FAP-2286.
To evaluate the biodistribution of 4AH29 and FAP-2286, different models were used, displaying a wide range of FAP expression. Indeed, U87-MG endogenously expresses FAP, HT-29 recruits FAP+ fibroblasts, and HEK-hFAP overexpresses the target. Despite the extensive use of HEK-hFAP in the literature, they represent an artificial model with non-clinically relevant FAP levels, thereby limiting the translational relevance of preclinical findings. Enhanced long-term tumor retention was observed with 4AH29 in U87-MG and HT-29 cells, whereas FAP-2286 persisted longer in HEK-hFAP cells. HEK-293 cells, which are embryonic kidney cells and here genetically modified to overexpress FAP, might have a different recycling system than the two other models used. 4AH29 is not expected to trigger FAP internalization, therefore limiting accumulation to the available FAP expressed at the surface. On the other hand, internalization of FAP-2286 was shown in HEK-hFAP [ ], thus, endocytosis combined with low excretion of the compound may explain the higher tumor uptake of FAP-2286 measured in HEK cells compared to the other tested models. In addition, the high expression levels might further enable an artificial retention of FAP-2286. FAP-2286 initially bound to FAP in the tumor can dissociate, and subsequently bind to the next available FAP in the tumor at very high levels of FAP expression, while at lower FAP densities it would be washed out of the tumor tissue.
Indeed, Millul et al. showed superior results obtained in HEK-hFAP upon administration of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 after 4 h, leading to 22.99%IA/g in HEK-hFAP, whereas it was limited to 3.42%IA/g in HT-1080-hFAP [ ]. Of note, FAP expression was 8.8-fold lower in HT-1080-hFAP than in HEK-hFAP in this study, which also affected tumor uptake efficiency.
[177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 followed the expected clearance of a peptide [ ] and a sdAb [ ] respectively. The radioactivity levels in the kidneys were in line with literature [ ] and illustrated renal excretion of the radiolabeled compounds in U87-MG, HT-29 or HEK-hFAP tumor-bearing mice. All evaluated compounds showed blood clearance dynamics as expected, with a fast first phase within one hour, followed by a longer second phase decay, with radiolabeled FAP-2286 showing the fastest overall blood half-life. Given that the time between T0h and T1h was extrapolated based on experimental data points, we might be overestimating the T1/2 fast, in other words, the blood clearance might be faster than what is reported here. Kidney clearance kinetics were as expected, with the most efficient clearance for radiolabeled small molecule FAPI-46, followed by comparable clearance kinetics for both 4AH29 and FAP-2286. The assessment of volume of distribution suggests that both 4AH29 and FAPI-46 exhibit more efficient tissue distribution, while FAP-2286 appears to have a more pronounced plasma presence. This might explain why we obtain a higher initial blood pool level for radiolabeled FAP-2286 compared to both other radiolabeled compounds. We should however interpret this data with caution given that circulating mouse FAP has been described in mice as previously mentioned in Dekempeneer et al., 2023 [ ]. This might impact blood pool retention and might influence the results obtained.
Along the same line, calculated dosimetry showed that the kidneys were the dose limiting organ. Applying the conservative external beam radiation therapy (EBRT) threshold of 23 Gy [ ] in the kidneys, maximum injected activity extrapolated a similar tumor absorbed dose of 19.42 Gy with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and 18.78 Gy with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286. However, the maximum tolerated dose of 53 MBq with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 highlighted a potential limitation of this study, where the single therapeutic injection of 60 MBq may lead to kidney toxicity. Reducing kidney uptake though renal protective measures would allow higher activity administration, thereby enabling more effective treatment of tumors with low FAP expression. Co-administration of a radiolabeled compound and amino acids infusion, such as Vamin, is a common strategy used in the clinic [ ], however is challenging to execute in rodents. Another strategy could be to administer the radiolabeled compounds fractionated to decrease kidney exposure and reduce toxicities [ ]. Moreover, pre-targeting can also be considered: a two-step injection, first the cold protein, followed by a delay before administration of the radioactive moieties attached to a linker. Interestingly, using click-chemistry, radiolabeled 4AH29 successfully demonstrated the potential of pre-targeting [ ].
To assess if the enhanced tumor retention observed with radiolabeled 4AH29 compared to FAP-2286 could translate into better tumor control and survival, mice were engrafted with U87-MG cancer cells. Single administrations of 60 kBq and 120 kBq of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 were selected in line with previous published work [ ]. Importantly, given the observed late-stage kidney toxicity observed in a 6 × 10 kBq regimen, the current drug product formulation was optimized with the inclusion of an excess of DTPA to chelate daughter radioisotope 213Bi to avoid its retention in kidneys. Ascorbic acid was added to the drug product formulation, predominantly to stabilize the 4AH29 in the presence of 225Ac, but it is also described to act as a radioprotector in mice treated with radiolabeled compounds [ ]. Given that kidney is the dose-limiting organ for both radiolabeled compounds, future work should include the assessment of late-stage toxicity to understand the respective impact of radiolabeled FAP-2286 and 4AH29 uptake in kidneys. One single shot of [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 or 1000-fold lower activity with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 led to significantly delay tumor growth compared to treatment with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286 or vehicle control. Of note, it translated into statistically longer survival in the groups injected with the radiolabeled sdAb than vehicle, whereas it was a trend with [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286. Comparison between FAP-2286 and 4AH29 showed that [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 statistically increased survival, however it was a tendency between [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286. Based on the herein presented dosimetry calculations as well as results described previously [ ], the highest absorbed dose to U87-MG tumors is expected for treatment with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29, followed by [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-4AH29 and finally [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-FAP-2286, which corresponds with the efficacy readouts by assessing mean tumor volumes (Fig. , panels B & C), suggesting an absorbed dose – response relationship. This observation should be further evaluated in efficacy studies with a higher number of dose cohorts.
Evaluation of FAP-2286 and 4AH29 in HT-29 showed no differences between the 177Lu-labeled compounds in controlling tumor growth or influencing survival. On the other hand, [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 led to treatment efficacy at 120 kBq. The distinct efficiency between U87-MG and HT-29 models is explained by a heterogeneous and restricted FAP expression in the latter. In addition, the targeting to mouse FAP instead of human FAP decreases the binding affinities of both 4AH29 and FAP-2286 [ , ]. Despite those limitations, high dose of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-4AH29 was the only treatment that prolonged the survival of HT-29 tumor-bearing mice, highlighting the interest of using an alpha emitter to target FAP.

## Conclusion
To conclude, this head-to-head study demonstrated better retention, tumor control, and survival benefit in mice treated with radiolabeled 4AH29 compared to radiolabeled FAP-2286 in models with a clinically relevant level of FAP. Those promising results with 4AH29 warrants a clinical investigation.
