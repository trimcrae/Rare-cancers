## ‌Purpose‌
This phase I, first-in-human study evaluated the safety, dosimetry, and preliminary efficacy of 177Lu-FAPI-XT, a novel fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-targeted radioligand, in patients with advanced solid tumors refractory to standard therapies.

## ‌Methods‌
This open-label, non-randomized, dose-escalation, investigator-initiated trial had a “accelerated titration” and “3 + 3” design involved a 6-week 177Lu-FAPI-XT treatment cycle in patients with advanced-stage solid tumors at 3.7GBq initially, with subsequent cohorts receiving an incremental 1.85GBq dose increase until dose limiting toxicity (DLT) was observed.

## ‌Results‌
177Lu-FAPI-XT was well tolerated and no DLT or grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAE) were observed. The whole-body effective dose was 0.039 ± 0.013 Sv/GBq. The mean effective half-lives for the whole-body and tumor lesions were 52.13 ± 12.33 h and 31.78 ± 15.03 h, respectively. According to RECIST stable disease (SD) were observed in 5 (5/14, 35.7%) patients, with the highest benefit observed in fibroblastic sarcoma subtypes with SD presented in 4 (4/8, 50%) patients. The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 4.63 m (95%CI: 1.25, NE). Metabolic reductions on PET/CT were observed in 2 sarcoma cases. (URL: hclinicaltrials.gov. Trial registration: NCT06211647, NCT06197139. Registered 4 January 2024.)

## ‌conclusion‌
177Lu-FAPI-XT demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with preliminary signs of tumor response observed. Complete/partial response deficiency in 177Lu-FAPI-XT therapy necessitate optimized strategies for efficacy improvement.

## Introduction
Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are among the most abundant cellular components of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in solid malignancies, and play a pivotal role in tumor progression. Activated CAFs express a diverse array of protein markers, with fibroblast activation protein (FAP) recognized as both a key biomarker and a functional mediator of CAF activity. While FAP is scarcely expressed in normal tissues and benign tumor stroma, it is significantly upregulated in over 90% of epithelial-derived malignancies [ , ]. This transmembrane serine protease promotes tumor growth and invasion through multiple mechanisms, including extracellular matrix remodeling, tumor cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune suppression within the TME.
Emerging evidence indicates that FAP expression is not limited to CAFs but also extends to immunosuppressive cells and vascular pericytes, further linking FAP to advanced disease progression, decreased survival rates, and poor prognosis across several cancer types [ – ]. Notably, FAP overexpression has been observed in various malignancies, including sarcomas and carcinomas of the breast, stomach, colorectum, pancreas, and ovaries [ , ].
The development of FAP inhibitors (FAPIs) has enabled selective targeting of FAP, establishing it as a promising theranostic target. Radioligand therapy (RLT) utilizing lutetium-177 (177Lu), a β-emitter with favorable physical properties (T_1/2 = 6.65 d, E_βmax = 498 keV), has gained clinical validation through the successful use of 177Lu-PSMA in prostate cancer and 177Lu-DOTATATE in neuroendocrine tumors [ , ]. Preliminary investigations of 177Lu-FAPI based therapies for advanced sarcomas, colorectal cancer, radioiodine-refractory differentiated thyroid carcinoma (RAIR-DTC), and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) have shown acceptable safety profiles and encouraging therapeutic potential [ , – ]. However, current clinical evidence is limited by small sample sizes and considerable inter-individual variability in response, underscoring the need for further research to establish the safety and efficacy parameters of FAPI-based radionuclide therapy.

177Lu-FAPI-XT (177Lu-XT117) represents a novel FAP-targeted radiotherapeutic agent incorporating an optimized ligand structure designed for enhanced tumor retention. Preclinical studies evaluating tumor dosimetry, biodistribution and pharmacodynamics of the animal models, which have not yet been published, alongside prior clinical investigations utilizing 68Ga and 64Cu labeled FAPI-XT tracers, have confirmed the strong FAP-targeting affinity and prolonged intratumoral retention of the precursor compound XT117, a next-generation FAP inhibitor (NCT05814835, NCT05930457) [ , ], supporting its therapeutic potential when labeled with 177Lu. This phase I study aims to evaluate the safety profile and preliminary efficacy of 177Lu-FAPI-XT in patients with FAP-overexpressing advanced solid tumors refractory to standard therapies.

## Materials and methods

## Study overview and regulatory compliance
This first-in-human, open-label, non-randomized, dose-escalation phase I trial was an investigator-initiated, prospective multicenter study conducted at three Chinese medical institutions: Chinese PLA General Hospital, Peking University Cancer Hospital, and the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University. The study protocol received approval from the institutional review boards of all participating centers and strictly adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Regulatory compliance and transparency were ensured through trial registration with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06211647, NCT06197139; registered 4 January 2024).

## Patients’ selection
Key inclusion criteria comprised: (1) adult patients (> 18 years) with histologically confirmed malignancies; (2) documented disease progression following standard therapeutic interventions including surgical resection (where applicable), systemic chemotherapy, and radiotherapy (if indicated); (3) FAP-positive tumor phenotype confirmed by FAPI PET/CT, defined as at least one measurable lesion with the maximum standard uptake value (SUVmax) > 10 and all target lesions with SUVmax ≥ 3. Exclusion criteria included: hemoglobin level below 8 g/dL, absolute neutrophil count below 1.5 × 10⁹/L, platelet count below 75 × 10⁹/L, creatinine level exceeding 1.5 mg/dL, bilirubin level surpassing 1.5 times the upper normal limit (UNL), and alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase levels exceeding 2.5 times the UNL (or more than 5 times the UNL in the absence of liver metastases). Relevant data, such as previous imaging, histopathology results, blood tests, and details of prior treatments, were collected for all patients. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Supplementary Table . All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

## Study design
This phase I dose-escalation trial initially included four cohorts. The baseline dose was 3.7 GBq, increasing by 1.85 GBq increments until dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) was observed. The initial dose of 177Lu-labeled FAPI-XT, was 3.7 GBq, with a maximum dose of 9.25 GBq. Treatment was administered at 6-week intervals, with up to four planned cycles per patient. Dose escalation beyond four cycles or increased dose group was permitted at the discretion of the investigator. Cohorts 1 and 2 (3.7 and 5.5 GBq/cycle, respectively) followed an accelerated titration design, enrolling one patient per cohort and escalating in the absence of DLT. From Cohort 3 (7.4 GBq/cycle) onward, the study transitioned to a conventional 3 + 3 dose-escalation design. Initially, three patients were included in the third-dose group (7.4 GBq/cycle). If none of the three patients in a cohort experienced DLT, another three patients were enrolled at the next higher dose level. If one of the three patients experienced DLT at a certain dose level, three more patients were enrolled at the same dose level. The highest dose with no more than one of the six patients experiencing DLT was the maximum tolerated dose (MTD). Adverse events (AE) were assessed, with DLT defined as grade ≥ 3 treatment-related events (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events 5.0, CTCAE v5.0). In cohorts where no DLT were observed, dose expansion was allowed. The dose escalation study design with 177Lu-FAPI-XT was shown in Fig.  . Primary endpoints were safety and maximum tolerated dose in solid tumor patients; secondary endpoints included dosimetry and preliminary efficacy of 177Lu-FAPI-XT radiotherapy.

 

## 177Lu-FAPI-XT synthesis and administration
Preparation of the 177Lu-FAPI-XT injection was performed as follows. First, the FAPI-XT precursor, supplied by Sinotau Pharmaceutical Group, was incubated with the required radioactivity of 177LuCl3 (ITM Isotope Technologies) in 0.5 mol/L sodium acetate buffer (adjusted to pH 4.0–4.5.0.5 with hydrochloric acid). After incubation at 75 °C for 15 min, the mixture was diluted with 0.9% saline solution and sterile-filtered. Samples were tested for quality, endotoxins, and sterility. Radiochemical purity, determined by thin-layer and high-performance liquid chromatography, was consistently higher than 95%.
No fasting, special diet, or other specific preparations were required on the day of 177Lu-FAPI-XT administration. The radiopharmaceutical was diluted with 30 mL of 0.9% saline and co-administered slowly via intravenous infusion over 30 min. Symptoms and vital parameters were monitored before, during, and after treatment. The treatment regimen was planned for up to six cycles, with an inter-cycle interval of 6 weeks.

## Scintigraphy and SPECT/CT imaging
Whole-body planar scintigraphy in the anterior and posterior projections was acquired at 0.5–1.5 h, 4–6 h, 16–24 h, 40–48 h, 60–72 h, and 156–168 h after administration, using a double-head γ-camera (Discovery NM/CT 670CZT; GE) with a medium-energy general collimator and a 20% energy window set symmetrically over the 208 keV photopeak. The table speed was 10 cm/min. Additionally, abdominal SPECT/CT was performed at 24 h post-administration, which included 43 s per frame, a 3° angular step, and 180° rotation. CT was performed for attenuation correction without contrast agent.

## Dosimetry protocol
Dosimetry analysis was based on a hybrid planar/SPECT approach following European Association of Nuclear Medicine (EANM) guidance (Fig. ) [ ]. As patients were not allowed to empty their bladders before the first scan, the total-body counts acquired immediately after injection were defined as 100% of the administered activity. Dosimetry estimation was performed with hybrid dosimetry software from Hermes Medical Solutions, Sweden. Major organs were manually drawn for each time point, and bioaccumulation data were obtained. Olinda/EXM Version 2.2.0 was used for fitting the time-activity curves and dosimetry calculations. Source organs included the kidneys, liver, spleen, lumbar vertebrae (L2-L4) for red bone marrow, lungs, and breasts. Lungs and breasts were only selected in patients with high uptake on visual assessment. Lumbar vertebrae (L2-L4), representing 6.7% of bone marrow, were used to determine red marrow absorption with 3D volumetric analysis. Regions of interest were selected and drawn manually over the source regions on the acquired scintigraphy images by the same physicist in collaboration with a nuclear medicine physician, who selected lesions with the highest uptake for dosimetry. The International Commission on Radiological Protection 89 adult model and the spheres model were used for normal organs and tumor lesions, respectively. 

## Clinical, radiologic, and laboratory follow-up
Complete blood counts, liver, and kidney function tests were systematically performed before and during post-treatment follow-up for all patients every three weeks. Patient records were reviewed for any incidence of hematological, gastrointestinal, renal, hepatic, or other AEs, which were graded according to CTCAE 5.0. All circumstances that resulted in cessation or delay of treatment were documented. Changes in circulating tumor marker levels were also recorded. According to RECIST version 1.1, tumor response was monitored at 6 weeks after each cycle, and objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) were assessed.

## Statistics analyses
All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 21.0) and GraphPad Prism software (version 10.4.2). Normally distributed quantitative data were expressed as mean ± SD, whereas non-normally distributed data were expressed as median with interquartile range.

## Results

## Patient characteristics
This prospective study enrolled 14 patients (8 males, 6 females; mean age 41.4 ± 15.4 years, range 22–68 years) with advanced solid tumors refractory to standard therapies between January and December 2024. The patient flow chart is shown in Fig.  . 
All participants demonstrated disease progression despite prior multimodal interventions including surgery (where applicable), chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, and/or immunotherapy. From 33 initially screened candidates, 19 were excluded: 14 patients due to negative ⁶⁸Ga-FAPI PET/CT uptake, and five due to other exclusion criteria, such as uncontrolled ascites, active infection, impaired renal function, inadequate washout period following prior anticancer therapy, or hemoglobin levels below the inclusion threshold. The final cohort consisted of 11 patients with sarcoma subtypes (2 leiomyosarcoma [LMS], 1 malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor [MPNST], 3 malignant solitary fibrous tumors [MSFT], 2 inflammatory myofibroblastic tumors [IMT], and 3 malignant myofibroblastoma) and 3 patients with non-sarcoma malignancies (1 colon cancer, 1 NSCLC, 1 RAIR-DTC). All patients underwent complete radiation dosimetry evaluations, and comprehensive baseline characteristics are summarized in Table  .

 

A total of 41 RLT cycles were administered to 14 patients. Of these, 4/14 patients (28.6%) received all six RLT cycles. The primary reasons for treatment discontinuation were tumor progression (8/14, 57.1%) and initiation of combined anti-tumor therapy (1/14, 7.1%).

## Safety and tolerability of 177Lu-FAPI-XT
Following 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT administration across four dose cohorts (3.7–11.1 GBq), no hepatotoxic events were observed. Hematologic stability was maintained in all patients, with no cases of myelosuppression. Two patients (14.3%) experienced three mild treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) potentially related to therapy: in the 3.7 GBq cohort, Patient 1 developed transient grade 1 thrombocytopenia (CTCAE v5.0) that resolved spontaneously, accompanied by concurrent grade 1 leukopenia and grade 2 neutropenia; in the 7.4 GBq cohort, Patient 8 exhibited asymptomatic grade 1 proteinuria that required no clinical intervention. No drug-related TEAEs were identified in the higher-dose cohorts (5.55–11.1 GBq). Importantly, no grade ≥ 3 AEs, DLTs, or severe complications occurred during treatment (Table  ). These findings collectively demonstrate the favorable safety profile of 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT across the evaluated therapeutic dosage range.

 

## Efficacy of 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT
Tumor response by RECIST is summarized in Table  . RECIST disease control after RLT was achieved in 4 of 14 patients (28.6%), specifically 4 of 12 (33.3%) among the 12 efficacy-evaluable patients (4 stable disease [SD]; primary endpoint: RECIST after RLT in Table  ). RECIST evaluation was not performed in 2 of 14 patients (14.3%), as one patient died due to Klebsiella pneumoniae infection before reaching the 6-week efficacy assessment timepoint (Patient 14), and another patient was discontinued from the study due to concomitant use of antitumor medications (Patient 1). Disease control after RLT was observed in 4 of 8 (50%) patients with fibrogenic sarcoma subtypes versus 0 of 4 (0%) patients with other solid tumors (Table  ).

 

The best RECIST response observed during RLT was achieved in 5 out of 14 patients, all of whom demonstrated SD (Table  ). The overall DCR was 35.7% (95% CI: 25.31, 80.78).
By January 2025, 8 patients (Nos. 2, 3, 4, 7–10, 13) had withdrawn due to documented disease progression, with progression-free survival (PFS) durations ranging from 0.75 to 7.5 months (Table  ). The median PFS was 4.63 m (95%CI: 1.25, NE). Two deaths were recorded: Patient 4 died 2.8 months post-progression from disease-related multiorgan failure, while Patient 14 (RAIR-DTC) died of Klebsiella pneumoniae-induced respiratory failure 21 days after the initial 177Lu-FAPI-XT infusion.

## Biodistribution

Biodistribution analysis following initial intravenous administration of 177Lu-FAPI-XT provided quantitative assessment of injected dose percentage (%ID) and retention time (MBq·h/MBq) across source organs and tumor sites. Peak %ID values in all source organs were observed at 1 h post-injection, with generally low uptake levels: pulmonary (3.878 ± 1.331%), hepatic (2.719 ± 2.93%), red marrow (2.014 ± 1.843%), renal (0.879 ± 0.398%), and splenic (0.361 ± 0.125%) (Supplementary Table S2). Organ retention times were relatively short overall, with the lungs (0.853 ± 0.339 MBq-h/MBq), liver (0.730 ± 0.953 MBq-h/MBq), and red marrow (0.659 ± 0.373 MBq-h/MBq) exhibiting the longest durations (Supplementary Table S3). Sequential whole-body imaging confirmed predominant tracer accumulation in excretory pathways (renal-urinary system) and neoplastic lesions, with minimal retention in non-target tissues.

## Dosimetry analysis

Dosimetric evaluation revealed absorbed doses across target organs as follows: kidneys (0.13 ± 0.047 Gy/GBq), lungs (0.103 ± 0.077 Gy/GBq), and red marrow (0.05 ± 0.015 Gy/GBq). The whole-body effective dose was 0.039 ± 0.013 Sv/GBq.
Effective half-lives were calculated as follows: whole body, 52.13 ± 12.33 h; lungs, 49.03 ± 13.50 h; kidneys, 47.76 ± 8.85 h; liver, 57.95 ± 12.32 h; red marrow, 54.20 ± 8.64 h; and spleen, 48.04 ± 17.05 h. For tumor lesions, the mean effective half-life of 177Lu -FAPI-XT was 31.78 ± 15.03 h.
Tumor dosimetry analysis focused on up to six dominant lesions per patient, demonstrating per-GBq absorbed doses ranging from 0.063 to 3.089 Gy/GBq (Supplementary Table S4).

## Discussion
We report the first case series of patients with advanced-stage solid tumors treated with 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT. Individualized dosimetry was performed for each patient to optimize safety and maximize the likelihood of therapeutic benefit.
This phase I trial enrolled 14 patients with advanced, treatment-refractory solid malignancies that had progressed despite standard therapies, including radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted agents. Our findings demonstrate that 177Lu-FAPI-XT has a favorable safety and tolerability profile, with only three cases of grade 1/2 AEs possibly related to the study drug. The incidence of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was lower than reported for other FAP-targeted radiopharmaceuticals [ ]. Notably, no DLT were observed in the higher-dose cohorts (9.25 and 11.1 GBq), and no TRAEs occurred after six administrations at the 9.25 GBq level. These results support the safety of delivering multiple high-dose cycles.
Disease control was observed in both primary and metastatic lesions of FAP-positive malignancies, indicating promising clinical activity. According to RECIST 1.1 criteria, the DCR was 35.7%, with notably favorable outcomes in MSFT and myofibroblastic sarcoma subtypes. For example, one patient with recurrent MSFT of the pleura and metastatic lesions achieved a PFS of 9.4 months after six cycles, more than tripling her previous PFS on first-line doxorubicin-based therapy (3 months). Another patient with intracranial MSFT and multifocal metastases maintained SD for 7.5 months with a 2.8% reduction in lesion size and notable pain relief (The CTCAE grade changed from G2 to G1). Two other malignant myofibroblastoma cases completed five cycles, with SD durations of 6.0 and 4.2 months and tumor reductions of 13.3% and 6.6%, respectively. Interim FAPI-PET/CT imaging revealed substantial decreases in SUVmax (Fig. ), indicating effective target engagement. These results are consistent with previous mechanistic studies of FAP-targeted RLT. Fendler et al. [ ] reported preliminary antitumor activity of FAP-directed radiopharmaceuticals in MSFT patients, while Hamacher et al. [ ] identified two key biological determinants: (1) MSFT showed the highest FAP mRNA expression among 813 sarcoma specimens of 126 histological subtypes; and (2) multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) demonstrated predominant FAP membrane localization on tumor cells, with minimal stromal expression, creating optimal conditions for β-emitter conjugates to induce tumoricidal effects through DNA double-strand breaks. 
No patients in this study achieved partial response (PR) or complete response (CR), which is consistent with findings from previous studies of FAP-targeted 177Lu radioligand therapies. Meta-analyses of similar agents have shown that approximately 43% of patients developed progressive disease (PD) after multiple treatment cycles, 48% achieved SD, and fewer than 10% achieved PR, with the majority ultimately classified as SD or PD [ ]. This pattern contrasts with the superior efficacy seen with established theranostics such as 177Lu-PSMA-617 and 177Lu-DOTATATE [ , ], highlighting the need for further therapeutic optimization. The limited efficacy likely results from two related factors: (1) the predominant localization of FAP in the tumor stroma rather than on malignant cell membranes, reducing direct radiation exposure to tumor cells; and (2) insufficient intratumoral retention time of current FAP tracers, which limits the cumulative β-particle radiation dose needed for effective cytotoxicity [ ].
Disease progression within 1–2 treatment cycles was observed in several patients in this study, potentially attributable to three interrelated factors: (1) aggressive tumor biology, as reflected by rapid progression in specific sarcoma subtypes—MPNST (Patient 4), LMS (Patients 6 and 7), and IMT (Patients 9 and 10)—may indicating potential subtype-specific resistance; (2) high tumor burden and advanced disease stage in these heavily pretreated, end-stage patients; and (3) limited intratumoral radiation delivery, with most lesions receiving < 0.5 Gy/GBq, although some foci achieved >1 Gy/GBq. The observed DCR of 35.7% with 177Lu-FAPI-XT aligns with the reported DCR range of 25–83% for other FAP-targeted radioligands, such as 177Lu -DOTAGA-FAPi, 177Lu-FAP-2286 and 177Lu-EB-FAPI, suggesting class-wide limitations potentially driven by intratumoral dose heterogeneity or variability in FAP expression [ , , , ]. Differential efficacy across tumor types has also been reported. For example, 177Lu-EB-FAPI achieved a DCR of 83% in RAIR-DTC but only 50% in MSFT, possibly reflecting tumor type-specific differences in radiosensitivity, with thyroid cancers known to be more responsive to radiation. The relatively lower DCR observed in this trial may be due to the subtherapeutic absorbed doses delivered by 177Lu-FAPI-XT in sarcomas (range: 0.069–3.089 Gy/GBq). Moreover, baseline SUVmax values did not correlate significantly with treatment response, indicating limited predictive utility. Nevertheless, two patients who achieved SD showed decreased tracer uptake on post-treatment PCT/CT, suggesting that reductions in uptake may serve as a surrogate for therapeutic benefit—a trend consistent with prior reports [ ]. To address the limited efficacy observed with current FAP-targeted agents, several strategic approaches are under development, such as conjugation with ²²⁵Ac to harness the high linear energy transfer of alpha-emitting radionuclides for improved cytotoxicity; implementation of dimerization strategies [ ] to enhance binding affinity and tumor retention; and incorporation of albumin-binding domains (e.g., EB-FAPI) to extend plasma half-life and increase tumor accumulation [ ].
In this study, a cumulative activity of 37 GBq (1000 mCi), delivered four cycles of 9.25 GBq, resulted in cumulative absorbed doses of 4.812 ± 1.752 Gy to the kidneys, 3.801 ± 2.854 Gy to the lungs, and 1.868 ± 0.567 Gy to the red bone marrow. These values remained below established organ-specific dose thresholds of 17.5 Gy (lungs), 23 Gy (kidneys), and 2 Gy (red marrow), respectively. These thresholds, derived from external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) tolerance limits (TD5/5 values) representing a 5% probability of organ-specific toxicity within 5 years [ ], which has some limitations of directly extrapolating EBRT thresholds to RLT, given differences in dose rate and radiobiological context. Notably, 177Lu-FAPI-XT resulted in lower bone marrow and kidney radiation exposure compared to agents such as 177Lu-FAP-2286 and 177Lu-EB-FAPI [ , ]. However, cross-study comparisons remain limited due to variability in imaging protocols, scanner performance, methodologies, and operator-dependent region-of-interest delineation. Our findings demonstrate that 177Lu-FAPI-XT maintains a favorable safety profile consistent with previous first-in-human trials. Tumor-absorbed doses for 177Lu-FAPI-XT showed substantial interpatient and interlesion heterogeneity, comparable to the literature-reported range of 0.6–10.8 Gy/GBq [ ].
The maximum tolerated activity (MTA) of radiopharmaceuticals is determined primarily by healthy organ radiation exposure, with quantitative dosimetry allowing for organ-specific dose limits. Current clinical practice favors achieving the MTA through multi-cycle fractionated administration rather than a single-cycle maximum dose. This approach enables early, personalized assessment of organ dosimetry and radiosensitivity following initial cycles, thus optimizing further dose escalation. The safety and efficacy of such fractionated regimens have been validated in multiple international Phase III randomized trials [ – ], and our study corroborates these favorable safety results.
In this study, despite the lack of significant therapeutic efficacy and the absence of DLT at the 11.1 GBq dose level, we did not pursue further dose escalation to potentially enhance treatment outcomes in this investigator-initiated trial (IIT). This decision was primarily based on the following considerations. First, the dosimetry of evaluation at 11.1 GBq indicated a potential dose-related risk to the bone marrow, which was 2.24 Gy; Furthermore, in comparable studies using 177Lu-labeled FAP-targeted agents, the highest reported dose was 250 mCi [ ]. As no improvement in efficacy was seen in the two patients treated at 300 mCi and due to possible safety concerns, further dose escalation was halted in this investigator-initiated trial. Further exploration of higher dosing will be considered in future registration studies.
This study has several limitations. First, the population that received RLT with 177Lu-FAPI-XT as a last-line compassionate-use therapy was small and included a relatively wide range of tumor types. Second, despite the lack of significant therapeutic efficacy and the absence of DLT, we did not pursue further dose escalation. Third, our study lacked long-term side effect monitoring, which will be addressed in future research. Finally, 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT was not compared to a control group; thus, our data can only be interpreted relative to published reports.

## Conclusion
In summary, this study provides the first evidence of the safety, feasibility, and dosimetry of 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT with escalating doses in patients with advanced solid tumors. 177Lu-FAPI-XT RLT exhibits a favorable safety profile (no DLTs, low TRAE incidence) in heavily pretreated solid tumor patients, and achieved disease control in 35.7% of cases, notably in MSFT. However, efficacy remains limited by stromal FAP localization and subtherapeutic radiation doses, highlighting the need for improved radioconjugate design to enhance tumor retention and target engagement.
