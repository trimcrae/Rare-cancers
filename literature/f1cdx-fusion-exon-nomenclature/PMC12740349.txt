## Abstract
Desmoplastic small round cell tumor (DSRCT) is a rare and aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. We report an autopsy case of DSRCT in a 24-year-old male with a rapidly fatal outcome due to unusual diffuse cystic liver metastases. Despite an initial complete response to therapy, the patient developed extensive hepatic involvement leading to acute liver failure. Autopsy revealed widespread DSRCT with cystic degeneration in the liver metastases, which was confirmed by EWSR1 rearrangement. This case highlights the unpredictable nature of DSRCT and its potential for an atypical presentation, while emphasizing the need for improved therapeutic strategies. 
Keywords: desmoplastic small round cell tumor, 
EWSR1-WT1 fusion, cystic liver metastases, ultra-rare cancer

## Introduction
Desmoplastic small round cell tumor (DSRCT) is a rare and highly aggressive malignancy that predominantly affects young individuals, with a marked predilection for males. This neoplasm typically originates in the abdominal and pelvic cavities and often reaches a considerable size before clinical detection ( , ). Patients frequently present with non-specific abdominal complaints, which delay the diagnosis. At the time of initial presentation, extensive peritoneal involvement and metastatic spread, particularly to the liver and regional lymph nodes, are common findings.
DSRCT is histologically characterized by small round blue cells embedded within a desmoplastic stroma. The tumor exhibits a distinctive “polyphenotypic” immunohistochemical profile, with concurrent expression of epithelial, mesenchymal, and neural markers. This unique pattern distinguishes DSRCT from other small, round cell tumors. The genetic hallmark of DSRCT is t(11;22)(p13;q12) translocation, which results in EWSR1-WT1 gene fusion ( , ).
Despite advances in multimodal treatment approaches that incorporate aggressive surgical resection, intensive chemotherapy regimens, and radiation therapy, the prognosis of patients with DSRCT remains poor ( ).
We herein present an autopsy case of DSRCT with an extremely rapid fatal outcome due to diffuse cystic liver metastasis. This case was notable for achieving a complete response during the course of treatment, followed by sudden and aggressive recurrence. This rare presentation provides insight into the unpredictable nature of DSRCT and its unusual metastatic patterns.

## Case Report
A 24-year-old male presented with a one-month history of abdominal pain and progressive difficulty in oral intake. The medical history was unremarkable. Family history revealed colon cancer in the paternal grandmother and lung cancer in the maternal grandfather, both of which had an unknown age of onset.
Abdominal computed tomography (CT) revealed ascites, multiple peritoneal dissemination nodules, and para-aortic lymph node enlargement. The clinical presentation was consistent with intestinal obstruction secondary to rectal stenosis caused by carcinomatous peritonitis. Ascitic fluid cytology revealed dense island-like aggregates of atypical epithelial cells with increased fine nuclear chromatin and high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios, suggesting carcinomatous peritonitis due to a small round cell tumor. Upper endoscopy and colonography failed to identify the origin of the primary organ.
The patient subsequently underwent transverse colostomy and a biopsy of the peritoneal dissemination nodule. A histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of DSRCT ( ). On immunohistochemistry, the neoplastic cells showed the characteristic co-expression of epithelial (cytokeratin AE1/AE3 positive), mesenchymal (vimentin-positive), and neural [neuron-specific enolase (NSE)-positive] markers. Importantly, the tumor cells were positive for WT1 and desmin (punctate cytoplasmic staining), which are highly specific for DSRCT. Complete immunohistochemistry results are summarized in .
 Upon referral for chemotherapy post-surgery, the patient's Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status score was 3. Laboratory examinations revealed elevations in white blood cell count (29,000/μL), neuron-specific enolase (18.6 ng/mL), and cancer antigen 19-9 (336.9 U/mL) ( ).
 First-line chemotherapy consisting of vincristine, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide (VDC/IE) was initiated as the standard of care for small round cell sarcomas. After six cycles, CT imaging showed a complete response, confirmed by positron emission tomography-CT, showing no significant uptake ( ).
 Approximately five months later, CT revealed the emergence of a pelvic lesion ( . Surgical resection was deemed unfeasible, thus leading to the initiation of second-line trabectedin treatment. Owing to disease progression, third-line treatment with eribulin was introduced, followed by fourth-line treatment with pazopanib. The subsequent lines of therapy were selected based on their established efficacy against advanced soft tissue sarcomas.
Follow-up CT two months after the initiation of pazopanib revealed pelvic lesion progression ( ) and the sudden appearance of diffuse cystic lesions occupying the entire liver ( ). The patient's condition deteriorated rapidly. The blood test results obtained four days before death are shown in .
 Autopsy revealed a large pelvic tumor, measuring approximately 15 cm in diameter, infiltrating adjacent structures, including the sigmoid colon, rectum, and bladder. The liver was markedly enlarged and heavy, with extensive tumor involvement ( ). A microscopic examination of the liver showed tumors that were morphologically similar to the pelvic tumor ( ), but with more pronounced necrosis and cystic degeneration ( ). A fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis of the liver tissue revealed characteristic EWSR1 gene rearrangement, confirming the diagnosis of metastatic DSRCT ( ). ## Discussion
We herein report a case of DSRCT with an unusually aggressive clinical course and an atypical metastatic pattern. DSRCT, first described by Gerald and Rosai in 1989 ( ), represents an ultra-rare cancer with an estimated incidence of less than 1 case per million person-years ( ), thus meeting the criteria for ultra-rare cancers (incidence ＜0.2 cases per 100,000 population). This ultra-rare status presents unique challenges in establishing standardized diagnostic criteria and treatment protocols, particularly for young males. This case provides valuable insights into the clinical progression, metastatic behavior, and diagnostic challenges associated with DSRCT, warranting detailed examination.
The initial presentation and diagnosis in this case aligned with the archetypal features of DSRCT, including abdominal discomfort, peritoneal dissemination, and characteristic histopathological findings ( , , ). Although liver metastases are relatively common in DSRCT ( ), the rapid progression to diffuse cystic liver metastases observed in the present case represents an exceptionally rare and particularly aggressive disease course. This unusual presentation underscores the heterogeneity of metastatic patterns in DSRCT and highlights the potential for atypical manifestations, deviating significantly from the more generally reported solid nodular liver metastases ( , ).
The extensive cystic transformation observed in the hepatic metastases of this DSRCT case presents an intriguing, yet poorly understood phenomenon. To the best of our knowledge, such cystic progression patterns in hepatic lesions have not been previously reported, even in comprehensive case series studies of DSRCT ( - ) that have documented various patterns of disease progression. Our review of the major published case series, including those with substantial patient cohorts, revealed no similar instances of rapidly progressive cystic transformation of hepatic metastases. Although the exact mechanism remains unclear, a number of factors, such as rapid tumor growth outpacing angiogenesis or the unique liver microenvironment, may contribute to this atypical presentation. One potential hypothesis is that the hepatic microenvironment may modulate the tumor cell phenotype, potentially altering the expression profiles of proteolytic enzymes and angiogenic mediators. Such phenotypic modifications could represent one of the possible mechanisms underlying therapeutic refractoriness and aggressive clinical course. The EWSR1-WT1 fusion protein may also affect cellular differentiation and stromal architecture at the secondary sites. Further molecular and histopathological investigations of similar cases are required to elucidate the precise mechanisms underlying this rare manifestation. Insights derived from such studies will potentially guide the development of innovative therapeutic approaches, ultimately enhancing the clinical outcomes of patients afflicted with this aggressive neoplasm.
Our patient's initial complete response to VDC/IE chemotherapy ( ), while encouraging, was followed by a swift and unexpected relapse, underscoring the challenging nature of achieving durable remission in DSRCT. Given the patient's poor performance status, the highly intensive P6 regimen was deemed to be inappropriate and thus was not administered ( ). The subsequent use of multiple lines of soft tissue sarcoma therapy, including trabectedin ( ), eribulin ( ), and pazopanib ( ), reflects the current lack of standardized treatment protocols for relapsed or refractory DSRCT. This case highlights the urgent need for more effective and targeted therapies for DSRCT, particularly in patients with recurrent or metastatic disease.
Of particular significance in this case is the availability of autopsy findings, which provided unequivocal confirmation of the diagnosis and offered invaluable insights into the extent and nature of the disease progression. This level of diagnostic confirmation is particularly valuable in the context of such an uncommon presentation, offering a solid foundation for future research and a clinical understanding of atypical DSRCT manifestations.
Although the diagnosis of DSRCT in this case was initially made based on the histopathological findings, it is important to note the evolving role of genetic testing in the diagnosis and management of DSRCT. The hallmark genetic finding in DSRCT is t(11;22)(p13;q12) translocation, resulting in the EWSR1-WT1 fusion gene ( , ). Although the genomic testing of tumors and germline cells was not possible for this patient, the WHO Classification of Tumours, 5th edition recommends genomic testing for the diagnosis of DSRCT. To date, no substantial reports have demonstrated the heritability of this fusion gene. In Japan, cancer genomic profiling tests have been covered by the national health insurance system since June 2019, thus making it possible to conduct genomic profiling tests within the insurance system. In the Center for Cancer Genomics and Advanced Therapeutics database, 19 cases of DSRCT were registered between June 2019 and August 2024 ( ). There were 17 males, with a mean age of 27 years (range: 13-73), which was consistent with previous studies showing a predominance of young males. The EWSR1-WT1 fusion gene was the most common and was detected in 14 cases. There were 2 cases of the WT1-EWSR1 fusion gene and 1 case of NR4A3-EWSR1. In one case, there was no EWSR1 fusion gene; however, the CIC-DUX4 fusion gene was identified as oncogenic. One case was examined using the OncoGuide™ NCC Oncopanel System, but the evaluation was inconclusive because the gene was not included in the panel. Although EWSR1-WT1 fusion was observed in most cases, it was not present in all cases. The presence or absence of this fusion does not consistently predict treatment response. However, it is important to emphasize that these observations are based on a limited number of cases in the database. Further studies with larger sample sizes are needed to draw more definitive conclusions regarding the relationship between genetic profiles and treatment outcomes in DSRCT.
 The defining genetic hallmark of DSRCT is the t(11;22)(p13;q12) translocation, which results in EWSR1-WT1 gene fusion. This genetic alteration plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of DSRCT and affects both cellular differentiation and tumor behavior. Although our understanding of the molecular basis of DSRCT has improved, the full spectrum of genetic alterations and their clinical implications remain areas of active research.
EWSR1-WT1 functions as a potent chromatin activator, and CCND1 ( ) has been identified as a key target gene in vitro. Previous studies have demonstrated that the EWSR1-WT1 fusion protein interacts with growth factor pathways involving insulin-like growth factor 2, IGF1R, and epidermal growth factor receptor ( ), thus suggesting its critical role in tumor progression through the modulation of these signaling cascades.
The further accumulation of cases with comprehensive genomic profiling is required to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying this distinctive cystic metastatic pattern.
In conclusion, this case of DSRCT with rapid progression to diffuse cystic liver metastases highlights the aggressive nature of this rare malignancy and challenges associated with its management. Autopsy findings provide a unique and valuable perspective on the terminal stages of the disease. Although significant progress has been made in our understanding of DSRCT since this case, much remains to be learned. The unusual presentation of cystic liver metastases underscores the need for further research on the biological mechanisms driving DSRCT progression and metastasis. Continued research, collaborative efforts, and integration of comprehensive genomic profiling into clinical decision-making are crucial for improving the outcomes of patients with this devastating disease.
