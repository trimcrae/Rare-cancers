## Abstract
The DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK) is an abundant nuclear protein that mediates DNA double-strand break repair by nonhomologous end joining (NHEJ). As such, DNA-PK is critical for V(D)J recombination in lymphocytes and for survival in cells exposed to ionizing radiation and clastogens. Peposertib (M3814) is a small molecule DNA-PK inhibitor currently in preclinical and clinical development for cancer treatment. We have developed a high-performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry method for quantitating peposertib and its metabolite in 0.1 mL human plasma. After MTBE liquid–liquid extraction, chromatographic separation was achieved with a Phenomenex Synergi polar reverse phase (4 μm, 2 × 50 mm) column and a gradient of 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile and water over an 8 min run time. Mass spectrometric detection was performed on an ABI SCIEX 4000 with electro-spray, positive-mode ionization. The assay was linear from 10 to 3000 ng/mL for peposertib and 1–300 ng/mL for the metabolite and proved to be both accurate (97.3%–103.7%) and precise (<8.9%CV) fulfilling criteria from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) guidance on bioanalytical method validation. This liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectroscopy (LC–MS/MS) assay will support several ongoing clinical studies by defining peposertib pharmacokinetics.
Keywords: assay, chromatography, DNA-PK, peposertib, tandem mass spectrometry, validation

## INTRODUCTION
The DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK) is a large, ~460 kDa DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs) associated with the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer ( ; ). Ku binds DNA double-strand break (DSB) ends, recruits DNA-PKcs, and the active holoenzyme phosphorylates serine and threonine residues on multiple proteins including itself, with autophosphorylation resulting in kinase inactivation ( ; ; ; ; ). When mammalian cells are exposed to ionizing radiation, DNA-PK is essential for DSB repair via the error-prone, canonical nonhomologous end-joining (NHEJ) mechanism, which is entirely responsible for the fast component of DSB repair, which has a half-life of 10–30 min ( ). NHEJ is active throughout the cell cycle, while homologous recombination, which is not error-prone but requires a sister chromatid template for replication repair, is generally restricted to S and G2-phase cells and is responsible, in part, for the slow component of DSB repair with a half-life of several hours.
Collectively, DNA-PK, and the related protein kinases ATM and ATR recognize DNA damage and coordinate the DNA damage response (DDR) that integrates DNA repair with cell cycle progression or cell death ( ). Since a majority of cytotoxic chemotherapies target DNA, there has been considerable interest in inhibitors of DNA-PK, ATM, and ATR, which may increase both the efficacy and therapeutic index of such therapies by exploiting acquired mutations in cancer cells that restrict the number of mechanisms available for DNA repair ( ). Preclinical studies identified DNA-PK kinase inhibitors as potent radiosensitizers that also potentiated cell killing by agents that induce DSBs including camptothecin ( ; ). Recent work identified loss of POLQ (polymerase theta, POLθ) and other genes in the microhomology-mediated end-joining (MMEJ) pathway as key predictors of sensitivity to DNA-PK inhibition ( ).
Peposertib (M3814, nedisertib) is a DNA-PK inhibitor in Phase 1 and Phase 2 ( ). To support both ongoing clinical trials of peposertib, a simple, accurate, and precise liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectroscopy (LC–MS/MS) assay for quantitating peposertib and its O-demethylated metabolite was developed and validated according to the FDA Bioanalytical Method Validation Guidance ( ). Assay application was demonstrated with patient samples from an ongoing clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04172532). The assay described here is supporting multiple NCI sponsored clinical trials of peposertib.

## EXPERIMENTAL
2.1 ∣.

## Chemicals and reagents
Peposertib, O-desmethyl-peposertib (metabolite), and [D8]-peposertib (IS) were manufactured and provided by Merck (KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany), see , for structures. Acetonitrile, water (both HPLC grade), and methyl tert-butyl ether were purchased from Fisher Scientific (Fairlawn, NJ, USA). Dimethyl sulfoxide and formic acid were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Control EDTA and heparinized human plasma were purchased from Lampire Biological Laboratories (Pipersville, PA, USA). Nitrogen for mass spectrometric applications was purified with a nitrogen generator (Parker Balston, Haverhill, MA, USA).

2.2 ∣.

## Chromatography
The LC system consisted of an Agilent (Palo Alto, CA, USA) 1200 SL autosampler and binary pump flowing into a Phenomenex (Torrance, CA USA) Synergi Polar-RP 80 Å (4 μm, 50 × 2.0 mm) column using a gradient style elution program. Needle wash consisted of acetonitrile:isopropanol:acetone (40:40:20, v/v/v). Mobile phase solvent A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water and mobile phase solvent B consisted of 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile. The initial mobile phase composition was 70% solvent A and 30% solvent B. From 0 to 2.0 min, at a 0.5 mL/min flow rate, solvent A was decreased to 55%, and conditions held until 2.1 min. Between 2.1 and 3.0 min, solvent A was maintained at 10% and held constant until 3.1 min, followed by a return to the initial composition at an increased flow rate of 0.8 mL/min until 5.0 min. The total run time was 5 min with an injection volume of 5 μL, resulting in a retention time of 2.1 and 1.1 min, respectively. Between 0 to 0.5 and 3.0 to 5 min, the HPLC flow was diverted to waste.

2.3 ∣.

## Mass spectrometry
Detection was accomplished with a SCIEX4000 tandem quadrupole mass spectrometer (Framingham, MA) utilizing electrospray ionization in positive-ion multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. The settings of the mass spectrometer in positive-mode scanning parameters were as follows: curtain gas 50, IS voltage 5000 V, probe temperature 500°C, GS1 40, GS2 40, DP of 50 V, EP of 5 V, CE of 30 V, and an exit potential of 10 V. The temperature of the autosampler was 4°C. The MRM m/z transitions monitored were 483.0 > 447.2 for peposertib, 490.0 > 454.3 for IS, and 468.0 > 432.2 for O-desmethyl-peposertib. Control of the LC system and mass spectrometer as well as data collection was accomplished with Analyst software (version 1.6.2).

2.4 ∣.

## Preparation of calibration standards and quality control (QC) samples
Stock solutions of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib were created by dissolving 1 mg/mL in DMSO and into a mixed working stock “A” of 0.1 and 0.01 mg/mL, respectively, in DMSO. Stock solution of IS was created by dissolving 1 mg/mL in DMSO and then storing all solutions at −80°C. On the day of the assay, peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib working stock were diluted in tenfold steps in DMSO to obtain 0.1 and 0.01 A mg/mL solutions. These solutions were diluted in human plasma to produce the following peposertib concentrations: 10, 30, 100, 300, 500, 1000, and 3000 ng/mL, and 1/10th those concentrations of O-desmethyl-peposertib. For each calibration series, zero and blank samples were also prepared from control plasma. QC stock solutions were prepared independently and stored at −80°C. These solutions were diluted in human plasma to produce the following QC samples of either: lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) 10/1 ng/mL, quality control low (QCL) 25/2.5 ng/mL, quality control mid (QCM) 250/25 ng/mL, and quality control high (QCH) 2500/250 ng/mL. On the day of the assay, [D8]-peposertib was diluted to 25 ng/mL in acetonitrile as an IS working stock. The maximum composition of DMSO in calibration standards and QCs was ≤5%.

2.5 ∣.

## Sample preparation
A volume of 100 μL of the standard, QC, or sample plasma was pipetted into a microfuge tube, and 10 μL 25 ng/mL IS was added. A total of 500 μL of MTBE was then added followed by vortexing for 30 s on a Vortex Genie-2 set at 10 (Model G-560 Scientific Industries, Bohemia, NY, USA). Samples were centrifuged at 12,000×g at room temperature for 2 min. Microcentrifuge samples were then placed in a −80 freezer for 25 min, supernatant was transferred to 12 × 75 mm borosilicate glass tube where it was dried down under nitrogen gas. Samples were then resuspended with 100 μL of water:acetonitrile (70:30), vortexed briefly, and supernatants were transferred to autosampler vials followed by injection of 5 μL into the LC–MS/MS system.

2.6 ∣.

## Validation procedures
2.6.1 ∣.

## Calibration curve and LLOQ
Calibration standards and blanks were prepared in human potassium EDTA plasma (see and ) and analyzed in triplicate to establish the calibration range with acceptable accuracy and precision.

2.6.2 ∣.

## Accuracy and precision
The accuracy and precision of the assay were determined by analyzing samples in human EDTA plasma at the LLOQ, QCL, QCM, and QCH concentrations in six replicates each in three analytical runs, together with independently prepared, triplicate calibration curves.

2.6.3 ∣.

## Selectivity and specificity
To investigate whether endogenous matrix constituents interfered with the assay, six individual batches of control, drug-free human EDTA plasma were processed and analyzed according to the described procedure. Responses of analytes at the LLOQ concentrations were compared with the response of the blank samples. The cross talk of peposertib and the IS, into one another, were characterized by monitoring MRM channels of separately spiked samples of 3000 ng/mL peposertib, 2.5 ng/mL IS and quantitating the response in the other channel.
Carryover was assessed by separately injecting plasma samples with 3000 ng/mL peposertib, or 300 ng/mL O-desm-peposertib, followed by serial plasma blank injections.

2.6.4 ∣.

## Extraction recovery and matrix effect
We determined the extraction recovery of peposertib and O-desm-peposertib from EDTA plasma by comparing the absolute response of an extract of control plasma to which the analyte had been added after protein precipitation, with the absolute response of an extract of plasma to which the same amount had been added before protein precipitation. The matrix effect by plasma matrix components was defined as the change of the absolute response of an extract of control plasma to which analyte had been added after the protein precipitation relative to the absolute response of solvent to which the same amount of the analyte had been added. Experiments were performed in replicates of four at the QCL, QCM, and QCH concentrations.

2.6.5 ∣.

## Stability
Stock solution stabilities were determined using freshly prepared stock solutions in DMSO and comparing them to stock solutions left on the benchtop at RT for 6 h (benchtop stability) or stored at −80°C for 55 months (long-term stability). Stock solutions were diluted to 100 ng/mL peposertib and 10 ng/mL O-desmethyl-peposertib (benchtop stability) or 1000 ng/mL (long-term freezer stability) in control plasma and prepared in replicates of four. Concentrations used to assess stability were derived by the same calibration curve and QC series.
The stability of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib in human EDTA plasma at −80°C was determined by assaying samples before and after storage for 29 months. The effect of 3 freeze/thaw cycles analyte concentrations on plasma was evaluated by assaying samples after they had been frozen (−80°C) and thawed on three separate days and comparing the results with those of freshly prepared samples. The stability in plasma during sample preparation was evaluated by assaying samples before and after 6 h of storage at room temperature and ambient light. All stability testing in plasma was performed in replicates of four at the QCL, QCM, and QCH concentrations, with the exception of long-term frozen (−80°C), which had replicates of five at the QCL, QCM, and QCH concentrations. To evaluate the stability of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib in reconstituted samples in the autosampler, QC samples and calibration curves were reinjected approximately 72 h after the first injection and compared with concentrations derived from the second injection with those derived from the first injection using the initial duplicate calibration curve, as well as relative to a fresh duplicate calibration curve. The results of the second runs were expressed as a percentage of their respective values in the first runs.

2.6.6 ∣.

## Additional validation items
The impact of hemolysis was assessed by adding 10% (v/v) pre-hemolyzed whole blood to blanks, LLOQ, and QCH samples (n = 4) and incubating at room temperature for 4 h. Heparinized plasma was evaluated as an alternative anticoagulated matrix to the standard EDTA plasma by analyzing LLOQ, QCL, QCM, and QCH samples (n = 6) prepared in EDTA plasma against a heparinized plasma based triplicate curve. The ability to analyze urine was evaluated by adding 10% (v/v) human urine to LLOQ and QCH samples (n = 4). Dilution integrity was shown by preparing plasma samples (n = 3) at 10,000 ng/ mL peposertib and 1000 ng/mL O-desmethyl-peposertib and analysis after tenfold dilution (to 1000 and 100 ng/mL) with control plasma.

2.7 ∣.

## Application of the assay
To demonstrate the applicability of the assay, we analyzed samples from three patients enrolled in a clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04172532) of peposertib in combination with radiation therapy. Patients had signed informed consent on an institutional review board approved protocol. The patients received a 100 mg PO dose of peposertib, and blood samples were collected between 0 and 24 h. Pharmacokinetic parameters were determined non-compartmentally.

2.8 ∣.

## Incurred sample reanalysis and external validation
Upon completion of the validation, incurred sample reanalysis was performed on 12 plasma samples from the ongoing clinical trial. Two separate analytical runs were performed on two different days, and the difference between the first (original) and second (repeat) concentration was calculated as: %diff = [(repeat—original) /mean (repeat, original)] × 100%. Per FDA guidance, the difference should be within ±20% for at least 67% of the samples analyzed ( ). Blinded QCs were prepared at three levels and provided by the Analytical Pharmacology Core at City of Hope Cancer Center.

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1 ∣.

## Validation of the assay
3.1.1 ∣.

## Chromatography
The retention time of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib was 2.1 and 1.1 min respectively, which corresponds to a capacity factor of 3.2 and 1.4 in conjunction with a void time of 0.5 min. Representative chromatograms of the LLOQ and IS in human plasma are displayed in .
 
3.1.2 ∣.

## Calibration curve and LLOQ
The selected assay range of 10–3000 and 1–300 ng/mL for both peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib fulfilled the FDA criteria for the LLOQ concentration and the calibration curve (calibrators within ±15% of nominal concentrations, with LLOQ within ±20%, and 75% and a minimum of six nonzero calibrators meeting these criteria in each run) ( ). Accuracy and precision for each concentration from triplicate calibration curves prepared on three separate days are reported in representative calibration curve, and the corresponding correlation and regression coefficient and corresponding accuracies are shown in .

3.1.3 ∣.

## Accuracy and precision
QC based accuracies ranged from 99.7% to 103.7% for peposertib and 97.3% to 101.3% for O-desmethyl-peposertib. The intra- and inter-assay precisions for the tested concentrations (LLOQ, QCL, QCM, QCH) were all within the defined acceptance criteria (accuracy: ±15% of nominal concentrations; except ±20% at LLOQ, precision: within-run and between runs: ±15% CV, except ±20% CV at LLOQ) ( ) ( ).
 
3.1.4 ∣.

## Selectivity and specificity
Chromatograms of six individual control plasma samples contained no co-eluting peaks >20% of the analyte areas at the LLOQ concentration (interference ≤3.2% peposertib and ≤13.3% O-desmethyl-peposertib) and no co-eluting peaks >5% of the IS area (interference ≤0.61%).
The cross talk of the 2.5 ng/mL IS into the peposertib channel was 5.03%, corresponding to 0.12 ng/mL of peposertib, which is acceptable; 3000 ng/mL peposertib produced 0.001% cross talk into the IS MRM channel, corresponding to 0.03 ng/mL IS, which is acceptable.
Carryover in the developed assay resulted in 0.003% of the original 3000 ng/mL peposertib and 0.008% of the original 300 ng/mL O-desmethyl-peposertib sample signal in the first sequential blank plasma injection ( ). Defining the maximum carryover as 20% of the LLOQ area, this would mean that any sample with a concentration of more than approximately 67 μg/mL peposertib, or 2.5 μg/mL O-desmethyl-peposertib could result in unacceptable carryover into the next sample.

3.1.5 ∣.

## Extraction recovery and matrix effect
The recoveries of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib ranged from 85.2% to 94.0% (CV 4.5% to 6.5%) and 80.0% to 84.9% (CV 2.6% to 6.5%), respectively. Matrix effect ranged from 7.5% to 8.8% (CV 2.4% to 5.2%) and 2.5% to 4.9% (CV 2.5% to 5.6%), respectively ( ).
 
3.1.6 ∣.

## Stability
Stock solution stability of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib in DMSO after 6 h at RT was 97.2% and 101.2%, respectively, and after 55 months at −80°C was 94.8% and 11.4%, respectively (N = 4). The stability of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib in human plasma is reported in . Benchtop stability in plasma after 6 h at RT with ambient light ranged between 99.0% and 101.3% for peposertib and 100.5% and 103.2% for O-desmethyl-peposertib (N = 4). Stability in plasma after 3 freeze–thaw cycles (−80°C to RT) for peposertib was between 97.7% to 102.9% and for O-desmethyl-peposertib was between 99.0% to 110.0% (N = 4). Long-term stability of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib in plasma at −80°C was adequate with recovery between 96.9%–112.2% and 102.2%–107.0%, respectively (N = 5). The ratio of analyte to IS of plasma extracts of peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib at QC levels, when reconstituted and kept in the autosampler for 72 h, ranged from 98.6% to 104.3% (CV ≤ 6.0%) and 92.3% to 109.9% (CV ≤ 7.2%) of the initial response (N = 6), while with fresh calibration curves, the range was 85.2% to 93.5% (CV ≤ 6.9%) and 92.3% to 109.9% (CV ≤ 7.2%) of the initial response while also passing the requirements set by the FDA (accuracy ±15% of nominal) ( ).
 
3.1.7 ∣.

## Additional validation items
Hemolyzed plasma did not affect quantitation with an accuracy of 103.8%–108.4% (CV ≤ 12.7%) for peposertib and 100.3%–102.4% (CV ≤ 5.6%) for O-desmethyl-peposertib. Heparinized plasma was free of interference and peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib calibrators prepared in heparinized plasma used to quantitate QC samples prepared in EDTA plasma resulted in adequate performance with an accuracy of 93.0%–96.5% (CV ≤ 4.7%) and 92.2%–99.7% (CV ≤ 7.8%), respectively. Urine, diluted 1:10 v/v with control plasma, did not impact quantitation with an accuracy of 95.7%–101.8% (CV ≤ 6.0%) for peposertib and 97.4%–102.9% (CV ≤ 7.4%) for O-desmethyl-peposertib, suggesting urine samples can be analyzed after tenfold dilution in control plasma. Dilution integrity of the assay was confirmed with 111.7% accuracy (CV 3.7%) for peposertib and 109.0% accuracy (CV 2.4%) for O-desmethyl-peposertib. The qualifier transition of peposertib (m/z 483.0 > 373.0) had an intensity of 3.6% of the quantifier transition m/z 483.0 > 447.2, with a suggested tolerance of ±50%, which results in boundaries of 1.8%–5.4% ( ). Across QCL, QCM and QCH samples, N = 6 each, the relative ion intensity of the qualifier ranged from 2.9% to 4.3%, well within tolerance. The qualifier transition of O-desmethyl-peposertib (m/z 468.0 > 372.0) had an intensity of ~21.5% of the quantifier transition m/z 468.0 > 432.2, with a suggested tolerance of ±25%, which results in boundaries of 1.8%–5.4%. Across QCL, QCM, and QCH samples, N = 6 each, the relative ion intensity of the qualifier ranged from 2.9% to 4.3%, also within tolerance. Endogenous phospholipids did not co-elute with phospholipid MRM channels, limiting the risk of interference ( ).

3.2 ∣.

## Incurred sample reanalysis and external validation
Incurred sample reanalysis of 13 samples yielded the following results: 7.3% of samples with a difference larger than 20%, 2.9% median difference, 8.6% median absolute difference for peposertib; and 8.3% of samples with a difference larger than 20%, 6.2% median difference, 6.6% median absolute difference for O-desmethyl-peposertib. These results were within the defined acceptance criteria of at least 67% of results having a difference within ±20% ( ).
Blinded QCs (three levels) prepared by an external laboratory were analyzed in replicates of five. Upon analysis and unblinding, median difference bias was −0.0% (range −7.2% to 11.5%).

3.3 ∣.

## Development
Development began with mass spectrometric tuning to optimize the precursor and product ions for peposertib, O-desmethyl metabolite and internal standard. Infusion solvents of 50/50 water/methanol (v/v) or 50/50 water/acetonitrile (v/v) resulted in similar ionization intensity. Acetonitrile was selected as the organic mobile phase component due to its reduced backpressure. Spectra from MS scan in positive ionization mode revealed modestly large m/z protonated molecular ion intensities for peposertib, metabolite, and IS, respectively; see . Initially, the standard curve for peposertib becomes saturated at the higher end of the desired concentration range. We therefore changed the mass transition for peposertib from 483.0 > 448.2 to 483.0 > 447.2, resulting in linear standard curves for the desired concentrations.
The LC method was developed with injections of neat standards into the LC–MS/MS system using three different Phenomenex columns: a Synergi Polar-RP 2 × 50 mm 4 μm, a Luna Phenyl-Hexyl 2 × 50 mm 5 μm, and a Kinetex C18, 2.6 μm, 2.1 × 50 mm. These columns were compared using a gradient mobile phase of acetonitrile and water each containing 0.1% formic acid and a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The Synergi Polar-RP and the Kinetex C18 showed very similar retention times and peak shapes, while the Phenyl-Hexyl column caused significant tailing. We selected the Synergi Polar-RP column because it provided similar results to the Kinetex column while having a lower back pressure.
For compound extraction, we evaluated both a dilute and shoot method with different amounts of trifluoroacetic acid in acetonitrile, and acetonitrile precipitation method followed by solvent blow down and resuspension. However, these attempts did not result in an acceptably low LLOQ. The methyl tert-butyl ether liquid–liquid extraction did result in an acceptable LLOQ and was therefore selected for the final method.

3.4 ∣.

## Application of the assay
All concentrations between 0.5 and 24 h post-administration were within the assay range ( ). At 100 mg, Cmax was 555 ng/mL occurring 2.1 h post dose, which agrees with the previously reported Cmax 437 ng/mL ( ). The AUC0-∞ was 4071 ng•h/mL, which is higher than the previously reported value of 2390 ng•h/mL at the same dose level. The half-life was 7.3 h, which was similar to the previously reported 6.3 h. Overall, the assay proved appropriate to accurately quantitate peposertib in plasma.
 

4 ∣.

## CONCLUSION
We developed and validated a clinically relevant LC–MS/MS assay to quantitate peposertib and its metabolite O-desmethyl-peposertib in plasma that is both accurate and reliable, which will support several clinical trials (Clinicaltrails.gov identifiers NCT04068194, NCT04092270, NCT04172532, NCT03983824, NCT04750954, and NCT04533750). The assay reported here was developed and validated to the criteria of the FDA guidance and proved able to accurately capture clinically relevant concentrations. Previously, an assay in mouse brain and plasma was reported that was slightly faster at a 4 min run time and was more sensitive geared toward mouse PK. However, metabolite was not included, no validation was reported, and an unrelated internal standard was used, suggesting lower robustness ( ). Our method will offer investigators a reliable, accurate, and precise method of quantitating peposertib and O-desmethyl-peposertib and determining their pharmacokinetic properties.
