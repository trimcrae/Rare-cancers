## Abstract

## Background
The HER2 targeted therapy paradigm has increasingly shifted in recent years from focussed treatment of only cancers with high HER2 expression towards those with also low HER2 expression, unlocking a larger subset of patients, in either single or combination treatment strategies. Radiotherapeutic HER2-targeting single-domain antibody (sdAb) 2Rs15d has been previously developed and characterised (pre-)clinically for HER2 high-expressing tumours. The current study explores the use of [131I]I-GMIB- and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-labelled sdAb 2Rs15d as single treatments or as combinations with the PARP-inhibitor Olaparib in HER2 low-expressing xenografts.

## Results
[131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d specifically bound to HER2-low-expressing DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells in vitro, inflicted DNA damage (elevated γH2AX) and decreased cell viability as a single agent, an effect that was invigorated upon combination with Olaparib. Radiotracers showed specific tumour uptake in DLD-1 BRCA2−/− tumour xenografts, correlating with the low HER2 expression in this model. Clear antitumour effects were observed in mice treated with fractionated [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (8 × 37 MBq, p’=0.00232) and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (8 × 20 kBq, p’=0.0149) compared to vehicle control. In combination with Olaparib (28 × 75 mg/kg), synergistic effects were observed for both [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d.

## Conclusion
[131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d showed therapeutic efficacy in tumour xenografts expressing low levels of HER2. Furthermore, when treatment was combined with Olaparib both radiopharmaceuticals prolonged survival further, underlining a synergistic effect.

## Introduction
Radioligand therapy (RLT) has (re-)emerged in recent years as a relevant therapeutic strategy in the fight against cancer, invigorated by the promising results of FDA approved drugs such as Pluvicto® or Lutathera® [ ]. Herein, the high specificity of the targeting vector is combined with a cytotoxic radionuclide (alpha or beta particles) payload, allowing precise tumour irradiation with limited exposure to healthy tissues [ ]. However, RLT is only suitable for specific cancer types that vastly express the biomarker of interest, thereby limiting its therapeutic range. To broaden this application range, currently several approaches are being followed. A first focus is on pan-cancer targets in the tumour stroma, such as fibroblast activation protein alpha and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor, to lose the dependency on cancer-specific targets. A second focus is on combination strategies, to increase therapeutic efficacy of RLT in patients with low target expression. Examples of these are immune-checkpoint blockades, chemotherapeutics, and DNA damage response (DDR) inhibitors [ , ].
Combination strategies with RLT have focused mainly on inhibiting key proteins involved in DDR (e.g. poly(adenosine diphosphate ribose) polymerase (PARP), heat shock protein 90, and DNA-dependent protein kinases), with the main goal of inducing synthetic lethality in tumour cells and increasing therapeutic response rates. PARP proteins have been thoroughly investigated as combinatorial targets and are implicated to play a role in the repair of single (single strand break repair pathway)- and double-strand (recruitment of factors to facilitate repair) DNA breaks [ – ]. The combination strategy focusses on hampering the DNA repair pathways of induced RLT DNA damage using PARP inhibitors, decreasing the survival of cancer cells. More concrete, ionizing radiation can induce either SSBs or DSBs in cancer, in which SSBs in a normal setting would be repaired using the SSBR pathway. However, repair via this pathway is blocked due to the use of PARPi resulting in an accumulation of SSBs ultimately leading to an abundance of DSBs [ ]. This combination effect has been described to work best in DNA-repair impaired cells such as BRCA mutations which results in a hampered homologous recombination, a DSB repair pathway [ ].
HER2 remains a target of interest in oncology and is overexpressed in multiple cancers, including ovarian, breast, colorectal, and gastric cancers, leading to unfavourable patient prognosis. Although the target has been heavily investigated, with several FDA approved HER2 targeting drugs in the market, research continues to develop next-generation HER2 therapies to tackle resistance problems and increase their effectiveness [ ]. Approved HER2 therapies only showed good efficacy in a subset of patients with HER2 overexpression but were hampered by a low prevalence of the target receptor (~ 20%); therefore, new strategies also focus on cancers with low HER2 expression (~ 30–60% prevalence), unlocking a larger patient pool [ , ]. The DESTINY-Breast04 (NCT03734029, N = 557) randomised multicentre phase 3 trial is the first to evaluate trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu®) in patients with low HER2+ metastatic breast cancer and showed a statistically and clinically significant increase in progression-free survival and overall survival compared to the standard-of-care treatment, indicating the relevance of the low HER2+ expressing patient pool [ ].
This study aimed to evaluate the potential and therapeutic efficacy of [131I]-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d in HER2 low-expressing tumour xenografts, as single agents or in combination with the PARP inhibitor Olaparib. sdAbs have been selected as bio-vectors of interest due to their high selectivity, deep tumour penetration, and fast clearance [ ]. Both β-RLT (131Iodine, medium energy, long travel time) and α-RLT(225Actinium, high energy, short travel time) were evaluated since they possess different intrinsic properties that could be of particular interest dependent on the pathology of the patients highlighting as well the versatility of sdAbs as a biovector [ ].
The therapeutic efficacy of both radioligands has already been validated in HER2 high-expressing SKOV3 xenografts [ , ], and [131I]-GMIB-2Rs15d even advanced to a phase 1 clinical trial (NCT04467515, N = 13), which demonstrated good tumour retention with an acceptable safety profile [ ]. Furthermore, a phase II clinical study using [68Ga]Ga-NOTA-2Rs15d showed high radiotracer uptake in cancer lesions with low levels of HER2 expression [ ], underlining the potential of 2Rs15d in the low HER2 space. The main goal of this study was to assess RLT in HER2 low-expressing cancer lesions and/or improve RLT by inducing synthetic lethality through PARP inhibition.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture conditions
Human SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells (RRID: CVCL_0532, ATCC), human colorectal DLD-1 cancer cells (RRID: CVCL_0248, Horizon Discovery) and human colorectal DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cancer cells (RRID: CVCL_HD57, Horizon Discovery) were cultured in RPMI1640 medium (Gibco). The medium was supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS (Gibco), 1 mM sodium pyruvate (Gibco), 2 mM L-glutamine (Gibco), and penicillin-streptomycin (100 units/mL and 100 µg/mL, respectively) (Gibco). Cells were cultured in incubators at 37 °C and 5% CO2. All cell lines were tested regularly for mycoplasma contamination.

## Assessment of HER2-expression on cells through flow cytometry
SKOV3, DLD-1, and DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells (1 × 105) in 100 µL of FACS buffer (PBS, 1% FBS, 2.5 mM EDTA) were plated in a U-bottom 96-well plate and incubated for 20 min at 4 °C with anti-human HER2/Neu PE-conjugated mouse IgG1 antibody (1/200) (BD Biosciences Cat# 340552, RRID: AB_400055) or PE-conjugated mouse IgG1 isotype control (1/200) (BD Biosciences Cat# 550617, RRID: AB_10050483). Subsequently, the cells were washed 3 times, resuspended, and subjected to flow cytometry using BD FACSCelesta (BD Biosciences). The flow cytometry results were analysed using FlowJo™ v10.8 Software (BD Life Sciences).

## Preparation of [131I]I-GMIB-sdAb
Radiolabelling of sdAbs with Iodine-131 has been reported elsewhere [ ]. Briefly, N-succinimidyl 4-(1,2-bis-tert-butoxycarbonyl)guanidinomethyl-3-(trimethylstannyl)benzoate precursor (SGMTB- Boc2; 50–500 µg) dissolved in anhydrous acetonitrile (0.2-1 mL) was incubated for 10 min at RT with Na[131I]I (2–16 GBq) containing N-chlorosuccinimide (NCS; 400–1500 µg) and acetic acid (12.5%), generating [131I]I-SGMIB-Boc2. In case required due to high starting activity, the radioactive mixture was enriched with a scavenger (10 mg/mL sodium ascorbate, 50 mg/mL mannitol) to prevent radiolysis. Subsequently, the BOC groups were deprotected using strongly acidic conditions such as TFA or H3PO4 (0.2–1 mL) at 75–80 °C for 2–10 min, depending on the application. [131I]I-SGMIB was purified using Sep-Pak tC18 Plus Short or Light Cartridge, and formulated with 400 µL − 1 mL EtOH: PBS (1:1 or 8:2). Furthermore, 2Rs15d or R3B23 (300–2500 µg, 70–750 µL) in 0.3 M borate buffer (pH 9.0) was incubated with [131I]I-SGMIB for 30 min at RT and purified by size exclusion chromatography (SEC) using PD-10, NAP-5 or HiTrap desalting columns (Sephadex, Cytiva), depending on the volume of the reaction mixture, with 0.9% NaCl solution (pH 5.2) containing ascorbic acid (35 mg/mL) and gentisic acid (17 mg/mL) as the mobile phase [ , ]. Sterility was obtained by 0.22 μm filtration. Quality control was evaluated by iTLC with methanol as mobile phase and by SEC on a Superdex 75 10/300 GL column, equilibrated in PBS (Flow rate: 1.0 mL/min; run time: 30 min).

## Preparation of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-sdAb
The radiolabelling procedure has been described previously [ ]. In short, 225Ac (1.89 MBq for in vitro assays, 3.90–3.97 MBq for biodistributions and 0.77–0.83 MBq for therapy) (Van Overeem Nuclear BV) was obtained in a carrier-free dried form and dissolved in 0.01 N nitric acid (HNO3). Conjugates DOTA-2Rs15d or DOTA-R3B23 in 0.2 M ammonium acetate solution pH 6 were prepared as described before [ ]. Afterwards, constructs were incubated with 225Ac for 30 min at 50 °C, after which the drug product was purified by NAP5 SEC (Cytiva) using a mobile phase of ascorbic acid 20 mg/mL, DTPA 2 mg/mL in 0.9% NaCl, pH 5.2 for in vivo studies and PBS for in vitro studies. The final formulation was filtered through a 0.22 μm filter and formulated for in vivo studies with Gelofusine (Braun) as a final concentration of ascorbic acid 10 mg/mL, DTPA 1 mg/mL in NaCl 0.9%, pH 5.2 / Gelofusine (50/50). Radio chemical purity was assessed by iTLC-SG using 0.1 M citrate buffer, pH 5, as the mobile phase. High molecular weight species were assessed using SE-HPLC (Superdex 75 5/150 GL column equilibrated in PBS at a flow rate of 0.25 mL/min and a run time of 15 min).

## Cell radioligand binding assays with radiolabelled anti-HER2 SdAbs
DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells (0.1–5 × 106) were suspended in microcentrifuge tubes prior to the start of the experiment. Next, the cells were washed and incubated for 1 h at 4 °C with [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d or [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (0–300 nM, n = 3) alone or with a 100 fold molar excess of the unlabelled sdAb. Subsequently, the cells were washed, and the bound radiotracer fraction was collected from the wells using 1 M NaOH. Relative affinities were calculated using GraphPad Prism 10 (specific bound CPM fraction = total bound CPM fraction - total bound CPM of the blocked fraction).

## γHA2X assessment
DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells were plated in a 24-well plate at a concentration of 7.5 × 104 cells per well. 6 h post cellular adhesion, some wells were treated with 1 µM of Olaparib (Fisher Scientific). The next day, cells were washed and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C with 500 µL of single treatment [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (1 MBq/mL), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (10 kBq/mL), or as a control with RPMI1640 medium. In wells incubated with combined treatments or treated only with Olaparib, 1 µM of Olaparib was added to the medium containing the radiolabelled sdAb or medium, respectively. 24 h later, cells were washed, collected, and stained with 1/1000 Zombie Green™ Fixable viability kit (Biolegend, cat 423112) diluted in PBS for 20 min at 4°C. Cells were washed twice with FACS buffer (PBS, 1% FBS, 2.5 mM EDTA) and fixed with 100 µL of Cyto-Fast Fix/Perm solution (Biolegend, cat 750000133) for 20 min at 4°C. Next, cells were washed and permeabilized for 5 min at RT with 100 µL per well of Cyto-Fast Perm/Wash (Biolegend, cat 750000135) diluted 10x in MQ water. Mouse anti-human-H2AX-BV421 (BD Biosciences, BD Horizon, cat 564720) was used in Cyto-Fast Perm/Wash solution at a dilution of 1/20 for 30 min at 4°C. The isotype counterpart mouse IgG1-BV421 (BD Biosciences, BD Horizon, cat 562438) was used at the same concentration under the same conditions as Mouse anti-human-H2AX-BV421. Stained cells were washed, resuspended in FACS buffer, and acquired on a BD FACSCelesta flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). The flow cytometry results were analysed using FlowJo™ v10.8 Software (BD Life Sciences).

## Cell viability assessment
DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells were plated in a 96-well plate at a concentration of 0.5–1 × 104 cells per well overnight. The next day, cells were washed and for single treatment incubated for 1 h at 37 °C with [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (1 MBq/mL or 10 MBq/mL), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (10 kBq/mL, 100 kBq/mL and 200 kBq/mL), or as a control with RPMI1640 medium. Subsequently, wells were washed and incubated for combination treatment with Olaparib (100–0.0001 µM) or as a control with RPMI1640 medium for 5 days. Cell viability was assessed by luminescence using the ID3 Spectramax ATP cell viability assay (Promega). The percentage cell viability was normalised to the percentage of untreated cells. Graphs were plotted using GraphPad Prism 10 using non-linear regression curve fit, selecting log (inhibitor) vs. response–variable slope (four parameters).

## Animal models
Female Crl: NU(NCr)-Foxn1nu mice (RRID: IMSR_CRL:490, Charles River, Massachusetts, USA) were injected subcutaneously into the neck with 5 × 106 DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells in 150 µL PBS and allowed to grow for 2–3 weeks. Biodistribution and dosimetry studies were performed when tumour volumes reached an average of 100 mm³. For therapy studies, animals were included in the study design when tumour volume reached > 50 mm3 and when no tumour necrosis could be observed. Tumour volumes were determined using calliper measurements. All animals undergoing [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d injection received drinking water supplemented with 3 mL lugol/L water 2 weeks before the start of the experiment to prevent the uptake of [131I]I in the thyroid. Animal studies were approved by the ethical committee of the Vrije Universiteit Brussel (ECD 21-272-2).

## Long-term biodistribution and dosimetry
DLD-1 BRCA2−/− tumour-bearing nude mice (n = 4) were i.v. injected with (i) [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (6.41 ± 0.10 MBq, ~ 5 µg), (ii) [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (57.04 ± 0.27 kBq, ~ 5 µg), (iii) [131I]I-GMIB-R3B23 (5.42 ± 0.042 MBq, ~ 5 µg), or (iv) [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-R3B23 (81.66 ± 1.44 kBq, ~ 5 µg), and sacrificed at different time points until 192 h p.i., after which animals were dissected for ex vivo analysis and dosimetry calculations, as previously described [ ]. Radiolabelled R3B23 controls was assessed only at 1 h p.i. to determine nonspecific tumour uptake. The results are presented as %IA/g. The S value per 1 g sphere for 131I (3.04 × 10− 11 J/Bq.s) and 225Ac (4.40 × 10− 9 J/kBq.s) was obtained from RADAR phantoms (https://www.doseinfo-radar.com/RADARphan.html) and used to calculate the absorbed dose in the organs. For 225Ac, the S value includes the decay of all the daughter radionuclides.

## Therapeutic efficacy
Combination therapy was assessed in HER2-low DLD1 BRCA2 −/− tumour-bearing nude mice. Animals (n = 12) were randomly allocated to the following groups: single agent treatment [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (8 × 40.45 ± 0.46 MBq, ~ [20–32 µg]), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (8 × 22.39 ± 0.73 kBq, ~ 15 µg), Olaparib (IP injection 75 mg/kg, 28 consecutive days), 0.9% NaCl or a combination of [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and Olaparib or [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d and Olaparib in an identical dose regimen as described above. Animals with a tumour volume < 50 mm³ were classified as non-tumour-bearing mice and excluded from the study. Animals developing tumour necrosis in the ongoing study or those who died of non-health-related reasons were also excluded from the study. Mice completed the study when the tumour volume reached > 2000 mm³.

## Statistical analysis
γH2AX values were tested for normality using a Shapiro-Wilk test and analysed by one-way ANOVA (Tukey correction for multiple comparisons). Therapy studies were analysed by Kaplan-Meier curves using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test. Adjusted p’ values are calculated using the Holm-Bonferroni correction. The test results were considered significant at α ≤ 0.05. Analyses and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.

## Results

## Radiolabelling
Specific activity and radiochemical purity obtained after radiotracer production is described in Table  .
 

## Cell characterization
HER2 expression in DLD-1, DLD-1 BRCA2−/−, and control SKOV3 cancer cells was evaluated using flow cytometry. All three cell lines showed HER2 expression, but DLD-1 and DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells showed 5-fold less HER2 expression than SKOV3 cells (Fig. ). DLD-1 BRCA2−/− xenografts were classified in the literature for HER2 as IHC + 1/+2, indicating low HER2 expression [ ]. DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells were selected to evaluate the synergistic effect of RLT and the PARP inhibitor Olaparib, as FDA approval was obtained for patients with BRCA mutations. SKOV3 cells were used as control cells because successful therapeutic efficacy could be established using [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d before [ , ].
 

## Cell radioligand binding assay
Saturation binding assays were executed to evaluate radiotracer binding potential to low HER2 expressing cells. The relative affinity of [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d was similar between HER2 expressing cell lines (Fig. A-C). For [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (Fig. D-F), the obtained relative affinities were slightly higher, but still in the low nanomolar range. This could potentially be explained by the variation in their coupling ratios, which are 1.18 and 2.51, respectively. Previous data has shown that the degree of conjugation can influence the affinity of the constructs [ ]. Furthermore, the highest specific signal (count per minute) was detected on SKOV3 cells, resembling high HER2 expression in line with the flow cytometry data.
 

## γHA2X assessment for early detection
γH2AX, a phosphorylated histone forms quickly after DNA double strand breaks (in a 1:1 manner) and is the first step in localizing and recruiting DNA breaks. Therefore, γH2AX was monitored in DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells, as an early DNA break biomarker, upon single treatment with Olaparib, [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d, [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d, or combined treatments. 24 h post incubation with radiolabelled 2Rs15d, γH2AX was assessed by flow cytometry (Fig. ). DLD-1 BRCA2−/− cells maintained in control medium were used to determine basal γH2AX expression compared to treated conditions. Cells incubated with Olaparib showed higher expression of γH2AX than control medium and was equal to cells receiving [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d as a single treatment. Of note, combined Olaparib and [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d led to more elevated γH2AX levels than the corresponding single treatment arms. Importantly, single treatment with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d, displayed enhanced γH2AX expression compared to the single Olaparib condition. Furthermore, the combination of Olaparib and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d induced the most potent γH2AX response compared to single treatments or medium control. As expected, α-RLT has been known to induce more DSBs compared to β-RLT, a result of its higher energy deposition, which could also be visualized in this assay [ ].
 

## Cell viability assessment
The objective of combination treatments is to obtain cell death in cancer cells and therefore this late stage DNA damage marker is also evaluated in this manuscript. All single treatments showed a decrease in cell viability in a dose-dependent manner. Olaparib at 1µM showed a cell viability of 60% (Fig.  A), treatment with [131I]-GMIB-2Rs15d at 1 MBq/mL and 10 MBq/mL reduced viable cells to 84.85 ± 4.18% and 36.54 ± 9.29% respectively (Fig.  B) additionally treatment with [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (10 kBq/mL, 100 kBq/mL, and 200 kBq/mL) measured 74.83 ± 7.64%, 22.46 ± 5.22% and 13.50 ± 4.24% respectively (Fig.  C).
 Cell viability could be further reduced in a combined setting with Olaparib (1µM) and [131I]-GMIB-2Rs15d (1 MBq/mL) or (10 MBq/mL) to 45% and 25% respectively (Fig.  D). Additionally, Olaparib (1µM) and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d yielded about 42% viable cells at 10 kBq/mL and about 20% at 100 and 200 kBq/mL (Fig.  E). An exploratory Chou-Talalay analysis was performed to evaluate the synergistic effect (Supplementary Figure ).

## Long term biodistribution and dosimetry
The biodistributions of [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d in DLD-1 BRCA2−/− tumour bearing mice were assessed up to 192 h p.i. (Figure  A and C; Supplemental Tables and ). Tumour uptake at 1 h p.i. was low but significantly higher for [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (1.47 ± 0.32%IA/g) compared to non-targeting [131I]I-GMIB-R3B23 (0.46 ± 0.099%IA/g, p = 0.0010, N = 4, Fig.  B and Supplemental Table ). Tumour uptake slowly decreased over time with 0.14 ± 0.029%IA/g remaining at 24 h p.i. [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d was quickly cleared through the kidneys, with only ~ 2%IA/g remaining at 6 h p.i. Dosimetry calculations revealed an absorbed dose in the tumour of 2.41 cGy/MBq, with kidneys as the dose-limiting organ, with an absorbed dose of 17.68 cGy/MBq (Table  ).
 Regarding tumour uptake, a similar trend was observed for [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (0.78 ± 0.10%IA/g), with limited but significant tumour uptake compared with that of the non-targeting [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-R3B23 (0.19 ± 0.026%IA/g, p < 0.0001, N = 4, Fig.  D and Supplemental Table ). Clearance also occurred through the kidneys; however, it was more retained over time, with > 2%IA/g remaining after 72 h p.i. (Fig.  C, Supplemental Tables ) The absorbed dose to the tumour was calculated to be 0.48 Gy/100 kBq with the kidney as a dose-limiting organ (10.86 Gy/100 kBq) (Table  ).
 

## Therapeutic effect assessment
The dosing regimens for fractionated therapy of [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d were prolonged to eight repeat injections compared with previous successful dose regimens in high HER2 expressing SKOV3 xenografts [ , ]. Olaparib (75 mg/kg) was administered daily for 28 days (Fig. A). Herein, [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and Olaparib confirmed a synergistic effect over single Olaparib treatment (p’=0.000483), single [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (p’=0.0000171) treatment, and control 0.9% NaCl (p’=0.00000208) (Fig. B). Similarly, [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d and Olaparib showed improved therapeutic efficacy compared to the single agent treatments [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (p’=0.0000465), Olaparib (p’=0.00198), and 0.9% NaCl (p’=0.00000502) (Fig. C). Additionally, single agent treatments [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d (p’=0.00232), [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d (p’=0.0149), and Olaparib (p’=0.0000746) showed significant therapeutic efficacy over vehicle control (Fig. B and C, Supplemental Figure A, C, E, G, I, K and S3 A, B). The therapeutic effect is related to the radionuclides itself, since administration of non-radiolabelled 2Rs15d showed no therapeutic effect in previous studies and only provides the targeting aspect of the RLT [ ].
 The therapeutic regimen was well-tolerated in all treatment groups, showing stable weight progression over the course of the therapy study (Supplemental Figure B, D, F, H, J, L and S3 A, B). The [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d combination group showed for one mouse (1/11) no palpable tumour at animal endpoint (120 days). In the [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and Olaparib combination group, no visible long-term toxicity was observed until the study endpoint at 150 days post-inoculation. Remarkably, at the endpoint, one mouse (1/12) had a tumour volume of only ~ 400 mm³. Notion has to be made indicating this as a single event in both cases and therefore, the conclusions of the therapeutic effect were made based on the averages of the treatment groups.

## Discussion
Low HER2 expression (~ prevalence 30–60%) was previously considered a non-actionable biomarker and was categorised as HER2 negative. However, the promising results of the DESTINY-Breast04 (NCT03734029, N = 557) clinical trial in HER2-low patients have shifted the treatment paradigm [ ]. Therefore, in this study, we investigate HER2 targeting radioligands [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d as single-agent or combination treatments in tumour xenografts expressing low levels of HER2. Promising results were already obtained in patients receiving diagnostic [68Ga]Ga-NOTA-2Rs15d, in which original HER2 negative disease patients could be recategorized as HER2 positive, using full body PET/CT [ ]. Depending on the application, either beta(131I)- or alpha(225Ac)- particle targeted therapy is recommended because of the different physical and radiobiological properties of the radionuclides [ ]. Alpha particles deposit a large amount of energy over a short distance inducing double strand DNA breaks in target cells without hampering surrounding tissues. Beta particles on the other hand deposit lower amounts of energy for further distances, inducing single strand DNA breaks. In a treatment set-up, initial debulking of large tumour masses can be treated with β-RLT since it can irradiate throughout the tumour or with α-RLT to initiate a heavy blow towards the tumour using high energy irradiation. In a later stage α-RLT is a suitable option for a total eradication of micro-metastases [ ].
Therefore, in this study, both radionuclides were evaluated for their therapeutic efficacy and toxicity. As a combination strategy, a PARP inhibitor, Olaparib, was selected, promoting inadequate repair of DNA damage and, therefore, synthetic lethality. Since Olaparib is FDA-approved for patients with BRCA-mutated tumours, we selected a similar tumour model, namely DLD-1 BRCA2−/− [ ]. BRCA1/2 mutation carriers have a low prevalence of conventional HER2 overexpressing cancers (approximately 9% in breast cancer), making our proposed dosing schedule strictly limited to a subset of patients [ ]. However, a recent investigation showed that in BRCA1/2 mutated patients, up to 47% have HER2 low-expressing cancers, which increases the therapeutic target group [ ].
Resistance to PARP inhibitor monotherapy is a major drawback to their continuous and successful use in the clinic and is argued to be related to three main mechanisms: restoration of replication fork stability, drug target-related resistance, and restoration of homologous recombination [ ]. Therefore, research is ongoing to explore combination strategies (for example RLT) to circumvent or address this resistance. Remarkably, since the main pathway of radioresistance is an increase in DNA repair mechanisms, PARP inhibitors can also assist in their turn by reducing radiotherapy resistance [ ]. The radiosensitization effect of PARP inhibition is expected to be higher in cancers with DNA repair mutated pathways, as explored in this manuscript; however, a synergistic effect of radiation with inhibitors of the PARP pathway was also observed in non-mutated cancers [ ]. The majority of these studies have been focussed on external beam radiation therapy (EBRT) and extrapolation to RLT, which consist of different dose rates, radiobiological effects and duration of radiation exposure, will require additional investigations to fully understand the mechanisms of action.
Combination strategies were tested in vitro for their potential using a two-step approach: testing an early DNA damage biomarker such as γH2AX and a final stage marker like cell death/viability. In both assays, the effect could be driven by Olaparib or [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d / [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d alone when concentrations were high enough. Only at suboptimal concentrations for single treatments of both drugs, a combination effect was obtained. γH2AX staining and ATP cell viability assessment were both selected based on their high selectivity and throughput screening possibilities. Unfortunately, no information could be drawn from either assays in selecting a desired concentration for in vivo studies, since complex mechanisms such as pharmacokinetics and tumour targeting come into play.
[131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d showed therapeutic efficacy as single agent administration and synergistic effect in combination with Olaparib in low HER2 expressing DLD-1 BRCA2−/− xenografts. The cumulative dose for [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d in this study was 296 MBq, resulting in an absorbed dose of 7.13 Gy to the tumour, with the kidneys as a dose-limiting organ receiving 52 Gy. The generally accepted threshold for kidney toxicity using EBRT is 23 Gy, which is half the calculated absorbed dose in our design [ ]. It is important to note that distinct differences exist between RLT and EBRT, such as lower energy over a longer period and heterogeneous distribution at the target sites. Furthermore, fractionation of the therapeutic regimen reduces toxicity [ ]. Recent clinical data using [131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d assessed a dose of up to 5550 MBq, which was considered safe [ ]. In addition, rodent studies evaluating a dosing regimen of 10 × 37 MBq of the highly similar Folate receptor α-targeting sdAb, [131I]I-GMIB-2BD42, revealed no signs of toxicity [ ].
The alpha-emitting counterpart, [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d, also showed prolonged survival with single dosing and further invigoration in combination with Olaparib in low HER2 expressing DLD-1 BRCA2−/− xenografts. The absorbed dose to the tumour was 0.77 Gy, with the kidneys as the dose-limiting organ receiving 17.38 Gy. Although we are below the aforementioned toxicity threshold of 23 Gy, in studies using 225Ac, a relative biological effectiveness of 5 is often used [ ]. Indeed, kidney toxicity was previously observed for other radiometal-labelled sdAbs at later time points [ ]. Future experiments could explore lower doses of [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d to reduce toxicity while maintaining a synergistic effect, however in doing so a dose titration study should be performed of both compounds. Recently, new approaches such as pre-targeting ([177Lu]Lu-DOTA-PEG7-tetrazine and 4AH29-TCO), using a two-step in vivo click reaction, have been successfully explored to reduce kidney retention and toxicity while maintaining therapeutic effects [ ]. This approach is of particular interest for 2Rs15d since we have previously shown that 2Rs15d does not actively trigger receptor-mediated internalization [ ].
Previously, combination strategies exploring PARP inhibitors were mainly focused on EBRT, where most insights were gained, but recently, a shift towards RLT as a partner in crime occurred. Other studies have also shown promising results using combinations of PARP inhibitors and RLT with a multitude of different biovectors and radioisotopes, such as [227Th]Th-HOPO-trastuzumab or [177Lu]Lu-DOTA-PSMA-617 [ , ]. New selective PARP1/2 inhibitors such as niraparib have also emerged which showed efficacy in patients with and without BRCA mutations. These characteristics might be of interest to explore in combination strategies beyond this proof of concept study [ , ]. DNA PKi (peposertib) emerged as a new promising DDR inhibitor, which showed an increase in complete response subjects for combination strategies over single treatments in models targeting CAIX and PSMA [ ]. The number of studies in this field continues to increase; however, key findings in elucidating the combination mechanisms, or radiobiology, remain absent, which is crucial for unlocking the full potential of this approach [ ].

## Conclusion
[131I]I-GMIB-2Rs15d and [225Ac]Ac-DOTA-2Rs15d showed significant tumour targeting in tumour xenografts expressing low levels of HER2, which translated to prolonged survival in a therapeutic setting. Furthermore, a synergistic effect was demonstrated by exploring the combination strategies with Olaparib, increasing the therapeutic window. This manuscript reports a new proof-of-concept strategy to increase synthetic lethality and to assist in tailored patient care.
