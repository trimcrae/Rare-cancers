## Highlights
•FBXW7–185aa is recruited to DNA damage sites in an ATM-dependent manner.
•FBXW7–185aa modulates non-homologous end joining repair efficiency in glioblastoma cells.
•FBXW7–185aa influences cellular radiosensitivity through regulation of DNA repair.
•FBXW7–185aa, by fine-tuning NHEJ, may serve as a molecular switch influencing the immunogenicity of radiotherapy in glioblastoma.
•circ-FBXW7 encodes a functional protein involved in the DNA damage response.
Keywords: FBXW7-185aa, Circ-FBXW7, Glioblastoma, Non-homologous end joining, DNA damage response, Radiosensitivity, Tumor immune microenvironment

## Abstract
DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are among the most severe forms of DNA damage, and their cellular consequences depend on the efficiency of DNA repair pathways. Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) provides a rapid mechanism to reconnect broken DNA strands and is a major pathway for maintaining genomic stability. FBXW7α is the substrate-selective unit within the SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, imparting specificity by recruiting proteins destined for ubiquitin-mediated turnover. It has recently been reported to mediate XRCC4 ubiquitylation during DNA damage, and is a recognized tumor suppressor, thereby promoting NHEJ. A circular RNA derived from FBXW7 gene exons 3 and 4 (circ-FBXW7) has been identified. This circRNA encodes a 185-amino acid protein, named FBXW7-185aa, which contains a spanning-junction open reading frame (ORF). We found that compared with FBXW7α, FBXW7-185aa was recruited more slowly to DNA damage sites in glioblastoma (GBM) cells. Functionally, FBXW7-185aa may enhance NHEJ repair by facilitating the removal of FBXW7α from DNA damage lesions, thereby promoting the subsequent steps of DNA repair. Efficient DNA repair can enable tumor cells to survive genotoxic stressors like radiotherapy. Radiotherapy is also known to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment. Therefore, our findings suggest that FBXW7-185aa may represent a potential molecular nexus between DNA damage response and tumor–immune interactions. Moreover, depletion of FBXW7–185aa was associated with increased sensitivity of GBM cells to pevonedistat, a drug that inhibits the ubiquitin-like modification of the protein NEDD8, when combined with radiation. Together, our results indicate that FBXW7-185aa acts as a fine-tuning regulator of NHEJ through its interaction with FBXW7α, adding a new layer to the complexity of FBXW7 gene-derived proteins in DNA repair. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for exploring how fine-tuned DNA repair dynamics may influence GBM radiosensitivity and potentially impact tumor immunogenicity, although direct immunological effects remain to be determined.

## Introduction
In adults, glioblastoma (GBM) is the most malignant primary brain tumor, and current treatment modalities still achieve only limited success. Its rapid proliferation, diffuse infiltration into surrounding brain tissue, and broad resistance to therapy contribute to its dismal prognosis. Despite extensive resection combined with radiation and temozolomide (TMZ), the expected median survival for individuals with this disease remains approximately 15 months, and fewer than 10 % of patients survive beyond 5 years [ ]. One major challenge in improving outcomes is that GBM cells possess intrinsic resistance and often further acquire adaptive resistance to DNA-damaging treatments, particularly radiation.
Radiotherapy remains a central component of GBM treatment because of its capacity to introduce lethal DNA lesions in tumor cells [ , , ]. Yet its benefit is frequently limited by the robust DNA damage response (DDR) and repair programs active in GBM [ , ]. Accordingly, disrupting DNA repair represents an attractive approach to enhance radiosensitivity and improve clinical response. A deeper understanding of how GBM cells regulate DNA repair at the molecular level may open new therapeutic avenues.
Characterized by its F-box and WD-repeat domains, FBXW7 plays a pivotal tumor-suppressive role by providing substrate specificity to the SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. FBXW7 drives the proteasomal turnover of several oncogenic substrates, such as Cyclin E, c-Myc, Notch, and mTOR [ ], and is frequently inactivated in a variety of cancers. Recent studies have revealed that FBXW7 is involved not only in protein degradation but also in genome maintenance and DNA damage responses. Specifically, Zhang et al. identified that FBXW7α, the nuclear isoform of FBXW7, promotes DNA double-strand break repair by mediating lysine 63 (K63)-linked polyubiquitylation of XRCC4, thereby supporting efficient NHEJ [ ]. NHEJ is initiated by the recruitment of the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer to DNA double-strand break sites, followed by the sequential assembly of core repair components, including XRCC4, DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs), XRCC4/DNA ligase IV (Lig4), XLF, and PAXX, to facilitate DNA end ligation and repair [ , ]. By binding the poly(ADP-ribose) chains synthesized at DNA damage sites, the WD40 domain of FBXW7α enables the protein to be swiftly recruited to the lesion, while serine 26 (Ser26) of the FBXW7α isoform is phosphorylated by ataxia telangiectasia mutated (ATM) kinase, further promoting its retention [ ]. In addition, FBXW7 modulates the stability of FAAP20, which participates in the Fanconi anemia DNA repair cascade, for degradation, thereby helping regulate the turnover of the FA core complex during interstrand cross-link repair [ ]. Our group recently found that loss of FBXW7 results in elevated expression of isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) in glioma cells by preventing the degradation of SREBP1. This upregulation disrupts NADPH homeostasis, and impairs DNA repair mechanisms, thereby sensitizing IDH1-mutant glioma cells to radiation therapy [ ].
Beyond the three major isoforms of FBXW7, including FBXW7α, FBXW7β, and FBXW7γ, which are generated via alternative splicing and have distinct N-terminal exons, recent studies have uncovered a previously unannotated short isoform of FBXW7, termed FBXW7-185aa, which is encoded by circ-FBXW7, a circular RNA created through FBXW7 exons 3 and 4 [ ]. In GBM cells, increased FBXW7-185aa slows the cell cycle and suppresses proliferation, partly by decreasing c-Myc stability [ ]. Circ-FBXW7, a circular RNA derived from the FBXW7 gene, has been shown to hinder the malignant progression of triple-negative breast cancer by repressing miR-197–3p and promoting FBXW7 upregulation [ ]. However, its role in cancer treatment, particularly in response to genotoxic stress, remains largely unexplored [ , ]. Given the shared protein sequences between FBXW7-185aa and the N-terminus of FBXW7α (167 residues), FBXW7-185aa represents an intriguing candidate for investigation in the context of DNA repair and radiotherapy resistance.
Here, we demonstrated that FBXW7-185aa localizes to DNA damage sites more slowly than FBXW7α. Functionally, FBXW7-185aa enhances NHEJ repair by facilitating the removal of FBXW7α from DNA damage sites, promoting later steps of DNA ligation. Furthermore, knockdown of FBXW7-185aa enhances the radiosensitization effects of protein neddylation inhibitor pevonedistat (MLN4924). In this study, we aimed to investigate whether FBXW7-185aa serves as a potential regulatory link connecting DNA damage repair, radiotherapy resistance, and modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment in glioblastoma. Given that radiotherapy is not only a cytotoxic agent but also a potent modulator of the tumor immune microenvironment, fine-tuning of DNA damage repair mechanisms may represent an important interface between tumor cell survival and anti-tumor immune responses, with FBXW7-185aa serving as a candidate factor for investigation. Elucidating this regulation may thus provide novel insights into the mechanisms of radioresistance and offer a speculative framework for understanding how DNA repair dynamics could potentially influence the tumor immune microenvironment, revealing potential targets for synergistic radio-immunotherapy strategies.

## Results

## FBXW7-185aa accumulates at DNA damage sites and its recruitment depends on ATM phosphorylation at Ser26
ATM phosphorylates the nuclear isoform FBXW7α at Ser26, enabling its recruitment to DNA damage foci [ ]. Given that the circ-FBXW7 encoded protein FBXW7-185aa and FBXW7α shares the same N-terminal 167 amino acids, including Ser26 (Figure S1A), we investigated whether FBXW7-185aa relocates to DNA lesions. To explore its recruitment kinetics, we transfected T98G GBM cells with GFP-FBXW7α or GFP-FBXW7-185aa and exposed the cells to laser micro-irradiation (micro-IR), with mCherry-Ku80 as a positive control. GFP-FBXW7α rapidly accumulated at damage sites within 1 min after treatment, with signal intensity increasing over time ( A and B). Interestingly, FBXW7-185aa showed delayed recruitment, appearing at damage sites at approximately 7.5–10 min post-irradiation ( A and B). Since FBXW7α recruitment is mediated by ATM phosphorylation at Ser26, we examined whether FBXW7-185aa is also an ATM substrate. Immunoprecipitation followed by immunoblotting using a p-S/TQ antibody (specific for ATM-/ATR-/DNA-PKcs-mediated phosphorylation sites) showed that FBXW7-185aa was phosphorylated after radiation, while ATM inhibition with KU55933, but not ATR or DNA-PKcs inhibition, significantly reduced this phosphorylation ( C), supporting the notion that FBXW7-185aa and FBXW7α share the same ATM phosphorylation motif [ ]. In line, KU55933 completely blocked GFP-FBXW7-185aa recruitment to damage sites ( D), indicating that ATM-mediated phosphorylation at Ser26 is required for its localization. To confirm the critical role of Ser26, we generated an FBXW7-185aa S26A mutant. This mutant showed significantly reduced phosphorylation and failed to relocate to the damage sites after micro-IR treatment ( E, F, and Figure S1B). Overall, these findings indicate that FBXW7-185aa is recruited to DNA damage sites in an ATM- and Ser26-dependent manner, supporting a potential role in the DNA damage response.
 

## Knockdown of FBXW7-185aa enhances GBM cell radiosensitization
We next examined the impact of FBXW7-185aa deficiency on the radiation survival of GBM cells. As shown in A, both siFBXW7α and siFBXW7-185aa effectively knocked down their respective targets. Clonogenic survival assays in U87 and T98G cells indicated that loss of FBXW7-185aa markedly increased radiosensitivity, comparable to the effect observed upon FBXW7α knockdown ( A and S2A). Notably, silencing FBXW7-185aa did not further enhance the radiosensitization triggered by FBXW7α knockdown, indicating a likely epistatic interaction between the two proteins ( A and S2A). To investigate whether reduced DNA repair capacity underlies this effect, we next quantified γH2AX levels by flow cytometry. Similar to the effect of FBXW7α silencing, we observed persistently elevated γH2AX levels in both U87 and T98G cells following FBXW7-185aa depletion ( B and S2B). In line with this observation, depletion of FBXW7-185aa in U87 cells led to significantly higher γH2AX levels at 1 and 2 hrs post-irradiation in the immunofluorescence analysis ( C and
 D). These findings suggest that circ-FBXW7 RNA-encoded FBXW7-185aa may play a positive regulatory role in DNA repair and could be explored as a target for modulating the radiation response in GBM cells.
 

## FBXW7-185aa facilitates NHEJ but not HR repair in GBM cells
Among the mechanisms that safeguard genomic stability, NHEJ and homologous recombination (HR) constitute the major strategies used by cells to mend DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) [ ]. To evaluate how FBXW7-185aa influences DNA repair pathways in GBM cells, we first examined its effect on NHEJ given that FBXW7α has been shown to promote NHEJ repair. U87 and T98G cells harboring the pEJ5-GFP NHEJ reporter were silenced for either FBXW7-185aa or FBXW7α, and GFP expression was detected by flow cytometry following I-SceI adenovirus infection. As expected, knockdown of FBXW7α impaired NHEJ repair, consistent with previous reports [ ]. Importantly, depletion of FBXW7-185aa also significantly inhibited NHEJ repair in both GBM cell lines. Knockdown of FBXW7-185aa or FBXW7α significantly impaired NHEJ activity, with no significant difference between the two conditions ( A,
 B, and S3A). To confirm this observation, we used a linearized pEYFP plasmid re-ligation assay to measure NHEJ repair in U87 and T98G cells [ ]. Again, depletion of FBXW7-185aa decreased plasmid re-ligation to a similar extent as FBXW7α silencing ( C and S3B). In contrast, using the DR-GFP reporter in U87 and T98G cells, no significant impacts of FBXW7-185aa or FBXW7α on HR repair were observed ( D,
 E, and S3C). Thus, these findings indicate that circ-FBXW7-encoded FBXW7-185aa promotes NHEJ repair.
 

## FBXW7-185aa binds to XRCC4/Lig4 complex and attenuates FBXW7α-mediated polyubiquitylation of XRCC4
Given that FBXW7α interacts with the XRCC4/Lig4 complex, a central component of the NHEJ repair pathway [ ], we first used immunoprecipitation to examine whether FBXW7-185aa also binds to the XRCC4/Lig4 complex. As expected, FBXW7-185aa was found to interact with both XRCC4 and Lig4 following radiation treatment at the indicated time points ( A), supporting its involvement in NHEJ repair. Furthermore, these results suggest that the N-terminal region of FBXW7α, which shares an amino acid sequence with FBXW7-185aa, mediates binding to XRCC4/Lig4, while the C-terminal WD40 domain of FBXW7α plays an essential role in promoting XRCC4 polyubiquitylation. Subsequently, we investigated the potential competitive interaction between FBXW7α and FBXW7-185aa for the binding of XRCC4/Lig4 complex. As anticipated, overexpression of FBXW7-185aa significantly reduced the interaction between FBXW7α and XRCC4, whereas the S26A mutant of FBXW7–185aa had little to no effect ( B). Conversely, silencing FBXW7-185aa enhanced the association between FBXW7α and XRCC4 ( C). These findings suggest that FBXW7-185aa and FBXW7α competitively interact with the XRCC4/Lig4 complex in a Ser26 phosphorylation-dependent manner.
 FBXW7α, the major isoform encoded by the FBXW7 gene, mediates K63-linked polyubiquitylation of XRCC4 as an E3 ubiquitin ligase [ ]. However, as FBXW7-185aa lacks the F-box (which connects to SKP1-Cullin1), dimerization domain (DD), and the conserved WD40 domain, it is unable to catalyze polyubiquitylation of XRCC4. Given the competitive binding between FBXW7-185aa and FBXW7α, we next studied the effect of FBXW7-185aa on FBXW7α-mediated XRCC4 polyubiquitylation. In vivo ubiquitylation assays revealed a marked increase in XRCC4 polyubiquitylation upon FBXW7α overexpression following radiation treatment ( D), while silencing FBXW7-185aa led to increased levels of polyubiquitylated XRCC4 compared to control siRNA, confirming the negative regulatory role of FBXW7-185aa in FBXW7α-catalyzed XRCC4 polyubiquitylation ( D). Overall, these results suggest that FBXW7-185aa competes with FBXW7α for binding to XRCC4 and restricts the polyubiquitylation of XRCC4. Despite this antagonistic effect, both FBXW7-185aa and FBXW7α facilitate NHEJ repair. We hypothesize that FBXW7-185aa may either promote the termination of FBXW7α-mediated XRCC4 polyubiquitylation or prevent excessive ubiquitylation of XRCC4 during NHEJ repair, thereby facilitating the later stages of DNA end-joining.

## Depletion of FBXW7-185aa enhances pevonedistat-induced radiosensitization in GBM cells
As a pharmacological inhibitor of the NEDD8-activating enzyme, pevonedistat (MLN4924) has advanced to phase II/III stages of clinical assessment, it blocks Cullin protein neddylation and inhibits Cullin-RING ligase activity [ , , ]. Pevonedistat has been validated to inhibit both NHEJ and HR during DNA repair, making it a potent radiosensitizer currently under investigation for use in treating malignancies [ , ]. In the context of NHEJ repair, pevonedistat disrupts the Cullin1-SKP1-FBXW7 E3 complex, thus eliminating FBXW7-mediated K63-linked polyubiquitylation of XRCC4. We hypothesized that, upon pevonedistat treatment, "non-functional" FBXW7 in DNA lesions could not be removed in the absence of FBXW7-185aa, further impairing NHEJ repair and increasing radiation sensitivity.
Therefore, we used the pEJ5-GFP NHEJ reporter assay to examine whether silencing FBXW7-185aa exacerbates NHEJ defects when combined with pevonedistat treatment. As expected, both FBXW7-185aa-depleted GBM cells and cells treated with pevonedistat exhibited impaired NHEJ. Importantly, the combination of FBXW7-185aa knockdown and pevonedistat treatment further dampened NHEJ repair ( A and S4A). To evaluate NHEJ capacity, we performed a linearized plasmid end-joining assay in GBM cells treated with FBXW7-185aa siRNA and pevonedistat. Cells receiving the combination showed a marked reduction in NHEJ efficiency compared with either treatment alone ( B and S4B), indicating that loss of FBXW7-185aa cooperates with pevonedistat to limit NHEJ repair. In line with this, clonogenic survival assays demonstrated that FBXW7-185aa knockdown and pevonedistat each enhanced radiosensitivity in U87 and T98G cells, with the combined treatment producing a pronounced sensitization effect ( C and
 D). These data suggest that FBXW7-185aa, or its upstream circ-FBXW7 transcript, may serve as a molecular determinant of pevonedistat-mediated radiosensitivity in GBM.
 

## Discussion
Beyond its recognized tumor-suppressive functions, FBXW7 also plays a direct role in the DNA damage response, particularly in supporting NHEJ repair. The SCF-FBXW7α complex promotes NHEJ by catalyzing K63-linked polyubiquitylation of XRCC4, thereby stabilizing its connection with the Ku70/Ku80 complex [ ]. Subsequent work showed that lysine demethylase 1A (LSD1) destabilizes FBXW7 through a mechanism independent of its demethylase activity, ultimately dampening FBXW7-mediated growth inhibition and NHEJ repair [ ]. In addition, hyperactivation of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase 1(PARP1), triggered by DNA damage or hyperglycemia, impairs FBXW7 function and aggravates endothelial injury [ ]. More recently, we reported that FBXW7 loss elevates IDH expression and tumor-derived metabolites such as 2-hydroxyglutarate, a metabolic shift that enhances oxidative stress tolerance and increases tumor radiosensitivity [ ]. Together, these findings highlight FBXW7 as a critical regulator of DNA repair networks and radiation response in cancer.
Given FBXW7’s established role in DNA repair, we investigated whether FBXW7-185aa, the small protein translated from circFBXW7, also contributes to the DNA damage response. Our findings suggest a previously unrecognized role of FBXW7-185aa in contributing to radioresistance in GBM by supporting NHEJ repair. Loss of FBXW7-185aa markedly sensitized GBM cells to ionizing radiation and was accompanied by sustained γH2AX accumulation after irradiation, consistent with defective DNA damage resolution. Importantly, FBXW7-185aa depletion did not further increase the radiosensitization observed in FBXW7α-deficient cells, suggesting an epistatic interaction between the two proteins. Overall, these findings support a role for FBXW7–185aa as a functional factor in the DNA damage response and suggest that its inhibition may enhance the therapeutic efficacy of radiotherapy in GBM.
Mechanistically, we find that FBXW7-185aa enhances NHEJ repair without affecting HR, aligning with previous work showing that FBXW7α promotes NHEJ fidelity through its interaction with XRCC4. Our results show that FBXW7-185aa associates with the XRCC4/Lig4 complex and attenuates FBXW7α-mediated polyubiquitylation of XRCC4 in a Ser26 phosphorylation-dependent manner. Although FBXW7-185aa lacks the F-box and WD40 domains required for E3 ligase activity, it appears to modulate FBXW7α function, likely preventing excessive or prolonged XRCC4 ubiquitylation during later phases of NHEJ. This competitive interface may help fine-tune the timing and completion of repair, providing an additional layer of post-translational regulation that merits further structural and temporal investigation.
Importantly, our findings have therapeutic implications, particularly in the context of combining FBXW7-185aa depletion with pharmacologic inhibition of the NEDD8 pathway. Targeting the NEDD8-activating enzyme, pevonedistat compromises Cullin-RING ligase complex activity and perturbs DNA repair pathways, including those reliant on FBXW7-catalyzed XRCC4 polyubiquitylation [ ]. We show that loss of FBXW7-185aa cooperates with pevonedistat to suppress NHEJ and markedly enhance radiosensitivity in GBM cells. This combined strategy may accentuate DNA repair defects and promote tumor cell killing, particularly in GBM subsets with elevated circFBXW7 or FBXW7-185aa expression. In this regard, FBXW7-185aa appears to function as a regulator of NHEJ and may help identify GBM cells with heightened sensitivity to radiation, offering a new entry point for therapeutic intervention. Future work should investigate the translational feasibility of targeting the circ-FBXW7-FBXW7-185aa axis together with DNA repair inhibitors in vivo and across broader glioma models. Overall, our results uncover an unexpected role for FBXW7-185aa in modulating NHEJ via XRCC4 and highlight opportunities for therapeutic intervention in GBM.
In addition to its direct cytotoxic effects, radiotherapy has emerged as a potent modulator of the tumor immune microenvironment [ , ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that radiation-induced DNA double-strand breaks can promote immunogenic cell death, enhance tumor antigen release, and activate innate immune sensing pathways such as cGAS–STING, ultimately facilitating anti-tumor immune responses [ , , ]. However, the magnitude and durability of these immune effects are highly dependent on the efficiency of DNA damage repair within tumor cells [ , ]. Rapid and effective repair of radiation-induced DSBs has been shown to limit cytosolic DNA accumulation, dampen STING signaling, and reduce immune activation following radiotherapy [ , , ].
In this context, our identification of FBXW7-185aa as a fine-tuning regulator of NHEJ repair provides a potential mechanistic link between tumor-intrinsic DNA repair capacity and radiotherapy-induced immune modulation. By facilitating efficient resolution of radiation-induced DSBs through modulation of XRCC4 ubiquitylation dynamics, FBXW7-185aa may enable GBM cells to more rapidly restore genomic integrity after irradiation. Such efficient DNA repair could, in principle, limit the persistence of unrepaired DNA fragments or micronuclei formation, thereby restraining activation of innate immune sensing pathways [ , ]. Although direct immunological evidence is currently lacking, this framework is consistent with emerging studies demonstrating that enhanced DNA repair capacity correlates with attenuated radiotherapy-driven immune activation.
Importantly, we acknowledge that the immunomodulatory implications of FBXW7-185aa proposed here remain speculative and extend beyond the experimental scope of the present study. No immune profiling or in vivo immune analyses were performed, and future studies using immunocompetent glioma models will be required to directly assess how modulation of FBXW7-185aa affects immune cell infiltration, cytokine signaling, and response to immune checkpoint blockade [ ]. Nevertheless, our findings provide a conceptual framework linking fine-tuned NHEJ repair to radiotherapy response and immune modulation, and suggest that targeting the circ-FBXW7-FBXW7-185aa axis may represent a rational strategy for enhancing the efficacy of combinatorial radiotherapy-based immunotherapies in GBM.

## Materials and methods

## Immunofluorescence
Cells seeded onto glass coverslips in 12-well plates received the appropriate treatments prior to fixation for immunofluorescence. The fixed samples were subsequently probed with antibodies against γH2AX and 53BP1 (Millipore), followed by nuclear staining with DAPI. An Olympus IX71 FluoView confocal microscope with a 60 × oil-immersion lens was used to capture fluorescence images. Imaging fields were chosen using DAPI to locate nuclei, and γH2AX foci quantification was based on scoring ≥100 nuclei per group.

## Micro-irradiation
Cells plated in MatTek glass-bottom dishes were transfected with pEGFP-FBXW7α or pEGFP-FBXW7–185aa (WT or S26A). After GFP became detectable, localized UVA stripes were generated using a 365-nm laser through a 60 × /1.2-W objective on a Zeiss Axiovert/LSM 520 Meta microscope, producing an approximately 12 × 0.1 mm (4.36 J/m²) damage region. Time-lapse imaging was conducted at 1-minute intervals.

## Ubiquitylation assays
The in vivo ubiquitylation assay was performed as described previously. After a 48-hour expression period of the respective plasmid constructs, U87 cells were harvested and lysed in extraction buffer. The resulting lysates were incubated with HA-conjugated beads for 3 h, followed by extensive washing and immunoblotting to assess XRCC4 polyubiquitylation.

## Linearized plasmid end-joining assay
For the assay, pEYFP-N1 (Clontech) was linearized using NheI, creating a break positioned between the EYFP promoter and coding region. The linearised DNA was purified from agarose gels and introduced into cells that had been serum-deprived overnight. Twelve hours after transfection, cells were collected and lysed to recover the plasmid DNA. qPCR signals from the rejoined EYFP junction were normalized to a local uncut region and compared with those from DMSO- or control siRNA-treated groups to determine end-joining efficiency.

## NHEJ I-scei reporter assay
Cells stably carrying the pEJ5 NHEJ reporter were plated in 12-well dishes and exposed to the indicated siRNAs or drug treatments. The cultures were then infected with an I-SceI–expressing adenovirus to induce a site-specific double-strand break. After 18 h, the medium containing the drugs was refreshed to minimise viral cytotoxicity. Twenty-four hours later, cells were collected, and NHEJ activity was determined by measuring the proportion of GFP-positive cells using flow cytometry.

## Clonogenic formation assay
Cells treated with siFBXW7–185aa and/or pevonedistat were subjected to radiation (0, 2, 4, 6, 8 Gy) as appropriate. After 24 h post-RT, cells were seeded at low density in 6-well plates and allowed to incubate for 10–14 days under standard culture conditions to allow colony formation. Colonies were then fixed with methanol and stained with 0.5 % crystal violet. Only colonies containing more than 50 cells were counted as survivors. Plating efficiency and surviving fraction were calculated relative to untreated controls. Each condition was tested in triplicate, and experiments were independently repeated at least three times. Radiation enhancement ratio (RER) > 1 indicates radiosensitization.

## Statistics
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 5. Data are presented as mean ± SD from at least three independent experiments. Differences between two groups were assessed using two-tailed Student’s t-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
