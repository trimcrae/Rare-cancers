## Highlights
•Overexpression of TNC is an independent prognostic factor for poor prognosis of ESCC patients.
•The novel nomogram established in the present study might be simple and easy to apply for assisting clinicians in more accurate prognoses prediction of ESCC patients.
•Diminishing over-expressed TNC inhibited ESCC cell proliferation and tumor growth effectively both in vitro and in vivo.
•Reducing TNC expression enhanced the sensitivity of ESCC cells to inhibitors of EGFR and downstream signaling pathways, providing a novel combination therapy strategy.

## Purpose
To establish a prognostic model of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients based on tenascin-C (TNC) expression level and clinicopathological characteristics, and to explore the therapeutic potential of TNC inhibition.

## Methods
The expression of TNC was detected using immunohistochemistry (IHC) in 326 ESCC specimens and 50 normal esophageal tissues. Prognostic factors were determined by Cox regression analyses and were incorporated to establish the nomogram. The effects of TNC knockdown on ESCC cells were assessed in vitro and in vivo. Transcriptome sequencing (RNA-seq) and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed to reveal signaling pathways regulated by TNC knockdown. The therapeutic significance of TNC knockdown combined with small-molecule inhibitors on cell proliferation was examined.

## Results
TNC protein was highly expressed in 48.77 % of ESCC tissues compared to only 2 % in normal esophageal epithelia (p < 0.001). The established nomogram model, based on TNC expression, pT stage, and lymph node metastasis, showed good performance on prognosis evaluation. More importantly, the reduction of TNC expression inhibited tumor cell proliferation and xenograft growth, and mainly down-regulated signaling pathways involved in tumor growth, hypoxia signaling transduction, metabolism, infection, etc. Knockdown of TNC enhanced the inhibitory effect of inhibitors targeting ErbB, PI3K-Akt, Ras and MAPK signaling pathways.

## Conclusion
The established nomogram may be a promising model for survival prediction in ESCC. Reducing TNC expression enhanced the sensitivity of ESCC cells to inhibitors of Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) and downstream signaling pathways, providing a novel combination therapy strategy.

## Introduction
Esophageal cancer seriously threatens human health worldwide. According to the report presented in GLOBOCAN 2020, esophageal cancer ranks as the tenth most prevalent cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer-associated death . In global esophageal cancer cases, 84.98 % are squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), and 15.02 % are adenocarcinoma (EAC) . Despite advances in multimodal treatment consisting of chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy and/or surgery, the overall five-year survival rate for ESCC patients remains 20 %∼30 % [ , ]. Therefore, it is crucial for stratifying patients at high risk of death and providing appropriate treatments .
The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system is widely used to stratify tumors, provide treatment guidance, and predict prognosis. Real-world studies reveal that patients with the same TNM stage present different prognostic outcomes , , . Prognostic models incorporating clinical information, pathological features and molecular changes may be more accurate in disease risk assessment than TNM staging systems alone .
It has been reported that tenascin-C (TNC) is upregulated in ESCC and is associated with tumor metastasis and poor prognosis in ESCC patients . TNC is a protein that is spatiotemporally expressed in embryos. After birth, it is mainly expressed in tissues that are subject to tension, such as tendons and smooth muscles, as well as in tissues with rapid cell renewal, such as bone marrow, lymphoid organs, and hair follicles , , , . Significant de novo expression of TNC is regarded as a hallmark of injury, reconstruction, and cancer , , . High expression of TNC is associated with vasculogenic mimicry in glioma , promoting metastasis in osteosarcoma and breast cancer , , , and poor prognosis in gastric [ , ], colorectal [ , ] and liver cancer .
The aim of this study is to evaluate the prognostic significance of TNC expression in a larger group of ESCC patients. We intend to develop a nomogram that integrates TNC expression and clinicopathological characteristics to accurately predict prognosis. Additionally, we focus on whether reducing TNC expression contributes to inhibiting tumor growth, highlighting the potential of TNC as a therapeutic target for ESCC treatment.

## Materials and methods

## Patients and specimens
A total of 326 surgically removed ESCC tissues and 50 normal esophageal tissues from operative margins were obtained from Linzhou, Henan Province, China. The patients included in the analyses met the following criteria: (1) pathologically diagnosed with primary esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; (2) underwent neither neoadjuvant chemotherapy nor radiotherapy prior to surgery; (3) underwent radical resection of the tumor; (4) death during follow-up was only associated with ESCC recurrence or metastasis. The information of gender, age, pathological type, tumor stage (pT stage), differentiation level, and lymph node metastasis were collected from the medical records. The overall survival (OS) of patients, defined as the time between the diagnosis and the date of death or being censored, was obtained through telephone follow-ups.
All specimens used in this study were residual tissues collected after diagnosis sampling. All patients signed separate informed consent forms for sampling and molecular analyses. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, CAMS & PUMC (No. 16-171/1250).

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated. The antigens were retrieved in Ethylene Diamine Tetraacetic Acid (EDTA, pH 9.0, #ZLI-9069, ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) buffer under high pressure. The primary and secondary antibodies were sequentially incubated after inactivation of endogenous peroxidase by hydrogen peroxide. TNC antibody (#NBP3-07202, Novus Biologicals, Centennial, USA) was used at a 1:200 dilution. The expression of TNC was detected by 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) staining (#PV9000, ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) and analyzed with NDP.view.2 software (Hamamatsu Photonics, Hamamatsu, Japan).
The expression of TNC was doubly evaluated by two independent investigators separately taking signal intensity and the immunostaining area into account . The signal intensity was stratified as follows: 0 represents no expression, and 1, 2 and 3 represent weak, moderate and strong staining, respectively. Eventually, the mean value for each sample was calculated as the final score, and the cutoff value for high or low expression was determined by X-tile software .

## Survival analyses and nomogram establishment
Survival curves were estimated by Kaplan-Meier (KM) method, and differences were compared using the log-rank test. The categorical variables between the training and validation cohorts were compared using the Chi-square test. The clinicopathological characteristics and expression level of TNC were analyzed by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses to determine independent prognostic factors. The nomogram was established to comprehensively assess the survival probability of patients based on these prognostic factors. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, the area under the curve (AUC) value, the calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to evaluate the effectiveness of the nomogram model. Cox regression, nomogram models, and calibration plots were generated by the R package “rms”. The R package “pec” was used for predicting survival probability. ROC and DCA curves were plotted using the R packages “timeROC” and “ggDCA”. Risk score plots, analyzed using the R package “ggrisk”, were used to visualize the distribution differences of prognostic factors between high-risk and low-risk populations identified by the nomogram.
The flow chart depicting the establishment and validation of the nomogram is shown in Fig. S1.

## Cell culture
ESCC cell lines KYSE30 and KYSE450 were obtained from Professor Y. Shimada at the University of Tokyo . The HEK-293T cell line was purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, USA). Cells were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 (RPMI-1640) medium or Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (#12115 and #M1805, XiGong Biological technology, Beijing, China) with 10 % fetal bovine serum (#FBSSA500-S, Ausgenex, Molendinar, Australia) at 37°C in an incubator containing 5 % CO2. Cell lines within tenth passages were used for experiments.

## Transient and stable inhibition of TNC expression in ESCC cells
Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting the human TNC gene (siTNC-1 and siTNC-2) and non-silencing siRNA (siNS) were synthesized by GenePharma Company (#A01001, Suzhou, China). Lentivirus vectors expressing short hairpin RNA (shRNA) against TNC (pLKO.1-shTNC-1 and pLKO.1-shTNC-2) and a non-silencing shRNA (pLKO.1-shNS) were constructed in our lab. Detailed sequences were provided in Table S1.
ESCC cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 105 cells/well and transfected with 100 nmol/L siRNA using a Lipofectamine 2000 kit (#11668019, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, USA). Lentiviruses were produced after transferring the pLKO.1 vectors and helper plasmids into HEK-293T cells and purified by a 0.45 μm filter (#SLHV033RB, Merck, Darmstadt, Germany). KYSE450 and KYSE30 cells (5 × 105 cells) were transfected with purified lentiviruses, and then selected by puromycin (#60209ES10, Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for one week and verified by Western blot.

## Western blot
Cells were collected and lysed by whole cell lysis buffer containing 20 mmol/L 4-(2-Hydroxyethyl) piperazine-1-ethanesulfonic acid (HEPES, pH 8.0, #H3375-100G, Sigma-Aldrich, Beijing, China), 10 mmol/L NaCl (#5-1, Yili Fine Chemicals, Beijing, China), 2 mmol/L MgCl2 (#7-22, Yili Fine Chemicals, Beijing, China), 1 % Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, #2076502, SERVA Electrophoresis GmbH, Heidelberg, Germany) and freshly added protease and phosphatase inhibitors (#B14001 and #B15001, Selleck Chemicals LLC, Houston, USA). Protein concentrations were detected by a BCA protein assay kit (#23227, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, USA). The proteins were loaded into 8 % sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gels for electrophoresis and transferred onto 0.2 μm polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (#ISEQ00005, Merck, Darmstadt, Germany). The membranes were orderly incubated with 5 % milk and primary and secondary antibodies and then detected with ImageQuant LAS 4000 (GE healthcare, Uppsala, Sweden). Antibodies against TNC (#ab108930) and PI3K-p85α (#ab191606) were from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Antibodies against p-EGFR (#3777), EGFR (#4267), p-ERK 1/2 (#4370), ERK 1/2 (#4695), PI3K-110α (#4249), p-Akt (#4060), Akt (#4691), p-mTOR (#5536) and mTOR (#2983) were purchased from Cell Signal Technology (Danvers, USA). β-actin (#60008) antibody was from Proteintech (Rosemont, USA), and horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (#C1308 and #C1309) were from Applygen (Beijing, China).

## Cell proliferation, colony formation and cell cycle detection
After siRNA transfection, the effects of TNC knockdown on cell proliferation, colony formation and cell cycle were detected.
A total of 1 × 103 cells were seeded into 96-well plates for cell proliferation detection. The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; #CK04, Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) was used to measure the absorbance for each plate at the 450 nm wavelength every 24 hours.
Five hundred cells were seeded into 6-well plates and cultured for 1∼2 weeks to form cell colonies approximately 3 mm in diameter. Cell colonies were fixed and stained with 0.05 % w/v crystal violet (#A600331, Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China), 1 % formaldehyde (#19-1, Yili Fine Chemicals, Beijing, China), 1 × PBS (#B1214, Applygen, Beijing, China) and 1 % methanol (#22-13, Yili Fine Chemicals, Beijing, China) solution. The number of colonies was counted.
ESCC cells (5 × 105) were incubated for 48 hours, and then treated by Cell Cycle Assay Kit (#C543, Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) under the manufacturing manual and detected by flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, Warwick, USA).

## Animal experiments
To ensure the animal welfare and the integrity of experimental outcomes, we used four-week-old female BALB/cA nude mice due to their less aggressive nature compared to males. The mice were purchased from Hua Fu Kang company (Beijing, China). After transportation, they were acclimatized for a week in a specific pathogen-free environment at 25°C with 40 %∼70 % humidity and 12-hour light/dark cycles. They were then randomly divided into control and experimental groups (n = 8 per group). The mice were group-housed (4 mice per cage) in individually ventilated cages (32.5 × 21.0 × 13.0 cm) with wooden bedding. They had free access to autoclaved water and standard mice chow (#1035, Hua Fu Kang company, Beijing, China), which contains ≥20 % protein, ≥4 % fat, approximately 50 % carbohydrate, and essential vitamins and minerals. A total of 1 × 106 KYSE450-shTNC cells or control cells were subcutaneously injected into the mice. Tumor sizes were measured every 3 days, and tumor volumes were calculated with the formula of 0.5 × length × width2
 . All the animal experiments were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, CAMS & PUMC (NCC2019A016).

## RNA sequencing (RNA-seq)
RNA-seq was performed to explore differentially expressed genes in KYSE450 cells transiently transfected with siTNC-1 or siNS. Total RNA was extracted by TRIzol reagent (#15596026, Thermo Fisher, Waltham, USA), and the library construction and sequencing were performed at the Beijing Genomics Institution (BGI). Clean data were generated by removing sequencing adapter and low-quality reads (base quality less than or equal to 15 accounted for more than 20 % of the total base number) from raw data. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (GRCh38) by Hierarchical Indexing for Spliced Alignment of Transcripts-2 (HISAT2). Fragments per kilobase million (FPKM) was used to evaluate gene expression. RNA-Seq by Expectation Maximization (RSEM) was used to calculate expression level of gene, and differentially expressed genes was determined by package “DESeq2” with q value ≤ 0.05 (or false discovery rate ≤ 0.001).

## Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)
In order to identify the signaling pathways regulated by TNC expression, GSEA was performed using “clusterProfiler” and “GseaVis” packages. The gene ratio, normalized enrichment score (NES), and p value were used to explore enriched Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways.

## Evaluating the synergistic inhibitory effects of TNC knockdown combined with multiple inhibitors
To simulate the long-term inhibitory impact of the inhibitor on the target, shRNA targeting human TNC was used, and shNS as the control. KYSE450-shTNC and KYSE450-shNS cells, as well as KYSE30-shTNC and KYSE30-shNS cells, were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well. Twelve hours later, 1 μmol/L customized inhibitor arrays (Selleck Chemicals LLC, Houston, USA) were added to the corresponding wells. After 72 hours, the absorbances were measured at the wavelength of 450 nm. The inhibition rates of cell proliferation after TNC knockdown alone and TNC knockdown combined with inhibitors were calculated. The inhibitory effects were evaluated using the Bliss model . In short, the effect of combined treatment was assessed with the following equation: Bliss score=Ecom/(ETNC+Einhibitor−ETNC × Einhibitor), where Ecom was the inhibition rate of combined treatment, ETNC and Einhibitor were the inhibition rate of TNC knockdown and inhibitor application alone, respectively. When Bliss score > 1, the combination is considered to have a synergistic inhibitory effect.

## Statistical analyses
SPSS 21.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, New York, USA) and R 4.1 (R Core Team (2022), Vienna, Austria) were used to perform data analyses. The correlation between TNC and EGFR mRNA levels was analyzed through the GEPIA public website . Curves of cell proliferation and xenograft growth were generated by GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, Boston, USA). Two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Expression of TNC was an independent prognostic factor in ESCC
In the present study, IHC was performed to detect the expression levels of TNC in 326 ESCC and 50 normal samples. The results showed that TNC was highly expressed in 48.77 % (159/326) of ESCC tissues but in only 2 % (1/50) of normal esophageal epithelia (p < 0.001, A). The median overall survival time of patients with high TNC expression (31.4 months, 95 % CI: 26.6-42.1) was significantly shorter than that of patients with low TNC expression ( B). Compared with the assessment of prognosis based solely on pT stage, lymph node metastasis, or tumor size, incorporating TNC expression level can more accurately distinguish the difference in prognoses ( C–E). 
In the whole population of this study, the univariate Cox analysis showed that tumor size with a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.703 (p = 0.001), pT stage (HR = 2.383, p < 0.001), lymph node metastasis (HR = 2.998, p < 0.001) and TNC expression level (HR = 2.815, p < 0.001) were risk factors for the poor survival. The multivariate analysis showed that pT stage (HR = 1.522, p = 0.032), lymph node metastasis (HR = 2.622, p < 0.001) and the expression of TNC (HR = 2.642, p < 0.001) were independent prognostic factors for the OS of the patients (Fig. S2).

## Construction of the prognostic nomogram for ESCC patients
A total of 326 patients were randomly divided into training and validation cohorts at a ratio of 2:1, and the data test between the two sets showed no significant difference (Table S2). The results of Cox regression analyses in the training and validation sets were similar to those in the whole population ( A, B, S2). The nomogram was built based on pT stage, metastatic lymph node status and TNC expression level, which were identified as the main risk factors. The total risk points could be calculated according to predictors on the nomogram ( C). For example, a patient with a local tumor in T3 stage, metastatic lymph node and high expression of TNC, would have a total risk point value of 243.57 and a probability of 1-year survival of 72.70 %, a 3-year survival probability of 21.85 %, and a 5-year survival probability of 13.41 %. 

## Validation of the prognostic ability of the nomogram
The AUC values of 1-, 3- and 5-year OS in the training dataset were 0.695 (95 % CI: 0.601-0.789), 0.794 (95 % CI: 0.734-0.854) and 0.788 (95 % CI: 0.721-0.855), respectively. The AUC values of 1-, 3- and 5-year OS in the validation dataset were 0.743 (95 % CI: 0.629-0.857), 0.780 (95 % CI: 0.695-0.864) and 0.788 (95 % CI: 0.688-0.888), respectively ( A). 
The calibration plots of the nomogram also exhibited high consistency between the predicted survival probability and actual observed OS in the two cohorts ( B), indicating that the nomogram model performed well in the OS prediction of ESCC patients who received surgery. Moreover, the DCA curves showed that the use of the nomogram for OS prediction within the risk threshold had clinical benefits ( C).
Next, risk score plots were used to visualize the differences in the distribution of individual parameters in high-risk and low-risk groups. The survival risk scores of patients were determined according to their pT stage, metastatic lymph node conditions and expression value of TNC. The best cutoff score of 0.23 distinguished patients into low-risk and high-risk populations. The group with a low-risk score had more living patients and longer survival time. In contrast, the high-risk group had a higher mortality rate and shorter survival. In addition, patients with higher risk scores exhibited higher TNC expression, more metastatic lymph nodes, and more advanced pT stage ( D). These results indicate that TNC might play a role in ESCC progression.

## Downregulation of TNC inhibited the malignant phenotypes of ESCC in vitro and in vivo
As high TNC expression was related to poor clinical outcomes of patients, we further explored whether downregulation of TNC inhibited the malignant phenotypes of ESCC cells. We transiently transfected siRNA against TNC into KYSE30 and KYSE450 cell lines, which had high TNC expression. Tumor cell proliferation and cell colony formation were significantly inhibited after TNC knockdown ( A, B). A decrease in TNC expression also induced cell cycle arrest in G1 phase ( C). Stable KYSE450-shTNC cells and KYSE450-shNS cells were used to develop xenograft tumor models in nude mice. The reduction of TNC expression yielded a robust suppressive effect on the growth of xenograft tumors ( D, E). 

## Knockdown of TNC down-regulated tumor growth-related signaling pathways
To reveal the signaling pathways regulated by TNC expression, we performed RNA-seq in KYSE450 cells which were transfected with siTNC-1 and siNS separately (Table S3). The GSEA results revealed that significant changes in gene expressions were mainly enriched in tumor growth-related signaling pathways, including MAPK signaling pathway, Ras signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, hypoxia signaling pathway, metabolic pathway, infection-related gene set, etc ( A). Multiple cancer-related signaling transduction pathways and metabolic pathways were down-regulated ( A, B). To verify the accuracy of GSEA results, we selected two suppressed tumor-related pathways, which were MAPK and PI3K-Akt signaling pathways ( C), and detected major molecular changes in these two pathways in both transient and stable TNC-knockdown KYSE30 and KYSE450 cells by Western blot ( D and S3). The Western blot showed the inhibition of EGFR and the downstream MAPK and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, which is consistent with GSEA results. Stable TNC-knockdown cells exhibited the consistent signaling changes with transient knockdown cells. 

## Decreasing TNC expression had a synergistic inhibitory effect with EGFR inhibitor
Since many genes which were positively regulated by TNC in GSEA results are targets for clinical therapies, we further explored whether reducing TNC expression could be combined with these inhibitors to improve therapeutic efficacy. Stable TNC-knockdown KYSE30 and KYSE450 cells and control cells were treated with the same panels of inhibitors (Table S4), and the inhibitory effects on cell proliferation were analyzed using the Bliss model. The mean ETNC value in KYSE30 and KYSE450 were 0.331 and 0.216, respectively.
Inhibitors with Bliss scores > 1 and Ecom > 0.50 were selected (Tables S5 and S6). Twenty-eight inhibitors showed synergistic inhibitory effects with TNC knockdown in both KYSE30 and KYSE450 cells ( A and Table S7). The KEGG enrichment analysis showed that genes targeted by the 28 inhibitors were mainly enriched in the signaling pathways involved in cell proliferation (ErbB, PI3K-Akt, Ras, MAPK, etc.), anti-tumor immune response (PD-L1 expression and PD-1 checkpoint pathway in cancer) and metabolism (carbon, choline, etc.) ( B, Table S8). The 28 inhibitors target EGFR (dacomitinib, osimertinib, poziotinib, erlotinib, afatinib and neratinib), PI3K-Akt-mTOR pathway (PF-04691502, uprosertib, LY2584702 and CC-115), MAPK pathway (pexmetinib and BI-847325), VEGFR (vandetanib), c-Met (tivantinib), MTH1 ((S)-crizotinib), Src (TPX-0005), FXR (turofexorate), ALK (ceritinib), Wee1 (adavosertib), topoisomerase (teniposide), CDK (dinaciclib), and others (epinephrine, pevonedistat, CB-839, fludarabine, vinorelbine, vinblastine, and docetaxel) ( C). Among them, most EGFR inhibitors ranked high in Bliss scores. Therefore, we emphasized the synergistic inhibitory effect of TNC and EGFR combined inhibition. 
The knockdown of TNC elevated the inhibitory efficiency to over 70 % for EGFR inhibitors, with an average combined inhibition rate of 0.85 and a Bliss score of 1.08 in KYSE30 cells, and an average combined inhibition rate of 0.77 with a Bliss score of 1.26 in KYSE450 cells ( D). Compared to monotherapy with EGFR inhibitors, the combination of TNC knockdown enhanced inhibitory effectiveness ( E). The results indicated a potential synergistic strategy between the two treatments.
Taken the GSEA and Western blot results together, the improvement of efficacy might be associated with dual inhibition of EGFR activity. Furthermore, we previously observed an elevation of EGFR protein and phosphorylation levels in KYSE30 and KYSE450 cells , and the mRNA expression of TNC and EGFR were positively correlated in a variety of cancers (Fig. S4). These suggested that combined inhibition of TNC and EGFR might have a wide range of applications.

## Discussion
The expression of TNC has been reported to be associated with aggressive behavior in several tumors. Ohtsuka et al. reported that TNC protein was expressed in 24.3 % of 111 ESCC patients and related to a shorter survival time. However, multivariate regression analysis in Ohtsuka's work showed that TNC expression was not an independent prognostic factor for survival. Yang et al. reported that 55.1 % of ESCC tissues and 10 % of adjacent non-tumor esophageal mucosa (in a total of 136 cancer specimens and 20 normal tissues) expressed TNC protein, and high expression of TNC was an independent adverse prognostic factor. We firstly verified the protein expression level of TNC in a larger cohort with 326 ESCC and 50 normal tissues and showed that TNC was highly expressed in 48.77 % of ESCC tissues but in only 2 % of normal esophageal tissues. The high expression of TNC was positively correlated with a shorter survival time of patients and was an independent factor for poor prognosis, which was consistent with Yang's work but partially different from Ohtsuka's conclusion. We speculate that the diversity may be related to the influence of sample size. Furthermore, we found that metastatic status of lymph node and pT stage were another two independent prognostic factors for the OS, which were absent in Yang's study.
In this study, a nomogram based on TNC expression and clinicopathological characteristics of patients was established, providing a simple and user-friendly method to comprehensively classify the prognosis of ESCC patients. Compared with conventional single factor, the nomogram model could better estimate the survival probability of patients by integrating the contribution of all influencing factors. Evaluation of the nomogram with AUC, calibration plot, DCA and risk scores demonstrated good performance in prognosis assessment and risk stratification in ESCC patients.
More importantly, we found that TNC expression may not only serve as an adverse prognostic biomarker, but also promote malignant behaviors and affect the sensitivity of ESCC cells to targeted inhibitors. In vitro and in vivo experiments indicated that down-regulation of TNC gene inhibited cell proliferation and tumor growth, suggesting that TNC had the potential to be a therapeutic target for ESCC. Furthermore, the reduction of TNC expression had a significant synergistic inhibitory effect with a variety of inhibitors, especially EGFR inhibitors. Evidence suggested that anti-EGFR-based targeted therapy showed clinical benefits for ESCC patients. A pilot study treated ESCC patients who were intolerant to chemoradiotherapy with concurrent erlotinib and radiotherapy, and found 2 (11.1 %) of 18 patients achieved complete response and 11 (61.1 %) achieved partial response, indicating the efficacy of concurrent erlotinib and radiotherapy in ESCC . A preclinical study of afatinib in ESCC showed that afatinib induced G0/G1 cell cycle arrest of ESCC cells and inhibited tumor growth in a dose dependent manner . A randomized, open-label, phase III trial compared the outcome of patients with resectable esophageal carcinoma by adding the EGFR antibody cetuximab to multimodal therapy, and the results suggested that the addition of cetuximab significantly reduced the local recurrence rate in whole population, and prolonged the OS both in EAC and ESCC patients .
Our research demonstrated that reducing TNC expression in ESCC cells contributed to enhance the efficacy of inhibitors targeting EGFR, VEGFR, c-Met, PI3K-Akt-mTOR signaling pathway, etc., providing a potential synergistic inhibitory strategy for treatment. The underlying mechanism might be related to the dual inhibition of corresponding signaling pathways.
As shown in the Western blot results, both of PI3K-p110α and PI3K-p85α protein levels decreased, and p110α subunit decreased more significantly. Similarly, Zhang et al. also observed a reduction of PI3K-p110α after TNC knockdown in glioma cells. PI3K-p110α (encoded by PIK3CA gene) and PI3K-p85α (encoded by PIK3R1 gene) subunits belong to subunits of Class IA PI3Ks, which control a variety of biological processes. The monomeric forms of p110 catalytic subunit and p85 regulatory subunit appear to be unstable, and both subunits can be stabilized when they bond to each other to form a heterodimeric PI3K . Another study reported that knockdown of PIK3CA significantly reduced p85α subunit level without altering PIK3R1 mRNA . According to our RNA-seq results, the mRNA level of PIK3CA was slightly downregulated in TNC-knockdown KYSE450 cells, whereas PIK3R1 mRNA level remained unchanged (Table S3). This indicates that the reduction in p110α protein level might be caused by the decrease in mRNA, while the decrease in p85α may occur during the post-translational stage.
The 14th EGF-like repeat of TNC has been shown to be a low-affinity ligand for EGFR . Zhou et al. reported that the interaction between TNC and EGFR directly activated the EGFR signaling pathway in muscle stem cells . Therefore, the inhibition of EGFR activity after knockdown of TNC might be related to TNC-EGFR interaction, which will be further investigated in our future studies. Also, we will set up different concentrations of EGFR inhibitors in further studies to obtain the optimal concentration of inhibitors in combination with TNC knockdown treatment.
In conclusion, we found that TNC overexpression had both prognostic and therapeutic significance in ESCC. The nomogram established according to TNC expression level and clinicopathologic characteristics provided a promising model for survival prediction. Decreasing TNC expression could improve the inhibitory effect of inhibitors against EGFR and downstream signaling pathways.
