 Annals of Oncology

 Effective immune-based treatment of extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma guided by next-generation gene profiling

 5 min to read

 Authors

 Evgeniy Galitskiy¹, Andrew J. Livingston², Tamera Plair², Artem Tarasov¹, Vladislav Putintsev¹, Anna Novokrestchenova¹, Vladimir Kushnarev¹, Ivan Zubarev¹, Vladislav Nikulin¹, Michael Hensley¹, Neeharika Makani¹, Sheila Yong¹, Anna Love¹, Jessica Brown¹, Nathan Fowler¹, Anthony P. Conley²

 BostonGene Corporation, Waltham, USA

 The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston, TX, USA

 Abstract

 Introduction

Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is a rare sarcoma subtype characterized by EWSR1 and NR4A3 gene rearrangements. EMC generally has a favorable prognosis, but it is prone to local and distant recurrences with limited systemic options for unresectable disease. This case shows the potential benefit of genomic and tumor microenvironment (TME) profiling in guiding systemic therapy for EMC.

Case Presentation

A 72-year-old male presented in October 2021 with a right gluteal mass, diagnosed as EMC based on biopsy and confirmed by the EWSR1-NR4A3 fusion. Initial treatment included neoadjuvant radiotherapy and radical resection of the gluteal mass in February 2022. The disease progressed in August 2022 with local recurrence and metastases to the lungs, bones, and soft tissues of the peritoneum. Given the lack of approved systemic therapy options for EMC, the patient underwent comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) to explore potential treatment targets. BostonGene’s RNA- and DNA-based comprehensive Tumor Portrait showed microsatellite stability (MSS), low tumor mutational burden (TMB, 0.67 mut/Mb), and TSC2 gene loss. Despite these findings, TME analysis revealed an immune-enriched/fibrotic TME with high PD-L1 expression by RNA-seq. After review by the institutional molecular tumor board, the patient began pembrolizumab. A near complete response was observed, with resolution or reduction of all pulmonary nodules and soft tissue lesions. The patient received 15 cycles of therapy and has not progressed to date. 

Discussion

TMB and MSS potentially fail to identify all patients who could benefit from immune-based therapy. Using a CGP assay, we identified additional markers of immune sensitivity in a rare cancer type, resulting in a dramatic and atypical response to pembrolizumab. Further prospective research is ongoing to validate the benefit of using CGP-based assays to identify patients with limited treatment options who may respond to targeted approaches.
