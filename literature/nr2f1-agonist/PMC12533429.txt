## Abstract

## Objective
To investigate the regulatory role of MACF1 and its upstream transcriptional control in focal adhesion remodeling and tumor progression in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).

## Methods
We employed in vitro loss- and gain-of-function assays using shRNA-mediated knockdown and ectopic overexpression of MACF1 and NR2F1 in LUAD cell lines (H1299 and Calu-3). Cell proliferation, adhesion, and migration were assessed by CCK-8, EdU, crystal violet, and Transwell assays. In vivo tumor growth and metastasis were evaluated using subcutaneous and tail vein xenograft models in nude mice. RNA-seq and GSEA were performed to identify MACF1-regulated pathways, followed by nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation, dual-luciferase reporter assays, and immunofluorescence to assess WNT/β-catenin activity. ChIP-qPCR and ChIP-seq data from ENCODE were used to validate NR2F1 binding to the MACF1 promoter.

## Results
MACF1 knockdown significantly suppressed LUAD cell proliferation, DNA replication, adhesion, and migration, and reduced tumor burden and lung metastases in vivo. Mechanistically, MACF1 activated WNT/β-catenin signaling by promoting CTNNB1 nuclear translocation, which upregulated focal adhesion genes (Paxillin, FAK, ITGB1). CTNNB1 agonist TWS119 restored focal adhesion in MACF1-deficient cells. Bioinformatic prediction and ChIP validation identified NR2F1 as a transcription factor directly targeting the MACF1 promoter. NR2F1 deficiency reduced MACF1 expression and phenocopied its functional loss, while MACF1 overexpression rescued the impaired phenotype.

## Conclusion
Our study uncovers a previously unrecognized NR2F1–MACF1–WNT axis that drives focal adhesion formation and LUAD progression. Targeting this regulatory circuit may offer new avenues for anti-metastatic therapy in lung adenocarcinoma.

## Highlights

NR2F1 is identified as a direct upstream transcription factor that activates MACF1 expression in LUAD.
MACF1 promotes LUAD cell proliferation, adhesion, and migration by enhancing focal adhesion assembly.
MACF1 activates the WNT/CTNNB1 signaling cascade, facilitating CTNNB1 nuclear translocation and downstream target expression.
Loss of MACF1 impairs focal adhesion formation and metastatic potential both in vitro and in xenograft and tail vein models.
The NR2F1–MACF1–WNT axis represents a novel regulatory circuit driving LUAD metastasis and offers potential therapeutic targets.

## Introduction
Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Among its subtypes, lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the predominant histological form of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), has witnessed a continuous rise in incidence over recent years [ – ]. Although advances in targeted therapies and immunotherapies have improved the prognosis of LUAD patients to some extent, therapeutic outcomes for those with locally advanced or metastatic disease remain unsatisfactory [ , ]. The high rates of metastasis and acquired resistance continue to pose major clinical challenges. Therefore, deciphering the molecular mechanisms governing LUAD cell migration, adhesion, and invasion is crucial for the development of new therapeutic strategies [ ].
During the metastatic cascade, tumor cells detach from the primary lesion, alter their interactions with the extracellular matrix (ECM), and undergo cytoskeletal remodeling to enhance motility [ ]. This process enables them to breach the basement membrane, enter the circulation, and eventually form distant metastases [ , ]. Focal adhesions, which serve as the principal structures for cell-ECM interaction and migration, are composed of transmembrane integrins and intracellular adaptor proteins such as Paxillin, Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK), and Talin [ , ]. These structures not only physically anchor cells to the ECM but also transmit biochemical signals that orchestrate cytoskeletal dynamics and cell motility [ ]. While focal adhesion activation is known to promote cancer cell invasiveness and metastatic potential, the upstream regulatory elements controlling focal adhesion assembly in LUAD remain poorly understood [ ].
Microtubule actin crosslinking factor 1 (MACF1), also known as ACF7, is a massive spectraplakin protein that simultaneously binds microtubules and filamentous actin [ ]. It has been shown to play essential roles in embryonic development, epithelial polarity establishment, wound healing, and neuronal migration [ , ]. Recent studies have implicated MACF1 in cytoskeletal dynamics and cell motility [ , ], yet its specific function in LUAD progression remains largely unexplored. Whether MACF1 governs focal adhesion assembly during tumor metastasis has not been previously investigated.
On the other hand, the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is well recognized for its central role in tumor initiation and progression [ ]. The key effector, CTNNB1 (β-catenin), translocates into the nucleus upon pathway activation and engages LEF/TCF transcription factors to induce downstream target gene expression [ ]. Persistent activation of the Wnt pathway has been implicated in epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), cancer stemness, and metastasis [ ]. Notably, several Wnt target genes such as ZEB1, CD44, and FN1 have been associated with adhesion and migratory behaviors of tumor cells [ , ]. However, whether MACF1 participates in Wnt signaling modulation—and consequently affects focal adhesion assembly—has not been experimentally confirmed in LUAD.
Furthermore, the nuclear receptor NR2F1 (nuclear receptor subfamily 2, group F, member 1) has been reported to regulate cellular dormancy and drug resistance in various tumor contexts [ , ]. Nevertheless, its function in LUAD remains largely undefined. Specifically, whether NR2F1 acts as an upstream transcriptional regulator of MACF1 expression is unknown. Thus, elucidating the transcriptional and signaling networks involving MACF1 and its upstream regulators is essential to understand LUAD metastasis and to identify novel therapeutic targets.
In this study, we demonstrate that MACF1 promotes focal adhesion formation and enhances LUAD cell growth and motility both in vitro and in vivo. Transcriptomic analyses and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) reveal that Wnt signaling is significantly suppressed following MACF1 knockdown. We further show that MACF1 modulates β-catenin nuclear localization and LEF/TCF transcriptional activity. Importantly, through transcription factor binding site (TFBS) prediction, chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR, and analysis of ENCODE datasets, we identify NR2F1 as a direct transcriptional activator of MACF1. Together, we define a novel functional signaling axis that orchestrates LUAD cell adhesion and migration, providing mechanistic insight into LUAD metastasis and identifying MACF1 as a potential therapeutic target.

## Material and methods

## Cell lines and culture
The human lung adenocarcinoma cell lines H1299 and Calu‑3 were acquired from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank. Cells were cultured in RPMI‑1640 medium (Gibco, Cat. 11875‑093) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Cat. 10099‑141) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, Cat. 15140‑122) in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Culture medium was changed every 2–3 days and cells were passaged at a split ratio of 1:3 to 1:5. Prior to experiments, cells were confirmed to be in exponential growth phase, morphologically normal, mycoplasma‑free, and regularly monitored for contamination.

## Lentiviral Infection and transfection
For stable MACF1 or NR2F1 knockdown, H1299 or Calu‑3 cells were infected with lentiviral particles encoding gene‑specific shRNAs (GenePharma, Shanghai) at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10. Twenty‑four hours post‑infection, 2 µg/mL puromycin (Sigma‑Aldrich, Cat. P8833) was added for selection over 3–5 days, yielding stable knockdown cell lines. MACF1 overexpression was achieved by infecting cells with a lentiviral vector encoding full‑length MACF1, followed by the same puromycin selection protocol. Knockdown or overexpression efficiency was validated by quantitative PCR (qPCR) and Western blot before downstream assays, including proliferation, adhesion, migration, and signaling pathway analyses.

## Drug treatment
To activate WNT/β-catenin signaling pharmacologically, cells were treated with the GSK3β inhibitor TWS119 (Selleck, S2924) dissolved in DMSO. We selected 10 µM for 24 h after small-scale titrations (2.5–15 µM; 6–36 h) designed to balance efficacy and tolerability in H1299 and Calu-3 cells: 10 µM for 24 h consistently produced marked β-catenin nuclear accumulation and increased LEF/TOP (TOPFlash) activity while maintaining > 90% viability by CCK-8. Lower concentrations yielded partial rescue, whereas higher concentrations or longer exposures did not further enhance WNT readouts and began to reduce viability. Unless indicated otherwise, all experiments in Fig.  used 10 µM for 24 h, with matched DMSO controls.
 

## Cell proliferation and DNA synthesis assays
Cell proliferation was assessed using the CCK‑8 assay (Dojindo, Cat. CK04). H1299 or Calu‑3 cells (5 × 103 per well) were seeded in 96‑well plates. At 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post‑seeding, 10 µL of CCK‑8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C, after which absorbance at 450 nm (OD_450) was measured using a Synergy microplate reader (BioTek). Experiments were performed in triplicate wells and repeated independently three times. DNA synthesis was evaluated by EdU incorporation assay using the BeyoClick™ EdU‑488 kit (Beyotime, Cat. C0071S). Cells were seeded onto coverslips in 24‑well plates, exposed to 10 µM EdU for 2 h, and processed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. DAPI counterstaining was performed and confocal images were acquired using a Leica SP8 microscope. The percentage of EdU‑positive nuclei was quantified from five randomly selected fields per sample.

## Adhesion and migration assays
For adhesion assays, 6‑well plates were coated with 0.1% gelatin (Sigma, Cat. G1890) for 1 h at 37 °C, washed twice with PBS and air‑dried. Single‑cell suspensions (1 × 105 cells/well) of H1299 or Calu‑3 were seeded and allowed to adhere for 30 min at 37 °C. Non‑adherent cells were gently washed away with PBS. Adherent cells were fixed and stained with 0.5% crystal violet (Solarbio) for 30 min, washed thoroughly, dried, and photographed. Five random fields were imaged per well and mean cell counts calculated. Migration assays were performed using Corning Transwell chambers (8 µm pore size). Cells (1 × 105) in serum‑free medium were added to the upper chamber and allowed to migrate toward 600 µL of medium supplemented with 10% FBS for 24 h. Non‑migrated cells were removed. Migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, stained with crystal violet for 30 min, and counted in five random fields under a light microscope.

## Nuclear–cytoplasmic protein fractionation and western blot
Subcellular fractionation was performed using the Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. 78833) following manufacturer’s instructions. Protein concentrations were quantified by BCA assay (Beyotime, Cat. P0012). Equal amounts of protein were resolved by SDS–PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% non‑fat milk for 1 h, incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies: MACF1 (Proteintech, 11452‑1‑AP, 1:1000), β‑catenin (Cell Signaling Technology, 8480, 1:1000), Paxillin (Abcam, ab32084, 1:1000), FAK (CST, 3285, 1:1000), ITGB1 (Abcam, ab24693, 1:1000), GAPDH (CST, 5174, 1:3000) as cytoplasmic loading control, and histone H3 (Abcam, ab1791, 1:3000) as nuclear loading control. HRP‑conjugated goat anti‑rabbit secondary antibody (ZSGB‑Bio; 1:5000) was used. Bands were visualized using ECL substrate (Thermo Fisher) and imaged using ChemiDoc MP (Bio‑Rad). Densitometry was performed using ImageJ software.

## Dual‑luciferase reporter assay
To assess Wnt/β‑catenin transcriptional activity, cells were co‑transfected in 24‑well plates with the TOPFlash reporter plasmid (LEF/TCF‑driven Firefly luciferase, Addgene #12456) and the pRL‑TK Renilla luciferase plasmid (Promega) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Cat. L3000008). Forty‑eight hours post‑transfection, cells were lysed and assayed using the Dual‑Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Cat. E1910) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Firefly and Renilla luminescence were measured sequentially on a BioTek Synergy reader. Firefly/Renilla luminescence ratios were normalized to control. Assays were performed in triplicate wells and repeated at least three times.

## Immunofluorescence
H1299 and Calu‑3 cells were seeded on sterilized round coverslips in 24‑well plates. After adhesion, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, Cat. P1110) for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X‑100 (Sigma, Cat. T8787) for 10 min, and washed three times with PBS. Blocking was performed with 5% BSA for 30 min. Coverslips were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Paxillin (Abcam, ab32084, 1:200) or β‑catenin (CST, 8480, 1:200). After washing, Alexa Fluor 488 or 594–conjugated secondary antibodies (Invitrogen, 1:500) were applied for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. F‑actin was stained using Phalloidin‑iFluor 647 (Abcam, ab176759, 1:1000), and nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1 µg/mL). Coverslips were mounted and imaged with a Leica SP8 confocal microscope. Fluorescence intensity and subcellular localization were quantified using ImageJ.

## Quantitative real‑time PCR (qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Cat. 15596026) and RNA purity and concentration quantified via NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher). One microgram of RNA was reverse‑transcribed using HiScript III RT SuperMix (+ gDNA wiper; Vazyme, Cat. R323) to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. qPCR was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Cat. Q711) on an ABI 7500 Real‑Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems). Primers were synthesized by Tsingke Biotech; GAPDH served as internal control. Reactions were run in technical triplicates and relative expression levels were calculated using the 2^–ΔΔCt method.

## In vivo tumor xenograft and lung metastasis model
Female BALB/c nude mice (4–6 weeks old, SPF grade; Nanjing University–Nanjing Model Animal Research Center) were used in accordance with approved protocols by the Animal Ethics Committee at Nanjing Medical University. Mice were housed in SPF barrier facilities at 22 ± 2 °C with a 12‑h light/dark cycle and ad libitum access to food and water. Mice were randomly allocated to experimental groups using a random number generator. Investigators performing tumor measurements, lung collection, and metastatic nodule counting were blinded to group assignments until after data collection was completed. For subcutaneous tumour formation assays, MACF1‑knockdown or control H1299 and Calu‑3 cells were suspended in PBS at 5 × 10⁷ cells/mL. A total of 1 × 10⁶ cells (100 µL) were injected subcutaneously into the right flank of each mouse (6–8 mice per group). Tumour length (L) and width (W) were measured twice weekly using calipers, and tumour volume was calculated as V = (L × W2)/2. On day 28 post‑injection, mice were euthanized; tumours were excised and weighed. Tumour tissues were divided for paraffin embedding for immunohistochemistry and snap‑frozen for protein analysis. For the lung metastasis model, 1 × 10⁶ cells (100 µL) were injected intravenously via the tail vein (6–8 mice per group). After 35 days, mice were sacrificed and lungs were harvested, rinsed in PBS, and photographed. Surface metastatic nodules were counted and maximal diameters measured. Lung tissues were processed for H&E staining and IHC detection of Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Formalin‑fixed (4% paraformaldehyde, 48 h) tumour and lung tissues were paraffin‑embedded and sectioned at 4 µm thickness. Sections were deparaffinised, rehydrated, subjected to antigen retrieval, treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide to quench endogenous peroxidase activity, and blocked with 5% BSA. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies: Ki‑67 (Abcam, ab15580, 1:200), β‑catenin (CST, 8480, 1:300), Paxillin (Abcam, ab32084, 1:300), FAK (CST, 3285, 1:300), and ITGB1 (Abcam, ab24693, 1:300). After incubation with HRP‑conjugated secondary antibodies, DAB staining was performed, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining. Slides were dehydrated, mounted, and imaged under a light microscope. Protein expression was quantified by staining intensity.

## RNA sequencing and bioinformatic analysis
Total RNA from control and MACF1-knockdown H1299 cells was extracted and assessed for quality using NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher) and Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Sequencing libraries were prepared and run on an Illumina NovaSeq platform (BGI or Oebiotech). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using thresholds of |log₂FC|> 1 and adjusted p‑value (p.adj) < 0.05. Volcano plots and heatmaps were generated. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using clusterProfiler (v4.2.2) on Hallmark pathway gene sets (c2.cp.v7.4.symbols.gmt), with results ranked by normalized enrichment score (NES) and significance defined at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25. For RNA-seq, three biological replicates were performed for each condition (control vs. MACF1-knockdown). Libraries were prepared using poly(A) selection, sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, and yielded an average of ~ 45–55 million paired-end reads (150 bp) per sample. Raw reads were subjected to quality control using FastQC (v0.11.9), and adapter trimming was performed with Trimmomatic (v0.39). All samples passed QC thresholds with > 95% bases at Q30. Reads were aligned to the human reference genome (hg38) using STAR (v2.7.9a) with an average mapping rate of > 92%. Transcript abundance was quantified using featureCounts (v2.0.1), and normalization plus differential expression analysis was conducted with DESeq2 (v1.38.0).

## TCGA data mining
The TCGA-LUAD gene expression and clinical dataset were accessed via UCSC Xena. Spearman correlation analyses were performed in R (v4.4.0) between MACF1 or NR2F1 and Wnt pathway components (e.g. CTNNB1, ZEB1, CD44, FN1) as well as focal adhesion genes (Paxillin, FAK, ITGB1). Patients were stratified into high and low MACF1 expression cohorts by median cut-off. Kaplan–Meier survival curves for overall survival (OS) and progression‑free survival (PFS) were generated using the survival and survminer packages; statistical significance was determined by the log-rank test (p < 0.05). Association between MACF1 expression and TNM staging was also evaluated. Kaplan–Meier survival curves for overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were generated using the survival and survminer packages in R (v4.4.0). Both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were conducted to evaluate the prognostic impact of MACF1 expression, adjusting for age, sex, smoking status, and TNM stage. Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated, and significance was defined as P < 0.05.

## Transcription factor binding site prediction and ChIP‑qPCR
TFBS predictions for the MACF1 promoter region (– 2000 bp to + 200 bp relative to the transcription start site) were performed using the TFBS R package and cross-referenced with the TRRUST database to identify candidate transcriptional regulators. NR2F1 emerged as a candidate and was validated by ChIP-qPCR using the Magna ChIP A/G Kit (Millipore). Chromatin from H1299 cells was crosslinked, lysed, and sonicated; immunoprecipitation was performed with anti‑NR2F1 antibody (Abcam, ab181137) or IgG control. Enrichment of MACF1 promoter fragments in precipitates was quantified by qPCR. ENCODE ChIP-seq bigWig data for NR2F1 in lung cancer cells and normal lung tissue were retrieved; peaks were called using MACS2 and enrichment at the MACF1 promoter (± 2 kb around TSS) was visualized with IGV. For ChIP-qPCR validation, primers targeting the MACF1 promoter region (– 2000 bp to + 200 bp relative to TSS) were designed and synthesized (Tsingke Biotech). Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1. ENCODE ChIP-seq data for NR2F1 were retrieved from the A549 lung adenocarcinoma cell line and compared against normal human lung primary tissue controls.

## Statistical analysis
All data are presented as mean ± SEM unless otherwise specified. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.4. For comparisons between two groups, an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used. For comparisons involving more than two groups, one-way or two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was applied. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Exact statistical tests used for each experiment are indicated in the corresponding figure legends.

## Results

## MACF1 promotes focal adhesion in lung adenocarcinoma cells
We first knocked down MACF1 in H1299 and Calu-3 cells and observed a significant suppression in cell growth upon MACF1 silencing (Fig.  A–C). Additionally, the DNA replication capacity of the cells was markedly reduced (Fig.  D). MACF1 (Microtubule Actin Crosslinking Factor 1) is known to regulate cytoskeletal remodeling by linking microtubules and actin filaments. We therefore examined the expression of focal adhesion-associated genes and found that Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1 were significantly downregulated following MACF1 knockdown (Fig.  E, F). Moreover, when the cells were seeded on gelatin-coated plates to assess adhesion capacity, MACF1-deficient cells displayed significantly reduced adhesion (Fig.  G). Immunofluorescence staining of Paxillin and F-actin revealed a marked decrease in membrane-localized Paxillin after MACF1 knockdown (Fig.  H). Given that increased adhesion often enhances cell migration, we further assessed the migratory ability of H1299 and Calu-3 cells. Notably, MACF1 knockdown significantly impaired cell migration in both cell lines (Fig.  I).
 

## MACF1 knockdown impairs tumor growth and metastasis in vivo
To evaluate the effect of MACF1 on tumor growth and focal adhesion in vivo, we subcutaneously injected H1299 or Calu-3 cells with stable MACF1 knockdown into nude mice. We observed a significantly reduced tumor growth rate in MACF1-deficient lung adenocarcinoma cells (Fig.  A, B). Furthermore, xenograft tumors derived from MACF1-knockdown cells showed markedly decreased staining for KI67 and CTNNB1 (Fig.  C, D). We then established a lung metastasis model by intravenously injecting MACF1-deficient cells via tail vein. Strikingly, the number of metastatic nodules in lung tissue was significantly reduced in the MACF1-knockdown group (Fig.  E–F). Additionally, the expression levels of FAK, ITGB1, and Paxillin in the metastatic lesions were markedly decreased, as determined by immunohistochemistry (Fig.  G–I).
 

## MACF1 promotes focal adhesion by activating the WNT signaling pathway
To further investigate the downstream molecular mechanisms regulated by MACF1, we performed RNA-seq analysis in H1299 cells with MACF1 knockdown and identified differentially expressed genes (Fig.  A, B). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) revealed that the gene set “HALLMARKS_WNT_SIGNALING_PATHWAY” exhibited the highest NES score (Fig.  C). This led us to hypothesize that MACF1 may promote cell proliferation and focal adhesion by modulating the WNT signaling pathway. We next examined the nuclear translocation of CTNNB1 and found that its nuclear accumulation was markedly reduced in both H1299 and Calu-3 cells upon MACF1 knockdown (Fig.  D–E). Consistently, LEF/TOP luciferase reporter assays showed a significant decrease in transcriptional activity in MACF1-deficient cells, indicating that WNT pathway activation was substantially suppressed (Fig.  F). Furthermore, we assessed the expression of WNT downstream genes that are associated with focal adhesion, including ZEB1, CD44, and FN1, and found their mRNA levels were significantly downregulated in MACF1-silenced cells (Fig.  G). In the TCGA-LUAD cohort, we stratified patients into high and low MACF1 expression groups and observed that MACF1 expression was positively correlated with the expression of genes related to WNT signaling and focal adhesion (Fig. S1A). In the TCGA-LUAD cohort, patients with high MACF1 expression displayed significantly poorer OS and PFS (Fig. S1B–C). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further confirmed that MACF1 expression is an independent prognostic factor for OS after adjusting for age, sex, smoking status, and stage (Fig. S1D).
 

## CTNNB1 agonists restore focal adhesion and proliferation in MACF1-knockdown cells
To determine whether activating CTNNB1 could rescue the impaired WNT signaling and focal adhesion in MACF1-deficient cells, we treated MACF1-knockdown H1299 and Calu-3 cells with the CTNNB1 agonist TWS119, which targets GSK3β to prevent CTNNB1 phosphorylation and degradation, thereby enhancing WNT pathway activation. We observed a significant increase in nuclear accumulation of CTNNB1 following TWS119 treatment (Fig.  A–B), accompanied by enhanced cell proliferation and DNA replication capacity (Fig.  C–D). In addition, the expression levels of focal adhesion-related genes Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1 were notably elevated in TWS119-treated H1299 and Calu-3 cells (Fig.  E). Immunofluorescence staining further revealed increased membrane-localized Paxillin (Fig.  F), and cell adhesion assays using gelatin-coated plates demonstrated improved adhesion capacity upon TWS119 treatment (Fig.  G). Furthermore, Transwell migration assays indicated a significant enhancement in cell motility following CTNNB1 activation (Fig.  H).

## MACF1 is transcriptionally regulated by the transcription factor NR2F1
To investigate the upstream transcriptional regulation of MACF1, we employed the TFBS package to predict potential transcription factors that may bind to the MACF1 promoter. Among the top candidates, NR2F1 was identified as a key regulator (Fig.  A–B). Notably, Spearman correlation analysis using TCGA-LUAD cohort revealed a significant positive correlation between NR2F1 and MACF1 expression levels (Fig.  C). To experimentally validate this regulatory relationship, we performed chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays using anti-NR2F1 antibodies, which demonstrated enrichment of the MACF1 promoter region in the NR2F1-immunoprecipitated complex (Fig.  D). Furthermore, we retrieved and analyzed ChIP-seq bigwig data for NR2F1 from the ENCODE database. These data revealed significantly higher genome-wide NR2F1 binding signals in lung cancer cells compared to normal lung tissue (Fig.  E–F), particularly at the transcription start site (TSS) of MACF1 (Fig.  G). To further determine the functional impact of NR2F1 on MACF1 expression, we knocked down NR2F1 in H1299 and A549 cells using lentiviral shRNAs. As expected, MACF1 expression was markedly reduced at both mRNA and protein levels upon NR2F1 silencing (Fig.  H–I). Collectively, these findings suggest that NR2F1 directly binds to the MACF1 promoter and positively regulates its transcription in lung adenocarcinoma cells.
 

## NR2F1 knockdown impairs LUAD cell proliferation and focal adhesion
A literature review indicated that NR2F1 has not been previously studied in the context of lung cancer. We observed that silencing NR2F1 significantly inhibited the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells (Fig.  A), accompanied by a marked reduction in DNA replication capacity (Fig.  B). Our earlier results demonstrated that NR2F1 regulates MACF1 transcription and that NR2F1 knockdown suppresses MACF1 expression. Given this regulatory axis, we further investigated changes in focal adhesion following NR2F1 silencing. We found that expression levels of Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1 were significantly reduced in H1299 and Calu-3 cells upon NR2F1 knockdown (Fig.  C). To assess changes in cell adhesion, cells were seeded on gelatin-coated plates and subjected to crystal violet staining. The number of adherent cells was significantly reduced in the NR2F1-knockdown group, suggesting impaired adhesion ability (Fig.  D). Immunofluorescence staining of Paxillin and F-actin further revealed decreased localization of Paxillin on the plasma membrane (Fig.  E), which was accompanied by reduced cell migratory capacity (Fig.  F). Given our previous findings that MACF1 promotes nuclear translocation of CTNNB1 and activation of the WNT signaling pathway, we next examined whether NR2F1 knockdown affects CTNNB1 activity. Western blot analysis following nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation and immunofluorescence staining both showed a substantial reduction in CTNNB1 nuclear localization in NR2F1-silenced cells (Fig.  G–H). Furthermore, the expression levels of downstream WNT target genes, including ZEB1, CD44, and FN1, were significantly downregulated following NR2F1 silencing (Fig.  I). These results suggest that NR2F1 promotes LUAD progression, at least in part, through regulation of the NR2F1–MACF1–CTNNB1–WNT axis.
 

## Overexpression of MACF1 restores focal adhesion in NR2F1-deficient LUAD cells
We next overexpressed MACF1 in NR2F1-deficient H1299 and Calu-3 cells (Fig.  A–B). We observed that overexpression of MACF1 significantly increased the nuclear localization of CTNNB1 (Fig.  C–D), accompanied by elevated LEF/TOP transcriptional activity and upregulation of downstream target gene expression (Fig.  E–F). Subsequently, we assessed whether restoration of MACF1 expression could reverse the focal adhesion impairment caused by NR2F1 knockdown. In both H1299 and Calu-3 cells, overexpression of MACF1 led to increased expression of focal adhesion-related genes Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1 (Fig.  G), and enhanced membrane-localized Paxillin as revealed by immunofluorescence (Fig.  H). Cell adhesion was also significantly increased when cells were seeded on gelatin-coated plates, as indicated by the number of adherent cells (Fig.  I). Moreover, we found that MACF1 overexpression significantly promoted the proliferation and migration of NR2F1-deficient H1299 and Calu-3 cells (Fig.  J–L). Collectively, these results support the conclusion that NR2F1 transcriptionally regulates MACF1 expression, which in turn activates the WNT signaling pathway via CTNNB1 nuclear translocation. This signaling cascade promotes the transcription of downstream focal adhesion-related genes, ultimately enhancing the proliferative and metastatic capacity of LUAD cells.
 

## Discussion
This study systematically delineates the functional role and regulatory mechanism of MACF1 in the progression of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). We demonstrate that MACF1 activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and significantly enhances the expression and membrane localization of key focal adhesion components, thereby promoting cell adhesion, migration, and in vivo metastatic potential. Furthermore, we identify that MACF1 transcription is directly regulated by the nuclear receptor transcription factor NR2F1. Based on a series of in vitro and in vivo experiments, as well as integrative transcriptomic and bioinformatic analyses, we propose a functional axis whereby NR2F1 transcriptionally activates MACF1, which in turn enhances Wnt signaling activity and facilitates focal adhesion formation. This regulatory cascade reveals a previously unrecognized mechanism driving LUAD metastasis.
MACF1 is a large spectraplakin protein that crosslinks microtubules and actin filaments [ , ]. Prior studies have primarily investigated MACF1 in the context of developmental biology, where it regulates epidermal polarity, neuronal guidance, and embryonic morphogenesis [ , ]. However, its role in tumor biology, particularly in LUAD, has remained poorly defined. Here, we show that MACF1 knockdown markedly impairs cellular proliferation, DNA replication, adhesion, and migration in LUAD cell lines. In gelatin-based adhesion and Transwell migration assays, MACF1-deficient cells exhibited significantly diminished adhesive and migratory capacities. These phenotypic changes correlated with a reduction in the expression of focal adhesion molecules such as Paxillin, FAK, and ITGB1, as well as diminished membrane localization of Paxillin. Structurally, MACF1’s ability to bridge microtubules and actin filaments positions it as a critical regulator of cytoskeletal remodeling [ , , ]. Functionally, our data suggest that MACF1 contributes to the dynamic assembly of focal adhesions, thereby coordinating cytoskeletal organization with cell–matrix interactions to drive metastatic behavior.
To dissect the signaling pathways downstream of MACF1, transcriptomic profiling and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed [ ]. Among the enriched pathways, the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade was significantly suppressed upon MACF1 knockdown, ranking first in normalized enrichment score (NES) among all Hallmark gene sets. Functional validation confirmed that MACF1 deficiency inhibited β-catenin nuclear translocation and reduced LEF/TCF transcriptional activity. Notably, the expression levels of canonical Wnt target genes associated with adhesion and EMT, including ZEB1, CD44, and FN1, were significantly downregulated. These findings implicate MACF1 as a positive regulator of Wnt signaling and suggest that it promotes LUAD progression via transcriptional upregulation of adhesion-related genes [ – ]. The Wnt agonist TWS119 partially rescued the loss of adhesion and migration phenotypes in MACF1-deficient cells, confirming that Wnt signaling mediates MACF1’s pro-metastatic function.
While prior studies have suggested that MACF1 may modulate β-catenin stability through interactions with Axin and GSK3β [ , ], our data provide new evidence that MACF1 promotes Wnt signaling via enhanced β-catenin nuclear translocation. These results expand our understanding of the noncanonical regulation of the Wnt pathway in LUAD and position MACF1 as a key upstream modulator of Wnt-dependent adhesion remodeling. Importantly, this study highlights the mechanistic link between cytoskeletal dynamics and transcriptional activation of pro-migratory programs in cancer cells.
To identify upstream regulators of MACF1, we integrated TFBS prediction [ ], TRRUST database [ ] mining, and correlation analyses using TCGA-LUAD datasets. NR2F1 emerged as a top candidate transcription factor. As a member of the nuclear receptor family, NR2F1 has been implicated in cancer cell dormancy, stemness, and therapy resistance in other tumor types, but its role in LUAD has not been reported [ , ]. In this study, we show that NR2F1 directly binds to the MACF1 promoter, as evidenced by ChIP-qPCR and ChIP-seq data from the ENCODE database. NR2F1 expression positively correlates with MACF1 levels across LUAD patient samples. Functionally, NR2F1 knockdown downregulates MACF1 expression, suppresses Wnt activity, and diminishes the expression of focal adhesion-related genes, resulting in impaired cellular adhesion and migration. Rescue experiments demonstrated that MACF1 overexpression restores Wnt signaling and focal adhesion integrity in NR2F1-deficient cells, establishing a causative regulatory relationship.
Collectively, we propose a regulatory cascade in which NR2F1 transcriptionally activates MACF1, which in turn promotes Wnt/β-catenin signaling and upregulates a transcriptional program supporting focal adhesion assembly and metastatic dissemination. This cascade reveals MACF1 as a molecular nexus linking transcriptional control, cytoskeletal remodeling, and signal transduction, thereby supporting aggressive tumor phenotypes. Analysis of the TCGA-LUAD cohort further showed that high MACF1 expression is significantly associated with worse overall survival, more advanced clinical stage, and elevated expression of Wnt and adhesion-related genes, reinforcing its prognostic and therapeutic potential. An important unresolved question concerns the mechanism by which MACF1 facilitates β-catenin nuclear translocation. Previous studies suggest that spectraplakin proteins such as MACF1 can act as cytoskeletal scaffolds, bridging microtubules and actin filaments to direct the trafficking of signaling proteins. It is therefore plausible that MACF1 may stabilize β-catenin complexes at the cytoskeleton, positioning them for nuclear import. Alternatively, MACF1 could interact with nuclear transport machinery, such as importins or nucleoporins, to promote β-catenin translocation into the nucleus, although this remains to be experimentally tested. Future studies should determine whether MACF1 functions primarily as a structural adaptor or whether it directly recruits nuclear transport cofactors. Elucidating these mechanisms will be critical for understanding how cytoskeletal regulators interface with transcriptional signaling during LUAD progression Table .

 Despite these findings, our study has several limitations. First, most experiments were conducted in vitro or in immunodeficient xenograft models, which do not fully recapitulate the tumor-immune microenvironment. Future studies should utilize genetically engineered LUAD models with intact immune systems to validate the NR2F1–MACF1–Wnt axis. Second, the precise molecular mechanism by which MACF1 facilitates β-catenin nuclear translocation remains to be elucidated. Whether MACF1 directly modulates GSK3β activity, Axin complex stability, or nuclear transport machinery is an open question. Finally, large-scale validation in clinical cohorts and functional dissection of NR2F1’s broader transcriptional network will be required to establish its value as a therapeutic target.
In summary, this study uncovers a previously unrecognized transcriptional signaling circuit in LUAD in which NR2F1 induces MACF1 expression to activate Wnt-dependent focal adhesion assembly and metastatic progression. Our findings identify MACF1 as a critical regulator of cytoskeleton-linked signal transduction and a promising candidate for therapeutic intervention in metastatic lung cancer.
