In lower vertebrates like zebrafish, these cells assume responsibility for spontaneous retinal regeneration, wherein endogenous Müller glia undergo proliferation, transform into Müller glia-derived progenitor cells, and subsequently regenerate the entire retina with restored functionality. Conversely, Müller glia in the mouse and human retina exhibit limited neural reprogramming. Müller glia reprogramming is thus a promising strategy for treating neurodegenerative ocular disorders. ## Introduction
Neuronal loss and the gradual deterioration of many nervous system areas are the defining features of neurodegenerative diseases (Cheng et al., 2021). Damaged retinal neurons result in blindness-related illnesses, including retinal degeneration disease, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and glaucoma (Wan and Goldman, 2016; Yan et al., 2023).
Exogenous and endogenous stem cell therapies are being investigated as potential substitutes for neurodegenerative diseases (Ikelle et al., 2020). Stem cell research progress has increased the possibility of using stem cell-derived photoreceptors and/or retinal pigmented epithelial cells to replace those that are damaged (Langhe and Pearson, 2020). However, many technical challenges in the sourcing xenotransplantation and replacement cells must still be overcome. The source of donor cells, safety, and efficacy are constant bottleneck issues. Using endogenous stem cells to generate new neurons after retinal damage or illness is an optimal approach. This strategy does not require transplanting and integrating cells from the subretinal or vitreous chamber, as new neurons are generated by Müller glia (MG) within the retina.
Zebrafish MG demonstrate extraordinary regenerative abilities (Hamon et al., 2016). These MG respond to retinal damage by initiating cell hypertrophy and intermediate filament protein expression up-regulation-characterized gliotic response (Hippert et al., 2015; Thomas et al., 2016). In zebrafish, the glial cell response is temporary, activating MG as retinal stem cells, then differentiating into all retinal cell types. However, mammalian MG undergo gliosis fate and rarely divide, leading to low cell replacement and gliosis. Understanding why might allow us to facilitate the regenerative potential of mammalian MG (Martin and Poche, 2019).
Some signaling pathways and stem cell-related transcription factors (TFs) have been discovered in the zebrafish MG reprogramming process (Lahne et al., 2020). Reprogramming zebrafish MG with defined factors has changed our understanding of cell identity and fate determination. Exploring how lower vertebrates and mammals respond to retinal injury could thus inform strategies for reprogramming mammalian MG (Hoang et al., 2020). Inspiration from retinal cell development has already been valuable for developing MG reprogramming strategies in mice (Buono and Martinez-Morales, 2020). Neuronal reprogramming in somatic cells has also allowed new possibilities for exploring MG reprogramming in mice (Gascon et al., 2017).
Many efforts have been made in the past decade to achieve MG reprogramming in mammals. Researchers have developed better techniques to manipulate MG cells into other retinal neurons. In these studies, MG were reprogrammed into inner retinal neurons (amacrine and bipolar cells), photoreceptors (rods and cones), and ganglion cells. MG-derived neurons not only expressed retinal neuron markers, they also exhibited neuronal electrical activity and even integrated into retinal circuits to restore visual function (Sanges et al., 2016; Jorstad et al., 2017, 2020; Pesaresi et al., 2018; Yao et al., 2018; Hoang et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2020; Xiao et al., 2021; Todd et al., 2022; Pinsonneault et al., 2023; and ). Therefore, MG have tremendous potential for various retinal diseases characterized by neuron loss, including retinitis pigmentosa and Stargardt disease (rod and cone loss), age-related macular degeneration (cone and retinal pigment epithelial cell loss), and glaucoma (retinal ganglion cell [RGC] loss) (Eastlake et al., 2021). Reprogramming MG into retinal neurons may thus provide a promising new therapeutic treatment for blinding retinal diseases, and potential help for other neurological disorders.
 Despite significant progress, current MG reprogramming strategies are not without limitations. MG reprogramming has low efficiency and effectiveness, and the reprogrammed cells differ from the retinal host neuron, making visual function repair challenging (Pinsonneault et al., 2023). It also remains unclear whether the neurons derived from reprogrammed MG will survive long-term in the diseased retina, or continuously improve visual function ( ). Thus far, most approaches for inducing MG reprogramming have used viral vectors to achieve gene overexpression or knockdown. Off-target effects or leaky expression of viral vectors has complicated the validation of MG differentiated to neurons and has led to unclear conclusions (Hoang et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2023). Current strategies are more focused on regulating individual or multiple genes, and altering MG signaling pathways. However, MG reprogramming in vivo occurs in a unique injury environment, and involves specific biochemical or mechanical signals. The barriers to MG reprogramming and inducing gliosis in mice include genetic limitations, epigenetic factors, and microenvironments. Strategies to promote reprogramming by regulating exogenous microenvironments are lacking. However, exploration of novel reprogramming factors and new strategy developments has recently increased (Hoang et al., 2020; Pinsonneault et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). MG reprogramming-mediated retinal repair provides a promising therapeutic direction for retinal degenerative diseases, with more advanced reprogramming strategies and techniques urgently needed.
 Herein, we summarize the past decade of successful strategies for MG reprogramming in mice, categorizing these strategies as direct and indirect ( and ). We also discuss MG reprogramming in relation to immune regulation and metabolic processes, and share six promising strategies for MG reprogramming in mice inspired by nervous system regeneration work.

## Retrieval Strategy
All literature searches were conducted in the PubMed and bioRxiv databases and published between January 1, 2013 and December 1, 2023 in this narrative review. We combined the following words and phrases to maximize search specificity and sensitivity: “Müller glia,” “reprogramming,” “epigenetic,” “inflammation,” “immune,” “microglia,” “metabolic,” “chemical small molecules,” “extracellular matrix,” “cell fusion,” “ATAC seq,” and “scRNA seq.” All papers were screened by title and abstract, only articles and reviews exploring strategies for cell reprogramming were included herein. No languages were restricted.

## Progress towards Relatively Successful Approaches to Induce Müller Glia Reprogramming in Mice
MG reprogramming in mice has paved the way for regenerating retinal neurons in mammals. “Indirect reprogramming” involves inducing adult cell proliferation, dedifferentiation into pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), and redifferentiation into specific cell types, in three steps, to achieve cell fate transformation (Lahne et al., 2020). “Direct reprogramming” also known as “transdifferentiation” refers to the forced transdifferentiation of adult somatic cells into other cell types without undergoing proliferation and intermediate pluripotent stem cell processes (Wang et al., 2021a; ).

## Indirect reprogramming of mouse MG
In zebrafish, MG undergo reprogramming events after retinal injury. First, they are activated by the apoptotic cells, and parts of them dedifferentiate. Then, MG acquire stem cell characteristics, proliferating and migrating into the nuclear layer of the damaged neurons. Ultimately, these MG differentiate into retinal neurons and integrate into extant neuronal circuits to repair the injured retina (Lahne et al., 2020; ). Zebrafish MG thus undergo indirect reprogramming after they are activated by injury. Indirect reprogramming entails attaining stable pluripotency and resetting epigenetic signatures (Samoylova and Baklaushev, 2020).
MG reprogramming in mice has been achieved in two ways; one is like MG reprogramming in zebrafish, the other is different. In the N-methyl-D-aspartate-damaged retina, a number of MG were found to be positive for PCNA and PhH3 by fusion of MG cells bone marrow cells (Pesaresi et al., 2018). Ascl1 overexpression, together with histone deacetylase inhibitor, induces Otx2 expression in MG. However, the majority of these MG-derived neurons were EdU-unlabeled (Jorstad et al., 2017). Similarly, MG-specific knockout of nuclear factor I enabled MG-expressed Crx, HuC/D, and NeuN, while most MG-derived neurons were not labeled with EdU (Hoang et al., 2020). Although MAP4Ks inhibitors or β-catenin can be used to promote MG proliferation, it is unclear whether reprogrammed cells were derived from proliferating MG (Yao et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2023; and ).
Indirect reprogramming of MG is more conducive to retinal repair, as it will not cause a shortage of MG cells. However, the precise manipulations of MG dedifferentiation, proliferation, and transdifferentiation are challenging. Some mouse retina signaling pathways, including YAP pathway (Hamon et al., 2019), Hippo pathway (Rueda et al., 2019), Stat signaling (Jorstad et al., 2020), Midkine (Campbell et al., 2021b), Cannabinoid pathway (Campbell et al., 2021a), Notch signaling (Sahu et al., 2021), nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling (Palazzo et al., 2022), ID (Taylor et al., 2023), mTORC1 signaling (Lim et al., 2021), and microglia depletion (Todd et al., 2020) influence glial reactivity and MG-derived progenitor cell formation.

## Direct reprogramming of mouse MG
Mouse MG have also been reprogramed into retinal neurons by simultaneous direct overexpression of single or multiple factors, such as: Pou4f2 plus Ascl1 plus Islet1; Pou4f2 plus Atoh7; Ikzf1 plus Ikzf4; NeuroD1; and knocking out individual factor Ptbp1 (Pinsonneault et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2021, 2022; Xu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2020). However, several MG strategies have been controversial. Xie et al. (2022, 2023) failed to find NeuroD1- and Ptbp1-mediated MG reprogramming in the mouse retina with advanced AAV tools and MG-lineage tracing mice. Hoang et al. (2022) also failed to show that Ptbp1 deletion induced MG reprogramming in mouse retina based on MG-lineage trace mice. Similarly, Wang et al. (2021b) found that NeuroD1-induced neurons are not derived from astrocytes in the brain, while endogenous neurons, and knocking down Ptbp1 could not convert astrocytes into neurons. AAV-based gene delivery strategies have faced challenges, while the Cre-loxp system-based gene editing approach was also defective, since it may have built a higher wall for cell transformation than exists in wild-type mice (Chen, 2021). Moreover, different outcomes have been reported from Ptbp1 knockdown-mediated reprogramming; one study reported that Ptbp1 knockdown generated RGC-like cells (Zhou et al., 2020), while another showed that Ptbp1 knockdown generated cone-like cells (Fu et al., 2020; and ).
The advantages of MG direct reprogramming for retinal repair are speed, effectiveness, and preservation of age-related epigenetic signatures. Essential questions for achieving MG direct reprogramming regard how cell identities of MG are kept, erased, and acquired (Samoylova and Baklaushev, 2020). However, direct reprogramming reduces MG numbers and must therefore be applied conservatively (Pinsonneault et al., 2023).
While direct MG reprogramming strategies have been more controversial, indirect MG reprogramming is more difficult to achieve. Current reprogramming strategies mainly emphasize the significance of genetic regulation for MG programming in mice. However, they have not achieved complete cell identity transformation. There is an urgent need to resolve new MG reprogramming strategies and improve verification.

## Classical Müller Glia Reprogramming Strategies through Transcription Factor Regulation in Mice
At present, the main approach to reprogramming MG involves genetic regulation. This means manipulating its specific genes, especially TFs (e.g., increasing expression of Aslc1, NeuroD1, Otx2, Crx, Nrl, Pou4f2, Atoh7, Islet1, Ikzf1, and Ikzf4; and ). However, the TFs used to regulate MG reprogramming in mice are relatively scarce.
 One current MG reprogramming strategy challenge in mice is identifying more TFs that can regulate MG cell fate conversion. Relying on retinal developmental biology and borrowing knowledge from other cell reprogramming is a slow process. To speed the discovery process, integrating multiple-omics datasets, such as genomics, transcriptomics (Bulk-RNA seq and scRNA-seq), and epigenomics (Chip-seq and ATAC-seq) holds promise. Multiple-omics enforce the roles of the described molecular mechanisms (Scimone et al., 2015, 2016, 2020). Using traditional batch RNA-seq cannot accurately capture transcriptome changes in heterogeneous reprogrammed cell populations. scRNA-seq is an ideal technology for identifying MG reprogramming gene regulatory networks, by relying on identifications of differentially expressed genes, clustering, and trajectory analysis. Using scRNA-seq and ATAC-seq, Hoang et al. (2020) revealed gene networks that regulate MG homeostasis, activation, and reprogramming is evolutionarily conserved and species-specific. Genes necessary for MG fate conversion may not be those with the highest differential expression. Researchers have adopted algorithm-based prediction of TFs and exhaustive genome-wide CRISPR activation to screen TFs and other DNA-binding regulators for cell fate conversion (Liu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021a).

## Potential Strategies for Inducing Müller Glia Reprogramming in Mice
Developing new strategies is another alternative for achieving MG reprogramming in mice. Complex manipulations are necessary for efficient, reliable MG reprogramming in complex (e.g., injured, inflamed, highly oxidative) microenvironments (Pena and Vazquez, 2022). A detailed description of the microenvironment state after retinal injury, including immune, metabolic, and extracellular matrix (ECM) changes, has facilitated screening and identifying regulatory targets affecting MG reprogramming. Valuable MG reprogramming insights have come from exploring zebrafish MG reprogramming; neural stem cell development mechanisms; mammalian brain glial, fibroblast, and cardiac reprogramming strategies; and the induction of adult cells into iPSCs (Lenkowski and Raymond, 2014; Li et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2019). Thus, in addition to direct manipulation by TFs, many alternative MG non-targeted regulatory approaches are available to achieve MG reprogramming regulation; these include epigenetic remodeling, metabolism modulation, immune regulation, chemical small-molecules regulation, ECM remodeling, and cell-cell fusion and communication.

## MG reprogramming through epigenetic remodeling
Genetics research in MG reprogramming has made considerable achievements in the last decade, especially lineage-specific transcriptional factors. Ascl1, NeuroD1, Otx2, Crx, Nrl, Pou4f2, Atoh7, and Islet1 are key transcriptional factors in the development of different retinal cell types (Xiang, 2013), and all have been widely used to achieve MG reprogramming in mice. The specific lineage gene Ascl1 can bind and open closed chromatin, allowing other classical TFs to play a role in development (Wapinski et al., 2013). In particular, Ascl1 has a binding affinity for potential Cis-regulatory regions close to neurogenic genes (VandenBosch et al., 2020). Thus, we speculate that epigenetic remodeling is essential for MG reprogramming, and that direct epigenetic manipulation of MG may be a valuable prospective approach to mouse MG reprogramming.
Epigenetic controls, including DNA methylation or acetylation, histone or non-histone modification, and non-coding RNA regulation, could maintain cellular stemness (Wang et al., 2017). Among these, histone modifications mainly include methylation, phosphorylation, acetylation, and ubiquitylation; non-coding RNA regulations include regulations of microRNA and lnRNA. The epigenetic landscape greatly affects cell development, differentiation, and cell fate determination (Aldiri et al., 2017).
Epigenetic regulation details have been well-characterized during MG development and reprogramming (Iwagawa and Watanabe, 2019; Lin et al., 2019; Jorstad et al., 2020; VandenBosch et al., 2020). MG is very similar to late stage retinal progenitor cells (RPC) in the epigenetic landscape (Dvoriantchikova et al., 2019). However, the high methylation and chromatin closure of the promoter region of genes related to neuronal development in MG cells severely hinder its reprogramming into RPC (Lin et al., 2019).

## Histone modifications
H3K4me3, H3K9me3, H3K27me3, and H3K27ac are the main histone modifications associated with transcription regulation. H3K4me3 appears to be necessary for successful cell reprogramming. Knocking out Kmt2b (a histone methyltransferase that catalyzes H3K4me3) drastically decreases the efficiency of inducing mouse embryonic fibroblasts into inducing neurons (Barbagiovanni et al., 2018). H3K27me3 and H3K9me3 are associated with transcriptional inhibition. Inhibition of H3K27me3 methyltransferase promotes mouse cardiac fibroblasts reprogramming into cardiomyocytes (Dal-Pra et al., 2017). Knockout of H3K9me3 reader RBMX or writer SUV39H1 enhances hepatic gene expression during early reprogramming (Becker et al., 2017). Histone methyltransferase inhibitor UNC0638 enhances early-stage cardiac reprogramming in mice (Hirai and Kikyo, 2014). H3K27ac facilitates gene expressions, and is enhanced by CBP/EP300 bromodomain and histone deacetylase inhibitor trichostatin A, thus promoting cellular reprogramming (Ebrahimi et al., 2019). In zebrafish, histone deacetylase affects MG reprogramming through the Her4.1-Lin28a axis (Mitra et al., 2018; A). This cumulative evidence suggests that H3K4me3, H3K27me3, H3K9me3, and H3K27ac may be epigenetic remodeling targets for MG reprogramming.
 Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) is a histone methyltransferase responsible for H3K27me3. EZH2 promotes neuronal differentiation, and its activity is crucial for the proliferation of chick MG, as it regulates TF expression related to retinal cell differentiation and development (Campbell et al., 2022). EZH2 is also the enzyme subunit and core component of polycomb repressive complex 2. EZH2 overexpression promotes ESCs and fibroblast differentiation into neurons which rely on polycomb repressive complex 2 activity (Liu et al., 2018). Lee et al. (2018) showed that miR-9/9* and miR-124 promote adult fibroblast conversion into neurons by triggering EZH2 inhibition, independent of polycomb repressive complex 2. Briefly, as a global epigenetic modulator, EZH2 is a potential target for cell reprogramming.
Inhibition of EZH2 by S-adenosylhomocysteine hydrolase suppression also promotes formation of proliferative MG, and changes chick MG fate. However, S-adenosylhomocysteine hydrolase inhibitors do not affect Ascl1 overexpression-mediated MG reprogramming in the damaged adult mouse retina. S-adenosylhomocysteine hydrolase is thus another potential target for proliferative MG formation in mice (Campbell et al., 2023). Finally, targeted epigenetic effectors and chromatin-modifying enzyme catalytic domains for chromatin markers change at specific genomic loci through small-chemical molecules; TALE or CRISPR/Cas9 systems may thus be tools for achieving MG epigenetic remodeling (Kungulovski and Jeltsch, 2016; Thakore et al., 2016).

## DNA methylation
DNA methylation plays an important role in cell fate conversions (Coppedè, 2024). Ablation of DNA methyltransferase Dnmt3a prevents mouse fibroblast reprogramming into neurons (Luo et al., 2019). Similarly, in Dnmt3l-KO mouse embryonic fibroblasts, H3K27ac is increased and H3K27me3 and H3K9me3 accumulation is decreased (Liao et al., 2015). Gene silencing mediated by Oct4 methylation prevents MG dedifferentiation in mice (Reyes-Aguirre and Lamas, 2016). Oct4, Sox2, and Klf4 coexpressed in RGCs promote axonal regeneration after injury by restoring young DNA methylation patterns. The beneficial effects of Oct4-, Sox2-, and Klf4-induced axonal regeneration require the involvement of DNA demethylases TET1 and TET2 (Lu et al., 2020).

## miRNA and lncRNA
The implications of miRNA for MG reprogramming have been highlighted in zebrafish studies. miRNAs implicated in MG reprogramming include miR-7, 9, 25, 27, 31, 124, 216a, 203, and let-7 (Konar et al., 2021). When miR-142b, 146a, 7a, 27c, and 31 are inhibited by Morpholino, zebrafish retinal proliferating cells are decreased (Quintero and Lamas, 2018). In mice, when miR-25 and miR-124 are overexpressed, or when let-7 is inhibited, approximately 40% of MG develop neuronal characteristics (Wohl et al., 2019). Inhibition and delivery (overexpression) of microRNA can be achieved in various ways, including through microRNA inhibitors, AAVs carrying interfering sequences, and CRISPR/Cas technology. Exosomes are also excellent carriers for delivering miRNA to MG, though targeted delivery of extracellular vesicles is more conducive to miRNA function.
Exploration of the role of lncRNAs in cell reprogramming was lacking, only lnc-NR2F1 was reported to reprogram mouse fibroblasts into inducing neurons (Ang et al., 2019). However, lncRNAs are significant in regeneration and iPSC reprogramming (Perry et al., 2018; Ilieva and Uchida, 2022; Liu et al., 2022), and are expected to serve as special regulators for MG reprogramming.
These cumulative studies thus provide a basis for achieving MG reprogramming in mice via direct epigenetic remodeling.

## MG reprogramming by immune regulation
Both zebrafish and mammalian immune systems include two types: innate and adaptive. Neutrophils, monocytes, microglia, macrophages complement cytokines and toll-like receptors to make up the innate immune system (Lo and Woodruff, 2020). Adaptive immune system cells include B cells, T cells, and natural killer cells (Oosterhof et al., 2014). Immune reactivity in the retina can be critically important in inflammation and infections (Yue et al., 2022). Retinal damage triggers immune cells to activate and recruit, releasing cytokines, soluble factors, and chemokines (Nagashima and Hitchcock, 2021).

## Microglia-mediated MG reprogramming
Microglia, as the retinal intrinsic macrophages, are involved in almost all immune responses, especially inflammation reactions (Liu et al., 2023). Retinal damage produces a series of inflammatory reactions, leading to microglia activation. Reactive microglia are also required for other inflammation-related signaling pathways and cytokines ( B).
In zebrafish, retinal regeneration requires acute inflammation to initiate and promote the development of progenitor cells derived from MG in response to retinal injury. After MG reprogramming is completed, microglia instantly evacuate the damaged area and quickly reinstate normal retinal homeostasis (Iribarne, 2021). In mammals, microglia are continuously and excessively activated during the period of retinal injury and degeneration, resulting in chronic inflammation and accelerating retinal photoreceptor cell apoptosis and MG neuroglial proliferation (Yu et al., 2020a). It is therefore crucial to mimic zebrafish-like dynamics in mammals, regulating microglia to exhibit anti-inflammatory phenotypes that promote MG reprogramming, instead of sustained proinflammatory and neuroglial proliferation phenotypes.
Csf1r inhibitors, including PLX3397 and PLX5622, are excellent tools for modulating microglia behavior (Chadarevian et al., 2023). Additionally, Csf1r-knockdown or pan-caspase inhibitor and ZVAD-fmk are other approaches to suppressing microglia accumulation (Mitra et al., 2022). In zebrafish models with chemical or mechanical photoreceptor damage, depleting or inhibiting microglia functions by immune suppressants (i.e., PLX3397) blocks MG regeneration, an effect reversed by microglial repopulation (White et al., 2017; Conedera et al., 2019). In contrast, Cheng et al. (2023) demonstrated that when mouse retina microglia are cleared by intermittently administering PLX3397, over-activated microglia under inflammatory conditions are manipulated to repopulate into the retina with a phenotype closer to steady-state microglia. Repopulated microglia also increased the number of MG-derived retinal progenitors. In addition, according to Todd et al. (2020), mouse retinal MG reprogramming is facilitated by microglial ablation. Obviously, the effect of microglia on MG reprogramming exhibits species diversity, altering microglia phenotype may thus be an effective strategy for promoting MG reprogramming.
Identifying the inflammatory molecular signals that microglia regulate in zebrafish stem cell functioning will improve treatment strategies for repairing damaged mammalian tissues. In addition, technological advancements in molecular biology, imaging, and single-cell analysis will support studying the dynamic responses of microglia to CNS damage, enhancing the potential for microglia-based treatments of CNS diseases. Better understanding communications between microglia and MG will allow us to indirectly regulate MG reprogramming by regulating the function of microglia. In particular, we need to further distinguish the differences in microglia phenotypes, and develop drugs specifically targeting disease-related microglia instead of normal microglia, shifting the balance towards beneficial microglia responses.

## Molecular mechanism of immune-mediated MG reprogramming
Many signaling pathways are related to cytokine-mediated immune responses, including JAK-STAT, NF-κB, mTOR, and TNF-α. JAK-STAT is a classical pathway in MG reprogramming. In zebrafish, STAT3 is required for MG reprogramming (Gorsuch and Hyde, 2014; Wan et al., 2014). The JAK/STAT signaling pathway is activated by leptin and IL-6 family cytokines that induce MG proliferation (Zhao et al., 2014). In chicks, inhibiting the phosphorylation of JAK or STAT3 decreases formation of proliferative MG (Todd et al., 2016). In contrast, activating STAT in mice weakens the ability of Ascl1 to reprogram MG into retinal neurons (Jorstad et al., 2020).
The typical NF-κB pathway involves in innate immune response and promotes expression of proinflammatory cytokines (Yu et al., 2020b). NF-κB activation in MG leads to an inflammatory response and enables MG to enter reprogramming, while NF-κB continuous activation inhibits MG proliferation in chicks and mice. Once microglia are eliminated, NF-κB activation is reversed, resulting in increased MG proliferation (Palazzo et al., 2020, 2022). Midkine, commonly referred to as a heparin-binding growth factor, is a potential contributor to acute and chronic inflammatory processes (Aynacıoğlu et al., 2019). Inhibiting NF-κB pathway activation with midkine knockdown is especially effective (Zhang et al., 2022), affecting generation of progenitor cells in the MG-derived retinas of zebrafish, chicks, and mice (Nagashima et al., 2020; Campbell et al., 2021b).
The immune response promotes regeneration of zebrafish retina in an mTOR-dependent manner, and mTOR is necessary for both MG dedifferentiation and proliferation (Zhang et al., 2020). A critical issue to address is how to determine the key factors that stimulate retinal regeneration and activate mTOR in inflammatory cells.
Furthermore, MG proliferation depends on proinflammatory cytokine TNF-α/TNF-r1 signaling in the damaged zebrafish retina (Nguyen-Chi et al., 2017). Retinal regeneration is also mediated by circulating regulatory T-like cells (Hui et al., 2017). Other cytokines, leukotrienes, and histamines are also targets for acute and chronic inflammation regulation. Their potential roles in mammalian retinal regeneration require further research.

## MG reprogramming through metabolism modulation
Due to metabolic differences between starting and target cells, cell fate transition requires metabolic changes. Metabolic remodeling is important for cell reprogramming (Zheng et al., 2016; Cliff and Dalton, 2017). Over the past decade, metabolic changes have been shown to determine stem cell fate, with functions similar to those of transcriptional networks (Bahat and Gross, 2019). Removing metabolic barriers may enhance direct neuronal reprogramming efficiency (Gascon et al., 2016), revealing that targeted regulation of stem cell metabolism could promote post-injury adult tissue regeneration.
A metabolic transition from low-to-high glycolysis may be necessary for MG cell fate conversion, since high glycolysis contributes to maintaining stemness (Tsogtbaatar et al., 2020). This may also be true of less-to-more oxidative phosphorylation (Magistretti and Allaman, 2015) and more-to-less lipid peroxidation, all of which occurs when neurons are reprogrammed (Gascon et al., 2016).
Antioxidant agents, including enzymatic and small-molecular antioxidants are widely used in metabolic regulation. Gascon et al. used ferroptosis inhibitors (Liproxstatin-1 or vitamin E), antioxidants (e.g., forskolin), and Bcl-2 co-transfected with vitamin D receptor to improve generation of direct in vivo neuronal reprogramming. Various agents that can inhibit lipid peroxidation (e.g., αToc, αT3, and Calcitriol) are also helpful for neuronal reprogramming (Gascon et al., 2016). Fullerol, as a nanomaterial, exhibits excellent broad-spectrum antioxidant activity and is widely used in oxidative stress-related diseases, including neurodegenerative diseases (Cha et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023). Thus, single or combined use of antioxidants and other drugs regulating MG metabolism may be feasible methods for achieving MG reprogramming.
Besides modulating metabolic state and metabolites, mitochondrial structure and function also influence cellular reprogramming (Bahat and Gross, 2019; Polyzos et al., 2019). By producing reactive oxygen species and tricarboxylic acid cycle metabolites, regulating the NAD+/NADH ratio, metabolizing pyruvate metabolism, and controlling mitochondrial dynamics, mitochondria determine the fate of mammalian stem cells ( C). Previous work has demonstrated that mitochondrial inner membrane protein (IMMT) participates in the production of iPSCs. During nuclear reprogramming, IMMT is significantly up-regulated. When down-regulating IMMT with shRNA suppression, nuclear reprogramming is inhibited (Liu et al., 2013). Mitochondrial transplantation has both neuroprotective properties and enables retinal stem cells reprogramming into RGCs (Nascimento-Dos-Santos et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022). Mahato et al. (2020) demonstrated that mitochondria-to-nucleus signaling acts as a direct chemical reprogramming mediator. Extracellular vesicles as independent, extracellular metabolic units can also convey cargo to alter concentrations of critical metabolites and reprogram metabolic pathways, thereby affecting the physiology of their microenvironment (Fridman et al., 2022). It is possible to regulate the MG metabolic microenvironment by transplanting exosomes carrying specific metabolites. However, MG reprogramming by mitochondrial or extracellular vesicle transplantation has yet to be achieved.
Research in metabolic reprogramming of stem and cancer cells has contributed insights into MG reprogramming in mice, gradually clarifying the metabolic trajectories of starting and ending cells.

## MG reprogramming through chemical small-molecules regulation
Small-molecules play an important role in cellular signal transduction and epigenetic modifications, as their size and excellent cell permeability allow them to target cell surface proteins (Wang et al., 2023). Small-molecules change somatic cell identities not only by direct reprogramming (Zhang et al., 2015; Mahato et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2021) but by creating iPSCs from somatic cells in mice and humans (Hou et al., 2013; Guan et al., 2022; Liuyang et al., 2023).
Guan et al. (2022) achieved chemical reprogramming from somatic cells to iPSC by designing small-molecules to replace Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and c-Myc ( D). Li et al. (2015) and Ma et al. (2021) also used cAMP agonist (i.e., Forskolin), GSK3β inhibitor (i.e., CHIR), neurogenic molecules (i.e., ISX9), and BET-bromodomain inhibitor (i.e., I-BET151), effectively establishing a chemical strategy to transform adult mouse fibroblasts or astrocytes into neurons ( D). Canonical signal transduction proteins, such as Wnt, SHH, MAPK, and epigenetic regulators like DNA methyltransferases and histone deacetylases, can be directly regulated by small molecules (Wang et al., 2023). Human astrocytes have been reprogramed into neurons through epigenetic and transcriptional regulation using a mixture of nine small molecules (Zhang et al., 2015). Thus, synergistically targeting cell signaling pathways and epigenetic modifiers is an effective chemical reprogramming approach.
Zhang et al. (2023) proposed that it is feasible to regenerate MG from mammalian retina into neurons using small-molecule cocktails. They used the drug DMX-5804 to activate YAP and reprogrammed MG to an RPC state, and then RPC-like MG expressed makers of amacrine and RGC after drug withdrawal. Their results introduced a novel pharmaceutical-based method for in vivo MG reprogramming in mice. Using the sci-Plex method, thousands of single nucleus RNA-seq conditions can be used in a single experiment (Srivatsan et al., 2020). Tresenrider et al. (2023) used sci-Plex to develop a platform for screening small-molecules that affect the reprogramming of MGs mediated by Ascl1, identifying 92 compounds and validating two small-molecule compounds that promote in vivo neurogenesis.
Within the field of manipulating MG fate, these chemical reprogramming studies have opened new frontiers and promoted the development of strategies for reprogramming mouse MG. Compared with genome editing technology, small chemical molecules are not integrated into the genome, and are highly controllable and efficient; they are thus easy to optimize, standardize, and manufacture, facilitating cocktail use (Wang et al., 2023). Thus, using small chemical molecules for MG reprogramming may be ideal for cell fate manipulation. However, the blood-retinal-barrier presents a major obstacle to small molecules entering the retina, and the risk of off-target effects must be considered when developing smallmolecule drugs (Zhang et al., 2023).

## MG reprogramming through extracellular matrix remodeling
The ECM of the eye includes the basement membrane (BM) and non-BM matrices. The BM contains the inner limiting membrane and Bruch’s membrane. The non-BM matrix includes the photoreceptor stroma surrounding the photoreceptor outer segment and filling the subretinal space, as well as the synaptic stroma surrounding the synapse (Serjanov and Hyde, 2023). MG are a primary source of ECM secretion.
In the contexts of disease, injury, retinal de-differentiation, and regeneration, the ECM is accompanied by extensive remodeling processes. ECM mechanical signals regulate reprogramming of somatic cells into stem cells in vitro, though the mechanism of this process still needs to be fully explored (Liu et al., 2021). Most ECM molecules promote glial scar formation, and create an inhibitory environment for retinal regeneration (Reinhard et al., 2015). Refilled microglia may modulate ECM remodeling to produce MG progenitor-like features (Cheng et al., 2023).
With soft substrates, cardiac reprogramming is more efficient by suppressing YAP/TAZ (Kurotsu et al., 2020). Stiffer matrices increase Sox2, Oct6, and Krox20 expressions, whereas c-Jun expression is reduced, indicating a link between substrate mechanical sensing and TF regulation in Schwann cells (Rosso et al., 2022). It has been reported that ECM-activated integrin signaling regulated stem cell self-renewal, proliferation via phosphoinositide 3-kinase activation (Wang and Xie, 2014).
Thus, though evidence of ECM-mediated MG reprogramming is relatively scarce, the microenvironment surrounding retinal cells influences MG reprogramming processes. Greater understanding of its involvement will require further exploration ( E).

## MG reprogramming through cell–cell fusion
A novel MG non-targeted regulatory approach, based on interactions between retinal cells, has emerged in recent years. Cell–cell fusion can substantially change gene expression, which can reset expression of pluripotent stem genes and completely reverse the lineage limitations of somatic identity (Brown and Fisher, 2021). In 2016 and 2018, Sanges et al. and Pesaresi et al., respectively, transplanted hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, and bone marrow cells, respectively, into mouse retina to drive reprogrammed MG toward a photoreceptor progenitor, or ganglion and amacrine cell, fates, respectively ( F). These studies suggest that cell-cell interaction is an innovative approach to accomplishing MG reprogramming. Microglia can regulate MG reprogramming through ablation or repopulation in zebrafish and mice (White et al., 2017; Conedera et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2023). Loss of gamma-aminobutyric acid released by horizontal cells from photoreceptor death also initiates MG reprogramming (Rao et al., 2017). Finally, Mitra et al. (2022) found that microglia/macrophage and vascular endothelial cells control MG reprogramming and proliferation through VEGF and Notch signaling pathways.

## Limitations
Herein, only MG reprogramming strategies in mice were reviewed. A comprehensive review of the MG reprogramming strategies in other mammals, such as primates, was beyond the current scope. Nor was an in-depth discussion of the mechanism of MG reprogramming included.

## Conclusion
During the past decade, inspiring developments have occurred in several complementary strategies to investigate the MG reprogramming process in adult mice. TF involvement in retinal development and cell identity were first discovered and studied for their reprogramming potentials. To date, relatively successful MG reprogramming strategies in mice have been achieved, and it is now understood that MG directly change identity from glia to retinal neurons. To encourage further research on MG reprogramming, we have reviewed several ways to identify more TFs and potential strategies for MG reprogramming in mice. These new MG reprogramming strategies are not independent, and each has a unique focus. Collaborative use of multiple strategies may be a trend in future MG reprogramming strategies.
However, specific challenges to MG reprogramming in mice remain to be resolved. Improving reprogramming efficiency and stability, and connecting MG-derived neurons to the retinal circuitry, are next-steps for MG reprogramming in mice. Indeed, MG have the characteristics of RPCs and neurogenic potential during development (Ahmad et al., 2020). This MG subset is in a resting state in the mature retina and can initiate reprogramming under certain conditions (Hoang et al., 2020). The low proportion of MG cells with neurogenic potential may be why mammalian reprogramming efficiency is low. Currently, there is a lack of research on how to identify and increase the proportion of MG subgroups with reprogramming capacity. In vivo reprogramming is more stable when antioxidants and survival factors are used, and regenerated neurons have a greater chance of surviving shortly after injury (when transformation occurs) in a reactive oxygen species-rich environment. Induced neurons can survive stably for more than 4 weeks in a damaged environment when treated with antioxidants combined with Neurog2 and Bcl-2 protein expression (Gascon et al., 2016). When inhibiting the p53 pathway and using BDNF and Noggin therapy, induced neurons have survived up to 8 weeks in an environment with spinal cord hemisection injury (Hachem et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2016). Therefore, improving the damaged retinal microenvironment benefits the stable survival of MG reprogrammed neurons. Newborn neurons migrate to the correct retinal layer, extend dendrites/axons, and form synaptic connections with neural circuitry in the host retina, a process termed “integration” (Jin et al., 2019; Blackshaw and Sanes, 2021). Connecting MG-derived neurons to established networks or forming de novo circuits in pathological environments with inflammation, cell loss, and cell remodeling is a huge challenge. Two reports using in vivo patch clamp electrophysiology have shown that newly regenerated bipolar neurons integrate into the host circuit (Jorstad et al., 2017; Yao et al., 2018). However, integration may also be misunderstood by material transfer (Pearson et al., 2016). More experiments are needed to promote and confirm the integration of MG-derived neurons into host retina circuitry. According to fish model data, the reconstructed retina need not match the normal retina to restore animal vision; rather, retinal photoreceptors and internal circuits must reach a sufficient level of regeneration. Future research is expected to refine functional recovery and visual behavior assessments.
Stable MG reprogramming protocols in mice are important for identifying and capturing the intermediate and final states of MG-to-neuron transformation, and for conducting comprehensive, detailed identification of MG-derived neurons. Some MG reprogramming strategies have been highly controversial regarding their technology adoption (Hoang et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2022, 2023). More efficient, specific tools will be needed to trace MG cell fate in vivo (Wang et al., 2021b; Gao et al., 2022). Finally, building successful collaborations between tool creators, modelers, and experimenters will be essential for developing glial biology, and is expected to spark significant advances in the study of MG reprogramming.
