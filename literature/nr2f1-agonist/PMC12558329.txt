## Abstract
The role of calcium metabolism-related genes (CMRGs) in gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is unclear, and potential biomarkers remain unidentified. This study aims to identify CMRGs as potential biomarkers for GDM and explore their functional roles, diagnostic performance, and associations with immune infiltration and signaling pathways in the pathogenesis of GDM. We conducted a retrospective transcriptomic and experimental validation study, integrating bioinformatics analyses from GEO datasets and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) validation. Candidate genes were identified by intersecting differentially expressed genes from 2 GDM-related GEO datasets (GSE154414 and GSE9984) with 178 known CMRGs. Biomarker potential was assessed through receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, consistency of gene expression across datasets, and functional enrichment analysis. Expression of selected genes was validated by qRT-PCR. Further mechanistic insights were obtained through immune infiltration analysis, regulatory network construction, drug prediction, and gene-disease association studies. Eight candidate genes were identified from the intersection of 649 differentially expressed genes and CMRGs. Among these, AGTR1 and ADCY4 were consistently downregulated in placental tissues of GDM patients and demonstrated strong diagnostic performance (area under the curve > 0.7). qRT-PCR validation confirmed significantly decreased expression levels of both genes. Functional enrichment analysis indicated involvement in ribosomal, lysosomal, and calcium signaling pathways. Immune infiltration analysis revealed a significant increase in activated B cells in GDM placentas, which were negatively correlated with AGTR1 (r = –0.738) and ADCY4 (r = –0.881) expression. Regulatory network analysis associated these genes with pathways including cAMP metabolism and vascular smooth muscle contraction. A total of 59 potential therapeutic compounds were identified. AGTR1 and ADCY4 are promising biomarkers for GDM, likely contributing to disease progression through dysregulation of calcium signaling and the immune microenvironment. These findings offer a theoretical foundation for further mechanistic studies and targeted therapeutic development for GDM.

## 1. Introduction
Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is the most common metabolic disorder in mid- to late-gestation, with a global prevalence ranging from 10% to 20%. It is strongly associated with an increased risk of developing maternal type 2 diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome in offspring later in life.[ ] The core pathophysiological mechanisms include placental hormone-mediated insulin resistance during pregnancy and a relative deficiency in pancreatic β-cell function. However, the risk factors, etiological mechanisms, and intervention criteria for GDM have not been fully defined.[ ] Therefore, understanding the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying GDM and identifying specific biomarkers are crucial for advancing clinical interventions and management strategies.
Calcium ions (Ca2+), acting as second messengers, play a pivotal role in regulating insulin secretion, glucolipid metabolism, and inflammation through molecules such as calmodulin and calcium-dependent protein kinases.[ ] Dysregulation of calcium signaling is implicated in the development of GDM by affecting pancreatic β-cell function. For instance, downregulation of the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) impairs glucose-stimulated insulin secretion and reduces islet cell proliferation, leading to islet dysfunction.[ ] Additionally, abnormal CaSR expression in the placenta can disrupt maternal-fetal calcium homeostasis, further exacerbating metabolic imbalances.[ ] Yao Wang et al demonstrated that calcium channel blockers can protect β-cells by alleviating endoplasmic reticulum stress and reducing β-cell apoptosis.[ ] Meanwhile, vitamin D enhances insulin secretion by promoting calcium influx and upregulating the expression of voltage-dependent calcium channels.[ ] These mechanisms highlight that calcium signaling is essential for β-cell function, with its regulation being context-dependent: under pathological stress, calcium channel blockers prevent excessive calcium accumulation, whereas vitamin D supports physiological calcium influx to promote insulin secretion. Together, these mechanisms suggest that calcium signaling may play a central role in the pathogenesis of GDM by affecting β-cell function and survival.
Notably, micronutrient deficiencies, including calcium, are prevalent during pregnancy, particularly in developing countries.[ ] It has been shown that calcium deficiency is associated with an increased risk of developing GDM.[ ] However, there remains a lack of in-depth studies investigating how altered expression of calcium metabolism-related genes (CMRGs) influences the progression of GDM. This knowledge gap limits the translational application of calcium metabolism-targeting strategies in GDM prevention and treatment. Consequently, comprehensive bioinformatics analysis represents a promising approach to exploring CMRGs and their mechanisms in GDM.
In this study, we identified CMRGs through differential expression analysis and functional enrichment using GDM transcriptome data from the GEO database. Further analysis elucidated the roles of these genes in the immune microenvironment, drug targeting, and regulatory networks, and we validated the expression profiles of key biomarkers by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). This study not only deepens our understanding of calcium metabolism in GDM but also highlights the potential role of related biomarkers in GDM development, offering new perspectives for the exploration of targeted therapeutic strategies based on calcium metabolism.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Study design and data acquisition
This case-control bioinformatics study aimed to identify GDM-related biomarkers through analysis of publicly available gene expression datasets and experimental validation. GDM-related datasets were obtained from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds). The training set, GSE154414 (GPL20301), included placental tissue samples from 4 GDM patients and 4 controls. GSE9984 (GPL570) comprised placental tissue samples from 4 GDM patients and 8 controls. Additionally, 178 CMRGs were sourced from published literature.[ ]

## 2.2. Differential expression analysis and identification of candidate genes
Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were first identified in the GSE154414 dataset using the limma package (v1.38.0) with |log2
FC| > 0.5 and P-value < .05 as the thresholds.[ ] Candidate genes were selected by intersecting these DEGs with CMRGs. To explore the biological functions and pathways associated with the candidate genes, gene ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional enrichment analyses were conducted using the clusterProfiler package (v4.7.1.001).[ ] The top 20 significant results were visualized. Furthermore, functional similarity between candidate genes was assessed using the Friends analysis via the GOSemSim package (v2.26.1).[ ]

## 2.3. Identification of biomarkers
To evaluate the predictive performance of the candidate genes for GDM, receiver operating characteristic curves were generated for both the GSE154414 and GSE9984 datasets. Genes with an area under the curve (AUC) value > 0.7 in both datasets were considered potential biomarkers. Subsequently, the expression levels of these genes in both datasets were analyzed. Biomarkers were selected based on consistent expression trends across datasets and significant differences between groups.

## 2.4. Functional analysis of biomarkers
To further investigate the related functions of the biomarkers, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the clusterProfiler package (v4.7.1.001).[ ] Spearman correlation analysis was performed between each biomarker and all genes in the training set, with the correlation coefficients serving as the ranking criteria for GSEA. The reference gene set used for GSEA was the KEGG background gene set from the MSigDB database.

## 2.5. Immune infiltration analysis
To explore the role of immune cells in GDM, GSVA (v1.48.2) was employed to calculate the enrichment scores of 28 immune cell types in each sample from the training set, using immune-related genes from the literature[ ] as the background gene set.[ ] Wilcoxon tests were used to compare the differences in immune cell abundance between the control and GDM groups. Finally, Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the relationships between differential immune cells and biomarkers.

## 2.6. Regulatory network analysis of biomarkers
Genes and signaling pathways associated with the biomarker functions were predicted using the GeneMANIA database, and their gene–gene interaction networks were constructed. The expression levels of biomarkers across various tissues were explored using the BioGPS database, meanwhile, standardized mean mRNA expression values were extracted for major organs/tissues, with tissue-specific expression patterns visualized through bar graphs generated. And biomarker-organ localization networks were constructed based on tissues with higher-than-average mRNA expression. To explore the regulatory mechanisms of biomarkers, their corresponding miRNAs and transcription factors (TFs) were predicted using the NetworkAnalyst and JASPAR databases, respectively. MiRNA-mRNA and TF-gene networks were visualized using Cytoscape software (v3.9.1).[ ]

## 2.7. Drug prediction and gene-disease association analysis
To identify potential drugs targeting the biomarkers, drug-target predictions were performed using the DGIdb database. Associations between genes and diseases were explored using the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database, where the inference score reflects the correlation between a gene and a disease, with higher values indicating stronger associations.

## 2.8. Expression validation of biomarkers
The expression levels of biomarkers in the training set were validated by qRT-PCR. A total of 5 paired samples from Jiangbin Hospital, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, were used, including 5 GDM samples and 5 age- and gestational week-matched control samples. In this study, “paired samples” refers to samples from women of similar age (±3 years) and gestational week (±1 week), with baseline characteristics matched to minimize confounding effects. The clinical characteristics of all paired samples, including age, gestational week, BMI, and blood glucose levels, are presented in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q421. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Jiangbin Hospital, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (approval number: LW2025-006). All participants signed written informed consent forms. Total RNA was extracted from frozen placental tissue using TRIzol reagent, and RNA quality was assessed with a NanoPhotometer N50. An equal amount of RNA was reverse-transcribed to synthesize cDNA, and qRT-PCR was performed. Gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Details of primer sequences, accession numbers and exon/intron locations, are listed in Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q421.

## 2.9. Statistical analysis
Bioinformatics analyses were performed using R software (v4.2.2). Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < .05.

## 3. Results

## 3.1. Identification of 8 candidate genes from DEGs and CMRGs
In the GSE154414 dataset, 649 DEGs were identified, including 525 upregulated and 124 downregulated genes (Fig. A, B). These DEGs were intersected with 178 CMRGs, yielding 8 candidate genes (Fig. C). Gene ontology enrichment analysis revealed that the candidate genes were associated with 420 functional terms, with key pathways including adenylate cyclase (AC)-activating G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling, cyclase activity, and lysosomal lumen (Figs. D and S1A, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q422). Additionally, the candidate genes were involved in 64 signaling pathways, such as adrenergic signaling in cardiomyocytes and the phospholipase D signaling pathway (Figs. E and S1B, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q422). The Friends analysis showed a high degree of functional similarity between the candidate genes, with the exception of PTGFR (Fig. F).
 

## 3.2. AGTR1 and ADCY4 as biomarkers for GDM
In both the GSE154414 and GSE9984 datasets, the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis of 5 genes – CAMK2D, AGTR1, PTGFR, ADCY4, and ADCY7 – showed that all 5 genes had an AUC value >0.7, indicating good diagnostic performance for GDM (Fig. A, B). Among them, AGTR1 and ADCY4 exhibited consistent expression patterns across both datasets and were significantly more highly expressed in the control group (Fig. C, D). Therefore, AGTR1 and ADCY4 were considered as potential biomarkers for GDM.
 

## 3.3. Biomarkers involved in ribosomal, lysosomal, and calcium signaling pathways
Further functional analysis revealed that both AGTR1 and ADCY4 were significantly enriched in the ribosome, lysosome, and calcium signaling pathways. Additionally, AGTR1 was notably enriched in the extracellular matrix (ECM) receptor interaction and basal cell carcinoma pathways. Similarly, ADCY4 was implicated in vascular smooth muscle contraction and VEGF signaling (Fig. A, B).
 

## 3.4. Correlation of biomarkers with activated B cells
In the immune infiltration analysis, enrichment scores for 28 immune cell types were calculated (Fig. A). Activated B cells were significantly upregulated in the GDM group and were identified as a key cell type in the context of GDM (Fig. B). A significant negative correlation was found between activated B cells and both AGTR1 and ADCY4, with correlation coefficients of −0.7381 and −0.881, respectively (Fig. C).
 

## 3.5. Construction of regulatory networks for biomarkers
A gene–gene interaction network was constructed, highlighting the top 20 genes and 7 signaling pathways associated with the biomarker functions. Key genes included AGTRAP, ANGPT2, and AGT, while signaling pathways such as cAMP metabolism and phospho-oxygen lyase activity were also identified (Fig. A). Additionally, the biomarker-organ localization network revealed relationships such as ADCY4-Tongue and AGTR1-Appendix (Fig. B). Subsequent profiling showed that AGTR1 was highly expressed in the placenta, adipocytes, fetal liver, and adult liver, whereas ADCY4 exhibited elevated levels in the adrenal cortex, adrenal gland, caudate nucleus, fetal thyroid, and thalamus (Fig. S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q422). MiRNA predictions indicated 12 miRNAs interacting with AGTR1 and 3 miRNAs with ADCY4, forming regulatory relationships, including hsa-miR-155-5p-AGTR1 and hsa-miR-146a-5p-ADCY4 (Fig. C). Furthermore, 13 TFs corresponding to AGTR1 and 4 TFs for ADCY4 were predicted, forming networks such as USF1-AGTR1, MAX-AGTR1, EN1-ADCY4, and NR2F1-ADCY4 (Fig. D).
 

## 3.6. Drug and disease predictions for biomarkers
Drug-target prediction via the DGIdb identified 59 potential drugs associated with AGTR1, including SAPRISARTAN and EPROSARTAN (Fig. A). No drugs were predicted for ADCY4. Additionally, diseases related to the biomarkers included weight loss and necrosis (Fig. B).
 

## 3.7. Validation of AGTR1 and ADCY4 expression
The total RNA extraction from placental tissues yielded RNA concentrations within the standard range (Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q421). The qRT-PCR analysis revealed that the mRNA expression levels of AGTR1 and ADCY4 were significantly lower in the GDM group compared to the control group (P < .001; Fig. A, B). These results were consistent with the expression patterns observed in the GSE154414 and GSE9984 datasets.
 

## 4. Discussion
GDM, a common complication of pregnancy, is primarily associated with insulin resistance and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction.[ ] In recent years, increasing attention has been directed toward the role of calcium metabolism abnormalities in metabolic disorders[ ]; however, the specific mechanisms by which CMRGs contribute to GDM remain poorly understood. In this study, we systematically identified 2 CMRGs, AGTR1 and ADCY4, that are consistently downregulated in placental tissues from patients with GDM. Unlike previous investigations that have predominantly focused on individual calcium transporter proteins such as TRPV5,[ ] our analysis explored the involvement of these genes across multiple biological dimensions, including calcium signaling, immune modulation, and metabolic pathways. Our findings suggest that AGTR1 and ADCY4 not only regulate key biological pathways but may also serve as novel biomarkers for GDM, offering a theoretical foundation for improved diagnostic precision and targeted therapeutic interventions.
We initially identified 8 candidate genes through an intersection analysis of DEGs and CMRGs. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these genes are primarily involved in the AC-activating GPCR signaling pathway, suggesting a potential role in mediating β-cell dysfunction via regulation of cAMP and calcium signaling pathways. Previous studies have shown that GPCRs can inhibit AC activity, leading to reduced cAMP and calcium signaling, ultimately suppressing insulin secretion through the Gαi/o signaling axis.[ ] Additionally, enrichment related to lysosomal luminal function suggests that disrupted calcium homeostasis may contribute to GDM pathogenesis through lysosomal autophagy defects,[ ] although the exact mechanisms remain to be experimentally validated. KEGG pathway analysis further demonstrated enrichment in several key pathways, including calcium signaling, adrenergic signaling, and gap junction pathways. These findings indicate that the candidate genes may collectively influence GDM pathogenesis by modulating metabolic, endocrine, and intercellular communication processes. The enrichment of calcium signaling pathways underscores the central role of calcium dysregulation in impaired insulin secretion. Excess intracellular calcium has been shown to disrupt calcium homeostasis in β-cells, inhibit insulin release, and form Ca²+-phosphatidylinositol (Ca²+-PIP) complexes that hinder membrane localization of essential signaling molecules, exacerbating insulin resistance.[ ] Moreover, activation of adrenergic signaling implies a possible contribution of sympathetic nervous system dysregulation in GDM. Specifically, β2 receptor-mediated sympathetic overactivation has been reported to disrupt the circadian rhythm of insulin secretion.[ ] Similarly, abnormalities in the gap junction pathway may impair electrical coupling between β-cells, further disrupting insulin secretion rhythms and promoting GDM development.[ ] Collectively, these results suggest that calcium signaling may influence islet function through both intracellular and intercellular mechanisms.
Next, we further evaluated the potential biomarker value of AGTR1 and ADCY4 by integrating multiple datasets, validating through RT-qPCR (P < .01), and assessing diagnostic efficacy. Notably, both AGTR1 and ADCY4 exhibited AUC values >0.87, suggesting strong potential for early detection and diagnosis of GDM. Compared with previously reported GDM-associated markers, such as hsa_circRNA_0039480 (AUC = 0.704), gCD59 (AUC = 0.681), and FPG (AUC = 0.80),[ – ]
AGTR1 and ADCY4 demonstrated higher diagnostic accuracy. Therefore, our study provides important clinical reference for early GDM detection by introducing AGTR1 and ADCY4. GSEA enrichment analysis revealed that both genes are involved in ribosomal, lysosomal, and calcium signaling pathways, suggesting that they may work synergistically to regulate protein synthesis, calcium homeostasis, and cellular degradation. The co-enrichment of the ribosomal pathway specifically suggests that these genes may influence insulin maturation through the regulation of protein synthesis. Notably, genetic variants in ADCY4 have been associated with defects in proinsulin conversion,[ ] while downregulation of AGTR1 disrupts the inhibition of S6K1, leading to its over-activation and contributing to GDM pathology.[ ] It is hypothesized that AGTR1 and ADCY4 may collaborate to exacerbate islet dysfunction through modulation of translational and posttranslational modifications. Furthermore, AGTR1 and ADCY4 exhibit functional complementarity within placental mechanisms. AGTR1, a key component of the renin-angiotensin system, was significantly enriched in the ECM receptor pathway, suggesting a role in maintaining placental structural integrity.[ ] This may contribute to insulin resistance in GDM through altered ECM deposition and vascular network imbalance. In contrast, ADCY4 was enriched in VEGF signaling and vascular smooth muscle contractile pathways, suggesting its involvement in regulating placental blood flow and vasodilatory function via the cAMP-Ca²+ signaling axis.[ ] Together, these findings highlight the complementary roles of AGTR1 and ADCY4 in structural maintenance and blood flow regulation, potentially driving placental pathology in GDM.
At the immune level, we observed significant enrichment of activated B cells in GDM placental tissues, accompanied by a strong negative correlation with AGTR1 and ADCY4 expression. This finding suggests that calcium signaling dysregulation may influence immune-metabolic homeostasis through B-cell infiltration. B cells are known to activate inflammatory responses and impair insulin sensitivity via antibody production and interactions with T cells and macrophages.[ ] However, whether calcium signaling mediates the immune-metabolic mechanism in GDM through B-cell activity remains to be further investigated.
Further regulatory network analysis revealed the tissue specificity of AGTR1 and ADCY4 in GDM, along with their regulatory mechanisms. Organ localization analysis showed that both AGTR1 and ADCY4 are specifically expressed in the appendix, as well as in the placenta, liver, and adipose tissue, suggesting that they may influence the systemic inflammatory response in GDM through multiple organs. The appendix plays a crucial role in maintaining intestinal microbiota homeostasis and mucosal immunity, while pregnancy-related intestinal dysbiosis is believed to exacerbate immune inflammation and reduce insulin sensitivity. Gut microbiota plays a key role in metabolism and immune-metabolic regulation during pregnancy.[ ] Dysbiosis can promote insulin resistance by activating the immune system and inducing chronic low-grade inflammation. Certain microbial metabolites, such as short-chain fatty acids, regulate inflammatory responses and help maintain insulin sensitivity; however, dysbiosis may alter the production of these metabolites, thereby enhancing inflammation and impairing insulin signaling.[ ] In addition, dysbiosis may interfere with the function of CaSRs, affecting calcium signaling-related pathways, which could further exacerbate local immune and inflammatory responses and disrupt gut homeostasis.[ ] Based on these insights, we hypothesize that alterations in gut microbiota along the appendix-gut axis may indirectly regulate the expression and function of genes such as AGTR1 and ADCY4 by modulating local or systemic calcium signaling. Future studies combining 16S rRNA sequencing and maternal-fetal transcriptomics could further validate the potential relationships among gut microbiota, calcium signaling, and GDM. Transcriptional regulatory network analysis indicated that AGTR1 is regulated by miR-155-5p, which has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity in GDM trophoblast cells.[ ]
ADCY4 is closely associated with the GATA2 TF, which is involved in trophoblast senescence,[ ] suggesting that ADCY4 may impact placental development by regulating cell fate, further refining its regulatory network in the pathogenesis of GDM.
In terms of drug prediction, our analysis suggests that AGTR1-targeted interventions hold potential translational value. In this study, we not only uncovered novel mechanisms of action for vitamin C and dexamethasone, but also identified promising new strategies, such as TRV-120027 and antisense oligonucleotides. Vitamin C has been shown to alleviate oxidative stress and mitigate high glucose-induced placental injury,[ ] while low-dose dexamethasone modulates dendritic cell calcium homeostasis, thereby inhibiting immune-inflammatory responses,[ ] providing a potential translational intervention for GDM. TRV-120027, a β-arrestin-biased AGT1R ligand, can block the pathogenic GPCR pathway while simultaneously activating protective signaling pathways.[ ] Additionally, antisense oligonucleotide (CHEMBL1390716) demonstrates highly specific gene regulatory potential.[ ] These findings not only expand the therapeutic targets for GDM but also pave the way for translating mechanistic research into clinical applications. However, the clinical translational value of these interventions must be systematically validated in experimental models.
Finally, disease phenotype prediction analysis revealed that ADCY4 was strongly associated with “delayed effects of prenatal exposure” and “inflammation,” while AGTR1 was more strongly linked to “hypertension” and “kidney disease.” Both genes were associated with “weight loss” and “tissue necrosis,” suggesting a synergistic relationship between placental metabolic disorders and cellular damage.[ ] Overall, ADCY4 primarily influences placental developmental regulation, while AGTR1 plays a central role in systemic metabolism and vascular regulation. Together, these genes contribute to the complex pathology of GDM.
The main strength of this study lies in the use of cross-validation across multiple datasets, combined with experimental validation, to confirm the stable diagnostic value of AGTR1 and ADCY4 in GDM. Compared with previous studies, our innovative approach integrates calcium metabolism gene analysis, immune microenvironment exploration, and regulatory network construction, overcoming the limitations of traditional single-pathway studies. This approach provides a new perspective for comprehensively understanding the role of CMRGs in GDM. Methodologically, independent cohort validation and functional enrichment analysis reinforced the reliability of our findings, while drug-target prediction suggests a potential direction for clinical translation. However, the current conclusions require further validation, as the identification of AGTR1 and ADCY4 was based on a relatively small number of patient samples, and heterogeneity in gene expression may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future studies with larger and more diverse cohorts, combined with gene function experiments, are warranted to confirm and extend these results.

## 5. Conclusion
In this study, through integrated bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation, AGTR1 and ADCY4 were identified as potential biomarkers for GDM. Both genes are involved in disease progression through mechanisms that regulate the calcium signaling pathway, the immune microenvironment, and metabolism-related pathways. This study provides a novel direction for clinical treatment by screening potential drug candidates through drug prediction. Future studies should further explore their molecular mechanisms and therapeutic potential through functional experiments to facilitate the development of precise intervention strategies.
