However, the cellular and molecular understanding of PSD is still yet to be illustrated. In current study, we prepared PSD rat model (MD) via unilateral middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) and chronic stress stimulation (DEPR), and isolated hippocampal tissues for single cell sequencing of 10x Genomics Chromium. First, we determined the presence of the increased cell population of endothelium and microglia and the compromised oligodendrocytes in MD compared to NC, MCAO and DEPR. The enriched functions of highly variable genes (HVGs) of endothelium and microglia suggested a reinforced blood-brain barrier in MD. Next, cell clusters of endothelium, microglia and oligodendrocytes were individually analyzed, and the subtypes with distinct functions were identified. The presence of expression profiles, intercellular communications and signaling pathways of these three cell populations of PSD displayed a similar but more aggressive appearance with DEPR compared to MCAO and NC. Taken together, this study characterized the specific gene profile of endothelium, microglia and oligodendrocytes of hippocampal PSD by single cell sequencing, emphasizing the crosstalk among them to provide theoretical basis for the in-depth mechanism research and drug therapy of PSD.

## Introduction
Post-stroke depression (PSD) is a common mental complication after stroke, which is manifested by depression, pessimism, lack of will, irritability, apathy, insomnia, anxiety and even suicidal tendencies. One third of stroke survivors will develop PSD, which seriously affects the recovery of neurological impairment in stroke patients, greatly increases the disability rate, recurrence rate and fatality rate of patients, and has become an independent risk factor for stroke recurrence [ ]. Several classes of antidepressants commonly used in clinic mainly exert antidepressant effects through selective inhibition of 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) reuptake in the synaptic space and increase of 5-HT concentration in the synaptic space through receptor and postreceptor mechanisms, such as fluoxetine hydrochloride. However, the effect of existing antidepressants is generally slow, with gastrointestinal discomfort, insomnia or lethargy, dizziness, decreased sexual desire and other side effects, and the therapeutic dose needs titration, individual differences are large, long-term use of heavy economic burden, affecting the treatment compliance [ ].
Although PSD was discovered more than 100 years ago, the etiological mechanism of PSD has only been studied for more than 50 years. At present, there is no unified conclusion on the etiological mechanism of PSD, and no specific biological indicators have been found for the diagnosis of PSD. Its occurrence may involve neurobiology, brain trauma, mental health and other complex factors. Among them, the bio-psycho-social theory and primary endogenous theory are generally accepted [ ]. Family, social, physiological and other factors lead to the imbalance of physical and psychological balance after stroke, leading to reactive depression, and chronic stress plays a major role. Primary endogenous theory suggests that the occurrence of PSD is related to the imbalance of monoamine transmitters (such as 5-HT and norepinephrine [NE]) caused by brain damage. The most direct evidence is that depressive symptoms are significantly relieved when patients take drugs (e.g., TCA, SSRI, MAOI) that enhance the function of monoamine neurotransmitters in the central nervous system or increase the concentration of such neurotransmitters in the synaptic space [ , ]. However, clinically, we found that the neurotransmitter increased within a short time after taking antidepressants, but the depressive symptoms were alleviated at least 2 weeks later, which suggests that the direct pathogenic factor of PSD is probably not the lack of monoamine neurotransmitters, but only an indirect factor [ ]. Beyond that, theories of neuroanatomy (frontal-cortical circuit, cerebellar-hypothalamic pathway and amygdala anterior cingulate-cortical circuit damage) [ ], Glu/GABA neurotransmitter imbalance [ ], genetics [ ], neurotrophic hypothesis [ ] can also partially explain the pathology and offer some aggressive treatment strategies. Nevertheless, current medical understanding of PSD is far from enough.
In view of this, elucidating the molecular mechanism of PSD will benefit a lot in drug treatment of PSD. In this study, rat model of unilateral middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) was developed for mimic stroke, then depression was further stimulated on this basis to complete PSD model. Hippocampus was isolated for single-cell RNA sequencing. Our study is the first to map the dynamics of multiple cell populations and changing genes in PSD, emphasizing the coordination between cell types as well as the possible affect from microenvironment on inflammation, immunity and cell death.

## Results

## Characterization of cell population in hippocampus
The behavioral examination had been used to evaluate the MCAO and depression. All examinations indicated a significant neurological damage by MCAO and depression compared to NC (Supplementary Fig.  ). The schematic diagram of four groups (“NC”: negative control, “MCAO”: stroke, “DEPR”: depression, “MD”: MCAO + DEPR or post-stroke depression) of hippocampus for scRNA-seq was shown in Fig.  . Each set of data contained five biological replicates. Data quality control of each specimen all indicated the good quality of viable cell population, sequencing and mapping (Supplementary Table S ). Based on the “ImmGen” Dataset [ ] and well-acknowledged biomarkers, we have defined seven cell populations of four samples after multi-base dimension reduction clustering by UMAP (Fig.  ), and they could be further divided into seventeen subtypes (Fig.  ). 
Cluster 1, 10-2 and 13 robustly expressed Sox10, Olig1 and Olig2 were oligodendrocyte cells (Fig.  ). In particular, Cluster 10-2 was additionally expressed Pdgfra, indicating an oligodendrocyte precursor cell population more likely. Cluster 13 could be defined as oligodendrocyte with Zfp536 robust expression. Cluster 1 displayed the specific phenotype of Rhou positive expression among all clusters (Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 5, 12 and 14 displaying a high expression of Cldn5 and Flt1 suggested a population of endothelium. Cluster 12 had many specific biomarkers (Hba-a2/3, Alox15, Alas2, Lgals5, Cd52) which could distinguish from other clusters. Cluster 14 could be considered as the endothelial population with a high expression of Thbd (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 7 was considered as T/NK cells due to the enrichment of Cd3d, Cd3e, Klrd1 and Nkg7 transcripts (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 8 and 15 were characterized as B cells by the evidence of vigorous expression of Cd19, Cd79b, Jchain and Mzb1 (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 2, Cluster 3 (Ttk+), Cluster 4 (Tnfsf9+) and Cluster 6 (Fcna+) were considered as microglia by the hallmarks of Cx3cr1, Trem2 and Cd68, and they also had their own specific biomarkers among microglia (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 9 was mural cells or pericytes with Tbx18, Vtn and Acta2 (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Cluster 10-1, Cluster 11, Cluster 5-2, Cluster 16 and Cluster 17 could not be accurately defined because of multicellular marker. Taken together, we summarized seven types of acknowledged cell populations based on the classical biomarkers of hippocampus in our system.

## Reinforced blood-brain barrier in hippocampus of MD
By the cell counts of each population in hippocampus varied across these four groups (Fig.  ), remarkably, the proportion of oligodendrocytes (Cluster 1) was reduced while endothelium (Cluster 5, 12), microglia (Cluster 6) and B cells (Cluster 8) were elevated in total clusters in MD compared to other three groups (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). By looking at the highly variable genes (HVGs) (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ) as well as the “Metascape” analysis for GO, pathway and process enrichment, it was notable that microglia population in Cluster 6 and endothelial population in Cluster 5 both showed a protein translation and stability regulation on the multiple genes such as NADH dehydrogenase (Nd2/6), proteasome 20S subunits (Psma/b/c), voltage dependent anion channel family (Vdac), ATP synthase associated genes (Atp5mc/po/pf/f1), NADH: ubiquinone oxidoreductase family (Ndufa/b/s), cytochrome c oxidase (Cox6/7/8) and ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase (Uqcrq/h/c/f), which were closely related with neurodegenerative disease (Fig.  ). The endothelial population in Cluster 12 highly expressed adhesion G protein coupled receptors (Adgrf/g), angiopoietin (Angpt2/14) and so on also suggested an accelerative ability of angiogenesis (Fig.  ). We determined the expression of classical tight junction protein Occludin by IF assay (Fig.  ). Taken together, our observations confirmed that a strengthened blood-brain barrier (BBB) composed of capillary endothelium and glial cells emerged in hippocampus of MD. 

## Diversified endothelial population with distinct functions in hippocampus of MD
Next, we further grouped the endothelial cells into 6 subpopulations (Fig.  ). Importantly, we observed that the distribution of these subpopulations were largely different among MCAO and DEPR, and the subtype pattern of endothelial cells in MD was more like that of DEPR rather than MCAO and NC (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, subpopulation 2 and 5 were particularly robust whereas 1 and 4 were remarkably compromised in MD compared to NC and MCAO. High expression of Sema3c [ ] and Nes [ ] in subpopulation 1 suggested the proliferating endothelial progenitor cells. On the other hand, HVGs of subpopulation 2 included Angpt2 [ ] and Adamts9 [ ] for brain capillaries proliferation, Ramp3 [ ] for capillary permeability reduction and inflammatory cells clearance as well as Fbn1 [ ] for microvascular fibrosis. On the contrary, HVGs of subpopulation 4 included Fbln5 [ , ], Gja4 [ ], Avp (vasopressin) [ ] as well as Aqp1 [ ], Cd52 [ ], Tmem86b [ ], Scna [ ] in subpopulation 5 implicated the leakage of BBB and vascular pathology with endothelial relaxation and angiogenesis inhibition (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ). Nr2f1 and Nr2f2 in subpopulation 6 indicated a lymphoendothelial cells [ ]. Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) also suggested that each endothelial subtype appeared to have its unique role without a significant functional overlap (Fig.  ). The function of subcluster 3 was closely linked with lipoprotein, indicating the cholesterol endothelial cells related with thrombus and plaque. But the number of this subgroup did not change significantly across the four samples. 
Pearson Correlation of the overall transcriptome among these six subpopulations deciphered that subpopulation 5 had the negative correlation with other five subpopulations (Supplementary Fig.  ), suggesting a pathological phenotype of endothelium. These subpopulations of endothelium with opposite effects were not causally related to each other, indicating the presence of injury and individual self-repair in DEPR and MD. To more intuitively see the changes of no cluster in the course of disease, we established a pseudo-time analysis on endothelium, total three status were observed in these four samples with two individual lines developing the whole biological event (Fig.  ). It was notable that endothelium with “State 1” largely occupied in DEPR and MD groups, while an increase in “State 3” labeled cells should be associated with stroke, and “State 2” indicated healthy endothelium (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ), suggesting that the cell status of endothelium in MD was a superposition of DEPR and MACO. On the other hand, except subpopulation 6, it was hard to clearly distinguish the distribution bias of each clusters in the time line (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Therefore, these diversified endothelial populations were independent of each other and not the different transitional status in the course of disease development.

## Transitional microglia with multiple functions superimposed on each other in hippocampus of MD
Microglial cells could be also divided into 9 subpopulations (Fig.  ). More obviously than endothelium, distribution and amount of different microglial were largely identical between DEPR and MD as well as between NC and MCAO (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). In general, correlation analysis showed that subpopulation 4, 8 and 9 had more similar transcripts, while the expression profiles of subpopulation 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 were similar to each other (Supplementary Fig.  ). By looking at the HVGs (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ) and GSVA (Fig.  ) of each subpopulation, unlike endothelium, distinct subpopulation of microglia displayed the similar genotypes and functions. Importantly, subpopulation 1 highly expressing Axl, P2ry12, Cx3cr1, and Ccl2 contained almost all the biological functions of microglia including sensing, housekeeping and neuroprotection [ ], and they were reduced in DEPR and MD. Instead, subpopulation 2, 3 and 4 displaying the almost identical features were elevated in DEPR and MD (Fig.  ). These subpopulations highly expressing Rpl4, Tmsb4x, Eef1a1 suggested an embryonic microglia compared to the postnatal microglia with Cst3 and Sparc (Subpopulation 1) (Supplementary Table S ), indicating that more naïve microglia was produced when DEPR and MD happened [ ]. Moreover, some subpopulations had partially overlapping functions. For example, mitochondria-related energy metabolism functions in the respiratory chain were most significant in the HVGs in subpopulation 5, but also in the other three subpopulation 7, 8 and 9. However, the specific biological functions of subpopulation 8 and 9 were negative in subpopulation 5 (Fig.  ). These mixed phenotypes suggested that microglia underwent a gradual process of change, leaving behind many intermediate states. Unlike endothelium, microglia displayed more variational biological status with multiple branches in cell trajectory (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). For MCAO, cells with all status were reduced (Supplementary Fig.  ). For DEPR, all four ends of the trajectory were offset toward the central two nodes (Supplementary Fig.  ), and the microglia labeled “State 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6” in the lower half branch were increased in DEPR and MD compared NC and MCAO (Fig.  ), indicating the depression-characteristic cells. Our observations indicated that subpopulations 1, 4 and 6 mostly constituted the microglia in healthy brain, while subpopulation 2 almost covered the phenotype of microglia in DEPR group. Other subpopulations were the intermediate in the developmental process (Supplementary Fig.  ). The effect of stroke weakened the cells at four ends of trajectory, in turn facilitated the DEPR-mediated transition towards to the central (Fig.  ). These observations revealed a certain enhanced effect of stroke on depression at the microglial level. 

## Oligodendrocytes presenting different regulation on myelin sheath
In turn, the presence of compromised oligodendrocyte population would fail to attach to the vessels, and was benefit the tight junction integrity of astrocyte endfeet and endothelial as previously described [ ]. Oligodendrocytes form myelin sheaths in the central nervous system to increase the speed and intensity of nerve impulse transmission, thereby increasing the synchronization between the nuclei of neurons. Although oligodendrocytes were divided into 11 subpopulations (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ), two main variations were observed actually, including subgroup 2, 3, 8 and 9 increased obviously in DEPR and MD, whereas subgroup 1, 4, 5 and 7 increased obviously in NC and MCAO (Fig.  ). Consistently, HVGs (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ) and biological functions (Fig.  ) of these 11 subpopulations indicated that there were two main types of oligodendrocytes with different status namely demyelination or remyelination. Cell trajectory analysis also suggested two trajectories in opposite directions (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). The original injured oligodendrocytes underwent apoptosis and were replaced by new ones. Bax, Capn2 (encoding Calpain) in subpopulation 1 and 5 indicated endoplasmic reticulum stress-induced apoptosis, meanwhile, Khdrbs1 (encoding p62) and Becn1 (encoding Beclin-1) in subpopulation 1 indicated cell autophagy. Acsl4 in subpopulation 11 indicated ferroptosis. On the contrary, high expression of Fth1 in subpopulation 2, 3, 8 and 9 suggested an anti-ferroptosis effect on these oligodendrocytes. Subpopulation 10 expressed both Gpx4 and Lpcat3, showing a paradoxical state of ferroptosis. 
Furthermore, we extended more HVGs to detail the positive and negative myelination (Supplementary Table S ), and finally harvested two lists of HVG displaying the opposite effects on myelination regulation. Hnrnpk [ ], Cxxc5 [ ], Pnn [ ], Khdrbs1 [ ], Brd7 [ ], Adnp [ ], Zbtb7a [ ], Nrdc [ ], Sec63 [ ], Ilk [ ], Rybp [ ], Vamp3 [ ], Selenot [ ], Stag2 [ ], Coq7 [ ] of “List A” have been reported to enhance oligodendrocyte differentiation, while Efna1 [ ], Acap2 [ ], Cd47 [ ], Acbd3 [ ], Rbbp4 [ ] and Hnrnpa2b1 [ ] of “List B” have been documented to connect with the axonal demyelination. We scored the weight of these two lists of gene set across all oligodendrocytes by addmodulescore analysis, and found that “List A” was largely increased in subpopulation 1, 4, 5, 7 (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ), while “List B” was mainly increased in subpopulation 2, 3, 8, 9 (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). It was suggested that the gene set related to myelin sheath could redefine the different roles that oligodendrocytes represent in MD. Our findings determined that DEPR and MD would cause one class of oligodendrocytes for negative regulation of myelination.

## The strengthened intercellular communication among the cell types in MD
To further study the potential connection among cell populations, we utilized the ligand-receptor database to figure out clusters via cellphoneDB method [ ]. We noticed that the number and intensity of the intercellular communications among all cell populations were both largely elevated in MD (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ) compared to DEPR (Supplementary Fig.  ) and MCAO (Supplementary Fig.  ). Compared with DEPR (Fig.  ) and MCAO (Fig.  ), we found that the connection of Subpopulation 2 and 5 of endothelium as well as Subpopulation 1 of oligodendrocytes with other cell type were increased and strengthened, whereas the signal input from immune cells is somewhat weakened. Furthermore, our findings also indicated some particular intercellular communications in the specific samples (Fig.  ). Take CXCL12/CXCR4 receptor ligand pairs as an example, Subpopulation 2 and 5 of endothelial cells can interact more with T/B lymphocytes in MD and DEPR via this way, but replaced by Subpopulation 1 of endothelial cell in MCAO, and only self-communication in endothelial cells (Fig.  ). In addition, the presence of signaling pathway activation of ANGPT and GAS among different cell types (Fig.  ) were similarly in DEPR and MD, far different from NC and MCAO. We concluded that MD did not generate the unique cell-cell communications compared to other three groups. The overall pattern of this crosstalk was more similar to DEPR rather than MCAO or NC, but also much stronger than DEPR. 

## Specific gene expression profiles of endothelium, microglia and oligodendrocytes in MD
To address the special regulatory signaling pathways involved in PSD, we further compared the current sequencing data with three sources of single-cell data on stroke [ ] or epilepsy [ ], depression [ ] across human and mouse species. We mainly focused on endothelium, microglia and oligodendrocytes, and attempted to find out the common and special rules behind the gene expression profiles of these cells in the PSD. The gene expression profile of endothelium were largely distinct from ours (Supplementary Fig.  ). A partial subsets of these cell populations especially microglia and ologodendrocytes in stroke (Fig.  ), epilepsy (Fig.  ) and depression (Fig.  ) could match our data exactly. Even that, these cell populations all accounted for a small percentage of MD compared to other three groups, indicating that the single-cell sequencing data from the MD group in our study was unique and could not be replaced by the previous simple stroke or depression datasets. 
Many low-expressed transcripts are usually unavailable due to the sequencing throughput of single-cell sequencing data, whereas bulk RNA-seq is a hybrid system due to its inability to distinguish between different cell types. We also employed the DEGs from the cortical tissue of suicidal patients with depression (GSE102556) [ ], as well as the DEGs from poststroke epilepsy (GSE241528) [ , ] by bulk RNA-seq, and compared with our data by cybersort methods. The significant changes in the proportion of most cell types among different groups indicated that oligodendrocytes were largely occupied the main proportion, whereas microglia actually made up only a small percentage compared to other types in bulk RNA-seq (Neurons and astrocytes were outside the scope in our system) (Fig.  ). Given the new expression profiles produced by the projection of the gene expression values of DEGs from bulk RNA-seq into these cells, we enrich the expression matrix of our single-cell sequencing data even if species or disease types were not exactly identical (Supplementary Table S ). 

## Discussion
There are more notable differences between PSD and major depressive disorder (MDD) beyond several similarities, including that PSD shows the closer link with vascular injury, more severe depressive symptoms, more cognitive impairment, less anhedonia and disturbances in sleep/cyclic functions as well as the higher prevalence of physical disability [ ]. PSD has a rapid onset due to the significant disruption of neural pathways essential for mood regulation caused by the lesion. In contrast, other mood disorders may develop gradually and persist throughout a person’s lifetime, with some cases potentially leading to major depressive disorder (MDD) and, in severe instances, suicide [ ]. In addition, previous studies have also uncovered some biomarkers that can distinguish PSD and MDD in clinical practice [ , ], but have yet to reveal the regulatory network behind it. Therefore, it is reasonable to include the cellular and genetic comparison between DEPR and MD into the scope of our investigation.
Depression affects several key primary brain regions, particularly those involved in mood regulation, motivation, decision-making, and memory, including prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, anterior cingulate cortex, nucleus accumbens, hypothalamus as well as insula. We have been focusing on hippocampal tissue in PSD studies [ , ], and we have found that the individual differences in the construction of the chronic unpredictable mild stress (CUMS)-induced depression model can sometimes be amplified to the extent that they significantly affect intra-group representation and signal pathway differences, which would have a much greater impact on single-cell sequencing than on bulk RNA-seq. Therefore, we have collected the contralateral hippocampus of model rats as a parallel control group. From our results, significant differences in a wide range of gene expression profiles between the DEPR and MD groups can be seen (Supplementary Fig.  ). Although we could not obtain data from a strictly defined depression group (stroke does indeed cause damage to the contralateral hippocampus [ ]), from the perspective of eliminating individual differences as a confounding factor, the data and results of this study remain valuable for reference by our peers.
It is regretful that we have failed to harvest the hippocampal neurons in our sequencing data, and we cannot address the status of neurons in PSD. The shape of neurons are irregular and those larger than 40 μm will be filtered out during the preparation of the single-cell suspension. In addition, the subsequent capture is random. When the cell amount of a certain type is less than 10%, it is very likely to fail to capture. Alternative single nuclear sequencing will lose the RNA information in the cytoplasm [ ], and prior flow cytometry or magnetic bead capture will affect the cell state and miss other cell types [ ].
Previous studies have indicated that oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) will be recruited by neurons, then differentiate into oligodendrocyte to fix the proper neuronal network function and reorganization [ , ]. In turn, OPCs also cause ferroptosis-mediated cell death in stroke [ ]. The rats used in this study are about one year old, and there is supposed to be no such effect of aging factors on them. The increased oligodendrocyte in MCAO compared to NC is explainable because of the myelination modulated by neuroplasticity. Therefore, the reduction of oligodendrocytes in MD compared to MCAO, DEPR and NC (Fig.  ) is also reasonable. In DEPR, neuroplasticity is largely disrupted, thereby compromised the repair capacity [ , ]. On the other hand, the main cause of weakening the structure, function and plasticity of neurons and oligodendrocytes is the immune cell-mediated inflammatory response. It is important to note that elevated cytokine production in MDD and other psychiatric disorders [ ]. Nevertheless, it is controversial that cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-10 also show the anti-inflammatory effect, and remain to be specified [ ]. Moreover, although the effect of the inflammatory response is dramatic in both MCAO and DEPR (Fig.  ), the immune system in the CNS functions in a unique way compared to peripheral tissues. In DEPR, microglia, neurons and astrocytes contribute the vast majority of cytokines compared to the peripheral immune cells due to the shielding of BBB. Whereas in ischemic stroke, peripheral immune cells assisted by microglia can infiltrate across the weakened or permissive BBB into the brain parenchyma [ , ]. Our observation by contrast suggests a strengthened BBB in MD (Fig.  ). Our explanation is as follows, if only in the case of stroke, microglia not only plays a pro-inflammatory role in itself, but also introduces more peripheral immune cells to secrete a large number of cytokines and chemokines. But in the case of depression, damaged nerve tissue wants more alternatively activated microglia to play an anti-inflammatory and nutritional role, rather than being attacked by an inflammatory response. In our model, depression is man-made, which is different from the actual PSD that the depression in patients with PSD is endogenous and spontaneous. According to our speculation, depression is first caused by stroke, then the damaged neurons in turn strengthen the blood-brain barrier protection, refuse to accept more immune cell infiltration, and inhibit activated microglia cells. However, this kind of response to PSD is not very drug friendly in clinical practice, because drugs that appear to support neuronutrition are also blocked by BBB and have a hard time getting into the brain to do their job. Therefore, for the treatment of PSD, in addition to reducing inflammatory response, our recommendation is to selectively open BBB and give neurotrophic supported drug therapy.
Single-cell analysis of tissues of the central nervous system during homeostasis in mice has revealed specific time- and region-dependent as well as context-dependent microglia subtypes with distinct molecular hallmarks and diverse cellular kinetics [ ]. The great heterogeneity of microglia displays the feature of M1 (neurotoxic) and M2 (neuroprotective) macrophages [ ], meanwhile also exerts non-immune functions such as facilitating oligodendrogenesis and maintaining activity of immune cell via secreted extracellular vesicles [ ]. In this study, we have figured out the phenotype of microglia of MD (Fig.  ). Unlike as previously described [ ], our data shows more complex subpopulations of microglia and changing relationships (Fig.  ). Taken an example, Spp1 and Cd74 are both highly expressed in subgroup 5, demonstrating both demyelination and remyelination effects. Ctsb/Lamp1 and Cst3/Sparc both appear in subpopulation 1, indicating a naive and mature microglia (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ).
In addition, we are curious that microglia do express a large number of hemoglobin transcripts in subpopulation 2, 3 and 4, the latter which is significantly increased group in DEPR and MD (Fig.  , Supplementary Table S ). Although cell-free hemoglobin could indeed induce microglial inflammatory response for neuronal injury [ , ], transcripts of hemoglobin are thought to come from hematopoietic stem cells rather than peripheral blood cells. It is suggested that a proportion of microglia might synthesize hemoglobin, and the consequent toxic effect of endogenous breakdown product hemin may be a novel source of hemoglobin in intracerebral hemorrhage and neuronal injury. In any case, elucidating the function of different population of microglial cells in PSD is a very meaningful direction in future study.
Unlike microglia, the phenotype and genotype of endothelium are much simpler. Endothelial differentiating, proliferating, cholesterol, and lymphatic endothelium as well as two pathological endothelium with high permeability and low growth. PSD seemingly leads to the absence of endothelial differentiation, but robust proliferation and fibrosis instead compared to NC and MCAO (Fig.  ), which will result in irreversible damage of BBB. Therefore, reducing the number and function of subgroup 2 of endothelial cells as much as possible will facilitate the function of BBB and PSD recovery. Even simpler are oligodendrocytes that actually only have two status, namely demyelination or remyelination presenting two trajectories in opposite directions (Fig.  ), and we have determined that these damaged oligodendrocytes undergo programmed cell death via apoptosis, autophagy and ferroptosis. It is worth mentioning that the single-cell sequencing in this study provides more detailed and accurate information compared to bulk RNA-seq. For example, one study [ ] has suggested that autophagy indicators Fez1, Nbr1, Ulk1, and Scoc are significantly overexpressed in the hippocampus of PSD (also confirmed in other bulk RNA–seq data shown in Supplementary Table S ), but our results go further, in which Fez1 is specifically overexpressed in oligodendrocytes of Cluster 1 and 13, and Ulk1 has only been highly expressed in endothelium of Cluster 14, whereas Nbr1 and Scoc are both highly expressed in all three clusters above (Supplementary Table S ). Moreover, Nbr1, Ulk1 and Scoc have been particularly highly expressed in Subpopulation 6 of endothelium (Supplementary Table S ). Fez1 is highly expressed in OPC subgroups 1 and 4, Nbr1was highly expressed in Subpopulation 4 of oligodendrocytes, while Scoc is highly expressed in Subpopulation 1 of oligodendrocytes (Supplementary Table S ). Our data, combined with previous studies, not only reveal the special biological significance of a new cell subpopulation, but also demonstrate the diversity and complexity of functional cells in PSD, which is not possible with bulk RNA seq, and is also the charm of single-cell sequencing.
Since we have observed the obviously elevated endothelium and microglia as well as the compromised oligodendrocytes in MD, finally we establish the intercellular communication among them via ligand-receptor manner. Our discoveries have determined that the crosstalk among these three cells of PSD are the combination of stroke and depression without new specific ligand-receptor pair, and are more affected by depression (Fig.  ). Many key signals such as ANGPT2/TEK, EFNA1/EPHA2, and GAS6/AXL are closely related to psychiatric disorders and neurodegenerative disease [ – ]. These intercellular communication signals on PSD are seldom acknowledged in current literatures, which will be part of our plans for future research, including the effects, the mechanisms behind as well as the potential drug target development and examination.
Overall, we characterize the single cell expression profile of hippocampal PSD, and unmask the differential expressed genes of endothelium, microglia and oligodendrocytes, emphasizing the crosstalk among them to provide theoretical basis for the in-depth mechanism research and drug therapy of PSD.

## Materials and methods

## Establishment of animal model
Due to the possible neuroprotective mechanism of estrogen level on cerebral ischemia injury and the effect of physiological fluctuation of hormone level on emotion, twenty male adult Sprague Dawley rats aged with 10–12 months (Weight 250–300 g) were randomly divided into two groups (n = 10). These two groups were subject to the unilateral intraluminal monofilament in middle cerebral artery for MCAO. One group is then received chronic stress stimulation for depression. This project was approved by Ethics Committee of experimental animal medicine of Shanghai Hospital of traditional Chinese Medicine (Serial No. 2021036).
For MCAO experimental operation in detail, the occlusion was performed to the right hemisphere of all rats. As previously described [ ], after abdominal anesthesia, the rats were fixed in supine position on the operating table, routinely prepared skin, disinfected, and insulated throughout the operation. The mouse incisors were hooked and fixed with sutures to stretch the neck. The common carotid artery (CCA), external carotid artery (ECA), and internal carotid artery (ICA) were exposed and separated by blunt dissection of glandular tissue and fascia of the neck using conventional ophthalmic scissors or scalpel. ICA and its extracranial branch pterygognathic artery were separated, and the pterygognathic artery was ligated with slipknot with sutures, or the pterygognathic artery was directly closed with vascular clamp to avoid insertion of thread plug into the pterygognathic artery, and the only open branch of CCA was maintained as ICA. The ECA trunk was separated, and the distal and proximal ends of the external carotid artery were ligated (leaving long wire heads), with a spacing of 5 mm, then cut the artery between the two ligation points. Then gently pull the proximal line to make this artery in line with the common carotid artery. The proximal end of the bifurcation of the common carotid artery and the external carotid artery was closed with a microarteriole clamp, and a small incision was cut in the residual segment of the external carotid artery. The prepared thread was inserted until the beginning of the middle cerebral artery, and the thread was inserted 17–18 mm. After ligation and disinfection, subcutaneous tissue and skin can be sutured layer by layer, and put back into the cage after the animal recovers from anesthesia. Neuroethology after 24 h of ischemia was evaluated by Longa scoring method via the following criteria ((I) No neurological impairment: 0; (II) Paralyzed side of the front paw fail to be fully extended: 1 point; (III) Turning to the paralyzed side while walking: 2 points; (IV) Falling to the paralyzed side while walking: 3 points; (V) Fail to walk automatically accompanied with a loss of consciousness phenomenon: 4 points). Wilcoxon rank sum test was used to analyze the difference between MCAO and non-MCAO groups.
For CUMS-induced depression experimental operation in detail, rats were randomly subjected to 11 types of different stimuli for 22 days: behavioral restriction for 2 h, ice water swimming for 5 min at 4 °C, fasting for 24 h, loss of access to water for 24 h, tail clamping for 1 min, light-dark reversal for 24 h, electric shock on a footplate for 10 min (1 mA electric current, 30 V voltage, once every 10 s, each for 1 s, total 60 times), inclination of the home cage at 45° for 24 h, moist padding for 24 h, an empty bottle for 2 h and shaking (50 Hz) at 45 °C for 5 min. During the entire period, only one stimulus was administered per day, and all 11 types of stimuli were randomly ordered twice in 22 days, so the rats could not adapt to the stimulus. Open field test and tail suspension tests were used to examine the behavior of depression model. In open field test, a glass arena (40 cm L × 40 cm W × 30 cm H) was divided into the center and periphery by 16‐beam animal activity monitors. First, each rat was released from the same corner of the arena, and the arena was explored freely for 10 min. The number of grids passed and the time of latency to the center were both recorded. In the tail suspension test, rats were hung by their tails 50 cm above the floor with adhesive tape for 6 min. The latency of the first episode of immobility and the duration of immobility in the entire 6-min period were recorded. Immobility was defined as a motionless head and body. The latency of immobility was determined as the time between the onset of the test and the first episode of immobility. One-way analysis of variance was used to analyze the differences between depression and non-depression groups.
After morphological examinations, rats were sacrificed via cervical dislocation, and hippocampus tissues of five rats in each group were immediately isolated for sequencing, whereas the other five rats’ hippocampus were stored for cellular and molecular experiments.

## Single-cell RNA sequencing and data processing
Hippocampus was freshly isolated, and washed by cold RPMI 1640 medium with 0.04% BSA for twice, then cut into small pieces about 0.5 mm3 in size, then digested into single cell using Neural Tissue Dissociation Kit (Cat. No. 130-092-628, Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany). Single-cell suspension was filtrated by BD FALCON 40 μm cell filter (BD, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), then centrifuged 300 g at 4 °C for 5 min. The supernatant was mixed by 500 μL medium and 500 μL red cell lysate (Cat. No. 130-094-183, Miltenyi Biotec) at 4 °C for 10 min, then centrifuged and washed twice, and finally resuspended by 100 μL medium. Cell concentration and viability was evaluated using Luna-IITM Automated Cell Counter (Logos Biosystems, Anyang-si, Gyeonggi-do, South Korea). Cell suspension with 1000 living cell/μL was used to construct library by Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3ʹ Reagent Kits v3.1 (Cat. No. 1000268, 10× Genomics, Pleasanton, CA, USA), and sequenced via Illumina Nova 6000 PE150 platform.
The Cell Ranger software pipeline (version 3.1.0) provided by 10× Genomics was used to demultiplex cellular barcodes, map reads to the genome and transcriptome using the STAR aligner, and down-sample reads as required to generate normalized aggregate data across samples, producing a matrix of gene counts versus cells. We processed the unique molecular identifier (UMI) count matrix using the R package Seurat (version 3.0). To remove low quality cells and likely multiplet captures, which is a major concern in microdroplet-based experiments, we apply a criteria to filter out cells with UMI/gene numbers out of the limit of mean value +/−2 fold of standard deviations assuming a Guassian distribution of each cells’ UMI/gene numbers. Following visual inspection of the distribution of cells by the fraction of mitochondrial genes expressed, we further discarded low-quality cells where a certain percentage of counts belonged to mitochondrial genes. Library size normalization was performed in Seurat on the filtered matrix to obtain the normalized count. Top variable genes across single cells were identified. Briefly, the average expression and dispersion were calculated for each gene, genes were subsequently placed into several bins based on expression. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to reduce the dimensionality on the log transformed gene-barcode matrices of top variable genes. Cells were clustered based on a graph-based clustering approach, and were visualized in 2-dimension using tSNE. Likelihood ratio test that simultaneously test for changes in mean expression and in the percentage of expressed cells was used to identify significantly differentially expressed genes between clusters. Here, we use the R package SingleR, a novel computational method for unbiased cell type recognition of scRNA-seq to infer the cell of origin of each of the single cells independently and identify cell types. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the Seurat package. P value < 0.05 and |log2foldchange|> 1 (or |log2foldchange|> 0.58) was set as the threshold for significantly differential expression. GO enrichment and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs were respectively performed using R based on the hypergeometric distribution.
Clean data was deposited into GEO Database assigned GSE232936 for free download.

## Immunofluorescence assay
As previously described [ ], the freshly-isolated whole brains within optimal cutting temperature compound were quick-frozen for 30 s, then sectioned into 20 μm pieces by freezing microtome, and placed in 1 × PBS and rinse three times for 10 min each time, and then transfered the slices to the mounting solution (1 × PBS 18.4 ml, BSA 0.2 g, 10% TritonX-100 800 μl, goat serum 800 μL). Primary antibody diluent (0.3% TritonX-100 75 μL, 1 × PBS 24.75 mL, goat serum 250 μL) and secondary antibody diluent (0.3% TritonX-100 250 μL, 1 × PBS 25 mL) were prepared. After sealing at 37 °C for 1 h, the brain tissue slices were placed in a 200 μL tube and transferred to a 4 °C refrigerator for primary antibody against Occludin (1:500, Cat. No. 27260-1-AP, Proteintech Group, USA) incubation overnight. The next day, the sections were rinsed three times with 1 × PBS for 10 min each time. All subsequent operations were carried out in the dark. The brain tissue sections were replaced again in a new 200 μL tube and incubate with the CoraLite 488-conjugated Goat Anti- Rabbit IgG (H + L)(1:50, Cat. No. SA00013-2, Proteintech Group, USA) at room temperature for 1 h. After washing by 1 × PBS for six times, the brain slice were placed on an adhesive glass slide, and placed it in a light-proof humidifier to dry it to avoid peeling off during the subsequent process. The sections were covered the sections with DAPI (20 μL), and removed the DAPI and absorbed the water around the tissue after 20 min of the nuclei staining, then added anti-fluorescence quenching mounting tablets (10–20 μL/sheet) to each tissue, and mounted the slides with a cover glass.
