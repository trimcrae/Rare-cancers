## Abstract

## Background
Gastric cancer (GC) is a highly heterogeneous and complex disease. Recently, integrin β (ITGB) superfamily members have been shown to play crucial roles in the initiation and progression of various human cancers. However, the precise role and molecular mechanisms of ITGB1 in GC are yet to be fully elucidated.

## Methods
This bioinformatics and clinical study systematically analyzed the pan-cancer expression patterns and prognostic significance of ITGBs using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas and Genotype-Tissue Expression portals. Multivariate regression analysis was performed to identify key factors influencing GC prognosis. Candidate noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) potentially regulating ITGB1 expression were identified via expression profiling, coexpression assessment, and survival correlation studies. Furthermore, the associations of ITGB1 and its related long ncRNA MIR99AHG with tumor-infiltrating immune cells, immune cell markers, and immune checkpoint molecules in GC were explored.

## Results
Compared with adjacent normal tissues, the ITGB1, ITGB2, ITGB4, ITGB5, and ITGB8 mRNA levels were significantly upregulated in GC tissues (p < 0.05). Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier survival analyses indicated that ITGB1 upregulation was associated with poor prognosis (univariable hazards ratio = 1.40, 95% confidence interval 1.008–1.956, p = 0.045) and served as an independent prognostic factor (multivariate hazards ratio = 1.46, 95% confidence interval 1.004–2.140, p = 0.048) in patients with GC. The MIR99AHG/hsa-mir-17-5p axis (r = − 0.56, p < 0.001) was identified as the most promising upstream ncRNA-related pathway regulating ITGB1 expression in GC. In addition, ITGB1 expression in GC and adjacent nontumorous tissues was validated using immunohistochemistry (H-score: 35.4 ± 19.2 vs. 28.4 ± 16.2, respectively; p = 0.035). MIR99AHG expression was similarly assessed through in situ hybridization (H-score: 32.4 ± 15.6 vs. 20.5 ± 11.0, respectively; p < 0.001). There was a positive correlation between ITGB1 expression and infiltrating CD4 + T cells (r = 0.17, p < 0.001), M2 macrophages (r = 0.37, p < 0.001), dendritic cells (r = 0.19, p < 0.001), and immune checkpoints (PD-L1, CTLA-4, and CD28). Particularly, high macrophage infiltration was associated with favorable prognosis in GC (p = 0.004).

## Conclusions
Our findings suggest that ncRNA-mediated ITGB1 expression is associated with poor prognosis and tumor-immune infiltration in GC. However, further validation through extensive mechanistic studies and large-scale clinical trials is warranted.

## Background
Gastric cancer (GC) is the fifth most frequently diagnosed malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [ , ]. Each year, more than a million new cases of GC are diagnosed, and most of these cases are diagnosed at an advanced stage [ , ]. Owing to the significant heterogeneity of the disease, numerous genetic alterations, and the tumor microenvironment, the 5-year survival rate for patients with advanced GC remains > 5% [ ]. The morbidity and mortality rates of GC are continuously increasing in many countries, and it is widely recognized as an extremely aggressive disease with a poor prognosis [ , ]. Therefore, further exploration of reliable biomarkers is crucial for improving the diagnosis and prognosis of GC.
The integrin family comprises heterodimeric transmembrane receptors that mediate cell adhesion and transmit signals to the cell interior. The family includes 18 identified α subunits and 8 identified β subunits [ , ]. The integrin β (ITGB) superfamily is known to modulate the behavior of metastatic cells by binding to various ligands in the extracellular matrix and initiating aberrant cellular signaling [ , ]. Hu et al. demonstrated that human telomerase reverse transcriptase promotes GC cell invasion by enhancing the ubiquitination of Forkhead box O3a (FOXO3a) and upregulating ITGB1 [ ]. ITGB4 expression was found to be elevated in cases of lymphovascular and perineural invasion in GC tissues compared with normal stomach tissues, suggesting that ITGB4 may serve as a potential novel marker for diagnosing these invasion types [ ]. The increase in the combined serum levels of ITGB6 and carcinoembryonic antigen contributed to the risk stratification of and prognostic prediction for patients with GC [ ]. However, a comprehensive understanding of the prognostic and oncologic characteristics of the entire ITGB superfamily in GC remains elusive.
Noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) play crucial roles in numerous physiological and pathological processes in normal cells. Recent studies have linked impaired ncRNA processing to several diseases, including GC [ – ]. Long ncRNAs (lncRNAs) typically exceed 200 nucleotides in length, while small ncRNAs are processed from longer precursors and include small nuclear RNAs and microRNAs (miRNAs) [ ]. Several studies have shown that ncRNAs (lncRNAs and miRNAs) associated with the ITGB superfamily exert both direct and indirect regulatory effects in cancer cells [ – ]. In non-small-cell lung cancer cells, Zhang et al. found that the lncRNA NR2F1-AS1 promotes tumorigenic behavior by sponging microRNA-493-5p and increasing ITGB1 expression [ ]. The ITGB superfamily has also been implicated in immune signaling and immune synapse formation. For instance, Qiu et al. reported that signaling through the ITGB1-DT/ARNTL2 axis may affect the immune microenvironment and lung adenocarcinoma progression [ ]. However, the ncRNA-associated regulation of ITGB1 and its association with tumor-immune infiltration in GC remain to be fully elucidated.
The regulatory role of ncRNAs in the modulation of ITGB1 expression during GC progression is poorly understood. This study reports that the novel MIR99AHG/miR-17-5p/ITGB1 axis plays a critical role in associating ncRNA-driven ITGB1 dysregulation with poor prognosis and altered immune infiltration in gastric tumors.

## Methods

## Tissue samples and clinical data collection
GC tissues and the corresponding adjacent nontumorous tissues were collected from 30 patients who underwent curative surgical resection between July 2023 and December 2024. This study was approved by the Research and Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital, Shandong University (Approval No. KYLL-2023(ZM)-617). Informed consent was obtained from all patients. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.
All GC diagnoses were histopathologically confirmed by two independent gastrointestinal pathologists according to the World Health Organization classification criteria (5th edition). The diagnostic features included: (a) malignant glandular architecture with stromal invasion, (b) high-grade nuclear atypia, and (c) aberrant mitotic activity. Discrepancies (< 5% of the cases) were resolved through reviews under a multihead microscope. Adjacent nontumorous tissues were strictly defined as samples that (a) were obtained > 3 cm from the tumor margins, (b) displayed intact mucosal layers without intestinal dysplasia, and (c) were confirmed to be cancer-free using pan-cytokeratin immunohistochemistry (IHC; AE1/AE3 antibody). All 30 cases exhibited tubular adenocarcinomas. Of the 30 patients, 20 were aged ≥ 60 years and 10 were aged < 60 years; 23 were men, while 7 were women; 23 showed high/medium differentiation, while 7 demonstrated poor differentiation; 15 were classified as having TNM stage I + II disease, while 15 had TNM stage III + IV disease; 10 exhibited lymph node metastasis, while 20 did not.

## Immunohistochemistry and in situ hybridization
To sequentially detect ITGB1 protein and MIR99AHG RNA in 30 GC and corresponding adjacent nontumorous tissues, 3-μm-thick formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections were subjected to antigen retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) using microwave heating. For IHC, slides were first incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-ITGB1 primary antibody (1:100 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), followed by incubation at room temperature for 2 h with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:500; Santa Cruz Biotechnology). After 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) visualization (Vector Laboratories) and hematoxylin counterstaining, the same sections were subjected to in situ hybridization (ISH) by incubating them overnight at 55 °C with digoxigenin-labeled MIR99AHG probes (1:50; Santa Cruz Biotechnology) in a humidity chamber. Post-hybridization, the slides were stringently washed at 60 °C using 2 × saline–sodium citrate buffer/0.1% Tween-20, incubated at 37 °C for 1 h with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-digoxigenin antibody (1:500), subjected to a second DAB development cycle, dehydrated, mounted with Vectashield (Vector Laboratories), and imaged using an Olympus BX53 microscope. To validate the specificity of the experiment, negative controls included no-primary-antibody IHC and RNase A-treated/sense-probe ISH sections. The results of IHC and ISH were quantified using an H-score system as follows: H-score = [1 × (% weak +) + 2 × (% moderate +) + 3 × (% strong +)]. The intensity for each technique was graded as follows: for IHC, the brown intensity of DAB (0–3 +); for ISH, the nuclear dot density (0: no dots; 1 +: 1–5 dots; 2 + : 6–10 dots; 3 + : > 10 dots/cell). This analysis was performed in five × 400 fields (excluding necrosis) with interobserver concordance.

## Download and analysis of data from the cancer genome atlas
First, we retrieved mRNA and miRNA sequencing data for 370 GC samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://cancergenome.nih.gov/), along with the corresponding clinical information (Supplementary Data). All raw RNA sequencing data, including data on lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs, were normalized using fragments per kilobase of the exon model per million mapped reads. MiRNA sequences were annotated with human mature miRNA names using the starBase version 2.0 database (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn). Differential expression analysis of ITGB1 was conducted using the limma package in R software (version 4.0.5). In the differential expression analysis comparing ITGB1-high and ITGB1-low GC samples, differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs) were identified by applying the thresholds of |log(fold-change)|> 0.3 and p < 0.05.

## Gene expression profiling interactive analysis database analysis
Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) is an advanced interactive web server that utilizes data from TCGA and the Genotype-Tissue Expression portals for analyzing gene expression and interactions between cancer genes and normal genes. GEPIA was employed to assess the expression of ITGB superfamily members in GC samples and to conduct survival analysis, encompassing overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival. It was also used to evaluate the prognostic value of candidate lncRNAs in GC. Log-rank p values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

## DIANA–LncBase database analysis
The DIANA–LncBase v3 database serves as a comprehensive reference repository containing experimentally validated miRNA targets on noncoding transcripts. The database encompasses approximately 500,000 entries and approximately 240,000 distinct tissue- and cell-type-specific interactions between miRNAs and lncRNAs. The DIANA–LncBase v3 database was used to identify potential lncRNAs corresponding to DEmiRNAs.

## starBase database analysis
starBase is a comprehensive database dedicated to the exploration of lncRNA and miRNA biology. The starBase algorithm was used to predict potential candidate lncRNAs capable of binding to ITGB1. The miRNA expression levels in GC and ITGB1 were also analyzed using starBase.

## Kaplan–Meier plotter analysis
The Kaplan–Meier plotter is capable of evaluating the association between the expression levels of all genes (mRNA, miRNA, protein) and survival outcomes in a data set comprising more than 30,000 samples from 21 different tumor types, including GC. The Kaplan–Meier plotter was used to perform survival analysis with regard to ITGB1 and mRNA expression in GC. Log-rank p values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

## TIMER database analysis
The TIMER web server is a comprehensive resource for systematically analyzing immune infiltrates across diverse cancer types. TIMER was used to examine the correlation between ITGB1/MIR99AHG expression level and infiltration level of immune cells or expression level of immune checkpoints in GC. The selection criteria were set at |R|> 0.1 and p < 0.05.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, Inc) and R software (version 4.0.5). The results are presented as medians with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan–Meier method along with the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression models were employed to examine the transcription factors that significantly influenced OS. Statistical significance was indicated by p values < 0.05. To control the familywise error rate in multiple hypothesis testing, we employed the Bonferroni and false discovery rate corrections. For analyses involving eight ITGB family members, we applied the Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05/8 = 6.25 × 10⁻3) to maintain strict control over familywise error rates during hypothesis testing. For the 29 miRNAs screened in coexpression networks, we used the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate method (q < 0.05) to balance sensitivity and specificity in this exploratory context. All reported p values were obtained through two-tailed statistical tests.

## Results

## Transcriptional expression of ITGBs in patients with GC
Data from TCGA data set confirmed the expression of ITGB1–8 in mammalian cells (Fig.  A and Supplementary Figures S1–S7). We compared the transcriptional levels of ITGBs between cancer and normal samples. Five ITGBs—ITGB1 (p = 0.045), ITGB2 (p < 0.001), ITGB4 (p < 0.001), ITGB5 (p = 0.027), and ITGB8 (p < 0.001)—were significantly upregulated in GC tissues compared with paired normal tissues (Fig.  B).
 

## Correlation of ITGBs expression with pathological stage and histological grade
The GEPIA data set was used to compare the mRNA expression levels of ITGBs between GC tissues and normal tissues (Fig.  ). The results showed that the expression levels of ITGB1, ITGB2, ITGB4, ITGB5, ITGB6, ITGB7, and ITGB8 (p < 0.05) were significantly higher in GC tissues than in normal tissues. In addition, elevated mRNA expressions of ITGB2 (p = 0.014) and ITGB7 (p = 0.037) were observed in patients carrying tumors with higher histological grades (Fig.  ). No association was found between the mRNA expression of ITGB3 and pathological stage or histological grade.
 

## Prognostic value of ITGBs for predicting OS in GC
The prognostic value of ITGBs and clinical data for predicting OS in patients with GC was assessed using data from TCGA data set. Univariate analysis identified the following factors to be associated with decreased OS: age over 65 years (p = 0.005), T2–T3 stage (p = 0.011), N2–N3 stage (p = 0.003), M1 stage (p = 0.004), pathological stages II–III (p < 0.001), residual tumor R1–R2 (p < 0.001), and elevated ITGB1 expression (p = 0.045). The results of multivariate analysis indicated that age over 65 years (p = 0.021), residual tumor R1–R2 (p < 0.001), and elevated ITGB1 expression (p = 0.048) may serve as biomarkers of unfavorable prognosis in patients with GC (Table  ).
 

## Prediction and analysis of differentially expressed miRNAs targeting ITGB1
To investigate whether ITGB1 expression was modulated by ncRNAs, we predicted DEmiRNAs that could bind to ITGB1. We identified a total of 29 DEmiRNAs, of which eight were selected through prognostic analysis. Expression of these eight DEmiRNAs was strongly correlated with prognosis of patients with GC (p < 0.05; Fig.  A). Correlation analysis further confirmed an association between ITGB1 expression and expression of six out of the eight DEmiRNAs in GC. Specifically, ITGB1 was significantly negatively correlated with hsa-mir-17-5p (r =  − 0.29, p < 0.001), hsa-mir-183-3p (r =  − 0.14, p = 0.018), and hsa-mir-200b-3p (r =  − 0.34, p < 0.001) and positively correlated with hsa-mir-217 (r = 0.11, p = 0.031), hsa-mir-23b-3p (r = 0.21, p < 0.001), and hsa-mir-490-3p (r = 0.36, p < 0.001) (Fig.  B). No statistically significant correlation was found between ITGB1 and the other two predicted DEmiRNAs (hsa-mir-30a-5p and hsa-mir-137). Therefore, the aforementioned six DEmiRNAs represent the most potential regulatory miRNAs for ITGB1 expression in GC.
 

## Prediction and analysis of differentially expressed lncRNAs targeting ITGB1
Using the starBase database, 18 potential lncRNAs related to ITGB1 were predicted. To exclude any overlapping results, we compared these 18 lncRNAs with those predicted using the LncBase v3 database on the basis of the six identified DEmiRNAs (Sect. “ ”). This comparison led to the identification of three specific lncRNAs: MALAT1 (log-rank p = 0.60), MIR99AHG, and OTUD6B-AS1 (log-rank p = 0.88). Among these, only MIR99AHG was significantly differentially expressed in GC tissues compared with normal tissues, according to the Kaplan–Meier plotter (log-rank p = 0.0025). Moreover, only the elevated expression of MIR99AHG was related to poorer OS (p = 0.0025; Fig.  A). In addition, correlation analysis revealed a significant positive association between ITGB1 and MIR99AHG expression (r = 0.31, p < 0.001). By contrast, ITGB1 and MIR99AHG exhibited a strong negative correlation with hsa-miR-17-5p levels (r = − 0.29, p < 0.001; r = − 0.56, p < 0.001, respectively; Fig.  B). Collectively, these findings suggest that the ITGB1/hsa-miR-17-5p/MIR99AHG regulatory axis may serve as a promising prognostic model warranting further investigation.
 

## Expression of ITGB1/MIR99AHG in GC tissues
The expression of ITGB1 and MIR99AHG in GC was further validated through IHC and ISH using our collection of GC tissues (Fig.  ). IHC demonstrated predominant cytoplasmic expression of ITGB1 in GC tissues, with significantly elevated H-scores (35.4 ± 19.2) compared with adjacent nontumor tissues (28.4 ± 16.2; p = 0.035; Fig.  A, ). Consistently, ISH analysis revealed overexpression of MIR99AHG with predominant nuclear localization in GC tissues (H-score = 32.4 ± 15.6) compared with adjacent nontumor tissues (H-score = 20.5 ± 11.0; p < 0.001; Fig.  C, ). The consistent overexpression patterns of ITGB1 and its regulatory lncRNA, MIR99AHG, signify that they play a collaborative role in promoting GC progression; thus, the potential prognostic significance of ITGB1 and MIR99AHG warrants further investigation.
 

## Correlation between immune infiltration and ITGB1/MIR99AHG expression in GC
We used the TIMER algorithm to investigate the potential relationship between ITGB1/MIR99AHG expression and immune infiltration in GC. The analysis included six types of tumor-infiltrating immune cells: B cells, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells. ITGB1 expression was found to be positively correlated with infiltration of CD4 + T cells (r = 0.17, p < 0.001), macrophages (r = 0.37, p < 0.001), and dendritic cells (r = 0. 19, p < 0.001) (Fig.  A). Conversely, ITGB1 expression exhibited no correlation with infiltration of B cells (r = 0.00, p = 0.965), CD8 + T cells (r = 0.00, p = 0.974), and neutrophils (r = 0.07, p = 0.181). We further investigated the relationship between ITGB1 expression and infiltration of M1/M2 macrophages. ITGB1 expression was significantly positively correlated with infiltration of M2 macrophages (M2_CIBERSORT-ABS: r = 0.42, p < 0.001; M2_CIBERSORT: r = 0.29, p < 0.001; Supplementary Figure S8), whereas its association with M1 macrophages was weaker (M1_CIBERSORT-ABS: r = 0.20, p = 0.002; M1_CIBERSORT: r = 0.09, p = 0.071; Supplementary Figure S9). MIR99AHG expression was positively correlated with infiltration of natural killer cells (r = 0.53, p < 0.001) and macrophages (r = 0.23, p < 0.001) but negatively correlated with infiltration of T helper 2 cells (r = − 0.39, p < 0.001), T helper 17 cells (r =  − 0.36, p < 0.001), and natural killer CD56 cells (r = − 0.15, p < 0.001) (Fig.  B). In addition, we conducted Kaplan–Meier survival analysis to investigate the impact of immune infiltration on the clinical prognosis of patients with GC (Fig.  C). The results indicated that high macrophage levels were associated with poor prognosis in patients with GC, particularly in those with a survival period of > 120 months (p = 0.004). These results suggest that tumor-infiltrating immune cells affect the clinical outcomes of ITGB1 expression in GC.
 

## Relationship between ITGB1/MIR99AHG expression and immune checkpoints in GC
To investigate the potential oncogenic role of ITGB1/MIR99AHG in GC, we assessed the relationship between ITGB1/MIR99AHG expression and four important immune checkpoints, namely, programmed cell death protein 1, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), and cluster of differentiation 28 (CD28). ITGB1 expression was significantly positively correlated with PD-L1 (r = 0.32, p < 0.001), CTLA-4 (r = 0.19, p < 0.001), and CD28 (r = 0.30, p < 0.001) in GC (Fig.  , Table  ). However, MIR99AHG expression was significantly associated only with CD28 (r = 0.28, p < 0.001; Fig.  , Table  ). These findings suggest that ITGB1-induced carcinogenesis in GC is associated with tumor-immune escape.
 

## Discussion
GC is one of the most aggressive malignancies. Despite the adoption of various therapeutic strategies, the overall prognosis for patients with GC remains unfavorable [ , ]. Clarifying the molecular mechanism underlying GC carcinogenesis is crucial for developing novel therapeutic targets and identifying reliable prognostic biomarkers to improve patient outcomes. The ncRNA regulatory network plays a pivotal role in the initiation and progression of various human cancers [ ]. However, few studies have explored the prognostic potential of the ncRNA regulatory network in GC. In this study, we first conducted expression and survival analyses to identify a key ncRNA network involving ITGB1, hsa-mir-17-5p, and MIR99AHG. Subsequently, we performed methylation and immune infiltration analyses to explore the potential biological functions of the ITGB1/MIR99AHG axis in GC.
First, we conducted a pan-cancer analysis of the expression profiles of ITGB superfamily members using TCGA data, followed by a focused analysis of their mRNA levels in GC. To validate the clinical relevance of the findings, the GEPIA database was used to examine the relationship between ITGB expression levels and prognosis in patients with GC. Previous studies have highlighted the critical role of integrin ITGB1 in the progression, invasion, and metastasis of GC. Hu et al. demonstrated that human telomerase reverse transcriptase facilitates the invasion of GC cells by enhancing the ubiquitination of FOXO3a, which subsequently upregulates ITGB1 expression [ ]. In addition, ITGB1 mediates the aberrant cell–matrix attachment and invasive behavior of cells with impaired E-cadherin [ ]. These findings are consistent with our results. Our findings uncovered a significant association between ITGB1 expression and OS in patients with GC. Notably, multivariate analysis identified ITGB1 as an independent biomarker of unfavorable prognosis in GC. These results underscore the potential of ITGB1 as a promising prognostic biomarker in GC.
Previous studies have demonstrated that miR-17-5p is overexpressed in various types of cancers, including hepatocellular carcinoma [ ], colorectal cancer [ ], lung adenocarcinoma [ ], and GC [ , ], and serves as a promising clinical prognostic marker. Xiao et al. demonstrated that miR-17-5p promotes metastatic progression by directly binding to and suppressing the activity of mitochondrial phosphatase in hepatocellular carcinoma [ ]. Moreover, miR-17-5p is highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma, and it directly targets the lncRNA MALAT1, promoting brain metastasis in lung cancer, and is associated with patient survival [ ]. In addition, miR-17-5p plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of GC by modulating stem cell characteristics, inhibiting dendritic cell maturation, and promoting cellular invasion by targeting RUNX3 and suppressor of cytokine signaling 6 [ , ]. However, no study has demonstrated a correlation between the expression levels of miR-17-5p and ITGB subfamily members. Emerging evidence highlights the context-dependent dual role of miR-17-5p in gastrointestinal cancer [ , ]. In GC, miR-17-5p enhances stemness and metastatic potential by driving epithelial–mesenchymal transition. In contrast, in colorectal cancer, miR-17-5p exerts tumor-suppressive effects by inhibiting the expression of heat shock protein beta-2 [ ]. Our data demonstrate significant miR-17-5p upregulation in GC tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues, and the upregulation was positively associated with advanced metastatic stage. Mechanistically, we identified a potential negative regulatory relationship between miR-17-5p and ITGB1, which may influence GC metastasis through integrin signaling modulation. These findings highlight the potential of miR-17-5p as both a diagnostic biomarker and a therapeutic target in GC. However, further experimental validation is required to confirm the direct interaction between miR-17-5p and ITGB1 and to elucidate the tissue-specific regulatory mechanisms underlying its dual function.
The MIR99AHG gene on chromosome 21q21.1 plays a crucial role in promoting cell proliferation and differentiation [ ]. It has been implicated in the pathogenesis of several malignancies, including lung and head and neck squamous cell carcinomas, and is associated with patient survival outcomes [ , ]. Ma et al. identified MIR99AHG as an immune-associated lncRNA significantly associated with survival in human pancreatic cancer and incorporated it into a prognostic model for pancreatic cancer [ ]. MIR99AHG has been reported to be significantly upregulated in GC, where it functions as a competing endogenous RNA for mir-577, thereby activating the Forkhead box protein 1-regulated Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [ ]. Consistent with these findings, this study also demonstrated that MIR99AHG was markedly upregulated in GC tissues, and MIR99AHG upregulation was closely associated with patient prognosis. Moreover, our findings revealed a strong correlation between the expression levels of MIR99AHG and integrin ITGB1, suggesting that MIR99AHG may contribute to integrin-mediated invasion and metastasis in GC.
Numerous recent studies have highlighted ITGB1 as a pivotal regulator of tumor-immune crosstalk across various malignancies, with distinct mechanisms driving the modulation of immune cells. This study revealed a significant positive correlation between ITGB1 expression and infiltration of immunosuppressive M2 macrophages in GC, whereas its correlation with M1 macrophages was relatively weak and less consistent across analytical methods. This finding supports the hypothesis that ITGB1 contributes to the polarization of macrophages toward the M2 phenotype. Consistent with this observation, our data indicated that ITGB1 expression was positively associated with immune checkpoint molecules (programmed cell death protein 1, PD-L1, CTLA-4), suggesting a dual role for ITGB1 in immune activation and evasion. Consistent with our findings, Saito et al. [ ] and Liu et al. [ ] demonstrated that the tumor cell-intrinsic activation of ITGB1 drives the recruitment and polarization of M2 macrophages via interleukin-6/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 signaling and chemokine crosstalk, thereby synergistically fostering an immunosuppressive microenvironment that accelerates tumor progression. Mechanistically, ITGB1 orchestrates context-dependent pathways: in metabolic liver disease, SPP1 binding activates T helper 17-driven inflammation via the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/AKT pathway; in Parkinson’s disease, CD4 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes induce endothelial ITGB1 expression through the interferon-γ/Janus kinase 2 axis, facilitating neurotoxicity [ ]. During Toxoplasma infection, dendritic cell hypermigration implicates ITGB1 in TIMP-1/CD63-FAK-RhoA/ROCK-mediated pathogen dissemination [ ]. Collectively, ITGB1 has emerged as a metabolic-immune nexus, rewiring stromal and immune cell functions through disease-specific pathways to shape tumor microenvironments [ ]. These findings underscore the potential of ITGB1 as a therapeutic target for modulating GC immunogenicity and enhancing immunotherapy responses, providing a theoretical foundation for further investigation into the mechanisms underlying ITGB1-mediated immune infiltration and its implications for improving immune microenvironments in patients.
Although bioinformatics analyses and correlative data provided a robust characterization of the MIR99AHG/miR-17-5p/ITGB1 regulatory axis, the absence of in vitro or in vivo functional validation constitutes a limitation of this study. Specifically, direct experimental evidence is necessary to establish causal relationships, such as by validating miR-17-5p targets through luciferase reporter assays, modulating pathways via MIR99AHG knockdown/rescue experiments, and assessing phenotypes following ITGB1 restoration. These mechanistic investigations are currently being prioritized in our ongoing follow-up research using GC cell lines and patient-derived organoids. Until sufficient functional evidence is obtained, the clinical interpretability of this regulatory axis remains speculative and requires cautious extrapolation. Furthermore, this study has some other limitations. First, a cohort size of 30 patients limits robust clinicopathological analyses, particularly subgroup evaluations, because of the absence of power calculations. These constraints warrant cautious interpretation of the correlative data despite the consistent ITGB1 dysregulation observed in both our cohort and TCGA data. Second, the causal roles of specific miRNAs derived from MIR99AHG in ITGB1-mediated immune microenvironment remodeling remain unverified, and experimental confirmation is lacking despite bioinformatic predictions. Third, the observed hazard ratios showed borderline statistical significance, with lower confidence intervals approximating unity and p values marginally below the conventional thresholds. This limitation in statistical power restricts robust clinical inference and necessitates cautious interpretation of survival associations. Future studies integrating prospective multiomics cohorts, larger validation samples, and experimental models will be critical to substantiate the therapeutic potential of this pathway.

## Conclusion
We systematically investigated the expression profiles and prognostic significance of ITGB superfamily members, with a particular focus on ITGB1, in GC. Our analysis identified the MIR99AHG/hsa-mir-17-5p axis as a potential upstream regulatory mechanism of ITGB1 in GC. In addition, our findings suggest that ITGB1/MIR99AHG may contribute to oncogenesis by promoting tumor-immune cell infiltration and upregulating immune checkpoint expression. Nevertheless, these observations require further validation through comprehensive mechanistic studies and large-scale clinical investigations to fully elucidate their translational potential.
