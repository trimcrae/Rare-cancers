## Highlights
•Thorough scRNA-seq Investigation of LUAD: This study offered critical insights into the tumor microenvironment of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), identifying key subtypes, particularly the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts cells, which was linked to tumor progression and poor prognosis.
•Cellular Communication Through MDK-SDC2 Ligand-Receptor pair Mechanisms: The research highlighted the role of the MDK-SDC2 ligand-receptor pair in facilitating communication between C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts cells andtumor cells, emphasizing its importance in tumor progression.
•Association of Metabolic Pathways: The C4 MYH11+ Fibroblast subtype exhibited associated biological pathways, such as calcium ion dynamics, which might have promoted the progression of LUAD.
•Advancement of a New Prognostic Assessment Model: The study constructed a prognostic model based on the MYH11+ Fibroblasts Risk Score, revealing risk genes linked to reduced survival, tumor purity, and increased sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents.
•Promising Therapeutic Targets for LUAD: The findings suggested that individualized treatment plans focused on the specific vulnerabilities of LUAD subtypes, including targeted drug therapies for high-risk and low-risk patients, had the potential to enhance clinical outcomes.

## Background
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) posed a significant threat to global human health. This study employed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to analyze transcriptomic data from nine LUAD patients at different stages of tumor infiltration, aiming to elucidate the tumor microenvironment and key biological processes of LUAD.

## Methods
In this study, we processed the scRNA-seq data using the Seurat package and sequentially applied principal component analysis followed by the Harmony package to effectively correct for batch effects, identifying 105,725 high-quality cells. Through cell clustering and gene expression profiling, we identified critical cell subpopulations and gene expression patterns in LUAD patients.

## Results
Our analysis revealed that the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts subtype was primarily involved in biological processes related to muscle function. Further investigations uncovered the MDK-SDC2 ligand-receptor pair as a critical regulator of tumor cell invasion, proliferation, and migration, driving LUAD progression. Additionally, we developed a gene-based prognostic model that effectively predicted patient survival, providing valuable clinical insights.

## Conclusion
This study provided a comprehensive atlas of the LUAD tumor microenvironment, highlighted the role of the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts in tumor progression. It also proposed the MDK-SDC2 ligand-receptor pair as a novel mechanism, addressing a significant gap in this area of research. And presented a gene-based prognostic model as a novel perspective for research into immunotherapy and drug sensitivity in LUAD.

## Introduction
Lung cancer is one of the deadliest malignant tumors. In recent years, the mortality rate among lung cancer patients has significantly increased, posing a serious threat to human life safety [ , ]. Among them, adenocarcinoma of the lung has the highest incidence rate. Despite rapid advancements in modern medical science, there are still significant challenges in early diagnosis and treatment in clinical practice [ ]. Most adenocarcinoma patients have no symptoms or exhibit hidden symptoms in the early stages, resulting in late-stage disease diagnosis in clinical settings [ ]. Clinical treatments mainly include surgery [ ], radiation therapy, chemotherapy [ ], molecular targeted therapy [ ], and immunotherapy [ ] with the aim of maximizing patient survival time, improving survival rates, controlling tumor progression and enhancing quality of life. Despite the variety of treatment options available for Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), significant limitations remain in its clinical application.
Surgery is the most effective treatment for early-stage LUAD, allowing complete tumor resection with the potential for cure in some cases. However, it is limited to early-stage patients and carries a risk of postoperative recurrence, requiring regular monitoring [ ]. Radiation therapy, particularly SBRT, offers precise local tumor control and is suitable for locally advanced or inoperable cases but may damage surrounding tissues, causing side effects like pneumonia or esophagitis [ ]. For metastatic patients, its efficacy is restricted to local control. Chemotherapy, a cornerstone for all LUAD stages, is especially critical for advanced and metastatic disease, though prolonged use may lead to drug resistance and diminished effectiveness [ ]. Immunotherapy offers the potential for long-term efficacy [ ]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (such as PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) activate the patient's immune system to target tumor cells, allowing some patients to achieve prolonged survival and sustained remission [ ]. Compared to chemotherapy, immunotherapy typically has fewer side effects, with common adverse events like fatigue, rash, and inflammation usually being manageable [ ]. This approach acts on the systemic immune system and is suitable for metastatic or widely disseminated LUAD. However, it is limited by a relatively low rate of immune response. Despite these limitations [ ], immunotherapy holds significant therapeutic potential in the treatment of LUAD. In contrast to the aforementioned therapies, single-cell sequencing offers significant advantages in the study of LUAD, providing robust support for precision medicine. Firstly, it revealed intratumoral heterogeneity, identifying key cellular subpopulations associated with therapeutic response or resistance [ ]. Secondly, single-cell sequencing enables the sensitive detection of minimal residual disease (MRD), aiding in the assessment of recurrence risk and the optimization of treatment strategies. Additionally, this technology facilitates the elucidation of mechanisms underlying tumor metastasis and drug resistance, offering novel targets for targeted and immunotherapeutic interventions.
Our research group utilized single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to analyze postoperative resected tissue samples from LUAD patients and identified a potential key role of fibroblasts in the development of LUAD. We explored the functional roles of C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts subtype within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Additionally, we identified a novel ligand-receptor pair, MDK-SDC2, which offered deeper insights into potential therapeutic targets for LUAD. This analysis filled a gap in molecular pathway research and contributed to advancing our understanding of LUAD treatment strategies. Furthermore, we constructed a prognostic model for LUAD, analyzing risk genes to identify potential immune checkpoints that might impact LUAD patients. Additionally, we aimed to explore drugs that could enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy in LUAD patients through drug sensitivity assays, thereby elucidating potential immunotherapeutic targets for adenocarcinoma and providing new perspectives for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

## Methods

## Source of LUAD patients’ data
This study utilized scRNA-seq data obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), with the accession number GSE189357, which primarily includes 9 cases of LUAD. For bulk sequencing, our research team collected data from the Cancer Genome Atlas program (TCGA) dataset (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), including clinical characteristics of patients (such as age, race, etc.) and genetic data related to somatic mutations. Since the data were derived from publicly available datasets, no ethical review was required.

## Single-Cell Sequencing and Quality Control
Data processing was carried out using R with the Seurat package. The “DoubleFinder” package was employed to eliminate double cells from the cells [ , , ]. To ensure data quality, stringent filtering criteria were applied a cell with mitochondrial gene expression below 20% and less than 5% expression of red cell genes overall. After applying these filters, high-quality cells were retained for analysis.

## Data Normalization and Clustering
Normalization was performed using Seurat's “NormalizeData” [ , , ] . To capture the most biologically relevant variation, the top 2,000 highly variable genes were identified using Seurat's “FindVariableFeatures” function [ , , ]. Next, the “ScaleData” function was used to scale the expression values. Principal component analysis (PCA) [ , ] was then performed to reduce dimensionality, followed by the use of the “Harmony” package to correct for batch effects across samples [ ].
To account for cell cycle effects, cell cycle phases were assigned using “CellCycleScoring”. For clustering, the components, based on the top 30 principal components, were used with Seurat's “FindNeighbors” and “FindClusters” functions to optimize the clustering process [ , , , ]. Visualization of gene expression patterns was performed using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) [ , , , , ]. We clustered cells into distinct types and annotated them as known cell types.

## Identification of Cell Subtypes
After selecting fibroblasts, the data were re-normalized, and the identification of highly variable genes and standardization functions were employed to ensure consistency across the dataset. PCA was then performed, followed by “Harmony” package to adjust for batch effects. These steps facilitated the reclustering distinct cell subtypes, allowing for a more accurate characterization of their heterogeneity.

## Cell Preference Analysis
In order to assess the cell preference of different cell types, odds ratios (OR) were calculated using the designated computational approach.

## Identification of Cells via inferCNV
To analyze genomic copy number variations (CNVs) using the inferCNV package. Endothelial cells (ECs) served as a reference, CNVs were detected, revealing genomic instability within cancerous cells. And inferred the CNVs of cell types based on the ECs' inferCNV analysis.

## Cell Lineage and Pseudotime Analysis
To predict the stemness of fibroblast subtypes, the R package CytoTRACE was utilized. Furthermore, the slingshot package was used to infer cell trajectories. The inferred pseudotime trajectories allowed the mapping of LUAD evolution and differentiation pathways, providing insights into cellular progression and heterogeneity. Branching patterns were fitted with smooth curves using the “getCurves” function.

## Evaluation of Stemness
To evaluate the stemness properties of cells, the AUCell method was employed [ ]. Gene sets associated with stemness were used as input, and the activity of these gene sets was assessed in individual cells. By quantifying stemness-related gene expression profiles, we identified cell types with enhanced stem-like properties, offering a deeper understanding cell heterogeneity [ ] .

## Enrichment Analysis of Cell types
Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified across clusters using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test via Seurat's “FindAllMarkers” function [ , ]. Enrichment analyses of these DEGs were conducted using the “ClusterProfiler” package [ ], utilizing Gene Ontology (GO) [ , , ] and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases [ , , , , , ]. Besides, GO classified genes into three main categories: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC) [ ]. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was utilized to perform enrichment analysis on gene expression data using these gene sets, revealing whether specific biological processes, molecular functions, or pathways were enriched between different groups.

## Cell-Cell Communication Networks
To dissect intercellular communication within the TME, the CellChat package was employed. Furthermore, we assessed communication strengths and patterns between cell types. In addition, we quantified distinct signaling pathways and ligand-receptor interactions [ , ].

## Regulatory Network Inference: pySCENIC Analysis
Regulatory networks governing gene expression were inferred using PySCENIC in Python. The process involved two major steps: GRNBoost was used to identify transcription factors (TFs) and their target genes, followed by motif enrichment analysis to refine the inferred networks. AUCell scoring was applied to quantify the activity of gene regulatory networks (regulons), highlighting the top 5 TFs with the most pronounced expression changes, offering insights into the transcriptional control of cell types.

## Development and Validation of a Prognostic Model
A gene-based prognostic model for LUAD was constructed to predict patient survival. We first screened for prognosis-associated genes using univariate Cox regression analysis [ , , , ]. Subsequently, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was applied to reduce dimensionality and identify the set of genes with optimal predictive power [ ]. Finally, multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to validate the independent predictive capacity of these genes, identifying the most prognostic ones [ ]. A risk scoring model was then developed, where the risk score was computed as a weighted sum of gene expression levels multiplied by their respective coefficients.RiskScore=∑inXi×Yi
Using the optimized cutoff from the “surv_cutpoint” function, patients were stratified into low-risk and high-risk groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis [ , ] was performed, and the model's performance was assessed using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves generated via the “timeROC” package [ , ]. The model's discriminatory ability was evaluated using the ROC curve, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The AUC ranged from 0.5 to 1, with higher values indicating better classification performance [ , , ].

## Tumor Mutational Burden Classification
Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was calculated based on the somatic mutations identified in the tumor samples, with mutations per megabase (mut/Mb) used to classify tumors into high and low TMB groups [ , ].

## Survival Nomogram Development
A nomogram was constructed to predict overall survival in patients, incorporating significant clinical variables such as age, stage, risk groups, and race [ , , ]. The nomogram's predictive accuracy was evaluated through concordance index and calibration curves [ , , ].

## Immune Microenvironment and Immunotherapy Response
The immune composition within the TME was analyzed using the “xCell”, “ESTIMATE” and “CIBERSORT” package [ , , ]. Immune infiltration scores were calculated for various immune cell types, and the differential expression of immune checkpoint-related genes was investigated [ ]. Correlations between immune cell populations, gene expression profiles, and risk scores were analyzed to understand the tumor's immune landscape [ , ].

## Drug Sensitivity Analysis
To explore drug sensitivity, the “pRRophetic” package was used to estimate the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of anticancer drugs for each patient sample. Drug sensitivity was precisely predicted by integrating pharmacogenomic data with patient-specific molecular characteristics, thereby supporting personalized treatment strategies.

## Statistical Analysis
All data analyses were conducted using R and Python software [ ]. A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all comparisons [ , ]. The reproducibility of results was ensured by adopting rigorous statistical methodologies across the study.

## ScRNA-seq revealed a landscape of unicellular and fibroblast subtypes of LUAD

Firstly, to investigate the tissue characteristics of different samples in LUAD, we conducted a comprehensive and systematic study on the cellular composition of LUAD tissues using single-cell sequencing. Initially, this data set comprises tissue samples from 9 patients with LUAD of varying genders, including adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), and invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC). Following initial quality control and unsupervised screening, we obtained a total of 105,725 high-quality cells. ## Pseudotime trajectories analysis of fibroblast subtypes during the process of differentiation
To further elucidate the role of distinct fibroblast subtypes in the pathogenesis and progression of LUAD, we employed CytoTRACE to predict their ordering. The findings revealed that subtype C3 RUNX3+ Fibroblasts exhibited the highest score, closely followed by subtypes C0 and C4 ( A). The greater the score, the more underdeveloped its form and the higher its potential for differentiation. Hence, we hypothesized that the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts exhibited a heightened capacity for differentiation. In line with this, we analyzed the CytoTRACE scores and corresponding distribution characteristics of each subtype, which corroborated our previous conclusion ( B). Notably, compared to other subtypes, subtype C4 displayed relatively heightened malignancy. Therefore, to gain deeper insights into the mechanism of action exerted by fibroblasts, we conducted Slingshot analysis ( C-D), unveiling two predominant lineages governing the evolution of fibroblast subtypes: Lineage 1 originated from the C2 subtype and terminated at the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts, following the specific trajectory of C2 → C1 → C0 → C4. (“→” indicated the orientation of differentiation along the pseudotemporal trajectory.) Lineage 2 originated from the C2 IRF8+ Fibroblasts and terminated at the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts, following the specific trajectory of C2 → C1 → C0 → C3. In Lineage 1, the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts predominantly corresponded to LUAD tissue samples known as IAC, which exhibited a higher degree of malignancy and enhanced capabilities in cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Consequently, when comparing Lineage 1 with Lineage 2, it became evident that Lineage 1 aligned more closely with the evolutionary progression from normal tissue to tumor cells. As time progresses during this developmental process, tumor malignancy increased while patients' conditions deteriorate further. Moreover, we conducted a comprehensive analysis encompassing both lineages, where heatmaps were employed to respectively display cell types and pseudotemporal analysis process alongside S.Score and G2/M.Score trend changes for each lineage ( E). Furthermore, based on the pseudotemporal variation characteristics of these two lineages, we divided fibroblast subtypes into 4 distinct clusters to elucidate time-sequential expression level changes for different genes within each lineage. Additionally, GO-BP analysis was performed on these 4 clusters and results were depicted in the heatmap. In conclusion, we had reason to believe that the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts played a promoting role in the progression of LUAD.

## Building a prognostic model and predicting risk factors
Next, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis, LASSO regression analysis and multivariate Cox regression analysis to determine whether the risk score could serve as an independent prognostic factor for LUAD patients compared to other common clinical-pathological parameters. Our focus was on discussing the risk factors. Firstly, using univariate Cox regression analysis ( A), we identified 19 genes associated with prognosis, where HR>1 indicated a risk factor detrimental to patient prognosis, while HR<1 indicated a protective factor beneficial to patient prognosis. To avoid multiple linear effects and ensure the reliability of our findings, we conducted LASSO regression analysis which resulted in selecting 14 prognostically relevant genes ( B). Furthermore, genes risk coefficient analysis was conducted on these selected genes ( C). Subsequently, the forest plot presented the results of multivariate analysis for age, race, T, N, M, and risk score. ( D). These variables were then amalgamated to derive a cumulative score for predicting the probability of overall survival at 1-year, 3-year and 5-year ( E). To evaluate the predictive accuracy of this model, we analyzed the cross-validation AUC performance at different time points ( F). The results showed that the median C-index values at each time point were close to the range of 0.5 to 0.75, indicating that the model's performance was relatively stable over the long-term predictions. Additionally, the similarity in C-index distribution across 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year suggested minimal variation in predictive performance over time. Overall, this figure demonstrated that the model maintained stable performance in predictions at 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year, with a reasonably good level of predictive accuracy. The calibration curve demonstrated a strong alignment between the prognostic predictions derived from the nomogram and actual observed values pertaining to survival probabilities at 1-year, 3-year and 5-year ( G). This was consistent with the conclusions obtained from F. In addition, we generated risk curves and survival scatter plots for high and low-risk groups ( H), revealing that the high-risk group exhibited a significantly worse prognosis with an increased likelihood of mortality compared to the low-risk group. Moreover, we performed a visualization analysis based on the differing expression levels of risk genes in the high and low-risk groups ( I). In addition, Kaplan-Meier analysis was performed on the high and low-risk groups ( J), revealing a significant disparity in overall survival rates between the two cohorts. Over time, patients in the high-risk group exhibited lower survival rates compared to those in the low-risk group. The AUC curve ( K) demonstrated close concordance between predicted values derived from our survival analysis and actual observed values, indicating a robust predictive model. Lastly, differential expression of risk genes between the high-risk and low-risk groups was visualized using a rain-cloud plot ( L). Genes such as ACTB, COL4A1, COL4A2, PPP1R12A, MAP1B, MGST3, PMEPA1, SMTN, VCL, and SEMA3C were found to be significantly upregulated in the high-risk group consistent with our heatmap analysis results. These genes were significantly expressed in the high-risk group, often indicating a poorer prognosis.

## Reassessment of immune infiltration in cohorts with high-risk and low-risk profiles
Subsequently, we conducted an analysis of the tumor immune microenvironment in LUAD tissue to characterize distinct cellular immune infiltration patterns. To assess immune infiltration in high and low-risk groups, box plots were employed ( A). The results revealed higher proportions of Macrophages M0, Macrophages M1, T cells CD4 memory activated, Neutrophils in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group. Notably, these cells were classified as immune cells. Therefore, it could be postulated that the poor prognosis observed in patients with LUAD was linked to somatic mutations in immune cells, leading to immune evasion and disease recurrence. Furthermore, we investigated the correlation between risk scores and immune infiltration. Additionally, Macrophages M0 and T cells CD4 memory activated exhibited more pronounced abundance of immune infiltration ( B). We also evaluated Estimate Score, Stromal Score, and Tumor Purity for both high-risk and low-risk groups. The high-risk group demonstrated significantly elevated Estimate Score and Stromal Score compared to the low-risk group ( C). Consistent with this finding, box plots indicated that Estimate Score and Stromal Score were more prominently expressed in the high-risk group ( D). To comprehensively investigate the heterogeneity of high-risk and low-risk groups, we visually represented the risk scores, risk genes, Stromal Score, Immune Score, Estimate Score, Tumor Purity, immune cells, as well as immune infiltration and expression levels of immune checkpoints using a heatmap ( E). To elucidate the interaction targets between risk factors and immune checkpoints, we depicted their relationship through a bubble chart ( F). In this analysis, we observed a positive correlation between OS and CD200R1 but negative correlations with CD276 and TNFSF14. This implied that higher expression of CD200R1 was associated with improved patient overall survival rates while higher expression of CD276 and TNFSF14 was linked to reduced patient overall survival rates. Risk exhibited a positive correlation with CD200, CD274, and CD276, among others, while displaying a negative correlation with ADORA2A, BTLA, CD200R1, and TNFSF15, among others. Furthermore, due to the DEGs levels of various immune checkpoints between high and low-risk groups, we conducted a visualization analysis ( G). In addition, we conducted an analysis of immune drug sensitivity to compare the sensitivities of CTLA4 and PD1 immune checkpoint inhibitors between the high- and low-risk groups ( H). Consequently, potential targets for immunotherapy could be analyzed. 

## Screening for cellular gene mutations and drug sensitivities
Initially, a waterfall plot was employed to illustrate the genetic alterations across diverse tissue sources of the samples and assess the TMB comprehensively ( A). Subsequently, the heatmap displayed the degree of co-occurrence or mutually exclusive of various gene mutations ( B). In particular, the degree of co-occurrence between DMD and COL4A1, as well as MAP1B and COL4A2, was more significant among these pairs. Furthermore, an analysis of the CNV scores for risk factors revealed that MGST3 and PMEPA1 exhibited a higher proportion of CNV-gain compared to other genes ( C). Subsequently, TMB analysis was conducted on both cohorts, namely the high-risk and low-risk groups, revealing a significantly higher expression level in the high-risk group ( D). Consequently, it could be deduced that an elevated TMB was associated with an unfavorable prognosis. The scatter plot ( E) illustrated the positive correlation between TMB and risk score, indicating that more pronounced TMB were associated with higher risk scores. Furthermore, we performed paired analysis of high-risk and low-risk groups based on TMB to establish 4 categories, enabling us to investigate overall survival rates among distinct patient cohorts. The findings suggested that both high/low-risk and TMB significantly impact patient survival ( F). Lastly, in the high-risk group, we conducted drug sensitivity screening and identified 6 drugs exhibiting sensitivity towards LUAD tumor cells. These drugs demonstrated relatively low IC50 values and could be administered at lower doses for effective treatment of LUAD patients ( G). 

## Discussion
As of now, despite significant advancements in medical technology, LUAD remains one of the most lethal diseases for us, with a generally poor prognosis for patients. However, there exists a certain degree of heterogeneity among patients. Therefore, to explore the differences, prognostic indicators, and intervention measures for LUAD patients, we employed single-cell sequencing to analyze the tissue cells from LUAD samples.
First, we collected samples from 9 patients with varying stages of LUAD progression and categorized the high-quality cells into 7 known cell types. We then observed that fibroblasts highly expressed a gene that has the potential to promote tumors. To further determine which subtypes are more closely associated with the progression of LUAD, we subdivided fibroblasts into 5 subtypes. Among them, the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts drew our attention, as it was more aligned with the most malignant form of IAC.
In addition, it also highly expressed the MYH11 gene. It was worth mentioning that existing research had shown a potential association between changes in MYH11 expression levels and lung cancer [ ]. Furthermore, abnormal expression of MYH11 was associated with the progression of various malignant tumors, such as bladder cancer, head and neck cancer, stomach cancer and so on [ , , ]. MYH11 might play a role in tumor cells by regulating cytoskeleton, enhancing cell migration, influencing cell proliferation and division, participating in gene fusion mechanism and other biological processes, thus promoting tumor growth and metastasis [ ].
In our study, the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts contributed to the progression of LUAD tumor cells. Subsequent GO-BP enrichment analysis confirmed this hypothesis. The C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts primarily functions through biological processes related to the muscle system. This process is linked to tumor cells in terms of dynamic cytoskeletal regulation, enhanced migration and invasion abilities, signaling pathway interactions, and abnormal regulation of muscle differentiation and development. Tumor cells exploit these mechanisms to promote growth, spread, and resistance to apoptosis [ ]. Furthermore, tumor progression was often accompanied by muscle wasting and atrophy, which explains why patients with advanced malignancies typically exhibit poor physical condition and weight loss [ ]. Additionally, the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts showed high CNV scores and CytoTRACE prediction scores, further demonstrating its differentiation potential and tumor-promoting capabilities.
Next, to clarify the specific mechanisms between LUAD cells and fibroblasts, we studied the interactions between fibroblast subtypes and other cells. The results revealed that tumor cells had a close quantitative and qualitative interaction with fibroblast subtypes. The characteristics of tumor cells allow them to proliferate uncontrollably, evade apoptosis and immune surveillance, and metastasize. Their unlimited proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, angiogenesis, and genomic instability are key factors in cancer development and progression [ ]. Non-cancerous cells in the TME were reprogrammed by tumor cells to support their growth and spread. These microenvironmental cells promoted angiogenesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, and even suppress anti-tumor immune responses, further aiding tumor progression. Subsequently, we explored specific pathway functions and focused on the MK pathway. The MK pathway played multiple roles in LUAD, including promoting tumor cell proliferation, enhancing invasiveness and metastatic potential, stimulating angiogenesis, inhibiting apoptosis, and regulating immune evasion. Specifically, the MK pathway upregulated matrix metalloproteinases, uPA, and uPAR, promoting tumor cell invasion [ ]. It also activated cell cycle-related genes and anti-apoptotic genes, thereby enhancing cell proliferation. Additionally, the MK pathway regulated cell adhesion molecules and Rho GTPases, further promoting tumor cell migration [ ]. These effects made MK a potential therapeutic target for LUAD [ ]. Blocking the MK pathway could help inhibit the progression and metastasis of LUAD, improving patient prognosis. Furthermore, studies had shown that the MDK-NCL ligand-receptor pair could serve as a potential therapeutic target in LUAD patients. To further investigate the mechanism of the MK pathway, we explored another cellular ligand-receptor pair, MDK-SDC2, which had been identified as a potential therapeutic target in other cancers, such as serous ovarian carcinoma [ ]. Subsequently, we conducted an analysis of TFs. Notably, several TFs were prominently expressed in the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts. We focused our analysis on THRB, CUX1, and GATA6. THRB had a dual role, on the one hand could inhibit tumor development, on the other hand can also promote tumor growth. In breast cancer, liver cancer and other tumors, THRB up-regulation is closely related to tumor proliferation, invasion and metastasis [ , , ]. In this study, THRB might have a role in promoting the development of lung adenocarcinoma. CUX1 could participate in normal physiological processes of the human body. Nevertheless, overexpression of CUX1 had been associated with the progression of multiple tumors, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and lung cancer [ , ]. CUX1 played a pivotal role in promoting tumorigenesis and malignant transformation by stimulating cell proliferation while inhibiting apoptosis [ ]. GATA6 exhibited heightened expression levels in diverse solid tumors such as pancreatic cancer, gastric cancer, and lung cancer [ , , ]. It potentially drove tumor progression through enhancing cellular proliferation, suppressing apoptotic processes, and regulating metabolic pathways [ ]. These interactions among TFs in tumor cells offer valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying tumor initiation and progression, thereby presenting potential therapeutic targets. In our analysis of stemness-related genes, we found that EPAS1 and CTNNB1 were highly expressed in the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts and were relatively prominent in IAC samples. Previous studies indicated that EPAS1 and CTNNB1 exhibited tumor-promoting effects, primarily by maintaining cellular stemness and drug resistance [ , , ]. Additionally, we constructed a high-quality prognostic model using univariate Cox regression analysis, LASSO regression analysis, and multivariate Cox regression analysis, identifying 14 risk genes [ , ]. We could infer overexpression or aberrant expression of COL4A1 might facilitate angiogenesis, tumor cell migration, and metastasis in specific cancer types. For instance, abnormal COL4A1 expression has been associated with increased invasiveness in breast cancer, gliomas, and other malignancies [ , ]. In certain cancers, COL4A1 was believed to be implicated in drug resistance by modulating the cellular response to chemotherapy drugs through regulation of cell-ECM interaction [ ]. PPP1R12A participated in cell migration, movement, and augmented invasiveness by governing dynamic changes in the cellular cytoskeleton. The migratory ability of tumor cells represents a critical step in tumor metastasis; hence PPP1R12A played a pivotal role in regulating tumor cell invasiveness. Alterations in SMTN expression were linked to the degree of malignancy and prognosis of tumors [ ]. We could speculate that elevated expression might correlate with the differentiation status of tumor cells while reduced expression might indicate dedifferentiation and heightened invasiveness. The coordinated action of these genes was crucial in tumor invasion, metastasis, and the modulation of the TME [ ]. Further investigation might elucidate their specific mechanisms underlying tumor development and progression.
Based on survival outcomes and DEGs, we divided patients into high-risk and low-risk groups. In the high-risk group, M1 Macrophages and other myeloid cells were present in higher proportions, leading us to speculate that genes in the high-risk group might help tumor cells evade immune surveillance by regulating immunosuppressive cells or inhibiting the activity of immune cells, thereby promoting immune evasion. To better predict potential therapeutic targets from an immunological perspective, we studied immune checkpoints [ , ], finding that many genes in the high-risk group were highly expressed. Among these, CD276 had the higher expression level. Previous studies have demonstrated the pivotal role of CD276 in non-small cell lung cancer, particularly with regards to prognosis, as its overexpression is often indicative of an unfavorable prognosis [ ]. Overexpression of CD276 in tumor cells might be closely related to immune evasion, invasiveness, metastasis and poor prognosis. Therefore, we inferred that CD276 primarily suppressed T-cell immune responses, enhances tumor cell proliferation and survival, and played a crucial role in the development of tumors [ ]. CD276 was a promising therapeutic target, especially in the field of immunotherapy [ ]. Targeting CD276 could provide new treatment options for cancer patients.
We then further examined the risk factors and found a positive correlation between TMB and risk score, indicating that the higher the TMB, the greater the risk. Soon afterwards, to assess drug sensitivity in the high and low-risk groups, we conducted drug sensitivity experiments, identifying 6 drugs with good efficacy. Among them, Shikonin showed the best results. Shikonin offered multiple advantages in treating LUAD, including inducing apoptosis in LUAD cells and blocking their cell cycle through various mechanisms [ ]. Shikonin also exhibits dual antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation [ ]. This was particularly important for LUAD because oxidative stress and chronic inflammation are often associated with the onset and progression of cancer [ ]. Overall, Shikonin demonstrates significant advantages in inducing tumor cell apoptosis, inhibiting cell proliferation, reducing invasion and metastasis, regulating metabolism, and enhancing the effects of chemotherapy and radiotherapy [ , ]. Its anti-tumor mechanisms and natural origin suggested that Shikonin held potential as a novel therapeutic agent for LUAD [ ]. However, despite its promising potential in cancer therapy, the potential toxicity of Shikonin could not be overlooked. Current studies suggested that Shikonin might exhibit some degree of hepatotoxicity, particularly at high doses. Additionally, it could cause gastrointestinal discomfort, such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. While Shikonin showed selective toxicity toward cancer cells, its potential effects on normal cells still required further investigation. In our study, although Shikonin demonstrated favorable drug sensitivity predictions, more safety assessments and toxicity studies were needed before its clinical translation. These investigations were crucial for developing personalized treatment strategies and balancing the efficacy and safety of the drug. Future studies might further reveal its clinical value. Although we conducted a systematic and comprehensive analysis, our study had several limitations. First, the dataset was somewhat limited; while this research analyzed single-cell samples from 9 patients with LUAD, the relatively small sample size may have restricted the generalizability of the results, particularly for different types or stages of LUAD. However, given the complexity and heterogeneity of LUAD, particularly in certain subtypes or early-stage cases, a small sample size is an unavoidable reality. We have minimized potential biases by applying strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. Future studies should include larger patient cohorts to validate these findings and further enhance the robustness and generalizability of the conclusions. Furthermore, despite performing numerous visual analyses, our study primarily remained theoretical and lacked experimental validation. Therefore, we planned to conduct in vitro and in vivo experiments based on this groundwork. Thus, it was essential to pursue more in-depth exploration.

## Conclusion
In summary, we have demonstrated the landscape of the TME in LUAD utilizing available data and identified crucial cell clusters, particularly the C4 MYH11+ Fibroblasts of fibroblasts. We have observed that this subtype exerts a pro-tumor effect by influencing the tumor through the MDK-SDC2 ligand-receptor pair. Furthermore, we have developed a prognostic model to identify potential targets for immunotherapy and drug sensitivity in this disease. Our findings aim to contribute to the application of immune-based approaches in treating LUAD.
