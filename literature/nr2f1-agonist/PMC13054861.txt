## Effects of Dex Treatment on Protein and Phosphopeptide Abundances
To achieve an integrative understanding of GC responses in different
breast cancer cell lines, Dex-induced changes in protein and phosphopeptides
were statistically evaluated using Student’s t test with permutation-based false discovery rate (FDR) correction.
GR is known to be hyper-phosphorylated upon ligand binding, and its
transcriptional activity is terminated by proteasomal degradation. h,i).
As expected from
previous findings, the MDA-MB-231 cell line presented the highest
GR expression in proteome, followed by MDA-MB-436 and MCF7 cells (Figure S1b). The basal level of pan-GR corresponded
to the Dex-mediated effect, as indicated by the counts of significantly
regulated protein groups and phosphosites ( j,k). Substantially fewer significant regulations
observed in MCF7 cells with zero protein groups and 67 phosphosites,
together with the absence of detectable phosphorylated GR by LC-MS/MS,
likely result from low endogenous pan-GR levels. Based on these findings,
further analyses were performed for only MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-436
cells.
Dex treatment induced significant abundance changes in
211 protein
groups in MDA-MB-231 cells and 72 protein groups in MDA-MB-436 cells,
with 27 proteins overlapping between the two cell lines. Common proteins
included GR and its well-established downstream effectors such as
FK506-binding protein 51 (FKBP5), a). In MDA-MB-231 cells, ∼81.5% (172
out of 211) of proteins with differential abundance changes were identified
as direct transcriptional targets of 13 TFs, excluding ZNF250 and
PER1 ( b and Table S5). Dex-induced effects in proteome were
supported by pronounced fold-change of FKBP5, a well-established GR
transcriptional target gene. Additionally, GR regulated the largest
number of transcriptional targets among all TFs.
 Next, functional pathway enrichment analysis was
performed with
172 transcriptional targets that were significantly regulated in MDA-MB-231
cells. The top five pathways were selected based on signal using the
STRING database d and Table S6). WikiPathways revealed that these proteins were associated with
the GR pathway (WP2880) and nuclear receptors meta-pathway (WP2882),
as anticipated. Additional enriched pathways included Gene Ontology
(GO) biological processes such as the regulation of cell adhesion
(GO:0030155), positive regulation of cardiac EMT (GO:0062043), and
regulation of cell motility (GO:2000145). Collectively, these findings
suggest that Dex treatment triggers proteomic rewiring of multiple
TFs and proteins associated with breast cancer cell metastasis in
the MDA-MB-231 cell line.

## Dex-Induced GR Transcriptional Activity in MDA-MB-436 Cells
To assess the differences driven by the BRCA1 mutation
in TNBC cells, the effect of Dex treatment was investigated in MDA-MB-436
cells. The same computational approach based on the TFLink database
was implemented to identify 37 transcriptional target genes significantly
regulated by 10 TFs in the proteome profile (Table S5). Interestingly, GR was not the topmost regulating TF, but
a prominent increase of FKBP5 abundance confirmed Dex-mediated effect
in MDA-MB-436 cells ( a,b). Similar to the result of MDA-MB-231 cells, the top five
functional pathways by STRING analysis revealed that these transcriptional
targets were enriched in GC-associated signaling pathways, including
the GR pathway (WP2880), nuclear receptors meta-pathway (WP2882),
and negative regulation of GR signaling pathway (GO:2000323). Interestingly,
they were also involved in melatonin metabolism and effects (WP3298)
and response to insulin (GO:0032868) ( c and Table S6).
 

## GSK3A/B Activity Suppression by Dex in MDA-MB-436 Cells
Next, we performed an integrative analysis combining the proteome
and phosphoproteome data from MDA-MB-436 cells. None of the 37 transcriptional
targets with significant abundance changes were identified as kinases
in the proteome. Among significantly regulated TFs, the phosphorylation
levels of SOX9 Thr-236, JUN Thr-239, and JUN Ser-243 were suppressed
by Dex ( d).
These three phosphosites are direct substrates of GSK3A and GSK3B,
whose activity was predicted to be suppressed by the RoKAI algorithm
( e). Conversely,
the total and phosphorylated levels of GSK3 proteins were not affected
by Dex (Figure S3a,b), indicating other
post-translational regulation of GSK3 activity after Dex treatment.
The flanking region of all 1709 significantly downregulated phosphosites
was enriched for the pS-xxx-S motif, which is a conserved recognition
motif of GSK3 f). GSK3 transcriptional targets were significantly
downregulated, except for CRMP1 in the phosphoproteome, further supporting
the inactivation of GSK3 kinases ( g).

## Discussion
Breast cancer is a prevailing cause of cancer-related
deaths in
females worldwide. Its treatment strategies include surgery, chemotherapy,
targeted therapy, and radiotherapy. To
prevent the known side effects of these treatments such as vomiting
and nausea, systemic GCs are often prescribed in combination with
conventional drugs. In this study, we
acquired the proteome and phosphoproteome of three breast cancer cell
lines after Dex treatments to better understand the regulatory mechanisms
of GCs. To date, this is the first study that compares the protein-level
signatures of GC responses in multiple breast cancer cell lines.
We confirm the biological diversity of MCF7, MDA-MB-231, and MDA-MB-436
cell lines in both the proteome and the phosphoproteome profiles.
PCA analysis revealed cell line-dependent clustering, and high Pearson
correlation coefficients were observed within cell lines. Significantly
regulated protein and phosphosite counts were comparably negligible
in MCF7 cells, which can be explained by the substantially lower protein
abundance of GR in MCF7 cells compared with that in MDA-MB-231 and
MDA-MB-436 cells. These results suggest that proteomic diversity,
especially the endogenous protein abundance of GR, is a major factor
influencing GC responses in breast cancer cells.
However, the
protein dynamics observed in this study must be interpreted
with caution in relation to the tumor subtypes. MCF7 cells and MDA-MB-231
cells are predominant cell models for breast cancer metastasis research,
summing up to over 80% of the total literature cited in PubMed. Yet,
they present limited genomic similarities to patient samples derived
from the corresponding luminal B or basal-like subtypes, respectively. High heterogeneity of TNBC cells also emphasizes
the need for additional validation to confirm subtype-specific responses.
Although MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-436 cells are both classified as mesenchymal-like
TNBC cells, they exhibit profoundly different phenotypes. Moreover,
the MDA-MB-231 cell line can be clustered into two cell populations
by elasticity and softness. As expected,
MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-436 cells displayed variable kinase and TF signaling
after Dex treatment, as depicted by global proteomic and phosphoproteomic
analyses. Therefore, it remains to be further investigated whether
MCF7, MDA-MB-231, and MDA-MB-436 cell lines are representative models
of breast cancer metastasis.
Ensuing, we demonstrate that Dex
treatment modulated the activity
of multiple TFs in MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-436 cell lines. The transcriptional
activity of GR was promoted after treatment, indicated by upregulation
of FKBP5 protein levels and functional pathway enrichment in GR-associated
signaling. Hyper-phosphorylation of GR, however, was not detected
in the phosphoproteome. This may be due to missing phosphorylation
signals for its functionally known residues, Ser-203, Ser-211, and
Ser-226. Further studies are necessary
to clarify whether phosphorylation at Ser-267 and Thr-8 residues relates
to its transcriptional activity.
Here, we suggest several noncanonical
TFs that are regulated by
Dex treatment. Phosphorylation of CEBPD and ZFP36L2 significantly
increased in the MDA-MB-231 cell line, and their transcriptional target
genes were overexpressed in distant breast cancer tumors. Transcriptional
activity of CEBPD can be enhanced by phosphorylation at Ser-167 by
GSK3B and at Ser-191 by PKA. Consistent with this, we observed increased
protein levels of ten transcriptional target genes of CEBPD, including
CEBPB, which is a well-recognized factor that promotes breast cancer
metastasis.
−

 However, further investigation is necessary to determine whether
phosphorylation of CEBPD at Ser-256 impacts its transcriptional activity,
as protein abundance of the other ten transcriptional targets was
significantly downregulated.
ZFP36L2 was another TF that was
phospho-upregulated after Dex treatment
in the MDA-MB-231 cell line. ZFP36L2 can be phosphorylated by LPS
stimulation or ERK, thereby promoting mRNA stabilization. SERPINE1, a transcriptional
target of ZFP36L2 according to the TFLink database, was outstandingly
increased in the proteome following Dex treatment. Recent reports
underscore the function of SERPINE1 in driving aggressiveness, chemotherapy
resistance, and metastasis of breast cancer.
−

 SNAI2, a major
TF in EMT that transcriptionally targets ZFP36L2 and SERPINE1, was
significantly increased in the level of phosphorylation at Ser-87.
It has been previously reported that SNAI2 phosphorylation at Ser-87
is induced by ERK, and we have recently
found that GC treatments promote ERK activation through phosphorylation
at Thr-202/Tyr-204. Collectively, these
findings support our hypothesis that Dex treatment drives transcriptional
and phosphorylation changes in metastasis-related proteins in the
MDA-MB-231 cell line.
Our data suggest that GSK3 catalytic kinase
activity was greatly
suppressed in Dex-treated MDA-MB-436 cells, thereby downregulating
phosphorylation of JUN and SOX9 among the significantly regulated
TFs. GSK3 is a potential target of metastatic breast cancer and GSK3B
inhibitors suppress mesenchymal and stem cell-like properties in TNBC.
,
 In this sense, a systemic GC treatment may be beneficial. However,
proteins that displayed increased phosphorylation were enriched in
the signaling pathways associated with cytoskeletal organization,
implying that cells can be more migratory. In vitro cell-based assays can be performed to validate cell migration and
invasion in breast cancer for additional analyses.
Here, limited
biological evidence is suggested based on the proteome
and phosphoproteome profiles obtained from in vitro cell lines. Importantly, TF activities were evaluated solely based
on the TFLink database. Integration of other TF databases, such as
ChEA3 or JASPAR, can help elucidate the Dex-induced effects highlighted in this study.
For example, enrichment analysis using ChEA3 or the DNA-binding information
available from JASPAR can be used to confirm transcriptional regulations
driven by the SNAI-ZFP36L2-SERPINE1 axis in MDA-MB-231 cells and by
CDK7 in MDA-MB-436 cells.
Lastly, note that the phosphoproteome
analysis was performed without
normalizing phosphopeptide intensities to their corresponding protein
intensities. The risk associated with normalization arises from varying
sample preparation and LC-MS/MS analytical workflows, which may result
in missing protein groups in the proteomic data set and consequently
lead to statistical bias. Instead, we show that Dex-induced fold-changes
in the proteome and phosphoproteome are linearly correlated, in general.
Additionally, scatter plots that display the differences of the highlighted
Dex-regulated phosphopeptides and the corresponding protein fold-changes
are provided to support unbiased interpretations (Figure S3c,d).
In this study, we provide a useful resource
for the breast cancer
research community by comparing Dex responses in three breast cancer
cell lines with differential pan-GR abundances. Our data suggest that
Dex treatment regulates proteins associated with breast cancer metastasis
through phosphorylation and transcriptional rewiring. However, it
is crucial to note that this study focuses on the protein- and peptide-level
modulations, and further molecular studies are required to confirm
the underlying biological mechanisms.
