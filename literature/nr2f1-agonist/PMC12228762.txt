## Abstract
Previous studies have demonstrated the detrimental effects of lactate on patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). In this study, six lactate-related hub genes (SLC16A7, GFM1, PDP1, TRMT5,COX5A, and KIF23) were identified in ESCC. Significant differences were observed in various immune cell populations, including naive B cells and memory B cells, between ESCC and normal samples. scRNA-seq analysis revealed distinct cell subpopulations, such as T cells, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells. The lactylation score was notably greater in the ESCC samples than in the normal samples. Further comparison of lactylation scores across subpopulations revealed significantly elevated levels of monocytes, B cells, epithelial cells, and neutrophils in ESCC samples, whereas T cells and fibroblasts had higher scores in normal samples. CellChat analysis revealed that lactylation-associated subpopulations, particularly epithelial and immune cells, presented increased intercellular communication in the high-lactylation group. Additionally, KIF23, TRMT5, TEFM, SLC25A13, TIMM50, DGUOK, DNM1L and COX5A exhibited trends consistent with expectations in the two ESCC cell lines. This study identified six hub genes relevant to ESCC, offering a theoretical foundation for potential therapeutic approaches in ESCC.

## Introduction
Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains one of the most aggressive malignancies and has a high global incidence and mortality rate. In 2022, an estimated 511,054 new cases of esophageal cancer occurred worldwide, leading to 445,391 cancer-related deaths . ESCC accounts for approximately 90% of all esophageal cancer cases . Owing to diagnostic challenges, the five-year survival rate for ESCC patients remains below 20% . Consequently, identifying biomarkers linked to ESCC could significantly enhance both prevention and diagnostic strategies.
Metabolic reprogramming is increasingly recognized as a hallmark of cancer cells . Even under normal oxygen conditions, tumor cells preferentially use glycolysis for energy production, a phenomenon known as the Warburg effect , . This shift in metabolism leads to lactate accumulation, which can influence tumor growth by altering the tumor microenvironment and promoting cell survival and proliferation . In the context of ESCC, mitochondrial D-lactate catabolism has been identified as a crucial oncogenic event driven by the upregulation of D-lactate dehydrogenase (LDHD), which is correlated with poor patient prognosis . Furthermore, lactate production plays a pivotal role in conferring resistance to ferroptosis in ESCC cells . A lactate-rich microenvironment also impedes CD8 + T-cell infiltration, reducing the therapeutic effectiveness of treatments such as chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy . Consequently, targeting lactate metabolism has become a focus of anticancer strategies, including the inhibition of hexokinase-2 expression with suppression of the Warburg effect shown to increase the radiosensitivity of ESCC .
Given the critical involvement of lactate metabolism in ESCC development and prognosis, identifying lactate-related genes (LRGs) may provide novel insights into disease pathogenesis and present potential diagnostic biomarkers. Most current research on LRGs in ESCC has focused on developing prognostic models , . In this study, bioinformatics analysis was employed to identify key LRGs in ESCC, followed by functional enrichment analysis and the construction of regulatory networks to explore their potential mechanisms. Additionally, immune infiltration analysis and machine learning algorithms were integrated to assess immune characteristics and construct a new diagnostic model. The identification of novel key genes not only enhances the accuracy of ESCC diagnosis but also holds substantial promise for early intervention and therapeutic advancements.

## Materials and methods

## Data acquisition
The research workflow is illustrated in Fig.  . ESCC transcriptome datasets (GSE17351, GSE20347, GSE23400, and GSE161533) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data (GSE209524) were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE17351 consists of 5 paired ESCC and normal tissue samples; GSE20347 includes 17 paracarcinoma tissue samples and 17 carcinoma tissue samples; GSE23400 contains 56 pairs of ESCC tumor and paraneoplastic tissue samples; and GSE161533 serves as a validation set with 28 ESCC and 28 normal samples. Additionally, GSE209524 provides scRNA-seq data from 10 ESCC samples and 4 normal samples. To identify lactate-related genes (LRGs), keywords “lactate” and “lactic” were queried in the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp) to obtain enriched pathways. Concurrently, genes with a relevance score greater than 5 for lactate-related terms were retrieved from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). The intersection of these two datasets yielded a total of 212 LRGs (Supplementary Table 1).
 

## Screening differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
To mitigate batch effects across the three ESCC transcriptome datasets (GSE17351, GSE20347, and GSE23400), the ComBat algorithm was employed. Differential expression analysis was then conducted via the “limma” package to identify DEGs between ESCC and normal samples with thresholds of |logFC| ≥ 0.2 and adj.p. value < 0.05. Volcano plots were used to visualize the DEG distribution, and a heatmap was generated to display the top 50 upregulated and downregulated DEGs.

## Enrichment analyses of DEGs
Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) , enrichment analyses were performed to elucidate the biological pathways and functions of the DEGs. These analyses were conducted via the “clusterProfiler” and “org.Hs.eg.db” packages, with visualizations generated via the “enrichplot” and “ggplot” packages.

## Screening of ESCC-related module genes
Weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) was applied to identify gene modules associated with ESCC. Genes and samples that failed to meet the 0.3 filtering criterion were excluded. A soft threshold (β) was selected to construct the coexpression network, with a scale-free R value of 0.9 and a mean connectivity approaching zero, ensuring that the network accurately reflected scale-free topology. The dynamic tree-cutting method was used to separate modules, with each module containing at least 30 genes. The modules most highly correlated with ESCC (|cor| > 0.8, p < 0.05) were designated key modules. The genes within these modules were consolidated, leading to the identification of key module genes associated with ESCC.

## Acquisition and analyses of LRG-related DEGs (LR-DEGs)
On the basis of the aforementioned studies, lactate-related differentially expressed genes (LR-DEGs) in ESCC were identified by overlapping DEGs, ESCC-related module genes, and LRGs. The chromosomal localization of the LR-DEGs was subsequently explored, and a distribution plot was generated via the “circlize” package . The expression of LR-DEGs was then compared between the ESCC and normal groups via the “limma” package. A heatmap of gene expression was created with the “pheatmap” package and boxplots were drawn via the “ggpubr” package . Finally, correlation analysis among the LR-DEGs was conducted, and a correlation plot was generated via the “corrplot” package .

## Identification and functional analysis of the hub genes
To identify feature genes, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), random forest (RF), and support vector machine recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithms were applied via the “glmnet” randomForest  and “e1071” packages  respectively. The intersecting feature genes from the three machine learning methods were defined as hub genes. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was calculated to evaluate the discriminatory power of these hub genes. The correlations among the hub genes and all the other genes were assessed via Spearman correlation analysis, and the genes were ranked on the basis of their correlation coefficients. To explore the regulatory roles of the hub genes in ESCC, microRNAs (miRNAs) were retrieved from miRTarBase (http://miRTarBase.mbc.nctu.edu.tw/), Starbase (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/), and TargetScan (http://www.targetscan.org). Key miRNAs were derived from the intersection of miRNAs across these three databases. Additionally, transcription factor (TF)-target interactions for the hub genes were identified via the TRANSFAC and JASPAR PWM datasets on the Enrichr platform (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/). A “miRNA-TF-gene” regulatory network was subsequently constructed on the basis of the identified key miRNAs, TFs, and hub genes. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) for the hub genes was performed via the “clusterProfiler” package and the org.Hs.eg.db gene set.

## Construction of the diagnostic models
Four predictive models were constructed using hub genes, including SVM, extreme gradient boosting (XGB), RF, and generalized linear model (GLM), to predict the value of the hub genes. After the prediction functions were calculated, the most suitable predictive model was selected, and the importance of the hub genes was evaluated through a comprehensive analysis involving residual box plots and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The predictive performance of these machine learning algorithms was further explored via the Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) tool, which was developed on Kaggle Notebook (https://www.kaggle.com/code).
A nomogram was subsequently constructed on the basis of the hub genes to predict the risk of ESCC via the “rms” package . Each gene was assigned a corresponding score, and the sum of all gene scores was calculated. A higher total score indicates a greater risk of ESCC. Decision curve analysis (DCA), performed via the “rmda” package was used to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram. A calibration curve was created to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the nomogram. Finally, the nomogram was validated in the validation set, and the ROC curve was used to assess its stability.

## Immune infiltration and regulatory relationship analysis
The proportions of 22 immune cell types in the ESCC and normal groups were calculated via the CIBERSORT algorithm. Differences in immune cell distributions between ESCC and normal samples were assessed via the Wilcoxon test (p < 0.05). The correlations between the hub genes and immune cell subsets were evaluated with the “psych” package .

## scRNA-seq analysis
Seurat objects for scRNA-seq data were generated via the Seurat package, with cells meeting the following criteria: gene count > 200 and < 5,000, minimum unique molecular identifier (UMI) = 600, mitochondrial percentage ≤ 10%, and hemoglobin percentage ≤ 0.1%, ensuring high data quality. Data normalization was performed via the normalizeData function in Seurat, followed by the identification of high-variance genes. Principal component analysis (PCA) was then conducted for dimensionality reduction. Unsupervised clustering of cells was executed via the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions in Seurat, with a resolution of 0.5, to define the cell clusters. Nonlinear dimensional reduction was employed to visualize the integrated and lineage-specific datasets. Cell subpopulations were identified, and marker genes were explored among cell clusters on the basis of RNA expression via the Seurat FindAllMarkers function with a log fold change threshold of |logFC| ≥ 1.0. Hub gene localization was analyzed within the subpopulations. AUCell analysis, which is based on a gene set of 212 LRGs, was used to calculate lactylation scores for these subpopulations, which were categorized into high- and low-lactylation groups. The lactylation scores of the ESCC and normal samples were subsequently compared (p < 0.05). To further explore the differences in intercellular communication between the high- and low-lactylation groups, CellChat analysis was performed. We used the “CELLCHAT” package and conducted a high-resolution characterization of cell–cell interactions, including the contribution of different cell types to signaling strength. This analysis enabled the visualization of specific signaling pathways, interaction networks, and detailed ligand–receptor pairs.
To further clarify the relationship between lactylation scores of cell subpopulations, we conducted a correlation analysis. Firstly, the “ssGSEA” algorithm was used to calculate the lactylation score in each cell. Then, the “psych” package was employed to correlate the lactylation score of each cell with hallmarker pathways. All significant hallmarker pathways in all cells were visualized through a heatmap using the ComplexHeatmap software package.

## RNA extraction and reverse transcription quantitative‒polymerase chain reaction (RT‒qPCR)
The EC109 and KYSE-30 cell lines (C1142, C1315, C1189, SHANGHAI WHELAB BIOSCIENCE LIMTED) were cultured in corresponding complete media (MC1142, MC1315, MC1189, SHANGHAI WHELAB BIOSCIENCE LIMTED). All the cell lines were incubated at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2.
To validate the expression level in LRGs, EC109, KYSE-30 and HEEC cells were cultured under adherent conditions until they reached approximately 80% confluence. Subsequently, RNA extraction was conducted via the Total RNA Kit II R6934 (R6934-02, OMEGA Bio-Tek Co., Ltd.), followed by RT‒qPCR via the Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (No Rox) (11201ES03, Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). The sequences of primers used for RT‒qPCR are detailed in Supplementary Table 2. The RT‒qPCR protocol included an initial denaturation step at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 amplification cycles at 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s. The target gene expression levels were quantified via the 2−ΔΔCt method, with β-actin serving as the reference gene for normalization.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed via R software (version 4.2.2), and differences between groups were assessed via the Wilcoxon test. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Identification of DEGs for ESCC
After removing batch effects from the three GEO datasets, the expression profiles of ESCC and normal samples were obtained, resulting in a gene count of 3,907 (Fig.  A, Supplementary Fig.  A-D). Differential analysis revealed 1,377 DEGs, including 714 upregulated genes and 663 downregulated genes, in the ESCC samples (Fig.  B-C). GO terms and KEGG pathways, including “organelle fission (GO-BP)”, “cell-substrate junction (GO-CC)”, “actin binding (GO-CC)”, and “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (KEGG)”, are shown in Fig.  D-E.
 

## Confirmation of ESCC-related module genes
The cluster analysis of all the samples revealed no outlier samples (Supplementary Fig. 2A-B). A soft threshold of 11 was selected, with R2 = 0.9 as the threshold to filter out the mean connectivity near 0 (Fig.  A). A hierarchical clustering tree was then developed, with each branch representing genes exhibiting similar expression profiles and biological functions (Fig.  B). The correlation heatmap of the selected genes is shown in Fig.  C. Four modules were identified, with the brown and turquoise modules demonstrating strong correlations (|cor| > 0.8, brown = 0.92, turquoise = 0.89), which were designated key modules. A total of 1,331 genes (466 in the brown module and 865 in the turquoise module) were classified as ESCC-related module genes (Fig.  D-F).
 

## Identification of LR-DEGs and corresponding analyses
The intersection of 1,377 DEGs, 1,331 ESCC-related module genes, and 212 LRGs yielded 13 LR-DEGs (Fig.  A). The locations of LR-DEGs on chromosome were shown in Fig.  B. Except for SLC16A7 and COX5A, which exhibited significantly higher expression in normal samples, the remaining genes were significantly upregulated in ESCC samples (p < 0.001) (Fig.  C). Correlation analysis confirmed a strong relationship among the LR-DEGs (Fig.  D). KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the LR-DEGs were enriched in necroptosis, metabolic pathways, and cardiac muscle contraction (Fig.  E). GO analysis revealed their involvement in mitochondrial function, oxidative phosphorylation, and transmembrane transporter activity, indicating their roles in energy metabolism and immune regulation (Fig.  F–H).
 

## Ascertainment of hub genes for ESCC
Using the LASSO, RF, and SVM-RFE algorithms, 7, 10, and 10 feature genes, respectively, were identified under their respective optimal conditions (Fig.  A-C). Six hub genes, including SLC16A7, GFM1, PDP1, KIF23, TMRT5, COX5Awere subsequently selected by overlapping the feature genes from the three machine learning methods (Fig.  D). The AUC values for the ROC curve of all hub genes except for COX5A exceeded 0.7, confirming their strong capacity to differentiate between ESCC and normal samples (Fig.  E). Furthermore, correlation analysis revealed a significant relationship between PDP1 and GFM1 (cor = 0.507, p < 0.001) as well as between KIF23 and TRMT5 (cor = 0.520, p < 0.001) (Fig.  F).
 From the miRTarBase, Starbase, and TargetScan databases, 367, 154, and 4320 miRNAs, respectively, were identified. The overlap of these miRNAs led to the identification of 38 key miRNAs for further investigation (Fig.  G). Additionally, five TFs were identified, including SRF, Pax6, NR2F1, RORB, and JUN. A miRNA‒mRNA‒TF regulatory network was constructed, in which hsa-miR-147a was found to regulate SLC16A7, GFM1, TRMT5 and KIF23, highlighting its key role in the regulation of ESCC through hub genes (Fig.  H).

## Functional enrichment analysis of the hub genes
To further investigate the biological functions and pathways of the hub genes, GSEA was performed. The corresponding KEGG pathways for each hub gene (Fig.  A-F) revealed that the cell cycle was enriched by most of the hub genes. Additionally, several ESCC-related pathways, such as the p53 signaling pathway, cytokine‒cytokine receptor interaction, vascular smooth muscle contraction, focal adhesion, and tight junctions, were also enriched with the hub genes.
 

## Machine learning algorithms determine the diagnostic role of hub genes, and the nomogram has better predictive efficiency
To assess the diagnostic performance of the hub genes, four machine learning models were developed, with GLM demonstrating the highest AUC (0.982). Consequently, GLM was selected as the optimal model for further analysis, and the importance of the hub genes was evaluated within this model (Fig.  A-C). The hub genes were used to construct a nomogram, and individual scoring scales for each of the six hub genes were determined. By summing the scores, the risk of ESCC was assessed (Fig.  D). The accuracy of the nomogram was evaluated via DCA and a calibration curve (Fig.  E-F). Further validation with the GSE161533 dataset confirmed the model’s excellent accuracy, with an AUC value of 0.970 (Fig.  G).
 Reevaluation of the machine learning models via the SHAP tool revealed that the GLM model achieved the highest precision (93.55%), which is consistent with our previous findings. It also demonstrated a high balanced accuracy (87.14%), recall (82.66%) and F1 score (87.06%), indicating its superior performance (Supplementary Table ).
Furthermore, the precision of all four models was greater than 88.0%, suggesting a low false positive rate. SLC16A7, KIF23 and PDP1 were identified as the most influential genes in the models (Supplementary Fig.  A-D).

## Investigation of immune infiltration and the regulatory network
Given the enrichment of immune-related pathways in the previous analyses, the role of immune infiltration in ESCC was subsequently explored. The infiltration levels of 22 immune cell types are shown in Fig.  A. Differential analysis revealed significant differences in four immune cell types—naive B cells, memory B cells, resting NK cells, and eosinophils—between ESCC and normal samples (p < 0.05) (Fig.  B). Correlation analysis indicated that SLC16A7 and KIF23 were positively and negatively correlated, respectively, with memory B cells (|cor| = 0.2–0.4, p < 0.05), whereas SLC16A7 were negatively correlated with eosinophils (|cor| = 0.2–0.4, p < 0.05) (Fig.  C).
 

## scRNA-seq defines the cellular composition of ESCC samples
To characterize the cellular composition of the ESCC samples fully, scRNA-seq analysis was performed. After quality control of the scRNA-seq data from the GSE209524 dataset, a total of 67,641 cells were retained (Supplementary Fig.  A-B). The top 10 principal components (PCs) among the 2,000 highly variable genes are shown (Supplementary Fig.  C). The first 20 PCs were selected for subsequent analysis via principal component analysis (PCA) (Supplementary Fig.  D-E). Through t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) clustering, 24 distinct cell clusters were identified, and their distributions in ESCC and normal samples are displayed (Fig.  A-B). Eight cell subpopulations, including T cells, fibroblasts, epithelial cells, neutrophils, monocytes, B cells, NK cells, and endothelial cells, were annotated (Fig.  C, Supplementary Fig.  A-B). The marker genes identified across different cell clusters are shown in Fig.  D. Proportional analysis of differential cell subpopulations revealed that endothelial cells, NK cells, epithelial cells, B cells, monocytes, and T cells were more abundant in ESCC samples than in normal samples, whereas neutrophils and fibroblasts were more prevalent in normal samples (Fig.  E). Differential analysis between ESCC and normal samples revealed that the proportion of T cells was greater in ESCC samples than in normal samples, whereas the proportion of fibroblasts was lower in normal samples (Fig.  F).
 The distribution of hub genes across cell subpopulations is shown in Fig.  G. Using AUcell analysis, lactylation scores for each cell subpopulation were calculated, and the cells were categorized into high- and low-lactylation groups (Fig.  H-I). A comparison of lactylation scores between ESCC and normal samples revealed significantly higher scores in the ESCC group (Fig.  J), indicating a potential association between ESCC and elevated lactylation levels; moreover, analysis across different cell subpopulations revealed that monocytes, B cells, epithelial cells, and neutrophils had higher scores in the ESCC group, whereas T cells and fibroblasts presented elevated scores in normal samples (Fig.  K).

## CellChat analysis and signaling pathways in the high- and low-lactylation groups
To investigate the differences in intercellular communication between the high- and low-lactylation groups, we performed CellChat analysis. The network diagrams revealed substantial differences in both the number and strength of intercellular interactions (Fig.  A). Under high-lactylation conditions, epithelial cells exhibit more frequent and stronger interactions with various immune cells, such as B cells, monocytes and NK cells. The total number of intercellular communications and the average interaction strength were markedly greater in the high lactylation group than in the low lactylation group (Fig.  B).
 The scatter plots illustrate the incoming and outgoing signaling strengths of each cell type (Fig.  C). Under high-lactylation conditions, epithelial cells, NK cells, and monocytes exhibited markedly enhanced communication activity, whereas endothelial cells presented a reduction in signaling strength. Heatmaps display the detailed incoming and outgoing signaling patterns of all the cell types in the high-lactylation group (Fig.  D) and low-lactylation group (Fig.  E). The bar plot of information flow highlights both conserved and condition-specific signaling pathways between the two groups (Fig.  F). The MHC-II, SPP1, and MIF signaling pathways were prominently upregulated, indicating enhanced immune regulation. Elevated activity of CD70, THY1, and BAFF further highlights the immunomodulatory role of lactylation, while increased MK signaling suggests potential involvement in tumor proliferation. Finally, a heatmap of differential communication frequencies highlighted notable differences in cell–cell interaction patterns under different lactylation states (Fig.  G).

## Identification of key pathways associated with lactylation scores for cell subpopulations
To further identify key pathways associated with lactylation, we analyzed the correlation between cells and Hallmarker pathways. The results showed that, except for neutrophils, the lactylation scores in other cell types were significantly correlated with pathways such as “adipogenesis”, “apoptosis”, “DNA repair”, “E2F targets”, “fatty acid metabolism”, “G2M checkpoint”, “glycolysis”, and “heme metabolism“(Supplementary Fig. 6).

## Expression of the LRGs
The RT-qPCR validation results for 13 LR-DEGs across two ESCC cell lines and a normal esophageal epithelial cell line revealed that KIF23, TRMT5, TEFM, SLC25A13, TIMM50, DGUOK, and DNM1L demonstrated significantly elevated mRNA expression levels (P < 0.05) in both the EC109 and KYSE-30 ESCC cell lines relative to the normal esophageal epithelial HEEC cells, corroborating prior predictions (Fig.  ). Similarly, COX5A exhibited significantly reduced mRNA expression levels (P < 0.05) in both ESCC cell lines, which was consistent with the predicted outcomes. Furthermore, SLC16A7 and GFM1 presented increased expression levels (P < 0.05) in EC109 cells compared with normal cells, in accordance with the predicted trend, whereas their expression in KYSE-30 cells was either lower or not significantly different. In contrast, HSPD1 and PDP1 displayed higher mRNA expression levels (P < 0.05) in KYSE-30 cells than in normal cells, in agreement with our predictions, whereas their expression in EC109 cells was either lower or not significantly different. These discrepancies may be attributed to variations in gene expression heterogeneity among different cellular origins. It’s also worth to notice that the trend of LYRM4 in tested cell lines were inconsistent with the predication. Although a few of the anticipated trends were not evident in specific cell lines, the majority of the observed trends aligned with the predictions of our model.
 

## Discussion
ESCC is an aggressive cancer with a poor prognosis, underscoring the need for novel hub genes and therapeutic targets. Lactate, a byproduct of anaerobic glycolysis, contributes to immune evasion and tumor progression by creating an immunosuppressive microenvironment , . Metabolic reprogramming, including dysregulated lactate pathways, is a hallmark of ESCC, establishing a link between metabolism and disease progression . Understanding these mechanisms may provide valuable insights into new therapeutic strategies and prognostic markers for ESCC, potentially improving patient outcomes. The goal of this study was to conduct a comprehensive analysis of lactate-related hub genes in ESCC, focusing on their potential as diagnostic predictors and their role in shaping the immune landscape of the TME. By integrating bulk and scRNA-seq data, this research aimed to identify key lactate-related genes that could serve as hub genes for ESCC diagnosis and guide the development of targeted therapies.
This study identified six lactate-related hub genes (SLC16A7, GFM1, PDP1, COX5A, TRMT5, and KIF23) with potential roles in ESCC. Previous studies have reported the the COX5A up-regulated in ESCC cancer cells . KIF23 is also highly expressed in ESCC, where it may promote cell proliferation and migration . While no studies have directly linked SLC16A7, GFM1, PDP1, and TRMT5 to ESCC, their involvement in other cancers has been documented. SLC16A7, a monocarboxylate transporter, facilitates the transmembrane transport of lactate and pyruvate and has been implicated in tumor metabolism and disease progression in breast and lung adenocarcinomas , . GFM1 may regulate the cell cycle through GATA-binding sites and promote tumor metastasis via activation of the RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway , . PDP1, the catalytic subunit of pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatase 1, is involved in tumor metabolism and growth across various cancers and is considered a potential therapeutic target , . TRMT5 upregulated expression is proved in HCC, correlating with poor prognosis, via the HIF-1α pathways . While the precise roles of these genes in ESCC remain unclear, their involvement in other cancers suggests that they may contribute to ESCC pathogenesis and progression. Further research is needed to elucidate their mechanistic roles and therapeutic potential.
GSEA revealed that multiple hub genes were significantly enriched in pathways related to the cell cycle, p53 signaling, and vascular smooth muscle contraction. The regulation of the cell cycle plays a pivotal role in ESCC proliferation, particularly through the overexpression of the cyclin D1/CDK4/6 complex, which accelerates the G1‒S phase transition, drives cell cycle progression and contributes to tumorigenesis . Inhibition of CDK4/6 has been shown to effectively suppress this activity, halting cell cycle progression, inhibiting tumor cell proliferation, and inducing apoptosis . Disruption of the p53 pathway, commonly via TP53 mutation, promotes genomic instability and resistance to apoptosis, underscoring its role in ESCC progression under oxidative stress . Genes associated with vascular smooth muscle contraction may reflect adaptive changes in the tumor microenvironment, where hypoxia-induced vascular remodeling and ROCK signaling facilitate ESCC metastasis , . These findings underscore the critical role of hub genes in ESCC, offering valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms driving its progression and highlighting potential therapeutic targets.
In this study, a nomogram based on a generalized linear model was developed using hub genes, with validation confirming its efficacy as a predictive tool for assessing ESCC risk. The risk prediction model closely aligned with the observed clinical outcomes, suggesting that hub genes are a reliable tool for predicting ESCC risk. Furthermore, SHAP analysis was employed to quantify the contribution of each feature gene to model predictions, further emphasizing the importance of hub genes in ESCC risk assessment. By integrating these hub genes, early diagnosis and treatment of patients with ESCC can be improved, ultimately improving patient outcomes.
The infiltration of 22 immune cell types was further analyzed, revealing significant differences in naive B cells, memory B cells, resting NK cells, and eosinophils between ESCC and normal samples. Additionally, immune cells such as memory B cells and eosinophils were found to be significantly correlated with the hub genes. In ESCC, memory B cells may facilitate cancer progression by modulating the tumor microenvironment and contributing to immune evasion . Dysregulation of memory B cells can impair the ability of the immune system to recognize and eliminate tumor cells, thereby promoting ESCC development . Furthermore, interactions between memory B cells and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) have been implicated as key mechanisms in ESCC progression . A study on genetic polymorphisms in ESCC indicated that specific genetic variants could influence inflammatory responses, including eosinophil activity, and thereby impact cancer risk . Eosinophils may modulate esophageal immune responses through interactions with other immune cells, influencing the progression from precancerous lesions to ESCC . In conclusion, immune cell infiltration, particularly that of memory B cells and eosinophils, plays a pivotal role in ESCC progression by shaping the tumor microenvironment and immune response. These findings highlight the importance of immune cell interactions in cancer progression and underscore potential therapeutic targets for intervention.
To elucidate the mechanisms driving immune cell dynamics, scRNA-seq is crucial for offering a more detailed view of the immune landscape in ESCC. Our scRNA-seq analysis identified eight distinct cell subpopulations, revealing that monocytes, B cells, epithelial cells, and neutrophils presented significantly increased lactylation scores in the ESCC group, whereas T cells, fibroblasts, and NK cells presented elevated scores in normal samples. Previous studies have demonstrated that LDHA overexpression in cancer cells accelerates lactate production, resulting in acidification of the tumor microenvironment. This acidification inhibits the function of tumor-infiltrating T cells and NK cells, substantially reducing their ability to secrete key cytokines such as IFN-γ  which aligns with our findings. Epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT), a key process in ESCC epithelial transformation, is correlated with tumor size, differentiation, and invasion depth . Lactate, acting as a signaling molecule, modulates EMT marker expression and promotes cancer cell invasion. Lactate induces EMT in oral and breast cancer cells, while lactate transport inhibitors have shown potential in reversing these effects further supporting the role of lactate in ESCC progression.
Through CellChat analysis, we found that in the high-lactylation microenvironment, interactions between epithelial cells and various immune cell types were significantly enhanced, as reflected by more frequent and stronger intercellular communication. This phenomenon may be attributed to the role of lactate in promoting immune modulation and intercellular signaling within the TME. Further analysis revealed that several signaling pathways associated with immune regulation and tumor progression, including MHC-II, SPP1, and MIF, were upregulated in the high lactylation group. Previous studies have shown that MHC-II can promote tumor progression via immunosuppressive mechanisms; for example, tumor-associated lymphatic vessels upregulate MHC-II expression to inhibit the activity of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes . SPP1 is overexpressed in various human cancers and is strongly associated with poor prognosis, potentially by promoting immune cell infiltration and interacting with oncogenic pathways . In addition, MIF, a key proinflammatory cytokine, is considered a critical link between chronic inflammation and tumor development. It regulates the expression of cancer-related genes and activates protumor signaling pathways. In prostate cancer, elevated MIF expression has been significantly associated with lymph node metastasis .
Moreover, we observed increased activity of the CD70, THY1, and BAFF pathways in the high-lactylation group. These pathways are known to be involved in immune cell activation and tumor immune evasion, further highlighting the potential role of lactylation in modulating the tumor immune microenvironment. The interaction between CD70 and its receptor CD27 plays a critical role in tumor immune regulation, where the activation of CD27 signaling can increase the frequency of regulatory T cells and thereby promote tumor growth . In addition, CD70 expression is associated with immune escape mechanisms and may contribute to tumor progression by impairing T-cell function . THY1 (also known as CD90) has been linked to tumor cell invasion and metastasis in various cancers. It may regulate immune cell function through interactions with components of the tumor microenvironment, and its high expression is frequently associated with poor prognosis , . BAFF, a key cytokine that influences B-cell survival and function, may facilitate tumor immune evasion by enhancing B-cell activation and interacting with immune checkpoint molecules, ultimately weakening immune surveillance , .

## Limitations
Despite these insights, this study still has certain limitations. Although we used multiple datasets for analysis, the small sample size poses a risk of insufficient statistical significance of the results. Therefore, expanding the sample size for further verification urgently needs to be addressed. The immune infiltration exhibits analysis limited specificity. Consequently, we plan to integrate spatial transcriptome technology to further elucidate and refine the spatial interaction patterns between biomarkers and immune cells. In addition, this study lacks clinical data at the patient level and multivariate survival analysis. Our findings are primarily based on bioinformatics predictions, and further experimental validation is required to confirm their biological functions and clinical relevance. Furthermore, the use of cell lines for validation may not fully encompass the heterogeneity inherent in tumor and the tumor microenvironment in vivo. Additionally, the RT-qPCR results from this study revealed inconsistent expression trends for certain genes in the two ESCC cell types examined. This inconsistency could potentially be attributed to multiple factors, especially in the difference form patient source and more specific pathological subtype, including cell type-specific transcriptional regulation, variations in transcription factors and epigenetic states, as well as gene mutations or copy number variations. Future research should aim to further validate the expression patterns and functions of these genes in alternative cell models or diverse tissue types, elucidate their precise roles in ESCC development, and explore their potential clinical applications.

## Conclusion
In summary, this study provides a comprehensive analysis of the potential impact of lactate-related genes on ESCC, identifying six key genes that may serve as novel diagnostic biomarkers. Utilizing single-cell RNA sequencing, we characterized the lactylation profile of esophageal cells in the context of ESCC, offering new insights into the relationship between lactate and ESCC. However, it is important to emphasize that these findings are preliminary in nature. Further clinical validation and large-scale cohort studies are required to confirm the clinical utility of these genes and to explore the precise role of lactylation in the pathogenesis of ESCC.
