## Abstract
Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. SCLC tumors are classified into four molecular subtypes each exhibiting distinct biological traits and therapeutic vulnerabilities. However, limited information is available on the expression profile of circular RNAs (circRNAs) in SCLC. To address this gap, we performed RNA sequencing to identify differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) in SCLC compared with normal lung tissue. Using CircInteractome and miRTarBase, we predicted miRNA partners and their downstream mRNA targets, thereby constructing circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory networks. The expression of circRNAs and a few predicted interacting miRNAs in SCLC and normal lung tissue was validated using qRT-PCR, and their diagnostic performance was assessed. Among the identified 23 DECs, 10 were significantly downregulated and 13 were upregulated in SCLC. In total, 241 miRNAs were predicted to interact with these 23 DECs, potentially regulating 7,804 mRNA targets. Further validation revealed significant downregulation of circLIFR expression and overexpression of circCAMSAP1 in SCLC. The circLIFR-associated network comprised 26 miRNAs and 2,447 mRNA targets, with miR-1234-3p and miR-375-3p significantly upregulated in SCLC. The circCAMSAP1-associated network included 14 miRNAs and 553 mRNA targets, with miR-145-5p significantly downregulated in SCLC. Interestingly, the expression of circCAMSAP1 significantly correlated with the response to therapy. As diagnostic biomarkers, circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 exhibited the AUC of 0.9177 and 0.764, respectively. In conclusion, certain DECs may regulate key hallmarks of SCLC through miRNA sponging. These findings highlight circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 as promising biomarkers, though functional validation is required to confirm their mechanistic roles.

## Introduction
Lung cancer ranks among the most common cancers and is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths, responsible for nearly 18% of all such fatalities worldwide . Histologically, lung cancer is divided into non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC), with SCLC representing only 15–20% of all lung cancers. SCLC tumors are highly aggressive and grow rapidly, leading to a poor prognosis for patients due to early metastatic spread and chemoresistance – . SCLC is classified into limited stage or extensive stage based on the presence or absence of distant metastasis . Current evidence suggests that SCLC can originate from various cell types within the lungs, but pulmonary neuroendocrine cells are considered the primary initiators of SCLC . Smoking is strongly linked to the pathogenesis of SCLC – . SCLC is primarily driven by loss-of-function mutations in tumor suppressor genes, particularly tumor protein p53 (TP53) and retinoblastoma 1, which are inactivated in approximately 70–90% of SCLC tumors – . Other commonly mutated genes in SCLC include members of the neurogenic locus notch homolog protein and myelocytomatosis oncogene (MYC) families, phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN), canonical wingless-related integration site pathway members, SRY-box transcription factor 2 (SOX2), slit guidance ligand 2, and epigenetic regulators like histone acetyltransferase p300, CREB binding lysine acetyltransferase, mixed lineage leukemia, and enhancer of zeste homolog 2 – .
SCLC has been classified into distinct molecular subtypes based on lineage transcription factors such as achaete-scute homolog 1 (ASCL1, SCLC-A), neurogenic differentiation factor 1 (NEUROD1, SCLC-N), yes-associated protein 1 (YAP1, SCLC-Y), and POU class 2 homeobox 3 (POU2F3, SCLC-P) . However, the existence of the SCLC-Y subtype is debatable, as some studies have not detected YAP1 protein expression in SCLC tumors . A recent study retained the SCLC-A/N/P subtypes but replaced SCLC-Y with the SCLC-inflamed (SCLC-I) subtype, characterized by an inflamed gene signature . While these classifications highlight potential therapeutic vulnerabilities, none have yet translated into clinical practice, largely due to limited tissue availability, lack of predictive biomarkers, and incomplete understanding of SCLC biology. In particular, the role of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), including circular RNAs (circRNAs) remains underexplored.
CircRNAs are covalently closed ncRNAs produced through back-splicing – . The presence of a continuous loop structure and the lack of 5′ to 3′ polarity and a polyadenylated tail make circRNAs more resistant to ribonucleases compared to mRNAs and other ncRNAs – . CircRNAs have been found to interact with DNA, mRNA, microRNAs (miRNAs), and proteins, regulating various cellular and biological processes such as splicing, chromatin remodeling, cell proliferation, differentiation, immune modulation, and apoptosis – . More importantly, the vast majority of circRNAs are found to function as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), through which they can regulate gene expression by competing for a particular miRNA or a set of miRNAs , . As ceRNAs and through their interactions with diverse cellular molecules, circRNAs are known to drive several crucial cancer phenotypes, making them ideal candidates for therapeutic intervention and cancer biomarkers – . However, their expression profile and clinical relevance in SCLC remain poorly defined , . Further, although circRNA research has expanded globally, India has had limited investigations into their role in cancer, and no prior study has focused on circRNA expression profiling in SCLC. This makes the first Indian study in this area particularly novel.
To address this gap, we conducted an exploratory, discovery-focused study to identify differentially expressed circRNAs (DECs) in SCLC using bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) of SCLC and normal lung tissue. We constructed ceRNA networks of the identified DECs and explored associated pathways through enrichment analyses. We also validated the expression of two circRNAs by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) in a relatively larger cohort of SCLC and normal lung tissue samples, and assessed their diagnostic and prognostic potential. This study provides a systematic investigation of circRNAs in SCLC, offering novel insights into their deregulated expression, role as ceRNAs, and potential as biomarkers.

## Results

## Baseline characteristics
The baseline characteristics of patients are detailed in Table . The median age of patients was 60 years [Standard deviation (SD): ± 8.97; range: 40–77 years]. The majority of patients were male (81.8%) and smokers (78.8%) with a median smoking index of 537.5 (SD: ± 360.78; range: 150–1600). Most patients tested positive for synaptophysin (24/25; 96%) and chromogranin (22/23; 95.6%). The median Ki-67 index was notably high at 85% (SD: ± 17.98; range: 35–95%). Nearly two-thirds of the patients (66.7%) were lost to follow-up and did not receive any treatment at our center after their histopathological diagnosis (Table ). Only 11 patients received treatment with a combination of cisplatin/carboplatin and etoposide chemotherapy. The response to chemotherapy could be evaluated in 9 patients, among whom 7 had progressive disease (PD) and only 2 patients had a partial response (PR) to the first-line chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were 185 days (SD: ± 51.2; range: 96–254 days) and 250 days (SD: ± 130.1; range: 98–538 days), respectively. The demographic characteristics of the control group (n = 31) were comparable to those of the SCLC group, with a median age of 58 years. The majority of controls were male (80.6%) and smokers (74.2%).
 

## Analysis of RNA-seq data revealed differential expression of 23 circRNAs in SCLC
RNA-seq analysis was performed using total RNA purified from SCLC and normal lung tissue to identify DECs in SCLC. Following genome alignment and transcriptomic annotation, a total of 26,072 chimeric junctions were identified. After filtering for at least 5 junctions across all 10 samples, this number was reduced to 77. Normalizing the counts of these 77 splice junctions and conducting differential gene expression analysis revealed 23 circRNAs with significant differential expression [Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) adjusted p-value < 0.05], including 10 downregulated and 13 upregulated circRNAs. The gene expression levels of these 23 circRNAs are illustrated in a volcano plot and a heatmap in Fig.  A,B, respectively. These 23 DECs correspond to 21 genes, with mannosidase alpha class 1A member 2 and erythrocyte membrane protein band 4.1-like 2 genes having two distinct circRNAs with different lengths and start or stop coordinates, both of which are significantly differentially expressed. Figure  C represents a principal component analysis (PCA) plot that shows the first two principal components explaining the data variability using regularized log count data. The first and second principal components account for 71% and 7% of the variance, respectively. The characteristics of all 23 DECs are summarized in Table .
 

## Construction of a ceRNA network and enrichment analysis revealed the involvement of multiple signaling pathways
We conducted an in silico analysis of all 23 DECs to build a ceRNA regulatory network. Using the CircInteractome database, we identified 241 miRNAs that could potentially bind to the 23 DECs (Fig.  D). Further analysis with the miRTarBase database uncovered 7804 mRNA-producing genes that could be regulated by these 241 miRNAs (Fig.  D). Enrichment analysis of all 7804 genes was performed to identify pathways impacted by DECs through their interactions with the identified 241 miRNAs.
The analysis of these 7804 genes revealed the enrichment of gene ontology (GO) terms related to processes such as mitotic cell cycle process, proteolysis involved in protein catabolic process, regulation of cell cycle process, modification-dependent macromolecule and protein catabolic process, mitotic cell cycle phase transition, proteasome-mediated ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolic process, regulation of protein stability, among others (Fig.  E; Supplementary Fig. A). Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis indicated the involvement of genes in pathways related to pathways in cancer, human papillomavirus infection, proteoglycans in cancer, focal adhesion, cellular senescence, Hippo signaling pathway, cell cycle, and the p53 signaling pathway (Fig.  F; Supplementary Fig. B). The relationship between the most enriched GO terms and their corresponding genes is depicted in Fig.  G.

## The expression of circRNA leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (circLIFR) is significantly downregulated in SCLC
The circLIFR (hsa_circ_0072309), derived from the back-splicing of exons 2–5 of the leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (LIFR) pre-mRNA on chromosome 5, was found to be significantly downregulated in SCLC compared to normal lung tissue based on our RNA-seq data (Fig.  A,B, Table ). To validate this finding, we assessed the expression of circLIFR and its host gene LIFR in SCLC and normal lung tissue using qRT-PCR. We observed a significant downregulation in the expression levels of circLIFR (p < 0.0001) and LIFR mRNA (p < 0.0001) in SCLC tissue compared to normal lung tissue (Fig.  A,B). Similarly, circLIFR expression was significantly downregulated in various human SCLC cell lines compared to immortalized human bronchial epithelial cells (Supplementary Fig. A). The sequence of mature circLIFR was confirmed by PCR using divergent primers, followed by cloning and Sanger sequencing, revealing a 100% match with the mature sequence available in the CircInteractome database (Supplementary Fig. B).
 Using the CircInteractome database, we identified 26 miRNAs predicted to interact with circLIFR (Supplementary Table ). We randomly selected two miRNAs, miR-1234-3p and miR-375-3p, and assessed their expression levels in the SCLC and normal lung tissue using qRT-PCR. In contrast to circLIFR, we observed significant upregulation of miR-1234-3p (p < 0.0001; Fig.  C) and miR-375-3p (p < 0.0001; Fig.  D) expression in SCLC tissue compared to normal lung tissue. Analysis of the GSE19945 dataset, comprising 25 SCLC tumors and 8 normal lung tissues, using Gene Expression Omnibus 2R also revealed significant upregulation of miR-375-3p expression in SCLC tumors [log2 fold change (FC): 3.292; adjusted p-value: 5.78 × 10–3; Supplementary Fig. C].
The 26 miRNAs identified to potentially interact with circLIFR were found to target 2447 mRNA-encoding genes using the miRTarBase database (Fig.  A). These 26 miRNA targets of circLIFR, along with the high-confidence 2447 mRNA targets for these miRNAs, were used to construct a circLIFR-mediated ceRNA regulatory network using Cytoscape (Fig.  B). The identified 2447 putative mRNA targets were subjected to enrichment analysis to identify enriched GO and KEGG terms. The most enriched GO terms were related to the mitotic cell cycle process, regulation of the cell cycle process, organelle localization, positive regulation of the catabolic process, autophagy, cell cycle, and mitotic cell cycle phase transition, among others (Fig.  C; Supplementary Fig. A). The most enriched KEGG terms included pathways in cancer, the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, human cytomegalovirus infection, breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, forkhead box, O subfamily (FoxO) signaling pathways, and the p53 signaling pathway (Fig.  D; Supplementary Fig. B). Figure  E illustrates the relationship between the most enriched GO terms and their corresponding genes.
 

## The expression of circRNA calmodulin-regulated spectrin-associated protein 1 (circCAMSAP1) is significantly upregulated in SCLC
The circRNA circCAMSAP1 (hsa_circ_0001900) is derived by back-splicing of the exons 2–3 of the calmodulin-regulated spectrin-associated protein 1 (CAMSAP1) pre-mRNA present on chromosome 9. Our RNA-seq data analysis revealed a significant overexpression of circCAMSAP1 in SCLC compared to normal lung tissue (Fig.  A,B, Table ). To validate this finding, we assessed the expression of circCAMSAP1 and its host gene CAMSAP1 in SCLC and normal lung tissue using qRT-PCR. We observed a significant overexpression of circCAMSAP1 (p < 0.0005) and CAMSAP1 mRNA (p < 0.0001) in SCLC tissue compared to normal lung tissue (Fig.  A,B). The expression of circCAMSAP1 was also found to be overexpressed in a few human SCLC cell lines compared to immortalized human bronchial epithelial cells (Supplementary Fig. A). The sequence of mature circCAMSAP1 was confirmed by PCR using divergent primers followed by cloning and Sanger sequencing, revealing a 100% match with the sequence available in the CircInteractome database (Supplementary Fig. B). Using the CircInteractome database, we identified 14 miRNAs predicted to interact with circCAMSAP1 (Supplementary Table ). We randomly selected three miRNAs, miR-1182, miR-593-5p, and miR-767-3p, and assessed their expression levels in the SCLC and normal lung tissue (n = 5 each) using qRT-PCR. Unfortunately, we were unable to obtain amplification, suggesting that these miRNAs may be expressed at levels beyond the detection limit of qRT-PCR (data not shown). Therefore, we searched the literature for circCAMSAP1-interacting miRNAs. A recent study indicated that circCAMSAP1 can sponge miR-145-5p. We subsequently quantified the expression level of miR-145-5p in SCLC and normal lung tissue using qRT-PCR. Interestingly, we observed significant downregulation of miR-145-5p expression in SCLC compared to normal lung tissue (p < 0.0001; Fig.  C). Analysis of the GSE19945 dataset also revealed significantly lower miR-145-5p expression in SCLC tumors (log2FC: -3.858; p-value: 6.85 × 10–9; Supplementary Fig. C).
 The 14 miRNAs identified to potentially interact with circCAMSAP1 were found to target 553 mRNA-encoding genes using the miRTarBase database (Fig.  A). These miRNA targets of circCAMSAP1, along with the high-confidence mRNA targets for each miRNA, were utilized to construct a circCAMSAP1-mediated ceRNA regulatory network using Cytoscape (Fig.  B). The identified 553 putative mRNA targets were subjected to enrichment analysis to identify enriched GO and KEGG terms. The most enriched GO terms were related to the MAPK cascade, stress-activated protein kinase signaling cascade, stress-activated MAPK cascade, negative regulation of the execution phase of apoptosis, and cerebellar neuron development (Fig.  C; Supplementary Fig. A). The most enriched KEGG terms included MAPK signaling pathway, cellular senescence, and chronic myeloid leukemia (Fig.  D; Supplementary Fig. B). Figure  E depicts the relationship between the most enriched GO term and its corresponding genes.
 

## Correlation of the expression levels of circRNAs and miRNAs with various clinicopathological parameters and survival outcomes
We analyzed the expression levels of circLIFR, circCAMSAP1, miR‑1234‑3p, miR‑375‑3p, and miR‑145‑5p in relation to key clinicopathological factors, including age, gender, smoking status, and treatment response. Patients were grouped by age (> 60 vs. ≤ 60, based on the median age of 60 years), gender (male vs. female), and smoking status (smoker vs. non‑smoker). As noted in Sect. “ ”, chemotherapy response data were available for nine patients: seven exhibited PD, while only two achieved a PR to first‑line chemotherapy. Therefore, circRNA and miRNA expression levels were compared between patients with PR and those with PD. Notably, circCAMSAP1 expression was significantly associated with treatment response: patients who experienced disease progression after first‑line chemotherapy showed markedly higher circCAMSAP1 levels than those who achieved a partial response (p = 0.0346; Table ). circLIFR expression showed a borderline association with treatment response (p = 0.0708; Table ). Aside from these findings, circRNA expression levels did not correlate with any other clinicopathological characteristics (Table ). Likewise, except for a significant association between miR‑375‑3p expression and gender (p = 0.0186), no other miRNAs showed correlations with the clinicopathological parameters examined (Supplementary Table ). We further assessed whether circRNA and miRNA expression levels were linked to PFS or OS in patients with SCLC (Supplementary Table ). However, univariate analysis indicated that neither clinicopathological factors nor the expression levels of miRNAs or circRNAs were associated with PFS (p = 0.837 for circLIFR and p = 0.192 for circCAMSAP1) or OS (p = 0.193 for circLIFR and p = 0.352 for circCAMSAP1; Supplementary Table ).
 

## Diagnostic performance of circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 expression in SCLC
We assessed the diagnostic performance of circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (Fig.  , Table ). CircLIFR demonstrated excellent diagnostic accuracy, with an AUC of 0.9177 and sensitivity and specificity values of 71.4 and 96.4%, respectively (Fig.  A, Table ). In comparison, circCAMSAP1 showed moderate diagnostic ability, with an AUC of 0.764 and sensitivity and specificity of 64.3 and 82.1%, respectively (Fig.  B, Table ). Among the predicted miRNAs interacting with these circRNAs, miR‑1234‑3p displayed outstanding diagnostic performance, achieving an AUC of 0.9729 and a sensitivity of 93.6% at 100% specificity (Fig.  C, Table ). In contrast, miR‑375‑3p (Fig.  D) and miR‑145‑5p (Fig.  E) showed moderate diagnostic performance, with AUC values of 0.8325 and 0.8267, respectively (Table ).
 

## Discussion
In this study, we provide a systematic characterization of circRNA expression in SCLC, the first-of-its-kind in the Indian context. We identified 23 DECs, with 10 significantly downregulated and 13 significantly upregulated compared to normal lung tissue. Through in silico analysis, we uncovered 241 miRNAs predicted to interact with these DECs and 7,804 putative mRNA targets, suggesting a complex regulatory network. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these targets are involved in critical biological processes, including mitotic cell cycle regulation, proteasome-mediated protein catabolism, and protein stability. KEGG pathway analysis further highlighted associations with cancer-related signaling, including p53, Hippo, focal adhesion, and cellular senescence pathways. Collectively, these findings reveal a previously unexplored layer of SCLC biology, more specifically in the Indian context, demonstrating that circRNAs may act as central regulators of oncogenic pathways. By mapping circRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions, our study not only highlights the involvement of circRNAs in SCLC but also points to their potential as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers.
There is limited evidence on the expression profile of circRNAs in SCLC tumors , . Zhang et al. identified 30 downregulated and 5 upregulated circRNAs in SCLC compared with adjacent normal lung tissue . Among these, circRNA syntaxin binding protein 5-like (circSTXBP5L), circRNA VAMP-associated protein A (circVAPA), circRNA thyroid hormone receptor-associated protein 3 (circTHRAP3), circRNA special AT-rich sequence-binding protein 1 (circSATB1), and circRNA SEC31 homolog A, COPII component (circSEC31A) were significantly upregulated, whereas circRNA caspase recruitment domain family member 6 (circCARD6), circRNA WD repeat domain 60 (circWDR60), and circRNA T-box transcription factor 5 (circTBX5) were downregulated. In silico analyses suggested that circSTXBP5L may sponge 24 miRNAs, including cancer‑associated miR‑182‑5p, miR‑512‑3p, and miR‑224‑3p, and potentially regulate oncogenic pathways involving nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF‑κB), protein kinase B (AKT), and phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA) .
In our study, circLIFR was significantly downregulated in SCLC compared to normal lung tissue. Notably, circLIFR has been implicated as a tumor suppressor in multiple cancers, including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) – , esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) , bladder cancer , papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) , breast cancer (BC) , , gastric cancer (GC) , , glioblastoma (GBM) , , and NSCLC , . In HCC, circLIFR suppresses tumor progression by sponging miR-624-5p, stabilizing glycogen synthase kinase-3 beta, and promoting β-catenin degradation . Another study identified a circLIFR/LIFR regulatory loop that enhances circLIFR expression and reduces sorafenib resistance . Conversely, circLIFR has also been shown to promote proliferation by interacting with TANK binding kinase 1 and activating NF-κB signaling . In bladder cancer, circLIFR enhances cisplatin chemosensitivity by promoting the interaction between MutSα and ataxia-telangiectasia mutated, stabilizing p73 . In PTC and BC, circLIFR acts as a tumor suppressor by sponging miR-429, and miR-492, respectively , . In GC, circLIFR suppresses tumorigenesis by activating the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma/PTEN pathway and inhibiting phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT signaling , and by sponging miR-34a-3p and miR-330-5p . Overexpression of circLIFR inhibited the motility and migration of ESCC and CRC cells in vitro and metastasis of ESCC and liver cancer cells in vivo . In GBM, circLIFR promotes autophagy and increases temozolomide sensitivity in p53 wild-type cells . It also stabilizes p53 by inhibiting its ubiquitination through sponging miR-100 . In NSCLC, circLIFR exerts tumor suppressive effects by upregulating PTEN and sponging miR-429 to enhance the expression of an RNA-binding protein, CUGBP Elav-like family member 2 . However, some studies report circLIFR overexpression in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) tissue and in the tissue and serum of NSCLC patients . In LUAD, circLIFR acts as an oncogenic circRNA by sponging miR-607 and stabilizing fat mass and obesity-associated protein .
Among 26 miRNAs predicted to interact with circLIFR, the expression of miR-1234-3p and miR-375 was significantly upregulated in SCLC. Literature on miR-1234-3p is limited. miR-1234 suppresses the expression of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 gene in diffuse large B cell lymphoma , and its serum levels correlate positively with OS in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) . In ovarian cancer, miR-1234-3p predicts chemotherapy response . Plasma levels of miR-1234-3p correlate with treatment response in stage IV melanoma patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors and with recurrence and prognosis in biliary tract cancer . In GC, hsa_circ_0006470 suppresses tumor growth by sponging miR-1234 and increasing TP53 inducible protein 11 expression . miR-1234-3p also regulates sorafenib chemosensitivity in liver cancer via the circPHBK/miR-1234-3p/CYP2W1 axis .
We previously showed that miR-375-3p is overexpressed in NSCLC compared with normal lung tissue . Other studies report elevated miR-375 in SCLC tissue , plasma , and in chemoresistant SCLC . ASCL1 induces miR-375 transcription in SCLC , , and miR-375 is expressed in SCLC-A but not SCLC-Y cell lines . ASCL1-induced miR-375 directly inhibits YAP1, regulating neuroendocrine features in lung cancer . The miR-375/YAP axis drives neuroendocrine differentiation and tumorigenesis in lung carcinoid cells . Exosomal miR-375-3p is elevated in SCLC patients and promotes metastasis by inhibiting claudin 1 . The circRNA SH3 and PX domains 2A (circSH3PXD2A) suppresses SCLC progression via the miR-375-3p/YAP1 axis . hsa_circ_0136666 sponges miR-375-3p to enhance the expression of protein kinase, DNA-activated, catalytic subunit gene, leading to programmed death-ligand 1 phosphorylation, reduced degradation, and immune suppression .
Unlike circLIFR, circCAMSAP1 is upregulated in SCLC. This aligns with reports of circCAMSAP1 overexpression in HCC , NSCLC , osteosarcoma , CRC , , endometrial cancer , and NPC . In HCC, NSCLC, and osteosarcoma, circCAMSAP1 enhances the expression of the GRAM domain containing 1A, survivin, and friend leukemia virus integration 1 (FLI1) by sponging miR-1294 , miR-1182 , and miR-145-5p , respectively. Notably, previously reported circCAMSAP1 (hsa_circ_0004338) derives from exons 2–7, whereas our identified circCAMSAP1 (hsa_circ_0001900) derives from exons 2–3. In CRC, circCAMSAP1 enhances malignancy via the miR-328-5p/E2F transcription factor 1 axis . In endometrial cancer, it sponges miR-370-3p to upregulate mitogen-activated protein kinase 1 . In NPC, circCAMSAP1 stabilizes serpin family H member 1 mRNA, reduces c-Myc ubiquitination, and forms a positive feedback loop with serine and arginine-rich splicing factor 10 to promote circCAMSAP1 biogenesis .
Among circCAMSAP1-intracting miRNAs, the expression of miR-145-5p was significantly downregulated in SCLC, consistent with a meta-analysis reporting its marked reduction in SCLC tissue . Similar downregulation of miR-145-5p has been documented in NSCLC , CRC , cervical cancer (CC) , and BC . In CRC , CC , and BC , miR-145-5p functions as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting cell division cycle-associated 3, krueppel-like factor 5, and SOX2 expression, respectively. Exosomes derived from bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells overexpressing miR-145-5p were shown to suppress NSCLC cell proliferation, invasion, and migration by targeting SOX9 . Another study demonstrated that miR-145 plays a critical role in mediating drug resistance in NSCLC cells .
Although limited, several studies have explored circRNA-mediated mechanisms in SCLC. Li et al. identified two exonic circRNAs from the FLI1 gene (FECR1 and FECR2), both highly upregulated in SCLC tissue and serum; their silencing inhibited metastasis . Mechanistically, FECRs sponged miR-584-3p, resulting in upregulation of rho-associated, coiled-coil containing protein kinase 1 . CircMYC (hsa_circ_0085533), another overexpressed circRNA in SCLC, promoted proliferation, invasion, and migration by sponging miR-145 and increasing matrix metalloproteinase-2 expression . Hua et al. reported significant overexpression of circVAPA (hsa_circ_0006990) in SCLC tissue and serum; circVAPA sponged miR-377-3p and miR-494-3p, leading to insulin-like growth factor1 receptor upregulation and PI3K/AKT pathway activation . Tolomeo et al. identified circRNA plasmacytoma variant translocation 1 (circPVT1; hsa_circ_0001821) overexpression in MYC amplified SCLC cells and their extracellular vesicles (EVs) . CircPVT1 enhanced proliferation and inhibited apoptosis, and MYC overexpression in circPVT1 high cells revealed a positive feedback loop . While ASCL1 and NEUROD1 remained unchanged, circPVT1 overexpression increased YAP1 and decreased POU2F3, suggesting a role in SCLC-Y and SCLC-P subtypes through modulation of MYC, YAP1, and POU2F3 .
CircRNAs have also been implicated in SCLC chemoresistance. Huang et al. reported reduced expression of circRNA epithelial splicing regulatory protein-1 (cESRP1) in chemoresistant SCLC cells; cESRP1 sponged miR-93-5p, promoting the expression of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1A and SMAD family member 7 and suppressing transforming growth factor-beta/SMAD signaling to enhance chemosensitivity . Chao et al. identified circSH3PXD2A (hsa_circ_0019869) as significantly downregulated in chemoresistant SCLC cells, with its serum EV levels negatively correlating with chemoresistance . CircSH3PXD2A inhibited chemoresistance by sponging miR-375-3p and upregulating YAP1 . Using the same dataset, Zhang et al. identified multiple dysregulated circRNAs in chemoresistant SCLC, including hsa_circ_0041150, whose serum and EV levels decreased after first-line chemotherapy but rose again at disease progression . Structurally formed from exons 3–6 of the Rabphilin 3A-like gene, hsa_circ_0041150 promoted cisplatin resistance when overexpressed .
Given that our observations of circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 dysregulation align with previously published reports, it is reasonable to propose that these circRNAs warrant further investigation as potential therapeutic targets in SCLC. For example, therapeutic restoration of circLIFR expression through circRNA mimics or vector‑based delivery could counteract the aggressive phenotype of SCLC. In contrast, targeted silencing of circCAMSAP1 using antisense oligonucleotides, siRNAs, or CRISPR‑based circRNA editing tools could sensitize tumors to chemotherapy and disrupt survival pathways that drive relapse. It is crucial to explore this further, given that chemoresistance is a defining challenge in SCLC management. Together, circLIFR and circCAMSAP1 exemplify the dual therapeutic strategy of circRNA replacement and circRNA inhibition, offering a novel layer of post‑transcriptional intervention that could complement existing SCLC treatments and address the disease’s notorious resistance to conventional therapy.
In this study, we did not find any correlation between circRNAs or miRNAs expression and survival outcomes, including PFS and OS. Both these molecules have the potential to affect the survival of patients with cancer. For example, specific circRNAs can sponge one or multiple miRNAs, preventing them from binding to target mRNAs. This can either suppress or enhance tumor progression depending on the miRNA involved. Also, circRNAs that sponge tumor-suppressive miRNAs can promote the survival of cancer cells despite treatment, worsening patient outcomes. Moreover, a few circRNAs are implicated in drug resistance , . In fact, a few studies reported an inverse correlation between circLIFR expression and survival outcomes in HCC , bladder cancer , BC , GBM , and NSCLC . Higher serum levels of circLIFR were associated with more bone metastasis foci, while post-operative OS and PFS were lower in patients with high serum levels of circLIFR . We also observed that SCLC patients who experienced disease progression after first-line chemotherapy exhibited significantly higher circCAMSAP1 expression compared to those who achieved a partial response. This finding suggests that circCAMSAP1 may be associated with chemoresistance and poorer treatment outcomes. While our study provides preliminary evidence of this relationship, further validation in larger cohorts is warranted to confirm its clinical utility as a biomarker to identify patients at risk of suboptimal response to standard therapy. Future studies should explore whether targeting circCAMSAP1 could improve therapeutic efficacy in SCLC.
Several studies have assessed the diagnostic potential of circLIFR and circCAMSAP1. In HCC , GC , and NSCLC , circLIFR demonstrated AUC values of 0.801, 0.7656, and 0.887, respectively. Its expression also distinguished patients with triple-negative breast cancer from non‑cancer controls, with AUCs of 0.78 and 0.81 in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded and frozen tissues . In our SCLC cohort, circLIFR showed an excellent AUC of 0.9177, although this should be interpreted cautiously due to the small sample size. CircCAMSAP1 expression was significantly elevated in the plasma of CRC patients compared with individuals with pre‑cancerous lesions and healthy controls , and was similarly increased in pre‑operative CRC serum samples . Notably, circCAMSAP1 levels declined significantly after surgical resection . As a diagnostic marker distinguishing CRC from non‑CRC samples, circCAMSAP1 achieved an AUC of 0.722 . In our study, we observed a comparable AUC of 0.764 for circCAMSAP1, suggesting limited diagnostic utility.
A key consideration when interpreting our findings is the marked heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in SCLC – . Increasing evidence shows that variations in immune cell infiltration, hypoxia, angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, and intratumoral microbiome composition can strongly influence tumor progression and treatment response – . These TME‑related factors have also been linked to the regulation of ncRNA expression, including circRNAs , . Although our study examined circRNAs and miRNAs in SCLC and normal lung tissues, we lacked TME‑specific profiling data, such as immune infiltration markers, angiogenesis signatures, or microbiome sequencing, which prevented us from directly evaluating how these microenvironmental features may shape circRNA expression patterns. This limitation is particularly relevant given the growing interest in how TME heterogeneity contributes to tumor biology and biomarker variability – . Future studies that integrate cell‑type‑specific circRNA analyses with comprehensive immune profiling and microbiome characterization will be crucial for fully understanding the biological and clinical significance of circRNAs in SCLC. Moreover, studies integrating circRNA profiling with larger, matched bulk transcriptomic cohorts and immune deconvolution frameworks may provide deeper insights into the immunological implications of circRNA-mediated regulation in SCLC.
A key strength of our study is the comprehensive profiling of circRNAs in both SCLC and normal lung tissues, which allowed us to identify circRNAs that are dysregulated in SCLC. This is particularly noteworthy because only limited studies have examined circRNA expression in SCLC tissue, and none have focused on the Indian population. Another major strength is the validation of two circRNAs and their predicted interacting miRNAs in an expanded cohort, along with the assessment of their associations with clinicopathological characteristics, treatment response, and patient survival. Furthermore, we predicted potential mRNA targets of these miRNAs to construct circRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory networks. However, we do recognize several limitations inherent to in silico target prediction tools. These include: (i) a high rate of false positives, as predicted circRNA-miRNA or miRNA-mRNA interactions may not manifest in vitro or in vivo due to structural constraints or subcellular localization; (ii) reliance on incomplete or biased databases, which can overlook less-studied ncRNAs and fail to capture cell- or tissue-specific expression patterns; (iii) inability to account for dynamic regulatory changes induced by conditions such as hypoxia or stress; and (iv) restricted functional insight, since these tools cannot determine whether predicted interactions result in meaningful biological outcomes. Another important consideration is the need for experimental validation of the predicted circRNA-miRNA interactions. While our in silico analyses provide valuable hypotheses, these interactions must be confirmed through established laboratory approaches. For example, luciferase reporter assays can be used to verify direct binding between circRNAs and miRNAs, RNA immunoprecipitation or RNA pull-down assays can demonstrate physical association, and circRNA knockdown or overexpression experiments can reveal functional consequences on downstream gene regulation. Incorporating such experimental strategies in future studies will be essential to substantiate the computational predictions, establish the mechanistic and clinical relevance of circRNA-mediated regulatory networks in SCLC, and strengthen the biological relevance of our findings.
An important limitation of this study is the use of surgically resected normal lung tissues from early-stage NSCLC patients as controls. This choice was made due to the non-availability of matched adjacent normal tissues from SCLC patients, reflecting the rarity of early-stage SCLC suitable for upfront surgical resection and the challenges in obtaining suitable specimens. However, we acknowledge that differences in underlying biology between NSCLC-derived normal tissues and true adjacent normal tissues may introduce potential confounding factors that could influence the interpretation of our results. To mitigate this, we carefully selected the control group to closely match the SCLC group in terms of age, gender, and smoking status. Future studies incorporating matched adjacent normal tissues or alternative control strategies, such as healthy donor lung samples, will be essential to validate and strengthen the conclusions drawn here.
Another limitation of this study is the relatively small sample size, mainly due to challenges in obtaining SCLC tissue and surgical specimens. It is also important to note that SCLC constitutes only ~ 15% of all lung cancer cases, and the availability of tissue samples suitable for molecular analysis is limited due to its significantly lower incidence compared to NSCLC and the more advanced stage of diagnosis. Despite collecting samples from more than 50 patients with SCLC, only a subset met the stringent quality and integrity requirements necessary for molecular studies, which restricted the number of samples analyzed. We acknowledge that a formal power analysis was constrained by the rarity of SCLC samples, and the limited cohort reduced the statistical power and generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, many patients did not receive treatment, preventing us from collecting survival data, and thereby limiting the emphasis on the role of circRNAs as cancer biomarkers. However, our study provides exploratory insights that can serve as a foundation for future investigations. We agree that future studies involving larger and more diverse patient populations will be essential to validate these results and to draw more robust conclusions.

## Conclusion
We identified 23 DECs in SCLC compared to normal lung tissue using RNA-seq. Upon further validation by qRT-PCR, we found that circLIFR was downregulated and circCAMSAP1 was upregulated in SCLC. We then predicted the interacting miRNA partners for all 23 DECs, including circLIFR and circCAMSAP1. Notably, miR‑1234‑3p and miR‑375‑5p, both predicted to bind circLIFR, were markedly overexpressed in SCLC tissue. In contrast, miR‑145‑5p, an interacting partner of circCAMSAP1, was significantly downregulated. To further explore the regulatory landscape, we predicted the putative mRNA targets of these miRNAs and constructed circRNA–miRNA–mRNA (ceRNA) networks, followed by enrichment analysis of the identified mRNAs. This analysis revealed significant enrichment of several cancer‑related pathways, including the mitotic cell cycle process, Hippo signaling, cellular senescence, autophagy, MAPK signaling, p53 signaling, and FoxO signaling pathways. These findings suggest that some of the identified DECs, particularly circLIFR and circCAMSAP1, may function as ceRNAs and influence SCLC biology by modulating these pathways. However, experimental validation of the predicted ceRNA interactions is essential. Functional assays will also be required to confirm the involvement of these circRNA‑mediated networks in regulating cancer‑associated pathways and to substantiate the mechanistic assumptions proposed in this study.

## Materials and methods

## Recruitment of patients, collection of samples, and purification of RNA
In this prospective study, we recruited 33 newly diagnosed and primarily untreated patients of SCLC from the outpatient Department of Pulmonary, Critical Care and Sleep Medicine, AIIMS, New Delhi, during the years 2020–2023. The diagnosis of SCLC was histopathologically confirmed by hematoxylin and eosin staining and immunohistochemistry for chromogranin A, synaptophysin, neural cell adhesion molecule/CD56, and Ki67 on the biopsy specimens collected during fibreoptic bronchoscopy. Epidemiological data, including age, gender, stage, and smoking habits, were recorded. For staging, computed tomography (CT) scans of the chest and upper abdomen were performed, with additional CT or magnetic resonance imaging scans of the brain and radionuclide bone scans if needed. Since SCLC is rarely treated surgically, the availability of normal lung tissue as a control specimen is a hindrance to molecular studies. Therefore, we collected normal lung tissue from patients with early-stage NSCLC undergoing surgical resection at the Department of Surgical Oncology, Dr. Bhim Rao Ambedkar Institute Rotary Cancer Hospital, AIIMS, New Delhi. Informed written consent was obtained from all participants.
Tissue biopsies were collected from all suspected cases of lung cancer undergoing fibreoptic bronchoscopy for diagnosis. The samples were placed in sterile nuclease-free cryovials (Tarsons) containing RNA stabilization solution (Qiagen). After a 24-h incubation at 2–8 °C, the tissues were transferred to −80 °C until further use. Following histopathological confirmation of SCLC, tissues were processed for total RNA purification using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). The concentration and purity of total RNA were assessed using the Qubit RNA Broad Range Assay Kit (Invitrogen) with the Qubit 3 fluorometer (Invitrogen). The purified RNA was stored at −80 °C for future use.

## Bulk RNA sequencing for profiling of circRNAs
We performed bulk RNA sequencing using total RNA purified from tissue biopsies of histopathologically confirmed SCLC (n = 5) and normal lung tissue (n = 5). Samples with RNA integrity number ≥ 7 underwent library preparation using the NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Library Prep kit for Illumina (NEB). Briefly, 200 ng of input RNA was used, and the ribosomal RNA (rRNA), constituting ~ 95% of the total RNA population, was removed using biotinylated, target-specific oligos combined with Ribo-Cop rRNA removal beads (Lexogen). Following purification, the ribo-depleted RNA was fragmented using divalent cations under elevated temperatures. The RNA fragments were copied into first-strand cDNA using reverse transcriptase. Second-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using DNA Polymerase I and RNase H enzymes. The cDNA fragments underwent a series of enzymatic steps to repair the ends, tail the 3′ end with a single ‘A’ base, and ligate the adapters. The adapter-ligated products were purified and enriched under the following thermal conditions: initial denaturation of 98 °C for 30 s; 13 cycles of 98 °C for 10 s, 65 °C for 75 s, and final extension of 65 °C for 5 min. PCR products were purified and checked for fragment size distribution on TapeStation using D1000 DNA ScreenTapes (Agilent). Prepared libraries were quantified using the Qubit High Sensitivity Assay Kit (Invitrogen). The libraries were pooled and diluted to the final optimal loading concentration before cluster amplification on the Illumina flow cell. Once the cluster generation was completed, the cluster flow cell was loaded on the Illumina HiSeq X instrument to generate 150 bp paired-end reads.

## Bioinformatics analysis for the identification of DECs
Initial quality assessment of the raw Illumina sequencing reads was conducted using FastQC (version 0.11.9). Subsequently, adapter clipping and trimming were performed using Trimmomatic (version 0.39) with default parameters. The trimmed reads were aligned to the human genome reference (GRCh38, Gencode v35) using STAR aligner (version 2.7.8a) , specifically using the parameters ‘chimOutType’, ‘chimOutJunctionFormat’, and ‘chimSegmentMin’ to obtain chimeric junction information. All parameters for STAR were default except ‘chimSegmentMin 12’ and ‘chimOutJunctionFormat 0’. Linear gene expression was quantified from the standard STAR alignment output, while chimeric reads were extracted from the STAR chimeric junction file for circRNA analysis. The linear gene count matrix was generated using the ‘featureCounts’ function from the ‘Subread’ package.
For circRNA analysis, the aligned chimeric reads were annotated using CIRCexplorer2 (version 2.3.8) , using default parameters and GRCh38 reference annotation (Gencode v35). CircRNA expression was quantified at the back-splice junction level, with read counts representing uniquely mapped chimeric reads supporting each circRNA event. Post annotation, differential expression, and functional analysis were performed using the R package ‘circRNAprofiler’ (Bioconductor version 3.11) . Differential gene expression was performed using the DeSeq2 algorithm, with BH correction for the p-values and alpha = 0.05, lfcThreshold = 1 filtering. The annotation names for the significantly dysregulated circRNAs were identified from circBase .

## Construction of ceRNA networks for DECs
The CircInteractome database was used to identify miRNAs that can interact with and be sequestered by circRNAs. Only circRNAs passing the differential expression criteria described in Sect. “ ” (adjusted p < 0.05, |log2FC|≥ 1) and supported by a minimum of five back-splice junction reads in at least one sample were retained for downstream ceRNA network construction. Using these 23 DECs, 241 interacting miRNAs were identified from CircInteractome, and downstream mRNA targets (n = 7,804 genes) were obtained from miRTarBase. CircInteractome uses the TargetScan algorithm, which identifies canonical 7-mer and 8-mer seed matches between miRNAs and circRNA sequences. From the arrays of possible mRNA targets, only those with high-confidence mRNA were selected based on robust validation methods such as reporter assays, western blotting, and qRT-PCR. Consequently, the potential miRNA targets and their downstream mRNA targets were identified. Utilizing these miRNA targets of circRNAs and the high-confidence mRNA targets for each miRNA, a ceRNA regulatory network was constructed using Cytoscape (Cytoscape version 3.9.1). These miRNA binding predictions were performed on circRNA-specific sequences derived from back-splice junctions, and miRNA interactions inferred from linear gene transcripts were not used for ceRNA network construction.

## Enrichment analysis for the identification of pathways involved
GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted to identify enriched biological processes and pathways related to the gene set under investigation, utilizing the clusterProfiler R package . GO enrichment analysis was performed for the Biological Process category, utilizing ENTREZ IDs as input genes. The Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method was employed for p-value adjustment in multiple tests, with a significance threshold of 0.05. Pathways comprising 3 to 800 genes were selected to optimize specificity and interpretability. The org.Hs.eg.db database was utilized to convert gene identifiers into human-readable symbols, facilitating annotation.
KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the same gene set utilizing the KEGG database , with the organism code ‘hsa’ for Homo sapiens. The BH method was applied for multiple testing correction, with a significance threshold of 0.05, retaining pathways containing between 3 and 800 genes. All analyses were conducted in R, ensuring reproducibility and utilizing current annotation resources for functional enrichment.
A category network (CNET) plot was created using the clusterProfiler package to illustrate the relationships among enriched GO terms and their corresponding genes. The pairwise similarity of terms was calculated using pairwise_termsim, and the plot was generated utilizing the Kamada-Kawai layout for optimal node arrangement. Label sizes were modified for enhanced clarity, and the ggforce style was used for annotations. This visualization highlights significant functional associations within the enriched gene set.

## Quantification of circRNA, mRNA, and miRNA expression levels
The expression levels of specific circRNAs and predicted interacting miRNAs in SCLC and normal lung tissue were analyzed using qRT-PCR. For quantifying circRNA or mRNA gene expression levels, ~ 2 µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems). Divergent primers specifically designed for amplifying circRNAs were used in qRT-PCR for quantifying the expression levels of circRNAs using PowerTrack SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) on a Light Cycler 480 II instrument (Roche). The ribosomal protein S18 (RPS18) gene was used to normalize the expression levels of circRNA and mRNA. The relative quantification of circRNA expression was calculated using the ΔCt method, where ΔCt = Ct (circRNA) − Ct (reference RPS18).
To quantify miRNA expression levels, total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using miRCURY LNA RT Kit (Qiagen). The miRCURY LNA SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen) and miRNA-specific miRCURY LNA miRNA PCR Assays (Qiagen) were used for quantifying miRNA expression levels. SNORD44 was used to normalize miRNA expression levels. The relative quantification of miRNA expression was determined using ΔCt method, where ΔCt = Ct (target miRNA) − Ct (reference SNORD44). A list of all the primers used is mentioned in the supplementary table .

## Confirmation of the sequence of circRNAs
We chose to confirm the sequence of circRNAs—circLIFR and circCAMSAP1, which were found to be differentially expressed in SCLC. We first amplified the entire mature sequence of circLIFR (580 nucleotides) and circCAMSAP1 (425 nucleotides) by PCR using a cDNA mix prepared from total RNA purified from SCLC cell lines NCI-H69, NCI-H82, and NCI-H446. Divergent primers designed for specific circRNAs were used. The PCR product was purified from agarose gel, restriction digested, and ligated into the pcDNA3.1 (+) circRNA mini vector (#60648, Addgene) using T4 DNA ligase (NEB). The ligated plasmid was then used to transform the competent DH5α E. coli strain. We selected 3–5 colonies for further culturing and plasmid DNA purification using a PureYield plasmid miniprep kit (Promega). The purified plasmid was then subjected to Sanger sequencing to verify the sequence of the specific mature circRNA.

## Statistical analysis
Descriptive data were expressed as median ± SD or mean ± SD. Median values of quantitative variables were used as the cut-off for high and low values. Student’s t-test was used to compare the mean of two groups, while the Pearson correlation coefficient was used to determine the linear relationship between two continuous variables. The Kaplan–Meier curve was plotted for survival analysis, and a log-rank test was used to compare the statistical significance between groups. OS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to death or last follow-up, while PFS was calculated from the date of the start of treatment to the date of progression, relapse, or death. ROC curves were plotted to calculate AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and likelihood ratio at multiple cut-offs. All statistical analyses were done using STATA 11.2 (Texas, USA) and GraphPad Prism v8.0.2, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.
