Recently, many new kinds of nucleases have been explored to expand genome editing tools, such as the obligate mobile element guided activity (OMEGA) system [ ], which is a new class of RNA-guided nucleases including TnpB, IscB, and Fanzor. The TnpB [ , ], IscB [ ], and Fanzor [ ] have been successively engineered into new miniaturized gene editing tools. These small-sized nucleases can be delivered to cells and tissues more easily and exhibit superior characteristics in targeted gene editing in vivo [ ], but still require a transposon-associated motif (TAM) to initiate the DNA cleavage, which is more complex than the PAM (protospacer adjacent motif) sequence [ ]. So the expansion of other nucleases’ range is necessary.
Previously, we reported an Archaeoglobus fulgidus flap endonuclease 1 (aFEN1) to build a gene editing tool [ ], which is guided by a hairpin-DNA guiding probe (hpDNA). The FEN1-hpDNA has been applied to eliminate viruses [ ] and effectively reverse liver fibrosis [ ], showing the potential for gene therapy applications. However, the robust RNA-guided CRISPR systems stimulated our interest in developing RNA-guided nucleases. Then we recombined a TaqTth nuclease, consisting of two domains of TaqPol and one domain of TthPol, and explored its cleavage of targeted RNA when guided by an hpRNA, fortunately with satisfied efficiency [ ].
Since the recombinant TaqTth has a 5′ nuclease domain similar to aFEN1 [ , ], a nuclease for both DNA and RNA targets, we hypothesize that TaqTth-hpRNA can also cleave targeted DNA. In this study, we are going to carefully study the capacity of TaqTth-hpRNA to cleave the genomic DNA of both Escherichia coli and mammalian cells. An investigation will be conducted into the sequence motif, specificity, and editing outcome.
In summary, in vitro biochemical studies showed that TaqTth-hpRNA could efficiently cleave DNA targets without PAM. It also achieved a high cleavage efficiency in E. coli. The DNA editing in mammalian cells induced large fragment deletions assisted by microhomology-mediated end joining (MMEJ) repair pathway [ ], which was different from indels generated from the common DNA editing systems. In addition, the cleavage by TaqTth-hpRNA was highly sensitive to mismatches in targeted regions. It can cleave single-nucleotide mutant APPlon without cleaving wild-type APP, which provides a complementary therapy option for autosomal dominant diseases.

## Materials and methods

## Plasmid construction
For in vitro assay, the TaqTth OFR was synthesized and assembled into the pET28a(+). The expression and purification of the TaqTth recombinant protein were carried out as previously described [ ].
For E. coli genome editing, the TaqTth ORF was subcloned into a vector containing the Tc-inducible promoter, chloramphenicol-selectable marker, and p15A replication origin (as the p-TaqTth plasmid). The pTargetF plasmid and the pSC101_TIMER plasmid were purchased from MiaoLing Biology. The p-hpRNA-S plasmids were constructed by ligating annealed oligonucleotides ( ) to the SpeI/EcoR I-digested pTargetF plasmid. The pSC101_TIMER plasmid digested by Hind III/SgrA I was ligated to a synthetic sequence ( ) to generate the transitional plasmid. To create p-hpRNA-A plasmids, the transitional plasmid was digested using NheI/EcoR I and ligated to annealed oligonucleotides ( ). Sequences with two J23119 promoters ( ) were synthesized and assembled into the pTargetF vector digested with BamH I/EcoR I in order to construct the p-biohpRNA plasmids.
For genome editing in mammalian cells, TaqTth OFR [ ] and TaqTthM OFR were PCR (polymerase chain reaction) amplified and inserted into the pcDNA3.1(+) vector, and HA tag and NLS were added to the N-terminus. The annealed oligonucleotides for hpRNA plasmids in mammalian cells ( ) were cloned into the U6-gRNA vector, which was digested with Bbs I and Sac I.
The TaqTth plasmid map and associated sequence information ( ) are available on Benchling (https://www.benchling.com/).

## Cleaving ssDNA in vitro
A 10 μl reaction mixture consisting of hpRNA (10 pmol), single-strand DNA (ssDNA, the 5′ end of the target ssDNA was labeled with the fluorescent group Cy5) substrate (1 pmol), Tris–HCl (pH 8.9) 10 mM, KCl 50 mM, MgCl2 1.5 mM, and an appropriate amount of purified TaqTth was prepared and incubated at 37°C for 2 h. The reaction products were combined with 2× formamide gel loading buffer (90% formamide, 0.5% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, 0.1% xylene cyanol, and 0.1% bromophenol blue), denatured at 98°C for 10 min, and separated by denaturing urea–PAGE (polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis) gel (20% TBE–urea, 160 V). DNA products were visualized by Cy5 fluorescence signal by Amersham Imager 600 (GE Healthcare). The artificially synthesized targeted ssDNAs and probes in vitro are provided in .

## Sequence preference detection and analysis
For in vitro assay, a DNA substrate library with seven randomized nucleotides and its hpRNA were incubated and reacted with TaqTth in vitro followed by agarose electrophoresis. Reactions without TaqTth were served as negative controls. The uncleaved substrates were recovered and amplified by PCR. Primers used for PCR were S1-F and S1-R ( ). The PCR program was 95°C for 5 min and 35 cycles of (95°C for 30 s, 60°C for 30 s, 72°C for 10 s).
For E. coli assay, the MG1655 carrying the p-TaqTth plasmid was electroporated with the p-biohpRNA plasmid and a plasmid library ( ) with two 5′ random nucleotides and four 3′ random nucleotides inserted at the target site. The remaining operations were performed as previously described [ ].

In vitro and E. coli samples were sequenced using MiniSeq Illumina by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). For analysis, the randomized sequences were extracted. Weblogo (https://weblogo.berkeley.edu/logo.cgi) was used to generate logo based on top 10% depleted sequences [ ].

## The cleavage site assay
The cleaved substrates were excised and purified from urea–PAGE gels, and then 3′ tailed with terminal transferase (Takara, #2230A) with dCTP according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The tailed cleaved substrates were amplified using primer CV-F and polyG ( ). The purified amplicons were cloned into pEASY-Blunt vector (TransGen Biotech, #CB101), transformed into Trans5α Chemically Competent Cell (TransGen Biotech, #CD201-01). Then, the clone was sequenced using the Sanger method by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).

## Genome editing in E. coli
The E. coli carrying the p-TaqTth plasmid was electroporated with the hpRNA-transcription plasmids. Three positive clones were selected from each group and incubated overnight at 37°C. The density of the cells in each group was then adjusted to 600 nm (OD600) to reach 0.05, and the cells in each group were plated on LB plates supplemented with the corresponding antibiotics with tetracycline (200 ng/ml) at 1000-fold dilution. After overnight incubation at 37°C, the number of survival clones was measured. The target sequences of hpRNA in E. coli are listed in .

## Determination of growth performance of E. coli after transformation with TaqTth-hpRNA
MG1655 transformed with TaqTth-empty vector plus nontargeting-hpDNA (VC-NT) plasmids and the TaqTth plus nontargeting-hpDNA (TaqTth-NT) plasmids were incubated with tetracycline (200 ng/ml) overnight at 37°C. All strains were subsequently diluted to a working concentration of OD600 = 0.1 and cultured at 37°C with shaking at ∼220 rpm. Two hundred microliters of samples of each group of strains were taken and added to 96-well plates for growth curve by OD600. Measurements were taken for 24 h.

## Cell culture and transfection
Cells were cultivated in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (HyClone), 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 μg/ml streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO2 incubation. For TaqTth-hpRNA plasmid transfection, cells were seeded into a 24-well plate with a density of 1.0 × 105 cells per well. The following day, each well was transfected with 3 μl TransIntro™ EL Transfection Reagent (TransGen Biotech, #FT201-01), 600 ng of pcDNA3.1(+)-HA-NLS-TaqTth plasmid, and 400 ng of hpRNA plasmid (200 ng of hpRNA-S and 200 ng of hpRNA-A) following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. After 72 h, cells were harvested to perform the following experiments. The CRISPR/Cas9 plasmid transfection procedure was identical to TaqTth-hpRNA plasmid transfection. The target sequences of RNA probes in mammalian cells are listed in .

## Flow cytometry
HEK293T-EGFP cells were trypsinized and resuspended in phosphate buffered saline. Cells were measured immediately by flow cytometry using FACS Calibur (Becton Dickinson). The data were analyzed by FlowJo software.

## Quantitative PCR
For RNA collection, cells were seeded in a 24-well plate and total RNA was isolated from cells using RNA-easy Isolation Reagent (Vazyme, #R701-01/02) following the manufacturer’s protocol. No more than 1 μg of RNA was used for cDNA (complementary DNA) synthesis with the HiScriptIII 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (+gDNA wiper) (Vazyme, #R312-01). The quantitative PCR (qPCR) reaction mixture was prepared using AceQ qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Low ROX Premixed) (Vazyme, #Q131-02) according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. The specific primers are provided in .

## Immunofluorescence
Cells were fixed (30 min, 4% paraformaldehyde solution), permeabilized (20 min, 0.2% Triton X-100), blocked [30 min, 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA)], and then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. Antibody dilutions were as follows: 53BP1 (Abclonal Technology, #A3859, 1:100) and HA tag (Abclonal Technology, #AE008, 1:100). Coverslips were incubated with goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) cross-adsorbed secondary antibody, Alexa Fluor™ 488 (Thermo Fisher, #A11008), or Alexa Fluor™ 546 (Thermo Fisher, #A11003) for 1 h at 37°C. The nuclei were stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole for 20 min. The coverslips were inverted onto slides and immersed in a mounting medium. A laser scanning confocal microscope FV10-ASW (Ver 2.1) (Olympus Corp., MPE FV1000) was used for co-localization analysis.

## Western blot
Cells were homogenized in RIPA buffer. Ten micrograms of protein extract was loaded onto a 10% or 13.5% SDS–polyacrylamide gel. Following electrophoresis, proteins were transferred onto a PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked with 3% BSA and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. The specific protein bands were stained with High-sig ECL Western Blotting Substrate (Tanon) and imaged using the Amersham Imager 600 (GE Healthcare). Primary antibodies included antibodies against HA (Abclonal Technology, #AE008, 1:1000), β-actin (Abclonal Technology, #AC026, 1:5000), APP (Abclonal Technology, #A3161, 1:1000), and γ-H2AX (Proteintech, #29380-1-AP, 1:1000).

## PCR and sequencing analysis for genome modification
Genomic DNA was extracted using the EasyPure® Genomic DNA Kit (TransGen Biotech, #EE101-01) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The genomic region surrounding TaqTth-hpRNA target site for each gene was PCR amplified. To reduce nonspecific PCR products, nested-PCR and PrimeSTAR® HS DNA Polymerase (Takara, #R010A) were used. The products were purified using EasyPure® PCR Purification Kit (TransGen Biotech, #EP101-02) following manufacturer’s protocol and analyzed on 1% or 2% agarose gels. Gels were stained with GelRed stain and imaged. For large deletion analysis, PCR products corresponding to genomic modifications were purified and cloned into the pMD18-T vector (Takara, #6011). Then, transformants for each group were randomly selected and sequenced to identify clones using the M13 primers. For indels analysis, PCR products were sequenced using MiniSeq Illumina by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The primers utilized in this study are provided in .

## Quantification of total genomic DNA to determine the deletion efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA
The deletion efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA was calculated via qPCR in mammalian cells [ ]. Primers within the deletion region (P1 and P2) and spanning the deletion region (P3 and P4) were designed at the endogenous CCR5 locus. The 258-bp DNA fragments (referred to as WT) were amplified in the genome of untreated HEK293T using primers (P1 and P2) as unedited templates. Furthermore, DNA fragments containing the deletion junctions were synthesized and inserted them into the PUC19 vector. This plasmid (referred to as edited) serves as edited templates that can amplify 244-bp DNA fragments using the primers (P3 and P4). Using indicated primers and templates to perform qPCR, two standard curves were generated. Similarly, primers within the deletion region (P5 and P6) and spanning the deletion region (P7 and P8) were designed at the endogenous EMX1 locus. The other two standard curves were established using the corresponding primers and templates. The standard curves were applied to calculate the absolute copy number of genomic DNA with and without deletion. The deletion efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA was calculated using the following equation: deletion efficiency = copy number of DNA with deletion/copy number of DNA with and without deletion. The specific primers are provided in .

## Genomic DNA sequencing
For whole-genome sequencing (WGS), genomic DNA was extracted from HEK293T cells transfected TaqTth-hpRNA plasmids. Qubit dsDNA HS assay kit (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, #N608301-0100) was used to test concentration and 1% agarose gel electrophoresis to confirm integrity. The library preparation and next-generation sequence was finished by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai). First, 500 ng quantified DNA was randomly fragmented by Covaris (Woburn, USA). Next, Hieff NGS®MaxUp II DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (YEASEN, Shanghai, China) was used for next steps. Briefly, Endprep enzyme was added to repair end and 3′ end A tail ligation. Then adaptor was ligated by enhancer and Fast T4 DNA ligase. Index primer was added by PCR and the amplified product ∼400 bp was selected by DNA selection beads. The library concentration and size was confirmed by Qubit 4.0 (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, USA) and 2% agarose gel electrophoresis, respectively. Finally, the libraries were pooled and loaded on NovaSeq 6000 (Illumina, San Diego, USA)/DNBseq-T7 (BGI, Shenzhen, China) sequencer by 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequence kit according to the manufacture’s instructions. Raw reads containing adaptor sequences and those with ambiguous or low-quality bases at the beginning or end were trimmed using Fastp. The qualified reads from each sample were aligned to human genome assembly hg38 using BWA (0.7.17) with default parameters. Duplicated reads were removed and coverage values were calculated using SAMtools. Visual analyses were performed via Integrative Genomics Viewer. The editing efficiency was calculated as previously described [ ].

## CCK-8 assay
The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8; Vazyme, #A311) was used to verify the cell viability according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The HEK293T cells were seeded in 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells per well) and incubated overnight. The cells were transfected with 0.75 μl of TransIntro™ EL Transfection Reagent, 150 ng of pcDNA3.1(+)-HA-NLS-TaqTth plasmid and 100 ng of hpRNA plasmid for the TaqTth-NT group, and 0.75 μl of TransIntro™ EL Transfection Reagent for the EL Transfection Reagent group. At 24, 48, and 72 h after transfection, CCK-8 was added to each well and then incubated at 37°C for 1 h. The optical densities (ODs) were measured at 450 nm using a a SynergyTM HT multi-mode reader (Bio-Tek, Winoosky, VT).

## Statistical analysis
All data were expressed as mean ± s.d. or mean ± s.e.m. from at least three individual experiments. GraphPad Prism 9 was used for statistical analyses. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed using two-tailed Student’s t-tests. Results with statistically significance were marked with asterisks. P< .05 was considered to reach statistical significance.

## Results

## The design of the TaqTth-mediated DNA cleaving tool and in vitro characterization
The TaqTth (Fig. ) has been recombined and assembled an RNA cleavage tool [ ]. Since it has a 5′ nuclease domain similar to aFEN1, which cleaves both DNA and RNA targets, we hypothesized that the TaqTth is also a DNA nuclease. One DNA guiding probe (probe 1) and four RNA guiding probes (probes 2–5) were employed to mate suitable guiding probes. Only the hpDNA (probe 1) and hpRNA (probe 3) successfully guided TaqTth to cleave targeted ssDNA (Fig. and ). Although the TaqTth-hpDNA possessed a higher ssDNA cleavage activity, the 3′ flap of hpDNA can be partly extended by TaqTth with dNTP (Fig. ), which means its structure will be destroyed in mammalian cells. The Tth domain of TaqTth may be responsible for this due to its relatively poor correction ability of 3′ terminal mismatched bases [ ]. Therefore, the hpRNA was chosen to guide the recombinant TaqTth and develop the DNA editing tool in the following study.
 To test the potential sequence motif requirements of TaqTth-hpRNA in DNA cleaving, a library of DNA substrates (ssDNA-N) containing seven randomized nucleotides was built. The hpRNA-N1 and hpRNA-N2 were designed to place the seven random nucleotides upstream and downstream regions of the target sequence, respectively. After 2-h reaction, the uncleaved substrates were recovered, purified, and amplified. The amplicons were sequenced using an Illumina platform. Compared to the negative control, the seven random nucleotides, whether located upstream or downstream region of the target sequence, reflected no stringent motif (Fig. ).
To test the cleavage position of TaqTth-hpRNA in vitro, we recovered and sequenced the cleaved product as described in the “Materials and methods” section. As shown in Fig. , the DNA break site was located between 1 and 2 nt at the 5′ end of the target sequence. Further, the stem-loop was advanced. The hpRNA-V1 and hpRNA-V2 were obtained with a better performance compared with hpRNA-V3 in vitro (Fig. ). The length of the guide sequence was also optimized and should not be shorter than 20 nt (Fig. ). To uncover more biochemical properties of TaqTth, we performed an in vitro ssDNA cleavage assay to analyze the effect of environmental factors on TaqTth nuclease activity. TaqTth was sensitive to temperature (>24°C), pH (>7), magnesium concentrations (<10 mM), and KCl concentrations (<100 mM) ( ). These results indicated the cleavage activity and properties of TaqTth-hpRNA in vitro, which laid a foundation for in vivo studies.

## TaqTth-mediated DNA cleavage in E. coli
To explore whether TaqTth is capable of programmed cleavage of genomic DNA in E. coli, we constructed a three-plasmid system (p-TaqTth, p-hpRNA-S, and p-hpRNA-A) as shown in Fig. . The p-TaqTth plasmid expresses TaqTth in E. coli under a tetracycline-inducible promoter. The p-hpRNA-S and p-hpRNA-A plasmids carry the constitutive promoter J23119 driving hpRNA transcription. When the three-plasmid system was transformed into MG1655, the expressed TaqTth would cleave the genomic DNA under the guidance of hpRNA, and then the bacterial cells would be killed because most bacterial species have a weak self-repair ability (Fig. ).
 The arpB is a nonessential pseudogene, whose mRNA (messenger RNA) cleavage would not reduce survival while DNA cleavage does [ ]. Compared to the TaqTth-empty vector plus nontargeting-hpDNA (VC-NT) control group, the TaqTth plus nontargeting-hpDNA (TaqTth-NT) group, and the TaqTth-empty vector plus targeting-hpDNA (VC-T) group, the TaqTth guided by the hpRNA targeting only the sense strand of arpB (arpB-S1 and arpB-S2) or antisense strand (arpB-A) did not reduce the number of survival clones (Fig. ). It inferred that targeting only one of the DNA strands in genomic DNA cannot achieve cleavage.
Then, hpRNA pairs targeting DNA double strands of arpB were further designed, and all of them significantly reduced the number of survival clones by up to 80.3% (Fig. ). To reduce the pressure of multiple plasmid transformations in bacteria, a p-biohpRNA plasmid transcribing both hpRNA pairs simultaneously was constructed (Fig. ). The p-TaqTth and p-biohpRNA targeting DNA double strands of arpB were transformed into MG1655 reducing the number of survival clones by up to 81.4% (Fig. ). The p-TaqTth and p-biohpRNA continued to target DNA double strands of sulA promoter, which reduced the number of survival clones by 83.8% (Fig. ). Thus, the assembly of hpRNA pairs into one plasmid did not affect the efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA cleavage of the E. coli genome. Moreover, the strain carrying TaqTth and nontargeting-hpDNA (TaqTth-NT) exhibited no obvious inhibition in cell growth under induced conditions ( ), which indicated that the expression of TaqTth did not affect the growth of E. coli. The dual plasmid system (p-TaqTth and p-biohpRNA) is a better option for future bacterial genome editing.
To further prove that TaqTth-hpRNA can cleave the genome of E. coli without sequence preference. A plasmid library was used with two 5′ random nucleotides and four 3′ random nucleotides inserted at the target site (Fig. ). The plasmid library, the p-TaqTth plasmid, and p-biohpRNA plasmid were co-transformed into MG1655 ( ). When targeting the plasmid library, the number of survival clones was reduced by 52.8% ( ). This indicated that TaqTh-hpRNA successfully disrupted the plasmid library. The remaining plasmids were extracted and subjected to high-throughput sequencing, and no sequence preference was found in these six loci (Fig. ).

## TaqTth-mediated genome editing in mammalian cells
To verify whether the TaqTth-hpRNA can be harnessed as a genome editing tool in mammalian cells, the plasmid encoding N-terminal nuclear localization sequence (NLS) fused TaqTth and the plasmid driven by U6 promoter for transcription of hpRNA were constructed. A polymerase-deactivated TaqTth mutation (TaqTthM, D785N) was introduced [ ], which eliminated the polymerization activity of TaqTth ( ). Consequently, TaqTthM may not bind to hpRNA [ ]. The NLS-TaqTth and NLS-TaqTthM were expressed well in the nucleus of HEK293T cells (Fig. ).
 The TaqTth-hpRNA activity was tested by targeting the EF1α promoter [ ] of EGFP (avoiding the noise from cleaving EGFP mRNA) in stable-transformed HEK293T cells. Compared to the hpRNA-nontargeting (NT) group, the TaqTth guided by the hpRNA targeting only the sense (hpRNA-S) or antisense strand (hpRNA-A) of the EF1α promoter did not reduce EGFP expression based on flow cytometry (Fig. ), just as the results in E. coli. Then as Fig. showed, sets of hpRNA pairs targeting the DNA double strand were designed. The spacer length between the two cleavage sites varied to generate 3′ overhangs (space −), blunt ends (space 0), and 5′ overhangs (space +) products, respectively. Among the spaces of −85 to 100 bp, the 0-, 8-, and 20-bp hpRNA pairs guided TaqTth to decrease EGFP expression with a maximum knockdown efficiency of 9%, while TaqTthM did not (Fig. and  ). Moreover, the stem-loops of hpRNA-V1 and hpRNA-V2 had similar efficiencies, and a ratio of TaqTth:hpRNA of 6:4 was better ( ).
With the optimized conditions, TaqTth-hpRNA also targeted the RB1 promoter [ ] to avoid the noise from cleaving RB1 mRNA. The result showed that TaqTth-hpRNA cleavage of the RB1 promoter caused a 41.7% decrease in RB1 mRNA (Fig. ). DNA damage response (DDR) is a finely tuned signaling network required to repair DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) [ ]. The expression of phosphorylated histone H2AX (γH2AX), as a DDR marker [ ], extracted from the above-treated cells was increased by ∼3.5-fold (Fig. ). Meanwhile, a high-level expression (54.4% >3 foci) of the p53-binding protein 1 (53BP1), also a DDR protein [ ], was observed by IF in HEK293T cells treated by TaqTth-hpRNA targeting RB1 promoter (Fig. ). These results demonstrated the DNA cleavage activity of TaqTth-hpRNA in mammalian cells. Considering the future clinical applications of gene editing tools, the effect of the nuclease-associated toxicities of TaqTth-hpRNA on cell growth was examined. At 24, 48, and 72 h after the TaqTth plus nontargeting-hpDNA (TaqTth-NT) plasmids transfection, the cell growth in the TaqTth-NT group was not different from that in the EL Transfection Reagent group, indicating that the cell growth was not affected ( ).
To define the DNA level editing caused by TaqTth-hpRNA, testing was turned to genomic DNA’s PCR and sequencing analysis for the endogenous CCR5 and EMX1 loci. No indels were detected at the endogenous CCR5 and EMX1 loci (  and S ). However, clone #5 at the CCR5 locus displayed a 1525-bp deletion, notably the breakpoint junction contains a microhomologous sequence (MHS) (10 bp) (Fig. A and  ). Similarly, clone #3 at the EMX1 locus displayed a 1909-bp large deletion with an MHS (Fig. B and  ). A large deletion with an MHS was also found at the RB1 promoter locus (  and S ).
 To calculate the deletion efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA at endogenous gene locus, qPCR was used to calculate the deletion efficiency at the CCR5 and EMX1 loci ( ). TaqTth-hpRNA resulted in 7.3% deletion efficiency at the CCR5 locus in HEK293T cells (Fig. ). At the EMX1 locus, TaqTth-hpRNA induced deletion efficiencies of 9.2%, 7.6%, and 6.2% in HEK293T cells, HepG2 cells, and A549 cells, respectively (Fig. and  ). Furthermore, clones with the same large deletion as in Fig. were identified in HepG2 cells and A549 cells ( ). These results indicate that TaqTth-hpRNA can efficiently cleave DNA and induce large deletions in mammalian cells.
The occurrence of MHSs at TaqTth-hpRNA-induced large deletions suggests the potential involvement of the MMEJ repair pathway. Therefore, a hypothesis on the mechanism of cutting genomic DNA by TaqTth-hpRNA was drawn. When TaqTth-hpRNA cleaves the DNA double strand and produces 3′ overhangs, it can accurately be repaired by the polymerase activity of TaqTth ( ), while blunt ends and 5′ overhangs can be disrupted by the 5′ exonuclease activity, generating large deletions. Notably, the MHSs (5–25 bp) in the 3′ flap can benefit the loss of sequence information (Fig. ).

## The high specificity of TaqTth-hpRNA to distinguish single-nucleotide mutation
To examine the specificity of TaqTth-hpRNA, the 1- or 2-nt mismatches were introduced into the guide sequences of hpRNA-EF1α-S or hpRNA-EF1α-A in the EGFP-stable cell line. Compared with the fully matched controls, all mismatched versions of hpRNA-EF1α-S did not reduce EGFP expression based on flow cytometry (Fig. , left), but the 1- and 2-nt mismatches at the 5′ end of the guide sequence of hpRNA-EF1α-A resulted in a decrease in EGFP expression (Fig. , right). It indicated that the TaqTth-hpRNA showed high sensitivity of mismatches in the regions close to the stem-loop structure. To investigate the genome-wide off-target effect of TaqTth-hpRNA, WGS assay was performed on an HEK293T cell sample treated with TaqTth-hpRNA (targeting EMX1). Obvious deletions were observed at the EMX1 locus and the editing efficiency reached 13.7% (Fig. and  ), which was similar to the previous qPCR results. In addition, the mutation frequency reads of six potential off-target sites were much fewer than that of the target site (Fig. and  ).
 To further utilize the high specificity, the pathogenic mutate allele was picked as the target. The APP Swedish (APPswe, K670N/M671L) and APP London (APPlon, V717I) mutations are examples of mutations that lead to an increased Aβ production and development of familial Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [ ]. Patients carrying the mutation usually have a wild-type APP (APPwt) allele that may have important physiological functions. Thus, selective disruption of the APPswe or APPlon allele holds therapeutic promise for familial AD. Two HEK293T cell lines stably expressing APPwt and 5×FAD were created using lentivirus (Fig. ). The HEK293T-5×FAD cell line contains the APPswe and APPlon mutations.
 To target the APPswe or APPlon mutation, two gRNAs (Swe-gRNA and Lon-gRNA) were designed for CRISPR/Cas9 to place these mutation sites in the guide sequence (Fig. ). The Cas9 system targeting APPswe reduced APP mRNA by 26.3% in the HEK293T-5×FAD cell line but not wild-type APP mRNA in the HEK293T-APP-WT cell line (Fig. ). However, the CRISPR/Cas9 system targeting APPlon failed to reduce APP expression (Fig. ). Hence, TaqTth-hpRNA was applied for this tricky locus.
Four hpRNAs targeting the APPlon single-base mutation were designed, including hpRNA-Lon-A1, hpRNA-Lon-S1, hpRNA-Lon-A2, and hpRNA-Lon-S2. The single-base mutation was placed in the guide sequence of these hpRNAs (Fig. ). The hpRNA-lon-S1 plus hpRNA-lon-A1, hpRNA-lon-S1 plus hpRNA-lon-A2, hpRNA-lon-S2 plus hpRNA-lon-A1, and hpRNA-lon-S2 plus hpRNA-lon-A2 are grouped as hpRNA pairs. Two HEK293T cell lines stably expressing 5×FAD or APPwt(HEK293T-5×FAD/HEK293T-APP-WT cell lines) were transfected with TaqTth and hpRNA plasmids (Fig. ). Compared to the hpDNA-nontargeting (NT) group, the APP mRNA in the HEK293T-5×FAD cell line was downregulated by 23.3% in the group of hpRNA-lon-S1 plus hpRNA-lon-A1 (Fig. ). In the HEK293T-APP-WT cell line, the TaqTth-hpRNA treatment showed no detectable change in wild-type APP mRNA (Fig. ). Moreover, the protein aspect of downregulation was also analyzed. Western blotting showed that TaqTth-hpRNA decreased the levels of APPlon protein (23.8%) in the group of hpRNA-lon-S1 plus hpRNA-lon-A1, but not APPwt protein (Fig. ). Further, two clones with large deletions with an MHS were detected at the APP locus in the HEK293T-5×FAD cell line (Fig. and  ). These results demonstrate the specificity of distinguishing single-nucleotide APPlon mutation by TaqTth-hpRNA. Thus, TaqTth-hpRNA can be used as a complementary DNA editing tool for targeting allelic mutations.

## Discussion
In this study, a novel DNA cleaving tool TaqTth-hpRNA, composed of compact TaqTth and hpRNAs, was developed. In vitro biochemical studies showed that TaqTth-hpRNA could efficiently cleave artificially synthesized ssDNA with no stringent motif like PAM or TAM, significantly broadening the scope of targetable genomic locus. The TaqTth-hpRNA system cleaved the genome with ∼80% efficiency in E. coli, exhibiting higher activity than HpSGN [ ]. In mammalian cells, we found that its products were large deletions mediated by MMEJ repair pathway. TaqTth-hpRNA also demonstrated its specificity of distinguishing single-nucleotide mutation, specifically knocking out the single-nucleotide APPlon mutation without altering APPwt. Thus, TaqTth-hpRNA can be used as a complementary gene editing tool when targeting sequences CRISPR/Cas9 does not work well.
Compared to the HpSGN system, the TaqTth-hpRNA system uses RNA as guiding probes and is more suitable for gene manipulation in bacteria. TaqTth-hpRNA can effectively degrade plasmids carrying resistance genes or disrupt specific resistance genes to develop novel antibiotics, providing new therapeutic strategies to reduce antibiotic resistance.
The current major gene editing tools Cas9 and Cas12 primarily induce small indels by non-homologous end joining [ , ]. In comparison, TaqTth-hpRNA predominantly induces large deletions by MMEJ repair pathway. TaqTth-hpRNA may be applicable for the deletion of pathogenic exons or introns, such as Usher’s syndrome [ ] and Duchenne muscular dystrophy [ ]. Cas9 or Cas12 treatments for such diseases require two DNA double-strand breaks to remove long fragments, which may increase the risk of off-target and chromosomal rearrangements [ , ]. MMEJ was thought to be dependent on DNA polymerase θ (Polθ) [ ], but recently DNA polymerase λ (Polλ) has also been reported to promote MMEJ in mammalian cells [ ]. Therefore, TaqTth’s polymerase may play an important role in the process of MMEJ repair pathway. MMEJ requires short homologous sequences to realign the end of the DSB to produce an annealing intermediate, which may be extended by the polymerase activity of TaqTth and finally ligated by ligase.
Currently, the CRISPR–Cas3 system has been reported to induce large deletions with an average editing efficiency of 26.6% at endogenous gene loci [ ]. In addition, it has also been reported to have high specificity and did not cause obvious off-target cleavage in the genome [ , ]. Although the CRISPR/Cas3 system exhibits higher large deletion editing efficiency than TaqTth-hpRNA, its effective component total gene size is larger than 5 kb, which prevents it from fitting into a single AAV [ , ]. When the dual AAV delivery strategy is adopted, the editing efficiency is usually lower than that of the single AAV delivery [ , ]. However, TaqTth has been proven to be compact to be packaged by a single AAV delivery [ ]. In addition to the limitations imposed by the overall gene size of CRISPR/Cas3, its PAM [ , ] also restricts its application in disease treatment. Therefore, TaqTth-hpRNA may be more valuable in disease treatment.
Future work is still needed to demonstrate TaqTth-hpRNA genome editing activity in animal models. The therapeutic effect of delivering TaqTth-hpRNA with a single AAV may be longer [ ]. Delivery of TaqTth-hpRNA in the form of mRNA via non-viral vectors such as LNPs for the treatment of genetic disorders may be safer and have lower off-target effects [ ]. In the future, the delivery form of TaqTth-hpRNA will be selected according to the different therapeutic needs of different genetic disorders. Compared with the CRISPR-associated system, the editing efficiency of the TaqTth-hpRNA system remains to be improved. For instance, strategies such as iterative protein engineering or phage-assisted discontinuous and sequential evolution [ , ] may be employed to enhance the editing efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA. It is possible that due to the currently low genome editing efficiency of TaqTth-hpRNA and the low sequencing depth of WGS, some rare mutations may not have been observed. Further work is needed to carefully predict and assess the potential off-target effects of TaqTth-hpRNA and define the principles for designing effective and specific hpRNA at different chromatin regions. Subsequently, TaqTth can try to fuse with deaminase to develop base editors. Since both TaqPol [ ] and TthPol [ ] have reverse transcriptase activity, TaqTth may have reverse transcriptase activity. Therefore, TaqTth-hpRNA may be designed as a precision editing tool similar to the prime editor system [ ]. Besides, TaqTth-hpRNA and single-stranded donor DNA may be designed as a precision gene editing tool, analogous to the click editor [ ]. The click editor comprises a Cas9 nickase that creates a nick in one strand of DNA and a DNA polymerase that utilizes the single-stranded donor DNA as a template for precision gene editing.
In summary, we hope the TaqTth-hpRNA, with characteristics of small size, no PAM requirement, high specificity, and large deletion products, can make its contribution to expand complementary genome editing tools and potential therapeutic strategy for treating autosomal dominant disorders in the future.
