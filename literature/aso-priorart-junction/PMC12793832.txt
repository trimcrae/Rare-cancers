## Abstract
Exon 20 insertions of HER2, encoded by erb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ERBB2), and other activating HER2 mutations occur in 2% to 4% of lung adenocarcinomas, but there are only limited therapeutic options available for these patients. Sevabertinib (BAY 2927088) is a potent and reversible dual EGFR–HER2 inhibitor that is selective with respect to wild-type EGFR. In this study, we report the preclinical activity of sevabertinib in lung cancer models harboring alterations of HER2, including exon 20 insertions, point mutations, and amplification of wild-type ERBB2. We furthermore demonstrate the activity of sevabertinib in a cancer cell line dependent on a fusion of neuregulin-1, a ligand for the HER2 family member and heterodimerization partner, HER3. Finally, we report patient responses to sevabertinib from a phase 1/2 clinical trial, indicating potential benefit for patients with HER2-mutant lung cancer.

## Significance:
Additional therapeutic options are needed for patients with lung cancer with HER2 activating mutations, including exon 20 insertions. Sevabertinib shows activity against ERBB2-encoded HER2 exon 20 insertions in preclinical models of lung cancer, corroborated by early data from a phase 1/2 clinical trial.

## Introduction
The erb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ERBB2), encoding the HER2 protein, is frequently altered in cancer. These alterations include ERBB2 amplifications in breast and other cancers ( ), HER2 exon 20 insertions in lung cancer ( , ), and activating point mutations of HER2 across cancers ( ). ERBB2 amplifications in breast cancer are successfully targeted by a suite of mAbs and antibody-drug conjugates, including trastuzumab, pertuzumab, margetuximab, trastuzumab emtansine, and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd; refs. – ). The small-molecule HER2 inhibitors, lapatinib, neratinib, and tucatinib, are additionally approved in the United States for the treatment of ERBB2-amplified breast cancer ( – ). ERBB2 amplification is also found in gastroesophageal cancer, but HER2-targeted therapies are less effective in this tumor type due to tumor heterogeneity and co-occurring cancer gene mutations ( , ). Oncogenic fusions of neuregulin-1 (NRG1), the ligand for HER3 (encoded by ERBB3), a heterodimerization partner of HER2, also occur in lung and other cancers ( ). Some patients with tumors driven by NRG1 fusions respond to treatment with zenocutuzumab, a bispecific HER2-HER3 mAb ( ).
Activating small in-frame exon 20 insertions of HER2, the majority of which are A775_G776insYVMA (A775insYVMA), occur in 1% to 2% of non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC; refs. , – ). Activating missense mutations of HER2 are found in a further 1% to 2% of NSCLC ( ), also occurring at comparable frequencies in primary and hormone therapy–resistant breast cancers ( , , ), bladder cancer, and gastroesophageal cancer ( ). Although small-molecule inhibitors of HER2 have shown some activity in HER2-mutant cancers ( , ), there are currently no small-molecule inhibitors for patients with lung cancer with HER2 mutations that have been awarded full FDA approval, and targeted therapy options for these patients remain limited. The antibody–drug conjugate T-DXd received accelerated approval for treatment of patients with lung cancer with activating mutations of HER2. However, the T-DXd objective response rate (ORR) of 55% ( ) suggests that there is still a need for improved therapeutic options for patients with cancer with HER2 mutations. The irreversible small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), zongertinib, has been tested in clinical trials for patients with HER2-mutant lung cancer, with an ORR of 71% ( ), and recently received accelerated approval from the US FDA ( ).
In this study, we demonstrate the activity of sevabertinib (BAY 2927088), a potent and reversible dual EGFR-HER2 exon 20 insertion inhibitor, against HER2 in diverse isogenic and endogenous HER2-mutant models in vitro. We also report the activity of sevabertinib in xenograft models expressing HER2 exon 20 insertions or amplified wild-type (WT) ERBB2. Finally, we report the response of patients with NSCLC harboring HER2 exon 20 insertions to treatment with sevabertinib as a part of a first-in-human, phase 1/2 clinical trial in patients with EGFR- or HER2-mutant NSCLC (NCT05099172).

## Results

## Sevabertinib Is a Highly Potent and Selective Inhibitor for HER2 Exon 20 Insertions
Sevabertinib was developed to potently and reversibly inhibit HER2 exon 20 insertions found in NSCLC, with highly selective activity against the HER2 exon 20 insertions relative to WT EGFR to minimize toxicity ( ) and additional selective activity toward other HER2 alterations and mutant EGFR ( ). Although biochemical selectivity was observed early in the development of sevabertinib, the potency of sevabertinib and other advanced molecules exceeded the detection limit of available biochemical assays. Therefore, the potency of sevabertinib at physiologically relevant ATP concentrations (2 mmol/L) against the WT HER2 and HER2 A775insYVMA kinase domains, as well as the WT EGFR and EGFR D770_N771insSVD kinase domains, were all determined to be <0.5 nmol/L ( ). Sevabertinib was also active against the HER4 kinase domain in the biochemical assay, albeit with significantly decreased activity (IC50 = 13.9 nmol/L), and with further decreased activity toward a subset of other kinases tested ( ; Supplementary Table S1). Due to the insufficient sensitivity of the biochemical assay, cellular assays were used for selectivity profiling during the lead optimization process, resulting in the discovery of sevabertinib (manuscript in preparation).
 A homology model of HER2 was built using an X-ray crystallographic structure of EGFR in complex with sevabertinib as a template as described in Supplementary Fig. S1A and S1B and Supplementary Table S2. The resulting three-dimensional (3D) structure highlights the key structural features responsible for ligand stabilization ( ). The backbone NH of Met801 in the hinge region anchors the ligand in the pocket by donating a hydrogen bond to the pyridine nitrogen. The lactam amide group forms hydrogen bonds with Lys753 and Asp863 of the DFG motif. Lys753, in addition, forms a salt bridge to Glu770 of helix C, a highly conserved interaction throughout the kinome which is indicative for the active conformation of the kinase domain. The aniline-free NH donates a hydrogen bond to the side chain of Thr862, leading the methoxy chlorophenyl fragment to the lipophilic subpocket beyond the gatekeeper (Thr798), whereas the dioxane ring masks the free pyrrole NH with an intramolecular hydrogen bond ( ). In contrast to irreversible inhibitors such as zongertinib ( ), no covalent interaction with Cys805 is present. The described interactions with HER2 make sevabertinib a highly efficacious reversible binder.
The off-target effects of sevabertinib were evaluated against a large panel of kinases with the KinaseProfiler platform (Eurofins), a radiometric activity–based assay platform directly measuring catalytic kinase activity. For enhanced assay sensitivity, kinase activity was measured in the presence of low ATP levels (10 μmol/L). In this assay, 1 μmol/L sevabertinib inhibited 18 of 373 tested kinases with more than 80% activity, including three EGFR kinase family members, EGFR, HER2, and HER4 (Supplementary Table S3; Supplementary Fig. S2). In the biochemical kinase activity assay with 2 mmol/L ATP, the most potently inhibited non-ERBB family kinase was DDR2, with an IC50 value of 53.3 nmol/L. This indicates greater than 100-fold selectivity of sevabertinib for WT HER2 versus DDR2, with even greater selectivity versus other non-ERBB family kinases tested (Supplementary Table S1).

## Sevabertinib Shows Potent Antiproliferative Activity in Isogenic Ba/F3 Cells Ectopically Expressing HER2 Exon 20 Insertions or HER2 Point Mutations
Using the Ba/F3 murine pro-B cell line normally dependent on Interleukin-3 (IL3) for survival and proliferation ( ), we generated a panel of isogenic Ba/F3 cells expressing either HER2 exon 20 insertions or activating HER2 point mutations ( ) that obviate the requirement for IL3 addition. These HER2-dependent Ba/F3 cell lines and similar EGFR-dependent Ba/F3 cell lines were also used to measure inhibitory activity during the compound optimization process.
 Sevabertinib strongly inhibited the proliferation of Ba/F3 cell lines that ectopically express HER2 exon 20 insertions or WT HER2 ( ). The antiproliferative activity of sevabertinib was comparable with the activity of the irreversible HER2 inhibitor, zongertinib, and comparable with or greater than the activity of the other FDA-approved small-molecule HER2 inhibitors tested, with about 20-fold selectivity for HER2 variants relative to WT EGFR in these cellular assays ( ; Supplementary Fig. S3A; Supplementary Table S4). STX-721, an irreversible EGFR/HER2 exon 20 insertion inhibitor ( ), showed similar activity as sevabertinib on the most common HER2 exon 20 insertion, A775insYVMA (Supplementary Fig. S3B). In addition to exon 20 insertions, recurring activating and oncogenic single-nucleotide missense mutations occur in the kinase domain, transmembrane domain, and extracellular domain of HER2 in lung and other cancers ( ; refs. , – ). Sevabertinib also inhibited proliferation in Ba/F3 cell lines expressing these HER2 missense mutations with high potency ( ), again comparing favorably with the activity of the other HER2 inhibitors tested ( ; Supplementary Fig. S3C; Supplementary Table S5; ref. ).
The antiproliferative activity of sevabertinib was also studied in isogenic Ba/F3 cell lines expressing putative secondary acquired resistance mutations of HER2, T798M, T798I, and C805S ( ), analogous to the acquired resistance mutations of EGFR, T790M, and C797S ( – ). Whereas the C805S mutation completely blocks the activity of irreversible HER2 inhibitors such as zongertinib (Supplementary Fig. S3D; ref. ), sevabertinib retains activity against Ba/F3 cell lines expressing HER2 exon 20 insertion mutations or point mutations in cis with C805S ( ). Sevabertinib also has significant activity against the putative secondary HER2 mutation, T798M ( ). Taken together, these results suggest that sevabertinib may be active against at least some of the predicted HER2 TKI resistance mutations in patients.

## Sevabertinib Inhibits Survival of Cancer Cell Lines Endogenously Harboring ERBB Family Alterations
In order to evaluate the activity of sevabertinib in cancer indications with genomic alterations other than ERBB2 exon 20 insertions, the antiproliferative effects of sevabertinib were further evaluated in 866 human cancer cell lines using the Profiling Relative Inhibition Simultaneously in Mixtures (PRISM) assay, a high-throughput multiplexed cancer cell line viability screen ( ). We first examined the relationship between sevabertinib response and sensitivity to ERBB family gene short hairpin RNA knockdown or CRISPR knockout using publicly available The Cancer Dependency Map Project (DepMap) data from the Broad Institute ( ). Not all cell lines in DepMap were profiled by both modalities, so we analyzed each modality separately. The three short hairpin RNA targets that best correlated with sevabertinib sensitivity were ERBB2, EGFR, and ERBB3 ( ), and the CRISPR targets that best correlated with sevabertinib sensitivity were ERBB2, MIEN1, GRHL2, EGFR, CAP1, STX4, and ERBB3 ( ). We then considered only the 23 cancer cell lines that respond best to sevabertinib ( ). These cell lines with a sensitivity Z-score of 2.5 or greater were selected for closer analysis. Although no cancer cell lines with HER2 exon 20 insertions were present in the PRISM screen cell line pool, strong responses to both sevabertinib and zongertinib were observed in several cell lines with ERBB2 amplifications ( ). There was also a strong correlation between sevabertinib sensitivity and ERBB2 mRNA expression, with a correlation coefficient of −0.32 and a q-value of 7.46 × 10-19 (Supplementary Fig. S4A). Surprisingly, no significant responses were seen in cell lines with HER2 point mutations ( and ). Keeping in mind that no cancer cell lines harboring HER2 exon 20 insertions are present in the PRISM cell line set, we found that several of the ERBB2-amplified cell lines also responded to knockout or knockdown of ERBB2 (Supplementary Fig. S4B and S4C). Unsurprisingly, no significant reduction in cell viability was seen upon ERBB2 knockout or knockdown in the EGFR-mutant cancer cell lines (Supplementary Fig. S4B and S4C). When considering all cell lines in the PRISM dataset together, we again found that a subset of ERBB2-amplified cancer cell lines was sensitive to sevabertinib or zongertinib ( ), overlapping with the ERBB2-amplified cancer cell lines that responded to ERBB2 knockout or knockdown (Supplementary Fig. S4D and S4E).
 The breast cancer cell line, MDA-MB-175, which endogenously harbors a DOC4–NRG1 (also known as TENM4–NRG1) fusion ( and ; ref. ), was also sensitive to both sevabertinib and zongertinib. The DOC4–NRG1 fusion results in constitutive membrane localization of NRG1, the ligand for HER3, a member of the HER protein family and preferred heterodimerization partner of HER2 ( ). Finally, among the cancer cell lines most responsive to sevabertinib, three harbored exon 19 deletions of EGFR ( and ), consistent with data indicating that sevabertinib is also potent and selective for EGFR classic mutations and exon 20 insertions and is unaffected by the C797S osimertinib resistance mutation (manuscript in preparation). Although the activities of sevabertinib and zongertinib were generally quite comparable in cancer cell lines with diverse HER2 alterations, zongertinib had no activity against the classic and exon 20 insertion EGFR mutations, as zongertinib is selective for HER2 versus EGFR ( ; ref. ).
No obvious genomic determinant of response was identified for 13 of the 23 most sensitive cell lines. Although one of these 13 remaining cell lines, the BT-16 cell line, was found to harbor a genomic fusion of MAK16 and NRG1, there was a predicted frameshift at the fusion site relative to the NRG1 coding sequence and the NRG1 EGF-like domain was furthermore excluded (Supplementary Fig. S5A). The number of RNA sequencing reads spanning the putative fusion breakpoint was low (Supplementary Fig. S5A), and no obvious noncanonical bands were detected on an anti-NRG1 immunoblot (Supplementary Fig. S5B). We therefore do not have any evidence to indicate that this MAK16–NRG1 gene fusion produces a functional fusion protein. We did observe elevated WT NRG1 expression in the BT16 cells and two other sevabertinib-sensitive cell lines lacking known sensitizing genomic alterations, TE617.T and DL (Supplementary Fig. S5B and S5C). We furthermore observed expression and phosphorylation of NRG1 receptors HER3 in the DL cells and HER4 in the BT-16 and TE617.T cells (Supplementary Fig. S5B). However, the physiologic relevance of these features with regards to sevabertinib response is not fully understood.

## Experimental Validation of Sevabertinib Activity in ERBB2-Dependent Cancer Models
We validated the effects of sevabertinib on cancer cell viability in a set of sensitive cancer cell lines endogenously expressing HER2 mutations or amplifications ( ). Sevabertinib strongly inhibited the proliferation of two lung cancer cell lines: NCI-H1781 cells harboring the HER2 exon 20 insertion mutation, A775insV,G776C (IC50 = 19.7 nmol/L), and NCI-H2170 cells harboring WT ERBB2 amplification (IC50 = 16.7 nmol/L). A similar response was observed in cells treated with the investigational HER2 inhibitor, zongertinib ( ). We also validated the activity of sevabertinib against the breast cancer cell line, MDA-MB-175-VII, which endogenously harbors the DOC4–NRG1 fusion ( ; ref. ). A431 cells are dependent on the activity of highly expressed endogenous WT EGFR for survival and were significantly less sensitive to sevabertinib, consistent with decreased activity of sevabertinib on WT EGFR. ( ). We additionally compared the activity of sevabertinib with the HER2 inhibitors, tucatinib, lapatinib, and neratinib, on three ERBB2-amplified cancer cell lines, HCC1419 (breast cancer), NCI-H2170 (lung cancer), and NCI-N87 (gastric cancer). In these three cell lines, the potency of sevabertinib was comparable with that of neratinib, and sevabertinib was more active than tucatinib or lapatinib (Supplementary Fig. S6A–S6D).
 We then investigated the inhibitory effects of sevabertinib on HER2 phosphorylation and the downstream MAPK signaling pathway in cancer cell lines. Treatment with sevabertinib resulted in decreased levels of phospho-HER2, phospho-MEK, and phospho-ERK in NCI-H1781 lung cancer cells with a HER2 exon 20 insertion, MDA-MB-175-VII breast cancer cells with an NRG1 fusion, NCI-H2170 lung cancer cells with amplification of WT ERBB2, and NCI-N87 gastric cancer cells with amplification of WT ERBB2. These data indicate inhibition of the HER2–MAPK pathway signaling by sevabertinib, as evidenced by dose-dependent decreases in levels of phospho-AKT, phospho-MEK, and phospho-ERK in these HER2-dependent cell lines ( ). Phosphorylation of HER3, an ERBB family protein that has no intrinsic kinase activity but can be phosphorylated by HER2 ( ), is also inhibited by sevabertinib in MDA-MB-175-VII and NCI-N87 cells ( ).
The cell lines with HER2 alterations did not express detectable levels of EGFR protein; the phosphorylated band in the NCI-2170 and NCI-N87 phospho-EGFR immunoblots is likely due to cross reaction of the phospho-EGFR antibody with phospho-HER2, which is larger and migrates more slowly by SDS-PAGE than phospho-EGFR. Inhibition of phospho-EGFR, phospho-MEK, or phospho-ERK was observed only at the highest concentrations of sevabertinib in the A431 cells overexpressing WT EGFR, consistent with decreased activity of sevabertinib on WT EGFR ( ).
Because we observed response to sevabertinib in a subset of cancer cell lines harboring amplifications of ERBB2 (P < 0.001, ), we analyzed the correlation of response and ERBB2 amplification in cell lines from breast cancers and gastroesophageal cancers, both characterized by recurring ERBB2 amplifications ( ). Response of breast and gastroesophageal cancer cell lines to sevabertinib was correlated with the presence of high-level, focal amplifications of ERBB2 (P < 0.001, ), and phosphorylation of HER2 and downstream signaling proteins is efficiently inhibited by sevabertinib in the gastric cancer cell line, NCI-N87, harboring an amplification of WT ERBB2 ( , Supplementary Fig. S6C). These results indicate that sevabertinib may also have activity in patients whose cancers harbor ERBB2 amplifications.

## Sevabertinib Shows Robust Antitumor Efficacy in Xenograft Models Expressing HER2 Exon 20 Insertions or Amplified WT ERBB2
After demonstrating the robust antiproliferative activity of sevabertinib on HER2-mutant cells in vitro, we investigated the antitumor efficacy of sevabertinib in NSCLC xenograft models harboring a HER2 exon 20 insertion. In the subcutaneous CTG-2543 patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model of NSCLC (HER2 exon 20 insertion A775insYVMA), sevabertinib inhibited tumor growth ( ; Supplementary Fig. S7A–S7D) and reduced tumor weight ( ) when dosed once daily at 10, 25, and 50 mg/kg when compared with vehicle. Treatment with sevabertinib at 10, 25, or 50 mg/kg resulted in treatment/control ratios of 0.22, 0.12, and 0.06, respectively ( ). In all mice at all dosing levels, sevabertinib reduced tumor volume, as demonstrated by the relative change in tumor volume at the end of the study (Supplementary Fig. S7E). Tumor response and treatment responses were defined using the modified RECIST criteria ( ). In the NCI-H1781 model harboring the HER2 exon 20 insertion A775insV,G776C, sevabertinib at 5 mg/kg twice daily or 10 mg/kg once daily demonstrated antitumor efficacy at both doses ( ). Similar antitumor responses were obtained using zongertinib at 15 mg/kg twice daily or 30 mg/kg once daily ( ). Given the in vitro cellular data suggesting an effect of sevabertinib on ERBB2-amplified cancers, we also tested an ERBB2-amplified xenograft model. In the NCI-N87 model harboring WT ERBB2 amplification, sevabertinib dosed at 2.5, 5, or 10 mg/kg (twice daily) inhibited tumor growth, showing robust in vivo antitumor efficacy comparable with T-DXd at 3 mg/kg ( ). In all three xenograft models, sevabertinib was found to be well tolerated, as evidenced by the lack of marked body weight loss (Supplementary Fig. S8A–S8C).
 

## Sevabertinib Shows Antitumor Activity in Two Patients with HER2 Exon 20 Insertion–Mutant NSCLC
Promising clinical activity has been observed in an ongoing first-in-human study of sevabertinib in patients with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 mutations (NCT05099172). Here, we present two case studies of patients with HER2 exon 20 insertion–mutant NSCLC who were treated with 20 mg sevabertinib twice daily ( and ).
 A 51-year-old female patient [Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) 0] with metastatic HER2 A775insYVMA NSCLC received two lines of systemic treatments before entering the trial: first-line pemetrexed and carboplatin for 4 months, and second-line docetaxel for 2 months. The patient achieved partial response (PR) with 41%, 52%, and 53% tumor shrinkage after 6, 18, and 30 weeks of treatment with sevabertinib, respectively ( ). Although nontarget lesion brain metastases were present and stable during the first 30 weeks of treatment, enlargement of the brain metastases after 30 weeks of study treatment resulted in designation as progressive disease. In total, the patient received 10 cycles of treatment for 219 days without permanent dose reduction.
A 55-year-old male patient (ECOG PS 1) with metastatic HER2 A775insYVMA–mutant NSCLC received pemetrexed and carboplatin as the first-line treatment for approximately 6 months. After entering the trial, the patient achieved PR with 54% and 78% tumor shrinkage after 6 and 54 weeks of treatment, respectively ( ), until progressive disease occurred after 80 weeks of treatment. In total, the patient received 28 cycles of treatment for 602 days without permanent dose reduction. No brain metastases were observed in this patient.

## Discussion
Lung cancer patients with tumors harboring HER2 exon 20 insertions currently have a poor prognosis and only limited options for targeted therapy ( ). Although T-DXd and zongertinib have received accelerated approval for the treatment of patients with NSCLC with HER2 exon 20 insertions ( ), there is still a need for active and well-tolerated oral therapies for these patients. Importantly, therapies for patients with HER2 exon 20 insertion NSCLC must have decreased activity on the related EGFR tyrosine kinase receptor to minimize EGFR-driven toxicity ( ). Sevabertinib is an oral and reversible inhibitor of HER2 exon 20 insertions, with about 20-fold selectivity versus WT EGFR in an isogenic Ba/F3 cell assay. Activity and selectivity of sevabertinib was demonstrated in Ba/F3 and cancer cell line survival assays, cancer cell line mechanistic assays examining signaling protein phosphorylation, and in vivo with a HER2 A775insYVMA PDX model and cell line–derived xenograft models. We also presented data demonstrating antitumor activity in two patients with lung cancer treated with sevabertinib as part of a phase 1/2 trial (NCT05099172), resulting in two PRs.
Beyond HER2 exon 20 insertions in lung cancer, we also observed activity of sevabertinib on a subset of ERBB2-amplified breast, gastric, and esophageal cell lines. This finding implies that sevabertinib could have activity in patients with these cancers as well, with the caveat of expected limited response of gastroesophageal cancer to HER2-targeted agents due to tumor heterogeneity and co-occurring mutations ( , ). We also observed activity of sevabertinib against activating and oncogenic point mutations of HER2 in the isogenic Ba/F3 system, but interestingly we did not identify sensitive cancer cell lines endogenously harboring a similar set of activating HER2 point mutations. This implies that cancer cell lines with endogenous HER2 missense mutations may have other oncogenic alterations that support cancer cell survival and proliferation independently of mutant HER2 activity or may have growth properties that mask the impact of HER2 inactivation. Conversely, we did observe activity of sevabertinib toward a breast cancer cell line endogenously harboring an oncogenic translocation of NRG1, the ligand of the ERBB family member and HER2 heterodimerization partner, HER3 ( ), implying that sevabertinib may show activity in these rare patients as well. Sevabertinib also has activity against cancer cell lines with classic activating mutations and exon 20 insertions of EGFR (manuscript in preparation). Finally, we did not detect relevant genomic alterations in 13 of the 23 cell lines most responsive to sevabertinib, raising the question of why these 13 cell lines are sensitive to sevabertinib. It is possible that epigenetic alterations instead are responsible for sensitivity, but further investigation will be required to address this question.
In addition to sevabertinib, zongertinib is also under clinical investigation for efficacy in patients with lung cancer with HER2 mutations ( ). Both molecules have similar preclinical efficacy in cancer cell lines with ERBB alterations. The primary difference between these two inhibitors is that sevabertinib is reversible and zongertinib is irreversible, covalently modifying C805 in HER2 ( ). Mutations of C805 will thus abrogate zongertinib activity ( ), whereas sevabertinib is unaffected by mutations of C805. The first phase 1 clinical data for zongertinib have now been reported, with ORRs of up to 71% in patients with NSCLC with mutations in the HER2 tyrosine kinase domain ( ). Interestingly, some responses in NSCLC patients with HER2 mutations outside the tyrosine kinase domain were also observed ( ). Because the toxicity profiles of EGFR inhibition and HER2 inhibition are thought to be partly overlapping ( , ), it is difficult to predict whether there will be a difference between the safety profiles of sevabertinib and zongertinib, as both inhibit WT HER2. Sevabertinib and zongertinib are both in phase 3 clinical trials; further differentiation between them awaits these clinical trial results.
In summary, we have demonstrated the preclinical activity of sevabertinib in cellular and in vivo models of NSCLC with HER2 exon 20 insertions. This activity extends to a subset of cancer cell lines harboring focal amplifications of ERBB2. We furthermore provide evidence for response of two patients with lung cancer with HER2 exon 20 insertions to sevabertinib. The phase 1 trial of sevabertinib in patients with NSCLC with HER2 mutations, including exon 20 insertions, is ongoing (NCT05099172). The phase 3 trial of sevabertinib in NSCLC (NCT06452277) and phase 2 open-label basket trial of sevabertinib in HER2-mutated solid tumors (NCT06760819) are now enrolling.

## Methods

## Compounds
Sevabertinib [3-(3-chloro-2-methoxyanilino)-2-(3-{[(2S)-1,4-dioxan-2-yl] methoxy} pyridin-4-yl)-1,5,6,7-tetrahydro-4H-pyrrolo[3,2-c] pyridin-4-one] was synthesized at Bayer AG. EGFR/HER2 TKIs tucatinib, lapatinib, neratinib, and zongertinib were purchased from Selleck Chemicals, and STX-721 from MedChemExpress.

## Biochemical Kinase Activity Inhibition Assay
The inhibitory activity of sevabertinib against WT EGFR, EGFR mutations, or WT HER2 was quantified using a time-resolved fluorescence energy transfer–based assay. Recombinant fusion proteins of N-terminal glutathione-S-transferase and a fragment of human EGFR or HER2 with or without respective mutations/insertions were produced for kinase reaction. These recombinant fusion proteins were expressed in Sf9 insect cells and purified via affinity chromatography using glutathione sepharose. Biotinylated peptide biotin-Ahx-AEEEEYFELVAKKK with C-terminus in amide form (Biosyntan) was used as a substrate for the reaction. Next, 50 nL of sevabertinib (100× concentration stock) and 2 μL of recombinant fusion protein in aqueous assay buffer [50 mmol/L Hepes, pH 7.0, 10 mmol/L MgCl2, 1 mmol/L dithiothreitol, 0.5 mmol/L EGTA, 0.3 mmol/L activated sodium orthovanadate, 0.005% (w/v) BSA, and 0.005% (v/v) Tween-20] were added onto a 384-well microtiter plate. The mixture was incubated at 22°C for 15 minutes to allow prebinding of sevabertinib to the enzyme before starting the kinase reaction. The kinase reaction was started by the addition of 3 μL of a solution of ATP (final concentration 2 mmol/L) and substrate (final concentration 1 μmol/L) in assay buffer to the wells and incubated at 22°C for 30 minutes. The concentration of EGFR/HER2 was adjusted depending on the activity of the enzyme lot. The reaction was stopped by the addition of 3 μL of a solution of HTRF detection reagents, consisting of 83.3 nmol/L streptavidin-XL665 (Cisbio Bioassays) and 1.67 nmol/L PT66-Tb-cryptate, a terbium cryptate–labeled anti-phosphotyrosine antibody (Cisbio Bioassays) in an aqueous EDTA solution [133.3 mmol/L EDTA and 0.2% (w/v) BSA in 50 mmol/L HEPES, pH 7.5]. The fluorescence readout was done using a HTRF reader, PHERAstar (BMG Labtechnologies), or a ViewLux (PerkinElmer). The data were normalized as described: reaction without inhibitor = 0% inhibition; all other assay components but no enzyme = 100% inhibition. IC50 values were calculated using Genedata Screener software (RRID: SCR_022506, Genedata).

## X-ray Modeling
A model of human HER2 was built using an internal X-ray of EGFR WT bound to the ligand (described in Supplementary Information) using the homology modeling tool Maestro (RRID: SCR_016748, Maestro, Schrödinger, LLC; energy-based model). 3D pictures of the model were made using PyMOL (RRID: SCR_000305, The PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, Version 3.0 Schrödinger, LLC).

## KinaseProfiler Panel Screen
Kinase activity screening of 373 kinases was conducted using the KinaseProfiler platform (Eurofins Scientific), a radiometric activity-based assay platform directly measuring the catalytic kinase activity. The assay was performed in the presence of 10 μmol/L ATP and 1 μmol/L sevabertinib according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The results were presented as a percentage of remaining activity after sevabertinib treatment: 100%: full activity; <0%: full inhibition.

## Generation of Isogenic HER2-Mutant Ba/F3 Cell Lines
The murine Ba/F3 cell line (RRID: CVCL_0161) was purchased from Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen–German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH. To generate Ba/F3 cells expressing various HER2 mutations, the cells were grown in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 10 ng/mL IL3 and infected with filtered retroviral supernatant at a 1:1 dilution. Next, polybrene was added to a concentration of 8 μg/mL, and plates were spun for 90 minutes and incubated overnight at 37°C. Twenty-four hours after infection, 2 μg/mL puromycin was added, and the infected cells were grown in the presence of puromycin and 10 ng/mL IL3 for at least 3 days. Afterward, Ba/F3 cell lines were expanded for 8 days without the presence of IL3 and then frozen as single-use aliquots for in vitro proliferation assays.

## 
In Vitro Antiproliferation Assay
The antiproliferative activity of sevabertinib and other EGFR/HER2 TKIs was determined in the newly generated HER2-mutant Ba/F3 and human cancer cell lines using CellTiter-Glo Luminescent Cell Viability Assay (Promega). The following human cancer cell lines endogenously expressing HER2 amplification/mutations were used: A431 epidermoid carcinoma (HER2 WT, EGFR amplified; RRID: CVCL_0037), NCI-H2170 lung cancer (HER2 WT amplified; RRID: CVCL_1535), NCI-H1781 lung cancer (HER2 A775insV, G776C; RRID: CVCL_1494), MDA-MB-175-VII breast cancer (NRG1 fusion; RRID: CVCL_1400), HCC1419 breast cancer; RRID: CVCL_1251, and NCI-N87 gastric cancer (HER2 WT amplified; RRID: CVCL_1603). The cell lines were obtained from the ATCC. BT-16 teratoid/rhabdoid tumor (RRID: CVCL_M156), TE 617.T rhabdomyosarcoma (RRID: CVCL_1755), and DL rhabdoid tumor (RRID: CVCL_U760) were obtained from PRISM platform at the Broad Institute. For each purchased cell line, a central master cell stock was generated, and freshly thawed, low-passage (<10) cell aliquots were used for experiments. Authentication of cell lines was done via short tandem repeat DNA typing at Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (DSMZ). Mycoplasma testing was performed every 6 months using the MycoAlert Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza).

## Immunoblotting
Cells were washed with PBS and resuspended in lysis buffer containing 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl, 2% w/v SDS, 10% glycerol, and 1× Halt dual phosphatase and protease inhibitor (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Lysates were then sonicated at 20% amplitude for 20 seconds. Protein concentration was measured using a bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit (Pierce). After adding the reducing 4× Laemmli SDS-Sample buffer (Boston BioProducts), samples were boiled for 5 minutes and separated by SDS-PAGE using Novex 8% to 16% Tris-Glycine mini gels (Invitrogen). Proteins were then transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Immobilon-FL, Millipore Sigma, Merck) using a wet-tank transfer system. Afterward, the membranes were blocked for 1 hour at room temperature with Intercept blocking buffer (LICORbio) and then incubated with primary antibodies diluted in the blocking buffer at 4°C, overnight. Antibodies against phospho-HER2 (Y1221/1222, #2243, RRID: AB_490899, 1:1,000), HER2 (#2242, RRID: AB_331015, 1:1,000), phospho-EGFR (Y1068, #2236, RRID: AB_331792, 1:1,000), EGFR (#4267, RRID: AB_2895042, 1:1,000), HER3 (1B2E, #4754, RRID: AB_10691324, 1:1,000; D22C5, #12708, RRID: AB_2721919, 1:1,000), phospho-HER3 (Y1289, #4791, RRID: AB_2099709, 1:1,000), HER4 (111B2, #4795, RRID: AB_2099883, 1:1,000), phospho-HER4 (Y1284, #4757, RRID: AB_2099987, 1:1,000), AKT (#2920, RRID: AB_1147620, 1:1,000), phospho-AKT (S474, RRID: AB_331168, #4058), phospho-MEK 1/2 (S217/221, #9154, RRID: AB_2138017, 1:1,000), MEK 1/2 (#4694, RRID: AB_10695868, 1:1,000), phospho-Erk 1/2 (T202/Y204, #9101, RRID: AB_331646, 1:1,000), Erk 1/2 (#9107, RRID: AB_10695739, 1:1,000), NRG1 (#2573, RRID: AB_1031011, 1:1,000), and vinculin (rabbit mAb, #13901, RRID: AB_2728768, 1:2,000) were obtained from Cell Signaling Technology. Mouse mAb vinculin antibody (#V9264, RRID: AB_10603627, 1:2,000) was obtained from Millipore Sigma. After 3× washing with TBS with 0.1% Tween buffer, the membranes were incubated with IRDye secondary antibodies (LICORbio) for 1 hour at room temperature. ## PRISM Cell Line Screen
The PRISM platform was developed by the Broad Institute of Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Harvard. A total of 866 human cancer cell lines were seeded in pools of between 20 and 25 adherent cell lines at 1,250 cells per well and suspension pools at 2,000 cells per well. The cells were treated on 384-well plates as triplicates with eight-point dose dilutions ranging from 3 μmol/L to 1.372 nmol/L with 3-fold dilutions for 5 days. At the end of incubation, cells were lysed, and mRNA was isolated. Barcode sequences were amplified by PCR and quantified using a Luminex FlexMap 3D scanner (RRID: SCR_026299, Luminex Corporation). Relative barcode abundance (compared with vehicle control) was then used to generate cell line sensitivity profiles for each compound and concentration tested.

## 
In Vivo PDX Study
The in vivo antitumor efficacy of sevabertinib was investigated in three subcutaneous xenograft models with HER2 alterations: Champions TumorGraft CTG-2543 low-passage PDX NSCLC model (HER2 A775insYVMA), NCI-H1781 NSCLC model (HER2 A775insV, G776C), and NCI-N87 gastric cancer model (WT ERBB2 amp). In brief, mice were subcutaneously inoculated with cancer cells or implanted with tumor fragments. Once tumor growth was established, mice were allocated to vehicle and treatment groups (9–10 mice/group) and treated orally with vehicle, sevabertinib (2.5–50 mg/kg), or zongertinib (15–30 mg/kg) once daily or twice daily or intraperitonially with an injection of T-DXd (3 mg/kg) every 3 weeks. Vehicle for sevabertinib in all xenograft models was 10% ethanol and 40% Solutol HS-15 in sterile water, pH 4.0. Body weight and tumor size were determined 2 to 3 times weekly during the study. Tumor growth was monitored twice a week using digital calipers, and the tumor volume was calculated using the formula [0.52 × (length × width2)]. All experimental procedures were performed according to the national animal welfare laws of Germany and guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and approved by local authorities. Detailed information for each model is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

## Clinical Phase 1/2 Study Design
NCT05099172 was an open-label, multicenter, first-in-human study to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of sevabertinib in patients 18 years or older with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 mutations. Patients were required to be relapsed/refractory to one or more systemic therapies.

## Patients and Clinical Data
Patients participated in an open-label, multicenter, first-in-human study (NCT05099172), which evaluates the safety, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of sevabertinib. Eligible patients were 18 years or older with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 activating mutations, relapsed/refractory to one or more systemic therapies. ECOG PS for each patient was defined. The clinical study was approved by the appropriate institutional review boards at each participating site and conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent. Assessment of therapeutic activity was based on CT scans.

## Statistical Analyses
Cell line information, including mutation, gene dependency, gene fusion, and segment-level copy number data were obtained from DepMap (RRID: SCR_017655, DepMap B. DepMap 24Q2 Public (Internet). Figshare+; 2024 (cited 2024 Dec 9). Available at https://doi.org/10.25452/figshare.plus.25880521.v1; ref. ). Gene dependency scores ≤ −1 were considered strong gene dependencies; scores ≤ −0.5 were considered weak gene dependencies. Gene fusions containing at least 1 mitochondrial gene were excluded from analysis. Focal copy number amplifications were calculated using GISTIC (version 2.0, RRID: SCR_000151) via GenePattern (RRID: SCR_003201; ref. ). Amplification scores ≥4 were considered high focal gene amplifications. The cell lines most sensitive to sevabertinib were determined via a mean-centered Z-score of the area over the curve, with a Z-score >2.5 considered sensitive. Correlations between sevabertinib response with ERBB2 amplification for all cell lines and a subset of breast and gastroesophageal cell lines were calculated via Mann–Whitney U test. Statistical analyses for in vivo model (tumor growth and final tumor weights) were performed using one-way ANOVA with Tukey multiple comparison analysis using SAS software (version 9.3; RRID: SCR_008567; SAS Institute). Adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.
