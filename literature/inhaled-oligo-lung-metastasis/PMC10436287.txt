Keywords: Osteosarcoma, SATB2-AS1, SATB2, Metastasis, Prognosis

## Abstract
Our previous report has identified a lncRNA SATB2-AS1, which was significantly up-regulated in osteosarcoma tissue and promotes the proliferation of osteosarcoma cells in vitro. However, the mechanisms of SATB2-AS1 regulating the growth and metastasis of osteosarcoma cells in vivo and its role in the prognosis of osteosarcoma patients are still unclear. In this study, the transcriptome sequencing data of 87 patients with osteosarcoma from the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET) database and 7 patients from our clinical center (GZFPH) was used to evaluate the importance of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 on the prognosis. The effect of SATB2-AS1 on the growth and metastasis of osteosarcoma cells in vivo was verified by a mouse tumor model. The potential mechanisms of SATB2-AS1 regulating SATB2 were further explored by dual-luciferase reporter gene assay, RNA pull-down assay, and bioinformatics analysis. The results suggested that increased co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was significantly associated with poor overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS), and was a biomarker for risk stratification in patients with osteosarcoma. Mechanistically, SATB2-AS1 promotes tumor growth and lung metastasis by regulating SATB2 in vivo. SATB2-AS1 directly binds to POU3F1 for mediating SATB2 expression in MNNG/HOS cells. In addition, SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 might be potential immunomodulators for negatively affecting immune cell infiltration by the IL-17 signaling pathway. In summary, SATB2-AS1 promoted tumor cell growth and lung metastasis by activating SATB2, thereby associated with poor prognosis in patients with osteosarcoma, which indicated that SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 might be novel biomarkers for risk stratification and promising therapeutic targets for osteosarcoma.

## Introduction
Osteosarcoma is the most common primary malignant bone tumor in adolescents and young people, which usually occurs in the epiphysis of long bones of extremities , , . Osteosarcoma is highly aggressive with a metastasis rate of about 20%. The most common sites of metastasis are the lungs and other bones. As the disease progresses, the cause of death is mostly distant metastases, especially lung metastases, with 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of<70% , . The treatment of osteosarcoma is a combination of chemotherapy and surgery as central components, and despite the important advances made in multimodal treatment (introduction of limb-preserving surgery, neoadjuvant and adjuvant chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy); however, prognosis has not improved greatly for patients with metastatic and recurrent osteosarcoma , , . Therefore, identifying novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets that affect the metastasis and prognosis of osteosarcoma is of great importance for the risk stratification and treatment of osteosarcoma.
Long-stranded non-coding RNA (lncRNA) is a class of RNA molecules with transcripts longer than 200 nt, which do not encode proteins or only encode very short polypeptides , . Recent studies have shown that lncRNAs are involved in a variety of important signaling regulatory processes such as epigenetic modification, transcriptional activation, and regulation of protein function , , and play a critical regulatory role in biological processes such as tumor growth, development, apoptosis, and metastasis , . Dysregulation of lncRNA expression has been demonstrated in a variety of tumors; thus, its roles in physiological and disease processes are gradually attracting widespread attention , , . Our previous report suggested that high expression of lncRNA SATB2-AS1 (special AT-rich sequence binding protein 2 antisense RNA 1) promoted the proliferation of osteosarcoma cells in vitro
 . Moreover, upregulated SATB2-AS1 predicts the recurrence of patients with osteosarcoma . These findings indicate that SATB2-AS1 plays a major role in the development of osteosarcoma. Therefore, further exploring the relationship of lncRNA SATB2-AS1 vs. prognosis and the mechanisms of its regulation of cell proliferation and metastasis was critical for risk stratification, development of new treatment options, improving the therapeutic effect, and reducing recurrence for osteosarcoma.
In this study, we analyzed the transcriptome sequencing data from 87 patients with osteosarcoma in the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET) database and 7 patients in our clinical center (GZFPH), and we found that increased co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was associated with poor OS and relapse-free survival (RFS). SATB2-AS1 could bond to SATB2 through dual-luciferase reporter gene assay and further verified its ability for promoting the growth and metastasis of osteosarcoma cells through animal experiments. Furthermore, the signaling pathways and potential mechanisms involved in the role of SATB2-AS1 and its target protein SATB2 were explored.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Osteosarcoma Patients’ samples
Osteosarcoma tissue samples from 7 newly diagnosed patients were obtained through tissue biopsy. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangzhou First People's Hospital. All participants were provided with written informed consent.

2.2.

## TARGET dataset
Transcriptome sequencing data of 87 de novo patients with osteosarcoma in the TARGET dataset were downloaded from the UCSC-XENA platform (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). Related clinical characteristics, including age, gender, primary tumor site, metastasis, OS, and RFS were listed in . The TARGET dataset was publicly available and therefore no approval from the local ethics committee was required.

2.3.

## Microarray and data analysis
A total of 7 osteosarcoma tissue samples were used for total RNA extraction, cDNA library construction, and sequencing in KangChen Biotech Company, China with the Human LncRNA Array v3.0 (8 × 60 K, Arraystar) and the Agilent Scanner G2505C scanned the arrays. Agilent Feature Extraction software (version 11.0.1.1) was used to analyze acquired array images. Quantile normalization and subsequent data processing were performed using the GeneSpring GX v11.5.1 software package (Agilent Technologies). Detailed information has been described in our previous publication .

2.4.

## Cell culture
A human osteoblast-like cell line, MNNG/HOS cells , , obtained from Cell Bank of the China Science Academy (Shanghai, China), and incubated in DMEM medium (Sigma, USA) containing 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C in an incubator with 5% CO2. All cells used in the experiments were at the exponential growth phase. MNNG/HOS cells are transformed from HOS by 0.01mcg/ml MNNG (a carcinogenic Nitrosamine).

2.5.

## Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)
The expression level of genes was detected using SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, China), and GAPDH was used as an internal control. qRT-PCR was performed on an ABI7500 real-time PCR detection system (Applied Biosystems). The signal was normalized to the internal control and expressed as 2-ΔΔct. The primer sequences were listed in .

2.6.

## Plasmid constructs, virus production, and established stable cells
Stable MNNG/HOS cells with knockdown of SATB2-AS1 and two types of stable MNNG/HOS cells with overexpression of SATB2-AS1 (including SATB2-AS1-OV-1 and SATB2-AS1-OV-2) were established and purchased from Shanghai Gene Chem Co Ltd, China. Detailed information has been described in our previous publication .

2.7.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
SATB2 3′ UTR was fused with the luciferase reporter gene in the psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega). The vector was transfected into cells overexpressing SATB2-AS1 or into negative control cells. After 48 h, levels of luciferase activity were detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega) and normalized with the Renilla values. Values are presented as the ratio of firefly/Renilla values.

2.8.

## siRNA interference
MNNG/HOS cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Cat.No. 11668019). The concentration of siRNA was 50 nM, and Opti-MEM (Invitrogen, Cat. No.31985070) medium was used for transfection. A total of 500 ul culture medium was used in a 24 well plate. 1.25 ul of siRNA mother solution (20 uM) was dissolved in 100 ul of Opti-MEM culture medium as solution A. 1ul of Lipofectamine 2000 was dissolved in Opti-MEM culture medium as solution B. After mixing solution B for 5 min, solution A and B were mixed. For 20 min, they were added to the cell culture plate, and then change to cell growth medium after incubation for 4 h. Finally, samples were harvested at 24 to 36 h for qRT-PCR detection of POU3F1, SATB2-AS1, and SATB2 expression levels. siRNA sequences targeting POU3F1 was listed in .

2.9.

## Tumorigenicity
To detect tumorigenicity, we injected cells into mice subcutaneously or via tail vein to establish subcutaneous tumor or metastatic tumor models, respectively. Female BALB/c nude mice at the age of 6–8 weeks were purchased from Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co, Ltd (Beijing, China). Mice were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions in the animal facilities of the South China University of Technology. For the subcutaneous tumor model mice were randomly divided into four groups, each group including 4 or 5 mice. MNNG/HOS cells (1 × 107 in 0.2 ml of PBS/mouse) were subcutaneously inoculated into the right flank of each nude mouse included in the study. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula: (L × W2)/2, with length (L) and width (W), measured every 2 days. Thirty days after implantation, mice were imaged in vivo and a final volume measurement of the subcutaneous tumors was performed. Finally, mice were euthanized by inhalation of carbon dioxide followed by cervical dislocation, and tumors were stripped intact for comparative photography. For the metastatic tumor model, negative control and SATB2-AS1 overexpressing MNNG/HOS cells (5 × 106 in 0.2 ml of PBS/mouse) were injected into the mice via the tail vein. Thirty days after implantation, mice were euthanized by inhalation of carbon dioxide followed by cervical dislocation. The lungs of the mice were then freed and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. All animal experiments conformed to our animal protocols approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the South China University of Technology.

2.10.

## Histological analysis
Lung tissues were harvested from the metastatic tumor model. After fixation and dehydration, they were paraffin-embedded and cut into 2 μm sections. The tissue sections were then dewaxed, hydrated, and subjected to Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) staining according to the HE staining kit (Solarbio, China).

2.11.

## RNA Pull-Down assay
SATB2-AS1 sequences were transcribed and labeled in vitro with Biotin RNA Labeling Mix and T7 RNA polymerase (Roche, Switzerland) and purified with the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Biotin-labeled SATB2-AS1 was then incubated with cell protein lysates after which the enriched proteins were purified and proteins bound to SATB2-AS1 were identified by mass spectrometry (MS) analysis.

2.12.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 22.0 (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA) and R (version 4.2.1, https://www.r-project.org/), as appropriate. Differences between the two and three groups of quantitative data were compared by Mann-Whitney-Wilcoxon and Kruskal-Wallis tests, respectively. The optimal cut points were obtained by the “maxstat” package or X-tile software (version 3.6.1), as appropriate . Differences in Kaplan-Meier curves were compared using the log-rank test in the R package “survival”. The 5-year restricted mean survival time (RMST) was determined by the R package “survRM2”. Uni- and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify independent prognostic factors. The nomogram model was constructed by “foreign” and “rms” R packages. The signaling pathways were analyzed by Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) in the R packages “org.Hs.eg.db”, “clusterProfiler”, and “enrichplot”. A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

3.

## Results
3.1.

## Increased Co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 predicted poor outcomes in osteosarcoma
Kaplan- Meier curve was a survival curve that was usually used in the analysis of gene expression levels with patient outcomes , , , . Therefore, to investigate the expression level of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 vs outcomes, we first performed the prognostic analysis using Kaplan- Meier curves, and the statistical difference was determined by the log-rank test. According to the optimal cut-point, patients with osteosarcoma were divided into low and high-expression subgroups, and high expression of SATB2-AS1 was associated with poor OS and shorter 5-year RMST in the TARGET dataset [P < 0.001; hazard ratio (HR) = 3.34; 95% confidence interval (CI):1.56–7.13] ( , A). These results were confirmed in RFS (P < 0.001; HR = 4.08; 95% CI: 2.18–7.64) ( B). In addition, high expression of SATB2 predicted poor OS and shorter 5-year RMST of patients with osteosarcoma (P < 0.001; HR = 4.53; 95% CI: 2.06 – 9.95) ( C). These results were again confirmed in RFS (P = 0.001; HR = 2.91; 95% CI: 1.47 – 5.77) ( D). Interestingly, SATB2-AS1 was positively correlated with SATB2 in patients with osteosarcoma in the TARGET dataset (R = 0.83, P < 0.001). This finding was also shown in the GZFPH dataset (R = 0.86, P = 0.024) ( A). Moreover, SATB2-AS1 binding to SATB2 was confirmed by dual-luciferase reporter gene assay in MNNG/HOS cells (P = 0.028, B). Importantly, the combination of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was used to predict clinical outcomes and found that osteosarcoma patients with SATB2-AS1high/SATB2high predicted poor OS and shorter 5-year RMST (P < 0.001; HR = 2.52; 95% CI: 1.60–3.98) ( C). This result was also indicated in RFS (P < 0.001; HR = 2.42; 95% CI: 1.65–3.54) ( D). Notably, SATB2-AS1high/SATB2high was an independent predictor for OS (P < 0.001; HR = 17.46; 95% CI: 5.90–51.63) and RFS (P < 0.001; HR = 9.47; 95% CI: 3.94–22.76) ( ). Therefore, increased co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was associated with poor OS and RFS in osteosarcoma.
 
3.2.

## Risk stratification for patients with osteosarcoma
Risk stratification can provide an important reference for clinicians to manage cancer patients and determine treatment options. Given this idea, we tried to combine SATB2-AS1/SATB2 and clinical prognostic factors to construct a risk stratification for patients with osteosarcoma. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis was used to select prognostic factors in the TARGET dataset. Primary tumor site, gender, and metastasis were independent OS predictors for patients with osteosarcoma besides SATB2-AS1/SATB2, which was then used to construct a nomogram model to predict 1-, 2-, 3-, and 5-year OS for osteosarcoma ( and A). And the nomogram model had a good performance in predicting OS in patients with osteosarcoma (C-index: 0.83, P < 0.001) ( A). Patients with osteosarcoma were divided into low-, intermediate-, and high-risk subgroups according to the optimal cut-points of total risk score (54 and 82) derived from the nomogram model in the TARGET dataset ( ). Compared with the low-risk score, the high-risk score was significantly associated with poor OS for osteosarcoma (P < 0.001; HR = 7.17; 95% CI: 3.76 – 13.67). This finding was again confirmed in RFS (P < 0.001; HR = 4.12; 95% CI: 2.61–6.50) ( B). Taken together, SATB2-AS1/SATB2 were potential biomarkers for risk stratification in patients with osteosarcoma.
 
3.3.

## SATB2-AS1 promotes the proliferation and metastasis of osteosarcoma in vivo
Our previous publication demonstrated SATB2-AS1 regulates cell cycle, cell proliferation, and cell growth, and SATB2-AS1 affects the translational expression of SATB2 using two osteosarcoma cell lines (including MNNG/HOS and U2OS) in vitro
 . However, when three osteosarcoma cell lines (MG63, MNNG/HOS, and U2OS cells) were used for in vivo subcutaneous tumor formation experiments, we found that only MNNG/HOS cells formed subcutaneous tumors in mice, but not MG63 and U2OS. Therefore, MNNG/HOS cells were used to explore the mechanisms of SATB2-AS1 regulating the growth and metastasis of osteosarcoma cells in vivo. Two types of stable MNNG/HOS cells overexpressed with SATB2-AS1 (including SATB2-AS1-OV-1 and SATB2-AS1-OV-2) were established, and then subcutaneously inoculated into BALB/c nude mice. The mRNA expression level of SATB2-AS1 significantly increased in the SATB2-AS1-OV-1 and SATB2-AS1-OV-2 groups, especially SATB2-AS1-OV-1, compared with negative control (NC), which detected by qRT-PCR ( ). More importantly, compared with the NC group, the subcutaneous tumor volume in SATB2-AS1-OV groups significantly increased (P < 0.05), while that in si-SATB2-AS1 group decreased (P < 0.01) ( A-B and C, left panel). This trend was also reflected in the weight of subcutaneous tumors (P < 0.05, C, right panel). Because SATB2-AS1-OV-1 promoted the proliferation of subcutaneous tumors more significantly than SATB2-AS1-OV-2, we selected the SATB2-AS1-OV-1 group of BALB/c nude mice to investigate its effect on lung metastasis. As expected, HE staining suggested that the infiltration of osteosarcoma cells in lung tissue of the SATB2-AS1-OV-1 group was significantly more than that of the NC group ( A, B). Furthermore, the result of qRT-PCR indicated that the expression level of SATB2-AS1 in lung tissue of the SATB2-AS1-OV-1 group was higher than that of the NC group (P < 0.001, C). In summary, SATB2-AS1 promoted the lung metastasis of osteosarcoma.
 
3.4.

## SATB2-AS1 directly binds to POU class 3 homeobox 1 (POU3F1)
Next, we explored the potential molecular mechanisms of SATB2-AS1 in the expression of SATB2. An unbiased method was used to identify the proteins that interact with intracellular SATB2-AS1. The biotinylated SATB2-AS1 was incubated with the total protein extract from MNNG/HOS cells and pulled down through streptavidin beads. The binding proteins were analyzed by MS ( A). Among the proteins identified by MS, there were a total of 54 transcription factors (TFs), of which 4 TFs had binding sites with SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 ( B). Therefore, we analyzed the correlation between these four TFs and SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 using patients’ samples, which indicated that only POU3F1 was significantly positively correlated with SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 in the TARGET dataset (R > 0.2, P < 0.01) ( C, left panel). These trends were confirmed in the GZFPH dataset (R > 0.7, P < 0.05) ( C, right panel). Importantly, the mRNA expression levels of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 were significantly decreased after knocking down POU3F1 in the MNNG/HOS cells (P < 0.05, D). These results suggested that SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 physically interacts with POU3F1 in MNNG/HOS cells.
 
3.5.

## The SATB2-AS1/SATB2 was negatively correlated with the immune cells in osteosarcoma
Because of the critical role of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in anti-tumor, we analyzed the correlation between SATB2-AS1/SATB2 and 22 immune cells estimated by CIBERSORT procedure in osteosarcoma ( A). SATB2-AS1 was negatively correlated with follicular helper T cells or memory B cells in the TARGET dataset, which were also shown in SATB2 (R <− 0.2, P < 0.05; B). Then, GSEA was used to explore the immune-related signaling pathways of SATB2-AS1 affecting lung metastasis. The transcriptome sequencing and GSEA analysis of the lung tissues of SATB2-AS1-OV-1 and NC groups in BALB/c nude mice indicated that SATB2-AS1 primarily regulated TILs through the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and IL-17 signaling pathways. These results were again confirmed in the TARGET dataset ( A-B). Finally, we analyzed the correlation between the genes in cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and IL-17 signaling pathways and prognosis in the TARGET dataset, suggesting that high expression levels of IL17RD, IL17RC, IL17RA, CCL2, IFN-γ, and CXCL10, belong to the IL-17 signaling pathway, were associated with favorable OS or RFS in osteosarcoma ( C).
 

4.

## Discussion
Osteosarcoma is highly aggressive and heterogeneous and has a poor prognosis. Risk stratification based on TNM (primary tumor, regional lymph node, distant metastasis) staging system serves an indispensable role in the management of patients with osteosarcoma and the determination of treatment options , . However, not all patients benefit from the risk stratification based on TNM, which may be partly due to genetic changes , , . Genetics has shown increasing advantages in risk stratification for cancer patients , , , . Therefore, it is necessary to explore novel biomarkers for more precisely prognostic stratification and treatment of osteosarcoma. The encoding gene of SATB2-AS1 is located on human chromosome 2q33.1, and its transcript SATB2-AS1 is a lncRNA with 3197 nucleotides in length. SATB2-AS1 is a natural antisense transcript of SATB2 , . Various studies have reported that SATB2-AS1 correlated with ferroptosis and immunity in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, and it can be used as a biomarker for prognostic stratification , . Although the high expression of SATB2-AS1 was associated with the recurrence of patients with osteosarcoma , the exploration of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 in risk stratification and personalized prediction of OS rate for patients was little known. This study demonstrated that increased co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was significantly associated with poor OS and RFS for patients with osteosarcoma. In addition, a nomogram model constructed by the combination of SATB2-AS1/SATB2, primary tumor site, gender, and metastasis could Visually and personally predict 1-, 2-, 3-, and 5-year OS for osteosarcoma. Importantly, the risk score derived from the nomogram model could be used for risk stratification in patients with osteosarcoma. Hence, SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 were potential biomarkers for prognostic stratification in osteosarcoma.
Our previous publication has demonstrated that overexpression of SATB2-AS1 promoted the proliferation of HOS cells in vitro
 , but its mechanisms for regulating cell proliferation and metastasis in vivo needed further exploration. In this study, we found that SATB2-AS1 promoted the growth and lung metastasis of subcutaneous osteosarcoma in nude mice. The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay indicated that SATB2-AS1 directly interacted with the promoter of SATB2, and there was a significant positive correlation between them. These findings were consistent with previous publications , . To further revealed the mechanism of SATB2-AS1 regulating the expression of SATB2, we identified the pull-down protein by MS, which suggested that POU3F1 bound to the promoters of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 and is positively correlated with both of them. POU3F1 is a member of the Pit-Oct-Unc (POU) family. The inhibition of POU3F1 combined with doxorubicin can induce G2 arrest and apoptosis in non-small cell lung cancer . In the presence of IFN-β and IFN-γ, the expression of POU3F1 in fibroblasts and macrophages increases, indicating its potential in regulating immune response . Moreover, POU3F1 mediates the effect on ulcerative colitis-associated colorectal cancer by regulating the inflammatory response of macrophages . POU3F1 promotes thyroid cancer cell proliferation and invasion . However, the role of POU3F1 in osteosarcoma remains unclear. This study suggested that SATB2-AS1 promotes the progression of osteosarcoma by regulating the expression of POU3F1.
Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in tumor microenvironments play a key role in tumor eradication . Thus, we investigated the correlation between SATB2-AS1/SATB2 and TILs, suggesting that they were negatively correlated with follicular helper T cells or memory B cells in osteosarcoma. Furthermore, SATB2-AS1 significantly negatively regulated IL-17 signaling pathways in osteosarcoma, especially IL17RD, IL17RC, IL17RA, CCL2, IFN-γ and CXCL10. Various studies have demonstrated that IL-17 expression is associated with favorable clinical outcomes for many cancer patients, supporting the role of IL-17 in anti-tumor immunity , , , , which is consistent with this study. Interestingly, IL-17 expression is also associated with more cytotoxic IFN-γ + CD4 + and IFN-γ + CD8 + T cells in ovarian cancer . IL-17 recruits anti-tumor neutrophils into the tumor environment to stimulate T-cell responses and predict favorable outcomes of colorectal cancer and lung cancer , . These results strongly supported that SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 might be potential immunomodulators, and could be used as biomarkers and targets of osteosarcoma immunotherapy. However, the potential mechanism of their role in osteosarcoma still needs further investigation.

5.

## Conclusions
Our study suggested that increased co-expression of SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 was associated with poor clinical outcomes. SATB2-AS1 promoted osteosarcoma cell metastasis and regulates immune response by activating SATB2. These data indicated that SATB2-AS1 and SATB2 might be novel biomarkers for risk stratification and promising therapeutic targets of osteosarcoma.
