## Summary
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) organoids that simulate the tumor microenvironment (TME) are an effective tool to identify how TME affects PDAC malignancy. We present a protocol for generating a fused pancreatic cancer organoid (FPCO) that partly reproduces the TME, including heterogeneous cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), using patient-derived PDAC cells and human-induced pluripotent cell-derived endothelial and mesenchymal cells. We also describe the procedure for analyzing FPCO characteristics. FPCO can provide a platform for establishing a reliable drug screening system.
For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Takeuchi et al. 
Subject areas: Cancer, Stem Cells, Organoids

## Before you begin
In vitro PDAC models have been generated for basic and applied research employing organoid technology. Stromal cells, including CAFs, were recently cocultured with PDAC organoids due to the strong association between TME and tumor malignancy. , , However, primary stromal cells have limited proliferative capacity and competency to produce the diverse types of cells detected in in vivo tumor tissue. To replicate the complexity of stromal cells in PDAC TME, we cocultured PDAC cells with hiPSC-derived mesenchymal and endothelial cells. Our organoids, called FPCO, simulate PDAC cell ductal structure and are associated with TME containing heterogeneous CAFs, tumor endothelial cells, and abundant extracellular matrix proteins. Here, we detail protocols for the generation of FPCOs, chemoresistance assays, and the evaluation of CAF heterogeneity.

## Step-by-step method details

## Establishment of pancreatic cancer organoid

Timing: 2 h

This section describes how to establish pancreatic cancer organoid from tumor specimens. When you transport the tumor specimen, it can be stored at 4°C or on ice up to 24 h in MACS tissue storage solution. This protocol is expected to result in a pancreatic cancer organoid establishment efficiency of more than 50% of the total processed PDAC tumor.
1.Obtain sample from surgically resected PDAC tumor.
2.Transport the tumor specimen in MACS tissue storage solution at 4°C.
3.Position the tumor specimen on a cell culture dish.
4.Add the organoid basic medium to the dish.
5.Remove the medium.
6.Repeat steps 4 and 5 three times.
7.Cut the tumor specimens into small pieces that are 2–4 mm in size. 
8.Transfer the tumor fragments to a 15 mL tube after harvesting them with organoid basic medium.
9.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
10.Prepare MACS tumor dissociation enzyme mix.a.Add 200 μL of Enzyme H, 100 μL of Enzyme R, and 50 μL of Enzyme A to 4.7 mL of RPMI 1640.
b.Maintain the enzyme mix at 4°C until it is put to use.

11.Remove the supernatant.
12.Resuspend the tumor fragments using the MACS tumor dissociation enzyme mix and transfer them to the gentleMACS C Tube.
13.Attach the gentleMACS C tube onto the gentleMACS dissociator.
14.Dissociate the tumor fragments with the gentleMACS Program “37°C_h_TDK_3.”
Note: Before and after images of the tumor fragments show with A.
Note: Select the gentleMACS Program “37°C_h_TDK_2” for soft tumor specimens. A hard tumor is white appearance due to the fibrosis. On the other hand, soft tumor is more pink appearance due to increased vasculature ( B).
15.Coat the 24-well culture plate using Matrigel GFR.a.Add 1 mL of PBS (−) per well.
b.Remove the PBS (−).
c.Add 100 μL of Matrigel GFR per well to the 24-well culture plate.
d.Incubate at 37°C for 15 min.

Note: Step 15a to c should be performed on ice.
16.After termination of program, remove the gentleMACS C tube from the gentleMACS dissociator.
17.Transfer the dissociated tumor cell suspension to a MACS SmartStrainer mounted on a 50 mL tube.
18.Wash the cell MACS SmartStrainer with 20 mL of organoid basic medium.
19.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
20.Remove the supernatant.
21.Repeat steps 18 and 19 three times.
Note: If the supernatant is cloudy, it may indicate the presence of pancreatic acinar cells. Contamination with pancreatic acinar cells causes digestion of Matrigel due to the digestive enzymes secreted from pancreatic acinar cells. It is therefore necessary to increase the number of washes to eliminate pancreatic acinar cells completely. In addition, cloudy supernatants are also caused by other factor such as fungal or bacterial contamination. Therefore, confirmation for supernatant under microscope is recommended. If the majority of cells are pancreatic cancer cells, the supernatant is mostly clear as shown in C.
22.Suspend pellet of PDAC cells with 100 μL of Matrigel GFR and place to well in the Matrigel-coated 24 well culture plate.
Note: If the obtained cell number is low, suspend pellet of PDAC cells with 50 μL of Matrigel GFR and plate them directly to plastic plate. In this case, you can omit step 15.
23.Incubate at 37°C for 15 min.
24.Add 800 μL of organoid culture medium to each well.
25.Culture at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator.a.Refresh the medium every 48 h.

Note: PCOs are generated within one to two weeks ( D).
Note: If you plan to perform chemoresistance test or reproliferation assay according to this protocol, you can introduce luciferase or secNanoluc gene to PCO with lentivirus carrying CSII-EF-Luc-IRES2-EGFP or CSII-EF-SecNanoluc-IRES2-hKuO1 (Refer to preparation of the media components step 5).
Pause point: Generated PCOs can be stored in liquid nitrogen on this step.
 

## Differentiation of endothelial cells from human iPSCs

Timing: 17 days

This section describes how to induce the formation of endothelial cells (ECs) and MCs from hiPSCs. ECs and MCs are differentiated from hiPSCs. Different media were employed to independently induce the formation of each cell type ( A, 2B, 2D, and 2E). ECs were defined as CD31+/CD144+/KDR+/CD140b- cells, while MCs were defined as CD73+/CD90+/CD140b+/CD166+ cells ( C and 2F). For the consistent induction of ECs and MCs, it is advised to refresh hiPSCs on a monthly basis.
26.Harvest the prepared hiPSCs.a.Discard the medium.
b.Add 10 mL of PBS (−).
c.Discard the PBS (−).
d.Add 2 mL of accutase.
e.Incubate for 8 min at 37°C with 5% CO2.
f.Harvest the dissociated hiPSC from the culture dish to a 50 mL tube.
g.Wash the culture dish with 10 mL of AK02N and transfer to the same tube.
h.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
i.Remove the supernatant.
j.Resuspend the hiPSCs with 4 mL of AK02N.
k.Count the number of cells.

27.Transfer 8.0 × 105 hiPSCs to a 50 mL tube.
28.Resuspend with 9 mL of AK02N and add 9 μL of 10 mM Y-27632 and 25 μL iMatrix511.
29.Plate the cells into a 55 cm2 dish.
CRITICAL: Plate the cell suspension promptly following the addition of iMatrix511 to avoid the adsorption of iMatrix 511 to the centrifugation tube.
30.Incubate at 37°C in 5% CO2 incubator for 24 h.
31.Replace the medium with Mesoderm induction medium.
32.Incubate for 72 h.a.Refresh the medium after 48 h.

33.Replace the medium with endothelial cell (EC) induction medium.
34.Culture for six days at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

35.Replate the differentiated cells.a.Remove the medium.
b.Add 10 mL of PBS (−).
c.Remove the PBS (−).
d.Add 2 mL of 0.05% trypsin-EDTA.
e.Incubate for 5 min at 37°C with 5% CO2.
f.Harvest the differentiated cells from the culture dish and transfer them to a 50 mL tube.
g.Wash the culture dish with 10 mL of trypsin-EDTA reaction stop medium and transfer it to the same tube.
h.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
i.Discard the supernatant.
j.Resuspend the differentiated cells with 4 mL of EC maintenance medium.CRITICAL: Ensure that the EC maintenance medium is employed to resuspend the cells. Contamination of FBS in the cell suspension causes the attachment of mesenchymal cells to the culture dish.
Pause point: ECs can be stored in liquid nitrogen on this step.

k.Count the number of cells.
l.Transfer the 1.0 × 106 cells to a 50 mL tube.
m.Add 10 mL of EC maintenance medium with 25 μL iMatrix511 per culture dish.
n.Seed the cells into a 100 cm2 culture dish.

36.Culture for 24 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.
37.Replace the medium with MiraCell EC Culture Medium.
38.Culture for 6 days at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 72 h.

Note: Differentiated ECs are recommended to use for generation FPCO on the day of differentiation endpoint to stabilize ECs’ condition between experiments. If the timing of usage needs to be changed, you can change the start timing of hiPSC differentiation. In addition, hiPSC-ECs can be stored in liquid nitrogen on step 35j. If you use the frozen stock, restart the culture 7 day ahead of coculture.
Note: CD31+/CD144+/KDR+/CD140b− over 90% by flow cytometer are recommended to validate efficiency of MCs differentiation. Confluency of ECs reaches nearly 100% at end of induction, but it does not affect ECs condition such as viability, proliferation potential and expression of marker gene.
 

## Differentiation of mesenchymal cells from human iPSCs

Timing: 10 days

39.Induce differentiation of the hiPSCs to mesoderm cells with steps 26 to 32a.
40.Replace the medium with mesenchymal cell (MC) induction medium A.
41.Culture for 48 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

42.Replate the differentiated cells.a.Coat a new culture plate with 0.1% gelatin in ddH2O.i.Incubate 1 h at 37°C.
ii.Before cell seeding in step 42m, remove 0.1% gelatin from a culture plate.

b.Discard the medium from the differentiated cells.
c.Add 10 mL of PBS (−).
d.Remove the PBS (−).
e.Add 2 mL of 0.05% trypsin-EDTA.
f.Incubate for 3 min at 37°C with 5% CO2.
g.Harvest the differentiated cells from the culture dish and transfer them to a 50 mL tube.
h.Wash the culture dish with 10 mL of trypsin-EDTA reaction stop medium and transfer it to the same tube.
i.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
j.Remove the supernatant.
k.Resuspend differentiated cells with 4 mL of MC induction medium A.Pause point: MCs can be stored in liquid nitrogen on this step.

l.Count the number of cells.
m.Seed the 1.2 × 106 cells to a gelatin coated culture dish with 10 mL of MC induction medium A containing 10 μM Y-27632.

43.Culture for 24 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.
44.Replace the medium with MC induction medium B.
45.Culture for 72 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

Note: Differentiated MCs are recommended to use for generation FPCO on the day of differentiation endpoint to stabilize MCs’ condition between experiments. If the timing of usage needs to be changed, you can change the start timing of hiPSC differentiation. In addition, MCs can be stored in liquid nitrogen on step 42k. You can restart MC culture 4 days ahead of the coculture.

Note: CD73+/CD90+/CD140b+/CD166+ over 90% by flow cytometer are recommended to validate efficiency of MCs differentiation. Confluency of MCs reach nearly 100% at the end of induction, but it does not affect MCs condition such as viability, proliferation potential and expression of marker gene.

## Generation of the proliferative FPCO

Timing: 4 days

This section details the procedure to generate the proliferative FPCO (pFPCO). While the characteristics of pFPCO develop over a period of approximately four days ( D), pFPCO can be maintained for about one month.
46.Harvest pancreatic cancer cells from PDAC organoids.a.Discard the medium from the wells without disrupting the Matrigel.
b.Scrape the Matrigel using the cell scraper.
c.Add 1 mL of TrypLE Express enzyme containing 10 μM Y-27632 and transfer the mixture into a 15 mL tube.
d.To dislodge the Matrigel cluster, pipette it up and down five times.Note: Inadequate loosening results in Matrigel indigestion.

e.Incubate at 37°C for 8 min in a water bath.
f.To ensure proper mixing, invert the tube every 2 min in step 46e.
g.Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
h.Discard the supernatant, then add 1 mL of accutase containing 10 μM Y-27632 and mix by pipetting up and down.
i.Incubate with 37°C for 60 min in a water bath.
j.During the step 46i, ensure proper mixing of the cell suspension by pipetting the mixture up and down every 10 min.
k.Centrifuge at 200 × g for 3 min.
l.Discard the supernatant, then add 5 mL of organoid basic medium and mix using pipetting to dilute accutase. Perform centrifugation at 200 × g for 3 min.
m.Remove the supernatant, resuspend with 2 mL of organoid basic medium.
n.Count the number of the PDAC cells.

47.Harvest hiPSC-derived ECs and MCs.a.Discard the medium from the dish.
b.Add 10 mL of PBS (−).
c.Discard the PBS (−).
d.Add 2 mL of 0.05% trypsin-EDTA.
e.Incubate for 1 min for ECs and for 3 min for MCs at 37°C and 5% CO2.
f.Harvest the cell suspension and transfer it to a 50 mL tube.
g.Wash the dish with 10 mL of DMEM (10% FBS, 1% P/S) and transfer it to the same 50 mL tube.
h.Centrifuge at 200 × g for 3 min.
i.Remove the supernatant, then add 5 mL of DMEM (10% FBS, 1% P/S).
j.Count the number of cells.

48.Prepare the Elplasia plate.a.Add 1 mL of PBS (−) to all wells.
b.Centrifuge at 400 × g for 3 min.

49.Mix the PDAC cell suspension (1.2 × 105), ECs (8.4 × 104) and MCs (2.4 × 105) to generate one FPCO.
50.Centrifuge at 200 × g for 3 min.
51.Discard the supernatant, then resuspend with 1 mL of cytokine reduced-FPCO medium containing 10 μM Y-27632 per FPCO.
Note: The use of old EGMs in cytokine reduced-FPCO medium impairs the formation of FPCOs. Therefore, the prepared EGM should be aliquoted, frozen for storage and used within one month.
52.Discard the PBS (−) from the well of the Elplasia plate.
53.Transfer 1 mL of the cell suspension to each well of the Elplasia plate.
54.Incubate for 24 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.
55.Place the cell culture insert in a 12-well cell culture plate.
56.Transfer the multicellular spheroids generated in one well of Elplasia plate using a pipette and transfer to a 1.5 mL tube per FPCO.
57.Centrifuge at 200 × g for 3 min.
58.Discard the supernatant, leaving approximately 10 μL of the medium.
59.Resuspend and generate spheroids’ droplets.a.Slowly mix the contents in the tip of a 20 μL pipette.
b.Wait for the multicellular spheroids to descend to end of the pipet tip, and then prepare the droplet filled with multicellular spheroids (the droplet is supposed to be about 3–5 μL).

Note: The efficient generation of spheroids’ droplets is facilitated by the repeated process of rapidly pipetting up and gently pipetting down ( A and 3B).
60.Place a spheroid-containing droplet onto the cell culture insert.
Note: Three spheroid-containing droplets can be placed in one cell culture insert ( C).
61.Add 600 μL of FPCO medium containing 10 μM Y-27632 below the cell culture insert.
62.Culture at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 24 h.
63.Replace the medium with FPCO medium.
64.Culture at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 48 h.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

 

## Generation of quiescent FPCO

Timing: 7 days

This section describes the procedure to generate quiescent FPCO (qFPCO). While the characteristics of qFPCO develop by about one week ( D), the qFPCO can be maintained for approximately one month.
65.Generate FPCO as per steps 46 to 62.
66.Replace the medium with cytokine reduced-FPCO medium.
67.Culture for five days at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

## Chemoresistance test for FPCO

Timing: 7–10 days

This section describes the protocol for verifying chemoresistance of cancer cells in PCO or FPCOs.
68.Generate pFPCO or qFPCO using luciferase-induced PCO, following steps 46 to 64a or 65 to 67a.
69.Replace the medium with FPCO medium or cytokine reduced-FPCO medium containing anti-cancer drug (e.g., gemcitabine, 5-Fluorouracil, paclitaxel).
70.Culture for 72 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

71.Add 1 mL of PBS (−) to the cell culture insert.
72.Detach the FPCOs from the insert using a pipette and transfer them to a round bottom 2 mL tube.
73.Add 100 μL of 1X Glo Lysis buffer and two 4 mm zirconia beads.
74.Lyse the FPCO using the TissueLyser LT system at 50/s for 3 min.
75.Freeze the FPCO lysate at −80°C.
76.At 25°C, thaw the FPCO lysate.
77.Repeat steps 74 to 76 three times.
78.Mix the same amount of FPCO lysate and ONE-Glo Luciferase Assay Substrate.
79.Incubate at 25°C for 5 min in a dark place.
80.Measure the luminescence intensity using the GloMax Explorer System.

## Reproliferation assay for FPCO

Timing: 1 month

This section details the protocol to verify reproliferating potential of cancer cells in PCO or FPCO. FPCOs should be generated one by one within the 24-well cell culture insert. It is necessary to prevent freeze-thaw cycle of the supernatant of the samples, as the activity of Nanoluc reduces significantly.
81.Generate pFPCO or qFPCO using the secNanoluc-introduced PCO, following steps 46 to 64a or 65 to 67.
82.Replace the medium with FPCO medium or cytokine reduced-FPCO medium containing 1 μM gemcitabine.
83.Culture for 48 h at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.

84.Replace the medium with FPCO medium or cytokine reduced-FPCO medium.
85.Culture for 1 month at 37°C with 5% CO2.a.Refresh the medium every 24 h.
b.Harvest the supernatant regularly and store at −80°C.

86.Prepare the Nano-Glo Luciferase Assay Reagent following the manufacture’s protocol.
87.Combine the Nano-Glo Luciferase Assay Reagent with the supernatant of FPCO in the same proportion.

## Expected outcomes
The luminal structure of PDAC cells is formed in FPCOs, which deposit an abundance of ECM in the interstitial space and contain a variety of CAFs. Importantly, FPCOs, which are linked to PDAC TME, can be generated without the use of exogenous ECM (e.g., Matrigel). Furthermore, FPCOs can be induced to resemble two distinct aspects of PDAC tissue by modifying culture conditions: pFPCO exhibit the potential to reproliferate following anti-cancer drug treatment, whereas qFPCO demonstrates stronger chemoresistance than PCO, the monoculture of PDAC cells. It is a widespread issue that anti-PDAC drug candidates identified through conventional in vitro screening systems failed to exhibit therapeutic effects in clinical trials. FPCO associated with TME, especially qFPCO, is expected to be a reliable system for identifying anti-cancer drug candidates.
FPCOs contain iCAF, myCAF, and apCAF-like cells, which differentiate from immature mesenchymal cells derived from hiPSCs. Therefore, it is feasible to monitor the differentiation process of CAFs and the manner in which each subtype influences the proliferation and/or metastatic characteristics of PDAC cells in detail. Additionally, the FPCO system can be adjusted to replicate the complexity of PDAC tissue by incorporating additional TME cells, such as macrophages and lymphocytes.

## Limitations
Compared to simple 2D or spheroid culture, the FPCO system requires the implementation of several techniques, such as primary PDAC cell culture, spheroid culture, and hiPSC maintenance and differentiation. Due to the limitation of patient PDAC tissue size, it could be difficult to establish PDAC cells derived from some patients’ tissues. Generating FPCOs by depositing spheroids onto a cell culture insert necessitates training. Using our method for pancreatic cancer organoid establishment (steps 1 to 25), the yield is about 60% to acquire PDOs that can be maintained even after 5 passages. This is lower than 75% reported in the previous works. , Using recombinant WNT3A and RSPO1 instead of their conditioned medium and a gentleMACS dissociator for PDAC cells from tumor tissues may reduce the efficiency of PDAC cell expansion. Therefore, researchers can also use the previous reported protocol. , 

## Troubleshooting

## Problem 1
FPCOs rapidly aggregate (e.g., day 1–2), and PDAC cells are exposed to the surface of FPCOs.

## Potential solution
It is important to maintain the condition of air-liquid interface (ALI) for FPCO creation. To avoid the medium from seeping inside the insert, the lower cavity is filled with a minimal quantity of medium (e.g., 12-well: 600 μL, 24-well: 400 μL).
If the media seeps into the insert due to the excess quantity of media in the lower space, the ALI environment cannot be maintained, and FPCO formation will be inefficient.

## Problem 2
Undifferentiated cells persist in hiPSC-derived mesenchymal and endothelial cells.

## Potential solution
The differentiation potential of hiPSC is significantly influenced by the passage number. Do not use hiPSC that have been passaged more than 25 times in step 26.

## Problem 3
In steps 35 to 37, endothelial cells could be contaminated with mesenchymal cell-like cells.

## Potential solution
The medium used to resuspend cells in step 35j should not contain FBS; rather, it should be EC maintenance medium. The presence of FBS in the culture medium results in the attachment of mesenchymal cell-like cells to culture dishes.
