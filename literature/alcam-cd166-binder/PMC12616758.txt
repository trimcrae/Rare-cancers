## Abstract
Recent years have seen a marked increase in studies focusing on the mechanistic role of Aldehyde dehydrogenase 2 (ALDH2) genetic polymorphisms in tumors, yet this field lacks systematic bibliometric analysis. By analyzing publication years, countries/regions, research institutions, disciplinary domains, authorship networks, journal distributions, and keyword networks, this study reveals that global research interest in ALDH2 and tumors continues to rise. China, the United States, and Japan form the core contributing triangle, with institutions such as Aichi Cancer Center and Stanford University leading cutting-edge exploration. The research landscape concentrates on oncology, biochemistry and molecular biology, and cell biology, while interdisciplinary immunology provides innovative perspectives. Key future directions include elucidating the regulatory networks of ALDH2 polymorphisms in the metabolism-cancer axis and their associations with gastrointestinal and hematologic malignancies, alongside mechanistic studies encompassing oxidative stress modulation, ferroptosis regulation, DNA adduct formation, apoptotic cascades, DNA methylation, senescence-associated secretory phenotypes and tumor immune escape microenvironment dynamics.

## Introduction
Cancer is a major public health problem worldwide and a major factor contributing to the global burden of disease. According to the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) of the World Health Organization in 2024, it was reported that there were about 20 million new cancer cases and 9.74 million deaths in 2022 [ ]. The dramatically increasing global cancer burden reflects the combination of an ageing population and the exposure to risk factors such as alcohol, tobacco, obesity and air pollution [ ].
Alcohol is a major risk factor in global disease burden [ , ]. Approximately 4% of cancers in the whole world are caused by alcohol consumption [ ]. Alcohol-related cancers include upper aerodigestive tract cancers (oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, and esophagus) as well as colon, rectum, liver, and women’s breast cancers. In 2020, a total of 6.3 million cases and 3.3 million deaths were attributed to these cancers globally [ ]. Alcohol-derived acetaldehyde accumulation is recognized as the primary genotoxic driver in alcohol-associated carcinogenesis. However, its detoxification efficiency exhibits dramatic interindividual divergence-chiefly governed by aldehyde dehydrogenase 2 (ALDH2) polymorphisms, which creates population-specific cancer susceptibility profiles.
ALDH2, the rate-limiting enzyme for acetaldehyde clearance processing > 80% systemic load, converting the alcohol metabolite acetaldehyde into nontoxic acetic acid [ ]. The ALDH2 gene spans a total length of 43,438 base pairs. Point mutations in the exons of this gene can lead to alterations in the normal allele configuration from ALDH2*1/1 (G/G) to either ALDH2*2/1 (G/A) or ALDH2*2/2 (A/A). These mutations result in the substitution of the glutamate residue at position 487 with lysine, thereby transforming the active ALDH2*1 variant into the inactive ALDH2*2 form. ALDH2 is involved in endogenous and exogenous aldehyde metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, hypoxia and DNA damage repair, regulation of the immune system and stem markers [ – ].
ALDH2 mutations are present in approximately 8% of the global population and about 40% of individuals of East Asian descent [ ]. ALDH2 dysfunction contributes to a wide range of clinical disorders, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, neurodegenerative diseases, stroke, and cancer [ – ]. Loss-of-function ALDH2 variants are associated with significantly elevated cancer risk, including esophageal carcinoma (OR = 1.69, 95% CI: 1.25–2.29, P = 0.001) and oral cavity cancer (HR = 2.01, 95% CI: 1.33–3041, P < 0.001) [ ]. Reduced ALDH2 enzymatic activity demonstrably correlates with shortened overall survival and inferior clinical outcomes [ ]. Such evidence underscores ALDH2 dysfunction as the molecular pivot linking alcohol consumption to cancer pathogenesis.
Bibliometrics as proposed by Prichard takes the literature system and bibliometric characteristics as the objects of study, and utilizes statistical methods to study the quantitative relationships and laws between literature and the literature system, in order to provide an overview of the literature and predict the development of scientific research [ ]. Quickly and accurately presenting trends and the most influential findings in the field is possible, and it can establish a theoretical foundation for further research. Despite the widespread use of bibliometrics in other fields, bibliometric analyses of ALDH2 and tumors have remained underreported until now. To fill this gap, based on SCIE, we conducted a bibliometric analysis of various bibliometric data (annual articles, country/region, authors, institutions, disciplines, journals, references, and keywords) published in the field of ALDH2 and oncology between January 1, 2014 and December 31, 2024, from the perspectives of co-occurrence and clustering, as well as using descriptive statistics. The purpose of this article is to provide an overview of the current status, trends, and frontiers of research on ALDH2 and tumor research with the aim of informing future relevant articles.

## The bibliometric analysis method
Complete article records with cited references were exported as plain text files (naming convention: ‘download_XXX.txt’). This file was subsequently imported into VOSviewer 1.6.18, Citespace, and Scimago Graphica, facilitating further analytical exploration.
The bibliometric data underwent processed into VOSviewer 1.6.18, where we adhered to the principal procedural steps in crafting visualized knowledge graphs. VOSviewer supports five bibliometric analyses: co-authorship, co-occurrence, citation, coupling, and co-citation analysis. These are visualized via three network types: network maps, overlay maps, and density maps. Nodes within the various graphs symbolize authors, institutions, countries, or keywords, with the size of each node reflecting the frequency of occurrence or citation, while the hue of a node signifies the year of occurrence or citation [ ].
Citespace emerges as a formidable tool for mapping and analysis, wherein raw data is imported into Citespace 6.3 for an in-depth examination of journal biplot overlay analysis and keyword highlighting analysis. The double journal map juxtaposes citing journals on the left and cited journals on the right, with the connecting lines illustrating their interrelations. The keyword burst mapping portrays the initiation and conclusion time of keyword highlighting. Term normalization followed the mapping framework in Supplementary Table S1.
Scimago Graphica, with its intuitive interface, swiftly conjures complex data visualizations through simple drag-and-drop interactions [ ]. Following the transformation of bibliometric data into a country cooperation network map via VOSviewer, the resultant GML file was exported and subsequently unveiled within Scimago Graphica, crafting a visual connection map of profound significance.
Outputs from Web of Science and VOSviewer-including authors, journals, institutions, countries, and references–were processed in Microsoft Excel to generate top 10 tables.
In our analytical endeavors, we harnessed the capabilities of Origin 2022 to create illustrative graphs, depicting the global number of publications and citations within the field from 2014 to 2024, alongside the publication and citation metrics for various countries and regions.

The salient aspects are as follows:

## ALDH2 and metabolism
The primary pathway of alcohol metabolism is orchestrated by two enzymes: alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) and aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH). Initially, alcohol is converted into acetaldehyde by ADH, which is then transformed into acetic acid by ALDH. ADH1B represents a major ADH isozyme, where its high-activity variant accelerates ethanol oxidation, while the low-activity variant reduces acetaldehyde generation. Nevertheless, ADH1B polymorphisms alone do not cause acetaldehyde accumulation in individuals with wild-type ALDH2*1/1(G/G) [ ]. Although both ALDH1 and ALDH2 contribute to systemic alcohol metabolism, ALDH1 primarily mediates retinaldehyde oxidation, whereas ALDH2 is predominantly responsible for acetaldehyde detoxification [ ].
Notably, the human ALDH2 gene has been documented to harbor 84 SNPs [ ]. Mutations within the ALDH2 gene precipitate diminished levels of ALDH2 protein expression, culminating in an impaired capacity to metabolize acetaldehyde, a phenomenon intricately linked to the onset, progression, and metastasis of various diseases [ , – ]. The consumption of alcohol, coupled with smoking, amplifies the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and head and neck squamous-cell carcinoma, particularly among individuals possessing genetic predispositions associated with ALDH2 gene mutations [ – ]. Indeed, ALDH2 gene mutations significantly heighten the susceptibility to alcohol-related diseases.
ALDH2 plays a vital role in the intricate processes of fatty acid metabolism, encompassing both synthesis and oxidation. Lipid synthesis involves the conversion of acetyl-CoA into fatty acids, primarily taking place in the liver and adipose tissue. On the other hand, the breakdown of fats includes two key processes: β-oxidation and α-oxidation, which predominantly occur in the liver and muscle tissues [ ]. In the fatty acid oxidation, ALDH2 plays a crucial regulatory role by promoting energy metabolism through the facilitation of β-oxidation of fatty acids [ ]. Observations from ALDH2 knockout animal models show that the rate of fatty acid oxidation significantly decreased. In addition, ALDH2 plays an important role in reducing oxidative stress that occurs during fatty acid oxidation. It effectively removes harmful aldehydes like 4-hydroxynonenal [ ]. In models of obesity induced by high-fat diets, a decrease in ALDH2 activity is associated with lower fat accumulation and energy expenditure, highlighting the enzyme’s essential role in fatty acid metabolism [ ]. Moreover, ALDH2 engages in a complex interplay with various enzymes integral to fatty acid metabolism, modulating their activities. The expression of ALDH2 enhances the activity of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor α, which is a crucial regulator that facilitates the process of fatty acid β-oxidation while also reducing lipid synthesis [ , ]. ALDH2 plays a significant role in the intricate interactions with several enzymes that are essential for fatty acid metabolism, influencing their functions [ ]. A decline in ALDH2 activity has been associated with tumorigenesis, with research indicating that ALDH2 plays a role in shaping the tumor microenvironment by influencing fatty acid metabolic pathways, which in turn affects tumor growth and metastasis [ ]. ALDH2 is not only an essential enzyme involved in fatty acid metabolism but also represents a promising target for tackling various metabolic diseases and the intricate processes of tumor formation.

## Association of ALDH2 gene polymorphisms with different types of cancer
The polymorphisms of the ALDH2 gene, especially the variants found at the rs671 locus, are closely associated with the risk of developing various types of tumors. One notable variant, Glu504Lys, is particularly common among East Asian populations and is significantly linked to an increased risk of esophageal cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and other digestive system malignancies. For instance, individuals who carry the ALDH2*2 allele show a greater tendency for tumor development in cases of esophageal and hepatocellular cancers. This heightened risk is likely due to the variant’s effect on acetaldehyde metabolism, which leads to an accumulation of this toxic compound. The resulting increase in DNA damage and mutations within cells contributes to the progression of these cancers [ ]. Mutations in the ALDH2 gene have been found to elevate the risk of developing multiple primary tumors in head and neck cancers, encompassing both oral and throat cancers [ ]. The ALDH2*2 genotype is associated with more severe cervical precancer [ ]. Alda-1, which acts as an activator of ALDH2, has demonstrated its ability to reduce the buildup of 4-hydroxynonenoic acid adducts, postpone the development of dermatitis caused by radiotherapy, and improve the sensitivity of tumors to radiation therapy [ ]. The extracellular vesicles derived from ALDH2-deficient hepatocytes release substantial quantities of deleterious oxidized mitochondrial DNA, which subsequently metastasize to adjacent hepatocellular carcinoma cells, while alcohol exposure activates a multitude of oncogenic pathways (JNK, STAT3, BCL-2, and TAZ) that propel the development of hepatocellular carcinoma [ ]. Endoscopic mucosal resection for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma has been observed to elevate the incidence of postoperative metachronous esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, influenced by the cumulative effects of ALDH2 rs671 alongside smoking or alcohol consumption [ , ]. Variations in the ALDH2 gene could significantly influence the development and progression of tumors by affecting how alcohol is metabolized and altering the internal cellular environment.

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms, oxidative stress and ferroptosis
Oxidative stress arises from various external factors, including the harmful effects of cigarette smoke and the appealing nature of alcohol. These influences lead cells to produce an excessive amount of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This surge of ROS disrupts the fragile balance of the antioxidant defense system, upsetting the equilibrium between oxidants and antioxidants [ – ]. The deficiency of ALDH2, combined with the impaired metabolism of both internal and external aldehydes, as well as increased levels of ROS in the body, acts as a trigger for the onset of cancer and the development of drug resistance [ , ].
In the intricate tapestry of cellular architecture, lipids play a role in preserving the structural integrity and functional expression of cell membranes. Yet, the insidious process of lipid peroxidation alters the very essence of cellular and subcellular membranes, leading to covalent modifications of proteins and nucleic acids. This phenomenon of lipid peroxidation propels the onset of ferroptosis within tumor cells [ ].
To combat oxidative stress, three avenues present themselves: the utilization of endogenous antioxidants, the introduction of exogenous antioxidants, and the strategic reduction of oxidative stress itself [ ]. ALDH2 stands as a guardian, converting aldehydes and scavenging ROS. Vitamin D, in its wisdom, induces the transcription of NRF2, which in turn upregulates ALDH2 expression, thereby mitigating oxidative stress [ ]. Furthermore, ALDH2 activates the H2S-AMPK-G6PD signaling pathway, enhancing the function and survival of β-cells under the duress of hyperglycemic conditions, all while maintaining the delicate balance of glutathione redox [ ]. Presently, the scientific community is keenly focused on the maintenance and impact of ALDH2 on the redox state within the organism.

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms and DNA adduct
Specific ALDH2 gene mutations disrupt acetaldehyde metabolism, driving accumulation of acetaldehyde. This compound engages in a direct reaction with the exocyclic amino group of deoxyguanosine, culminating in the formation of DNA adducts, single-strand breaks, and double-strand breaks. The consequences extend to point mutations, short consensus repeats, DNA-DNA cross-links, micronuclei, and macromolecular chromosomal aberrations, all of which contribute to profound DNA damage and propel the progression of cancer, particularly heightening susceptibility to squamous cell carcinoma [ ]. The spectrum of squamous cell carcinomas linked to ALDH2 encompasses esophageal, head and neck, oral cavity, lung, and nasal cavity malignancies. Its presence is also noted in breast cancer and hepatocellular carcinomas [ – ].

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms and apoptosis
Apoptosis, the quintessential non-inflammatory mode of cellular demise, is intricately influenced by ALDH2 through the regulation of multiple apoptotic signaling pathways. The inhibition of ALDH2 precipitates alterations in apoptosis-related proteins, manifesting as an up-regulation of Bax and a down-regulation of Bcl-2, thereby fostering the activation of apoptotic signaling [ ]. Furthermore, the impediment of ALDH2 function, resulting in the accumulation of ACE, incites apoptosis and DNA damage within tumor cells via the activation of the JNK/p38 MAPK signaling pathway [ ]. Notably, the overexpression of ALDH2 significantly curtails JNK activation and its downstream caspase-3 activation in the context of an acute stroke model, thus mitigating apoptosis [ ]. These investigations illuminate that ALDH2 not only modulates apoptosis by directly influencing intracellular oxidative stress levels but also shapes the survival and demise of tumor cells through intricate signaling pathway interactions. Consequently, ALDH2 emerges as a potential focal point in tumor therapy, poised to sway the fate of tumor cells by orchestrating apoptotic signaling pathways. The recurrence of acute myeloid leukemia is intricately linked to ALDH2 gene silencing, with ALDH2-deficient acute myeloid leukemia cells being amenable to elimination through the blockade of UBE2T/FANCL-mediated ubiquitination [ ]. At this juncture, polymorphisms of the ALDH2 gene have been discerned and correlated with acute leukemia and other malignancies.

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms and DNA methylation
DNA methylation refers to the process of adding a methyl group (–CH3) to the 5′ carbon atom of a cytosine (C) residue within a DNA molecule. This modification is a key player in various biological processes, including the regulation of gene expression, the maintenance of genome stability, and the facilitation of cell differentiation. Research has indicated that irregular changes in DNA methylation patterns are significantly linked to the development and progression of numerous diseases, particularly cancer. In tumor cells, abnormal DNA methylation often results in the silencing of tumor suppressor genes, which can subsequently drive the process of tumorigenesis [ ]. DNA methylation plays a crucial role in regulating gene expression by affecting how transcription factors bind to the DNA strand. DNMT3A and MBD4 epigenetically suppress ALDH2 expression through promoter hypermethylation, thereby potentiating malignant phenotypes in tumor cells [ ]. This regulatory axis establishes DNMT3A, MBD4 and ALDH2 as compelling combinatorial therapeutic targets for reversing oncogenic transformation [ ]. In patients with acute myeloid leukemia, the abnormal expression of ALDH2 is associated with changes at particular DNA methylation sites. This suggests that ALDH2 plays a role in the development and progression of the disease by influencing the methylation status of genes [ ]. The deletion of ALDH2 not only affects its own expression but may also worsen tumor progression by altering the methylation status of related genes.

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms and cellular senescence
ALDH2 plays a vital role in cellular senescence, as its expression is closely connected to the levels of oxidative stress in the cell. ALDH2 expression increases during mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) senescence. Overexpression of ALDH2 accelerates aging in young MSCs, while its knockdown attenuates premature aging phenotypes in aged MSCs [ ]. In the ischemia-reperfusion model, activation of ALDH2 helps reduce inflammation in cells. It does this by blocking the NF-κB signaling pathway, which is involved in inflammation [ ]. Furthermore, ALDH2 physically interacts with Heat Shock Protein Family A Member 8, and this binding event suppresses ALDH2 enzymatic activity while promoting cellular senescence [ ]. ALDH2 inhibition promotes cell cycle progression and increases cell proliferation and metastasis in colorectal cancer cells [ ].Conversely, the activation of ALDH2 induces cell cycle arrest, particularly within the G0/G1 phase, a process closely intertwined with the senescence characteristics of cells [ – ]. ALDH2 is crucial for the regulation of cellular aging, providing new perspectives for cancer treatment.

## ALDH2 gene polymorphisms and immune escape
ALDH2 plays a crucial role in regulating immune cell function within the complex environment of tumors. When ALDH2 is deficient or inhibited, T cell effectiveness decreases, which can lead to increased tumor growth and help the tumor evade the immune system. The relationship between ALDH2 and the tumor immune microenvironment is significantly positive, indicating that ALDH2 could serve as a valuable biomarker for predicting the recurrence of prostate cancer following radical radiotherapy [ ]. Furthermore, inhibition of ALDH2 suppresses the NOD/NF-κB/VISTA axis, thereby revitalizing CD8+ T cell-mediated antitumor immunity [ ]. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, ALDH2 expression positively correlated with immune cell infiltration; patients exhibiting diminished ALDH2 expression were often accompanied by a poorer prognosis and reduced levels of immune cell infiltration [ ]. These revelations illuminate the dual role of ALDH2, not merely as an inhibitor of tumor cells but as a significant player in the dynamics of immune escape and the formation of the tumor microenvironment through the regulation of immune cell function.

## Advantages and limitations
The Web of Science stands as an independent platform, seamlessly integrating global academic information databases, and boasts powerful citation indexing functions alongside robust academic impact evaluation capabilities. Among its esteemed offerings, SCIE emerges as one of the most authoritative databases, with the literature it retrieves being both comprehensive and representative.
The principal limitation inherent in this study resides within the literature analysis segment, particularly concerning the citation metrics, which are susceptible to a myriad of influencing factors. Articles that grace the earlier annals of publication often enjoy a heightened citation rate, while certain seminal works, despite their limited citation count, may regrettably elude recognition [ ]. Although the literature search ambitiously encompassed the period from January 1, 2014, to December 31, 2024, it is plausible that this timeframe does not encapsulate the entirety of published articles, thereby introducing a potential bias stemming from the incomplete retrieval of pertinent literature within the field. Nevertheless, the focal point of this investigation is to visualize and scrutinize the articles from the past decade, with the intent to illuminate research hotspots and steer the trajectory of inquiry within this domain. Thus, it is anticipated that we shall not forfeit a significant volume of information that could impact the analysis of these hotspots.

## Conclusion
The overall corpus of articles pertaining to ALDH2 and oncology is witnessing a notable ascent. International collaboration networks show increasing trend among various institutions across specific country count. Through comprehensive and profound research endeavors, studies on ALDH2 encompass investigations into gene polymorphisms, alcohol metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and their intricate relationships with the emergence and progression of various malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinoma, esophageal squamous carcinoma, and head and neck carcinoma, alongside the exploration of associated molecular mechanisms such as apoptosis, oxidative stress, ferroptosis, DNA methylation, DNA adducts, cellular senescence and immune evasion. Current evidence indicates three pathway-focused domains: biochemistry, endocrine metabolism, and immunity. Research on alcohol metabolic mechanisms approaches exhaustive exploration, whereas substantial knowledge gaps persist regarding immunomodulation within the tumor microenvironment, epigenetic regulation, and cellular senescence. Cross-cancer analyses reveal that lung cancer, acute leukemia, and prostate cancer have constituted predominant research foci in recent biennia, with prostate cancer studies demonstrating particularly robust associations with biochemical recurrence. Consequently, the clinical translation priorities for ALDH2 research should focus on the following three pathways: (i) immunometabolic crosstalk-investigating ALDH2-mediated immune checkpoint synergy (e.g., PD-L1/VISTA) in breast neoplasms and melanoma T-cell exhaustion models; (ii) epigenetic reprogramming pathways across these malignancies- DNMT3A and MBD4 epigenetically suppress ALDH2 expression through promoter hypermethylation, thereby potentiating malignant phenotypes in LUAD cells; and (iii) the cellular senescence-related gene prognostic index risk score, constructed using ALCAM and ALDH2 expression signatures, represents a validated biomarker for predicting prognostic metastatic propensity and radioresistance in prostate cancer.
