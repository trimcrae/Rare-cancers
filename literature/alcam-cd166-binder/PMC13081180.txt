## Abstract

## Background & Aims
Single-cell fixed RNA profiling (FLEX) is a novel technique that captures RNA expression in frozen tissues at a single-cell resolution. We applied FLEX to mouse model of liver fibrosis progression and regression to identify novel antifibrotic targets.

## Methods
Mice were administered intraperitoneal thioacetamide for 10 weeks to induce fibrosis, regression was assessed 2 weeks after cessation. The livers were fixed, dissociated, and analysed using FLEX. Molecular validation included immunoblotting, immunohistochemistry, gene silencing/overexpression, cytokine/phosphokinase arrays, and human liver samples.

## Results
Approximately 40,000 liver cells were profiled, integrated, and annotated into 10 major cell types using lineage-specific markers. Pericentral signature restoration in hepatocytes, scar-resolving genes (Mmp14 and Ctsl), fenestrae restoration in liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, anti-inflammatory Kupffer cells, reduced fibrogenic cholangiocytes, and recovery-associated immune cell subsets were observed. During fibrosis, monocyte-derived macrophages secrete semaphorin-4D (SEMA4D), which binds to Plexin B2 on hepatic stellate cells (HSCs). SEMA4D+ cells were upregulated in mouse fibrotic livers (n = 6, p <0.05). Recombinant SEMA4D induces type I collagen in HSCs, whereas humanised monoclonal IgG4 SEMA4D blockade (VX15/2503) attenuates fibrosis in vivo (n = 5, p <0.05). LIM and cysteine-rich domains 1 (LMCD1) was enriched in fibrotic HSCs and suppressed during regression. LMCD1 knockdown reduced the expression of fibrotic protein, while LMCD1 overexpression promoted the expression of fibrotic protein via AKT/mTOR signalling. LMCD1 and SEMA4D are localised in the fibrotic septa and are correlated with the fibrotic stage of MASLD (n = 34, p <0.05) and HCV (n = 76, p <0.05) in humans.

## Conclusions
This FLEX-based single-cell atlas revealed critical transcriptional programs and cell–cell interactions, identifying SEMA4D and LMCD1 as promising therapeutic targets for liver fibrosis.

## Impact and implications
In this study, we applied a novel technique, single-cell fixed RNA profiling, to profile 38,136 cells obtained from control, thioacetamide-induced liver fibrosis, and regression-phase mouse livers, generating a high-resolution atlas of liver fibrosis progression and regression. We identified the transcriptomic patterns of regressed cell subpopulations and uncovered two key therapeutic targets: the macrophage-derived factor semaphorin-4D (SEMA4D) and the hepatic stellate cell-specific transcription factor LIM and cysteine-rich domains 1 (LMCD1). Treatment with a humanised monoclonal IgG4 antibody against SEMA4D significantly alleviated liver fibrosis in the thioacetamide-induced mouse model. SEMA4D and LMCD1 expression correlated with the metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease-related fibrosis stage, and the attenuation of SEMA4D and LMCD1 after HCV-sustained virologic response reduced the risk of progression to hepatocellular carcinoma.

Keywords: Recovered hepatocytes, RARRES2, Regression-phase LSECs, Immune recovery, HCV-SVR

## Introduction
Fibrosis is a prevalent pathological condition associated with advanced chronic liver disease induced by hepatitis virus infection, alcohol abuse, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, often leading to decompensated cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Over the past four decades, remarkable advancements have been made in understanding the biological mechanisms underlying liver fibrosis progression and regression; however, urgent unmet medical needs have yet to yield effective therapies for liver fibrosis resolution to improve the clinical outcomes of patients. , 
Although our understanding of the cellular mechanisms involved in liver fibrosis progression and regression has expanded, the mechanism underlying fibrosis regression remains poorly understood compared with fibrosis progression; research in this field has focused predominantly on functional analyses of hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) , , , and a comprehensive understanding of the role of cell–cell interactions among hepatic constituent cells, remains largely unknown. Current pharmacological therapies are typically prophylactic during the preclinical induction of chronic liver injury or are administered shortly after the establishment of mild disease in mice. However, in clinical trials, drugs need to be developed that can be administered to patients with already established stage 3 or 4 fibrosis and have the pharmacological effect of eliminating fibrosis bundles.
Recent advancements have opened new avenues for a more nuanced understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying liver fibrosis. Notably, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has been used to create comprehensive atlases of hepatic constituent cells in both human and mouse models of intact and diseased livers. , , However, sample quality remains a critical factor that influences sequencing outcomes and data analysis. Enzymatic digestion of whole liver tissue requires careful perfusion of solutions at specific flow rates, as improper perfusion can lead to tissue damage and alterations in gene expression profile. , One notable limitation of single-nucleus RNA-seq (snRNA-seq) is its inability to sequence cytoplasmic RNA, as it exclusively collects and analyses nuclear transcripts. In contrast, single-cell fixed RNA sequencing (FLEX) represents an innovative approach for isolating cells from frozen tissues and preserving RNA using paraformaldehyde (PFA) fixation. Our group is the first to apply FLEX to frozen liver tissues which enables high transcript yield, recovering a significant number of hepatocytes along with other cells, and reducing mitochondrial gene bias. This is a great compromise between the need for fresh tissue (i.e. scRNA-seq) and losing cytoplasmic RNA (i.e. snRNA-seq from archived tissue) and could become a widely used tool for other investigators studying liver biology. FLEX is particularly advantageous for studying rare cell subpopulations, and offers researchers increased flexibility in high-throughput scRNA-seq applications. 
To gain comprehensive insights into the phenotypic alterations of liver cell types and their interactions contributing to fibrosis progression and regression, we are the first group to use this FLEX method on frozen liver tissues from normal, thioacetamide (TAA)-induced cirrhosis and its regression phases in mice. Our analysis revealed phenotypic alterations in 10 primary hepatic cells, along with their interactions during fibrosis progression and regression. The results obtained from this study could contribute to the development of effective antifibrotic therapies by targeting LIM and cysteine-rich domains 1 (LMCD1) and semaphorin-4D (SEMA4D).

## Results

## FLEX comprehensively unveiled the hepatic cell population atlas during liver fibrosis progression and regression in a TAA-induced mouse model
To elucidate the cellular landscape of liver fibrosis progression and regression, we used a TAA-induced mouse model of advanced liver fibrosis. TAA was administered for 10 weeks ( ) leading to significant liver injury ( ), characterised by bridging fibrosis between the portal and central veins, accompanied by an increase in ACTA2+ activated HSCs, marked CD68+ macrophage infiltration, and proliferation of Ki67+ hepatocytes ( ).
To dissect cell type-specific dynamics and intercellular interactions involved in liver fibrosis progression and regression, we performed FLEX using the 10X Chromium platform, with three biological replicates per group ( A). shows contrast and propidium iodide-stained images of cell nuclei from dissociated cells in the healthy, fibrosis, and regression groups of our FLEX samples. Following integration via Seurat's reciprocal principal component analysis method and normalisation at the gene and cell levels, we obtained transcriptomic data from 38,136 cells, of which 13,948, 14,986, and 9,202 cells were from the control, fibrosis, and regression groups, respectively, with an average sequencing depth of 1,637 genes per cell ( ). Based on canonical markers, we annotated 10 major hepatic cell types ( B–D), including hepatocytes (HEPs) expressing Alb, Ttr, Trf, Serpina1a1, Hnf4a, and Aox3; liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) expressing Kdr, Aqp1, Pecam1, and Eng; macrophages (MACs) including Kupffer cells expressing Vsig4, Adgre1, Clec4f, Lgals3, Ccr2, and Cd68; HSCs expressing Rgs5, Colec11, Lrat, Dcn, Cygb, and Des; cholangiocytes (CHOLs) expressing Epcam, Krt7, Spp1, and Krt19; B cells expressing Cd79a, Cd79b, Ebf1, and Ms4a1; natural killer and T cells (NK&T cells) expressing Cd3d, Il7r, Nkg7, and Gzma; mesothelial cells expressing Msln and Upk3b; neutrophils (NEUs) expressing Cxcr2 and S100a9; and dendritic cells (DCs) expressing Clec9a, Siglech, and Cox6a2 ( E; ). Differential abundance analysis of cell-type proportions across the three experimental groups revealed that fibrotic livers exhibited significantly increased proportions of MACs, immune cells (B cells, NK/T cells, NEUs, and DCs), CHOLs, and HSCs, alongside a reduced abundance of HEPs and LSECs compared with control and regression groups ( F; ). These results establish a high-resolution atlas of cell populations across liver disease states.
 

## RecoHEPs: A pericentral hepatocyte subpopulation with xenobiotic and antioxidant functions in fibrosis regression
To resolve HEPs heterogeneity, we re-clustered the hepatocytes across conditions into 20 subclusters ( A and B; ). Fibrotic livers showed an increased proportion of cells in the G2/M phase ( ), consistent with Ki67 staining ( ). Clusters S0, 8, and 16 were enriched in regressed hepatocytes ( ). Given the importance of hepatocytes zonation in liver function and disease progression, we analysed the zonation using pericentral (PC) and periportal (PP) signature scores mapped to each HEP ( C and D; ).
 A distinct subcluster S0, termed recoHEPsCyp2e1, Cyp1a2, Glul, Oat, Mgst1, Txn1, Gulo and Car3, located in the PC area, expressed high levels of xenobiotic-related (Cyp2e1 and Cyp1a2), metabolic-related (Glu and Oat), and antioxidant-related (Mgst1, Txn1, Gulo, and Car3) genes ( E). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed that these S0-recoHEPs were functionally similar to S7 from control PC hepatocytes ( ). Heatmap and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis of the transcriptional profiles of PC and PP hepatocytes from all groups clearly showed that PC hepatocytes in the recovery group exhibited a robust activation of central metabolic genes and antioxidant signalling ( F; ). Furthermore, IHC for PC markers (GLUL, CYP2E1, CYP1A2, and OAT) confirmed Zone 3 localisation, which was diminished during fibrosis progression and significantly restored during regression, whereas Zone 1 markers (CYP2F2 and HAL) increased during fibrosis and declined during regression ( G).
MultiNicheNet analysis predicted the upregulation of the top 50 ligand–receptor pairs during regression, with Rarres2–Ccrl2 emerging as a prominent axis mediating signals from HEPs to HSCs and among HSCs themselves ( ). Rarres2 was expressed in both pericentral HEPs and HSCs in healthy livers ( ). Spatial transcriptomics of published human liver datasets showed that RARRES2 was expressed in pericentral hepatocytes in healthy human liver ( ). Validation experiments demonstrated that RARRES2 expression was high in healthy livers, reduced in fibrotic livers, and restored during regression in both tissues and serum ( H and I; ). In primary mouse HSCs (mHSCs), Rarres2 expression was highest in quiescent HSCs and significantly decreased during activation ( J). Functional assays further showed that RARRES2 knockdown using siRNAs in primary human HSCs (HHSteCs) increased the expression of fibrosis-related genes (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL5A1, FN1, LMCD1, ACTA2, and MYL9) ( K). These findings reveal a specific phenotype of PC HEPs in liver fibrosis regression and Rarres2 acts not only as a marker of pericentral hepatocytes and quiescent HSCs, but also as a functional mediator involved in orchestrating fibrosis resolution.

## Remodelling of LSECs during fibrosis regression
Although zonation-specific changes in LSECs under normal and fibrotic conditions have been reported, , , their behaviour during regression remains unclear. We re-clustered LSECs from control, fibrotic, and regressed livers into nine transcriptionally distinct subclusters ( A and B; ). Heatmap revealed distinct gene expression profiles among the groups ( ) and cell composition analysis revealed enrichment of S2–S4 in healthy livers, S1 and S5 in fibrotic livers, S0 in regressed livers, S6 in regressed and healthy livers, and S7/S8 across all groups ( C). Zonation mapping indicated that S7 (Adgrg6, Vegfc, Jag1, and Npr3) and S8 (Rspo3, Wnt9b, Bmp4, and Selp) represented portal and central ECs, respectively ( D). Spatial signatures showed S2/S4 as PP/midzonal (Adam23, Msr1, Cd36, and Lyve1), and S3 as PC (Wnt2, Thbd, and Kit). Healthy clusters (S2–S4) were enriched in pathways related to small GTPase signalling, Ras, adhesion, and metabolism ( E). In contrast, fibrotic clusters (S1 and S5) displayed reduced homeostatic gene expression and increased angiogenesis, endothelial migration, and extracellular matrix (ECM)-related gene expression. S1 was enriched in inflammatory/apoptotic genes (Cdkn1a, Bax, and Il1a), and S5 in ECM components (Col1a1, Col1a2, and Ednrb) ( ; ), resembling human fibrotic LSECs ( ).
 Regression-phase LSECs exhibited heterogeneity: S0 retained injury features, whereas S6 resembled healthy S2. Regression clusters showed the upregulation of ECM-degrading genes (Ctsl and Mmp14), interferon-stimulated genes (Ifitm1–3), and antioxidant genes (Selenop and Txnip) ( F). Monocle 2 trajectory and hierarchical clustering illustrated recovery-associated transitions ( G; ), with restored expression of genes involved in antioxidant defence (Pon1), lipid metabolism (Slc27a2), and nitrogen clearance (Tdo2, Gnmt, and Otc).
Flow cytometry analysis showed that CD146+ LSECs decreased from 32.1% in controls to 13% in fibrosis, partially restored to 25.8% in regression ( H, top). Scanning electron microscopy revealed fenestrae recovery, with decreased P21 and re-expressed LYVE1 ( H, bottom). Cytokine arrays showed elevated angiokines (including HGF, MMP9, and VCAM) in fibrotic LSECs, which decreased in regression. In contrast, CCL21 and resistin were lower in both fibrosis and regression phases ( I).
Finally, ligand–receptor predictions revealed key axes in regression: Vwf–Itga2b, promoting platelet adhesion; 
Timp3–Kdr, inhibiting VEGF-induced angiogenesis; and St6gal1–Egfr, involved in cell migration ( J). These findings revealed LSECs’ zonal remodelling and regenerative plasticity during fibrosis regression.

## Cholangiocytes contribute to fibrosis progression via cytokine production
Compared with healthy controls, TAA treatment increased the number of CK19+ CHOLs, which decreased following 2 weeks of regression ( ). Re-clustering of the CHOLs revealed six subclusters ( A–B; ), all of which expressed Krt7, Krt19, Epcam, Cd24a, Sox9, and Spp1 ( D). Fibrotic CHOLs were distributed across S0, S2, and S4; with S2 enriched in profibrotic and proinflammatory genes such as Lcn2, Scube3, Thbs1, Tgfb2, Pdgfrb, Cxcl16, Tnfrsf12a, Icam1, and Vcam1 ( C and D), along with cytokine-mediated signalling pathways ( E, left panel). The recovered CHOLs were mainly enriched in S3 and S5 ( C) in the negative regulation of programmed cell death and triglyceride metabolic processes ( E, middle and right panels; ). Trajectory analysis of CHOLs from fibrosis to regression ( F) revealed that Mmp7 was the gene with the greatest degree of downregulation ( G). Volcano plot analysis of the differentially expressed genes also revealed that Mmp7 was markedly upregulated in fibrotic livers compared with both control ( H, upper panel) and recovered livers ( H, lower panel) and was confirmed at the mRNA ( I, left panel) and serum levels ( I, right panel). Serum MMP7 levels were significantly increased in all other mouse models of liver fibrosis induced by carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) or liver cholestasis induced by DDC, bile duct ligation, or CDAA diet-induced steatohepatitis ( ). Transcription factor analysis revealed that CHOLs S2 upregulated inflammatory intracellular signals, including Nfkb1-2, Nfkbiz, Rela, Relb, Ikbkb, Ikbke, Ikbkg, Traf1-5, Tnfaip1-3, Tradd, Fadd, Ripk1-4, and Irak1-2 ( J). We further investigated the CHOLs-specific secretomes by cytokine profiling of primary isolated and cultured EPCAM+CHOLs, and found that, compared with other groups, fibrotic CHOLs secreted various cytokines and chemokines (red boxes), including IGFBP6 and osteoprotegerin (OPG, encoded by the Tnfrsf11b gene) ( K, circle boxes). Increased expression of MMP7, IGFBP6, and TNFRSF11B was also observed in the scar area of human PSCs compared with that of healthy donors via spatial transcriptomic analysis of published datasets (GSE245620 and GSE240029) ( ). Overall, CHOLs from regressed livers were enriched in the negative regulation of programmed cell death and triglyceride metabolic processes, whereas those from fibrotic livers secreted inflammatory cytokines that contributed to fibrosis progression.
 

## SEMA4D+ macrophages are key drivers of liver inflammation and fibrosis
To explore macrophage heterogeneity in fibrosis, we analysed macrophages from control, fibrotic, and regression-phase livers, and identified 12 transcriptionally distinct subclusters ( A). Based on canonical markers , subclusters S1–S5 and S7 (Mrc1+Adgre1+Cd163+Timd4+Folr2+Marco+) were classified as resident Kupffer cells (KC1), while S0, S6, and S8 (Mrc1+Adgre1+Trem2+Gpnmb+Cd63+Folr2-) were designated KC2. Proliferating KC3 (S10) co-expressed KC1 and KC2 markers with Mki67 and Top2a. Monocyte-derived macrophages (Momacs; S9 and S11) expressed Itgam and Fcgr2b ( B; , ).
 Flow cytometry analysis confirmed the dynamic distribution of subsets: KC1 (CD45+F4/80+CD11b-MHCII+FOLR2+) predominated in healthy livers, decreased in fibrotic livers, and rebounded during regression. KC2 (CD45+F4/80+CD11b-MHCII+FOLR2-) and Momacs (CD45+CD11b+F4/80-Ly6G-) significantly expanded during fibrosis but declined in regression ( C and D; , ). KC3 could not be separated from KC1 by flow cytometry because of the intracellular Ki67 marker. Neutrophils (CD45+F4/80-CD11b+Ly6G+) showed similar dynamics ( D, bottom).
Morphologically, Momacs displayed an enlarged, heterogeneous cytoplasm ( ). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that KC1 was associated with metabolic homeostasis; KC2 and Momacs were associated with inflammatory pathways; and KC3 was associated with mitotic division ( , ). Cytokine profiling showed KC2 and Momacs secreted high levels of inflammatory mediators (including MCP-1, IL-1β, TNFα, MMP9, and OPN), while KC1s secreted noninflammatory DPP4 (CD26) ( ). KC2 also upregulated MMPs, ADAMs, and cathepsins signatures ( ), and the regressed macrophages showed increased complement and phagocytosis-related genes ( ).
Sema4d was prominent in KC2 and Momacs ( E), and trajectory analysis topically implicated it in Momacs ( ). Sema4d mRNA and protein levels were elevated in both KC2 and Momacs ( F, two left panels; ), conditioned medium, serum ( F, two right panels) and liver tissues ( G). Moreover, validated data from other mouse models of liver fibrosis showed the consistent results with a markedly increase of SEMA4D-positive area in the livers under CCl4 administration and bile duct ligation, compared with controls ( ).
When the signalling communication probability was examined by CellChat analysis on Sema4d–Plxnb2 signalling, we found that while the main communication probability between DCs and other cell types did not change among groups, the communication probability of DCs-HSCs and MACs–HSCs changed. In particular, the crosstalk between MACs and HSCs via Sema4d–Plxnb2 signalling was not found in the control but was observed in the fibrotic group and was lost in the recovery phase ( H; ). Although Plexin B1/2 was widely expressed ( ), MultiNicheNet ranked Sema4d–Plxnb2 as the top MACs–HSCs interaction in fibrosis ( ). Plxnb2 expression was induced in HSCs under fibrotic conditions and reduced after regression ( ). In vitro, PLXNB2 and fibrosis markers (ACTA2 and COL1A1) increased in activated HSCs over time ( ), and recombinant human SEMA4D significantly induced COL1A1 expression in LX2 cells ( I).
Therapeutic blockade of SEMA4D with monoclonal antibody VX15/2503 (0.5–5 mg/kg) in TAA-induced models reduced fibrosis, ductular reactions (CK19), HSCs activation (ACTA2, DESMIN), inflammation (CD68), and proliferation (Ki67) ( J–K; ).
Taken together, SEMA4D from KC2 and Momacs is a key driver of hepatic inflammation and fibrosis. Its inhibition reverses fibrotic features, offering a promising therapeutic strategy.

## LMCD1+ HSCs regulates fibrosis progression via AKT-mTOR signalling
To investigate HSCs heterogeneity across liver states, we subclustered HSCs from control, fibrotic, and regressed livers into nine subsets (S0–S8; A–C), based on validated murine HSCs markers. In healthy livers, HSCs were enriched in clusters S2, S3, and S8, expressing Reln, Tnxb, Colec10, Fga, and Vipr1, with functional enrichment in cell–cell junction assembly (S2), fibrinolysis and plasminogen activation (S3), peptide hormone response and lipid metabolism (S8) ( C–E; ). In contrast, fibrotic HSCs were localised in clusters S0 and S4, marked by canonical fibrogenic genes (Col1a1, Col1a2, Col3a1, Eln, Lox, and Col15a1). S0 was associated with ECM organisation, whereas S4 was enriched in integrin-mediated signalling (Lama1, Lamb1, Lamc1, Steap4, and Notch3).
 In regressed livers, clusters S5 and S6 showed reduced fibrogenic gene expression but upregulated expression of inactivated-related genes (Cadm3, Agtr1a, Ehd3, Tmem56, Alcam, Emp1, and Tcf21), with enrichment in vasculogenesis, vessel morphogenesis, and ECM remodelling ( D–E). S1 and S7 contained HSCs from all conditions and were enriched in triglyceride metabolism and elastic fibre assembly. Recent evidence indicates that HSCs govern liver zonation and cellular homeostasis via the secretion of RSPO3, HGF, NTF3, BMP9-10, and ECM1. , Our transcriptomic analysis revealed that while the expression of Hgf, Rspo3, and Ntf3 was highest in control HSCs and remained suppressed in the recovery group ( ), other key mediators – specifically Bmp9/10 (signalling to LSECs and Kupffer cells), Ecm1, and Rarres2 – were successfully regained during regression, reaching levels similar to those in control HSCs. These results suggest that while some homeostatic functions of HSCs are rapidly restored during fibrosis regression, others may require a longer recovery period to fully return to baseline.
Trajectory analysis revealed a phenotypic shift during regression, with downregulated activation, and upregulated inactivation genes ( F–G). Interestingly, we found that LIM domain-containing proteins – FHL2, FHL3, PDLIM2, PDLIM7, LIMK2, LIMA1, and LMCD1 – were upregulated in fibrotic HSCs and downregulated during regression ( H). In vitro validation using primary mHSCs confirmed the increased expression of these genes at Days 4 and 7 post-isolation, at both mRNA and protein levels ( I; ). In HHSteCs, spontaneous activation (S– condition) or rhTGF-β1 (2 ng/ml) stimulation significantly upregulated LMCD1, LIMK2, PDLIM5, and PDLIM7 at both transcriptional and protein levels ( ). LMCD1 expression was induced by rhTGF-β1 and was suppressed by SB431542, a TGFβ receptor inhibitor ( ). IHC staining showed LMCD1 localised predominantly to the fibrotic septa and reduced expression in fibrosis regression ( ). In published single-cell datasets from healthy and non-alcoholic steatohepatitic (NASH) livers (GSE212837), 
LMCD1 and other LIM domain genes were upregulated in fibrotic HSCs ( ). LMCD1 was specifically expressed in HSCs in a published human fibrotic livers dataset ( ). Double immunofluorescence of human metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver diseases (MASLD) confirmed the colocalisation of LMCD1 with ACTA2 and VIMENTIN ( ).
Functionally, LMCD1 knockdown using two siRNAs (95% knockdown efficiency) in HHSteCs suppressed the expression of fibrosis-related genes (COL1A1, COL5A1, FN1, ACTA2, MYL9, NDGR1, and EIF4EBP1) and proteins (COL1A1, FN1, and ACTA2) ( J and K). In contrast, adenoviral LMCD1 overexpression (AdLMCD1) promoted significant fibrogenic genes (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, COL5A1, COL6A3, ACTA2, FN1, MYL9, EIF4EBP1, and NDGR1) ( L). Phosphokinase array analysis revealed enhanced AKT phosphorylation (p-AKTSer473) in LMCD1-overexpressing HHSteCs ( M). Immunoblotting confirmed that LMCD1 overexpression induced COL1A1, ACTA2, and p-AKTSer473 expression in HHSteCs in a time-dependent manner ( N).
String database enrichment analysis of phosphoprotein expression by sandwich immunoassay in HHSteCs transfected with AdGFP or AdLMCD1 vectors showed that LMCD1 overexpression upregulated proteins related to TOR, TORC1, and TORC2 complexes ( A and B). Pharmacological mTOR inhibition using AZD8055 in LMCD1-overexpressing HHSteCs suppressed COL1A1, ACTA2, and LMCD1 expression; and downregulated 16 of 37 phosphoproteins including CREB, c-JUN, TP53, WNK1, GSK3B, ERK1/2, P70S6K, and RSK1/2/3 ( C and D). In culture conditions, treatment of AZD8055 also mitigated primary mHSCs activation spontaneously ( E) or HHSteCs under spontaneous activation by culture without supplement (S-) ( F) or rhTGFβ-1 induction ( G) in a dose-dependent manner. Collectively, these findings indicate that LMCD1+ HSCs modulate fibrogenesis by activating the AKT-mTOR signalling axis.
 

## Immune cells signature in liver fibrosis regression
Although immune cells are known for their roles in liver fibrosis progression, their contribution to regression remains underexplored. We investigated NK & T cells, NEUs, and B cells during fibrosis regression.
Clustering of NK&T cells from the control, TAA-treated, and regression-phase livers revealed eight subclusters ( ; ). Subclusters expressing Cd3d, Cd3e, and Cd3g were annotated as T cells, whereas cluster 1 expressing Gzma and Ncr1 represented NK cells. Notably, subclusters S5 and S6 originated primarily from regressed livers. S6 expressed Cd3d, Cd4, Cd8a, Sell, and S1pr1 but lacked Cd44, consistent with naïve T cells. S5 expressed Cd8 and Cd44 but lacked Sell and S1pr1, suggesting the presence of CD8+ memory T cells ( ).
GO enrichment analysis revealed that S6 upregulated pathways associated with T-cell activation, endothelial barrier formation, and cytokine response, whereas S5 was enriched in antigen receptor signalling and NK chemotaxis ( , ). Cytokine-related genes such as Il17ra, Il6ra, Il7r, Cxcr6, Ifngr1, Ccl5, and Gzmk were highly expressed in both clusters, along with S2 ( ). Transcription factors Mef2a, Mef2b, and Mef2c, which regulate mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation and T-cell differentiation, , were upregulated in S6 ( ). Cell–cell interaction analysis revealed enhanced CCL and CXCL signalling networks in regressed livers ( ). Flow cytometry confirmed recovery of CD4+, CD8+, and NK1.1+ populations, supporting the presence of naïve and memory T cells, while Foxp3+ Tregs remained stable ( ).
B cells formed six subclusters (S0–S5; ), with S0 was enriched in regressed livers. GO enrichment analysis indicated the upregulation of cytoskeletal regulation, intracellular transport, BCR signalling, glucose metabolism, and mRNA splicing ( , ). Transcription factor analysis showed increased Ets1 and Spi1 expression in S0, both essential for B-cell development and BCR signalling , ( ), highlighting their role in immune restoration.
NEUs were re-clustered into four subpopulations (S0–S3; ), with S3 was enriched in regressed livers. GO enrichment analysis revealed the activation of the Toll-like receptor, IL-12, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolism pathways ( ). Transcription factors Stat3, Stat5b, Spi1, and Cebpa were highly expressed in S3 ( ).
Thus, our data revealed that immune recovery during fibrosis regression involves naïve and memory T-cell restoration, B-cell activation and signalling, and NEUs-mediated immunoregulation, suggesting that these immune subsets support tissue repair and the resolution of inflammation.

## Cell–cell communication networks driving fibrosis progression and regression
We performed a global ligand–receptor network analysis using CellChat to characterise the intercellular interactions associated with fibrosis progression and regression. We focused on the interactions between all other cell types and HSCs. Bubble plot analysis comparing the probability of ligand–receptor interactions between TAA-treated cells and control cells revealed that under healthy conditions, the ligand‒receptor reactions (red font) in order from the strongest one were from HSCs, mesothelial cells, CHOLs, LSECs, and HEPs to HSCs, whereas other cell types, including MACs, NEUs, NK&T, and B cells, and DCs, showed no significant interactions with HSCs ( ). In contrast, TAA-treated HSCs received strong incoming signals (interactions in blue font) from all other cell types, with the strongest signal originating from autocrine interactions. shows that the interaction strengths were diminished during the regression phase (interactions in blue font).
To further investigate the altered intercellular signalling, CellChat was used to plot the predicted relative information flow of significantly altered pathways in TAA vs. control ( ) and TAA vs. recovered liver cells ( ). Pathways that were significantly regulated in healthy livers but not in TAA-treated liver cells, such as FGF, IL-1, and CEACAM, were also found in recovered liver cells (black arrows).
CellChat revealed that the majority of the outgoing signalling patterns among all groups were from HSCs, which increased during fibrosis progression ( ), whereas the LSECs population demonstrated increased outgoing signalling in the recovered cells. Exploring incoming signalling patterns, the results revealed that MACs were the main receiver cells in all groups ( ), HEPs were the secondary receiver cells in healthy livers, and DCs, NEUs and LSECs were the main receiver cells contributing to the signalling of recovered cells.

## Elevated LMCD1 and SEMA4D expression in human liver fibrosis decreases after HCV with sustained virologic response and is associated with reduced HCC risk
To evaluate the translational relevance of SEMA4D and LMCD1 in human liver fibrosis, we performed IHC staining for SEMA4D and LMCD1 in liver biopsy samples from patients with MASLD and chronic HCV infection, as well as in the PT regions of HCC specimens. We found that SEMA4D expression was significantly increased in advanced fibrosis and positively correlated with the fibrosis stage in both the MASLD and HCV cohorts. Consistent with the tissue findings, serum SEMA4D levels were also elevated in patients with MASLD ( A and B).
 Similarly, LMCD1 was predominantly localised within the fibrotic septa, and its expression showed a strong positive correlation with fibrosis severity in both MASLD and HCV cohorts ( C and D).
Next, we examined the expression of LMCD1, SEMA4D, and ductular reaction marker CK19 in paired liver biopsy samples obtained before and after sustained virological response (SVR). This analysis included 18 patients who achieved SVR without subsequent HCC development and six patients who developed HCC after SVR. In patients who developed HCC following SVR, the expression of LMCD1, SEMA4D, and CK19 remained persistently elevated. In contrast, patients who did not develop HCC exhibited marked reductions in LMCD1, SEMA4D, and CK19 expression after achieving SVR ( E).
In summary, using fixed single-cell RNA sequencing, we constructed a high-resolution cellular atlas and their interactions across healthy, fibrotic, and regressed mouse livers. Our integrative analyses identified key subpopulations and their interactions that orchestrate both the progression and resolution of liver fibrosis. Notably, we identified SEMA4D (from Momacs) and LMCD1 (from HSCs) as novel regulators of fibrosis and promising therapeutic targets.

## Discussion
This is one of the first studies to apply FLEX to liver fibrosis models. Unlike conventional scRNA-seq, which may introduce dissociation artefacts, FLEX preserves native transcriptional profiles by fixing tissues before dissociation. This approach is critical for fragile cells such as HEPs and LSECs and allows the retention of cells from necrotic and inflamed regions. FLEX enabled the detection of rare but relevant populations, such as recoHEPs, restorative LSECs, inactivated HSCs, and proresolving immune subsets, while preserving the morphology of both intracellular and nuclear RNA, facilitating downstream sequencing and data analysis.
In HEPs, we identified recoHEPs, a PC subpopulation enriched in detoxification and antioxidant genes (including Cyp2e1 and Txn1), indicating functional regeneration. These recoHEPs expressed Rarres2 along with regressed HSCs. Silencing RARRES2 in HSCs increased the expression of fibrosis-related genes in vitro.
Regression-phase LSECs displayed re-fenestration and upregulation of matrix-degrading (Mmp14, Ctsl) and antioxidant (Pon1, Tcn2) genes. These findings align with previous reports on VEGF–MMP2/14-driven fibrosis resolution and expand on the role of LSECs in antioxidant and ECM remodelling.
The MACs landscape also shifted dynamically. We identified KC1, KC2, and Momacs with distinct roles in fibrosis. KC1 dominated in regression, expressing the complement and antioxidant genes. This contrasts with the proinflammatory Momacs seen in fibrosis progression, as reported by Krenkel et al. A previous study by Wang et al. showed that SEMA4D was expressed in activated HSCs, and SEMA4D knockout studies demonstrating reduced fibrosis through the suppression of AOX1 in retinol metabolism and modulation of T-cell subsets. Elevated SEMA4D serum levels in patients with MASLD correlated with fibrosis severity and have also been implicated in parasitic liver fibrosis, such as schistosomiasis. While Wang et al. suggested SEMA4D expression in activated HSCs, our single-cell FLEX profiling reveals that SEMA4D is predominantly expressed in immune cells, specifically macrophages (MACs), NKT cells, and B cells, rather than HSCs. A deeper analysis of MACs subpopulations showed that SEMA4D is not expressed in resident Kupffer cells but is highly enriched in KC2 and Momacs. This secreted factor interacts with the PLXNB2 receptor on HSCs to promote COL1A1 production. Furthermore, we demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition using VX15/2503 (anti-SEMA4D) at 0.5–5 mg/kg significantly reduced Sirius Red-positive fibrosis, ductular reaction (CK19), HSC activation (ACTA2, DESMIN), MAC infiltration (CD68), and HEP proliferation (Ki67) in vivo. These results confirm the SEMA4D–PLXNB2 signalling axis as a central driver of the MAC–HSC crosstalk. Given its correlation with fibrotic stage and preclinical success in models of rheumatoid arthritis and neurological disease trials, SEMA4D has strong translational potential for liver fibrosis.
CHOLs showed disease-stage specific profiles. A fibrogenic S2 subset during fibrosis expressed Mmp7 and Pdgfb, which decreased during regression. These findings are consistent with prior biliary expansion observations but now establish a link between CHOLs-derived cytokines and fibrogenesis.
HSCs exhibited transcriptional diversity across stages. Regression-specific clusters upregulated the inactivation genes (Cadm3 and Tcf21) and downregulated fibrosis-related genes. Among fibrotic HSCs, we identified LMCD1, a LIM domain transcriptional co-regulator, as an activated marker. A previous study by De Minicis et al. demonstrated that Lmcd1 expression is upregulated in activated mouse HSCs, particularly when co-cultured with macrophages. However, the functional significance of LMCD1 in regulating HSC activation remained unclear. In this study, we demonstrate that silencing LMCD1 reduces the expression of key profibrotic proteins (COL1A1, ACTA2, and FN1), whereas LMCD1 overexpression promotes collagen gene expression via AKT signalling. Furthermore, pharmacological inhibition of the LMCD1–AKT–mTOR axis using AZD8055 significantly reduced HSC activation. LMCD1’s profibrotic role aligns with studies on lung and kidney fibrosis, , where it modulates ECM remodelling and inflammation. LMCD1 expression correlated with the fibrosis stage of MASLD and HCV cohort, and high LMCD1 expression after HCV-SVR predicted HCC progression.
Regression-phase livers exhibited recovery of CD4+, CD8+, and NK cells, with upregulation of T-cell activation and endothelial barrier-associated genes. Enriched populations included naïve T cells (Cd3d+, Cd4+, Cd8a+, Sell+, and S1pr1+) and CD8+ memory T cells (Cd8+, Cd44+), resembling KLRG1+ NK cells with antifibrotic potential observed in chronic HBV infection patients. The recovered B cells and NEUs shifted toward anti-inflammatory states, marked by enhanced ROS regulation and BCR signalling pathways.
Despite these key insights, our study has some limitations. First, the TAA-induced fibrosis model may not fully reflect the heterogeneity of human fibrotic liver diseases such as MASLD or viral hepatitis. We cross-referenced our single-cell clusters with published human datasets including matching LSEC signatures to human fibrotic LSECs and validating HSCs markers in the NASH datasets. Furthermore, validation of the mouse findings in humans revealed that SEMA4D levels were elevated in the serum of patients with advanced fibrosis and MASLD, correlating with fibrotic stage. We confirmed LMCD1 expression in human tissues, specifically showing that it localises to fibrotic septa in the MASLD and HCC cohorts and correlates with fibrosis severity. Nevertheless, broader patient-derived data and further integration with spatial transcriptomics are required to fully translate these mechanistic insights into human pathology. Second, FLEX captures transcriptomic states but not temporal dynamics; integration with spatial transcriptomics or lineage tracing would enhance mechanistic insights. Third, in vivo knockout studies of SEMA4D and LMCD1 are required to confirm causality.

## Conclusions
This study presents a detailed single-cell atlas of liver fibrosis progression and regression using FLEX, revealing the complex intercellular crosstalk and novel therapeutic targets. We identified SEMA4D and LMCD1 as the central regulators of fibrosis with high translational relevance. These findings lay the foundation for precise antifibrotic therapies, driven by deep cellular insights into liver regeneration.
