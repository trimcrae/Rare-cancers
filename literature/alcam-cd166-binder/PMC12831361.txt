Single-cell sequencing, machine learning, and Mendelian randomization have emerged in recent years as excellent methods for identifying tumour markers. We identified 3 genes (RAB3B, SPON2, and SLC4A4) through single-cell sequencing and a machine learning screen. Among them, RAB3B (Ras-related protein, Rab-3B) had a higher area under the diagnostic curve for PSA < 10 ng/mL PCa (0.763) than the other two genes did. Mendelian randomization analysis revealed that the occurrence of PCa led to an increase in circulating levels of the RAB3B protein. Western blot and immunohistochemical analyses demonstrated that RAB3B was overexpressed in PCa tumour. Subsequently, elevated levels of RAB3B were detected in the culture medium of PCa cells. Furthermore, urinary RAB3B protein levels were significantly increased in PCa patients. For patients with PSA levels < 10 ng/mL, urinary RAB3B exhibited an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.733 for PCa diagnosis, outperforming both PSA concentration (AUC = 0.682) and age (AUC = 0.651). Notably, the AUC of the multivariate model urine RAB3B + PSA + Age is 0.8. GSEA at al analysis and functional experiments, including gain-of-function and loss-of-function studies revealed that RAB3B promotes the proliferation and migration of PCa cells. In summary, urinary RAB3B, discovered through a multiomics approach, is a promising biomarker that is complementary to serum PSA, and together these factors can improve diagnostic decision-making in the PSA grey zone.

## Introduction
Prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most prevalent malignancy in men worldwide, with approximately 1.4 million new cases and 370,000 deaths in 2020. The incidence of PCa is increasing because of the ageing population, with a reported increase of 42% in recent years [ , ]. Primary diagnostic methods for PCa include prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing, tumour tissue biopsy, and magnetic resonance imaging. Although PSA remains a widely used biomarker, its diagnostic accuracy for PCa in individuals with PSA concentrations less than 10 ng/mL is limited to 25%−40% [ ]. Despite the potential for active surveillance to customize treatment on the basis of disease progression, there is no consensus on the optimal follow-up strategy [ ].
Ultrasound-guided biopsy is the gold standard for acquiring histopathological data to diagnose PCa, but the difficulty in sample collection presents a significant diagnostic challenge [ ]. Moreover, complications of prostate biopsy, such as bleeding and infection, can lead to extended hospital stays. Compared with histopathological diagnosis, liquid biopsies and biomarkers derived from body fluids reduce the risks that come with invasive procedures and provide valuable insights into disease progression. Blood tests derived from PSA, such as the Prostate Health Index (PHI), and urine tests such as the Mi-prostate score (MiPS) and SelectMDx, which require prior digital rectal examination, have been developed mainly for patients with a PSA concentration above 10 ng/mL [ – ]. Therefore, identifying simple and accurate biomarkers for diagnosing PCa in individuals with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL, such as blood or urine markers, is crucial for improving patient outcomes and reducing health care burden.
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) allows for the analysis of gene expression at the level of individual cells or cell populations, providing precise gene expression data and aiding in cell classification and clustering. The process involves cell capture, reverse transcription, library construction, and data analysis [ ]. This technology enables a greater understanding of the gene expression profiles of specific cell populations, revealing their diversity and functional characteristics, thus offering novel and deeper insights into the diseases studied [ ]. Given its ability to minimize interference from other cell populations, scRNA-seq has become an essential tool for identifying tumour markers and understanding tumour development and immune responses [ , ]. Despite its strengths, scRNA-seq faces challenges such as variability in gene expression, transcriptional noise, and batch effects. To address these issues, researchers have incorporated machine learning into single-cell analysis, selecting optimal models from a set of alternatives to fit observational data while reducing uncertainties from gene expression variability and batch effects [ ]. This integration enhances the precision and reliability of the results [ – ]. Additionally, Mendelian randomization (MR), which uses single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as instrumental variables, has proven effective in establishing causal relationships between various biomolecules and cancer, further validating the role of emerging biomarkers [ ].
In this study, on the basis of the results of scRNA-seq, the profiles of PCa tissues from patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL and healthy prostate tissues were analysed, and machine learning was used to identify the diagnostic gene Ras-related protein (RAB3B). MR analysis indicated that PCa promotes increased circulating levels of the RAB3B protein. We detected increased levels of RAB3B in tumour cells and in the urine of patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL, with the diagnostic efficacy of urinary RAB3B surpassing that of PSA level and age. Finally, we conducted functional, metabolic, and cellular communication analyses to understand the role of RAB3B in PSA < 10 ng/mL PCa.

## Methods

## Public data sources
Single-cell data of tumour and healthy prostate tissues were obtained from GSE181294 in the GEO database. This dataset includes 17 tumour samples, 13 of which had an initial PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL and 4 of which had an initial PSA concentration >10 ng/mL, and 4 healthy prostate samples. Detailed information about the samples is shown in Supplementary Table 1. GEO prostate cancer transcription data were obtained from GSE183019, which includes 70 tumour tissue samples with a preoperative PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL and 10 tumour tissue samples with a preoperative PSA concentration >10 ng/mL. The prostate cancer data from the Chinese High-risk Prostate Cancer Multiomics Atlas (CHPCMA) is derived from data [ ]. This transcriptomic data includes 33 prostate cancer samples with a PSA level of < 10 ng/ml and 32 matched normal prostate tissue samples, as well as 92 prostate cancer samples with a PSA level of >10 ng/ml and 90 matched normal prostate tissue samples. The data were corrected by the “Normalization” function of the “limma” (3.54.2) package. A total of 1089 genes encoding urinary exosome proteins were obtained from the UrinaryExosome database. The “TCGAplot” (v4.4.0) package was used to obtain and utilize normal and tumour data from the TCGA database. Protein quantitative trait locus (pQTL) data were acquired from publicly available large-scale whole-genome association studies of the plasma proteome(ID:16847_39_RAB3B). Additionally, statistical data on PCa as an exposure (ieu-b-85) were obtained through the Prostate Cancer Association Group to Investigate Cancer-Associated Alterations in the Genome (PRACTICAL) consortium, including the genomic data of 79,148 European prostate cancer patients and 61,106 European controls [ ].

## Urine and plasma sample origin and collection
Urine and blood samples from 60 prostate cancer and benign prostatic hyperplasia patients were collected from April 2022 to March 2024 at the Department of Urology, the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University.
This dataset included 30 patients in the prostate cancer group (PSA < 10: n = 20; PSA > 10: n = 10) and 30 patients in the benign proliferation group (PSA < 10: n = 20; PSA > 10: n = 10). All diseases were diagnosed via pathological examination. The inclusion criteria for patients were (1) no prior treatment for other cancers; (2) the absence of other tumours or severe chronic diseases; and (3) the absence of severe mental illness. The patient enrollment flow was detailed in Supplementary Fig. 1, while baseline characteristics were summarized in Supplementary Table 2. Morning urine samples (100 mL) were collected from patients following prostate massage and stored at −80 °C. Whole blood was drawn using EDTA anticoagulant tubes and centrifuged at 1900 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant plasma was then recentrifuged at 3000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, and the final samples were stored at − 80 °C. The laboratory staff who performed the biomarker assays were blinded to the final pathological diagnosis (PCa or BPH) and group allocation of the samples during the analytical process.

## Cell lines used in the experiments
RWPE-1 and BPH-1 cells were obtained from our institute’s laboratory, and prostate epithelial cancer cells (DU145, LNCaP and C4-2) were purchased from the ATCC cell bank. All cells were cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen.

## Single-cell data processing and analysis
Following quality control to retain high-quality cells (genes detected >300, mitochondrial gene percentage < 10%), scRNA-seq data were normalized and scaled using the Seurat package (v4.3.0). The top 2000 highly variable genes were identified using the “FindVariableFeatures” function, and principal component analysis was performed for initial cell subgroup identification. Batch effects were corrected using the “RunHarmony” function from the harmony package (v1.2.0), and results were visualized via UMAP projection. Cell types were preliminarily labelled using SingleR (v2.0.0), and the following analyses were conducted. After re-clustering the epithelial cells, we annotated new cell groups on the basis of the genes given in the original article [ ]. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between normal and PSA < 10 ng/mL tumor epithelial cells, as well as between RAB3B + and RAB3B − epithelial cells, were identified using the “FindMarkers” function (|LogFC| >0.5, FDR < 0.05) and visualized with ggplot2 (v3.4.2). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed on RAB3B + vs. RAB3B − epithelial cell DEGs using the “clusterProfiler” package (v4.6.2). Metabolic activity across epithelial cell subtypes was assessed with “scMetabolism” (v0.2.1), and intercellular communication was analyzed using “CellChat” (v1.6.1).

## Selection of instrumental variables
In this study, the selected SNPs as instrumental variables met the following criteria. (1) They demonstrated a significant genome-wide association (P < 5 × 10− 8) with their respective traits. (2) They are lead SNPs according to the standard of linkage disequilibrium (LD) (R2 = 0.01, 10000 kb). (3) Palindromic and ambiguous SNPs were discarded. (4) The F value was used to assess the tool strength of each SNP. An average F statistic >10 indicate that the SNP is a vital instrument [ ]. ## Mendelian randomization analysis
The causal relationship between PCa and pQTL is primarily estimated using the inverse variance weighting (IVW) method in Mendelian randomization analysis. When the instrumental variables meet all the assumptions, the IVW method can provide consistent estimates of causal effects and is considered the most substantial MR method. To ensure the reliability of the results, the following analyses were conducted: (1) The Q test from IVW and Mendelian randomization–Egger regression (MR‒Egger) was used to detect heterogeneity among individual associations. (2) MR‒Egger’s intercept was applied to estimate horizontal pleiotropy. (3) Different modelling assumptions were used to increase the stability of the results. (4) Mendelian randomization pleiotropy residual sum and outlier (MR-PRESSO) was used to detect outliers and correct for horizontal pleiotropy. (5) Reverse causation was assessed using the Steiger test. (6) Finally, individual SNP analysis and leave-one-out analysis were applied to evaluate the correlations of individual SNPs with outcomes.

## Validation of the diagnostic model
The “pROC” (v1.18.5) package was used to construct the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and the diagnostic performances of the models and individual variables were compared on the basis of the area under the curve (AUC). Decision curve analysis (DCA), drawn using the “rmda” (v1.6) package, was used to analyse the net effect of the model. Analyze the Net Reclassification Index (NRI) of the model using nricens (v1.6).

## Western blotting
Total protein was extracted from PCa cells using a Total Protein Extraction Kit (KeyGEN, Nanjing, China) and quantified via a BCA assay (KeyGEN) according to standard protocols. Primary antibodies against β-actin (66009-1-Ig; Proteintech; 1:10,000 dilution) and RAB3B (15774-1-AP; Proteintech; 1:4,000 dilution) were incubated with the membranes, followed by incubation with a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (GB23303; ServiceBio, Wuhan; 1:3,000 dilution). Protein signals were visualized using WesternBright ECL substrate (Advansta, USA) and images were captured with a ChemiDoc MP Imaging System (Bio-Rad, USA).

## Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining
The sections were deparaffinized in 100% xylene and rehydrated with gradient ethanol (100%, 90%, 70%, 30%, and 0%). Following inactivation of endogenous peroxidase activity using 3% hydrogen peroxide and antigen retrieval in citrate buffer by heat, IHC staining was performed with a VECTASTAIN Universal Quick Kit (PK-8800; Vector Laboratories, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. An anti-RAB3B antibody (15774-1-AP; Proteintech Group, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was applied at a dilution of 1:250.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The RAB3B protein in the cell culture medium, plasma, and urine was detected according to the methods of the RAB3B ELISA Kit (EK7820; Signalway Antibody, USA). The website https://www.mycurvefit.com/ was used to calculate the concentrations of standards and test samples on the basis of the formula (four-parameter, 4PL) by entering the corresponding OD values.

## Plasmid construction and cell transfection
Plasmids encoding a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting human RAB3B and a corresponding overexpression construct were generated to investigate the functional role of RAB3B in prostate cancer C4-2 cells. shRNA targeting RAB3B (shRAB3B; Product No. G70023) and a non‑targeting shRNA control (sh‑NC; Product No. P40959) were designed and synthesized by MiaoLing Plasmid (China). Similarly, the RAB3B overexpression plasmid (OE‑RAB3B; Product No. G70022) and the corresponding empty vector control (OE‑NC; Product No. P40122) were also obtained from MiaoLing Plasmid. Each plasmid was packaged into lentiviral particles. For stable transduction, C4-2 cells were infected with the respective lentiviruses in the presence of 5 µg/mL polybrene (GenePharma, Suzhou, China). Forty‑eight hours post‑infection, the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing 5 µg/mL puromycin to select for stably transduced cells. After 72 h of selection, when cell death had ceased, stably transfected cell pools were established for subsequent experiments.

## Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
C4-2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 10³ cells per well and cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂) for 48 h. Cell viability was then assessed using a CCK-8 assay kit (Dojindo, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, after treatment, CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, and the plates were further incubated. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA).

## 5-Ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EDU) assay
The proliferative ability of PCa cells was evaluated using an EDU assay kit (RiboBio, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. EDU-positive cells were then quantified under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus Optical).

## Transwell migration assays
Transwell migration assays were performed using inserts with 8 μm pores (BD Biosciences, CA, USA) placed in 24-well plates. The upper chambers received 200 µL of serum-free medium containing 0.5–5 × 10⁴ cells, while the lower wells were filled with 600 µL of complete medium supplemented with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 12–24 h of incubation at 37 °C, the migrated cells on the lower membrane surface were stained with 0.5% crystal violet and quantified.

## Wound healing assay
Briefly, C4-2 cells were seeded in six-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁵ cells per well and cultured until they reached 70% confluence. A straight wound was then created in the cell monolayer using a sterile pipette tip. The wound areas were imaged at 0 and 48 h after scratching using a microscope system (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The cell migration distance was calculated on the basis of the changes in wound width.

## Statistical analysis
The data were statistically analysed using R software version 4.2.2. For normally distributed data, comparisons between groups were made using t tests. For data that did not follow a normal distribution, comparisons between groups were made using the rank-sum test. To identify differentially expressed genes, we used LogFC > 0.5 or < −0.5 and FDR < 0.05 as the criteria for statistical significance; for the remaining genes, we considered the results to be statistically significant when p < 0.05.

## Results

## Framework of the study
As shown in Fig.  , our study consisted of two main parts. In the first part, a screen for a diagnostic marker of PCa in patients with a PSA concentration less than 10 ng/mL was conducted. We identified three genes, RAB3B (Ras-related protein Rab-3B)​, SPON2 (Spondin-2)​, and SLC4A4 (Solute carrier family 4 member 4)​​, through single-cell sequencing and machine learning experiments. Using transcriptomics, pan-cancer analysis, and immunohistochemistry, we subsequently assessed the expression distribution and diagnostic efficacy of these three genes. On this basis, we ultimately selected the gene RAB3B. The second part of our study validated the diagnostic efficacy of RAB3B and evaluated its influence on PCa progression using multiomics methods.

 

## Identification of genes highly expressed in PCa cells with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL
As depicted in Fig.  A, we created a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of both PCa and healthy prostate samples, comprising 17 tumour samples (13 of which had an initial PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL and 4 that had a PSA concentration > 10 ng/mL at diagnosis) and 4 healthy prostate samples. In total, we analysed 91,539 cells, including cells from normal tissue (HP, healthy people) (n = 28,746), tumour tissues with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (PCa below 10) (n = 54,222), and tumour tissues with a PSA concentration > 10 ng/mL (PCa above 10) (n = 8,571). Eighteen significant cell clusters were identified (Supplementary Fig. 2 A). Utilizing marker genes for each cell cluster (Supplementary Fig. 2B), these clusters were classified into seven primary cell subgroups, comprising smooth muscle cells, monocytes, epithelial cells, T cells, B cells, endothelial cells, and natural killer cells (Fig.  B).

 

To explore genes specifically overexpressed in tumour cells with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL, we re-clustered the epithelial cells and identified 14 significant cell clusters (Supplementary Fig. 2 C). Bubble plots of the expression of annotated genes in normal tissue (Supplementary Fig. 2D) and cancer tissue (Supplementary Fig. 2E) were constructed. On the basis of the marker genes of each cell group, we identified six primary cell subgroups, which included basal cells, prostate club cells, prostate luminal cells, prostate hillock cells, PSA below 10 ng/mL prostate tumour cells (tumour below 10), and PSA above 10 ng/mL prostate tumour cells (tumour above 10) (Fig.  C).
Afterwards, a differential expression analysis was carried out. The volcano plot displayed in Fig.  D illustrates the genes whose expression was upregulated and downregulated in tumours with a PSA concentration less than 10 ng/mL compared with that in other nonepithelial cells (such as smooth muscle cells, monocytes, T cells, B cells, endothelial cells, and natural killer cells), revealing 444 genes whose expression was significantly greater in tumours with a PSA concentration less than 10 ng/mL (logFC > 0.5, FDR < 0.05). Similarly, the volcano plot in Fig.  E shows the differential gene expression between tumours with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL and other epithelial cells (basal cells, prostate club cells, prostate luminal cells, and prostate hillock cells), yielding 74 genes with high expression in tumours with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (logFC > 0.5, FDR < 0.05). The intersection of these upregulated genes identified a total of 60 genes that were specifically highly expressed in tumour cells with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (Fig.  F). To improve specificity for expression in prostate tissue and ensure detectability in body fluids, we further intersected these 60 genes with 1,089 genes detectable in urine, identifying 5 genes (RAB3B, SLC4A4, SPON2, COBLL1, and ALCAM) (Fig.  G).

## Further screening of candidate genes using machine learning
We subsequently applied two machine learning methods, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), utilizing the GSE183019 dataset (comprising 84 normal tissue samples and 70 preoperative tumour tissue samples with PSA concentrations below 10 ng/mL), to further identify genes whose expression distinctly differed in these two types of tissue. Through the application of nested cross - validation, a more reliable evaluation of the model’s performance can be achieved. An average accuracy of 0.72 was obtained from the outer cross - validation. In the context of LASSO logistic regression, parameters were fine - tuned using 10 - fold cross - validation, and 3 genes were identified (Fig.  A-B). By employing the SVM-RFE algorithm, we subsequently selected 5 genes to determine the optimal gene combination. This method, yielding a maximum accuracy of 0.75 and a minimum RMSE of 0.25, ultimately identified 5 genes as the most effective feature genes (Fig.  C-D). The overlap of genes derived from both algorithms led to the identification of 3 genes (RAB3B, SLC4A4, and SPON2) (Fig.  E).

 

To further evaluate the three genes identified above, their expression characteristics in tumours with PSA levels below 10 ng/mL and normal tissues in the GSE183019 dataset were analysed. The results revealed that all three genes were significantly overexpressed in tumours with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (p < 0.05) (Fig.  F, H, J). Pan-cancer analysis demonstrated that these genes are overexpressed in multiple cancers, including prostate cancer (Fig.  G, I, K), with RAB3B expression being particularly elevated in prostate cancer, bladder cancer, head and neck cancer, thyroid cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and lung squamous cell carcinoma but predominantly expressed in the prostate (Fig.  G). Bubble plots illustrated the expression of these three genes in single-cell dataset, revealing high expression in PCa epithelial cells only (Fig.  L), with no significant difference in RAB3B and SLC4A4 expression levels between PSA < 10 ng/mL PCa and PSA > 10 ng/mL PCa (Fig.  M). These findings suggest that RAB3B is primarily overexpressed in prostate tissue and associated with cancer occurrence.

## Diagnostic efficacy analysis of candidate genes
We next focused on the diagnostic performance of the three candidate genes. ROC curve analysis based on the training set (GSE183019) showed that the AUC value of RAB3B for diagnosing PCa was 0.771 (95% CI: 0.697–0.841), which was higher than that of SLC4A4 (0.726, 95% CI: 0.643–0.799) and SPON2 (0.681, 95% CI: 0.599–0.758) (Fig.  A; Table  ). This advantage of RAB3B was confirmed in the external validation set (CHPCMA), where its AUC reached 0.858 (95% CI: 0.809–0.900), again surpassing SLC4A4 (0.818, 95% CI: 0.763–0.867) and SPON2 (0.817, 95% CI: 0.762–0.867) (Fig.  C; Table  ).

 

 

For diagnosing PCa with PSA < 10 ng/mL, RAB3B also achieved the highest AUC in the training set (0.763, 95% CI: 0.677–0.838), outperforming SLC4A4 (0.713, 95% CI: 0.629–0.797) and SPON2 (0.683, 95% CI: 0.595–0.772) (Fig.  B; Table  ). Similarly, in the external validation set, RAB3B yielded an AUC of 0.825 (95% CI: 0.723–0.916), which was higher than that of SLC4A4 (0.779, 95% CI: 0.653–0.892) and SPON2 (0.790, 95% CI: 0.665–0.893) (Fig.  D; Table  ). These findings suggest that RAB3B exhibits superior diagnostic efficacy for both overall PCa and PCa with low PSA levels (< 10 ng/mL), indicating its potential as an optimal biomarker for such cases.

 

We further compared RAB3B with KLK3, the gene encoding PSA. In the training set, the AUC of KLK3 for diagnosing PCa with PSA < 10 ng/mL was 0.731 (95% CI: 0.643–0.817), lower than that of RAB3B (0.763) (Fig.  E; Table  ). KLK3 also showed lower specificity (0.376) compared to RAB3B (0.630), indicating a reduced ability to correctly identify negative cases. This trend was corroborated in the external validation set, where KLK3 had an AUC of 0.703 (95% CI: 0.567–0.836), again lower than that of RAB3B (0.825) (Fig.  G; Table  ). These results demonstrate that while the PSA-encoding gene KLK3 has relatively low specificity, RAB3B provides more stable diagnostic performance.

 

A combined diagnostic model integrating RAB3B and KLK3 was subsequently developed. The model achieved AUCs of 0.768 (95% CI: 0.687–0.843) in the training set and 0.848 (95% CI: 0.750–0.934) in the external validation set for diagnosing PCa with PSA < 10 ng/mL (Fig.  F and H; Table  ). Decision curve analysis indicated a favorable net benefit of the model (Fig.  I and K), and the calibration curves showed good agreement between predicted and actual probabilities (Fig.  J and L). Additionally, a nomogram incorporating KLK3 and RAB3B expression was constructed to facilitate individualized risk assessment for PCa with PSA < 10 ng/mL (Fig.  M and N).

## Mendelian randomization analysis revealed that PCa promoted an increase in Circulating RAB3B protein levels
The results of the above analysis confirmed that RAB3B was an ideal diagnostic marker at the gene expression level, and immunohistochemistry suggested that the occurrence of PCa promoted the expression of the RAB3B protein. Therefore, if the RAB3B protein can be detected in body fluids, the convenience of detection could be improved. Therefore, we conducted a Mendelian randomization analysis to assess the association between PCa occurrence and circulating RAB3B protein levels. The IVW method revealed a positive correlation between PCa occurrence and circulating RAB3B protein levels (Fig.  A, C), with an OR of 1.027 (95% CI = 1.007–1.046; p = 0.006) (Table  ). The funnel plot revealed that the causal effects were approximately symmetrical (Fig.  B). No horizontal pleiotropic effects were observed in the MR‒Egger regression intercept (p = 0.885), further indicating that there was no bias due to pleiotropy in the causal effects (Table  ). In Fig.  D, an additional systematic MR analysis was performed on the remaining SNPs and the results remained consistent, demonstrating significant causal effects of the calculated SNPs. Furthermore, we did not identify IVW heterogeneity (Q = 86.3; p = 0.851), horizontal pleiotropy (MR-PRESSO; p = 0.859), or reverse causality (steiger_pval = 0) (Table  ).

 

 

## RAB3B was highly expressed in the tumours and urine of PCa patients
The MR analysis suggested that PCa promotes an increase in circulating RAB3B protein levels. Here, western blot analysis revealed significantly elevated RAB3B protein expression in prostate cancer cell lines (DU145, C4-2, and LNCaP) compared with that in normal prostatic epithelial cells (RWPE-1) and hyperplastic cells (BPH-1) (Fig.  A). Immunohistochemistry confirmed pronounced RAB3B overexpression in tumour tissues (Fig.  B). Subsequent ELISA quantification using a standardized calibration curve (Fig.  C) demonstrated markedly increased RAB3B levels in conditioned media from LNCaP (p = 0.045) and C4-2 (p = 0.018) prostate cancer cells compared with normal epithelial cells (Fig.  D).

 

ELISAs were also performed to measure RAB3B protein levels in the body fluids of BPH and PCa patients. We found that compared with those in the urine of BPH patients, RAB3B protein levels in the urine of PCa patients (n = 30, p = 0.038) and PCa patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (n = 20, p = 0.030) were significantly elevated (Fig.  E). However, no such difference was observed in blood samples (PSA < 10; PCa n = 16 vs. BPH n = 8; p = 0.096) (Fig.  F). These results suggest that elevated levels of the RAB3B protein are detectable in the urine of PCa patients compared with that in the urine of BPH patients.

## Diagnostic efficacy analysis of models for distinguishing PCa with PSA < 10 ng/mL from BPH
To assess the diagnostic ability of models in differentiating prostate cancer (PCa) with PSA < 10 ng/mL from benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), analyses including ROC curves, confusion matrix, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis were conducted. For individual predictors, ROC curve results showed that UrineRAB3B had an AUC of 0.733 (95% CI: 0.565–0.878) with an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.878, which was higher than that of PSA (AUC = 0.682, 95% CI: 0.505–0.847; optimism-corrected AUC = 0.847) and age (AUC = 0.651, 95% CI: 0.464–0.828; optimism-corrected AUC = 0.828) (Fig.  A).

 

Regarding the multivariate model, its ROC curve exhibited an AUC of 0.8(Fig.  B). Using Youden’s index to determine the optimal cutoff value (0.499), the model achieved a sensitivity of 0.75 and a specificity of 0.8. The corresponding confusion matrix illustrated the classification performance: among actual PCa cases, 15 were predicted correctly and 5 were misclassified; for actual BPH cases, 15 were predicted correctly and 5 were misclassified(Fig.  C). Calibration curves were used to compare the PSA-only model (Model 1) and the multivariate model (Model 2) (Fig.  D-E). Model 2 showed better calibration, with a mean absolute error of 0.023, and its predicted probability of PCa (PSA < 10 ng/mL) was more consistent with the actual incidence(Fig.  E). In contrast, Model 1 had a mean absolute error of 0.051 and deviated more from the ideal calibration line(Fig.  D). Decision curve analysis demonstrated that the multivariate model provided a higher net benefit than the PSA-only model across a range of threshold probabilities, indicating that the multivariate model is more clinically valuable for distinguishing PCa (PSA < 10 ng/mL) from BPH(Fig.  F). Additionally, we calculated the NRI to compare the multivariable model against PSA alone. The NRI was 0.40 (95% CI: −0.05 to 0.87)(Table  ), indicating a significant improvement in reclassification capacity when adding urinary RAB3B to the model.

 

## Functional and communication analyses of RAB3B in PCa cells with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL
The functional role of RAB3B in PCa patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL remains unclear. Here, utilizing single-cell data, we categorized tumours with a PSA concentration less than 10 ng/mL into RAB3B + EP and RAB3B- EP cells on the basis of their RAB3B expression levels. Differentially expressed genes between these two cell groups were subjected to GSEA functional analysis, and the results indicated that RAB3B primarily participates in the metabolism of essential substances and vesicle transport in PCa patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL (Fig.  A). Further exploration of metabolism revealed that compared with RAB3B- EP cells, RAB3B + EP cells exhibited increased metabolic activity, which is required for several vital biological processes, including the degradation of other glycans, pantothenate and CoA biosynthesis, porphyrin and chlorophyll metabolism, terpenoid backbone biosynthesis, the biosynthesis of ubiquinone and other terpenoid-quinones, and folate biosynthesis (Fig.  B).

 

Cell communication analysis can reveal interactions between cells. Here, we investigated the interactions of RAB3B + EP and RAB3B- EP cells with other cells. Compared with RAB3B- EP cells, RAB3B + EP cells received more autocrine signals from RAB3B + EP cells (Fig.  C). The main receptor–ligand pairs were MIF–(CD74 + CD44) and PTN–SDC2 (Fig.  D). Compared with RAB3B- EP cells, RAB3B + EP cells transmitted more signals to basal epithelial cells and club epithelial cells (Fig.  E), with the main receptor–ligand pairs being PTN–SDC1, PTN–SDC2, and PTN–SDC4 (Fig.  F).
In summary, compared with RAB3B- EP cells, RAB3B + EP cells exhibit increased autocrine and paracrine signalling.

## RAB3B promotes proliferation and migration of PCa Cells​
To investigate the effect of RAB3B on the malignant phenotype of PCa cells, we conducted gain-of-function and loss-of-function experiments in C4-2 cells. As shown in Fig.  A and B, the results of western blot analysis verified the effective knockdown of the RAB3B gene by shRAB3B and the high expression efficiency of the overexpression vector (OE-RAB3B). Proliferation assays demonstrated that knockdown of RAB3B expression significantly decreased the viability of C4-2 cells (CCK-8 assay, Fig.  C) and DNA replication activity (EDU assay, Fig.  D, E). Conversely, overexpression of RAB3B markedly promoted cell proliferation. In terms of cell migration ability, both Transwell migration assays (H, I) and wound healing assays (Fig.  F, G) consistently revealed that compared with that in the control group, the migration ability of C4-2 cells in which RAB3B was knocked down was significantly weakened. In contrast, overexpression of RAB3B significantly enhanced cell migration ability (all statistical results showed P < 0.01).

 

These experiments collectively demonstrate that RAB3B can significantly increase the proliferation and migration capabilities of prostate cancer C4-2 cells, suggesting that it may act as an oncogene in the progression of PCa.

## Discussion
PCa is among the most prevalent malignancies among men in developed nations, notably in Europe and North America, with the second-highest mortality rate among all male cancers. In China, the incidence of prostate cancer is also steadily increasing annually [ ]. Currently, the diagnostic rate of PCa remains suboptimal [ ], particularly for patients whose serum PSA concentration is less than 10 ng/mL [ ]. Owing to the lack of apparent early symptoms and decreased diagnostic precision for those with low PSA levels, numerous patients are already in advanced stages at the time of diagnosis. Early localized PCa can be cured by radical surgery or radiotherapy, with a five-year survival rate approaching 100%. Nevertheless, in the case of advanced prostate cancer, the five-year survival rate is only 28% [ ]. In advanced cases, androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) and androgen receptor (AR) inhibition are among the primary treatment options. Despite the typically positive initial responses to treatment, the majority of patients ultimately transition to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) within 1–3 years [ ]. Therefore, identifying novel diagnostic biomarkers for early screening is critical for improving PCa prognosis.
In this study, we initially re-clustered epithelial cells using single-cell sequencing technology and identified genes that were specifically upregulated in tumour epithelial cells with PSA concentrations less than 10 ng/mL. The advantage of single-cell sequencing lies in its ability to precisely and clearly detect gene expression within individual cells or cell populations. In the field of PCa research, this technology has been widely used to identify various specialized cell populations, including but not limited to enzalutamide-resistant tumour cells [ ] and invasive tumour cell clusters [ ]. We subsequently employed machine learning to refine the selected upregulated genes, pinpointing three candidates: RAB3B, SLC4A4, and SPON2. The advantage of machine learning lies in its ability to learn from large datasets and discover patterns to provide accurate predictions and analyses. By using models to understand complex data relationships, automatic feature extraction and classification results can help better interpret and utilize data. This approach is now being widely applied to prostate cancer biomarker screening [ ]. Among these three genes, RAB3B exhibited the best diagnostic performance for PCa cells with PSA concentrations < 10 ng/mL at the transcriptomic level. Pan-cancer analysis revealed that RAB3B was more highly expressed in prostate tissue than in other tissues, indicating its specificity. Thus, we selected RAB3B for further investigation.
The results of the aforementioned research confirmed that at the transcriptomic level, RAB3B demonstrated the most promising diagnostic potential for prostate cancer (PCa) when the serum PSA concentration was less than 10 ng/mL. To identify a noninvasive biomarker detectable in bodily fluids that can mitigate the risks associated with invasive biopsy, such as infection and bleeding, we first performed Mendelian randomization analysis, which provided evidence of a causal relationship: PCa development actively promotes increased circulating RAB3B protein levels. Subsequent experimental validation supported these findings: we detected significantly elevated RAB3B levels in the culture supernatants of PCa cell lines, more importantly, in the urine of patients with PCa and a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL. Although Alicia Llorente et al. previously reported elevated urinary RAB3B levels in PCa patients compared with healthy males using protein blotting [ ], their study approached RAB3B only as a general biomarker for prostate diseases and did not investigate its expression or diagnostic value in the clinically critical low-PSA (< 10 ng/mL) subgroup, a gap addressed by our work.
Our diagnostic efficacy analysis revealed that urinary RAB3B levels achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.733 for diagnosing PCa in patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL, which falls within the 0.7–0.8 range generally recognized as indicating moderate diagnostic performance. This method demonstrates superior diagnostic efficacy compared to serum PSA, primarily due to the limited specificity of PSA within the “grey zone” (4–10 ng/mL), where elevated levels are often attributable to benign conditions like benign prostatic hyperplasia or prostatitis.When compared to ExoDx Prostate (EPI), another well-validated, nondigital rectal exam (DRE) urine-based tool for low-PSA PCa, urinary RAB3B demonstrates comparable efficacy [ ]. EPI, which requires complex exosome isolation and RNA analysis, increasing operational complexity and cost. In contrast, RAB3B can be detected using a straightforward ELISA, a technique widely available in routine clinical laboratories. Notably, our model combining PSA, urinary RAB3B, and age achieved an AUC of 0.8 for diagnosing PSA < 10 ng/ml PCa, indicating that urinary RAB3B holds significant promise in assisting PSA for diagnosing PCa within the PSA gray zone.
RAB3B is a member of the RAB GTPase family of small GTP-binding proteins and functions as a regulator of vesicle transport, protein trafficking, and membrane fusion [ ]. The role of RAB GTPases in cancer remains largely unexplored. Studies have shown that RAB27A and RAB27B are associated with cancer invasion and metastasis [ ], whereas the downregulation of RAB6B is correlated with poor prognosis in adenocarcinoma patients [ ]. Compared with research on other RAB isoforms, research on the relationship between RAB3 isoforms and cancer is relatively scarce. RAB3 consists of four isoforms: RAB3A, RAB3B, RAB3C, and RAB3D. These proteins function as oncogenes by promoting tumour formation, proliferation, and metastasis [ ]. Among these isoforms, RAB3D is the most extensively studied and has been shown to be associated with malignancy in various tumours, including gastrointestinal tumours [ ]. However, RAB3B has been less studied, and its expression profile in cancer cells, as well as its roles in cancer cell proliferation, migration, and signalling pathways, remain unclear. Jiqin Xu et al. reported that RAB3B expression is associated with cervical cancer progression [ ]. In PCa, Peck Yean Tan et al. demonstrated that NKX3-1 and FoxA1 directly upregulate RAB3B to promote PCa cell survival [ ]. GSEA and metabolic analysis revealed that compared with RAB3B-negative cells, RAB3B-positive tumour epithelial cells exhibit enhanced metabolism (e.g., pantothenate and CoA biosynthesis and porphyrin metabolism) and more efficient cell communication via receptor–ligand pairs such as PTN–SDC1/2/4 (which are known to promote cell growth) [ ]. Functional assays (CCK-8, EdU, Transwell, and wound healing assays) confirmed that RAB3B promotes the proliferation and migration of C4-2 cells, suggesting that it may act as an oncogene in PCa.
Using mouse models and cell experiments, Geppert M et al. reported that the deletion of specific RAB3 isoforms does not significantly impact the survival of normal cells [ ]. These findings indicate that targeting RAB3 isoforms may selectively affect cancer cells while having a minimal effect on normal cells. On the basis of these findings, targeting RAB3 isoforms could represent a promising and safe therapeutic strategy for PCa patients [ ].

## Conclusion
In conclusion, urinary RAB3B levels have moderate diagnostic performance (AUC 0.733) for PCa patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL, outperforming serum PSA levels and offering practical advantages as a noninvasive tool. Its intended role in guiding clinical decisions is that of an auxiliary to serum PSA levels, particularly for patients in the PSA grey zone, rather than replacing existing diagnostic methods. With further validation, urinary RAB3B has the potential to become a valuable addition to the PCa diagnostic armamentarium, improving early detection and reducing unnecessary interventions.
