## Abstract
Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are present in small quantities in tumor populations. To permit various analyses of CSCs, we attempted to enrich and expand ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) degron-transduced colorectal cancer (CRC) cells, which retain low proteasome activity. ZsGreen fluorescence-positive (ZsGreen+) cells were collected by sorting the ODC degron-transduced HCT116, DLD1, and SW480 cells, which were defined as enriched ZsGreen+ cells. ZsGreen+ cells still maintained CSC properties. These cells had higher stem cell marker expression and increased resistance to chemotherapy with 5-fluorouracil and oxaliplatin. ZsGreen+ HCT116 and DLD1 cells had greater sphere-forming ability and enhanced tumorigenicity compared to ZsGreen- control cells. Time-lapse microscopy showed that a single enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cell had asymmetric cell division. Thus, cancer stem model cells were acquired in sufficient quantity. Using these cells, we performed a comprehensive microRNA analysis; miR-491-3p was a candidate to suppress cancer stemness. Finally, we found that up-regulated genes in the enriched HCT116 ZsGreen+ cells correlated with those up-regulated in human clinical spheroid samples established from patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) derived from CRC tissue samples, further supporting the acquisition of enriched model CSCs. These cells with the aid of clinical spheroid samples would be useful in identifying novel CSC markers and developing medicine for anti-CSC therapy.

## Introduction
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most prevalent human malignancies in the world and the leading cause of cancer-related death . Despite recent advances in cancer treatment, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, and immunotherapy, CRC is still commonly recurrent, metastatic, and eventually refractory to every treatment. The concept of cancer stem cells (CSCs) is drawing attention because CSCs are considered to confer resistance to conventional therapies including chemotherapy and radiation therapy and to lead to cancer recurrence – .
The stem cell hierarchy is well established in hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). HSCs remain quiescent at a steady state to preserve their self-renewal potential and protect themselves against cell damage. Under stress conditions, such as inflammation and toxic agents, hematopoietic stem cells can be activated and enter the cell cycle, starting their self-renewal and differentiation – . CSCs were first identified in human cancer in 1994 in leukemia . Subsequently, the existence of CSCs extended to breast cancer, brain tumors, and other solid tumors – . In 2006, the American Cancer Society defined CSCs as cells within a tumor that possess the capacity to self-renew and to cause the heterogeneous lineages of cancer cells that comprise the tumor . As in the normal stem cell hierarchy, progenitor cells are present in the CSC hierarchy, designated as transit-amplifying (TA) cells – . CSCs undergo asymmetric cell division, turning into one CSC and one pluripotent TA cell. TA cells proliferate rapidly and, after several cell divisions, become differentiated tumor cells, which form the bulk of cancer tissues.
The scarcity of CSCs in tumor tissue makes direct analysis challenging. To detect the CSC-like cells, we and other investigators utilize the ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) degron system in which the degron sequence is recognized directly by the 26S proteasome, which leads to destruction of the involved protein. Cancer cells with low proteasome activity are visualized under the microscope by stably expressed green fluorescence (ZsGreen) fused to the ODC degron at a very low incidence (usually less than 1%). Such cells are likely to exhibit CSC-like properties (i.e., enhanced tumorigenicity, high sphere-forming ability, and high expression of CSC markers and low reactive oxygen species levels) – . Moreover, the proportion of low proteasome activity cells (LPACs) increase with radiation therapy or chemotherapy in tumor cell lines , , , .
To enable direct analysis of CSCs in various experiments, such as drug sensitivity tests, biochemical assays, genetic analyses, and in vivo therapeutic models, we enriched LPACs by cell sorting and culture to ensure their quantity and confirmed that the enriched LPACs maintained their CSC properties. Finally, we compared the gene expression profiles of enriched LPACs to those of spheroid samples established from patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) derived from clinical CRC tissues to support the reliability of the model CSCs and to offer better selection of novel CSC markers and therapeutic targets.

## Materials and methods

## Cell lines and cell culture
Human CRC cell lines HCT116, DLD1, and SW480 were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The HCT116 cells are known to have a potent cancer stem-like property – . DLD-1 is derived from a colon cancer patient and the cells exhibit aggressive phenotype in a murine model displaying moderate to poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma . SW480 is derived from a Dukes C adenocarcinoma of the colon which expresses ras, myc, and myb, oncogenes. Extent of putative CSC markers in DLD1 and SW480 are reportedly moderate and modest, respectively . All cells were grown in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and maintained at 37℃ in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Mycoplasma testing was performed before use.

## Retroviral transduction of the degron reporter into CRC cell lines
Degron is the proteasome recognition site of ODC, which is protein resolved by proteasome without ubiquitin labeling – . The retroviral expression vector pQCXIN-ZsGreen-cODC was transfected into platinum retroviral packaging cells, and CRC cell lines were infected with the supernatant containing the retrovirus as reported previously , . Stable transfectants were selected using G418 solution (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). The fluorescent fusion protein ZsGreen accumulated in the cells with low proteasome activity. ZsGreen-positive cells were observed under fluorescent microscopy (BZ-X700; Keyence, Osaka, Japan).

## Preparation of human spheroids
Surgically resected colorectal tumors were obtained from eight patients at Osaka University Hospital between November 2017 and June 2019. The tumors were washed with cold PBS containing 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (Nacalai Tesque, Inc., Kyoto, Japan) and chopped into approximately 2-mm pieces. Minced tumors in 100 µL Matrigel (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) were transplanted subcutaneously into both flanks of 4 to 8-week-old female SCID Beige mice (CLEA Japan, Inc., Tokyo, Japan). Tumors were harvested when they reached approximately 200 mm3 in 1–2 months and chopped into approximately 2-mm pieces. The minced tumors were dissociated using a gentle MACS Dissociator (Miltenyi Biotec GmbH, San Diego, CA, USA) in human tumor mode according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The tumor cell mixture with dissociation buffer was filtered using a 7-μm strainer, washed with DMEM, and centrifuged at 300 g for 7 min. The pellet was resuspended in 120 µL Matrigel, seeded on 24-well plates, and incubated in ES medium at 37℃. Spheres were formed in 1 week, frozen and stored until use.

## Flow cytometry
Cells infected with retroviral vector were suspended in FluoroBrite DMEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS. Using Cell Sorter SH800Z (Sony Biotechnology, Inc., San Jose, CA, USA), the cell population with high fluorescence intensity was collected by the EGFP channel as described previously , . The cut-off value was determined by using the negative control, which was infected with empty vector, for each cell line.

## Cell proliferation assay
Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1,000 cells per well (n = 4 for each cell group) and cultured for 24 to 96 h. Cell viability was measured every 24 h using Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The absorbance was detected at 450 nm using a spectrophotometer.

## Sphere formation assay
Serum-free medium was prepared as DMEM/F-12 containing 20 ng/mL human recombinant epidermal growth factor (EGF; Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA), 20 ng/mL human recombinant basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF; Invitrogen), and 100 μg/mL penicillin G. Cells were seeded (n = 3, in each cell group) in ultra-low attachment 96-well plates (Corning, Inc. Corning, NY, USA) in a single cell state at a density of 200 cells per well and grown in 100 µL of serum-free medium at 37℃ in 5% CO2. Every 2 days, 20 µL of serum-free medium was added to each well. The number of spheres ≥ 100 µm in size was counted after 14 days of culture.

## Chemosensitivity assay
Cells were seeded (n = 4, in each cell group) in 96-well plates at a density of 3,000 cells per well and treated with chemotherapeutic agents 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and oxaliplatin (L-OHP) at a variety of concentrations. The WST-8 assay was performed 48–72 h after treatment.

## RNA extraction and quantitative RT-PCR
RNA was collected using the miRNeasy Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). The concentration and purity of the RNA was evaluated using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The cDNA was biosynthesized from 1.0 µg of total RNA using the High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Quantitative real-time PCR was carried out in triplicate using the LightCycler 480 Real-Time PCR system (Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland). GAPDH was used as an internal control to normalize the expression level of each gene. Each specific primer used for PCR is summarized in Supplementary Table .

## Immunohistochemistry
Cells were fixed in 10% buffered formalin. After permeabilization with cold 70% ethanol, the sections were treated with 3% bovine serum albumin solution for blocking at room temperature. They were then incubated with the rabbit polyclonal anti-human CK20 antibody at a dilution of 1:100 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) for 30 min at room temperature, followed by incubation with a secondary antibody, Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit IgG (Vector Laboratories, Newark, CA, USA), for 30 min. Nuclei were stained with Hoechst 33,342. Stained sections were observed under a BZ-X700 fluorescent microscope.

## Tumorigenicity assay
Tumor cells at a concentration of 1 × 101, 1 × 102, 1 × 103 or 1 × 104 cells were collected in 50 µL of PBS. Each sample was mixed with 50 µL Matrigel (BD Biosciences), and a total volume of 100 µL was injected subcutaneously into four sites of SCID Beige mouse under anesthesia. Four mice for each group (HCT116: ZsGreen- and enriched ZsGreen+, DLD1: ZsGreen- and enriched ZsGreen+) were prepared and a total of 16 mice were used in this experiment. Four weeks after injection, tumors were resected and measured. Tumor volume (V) was calculated as L × W2 × 0.5, where L and W represent the larger and the smaller tumor diameter, respectively. All animals were housed and maintained under conditions of 50% humidity and a 12 h:12 h light: dark cycle. The mice were fed a standard pellet diet (MF, Oriental Yeast Co., Tokyo, Japan) and tap water ad libitum. Euthanization was performed by exsanguination under anesthesia (4–5% isoflurane). The Osaka University Animal Experiment Committee approved all animal experiments. This study was approved by the institutional review board of our institution (Permission No. #15,218).

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed in JMP Pro 13.0 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). Each experiment was repeated at least three times. Data are expressed as the mean ± SD. Mean values were compared using the Student’s t test or one-way ANOVA. P-values < 0.05 were considered significant.

## Results

## Enrichment of ZsGreen+ CRC cells
After transduction of ODC degron into HCT116, DLD1, and SW480 cells, flow cytometric analysis indicated that these CRC cell lines had only minimal ZsGreen+ cells at an incidence of 0.2%, 0.1%, and 0.05% (Fig.  a). After sorting the ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells, they were grown for 4 days, when the proportion of ZsGreen+ cells increased from 0.2% to 20.0% (Fig.  b and c). By sorting the ZsGreen+ DLD1 and SW480 cells, the ZsGreen+ cell fraction increased up to 52.0% and 28.0%, respectively (Fig.  b). These experiments were repeated at least two times and similar results were obtained.
 

## Enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells had increased expression of cancer stem markers
To determine whether enriched ZsGreen+ cells would retain cancer stem-like properties, we examined the levels of several CSC markers, including Lgr5, Bmi1, and Lrig1 – . After enrichment (Fig.  b), the ZsGreen+ cell fraction was collected and directly placed into Trizol RNA storage reagent. RT-qPCR indicated that the enriched ZsGreen+ cells had significantly higher RNA levels of Lgr5, Bmi1, and Lrig1 compared with ZsGreen- cells in the three CRC cell lines (Fig.  a). These experiments were repeated at least two times and similar results were obtained. Moreover, western blot analysis showed that several CSC markers increased at protein level in enriched ZsGreen+ cells; Bmi1 in HCT116, Lgr5 and Bmi1in DLD1, and Lrig1 in SW480 (Fig.  b).
 

## Chemoresistance in enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells
In HCT116 cells, enriched ZsGreen+ cells had higher resistance to chemotherapy with 5-FU and L-OHP than ZsGreen- cells at the indicated drug concentrations at 48 h (P < 0.05, Fig.  ). At 72 h, significantly higher resistance was found with treatment of 5-FU or L-OHP (data not shown). Similar chemoresistance was noted in DLD1 and SW480 cells at 72 h (P < 0.05, Fig.  ). These experiments were repeated at least two times and similar results were obtained.
 

## In vitro sphere formation and in vivo tumor formation
Enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells had the ability to generate significantly larger spheres in vitrothan the ZsGreen- HCT116 and DLD1 cells (Fig.  a). These experiments were repeated three times and similar results were obtained.
 By injecting ZsGreen- or enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells at a concentration of 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 cells into 4 sites of mouse flank (4 mouse in each group), 10,000 and 1,000 cells of HCT116 and DLD1 exclusively produced subcutaneous tumors (Table ). When 100 or 10 cells were injected, on the other hand, enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells tended to form subcutaneous tumors in nude mice compared with ZsGreen– cells (Table ). As for tumor size, 1, 000 ZsGreen+ CRC cells produced significantly larger tumors than ZsGreen- CRC cells (P < 0.05, Fig.  b).
 

## ZsGreen+ cells exhibited asymmetric division
Time-lapse microscopy showed that the enriched single ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells exhibited asymmetric cell division at 6 and 12 h, subsequently turning into one ZsGreen+ cell and several ZsGreen- daughter cells at 24 and 48 h (Fig.  a). Immunostaining showed that the ZsGreen- cells generally expressed cytokeratin 20 as red fluorescence, which indicates that the majority of cells are epithelial and differentiated. On the other hand, enriched ZsGreen+ cells occasionally underwent asymmetric cell division; one expressed a green fluorescent signal and the other expressed red fluorescence (Fig.  b).
 When we examined aurora kinase A and musashi 1 expression, both of which are reported to be tightly linked to asymmetric division , , gene expression was significantly up-regulated in enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells compared to ZsGreen- HCT116 cells (Fig.  c).

## Investigation of therapeutic miRNAs to suppress cancer stem-like cells using ZsGreen+ cells
Using a sufficient cell quantity, we performed comprehensive microRNA expression analysis using nCounter (NanoString Technologies, Seattle, WA, USA) and found that 15 miRNAs were down-regulated > 2.5-fold in ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells compared with ZsGreen- HCT116 cells (Supplementary Table ). Among them, cell viability assays indicated that miR-491-3p inhibited the cell viability of both ZsGreen+ and ZsGreen- HCT116 cells at 72 h to a similar extent as the putative tumor-suppressive miRNA-34a (Fig.  a). A time course study indicated that miR-491-3p significantly inhibited cell proliferation compared with the miR-negative control in ZsGreen+ HCT116 and DLD1 cells (Fig.  b). Moreover, miR-491-3p significantly decreased the expression of CSC markers Lgr5, Bmi1, and CD44v9 at the mRNA level compared with the miR-negative control in the both CRC cell lines (Fig.  c).
 

## Comparative gene expression profile between ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells and clinical CRC spheroids
We examined the expression of five CSC markers [ALCAM (CD166), PROM1(CD133), LGR5, BMI1, and CD44] in eight CRC spheroid samples that were established from PDXs derived from human CRC tissue samples (Fig.  a). For each CSC marker, we calculated the spheroid/primary tumor ratio. Notably, PROM1(CD133) levels increased > 2.0-fold in most spheroid samples (Fig.  b). In contrast, the other CSC markers presented a diverse fold change in each CRC case (Fig.  b, Table ).
 To investigate a possible correlation of gene expression between ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells and clinical spheroid samples, we made a list of the top 500 ranked genes expressed by ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells and checked whether each gene coincided with the gene in which the spheroid/primary tumor ratio was > 2.0 as a hallmark of cancer stemness. As a result, the matched genes were abundant among the top 200 genes expressed by ZsGreen+ cells (Fig.  a, Supplementary Fig. ). Furthermore, a tight correlation was revealed when the hit number was counted in every 50 ranks (P = 0.0002, Fig.  b).
 

## Discussion
According to the conventional CSC model, CSCs have the capacity for self-renewal, pluripotent differentiation, tumorigenicity from a small number of tumor cells, and resistance to anticancer drugs or irradiation, and non-stem tumor cells are differentiated from CSCs in tumor tissues – , – .
Actively dividing CSCs, which may be designated as TA cells, produce differentiated daughter cells and contribute to the formation of bulky tumors – . Eradication of CSCs is important to overcome cancer diseases, but an effective therapy targeting CSCs has yet to be established. One of the reasons is attributed to CSCs residing very rarely in tumor tissues, and this scarcity renders detailed analyses of CSCs difficult. In this study, we tried to expand actively dividing CSC model cells and to find novel gene sets related to CSCs through integration with information obtained from clinical spheroid samples.
Using the ZsGreen‐labeled degronODC (Gdeg) proteasome reporter system, studies have shown that human cancer cells, including colon, lung, pancreatic, and liver cancer with low proteasome activity, present stem cell properties, such as up-regulated expression of stem cell markers, resistance to chemotherapy and radiation, and enhanced tumorigenicity – . However, ZsGreen-labeled LPACs are generally rare, comprising < 1% of cells in most cell types. To allow various assays with CSCs, we attempted to increase ZsGreen-labeled LPACs by cell sorting and culture. With this enrichment process, we obtained a considerable increase in the population of ZsGreen+ cells, up to 20.0%, 52.0%, and 28.0% for HCT116, DLD1, and SW480 cells, respectively. Enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells exhibited enhanced resistance to chemotherapeutic treatments. Enriched ZsGreen+ CRC cells had increased expression of stem cell markers Lgr5, Bmi1, and Lrig1 at RNA level and several CSC markers were found to be increased also at protein level. Enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 and DLD1 cells maintained increased sphere-forming ability and enhanced tumorigenicity. Moreover, enriched HCT116 ZsGreen+ cells had asymmetric cell division.
With sufficient material, various assays become possible, including cell viability assays, examination of protein expression and biochemical function, and animal models using model CSCs for the therapeutic efficacy of test reagents. Using enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells, we examined miRNA expression that decreased > 2.5-fold in enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells compared with ZsGreen- cells. Among several therapeutic candidate miRNAs, we found that miR-491-3p was effective at inhibiting ZsGreen+ HCT116 and DLD1 cells and decreased the expression of stem markers Lgr5, Bmi1, and CD44v9, suggesting that this miRNA may be useful for suppressing cancer stemness. Although recent studies have reported that miR-491-3p functions as a tumor suppressor in non-small cell lung cancer and osteosarcoma cells , , no report has described a direct link between miR-491-3p and cancer stem properties. As one of the underlying mechanisms of inhibition of cancer stemness by miR-491-3p, we noticed that 3’UTR of CD44v9 (isoforms 1–3) and CD44 standard (isoform 4) contain predicted target region of miR-491-3p as searched by TargetScanHuman 8.0 and Basic Local Alignment Search Tool nucleotide BLAST . Indeed, miR-491-3p mimic decreased CD44v9 RNA expression both in HCT116 and in enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells, while CD44 standard RNA expression was relatively low (Supplementary Fig. a). At protein level, CD44v9 protein expression increased in enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells compared with parental cells and treatment with miR-491-3p mimic decreased CD44v9 protein expression compared with miR-negative control treatment (Supplementary Fig. S2b). On the other hand, no predicted target region was found in Lgr5 and Bmi1. Taken together, it is likely that miR-491-3p inhibits CD44v9 via direct binding and that down-regulation of Lgr5 and Bmi1 may be an indirect effect of miR-491-3p.
The property of cancer stemness is supposed to increase from primary tumors to PDXs, and further to spheroids established from the PDX , . The estimated density of CSCs is estimated to be 1/160–195 in PDX tumors serially transplanted onto immune-deficient mice and could be further concentrated to 1/30–45 in spheroid cultures . The formation of a sphere or spheroid is one of the hallmarks of CSCs , . Using eight pairs of primary CRC tumor tissue and spheroids generated from PDXs, we examined the extent to which cancer stemness was enhanced in the spheroids by quantifying the expression of five putative cancer stem markers. The cancer stemness of spheroids varied among CRC cases (Table ), and we observed broad diversity in CSC marker expression among individual CRC cases (Fig.  a&b). This finding is not unique because spheroid culture reflects not only cancer stemness, but hypoxia and nutrient starvation also affects gene expression profile in spheroids. In addition, micro-environmental mechanical stress could be involved – . Nevertheless, we observed a considerably tight correlation between highly up-regulated genes in enriched ZsGreen+ HCT116 cells and the genes up-regulated in spheroids relative to primary CRC tissue. Among ‘matched’ genes there are several CSC related genes. These include FAM64A, KIFC1, KIF18B, CDCA8, and RAD51AP1 – . Therefore, the enriched ZsGreen+ cell model is useful for identifying novel CSC-related genes, especially when combined with the data from clinical spheroid samples. The identification of novel CSC associated genes is currently in progress in our laboratory.
In conclusion, we successfully enriched the ZsGreen+ cancer cells, which ensures that sufficient cell materials are available for various genetic and biochemical assays. These model cells, with the aid of clinical spheroid samples, should be a powerful tool for identifying novel CSC-related genes.
