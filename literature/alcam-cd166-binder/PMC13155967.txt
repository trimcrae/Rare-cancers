## Abstract
Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) is driven by hematopoietic stem cells carrying leukemia-associated mutations that expand in the bone marrow. Several prior studies have revealed that the spatial organization of hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow affects clonal behaviors. Specifically, leukemic blasts have been found to expand almost exclusively in a subset of marrow cavities that are undergoing active bone remodeling, but whether these cavities also support the expansion of nonmalignant mutant clones has never been visualized. Although it is widely appreciated that systemic inflammation promotes the selection of mutant clones, this view has emerged without considering the potential heterogeneity in the inflammatory landscape shaped by local bone remodeling. Leveraging intravital imaging and a murine model of CHIP (Tet2+/−), we demonstrated transcriptional and functional compartmentalization of the marrow microenvironment. Macrophages within nonresorptive cavities are inherently anti-inflammatory, which suppresses disease-initiating Tet2+/− cells while preserving their healthy counterparts. Time-lapse imaging further revealed nontransient association between Tet2+/− clones and CD206+ macrophages. Spatially resolved single-cell transcriptomic profiling and functional assessment revealed that physiological bone remodeling influences CD206+ macrophage plasticity and cytokine secretion, which regulate the clonal burden. In addition, antitumor immunity alteration within the microenvironment occurred as early as the formation of initial clones. Suppressing bone remodeling with zoledronate or targeting macrophage-associated niche factors mitigated clonal development. Collectively, our study reveals a previously unrecognized inflammatory landscape shaped by local bone remodeling. The finding presents targetable mechanisms and warrants further studies on the use and precautions of bone-modulating management in clonal blood disorders.

Somatic Tet2 mutations in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are a common cause of clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP). Lee and colleagues used spatial intravital imaging and single-cell transcriptomic profiling to dissect how the bone marrow (BM) niche is remodeled in a Tet2-mutant murine model of CHIP. The authors show that distinct macrophage activation states segregate individual BM cavities into “hot” and “cold” niches that determine whether Tet2-mutant clones expand or remain dormant, helping to explain, at least in part, the heterogenous natural history of Tet2-mutant CHIP in patients.

## Introduction
Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) widely affects blood neoplasms and cardiovascular disorders. , , It is driven by hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSCs/HSPCs) carrying leukemia-associated mutations that expand in the bone marrow. Restraining CHIP clonal expansion while preserving a steady pool of healthy cells will be central in prevention and therapies.
Several prominent studies have shown that the spatial organization of hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow affects cell fate and function. , , , , , , In myeloproliferative neoplasms, differential cell expansion and disease phenotypes were observed when the mutant clones carrying the same driver mutation are associated with distinct marrow microenvironments (niches). Based on the intravital tracking of the Mds1GFP/+
Flt3Cre long-term HSCs and the HoxA9-Meis1 leukemic blasts, we showed that the bone marrow is compartmentalized into cavities. Cavities with bone resorptive (RE) activities supported the expansion of stressed HSCs and leukemia cells, whereas cells remained solitary in cavities predominated by bone formation. , 
The role of skeletal homeostasis in regulating hematopoiesis has been well documented , , ; however, the existence of nonresorptive (NR) sites that suppress cell expansion is a relatively new concept. , A plausible hypothesis for the compartmental phenomenon is that distinct stages of bone turnover shape the intrinsic differences in the niche cells that reside within the bone marrow cavities. Bone remodeling is a process known to release danger signals (eg, calcium ions and bone particles) that provoke inflammatory cascades. Although inflammation is widely recognized to regulate clonal disorders, , , , this view has emerged without taking into consideration the potential heterogeneity across the multitude of bone marrow cavities. Nonuniform cell expansion observed in stressed hematopoiesis and leukemia models raised questions of whether the spatial localization of CHIP clones in the bone marrow also regulates clonal development.
TET2 loss-of-function is a prevalent mutation spanning across CHIP, myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), and leukemia. , , We recently reported that ultralow-dose (0.5 Gy) irradiation provides a low-grade inflammation setting to study the selection of CHIP clones in a physiologically relevant microenvironment. Leveraging longitudinal tracking, we found the nontransient association of Tet2+/− clones with CD206+ bone marrow macrophages (MϕBM) and Tet2+/− cell expansion almost exclusively occurred in cavities that had undergone bone resorption. Spatially resolved single-cell transcriptomic profiling and functional assessment showed that physiological bone remodeling influences CD206+ macrophage plasticity and cytokine secretion that regulate the clonal burden. Suppressing bone remodeling or targeting the MϕBM-associated niche factors mitigated clonal development.

## Materials and methods

## Animals
Wild-type (WT) male and female C57BL/6J mice were purchased from the Jackson Laboratory and bred in house at the University of Rochester Medical Center. Heterozygous B6 (Cg)-Tet2tm1.2Rao/J transgenic mice (courtesy of Laura M. Calvi laboratory) were crossed with the C57BL/6-Tg (UBC-GFP)30Scha/J (UBI-GFP) reporter mice (JAX 004353). Heterozygous Csf1r-cre (JAX 029206) was crossed with homozygous Il-10FL (JAX 036598). All animal experiments conducted in this study are in accordance and approved by the University Committee on Animal Resources (UCAR) under protocol number UCAR-2022-001E. Experiments were performed in accordance with UCAR ethical standards and guidelines listed in the US Animal Welfare Act, Public Health Service Policy (University of Rochester D16-00188 [A3292-01]), and the Public Health Act of New York State. UCAR is accredited by the Association for the Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care.

## Whole bone marrow transplantation in irradiated mice and peripheral blood flow cytometry
Two to 2.5 million whole bone marrow cells (Tet2+/− or Tet2WT; green fluorescent protein–positive/negative [GFP+/−]) harvested from the long bones from adult mice (3- to 12-month-old) were transplanted into recipient C57/BL6 mice (8 to 12-week-old). Both females and males were used. Recipient mice were irradiated using a cesium irradiator operating at a dose of 0.5 Gy with a 2- to 6-hour interval before transplantation. Peripheral blood analysis of transplanted recipients was performed in 4-week intervals for 20 weeks. Whole bone marrow analyses were performed at week 1 and after week 20 ( , available on the Blood website).

## Intravital imaging of mouse calvarium and image quantifications
Mice were prepared for calvarial in vivo imaging using a video-rate 2-photon microscope as previously described. Longitudinal tracking was performed for 60 minutes with 1-min time intervals. See the for hardware setup and image quantifications.

## Image-guided tissue harvest for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)
The calvarial bone was imaged with intravital imaging to identify the coordinates of marrow cavities containing dense and sparse GFP+
Tet2+/− cells during early clonal development (week 1) and higher-burden stage (week 22 after Tet2+/− transplantation). NR and RE cavities were labeled with bone front dye 2 days before imaging and on the day of imaging to distinguish between cavity types. Animals were then perfused with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove red blood cells before performing image-guided tissue harvest. Individual segments of bone of ∼0.5 × 0.6 mm2 were then collected and rescreened via imaging to allocate the samples into hot spots (intermediate to high burden) vs cold spots containing only a few solitary cells ( ). NR and RE cavities were isolated after undergoing the same process (data available upon request).

## scRNA-seq and analysis
Samples were processed using 10× Genomics platform (Chromium Single Cell 3 v2 Reagent kits, NovaSeq 6000, 100 000 reads per cell). Read count matrices were obtained by aligning the reads to the mm10 mouse reference genome using CellRanger (version 3.0.2), then filtered and analyzed using Seurat (version 5.1). Cells were annotated using both EnrichR (Seurat) and signature genes ( ). The F4/80+ cells were subclustered based on conserved markers of each subset across all conditions. All statistically significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were used as an input to DAVID Functional Annotation for Gene Ontology enrichment analyses. A false discovery rate (FDR) value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Cell culture
Sorted mouse F4/80+/Ly6c−/CD206+/CD74+ macrophages ( ) cultures were maintained using 10% fetal bovine serum RPMI 1640 media (catalog no. 10-041-CV; Corning) with 1× penicillin-streptomycin (catalog no. 15140122; Gibco). A total of 30 × 103 to 50 × 103 cells were seeded to 96-well collagen-coated plates as described previously. Six hours after plating, the culture media was then replaced with media supplemented with or without calcium chloride (catalog no. J63122.AE; Invitrogen) to desired calcium concentrations, hydroxyapatite nanoparticles (100 μg/mL, catalog no. 677418; Sigma), and lipopolysaccharide (10 ng/mL, catalog no. L4391; Sigma). Cell supernatants were collected at 12 hours after treatment for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (catalog no. MHSLB00; R&D Systems).

## Animal treatments
Interferon gamma (IFN-γ, 100 μg/mouse, intraperitoneally [IP], catalog no. 575306; BioLegend) was administered 18 hours before intravital imaging of major histocompatibility complex II (MHC-II). Zoledronate (ZOL, catalog no. 1724827; Sigma) was prepared freshly (15μM in PBS) before treatment (0.4 μg/mouse, IP, every 2 days for a week). DUSP1/6 inhibitor (BCI, 10 mg/kg, IP, catalog no. 317496; Sigma) was diluted in corn oil (2.5 mg/mL). Anti–MHC-II (1 mg/mouse diluted in PBS, catalog no. BE0108; BioXCell) and clodronate liposome (4.6 mg/mouse, catalog no. CLD-8909; Encapsula NanoSciences) were administered via retro-orbital injection. Interleukin-4 (IL4)/IL-4 complex (IL-4c) was prepared in a 1:5 ratio (IV, catalog no. 214-14-20UG; PeproTech; catalog no. BE0045, clone: 11B11; BioXcell). Mice were imaged on the day before and after BCI, anti–MHC-II, clodronate liposome, and IL-4/IL-4c treatment ( ).

## Results

## Potential influence of bisphosphonates on MDS incidence
We previously found that stressed HSCs and leukemic blasts expand almost exclusively in marrow cavities with bone resorptive activities, but not in cavities predominated by bone deposition. , To investigate whether inhibiting bone resorption may have an impact on the development of premalignant clonal disorders in patients, we performed a cohort study using a federated patient database, TriNetX ( ) and examined the risks of MDS in patients with osteoporosis treated with antiresorptive bisphosphonates (BSs) compared with patients who did not receive osteoporotic treatment. The incidence of MDS was lower in the group treated with BS (hazard ratio, 0.54; P < .001; ). This result led to a hypothesis that distinct stages of bone remodeling may shape the fitness landscape of mutant clones and prompted us to further examine the spatial organizations of CHIP clones in the bone marrow. 

## Expansion of Tet2+/− clones in the bone marrow is restricted to marrow cavities undergoing active bone remodeling
We recently reported that ultralow-dose (0.5 Gy) whole-body irradiation allowed for stable engraftment and intravital tracking of nonmalignant clones. Leveraging the same approach ( A), we showed that the model allowed propagation of Tet2-deficient lineages (Tet2+/−) ( B), deviating peripheral blood parameters with elevated lymphocytes and monocytes ( A). This is confirmed by the expansion of progeny populations in the marrow at both early stage (week 1) and intermediate stage (week 22) ( B). The fractions of mutant SLAM-HSCs (Lin−/Kit+/Sca1+/CD48−/CD150+) and KLS-HSPCs (Lin−/Kit+/Sca1+) in the bone marrow upon transplantation were close to the fraction reported in the global Tet2−/− mice, reduced during propagating in the WT microenvironment, and bounced back over time ( C). 
Notably, despite a systemic low-grade inflammation induced by 0.5 Gy irradiation, we found via intravital imaging that the expansion of Tet2+/− clones was highly nonuniform, forming growth hot (dense Tet2+/− cells) and cold (solitary Tet2+/− cells) spots ( D). Further analyses revealed the presence of ESAM+/CD150+ cells, and the CD34+
Tet2+/− HSPCs in the growth hot spots, suggesting the disease initiating potential of these cavities ( E; B). Recognizing the overlapping biology of CHIP and leukemia, we asked whether the RE cavities served as growth hot spots to support the development of CHIP clones. Using the sequential delivery of bone front staining ( A), we showed that heterogeneity in the stages of bone turnover exists before CHIP colonization ( B). There was a significant expansion in RE cavities, which was not due to early preferential cell seeding on day 1. WT cells exhibited the same trend but expanded to a less degree ( F). During CHIP progression, the Tet2+/− burden in a cavity stayed steady or decreased when the marrow cavity was transitioning into NR status ( G; C), suggesting that bone resorption is not just the consequence of Tet2+/− cell expansion.

## Intravital tracking reveals affinity of Tet2+/− cells to nonmigratory macrophages (MϕBM) in the bone marrow
Prompted by the spatial compartmentalization of Tet2+/− clusters, we asked whether Tet2+/− clones interact with the nonmigratory niche cells, which are either phenotypically or functionally distinct among cavities. Time-lapse tracking of Tet2+/− clones in the calvarium revealed spatial association between the marrow autofluorescent (AF) population and Tet2+/− cells ( A-B) irrespective of the migratory behavior of the Tet2+/− cells. 
The identity of marrow AF cells has long been postulated as macrophages but, to our knowledge, it has never been characterized. , Comparing in vivo imaging and/or flow cytometry analysis, we confirmed the presence of F4/80 pan-macrophage marker, CD206, CD74, and VCAM1 on most of the AF cells. A minor AF/CD206+/CD74+/VCAM1+ fraction is contributed by the CD36+ subset ( C-D; ; ). Within the Adgre1(F4/80)high cells from the WT and Tet2+/− scRNA-seq data, the Apoehigh/Adgre4high/Ly6Clow MϕBM clusters are as follows: (1) Mrc1+(Cd206)/Vcam1high that has classical M2 macrophage profiles, (2) Cd206+/Cd74+/Vcam1medium that has mixed expression, and (3) Cd36+ with low Cd206 but high M1 macrophage profiles ( E; A-D). These results suggested that the microscopy of AF cells does not represent all MϕBM, which includes a substantial fraction of the CD36+ subset. Consequently, discrepancies may present between instruments. In vivo imaging sampled primarily the endosteal zones due to attenuation of immunofluorescence with depth. A higher fraction of Cd36+ subset from scRNA-seq characterizations may suggest discrepancies in the transcriptome and proteome ( E).
Next, to examine whether MϕBM were required for Tet2+/− development in the marrow, we performed longitudinal imaging to track clonal development before and after depleting MϕBM using clodronate liposomes. A single dose of clodronate liposomes efficiently depleted AF MϕBM, along with a significant suppression of Tet2+/− clones in the bone marrow ( F; A-B). We also found that these AF cells are stable residents within individual cavities, revealed by their low motility, compared with the Tet2+/− mutant cells that could reach a total displacement of over 40 μm over an hour ( C). The number of AF cells is comparable between NR and RE cavity types ( D). These results suggested an affinity of Tet2+/− cells to MϕBM, instead of macrophages mounting an immune response and migrating toward Tet2+/− hot spots.

## Tet2+/− clones exhibited spatial association with the CD206+ MϕBM starting from the early stage of clonal development
A growing body of evidence has suggested that MϕBM participate in HSC maintenance , , and quality control. In addition, the CD206+ MϕBM were recently shown to promote the survival of acute myeloid leukemia blasts. , Therefore, we next examined whether the premalignant Tet2+/− clones also exhibit spatial association with the CD206+ subset. Notably, although CD206+ AF number may vary between mice and regions and CD206 was highly expressed in healthy mice ( E; D), the nonrandom association of CD206+ AF cells and Tet2+/− clones was observed at both week 1 and week 20 posttransplantation ( G-I). All these results suggested the participation of nonmutant MϕBM during the pathogenesis stage (week 1) and that the same microniche supports the Tet2+/− cells at the CHIP stage (week 20).

## Image-guided spatial single-cell sequencing revealed a transcriptionally distinct MϕBM landscape and an anti-inflammatory characteristic in the cold spots
Given that native CD206+ MϕBM are associated with Tet2+/− cells in both the hot and cold growth spots ( G), we next asked whether these MϕBM are transcriptionally different based on their spatial localization. To answer this question, we harvested “bone buttons” that typically contain 1 to 2 marrow cavities from the growth hot spots (dense Tet2+/− cells) and cold spots (solitary Tet2+/− cells) under 2-photon image guidance ( A). Calvarial tissue pieces from similar anatomical areas were also harvested from a control mouse that received 0.5 Gy irradiation without receiving Tet2+/− cells. Subsequently, cells from the 3 groups were isolated for scRNA-seq. The bone button harvest techniques revealed classical hematopoietic populations consistent with existing literature ( B; A-B). , Based on the intravital observation, we sought to phenotype the heterogeneity of the MϕBM population in growth hot spots, cold spots, and the control group. The similar fraction of Cd206+/Cd74+, Cd206+/Vcam1+, and Cd36+ subsets between the hot and cold spots suggests that the difference may lie at the transcriptional level ( C). 
Considering Cd206-expressing cells were enriched in the Cd74 and Vcam1 subsets ( E), we next examined the DEGs of these clusters between the hot spots, cold spots, and control groups. In agreement with the proinflammatory nature of Tet2+/− clones, , , transcriptional profiles from the hot spots showed an expected upregulation of cellular response to lipopolysaccharide and immune cell recruitment ( C). Notably, IL-1β is well documented to promote myeloid skewing and CHIP development. , , , The results suggested that increased Il-1b from “healthy” macrophages was present at the early clonal expansion before the clinical definition of CHIP ( D). Despite being a classical M2 phenotype, the Vcam1+ cluster underwent transcriptional reprogramming toward proinflammatory profiles (Hmga1, 
Casp4, and Zhx2 based on significant Gene Ontology terms) ( D). Within this proinflammatory setting, the most strongly DEG, was Dusp1, along with the increased expression of genes (Zfp36 and Socs3) that have been implicated in suppressing the antitumor immunity ( B). Although a similar fraction of CD36 subset, B and T cells were found across conditions ( D-E), these populations exhibited defective antitumor immune response during early clonal development ( F).
Alternatively, IFN-γ has been shown to promote the selection of CHIP clones. Although the Tet2+/− cells are present in the cold spots and irradiation itself increases antigen presentation ( F), MϕBM from these locations were significantly downregulated in MHC-II and in response to IFN-γ ( E; C). In addition, the healthy HSPC cluster in the cold spots was found enriched with quiescent signatures compared with that in the control group, with a significantly downregulated cell cycle (FDR = 5.71E−24), cell division (1.19E−23), and response to oxidative stress (FDR = 8.31E−3) ( G). Over the course of CHIP progression, both the hot and cold spots underwent aggravated inflammation and autocrine signaling. The cold spots also demonstrated an increased response to type II interferon ( H).
These results suggested that native MϕBM were altered to carry pro-CHIP factors at the extremely early stage of clonal development. The hot spots are enriched with Il-1b but exhibited defective immunity. In contrast, the cold spots remained “immunologically cold” even in the presence of proinflammatory Tet2+/− cells and may favor the WT HSPCs in the context of clonal competition.

## Physiological bone remodeling shapes a nonuniform inflammatory landscape of MϕBM that regulates Tet2+/− clonal burden
Given the preferential clonal expansion in RE cavities and recognizing that bone resorption is a process of releasing danger signals that provoke inflammation cascades, we asked whether the inflammatory landscapes are predefined by physiological bone remodeling, leading to the selection of Tet2+/− clones in the RE cavities. We found that CD206+/CD74+ MϕBM excrete higher IL-1β when exposed to danger signals (calcium ions and hydroxyapatite particles , , ) released by bone resorption ( A-B). Reanalyzing the scRNA-seq data previously published and from our own data set, we showed that MϕBM from NR cavities exhibited a downregulated inflammatory process ( G) and an increase in classical M2 markers ( C) when compared with those from RE cavities. In agreement with downregulated MHC-II in the growth cold spots, intravital imaging confirmed a significantly lower MHC-II staining in the NR sites at the steady state and under IFN-γ stimulation that promotes M1 polarization ( D-E; and ). Given that IL-10 is one of the key anti-inflammatory cytokines secreted by M2 MϕBM that also suppress antigen presentation, we next asked whether it is the factor that maintains the cold spots. Using Csf1r-cre; Il10floxed mice, we demonstrated that the deletion of Il-10 from MϕBM markedly reduced the cold spots and aggravated Tet2 expansion ( F-G). Although Tet2 growth hot spots and RE cavities are both inflammatory, DEG analyses between the 2 revealed consistent upregulation of Dusp1 gene in the Tet2+/− growth hot spots ( H), supporting that Dusp1 expression in the hot spots is acquired from the presence of the mutant clones. All these results uncovered that physiological bone remodeling shapes a nonuniform inflammatory landscape of MϕBM that regulates Tet2+/− clonal burden. 

## Inhibiting bone remodeling using antiresorptive ZOL suppresses expansion of Tet2+/− cells
Compartmentalized inflammatory landscapes shaped by local bone remodeling prompted us to assess whether US Food and Drug Administration–approved anti–bone RE treatment promotes the cold spots and curbs CHIP progression. With a course of ZOL treatment ( A), calvarial imaging results showed a significant reduction in Tet2+/− cells per surveyed volume of bone marrow at 1 week after transplantation ( A-D). This is accompanied by disrupted spatial association between Tet2+/− cells ( E-F) and MϕBM despite a notable increase in the CD206+/CD74Neg population ( G). CD74 is a chaperon protein of MHC-II, suggesting that the microenvironment was primed toward the cold spots. Supporting the imaging finding, transcriptomic profiling revealed that ZOL downregulated cell adhesion pathways for cell retention (eg, Vcam1 ) ( H) and upregulated phagocytic pathways in the RE cavities ( I). Consistently, T and B cells were also found to have upregulated nitric oxide synthase–binding activities involved in cell activation (eg, Calm1, Actb, Actg1, Cd74, and T-cell FDR = 0.03 and B-cell FDR = 0.017), phagocytosis (B-cell FDR = 6.98E−4), and B-cell receptor signaling (FDR = 0.003) ( B). 
Considering that ZOL has been shown to transiently enrich HSPCs, we next examined whether the treatment enriched the disease-initiating progenitors. Whole bone marrow analyses at week 1 revealed a trend of reduction in the mutant KLS population ( C-D). A significant reduction in the mutant-to-WT KLS fraction was noted in the ZOL-treated group, likely attributed by a mild increase in their healthy counterparts. Although such a change subsided at week 20, there was no apparent enrichment in the disease-initiating cells ( C-D). Despite the Tet2+/− burden being unabated in the peripheral blood ( E-F), the ZOL-treated group skewed the disease phenotype toward age-matched healthy controls, revealed by the complete blood count performed at week 20 ( J).

## M2 polarization, inhibiting Dusp1, or neutralizing MHC-II restrained formation of growth hot spots in the bone marrow
To further examine whether Tet2 cell clonal development in vivo can be controlled via modulating macrophage polarization or the top differentially expressed niche factors induced in the hot and cold spots, we treated the mice with (1) IL-4/anti–IL-4c that have been shown to induce alternatively activated macrophages while enhancing adaptive immunity, , (2) Dusp1 inhibitor (BCI) when apparent hot spots were formed (day 6), or (3) MHC-II–neutralizing antibody (InVivoMab) during the initiation stage of clonal expansion (day 3). By tracking the same calvarial regions, we found that the formation of growth hot spots was restrained in IL-4c–, BCI-, and InVivoMab-treated groups compared with those in vehicle-treated controls ( ), without inducing cytotoxicity on the surrounding marrow macrophages. 

## Discussion
In this study, we aimed to identify the microenvironmental factors pertinent to marrow cavities that mediate the expansion of CHIP clones. Leveraging intravital imaging, we showed that the biology of niche cells and CHIP clones is affected by the bone marrow cavities where they reside through local bone remodeling activities. MϕBM are inherently less immunogenic in NR cavities, which suppress disease-initiating cells while protecting their healthy counterparts. Suppressing bone remodeling or targeting the MϕBM niche factors reduced the clonal burden in the bone marrow. Overall, this study reveals a previously unrecognized spatial heterogeneity in hematopoietic niches and highlights its contribution during disease progression.
The roles of skeletal homeostasis in blood malignancies have been widely reported; however, they have been largely focused on the degradation of the osteogenic components at a higher disease burden. , , It has not been well appreciated that the bone marrow is compartmentalized into cavities, of which the NR cavities represent ∼15% of calvarial bone marrow and were found suppressing disease-initiating cells across multiple murine models. , Although vascular density may vary with bone remodeling, the nonuniform expansion is not biased by preferential homing to RE sites ( F). Notably, the CD36 subset is enriched with bone catabolic genes (eg, Cx3cr1 and Il1b), suggesting its potential contribution to the increase in inflammatory milieu and bone resorption during disease progression. Nevertheless, the overall bone remodeling landscapes in mice that underwent Tet2+/− transplantation were not significantly altered from our prior measurements at the steady state. These results suggested that bone remodeling is not just a passive responder to the proinflammatory clones.
We demonstrated that nonmigratory CD206+ macrophages are closely engaged with Tet2+/− cells and have distinct transcriptomic profiles in the hot vs cold spots at an extremely early stage of clonal development. Note that the DEGs from the hot vs cold comparison yielded some different genes compared with those in hot vs control mice that only received irradiation. We reason that the presence of the Tet2+/− cells may change macrophage expression at the base level and affect data normalization. Although the CD206+ subset has been reported to secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines, we show that their phenotype in the bone marrow depends on local bone remodeling activities. IL-1 is a known pro-CHIP factor, yet the roles of IL-10 are less clear and are likely dependent on the disease stage. Our results suggested that its role is to promote the cold spots, whereas it has been shown to suppress antitumor immunity in high-risk patients. Although ZOL was shown to deplete tumor-associated macrophages in the solid tumor models, , , we showed that ZOL (at this dose regimen) did not deplete MϕBM; rather, it decreased anchors for Tet2+/− clones and reversed the antitumor immunity. The CD206+/CD74Neg cluster (Cx3Cr1low) that increased after ZOL treatment resemble the phenotypes reported by Chow et al and Dutta et al, which may have roles in the maintenance of healthy HSCs , and in erythropoiesis. , This macrophage subset expresses low to mid-levels of osteomac markers such as Alcam (CD166). , , Overall, MϕBM and osteoclasts have been well reviewed for shared roles such as promoting bone resorption, antigen presentation, and immunosuppression. , These common features observed in the growth hot spots suggested that the 2 populations likely provide synergy on promoting Tet2+/− clones. However, the depletion of macrophages using clodronate liposome had much more pronounced effects on Tet2+/− depletion than using ZOL (that induced osteoclast apoptosis). Our data suggested that the CD206+ MϕBM might help anchor Tet2+/− clones. Their spatial proximity to Tet2+/− cells may also provide stronger IL-1 and IL-10 gradients to mutant cells compared with those by osteoclasts that are not professionally known to secrete these cytokines.
Overall, to our knowledge, this study presents the first in vivo imaging of CHIP and reveals the predisposition of pro- and anti-CHIP microenvironments during pathogenesis. The discovery of the proinflammatory and anti-inflammatory microniches in intestinal tissue lays the foundation of understanding the mechanisms controlling immune tolerance to local microbiota while defending against external pathogens. , To our knowledge, this is the first report in the bone marrow, in which our results uncovered the spatial compartmentalization of MϕBM, showing that phenotypically identical MϕBM subsets can exhibit distinct transcriptomic profiles, antigen presentation, and cytokine secretion shaped by local bone remodeling. Our study is limited by the technical limitations to measure low levels of IL-10 or local cytokine levels within individual cavities using commercial kits. Nevertheless, inflammatory stress such as aging and chemotherapy can substantially increase RE cavities. This finding warrants future investigations on bisphosphonate exposure or bone marrow macrophage polarization (IL-10/MHC-II) in relation to TET2 clonal dynamics and age-related premalignant clonal disorders.
