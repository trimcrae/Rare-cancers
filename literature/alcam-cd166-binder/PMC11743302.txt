## Abstract
Liver cancer stands as the fifth leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) comprises approximately 85%–90% of all primary liver malignancies. However, only 20–30% of HCC patients qualify for curative therapy, primarily due to the absence of reliable tools for early detection and prognosis of HCC. This underscores the critical need for molecular biomarkers for HCC management. Since proteins reflect disease status directly, proteomics has been utilized in biomarker developments for HCC. In particular, proteomics coupled with liquid chromatography-mass spectrometer (LC-MS) methods facilitate the process of discovering biomarker candidates for diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic strategies.
In this work, we investigated LC-MS-based proteomics methods through recent reference reviews, with a particular focus on sample preparation and LC-MS methods appropriate for the discovery of HCC biomarkers and their clinical applications.
We classified proteomics studies of HCC according to sample types, and we examined the coverage of protein biomarker candidates based on LC-MS methods in relation to study scales and goals. Comprehensively, we proposed protein biomarker candidates categorized by sample types and biomarker types for appropriate clinical use.
In this review, we summarized recent LC-MS-based proteomics studies on HCC and proposed potential protein biomarkers. Our findings are expected to expand the understanding of HCC pathogenesis and enhance the efficiency of HCC diagnosis and prognosis, thereby contributing to improved patient outcomes.
Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Proteomics, Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry, Protein biomarker

## Introduction
1.1.

## Limitations in current surveillance of hepatocellular carcinoma
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the fifth most common cancer and ranks third in terms of mortality among all types of carcinoma worldwide, despite substantial progress in early diagnosis and treatment options [ ]. The morbidity of HCC is increasing globally due to the rising prevalence of hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, and chronic liver diseases such as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) or non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) related to metabolic syndrome—most frequently the underlying etiological factors of HCC [ , , , , , , ]. Consequently, the number of patients at risk of developing HCC is gradually increasing each year, necessitating parallel structured surveillance of HCC.
HCC surveillance encompasses a comprehensive range of methods aimed at detecting HCC and monitoring its progression in patients. The primary objectives of surveillance include early-stage HCC diagnosis and the evaluation of therapeutic effects to facilitate complete remission. Transabdominal ultrasonography (US), recognized as a safe and rapid method for detecting neoplastic masses, currently stands as the sole recommended method for HCC surveillance. Despite its demonstrated cost-effectiveness, the sensitivity of US depends on various factors such as patient characteristics, imaging device quality, and operator competence. Notably, previous reports indicate that the sensitivity of liver US is diminished in obese patients with NASH, making it less reliable for HCC detection in such cases [ ]. In patients with liver cirrhosis (LC), the discrimination of regenerative nodules from malignant tumors using US proves challenging, reducing the sensitivity of US for early HCC detection by 32% [ , ]. Meanwhile, HCC surveillance through contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance tomography (MRT) is comparatively expensive compared to liver US. Moreover, these methods are associated with additional radiation exposure, elevating the risk of carcinogenesis and/or contrast media-related deterioration of organs.
As current surveillance methods based on imaging modalities often exhibit insufficient diagnostic performance or economic disadvantages, there has been a search for complementary methods in HCC surveillance. It has been established that differentially expressed biomolecules, such as genes or proteins, can provide insight into physical conditions, serving as indicators or causative factors of diseases. These biomolecules, widely called as molecular biomarkers, are anticipated to play a crucial role in HCC diagnosis and prognosis. These biomolecules exhibit specific expressions that enable the differentiation of diseases, such as HCC, from other conditions in patient-derived specimens, including cells, tissue, blood, urine, etc. Tissue biomarkers or HCC cell biomarkers can also serve as identifiers of molecular subtypes or as target molecules for novel drugs. Meanwhile, serological biomarkers have garnered attention as valuable diagnostic tools due to their minimally invasive measurement through blood sampling.

1.2.

## Current protein biomarkers of HCC
Proteins as well as post-translational modifications (PTMs) play a pivotal role in most biological processes. Investigations are underway to ascertain their potential use as biomarkers for HCC. Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) stands out as a well-known and widely utilized serological protein biomarker for HCC diagnosis. However, recent surveillance guidelines in the United States and Europe have excluded AFP measurement due to its low sensitivity and specificity. This decision is grounded in previous reports indicating that nearly 80% of HCCs with small tumors (<3 cm) do not exhibit elevated AFP levels, resulting in a sensitivity decrease to 25% [ ]. Despite this, many physicians continue to combine serum AFP measurement with US to minimize the risk of overlooking small lesions in the cirrhotic liver, which may remain undetected by US alone.
Other notable serological biomarkers include the ratio of glycosylated AFP (L3 fraction, AFP-L3) to total AFP and des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin (DCP), also known as prothrombin induced by vitamin K absence II (PIVKA II) [ , , ]. Several studies have demonstrated that combining these biomarkers with imaging data, followed by clinical manifestation, can detect HCC with a respectable sensitivity of up to 94% [ ]. However, the current combinations of these markers are limited to detecting early-stage HCC, displaying a sensitivity of less than 63% [ , ]. This limitation underscores the necessity for alternative or additional biomarkers to enhance the diagnostic performance of HCC. Additionally, ongoing efforts aim to identify protein biomarkers capable of discriminating molecular subtypes of HCC or serving as targets for novel drugs.

2.

## Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)-based proteomics as a navigator for biomarker discovery
Classical antibody-based protein quantification assays, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), can quantify the expressions of a single or a few proteins. Although these measurements are highly specific, they have drawbacks in capturing changes in the expression of multiple proteins simultaneously. Additionally, these assays require epitope-specific antibodies, which may not be universally available for every protein. In cases where a commercially available antibody is absent, its production involves a costly and tedious manufacturing process, leading to delays in ongoing experiments. Moreover, the variability in the quality of mass-produced antibodies undermines the validity of biochemical experiments [ ]. In recent developments, novel assays, including bead- or aptamer-based assays, along with proximity extension assays, have emerged, supplementing traditional methods and showcasing enhanced multiplex quantification capabilities. Despite their considerable advancement, these assays predominantly emphasize the measurement of known proteins of interest rather than facilitating the discovery of novel protein biomarkers [ ].
LC-MS has evolved into a powerful tool for the identification and quantification of the proteome. Through the integration of high-resolution LC-based separations with fast, sensitive, and accurate MS analysis, LC-MS-based techniques have facilitated quantitative measurements of more than ten thousand proteins simultaneously, comparable to transcriptome-scale coverage. Modern LC-MS-based proteomics, especially, enables a reliable and robust measurement of low-abundant proteins in complex specimens, including transcription factors and growth factors. This high proteome coverage has been achieved primarily due to gradual improvements in the resolution, sensitivity, and speed of MS instruments. Additionally, advancements in proteomic sample preparation methods, such as filter-assisted methods, help researchers analyze the deep proteome. The integration of these improvements not only accurately quantifies thousands of proteins but also allows the discovery of novel protein biomarkers that are dysregulated under medical conditions. Consequently, LC-MS-based proteomics presents distinctive advantages in the discovery of novel protein biomarkers compared to antibody-based assays [ , , , , ].
2.1.

## Untargeted LC-MS-based proteomics
Improving the coverage of identifiable and quantifiable proteins is the overarching goal in the LC-MS-based untargeted proteome profiling [ ]. Recent LC-MS-based proteomics have enabled the quantification of over 10,000 human proteins, including low-abundant transcription factors present in small amounts in the cell [ , , ]. Two primary approaches represent untargeted LC-MS-based proteomics; label-free proteomics strategy and label-based proteomics strategy with global protein- or peptide-labeling.
In recent proteomics studies, the utilization of sample preparation methods that use cellulose filters to analyze peptides has demonstrated high reproducibility and efficiency in enzymatic digestion which is a mandatory step in bottom-up proteomics, contributing to high-throughput proteomics. The filter-based methods consisted of filter-assisted sample preparation (FASP) and suspension-trap (S-Trap) [ , , ]. Additionally, a more effective technique for peptide prefractionation has been devised for in-depth peptide analysis. Peptide prefractionation is a method of reducing complexity by pre-dividing samples according to their physicochemical characteristics. Although there is a trade-off in increasing the number of analyses, this strategy allows LC-MS to analyze more peptides. Among various peptide prefractionation tools, strong cation exchange (SCX) has been the favored method. Nevertheless, it was eventually substituted in favor of basic pH reverse-phase (BPRP) fractionation because of its enhanced separation efficiency [ , ]. The BPRP method became widely favored in many clinical proteomic researches [ , , , ].
Labeling methods have been developed to expand the number of samples that can be analyzed in a single experiment. Chemical labeling methods, which directly label all the proteins or peptides in each sample with unique chemical reagents have been predominantly utilized as sample multiplexing techniques. Chemical labeling involves chemical reagents that react with specific residues of proteins or peptides, including isotope-coded affinity tag (ICAT) [ ], isobaric tag for relative and absolute quantitation (iTRAQ) [ ], and tandem mass tag (TMT) [ ]. The ICAT technique has been rarely used in recent years due to its relatively inferior multiplex capacity. In contrast, the masses of reporter ions generated by ion fragmentation (MS/MS) can discriminate between 10 or more samples in the case of iTRAQ and TMT techniques. Particularly, the TMT technique enables the simultaneous analysis of up to 18 samples, which is called TMTpro 18-plex, and over 8500 proteins could be identified using this technique [ ].
In terms of the MS method, label-free data-independent acquisition (DIA)-MS has gained attention recently. DIA-MS is different from the conventional data-dependent acquisition (DDA) method in that it does not select which peptides are subjected to fragmentation; DIA-MS performs fragmentation of all the peptides in a pre-defined mass range. Conventional DIA-MS required additional spectral libraries or synthetic heavy-isotope-labeled peptides to verify peptide sequences. However, library- and label-free DIA-MS has been recently developed and proved to be applicable to achieve in-depth proteomics [ , , , , ]. A recent study identified over 10,000 proteins by DIA-MS analysis with a rapid sample preparation step [ ]. In addition, DIA-MS has been reported to outperform classical DDA-MS in terms of analytical reproducibility [ ].
Comprehensively, advancements in LC-MS-based untargeted proteomics techniques contribute to discovering novel protein signatures for HCC, given characteristics of in-depth proteomic coverage and multiplex capacity. This enhancement can allow researchers to study the proteome of HCC with unprecedented depth.

2.2.

## Targeted LC-MS-based proteomics
Another analytical technology contributing to clinical proteomics is targeted LC-MS, which is characterized by high sensitivity and selectivity in the quantitation of proteins of interest during the analysis of multiple proteins, minimizing interfering signals. Several targeted MS technologies such as multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) and parallel reaction monitoring (PRM) quantify a dozen to a hundred proteins with high reproducibility and are specialized for verifying and validating biomarker candidates determined in the previous discovery phase [ ]. MRM and PRM are mainly implemented by triple quadrupole mass spectrometers and the Orbitrap MS instrument, respectively. In PRM, all product ions are detected and analyzed on an Orbitrap after fragmentation [ ]. Conversely, the MRM involves multistep selection including precursor ion selection, fragmentation of filtered ions, and measurement of the selected transition (production) [ ]. Furthermore, the feasibility of MRM-based assays was already demonstrated in several previous reports, representing the potential for reproducible assays for large-scale clinical proteomics research [ ].
Regarding the clinical biomarker studies using targeted MS, it is important to obtain a sufficient number of cohorts to generate statistically reliable results and uniformly prepare large samples with low technical and analytical variations. To monitor analytical reproducibility and precise quantitation of multiple proteins of interest, strategies based on stable isotope dilution (SID) have enabled researchers to quantify relative or absolute protein amounts in clinical samples [ ]. The relative or absolute concentrations of peptides corresponding proteins of interest are calculated as the relative peak area ratio of the mass spectrometric signal for the endogenous peptide and that of the crude (>75%) and high-purity (>95%) stable isotope standard (SIS) peptide in the sample, respectively. Recently, these targeted MS-based methods have been actively applied in clinical proteomics studies to quantify disease-related biomarker candidates and address clinical unmet needs [ , , , , , , ].
This review focuses on protein biomarkers investigated through advanced LC-MS-based proteomics. Furthermore, it explores their comprehensive applications in HCC management, encompassing the diagnosis and detection of HCC, as well as the prediction of HCC progression.

## LC-MS-based proteomics for biomarker discovery of HCC
In this review, proteomics studies on HCC were investigated in terms of HCC sample types that substantially contribute to the study design, objective, and final results. A schematic overview of LC-MS-based analysis strategies for HCC proteomics is presented in [ ]. In addition, proteomics studies on HCC were classified with a focus on the types of LC-MS methods [ ].
 3.1.

## LC-MS-based proteomics using HCC cell line samples
Cell lines are commonly employed as integral components in proteomic investigations, offering valuable insights into potential protein biomarkers associated with HCC and the underlying mechanisms driving its pathogenesis. The ready availability of HCC cell lines, coupled with the ability to generate large cell populations through cell culture, enhances the efficiency of investigations, ensuring the reproducibility of results under controlled conditions.
The following studies delve into the protein expression levels among diverse human HCC cell lines exhibiting distinct metastatic potentials. Qin et al. conducted a comprehensive analysis of three human HCC cell lines, each characterized by varying metastatic potentials. Their findings indicated a correlation between PKM2 and ARPC4 expression and the overall survival rates of HCC patients, proposing these proteins as potential biomarkers for HCC metastasis [ ]. In a distinct study, Sun et al. meticulously extracted and enriched ubiquitinated peptides from different HCC cell lines, pinpointing XRCC5 as a promising protein biomarker associated with metastasis in HCC among 83 differentially expressed ubiquitinated proteins [ ]. Liu et al. focused on side population (SP) cells derived from HCC cell lines, identifying TKT, CP, and ITGB1 as proteins potentially contributing to HCC metastasis through the regulation of cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. This underscores the reliability of these proteins as putative markers for HCC metastasis [ ]. Numerous other studies have successfully identified and validated proteins with the potential to serve as biomarker candidates for HCC metastasis by leveraging advanced proteomic technologies [ , ].
Several studies evaluated the anti-cancer effects of drugs using HCC cell lines as in vitro models. Hou et al. treated MHCCLM3 cell lines with dihydroartemisinin and sorafenib, identifying 101 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) and proposing APOA1, MYH11, and GALNT10 as potential protein drug targets for anti-HCC [ ]. In a parallel study, Wang et al. treated HepG2 cell lines with the recombinant carbohydrate recognition domain of Galectin-3 (Gal3C), revealing 190 DEPs and identifying CLU, NDRG1, ALCAM, LGALS1, and S100A11 as candidate drug targets for anti-HCC [ ].
In a separate study, researchers explored the proteomes of HepG2 cell lines transfected with the HBV X protein of different genotypes. Eight proteins, including MACF1, HMGB1, ANXA2, LMNA, S100A11, S100A6, S100A4, and PSMA3, were identified as potential markers associated with cell migration and metastasis in HCC [ ]. Feng et al. studied proteomes of HCC cell lines genetically engineered with replicative HBV genomes of different genotypes, presenting five proteins (A2M, APOA1, AHSG, ITIH2, and C3) as DEPs and potential candidate prognostic biomarkers for HCC [ ]. Niu et al. treated HepG2 and HepG2.2.15 cell lines with 5-aza-2ʹ-deoxycytidine, revealing expression level changes of S100A6 and ANXA2 and proposing both proteins as biomarker candidates for detecting HCC associated with HBV [ ].

3.2.

## LC-MS-based proteomics using body fluids from HCC patients
Body fluids represent reservoirs of potential biomarkers acquired through interactions with tissues, offering valuable insights into the physiological status of patients [ ]. Recent advancements in clinical proteomic analysis involve the collection of body fluid samples, with blood samples being a primary focus in clinical biomarker studies due to their easy accessibility and less invasive nature. The accessibility of blood samples allows for the continuous monitoring of the liver, facilitating early diagnosis of HCC.
Dong et al. conducted a study extracting small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) from plasma samples of 21 HCC patients and 16 normal controls (NCs). Analysis of sEV proteins identified 27 DEPs, with protein level changes of C1QB, C1QC, C4BPA, and C4BPB validated in both the discovery and validation sets (7 HCC patients and 7 NCs) through western blotting. The study concluded that these four proteins may serve as putative biomarkers for the diagnosis of HCC [ ]. Zhao et al. suggested 10 serum-derived exosomal proteins with differential expression levels between 10 NCs and HCC patients [ ]. Abdel-Wahab et al. identified 33 differentially expressed serum proteins in a comparison of 31 HCC patients infected with HCV and 8 NCs. Among the DEPs, LGALS3BP protein had the highest AUC value of 0.91 in the comparison of patients with HCV-infected HCC and HCC patients without infection [ ].
Several studies have investigated samples from patients at high risk of HCC to explore biomarker candidates for early diagnosis. Mas et al. compared 25 HCV-induced HCC patients and 10 HCV-cirrhotic patients, identifying 6 proteins (CFP, APOD, TF, APOC3, PPBP, and HNRNPAB) as DEPs between the two groups, suggesting their potential as biomarkers for screening HCC patients [ ]. Ye et al. conducted proteomic research on plasma samples from HCC patients, NCs, and patients at high risk of HCC (chronic hepatitis B and LC). In their research, C9, LBP, SVEP1, and VWF distinguished HCC from other groups, achieving AUC values higher than 0.85 [ ]. Liu et al. suggested that VWF may serve as a biomarker for HCC progression [ ].
In a glycoproteomics study for HCC, Tan et al. investigated core-fucosylated glycopeptides in serum samples collected from HCC patients and cirrhosis patients. They identified 26 core-fucosylated proteins with different expressions between the HCC and cirrhosis groups. Combining core-fucosylated peptides with AFP, they concluded that the combination of the core-fucosylated peptide from LUM protein and AFP showed the highest AUC value of 0.86 in discriminating HCC from cirrhosis [ ].
Other types of body fluid samples offer non-invasive alternatives for discovering potential biomarkers for HCC. Saliva samples and urine samples provide easier collection compared to blood samples. Ding et al. discovered the SOD2 protein as a potential salivary biomarker for HCC diagnosis, showing an AUC value of 0.91 in the validation set [ ]. Abdalla et al. compared HCV-infected HCC patients with HCV-infected patients and NCs. They combined CHAF1A and HSPD1, achieving an overall diagnostic accuracy of 77% in the comparison of the HCC and HCV-infected groups [ ]. Other research groups conducted studies on urinary protein biomarkers for HCC diagnosis, validating specific biomarkers (HPX, GOT1, APOH, APCS, GLRX, PLG, NCR3LG1, AFP, and ORM1), reporting AUC values higher than 0.85 in discriminating HCC [ , ]. Zhang et al. analyzed proteins from tissue interstitial fluid (TIF) and found 489 DEPs between TIF samples from HCC tumor tissue and adjacent non-tumor tissue. In this study, they validated SPARC and THBS2 on serum samples using ELISA, demonstrating that the combination of both proteins could distinguish HCC from NCs with high diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.97) [ ].

3.3.

## LC-MS-based HCC proteomics in HCC tissue
Tissue samples encompass heterogeneous cell populations that were orchestrated to function as an organ. As tissue samples are obtained from the proximity of the disease origin, they provide valuable insights into disease-specific proteins. Tissue samples often include paired sets from tumor and adjacent non-neoplastic regions.
Chaerkady et al. conducted a study analyzing proteins in tumor tissue and adjacent non-neoplastic tissue from HCC patients. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) verified expression changes of FGL2, MYADM, and OTC, proposing these proteins as potential diagnostic biomarkers for HCC [ ]. Another study by Suo et al. demonstrated that RPL27 and TARS2 may serve as prognostic biomarkers for HCC [ ]. In addition, MCM7 was nominated as a biomarker candidate for HCC progression elsewhere [ ]. Megger et al. identified differential expressions of MVP and BHMT, which were considered potential diagnostic biomarkers for HCC [ ]. Megger and colleagues also propose RAB1A as a potential diagnostic marker, which showed an AUC value of 0.93 in the comparison of HCC tumor tissue and adjacent non-tumorous liver tissue [ ]. Gao et al. discovered 338 DEPs, validating nine putative proteins for early HCC diagnosis [ ]. In other research, SCAMP3 and CLTC showed consistent expression levels between MS and IHC, suggesting them as potential diagnostic markers [ ]. Naboulsi et al. identified 547 DEPs, with VCAN showing an AUC of 0.85 in detecting HCC in the validation set [ ]. Hu et al. discovered 88 differentially expressed proteins between HCC patients with different CYP2D6∗10 genotypes, proposing validated ANGPTL6 as a potential biomarker for discriminating different types of HCC [ ].
Wei et al. examined tumor tissues and para-cancer tissues collected from HCC patients, categorizing them based on AFP levels–higher than 400 ng/mL and lower than 400 ng/mL. A total of 151 and 95 proteins were differentially expressed at the AFP level cutoff point of 400 ng/mL between tumor and para-cancer tissue groups, respectively. They finally revealed that four proteins (C1QBP, HSPE1, CHDH, ITGAL) are related to the overall survival rate of HCC patients, proposing them as prognostic biomarker candidates [ ].
In a study involving different tissue types, Yi et al. analyzed a total of 157 tissue samples (106 frozen tissues for the discovery cohort and 51 FFPE tissues for the independent validation cohort) to identify protein biomarkers capable of discriminating HCC from intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), another type of liver cancer. In the discovery set, APOE, PKLR, and GALK1, which showed common significant alterations of expression levels between tumor and benign tissues for each cancer, were finally determined as biomarker candidates. As a result, a three-protein classifier consisted of APOE, PKLR, and GALK1 distinguished HCC from CCA, achieving AUC of 0.92 and an accuracy of 90% in the independent validation set. These results proposed that this panel may contribute to developing more efficient molecular diagnostic means of HCC and new targeted drug treatments [ ]. In another study, Huang et al. utilized HCC cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) separated by laser capture microdissection (LCM). Among 1688 DEPs, upregulated DEPs in HCC cells were primarily associated with the metabolism of various substrates, while those in CAFs were related to ECM-receptor interaction pathways. Finally, GPC3, CDK1, and TLR2 were proposed as possible candidate markers of therapies targeting HCC [ ].
To ensure the clarity, specific information on the biomarker candidates discovered by LC-MS-based proteomics covered in this review is presented in [ ].

## Large-scale proteomics for clinical application in HCC
Recent clinical proteomics studies have been performed for the early diagnosis of HCC and the prediction of HCC progression. These studies primarily utilized blood samples, which are easily available for diagnosis compared to tissue. In recent studies, blood samples collected from large cohorts, comprising multiple independent cohorts, were utilized to discover protein biomarker candidates, which were then subjected to verification and validation steps of biomarker development [ ].
A notable recent study by Xing et al. involved a large cohort of 1002 subjects and proposed a serum biomarker combination for early HCC diagnosis [ ]. Through a staged LC-MS-based process, the group identified two biomarker candidates (HABP2 and CD163) along with established markers (AFP and PIVKA II). This multi-marker panel exhibited excellent discriminatory power, distinguishing HCC from liver cirrhosis (LC) (AUC = 0.98, sensitivity = 0.93, and specificity = 0.91) and healthy controls (HCs) (AUC = 0.99, sensitivity = 0.98, and specificity = 1.00) in an independent validation cohort. In another extensive study focusing on early HCC diagnosis, a total of 28 peptides from serum proteins (SERPINC1, CAT, SERPINA5, THBS1, FABP1, C1QC, MPO, CETP, LDHA, FCN3, ITIH1, BCHE, APOC3, AGT, FN1, MBL2, SERPINF2, CPN2, CFHR2, C9, KNG1, F9, TTR, C1R, and EPRS) were determined as final biomarker candidates in the discovery set (150 subjects) [ ]. Subsequently, a multi-marker panel combining these biomarker candidates was developed and evaluated in the test set (713 subjects) and validation set (305 subjects). As a result, the panel clearly distinguished HCC from a high-risk control group comprising CHB, CHC, and LC (AUC = 0.99), outperforming the diagnostic performance of sole AFP (AUC = 0.78). Similarly, the same research group proposed 17 serum biomarker candidates (PPBP, SERPINC1, MCAM, CFL1, C4A, AMBP, THBS1, UCHL3, LCAT, C1QC, CNDP1, C2, CA2, C6, SAA4, SERPINA10, and APOH) for early diagnosis of HCC through reanalysis of samples (727 subjects) collected from the same cohorts used in previous study [ ]. As a result, a multi-marker panel consisting of 17 candidates showed a decent performance (AUC = 0.94) in discriminating early-stage HCC and high-risk control groups.
In another study, 320 serum samples consisting of HCs, CHB, LC, and HCC were collected from different cohorts (discovery, validation, and independent validation cohorts) and analyzed by DIA and PRM-MS methods. In the discovery set, 18 proteins (SERPINA1, C4B, PLG, C9, CLU, LUM, HSPG2, CPB2, C2, F7, F13B, KLKB1, SERPIND1, C3, C5, C4BPB, C4BPA, and C6) were finally determined as biomarker candidates to discriminate HCC from HCs, CHB, and LC. Furthermore, multi-marker panels with proper combinations of these candidates for pairwise comparisons (HCs vs HCC, CHB vs HCC, and LC vs HCC) showed good performances (AUC > 0.80) in the validation sets. This proposed that these 18 proteins have the potential to predict HCC progression as well as detecting HCC [ ].
Another noteworthy research by Ramachandran et al. explored serum glycoprotein markers for distinguishing HCC from HCs and NASH. In the discovery set, 187 glycopeptides and peptides for discriminating NASH from HCs and 254 targets for discriminating HCC from HCs were discovered as biomarker candidates. Consequently, a multi-marker model consisted of 20 glycoproteins (SERPINA1, A1BG, A2M, SERPINA3, ORM1, APOC3, APOM, CFI, CLU, C4A, C6, C8A, HP, IGHA2, IGHG2, IGHM, KLKB1, TF, VTN, and AZGP1) were proposed as biomarkers for differential diagnosis of HCC, supported by good performances in distinguishing HCC from other groups in the training set (AUC = 0.99) and test set (AUC = 0.89), respectively [ ]. These large-scale proteomic studies signify significant progress in developing clinically relevant biomarkers for HCC diagnosis and progression prediction.

5.

## Summary and outlook
HCC poses a significant challenge due to its often poor prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate below 20%, despite advancements in diagnostic and treatment modalities [ ]. The critical factor influencing HCC outcomes is the stage at which the tumor is detected, with curative treatments primarily available for early-stage patients. This underscores the vital role of surveillance in early HCC detection. However, current surveillance via US lacks accessibility, and blood test measuring AFP have limited sensitivity in detecting early-stage HCC, resulting in high false negative rates. To address these limitations, there is a growing interest in the exploration of more convenient and sensitive molecular biomarkers for HCC, capable of early detection and continuous monitoring. In this paper, we investigated recent publications reporting studies on protein biomarker discovery of HCC using cutting-edge LC-MS-based proteomics. To help further studies on HCC biomarker development, we summarized biomarker candidates proposed in the papers introduced in this review and categorized them based on sample types and biomarker types [ ].
Recent advancements in LC-MS-based proteomics have significantly contributed to the identification of potential biomarker candidates for HCC, with a focus on early diagnosis and prognosis. The reviewed studies involving HCC cell lines primarily aim to identify proteins with significant expression changes associated with HCC metastasis and potential drug targets. Notably, single-cell proteomics has emerged as a powerful tool, unraveling the inherent heterogeneity among cancer cells within tumors. Since the molecular differences between individual cancer cells within a tumor have been revealed through several studies [ , ], single-cell proteomics applied to the analysis of HCC cell lines or HCC tissue will allow us to unambiguously understand differences in anticancer drug susceptibility and metastatic characteristics between patients. Moreover, investigations using HCC patient-derived body fluids, particularly blood, have successfully identified biomarker candidates and evaluated their diagnostic performance. Recognizing the limitations of single markers, which may lack discriminatory power or be associated with benign liver diseases or other cancers, there is a shift towards developing multi-marker panels. Multi-marker panels consist of multiple biomarkers, and the expression profiles of each biomarker can reflect specific pathological changes in the body associated with the disease. Inspecting multiple biomarkers simultaneously enables the examination of comprehensive protein profiles related to the specific disease while taking into account the heterogeneous nature of the disease. Recent studies have already developed various combinations of markers to supplement AFP or PIVKA II [ , , ]. Although proving decent performance in diagnosis and prognosis of HCC, multi-marker panels require thorough evaluation in independent cohorts to be registered by the administration as a tool for surveillance.
The future trajectory of LC-MS-based proteomics in HCC research holds immense potential for unraveling novel protein biomarkers. The ongoing evolution of LC-MS techniques is anticipated to play a pivotal role in enhancing our understanding of HCC pathogenesis and facilitating the development of effective diagnostic and prognostic tools for improved patient outcomes.
