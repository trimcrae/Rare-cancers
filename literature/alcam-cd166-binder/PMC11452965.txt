## Background
Muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) is a life-threatening malignant tumor characterized by high metastasis rates, poor prognosis, and limited treatment options. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting PD-1 and PD-L1 represent an emerging treatment for MIBC immunotherapy. However, the characteristics of patients likely to benefit from immunotherapy remain unclear.

## Methods
We performed single-cell mass cytometry (CyTOF) analysis of 179,483 single cells to characterize potential immunotherapy-related cancer stem cells (CSCs)-like populations in the tumor microenvironment of 38 MIBC tissues. The upregulated expression of IGF2BP3 in CD274 + ALDH + CSC-like cells, which was associated with poor clinical prognosis, was analyzed by bulk RNA-sequencing data from an in-house cohort. The functional role of IGF2BP3 was determined through cell proliferation, colony formation, cell apoptosis and sphere formation assays. The regulation of SPHK1 expression by IGF2BP3 was  investigated using methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq) and bulk RNA-sequencing (bulk RNA-seq). We further utilized single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) data from 67,988 cells of 25 MIBC tissues and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from MIBC patient-derived organoids to characterize the molecular features of bladder cancer cells co-expressing IGF2BP3 and SPHK1. Spatial transcriptomics (ST) and co-detection by indexing (CODEX) analysis were used to describe the spatial distribution and interactions of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + bladder cancer cells and immune cells.

## Results
A subset of CD274 + ALDH + CSC-like cells was identified, associating with immunosuppression and low survival rates in MIBC patients. IGF2BP3, an m6A reader gene, was found to be upregulated in the CD274 + ALDH + CSC-like cell population and linked to poor clinical prognosis in MIBC. Knockout of IGF2BP3 dramatically promoted cell apoptosis and reduced cell proliferation in T24 cells. By integrating MeRIP-seq and bulk RNA-seq analyses, we identified SPHK1 served as a substrate for IGF2BP3 in an m6A-dependent manner. Further snRNA-seq, scRNA-seq, ST, and CODEX analysis revealed a closer topographical distance between IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + bladder cancer cells and exhausted CD8 + T cells, providing one explanation for the superior response to immunotherapy in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + bladder cancer cells-enriched patients. Finally, an ICI-associated signature was developed based on the enriched genes of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + bladder cancer cells, and its potential ability to predict the response to immunotherapy was validated in two independent immunotherapy cohort.

## Conclusions
Our study highlighted the critical involvement of the IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling in maintaining the stemness of CSCs and promoting MIBC progression. Additionally, these findings suggested that the IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling might serve as a biomarker for prognosis and immunotherapy response in MIBC.

## Introduction
Bladder cancer (BLCA) is one of the most common types of malignancies in the urinary system, and its incidence and mortality rates are rising [ , ]. In 2020, there were over 550,000 new cases and more than 200,000 deaths from BLCA worldwide [ ]. BLCA is mainly categorized into non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) and muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), with NMIBC accounting for about 75% of cases [ ]. Transurethral resection of the bladder tumor (TURBT) and adjuvant intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) instillations are the first choice for NMIBC patients, but about 30% of NMIBC patients may still progress to MIBC or distant metastasis after treatment [ ]. For MIBC, approximately 25% of patients have lymph node metastasis, and 5% have distant metastasis, which leaves limited treatment options [ ]. Cisplatin-based chemotherapy is the standard treatment for MIBC, yet it provides only a short median survival for metastatic MIBC. Over the past few decades, there has been almost no improvement in the survival rate for BLCA patients [ , ].
The field of cancer immunotherapy is rapidly evolving and opening up new avenues for anti-tumor treatments. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the PD-L1/PD-1 axis have become research hotspots in cancer therapy. These inhibitors have been demonstrated significant clinical efficacy in a variety of malignancies. Although the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has recognized that programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression on tumor or immune cells can be used to predict ICI response in diverse tumor types [ , ], using PD-L1 alone as a biomarker is insufficient to effectively identify patients most likely to benefit from ICI therapy [ ]. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to investigate robust biomarkers of ICIs efficacy to select optimal treatment for each patient.
The tumor microenvironment (TME) significantly impacts the prognosis and ICI responsiveness of various cancers . Cancer cells, stromal cells, and immune cells engage in complex interactions, ultimately influencing cancer inhibition or progression [ – ]. PD-L1 expressed by tumor cells can bind to the PD1 of T cells, reducing their cytotoxicity and allowing tumor cells to evade immune surveillance [ – ]. PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors can relieve this inhibition, enhancing the immune system's ability to kill tumor cells [ ]. Therefore, exploring the TME may improve immunotherapy stratification. Current advances in single-cell analysis and spatial biology have opened new prospects for understanding the TME. Studying the regulatory mechanism of the PD-L1 signal in MIBC from a single-cell perspective is crucial for a deeper understanding of the characteristics of immunotherapy-responsive populations.
Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are tumor cells with self-renewal capabilities, typically marked by the expression of classic biomarkers such as CD44, CD326, CD133, ALDH, c-Myc, KLF4, NANOG, and SOX2/4 [ – ]. As a critical subpopulation within the TME, CSCs interact with stromal and immune cells within the TME, forming a complex CSCs niche. These niches support the maintenance of CSC properties and contribute to the establishment of an immunosuppressive TME [ ]. In this study, we integrated single-cell mass cytometry (CyTOF) and bulk RNA sequencing and identified a population of PD-L1-expressing CSCs associated with poor prognosis in MIBC patients. Moreover, we discovered that IGF2BP3 is potentially involved in sustaining the stemness and immunosuppressive phenotypes of these CSCs. Through functional studies and multi-omics analyses, including methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq), single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq), single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), spatial transcriptomics (ST), and co-detection by indexing (CODEX), we further revealed that the activation of the IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling pathway may play a key role in maintaining tumor stemness and immunosuppressive phenotypes (Fig.  ). These findings might inform decisions regarding immunotherapy choices. 

## Materials and methods

## Clinical samples
In this study, we assembled two in-house cohorts of MIBC patients and conducted sequencing and analysis of the collected samples. 52 MIBC tissues and 38 adjacent normal tissues used for bulk RNA-seq and CyTOF and high-throughput targeted resequencing (Hi-Reseq) were collected from February 2018 to October 2019 at the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (In-house cohort 1). Specifically, bulk RNA-seq and Hi-Reseq were conducted on all 52 MIBC tissues from in-house cohort 1, and 38 of these tissues also underwent single-cell mass cytometry (CyTOF) sequencing (Supplementary Table 1). Another 72 MIBC tissues and 19 adjacent normal tissues used for qRT–PCR and immunohistochemistry were collected in the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University from September 2015 to September 2019 (In-house cohort 2). Specifically, we performed immunohistochemistry on all 72 MIBC tissues from in-house cohort 2, and qRT-PCR on 19 of these tissues (Supplementary Table 1). All specimens were collected using radical resection from patients without history of MIBC or adjuvant therapy and harvested after obtaining patients’ written consent. The Medical Ethics Committee approved the collection and the experimentation of Guangxi Medical University. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (2024-E444-01, Supplementary Table 2).

## Public databases and analysis
We enrolled the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO): GSE32894, GSE48276, GSE176307, GSE35640 and GSE91061 cohorts; gene expression profiles and clinical information were collected from the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). For the TCGA-BLCA cohort, we obtained the gene expression profiles, and gene mutation data of MIBC patients from the TCGA Data Portal (https://www.cancer.gov/tcga/). For the immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) cohort, we obtained gene expression profiles and clinical information from cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/). For all the BLCA cohorts mentioned above, only patients with MIBC and complete clinical prognosis information were included for further analysis. Single-nucleus RNA-sequencing data, single-cell RNA-sequencing data, spatial transcriptome data and co-detection by indexing data were obtained from relevant published literatures [ , ]. In addition, we included five immunotherapy cohorts to assess the prognostic and predictive value of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature. Among them, bulk RNA sequencing was performed on 311 cases of clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) [ ], 339 cases of lung cancer [ ], and 121 cases of melanoma [ ]. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was conducted on 8 cases of ccRCC [ ], and spatial transcriptome sequencing was performed on 8 cases of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [ ].

## Cytometry by time of flight (CyTOF) analysis
The mass cytometry experiments were performed as detailed previously [ ]. Briefly, dead cells were identified by cisplatin (Fluidigm) staining. After staining with the antibody cocktails, the cells were incubated overnight at 4℃. Data were obtained using a CyTOF2™ mass cytometer (Fluidigm). After the FCS files were generated by the CyTOF2™ mass cytometer, the files were uploaded to the Cytobank platform. Populations of interest were gated manually and these population events were exported as.fcs files. A total of 179,483 single cells were randomly sampled from 38 MIBC sample for analysis. After arsinh transformation of the data matrix, FlowSOM and PhenoGraph dimensionality reduction clustering were performed to obtain cell clusters. The Rtsne function was used for t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) visualization (Supplementary Table 3). The ComplexHeatmap R package was used to draw the cell cluster heatmap. Patients with a CD274 + ALDH + (C1) proportion of more than 10% were considered as high proportion patients, and patients with a CD274 + ALDH + (C1) proportion of less than 0.01% were considered as low proportion patients.

## Quantitative real‑time PCR (qRT–PCR)
Total RNA was extracted from tissues and cells using HiPure Universal RNA kits (Magen, China) and then cDNA was synthesized using a PrimeScript™ RT reagent kit (TaKaRa), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. mRNA expression levels were assessed by qRT-PCR using a SYBR Premix Ex Taq Kit (TaKaRa) on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System, with GAPDH used as internal control. Primer sequences were: 5′-GAGAAGGCTGGGGCTCATTT-3′ (forward) and 5′-AGTGATGGCATGGACTGTGG-3′ (reverse) for GAPDH; 5′-CCAGGATCCGTATCCAAGCA-3′ (forward) and 5′-TGATGTTCCGAATGGTGGCA-3′ (reverse) for IGF2BP3. Primer sequences were: 5′-GTGAACCATGAGAAGTATGACAAC-3′ (forward) and 5′-CATGAGTCCTTCCACGATACC-3′ (reverse) for GAPDH; 5′- GCTGGCAGCTTCCTTGAACCAT-3′ (forward) and 5′-GTGTGCAGAGACAGCAGGTTCA-3′ (reverse) for SPHK1.

## Immunohistochemistry
For immunohistochemistry, the expression levels of IGF2BP3 were measured by immunohistochemical staining with antibodies against IGF2BP3 (1:100) (ab179807, Abcam, USA). In brief, tissues were collected within 30 min after tumor resection and fixed in formalin for 48 h. The sections were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The corresponding secondary antibodies conjugated to horseradish peroxidase were incubated with the sections for 1 h at room temperature. After washing with PBS, the sections were reacted with diaminobenzidine prior to counterstaining with hematoxylin. Images were acquired using the Leica Microsystems (Leica, Germany).

## High-throughput targeted resequencing (Hi-Reseq)
The Hi-Reseq were performed as detailed previously [ ]. DNA samples were extracted from MIBC tissues, which were further processed and sequenced by Shanghai Yihe Application Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina X-10 sequencing platform. After library construction, data quality control, sequence alignment, SNP calling, mutation detection and filtering, mutation annotation and other processing, we got the gene mutation information of the sample.

## Bladder cancer cell and cell culture
Bladder cancer cell (T24) lines were provided by the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank (Shanghai, China). All cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 containing 10% foetal bovine serum medium (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). All cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C and 5% carbon dioxide.

## CRISPR/Cas9-based IGF2BP3 knockout in T24 cell line
To construct a T24 cell line with IGF2BP3 knockout, a single guide RNA (sgRNA) for a frameshift mutation of the IGF2BP3 (sequence: TGTGATTTGCCTCTGCGCCT) was designed by Crispr Can (https://www.Crispr.org) and Crispr Direct (http://Crispr.dbcls.jp). Annealed double-stranded DNA was inserted into the px458 vector using the BbsI (NEB, USA) site. Then the purified recombinant plasmid was transfected into T24 cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). After puromycin screening, cells were separated into 96-well plates by limiting dilution and collected to test the knockout efficiency. IGF2BP3 expression was verified by western blotting. The stable IGF2BP3 knockout cell line was used for cell experiments in vitro.

## Stable cell line with SPHK1 overexpression
Lentiviral vectors for SPHK1 overexpression were purchased from GeneChem (Shanghai, China), and empty vector was used as the negative control (NC). Green fluorescent protein (GFP) was used to assess the transduction efficiency, and puromycin was used to select stably transduced cells. After transduction, SPHK1 expression levels were detected by qRT-PCR.

## Western blot assay
Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (Boster, China). Lysates were denatured at 95 °C for 5 min and cooled using ice. Lysates were loaded onto the sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel (10% SDS–PAGE) and electrotransferred onto the polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. PVDF membranes were blotted with the primary antibody at 4 °C for 12 h and incubated with HRP-labeled secondary antibody (Invitrogen, USA) at room temperature for 2 h. The primary antibodies were as follows: rabbit monoclonal antibody to IGF2BP3 (Abcam, USA). The secondary antibodies were as follows: rabbit mono-clonal antibody to β-actin (Abcam, USA).

## Colony formation assay
T24 IGF2BP3-WT, T24 IGF2BP3-KO, T24 SPHK1-WT, and T24 SPHK1-OE cells, each at a density of 500 cells per well, were seeded in 6-well culture plates. These were then incubated in an environment maintained at 37 °C and 5% carbon dioxide. The culture medium was replaced every week to ensure optimal growth conditions. Following a two-week incubation period, the wells were gently washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove any non-adherent cells or debris. Subsequently, the adherent cells were fixed in absolute methanol for 30 min at ambient temperature to preserve their morphology, after which they were stained with 0.2% crystal violet solution for an additional 15 min to visually enhance the colonies. This staining process facilitated clear visualization under a light microscope. Post-staining, the plates underwent thorough rinsing with PBS three times to remove excess dye, thereby ensuring an unobstructed view of the stained colonies. Any colony consisting of more than 50 cells was considered as a viable clone. Finally, the colonies were counted manually under a light microscope by researcher blinded to the experimental conditions, and the results were subsequently verified using automatic counting with ImageJ to ensure unbiased quantification. The mean and standard deviation were calculated from three independent experiments performed in triplicate.

## Cell viability assay
Cell proliferation assessments were conducted employing the MTT assay. T24 IGF2BP3-WT and T24 IGF2BP3-KO cells, each at a seeding density of 1000 cells, were cultivated in 96-well plates for durations spanning 12, 24, 36, 48, and 72 h. Upon completion of the respective incubation intervals, 10 µl of MTT reagent were introduced to individual wells and further incubated for 4 h at a constant temperature of 37 °C. Thereafter, 100 µl of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were added to solubilize the formazan crystals formed, and the absorbance of the resulting solution was quantified at a wavelength of 450 nm utilizing a microplate reader manufactured by BioTek Instruments, headquartered in Winooski, Vermont, USA. The viability of cells at differing time points was derived through normalization, specifically by relating the absorbance values obtained at specific time points back to the baseline absorbance recorded at the onset (0 h), thereby yielding a comprehensive profile of cellular proliferation dynamics over time.

## Cell apoptosis assay
T24 IGF2BP3-WT and T24 IGF2BP3-KO cells, each at a density of 2 × 105, were cultivated in 6-well culture plates and allowed to grow for a period of 48 h. Post-incubation, the cells were subjected to harvesting procedures, subsequently being washed with Phosphate-Buffered Saline (PBS) to remove residual media and debris, and then resuspended within the designated staining buffer. Following the manufacturer’s protocol, the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Kit (MultiSciences, Hangzhou, China) was employed to assess cell apoptosis. Stained cells were examined using the Accuri C6 Plus flow cytometry.

## Wound healing and transwell assays
T24 IGF2BP3-WT and T24 IGF2BP3-KO cells, each at a density of 7 × 104 cells per well, were seeded into 6-well culture plates. Wounds were created by making a scratch on the plate using a sterile tip. Following this, cells were washed with PBS and incubated in serum-free culture medium. After the specified duration, the distance between the two wound margins was measured. The distance between the two edges of the wound was measured at multiple points along the wound edge using ImageJ software. The average distance was calculated from at least five measurements per well to ensure accuracy and reproducibility. Data for both IGF2BP3-WT and IGF2BP3-KO cells were recorded at 0, and 24 h, respectively.

## Sphere formation assay
T24 IGF2BP3-WT and T24 IGF2BP3-KO cells were resuspended in serum-free DMEM/F12 medium containing bFGF (20 ng/ml), EGF (20 ng/ml), 1 × B27, penicillin (100 U/ml) and streptomycin (100 U/ml), and cultured in ultralow attachment six-well plates (Corning Inc., Corning, NY, USA) at a density of 5000 cells per well. The tumor sphere medium was refreshed every 3 days. Spheres that arose within 7 to 10 days were counted under a microscope. To quantify tumor sphere, three fields from each plate were randomly selected and counted.

## Imaging mass cytometry
The imaging mass cytometry experiments were performed as detailed previously [ ]. Briefly, tissue slides were incubated at 65 °C, then deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated through graded ethanol, followed by washing with double distilled water. Antigen retrieval was performed using Tris–EDTA buffer at 96 °C, followed by cooling to room temperature. The slides were blocked with 3% BSA, then incubated with metal-conjugated antibodies cocktail overnight at 4 °C. Next, the slides were stained with Cell-ID™ Intercalator-Ir, washed, dried, and imaged using Hyperion mass cytometry. Finally, the data were exported, converted to tiff files, and analyzed using CellProfiler.

## Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq) analysis
MeRIP-seq was performed according to the instructions of the Geneseed RIP Kit (Guangzhou, China). Briefly, magnetic beads were mixed with anti-m6A/IGF2BP3/IgG antibodies before the addition of cell lysates. Then, the bound complexes were thoroughly washed, eluted, purified, and analyzed by qRT-PCR. Enrichment of precipitated RNAs was normalized relative to input controls.

## Bulk RNA sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) analysis
For the bulk RNA-seq, the total RNA was extracted from T24 IGF2BP3-WT, T24 IGF2BP3-KO cells and 52 MIBC patient-derived tissue samples. The extracted RNA was subsequently subjected to sequencing using the Illumina platform. The Fastqc was used for the quality control of sequencing data, and the Hisat2 software was used for sequence alignment. The Samtool and the Stringtie were used to convert the data format and assemble the transcript. The DESeq2 was used for differential expression analysis.

## Single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis
The original single-nucleus RNA sequencing data were downloaded from relevant published literatures [ ] and analyzed after quality control. As previously described [ , ], the integrated data matrix was reduced by principal component analysis (PCA). We used the FindClusters function in Seurat to identify subsets and visualized them using the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) diagram. Bladder cancer cells were identified by combination of KRT17 and EPCAM expression [ , ] and inferCNV analysis. FindMarkers were used to compare differentially expressed genes (DEGs) among different cell types. The pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs in bladder cancer cell subtypes was performed using Metascape. CellphoneDB was utilized to analyze receptor-ligand interactions between celltype. The processing methods for scRNA-seq data are similar to those used for snRNA-seq data.

## Spatial transcriptome (ST) analysis
For spatial transcriptomes, we used spatial cellular estimator for tumors to calculate the tumor region. ssGSEA was used for the hallmark pathway enrichment score for each spot.

## Co-detection by indexing (CODEX) analysis
The analysis process for CODEX data was consistent with that described in previous articles [ ]. ComplexHeatmap was used for antibody clustering of 35 parameters in CODEX. ggplot2 package was utilized to visualize the spatial distribution of different cell types within tumor microarray (TMA). Euclidean distance calculation formula was used to calculate the distance between the cells.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS v17.0 (IBM Co., Armonk, NY, USA). The relationship between IGF2BP3 expression and pathological features of MIBC was analyzed by chi square test and Fisher's exact test. For survival analysis, the optimal cut-off value of gene expression was used as the grouping threshold. All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (n ≥ 3). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## A PD-L1 positive cancer stem cell-like cluster associated with poor prognosis in MIBC patients was identified by single-cell proteomic analysis
To investigate the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in MIBC, we collected 38 cases of MIBC tissue for single-cell proteomics analysis. Through an extensive literature review, we designed an antibody panel containing 33 TME-related markers, and different cell clusters were evaluated using CyTOF. Our study focused on assessing the correlation between CSC-related clusters and PD-L1 expression at the single-cell level. Therefore, we extracted CD45-negative and PD-L1-positive cell clusters for further analysis.
A total of 179,483 single cells were randomly selected from 38 MIBC samples and analyzed using the t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) algorithm. The t-SNE maps were created based on unique protein expression patterns observed among individual cells in the MIBC samples (Fig.  A). To divide the high-dimensional single-cell data, the FlowSOM and PhenoGraph algorithms were utilized, resulting in the identification of 15 distinct clusters characterized by specific marker expression patterns (Fig.  B–D). Examination of these 15 clusters revealed unique enrichments, showcasing variations in the composition of single-cell subsets and emphasizing the heterogeneous cellular diversity present (Fig.  D, E). Phenotypic clustering analysis identified four CSC-like cell clusters with PD-L1 expression: CD274 + ALDH + (C1), CD274 + c-Myc + (C3), CD274 + SOX2 + (C4), and CD274 + OV6 + (C6) (Fig.  D). Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that higher abundances of CD274 + ALDH + (C1) and CD274 + OV6 + (C6) were prognostic risk factors affecting patient survival (Fig.  F). Additionally, the abundance of CD274 + ALDH + (C1), but not CD274 + OV6 + (C6), was significantly and positively correlated with the expression of CTLA4, LAG3, FOXP3, and TIGIT, as well as the activation of T cell exhaustion and proliferation signals (Fig.  G). These results suggested that the PD-L1 expressing C1 cluster in MIBC patients might be associated with poor prognosis and increased immunosuppression. 

## IGF2BP3 was enriched in CD274 + ALDH + CSC-like cell cluster and associated with MIBC poor prognosis
To further explore the molecular characteristics of the C1 cluster, we performed differential gene expression analysis between patients with high and low C1 proportions using bulk RNA sequencing data from the same MIBC patients. Notably, the expression of IGF2BP3, an m6A reader, was significantly increased in MIBC patients with high C1 proportions (Fig.  A). We then constructed a gene set using ALDH and CD274 and applied it to calculate ssGSEA scores for patients in the TCGA-BLCA cohort, representing the abundance of C1. Correlation analysis revealed a significant positive association between IGF2BP3 expression and C1 abundance (Fig.  B). Previous investigations have reported that IGF2BP3 can upregulate PD-L1 expression through the regulation of m6A methylation, contributing to a tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment [ , ]. 
We compared IGF2BP3 gene expression levels in MIBC tissues and adjacent normal tissues using bulk RNA-seq data from a clinical in-house cohort and the TCGA-BLCA cohort. The results showed that IGF2BP3 expression was significantly upregulated in MIBC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Fig.  C, D). This finding was further validated by qRT-PCR, which confirmed the high expression of IGF2BP3 in MIBC tissues (Fig.  E). Similarly, immunohistochemical analysis of the in-house cohort (Fig.  F and Table  ) and the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (Fig.  G) revealed that the expression of IGF2BP3 protein was significantly upregulated in bladder cancer tissues. Furthermore, IGF2BP3 protein expression was positively correlated with lymph node metastasis and tumor invasion (Table  ). Analysis of Kaplan–Meier survival curves demonstrated that patients with MIBC exhibiting high levels of IGF2BP3 expression had lower overall survival rates in comparison to those with low levels of IGF2BP3 in the TCGA-BLCA cohort (p = 0.0038), GSE32894 cohort (p = 0.018), GSE48276 cohort (p = 0.031) and a clinical in-house cohort (p < 0.0001) (Fig.  H–K). These data suggested that IGF2BP3 might play an important role in MIBC progression.
 

## IGF2BP3 was implicated with cell proliferation and immune cell exhaustion signaling in MIBC
To comprehensively understand the biological implications of IGF2BP3 in MIBC, we conducted a transcriptome-level investigation of the potential functions associated with abnormal IGF2BP3 expression using bulk RNA-seq technology. A total of 52 MIBC patients were included from an internal dataset and categorized into two groups based on the expression level of IGF2BP3: the high IGF2BP3 expression group and the low IGF2BP3 expression group. A heatmap plot was generated to illustrate 301 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), comprising 100 upregulated and 201 downregulated genes (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that upregulated DEGs in MIBC patients with high IGF2BP3 expression exhibited significant enrichment in signaling pathways such as the meiotic cell cycle checkpoint, transporter activity, and regulation of organelle transport along microtubules, which are related to the cell cycle and intercellular communication (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Results from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis also indicated that upregulated DEGs were notably enriched in pathways such as transcriptional misregulation in cancer, serotonergic synapse, and nicotine addiction (Supplementary Fig. 1C).
The correlation analysis revealed a robust positive correlation between IGF2BP3 expression and established gene signatures [ , ] related to the cell cycle and immune cell exhaustion (Supplementary Fig. 1D). Notably, genes associated with transporter activity (SLC27A6, SLC7A3, ATP6V1B1) and cell cycle regulation (WNT11, ADRA1A, ROS1) exhibited higher expression levels in MIBC patients with high IGF2BP3 expression compared to those with low IGF2BP3 expression (Supplementary Fig. 1E–H). These transcriptome level findings suggested that high IGF2BP3 expression in MIBC was associated with signaling pathways linked to intercellular communication, immunosuppression, and cell cycle. Additionally, through the comparison of genomic differences, we found that MIBC patients with high IGF2BP3 expression have a higher mutation rate in TP53 and a lower mutation rate in FGFR3 (Supplementary Fig. 1A and Supplementary Fig. 1E).
To validate the potential biological roles of IGF2BP3 in MIBC, knockout experiments were conducted utilizing CRISPR/Cas9 technology to target the expression of IGF2BP3 in T24 cell lines (Fig.  A). The efficiency of IGF2BP3 knockout was illustrated in Fig.  A. Results from colony formation assays indicated a reduction in the number of clones in T24 cells following the knockout of IGF2BP3, suggesting an impairment in colony-forming ability (Fig.  B). Furthermore, MTT assays revealed a notable decrease in cell proliferation after IGF2BP3 knockout in T24 cells (Fig.  C). Cell apoptosis assays also demonstrated a significant increase in apoptotic cells upon IGF2BP3 knockout in T24 cells (Fig.  D and Supplementary Fig. 2A). Sphere formation assays indicated smaller spheroids in the IGF2BP3-KO group than those in the IGF2BP3-WT group (Supplementary Fig. 2B). However, no significant disparities were observed in the abilities of cell migration and invasion between IGF2BP3-KO and IGF2BP3-WT cells (Supplementary Fig. 2C-2D). These findings suggested that knockout of IGF2BP3 attenuates proliferation and stemness, and induced apoptosis in T24 bladder cancer cells. In addition to in vitro cell experiments, we employed imaging mass cytometry (IMC) technology to stain pathological sections from MIBC patients with high and low IGF2BP3 expression for stemness and immunosuppressive markers. The IMC results demonstrated that CD44, CD326 and PD-L1 were highly expressed in the high IGF2BP3 expression group (Fig.  E and Supplementary Fig. 2E). 

## IGF2BP3 regulated SPHK1 mRNA expression in an m6A‑dependent manner
IGF2BP3, an m6A reader, recognizes and binds to mRNA transcripts through the m6A motif, playing a crucial role in mRNA stabilization [ ]. To investigate the regulatory mechanism of IGF2BP3 in bladder cancer cells, we applied bulk RNA-seq and MeRIP-seq to identify potential target genes regulated by IGF2BP3 in an m6A-dependent manner (Fig.  A, B). The quality of m6A data was validated (Supplementary Table 4), and the genome distribution of the m6A peak was determined using the IGF2BP3 motif (GGAC) (Fig.  C). Subsequent analysis revealed that the overall m6A distribution patterns were similar in both IGF2BP3-KO and IGF2BP3-WT groups, with peaks mainly enriched in Coding Sequence (CDS) regions (Fig.  D). At the intersection of findings from bulk RNA-seq and MeRIP-seq, 191 genes showing both differential expression and differential m6A peaks between IGF2BP3-KO and IGF2BP3-WT cells were identified, including SPHK1 (Fig.  E). Potential target genes of IGF2BP3, including WDR6, SPHK1, POMT2, FAM213A, ARSJ, MRPS18A, AJUBA, UBE2G1, SAV1, and MAP3K5, were identified based on significantly decreased m6A peaks upon IGF2BP3 knockout in T24 cells (Fig.  F). To verify that IGF2BP3 modulated the expression of these target genes via m6A modification, we conducted gene expression correlation analyses between IGF2BP3 and these genes in MIBC samples. Both TCGA-BLCA cohort and in-house cohort showed a positive correlation between IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 expression, suggesting that IGF2BP3 might regulate SPHK1 expression through m6A signals (Fig.  G and Supplementary Fig. 3A). To further understand the biological function of SPHK1, we characterized the oncogenic phenotypes in T24 cells with SPHK1 overexpression (Fig.  H). Colony formation assays revealed that SPHK1 overexpression significantly promoted clone formation abilities in T24 cells (Fig.  I, J). This preliminary result demonstrated the role of SPHK1 in promoting MIBC progression. 

## Co-expression of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 may potentially induce immunosuppression through intercellular interactions in MIBC
To determine the clinical significance of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 gene expression, we grouped patients according to the expression level of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1. Kaplan–Meier survival curves indicated that MIBC patients with low IGF2BP3 and low SPHK1 expression had better survival than those with high IGF2BP3 and high SPHK1 expression in the in-house cohort (Supplementary Fig. 3B). The observation of the worst prognosis in MIBC patients with high co-expression of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 prompted further analysis of the role of bladder cancer cells co-expressing IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 (IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA) in regulating the development and progression of MIBC. We conducted a comparative analysis of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA using 25 MIBC single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) data (Fig.  A). Initially, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) clustering was performed on 67,988 cells from 25 MIBC samples (Fig.  A). Based on the highly variable genes (HVGs) within each cell cluster, we identified five classes of cell types: bladder cancer cells, endothelial cells, myeloid cells, fibroblasts, and lymphocytes (Fig.  A-B). We further confirmed the presence of extensive copy number variation in bladder cancer cell cluster using inferCNV (Supplementary Fig. 4A). There was great heterogeneity in the cellular composition among patients (Fig. 6C). Among all the cell types, bladder cancer tumor cells accounted for an average of 87.7% (ranging from 63.7% to 98.8%) (Fig. 6C). Herein, we focus on the relationship between the expression of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 and the molecular characteristics of bladder cancer cells. Notably, IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 expression was predominantly observed only in a subset of bladder cancer cells (Supplementary Fig. 4B). Consequently, we categorized bladder cancer cells according to the expression patterns of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 into four groups: IGF2BP3-SPHK1- BLCA, IGF2BP3-SPHK1 + BLCA, IGF2BP3 + SPHK1- BLCA, and IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA (Fig.  D). Pathway enrichment analysis indicated the upregulated genes in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA were significantly enriched in the m6A signaling pathway (Supplementary Fig. 4C). Additionally, IGF2BP3 expression showed a positive correlation with SPHK1 expression in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA (Supplementary Fig. 4D). These results suggested that the upregulated gene expression of SPHK1 in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA may be modulated by IGF2BP3. 
Interestingly, gene expression of CD274 and tumor stemness-related signals were significantly upregulated in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA (Fig.  E), indicating that the co-expression of IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 plays an important role in maintaining tumor stemness and immunosuppression. To better define the role of the IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA subtype in MIBC progression, we performed differentially expressed genes and pathway enrichment analysis to determine subtype-specific gene programs. The upregulated genes in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA were enriched in numerous pathways related to the cell cycle and intercellular interactions, such as regulation of epithelial cell proliferation, hallmark epithelial-mesenchymal transition, tube morphogenesis, growth, and secretory granule lumen (Supplementary Fig. 4E). Subsequently, we identified significant receptor-ligand interactions between different cell types using CellPhoneDB. Compared to IGF2BP3-SPHK1- BLCA, IGF2BP3-SPHK1 + BLCA, and IGF2BP3 + SPHK1- BLCA, the IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA subtype exhibited the most prolific intercellular interactions (Fig.  F).
As research into tumor mechanisms deepens, there is an urgent need for effective tumor models. Tumor organoids are recognized for recapitulating the genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic features of the parental tumors. This tumor model can closely mimic the structure and function of tumor tissues within the patient's body and demonstrate interactions between cells as well as between cells and the extracellular matrix. Here, we analyzed scRNA-seq data from parental tumors (PTs) and patient-derived organoids (PDOs), and validated the findings observed in snRNA-seq. Specifically, 8,064 cells from two PTs and their corresponding organoids were dimensionally reduced and clustered into 13 cell clusters (Fig.  G). Based on the HVGs within each cell cluster, we categorized these clusters into six distinct cell types (Fig.  G and Supplementary Fig. 4F). After identifying the cell types, we further classified the bladder cancer cells into four subtypes: IGF2BP3-SPHK1- BLCA, IGF2BP3-SPHK1+ BLCA, IGF2BP3+SPHK1- BLCA, and IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ BLCA. Consistent with previous findings, IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ BLCA showed significantly high expression of EPCAM, CD44, and CD274 (Supplementary Fig. 4G). Notably, the proportion of IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ BLCA was 3% in PDOs derived from higher tumor stage PTs (BLCa98, T3a), while it was 0% in PDOs from lower tumor stage PTs (BLCa86, T2b) (Fig.  H). Correspondingly, whole-mount immunofluorescence staining revealed that PDOs with a higher proportion of IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ BLCA exhibited a significant increase in the percentage of CD44-positive cells (Supplementary Fig. 4H). The cellphoneDB analysis revealed that IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ BLCA exhibited the highest number of interactions with other cell types (Supplementary Fig. 4I). To verify these results, we employed Visium spatial transcriptomics (ST) technology to investigate the spatial distribution of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA in tumors from the snRNA-seq cohort. Patients were stratified based on the frequency of IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA into IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched and -depleted groups (Supplementary Table 5). Using the GSVA algorithm to estimate pathway activity variations in the spatial transcriptomic data of tumor regions, we found that tumor regions in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients showed significant activation in pathways related to intercellular interactions, such as reactome other semaphorin interactions, neurofascin interactions, and protein–protein interactions at synapses (Supplementary Fig. 4J). Furthermore, tumor regions in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients exhibited significantly decreased expressions of differentiation and inflammatory response signatures, while showing significantly increased signatures associated with CD8 T cell exhaustion. (Fig.  I-K and Supplementary Fig. 4K). These results indicated that bladder cancer cells from IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients exhibited a dedifferentiated state and might promote immunosuppression through intercellular interactions.

## IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature may be a potential indicator to predict immunotherapy response in MIBC
To validate that bladder cancer cells from IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients exhibit a dedifferentiated phenotype and cause immunosuppression at the single-cell protein level, we conducted a 35-plex IHC panel using the co-detection by indexing (CODEX) platform on tumor tissue microarrays from the same tumors analyzed in the snRNA-seq cohort (Fig.  A). Bladder cancer cells from IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients (Bladder419 and Bladder912) showed significantly upregulated KRT13 protein expression and downregulated UPK3 protein expression compared to those from IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-depleted patients (Bladder674 and Bladder1204) (Fig.  B, C and Supplementary Fig. 4L). Dedifferentiated bladder cancer cells (Bladder cancer cell_KRT13) and exhausted CD8 T cells were enriched in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients (Supplementary Fig. 4M). Notably, we observed a closer physical spatial distance between bladder cancer cells and their nearest exhausted CD8 T cells in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients (Fig.  D and Supplementary Fig. 4N). These results suggested that IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA had the potential to promote MIBC progression and impair anti-tumor immunity through intercellular interactions. 
The observation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients prompted us to further analyze the role of IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling in predicting immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy efficacy (Fig.  K). We generated IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature genes and scored each ICB cohort sample for these signatures using ssGSEA. To explore the prognostic value of the IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature genes for ICB therapy, we assigned patients in the ICB cohort to high or low IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature groups. Strikingly, patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature scores had significantly longer overall survival than those with low scores in the ICB cohort (Fig.  E). This result was further validated in another ICB cohort, namely GSE176307 cohort (Fig.  F). Additionally, SubMap analysis revealed that patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signatures might be more sensitive to immunotherapy (Fig.  G, H).
To validate the prognostic and immunotherapeutic response predictive value of the IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signatures in pan-cancer, we analyzed five immunotherapy cohorts using ssGSEA. Specifically, we scored 311 ccRCC, 339 lung cancer, and 121 melanoma patients, then stratified them into high or low signature groups based on their scores. Survival analysis revealed that patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signatures had significantly better outcomes across all three cohorts (Fig.  A). We also analyzed scRNA-seq data from 8 ccRCC samples, comparing scores of malignant cells between ICI responders and non-responders (Fig.  B). Malignant cell scores were consistently higher than non-malignant cells, and ICI responders had notably higher scores than non-responders (Fig.  B). For the spatial transcriptome data of 8 HCC cases, we calculated scores for tumor and non-tumor regions, then compared these between ICI responders and non-responders (Fig.  C). Tumor regions had significantly higher scores than non-tumor regions, and ICI responders had higher scores than non-responders (Fig.  C). These findings indicated that the IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature had the potential to predict immunotherapy response in MIBC and various other cancers, highlighting its value as a biomarker for guiding treatment strategies. 

## Discussion
Muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) represents a group of molecularly heterogeneous diseases with a complex TME [ , ]. A comprehensive understanding of the composition of MIBC tumor tissue and the spatial interaction mechanisms between cells is crucial for exploring the molecular mechanisms of tumor invasion and metastasis. CSCs are a unique subset of cancer cells with the ability of differentiation and self-renewal, which are considered to be the driving force behind tumor growth, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance [ – ]. The stemness characteristics of CSCs depend on a variety of molecular targets, and inhibition of these targets may offer hope for effective cancer treatment. Understanding the role of CSCs in MIBC and their regulatory mechanisms can contribute to the treatment of cancer and improve the prognosis of patients. An in-depth investigation into the specific mechanisms of CSCs in the MIBC immunosuppressive microenvironment can help to identify potential biomarkers and guide the formulation of clinical immunotherapy strategies.
Although CSC-based therapy is still far from clinical application and remains in its infancy, it holds promise for overcoming drug resistance and preventing cancer growth and spread [ ]. Therefore, comprehensive investigation of CSCs at the single-cell level is imperative to elucidate their molecular characteristics and pinpoint potential intervention targets. CSCs express a variety of stem cell markers, but the expression of these stemness markers varies depending on the type of cancer. In bladder cancer, ALDH1, CD44, CD133, and SOX2 have been extensively reported to be associated with the stemness of tumor cells [ , – ]. Namekawa et al. reported that knockdown of ALDH1A1 significantly reduced the sphere-forming ability and proliferation capacity of bladder cancer cells [ ]. In addition to bladder cancer, ALDH1A1 is also considered a CSC-associated marker in breast cancer [ ], ovarian cancer [ ], and colorectal tumors [ ]. Zhan et al. found that knockdown of SOX2OT suppressed the stemness phenotype of bladder cancer stem cells, whereas overexpression of SOX2 reversed the inhibitory effect of SOX2OT silencing on the stemness phenotype of bladder cancer stem cells [ ]. Considering the heterogeneity of tumors, we also included markers widely reported to be associated with tumor stemness in the antibody panel of the CyTOF assay, including CD90 [ ], OV6 [ ], CD166 [ ], LGR5 [ , ], CD326 [ , ] and KLF4 [ , ] and so on. In this study, using single-cell proteomics profiling techniques, we identified a population of CSC-like cells (CD274 + ALDH + C1) associated with immunosuppression and poor survival in MIBC patients. The m6A reader gene IGF2BP3 was found to be significantly upregulated in the CD274 + ALDH + C1 cell cluster. Clinical and pathological data revealed a higher positive rate of IGF2BP3 in specimens with lymph node metastasis and invasive characteristics. These findings suggested that IGF2BP3 might play an important role in promoting the progression of MIBC.
Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA binding protein 3 (IGF2BP3) is a critical m6A “reader,” pivotal for recognizing m6A modifications and regulating mRNA stabilization and translation [ – ]. Numerous studies have shown that IGF2BP3 is implicated in tumorigenesis and poor outcomes in multiple cancers [ , , ]. Initially identified as a highly overexpressed gene in pancreatic cancer, IGF2BP3 has been shown by increasing evidence to be abnormally expressed in various tumor tissues, associating with patients' poor prognosis [ ]. For instance, IGF2BP3 expression levels are significantly higher in high-grade compared to low-grade invasive urothelial carcinomas [ ]. Another study found that IGF2BP3-negative patients had notably better survival rates than IGF2BP3-positive patients [ ]. Although extensive reports indicated that high expression of IGF2BP3 is associated with tumor progression, the exact mechanism and role of IGF2BP3 in promoting bladder cancer progression remain to be fully elucidated. In this study, through analyzing bulk RNA sequencing data from an in-house cohort, we found that the upregulated genes in patients with high IGF2BP3 expression were significantly enriched in cell cycle and extracellular matrix constituent secretion pathways by pathway enrichment analysis. Furthermore, we conducted in vitro experiments, which indicated significant differences between IGF2BP3 knockout cells and control cells in terms of proliferation, clone formation, and apoptosis.
As a protein that recognizes and binds to mRNA modified by m6A, IGF2BP3 stabilizes target mRNA and promotes translation, playing a crucial role in the specific gene transcription programs of cancer cells. IGF2BP3 promotes tumor metastasis and stemness by stabilizing its downstream-regulated mRNAs through an m6A-dependent mechanism [ , ]. By combining methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq) and bulk RNA sequencing, we unexpectedly found that IGF2BP3 upregulated SPHK1 mRNA expression in an m6A-dependent manner. SPHK1, a pivotal enzyme for catalyzing the formation of Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), a bioactive metabolite known for enhancing cell proliferation and motility [ ], might play a significant role in promoting tumor growth across various cancer types [ – ]. Previous studies have shown that increased expression of SPHK1 can promote tumor progression by increasing the stemness of tumor cells [ , ]. In this study, using MeRIP-seq and bulk RNA-seq data, we found that both the mRNA expression and m6A peak of SPHK1 were downregulated after IGF2BP3 knockout in T24 cells. The correlation between IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 expression was further validated in two independent patient cohorts. Our finding suggested that SPHK1 was a new target gene regulated by IGF2BP3, and the IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling might play an important role in MIBC progression.
By integrating single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics (ST) data, we identified a small subpopulation of bladder cancer cells co-expressing IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 (IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA), which exhibit high expression of cancer stem cell markers and proliferative signaling pathways. These cell populations also displayed significant interactions with immune cells and stromal cells. These findings were further validated using scRNA-seq data from PDOs. Co-detection by indexing (CODEX) demonstrated that bladder cancer cells in IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA-enriched patients were spatially closer to exhausted CD8 + T cells, providing a possible mechanistic explanation for the formation of an immunosuppressive TME.
To explain why patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature may benefit more from immunotherapy, we employed five different methods to assess the immune infiltration levels of each MIBC patient in two independent cohorts. The results demonstrated that patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature exhibit higher cancer stem cell markers scores and PD-1 signaling, as well as lower differentiation and FGFR3-coexpressed genes signals. Importantly, patients with high IGF2BP3 + SPHK1 + BLCA signature exhibited higher levels of immune infiltration, which may partly explain why these patients show significantly improved clinical outcomes following immunotherapy (Supplementary Fig. 5).
The rapid advancement of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy has brought hope for the treatment of BLCA. However, only a minority of patients have shown significant therapeutic responses to immune intervention, with the majority experiencing minimal benefit [ ]. Therefore, it is crucial to identify biomarkers that can accurately indicate which patient subgroups respond to immunotherapy. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) is an indicator reflecting the number of mutations in cancer cells. Higher TMB levels suggest that tumors may produce more neoantigens, thereby increasing sensitivity to immunotherapy [ ]. Similarly, microsatellite instability (MSI) as a marker of genetic instability is increasingly being used to identify patients who may benefit from immunotherapy [ ]. The calculation of TMB and MSI typically requires DNA sequencing. However, the costs of DNA sequencing and the complexity of analyzing data pose barriers to measuring TMB and MSI. In this study, we identified a subtype of bladder cancer cells co-expressing IGF2BP3 and SPHK1 at the single-cell level that were associated with patient prognosis and response to immunotherapy. We utilized the characteristics of this cell population to develop a convenient method for predicting immunotherapy response, offering a novel and potentially valuable biomarker for clinical treatment.
In summary, our findings suggest that the activation of IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling is crucial in maintaining tumor stemness and immunosuppressive phenotypes, supporting decision-making regarding the choice of immunotherapy. These insights could contribute to personalized care, enabling more accurate clinical decision-making.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, by analyzing sequencing data from MIBC patient-derived tumors and PDOs, we revealed that IGF2BP3+SPHK1+ bladder cancer cells promote tumor progression through cell-cell interactions. However, these results should be further validated in multi-dimensional animal models. Although PDOs can mimic the three-dimensional structure and microenvironment of tumors, it is difficult to simulate the complex changes of tumors caused by the alterations of multiple specific genes. Thus, constructing cell line-derived xenograft (CDX) models or genetically engineered mouse models (GEMMs) is crucial for further exploring the specific mechanisms of the IGF2BP3/SPHK1 signaling in tumor cells. Second, the limited sample size may affect the external validity and generalizability of our findings across different populations. Therefore, future larger-scale, multicenter, prospective studies are crucial for validating our findings.
