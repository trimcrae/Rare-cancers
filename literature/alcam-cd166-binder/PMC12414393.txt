## Abstract
microRNAs (miRNAs) derived from viruses, have been detected in body fluids and are known to regulate the expression of host genes. Recent evidence indicates that SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs could contribute to pulmonary disease. Pulmonary fibrosis is an important complication in SARS-CoV-2 infected patients, either during hospitalization or after discharge, however, the underlying mechanisms are not fully elucidated. Here, we report a SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNA, miR-nsp3-3p, facilitates host pulmonary fibrosis by inhibiting expression of activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule (ALCAM) and promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). First, we detected miR-nsp3-3p in clinical specimens and found it was remarkably increased in throat swabs and alveolar lavage fluids from severe/critical COVID-19 patients compared to control groups or mild/moderate patients. We further revealed that adeno-associated virus (AAV)-nsp3 infection can induce pulmonary fibrosis in BALB/c mice while miR-nsp3-3p antagomirs can reverse that, and ALCAM was found to be as a target gene of miR-nsp3-3p. miR-nsp3-3p overexpression can inhibit the expression of ALCAM and promote EMT of pulmonary epithelial cells. Moreover, overexpression of ALCAM can reverse the miR-nsp3-3p-induced EMT and fibrosis. These findings highlight the essential role of SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs in promoting the pathological progression of lung disease, and provide novel insights into the interactions between viral miRNAs and host pathology.
Keywords: microRNA (miRNA), SARS-CoV-2, Pulmonary fibrosis, Activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule (ALCAM), Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)

## Highlights
•SARS-CoV-2 encoded miR-nsp3-3p were highly expressed in severe/critical COVID-19 patients.
•miR-nsp3-3p exerts a post-transcriptional regulatory effect on ALCAM by binding to the 3′UTR of ALCAM.
•miR-nsp3-3p promotes the occurrence of EMT in lung epithelial cells by downregulating ALCAM.
•miR-nsp3-3p induces pulmonary fibrosis in mice by promoting EMT.

## Introduction
microRNAs (miRNAs) are highly conserved, small (∼22 nucleotides) non-coding RNAs which usually bind to the 3′ untranslated region of messenger RNAs (mRNAs) to restrict their post-transcriptional translation ( ). It has been well documented that miRNAs can be produced in plants, animals, and pathogens ( ). Virus-encoded miRNAs were first reported in the Epstein-Barr virus in 2004 ( ). Subsequently, more miRNAs have been found to be encoded by DNA viruses and RNA viruses ( ; ; ). A study discovered three small viral RNAs derived from the nsp3 (nsp3.1 and nsp3.2) and N genomic regions of SARS-CoV, which can facilitate in vivo lung pathology and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression ( ).
Likewise, there is growing evidence supporting the generation of miRNAs from the SARS-CoV-2 since the pandemic of COVID-19. Meng et al. first reported three miRNAs encoded by the SARS-CoV-2 N gene by deep sequencing and experimentally validated their association with cellular metabolic and biosynthetic pathways by targeting interleukin 1 beta (IL-1β), NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3), and caspase 1 (CASP1) host genes ( ). An in silico analysis predicted 90 mature miRNAs potentially encoded by SARS-CoV-2 and suggested that the immune response and cytoskeletal organization are two of the most notable biological processes regulated by the infection-modulated miRNAs, based on analysis of the viral miRNA targets in host genes ( ). Another study came to similar conclusions that SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs were predicted to be able to regulate the expression of genes involved in immunity, cytoskeletal dynamics, and apoptosis ( ). One of SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNA (MR147–3p) was also verified to target host genes, including exocyst complex component 7 (EXOC7), RAD9 checkpoint clamp component A (RAD9A), and transcription factor binding to IGHM enhancer 3 (TFE3) in the Vero E6 and Beas-2B cell lines ( ). SARS-CoV-2 encoded miRNAs are not recognized as alloantigens by the human immune system due to their small size, providing an opportunity to inhibit and disrupt the immune system by targeting related genes ( ). Moreover, SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs have been reported to be involved in the pathogenesis of various organs and tissues. For instance, MD241-3P suppresses the bone morphogenetic protein receptor type 2 (BMPR2) gene to affect the TGF-β signaling pathway which plays a role in pulmonary vascular homeostasis, finally causing respiratory lung disease ( ). CvmiR-5-5p was speculated to be associated with neurological disorders including olfactory and gustatory dysfunction, impaired consciousness and severe headaches ( ). MR147–3p was presumed to target the transmembrane serine protease 2 (TMPRSS2) enhancer to increase intestinal inflammation and cause diarrhoea ( ). Nevertheless, most of these studies are mainly based on computational approaches, and there is insufficient experimental evidence regarding the effects of SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs on cells. Few experimental studies have verified that SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs can facilitate viral replication by targeting host genes ( ; ).
A previous study by our group has shown that SARS-CoV-2-encoded miR-nsp3-3p can serve as a biomarker to distinguish between mild and severe COVID-19 patients ( ). The expression of miR-nsp3-3p in severe patients was significantly higher than that in mild/moderate patients. Besides, the expression of miR-nsp3-3p significantly increased during the pre-stage and mid-stage of severe progression, and decreased during the recovery period, indicating that it has predictive value for the progression of COVID-19. Moreover, miR-nsp3-3p was superior to other indicators including D-dimer, c-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and phospholipase C (PLC) for stratifying severe patients from mild/moderate ones and could identify high-risk individuals before the manifestation of severe symptoms. However, whether miR-nsp3-3p promotes the progression of disease during SARS-CoV-2 infection remains unverified.
As described above, SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs have been shown to play a role in pathogen-host interactions. Thus, at the outset of this study, we hypothesized that miR-nsp3-3p may contribute to disease development primarily by interfering with immune functions or promoting lung and other organ damage. In this study, we have proved this hypothesis by identifying that miR-nsp3-3p is overexpressed in respiratory specimens from severe/critical patients during SARS-CoV-2 infection, and is involved in cross-species regulation of host pulmonary fibrosis by targeting activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule (ALCAM) in the host.

## Results

## Identification of miR-nsp3-3p in clinical samples of COVID-19 patients
The first step is to verify whether SARS-CoV-2 encoded miR-nsp3-3p still exists in clinical samples from patients infected with the Omicron strain during the new wave in early 2023, since miR-nsp3-3p was discovered in 2021 when the Delta strain was mainly circulating. We collected three different types of clinical specimens from COVID-19 patients and control groups without SARS-CoV-2 infection at Nanjing Jingling Hospital, including plasma, throat swabs, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) samples. Interestingly, contrary to the previous results ( ), there was no significant differences in miR-nsp3-3p expression between mild/moderate and severe/critical COVID-19 patients in plasma samples ( A), indicating that miR-nsp3-3p encoded by the Omicron strain could not circulate into the blood or its expression was extremely low below the detection limit. However, the expression level of miR-nsp3-3p in throat swabs from mild/moderate patients was noticeably higher compared to that in uninfected individuals, and further elevated in severe/critical patients compared to mild/moderate patients ( B). Additionally, the expression level of miR-nsp3-3p was significantly increased in BALF samples from severe/critical patients when compared to non-viral pneumonia patients ( C). To detect the consistency of the expression level of miR-nsp3-3p and SARS-CoV-2 within the host, we detected the correlation between miR-nsp3-3p expression levels and viral nucleocapsid (N) gene CT values. However, the expression level of miR-nsp3-3p presented no correlation with the CT value of viral N gene ( ). Taken together, these results indicate that miR-nsp3-3p produced by the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron strain mainly existed in the respiratory tract of COVID-19 patients.
 

## miR-nsp3-3p is correlated with pulmonary fibrosis in vivo
Based on the above findings that miR-nsp3-3p was detected in respiratory tract samples, we speculated that miR-nsp3-3p may be associated with the development of pulmonary pathology. To confirm it, adeno-associated virus (AAV)-nsp3 was constructed for the overexpression of miR-nsp3-3p, and then administrated to the respiratory tracts of BALB/c mice to observe the pathological changes of lungs. Groups were divided into mock (PBS), AAV vector, AAV-nsp3, AAV-nsp3 ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p antagomir. In AAV-nsp3 ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p antagomir group, AAV was intranasally injected into BALB/c mice. After two weeks, miR-nsp3-3p antagomirs were intranasally administered to the same mice every two days for an additional four weeks ( A). As shown in B, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of lung tissue revealed that AAV-nsp3 caused inflammatory infiltration and pulmonary interstitial expansion, which could be partially suppressed by miR-nsp3-3p antagomir. The Ashcroft score, a method used to assess the degree of pulmonary fibrosis, was higher in the AAV-nsp3-treated group than in mock and AAV vector groups ( C). Compared to mock group, the AAV-nsp3-treated group showed a significant increase in pulmonary fibrosis and collagen fiber deposition, while after the treatment of miR-nsp3-3p antagomir, there was partial improvement in pulmonary fibrosis and collagen deposition, as shown by Masson’s trichrome staining ( D and E). The expression level of miR-nsp3-3p in mouse lung tissue and BALF was significantly higher in the AAV-nsp3 group compared to the mock and AAV vector groups ( F and G), indicating that miR-nsp3-3p can reach the lungs and remain stable for a period of time, causing pathological damage. A higher hydroxyline and wet/dry ratio of lung tissue in the AAV-nsp3 group also supported the evidence of the pulmonary fibrosis induced by miR-nsp3-3p ( H and I). Furthermore, some fibrosis-related genes in lung tissue were detected by qRT-PCR assay, including collagen type Ⅰ alpha 1 chain (Col1a1), collagen type Ⅲ alpha 1 chain (Col3a1), collagen type Ⅳ alpha 1 chain (Col4a1), tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases 1 (Timp1), matrix metalloproteinase 1 (Mmp1), matrix metalloproteinase 2 (Mmp2), matrix metalloproteinase 7 (Mmp7) and matrix metalloproteinase 9 (Mmp9). The data showed that except for Mmp7, there was a substantial increase in the expression level of other fibrotic genes after administration of AAV-nsp3, which could be partially reversed by the antagomir ( J). Overall, these findings demonstrated that miR-nsp3-3p is closely associated with pulmonary fibrosis in vivo.
 

## Prediction of potential target genes for miR-nsp3-3p
The potential target genes of miR-nsp3-3p were predicted using miRanda v3.3a and Targetscan 7.0, based on the principle of complementary pairing between miRNA seed sequence and mRNA 3′UTR. A total of 103 genes were identified via miRanda v3.3a, all of which were included in the 242 predicted by Targetscan 7.0 ( ). The details of these 103 genes are provided in . Given that miR-nsp3-3p has been experimentally verified to significantly induce pulmonary fibrosis in mice, we selected genes potentially associated with pulmonary fibrosis from these 103 genes through a literature search. Finally, seven potential genes were selected for further identification, as shown in .

## miR-nsp3-3p inhibits ALCAM expression by targeting its 3′UTR
To identify the true target genes that can bind to miR-nsp3-3p, the 3′UTRs of seven potential genes were synthesized and cloned into the pmirGLO plasmid for luciferase assays. We found that the luciferase expression of the pmirGLO vector containing ALCAM 3′UTR was weakened after transfection with miR-nsp3-3p mimic for 24 ​h ( A). For further validation, an ALCAM 3′UTR mutant was constructed as a control for the wild type. As expected, luciferase expression was reduced by miR-nsp3-3p mimic only in the wild type of ALCAM 3′UTR ( B). The seven-base pairing between ALCAM 3′UTR and the miR-nsp3-3p seed sequence was shown in C. Collectively, the above results indicated that ALCAM was directly downregulated by miR-nsp3-3p, and may be a target gene contributing to pulmonary fibrosis.
 

## miR-nsp3-3p induces epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by inhibiting ALCAM in vitro
As reported, ALCAM is known as a type of cell-adhesion molecule involved in cell proliferation and migration ( ), and previous studies revealed that decreased ALCAM expression was associated with epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and fibrosis (Kim et al., 2022; ). However, whether ALCAM is also a key regulator of miR-nsp3-3p-induced pulmonary fibrosis remains unclear. Here, we first confirmed the relationship between ALCAM and miR-nsp3-3p-related EMT in A549 and Beas-2B cells. Since lentivirus (LV) can efficiently infect cells and stably express exogenous genes for a long time, we constructed LV with miR-nsp3-3p overexpression for cell experiments. Cells with different treatments were divided into mock, LV vector, LV-nsp3, LV-nsp3 ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p inhibitor. The miR-nsp3-3p levels in infected A549 and Beas-2B cells of the above four groups were detected to verify the infection efficiency ( A). Interestingly, at 48 ​h post-infection, there was an enhancement in the invasion ability of pulmonary epithelial cells infected with LV-nsp3, which was attenuated after intervention with miR-nsp3-3p inhibitor ( A). In addition, miR-nsp3-3p overexpression significantly decreased the expression of E-cadherin and increased the expression of Vimentin and alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), while inhibition of miR-nsp3-3p via its inhibitor significantly reversed the changes in expression of these proteins ( B and C). There was no significant difference in ALCAM mRNA level among the three groups, and the mRNA levels of E-cadherin, Vimentin, and α-SMA was in correspondence with the changes in protein levels ( D and E). Immunofluorescence staining distinctly revealed that the expression of Vimentin was decreased after LV-nsp3 infection, whereas miR-nsp3-3p inhibitor could evidently reverse the effects of LV-nsp3 infection ( F). Overall, in vitro studies demonstrated that miR-nsp3-3p overexpression can regulate EMT-related markers and promote EMT of pulmonary epithelial cells.
 

## ALCAM reverses miR-nsp3-3p-induced EMT in vitro
Next, it needs to clarify whether ALCAM overexpression can reverse miR-nsp3-3p-induced EMT. For this purpose, cells were divided into four groups: mock, LV-nsp3, LV-nsp3 ​+ ​NC, LV-nsp3 ​+ ​ALCAM groups. To confirm infection efficiency, the miR-nsp3-3p levels were measured in mock-, LV-nsp3-, LV-nsp3- ​+ ​NC, LV-nsp3 ​+ ​ALCAM-infected A549 and Beas-2B cells ( B). Intriguingly, we found that the enhanced cell invasion induced by miR-nsp3-3p was dramatically inhibited by ALCAM overexpression ( A). Furthermore, ALCAM overexpression significantly attenuated the miR-nsp3-3p-mediated effects on EMT markers: it prevented the downregulation of E-cadherin and the upregulation of Vimentin and α-SMA ( B and C). Consistently, immunofluorescence analysis revealed reduced Vimentin expression upon ALCAM overexpression ( D). These data strongly suggested that miR-nsp3-3p promotes EMT by suppressing ALCAM expression, and that ALCAM overexpression can reverse miR-nsp3-3p-induced EMT.
 

## miR-nsp3-3p induces pulmonary fibrosis by promoting EMT in vivo
Finally, we investigated whether miR-nsp3-3p promotes EMT by downregulating ALCAM, thereby facilitating pulmonary fibrosis in vivo. The BALB/c mice were divided into four groups (n ​= ​6): mock (50 ​μL PBS), AAV vector, AAV-nsp3, and AAV-nsp3 ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p antagomir. Similar to the in vitro studies, the expression of ALCAM and E-cadherin was downregulated, and the expression of Vimentin and α-SMA was upregulated by AAV-nsp3, which could be recovered by miR-nsp3-3p antagomirs ( A), indicating that EMT-related markers in lung tissues can be regulated by miR-nsp3-3p in vivo. Immunohistochemical results showed markedly excessive collagen deposition in lung tissue in the AAV-nsp3-treated group, which was alleviated after antagomir administration ( B). In summary, we proposed that the SARS-CoV-2-encoded miR-nsp3-3p contributes to the progression of pulmonary fibrosis by targeting ALCAM and promoting EMT in pulmonary epithelial cells ( C).
 

## Discussion
Based on a previous study that identified miR-nsp3-3p encoded by SARS-CoV-2, in which we participated ( ), we further explored its biological role in disease progression. This study demonstrates that SARS-CoV-2 encoded miR-nsp3-3p contributes to the progression of COVID-19-associated pulmonary fibrosis by downregulating ALCAM and promoting EMT, according to the following evidence: i) high expression of miR-nsp3-3p in respiratory tract samples from severe and critically ill COVID-19 patients; ii) promotion of pulmonary fibrosis in vivo by overexpression of miR-nsp3-3p in the lungs of BALB/c mice; iii) downregulated expression of plasmid containing ALCAM 3′UTR after miR-nsp3-3p transfection; iv) significant changes in expression of EMT-related genes in vitro after overexpression of miR-nsp3-3p, and reversal of alterations in expression of EMT-related genes after ALCAM overexpression; v) regulation of EMT markers and collagen deposition in mouse lung tissue via miR-nsp3-3p overexpression. Thus, our findings suggest that miR-nsp3-3p likely serves as an important pathogenicity factor in SARS-CoV-2-related pulmonary fibrosis.
ALCAM, also called CD166, is a transmembrane receptor involved in lymphocyte development and migration, inflammation, cell-to-cell adhesion, and oncogenesis ( ). In this study, we confirmed that decreased ALCAM expression is associated with EMT, a significant factor in promoting fibrosis. A genome-wide association study of subclinical interstitial lung disease found reduced ALCAM expression in lung tissue from 15 adults with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis compared to 15 adults without lung disease (mean foldchange: 0.58; P ​= ​0.03) ( ). A recent study confirmed that ALCAM deficiency exacerbates TGF-β1-induced pulmonary fibrosis and alveolar remodelling (Kim et al., 2022). In our in vivo experiments, lung tissue treated with AAV-nsp3 exhibited obvious alveolar exudates, interstitial enlargement, extracellular matrix proliferation, and fibrosis, accompanied by alterations in fibrosis-related genes, including collagens and MMPs. Compared to negative controls, AAV-nsp3-treated mice showed reduced ALCAM expression and changes in EMT-related markers such as E-cadherin, Vimentin, and α-SMA. Consistent with the in vivo data, the in vitro study using human A549 and Beas-2B cells also showed ALCAM reduction in LV-nsp3-treated cells, accompanied with altered expression of E-cadherin, Vimentin, and α-SMA. Increased α-SMA expression is characteristic of fibroproliferative diseases. ( ). E-cadherin and Vimentin are the major phenotypes of epithelial cells and stromal cells, respectively. The decrease of E-cadherin and the increase of Vimentin are markers of EMT, which leads to fibrous proliferation and fibrous foci formation ( ). It is worth mentioning that miR-nsp3-3p also promotes the invasion ability of lung epithelial cells by targeting ALCAM, as EMT is a developmental program that enables stationary epithelial cells to gain the ability to migrate and invade as single cells. miR-483–5p was reported to promote EMT, accompanied by the invasive property of lung epithelial cells via targeting ALCAM ( ). Therefore, our data provide strong evidence that miR-nsp3-3p acts as an essential positive regulator in EMT of lung epithelial cells.
EMT is the process by which epithelial cells lose their characteristics and acquire those of mesenchymal cells. This process usually involves a reduction in intercellular adhesion and loss of polarity, allowing the cells to be more migratory and invasive ( ). In lung fibrosis, EMT is considered to be a key pathological mechanism. Studies have shown that alveolar epithelial cells can undergo EMT after injury, which promotes the progression of fibrosis ( ; ). The role of EMT in lung fibrosis can be understood from the following aspects. First, during EMT, epithelial cells lose some epithelial features while acquiring mesenchymal cell features such as increased expression of α-SMA and fibronectin ( ). This transformation allows cells to migrate to the site of injury and participate in the repair process, but it may also lead to excessive fibrosis. Second, cells undergoing EMT are more likely to undergo apoptosis than resident lung fibroblasts, which may affect the repair process of lung tissue ( ). Such cell apoptosis may contribute to the persistence of the inflammatory response and the exacerbation of fibrosis. Additionally, cytokines and growth factors released by EMT cells can influence the behavior of surrounding cells and further aggravate fibrosis. Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) released by EMT cells can activate fibroblasts to proliferate and secrete collagen, further worsening fibrosis ( ). Interleukins (IL) including IL-6, IL-4, and IL-13 can recruit and activate macrophages, causing them to release more fibrogenic factors ( ). Overall, moderate EMT can promote tissue repair and regeneration, whereas excessive EMT may lead to the development of fibrosis.
Pulmonary fibrosis, as a major complication in patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection, deserves more attention ( ). It is associated with disease severity, age, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), longer hospital stays, tachycardia, non-invasive mechanical ventilation and higher initial chest computed tomography (CT) score ( ; ; ). The lungs of autopsy cases from severe COVID-19 patients showed extensive pulmonary fibrosis, including thickening of the alveolar septum and fibroblast proliferation ( ). Pulmonary fibrosis is also observed as a major sequela of COVID-19, although it is partially reversible in most recovered individuals ( ). A study has shown that pulmonary fibrosis occurs not only during SARS-CoV-2 infection but also during the 6-month follow-up period after discharge in more than one-third of infected patients who survived severe COVID-19 ( ). The relationship between SARS-CoV-2 infection and pulmonary fibrosis is increasingly reported, however, the mechanisms underlying the COVID-19-related pulmonary fibrosis are not fully understood. Two main possible mechanisms of COVID-19-related pulmonary fibrosis have been proposed: 1) direct lung damage caused by the virus binding to ACE2 receptors and the subsequent irregular and incessant restoration of wounds; 2) the excessive release of profibrotic and proinflammatory cytokines/factors due to an abnormal immune response ( ).
In recent years, miRNAs have been proven to play an important role in the occurrence and development of pulmonary fibrosis. miRNA-155–5p was reported to inhibit EMT by targeting glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK-3β) during radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis ( ). miRNA-15b was reported to promote pulmonary fibrosis by downregulating SMAD specific E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 2 (Smurf2) ( ). Emerging evidence indicates that SARS-CoV-2-encoded miRNAs are involved in the pathology of virus infection, including the regulation of the immune system ( ), regulation of cell metabolism ( ), induction of neurological symptoms ( ), and promotion of lung pathological injuries ( ). A bioinformatics study predicted that SARS-CoV-2 encoded miRNAs may cause increased lung injury by interacting with genes related to cytoskeletal components and inflammatory responses ( ). Another study reached a similar conclusion that two SARS-CoV-2 encoded miRNAs are able to target host genes to regulate cell skeleton organization in lung tissue ( ). A study confirmed eight mature miRNAs from SARS-CoV-2 and predicted 28 potentially targeted genes, most of which are associated with respiratory diseases and viral infection ( ). The inflammatory infiltration and fibrosis induced lung injury in mice that were observed in our study supports and complements these studies. Although sequences of SARS-CoV-2 encoded miRNAs in current studies are often inconsistent, the bioinformatics predictions or experimental results of corresponding target genes are mostly related to the regulation of the host immune system and promotion of pulmonary disease.
It is worth mentioning that a previous study proved that miR-nsp3-3p can be discovered early from serum specimens ( ), while the present study showed that miR-nsp3-3p was detected in throat swab and alveolar lavage fluid specimens instead of serum. The possible reason could be that compared to the previously studied predominant strain Delta, the predominant strain Omicron during our clinical sample collection in early 2023 may have lower virulence and be less invasive into the blood. Our findings at least imply that the presence of miR-nsp3-3p during the viral infection can promote pulmonary fibrosis, and the production of miR-nsp3-3p in at least two viral strains suggests its conservation. Nonetheless, it remains uncertain whether miR-nsp3-3p can persist in the host after viral clearance or following patient recovery upon discharge. For political and safety reasons, we did not employ actual viruses for this portion of the validation. Another point to note is that miR-nsp3-3p may also target other host genes to participate in pulmonary fibrosis or other pathological processes during the viral infection, as it is well known that one miRNA can often regulate multiple mRNAs simultaneously. The specific and comprehensive mechanism requires further investigation.

## Conclusions
In summary, this study demonstrates that miR-nsp3-3p contributes to pulmonary fibrosis during infection by directly targeting ALCAM and promoting EMT in pulmonary epithelial cells, emphasizing the essential role of virus-encoded miRNAs in disease progression. Our research reveals the mechanisms of virus-host interaction and may shed new light on the pathogenesis and therapeutic strategies of related complications and sequelae.

## Materials and methods

## Human samples
Plasma, throat swabs, and BALF samples were collected from Nanjing Jinling Hospital (China). All samples were collected from COVID-19 patients or control groups and written informed consent was obtained from the participants. The control groups in A and B were primarily SARS-CoV-2-uninfected individuals, whereas the control group in C mainly had bacterial or fungal pneumonia. The clinical characteristics of the COVID-19 patients and control groups are described in . This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Jinling Hospital (approval number: 2023DZGZR-035) and performed under the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Cell lines
HEK293T, A549, and Beas-2B cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM, Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA), 100  U/mL penicillin, and 100 ​μg/mL streptomycin. All cells were maintained at 37 ​°C in a 5% CO2 humidified incubator.

## miRNA target prediction and luciferase assay
Two prediction tools, miRanda v3.3a and Targetscan 7.0, were employed to predict potential miR-nsp3-3p targets in all human genes, following the principle of complementary pairing between miRNA seed sequence (bases 2–8 of the 5′ end) and mRNA 3′ UTR sequence, and the binding free energy was lower than −20 ​kcal/mol. The human genome annotation information was downloaded from NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/genome/guide/human/index.shtml).
Wild type or mutant 3′ UTRs of potential target sequences were synthesized and cloned into the luciferase reporter plasmid vector pmirGLO according to the manufacturer's instructions (General Biol, China). HEK293T cells were seeded in 24-well plates. Following the protocol of Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA), we mixed the 0.5 ​μg mRNA 3′ UTR pmirGLO with 100 ​nM miR-nsp3-3p mimics/NC and specific transfection reagent. The above mixture was added into the DMEM medium of HEK293T cells, which were subsequently incubated at 37 ​°C. At 24 ​h post-transfection, cells were lysed, and firefly and Renilla luciferase activities from the cell extracts were measured using the Luciferase Assay Kit (Promega, USA). The sequences of miR-nsp3-3p mimic and related oligonucleotides are listed in .

## Mimics transfection and LV infection
miR-nsp3-3p mimics and inhibitors were purchased from General Biol (China). The ALCAM gene and its negative control was synthesized in General Biol (China) and subcloned into the protein expression vector pcDNA3.0. A549 and Beas-2B cells were cultured in 24-well plates before transfection. After approximately 12 ​h when the cell density reaches 70%–80%, the cell culture medium was replaced with Opti–MEM medium (Invitrogen, USA). The transfection of miR-nsp3-3p mimics and inhibitors was performed with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions.
LV was purchased from Genechem (China). LV-nsp3 contained the partial sequence of SARS-CoV-2 nsp3, within which lies the primer miR-nsp3-3p sequence. The sequence is presented in . LV infection efficiency was determined by LV expressing green fluorescent protein (GFP). To investigate the role of miRNA, groups were divided into mock, LV vector (MOI ​= ​20), LV-nsp3 (MOI ​= ​20), and LV-nsp3 (MOI ​= ​20) ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p inhibitor (100 ​nM) groups. For recovery experiment, four groups were designed, including mock, LV-nsp3 (MOI ​= ​20), LV-nsp3 (MOI ​= ​20) ​+ ​NC (1 ​μg pcDNA3.0), LV-nsp3 (MOI ​= ​20) ​+ ​ALCAM (1 ​μg pcDNA3.0-ALCAM) groups. The transfection of ALCAM vector was performed with Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 ​h transfection, the cells were collected and utilized for further experiments.

## Animal experiments
Eight-week-old male BALB/c mice (20–25 ​g) were purchased from Wukong Experimental Animal Co., Ltd. (China). Mice (6 per cage) were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions and fed autoclaved food and water as needed. Mice were maintained under environmentally controlled conditions (12 ​h/12 ​h light-dark cycle, temperature 24 ​°C, 45% humidity). The grouping of mice and the dose of per mouse per injection were as follows (for each group, n ​= ​6): mock (50 ​μL PBS), AAV vector (1 ​× ​1011 vg), AAV-nsp3 (1 ​× ​1011 vg), AAV-nsp3 (1 ​× ​1011 vg) ​+ ​miR-nsp3-3p antagomir (2 ​nmol). AAV vector (1 ​× ​1011 vg), AAV-nsp3 (1 ​× ​1011 vg), and miR-nsp3-3p antagomirs (2 ​nmol) were dissolved in 50 ​μL PBS. Antagomirs are miR-nsp3-3p antagonists, which are chemically modified ( ) and more stable for experiments in vivo. AAV (GeneChem, China) was intranasally injected into BALB/c mice. The mice were sacrificed at 42 days, then BALF samples and lung tissues were collected for further analysis. All animal experiments were strictly performed in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the National Institutes of Health, and received the approval of Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Nanjing University Affiliated Jinling Hospital (approval number: 2023JLHGZRDWLS-00041).

## Histology and immunohistochemical staining
Paraffin-embedded mice lung sections were prepared by a routine procedure. Sections (4 ​μm thick) were stained with H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining to observe lung injury and fibrosis. Quantification of the fibrotic area of Masson’s trichrome staining was calculated by using Image Pro-Plus v. 6.0 software (Bethesda, USA). Immunostaining for collagen I was performed. The lung sections were incubated with anti-collagen I (1:100; Abclonal, A1352) overnight at 4 ​°C, followed by incubation with goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (1:500; ABCAM, ab6721) for 1 ​h at 37 ​°C and developed with diaminobenzidine (DAB). Then tissue sections were counterstained with hematoxylin and permanently mounted.

## qRT-PCR assay
Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The cDNA was reverse-transcribed from the total RNA obtained by HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR kit (Vazyme, China). miRNAs were extracted using a column-based miRcute miRNA isolation kit (TIANGEN, China). miRNA quantitative PCR was carried out using a miRNA PCR Kit (Vazyme, China) as described previously ( ). For synthesis of miRNA cDNA, stem-loop primers were designed and used to reverse transcribe the miRNA into specific stem-loop cDNA. qRT-PCR was performed using AceQ qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, China) and was run on the Roche LightCycler 96. mRNA and miRNA expression levels were normalized to GAPDH and miR-16-5p, respectively. The 2−ΔΔCt method was used to calculate relative gene expression. All primer sequences are listed in .

## Western blotting
Total proteins from mouse lung tissues and cultured cells were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Sigma, USA, R0278) supplemented with protease inhibitors (Roche, USA, 04693116001). A total of 30 ​μg proteins added per line and separated by 10% SDS-PAGE, then transferred to 0.45 ​μm PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA, 24937-79-9). The PVDF membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk and incubated at 4 ​°C overnight with primary antibodies. The primary antibodies include: anti-ALCAM (1:2000; Abclonal, A23441), anti-E-cadherin (1:1000; Abclonal, A24874), anti-Vimentin (1:500; Abclonal, AP0806), anti-α-SMA (1:2000; Servicebio, GB111364-100), anti-GAPDH (1:3000; Servicebio, GB15002-100). Then incubated with the secondary antibodies for 1 ​h at room temperature. The secondary antibodies include: Goat Anti-Mouse IgG H&L (HRP) (1:10000; ABCAM, ab205719), Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (1:10000; ABCAM, ab6721). The antibody-antigen complexes were detected using the Automatic Chemical Exposure Analysis System (Bioworld, China).

## Invasion assay
Diluted Matrigel was coated onto the Transwell chamber surface (Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, CA) and incubated at 37 ​°C for 1 ​h to allow gelation. After the cells were prepared into suspension, 100 ​μL cell suspension were seeded into the upper chamber and 600 ​μL DMEM with 15% FBS in the lower chamber. The cells were incubated for 24 ​h, fixed in 70% methanol for 30 ​min, and subsequently stained by crystal violet solution for 10 ​min. Finally, we observed the cells under a microscope and took photographs for counting.

## Immunofluorescence microscopy
A549 and Beas-2B cells were rinsed with PBS three times and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 ​min at room temperature. After being permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 30 ​min, cells were blocked with 3% BSA for 40 ​min and incubated with primary antibody overnight at 4 ​°C. Cells were rinsed with PBS three times and were incubated with FITC-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature in the dark for 1 ​h followed by incubating with DAPI solution for 5 ​min. After being mounted with anti-fluorescence quencher mounting medium, the fluorescent signals were visualized under fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan). Quantitative analysis of the fluorescence intensity images was processed using ImageJ (Bethesda, MD, USA).

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism Software 9 (San Diego, CA, USA). Normal distribution of data was assessed in all cases by Shapiro-Wilk test. The data shown are presented as the means ​± ​SD. Data following or not following a normal distribution were analyzed with parametric (unpaired Student's t-test) or non-parametric (Kruskal-Wallis test) statistics, respectively. Pearson correlation test was employed for correlation analysis. Differences were considered significant at P ​< ​0.05, and non-significant differences was indicated by ns.
