No use, distribution or reproduction is permitted which does not comply with these terms.Transdifferentiated melanoma is a rare variant of melanoma characterized by phenotypic plasticity, loss of conventional melanocytic markers, and acquisition of features from other tissue lineages, often associated with aggressive biological behavior. Among these, melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation represents an exceptionally rare presentation that poses significant diagnostic and clinical challenges, particularly in the context of immunotherapy. We report the case of a 38-year-old male with a remote history of cutaneous melanoma who presented with an isolated left axillary nodal recurrence. Histopathological examination revealed a biphasic melanoma with a dedifferentiated component showing focal rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation. The patient was treated with neoadjuvant immunotherapy using ipilimumab and nivolumab. Treatment was discontinued after two cycles due to immune-related hepatitis and aseptic meningitis requiring immunosuppressive therapy. Post-treatment imaging demonstrated a mixed metabolic response. Subsequent axillary lymphadenectomy revealed a complete pathological response of the melanocytic component, while a high-grade sarcomatoid/rhabdomyoblastic component remained viable, exhibiting high proliferative activity and loss of melanocytic markers. Molecular profiling identified NRAS Q61R and TERT promoter mutations, equivocal MYC amplification, and an elevated tumor mutational burden. The patient received adjuvant radiotherapy and remains under close oncologic surveillance. This case illustrates an atypical pattern of pathological response to neoadjuvant immunotherapy, characterized by eradication of the differentiated melanocytic component and persistence of a dedifferentiated, rhabdomyoblastic tumor population. These findings suggest phenotypic plasticity and immune escape as potential mechanisms of resistance, underscoring the need for integrated histopathological and molecular assessment when evaluating pathological response in rare melanoma variants. As neoadjuvant immunotherapy becomes increasingly incorporated into clinical practice, it is crucial to characterize its impact across the diverse presentations of melanoma and to understand the distinct patterns of pathological response.

On January 14, 2025, he underwent left axillary lymphadenectomy (levels I, II, and III), along with interpectoral lymph node dissection. Pathological examination of the axillary lymphadenectomy specimen revealed fibroadipose tissue free of neoplastic involvement, while the lymphadenectomy specimen showed a high-grade sarcomatoid malignant neoplasm, with no necrosis, a mitotic index of 51 mitoses per 10 high-power fields, no evidence of lymphoid tissue infiltration by the tumor, and no neoplastic involvement in any of the 17 evaluated lymph nodes. Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated a high proliferative index with Ki-67 staining between 70–80%, supporting the high-grade nature of the tumor. Focal positivity was observed for smooth muscle actin (SMA), desmin, and CD99, indicating mesenchymal and myogenic differentiation. Additionally, focal positivity for Myogenin and MyoD1 provided clear evidence of rhabdomyoblastic differentiation. In contrast, markers typically associated with melanocytic differentiation, including S-100, SOX-10, Melan-A, and HMB-45, and present at baseline, were all negative, indicating loss of the melanocytic phenotype ( ).
 Following multidisciplinary review involving pathology and nuclear medicine specialists, these findings were interpreted as consistent with complete regression of the original differentiated/melanocytic component and persistence of a dedifferentiated, rhabdomyoblastic sarcomatoid component, in keeping with a high-grade sarcomatoid melanoma variant.
Comprehensive molecular profiling was subsequently performed using a next-generation sequencing (NGS) panel targeting gene fusions and clinically relevant genomic alterations of the residual, viable component. No clinically relevant fusions were detected. However, the analysis revealed a pathogenic NRAS Q61R mutation (MAF 43.1%) and a TERT promoter c.-146C>T mutation (MAF 54.9%). An equivocal MYC amplification was also detected (6 copies, chr8q24.21). The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was elevated at 18 mutations per megabase, while microsatellite stability was preserved (MSI-stable).
Therefore, adjuvant radiotherapy was administered to the left axillary region, supraclavicular fossa, and internal thoracic (mammary) chain. Treatment was delivered between March 14 and April 10, 2025, at a total dose of 48 Gy in 20 fractions. The patient tolerated the regimen well and remains under close oncological surveillance. At the most recent imaging assessment (November 24, 2025), there was no evidence of disease.

## Methods
3.1

## Histopathology and immunohistochemistry
The post-treatment axillary lymphadenectomy specimen (levels I–III) was reviewed by dedicated pathologists. Given the clinical history of neoadjuvant immunotherapy, immunohistochemistry was performed on five tumor blocks/samples selected from areas considered most suspicious for residual melanoma. The immunohistochemical panel included epithelial markers (AE1/AE3, EMA, CK7), melanocytic markers (SOX10, S-100, Melan-A, HMB45, PRAME), myogenic markers (smooth muscle actin, desmin, myogenin), and other ancillary markers (CD34, CD99, pan-TRK, H3K27M, INI-1), as well as Ki-67 for assessment of proliferative index. Ki-67 was reported as 70–80%, and INI-1 expression was retained.

3.2

## Molecular profiling (NGS and fusion testing)
Comprehensive molecular profiling was performed on the viable sarcomatoid tumor component from the lymphadenectomy specimen. The estimated tumor cellularity in the analyzed area was 90%. Next-generation sequencing was carried out using the institutional HSL500 genomic panel (Illumina TruSight™ Oncology 500) and an RNA fusion assay (Archer® FusionPlex® HSL Sarcoma). Sequencing for the TSO500 assay was performed on the Illumina NextSeq 550, and analysis of DNA/RNA data was conducted using DRAGEN TSO500 HT v2.5.2 via Illumina Connected Analytics, with variant prioritization supported by Illumina Connected Annotations. The fusion assay evaluates 67 genes for rearrangements using AMP™-based methodology followed by NGS on Illumina MiSeq, with data analyzed using Archer® Analysis v7.
Tumor mutational burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) were reported by the assay; TMB was calculated as eligible coding SNVs divided by the adequately covered genomic territory (mutations/Mb), excluding likely germline variants, and MSI status was derived from analysis of mononucleotide repeat sites using the assay-defined thresholds. Copy-number results were interpreted according to institutional thresholds, with “equivocal amplification” defined as copy number >6 and below the assay threshold for unequivocal amplification.

## Discussion
Transdifferentiated melanoma is a rare and poorly characterized phenomenon, typically arising in the context of advanced disease progression or therapeutic resistance, although these tumors may retain molecular features associated with immunogenicity, including high tumor mutational burden ( ).
Among these, melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation is an exceedingly rare and poorly characterized phenomenon. The literature consists predominantly of isolated case reports, and although histopathological features have been increasingly documented, there remains a lack of therapeutic guidance for this distinct variant ( , ).
Histologically, melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation is characterized by tumor cells exhibiting features of striated muscle differentiation, including abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, perinuclear filament aggregates, and positivity for myogenic markers such as Myogenin and MyoD1, while often losing expression of conventional melanocytic markers such as SOX-10, S-100, Melan-A, and HMB-45 ( – ).
The immunohistochemical profile of this case is consistent with the findings reported in the literature for melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation, demonstrating complete loss of conventional melanocytic markers (S-100, HMB-45, Melan-A), along with focal expression of Myogenin and MyoD1 ( , ).
Notably, in our patient, the tumor evolved from a clearly melanocytic phenotype at initial recurrence to a fully dedifferentiated sarcomatoid morphology following neoadjuvant immunotherapy. This phenotypic transformation highlights the known plasticity of melanoma cells, likely stemming from their neural crest origin and retained multilineage potential under therapeutic selective pressure ( , ).
The concept of melanoma transdifferentiation has gained support from molecular studies demonstrating that these histologic changes often occur without loss of key oncogenic drivers. In our patient, next-generation sequencing confirmed the presence of NRAS Q61R and TERT promoter mutations, consistent with cutaneous melanoma, along with elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB = 18 muts/Mb) and equivocal MYC amplification. These findings parallel those reported by Weigelt et al., who reported a primary cutaneous neoplasm with rhabdomyosarcomatous morphology that closely resembled melanoma both morphologically and molecularly. In their case, next-generation sequencing revealed a mutational profile strongly supportive of melanocytic origin, including NRAS Q61R and TERT promoter mutations, similar to our patient, as well as CDKN2A and TP53 mutations ( ). Despite its myogenic phenotype, molecular analysis confirmed a melanoma-like genetic landscape, reinforcing the diagnostic and therapeutic relevance of integrating histopathologic and genomic data in such ambiguous presentations. Ferreira et al. have similarly shown that transdifferentiated melanomas frequently preserve their oncogenic drivers, suggesting that molecular identity is maintained despite dramatic morphological evolution ( , ).
From a diagnostic perspective, these tumors pose a formidable challenge. Loss of melanocytic markers and acquisition of a myogenic immunophenotype may lead to misclassification as primary sarcomas or other soft tissue tumors ( , ). PRAME (Preferentially Expressed Antigen in Melanoma) has emerged as a useful diagnostic adjunct in such settings, aiding in confirmation of melanocytic lineage when conventional markers are lost. In a recent study by Choy et al., PRAME expression was retained in 73% of dedifferentiated or transdifferentiated primary cutaneous melanomas, despite complete loss of conventional melanocytic markers. In cases with ambiguous morphology, including those with rhabdomyoblastic features, strong nuclear PRAME staining supported a diagnosis of melanoma ( , ).
Therapeutic strategies for melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation remain undefined. These tumors are widely considered aggressive and chemoresistant, although sporadic reports have described responsiveness to immunotherapy and radiotherapy ( , ). Gupta et al. reported a case of metastatic melanoma with rhabdomyosarcomatous differentiation that responded well to combined immunotherapy and palliative radiotherapy. The patient experienced significant tumor reduction and symptom relief, with possible abscopal effect, despite complete loss of melanocytic markers ( ). This highlights radiotherapy’s potential role beyond palliation, including immune modulation. Similarly, in our case, adjuvant radiotherapy was chosen after neoadjuvant immunotherapy due to the tumor’s high-grade, dedifferentiated profile and uncertain prognosis.
Neoadjuvant immunotherapy has emerged as a promising approach in resectable stage III melanoma, supported by accumulating evidence from trials such as OpACIN, OpACIN-neo, and PRADO ( – ). The recent phase III NADINA trial further validated this approach, showing that neoadjuvant ipilimumab (1 mg/kg) plus nivolumab (3 mg/kg) administered every three weeks for two cycles, followed by therapeutic lymph node dissection and response-adapted adjuvant therapy, significantly improves outcomes compared to the standard strategy of upfront surgery followed by one year of adjuvant nivolumab. With a median follow-up of 25 months, the neoadjuvant approach achieved 2-year EFS rates of 77.3% versus 55.7% (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.28–0.57) and 2-year DMFS rates of 82.8% versus 63.9% (HR 0.43; 95% CI 0.29–0.64), and yielded superior depth of pathologic response and early systemic disease control; moreover, IFNγ, PD-L1, and TMB emerged as predictive biomarkers, with biomarker-high subgroups reaching 24-month EFS rates of up to 96–100% in the neoadjuvant arm. Patients achieving a major pathological response (MPR, ≤10% viable tumor) demonstrated particularly favorable outcomes, reinforcing MPR as a clinically meaningful surrogate endpoint ( , , ).
Consensus guidelines, including those from the International Neoadjuvant Melanoma Consortium (INMC), define pathologic complete response (pCR) as the absence of residual invasive tumor in the resected specimen. Other pathologic substages, including near pCR (>0% but ≤10% viable tumor), pPR (>10% to ≤ 50% viable tumor), and no pathologic response (pNR; >50% viable tumor), are increasingly used to guide adjuvant treatment decisions. In general, major pathologic response (≤10% viable tumor) allows for treatment de-escalation, while lesser responses indicate a need for further systemic therapy ( ).
Our case raises important questions regarding the interpretation of pathological response in the setting of dedifferentiated or transdifferentiated tumors. While the absence of conventional melanoma cells might suggest a favorable outcome, the persistence of a high-grade sarcomatoid component, known to portend poor prognosis, introduces clinical uncertainty. This scenario underscores the need for refinement in how pathologic response is defined and interpreted, particularly in the setting of rare histological variants.
Emerging evidence suggests that transdifferentiation following immunotherapy may represent a mechanism of immune escape, potentially driven by clonal selection or phenotypic adaptation ( , ).
Tran et al. described a progression model in which melanoma evolves from conventional histology through an undifferentiated sarcomatoid phase to a rhabdomyosarcomatous phenotype. In their 2019 report, they presented a case of metastatic melanoma harboring a BRAF V600E mutation that initially responded to targeted therapy but relapsed with an undifferentiated sarcoma-like tumor expressing mesenchymal and myogenic markers, ultimately transitioning to a rhabdomyoblastic phenotype with strong desmin and Myogenin positivity. Molecular analysis confirmed clonal relationship across histologic stages, supporting true lineage continuity ( ).
Importantly, both the intermediate and final phenotypes could have been misdiagnosed without clinical context and genomic data. Our findings are consistent with this hypothesis and raise the possibility that immune checkpoint blockade, while initially effective, may inadvertently select for resistant dedifferentiated clones.
In conclusion, this case highlights the complexity of melanoma evolution under therapeutic pressure and the limitations of current response-based algorithms. As neoadjuvant strategies become increasingly integrated into standard practice, the field must prepare to address rare and biologically distinct tumor phenotypes. Collaborative registries, centralized pathology review, and integration of molecular diagnostics will be crucial for advancing our understanding and improving outcomes in these challenging cases. Our case contributes to this emerging body of knowledge, providing important insights into the biology, diagnostic considerations, and therapeutic implications of this rare melanoma variant in the immunotherapy era.
This report has several limitations. As a single case, its findings may not be generalizable. In addition, the absence of longitudinal molecular analyses limits the ability to fully characterize the mechanisms underlying phenotypic transformation and therapeutic resistance.

## Patient perspective
The patient reported significant concern upon noticing the axillary mass after a long disease-free interval. He described the immune-related adverse events and hospitalization as physically and emotionally challenging. Following surgery and radiotherapy, he expressed relief with the current clinical status and remains motivated to adhere to close oncologic surveillance.
