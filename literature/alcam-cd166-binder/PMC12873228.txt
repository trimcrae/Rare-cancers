## Abstract
The potential cytotoxicity of dextran in deswelling media for DMEK graft preparation remains controversial. This study compared endothelial viability and viable endothelial cell density (vECD) in paired deswelled and control (non-deswelled) corneas. Eight pairs of human corneas were preserved in TISSUE-C (standard organ culture medium). Baseline endothelial cell densities (ECD1, ECD2) at two time points were equivalent. Two days before DMEK preparation, one cornea from each pair was transferred to CARRY-C (6% dextran deswelling medium), while its pair remained in TISSUE-C (control). On the preparation day, ECD3 was measured. Viability (percentage of viable endothelial areas) and vECD were determined by Hoechst-33,342/Calcein-AM staining, and cell morphology was assessed with CD166 immunofluorescence. ECD3 was higher in control corneas than in deswelled corneas (2150 ± 737 vs. 1708 ± 745 cells/mm², p = 0.0176), confirmed by Hoechst-based ECD (2025 ± 723 vs. 1775 ± 706, p = 0.0167). Viability was significantly higher in deswelled corneas (78% vs. 96%, p = 0.0013), yielding comparable vECD between groups (1628 ± 715 vs. 1712 ± 723, p = 0.3733). Non-viable areas in control corneas corresponded to endothelial folds from corneal edema. Deswelling markedly reduced folds, and cell migration from viable to non-viable areas was observed, explaining the lower ECD after deswelling. In conclusion, short-term deswelling lowers the measured ECD but does not reduce the total number of viable endothelial cells. The apparent decrease reflects cell migration from viable areas to cover the entire endothelial surface. These findings support the safety of dextran-based deswelling media for DMEK graft preparation and likely apply to penetrating keratoplasty as well.

## Introduction
The corneal endothelium is a monolayer of hexagonal cells attached to the Descemet’s membrane (DM), lining the inner surface of the cornea. It plays a crucial role in maintaining corneal transparency by regulating stromal hydration. Corneal endothelial dysfunction is a leading indication for corneal transplantation . A total of 30,804 of 48,799 (63.1%) keratoplasty procedures in the United States in 2023 were performed for endothelial failure .
For treating endothelial diseases, different surgical techniques may be used: penetrating keratoplasty (PK), Descemet’s stripping automated endothelial keratoplasty (DSAEK), and Descemet’s membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK). DMEK, known for superior outcomes such as enhanced visual recovery, reduced rejection risk, and improved graft survival, appears as the treatment of choice – . According to the Eye Bank Association of America (EBAA), DMEK increased from 6% of all corneal transplants in 2014 to 34% in 2023 , . This trend is consistent in Europe, where DMEK accounted for 29.7% of corneal transplants in 2018 , and notably in Germany, where it became the predominant procedure, escalating from 53% in 2016 to 65% in 2021 .
DMEK graft preparation, which involves the DM peeling, is a delicate surgical procedure. The optimal preservation of corneal endothelial cells (CECs) on the graft is essential for its success. Graft quality is typically assessed by measuring endothelial cell density (ECD). A higher ECD is a critical factor for ensuring longer graft survival.
In Europe, for eye banks using the organ culture (OC) system, depending on the protocol of the eye bank or surgeon, DMEK grafts may be prepared either from edematous corneas or from deswelled corneas with near-normal thickness after a 1–3 day immersion in a deswelling medium containing a high concentration of macromolecules, most commonly 5–6% T-500 dextran – . The hyperosmotic medium reduces corneal thickness and consequently edema induced-endothelial folds, which may facilitate the manual peeling of DM. However, a significant reduction in ECD, assumed to be caused by dextran cytotoxicity, has been reported after deswelling during years 90–2000 – . Conversely, several clinical studies have shown comparable postoperative ECD in patients receiving DMEK using corneas stored without or with the final deswelling step , .
This controversy prompted us to investigate whether short-term corneal deswelling using a medium containing a high concentration (5–6%) of T-500 dextran induces a decrease in effective viable endothelial cell density (vECD).

## Methods

## Human Corneas and ethical statement
This study used eight pairs of organ-cultured (OC) human corneas, rejected by the Eye Bank of Saint-Etienne for not meeting transplantation criteria (ECD < 2000 cells/mm² or non-compliant serology). Donor age was 79 ± 11 (61–93) years, and the time between death and procurement (postmortem time) was 17 ± 6 (6–25) hours. All corneas were initially stored at 31 °C in 100 mL of TISSUE-C (TIS 001 − 00, Alchimia, Antony, Italy), a commercial CE-marked OC medium that does not contain dextran. The BiiO Laboratory is authorized to use human tissues under approval from the French Ministry of Higher Education, Research, and Innovation (authorization number DC-2023-5458). All procedures adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and to French regulations regarding the scientific use of human tissues.

## Experimental workflow
Inclusion criteria required an initial ECD (ECD1) > 1500 cells/mm², measured on day 3 ± 1 (2–4), and an intra-pair ECD difference of < 15%. After ECD1 measurement, the medium was renewed. A second ECD assessment (ECD2) was performed after a total storage time of 13 ± 3 (7–17) days in TISSUE-C. One cornea from each pair was then randomly transferred to CARRY-C (CAR 001 − 00, Alchimia, Antony, Italy), the deswelling medium containing 6% T-500 dextran, while its mate was returned to TISSUE-C (same bottle, without a second renewal). Two days later, a third ECD (ECD3) was measured before DMEK graft preparation. Following pre-dissection, corneas were sent to the research laboratory for analysis within approximately 2 h. Endothelial viability and viable ECD (vECD) were assessed using Hoechst 33,342 and Calcein-AM double staining (Fig.  ). Immunofluorescence (IF) for endothelial morphology was performed on only two pairs.
 

## Preparation of Pre-dissected (-stripped) DMEK grafts
Pre-dissected DMEK grafts were prepared using a slightly modified “SCUBA” technique . Briefly, under an operating microscope, after installing the cornea on a white trephine support to improve visualization, a 360° peripheral rupture of DM was created using fine-toothed forceps, aided by 0.2% Trypan blue (TB) staining (0.4% TB diluted 1:1 with BSS). A curved separator was used to free a ~ 0.5 mm DM border from the stroma. After filling the endothelial side with TISSUE-C or Balanced Salt Solution (BSS, 0065-0795-15, Alcon), DM was detached halfway across the cornea at four 90° intervals, leaving the center attached. Finally, DM was repositioned against the stroma by removing fluid with a cellulose surgical sponge.

## Eye bank-Routine ECD measurement
ECD1–3 were measured at the Eye Bank using light transmission microscopy (LEITZ Laborlux S, Wetzlar, Germany). After staining with 0.4% TB for one minute followed by 0.9% sodium chloride for four minutes, CECs were observed at 10× magnification. Ten images of the central 8 mm area were captured, and ECD was calculated by counting at least 300 cells across three images using the Sambacornée™ analyzer (Sambatechnologies, France) , .

## Viable ECD measurement

## Staining protocol for Pre-dissected DMEK grafts
An optimized staining technique was employed . Briefly, the staining mixture, comprising 4 µM Calcein-AM (FP-FI9820, Interchim) and 5 µg/mL Hoechst 33,342 (B2261, Sigma-Aldrich), was prepared in Opti-MEM phenol red-free medium (11058021, Gibco). Pre-dissected DMEK grafts, still attached to the corneas, were gently rinsed in TISSUE-C, placed endothelial side up in a concave-shaped support, and incubated in the dark at room temperature for 45 min with 200 µL of the staining mixture. To mitigate undesirable stromal fluorescence from Calcein-AM and staining of dying CECs by Hoechst 33,342, the pre-dissected grafts were immersed in 0.2% TB (0.4% TB diluted 1:1 with BSS) on the endothelial side. Gentle agitation facilitated TB entry between the detached DM and stroma. After a 20-second incubation, TB was removed, and the grafts were immersed in TISSUE-C to eliminate excess dye. As TB quenches fluorescence, this final TB staining may stain the stromal side of DM to suppress fluorescence from viable stromal cells labeled by Calcein, thereby enhancing the contrast of Calcein-AM on pre-dissected DMEK grafts. In addition, TB quenches the nuclei of dying CECs, allowing Hoechst staining to label only viable CECs.

## Imaging
A fluorescence macroscope (also called macro-zoom microscope), MVX10 (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), equipped with CellSens imaging software (Olympus, Hamburg, Germany) and a DP74 color and monochrome camera, was used to acquire images. For viability measurement, a Z-stack image (Z-interval ~ 400 μm) was acquired across the entire endothelial surface using a 1x objective (MV PLAPo, Olympus) and 0.8x zoom. A single, fully focused composite image was automatically generated by CellSens software to account for corneal curvature. For Hoechst-highlighted ECD (ECD-h), five Z-stack images (Z-interval ~ 40 μm) were acquired from the center and four peripheral quadrants of the central 8 mm area using a 1x objective and 6.3x zoom . By definition ECD-h is an accurate measurement of cell density outside the folds where cells are absent.

## Viability, ECD-h, and vECD analysis
Endothelial viability (% of area covered by Calcein + cells within the central 8 mm) and ECD-h were semi-automatically analyzed using the CorneaJ plugin in ImageJ . To ensure high accuracy, fidelity and reproducibility, on average, approximately 5500 Hoechst-stained CEC nuclei were counted per cornea to determine the ECD-h. The vECD was calculated by multiplying the ECD-h by the percentage viability, reflecting the effective ECD by accounting for all dead/denuded areas. In other words, vECD directly quantifies the pool of viable CECs on the endothelial grafts.

## Immunofluorescence on flat mounts of DMEK grafts
Immunofluorescence (IF) on flat-mounted corneas was performed as previously described , , with specific tips for IF after Calcein-AM staining detailed elsewhere . Briefly, following the viability assay, the pre-dissected DMEK graft (still attached to the central stroma) was immersed in 25 mL of pure methanol for 30 min at room temperature. Corneal rehydration was performed in 25 mL of PBS for 24 h to remove residual Calcein fluorescence. The DM was gently separated from the corneal stroma and incubated in blocking buffer at 37 °C for 30 min. For IF, the DM was incubated with the primary antibody CD166 (1/200, Mouse IgG, 559260, BD Pharmingen) for 1 h at 37 °C. After rinsing in PBS, samples were incubated with secondary antibodies (1/500, Alexa Fluor 488 goat anti-mouse IgG, A-11001, Invitrogen) for 1 h at 37 °C, supplemented with 5 µg/mL DAPI for nuclear counterstaining. Following further rinses, the DM was delicately spread on a drop of fluorescent mounting medium (NB-23–00158, NeoBio Mount Fluo, NeoBiotech) on a glass slide. A cut was made to prevent folding, and a coverslip was applied.
Imaging was performed using an Olympus IX81 fluorescence inverted microscope (Olympus), equipped with CellSens software and an ORCA-Flash 4.0 monochrome camera (C11440, Hamamatsu, Japan).

## Statistics
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, MA, USA). Normality was assessed with the Shapiro-Wilk test. As data were normally distributed, paired t-tests were used for comparisons (Figs.  , and ). Statistical significance was set at *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (min–max).
 

## Results

## Lower ECD observed in the deswelling group
ECD 1, 2 and 3 was assessed at three time points at the eye bank using a routine technique. Their statistical comparison is shown in Fig.  . ECD1, measured on day 3 of storage in TISSUE-C, served as the initial baseline and showed no significant difference between groups. ECD2, assessed just before the deswelling step, also showed no significant difference. In contrast, ECD3 measured just before DMEK pre-dissection (i.e., after a 2-day storage in CARRY-C for the deswelling group) showed a significant difference. Corneas in the non-deswelling group had an ECD approximately 25% higher (+ 442 cells/mm²) than those in the deswelling group.

## Higher endothelial viability observed in the deswelling group
A significant 23% relative (18% absolute) increase in endothelial viability was observed in the deswelling group (Fig.  B). Viability was defined as the percentage of the central 8 mm area stained by Calcein-AM (Fig.  A). This increase resulted from a marked reduction in non-viable areas, which corresponded to endothelial folds present in control corneas after OC but were dramatically reduced following the deswelling process.

## Equivalent viable ECD between groups
ECD-h was analyzed within the central 8 mm, with an average of 5,500 cells counted per cornea. ECD-h values were equivalent to the ECD3 values provided by the eye bank. However, similar to ECD3, ECD-h represents only the viable regions and does not reflect the total number of viable cells on the graft. Therefore, vECD was calculated as ECD-h × viability. No statistically significant difference in vECD was observed between the two groups (Fig.  ).

## Cell migration from adjacent regions allowed closure of Non-viable areas in the deswelling group
The non-viable areas observed in the control corneas corresponded to endothelial folds. Microscopic imaging (Hoechst nuclear staining and CD166/DAPI immunofluorescence) revealed sharply demarcated acellular regions. In the deswelling group, these non-viable areas were markedly reduced, and migration of neighboring viable cells into these denuded zones was evident (Fig.  A, B). No apparent cellular or nuclear morphological abnormalities were observed.

(A2, A2’) Higher-magnification views of the boxed regions in A1/A1’, showing Hoechst 33,342 nuclear staining (white) and detailing cell distribution within non-viable areas. (B1, B1’) Viability staining of a pair of pre-dissected DMEK grafts. (B2, B2’) IF staining of cell membranes with an anti-CD166 antibody (green). ## Discussion
The use of a medium containing 5–6% dextran is essential at the end of OC to achieve corneal deswelling. This step is particularly important for penetrating keratoplasty (PK), where restoration of near-physiological thickness is required to allow accurate suturing. However, such deswelling media have been associated with significant endothelial toxicity – .
For DMEK graft preparation, endothelial dissection can be performed using either edematous corneas stored in OC or corneas that have been thinned by deswelling , . While edematous corneas frequently develop endothelial folds, particularly non-centripetal folds that can complicate DM peeling, reduced ECD has been reported in DMEK grafts prepared from corneas deswelled in dextran-containing medium . Although several studies have described endothelial damage due to exposure to high concentrations (5–6%) of dextran, others have demonstrated equivalent postoperative endothelial cell counts between deswelled and non-deswelled grafts , . Thus, the impact of short-term exposure (up to several days) to high-concentration dextran on corneal endothelial cell viability during OC remains controversial.
In the present study, we evaluated the effects of a 48-hour exposure to CARRY-C, a 6% dextran-supplemented medium, on corneal endothelium prior to DMEK graft preparation. We used paired corneas with comparable baseline ECD (ECD1) to increase statistical power while minimizing tissue use. Prior to deswelling, all corneas were stored for two weeks in 100 mL of TISSUE-C, a commercial OC medium known to induce marked stromal swelling . As expected, after deswelling, ECD3 was significantly lower in deswelled corneas compared with non-deswelled controls seemingly confirming the detrimental effect of dextran.
However, a limitation of routine eye bank ECD measurement (ECD1 to ECD3) is that cell counting is performed only in areas where CECs are visible by phase-contrast microscopy, which relies on sodium chloride–induced intercellular space dilation. Zones devoid of visible cells (non-viable areas) are not included in the routine ECD calculation. Therefore, viability assessment using Calcein-AM staining quantifying the entire area covered by viable CECs is essential to determine the total number of viable cells on the graft. Contrary to the routine ECD results, viability was significantly higher in the deswelled group. Consequently, the calculated vECD a more reliable predictor of postoperative ECD than standard eye bank measurements , was equivalent between groups.
These apparently contradictory findings can be explained by CEC redistribution from viable to denuded areas, a wound-healing response known to occur during OC . Non-deswelled corneas exhibited non-viable regions corresponding to deep endothelial folds caused by OC-induced stromal edema, a phenomenon particularly prominent after long-term storage in TISSUE-C. In deswelled corneas, these folds were markedly reduced, and the non-viable areas were significantly smaller. Microscopic evidence indicated that neighboring CECs migrated to repopulate these denuded zones.
This redistribution of a constant number of viable cells over a larger posterior surface area results in a lower routine ECD, while the total number of viable cells and thus the vECD remains unchanged. In swollen corneas, viable cells are tightly packed at a locally higher density outside of deep folds, which represent non-viable areas. In deswelled corneas, however, viable cells are distributed uniformly across the entire posterior surface at a slightly lower density. Consequently, the total viable cell pools are comparable between groups.
In this context, deswelling may simply anticipate the wound-healing process that would otherwise occur in the recipient eye. Our findings help reconcile the apparent controversy in the literature: studies relying on routine ECD measurements may report a negative effect of deswelling , whereas those evaluating clinical outcomes demonstrate equivalence , .
We highlighted several potential limitations of this study. The mean age of the donors included was 79 ± 11 years (61–93), which is higher than the mean donor age in Europe (70.5 years), as reported in the 2022 EEBA statistical report . Because corneal endothelial quality decreases with age, future studies would benefit from repeating this investigation using corneas from younger donors. Furthermore, we selected a 48-hour deswelling period because it represents a reasonable and logistically convenient duration for corneal assessment and transportation to surgeons. However, shorter or longer periods such as 24–72 h are also commonly used. Since cell migration requires time, it would be valuable in future work to examine endothelial status after different deswelling durations.
In conclusion, short-term deswelling in CARRY-C facilitates the redistribution of CECs, enabling them to repopulate non-viable areas associated with endothelial folds from OC-induced edema, without incurring additional cell loss. While this process lowers routine ECD, the total pool of viable cells (vECD)—a key predictor of graft survival—remains intact, reinforcing the safety and clinical relevance of dextran-based deswelling for DMEK graft preparation.
