Peptidylarginine deiminase 2 (PAD2) is an enzyme responsible for catalyzing the post-translational modification of arginine into citrulline, a process known as citrullination. In this study, we investigated the effect of AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, on survival and function of CECs. Cultured human CECs (hCECs) were treated with AMF30a, followed by treatments with or without AMF30a under transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β). Cells were cultured from donor corneas and analyzed for viability (CCK-8), proliferation (BrdU assay), cytotoxicity (LDH assay), and oxidative stress (DCF-DA). Adhesion and morphology were evaluated via crystal violet staining and imaging software. Western blot and immunofluorescence identified protein expression and localization. RNA sequencing revealed differentially expressed genes, with functional enrichment via GO analysis. AMF30a enhanced cell viability, proliferation, and adhesion while reducing cytotoxicity and oxidative stress. Morphological analysis revealed reduced cell size and elongation factor, indicating structural changes. AMF30a modulated the HIPPO signaling pathway by increasing YAP phosphorylation and reducing its nuclear translocation, while downregulating ERK1/2 activation. Transcriptome analysis identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with AMF30a treatment, highlighting pathways related to GPCR signaling and protein targeting. Additionally, AMF30a counteracted TGF-β-induced effects, including increased oxidative stress, citrullination, and ROCK/HIPPO signaling activation, thereby promoting cell cycle progression and reducing senescence. PAD2-mediated citrullination is essential for TGF-β/ROCK/HIPPO signaling pathway. AMF30a promoted the proliferation and protected against TGF-β-induced senescence in hCECs, suggesting that PAD2 plays a significant role in the survival and function of hCECs. Thus, AMF30a may be a promising therapeutic strategy for hCEC diseases.

## Introduction
The corneal endothelium is a layer of cells that lines the posterior surface of the cornea, which is the clear front part of the eye . The primary function of corneal endothelial cells (CECs) is to maintain corneal transparency by pumping fluid out of the cornea and keeping it in a relatively dehydrated state . When the cell density is reduced below a critical level due to severe injury or disease, corneal edema develops and vision is compromised , , requiring corneal transplantation because they cannot regenerate in vivo . Thus, there have been a lot of efforts to develop therapies to treat corneal endothelial dysfunction effectively through developing the methods to enhance the regenerative potential of human CECs (hCECs). TGF- β and senescence have been reported to be implicated in the decreased regenerative capacity of hCECs , , but the intricate mechanisms underlying this relationship remain largely unexplored. TGF-β induces senescence and involves aging-related pathology . TGF-β activates the ROCK signaling pathway through TGF-β receptors that stimulate the phosphorylation of RhoA GTPase, an upstream regulator of ROCK . Furthermore, TGF-β interacts with the HIPPO pathway by regulating the localization and activity of YAP and TAZ, which involves TGF-β-induced changes in cell shape and cytoskeletal dynamics , . The HIPPO signaling pathway plays a significant role in corneal endothelial cells by regulating their proliferation, apoptosis, and barrier function, which are essential for maintaining corneal clarity and hydration , . ROCK, HIPPO and citrullination have been reported to involve senescence – .
Citrullination, the post-translational modification of proteins by the conversion of arginine residues to citrulline, has implications in various cellular processes, including TGF-β, WNT, MEK1-ERK1/2-IGF2BP1-SOX2, JAK2/STAT3 and EGF signalling pathways – . It has the effects on proteins, including potentially altering the function of the protein and disrupting protein-protein interactions . Citrullination of intracellular or extracellular proteins affects a variety of signaling pathways including TGF-β signaling, which may affect the hCECs. Citrullination is mediated by calcium-dependent peptidyl-arginine deiminases (PADs) , of which PAD2 is one of the most studied isoforms . Citrullination by PAD2 is implicated in several physiological and pathological processes, including degeneration of retina and brain , , aging-related processes and age-related diseases , , . PAD2-mediated citrullination has been implicated in neurodegenerative processes and inhibiting PAD2 with compounds like AMF30a could modulate disease progression by affecting protein citrullination pathways . PAD2 regulates AKT/NF-κB pathway , and oxygen consumption rate . Reactive oxygen species (ROS) is associated with PAD2 activity . Oxidative stress is involved in the pathogenesis of corneal endothelial diseases , , and anti-oxidative treatments have shown to enhance the CECs survival – . Recently, cell therapy for corneal endothelial diseases has been studied and introduced into clinical practice, where a critical challenge is to ensure proper adhesion of CECs to the stroma . PAD2-mediated citrullination can alter the properties of proteins involved in cell adhesion, potentially impacting cell-cell interactions and cell adhesion to the extracellular matrix . In addition, PAD2-mediated citrullination of histones affects chromatin structure and gene transcription, resulting in the regulation of gene expression . PAD2 has been shown to citrullinate and enhance the nuclear factor-kappa-light-chain enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) , which is activated by TGF-β. Although citrullination is not a direct component of the canonical TGF-β signaling pathway, it may indirectly affect TGF-β signaling through its effects on key regulatory proteins. However, the role of PAD2 in TGF-β signaling has not been described in the previous studies although it has the potential to affect the corneal endothelium. Thus, in this study, we investigated the role of AMF30a, a PAD2 inhibitor, on TGF-β-induced senescence of hCECs.

## Methods

## Cell culture
This study was performed in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board/ethics committee of the Hallym University Medical Center. Cells were cultured according to previously published methods . The research corneas purchased from Eversight or corneas remaining and subsequently discarded after corneal transplantation were used. Because it was practically impossible to obtain consent from research subjects or human material donors in the case of human material research during the research process, the institutional review board/ethics committee of the Hallym University Kangnam Sacred Heart Hospital waived informed consent. Corneas from three donors were utilized. hCEC-Descemet’s membrane complex underwent a 10-minute incubation in a 0.25% trypsin/0.02% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) solution. Subsequently, the cells were seeded in 6-well plates pre-coated with a fibronectin–collagen combination (FNC) coating mix (Athena Environmental Sciences, Inc., Baltimore, MD, USA), achieving confluence after 10–14 days. Cell were cultured in Opti-MEM-I (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA) supplemented with 8% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Lonza, Walkersville, MD, USA), 5 ng/mL human recombinant EGF (Invitrogen), 20 ng/mL human recombinant nerve growth factor (Invitrogen), 100 µg/mL bovine pituitary extract (Invitrogen), 0.5 mM L-ascorbic acid 2-phosphate (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), 200 mg/L calcium chloride (Sigma-Aldrich), 0.08% chondroitin sulfate (Sigma-Aldrich), and 50 µg/mL gentamicin (Invitrogen). Passaging occurred at a 1:3 ratio using 0.25% trypsin/0.02% EDTA solution. Treatment with a AMF30a (5µM, PAD2 inhibitor, Cayman, Ann Arbor, Michigan) , or TGF-β (10ng/mL; ab50036, Abcam, Cambridge, MA) was applied for 48 h. AMF30a and TGF-β concentrations were determined from previous studies – .

## Cell viability, proliferation and cell cycle analysis
Cell Counting kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Kumamoto) was employed to evaluate cell viability. Cells, seeded at a density of 1 × 104 per well in a 96-well plate, were exposed to various drugs or cytokines for a duration of 24 h. The wells were treated with CCK-8 solution for 2 to 4 hours. Cell viability was quantified using a microplate spectrophotometer (Synergy HTX), with absorbance readings taken at 450 nm. Data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation, calculated as a percentage relative to the control condition, defined as 100%. The experiments were repeated three times to ensure reproducibility, and a single representative experiment is illustrated in the figure.
The rate of cell proliferation was assessed using the bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) incorporation assay (Roche Diagnostics, GmbH, Mannheim). Cells (5 × 103 cells/well) were plated in 96-well plates and incubated with BrdU at 37 °C in 5% CO2. For cell cycle analysis, Cytoflex flow cytometry equipped with propidium iodide (PI) staining (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA) was employed. The experimental protocol involved trypsinizing the cells, subsequent washing with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fixation with cold 70% ethanol, and storage at −20 °C. The staining process was conducted with cells incubated in a solution containing 50 µg/mL PI and 100 µg/mL RNase A.

## Cell cytotoxicity and cell morphology
Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) cytotoxicity assay was conducted using a commercial kit (MK401; Takara Bio, Otsu, Japan). Cells (1 × 104 cells/well) were cultured in 96-well plates for 24 h. The supernatant was moved to new 96-well plate and the reaction substrates were added to each well. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. A microplate reader (Synergy HTX) was used to assess optical density at 490 nm. Cytotoxicity was presented as the fold-change in the controls after subtraction of the corresponding blank.
The cell size and cell shape were evaluated. Cell boundaries were outlined with AxioVision, Rel. 4.7 software (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena) for quantification of cell morphological changes.

## Phalloidin
The actin staining marker phalloidin was used to assess cell adhesion. F-actin was stained with Alexa Fluor 488 H phalloidin (Molecular Probes) and Hoechst 33,342 nuclear stain, and cells were examined using a microscope (DMi8; Leica Microsystems).

## Intracellular oxidative stress levels evaluation
DCF-DA was used to assess the level of intracellular oxidative stress. The cells were treated with 10 µM 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescin diacetate reagent (D-6883, Sigma-Aldrich) for 10 min at 37 °C in the dark. A Cytoflex flow cytometer (Beckman-Coulter, Brea, CA) was employed to assess the fluorescence intensity.

## Cell adhesion assay using crystal Violet assay
Cultured cells were treated with 5 µM AMF30a for 30 min. Cells were trypsinized, resuspended in the appropriate culture medium and standardized to a same cell number of 2 × 105/mL. Each well of a 96-well plate was filled with the same number of cells (50 µL). Cellular attachment to the bottom of the plates was allowed for 2–6 h, and non-adherent cells were washed off with PBS. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min at 25 °C, followed by staining with 0.1% crystal violet solution for 1 h. The culture plates were thoroughly washed with distilled water, and the crystal violet stain was dissolved with 10% acetic acid. A multimode spectrophotometer was used to determine the optical density at 570 nm.

## Wound healing assay
Cells are cultured at confluence in 12-well culture plates. A wound was scratched with scratcher (SPL) and observed for the migration area. The wound areas were measured using Axiovert 4.7 (Zeiss) after 0 and 24 h of incubation.

## Western bot
Radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Biosesang, Seoul) including protease (Sigma-Aldrich) and phosphatase (PhosSTOP; Roche, Basel) inhibitor cocktails was employed for the isolation of total cellular proteins. Western blotting was carried out in accordance with standard protocols. Nonspecific binding was blocked for 1 h using 5% skim milk or gelatin. Primary antibodies were as follows: rabbit anti-human CD166 antibody (ab109215, Abcam, 1∶500 dilution), rabbit anti-human ZO-1 antibody (sc-10804, Santa Cruz, 1∶500 dilution), mouse anti-human ERK1/2 antibody (sc-514032, Santa Cruz, 1∶500 dilution); mouse anti-human pERK1/2 antibody (sc-136521, Santa Cruz, 1∶500 dilution); mouse anti-human YAP antibody (sc-376830, 1∶500 dilution); rabbit anti-pYAP antibody (PA5-17481, Invitrogen, 1∶500 dilution); rabbit anti-human PAD2 antibody (12110-1-AP, Proteintech, 1∶500 dilution); mouse anti-ATF4 antibody (sc-390063, 1∶500 dilution); mouse anti-human ROCK1 antibody (sc-17794, 1∶500 dilution); mouse anti-ROCK2 antibody (sc-398519, 1∶500 dilution); or rabbit anti-GAPDH antibody (LF-PA0212, Abfrontier, 1∶5000 dilution). Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody and a WEST-Queen™ Western blot detection kit (iNtRON Biotechnology, Seongnam, Korea) was employed for the detection of the immunoreactive bands. Video image analysis (Luminograph II, Atto, Tokyo, Japan) was used to quantify the data. The protein bands were measured by means of densitometry.

## Immunofluorescence staining
hCECs were grown in 12-well plates, and fixed for 20 min in 4% paraformaldehyde solution. The cells were permeabilized for 10 min with 0.5% Triton X-100 in PBS and blocked for 1 h with 5% skim milk in PBS at 25 °C. The cells were treated overnight with either mouse anti-E-cadherin antibody (sc-8426; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Santa Cruz, CA, USA) or mouse anti-YAP antibody (sc-376830; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), rabbit anti-PAD2 antibody (sc-293271; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), mouse anti-peptidyl-citrulline antibody (MABN328, Merck Millipore, Feltham, UK), and rabbit anti-NF-κB antibody (ab7970, Abcam, 1:100) at 4 °C and then rinsed with PBS. The cells were treated with either fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG antibody or rabbit anti-goat antibody (1:100) for 2 h at 37 °C in the dark, and followed by counterstain with Hoechst 33,342 nuclear stain (1:2000; Molecular Probes, Eugene, OR, USA). Samples were mounted with a drop of mounting medium to reduce photobleaching after extensive washing with PBS. Negative control staining was performed in parallel without the primary antibodies. The plates were examined using a fluorescent microscope (DMi8; Leica). Nuclear translocation in cells was examined by immunocytochemical method, as previously described , . Results were expressed as percentage (mean ± SD) of nuclear NF-kB or YAP positive cells in total cells counted from four randomly chosen fields in each well under 200 magnifications. Each experiment was performed in triplicate.

## Senescence-associated β-galactosidase assay
SA-β-gal staining kit (Biovision) was used for Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining. The cells were rinsed once with PBS after the growth medium had been removed from the cells. Cells were fixed for 10–15 min at 25 °C by adding fixative to each well. Cells were incubated in the β-galactosidase staining solution overnight at 37 °C in a dry incubator after washing with PBS.

## Transcriptome analysis and analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and functional analyses of DEGs
RNA sequencing was performed at MacroGen Inc. (www.macrogen.com) using Illumina HiSequation 2000 platform . The EdgeR package was used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between groups . Genes with log2(fold change (FC)) ≥ 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were identified as significant DEGs. StringTie version 1.3.4d and DESeq2 software were used to calculate transcript abundances and confirm DEGs between treated and control groups , . Fragments per kilobase of transcripts per million mapped reads (FPKM) were used to calculate transcript abundance. FDR control was performed by adjusting p-value calculated by Benjamini–Hochberg algorithm.
Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed for classification of DEGs based on specific biological functions using a TOPPGENE (https://toppgene.cchmc.org/), Enrichr (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/) and Gonet (https://tools.dice-database.org/GOnet/). GO enrichment analysis covers three areas: Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC). Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analysis (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html, accessed on 16 September 2022) was employed for the identification of metabolic pathways or signal transduction pathways that were significantly enriched in differentially expressed genes . GO terms and pathways with an adjusted p-value < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

## Statistics
Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Unpaired Student’s t-test for two group comparisons and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used for statistical analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism 9.3.1 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. All experiments were replicated at least three times.

## Results

## AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, promotes in vitro proliferation of human corneal endothelial cells by suppressing oxidative stress
To identify the hCECs, immunofluorescence staining of ZO-1 and CD166 (ALCAM) was used. Cells were positively stained with ZO-1 and CD166, which are the markers for hCECs (Fig.  A). In this study, AMF30a, a PAD2 inhibitor, was investigated to understand its impact on cellular morphology and function, an integral aspect of cell health. The evaluation focused on two morphological parameters: cell size and the elongation factor. The results revealed that AMF30a significantly altered cell morphology, reducing both cell size and the elongation factor (Fig.  B and C). Further investigations into the effect of AMF30a on cell proliferation and cytotoxicity were conducted using the CCK-8 assay, BrdU proliferation assay, and LDH cytotoxicity assay. These assays demonstrated that AMF30a increased cell viability and proliferation while simultaneously reducing LDH toxicity (1.002 ± 0.047 vs. 0.925 ± 0.018; Fig.  D and F). Cell cycle analysis, conducted to ascertain the distribution of cells across different phases based on DNA content, indicated that AMF30a facilitated cell cycle progression (Fig.  G and I). To evaluate the effect of AMF30a on wound healing, scratch assay was performed. The wound healing of cultured hCEnC at 24 h after scratch is shown in Fig.  J, and the cells treated with AMF30a showed faster migration and wound healing (Fig.  K). The evaluation of intracellular oxidative stress levels was conducted by measuring DCF-DA fluorescence intensity through flow cytometry, revealing a reduction in oxidative stress following treatment with AMF30a (Fig.  L and N).

 

## AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, promotes the adhesion of human corneal endothelial cells through HIPPO signaling pathway
Crystal violet assay was conducted to quantitatively evaluate the effect of AMF30a on adhesion properties of hCECs. The findings indicated that AMF30a significantly enhanced the adhesion of hCECs (Fig.  A and C). Immunofluorescence staining was used to assess the expression of cytoskeleton components, specifically E-cadherin and F-actin. It was observed that E-cadherin expression was increased by AMF30a (Fig.  D), whereas the intensity and organization of F-actin, a crucial cytoskeletal protein, were diminished (Fig.  E and F). Furthermore, the impact of AMF30a on signaling pathways was investigated through western blot analysis, revealing a reduction in the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 (Fig.  G and H). The phosphorylation and localization of YAP, a key factor in the HIPPO signaling pathway, were also examined, demonstrating that AMF30a elevated phosphorylated YAP (pYAP) levels while preventing its nuclear translocation (Fig.  I and L). To analyze the mechanism by which these changes in hCECs occur, transcriptome analysis was performed.

 

## AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, on transcriptome of human corneal endothelial cells in vitro
Initially, a comprehensive transcriptome analysis employing NGS was undertaken to systematically identify and characterize the entirety of genes associated with AMF30a. This study identified DEGs and investigated the patterns of gene expression alterations induced by AMF30a (Fig.  A and B). This comprehensive transcriptome analysis provides valuable insights into the global shifts in gene expression and identifies specific genes that undergo upregulation or downregulation in response to AMF30a. The DEGs of AMF30a-treated cells are presented in Table  . Figure  A and B shows a smear diagram and volcano plot of DEG levels. Gene ontologies are presented in Fig.  C and G; Table  . G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling pathway, eye photoreceptor cell differentiation, and protein targeting ER are involved. The gene network was described in Fig.  H. Therefore, based on this transcriptome analysis, in vitro experiments were conducted with hCECs.

 

 

 

## Effect of cytokine on PAD2 expression of human corneal endothelial cells in vitro
Therefore, an in vitro experiment was carried out with hCECs to determine the cytokines that affect PAD2 and ER stress. The study investigated the impact of cytokines TGF-β and IFN-γ on the expression and localization of PAD2 in hCECs, utilizing western blotting for quantitative analysis and immunofluorescence staining for spatial visualization. Immunofluorescence staining provided insights into the localization and expression levels of PAD2 under cytokine influence (Fig.  A). The administration of TGF-β and IFN-γ was found to significantly augment the levels of PAD2 and ATF4 (Fig.  B and E) and to increase peptidyl-citrulline formation (Fig.  F), evidencing a direct influence of these cytokines on PAD2 expression and associated citrullination processes in hCECs.

 

## AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, resists TGF-β-induced changes of human corneal endothelial cells in vitro
To evaluate the effect of TGF-β on protein citrullination within cells, immunofluorescence staining for peptidyl-citrulline was performed, demonstrating that TGF-β increased citrullination, an effect that was reversed by AMF30a treatment (Fig.  A). Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay, revealing a reduction caused by TGF-β, which was inhibited by the treatment with AMF30a (Fig.  B). The extent of cellular senescence was evaluated through cell size analysis via inverted microscopy and SA-β-gal staining, both of which were increased by TGF-β and attenuated by AMF30a (Fig.  C and F). Intracellular oxidative stress levels, measured by DCF-DA probe, were increased by TGF-β and attenuated by AMF30a (Fig.  G and I). Cell cycle analysis, to differentiate cells in the cell cycle phases, showed the G0/G1 arrest by TGF-β, which was attenuated by AMF30a (Fig.  J and L).

 

## AMF30a, PAD2 inhibitor, affects TGF-β-mediated ROCK/HIPPO signaling pathway of corneal endothelial cells
The study utilized Western blotting to investigate the impact of TGF-β on protein expression within several signaling pathways, with a specific focus on the modulation of these effects by AMF30a. Notably, ERK1/2 activation, induced by TGF-β, was mitigated by AMF30a treatment (Fig.  A and B). The treatment with AMF30a also reversed the TGF-β-driven decrease in YAP phosphorylation, a key aspect of the HIPPO signaling pathway (Fig.  C and D), and countered the increased nuclear translocation of YAP (Fig.  E and F) and the upsurge in ROCK2 levels (Fig.  G and I) prompted by TGF-β. Furthermore, AMF30a attenuated the TGF-β-induced nuclear translocation of NF-ҡB (Fig.  J and K) and the elevation of ATF4 levels, associated with ER stress (Fig.  L and M).

 

## Discussion
In this study, we found that inhibiting PAD2 in hCECs led to a decrease in cell size and elongation factor, coupled with an increase in proliferation, by attenuating oxidative stress. hCECs typically exhibit limited proliferation in vivo , with a tendency to arrest in the G1 phase . Following injury, loss, or damage to the corneal endothelium, hCECs undergo a cell enlargement to compensate for the cell deficit , , which is similar to cellular senescence, which involves an irreversible cessation of cell proliferation while maintaining metabolic activity , . Although hCECs increase in size rather than proliferate in response to injury or stress, they do not necessarily undergo the full senescence programme. Instead, they may exhibit some senescence-like characteristics as part of their response to tissue damage. This study shows that AMF30a facilitates cell proliferation by promoting cell cycle progression. Citrullination, a post-translational modification mediated by PAD2, alters the functionality and architecture of critical proteins, including nuclear histones, the erythroblast transformation-specific (ETS)-like protein-1 (ELK-1) transcription factor, p53, and the inhibitor of growth protein 4 (ING4), which induce cell cycle arrest and inhibit cell growth – . Furthermore, the proliferative dynamics of CECs are intricately linked to the balance of intracellular oxidative stress . Excessive ROS can disrupt mitochondrial function, potentially reducing the metabolic capacity needed for S phase entry and progression . Elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) induce oxidative damage to DNA, proteins, and lipids, whereas moderated ROS levels are essential for the regulation of gene expression, cell proliferation, differentiation, and the management of stress responses . Our results demonstrate that AMF30a alleviates intracellular oxidative stress. PAD2-induced citrullination is linked to oxidative stress through affecting the structure and function of proteins that respond to oxidative stress . In this study, we found that AMF30a increased the adhesion of hCECs via modulation of the HIPPO signaling pathway. Cell adhesion, the process by which cells attach either to other cells or to the extracellular matrix (ECM), plays a central role in several physiological functions such as tissue development, cell proliferation, invasion, and wound healing , . We evaluated cytoskeleton, E-cadherin and F-actin. AMF30a increased E-cadherin and decreased F-actin. E-cadherin is a key mediator of adherens junctions, playing a pivotal role in maintaining intercellular cohesion and epithelial phenotype . In contrast, F-actin is a major component of the cytoskeleton involved in maintaining cell shape, generating contractile force, and enabling migration . The observation that E-cadherin expression increases while F-actin decreases following treatment suggests a shift in the balance between adhesion and motility or stress fiber formation . This differential regulation may reflect a transition from a more quiescent, adherent cellular phenotype conducive to corneal endothelial repair . E-cadherin is found in the membrane and the cytoplasm . Membranous E-cadherin acts as a cell-to-cell adhesion molecule and is primarily found at adherens junctions in the plasma membrane . Cytoplasmic E-cadherin is also functionally significant, being involved in signalling pathways . The internalization of E-cadherin contributes to cell motility, proliferation and resistance to apoptosis . YAP/TAZ, the members of HIPPO signaling pathway, translocate to the nucleus and interact with transcription factors to regulate the expression of genes associated with cell adhesion molecules, ECM components, and cytoskeletal dynamics – , influencing processes fundamental to cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation , . HIPPO signaling is involved in the control of essential molecules such as fibronectin through the FAK-Src-PI3K pathway , KIRREL1 , and E-cadherin for cell adhesion .
In this study, we explored the impact of AMF30a on the transcriptomic landscape of hCECs to elucidate the mechanisms involved. Our analysis revealed that AMF30a significantly affects GPCR signaling pathway, peptidase inhibitor activity, endopeptidase regulator activity, protein targeting ER and protein localization to ER. GPCRs, a large and diverse group of cell surface receptors, play a crucial role in converting extracellular cues into intracellular signals, facilitating a variety of physiological responses to cytokines , . Peptidase inhibitors and proteases are essential for the maintenance of biological processes such as protein turnover, signal transduction, and the immune response – . The quality control system in ER, a critical site for protein synthesis, post-translational modifications, and folding, depends on the activity of several proteases – . PAD2 may affect the physiological function and changes in hCECs by influencing these pathways.
This study provides evidence that pro-inflammatory cytokines TGF-β and IFN-γ upregulate PAD2 expression and activity in human corneal endothelial cells (hCECs), accompanied by increased ER stress signaling and peptidyl-citrulline formation. These findings suggest a link between cytokines and PAD2-mediated protein citrullination in the corneal endothelium. The upregulation of ATF4 alongside PAD2 indicates that cytokine-induced ER stress may play a role in modulating PAD2 expression. Given the crucial role of PAD2 in catalyzing citrullination, a post-translational modification implicated in protein misfolding, immune activation, and cellular stress , its induction by TGF-β and IFN-γ may represent a pathophysiological pathway relevant to corneal endothelial diseases. This study suggests the potential of targeting PAD2 as a therapeutic strategy to mitigate cytokine-induced damage and preserve corneal endothelial cell integrity.
To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of AMF30a, a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis using NGS was performed. Functional enrichment analysis of the DEGs revealed significant involvement of biological pathways such as GPCR signaling, protein targeting to the endoplasmic reticulum, and photoreceptor cell differentiation . These findings suggest that AMF30a may modulate not only stress-response pathways but also mechanisms related to cellular identity and protein trafficking. GPCRs transactivate the TGF-β receptors type-1 (TGFBR1) leading to pSmad2C and TGFBR1 transactivates GPCR, which relies on cytoskeletal rearrangement such as F-actin , and regulates HIPPO/ERK1/2 signaling pathway , . The gene network analysis further supports the hypothesis that AMF30a exerts its effects through a coordinated regulation of interconnected signaling pathways .
In this study, we investigated the effect of AMF30a on oxidative stress, ER stress, and senescence in hCECs using TGF-β to elucidate these processes. Although several cytokines including TNF-α , syndecan-2 , bFGF , VEGF , and 17β-estradiol have been reported to regulate PAD2, the regulatory effect of TGF-β, a cytokine involved in the modulation of oxidative stress, senescence , , , and ER stress , on PAD2 has remained unexplored. In normal hCECs, PAD2 is located in nucleus and citrullinates histones H3 and H4, suggesting transcriptional regulation . However, upon treatment with TGF-β, a marked translocation of PAD2 to the cytoplasm was observed, where it facilitates the citrullination of cytoplasmic proteins and subsequently escalates ER stress. Citrullination may modify the structures of proteins within the TGF-β signaling pathway and alter their interactions with other signaling proteins. Furthermore, the impact of citrullination extends to chromatin architecture and gene expression , which may indirectly modulate the expression of components within the TGF-β pathway and their associated genes. In addition, extracellular citrullination may alter the function of matrix associated TGF-β, thereby adding another layer of complexity to its regulatory mechanisms . This study showed that AMF30a reduced TGF-β-induced cellular senescence. Cellular senescence, characterized by an irreversible cessation of cell division and often associated with oxidative stress, is exacerbated by TGF-β. TGF-β activates ROS generation primarily through the activation of NADPH oxidase, an enzyme complex responsible for producing ROS such as superoxide, and concurrently suppresses the expression of key antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutase and catalase – . This process leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and triggers senescence, particularly through p53-p21 and p16INK4a , , . We also investigated related signaling pathways and found that AMF30a modulates the TGF-β-induced ROCK/HIPPO signaling pathway in hCECs. RhoGEF, a component of Rho pathway, is citrullinated by PAD2 , suggesting a direct involvement of PAD2 in the regulation of cellular dynamics. This modulation results in decreased E-cadherin and increased Rho GTPase expression. Inhibition of PAD2 reduces levels of RhoA, Rac1, and Cdc42 levels, leading to a shift towards an epithelial phenotype characterized by increased E-cadherin . In addition, our results suggest that the regulatory capacity of PAD2 extends to activating YAP within the HIPPO pathway and facilitating the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 . ERK1/2 is a downstream effector of various cytokine-activated pathways, including thee modulated by TGF-β , which was utilized in our study. Moreover, ERK1/2 signaling has been implicated in regulating cytoskeletal remodeling, cell adhesion, and cellular stress responses —key biological processes affected by PAD2 activity. PAD2 has been reported to citrullinate the NF-κB , which is widely recognized as an important regulator of the aging process, by contributing to the secretion of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype .
In summary, the process of citrullination, facilitated by PAD2, serves as an indirect regulator of this pathway by targeting and altering the function of essential proteins . This enzymatic alteration, specifically the citrullination of arginine residues, modifies the operational efficacy of proteins within the TGF-β signaling process . TGF-β is crucial for regulating the production of the ECM, and citrullination plays a role here as well, affecting ECM proteins such as collagen and fibronectin , . This could lead to changes in the microenvironment that are conducive to TGF-β signaling , . Additionally, the citrullination of TGF-β receptors or proteins within TGF-β receptor complexes has the potential to modify the activation and subsequent signaling of TGF-β, suggesting a significant interaction between citrullination and the TGF-β/ROCK/HIPPO signaling pathway. In conclusion, these results suggest that AMF30a, a PAD2 inhibitor, promoted the proliferation of hCECs and protected against TGF-β-induced cellular senescence and death, suggesting that PAD2 may play an important role in regulating hCEC survival and function. Furthermore, PAD2-induced citrullination is essential for TGF-β/ROCK/HIPPO pathway. These findings suggest that targeting PAD2 may have therapeutic potential for diseases associated with hCEC dysfunction or damage.
