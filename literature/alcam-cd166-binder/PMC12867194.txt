## Abstract

## Background:
Ovarian cancer is a rare cancer, it has the worst prognosis and the highest mortality rate, especially in high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC). High-throughput data generation is developed and provides an opportunity to investigate molecular pathways involved in cancer progression. The purpose of this study is to explore the role of main genes linked to the immune system and immune microenvironment in HGSOC using bioinformatics approaches to introduce promising biomarkers.

## Materials and Methods:
After a systematic search was performed in the Gene Expression Omnibus database, high-throughput sequencing, two RNAseq datasets were selected. The integrated dataset was used to obtain differentially expressed (DE) genes. The immune infiltrate abundances related to DE genes were determined. The pathway, gene-set, and hallmark analyses were performed for the extraction of the immune system-related genes. Then, genes that were included in an integrated network, with acceptable AUC for immunotherapy response, were selected, and survival, gene expression, and mutation analyses were done.

## Results:
The NCF1, MAP4K1, CIITA, CD3E, PTPN22, SAMHD1, LCP2, TSHR, DOCK2, FANCD2, GNAI1, GNG12, ZBP1, TXK, STAT4, and RASGRP2 genes offer as key genes linked the immune system. MAP4K1 and TXK genes have targeted compounds.

## Conclusions:
The current study recognized the most relevant genes involved in the immune system in high-grade serous ovarian cancer. The findings of this study provided a holistic understanding of the tumor microenvironment that can be used for introducing therapeutic targets.

Keywords: Bioinformatics, high-grade serous ovarian cancer, high-throughput sequencing, immune system

## INTRODUCTION
Ovarian cancer is the main cause of mortality from neoplasms in women. Although ovarian cancer is a rare cancer, it has the worst prognosis with the highest mortality rate.[ ] Ovarian cancer is a heterogeneous disease with different clinical and molecular characteristics.[ ] The most common type of ovarian cancer is high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC), which is the most lethal type and is responsible for most deaths due to the high rate of recurrence and resistance to chemotherapy.[ ] Thus, there is a critical need to discover and study to identify potential targeted therapy. The tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes revealed in many ovarian cancer tumors introduce it as an immunogenic disease[ ] that could be suitable for immune treatment.[ ] The majority of immunotherapy studies have focused on elucidating the role of programmed cell death-1 (PDL-1).[ ] Despite the promise of immunotherapy, two-thirds of ovarian tumors revealed a modest expression of PD-L1 with a low response rate to PD-L1 inhibition and limited to specific patients.[ , ] The understanding of the immune microenvironment in subtypes of ovarian cancers, such as HGSOC, has not been fully clarified. Indeed, the tumor microenvironment and its heterogeneity could be the reason for the cancer complexity. So, identifying immunotherapy candidate genes could be crucial for improving response rates and developing personalized treatments. Therefore, there is a critical need to understand the immune microenvironment in ovarian cancer to improve immune oncologic treatments. According to the availability of numerous big biological data, we could use these data to translate into clinically valuable knowledge. For this purpose, in this study using bioinformatics approaches, a comprehensive investigation was performed for the identification genes related to the immune system in HGSOC progression.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## RNA-sequencing data processing and DE genes determination
We used the gene expression database, Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO),[ ] based on the following search strategy: “high grade serous ovarian cancer” term, expression profiling by high-throughput sequencing, tissue samples, and Homo sapiens organism. We focused on the datasets with high enough samples, without treatment, and with acceptable quality that identified via the principal component analysis (PCA) approach using ggplot2 package and prcomp function of R software.[ ] With the NetworkAnalyst database,[ ] the differentially expressed (DE) genes were identified from the integrated raw RNAseq files (GSE211669 and GSE217672) with 133 selected tumor samples and seven control samples from the GSE217674 dataset. In this step, after normalization using the log2-counts per million (logCPM) transformation, the specific comparison of gene expression of tumor versus control samples was performed via the limma statistical method. The adjusted P value less than 0.05 and | log fold change (FC)| ≥1 were considered as thresholds for determining the DE genes.

## Immune infiltration analysis
Using the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) 2.0 database, a comprehensive resource for systematical analysis of immune infiltrates abundances across diverse cancer types, the immune features related to DE genes were assayed to assess the immune infiltration status in the tumor microenvironment.[ ] In an alternative approach, using the Immune Cell Abundance Identifier (ImmuCellAI) method,[ ] the abundance of immune cell types in the tumor group compared to control samples was estimated. The P value less than 0.05 was regarded as the threshold for determining significant results.

## Gene set, pathway, and hallmark enrichment analysis
For interpreting the function of gene expression data, the enrichment analysis was performed by GSEA 4.3.2 software.[ ] The h.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt in the MSigDB database and the Human_Gene_Symbol_with_Remapping_MSigDB.v2023.2.Hs.chip were selected as the reference gene set and chip platform, respectively. Gene sets with the false discovery rate (FDR) less than 0.05 were considered for the determination of the significance results. The pathway analysis was performed based on KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) and Reactome pathway databases.[ , ] The minimum 50% involved genes and the Pvalue 0.05 corrected with the Bonferroni method were considered cutoff. In the alternative approach, cancer hallmark enrichment analysis (cancerhallmarks.com/) was performed, and results of tumor-promoting inflammation and evading immune destruction were selected.

## Gene interaction network and immunotherapy response analyses
The interactions of DE genes were retrieved based on the STRING database[ ] using the CluePedia program in Cytoscape.[ ] The connections are based on activation, binding, catalysis, expression, inhibition, post-translational modification, and reaction with a confidence cutoff of 0.9. The immune system-related genes obtained from gene-set, pathway, and hallmark enrichment analyses were extracted from the interconnected network. In the following analysis, the ROC plots, a validation tool for predictive biomarkers, were created to link the gene expression and immunotherapy response using transcriptome data from samples of tumor tissue in several types of cancers.[ ]

## Survival analysis
For the clinical significance assay, the survival analysis of immune system-related genes was investigated with the Kaplan–Meier (KM) plotter online database (kmplot.com) to estimate the effect of gene expression on survival in ovarian cancer. This correlation analysis utilizes the data from samples of the integrated cohort data of ovarian cancer.[ ]

## Gene expression levels and somatic mutation data extraction
The expression levels of the genes were approved by TNMplot, a web tool, which extracts expression data in tumor, normal, and metastatic tissues of cancers including breast, bladder, colon, lung, liver, esophageal, prostate, pancreas, renal, and ovarian cancer. Data extract information was from GEO, GTEx, TCGA, and TARGET databases.[ ] The Mann–Whitney U test was used to detect the significant differences between cancer and normal tissues. The significant results are identified and distinguished with the red asterisk signs. Also, the information about the mutation and genetic alterations of the genes was determined. Data created from the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC)[ ] and OncoPrint in cBioPortal databases were used for mutation data extraction.[ ] The workflow of this study is depicted in the flowchart [ ].
 

## RESULTS

## Determination of DE genes
The integrated datasets of high-throughput sequencing, GSE211669 and GSE217672, with 133 transcriptomic profiles of HGSOC samples, were selected. The histology has been confirmed as grade II or III high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma. The seven healthy samples as controls were used from the GSE217674 dataset. The PCA result shows that the samples of each group are separated correctly and have acceptable quality [ ]. Using the limma statistical method, 6965 genes were determined as significantly differentially expressed genes.
 

## The tumor-infiltrating immune cells analysis
The computational algorithms were used for correlation analysis of the DE genes and the tumor-infiltrating immune cells. The results indicate the remarkable infiltration of Cancer-associated fibroblast and also Monocyte, Macrophage, B cell, Endothelial cell, Neutrophil, T cell CD8+, Myeloid dendritic cells, NK cell, Macrophage M2, and T cell CD4+ memory resting. Also, the ImmuCellAI method predicts the significant difference of infiltration of some T-cell subsets (CD8+ T cell, NKT, Th1, Th17, Tfh, Tr1, iTreg, TC, Tex, and MAIT cells) and other important immune cells such as macrophages, neutrophil, B cell, and natural killer (NK) cells in the tumor in comparison to control samples. The results are shown in .
 

## Gene set, pathway, and hallmark enrichment analyses
For mining of the genes related immune system, gene set, pathway, and hallmark enrichment analyses were performed. The results from 22 GSEA significance results, Interferon Gamma response, Interferon Alpha response, TNFA signaling via NFKB, TGF Beta signaling, and allograft rejection are correlated with the immune system [ ]. Pathway analysis using KEGG and Reactome databases revealed 90 results of enriched pathways that are mainly related to the immune system, DNA repair, and extracellular matrix organization supergroups. The results are presented in . Besides PD-1 signaling, the other main enriched pathways related to the immune system are primary immunodeficiency, GPVI-mediated activation cascade, Interleukin-10 signaling, TNFs bind their physiological receptors, RUNX1 and FOXP3 control the development of regulatory T lymphocytes (Tregs), and Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation [ ]. Also, besides the sustained angiogenesis, genome instability, sustaining proliferative signaling, replicative immortality, resisting cell death, evading growth suppressors, reprogramming energy metabolism, and tissue invasion and metastasis hallmarks, DE genes remarkably enriched with tumor-promoting inflammation and evading immune destruction. The related DE genes are shown in .
 

## Network and immunotherapy response biomarker analyses
Toward the extraction of the genes linked in the interconnected network, interaction network analysis was done. A complex network with 1926 nodes and 49,103 edges was constructed, and from 664 immune system-related genes, 462 genes are in the interconnected network. To validate the predictive biomarkers, the ROC Plotter for immunotherapy response analysis was performed, and results show that from the 462 selected genes, 28 genes have acceptable AUC, that have 0.6 < AUC < 0.7. These results are from data generated from patients with any immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy during pretreatment and on-treatment. NCF1 (0.645), MAP4K1 (0.642), CD247 (0.641), CIITA (0.629), CD3G (0.628), CD3E (0.627), ITK (0.626), PTPN22 (0.622), SAMHD1 (0.621), CD3D (0.619), LCP2 (0.617), PRKCQ (0.616), TSHR (0.616), CD8A (0.615), DOCK2 (0.614), FANCD2 (0.614), IL10RA (0.614), GNAI1 (0.61), CCL4 (0.607), GNG12 (0.607), ZBP1 (0.607), TXK (0.606), PARP1 (0.606), CD96 (0.604), STAT4 (0.604), RASGRP2 (0.604), BLNK (0.603), and TRAT1 (0.602) are the most promising gene candidates that could be clinically valuable in predicting therapy response.

## Survival analysis
The Kaplan–Meier curve analysis demonstrates the cumulative survival probabilities and indicates the prognosis. NCF1 (P value: 0.0066, HR: 1.2), MAP4K1 (P value: 0.0015, HR: 1.29), CIITA (P value: 0.0452, HR: 0.88), CD3E (P value: 0.0142, HR: 0.84), PTPN22 (P value: 0.0352, HR: 1.24), SAMHD1 (P value: 1.5e-5, HR: 1.32), LCP2 (P value: 0.0005, HR: 1.25), TSHR (P value: 0.0286, HR: 1.17), DOCK2 (P value: 1.0e-5, HR: 1.34), FANCD2 (P value: 0.0012, HR: 1.36), GNAI1 (P value: 0.0004, HR: 1.29), GNG12 (P value: 0.0004, HR: 1.25), ZBP1 (P value: 3.0e-5, HR: 1.5), TXK (P value: 0.001, HR: 0.8), STAT4 (P value: 0.0196, HR: 0.85), and RASGRP2 (P value: 0.0229, HR: 1.16) have significant correlation with survival in serous ovarian cancer. The hazard ratio (HR) results indicate that the differential expression of genes affects the probability of survival. HR ≠ 1 indicates that the high/low expression of genes impacts survival. For the significance result, the P value < 0.05 is considered.

The log fold change (FC) is the log ratio of transcript expression values in comparison to two different conditions. The logFC genes are NCF1 (4.043), MAP4K1 (3.0851), CIITA (2.9925), CD3E (4.9544), PTPN22 (1.7608), SAMHD1 (1.5754), LCP2 (7.6892), TSHR (2.8966), DOCK2 (5.2919), FANCD2 (1.4921), GNAI1 (-1.2971), GNG12 (-1.3172), ZBP1 (2.395), TXK (-2.4118), STAT4 (1.3857), and RASGRP2 (1.3457). For determination, the role of the genes in cancer progression, the expression levels of the genes, and genetic alterations were identified. Several types of cancer, including adrenal, bladder, breast, colon, esophageal, liver, lung, ovary, pancreas, prostate, rectum, renal, skin, stomach, testis, thyroid, and uterus, were investigated in the TNMplot database. The results showed significant differences in the expression of NCF1 especially in lung, stomach, and testis; MAP4K1 in tumor testis; CIITA in lung, stomach, esophageal, ovary, pancreas, and renal tumors; CD3E in lung, pancreas, ovary, renal, stomach, testis, and uterus tumors; PTPN22 in lung, stomach, and pancreas; SAMHD1 in most of the tumors; LCP2 in lung, renal, skin, stomach, and testis tumors; TSHR in thyroid tumor; DOCK2 in lung and renal tumors, FANCD2 in rectum and testis tumors; GNAI in most tumors; GNG12 in most types of tumors; ZBP1 in breast, ovary, esophageal, pancreas, rectum, renal, stomach, and testis tumors; TXK in lung, ovary, and uterus; STAT4 in testis; and RASGRP2 in breast, colon, bladder, lung, and uterus. ## DISCUSSION
Ovarian cancer is the main cause of mortality from gynecological malignancies in women.[ ] The tumor infiltration of immune cells in ovarian cancer makes a potential immune treatment for patients. PD-L1/PD-1 inhibition is a disappointing treatment in ovarian cancer[ ]; thus, it is reasonable to explore novel immune-related targets.
For this purpose, in this study, we investigated DE genes from raw RNAseq datasets of HGSOC with 133 samples. The immune cell infiltration analysis results show the prominent infiltration of cancer-associated fibroblasts based on DE genes. Cancer-associated fibroblasts, using the releasing factors, control tumor occurrence, development, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance.[ ] In ovarian cancer, cancer-associated fibroblasts significantly increase chemoresistance, which can be implicated as targeted therapy.[ ] Also, the infiltration of various T-cell subsets (CD8+ T cell, Th1, Tfh, Tr1, iTreg, nTreg, TC, Tex, and MAIT cells), dendritic cells, macrophage, and NK cells was predicted. The alterations of T cell subsets have been studied in various cancer types, including ovarian cancer.[ ] The dendritic cell-based vaccine was developed and tested in epithelial ovarian carcinoma patients, and data in randomized clinical trials show the clinical benefits and improved overall survival.[ ] The studies have shown the high level of tumor-associated macrophages correlates with poor overall survival in HGSOC through enhancing the invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and chemoresistance. Thus, it is a significant approach for further research.[ ] There is promising subclinical evidence for the role of NK cells in ovarian cancer and their potential therapeutic effect.[ ]
For mining, the immune system-related genes, gene-set, pathway, and hallmark analyses were performed. To identify the possible new genes that are capable of serving as biomarkers of response immunotherapy, the network analysis was performed. The immune system-related genes in the integrated network were selected, and immunotherapy response analysis was done to determine the biomarkers of immunotherapy response. 28 genes have acceptable AUC, 0.6 < AUC < 0.7. Then, the Kaplan–Meier curve analysis demonstrates the differential expression of NCF1, MAP4K1, CIITA, CD3E, PTPN22, SAMHD1, LCP2, TSHR, DOCK2, FANCD2, GNAI1, GNG12, ZBP1, TXK, STAT4, and RASGRP2 genes in tumor tissues, which have the significant association with survival in serous ovarian cancer. The differential expression and mutation of these genes has been reported in several types of cancer.
NCF1 (neutrophil cytosolic factor 1) is a subunit of the NADPH oxidase (NOX) complex, and its expression was significantly associated with the infiltration of numerous immune cells and related to autoimmune diseases. Its mutations are commonly correlated to the chronic granulomatous disease (CGD).[ ] Also, it contributes to the growth and metastasis of some tumors by affecting the reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and cancer-related inflammation.[ ] The function of MAP4K1 (Mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase kinase 1), an MAPK that is highly expressed in the human glioblastoma specimens, is related to cell proliferation and growth of glioma with modification of cytokine-chemokine networks.[ ] The MAP4K1 is also reported as a tumor promoter through MAPK and DNA damage or repair pathways. MAPK pathway using the JUN and JNK factors plays an important role in drug resistance in acute myeloid leukemia patients.[ , ] Also, MAP4K1, as an immune regulator, modulates the chemokine signaling pathway that affects the inhibition of a range of immune cells, including T cells.[ ] CIITA (Class II Major Histocompatibility Complex Transactivator), an NLR (nucleotide-binding domain, leucine-rich-repeat containing) protein, is a key transcription coactivator of MHC class II genes that regulates the MHC class II antigen presentation pathway and acts as a viral inhibitor. Its role in cancer, especially in blood cancers including chronic myeloid leukemia and some cancers related to viral infections such as Epstein–Barr virus-associated gastric adenocarcinomas, was investigated, which shows its key role in the regulation and alterations of the immune response and evasion.[ ] CIITA regulation defines the MHC-II expression level, that for inflammatory diseases and cancer known as a therapeutic target.[ ] CD3E (CD3 epsilon subunit) of the T-cell receptor complex was introduced as a potential prognostic factor in low-grade glioma patients by affecting the microenvironment of the tumor; the overexpression of CD3E indicated less likely survival of patients.[ ] PTPN22 (protein tyrosine phosphatase nonreceptor type 22) is a protein tyrosine phosphatase that is involved in multiple pathways, such as the IFNα receptor and T-cell receptor. It is a suppressor of T-cell signaling. Studies have shown that PTPN22 deficiency improves the cancer immunotherapies in multiple types of cancers, such as non-melanoma skin cancers. Thus, the inhibition of PTPN22 could be a therapeutic procedure.[ , ] SAMHD1 (sterile alpha motif and histidine/aspartic acid domain-containing protein 1) is balancing the intracellular deoxynucleotide (dNTP) pool known as a tumor suppressor and also a prognostic marker in hematological cancers, including chronic lymphocytic leukemia, and some solid cancers, such as ovarian cancer. SAMHD1 functions are related to the regulation of DNA damage repair and activation of the innate immune response. In ovarian cancer, its inhibition is correlated with innate immune cell signaling activation and improves the prognosis.[ , ] Lymphocyte cytosolic protein 2 (LCP2) is involved in the activation variety of signal transductions, including T-cell receptor activation. Differential expression of LCP2 affects the production of proinflammatory cytokines, leading to immune dysregulation in the tumor microenvironment, and influences the survival of patients.[ ] The role of LCP2 has been reported in lung adenocarcinoma, gastric cancer, and breast cancer patients.[ ] Thyrotropin receptor (TSHR) signaling as an oncogenic pathway is mainly expressed in thyrocytes. TSHR is a cell surface glycoprotein receptor that controls growth, differentiation, and thyroid hormone synthesis. It is also expressed in other tissues, such as the liver, lung, breast, and ovary. Thus, its dysregulation could be involved in their malignancies.[ , ] The function of DOCK2 (Dedicator of cytokinesis protein 2) is to activate the small GTPase Rac, which is critical in immune cells. It is involved in the development of inflammatory diseases, including allergic diseases, infection, and graft rejection, and is also associated with vascular diseases and various tumors, including colorectal, gastric, and prostate cancers that may be a predictive factor for some tumors, but its mechanism is not clear.[ , ] FANCD2, Fanconi anemia group D2, is involved in DNA repair pathways. A few studies have explored the role of FANCD2 in some tumors. In a recent study, the role of FANCD2 was investigated in the proliferation, migration, and invasion of lung adenocarcinomas.[ ] Also, the overexpression of FANCD2 is known as a poor prognostic factor in ovarian cancer. FANCD2 expression regulates the immune microenvironment of tumors.[ ] GNAI1 (G Protein Subunit Alpha I1) contributes to the G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) and non-GPCR signaling pathways in immune cells. The involvement of GNAI1 in the development and progression of colon tumors was evaluated and validated in the knockout mouse models study by affecting the IL6 Signaling pathway.[ ] GNG12 (G Protein Subunit Gamma 12) participates in the activation of the NF-kB signaling pathway and impact on transcription of the PD-L1 gene. The studies showed that GNG12, by activation of the NF-kB pathway, modulates the cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and inflammatory pathways in tumors.[ ] ZBP1 (Z-DNA Binding Protein 1), that involved in autoinflammatory disease and with roles in cancer,[ ] is a key regulator of tumor necroptosis, inflammatory cell death, during tumor development. The glucose deprivation initiates ZBP1-dependent necroptosis that helps to control metastasis.[ ] The TXK (tyrosine-protein kinase) functions, an immune-associated protein, may be done through the NF-κB signaling pathway. The association of TXK with breast cancer has been reported, but its mechanism is not clear.[ ] The STAT4, signal transducer and activator of transcription 4, is stimulated by interleukin (IL) 12 or IL-23. It is a transcription factor that is involved in tumor progression in various types of cancers, such as epithelial ovarian cancer. It is associated with poor outcomes in patients and has a critical role in ovarian cancer metastasis.[ ] The RASGRP2 (RAS guanyl-releasing protein 2), with the known role in maintaining homeostasis of blood vessels and vascular disorders,[ ] was reported as an immune-related biomarker in lung adenocarcinoma. It has a key role in the regulation of mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis that could affect cancer progression.[ ] In general, the roles of most of these genes in HGSOC and their clinical correlation are uncertain or insufficient and thus require more investigation. Furthermore, also based on data reported from the ICGC database, MAP4K1 and TXK are targeted with FDA-approved protein kinase inhibitors. These inhibitors are used for the treatment of the subtypes of patients as follows: Bosulif for chronic myelogenous leukemia; Caprelsa for treating medullary thyroid cancer; Nilotinib for the treatment of chronic myeloid leukemia; Pazopanib for advanced renal cell cancer and advanced soft tissue sarcoma; Sorafenib for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma, renal carcinoma, and thyroid carcinoma; Sprycel for the treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukemia or chronic myeloid leukemia; Sunitinib for the treatment of renal cell carcinoma and imatinib-resistant gastrointestinal stromal tumor; Xalkori for the treatment of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer; Afatinib for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC); Erlotinib for the treatment of small cell lung cancers or advanced metastatic pancreatic cancers; and Iressa for treatment of non-small cell lung carcinoma (go.drugbank.com/).
In conclusion, a holistic assay of the immune microenvironment in HGSOC was performed with the immune characteristics, gene set enrichment, interaction gene network, immunotherapy response, survival, expression, and mutation analyses. These data revealed several potential genes that may be involved in the progression of HGSOC with an impact on the immune microenvironment. The results of these studies help prioritize genes to discover new clinically useful biomarkers and therapeutic targets. In general, the most important limitation of RNA expression analysis is no consideration of changes in modifications of the post-translational process that may be involved.[ ] Also, the validation of results is important, so we consider the confirmation of the findings with reliable methods, based on combining RNA and protein patterns in future studies.
