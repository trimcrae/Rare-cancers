Nevertheless, the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) profile of LUAD-LM patients remains poorly characterized and reliable CSF diagnostic biomarkers for LUAD-LM have yet to be established. Using single-cell RNA sequencing data of CSF cells from six LUAD-LM patients, we drew a systematic transcriptomic atlas of the CSF cellular landscape. Our analysis revealed that LUAD-LM reprograms CSF into an immunosuppressive state, marked by the emergence of pro-tumoral LGMN-SELENOPhigh macrophages and proliferating CSF circulating tumor cells (CSF-CTC). Cell-cell communication analysis showed that CSF-CTC reinforces immunosuppression by co-inhibitory checkpoint axis NECTIN2_TIGIT axis with the CD8+T/NK cells, and via CD47_SIRPA axis with antigen-presenting cells. It showed strong diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing LUAD LM from Non-LM or NC, and PBM did not influence the CSF TROP2 level. Collectively, our findings advance the understanding of LUAD-LM pathogenesis and highlight the potential of CSF TROP2 as a diagnostic biomarker for LM.

## Introduction
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is one of the most common primary tumors that give rise to brain metastases (BM). BM can be classified into parenchymal brain metastases (PBM) and leptomeningeal metastases (LM). Circulating tumor cells (CTC) detected in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) serve as the gold standard for diagnosing LM and offer valuable insights for investigating its underlying mechanisms , .
Several single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) studies on non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) have primarily examined primary tumors, distant metastases , , brain metastases – , as well as metastatic lesions before and during targeted therapy . The tumor microenvironment (TME) contains a complex stroma—composed of infiltrating immune cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts, and endothelial cells—that has been widely linked to therapeutic sensitivity and resistance . Although our previous work characterized CTC from the CSF of LUAD-LM patients , a comprehensive understanding of the CSF TME in LUAD-LM remains lacking. Here, we performed scRNA-seq on CSF cells from LUAD-LM patients to characterize the cellular dynamics and molecular features of the CSF TME. First, we analyzed monocytes and macrophages—a highly diverse and prominent component of the TME—to delineate macrophage heterogeneity. Second, we focused on CSF-CTC to identify key subpopulations. Third, we examined cell-cell interaction networks to elucidate the complex interplay among different CSF cell types, which may influence prognosis and treatment efficacy. Fourth, we constructed gene regulatory networks (GRN) for CSF-CTC and tumor cells from parenchymal brain metastasis tissues (PBM-TC) of LUAD patients to identify critical regulators involved in tumorigenesis. Finally, we compared the gene expression profiles between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC.
The limited sensitivity of CSF cytology (45–67% initially, 80–89% with repeated sampling) underscores the need for supplementary biomarkers in the diagnosis of LM . Currently, CSF-based tumor biomarkers for LUAD-LM remain poorly defined. Our previous studies have identified CEACAM6 and HE4 in CSF as potential diagnostic markers for LM , . Here, we discovered that CSF-CTC from LUAD-LM patients exhibit high expression of Trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (TROP2). Overexpressed in many tumor types, TROP2 acts as a pivotal cell-surface receptor and tumor-promoting factor that regulates oncogenic pathways involved in growth, proliferation and metastases . Recent clinical studies suggest TROP2 may be a viable therapeutic target in LUAD and breast cancer (BC) . Serum TROP2 levels are moderately elevated in NSCLC relative to healthy and benign disease controls . However, to our knowledge, CSF TROP2 level have not been previously investigated. In this study, we systematically evaluate the diagnostic performance of CSF TROP2 in LUAD-LM and preliminarily explore its utility in BC LM. Our findings offer new insights into the pathogenesis of LUAD-LM and support the development of novel CSF-based diagnostic markers for clinical application.

## Results

## High-resolution cell-type landscape of CSF cells in LUAD-LM patients
We performed scRNA-seq on CSF cells from six LUAD-LM patients (samples LM-1 to LM-6). Following stringent quality control, a total of 49,051 high-quality cells were retained for downstream analysis. Using unbiased clustering, we identified 12 distinct cell populations—including CD4+ T cells (CD4), CD8+ T cells (CD8), regulatory T cells (Treg), natural killer (NK) cells, monocytes (Mono), macrophages (Mac), type 1 and type 2 myeloid dendritic cells (mDC1 and mDC2), type 1 and type 2 B cells (B1 and B2), CSF-circulating tumor cells (CSF-CTC), and proliferating cells (Proli)—based on the expression of canonical marker genes and highly variable features (Fig. , Table ). Key markers used for annotation were: general T cells (CD2, CD3D/E/G, TRAC), CD4 (IL7R, CD4), CD8 (CD8A/B), Treg (FOXP3, CTLA4), NK (GNLY, NKG7, KLRB1, KLRC1, KLRD1, XCL1/2), myeloid-lineage cells (LYZ, CD14, FCGR3A), Mono (S100A8/A9, VCAN, FCN1), Mac (APOC2, LYVE1, APOE, APOC1, SPP1, FCGBP, C1QA/B/C), mDC1 (XCR1, DNASE1L3), mDC2 (CD1E, CD1C, FCER1A), B1 (XBP1, MZB1, CD38), B2 (CD79A/B, MS4A1), CSF-CTC (EPCAM, KRT8/18, NAPSA, SFTPB, SFTA2, CEACAM6, WFDC2), and Proli (MKI67, CDK1, TOP2A, ATAD2) (Fig. ). 1592 proliferating cells (Proli) consisted of proliferating (P_) Mac (P_Mac), P_Mono, P_CD8, P_CD4, P_NK, P_Treg, P_DC, and P_B (Figure A– , Table ). Figure illustrates the proportion of each cell type within every sample (left panel) and, conversely, how each sample contributes to the overall abundance of every cell type (right panel). 

## Distinctive monocytes and macrophages compositions in CSF of LUAD-LM patients
Monocytes (Mono) and macrophages (Mac) cells were the prominent component in TME, 25,423 Mono/Mac cells were re-clustered into six subclusters based on signature genes, which were defined as VCAN-FCN1high Mono (VCAN-FCN1 Mono, 4857 cells, 19.10%), EMP3-S100A8high macrophages (EMP3-S100A8 Mac, 4018 cells, 15.80%), CCL3-CCL4high Mac (CCL3-CCL4 Mac, 3430 cells, 13.49%), LGMN-SELENOPhigh Mac (LGMN-SELENOP Mac, 1867 cells, 7.34%), and VEGFA-AREGhigh Mac (VEGFA-AREG Mac, 10307 cells, 40.54%), and Proliferating subcluster (Proli_M, 944 cells, 3.71%) (Fig. ). Proli_M were combined of proliferating Mono (P_Mono, 360 cells, 1.42%) and proliferating Mac (P_Mac, 584 cells. 2.30%). 
The expression of the subcluster markers were not exclusively unique (Fig. , Table ). VCAN-FCN1 Mono upregulated a number of genes typical to classical monocytes (VCAN, FCN1), and proinflammatory markers (S100A8, S100A9). EMP3-S100A8 Mac were characterized by high EMP3/S100A8 expression. CCL3-CCL4 Mac expressed high levels of cytokines including CCL4L2, CCL4, CCL3, CCL3L1, CCL2, IL1B, suggesting the proinflammatory and anti-tumor functions. VEGFA-AREG Mac exhibited an enrichment of genes related to angiogenesis VEGFA and OLR1, and highly upregulate epidermal growth factor AREG. LGMN-SELENOP Mac showed upregulation of M2-associated genes GPNMB, LGMN, SELENOP and SLC40A1 , as well as genes involved in lipid metabolism (FABP3, FABP5) and some lipid receptors (LGALS3 and TREM2) . P_Mono and P_Mac highly and uniquely expressed genes associated with proliferation (MKI67, TOP2A, CDK1).
To deeply dissect the roles of Mono/Mac subclusters in the development of LUAD-LM, we interrogated the function scores by ssGSEA based on the signature genes (Table ) of Antigen processing and presentation, Inflammation, Angiogenesis, Phagocytosis, Interleukin signaling pathway, Complement, M1-subtype and M2-subtype signatures (Fig. ) , . VEGFA-AREG Mac exhibited enrichment of genes related to Angiogenesis, CCL3-CCL4 Mac were closely associated with Inflammation, Phagocytosis, and M1-subtype signatures, which are important in immune response. LGMN-SELENOP Mac harbored significantly increased scores of Complement and M2-subtype signatures. Furthermore, LGMN-SELENOP Mac had higher expression of immune checkpoint molecules, including LGALS3, LGALS9, SIRPA, and TNFRSF14, than other subclusters (Fig. ).
CytoTRACE analysis indicated that Mono showed less differentiated than Mac (Fig. , Figure ). Within the Mac compartment, the P_Mac appeared less differentiated compared to other Mac clusters (Fig. ). Within the Mono compartment, the P_Mono appeared less differentiated compared to VCAN-FCN1 Mono (Figure ). Monocle2 was applied to reconstruct the developmental trajectory of these Mono/Mac populations (Fig. ). The pseudotime trajectory originated from a root state occupied by both P_Mac and P_Mono. From this root, the trajectory bifurcated into two major branches: branch 1 was predominantly composed of VCAN-FCN1 Mono, while branch 2 was primarily occupied by Mac clusters.

## One subpopulation of proliferating CSF-CTC
We performed subclustering analysis on 7222 CSF-CTC. The majority of CSF-CTC clustered primarily by patients of origin, reflecting inter-patient heterogeneity. We identified a distinct subcluster characterized by elevated expression of proliferation markers MKI67 and TOP2A, which was designated as proliferating CTC (Proli_CTC) (Fig. , Figure ). The Proli_CTC subcluster was relatively small in size, consisting of 631 cells (8.74% of the total CSF-CTC population, Fig. , Figure ). Differential expression analysis revealed that, compared to other CSF-CTC subclusters, the Proli_CTC cluster was highly enriched for genes (Fig. and Table ) involved in the KEGG Cell Cycle pathway (e.g., PTTG1, CCNB1, CCNB2, CDC20, MAD2L1) and in Gene Ontology (GO) terms related to cell cycle/division/mitosis (including ANLN, ASPM, AURKA, BIRC5, CDKN3, CENPA, CENPE, CENPF, CEP55, CKS2, DLGAP5, GTSE1, KNSTRN, NEK2, NUSAP1, PRC1, PTTG1, TPX2, MKI67, TOP2A). Consistent with this, pathway activity comparison using ClusterProfiler (v4.14.6) demonstrated significant enrichment of signatures associated with Cell cycle, Cell division and Cytokinesis in Proli_CTC (Fig. ). Interestingly, the Proli_CTC subcluster was enriched for nine genes previously implicated in the regulation of cancer stem cells (CSC), including ANLN , , ASPM , BIRC5 DEPDC1 , HMMR , NUF2 , NUSAP1 , PTTG1 and UBE2C , (Fig. and Table ). In contrast, classical lung cancer (LC) CSC markers such as ALDH1A1, ALDH1A2, ALDH1A3, CD44, PROM1, POU5F1, and ABCG2 were minimally expressed in Proli_CTC cluster (Figure ). CytoTRACE analysis further indicated that Proli_CTC exhibit less differentiated compared to other CTC (Figure C and ). Notably, high expression of the nine CSC-associated genes was correlated with poor prognosis in LUAD (Fig. and Figure ). We now emphasize that while Proli_CTC express CSC-related genes, we cannot definitively conclude that they are CSC. Collectively, these findings suggest that Proli_CTC may possess stem-cell-like properties. 

## CTC-driven immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) via cellular crosstalk
In LUAD-LM patients, CD8/P_CD8 and NK/P_NK had high expression of cytotoxic factors, including GZMA, GNLY, PRF1, GZMB, GZMK, IFNG, NKG7, and CST7 (Figure ). For the interaction analysis, proliferating cells were first excluded, and CellPhoneDB was run separately for each individual sample. Analysis of the cell–cell interaction network identified by CellPhoneDB indicated that in LUAD-LM patients, CSF-CTC engaged in the most extensive interactions with other cell types and exhibited particularly strong communication with myeloid cells (Figure , Table ).
Focusing on the co-stimulatory and co-inhibitory checkpoints between CSF-CTC and tumor-infiltrating immune cells (Fig. , Figure ), our analysis revealed prominent co-inhibitory signaling axes—NECTIN2_TIGIT, NECTIN3_TIGIT, and PVR_TIGIT—between CSF-CTC and lymphoid cells (CD8, CD4, and NK). The inhibitory receptor TIGIT expressing on T/NK transmits suppressive signals by binding to its ligands, including NECTIN family members and PVR, on tumor cells or antigen-presenting cells (APC) to suppress T/NK function . Prognostic assessment further revealed that high expression of NECTIN2 and PVR was significantly associated with worse disease-free survival and overall survival in LUAD (Figure A, ). Additionally, the co-inhibitory axis CLEC2D_KLRB1 between CSF-CTC and NK may further facilitate immune escape . Another key mechanism of immune evasion involves the CD47_SIRPA axis between CSF-CTC and APC (Mac, Mono and mDC2). Overexpression of CD47 on tumor cells enables escape from APC-mediated phagocytosis and impairs antigen presentation . Furthermore, the LGALS9_HAVCR2 axis between CSF-CTC and Mac supports an immunosuppressive TME conducive to tumor escape . To support these findings, cell–cell interaction network analysis was further performed in two independent LUAD-LM samples (P‑A and P‑D; GEO dataset GSE150681). Consistent with our observations, CSF‑CTC in these samples similarly delivered co‑inhibitory signals—including NECTIN2_TIGIT to CD8/NK, CD47_SIRPA to APC (Mac, Mono and mDC2), and LGALS9_HAVCR2 to Mac (Fig. , Figure ). 
Among the co-stimulatory checkpoints between CSF-CTC and various immune cells in the six LUAD-LM patients, the most prominent pairs were PVR_CD226, NECTIN2_CD226, CD55_ADGRE5 and ALCAM_CD6 (Figure ). Notably, the same set of co-stimulatory signals—NECTIN2_CD226, CD55_ADGRE5 and ALCAM_CD6—were also observed in samples P‑A and P‑D, where they were similarly delivered to various immune cells (Figure ). In addition, CSF-CTC engage in other co-inhibitory and co-stimulatory signaling interactions with immune cells present in the CSF of 8 LUAD-LM patients (Fig. , Figure A, ).
In breast cancer leptomeningeal metastases (BC LM) patients (P‑B, P‑C, P‑E, P‑F; GEO datasets GSE150681 and GSE202501), prominent co‑inhibitory interactions were also identified between CSF‑CTC and immune cells, including NECTIN2_TIGIT (CSF‑CTC to CD8/NK), LGALS9_HAVCR2 (CSF‑CTC to Mac), and CD47_SIRPA (CSF‑CTC to APC) (Figure A, ). For co‑stimulatory signaling, CSF‑CTC engaged with immune cells via ALCAM_CD6, NECTIN2_CD226, and CD55_ADGRE5 pairs (Figure A, ).

## Treg-driven immunosuppressive escape through cellular crosstalk
Analysis of cell–cell communication revealed several common co‑inhibitory and co‑stimulatory interactions involving Treg (Fig. , Figure ). Regarding co‑inhibitory signaling, TIGIT on Treg engaged PVR, NECTIN2, or NECTIN3 on CSF‑CTC, as well as NECTIN2 on APC—a pathway known to enhance the immunosuppressive function of Treg . In addition, the CD86_CTLA4 and CD80_CTLA4 axes delivered inhibitory signals from APC to Treg. By binding to CD80/86 with higher affinity than CD28, CTLA4 on Treg can restrain conventional T‑cell activation , . Co‑stimulatory interactions between APC and Treg were also frequently observed, including CD80_CD28, CD86_CD28 , and ICOSLG_ICOS , which may contribute to Treg‑mediated immune evasion. Beyond these, several other co‑inhibitory and co‑stimulatory signals were identified as participating in the regulation of Treg‑driven immunosuppression (Figure ).

## Macrophage-driven immunosuppressive landscape through cellular crosstalk
In the CSF TME network of LUAD-LM patients, interactions between CSF-CTC and Mac were the most prominent. We identified a total of 55 ligand-receptor interactions across five of the six analyzed LUAD-LM patients (Fig. , Figure ). Interestingly, the expression of PPIA_BSG, DSC2_DSG2, and SEMA3C_NRP2 pairs was positively correlated, with high levels trending toward worse survival in LUAD (Figure A– ). These interactions are linked to critical pathways: PPIA_BSG is a promising anticancer therapeutic target , DSG2 drives LUAD progression and drug resistance , and NRP2/SEMA3C promote TGF-β1-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition . Relevant to the TME, the NRP2_SEMA3C axis supports macrophage-dependent spinal cord development and also supports M2 polarization in diabetic foot ulcer via Hedgehog signaling . In addition, prominent interactions included ligand-receptor pairs implicated in growth factor and metastases signaling—such as VEGFA_NRP1, VEGFA_NRP2, VEGFB_NRP1, HBEGF_EGFR, and HBEGF_ERBB2 , —as well as TNFR‑family signaling mediated by TNFSF10_TNFRSF10B and TNFSF12_TNFRSF12A . A subset of these signaling axes was also detected in the macrophage‑regulatory networks of samples P‑A and P‑D (Fig. , Figure ).
We next screened for ligand-receptor pairs mediating communication between Mac and other immune cells (Fig. , Figure ). Mac were found to express NECTIN2, which interacted with TIGIT on NK/CD8. Furthermore, investigation of the major chemokine signaling pathways revealed that the CCL5_CCR1 and CCL5_CCR5 axes served as the dominant ligand-receptor pairs between CD8 and Mac, whereas CCL2_CCR2 and CCL3_CCR1 were involved in Mac interactions with mDC2 and Mono. These chemokine pathways likely play important roles in the pathogenesis of LM. Notably, a subset of signaling axes was also detected in the macrophage-regulatory networks of the independent samples P‑A and P‑D (Fig. , Figure ).
The above findings indicate that the TIGIT_NECTIN2 axis is active in immunosuppressive networks regulated by CSF‑CTC, Treg and Mac, underscoring its pivotal role in TME of LM. Given the limited number of proliferating cells in our samples, we pooled all six samples for CellPhoneDB analysis to enhance statistical power and examine whether TIGIT_NECTIN2 axis is also widely present in interactions involving proliferating cells. The results revealed that TIGIT_NECTIN2 axis is ubiquitously present across multiple cell–cell interactions, including those coordinated by proliferating cells (Figure A, , Table ). Together, these observations highlight TIGIT_NECTIN2 axis (rather than PD1_PDL1/L2 axis) as a central immunosuppressive mechanism in LM, pointing to its potential as a promising therapeutic target.

## The gene regulatory networks (GRN) of CSF-CTC and PBM-TC in LUAD
To elucidate the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms in CSF-CTC from LUAD-LM patients, we performed single-cell regulatory network inference and clustering (SCENIC) analysis to construct GRN. The analysis was conducted using two independent patient cohorts. In the first set (patients LM-1 to LM-6), we identified 35 transcription factors (TF) shared among the CSF-CTC of six LUAD-LM patients (Fig. , Table ). Concurrently, network analysis using CytoHubba revealed 19 hub genes ranking within the top 5% of nodes across all six patients in this set. The intersection of 35 TF and 19 hub genes yielded 14 key TF: ATF3, ATF4, BCLAF1, CREB5, EHF, ELF2, ELK3, HCFC1, IRF1, JUN, POLR2A, TAF1, YY1 and ZNF274. In the second set (patients P1, P2, P4, P6, P7 and P-A), 26 TF shared across all six patients and 18 hub genes (top 5%) were identified (Fig. , Table ). The intersection of 26 TF and 18 hub genes resulted in 17 key TF: BCLAF1, BRCA1, EGR1, EHF, ELF1, ELF2, EP300, ETV3, ETV5, ETV6, FOSL2, HCFC1, NR3C1, POLR2A, SP1, YY1 and ZNF143. Finally, to identify TF consistently significant across both independent sets, we intersected 14 TF from the first set with 17 TF from the second set (Fig. , Table ) and pinpointed six TF: BCLAF1, EHF, ELF2, HCFC1, POLR2A and YY1. The six TF are likely to play pivotal roles in the GRN of CSF-CTC in LUAD-LM patients. 
To compare GRN between CSF-CTC and tumor cells from parenchymal brain metastases (PBM-TC), we analyzed 15,423 PBM-TC from 10 LUAD-PBM patient samples (NS02, NS03, NS04, NS06, NS07, NS12, NS13, NS16, NS17, NS19). PBM-TC from these patients shared 10 TF, 8 of which were defined as hub genes ranking within the top 2% of nodes (Fig. , Table ): ATF3, FOS, JUNB, POLR2A, YY1, EGR1, JUN, and ELF1. To validate this, we performed GRN analysis on two additional LUAD-PBM samples (P895 and P896) and confirmed 7 of 8 TF as hub genes ranking within the top 2% of nodes in the two samples (Fig. , Table ): ATF3, FOS, JUNB, POLR2A, YY1, EGR1, and JUN. 
Comparative analysis indicates that the GRN of CSF-CTC and PBM-TC exhibit both shared features and distinct specificities. The core TF POLR2A and YY1 consistently appear as hub genes in both cell types, implying their universal and critical regulatory functions in LUAD CNS metastases. However, the two cell groups also display unique regulatory characteristics: BCLAF1, EHF and HCFC1 were prominently identified as core regulators in CSF-CTC but were not highlighted in PBM-TC. Conversely, in stark contrast to their prominent role in PBM-TC, FOS and JUNB were strikingly absent as key regulators in nearly all CSF-CTC samples, whereas JUN, ATF3 appeared in the first set derived from CSF-CTC. This difference likely reflects the adaptive transcriptional reprogramming of tumor cells in response to two distinct microenvironment.

## Single-cell transcriptomic differences between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC of LUAD patients
The identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC at single-cell level could eliminate the interference of nonepithelial cells. Here, we enrolled four datasets, Dataset1 (DS1): CSF-CTC from LM-1, LM-2, LM-3, LM-4, LM-5, LM-6; DS2: CSF-CTC from samples P-A and P-D; DS3: PBM-TC from 10 PBM patients (NS02, NS03, NS04, NS06, NS07, NS12, NS13, NS16, NS17, NS19) labeled NS; DS4: PBM-TC cells from two PBM samples (P895, P896). We compared the different datasets (DS1 vs. DS3, DS1 vs. DS4, DS2 vs. DS3, DS2 vs. DS4) and discovered a total of 35 genes upregulated in CSF-CTC (adjusted value < 0.05, Fold change > 1.2, Fig. , Table ). The higher expression of CEACAM6 in CSF-CTC compared to PBM-TC supported its important roles in CSF-CTC. We have confirmed CEACAM6 could serve as a biomarker for the diagnosis of LUAD-LM , . Among the DEGs, JAK1 and EGFR are of significant biological relevance and represent well-established drug targets in LC. 
PBM-TC highly expresses 43 genes (adjusted value < 0.05, Fold change > 1.2, Fig. , Table ) compared to CSF-CTC. FOSB, FOS and JUNB were upregulated in PBM-TC. Dimerization between Fos (FOSB, FOS) and Jun proteins (JUNB) yields the activator protein-1 (AP-1) complex, which orchestrates a wide spectrum of biological functions implicated in cancers . Transformed mesenchymal cells secrete CXCL2 attracting M2-type macrophages to promote lung cancer metastases . In addition, LY6E, identified as a promising target for antibody-drug conjugates , were highly expressed in PBM-TC.
To understand the metabolic landscape of CSF-CTC and PBM-TC, we utilized scMetabolism for analysis. Surprisingly, compared to PBM-TC, CSF-CTC showed lower activity in many pathways, such as Arachidonic acid metabolism, Fructose and mannose metabolism, Thiamine metabolism, Pentose phosphate pathway, Glycolysis/Gluconeogenesis, Glutathione metabolism, which point to a globally heightened metabolic state in PBM-TC (Fig. , Table ).
To investigate the pathway enrichments of CSF-CTC and PBM-TC, we calculated the scores of gene sets (Table ) of Wound Healing, Regulation of Cell Adhesion, Extracellular matrix structural constituent (ECM), Angiogenesis, Collagen fibril organization, Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and Inflammatory response. The results showed CSF-CTC displayed the lower scores of EMT and ECM pathways than PBM-TC, indicating that the CSF-CTC had lower ability of metastases than PBM-TC (Fig. ). In addition, the percent of cells in G2/M phases showed no difference between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC (Figure ). We further analyze the differences in the expression of cancer-testis antigens (CTA) between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC. First, we focused on CTA undetectable in normal lung epithelial cells. Among these, CT70, CT66, MAGEC3 and CT69 were exclusively expressed in CSF-CTC, while MAGEA3, MAGEA12, CSAG1, CTCFL and PAGE1 were restricted to PBM-TC. Additionally, XAGE1B and PRAME were highly and broadly expressed in both CTC populations. We then examined CTA that show minimal or low-level expression in normal lung epithelial cells but exhibit markedly elevated and prevalent expression in tumor samples. In this category, BRDT and TMEFF2 were frequently detected in both CSF-CTC and PBM-TC samples. Moreover, CEP55, SPAG4 and ATAD2 were nearly universally expressed across almost all CSF-CTC and PBM-TC specimens (Figure ).

## Diagnostic value of CSF TROP2 for LM
The diagnosis of LM remains challenging, mainly due to the limited sensitivity of CSF cytology and ambiguous magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings. TROP2 is a member of the tumor-associated calcium signal transducer (TACSTD) family. The successful development of TROP2-targeted therapies has revolutionized treatment for patients with Trop2-positive cancers . In our study, CSF-CTC of LUAD-LM broadly expressed TROP2 (Figure A– ). To evaluate their diagnostic potential for LM, we measured the soluble levels of TROP2 in CSF. CSF TROP2 level was measured by ELISA. The testing cohort consisted of 115 LUAD-LM patients, 67 LUAD patients without LM (LUAD Non-LM) and 92 normal controls (NC). The validation cohort included 78 LUAD-LM, 46 LUAD Non-LM and 60 NC patients. Demographic and clinical characteristics of all groups are summarized in Table . In testing cohort, LUAD-LM patients showed markedly elevated CSF TROP2 concentrations (259.70 ± 36.78 pg/mL), representing a more than 10-fold increase compared with Non-LM (25.09 ± 0.94 pg/mL) and NC groups (23.82 ± 0.20 pg/mL, Fig. , Table ). ROC analysis confirmed the strong diagnostic performance of CSF TROP2, with an AUC of 0.952 for distinguishing LUAD-LM from Non-LM patients. At an optimal cutoff of 30.00 pg/mL, the assay achieved high sensitivity (0.878) and specificity (0.940). Similar discriminatory power was observed versus NC (AUC = 0.961), retaining 0.878 sensitivity and reaching 0.967 specificity at the same threshold (Fig. , Table ). In the validation cohort, CSF TROP2 levels remained significantly higher in LUAD-LM patients (220.40 ± 33.08 pg/mL) compared with both Non-LM (24.66 ± 0.72 pg/mL) and NC groups (23.55 ± 0.11 pg/mL) (Fig. , Table ). Using the established 30.00 pg/mL cutoff, TROP2 demonstrated robust diagnostic performance, with AUCs of 0.878 (LM vs. Non‑LM) and 0.910 (LM vs. NC), respectively (Fig. , Table ). 
Notably, the 113 patients in the Non-LM group comprised 30 LUAD patients without CNS metastases (LUAD group) and 83 LUAD patients with parenchymal brain metastases (LUAD-PBM group). CSF TROP2 levels did not differ significantly between the LUAD and LUAD‑PBM subgroups (Fig. ). At the 30.00 pg/mL cutoff, TROP2 effectively discriminated LUAD‑LM from both LUAD (AUC = 0.928) and LUAD‑PBM (AUC = 0.885) groups, indicating that PBM does not elevate CSF TROP2 level and confirming its diagnostic specificity for LM (Fig. , Table ). Interestingly, when LM patients were stratified by CSF tumor cell percentage (high burden: > 5% tumor cells; low burden: < 5% tumor cells), no significant difference in CSF TROP2 levels was observed between the two subgroups (Fig. ). This finding underscores the particular value of TROP2 in improving diagnostic sensitivity for clinically challenging low-burden LM cases.
Given that CSF‑CTC from BC‑LM patients also broadly expressed TROP2 (Figure C, ), we further assessed the diagnostic utility of TROP2 for BC LM. CSF samples from 42 BC Non-LM patients and 49 BC LM patients were analyzed. The BC LM group exhibited substantially elevated CSF TROP2 levels (535.40 ± 80.86 pg/mL) compared with BC Non-LM controls (25.99 ± 1.59 pg/mL) (Fig. , Table ). At a cutoff of 65.00 pg/mL, TROP2 showed strong diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.915, sensitivity 0.816, specificity 0.976) (Fig. ), supporting its potential as a biomarker for BC‑LM, although further validation in larger cohorts is warranted. 

## Discussion
In the present study, we depicted a large compendium of single-cell landscape in LUAD-LM. Macrophages significantly contributed to the immune heterogeneity in LUAD-LM patients. Herein, we clarified Mono/Mac cells into seven clusters, which were more complex than the conventional M1/M2 model. LGMN-SELENOPhigh Mac tended to be M2-subtype and CCL3-CCL4high Mac tended to be M1-subtype. Moreover, we revealed that only a small subset (631, 8.74%) of CTC exhibited high proliferative activity (Proli_CTC). The Proli_CTC upregulated cell cycle/division/mitosis-related genes, and enriched pathways involved in cell cycle, cell division and cytokinesis. CytoTRACE analysis further confirmed that Proli_CTC exhibit less differentiated compared to other CTC. Considering that the CSF is a nutrient-poor microenvironment generally unfavorable for cell growth, this prevalent quiescent state might represent a survival strategy for CSF-CTC. However, Proli_CTC appeared capable of overcoming these environmental constraints and maintaining proliferative potential, suggesting they possess unique biological properties. Notably, our single-cell analysis pinpointed nine genes (PTTG1, BIRC5, ASPM, HMMR, ANLN, NUSAP1, UBE2C, DEPDC1, NUF2) that were both remarkably highly expressed in Proli_CTC and have been individually linked to CSC properties and LUAD progression. For instance, UBE2C is identified as CSC marker in pancreatic adenosquamous carcinoma . A subset of UBE2Chigh tumor cells constantly increase during the LUAD invasive process . DEPDC1 has the ability to modulate CSC-like features in anaplastic thyroid carcinoma . It has been found that NUF2 is involved in CSC-related properties in BC . However, LUAD CSC markers ALDH1A1, ALDH1A2, ALDH1A3, PROM1, POU5F1, ABCG2, CD44 were rarely expressed in CSF-CTC. This pattern finds strong parallel in a prior single-cell study of renal cell carcinoma, which identify a novel CSC subpopulation defined not by classical markers (e.g., ALDH1A1, CD44, and PROM1), but by high expression of a specific gene set overlapping with ours (e.g., BIRC5, PTTG1, UBE2C, MKI67, TOP2A, TPX2, CENPF and CDKN3) . We propose two non-mutually exclusive explanations for this observation: (1) technical limitations in detecting lowly expressed classical markers via scRNA-seq, (2) the biological inapplicability of these classical markers to the unique CSF TME in LUAD-LM. Consequently, we posit that these nine genes are correlated with poor LUAD prognosis. However, we emphasize that while Proli_CTC expresses this CSC-related gene signature, this association does not definitively equate to a functional CSC identity. The origin and developmental trajectory of putative CSC within the CSF of LUAD-LM patients remain to be determined and warrant further investigation.
Targeting TIGIT-NECTIN2 as a potential therapeutic strategy have been identified for patients with BC liver and PBM metastases , and LUAD-PBM . In our study, although CD8⁺ T and NK cells in LUAD-LM retained a cytotoxic phenotype, they exhibited low expression of PD-1, and no prominent PD-1 checkpoint pathways (e.g., PDCD1-CD274, PDCD1-FAM3C, PDCD1-PDCD1LG2) were detected in the intercellular communication network. Instead, we identified the immune-checkpoint pair TIGIT-NECTIN2 between NK/CD8⁺ T cells and CSF-CTC or APC, which may contribute to the immunosuppressive CSF TME in both LUAD-LM and BC LM. The immune checkpoint TIGIT has gained considerable attention in recent years, exemplified by the approval of the anti-TIGIT antibody etigilimab for the treatment of NSCLC . Treg expressing TIGIT can suppress Th1 and Th17 immune responses , suggesting that targeting TIGIT on Treg represents a promising therapeutic approach . In LUAD-LM, we further identified TIGIT-PVR/NECTIN2 axes between Treg and CSF-CTC, as well as TIGIT-NECTIN2 axis between Treg and APC. Collectively, these findings highlight the strong therapeutic potential of anti-TIGIT therapy for LM.
Here, we focused on the difference between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC of LUAD patients. We observed that CSF-CTC showed lower activity in multiple metabolic pathways and a less pronounced EMT/ECM signature compared to PBM-TC. This divergence may be attributed to their distinct TME. The nutrient-poor, hypoxic CSF environment likely forces CSF-CTC to adopt an energy-saving strategy , downregulating energy-intensive processes like EMT and thereby reallocating limited resources to essential survival mechanisms. In contrast, the vascularized TME of PBM-TC is relatively nutrient-rich, which provides energy to sustain invasive phenotype, and PBM-TC actively reprogram surrounding stromal cells to further support metastatic progression . Thus, we propose that the differential EMT/ECM signature reflects a functional adaptation to energy constraints. It is important to emphasize that the less pronounced EMT/ECM signature in CSF-CTC represents a relative diminishment compared to PBM-TC, rather than an absence of metastatic potential. CSF-CTC in LM, a late-stage event in LUAD, nonetheless retains substantial inherent metastatic capability. The EMT/ECM signature difference deserves to be validated in future studies.
Tumor biomarkers demonstrate technical simplicity and cost-effectiveness, facilitating their widespread clinical adoption. There remains an urgent need for reliable CSF markers to improve LM diagnosis. In our study, CSF TROP2 level showed a 10-fold increase and exhibited significant diagnostic value for LUAD-LM. TROP2, functions as a calcium signal transducer and is frequently overexpressed across a range of epithelial cancers . Studies indicate that TROP2 is overexpressed in approximately 64% of LUAD, 75% of squamous cell carcinomas, 78% of triple-negative BC . TROP2 is a highly attractive therapeutic target due to its frequent overexpression in a wide range of solid tumors, contrasting with its limited presence in normal tissues (such as brain tissue ). Consequently, the successful development of TROP2-targeted therapies, particularly antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), has revolutionized treatment for patients with TROP2-positive cancers . The mechanistic role of TROP2 in CSF-CTC remains unknown. Based on its established functions in tumors, we hypothesize that TROP2 may play a pivotal role in CSF-CTC primarily through the following two aspects. 1)Promoting the proliferation, survival and metastases of CSF-CTC: TROP2 interacts with ligands such as IGF-1 and continuously activates key downstream signaling pathways, including PI3K-AKT, MAPK, and JAK2/STAT3 – . The activation of these pathways maybe crucial for sustaining the capacity for survival, proliferation, and metastases of CSF-CTC in the nutrient-poor CSF environment. 2) Mediating immune evasion by CSF-CTC: In NSCLC, high TROP2 expression has been shown to correlate with reduced intratumoral T-cell infiltration and to contribute to primary resistance to PD-L1 blockade . We speculate that in CSF-CTC, TROP2 may similarly aid in evading immune surveillance.
In Non-LM group, tumor cells were definitively absent in the CSF. The Non-LM group included patients with non-CNS metastases and those with PBM. Although blood-brain barrier (BBB) disruption was also present in PBM patients, only a few cases showed a mild elevation in TROP2 levels. Therefore, the disruption of the BBB has a limited impact on CSF TROP2 levels, and we propose that TROP2 is primarily derived from CSF-CTC. Furthermore, when treatment outcomes were classified according to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) efficacy evaluation system for LM , no significant difference in TROP2 levels was observed between patients with Stable Disease (SD) and those with Progressive Disease (PD) (Figure ). Additionally, TROP2 expression showed no statistically significant correlation with overall survival (Log-rank test, p > 0.05, Figure ). In our cohort, CSF TROP2 levels did not serve as a predictor of treatment response or prognosis in LUAD-LM.
Our cohort additionally comprised BC LM cases. TROP2 was significantly elevated in CSF of BC LM compared to BC Non-LM and showed discriminative capacity. However, the modest sample size restricted further stratification analyses. The predominant subtypes that develop LM are NSCLC, BC, and melanoma . Although this study has confirmed the potential of TROP2 as a diagnostic biomarker for LM in LUAD and BC, we were unable to establish complete analytical cohorts for LM-rare cancers such as lung squamous cell carcinoma, large cell lung cancer, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer and gastric cancer. Moreover, scRNA-seq data (GSE174401) and prior research collectively have demonstrated negligible TROP2 expression in melanoma, limiting its applicability in melanoma LM. The value of TROP2 as an LM diagnostic biomarker warrants further investigation through expanded cancer-type coverage and larger sample sizes. EPCAM (also known as TACSTD1), the other member of the TACSTD family and a classic epithelial marker, is highly expressed on CSF-CTC. We attempted to quantify CSF EPCAM levels using ELISA, yet EPCAM was detectable in only a small subset of LUAD-LM patients (Figure ). A plausible explanation for this observation is that EPCAM is not shed as efficiently into the CSF as TROP2. This pattern is consistent with observations in peripheral blood. Previous studies have reported that serum EPCAM levels in LC patients often do not significantly differ from those in healthy controls , whereas TROP2 shows a mild but consistent elevation . It is also plausible that the current method for detecting EPCAM might not be sensitive enough for its low abundance in CSF.
In summary, the study assayed the CSF TME compartment in LUAD-LM patients, revealing the phenotypic and functional heterogeneity of macrophages and CSF-CTC, deciphering the intercellular communication networks, and exploring the transcriptomic difference between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC. Crucially, the study identified CSF TROP2 as a diagnostic biomarker for LUAD-LM. These findings deepen our understanding of LM TME, offer new insights into disease progression, and provide a valuable biomarker for clinical diagnosis.

## Methods

## Single-Cell RNA Sequencing and Data Quality Control and Visualization
The six CSF samples from different LUAD-LM patients (LM-1, LM-2, LM-3, LM-4, LM-5, LM-6) were centrifuged and removed the supernatant, the pelted cells were suspended in red blood lysis buffer. Cell suspensions were mixed with 0.4% trypan blue dye, and the proportion of living cells should exceed 90% for quality control. Then, the single-cell RNA sequencing of CSF cells from the six samples was performed using the 10× Genomics Chromium single-cell platform. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Huashan Hospital, Fudan University (Approval number: KY2023-515), and was in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients.
Single-cell RNA-seq data were processed using Cell Ranger (v6.0.2) to align reads to the reference genome (GRCh38), quantify gene expression and generate a feature-barcode matrix. Quality control was performed to remove low-quality cells using the following thresholds: cells with fewer than 600 unique genes, fewer than 1000 total counts, or a mitochondrial gene ratio exceeding 20% were filtered out. Subsequently, 2000 highly variable features were selected for downstream analysis. Data integration and batch effect correction were carried out using the Harmony algorithm on the first 50 principal components (PCs). Clustering analysis was performed with the Seurat package (v4.4.0) on the harmony-corrected embeddings. Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the FindAllMarkers function in Seurat R package (v4.4.0).

## Study participants and sample collection
CSF samples were obtained from Huashan Hospital, Fudan University, between 2016 and 2024. Testing cohort included 115 LUAD-LM patients, 67 LUAD patients without LM (LUAD Non-LM), and 92 normal controls (NC). Validation cohort consisted of 78 LUAD-LM, 46 LUAD Non-LM, and 60 NC patients. The 113 patients in Non-LM group comprised 30 LUAD patients without central nervous system (CNS) metastases (LUAD group) and 83 with parenchymal brain metastases (LUAD-PBM group). In addition, CSF from 42 breast cancer (BC) Non-LM patients and 49 BC LM patients were enrolled in the study.
LUAD-LM and BC LM cases were confirmed by cytologic detection of tumor cells in CSF, the definitive diagnostic method for LM. LUAD and BC patients in Non-LM group were negative for CSF tumor cells on cytological assessment, and displayed no radiographic evidence of LM at sample collection or during the subsequent 6-month follow-up. The NC group CSF samples were obtained from patients undergoing evaluation for suspected neurological disorders (including neuroinfectious, neurodegenerative, and neuroinflammatory diseases), all of whom showed normal results across comprehensive CSF analyses, including general, chemical composition, cytology, pathogenic microorganisms examination.

## Data Collection and Processing
Single-cell RNA-seq data (Smart-seq2) of CSF-CTC from five LUAD-LM patients (P1, P2, P4, P6 and P7) were access on the NCBI BioProject database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/, PRJNA602172) . Single-cell RNA-seq data of CSF cells from LUAD-LM and BC LM patients were downloaded from the GEO dataset with the accession number GSE150681 (BC LM, P-B, P-C, P-E; LUAD-LM, P-A, P-D) and GSE202501 (BC LM, P-F) .
Single-cell RNA-seq data of tumor cells in PBM from 10 LUAD patients (NS02, NS03, NS04, NS06, NS07, NS12, NS13, NS16, NS17, NS19) are access on GSE131907 , and from two LUAD-LM patients (P895, P896) are available on GSE186344 .

## Single-Cell Analyses: Gene Regulatory Networks, Cell Interactions, and Trajectories
The GRN were constructed by R-package SCENIC (v1.3.1), hub gene was identified by CytoHubba in Cytoscape (v3.10.4), and Cell–Cell Interaction Analysis was performed by the python CellPhoneDB package (v5.0.1). Pseudotime analysis was performed using R package Monocle2 (v2.36.0). Cellular developmental potential was assessed using the CytoTRACE2 algorithm (v1.0.0).

## Pathway and gene signature score analysis
The score of the mentioned pathways or signature was calculated by gene set variation analysis (GSVA) or single-sample enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm using GSVA R package (v2.0.7). The scMetabolism R Package was carried out to quantify metabolic pathway gene sets . Additionally, KEGG and GO pathway enrichment were performed using the ClusterProfiler R package (v4.14.6).

## Measurement of CSF TROP2 Levels and Diagnostic Evaluation
After centrifugation (3000 × g, 5 min), the CSF cell pellet was discarded, and the cell-free supernatant was aliquoted and cryopreserved at −80 °C for subsequent analysis. The TROP2 level (Sino Biological, Cat: KIT10428) and EPCAM level (Sino Biological, Cat: SEK10694) were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The TROP2 KIT’s detection range was 23.44–1500 pg/mL, the EPCAM KIT’s detection was 15.625–1000 pg/mL. Values outside these limits were recorded as the corresponding detection threshold.
Inter-group differences in CSF TROP2 and EPCAM levels were evaluated with GraphPad Prism software (v8.0.2). Because the data were non-normally distributed, the Mann-Whitney U test was applied for comparisons between two independent groups. Diagnostic performance was assessed by receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis using GraphPad Prism (v8.0.2). The area under the curve (AUC) along with 95% confidence interval (CI) was calculated to quantify the discriminatory ability of each biomarker. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Statistical analyses
Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CSF-CTC and PBM-TC were identified using FindAllMarkers function in the Seurat R package (v4.4.0). Additionally, other statistical methods—including t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis tests in stats R package version 4.2.2 —were applied as appropriate for specific analytical requirements. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed using R with the survival (v3.8.3) and survminer (v0.5.1) packages. Survival curves were visualized using ggplot2 (v4.0.0).
