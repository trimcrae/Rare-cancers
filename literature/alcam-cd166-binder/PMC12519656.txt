## Abstract

## Objective
Using whole exome sequencing to detect pathogenic genes in families with inherited retinal dystrophy, and also to investigate the effects of CDH23 gene knockdown on the retinal structure of zebrafish.

## Methods
In this study, we collected data from a patient diagnosed with retinitis pigmentosa (RP) at Ningxia Eye Hospital in 2022. Comprehensive ophthalmic and systemic examinations were conducted, and peripheral venous blood samples were obtained from the proband and family members. Whole exome sequencing was used to screen for pathogenic gene mutations in the proband, and Sanger validation was performed on the detected likely pathogenic sites. The zebrafish CDH23 gene was knocked down using gene-editing technology. Wild type and CDH23-knockdown zebrafish at 5 days postfertilisation were selected for histological analysis and immunofluorescence staining. This allowed us to preliminarily analyse the effects of CDH23 variants on the structure of the zebrafish eye and retina.

## Results
The proband presented with a 20-year history of gradually progressive visual loss with nyctalopia in both eyes, with the best-corrected visual acuity of hand movements in the right eye (OD) and 0.1 in the left eye (OS). Funduscopic examination revealed pale optic discs, atrophic macular regions and large osteoclast-like pigment deposits on the retinal surface, and the retinal vasculature appeared thinner. Electroretinography suggested significant cone-rod dysfunction in both eyes and the pure tone audiometry suggested perfectly normal hearing. Whole exome sequencing revealed that the proband carried compound heterozygous variants in the CDH23 gene: c.2572G>A (p.Val858Ile) and c.8344G>A (p.Asp2782Asn), and Sanger sequencing confirmed that the phenotypically normal parents of the proband carried a heterozygous variant, respectively. Zebrafish knockdown experiments suggested that the gene variant could result in a significant thinning of the retinal photoreceptor layer, a substantial shortening of photoreceptor cell length and a sparse distribution of these cells.

## Conclusion
In this study, we reported the first case of non-syndromic RP resulting from a CDH23 variant, which was demonstrated to cause abnormalities in retinal microstructure by knocking down the CDH23 gene in a zebrafish model.

## WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC
It is currently known that mutations in the CDH23 gene are primarily associated with Usher syndrome and non-syndromic deafness.
However, the role of the CDH23 gene in retinal diseases remains unclear.
This prompted us to explore whether variations in the CDH23 gene might be associated with retinal diseases.

## WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS
This study first reports a case of non-syndromic retinitis pigmentosa (RP) caused by CDH23 gene variants.
Using CRISPR/Cas9 (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats/CRISPR-associated protein 9), we knocked down CDH23 in zebrafish and observed significant structural changes in photoreceptor cells of knockdown zebrafish via immunofluorescence staining.

## HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY
This study provides new insights into the pathogenesis of RP, revealing the potential role of the CDH23 gene in retinal diseases and offering important scientific evidence for the development of future therapeutic strategies targeting RP.

## Introduction
 Retinitis pigmentosa (RP) is a group of blinding fundus diseases characterised by progressive apoptosis of photoreceptor cells and the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), with a prevalence of about 1/2000. RP typically develops in both eyes, usually starting in childhood or early adolescence, worsening through puberty, and eventually leading to vision loss. Clinically, RP can be categorised into syndromic and non-syndromic types according to whether it involves other body organs. Usher syndrome, the most common syndromic form of RP, can be further subdivided into USH1, USH2, USH3 and atypical USH, depending on the clinical phenotypes. USH1 represents the most severe phenotype, which is characterised by congenital deafness, bilateral vestibular dysfunction and RP occurring before the age of 10 years. In 2001, the gene variant was first identified as causing USH1 in a Cuban family, followed by successive reports of similar cases in different countries and regions. 
The gene is located in the 10q21 region and comprises 69 exons. It encodes the transmembrane Ca2+-dependent adhesion protein cadherin 23 (CDH23), which is a Ca2+-mediated single-transmembrane cell-cell adhesion molecule. This protein consists of 3354 amino acids and belongs to the cadherin superfamily of cell surface adhesion proteins. During late embryonic or early postnatal development, the CDH23 protein is required for establishing and maintaining the proper organisation of the stereocilia bundles of hair cells in the cochlea and the vestibule. It is a putative calcium-dependent adhesion molecule required for proper morphogenesis of hair bundles of the inner ear neurosensory cells. Additionally, previous studies indicated that cadherin 23 is a component of tip links, which are thought to compose mechano-electrical transducer channels of the hair cells. Consequently, gene variants have been associated with non-syndromic deafness 12, in addition to USH1. To date, all patients with the variant reported in the literature have been comorbidly deaf. In this study, we reported the first case of the compound heterozygous variant in the CDH23 gene causing non-syndromic RP, and it was confirmed that the gene variant resulted in the structural destruction of retinal photoreceptor through gene knockdown and immunofluorescence tissue staining, thus affecting its function, revealing the possible mechanism by which gene variant contributes to the development of non-syndromic RP.

## Materials and methods

## Clinical data

## Subject
In this study, a patient clinically diagnosed with RP in Ningxia Eye Hospital was included as the research object. The proband’s current medical history, past medical history, personal history, family history and marital history were asked and recorded in detail, and the pedigree was drawn. Comprehensive ophthalmological examinations were conducted, including slit-lamp microscopy, indirect ophthalmoscopy, uncorrected visual acuity, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), wide-range colour fundus photography (Optos DaytonaP200T), colour fundus photography (Japan TOPCON, TRC-NW300), optical coherence tomography (OCT) (HD-OCT4000, Carl Zeiss Meditec, USA), optical ElectroPhysiological Study examination (Roland Consult Stasche, Finger GumbHD-14770, Germany), perimetry (Humphrey Field Analyzer750i, Germany), pure tone audiometry (TDH-39PSN, Denmark) and other systemic examinations. The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the patients themselves or their legal guardians, and all participants included in the study signed the relevant informed consent forms.

## Genomic DNA extraction
From the subject, 5 mL of peripheral venous blood was collected, and genomic DNA was extracted using the Qiamp Blood Mini Kit (Qiagen, Germany) following the standard protocol. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were detected by UV spectrophotometer and 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis, and then the DNA samples were stored in a refrigerator at −20°C.

## Whole exome sequencing
Genome-wide exome capture was performed using the Agilent SureSelect Kits, and sequencing was conducted using a high-throughput sequencer (Illumina) at 100 × depth. The raw sequencing data were processed by Illumina base-calling software V.1.7 and compared with the human genome DNA reference sequence (National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) build V.37.1) from the NCBI. Single nucleotide variants and insertion and deletion variants (Indel) were analysed using SOAP (Short Oligonucleotide Alignment Program) software and BWA (Burrows-Wheeler Aligner) software (http://bio-bwa.sourceforge.net), respectively, to identify the full range of variants occurring in the DNA sequences in the samples. High-frequency variant sites with minor allele frequency >1% in the database (db135) were filtered out. Variants with no effect on protein structure and function were also filtered out. Candidate pathogenic gene variants were obtained after stepwise screening of the number of variants shared by all patients in the family, followed by screening of the variants present in the family with no diseased relatives. The candidate pathogenic gene variants were validated using Sanger sequencing to exclude false positives, and further validated by co-segregation of genotypes and phenotypes among normal family members ( ).

## Variant pathogenicity classification
The rules for interpreting sequence variant data referred to the genetic variant classification criteria and guidelines issued by the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG). Subject to the principle of cumulative effect of sequence variation in pathogenicity evidence, each type of evidence was analysed individually. Ultimately, each criterion was combined according to the scoring rules in the Guidelines to classify the variant within a five-level system. In other words, the cumulative effect of pathogenicity evidence was used to determine whether a sequence variant was pathogenic, likely pathogenic, likely benign or benign. If a variant did not meet the above criteria or if the pathogenicity evidence contradicted the benign evidence, it was judged to be of uncertain significance. The online analysis tool Multalin (http://multalin.toulouse.inra.fr/multalin/) was used for the conservation analysis of variant sites.

## Experiments on retinal function in zebrafish

## Breeding of zebrafish
Adult zebrafish strains were reared in weakly alkaline recirculating water at a standard temperature of 28°C, with a photoperiod of 14 hours of light and 10 hours of darkness, and fed two to three times per day, and bred under such conditions. All embryos were collected and kept at a water temperature of 28.5°C, and their developmental stages were assessed within a few hours postfertilisation. Wild type AB zebrafish was used as the research object in this study. Juvenile fish were reared in an E3 medium. All zebrafish experiments were approved by the Animal Use and Care Committee of the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui autonomous region and were conducted subject to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the Zebrafish Information Network.

## Antibodies
Clone zpr1 (antizebrafish monoclonal antibody, CABT-B324) and Clone zn5 (antizebrafish monoclonal antibody, CABT-B310) were purchased from Creative Diagnostics (https://www.creative-diagnostics.com/); Z0-1 Monoclonal Antibody (Z01-1A12) was purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific c.htm (https://www.thermofisher.cn/cn/zh/home/brands/thermo-scientific); Cas9 protein was purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (https://www.thermofisher.cn/cn/zh/home.html).

## Generation of CDH23 CRISPants
In order to further investigate the role of the gene in zebrafish eyeball development, CRISPants technology was applied to knockdown the gene in zebrafish, aiming to downregulate the expression level of the gene in zebrafish, and to observe the specific effects of the reduced expression of the gene on the structure and function of the zebrafish eyeball. This would then reveal the role of the gene in visual phylogeny. CRISPants technology was generated based on the CRISPR/Cas9 experimental methodology, which has successfully knocked down the gene of zebrafish. The optimal target site, located in the second exon of the CDH23 gene, was selected based on the amino acid sequence of zebrafish CDH23. Primer V.3.0 software was then used to design a pair of sgRNA (single-guide RNA) primers (forward primer sgRNA-F and reverse primer sgRNA-R) ( ). A promoter sequence and a guard base were added to the 5' end of sgRNA-F to facilitate in vitro synthesis of sgRNA. The in vitro synthesis of sgRNA was performed using the T7 Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). During the synthesis, two sgRNA variants were prepared: sgRNA1 and sgRNA2. The synthesised sgRNA was purified and recovered by an RNA Purification Kit (Qiagen), ensuring the purity and stability of sgRNA. Cas9 protein was used to form the CRISPR/Cas9 complex by binding to sgRNA. A mixture of sgRNA1 and sgRNA2 (1 µg/µl each) and 1 µg/µL Cas9 protein (Novoprotein, E365-01A) was injected at a volume of 1 nL into each zebrafish embryo at the one-cell stage. The injected embryos were incubated at 37°C. When the embryos developed to 36 hours, control embryos and injected embryos were randomly selected for efficiency testing. Genomic DNA was extracted from the zebrafish with confirmed mutations, and the genotype was determined. The mutant zebrafish were then crossed with wild type zebrafish to obtain the F1 generation of mutants. The F1 mutants were further genotyped to select the mutant strain that caused a protein-coding frameshift. Ultimately, a stable genetically knocked-down mutant of the zebrafish gene was established.

## Histological staining analysis
Five-day-old fertilised zebrafish embryos were selected and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 4°C for more than 24 hours, after which the excess fixative was rinsed off with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The tissues were then successively dehydrated, clarified and embedded in paraffin. From the paraffin-embedded tissue, 5 µm thick sections were cut. The sections were deparaffinised in xylene, rehydrated in anhydrous ethanol and gradient concentrations of ethanol, and then stained with H&E following the instructions provided with the H&E staining kit (Phygene, PH0516). After staining, the sections were dehydrated and sealed again. Pathological analysis of the target tissue was performed using a stereomicroscope.

## Immunofluorescence histochemical analysis
The above-mentioned paraffin sections were deparaffinised in PBS, and antigen retrieval was performed to improve the detection rate. The sections were blocked with 10% goat serum in PBS for 30 min to block non-specific binding sites (as indicated by the blocking reagent’s instructions). The sections were incubated with the primary antibody and blocking reagent at 4°C overnight and washed with PBS the next day. The sections were then incubated at room temperature with goat antirabbit IgG (H+L) highly cross-adsorbed secondary antibody Alexa Fluor 488 (Thermo Fisher, A-11034, 1: 200) and goat antimouse IgG (H+L) highly cross-adsorbed secondary antibody Alexa Fluor Plus 555 (Thermo Fisher, A32727, 1:200) together in the dark for 2 hours, and unbound secondary antibodies were subsequently washed away with PBS. The tissue sections were stained with DAPI (4′,6-Diamidino-2-Phenylindole) aqueous dye (Abcam, ab104139) and imaged using confocal microscopy.

## Results

## Clinical evaluation
The proband, a 32-year-old woman, presented with reduced vision in both eyes with night blindness for 20 years, and a worsening condition for 7 years. She had no family history of genetic diseases and no history of consanguineous marriage. The BCVA of hand movements in the right eye was (−1.25 DS/−1.50 DC × 10°), and in the left eye was 0.1-(−1.50 DS/−1.25 DC × 155°). There were no abnormalities found during the anterior segment examination of both eyes. Fundus examination showed that the optic disc was pale, the macular region was atrophic, there was large-scale osteoblast-like pigment deposition on the surface of the retina, and the retinal vessels were thinning ( ). Chromoscopy results were normal. There was no light reflex signals at the local ellipsoidal zone and interdigitation zone in the fovea centralis, along with neurodegeneration of the RPE ( ). Perimetry suggested severe damage to the binocular visual field ( ). Electroretinography suggested significant cone-rod dysfunction in both eyes (severe reduction in b-wave amplitude for the scotopic rod response in both eyes and a severe reduction in the flash response amplitude of 30 Hz stimuli in both eyes) ( ). Pure tone audiometry suggested that the patient’s hearing was completely normal ( ). Due to the patient’s extremely poor visual acuity, vestibular function tests could not be performed. However, on reviewing the patient’s medical history, it was noted that the patient’s walking development in early childhood was completely normal (delayed walking development is considered indirect evidence of vestibular dysfunction in young children), and in adulthood, there was no motion sickness, seasickness, etc, which suggested that the vestibular function of the proband was normal. The proband’s parents and daughter had normal phenotypes, and the proband was initially diagnosed with binocular RP.
 

## Val858Ile and Asp2782Asn variants of CDH23 were identified in the proband of this family
The proband carried a compound heterozygous variant in the CDH23 gene, consisting of c.2572G>A; p.Val858Ile and c.8344G>A; p.Asp2782Asn, as determined by whole exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing ( ). It was found by comparing the human protein sequence with those of eight other species, including Pan troglodytes (chimpanzee), Rattus norvegicus (Norway rat), Papio anubis (olive baboon), Canis lupus familiaris (dog), Macaca mulatta (Rhesus macaque), Rattus norvegicus (rat) and Mus musculus (mouse), that the valine at position 858 and aspartic acid at position 2782 in the amino acid sequence translated by the gene is highly conserved in protein sequences from different species ( ). A compound heterozygous variant including the c.2572G>A; p.Val858Ile variant has been reported in a family with USH1 and is considered a likely pathogenic variant, and the proband’s father with the normal phenotype carried such a variant. The c.8344G>A; p.Asp2782Asn variant is a newly identified one that causes the codon 2782 to change from encoding aspartic acid to encoding asparagine, and the proband’s mother with the normal phenotype carried such a variant. According to the ACMG guidelines for the classification of pathogenic variants, the c.8344G>A; p.Asp2782Asn variant was not identified in databases of normal populations or databases of known benign sites. As a result, it was considered a de novo variant site and was classified as Moderate Pathogenic (PM2). Such a variant was co-segregated in the family, which is considered Pathogenic Supporting (PP1). It is known that the missense variant is the cause of USH1D (usher syndrome type 1D) disease, so the new missense variant found in the gene is Pathogenic Supporting (PP2). It has been predicted by multiple in silico software that such a variant will have deleterious effects on the gene or gene product as Pathogenic Supporting (PP3). The phenotype of the variant carriers was highly consistent with the single-gene inherited retinal disease RP, which is considered Pathogenic Supporting (PP4). Therefore, based on the ACMG standards and guidelines for the interpretation of sequence variants, these variants are classified as pathogenic (PM2+PP1+PP2+PP3+PP4). Protein configuration analysis showed that there was a significant change in the interaction between the amino termini of the mutant protein. In the wild type, the valine at position 858 formed backbone hydrogen bonds with alanine at position 874, with hydrogen bond distances of 2.9 angstrom and 2.8 angstrom. The group of the side chain formed alkyl conjugate interactions with isoleucine at position 817, valine at position 800 and leucine at position 826; their conjugate bond distances were 5.2 angstrom, 6.0 angstrom and 5.1 angstrom, respectively. The methyl group of the side chain engaged in a π-alkyl conjugate interaction with tryptophan at position 778 of the protein, with a conjugate bond distance of 4.9 angstrom. The p.Val858Ile caused the non-polar valine at position 858 to be mutated to the non-polar isoleucine. The mutant isoleucine at position 858 formed a backbone hydrogen bond with alanine at position 874, with a hydrogen bond distance of 2.8 angstroms and 3.0 angstrom. The group of the side chain formed alkyl conjugate interactions with isoleucine at position 817, Val 800 and Leu 826, their conjugate bond distances were 5.2 angstrom, 5.2 angstrom and 5.5 angstrom, respectively. The methyl group of the side chain formed π-alkyl conjugate interaction with Trp 778 of the protein, and their conjugate bond distance was 6.1 angstrom. The change in the interaction distance between wild type and mutant proteins further indicates spatial changes, which in turn lead to changes in protein function ( ). As stated above, the compound heterozygous variants in the CDH23 gene, c.2572G>A; p.Val858Ile and c.8344G>A; p.Asp2782Asn, were determined to be the pathogenic causes for the patient’s condition.

## Effects of CDH23 gene knockdown on the structure of the zebrafish eyeball
To explore the effect of gene knockdown on the macrostructure of zebrafish eyes, we conducted a comparative analysis of knockdown zebrafish and wild type zebrafish in vivo at 3 days and 6 days after fertilisation. The results showed that at this stage of development, the ocular appearance of knockdown zebrafish did not exhibit obvious abnormalities compared with the eyes of wild type zebrafish ( ). To further observe the detailed changes in the structure of the zebrafish eye, the transverse section of the eye of a 5-day-old zebrafish was stained with H&E. The results also indicated that there was no significant difference in the appearance of the zebrafish eyeballs ( ). These results suggested that CDH23 is not involved in the development of eyeball appearance in zebrafish. However, it currently remains unclear whether CDH23 affects the microstructure or function of the retinal photoreceptors of zebrafish. As for this reason, we stained retinal sections from knockdown zebrafish and wild type zebrafish using immunofluorescence histochemistry to observe the differences between them. Because the ciliary marginal zone (CMZ) and the RPE both contain stem cells, it has been observed that zebrafish Müller glial cells can restart cell division and de-differentiate into retinal stem-like cells after retinal injury. These cells express activated leucocyte cell adhesion molecule A (Alcama). In addition, it has also been demonstrated that Alcama/Zn5 is a novel marker for pluripotent retinal stem cells in the retina. Based on the aforementioned results, immunofluorescence histochemical staining was conducted on the retinas of 5-day-old wild type and CDH23 knockdown zebrafish using an Alcama/Zn5 monoclonal antibody. The findings revealed noticeable differences between the two groups in the retinal photoreceptor layers. Specifically, the photoreceptor layer in the CDH23-knockdown zebrafish was significantly thinner, and the length of the retinal photoreceptor cells was markedly shortened and sparsely distributed. This change was particularly pronounced near the ciliary body ( ). Staining with multi-PDZ-domain protein (MPDZ) polyclonal antibody indicated that the retinal photoreceptor cells of CDH23-knockdown zebrafish were more disorganised than those in the wild type, exhibiting shorter cell lengths, sparser cell distribution and disordered arrangement ( ). These findings were consistent with the results obtained using the Alcama/Zn5 antibody. Additionally, immunohistochemical staining of the retinas of 5-day-old wild type and CDH23-knockdown zebrafish with the monoclonal antibodies Arrestin 3/ZO-1 (Zonula Occludens-1) revealed that the photoreceptors in the retina of CDH23-knockdown zebrafish were more sparsely distributed than those in the wild type ( ). However, contrary to the results from the first two antibodies, the Arrestin 3/ZO-1 antibody showed not only a decrease in the number of photoreceptors but also more closely packed connecting structures between the photoreceptors (the specific measurements are detailed in ).
 

## Discussion
Cadherin 23 is known to be expressed in the retinas of mice, monkeys and humans, 
21 and has been reported to be present during the developing retina. The gene contains 69 exons and encodes a predicted 3354 amino acid protein with 27 cadherin extracellular (EC) repeats, a transmembrane domain, and a unique cytoplasmic domain. Each EC domain contains cadherin-specific amino acid motifs such as LDRE (Linker-Domain Repeat Element), DXD (di-aspartic-acid motif (Asp-X-Asp)) and DXNDN (D1X-D2-D3 N-terminal Domain Node), which are highly conserved in sequence and spacing. These motifs are essential for cadherin dimerisation and calcium (Ca²+) binding. 
Different types of CDH23 variants lead to different diseases. For example, missense mutations are the predominant mutation type in patients with DFNB12 (autosomal recessive nonsyndromic hearing-loss), contrasting with the multiple mutation types observed in patients with USH1D. Additionally, asymptomatic RP-like manifestations were found in the ophthalmological findings of patients with DFNB12, suggesting that missense mutations may have mild effects on the retina. In this family study, compound heterozygous variants in the CDH23 gene were detected in the proband: c.2572G>A; p.Val858Ile and c.8344G>A; p.Asp2782Asn, both of which are missense variants. Although missense variant abnormalities have been reported in patients with DFNB12, the proband in this study exhibited a non-syndromic RP without concomitant deafness symptoms. This could possibly be due to the weak effect of the mutations on protein function. These mutations may still allow some auditory function for the cochlea but lead to visual dysfunction in the retina. From this, we further deduced that different regions of the gene have specific functions. For example, most of the missense variant mutations in patients with DFNB12 occur within calcium-binding motifs such as DXNDN, LDRE and DXD. In contrast, the majority of the variants associated with their phenotypes were located in EC structural domains in patients with USH1. The two variant sites carried by the proband in this study were located within EC structural domains and showed only abnormal visual function, suggesting that these variant sites may be located in specific regions of EC domains that are crucial for visual function. We hypothesise that both variant sites in the proband are located in regions of the EC structural domain that are critical for visual function. These variant sites likely have minimal or no effect on hearing, which could explain why the proband exhibits only a visual impairment. For patients who exhibit only a hearing abnormality, we speculate that the variant sites are primarily located outside the EC structural domain. In the case of a small proportion of variants, they predominantly affect auditory function rather than visual function. Patients with USH1 exhibit abnormalities in both auditory and visual functions. This may be due to variant sites affecting regions within the EC domain that are related to both auditory and visual functions, or it could be because these variant sites impact common pathways or mechanisms shared by auditory and visual functions. However, further experimental studies and clinical data analysis are needed to verify this hypothesis. This study is the first to correlate CDH23 with non-syndromic RP, thereby expanding our understanding of the phenotypical profile associated with the CDH23 gene and offering new insights into the role of CDH23 in various diseases.
Zebrafish are easy to maintain, develop rapidly and have a short life cycle. Their eyeballs, retinal structure and proportion of rod cells are similar to those of humans. Additionally, zebrafish genes are homologous to human genes to a certain extent, which is conducive to studying the mechanism of related diseases. In this study, we used three different monoclonal antibodies (Alcama/Zn5, MPDZ and Arrestin 3/ZO-1) to perform immunofluorescence histochemical staining of the retinas of zebrafish with a CDH23-knockdown. The results indicated that the knockdown of the CDH23 gene resulted in a shortening of the length of retinal photoreceptor cells, a decrease in cell density and disorganised cell arrangement in zebrafish. These structural changes are likely the underlying cause of visual dysfunction. In particular, staining with the Alcama/Zn5 monoclonal antibody revealed more pronounced changes in photoreceptor cells near the ciliary region. We speculate that this heightened sensitivity may be attributed to the more active mitotic proliferation of cells in the CMZ of fish compared with mammals. This higher activity allows for the continuous generation of new neurons that integrate into the retina, which could account for the more pronounced effects observed in this study. Furthermore, the staining results from the Arrestin 3/ZO-1 monoclonal antibody indicated that the connecting structures between photoreceptors became more closely associated. We speculate that this observation may indicate a compensatory mechanism in zebrafish, triggered by the damage to the retinal photoreceptor structure following CDH23-knockdown, aimed at maintaining retinal function. This finding is consistent with observations in Xenopus, where the photoreceptor layer was also significantly thinner after CDH23-knockdown. However, studies in mouse models have demonstrated that CDH23 variants lead to retinal dysfunction without affecting the structural development of the retina. This may be due to the fact that some mouse strains used in the experiment do not express CDH23 in the retinal photoreceptors, or it could be because the mouse retina is predominantly composed of rod cells. In contrast, in patients with USH1, damage to rod cells is a primary feature in the early stages of the disease. Such slight damage may have a minimal impact on the overall retina and may not be readily apparent. Although previous studies have found that zebrafish larvae with CDH23 gene variants do not exhibit obvious signs of retinal degeneration or dysfunction, this study highlights the importance of the CDH23 gene in maintaining the structural integrity of retinal photoreceptor cells. This may be due to the variant sites in zebrafish from previous studies retaining some retinal function. Concurrently, this study also found that staining with the Arrestin 3/ZO-1 antibody revealed that the connections between retinal photoreceptor cells in zebrafish with reduced CDH23 expression were closer. We speculate that this may be due to the activation of a compensatory mechanism following damage to the photoreceptor tip link structure, which aims to maintain retinal function by strengthening intercellular connections. In the preliminary stage of this study, we assessed the responsiveness of zebrafish to acoustic stimuli. This was done by ringing a bell at a fixed distance and repeatedly exposing 5-day-old zebrafish to the sound. The results showed that both the knockdown zebrafish and the wild type zebrafish showed similar escape or orientation behaviours in response to the bell sound, showing high sensitivity to the bell sound and a consistent response pattern. There was no significant difference between the knockdown and wild type zebrafish. This is consistent with the phenotype of the proband in our study. However, given that the zebrafish used in this study were 5-day-old juveniles, it is possible that the hearing abilities may not yet have been significantly impacted by gene knockdown at this stage of development. Gene expression and cell function can vary across different stages of development, which could lead to different outcomes as the zebrafish mature.
CDH23 is crucial for maintaining the function of the tip link in ciliary cells. In conjunction with the dimer composed of PCDH15, it plays a role in the formation and regulation of the tip link in mammalian ciliated cells, directly influencing the length of the stereocilia. Mutations in any gene involved in the tip link can result in ciliary dysfunction, which may subsequently lead to abnormal photoreceptor cell function and even affect other bodily systems. CDH23 interacts with the purinergic signalling system to jointly regulate sensory signalling. The normal physiological function of ciliated cells, which demand high energy, relies on an adequate supply of energy. CDH23 is critical for maintaining this energy supply process, as it is involved in the production and targeted delivery of energy. When CDH23 is knocked down, it may disrupt these processes, potentially leading to metabolic ATP disorders. ATP is a key molecule for intracellular energy conversion, and metabolic disorders of ATP can impair purine metabolism and affect ATP production. Since P2RX7, the purinergic receptor P2X ligand-gated ion channel 7, is sensitive to ATP levels, disruptions in ATP generation can impair the function of the P2RX7 ion channel. When the intracellular and EC exchange of ions is compromised, it can ultimately affect the structure and signalling function of photoreceptors, or even lead to their death. Therefore, we believe that mutations in the CDH23 gene lead to abnormal adhesion of retinal photoreceptor cells, which in turn affects cellular energy metabolism and ion exchange. This disruption of intercellular signalling is likely the primary cause of visual impairment. Given that previous studies on CDH23 have typically focused on phenotypes associated with deafness, and considering that CDH23-wave protein complexes have distinct compositions and functions in retinal and cochlear cells, it is clear that the mechanisms of action of CDH23 in the retina and cochlea are not identical. Therefore, we need to conduct more targeted research to explore its specific mechanism of action in the retina more deeply.
So far, we have identified only one patient with a CDH23 variant associated with non-syndromic RP. Given the limited sample size, we cannot exclude the influence of other factors that may contribute to the proband’s normal hearing in this study. Therefore, we will continue to collect similar cases to expand our sample size, thereby enhancing the reliability of our study’s findings. Concurrently, since the CDH23 variant results in the disruption of photoreceptor tip linkages and leads to compensatory phenomena due to tighter intercellular linkages, further in-depth research is necessary to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms and the developmental processes involved.

## Conclusion
In this study, we correlated the CDH23 gene with non-syndromic RP for the first time, thereby expanding the known phenotypical spectrum associated with this gene. Additionally, immunofluorescent tissue staining of the zebrafish retina using monoclonal antibodies, such as Alcama/Zn5, clearly demonstrated the critical role of the CDH23 gene in the structure and function of retinal photoreceptor cells. This finding emphasises the significant impact of CDH23 on retinal function and structure. Furthermore, our results underscore the need for further research to investigate the specific mechanisms by which CDH23 variants lead to visual impairment and to explore potential therapeutic strategies for such conditions. We hope that these efforts will offer new hope for the restoration of vision and contribute to the treatment of related diseases.
