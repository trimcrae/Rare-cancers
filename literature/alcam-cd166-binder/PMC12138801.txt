## Key Points

## Question
Are cell adhesion molecules (vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 [VCAM-1] and intracellular adhesion molecule 1 [ICAM-1]) associated with adverse outcomes in chronic heart failure?

## Findings
In this biomarker substudy of the Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse-outcomes in Heart Failure (DAPA-HF) randomized clinical trial, 3051 participants had VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 levels measured. The risks of fatal and nonfatal outcomes were greater in those with higher VCAM-1 levels, even after adjustment for other known prognostic variables; there was no association between ICAM-1 levels and outcomes.

## Meaning
Results suggest that VCAM-1 levels may reflect a distinct inflammatory/immune pathophysiological pathway in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

## Abstract

## Importance
Vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM-1) and intracellular cell adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) are responsible for immune cell-cell interactions. Systemic levels of VCAM-1 are associated with incident heart failure (HF).

## Objectives
To determine if VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 levels are associated with progression of established HF.

## Design, Setting, and Participants
Participants enrolled in the biomarker substudy of the Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Heart Failure (DAPA-HF) randomized clinical trial had VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 levels measured at baseline and 12 months. The DAPA-HF trial was conducted at 410 sites in 20 countries. Patients with HF and reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) in New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II to IV with elevated natriuretic peptides were enrolled between February 15, 2017, and August 17, 2018, with final follow-up on June 6, 2019. Data were analyzed from January 2023 to January 2025.

## Interventions
Dapagliflozin, 10 mg, once daily vs placebo.

## Main Outcomes and Measures
The primary outcome was the composite of a worsening HF event or cardiovascular death. The associations between VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 levels at baseline and the primary outcome, its components, and all-cause death were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression models adjusted for known prognostic variables including estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-TnT), as well as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein.

## Results
A total of 3051 participants (mean [SD] age, 67.2 [10.5] years; 2386 male [78.2%]) were included in this study. Mean (SD) follow-up time was 17.6 (5.2) months. The median (IQR) baseline VCAM-1 level was 997 (816.7-1218.8) ng/mL. Compared with patients with lower concentrations of VCAM-1, those with higher concentrations of VCAM-1 were older (mean [SD] age T3 vs T1, 69.7 [9.7] years vs 64.1 [10.7] years; P < .001), in worse NYHA class (T3 vs T1, NYHA class III/IV 35.6% [362 of 1017] vs 26.5% [269 of 1017]; P < .001), and had higher NT-proBNP (median [IQR] T3 vs T1, 2018 [1126-3753] pg/mL vs 1118 [693-1830] pg/mL) and hs-TnT (median [IQR] T3 vs T1, 24.7 [17.1-37.5] ng/L vs 16.6 [11.6-24.9] ng/L) concentrations, and lower eGFR (mean [SD] T3 vs T1, 58.4 [17.6] mL/min/1.73 m2 vs 71.7 [18.0] mL/min/1.73 m2). Patients in tertile 3 of VCAM-1, compared with tertile 1, had the highest risk of each outcome (eg, adjusted hazard ratio [HR] for primary outcome 1.40; 95% CI, 1.11-1.77; P = .004). ICAM-1 level was not associated with an elevated risk of any outcome. The benefit of dapagliflozin vs placebo in reducing the risk of the primary outcome was consistent across VCAM-1 tertiles: HR, 0.76 (95% CI, 0.54-1.06), 0.82 (95% CI, 0.59-1.12), and 0.77 (95% CI, 0.61-0.98) for tertiles 1, 2 and 3, respectively (P for interaction = .93). There was no significant change in VCAM-1 level with dapagliflozin at 52 weeks.

## Conclusions and Relevance
Results of this substudy of the DAPA-HF randomized clinical trial demonstrate that higher VCAM-1 levels, possibly reflecting a distinct inflammatory/immune pathophysiological pathway in HFrEF, were associated with worse outcomes, even after adjustment for conventional prognostic variables.

## Introduction
Cell adhesion molecules (CAMs) belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily include intracellular cell adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM-1), which mediate interactions between different cell types. , , ICAM-1 expression is required for leukocyte interactions, including those that initiate inflammatory responses, and VCAM-1 is considered an endothelial cell protein that binds lymphocytes. Cardiovascular interest in these CAMs has been focused on their potential role in atherosclerosis. , , However, VCAM-1 expression (but not ICAM-1 expression) is increased in ventricular cardiomyocytes from patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), compared with controls, and positively correlated with infiltration of T lymphocytes and other immune cells. Similarly, VCAM-1 (but not ICAM-1) expression, was increased in the atrial tissue of patients with atrial fibrillation (AF), with greater expression in patients with lower left ventricular EF (LVEF). ICAM-1 expression was found to be increased in myocardial biopsies from patients with HF and preserved EF (HFpEF) but not in those with HFrEF. 
Circulating soluble isoforms of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 are detectable in plasma and are thought to reflect proteolytic cleavage (ie, shedding) of the molecules. Systemic levels of VCAM-1 were predictive of incident HF in the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA). Only 2 small (74 and 120 patients, respectively) single-center studies , published in 2003 and 2013, respectively, had previously attempted to examine the relationship between VCAM-1 and outcomes in HFrEF and gave conflicting results.
Given these findings, in this post hoc analysis, we hypothesized that VCAM-1 (but not ICAM-1) would be associated with clinical outcomes among patients with established HFrEF and that this association would be independent of other biomarkers reflecting myocardial wall stress and injury.
We tested this hypothesis in the biomarker substudy of the Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse-outcomes in Heart Failure (DAPA-HF) randomized clinical trial, comparing dapagliflozin with placebo in patients with HFrEF. 
We also tested the hypothesis that dapagliflozin would reduce circulating VCAM-1 concentration, because another sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, empagliflozin, was shown to reduce VCAM-1 when incubated in vitro with skinned myocardial fibers isolated from left ventricular biopsies taken from patients with HFpEF. 

## Methods
DAPA-HF was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial that compared dapagliflozin, 10 mg, once daily with placebo in patients with HFrEF. Ethics committees for the 410 participating institutions in 20 countries approved the protocol, and patients gave written consent. Participation in the biomarker substudy was offered to patients in countries where local restrictions allowed prospective sample collection. This study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guidelines.

## Study Patients and Treatment
Patients in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II to IV, with a LVEF of 40% or less and an elevated N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-pro-BNP) concentration, were eligible for study inclusion if receiving standard pharmacological and device therapy. The key exclusion criteria included type 1 diabetes and an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) less than 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. As required by the US Food and Drug Administration for participants in clinical trials, patients self-reported the following races and ethnicities: Asian, Black, White, and other, which included American Indian or Alaska Native, Asian/Puerto Rican, Black Hispanic, Caribbean, Hispanic, Latino, Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander, and South American Indian.

## Measurement of Vascular Adhesion Molecules
VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 were measured at baseline and 12 months after randomization. Details of sample collection, assay, and assay performance are provided in eTable 1 in .

## Outcomes
The primary outcome was the composite of a worsening HF event (HF hospitalization or urgent visit for HF requiring intravenous therapy) or cardiovascular death, whichever occurred first. We investigated the association between baseline CAM levels and the risk of the primary outcome, its components, all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction, and stroke. We also examined the effect of dapagliflozin compared with placebo, according to baseline CAM level, on the primary end point and its components.

## Statistical Analysis
Baseline CAM levels were categorized by tertiles. Baseline characteristics according to serum CAM levels are presented as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables and means with SD or medians with IQR for continuous variables. A nonparametric test for trend was used to examine for variation in continuous baseline characteristics across CAM tertiles. Correlations between adhesion molecules, NT-proBNP level, high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-TnT) level, and eGFR were determined using the Pearson correlation coefficient with log-transformed values as appropriate. The association between clinical and laboratory variables and ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 was examined using multivariable linear regression.
Incidence rates for each outcome are presented per 100 person-years of follow-up. Event rates in each CAM tertile were estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. The associations between baseline CAM levels and outcomes were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression models stratified by diabetes status and adjusted for a history of HF hospitalization (except the model for all-cause mortality). Further adjustment was performed for age, sex, heart rate, systolic blood pressure (BP), body mass index (BMI), ischemic HF etiology, LVEF, NYHA classification, AF, log NT-proBNP, log hs-TnT, log high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), and eGFR.
The association between CAMs as continuous variables and outcomes was analyzed using restricted cubic splines with 5 knots using the median value as the reference. Both VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 were also dichotomized at or above/below an optimal cut point using the Youden method to maximize the product of the sensitivity and specificity of the cut point related to the primary composite outcome. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were constructed to illustrate the likelihood of the primary outcome with levels above the cut point.
A C index at 2 years was calculated for the primary outcome from a multivariable Cox model including clinically relevant variables known to be associated with this outcome (history of HF hospitalization, randomized treatment, age, sex, heart rate, systolic BP, BMI, ischemic HF etiology, LVEF, NYHA class, AF, and eGFR) and stratified by diabetes status. We compared the C index derived from this clinical model with the C index derived from a model after adding VCAM-1 or ICAM-1. For context, we also added NT-proBNP, hs-TnT, or hs-CRP to the model and calculated the respective C index. Finally, we tested whether VCAM-1 or ICAM-1 remained significant predictors in the model after adding these other biomarkers. 
The effect of dapagliflozin compared with placebo on each outcome was calculated as hazard ratio (HR) and 95% CI derived from Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for a history of HF hospitalization and treatment assignment and stratified by baseline diabetes status (models for all-cause mortality did not include previous HF hospitalization). The effect of dapagliflozin on the primary outcome according to the level of CAM (analyzed as a continuous variable) was examined using a fractional polynomial analysis using the Stata mfpi command (StataCorp).
The effect of dapagliflozin on CAM levels between baseline and 12 months is presented as a ratio of geometric means derived from a linear regression model adjusted for baseline values.
All analyses were performed using Stata, version 17 (StataCorp). A 2-sided P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant (with no adjustment for multiplicity, given the exploratory nature of the analyses). Study data were analyzed from January 2023 to January 2025.

## Results
Of the 4744 patients randomized in the DAPA-HF trial, 3051 (64.3%; mean [SD] age, 67.2 [10.5] years; 665 female [21.8%]; 2386 male [78.2%]) had a baseline measurement of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1. Patients self-reported the following races and ethnicities: 562 Asian (18.4%), 86 Black (2.8%), 2391 White (78.4%), and 12 other (0.4%). A total of 2359 patients (77.3%) had repeated measurement of VCAM-1 at 12 months, and 2364 patients (77.5.%) had repeated measurement of ICAM-1 at 12 months. Patients with measurements of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 were more often White race (2391 of 3051 [78.4%]) and less often Asian race (562 of 3051 [18.4%]) compared with those without measurements (White race, 942 of 1693 [55.6%]; Asian race, 554 of 1693 [32.7%]) and more often had a history of myocardial infarction (1446 of 3051 [47.4%] vs 646 of 1693 [38.2%]) and an implanted device (956 of 3051 [31.3%] vs 286 of 1693 [16.9%]) (eTable 2 in ). Mean (SD) follow-up time was 17.6 (5.2) months. The distribution of both CAMs at baseline is displayed in eFigure 1 in . The median (IQR) VCAM-1 level was 997.0 (816.7-1218.8) ng/mL, and the mean (SD) was 1045.0 (363.8) ng/mL. Tertiles of VCAM-1 were as follows: T1, less than or equal to 876.3 ng/mL; T2, 876.4 to 1126.3 ng/mL; and T3, greater than or equal to 1126.4 ng/mL (for ICAM-1, the tertile values were T1 ≤382.7 ng/mL, T2 = 382.8-486.6 ng/mL, and T3 ≥486.7 ng/mL).

## Patient Characteristics

## VCAM-1
Patients with higher concentrations of VCAM-1 were older (mean [SD] age T3 vs T1, 69.7 [9.7] years vs 64.1 [10.7] years; P < .001) and had worse NYHA functional class (T3 vs T1, NYHA class III/IV 35.6% [362 of 1017] vs 26.5% [269 of 1017]; P < .001) than those with lower concentrations of VCAM-1. They also had higher NT-proBNP (median [IQR] T3 vs T1, 2018 [1126-3753] pg/mL vs 1118 [693-1830] pg/mL; to convert to nanograms per liter, multiply by 1) and hs-TnT (median [IQR] T3 vs T1, 24.7 [17.1-37.5] ng/L vs 16.6 [11.6-24.9] ng/L; to convert to micrograms per liter, multiply by 0.001) levels and lower mean systolic BP (mean [SD] T3 vs T1, 120.9 [15.4] mm Hg vs 122.7 [16.0] mm Hg), eGFR (mean [SD] T3 vs T1, 58.4 [17.6] mL/min/1.73 m2 vs 71.7 [18.0] mL/min/1.73 m2), and median KCCQ-TSS score (median [IQR] T3 vs T1, 75.0 [56.2-90.6] vs 81.2 [64.6-93.8]) ( ). Median (IQR) hs-CRP concentrations at baseline were higher in VCAM-1 T3 compared with T1 (2.60 [1.04-5.91] mg/L vs 1.73 [0.72-3.94] mg/L; P < .001). However, the duration of HF, history of HF hospitalization, and LVEF did not differ across tertiles of VCAM-1. Compared with those in VCAM-1 T1, patients in T3 more often had a history of diabetes (48.6% [494 of 1017] vs 40.6% [413 of 1017]; P < .001), hypertension (79.0% [803 of 1017] vs 74.1% [754 of 1017]; P = .01), and AF (52.0% [529 of 1017] vs 27.5% [280 of 1017]; P < .001), but patients with higher VCAM-1 levels were less likely to have obesity than patients with lower VCAM-1 (obesity class I, 20.7% [211 of 1017] vs 26.0% [264 of 1017]; P = .03). There was no difference in prevalence of peripheral or coronary artery disease across VCAM-1 tertiles. Patients receiving renin-angiotensin system blocking treatment and statins had lower VCAM-1 levels than those not receiving these therapies (VCAM level, 1036.1 [351.1] ng/mL vs 1125 [455.6] ng/mL; P <.001 in those receiving renin-angiotensin system [RAS] inhibitor therapies vs those not receiving RAS inhibitor therapies; VCAM level, 1035.7 [359.2] ng/mL vs 1064.9 [372.7] ng/mL; P =.04 in those receiving statin therapy vs those not receiving statin therapy).

Over a median (IQR) follow-up of 18.2 (13.6-21.6) months, the primary outcome occurred in 562 patients. Cardiovascular death occurred in 314 patients, and 379 died of any cause.
The cumulative incidence of the outcomes of interest according to tertiles of VCAM-1 are shown in . The rate of events did not increase proportionally across tertiles of VCAM-1, eg, although the unadjusted risk of the primary outcome was slightly higher among patients in T2 compared with T1 (HR, 1.12; 95% CI, 0.88-1.41; P = .36), patients in T3 had the highest risk in the unadjusted analysis (HR, 2.21; 95% CI, 1.80-2.72; P < .001). Patients in tertile 3 of VCAM-1, compared with tertile 1, had the highest risk of each outcome (eg, adjusted HR for primary outcome, 1.40; 95% CI, 1.11-1.77; P = .004). Baseline levels of VCAM-1 were not associated with a greater risk of stroke or myocardial infarction although these occurred at low rates.

When added to clinical variables, VCAM-1 but not ICAM-1 improved the C index for the prognostic model (eTable 6 in the ). ## Effect of Dapagliflozin on CAM Concentrations
There was no significant change in VCAM-1 level with dapagliflozin at 52 weeks. The placebo-adjusted change in VCAM-1 52 weeks after randomization was −7.18 (−32.7 to 18.4 ng/mL; P = .58). There was also no significant change in ICAM-1 at 52 weeks (−4.09; −14.9 to 6.8 ng/mL; P = .47).

## Discussion
In this substudy of the DAPA-HF randomized clinical trial, we found that VCAM-1 was associated with nonfatal and fatal outcomes in patients with HF, and these associations persisted despite adjustment for other prognostic variables including LVEF, eGFR, NT-proBNP, and hs-TnT levels.
Prior studies had shown that VCAM-1 levels are increased in patients with HF compared with controls. However, only 2 studies , examined the association between VCAM-1 and outcomes in HFrEF. The larger study of the 2 included 120 patients with chronic HF, LVEF less than 45%, and coronary artery disease. Over 13 months of follow-up, 11 participants died, and 13 were hospitalized for HF. VCAM-1 was associated with all-cause mortality. However, this was not found in another study of 74 patients with chronic HF and LVEF less than 50%. Results of our much larger cohort confirm that VCAM-1 was associated with outcomes in HFrEF.
The explanation for high VCAM-1 levels in patients with HFrEF and why they are associated with HF hospitalization and death is uncertain. There was a decrement in kidney function across ascending tertiles of VCAM-1. It is not known whether VCAM-1 is cleared by the kidneys; although, in the general population, eGFR is inversely associated with circulating VCAM-1 concentration. Nevertheless, in the present study, VCAM-1 was only modestly correlated with eGFR and remained predictive of outcomes, despite adjustment for eGFR.
Interestingly, in people with metabolically healthy obesity, levels of VCAM-1 are not elevated compared with metabolically healthy lean controls (in contrast to ICAM-1). We found that patients in the lowest tertile of VCAM-1 were less likely to have obesity than those in the highest tertile, a pattern opposite of what is observed with natriuretic peptides (and this pattern was not observed for ICAM-1).
Another striking difference was the association between VCAM-1 levels and AF, with more than a 2-fold higher prevalence of AF in patients in the highest compared with lowest tertile of VCAM-1. In our study, this association persisted despite extensive adjustment for other important markers of severity of HF. In a prior study, AF was associated with increased tissue expression of VCAM-1 (but not ICAM-1) in right atrial appendage samples obtained from 320 consecutive patients with (n = 61) and without (n = 259) AF undergoing cardiac surgery. In a supportive study, rapid pacing of human atrial tissue slices led to a significant upregulation of VCAM-1 expression. The authors suggested that the endocardium was the source of VCAM-1. Furthermore, VCAM-1 was associated with the risk of incident AF over 20 years in a population-based cohort study, with a concentration-response–type relationship.
Another potential source of VCAM-1 and a possible link between VCAM-1 and prognosis is inflammation of the myocardium. An association between serum VCAM-1 levels and myocardial inflammation was first shown in a small study of 47 patients with nonischemic HF undergoing right ventricular biopsy. In 41% of those with elevated serum levels of VCAM-1, the myocardial biopsies showed increased lymphocytic infiltration and enhanced expression of the major histocompatibility complex antigens of class I/II indicating active inflammation. A recent larger study examined 313 samples of ventricular myocardium obtained from 177 patients with HF (and 136 healthy controls). VCAM-1 expression was significantly higher in patients with HF, and VCAM-1 expression positively correlated with a variety of T cells and other immune cells, which were significantly elevated in the myocardial samples from patients with HF, compared with controls. Collectively, these studies suggest that circulating VCAM-1 and myocardial VCAM-1 expression are associated with myocardial immune infiltration (and that VCAM-1 may facilitate immune cell infiltration). Potentially relevant to a myocardial origin of VCAM-1 is a small study demonstrating a rapid decrease in circulating levels of this biomarker after heart transplant, although the levels do not normalize. Elevated VCAM-1 levels may, therefore, be associated with worse outcomes in HFrEF because they reflect heightened immune and inflammatory activity, and downstream oxidative stress, in the myocardium. This hypothesis would also explain why the association is independent of natriuretic peptides, reflecting a distinct pathophysiological process. Importantly, this hypothesis suggests the inflammation reflected by VCAM-1 levels is focused in the myocardium because, although hs-CRP concentrations, reflecting systemic inflammation, were significantly higher among patients in the highest tertile of VCAM-1, the correlation between VCAM-1 and hs-CRP was weak. VCAM-1 reflects a more localized and specific role at the site of inflammation (ie, the myocardium) than hs-CRP. , 
What is not clear from these studies is whether cardiomyocytes, the endocardium, or vascular endothelial cells within the cardiac vasculature are sources of VCAM-1. In HFpEF, it has been suggested that VCAM-1 is linked to inflammation and microvascular dysfunction, processes thought to be important in the pathophysiology of that HF phenotype. , , 
An additional potential source of VCAM-1 is the venous circulation. Colombo and colleagues carried out a venous stress test (inflating a cuff around the upper arm above 30 mm Hg to increase venous pressure) in patients with HFrEF to mimic venous congestion. This led to an increase in VCAM-1 in venous blood drawn from the test arm, compared with the control arm. 
Despite the suggestion that SGLT2 inhibition reduces myocardial VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 expression in vitro, we did not find that dapagliflozin reduced circulating levels of either CAM, although our analysis was limited by having only 1 follow-up measurement after 12 months of treatment. However, our findings are in keeping with those from the EMPEROR (Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Patients With Chronic Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction; Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Patients With Chronic Heart Failure With Reduced Ejection Fraction) trials, in which 2 large-scale proteomic studies did not show differential expression of VCAM-1 or ICAM-1 after 3 months of treatment with empagliflozin or placebo. , Several other cell adhesion–related proteins were differentially expressed with empagliflozin with a 10-fold or greater increase as compared with placebo. Intracellular adhesion molecule 4 (ICAM-4) is a protein expressed on erythroid cells, and the increase in ICAM-4 is potentially consistent with the known erythropoietic effect of SGLT2 inhibitors. Epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) is thought to be important in the regeneration of kidney tubular cells in animal models and may account for some of the kidney protective effects of SGLT2 inhibitors. Other differentially expressed proteins were carcinoembryonic antigen–related cell adhesion molecule 8, junctional adhesion molecule C, and cadherin-related family member 2; however, the potential link between the biological effect of these proteins and the clinical benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors has yet to be established.
Experimentally, angiotensin II upregulates VCAM-1, and of interest, angiotensin II receptor blockade has been shown to reduce VCAM-1, , and a VCAM-1–neutralizing antibody attenuates angiotensin II-induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis. Interestingly, we found that individuals receiving therapies targeting the renin-angiotensin system had lower VCAM-1 levels than those not taking these therapies. Similarly, atorvastatin has been shown to reduce VCAM-1 levels at 4 weeks and improve endothelial function in patients with heart failure. We found lower VCAM-1 levels in those taking statins compared with those not taking statins.
As stated previously, ICAM-1 levels were not elevated in this study, and this was also true for another CAM, activated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule, in a previous study in patients with HFrEF, supporting a VCAM-1–specific perturbation in HFrEF. 

## Limitations
This study has some limitations. This was a post hoc exploratory analysis and is subject to confounding. The patients studied were selected according to trial inclusion and exclusion criteria. We did not have data on potentially upstream inflammatory cytokines. Biomarker collection was not performed in all participating countries, and not all randomized patients in the DAPA-HF trial were included. Therefore, there may be a degree of selection bias in these analyses and the results may not be generalizable to all patients with HFrEF or to patients with HFpEF.

## Conclusions
In this substudy of the DAPA-HF randomized clinical trial, circulating VCAM-1 levels were associated with outcomes in HFrEF, even after adjustment for conventional prognostic variables including NT-proBNP, hs-TnT, and eGFR. VCAM-1 levels may reflect a distinct inflammatory/immune pathophysiological pathway in HFrEF. Dapagliflozin was of benefit across the range of VCAM-1 at baseline but did not reduce VCAM-1 level at 12 months. ICAM-1 was not associated with outcomes in HFrEF although this CAM may play a role in HFpEF.
