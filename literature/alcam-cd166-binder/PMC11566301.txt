## Abstract
Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) is a vital regulator of the fibrinolytic mechanism and has been intricately involved in various physiological and clinical processes, including cancer, thrombosis, and wound healing. The PAI-1 signaling pathway is multifaceted, encompassing numerous signaling molecules and nodes. Recent studies have revealed a novel contribution of PAI-1 during cellular senescence. This review introduces a pathway resource detailing the signaling network events mediated by PAI-1. The literature curated on the PAI-1 system was manually compiled from various published studies, our analysis presents a signaling pathway network of PAI-1, which includes various events like enzyme catalysis, molecular association, gene regulation, protein expression, and protein translocation. This signaling network aims to provide a detailed analysis of the existing understanding of the PAI-1 signaling pathway in the context of cellular senescence across various research models. By developing this pathway, we aspire to deepen our understanding of aging and senescence research, ultimately contributing to the pursuit of effective therapeutic approaches for these complex chronic diseases.

## Background

## Plasminogen Activator Inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) Insight
Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), also known as SERPINE-1, is a pivotal serine protease inhibitor that plays a critical function in regulating the fibrinolytic system, which is responsible for breaking down blood clots and preventing thrombosis [ , ]. Numerous studies have established PAI-1 as the primary inhibitor of the fibrinolytic system by regulating the expressions of tissue-type plasminogen activator (tPA) and urokinase-type plasminogen activator (uPA) [ ], these enzymes facilitate the transformation of plasminogen into plasmin the enzyme responsible for degrading fibrin in blood clots [ ].
 PAI-1 was first identified in cultured bovine endothelial cells and various other cell types across different tissues [ ]. Over the past decade, numerous laboratory and clinical studies have examined PAI-1's role in regulating fibrinolysis, contributing to the deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) and tissue fibrosis, myocardial infarction, and various other disorders. Elevated levels of PAI-1 are linked to an increased risk of thrombosis whereas lower levels are associated with a heightened risk of bleeding. PAI-1 also participates in inflammation and tissue repair [ ]. Additionally, PAI-1 regulates proliferation, cell adhesion, migration, and apoptosis, with its secretion being strongly influenced by pro-inflammatory cytokines [ – ].
The activity of PAI-1 plays a significant role in several acute and chronic pathophysiological processes and is controlled by the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling cascade, which can be further modulated through pro-fibrotic signaling pathways. Notably, PAI-1 is abundantly present in aortic valves [ – ].
Our previous studies, along with others, have underscored PAI-1's substantial function in the pathogenesis of lung fibrosis. However, the key underlined mechanisms are not yet known [ – ]. Recent findings have highlighted PAI-1's contributions beyond inhibiting the fibrinolytic system; it also serves as a marker of cellular senescence [ , ]. These findings suggest that PAI-1 is a potential target for treatment of various diseases, such as lung disorders, renal diseases and aortic stenosis associated with inflammation and cellular senescence.
Here, we have created a comprehensive resource outlining the signaling cascade events mediated by the PAI-1 network, synthesizing findings from published research articles that include previous investigation on detailed networking maps involving IL-17A, IL-18, IL-33, SARS-CoV-2, and C–C motif chemokine ligand 18 (CCL18) [ – ]. This literature annotation demonstrates the unique function of PAI-1 during aging and senescence.

## Structure and function of PAI-1
The serpin superfamily, or serine protease inhibitors, comprises of 1,500 proteins that functions either as inhibitors or non-inhibitors exhibiting a broad distribution across various species, including plants, humans, animals, and bacteria [ ]. Emerging evidence has identified PAI-1 as the most robust physiological inhibitor of both uPA and tPA [ ]. As an inhibitory serpin, PAI-1 contains a solvent-exposed reactive center loop that provides specificity for target proteases. Designated as Serpin-E, PAI-1 is distinctive among serpins due to its remarkable capacity to spontaneously transition into a thermodynamically stable dormant form, which has a half-life of approximately 2 h at 37 °C under in vitro conditions [ ]. In this form, the Reactive Center Loop inserts into the central A-sheet, with a slightly longer half-life in vivo due to a higher affinity for the somatomedin domain of vitronectin [ , ].
The human PAI-1 gene, which has a molecular weight of 12.3 kb, is located on chromosome 7 (7q21.3-q22) and consists of eight introns and nine exons. These exons encode are responsible for encoding a signal peptide consisting of 23-amino acid and a mature peptide of approximately 379 amino acids [ ].
 PAI-1 has a well-conserved structure characterized by three β-sheets and nine α-helices [ ]. As the foremost inhibitor of plasminogen activators, PAI-1 promptly deactivates both uPA and tPA, ensuring the activation of all serine protease reactions [ ]. While uPA has a higher binding affinity for cell surfaces, contributing to its proteolytic effects, tPA primarily facilitates intravascular fibrinolysis [ , ]. The transition tolatency transition of PAI-1 is irreversible under physiological conditions, however it can be reverted into its native form through refolding induced by various latency-inducing agents [ , ].
By blocking the binding of vitronectin to integrins, PAI-1 acts as a key regulator of cell motility and adhesion, independent of its protease inhibition activity [ ]. Crystallographic structures of PAI-1 in complex with vitronectin, uPA, and tPA reveal spatially discrete interactions within the molecule [ – ].
The reactive center loop in PAI-1's structure comprises a "bait" peptide bond, designated as P1-P1, which mimics the standard substrates of target proteases [ ]. The "P" notation indicates residue positions relative to the scissile bond. The interaction between the active sites of uPA or tPA and this bait region forms a 1:1 stoichiometric complex, leading to structural rearrangements that facilitate the insertion of the N-terminal Reactive Center Loop into the β-sheet A and the translocation of the protease to the opposite end of the PAI-1 molecule [ ].
In an in vivo pharmacology, identifying the antigenic determinant of PAI-1 inhibitors is crucial for understanding the mechanisms of molecular blockade [ ]. However, a well-defined structural model of PAI-1 bound to an inhibitory antibody has yet to be established [ ]. The stable protease complex of PAI-1 results in both its inactivation and the inhibition of proteases, making PAI-1 a substrate in instances of slow translocation by specific ligands [ ].
PAI-1 functions as a crucial inhibitor of plasma fibrinolytic function and is swiftly generated by various cell types such as endothelial cells, fibroblasts, smooth muscle cells, megakaryocytes, liver cells, cardiac myocytes, mesothelial cells, endometrium, peritoneum, and macrophages [ ]. Following its synthesis, PAI-1 is primarily stored in platelets, although it can also accumulate in the subendothelial matrix or be secreted into the bloodstream, where it acts in its active form upon secretion [ , ].
Various factors, beyond genetics, influence the synthesis and secretion of PAI-1, including plasma lipids, body mass index, and metabolic determinants like insulin resistance [ ]. Studies indicate that PAI-1 concentrations vary by gender and ethnicity/race, likely due to differences in adipose tissue distribution and body composition [ ]. Notably, plasma PAI-1 levels exhibit circadian variability, with peak concentrations observed in the early morning [ ].

## Methods
To develop a consolidated PAI-1 signaling cascade map, an comprehensive literature review examining research articles related to PAI-1-driven signaling was performed. We utilized the PubMed to survey published literature comprehensively. The search query included the terms like: "Plasminogen activator inhibitor type 1," "SERPINE1," "PAI," "PAI-1," "PAI1," "PLANH1," and "Serpin family E member 1," in conjunction with "Pathway" or "Signaling." This search yielded a total of 2,961 articles published on PAI-1, of which 2,718 were research articles and the remainder were reviews.
The PAI-1 mediated signaling reactions were categorized into several groups: activation/inhibition events, protein/gene regulation, enzyme catalysis/post-translational modifications (PTMs), protein translocation between cellular organelles, and molecular associations. We documented information on the types of experiments performed, the cell lines or tissues used, and details about post-translational modification sites and residues.
To illustrate and visualize the signaling pathway map, we employed the PathVisio tool, incorporating the curated signaling events.

## Integration of Data for the Development of the PAI-1 Signaling Pathway
To construct a robust PAI-1 signaling pathway, we systematically integrated data from various sources. Our approach involved collating findings from both primary research articles and reviews focused on PAI-1. Each selected article underwent thorough annotation based on NetPath criteria, ensuring that we captured the most relevant signaling events and molecular interactions. We categorized the data into distinct signaling event types, including activation/inhibition, molecular associations, translocation events, enzyme activities, gene regulation, and protein expression. This categorization allowed us to visualize the complexity of the PAI-1 signaling network, highlighting the interplay between various signaling molecules and pathways involved in cellular senescence and molecular aging. The integration process culminated in the creation of a comprehensive signaling pathway map and a cohesive framework. This map will be serve as an important tool for understanding the role of PAI-1 in cellular processes and will be made freely accessible to facilitate further research in this area.
The regulation of PAI-1 expression and activity is complex, involving multiple signaling molecules and pathways. In-depth annotation was conducted to explore the function of PAI-1 in cellular senescence and longevity. We performed a search in PubMed using relevant query terms, resulting in 65 articles associated with the PAI-1 signaling pathway. A manual screening and annotation was conducted according to the NetPath criteria, identifying 36 articles that specifically addressed PAI-1 driven cellular senescence. This investigation uncovered a total of 107 molecules involved in PAI-1-driven signaling events, which included 2 molecular associations, 14 activation/inhibition events, 1 translocation event, 12 enzyme events, 33 gene regulation events, and 45 protein expression events. The detailed annotation sheet is provided in Supplementary Data S1. These findings were utilized to develop a extensive signaling network map of the PAI-1-driven signaling cascade.

## The PAI-1 signaling pathway during cellular senescence in lung-based diseases
Cellular senescence is a key phenomenon associated with accelerated aging and age-related diseases, characterized by an irreversible growth arrest. Cells undergoing senescence become metabolically inactive, exhibiting a gene expression profile distinct from that of proliferating or quiescent cells [ , ]. A study has shown that cellular senescence is marked by a stable cessation of cellular proliferation, with DNA damage from persistent oxidative stress being a primary driver through a process known as stress-induced premature senescence (SIPS) [ ], these senescence modulators are collectively identified to as the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) or senescence-messaging secretome (SMS) [ , ]. Emerging evidence indicates that PAI-1 plays a pivotal role in cellular senescence across various pulmonary diseases [ ]. Notably, studies have identified PAI-1 as both a mediator and a marker of cellular senescence [ ]. Furthermore, PAI-1 exhibits a dual role in lung cells, functioning as both a pro-senescence and an anti-senescence factor (Fig.  ). Upregulation of PAI-1 during cellular senescence is documented in various studies. For example, Kortlever and colleagues were among the first to demonstrate that the overexpression of PAI-1 alone can trigger proliferative senescence in fibroblasts, even in the absence of p53 expression [ ]. Subsequent studies have further established PAI-1's role as both a marker and enhancer of cellular senescence in various models, including vascular cells and keratinocytes. These findings exhibit a strong correlation with in vivo mechanisms driving cellular senescence within the cardiovascular system [ ].
 An interesting study by Jiang et al. revealed the dual role of PAI-1: while it is highly expressed in alveolar epithelial type II (ATII) cells in models of bleomycin-induced idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), it also contributes to the resolution of lung fibrosis and the senescence of ATII cells [ ]. Furthermore, research using kl/kl mice demonstrated that partial deficiency of PAI-1 extended median survival nearly threefold and preserved telomere length, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for mitigating cellular senescence [ ].
Additionally, PAI-1 has also been identified as a potential target for SMS components that may alleviate age-related senescence alterations, such as emphysema [ ]. Recent findings indicate that PAI-1 deficiency normalizes IL-6 and IGFBP-3 levels in kl/kl mice, promoting the activity of key SASP factors and suggesting these molecules as downstream mediators of the senescence pathway [ ].
A study by Rana et al. explored the pro-senescent activity of PAI-1 in TGF-β-induced cellular senescence. The expression of PAI-1 is regulated by TGF-β through various signaling cascades that activate the senescence program [ , ]. Further investigations revealed that PAI-1 binds to proteasome components, inhibiting cellular senescence and promoting p53 degradation in primary mouse ATII cells and A549 cells. Notably, PAI-1 overexpression led to ATII cell senescence via a p53-independent pathway [ ]. Silencing PAI-1 activity significantly reduced doxorubicin- and bleomycin-induced senescence and p53 expression in pulmonary rat epithelial (L2) cells [ ].
Emerging evidence also highlights the efficacy of the small molecule inhibitor TM5441 in reducing doxorubicin-induced senescence in fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes, and endothelial cells by decreasing the expression of p16, p21, and p53 [ ]. Moreover, the binding of PAI-1 to the proteasomal α3 subunit inhibits the downregulation of IκBα and p53 in endothelial cells [ ]. Jiang et al. further demonstrated that PAI-1 regulates senescence in lung ATII cells by activating cell cycle pathways involving p53, p21, and pRb [ ].
Overall, these findings suggest that elevated PAI-1 levels during stress enhance reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, inhibit the fibrinolytic system, and promote downstream senescence mediators such as p53, p16, p21, and IGFBP3. Reducing pharmacological levels of PAI-1 may help mitigate the aging process by controlling stress-induced cellular senescence [ , ].
Recent observations by Born et al. indicate that hypoxia elevates PAI-1 mRNA expression alongside various SASP genes, including VEGF-α, Osteopontin, CCL-2, IL-6, and IL-1β [ ]. Chung et al. confirmed that recombinant truncated PAI-1 enhances cellular senescence by amplifying the actions of naturally occurring wild-type PAI-1 during radiation-induced pneumocyte senescence [ ]. In vivo studies have noted increased PAI-1 levels in murine models of aging, such as kl/kl and BubR1H/H mice, particularly in emphysema models [ , ].The hallmarks and molecular cascades illustrating the contribution of PAI-1 during cellular senescence are represented in Fig.  .
 

## PAI-1 is a legitimate indicator of the senescent state and aging process in various disease states
The primary function of PAI-1 in various types of stress-related senescence, associated with both chronological and accelerated aging processes, is well-documented [ , ]. Emerging evidence indicates age-related responses in PAI-1 expression occur across various tissues and correlate with heightened PAI-1 levels in stress-induced thrombosis in in vivo models [ ].
Notably, integrin β3 also influences cellular senescence. Recent findings show reduced levels of integrin αvβ3 in proliferative cells, while senescent cells exhibit significantly elevated integrin β3 levels, attributed to the reduction in CBX7, a polycomb protein that suppresses integrin β3 expression. Increased integrin β3 levels have been reported in the aged tissues of various organisms [ , ]. Additionally, elevated levels of the cytokine TGF-β, linked to increased integrin αvβ3, further promote cellular senescence through paracrine and autocrine mechanisms via the p53 and p21 signaling pathways [ ].
An intriguing finding by Lu and colleagues highlighted the epigenetic regulation of the PAI-1 gene, revealing a connection between DNA methylation levels of PAI-1 and type 2 diabetes, human lifespan, and comorbidity [ ]. PAI-1 is also implicated in astrocyte senescence, with studies demonstrating increased PAI-1 expression during H₂O₂-induced senescence. This elevation is linked to enhanced SA-β-gal activity and increased p53 expression in both mouse and human astrocyte models. Conversely, silencing, deleting, or inhibiting PAI-1 attenuates astrocyte senescence and p53 levels [ ].
Additionally, several studies illustrate a connection between the cysteine homolog homocysteine (Hcy) and PAI-1 in fibroblast senescence. Hcy not only induces PAI-1 but also inhibits the binding of tPA to its cellular receptor, ultimately leading to endothelial dysfunction. Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) was shown as a novel mediator of senescence in fibroblasts. Overexpression of Nrf2 has been shown to induce fibroblast senescence. Hiebert and colleagues demonstrated that activated Nrf-2 in fibroblasts contributes to the deposition of a senescence-inducing matrix, along with PAI-1, a key regulator and marker of the senescence program [ ].
Moreover, Chen and colleagues have established an intriguing link between SIRT-1 and PAI-1 during endothelial aging, both cell culture and in a hyperglycemia-triggered vascular murine model. They found that hyperglycemia led to increased PAI-1 levels, along with elevated p53 and p21 expressions. In comparison, elevated levels of of SIRT-1 resulted in reduced levels of PAI-1, p53, and p21 by lowering the physiological levels of superoxide dismutase (MnSOD), thereby inhibiting vascular senescence [ , ].
A research inquiry involves the signaling dynamics that facilitate the pro-senescent effects of the PAI-1 gene. One significant pathway was highlighted by Elzi and co-workers, demonstrating that depletion of PAI-1 inhibits t-PA-regulated proteolysis by reducing levels of IGFBP-3 in stress-induced cellular senescence in breast cancer cells [ , ]. Recently, Ozcan and colleagues illustrated that the PAI-1-IGFBP3 interaction is one of three molecular pathways active in the stress-mediated replicative senescence of mesenchymal stromal cells, utilizing mass spectrometry-based LC–MS/MS analysis and Ingenuity Pathway Analysis of secretomes and SASP [ ].
Notably, Kortlever and colleagues identified a novel association between the PAI-1-uPA system during replicative senescence. The SERPINE and ECM-associated gene PAI-1 downregulates the activity of the secreted protease uPA by forming a stable complex. Importantly, PAI-1 is a well-known direct target of p53 expressions occurring in aging fibroblasts, marking it as a potential indicator of replicative senescence [ , ]. This research group was among the first to suggest PAI-1 as an enhancer of senescence, concluding that it modulates cellular senescence through the PI3K-PKB-GSK3 (phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-protein kinase B-glycogen synthase kinase 3) signaling pathway including cyclin D1.
Recently, Khan and colleagues reported that null mutations of PAI-1 can prevent biological aging in humans, demonstrating longer leukocyte telomere length (LTL) and increased lifespan among carriers of the null SERPINE1 allele [ ]. The involvement of PAI-1 in cellular senescence has broad implications, as age-related elevated PAI-1 levels have been recognized in plasma in elderly wild-type mouse models (Klotho and BubR1H/H), correlating with findings in humans [ ]. Furthermore, pharmacological and genetic inhibition of PAI-1 has been shown to prevent the development of arteriosclerosis and hypertension [ ].
Collectively, these consistent and relevant findings from various experimental models and research laboratories underscore the critical role of PAI-1 as an initiator, parameter and enhancer of cellular senescence through the modulation of various downstream SASP markers.

## The PAI-1 signaling pathway
This article presents an innovative perspective compared to other review articles by constructing a PAI-1 signaling pathway that highlights the potential function of PAI-1 in cellular aging. For instance, a study by Wang et al. demonstrated that inhibiting the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-17A increases the concentrations of PAI-1 and p21 by downregulating SIRT-1 activity in ATDC-5 chondrocytes [ , ]. Additionally, bleomycin (BLM) treatment elevates PAI-1 expression in alveolar type II (ATII) cells and L2 cells, inducing phosphorylation of the tumor suppressor p53 (P-p53) and increasing levels of the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1A (CDKN1A) and retinoblastoma protein (Rb1), which ultimately promotes cellular senescence through the activation of p53 and p21 signaling pathways [ , , ].
TGF-β1 treatment also upregulates PAI-1 by increasing the expression of IL-6, IGFBP3, IL-4, IL-13, and PDGF via CDKN2A activation, leading to cellular senescence in ATII cells [ ]. PAI-1 plays a significant role in stress-induced senescence; for example, doxorubicin induces the synthesis of reactive oxygen species and suppresses catalase (CAT), which, along with increased expression of PAI-1, TP53, IGFBP-3, CDKN2A, CDKN1A, and cleaved CASP-3, contributes to senescence in human cells [ , , , ]. Furthermore, doxorubicin treatment in MCF-7 breast cancer cells upregulates SERPINE-1 via AKT1 inhibition, resulting in the inactivation of tissue-type plasminogen activator (PLAT) and subsequent IGFBP3 expression, inducing senescence [ ].
Studies indicate that late passage in human aortic endothelial cells (HAECs) leads to downregulation of SIRT-1 and upregulation of SERPINE-1, promoting senescence [ ]. Mitomycin activation of AKT-1 leads to phosphorylation of GSK3B (Ser 9), resulting in upregulation of CDKN1A and SERPINE-1, which induces senescence in alveolar epithelial cells [ ]. In cardiomyocytes, doxorubicin treatment causes downregulation of IL-4 and SIRT-1 while upregulating IL-17, IL-6, TNF, and SERPINE-1, which triggers inflammation and cellular senescence [ , ]. IL-1A induces SERPINE-1 expression in HUVECs, contributing to senescence [ ], while IL-1B enhances mRNA and protein levels of PAI1, CDKN1A, and CAV1 in chondrocytes, promoting senescence [ , ].
Interestingly, PAI-1 is implicated in cellular senescence linked to RNA binding protein depletion. For instance, RBM4 binds to pri-miR1244, maintaining its levels and inhibiting SERPINE-1 expression. When RBM4 is depleted, miR-1244 levels drop, leading to increased SERPINE-1 expression and subsequent senescence [ ]. High phosphate levels downregulate LMNB-1 and upregulate SERPINE-1, CDKN2A, ACTA-2, and CTGF, causing senescence and fibrosis in normal rat kidney (NRK) cells [ , , , ].
Recent evidence suggests PAI-1’s active role in senescence during Alzheimer's disease. Studies show that human neuronal cells treated with Aβ increase CDKN-1A, SERPINE-1, and MMP-3 levels while downregulating SIRT1, indicating Aβ-induced neuronal senescence. Aβ1-42 oligomers stimulate the upregulation of SERPINE-1 and CDKN-1A, alongside SIRT1 downregulation, leading to senescence in M17 neuronal cells [ ]. Co-workers found that treating human microglial cells HMC3 with Aβ induces gene and protein upregulation of SERPINE-1 and CDKN1A while downregulating SIRT-1 and SIRT-5, alongside Reactive Oxygen Species generation, promoting cell senescence [ ].
An in vivo study using Klotho-deficient (kl/kl) mice showed that elevated PAI1 expression increases IGFBP-3 and IL-6 levels, further enhancing senescent phenotypes [ ]. Klotho is known as an aging suppressor. Another study found that treating vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) with high glucose (HG) and PGF2-α induces PTGFR upregulation, leading to SRC phosphorylation and PAI1 activation, promoting cellular senescence [ ]. H2O2 treatment increases CDKN2A, SERPINE-1, VCAM-1, ICAM-1, IL6, and miR-34a while downregulating SIRT-1, NOS-3, and CAT, inducing endothelial senescence [ ]. Silencing SIRT6 upregulates SERPINE-1 and ICAM-1 and downregulates NOS3 through CDKN1A, leading to premature senescence in endothelial cells [ – ].
Recent findings indicate that homocysteine treatment in human endothelial cells induces MAPK1/3 phosphorylation, resulting in PAI1 upregulation and subsequent cellular senescence, involving associations with ITGAV and PPP1CA, as well as increased levels of CDKN2A, TP53, CDKN1A, and NRF2 [ , , ]. Furthermore, Nrf2 activation in fibroblasts upregulates ECM proteins like SERPINE1, ALCAM, UGT1A-1, Ugt1a7c, CDH-2, and GPC-1 [ ]. A study by Hiebert et al. revealed that the downregulation of SERPINE-1, COL1A-1, COL3A-1, FN-1, and ELN induces fibroblast senescence during wound healing and tumorigenesis [ , ].
Interestingly, research has also focused on PAI-1's contribution to aging. For instance, leukotriene D4 (LTD4) treatment in osteoblasts leads to SIRT1 downregulation and TP53, CDKN-1A, and SERPINE-1 upregulation, which are associated with cellular senescence [ ]. In the brains of aged Tg2576 mice, there is downregulation of PLAT and upregulation of SERPINE-1, contributing to amyloid pathogenesis in Alzheimer's disease [ ].
Elevated PAI-1 expression also increases TGF-β1 levels, which in turn raises ACTA2 expression in aged mice, promoting lung fibrosis [ , ]. The concentration of PAI-1 is notably higher in the plasma and serum of aged C57BL6 mice compared to younger counterparts, indicating its involvement in aging [ , ]. Additionally, there is upregulation of SERPINE-1, PLAT, and PLAU in the brains of aged wild-type mice, further implicating these markers in the aging process [ , ]. Arsenic exposure in human chondrocytes increases CDKN-1A, SERPINE-1, TP53, SQSTM-1, and GATA-4 levels through MAPK pathway activation, contributing to chondrocyte senescence and accelerated aging in rat articular cartilage [ ].
An intriguing finding shows that pneumolysin treatment in fibroblast cells elevates PAI-1 expression, leading to cell proliferation arrest in a concentration- and time-dependent manner by enhancing phosphorylation of p53, SIRT-1, and various MAPK pathways, along with increased Reactive Oxygen Species production. This upregulation of PAI-1 subsequently increases CDKN-1A, CDKN-2A, CDK2, and RB-1, inducing cellular senescence and accelerating aging [ ]. H2O2 induces stress responses, leading to upregulation of CDKN1A and SERPINE-1, while downregulating SIRT1 in senescent WI-38 cells [ – ]. Furthermore, it decreases SIRT1 and NOS3 levels, while upregulating SERPINE1, TP53, CDKN1A, and HMOX1, along with activating AKT1 and promoting nuclear translocation of NFE2L2 in HUVECs, inducing vascular senescence through the NFE2L2/HMOX-1 pathway [ , ].
Over the past decade, numerous studies have explored aging in vivo models to understand cellular senescence. In aging mice, high protein levels of CDKN-1A, CDKN-2A, TP53, PTGFR, and SRC activation, combined with SERPINE-1 upregulation via the FP receptor/Src/PAI-1 signaling pathway, play significant roles in VSMC senescence induced by high glucose [ , ]. Additionally, LPS stimulation activates CD14 and TLR4, upregulating NFKB1 and leading to increased PAI-1 and TNF levels, which drive inflammatory responses in aged mice [ ].

## Putative role of PAI-1 during HIV and aging
The role of HIV infection and aging is increasingly recognized, particularly due to its implications for inflammation and cellular senescence.
A study by Andreas et al. reported elevated PAI-1 levels in people living with HIV (PLWH) who had independent risk factors for cardiovascular disease, including antiretroviral therapy and specific HIV parameters. They concluded that PAI-1 could be a potential biomarker for predicting age-related congenital heart defects (CHDs) [ ]. In contrast, other research indicated decreased PAI-1 levels in PLWH, suggesting that pathophysiological factors and a shorter lifespan may influence these findings. Gruevska et al. compared inflammatory and senescence markers in PLWH and healthy individuals, reporting reduced expression of senescence genes such as p53 and SERPINE-1 in the context of HIV [ ]. Additionally, another study highlighted enhanced PAI-1 expression in PLWH with metabolic disorders compared to healthy controls [ , ].
Despite the limited reports on PAI-1's role in PLWH concerning cellular senescence and molecular aging, this remains a significant area for future research. Understanding the contribution of PAI-1 and the fibrinolytic system during senescence and HIV aging is crucial. The interplay between PAI-1, HIV, and aging underscores a complex relationship that may influence health outcomes in older HIV-positive individuals. Understanding this relationship could pave the way for targeted interventions aimed at reducing PAI-1 levels, mitigating inflammation, and improving overall health in this vulnerable population. Further research studies are required to understand the mechanisms by which PAI-1 mediates these processes and to explore potential therapeutic strategies.

## Summary and conclusions
In this study, we aim to illustrate a pathway map of PAI-1, highlighting its contributions to cellular senescence and aging. PAI-1 is a critical component in the iniation of cellular senescence, and our findings underscore the pivotal role of the SERPINE1 gene in this process. Targeting PAI-1 offers a promising strategy to mitigate cellular senescence and associated age-related conditions such as emphysema, arteriosclerosis, organ fibrogenesis, and thrombosis. The publicly available PAI-1 pathway map will aid researchers in understanding the various molecules involved in modulating this pathway in pathological and physiological contexts. This resource provides insights for identifying other related molecules participating in this signaling network and may lead to innovative pharmacological means for managing cellular senescence and addressing diseases linked to PAI-1. The schematic representation of PAI-1-induced signaling reactions outlines the ligand-receptor interactions and subsequent molecular events following PAI-1 induction, including catalysis, gene regulation, translocation, and molecular associations. Additionally, the pathway highlights post-translational modification sites and residues (Fig. ). Accumulating evidence, including our laboratory's research, positions PAI-1 as a molecular signature of cellular senescence and a potential inducer and mediator of maturation. Our research team is currently exploring the essential function of PAI-1 and the fibrinolytic system in age-related fibrotic lung conditions through pharmacological PAI-1 inhibitors. In our ongoing and future studies, we aim to further clarify the crucial function of PAI-1 in cellular senescence and its connections to the fibrinolytic system, particularly in age-associated mortality and morbidity.
