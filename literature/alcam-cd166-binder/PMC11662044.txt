## Abstract
Accumulating evidences have indicated that cancer stem cells (CSCs) can initiate tumor progression and cause recurrence after therapy. However, specific markers of gastric CSCs (GCSCs) from different origins have not been comprehensively revealed. Here, we further detected whether cell populations labelled with CD44 and Lgr5, well-recognized stem markers for gastric cancer (GC), can better emphasize cancer initiation, therapeutic resistance and recurrence. Flow cytometry was utilized to sort the CD44 + Lgr5 + and CD44 + Lgr5- cells from GC cell line HGC-27 and primary GC cells. The influences of CD44 and Lgr5 GCSCs on the malignant behaviors and their potential mechanisms was investigated, respectively. In our study, we reported the identification and validation of CD44 + Lgr5 + cells that presented stronger stemness characteristics, as evidenced by increase of sphere forming ability, elevation of stem cell transcriptional activity. Additionally, CD44 + Lgr5 + double positive cells have lower apoptosis, greater chemotherapy resistance, and higher EMT capacity and LC3 density compared with CD44 + Lgr5- cells. Tumor xenograft experiments also verified the faster carcinogenesis of CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs. Furthermore, a series of key proteins in the Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, and TGF-β pathways were elevated in the CD44 + Lgr5 + double positive subpopulation, except for Notch 1 and Smad 1. In conclusion, the binding of CD44 and Lgr5 can serve as a precise GCSCs marker that initiate malignant progression and chemotherapy resistance in GC by activating Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, TGF-β pathways. Those evidences raise the needs to target both markers simultaneously as a potential approach for the GC treatment.

## Introduction
Gastric cancer (GC) is estimated to cause 1,089,103 new patients and 768,793 deaths worldwide annually, ranking five among all tumor types (Sung et al. ). Currently, the treatment strategies for GC include comprehensive treatments based on multidisciplinary cooperation, i.e., the combination of surgery and chemotherapy (neoadjuvant chemotherapy), immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and radiotherapy (Machlowska et al. ). Due to the frequent occurrence of chemotherapy resistance and the lack of targeted therapies, the overall survival rate of gastric cancer worldwide remains at a relatively low level (Liu et al. ).
Cancer stem cells (CSCs), a small portion of tumor cells with self-renewal and differentiation capacity, serve a crux effects on occurrence, chemotherapy resistance and immune system escape of cancer (Clarke et al. ; Walcher et al. ). In GC, emerging evidence has highlighted that targeting gastric CSCs (GCSCs) can increase the treatment response (Rao et al. ). Next to the sphere formation, GCSCs can be identified using specific markers such as CD44 and its variants, CD90, ALDH1, CD133, CD166, Lgr5, Oct4, and Sox2 (Bekaii-Saab and El-Rayes ). These markers could be employed individually or in combination to evaluate the GCSCs properties. In specific, CD44 is currently the most widely-recognized surface marker for identifying GCSCs (Nosrati et al. ; Saikawa et al. ; Takaishi et al. ; Zhao et al. ); however other study has revealed that it has not high specificity for primary tumor (Rocco et al. ). Recently, emerging evidences have implied the important role of Lgr5 for identifying GCSCs (Li et al. ; Wang and Liu ). Moreover, it has been uncovered that AQP5 complements with Lgr5 to decide the fates of GCSCs (Zhao et al. ). Aforementioned findings stated that combination of CD44 and Lgr5 could better specifically identify GCSCs, as well as enhance our understanding of the stemness characteristics of GCSCs.
In addition to the re-regulation of stemness-associated genes, characteristic properties of GCSCs could be relevant with signaling pathways such as Hedgehog, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Notch, and Wnt/β-catenin (Pádua et al. ). Despite compelling findings, the molecular mechanisms underlying the maintenance of CD44 and Lgr5 positive GCSCs stemness require further investigated. Here, we demonstrated for the first time that CD44 + Lgr5 + cells sorted from GC cell line HGC-27 and primary GC cells represented stemness phenotype both in vitro and in vivo. More strikingly, it has been validated that Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, TGF-β signaling pathways were markedly enhanced. Overall, the roles of CD44 and Lgr5 in GCSCs were simultaneously investigated, which can supply novel insights into clinical targeted therapy.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture
GC cell line HGC-27 were obtained from Cell Resource Center, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Beijing, China). HGC-27 cells were cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), 100U/ml penicillin, and 100 µg/ml streptomycin (Nanjing Kaiji Biotechnology Development Co., LTD, Nanjing, China).

## Establishment of primary GC cells from fresh tumor tissues
Tumor tissues were acquired from patients who underwent gastrectomy for gastric tumors at Chinese PLA General Hospital. Informed consent was signed from patients, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (S2021-022–01). Sorted primary GC cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium with 20 ng/ml EGF and 10 ng/ml FGF (Peprotech, Suzhou, China).

## Sorting of GCSCs by flow cytometry
HGC-27 and primary GC cells were washed with phosphate buffered saline [PBS] containing 5% FBS, centrifuged at low speed for collection, and incubated with 50 µL of solution containing human cell blocking antibody (anti-human Fc, eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA) at 4 °C for 30 min. Then 50 µL of staining solution containing specific antibody was added (CD44-APC, Lgr5-PE, Biolenged, San Diego, CA, USA). Finally, the cells (1 × 106/mL) were sorted by flow cytometer (FACS Aria III, BD, San Jose, CA, USA).

## CCK-8 assay
Sorted CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells (approximately 1 × 105 per well) were seeded into 6-well plates, with a cell suspension volume of 100 µL. After 48 h of cultivation in a constant temperature incubator, CCK-8 solution (DOJINDO, Kumamoto, Japan) was added, and OD values were monitored by microplate reader (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA).

## Stem cell sphere formation assay
Sorted CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were collected and incubated into DMEM/F12 (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) with 20 ng/mL EGF, 10 ng/mL bFGF (Peprotech, Suzhou, China) and 0.4% B27 (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA). The cell suspension (approximately 2 × 103 cells) was sub-packaged in 6-well plate and then sphere cells (diameters > 100 μm) were observed after 10 days of culture. Next, the sphere cells were sieved through a 70-μm filter, and then digested, counted and plated for secondary spheroidizing culture. The ability of sphere formation was detected by microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

## Western blot assay
Total protein from CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells was extracted using RIPA buffer, and then 30 µg of protein sample was separated. After transferring the protein to PVDF membrane, primary antibodies (anti-Nanog, 1:1000, 4893; MDR1, 1:1000, 13,342; Gli1, 1:1000, 3538; Smo, 1:1000, 92,981; Notch 1, 1:1000, 3608; Notch 2, 1:1000, 5732; Notch 3, 1:1000, 5276; Notch 4, 1:1000, 2423; NICD, 1:1000, 4147; Smad 1, 1:1000, 6944; Smad 2, 1:1000, 5339; Smad 3, 1:1000, 9523; Smad 4, 1:1000, 46535; CST, Boston, MA, USA; anti-E-cadherin, 1:50, ab1416; anti-N-cadherin, 1:5000, ab76011; anti-Twist, 1:500, ab50887; anti-Snail, 1:1000, ab216347; anti-ZEB1, 1:500, ab203829; anti-Caspase 3, 1:5000, ab32351; anti-Caspase 9, 1:2000, ab32539; anti-Bcl-2, 1:1000, ab32124; anti-ERCC1, 1:2000, ab129267; anti-β-catenin, 1:5000, ab32572; anti-TCF4, 1:5000, ab217668; anti-TGF-β1, 1:1000, ab215715; anti-OCT4, 1:1000, ab181557; anti-SOX2, 1:800, ab97959; GAPDH, 1:8000, ab8245; Abcam, Shanghai, China) were incubated at 4 °C for 8–12 h. Subsequently, the membrane was incubated with goat anti-mouse IgG (M21001L, Abmart, Shanghai, China) for 60 min. Finally, enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate solution was coated onto the PVDF membranes and the gray values of target bands were analyzed by Image Analysis Software (Quantity-One, Bio-Rad, Richmond, CA, USA).

## qRT-PCR assay
Sorted CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were lysed in TRIzol reagent (Tiangen, Beijing, China). Next, cDNA was synthesized according to the instructions of PrimeScript™ RT reagent kit with GDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, Tokyo, Japan) and PCR amplification was performed using a probe kit (Premix Ex Taq™ II, TaKaRa, Tokyo, Japan). Primers were listed in Table  and the relative quantitative analysis of gene expression was analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

 

## Chemotherapy drug resistance assay
CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells (1 × 104/well) were subjected to gradient pretreatment with chemotherapy solutions of different concentrations, including 5-fluorouracil (5-FU, 0, 50, 100, 150, 200 µM), taxol (0, 50, 100, 150, 200 µM), and oxaliplatin (0, 4, 8, 12, and 16 µM) for 24 h. Among them, CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were further divided into two groups including CD44 + Lgr5 + shRNA and CD44 + Lgr5 + siRNA (transfected with control plasmid and Lgr5 siRNA, respectively). All cells were cultured in the same environment for the same duration. MTT (20 µL, 5 mg/mL) was subsequently applied for staining, and OD values were determined by microplate reader (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA). The inhibition rate was counted as follows: Inhibition rate = (OD control group—OD experimental group) / OD control group.

## Tumor xenograft experiments in nude mice
Twelve 6-week-old BALB/c nude T cell immunodeficient mice (strain code 401, Vital River, Beijing, China) were adaptively fed for 3–7 days. CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells (1 × 107 cells/mL) from primary GC cells were subcutaneously injected in the left back of each mouse. Tumor volume was approximately 50 mm3 after 10 days, and the survival state of mice was observed. After 44 d, the mice were sacrificed. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Beijing Viewsolid Biotechnology Co. LTD (VS2126A23528).

## Immunohistochemistry assay
Tumor tissues were fixed with 4% polyformaldehyde, dehydrated with ethanol and embedded in paraffin. Slices (4 μM) incised from paraffin embedded tissue were heated and quickly transferred to xylene solution for dewaxing. Then the slices were hydrated and immersed in 0.5% Triton X-100. Slides were covered with three drops of endogenous peroxidase blocking solution and then covered with two drops of 5% goat serum blocking solution. Primary antibodies (Ki67, ab15580, 1:500; Cyclin D1, ab16663, 1:250, Abcam, Shanghai, China) were incubated in the dark at 4 °C overnight. DAB solution at a dilution of 1:500 was added, with a color development time of 1 min 20 s. Hematoxylin counterstaining was performed for 2 min, and nuclei were dyed with Prussian blue dye for 2 min. The staining sections were observed under microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

## Transwell assay
CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were cultured into medium without FBS for starvation culture for 24 h. The starved cultured cells (400 µL, 1.25 × 105/mL) were slowly added into the upper chamber (i.e., the total number of cells was approximately 5 × 104), whilst, culture solution (500 µL) containing 10% FBS was added into the bottom chamber. After 48 h, the migrating and invading cells were fixed with glacial acetic acid, dyed with crystal violet, and photographed under the microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

## Apoptosis assay
Approximately 1 × 106/mL CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were added into a round-bottom flow sample tube, and then 5 µL of apoptosis detection reagent was added, fully mixed, and incubated for 15 min. After adding 5 µL of propidium iodide (PI) and 200 µL of binding solution, samples were assayed using a multi-color flow cytometer (FACS Calibur, BD, San Jose, CA, USA).

## Cell cycle analysis
CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were fixed with 75% ethanol solution at 4 °C for 6 h, followed by adding 1000 µL of PI and 100 µL of ribonuclease A (RNase A). After incubation, samples were assayed using a multi-color flow cytometer (FACS Calibur, BD, San Jose, CA, USA), and statistical analysis software for cell cycle progression (ModFit LT, Software house) was used to sort and analyze the experimental results.

## CYTO-ID autophagy test
To detect autophagy in target cells, CYTO-ID autophagy detection kit (Enzo Life Sciences, Farmingdale, NY, USA) was utilized. To be specific, CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were digested, centrifuged, mixed with Cyto-ID solution, and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark. Blue fluorescent nuclear dye was added, and the cells were washed to remove unbound dye in the free state. Autophagy was observed under a laser confocal microscope (Zeiss, Jena, Germany).

## Statistical analysis
Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and statistical analysis was performed using SPSS22.0 software and GraphPad Prism 5 software. The t-test was used for to compare of each group, with P < 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

## Results

## CD44 + Lgr5 + GC cell populations displayed greater stemness-like characteristics
Microscopic observation showed that HGC-27 and the primary GC cells were in good growth conditions, with smooth morphologies and no impurities or microbial infections (Fig. A). Positive subpopulations of CD44 and Lgr5 GCSCs from gastric cancer cell line HGC-27 and primary gastric cancer cells were successfully sorted by flow cytometry (Fig.  A and B). Western blotting also verified the levels of CD44 and Lgr5 in the sorted populations (CD44 + Lgr5 + and CD44 + Lgr5-) (Fig.  C). The obtained CD44 + Lgr5- and CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were cultured continuously, and the cell growth states were photographed and recorded (Fig. B and C). Regarding cell morphology, we found that CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs differed from CD44 + Lgr5- cells, with a higher nucleus to cytoplasm ratio, significant nucleus, one or more nucleoli, scattered chromatin, and few organelles in the cytoplasm except for free ribosomes.
 Additionally, CCK-8 results suggested that the OD value of CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs was higher than that of CD44 + Lgr5- single positive cells (P < 0.05) (Fig.  D). In line, sphere forming assay showed that the CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were plump and concentrated, with larger diameters and more cells counts compared with the CD44 + Lgr5- subset (Fig.  E-G). These evidences indicated that CD44 + Lgr5 + GC cell populations displayed greater stemness-like characteristics than CD44 + Lgr5- subsets. Furthermore, spheroid monolayers were cultured from 1 to 7 days to re-differentiate. Results demonstrated that the Lgr5 level was gradually decreased with the CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs differentiation time extended, supporting the ability of asymmetric division in CSCs (Fig. ).

## CD44 + Lgr5 + GC cell populations displayed greater stemness-related transcriptional activity and epithelia-mesenchymal transition potential
As displayed in Fig.  A-D, the protein levels of stemness-related transcriptional markers including OCT4, SOX2, and Nanog were notably higher in CD44 + Lgr5 + populations than those in CD44 + Lgr5- cells (P < 0.05). In terms of OCT4, SOX2, and Nanog mRNA expressions, qRT-PCR results were in conformity with the trend results of western blot (Fig.  E-G).
 Chemotherapy resistance results determined that the absorbance values in the all groups were progressively declined with increased concentrations of chemotherapeutic drugs (oxaliplatin, taxol, and 5-FU), reflecting a progressive decline in the number of surviving GCSCs. When the concentrations of oxaliplatin, taxol, and 5-FU were highest, the survival rate in the CD44 + Lgr5 + group was greater than that of CD44 + Lgr5- group (P < 0.05); whilst, silencing of Lgr5 caused a decrease in surviving tumor stem cells in the CD44 + Lgr5 + group (Fig.  H-K). Correspondingly, the inhibitory rates of all groups were notably elevated, with the increase of chemotherapy concentrations. Among all cancer stem-like subsets, CD44 + Lgr5 + CSCs from primary GC cells displayed the strongest resistance, followed by CD44 + Lgr5 + CSCs from HGC-27 cells. Compared with CD44 + Lgr5 + cells, CD44 + Lgr5- cells represented the lower cell viability after treatment with oxaliplatin, taxol, and 5-FU (P < 0.05) (Fig.  L-N).
To further determine the relationship between CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs and EMT progress, western blot and qPCR assays were performed. Results demonstrated that GCSCs suspensions from primary GC cells and HGC-27 cells were more enriched in N-cadherin, Twist, Snail, and ZEB1 than those of adherent cells, while were less E-cadherin expression (P < 0.05) (Fig. A- F). Intriguingly, N-cadherin, Twist, Snail, and ZEB1 were overexpressed in CD44 + Lgr5 + double positive GCSCs compared with in CD44 + Lgr5- cells. Finally, qPCR experiments focusing on the aforementioned transcription factors showed similar results (Fig. G- K).
 

## CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs had stronger tumorigenesis, and proliferation abilities in vivo and in vitro
To explore whether CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs possessed different tumorigenic and proliferative capabilities, we use a subcutaneous tumor xenograft model, which showed that the average size of tumors generated by CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs was dramatically larger than those generated by CD44 + Lgr5- cells (P < 0.05) (Fig.  A-D). Body weights were not memorably different between the two groups (P > 0.05) (Fig.  E). Immunohistochemical staining of xenograft tumors demonstrated that the protein levels of Ki-67 and Cyclin D1 were remarkably elevated in CD44 + Lgr5 + group than those in CD44 + Lgr5- group (P < 0.05) (Fig. F-I).
In vitro, transwell assay stated that CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs had higher migration ability than CD44 + Lgr5- population (P < 0.05) (Fig.  J, L). Similarly, the proportion of cells in S phase was significantly increased in CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs compared with the CD44 + Lgr5- GCSCs (Fig.  K, M).

## CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs displayed lower apoptosis, higher autophagy and greater chemotherapy resistance
As demonstrated in Fig.  A, B, the apoptotic proportion of CD44 + Lgr5 + cells was observably lower than that of CD44 + Lgr5- cells (P < 0.05). Consistently, lower apoptosis was also evidenced by the increase of Bcl-2 level and decreases of Caspase 3 and Caspase 9 levels in CD44 + Lgr5 + cells (P < 0.05) (Fig.  C-F). Immunofluorescence staining was applied to detect autophagy, and data demonstrated that CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs exhibited stronger nuclear LC3 protein accumulation, which was more obvious in primary GCSCs (Fig.  G). Moreover, CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs had higher levels of the resistance-related proteins including ERCC1 and MDR1 by comparison with CD44 + Lgr5- GCSCs, when these cells were treated with oxaliplation and 5-FU (P < 0.05) (Fig.  H-K). These transcriptional changes in ERCC1 and MRD1 were confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig.  L, M). To further uncover the association between Lgr5 expression and chemotherapy resistance, silencing Lgr5 was conducted. Results stated that the levels of ERCC1 and MRD1 were remarkably decreased in CD44 + Lgr5 + siRNA-Lgr5 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. ).
 

## CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs activate the Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, and TGF-β pathways, and exhibited greater stemness phenotypes
Several fundamental signaling pathways are known to participate in the maintenance of stemness characteristics in CSCs. Herein, classical pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin, Hedgehog/Gli1, Notch, and TGF-β/Smad signaling were examined in both CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs and CD44 + Lgr5- cells. As a result, overexpression of β-catenin and TCF4 were observed in CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs, as well as Gli1 and Smo (P < 0.05) (Fig.  A-F). In terms of the Notch pathway, all Notch proteins and Notch intercellular domain (NICD) were elevated in CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs in addition to Notch 1 protein (Fig.  G-L). Moreover, TGF/β components including TGF-β1 and Smad 2, 3, and 4 were increased in GCSCs compared with CD44 + Lgr5- cells (P < 0.05) (Fig.  M-R).
 

## Discussion
It’s well known that identifying GCSCs and targeting these cells are considered to be important for comprehending the cancer etiology and improving the clinical efficacy of therapies (Bekaii-Saab and El-Rayes ; Rao et al. ). However, the standard procedure and ‘gold-standard’ surface markers for identifying GCSCs are still under debate. In this study, we identified and validated that the CD44 + Lgr5 + population derived from primary GC cells and HGC-27 represented greater stemness phenotype, as evidenced by increase of sphere forming ability, elevation of stem cell transcriptional activity. At the functional level, CD44 combined with Lgr5 can synergistically accelerate carcinogenesis. Those evidences determined that CD44 + Lgr5 + is an important tumor-initiating population and may serve as a precise GCSCs marker.
CD44 is a broadly present transmembrane glycoprotein that co-ordinate adhesion and signaling events (Ponta et al. ; Thorne et al. ). In the relationship between CD44 and CSCs, Takaishi et al. (Takaishi et al. ) have first proved that the subpopulation of CD44 + cells displayed the stem cell properties of self-renewal. Lgr5 was originally isolated and identified in 1998 by Hsu et al. (Hsu et al. ), and further research has shown that Lgr5 is highly correlated with the malignant behaviors of tumors such as colorectal cancer (Kemper et al. ; McClanahan et al. ; Takahashi et al. ), liver cancer (Cao et al. ), and esophageal cancer (Yoon et al. ). Of note, lineage tracing in a transgenic mouse model demonstrated that Lgr5 + stem cells were probably the origin of intestinal-type gastric cancer (Li et al. ), implicating its importance in identifying gastric CSCs. In the current study, purity of sorted CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs was more than 90%. Sphere forming assay verified that the CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs displayed greater stemness-like characteristics than CD44 + Lgr5- subsets. Additionally, CD44 + Lgr5 + double positive cells have lower apoptosis, and higher proliferation, migration, and EMT capacity and faster carcinogenesis, by comparison with CD44 + Lgr5- cells. Those evidences determined that the binding of CD44 and Lgr5 serves as a precise GCSCs marker for initiating malignant progression.
On the other hand, CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs presented greater chemotherapy resistance, compared with CD44 + Lgr5- GCSCs. To further investigate the roles of Lgr5, silencing of Lgr5 in CD44 + Lgr5 + cells were performed and results displayed that cell viability in the CD44 + Lgr5 + siRNA-Lgr5 groups were obviously decreased compared with CD44 + Lgr5 + group when these were treated with the oxaliplation, Taxol, and 5-FU. Those results supported the crux effects of Lgr5 + GCSCs on chemotherapy resistance. In line, Hou et al. (Hou et al. ) have understood the influences of Lgr5 HCC-CSCs on chemo-resistance. Another similar studies have demonstrated that Lgr5 accelerate CSCs traits and chemoresistance (Cao et al. ; Liu et al. ). As far as we know, autophagy is required to maintain the CSCs stemness in various tumor types (Lei et al. ; Li et al. ). CD44, has been shown to sensitize chemoresistance by targeting autophagy (Praharaj et al. ). In the current study, we also noticed that CD44 coordinates with Lgr5 to facilitate the autophagy compared with alone CD44 GCSCs, as determined by elevation of LC3 level. Thus, we postulated that CD44 coordinating with Lgr5 serve biological effects on GCSCs including autophagy and chemotherapy resistance.
Furthermore, signaling such as Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, and TGF-β pathways were activated in the CD44 + Lgr5 + double positive subpopulation. Consistently, prior studies have presented these signaling involves in the activation of regeneration function of stem cells. In specific, Wnt signaling pathway is a pathway associated with the Lgr5 protein in gastrointestinal tumors that has been intensively studied (Fu et al. ; Reya and Clevers ). Similar study has uncovered that Lgr5, a novel CSC marker, facilitate the glioma progression via activation Wnt/β-catenin pathway (Zhang et al. ). Additionally, Hedgehog pathway is activated in various tumors, which is related to proliferation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis of gastric tumor (Akyala and Peppelenbosch ). With regards to stemness, Bigagli et al. (Bigagli et al. ) have demonstrated the roles of stemness-associated genes including Lgr5 and pathways such as Hedgehog, Notch signaling. Besides, Varlı et al. (Varlı et al. ) have also confirmed the Hedgehog and Notch pathways are the key mechanisms underlying stemness activity. TGF-β pathway, as another crux mechanism on cancer development, prior study has clarified that TGF-β1/Smad pathway involves in the regulation of stemness of CSCs and mesenchymal properties including GC (Chen et al. ; Leng et al. ; Ni et al. ; Peng et al. ). Importantly, we also observed that CD44 + Lgr5 + GCSCs activate the Notch signaling pathway through the Notch2, Notch3, and Notch4 receptors, independent of Notch1 receptor; and up-regulate the TGF-β1 expression via Smad2/3/4 transcription complex. Overall, Wnt, Hedgehog, Notch, and TGF-β pathways may be main pathways supporting GCSCs function.
Only verifying the effect of silencing Lgr5 on GCSCs resistance is a limitation of the current study. In future, whether silencing Lgr5 alters other GCSCs properties will be further investigated. Additionally, various GC cell lines will be chosen to verify conclusions in this study.
In conclusion, our study demonstrated that CD44 together with Lgr5, could be a potential biomarker for identifying GCSCs, as determined by greater tumorgenicity, chemotherapy resistance, and activation of stemness-related pathways. Further research confirming Lgr5 and CD44 as GCSCs markers may facilitate the development of GC targeted therapy.
