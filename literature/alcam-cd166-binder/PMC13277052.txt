## Abstract

## Introduction
While intratumor heterogeneity (ITH) is recognized as a fundamental driver of treatment resistance and disease progression in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), existing prognostic models often fail to quantify ITH as a biomarker robustly.

## Methods
This study obtained a training set from the METABRIC, while validation sets were sourced from the GEO database. ITH scores were calculated using the DEPTH2 algorithm, a computational method to infer heterogeneity from bulk transcriptomic data. Patients were stratified into distinct high-ITH and low-ITH groups based on these scores. Ten machine learning algorithms were integrated to generate 101 modeling approaches in the training set. The StepCox[backward] + GBM model, incorporating seven ITH-related signature (IRS) genes (IRF8, MAGEA4, IL18R1, IL7R, TBC1D10C, TEX261, and CD6), was selected as the optimal prognostic model. Then, we validated the cellular distribution and intercellular communication networks of core genes by single-cell sequencing data. Finally, quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed on matched TNBC tumor and adjacent normal tissues to experimentally validate the differential expression of the IRS genes.

## Results
The IRS model demonstrated robust prediction of patient overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in three independent cohorts. Patients in the high IRS score group showed worse prognosis, elevated Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) scores, lower Microsatellite Instability (MSI) scores, reduced expression of immune checkpoints, lower immune cell infiltration. And high IRS score was associated with poorer immunotherapy response in the GSE194040, GSE173839, GSE124821, and IMvigor210. The high and low-IRS score groups also exhibited differences in related functions and pathways. The high-risk group was significantly enriched in metabolic pathways, particularly N-Glycan biosynthesis and O-Glycan biosynthesis. Conversely, the low-risk group was predominantly associated with robust immune-related pathways, including adaptive immune response and immune response-regulating cell surface receptor signaling pathways. The IRS core genes were specifically distributed in CD8 + T cells and macrophages, and the interaction networks between CD8 + T cells and macrophages in the GALECTIN and CD45 signaling pathways were elucidated. Drug sensitivity analysis showed that the high-risk group was more sensitive to chemotherapeutic agents, especially to poly ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP)inhibitors. qRT-PCR validation confirmed the differential expression of seven critical genes between TNBC and adjacent normal tissues.

## Conclusion
Our study established an IRS model integrating machine learning and single-cell RNA sequencing. This model improves the accuracy of prognosis prediction for TNBC patients and effectively distinguishes risk subgroups with different immune microenvironment characteristics and treatment responses, providing preliminary data for precision medicine for TNBC.

## Introduction
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is the most aggressive and poorly prognostic subtype of breast cancer(BC), with a high risk of recurrence and significantly lower survival than other subtypes [ ]. Although emerging therapies, such as immune-checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), have brought hope to some patients, the high Heterogeneity and the complex tumor microenvironment (TME) of TNBC have led to significant differences in individual response to treatment [ , ]. Hence, there is a pressing demand to develop accurate prognostic tools to guide stratified treatment.
Lehmann et al. classified TNBC into six main subtypes through transcriptomic profiling: Basal-like1(BL1), Basal-like 2(BL2), Immunomodulatory(IM), Luminal androgen receptor(LAR), Mesenchymal(M), and Mesenchymal stem-like(MSL) [ ]. Each subtype exhibits distinct biological characteristics and clinical outcomes. For example, the Basal-like 1 subtype has a higher pathological complete response rate to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and better overall survival. Burstein et al. identified four main subtypes based on RNA expression and DNA copy number: Basal-like immune-activated, Basal-like immune-suppressed, Luminal androgen receptor, and Mesenchymal [ ]. Among these, the Basal-like immune-activated subtype showed the most optimal survival outcomes, while the Basal-like immune-suppressed subtype had the least favorable prognosis. Additionally, based on genomic and transcriptomic analyses of Chinese patients, Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC) performed molecular typing of TNBC [ , ]. This typing integrated transcriptomic features of mRNA and Long non-coding RNA(lncRNA) and was able to accurately classify TNBC into BLIS, IM, LAR, and MES subtypes. Consistent with Lehmann’s classification, the IM subtype exhibited amplification of cytokine signaling, immune cell signaling, and TILs, and significant activation of adaptive immune system-related pathways, including interferon-γ-related pathways.
Intratumor heterogeneity (ITH) is a core factor driving tumor evolution, therapeutic resistance, and immune escape [ ]. It refers to the coexistence of distinct cellular subpopulations with diverse molecular and phenotypic profiles within a single tumor. This heterogeneity manifests across multiple dimensions, including the genetic (e.g., somatic mutations, copy number alterations), epigenetic (e.g., DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility), transcriptomic (gene expression profiles), and functional (e.g., pathway activities) levels [ ]. TNBC exhibited substantial ITH as evidenced by both single-cell and spatial transcriptomics sequencing analyses [ , ]. Meanwhile, compared with other subtypes of BC, TNBC has more significant spatial subclonal diversification and higher ITH scores [ , ]. High ITH is often associated with subclonal competition, microenvironmental remodeling, and poor prognosis [ ]. Existing methods for ITH quantification mostly rely on genomic variation or transcriptomic diversity, exhibiting limited clinical applicability. In addition, traditional prognostic models are often limited to a single set of omics data, making it difficult to fully explore the molecular complexity of TNBC, resulting in insufficient predictive efficacy.
In this study, we applied machine learning algorithms to develop an ITH-related signature (IRS) prognostic model for TNBC. Then, we validated the cellular distribution and intercellular communication networks of IRS-related genes by single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. Furthermore, we assessed the associations between IRS and the tumor immune microenvironment, immunotherapeutic response, and chemotherapeutic drug sensitivity. Finally, we validated the expression of vital genes in TNBC patient tissues. The IRS model offers a reliable prognostic tool for TNBC with a simplified seven-gene model, which can quickly identify high-risk patients to guide treatment. The single-cell interaction network reveals the communication hub role of macrophages and CD8 + T cells, laying the foundation for combination therapies targeting the tumor microenvironment. ## Materials and methods

## TNBC data acquisition and pretreatment
The METABRIC dataset was accessed through the cBioPortal platform (https://www.cbioportal.org/). After excluding samples with incomplete information, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data and corresponding clinical information from 320 TNBC patients were retained for analysis as the training set. Validation sets were sourced from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), including 101 TNBC patients from the GSE58812 cohort and 85 TNBC patients from the GSE21653. The baseline demographic and clinicopathological characteristics of all cohorts are detailed in Supplementary Table . The age distributions were broadly consistent across the TNBC cohorts, with median ages ranging from 56 to 57. All RNA-seq data and clinical records underwent standardized pretreatment to ensure analytical consistency. To validate the predictive efficacy of the IRS for immunotherapy response, we incorporated immunotherapy cohorts from TNBC patients and murine models, including GSE194040 (n = 29), GSE173839 (n = 21), and GSE124821 (n = 202), as well as the IMvigor210 cohort of 298 patients with metastatic urothelial carcinoma (UC).

## ITH score and survival analysis
ITH scores of METABRIC-TNBC were calculated using the DEPTH2 algorithm [ ], which evaluates ITH levels from bulk RNA sequencing data based on the standard deviations of absolute z-scored transcriptome levels within the sample. The higher the DEPTH2 score, the greater the transcriptional heterogeneity. The optimal stratification threshold was established using the“surv_cutpoint” function, categorizing patients into high- and low-ITH subgroups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves for overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were analyzed with log-rank tests (significance: p < 0.05). To explore whether there are differences in ITH scores between different Lehmann subtypes, we subtyped the METABRIC-TNBC samples according to the Lehmann molecular subtyping tool (TNBCtype online tool, http://cbc.mc.vanderbilt.edu/tnbc/),which categorizes TNBC into six molecular subtypes(BL1, BL2, M, MSL, LAR, and IM). Additionally, the category “UNS”(unclassified) was used to represent patients in our dataset that could not be matched to any of the defined subtypes. Nonparametric statistical analyses were used to correlate ITH scores with clinical characteristics.

## Construction of the IRS prognostic model based on machine learning
The whole METABRIC-TNBC dataset served as the training cohort; two independent datasets, GSE58812 and GSE21653-TNBC, were used for external validation. In the training set, a total of 43 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected through the execution of differential expression analysis based on the “ limma” R package (|logFC| > 1.5, p < 0.05) for high and low ITH score groups. Firstly, we corrected for batch effects using the ComBat algorithm (“sva” R package) and took the intersection of the training and validation RNA-seq datasets, then we screened 28 genes significantly associated with TNBC prognosis by univariate Cox regression analysis (p < 0.01). Subsequently, 10 algorithms, including Ridge, Lasso, generalized boosted regression modeling (GBM), CoxBoost, stepwise Cox, random survival forest (RSF), elastic net (Enet), supervised principal components (SuperPC), partial least squares regression for Cox (plsRcox), and survival support vector machine (survival-SVM), were integrated to generate 101 kinds of modeling approaches in the METABRIC-TNBC training set through algorithm cross-combination, while feature optimization and predictive model development based on ten-fold cross-validation were completed. All models were validated in the GSE58812 and GSE21653-TNBC cohorts. Based on validation set robustness and clinical utility, StepCox[backward] + GBM was ultimately used to construct the prognostic model. StepCox[backward] screened out seven hub genes by stepwise elimination of the least significant variables (p > 0.05), and GBM optimized the log loss function by iteratively fitting a decision tree to output risk scores. The median risk score was employed as the stratification threshold to classify patients into high- and low-risk subgroups.

## Tumor immune microenvironment and immunotherapy
To compare the expression of immune checkpoint genes between the high and low-risk subgroups, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied. Functional enrichment of immune-related pathways was quantified via single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA), implemented in the R package “GSVA”. Gene sets for immune cell types and functional terms were curated from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). The stromal and immune cell infiltration levels were estimated using the ESTIMATE algorithm [ ] (R package“estimate”), which calculated ImmuneScore, StromalScore, and TumorPurity based on gene expression profiles. Since direct ICIs response data for the TNBC cohort were unavailable, to infer tumor immune evasion and forecast clinical responses to ICIs, we utilized the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) portal(http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) to determine the TIDE score and microsatellite instability (MSI) status. A higher TIDE score or lower MSI status indicates a greater likelihood of immune escape and poor response to immunotherapy [ ].

## Single-cell transcriptomic profiling and cellular interaction mapping
ScRNA-seq data from nine patients (GSE176078 dataset) were processed using the “Seurat” R package. Cells were retained for downstream analysis only if they met all quality control thresholds: unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts between 250 and 40,000, the detection of 200 to 6,000 genes per cell, and a mitochondrial gene percentage below 20%. From an initial pool of 38,582 cells, 37,584 high-quality cells passed these filters. Following data normalization and the selection of highly variable genes, we performed principal component analysis (PCA) on the 2,000 most variable genes. We used the “Harmony” package to mitigate patient-derived batch effects in the data. Cells were subjected to clustering analysis using the “FindClusters” and “FindNeighbors” functions(resolution: 0.1), and the results were then visualized through the “UMAP” method. Twelve major cell types were annotated based on canonical markers (Supplementary Table ). IRS-associated core genes were mapped across cell types using log-normalized expression values. To infer intercellular communication networks, ligand-receptor (L-R) interactions were analyzed with the “CellChat” R package.

## Drug sensitivity analysis
Drug sensitivity analysis was performed using the “oncoPredict” R package, which provides in silico estimates of half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) by correlating tumor gene expression profiles with drug response data of cancer cell lines from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer(GDSC) [ ]. The analysis focused on chemotherapeutic agents relevant to contemporary TNBC treatment, including platinum agents (Cisplatin, Oxaliplatin), taxanes (Paclitaxel, Docetaxel), PARP inhibitors (Olaparib, etc.), and other standard drugs (e.g., Gemcitabine, 5-Fluorouracil).

## Quantitative real-time PCR
Surgical tissue samples were obtained from 40 female patients diagnosed with TNBC at the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University. Their age ranged from 35 to 72 years (average 54.8 years). The total RNA from the samples was extracted using FreeZol reagent (R711, Vazyme, China). Subsequently, the samples were reverse transcribed into cDNA using the HiScript III RT SuperMix kit (R323, Vazyme, China). Finally, the relative expression of genes was determined by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) on a Bio-Rad CFX96 system (Bio-Rad, USA) utilizing the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711, Vazyme, China). The primer sequences are delineated in Supplementary Table .

## Statistical analysis
All analyses were implemented using R software (v4.4.0). The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was conducted with the Log-rank test to assess significant differences in survival curves. For comparing differences between the two groups, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was utilized. Categorical data were analyzed using the Chi‑square test or Fisher’s exact test. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

## Results

## The ITH score of TNBC cases
The ITH scores of the METABRIC-TNBC cohort are shown in Supplementary Table . TNBC patients were stratified into high- and low-ITH subgroups using the optimal cutoff value determined by the “surv_cutpoint” function. Survival analysis showed that TNBC patients with high ITH scores had worse DFS and OS compared with those with low ITH scores (Fig.  A, p = 0.003, Fig.  B, p < 0.001). Clinical correlation analysis revealed that patients over the age of 65 exhibited significantly higher ITH scores when compared with patients under the age of 65. (Supplementary Fig.  A, p = 0.033). ITH scores were higher in patients with Grade 1 than in patients with Grades 2 and 3 (Supplementary Fig.  B, p1−2 = 0.0018, p1−3 = 0.0023). There was no difference in ITH scores among patients in terms of T, N, and Stage(Supplementary Fig.  C-E). We further investigated the relationship between ITH and the Lehmann molecular subtypes in the METABRIC cohort. Our analysis revealed significant differences in ITH scores across subtypes (Supplementary Fig.  F), with the mesenchymal stem-like(MSL) subtype exhibiting the highest heterogeneity, and conversely, the immunomodulatory(IM) subtype exhibiting the lowest heterogeneity(p = 0.004).
 We obtained 43 differentially expressed genes(DEGs) between the high and low ITH score groups (|logFC|>1.5, p < 0.05). After cross-validation of METABRIC-TNBC with GSE58812 and GSE21653-TNBC datasets, we screened out 38 shared genes. We further performed univariate Cox regression analysis with DFS as the endpoint event, and finally identified 28 DEGs significantly associated with TNBC prognosis (Fig.  C).

## Construction of a prognostic IRS based on machine learning
Based on 28 prognosis-related genes in TNBC, this study developed an IRS model composed of 101 machine learning algorithms (Fig.  A). After training and evaluating the predictive model using a tenfold cross-validation framework, we observed that the top five models with the highest C-index values exhibited excellent performance in the training set (mean C-index = 0.92) but showed a significant drop in the validation set (mean C-index = 0.64). Thus, these models that overfitted the training set were excluded. Further analysis found that the GBM model (28 feature genes) and the StepCox[backward] + GBM model (7 feature genes) both maintained robust predictive efficiency during multiple validations. Although the predictive accuracy of both models was similar, considering the clinical need for model simplicity and interpretability, the StepCox[backward] + GBM model with simplified features was ultimately selected as the optimal translational model. The median risk score, derived from the model, was employed as the cutoff to classify patients into high-risk and low-risk subgroups.
 The StepCox[backward] algorithm identified seven core prognostic genes(IRF8, MAGEA4, IL18R1, IL7R, TBC1D10C, TEX261, and CD6) through backward stepwise elimination (Fig.  B). Consistent with model predictions, Adverse events in patients are more concentrated in the high-risk group (Fig.  C). In the METABRIC-TNBC cohort, high-risk group patients have worse DFS and OS compared to low-risk group patients (Fig.  D, pDFS<0.001, pOS<0.001). In the GSE58812 cohort, Fig.  E showed that the DFS and OS of patients in the high-risk subgroup are much shorter than those in the low-risk group (pDFS<0.001, pOS=0.001). Similar patterns were observed in the GSE21653-TNBC cohort (Fig.  F, p = 0.005).

## Verification of the IRS model
To verify whether IRS is an independent prognostic factor for TNBC, we performed univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses on DFS and OS in each independent cohort. In the METABRIC-TNBC cohort, Fig.  A demonstrated that IRS was a significant risk factor for DFS in both univariate (HR 2.532, 95% CI 2.097–3.056, p < 0.001) and multivariate analyses (HR 2.377, 95% CI 1.955–2.890, p < 0.001). IRS was also significantly associated with OS in both univariate (HR 2.029, 95% CI 1.729–2.381, p < 0.001) and multivariate analyses (HR 1.909, 95% CI 1.613–2.260, p < 0.001) (Fig.  B). The larger HR values of IRS compared to other clinical characteristics highlight its stronger independent predictive ability for TNBC. In the GSE58812 cohort, Fig.  C showed IRS was significantly linked to DFS in univariate (HR 1.644, 95% CI 1.166–2.317, p = 0.005) and multivariate analyses (HR 1.513, 95% CI 1.076–2.128, p = 0.017). Figure  D revealed IRS remained significantly associated with OS in univariate (HR 1.626, 95% CI 1.140–2.318, p = 0.007) and multivariate analyses (HR 1.456, 95% CI 1.028–2.063, p = 0.035). In addition, IRS remained an independent prognostic factor for DFS in the GSE21653-TNBC cohort (Fig.  E, HR 1.786, CI 1.214–2.627, p = 0.003).
 To assess the predictive efficacy of the model, we systematically analyzed the predictive ability of DFS at different time points. Based on each independent dataset, the ROC (Supplementary Fig.  ) was constructed for 2, 3and 5-year DFS, and the AUC was calculated. The results showed that the AUC values of the METABRIC-TNBC cohort at 2, 3, and 5 years were 0.778, 0.789, and 0.798, respectively, and the AUC values at the corresponding time points of the GSE58812 cohort were 0.665, 0.664, and 0.663. The AUC values for the GSE21653-TNBC cohort were 0.745, 0.730, and 0.664 (Fig.  A). Comparative analysis of prognostic prediction performance revealed that IRS demonstrated a significant advantage in most clinical variables, including Age, Grade, Stage, T, N, tumour mutation burden(TMB), adjuvant chemotherapy(ACT), Nottingham prognostic index(NPI), and Radiotherapy (except for a lower AUC value for IRS compared to Age in GSE58812) (Fig.  B-D).
 

## Construction and validation of a nomogram
To enhance the clinical applicability of IRS, a nomogram was developed based on independent prognostic determinants screened in multivariate analyses, including Stage, T, N, ACT, NPI, and IRS (Fig.  E). The model showed excellent discriminant efficacy in predicting 2, 3, and 5-year DFS, with AUC values of 0.742, 0.814, and 0.800, respectively (Fig.  F). The calibration curves further confirmed that the nomogram showed good agreement with actual observations at each time point (Fig.  G). In summary, the IRS-Integrated nomogram provided a reliable and accurate tool for personalized prognostic prediction in TNBC.

## Revealing the IRS-mediated molecular mechanisms in TNBC
GSEA using GO gene sets revealed distinct functional profiles between risk groups. The high-risk group exhibited significant enrichment in ncRNA processing, ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, and ribosome biogenesis (Fig.  A). Conversely, the low-risk group displayed strong associations with immune functions, particularly adaptive immune response, cell surface receptor signaling regulating immunity, and lymphocyte-mediated immunity (Fig.  B). Pathway analysis further demonstrated high-risk enrichment in biosynthesis pathways, including N-Glycan and O-Glycan synthesis, along with folate-mediated one-carbon metabolism (Fig.  C). Meanwhile, low-risk signatures correlated with cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, and natural killer cell cytotoxicity pathways (Fig.  D).
 

## Correlation of IRS with the tumor immune microenvironment and immunotherapy
Subsequent analysis revealed that the expression of immune checkpoint genes was significantly up-regulated in the low-risk group (Fig.  A, p < 0.05). Assessment of the tumor microenvironment via the ESTIMATE algorithm revealed that the low-risk cohort demonstrated elevated immune and stromal scores. Conversely, the high-risk group exhibited characteristics of immune evasion, including a higher TIDE score, lower MSI score, and reduced immune infiltration (Fig.  B-E, p < 0.05). To evaluate the value of the IRS in predicting immunotherapy efficacy, we analyzed IRS scores across four independent immunotherapy cohorts. In the TNBC (GSE194040) cohort, patients who achieved pathological complete response (pCR) exhibited significantly lower IRS scores than non-pCR patients, and the pCR rate was significantly higher in the low-risk subgroup. (Fig.  G, p < 0.05). Similar results were observed in the GSE173839 cohort (Fig.  H, p < 0.05). In the GSE124821 cohort, TNBC mice with immunotherapy resistance exhibited higher IRS scores, and the immunotherapy response rate in the high-risk subgroup was significantly lower (Fig.  I, p < 0.001). In the IMvigor210 UC cohort, patients with progressive disease/stable disease (PD/SD) had significantly higher IRS scores than those with complete response/partial response(CR/PR), and high IRS scores were associated with lower immunotherapy response rates (Fig.  F, p < 0.05). These findings indicate that patients with lower IRS scores are more likely to benefit from immunotherapy.
 

## Multidimensional association of IRS with single-cell features
Given that the IRS signature was significantly associated with immune microenvironment status, we further performed single-cell RNA sequencing analysis to dissect the cellular distribution and functional context of IRS core genes. In this study, 12 cell types were identified by UMAP, including epithelial cells, fibroblasts, plasmablasts, dendritic cells (DC), myoepithelial cells, CD8 + T cells, macrophages, B-cells, smooth muscle cells (SMC), endothelial cells, cancer stem cells (CSCs), and Tprolif (Fig.  A). The top 3 marker genes of each cell subpopulation were visualized by bubble map (Fig.  B), with the complete top 5 marker gene list provided in Supplementary Table S5. Single-cell RNA sequencing further revealed the specific distribution of IRS core genes(Fig.  C). TBC1D10C, IL7R, IL18R1, and CD6 were significantly enriched in CD8 + T cells, whereas IRF8 and TEX261 were predominantly highly expressed in macrophages. Notably, while MAGEA4 is a known cancer-testis antigen, its overall expression in the single-cell dataset was low. Our results suggest that the high or low IRS score is likely driven by these specific cell subsets. Based on the cell communication network, the strength and number of interactions among 12 cell types showed significant heterogeneity in the TNBC tumor microenvironment (Fig.  D).
 When we focused on the interaction between CD8 + T cells and macrophages, we observed that the communication strength between CD8 + T cells and macrophages was most prominent when CD8 + T cells were the core nodes. In the interaction network dominated by macrophages, in addition to CD8 + T cells, their interaction with DC and Tprolif was significantly enhanced (Fig.  E). As illustrated in Fig.  F, GALECTIN signaling contributed the most in the interaction between CD8 + T cells and macrophages when macrophages were the sender, while when CD8 + T cells were the sender, the CD45 signaling pathway dominated. The above pathway-interaction network was further visualized by a circle diagram (Fig.  G). After calculating the contribution of each L-R pair to the signaling pathway, we discovered that the communication between CD8 + T cells and macrophages mainly depended on specific L-R pairs, including LGALS9-CD44 and PTPRC-MRC1 (Supplementary Fig. ).

## Analysis of the correlation between IRS and the sensitivity of TNBC chemotherapy drugs and validation of model genes
Based on drug sensitivity analysis, the high and low-risk groups showed significant heterogeneity in response to chemotherapeutic agents. The high-risk group showed significantly increased sensitivity to 5-Fluorouracil (Fig.  A, p = 6.6e-12), Gemcitabine (Fig.  B, p < 2.2e-16), Cisplatin (Fig.  C, p < 2.2e-16), Oxaliplatin (Fig.  D, p = 3e-12), Docetaxel (Fig.  E, p = 4.2e-12), Paclitaxel (Fig.  F, p = 2.6e-06), Vinorelbine (Fig.  J, p = 1.3e-11), Epirubicin (Fig.  K, p < 2.2e-16) and Cyclophosphamide (Fig.  L, p = 1e-11). The sensitivity of PARP inhibitors Niraparib (Fig.  G, p = 8.9e-15), Olaparib (Fig.  H, p = 5.3e-16), and Talazoparib (Fig.  I, p < 2.2e-16) was typically decreased in the low-risk group, which may indicate a potential DNA repair defect phenotype.
 qRT-PCR was employed to evaluate the differential expression of seven critical genes ( IRF8, MAGEA4, IL18R1, IL7R, TBC1D10C, TEX261, and CD6) between TNBC and adjacent normal tissues. Cancer tissues showed significant upregulation of MAGEA4, TBC1D10C, IL7R, and TEX261, while normal tissues exhibited elevated IL18R1 expression. No statistically significant difference was observed for CD6 or IRF8 between the two groups (Fig.  M–S).

## Discussion
In early-stage TNBC, precise risk stratification guides adjuvant chemotherapy intensity. In metastatic TNBC, biomarkers are needed to select agents beyond conventional immunotherapy. However, precise treatment strategies face significant challenges due to TNBC’s profound molecular heterogeneity and dynamic tumor microenvironment (TME). Existing prognostic models often fail to: [ ] ITH as a quantitative biomarker [ ], capture immune evasion mechanisms linked to therapy resistance, and [ ] dynamically predict immunotherapy benefit.
To address these gaps, this study employed the DEPTH2 algorithm to quantify ITH in TNBC. This algorithm calculates a score based on the whole-transcriptome data of bulk tumor tissue, which reflects the global transcriptional diversity arising from all cellular components [ ]. Based on ITH scores, patients in the METABRIC-TNBC cohort were stratified into high- and low- ITH groups, and DEGs between the two groups were identified. Using these ITH-associated DEGs, we constructed an IRS prognostic model using machine learning and validated its robust predictive performance for OS and DFS in two independent cohorts (GSE58812 and GSE21653-TNBC). The model ultimately identified seven core genes closely related to ITH, including IRF8, MAGEA4, IL18R1, IL7R, TBC1D10C, TEX261, and CD6. Further analysis revealed that patients in the high IRS score group showed worse prognosis, higher TIDE scores, lower MSI scores, reduced expression of immune checkpoints, lower immune cell infiltration. The predictive value of IRS for immunotherapy response was evaluated in multiple TNBC cohorts, including two patient cohorts (GSE194040 and GSE173839) and one murine model cohort (GSE124821), where high IRS consistently correlated with reduced responsiveness. To further assess the generalizability of the IRS model, we additionally validated it in a large-scale cohort of metastatic urothelial carcinoma (IMvigor210), a different cancer type, where similar predictive performance was observed. Additionally, the high and low-IRS score groups also exhibited differences in related functions and pathways. The high-risk group was associated with metabolic-related pathways, such as N-Glycan biosynthesis and O-Glycan biosynthesis, while the low-risk group was mainly involved in immune-related pathways, such as adaptive immune response and immune response-regulating cell surface receptor signaling pathway. Lastly, scRNA-seq data analysis based on the GSE176078 dataset revealed that the IRS core genes were predominantly enriched in immune cell subsets rather than in tumor epithelial cells. These findings indicate that the IRS model quantifies ITH from the dimension of the immune microenvironment, revealing its intrinsic association with the tumor immune microenvironment at the single-cell level. Furthermore, we resolved the intercellular communication networks between CD8 + T cells and macrophages, particularly their interactions within the GALECTIN and CD45 signaling pathways, providing cellular-level mechanistic insights into how the IRS may regulate the immune microenvironment.
Previous studies have elucidated the roles of some IRS core genes in tumor progression and the immune microenvironment. IRF8, a key regulator of myeloid cell differentiation, is associated with M2-macrophage polarization when expressed at low levels [ , ]. In BC, tumor-associated macrophages promote cytotoxic T lymphocyte exhaustion via IRF8, and the upregulation of IRF8 is relevant to the CD8 + T-cell infiltration [ , ]. Consistent with this, our results showed that IRF8 expression and CD8 + T-cell infiltration were significantly reduced in the high-risk group, aligning with the “immune-cold” phenotype of TNBC commonly associated with poor prognosis and limited immunotherapy benefit [ ]. Xiao et al. [ ] reported that MAGEA4 expression was significantly higher in TNBC than in non-TNBC subtypes and was associated with prolonged DFS, whereas another study found that MAGEA4 positivity correlated with worse 5-year OS in BC [ ]. Meanwhile, MAGEA4 is presented to CD8 + T cells via MHC class I molecules, initiating the body’s specific immune response to tumors [ ]. As a T cell co-stimulatory molecule, CD6 plays a crucial role in promoting the activation and proliferation of T cells via interacting with ligands such as CD166/ALCAM [ ]. In vitro experiments have demonstrated that the anti-CD6 monoclonal antibody (UMCD6) can directly modulate CD8 + T cells and NK cells, enhancing their cytotoxicity against BC cells [ , ].
IL7R, the core receptor of the IL-7 signaling pathway, is crucial in maintaining T cell homeostasis and anti-tumor immunity [ ]. Our study found that the low-risk group exhibited high IL7R expression, along with enrichment of CD8 + T cells and enhanced cytotoxic activity. This finding is consistent with Crawley et al. [ ], who confirmed that IL7R activates STAT5 and PI3K-AKT pathways to sustain memory CD8 + T-cell survival and enhance NK cell cytotoxicity. Furthermore, IL7R expression is positively associated with the efficacy of Programmed cell death protein 1(PD-1) inhibitors in melanoma and lung cancer [ ], supporting our finding that the low-risk group had a higher immunotherapy response rate. For IL18R1 and TBC1D10C, prior studies have shown that downregulation of IL18R1 promotes proliferation and migration of lung cancer cells and correlates with poor prognosis [ ], while TBC1D10C has been linked to immune checkpoint therapy response [ ]. In our study, the low-risk group showed elevated expression of both genes, along with enhanced PD-1 response, underscoring their potential relevance in immunotherapy sensitivity.
The connection and relevant molecular mechanisms between glycosylation-related pathways enriched in the high-risk group and tumor immune escape have been documented in prior studies [ , ]. Aberrant glycosylation can suppress antitumor immunity in multiple ways. For instance, N-glycosylation can enhance the stability of programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) by reducing its protease degradation, thereby boosting its immunosuppressive activity [ ]. Our study is the first to propose the hypothesis that in TNBC, patients in the high-risk group of IRS had an immunosuppressive phenotype (higher TIDE score and lower expression of immune checkpoint genes), possibly due to the activation of specific glycosylation-related pathways, suggesting that targeting glycosylation enzymes may reverse the IRS-mediated immunosuppressive phenotype.
Our in silico drug sensitivity analysis indicated that the high-risk group showed increased sensitivity to conventional chemotherapeutics (e.g., platinum agents, taxanes, gemcitabine, and 5-fluorouracil), whereas the low-risk group was more sensitive to PARP inhibitors. This finding suggests that high-risk tumors may harbor DNA repair defects, rendering them susceptible to DNA-damaging agents, while low-risk tumors may rely on alternative pathways vulnerable to PARP inhibition. Notably, the differential sensitivity to PARP inhibitors aligns with the expression pattern of MAGEA4, which has been implicated in translesion DNA synthesis and PARP inhibitor resistance [ , ]. These results provide preliminary reference for individualized chemotherapy selection in TNBC patients.
The IRS model developed in this study demonstrated favorable efficacy in predicting prognosis for TNBC. However, its clinical applicability may be influenced by the demographic characteristics of the public cohorts utilized. Age is a critical factor associated with the tumor immune microenvironment in TNBC. Elderly patients often exhibit an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment characterized by increased infiltration of regulatory T cells and impaired T cell function, which may reduce the response rate to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors [ ]. The three cohorts employed in this study (METABRIC-TNBC, GSE58812, and GSE21653-TNBC) had similar median ages (56–57years). This consistency in age distribution supports the stability and reliability of the IRS model in predominantly middle-aged and elderly TNBC populations. Nevertheless, the predictive performance of this model in younger patients (< 50 years) requires targeted validation in future studies. Furthermore, none of the datasets included information on parity, an established independent prognostic factor in TNBC that influences disease progression through hormonal regulation and immune microenvironment remodeling [ ]. Incorporating such variables would enhance the precision of prognostic stratification. Additionally, the three cohorts predominantly comprised Caucasian populations, with limited representation of Asian, African, and other ethnic groups. This homogeneity in demographic characteristics may result in better predictive performance of the model in elderly Caucasian TNBC patients, whereas its applicability in younger patients, diverse ethnic populations, and individuals with varying parity status remains to be validated.
Our study also has several limitations. Firstly, although we have applied “ComBat” to minimize batch effects, inherent biological differences between retrospective cohorts remain a potential confounder. Final confirmation of clinical utility necessitates a prospective, multi-center trial. Secondly, the functions of IRS core genes have not yet been clarified through in vivo and in vitro experiments, and their specific regulatory networks need to be further explored. Lastly, our drug sensitivity analysis mainly relied on computational predictions based on cell line data, and it still requires validation in actual patients.

## Conclusion
Our study constructed and validated an IRS model by integrating machine learning and single-cell RNA sequencing. This model improves the accuracy of prognosis prediction for TNBC patients and effectively distinguishes risk subgroups with different immune microenvironment characteristics and treatment responses, providing preliminary data for future exploration of personalized treatment options for TNBC.
