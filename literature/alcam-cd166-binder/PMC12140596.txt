## Abstract

## Introduction
Intracellular Ca2+ oscillations of unknown function occur in human adipose tissue stem cells (ASCs). In the present study, we investigated whether Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs from subcutaneous fat tissue derived from female patients were driving albumin endocytosis by involving the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which is mediating the recycling of albumin and IgG.

## Methods
Intracellular Ca2+ oscillations were monitored by Fluo-4 microfluorometry. Uptake of fluorescence-labeled albumin was assessed by confocal laser scanning microscopy in the presence of endocytotic pathway inhibitors. FcRn was inactivated either by use of the antagonizing antibody nipocalimab or siRNA technology.

## Results
Endocytosis of albumin occurred by macropinocytosis (inhibition by cytochalasin D, wortmannin, and ethylisopropylamiloride [EIPA]), caveolae-dependent (inhibition by genistein and nystatin) and clathrin-mediated (inhibition by Dyngo-4a) pathways. In serum-free medium, Ca2+ oscillations were absent, but were induced by the addition of either fetal bovine serum, albumin, IgG or a stimulating antibody against FcRn. Consequently, FcRn expression was demonstrated in ASCs by Western blot analysis and immunohistochemistry, and colocalized with endocytosed albumin. Ca2+ oscillations were inhibited by the Ca2+ chelating agent BAPTA, the store-operated Ca2+ entry inhibitor SKF96365, the Ca2+-sensing receptor (CaSR) inhibitor NPS-2143, the macropinocytosis inhibitors cytochalasin D, wortmannin, and EIPA, the caveolae-dependent endocytosis inhibitors genistein and nystatin, and the clathrin-mediated endocytosis inhibitor Dyngo-4a. Uptake of fluorescence-labeled albumin was inhibited by agents interfering with Ca2+ oscillations and endocytosis blockers. Notably, not only intracellular albumin and IgG accumulation, but also Ca2+ oscillations were inhibited by the FcRn-blocking antibody nipocalimab, which interferes with the IgG binding site of FcRn. Moreover, siRNA-mediated downregulation of FcRn protein expression significantly reduced intracellular albumin content, the number of cells displaying Ca2+ oscillations, and the duration and amplitude of Ca2+ signals.

## Conclusion
Our data suggest that Ca2+ oscillations in human ASCs regulate albumin uptake and presumably IgG recycling via FcRn.

## Inhibition of Ca2+ Oscillations in Human ASCs by Ca2+ Chelation, the Store-Operated Ca2+ Entry Inhibitor SKF96365, and the CaSR Inhibitor NPS-2143
Ca2+ signaling in non-excitable cells occurs via an interplay of Ca2+ entry from the extracellular compartment and Ca2+ release from intracellular stores, e.g., the endoplasmatic reticulum. To investigate the mechanism of Ca2+ oscillations ASCs were incubated with the Ca2+ chelator BAPTA, AM (20 μm) ( a), the store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) inhibitor SKF96365 (4 μm) ( b), and the CaSR inhibitor NPS-2143 (5 μm) ( c), which terminated Ca2+ oscillations in 81%, 49%, and 72% of active cells, respectively. This observation indicates that Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs are driven by an interplay between intracellular Ca2+, Ca2+ influx via SOCE, and Ca2+ sensing via CaSR.
 

## Albumin Endocytosis in Human ASCs
Endocytotic processes via clathrin- and caveolin-mediated pathways in ASCs have been previously reported [ ]. To corroborate these results, cellular uptake of fluorescent Alexa-594 albumin was monitored in living ASCs. Our data showed that Alexa-594 albumin fluorescence was visible in a punctate pattern within the cytoplasm already after 30 min of incubation and reached a plateau after 5–6 h ( a).
 The vast majority of animal cells entail macropinocytosis (caveolae/clathrin-independent) pathways for cargo (e.g., growth factors) intake. To investigate macropinocytosis in ASCs, we applied EIPA (50 μm, 100 μm) which inhibits macropinocytosis by interfering with the NHE (Na+/H+ exchanger) ( b), cytochalasin D (10 μm, 20 μm) which is an inhibitor of actin polymerization and depolymerization ( c), or the PI3 kinase antagonist wortmannin (20 µm) ( d). It was evident that all applied macropinocytosis inhibitors significantly antagonized albumin uptake. To assess further endocytosis pathways in ASCs, we applied Dyngo-4a which is a dynamin inhibitor and involves in clathrin-mediated endocytosis and genistein, nystatin, and nocodazole which interfere with caveolae-dependent endocytosis and have been previously shown to be involved in albumin uptake in several cell types [ ]. Albumin uptake was inhibited upon treatment of cells with either Dyngo-4a (10 μm) ( e), the tyrosine kinase inhibitor genistein (25 μm) ( f) or nystatin (10 μm) ( g) which prevents aggregation and sequesters lipid rafts which are sterol- and sphingolipid-enriched microdomains, thereby inhibiting caveolae-dependent endocytosis [ ]. Moreover, inhibition of albumin endocytosis was achieved upon treatment with nocodazole (30 μm, 40 μm) ( h), which is a well-known microtubule-disrupting agent [ ]. Glycosphingolipid analogs are selectively internalized via a caveolae-related mechanism in most cell types [ ]. To further characterize caveolae-mediated endocytosis in ASCs, we incubated cells with BODIPY FL C5-LactosylCeramide-BSA (Lac-Cer) ( i), which has been previously demonstrated to be endocytosed by caveolae-dependent endocytosis [ ]. Our data demonstrated that Lac-Cer (2 μm, 4 μm) was readily endocytosed, and uptake was inhibited in the presence of genistein (25 μm), thus supporting the presence of caveolae-mediated endocytotic pathways in ASCs.
Since the data of the present study suggested that several endocytotic pathways for albumin acted in parallel, we co-incubated ASCs with inhibitors of different endocytotic pathways, e.g., EIPA or wortmannin as inhibitors of macropinocytosis concomitant with genistein or wortmannin as inhibitors of caveolae-dependent endocytosis. This experimental regimen resulted in higher rates of albumin uptake inhibition ( j), underscoring the redundancy of endocytotic pathways for albumin in ASCs.

## Co-Localization of Albumin with Megalin and Caveolin-1
To corroborate our findings of clathrin- and caveolin-mediated albumin uptake in ASCs, we investigated megalin and albumin co-localization ( A). Megalin, together with its partner cubilin and its endocytotic adapter protein Dab2, binds to albumin and mediates albumin uptake by clathrin-mediated endocytosis [ ]. To achieve this aim, ASCs were incubated for 1 h with Alexa-594 albumin and subsequently fixed and labeled with antibodies directed against either megalin or Cav1. Indeed, the data of the present study demonstrated the co-localization of albumin with megalin, indicating clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Furthermore, co-localization of albumin and Cav1 was observed in ASCs ( B), supporting the notion of a caveolae-mediated albumin uptake process in ASCs.
 

## Inhibition of Ca2+ Oscillations in Human ASCs by Interference with Endocytotic Pathways
The data of the present study demonstrate the initiation of Ca2+ oscillations by albumin and cellular albumin uptake via distinct endocytotic processes. To investigate the association of Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs with endocytosis, cells were preincubated with agents interfering with endocytotic pathways, i.e., Dyngo-4a ( a), inhibiting clathrin-mediated endocytosis, nystatin ( b) and genistein ( c), inhibiting caveolae-mediated endocytosis, EIPA ( d), cytochalasin D ( e), and wortmannin ( f), inhibiting macropinocytosis. Subsequently, Ca2+ oscillations were monitored. It was observed that inhibition of different endocytotic pathways significantly interfered with Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs (Dyngo-4a [10 μm], 21% inhibition, nystatin [10 μm], 44% inhibition, genistein [25 µm], 69% inhibition, EIPA [50 μm], 78% inhibition, cytochalasin D [20 μm], 39% inhibition, wortmannin [10 μm], 58% inhibition), indicating that both endocytosis and Ca2+ oscillations were mechanistically connected. Notably, treatment of ASCs with the microtubule-disrupting agent nocodazole did not inhibit Ca2+ oscillations (data not shown) while downregulating albumin uptake (see h).
 

## Inhibition of Endocytotic Pathways in Human ASCs by Interference with Ca2+ Oscillations
Our data showed that Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs were abolished upon incubation of ASCs with inhibitors of clathrin-mediated endocytosis, caveolae-mediated endocytosis and macropinocytosis. It was, therefore, hypothesized that inhibitors of Ca2+ signaling pathways would abolish endocytotic uptake of albumin. We, therefore, preincubated ASCs with the Ca2+ chelator BAPTA (10 μm) ( a), the SOCE inhibitor SKF96365 (2 μm) ( b), or the CaSR inhibitor NPS-2143 (5 μm) ( c). Indeed, all applied inhibitors of Ca2+ oscillations significantly downregulated albumin uptake in ASCs.
 

## Interplay between FcRn-Mediated Intracellular Albumin Accumulation and Ca2+ Signaling
FcRn functions as a recycling or transcytosis receptor that is responsible for preserving IgG and albumin in the circulation, and bi-directionally transporting these two ligands across polarized cellular barriers [ ]. Moreover, interaction between megalin and FcRn in the endosomal compartment, and its increase during albumin endocytosis, has been previously reported [ ]. Since FcRn expression has so far not been investigated in human ASCs, we demonstrated FcRn protein expression in ASCs derived from 27, 38, and 75-year-old female patients in Western blot experiments ( A). No significant change in FcRn protein expression with increasing patient age was observed. In immunocytochemical analysis, FcRn appeared as a punctate pattern and colocalized with Alexa-594 albumin ( B). To further investigate this assumption, we downregulated FcRn expression by using a siRNA-based approach. Treatment with siRNA directed against FcRn resulted in significant downregulation of FcRn expression as evidenced by immunocytochemistry (online suppl. Fig. 1). Notably, siRNA-mediated downregulation of FcRn significantly diminished the intracellular albumin content ( C). Moreover, FcRn siRNA-treated ASCs displayed significantly decreased numbers of cells displaying Ca2+ oscillations ( D). The number of Ca2+-active cells was decreased ( E). Ca2+ spikes showed a significantly lower amplitude ( F) and frequency ( G) of Ca2+ oscillations compared to untreated ASCs and cells treated with scrambled scRNA.
 To corroborate our experiments with siRNA-mediated downregulation of FcRn, we applied the next-generation FcRn blocker nipocalimab (M281), which is a recombinant IgG1 monoclonal antibody. It competitively binds with IgG binding sites of FcRn, imparting lesser effect on albumin binding [ ]. Our data demonstrated that nipocalimab treatment (5–15 μg/mL) significantly and dose-dependent reduced the intracellular albumin content ( a). When cells were treated for 1 h with human IgG (15.7 μg/mL), nipocalimab dose-dependently reduced the intracellular content of IgG as visualized using a FITC-labeled IgG antibody ( b). Finally, we investigated the effect of nicocalimab on the occurrence of Ca2+ oscillations in ASCs. We observed a dose-dependent inhibition of Ca2+ oscillations with a concentration of 1 μg/mL being without effect and significant inhibition with a nicocalimab concentration of 25 μg/mL ( c). These data clearly demonstrate that albumin and IgG are endocytosed in ASCs, which is related to the occurrence of Ca2+ oscillations. The intracellular IgG and albumin content in ASCs is preserved by FcRn and is depleted through induced degradation if FcRn is absent or functionally inactivated.
 

## Discussion
ASCs are not only an easily accessible source of stem cells usable for cell therapy approaches, but also exert immunomodulatory properties [ ] and may be used as a cargo carrier for cell-mediated drug therapy [ ]. Female patients were used in the present study to avoid bias from differences in the transcriptome previously shown in ASCs from males and females [ ].
The data of the present study demonstrate that Ca2+ oscillations occurring spontaneously in ASCs are dependent on bovine serum in the complete ASC cell culture medium and could be elicited by albumin, IgG and a stimulating antibody directed against FcRn. The latter is a major player in IgG and albumin recycling, prevents degradation and extends the half-life of IgG and albumin in the circulation [ ]. The observation of albumin and IgG as prerequisites for Ca2+ oscillations led us to hypothesize that Ca2+ signals may orchestrate endocytotic pathways in ASCs and are involved in albumin and IgG recycling. Previous studies have demonstrated that ASCs possess clathrin- and caveolae-dependent endocytotic pathways [ ], but their relation to intracellular Ca2+ signals was not investigated. Using established inhibitors of macropinocytosis, caveolae-mediated endocytosis, and clathrin-mediated endocytosis, we confirmed the data of Sun et al. [ ] and additionally found evidence for macropinocytosis in ASCs. Previous reports by others have demonstrated that endocytosis of albumin can occur by macropinocytosis in macrophages [ ], rodent pulmonary endothelial cells [ ], and pancreatic cancer cells, where the lysosomal glutamine and asparagine transporter SNAT7 activates mTORC1 after albumin uptake [ ]. Recently, it was shown that albumin activates cell surface receptors to initiate K-RAS-dependent macropinocytosis in lung and pancreatic cancer cells. This is of clinical importance since this pathway enables cancer cells with K-RAS mutations to transport extracellular protein into intracellular amino acid pathways to support their unique metabolic needs [ ]. Caveolae-dependent endocytotic pathways mediating albumin endocytosis were shown to be present in human renal glomerular endothelial cells [ ], human podocytes [ ] and several human cancer cells [ ]. In the experiments of the present study, we observed expression and co-localization of Cav1 with fluorescence-labeled albumin, and endocytotic uptake of Lac-Cer, which was inhibited by genistein, thus clearly demonstrating the activity of caveolae-mediated endocytosis in ASCs. Moreover, we found evidence for clathrin-mediated endocytosis since albumin uptake was inhibited by Dyngo-4a, which inhibits dynamin-dependent endocytosis [ ]. Furthermore, expression of megalin and co-localization with fluorescence-labeled albumin was evidenced. When megalin binds ligands on the plasma membrane, clathrin-mediated endocytosis is initiated and the complex of megalin and its ligands is sorted into clathrin-coated vesicles by endocytic adapter proteins [ ]. In our experiments, inhibitors of specific endocytotic pathways did not completely inhibit albumin endocytosis, indicating that albumin endocytosis occurs simultaneously through different endocytotic mechanisms. We, therefore, co-incubated ASCs with inhibitors of different endocytotic pathways, e.g., EIPA or wortmannin as inhibitors of macropinocytosis concomitant with genistein or wortmannin as inhibitors of caveolae-dependent endocytosis. This treatment regime resulted in additive effects on endocytosis and substantiated the notion that different endocytotic pathways act in parallel in ASCs.
In the present study we correlated intracellular Ca2+ oscillations occurring in ASCs with endocytosis. The Ca2+ dependence of endocytotic pathways is well described in nerve terminals, secretory cells and phagocytic cells [ ], but only a few reports exist in non-excitable cells which are devoid of voltage-activated Ca2+ channels. Previous studies by our group demonstrated that endocytosis is regulated by Ca2+ oscillations in primitive endodermal cells derived from mouse embryonic stem cells [ ]. In proximal tubular epithelial cells, receptor-mediated endocytosis was demonstrated to be regulated by ORAI1-3 channels [ ], which are involved in the regulation of store-operated Ca2+ channels (SOCCs) [ ]. Macropinocytosis can occur in several cell types by stimulation by growth factors, but in selected cell types, notably macrophages and dendritic cells, macropinocytosis occurs constitutively. Previous data have demonstrated that constitutive macropinocytosis is stringently Ca2+-dependent, while stimulus-induced macropinocytosis is not [ ]. The data of the present study, therefore, suggest that Ca2+-mediated endocytotic pathways in ASCs are constitutive and involve CaSR, SOCE, and intracellular Ca2+ since the CaSR inhibitor NPS-2143, the SOCE inhibitor SKF96365 and the intracellular Ca2+ chelator BAPTA inhibited Ca2+ oscillations. Expression of CaSR has been evidenced in human adipocytes and adipocyte progenitor cells [ ] as well as in human ASCs [ ]. Notably, inhibition of Ca2+ oscillations likewise downregulated albumin endocytosis, clearly underscoring that the endocytotic process is Ca2+-dependent. This finding was substantiated by the observation that interference with different endocytotic pathways in ASCs inhibited Ca2+ oscillations. Interestingly, treatment with nocodazole inhibited endocytosis but failed to interfere with Ca2+ oscillations. Nocodazole disrupts microtubule assembly/disassembly that produces loss of morphologic caveolae to inhibit caveolae-dependent endocytosis. Recently, a phagocytosis control system has been demonstrated in macrophages which involves Ca2+ influx, the destabilization of microtubules, and activation of actin network remodeling, followed by the translocation and acidification of phagosomes on the microtubule bundles. This indicates the interaction of the microtubular network and actin network during phagosome maturation downstream of CaSR activation, which may be independent of intracellular Ca2+ transients.
The participation of FcRn in the mentioned different endocytotic pathways is not sufficiently investigated, although several studies have reported involvement of FcRn in clathrin-mediated endocytosis as well as macropinocytosis. Notably, FcRn itself is continuously endocytosed from and recycled back to the plasma membrane [ ]. Generally, endocytosed albumin is rescued from lysosomal degradation after internalization into cells by FcRn-mediated exocytosis. Albumin binds more tightly at the acidic pH in endosomes as compared to the neutral pH environment of extracellular fluids [ , ]. Recent data have shown that megalin and FcRn colocalize in murine mesangial cells along with the megalin adapter Dab2 [ ], indicating that FcRn is involved in clathrin-mediated endocytosis. During endocytosis of albumin in proximal tubule kidney cells, redistribution of megalin and FcRn into clathrin vesicles and early endosomes was shown, and trafficking into clathrin vesicles was impaired in megalin-deficient cells, thus supporting the role of megalin in FcRn redistribution [ ]. An association between macropinocytosis and FcRn has been recently evidenced in primary human endothelial cells, where human serum albumin was partitioned into FcRn-positive tubules derived from maturing macropinosomes, which are then transported toward the plasma membrane [ ]. Moreover, albumin as well as IgG endocytosed by macropinocytosis in primary mouse macrophages selectively expressing the human FcRn colocalised to SNX5-positive membrane domains and were partitioned into tubules emanating from early macropinosomes for delivery in transport carriers to the plasma membrane [ ]. In contrast to clathrin-mediated endocytosis and macropinocytosis, the association of FcRn to caveolae and caveolae-mediated endocytosis has – to our knowledge – so far not been described. Investigations on placental transfer of IgG from maternal circulation to that of the fetus revealed that IgG transport across the first layer, the syncytiotrophoblast, was mediated by FcRn, whereas in the villus endothelium, an alternative receptor for IgG, namely, Fc gammaRIIb2, was expressed which mediated transfer of IgG without the participation of caveolae [ ].
The data from the present study demonstrated that siRNA-mediated downregulation of FcRn resulted in significant inhibition of albumin accumulation in ASCs. In parallel, we observed that FcRn knockdown significantly reduced the percentage of cells displaying Ca2+ oscillations and reduced the amplitude and frequency of the remaining responsive cells which indicates that FcRn signaling is Ca2+-dependent and the presence of FcRn is necessary for the endocytotic pathway. To corroborate these findings, we investigated the effect of preincubation of ASCs with the FcRn-inactivating antibody nipocalimab (M281) on Ca2+ oscillations and albumin as well as IgG uptake and found that both were dose-dependent inhibited. This finding is of importance since IgG and albumin binding sites on FcRn are distinct and do not overlap [ , ]. Notably, different antibodies interfere with the IgG binding site of FcRn and are clinically investigated to lower circulating autoimmune antibodies likewise downregulated serum albumin levels, which may be due to accelerated FcRn degradation and/or steric hindrance of the albumin binding site on FcRn [ ]. Accordingly, recent clinical studies reported that nipocalimab treatment resulted in mild dose-dependent decreases from baseline in the mean serum albumin levels [ , ], thus corroborating the findings of the present study of reduced albumin content under conditions of nipocalimab treatment. Currently, different antibodies targeting FcRn have been approved for clinical use against antibody-mediated autoimmune diseases like myasthenia gravis or immune thrombocytopenia [ ]. Since these antibodies interfere with IgG recycling, they may have severe impact on the immune status of the recipient. Moreover, a recent study demonstrated that IgG accumulates in adipose tissue, including adipose progenitor cells, during obesity development via FcRn-mediated IgG recycling, thus triggering insulin resistance, macrophage infiltration and inflammation [ ]. Targeting FcRn normalized metabolic function and reversed insulin resistance, which suggests that antibodies against FcRn may be used in the treatment of the metabolic syndrome. On the other hand, albumin with its enormous binding capacity is increasingly recognized as a reliable drug-delivering molecule to be used to treat a variety of life-threatening diseases, including cancer [ ]. Since ASCs exhibit tumor tropism [ ], it is of importance to investigate the impact of FcRn on different endocytotic pathways in detail to avoid severe side effects of FcRn-based immunotherapies.
