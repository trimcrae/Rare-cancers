## ABSTRACT
Adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) are characterized by their low immunogenicity and unique immunosuppressive properties, providing many opportunities for autologous transplantation in regenerative medicine and plastic surgery. These methods are characterized by low rejection rates and intense stimulation of tissue regeneration. However, procedures during which fat tissue is harvested occur under local anaesthesia. To better understand the effects and mechanisms of anaesthetic compounds in cosmetic and therapeutic procedures, the present study used a mixture of these compounds (0.1% epinephrine, 8.4% sodium bicarbonate, and 4% articaine) and examined their impact on a human adipose-derived stem cell line. The results showed anesthetics’ negative, dose-dependent effect on cell viability and proliferation, especially during the first 24 h of incubation. After extending the exposure to 48 and 72 h of incubation, cells adapted to new culture conditions. In contrast, no significant changes were observed in immunophenotype, cell cycle progression, and apoptosis. The results obtained from this study provide information on the effect of the selected mixture of anaesthetics on the characteristics and function of ASC52telo cells. The undesirable changes in the metabolic activity of cells suggest the need to search for new drugs to harvest cells with unaltered properties and higher efficacy in aesthetic medicine treatments.
KEYWORDS: Adipose-derived stem cells, anaesthetics, cytotoxicity, cell cycle, apoptosis

## Introduction
Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs) have great therapeutic potential in regenerative medicine because of their self-renewal and multidifferentiation abilities [ , ]. MSCs can be isolated from several adult tissues such as bone marrow, adipose tissue, umbilical cord, muscle, tendon, peripheral blood, and dental pulp [ , ]. Notably, they can differentiate into all cell types of mesodermal origin (osteocytes, chondrocytes, and adipocytes) [ ].
White adipose tissue is considered one of the best sources of adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs). ADSCs have been shown to have MSC-like properties, as indicated by their similar but not identical surface marker profiles and the ability to differentiate into tissues of mesodermal origin [ ]. These include osteoblasts, chondrocytes, adipocytes, cardiomyocytes, myocytes, and neurons [ ].
ADSCs exhibit several properties that make them an excellent tool for clinical applications. Not only do they possess a high rate of proliferation but they are also known for their low immunogenicity. According to in vitro studies, low MHC class II expression makes them suitable for allogeneic cell therapies. However, in vivo studies have revealed that ADSCs induce humoral and cellular responses that can result in xenotransplant rejection. These differences may be due to the high concentration of ADSCs in the culture environment, which makes their immunosuppressive properties dominant. In contrast, in the physiological environment, their concentrations are lower, and it is most likely that MHC class II is expressed at inflammatory sites [ ].
Autologous ADSC transplantation is generally considered both immunocompatible and safe. Their use raises very few ethical dilemmas. Owing to ability of ADSCs to enhance angiogenesis and minimize inflammation during the healing process, they are considered effective in soft tissue reconstruction (e.g. breast reconstruction), skin healing, and nerve regeneration in spinal cord injuries. These cells, combined with 3D scaffolds made of compatible biomaterials and appropriate growth factors, offer countless possibilities for plastic surgery and regenerative medicine procedures, where significant anatomical defects limit the intervention of other medical specialities [ ].
ADSCs are derived from isolated and enzymatically digested adipose tissue fragments known as the stromal vascular fraction (SVF) [ ]. The properties of these cells depend on the quality of SVF, which is influenced by many factors, including age, body mass index (BMI), diabetes, and prior cancer treatment [ ]. The importance of these features was demonstrated in a study that compared the properties of infant and adult ADSCs. Infant-origin cells displayed several improved qualities, such as reduced cellular senescence and replicative stress, increased proliferation rates, and more robust antioxidant defences. Foetal and adult ADSCs exhibited different differentiation potentials despite the use of identical induction media. Although foetal ADSCs have a greater tendency for chondrogenic, adipogenic, and neurogenic differentiation, adult ADSCs possess osteogenic and tenogenic potential [ ].
Other factors can also affect ADSC properties. It has been shown that culturing these cells in a medium supplemented with human serum (HS), rather than foetal bovine serum (FBS), yields higher ADSC proliferation without altering the expression of cell surface markers. The same study also demonstrated faster and more pronounced ability of ADSCs to differentiate into cellular osteocytes using an HS-containing medium [ ].
MSCs are most commonly obtained from the bone marrow and adipose tissue. However, the procedure for isolating BMSCs is highly invasive and can lead to many complications, such as bleeding and risk of infection. Owing to its abundance in the human body, subcutaneous adipose tissue is a more practical source of MSCs. The great advantage of ADSCs from adipose tissue is their high cell viability and quantity. One gram of adipose tissue provides 5 × 103 cells, making it a more efficient source of stem cells than the bone marrow. Liposuction remains the primary method for obtaining fat tissue [ ]. The most common modification of this technique, called tumescent liposuction, involves injecting low concentrations of lignocaine and epinephrine, after which fat is suctioned out using microcannulas [ ].
Local anaesthetics dissociate after entering the body. They penetrate the neuronal membrane and reversibly bind to the sodium-potassium channel. This reaction occurs only when the sodium-potassium channel is open. By blocking voltage-dependent channels, they inhibit the action potential of the cell membrane, consequently preventing the transmission of impulses along the nerve. Increasing the anaesthetic concentration leads to a decrease in the peak action potential, impaired impulse conduction, and prolongation of the refractory period. The corresponding increase in concentration can inhibit the entire nerve conduction [ ].
Articaine is an aminoamide with an ester group that allows it to be hydrolysed by plasma esterases and metabolized by liver enzymes. The thiophene group is responsible for the increased lipid solubility of articaine compared to lidocaine, mepivacaine and bupivacaine, leading to potentially better tissue penetration. It has a more potent effect than lidocaine and induces less toxicity in the nervous and cardiovascular systems through rapid degradation to an inactive metabolite [ , ]. The addition of epinephrine to the anaesthetic mixture is intended to constrict blood vessels, reducing blood flow and the occurrence of potential haemorrhage, which can cause slower systemic absorption, resulting in a prolonged anaesthetic effect [ , ]. It can potentially minimize its impact on the viability and function of adipose-derived stem cells within the experimental setup [ ]. Sodium bicarbonate was used to alkalize the acidic pH of the anaesthetic mixture [ ]. It helps in buffering the local anaesthetic solution, adjusting the pH to a more physiological range. This adjustment can minimize potential cellular stress or damage due to pH changes, which is essential for maintaining cell viability in an in vitro setting [ ]. Importantly, this procedure neutralizes discomfort during the administration of anaesthesia and facilitates tissue penetration owing to an increase in the number of undissociated particles [ , ].
The administration of anaesthetics can cause IgE-dependent type 1 hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis. The incidence of such an organismal response is less than 1%, indicating the safety of the therapy provided. Type 4 hypersensitivity reactions involving allergic contact dermatitis occur in 2.4% of patients [ ]. The incidence of adverse reactions is low, but it indicates interference of the anaesthetic with the body’s physiology. Local drugs also exhibit several other characteristics: they reduce the adhesion, migration, and accumulation of multinuclear leukocytes at the site of inflammation while altering the function of macrophages and monocytes [ ]. Identifying an agent that does not affect the body’s hypersensitivity response is necessary to isolate ADSCs with unaltered phenotypic characteristics. Adipose stem cells are closely related to the immune system [ ]. Therefore, inducing an immune response most likely affects the function of isolated stem cells.

## Results
2.1.

## Cell morphology
After 24 h of pre-incubation, a mixture of anaesthetic compounds was added to the plate at different concentrations. Cells in the control group were left untreated. The control group of cells displayed an elongated, spindle-shaped morphology, specific to fibroblast-like cells ( ).

 In samples containing a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetic compounds, the cells appeared shrunken and underwent disintegration. Cells treated with a mixture of anaesthetic compounds and cell culture medium at a ratio of 4:1 (10%) showed less shrinkage and still exhibited fibroblast-like morphology.

2.2.

## Cell viability
MTT assay revealed that cell viability gradually decreased with incubation time in a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetic compounds ( ). In samples containing mixtures of anaesthetic compounds and medium at concentrations of 6.25%, 10%, and 11.25%, there was a noticeable difference between the samples incubated for 24 h and those incubated for 48 h and 72 h. ASC52telo cells incubated for a prolonged period showed slightly lower survival rates than the 24-hour sample. Cells incubated for 48 h and 72 h displayed similar metabolic activity at 6.25%, 10%, and 11.25% of the mixture concentration, indicating their ability to adapt to new culture conditions.

2.3.

## Cell proliferation
The proliferative activity of the cells was examined using the BrdU incorporation assay ( ). ASC52telo cells incubated in a 6.25% mixture of anaesthetic and medium showed a gradual decrease in viability with increasing incubation time. This was not the case for cells incubated in 10% anaesthetic mixture; it was observed that 48-hour incubation resulted in the highest percentage of viable cells. ASC52telo incubated for 48 h and 72 h in a 12.5% mixture showed higher survival rates than those incubated for 24 h, which accounted for the lowest viability rate in this sample. For ASC52telo cells treated with 11.25% and 12.5% mixture of anaesthetics, 48- and 72-hour incubation showed almost identical survival rates. Similar to the MTT assay, this suggests the ability of ASC52telo to adapt.
Based on the results of the xCELLigence system, the proliferation of cells in a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetic compounds sharply decreased with incubation time. Cells cultured in a 10% concentration of the compound mixture showed a slightly slower rate of cell proliferation than the control group. Cells incubated in a 6.25% mixture of anaesthetic compounds with the medium showed proliferation rates similar to those of the control group.

2.4.

## Expression of cell surface markers
Cell immunophenotype analysis revealed high levels of CD44 and CD90 marker expression on the surface of the MSCs. In contrast, low expression levels of CD34 and CD45 have been reported ( ).
 24-hour long incubation with a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetics caused a significant decrease in the expression of CD44 in ASC52telo cells; in the control group, it was 89.4 ± 3.4%, while in the test sample, the value was 70.7 ± 2.5%. It was observed that after prolonged cell exposure (48 and 72 h) to a 12.50% mixture, CD44 expression was very similar to the 24-hour sample.
In contrast, CD90 and CD34 marker expression in the test sample did not differ from that in the control group, even after prolonged exposure to the mixture of compounds.
In the case of the CD45 marker, the control group displayed an expression value of 1.5 ± 0.6% after 24 h of incubation, while the test group showed a slight increase to 2.5 ± 0.1%. A similar tendency was observed after 48 h and 72 h.

2.5.

## Cell cycle progression
Cell cycle analysis showed no significant changes in the distribution of cells in the subG1, G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases ( ). A slight difference was observed in the number of cells in the G2/M phase after 24 h of incubation; in the control group, the cell population was 18.8%, whereas in the test sample, it was 11.8%. An extended incubation time (72 h) with a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetics did not affect the cell cycle progression.

 
2.6.

## Apoptosis induction
The annexin V assay showed no significant changes in the cell membrane integrity of ASC52telo cells after treatment with a 12.5% mixture of anaesthetic compounds ( ). The most noticeable difference was seen after 24-hour incubation; 0,7% of late-apoptotic cells were in the control group, while this number increased to 2.8% in the treated group. The distribution of the membrane phospholipids did not change after 72 h of incubation.

## Discussion
Adipose tissue affects the function of many systems, including the immune, haematopoietic, reproductive, and endocrine systems. The cell types that make up their structures display unique regulatory and functional properties. Catabolic processes occurring in brown and white adipose tissues are characterized by different mechanisms and intensities. This affects the secretory diversity and expression of specific immunophenotypic markers of ADSCs in each body region. This is due to the characteristic individual features of the donor and the microenvironmental factors of particular stem cell niches. Unravelling the diversity of ADSCs, considering the characteristics of their immunogenetic and secretory profiles, can significantly improve their therapeutic value [ ].
According to the International Society for Cellular Therapy (ISCT) and numerous literature reports, ADSCs are characterized by the positive markers CD44, CD90, CD73, CD105, and CD13. In contrast, the marker CD45, present on the surface of cells of haematopoietic origin, remains at low or undetectable levels [ ]. Immunophenotypic analysis of human ASC52telo stem cells was performed by flow cytometry to determine the expression levels of specific surface markers. The results showed that cells treated with a pure mixture of anaesthetics exhibited changes in the expression of CD44 and CD45. Conversely, no significant differences in immunophenotypes were observed for CD90 and CD34 markers.
In a study by Travnickova et al., 15 patients were subjected to tumescent liposuction to obtain adipose tissue from different body regions. The anaesthetic mixture used for the procedure was prepared as a saline solution containing 0.1% epinephrine and 8.4% bicarbonate. The researchers decided not to use local anaesthetic to avoid potential toxicity. The expression of the surface marker CD90 varied from 99.4 to 99.6% depending on the harvest region. CD34 expression ranged from 0.4 to 1.1%. Similarly, ASC52telo in the control group showed comparable expression of the two CD markers. Slightly different results were obtained for the marker CD45; in the control group, its expression ranged from 1.2 to 1.7%, whereas according to the study conducted by Travnickova et al., these values ranged from 1.8 to 5.2% [ ].
ADSCs in the body are located within specific cellular niches, for example, at the base of the hair follicles. They are embedded in the extracellular matrix, along with other helper cells. The relationship between ADSCs and their microenvironment is intricate and needs to be fully understood. It has been proven that surrounding ECM and local immune cells can modulate the natural properties of ADSCs to proliferate, differentiate, and migrate to damaged tissues [ ]. However, some properties of adipose stem cells appear only after the initial activation with pro-inflammatory cytokinins. Moreover, studies have shown the growing importance of intracellular communication through exosome-transmitted RNA released by MSCs, which can also alleviate chronic inflammation and enhance the desired effects of MSCs [ ]. This is further evidence of the strong influence of the microenvironment in which ADSCs are located on their functions. Liposuction is an invasive procedure for harvesting SVFs and is likely to affect the physiology of the harvested cells. The anaesthetics used in this procedure alter the expression of surface markers and affect cell viability and proliferation. Such significant changes affect the overall physiology of cells, which may impair their therapeutic value during the procedure.
Our study demonstrated an adverse effect of the anaesthetic mixture on the viability and proliferation of human ASC52telo stem cells. In clinical setting adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) may also encounter residual anaesthetics for a longer duration post-extraction due to their slower clearance from the tissue. Mimicking this prolonged exposure in vitro allows for a more comprehensive assessment of potential cellular responses [ ]. Cells treated with 12.5% anaesthetic solution showed a significant reduction in both viability and proliferation. However, the mixture of anaesthetics at different concentrations had significantly less effect on the measured parameters of these cells. Prolonged incubation with the tested substances did not cause changes in cell viability, indicating their ability to adapt to the new culture conditions. According to a study conducted by Girard et al., cytotoxicity induced by lidocaine is dose-dependent and closely related to exposure time. Significant levels of cytotoxicity measured by the LDH assay were noted for the highest lidocaine concentration (1.6 mg/mL) after 12 and 24 h of exposure and reached 60 and 70%, respectively. At a one µg/mL concentration, adrenaline did not induce changes in cell metabolic activity, as the cytotoxicity was comparable to that of the control group. ADSCs were also treated with epinephrine in combination with lidocaine, and cytotoxicity did not differ from that obtained with lidocaine alone [ ]. In a study conducted by Wang et al., the viability of ADSCs was assessed based on adherence to the cell culture surface after incubation with lidocaine and lidocaine combined with epinephrine. As previously mentioned, cell survival depended on the concentration of the tested substances. In contrast to the study by Girard et al., the test sample containing lidocaine and epinephrine showed lower survival rates than the sample containing lidocaine alone. However, these differences were not significant [ ].
The changes in morphological features observed using an inverted optical microscope demonstrated the significant effects of anaesthetics on stem cell physiology. Incubation of ADSCs with a pure mixture of drugs caused shrinkage and subsequent disintegration of the cells. The characteristic fibroblast-like morphology of ASC52telo was visible in the 10% solution, but the cells continued to shrink. Based on observations made by Girard et al., the highest lidocaine concentration causes the cells to become round. After prolonged exposure, the cells lost their adherence to the plate surface, which was particularly evident 24 h after lidocaine application [ ]. The xCELLigence system can also be used to measure cell adhesion based on real-time changes in the mean cell index values [ ]. As shown in our study, cells incubated in a 6.25% mixture of anaesthetics displayed adhesion similar to that of the control group. Starting from a 10% mixture of anaesthetics, cells slowly began to lose adhesion to the E-plates, whereas concentrations of 11.25% and 12.5% caused significant cell detachment. As mentioned above [ ], changes in cell adherence were only apparent 24 h after the start of the test.
In our study, the analysis of apoptosis using the annexin V assay showed no significant effect of the tested substances on the induction of natural cell death. The most noticeable difference was between the test and control groups undergoing programmed cell death after 24-hour incubation (2.1%). A flow cytometry analysis by Girard et al. provided information on the effect of 1.6 mg/mL lidocaine solution on the induction of apoptosis and necrosis of ADSCs. Positive annexin V staining revealed that after a 24-hour incubation, there was no significant difference between the lidocaine-treated cells and the control group. Interestingly, propidium iodide staining further showed that lidocaine could induce necrosis after prolonged exposure (24 h), which was not evident in the samples treated for 1 and 2 h. Again, epinephrine did not induce apoptosis or necrosis in ADSCs [ ]. Different conclusions have been drawn based on a study conducted by Wang et al. This group used a 3% lidocaine/epinephrine solution to assess cell apoptosis and cultured ADSCs for six days. These results implied that lidocaine induced cell death by apoptosis rather than necrosis, as evidenced by a significantly higher percentage of apoptotic cells in the lidocaine/epinephrine sample than in the control group [ ].
Many studies have discussed the adverse effects of lidocaine on ADSCs; however, it is the most commonly used anaesthetic in liposuction procedures. The advantage of tumescent liposuction over classic liposuction is the use of a diluted anaesthetic solution with epinephrine. To determine the actual effect of lidocaine on ADSCs, a group of scientists decided to obtain SVF from the same patient using two different methods. SVF was harvested from one body area using standard tumescent liposuction with lidocaine in the anaesthetic mixture, whereas modified liposuction without lidocaine was performed on the opposite side. Goldman et al. revealed a noticeable difference in cell viability and apoptosis between the two groups. The viability of the cells obtained by tumescent liposuction with lidocaine was almost 20% lower than that observed in the group without this anaesthetic. A similar tendency was observed for cells undergoing apoptosis; in the group in which lidocaine was used, annexin-binding cells remained at 28.5% ± 3.8%, whereas in the tested group without lidocaine, they reached 11.5% ± 3.1% [ ].
The close incorporation of ADSCs into the extracellular matrix of the niche and their connection with epithelial cells are the first significant limitations in the clinical application of adipose stem cells. This is due to drastic changes in the microenvironmental conditions during extraction. Another negative aspect is the inhibitory effect of local anaesthetics on the viability and number of transplanted ADSCs. The ability of administered cells to migrate and differentiate into other cell types may be hindered. This is usually caused by the initiation of apoptosis a few days after the transplantation. The widespread use of these cells in clinical and preclinical studies may be less effective because of the method of administration and the problems described above [ ]. However, desirable properties of ADSCs can be improved, as demonstrated in a previous study. Adipose tissue-derived stem cells were transfected with micro-RNA-146a. This interference makes it possible to enrich the cell population with exosomes responsible for tissue regeneration and wound healing. Compared to the exosomes of non-transfected stem cells, the exosomes of transfected cells showed increased concentrations of proangiogenic and anti-apoptotic miRNAs, with decreased concentrations of their antagonists. Secretome 146a can be used for therapeutic purposes; however, in vivo studies using chronic wound models are needed to confirm its efficacy [ ]. In some cases, it is necessary to implant stem cells attached to the scaffolds to restore the correct shape of the cellular structure. Compared to xenogeneic EMC-based scaffolds, bio-scaffolds based on the human extracellular matrix (ECM) are characterized by higher biocompatibility due to lower immunogenicity and lower probability of pathogen transmission. They can retain their structure and volume, which promotes angiogenesis and transport of chemical signals. A 3D scaffold based on human ECM can take the form of a sheet, microspheres, squares, films, hollow tubes, beads, or gels [ ]. These features attest to the considerable role of the natural microenvironment in stem cell therapy.

4.

## Conclusions
Adipose tissue-derived stem cells have remarkable therapeutic potential in numerous branches of reconstructive and aesthetic medicine. However, changes in the microenvironment of cells during the extraction process adversely affect their desired properties. The results obtained in this study showed a negative, dose-dependent effect of anaesthetics on the metabolic activity and proliferation of human adipose-derived stem cells ASC52telo. These compounds did not change the immunophenotype of ASC52telo cells during prolonged in vitro culture, nor did they affect cell cycle inhibition or the induction of apoptosis in the tested cell line. These findings indicate the importance of changing the composition of an anaesthetic mixture or searching for alternative substances that do not induce harmful changes in cells. Ultimately, this will result in a significant increase in stem cell utility for all medical applications.

## Materials and methods
5.1.

## Cell line and cell culture
ASC52telo cells immortalized by hTERT were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, SCRC-4000). These cells were isolated from the adipose tissue of a white female and characterized by a fibroblast-like structure, similar to elongated, spindle-shaped forms. ASC52telo cells express high levels of CD29, CD44, CD73, CD90, CD105, and CD166 and low levels of CD14, CD19, CD34, and CD45.
ASC52telo cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. Cells were cultured using DMEM/Ham’s F-12 (Corning, 10–092-CV) supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS) (PAN-Biotech, P30–3306), 5 ng/ml bFGF (Invitrogen, ENRFGFB50), and 0,2 mg/ml G418 (Gibco 10,131,027).

5.2.

## Anesthetic mixture
The mixture of anaesthetics consisted of three substances: epinephrine (Warszawskie Zakłady Farmaceutyczne 100,040,315), articaine (Molteni Dental), and sodium bicarbonate (Zakłady Farmaceutyczne Polpharma S.A., 5909990248513). The selection of articaine with epinephrine and sodium bicarbonate was based on their combined effects, which are tailored to optimize anaesthesia while minimizing adverse effects on cellular viability.
The initial anaesthetic mixture was prepared in saline containing 0.1% epinephrine, 4% articaine, and 8.4% sodium bicarbonate, resulting in a total anaesthetic content of 12.5%. Four concentrations of anaesthetic mixture were used in the study: the initial mix of anaesthetic compounds (12.5%), the mixture of compounds and DMEM/Ham’s F-12 medium at a ratio of 9:1 (11.25%), a mixture of compounds and DMEM/Ham’s F-12 medium at a ratio of 4:1 (10%), and a mixture of compounds and DMEM/Ham’s F-12 medium at a ratio of 1:1 (6.25%). The concentrations chosen were above the clinical setting due to the fact that those provide valuable insights into dose-response relationships and potential mechanisms of action that may not be apparent at lower concentrations [ ].

5.3.

## Cell number and cell viability
Cell number was assessed using 0,4% trypan blue solution (Sigma, T8154) in a haemocytometer. The test is based on the fact that non-viable cells with damaged cell membranes absorb the dark blue dye, while living cells remain transparent. The cells from each corner of the Neubauer chamber were counted under a light microscope.
Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. In this colorimetric test, the dehydrogenase of metabolically active cells converts the yellow tetrazolium salt to purple formazan. The intensity of the colouration was directly proportional to the number of viable cells.
ASC52telo cells were seeded onto a 96-well plate at a density of 2,5×103/cm2. After 24 h of preincubation, the standard growth medium was changed to one containing a mixture of anaesthetics from 6.25% to 12.5%. The cells were then incubated for 24, 48, and 72 h, after which the medium was discarded and the cells were washed with PBS. ASC52telo cells were then incubated for 2 h at 37°C with MTT reagent. Formazan crystals were dissolved in DMSO, and the absorbance was read using an iMark microplate reader (Bio-Rad) at 570 nm.

5.4.

## Cell proliferation
5.4.1.

## BrdU assay
A commercially available BrdU assay (Merck-Millipore, QIA58) was used to determine the cell proliferation. The principle of the BrdU assay is to incorporate a thymidine analogue into the newly synthesized DNA strands of proliferating cells.
ASC52telo cells were seeded onto a 96-well plate at a mean number of 2,5×103/cm2. After preincubation for 24 h, the standard growth medium was changed to a medium containing anaesthetics at concentrations ranging from 6.25 to 12.5%. Cells were cultured in the presence of anaesthetics for 24, 48, and 72 h, after which the medium was discarded. BrdU reagent was added to each well and the cells were incubated for 24 h. After incubation, the cells were washed thrice with wash buffer in the next step, and a primary antibody was added to the wells. After 1-hour incubation and subsequent washing, a secondary antibody was added and the plates were incubated for 30 min at room temperature. Before adding the TMB solution, the cells were washed for the final time. After the 30-minute incubation period, the stop solution was added to the plate, and the absorbance of the final solution was read at 450/550 nm on a microplate reader (iMark).

5.4.2.

## xCELLigence system
Cell proliferation was analysed using the xCELLigence system (Roche). This system is based on electrical impedance measurements. Such changes in cell number, surface attachment, and morphology influence minor impedance fluctuations that the microelectrodes can detect.
The culture medium was added to the E-plate wells and incubated at 37°C for 10 min. Subsequently, the background impedances of the plate wells were measured. The cells were then seeded at a density of 3 × 103/cm2 and incubated for half an hour at 37°C. Samples containing different concentrations of the tested substances were subjected to xCELLigence analysis for 72 h.

5.5.

## Immunophenotype
Evaluation of the expression level of antigens found on the surface of cells allows their identification and provides information on their role in the immune system. The markers chosen for assessment were selected based on their relevance to our research objectives and their established association with stem cell behaviour and function. CD44, CD90, CD34, and CD45 were specifically targeted due to their documented roles in stem cell biology and potential involvement in mediating cellular responses to anaesthetics [ ]. To analyse the ASC52telo cell immunophenotype, cells were first seeded at a density of 8 × 103/cm2. After 24-hour preincubation, the standard growth medium was replaced with one containing 12.5% mixture of anaesthetics. The cells were detached from the growth surface using trypsin, centrifuged, and suspended in PBS (Corning, 21-040-CV). Fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies were then added to the cell suspensions. After 30 min of incubation, PBS was added and the cells were centrifuged and washed twice. Finally, ASC52telo cells were suspended in FBS (554656; BD Biosciences) and analysed by flow cytometry (FACS Canto II, BD Bioscience).

5.6.

## Cell cycle
The Tali Cell Cycle Kit (A10798; Invitrogen) was used to evaluate cell cycle progression. This protocol is based on the fact that the amount of genetic material in a cell correlates with the phase of the cell cycle. Propidium iodide binds stoichiometrically to cellular DNA, making the degree of fluorescence proportional to the amount of genetic material.
The cells were first seeded at a mean density of 8 × 103/cm2 onto the 6-well plates. After 24-hour preincubation, the standard growth medium was changed to a medium containing 12.5% anaesthetics for 24, 48, and 72 h. The cells were detached from the surface, centrifuged, and resuspended in PBS. The cells were centrifuged again, fixed in 70% ethanol solution, and incubated at −20°C. After centrifugation, ethanol was discarded, and ASC52telo cells were suspended in PBS. The cells were centrifuged and suspended in Tali Cell Cycle Solution. The cells were ready for analysis after the final 30-minute-long incubation period.

5.7.

## Apoptosis
The FITC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit II (556570; BD Biosciences) was used to determine cell apoptosis. The fundamental principle of this method is that Annexin V displays a high affinity for anionic phospholipid phosphatidylserine (PS), located on the inner surface of the plasma membrane in normal cells. During apoptosis, the fluidity of the cell membrane changes, and PS molecules move to the outer surface of this structure. Fluorescently labelled annexin binds to PS and yields a signal that can be measured using a cytometer.
To perform the test, the cells were seeded at a number of 6,5×103/cm2 on 6-well plates. After 24-hour preincubation, a 12.5% anaesthetic mixture was added to the plate. The cells were detached from the growth surface, centrifuged, and resuspended in PBS. The binding buffer, propidium iodide, and annexin V were added to the suspension at appropriate volumes. After incubation, binding buffer was added, and ASC52telo cells were analysed using a cytometer.

5.8.

## Statistical analysis
The results are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of at least three independent experiments with three replicates per point. All analyses were performed using STATISTICA 13.1 (StatSoft, Cracow, Poland). The Student’s t-test was used for two-group comparisons. Welch’s t-test was used for samples with unequal variance. A confidence level of 95% was used for all the statistical tests.
