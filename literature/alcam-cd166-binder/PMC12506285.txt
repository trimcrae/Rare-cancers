## Background
Anti-melanoma differentiation-associated protein 5 antibody positive dermatomyositis (MDA5+ DM) is an autoimmune disease related to rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease (RPILD) with high mortality. However, the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM with RPILD remains unclear. We aimed to explore the peripheral immune landscape of MDA5+ DM with RPILD using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq).

## Methods
We performed scRNA-seq of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from MDA5+ DM with RPILD (n = 4), MDA5+ DM with ILD (non-RPILD, n = 3), and healthy controls (HCs, n = 3).

## Results
The proportion of CD14+ monocytes increased, but the proportion of natural killer cells, CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD compared with HCs. Obvious antiviral response was the main feature of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, and the expression of several interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) related to RIG-I pathway increased, including IRF7, DDX60, IFI27 and IFI6. However, this antiviral response was not significant in MDA5+ DM with ILD. In addition, multiple immune pathways were downregulated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, including antigen processing and presentation, translation initiation, mRNA splicing, and activation of T and B cells. Cell communication analysis revealed that multiple signaling pathways, including MHC-I and MHC-II, were attenuated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. Notably, MHC-II signaling was absent in CD4+ naïve T cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD.

## Conclusions
This study demonstrates that antiviral response plays an important role in the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, as well as changes in downstream immune pathways, providing potential therapeutic targets for future treatment.

## Introduction
Anti-melanoma differentiation-associated protein 5 (MDA5) antibody positive dermatomyositis (MDA5+ DM) is a special subtype of dermatomyositis characterized by a series of unique skin and systemic manifestations, including skin ulcers and interstitial lung disease (ILD). Among them, ILD is the most common complication of MDA5+ DM, and in many cases can develop into rapidly progressing ILD (RPILD) [ – ].
MDA5+ DM was first reported by Sato et al. in a cohort of clinically amyopathic dermatomyositis (CADM) in 2005, characterized by a high incidence of RPILD without muscle involvement [ ]. Subsequent research quickly recognized this unique subtype of dermatomyositis [ ]. In recent years, new progress has been made in the treatment of MDA5+ DM, and the combination of high-dose glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants can improve the prognosis of patients to a certain extent [ , ]. However, MDA5+ DM still has a high mortality rate, and the poor response of patients with RPILD to traditional immunosuppressive therapy is the main cause of death for MDA5+ DM [ ]. A recent retrospective study showed that more than 50% of patients with MDA5+ DM in China developed RPILD, with a mortality rate of about 40% [ ].

MDA5, encoded by interferon (IFN) induced with helicase C domain 1 (IFIH1), is an intracellular sensor of viral RNA that triggers innate immune responses. After binding to dsRNA, MDA5 interacts with mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein (MAVS), leading to the transcription of genes encoding IFN [ ]. Interestingly, some studies have shown striking similarities between MDA5+ DM and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) [ , ]. These evidences indicate that viral infection and antiviral immune response play important roles in the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM.
In recent years, there have been many new understandings about the etiology of MDA5+ DM. Multiple immune cells, including innate and adaptive immunity, are involved in the pathophysiology of MDA5+ DM [ , ]. However, the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM is still poorly understood, especially when combined with RPILD. Here, we analyzed the peripheral immune landscape of patients with MDA5+ DM with RPILD by collecting peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). We hope that this study will expand the understanding of the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, and provide new clues for future therapeutic targets.

## Results

## Single-cell landscapes of PBMCs from patients and controls
To demonstrate the peripheral immune response of MDA5+ DM in detail, we collected PBMCs from patients and healthy controls (HCs) for scRNA-seq (Fig.  A, Supplementary Table). We divided 10 samples into three groups: MDA5+ DM with RPILD (n = 4), MDA5+ DM with ILD (non-RPILD, n = 3) and HCs (n = 3). After quality screening, we obtained a total of 28,120 cells, with an average of 1607 genes and 3886 unique molecular identifiers per cell (Supplementary Figure S1A). Through unsupervised clustering, we identified 16 cell types based on their marker genes, including CD14+ monocytes (CD14+ LYZ+, also known as classical monocytes), CD16+ monocytes (FCGR3A+ LYZ+, also known as non-classical monocytes), natural killer (NK) cells (GNLY+ NKG7+), dendritic cells (DCs, CD1C+ FCER1A+), CD4+ naïve T cells (CD3D+ CD4+ CCR7+), CD4+ memory T cells (CD3D+ CD4+ AQP3+), CD8+ naïve T cells (CD3D+ CD8A+ CCR7+), CD8+ memory T cells (CD3D+ CD8A+ GZMK+), CD8+ cytotoxic T cells (CD3D+ CD8A+ GZMA+), proliferative T cells (CD3D+ MKI67+), double-negative T (DNT) cells (CD3D+ CD4− CD8−), B cell (CD79A+ MS4A1+), plasma cells (MZB1+ CD38+), neutrophils (CSF3R+ FCGR3B+), erythrocytes (HBA1+ HBB+) and megakaryocytes (PF4+ PPBP+) (Fig.  B–D and Supplementary Figure S1B). The basis for naming the cell clusters and the selection of marker genes were based on CellMarker 2.0 [ ]. 
The proportion of multiple cell types in the three groups showed distinct changes, suggesting that both innate and adaptive immunity are involved in the pathogenesis (Fig.  A and 2B). Most cell types did not show statistical differences among different groups due to the small sample size, but we still observed some trends of changes in the two disease groups (Fig.  C). In innate immunity, the proportion of CD14+ monocytes in the two MDA5+ DM groups was higher than that in HC group, while the proportion of NK cells was lower. In adaptive immunity, the proportion of B cells and most types of T cells in the two MDA5+ DM groups were lower than those in the HC group, and this phenomenon was especially obvious in the MDA5+ DM with RPILD group. Notably, we identified a class of poorly differentiated T cells, defined as DNT cells. Compared with the HC group, DNT cells were increased in the MDA5+ DM group, especially in the MDA5+ DM with RPILD group, suggesting that this cluster of cells may reflect the progression of the disease. Recent studies showed that patients with MDA5+ DM had increased CD14+ monocytes and decreased NK cells and T cells, which proved the reliability of our results [ , ]. In addition, further studies showed that significant reductions in peripheral T cells and NK cells were associated with disease activity and poor prognosis [ , ]. In general, these evidences prove that CD14+ monocytes, NK cells and T cells are likely to be involved in the development of the disease. 

## Distinct antiviral response in innate immune cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD
As is well known, antiviral response and type I IFN pathway play a crucial role in MDA5+ DM [ , ]. However, the downstream pathophysiological changes triggered by antiviral response are still unclear. Next, we investigated the changes in innate immune cells in the three groups of samples. Compared with the HC group, the CD14+ monocytes and CD16+ monocytes in the MDA5+ DM with RPILD group showed significantly increased expression of multiple IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs), including ISG15, IFIT3, IFI27, and IFI44L, suggesting that these two monocytes play a major role in the antiviral response (Fig.  A and Supplementary Figure S2A). In addition, CD74 and multiple MHC class II molecules in CD14+ monocytes, CD16+ monocytes and DCs were significantly decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD group, suggesting that the antigen processing and presentation function were impaired. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis that four kinds of innate immune cells were enriched in antiviral response pathways, including “type I IFN signaling pathway”, “IFN-gamma-mediated signaling pathway” and “viral process” (Fig.  B and Supplementary Figure S2B–D). Moreover, several pathways were enriched in four kinds of innate immune cells, including “translational initiation”, “SRP - dependent cotranslational protein targeting to membrane”, “RNA splicing”, “mRNA splicing via spliceosome” and “antigen processing and presentation”. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) showed that the scores of these pathways were significantly decreased in the four innate immune cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, while MDA5+ DM with ILD was less affected (Fig.  C). 
Considering the obvious antiviral response of innate immune cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, we further investigated the DEGs in type I IFN signaling pathway (Supplementary Figure S3A) and “IFN - gamma - mediated signaling pathway” (Supplementary Figure S3B). In addition, we identified the ISGs in DEGs with reference to a previous gene set [ ] (Supplementary Figure S3C). Several key ISGs are highly expressed in innate immune cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, including EIF2AK2, IFIT1, IFIT3, IFITM3, IRF7, ISG15, MX1, MX2, OAS2 and RSAD2 [ ] (Fig.  D and Supplementary Figure S4A), suggesting the activation of antiviral response. It is worth noting that the antiviral response of the MDA5+ DM with RPILD was stronger than that of the MDA5+ DM with ILD, indicating that the intensity of antiviral response was related to disease activity. In addition, some novel ISGs were identified to be significantly elevated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, including DDX60, IFI27, IFI44, IFI44L, and IFI6. In brief, DDX60 can activate IFN response [ ], while IFI27 [ ], IFI44 [ ], IFI44L [ ], and IFI6 [ ] can inhibit IFN response. These reflect the complex positive and negative feedback regulation process in antiviral response.
Antigen processing and presentation play a crucial role in the antiviral process [ ]. Our results showed that the expression of most MHC class I and II molecules in the innate immune cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD was significantly reduced, indicating impaired antigen processing and presentation (Fig.  E and Supplementary Figure S4B). In addition, CD74, which regulates antigen presentation through the MHC-II pathway [ ], was significantly reduced in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. Similarly, the antigen processing and presentation of MDA5+ DM with ILD were less damaged.
Ribosome biogenesis is involved in “translation initiation” and “SRP - dependent cotranslational protein targeting to membrane” [ ], and plays an important role in cell proliferation, differentiation and apoptosis [ ]. The expression of ribosomal protein genes in innate immune cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD was generally reduced, reflecting the impairment of ribosomal biogenesis (Supplementary Figure S3D). Notably, NCL and NPM1, two genes that play key roles in ribosome biogenesis [ ], were significantly decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  F and Supplementary Figure S4C).
mRNA splicing via spliceosome, especially alternative splicing, plays a crucial role in gene regulation [ ]. Our results showed that the expression of genes involved in mRNA splicing via spliceosome was generally decreased in innate immune cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Supplementary Figure S3E). Consistent with GO enrichment analysis, NK cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD were most severely affected, reflecting that the impaired mRNA splicing may be related to the reduction of NK cells. In addition, HNRNPL and HNRNPA1, two genes that play important roles in alternative mRNA splicing [ ], were significantly decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  G and Supplementary Figure S4D).
Together, these results suggest that innate immune cells with MDA5+ DM with RPILD have hyperactivated antiviral response and impaired functions in antigen processing and presentation, ribosome biogenesis and mRNA splicing. In addition, these changes were relatively small in MDA5+ DM with ILD, suggesting that these signaling pathways may be related to the disease activity of MDA5+ DM.

## Features of T cell subsets in MDA5+ DM
To analyze the changes of T cells in MDA5+ DM in detail, we divided T cells into different subsets for subsequent analysis (Supplementary Figure S5A). CD8+ cytotoxic T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD showed the strongest antiviral response, while ribosomal protein genes were most markedly reduced in DNT cells (Fig.  A and Supplementary Figure S5B). Consistent with innate immune cells, “type I IFN signaling pathway”, “translational initiation”, and “mRNA splicing via spliceosome” were significantly enriched in T cells (Fig.  B, 4 C and Supplementary Figure S6A–D). In addition, “T cell activation” and “T cell receptor signaling pathway” were also enriched in different T cell subsets. GSVA showed that “mRNA splicing via spliceosome”, “translational initiation” and “T cell activation” were significantly decreased in T cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD, while only a part of T cells in MDA5+ DM with ILD were affected (Fig.  D). It is worth noting that these changes are most obvious in DNT cells and correlated with disease activity, suggesting that these pathways may affect the function of this T cell subset. 
Similarly, T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD showed an enhanced antiviral response (Supplementary Figure S7A–C). Notably, the expression of ISGs in CD8+ cytotoxic T cells was the highest in different T cell subsets (Fig.  E and Supplementary Figure S8A).
T cell signaling is essential for T cell development and activation, and dysregulation of T cell receptor signaling can lead to loss of T cell function [ ]. Considering that in the two groups of MDA5+ DM, the proportion of most T cell subsets decreased while the proportion of DNT cells increased, we suspected that the disease state affected the activation of T cells. The results showed that genes associated with T cell activation in both disease groups were generally decreased in different T cell subsets, especially in DNT cells (Fig.  F, Supplementary Figure S7D and S8B). The decrease in the expression of these genes proved that the activation of T cells was impaired in MDA5+ DM, and correlated with disease activity.
Ribosome biogenesis and mRNA splicing via spliceosome were significantly impaired in T cells with MDA5+ DM with RPILD, especially in DNT cells (Supplementary Figure S7E and S7F). Similarly, the expression levels of key genes for ribosome biogenesis (NCL and NPM1) and alternative mRNA splicing (HNRNPL and HNRNPA1) were significantly decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  G, 4H, Supplementary Figure S8C and S8D).
Collectively, our results showed that T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD exhibited similar features to innate immune cells, including enhanced antiviral response, attenuated ribosome biogenesis and mRNA splicing. Importantly, the development and activation of T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD were significantly attenuated. This finding may explain the significant decrease of T cells in MDA5+ DM [ , ].

## Features of B cells and plasma cells in MDA5+ DM
B cells and plasma cells also have a potential pathogenic role in MDA5+ DM with RPILD [ ]. Therefore, we explored the changes of B cells and plasma cells in the disease. The down-regulated genes in B cells and plasma cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD were mainly ribosomal protein genes and MHC class II molecules (Fig.  A and Supplementary Figure S9A). Consistently, “translation initiation” and “antigen processing and presentation” were enriched in B cells and plasma cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  B and Supplementary Figure S9B). GSVA showed that the functions of “translation initiation”, “antigen processing and presentation”, “B cell activation” and “B cell receptor signaling pathway” were weakened in MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  C). 
B cells, but not plasma cells, in MDA5+ DM with RPILD showed an enhanced antiviral response (Supplementary Figure S9C–E). Compared with HCs, IFI44, IFI44L and MX1 were significantly increased in B cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  D and Supplementary Figure S10A). In addition, the expression of multiple genes involved in antigen processing and presentation was significantly reduced in B cells and plasma cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Fig.  E and Supplementary Figure S10B). Moreover, many genes related to “B cell receptor signaling pathway” were markedly down-regulated in B cells and plasma cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD (Supplementary Figure S9F), including CD79A, CD79B, PTPRC, BTK and SYK (Fig.  F and Supplementary Figure S10C). These results demonstrated that B cell signaling was attenuated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. Furthermore, the ribosome biogenesis of B cells and plasma cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD was impaired (Supplementary Figure S9G), and the expression of NCL and NPM1 was decreased (Fig.  G and Supplementary Figure S10D).

## Cell communication
We used CellChat to analyze cell communication among major immune cells. The results showed that the number of inferred interactions and interaction strength were significantly reduced in the two disease groups, and were related to disease severity (Supplementary Figure S11A and S11B). Except for CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, the outgoing and incoming interaction strength of most immune cells were significantly decreased in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, while the interaction strength of DNT cells almost disappeared (Supplementary Figure S11C). Notably, EGF and NRG signaling pathways only appeared in two disease groups (Fig.  A and Supplementary Figure S11D). In contrast, many signaling pathways related to immune activation were significantly attenuated in the two disease groups, including MHC-I, MHC-II, CCL, GALECTIN, CD45, CD22, LCK, CD6, ALCAM, CD86, SELPLG, CLEC, APP, SEMA4, BTLA, NEGR, IL16 and CD48. 
EGF and NRG participate in EGFR signaling pathway and play an important role in cell development [ ]. Recent studies have found that viral infection can trigger downstream immune responses through the EGFR signaling pathway [ ]. We found that EGF and NRG signaling pathways were mainly appeared in DNT cells and DCs in the two disease groups (Fig.  B), suggesting that the EGFR pathway induced by virus infection may lead to the inactivation of DNT cells.
Professional antigen presenting cells (APCs) in PBMCs mainly include DCs, monocytes and B cells. They activated CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells to perform antigen processing and presentation through MHC-I and MHC-II signaling pathways, respectively [ ]. Consistent with our previous results, the MHC-I signaling of MDA5+ DM with RPILD was attenuated (Supplementary Figure S11E), while the MHC-II signaling was almost exhausted (Fig.  C). Next, we explored the changes in the major ligand-receptor pairs between the professional APCs and T cell subsets in the three groups (Fig.  D). Compared with HCs, ligand-receptor pairs associated with MHC-I signaling were enhanced in CD8+ cytotoxic T cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD, but weakened in CD8+ memory T cells and CD8+ naïve T cells. In addition, ligand-receptor pairs associated with MHC-II signaling were largely attenuated in CD4+ memory T cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD, and completely absent in CD4+ naïve T cells. Consistently, CD22–PTPRC and LCK–(CD8A + CD8B1), which play key roles in T cell activation, were significantly attenuated in the two disease groups. In addition, the interactions of ALCAM–CD6, APP–CD74, IL16–CD4, LGALS9–CD44, LGALS9–CD45 and SELPLG–SELL were attenuated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, suggesting that the overall immune activity of T cells was weakened. In contrast, some interactions associated with infection and inflammation, including SIGLEC1 –SPN and THBS1 – CD47, were enhanced in T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD.

## Discussion
In recent years, two scRNA-seq studies on MDA5+ DM have revealed the important role of viral infection and type I IFN in the pathogenesis [ , ]. However, the characteristics of MDA5+ DM with RPILD and the changes of downstream pathways are still largely unknown. Unlike previous studies, we focused on the immune landscape of PBMCs in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, and compared them with a relatively mild disease group. We found that in addition to the distinct antiviral response, MDA5+ DM with RPILD had impaired functions in multiple immune pathways, including antigen processing and presentation, translation initiation, mRNA splicing, and activation of T and B cells. Notably, MHC-II signaling pathway was almost exhausted in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. Changes in these signaling pathways also occurred in MDA5+ DM with ILD, but to a lesser extent. This further demonstrates that these pathways play an important role in the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM, and are related to the activity of the disease.
Several studies have demonstrated that viral infection and type I IFN are closely related to the development of MDA5+ DM [ , ]. In this study, we found that PBMCs in MDA5+ DM with RPILD exhibited significant antiviral response, several important ISGs were identified, including DDX60, EIF2AK2, IFI27, IFI44, IFI44L, IFI6, IFIT1, IFIT3, IFITM3, IRF7, ISG15, MX1, MX2, OAS1, OAS2, OAS3 and RSAD2. The antiviral response was most obvious in CD14+ monocytes, CD16+ monocytes, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and DCs from MDA5+ DM with RPILD, which was generally consistent with previous results [ ]. Importantly, the antiviral response of the MDA5+ DM with RPILD was stronger than that of the MDA5+ DM with ILD, indicating a high correlation between antiviral response and disease activity. As is well known, several antiviral effectors play important roles in blocking the process of virus transport, translation and replication, including MX proteins, OAS proteins, IFIT proteins, EIF2AK2, IFITM3, ISG15 and RSAD2 [ ]. IRF7 is a key positive regulator in the RIG-I-like receptor pathway, which can induce the expression of IFN and a subset of ISGs [ ]. In addition, DDX60 activated the RIG-I-mediated IFN response [ ], while IFI27 and IFI6 attenuated the RIG-I-mediated IFN response [ , ]. These results demonstrated the critical role of RIG-I signaling pathway in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, which was consistent with a recent study [ ]. Furthermore, some studies have found that IFI27, IFI44, IFI44L and IFI6 have important roles in the pathogenesis of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and MDA5+ DM, especially IFI27 [ , ]. It has been reported that the gene expression of IFI27 is associated with the prognosis of COVID-19, and is also upregulated in other respiratory viral infections [ ]. Therefore, IFI27 may become a new candidate molecular marker for the occurrence and development of MDA5+ DM with RPILD.
The expansion of DNT cells has been confirmed in a variety of autoimmune diseases, including autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS), SLE and Sjogren's syndrome [ , ]. However, there has been no report on DNT cells in MDA5+ DM. This study found that the proportion of CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells decreased, while DNT cells expanded in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. For the expansion of DNT cells, a general hypothesis is that the abnormal Fas-mediated apoptosis affects the clearance of DNT cells in the peripheral blood [ ]. In addition, our study showed that EGF and NRG pathways were activated in the DNT cells of MDA5+ DM. EGF and NRG are involved in EGFR pathway, and virus infection can affect cell proliferation and homeostasis through EGFR signaling pathway [ , ]. Therefore, the EGF and NRG pathways of DNT cells may be the therapeutic targets for MDA5+ DM with RPILD.
MHC-I and MHC-II molecules present antigen peptides to CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells for antigen processing and presentation, respectively. Activated T cells can eliminate pathogens in infected cells and help B cells produce antibodies [ ]. However, some viral infections can lead to a decrease in the expression of MHC class I and II molecules to achieve immune escape [ , ]. Here, we found that the function of antigen processing and presentation in MDA5+ DM with RPILD was significantly reduced, especially MHC-II signaling. CD74, a gene that plays a key role in MHC-II signaling pathway [ ], was significantly decreased in professional APCs from MDA5+ DM with RPILD. In addition, the results of cell communication showed that ligand-receptor pairs related to MHC-II signaling pathway were depleted in CD4+ naïve T cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD. These results suggest that virus infection may mainly block the MHC-II pathway of MDA5+ DM with RPILD and inhibit the activation of CD4+ naïve T cells.
The expression of MHC-I and MHC-II molecules can affect the activation of T cells and the interaction between T cells and B cells [ , ]. Our results showed that the proportion of T cells and B cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD was decreased. In addition, T cell and B cell receptor signaling pathways were significantly weakened in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. The interactions of important ligand-receptor pairs related to T cell activation were attenuated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, including CD22 – PTPRC and LCK − (CD8A + CD8B1). Moreover, a recent study showed that RIG-I pathway was activated in MDA5+ DM and induced apoptosis of T cells [ ], which was consistent with our results. Overall, these results may explain the decrease of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in patients with MDA5+ DM [ ].
mRNA splicing, especially alternative splicing, plays a crucial role in maintaining peripheral immune homeostasis [ ]. However, viruses can promote viral replication and evade antiviral response by regulating alternative mRNA splicing [ ]. Our results showed that mRNA splicing and key genes for alternative splicing (HNRNPL and HNRNPA1) were significantly attenuated in innate immune cells and T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD [ ]. In addition, PTPRC was decreased in T cells and B cells from MDA5+ DM with RPILD. PTPRC, which is regulated by HNRNPL, performs alternative splicing in T and B cells and plays a key role in T and B cell activation [ ]. These results suggest that alternative mRNA splicing may play an important role in MDA5+ DM with RPILD.
Ribosome biogenesis plays a key role in translation initiation [ ]. Similarly, viral infection can inhibit the translation initiation and ribosome biogenesis [ , ]. In this study, we found that the restriction of translation initiation and ribosome biogenesis were the most obvious manifestations of all cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. In addition, EIF2AK2, an ISG that plays a key role in antiviral response, can inhibit translation initiation in host cells [ ]. EIF2AK2 was significantly elevated in innate immune cells and T cells in MDA5+ DM with RPILD, suggesting that this may be a potential reason for the limited translation initiation. Consistently, NCL and NPM1, two key genes regulating ribosome biogenesis [ ], were decreased in most cells of MDA5+ DM with RPILD. Notably, COVID-19 can achieve immune escape by regulating the ribosome biogenesis of host cells through multiple pathways [ ]. This similarity further proved that antiviral response plays an important role in the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM with RPILD.
This study has some limitations. First, participants in the disease group were older than those in the HC group, and the patients received initial treatment with glucocorticoids, which may have some impact on the observed results. Second, the sample size we included was relatively small. Third, we did not conduct further experiments to verify the function of key genes. Finally, the platform we use was DNBelab C Series High-throughput Single-cell RNA Library Preparation Set V3.0 (MGI, China), which was not as common as 10 × genomics. In future research, we will validate our findings in larger patient cohorts and animal models.

## Conclusions
Our study provides novel insights into the pathogenesis of MDA5+ DM with RPILD. We found that MDA5+ DM with RPILD had obvious antiviral response, and multiple downstream immune signaling pathways were downregulated. Several ISGs related to RIG-I pathway including IRF7, DDX60, IFI27 and IFI6 were significantly elevated in MDA5+ DM with RPILD. These results provide clues for finding new therapeutic targets.
