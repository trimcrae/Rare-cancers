## 1. Introduction
Depression is a serious affective mental disorder characterized by anhedonia, cognitive and behavioral impairments, and autonomic dysfunction, significantly impacting patients’ social functioning.[ ] Moreover, nonadherence to treatment is a common challenge among patients with major affective disorders, often driven by comorbid substance use, illness severity, and side effects of antidepressants, all of which may significantly compromise clinical outcomes.[ ] Globally, over 350 million individuals are affected by depression, with China accounting for more than 100 million cases, representing 30% of the global total. Projections indicate that by 2030, depression will become the most prevalent disease worldwide.[ – ] At the same time, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated psychosocial stress in patients with major depression and reduced their quality of life, thereby increasing their likelihood of being affected by stress-related events.[ ] Its pathogenesis involves multiple factors, including neurotransmitter abnormalities, elevated inflammatory cytokines, dysfunctions in the hippocampus and hypothalamus, gut microbiota imbalance, and mitochondrial dysfunction.[ ]
Studies have demonstrated that Guizhi Decoction exhibits significant anti-inflammatory effects, capable of directly or indirectly inhibiting the activities of NF-κB and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways, thereby reducing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-6 and IL-1β in rats.[ , ] The NF-κB signaling pathway primarily regulates immune responses, inflammatory reactions, as well as cell growth and death, with pro-inflammatorycytokines being key activators of this classical pathway.[ ] The MAPK signaling pathway is a crucial intracellular signaling system involved in regulating various physiological activities, including cell growth, development, division, death, and the transmission and amplification of intercellular signals.[ ]
Guizhi Decoction, 1st recorded in Zhang Zhongjing “Treatise on Febrile and Miscellaneous Diseases,” is a classic formula composed of 5 medicinal ingredients: Guizhi (Cinnamomi Ramulus), Baishao (Paeoniae Radix Alba), roasted licorice, jujube, and ginger. Among these, Guizhi and Baishao serve as the principal components. Research has found that formulas containing Guizhi demonstrate a high efficacy rate in treating depression.[ ] Despite current clinical efficacy, systematic studies on the use of Guizhi Decoction in treating depression remain limited.
Network pharmacology, integrating disciplines such as biology, pharmacology, and information science, utilizes data mining and bioinformatics analyses to predict drug mechanisms of action and has been widely applied in modern research of traditional Chinese medicine, providing preliminary mechanistic insights for clinical treatment and basic research.[ ] Molecular docking technology can calculate the affinity and binding modes between receptors and ligands, thereby evaluating and screening core components for treating specific diseases.[ ] This study aims to systematically investigate the antidepressant mechanism of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair based on network pharmacology and molecular docking, providing bioinformatics data for subsequent clinical and basic research on this treatment. Therefore, we hypothesize that the active ingredients of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair may target multiple depression-related proteins and pathways, thereby exerting antidepressant effects. Moreover, molecular docking may further validate the interaction between core components and key targets, supporting their pharmacological potential.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Screening of potential active compounds and targets
Potential active compounds were screened from the TCMSP database (https://www.tcmsp-e.com/index.php) using “Cinnamomi Ramulus” and “Paeoniae Radix Alba” as keywords. Screening criteria were set as oral bioavailability (OB) ≥ 30% and drug-likeness (DL) ≥ 0.18. Corresponding target protein information was retrieved using compound IDs and imported into the UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/) to convert predicted target protein names into gene names.

## 2.2. Acquisition of depression-related targets
Depression-related targets were retrieved from the GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), OMIM (https://www.omim.org/), TTD (https://db.idrblab.net/ttd/), and DrugBank (https://www.drugbank.ca/) databases using “Depression” as the keyword. Data from these 4 databases were consolidated into an Excel file, duplicates were removed, and the UniProt database was used for further validation to obtain accurate disease target data.

## 2.3. Construction of protein–protein interaction (PPI) network
After intersecting drug targets with disease-related proteins, the data were imported into the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/), selecting “Homo sapiens” as the species and setting a medium confidence threshold (medium confidence = 0.4), with other parameters at default settings, to obtain the PPI network. The results were saved in TSV format and imported into Cytoscape 3.10.3 software (Cytoscape Consortium, San Diego) for topological analysis. Betweenness centrality was used to assess the importance of each node, reflecting the frequency a node acts as a bridge along the shortest path between 2 other nodes in the network. Higher values indicate greater importance, leading to the identification of core proteins.

## 2.4. Construction of drug–disease network
The active compounds of Cinnamomi Ramulus and Paeoniae Radix Alba, along with intersecting target proteins, were imported into Cytoscape 3.10.3 software for topological analysis to construct the compound–target interaction network. In this network, nodes represent compounds and targets, while edges represent interactions between them.

## 2.5. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis
The intersecting drug–disease targets were uploaded to the DAVID database (https://davidbioinformatics.nih.gov/), selecting “OFFICIAL_GENE_SYMBOL” as the gene identifier and “Homo sapiens” as the species. DAVID’s GO function was utilized to analyze the roles of antidepressant targets in gene functions from 3 aspects: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed on the intersecting targets to identify relevant signaling pathways associated with the antidepressant effects of Cinnamomi Ramulus and Paeoniae Radix Alba. The top 10 entries from BP, CC, and MF in GO functions, and KEGG pathways related to depression (P < .05) were selected as the main gene function enrichment processes and signaling pathways for the antidepressant effects of Cinnamomi Ramulus and Paeoniae Radix Alba, further predicting their mechanisms of action. Finally, the results were imported into the Bioinformatics platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) for visualization.

## 2.6. Molecular docking validation and screening
The intersection of core proteins from the PPI network and the top 10 proteins ranked by degree in the drug–disease topological analysis were used as receptors, with corresponding drug compounds as ligands for molecular docking. First, 2D structures of drug compounds were obtained from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and converted to 3D structures using Chem3D.exe software, saved as lig.mol2 files. Next, crystal structures of proteins were downloaded from the PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/), and PyMOLWin.exe software (Schrödinger, LLC, New York) was used to remove water molecules (H2O) and small molecule ligands, saving as rep.pdb files. The rep.pdb and lig.mol2 files were imported into AutoDockTools 1.5.7 software (The Scripps Research Institute, La Jolla), converted to PDBQT format, and active pocket files (grid.gpf) were generated. Finally, AutoDock Vina and PyMOLWin.exe were used for molecular docking and visualization. The graphical summary is shown in Figure .
 

## 3. Results

## 3.1. Acquisition of potential active compounds and targets for the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair
In the TCMSP database, using OB ≥ 30% and DL ≥ 0.18 as screening criteria, a total of 20 active compounds from the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair were identified, including 7 from Guizhi and 13 from Baishao. Subsequently, target prediction was performed for these compounds. By importing the predicted target protein names into the UniProt database, converting them into gene names, and eliminating duplicate entries as well as non-human targets, 87 target proteins associated with the active compounds of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair were ultimately obtained. Detailed data are presented in Table .
 

## 3.2. Screening of depression-related target proteins
A total of 5543, 1, 93, and 117 target genes were obtained from the GeneCards, OMIM, TTD, and DrugBank databases, respectively. After deduplication and integration, and subsequent validation via the UniProt database, 5502 depression-related target genes were acquired, as shown in Figure . The intersection between the targets of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair and the depression-related targets yielded 77 common targets, which represent the therapeutic targets for depression, as illustrated in Figure .
 

## 3.3. PPI network analysis
The 77 common targets were imported into the STRING database for PPI network analysis. With the species set to “Homo sapiens” and the confidence threshold set to medium (0.4), 612 interactions among 75 target genes were identified (Fig. ). The resulting TSV file from STRING was imported into Cytoscape 3.10.3 for topological analysis, facilitating the identification of core proteins and the visualization of the PPI network (Fig. ). In this network, 75 nodes and 612 edges were observed; the outer green circles represent the related proteins, while the inner red circles denote the core proteins, including RAC-αserine/threonine protein kinase (AKT1), prostaglandin G/H synthase 2 (PTGS2), IL-6, tumor necrosis factor (TNF), estrogen receptor 1 (ESR1), acetylcholinesterase (ACHE), solute carrier family 6 member 4, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG), cytochrome P450 family 3 subfamily A member 4 (CYP3A4), and NF-kappa B p65 protein (RELA).
 

## 3.4. Construction of the drug–disease network
Using Cytoscape 3.10.3, a topological analysis was conducted on the compound–target data to construct the drug–disease network (Fig. ). This network comprises 88 nodes and 110 edges, where the inner nodes represent target proteins and the outer circular nodes represent the active compounds of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair. The results indicated that the main active compounds in the network include kaempferol, β-sitosterol, pinocembrin/dihydroquercetin, and catechin.
 

## 3.5. GO enrichment analysis
The intersecting drug–disease targets were uploaded to the DAVID database for GO enrichment analysis, resulting in the identification of 506 BP terms. Using a threshold of P < .05, 415 significant GO terms were selected, including 287 BP terms, 48 CC terms, and 80 MF terms. The top 10 terms from BP, CC, and MF were then visualized using the Bioinformatics platform to generate bar charts (Fig. ) and bubble charts (Fig. ). In the bar chart, the horizontal axis represents the number of genes enriched in each pathway, while in the bubble chart, the horizontal axis represents the false discovery rate after multiple hypothesis testing correction. The size of each bubble reflects the number of genes enriched in the corresponding pathway, and the color gradient (from green to red) indicates significance, with deeper red denoting higher significance. These findings suggest that the antidepressant mechanism of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair may be associated with BPs such as response to xenobiotic stimulus, response to hypoxia, positive regulation of the MAPK cascade, response to lipopolysaccharide, and signal transduction; involve CCs such as the postsynaptic membrane, synapse, dendritic membrane, and cytosol; and pertain to MFs including enzyme binding, protein homodimerization activity, heme binding, transcription coactivator binding, and G protein-coupled serotonin receptor activity.
 

## 3.6. KEGG pathway analysis
KEGG pathway enrichment analysis performed via the DAVID database identified 136 pathways related to the antidepressant effects of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair. Based on a threshold of P < .05, 127 pathways were selected. The top 44 pathways, ranked by P value, were visualized as a bubble chart using the Bioinformatics platform, where the horizontal axis represents the false discovery rate after multiple hypothesis testing correction. In this chart, the size of each bubble corresponds to the number of genes enriched in the pathway, and the color gradient (from green to red) reflects the significance, with deeper red indicating greater enrichment significance (Fig. ). The potential signaling pathways involved in the antidepressant effects of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair include the TNF signaling pathway, neuroactive ligand–receptor interaction, calcium signaling pathway, IL-17 signaling pathway, apoptosis, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, T helper 17 (Th17) cell differentiation, and NF-kappa B signaling pathway.
 

## 3.7. Molecular docking results
Binding free energies lower than −5 kJ/mol indicate favorable binding affinity between receptors and ligands.[ ] Molecular docking analyses were conducted to assess the binding affinities between core target proteins (AKT1, PTGS2, IL-6, TNF, ESR1, ACHE, PPARG, CYP3A4, and RELA) and their corresponding active compounds : kaempferol, (−)-dihydroquercetin, β-sitosterol, epicatechin, dihydroquercetin, (+)-catechin, and paeoniflorin. The results demonstrated that all target proteins exhibited binding affinities with their respective compounds below −5 kJ/mol. Detailed data are presented in Table . Molecular docking models illustrating the interactions between target proteins and their high-affinity compounds are presented in Figure . These findings suggest that the active components of the drug possess strong binding capabilities with their corresponding targets.
 

## 4. Discussion
Results of this study indicated that, through network pharmacology analysis, 77 potential targets were identified for the active compounds of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair in the treatment of depression. Moreover, 10 key targets were found in the PPI network, namely AKT1, PTGS2, IL-6, TNF, ESR1, ACHE, solute carrier family 6 member 4, PPARG, CYP3A4, and RELA. To determine the efficacy of Guizhi–Baishao active compounds against depression, it is essential to consider their pharmacokinetic properties. In this study, only compounds with OB ≥ 30% and DL ≥ 0.18 were regarded as pharmacokinetically active, as these are more likely to be absorbed and distributed in the human body. A total of 20 bioactive ingredients meeting these criteria were identified and mapped to the 77 intersecting targets. The network analysis revealed that the main active compounds included kaempferol, β-sitosterol, dihydroquercetin, catechin, epicatechin, and paeoniflorin, all of which may play key roles in the treatment of depression.
Depression is a common psychiatric disorder characterized by complex and multifactorial pathogenesis and has become a prevalent global health concern. One of the most widely accepted is the monoamine neurotransmitter hypothesis, which posits that depression is closely associated with reduced synthesis and secretion of monoamine neurotransmitters such as serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine.[ ] Additionally, the inflammation hypothesis suggests that aberrant inflammatory factors can activate inflammatory responses, leading to elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL and TNF, thereby causing inflammatory damage to neurons and glial cells.[ ] The neuroplasticity hypothesis has emerged as a significant breakthrough in depression etiology research. The hippocampus plays a pivotal role in neuroplasticity, and structural and functional impairments in the hippocampus and its neurons are considered central factors in depression. This process is closely linked to the expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF).[ , ] The neurotrophic factor hypothesis further asserts that downregulation of BDNF, a crucial neurotrophic factor in the central nervous system, can lead to mood deterioration and subsequently to depression.[ , ] Moreover, depression is closely related to dysfunction of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis. The HPA axis, comprising the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, adrenal glands, and related target organs, plays an essential role in regulating stress responses. Hyperactivity of the HPA axis and disrupted negative feedback mechanisms can result in elevated glucocorticoid levels, triggering a series of physiological reactions that are key factors in the onset of depression.[ ] Currently available treatments such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors can alleviate symptoms but are often limited by slow onset, treatment resistance, and significant side effects.[ ] Therefore, the search for safer and more effective alternative therapies is an urgent need.
Both Guizhi and Baishao have demonstrated various pharmacological activities, potentially acting on multiple targets and pathways involved in depression. Studies suggest that the Guizhi–Baishao pair may exert antidepressant effects by inhibiting neuronal apoptosis and reducing the production and release of inflammatory mediators, thus alleviating inflammation and protecting neuronal cells.[ ] In this study, bioinformatics databases and tools were employed to integrate network pharmacology and molecular docking approaches to identify potential targets, pathways, and mechanisms underlying the antidepressant effects of the Guizhi–Baishao pair. These findings may provide a basis for developing safer and more effective traditional Chinese medicine-based interventions.
KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that, in addition to significant enrichment in IL-17 and TNF inflammatory signaling pathways, key components in the Guizhi–Baishao pair also target several other signaling cascades closely associated with depression pathogenesis. These include Th17 cell differentiation, NF-κ B signaling, and apoptotic pathways, suggesting that the herbal pair may act through multiple mechanisms to modulate the onset and progression of depression. IL-17A, also known as IL-17, is a signature cytokine secreted by Th17 cells, playing a vital role in maintaining immune homeostasis and regulating immune dysregulation. Concurrently, Th17 cells produce IL-17F, TNF, and other cytokines; thus, inhibiting Th17 cell activity is crucial for preventing excessive inflammatory responses.[ – ] As a central transcription factor in inflammatory responses, NF-κB functions by regulating downstream effector molecules.[ ] Cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 are key downstream molecules of the NF-κB signaling pathway, participating in various inflammatory responses and pathological processes.[ ] Studies have found that activating glial cells, modulating the imbalance of inflammatory cytokines and neurotrophic factors, and inhibiting apoptosis in hippocampal tissue can significantly improve depressive behaviors in rats.[ ]
Molecular docking analysis revealed strong binding affinities between multiple active compounds and key targets, indicating the multicomponent therapeutic potential of the Guizhi–Baishao pair. For example, kaempferol showed stable binding to AKT1, TNF, PTGS2, ACHE, PPARG, CYP3A4, and RELA; (−)-dihydroquercetin interacted with PTGS2 and RELA; paeoniflorin bound to IL-6 and TNF. These interactions highlight kaempferol as a core compound in the antidepressant mechanism. Kaempferol, a natural flavonoid, enhances neuronal anti-apoptotic capacity by modulating NF-κB and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways.[ ] Studies have shown that it reduces hippocampal neuronal apoptosis through anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and inhibition of autophagy in the hippocampus, thereby alleviating hippocampal damage in depressive model rats.[ ] Dihydroquercetin, a reduced product of quercetin, exhibits more pronounced antioxidant effects. Research indicates that dihydroquercetin can attenuate inflammatory responses by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and reducing the expression of IL-1β and IL-18.[ ] Moreover, kaempferol and quercetin not only inhibit the release of pro-inflammatory factors and improve neuroinflammation but also promote BDNF expression and neurogenesis, while modulating the balance of the HPA axis and monoamine neurotransmitters.[ ] Catechin has demonstrated notable anti-inflammatory effects by suppressing IL-1β and IL-6 secretion and inhibiting iNOS protein expression.[ ] Paeoniflorin, a multitarget monoterpene glycoside, possesses antidepressant, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective, and cognitive-enhancing pharmacological properties. It significantly lowers levels of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α, alleviating pathological processes related to various diseases, including cell proliferation, apoptosis, ferroptosis, and inflammation.[ , ]
Further analysis showed that most active compounds in the Guizhi–Baishao pair exhibit strong binding affinity to PTGS2. The binding energies of (−)-dihydroquercetin, β-sitosterol, (+)-catechin, epicatechin, kaempferol, and dihydroquercetin with PTGS2 were –7.5, –6.2, –8.7, –8.4, –9.3, and –9.1 kcal/mol, respectively, suggesting that PTGS2 may serve as a major target for the treatment of depression. PTGS2 (cyclooxygenase-2) mediates inflammatory responses by participating in lipid peroxidation control, regulating the secretion of pro-inflammatory signaling molecules, and playing a significant role in the pathophysiology of depression.[ ] In summary, the bioactive components of the Guizhi–Baishao pair may exert antidepressant effects by targeting PTGS2, which not only showed strong binding with major active compounds, but also ranked as a central hub in the PPI network with the second highest betweenness centrality, indicating its critical role within the network.
This study suggests that the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair exerts antidepressant effects via multiple compounds, multiple targets, and multiple signaling pathways, providing a solid theoretical foundation. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the findings rely heavily on the accuracy and timeliness of database information. Second, network pharmacology is based on static datasets and lacks dynamic simulation of BPs. Finally, as a predictive tool, molecular docking requires extensive experimental validation to support its reliability. Future work should focus on experimental studies to optimize models and improve predictive accuracy.

## 5. Conclusion
In conclusion, this study explored the potential mechanisms of the Guizhi–Baishao herbal pair in the treatment of depression using network pharmacology. The results suggest that bioactive compounds such as kaempferol, paeoniflorin, (+)-catechin, (–)-dihydroquercetin, β-sitosterol, epicatechin, and dihydroquercetin may exert antidepressant effects by interacting with key targets including AKT1, PTGS2, IL-6, TNF, ESR1, ACHE, PPARG, CYP3A4, and RELA, while also modulating major signaling pathways including IL-17, NF-κ B, and TNF. This supports a multicomponent, multitarget, and multipathway mechanism. Molecular docking analysis confirmed the strong binding interactions between these active compounds and their respective targets. While these findings provide valuable insights, further pharmacological and experimental studies are warranted to validate these conclusions.
