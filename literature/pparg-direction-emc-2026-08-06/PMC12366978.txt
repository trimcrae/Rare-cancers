## Abstract
Breast cancer (BRCA) is one of the most common neoplastic diseases among the female population. There were 43,780 BRCA-related deaths, with a mortality rate of 15% in the United States in 2022. Therefore, it is necessary to screen and validate potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers related to BRCA through bioinformatics technology. The GSE42568 and GSE5364 datasets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database, and the Cancer Genome Atlas BRCA dataset was sourced from the Xena functional genomics platform. Differentially expressed genes were identified across these 3 BRCA datasets using Venn diagram software. Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway enrichment analysis were carried to investigate the role of these genes and potential underlying mechanisms in BRCA. A protein-protein interaction network was constructed using the STRING database, and key genes were identified using the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape software. Hub genes were determined via Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and were validated using the GEPIA, UALCAN platforms. In addition, the expression of these genes in tumor and adjacent tissues of BRCA patients was validated by reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction. A total of 84 overlapping differentially expressed genes were preliminarily screened at the initial stage, including 33 upregulated and 51 downregulated genes. The top 20 highly connected key genes were identified subsequently using Cytoscape software. These hub genes show good consistency with the progression of the BRCA. In addition, the results showed that the expression levels of these 5 hub genes are significantly different among BRCA subtypes, which may provide support for identifying BRCA subtypes and optimizing treatment strategies. Four hub genes (AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2) were finally identified through reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction. When compared to normal tissues, all of these genes exhibit high expression levels in BRCA tissues. The expression levels of AURKA, DTL, and RRM2 are positively correlated with NUSAP1, indicating a common regulatory relationship among these genes. Our present study demonstrated that AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2 are potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for BRCA, and may also be potential treatment targets for clinical implication in the future.

## 1. Introduction
Breast cancer (BRCA) is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths among the female population.[ ] BRCA can be divided into 4 main subtypes according to molecular typing, including luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, and triple-negative.[ ] According to the GLOBOCAN data compiled by the International Agency for Research on Cancer from 185 countries, there were 2.3 million new cases of BRCA in 2018, accounting for 11.7% of all new cancer cases, with a mortality rate of 6.9%.[ ] The pathogenesis of BRCA remains incompletely understood. Research indicated that the occurrence and progression of BRCA are related to several risk factors, such as genetic tendency, elevated breast density, reproductive characteristics, dietary habits, obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and alcohol consumption.[ – ] Despite recent advancements in early detection and treatment that have contributed to a decline in BRCA mortality rates, preventing and treating BRCA remain ongoing challenges.[ , ]
The advancements in microarray analysis and genome-wide sequencing have accelerated the discovery of novel prognostic biomarkers, which have the potential to improve the accuracy of individualized survival predictions by reflecting the molecular progression of tumors. At present, significant progress has been made in the research on predictive biomarkers for BRCA. Estrogen receptor (ER) is one of the earliest and most commonly used BRCA biomarkers,[ ] with approximately 80% of BRCA being ER-positive (ER+). ER+ BRCA are typically well-differentiated, less invasive, and associated with a better prognosis. In contrast, ER-negative (ER−) BRCA are more aggressive and have a worse prognosis.[ , ] Yu et al identified a 2-gene signature (CRYAB and RBP4) associated with survival, which is significantly correlated with the diagnosis and prognosis of BRCA patients.[ ] These studies highlight the potential of genetic biomarkers for early diagnosis and prognostic evaluation in BRCA. However, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified by microarray are influenced by factors such as ethnicity, tumor staging, sample size, and grading. Therefore, the overlapping genes obtained from different microarrays may be more representative.
To seek potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for BRCA, we processed a series of analyses based on high‐throughput sequencing data obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) BRCA databases. As a result, we demonstrated that Aurora Kinase A (AURKA), denticleless E3 ubiquitin protein ligase homolog (DTL), Nucleolar Spindle Associated Protein 1 (NUSAP1), and Ribonucleotide Reductase M2 (RRM2), are all related to prognosis of patients with BRCA. Our study explored potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets for BRCA treatment.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Microarray data
TCGA BRCA dataset, which comprises mRNA expression and clinical data for 112 normal samples and 1075 tumor samples, was obtained from the Xena Functional Genomics Explorer (https://xenabrowser.net/). Subsequently, the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was searched using the keywords “breast cancer,” “papillary breast carcinoma,” “human,” and “microarray expression profiles” to obtain relevant mRNA expression and clinical data.[ ] From this search we selected and downloaded the datasets GSE5364 and GSE42568, which are based on the GPL96 Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Array and GPL570 Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array, respectively. These datasets meet the following criteria: (1) human breast tissue samples; (2) samples from both BRCA and normal tissues; and (3) a minimum of 50 samples. The dataset GSE5364, consists of 183 tumor samples and 13 normal tissue samples. In contrast, the dataset GSE42568, features 104 tumor samples and 17 normal tissue samples.

## 2.2. Data processing and identification of DEGs
In this study, the TCGA BRCA data were analyzed for differential expression using the Limma package and R×64 4.4.1; GEO2R (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r/) was used to identify overlapping DEGs between cancer tissues and normal tissues in the datasets GSE5364 and GSE42568. All datasets were selected based on the criteria that P < .05 and |Log2FC| > 2. Following this, we employed the online tool Jvenn (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/static/others/jvenn/example.html) to create a venn diagram and identify the shared differential genes across the 3 gene expression microarray datasets. Ultimately, we selected the upregulated and downregulated genes.

## 2.3. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis
Using the online tool DAVID (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/), we conducted GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses to reveal the functions of DEGs. DAVID is a tool designed for the functional annotation and enrichment analysis of gene or protein lists.[ ] It provides a range of bioinformatics functions, including GO analysis, KEGG pathway analysis, and other enrichment analyses, to help elucidate the biological significance of genes and their roles in various biological processes. P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

## 2.4. Protein-protein interactions (PPIs) network and module analysis
Grasping the functional interactions between proteins is crucial for unraveling the molecular mechanisms and metabolic processes underlying cancer. To explore potential interactions among overlapping DEGs, the STRING database (https://string-db.org) and the Molecular Complex Detection plugin in Cytoscape software were used.[ , ] This approach allows for the identification of hub nodes and the detection of densely connected clusters in the PPI network. In the end, the selection comprised the top 20 hub genes with connection degrees >10 and the top 2 modules with degrees >3.

## 2.5. Survival analysis using the selected hub genes
The survival analysis for the selected genes was conducted using the Kaplan–Meier Plotter online tool (https://kmplot.com/analysis/).

## 2.6. Hub genes validation by GEPIA
GEPIA is an online platform that utilizes data from Genotype-Tissue Expression and TCGA projects to analyze and visualize gene expression in cancer and normal tissues,[ ] helping to explore the role of genes in cancer and the potential of biomarkers. Following survival analysis, GEPIA was used to verify the upregulation and downregulation of the selected hub genes, as well as their contributions to BRCA tumor staging.

## 2.7. Hub genes validation on UALCAN
UALCAN is an integrated online platform designed for the analysis of cancer data, focusing on gene expression, clinical information, and survival data.[ ] Using UALCAN, we conducted a reanalysis of hub genes in relation to different subclasses of BRCA and their correlations.

## 2.8. Hub genes validation by reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
We collected tissue samples from 5 BRCA patients at Liaocheng People’s Hospital as the experimental group, with the control group being their corresponding adjacent noncancerous tissues. RNA was extracted from the tissues using the TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Waltham), and reverse transcribed into cDNA using a PrimeScript RT kit (Takara Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Dalian, China). Amplification was performed using a SYBR Green qPCR kit (Takara Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), and the primers listed in Table . GAPDH was selected as the internal reference gene. The statistical analysis of the data results was performed using the Student t test, with P < .05 considered statistically significant. This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Liaocheng People’s Hospital (no. 2025159).
 

## 3. Results

## 3.1. Identification of DEGs
This study was conducted according to the flowchart shown in Figure . Using R×64 4.4.1 and the Limma package, it is found that the TCGA BRCA dataset contains 2259 upregulated genes and 1263 downregulated genes, with P < .05 and |log2FC| ≥ 2 as the filtering criteria. From the GSE42568 dataset, 469 genes were found to be upregulated and 726 genes downregulated. In the GSE5364 dataset, 155 genes were upregulated and 141 genes downregulated. The volcano plot of the expression profile data is shown in Figure . To verify the accuracy of the DEGs in BRCA, we analyzed the overlapping DEGs across the 3 datasets, identifying 33 upregulated genes and 51 downregulated genes (Fig. , Table ).
 

## 3.2. Functional enrichment
In order to investigate the biological functions of 84 overlapping DEGs, we performed GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis using DAVID. The GO functional analysis revealed that the overlapping DEGs are predominantly enriched in biological process associated with cell proliferation and division, protein phosphorylation, lipid metabolism, responses to bacterial infection, and the negative regulation of gene expression. In terms of cellular components, these overlapping DEGs are primarily associated with the cytoplasm and nucleus, highlighting crucial structures involved in the cell division process, including the midbody, centrosome, and spindle. Regarding molecular functions, these overlapping DEGs predominantly exhibit protein kinase activity, binding capabilities, and signal transduction, which underscores their essential roles in cell proliferation, cell cycle regulation, and responses to external stimuli (Table ). The KEGG pathway analysis further indicated that the overlapping DEGs are implicated in the PPAR signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, and pathways associated with adipocytokines (Table ). These genes play significant roles in regulating cellular metabolism, proliferation, and apoptosis, while also influencing tumor inflammatory responses, hormone levels, and various metabolic pathways (Fig. ).
 

## 3.3. PPI network construction
A PPI network of overlapping DEGs was constructed using the online STRING database, comprising 186 nodes and 691 interactions to identify the relationships among genes. The network was then imported into Cytoscape to pinpoint hub genes, with the top 20 genes exhibiting the highest connectivity designated as hub genes (Table ). Module analysis uncovered significant clustering modules within the PPI network, resulting in the identification of the top 2 scoring modules (Fig. ). The vertex weighting analysis indicates that Module 1 has a score of 13.846, associated with cell division, proliferation, and development, while Module 2 has a score of 7.556, linked to glucose and lipid metabolism.
 

## 3.4. Kaplan–Meier analysis of hub genes
To assess the clinical significance of these key genes, we conducted a Kaplan–Meier analysis. The results indicated that patients with higher expression levels of AURKA (HR [hazard ratio] = 1.65, P < .001), DTL (HR = 1.52, P < .001), NUSAP1 (HR = 1.65, P < .001), and RRM2 (HR = 1.57, P < .001) had shorter overall survival, whereas patients with higher Fibroblast Growth Factor 2 (FGF2) expression (HR = 0.8, P = .019) had longer overall survival (Fig. ).
 

## 3.5. Validation of hub genes and clinical stage analyses
Utilizing GEPIA, an online platform that integrates data from Genotype-Tissue Expression and TCGA, we confirmed the expression of these 5 hub genes in the context of BRCA. As illustrated in Figure , the expression levels of the upregulated hub genes DTL, RRM2, AURKA, and NUSAP1 in BRCA are significantly elevated compared to adjacent normal tissues, whereas the expression level of the downregulated hub gene FGF2 is markedly reduced relative to adjacent normal tissues. These findings suggest that the selected hub genes display significant expression disparities in BRCA. We further investigated the expression of each hub gene in BRCA patients stratified by TNM stage. As shown in Figure , the expression levels of DTL, RRM2, AURKA, and NUSAP1 were higher in patients with clinical stages II, III, or IV compared to stage I, indicating a positive association between these upregulated hub genes and tumor progression. Conversely, the expression levels of FGF2 were lower in patients with clinical stages II, III, or IV than in stage I, implying that this downregulated hub gene may serve as a predictor for BRCA progression.
 

## 3.6. Validation of gene through UALCAN
We performed a reanalysis of the hub genes across different BRCA subtypes in UALCAN. The results revealed that the expression levels of these 5 genes varied among the different BRCA subtypes (Fig. ). A positive correlation was observed between DTL, RRM2, AURKA, and NUSAP1 (Fig. ). NUSAP1 showed correlation coefficients of 0.61, 0.71, and 0.61 with the other 3 genes, respectively.
 

## 3.7. Validation of hub gene through RT-qPCR
We validated the in vivo expression of hub genes such as AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, RRM2, and FGF2 by collecting tumor tissue samples from the affected sites of BRCA patients and their adjacent noncancerous tissues. The results showed that AURKA (P < .01), DTL (P < .01), NUSAP1 (P < .01), and RRM2 (P < .001) were highly expressed in tumor tissue samples, with statistically significant differences. Although FGF2 (P > .05) showed a slightly downregulated trend in tumor tissue samples, the difference was not significant (Fig. ).
 

## 4. Discussion
BRCA has the highest incidence rate among women, and listed as the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death from 2005 to 2020.[ ] Over the past decade, various technologies have been developed to assist in the diagnosis and treatment of BRCA. An increasing body of research has delved into cancer-associated biomarkers, particularly in the context of cervical cancer,[ ] BRCA,[ – ] and bladder cancer,[ ] employing sophisticated methodologies that encompass lncRNA,[ , ] miRNA,[ , ] and genetic polymorphisms.[ , ] but the pros and cons of each method restrict their use as stand-alone tools. Therefore, it is still necessary to search for more specific molecular targets for the diagnosis and treatment of BRCA.
In our study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of the GSE5364, GSE42568, and TCGA BRCA datasets, identifying 84 overlapping DEGs that are commonly differentially expressed across these datasets. Through further validation using online bioinformatics tools, 5 potential biomarkers for BRCA diagnosis and prognosis, including DTL, RRM2, AURKA, FGF2, and NUSAP1 were identified.
DTL, a homolog of E3 ubiquitin ligases, serve as a substrate receptor for Cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases and is a number of the DCAF protein family.[ ] Previous research indicates that DTL is aberrantly expressed in several cancer types, including BRCA, hepatocellular carcinoma, ovarian surface epithelial cancer, Ewing sarcoma, gastric cancer, and melanoma.[ – ]
DTL is crucial for DNA damage repair and cell cycle regulation, positioning it as a significant regulator of cell cycle progression and genome stability.[ , ]
Ribonucleotide reductase essential for DNA replication and repair, comprise 2 structural units: RRM1 and RRM2. The RRM2 subunit contains a binuclear iron center and a tyrosyl radical, which facilitate the enzymatic conversion of ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides.[ ]
RRM2 is synthesized during the late G1 phase prior to DNA replication and is degraded in the late S phase or early G2 phase, underscoring its vital role in DNA synthesis and repair.[ ] Additionally, RRM2 is involved in processes such as cell growth, migration, invasion, and aging.[ ] Diseases associated with RRM2 include glioblastoma,[ ] lung adenocarcinoma,[ ] and oral squamous cell carcinoma.[ ] Notably, Shah et al demonstrated that RRM2 is upregulated in both ER− and tamoxifen-resistant ER+ BRCA,[ ] highlighting its critical role in BRCA progression and drug resistance.
AURKA is a serine/threonine kinase that regulates centrosome stability and mitotic spindle formation. Its overexpression leads to tumorigenic transformation and DNA instability,[ ] which are associated with tumor growth, a basal-like phenotype and poor prognosis[ – ]; AURKA is implicated in several cancers, including skin cancer, colon cancer, and ovarian cancer.[ – ] It has been reported in studies that AURKA can translocate to the nucleus, where it enhances the phenotype of BRCA stem cells and promotes distinct oncogenic characteristics in malignant cells.[ ]
NUSAP1, a microtubule-binding protein, located on chromosome 15q15,[ ] plays a crucial role in key processes during mitosis, including spindle assembly, cytokinesis, and chromosome segregation.[ ] Several studies have demonstrated that NUSAP1 has an important regulatory role in tumors, including cervical cancer,[ ] colorectal cancer,[ ] lung cancer,[ ] prostate cancer,[ ] and BRCA,[ ] with high expression of NUSAP1 significantly associated with poor prognosis in BRCA.[ ] Functional cell experiments have confirmed that NUSAP1 is linked to the proliferation, migration, and invasion of BRCA cells.[ ]
FGF2, also known as basic FGF, is a 146-amino acid polypeptide that plays a crucial role in various cellular processes, including angiogenesis,[ ] cellular proliferation,[ ] development,[ ] and the maintenance of normal stem cells.[ ]
We mutually validated the expression profiles of AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, RRM2, and FGF2 in normal and cancer tissues using the TCGA, GEO, and GEPIA databases. The results showed that AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2 were highly expressed in tumor samples and lowly expressed in normal tissues, while FGF2 exhibited the opposite pattern. Survival curve analysis indicated that high expression of AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2 was associated with shorter survival, while higher expression of FGF2 was linked to longer survival. We further analyzed the expression patterns of these genes in different cancer stages and found that their expression varied across different stages of BRCA, suggesting that AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, RRM2, and FGF2 may play roles in cancer growth and progression, providing potential value for BRCA diagnosis and prognosis prediction.
BRCA is a highly heterogeneous malignant tumor, and subtyping plays a critical role in guiding precise treatment and prognostic assessment. Gene expression is pivotal in the development and characteristic features of different BRCA subtypes, such as HER2-positive, triple-negative, and luminal types.[ ] In HER2-positive BRCA, overexpression or amplification of the HER2 gene activates pathways that promote tumor cell growth and invasion.[ ] Targeted therapies like trastuzumab are commonly used, but genetic changes related to drug resistance continue to present challenges.[ ] Triple-negative BRCA, which lacks ER, progesterone receptor, and HER2 expression, exhibits upregulation of genes associated with high proliferation and invasion. The tumor microenvironment also influences gene expression. While chemotherapy remains the main treatment option, the prognosis for triple-negative BRCA is generally poor. Luminal A type, characterized by high ER and progesterone receptor expression, low proliferation, and a better prognosis, is primarily driven by ER signaling.[ ] In contrast, luminal B is more aggressive, often exhibiting HER2 positivity and/or higher proliferation rates.[ ] Endocrine therapies for luminal subtypes may face resistance due to underlying genetic alterations. In our study, we identified 5 hub genes that show significant differential expression between normal tissue and various BRCA subtypes. AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2 are overexpressed in luminal, HER2-positive, and triple-negative types, while FGF2 expression is decreased. These findings indicate that these 5 hub genes have significant value in identifying the subtypes of BRCA and formulating follow-up treatment strategies.
In addition, we found a significant positive correlation between the expression levels of AURKA, DTL, RRM2, and NUSAP1, which prompted us to consider their potential synergistic roles in cellular functions and disease mechanisms. AURKA, DTL, RRM2, and NUSAP1 all play important roles in cell cycle regulation, with overlapping functions involved in cell division, DNA replication and repair, and microtubule dynamics. Moreover, the expression levels of these genes are closely associated with cancer cell proliferation and migration, suggesting that they may act synergistically to promote tumor growth and survival in cancer cells. Therefore, the coordinated expression of AURKA, DTL, RRM2, and NUSAP1 may provide new insights into early cancer diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and the identification of therapeutic targets.
Validating bioinformatics data through both in vivo and in vitro methods is crucial. We have validated that AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2 are highly expressed in BRCA patients using RT-PCR. However, FGF2 did not show statistical significance. The role of FGF2 in cancer is complex and has a dual nature. Research has shown that FGF2 can inhibit tumor cell proliferation through multiple mechanisms. For instance, in MCF-7 human BRCA cells, renal chondrosarcoma, and pituitary lactotrope GH4 cells, FGF2 induces G1-phase cell cycle arrest.[ ] In Ewing sarcoma, FGF2 can trigger p53-independent cell death,[ , ] and in chondrocytes, it promotes apoptosis.[ ] Animal studies have further demonstrated that exogenous recombinant FGF2 can irreversibly inhibit the proliferation of Ras-dependent malignant mouse cells,[ ] where FGF2 exhibits both antiproliferative and antitumor growth effects. Conversely, FGF2 is closely associated with the initiation, progression, and metastasis of many cancers. By binding to receptors such as FGFR1, FGFR2, and FGFR3, FGF2 activates intracellular autophosphorylation of tyrosine residues, a process that enhances tumor cell proliferation and invasion across various cancer types.[ ]
This study also has certain limitations. Our clinical information primarily comes from datasets in the TCGA and GEO databases. Unfortunately, owing to current resource limitations and funding constraints, we were unable to employ cell models (such as tumor cell lines) to perform functional experiments (e.g., proliferation, migration, and apoptosis assays) to investigate the biological functions of overlapping DEGs. To address this limitation, we have consulted extensive literature. The experimental results conducted by Qin et al showed that AURKA promotes cell cycle progression by activating proliferation-related signaling pathways, including Cyclin B1, Wnt, and Myc.[ – ] More importantly, Carol et al found that downregulation of AURKA suppresses cell proliferation and may lead to tetraploidy, apoptosis, and senescence in vitro.[ – ] Xuan et al conducted that DTL promotes the proliferation, invasion, and migration of melanoma cells, leading to poor prognosis.[ ] Similarly, studies have revealed that dysregulation of RRM2 disrupts mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) synthesis; this results in oxidative damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and tissue degeneration, all of which negatively impact cellular functions.[ – ] Additionally, extensive research on NUSAP1 has further clarified its role in cancer progression. Ge et al found that downregulation of NUSAP1 significantly inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells by suppressing the mTORC1 signaling pathway.[ ] Moreover, Zhang et al demonstrated that silencing NUSAP1 induces apoptosis and inhibits the cell cycle in ovarian cancer cells.[ ] Furthermore, other studies have confirmed that NUSAP1 is upregulated in various cancer tissues, contributing to cancer development and progression by promoting epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, regulating the transition of the mitotic cell cycle, and modulating multiple immune-related pathways.[ ] These findings are consistent with the bioinformatics conclusions of our study, offering indirect support for our results. To deepen the understanding of tumor molecular mechanisms, we have incorporated experimental validation into our subsequent research, aiming to provide more direct empirical evidence.

## 5. Conclusion
Our present study identifies 4 potential biomarkers for BRCA including AURKA, DTL, NUSAP1, and RRM2. All these hub genes have the capability to be diagnostic and prognostic indicators in BRCA, and new treatment targets as well. Of course, their exact function still needs further clinical trials to verify.
