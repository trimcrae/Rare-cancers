## Highlights
•Chlorella pyrenoidosa monoculture water (CMW) induced higher antioxidant activity in juvenile hybrid groupers.
•CMW enhanced innate immune responses in juvenile hybrid groupers.
•Short-term rearing in CMW mitigated organ damage and increased survival of juvenile hybrid groupers during vibrio harveyi infection.
•CMW regulated the expression innate immune genes of juvenile hybrid groupers during short-term rearing.
Keywords: Microalgae; Grouper, aquaculture; Vibriosis; Green water

## Abstract
Microalgae Chlorella is widely used as immune-enhancer in aquaculture. To investigate the immunoregulatory mechanisms induced by Chlorella pyrenoidosa, the short-term (15 days) and long-term (60 days) immunostimulatory effects of C. pyrenoidosa on hybrid groupers (E. lanceolatus × E. fuscoguttatus) were examined, revealing that juveniles reared in Chlorella monoculture rearing water (CMW) display better survival upon pathogenic Vibrio harveyii challenge than those otherwise. The findings showed reduced free radical activity and higher antioxidant capacity in fish reared short-term and long-term in CMW. The innate immunity was robustly enhanced in short-term CMW rearing, with more mucus cells covering the gill secondary lamellae and the formation of more melano-macrophage centers (MMCs) in the spleen. CMW mitigated organ damage in V. harveyi challenged fish in both short- and long-term trials, but a higher survival was only observed in fish reared short-term in CMW. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that short-term rearing in CMW regulated the expression of innate immune (IRF4, IL-11, IL12RB2, CK3), anti-inflammatory (SELE, PTGIS, HMOX), apoptosis-inhibitory (NR4A1, NR4A3, CTRP3, BCL3), and proteolytic (UBB, HSP70, HSP90, KDM6B, SCARB2) genes. Elevated oxidative phosphorylation-related gene expressions were stimulated, possibly due to the high metabolic commitment of immune system regulation. The findings extend the understanding and provide valuable insights on the immuno-enhancing properties of CMW. The application of CMW would potentially improve marine larviculture performance.

## Introduction
Grouper is an important marine fish species in Asia. They are cultured mainly in China, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia, with an estimate global production of 226,200 tonnes in 2020 (FAO). The rise in consumer demand has led to the intensification of hybrid grouper culture, due to its better growth performance as compared to its parent species [ ]. With the intensification comes various issues and problems which hamper the production and development of this aquaculture species in the last decades. Inadequate hygiene and frequent overcrowding have facilitated disease outbreaks in commercial hatcheries and production facilities, resulting in mass mortalities and financial losses [ , ].
Attributed to the benefits it provides to aquatic organisms, green water has long been used in aquaculture [ ]. Green water is primarily composed of microalgae (phytoplankton), which serve to improve water quality in rearing water while sheltering cultured organisms from direct sunlight exposure, hence reducing stress [ , , , ]. Microalgae's beneficial effects have been found to promote fish survival during culture, with cobia (Rachycentron canadum) as an example. In the latter, the addition of live Isochrysis galbana and Nannochlopsis oculata to the rearing water boosted larval survival to 23.3 %, from an initial 8.2 % without the presence of microalgae [ ]. Other marine and freshwater fish and shellfish species showed a similar trend of survival improvement, albeit the magnitude differed depending on the type of microalgae used during culture [ ].
C. pyrenoidosa is perhaps the most widely used microalgae species in fish production. C. pyrenoidosa immunomodulatory effects have been demonstrated in many economically important species, such as carp [ , ], Nile tilapia [ , ] and rainbow trout [ ], with results indicating that it has the ability to improve the survival and immune response of many fish species to diseases [ ]. As example, feeding enriched Daphnia magna with C. pyrenoidosa increased the larval loach survival from 21.23 % to 73.19 % [ ] and in another study, the same application of C. pyrenoidosa improved growth performance of common carp [ ]. Although the beneficial effects of C. pyrenoidosa in promoting disease tolerance have been revealed, the mechanisms involved and stimulatory role of microalgae on activating fish immune response remain vague.
Our previous study demonstrated that 15-days and 60-days rearing in live C. pyrenoidosa-containing culture water had enhanced overall gastrointestinal health in hybrid groupers [ ]. The present study is a continuum to explore the immunoregulatory mechanisms of C. pyrenoidosa in hybrid grouper culture. The short-term and long-term effects of co-cultivation with C. pyrenoidosa in hybrid groupers, including histological changes, susceptibility towards common aquaculture pathogen (Vibrio harveyi), antioxidant capacity, as well as differential gene expressions were unraveled. The findings of this study potentially contribute to an improved understanding of fish immunity and the efficacy of using microalgae in improving marine larvae performance in the closed system, as well as to combat Vibriosis and/or other widespread bacterial diseases in aquaculture.

## Materials and methods

## Experimental fish and C. pyrenoidosa
Hybrid groupers (E. lanceolatus♂×E. fuscoguttatus♀) obtained from Tianjin Lida Sea Water Resources Development Co., Ltd. China were acclimated in filtered seawater. Fish were fed with 3 % of the total body weight, twice a day for 2 weeks with commercial pellets (EP feed YouShi #2, Santong Bio-engineering (Weifang) Co., Ltd.). Live C. pyrenoidosa cells cultured in liquid medium were supplied by Qingdao Langyatai Group Co., Ltd. China biweekly and kept at 4 °C until use.

## Experimental design
After acclimatization, grouper juveniles with an average body weight of 10 g and average body length of 9 cm, were placed into 6 separate fiberglass aquariums (80 cm diameter × 80 cm height) filled with 200 L filtered seawater (three tanks for each group), each tank consisting of 150 individuals. The fish in the control group (T1) and treatment group (T2) were fed basal diet twice daily for a period of 60 days, whereas T2 group was reared in filtered seawater with the presence of 107 cells mL-1C. pyrenoidosa. The microalgae concentration was measured by haemocytometer and adjusted to 107 cells mL-1 in the T2 grouper rearing tank daily. The short and long-term effects of rearing water containing C. pyrenoidosa was assessed for 15 (15d) and 60 days (60d), respectively. Fifty percent of the rearing water was replaced twice daily and aeration was provided to maintain oxygen level. The culture conditions were maintained at 25±1 °C, dissolved oxygen (DO) > 5 mg l-1, pH 7.0 ± 0.1 and salinity 26±2 psu.
At the end of 15d and 60d trials, the weight gain and specific growth rates were calculated according to the formula below:
Weightgainrate(WGR)(%)=100×finalbodyweight−initialbodyweightinitialbodyweightSpecificgrowthrate(%/d)=100×Lnfinalbodyweight−LninitialbodyweightnumberofdaysFish from all experimental groups were randomly selected for transcriptomic analysis, antioxidant capacity measurement and histological observation. The tolerance towards V. harveyi was also examined in all experimental groups as followed: T1 and T2 in 15 days trial (T1–15d and T2–15d), and in 60 days trial (T1–60d and T2–60d).

## Specimen collection
Grouper specimens from each group were collected in triplicate and anesthetized with MS-222 (250 mg l-1) prior to blood collection. Fish were rinsed once with sterile dH2O and 75 % ethanol, followed by three rinse with sterile dH2O. Blood was collected through the caudal vessels with a syringe filled with anticoagulant agent (citrate sodium). The blood was stored in liquid nitrogen and further kept at −80 °C for transcriptomic analysis. The blood serum was used for antioxidant capacity and non-specific immune response assays. Fish were then sacrificed by pithing and the spleen were removed by dissection. The weight of the spleen was determined and the spleensomatic indices (SSI) were calculated according to the following formula:
SSI(%)=100×Spleenweightbodyweight

## Antioxidant capacity and non-specific immune response assays
The blood serum activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), malondialdehyde (MDA), alkaline phosphatase (AKP), acid phosphatase (ACP) and alanine transaminase (ALT) were determined according to the protocols provided by commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Biological Engineering, China).

## Transcriptomic analysis

## RNA extraction and sequencing
Blood total RNA was extracted using TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. The RNA quality was assessed by Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA) and RNase free agarose gel electrophoresis. The eukaryotic mRNA was enriched by Oligo(dT) beads and fragmented into short fragments using fragmentation buffer. The short RNA fragments were then reverse transcribed to synthesize cDNA libraries with random primers. The second-strand of cDNA was synthesized and purified with QiaQuick PCR extraction kit (Qiagen, The Netherlands). The cDNA was then ligated to Illumina sequencing adapters and the ligation products were sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq 6000 system (Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co., China).

## RNA-seq data pre-processing, de novo assembly and unigenes annotation
The raw reads generated from paired-end sequencing of 12 transcriptome libraries were trimmed and quality-checked with fastp [ ] to remove adaptor sequences, ambiguous and low-quality bases. All reads were then used for de novo assembly by Trinity software [ ]. The assembled unigenes were subjected to BUSCO v5 completeness assessment [ ] against “actinopterygii” lineage. Unigenes were annotated against five public databases as followed: non-redundant protein (Nr) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), the Swiss-Prot protein database (http://www.expasy.ch/sprot), the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (http://www.genome.jp/kegg), COG/KOG database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/COG) and Gene Ontology (GO) Resource (http://geneontology.org/). The highest similarity hit was annotated for each unigene.

## Gene expression and enrichment analysis
The cleaned reads were aligned to unigenes via Bowtie2 [ ] and quantified by RSEM [ ]. The read count of all samples was then normalized and differential expression analysis were performed by DESeq2 [ ] in four pair-wise comparisons between control and treatment groups: T2–15d versus T1–15d (T2–15dvsT1–15d), and T2–60d versus T1–60d (T2–60dvsT1–60d). The thresholds of differential expressed genes (DEG) were set at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and log2FoldChange ≥ 2 or ≤ −2. The DEGs were then subjected to enrichment analysis by clusterProfiler v4.6.2 [ ]. The protein-protein interactions within DEGs were mapped by using STRING database [ ].

## DEG validation by quantitative PCR (qPCR)
The expression of nine DEGs (HSP70, BAX, CDC42, CASP3, TLR7. mapk14a, ccnd1, MMP9, CCR7 and β-actin) were quantified by qPCR in triplicate. The reaction mixture was prepared in TB Green® Fast qPCR Mix (Takara, Japan) following manufacturer's protocol and quantified by Step One Plus qPCR system (Thermo Fisher, USA). The primers’ sequences are as in Supplementary file 1. Relative expression of the DEGs were calculated by using 2−ΔΔCT method [ ], with β-actin gene as reference gene.

## Challenge test
V. harveyi provided by Tianjin Fishery Research Institute, inoculated into TSA media and agitated at 120 rpm under 28 °C for 48 h prior to challenge test. Upon the short-term and long-term trials, the groupers were challenged with V. harveyi for 96 h. The short-term groups were injected intraperitoneally with 0.2 mL of 4.36×108 CFU/mL V. harveyi, and long-term groups were injected with 0.4 mL 4.27×108 CFU/mL of V. harveyi [ ].
The survival prercentage (%) of each experimental group was calculated using the following equation.
Survival(%)=100×No.offishatendNo.offishatstart

## Histopathogical evaluation
Prior to the dissection, the groupers were rinsed with sterile dH2O and 75 % ethanol, followed by three repeated rinse by sterile dH2O. Sections of the gill, spleen, anterior kidney and liver of non-challenged and challenged fish (n = 3) were collected immediately after 96 h of challenge and fixed in 10 % formalin prior to embedding with paraffin [ ]. Tissues were sectioned to a thickness of 3 μm and subsequently stained with haematoxylin and eosin. The specimen was observed for histological changes (if any) with a scanning microscope (Motic BA600 Mot).

## Statistical analysis
The differences among the control and treatment groups were evaluated by One-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) using SPSS 16.0 software (SPSS Inc.) and Duncan's multiple range test was employed for comparison between the groups. The data were presented as mean ± standard error (SE) in three replicates and the p-value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

The SGR for T1–15d and T2–15d were 3.4 ± 0.6 % and 3.1 ± 0.7 %, whereas SGR for both T1–60d and T2–60d was 2.4 ± 0.3 % ( ). T2–15d had higher SSI value than T1–15d, but the index value decreased in T2–60d and lower than T1–60d ( ).
 The antioxidant capacity was assessed in all experimental groups ( ). The differences of SOD levels between T1 and T2 groups were insignificant (p > 0.05) in both short-term and long-term trials. The CAT and MDA levels in T2–15d was lower than T1–15d, but higher level of CAT and comparable level of MDA was detected in T2–60d when compared to T1–60d The levels of ACP, AKP and ALT were significantly higher (p < 0.05) in T1–15d than T2–15d, but lower in T2–60d than T1–60d

## Discussion
In the free-living aquatic environment, fish depends on the innate immune system for survival, due to the delayed responses of the adaptive immune system [ , ]. This first line immune system which primarily consists of three main constituents namely the mucosal barrier, humoral, and cellular components, prompts quick defense responses for pathogen attenuation and inactivation during infection [ , ]. Specifically, the mucosal barrier refers to mucosal-associated lymphoid tissues (MALT) [ ], such as those present in skin and gill epithelium, containing various immune cells that prevent the entry of pathogens; the humoral factor involves production of inhibitory substances such as cytokines, lectins, proteolytic machinery, and complement components to eliminate pathogens [ , , ]; and the cellular components, which refers to the activation of immune cells upon the binding of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) with host cell receptors [ ].
CMW was shown to enhance the innate immunity of hybrid groupers in short-term rearing through several putative modes of action, hence the higher survival of T2–15d-challenged fish against common grouper opportunistic pathogen, V. harveyi ( c). The mucosal cell proliferation in the gill epithelial layer of secondary lamellae ( b) provided a mucosal defense barrier and served as one of the innate immunity components [ ]. The formation of numerically more melano-macrophage centers (MMCs) in the spleen ( f) and higher ACP activity in T2–15d implied that T2–15d responded through macrophage activation and immune cells recruitment [ , ]. In teleost fish, MMCs are the important structure of the splenic immune response, usually pigmented and packed with macrophages and lymphocytes, which are speculated to play a similar protective role as lymph nodes in higher mammals [ ].
Structural changes found in T2–15d gills and spleen were presumably mediated by the highly expressed interferon regulatory factor 4 (IRF4) and interleukin 11 type b (IL-11) genes in T2–15d The irf4 protein was known to promote mucosal cell proliferation and modulate anti-inflammatory (activated) macrophages in higher vertebrates (Hu et al. 2022). IL-11 ought to be multifunctional; it acts as an anti-inflammatory cytokine to modulate macrophages but it may also associate with the inflammation event in other vertebrate models [ , ].
Histological investigation on challenged fish also demonstrated that prior short-term rearing in CMW greatly mitigated the organ damages caused by V. harveyi infection ( , ). Consequently, mature granulomas formed in the gill, spleen, and anterior kidney of the T2–15d challenged fish were visibly more than T1–15d challenged fish, indicative of the healing or defensive progression. Granulomas formed when cells responded through innate immunity, recruited mononuclear cells, and activated them into macrophages, which formed the unique epithelioid shape at the injury site [ ]. The emergence of granulomatous inflammations was previously linked to the active participation of several genes, including interleukin-12 (IL-12) and the chemokines [ ]. Thus, the elevated expression of interleukin-12 receptor subunit beta-2 (IL12RB2) and chemokine CK3 induced by CMW in this study, may have promoted the appearance of enlarged granulomas ( f) in challenged T2–15d spleen upon V. harveyi infection. In addition, CK3 belongs to the CC chemokine family, also exhibits antimicrobial properties [ ].
The PPI mapping ( ) revealed the interaction of four upregulated genes (UBB, HSP70, HSP90, and KDM6B) in T2–15d, inferred a more active proteolytic machinery, the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS). UPS is crucial for various cellular processes, including DNA repair, stress responses, and cell differentiation in vertebrate [ , ]. As the lysosome membrane protein 2 (SCARB2) gene was also upregulated in T2–15d, it is assumed that the proteasome and lysosome are incorporated as ‘damaged cells scavenger’, where the proteasome degrades defective proteins and lysosome gets rid of cytoplasmic content, a common proteolytic mechanism reported in human [ ]. The role of kdm6b in innate immune signaling and hematopoiesis, as well as cell growth and survival, were previously reported in mice [ , ]. These genes may boost up damaged cell repair or removal in challenged T2–15d and result in higher survival rate ( c).
In addition, CMW also induced the overproduction of several anti-inflammatory proteins, such as E-selectin (SELE), prostacyclin synthase (PTGIS), and multiple heme oxygenase (HMOX). Previous study showed that the overexpression of anti-inflammatory genes, such as HMOX, suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines in grass carp [ ]. Apoptosis inhibitors, such as nuclear receptor subfamily 4 (nr4a1 and nr4a3), complement C1q/tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-related protein-3 (CTRP3) and B-cell lymphoma 3 protein (BCL3) genes, were also highly expressed in T2–15d Studies have shown that CTRP3 may also exhibit an anti-inflammatory effect in human model [ , ]. In fish, apoptosis plays a role in immunity and cytotoxicity, as well as maintaining homeostasis and tissue growth, which assist the aquatic organisms in surviving a highly-fluctuating environment [ ].
In overview, the effects of CMW reported in this study were similar to the effects of dietary Chlorella on other marine fish [ , ]. However, the persistency of the immunomodulatory effect of Chlorella varied among different fish species. For instance, the improvement of antioxidant capacity, anti-inflammatory and apoptosis alleviation induced by 60-days Chlorella supplement on juvenile largemouth bass [ ]. In Nile tilapia, the protective effect of Chlorella was retained for 21 days [ ].
Immunostimulation is an energy-consuming process in aquatic animals [ ], and prolonged use of immune-enhancer could lead to immune fatigue [ , ]. In this study, the immunomodulatory effect of CMW on juvenile hybrid groupers weakened at 60 days. Even though the organ damage in T2–60d challenged-fish were not as worse as in T1–60d ( , ), the survival rate of T2–60d was not greater than that of T1–60d Similar to T2–15d, the CMW-induced MMC formation in the T2–60d spleen was enlarged but numerically reduced ( h). The long-term rearing in CMW led to more cytoplasmic inclusion in the T2–60d liver ( h), though this observation is common in presumably healthy fish [ ]. Somehow the antioxidant activity remained higher than T1–60d, with significantly higher CAT activity ( ). T2–60d also exhibited lower ACP, AKP and ALP activities, which implied greater spleen and liver functions.
Transcriptomic analysis revealed that the pro-apoptotic gene, tumor protein p53-inducible nuclear protein 1 (TP53INP1), was downregulated in T2–60d, indicating that T2–60d still unleashed certain immune responses. Although the proteolysis-related genes (lysozyme C and proteasome subunit alpha type-3) remain upregulated, the lower expression of anti-inflammatory genes, SELE and hmox and innate immune genes, B-cell receptor (CD22), lily-type lectin (CsLTL), interleukin-1 receptor type 2 (IL1R2), leukotriene B4 receptor 1 (LTB4R), and mucin-2, suggested that the innate immunity were not as active as T2–15d In teleost fish, lectin and interleukins are important for T cells stimulation and host defense mechanisms [ ]. Thus, the lower expressions of these defense genes may indicate the immune exhaustion effects of prolonged-use CMW.
The gene expression analysis also demonstrated that CMW potentially mediated energy metabolism. Mitochondrial protein genes, including multiple cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4 and 6c (COX4I1 and COX6C), and ADP ATP translocase 2 (SLC25A6) were mediated in T2–15d The oxidative phosphorylation pathway and related GO terms (respiratory electron transport chain, NADH dehydrogenase (quinone) activity, nicotinamide nucleotide metabolic process, and oxidoreductase activity) were also enriched in T2–60d ( , , b). Multiple electron transport chain-related genes were upregulated (Supplementary file 3), conjecturing highly active ATP synthesis in T2-15d and T2-60d
The pivotal role of mitochondria in the regulation of the immune system was discussed previously [ ], including the molecular signaling in macrophage polarization and function observed in this study. In other vertebrate models such as mice and human, innate immunity activation is energy-consuming with upregulated metabolic pathways [ , , ]. Increased ATP synthesis in this study was speculated to support the metabolic cost of innate immunity regulations induced by CMW, as the complex regulation of host immune and metabolic mechanisms by the co-living microorganism was also exemplified in mammals [ ]. As cell energy metabolism co-supplies cell growth and other metabolic pathways, metabolic commitment must be maintained between immunity and other essential cellular processes [ ]. Hence, CMW-induced regulations on immunity was weaken in long-term trial.

## Conclusion
The outcomes from this study demonstrated the positive effects of short-term rearing of juvenile hybrid grouper with C. pyrenoidosa monoculture water (CMW). Fish reared in CMW for 15 days had reduced free radical activity and a greater ability to resist inflammation and apoptosis. Short-term rearing in CMW alleviated the organ damage upon V. harveyi infection. The findings also speculated that the strong immunomodulatory effect of CMW is temporary, as fish reared in CMW for 60 days did not show a higher survival rate than the control group, although the antioxidant activities remained higher than the control group. Thus, long-term use of CMW is not recommended. The outcomes suggest that short-term rearing of larval or juvenile hybrid groupers in CMW is sufficient to improve general immune responses among juvenile hybrid groupers, which can be potentially applied in commercial fisheries. Future studies are recommended to expand into optimization of CMW for large-scale application.
