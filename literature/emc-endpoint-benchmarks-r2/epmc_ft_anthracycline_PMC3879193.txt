Its sensitivity to chemotherapy is reported to be low.</p></sec><sec id="sec2" disp-level="2"><title>Methods</title><p>We retrospectively reviewed a series of 11 EMC patients treated as from 2001 within the Italian Rare Cancer Network (RCN) with anthracycline-based chemotherapy. Ten patients are evaluable for response. Overall, best response according to RECIST was: partial response (PR) = 4 (40 %), stable disease (SD) = 3, progressive disease (PD) = 3 cases. Median PFS was 8 (range 2–10) months.</p></sec><sec id="sec4" disp-level="2"><title>Conclusions</title><p>By contrast to what reported so far, anthracycline-based chemotherapy is active in a distinct proportion of EMC patients.</p></sec><sec id="kwd-group1" sec-type="kwd-group" disp-level="2"><p><bold>Keywords:</bold> Sarcoma, Chondrosarcoma, Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma, Chemotherapy, Anthracycline, Doxorubicin, Ifosfamide</p></sec></abstract><custom-meta-group><custom-meta><meta-name>status</meta-name><meta-value>released</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>display-pdf</meta-name><meta-value>yes</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-olf</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-manuscript</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-preprint</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-journal-matter</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-scanned</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta><custom-meta><meta-name>is-retracted</meta-name><meta-value>no</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta><notes notes-type="article-notes"><sec id="historyarticle-meta1" sec-type="history" disp-level="2"><p>Received 2013 Nov 13; Accepted 2013 Dec 13; Collection date 2013.</p></sec></notes></front><body><sec id="sec5" disp-level="1"><title>Background</title><p>Extraskeletal myxoid chondrosarcoma (EMC) is a very rare sarcoma of uncertain differentiation
[<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>] that, despite its name, does not exhibit any cartilaginous differentiation. EMC usually originates from the deep soft tissue, the thigh being the most common site
[<xref rid="B2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. Demicco and Coll. recently reported on 5 cases of molecularly confirmed EMC arising primarily in the bone
[<xref rid="B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. On this basis, they proposed to relabel this tumor as myxochondroid sarcoma, either osseous or extraskeletal.</p><p>Microscopically, EMC can be subdivided into a conventional well-differentiated and a cellular high-grade EMC, the latter being marked by the presence of a predominantly epithelioid morphology, high mitotic rate and necrosis
[<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Cases of dedifferentiated ECM were also described
[<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].</p><p>EMC is marked by a specific chromosomal rearrangements, involving the <italic>NR4A3</italic> gene locus on chromosome 9
[<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. More often <italic>NR4A3</italic> (also called <italic>CHN</italic> or <italic>TEC</italic>) is fused to <italic>EWSR</italic> on chromosome 22
[<xref rid="B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], although four chimeric variants were described to date
[<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B8" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. <italic>NR4A3</italic> translocation is relevant in case of differential diagnosis with other myxoid-mesenchymal neoplasms
[<xref rid="B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>The natural history of the EMC is marked by a relatively indolent behavior with a 10-year survival rate ranging between 65% and 85%, and 40% risk of metastases at 10 years
[<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], lung being the most frequent site of secondary lesions.</p><p>Available literature reports EMC as a disease poorly sensitive to cytotoxic chemotherapy
[<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>-<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].</p><p>We herein report on a retrospective series of 11 patients with locally advanced/metastatic EMC, molecularly confirmed by the presence of <italic>NR4A3</italic> rearrangement<italic>,</italic> treated with anthracycline-based chemotherapy at our institution and within the Italian Rare Cancer Network.</p></sec><sec id="sec6" disp-level="1"><title>Methods</title><sec id="sec7" disp-level="2"><title>Patients selection</title><p>We retrospectively identified 11 patients with progressive, metastatic, molecularly confirmed EMC treated with anthracycline-based chemotherapy at Fondazione IRCCS Istituto Nazionale Tumori, Milano and those included in the data-base of the Italian Rare Cancer Network, registered by other Italian institutions, from January 2001 to June 2013. The analysis was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee.</p><p>Pathological diagnosis was centrally reviewed in all cases by 2 expert pathologists (SP and APDT) and confirmed by the evidence of <italic>NR4A3</italic> rearrangement. All patients had evidence of Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) ≤3 and an adequate bone marrow and organ function. All patients provided a written informed consent to data collection within the network and to the treatment. Data were extracted from individual patient file and analyzed.</p></sec><sec id="sec8" disp-level="2"><title>Pathology and molecular analysis</title><p>The diagnosis was rendered according to the last WHO classification

[<xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Cases with a morphology consistent with EMC but without the evidence of <italic>NR4A2</italic> rearrangement were excluded from this series.</p></sec><sec id="sec9" disp-level="2"><title>Treatment</title><p>Patients were treated with anthracyclines as single-agent (doxorubicin 60–75 mg/smq, i.v., bolus), or in combination with ifosfamide (epirubicin 105 mg/smq + ifosfamide 9000 mg/smq, i.v., in 3 days). Mesna was added to ifosfamide. Chemotherapy was administered every 3 weeks, together with steroids and antiemetics. Prophylactic granulocyte colony stimulating factors were administered.</p><p>Treatment was withheld for haematologic grade ≥3 adverse events (AE) and for non haematologic grade ≥2 AE (as defined by the National Cancer Institute Common Toxicity Criteria, version 3.0) and restarted after recovery to grade &lt;2 in case of haematologic or grade &lt;1 in case of non-haematologic.</p></sec><sec id="sec10" disp-level="2"><title>Clinical assessment</title><p>Full blood cell count and biochemistry were assessed at baseline and before every administration. AE were recorded. Disease status was assessed at baseline by a whole body computed tomography scan (CT), a CT or magnetic resonance (MRI) of the site(s) of disease, and a whole body bone scan. CT/MRI were repeated after the first 2 or 3 cycles of treatment then every 2–3 months.</p><p>Response to treatment was evaluated by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumor (RECIST)
[<xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec11" disp-level="2"><title>Statistical analysis</title><p>Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were estimated with Kaplan-Meyer method
[<xref rid="B18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Failure for PFS was progressive disease according to RECIST, or death. OS, failure was death due to any cause. For PFS and OS, patients who interrupted their treatment without evidence of disease progression and underwent complete surgery were censored at the time of surgery. Alive patients were censored at the time of the last contact.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec12" disp-level="1"><title>Results</title><p>Eleven patients with measurable disease were treated with an anthracycline-based chemotherapy. Five patients with a previous diagnosis of EMC were not included in this series since diagnosis was not confirmed by the presence of <italic>NR4A3</italic> rearrangement. Ten patients were evaluable for response (in one case treatment was interrupted early due to toxicity). We observed 4 RECIST PR out of 10 patients evaluable for response, with a median PFS of 8 months and 50% patients progression-free at 6 months. As EMC is an extremely rare mesenchymal malignancy, rare cancer networks represent a valuable tool in order to collect case series, in addition to sharing and developing clinical expertise.</p><p>In fact, EMC represents a small subgroup among sarcomas and no prospective study focusing on their medical treatment is available as of today. Despite rarity, in order to collect an homogeneous series, we decided to include only cases in which diagnosis had been confirmed by the presence of <italic>NR4A3</italic> rearrangement.</p><p>In our series the response rate to anthracycline-based chemotherapy looks greater than previously reported. The only responses to chemotherapy were described in 2001 by McGrory in 2 of 6 metastatic EMC patients responsive to a multi-agent chemotherapy
[<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>], and more recently by Han who observed a complete remission in one patient treated with anthracyclines plus ifosfamide
[<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. No objective responses were observed in the two largest retrospective series published so far, which collected cases selected over a period of 30 years starting from the 70′s
[<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. In fact, no patient had a response amongst 10 treated with doxorubicin and dacarbazine-based regimens in the MD Anderson’s retrospective study
[<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] published in 1995, as well as none of the 21 patients treated with different regimens, mostly anthracycline-based, reported by Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and Royal Marsden Hospital in 2008
[<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. In the latter analysis, the best response was represented by stable disease lasting ≥ 6 months in only 25% of cases, with an estimated 40% median-PFS at 6 months. More recently, Ogura and Coll. reviewed their institutional series of 22 patients, with no response in 4 cases treated with ifosfamide-based chemotherapy
[<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. An explanation for this discrepancy may well be that diagnostic criteria for EMC have improved in the last years and possibly older series may have included other histological types with overlapping morphologies, such as myoepithelial carcinomas. In particular, the analysis to detect <italic>NR3A4</italic> translocation, which is specific of EMC, was described for the first time in 1985
[<xref rid="B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>] and was not routinely used to confirm the diagnosis until recently. It is now evident that EMC are morphologically and molecularly distinct from conventional bone chondrosarcoma, whose lack of sensitivity to chemotherapy is well known
[<xref rid="B20" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Indeed, EMC and conventional bone chondrosarcomas are completely unrelated.</p><p>As already mentioned, the differential diagnosis is rather broad and includes malignant myoepithelioma/myoepithelial carcinoma, whose natural history and chemosensitivity is still not well understood
[<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>], but also low grade fibromyxoid sarcoma, myxoid liposarcoma and synovial sarcoma with myxoid changes
[<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>-<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Immunohistochemical analysis plays an important role: however, there is some degree of overlapping that may represent an additional challenge
[<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Although not frequently observed, synaptophysin expression
[<xref rid="B25" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>] has been recently confirmed by gene expression profiling analysis
[<xref rid="B23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Finally, PPR-gamma, firstly described to be involved in ECM signaling pathway
[<xref rid="B23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], is also expressed in many other cancers including myxoid liposarcomas
[<xref rid="B26" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>-<xref rid="B28" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>], and therefore it can hardly be diagnostically helpful. From these findings it appears that cytogenetic-molecular analysis substantially helps in distinguishing EMC from other tumor entities. For these reason, due to the lack of <italic>NR4A3</italic> rearrangement, we excluded 5 of 16 cases initially diagnosed as EMCS according to morphology/immunohistochemistry.</p><p>EMCS is an indolent disease and in some cases it is characterized by a slow progression also in the metastatic phase, affecting about 40% of cases. However, in case of advanced and progressive disease, a medical treatment is needed. Our series suggests that anthracycline-based chemotherapy can have a role in this setting. Of note, 3 patients of our series were completely resected after having a response to chemotherapy. None of them was cured, but they all recurred after &gt;12 months from surgery, suggesting that chemotherapy may have played a role.</p><p>In case of resistance to conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy, further medical treatment are needed in EMC. We recently reported on the activity of sunitinib in two patients carrying a metastatic EMC pretreated with chemotherapy
[<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. These preliminary results are under confirmation in a larger series, while a prospective study on pazopanib, another antiangiogenic agent, is planned.</p></sec><sec id="sec17" disp-level="1"><title>Conclusions</title><p>By contrast to what reported so far, anthracycline-based chemotherapy is active in a distinct proportion of EMC patients. Series like ours may serve as external controls for future clinical studies on new agents in such a rare histology.</p></sec><sec id="sec18" disp-level="1"><title>Competing interests</title><p>None of the authors declared any financial or non-financial competing interests.</p></sec><sec id="sec19" disp-level="1"><title>Authors’ contributions</title><p>SS contributed to study design and coordination, data collection, analysis and interpretation, and drafted the manuscript.
