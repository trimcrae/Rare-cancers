## Abstract
The cGAS-STING signaling pathway serves as a critical link between DNA sensing and innate immunity, and has tremendous potential to improve anti-tumor immunity by generating type I interferons. However, STING agonists have shown decreasing biotherapeutic efficacy in clinical trials. Tumor metabolism, characterized by aberrant nutrient utilization and energy production, is a fundamental hallmark of tumorigenesis. And modulating metabolic pathways in tumor cells has been discovered as a therapeutic strategy for tumors. As research concerning STING progressed, emerging evidence highlights its role in metabolic reprogramming, independent its immune function, indicating metabolic targets as a strategy for STING activation in cancers. In this review, we delve into the interplay between STING and multiple metabolic pathways. We also synthesize current knowledge on the antitumor functions of STING, and the metabolic targets within the tumor microenvironment (TME) that could be exploited for STING activation. This review highlights the necessity for future research to dissect the complex metabolic interactions with STING in various cancer types, emphasizing the potential for personalized therapeutic strategies based on metabolic profiling.

## Introduction
The cGAS-STING signaling pathway is an important link between DNA sensing and innate immunity, involved in multiple cellular processes, including inflammation, apoptosis, and autophagy [ , ]. Many studies has reported the cell-intrinsic tumour suppressive activity of STING [ – ]. However, STING is epigenetically silenced in various cancer types [ – ]. Intratumoral injection of STING agonists has been proved to be beneficial for antitumor immunity and tumor immunotherapies [ – ]. However, the hydrophilicity and instability of cyclic dinucleotides (CDNs) restrict its use to intratumoral administration [ , ]. Some recently investigated small-molecule STING agonists with good stability are also confronted with challenges of poor bioavailability and off-target inflammatory responses [ , ].
Cancers, which maintain cell viability and a high proliferation rate in tumour cells through facilitating the uptake and utilization of essential nutrients, exhibit metabolic reprogramming as a hallmark [ – ]. For example, Warburg effect is a hallmark of cancer fueling the rapid metabolic demands with aerobic glycolysis to promote malignant cell proliferation [ – ]. Higher lipid biosynthesis and fatty acid oxidation activity are associated with the progression of multiple cancers [ – ]. In addition, the remodeling of amino acid metabolism, such as methionine and arginine metabolism, has been discovered in many cancers [ – ]. Targeting metabolism in the tumor cells has emerged as a promising antitumor therapy [ – ].
Metabolism and innate immunity are highly intertwined processes in the host to fulfill cellular functions [ , ]. Recent years, more and more research has confirmed STING as a critical molecule in metabolic reprogramming, such as glycolysis, cholesterol biosynthesis, fatty acid catabolism [ , – ]. The downstream effectors of STING, TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3), also have been found to regulate central metabolic pathways [ – ]. Therefore, it is necessary to explore the interaction between STING and metabolism in the TME, and how these insights can be applied to the development of more effective anticancer strategies. In this review, we outline the interaction of STING with multiple metabolic pathways and the antitumor function of STING. We also provide potential metabolic targets for future cancer treatment through activation of STING.

## An overview of the cGAS-STING signaling pathway
Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) is a cytoplasmic DNA sensor discovered in 2013, which can detect aberrantly cytoplasmic DNA within the cell [ – ]. (Fig.  ) Upon binding to DNA, cGAS undergoes a conformational change to become active and synthesizes the second messenger cyclic GMP–AMP (cGAMP) from ATP and GTP [ , ]. The second messenger cGAMP rapidly binds to stimulator of IFN genes (STING), a transmembrane protein that resides on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) [ – ]. cGAMP binding to STING leads to profound conformational changes that trigger STING oligomerization, liberation from anchoring factors, interaction with trafficking factors, transport to Golgi and polymerization [ , ]. STING then recruits the TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and transcription factor interferon (IFN) regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) to form a functional signalosome [ – ]. IRF3 undergoes TBK1-mediated phosphorylation, dimerization, and nuclear translocation to induce gene expression of type I interferons [ , ]. In addition, STING also recruits IKK and NIH to activate the nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) transcription factor, driving the transcription of genes encoding proinflammatory cytokines [ , ]. Finally, STING is transported to lysosoma to end signaling, which is associated with endosomal sorting complexes required for transport (ESCRT)-driven microautophagy and adaptor protein complex 1 (AP- 1)-mediated transport vesicles formation [ – ].
 Apart from cytosolic DNA sensing by cGAS, STING activation can also be induced by cGAMP produced from other cells, through gap junctions or extracellular matrix cGAMP transporters [ – ]. Previous research reported a cGAS- and cGAMP-independent mode of STING activation, which requires the absence of the lysosomal membrane protein Niemann-Pick type C1 (NPC1) and the guiding role of Sterol Regulatory Element-Binding Protein 2 (SREBP2) [ ].
As the research has advanced, more and more noncanonical functions of STING have been discovered. For example, STING is involved in IFN-independent cellular process including autophagy, unfolded protein response (UPR), apoptosis, and cellular senescence [ – ]. IRF3 has also been reported to have noncanonical functions, such as cellular condensation and apoptosis [ , ]. Numerous studies suggest that the role of STING in humans is far more profound than the IFN-I production.

## Lipid metabolism

## Lipid metabolism regulates STING

## STING activation
The harmful effects of prolonged exposure to excess lipids are referred to as “lipotoxicity”, the downstream effects of which include endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Lipotoxic molecules induced STING activation mediated by mitochondrial damage and mtDNA release have been observed in the progress of many diseases [ – ]. (Fig.  ) Free fatty acids, particularly palmitic acid, directly induce metabolic stress that activates STING pathway [ , ].
 Carnitine acetyltransferase (CRAT) plays a critical role in regulating mitochondrial metabolic homeostasis, catalyzing the conversion of acetyl-CoA to acetylcarnitine, which enables its export into cytosol [ ]. Carnitine acetyltransferase (CRAT) deficiency in cardiomyocytes augments cholesterol catabolism via the bile acid synthesis pathway, culminating in the intracellular accumulation of bile acid intermediates, such as 7α-hydroxyl- 3-oxo- 4-cholestenoic acid (7-HOCA) [ ]. This accumulation induces mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) stress, thereby activating the cGAS–STING pathway [ ]. Also as a vital enzyme in de novo biosynthesis of fatty acids and cholesterol, adenosine 5′-triphosphate citrate lyase (ACLY), the inhibiton of which was reported to induce cGAS-STING pathway [ , ]. In mechanism, the suppression of ATP citrate lyase (ACLY) modulates cellular lipid metabolism, which culminates in the accumulation of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and their subsequent peroxidation [ , ]. This metabolic perturbation induces mitochondrial dysfunction, precipitating the release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytosol, thereby inducing the cGAS-STING pathway [ ]. In addition, pharmacological and genetic inhibition of ACLY was proved to overcome cancer resistance to anti–PD-L1 therapy in a cGAS-STING-dependent manner, and dietary PUFA supplementation also contributed to similar effects [ ].
The modulation of the STING signaling pathway by lipid metabolism orchestrated through Liver X receptors (LXRs) is an important link between lipid metabolism and STING signaling [ , ]. Upon activation mediated by endogenous lipid metabolites, including cholesterol and 27-hydroxycholesterol, LXRs upregulate the expression of sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase acid-like 3 A (SMPDL3 A), an enzyme that selectively degrades the cyclic dinucleotide cGAMP, attenuating STING activation [ , , ].
In macrophages, short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) acts as signaling molecules by engaging the G-protein-coupled receptor GPR43 [ , ]. Subsequent activation of GPR43 induces a rise in intracellular calcium, which in turn promotes the translocation of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) to the cytosol, initiating cGAS-STING signaling [ ].

## Functions of STING on ER and Golgi
Lipid-protein interactions in cells are involved in various biological processes, including metabolism, trafficking, signaling, and transmembrane transport. STING is a dimeric transmembrane protein that resides on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) [ , ]. The retention of STING in the ER is governed by cholesterol-binding motifs within its sequence, which serve as critical structural determinants for its subcellular localization and activation status [ ]. Upon stimulation with cGAMP, a transient reduction in endoplasmic reticulum (ER) cholesterol ensues, attributed to the esterification of cholesterol by sterol O-acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1), augmenting the propensity for STING to translocate from the ER to the Golgi apparatus, where it can assemble signaling complexes [ ]. (Fig.  ) Consistent with this, researchers discovered that Methyl-β-Cyclodextrin (MβCD), a widely used cholesterol-depleting agent, enhances anti-tumor immune responses by enhancing STING signaling and promoting immune cell activation in the tumor microenvironment [ ]. On the contrary, cholesterol in the Golgi membrane may help modulate the lipid microenvironment within the trans-Golgi network (TGN), thereby enhancing the lipid order necessary for the palmitoylation-dependent aggregation of STING [ ]. Researchers found that treatment with oxysterol-binding protein inhibitor (OSW- 1), a steroidal saponin originated from plants used to restrict cholesterol transport, suppressed the formation of clusters consisting of > 800 STING and its signaling [ ]. 25-Hydroxycholesterol (25-HC) is reported to interfere with cholesterol enrichment in TGN, thus inhibiting STING activation [ ]. Consistent with this, 25-hydroxylase (CH25H), which converts cholesterol to 25-HC, has been considered an important regulator of immune function [ ], suppressing the expression and type I interferon production [ , ].
Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are structural components of membrane phospholipids and influence cellular function via effects on membrane properties [ ]. A study found that reduced polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) biosynthesis induced STING activation through significant lipidomic remodeling in acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells [ ]. In addition, the genetic deletion of Stearyl-CoA Desaturase 2 (SCD2) leads to a reduction in monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA) levels, which in turn affects the composition of lipid droplets and potentially alters the interaction between lipid droplets and the endoplasmic reticulum. This metabolic shift is crucial for the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway [ ].

## STING translocation
The translocation of STING includes the retrograde and anterograde transport of proteins between the ER and Golgi, post-Golgi trafficking, and degradation by the lysosome [ ]. These processes are associated with many cofactors, through which some lipid molecules regulate STING translocation, further influencing its biological effects.
Phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI4P) on TGN membranes has been reported to induce membrane curvature and promote vesicle formation, budding, and trafficking through various PI4P-binding proteins [ ]. Previous research has demonstrated that enhanced PI4P levels at the Golgi promoted STING signaling [ , , ]. (Fig.  ) For example, Fang et al. revealed that, by interacting with STING at the Golgi, armadillo-like helical domain containing 3 (ARMH3) recruited phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta (PI4 KB) to enhance PI4P synthesis. Increased PI4P facilitated the translocation and activation of STING from the Golgi to the endoplasmic reticulum through PI4P binding proteins, such as adaptor protein 1(AP- 1) [ ]. Acyl-CoA binding domain containing 3 (ACBD3) is another multifunctional protein involved in a variety of cellular processes, including lipid metabolism and vesicular transport [ ]. Similarly, Luteijn et al. found that ACBD3 promotes the synthesis of PI4P by interacting with PI4 KB, further promoting the transport and activation of STING. [ ] In addition, OSBP inhibitors such as itraconazole was proved to promote STING activation and immune responses by increasing PI4P levels at the Golgi [ ]. PI4P also drives the formation of Ras-related protein 22 A (RAB22 A)-induced non-canonical autophagosomes that package and transport activated STING. Specifically, RAB22 A interacts with PI4 K2 A to promote PI4P synthesis. Increased PI4P levels facilitate the formation of non-canonical autophagosomes for STING, driving its transport and subsequent secretion. RAB22 A also inhibits RAB7 activity to prevent STING fusion within lysosomes, further promoting STING secretion. The secreted STING activates IFNβ production in recipient cells, thereby enhancing antitumor immune responses [ ]. (Fig.  ).
Myristic acid is a common long-chain saturated fatty acid (SFA), regarded as an energy source and structural element for protein modification (termed myristoylation), thereby altering their functions [ ]. Myristic acid functions as a metabolic checkpoint in the modulation of the STING signaling pathway [ ]. This regulation is mediated through the enhancement of N-myristoylation on ARF1 [ , ], a pivotal regulator governing STING's membrane trafficking, thereby facilitating the autophagic degradation of the STING complex and contributing to the maintenance of immune homeostasis [ ]. Sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP) is a critical protein in lipid metabolism, sensing cellular lipid levels and controling the transcriptional regulation of genes involved in the biosynthesis and uptake of lipids [ ]. A study found that SREBP2 promotes STING activation independent of cGAS and cGAMP. In mechanism, reduced ER cholesterol levels activates SREBP2 translocation from the ER to the Golgi, which in turn facilitates STING transport from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to the Golgi apparatus through physical connections [ ]. SREBP-Cleavage Activating Protein (SCAP) is also reported to promote STING signaling through recruiting IRF3 onto the STING signalosome [ ].

## STING modification
Post-translational modifications (PTMs) orchestrate the activation and degradation of STING through temporarily changing the structure of STING, including phosphorylation, ubiquitylation, and palmitoylation [ , – ]. At the Golgi, the palmitoylation of the cysteines 88 and 91 of STING is essential for its activation [ ]. 4-Hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), one of the products of lipid peroxidation, the accumulation of which have been proved to lead to the carbonylation of STING, particularly at cysteine 88, which antagonizes palmitoylation—a modification necessary for STING activation [ , ]. And lipid peroxidation inhibitors, such as liproxstatin- 1 (LX- 1) and vitamin E, has been proved to significantly restore STING activation and enhance the immune response after DNA virus infection [ ]. Consistent with previous research, a recent study proved that the reduction in 4-HNE levels mediated by autophagy-related protein 16-like 1 (ATG16-L1) depletion inhibits STING carbonylation, while enhancing STING palmitoylation, thereby promoting STING activation [ ]. In macrophages, fatty acid synthase (FASN) inhibition induces a metabolic shift, leading to the accumulation of malonyl-CoA [ – ]. This metabolite upregulation may inhibit the palmitoylation status of STING, specifically at the C91 residue, which is crucial for its activation [ ]. Nitro-fatty acids (NO₂-FAs)) modulate the signaling cascade of the STING by covalently targeting cysteine residues 88 and 91 within the STING protein, thereby disrupting its palmitoylation [ ].
NLR family CARD domain containing 3 (NLRC3) is a negative regulator that attenuates type I interferon (IFN-I) response by sequestering and attenuating stimulator of interferon genes (STING) activation, and DNA binding to NLRC3 has been reported to counteract this negative effect [ – ]. In the context of obesity, elevated levels of saturated fatty acids induce the expression of NLRC3, which in turn dampens the STING-mediated immune response, leading to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that facilitates cancer immune evasion [ ].

## STING regulates lipid metabolism
In the context of inflammation, STING regulates lipid metabolism through downstream inflammatory signaling IFN and NF-κB, further regulating lipid metabolism-related gene transcription [ – ]. Consistent with this, many researchers have observed lipid accumulation during STING activation [ – ]. In Ewing sarcoma, STING activation leads to reduced cholesterol biosynthesis and upregulated cholesterol efflux, which may be explained by the disturbed metabolic state of tumor cells [ ]. A study reported that STING-NF-κB downregulated cholesterol biosynthesis through repressing the expression of farnesyl-diphosphate farnesyltransferase 1 (FDFT1), independent of cytokine production [ ]. In addition to gene transcription induced by downstream signaling, STING inhibits the activity of fatty acid desaturase 2 (FADS2), thereby directly suppressing the biosynthesis of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (LC-PUFAs) [ ].

## Lipophagy
Lipophagy is a novel type of lipid metabolism, defined as the autophagic degradation of intracellular lipid droplets (LDs) [ ]. In the process of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), STING activation induced restricted lipophagy plays a critical role. p62 is one of the major components of the hepatocyte protein inclusion, promoting the selective degradation of lipid droplets [ ]. The activation of the STING-TBK1 signaling cascade induces the phosphorylation of the autophagy receptor Sequestosome 1 (p62), further causing hepatic protein inclusions and lipid accumulation in the liver [ ]. Mammalian Target of Rapamycin Complex 1 (mTORC1) was reported to be an important signaling molecule that regulates lipid homeostasis [ ]. STING interacts with the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (MTORC1) and promotes the assembly and activation of the MTORC1 complex, inhibiting the process of lipophagy, consequently leading to intracellular lipid accumulation within hepatocytes [ ]. TBK1 is essential for the interaction between STING and MTORC1 [ ], and has also been reported to contribute to the activation of mTORC1 [ , ]. However, some research discovered that TBK1 interacts with the main components of mTORC1, directly inhibiting mTORC1 [ – ]. The effect of STING and TBK1 on MTORC1 could vary depending on the cell contexts. In macrophages, the knockout of STING promotes the yes-associated protein (YAP) nuclear activity, which in turn promotes autophagic degradation of lipid droplets and reduces lipid accumulation [ ]. Activated STING in macrophages may inhibit lipid droplet degradation by suppressing the nuclear activity of YAP.

## Fatty acids oxidation
Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α (PPARα) and PPARγ are transcription factors that regulate fatty acid oxidation (FAO) [ ]. STING deficiency inhibits the proteasomal degradation of ubiquitinated PPARγ, leading to increased FAO [ ]. IRF3 also exerts a suppressive effect on PPARγ expression, thereby inhibiting the adipogenesis in pre-adipocytes [ ]. A recent study revealed a bistable circuit that encodes long-term intestinal tolerance through a positive feedback loop involving IL- 18 and STING [ ]. In mechanism, IL- 18 activates the Na-Cl cotransporter SLC12 A3 (NCC) to induce sodium influx, initiating fatty acid oxidative reprogramming. Then IFN-I production induced by STING activation promotes the expression of fatty acid oxidation genes, further facilitating reprogramming of macrophages from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation [ ].
In general, TBK1 regulates lipid metabolism through downstream cytokine production upon STING activation. A study reported a noncanonical function of TBK1 in FAO. In mechanism, unphosphorylated TBK1 promotes FAO by functioning as a scaffold protein that localizes acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 1 (ACSL1) to the mitochondrial outer membrane [ ]. ACSL1 functions to catalyze the formation of acyl-CoA from fatty acids and coenzyme A, representing the initial step for fatty acids to enter the mitochondria for β-oxidation [ , ]. Conversely, increased TBK1 kinase activity diminishes its interaction with ACSL1, redirecting the enzyme towards the ER to favor fatty acid re-esterification over oxidation [ ]. Considering TBK1 activation is one of the downstream effects of STING, whether STING is involved in this metabolic regulation is worth exploring.

## Glucose metabolism

## Glucose metabolism regulates STING
Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) is an important mediator of epithelial glucose transport and is diffusely expressed in malignant cancer cells [ , ]. NOP2/Sun domain containing 2 (NSUN2) is a direct glucose sensor in tumor cells and has been reported to inhibit the cGAS-STING pathway [ ]. In mechanism, glucose directly binds to NSUN2, promoting its oligomerization and activation, maintaining the global m5 C RNA methylation of TREX2, which in turn restricts cytosolic dsDNA accumulation and inhibits the cGAS-STING pathway [ ]. (Fig.  ) These may explain why STING is epigenetically silenced in various cancer types from a metabolic aspect [ , ]. In addition, SGLT2 inhibitors restrict the glucose uptake of tumor cells, and have been proved to activate STING through the suppression of AKT phosphorylation, thus blocking tumorigenesis [ ]. In dendritic cells, glycolysis fuels ATP production, which in turn activates STING signaling, leading to the stabilization and accumulation of HIF- 1α [ ]. HIF- 1α then induces the expression of glycolytic enzymes, such as PKM2, enhancing glycolysis and establishing a positive feedback loop that further augments STING signaling and promotes antitumor immune responses [ ].
 AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) is a principal sensor under glucose deficiency and modulates various metabolic pathways [ , ]. AMPK activation upon glucose decrease, which directly phosphorylates TBK1 at S511, enhancing the recruitment of IRF3 and the assembly of STING signalosomes, thereby potentiating pathogen sensing and innate immunity [ ].
Itaconate is made from cis-aconitate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA cycle) in macrophages [ ]. Itaconate is reported to inhibit the interaction between Keap1 and Nrf2, thereby preventing Nrf2 degradation [ ]. This stabilization and nuclear translocation of Nrf2 lead to the transcriptional repression of STING, consequently attenuating the type I interferon response and curbing inflammation [ , ]. A recent study discovered that itaconate suppresses the activity of succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), thereby disrupting the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and inducing mitochondrial dysfunction. This disruption leads to the release of mtDNA into the cytosol, further activating STING [ ]. 4-OI, which is a permeable itaconate derivative, can directly alkylate cysteine sites 147 of STING, thereby inhibiting its phosphorylation [ ]. (Fig.  ).

## STING regulates glucose metabolism
Many researchers have proved that STING modulates the activity of certain transcription factors that target glycolysis or glucose transport, in turn regulating glycolysis. (Fig.  ) For example, the activation of STING leads to IRF3 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, which in turn binds to the promoter region of 6-phosphofructo- 2-kinase/fructose- 2,6-bisphosphatase 3 (PFKFB3), enhancing its transcription [ ]. The increased expression of PFKFB3 drives the glycolytic process by activating phosphofructokinase- 1 (PFK- 1), a rate-limiting enzyme in glycolysis [ , ]. Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) has been proved to facilitate glycolysis through promoting the expression of key glycolytic genes such as hexokinase 2 (HK2) and 6-phosphofructo- 1-kinase (PFK1) [ , ]. Consistent with this, a study discovered that STING activation induces enhanced glycolysis through promoting the phosphorylation of STAT3 in epithelial cells [ ]. A study discovered that STING activation triggers AMPK phosphorylation, which in turn promotes TBC1 domain family member 1 (TBC1D1)-mediated glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4) translocation to the cell membrane, enhancing glucose uptake in response to energy stress [ ].
STING orchestrates metabolic reprogramming in immune cells, an example is macrophage polarization [ , ]. Specifically, activation of STING facilitates the activation of IRE1α, which is a key signaling pathway in the unfolded protein response (UPR) [ ]. IRE1α drives the expression and stabilization of hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF- 1α), leading to enhanced glycolysis, as HIF- 1α can promote the expression of genes associated with glycolysis [ ]. Another study proved that, the activation of STING enhances intracellular succinate levels and drives the production of mROS, leading to the stabilization of HIF- 1α, contributes to an inflammatory M1-like macrophage profile [ ]. In mechanism, the accumulation of succinate inhibits prolyl hydroxylase (PHD) and preventes the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of HIF- 1α, driving the stabilization and accumulation of HIF- 1α within the cell [ , ].
In adipocytes, IRF3 promotes interferon-stimulated gene 15 (ISG15) covalently attached to glycolytic enzyme through ISGylation [ ]. ISG15 then regulates the activity of LDHA by covalently modifying a specific lysine residue (Lys57), thereby inhibiting its enzymatic function and reducing lactate production [ , ].
In addition to these indirect effects, STING's N-terminal transmembrane domain binds to HK2, inhibiting its activity and altering its localization away from mitochondria, which in turn restricts glycolysis [ ].
Considering the STING signaling pathway as a reason for metabolic inflammation, it is strongly associated with insulin resistance. STING is involved in the production, secretion, and downstream signaling of insulin. (Fig.  ) Depletion of STING or IRF3 ameliorated PA-induced inflammation and apoptosis in INS- 1 cells and reversed the impairment in insulin synthesis [ ]. Paired Box 6(Pax6) is a necessary transcription factor for normal islet development and function, especially for GSIS [ , ]. Gene silencing of STING suppresses Pax6 expression, nuclear localization, and binding activities to the promoter regions of its target genes like Slc2a2, leading to the glucose-stimulated secretion of insulin [ ]. Another study revealed that the activation of the STING-IRF3 pathway induces the expression of AIG1 [ ]. AIG1 is an endogenous hydrolase of fatty acid esters of hydroxy fatty acids (FAHFAs) [ ], which promotes insulin secretion through activating the GPR40 receptor [ , ]. In insulin target cells like adipocytes and hepatocytes, STING signaling is also integrated into the downstream effects of insulin. TBK1 can directly phosphorylate serine 994 (S994) of the insulin receptor β (IRβ) in the livers [ ]. This serine site phosphorylation is considered inhibitory, as it blocks the activity of the insulin receptor tyrosine kinase and the subsequent downstream signaling pathways [ ].Upon the activation of the STING-IRF3 pathway, on the one hand, IRF3 phosphorylates IRs1 through interaction with JMJD8 [ ], and on the other hand, IRF3-mediated induction of Ppp2r1b amplified PP2 A activity, with subsequent dephosphorylation of AMPKα and AKT [ ]. The PI3 K-AKT pathway is considered essential in insulin signaling [ ]. Inflammation in different tissues is tightly linked to insulin resistance, in which STING also plays an important role [ – ].The activation of STING-NF-κB drives the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL- 6 [ , ]. TNF-α attenuates insulin-stimulated tyrosine phosphorylation of the insulin receptor and IRs1, thus promoting insulin resistance [ , ].The hepatic activation of IKKβ and NF-κB leads to systemic insulin resistance through the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines [ ]. Another study revealed that the interaction of IRF3 with IKKβ inhibited proinflammatory IKKβ/NF-κB signaling in the liver, mitigating insulin resistance, which is different from the contributor IRF3 plays [ ]. STING also promotes macrophage-mediated insulin resistance [ ]. In mechanism, STING activation in macrophages impairs glucose-stimulated insulin secretion by mediating the engulfment of β cell insulin secretory granules [ ].
 

## Nucleic acid metabolism
The cGAS-STING signaling pathway serves as a bridge connecting DNA sensing to immune responses, which is tightly related to the modification, repairing, and degradation of nucleic acid. In the context of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (mIDH1) cancers, the metabolic enzyme IDH1 is compromised, leading to the production of the oncometabolite (R)− 2-hydroxyglutarate (R- 2HG) [ – ]. This accumulation results in the inhibition of the DNA-demethylating enzyme Ten-eleven translocation 2 (TET2), consequently impairing the epigenetic activation of interferon-γ (IFNγ)-induced genes and promoting immune evasion [ , , ]. Pharmacological inhibition of mIDH1 reduces R- 2HG levels, reinstating TET2 activity and demethylating the promoter region of the cGAS, triggering the cGAS-STING signaling pathway [ ]. The deficiency of mutL homolog 1 (MLH1) in tumor cells activates the cGAS-STING signaling pathway through cytosolic DNA accumulation [ ]. The deficiency of (MLH1), a crucial component of the MutLα heterodimer in the DNA mismatch repair (MMR) system, leads to the dysregulation of exonuclease 1 (Exo1) activity during DNA repair processes [ , ]. This aberrant loss of control results in excessive resection of DNA by Exo1, culminating in the accumulation of single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) [ ], Consequently activating cGAS-STING signaling pathway [ , ]. Phospholipase D3 (PLD3) and PLD4 are single-stranded acid exonucleases that regulate endosomal nucleic-acid sensing [ ], modulating the STING signaling pathway by degrading single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) and ssRNA within the endoplasmic reticulum [ ]. Deficiency or dysregulation of PLD3 also leads to the accumulation of mtDNA within lysosomes, causing lysosomal leakage, resulting in the translocation of mtDNA into the cytosol, where it activates the cGAS-STING pathway [ ]. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD +) is a coenzyme essential for cellular energy metabolism [ ], involved in nucleotide metabolism [ ]. Banerjee et al. initially reported that the activation of the cGAS-STING signaling pathway leads to a reduction in NAD + levels in THP1 cells. [ ] In mechanism, the STING-IRF3 pathway enhances cytosolic adenylate cyclase (CD38) expression, an enzyme with both intracellular and extracellular NADase activity [ ], contributing to the reduction of NAD + levels within endothelial cells [ ]. This NAD + decline induced by CD38 activity disrupts mitochondrial homeostasis, leading to endothelial dysfunction and, ultimately, cardiotoxicity [ ]. In addition, targeting cytidine triphosphate synthase 1 (CTPS1) possibly causes cytoplasmic dsDNA accumulation, resulting in the activation of innate immune responses mediated by cGAS-STING [ ]. However, the underlying mechanisms connecting CTPS1 deficiency to the tumor microenvironment need further investigation.

## Amino acids metabolism
Previous research proved that IFN-I production induced by STING activates indoleamine 2,3 dioxygenase (IDO) in the tumor microenvironment, resulting in the catabolism of tryptophan into kynurenine in Lewis Lung Carcinoma (LLC) [ ]. Interestingly, in the context of bladder cancer, chemotherapy enhances the activity of indoleamine 2,3 dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), which catalyzes the conversion of tryptophan (Trp) into kynurenine (Kyn) [ ]. Kyn, acting as a ligand for the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR), activates AhR and promotes the formation of a complex between AhR, STING, and the CUL4B/RBX1 E3 ligase, facilitating STING degradation via the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway [ ]. Serine deprivation induces mitochondrial dysfunction [ ], leading to the accumulation of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) in the cytosol, which in turn activates the cGAS-STING pathway [ , ]. Under chronic ER stress, disruption of serine metabolism impairs cGAS-STING signaling through ROS production mediated by reduced glutathione (GSH) synthesis [ , ]. Arginine starvation causes mitochondrial dysfunction and reduced DNA repair gene expression, leading to ROS production and DNA damage [ , – ]. This triggers the accumulation of DNA repair proteins and chromatin leakage into the cytosol, activating the cGAS-STING signaling pathway [ ]. In addition, in glioblastoma stem cells (GSCs), lysine catabolism modulates the cGAS-STING signaling pathway by enhancing histone Kcr crotonylation [ ].

## An overview of the antitumor function of STING
As a key component of intracellular DNA recognition, the cGAS-STING pathway enhances tumor immunogenicity mainly through triggering IFN-I expression to promote the antitumor function of different immune cells. Many studies have reported that STING promotes dendritic cell maturation and restores antigen-presenting function, further promoting CD8 + T cell proliferation and activation [ – ]. The activation of STING promotes NK cell infiltration in a wide range of tumors and its anti-tumor functions [ – ]. STING has also been proved to promote polarization of M1-type tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) [ – ]. Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) suppress immune cell functions, leading to an immunosuppressive microenvironment [ , ]. The cGAS-STING pathway activation has a positive effect on the TME through reducing the activation and aggregation of MDSCs [ ]. In addition, STING plays a critical role in autophagy and has been discovered to kill tumor cells through autophagy, especially in the context of ferroptosis [ – ].
However, the regulation of tumor immunity by the cGAS-STING pathway is dichotomous; STING activation has been observed to lead to immune evasion and metastasis in multiple tumors, promoting tumor growth [ – ]. In some breast and ovarian cancer patients, increased expression of STING was found to be associated with an increased risk of tumor recurrence. STING also promotes autophagy, leading to esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) progression [ , ]. In addition, tumors was observed to induce STING-mediated T-cell death to evade immune control [ , ]. Activation of the STING signaling pathway also leads to enhanced B-cell regulation, inhibiting NK cell proliferation and anti-tumor responses [ ]. Immunosuppression due to increased regulatory T cells in the TME has been shown to be closely linked to STING [ , ].

## STING agonists in cancer therapy

## Cyclic dinucleotides
Cyclic dinucleotides (CDNs) are the primary enzymatic products of cGAS and consist of multiple CDN families. Natural CDN agonists include cGAMP, c-di-GMP, c-di-AMP, and variants of cGAMP (such as 3′3′-cGAMP and 2′3′-cGAMP) [ , ]. Current research lays emphasis on developing nonhydrolyzable CDN derivatives that can effectively mimic the naturally occurring cGAMP [ ]. (Table  ) For example, 2′,3′-cGAMP has a strong interaction with STING, and c-di-GMP triggers a balanced immune response of Th1 and Th2, as a promising adjuvant for vaccine development [ ].

 However, these CDNs are confronted with challenges, such as poor pharmacokinetic profiles, which hinders their clinical use as vaccine adjuvants [ ]. To improve the pharmaceutical properties of CDNs, research modified the phosphodiester, the 2′/3′-OH, the nucleobase, and the linkage position of CDNs [ ]. For example, aduro's ADU- 100, with a locked nucleic-acid structure on the ribose ring, exhibits improved cellular permeability and uptake compared with 2′,3′-cGAMP and ADU-S100 [ ]. However, modified CDNs often show poor metabolic stability, low utilization rate and limited cellular uptake [ ]. In addition, as a consequence of the strong hydrophilicity and negative charges of CDNs, most clinical research depend on intra-tumoral drug delivery, limiting its broader application [ , ]. Notably, as STING-induced apoptosis of immune cells in the TME may contribute to a more immunosuppressive environment, it is necessary to test the adjuvant dose-dependent safety profile in toxicology models before clinical evaluation [ ,  ].

## Nonnucleotide small molecule agonists
With higher resistance to the CDN-degrading hydrolase and stronger metabolic stability in vivo, non-CDN STING agonists were administered systemically in clinical research [ ]. (Table  ) 5, 6-dimethylxanthenone- 4-acetic acid (DMXAA) and flavone acetic acid (FAA), initially designed as vascular-disrupting agents, were reported to induce hemorrhagic necrosis effectively in murine tumors in preclinical studies [ ,  ]. Unfortunately, they failed in clinical trials because they did not bind to human STING [ , ]. While a recent study reported that DMXAA could act as a partial agonist of human STING at higher doses [ ]. To overcome species specificity, some derivatives of DMXAA have been designed, such as 10-carboxymethyl- 9-acridanone (CMA) and α-Mangostin. Among them, α-Mangostin, a natural product isolated from mangosteen, was proved to exhibit higher efficiency in activating human STING than mouse STING [ , ]. Future emphasis should be laid on developing synthetic agonist compounds capable of binding to human STING, enhancing stability, and resisting enzymatic degradation [ ].
First reported in 2018, amidobenzimidazole (ABZI)-based compounds represent a special type of STING agonists [ ]. Compared to cGAMP inducing a closed conformation of the STING, ABZI bounds to STING in an open conformation and is capable of activating both mSTING and hSTING [ ]. Notably, di-ABZI was reported to exhibit over 400 times greater potency than cGAMP in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells, inducing a stronger antitumor activity to completely inhibit tumor growth [ ]. SR- 717, an imidazole-pyridazine compound, has been reported to promote the activation of CD8 T cells, NK cells, and DCs in the TME when subcutaneously administrated [ ]. And it has entered preclinical studies for melanoma [ ]. Notably, a benzothiophene compound named MSA- 2, is the first STING agonist discovered that is suitable for oral administration [ ]. It has a high cellular uptake and retention capacity, showing preference for acidified tumor microenvironments with high permeability [ ]. When combined with the anti-PD-L1 antibody YM101, MSA- 2 showed significant long-term antitumor immunity in many cancer models, including MC38 and CT26 colorectal, LL- 2 lung cancer, and B16-F10 melanoma [ ].

## Other agonists
Indirect agonists of the cGAS-STING pathway generally function by inhibiting ectonucleotide pyrophosphatase phosphodiesterase 1 (ENPP1), promoting intracellular ROS production and transcriptional regulation. ENPP1, as a kind of hydrolyzing enzyme of cGAMP, inhibits the activation of innate immunity and promotes immune evasion through downregulating STING signaling [ , ]. Some nucleotides and non-nucleotide ENPP1 inhibitors have been developed to counteract its negative regulation of STING signaling, such as di-adenosine polyphosphonate derivatives, imidazopyridine- and purine-thioacetamide derivatives, as well as quinazolin- 4-piperidine- 4-methyl sulfamides and their derivatives [ – ]. However, these inhibitors are confronted with poor pharmacokinetic parameters, which limit them in clinical trials. Iron oxide nanoparticles (IONPs) have been used for cancer diagnosis and drug delivery clinically [ ]. Chen et al. presented a method of enhancing STING pathway activation using an acid-ionizable iron nanoadjuvant (PEIM). By combining IONPs with the STING agonist MSA- 2, the generation of ROS by IONPs and the subsequent activation of NF-κB are harnessed to boost IFN-I production, thereby amplifying the STING signaling. This method has demonstrated antitumor efficacy in the B16-OVA melanoma and MC38 colorectal cancer models [ ].

## Potential metabolic targets for STING activation in the TME
Currently, STING agonists are facing many challenges in clinical applications, which have been discussed above. However, the interaction between STING and metabolism in the TME, which has been mentioned above, provides a theoretical basis for targeting metabolic reprogramming to induce STING activation in cancer therapies. It has the potential not only to overcome the limitations of traditional agonists but also to affect the growth of tumor cells from the source and facilitate an antitumor immune response. We discussed several metabolic targets that are promising for STING activation in the following paragraph. (Table  ).

 

## Lipid metabolism
Many drugs targeting PI4P metabolism have been reported to enhance PI4P levels at TGN and promote STING activation, such as BF738735, itraconazole, and OSBP inhibitors [ , , , ]. Notably, itraconazole, as an established antifungal, is being evaluated as an anticancer drug [ ]. Apart from OSBP, itraconazole also regulates NPC1 activity [ ], possibly promoting STING signaling through inhibiting NPC1 in part [ ]. While the structurally unrelated OSBP-inhibitor OSW- 1 is highly specific for OSBP [ ]. In addition, it is worth mentioning that targeting PI4P to restore inadequate STING activation relies on fine-tuning, because evaluated PI4P may contribute to malignant secretory phenotypes, which feature in more aggressive and difficult to treat cancers [ ]. Drug development targeting phosphatidylinositol 4 kinases (PI4 Ks) is confronted with a challenge of highly selective inhibitors as the structural similarity of different PI4 K isoforms [ ].
Considering that cancers have higher levels of cholesterol to meet their great demand for rapid proliferation [ ], drugs targeting cellular cholesterol levels have been developed as an antitumor therapy [ , ]. Strategies include targeting the cholesterol synthesis, transformation, transport, efflux, and LXR as well as SREBP2 signaling [ ]. It has been proved that statin drugs can restrict the progress of many cancers and reduce the mortality rate of patients, including stomach cancer, breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, etc [ ]. A recent study reported that Methyl-β-cyclodextrin (MβCD), as an effective depletors [ , ], restricted cancer cell survival and was involved in the STING agonist induced antitumor immunity as well as checkpoint blockage [ , , ]. Previously, STING agonists MK- 1454 and ADU-S100 in combination with checkpoint inhibitor treatment was reported to show poor effects in phase-I clinical trials [ ]. Therefore, reducing cholesterol levels in the TME combined with STING agonists could be a viable anti-tumor strategy. However, high levels of MβCD was discovered to counteract the antitumor response compared to low doses of MβCD [ ]. Therefore, it is necessary to regulate cholesterol in the ER and Golgi TGN accurately to drive STING-mediated anticancer immunity. In addition, some drugs targeting cholesterol metabolic enzymes mentioned above have been proved to inhibit tumor growth effectively. (Table  ) For example, Lomitapide (lomi), a microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTP) inhibitor used for severe hypercholesterolemia [ , ],, has been proved to reduce cholesterol biosynthesis by inhibiting MTP, which overcomes circulating tumor cell evasion through inducing STING signaling [ ]. LXR, acting as a bridge between cholesterol metabolism and innate immunity, the agonists of which have been proved to modulate the abundance of regulatory T cells and block tumor metastasis [ , ]. However, research discovered that LXR activation upregulated the expression of SMPDL3 A, leading to cGAMP degrading and dampening STING-induced immunity [ ]. SR9243, an inhibitor of LXR, reduces FA biosynthesis and induces apoptosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) [ ]. Therefore, how LXR regulates STING in the TME deserves further exploration. In addition, engineering a cGAMP analog with resistance to SMPDL3 A may be a promising future for designing novel agonists of STING [ ]. As an important transcription factor regulating cholesterol synthesis, SREBP plays an indispensable role in maintaining tumor growth, such as hepatocellular carcinoma and brain cancer [ – ]. However, SREBP2 primes STING signaling by facilitating STING trafficking from the ER to the Golgi, a process independent of cGAS and cGAMP [ ]. Excessive inhibition of SREBP may lead to metabolic adaptation and therapeutic resistance in tumor cells [ ]. Therefore, SREBP homeostasis in TME is important for the regulation of cholesterol metabolism and immune cell function.
Most tumors undergo rising endogenous fatty acid biosynthesis, showing greatly upregulated ACLY expression [ , ]. ACLY inhibitors, which proved to reduce tumorigenesis in vivo, have recently drawn great attention as promising antitumor agents [ , ]. Inhibition of ACLY was discovered to promote cGAS-STING signaling via lipid peroxidation, especially MDA accumulation [ ], in cancer cells, and PUFA supplementation is sufficient to achieve the same effects [ ]. However, 4-HNE, also a lipid peroxidation product, was proved to suppress STING activation [ – ]. It is worth noting that bempedoic acid (BemA), an ACLY inhibitor, has been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol recently [ ]. But it needs to mention that ACLY inhibition and PUFAs also drive immunosuppression in tumors [ ,  ]. Further study will be needed to explore how antitumor immunity is influenced by ACLY inhibition-induced metabolic reprogramming.

## Glucose metabolism
Glycolytic reprogramming is a metabolic hallmark of activated DCs [ ]. A study showed that pharmacological promotion of glycolysis-dependent ATP production enhances STING activity and accelerates HIF- 1α-mediated glycolysis to facilitate dendritic cell antitumor function [ ]. (Table  ) Given that targeting HIF- 1α eliminates PD-L1-mediated immune evasion in TME, it implies HIF- 1α is a potential target for promoting anticancer therapy [ , ]. Itaconate is an important metabolite connecting macrophage immunity and metabolism [ , ]. Itaconate can block immune response via alkylation of STING [ ]. However, previous studies mainly focused on its anti-inflammatory biological effects, and still little is known about the effect of itaconate on STING in TME [ ]. A growing number of studies have proved that itaconate contributes to tumor growth and blocking the IRG1-itaconate axis inhibits tumor progression effectively [ – ]. Ibuprofen was found to restrict HCC progression by targeting IRG1-itaconate-dependent tumor immunoevasion [ ]. In the future, it is worth further exploring whether the anti-tumor immunity mediated by STING can be enhanced by targeting IRG1.

## Amino acid metabolism
The vigorous proliferative capacity of tumor cells indicates a strong demand for amino acids to meet the enhanced proliferation [ ]. Given the fact that amino acids are usually involved in signal transduction and have direct biological activities, targeting amino acid metabolism to regulate anti-tumor immunity through the cGAS-STING signaling pathway may have great potential [ ].
Trp is essential for T cell activation and proliferation [ , ]. Trp is mainly metabolized through the Kyn pathway mediated by IDO, a universal enzyme in the host [ , ]. Tumors are demonstrated to have increased Trp uptake, catabolisms and enhanced IDO expression [ , , , ]. These facts show that tumor cells possibly escape from immunosurveillance through the negative feedback regulatory mechanism of IDO1-AhR-STING [ ]. Previous research proved that inhibition of the Trp-IDO pathway diminished tumor metastasis and enhanced CD8 T-cell function [ ]. Recently, metabolism-regulated nanoparticles were prepared through metal coordination-driven assembly of an IDO1 inhibitor (NLG919) and a stimulator of interferon genes (STING) agonist (MSA- 2), significantly inhibiting the development of primary and metastatic tumors, as well as B16 melanoma [ ].
Tumor cells have been observed to have increased expression of enzymes catabolizing ʟ-arg and surprisingly low concentrations of ʟ-arg [ – ]. Arginine-synthesis deficiency is among the most frequently observed tumor cell phenotypes and has been exploited therapeutically [ ].
Because of these, tumors was observed to be dependent on exogenous arginine, susceptible to cGAS-STING activation induced by arginine starvation treatment [ ]. In the past, arginine was considered an agonist for immune cells [ – ]. However, some research discovered that arginine deprivation induces immune cell infiltration [ , ].
The non-essential amino acid serine is regarded as promoting oncogenesis [ – ]. Inhibition of serine in tumor cells exerts anti-tumor effects in many cancer types [ , – ]. Saha et al. found that serine deprivation induces cGAS-STING to suppress tumor growth. [ ] Considering normal exogenous serine levels are required for lymphocyte proliferation and macrophage activation, it is necessary to control proper exogenous serine supply [ – ]. Saha et al. reported that tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes involved in tumor control as elicited by restricting endogenous and exogenous serine to malignant cells are functional, reasoning that the initiation of tumor-targeting immunity would benefit from exogenous serine restriction to cancer cells, while the effector phase would benefit from normal serine levels for immune cells [ ].

## Discussion
The cGAS-STING pathway, traditionally recognized for its role in cytosolic DNA sensing, has emerged as a central metabolic regulator that orchestrates cellular bioenergetics and immune responses. Beyond its canonical function in type I interferon production, STING directly modulates glycolysis, lipid metabolism, and mitochondrial dynamics [ , , ]. For instance, STING activation suppresses glycolysis by sequestering hexokinase 2 (HK2) away from mitochondria, thereby restricting ATP production in tumor cells [ ]. Conversely, in dendritic cells, STING-driven HIF- 1α stabilization enhances glycolytic flux to fuel antitumor immunity [ ]. Lipid metabolism is similarly influenced, with STING activation repressing cholesterol biosynthesis via NF-κB-mediated downregulation of FDFT1 and promoting fatty acid oxidation through IRF3-dependent suppression of PPARγ [ ]. These findings highlight STING’s dual role as both a metabolic effector and a modulator of immune cell function. However, the metabolic consequences of STING activation vary across tissues and cancer types. For example, hepatic STING promotes lipid accumulation by inhibiting lipophagy [ ], whereas in adipocytes, it disrupts insulin signaling via TBK1-mediated phosphorylation of insulin receptors [ ]. Understanding this heterogeneity is critical for developing context-specific therapies.
Metabolic reprogramming in the tumor microenvironment (TME) creates both opportunities and challenges for STING activation. Cholesterol, PI4P, and lipid peroxidation products act as critical checkpoints regulating STING trafficking, post-translational modifications, and signalosome assembly [ , , , ]. For example, ER cholesterol retains STING in an inactive state, while cGAMP-induced cholesterol esterification facilitates its Golgi translocation [ ]. The compartmentalization of metabolic enzymes, such as SMPDL3 A in lysosomes vs. ACLY in the cytosol, creates spatially distinct pools of metabolites that differentially regulate STING [ , ]. Advanced imaging techniques are needed to map metabolite-STING interactions in real time. These findings underscore the intricate metabolic regulation of STING, which tumors exploit to evade immune detection. The dual roles of metabolites is another challenge. For example, ACLY inhibition induced PUFA accumulation activates STING by triggering mtDNA leakage [ ], while excessive lipid peroxidation generates immunosuppressive aldehydes like 4-HNE [ , ]. Balancing pro- and anti-inflammatory lipid signals remains a therapeutic hurdle.
The clinical potential of STING agonists has been limited by pharmacokinetic challenges and immune-related toxicities. However, targeting metabolic vulnerabilities in the TME offers a promising strategy to enhance STING activation while minimizing systemic side effects. CDNs like ADU-S100 show restricted efficacy due to rapid enzymatic degradation, but nanoparticle-based delivery systems, such as PEIM-MSA- 2, improve stability and tumor specificity [ ]. Non-nucleotide agonists, such as MSA- 2, exploit acidic tumor microenvironments for selective activation, yet their long-term safety requires validation [ ]. Metabolic adjuvants, including ACLY inhibitors (bempedoic acid), SGLT2 antagonists (canagliflozin), and IDO1 blockers (NLG919), have been proved to synergize with STING agonists by inducing immunogenic stress and reversing immunosuppressive feedback loops [ , ]. While STING’s role as a metabolic regulator provides novel avenues to disrupt cancer progression, the complexity of metabolic checkpoints demands a nuanced approach. Future research must address tissue-specific metabolic adaptations, optimize combinatorial regimens, and leverage advanced delivery systems to translate preclinical insights into clinically viable therapies. By integrating metabolic profiling with immune monitoring, we may unlock the full potential of STING-targeted strategies in precision oncology.
