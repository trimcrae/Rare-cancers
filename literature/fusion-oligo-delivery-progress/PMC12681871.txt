## Highlights
•
The dysregulation of STK32 kinases is observed in cancer.

•
STK32 kinases exhibit tissue-specific oncogenic roles across malignancies.

•
Structural features and unique activation mechanisms of STK32 are detailly described.

•
STK32s are emerging as biomarkers and therapeutic targets for cancer treatment.

As a subgroup of AGC kinases, serine/threonine kinase 32 family (STK32, also known as Yet Another Novel Kinases family) consists of three members: STK32A, STK32B and STK32C. Recently, emerging evidences have implicated the aberrant expression and dysregulated functions of STK32 kinases in human malignancies. However, there is a lack of systematic review on this topic. Here, we aim to detailly examine the expression and functional features of STK32 family kinases within the context of present reports and public datasets related, and outline current understanding on the multifunctional roles and up-stream regulatory mechanisms and down-stream effectors of STK32 kinases to highlight key research focus for future exploration and provide rationale for developing STK32-targeted therapeutics in cancer.

## Introduction
It is well-recognized that protein kinases orchestrate the transduction of majority signals within or between eukaryotic cells, and contribute to modulating critical cellular biological programs in cancer initiation and progression [ ]. Small molecule kinase inhibitors employed for targeted cancer therapies have achieved satisfactory therapeutic efficiencies in treating human malignancies like lung and breast cancer [ ]. While the significance of protein kinases as therapeutic targets in cancer has been acknowledged, there remains a lack of understanding regarding the functional roles of approximately one-third of protein kinases, termed as dark kinases [ ]. AGC protein kinases are one of the most conserved kinase family in evolution and widely presented in eukaryotes. Among the subgroups of AGC kinases, serine/threonine kinase 32 (STK32, also named as Yet Another Novel Kinase, YANK) family comprises of three members, and represents a relatively under-characterized group of dark kinases, including STK32A, STK32B, and STK32C (also named as YANK1, YANK2, and YANK3, respectively). Recently, with the development of omics techniques and experimental exploration, emerging evidences indicate the aberrant expression and functional roles of three STK32 proteins in human neoplastic diseases. The present review systematically examines current reports on these three STK32 kinases, focusing on their structure, biological and pathophysiological functions, and relevant mechanisms in the pathogenesis of cancer. Moreover, discussion is provided on the oncogenic mechanisms of STK32 proteins and the potential implications of therapeutics targeting them in cancer therapy, with the aim of identifying potential targets for developing cancer treatment strategies and advancing research into the molecular mechanisms of STK32 kinases.

## Structure and physiological regulatory mechanisms of STK32 kinases
STK32 kinases exhibit significant structural conservation within the protein kinase superfamily ( A-C, and Supplementary Figure 1). STK32A, STK32B and STK32C are located at chromosome 5q32, 4p16.2, and 10q26.3, respectively. The structures and activation mechanisms of human STK32 have received limited investigation, and the potential structural features and activation mechanisms within the STK32 family kinases are primarily provided based on the conserved structures and catalytic domains of the eukaryotic protein kinase (EPK) superfamily ( ), as well as the established research regarding the sequences, structures, and activation mechanisms of AGC kinase family. It should be noted that STK32B proteins differ from the conserved sequences and residues of AGC proteins in key motifs and residues, although they possess conserved structural features of AGC kinases. This may result in distinctive activation mechanisms within the STK32 family compared to other AGC kinases. 

## The structure and conserved catalytic domains of STK32 kinases
The structure of STK32 kinases conforms to the general structural characteristics of the EPK superfamily, which is the bilobed kinase fold consisting of a small N-lobe and a large C-lobe ( B) [ ]. In the N-lobe, the STK32 proteins possess a conserved phosphate-binding loop (P-loop) catalytic domain (Gly–Lys–Gly–Ser–Phe–Gly) identical to the P-loop sequence of Protein Kinase C (PKC), both containing a serine residue ( A-C) [ ]. The αC helix and β-strand 3 of the STK32 kinases are similar to the EPK superfamily, and possess conserved glutamate and lysine residues ( B), which forms a salt bridge driving the transfer of phosphate group to the substrate in the EPK superfamily [ ]. A recent study employing STK32A wild type and mutant STK32A Lys52Arg adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors discloses that the substitution of the Lys52 in β-strand 3 of STK32A with arginine may influence its kinase activity [ ].
Compared to the N-lobes, the C-lobes of the STK32 kinases contain a conserved catalytic loop (His–Arg–Asp–x–Lys–Pro–Asp–Asn), which includes the His–Arg–Asp (HRD) motif (Figure1B, C) [ ]. Indeed, the substitution of aspartate with alanine in the HRD motif of STK32C in triple-negative breast cancer (26) cells leads to a significant reduction of kinase activity in the mutant STK32C Asp216Ala, indicating the critical involvement of the HRD motif in regulating STK32C activity [ ]. This is consistent with the previous demonstration on the essential role of the HRD motif in regulating protein kinase activity [ ].

## The potential activation mechanisms of STK32 family members
The phosphorylation of amino acid residues within the activation segment facilitates the active conformation of protein kinases [ ]. Broadly, AGC protein kinases possess phosphorylation sites not only within their activation segment, but also in the hydrophobic and turn motifs, all of which contribute to their activation (9). The activation segment of AGC kinases defined as the sequence from DFG (Asp–Phe–Gly) motif, the crucial catalytic element, to APE (Ala–Pro–Glu) motif situated within the C-lobe [ ]. In general, the DFG motif positions ATP for phosphoryl transferring ( B, C) [ ]. The glycine residue in the DFG motif of STK32 kinases is replaced by asparagine ( C). The glycine in the DFG motif is crucial for the switch between active and inactive conformations of protein kinases, in which glycine form a hydrogen bond with aspartate in the DFG motif under the active conformation [ ]. By contrast, glycine undergoes extreme twisting and breaking the hydrogen bond with aspartate upon inactivation [ ]. This ability to twist without steric hindrance is due to the unique property of glycine lacking a side chain [ ]. Substituting the small and flexible glycine in the DFG motif with asparagine, which possesses a large and bulky side chain, alters the conformation of protein kinases, thereby affecting their kinase activities [ ] and affinity to compounds [ ]. Given that the activation segment of AGC protein kinases typically contains a highly conserved phosphorylation site at serine and threonine residues necessary for AGC kinase activation ( C) [ ], further investigation is required to elucidate the impact of glycine replaced by aspartate in the DFG motif of STK32 proteins on their conformation and kinase activities. Besides, STK32A and STK32B contain conserved threonine and serine residues at the conserved phosphorylation sites in their activation segments, respectively ( C). This conserved phosphorylation site is replaced by an alanine residue in STK32C, suggesting alternative mechanisms for STK32C activation ( C). It is noteworthy that most AGC protein kinases possess a conserved hydrophobic motif (Phe–x–x–Phe–Ser/Thr–Tyr) locating on the C-terminal tail [ ]. Considering that the phosphorylation of serine and threonine residues is critical for the activation of AGC family kinases and there is lack of phosphorylation sites in hydrophobic motif of STK32 proteins (Phe–Val/Ile–Val/Ile–Phe–Asn–Arg; B, C), it is reasonable to suppose that the activities of STK32 couldn’t be regulated by the phosphorylation on the hydrophobic motif. This is consistent with the previous illustration that the phosphorylation of hydrophobic motif is not a prerequisite for the activation of AGC proteins [ ]. The tyrosine residue in the conserved hydrophobic motif of AGC kinases is replaced by a basic arginine residue in STK32 proteins ( C), and there is a serine residue located in the turn motif of STK32 at the C-terminal tail, preceding the hydrophobic motif ( B, C). Since the turn motif is associated with the full activation of AGC kinases [ ], the impact of turn motif phosphorylation on the activation of STK32 kinases is worthy further elucidation.
A recent study provides evidences for the binding of the non-receptor tyrosine kinase Fyn with STK32B in glioma cells, which phosphorylates STK32B at Tyr110 [ ]. Fyn mediated Tyr110 phosphorylation of STK32B enhances STK32B protein stability and promotes glioma cell growth both in vitro and in vivo, implicating its oncogenic role in supporting glioma progression [ ]. However, this phosphorylation site of STK32B is not located in the canonical motifs associated with AGC kinase activation, including the kinase activation segment, hydrophobic motif, and turn motif. Given that Tyr110 and the related sequence (Gly–Asp–Leu–Arg–Tyr–His–Leu–Gln–Gln) is highly conserved in STK32 family proteins ( C) [ ], investigation is warranted to further clarify whether Fyn-mediated Tyr110 phosphorylation is a common mechanism for regulating STK32 kinase activities. Additionally, STK32C has been implicated as a putative substrate for cyclin-dependent kinase-5 (CDK5) in brain tissue at the phosphorylation site Ser18, providing a target for further clarification on upstream regulatory mechanisms of STK32C [ ].
It has been demonstrated that STK32 proteins and distantly related kinases on the kinome tree, such as casein kinase 1/2 (CK1/2) and glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK3), converge to phosphorylate similar motif sequences [ ]. According to modelling p63 peptide bound to CK1δ (Protein Data Bank (PDB): 6RU8) onto STK32A, phosphorylated residues at positions -3 and +2 of pSer are disclosed as potential binding sites for STK32A (PDB: 4FR4) [ ]. Moreover, there are regulatory mechanisms limiting CK1 and GSK3 exhibiting their kinase activities. The CK1 protein kinase prefers substrates with priming phosphorylation at position -3 relative to the phosphorylation site, while the GSK3 family favors substrates with priming phosphorylation at position +4 relative to the phosphorylation site [ , ]. Similarly, the STK32 family may be regulated by the mechanism requiring priming phosphorylation of substrates by another kinase to enable subsequent substrate phosphorylation [ , ]. This offers a direction for investigating the substrate regulation mechanisms of STK32 members.

## STK32 mediated substrate phosphorylation
Present evidences suggest that STK32 kinases exhibit preference for negatively charged residues, distinguishing them from most other basophilic AGC kinases [ , ]. However, there is no direct mechanistic elucidation on their preference of acidic substrates.
There are several studies identifying phosphorylated substrates of STK32 proteins. According to the verification in glioma cells, STK32B interacts with the p70 ribosomal S6 kinase (p70S6K) and phosphorylates p70S6K at hydrophobic motif Thr389 [ ]. Meanwhile, a human kinome library screening with in vitro kinase assays identified that STK32B efficiently phosphorylates Thr1079 in the hydrophobic motif of human truncated large tumor suppressor 1 (LATS1) purified from Eschericia coli, which is potentially involved in regulating the Hippo signaling [ ]. STK32B and STK32C primarily select substrates with a phosphorylated tyrosine residue at the +2 position relative to the serine or threonine phosphorylation site [ ]. Additionally, STK32A negatively regulates the localization of G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) family member GPR156 at the apical cell boundary of hair cells, which depends on its kinase activity and indicates the involvement of phosphorylation-dependent regulatory mechanism regulating the distribution of GPR156 [ ]. A novel mechanism has recently been identified whereby STK32C binds to c-MYC and phosphorylates it at the conserved S420 residue [ ]. This STK32C mediated phosphorylation prevents ubiquitin-mediated degradation of c-MYC, thereby stabilizing c-MYC protein and enhancing downstream proliferative signaling [ ].

## Subcellular localization and the involvement of STK32 kinases in signal transduction
The proper subcellular localization of protein kinases is required for their achievement of transducing specific signaling. According to Human Protein Atlas, STK32 family kinases locate in distinct subcellular compartments [ ]. In particular, STK32A protein localizes to the centrosome, while STK32B protein could be found in the nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, and cytosol [ ]. Although STK32C is predicted to be a cell cytosolic kinase [ ], there is a report indicating that STK32C protein locates in both the nucleoplasm and cytoplasm [ ]. Indeed, the subcellular localization of STK32C has been indicated to associated with its functional properties [ ]. STK32C has been observed to locate in both the nucleus and cytoplasm of doxorubicin-resistant TNBC cells, and STK32C overexpression in the nucleus did not affect the sensitivity of TNBC cells to doxorubicin [ ], in contrast to cytoplasmic STK32C elevation significantly decreased the sensitivity of TNBC cells to doxorubicin by manipulating glycolysis in these cells [ ].
Interestingly, STK32 kinases may translocate to the plasma membrane for signal transduction. Myristoylation is an important post-translation modification mechanism by which AGC protein kinases translocate to the cell membrane and exert their biological functions [ ]. STK32A and STK32B have been identified as N-myristoylated proteins [ , ]. Wild type N-myristoylated STK32A protein predominantly localizes to the plasma membrane after myristoylation. By contrast, STK32A Gly2Ala mutant protein lacking myristoylated potency is observed to locate in the cytoplasm [ ]. This supports myristoylation as a regulatory approach for the intracellular translocation of STK32A to cell membrane, which represents a potential molecular mechanism for STK32A mediated membrane protein phosphorylation. Additionally, a motif-based analysis identifies that, compared to wild type STK32A overexpression, exogenous expression of STK32A mutant lacking N-myristoyl glycine at position 2 is unable to reverse the orientation of stereo-ciliary bundles in the lateral utricle hair cells [ ], indicating the requirement of myristoylation at this residue for STK32A to function properly.

## Functional participation of STK32 kinases in cancer
The dysregulation of STK32 is primarily observed in neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders, especially as the molecular markers for neurodegenerative diseases [ , , , , ]. Currently, there are evidences from experimental validation supporting the association between abnormal expression and phosphorylation levels of STK32 kinases and oncogenic signaling pathways. While exhibiting aberrant expression in cancer, STK32 proteins have been implicated as potential hub kinases for tissue-specific oncogenic signal transduction involved in regulating multiple cancer hallmarks [ , ].

## Aberrant expression and prognostic relevance of STK32 kinases in cancer
According to the analysis of Tumor Immune Estimation Resource 2.0 (TIMER 2.0, http://timer.cistrome.org/) including 33 types of cancer using pan-cancer data obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), three STK32 members exhibit context-dependent expression in human malignancies ( A-C). The upregulation of STK32B and STK32C in glioma and bladder cancer has been validated with human clinical samples and in vitro assays, respectively [ , ]. Besides, the upregulation of STK32C has been observed in breast cancer [ ]. Analysis of a colorectal cancer (CRC) patient cohort revealed significant STK32C upregulation in tumor samples compared to adjacent normal tissues [ ]. The differential expression pattern of STK32 family proteins in diverse types of cancers may attribute to inter-tumor or intra-tumor heterogeneity and reflect tissue-specific tumorigenesis mechanisms, which is affected by etiological and biological variations [ , ] . 
Moreover, STK32B expression has been reported to vary between different cancer subtypes, which potentially serves as a specific molecular marker for the identification of different subtypes of these malignancies [ , , ]. Compared to histologically normal epithelium samples of breasts containing estrogen receptor (ER)-negative cancer, STK32B is upregulated in histologically normal epithelium samples of breasts containing ER-positive breast cancer, in comparison with ER-negative counterpart [ ]. Indeed, the CpG island in STK32B promoter has been disclosed to present hypermethylation in ER-negative breast cancer cells [ ]. STK32B elevation is also observed in the clear cell A subtype of clear cell renal cell carcinoma, which exhibits not only superior prognosis, but also cancer subtype-specific and recurrence-free survival in comparison to the clear cell B subtype [ ]. Additionally, compared to IDH-mutant gliomas, the expression of STK32B is upregulated in IDH-wild type glioma [ ].
Likewise, the abnormal expression of STK32 proteins also exhibits cancer type-specific relevance to patient survival in malignancies. STK32A mRNA expression positively correlates with favorable survival in bladder cancer and lung adenocarcinoma [ , ]. STK32B mRNA elevation is correlated with favorable prognosis and longer disease-free survival in breast cancer[ , ]. By comparison, high STK32C expression is correlates with advanced tumor stages and indicates significantly poorer overall survival in CRC, underscoring its prognostic value in this malignancy [ , ]. Besides, STK32A and STK32C mRNA elevation indicates poor prognosis in melanoma [ , ]. The hypomethylation of CpG cg09088988 in STK32A promoter has been implicated to be significantly correlated with reduced overall survival of stomach adenocarcinoma patients [ ]. Increased STK32A mRNA expression suggests poor prognosis in gastric and clear cell renal cancer [ , ]. Similarly, STK32B mRNA elevation indicates shorter overall survival in glioma patients, and STK32C mRNA upregulation indicates poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma [ , ].

## Mutation, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP) and copy number variation (CNV) of STK32 in cancer
Except for abnormal expression of STK32 kinases in cancer, the mutation analysis of STK32 member encoding genes based on TCGA discloses that they exhibit the highest mutation frequencies in uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), with mutation frequencies of STK32A, STK32B, and STK32C in UCEC being 2.2371 %, 4.9217 %, and 4.0268 %, respectively ( D and Supplementary Table 1). However, there is no direct evidence demonstrating the correlation between STK32 family gene mutations and their abnormal expression in cancer. The predicted sites and occurrence probabilities of STK32 family gene mutations in human cancers are summarized from TCGA pan-cancer cohort and shown in A. Indeed, most of mutations in STK32 family members are missense mutations. Mutations in the protein kinase domains including the P-loop and activation segment are frequently activating mutations in cancer, and contribute to cancer development [ ]. Therefore, the following discussions focus on summarizing the missense mutations in the motifs related to the activation mechanism of AGC kinases including activation segment, hydrophobic and turn motifs, and the catalytic domains like the P-loop and HRD motif in STK32 family proteins ( A). 
SNP and single nucleotide variants (SNV) of STK32 member encoding genes have been observed in cancer [ , ]. A Genome-wide association study (GWAS) in the Chinese population discloses the evidence for SNP rs2895680 located in intron 2 of STK32A as a susceptibility locus for lung cancer [ ]. In addition, the presence of SNP rs17464616 in STK32B has been demonstrated in primary gastrointestinal stromal tumors [ ], and the occurrence of SNP rs10012412 in STK32B is significantly associated with the clinical stages of breast cancer progression [ ]. In a study of Wilms tumor (WT, or nephroblastoma), an embryonal tumor arising from renal precursor cells, STK32C V339M mutation was detected in one WT sample employed for 3D spheroid culture [ ].
Given that individual genomic variation is primarily due to CNV, the CNV of STK32 family members is analyzed in 33 types of cancer, according to TCGA pan-cancer dataset ( B). Indeed, there are both of copy number deletions and amplifications of three STK32 members in 29 types of cancer ( B). In acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and lymphoid neoplasm diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), only copy number deletions of STK32A and STK32B are observed, with no evidence of copy number amplifications of three STK32 members encoding genes. Similarly, in low-grade glioma (LGG) and uveal melanoma (UVM), there are only observation of copy number deletions of STK32C ( B). Although there is no direct evidence supporting the role of the high frequency CNV of STK32 members in tumorigenesis and cancer progression, a lung cancer study suggests that the minimal deletion region of the 4p16 allele, including STK32B, is associated with the transition from carcinoma in situ to invasive tumor [ ], indicating the possible involvement of STK32B deletion in the metastasis and recurrence of lung cancer.

## Functions and engagements of STK32 kinases in dysregulated oncogenic signal transduction
STK32 kinases have been indicated to be involved in mediating aberrant signaling transduction in malignancies ( and ), which enables them to function in the hallmarks of cancer like sustaining proliferative signaling and activating invasion and metastasis. 

## Transducing oncogenic signals
STK32A is shown to be involved in the dysregulated transforming growth factor beta (TGF-beta) signaling pathway in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, in which STK32A reduction was observed at 24–48 hours after SMAD4 overexpression in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cells [ ]. Moreover, knockdown of SOX2 in a primary low passage serum-cultured glioblastoma (GBM) cell line induces markedly reduced expression of STK32A [ ], suggesting the potential involvement of SOX2 in manipulating STK32A expression. Moreover, STK32B is phosphorylated at Tyr110 by Fyn, and Fyn inhibition leads to decreased STK32B expression in glioma cells [ ]. STK32B has been reported to be overexpressed in GBM with fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR) alterations including FGFR2 amplification and FGFR2-TACC2 fusion [ ]. STK32B phosphorylates p70S6K at Thr389 in glioma cells, indicating an alternative signaling pathway for p70S6K activation mediated by STK32B, which is independent of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway [ ]. In addition, Restraining STK32B induces retinoblastoma protein (pRB)-mediated growth suppression in human osteosarcoma cells, especially G1 arrest [ ].
STK32C exhibits elevated expression in mice optic glioma with both absent neurofibromatosis type 1 (Nf1) and phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) gene expression, compared to optic glioma mice with absent Nf1 gene expression alone [ ]. This supports STK32C serving as a potential downstream regulatory target of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, and provides a clue for molecular mechanism studies of STK32C. Likewise, STK32C has been shown to be the target of GLI2 transcription factors by both chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) and bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis with melanoma cell samples [ ]. STK32C has been indicated to play an essential role in promoting epithelial bladder cancer progression by modulating HMGB1 pathway, in which knocking down STK32C efficiently reduced mRNA and protein expression of four key components (DDX58, PAK1, IL1, and PIK3R1) of HMGB1 signaling [ ]. STK32C knock-down also leads to the upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2) protein in epithelial bladder cancer, instead of CDK2 mRNA, indicating a potential STK32C-mediated post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism on CDK2 protein, which possibly contributes to the cell cycle regulation in these cells [ ]. In addition, STK32C directly engages c-MYC and induces phosphorylation at the conserved S420 position [ ]. This post-translational modification shields c-MYC from proteasomal degradation by reducing its ubiquitination, thereby prolonging the stability of c-MYC onco-protein and reinforcing the corresponding oncogenic signaling cascades promoting CRC progression[ ].

## Facilitating malignant cells evading growth suppressors and resisting apoptosis
Malignant cells presents uncontrolled proliferation with the features of evading growth suppressors and resisting to cell death, in which the contribution of STK32 kinases have been indicated [ ]. As proof, the phosphorylation of STK32B activates mTOR pathway and promote the proliferation of glioma cells by transducing oncogenic Fyn signaling [ ]. STK32C also has been shown to promote neoplastic cell proliferation and inhibit cancer cell apoptosis in bladder cancer [ ]. There is a study revealing that STK32C overexpression significantly enhances the proliferative capacities of CRC cells, as evidenced by increased DNA synthesis, ATP production, and colony formation [ ]. Conversely, STK32C silencing impaired these biological processes, indicating its essential role in sustaining malignant cell growth[ ]. Genetic inhibition of STK32B suppresses the proliferation of malignant cells in oral squamous cell carcinoma [ ]. Besides, STK32B protein expression is validated to be significantly correlated with clinicopathological characteristics of oral squamous cell carcinoma like tumor size and metastasis [ ]. Previously, it has been reported that both STK32A and STK32C are capable of activating human telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT) promoter luciferase reporter construct and function as hTERT regulatory kinases in lung adenocarcinoma, and the subsequent upregulation of hTERT circumvents telomere-dependent replicative senescence and promotes tumor progression [ ]. The exposure of melanoma cells to the secretome of senescent melanoma cells elevates their STK32B expression, which could be abrogated by signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) inhibition [ ]. This indicates the potential engagement of STAT3 activation on inducing STK32B upregulation [ ]. Additionally, transfection of STK32C constitutive active kinase vectors to human fibroblasts decreases their proliferation capabilities and induces the upregulation of p16 and senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) cytokine encoding genes, including IL1A, IL1B, IL5, and IL8. This observation suggests the potential involvement of STK32C in regulating cellular senescence [ ]. Due to the intimate association between senescence and cancer progression, the specific role of STK32C in cancer senescence is worthy further investigation.

## Activating invasion and metastasis
The activation of invasion and metastasis are interrelated pathophysiological processes for cancer progression [ ]. STK32C has been demonstrated to enhance the migration and invasion of malignant cells in bladder cancer [ ]. In parallel, its upregulation also markedly elevates the migratory and invasive activities of colon cancer cells in vitro [ ]. Genetic inhibition of STK32C reversed these effects, and loss of STK32C suppressed tumor progression, inducing reduced Ki-67 and c-MYC expression, and extended survival in mouse xenograft models [ ]. As for STK32B, its expression demonstrates negatively correlation with tumor invasion and metastasis in several types of cancer. Silencing of hnRNP A2/B1 negatively modulates the epithelial-mesenchymal transition process in lung cancer cells, while leading to downregulation of STK32B mRNA expression [ ]. STK32B expression is upregulated in a specialized subgroup of malignant B cell isolated from ARH-77 human malignant B-cell line characterized by high adhesiveness, low motility, and highly tumorigenic potential [ ]. Renal cancer cells elevate their STK32C expression after micro-peptide inhibiting actin cytoskeleton (MIAC) overexpression, while MIAC inhibits their migration by binding to AQP2 protein [ ].

## Epigenetic modification
There are non-mutational epigenetic reprogramming mechanisms associated with STK32 kinases in cancer. In KRAS-inhibited pancreatic cancer cells, both CpG sites at the promoters of STK32A and STK32B are hypomethylated [ ]. Lung cancer cells with KRAS overexpression present hypermethylated CpG sites at STK32C promoter [ ]. Additionally, an integrated molecular profiling of epigenomic and transcriptional alterations in osteosarcoma identifies the hypomethylation of CpG units in STK32C promoter [ ]. Given that CpG methylation is an important epigenetic modification mechanism manipulating gene expression, these observations provide clues for clarifying epigenetic mechanisms inducing aberrant expression of STK32 proteins in cancer. Furthermore, STK32B mRNA shows elevated expression in H3.3 K27M mutant diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) cells. This may attribute to the deletion of the repressive mark H3K27me3 on super-enhancers brought by H3.3 K27M mutation, which augments chromatin accessibility and potentially activates STK32B gene expression [ ].

## Potential association of STK32 with cancer stemness and tumor microenvironment (TME) components
Aside from the above identified engagements of STK32 kinases in cancer, a study with retinoblastoma Y79 cell line demonstrates that STK32B is among the top 30 expression upregulated genes in CD133low/CD44high cancer stem cells compared to CD133high cells [ ]. STK32C silencing leads to a 1.5-fold increase in the cell death rate of brain cancer stem-like cells (BCSCs) as determined by flow cytometric analysis in vitro, indicating the possible link between STK32C elevation and BCSC survival [ ]. Interestingly, reduced STK32B expression is observed in cancer-associated fibroblasts compared to non-cancer fibroblasts in gastric cancer [ ]. Although there lacks of direct evidences, these reports implicate the possible connection between the expression changes of STK32 proteins and stemness and TME regulations worthy further clarification.

## Potential mechanisms underlying STK32 downregulation in cancer
As shown in , the STK32 family proteins exhibits cancer-type specific expression. Estrogen receptor-α (ERα) belongs to nuclear receptor family transcriptional factor and functions as an indispensable transcriptional regulator in human breast cancer cells via binding to genome-wide cis-acting sites. STK32B is identified as a target of ERα by genome-wide chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) cloning [ ], in which STK32B mRNA expression is inhibited by estradiol, the ligand of ERα. This indicates the potential repression of ERα on STK32B [ ]. STK32B elevation is observed in breast cancer cells after genetic ablation of RPS6KB1, which encodes p70S6K, a downstream effector of PI3K/AKT/mTOR, and recently identified to serving as a molecule phosphorylated by STK32B at Thr389 in a glioma study [ ]. Additionally, STK32A upregulation is observed in a previous study by disrupting KRAS in human colon cancer cell line, HCT116 [ ], implicating latent repression of KRAS on it.

## Therapeutic vulnerabilities and potential cancer treatment strategies targeting STK32 kinases
The three STK32 kinases exhibit variable expression under the administration of small molecular inhibitor in cancer models of TNBC and melanoma, which implicates their potential involvement in mediating therapeutic susceptivity in cancer. For example, while assessing the adaptive response to trametinib in TNBC, the consistent reduced transcriptional response of STK32B to trametinib is observed across the four basal-like TNBC tumor patient samples [ ]. Likewise, recent studies showed the administration of MEK inhibitors regulating the expression of STK32A and STK32B in cancer, although lacking mechanistic clarification [ , ]. Six-hour exposure to the MEK inhibitor PD0325901 provokes STK32A upregulation in human melanoma cells with BRAFV600E mutation [ ]. In contrast, evidences from mouse melanoma experiments under the same BRAFV600E mutation context, STK32A expression is significantly reduced at both 1 and 12 weeks of combined treatment of BRAF inhibitor (dabrafenib) and MEK inhibitor (trametinib) [ ]. Regarding the inconsistent regulatory effects of MEK inhibitor (trametinib) on STK32A expression in BRAFV600E melanoma, it could be speculated that this discrepancy may be partly due to the differences in treatment duration. As the duration of MEK inhibitor treatment extends, STK32A may be subject to regulation by other signaling pathways, a mechanism possibly linked to the resistance to protein kinase inhibitors. Indeed, trametinib treatment of BRAFV600E mouse melanoma reduces STK32A expression more significantly at 12 weeks compared to 1 week [ ]. Similarly, STK32B expression varies with the extended treatment duration of protein kinase inhibitors. A study elucidating the resistant mechanism of chronic myeloid leukemia to the selective BCR-ABL protein tyrosine kinase inhibitor (imatinib) discloses STK32B upregulation after treatment for 12 months, instead of 6 months, in which STK32B is considered as the candidate gene mediating imatinib resistance [ ]. Moreover, as previously indicated, silencing STK32B sensitizes pancreatic cancer cells to Aurora kinase inhibitor AKI-1 [ ]. STK32B is among the top 50 genes with reduced expression induced by MP1 (Marinopyrrole/Pyrrolomycin-1) treatment, a marinopyrrole natural derivative, in Group 3 medulloblastoma cells with MYC amplification [ ], indicating the potential inhibition of MP1 on STK32B. Besides, there are researchers observing significant downregulation of STK32B in TC-71 Ewing sarcoma cells treated with the bromodomain and extra-terminal (BET) proteins specific inhibitor JQ1 [ ]. The examination of three breast cancer cohorts discloses STK32B as one of gene candidates associated with chemoresistance to taxane-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy and connected with other nodes identified by protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis [ ]. Besides, STK32B expression is negatively correlated with the enhanced sensitivity to paclitaxel and anthracycline chemotherapy in breast cancer [ ]. Similar to the observations on STK32B, STK32C has been reported to show notable upregulation in TNBC and contribute to doxorubicin resistance by modulating glycolysis in TNBC cells [ ]. These observations suggest the close association of STK32 family kinases to cancer therapy resistance, and that targeting STK32 family members may provide clues for developing cancer therapeutic strategies and improving current treatment regimen. In addition, STK32A and STK32C were latent candidates mediating radio-resistance in colorectal cancer. After treated with 5-fluorouracil and 4 Gy radiation, colorectal cancer cells exhibits STK32A promoter hypermethylation, suggesting its candidacy as a gene associated with chemoradiation resistance [ ]. Meanwhile, STK32C expression is upregulated in B7-H3 knock-down colorectal cancer cells after exposure to 4 Gy irradiation, suggesting its potential involvement in B7-H3-mediated radio-resistance of colorectal cancer [ ]. It is suggested that STK32A expression was downregulated in p53 knock-out human bronchial epithelial cells exposed to radiation, indicating the involvement of STK32A in p53-mediated DNA damage repair [ ].
It is noteworthy that the current reports on STK32 protein inhibitors remain limited, in contrast to recent efforts in developing small-molecule AGC kinase inhibitors which achieves progress and demonstrates efficacy in clinical therapy across several types of cancer [ ]. An inhibitor screening of 27 AGC kinases with commercially available small molecular compounds discloses PKC-412, a non-selective inhibitor against multiple kinase candidates, pyrazolopyrimidine (PP)-based inhibitors including PP1, PP2, 1-naphthyl PP1, and CGP57380, and p38 MAP inhibitors including SB239063 and SB203580 as STK32B inhibitor candidates [ ]. The elucidation of the structural basis for PP1 on restraining kinase activity implicates that the conserved gatekeeper residue valine at the C-terminal of β-strand 5 ( C) in the STK32 family enhances the efficacy of PP1, providing a structural perspective for the development of inhibitors specifically targeting STK32 kinases [ ].
Moreover, the recent advancement achieved in carriers for delivery of cancer therapeutics provides alternative method for the development of targeted strategy on STK32 kinases in cancer. For example, nanoparticles have been accepted for the delivery of cancer therapeutic reagents, which have significant advantages in their biocompatibilities, tumor targeting abilities and high accumulation in TME compared to conventional cancer chemotherapy strategies [ ]. Therefore, except typical approaches of developing STK32 family specific small molecule inhibitors, RNA nanoparticles like vectors delivering siRNA targeted STK32 members are also a method worth investigating for the development of potential treatment strategies targeting STK32 kinases in cancer.

## Open questions and future perspectives
As of present, compared to the well-recognized AGC family members like protein kinase B (AKT) and 3-phosphoinositide dependent protein kinase-1 (PDK1), the understanding on the functions of STK32 kinases in cancer, particularly related molecular mechanisms, remain considerably limited [ ]. The following issues require prior to solve. Firstly, the structural properties and activation mechanisms of STK32 kinases are essential for future research. This will help to clarify whether the functions of the STK32 kinases are predominantly attributable to their kinase activities or aberrant expression in cancer. Secondly, although the potential of STK32 family members as therapeutic targets in cancer treatment has been implicated, there still lacks specific strategies effectively targeting them. In the activation segment of STK32 family, the conserved glycine in the DFG motif is replaced by asparagine. This distinctive sequence might lead to disparate conformations, providing a cue for the development of inhibitors highly selective for STK32 kinases. Besides, the disparate expression among the STK32 family proteins observed in different tumor types might be attributed to tissue-specificity and intratumor heterogeneity [ ]. Thus, the upstream regulatory molecules and downstream substrates of STK32 in cancer require future investigation to decipher the context-dependent roles of STK32 proteins in the initiation and progression of cancer. In particular, STK32 proteins has been implicated as the hub mediators in neurodevelopment, and the mutation and dysregulated expression of STK32 members has been detected in neurodegenerative diseases [ ]. Considering the hijacking of normal tissue developmental programs by cancer cells for their survival and proliferation, the roles of STK32 proteins in the initiation and progression of human malignancies in central nerve system (CNS) and their associations with oncogenic pathway like WNT signaling, which play fundamental roles in the development of CNS malignant tumors, require further priority clarification [ , ]. Future exploration of STK32-mediated cancer-type specific oncogenic mechanisms may reveal additional therapeutic targets and provide potent approach for developing more effective and personalized cancer treatment strategies.
