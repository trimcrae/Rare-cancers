## Background
Xp11.2 translocation renal cell carcinoma (Xp11.2 tRCC) is a very rare and aggressiveness malignancy with poor outcome. Previous studies suggested that programmed cell death protein-1 ligand 1 (PDL1) was characterized with high mRNA and low protein in Xp11.2 tRCC, however, the potential mechanism is still blurry.

## Methods
Immunohistochemistry was conducted to verify Cyclin D1 and PDL1 expression in Xp11.2 tRCC. ChIP and dual-luciferase reporter gene assay were applied to evaluate transcriptional-regulation of TFE3 fusion proteins on CCND1/Cyclin D1 and NR1D1, we used RNA-seq to detect the regulation role of NR1D1 on CCND1/Cyclin D1, half-life experiment and autophagy flux were employed to demonstrate Cyclin D1-CDK4 speeded PDL1 degradation.

## Results
Here, we demonstrated that CCND1/Cyclin D1 was not only a direct target gene for positive regulation of TFE3 fusion proteins, but also up-regulated by nuclear receptor subfamily 1 group D member 1 (NR1D1) which was positively transcriptional regulation of TFE3 fusion proteins. Besides, TFE3 fusion proteins reduced the degradation of Cyclin D1 by activating the AKT/mTOR pathway. As a result, the high-expression of CCND1/Cyclin D1 mediated degradation of PDL1 protein through ubiquitin-proteasome system and autophagy pathway.

## Conclusion
This research found that CCND1/Cyclin D1 was upregulated in Xp11.2 tRCC through three mechanisms, high-expression CCND1/Cyclin D1 inducing PDL1 degradation. Overall, the study provided a theoretical basis for sequentially using CDK4 inhibitors and anti-PDL1 for Xp11.2 tRCC treatment.

## Introduction
Xp11.2 translocation renal cell carcinoma (Xp11.2 tRCC) was first defined as a distinctive renal cell carcinoma (RCC) entity in 2004 [ ] and then reclassified into molecularly defined renal cell carcinoma in the 2022 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of renal tumors [ ]. Xp11.2 tRCC is caused by breakage of the transcription factor enhancer 3 (TFE3) gene on the X chromosome and then balanced translocation with partner genes, such as ASPL, PRCC, SFPQ, NonO, and CLTC [ , ]. A majority of TFE3 fusions retain the DNA-binding domain of wild-type TFE3 and are involved in the regulation of cell cycle, immunity, autophagy, lysosomal biogenesis, and metabolism and are viewed as oncogenic factors [ ].
Immune checkpoint blockade through the targeting of programmed cell death protein-1 (PD1)/PD-1 ligand 1 (PDL1) has demonstrated promising antitumor effects in cancer immunotherapy of multiple solid tumors, including RCC [ ]. The expression level of PDL1 on tumor cells is positively related to the curative effect of anti-PDL1 therapy [ ]. A previous study found that TFEB, along with TFE3, which is another member of the microphthalmia-associated (MiT) transcription factor family, participates in mTOR resistance and immune escape in RCC through the transcriptional upregulation of PDL1 [ ]. However, PDL1 was characterized by high mRNA and low protein expression in Xp11.2 tRCC [ ]. Therefore, further exploration of the underlying mechanisms behind this phenomenon will provide insights into the mechanisms of cancer escape from immune surveillance and the potential response to anti-PDL1 therapy in Xp11.2 tRCC.
CCND1/Cyclin D1 initiates the cell cycle, and its expression level was high in Xp11.2 tRCC [ ]. A recent study revealed that TFE3 could enhance cell cycle and cancer progression by binding to the hTERT and CCND1 promoters [ ]; however, that study focused on wild-type TFE3. In addition to regulating cell cycle, CCND1/Cyclin D1 combines with its related kinase CDK4 could exert immune regulation role [ ]. Study demonstrates that Cyclin D1-CDK4 can phosphorylate and stabilize Speckle-Type POZ Protein (SPOP), an E3 ligase which was responsible for PDL1 degradation through ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) [ ]. In addition, Cyclin D1-CDK4 suppresses NF-κB activity and PDL1 expression by phosphorylating RB to promote tumor immune evasion [ ]. Therefore, we hypothesized that high expression of Cyclin D1 was responsible for PDL1 protein degradation in Xp11.2 tRCC.
Here, our study demonstrated that CCND1/Cyclin D1 was not only a direct target gene of TFE3 fusion proteins with positively regulated, but also was upregulated by nuclear receptor subfamily 1 group D member 1 (NR1D1), which was positively transcriptional regulation by TFE3 fusions. In addition, TFE3 fusion proteins reduced the degradation of Cyclin D1 by activating the AKT/mTOR pathway. As a result, the high expression of CCND1/Cyclin D1 mediated the degradation of PDL1 protein through UPS and autophagy pathway. Overall, the study provides a theoretical basis for sequentially using CDK4 inhibitors and anti-PDL1 for Xp11.2 tRCC treatment.

## Methods

## Cell culture
The Xp11.2 tRCC cell lines UOK109 (RRID: CVCL_B087) harboring NONO-TFE3 fusion gene and UOK120 (RRID: CVCL_B099) owning PRCC-TFE3 fusion gene were extracted from primary papillary cell carcinoma as previously described [ ], and tumors arising in a 30-and a 39-year-old male, respectively. These cell lines were gifts from Dr. W. Marston Linehan (National Cancer Institute, Bethesda, MD). HEK293T (RRID: CVCL_0063), HK-2 (RRID: CVCL_0302) and 786-O (RRID: CVCL_1051) cell lines were purchased from Type Culture Collection of Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in DMEM (Gibco, Grand Island, NY) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) in a standard humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 °C, and Myco-Zero™ Mycoplasma Removal Agent (Beyotime, C0280S) were applied to verify all experiments were performed with mycoplasma-free cells. Furthermore, strict laboratory rules were obeyed to validate the integrity and authenticity of our cell lines. In sum, we monitored morphology, growth patterns and specific marker expression to verify the cells used in this study. We were assured of the honesty of our methods and the legality of the data.

## Tissue samples
Tissue samples diagnosed as Xp11.2 tRCC with FISH probe or RNA-seq were obtained from Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital. Informed consent is signed by all study participants that their tissues will be used into scientific research.

## RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assay
RNA-easy reagent (Vazyme, R401) was applied to extract total RNA according to the product procedure. Then Reverse Transcriptase Master mixing kit (Vazyme, R201) was used to transcribe RNA into cDNA, which was quantified by qPCR using SYBR qPCR (Vazyme, Q711). 18s rRNA was chosen as internal control and Comparative 2−(ΔΔCt) method was calculated for relative expression. The primers for RNAs are shown in Table s1.

## ChIP assay
To evaluate whether NONO-TFE3 and PRCC-TFE3 bind to CCND1 and NR1D1 promoter region, ChIP assay was performed according to the protocol (Thermo Scientific, Carlsbad, CA). HEK293T cells were overexpressed NONO-TFE3 and PRCC-TFE3, respectively. Then cells were fixed, lysed and sonicated to appropriate fragments. The prepared chromatin was precipitated using specific antibodies overnight. Then, the binding complexes were thoroughly washed, eluted, purified and analyzed by qPCR. Primers designed to target those regions containing potential NONO-TFE3 and PRCC-TFE3 binding sites in CCND1 and NR1D1 promoter were supplied in Table s2.

## Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay
UOK109 and UOK120 cells were seeded in 24-well plates as well as TFE3 was knocked down. Then, cells were transfected with pGL3-Basic-CCND1 or pGL3-Basic-NR1D1 vector, respectively. pRL-TK-Renilla luciferase reporter plasmid was used as an internal control. Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Vazyme) was applied to detect the firefly luciferase and Renilla luciferase activity in each cell.

## Western blotting
Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with Protease Inhibitor Cocktail and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail I and II (MCE). Then BCA Protein Quantification Kit (Vazyme, E112) was applied to assess protein concentration. Lysates were mixed with 5 × loading buffer and boiled at 100 °C for 10 min. Denatured proteins were separated using SDS-PAGE and the PVDF membrane (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) by standard procedures. Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature and then specific primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4 °C in Tris Buffered Saline Tween (TBST). HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were incubated 1 h at room temperature and ECL solution (Vazyme) was applied to visualize the bands. Additionally, ACTB, GAPDH and Lamin B1 were regarded as reference genes. Antibodies used were included in Table s3.

## Flow cytometry
After cells were treated as indicated, flow cytometry was conducted according to the protocol. Annexin V-FITC/Propidium Iodide (PI) Apoptosis Kit (Vazyme) was applied to evaluated apoptosis rate and PI staining solution from a PI/RNase Staining Kit (BD Biosciences) was used to study the cell cycle distribution using a BD Beckman cytometer (BD Biosciences) and FlowJo software.

## CCK-8, 5-ethyny-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU), MTT and TUNEL assay
The indicated cells were seeded in the plates. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Vazyme), MTT (Solarbio) and EdU (Beyotime) was performed to assess the cell proliferation according to the manufacture’s protocol. Cells were seed into 6-well plate and then TUNEL assay was operated to assess the apoptosis rates.

## Transwell and wound healing assay
Wound Healing assay was conducted to evaluate the migration capacity after indicated treatment, in brief, cells resuspended in complete medium was seeded in 6-well plate and scratched after 6 h along with replacing the complete medium with DMEM. Transwell assay to grade the capacity of cell migration and invasion was performed. 1 × 104 cells resuspended in 200 µL DMEM was seeded into the upper chamber, with 500 µL complete medium added into the lower chamber. Cells traversed over the membrane were counted and analyzed under microscope 24 h later.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Xp11.2 tRCC FFPE tissue samples were firstly deparaffinized and then antigen retrieval, then Triton X-100 was used to punch accompanied by 3% BSA blocking for 1 h. After diluting primary antibody following the manufacture’s instruction, every slide was added 100 µL to incubate at 4 °C overnight. After incubation with HRP conjugated secondary antibody, DAB Substrate Kit, hematoxylin and 1% HCl were used according to protocol.

## Plasmid construction, small interfering RNA (siRNA) and cell transfection
OBiO Technology (China) constructed Lentivirus (TFE3-shRNA). The sequences of siRNAs validated came from Sigma-Aldrich, then transfection reagents LipoFiter 3.0 (Hanbio) or PolyPlus were used following the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were harvested 72 h after transfection.

## Immunofluorescence (IF)
Cells treatment as indicated were seeded into glass-bottom culture dishes, after a period of time, cells were fixed, permeabilized and blocked successively. Then, primary antibodies were used to conjugate the antigen overnight at 4 °C and with secondary antibodies for 1 h. Glass bottom was mounted with 4’,6‐diamidino‐2‐phenylindole (DAPI) (Beyotime, P0131). Fluorescent images were examined and photographed on a confocal microscope (Olympus FV3100).

## Nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction
Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagents Kit (Beyotime, P0027) was applied to isolate nuclear and cytoplasmic protein. All reagents must be pre-cold, after harvested cells, cytoplasmic protein extraction reagents A and B containing PMSF were used to resuspend cell pellet, then vortexed fiercely and centrifuged for 5 min at a speed of 16 000 x g to obtain a cytoplasmic protein. Nuclear protein extraction reagent was added into precipitate, after vortexed every 2 min for 30 min and centrifuged at 16 000 x g for 10 min, supernatant was nuclear protein.

## Immunoprecipitation (IP)
Cells with indicated treatment were harvested, then IP assay was conducted by using a Pierce Magnetic Co-IP Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 88804) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

## RNA-seq
After NR1D1 was knocked down, RNA was isolated, then we performed the 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequencing (PE150) on an illumina Novaseq™ 6000 (LC-Bio Technology CO., Ltd., Hangzhou, China) following the vendor’s recommended protocol. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined as fold change > 2 or fold change < 0.5 and with parametric F-test comparing nested linear models (p value < 0.05). The data is available in GEO database (GSE240242).

## Ribosome extraction
Cells were treated as indicated, then they were harvested and centrifuged at different speeds and finally got precipitate, which was the complex of ribosome with mRNA, then the latter was isolated as depicted in RNA isolation.

## Label-free quantitative proteomics
CCND1/Cyclin D1 was overexpressed in UOK109 and UOK120 and CO-IP was performed with Cyclin D1 antibody. Label-free quantitative proteomics was operated to determine the protein that was pulled down Fold change (FC) > 2 and protein expression > 1e-5 was regarded as differential proteins within group.

## Statistical analysis
SPSS 25.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) was applied to analyze statistics. For normally distributed data, it was descripted with means ± standard deviation, measurement data was expressed as median, and percentage for count data. After processed data, GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) was applied to draw the picture. Student’s t test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used to assess the significance of differences. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significance (*P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, and *** P < 0.001).

## Results

## Cyclin D1 induced high RNA and low protein expression of PDL1
Previous studies have demonstrated that PDL1 expression was low at the protein level [ ] and high at the RNA level in Xp11.2 tRCC [ ]. qPCR suggested that PDL1 was higher in UOK109 and UOK120 than that in HK-2 (Fig. A), which further confirmed the above results. However, the protein level of PDL1 showed inconsistency with RNA level (Fig. B). To ulteriorly affirm this result, 29 Xp11.2 tRCC cases were evaluated, and only 10 cases was positive for PDL1 (Table ), with positive puncta in the cytoplasm (Fig. C). Results here substantiated that PDL1 in Xp11.2 tRCC was characterized by high RNA and low protein. Wild-type TFE3 was positively related to CD274, the gene of PDL1 (Fig. S1A) and TFEB could induce transcriptional upregulation of PDL1 in ccRCC [ ]. Moreover, data from GSE167573 showed that PDL1 RNA was highly expressed in Xp11.2 tRCC (Fig. S1B); thus, it was inferred that TFE3 fusion proteins could upregulate PDL1. However, when TFE3 was knocked down, the RNA level of PDL1 was almost unaffected in UOK109 cells and slightly decreased in UOK120 cells (Fig. D), whereas the protein expression increased instead (Fig. E-F). These results suggested that TFE3 fusion proteins did not regulate PDL1 expression at the transcriptional level. In addition, factors which stabilized or destabilized PDL1 showed discordance in UOK109 and UOK120 cells (Fig. S1C), which suggested that they were not the major contributors to PDL1 degradation. More and more studies have demonstrated that Cyclin D1 is involved in PDL1 posttranslational modification [ ]. Therefore, it was speculated that Cyclin D1 might mediate PDL1 protein degradation in Xp11.2 tRCC. Subsequently, when CCND1/Cyclin D1 was interfered, PDL1 RNA was unchanged in UOK109 and UOK120 (Fig. G); however, the protein levels of PDL1 increased significantly (Fig. H-I), and parallel results were obtained when CDK4 was knocked down (Fig. S1D-F). When administration of CDK4 inhibitors LY2835219 and HY50767, comparable results verified that Cyclin D1-CDK4 mediated PDL1 degradation in Xp11.2 tRCC. Interestingly, PDL1 showed reverse trend as LY2835219 concentration increased, to be exact, PDL1 increased as the concentration of LY2835219 rising until 800 nM, however, after that, PDL1 levels decreased (Fig. S1G-L). This phenomenon was presumably attributed to the complex mechanism of LY2835219, as it was previously reported that low concentration of LY2835219 could induce cell cycle arrest, whereas high concentration led to cytotoxic effect characterized by cytoplasmic vacuoles [ ]. It was supposed to pay more attention to the dosage of LY2835219 when it was orally administration in the clinic. Surprisingly, HY50767 did not show concentration-dependent until 2000 nM (Fig. S1M-N), which was probably due to insensitive to HY50767.

 

 

## Overexpression of CCND1/Cyclin D1 was a poor prognosis factor for Xp11.2 tRCC
TFE3 is thought to be involved in cell cycle regulation [ ]. Many pathways related to the cell cycle were enriched in Xp11.2 tRCC, such as Cell Cycle and E2F Targets (Fig. S2A-D), as well as many vital molecules involved in the cell cycle were altered, such as CDKN1A, CCND1, CDK4 and CDK6 (Fig. S2E) [ , , ]. EdU and MTT assays found that cell proliferation was reduced significantly after TFE3 knockdown (Fig. S2F-G), suggesting that TFE3 fusion proteins could promote cell proliferation. Interestingly, after TFE3 knockdown, the ratio of cells in the G1 phase decreased most significantly (Fig. S2H). In addition, when CCND1/Cyclin D1 or CDK4 was knocked down, the percentage of G1 phase cells increased accordingly, as indicated by flow cytometry (Fig. S2I-J). In addition, we found that CCND1/Cyclin D1 was significantly decreased after TFE3 was knocked down among the molecules related to cell cycle regulation (Fig. A-D). GEPIA2.0 database was utilized to confirm that CCND1 was high expression in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (KIRC) and papillary renal cell carcinoma (KIRP) (Fig. S2K). Besides, in Xp11.2 tRCC cell lines, the qPCR and Western blotting results showed that the expression of CCND1/Cyclin D1 was higher than that in HEK293T (Fig. E-F). To further assess the expression level of Cyclin D1 associated with tumor biological characteristics in human Xp11.2 tRCC, we evaluated 33 human specimens from patients who underwent surgery for Xp11.2 tRCC in our hospital between January 2007 and December 2021. The expression levels of Cyclin D1 were variable (Table ), of the 33 studied patients, Cyclin D1 was positive in 24 cases (Fig. G). Furthermore, survival analysis demonstrated that Cyclin D1 was positively related to overall survival (Fig. H). These data indicated that overexpression of Cyclin D1 was a poor prognosis factor for Xp11.2 tRCC.

 

 

## TFE3 fusion proteins transcriptionally upregulated the expression of target gene CCND1/Cyclin D1 directly
To evaluate the potential mechanisms that upregulated CCND1/Cyclin D1, ChIP-seq data from Cistrome database were employed to obtain TFE3 target genes related to cell cycle. It was found that TFE3 was enriched in the promoter region of CCND1 and higher than the input (Fig.  A). To further confirm this conclusion, TFE3 was overexpressed in HEK293T cells, then ChIP-qPCR was performed, and result proved that TFE3 could directly interact with the CCND1 gene promoter (Fig.  B). These results demonstrated that CCND1 is a potential target gene of wild-type TFE3. Given that TFE3 fusion proteins are oncogenes with constitutively active transcriptional activity, we wondered whether TFE3 fusion proteins could induce the upregulation of CCND1. After we knocked down TFE3 with shRNA in UOK109 and UOK120, both mRNA and protein levels of Cyclin D1 significantly decreased (Fig.  C-D). Furthermore, ChIP assays showed that both NONO-TFE3 and PRCC-TFE3 directly interacted with the promoter of CCND1, with only one binding site in the CCND1 promoter (Fig.  E). The CCND1 promoter region was cloned and inserted into a luciferase reporter plasmid designated pGL3-Basic-CCND1 to identify the potential regulatory mechanism involved. The results showed that the luciferase activity of the shRNA (NC) was markedly higher than that of the shRNA (TFE3) (Fig.  F). These data demonstrated that NONO-TFE3 and PRCC-TFE3 transcriptionally positively regulated CCND1, and CCND1 was one of the downstream target gene of TFE3 fusion proteins.

 

## TFE3 fusion proteins upregulated CCND1/Cyclin D1 indirectly through transcriptionally upregulating NR1D1
A previous study demonstrated that NR1D1 is an upstream transcription factor of CDKN1A and CCNA2 [ ], and ChIP-seq data of UOK109 from our center showed that NR1D1 was the target gene of NONO-TFE3 [ ], which ranked first (Table ). Compared with NONO-TFE3, PRCC-TFE3 retained more domains of wild-type TFE3, thus speculating that NR1D1 was a target of PRCC-TFE3 as well. Data from GSE167573 proved that NR1D1 was high expression in tumors (Fig. S3A). Furthermore, when knocked down TFE3, NR1D1 decreased (Fig. A-C), which indicated that NR1D1 perhaps was positively transcriptional control by TFE3 fusion proteins. The NR1D1 promoter region was cloned and inserted into a luciferase reporter plasmid and results showed that the luciferase activity decreased significantly when TFE3 was knocked down (Fig. D-E). Moreover, ChIP assays demonstrated that TFE3 fusion proteins directly interacted with the promoter of NR1D1, with one binding site in the NR1D1 promoter (Fig. F). These results confirmed that NR1D1 was a target gene of TFE3 fusion proteins. To further confirm the relationship of NR1D1 with CCND1/Cyclin D1, four shRNAs for NR1D1 were designed, with the second one being the most effective (Fig. S3B), therefore, this one was selected for further assay unless otherwise noted. RNA-seq was performed after NR1D1 was knocked down. PCA indicated that there were slight differences between groups (Fig. S C and G). The DEGs were depicted in Fig. H and Fig. S3D. GSEA analysis showed that cell cycle pathway was similarly enriched (Fig. I, Fig. S3E). Furthermore, NES of cell cycle in UOK109 was −1.86 (Fig. J), similar results were obtained for UOK120 as well (Fig. S3F). Then, a volcano plot was constructed to visualize the genes enriched in cell cycle and CCND1 decreased significantly among these genes in both cell lines (Fig. K, Fig. S3G). These results proved that CCND1/Cyclin D1 mediated the role of NR1D1 in the cell cycle. To assess the role of CCND1/Cyclin D1 regulated by NR1D1, it was found that CCND1/Cyclin D1 decreased indeed after NR1D1 knockdown (Fig. A-C). When NR1D1 was knocked down, the number of cells in the G1 phase decreased (Fig. D-E), which conform to the role of CCND1/Cyclin D1 in promoting the progression of G1 phase; furthermore, proliferation declined, as indicated by the results of CCK-8 (Fig. F) and EdU assays (Fig. S4A). To further confirm these results, NR1D1 was overexpressed after TFE3 was interfered (Fig. S4B), and both MTT and EdU results suggested that cell proliferation expedited (Fig. G-H). These results demonstrated that NR1D1 could accelerate the cell cycle via the up-regulation of CCND1/Cyclin D1. Other roles of NR1D1 were also evaluated. When NR1D1 was knocked down, the ratio of Bcl2/Bax decreased, caspase 3 and Bad increased (Fig. S4C), and the apoptosis rate elevated as indicated by flow cytometry (Fig. S4D-E). Similar results were obtained by TUNEL assay (Fig. S4F). These results verified that NR1D1 counteracted apoptosis. When NR1D1 was knocked down, cells penetrability reduced significantly as manifested by Transwell assay (Fig. S4G); in addition, Wound Healing areas also lessened (Fig. S4H). Therefore, it was concluded that NR1D1 worsened Xp11.2 tRCC not only by speeding up the cell cycle through the up-regulation of CCND1/Cyclin D1, but also by antagonizing apoptosis and facilitating cell immigration.

 

 

 

## TFE3 fusion proteins reduced Cyclin D1 degradation by activating the AKT/mTOR pathway
Extracellular signals influence Cyclin D1 levels, which was a hub that integrates mitogenic signaling with the cell cycle machinery to determine cell growth or differentiation, and AKT/mTOR pathway coordinates cell growth with environmental signals [ ]. Previous studies demonstrated that AKT/mTOR pathway was activated in Xp11.2 tRCC [ , ], thus speculating that activation of this pathway was relevant to Cyclin D1. AKT/mTOR and mTORC1 pathways were enriched in Xp11.2 tRCC (Fig. S5A-B). After TFE3 knockdown, P-AKT (Ser 473) decreased, indicating that AKT was activated by TFE3 fusion proteins, as did P-S6 (Ser 240/244) (Fig. A-B), the activation index of mTORC1. In addition, it was also speculated that mTORC1 was activated not merely through AKT (Fig. S5B). Since the small Rag GTPases RRAGA, RRAGB, RRAGC and RRAGD were vital nutrition sensors for mTORC1 activation [ ], when TFE3 was knocked down, RRAGD RNA was downregulated the most significantly (Fig. C-D), and RRAGD protein was also downregulated (Fig. E-F). These results verified that TFE3 fusions upregulated RRAGD. To further confirm mTORC1 activated by RRAGD, it was found that mTOR aggregated in the nucleus, and TFE3 knockdown showed no effect on the subcellular location of mTOR in UOK109 (Fig. S5C), while in UOK120, mTOR accumulated in the cytoplasm, and TFE3 knockdown led to mTOR aggregating in the nucleus (Fig. S5D). Similar results were obtained by co-localization analysis (Fig. G). Thus, in UOK109, TFE3 fusion activated mTORC1 through the upregulation of RRAGD, whereas in UOK120, mTORC1 activation was mainly due to physical distance changing with RRAGD. These results firmly confirmed that AKT/mTOR pathway was activated by TFE3 fusions. GSK3β is a serine/threonine kinase that was involved in cell proliferation, cell cycle and many other cellular processes. GSK3β, which can lead to Cyclin D1 degradation, was previously reported to be inactivated by AKT through phosphorylation [ ]. After TFE3 was knocked down, the inactivation form of GSK3β, P-GSK3β (Ser 9) reduced (Fig. H-I), and when the GSK3β inhibitor SB216763 was used, Cyclin D1 increased significantly (Fig. J-K). When treated with AKT inhibitor MK2206, Cyclin D1 decreased accordingly (Fig. L-M). These results suggested that after being activated by TFE3 fusions, AKT can inactivate GSK3β by phosphorylating it at Ser 9, which reduced Cyclin D1 degradation.

 

## High-expression Cyclin D1 promoted nuclear export of PRCC-TFE3 protein but not NONO-TFE3
A previous study demonstrated that the deposition of TFE3 fusion proteins in the nucleus is an important prerequisite for their function [ ]. It has been demonstrated that there was a nuclear export signal (NES) in exon 4 of wild-type TFE3, which is regulated by the Cyclin D1-CDK4 complex [ ]. The PRCC-TFE3 fusion gene was formed by translocation of the TFE3 exon (4–10) and the PRCC exon 1 in UOK120. In UOK109, the TFE3 exon (6–10) and the NONO exon (1–9) generated NONO-TFE3 fusion transcript [ ]. Thus, the NES of TFE3 fusion regulated by Cyclin D1-CDK4 was theoretically retained in UOK120. When treated with the Cyclin D1-CDK4 inhibitor LY2835219, the subcellular localization of NONO-TFE3 fusion in UOK109 was not changed, whereas PRCC-TFE3 in UOK120 shifted to the cytoplasm (Fig. A-B). These findings could explain why NONO-TFE3 accumulated in the nucleus more than PRCC-TFE3 did. After extracting nuclear and cytoplasmic protein when CCND1/Cyclin D1 was knocked down, TFE3 fusion protein in UOK109 was not influenced, while the amount of PRCC-TFE3 that migrated to the cytoplasm decreased (Fig. C-D). Analogous results were obtained when CDK4 was knockdown (Fig. S6A-B). These results suggested that high-expression of Cyclin D1 promoted PRCC-TFE3 shift to cytoplasm, while exhibited no effect on NONO-TFE3 subcellular localization. These findings were further confirmed, by transfecting plasmids with overexpressed TFE3 exon 2–10, exon 3–10, exon 4–10, exon 5–10, and exon 6–10 into HEK293T respectively. Both the exon 5–10 and exon 6–10 segments of TFE3 were not affected by LY2835219, as indicated by immunofluorescence (Fig. E) and Western blotting (Fig. S6C). TFEB can bind TFE3 to form a heterodimer. Results showed that the ability of Cyclin D1-CDK4 to promote TFEB nuclear export was similar in UOK109 and UOK120 after CCND1/Cyclin D1 or CDK4 was knocked down (Fig. F). These findings suggested that Cyclin D1 could promote the nuclear export of PRCC-TFE3 protein and TFEB, but not NONO-TFE3 protein.

 

## High-expression Cyclin D1 mediated PDL1 degradation through UPS and autophagy pathways
The UPS and autophagy pathways are involved in protein degradation. Preceding investigations suggested that PDL1 can be degraded through the ubiquitin pathway [ , ]. Firstly, following the administration of cycloheximide, an inhibitor of protein synthesis, PDL1 degradation was prolonged after the Cyclin D1-CDK4 complex was inhibited by LY2835219 (Fig. A-B), indicating that Cyclin D1-CDK4 facilitated the degradation of PDL1. Moreover, when the proteasome inhibitor MG132 was employed, the PDL1 was upregulated after CCND1/Cyclin D1 knockdown in vitro (Fig. C-D), and equal results were obtained when CDK4 was knocked down (Fig. S7A-B). Taken together, these data demonstrated that Cyclin D1-CDK4 complex advocated PDL1 degradation over the UPS pathway. Additionally, earlier research established that PDL1 degradation also occurs via the autophagy pathway [ ]. Co-localization analysis was operated to examine the distribution of PDL1 and the lysosome marker LAMP2, which illustrated more co-localization of PDL1 and LAMP2 in UOK109 and UOK120 than 786-O (Fig. S7C). What’s more, when LY2835219 was applied, co-localization was significantly diminished, attending by the reduction in the number of LAMP1-positive foci (Fig. E). These events exhibited that LY2835219 may shorten PDL1 degradation by leveraging lysosomes. Subsequently, when CCND1/Cyclin D1 or CDK4 was knocked down, the pH value of lysosome increased as indicated by the LysoSensor probe (Fig. F). Furthermore, GFP-LC3 was transfected to monitor the autophagy flux. When LY2835219 was administrated, the number of green fluorescence puncta decreased (Fig. G), indicating that autophagy flux was blocked. It was concluded from these consequences that Cyclin D1-CDK4 impacted autophagy flux by regulating lysosome function. When CCND1/Cyclin D1 was impeded, the ratio of LC3 II/I decreased, and PDL1 increased when the cells were treated with autophagy flux inhibitor Bafilomycin A1 (Baf A1) or 3-Methyladenine (3-MA) (Fig. H-K); and identical results were gathered when CDK4 was disrupted (Fig. S7D-G). All these validated that Cyclin D1-CDK4 stimulated PDL1 degradation through the autophagy pathway by enhancing lysosome function. p62 is an adaptor protein of many autophagy substrates [ , ]; interestingly, p62 scaled down when autophagy as well as Cyclin D1-CDK4 was inhibited, indicating that it was not the adaptor protein of PDL1 in Xp11.2 tRCC. We further investigated the mechanism by which Cyclin D1-CDK4 upregulated p62 by aggrandizing the amount of SQSTM1/p62 bound to the ribosome and thus speeding translation in Xp11.2 tRCC (Fig. S8, Tables and ). These findings proved that high-expression Cyclin D1 mediated PDL1 degradation through the UPS and autophagy pathways.

 

 

 

## Discussion
The expression of PDL1 in tumor cell cytomembrane was positively correlated with anti-PDL1 therapy. PDL1 expression in Xp11.2 tRCC was high at the mRNA level and low at the protein level, except for the MED15-TFE3 subtype [ , ], Cyclin D1 is related to PDL1 post-translational modification [ , ]; therefore, whether Cyclin D1 is involved in PDL1 protein degradation in Xp11.2 tRCC remains to be clarified. In the present study, it was demonstrated that TFE3 fusion proteins mediated PDL1 protein degradation by upregulating CCND1/Cyclin D1. Mechanistically, TFE3 fusion proteins mediate CCND1/Cyclin D1 upregulation in three ways, firstly positively transcriptional regulation, secondly, transcriptionally upregulating NR1D1, and thirdly, activation of AKT/mTOR pathway which decreases the degradation of CCND1/Cyclin D1. Overexpression of CCND1/Cyclin D1 promoted PDL1 protein degradation through the UPS and autophagy pathways in Xp11.2 tRCC.
CCND1 is a well-established oncogene that is commonly overexpressed and reinforces several potentially oncogenic responses, such as cell proliferation, growth, angiogenesis, and resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy [ ]. Substantial evidence has confirmed the involvement of CCND1/Cyclin D1 amplification and overexpression in breast cancer, lung cancer, melanoma, and oral squamous cell carcinoma, which are relevant to increased metastasis and poorer outcome [ ]. Cyclin D1 governs the cell cycle transition from G1 to S phase by cooperating with its regulatory partner CDK4/6. To this end, three CDK4/6 inhibitors, palbociclib, ribociclib, and abemaciclib, have been recently approved by the FDA for breast cancer treatment with high performance [ ]. A previous study finds that in alveolar soft part sarcoma, a rare mesenchymal malignancy driven by the ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion, CCND1/Cyclin D1 is the target gene of ASPSCR1::TFE3 fusion [ ]. The results of our study indicated that TFE3 fusions mediated Cyclin D1 high-expression through two patterns, one was positively transcriptional control, and another inhibited degradation. For this purpose, CDK4/6 inhibitors may be tentatively used in Xp11.2 tRCC, which temporarily lacks effective treatments.
Findings from Choueiri et al. [ ] demonstrated that PDL1 protein-positive rate was 3/10 (30%) in Xp11.2 tRCC, albeit indeed higher than those in other non-clear renal cell carcinoma subtypes. In our study, the positive rate of PDL1 protein was 10/29 (34.48%), approximately to the above research, which was relatively low. The PDL1 expression level is influenced by many factors, such as gene mutation, transcription regulation and post-translational modification [ ]. Gene amplification, chromosomal translocation and structural variation in the 3’ UTR region promote PDL1 expression [ ]. Under hypoxia, HIF-1α transcriptionally upregulates PDL1 on the surface of macrophages, dendritic cells, and tumor cells, making the combination of anti-PDL1 and HIF-1α inhibition a new immunotherapeutic target for cancer [ ]. There are four glycosylation sites in the amino terminus of PDL1 and glycosylation is conducive to the stability of PDL1 and the binding of PD-1 [ ]. Ubiquitination also plays an important role in the post-translational regulation of PDL1. CDK1 can phosphorylate and stabilize SPOP ubiquitin ligase, which results in PDL1 degradation, and ATR inhibitors can inhibit CDK1; therefore, the combination of ATR inhibitors and anti-PDL1 therapy is suitable for treating tumors with DNA damage repair defects, such as prostate cancer [ ]. In our study, PDL1 was degraded by CCND1/Cyclin D1 in virtue of both the UPS and autophagy pathways in Xp11.2 tRCC; thus, the application of anti-PDL1 therapy alone has certain limitations. Researches have proved that CDK4/6 inhibitors can enhance T-cell activation and upregulate PDL1 on the tumor cell surface [ ], and the combination administration of CDK4/6 inhibitors with anti-PD-1/PDL1 had entered phase II clinical trials for many tumors, such as breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, and prostate cancer [ ], which indicates that sequential administrated CDK4/6 inhibitors and anti-PDL1 was a potential therapy strategy for Xp11.2 tRCC.
AKT/mTOR kinases pathway functions in various aspects of cellular processes that play extreme roles in essential parts of cells, including proliferation, transcription, translation, survival, and growth in normal physiological circumstances and various pathological disorders, including cancer [ ]. AKT activation is mainly controlled by PI3K, which responds to extracellular stimuli, such as insulin, IGF-1, FGF, and EGF [ ]. However, the activation of mTOR is pretty complex. Various growth factors and their corresponding receptors function as positive growth regulators and convey signals to mTOR via PI3K/AKT. In addition, mTORC1 is also activated through small GTPases sensing nutrient availability, which recruits mTORC1 to the lysosome and then is activated by RHEB proteins [ – ]. In this study, AKT and mTOR were similarly activated by TFE3 fusions, in which the activation of AKT was probably a consequence of PI3K. TFE3 fusion activated mTORC1 through the upregulation of RRAGD in UOK109, and mTORC1 activation was mainly due to changes in the physical distance of mTOR with RRAGD in UOK120. These results imply that targeting the AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in Xp11.2 tRCC presumably is a novel therapeutic strategy.

## Conclusions
Overall, our research indicated that CCND1/Cyclin D1 overexpression mediated by TFE3 fusions in Xp11.2 tRCC promoted PDL1 protein degradation. Therefore, sequential administrated CDK4/6 inhibitors and anti-PDL1 was a potential therapy method for Xp11.2 tRCC, which currently is lack of postoperative medicine.
