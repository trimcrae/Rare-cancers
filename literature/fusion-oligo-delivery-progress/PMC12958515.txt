## Abstract

## Background
Rhabdomyosarcoma, the most common pediatric soft tissue sarcoma, remains a therapeutic challenge due to its aggressive nature and poor outcomes in high-risk patients. Herein, we explore the potential of CAR-T cell therapy as a transformative approach for rhabdomyosarcoma, focusing on target antigens such as HER2, CD276, FGFR4, PDGFR-α, and others.

## Main body
While preclinical studies are promising, antitumor activity in clinical studies to date has been limited. Significant clinical hurdles include antigen heterogeneity, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, and logistical barriers in manufacturing. Innovative strategies like dual-targeting CARs, combinatorial checkpoint blockade, and allogeneic platforms are discussed to address these limitations. Additionally, we highlight emerging modalities such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, oncolytic viruses, and small molecule inhibitors that may synergize with CAR-T cell therapy.

## Conclusions
By integrating these advances, rationally designed multimodal therapies could overcome resistance mechanisms and improve outcomes for refractory rhabdomyosarcoma, offering hope for a historically incurable malignancy.

## Introduction
Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy has revolutionized oncology, achieving remarkable success in relapsed/refractory B-cell malignancies. United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved products targeting CD19 (e.g., tisagenlecleucel, axicabtagene ciloleucel) induce durable remissions in B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) and lymphomas [ , ]. Beyond hematologic cancers, early-phase trials demonstrate CAR-T cell efficacy against solid tumors. For example, proof-of-concept in pediatric solid tumors is highlighted by GD2-CAR-T cell therapy (GD2-CART01), which achieved a 63% overall response rate and encouraging 3-year overall survival (60%) in children with relapsed/refractory high-risk neuroblastoma, demonstrating feasibility and sustained antitumor activity [ ]. These advances underscore CAR-T cell therapy’s potential to overcome conventional treatment barriers, warranting exploration in other aggressive malignancies.
Fundamentally, CARs are synthetic receptors that redirect T-cell cytotoxicity toward predefined tumor antigens. Their design hinges on two critical biological components: [ ] An extracellular antigen-binding domain—typically derived from fully human, humanized, or animal-derived monoclonal antibodies as single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) or nanobodies—confers target specificity by recognizing surface markers unique to cancer cells [ – ]; [ ] Intracellular signaling domains (CD3ζ with co-stimulatory modules like CD28 or 4-1BB) activate T-cell effector functions upon antigen engagement [ – ]. This architecture genetically fuses the precision of antibody-based targeting with the potent cytolytic machinery of T lymphocytes, enabling direct tumor lysis and immunomodulatory cytokine release. For solid tumors, ongoing engineering optimizes these elements—refining scFv affinity to mitigate off-tumor toxicity, leveraging nanobodies for compact epitope access, and tailoring co-stimulation to resist exhaustion—all while preserving the core mechanism: reprogramming patient-derived or allogeneic T cells into living therapeutics that seek and destroy antigen-expressing malignancies [ – ].
Rhabdomyosarcoma, the most common pediatric soft tissue sarcoma, presents urgent unmet needs. Despite intensive multimodal regimens, high-risk patients—particularly those with metastatic or fusion-driven disease—face limited salvage options and poor prognoses [ , ]. Rhabdomyosarcoma pathogenesis involves dysregulation of developmental myogenic pathways and oncogenic drivers, fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) that impedes immune surveillance [ , ]. Anatomical constraints often preclude curative resection, while chemotherapy and radiotherapy cause severe long-term toxicities [ ]. Critically, relapse rates remain high due to chemoresistance and dynamic immune evasion mechanisms [ , , ]. These challenges position rhabdomyosarcoma as a compelling candidate for CAR-T cell immunotherapy, leveraging lessons from hematologic and solid tumor applications.
This review discusses progress in CAR-T cell engineering for rhabdomyosarcoma, focusing on antigen selection, structural innovations, and combinatorial strategies to overcome solid tumor barriers. We explore preclinical/clinical evidence of CAR-T cell efficacy while addressing hurdles like antigen heterogeneity and TME immunosuppression. Beyond CAR-T cells, synergistic modalities—immune checkpoint inhibitors, oncolytic viruses, and small molecule adjuvants—are examined for their potential to augment response durability. By integrating these advances, rationally designed multimodal therapies could extend the curative promise of cellular immunotherapy to rhabdomyosarcoma, offering new hope for pediatric and adolescent patients.

## Rhabdomyosarcoma definition, treatment, and challenges
Rhabdomyosarcoma is an aggressive malignancy arising from primitive mesenchymal cells committed to skeletal muscle differentiation, representing the most common soft tissue sarcoma in children and adolescents [ , ]. Histologically classified by the World Health Organization into four subtypes—embryonal (ERMS), alveolar (ARMS), pleomorphic, and spindle cell/sclerosing—rhabdomyosarcoma exhibits distinct molecular and clinical behaviors [ ]. ERMS, accounting for ~ 60–70% of cases, typically affects younger children (0–10 years) and favors head/neck, genitourinary, and orbital regions [ ]. In contrast, ARMS (~ 20–30%) presents in adolescents and young adults, frequently involving extremities, trunk, and parameningeal sites, and is defined by pathognomonic PAX3/7::FOXO1 fusion genes that drive aberrant transcriptional programs and correlate with metastatic potential [ ]. Pleomorphic and spindle cell subtypes are rare and predominantly occur in adults.
Rhabdomyosarcoma pathogenesis involves dysregulation of developmental myogenic pathways, with key oncogenic drivers [ – ]. Notably, ARMS is typically driven by PAX3::FOXO1 or PAX7::FOXO1 gene fusions, which act as potent oncogenic transcription factors that reprogram enhancer landscapes and activate distinct downstream programs [ , ], whereas ERMS is usually fusion-negative and more commonly characterized by RAS-pathway and other somatic mutations; these molecular differences underlie divergent clinical behavior and therapeutic vulnerabilities [ ]. Despite multimodal therapy, outcomes remain dismal for high-risk groups: metastatic disease at diagnosis confers < 30% 5-year survival, while fusion-positive ARMS has a 4-year event-free survival of ~ 50% [ , ].
Current rhabdomyosarcoma management follows risk-stratified protocols (Children’s Oncology Group or European paediatric soft tissue sarcoma Study Group) integrating surgery, multi-agent chemotherapy, and radiotherapy [ , ]. Localized disease typically receives neoadjuvant chemotherapy (VAC regimen: vincristine, actinomycin D, cyclophosphamide) to facilitate organ-sparing resection, followed by adjuvant chemo-radiation [ ]. High-risk patients (fusion-positive ARMS, metastatic ERMS/ARMS) receive intensified regimens (e.g., VAC/VI: adding irinotecan) with dose-compressed cycles [ ]. Despite these efforts, significant challenges persist. First, surgical resection is often incomplete or mutilating in anatomically complex sites (such as orbit or bladder). Second, chemotherapy induces dose-limiting toxicities; vincristine causes neurotoxicity in patients, cyclophosphamide associates with infertility and secondary malignancies, and anthracyclines (added for advanced disease) carry cumulative cardiotoxicity [ – ]. Radiotherapy, while improving local control, causes growth impairment, organ dysfunction, and secondary cancers in developing tissues [ , ]. Critically, a high number of metastatic patients experience relapse within 18 months—a scenario with median survival under 12 months due to chemoresistance [ – ]. Salvage options yield low response rates, highlighting the therapeutic ceiling of conventional approaches [ – ].
The biological complexity of rhabdomyosarcoma underpins its clinical challenges. Metastasis, present in 15–25% of patients at diagnosis, involves dynamic interactions between tumor cells and the microenvironment. αvβ3 integrin-mediated invasion and CXCR4/SDF-1 chemotaxis enable hematogenous spread to bone marrow, lungs, and lymph nodes [ – ]. The immunosuppressive TME further impedes therapy, featuring M2-polarized macrophages and deficient antigen presentation [ , ]. Rhabdomyosarcoma cells evade immune surveillance through checkpoint upregulation (B7-H3/CD276) as well [ – ]. Additionally, target antigen heterogeneity—variable expression of HER2, GD2, or FGFR4 across tumors and metastatic niches—facilitates immune escape. These factors collectively explain the failure of historical immunotherapies (e.g., IL-2, anti-GD2 antibodies). Novel strategies, even though not explored in the context of rhabdomyosarcoma yet, must overcome physical barriers (e.g., collagen-rich stroma impeding T-cell infiltration), metabolic constraints (hypoxia, nutrient depletion), and adaptive resistance mechanisms (antigen loss, survivin upregulation) [ , ]. Addressing these challenges requires rationally engineered cellular therapies capable of dual antigen targeting, TME remodeling, and sustained persistence.

## Limitations of CAR-T cell therapy of rhabdomyosarcoma

## Target antigen selection
Target antigen selection in rhabdomyosarcoma immunotherapy is fundamentally shaped by the trade-offs between tumor-specific antigens and pan-cancer antigens. Tumor-specific antigens offer theoretical advantages of restricted expression, minimizing on-target/off-tumor toxicity risks; however, their applicability is often limited to molecular subsets (e.g., fusion-positive ARMS) and may exclude embryonal or fusion-negative cases [ ]. Conversely, pan-cancer antigens provide broader applicability across histologic subtypes but inherently carry greater risks of expression in vital tissues, highlighting the importance of meticulous safety profiling.
The clinical translation of CAR-T cell therapy in rhabdomyosarcoma faces multifaceted biological constraints rooted in this dichotomy. Unlike hematologic malignancies with conserved lineage markers (e.g., CD19), rhabdomyosarcoma exhibits pronounced heterogeneity [ , ]. Critical antigens like HER2, GD2, or fibroblast growth factor receptor 4 (FGFR4) display variable density across patients and metastatic sites, enabling immune escape through antigen-loss variants [ – ]. This plasticity is further exacerbated by the dynamic modulation of target expression under therapeutic pressure. Furthermore, a number of targetable antigens (primarily pan-cancer candidates) might exhibit expression in normal tissues (e.g., HER2 in cardiomyocytes, CD276 in epithelial cells), possibly risking severe toxicities such as cardiomyopathy or neurotoxicity [ , ]. The absence of truly universal, rhabdomyosarcoma-exclusive antigens underscores the reliance on pan-cancer targets, proposing stringent patient stratification [ – ]. Even when targets are present, suboptimal antigen density—common in solid tumors but not specifically investigated in rhabdomyosarcoma—compromises immunological synapse formation and CAR-T cell efficacy [ – ]. These collective limitations motivate the exploration of innovative strategies like dual-targeting CARs or combinatorial antigen modulation to mitigate escape, irrespective of antigen classification.

## Immunosuppressive TME
The immunosuppressive TME imposes equally critical barriers. Rhabdomyosarcoma tumors are characterized by dense collagenous stroma (namely, COL4A1/COL18A1-rich matrices; as reported by Lian and colleagues by assessing the expression of seven different collagens, fibrillins, and collagen-modifying proteins in 48 murine and 4 human sarcomas) that might physically impede CAR-T cell infiltration [ ]. Beyond impaired infiltration, inadequate trafficking and heterogeneous intratumoral localization prevent engineered immune cells from accessing microscopic tumor foci, producing perivascular sequestration and antigen-protected niches that evade immune clearance; addressing this challenge may require strategies to enhance homing and penetration (for example, chemokine-receptor engineering, extracellular matrix-modifying enzymes, combination with radiotherapy, or regional delivery) [ , ]. Hypoxic niches within the TME might further impair T-cell metabolism, shifting toward immunosuppressive adenosine accumulation and PD-L1 upregulation [ , ]. Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), amplified by tumor-derived G-CSF, secrete arginase and reactive oxygen species that disrupt CAR-T cell proliferation and cytotoxicity [ , ]. This immunosuppressive milieu not only limits initial CAR-T cell trafficking but also induces early exhaustion phenotypes, characterized by elevated PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3 [ ]. Crucially, metabolic competition—where tumor cells outcompete T cells for glucose and tryptophan via Warburg effect and IDO1 activity—starves infiltrating CAR-T cells of essential nutrients, uncoupling effector functions from antigen recognition [ – ]. While some of these mechanisms might be general to solid tumors and not specifically rhabdomyosarcoma, they might constitute the immunosuppressive nature of the rhabdomyosarcoma TME.

## Cell-intrinsic and logistical challenges
Cell-intrinsic and logistical challenges further constrain clinical implementation. CAR-T cell products derived from heavily pretreated cancer patients often exhibit in vitro dysfunction due to chemotherapy-induced lymphopenia and telomere erosion, driving exploration of allogeneic or cytokine-induced killer (CIK)-cell approaches that risk graft-versus-host disease [ , ]. Even with successful manufacturing, CAR-T cell persistence in solid tumors rarely exceeds weeks—contrasting with years in leukemia—due to tonic signaling-induced exhaustion and hostile TME conditions [ , ]. Toxicities unique to solid tumors but not specifically rhabdomyosarcoma, including cytokine release syndrome (CRS) from macrophage activation and on-target off-tumor effects, require aggressive immunosuppression that further compromises efficacy. Logistically, centralized CAR-T cell production incurs delays (3–8 weeks) incompatible with rapidly progressive rhabdomyosarcoma, while costs exceeding $500,000 limit accessibility globally [ , ]. Pediatric-specific challenges include thymic output limitations affecting naive T-cell pools and long-term sequelae of lymphodepletion in developing organs [ , ]. Addressing these multifaceted hurdles requires advances in allogeneic platforms, exhaustion-resistant CAR designs, and decentralized manufacturing to transform CAR-T cell therapy from a bespoke intervention into a scalable, curative modality for pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma, or other solid tumors in general.

## CAR-T cell therapy of rhabdomyosarcoma
CAR-T cell therapy for rhabdomyosarcoma has focused on a range of target antigens, with varying levels of preclinical and clinical investigation. Below, we summarize key targets and their current developmental status (Table  ).
 

## HER2
While human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/ERBB2) gene amplification is rare in rhabdomyosarcoma, its aberrant overexpression is observed in a subset of tumors, particularly alveolar and embryonal subtypes [ , ]. This overexpression is implicated in driving oncogenic signaling; in other HER2-dysregulated cancers, such activation triggers downstream PI3K/AKT and MAPK cascades that promote proliferation, metastasis, and therapy resistance [ ]. Although detailed mechanistic studies in RMS are ongoing, this established biology positions HER2 as a rational immunotherapeutic target for high-risk rhabdomyosarcoma. Hegde et al. reported a notable exceptional response providing proof-of-concept for HER2-redirected CAR-T cell efficacy in a single pediatric patient with refractory bone marrow-restricted metastatic rhabdomyosarcoma (from the multi-tumor basket trial NCT00902044) [ , ]. In this case, autologous HER2-redirected CAR-T cells administered after lymphodepleting chemotherapy induced a complete remission, consolidated through seven total infusions (Fig. ) [ , ]. Serial immune analyses documented dynamic restructuring of the T-cell receptor landscape, characterized by clonal expansions and de novo autoantibodies targeting oncogenic pathway antigens [ , ]. After transient relapse at six months, a second remission was achieved using HER2-redirected CAR-T cells plus pembrolizumab, with sustained disease-free survival exceeding 20 months at the time of reporting [ , ]. This case highlights a potential application for CAR-T cell therapy in a specific metastatic context but underscores the need for broader validation in rhabdomyosarcoma. Further exploring HER2-redirected therapies, Merker et al. proposed an innovative preclinical strategy to address the poor prognosis of high-risk rhabdomyosarcoma in adolescents/young adults, using ERBB2 (HER2)-targeted CIK cells as a potential alternative to conventional CAR-T cell therapy [ ]. Recognizing limitations of patient-derived T cells in solid tumors, they leveraged CIK cells—haploidentical, minimally alloreactive T/NK hybrids from healthy donors—genetically modified to express HER2-specific CARs [ ]. In disseminated rhabdomyosarcoma xenograft models, untreated mice exhibited tumor engraftment within 13–35 days and uniform mortality (median survival 62 days). Early intervention with wild-type (WT) CIK cells delayed/eliminated tumors in 67% (4/6) of mice, while HER2-redirected CAR CIK cells achieved 100% (8/8) initial tumor control and superior infiltration at metastatic sites [ ]. During advanced disease, both WT and CAR-CIK delayed progression but lacked curative efficacy. Critically, HER2-redirected CAR CIK cells uniquely stimulated innate immunity, recruiting NK/T-NK cells to tumor sites [ ]. This study positions HER2-redirected CAR CIK cells as a promising “off-the-shelf” therapeutic alternative for high-risk rhabdomyosarcoma, synergizing CAR specificity with CIK cells’ innate cytotoxicity and haploidentical scalability [ ]. Collectively, these preclinical and clinical findings validate HER2 as a therapeutically actionable target in high-risk rhabdomyosarcoma, with engineered CIK cells offering an “off-the-shelf” alternative to conventional CAR-T cell approaches [ ]. The durable remission achieved in refractory metastatic disease (NCT00902044), particularly through CAR-T cell/pembrolizumab synergy after relapse, might underscore the potential of HER2-redirected immunotherapy to transform outcomes for historically incurable rhabdomyosarcoma patients [ , ].
 

## Platelet-derived growth factor receptor α
Platelet-derived growth factor receptor α (PDGFR-α) has emerged as a significant contributor to rhabdomyosarcoma tumor pathogenesis and malignant progression [ , ]. A study by Taniguchi and colleagues in alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma has shown that PDGFR-α is highly activated in both primary and metastatic tumors, and its inhibition significantly suppresses tumor growth in vitro and in vivo, establishing it as a potential therapeutic target [ ]. However, the clinical translation of PDGFR-α-redirected therapies, including CAR-T cells, is complicated by its widespread and critical physiologic expression. PDGFR-α signaling is fundamental for the proliferation, migration, and survival of various mesenchymal cell types [ – ]. Its activation is a key driver in vital processes such as organogenesis during embryonic development, wound healing through fibroblast recruitment and activation, and angiogenesis via pericyte stabilization of blood vessels [ – ]. Consequently, targeting PDGFR-α carries a significant risk for on-target/off-tumor toxicities. Potential adverse effects might include impaired tissue repair, vascular dysfunction, and damage to PDGFR-α-expressing stromal cells in vital organs such as the lungs, heart, and skin.
In a pivotal investigation, Xiao et al. developed second-generation PDGFR-α-specific CAR-T cells incorporating an scFv targeting domain and evaluated their antitumor activity [ ]. These engineered T cells exhibited antigen-dependent proliferation when co-cultured with the RD rhabdomyosarcoma cell line, which expresses PDGFR-α in over 95% of cells, unlike non-transduced T cells targeting RD cells or PDGFR-α-redirected CAR-T cells exposed to PDGFR-α-low RH30 cells [ ]. Furthermore, the CAR-T cells demonstrated potent, dose-dependent cytotoxicity against RD cells across multiple effector-to-target ratios concomitant with secretion of proinflammatory cytokines including IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2, and GM-CSF [ ]. Importantly, in vivo studies using subcutaneous RD xenograft models substantiated these findings, as PDGFR-α-redirected CAR-T cells significantly suppressed tumor growth and prolonged survival compared to control cohorts [ ]. To our knowledge, this represents the first demonstration of antitumor efficacy for PDGFR-α-redirected CAR-T cell therapy against rhabdomyosarcoma.
While these preclinical results are promising, further rigorous preclinical validation is imperative to define the therapeutic window between antitumor efficacy and on-target toxicity. This must include extensive biodistribution studies in immunocompetent models and detailed toxicological assessments to identify potential target organs, and clinical studies are imperative before establishing the clinical applicability of PDGFR-α as a CAR-T cell target for this malignancy. Strategies to mitigate risk, such as tuning CAR affinity, implementing safety switches, or developing logic-gated CAR systems that require a second tumor-specific antigen, may be essential for the safe clinical development of this approach.

## FGFR4 and CD276 (B7-H3)
FGFR4 is a key driver of rhabdomyosarcoma pathogenesis, where genomic alterations—including activating mutations, gene amplifications, and overexpression—dysregulate downstream MAPK and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling cascades, promoting tumor cell survival, proliferation, and metastasis [ – ]. In preclinical validation of FGFR4 targeting, Sullivan et al. demonstrated that while FGFR4-specific CAR-T cells initially failed to control orthotopic RH30 rhabdomyosarcoma xenografts in NSG mice due to therapy-induced collagen-rich stroma and myeloid immunosuppression, efficacy was achieved through two critical refinements: (1) optimized scFv binders targeting FGFR4’s membrane-proximal domain, and (2) combinatorial “anti-myeloid polypharmacy” disrupting immunosuppressive pathways (CSF1R, IDO1, iNOS, TGFβ, PD-L1, MIF) [ ]. This dual strategy reversed microenvironmental resistance, enabling complete tumor clearance and establishing that FGFR4-redirected CAR-T cell efficacy requires concurrent stromal modulation to overcome intrinsic immunosuppression in rhabdomyosarcoma [ ]. Complementarily, Alijaj et al. isolated high-affinity (picomolar-nanomolar) FGFR4-specific single-domain antibodies (sdAbs) from phage libraries that effectively blocked FGF19-induced MAPK signaling and enabled three distinct therapeutic approaches, including targeted disruption of FGFR4 oncogenic signaling, receptor-specific delivery of vincristine via decorated liposomes internalized by rhabdomyosarcoma cells, and generation of FGFR4-redirected CAR-T cells exhibiting potent antigen-specific cytotoxicity against rhabdomyosarcoma models [ ]. Tian et al. developed FGFR4-redirected CAR-T cells using the 3A11 murine mAb, which demonstrated robust antigen-specific cytokine production and cytotoxicity against rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines in vitro while sparing healthy human primary cells, confirming tumor selectivity based on FGFR4 overexpression [ ]. These engineered T cells persisted effectively in vivo and mediated significant tumor elimination across multiple metastatic and orthotopic rhabdomyosarcoma models, establishing the preclinical rationale for clinical translation in relapsed/refractory pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma [ ].
CD276 (B7-H3), an immune checkpoint molecule of the B7 superfamily, and FGFR4, a receptor tyrosine kinase, represent two distinct yet therapeutically significant targets in rhabdomyosarcoma [ , ]. CD276 is broadly overexpressed in rhabdomyosarcoma, as well as various other pediatric solid tumors, and correlates with aggressive disease, poor prognosis, and immune evasion through its modulation of T-cell function within the TME [ , , , ]. The tumor-restricted overexpression of CD276 and the oncogenic dependency of rhabdomyosarcoma on aberrant FGFR4 signaling (induced by the oncogenic mutant form) collectively position these antigens as promising candidates for dual-targeting immunotherapies, including CAR-T cell approaches. In this regard, Timpanaro et al. systematically optimized CD276- and FGFR4-specific CAR constructs, identifying CD276.V-CAR-T cells (276.MG.CD28HD/TM.CD28CSD.3ζ) and FGFR4.V-CAR-T cells (F8-FR4.CD28HD/TM.CD28CSD.3ζ) as the most potent variants, with the former inducing complete remission in CD276-high rhabdomyosarcoma xenografts and the latter showing specific cytotoxicity in vitro but only delayed tumor control in vivo [ ]. Critically, dual-targeting CAR-T cells co-expressing both constructs achieved complete tumor eradication in an aggressive Rh4 model, overcoming the limitation of antigen density-dependent escape observed in CD276-low tumors and demonstrating synergistic efficacy without compromising CD276-targeted activity [ ]. Tian et al. developed an optimized FGFR4-redirected CAR by replacing its CD8 hinge/transmembrane and 4-1BB co-stimulatory domains with CD28 counterparts, enhancing antitumor activity in most rhabdomyosarcoma xenografts though residual resistance persisted in the aggressive RMS559 model [ ]. Through MYOD1 chromatin profiling, they identified CD276 as a co-target and developed bicistronic CAR-T cells (BiCisCAR) simultaneously engaging FGFR4 and CD276 with distinct co-stimulatory domains [ ]. These BiCisCAR-T cells exhibited superior persistence and antitumor potency compared to optimized single-target FGFR4-redirected CARs or bicistronic constructs using uniform co-stimulation, overcoming heterogeneity-driven resistance and providing a validated strategy for dual antigen targeting in high-risk rhabdomyosarcoma [ ]. Concurrently investigating novel targets, Piccand et al. confirmed CD276 and FGFR4 expression while identifying L1CAM as a candidate antigen through surfaceome profiling [ ]. Their optimized CD276.CD28HD.28TM.28CSD.3ζ CAR-T cells and dual-targeting F8-FR4.CD28HD.28TM.28CSD.3ζ CAR-T cells achieved tumor eradication in orthotopic rhabdomyosarcoma models, whereas FGFR4-CAR-T cells showed only partial responses [ ]. CD276-redirected CAR efficacy was limited in tumors with low antigen density, eradicating tumors in only 1/5 mice [ ]. Hinge domain optimization revealed FGFR4-redirected CARs with IgG4 hinges exhibited superior in vitro cytotoxicity and IFN-γ secretion [ ]. Similarly, L1CAM-redirected CAR-T cells with IgG4CH2CH3 hinge and CD28 costimulatory domain demonstrated potent in vitro activity despite limited in vivo efficacy in pilot studies [ ]. Critically, histopathological analysis revealed extensive collagen deposition (COL4A1, COL4A2, COL18A1) in rhabdomyosarcoma xenografts, prompting the hypothesis that co-expressing collagenase could enhance CAR-T cell infiltration by degrading the extracellular matrix [ ].
Pinto and colleagues published a recent paper on the results of the first-in-human Phase I trial evaluating B7-H3-redirected CAR-T cells in young patients with relapsed/refractory solid tumors, including rhabdomyosarcoma (NCT04483778) [ ]. Briefly, the CAR-T cells were tolerable with no acute on-target/off-tumor toxicity [ ]. Antitumor activity was limited after the first infusion, with low CAR-T cell expansion [ ]. However, a second infusion in one patient led to a dramatic increase in CAR-T cell levels, an objective partial response, and manageable CRS and transaminitis [ ]. This suggests that achieving high CAR-T cell expansion may be critical for efficacy, though host and TME factors remain significant barriers [ ]. Additionally, a Phase I clinical trial (NCT06865664) initiated in 2025 aimed to recruit children and young adults with recurrent or refractory rhabdomyosarcoma to estimate the maximum tolerated dose of FGFR4-redirected CAR-T cells; however, no results have been published to date.
Collectively, these findings substantiate CD276 and FGFR4 as high-priority immunotherapeutic targets in rhabdomyosarcoma, with combinatorial CAR-T cell strategies demonstrating synergistic efficacy against antigen-heterogeneous tumors. To fully realize this potential, future efforts must address the dual barriers of antigen density limitations and stromal immunosuppression through rationally engineered cellular therapies optimized for solid TMEs [ ].

## EPHB4
Ephrin type-B receptor 4 (EPHB4), a cell surface molecule, exhibits documented expression across various malignancies including breast, lung, and colon carcinomas, as well as soft tissue sarcomas such as rhabdomyosarcoma [ – ]. This expression profile, coupled with its functional duality, positions EPHB4 as a candidate target for cancer immunotherapy. Ligand-induced EPHB4 signaling can trigger pro-apoptotic pathways, whereas its constitutive activation promotes tumor cell proliferation and aggressiveness [ ]. Capitalizing on these characteristics, researchers have pursued EPHB4 targeting for therapeutic intervention [ ]. Kubo et al. recently developed second-generation CAR-T cells using an ephrin B2-based targeting domain (the natural ligand for EPHB4) delivered via PiggyBac transposition [ ]. These engineered T cells demonstrated potent, dose-dependent cytotoxicity in vitro against EPHB4-expressing rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines (Rh30, Rh41, RD) across multiple effector-to-target ratios and conferred protection against tumor cell rechallenge, suggesting a memory response [ ]. Furthermore, administration of these CAR-T cells induced significant tumor regression and substantially prolonged survival in Rh30 xenograft models established in SCID-beige mice (through Rh30 injection into the dorsal areas of each mouse) compared to control groups [ ]. These collective findings demonstrate the therapeutic potential of EPHB4-redirected CAR-T cell therapy for rhabdomyosarcoma, though comprehensive preclinical and clinical studies remain essential to validate these initial results.

## CD56
CD56 (neural cell adhesion molecule, NCAM), a transmembrane glycoprotein, is constitutively expressed on neurons, glial cells, and skeletal muscle, and serves as a canonical marker for natural killer (NK) cells while also being present on various immune cell subsets including dendritic cells, monocytes, and αβ/γδ T lymphocytes [ ]. Neoplastic overexpression of CD56 has been documented in malignancies such as small cell lung cancer (SCLC), neuroblastoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma [ – ]. Consequently, CD56-redirected CAR-T cells have been developed and demonstrate potent antitumor activity in vitro against neuroblastoma, glioma, and SCLC cell lines, as well as significant tumor growth suppression in CD56-expressing human xenograft models of neuroblastoma and SCLC [ ]. Notably, these in vitro studies showed no evidence of CAR-T cell fratricide, suggesting CD56 targeting mitigates a key translational concern [ ]. Supporting clinical translation, Jiang et al. reported a case study of CD56-redirected CAR-T cell therapy in a 2-year-old male patient with recurrent, chemotherapy-refractory embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma originating in the bladder [ ]. Following three surgical resections and multimodal chemotherapy, the patient experienced recurrence, prompting exploration of CD56-redirected CAR-T cells to target minimal residual disease and prevent relapse [ ]. Treatment was well-tolerated without CRS, exhibiting only transient IL-6 elevation that peaked on day 7 post-infusion before returning to baseline; subsequent follow-up over 3.5 years indicated sustained stable disease [ ]. This preliminary evidence suggests CD56-redirected CAR-T cell therapy warrants further investigation as a potential salvage treatment for pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma patients with refractory disease or those with limited treatment options [ ]. Limitations of this study include the absence of clinical trial registration and the lack of reported details regarding the CAR construct and the T-cell manufacturing procedure.

## αvβ3
αvβ3 integrin, a transmembrane receptor for extracellular matrix proteins (e.g., vitronectin, fibronectin), drives tumor progression by facilitating invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis across diverse malignancies including glioblastoma, melanoma, and breast cancer [ ]. Its overexpression correlates with advanced disease and poor prognosis, positioning it as a therapeutic target [ ]. In rhabdomyosarcoma, αvβ3 is pathologically upregulated on tumor cells and associated vasculature, promoting migration, survival, and therapy resistance [ – ]. This tumor-selective expression profile and functional role in rhabdomyosarcoma pathogenesis make αvβ3 a compelling candidate for targeted immunotherapy [ – ]. Lulu et al. proposed bispecific CAR-T cells co-targeting αvβ3 integrin and HER2 to mitigate antigen escape risk [ ], building on prior success where αvβ3-redirected CAR-T cells controlled glioblastoma [ ] and HER2-CAR-T cells mediated medulloblastoma regression [ ]. HER2 expression in rhabdomyosarcoma and a documented case of transient remission in pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma further supported this strategy [ , ]. Flow cytometry confirmed αvβ3 and/or HER2 co-expression across all six alveolar/embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma lines tested, with five co-expressing both antigens. In vitro, αvβ3-redirected CAR-T cells and HER2-redirected CAR-T cells exhibited rapid, antigen-specific killing of rhabdomyosarcoma cells, achieving 70% cytolysis at low target densities, suggesting reduced vulnerability to antigen loss [ ]. Specificity was validated through antibody blockade and CRISPR knockout of ITGB3 (αvβ3) or ERBB2 (HER2), which abrogated cytotoxicity [ ]. CAR-T cell co-culture with rhabdomyosarcoma triggered potent T-cell activation, upregulating CD107a, CD137, CD25, CD69, and PD-1. These findings might establish αvβ3 and HER2 as compelling targets for bispecific CAR-T cell therapy in rhabdomyosarcoma, with current efforts focused on developing αvβ3/HER2 bispecific constructs anticipated to surpass single-target efficacy by preempting escape mechanisms [ ].

## IGF1R and ROR1
The insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF1R), a tyrosine kinase driving rhabdomyosarcoma proliferation through PI3K-AKT/mTOR and MAPK pathways, and receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 1 (ROR1), an oncofetal Wnt ligand receptor promoting metastasis and survival, represent promising targets due to their tumor-selective overexpression in rhabdomyosarcoma with limited normal tissue distribution [ – ]. Huang et al. leveraged this biology by evaluating bispecific CAR-T cell therapy against metastatic/refractory sarcomas [ ]. IGF1R was universally expressed (15/15) and ROR1 frequently expressed (11/15) across sarcoma cell lines, including alveolar and embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes [ ]. Using the Sleeping Beauty transposon system, the research team generated IGF1R- and ROR1-specific CAR-T cells from healthy donors and sarcoma patients [ ]. In vitro, these CAR-T cells demonstrated potent antigen-specific cytotoxicity against sarcoma cells and secreted elevated IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-13. Critically, patient-derived CAR-T cells retained functionality, producing significant IFN-γ upon tumor stimulation [ ]. In vivo, adoptive transfer of patient-derived IGF1R/ROR1-redirected CAR-T cells achieved substantial tumor reduction in systemic and localized osteosarcoma xenograft models (NSG mice) and prolonged survival in localized sarcoma models (NOD/scid mice) [ ]. Although the in vivo experiments were conducted in tumor types other than rhabdomyosarcoma, this study identifies IGF1R and ROR1 as actionable targets for CAR-T cell therapy in high-risk sarcomas and indicates that the Sleeping Beauty platform offers a clinically translatable, cost-effective manufacturing approach [ ].

## Fetal acetylcholine receptor
The fetal nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (fAChR) is a pentameric ligand-gated ion channel, distinct from the adult isoform predominant after birth where the γ-subunit is replaced by ε; postnatally, fAChR expression becomes restricted primarily to thymic myoid cells, extraocular muscles, and denervated striated muscle [ ]. Crucially, fAChR is re-expressed in a significant proportion of rhabdomyosarcoma cases, serving as both a diagnostic biomarker and a discriminator between rhabdomyosarcoma and non-rhabdomyomatous tumors or normal muscle tissue [ ]. This tumor-specific expression profile positions fAChR as a viable immunotherapy target. Supporting this, anti-fAChR immunotoxins, constructed from a fully human anti-fAChR Fab fragment conjugated to Pseudomonas exotoxin A, demonstrated potent eradication of human embryonal and alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines in vitro while sparing fAChR-negative controls, and delayed tumor progression in preclinical rhabdomyosarcoma models [ ]. Regarding CAR-T cell approaches, Gattenlöhner et al. developed fAChR-redirected CAR-T cells which exhibited specific activation and proinflammatory cytokine release upon co-culture with fAChR-positive cell lines (TE-671, Ax-OH-1, Birch, FL-OH-1), yet spared fAChR-deficient A-204 cells [ ]. However, these CAR-T cells failed to exert significant cytotoxicity within a standard 4-hour assay despite antigen-specific IFN-γ production [ ]. Overcoming this limitation required increased fAChR antigen density via transient transfection, which rendered target cells susceptible to robust CAR-T cell-mediated lysis correlating with elevated granzyme B secretion [ ]. Subsequent research into rhabdomyosarcoma resistance mechanisms revealed absent CD80/CD86 but variable inducible T-cell co-stimulator ligand (ICOS-L) expression in rhabdomyosarcoma samples, alongside consistent overexpression of the anti-apoptotic protein survivin and other inhibitor of apoptosis (IAP) family members in rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines and biopsies [ ]. Critically, in vitro downregulation of survivin (pharmacologically or via siRNA) sensitized rhabdomyosarcoma cells to fAChR-redirected CAR-T cell cytotoxicity [ ]. In vivo, significant tumor growth inhibition in rhabdomyosarcoma-bearing mice was achieved only through the combination of fAChR-redirected CAR-T cells and survivin blockade, establishing survivin inhibition as a necessary sensitizing strategy for effective fAChR-targeted CAR-T cell therapy in rhabdomyosarcoma [ ].

## GD2
GD2, a disialoganglioside overexpressed in multiple solid tumors including a subset of rhabdomyosarcoma, represents a promising target for CAR-T cell therapy [ , ]. Pezzella et al. evaluated the iCasp9.2 A.GD2.CAR-CD28.4-1BBζ construct (CAR.GD2) against GD2-positive sarcomas, which collectively comprise 10–15% of pediatric cancers. Flow cytometry confirmed GD2 expression in 55% of primary pediatric sarcoma biopsies, with alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma and osteosarcoma exhibiting the highest levels and Ewing sarcoma the lowest, with osteosarcoma contributing most strongly to this proportion and alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma showing expression in only a subset of cases, while Ewing sarcoma exhibited the lowest levels [ ]. CAR.GD2 T cells demonstrated significant tumor control in both in vitro and in vivo models of GD2-expressing sarcomas, including disseminated rhabdomyosarcoma and orthotopic osteosarcoma [ ]. Critically, pretreatment with the enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) inhibitor tazemetostat upregulated GD2 expression on GD2dim sarcoma cells, sensitizing them to CAR.GD2-mediated cytotoxicity [ ]. While CAR.GD2 T cells exhibited vigorous antitumor activity and long-term persistence in mouse models, their efficacy was compromised by immunosuppressive MDSCs expanded by tumor-derived G-CSF [ ]. These preclinical findings support CAR.GD2 as a viable therapeutic strategy for GD2-positive sarcomas, notably including the GD2-positive subset of rhabdomyosarcomas, but underscore the necessity of combinatorial approaches targeting MDSCs to optimize clinical outcomes [ ]. It is also relevant for translational context that sarcoma-level GD2 data are heavily influenced by osteosarcoma; indeed, some GD2-redirected CAR-T cell clinical studies (NCT02107963) included osteosarcoma while excluding rhabdomyosarcoma, reflecting this differential expression [ ]. This preclinical promise is mirrored by highly positive clinical results; for instance, the GD2-CART01 therapy for relapsed/refractory neuroblastoma demonstrated a 63% overall response rate and an encouraging 60% 3-year overall survival, establishing a strong proof-of-concept for GD2-redirected CAR-T cells in pediatric solid tumors [ ].

## Other novel treatment modalities for rhabdomyosarcoma
Beyond CAR-T cell therapy, significant research effort is directed towards diverse immunotherapeutic strategies aiming to overcome the limitations of conventional treatment for rhabdomyosarcoma. A recent study by Reindl et al. has expanded the immunotherapeutic arsenal for rhabdomyosarcoma by exploring epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-targeted CAR NK cells, delivered in combination with radiotherapy [ ]. The investigators engineered primary human NK cells to express a cetuximab-based EGFR-redirected CAR via lentiviral transduction. These EGFR-redirected CAR NK cells demonstrated potent, enhanced cytotoxicity against rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines in both monolayer and spheroid cultures, and were also effective against chemotherapy-resistant and primary patient-derived rhabdomyosarcoma cells [ ]. In vivo, the engineered cells exhibited superior tumor homing compared to non-transduced NK cells in a xenograft model. Critically, the combination of EGFR-redirected CAR NK cell therapy with fractionated radiotherapy further augmented NK cell infiltration into tumors and significantly suppressed tumor growth [ ]. This work provides preliminary evidence for the feasibility of EGFR-redirected CAR NK cells and suggests a potential synergistic interaction with radiotherapy. However, comprehensive preclinical assessment, including investigations into long-term efficacy, safety, and on-target/off-tumor risks, alongside future clinical studies, will be necessary to establish the viability of this combinatorial approach for rhabdomyosarcoma.
To systematically identify new immunotherapeutic targets, Timpanaro and colleagues conducted a comprehensive surfaceome analysis of fusion-positive and fusion-negative rhabdomyosarcoma cell lines and patient-derived xenografts [ ]. Their profiling not only confirmed known targets such as FGFR4, NCAM1, and CD276 (B7-H3) but also revealed novel candidate antigens including AGRL2, JAM3, and MEGF10 [ ]. Notably, they identified L1CAM as a particularly promising target, with strong expression detected in over 80% of alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma tumors, establishing it as a viable candidate for antibody-based therapeutic strategies [ ]. Such systematic profiling efforts are crucial and must be expanded upon by the research community to continuously uncover novel and suitable targets for this aggressive malignancy.
Beyond pharmacological combinations, the integration of CAR-T cell therapy with conventional local modalities like surgery and radiotherapy is a critical consideration. Clinical experience, particularly in pediatric solid tumors, suggests that the efficacy of adoptive cell therapy is enhanced in the setting of minimal residual disease following consolidative radiotherapy [ ]. High-burden solid tumors present physical and immunosuppressive barriers that can overwhelm adoptively transferred T cells. Therefore, employing surgery to debulk macroscopic disease and radiotherapy to control localized lesions might create a more favorable immunological environment for CAR-T cells. This approach may improve T-cell infiltration and persistence while reducing the risk of severe CRS associated with high antigen load. Future clinical trials for rhabdomyosarcoma should strategically incorporate these modalities, using CAR-T cells as a consolidative therapy after maximal tumor debulking.
Immune checkpoint blockade represents a major focus, targeting inhibitory receptors such as PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 to reinvigorate endogenous antitumor T-cell responses hindered within the immunosuppressive TME [ , ]. While early-phase clinical trials evaluating single-agent anti-PD-1 antibodies (ipilimumab, nivorulimab) in relapsed pediatric solid tumors, including rhabdomyosarcoma, demonstrated modest objective response rates, they provided crucial proof-of-concept for immune activity [ , ]. Current investigations increasingly prioritize rational combinations, such as pairing checkpoint inhibitors with conventional chemotherapy or radiation to enhance antigen release and immune priming, or with targeted agents like tyrosine kinase inhibitors to modulate the TME [ , ]. Targeting alternative checkpoints highly expressed in rhabdomyosarcoma, notably B7-H3 (CD276), using monoclonal antibodies or antibody-drug conjugates, is also under active preclinical and clinical development in other tumors, offering potential synergy with T-cell-directed therapies by addressing distinct immunosuppressive mechanisms [ – ]. Furthermore, strategies combining checkpoint blockade with agents disrupting MDSCs function or adenosine signaling are being explored to comprehensively dismantle immune resistance barriers [ ].
Oncolytic virotherapy and cancer vaccines constitute another promising frontier. Engineered viruses, such as herpes simplex virus or adenovirus strains selectively replicating in tumor cells, induce immunogenic cell death, releasing tumor-associated antigens and danger signals that stimulate robust innate and adaptive immune responses [ ]. Recently, Yoshida et al. demonstrated that Ad5/3-pMYOG(S)-mMEF2, a mutant pMYOG-controlled oncolytic adenovirus, selectively replicates in PAX3::FOXO1–positive ARMS, inducing tumor cell lysis and in vivo growth suppression [ ]. Therapeutic cancer vaccines aim to actively prime or boost tumor-specific immunity. Approaches under investigation include peptide vaccines targeting rhabdomyosarcoma-associated antigens like survivin or PAX3::FOXO1 fusion peptides, dendritic cell vaccines loaded with tumor lysates or specific antigens, and novel mRNA vaccine platforms encoding tumor neoantigens identified through genomic sequencing [ , ]. These vaccine strategies seek to overcome tumor antigen ignorance or tolerance, potentially creating a more favorable immunological context for other immunotherapies, including checkpoint inhibitors or adoptive cell transfer, by expanding tumor-reactive T-cell clones.
Small molecule inhibitors targeting dysregulated signaling pathways and epigenetic modulators offer complementary therapeutic avenues, often with immunomodulatory potential. Given the frequent activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and RAS/MAPK pathways in rhabdomyosarcoma pathogenesis, agents like PI3K inhibitors (such as BYL-719 and CAL-101), mTOR inhibitors (e.g., everolimus), and MEK inhibitors (e.g., trametinib, selumetinib) are under evaluation, both as monotherapies and in rational combinations [ , – ]. Aurora A kinase inhibitors (e.g., alisertib) exploit mitotic vulnerabilities in sarcoma cells and have shown activity in early trials [ , ]. Epigenetic modifiers, such as histone deacetylase inhibitors (e.g., vorinostat, panobinostat) and EZH2 inhibitors (e.g., tazemetostat), can reverse aberrant gene silencing patterns contributing to tumor growth and immune evasion [ – ]; tazemetostat has demonstrated the additional benefit of upregulating targetable antigens like GD2 [ ]. The IGF1R remains a validated target, with monoclonal antibodies (e.g., figitumumab, cixutumumab) showing activity in subsets of patients [ , ]. Critically, many of these targeted agents exhibit off-target immunomodulatory effects—such as reducing immunosuppressive cytokine production, enhancing antigen presentation, or altering the tumor stroma—making them attractive partners for combination with immunotherapies to achieve synergistic efficacy against high-risk and refractory rhabdomyosarcoma.

## Conclusion
Rhabdomyosarcoma remains a daunting therapeutic challenge, particularly for high-risk and metastatic patients, despite intensive multimodal regimens combining surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. The emergence of CAR-T cell therapy represents a transformative frontier, offering the potential for precise tumor targeting through engineered immune cells directed against antigens like HER2, CD276, FGFR4, PDGFR-α, and others. Preclinical and early clinical evidence underscores the promise of this approach, with one documented case of durable remission in a pediatric patient with refractory bone marrow metastatic rhabdomyosarcoma. However, formidable obstacles persist, including profound antigen heterogeneity enabling immune evasion, an immunosuppressive TME limiting T-cell infiltration and function, risks of secondary primary malignancies [ ], and logistical hurdles in manufacturing and toxicity management. Innovative strategies such as dual-targeting CARs, combinatorial checkpoint blockade, “off-the-shelf” allogeneic platforms, and microenvironment-modulating adjuvants are actively being developed to overcome these barriers. Beyond CAR-T cell therapy, integrating checkpoint inhibitors, oncolytic viruses, cancer vaccines, and pathway-specific small molecules holds significant potential for synergistic efficacy. In addition, T-cell receptor (TCR)–engineered T cells might represent a promising and complementary immunotherapeutic strategy for rhabdomyosarcoma. Unlike CAR-T cells, which are limited to recognizing cell surface antigens, TCR-engineered T cells can specifically target intracellular tumor-associated antigens that are processed and presented on major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules [ – ]. This capability substantially broadens the spectrum of actionable targets, including transcription factors, fusion oncoproteins, and lineage-specific antigens that are not accessible to antibody-based approaches [ – ]. Given the presence of recurrent oncogenic drivers and aberrantly expressed developmental proteins in rhabdomyosarcoma, TCR-based therapies may enable highly specific tumor recognition while overcoming one of the principal limitations of CAR-T cell therapy in solid tumors [ – ]. Although clinical experience with TCR-engineered T cells in rhabdomyosarcoma remains limited, advances in antigen discovery, TCR affinity optimization, and strategies to mitigate MHC restriction and tumor immune evasion suggest that this approach may warrant preclinical and clinical evaluation in this disease. The future of rhabdomyosarcoma treatment lies in rationally designed multimodal strategies that harness complementary mechanisms, address resistance pathways, and are optimized for the unique biological and clinical realities of pediatric solid tumors. Concerted efforts to translate these advances into accessible, scalable clinical solutions are essential to improve survival and quality of life for patients facing this aggressive malignancy.
