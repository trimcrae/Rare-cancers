## Background
Glypican-2 (GPC2), a member of the GPC gene family, primarily functions in developing neural and thyroid cancer tissues, exerting influence on protein transduction, cellular proliferation and differentiation, as well as oncogenic signatures. GPC2 exhibits significant overexpression in the majority of neuroblastoma (NB) samples while remaining nearly undetectable in normal pediatric tissue samples.

## Methods
Overall survival (OS) was employed as a key parameter to investigate the correlation between GPC2 expression and pan-pediatric cancers. To assess the association between GPC2 expression and clinical parameters of NB, box plots followed by t-tests were utilized. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks and gene-gene interaction networks were constructed. Functional roles were determined through Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. The XCell was employed to analyze the relationship between GPC2 expression and immune-related cells. Additionally, we retrospectively collected clinical data and survival information from a cohort of 51 patients diagnosed with NB and conducted immunohistochemistry (IHC) on the specimens as a validation set.

## Results
A significant correlation between elevated GPC2 expression and reduced survival rates was observed in NB and brain tumors (P < 0.05). Notably, high GPC2 expression showed a non-significant trend toward reduced survival in four other pediatric tumor types except osteosarcoma. Notably, the MYCN amplified group exhibited significantly higher levels of GPC2 expression. Furthermore, GPC2 expression showed a positive correlation with infiltrating basophils, CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, CD8 naïve T cells, Tgd cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells and pro B cells, while demonstrating a negative correlation with infiltrating fibroblasts, macrophages M1 and M2 subtypes, monocytes neutrophils and pDCs. Among all 51 pediatric NB patients analyzed in this study, the MYCN amplified group displayed significantly higher levels of GPC2 expression compared to the MYCN not-amplified group. Additionally, survival analysis revealed that individuals with high GPC2 expression had significantly worse OS compared to those with low expression (P = 0.018).

## Conclusion
Elevated GPC2 expression was significantly correlated with reduced survival rates in NB and brain tumors (P < 0.05), with a non-significant trend toward reduced OS in four other pediatric tumor types. Furthermore, the expression level of GPC2 in NB showed a positive association with MYCN status and levels of immune cell infiltration

## Background
Glypican (GPC) belongs to a family of extracellular heparan sulfate proteoglycans, which consists of at least six different genes, known as GPC-1 to GPC-6, respectively [ ]. All GPC1-6 exhibit high expression in cancer and serve as potential therapeutic targets for cancer treatment [ ]. GPC2, a member of the GPC family of genes, shows predominant activity in developing neural tissues and thyroid cancer tissues [ ]. In general, GPC2 exerts an influence on protein transduction, cellular proliferation and differentiation, as well as oncogenic signatures [ ]. A research analyzed GPC2 in 8 pediatric tumors such as osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma (EWS), thereby highlighting the diverse functional role of GPC2 across childhood malignancies [ ].
Neuroblastoma (NB) refers to a sympathetic neural crest disease, with the majority of primary tumors predominantly found in the adrenal gland, although it can potentially manifest anywhere within the sympathetic nervous system [ ]. As the most prevalent extracranial solid tumor in pediatric patients, NB occurs predominantly (90%) in children below the age of 5, and nearly 15% of pediatric tumor-related deaths [ ]. NB patients are divided into four groups based on their risk of death, including extremely low-risk, low-risk, intermediate-risk, and high-risk [ ]. The survival rate of the first three risk groups is close to 100%, while the 5-year survival rate of high-risk NB patients is less than 50% [ , ]. Due to the genetic, morphological, and clinical heterogeneity exhibited by NB [ ], the treatment of NB requires greater precision and individualization. The amplification and mutation of multiple genes have been proven to be related to the development and prognosis of NB, including MYCN amplification, ALK amplification and activating mutations, LIN28B polymorphism, and TERT rearrangement etc. [ ], but it also provides new targets for the treatment of NB.
Given its pivotal role in neuronal axon proliferation and differentiation, mounting evidence has substantiated the upregulation of GPC2 in NB, a prevalent childhood malignancy [ , ]. GPC2 has been shown to be overexpressed in most NB samples, but in normal pediatric tissue samples is nearly undetectable [ , ]. The expression of GPC2 in NB is strongly correlated with MYCN amplification, which serves as the most prevalent marker for NB, indicating a heightened risk of mortality and unfavorable prognosis [ – ]. Therefore, GPC2 may become an important target for immunotherapy of NB.
Microsatellite instability (MSI), which caused by frequent polymorphisms and single-nucleotide substitutions in short repetitive DNA sequences, arises from DNA mismatch repair (MMR) deficiency [ ]. MMR deficiency is not uncommon in pediatric cancers but is enriched in specific high-risk subtypes (e.g., high-grade gliomas) and is directly associated with treatment response [ ]. DNA methylation is a key epigenetic regulatory mechanism that plays an important role in various cellular processes, and its dysregulation contributes significantly to the initiation and progression of cancer [ ]. Abnormal DNA methylation represents a critical driving event in pediatric cancers, particularly NB, where DNA methyltransferases are directly involved in tumorigenesis and the regulation of treatment response [ ]. Homologous recombination repair (HRR) is a crucial repair pathway when DNA is damaged. Homologous recombination repair deficiency (HRD) has been identified in many types of tumors, and in pediatric gliomas, it may be associated with sensitivity to PARP inhibitor therapy [ ]. Disulfidptosis, a novel form of cell death dependent on cysteine metabolism and disulfide bond accumulation, exhibits a high degree of alignment between its regulatory mechanisms and the metabolic reprogramming characteristics of pediatric tumors [ ]. In summary, MMR, DNA methylation, HHR and disulfidptosis are all key molecular features of high-risk pediatric tumors. Therefore, this study conducted investigation on these pathways, aiming to comprehensively decipher the molecular regulatory network and clinical application value of GPC2 in pediatric tumors.
Given the lack of bioinformatic pan-pediatric cancer studies in previous research, we conducted a comprehensive poly-omics investigation (including transcriptomics, epigenomics, and immunomics) on GPC2, employing an integrated suite of tools and datasets encompassing diverse pediatric cancers. Our aim was to elucidate the associations between this gene, clinical characteristics, and multi-omic heterogeneity. The effects of GPC2 on the malignant phenotype of NB were verified through in vitro experiments. Screening efforts were also used to identify GPC2-activating drugs with potential value for use in cancers.

## Methods

## Data collection and processing
The expression of GPC2 in different cancer cell lines was analyzed utilizing the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE, https://sites.broadinstitute.org/ccle) database [ ]. The mRNA expression profiles and correlative overall survival (OS) from 7 types of pediatric cancer samples were downloaded from The Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET, https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/target), the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and Pediatric Brain Tumor Atlas_ CBTTC (https://cbtn.org/projects/). Detail information regarding the nomenclature and abbreviations of the 7 pediatric tumor types included in this study was shown in Table . The transcriptional data were converted to transcripts per million (TPM), followed by log2-transformation and filtering of genes with low expression. 

## Correlation between GPC2 and prognosis
OS was utilized as a key parameter to investigate the correlation between GPC2 expression and patient prognosis. For survival analysis, tumor samples were categorized into low and high GPC2 expression groups based on the median levels of expression. The Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test were employed for each specific cancer type. The R packages 'survival' and 'survminer' were utilized to generate the survival curves. The difference was identified by the standard of p-value < 0.05. Additionally, we utilized the R package 'ggplot2' to establish the association between GPC2 expression and pan-pediatric cancer survival.

## DNA mismatch repair, DNA methyltransferases, homologous recombination repair and disulfidptosis analyses
The relationship between five MMR genes [ ], four DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) [ ] and the expression of GPC2 was investigated through visual analyses. In addition, the ARIEL3 clinical trial was utilized to identify 30 genes associated with HRR [ ], and the correlations between GPC2 mRNA levels and HRR-related genes were assessed. A study demonstrated that the aberrant accumulation of intracellular disulfides in SLC7A11high cells during glucose starvation triggers a previously uncharacterized form of cell death distinct from apoptosis or ferroptosis, which was termed as disulfidptosis [ ].The associations between GPC2 mRNA levels and disulfidptosis-related genes were also evaluated. The cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/) was searched for gene expression information of pan-pediatric cancer. Acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL) (TARGET, 2018, 1978 samples), Pediatric Rhabdoid Tumor (TARGET, 2018, 72 samples), Pediatric Wilms' Tumor (TARGET, 2018, 657 samples), Pediatric acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) (TARGET, 2018, 1025 samples), NB (TARGET, 2018, 1089 samples) were included. Expression of GPC2 in cancers were explored with the “Co-expression” module.

## Relationship Between GPC2 expression and clinical parameters of NB
The relationship between GPC2 expression and clinical parameters of NB were calculated in TARGET-NB dataset, and the box plots were generated using the 'ggplot2' R package. The expression data were subjected to log2 transformation, followed by t-tests. The differential expression between different clinical parameters was determined based on a significance threshold of p-value < 0.05.

## Functional enrichment and interaction analyses
The STRING database was utilized to construct a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network for GPC2 [ ]. Subsequently, the PPI network was visualized by Cytoscape software (version 3.5.1) (www.cytoscape.org/). The R 'clusterProfiler' package and the R 'org.Hs.eg.db' package were utilized for Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis, respectively [ ]. The GENEMANIA platform (http://genemania.org/search/) was utilized to construct a gene-gene interaction network for genes found by PPI, facilitating the assessment of their functional roles [ ].

## Relationship between GPC2 expression and immune cell infiltration
XCell (https://xcell.ucsf.edu/) was used to analyze the relationship between GPC2 expression and immune-related cells [ ]. Additionally, the associations between GPC2 and each immune cell infiltration level were visualized using R software packages "ggplot2" and "ggpubr".

## MicroRNA coregulatory network and transcription factor-gene interactions
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) represent a class of endogenous short non-coding RNAs, which mediate the degradation of target mRNA or inhibit translation [ ]. Transcription factors (TFs) are proteins that specifically bind to DNA sequences and regulate gene expression. The interaction between miRNAs, TFs and genes were analyzed using NetworkAnalyst 3.0 (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) to evaluate the impact of the transcription factor on gene expression and functional pathways [ ]. Relevant results were also visualized by Cytoscape.

## Prediction of candidate drugs
Evaluating protein-chemical interactions is crucial for screening potential drugs. The interaction between GPC2 and chemicals were analyzed using NetworkAnalyst 3.0. The data is based on data from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) (downloaded on Nov. 2016).

## Clinical and laboratory validation
We retrospectively collected the clinical data of pediatric NB patients (age≤18 years old) from November 2017 to August 2019 at the Beijing Friendship Hospital of Capital Medical University. For immunohistochemistry, tissue samples from these NB patients were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 14 h, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned. After dewaxing, rehydration, and blocking (5% BSA), the sections were treated with GPC2 (1:1000) (sc-393824, Santa Cruz, USA) overnight at 4°C. On the next day, HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse secondary antibody (BOSTER, Wuhan) was added to the tissue sections and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. Finally, after staining the samples with the nuclear stain DAPI, the staining of the aforementioned indicators was observed under a microscope to compare the expression differences between groups. Image acquisition and analysis were performed using microscope combined with Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software. Five high-power fields (×400) were randomly selected from each tissue section to ensure coverage of both the tumor core and stromal regions. With brown-yellow granules as the criterion for positive staining, the software was used to calculate the Mean Optical Density (MOD) and Positive Staining Area Percentage (PSAP) of each field. The average value of the five fields was taken as the final quantitative result for each sample. The study is in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the local Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital (NO.2025022418034941).

## Results

## Pan-pediatric cancer analyses of GPC2 expression
The flow chart of the study was shown in Fig. . The relative GPC2 expression levels across different cell lines obtained from CCLE data are presented in Figure S1. The expression level of GPC2 is significantly elevated in autonomic ganglia. Then, the datasets presented in Table were initially utilized to perform a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis of GPC2 mRNA expression. This approach revealed the correlation between OS and differential GPC2 expression across 7 pediatric cancer types (Fig.  ). Except osteosarcoma, high GPC2 expression showed a trend of lower survival rate in the remaining six pediatric tumors, particularly in cases of NB and brain tumors. 

## GPC2 Levels are associated with DNA repair, methylation, HRR and disulfidptosis
The associations between GPC2 expression levels and genes associated with MMR(MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, PMS2 and EPCAM), DNMT(DNMT1, DNMT3A and DNMT3B), HRR signatures(ATM, ATR, ATRX, BARD1, BLM, BRCA1, BRCA2, BRIP1, CHEK1, CHEK2, FANCA, FANCC, FANCD2, FANCE, FANCF, FANCG, FANCI, FANCL, FANCM, NBN, PALB2, RAD50, RAD51, RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D, RAD52, RAD54L and RPA1), and disulfidptosis (SLC7A11, SLC3A2, RPN1, NCKAP1, WASF2, CYFIP1, ABI2, BRK1 and RAC1) were subsequently assessed in Wilms Tumor, Rhabdoid Tumor, NB, AML and ALL. Among the analyzed pediatric cancer datasets, a positive correlation was detected between GPC2 and MMR gene expression (MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, PMS2) in Wilms’ Tumor and NB, with a particularly pronounced relationship in NB (Fig. B). A positive correlation was observed between GPC2 and the expression of a range of DNMT genes in pediatric cancers, with a particularly pronounced association evident in NB (Fig. A). A positive correlation was observed between GPC2 and the majority of HRR signatures in Wilms' Tumor and NB (Fig. C). A positive correlation was observed between GPC2 and disulfidptosis-related genes in AML (Fig. D). 

## Correlation of GPC2 expression with clinical parameters of NB
The box plots in Fig. illustrate the expression levels of GPC2 and different clinical parameters in NB. Significantly higher expression of GPC2 was observed in the MYCN amplified group compared to the MYCN not-amplified group. Moreover, a significantly higher expression level of GPC2 was found in the histology unfavorable group as opposed to the favorable group. Additionally, elevated levels of GPC2 were observed in both the high-risk Children’s Oncology Group (COG) risk group and age group above 18 months when compared to their respective counterparts (non-high risk COG risk group and age group below 18 months). However, it is important to note that these differences did not yield statistically significant p-values. 

## Functional enrichment and interaction analyses
We utilized the String online database to construct a PPI network, with Homo sapiens selected as the organism for subsequent analysis. Our investigation revealed that GPC3, GPC4, CSPG5, GLCE, IGHV3-43D, MDK, NDST3, SDC1, SDC2 and SDC3 exhibited associations with GPC2. We performed the GO functional annotation and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on all the 11 genes. For biological processes (BPs), these genes mainly involved Cell migration and Glial cell projection elongation. For the cellular component (CC), the genes were associated with Golgi lumen, Lysosomal lumen, Cell surface, Golgi apparatus, Collagen-containing extracellular matrix and Anchored component of plasma membrane (Fig. A, Table S1). Additionally, KEGG databases were selected as the preferred sources for pathway analysis, these genes mainly involved Glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis-heparan sulfate/heparin, Cell adhesion molecules, Proteoglycans in cancer and Malaria (Fig. B, Table S2). The visualization of the network in String was then imported into Cytoscape software (Fig. C). Finally, the gene–gene interaction network for the 11 genes was constructed by GeneMANIA (Fig. D). 

## Relationship between GPC2 expression level and tumor immune cell infiltration
Our XCell analysis revealed a significant association between levels of immune cell infiltration and GPC2 expression in NB (Fig. ). Specifically, GPC2 expression exhibited a positive correlation with infiltrating Basophils, CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, CD8 naïve T cells, Tgd cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells and pro B cells; while showing a negative correlation with infiltrating Fibroblasts, Macrophages, Macrophages.M1, Macrophages.M2, Monocytes, Neutrophils and pDC. 

## Prediction of Target miRNAs, TFs-gene and candidate drugs
In order to elucidate the transcriptional changes and gain insights into the regulatory molecules governing central proteins, we employed a network-based approach to decode regulatory miRNAs and TFs (Fig. ). Through analysis of the miRNA-gene and TF-gene interaction networks, it has been determined that 233 miRNAs and 41 TFs regulatory characteristics are regulated by genes related to GPC2. Moreover, the investigation of protein-chemical interactions is crucial for therapy. Regarding GPC2 as a prospective drug target in NB, we have identified 12 potential drug compounds based on CTD analysis. These compounds exhibit promising efficacy as drugs for NB. 

## GPC2 serves as a prognostic biomarker in NB
A total of 51 pediatric NB patients (31 girls and 20 boys) after surgery were finally enrolled in this study, and the clinical characteristics of the patients were summarized in Table . Among all the 51 patients, the pathology was ganglioneuroblastoma in 20 patients and NB in 31 patients. A total of 41 patients were diagnosed with INSS stage IV, the MYCN gene was found to be amplified in 27 patients. 2 patients belong to the COG low-risk group, 16 patients classified under the intermediate -risk group, and 33 patients falling into the high-risk group. The expression level of GPC2 in the surgical samples from NB patients was assessed using immunohistochemistry. Subsequently, based on the median expression level, all patients were categorized into either a high or low expression group. The box plots in Fig. illustrate the expression levels of GPC2 and different clinical parameters in NB. Significantly higher expression of GPC2 was observed in the MYCN amplified group compared to the MYCN not-amplified group. Besides, survival analysis revealed that individuals with high GPC2 expression exhibited significantly worse OS compared to those with low expression (P = 0.018). 

## Discussion
Pediatric solid tumors and brain tumors often arise within the context of stalled developmental programs, exhibiting restricted expression of fetal antigens that are minimally expressed in non-malignant postnatal tissues [ ]. GPC2, a prototypical oncofetal target, demonstrates high expression levels in the developing nervous system where it functions as a signaling co-receptor regulating cellular growth [ ]. The data from CCLE revealed a significant upregulation of GPC2 expression in autonomic ganglia. Through pan-cancer analysis, GPC2 has emerged as a prominent gene due to its significant upregulation across various cancer types [ ]. Besides, previous studies have demonstrated that GPC2 is overexpressed in NB and other solid tumors, yet its postnatal expression remains limited [ ]. In this study, we first conducted a bioinformatics analysis of GPC2 expression and its prognostic value across pan-pediatric cancers, shedding light on its regulatory pathways, associated genes, and potential for prognostic prediction.
Previous study detected significant differences of GPC2 expression between tumors and normal tissues of 20 forms of adult cancer [ ]. Furthermore, a reported findings suggest that the unique structure of GPC2 facilitates its interaction with the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby modulating MYCN expression and governing tumor cell proliferation [ ]. Given that MYCN amplification serves as an independent prognostic factor associated with unfavorable progression free survival (PFS) and OS in NB [ ], the overexpression of GPC2 may be indicative of an unfavorable prognosis in NB. Nan Li et al. have found that GPC2 protein is highly expressed in about half of NB cases and that high GPC2 expression correlates with poor OS compared with patients with low GPC2 expression [ ]. The present study revealed a significant association between high GPC2 expression and reduced survival rates in six pediatric tumors, particularly in cases of NB and brain tumors. Additionally, a significantly elevated expression of GPC2 was observed in the MYCN amplified cohort, which was further validated in 51 NB patients at our institution. To our knowledge, this is the first study to validate the associations between GPC2 expression levels and prognostic markers (including MYCN amplification status) in clinical samples from Chinese NB patients.
The DNA damage response is a complex series of mechanisms that play a pivotal role in maintaining the stability and integrity of the genome through precise detection and elimination of aberrant sequences and structures within chromosomes [ ]. Tumor cells develop mechanisms, such as the co-option of MMR [ ] and HRR [ ] pathways, to evade specific therapeutic strategies, thereby acquiring stem-like self-maintenance abilities [ ]. In this study, correlations were found between GPC2 and the expression of MMR, DNMT and HHR genes in NB. These findings suggest that GPC2 may regulate progression of NB by influencing DNA damage repair mechanisms. Altered metabolism is a hallmark of cancer and represents an exploitable vulnerability in treatment of cancer. RCD plays a pivotal role in the therapeutic targeting of cancer metabolism. A recent study has unveiled disulfidptosis as a novel form of metabolic-associated RCD [ ]. A positive correlation was observed between GPC2 and disulfidptosis-related genes in AML in this study, suggesting that GPC2 might play a pivotal role in the therapeutic targeting of cancer metabolism through RCD.
The origin of NB can be traced back to the neuroendocrine tissue located in the sympathetic nervous system, and GPC2 plays a crucial role in neuronal cell adhesion and neurite outgrowth. The expression of GPC2 is significantly upregulated in NB compared to normal tissues, and preclinical models have demonstrated promising anti-tumor effects of immunotoxins and an antibody-drug conjugate specifically targeting GPC2 in vivo [ , ]. Nan Li et al. have utilized phage display technology to isolate human single-domain antibodies that specifically target GPC2, and their findings demonstrate the efficacy of these antibodies [ ].
In previous studies, GPC2 has been implicated in the transduction of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which plays a crucial role in regulating the differentiation and development of macrophages, B cells, and other immune cells [ , ]. The findings also demonstrate that the silencing of GPC2 through CRISPR-Cas9 or siRNA leads to the inhibition of NB tumor cell growth. Moreover, GPC2 silencing effectively inactivates Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and downregulates the expression of N-Myc, a crucial oncogenic driver involved in neuroblastoma tumorigenesis [ ]. Our XCell analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between GPC2 expression with CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, CD8 naïve T cells, Tgd cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells and pro B cells. T cells engineered with chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) specifically targeting highly and uniformly expressed B lineage antigens demonstrate remarkable clinical efficacy while maintaining an acceptable safety profile. To investigate the potential of GPC2 as a therapeutic target in NB, Nan Li et al. have developed two types of antibody-based therapeutics, namely immunotoxins and CAR-T cells. The administration of immunotoxin treatment demonstrated significant inhibition of NB growth in mice, although with a slightly diminished binding affinity compared to human GPC2. Additionally, CAR-T cells targeting GPC2 effectively eradicated tumors in a disseminated NB mouse model with metastasis observed at multiple clinically relevant sites including the spine, skull, legs, and pelvis [ ]. A previous study successfully developed CARs that specifically target GPC2, demonstrating robust efficacy and safety of a leading GPC2-CAR candidate suitable for clinical evaluation [ ]. These findings underscore the promising potential of GPC2 as a viable therapeutic target for NB. Further researches are necessary in the development of CAR-T therapies with enhanced binding affinity towards human GPC2, and additional clinical trials are imperative to explore its efficacy and therapeutic advantages.
However, there were several limitations in the present study. Firstly, the sample size of patients with validated clinical data was small. Secondly, additional experiments such as qRT-PCR and Western blot should be conducted at cell and animal levels to validate the protein levels of GPC2.

## Conclusion
Taken above, our bioinformatics analysis revealed a significant association between high GPC2 expression and reduced survival rates in NB and brain tumors. The expression level of GPC2 in NB is positively correlated with MYCN status as well as levels of immune cell infiltration, and may have a potential link with the DNA methylation regulatory pathway, but whether it affects tumor aggressiveness through this pathway requires further research verification.
