## Abstract
In humans, the liver is a central metabolic organ with a complex and unique histological microenvironment. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), which is a highly aggressive disease with a poor prognosis, accounts for most cases of primary liver cancer. As an emerging hallmark of cancers, metabolic reprogramming acts as a runaway mechanism that disrupts homeostasis of the affected organs, including the liver. Specifically, rewiring of the liver metabolic microenvironment, including lipid metabolism, is driven by HCC cells, propelling the phenotypes of HCC cells, including dissemination, invasion, and even metastasis in return. The resulting formation of this vicious loop facilitates various malignant behaviors of HCC further. However, few articles have comprehensively summarized lipid reprogramming in HCC metastasis. Here, we have reviewed the general situation of the liver microenvironment and the physiological lipid metabolism in the liver, and highlighted the effects of different aspects of lipid metabolism on HCC metastasis to explore the underlying mechanisms. In addition, we have recapitulated promising therapeutic strategies targeting lipid metabolism and the effects of lipid metabolic reprogramming on the efficacy of HCC systematical therapy, aiming to offer new perspectives for targeted therapy.
Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Invasion; Metastasis, Lipid metabolism; Tumor microenvironment

## Introduction
In China, liver cancer is the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality, illustrating its high malignancy.[ ] Moreover, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for more than 80% of primary liver cancer, and is usually characterized by high rates of recurrence as well as metastasis, evolving from chronic liver diseases such as alcoholic liver disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and viral hepatitis.[ ] Furthermore, the crosstalk between different components of the liver microenvironment shapes the various functions and dynamic balance of the liver, while also contributes to the occurrence and development of diseases. Due to the shift in the spectrum of HCC and improved understanding of its tumor microenvironment (TME), the metabolic environment has been linked with the oncogenesis, progression, and metastasis in HCC. Specifically, metastasis is one of the most crucial malignant characteristics and most common mortality-related factors of HCC, whose metabolism characteristic is currently a burgeoning field of study. From formation of an aggressive phenotype to the production of circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and metastatic colonization, the metabolism of glucose, glutamine, and lipids is subjected to dynamic alternation, imparting cancer cells with the ability to metastasize.[ ] Notably, lipid metabolism mediates changes in the membrane structure, energy supply, and signaling transduction, fulfilling physiological demands while creating conditions for the occurrence and development of liver cancer, especially metastasis.[ , ]
Additionally, the treatment of HCC has also kept up with the times, which includes not only traditional surgical treatment, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE), local treatment, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, but also immunotherapy and molecular targeted therapy.[ ] Existing treatments have indeed improved the overall survival and prognosis of HCC patients to a certain extent. However, the emergence of drug resistance and tumor heterogeneity still limits treatment efficacy, reminding that it is still urgent to develop novel drugs with excellent curative effectiveness and fewer side effects to expand the range of treatment options.[ ]
In this review, we illustrated the structure and composition of the liver microenvironment as well as the physiological nature of liver metabolic function, describing the landscape of liver homeostasis. We also reviewed the crosstalk between lipid metabolism and HCC metastasis, especially fatty acids (FAs) and cholesterol, although lysophospholipids and other types of lipids also contribute to the invasion of HCC by regulating the structure of the cell membrane and intracellular signaling. FAs and cholesterol play a vital role in energy metabolism and signal transduction, elucidating the role of lipid intake and efflux, biosynthesis, catabolism, and storage in HCC metastasis.[ ] Finally, we overviewed the basic research status about the effect of targeted lipid metabolism on HCC progression and approved HCC drugs. We aim to elaborate on the crucial position of lipid metabolic reprogramming in HCC metastasis, providing insights for future molecular targeted therapies.

## Physiological Lipid Metabolic Microenvironment of the Liver
The complex functions of the liver are reflected in its sophisticated structure and microenvironmental composition, which also plays a role in HCC metastasis. At the tissue and cellular levels, the hepatic lobule is the basic structural and functional unit of liver, mainly containing parenchymal and non-parenchymal cells. Parenchymal cells of the liver include hepatocytes and cholangiocytes, which are responsible for the main physiological functions of the liver, occupying 80% of its mass and constituting 60% of its cell population.[ ] Sinusoidal cells, namely, non-parenchymal cells in the liver, include Kupffer cells (KCs), hepatic stellate cells (HSCs), and liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) [Figure  A]. KCs are resident macrophages located in the liver, which have the capacity to regulate inflammation and metabolism, clear senescent cells and organelles, as well as recognize and resist pathogens.[ , ] Located in the space of Disse, HSCs are widely recognized as the main players of liver fibrosis, during which they are activated and alter their phenotype to promote fibrogenesis, accumulation of the extracellular matrix (ECM), secretion of multiple cytokines, and metabolic regulation.[ ] LSECs construct a barrier between the blood and other hepatic cells, with the function of selective filtration, endocytosis, and angiogenesis, as well as proinflammatory signaling.[ , ]
 In addition to the intrinsic structure of the liver, the abundant blood supply shapes an intricate immune environment. In the portal area, the portal vein, hepatic artery, and bile ducts construct the portal triad flowing toward the central vein, which ultimately leaves the liver by pooling into the hepatic vein [Figure  B]. Circulating blood brings a wide range of immune cells and regulatory molecules into the liver microenvironment, where they interact with resident cells of the liver. Consequently, they regulate the immune status of the liver and remodel the functions of hepatic resident cells to shape the liver microenvironment under different physiological or pathological conditions. The microenvironment of the liver not only endows it with the ability to convert numerous metabolites, deal with the detoxification of the organism, and secrete regulatory molecules such as hormones and coagulation factors, but also provides an opportunity for exposure to pathogens, metabolic abnormalities, and other risk factors.[ ]
Apart from the traditional liver zonation, advanced sequencing techniques have enabled us to revisit it from a metabolic perspective with unprecedented resolution. The relative oxygen content is a crucial factor of liver zonation, forming a metabolic pattern in which gluconeogenesis, glutamine hydrolysis, fatty acid oxidation (FAO), and cholesterol synthesis are preferentially active in the periportal area, while glycolysis, glutamine synthesis, lipogenesis, and bile acid (BA) synthesis tend to be more actively close to the central vein. Meanwhile, Wnt/β-catenin signaling is another determinant of liver zonation, which is also one of the main causes of HCC, reminding the capacity of metabolism to become a prospective pathogenesis in the liver [Figure  C].[ , , , ] Lipid metabolism, as an important part of metabolism, may engage in HCC metastasis along with other pathological changes in the liver microenvironment via activating angiogenesis, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and other responses.[ ]

## Physiological Lipid Metabolism of the Liver
Lipids are hydrophobic or amphipathic molecules that are insoluble in water but soluble in nonpolar organic solvents.[ , ] In the enormous and complex family of lipids, FAs are the basic components of most members while cholesterol is a vital component of membrane structures, widely involved in energy metabolic regulation, signal transduction, and cell membrane construction.[ , ] As the most active metabolic organ in vivo, the liver plays a central role in lipid metabolism. It participates in the uptake, synthesis, catabolism, transport, and storage of lipids, interacting with glucose metabolism and amino acid metabolism to construct the transformation network between different types of metabolites and maintain their homeostasis [Figure  ].[ ]
 In circulation, lipids other than free fatty acids (FFAs) are transported in the form of lipoproteins comprising different apo-lipoproteins (apos). Under the influence of multiple types of enzymes, especially lipoprotein lipase (LPL), these lipoproteins are eventually degraded in hepatocytes.[ ] During these processes, FFA uptake through CD36 and fatty acid transport proteins (FATPs), whereas cholesterol mainly undergoes uptake by hepatocytes through the low-density lipoprotein (LDL) pathway.[ , ] In hepatocytes, FAs are subjected to oxidation in mitochondria, which transforms them into acetyl-coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA). Some of the resulting two-carbon units are subsequently transformed into ketone bodies, while others are fully oxidized via the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA). By contrast, cholesterol cannot be completely degraded, and is mainly excreted in the form of BA. Additionally, the liver is one of the main synthesis places for endogenous FAs and cholesterol, while the synthesis of them is also a crucial means to regulate lipid metabolism. The acetyl-CoA for lipogenesis is mainly derived from citrate via cytosolic ATP citrate lyase (ACLY), and is further converted into FAs by FA synthase (FASN).[ , ] In terms of cholesterol biosynthesis, acetyl-CoA must undergo various types of enzymatic reactions to transform into cholesterol under the regulation of sterol regulatory element-binding protein 2 (SREBP2), 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl CoA reductase (HMGCR), and squalene monooxygenase (SQLE).[ , , ]
Moreover, to adapt to the human body’s dynamic demand for lipids, lipids can eventually tend to be stored in hepatocytes or transported to other cells. In vivo, FAs and cholesterol are, respectively, converted into triacylglycerols (TAGs) and cholesterol esters (CEs) via esterification reaction before the formation of lipid droplets (LDs), which is their major form of storage.[ ] Besides, they can combine with apos to complete their mission in lipid metabolism in the form of a free state, getting to the destination under regulation of the signal transduction network.

## Lipid Metabolic Reprogramming in HCC
Lipid metabolic reprogramming is an emerging characteristic of cancers with wide-ranging implications.[ ] Lipid metabolic reprogramming has been observed in various types of cells in the TME, including immune cells, stromal cells, and tumor cells, which together shape the cancerous metabolic microenvironment.[ ] Focusing on the liver, alterations in the lipidomic characteristics have been certificated to be involved in the pathogenesis of various liver diseases including HCC,[ ] which exhibits unique alterations of different kinds of lipids.[ ] In the HCC microenvironment, HCC cells play a leading role in inducing changes in the TME. In studies of proteins implicated in HCC metastasis, such as nuclear protein 1 (NUPR1) and platelet-activating factor acetyl hydrolase 1B3 (PAFAH1B3), they have been found to modulate the lipid metabolism pathways.[ , ] Moreover, the study of lysine demethylase 6A (KDM6A) revealed further changes in the expression of key enzymes related to lipid metabolism.[ ] Based on these studies, we focus on the lipid metabolism in HCC cells to uncover the relationship between the liver microenvironment and primary HCC metastasis [Figure  ].
 

## Lipid intake and efflux
As previously mentioned, lipoproteins undertake the function of lipid transport, combining with their receptors to accomplish the mission from transport to intake. High-density lipoprotein binding protein (HDLBP) combines with high-density lipoprotein from the circulation, performing the biological function of regulating cellular cholesterol homeostasis, which has been found to be associated with the HCC metastasis when it is upregulated.[ ] Similarly, low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) influences cholesterol homeostasis via regulating the uptake of LDL. Downregulation of LDLR was found to foster the proliferation and metastasis abilities of HCC cells. High cholesterol diets attenuate lung metastasis in HCC model mice, whereas LDL suppresses the sterol regulatory element-binding protein cleavage-activating protein (SCAP) to restrict the migration of HCC cells in vitro, which indicates that cholesterol intake plays a vital role in HCC metastasis.[ ] Meanwhile, low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1B (LRP1B) is another member of the LDLR gene family, inducing HCC metastasis and drug resistance of doxorubicin and depressing the PERK-ATF4-CHOP pathway.[ ] Additionally, microRNA-148a (miR-148a) prevents lung colonization of HCC, with real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) findings indicating that genes related to FA intake were upregulated in miR-148a KO mice.[ ] ATP binding cassette subfamily A member 8, which has been established to regulate cholesterol efflux and HDL levels, is regulated by miR-374b-5p and impedes the metastasis of HCC. It performs this function via the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)/Zinc-finger E-binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1) signal pathway, whereby the key molecular mechanism may be the inhibition of ERK activation.[ ] In view of the bidirectional role of cholesterol intake in HCC metastasis, the activation of diverse signaling pathways and metabolic cascades induced by different cholesterol transporters needs further exploration.
As for FA intake, FA treatment can increase the expression of EMT markers in HCC cells. This research also found that FA treatment can induce transcriptional growth factor β (TGFβ) expression, activate matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9), and trigger the accumulation of β-catenin, which is associated with the malignant progression of HCC.[ ] In addition, the oxidized cholesterol derivative 25-hydroxycholesterol (25-HC) has been found to promote the metastasis of HCC. It can upregulate various signaling pathways, including toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), which increases the expression of FABP4 to upregulate MMPs in HepG2 cells.[ ] FABP5, another pivotal FABP mediating FA transport, was found not only to modulate the IL6/STAT3/VEGFA pathway in HCC cells, but also to regulate the tube formation, viability, and migration of human umbilical vein endothelial cells, indicating a potential angiogenic role of FABP5 in HCC.[ ] Moreover, Ma et al[ ] demonstrated that long intergenic noncoding RNA 01116 (LINC01116) blocks the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway to stabilize EWS RNA-binding protein 1 (EWSR1). Subsequently, EWSR1 acts as an m6A reader to regulate the stability of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α (PPARα), increasing the expression of FABP1 to enhance FA intake, which facilitates the metastasis and growth of HCC. Signaling pathway deficiency of thousand and one protein kinases 1 (TAOK1), associating with E-cadherin and impacting migration of HCC cells, interact with serine/threonine protein kinase 25 to induce the abundance of acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC). This study carried out a more refined metabolic analysis, which found that the interaction between these two proteins reduces the FA uptake rate and facilitates the secretion of very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) with TAG and β-oxidation, which has a pro-metastatic effect.[ ] Additionally, microRNA also plays a significant role in the lipid metabolism regulatory network. MiR-3180 has been demonstrated to inhibit the intake of lipids and reduce the lipid content, linking with HCC metastasis both in vivo and in vitro. It functions in a target-dependent way via targeting the CD36 3′-UTRs sequence.[ ] Critically, CD36 is another FA transport protein located in the cell membrane, which can increase the intake of FA through enhancing aldo–keto reductases family 1 member C2 expression to promote the proliferation and metastasis of HCC.[ ] Cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) derived from HSCs activates the mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase (MEK)/ extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT) signaling pathways mediated by CD36. Furthermore, it is also found to participate in EMT of HCC with the upregulation of E-cadherin, N-cadherin, and MMPs in these two pathways.[ ]
In conclusion, the exchange of lipids, especially intake, plays a complex role in HCC metastasis. In this field, different cell membrane surface receptors associated with lipid metabolism are the main factors regulating the intake and efflux of lipids, implying a crucial role in regulation of the intracellular lipid content in HCC cells, which makes them a potential drug target.

## Lipid biosynthesis

## FA synthesis
As the fundamental component of complex lipids, the synthesis of FAs has been extensively studied. According to Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis, long-chain acyl CoA synthetase 4 (ACSL4) is related to the cellular lipid content by influencing various lipid-converting enzymes to facilitate de novo lipogenesis (DNL). ACSL4 induces the upregulation of SREBP1 via c-Myc to mediate HCC cell proliferation and metastasis, implying that it may be a potential prognostic biomarker of HCC.[ ] MiR-3180 inhibits FA synthesis by acting on the 3′-UTR of stearoyl-CoA desaturase-1 (SCD1), which is a key enzyme of monounsaturated FA production and has been shown to be among the crucial downstream molecular targets through which miR-3180 inhibits the growth and metastasis of HCC cells.[ ] Analogously, miR-4130 restrains the expression of FASN and SCD1 to decrease lipogenesis in HCC cells by targeting their 3′-UTR, which in turn reduces invasion and migration.[ ] Moreover, knockdown of SCD1 induces lipid metabolic reprogramming and impedes HCC metastasis mediated by membrane fluidity, which is triggered by a mechanical pathway. The expression of SCD1 has a positive correlation with matrix stiffness through integrin β1/ focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling pathway activation and downregulation of SCD1 ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation, whereas the pro-metastatic effect induced by upregulation of SCD1 can be revised by oleic acid.[ ] Furthermore, combination treatment with N-nitrosodiethylamine (DEN) and a high-fat diet (HFD) induced both fibroblast growth factor 15 (FGF15)/fibroblast growth factor receptor 4 (FGFR4)/β-klotho and FGF19/FGFR4/β-klotho pathways and the upregulation of FASN expression, increasing DNL to activate epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) and Wnt/β-catenin signaling to accentuate the EMT of HCC, indicating the significant role of these molecular pathways in the carcinogenic transformation from NAFLD to HCC and further malignant progression.[ ]
As a type of noncoding RNA (ncRNA), long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are also widely involved in lipid metabolic reprogramming and tumor cellular migration. LncRNA RP11-386G11.10 targets the miR-345-3p/heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein U (HNRNPU) axis to aggravate HCC progression and FA synthesis, which is proved by the upregulation of de novo lipid synthase system including FASN, ACLY, ACC, and SCD. Interestingly, zinc finger and bric-à-brac (BTB) domain containing protein 7a, a transcription factor, can be enhanced by HNRNPU to bind with lncRNA RP11-386G11.10 promoter region, forming a positive feedback loop to accelerate lipid accumulation and progression of HCC.[ ] Peng et al[ ] identified a new type of lncRNA named FA synthesis-related lncRNA, acting on ACC1 to suppress its phosphorylation to promote FA biosynthesis and HCC metastasis via upstream stimulatory factor 1 binding with FA synthesis-related lncRNA superenhancer both in vivo and in vitro. Guided by transcriptomics and proteomics analysis, the knockdown of lncRNA metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 decreases the content of unsaturated FAs and TAG, inhibiting migration and invasion by regulating lipogenesis-related genes at the epigenetic and transcriptional levels.[ ] In addition to lncRNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs) were also found to contribute to the regulation of lipid metabolism associated with HCC metastasis. CircLARP1B interacts with heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein D to destabilize the mRNA of liver kinase B1, which induces the lipid metabolic reprogramming to mediate the pro-metastatic effect and lipid surplus resulting from increased lipid synthesis via regulating the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway.[ ]
SREBPs are a family of proteins that control diverse lipid metabolic pathways. DExD-box helicase 39B is a helicase participating in pre-mRNA splicin and maintenance of genomic stability. It restrains the ubiquitination and degradation of SREBP1 to reduce the de novo synthesis of FAs and facilitate HCC metastasis.[ ] Mitochondrial fission has been found to affect the lipid content of HCC cells, especially FA, combining with SREBP1 to enhance the expression of its downstream genes to promote lipogenesis, which has been established to be associated with HCC progression.[ ] Similarly, CD147, a type of transmembrane glycoprotein, specifically upregulates SREBP1c by activating the AKT/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathway, and increasing the expression of lipogenic enzymes such as ACC1, FASN, and SCD1. Conversely, knocking out CD147 can reverse the effect of lipogenesis on the promotion of HCC proliferation, migration, and invasion.[ ] Upregulated expression of Ephrin-A3 was observed in the hypoxic TME of HCC, where it can drive malignant progression. Ephrin-A3 acts on EphA2 to increase its tyrosine kinase activity, activating the downstream gene SREBP1-ACLY to mediate lipid metabolic reprogramming, which affects the malignant characteristics of HCC.[ ]

## Cholesterol synthesis
Cholesterol is another type of lipid involved in CE and cell membrane construction, while its content is mainly controlled by regulating synthesis. Under ER stress, T537 of SEC63 is phosphorylated and inhibits the ubiquitination of ACLY to maintain its stability, which enhances the biosynthesis of lipids, including cholesterol. In HCC, SEC63 upregulates Snail1 expression to facilitate the EMT, further leading to ACLY-dependent HCC metastasis.[ ] Under hypoxic conditions, the Ephrin-A3/EphA2 axis is activated and targets SREBP1-ACLY to impact the cholesterol content of HCC cells, which is related to their invasion capacity.[ ] Meanwhile, Lu et al[ ] identified that acyl-CoA thioesterase 12 (ACOT12) is downregulated in metastatic HCCs via genome-wide cDNA array analysis. The anti-metastatic effect of ACOT12 relies on the reduction of acetyl-CoA and histone H3 acetylation, indicating the relation to lipid biosynthesis, and the inhibition of TWIST2 expression in epigenetic formation impedes HCC metastasis.
In addition, exploring the interference of specific rate-limiting enzymes of cholesterol synthesis on HCC metastasis is also an important research direction. Overexpression of miR-148a exhibits antitumor activity vs. absence of it accelerates cholesterol accumulation both in serum and liver by directly targeting the expression of cholesterol biosynthetic genes, including HMGCR, indicating the potential of miR-148a as a new drug for liver diseases.[ ] SQLE is another rate-limiting enzyme that is highly expressed in HCC. The upregulation of hydroxyl-CoA dehydrogenase alpha subunit (HADHA) activates the SREBP2/HMGCR cascade to induce downstream genes, including mevalonate diphosphate decarboxylase and SQLE, which intensifies cholesterol synthesis and changes cell membrane fluidity to facilitate the invasion of HCC cells.[ ] Furthermore, transmembrane protein 147 interacts with signal transducer and activator of transcription factor 2 (STAT2) to activate 7-dehydrocholesterol reductase, driving the production of 27-hydroxycholesterol (27-OHC) to induce M2 polarization of macrophages, which aggravates the progression of HCC.[ ] The above researches show that these key enzymes in cholesterol synthesis extensively participate in immunomodulation, phenotypic remodeling, and cytostructural change to promote HCC metastasis, which reminds us of their prospect to become therapeutic targets in return.
P90 ribosomal S6 kinase 2 (RSK2) mutations are most commonly seen in HCC among cancers, and indicate a more aggressive phenotype. Restoring RSK2 expression in RSK2-deficient HCC cells remodels a negative feedback circuit relying on SOS1/2 to attenuate the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade, which decreases cholesterol biosynthesis and prevents HCC from escalating into a more aggressive phenotype in an mTOR-dependent manner, while inducing insensitivity to sorafenib compared with the RSK2-deficient group.[ ] Chen et al[ ] reported that downregulation of LDLR not only affects cholesterol intake but also mediates cholesterol biosynthesis via the MEK/ERK signaling pathway, resulting in angiogenesis and deterioration of HCC, which can be reversed by simvastatin in vitro. Zhao et al[ ] demonstrated that inhibition of cholesterol de novo synthesis induces changes in the expression of genes related to arachidonic acid metabolism, especially prostaglandin E synthase 2, which is regulated in an SREBP2-dependent manner, while its overexpression facilitates HCC metastasis. Moreover, sarcopenia is a common complication of HCC that in turn also promotes HCC progression. Chitinase-3 like-protein-1 secreted by muscle cells interacts with tumor necrosis factor receptor-1 to protect muscular tissue vs. induces oxidative stress and invasion and suppresses cholesterol metabolism in HCC.[ ]
Taken together, most studies on lipid biosynthesis revealed a positive correlation between cholesterol and FA synthesis and HCC metastasis. This result is consistent with previous findings on the serum and tumor lipid content in various other cancer types, confirming that enhancement of lipid synthesis can be a biomarker of metastasis and poor prognosis.[ ]

## Lipid catabolism

## FA catabolism
β-oxidation is the core process of FA catabolism involving carnitine acyl transfer 1 and FA β-oxidation system. Acyl-CoA oxidase 2 (ACOX2), a rate-limiting enzyme of β-oxidation, is discovered to be associated with HCC via data-independent acquisition quantitative proteomics and KEGG enrichment analysis. ACOX2 is downregulated in HCC tissues compared with adjacent non-cancerous tissues and restrains HCC migration by activating PPARα.[ ] In addition to the enhancement of de novo FA synthesis, mitochondrial fission downregulates the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma coactivator-1 alpha (PGC-1α)/PPARα signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting carnitine palmitoyl transferase 1A (CPT1A) and ACOX1 to control FAO, while mitochondrial fission suppresses NAD/Sirtuin 1 to reprogram FA metabolism during HCC progression.[ ] Overexpression of miR-132-3p attenuates solute carrier family 25 member 20 (SLC25A20) expression to hamper the transport of acylcarnitines, resulting in the suppression of FAO while inducing cell cycle arrest and promoting cell apoptosis to mediate HCC metastasis and growth, respectively.[ ] Furthermore, Ma et al[ ] discovered that nuclear accumulation of SREBP1 is mediated by high levels of caveolin-1, directly targeting medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (ACADM) to block the first step of FAO in mitochondria, which restrains the progression of HCC. This change can be reversed by combination therapy with an SREBP1 antagonist and sorafenib. In addition to the impact of de novo FA synthesis, CD147 attenuates the expression of PPARα via p38 MAPK signaling, decreasing CPT1A and ACOX1 to modulate FAO, which mediates the invasion of HCC.[ ] Likewise, Ma et al[ ] showed that ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2O (UBE2O) impels metastasis and proliferation. By contrast, HADHA has the opposite effect, and its expression is negatively correlated with that of UBE2O. The interaction mechanism of these two proteins is that UBE2O targets the K129 residue of HADHA to trigger its degradation, mediating the downregulation of lipogenic genes while upregulating genes involved in FAO as revealed by qPCR analysis, shaping a microenvironment conducive to lipolysis.
However, other studies on the connection between metastasis and FAO arrived at opposite conclusions. Yang et al[ ] reported that transcription factor Yin Yang 1 (YY1) mediates the upregulation of organic cation/carnitine transporter 2 (OCTN2), which facilitates mitochondrial biogenesis and enhances mitochondrial functions such as oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and FA β-oxidation, promoting HCC metastasis and other malignant features via PGC-1α. MiR-377-3p targets CPT1C 3′-UTR to inhibit FA transfer into mitochondria, thereby suppressing FAO which brings about HCC cell migration and growth.[ ] As a key stage of HCC progression, the EMT is regarded as a pivotal process and feature of metastasis. The regulatory role of miR-612 in lipid metabolic reprogramming of HCC has been established by Liu et al.[ ] Extensionally, miR-612 directly targets HADHA to control FAO in mitochondria and modulate the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which participates in the formation of invadopodia and EMT to promote the ability of metastasis in HCC, respectively. Zhao et al[ ] discovered that stromal interaction molecule 1 (STIM1) is more abundant in metastatic HCC and forms a negative feedback loop with Snail 1 to impact the EMT through the store-operated Ca(2+) entry (SOCE)/Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII)/AKT/ glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta (GSK-3β) signaling cascade. Specifically, STIM1 activates glycolysis and the FA synthesis pathway as well as liver kinase B1 (LKB1)/AMPK-dependent FAO, which is necessary for Snail 1-associated metastasis. Treatment with TGF-β induces EMT in HCC cell lines and gives rise to lipid reprogramming via the upregulation of FAO-related enzymes, except those related to FA transport into mitochondria. Moreover, it activates the TCA and OXPHOS while suppressing aerobic glycolysis.[ ] In addition, the absence of a general control of amino acid synthesis 5 like-1 reduces acetylation of FAO enzymes to intensify FAO, triggering high reactive oxygen species levels to mediate ERK/dynamin-related protein-1 (DPR1) signaling, which eventually induces mitochondrial fission, EMT, and HCC migration.[ ] Moreover, silencing of TAOK1 remarkably reduces the content of ACC to facilitate FAO and promote the EMT.[ ]
Additionally, ketogenesis is also an essential pathway of FA metabolism. Based on an analysis of proteomic data and clinical samples, Cui et al[ ] found that ketogenesis-related hydroxy-methyl-glutaryl-CoA lyase increases the content of β-hydroxy-butyric acid to facilitate acetylation at histone H3K9, mediating the transcription of dipeptidyl peptidase 4 to drive the process of ferroptosis and prevent the metastasis of HCC both in vivo and in vitro.
Thus, FAO showed completely opposite effects on HCC metastasis in different studies. The underlying mechanisms may be attributed to the degree of change in FAO activity and the imbalance of FA species caused by FAO.[ , ] Overall, more studies are needed to clarify the principles and mechanisms of lipid catabolism.

## Cholesterol catabolism
In the liver, catabolism of cholesterol is the procession from cholesterol to BA, producing the final metabolite, namely the intermediate metabolite such as 7α-hydroxycholesterol, 27-OHC, and some other constituents through classical pathway or alternative pathway. Mou et al[ ] developed a risk model related to lipid metabolism, discovering the downregulation of cytochrome P450 family 27 subfamily A member 1 (CYP27A1) in HCC samples, indicating a relation between BA biosynthesis and HCC.
Snail is a zinc-finger transcription factor that is widely regarded to participate in EMT to mediate HCC invasion. Notably, Snail has recently been found to suppress the synthesis of BAs via downregulating enzymes such as cytochrome P450 8 subfamily B member 1 (CYP8B1) and cytochrome P450 family 7 subfamily B member 1 (CYP7B1), indicating an underlying connection between HCC metastasis and cholesterol catabolism.[ ]In vitro, S-adenosylmethionine prevents lithocholic acid from inducing the upregulation of MAF bZIP transcription factor G, which has been established to suppress the synthesis of BA and promote the angiogenesis of HCC.[ ]
In addition, here we summarized the roles of different types of BAs in HCC metastasis, providing distinct consequences compared with changes in BA synthesis. Gao et al[ ] found that the total BA levels indicate a poor prognosis in HCC patients, while glycochenodeoxycholate mediates autophagy in HCC cells through the AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway, exhibiting a pro-metastatic effect both in vitro and in vivo. Gong et al[ ] identified a new kind of BA, elevating the expression of programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1) by inhibiting the farnesoid X receptor (FXR)/small heterodimer partner (SHP) axis and deriving exosomes to assist the formation of immunosuppressive microenvironment, which can increase the invasion capacity of HCC in vitro.
In conclusion, in the field of catabolism, most studies focus on FA catabolism and the research results show enormous heterogeneity in relation to HCC metastasis. In terms of cholesterol catabolism, the dual role of metabolic enzymes and metabolites in HCC metastasis may not be simply analyzed from the perspective of cholesterol catabolism itself. Since cholesterol catabolism and BA production constitute only a small portion of the liver cholesterol pool, different types of BAs may have a more significant impact, which needs to be explored in conjunction with other factors that regulate BA production. Tumor metastasis is an elaborate process consisting of multiple stages, whereby phenotypic transition and alternations in energy requirements in different periods may variously drive lipid catabolic reprogramming.

## Lipid storage
In humans, LDs are the primary organelle of lipid storage, controlling the excess lipids in cells as well as having other vital biological functions beyond energy storage. The biogenesis of LDs requires neutral lipids, mainly including TAGs and sterol esters, and is directly affected by intracellular lipid levels.
Overexpression of NUPR1 enhances the interaction with SREBP1 to promote its nuclear transport and expression, inducing the upregulation of FASN to mediate the DNL leading to the accumulation of LDs, which fosters the growth and metastasis of HCC.[ ] Cullin-associated and neddylation-dissociated 1 (CAND1) negatively modulates SKP1-Cullin-1-F-box protein 11 complex, which recruits heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A2/B1 to mediate its ubiquitination and degradation, increasing the expression of lipogenic enzymes to promote the accumulation of neutral lipids and LDs, which influences the malignant progression of HCC. Consequently, CAND1 has been identified as an oncogene of HCC.[ ] The NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase-1/p53 target SREBP1 forms a signaling axis to enhance the content of lipids, including TAG and cholesterol, and maintain Snail protein levels by restraining its ubiquitylation, thus mediating the EMT, angiogenesis, and proliferation while inhibiting autophagy in HCC, which makes it a potential prognostic biomarker.[ ] Liu et al[ ] reported that stomatin-like protein 2 (SLP2) could be a prospective prognostic biomarker for HCC. It can combine with c-Jun N-terminal kinase isoform 2 (JNK2) to inhibit its ubiquitylation and degradation, as well as be regulated by methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3) and YT521-B homology m6A RNA-binding protein 1. Specifically, SLP2 combines with JNK2 to facilitate the nuclear translocation of SREBP1, upregulating the lipid metabolism to manifest an increase in the number of intracellular LDs, which induces the invasion and migration of HCC. In a proteomics study sponsored by the Chinese Human Proteome Project consortium, researchers found that suppression of sterol O-acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1) downregulates the expression of metastasis-related receptors and cholesterol transporters, indicating that SOAT1 promotes HCC progression via regulating the cholesterol content of the plasma membrane.[ ] In another study, Zhu et al[ ] revealed that SOAT1 mediates cholesterol esterification to facilitate hepatocarcinogenesis, demonstrating the complicated role of SOAT1 in cholesterol metabolism and HCC. Moreover, hypoxia is a unique characteristic of the TME. Liu et al[ ] found that mesenchymal stem cells under hypoxic conditions (hypo-MSC) can promote HCC progression via a cyclooxygenase 2 and yes-associated protein (YAP) associated pathway. Hypo-MSC targets YAP to activate the AKT-mTOR-SREBP1 pathway, which enhances the content of neutral lipids, while prostaglandin E receptor 4 acts as the receptor of hypo-MSC-derived prostaglandin E2 to regulate lipogenesis and HCC progression.
The use of biological extracts has also made many new advances in the field of HCC remission by regulating lipid metabolism. Pan et al[ ] revealed that diosmetin inhibits the expression of cell cycle-related proteins and influences the metabolite related to FA synthesis and glycerolipid metabolism, which hampers the proliferation and metastasis of HCC. Cinobufacini injection not only reduces the synthesis of cholesterol but also the content of TAGs via the AMPK/SREBP1/FASN signaling pathway, illustrating the extensive role of this pathway in lipid metabolic reprogramming related to HCC proliferation and migration.[ ] High glucose treatment aiming to simulate the hyperglycemia in HCC induces the upregulation of FASN to increase the content of intracellular neutral lipids, activating MAPKs/NF-κB signaling and enhancing the expression of MMP2/9 to mediate inflammation, fibrosis, and invasion of HCC, which can be partially impeded by the polyphenol-rich leaf extract of Posidonia oceanica.[ ] Moreover, ncRNAs are also found in the role of regulating lipid metabolism in the context of HCC. Brain and muscle arnt-like protein 1, a circadian rhythm component associated with transcription factors and downregulated by miR-494-3p, inhibits the expression of mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase in an enhancer of zeste homolog 2-dependent manner, suppressing glycerolipid synthesis to limit HCC invasion and growth.[ ] High expression of lncARSR has been found in HFD model mice and HepG2 cells, where it specifically binds to YAP1 to inhibit its phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, thereby activating the insulin receptor substrate 2 (IRS2)/AKT signaling pathway. At the same time, lncARSR downregulates the expression of lipogenic enzymes to accelerate TAG accumulation, triggering the metastasis of HCC.[ ] Analogously, Wang et al[ ] reported that LINC01468 is upregulated in NAFLD-associated HCC. It can be upregulated by α-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase alkB homologue 5-related m6A modification and activates the AKT/mTOR pathway, whereas mediates src homology 2-containing inositol 5-phosphatase 2 ubiquitinated degradation, inducing the malignant development and increasing sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs through lipid accumulation.
In summary, intracellular lipid storage is conducive to HCC metastasis, consistent with the metabolic preferences of tumor cells.[ ] The extent of intracellular lipid accumulation is determined by multiple factors, such as the increase of lipid synthesis and the upregulation of lipid esterification enzymes. Interfering with different signaling pathways to block the process from lipid synthesis to lipid storage is an attractive research direction to disturb the positive feedback loop of HCC progression.

## Lipid Metabolism as Therapeutic Targets
Lipid metabolism is extensively involved in the overall progression of HCC, including hepatocarcinogenesis, proliferation, and metastasis. Accordingly, lipid metabolism has been considered as a promising therapeutic target.

## Targets in lipid metabolism of HCC
Dabrafenib is an orally administered selective inhibitor of RAF kinases without established antitumor activity in HCC. Interestingly, the downregulation of HDLBP increased the antitumor effect of dabrafenib and realized the inhibition of metastasis.[ ] Tang et al[ ] constructed an obesity-induced NAFLD mouse model via a high fat–high carbohydrate diet. The authors also used a high cholesterol diet to detect the quantity and function of natural killer T cells in NAFLD and explore the underlying mechanism. Indeed, the research demonstrated that SREBP2 mediates the cholesterol accumulation causing a reduction in the abundance of NKT cells and loss of liver immunosurveillance function in the NAFLD microenvironment, inducing obesity-promoted HCC progression by lowering cholesterol levels, with concomitant resistance against vistusertib and statins. In addition, Liu et al[ ] found that terbinafine, a traditional medicine used to treat fungal infections, remarkably reduces the accumulation of cholesterol and CE in the liver by specifically targeting SQLE and inducing its degradation, blocking the progression from NAFLD to HCC. Interestingly, combination treatment with PD173074 and ETC-1002, which are inhibitors of FGFR1 and ACLY, respectively, showed distinguished efficacy against metastasis in HCC model mice, indicating the potential of FGFRs and regulation of the cholesterol content as a therapeutic target in vivo.[ ] Depletion of eukaryotic initiation factor 6 (eIF6) enhances FAO and inhibits DNL via a YY1-associated alternative translation pathway and translation of lipogenic transcription factors, respectively, inducing the procession from NAFLD to HCC. The inhibitors eIFsixty-1 and eIFsixty-6 block the binding of eIF6 to the 60 S subunit, partially realizing the function of eIF6 depletion.[ ]
Additionally, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) are a common tool in basic research for downregulating target genes. The deficiency of FASN compensatively activates the HMGCR/SREBP2 axis to enhance cholesterol synthesis. As expected, the use of siRNAs in vivo as well as the application of C75 and atorvastatin in vitro remarkably restrained HCC growth, demonstrating that concurrent targeting of FASN and HMGCR may be an effective therapeutic strategy.[ ] Peng et al[ ] reported that the use of siRNA targeting ACSL4 in conjunction with the knockout of METTL5 showed a significant curative effect by reducing the intracellular abundance of FAs, LDs, TAGs, and cholesterol while impeding tumor progression. Thus, these two genes play a synergistic role in lipid metabolic reprogramming, and targeting both of them at the same time may be a novel therapeutic strategy for HCC treatment at the translational level. Zuo et al[ ] developed a new-type nanoplatform based on polymeric nanoparticles and poly (lactic/glycolic) acid, encapsulating si-LINC00958 and spermidine, which can remarkably suppress the growth and prolong the overall survival of HCC model mice without prominent hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity. Zhang et al[ ] utilized the adeno-associated virus injections to knockdown CAND1, downregulating the expression of downstream genes, including FASN, ACC1, and ACLY. This had a restraining effect on HCC growth by reducing the accumulation of lipids, which demonstrates that regulation of the CAND1-SCFFBXO11-hnRNPA2B1 axis may be a potential therapeutic target. SR8278 elevates the expression of BMAL1 at the transcriptional level via competitively antagonizing the nuclear receptor REV-ERB, inhibiting the growth and lung metastasis of HCC.[ ] In addition, avasimibe targets SOAT1 to modulate cholesterol metabolism and influence the progression of HCC, indicating the potential of SOAT1 as a therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker.[ ] Cinobufacini injection attenuates the M2 polarization of macrophages and delays HCC progression by the AMPK/SREBP1/FASN pathway, showing the powerful therapeutic effect of reversing suppressive TME and the capacity to regulate lipid metabolism.[ ] Besides, 3β-hydroxysteroid-Δ24 is downregulated by genkwadaphnin to reductase its expression and activation, blocking cholesterol synthesis derived from it to restrain HCC progression in vivo and in vitro models, which is correlated with poor prognosis.[ ]
As described in this section, numerous targets associated with lipid metabolism have been identified in preclinical studies. The related literature indicates broad target selection among multiple stages of lipid metabolism with varying degrees of encouraging efficacy. However, more reliable evidence is needed to translate these hopeful targets and novel drugs from experiments in animal models to clinical applications.

## Influence of lipid metabolic reprogramming on the efficacy of systematic therapy
The American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases presented updated clinical guidelines for HCC in May 2023. In the section on systemic therapy, the approved drugs can be broadly divided into anti-angiogenesis targeted therapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).[ ] Sorafenib, lenvatinib, and donafenib serve as first-line therapies in USA or China. Notably, these multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (mTKIs) have also been discovered to be influenced by lipid metabolic reprogramming.[ , ]
Guo et al[ ] reported that low expression of KDM6A leads to drug resistance against lenvatinib, indicating that KDM6A may be a biomarker for clinical decision-making and prognosis for patients on a lenvatinib therapeutic schedule. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/STAT3/ATP Binding Cassette Subfamily B Member 1 (ABCB1) signaling pathway mediates the lenvatinib tolerance through enhancing the exocytosis of HCC with abnormally elevated cholesterol synthesis and lipid rafts activation of cerium oxide nanoparticles, which can be reversed by erlotinib to overcome lenvatinib tolerance, providing a prospective combination therapy.[ ]
As for sorafenib, there is more extensive research on it in terms of combination therapy, for which it is the first effective systemic therapy for advanced-stage HCC patients.[ ] Ma et al[ ] discovered the inhibitory effect of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) on SREBP1 maturity. Moreover, its combination therapy with sorafenib reveals a more obvious effect compared with the monotherapy of each drug in the subcutaneous xenograft mouse model, suggesting that SREBP1 antagonist promotes the perturbation of sorafenib in HCC. Ding et al[ ] found that aramchol exhibits synergy with donafenib to facilitate the ferroptosis of patient-derived HCC organoids, while it identified high expression of unconventional prefoldin RPB5 interactor (URI) and SCD1 triggering sorafenib resistance in HCC patients, especially in the p53 wild-type subclass. Mildronate, a cardioprotective medication, selectively antagonizes OCTN2, thereby inhibiting the growth and lung metastasis of HCC when compared with the control group. Moreover, when combined with sorafenib, it shows enhanced therapeutic efficacy compared with the administration of each drug individually.[ ] RSK2-mutant HCC cell lines exhibit a more progressive phenotype with higher sensitivity to sorafenib compared with the RSK2 wild-type tumor cells. Furthermore, the promotion of cholesterol synthesis can be restrained by treatment with U0126 or rapamycin, respectively, targeting MEK and mTOR.[ ] Additionally, ND-654 is an allosteric inhibitor that selectively inhibits ACC to mimic the ACC phosphorylation, suppressing DNL resulting in the delaying of HCC development and enhancing the curative effect of sorafenib, especially in S2 subclass.[ ] Interestingly, maprotiline, an atypical antidepressant, targets cellular retinoic acid binding protein 1 (CRABP1) to attenuate cholesterol biosynthesis through the MAPK/ERK pathway and phosphorylation of SREBP2, blocking the EMT of HCC and enhancing the curative effect of sorafenib.[ ]
In addition to mTKIs, immunotherapy is another significant effective therapy for advanced HCC, whose prime targets includes programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), PD-L1, and cytotoxic T lymphocyte–associated protein 4 (CTLA4).[ ] Pan et al[ ] found that METTL3 upregulated the mRNA expression of SCAP in an m6A-dependent manner, facilitating cholesterol synthesis to attenuate CD8 T cell function. Meanwhile, this research revealed that hepatocyte-specific knock-in of METTL3 regulates antitumor immunity by facilitating cholesterol synthesis in NAFLD-HCC tissues, while combining with METTL3 inhibitor can enhance the sensibility of anti-PD-1 therapy to restrain NAFLD-HCC. Additionally, Zhu et al[ ] estimated that the efficacy of the combination of CD36 inhibitor and anti-PD-1 therapy is notably higher than each single agent, reshaping the immunosuppressive TME of HCC. Among ncRNAs, LINC01116 shows potential as a promising therapeutic target. Antisense oligonucleotides targeting LINC01116 improved the curative effect of anti-PD-1 therapy in a mouse model, regulating antitumor immunity and inducing tumor regression of HCC.[ ] Simvastatin, an inhibitor of cholesterol synthesis, intensifies the efficacy of PD-L1 antibodies to synergistically resist HCC metastasis and proliferation when applied via LSEC-targeted nano-delivery.[ ]
ICIs and mTKIs are most momentous drugs of systemic therapy. The clinical researches and clinical practice show that the sensitivity, validity of drugs, and drug resistance in patients are barriers to prolong patients’ survival. In mice model and HCC cell lines, drugs targeting lipid metabolism can bolster the efficacy of mTKIs while it can remodel TME to recover antitumor immunity and the sensitivity of ICIs. More importantly, the great achievements in basic research provide a new perspective for future combination therapy and conversion therapy.

## Discussion and Conclusions
Over the past decades, the role of lipid metabolism in cancer metastasis was gradually revealed. From initially engaging energy metabolism to influencing cell signal transduction, the understanding of lipid metabolism in HCC metastasis has deepened. Currently, numerous studies focus on each particular lipid metabolic process or key enzymes aiming to unravel the interaction network and crosstalk mechanisms between them and HCC metastasis in this new perspective.
Despite these momentous theoretical advances, there are still many unanswered questions waiting to be solved. The Warburg effect is the most far-reaching characteristic of cancer cells, offering sufficient acetyl-CoA for lipogenesis, which supports the findings of lipid accumulation both in lipid biosynthesis and storage.[ ] Meanwhile, discoveries in lipid transport and catabolism exhibit vast heterogeneity among studies, indicating the complexity of lipid metabolism and its dynamic balance to adapt to the demands at different stages of HCC metastasis. For example, virus-related HCC and NAFLD-HCC have different proportions of metabolic subtypes and aberrant signaling, which are driven by virus-associated molecular alterations vs. oxidative stress and immune response induced by lipids.[ ] Moreover, the roles of diverse metabolic types in HCC metastasis could not be independently confirmed. Compared with the metabolism of glucose and amino acids, the characteristics of lipid metabolism reprogramming seem a more credible predictor. This is especially true for FAO, however, its high heterogeneity brings some challenges for clinical translation.[ ] Therefore, linking lipid metabolism with other components of the liver microenvironment to form an unambiguous interaction network is an important way to better dissect HCC metastasis.
In terms of treatment, basic research has shown the extraordinary potential of mTKIs and ICIs, which is in stark contrast to the somewhat lagging clinical research and drug development. Nowadays, there are a number of inhibitors targeting multiple molecules in stages of basic, preclinical, or clinical trials, including FASN, ACLY, and SCD1.[ ] Unfortunately, there are few clinical studies or approved drugs targeting lipid metabolism in HCC. Moreover, research based on multi-omics analysis can elucidate the relation between different metabolic subtypes of FA degradation and multiple types of therapies, including sorafenib, TACE, and ICIs. This indicates the potential of lipidomic signatures as biomarkers of treatment options and evaluation of therapeutic effects.[ ] Additionally, modulating lipid metabolic reprogramming via the utilization of ncRNA therapy or epigenetic ways may be two promising research orientations, especially affecting the stability of key enzymes related to lipid metabolism through the pathway of ubiquitination.[ ]
Indeed, the development of targeted drugs for the modulation of lipid metabolism is extremely promising. However, the high heterogeneity of HCC and the microenvironmental diversity of lipid metabolism cast a shadow over the actual efficacy of these drugs. Thus, there is still a long way to go before drugs targeting lipid metabolism combined with immunotherapy and mTKIs become an effective therapy for HCC. Overall, the fundamental research based on lipid metabolism gives us the hope to deconstruct HCC at multiple levels. In the foreseeable future, it is imperative to accelerate the clinical translation of laboratory achievements, which positions lipid metabolism as an entry point for HCC treatment.
