## Abstract
We describe in this review the historical evidence leading up to the concept and design of Vigil and subsequent clinical applications including safety and efficacy in a randomized, controlled Phase IIB trial. Vigil (gemogenovatucel-T) is a unique triple function targeted immunotherapy that demonstrates preclinical and clinical systemic anticancer activity. Construction of Vigil involves harvest of autologous malignant tissue for neoantigen targeting (ideally containing clonal neoantigens) followed by a two-day process involving transfection with a plasmid to provide a permissive ‘training environment’ for the patient's immune system. Transfected plasmid components contain an expressive human GMCSF DNA segment to enhance anticancer immune functional response and a second component expressing bi-shRNAfurin which reduces TGFβ isomers (TGFβ1 and TGFβ2) thereby reducing cancer inhibition of the targeted immune response. Results generated to date justify advancement to confirmatory clinical trials supporting product regulatory approval.
Keywords: : anticancer, bi-shRNA, cancer, clonal neoantigen, immunotherapy, ovarian, plasmid, vigil

## Plain Language Summary
Vigil is an anticancer treatment that employs three methods of enhancing the body's immune system to identify and kill cancer cells. The construction of Vigil involves cancer cells from the same person being treated (personalized therapy) in combination with added anticancer genetic signals to enhance the number and function anti-anticancer immune cells and to guide the immune cells to the cancer and not to normal organs of the body. In this manner, an army of immune cells are created that can move to attacking the cancer using blood vessels to get to the cancer anywhere it tries to grow in the body. One study (Phase I) performed with this product to determine safety and dose range demonstrated an optimal dose and schedule. Another study (Phase IIA) showed initial clinical benefit. A third more complex study (Phase IIB) in patients treated with Vigil compared with standard of care without Vigil demonstrated the ability to prolong the patients life and time without their cancer getting worse without any significant side effects associated with the treatment in a unique subset of ovarian cancer patients, those with the ability to repair their DNA. Based on the composite of these results, Vigil is an attractive targeted immunotherapy justified for late-stage clinical testing.

## Vigil: mechanism
Vigil is constructed from autologous tumor cells transfected with a plasmid encoding a GMCSF expressive unit, bi-shRNA directed to furin and clonal neoantigens. Clonal neoantigens are associated with increased clinical benefit to immune therapies.

Vigil plasmid is produced using GMP manufacturing.

Vigil is constructed via a 2 day manufacturing process consisting of tissue dissociation, electroporation and irradiation.

## Preparation of the drug product
The drug substance is formulated to appropriate concentration using freeze media. Every Vigil batch/lot is tested for product release criteria which has been verified for regulatory suitability.

## Vigil clinical experience
230 patients have received 1433 doses over various clinical trials in solid tumors.

## Vigil Phase I testing in solid tumor cancer
Phase I testing of Vigil demonstrated manufacturing optimization, appropriate dose, safety and immune response in advanced solid tumor patients.

## Vigil Phase I/II testing in Ewing's sarcoma
Vigil has been tested as monotherapy and in combination with temozolomide/irinotecan in Ewings sarcoma and demonstrated safety and evidence of efficacy.

## Vigil Phase IIA/IIB evaluation in ovarian cancer
In Phase IIA trial in newly diagnosed Stage IIIB/IV ovarian cancer patients, Vigil demonstrated immune activation via ELISpot assay which correlated with clinical benefit.

In both trials, Vigil demonstrated no ≥ Grade 3 adverse events.

Several biomarkers have been identified retrospectively including TP53 mutation, and high ENTPD1 expression.

Vigil demonstrates a strong safety profile combined with durable clinical benefit.

## Background
Vigil is a novel immunotherapy [ ] constructed with a unique dual GMCSF-wildtype/bi-shRNAfurin plasmid transfected into autologous tumor tissue. It is designed to specifically deliver personalized clonal neoantigen targets. The mechanistic purpose of Vigil therapy is to expand “educated” CD8+ T cell neoantigen-targeting effector cells within the tumor microenvironment. By concurrently introducing the immunostimulatory cytokine GMCSF and reducing immunosuppressive blockade related to TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 at the intradermal site of administration, Vigil also enhances the specificity of the dendritic and T cell targeting [ ] in association with clinical demonstration of statistically significant recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS) benefit combined with minimal off target toxicity. Extensive Phase I testing involving 80 advanced recurrent solid tumor patients with 19 different histologic cancer types characterized safety, verified systemic immune response utilizing enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assay, supported dose selection and suggested early durable survival advantage in patients achieving immune activation as defined by ELISpot response [ , ]. Subsequently, a Phase IIA trial in newly diagnosed ovarian cancer [ , ] and separate studies in recurrent Ewing's Sarcoma [ ] revealed further evidence of durable survival advantage compared with standard of care. Clinical evidence of Vigil and checkpoint inhibitor combination also support combination therapeutic strategies [ , ]. Results of Phase IIB study involving patients with newly diagnosed ovarian cancer provided further evidence of RFS and OS benefit in the BRCA-wildtype (wt) ovarian cancer population [ ] and, most significantly, in the BRCA-wt population [ , , ] with homologous recombination proficient (HRP) profiling, a subpopulation in ovarian cancer with unmet medical need.
We recently published the importance of cancer clonal neoantigens in advanced stage disease patients as a predictor of effective anticancer immunotherapy [ ]. Results support targeting of clonal neoantigens as a critical component for success of immunotherapy and provide the rationale to focus treatment with immune response modifying therapy toward cancer patients with tumors expressing a high proportion of clonal neoantigens. Clonal neoantigen enrichment appears more frequently in tumors with HRP profile consequent to maintenance of DNA repair, which likely explains the enhanced clinical benefit of Vigil demonstrated among newly diagnosed and recurrent ovarian cancer patients with HRP molecular profile tumors [ , ].
In this review, we characterize results of historical, preclinical and clinical work related to relevant Vigil product cytokine (GMCSF, TGFβ) expression and cognate plasmid based cellular immunotherapy products (GVAX, Lucanix, TAG). We will assess the role of Vigil in induction of cancer specific targeting effector cells and correlation with early clinical results and long term clinical follow-up of Vigil. Finally, future direction of investigating Vigil's mechanism of action with relationship to clonal neoantigen expression and HRP profile ovarian cancer patients will be discussed.

## Rationale of tumor antigen based immunotherapy
A critical component of immunotherapy in cancer management involves targeting the immune response against cancer cells rather than normal cells. Simply put, target the appropriate target. Robust evidence has demonstrated markers more highly expressed on cancer cells and unique to the surface of cancer cells compared with normal cells from the same organ from which the cancer cells were derived [ , , ]. Some of these markers are known as tumor associated antigens (TAA) [ ]. TAAs are more highly expressed on cancer cells but are also expressed at low levels on normal cells. Sipuleucel-T for example is a successful autologous dendritic cell immunotherapy demonstrating improved survival in patients with metastatic prostate cancer based on TAA principle [ ]. There are also tumor specific antigens (TSA) that are found only on cancer cells and not on normal cells. Peptides of TSAs and TAAs are expressed on the cancer surface. Some via the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) others outside of MHC expression. TAA and TSA surface expression has been the focus of numerous immunotherapies. However, consistent, validated study results have generally met with limited clinical success.
More recently however additional evidence, has linked immune sensitivity to cancer specific targets related to unique TSA mutations within tumor cells [ , ]. These involve “clonal” mutations (or targets) that play a role in initiating cancer phenotype over normal cell phenotype. These contain driver mutations that are identified on all cancer cells as opposed to most cancer mutations which involve only subpopulations of cancer cells (subclonal) [ , , , ]. Also not all clonal mutations are surface expressed. Retrospective evidence involving checkpoint inhibitor response supports consideration of clonal neoantigen [ , , ] and/or clonal tumor mutation burden (cTMB) [ ] targeting as a potentially impactful biomarker in defining response to immunotherapy. Design of Vigil strategically incorporates autologous tumor tissue in order to preserve clonal neoantigen display.

## Historical background of GMCSF/TGFβ plasmid based immunotherapies
Extensive historical evidence support the role of GMCSF in relation to anticancer immune response [ ]. GMCSF protein [Leukine (sargramostim)] is a well explored immune regulatory cytokine with capacity to functionally enhance anticancer immune response via its influence on growth, differentiation and functional enhancement of immune effector cells. Activity includes augmentation of antigen presentation, increased expression of effector cell MHC II expression and amplification of T cell proliferation. More than 20 years ago, overall survival benefit related to combination of myeloid growth stimulatory activity and anticancer functional enhancement activity results lead to FDA supported product registration of Leukine (recombinant human GMCSF) in autologous marrow transplant patients who developed marrow graft failure [ ]. Results also lead to a prophylactic Leukine indication in patients with non-Hodgkin's lymphoma undergoing autologous BMT [ ].
Intracellular expression of GMCSF via plasmid transfer to autologous cancer cells is associated with enhanced immunologic activity related to intracellular facilitation of MHC expression of neoantigens [ , ]. This activity assists the dendritic – T cell interaction toward T cell receptor (TCR) development and education specifically targeting clonal neoantigens [ ]. Clinical trials exploring GMCSF plasmid-based immunotherapy (GVAX) show evidence of benefit in correlation with higher dose of GMCSF secretion (>40 mg/million cells, 24 h) [ , ]. However, confirmatory trials failed to demonstrate sufficient clinical response to justify FDA registration of GVAX. Although encouraging long-term activity, suggestive of induced anticancer memory revealed significant overall survival correlation in one study in patients with product secreting high GMCSF levels (>40 mg/day per 106 GVAX cells) [ ]. Results with sipuleucel-T (Provenge®, Dendreon Pharmaceuticals, Seal Beach, CA), a combined product of autologous dendritic cells loaded with engineered fusion protein of combined prostatic acid phosphatase and GMCSF, did also successfully demonstrate survival advantage in advanced prostate cancer patients and results led to FDA registration of that GMCSF-based product [ ]. Long term follow-up demonstrates that 38% more patients with advanced prostate cancer who received Provenge® were alive 3 years post treatment compared with standard of care (SOC) control, thereby supporting long-term benefit related to GMCSF activity. Additional clinical evidence of immune inducing anticancer benefit related to GMCSF is also demonstrated in another FDA-approved technology employing GMCSF plasmid. Talimogene Laherparepvec (OncoVEXmGMCSF, also known as T-VEC, Amgen, Thousand Oaks, CA) which is an oncolytic herpes simplex virus encoding GMCSF [ ]. Results of the Phase III study (OPTIM trial) in advanced injectable but not resectable Stage IIIB/C and IV melanoma revealed durable response benefit in patients receiving tumor injected OncoVEX® vs. GMCSF protein (16.3%, 48/295 patients vs. 2.1%, 3/141 patients, p = 0.0001) [ , ]. These results lead to FDA approval of OncoVEX® in unresectable advanced melanoma in 2015. Long term follow-up continued to demonstrate durable response rate advantage to OncoVEX® over GMCSF protein (19.3% vs. 1.4%) and also demonstrated OS advantage with longer follow-up in the OPTIM trial to OncoVEX® (median 23.3 months vs. 18.9 months). Mechanistically these results support benefit relating to GMCSF expression combined with enhancement of neoantigen exposure related to oncolysis with OncoVEX® [ ]. Moreover, another oncolytic viral product, CG0070 using a conditionally replication proficient adenovirus 5 (Ad5) viral particle involved in late-stage registration trial, also employs a GMCSF transgene component and E2F promoter. Phase II study has demonstrated a complete response rate of 47% [ ] in early stage (CIS, Ta, T1) non muscle invasive bladder cancer at 6 month assessment and in Phase I trial overall complete response (CR) of 48.6% [ , ].
One explanation for the variability in response to GVAX was thought to be related to uncontrolled cancer immune suppression via cytokines such as TGFβ that counterbalance the immunostimulatory activity of GVAX. Belagenpumatucel-L (Lucanix®, NovaRx Corp., Wayne, PA) was designed to limit knockdown of TGFβ2 via use of antisense DNA technology. Results in advanced non-small cell lung cancer demonstrated positive clinical response data but only in limited subsets of patients [ ]. Phase III trial of Lucanix® was also insufficient for product registration [ , ] although clinical benefit may have been limited by lack of anticancer functional immune enhancement (lack of GMCSF effect) and lack of TGFβ1 knockdown.
Our rationale for Vigil construction was based in part on strengths and limitations of prior single modality treatment seen with GVAX and Lucanix® results ( ). We hypothesized that while upregulation of GMCSF and silencing of TGFβ2 alone via plasmid cellular platform each provide limited clinical benefit, synergistic immune function modulation by concurrent impact on both cytokines would enhance immune response and may improve anticancer activity [ ]. We consequently constructed a plasmid containing both the GMCSF expressive sequence and the TGFβ2 antisense sequence previously utilized in single modality clinical trials of GVAX and Lucanix®. We identified this plasmid as “TGFβ autologous tumor tissue-GMCSF product” (TAG). Nineteen advanced refractory cancer patients were treated [ , , ]. No Grade 3/4 toxicity was demonstrated to TAG following 93 injections. Significant increases in GMCSF expression and knockdown of TGFβ2 but not TGFβ1 (TAG was not designed to knockdown TGFβ1) were demonstrated as components of product release criteria. Study results demonstrated that 11 of 17 (65%) evaluable patients maintained prolonged stable disease. One patient achieved CR. In summary, TAG demonstrated clinical safety and evidence of benefit (BB-IND-13650) justifying further exploration [ ].
 Given that furin, the proprotein convertase required for export/secretion of both TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 isoforms [ ], is necessary for TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 expression, we conceived that knockdown of furin would potentially knockdown both TGFβ1 and TGFβ2. In vitro testing of the furin knockdown and downstream TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 expression was shown to be successful using a newly discovered proprietary bifunctional short hairpin RNA interference plasmid technology (bi-shRNAfurin) which we found to be greater in activity when compared with siRNA against the same target [see next section, [ ]]. We then constructed a dual plasmid that expressed both wildtype GMCSF and bi-shRNAfurin and engineered autologous cancer cells to produce both GMCSF and furin knockdown, thereby inhibiting secretion of both TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 [ ].

## Bi-shRNA mechanism
Bi-functional shRNA manipulates gene silencing machinery within cancer cells to improve efficiency and durability of target knockdown [ ]. Gene specific (mRNA specific) targeted shRNAs can be designed so that siRNA and miRNA like molecules can be effectively processed and loaded onto both the cleavage-dependent and the cleavage-independent RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) ( ). The purpose of RISC is to degrade target mRNA (i.e. furin if so designed) which reduces levels of transcript available to be translated by ribosomes and in such a manner prevents or knocks down mRNA and protein expression of the target. The differential processing of the bi-shRNAi is mediated by two pathways primarily dependent on strand complementarity and/or access to RNase-H cleavage. We have demonstrated that bifunctional shRNA can achieve a higher level of efficacy and greater durability of response compared with siRNA or miRNA alone, with a more rapid rate of expression silencing compared with shRNA or siRNA [ ]. The ‘bifunctional’ shRNA, by virtue of being able to load onto multiple types of RISCs, simultaneously target mRNA degradation while concurrently inhibiting translation through mRNA sequestration. By manipulating the double-stranded RNA structure, the bi-shRNA promotes loading of the guide strand onto both cleavage-dependent RISC and cleavage-independent RISC, as such the target mRNA is silenced through both mRNA degradation and translational inhibition [ ].
 The bi-shRNA expression unit is constructed of two stem-loop shRNA functional components. One is a fully matched passenger and guide strand for cleavage-dependent RISC loading and the other is a mis-matched passenger strand for cleavage-independent RISC loading. The bi-functional shRNA mechanism simulates natural processes by target mediation of mRNA knockdown through both siRNA and miRNA RNAi pathways and complexes. The active component of RISCs, involve Ago1, 2, 3 and 4 [ ].
RNAi therapeutics have also been shown to be reasonably well tolerated in animal models [ , ]. Also, over 10 RNAi-based drugs have entered clinical trial [ ], two are oncology related; one against the M2 subunit of ribonucleotide reductase (RRM2) [ ] and the other against tenascin-C [ ]. Animal studies with siRNA inhibitors to RRM2 reveal benefit [ ] and safety in primates [ ]. Treatment of hepatitis B with shRNA was also recently approved for clinical trial by the FDA. Currently there are now four RNAi-based therapeutics approved by the FDA; patisiran, givosiran, lumasiran and inclisiran.

## Vigil: mechanism
Vigil is composed of autologous tumor cells harvested from patients at the time of surgery transfected with a dual plasmid expression unit of wildtype GMCSF and bi-shRNAfurin. Harvested cells, transfected with the Vigil plasmid, encodes the wildtype GMCSF protein and simultaneously knocks down furin which is the critical convertase responsible for TGFβ isoforms (specifically TGFβ1 and TGFβ2) production [ , ]. Vigil thus provides an optimal environment for immune anticancer activity utilizing neoantigens (including clonal neoantigens in particular) facilitated by local dendritic and T cell stimulating activity related to GMCSF concurrently with deconvolution of cancer suppressive activity related to knockdown of TGFβ1 and 2 [ ]. Vigil treatment is administered as an intradermal injection to maximize exposure to dendritic cells which are highly concentrated in the dermis layer. Vigil product displays the personal clonal neoantigens which optimize the dendritic cell response and T cell education against malignant disease ( ). Antitumor immunity thus results from local and intracellular GMCSF expression to enhance neoantigen MHC expression while simultaneously increasing local secreted GMCSF to attract dendritic cell response toward the locally concentrated GMCSF at the injected Vigil site. Results of Vigil design and mechanism were validated by ELISpot assay. ELISpot reactivity was also shown to persist for up to a year in many patients after Vigil discontinuation suggesting evidence of memory cell expansion.
 Ultimately expansion of effector cells containing “trained” TCR regions able to bind and react to relevant neoantigens on all cancer cells (clonal neoantigens) is the designed purpose of Vigil product. Subclonal neoantigens which are not contained on all cancer cells have been shown to be less or not effective for immune targeting. Further evidence of support to clonal neoantigen targeting has been well demonstrated by measurement of the clonal tumor mutation burden (cTMB) prior to CPI therapy in which high correlation of overall survival benefit has been demonstrated in patients with high cTMB and high clonal neoantigens compared with low levels or subclonal targets involving natural response after high risk early stage cancer resection or following CPI therapy [ , , , , ]. Most mutation-driven neoantigens are derived from passenger mutations, which are divergent subclonal mutations acquired following clonal induction of cancer driver mutation. Subclonal mutations can drive cancer progression of cancer subpopulations where they provide a selective genetic advantage in even more aggressive tumor growth and/or spread. However, subclonal neoantigens are not incorporated within all cancer cells. Thus, targeting subclonal neoantigens are not likely an effective long term anticancer strategy [ , , , , ]. Furthermore, increasing the expression of subclonal neoantigens has been shown to hinder cognate peptide-MHC complex affinity and actually limit the immune response [ ]. UVB mutagenesis studies in syngeneic mice by Wolf et al. showed that a reduction of subclonal neoantigens correlates with response improvement in correlation with lower tumor growth [ ]. Clinical data also show cancer patients with high tumor subclonal neoantigen content receiving CPIs show a decrease in OS compared with similar patients with lower subclonal neoantigen content and high clonal neoantigen burden [ , ].

## Vigil construction & product delivery
The Vigil manufacturing is a two-day process ( ) which starts in the operating room utilizing a sterile container with proprietary medium for collection of cancer-derived tissue (typically solid tumor and/or pleural/ascites fluid) from a single subject. The tissue is then shipped to the central manufacturing facility (Gradalis, Inc, TX, USA). There the tissue is mechanically and enzymatically dissociated into a single cell suspension. Tissue-derived cells are counted and subsequently transfected with the Vigil plasmid via electroporation at a proprietary optimized frequency. Vigil plasmid is a 2 stem-loop structure with a miR-30 backbone. It contains a complete complementary guiding strand and passenger strand and has two base pair mismatches at positions 11 and 12 (for furin targeting) of the passenger strand. Transfected cells are then incubated overnight (14–22 h). Further description of Vigil plasmid design and function have been published [ , ].
 Transcription of the Vigil plasmid into patient tumor cells demonstrated GMCSF protein production and concomitant TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 knockdown beyond product release threshold. Transfection is accomplished by electroporation of the tumor cells using a Bio-Rad electroporator. Plasmid is combined with 2 × 107 cells in a sterile 0.4 cm gap cuvette and an exponential decay pulse waveform is applied. Following electroporation, tumor cells are incubated overnight at 37°C. The cells are incubated to allow transcription of the bi-shRNAfurin and the GMCSF mRNA. Following incubation, the cells are harvested, washed and enumerated by hemocytometer using the validated Trypan Blue dye exclusion assay. The harvested cell suspension is irradiated (100 Gy in an x-ray or gamma-ray irradiator) to create the final drug substance. The size of the batch (yield) is determined by the number of cells harvested, processed and irradiated, which is dependent on the variable amount of available starting material.

## Preparation of the drug product
To create the drug product (Vigil), the drug substance is formulated to the appropriate concentration using Freeze Media, aliquoted and frozen at a controlled rate. The Freeze Media consists of 10% DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide; Cryoserv USP; Mylan), and 1% Human Serum Albumin (Octapharma) in Plasma-Lyte A, pH 7.4 (Baxter). After controlled-rate freezing, the cells are stored at <-15 degrees C in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (LN2) until all product testing is completed, all necessary product release approvals are obtained and the patient is deemed ready for treatment. Single-use vials are transferred on a per-treatment basis to the clinical site in cryogenic containers. The treatment dose and regimen are set by the clinical protocol. The final product consists of a 1 ml intradermal administration volume, Patients receive 4–12 doses (1 × 107 cells/injection) of Vigil every 3 weeks at an appropriate site and are observed for potential adverse reactions.
Vigil product manufacturing has been previously published [ , , ]. Every Vigil batch/lot is tested for GMCSF expression and TGFβ knockdown. Each released and patient dispositioned batch/lot must meet manufacturing release criteria. To determine GMCSF expression and TGFβ knockdown, pre-transfection, pre-irradiation samples are compared with post-transfection, post-irradiation samples. To pass manufacturing release criteria, GMCSF expression must be above 30 pg/ml per 106 cells and TGFβ knockdown must be at least 30% below non transfected autologous tumor control expression. During performance of the VITAL trial, median GMCSF expression of 860 pg/ml per 106 cells was achieved. Tumor GMCSF expression without Vigil transfected plasmid is generally well below 30 pg/ml per 106 cells. TGFβ knockdown was over 80% below control expression in 78 of the 91 patients [ ].
Product release criteria are shown in . Regulatory suitability of key assays used for product release, including functional protein expression, cell count, identity and safety assays (mycoplasma, endotoxin, sterility) have been verified with FDA oversight. Selected Vigil final product batches have been tested for stability criteria. Stability criteria include, safety (sterility), potency/quantity (cell counts), viability (% of intact cells) identity (PCR) and potency (GMCSF production and TGFβ1 production).

## Vigil clinical experience
Two hundred thirty cancer patients have received 1433 doses of Vigil since the initiation of clinical trials. The most frequently reported adverse reactions attributed to Vigil are injection site reactions that have been reported by the treating investigator as mild to moderate in intensity (≤ Grade 2), consisting primarily of redness and swelling at the intradermal injection site. No severe or life threatening (Grade 3 or 4) systemic adverse events have been attributed to Vigil treatment. A Phase I trial of Vigil successfully demonstrated appropriate dose, safety and predicted immune response (ELISpot assay) in advanced solid tumor patients. Moreover, clinical OS benefit was shown in correlation with post Vigil positive ELISpot response compared with a portion of patients with no ELISpot response [ , ]. These benefits were maintained in long term follow-up through 3 years with median OS of 836 days in ELISpot positive vs. 440 days in ELISpot negative patients, p = 0.04 [ ]. Validation of ELISpot results which support the mechanism of Vigil were also observed with demonstration of CD3+/CD8+ circulating lymphoid cell increase post Vigil [ ], a high tumor inflammation signature (TIS) score high (TIS >6) and increased MHC II expression demonstrated by NanoString post Vigil [ ] correlated with clinical OS benefit in recurrent/resistant ovarian cancer patients [ , , ]. ELISpot also predicted clinical benefit in recurrent/resistant ovarian cancer patients with median OS of “not reached” in ELISpot positive patients versus 16.1 months for ELISpot negative patients (p = 0.0098, HR 0.19; CI: 0.021–1.7) from time of tissue procurement.

## Ewing's sarcoma
Ewing's sarcoma is a rare pediatric and young adult bone cancer which frequently metastasizes quickly to the lung. Following recurrence, the majority of patients do not respond to second line chemotherapy [ ], median overall survival is 14 months and 5 year OS is only 13% [ ]. Relapse that occurs 2 years after initial diagnosis is shown to have 2-year survival of only 7% with standard of care (SOC) therapy [ ]. Phase I testing of Vigil (n = 12) determined safety and evidence of response in advanced, metastatic Ewing's sarcoma patients previously treated with 2 or more chemotherapy regimens. No ≥ Grade 3 drug related toxicity was observed. Eight patients were evaluated for ELISpot response, and all converted to positive ELISpot from negative baseline assessment. The robust ELISpot response to Vigil in Ewing's sarcoma may be reflective of consistent clonal neoantigen and driver gene target EWS-FLI1. Additionally, median survival at Year 1 was 75% through Kaplan-Meier analysis [ ] compared with historically expected OS of 23% [ , ]. In an expansion of Vigil assessment, Phase II trial results observed OS was also significant with median OS of 731 days in the Vigil treated compared with 207 days in a similar patient profile historical control, equating to a 17.2 month improvement [ ]. One patient achieved a durable complete response following a series of Vigil treatments which correlated with elevated ELISpot activity determined by harvested circulating mononuclear cells [ ].
Vigil has also been sequentially assessed in combination with temozolomide and irinotecan (TEM/IRI) following single agent success in the Phase I clinical trial and showed evidence of safety and benefit [ ]. Historical evidence for recurrent and primary refractory Ewing's sarcoma patients treated with TEM/IRI demonstrated median PFS of 4.7 months and median OS of 13.9 months [ ]. Comparatively, the 8 patients receiving Vigil/TEM/IRI demonstrated median PFS of 8.2 months and median OS of 18.5 months. Results justify further clinical exploration of Vigil in advanced Ewing's sarcoma.

## Ovarian cancer
Vigil immunotherapy has demonstrated durable evidence of benefit for both RFS and OS in BRCA-wt and HRP population involving randomized, placebo-controlled trial [ , ]. Relevant cytokine expression (GMCSF↑, TGFβ1↓, TGFβ2↓) and ELISpot based tumor immune systemic response observed in Phase IIA analyses confirmed Vigil mechanism of action and built on Phase I evidence using the ELISpot assay. Specifically, positive ELISpot responses to autologous tumor provide evidence of γ interferon release against tumor cell neoantigens (likely clonal neoantigens) from blood harvested mononuclear cells following Vigil treatment. A Phase IIA trial (NCT01309230) explored Vigil treatment in Stage IIIB-IV, resectable newly diagnosed ovarian cancer patients (n = 31) compared with randomized control patients (n = 11). The trial design comparing Vigil vs. SOC for maintenance therapy after debulking surgery/induction with platinum-based chemotherapy. Similar lack of Grade 3 toxicity related to Vigil was observed and robust ELISpot response was demonstrated. Specifically, all 31 Vigil treated patients had positive ELISpot responses compared with 0/8 measurable control patients who did not receive Vigil as maintenance. However, all control patients (n = 8) who crossed over to Vigil at progression subsequently demonstrated ELISpot response within 2–3 months after receiving Vigil [ ]. Moreover, RFS was shown to be significantly improved in Vigil treated patients compared with control patients not receiving Vigil-median Vigil RFS from tumor tissue procurement was 19.8 months vs. control of 12.4 months; HR 0.43, p = 0.033 [ , ]. Phase IIA data was updated 4 years after initial analysis and results continued to provide evidence of durable benefit in RFS (19.9 months. vs. 12.4 months; HR 0.52, p = 0.0399) [ ].
Subsequently, a Phase IIB randomized double blind trial was conducted in 91 newly diagnosed ovarian cancer patients with Stage IIIB-IV resectable disease undergoing debulking surgery and neoadjuvant or adjuvant platinum therapy as per ASCO guidelines. This study explored RFS and OS difference with Vigil vs. placebo as maintenance therapy (VITAL study) at 25 leading gynecologic oncology sites. Women ≥18 years of age with Stage IIIB-IV high-grade serous, fallopian, or clear cell ovarian cancer in clinical complete response following combination of surgery and 5–8 cycles of chemotherapy involving carboplatin and paclitaxel were included. Ninety-one subjects were randomized 1:1 (stratified by microscopic vs. macroscopic surgical outcome, and adjuvant vs. neoadjuvant chemotherapy) to Vigil (n = 47) vs. placebo (n = 44) [ , ]. Subjects remained on treatment until disease recurrence or exhaustion of the subject's Vigil or placebo supply-no patients were discontinued for Vigil toxicity. Disease recurrence was evaluated by Blinded Independent Committee Review [WorldCare Clinical (WCC) (Boston, MA)] using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors Version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1) and third-party statistical assessment per the study statistical analysis plan. An additional post-hoc analysis to analyze relationship of homologous recombination deficient (HRD) and HRP status to Vigil treatment effect was performed to accommodate a new biomarker (HRD/HRP Myriad MyChoice™ testing) implemented into practice after the Phase IIB study initiation [ ]. Analysis was performed in blinded fashion by a third-party statistician with FDA acceptance and review thereby justifying focus on HRP subpopulation in which greater benefit was observed.

## Vigil-related biomarkers
As part of our Vigil development program, we retrospectively studied individual molecular biomarkers that may relate to sensitivity to Vigil therapy. We identified a relationship between TP53 DNA mutation and Vigil clinical response via network-based analysis [ ]. All patients enrolled in the VITAL trial underwent next generation sequencing of normal and tumor tissue using a 981 gene panel. Following variant classification the STRING application created a protein-protein interaction network. C-scores and cumulative C-scores were calculated from topological distance and probability of comutation. This analysis revealed patients with TP53-BRCA-wild type-homologous recombination proficient molecular profile as a subset population with improved relapse free survival (21.1 months vs. 5.6 months, p = 0.0013) following Vigil treatment.
Additionally, we used the NanoString PanCancer Immuno-Oncology 360™ CodeSet via the nCounter SPRINT platform (NanoString Technologies, Seattle, WA) to analyze RNA expression of 750 genes. This analysis demonstrated correlation of the molecular biomarker, ENTPD1 (high expression over low expression) to both RFS (not achieved vs. 8.1 months, p = 0.00007) and OS benefit (median not achieved vs. 41.4 months, p = 0.013) [ ]. The ENTPD1 gene, also referred to as CD39, is a protein coding gene that functions as a limiting step in the adenosine metabolic pathway found in immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments.
We found no relationship of Vigil response to tumor mutational burden [ ] or PD-L1 expression [ ]. Upcoming clinical investigation will explore prospectively p53 protein/mRNA, ENTPD1 protein/mRNA and clonal gene mutation biomarkers using whole exome sequencing (Frontage Laboratories Inc, Deerfield Beach, FL).

## Vigil-related combination therapy
Vigil through it's unique triple mechanism and robust safety profile provides unique combination strategies. For example, checkpoint inhibitors have demonstrated clinical benefit in some but not all patients. Previously, we have demonstrated safety and clinical activity with Vigil in combination with atezolizumab and durvalumab [ , ]. Vigil plus durvalumab in advanced recurrent/refractory BRCA-wt triple negative breast and showed response with median PFS of 7.1 months. Overall survival was prolonged and not reached at time of analysis. PFS was improved in patients with PD-L1 positive tumors compared with negative (HR 0.304, p = 0.0472) [ ]. Vigil was also explored in sequence with atezolizumab in relapsed OC. When administered first, Vigil decreased the amount of Grade 3/4 adverse events (5.1 vs 17.2%). The Vigil first, BRCA-wt subset also demonstrated improved OS (NR vs 5.2 months HR 0.16, p = 0.027) [ ]. By providing the full complement of the patients neoantigens, Vigil may educate and prime relevant immune effector cells thus effectively targeting the immune system to the tumor reducing adverse effects and maximizing clinical benefit.
Another potentially beneficial combination is Vigil with bevacizumab. Bevacizumab, a VEGF inhibitor has also shown benefit in advanced cancer patients. VEGF is a key driver of angiogenesis and tumor proliferation. However, VEGF has also shown immune inhibitory effects including T cell suppression via increased T regulatory cells [ , ]. VEGF inhibitors have shown the ability to transform the tumor microenvironment into an immune stimulatory environment by increasing dendritic cells, recruitment of T cells, suppression of Tregs and decreasing immune suppressive cytokines [ , ]. Vigil may work in synergy with bevacizumab by providing T cell priming and education against cancer neoantigens, increasing DC abundance by GMCSF expression and down regulation of the immune inhibitory cytokine TGFβ.

11.

## Autologous vs allogenic neoantigen approaches
Vigil an autologous therapy is derived from the patients own tumor and therefore provides the complete repertoire of the patients neoantigens; both clonal and subclonal. A variety of allogenic/synthetic approaches are currently being investigated in the clinic. mRNA based approaches use bioinformatics pipelines to identify what is assumed to be the relevant neoantigens. However, there is a risk that the pipeline does not identify the most relevant or immunogenic neoantigens and the amount of neoantigens incorporated is limited. The use of autologous approaches overcomes these limitations. In the clinic mRNA approaches have demonstrated mixed responses. One approach which combines 20 common cancer driver mutations into a self-amplifying mRNA delivered using chimp adenovirus demonstrated a response rate of 0% in advanced or metastatic solid tumors when combined with the checkpoint inhibitors ipilimumab and nivolumab [ ]. Another approach which identifies up to 34 patient specific neoantigens through a bioinformatics pipeline (mRNA-4157 (V940)) has demonstrated RFS improvement in combination with pembrolizumab in high-risk resected melanoma patients, however overall survival benefit has not been demonstrated [ ]. Responses maybe variable due to the accuracy and specificity of the bioinformatics pipelines to identify the most relevant cancer neoantigens.

12.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, a broad and robust body of clinical data demonstrates the impact of Vigil activity on 3 necessary immune mechanisms involved in generating a meaningful systemic clinical response. These results build upon a foundation of data generated from prior approaches using one or more of these three mechanisms (provision of relevant autologous tumor antigens, GMCSF upregulation and TGFβ downregulation). Evidence of durable survival benefit demonstrated by continued ELISpot responsiveness of harvested patient mononuclear cells following completion of Vigil therapy and long-term correlation of RFS and OS to achievement of positive ELISpot activation support memory cell induction to relevant tumor neoantigens and robust expansion of neoantigen targeting effector cells. Further assessment of anticancer memory induction, clonal tumor mutation burden and specific clonal neoantigen effector cell induction within these clinical studies is underway. Observation of optimal clinical response (RFS, OS) in patients with HRP type tumors as opposed to HRD, BRCA-m patients supports the likelihood of a systemically induced clonal neoantigen targeting effector cell response induction by Vigil. Knowledge of specific clonal tumor mutation burden and clonal neoantigens associated with clinical benefit following treatment with Vigil may further facilitate identification of optimal patient candidates for future assessment with Vigil therapy. Such an approach, however, may be particularly relevant to advanced stage HRP ovarian cancer patients who have no acceptable therapy following diagnosis and are considered an unmet medical need population in comparison to late-stage ovarian cancer patients who have HRD, BRCA-m molecular profile.

13.

## Future perspective
Bevacizumab is currently utilized in combination with chemotherapy following debulking surgery in late-stage ovarian cancer and is continued as single agent as maintenance, but those supporting clinical trials were not performed in a prospective HRP defined population, and no impact on survival has been observed, moreover PFS advantage is small. Biomarker assessment, including p53 and ENTPD1 as well as “mechanistic” biomarkers such as high clonal TMB, clonal neoantigen and/or low subclonal neoantigen expression in patients for whom the Vigil product is specifically constructed, may identify a subset population with exquisite clinical response, expanding future directions for Vigil development and treatment within and beyond ovarian cancer. Patients with HRP molecular profile represent proof of principle of enhanced Vigil activity in a population whose tumors likely harbor a high clonal neoantigen population and consequently a higher target population for successful clinical benefit with Vigil treatment. Evidence of response benefit related to clonal tumor mutation burden and/or clonal neoantigen characterization would likely support advantages to combination Vigil with CPI therapy. Two small trials of Vigil and CPI combination have in fact already demonstrated evidence of benefit [ , ].
