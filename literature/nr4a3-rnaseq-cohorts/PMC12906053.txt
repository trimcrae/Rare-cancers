## Abstract

## Objective
Sinonasal renal cell-like adenocarcinoma (SNRCLA) is a rare sinonasal carcinoma that is easily misdiagnosed. We aimed to reveal clinical clinicopathological features, molecular genetic characteristics, treatment and prognosis of SNRCLA.

## Methods 
The pathological diagnosis and differential diagnosis of a case of SNRCLA and the literature was reviewed.

## Results
Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated positive expression of cytokeratin (CK)7, Sox-10, and carbonic anhydrase (CA)-IX, while negative expression was observed for CK20, CDX-2, Villin, Thyroid transcription factor(TTF)-1, Dog-1, Epstein–Barr virus viral capsid antigen (EBV-VCA), CD10,Renal cell carcinoma marker(RCC Ma), paired box(PAX)8, calponin, p63,and S-100. The positive proportion of Ki67 is less than 5%, and periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining confirmed glycogen content in the tumor cells. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) protein was overexpressed, but the EGFR gene was not mutated. The ETV6 gene was analyzed using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) technology, but no rearrangement was detected. These mutations were detected using next-generation sequencing (NGS), including BRAF NM_004333:exon11 c.1357 C > G p.P453A, NM_000314:exon1 c.70_75del p.D24_L25del, MTOR NM_004958:exon39 c.5417 A > G p.H1806R, and PIK3CA NM_006218:exon5 c.1031T > G p.V344G. Following surgery and radiotherapy, no clinical or radiological evidence of recurrence or metastasis was observed during a follow-up period of 76 months.

## Conclusion
It is beneficial for diagnosis of SRCLC that clinical history, morphological characteristics and immunohistochemistry are combined. It is an indolent tumor, the potential for local recurrence requires individualized management and post-treatment surveillance. Meanwhile, It warrants further examination in molecular genetic characteristics.

## Background
SRCLA, a low-grade non-intestinal adenocarcinoma exhibiting histological features resembling renal cell carcinoma, is an extremely rare and indolent tumor. Zur et al. [ ] first reported this case in 2002, and 25 cases have been reported worldwide, in which the descriptions of genetic features were not provided. We report a case of SRCLA in which molecular genetic characteristics were detected.

## Materials and methods
The surgical specimen was fixed in 10% buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, and serially sectioned into4-µm-thick slices. Routine staining with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) was performed. Immunohistochemical staining was performed using the EnVision two-step method, with DAB color development and hematoxylin contrast staining. All the antibodies were purchased from Fuzhou Maixin. Primary antibodies included anti- CK7, CK20, CDX-2, villin, TTF-1, Sox-10, P63, Calponin, S-100, Dog-1, CAⅨ, PAX8, CDl0, RCC, EBV, EGFR and Ki-67. The EnVision kit was purchased from DAKO, Japan). Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) was used for dyeing. FISH tests were performed according to standardized ETV6 gene separation probe procedures. Judging criteria: more than one fusion signal between red and green signals was interpreted as red and green signal separation, 100 non-overlapping nuclei were counted, and when ≥ 10% of tumor cells showed separation signals, the result was positive. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed using the solid tumor 107 gene test kit from Space Biology.

## Results

## Clinical features
A 42-year-old female patient complained of nasal obstruction and bleeding for > 2 months. A polypoid lesion was observed in the nasal sinus during nasal endoscopy. Computer tomography (CT) scanning revealed an expansive soft tissue mass in th left nasal cavity and ethmoid sinus which partially broke into the right nasal cavity, the left maxillary sinus and the medial wall of the left maxillary sinus, and the medial wall of the orbit and partially destroyed the septal bone, with a size of about 2.9 cm×4.2 cm×2.9 cm, with visible lobulation. The enhanced scan showed nonuniform significant enhancement. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a mass in the left nasal cavity and ethmoid sinus with long T1 and T2 signals (Figs.  A-D).
 

## Histologic features
The tumor exhibited follicular and glandular structures with intervening spots on the fibrous septa. The tumor cells were uniform cuboidal to polyhedral, with abundant clear or eosinophilic cytoplasm and subnuclear vacuoles. Nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic activity were minimal. No necrosis or perineural invasion was observed in the tumor. Small blood vessels and a small number of chronic inflammatory cells were observed in the tumor stroma (Fig. A).
 

## Immunohistochemical staining
Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were positive for CK7, Sox-10, CA-IX, and EGFR and negative for CK20, CDX-2, Villin, TTF-1, Dog-1, EBV-VCA, CD10, RCC, PAX8, Calponin, p63, and S-100. The ki-67 value-added index in all cases was < 5%. (Figs.  B-D).

## Molecular genetics
The ETV6 gene was analyzed by FISH, and its rearrangement was not detected. These mutations were detected using next-generation sequencing (NGS), including BRAF NM_004333:exon11 c.1357 C > G p.P453A, NM_000314:exon1 c.70_75del p.D24_L25del, MTOR NM_004958:exon39 c.5417 A > G p.H1806R, and PIK3CA NM_006218:exon5 c.1031T > G p.V344G. However, a genetic mutation in EGFR has not yet been found.

## Discussion
There had been 25 patients described since it was first reported in 2002 [ – ]. The age of the patients had a broad distribution ranging from 22 to 75 years (mean, 50 years). Male predominance was observed at 14:11. Clinical manifestations such as nasal obstruction, epistaxis, and headache are relatively nonspecific. The nasal cavity is the most common site of involvement, followed by the paranasal sinus and nasopharynx.
Thus far, the histogenesis of SRCLC remains unclear. There have been some reports of DOG1, SOX10, and S100, which are new markers defined for seromucinous glands, including sinonasal glands [ , ], being positive for SRCLC [ – , , , – ]. This suggests a possible seromucinous derivation of SRCLC. In our study, the tumor cells in the SRCLC were positive for SOX10.
Owing to its similar morphological features, SNRCLA should be differentiated from other clear cell tumors, including primary salivary tumors, primary non-salivary gland-derived tumors, metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma, and clear cell variant thyroid follicular carcinoma. There are many similarities between Seromueinous hamartoma (SMH) and SNRCLA, in terms of both clinical and morphological features. However, compared with SNRCLA, SMH has a clearer boundary, and the mucus glands cannot form solid lamellar or nest-like structures similar to SNRCLA, but aggregate in a lobulated shape. In addition, myoepithelium, which is p63, is calponin-positive around the glands of SMH, but not in SNRCLA. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is the most common malignant salivary gland neoplasm and is composed of mucous, epidermoid, and intermediate cells. Occasionally, it arises in the sinonasal tract [ , ], meanwhile, epidermoid and intermediate cells undergo clear changes and are predominant. In this case, distinguishing between MEC and SRCLC was challenging. However, p63 and CK5/6 diffuse positive expression, CK7 negative expression, and the presence of CRTC1-MAML2 or CRTC3-MAML2 fusion genes contribute to the diagnosis of MEC. Salivary of clear cell carcinoma (SCCC) mostly occurs in the small salivary glands and rarely in the nose and sinuses. Therefore, SCCC in the nose and sinuses usually metastasize from the kidney. However, SCCC expresses squamous cell markers such as p63/p40, EWSR1 gene rearrangement, and EWSR1-ATF1 gene fusion, which are not detected in SRCLA. Epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma (EMC) occurs mainly in the parotid gland with microscopic features of spindled cells and basement membrane deposition. The expression of myoepithelial marker P63/SMA can be used for the diagnosis of EMC. Occasionally, there is clear cell morphology in the focal area of acinic cell carcinomathe (ACC). The positive expression of NR4A3 can help in the diagnosis of ACC and exclude SNRCLA. The clear cell variant of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) also occurs in the sinonasal tract. The positive expression of high molecular weight cytokeratins, such as CK5/6, P63, and 34βE12, in the clear cells is the best way to establish the squamous nature of the tumor. When melanoma occurs in pure clear-cell variants, it can be confused with SRCLC. In such cases, immunohistochemical staining can play an important role, despite the fact that SRCLC is uniformly positive for S100, SOX10 but negative for HMB-45 and Melan-A. Metastatic clear cell variant renal cell carcinoma (CCRCC) should certainly be distinguished from SRCLA. Both express CA-IX, while CCRCC does not express CK7, but RCC, CD10, and PAX8. Our report showed that ETV-6 gene rearrangement was not confirmed by FISH in SRCLC, which is consistent with the results of Yuan et al. Reported [ ]. In 17 cases of sinonasal low-grade non-intestinal adenocarcinoma, likely oncogenic molecular alterations were identified in 76% of cases, most notably, CTNNB1 p.S33F mutations in 2 cases, concomitant BRAF p.V600E and AKT1 p.E17K mutations in 2 cases, and ETV6::NTRK3, PRKAR1A::MET, FN1::NRG1, and DNAJB1::PRKACA fusions in 1 case each [ ]. An EGFR::ZNF267 fusion was identified in a case with overlapping features of seromucinous hamartoma and low-grade SNAC [ ]. As a special type of low-grade non-intestinal adenocarcinoma, the oncogenic molecular alterations mentioned above have been identified in SRCLA. In the present study, SRCLA cells exhibited EGFR overexpression. This finding adds to the evidence that EGFR expression may be altered. However, the EGFR gene was not found to be mutated by NGS. The reason for this phenomenon may be that there are mutations in exons other than 12 and from18 to 21 that can lead to EGFR protein overexpression. If EGFR overexpression has been detected earlier than the chemotherapy regimen, it may have changed [ ], and better outcomes may have been achieved. These mutations were detected using next-generation sequencing (NGS), including BRAF NM_004333:exon11 c.1357 C > G p.P453A, PTEN NM_000314:exon1 c.70_75del p.D24_L25del, MTOR NM_004958:exon39 c.5417 A > G p.H1806R, and PIK3CA NM_006218:exon5 c.1031T > G p.V344G. PTEN D24del results in the deletion of an amino acid within the phosphatase tensin-type domain of PTEN protein at amino acid 24. D24del results in reduced phosphatase activity in a yeast assay, and therefore is predicted to lead to a loss of PTEN protein function [ ]. The PIK3CA V344G mutation is located between the RAS-binding domain and C2 domain of the protein. This mutation has been identified in glioblastomas [ ]. Expression of this mutation in Ba/F3 and MCF10A cell lines demonstrated that it is activated as measured by increased proliferation in the absence of growth factors compared to wild-type PIK3CA [ ]. Expression of this mutation in a transgenic mouse model is insufficient to drive glioblastoma formation [ ]. In addition, in this study, the related mutations of BRAF and MTOR were nonsense mutations.

## Conclusions
SNRCLA is a rare, low-malignant sinonasal carcinoma that is easily misdiagnosed. It is beneficial for diagnosis that clinical history, morphological characteristics, and immunohistochemistry are combined. Surgery is the main treatment for SNRCLA, and the effects of adjuvant radiotherapy require further research. Although SRCLC is an indolent tumor, the potential for local recurrence requires individualized management and post-treatment surveillance. Meanwhile, in EGFR-overexpressing SRCLA, molecular-targeted chemotherapy, such as cetuximab, might be performed as an optional treatment.
