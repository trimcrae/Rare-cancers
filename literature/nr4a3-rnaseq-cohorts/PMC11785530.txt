## Abstract
T cells recognize neoepitope peptide-major histocompatibility complex class I on cancer cells. The strength (or avidity) of the T cell receptor–peptide-major histocompatibility complex class I interaction is a critical variable in immune control of cancers. Here, we analyze neoepitope-specific CD8 cells of distinct avidities and show that low-avidity T cells are the sole mediators of cancer control in mice and are solely responsive to checkpoint blockade in mice and humans. High-avidity T cells are ineffective and immune-suppressive. The mechanistic basis of these differences lies in the higher exhaustion status of high-avidity cells. High-avidity T cells have a distinct transcriptomic profile that is used here to calculate an ‘avidity score’, which we then use for in silico identification of low-avidity and high-avidity T cells in mice and humans. Surprisingly, CD8+ T cells with identical T cell receptors exhibit wide variation in avidities, suggesting an additional level of regulation of T cell activity. Aside from providing a better understanding of endogenous T cell responses to cancer, these findings might instruct future immunotherapy strategies.
Subject terms: Tumour immunology, Cancer immunotherapy, CD8-positive T cells

Here the authors show that, in mouse tumors, CD8+ T cells with a low-avidity TCR are the mediators of antitumor and immunotherapy responses, in part because T cells with a high-avidity TCR are more exhausted.

## Main
CD8+ T cells recognize antigenic peptides presented by major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I) molecules of cancer cells. On the T cell side, this recognition is made through T cell receptors (TCRs). Although the affinity of a TCR for a peptide–MHC-I complex (pMHC-I) is a centerpiece of T cell–tumor cell interaction, other factors, such as the number of TCR molecules on T cells, the number of cognate pMHC-I complexes on cancer cells and the number of coreceptors on T cells, contribute to the strength of the TCR–pMHC-I interaction, termed as TCR avidity . Considerable effort has gone into understanding the role of TCR avidity in a wide range of antigens in tumor control. These antigens include nonmutated self-antigens – , foreign antigens , model antigens expressed as tumor antigens , cryptic antigens or oncogene products , . These studies typically analyzed monoclonal T cell populations adoptively transferred into mice or humans. In view of the heterogeneity of these antigens and models, these studies resulted in every possible conclusion: (1) high-avidity responses are more effective , , , – ; (2) low-avidity and high-avidity TCRs can be effective and equally so , , ; (3) high-avidity responses risk autoimmunity while low-avidity responses are less likely to do so , , ; and (4) low-avidity and intermediate-avidity responses are more effective at tumor control , . Studies in human cancers tested the influence of T cell avidity on neoepitopes in vivo using adoptive transfer of transgenic TCRs in immunocompromised mice rather than in humans .
Much more so than self-antigens, model antigens, oncogene-encoded antigens and foreign antigens, cancer neoepitopes have emerged as potent stimulators and key targets of antitumor immune responses in mice and humans. CD8 and CD4 T cell responses to natural neoepitopes are the subjects of intense scrutiny. Despite such centrality, the role of TCR avidity in the endogenous CD8+ T cell response to neoepitopes remains unexplored. In this study, we addressed such critical lacuna in mice and humans and examined the endogenous polyclonal CD8+ T cell response to natural cancer neoepitopes that elicit tumor rejection. By analyzing neoepitope-specific CD8 cells with distinct avidities to several validated neoepitopes of two mouse tumors , , we observed that low-avidity T cells are the sole mediators of cancer control in vivo.

## Results

## Experimental system
We previously identified several naturally occurring neoepitopes of a murine sarcoma Meth A using mass spectrometry (MS). Of these, GTF2bMUT and PDPRMUT elicited CD8+ T cell responses and mediated tumor control in vivo. In this study, we identified antigen-specific CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) as cells that stain equivalently for the same tetramer labeled with two distinct fluorophores (Extended Data Fig. , left). These same tetramers did not stain any TILs from the murine breast carcinoma 4T1 cell line, which does not express PDPRMUT (Extended Data Fig. , right), thus establishing their specificity. Tetramer-sorted CD8+ T cells produced interferon-γ (IFNγ) only on restimulation with their cognate mutant but not the corresponding wild-type peptides (Extended Data Fig. ). In this study, we used this well-characterized system to understand the heterogeneity of T cell phenotypes among the endogenous CD8+ T cells specific to natural cancer neoepitopes, which mediate tumor rejection in vivo.
 

## A modified tetramer decay assay
Savage et al. developed a tetramer decay assay to measure the dissociation of pMHC tetramers from T cells, thus providing a measure of TCR avidity. Cells with persistent binding of tetramers are deemed as having high avidity. However, that method is incapable of separating T cells with low and medium avidities. We modified this foundational assay to enable preparative isolation and functional characterization of low-avidity, medium-avidity and high-avidity subpopulations of antigen-specific T cells from a heterogenous population of T cells. TILs were isolated from Meth A tumors 28 days after implantation and stained with a mixture of Dd-PDPRMUT tetramers labeled with phycoerythrin (PE) (TetPE) or Brilliant Violet 421 (TetBV421); tetramers were allowed to dissociate from CD8+ T cells for varying periods of time in medium containing anti-MHC-I antibody to prevent reassociation of dissociated tetramers. T cells that dissociate early are of low avidity, those that dissociate later are of medium avidity and those that remain associated even after a prolonged time are of high avidity (Fig. and ). We observed empirically that inclusion of an anti-PE antibody during tetramer decay prevented the dissociation of TetPE over 120 min. The tetramer is thus stabilized (Extended Data Fig. , right, and Extended Data Fig. ). In contrast, the nonstabilized TetBV421 dissociates normally (Extended Data Fig. ). Stabilized binding of TetPE serves as the reference point against which decay of TetBV421 is compared. Addition of a stabilizing antibody is a critical innovation in this assay: in its absence, low-avidity and medium-avidity cells will be indistinguishable from antigen-nonspecific T cells because they will also be tetramer-negative. We used tetramer decay as a measure of TCR avidity because kinetic separation of T cells using tetramer decay is superior to tetramer intensity .
 Sorted T cells with low and high avidity were characterized. The studies in Fig. were carried out with TILs. Functional studies of PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ T cells in vitro were pursued with lymph node (LN)-derived CD8+ T cells rather than TILs because of the relative paucity of antigen-specific CD8+ TILs. CD8+ T cells from the LNs of mice immunized with PDPRMUT were tested for tetramer decay as in Fig. . Indeed, low-avidity and high-avidity CD8+ T cells were observed in equal proportions between LNs and TILs (Extended Data Fig. ). To test if the low-avidity and high-avidity T cells isolated as in Extended Data Fig. are truly of distinct avidities, they were re-stained and re-decayed to determine if they ‘sorted true’ as described under ‘Tetramer re-decay’ in the . Almost all cells that were TetPE+TetBV421+ at t15 (15 min after the CD8+ T cells were shifted to room temperature; see ) remained TetPE+TetBV421+ after re-decay for 15 min, while most cells that were TetPE+TetBV421− at t15 became TetPE+TetBV421− upon re-decay for 15 min (Fig. ). This was not attempted for TetPE+TetBV421+ and TetPE+TetBV421− at 105 min because TCR-signaling-mediated TCR downregulation would be a confounding variable due to the very long duration of tetramer staining.
The purified low-avidity and high-avidity populations were characterized with respect to classical markers of phosphorylation and cytokine secretion. Phosphorylation of ZAP70 at tyrosine 292 (ZAP70-pY292) is a TCR-pMHC affinity-dependent event . CD45.1 congenic PDPRMUT-pulsed splenocytes were cocultured (as antigen-presenting cells (APCs)) with low-avidity and high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells sorted from vaccine-draining LNs of PDPRMUT-immunized mice and stained for ZAP70-pY292. We used LN-derived CD8+ T cells rather than TILs because TILs express high levels of coinhibitory receptors that can dephosphorylate ZAP70 (ref. ), thus confounding the interpretation of the result. High-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells had a significantly greater proportion of cells that were ZAP70-pY292+ and had significantly greater ZAP-pY292 mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) than low-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells and tetramer-negative T cells from the same LNs (Fig. ).
Production of IFNγ by low-avidity and high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells was tested in the absence or presence of PDPRMUT peptide. No IFNγ was detected in unstimulated cells, while both low-avidity and high-avidity T cells made significant IFNγ upon stimulation. Control (tetramer-negative) T cells did not make any IFNγ with or without stimulation. A higher proportion of high-avidity T cells made IFNγ than low-avidity T cells (Fig. ). Killing of PDPRMUT peptide-pulsed target cells by low-avidity and high-avidity T cells, as well as tetramer-negative cells, was tested; low-avidity and high-avidity cells were equally cytotoxic while tetramer-negative cells showed negligible killing (Fig. ).

## Tumor control is dependent on low-avidity T cells only
The antigen-specific low-avidity and high-avidity populations were tested individually for their ability to control tumors in vivo. One thousand control (tetramer-negative), low-avidity or high-avidity cells (all obtained from the LNs of immunized mice and isolated as in Extended Data Fig. ) were adoptively transferred (intravenously) into mice bearing progressively growing, 10-day-old palpable Meth A tumors. Tumors in mice that had received high-avidity T cells grew faster than those in control-treated mice, while tumors in mice that received low-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells grew significantly slower or regressed (Fig. and Extended Data Fig. ). These results are also reflected in a significantly greater survival of mice that received low-avidity T cells over those that received tetramer-negative T cells or high-avidity T cells (Fig. ). Adoptive transfer of as many as 4,000 high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells did not mediate tumor control (Extended Data Fig. ). Antitumor activity of low-avidity over high-avidity T cells was also observed in antigen-specific T cells that recognized the neoepitope GTF2bMUT (Fig. and Extended Data Fig. ). The generality of this phenomenon was further tested in an additional mutated neoepitope (STFLYFSFF) of MC38-FABF colon carcinoma in C57BL/6 mice (Extended Data Fig. ).
 An additional analysis of consolidated data of survival of mice that had received low-avidity, high-avidity or control CD8+ T cells for all neoepitopes confirmed the exclusive power of low-avidity T cells to mediate tumor control with high statistical significance (P < 0.0001 between control and low-avidity T cells, as well as between low-avidity and high-avidity T cells) (Extended Data Fig. ).

## High-avidity T cells express markers of exhaustion
The absence of antitumor activity of high-avidity CD8+ T cells in vivo (Fig. ) contrasted with the equivalent cytotoxic activity of low-avidity and high-avidity T cells in vitro (Fig. ). The hypothesis that this discrepancy results from differential exhaustion of CD8+ T cells in vivo was considered; hence, low-avidity and high-avidity T cells were examined for markers of exhaustion. Twenty-eight-day TIL-derived low-avidity and high-avidity T cells specific for PDPRMUT were characterized for TOX , and TIM3 (ref. ) (Fig. ). High-avidity TILs showed a significantly higher proportion of TIM3+ cells and higher MFI of TIM3. Compared to low-avidity TILs, high-avidity TILs also showed a higher proportion of TOX+ cells, as well as a higher proportion of TIM3+ cells and higher MFI of TIM3. Regardless of the source of sampling (TILs or vaccine-draining LNs or tumor-draining LNs), high-avidity T cells were more exhausted than low-avidity T cells according to every parameter tested (Extended Data Fig. ).
 The association of avidity with exhaustion was also seen in T cells specific for the neoepitope GTF2bMUT (Extended Data Fig. ). CD8+ T cell responses against two additional neoepitopes of Meth A, TRIB3MUT (Dd-restricted) and PRPF19-1MUT (Kd-restricted) , reproduced the association between TCR avidity and the exhaustion phenotype seen with PDPRMUT and GTF2bMUT (Extended Data Fig. ). The findings were further reproduced in the CD8+ T cells specific for the Kb-restricted STFLYFSFF neoepitope of MC38-FABF (Extended Data Fig. ), a tumor with a different haplotype and distinct histological origin from Meth A .
 Expression of exhaustion-related markers in low-avidity and high-avidity T cells was examined in vaccine-draining and tumor-draining LNs. The high-avidity T cell population had a significantly greater proportion of TIM3+TCF1− cells (exhausted population) than the low-avidity T cell population in vaccine-draining (Fig. left) and tumor-draining (Fig. , left) LNs. Conversely, the high-avidity T cell population had a significantly lower proportion of TIM3−TCF1+ (stem-like population) cells than the low-avidity T cell population in both vaccine-draining and tumor-draining LNs (Figs. , right and , right).
Antigen-specific TILs of varying avidities were also probed using multi-omic sequencing. TILs were isolated from tumor-bearing mice (n = 11) 28 days after tumor challenge and stained with surface cellular indexing of transcriptomes and epitopes by sequencing (CITE-seq) antibodies against PD-1, CX3CR1, TIM3 and LY108, which are well-known markers of exhausted T cells in cancers and chronic infections. These cells were sorted into low-avidity, medium-avidity and high-avidity cells as in Fig. and subjected to single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), TCR sequencing and CITE-seq sequencing (Fig. ). Unbiased clustering of the filtered CD8+ TILs based on their transcriptomes revealed five clusters: stem-cell like (stem-like), natural killer (NK)-like (NK-like), effector exhausted (TEFF‐EX), terminally exhausted 1 (TEX1) and terminally exhausted 2 (TEX2) (Fig. ). See the for the description of clusters. A uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) showing the TCR avidities of each T cell illustrates that T cells of all avidities are represented in every cluster (Fig. ).
The distinction between low-avidity and high-avidity T cells is not restricted to exhaustion-related pathways. Other pathways with significant differences include those related to ribosomal content, oxidative phosphorylation, cytoskeletal rearrangement, responsiveness of IFNγ and regulation of apoptosis (Extended Data Fig. ).
 Trajectory inference, based on transcriptional dynamics and RNA velocity, was used to understand the differentiation of PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs. When inferring trajectories from TILs of all avidities, stem-like cells were predicted to give rise to NK-like and TEFF‐EX cells; TEFF‐EX cells then differentiate into either TEX1 or TEX2 cells (Fig. ).
Flow cytometry of PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs from 28-day Meth A-bearing mice (n = 20) was used to identify the phenotypes observed in Fig. and to determine if they were differentially distributed among cells with low and high avidity. Our validation of the transcriptionally derived signatures (Fig. ) using CITE-seq (Fig. ) allowed testing this using flow cytometry. The proportion of low-avidity or high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs that were stem-like (PD-1+TIM3−LY108+TCF1+), TEFF‐EX (PD-1+CX3CR1+ TOX+), TEX1 (PD-1+CX3CR1−TIM3+) or TEX2 (PD-1highCX3CR1−TIM3−) were compared (gating strategy shown in Extended Data Figs. and ). Stem-like and TEFF‐EX cells formed a significantly greater proportion of low-avidity than high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs. Additionally, a significantly smaller proportion of TEX1 and TEX2 cells were found in PDPRMUT-specific TILs with low avidity than high avidity (Fig. ). Broadly, all four phenotypes were observed, albeit in varying proportions, in low-avidity and high-avidity CD8+ TILs.

## T cell exhaustion is dependent on TCR avidity
PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs that had a single pair of TCRαβ sequences and belonged to clonotypes of more than 40 cells were selected from multi-omic sequencing for further analysis (Fig. ). The threshold of 40 cells per clonotype was chosen to overcome extreme skewness and outliers. Cells in a clonotype had identical nucleotide sequences for CDR1, CDR2 and CDR3 for both TCRα and TCRβ. Each clonotype was assigned a unique numerical identifier and a color (Fig. ). Unbiased k-mean clustering on the proportion of low-avidity, medium-avidity and high-avidity T cells in each clonotype (shown in Fig. ) identified two clusters, KM_1 (containing clonotypes 2, 3, 5, 6, 11, 13, 27, 29, 43 and 50) and KM_2 (containing clonotypes 8, 9, 24, 32 and 44). The KM_1 group had a significantly greater proportion of cells with measured low or medium avidity than KM_2. The KM_2 group had a significantly greater proportion of cells with measured high avidity than KM_1 (Fig. ). Cells in the KM_2 group expressed significantly higher levels of genes (see Supplementary Table for a full list) related to TCR signaling or TCR-pMHC affinity than those in KM_1 (Fig. ).
 As chronic and excessive TCR signaling contributes to T cell exhaustion , , we sought to understand the role of TCR avidity on CD8+ TIL differentiation toward the exhausted phenotype. The proportion of cells in each clonotype that was stem-like, TEFF‐EX and TEX (the sum of TEX1 and TEX2) was compared between the two KM groups. The KM_2 group had a significantly greater proportion of TEX cells and a significantly smaller proportion of TEFF‐EX cells than KM_1 (Fig. ). There was no difference in the proportion of stem-like cells between the two KM groups (Fig. ). Median surface expression of CX3CR1 (Fig. ) was significantly higher, while that of TIM3 (Fig. ) was significantly lower, in the KM_1 group. Cells in the KM_1 group expressed higher levels of the cytotoxicity-associated genes Gzmk and Nkg7, while those in the KM_2 group expressed higher levels of suppression-associated genes, such as Tgfb1, Cd274 and Entpd1 (Extended Data Fig. ).
 To identify potential avidity-dependent differences in TIL differentiation, trajectory inference based on transcriptomes and RNA velocity was carried out at two levels, that is, clusters and individual cells; identical results were obtained. The most confidently inferred trajectory at the cluster level was for KM_1 (Fig. ) and KM_2 (Fig. ). Analysis at the single-cell level is shown in Extended Data Fig. . At both levels of analyses, differentiation of stem-like cells toward TEFF‐EX cells and NK-like cells did not differ between the two KM groups. However, TEFF‐EX cells in the KM_1 group were restricted to differentiation toward TEX1, while TEFF‐EX cells in the KM_2 group differentiated predominantly toward TEX2.

## Large variation in measured TCR avidity in clonotypes
Figure shows that each clonotype consisted of cells with low, medium and high avidities despite their identical TCR sequences for both α and β chains. Ten clonotypes (KM_1) had predominantly low avidity and five (KM_2) had predominantly high avidity (Fig. ). Binding of the CD8 molecule to MHC-I greatly increases the strength of the TCR–pMHC-I interaction . To further understand the differences in the measured avidity in cells of the same clonotype, the mean expression of Cd8a and Cd8b1 was compared between cells with a low or high measured avidity. In a given clonotype, cells with a high measured avidity had significantly higher mean expression of Cd8a, but not Cd8b1, than cells with a low measured avidity (Fig. ). Variation in the number of TCR molecules on the T cell surface can also contribute to differences in TCR avidity . Indeed, cells with a high measured avidity had significantly higher mean expression of the TCRα chain (Trac) than cells with a low measured avidity in the same clonotype (Fig. ).
 Changes in TCR avidity are associated with changes in TCR signaling . Cells with low and high avidity in clonotypes were analyzed for corresponding changes in TCR signaling-related transcription factors NR4A1 and NFKB2. Indeed, when compared in a pairwise fashion, mean expression of Nr4a1 and Nfkb2 is significantly higher in high-avidity T cells than in low-avidity T cells of the same clonotype (Fig. ). Low-avidity and high-avidity cells of the same clonotype were also compared for avidity-dependent differences in T cell phenotype. High-avidity populations consisted of significantly more TEX and fewer TEFF‐EX cells than the low-avidity populations in the same clonotype (Fig. ), as previously observed in the total TIL population agnostic of the clonotypic composition (Figs. and ).

## Only low-avidity T cells respond to checkpoint blockade
We sought to test if TCR avidity determines the response to immune checkpoint blockade in vivo. As controls, 1,000 tetramer-negative CD8+ T cells were adoptively transferred (intravenously) into mice bearing 10-day-old Meth A tumors, which were also treated with anti-PD-1, CTLA-4 or isotype control antibody. Neither treatment had any effect on tumor growth as measured using growth kinetics or survival (Fig. ).
 In another arm, 1,000 low-avidity or high-avidity CD8+ T cells (isolated as in Fig. ) were adoptively transferred into mice bearing 10-day-old Meth A tumors, followed by treatment with anti-PD-1, CTLA-4 or isotype control antibody. Two distinct observations were made. First, as shown in Fig. , adoptively transferred low-avidity T cells mediated tumor control, while high-avidity T cells did not. Second, PD-1 or CTLA-4 blockade enhanced the antitumor activity of low-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells but had no effect on the activity of high-avidity T cells (Fig. ). This improvement in tumor control was also reflected in the improved survival of mice receiving low-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells with either PD-1 or CTLA-4 blockade in vivo. No such improvement in survival was seen in mice receiving high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells (Fig. ).
The effect of the checkpoint blockade on the endogenous response to PDPRMUT was examined. Six-day-old tumor-bearing mice (n = 20 per group) were treated with anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4 or isotype control antibody. The total number of low-avidity and high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific TILs in mice treated with anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4 or isotype control antibodies was compared. The number of stem-like TILs with low and high avidity was compared in mice treated with anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4 or isotype control antibodies. The number of low-avidity stem-like TILs increased in mice treated with antibodies to PD-1 or CTLA-4; no such change was noted in high-avidity stem-like TILs (Fig. ).

## High-avidity CD8+ TILs in human cancers are more exhausted
We sought to develop a method for the in silico determination of TCR avidity using only single-cell gene expression data. All the antigen-specific CD8+ TILs were divided evenly into a training and a test dataset. Differential gene expression analysis between cells with low and high measured avidities in the training dataset was used to identify 375 genes (Supplementary Table ). Expression of these genes in the low-avidity, medium-avidity and high-avidity cells of the training and test datasets is shown in Fig. . The test and training datasets showed nearly identical patterns of gene expression (Pearson r2 = 0.998). Using these differentially expressed genes (DEGs), a composite avidity score was derived using the method described elsewhere . This score was applied to the low-avidity and high-avidity TILs of the test dataset; the avidity score distinguishes low-avidity and high-avidity populations with very high statistical significance (P < 10−275) (Fig. ). Populations with 95% purity (for low avidity) and 96% purity (for high avidity) can be obtained by taking cells one s.d. below and above the mean avidity score, respectively (Fig. ).
 TILs in the human Pan-Cancer TIL Atlas were analyzed using the avidity score. Nicolas et al. recently showed that the proximal TCR signaling network is highly conserved both qualitatively and quantitatively across human and mouse T cells; thus, this network should behave similarly when applied to human and mouse T cells . This TIL Atlas includes scRNA-seq data from the CD8+ TILs of 36 patients with cancer across eight cancer types. Unlike the mouse data (Fig. ), the distribution of the avidity score in human TILs is bimodal and not normal (Fig. ). This lack of a normal distribution warranted refinement of the mouse avidity score for human TILs. To eliminate noise, we looked at genes that are common between the 375 DEGs that constitute the basis of mouse avidity score, and the 2,703 gene targets of TCR-signaling-related transcription factors (see the legend to Fig. ) as predicted by the ARCHS4 database . The resulting 97-gene list, which constitutes the basis of the human avidity score, was used to characterize the human TIL population (Supplementary Table ) as in Fig. and resulted in a positively skewed normal distribution (Fig. ). Differential gene expression analysis of the pseudobulk transcriptomes of human TILs with low and high avidity based on the human avidity score showed significantly higher expression of exhaustion-related genes (CD38, TOX2, ENTPD1, CTLA4, HAVCR2, TOX, PDCD1 and TIGIT) in high-avidity TILs, while low-avidity TILs expressed significantly higher levels of stemness-related genes (TCF7, LEF1, IL7R and CCR7) (Fig. ). Expectedly, human TILs deemed as having high avidity by the human avidity score expressed significantly higher levels of CD8A and TRAC than T cells deemed as having low avidity (Extended Data Fig. ).
 

## Low-avidity human TILs are predictive of response to immunotherapy
Pretreatment scRNA-seq of human CD8+ TILs of 37 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma , melanoma and triple-negative breast cancer was used to estimate the proportion of low-avidity and high-avidity T cells in each patient by the human avidity score. Responders (according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) or modified RECIST criteria) to PD-1 or CTLA-4 blockade had significantly higher proportions of low-avidity CD8+ TILs (P < 0.05) and lower proportions of high-avidity CD8+ TILs than nonresponders (P < 0.001) (Fig. ).

## Discussion
Our results place low-avidity CD8+ T cells at the center of the natural immune response to several neoepitopes of two mouse cancers with different haplotypes and histologies. Retrospectively analyzed data from a small series of human cancers are consistent with the data from mice. The relative resistance of low-avidity cells to exhaustion forms the mechanistic basis for their superior antitumor activity. At every level of resolution tested, using flow cytometry analysis of surface markers on antigen-specific CD8+ T cells, or scRNA-seq of the antigen-specific CD8+ T cells, or at the level of TCR clonotypes, or at the level of individual cells in each clonotype, the connection between avidity and exhaustion is persistent and significant as also pointed out elsewhere . Low-avidity and high-avidity CD8+ T cells in the same clonotype (Fig. ) show the same association between avidity and exhaustion as observed between the total low-avidity and high-avidity populations. Identical contributions of TCR avidity on T cell phenotype were seen in interclonotypic (Fig. ) and intraclonotypic (Fig. ) comparisons. Analysis of CD8+ TILs of 36 patients with cancer across eight cancer types, agnostic of their antigenic specificity, arrived at the same linkage between higher avidity and exhaustion.
Our results show that low-avidity neoepitope-specific CD8+ T cells are the sole responders to the PD-1 and CTLA-4 blockades, as also shown independently . Among patients, responders to the PD-1 blockade had higher proportions of low-avidity CD8+ TILs and lower proportions of high-avidity CD8+ TILs than nonresponders. The correlation between response to the PD-1 blockade and the activity of neoepitopes has been established previously , .
We observed with some surprise that mice with high-avidity T cells did worse than control mice regarding tumor growth and survival. It is interesting in this context that Vignali et al. observed that exhausted CD8+ TILs, which constitute a large proportion of high-avidity T cells, possessed the transcriptional features of CD4+ regulatory T cells (Treg) and suppressed T cell proliferation ex vivo. We observed that high-avidity T cells differentially expressed higher levels of PDL1 and TGFβ, which are among the transcriptional features of Treg cells (Extended Data Fig. ). Vignali et al. also showed that CD39 is needed in exhausted T cells for their immunosuppressive property. High-avidity T cells also differentially expressed higher levels of CD39 and HIF1A, a marker of hypoxia (Extended Data Fig. ).
Our study revealed an unexpected diversity of avidities among cells of a single clonotype in vivo. This observation is not limited to one or few clonotypes; it is seen in all clonotypes. This variation derives, in part, from natural variation of CD8α expression among daughter cells of the same clonotype. Kroger and Alexander-Miller reported how CD8+ T cells dynamically modulate CD8 expression in vitro, possibly by fine-tuning the sensitivity of the T cell to the antigen. The observation that CD8 levels can determine the performance of T cells in a tetramer-binding assay as an independent variable has also been reported – . Our study showed that this variation occurs in vivo in an endogenous response and is the rule rather than the exception.
The evidence that our results and conclusions apply to human cancers is necessarily limited at present, although analysis of human cancers and T cell responses to them (Fig. ) supports the applicability of the avidity score to human T cell responses. Future work will involve isolation of antigen-specific low-avidity and high-avidity T cells and testing of their antitumor activity by adoptive transfer in patients. Although several studies reported isolation of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells of well-characterized clonotypes against human cancers , – and showed activity in vivo upon adoptive transfers in large numbers, such T cells invariably have higher avidity; low-avidity CD8+ T cells against human cancer antigens, with proven activity in vivo, are currently unavailable. The modified tetramer decay assay developed in this study can aid in the isolation of such low-avidity CD8+ T cells. The human avidity score developed in this study can aid in quality assessment of low-avidity and high-avidity T cells. In fact, the possibility may be entertained that the human avidity score, in and of itself, may correlate with antitumor activity in vivo, independently of the putative relationship with avidity.
Our results may have a bearing on the prospects of cancer immunotherapy. Avidity enhancement of T cells continues to be actively sought , – ; while that approach is intuitively attractive, adoptive transfer of high-avidity T cells into patients has culminated in acute, immunologically dependent, severe toxicity or death in most patients , , . Our results suggest that the opposite direction, that is, avidity reduction in T cells, by modifying the TCR or CD8a or using other methods, is more appropriate. However, this suggestion is tempered by the fact that there is no evidence to date that decreasing T cell avidity leads to reprogramming of the state of exhaustion of T cells. An indirect approach to avidity reduction in vivo may lie in enhancing the CD4 response to neoepitopes ; CD4 help promotes priming of low-avidity CD8+ T cells , and helps prevent exhaustion . Finally, the avidity score derived from the quantitative expression of the genes shown in this study that is associated with high-avidity mouse T cells can also distinguish human high-avidity from low-avidity T cells. In light of our observation that the proportion of low-avidity T cells is predictive of the response to the checkpoint blockade in human cancers, the avidity score of pretreated TILs can be useful in identifying potential responders. Should the potency of low-avidity T cells in tumor control be further validated, the avidity score may be used to monitor and predict the effects of standard and experimental cancer treatments.

## Methods

## Mice
Female BALB/c (strain no. 000651) and CD45.1-BALB/c (strain no. 006584) mice were obtained from The Jackson Laboratory. All mice were between 6 and 8 weeks of age at the start of the experiment. The numbers of mice used are indicated in the figure legends. Mice were housed in specific pathogen-free animal facilities under a 12 h light–dark cycle at the University of Connecticut Health Center. The temperature in the housing facilities is kept at a maximum of 24 °C and a minimum of 21 °C. Humidity in the room is maintained at a maximum of 61% and a minimum of 25%. Mice were fed diet nos. 2918 and 2919 (Inotiv). All experiments conducted were part of animal protocols approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the University of Connecticut School of Medicine (AP-2000633-0325).

## Tumor growth assay
Meth A cells that have been in our laboratory since 1986 were originally obtained from Lloyd J. Old. They were passaged in ascites and were free from Mycoplasma contamination. The MC38-FABF tumor cell line was provided by A. B. Frey at the New York University Langone Medical Center in 2010. Cells were screened for Mycoplasma and tested negative.
Meth A cells were grown in the ascites of mice for 4 days before injection (95,000 live cells injected intradermally into the right flank of mice). MC38-FABF cells were grown in culture as described elsewhere . Tumors on mice were measured twice weekly with calipers for about 6 weeks. The maximum tumor size or burden permitted by our ethics committee is a diameter of 2 cm per 4.2 cm3volume. This limit was not violated. Mice with tumors exceeding a 15-mm diameter were euthanized. Tumor volumes were calculated using the equation 0.4 × l × w2 where l represents the length and w represents the width of the tumor. The tumor control index was calculated as described in ref. , using average tumor diameter measurements.

## In vivo immune checkpoint blockade
Isotype-treated mice were injected twice weekly (intraperitoneally) with 65 µg of mouse IgG2b (clone MPC-11) and weekly with 30 µg of rat IgG2a (clone 2A3) antibody. Anti-PD-1-treated mice were injected twice weekly (intraperitoneally) with 65 µg of mouse IgG2b and weekly with 30 µg of αPD-1 (clone RPMI-14) antibody. Anti-CTLA-4-treated mice were injected twice weekly (intraperitoneally) with 65 µg αCTLA-4 (clone 9D9) and weekly with 30 µg of Rat IgG2a antibody.

## Isolation of CD8+ T cells from tumors and LNs
Tumors were excised from mice, using scalpels, 28 days after tumor challenge and digested using the Tumor Dissociation Kit, mouse on a gentleMACS Octo Dissociator (Miltenyi Biotec). The digested tumor solution was then washed twice with cold PBS and subjected to density-based separation of lymphocytes using Lympholite M (Cedarlane Laboratories). LNs were excised and subjected to EasySep Mouse CD8+ T cell Isolation Kit (STEMCELL Technologies).

## Modified tetramer decay assay
CD8+ T cells, extracted as described in ‘Isolation of CD8+ T cells from tumors and LNs’, were then subjected to Fc blocking with mouse IgG isotype antibody (1:50 dilution, Invitrogen) for 20 min on ice. Cells were washed and resuspended in tetramer staining solution containing 1% v/v tetramers (MBL Technologies) loaded with the same antigen labeled with either BV421 or PE at a concentration of 10 million cells per ml for 30 min on ice. The tetramer staining solution was then washed off and cells were stained with a surface antibody staining solution for 20 min on ice. We observed that inclusion of an anti-PE antibody during tetramer decay prevented the dissociation of TetPE over 120 min; thus, the tetramer was stabilized (Extended Data Fig. , right and Extended Data Fig. ). In contrast, the nonstabilized TetBV421 dissociated normally (Extended Data Fig. ). Stabilized binding of TetPE served as the reference point against which decay of TetBV421 was compared. The addition of a stabilizing antibody was a critical innovation in this assay: in its absence, low-avidity and medium-avidity cells are indistinguishable from antigen-nonspecific T cells because they are also tetramer-negative.
Therefore, cells were split into three samples and subjected to tetramer decay at room temperature in anti-H2-Dd (clone 34-5-8S, 20 µg ml−1) antibody for either 15 (low avidity), 75 (medium avidity) or 105 (high avidity) minutes. Anti-PE (clone PE0001, 1:100 dilution) was added at either 0 (low and high avidity) or 45 (medium avidity) minutes to stabilize the binding of the PE-labeled tetramer.
Staining was then analyzed using flow cytometry (LSR-II & Cytek Aurora, BD Sciences) or isolated using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACSAria II, BD Sciences). If cells were to be isolated, samples were immediately placed in ice and subjected to sorting at 4 °C. If cells were to be used for flow cytometry, cells were mixed into a 1-ml fix and permeabilization solution, made using the Foxp3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set (Invitrogen), for 30 min on ice before intracellular staining with the indicated flow cytometry antibodies for 60 min on ice.
Operationally, T cells shedding the tetramers during the first 15 min were defined as having low avidity (Fig. ) and were sorted as TetPE+TetBV421−. Tetramer-associated T cells after 105 min of dissociation were defined as having high avidity and were sorted as TetPE+TetBV421+. To isolate medium-avidity T cells in a separate reaction, TetPE and TetBV421 were allowed to dissociate from T cells for 45 min to allow for the exclusion of low-avidity T cells. Anti-PE stabilizing antibody was added to the assay at t45 to prevent further dissociation of TetPE while still allowing the dissociation of TetBV421 for 30 min. Thus, medium-avidity T cells were sorted as TetPE+TetBV421− at t75 (Fig. ). This assay was repeated more than 100 times with reproducible results for all antigens tested.

## Staining for flow cytometry and CITE-seq
Before surface staining, cells were incubated with mouse IgG isotype antibody (1:50 dilution, Invitrogen) for 20 min on ice. Cells were then stained with labeled antibodies against CD8α (clone KT15, 1:200 dilution), CD45.1 (clone A-20, 1:100 dilution), PD-1 (clone RMPI-30, 1:200 dilution), TCR-β (clone H57-597, 1:200 dilution), TIM3 (clone 5D12, 1:200 dilution), TOX (clone TXRX10, 1:50 dilution), LY108 (clone 13G3, 1:200 dilution), CX3CR1 (clone SA011F11, 1:100 dilution), pZAP70-Y292 (clone A16048B, 1:20 dilution) or TCF1 (clone C63D9, 1:50 dilution), when indicated.

## Immunization and isolation of CD8+ T cells
Bone marrow isolated from the tibia and femur of syngeneic mice were cultured in complete Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 (RPMI 1640) medium with 20 ng ml−1 of murine granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (PeproTech) for 1 week to generate bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs). Mice were immunized (intradermally) with 6–9 million BMDCs pulsed with 100 µM of the indicated peptide in complete RPMI 1640 for 2 h at 37 °C. Five-to-seven days after immunization, vaccine-draining LNs were excised and isolated with the EasySep Mouse CD8+ T cell Isolation Kit followed by sorting of control (tetramer-negative), low-avidity or high-avidity cells as described in ‘Modified Tetramer Decay Assay’.

## Tetramer re-decay
Cells obtained using the method described in ‘Immunization and isolation of CD8+ T cells’ were sorted as TetPE+TetBV421− and TetPE+TetBV421+ at t15 after tetramer staining and tetramer decay. We then re-stained these cells with tetramers in both colors, so that all cells were TetPE+TetBV421+ again, and subjected them to another round of tetramer decay for 15 min before analysis using flow cytometry.

## Ex vivo restimulation
Ten thousand control, low-avidity or high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells were isolated as described in ‘Immunization and isolation of CD8+ T cells’ and cocultured with 1,000,000 congenic splenocytes pulsed with 10 µM PDPRMUT. To determine the phosphorylation of ZAP70, cells were cocultured in complete RPMI 1640 for 5 min at 37 °C. To determine IFNγ production, cells were cocultured with 1,000,000 BMDCs pulsed with 10 µM PDPRMUT and brefeldin A (1:1,000 dilution) in complete RPMI 1640 for 4 h at 37 °C. Cells were first stained for the expression of surface molecules for 20 min on ice, then fixed and permeabilized using the Foxp3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer for 30 min on ice before intracellular staining with the indicated flow cytometry antibodies for 60 min on ice. Staining was then detected using flow cytometry (Cytek Aurora) followed by analysis using FlowJo.

## In vitro killing assay
Five thousand congenic splenocytes were pulsed with 10 µM PDPRMUT in complete RPMI 1640 for 2 h at 37 °C and used as target cells for the killing assay. Five thousand CellTrace Violet-labeled congenic splenocytes pulsed at 37 °C with dimethyl sulfoxide for 2 h and used as antigen-negative cells for the killing assay. Ten thousand control, low-avidity or high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific T cells were isolated as described in ‘Immunization and Isolation of CD8+ T cells’ and cocultured with target cells and antigen-negative cells for 4 h at 37 °C. Killing of target and bystander cells was assessed using flow cytometry (Cytek Aurora) followed by analysis using FlowJo. Specific killing, defined by the difference in the proportion of dead cells in the target population and the bystander population, is shown.

## Adoptive cellular transfer
One thousand cells, isolated as in ‘Immunization and Isolation of CD8+ T cells’ were injected retro-orbitally into mice 10 days after the tumor challenge. In experiments testing the efficacy of the immune checkpoint blockade, mice receiving control, low-avidity or high-avidity cells were treated with isotype, αPD-1 or αCTLA-4, as indicated in ‘In vivo immune checkpoint blockade’, starting 13 days after the tumor challenge.

## Preparation of samples for single-cell and multi-omic sequencing
Low-avidity, medium-avidity and high-avidity PDPRMUT-specific CD8+ TILs were stained with TotalSeq-C antibodies against PD-1, TIM3, LY108 and CX3CR1 (BioLegend) and sorted from 11 tumor-bearing mice as in ‘Modified tetramer decay assay’. About 6,000 cells for each condition were captured using the Next GEM Single Cell 5′ GEM Kit v2 (10X Genomics). Three libraries were generated for each condition using the Library Construction Kit, 5′ Feature Barcode Kit and Single Cell Mouse TCR Amplification Kit (10X Genomics) using the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 5′ Reagent Kit v2 (Dual Index) (CG000330 Rev D protocol) before next-generation sequencing with the NovaSeq system (Illumina).

## Preprocessing of scRNA-seq data
Sequencing data were preprocessed as described in Extended Data Fig. . Briefly, separate FASTQ files for gene expression, TCR sequences and CITE-seq were generated using CellRanger . Loom files containing RNA splicing information for each sample were then generated from FASTQ files for gene expression using Velocyto . Outputs from these analyses were integrated using the scanpy , scirpy , MUON and scVelo packages in Python. Normalization of the CITE-seq antibody counts was conducted using DSB , as implemented in the MUON package in Python. Low-quality cells (with fewer than 500 genes, greater than 5% mitochondrial gene content) were filtered out based on gene expression. The remaining cells were subjected to unbiased clustering based on their transcriptomes (Extended Data Fig. ). Only clusters 0, 1, 3, 4 and 8, with significantly upregulated expression of CD8+ T cell marker genes (Cd8b1, Trac and Cd3e), were used for the subsequent analysis (Extended Data Fig. ).

## scRNA-seq processing
The remaining cells were then subjected to principal component analysis, neighbor graph analysis and UMAP graph analysis before being clustered with scanpy using the Leiden algorithm at a resolution of 0.6. The intersection of DEGs between clusters using three different statistical methods (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, t-test with overestimated variance and logistic regression) was then used to characterize the clusters. All plots for subsequent analyses were created using the scanpy and scVelo packages in Python.

## Cluster annotation
The description of the clusters is based solely on DEGs (Fig. ) (see Supplementary Table for the full list of genes). Stem-like cells expressed significantly higher memory-associated markers (Tcf7, Xcl1 and Bcl6) – and significantly lower exhaustion-associated markers (Pdcd1, Havcr2, Lag3, Tigit, Entpd1 and Cd38) , – . TEFF‐EX cells expressed significantly higher levels of transcription factors associated with effector functions (Tbx21, Ikzf2) than stem-like and TEX cells. They also expressed significantly higher levels of exhaustion-associated markers than stem-like cells, but significantly less than TEX1 cells. TEX2 cells were characterized by significantly high expression of certain markers of exhaustion (Tox, Entpd1 and Cd38), but low expression of others such as Havcr2. CD8+ T cells with a similar transcriptional profile have been reported previously . Finally, NK-like cells were characterized by significantly high expression of NK cell receptors such as Klrc1, Klrc2, Klrd1, Klrk1 and Klre1 (Fig. ).
Validation of these transcriptional signatures was sought using CITE-seq with antibodies to PD-1, LY108, TIM3 and CX3CR1 (Fig. ). This is particularly important for genes that are poorly detected by RNA-seq (Cx3cr1 and Slamf6). Stem-like cells expressed significantly higher levels of LY108 and significantly lower levels of PD-1 and TIM3. TEFF‐EX cells expressed significantly higher levels of the effector marker CX3CR1 and significantly lower levels of TIM3. TEX1 cells expressed significantly higher levels of TIM3 and significantly lower levels of CX3CR1 and LY108. TEX2 cells expressed significantly higher levels of PD-1 and significantly lower levels of TIM3 and CX3CR1. Finally, NK-like cells expressed significantly higher levels of TIM3 and significantly lower levels of PD-1. Altogether, surface expression of these four proteins corroborated the phenotypic descriptions of the clusters based on their transcriptomes (Fig. ).

## TCR sequence and trajectory inference analysis
Cells with single-chain TCRαβ were grouped into clonotypes based on identical CDR1, CDR2 and CDR3 amino acid sequences on both chains using scirpy . Only clonotypes with more than 40 cells were used for subsequent analysis. Clonotypes were then clustered on the proportion of cells in each clonotype measured to have low, medium or high avidity using the k-mean algorithm using the scikit-learn package in Python. The resulting two clusters were deemed low-avidity and high-avidity clonotypes based on the significant difference of cells in each clonotype with low or high avidity, as measured in Fig. . Trajectory inference of low-avidity and high-avidity clonotypes was then conducted using scVelo .

## Gene score development
Sequenced cells were randomly assigned to either the test or training dataset using the sampling function of pandas with frac = 0.5. Low-avidity and high-avidity T cells in the training dataset were then subjected to differential gene expression analysis with DESeq2 (avidity and cluster were used as design variables) using PyDESeq2 (ref. ). Genes with a false discovery rate (FDR) of less than 0.1, a log2 fold change greater than 0.75 and a base mean greater than 1 were used as a gene score for the determination of low and high avidity in silico. This list was then intersected with predicted targets of TCR-related transcription factors, as annotated in the ARCHS4 database to develop a second gene list.

## Pan-human cancer TIL atlas
Preprocessed transcriptomic data from the Pan-Cancer TIL Atlas was obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (accession no. GSE156728). Gene scoring on all CD8+ T cells obtained was implemented using the scanpy package in Python through the scanpy.tl.score_genes function with the genes described in ‘Gene score development’ as inputs for the gene_list parameter. Cells one s.d. below and above the mean of the gene score were deemed low and high avidity, respectively. Differential gene expression analysis using DESeq, with avidity as the only design variable, was used to compare low-avidity and high-avidity T cells. DEGs (as defined in ‘Gene score development’) were plotted using the volcano function of PyDESeq2 (ref. ).

## Immune checkpoint blockade response prediction
Transcriptomic data of pretreated TILs was obtained from the GEO and European Genome-phenome Archive (EGA) database for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (accession no. EGAS00001007547), melanoma (accession no. GSE120575) and triple-negative breast cancer (accession no. GSE169246). CD8+ T cell clusters, as defined using the Leiden algorithm, were then extracted to determine avidity in silico using the methods described in ‘Pan-human cancer TIL atlas’. The proportion of low-avidity and high-avidity CD8+ T cells was then compared between responders and nonresponders using a two-way ANOVA.

## Statistical methods
Statistical tests were conducted using Prism (GraphPad Software). Statistical tests included paired t-tests, unpaired t-tests, one-way ANOVA, two-way ANOVA and Mantel–Cox survival analysis. Paired t-tests were used in place of unpaired t-tests when comparing matched low-avidity and high-avidity T cells derived from the same samples. When present, error bars denote the s.d. of samples.
No statistical methods were used to predetermine sample sizes, but our sample sizes are similar to those reported in previous publications . Individual data points are shown in each figure; data distribution was assumed to be normal but this was not formally tested. For the purpose of randomization, all mice that participated in an experiment were placed in a single container; they were randomly picked and assigned sequentially to individual groups. No data were excluded from the analyses. Blinding was not necessary because no data were excluded and only objective measurable criteria were used.
