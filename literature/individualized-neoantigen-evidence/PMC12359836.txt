## Background
Tumor vaccines enhance the immune response and cytotoxicity of tumor-specific T cells and show promising clinical therapeutic efficacy. Although most cervical cancer cells express HPV-related oncoproteins, some do not. Additionally, neoantigens worth attracting attention for cervical cancer (CC) may hold the potential for breakthroughs in the development of cervical cancer vaccines.

## Methods
A comprehensive computational analysis was conducted based on the tumor genome, transcriptome, and proteome data from 284 cervical cancer samples obtained from the TCGA database. Frequently mutated genes were identified. The levels of immune cell infiltration were analyzed using RNA-seq data, high-frequency mutated genes were identified as candidate genes that were significantly related to immune infiltration. Focusing on MHC class I epitopes recognized by CD8 + T cells, we predicted potential neoantigen peptides using the NetMHCpan-4.0 and NetCTL-1.2 algorithms. To further confirm the immunogenicity of the synthesized peptides, we performed flow cytometry and real-time PCR in vivo to examine markers of T cell activation and cytotoxicity. We also stimulated PBMC from patients with the corresponding HLA type with the synthesized peptides using an ELISpot assay.

## Results
We identified 30 highly mutated genes, among which TTN, PRKDC, PCLO, MUC17, HUWE1, RYR2, and CREBBP positively correlated with immune cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment. PCLO exhibited higher protein expression in tumor tissues than in the corresponding normal tissues, making it a potential tumor antigen. The PCLO peptides SISRFTLEK and LSEAGHFFY exhibited the highest predicted scores among the cancer antigens and strong immunogenicity in vivo.

## Conclusion
Our analysis highlights the potential of PCLO as a candidate gene for enhancing immune cell infiltration and activating immune responses in tumors. The peptides SISRFTLEK (PCLOL4169F) and LSEAGHFFY (PCLOA3000S) are promising targets for tumor vaccines.

## Introduction
Cervical cancer (CC) is one of the most common malignant tumors in women, with an estimated 606,000 new cases and 342,000 deaths worldwide in 2020 [ ]. Although the incidence of CC has significantly decreased in developed countries owing to early screening and the wide application of prophylactic human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccines, the prevention and control of CC remains a challenge in most developing countries. Immunotherapy has emerged as an important treatment strategy for CC, and tumor vaccines show great promise in cancer immunotherapy [ ]. Tumor vaccines have superior targeting effects and directly induce or amplify existing tumor-specific T-cell responses, making them highly promising for clinical applications.
The HPV E6/E7 protein plays a crucial role in the malignant transformation of normal cervical cells. Significant progress has been made in the development of therapeutic vaccines targeting HPV E6/E7 for CC treatment. VGX-3100, a DNA plasmid-based vaccine targeting HPV 16/18 E6 and E7, was recently shown to be effective in promoting the regression of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia 2/3 lesions or clearing HPV 16/18 viruses in a phase III clinical trial [ , ]. Furthermore, ongoing clinical trials are investigating a combination of HPV vaccines and immune checkpoint blockades. For example, a synthetic long-peptide HPV-16 vaccine, ISA 101, was reported to enhance the efficacy of nivolumab in incurable HPV-16-positive cancer, with an overall response rate of 33% (RECIST 1.1) [ ]. This combination treatment has entered phase II clinical trials, highlighting its potential and the need for further improvement. Recent research has also revealed that host cells do not express relevant pathogenic peptides in some cases, even when HPV is integrated into the host genome, providing a potential explanation for the differences in the therapeutic efficacy of HPV protein-related vaccines among patients [ ]. These findings highlight the importance of identifying novel immunotherapeutic targets for CC to compensate for the limited efficacy of HPV protein-based vaccines in certain patients.
The tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) is a highly dynamic and heterogeneous ecosystem composed of tumor cells, immune cells, stromal cells, and soluble mediators, where immunosuppressive mechanisms often dominate and contribute to immunotherapy resistance [ ]. During the antitumor immune response, antigen-presenting cells (APCs), such as B cells and dendritic cells, capture tumor-associated antigens and migrate through tumor-draining lymphatic vessels to the lymph nodes, where they prime naïve T cells. Upon antigen recognition, T cells undergo activation, clonal expansion, and differentiation into effector cells capable of mounting tumor-specific immune responses [ ]. Emerging evidence suggests that TIME-related biomarkers have a significant prognostic value. For instance, the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), which integrates platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte counts, has been validated as a robust predictor of clinical outcomes in lung cancer [ ]. Additionally, plasma cell signatures have demonstrated strong predictive power for immunotherapy respons [ ]. Notably, a favorable TIME is characterized by increased immune cell infiltration and robust antitumor immunity, which correlate with improved patient survival [ ]. These findings underscore the critical role of the TIME in shaping therapeutic efficacy and prognosis, suggesting that modulating its immunosuppressive nature could be pivotal for enhancing treatment outcomes in patients with cancer.
Genetic mutations are a committed step in the development and progression of cancer by altering the function and behavior of tumor cells. However, some of these mutations give rise to immunogenic proteins called tumor neoantigens, which stimulate the immune system to recognize and eliminate tumor cells [ , ]. Tumor neoantigens have emerged as highly valuable targets in clinical applications owing to their ability to induce a robust and specific immune response against cancer cells. One of the key characteristics of tumor neoantigens is their exclusive expression in tumor cells, whereas they are absent or minimally expressed in normal tissues [ , ]. Consequently, targeting tumor neoantigens has an immense potential for effective tumor immunotherapy and has been widely implemented in clinical practice. Foreign proteins generated by genomic mutations are the main source of neoantigens absent in normal tissues [ ]. Therefore, neoantigens are promising targets for proactive tumor immunotherapy. Importantly, mature genome sequencing and analysis techniques have sparked substantial attention toward leveraging genomic mutations for neoantigen discovery. There is growing interest in exploring neoantigens derived from single-nucleotide variants (SNV), which are the most prevalent type of genetic mutations in tumor cells [ ]. By focusing on single nucleotide variants (SNVs) and using genome sequencing methodologies, this study aimed to identify new antigens in tumor cells that could serve as potential targets for tumor immunotherapy.
Multiple studies have provided insights into the genomic landscape of cancer, revealing high-frequency mutation sites with implications for cancer diagnosis and treatment [ ]. Similarly, a population-based study identified high levels of mutations in the CC cell genome [ ]. Therefore, our study aimed to explore immunogenic neoantigens in CC, that could potentially offer new targets for immunotherapy. In this study, we conducted a combination analysis using tumor genomic whole-exome sequencing (WES) data, transcriptomic data, and data from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database. We identified a highly and frequently mutated gene, PCLO, which shows promise as a target for tumor vaccines against CC. In addition, we predicted MHC-associated peptides based on the PCLO mutation site using NetMHCpan-4.0, and NetCTL-1.2, and identified SISRFTLEK (PCLOL4169F) and LSEAGHFFY (PCLOA3000S) as highly promising targets for tumor vaccines. These findings open new avenues for immunotherapy research in CC and provide potential targets for the development of novel treatments.

## Materials and methods

## Variant calling and gene/protein expression
Sequencing data of RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and WES, along with clinical information for the CC cohort, were downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Somatic mutations in tumor samples were detected using blood samples or non-tumor tissues as controls, and data analysis and visualization were performed using RStudio (4.2.3). The maftools package was specifically used to analyze the WES data, and the ggplot2 package was used for visualization [ ]. The gene set variation analysis (GSVA) package was used to calculate the relative abundance of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL) in the patients to estimate immune infiltration in the tumor samples [ , ]. Protein expression of genes was validated using the human protein atlas (HPA), an immunohistochemistry platform that encompasses multiple cancers [ ] (www.proteinatlas.org) [ ].

## Genomic alteration analysis
The cBioPortal (http://www.cbioportal.org/) is an online tool that houses a wide range of cancer genomics datasets, and was used to conduct analysis of the mutation frequency and mutation types of PCLO across cancers, including the identification of specific mutation sites in amino acids of PCLO [ ].

## Functional and pathway enrichment analysis
The Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING) (www.string-db.org) was applied to predict the interaction relationship and construct protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks [ ]. This tool facilitated the search for the top genes that exhibited a positively correlation with PCLO across various cancers. The ggplot2 package and cluster Profiler package in R studio were used to perform a functional enrichment analysis of Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genes (KEGG) [ ] and gene ontology (GO) [ ] by comparing the PCLO mutated group with the PCLO wild-type group.

## Peptide prediction and synthesis
For each somatic mutation site, we utilized the NetMHCpan-4.0 [ ] and NetCTL-1.2 [ ] computational tools to identify HLA-restricted peptides [ ]. The criteria were as followed: (1) the length of peptide was 9 amino acids; (2) the mutation was considered immunogenic only if the mutant peptide showed strong binding affinity with MHC-I class molecule. %Rank_EL score was obtained from NetMHCpan-4.0 prediction method which indicates the likelihood of the peptide being a naturally existing peptide. A lower score corresponds to a higher possibility of predicted peptide being a non-natural peptide. Additionally, the NetCTL 1.2 method was used to identify CD8+ T cell epitopes [ ]. A higher score indicates a stronger likelihood of these peptides being potential ligands for MHC proteins. By comprehensively analyzing the scoring outcomes from both algorithms, we were able to identify the most promising neoantigen peptide candidates. The candidate peptides were synthesized by the company (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) following standard procedure. The purity of the synthesized peptides exceeded 95%, and dissolved in PBS (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) with 10% DMSO (Sigma-Aldrich, Poole, UK).

## Mouse immunization for immunogenicity testing
C57BL/6 mice were purchased from GemPharmatech, Co., Ltd (Jiangsu, China) and housed in specific pathogen free mouse facilities in the Institute of Experimental Animal Center of Tongji Hospital. To test the immunogenicity of predicted peptide, 100 μg of the synthesized peptide was mixed with 50 μg of poly-ICLC (ApexBio, Cat No. 24939-03-5) dissolved in 200 μl of phosphate-buffered solution (PBS). This mixture was then subcutaneously injected into the backs of male C57BL/6 mice (6 weeks of age). As a control group, the mice were injected subcutaneously with 50 μg poly-ICLC dissolved in 200ul PBS. Each mouse received two injections, administered on days 0 and 7, and sacrificed on day 14. Multiple organs were collected upon sacrifice, including heart, liver, renal, lung and spleen.

## Splenocyte isolation and antigen peptide stimulation
To collect splenocyte from sacrificed mice, the spleens were collected and washed once with PBS. Splenocytes were then isolated into a single-cell suspension by pushing the spleens through a 40-μm cell strainer. Cells were centrifuged for 5 min at 500 g at room temperature (RT), and the pellets were suspended in a red blood cell lysis buffer to remove erythrocytes. The resulting splenocytes were washed using 5 ml of PBS containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin solution. The cells were then counted (5 × 105 cells/well) and plated in a 6-well plate for further experiments.

## Processing of immune cells isolated from Splenocyte for flow cytometry analysis
Cultured splenocytes were incubated for 6 h at 37 °C in 5% CO2 with the synthesized peptide. Following incubation, the splenocytes were washed and stained using the following conjugated antibodies: anti-mCD3-APC (BD Biosciences, Cat No.553066), anti-mCD8-FITC (BD Biosciences, Cat No.564422), anti-mCD62L-PerCP-Cy5.5 (BD Biosciences, Cat No. 560513), and anti-mCD44-APC-CY7 (BD Biosciences, Cat No.560568) and Cytofix/Cytoperm solution (BD PharMingen) was used to fix and permeabilize the splenocytes before washing and incubation with anti-mIFN-γ-PerCP-Cy5.5 (BD Biosciences, Cat no.560660) anti-mCD107a-PE (BD Biosciences, Cat No. 558661). Spleen single cell suspension was adjusted the cell count to 1 × 106 cells/100 μl and antibodies was 1 μl/100 μl for 30 min in PBS containing 2% FBS at 4 °C. The subsequent detection was performed using a CytoFLEX Flow Cytometer. The data were analyzed and visualized using flowJo.V10 software.

## Analysis of immune-related gene expression qRT-PCR
Total RNA was extracted from spleen cells using TRIzol Reagent (Sigma, Cat No. T9424), followed by reverse transcribed into cDNA according to the manufacturer’s instructions (TaKaRa, Cat No. RR037A). Quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was carried out using ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Cat No.Q331) and analyzed using the Bio-Rad CFX96 TouchTM system. The relative mRNA expression levels of genes were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, normalized to Gapdh, which served as an internal loading control.

## Analyze the possible structural models of the candidate peptide and HLA-A molecule
To unravel the underlying structural basis of how neoantigen peptides interact with HLA-A complexes, we initiated by obtaining the protein crystal structures for HLA-A01:01 (ID 6MPP as templates) and HLA-A*03:01 (ID 7MLE as templates) from PDB protein structure database [ ]. Subsequently, these structures files were computationally imported into the HPEPDOCK Server, an online software platform, to simulate the docking of the neoantigen peptides into the peptide-binding grooves specific to HLA-A01:01 and HLA-A03:01 [ ]. This approach allowed us to gain insights into the molecular interactions and structural dynamics that govern the binding of neoantigen peptides to HLA-A complexes, which is crucial for understanding the immune response and its implications in cancer immunotherapy.

## ELISpot assay
PBMCs were cultured for 2 weeks in complete medium with IL-2, IL-7, IL-15 and peptide of predicted neo-antigens. ELISpot assays were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In brief, we seeded at 5 × 105 cells with 10 μg/mL of the synthesized peptide in each well at 37 °C in ELISpot plates pre-coated with anti-IFN-γ (Mabtech, Cat No.3420-2AST-2) for 24 h. Negative control wells lacked peptides, and positive control wells included Anti-CD3 mAb. The plates were washed and incubated with anti-human IFN-γ and then plates were developed for 20 min with BCIP/NBT Substrate (Mabtech). All assays were performed in duplicate. Finally, ELISpot plates were scanned and counted using an ImmunoSpot plate reader and associated software (Cellular Technologies, Ltd.).

## Hematoxylin–eosin staining
All tissues were immediately fixed in 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, sliced into 5-micron sections, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Images were captured using a slide scanning image system (SCANIM SQS-40P).

## Statistical analysis
Bioinformatical data analysis and visualization were conducted using R studio (http://www.R-project.org). The experimental data were statistically and visually analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. All measurement data are presented as mean ± SEM. Student’s t test was employed for comparisons between groups, and p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Preliminary screening of candidate gene for tumor neoantigens
Somatic genomic alterations in tumor tissues, including SNVs, base insertions and deletions, and gene fusions, are the main candidates for the production of tumor neoantigens [ ]. Among these, SNVs are the most prevalent type of mutations, and missense mutations are positively correlated with neoantigen load [ , ]. To investigate the highly mutated genes in CC, we downloaded sequencing data from 284 tumor samples diagnosed with CC from the TCGA database. High-depth WES and RNA-seq data were analyzed using the same procedure. As expected, the results revealed highly mutated genes in CC. The top 30 mutated genes in this cohort, including TTN, PIK3CA, KMT2C, MUC16, KMT2D, FLG, EP300, DMD, SYNE1, FBXW7, LRP1B, RYR2, DST, USH2A, ADGRV1, HUWE1, MUC17, LRP2, SYNE2, NAV3, NEB, PCLO, CREBBP, DNAH2, FAT1, MDN1, MUC5B, CSMD1, PRKDC, and PTEN, accounted for 7–29% of the population (Fig.  A). Moreover, WES identified over 4000 missense mutations, representing the dominant variant classification in the CC cohort (Fig.  B). Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were the most frequent variant types, with the most common SNV class being C > T (Fig.  C, E). The mean number of variants per sample was 73, which was relatively high compared to the pan-cancer data [ ], indicating the potential for neoantigen generation (Fig.  D). Additionally, the average tumor mutation burden in the TCGA CC cohort was relatively high across all cancers (Fig.  F). Exploratory analyses revealed no significant differences in the common clinical characteristics associated with CC prognosis, such as clinical stage, histological type, HPV type, and differentiation between patients with and without highly mutated genes (Figure S1). This suggested that the mutated genes were unbiased in the CC population. Collectively, these findings demonstrated the potential of exploring neoantigens in CC. 
However, not all mutation-derived neoantigens are potential targets for tumor vaccines because of their immune escape [ ]. Ideal neoantigens for tumor vaccines should be able to promote an immune response in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Therefore, we evaluated the infiltration of immune cells into each sample using RNA-seq data and examined the correlation between the above-mentioned highly mutated genes and immune cell infiltration. Using the ssGSEA algorithm, the infiltration scores of the 28 immune cell types showed that the 284 CC samples could be divided into two groups: high and low immune cell infiltration (Fig.  A). Interestingly, we observed significant variations in the tumor immune microenvironment across different samples, suggesting the complex nature of immune responses in CC [ ]. We analyzed the correlation between gene mutations and immune infiltration, as depicted in Fig.  B. Our method for screening target genes is based on identifying genes that exhibit a positive correlation with at least three or more immune cell types among the 28 immune cells, with a p-value of less than 0.05 (Table S1). Notably, we identified seven predicted mutated genes: TTN, PRKDC, PCLO, MUC17, HUWE1, RYR2, and CREBBP (P < 0.05), which exhibited the strongest positive correlations with immune cell infiltration. Taken together, these findings indicate that these genes encode tumor-specific antigens (TSA) with the potential to stimulate immune responses in the TME, making them attractive candidates for further investigation. 

## PCLO was the most potential target as a tumor vaccine in patients with CC
The transition from gene mutation to neoantigen presentation involves several complex stages. The expression of these mutated genes forms the basis for the elicitation of specific immune response [ ]. Subsequently, we analyzed the protein expression levels of the candidate genes using the HPA database. Initially, we assessed the differential protein expression levels of candidate genes between CC and the corresponding normal tissues. Figure  illustrates that, in general, most genes showed high or no significant expression in normal tissues, except for PCLO and PRKDC. This indicates that PCLO and PRKDC are the most likely candidates for TSA. Considering that immune cells primarily recognize tumor antigens during antitumor immunity and are important components of the TME, we also examined the expression of candidate genes in lymph nodes, which are the organs where the immune cells gather (Fig.  ). The data revealed that HUWE1, CREBBP, and PRKDC were positively expressed in the lymph nodes, whereas RYR2, TTN, PCLO, and MUC17 were not. Based on these results, PCLO has emerged as the most promising candidate tumor vaccine for CC. 

## The underlying biological mechanisms of PCLO mutation in CC
To investigate the biological functions of PCLO, a protein–protein interaction network was used to explore the relationship between PCLO and functionally related proteins. The top ten proteins associated with PCLO are depicted in the network (Fig.  A). Among these, seven proteins (BSN, SYN1, RAB3A, RIMS2, RAPGEF4, CSMD3, and LRP1B) were co-expressed with PCLO and were involved in regulating the formation, transport, and exocytosis of vesicles. Intriguingly, we also observed a positive correlation between LRP1B, which has been reported as a tumor suppressor in various cancers, such as head and neck cancer, and TMB in lung cancer [ , ]. 
To gain further insights into the biological processes associated with PCLO mutations in CC, we performed a differential analysis that identified 2977 differentially expressed genes between the wild-type and mutated-type groups (Fig.  B). Subsequently, enrichment analysis was conducted to determine the biological processes associated with the PCLO mutation groups. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genome (KEGG) analysis revealed that the upregulated genes in PCLO mutations were predominantly enriched in the metabolism of xenobiotics, retinol, tyrosine, and pyruvate, as well as in chemical carcinogenesis and secretion of bile, saliva, and pancreas. In contrast, the downregulated genes were particularly abundant in salivary secretion, regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, calcium signaling pathway, and Staphylococcus aureus infection (Fig.  C). Regarding molecular functions, genes linked to PCLO mutations were enriched in the matrices of cellular enzyme inhibitor activity, signaling receptor activator, and glycosaminoglycan binding (Fig.  D). In terms of biological processes, the genes associated with PCLO mutations were primarily involved in the regulation of peptidase activity, body fluids, hormone metabolism, and acute inflammatory responses (Fig.  E). Considering the cellular components, PCLO-mutated genes were principally associated with extracellular matrix collagen, apical plasma membrane, and vesicle lumen (Fig.  F). Taken together, these findings suggest that PCLO mutations do not drive malignant behavior in tumor cells, but instead stimulate immune-related signaling pathways with potential immunogenicity.

## PCLO was a potentially shared neoantigen across cancers
Gene mutations are common in tumors, and some genes, such as KRAS, are frequently mutated across multiple tumor types [ ]. The neoantigens generated by these mutations can be shared among patients, making them readily accessible targets for immunotherapy [ ]. To further explore the expression of PCLO pan-cancers, we investigated the protein expression levels in 20 cancer types and 45 normal tissues using the HPA database. The results revealed that PCLO was upregulated in four other cancer types compared to their corresponding normal tissues, including lung, breast, ovarian, and thyroid cancers (Fig.  A, B, and Supplementary Fig. S2). Subsequently, we explored the prevalence of PCLO somatic mutations in the clinical samples of these five cancer types from TCGA datasets using the cBioPortal database, which contains 3835 samples of breast cancer, 607 samples of CC, 3702 samples of lung cancer, 1691 samples of ovarian cancer and 2001 samples of thyroid cancer. These results revealed multiple dispersed mutation sites within the amino acid sequence of PCLO, indicating that PCLO can produce multiple types of mutated peptides in different cancers (Fig.  C). Furthermore, the most frequent mutation types were mutations and amplifications, which were most observed in lung cancer subtypes (Fig.  D). These results demonstrate that PCLO is overexpressed and widely mutated in tumor tissues across five different types of cancers, including those of the cervix, lung, breast, ovary, and thyroid, indicating that PCLO has the potential to serve as a source of shared neoantigens. These data suggest that patients with these cancers may benefit from immunotherapy targeting PCLO. 

## Potential neoantigen peptide sequences of PCLO
Neoantigens, primarily derived from gene mutations and SNV, are unique to tumor cells and are highly likely to activate T cell responses in vivo [ – ]. Multiple in silico methods have been employed to predict optimal neoantigen peptide sequences based on the affinity of mutant peptides for MHC and TCR recognition [ ]. As mentioned above, PCLO was identified as a potential target among the five cancer types, and its ability to elicit a cytotoxic T-lymphocyte response as a neoantigen was further investigated. Through the use of NetMHCpan-4.0 and NetCTL-1.2, a total of 114 predicted neo-peptides from SNVs of PCLO were identified as potential immunogenic peptides specific to human leukocyte antigen (HLA) types, HLA-A01:01, HLA-A02:01, HLA-A03:01, HLA-A11:01, and HLA-A24:02, which represent the most common variants of the HLA-A type [ , , ] (Table  ).
 
As shown in Table  , the mutation site present in the CC sample corresponded to the 9th-ranked neoantigen peptide SISRFTLEK. Concurrently, the top neoantigen peptide was identified in lung cancer samples. Furthermore, molecular docking simulations were used to predict the interaction models of these peptides with their respective HLA complexes using the HPEPDOCK Server [ ]. The resulting structural models demonstrated that the peptides SISRFTLEK and LSEAGHFFY could bind to the grooves of HLA-A01:01 and HLA-A03:01, respectively, as illustrated in Fig.  . This indicates the potential role of these peptides in the immune response against cervical and lung cancers and provides valuable insights for the development of targeted immunotherapies. 

## Peptides SISRFTLEK showed significant immunogenicity in vivo
To evaluate the immunogenic potential of the PCLO mutation site detected in CC, we synthesized the mutated peptide SISRFTLEK (henceforth PCLO-MT) and the corresponding wild-type peptide SISRLTLEK (henceforth PCLO-WT) and administered them subcutaneously to female C57BL/6 mice on days 0 and 7. On day 14, the mice were humanely euthanized and their organs were carefully harvested for further analysis (Figure S3A). It is well-established that CD107a and IFN-γ serve as key indicators for the assessment of T cell activation, particularly in the context of evaluating the efficacy of tumor therapeutic vaccines in vivo [ , ]. Subsequent flow cytometry analysis of CD107a and IFN-γ was conducted on the harvested splenocytes following in vitro stimulation with PCLO-MT or PCLO-WT to identify the immunogenicity. The results indicated that the subcutaneous administration of PCLO-MT peptide led to a marked increase in the number of IFN-γ and CD107a positive cells compared to PBS or PCLO-WT peptide treatment groups. This enhanced immune cell activation highlighted the ability of neoantigen peptides to elicit a robust cellular immune response in mice. We also quantified the expression levels of additional T cell cytotoxicity markers, including Gzmb and Tnfrsf9 (4-1BB), using qPCR. The data revealed a significant upregulation of these effector markers in the groups treated with PCLO-MT peptide, further confirming their immunogenic properties and ability to activate cellular immunity (Fig.  A, B, and S3C). 
Preclinical studies have demonstrated that memory CD8+ T cells provide long-term protection against secondary tumor attacks. Therefore, the ability to form a long-lasting population of memory CD8+ T cells is an important indicator for evaluating the efficacy of vaccines [ ]. Therefore, we further detected markers of memory CD8+ T cells using flow cytometry and measured the expression of transcription factors that promote the formation of memory T cells using qRT-PCR. The results showed that the proportion of memory CD8+ T cell subsets was significantly increased (Fig.  C), and the expression of transcription factors that regulate the differentiation of CD8+ T cells into memory populations was upregulated in immunized mice treated with PCLO-MT peptide (Figure S3D). These findings suggest that neoantigen peptides have the potential to stimulate the production of long-lasting immune responses. Furthermore, histological analysis via H&E staining of important organs (kidneys, lungs, spleen, liver, and heart) revealed no significant changes including redundant inflammatory cell infiltration, owing to the PCLO-MT peptide, indicating that the PCLO-mutated peptide is safe(Figure S3B). This indicates that the PCLO-mutated peptide was capable of eliciting immune responses while maintaining a low level of toxicity.
In addition to their detection in mice, the immunogenicity of these peptides has been determined by stimulating immune cells in humans. Given that LSEAGHFFY is ranked as the leading neoantigen peptide, we investigated its potential to activate an immune response. Following the experimental protocol applied in Fig.  D, we isolated peripheral blood mono­nuclear cells (PBMC) from six healthy donors with corresponding HLA-typing and performed an enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) assay to quantify IFN-γ secretion against neoantigen peptides. We observed strong reactivity against the peptide in the wells treated with SISRFTLEK and LSEAGHFFY compared to the untreated control (DMSO) (Fig.  E).
In conclusion, these results highlighted the immunogenicity of the peptides SISRFTLEK (PCLOL4169F) and LSEAGHFFY (PCLOA3000S). This underscores their potential as viable targets for the development of novel immunotherapeutic strategies. These findings provide a compelling rationale for the development of these peptides as candidates for cancer vaccines, to enhance the immune response against cancer cells.

## Discussion
Neoantigens are highly immunogenic due to their lack of central tolerance, which means that they can effectively stimulate the immune system to recognize and attack cancer cells. Initiating widespread and diverse T cell responses and simultaneous immune memory to achieve tumor reduction and prevent tumor metastasis and recurrence are the significant advantages of neoantigen vaccines. Recent clinical trials have demonstrated that neoantigen vaccines can induce high-frequency polyfunctional T cell responses, with some patients exhibiting T cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment at levels previously unattainable with traditional therapies [ ]. Neoantigen vaccines are increasingly being explored in combination with other immunotherapies such as immune checkpoint inhibitors. This combination strategy aims to overcome immune tolerance and enhance overall therapeutic outcomes. For example, a phase II study combining a personalized neoantigen vaccine with pembrolizumab for high-risk resected melanomas demonstrated a significant increase in recurrence-free survival [ ]. In this study, we performed rigorous screening to identify two immunogenic peptides derived from PCLO. Neoantigen screening criteria included a positive correlation with immune infiltration, elevated expression in tumor cells, non-promotion of tumor progression, and predictive immunogenicity screening. The immunogenicity of these peptides was confirmed by mouse experiments and ELISpot assays using PBMC from the human blood. The findings of this study offer additional avenues for antigenic screening for the development of tumor vaccines.
Neoantigens are composed of personalized neoantigens specifically discovered for each patient or shared neoantigens expressed in different patients or cancer types [ ]. Because of the patient specificity of personalized neoantigens, they can only be used to target certain patients; however, the larger the population to which they are applicable, the more valuable the vaccine becomes [ ]. With the latest progress in high-throughput sequencing, screening strategies for neoantigens can help discover shared antigens that target the immunogenic epitopes of malignant tumors. In this study, we predicted the highly frequently mutated genes with potential immune-related characteristics in CC. We conducted a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis to further extend the application potential of the identified genes. Intriguingly, our findings demonstrate that PCLO has high-frequency mutations across multiple cancer types, including cervical, lung, breast, ovarian, and thyroid cancers. This strategy allows as many patients as possible to benefit from the identified neoantigen peptides. Furthermore, our investigation revealed that PCLO was overexpressed at the protein level in tumor tissues compared to that in the corresponding normal tissues, as shown in Fig.  . Thus, the target identified in our study has certain patient coverage and possesses potential clinical application prospects that can be applied to five different cancer types, allowing as many patients as possible to benefit from this target.
Neoantigens are altered or novel protein products produced by cancer cells through mutations in DNA sequences, which can be recognized by the immune system as “non-self” [ ]. Therefore, stable and sustained expression of neoantigens at the protein level is important for the appropriate presentation of MHC molecules and the stimulation of a sustained immune response [ ]. This process involved multiple steps, ranging from gene mutations to neoantigen presentation. Several factors such as neoantigen depletion at the DNA level, silencing of mRNA expression, and loss of HLA heterozygosity, can affect neoantigen presentation [ ]. Therefore, it is crucial to evaluate neoantigen expression. Our findings revealed significant overexpression of the PCLO gene at the protein level in tumor tissues, along with its negative expression in lymphocytes, indicating that PCLO possesses the characteristics of TAA and TSA, and is a potential target for designing tumor vaccines.
However, mutated proteins have the potential to induce malignant behaviors in tumors. Piccolo, a protein encoded by PCLO, is a crucial component of the presynaptic cytoskeletal matrix that plays a pivotal role in establishing active synaptic zones and facilitating vesicle trafficking [ ]. Our enrichment analysis revealed that PCLO-related proteins are primarily involved in regulating the formation, transport, and exocytosis of vesicles. In summary, PCLO mutation-related genes were enriched in various cellular secretion processes, protein transport and metabolism, immune responses, and inflammatory responses. These findings suggest that mutations in PCLO do not promote tumor progression, but rather activate signaling pathways related to the immune response, making it a safe alternative antigen for tumor vaccines.
Not all peptide sequences generate immune responses, particularly specific cellular immunity. Although in silico methods are widely used to predict mutations in tumors, precisely identifying specific mutant genes that can stimulate an immune response remains a challenge [ ]. Numerous basic studies and clinical trials have focused on screening mutant peptides by combining in silico methods with laboratory examinations to detect the antitumor capacity of neoantigen-targeting vaccines, particularly against melanoma and lung cancer [ , ]. Extensive studies have demonstrated that neoantigens effectively mediate T cell activation and tumor regression without harming normal tissues in vivo [ ]. In this study, we focused on the most common HLA-A subtypes and predicted tens of neoantigen peptides derived from the PCLO mutation sites. Furthermore, immune recognition serves as the foundation for neoantigens to exert antitumor immune efficacy. Through in vivo experiments, we verified the immunogenicity of the PCLO mutant peptides and observed a marked increase in the activation of CD8 + T cells, indicating an enhanced T cell response mediated by the tumor neoantigen peptide. Thus, the peptides identified in this study possess the capacity to activate immune responses and have potential clinical applications.
The current study has some limitations. Here, we performed a comprehensive analysis to identify highly mutated genes in CC. However, the data were only based on the TCGA database, with a limited population of 284 samples. Therefore, additional samples are required to identify other highly mutated gene sites. Furthermore, multiple algorithms have been developed to predict neoantigen peptides, and the two most used methods were adopted in this study. The top predicted neoantigens of CC were used to examine immunogenicity in mice by detecting activated and memory markers of CD8 + T cells. Although experiments in mice are an important aspect of scientific research, experiments conducted using human samples would provide valuable insights into functional implications and enhance the credibility of our findings [ ]. We further tested their immunogenicity by ELISpot assay using human PBMCs. However, extending the functional assessment of cytotoxicity in vivo would help confirm the potential of these predicted neoantigens in the development of vaccines.

## Conclusions
Our strategy for screening tumor neoantigens based on mutant antigens in CC identified PCLO as a promising therapeutic biomarker. We successfully predicted tens of PCLO mutant-related peptides, with two candidates shown to induce specific CD8 + T cell response. This study provides a pipeline for predicting potential biomarkers for tumor vaccines pan-cancers. Collectively, our findings highlight the potential of PCLO as a valuable biomarker for the development of tumor vaccines for different cancer types.
