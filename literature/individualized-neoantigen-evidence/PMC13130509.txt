These clinical limitations necessitate a shift away from empirical monotherapy toward precision-guided combination strategies that actively reprogram immune resistance. In this review, we integrate contemporary clinical and translational evidence regarding ICI-based combinations with radiotherapy (RT), chemotherapy, and emerging non-cytotoxic sensitization approaches, with particular emphasis on neoadjuvant and perioperative treatment settings. Central to these strategies is the dynamic remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), rather than simple amplification of immune activation. Recent studies demonstrate that immune-sensitizing interventions – including RT-induced immunogenic cell death, innate immune pathway activation, metabolic and microbiome modulation, and bioengineered drug delivery systems—can convert immunologically inert tumors into immune-interrogable tissues by restoring antigen presentation, spatial immune organization, and effector T-cell competence. Concurrently, integrative biomarker frameworks—encompassing PD-L1 expression, tumor mutational burden, tertiary lymphoid structures, tissue-resident memory T cells, and immune spatial organization—are redefining approaches to patient stratification and therapeutic sequencing. Despite compelling mechanistic rationale, clinical translation remains hindered by inadequate biomarker integration, heterogeneous clinical trial design, and discordance between biological endpoints and survival outcomes. Collectively, these advances delineate a transition from empiric combination therapy toward immune-centric precision immuno-oncology in HNSCC, underscoring the necessity for biomarker-driven trial design, longitudinal immune monitoring, and multidisciplinary collaboration to translate mechanistic synergy into durable clinical benefit.

## Introduction
Head and neck cancers are ranked as the sixth most common type of malignancy worldwide, with around 890,000 new cases per year [ , ]. Nearly 90% of all head and neck cancers originate from the mucosal epithelium of the oral cavity, pharynx, and larynx and are collectively classified as head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC). The standard treatment for locally advanced but resectable tumors is the surgical resection of the primary tumor (plus involving lymph nodes), followed by risk-adapted adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) with or without platinum-based chemotherapy. However, in recent years, the standard of care for recurrent/metastatic (R/M-) HNSCC in all programmeddeath-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-defined subgroups has increasingly shifted from the historical cetuximab-fluorouracil-platinum regimen (EXTREME) to the combination of the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) inhibitor pembrolizumab plus fluorouracil and a platinum chemotherapy. The shift has occurred due to the overall survival advantage of the latter over EXTREME in the pivotal phase III KEYNOTE-048 trial [ , ].
Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are used for various cancers, and tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) characterization is crucial for optimizing therapy. T-cell infiltration and activation level in the tumor microenvironment (TME) greatly affect the therapeutic impact of ICIs. HNSCC is generally considered an immunosuppressive tumor that evades immune surveillance by disrupting innate immune signaling. In contrast, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in tumor tissues are found to contain a high proportion of regulatory T cells (Tregs). Therefore, these immune checkpoint blockade can reinvigorate T cell function in patients [ – ].
One of the major cancer therapeutic strategies currently in focus is treatment with ICIs and its combination therapies. In addition to ICI-chemotherapy combination therapies, a number of agents are currently under development to improve the therapeutic efficacy of ICI. Besides pharmacotherapy, RT is also a pivotal method of cancer treatment. While numerous studies have explored the efficacy of ICIs in combination with chemotherapy and as an adjuvant therapy following surgery, there is a paucity of data investigating the use of ICIs as neoadjuvant therapy or non-antitumor agents aimed at enhancing ICI sensitivity. Other obstacles to the practical application of new treatments include the prolonged timelines required for drug development, challenges in patient recruitment for clinical trials, the high costs associated with certain therapies, and the difficulty in providing uniform standards of care across different healthcare facilities.
In this Review, we present integrated evidence encompassing both established and emerging ICI-based combination strategies for HNSCC, with particular focus on neoadjuvant and RT-based strategies as well as emerging ICI-sensitizing modalities. It further highlights the translational gap between preclinical discovery and clinical application and discusses how biomarker-guided frameworks and optimized treatment sequencing may bridge this divide, paving the way toward precision immuno-oncology in HNSCC.

## Methods; literature selection criteria
The published documents were retrieved from electronic databases including PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and ScienceDirect. Studies published from 1996 to 2025 were included. While earlier reports were referenced mainly to provide contextual or supporting information, the principal evidence discussed herein is largely based on studies published in the 2020s. In addition, information regarding the progress, outcomes, and publication status of clinical trials was obtained from the ClinicalTrials.gov database (https://clinicaltrials.gov/).
The predefined keywords included ‘cancer immunotherapy’, ‘immune-checkpoint inhibitor’, ‘head and neck cancer’, ‘oral cancer’, ‘precision immunotherapy for cancer’, ‘combination immunotherapy for cancer’, ‘irAE’, ‘overlapping toxicities’, ‘immune-checkpoint inhibition and radiation’, ‘neoadjuvant immunotherapy’, ‘adjuvant immunotherapy’, ‘immunotherapy and radiation’, ‘abscopal effect and cancer immunotherapy’, ‘cancer immunotherapy synergy’, ‘drug delivery system for cancer immunotherapy’, ‘nano-drug delivery system for cancer immunotherapy’, ‘STING agonist for cancer immunotherapy’, ‘cancer vaccination and cancer immunotherapy’, ‘PROTAC for cancer immunotherapy’, and ‘gut microbiota and cancer immunotherapy’. These terms were searched individually or in appropriate combinations. Search strategies were predefined and applied consistently across databases, with database-specific adaptations made solely to accommodate platform-specific syntax and technical constraints without altering the conceptual scope of the search. Records retrieved from databases were assessed for relevance through iterative title and abstract screening. Duplicate records across databases were removed based on DOI and bibliographic information using a reference management tool; however, the precise number of duplicates was not tracked, as quantitative enumeration of intermediate screening steps was beyond the scope of this concept-driven review. Studies not relevant to tumor immunology, immune checkpoint regulation, or drug delivery systems (DDSs) were excluded, whereas literature providing mechanistic, translational, or clinically relevant insights was prioritized for inclusion. This review was designed as a concept-driven synthesis, integrating mechanistic, translational, and clinical perspectives in cancer immunotherapy, rather than as a systematic review aimed at exhaustive quantitative accounting; therefore, formal numerical tracking of records at each intermediate screening stage was not undertaken. The overall literature selection process was guided by the principles of the PRISMA 2020 framework [ ] to enhance transparency and methodological clarity, as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1.

## Overview: Evidence from clinical trials for HNSCC ICI therapies
Based on the results from KEYNOTE-012 and CheckMate-141, pembrolizumab and nivolumab were first approved by the FDA and EMA as monotherapies for R/M-HNSCC that had progressed on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. Subsequent phase III trials, KEYNOTE-040 and KEYNOTE-048, further established pembrolizumab as a standard first-line therapy for PD-L1–positive disease [ – ]. Thereafter, several ICI agents have been investigated for R/M-HNSCC cases. In the phase Ib expansion of the JAVELIN Solid Tumor trial, avelumab (anti-PD-L1 inhibitor) monotherapy in patients with platinum-refractory or ineligible R/M-HNSCC also achieved an ORR of 9.2% and a median OS of 8.0 months, with grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) in 6.5% and no treatment-related deaths, indicating modest efficacy and manageable safety [ ].
Although evidence regarding the benefits of combination therapies for ICI treatment in R/M-HNSCC is limited, several recent studies have reported promising findings. However, double-agent ICI was not found to improve survival in the Checkmate-651 (Nivolumab + Ipilimumab vs. EXTREME) and KESTREL (Durvalumab + Tremelimumab vs. Durvalumab vs. EXTREME) trials, respectively [ , ]. In CheckMate-651, it did not meet its primary OS endpoint (13.9 vs 13.5 months, HR = 0.96 (0.80–1.15)) but showed a favorable safety profile and a trend toward improved survival in PD-L1–high patients (17.6 vs 14.6 months, HR = 0.78 (0.59–1.03)) [ ]. In addition, the KESTREL trial also did not meet its primary endpoint of improved OS with durvalumab (alone or with tremelimumab) versus EXTREME regimen in first-line R/M-HNSCC (10.9 vs 11.2 vs 10.9 months, HR = 0.96 (0.69–1.32)) but showed indications of durable responses and a favorable safety/TRAEs profile [ ]. The biological rationale for combining ICIs in these trials is that nivolumab releases inhibitory signaling from effector T cells, whereas ipilimumab promotes their activation. Each agent demonstrated distinct antitumor activity, leading to the design of CheckMate-651 to assess their potential synergy. However, despite the strong mechanistic rationale, the trial did not demonstrate a significant survival benefit, suggesting that the balance between T-cell activation and immune regulation within TME may still limit clinical efficacy [ ].
In contrast to the established use of ICIs in R/M settings, ongoing trials are exploring their integration into recent curative treatment frameworks for locally advanced (LA-) HNSCC. Among these, the ADRISK trial (NCT04847466) is a randomized, adaptive phase II study evaluating whether the addition of pembrolizumab to cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy (CRT) can improve DFS and OS compared with CRT alone. The study also assesses safety and toxicity profiles of the combination regimen [ ]. Another ongoing study, the phase III NIVOPOSTOP trial (GORTEC 2018–01, NCT03576417), evaluated the addition of nivolumab to adjuvant cisplatin-based CRT in patients with resected, LA-HNSCC. Data presented at ASCO 2025 (LBA2) demonstrated that this combination significantly improved DFS without compromising treatment compliance or surgical feasibility [ ]. These findings indicate the potential benefit of integrating PD-1 blockade into postoperative CRT for high-risk patients with LA-HNSCC.
Furthermore, clinical investigations have extended to the neoadjuvant and adjuvant use of ICIs. The phase Ib/II IMCISION trial (NCT03003637) evaluated the feasibility and immunologic effects of neoadjuvant ICI in resectable HNSCC. Patients were treated with nivolumab alone or in combination with ipilimumab before curative-intent surgery. The treatment was well tolerated, with no delays in surgery or increases in perioperative complications. Notably, major or partial pathologic responses (PR) were observed in approximately 35–50% of patients, particularly in the dual-ICI cohort, and were associated with marked increases in CD8⁺ effector T-cell infiltration, T-cell receptor (TCR) clonality, and the expansion of tumor-reactive clones in both tumor tissue and peripheral blood. These findings demonstrate that short-course preoperative ICI therapy can effectively reprogram the TIME toward an inflamed phenotype without compromising surgical feasibility, thereby providing a compelling biological rationale for subsequent large-scale phase III trials such as KEYNOTE-689 [ ].
The KEYNOTE-689 trial is a recent phase III, open-label, randomized, active-controlled study in patients with resectable, previously untreated LA-HNSCC, which demonstrated that the addition of neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab to standard therapy significantly improved event-free survival (EFS). Importantly, neoadjuvant administration did not interfere with the feasibility of planned surgical resection, and the major pathological response (mPR), a key secondary endpoint, was improved by 9.3% in the pembrolizumab arm compared with the control arm. Moreover, the safety profile was generally consistent with previous experience with PD-1 inhibitors, and no new safety signals were observed. Regarding OS, the estimated 3-year OS favored the pembrolizumab arm, showing a trend toward improved survival: CPS ≥ 10 cohort, 68.2% vs. 59.2% (HR = 0.72); total population, 68.4% vs. 61.1% (HR = 0.76; 95% CI, 0.59–0.98). Although OS was not yet reached as the primary endpoint at the time of analysis, the results suggest a survival benefit in favor of pembrolizumab [ ]. As mPR was a secondary endpoint, further detailed immunologic and genomic investigations are warranted to elucidate its biological correlates and to refine patient selection for this therapeutic approach.
Taken together, survival outcomes across major HNSCC trials demonstrate that PD-1–based therapy does not confer uniform superiority over existing standards; however, several studies have shown clear survival benefit, while others achieved comparable efficacy with substantially reduced high-grade toxicity (detailed later). These findings suggest that the clinical value of ICIs in HNSCC lies not only in survival extension but also in maintaining acceptable tolerability, underscoring the need for refined patient selection to fully realize their therapeutic potential.

## Immune-related adverse events (irAEs), systemic toxicities, overlapping toxicities, long-term sequelae of ICIs, and their management
ICI therapy is characterized by robust antitumor activity and a more favorable severe toxicity profile compared with conventional cytotoxic regimens [ ]. Beyond the incidence and spectrum of irAEs, the potential for surgical delay is a critical consideration in neoadjuvant applications [ ]. The irAEs are commonly managed according to established clinical practice guidelines, such as those proposed by ASCO, which largely rely on CTCAE-based severity grading [ ]. In an exploratory analysis of CheckMate-141, nivolumab maintained symptom and functional stability, whereas investigator’s choice therapy produced clinically relevant deterioration, resulting in delayed quality-of-life decline in platinum-refractory R/M-HNSCC [ ].
Across clinical trials, PD-1 monotherapy (KEYNOTE-040, CheckMate-141) consistently demonstrated fewer grade ≥ 3 AEs than chemotherapy-based comparators. Dual ICI therapy (PD-1 + CTLA-4) increases immune-mediated toxicities—such as hepatitis, colitis, and endocrinopathy—yet still produces less cytotoxic-type toxicity than the EXTREME regimen [ , ]. Neoadjuvant PD-1 blockade (CheckMate-358) was well tolerated and met primary safety endpoints without surgical delays, with manageable grade 3–4 irAEs in both HPV + (19.2%) and HPV– (11.5%) cohorts [ ]. Similarly, neoadjuvant/adjuvant pembrolizumab in KEYNOTE-689 maintained surgical feasibility with a predictable toxicity profile [ ]. Preliminary data from the ongoing ADRISK trial suggest that adding pembrolizumab to cisplatin-based CRT is feasible and consistent with historical tolerability [ , ] (Table  ). 
Table provides a consolidated overview of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs across major HNSCC trials as of November 2025. Overall, severe toxicity rates are comparable between single-agent and dual-agent ICI regimens and are substantially lower than those observed with cytotoxic chemotherapy. CheckMate-651 demonstrated that nivolumab plus ipilimumab yields modest rates of severe AEs, largely restricted to immune-mediated events, whereas EXTREME exhibits markedly higher hematologic toxicity. In the neoadjuvant setting, exemplified by CheckMate-358, PD-1 blockade continues to show excellent tolerability. Importantly, although immune-mediated grade ≥ 3 events may increase modestly in neoadjuvant regimens, they have not been associated with surgical delays. These patterns highlight the mechanistic divergence between immune-based and cytotoxic modalities and underscore the complexity of attributing overlapping toxicities in multimodal treatment strategies. 
Chemotherapy and RT activate innate immune pathways—including DAMPs, STING–type I IFN, and NF-κB signaling—that synergize with ICIs to promote immunogenic cell death [ – ]. Such convergence can enhance antitumor efficacy but may also amplify inflammation within shared organ systems, particularly mucosal, cutaneous, and gastrointestinal tissues, which represent common toxicity sites in combined-modality therapy [ – ].
Diagnostically, distinguishing irAEs from TRAEs remains challenging, as mucositis, dermatitis, or hepatitis may present with overlapping clinical and histologic features. Current best practice relies on multimodal assessment integrating temporal patterns, anatomic distribution, and biopsy findings, although no standardized diagnostic framework exists for combined-modality regimens in HNSCC [ – ]. Harmonized criteria and biomarker-guided attribution algorithms are therefore needed to improve diagnostic accuracy. Future efforts should integrate clinical phenotype, imaging, pathology, and molecular signatures to define reproducible toxicity clusters and guide early differentiation of overlapping adverse events.
Management of grade ≥ 2 irAEs continues to follow guideline-based algorithms, with corticosteroids (0.5–1 mg/kg/day prednisone equivalent) as first-line therapy and infliximab or mycophenolate reserved for steroid-refractory cases [ , , ]. Early multidisciplinary coordination is essential to avoid overtreatment and preserve tumor-control efficacy.
The most frequent long-term sequelae of PD-1 blockade are endocrine disorders—often permanent—whereas colitis, hepatitis, or dermatitis typically resolve; however, chronic pulmonary or neuromuscular complications have been reported. Late-onset irAEs (> 12 months) occur in 5–10% of long-term survivors and require ongoing surveillance [ , ].
Collectively, these findings reflect a transition in ICI safety management from acute toxicity control to long-term survivorship care. A future framework integrating early detection, biomarker-guided prediction, and multidisciplinary management will be critical to preventing cumulative and delayed toxicities while sustaining durable antitumor immunity.

## ICI therapy in the neoadjuvant setting for HNSCC and the challenges
Therapeutic intervention delivered ahead of definitive treatment is increasingly recognized for its capacity to reduce primary tumor burden and mitigate the risk of distant disease [ ]. Recently, multiple studies have investigated the usefulness of ICIs as neoadjuvant therapy for patients with HNSCC [ – ]. Neoadjuvant ICIs preserve tumor neoantigens, activating T cells and priming the adaptive antitumor immune response [ – ]. Moreover, ICIs are generally associated with improved tolerability compared with conventional cytotoxic therapy, helping preserve perioperative physical strength [ ]. The first phase II clinical trial evaluating neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab in resectable LA, human papillomavirus (HPV)-unrelated HNSCC suggested that pembrolizumab was well tolerated, with evidence of pathologic response observed in 44% of patients and no cases achieving a pathologic complete response (pCR). One-year relapse outcomes in patients with high-risk pathology compared favorably with historical reference data [ ]. A phase II clinical trial evaluating neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab in resectable LA-HNSCC demonstrated an improved 1-year DFS rate in intermediate-risk, but not high-risk, patients when compared with historical controls, leading to the conclusion that PR to neoadjuvant ICI may serve as a promising surrogate marker for favorable DFS [ ]. A systematic review also found neoadjuvant ICI for HNSCC had favorable response rates with no unexpected toxicities [ ].
The optimal timing of surgical resection following neoadjuvant ICI therapy remains an open question, as it may influence the magnitude and durability of antitumor immune responses. In the clinical trial NCT03238365, surgery was performed 31 days after nivolumab administration [ ]. Similarly, in the clinical trial NCT03618654, approximately four weeks elapsed between durvalumab administration and surgical resection [ ]. Although neoadjuvant ICIs preserve tumor neoantigens and enhance systemic immune priming, emerging evidence suggests that the timing between neoadjuvant ICI administration and surgical resection may critically affect therapeutic efficacy. Preclinical models have demonstrated that either excessively short or prolonged intervals between ICI initiation and surgery can diminish antitumor effects. Furthermore, the addition of adjuvant ICI doses after standard neoadjuvant treatment did not significantly improve tumor-free survival, regardless of the combination used (anti–PD-1 + anti–CD137 or anti–CTLA-4 + anti–PD-1) [ ]. While these findings provide mechanistic insights, they are derived from murine models, and their translational relevance remains to be validated in prospective clinical settings.
These considerations regarding surgical timing naturally led to the question of how therapeutic response to neoadjuvant ICI should be evaluated. Conventional pathologic response criteria predominantly focus on morphologic changes, such as tumor cell shrinkage and necrosis [ ]. However, in the context of ICI, changes in the TIME, including immune cell distribution and fibrosis, are crucial indicators. Because the mechanisms of ICI differ fundamentally from those of traditional cytotoxic chemotherapy and RT, it is essential to establish ICI-specific pathologic response criteria. Consequently, reliance on conventional response criteria may fail to fully capture the extent of pathologic response, which is currently the most reliable predictor of recurrence-free survival (RFS) [ , ]. Proposed criteria for evaluating ICI-related pathologic responses include immune-related features, such as immune cell infiltration, along with wound-healing characteristics (e.g., immature proliferative fibrosis and neovascularization) [ ]. To enhance the precision of pathological evaluation and response prediction in patients treated with neoadjuvant ICI, the development of robust biomarkers remains an urgent priority [ ]. Table outlines several additional methods for monitoring responses to neoadjuvant ICI, utilizing both resected and non-resected samples [ , , – ]. However, many of these approaches are still under development and cannot yet be fully integrated into routine clinical practice. 
Beyond pCR, mPR—defined as ≤ 10% residual viable tumor cells after neoadjuvant therapy—has emerged as a robust histopathologic surrogate reflecting substantial tumor regression and immune activation [ , ]. In recent HNSCC studies, mPR has been adopted to capture clinically meaningful treatment effects and correlate them with immune remodeling within TME [ ]. A recent multicenter phase II study evaluated neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy with carboplatin, nab-paclitaxel, and durvalumab in patients with resectable LA-HNSCC. Post-treatment imaging revealed an ORR of 57%. The pCR and mPR were achieved in 29% and 49% of patients who underwent surgery, respectively, with a 1-year progression-free survival (PFS) rate of 83.8%. The most common TRAEs included anemia, cytopenias (platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte decrease), fatigue, nausea, and alopecia, most of which were grade 1–2 in severity. The investigators concluded that neoadjuvant therapy with carboplatin, nab-paclitaxel, and durvalumab was safe and feasible, as evidenced by the favorable rates of pCR and clinical-to-pathologic downstaging [ ]. A comprehensive analysis of neoadjuvant ICI therapy in patients with LA-HNSCC highlighted higher mPR rates when ICIs were combined with RT or chemotherapy, compared with ICI monotherapy. Across both monotherapy and combination regimens, safety profiles were manageable, with no treatment–related surgical delays or therapy-associated deaths reported [ ].
Accumulating evidence indicates an association between irAE severity and favorable ICI outcomes, implicating heightened immune priming within the tumor immune microenvironment and distinct pre-treatment cytokine profiles [ , ]. In this context, patients who develop irAEs may represent a biologically informative subset in whom measurable pretreatment immune features could predict both therapeutic benefit and the risk of excessive immune activation. Such biomarkers could inform clinical trial design by enabling the rational integration of immunomodulatory strategies aimed at mitigating severe toxicity without compromising antitumor immunity [ ].
One of the main challenges in this therapeutic setting is tumor growth—either true progression or pseudoprogression—occurring during or after ICI treatment, which may sometimes result in surgical difficulties and overall treatment failure. However, there are currently no consensual definitions of these phenomena, and their impact on daily clinical practice remains uncertain. When tumor enlargement is observed during or after ICI therapy, distinguishing between true progression and pseudo-progression is essential first [ ]. As summarized in a current synthesis by Frelaut et al., the incidence of pseudoprogression ranges from 0 to 15%, typically characterized by transient tumor enlargement followed by disease stabilization, while hyperprogression—reported in 4% to 29% of cases depending on the definition used—is associated with poorer survival outcomes. However, these data are not specific to HNSCC [ ]. Distinguishing true progression from pseudo-progression and hyper-progression remains challenging, as this differentiation can often only be made retrospectively. Further clinical and preclinical studies are warranted to clarify the underlying mechanisms and identify predictive biomarkers that can help prospectively distinguish between these entities.

## Changes in the TIME by neoadjuvant ICI

## TIME modulation by neoadjuvant ICI
 Overall, neoadjuvant ICI treatment can modulate systemic cytokine profiles, which affect the survival of patients with HNSCC undergoing radical surgery. It can also be used to predict the onset of irAEs [ ]. Three major biological changes occur in the TIME of HNSCC after neoadjuvant ICI treatment that can be used to prevent recurrence after tumor resection. 1) Increase in immune cell infiltration: ICI therapy is associated with increased CD8 + T cell infiltration within the tumor and strengthening of antitumor immunity in HNSCC [ ]. 2) PD-L1 expression changes: post-ICI treatment, there are alterations in PD-L1 expression in tumors [ ] and immune cells [ ], which may correlate with treatment efficacy [ ]. 3) Reduction in immunosuppressive environment: anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies reduce the suppressive function of Tregs and alleviate immunosuppression in the tumor [ ]. In addition to these immunologic alterations, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) play a pivotal role in shaping the TIME after ICI therapy. Their phenotypic reprogramming from an immunosuppressive (M2) to a pro-inflammatory (M1) state may influence treatment efficacy and postoperative tumor recurrence. Indeed, several studies have demonstrated that TAMs can undergo phenotypic reprogramming from the M2 to M1 state during ICI therapy [ ]. ScRNA-seq combined with dynamic transcriptomic analyses during ICI treatment has provided evidence that enhanced crosstalk between macrophages and T cells is a key contributor to therapeutic responsiveness. Building on this mechanistic understanding of IFN-γ– and PD-1–associated signaling networks underlying ICI sensitivity [ ], the broader context of immune checkpoint regulation becomes critical, as immune cells express inhibitory markers such as CTLA-4, PD-1, PD-L1, and PD-L2 that play central roles in tumor development and progression [ ]. Preclinical studies have shown that neoadjuvant PD-1 blockade can reverse functional immunodominance and induce a polyclonal T-cell response, thereby enhancing antitumor immunity against both dominant and subdominant tumor antigens. In these models, neoadjuvant PD-1 inhibition also facilitated the establishment of durable immunologic memory, conferring protection against subsequent tumor rechallenge. Collectively, these findings lend support to the concept that neoadjuvant or adjuvant ICI therapy may amplify the therapeutic benefit of surgical resection with curative intent, particularly in advanced tumors with high risk of locoregional or distant failure [ ] (Fig.  ). 

## Emerging Biomarkers for Predicting Responses to Neoadjuvant ICI
Identification of predictive biomarkers following neoadjuvant ICI therapy remains challenging for achieving durable therapeutic benefit, encompassing tumor regression and sustained antitumor immunity beyond surgical resection. An et al. investigated TIME alterations following neoadjuvant ICI therapy using multiplex immunohistochemistry. In pretreatment samples, patients who achieved a pathologic response displayed significantly higher densities of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells, Tregs, and PD-1⁺/PD-L1⁺ cells than non-responders. After treatment, the responder group showed an overall reduction in these immune markers, particularly with decreased frequencies of TIM-3⁺ and LAG-3⁺ T cells, resulting in lower ratios of exhausted T-cell populations compared with non-responders. Notably, CD8⁺ T cells were more abundant at the tumor margin in responders, suggesting enhanced cytotoxic activity and spatially focused antitumor immunity [ , ]. Alterations in these immune cell proportions may serve as predictive markers for the efficacy of neoadjuvant ICI in HNSCC and aid in the optimization of therapeutic strategies. In addition, Sadeghirad et al. performed transcriptome profiling of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) and germinal centers, revealing a marked upregulation of genes associated with immune modulation, immune-cell recruitment, and IFN signaling with TLSs following neoadjuvant ICI treatment. Importantly, the spatial distribution of TLSs relative to tumor nests differed significantly between response groups. In particular, TLSs located in close proximity to tumor cells correlated with improved ICI response, whereas distant TLSs were associated with poor outcomes [ ]. Likewise, Luoma et al. analyzed dynamic immune responses in patients with HNSCC during the short interval between neoadjuvant ICI initiation and surgery. They observed a marked accumulation of tissue-resident memory T cells (TRMs) within tumor tissue, accompanied by an increase in circulating T cells in the peripheral blood. These distinct spatial and temporal patterns of T-cell kinetics were associated with improved immune activation and may serve as promising biomarkers to predict therapeutic efficacy and patient prognosis in neoadjuvant ICI settings [ ]. Furthermore, Redman et al. reported promising findings: neoadjuvant therapy with bintrafusp alfa, which simultaneously inhibits PD-L1 and TGF-β, led to an increase in proliferating CD8+ T cells within the tumor and a spatial redistribution of Tregs from the tumor center to the periphery. This shift was interpreted as enabling spatial segregation of CD8+ T cells from immunosuppressive zones, creating a micro-niche that facilitates their antitumor activity. These findings suggest that the “location” of Tregs may be more critical than their “status,” and that dual PD-L1/TGF-β inhibition by bintrafusp alfa may enhance the TIME by modulating Treg localization [ ]. Notably, Oliveira et al. conducted a phase II trial (NCT02296684) evaluating two doses of neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 therapy in patients with resectable HNSCC. Remarkably, 45% of patients achieved marked pathologic tumor regression (≥ 50%). In responding tumors, paired pre- and post-treatment analyses revealed clonally expanded, tumor-specific exhausted CD8+ TILs that exhibited a TRM transcriptional program characterized by ZNF683 expression and high cytotoxic potential within the baseline TME. This study provided two major insights: 1) ZNF683+ TRMs represent a potential biomarker for predicting anti-PD-1 treatment efficacy; and 2) therapeutic benefit depends primarily on the activation of pre-existing TRM populations rather than the recruitment of new T cells, underscoring that the “quality” of T cell immunity may be more critical than its “quantity” [ ]. Finally, van der Leun et al. provided critical mechanistic insights into dual ICI treatment (anti–PD-1 and anti–CTLA-4) in patients with HNSCC. In pre-treatment tumor tissues, patients with a high proportion of activated Tregs (4-1BB/OX40-positive) exhibited a more favorable response to dual ICI therapy. Among responders, the proportion of activated Tregs markedly decreased after treatment, accompanied by reduced expression of activation- and dysfunction-related genes in intertumoral dysfunctional CD8+ T cells. These findings reflected a parallel transcriptional reprogramming in both Tregs and CD8+ T cells, suggesting that dual ICI induces analogous immune remodeling across these key subsets. In contrast, non-responders exhibited early cytotoxic activation in natural killer (NK) cells, indicating a compensatory yet insufficient immune response to dual ICI. Collectively, these results highlight that the baseline abundance of activated Tregs may function as a predictor of dual ICI efficacy, and that coordinated monitoring of Treg and CD8⁺ T-cell dynamics could optimize therapeutic benefit while mitigating immune-related toxicities [ ].
Biomarkers predicting responses to neoadjuvant ICI are not limited to immune cells. Obradovic et al. performed high-dimensional scRNA-seq on advanced-stage HNSCC tumors collected before and after neoadjuvant nivolumab treatment [ ]. They identified distinct cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) subpopulations that underwent marked compositional shifts following therapy. One CAF subtype was enriched in responders and exhibited an immunostimulatory phenotype—reducing TGF-β–dependent PD-1⁺ TIM-3⁺ exhausted CD8⁺ T cells, promoting CD103⁺ NKG2A⁺ TRM differentiation, and enhancing overall T-cell cytolytic activity. Conversely, another CAF subtype was associated with an immunosuppressive milieu and poor ICI response. These findings underscore that stromal remodeling, in concert with immune modulation, plays a pivotal role in shaping neoadjuvant ICI sensitivity [ ].
Collectively, these studies provide important insights into PR patterns, predictive biomarkers, immune cell phenotypes, spatial organization, and mechanistic correlates of neoadjuvant ICI response. To maximize the therapeutic benefit of neoadjuvant ICI, biomarker evaluation must move beyond static, baseline-based approaches and adopt stage-specific, longitudinal assessments of immune and stromal dynamics. Rather than asking which biomarkers predict response at a single time point, future studies should focus on how neoadjuvant ICI reshapes the tumor–immune ecosystem over time, and which immune state transitions ultimately determine durable therapeutic benefit Fig. . 

## ICIs as radiosensitizers and RT as an ICI sensitizer

## Combination of RT and ICIs
Although concurrent RT with high-dose cisplatin (100 mg/m2, days 1, 22, and 42) is the standard of care for unresectable HNSCC in curative settings, it correlates with marked toxicity [ ]. In contrast, severe toxicities associated with ICI therapy are generally less frequent and milder than those induced by the standard platinum-based chemotherapies [ , ]. Because most patients with HNSCC are elderly, systemic toxicities may increase perioperative morbidity and should be minimized whenever possible [ ].
The treatment of a primary lesion with ionizing radiation that results in tumor control at a distant untreated metastatic site is known as the abscopal effect and is thought to be mediated by activation of the adaptive immune system [ , ]. RT exposure itself may promote lymphocyte infiltration into the tumor through two main mechanisms: (1) normalizing tumor vasculature [ ] and upregulating endothelial adhesion molecule expression [ ] to facilitate immune cell transmigration across the endothelium, and (2) release of chemokines to trigger immune cell migration and invasion [ ]. Moreover, RT may transiently convert the hypoxic TME into a more oxygenated state through vascular remodeling and increased perfusion [ ], thereby reshaping immune cell composition and function within the TIME. However, the impact of RT on tumor oxygenation remains controversial. Preclinical studies using human tumor xenografts and murine models have demonstrated that RT induces dose-dependent vascular damage, resulting in reduced blood perfusion and secondary hypoxia at higher doses [ , ]. In addition, RT has been shown to induce transient alterations in tumor perfusion, marked by an initial increase followed by a subsequent decline [ ]. Thus, the precise mechanisms of RT-induced TME and TIME are extremely complex and yet to be fully elucidated. Nevertheless, an increasing body of preclinical and clinical evidence supports the therapeutic synergy of combining RT with ICI therapy in HNSCC and other solid tumors [ , ]. These findings suggest that RT-induced alterations in tumor perfusion exhibit a biphasic pattern, with transient normalization followed by vascular collapse at higher doses. This dynamic remodeling of tumor vasculature critically influences oxygenation, immune cell trafficking, and the efficacy of subsequent therapies. Therefore, integrating RT dose and timing with ICI scheduling may optimize perfusion-mediated immune activation while minimizing hypoxia-driven immunosuppression [ ].
Building on this rationale, early-phase clinical trials have explored neoadjuvant immuno-radiotherapy as a strategy to exploit RT-induced immune activation and improve surgical outcomes. In a phase Ib clinical trial of neoadjuvant immuno-radiotherapy, 21 patients with LA-HNSCC underwent combined treatment, which was well tolerated and did not delay subsequent surgery. Histopathologic evaluation of post-treatment specimens revealed prominent immune-mediated regression features, including dense inflammatory infiltrates within the regression bed, enriched plasma cells, and cholesterol clefts, consistent with active antitumor immune remodeling. In this cohort, mPR was observed in 86% of patients, including a 67% rate of pCR, and clinical-to-pathologic downstaging occurred in 90% of cases [ ]. Together, these findings highlight the capacity of RT to function as a potent immunologic amplifier within ICI-based regimens, driving immune-mediated tumor remodeling and deep PRs that extend beyond local tumor control and may ultimately translate into durable clinical benefit in both LA- and R/M-HNSCC [ , ].
Beyond local control, the combination of RT and ICIs has also been associated with systemic immune activation, typified by the abscopal effect, in which irradiation of one tumor site induces regression of distant, non-irradiated lesions, particularly when RT is administered in an immunologically permissive context created by ICIs [ ]. This phenomenon provides mechanistic evidence that localized RT can, under certain immunologic conditions, translate into systemic tumor immunity.

## Abscopal effect
Indeed, accumulating studies have provided mechanistic insights into how RT enhances the efficacy of ICI therapy in solid tumors: (1) After RT, tumor cell death promotes the release and presentation of tumor antigens, thereby promoting T-cell activation and expansion [ – ]; (2) RT enhances tumor antigen and neoantigen production with low mutation burden, enhancing their sensitivity to ICIs [ – ]; (3) RT induces the abscopal effect and enhances PD-L1 expression, increasing ICI sensitivity [ , , , ]. Together, these preclinical insights indicate that RT can extend immune activation beyond the irradiated field through systemic propagation of antitumor responses. Consistent with this concept, emerging clinical evidence has documented abscopal-like immune phenomena in patients treated with concurrent or sequential RT and ICIs [ ].
Despite a strong preclinical rationale, randomized prospective trials have not consistently demonstrated a reproducible abscopal effect in HNSCC. In a phase II randomized study (NCT02684253), the addition of stereotactic body RT (SBRT) to nivolumab failed to improve ORR or PFS, and no abscopal responses were documented [ ]. Similarly, in the multicenter PEMBRO-RT phase II trial of metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer, the combination of SBRT with pembrolizumab yielded a higher ORR (36% vs 18% with pembrolizumab alone) but did not translate into consistent improvements in PFS or OS, underscoring that the abscopal effect remains rare and biologically context-dependent [ ]. Currently, a phase II trial (NCT04454489) is assessing QUAD SHOT hypofractionated RT with pembrolizumab in R/M-HNSCC, representing one of the limited prospective efforts to integrate short-course RT with ICIs [ ].
Collectively, current evidence suggests that while RT can potentiate antitumor immunity, the translation of this mechanism into consistent abscopal responses in HNSCC remains to be clinically validated. This discrepancy highlights a critical gap between mechanistic promise and durable clinical benefit, underscoring the need for biomarker-driven and context-aware trial designs [ , ].
Beyond mechanistic insights and early clinical observations, correlative imaging evidence has emerged that further illustrates the systemic immune activation characteristic of the abscopal phenomenon. Clinical evidence suggests the potential for an abscopal effect when RT is combined with ICIs in HNSCC [ ]. This concept is further supported by correlative positron emission tomography–computed tomography (PET-CT) findings from clinical trials of neoadjuvant ICIs. These studies highlight the importance of draining lymph nodes, as a high incidence of increased fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) uptake in the draining cervical lymph nodes was observed on interval PET-CT following the initiation of neoadjuvant ICIs. Importantly, surgical pathology of these lymph nodes revealed only reactive changes, without evidence of malignancy. Accordingly, the increase in FDG uptake observed on imaging may reflect an ongoing immune activation [ ]. This phenomenon may represent an immunologic analogue of the abscopal effect, reflecting systemic immune activation beyond the primary tumor. It suggests that neoadjuvant ICIs can induce secondary immune priming in draining and regional lymph nodes—a process conceptually similar to radiation-induced antigen release and cross-presentation that underlies the abscopal paradigm. Such findings further support the notion that the abscopal effect may not be confined to RT but can also arise from robust systemic immunomodulation initiated locally within the TME.

## Biological insights into RT and ICI synergy
In the preclinical studies, Deng et al. demonstrated that combining RT with PD-L1 blockade potentiates antitumor immunity by enhancing T cell-derived TNFα production, leading to the elimination of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). However, RT also induces local upregulation of the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, which can suppress radiation-induced immune responses and limit durable therapeutic efficacy [ ]. Other studies have demonstrated that RT upregulates immune checkpoint molecules while concurrently promoting the priming and activation of cytotoxic T cells, providing a mechanistic basis for combinatorial strategies involving RT and ICIs [ ]. RT and the subsequent release of various stimuli, including DAMPs and TAAs, result in a potent type I IFN response. Since type I IFNs further mediate immunostimulatory processes, including the activation of dendritic cells (DCs) and the priming of CD8⁺ T cells, a detailed analysis is warranted to elucidate how ICIs interact with these pathways to shape antitumor immunity [ , ].
The cyclic GMP–AMP synthase (cGAS)–stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling axis constitutes a central component of innate immune surveillance by detecting double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) derived from mislocalized nuclear or mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and orchestrating type I IFN responses [ ]. RT can activate this pathway through the accumulation of cytosolic dsDNA in irradiated tumor cells, leading to type I IFN production, DC maturation, and subsequent CD8⁺ T-cell priming [ , ]. In HNSCC, STING activation also induces chemokines such as CXCL10 and CCL5, facilitating the recruitment of effector immune cells—including CD8⁺ T cells, NK cells, and DCs—into the TME [ , ].
The immunologic outcome of RT-induced cGAS–STING signaling may be shaped by RT dose and fractionation. DNA-repair inhibition or fractionated RT schedules can amplify cytosolic DNA accumulation and STING activation, whereas ablative single doses may upregulate TREX1, degrading cytosolic DNA and suppressing type I IFN responses [ , ]. Moreover, tumor- and immune-compartment heterogeneity in STING expression, as well as HPV status, can influence the extent of immune activation [ , ]. cGAS–STING signaling can also modulate immunosuppressive MDSCs: RT-induced STING activation may attract MDSCs via CCL2 yet simultaneously attenuate their suppressive phenotype, allowing enhanced T-cell activity [ ].
Collectively, these findings suggest that RT enhances antitumor immunity in HNSCC through cGAS–STING–driven type I IFN signaling and T-cell priming, particularly when combined with STING agonists or immune checkpoint blockade [ ]. Nevertheless, chronic or excessive STING activation may conversely promote immunosuppressive cytokine release and MDSC recruitment, underscoring the need for careful preclinical and clinical evaluation [ ].

## Challenges in RT and ICI combination trials
Only a few clinical trials have demonstrated the therapeutic efficacy of combining RT with ICIs in HNSCC. The JAVELIN Head and Neck 100, a phase III trial of avelumab (anti-PD-L1 inhibitor) and CRT for patients with LA-HNSCC, was terminated because interim analysis conducted according to the prespecified plan indicated a low probability of achieving statistical significance for the primary PFS endpoint (NCT02952586) [ , ]. Subsequently, the KEYNOTE-412, a phase III trial of pembrolizumab plus concurrent CRT versus placebo plus concurrent CRT in patients with LA-HNSCC, failed to improve EFS compared with CRT alone in molecularly unselected patients (NCT03040999) [ – ]. Another ongoing clinical trial (NCT03576417) is currently evaluating the efficacy of nivolumab in combination with RT plus cisplatin and may redefine the standard of care (Table  ). Collectively, these results indicate that, despite strong preclinical rationale, the clinical translation of RT–ICI synergy in HNSCC has remained challenging, with most phase III trials failing to demonstrate statistically significant survival benefit over standard CRT. Further optimization of trial design, biomarker-driven patient selection, and RT fractionation or sequencing may be required to fully harness the immunomodulatory potential of RT in combination with ICIs. 
Several factors may account for the discrepancy between promising preclinical findings and the disappointing outcomes of clinical trials [ ]. For example, a multi-omic reanalysis of the phase III JAVELIN Head and Neck 100 trial showed that intratumoral bacterial abundance and heightened neutrophil/myeloid programs were key features of resistance to avelumab plus CRT, with increased neutrophil/myeloid activity associated with worse outcomes in the avelumab arm [ ]. Similar associations between higher intratumoral bacterial abundance and anti-PD-L1 resistance have also been reported in independent HNSCC cohorts [ ].

## Perspectives for RT + ICI combination therapy
The clinical success of RT–ICI combinations will depend not only on mechanistic synergy, but also on precise patient selection, biomarker-driven trial design, and immunologically informed RT planning [ ]. Bridging these elements is essential to translate the robust preclinical immunomodulatory effects of RT into durable therapeutic synergy in HNSCC.
A central challenge moving forward is to define the optimal temporal integration of RT within combination regimens. Although most current protocols deliver RT and ICIs concurrently, RT induces dynamic and time-dependent alterations in both the TME and TIME, raising important questions regarding optimal sequencing. Whether administering RT prior to ICI to sensitize antitumor immunity, or following ICI to enhance radiosensitivity, confers superior clinical benefit remains an open question. Systematic evaluation of sequencing strategies may therefore unlock more effective and durable clinical benefit [ – ]. In parallel, the identification of predictive biomarkers will be critical to guide treatment sequencing and patient stratification. Biomarker-guided RT scheduling–integrating immune context, lymphoid involvement, and host immune competence–may represent a cornerstone for truly personalized RT–ICI combination therapy (Fig. ). 
Ultimately, realizing the full therapeutic potential of RT–ICI combinations in HNSCC will require an integrated framework that bridges radiobiology, tumor immunology, and clinical translation. Well-designed, biomarker-stratified trials that explicitly incorporate immunological timing and context will be essential to establish RT–ICI therapy as a rational and durable treatment paradigm.

## Emerging multimodal approaches to augment ICI efficacy
Beyond RT and chemotherapy, accumulating data indicate that ICI efficacy can be augmented through additional non-cytotoxic and non-radiotherapeutic modalities. Here, we highlight emerging strategies that sensitize tumors to ICI therapy, many of which—although not yet widely studied in HNSCC—are supported by compelling mechanistic rationale.
The concept of DDS is critical for ensuring that cancer therapeutics reach the tumor tissue effectively and alter the biological conditions of the TME in a treatment-favoring manner. In addition to traditional cytotoxic chemotherapies, various approaches have been explored to chemically and non-chemically enhance the pharmacokinetics, tissue distribution, and functional engagement of ICIs within the TME. These include emerging strategies that apply the concepts of proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTACs) or prodrug-like designs to extend or modulate the activity and selectivity of ICIs [ , ]. Moreover, recent preclinical findings in the field of gut microbiota also have important implications for ICI sensitization [ ]. However, despite these advances, clinical application of these technologies remains limited. Challenges include the discovery of biomarkers that benefit from these innovations and selecting technologies that account for the specific biological environment of the primary tumor site.

## Modified DDSs to enhance ICI efficacy
Recent advances in bioengineering have enabled the development of increasingly sophisticated DDS platforms. These platforms are designed to enhance responses to ICIs. Among them, nano drug delivery systems (NDDSs) allow targeted delivery of diverse therapeutic cargos, including nucleic acids, peptides, and small molecules to modulate key pathways governing antitumor immunity [ ].
Several studies have demonstrated that lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-mediated delivery of STING agonists can overcome resistance to PD-1 blockade while mitigating systemic toxicity. In particular, Sun et al. showed that cyclic dinucleotide–manganese nanoparticles markedly amplified STING activation, leading not only to potent local innate immune activation but also to systemic antitumor immunity characterized by enhanced type I IFN signaling, DC priming, and CD8⁺ T-cell responses [ ]. Direct administration of STING agonists such as cyclic guanosine monophosphate–adenosine monophosphate (cGAMP) is often limited by dose-dependent toxicities. Encapsulation within LNPs enables more localized and controllable STING activation. This results in enhanced type I IFN signaling and promotes NK cell activation and secondary induction of PD-L1 expression on tumor cells [ , ].
Beyond STING agonists delivery alone, combinatorial innate immune stimulation has also been explored. Chibaya et al. demonstrated that LNP-based co-delivery of STING and TLR4 agonists can effectively remodel the immunosuppressive TME. This strategy was evaluated in PDAC. When combined with senescence-inducing RAS-targeted therapies, it activated type I IFN–driven innate and adaptive immune programs. As a result, both NK and CD8⁺ T cell responses were enhanced. Durable antitumor efficacy was observed in preclinical models [ ]. Collectively, these observations support the integration of STING-loaded nanoparticles with ICIs as a strategy to counteract adaptive immune resistance. Notably, STING-based nanovaccines have been shown to overcome tumor-intrinsic immune escape programs in HNSCC; a STING-inducing nano vaccine effectively mitigated SOX2-driven immune evasion, restoring dendritic cell activation and CD8⁺ T-cell infiltration in preclinical models [ ].
DDS-based approaches have also been applied to directly modulate immune checkpoint expression. Jung, et al. developed poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticle encapsulating PD-L1–targeting siRNA (siPD-L1@PLGA). This formulation achieved efficient and sustained PD-L1 silencing without detectable cytotoxicity. In PDAC, patient-derived xenograft models, siPD-L1@PLGA significantly suppressed tumor growth. Treatment increased infiltration of IFN-γ–positive CD8⁺ T-cells. Granzyme B expression was also elevated, indicating enhanced cytotoxic immune activity [ ].
Nanoparticle platforms have further evolved to support co-delivery of multiple therapeutic agents. Reda, et al. developed a nanoparticle-based immunotherapy termed ARAC (Antigen Release Agent and Checkpoint Inhibitor), which simultaneously delivers a PLK1 inhibitor (volasertib) and a PD-L1 antibody. ARAC significantly reduced the effective doses of both agents. Despite dose reduction, robust CD8⁺ T cell-mediated antitumor efficacy was maintained. Notably, this effect was observed even in tumor models resistant to combined PD-1 and CTLA-4 blockade [ ]. Such modular DDS platforms enable flexible incorporation of diverse immunomodulatory cargos. These include STING agonists, siRNAs, and mRNA. Agents that modulate the TME, such as those targeting hypoxia or aberrant pH, can also be incorporated. Through this flexibility, organ- and context-specific therapeutic adaptation becomes feasible [ ]. Beyond localized or intratumoral delivery strategies, recent work has demonstrated that systemically administered, immunoengineered nanoparticles can reprogram the TIME into an ICI-permissive state. Cho et al. reported that systemic delivery of a TLR3 agonist combined with manganese nanoparticles induced robust type I IFN signaling, DC activation, and CD8⁺ T-cell infiltration while suppressing MDSCs, thereby converting immunologically “cold” tumors into inflamed, immune-accessible states even in the absence of direct checkpoint blockade [ ]. Type-I interferon signatures remodel the myeloid cell compartment in the tumor microenvironment to support the expansion of cDC1 subset, which is adept at cross-priming CD8+ effector T-cells. However, the type-I interferon system is often constrained by the metabolic features of the tumor microenvironment. Recent studies have shown that saturated fatty acids suppress STING signaling and attenuate type-I interferon responses, thereby dampening tumor immunogenicity despite the presence of cytosolic DNA sensing machinery [ ]. In addition to lipid-mediated constraints on STING signaling, the evidence suggests that intratumoral glutamine metabolism can also metabolically restrain STING activation in certain tumor contexts. In preclinical HNSCC models, a glutaminase inhibitor prodrug sensitized tumors to STING agonists, resulting in enhanced type-I IFN signaling and antitumor immunity [ ].
Although these DDS- and NDDS-based strategies have not yet been sufficiently validated in HNSCC, their capacity to enhance intratumoral immune activation while minimizing systemic toxicity highlights their potential as a future avenue for precision immunotherapy (Fig. ). 

## Cancer vaccination and ICI sensitization
Various cancer vaccines have been developed to date, including antigen, DCs, DNA/RNA, and whole cell vaccines [ ]. Importantly, a clinical factor to consider closely in HNSCC is HPV infection, which is believed to influence tumor metabolism and immune evasion differently from HPV-negative tumors [ ]. HPV⁺ HNSCC, particularly oropharyngeal cancers, often exhibit relatively higher PD-L1 expression and increased infiltration of immune cells, including CD8⁺ T cells, CD4⁺ T cells, and B cells, which can be spatially organized into TLS [ , ]. These immunologically "hot" tumors are more likely to derive benefit from ICI therapy. Indeed, in the KEYNOTE-048 trial, stratification incorporating HPV-associated status was implemented, and subgroup analyses suggested a potential differential benefit of PD-1 inhibition across biologically distinct HNSCC subsets. Moreover, the presence of TLS and other immune markers in these tumors may serve as candidate predictive biomarkers for response to ICIs in this subgroup [ ]. In CheckMate-358, HPV⁺ tumors demonstrated immune responsiveness and signals of clinical activity to neoadjuvant nivolumab. The HPV⁺ cohort also achieved higher rates of mPR, which is consistent with an inflamed TME and pre-existing immune infiltration typically observed in HPV-driven HNSCC. However, this mPR did not show a clear correlation with short-term survival outcomes, indicating that the association between mPR and survival remains uncertain in this setting [ ].
Although therapeutic HPV vaccines have elicited strong immune responses against HPV-16, vaccination alone has generally been insufficient for the control of invasive HNSCC [ ]. In a study by Peng et al., intratumoral administration of the TA-CIN vaccine targeting HPV16 E6/E7, in combination with PD-1 blockade, significantly enhanced the infiltration of antigen-specific CD8⁺ T cells within the tumor and led to complete tumor regression. This combination therapy demonstrated a markedly superior antitumor effect compared to either modality alone [ ]. Moreover, a single-center phase 2 clinical trial of an HPV-16 vaccine (inducing HPV-specific T cells) demonstrated encouraging clinical activity, including improved response rates and survival outcomes compared with historical PD-1 monotherapy cohorts [ ]. This combination has remained promising in long-term follow-up, showing increased infiltration of PD-1+ T cells and macrophages, which was associated with favorable therapeutic response, particularly in association with IFN-γ-related immune activation and immune infiltration [ ]. Another clinical study revealed that a DNA vaccine targeting HPV-16/18 E6/E7 with an IL-12 adjuvant (MEDI0457), combined with the PD-L1 inhibitor durvalumab, could enhance HPV-16/18-specific T-cell responses. This study further demonstrated that HPV-16/18-specific immune responses and signals of clinical activity were observed regardless of tumor PD-L1 expression level (≥ 25% vs. < 25%), suggesting that vaccine-driven immunogenicity may augment the antitumor activity of PD-L1 blockade [ ]. Moreover, previous reports indicate that HPV+ HNSCCs are characterized by elevated PD-L1 expression and abundant TILs, particularly CD8⁺ and PD-1⁺ T cells, indicating an immunologically active TME that may confer increased responsiveness to ICI treatments [ – ]. Collectively, these findings suggest that susceptibility to ICI appears to be relatively higher in HPV+ HNSCC. These findings collectively support the potential of HPV vaccines to contribute to HPV+ tumor control.

In parallel, the development of several cancer vaccines targeting HPV is underway, with encouraging preliminary signals reported from clinical trials evaluating their combination with ICIs [ – , – ]. 
Various late-phase clinical trials are currently underway across different organs and tumor types investigating the combination of mRNA-based cancer vaccines with ICIs. For instance, in melanoma, the combination of pembrolizumab with mRNA-4157 (V940), a novel mRNA-based individualized neoantigen therapy, improved the 18-month RFS from 62% with pembrolizumab monotherapy to 79% with the combination, while the proportion of recurrence or death events decreased from approximately 40% to 22%. Moreover, the incidence of irAEs in the combination arm was comparable to that observed with pembrolizumab alone [ ]. Nevertheless, many other late-phase clinical trials remain ongoing.
Recently, a study demonstrated that SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccines—originally designed for infectious disease prevention (e.g., BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273)—can sensitize tumors such as advanced non-small-cell lung cancer and metastatic melanoma to ICIs. The authors showed that even though the SARS-CoV-2 mRNA contains N1-methyl-pseudouridine modifications that reduce its intrinsic immunogenicity, it still weakly stimulates innate immune sensors, leading to activation of, at least in part, the IRF3/IRF7/Type I IFN signaling pathway. This, in turn, enhances APC activation and strengthens the CD8⁺ T-cell response against tumor-associated antigens. However, vaccination with SARS-CoV-2 mRNA alone exhibited only a limited effect on suppressing “immune-cold” tumors, whereas the combination with ICI therapy produced markedly enhanced antitumor efficacy. Furthermore, SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination was found to upregulate PD-L1 level within the TME, providing an additional mechanism for enhanced ICI responsiveness [ ].
This ICI-sensitizing property of mRNA vaccines is conceptually groundbreaking. It warrants further investigation in HNSCC, particularly HPV-negative and treatment-refractory subsets, which are often considered immunologically “cold.” Moreover, the development of combinatorial vaccines capable of simultaneously promoting HPV control and tumor immune activation may represent an innovative next-generation ICI-based therapeutic strategy.

## Cell/Gene therapy for ICI sensitization
Cell and gene therapies are rapidly emerging as transformative modalities in targeted cancer treatment. Several reports on these therapies have focused on the use of genetically engineered viruses to modulate T-cell dynamics [ ]. Heiniö, et al. showed that oncolytic adenoviral therapy with Ad5/3-E2F-d24-hTNFa-IRES-hIL2 holds promise as a single-agent treatment for ovarian cancer. In addition, it may sensitize tumors to subsequent T cell-dependent ICI therapy [ ]. Moreover, Hwang et al. demonstrated that intratumoral expression of the mitochondrial antiviral-signaling gene (MAVS) induces both local and systemic tumor antigen-specific T-cell responses in colorectal carcinomas. Mechanistically, MAVS activates type I IFN signaling pathways, enhancing DC function and promoting cytotoxic T-cell priming. This immune-stimulatory effect was associated with increased responsiveness to PD-L1 blockade, suggesting that MAVS induction could be exploited to overcome ICI resistance in otherwise poorly responsive cancers [ ].
Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-modified T-cells are genetically engineered T cells that target cancer cell-specific antigens. They have been widely investigated, especially in hematologic malignancies, and are designed to overcome immunosuppressive tumor-associated mechanisms [ ]. In HNSCC, Rosewell et al. reported that pretreatment with a binary oncolytic adenovirus capable of delivering cytokines and ICIs significantly enhanced CAR-T cell efficacy in tumor-bearing mice, when compared with either approach alone [ ]. Although this strategy does not represent a conventional form of ICI sensitization, it may hold promise for use in tailored ICI therapies in the future, if the safety concerns associated with it, including cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity, are adequately addressed [ ]. The drawbacks of this approach include limited efficacy against solid tumors, high costs, complex product processing, and uncertainty regarding long-term outcomes [ ].
Collectively, cell- and gene-based sensitization strategies highlight a paradigm in which ICI resistance can be overcome not by incremental immune stimulation, but by enforced reactivation of innate antiviral signaling and tumor antigen presentation.
By reshaping tumors from immunologically inert targets into immune-interrogable tissues, these approaches redefine ICI sensitization as a process of immune system reconstitution rather than simple immune augmentation.

## Chemically engineered antitumor-supportive strategies
A growing body of work has led to the development of chemical engineering strategies to generate antitumor-supportive agents, among which bispecific and multispecific antibody formats have emerged as particularly promising [ ]. Szijj et al. developed biotin-functionalized three-protein conjugates, which included two checkpoint inhibitory T-cell engagers (CiTEs) molecules (one containing an anti-PD-1 Fab region and the other containing an immunomodulatory enzyme) via a chemical method. This anti-PD-1 Fab region enhanced T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against cancer cells. They demonstrated that CiTEs exhibited greater ICI-supportive efficacy than conventional bispecific T-cell engagers [ ]. Mechanistically, these multispecific constructs act not only by bridging T cells and tumor targets but also by local checkpoint blockade at the immune synapse, thereby reinforcing effector-cell activation while limiting systemic irAEs [ ]. Such in situ checkpoint modulation may represent a new generation of “precision immunoengineering,” although issues including molecular size, tumor penetration, pharmacokinetics, and manufacturing complexity remain to be optimized for clinical translation [ , ]. Future studies will need to determine whether these CiTEs maintain selective activation in the immunosuppressive TME of HNSCC, where T-cell exhaustion, PD-1 upregulation, and stromal barriers coexist.
PROTACs have emerged as a novel therapeutic modality in cancer therapy because they can degrade proteins that remain challenging to address using conventional drugs. In particular, targeting immunosuppressive proteins has the potential to improve the TIME and enhance the efficacy of ICI therapy. There have been several reports of PROTACs, which utilize E3 ligands as foundational components, being developed to enhance ICI efficacy. One of those studies demonstrated that PROTAC-mediated degradation of the nuclear receptor NR4A1 in exhausted CD8⁺ T cells was associated with remodeling of the TIME, characterized by increased intratumoral effector memory CD8⁺ T cells and B cells, along with reduced immunosuppressive monocytic MDSCs, and was consistent with enhanced antitumor immunity. These findings suggest that NR4A1-targeting PROTAC could function as a potential sensitizer to ICI, especially in immunosuppressive tumor contexts [ ]. Beyond NR4A1, several PROTACs have been designed to degrade PD-L1 or other immune-evasive signaling molecules, shifting from simple inhibition to complete elimination of immune checkpoints [ ]. This degradation-based strategy may also theoretically lead to the generation of neoepitopes from degraded proteins, providing additional antigenic substrates for cross-presentation and immune priming [ ]. However, the translational application of PROTACs still faces challenges such as cell permeability, stability, tumor-specific E3 ligase expression, and potential off-target immune activation. Moreover, their optimal timing and dosing in combination with ICIs remain undefined and may require integration with spatiotemporal biomarker monitoring of TME remodeling.
Collectively, recent studies have shown that PROTACs can not only degrade proteins conferring resistance to ICI but may also generate novel antigenic peptides that enhance antitumor immune responses. However, to date, none of these studies have specifically targeted HNSCC, highlighting the need for further investigation in this disease setting [ – ]. Given the immune heterogeneity and the coexistence of exhausted T cells, suppressive myeloid cells, and dense stroma characteristic of HNSCC, both CiTE-based multispecific engagers and PROTAC-mediated protein degradation represent compelling directions for next-generation ICI sensitization strategies—bridging chemical design with the TIME.
Notably, chemically engineered strategies such as CiTEs and PROTACs extend ICI sensitization beyond immune activation toward molecularly precise rewiring of immune–tumor interactions. By enabling spatially restricted checkpoint modulation and selective degradation of immunosuppressive regulators, these approaches introduce a new dimension of immunotherapy design in which chemical precision is directly leveraged to overcome context-specific immune resistance.

## TIME modulation by sonodynamic therapy (SDT)
SDT is a non-invasive anticancer modality that uses ultrasound to induce reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation within tumor tissues, either directly or via the activation of sonosensitizing agents, resulting in tumor cell death through apoptotic and necrotic mechanisms [ ]. Beyond direct cytotoxicity, emerging preclinical evidence suggests that SDT may modulate the TIME, with features consistent with enhanced antitumor immune activation [ ]. This process involves the release of DAMPs—including calreticulin surface exposure and extracellular release of ATP and HMGB1—which promote DC maturation and antigen presentation [ ]. Furthermore, SDT has been shown to reduce Tregs within the TME, promote macrophage polarization from the M2 to the M1 phenotype [ ], and enhance DC maturation, collectively associated with enhanced antigen-specific T-cell priming and adaptive immune activation [ ].
Importantly, SDT facilitates immune cell infiltration into tumors by promoting vascular dilation and functional normalization, and upregulating endothelial adhesion molecules such as ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 [ ]. These molecules mediate firm adhesion and transmigration of effector lymphocytes—including CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells—across the vascular endothelium, thereby enhancing immune access to the tumor parenchyma, a prerequisite for optimal response to ICIs [ ]. In ICI therapy, vascular functional normalization, regardless of the upstream modality, can synergize with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade by increasing the number of activated T cells physically capable of engaging tumor targets, thereby potentially amplifying treatment efficacy [ ].
Consistent with these observations, Nesbitt et al. reported that SDT treatment elicited an adaptive immune response that was further potentiated by anti-PD-L1 therapy in a murine model of pancreatic cancer. They demonstrated that SDT induces a reduction in intratumoral MDSCs, activation of DCs, and an increase in CD8⁺ TILs, thereby enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of subsequent anti-PD-L1 treatment [ ]. Although this approach offers the advantage of minimal systemic toxicity and localized immunomodulation, definitive clinical evidence remains limited, warranting further investigation into its integration with ICIs across tumor types, including HNSCC.

## Gut microbiome-mediated sensitization of ICI therapy
Recent studies have revealed that the gut microbiome significantly influences host immune homeostasis and responsiveness. In particular, the composition of the gut microbiome is closely associated with patient outcomes following cancer immunotherapy [ – ]. One study demonstrated that the formulation of inulin, a widely consumed dietary fiber, as a colon-retentive gel, effectively modulates the gut microbiota in situ and enhances the antitumor activity of anti-PD-1 therapy by inducing a systemic memory T cell response. This study not only demonstrated enhanced ICI efficacy but also revealed that oral administration of inulin gel to mice increased the relative abundance of key commensal microbial taxa and their short-chain fatty acid metabolites. This, in turn, amplified the IFN-γ+ CD8+ T-cell recall responses, leading to the establishment of stem-like T cell factor-1 (TCF-1)+ PD-1+ CD8+ T-cells capable of surviving in the TME [ ].
A pilot clinical study is currently investigating the efficacy of inulin as an ICI-sensitizing intervention. PRINCESS trial (NCT05821751) is a prospective clinical study investigating the impact of inulin supplementation on the gut microbiota and immune response in patients with R/M-HNSCC undergoing ICI therapy (nivolumab or pembrolizumab) with or without chemotherapy. In this study, patients receive 10 g of oral inulin daily, and stool and blood samples are collected before and after treatment to evaluate changes in gut microbial diversity and cytokine profiles. Interim results presented at a conference in October 2023 indicated that 9 out of 16 patients achieved a PFS of at least 6 months. Notably, shifts in specific gut microbial populations were observed, with elevated IL-5 and IL-10 levels among patients with disease progression and increased IL-12 in those without progression. These findings suggest an association between dietary inulin, gut microbiota modulation, and systemic immune responses. This trial represents an important step toward leveraging microbiota-targeted interventions using inulin to enhance ICI efficacy in HNSCC [ ].
Furthermore, the pathological link between the oral microbiota and the gut microbiota, as well as their relationship with systemic diseases, has been investigated [ ]. While previous studies have primarily focused on the impact of oral bacteria on systemic diseases such as cerebrovascular and liver diseases in the context of periodontal disease, the mechanisms linking oral microbiota, gut microbiota, and systemic immune modulation remain incompletely understood. To date, the effects of oral bacteria on cancer immune responses and the subsequent alterations in the gut microbiota composition influencing the efficacy of ICIs have been suggested; however, no direct evidence has been reported in the literature. According to recent evidence, Fusobacterium nucleatum and Porphyromonas gingivalis, common constituents of the oral microbiota associated with periodontal disease, have been associated with increased interleukin production. Additionally, Klebsiella spp. activate macrophages in the intestinal mucosa, thereby enhancing the induction of Th17 cells via IL-1β signaling [ , ], consistent with broader evidence that oral and gut microbiota can shape antitumor immunity through cytokine-mediated pathways [ ]. Another study investigated the association between the salivary microbiome and the response to neoadjuvant ICI in patients with oral SCC. In a clinical trial (NCT04649476) involving 47 patients, metagenomic profiling of pretreatment saliva samples was performed and patients were stratified according to PR. Non-responders exhibited lower microbial diversity and were characterized by a reduced abundance of anaerobic bacteria, Eubacterium infirmum, Actinobaculum, and Selenomonas (collectively referred to as EAS). Lower EAS levels correlated with decreased intratumoral infiltration of CD4⁺, CD14⁺, CD68⁺, and FOXP3⁺ immune cells, suggesting that the salivary microbiome composition may reflect the immune activity of the TME. A predictive model based on EAS levels achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.81, indicating robust discriminative ability and highlighting the potential of salivary microbiota as a candidate non-invasive biomarker of immunotherapy responsiveness. Functional pathway analysis revealed enhanced metabolic activity associated with EAS in responders, implying that microbial metabolism may modulate host immune responses. Collectively, these findings suggest that the salivary microbiome may also function as a determinant of ICI sensitivity in oral SCC through its interaction with the tumor–immune–microbiota axis [ ].
Collectively, these findings highlight the gut microbiome as a distal yet powerful regulator of ICI responsiveness, capable of shaping systemic immune competence independently of direct tumor targeting. Unlike local immunomodulatory strategies, microbiome-based interventions offer a unique opportunity to reprogram antitumor immunity through dietary and microbial manipulation, positioning the gut–immune axis as an actionable lever for personalized immunotherapy. In HNSCC, where immune exclusion and heterogeneity are prominent, such non-invasive and system-level modulation may provide a critical complement to tumor-centered sensitization strategies.

In the future, it is anticipated that preclinical and clinical investigations will further advance the development of these approaches, ultimately enabling the implementation of more personalized ICI-based therapeutic strategies, potentially through the combination of one or several of these modalities. In parallel, the identification and validation of promising biomarkers capable of guiding such individualized treatments are also highly warranted. 

## Does tumor perfusion and platelet modulation sensitize ICI therapy?
Notably, hypoxia and aberrant tumor perfusion are closely linked to platelet activation, thrombosis, and the establishment of a pro-tumorigenic inflammatory milieu. Choi et al. reported that the anticoagulant enoxaparin can enhance tumor blood perfusion and alleviate hypoxia by preventing cancer-associated thrombosis, thereby potentially enhancing the efficacy of ICI therapy by reducing hypoxia and fostering an immune-supportive TME. In this study, the novel oral anticoagulant STP3725 was utilized to improve intratumoral blood flow and alleviate hypoxia. As a result, decreases in immunosuppressive cytokines and MDSCs, along with increased accumulation and functional activation of effector T cells, were observed. Furthermore, the combination of STP3725 with anti-PD-1 antibody therapy resulted in augmented tumor growth inhibition. These effects were consistently demonstrated across multiple tumor models, including the B16F10 melanoma model and a lung cancer model harboring K-Ras mutations [ ].
The improvement of hypoxia in the TME significantly impacts cancer proliferation, metabolism, and metastasis. However, its effects are highly universal yet complex, involving the intricate interplay of numerous factors [ , ]. A meta-analysis including 4,096 HNSCC patients with platelet count and 2,327 HNSCC patients with platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) data shows that tumor-associated thrombocytosis and high PLR are inversely correlated with HNSCC outcomes [ – ]. In a cohort of 1,051 HNSCC patients, lower circulating platelet counts and antiplatelet therapy are independent positive prognosticators [ ]. Baseline thrombocytosis is inversely correlated with OS in patients receiving ICI [ ]. These clinical associations raise the possibility that platelet-driven processes are not merely prognostic biomarkers, but may actively reshape antitumor immunity.
Beyond anticoagulation, pharmacologic modulation of platelet activity has also been implicated in immune regulation. Naproxen, a propionic acid nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) antiplatelet drug, showed significant cancer prevention efficacy in an HNSCC rat model [ ]. In agreement, Naproxen reduced tumor burden associated with Lynch Syndrome [ ]. Platelet-bound TGF-β1 can be released to drive cancer immune escape [ ]. NSAID-mediated prevention depends on their impact on TGF-β signaling [ ]. Collectively, these mechanistic insights underscore the importance for preclinical models that interrogate platelet-driven immune suppression and therapeutic resistance observed in human HNSCC.
Together, these findings illustrate that while modulation of tumor perfusion and hypoxia represents a biologically plausible strategy to enhance antitumor immunity, its translation into clinical benefit requires careful consideration of therapeutic context and risk–benefit balance. Tumor-associated thrombocytosis has emerged as a mechanism driving HNSCC immune escape. Thus, combinations beyond ICI that target different compartments of the TME need to be promptly evaluated for potential clinical translation. The recent development of high-fidelity syngeneic HNSCC models further substantiates the preclinical modeling for promising combinations. For example, NOOC1 and NOOC2 are single-cell-derived, C57BL6/J-syngeneic, male oral squamous cell carcinoma cell lines sharing many hotspot, common mutations as human HNSCC, such as Trp53 and Fat1. The overall mutational signatures of NOOC1 and NOOC2 share 91% and 95% in similarity to human tobacco-associated cancers, respectively [ , ]. These models are refractory to ICI and therefore provide a robust and clinically relevant platform to rapidly evaluate rational combinatorial strategies aimed at overcoming ICI resistance in HNSCC.

## Discussion
In this review, we summarized ICI therapy for HNSCC including neoadjuvant ICI, combination with RT, and the development of supportive treatments aimed at enhancing ICI efficacy, rather than the widely studied combinations with standard chemotherapy. A key focus is neoadjuvant ICI, where establishing histopathological grading systems for the assessment of therapeutic response and identifying reliable biomarkers remain critical priorities. However, biomarker-driven research and its prospective validation in clinical trials have lagged behind the rapid expansion of therapeutic strategies.
We also highlighted several emerging perspectives within the TME with potential effects on ICI response, including the presence and spatial reorganization of TLSs [ ] and TRMs [ ], the redistribution of Tregs under ICI treatment [ ], qualitative rather than quantitative features of CD8⁺ T cells such as ZNF683⁺ subsets [ ], and T-cell reprogramming driven by distinct CAF populations [ ]. Notably, the spatial proximity of TLSs to tumor-infiltrating regions has been suggested as a determinant of sustained T-cell priming and durable immune responses (Fig.  ) [ ]. While these observations collectively point toward a more refined, biology-driven framework for patient stratification and treatment optimization, their translation into clinical decision-making remains limited.
Additionally, the diversity of HNSCC biology—including the influences of oral microbiota, HPV, and EBV infections—adds another layer of complexity. These factors not only modulate immune surveillance but also reprogram tumor metabolism and stromal crosstalk [ , ]. Future work should therefore adopt an integrative systems-immunology approach that combines microbiome analysis, spatial transcriptomics, and immune-repertoire profiling to delineate how these variables interact to shape ICI responsiveness.
While several ICI-based strategies discussed here are still being evaluated in ongoing trials, their rapid expansion underscores the urgency of refining trial design and biomarker integration to fully realize their clinical potential. Importantly, recent clinical trials have not only demonstrated the therapeutic potential of ICIs in HNSCC but have also exposed a critical disconnect between biological rationale and clinical trial execution. Neoadjuvant ICI trials have generated important insights into early immune activation and tumor–immune interactions; however, they have simultaneously highlighted fundamental limitations in current evaluation frameworks. PR metrics such as mPR and pCR have not consistently correlated with overall or progression-free survival [ ], indicating that conventional histopathological endpoints may be insufficient surrogates for durable immune-mediated disease control. More importantly, many neoadjuvant trials have been designed primarily to assess feasibility and short-term PR, rather than to prospectively validate immune-based predictive biomarkers. As a consequence, immune infiltration patterns and activation states—while biologically informative—remain largely retrospective descriptors and have yet to function as robust pre-treatment decision-making tools. Addressing this gap will require large, biomarker-integrated prospective studies that redefine therapeutic success beyond tumor regression and align immune dynamics with long-term clinical benefit.
A similar disconnect between biological rationale and trial design is evident in studies combining RT with ICIs. Although preclinical and translational data support RT-induced immunogenic modulation and the potential for abscopal effects, clinical reproducibility has been limited. Across trials, radiation dose, fractionation, and sequencing relative to ICI administration vary substantially, often without explicit mechanistic justification. Consequently, negative or inconclusive outcomes in RT–ICI trials may reflect suboptimal biological alignment rather than an intrinsic lack of therapeutic synergy. Future clinical development will therefore require biologically informed trial designs that explicitly integrate immune parameters, such as spatial and temporal immune responses, as co-primary or adaptive endpoints.
Finally, emerging biomarkers—including TLSs, TRMs, qualitative CD8⁺ T-cell states, and microbiota-associated immune modulation—are increasingly recognized as biologically relevant determinants of ICI responsiveness. Nevertheless, their limited integration into prospective clinical trials highlights a fundamental constraint of current trial paradigms, in which complex immune features are rarely incorporated into eligibility criteria, stratification, or endpoint definition and are instead relegated to retrospective correlative analyses. A major obstacle to the prospective implementation of immune biomarkers is the implicit reliance on single baseline sampling to predict a fundamentally dynamic immune process. Longitudinal and adaptive trial designs that incorporate early on-treatment immune changes may therefore be required to operationalize spatial and temporal biomarkers such as TLSs, TRMs, and microbiota-associated immune modulation. Rather than acting through simple additive effects, effective ICI-based combinations may function by inducing discrete immune state transitions, underscoring the need for trial designs that capture qualitative shifts in immune architecture rather than incremental changes in magnitude.
Overcoming these challenges will require a conceptual shift in clinical trial design—from drug-centric frameworks that primarily assess the incremental benefit of adding or combining agents, toward immune-centric trial architectures that treat the immune system itself as a dynamic, quantifiable therapeutic target. Such a transition will be essential for translating mechanistic synergy into durable clinical benefit in HNSCC.
In conclusion, the field is now transitioning from empirical combination strategies toward precision immuno-oncology, where rational trial design will hinge on biomarker-guided patient selection and mechanistic understanding of the TME. Combination therapies—spanning neoadjuvant ICI, RT, and emerging sensitizers such as small molecules, gene/cell therapies, engineered antibodies, and microbiome modulation—represent the next frontier in personalized treatment for HNSCC. Moving forward, success will depend on establishing robust predictive frameworks, standardized evaluation systems, and collaborative data-sharing networks to translate mechanistic synergy into durable clinical benefit.
