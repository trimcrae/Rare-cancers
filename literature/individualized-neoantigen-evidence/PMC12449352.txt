## Abstract
Specific immunotherapy (SIT) is key in allergic diseases, tumor immunity, and autoimmune regulation. In recent years, the mechanism of action of drugs in SIT has attracted much attention, including the induction of hypersensitivity responses and modulation of immune tolerance. However, scientific challenges remain regarding their mechanism of action and optimization strategies. Studies on pharmacological SIT have been accumulated in the past, and there is an urgent need for bibliometric analyses to review and prospect these results for future academic development. Strict search criteria were developed to screen and download literature information from the Web of Science Core Collection. Six elements of the included literature were analyzed and visualized using Citespace, VOSviewer software, and the Bibliometrix package. A total of 682 publications related to the drug SIT were included in this study. The growth trend in the number of publications is evident and entering a new phase with great potential for the future, with the highest total number of citations in 1998. The countries and institutions with the most publications were the USA and the University of Genoa. The author who contributed the most to the field was Incorvaia, Cristoforo. Moreover, Bousquet J was the most influential author. Allergy was considered the leading core source journal, and the Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology is the most influential. The reference with the highest outbreak intensity is Roberts G, 2018, Allergy, V73, P765, https://doi.org/10.1111/all.13317. The analysis of the keywords by the various metrics shows that the research hotspot is the tumor-associated SIT, the cutting-edge topic is the mechanism of action of the drug AIT, and the respiratory SIT application is the cutting-edge hot issue. Drug SIT has made good progress with the joint participation of global research institutions, scientists, and various journals. However, cooperation and communication are weak, and it is necessary to build a new cooperation mode to promote the globalization of research results. We have found the research hotspots and cutting-edge issues in this field, which will guide us to make breakthroughs in a more precise direction and play the important role of drug safety in safeguarding human life and health.

## Introduction
Specific immunotherapy (SIT) is a therapeutic strategy based on antigen-specific immunomodulation, the core of which lies in the precise modulation of the body’s immune response to specific antigens, thereby achieving immune tolerance or enhancing immune clearance (Burton et al. ). The applications of SIT cover a wide range of fields, such as allergen immunotherapy (AIT), autoimmune disease immunotherapy, anti-infection immunomodulation, and anti-tumor immunotherapy (Larché et al. ; Prakken et al. ; Pol et al. ; Jäger et al. ). Traditionally, SIT is mainly used in treating allergic diseases, aiming to alleviate allergic symptoms by inducing the production of regulatory T cells and allergen-specific IgG4 through the long-term administration of specific allergens to inhibit Th2-type immune responses (Martín-Cruz and Palomares ; Durham and Shamji ). However, the direction of SIT in anti-tumor immunotherapy is opposed to AIT treatment, and its goal is to promote the body’s immune recognition and clearance of tumor antigens through activation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and enhancement of antigen-presenting function (Zhan et al. ). In recent years, with the rapid development of biopharmaceutical technology, the role of drugs in SIT has received increasing attention. On the one hand, drugs can act as sensitizers to trigger drug hypersensitivity reactions. This situation severely limits the clinical application of key therapeutic agents (e.g., β-lactam antibiotics, platinum-based chemotherapeutic agents, paclitaxel compounds, and monoclonal antibodies). It poses a threat to patients’ disease management (Cheong et al. ). On the other hand, certain drugs can act as immunomodulators and play an adjuvant role in SIT, such as the application of small molecule inhibitors, cytokine modulators, and nano-delivery systems, all of which can influence the effect of SIT in inducing immune tolerance (Li et al. ). Finally, drugs can enhance the ability of the immune system to recognize and clear tumors in tumor SIT but face challenges such as tumor escape mechanisms, individual differences, drug resistance, and immune-related side effects (Ji et al. ). Therefore, how to rationally recognize and apply drug SIT has become one of the key issues in current research.
Bibliometric analysis is the comprehensive use of theories and methods of econometrics and informatics to quantify and analyze the quantity and quality of literature by quantifying and analyzing factors such as the time of publication, country/region, authors, institutions, and journals; it can reflect the quantity, quality, and influence of the literature in a more systematic way, and also intuitively understand the development trend of disciplines, cutting-edge directions, hot contents, and the current status of cooperation, and provide a reference for the future academic research, discipline construction, and scientific research to provide reference (Wang et al. ; Yu et al. ). Since entering the twenty-first century, bibliometrics has been widely used in many research fields, including medicine. It has become an effective method for researchers to summarize the past and look forward to the future (Wang et al. ; Zhao et al. ). To the best of our knowledge, no bibliometric analysis of drug SIT has been published in major global databases. In this study, we mainly used the Citespace, VOSviewer, and Bibliometrics packages to visualize and analyze the published high-quality literature in the last 34 years, and the ultimate goals were mainly to (i) clarify the basic information of publications related to drug SIT, (ii) elucidate the hotspots and cutting-edge issues in the field of research, and (iii) depict and build a collaboration conducive to the development of an academic framework.

## Materials and methods

## Data sources and search strategies
Web of Science Core Collection (Science Citation Index Expanded) (WOSCC [SCIE]) covers authoritative and high-impact academic journals around the world. It mainly contains research results in the field of natural sciences, with detailed citation information, high accuracy in retrieval, and an extended period (Birkle et al. ). Therefore, it is recognized as the most suitable database for bibliometrics (Tian et al. ). WOSCC has higher accuracy and periodicity than Scopus and other databases. Although PubMed is the central biomedical search platform, it is a typical abstract-based database that lacks citation information. Google Scholar data sources are not subject to rigorous screening, which makes it difficult to ensure the quality of the data and inaccurate citation calculation. We used WOSCC to screen the literature to satisfy the dual characteristics of high quality and wide range and to reduce statistical errors when using analysis software (Falagas et al. ). We completed the data search to prevent statistical errors due to database updates. We downloaded it on February 24, 2025, with the search formula ((((TS=(“desensitization therapy”)) OR TS=(“specific immunotherapy”)) OR TS=(“desensitization treatment”)) OR TS=(“allergen immunotherapy”)) AND TS=(drug). TI stands for subject and TS stands for topic in the search strategy, and we can ensure the accuracy of retrieval by using both at the same time. We also arranged for two researchers to validate the comprehensiveness of the results obtained by the search strategy to ensure that it included studies of small molecule drugs and other drugs (e.g., monoclonal antibodies). The timeframe for data collection was specified as January 1, 1990, to December 31, 2024. Only research articles and review articles whose language was English were included, as shown in the search flowchart in Fig. . Two authors (CUI and CHEN) independently collected and compared all the data, with a concordance rate of 0.98. Eventually, a total of 682 articles that met the requirements were included in this study.
 

## Data download and visualization
We exported the screened articles from WOSCC [SCIE] as TXT files under “Full Record with Cited References” and subsequently imported them into bibliometric software for analysis. In this study, we used Citespace(6.2.R4) (Chen ) and VOSviewer (1.6.18) (Eck and Waltman ) to analyze the co-occurrence, co-citation, burst detection, and clustering of country/region, institution, authors, journals, keywords, and references, respectively, and to present the basic information of SIT-related publications in a user-friendly mapping and aesthetically pleasing visual function, which supports researchers and readers to have a clearer picture of the SIT-related publications. The Bibliometrics package in R (Aria and Cuccurullo ) is a powerful and widely used bibliometric tool, which we used to assess international collaborations and perform Bradford’s law analysis on journals. The study also used Excel software to summarize statistics for various types of data, including plotting trends in the number of publications issued and citations per year.

## Results

## Trends in the number of publications
The number of drug SIT-related publications made a large leap from 1990 to 2024, with a maximum 55-fold increase in annual publications. Figure A illustrates the trend of publications over the last 35 years, and based on the interval of the number of publications, we have divided the entire period into three parts to summarize the changes in trend development. The first stage is from 1990 to 2002, where the annual number of articles is concentrated within 10, and the total number of articles reaches 67, the initial budding stage. The second phase is from 2003 to 2019, where the annual number of articles was accompanied by a couple of pullbacks in the growth, crossing the 30-article target, and the overall trend was positive, with the total number of articles exceeding 440, which can be summarized as a fluctuating upturn. 2020 to 2024 is the third phase, where the annual number of articles stabilized at more than 40 and exceeded 50 for the first time in 2020, with the peak of the number of articles reached in 2022 (n=55), which is a high growth phase in the field of drug SIT. As shown in Fig. B, the average number of citations per paper was highest in 1998, with 129.29, followed by 2009 (n=74.81) and 1996 (n=71.25).

The results of the citation burst detection analysis (Fig. A) show that the bursts of literature all span within 5 years, with the earliest starting in 2001. Two citation bursts lasted until 2024. The intensity of citation outbreaks is between 5.28 and 13.53, among which “Roberts G, 2018, ALLERGY, V73, P765, DOI. 10.1111/all.13317” has the strongest outbreak. At the same time, it is one of the most recent literature with the latest outbreak time. The co-cited literature can reflect the basics of research, and Fig. B demonstrates the interconnections between citations. There are a total of three clusters that make up the network, and the dense links between them show that the connections are very strong, suggesting that the foundation of research in this field is solid. We also screened the 10 citations with the highest total citation frequency among drug SIT-related papers and summarized the details in Table 6 (supplementary file). ## Discussion
From 1990 to 2024, the overall growth trend in the number of drug SIT publications is relatively obvious. However, the details can be summarized by the word “zigzag,” and we analyze that this phenomenon may be related to the time difference in the iterative development of new drugs. A lower number of publications in a given period does not indicate a lack of enthusiasm or quality of research. For example, 1998 had fewer publications but the highest number of citations in the entire cycle; 2009 was at the bottom of the second phase of publications but also had a high number of citations. All of the above shows that the research in the relevant years greatly influences and inspires the subsequent scientific research, so the papers in these periods need to be emphasized and referred to. New academic advances are realized based on previous research results, so the highest point of citation in the first stage exceeds that in the second stage, and the citation in the second stage is higher than that in the third stage in general, which is inversely proportional to the number of publications, indicating that such academic advances have already been manifested. Although the number of articles published in phase 3 has increased considerably, the number of citations is at a low value, which we believe is related to the more recent time of publication. Moreover, this phenomenon is only temporary, and the number of citations in phase 3 will continue to increase as the research continues.
Our review and data analysis revealed that the surge of phase 3 papers reflected academic progress, and important events also drove the trend. The launch of Omalizumab in August 2017 in the populous country of China, which became the first monoclonal antibody for asthma to be marketed in the country, added dynamism to the publication of related research results in the years that followed 2019 saw the emergence of the use of PD-1 inhibitors in the breakthrough applications in tumor immunotherapy became the focus of the global medical community. The introduction of these drugs marked a new era in tumor immunotherapy, with breakthroughs, especially in the treatment of lung cancer, melanoma, and other solid tumors (Han et al. ; Yu et al. ). Around 2021, positive progress was made in clinical trials of immunomodulatory drugs, which offer new therapeutic strategies for immune modulation in allergic, neoplastic, and autoimmune disorders (Charlinski et al. ). Academic research has been fueled by new changes that have brought light to clinical care.
Summarizing the distribution of drug SIT issuing countries and institutions is the basis for future international cooperation and exchange. Based on the study results, the USA has the greatest contribution and influence. However, it lacks cooperation and exchange with major authoring countries such as Italy, Germany, and England. The research is characterized by localization, and each of these countries should build closer academic clusters and promote inter-cluster integration to make the output of scientific research results evolve from multi-polarization to integration. Developed countries have more abundant results in this field, attributed to their many advanced pharmaceutical manufacturing enterprises. Developing countries should actively participate in the multi-center clinical trials of new drugs and carry out drug SIT research that meets the country’s needs based on the results of the trials. Although the vast majority of the institutions with many publications are from Europe, there is an obvious lack of cooperation among them. As the only research institution with high output and strong cooperation, the Berlin Institute of Health should first strengthen the interconnection with other institutions in its own country to form a small-scale cluster, improve the quality and efficiency from cooperation, and then expand the scale of the cluster through inter-country exchanges and continue to develop into an international platform.
The United States’ leading position in drug SIT is mainly due to the combined effect of several factors. First, the strong research funding support system in the USA, especially from the National Institutes of Health and other organizations, has provided sufficient financial security to promote innovative research and development of SIT-related drugs and technologies. Second, the USA has a world-class research infrastructure, including many top universities and research institutions, which provide advanced experimental conditions for basic research and promote interdisciplinary collaboration and technology translation. Meanwhile, the USA faces a high incidence of drug allergies in clinical practice, and this practical need has prompted the scientific research community to work on optimizing the immune tolerance of drugs and the management of allergic reactions, thus accelerating the development of the application of SIT research. Finally, the U.S.’s global research collaboration and influence have strongly supported its leadership in this field. The synergistic effect of the above factors has enabled the USA to occupy a central position in the research and practice of drug SIT and become an important force in promoting the development of the field. Institutions with leading publication volumes have actively contributed to promoting scientific advances in this field. Research at the University of Genoa focuses on the clinical application of SIT in allergic diseases, especially in immunotherapy for respiratory diseases (e.g., allergic rhinitis and asthma), working to optimize treatment regimens and assess the effects of immune tolerance and promoting the practical clinical application of drug SIT (Rosa et al. ). Stallergenes Greer, a global leader in allergy immunotherapy, focuses on the development of novel immunotherapeutic drugs, especially on delivery systems for drug AIT and therapeutic strategies with combination biologics, which have made significant progress and provided key support for improving the efficacy of SIT (Demoly et al. ). In addition, these institutions have promoted in-depth exploration of immune mechanisms and drug regulation through interdisciplinary cooperation platforms, especially in the areas of changes in the dynamics of IgE and IgG4 immunity and immune cell regulation, which have provided theoretical basis and practical guidance for the mechanism resolution and clinical treatment of drug SIT.
Patterns of cooperation between different countries and institutions are influenced by several factors, particularly geographical, political, and economic factors. First, geographical factors play a crucial role in international cooperation. For example, some major developed countries in the USA, Europe, and Asia may have more limited direct collaborations between their research institutions due to distance, language differences, and different cultural backgrounds. Research institutions in the USA and Europe have strong independence and competitiveness in drug SIT. However, due to geographic distance, cooperation is more dispersed, resulting in less centrality of international cooperation and relatively weak partnerships. Secondly, political factors may also play a role in constraining scientific cooperation between different countries. International political relations, policy constraints, and the allocation of research funds may affect academic cooperation in different countries. For example, due to international sanctions or trade restrictions, certain countries may be subject to restrictions on research funding or technology exchanges, leading to their relative isolation in international cooperation. In addition, policy support and strategic direction of scientific research between countries may also impact the mode of cooperation, and some countries may prefer to strengthen cooperation with their scientific research institutions or economic partners while neglecting transnational cooperation. Economic factors are another key factor influencing international cooperation. The funding source for scientific research projects, the economic input, and the ability to transform technology usually determine whether a country or institution can participate in high-level international cooperation. Developed countries, such as the USA, Germany, and Switzerland, have stronger financial support for scientific research and advanced infrastructure and can afford high-cost clinical trials and drug research and development, promoting the rapid development of drug SIT-related research. On the other hand, some developing countries rely on external financial support due to a lack of funds and show relative lag in cooperation. Therefore, economic disparity and unbalanced distribution of research resources can lead to weaker cooperation between some countries and regions, further affecting the sharing of research results and the deepening of cooperation.
Authors and co-cited authors are the mainstay of drug SIT research, and their roles are often interchangeable. Our study found that authors with high output in this field do not have much difference in their contributions, and their collaboration is relatively limited, which is in line with the institutional collaboration described above. Therefore, in the future, the focus of strengthening academic exchange is to consider the “authors-institutions” as a whole and to closely interconnect with each other to promote the development of the discipline by exploring different forms of scholars’ visits, laboratory co-construction, and peer-to-peer support. Incorvaia, Cristoforo is one of the most influential authors in the field, focusing on using the SIT in respiratory allergy (Incorvaia et al. ). Akdis, Cezmi A’s work focuses on searching for biomarkers to diagnose and treat AIT (Ogulur et al. ). As a co-cited author with outstanding contributions in the field, Bousquet J has been in the limelight in recent years for several reviews of respiratory (allergic rhinitis and asthma) SIT in the Journal of the European Society of Allergy and Clinical Immunology and for exploring the association of COVID-19 with allergic diseases (Bousquet et al. ; Pfaar et al. ). Although Durham SR was also involved in the above review studies, he was more interested in clinical drug research in AIT, proposing to use toll-like receptor agonists as adjunctive therapy for AIT (Kirtland et al. ). The research direction of the lead authors is more directed towards respiratory AIT, but this is one-sided in the actual clinical setting, and all clinical disciplines should establish their own drug AIT research teams to study groups and diseases to promote drug safety practically better.
Journals have an important mission to disseminate drug SIT knowledge and are important vehicles for scholarly communication. Although the clusters of journals have taken shape, the publication volume and cross-citation rate of major journals are not high and still need to continue to develop. The cluster distribution of co-cited journals is relatively centralized, indicating that there are obvious disciplinary attributes in the field and also the existence of journal categories that match them, which are closely related to immunology and pharmacy, as can be seen from the initial browsing of the names of journals with a high number of citations. The results of the biplot overlay also confirm this idea and complement other disciplinary citation relationships. The various types of journals can be used as a bridge and a clue for publishing collaborations and hierarchical sharing of information about manuscripts on the drug SIT. We found that although 60% of the top ten journals in terms of publications are located in Q1, there is a distribution from Q2 to Q3. The amount of submissions received by high-level journals is huge, and some research results that could not be published in such journals due to space reasons should be emphasized. They can be recommended by the journals to journals in the next division to promote the rapid dissemination of disciplinary knowledge. Our selection of core source journals can actively lead to improving the quality of drug SIT publications by, for example, developing assessment strategies.
Mining key references provides a comprehensive picture of the current state of research, and reviewing previous findings can help develop the next steps in research. The Joint Working Group on Allergy, Asthma, and Immunology Practice Parameters led by Dykewicz, MS et al. (Dykewicz et al. ) developed a complete guideline for the diagnosis and management of rhinitis stating the different types of rhinitis as well as an AIT for allergic rhinitis. This guideline has received a great deal of attention from both scientists and clinicians alike. The most influential reference in recent years was written by Chu, Derek K et al. (Chu et al. ). They systematically evaluated the efficacy and safety of oral immunotherapy versus allergen avoidance or placebo for treating peanut allergy and showed that the available oral immunotherapy regimens for peanuts greatly increased allergies and allergic reactions. Our statistical analysis of the two most recent references at the time of the outbreak revealed the following: they are both guidelines for allergic diseases developed by the European Society of Allergy and Clinical Immunology for allergic rhinoconjunctivitis and house dust mite-driven allergic asthma, which exemplify specific applications of SIT (Roberts et al. ; Agache et al. ).
The keywords are grouped in the most important fields of study, summarized as “Drugs for immunity and allergy,” which perfectly matches this paper’s theme and confirms the topic’s accuracy and relevance. The other subject areas are the next-level branches of the theme, showing the distribution of drug SIT applications in different systems and populations and constructing the main framework for future development. Considering the results of keyword clustering, frequency of occurrence, and outbreak detection, and analyzing the results based on the references, we agree that the research hotspot in the field of drug SIT is tumor-related SIT, the frontier topic is the mechanism of action of drug AIT, and the application of SIT in the respiratory system is the frontier hotspot issue.
With the rapid development of immune checkpoint inhibitors, over-the-counter cell therapy, individualized neoantigen vaccines, bispecific antibodies, and lysosomal virus therapy, tumor SIT has become an important direction in the treatment of malignant tumors; however, its efficacy is limited by the immune escape mechanism of the tumor and the immunosuppressive status of the tumor microenvironment (Rastin et al. ). Therefore, combination drug therapy has become a key strategy to improve the response rate of tumor SIT treatment. Studies have shown that TGF-β inhibitors can reduce the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells (e.g., regulatory T cells Treg, myeloid-derived suppressor cells), thus improving the efficacy of tumor SIT (Niu et al. ). Metabolic regulatory drugs (e.g., the IDO inhibitor epacadostat) can restore T-cell function and reduce immune escape brought about by the metabolic adaptation of tumors (Cicin et al. ). In addition, epigenetic modulatory drugs enhance antigen presentation and improve the role of tumor SIT in low-immunogenic tumors (Hmmier and Dowling ). Over-the-counter cell therapy has made breakthroughs in hematologic malignancies, especially chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy, which has demonstrated high efficacy in B-cell lymphoma and leukemia. However, in solid tumors, the efficacy of CAR-T cells is limited by tumor antigenic heterogeneity, immunosuppression by TME, and T-cell failure (Abdo et al. ). It has been found that cytokines such as IL-15 or IL-21 enhance the expansion and survival of CAR-T cells, TGF-β inhibitors reduce the effects of suppressive signaling in TME, and the combination of PD-1 inhibitors slows down CAR-T cell exhaustion and enhances their sustained antitumor effects (Trojaniello et al. ; Shimizu et al. ; Cesano et al. ). In addition, novel T-cell receptor-engineered T-cell therapies targeting tumor-specific antigens have shown promising efficacy in some solid tumor clinical trials. In contrast, by directly lysing tumor cells and enhancing their immunogenicity, lysogenic viruses can significantly improve immunotherapeutic efficacy by combining them with oncolytic SIT (Chen et al. ; Kalsi et al. ). Important progress has also been made in the research of tumor vaccines, especially individualized neoantigen vaccines that can induce specific T-cell responses based on patients’ tumor mutation characteristics. In particular, mRNA neoantigen vaccines (e.g., vaccine candidates developed by BioNTech and Moderna) combined with nano-delivery systems can enhance antigen expression and improve immune activation (Chandra et al. ). To further enhance vaccine efficacy, researchers have developed a variety of immunomodulatory drugs: TLR agonists to promote dendritic cell maturation and improve antigen presentation efficiency (Magdalena et al. ); STING agonists to enhance type I interferon signaling and improve immune response strength (Papaevangelou et al. ); and HDAC inhibitors to modulate tumor antigen expression and enhance T-cell recognition (Tempora et al. ). Combining these drugs provides a new strategy for promoting vaccine therapy in the clinic. Tumor SIT, as one of the important areas, is receiving extensive attention. Research in this field focuses on activating the body’s immune system to recognize and remove tumor cells through immunotherapy, especially on breakthroughs in tumor immune escape mechanisms. Key research questions include the immunosuppressive state of the tumor microenvironment and the application of immune checkpoint inhibitors in combination therapy with SIT (Chen et al. ). The clinical potential of tumor SIT is enormous, especially in malignant tumors resistant to conventional treatments. By combining with immune checkpoint inhibitors, tumor SIT is expected to significantly enhance the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy and provide new clinical therapeutic options, especially to improve the survival rate of tumor patients and address the problem of immune escape. However, individual differences in the clinical efficacy of tumor SIT still exist, especially regarding the complexity of immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment. Different tumor types respond differently to SIT, resulting in less predictable clinical outcomes. In addition, the immune escape mechanism of tumor SIT remains incompletely resolved, and many tumors can still evade immune system attacks by altering immune escape pathways. More extensive clinical trial data and evaluation of long-term efficacy remain the focus of future research.
AIT is currently the only treatment capable of altering the natural course of allergic diseases, and its core mechanism involves the establishment of immune tolerance, modulation of effector cells, and dynamic changes in antibody subtypes. Drugs play an important role in AIT as immunomodulators to enhance the therapeutic effect, reduce immune-related adverse reactions, and improve the safety and stability of treatment (Buta et al. ). Mast cells (MCs) play a key role in type I hypersensitivity, leading to the release of histamine, chemokines, and proinflammatory cytokines through FcεRI receptor-mediated IgE binding, which triggers a typical tachyphylaxis hypersensitivity reaction (Zemelka-Wiacek et al. ). To inhibit the activation of MCs, pharmacological modulation strategies mainly include antihistamines (e.g., H1 and H2 receptor antagonists), leukotriene receptor antagonists (e.g., montelukast), T-cell inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine), and FcεRI antagonists, which reduce MCs degranulation and decrease the release of inflammatory mediators and thus improve the safety of AIT. In addition, AIT inhibits Th2 cell-mediated allergic responses by inducing the activity of regulatory T cells (Treg), which secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-10, TGF-β, and IL-35, and up-regulate co-inhibitory molecules, such as CTLA-4 and PD-1, thus decreasing the activation of T cells by antigen-presenting cells (APCs) (Qiao et al. ). In addition, AIT induced functional shifts in intrinsic lymphoid cells and monocyte subpopulations, which modulated the immune system toward hyporesponsiveness. Notably, allergen-specific IgE levels may be transiently elevated during AIT in the early stages, followed by a gradual decline, whereas allergen-specific IgG4 levels are persistently elevated (Strobl et al. ). IgG4 competitively binds to allergens, thereby preventing IgE-mediated degranulation of MCs and reducing allergen-induced inflammatory responses through FcγRIIb receptor-mediated inhibitory signaling (Cady et al. ). It (Shamji et al. ) was found that B-cell-targeting drugs reduced allergy-associated B-cell subsets and increased IgG4 expression, thereby enhancing the long-term tolerability of AIT. In addition, significant reductions in MCs and eosinophils accompanied by a decrease in skin test reactions during long-term intervention with AIT further demonstrated the clinical effectiveness of AIT (Larenas-Linnemann et al. ). However, AIT still faces problems such as unstable immune tolerance maintenance time, large individualized differences, and incompletely elucidated mechanisms. Therefore, future studies should focus on exploring the optimization strategy of AIT combination drug therapy, such as the combination of HDAC inhibitors, TGF-β inhibitors, FcεRI antagonists, or IL-4/IL-13 signaling pathway inhibitors to enhance AIT’s long-term tolerance maintenance ability. Combining genomics and biomarker screening to optimize patient stratification improves the precision of individualized therapy, and novel delivery systems are explored to enhance the specificity and durability of AIT. On the other hand, research on the mechanism of action of pharmacological AIT focuses on how to effectively improve patients’ immune tolerance to specific drugs and address drug-induced hypersensitivity reactions. With the introduction of novel immunomodulators, studying the drug AIT has become a hot spot. The core issues include how to induce effective immune tolerance, especially for IgE-mediated hypersensitivity reactions, and optimize the combination of immunomodulatory drugs to improve efficacy and reduce immune-related adverse reactions. In addition, research on individualized immunotherapy has provided new directions for the drug AIT. Drug AIT can provide safer therapeutic options for drug-allergic patients and optimize the clinical application of key drugs, reduce the risks associated with drug allergy, and greatly improve patient tolerance to drugs, thereby improving therapeutic outcomes. Despite the potential shown by pharmacological AIT, its widespread use is still limited by multiple constraints, including differences in drug responsiveness, long treatment cycles, and issues of sustainability of immune tolerance. In addition, the precise prediction of which patients will benefit from pharmacological AIT remains an open question, and individualized treatment strategies are not yet fully established.
SIT is crucial in managing respiratory diseases, especially allergic rhinitis (AR) and allergic asthma (AA). Currently, SIT drugs mainly include subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT) preparations and sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) preparations, of which SCIT is still the most widely used form of AIT in clinical practice. At the same time, SLIT has become the most widely studied form of AIT in recent years due to its safety and convenience. SLIT has become a focus of research and application in recent years (Lisiecka ). SCIT drug formulations mainly consist of standardized allergen extracts and are commonly used to treat patients with moderate-to-severe AR and concomitant AA. The dust mite SCIT vaccine is one of the most widely used AIT medications, and it is effective in lowering rhinitis symptom scores, improving lung function, and reducing the frequency of acute AA episodes (Suratannon et al. ). In addition, pollen-allergic patients can obtain long-term immune tolerance through SCIT treatment, especially seasonal AR patients, and the use of antihistamines and glucocorticoids can be reduced after receiving SCIT treatment, thus reducing drug-related adverse reactions (Turková et al. ). However, conventional SCIT is associated with adverse risks such as discomfort at the injection site and rare but potentially systemic allergic reactions; therefore, safer and more efficient SCIT formulations have been developed in recent years through adjuvant optimization and delivery system modification. Incorporating CpG oligodeoxynucleotides as adjuvants into SCIT vaccines enhances Th1 immune response and reduces Th2-mediated allergic reactions, thereby improving immune tolerance (Babayeva et al. ). In addition, SCIT formulations containing polyethylene glycol modifications extend the half-life of the antigen in vivo, thereby reducing the number of injections and improving patient compliance (Cao et al. ). SLIT medications are given to patients as tablets or drops, which deliver the allergen through the sublingual mucosa and stimulate a local immune tolerance response. Dust mite SLIT tablets are currently the only FDA- and EMA-approved oral medication for AIT, and are widely used in the long-term treatment of adult and pediatric AR patients, and have shown significant clinical efficacy (Macedo et al. ). Studies have shown that SLIT is not only effective in reducing AR symptoms but also in reducing the risk of AA during the follow-up period. In addition, SLIT formulations of herb pollen, birch pollen, and cat and dog dander have also demonstrated better immune tolerance in several clinical studies. They can significantly reduce the need for antiallergic drugs in patients (Gianni ). To further improve the efficacy and safety of SLIT, novel delivery systems are being extensively investigated. Nanoparticle-encapsulated SLIT drugs can increase the bioavailability of allergens, enhance the induction of immune tolerance, and reduce systemic adverse effects (Li et al. ). In addition, liposomal carrier systems have been applied to the development of SLIT vaccines, which can prolong the retention time of the drug in the oral mucosa and thus enhance the therapeutic efficacy (Aliu et al. ). Combination therapeutic strategies of SIT and biologics provide new therapeutic options for refractory respiratory allergic diseases. The combination of anti-IgE monoclonal antibodies and SIT can improve the immune tolerance of SIT while lowering the serum IgE level, which is especially suitable for patients with severe asthma (Giavina-Bianchi and Giavina-Bianchi ). In addition, biologics such as anti-IL-5 and anti-IL-4/13 have been explored for adjunctive treatment of SIT, and studies have shown that these biologics can significantly reduce eosinophil-mediated airway inflammation and enhance the long-term therapeutic effects of SIT (Ménonville et al. ). Personalized SIT drug therapy has also become a research hotspot based on the development of precision medicine. Genetic testing and biomarker analysis can predict patients’ responsiveness to different SIT drugs, thus optimizing the treatment plan and reducing the treatment failure rate. In addition, artificial intelligence and big data analytics have been used for SIT drug efficacy assessment and patient management, which can optimize the dosage and course of drug administration according to the patient’s immune profile and improve the science of clinical decision-making (Qin et al. ). In addition to optimizing traditional SIT drugs, novel vaccine development is also an important direction for SIT drug innovation. Genetically recombinant allergen vaccines improve the safety and specificity of vaccines by removing sensitizing epitopes and enhancing immune tolerance. Virus-like particle vaccines can effectively enhance the body’s immune response and reduce the risk of allergic reactions due to their ability to mimic the immune properties of natural virus particles. Regarding respiratory SIT applications, with the advancement of immunotherapeutic drugs, establishing immune tolerance in allergic rhinitis and allergic asthma has become a hot research area. Researchers are delving into how to further improve the efficacy and safety of SIT through novel drug delivery systems such as SLIT and SCIT. Current key issues include optimizing the drug delivery system to reduce side effects, prolong the duration of immune tolerance, and address instability during immune tolerance establishment. The potential clinical significance lies in the fact that SIT treatment can significantly improve patients’ allergic symptoms. Establishing long-term immune tolerance can reduce drug dependence and lower the need for traditional allergy medications, thus improving patients’ quality of life. With the advancement of personalized treatment, respiratory SIT will provide a more precise and effective treatment option for patients with allergic diseases. There are individual differences in the efficacy and safety of SLIT, and some patients may still experience adverse reactions after receiving treatment. Although SLIT has shown better efficacy in some common allergens (e.g., dust mites and grass pollen), it still has limited efficacy in patients with certain allergens (e.g., animal hair) and severe allergic asthma. In addition, SLIT has a long treatment period and short maintenance of immune tolerance, and further studies need to address the persistence of efficacy and optimization of treatment regimens.
Above, we have only given a brief overview of the hotspots and cutting-edge issues, and there are still many details not mentioned that deserve to be dug deeper by researchers in various fields in the future to make more significant breakthroughs for drug SIT research.
Reviewing the collaborative network again, we find that the research directions in drug SIT in the USA, Italy, Germany, and other countries leading in the number of publications are highly compatible with the current research hotspots. Institutions such as Stallergenes Greer and the University of Genoa in the United States focus on the immunomodulatory role of drug AIT and drug delivery systems, which are closely related to the current cutting-edge topics of drug SIT. Meanwhile, research on tumor-related SIT has become a hot topic in recent years, especially in the application of tumor immunotherapy, and the U.S. leadership in emerging immunotherapeutic technologies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors and over-the-counter cellular therapy, has further promoted research related to tumor SIT. Research at institutions such as the University of Genoa and Imperial College London has focused on allergic rhinitis, closely related to the current cutting-edge topic of SIT drugs. The University of Genoa and Imperial College London are focusing on establishing immune tolerance and optimizing drug treatment efficacy in diseases such as allergic rhinitis and asthma, which fits the current research direction of SIT in managing respiratory diseases. Although there are strong collaborations between individual institutions, the density of global research collaborations is low, especially between different countries. This may be due to geographic distance, language barriers, and unequal distribution of research resources, where collaboration across countries is often limited to separate academic systems and fails to form strong transnational collaborative alliances. In the future, the field of drug SIT needs to build more diversified international cooperation and resource sharing, especially in key technology areas, to promote collaboration in the global scientific research community, optimize the existing cooperation mode, and form closer academic clusters.
Drug SIT faces multiple challenges in clinical application. First, safety issues are a major obstacle in the application of drug SIT. Immunomodulatory drugs may cause adverse reactions in the immune system, such as immune hyperactivation, allergic reactions, and suppression of the immune system, and they may increase the risk of infection. Although drug SIT helps to improve drug tolerance, the instability of efficacy, especially the problem of establishing and maintaining immune tolerance in some patients, remains an important factor affecting its widespread use. In addition, drug SIT treatment takes a long time, and there are individual differences in efficacy, which makes patient compliance another important issue. From a cost-effectiveness perspective, the high cost of pharmacologic SIT is one of the major barriers to its widespread use, especially since many immunomodulatory drugs are expensive and require regular monitoring and follow-up during treatment, which increases the financial burden of treatment. Although SIT is expected to improve treatment safety and efficacy, its economic benefits must be further validated, especially in treating nonfatal allergic reactions, where the cost of pharmacologic SIT may be higher than that of other treatments. In addition, the accessibility of drug SIT is constrained by the uneven distribution of resources, especially in developing countries and regions, where the shortage of treatment facilities and professionals limits the widespread use of SIT.
Although drug SIT has been gradually incorporated into routine treatment regimens in developed countries, accessibility of treatment remains a major challenge for patients in some economically less developed regions. Translating the results of drug SIT research into clinical practice faces many challenges. However, significant progress has been made, especially in improving patient prognosis, reducing adverse drug reactions, and driving clinical guideline updates. First, drug SIT improves patient outcomes and survival by improving immune tolerance and reducing drug allergic reactions. Immunomodulators such as omalizumab in drug allergy management have been proven to significantly reduce allergic reactions triggered by common drugs such as β-lactam antibiotics and platinum-based chemotherapeutic drugs, improving patients’ tolerance to drugs. Drug AIT not only effectively reduces allergic reactions but also avoids treatment interruptions due to drug allergy, thus improving patient compliance and, thus, long-term prognosis. Studies of tumor SIT have shown that combining immunotherapy and conventional therapy, especially in treating drug-resistant tumors, significantly improves patient survival and immune tolerance. Second, drug SIT significantly reduced the incidence of adverse drug reactions, which is especially important in the management of drug allergies. Immunomodulatory drugs show strong potential by improving patients’ drug tolerance, especially in managing allergic reactions. Immunomodulatory drugs enhance the safety of treatment by modulating the immune system response and reducing allergic reactions, especially for patients who are allergic to conventional medications; the drug AIT provides a new therapeutic option and reduces the adverse reactions of the immune system. Finally, the research results of drug SIT have driven the updating and development of clinical treatment guidelines. With the gradual validation of the efficacy of the drug SIT, especially in managing allergic diseases and drug-allergic reactions, more and more clinical guidelines are incorporating it into the routine treatment regimen. For example, guidelines developed by the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI) and the American Academy of Allergy, Asthma, and Immunology (AAAAI) have recommended the use of immunomodulatory drugs and pharmacological AIT in allergic rhinitis, asthma, and other diseases (Pfaar et al. ; Cho et al. ). Meanwhile, advances in tumor immunotherapy, especially the combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors and SIT, are gradually influencing the standard treatment protocols for tumor therapy. The research results of drug SIT have promoted the clinical application of individualized therapy and immunomodulation strategies, providing clinical medicine with more precise and safer treatment options.
The most promising avenues for future drug SIT research include personalized therapy, a combination of immunomodulatory drugs, the development of novel delivery systems, the exploration of novel immunotherapies, and the application of biomarkers. Personalized therapy will rely on advances in genomics and immunology to improve efficacy and reduce adverse effects by accurately evaluating the characteristics of a patient’s immune response and developing a personalized treatment plan. Combining immunomodulatory drugs with traditional SIT can enhance immune tolerance and reduce side effects, but the safety and long-term effects of immunomodulatory drugs still need to be addressed. Novel delivery systems, such as nanotechnology and smart carriers, will improve the precise delivery of drugs and optimize the intensity of immune stimulation. Novel immunotherapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors and CAR-T cell therapies, in combination with SIT, may provide new treatment strategies for cancer and allergic diseases. Biomarker development can help monitor treatment efficacy, predict patient response, and support personalized therapy. Addressing the challenges of individual differences, drug delivery, and persistence of efficacy will drive the application and advancement of SIT in multiple fields.
Bibliometric analysis provides valuable information for developing personalized drug SIT strategies by systematically quantifying and visualizing drug SIT-related literature. Bibliometric analyses using population characteristics as screening criteria can reveal differences in the response of different patient groups to SIT treatments, helping to identify the key role of individual immune mechanisms in treatment and to tailor the treatment to the individual. Bibliometric analysis with intervention method as the research object helps identify the key factors in establishing immune tolerance. Through the hotspot collection of different treatment methods, it can comprehensively and accurately provide data support for personalized SIT. Finally, bibliometric analysis is conducted with prognosis as the keyword. In that case, it can also reveal the risk factors affecting the efficacy of drug SIT, which helps to guide the scientific standardization of drug use. Conducting bibliometric analysis from different research perspectives can provide a scientific basis for the clinical development of personalized drug SIT strategies, help to accurately predict patients’ response to treatment, and minimize the risk of treatment failure, which is worthwhile work in the future.

## Advantages and limitations
This is the first quantitative analysis of high-quality papers related to drug SIT using bibliometric methods. We visualized the essential elements of the paper according to the strengths of different bibliometric tools to give rigor and scientific validity to the article’s content. However, this paper has its shortcomings, as the inclusion criteria in the study reduced the number of papers involved, e.g., the database was limited to WOSCC, the language was English only, and the types of studies were only “article” and “review.” However, these limitations were intended to improve the precision and quality of the studies and had no significant negative impact on exploring the hotspots and prospects of drug SIT research.

## Conclusion
In this study, we comprehensively reviewed drug SIT-related publications over the past 35 years. By analyzing the publications’ basic information and key elements, we concluded that the global cooperation in drug SIT is insufficient. New modes of cooperation have been proposed according to different levels, which are committed to forming a multidisciplinary and globalized framework for close cooperation. In terms of research direction, tumor-associated SIT is the main research topic. At the same time, the mechanism of action of the drug AIT has received more attention in recent years, and we predict that the results related to the application of SIT in the respiratory system will be more fruitful in the future. Through the joint efforts of scientists around the world, drug SIT will enable drugs to play a greater role in safeguarding human life and health.
