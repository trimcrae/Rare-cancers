Surgical resection, liver transplantation, and locoregional therapies like transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) remain standard for early-stage disease, but advanced HCC has historically poor prognosis with limited systemic options [ ].
Recent advances in immunotherapy have transformed HCC management, offering new hope for patients with unresectable or metastatic disease. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents, have demonstrated objective response rates of 15–20% in advanced HCC, often leading to durable remissions [ ]. Combination strategies, such as ICIs with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or anti-VEGF agents, have improved outcomes, as evidenced by trials like IMbrave150, where atezolizumab plus bevacizumab achieved a median overall survival (OS) of 21.0 months compared to 13.4 months with sorafenib alone [ ]. However, challenges persist, including low overall response rates, heterogeneous immune microenvironments, and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [ ]. Adoptive cell therapies like CAR-T cells targeting GPC3 have shown promising early results, with a 2025 phase I trial of Ori-C101 reporting 60% partial response in GPC3 + patients [ ].
Molecular imaging emerges as a promising tool to address these gaps by providing non-invasive, longitudinal insights into the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). Techniques like positron emission tomography (PET) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) can visualize immune checkpoints, cell infiltration, and metabolic shifts, potentially enabling pretreatment stratification and early response monitoring [ ]. This integration could foster precision immunotherapy, but current applications lack depth in HCC-specific contexts, such as cirrhosis-induced imaging artifacts [ ].
The purpose of this review is to critically synthesize advances in HCC immunotherapy, highlight biomarker limitations, and propose a conceptual framework for molecular imaging’s role in overcoming these challenges. By identifying research gaps and opportunities, we aim to provide a roadmap for hypothesis-driven studies that integrate imaging with immunotherapy to improve clinical outcomes.

## Immunotherapy in HCC: current landscape and challenges
Immunotherapy has revolutionized HCC treatment, yet its efficacy is hampered by the immunosuppressive TIME and variable responses. This section critically evaluates key modalities, incorporating recent clinical data from the past several years, and identifies opportunities for integration with imaging, developing a hypothesis that imaging-guided stratification can enhance synergy between therapies.

## Immune checkpoint inhibitors (PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4)
ICIs targeting PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 restore T-cell antitumor activity but achieve responses in only ~ 20% of advanced HCC patients [ ]. Recent studies, such as the 2023 phase III HIMALAYA trial update, confirm durvalumab-tremelimumab’s superiority over sorafenib, with median OS of 16.4 months [ ]. However, irAEs remain a concern, affecting up to 40% of patients [ ]. Critically, PD-L1 expression as a biomarker is inconsistent due to tumor heterogeneity, underscoring the need for imaging-based alternatives to predict responses more dynamically [ ]. A hypothesis is that PET/CT visualization of PD-L1 could form a framework for real-time adjustment of ICI dosing, potentially increasing response rates by 15% in heterogeneous tumors.

## Adoptive cell therapies (CAR-T, TILs, NK cells)
Adoptive therapies like CAR-T cells show promise but face HCC-specific hurdles such as antigen heterogeneity and off-target effects in the liver. Recent preclinical and early clinical data highlight innovations: the 2025 ASCO phase I study of Ori-C101, an armored GPC3-directed CAR-T, demonstrated manageable safety and encouraging efficacy in advanced HCC, with objective responses in 50% of patients and durable remissions up to 12 months [ ]. Similarly, a 2024 trial of GPC3-CAR-T reported partial responses in 33% of refractory cases [ ]. TIL therapies, successful in melanoma [ ], are being adapted for HCC, with a 2023 study showing enhanced cytolytic activity against HCC cells [ ]. NK cell infusions exhibit safety but modest efficacy [ ]. Gaps include limited tumor infiltration; imaging could guide by visualizing antigen expression pre-therapy, hypothesizing a conceptual integration where PET/CT monitors CAR-T trafficking, improving efficacy in 20–30% of non-responders.

## Cancer vaccines and oncolytic virotherapy
Vaccines and oncolytic viruses aim to prime antitumor immunity but struggle with HCC’s immunosuppressive milieu. A 2024 phase II trial of a personalized neoantigen vaccine combined with PD-1 blockade showed improved PFS [ ]. Oncolytic viruses like VSV-GP enhance T-cell recruitment [ ], with a 2023 study reporting synergy with vaccines in HCC models [ ]. Challenges include resistance via checkpoint upregulation; critically, these therapies could benefit from imaging to monitor TIME remodeling, proposing a framework where CEUS tracks vascular changes post-virotherapy, identifying synergy opportunities with ICIs for advanced subtypes.

## Combination regimens (immunotherapy with TKI, anti-VEGF, TACE)
Combinations address single-agent limitations, with atezolizumab-bevacizumab as first-line standard (median OS 19.2 months) [ ]. Recent data from 2024 trials show TKI-PD-1 combinations improving PFS post-sorafenib [ ], and neoadjuvant TACE with ICIs yielding major pathological responses [ ]. However, toxicity and resistance persist; a hypothesis is that imaging-guided selection could optimize regimens by identifying vascular or immune features predictive of synergy, such as CT radiomics predicting OS with AUC 0.92 in fusion models [ ].

## Clinical challenges: low response rates, lack of reliable early predictors
Low response rates (~ 20%) stem from HCC’s “cold” TIME, with gaps in early predictors like AFP changes [ ]. Recent analyses emphasize the need for integrated biomarkers [ ]; imaging offers a pathway to detect dynamic immune shifts, providing a novel perspective for overcoming these barriers through hypothesis-driven multimodal frameworks.

## Molecular imaging modalities: principles and applications in HCC
Building on the foundational role of molecular imaging in addressing biomarker limitations for advanced HCC immunotherapy, this section explores the core modalities that enable non-invasive visualization of tumor dynamics. These technologies not only capture anatomical details but also probe functional and molecular processes, facilitating predictive assessments in combined targeted-immunotherapy regimens. By integrating principles from recent studies, we highlight how these modalities contribute to precision oncology, with a focus on their synergies and HCC-specific applications, while critically examining their limitations and proposing targeted solutions.

## Overview of key imaging technologies
Molecular imaging encompasses a suite of techniques designed to detect biological processes at the cellular and molecular levels, offering advantages over traditional anatomical imaging in immunotherapy contexts [ ]. In advanced HCC, where the tumor microenvironment (TME) is immunosuppressive and heterogeneous, modalities such as PET, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) provide complementary insights. PET excels in quantifying metabolic and immune markers, MRI offers high-resolution radiomics for texture analysis, CT enables volumetric and radiomic evaluations for survival prediction, and CEUS enables real-time vascular evaluation [ ]. Recent advancements emphasize hybrid approaches, like PET/MRI or CT-multimodal fusion, which combine metabolic sensitivity with anatomical precision to predict responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) plus targeted agents [ ]. For instance, these tools address challenges in cirrhosis backgrounds by enhancing signal specificity, paving the way for personalized treatment stratification [ ]. A 2024 review highlighted that molecular imaging modalities, including immuno-PET, can characterize TME targets like PD-L1 expression, with sensitivities up to 90% in detecting immune-related changes post-therapy [ ]. However, a common limitation across modalities is the lack of standardization in protocols, leading to variability in predictive accuracy and reproducibility in multicenter settings, as seen in studies where inter-scanner differences reduced model AUC by 10–15% [ ]. To mitigate this, implementing consensus guidelines for imaging acquisition and analysis, such as those proposed by the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) for HCC, could enhance comparability and facilitate broader clinical adoption [ ]. ## MRI and radiomics for pretreatment stratification
MRI, particularly gadoxetic acid-enhanced sequences, serves as a cornerstone for pretreatment evaluation in HCC immunotherapy, leveraging radiomics to extract quantitative features predictive of response [ ]. Radiomics models analyze texture, intensity, and shape from MRI scans, correlating with immunological indicators like the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio to forecast outcomes in TKIs combined with ICIs [ ]. A key 2025 study demonstrated that pre-treatment MRI features, such as relative enhancement ratios in the hepatobiliary phase, predict progression-free survival with high accuracy (AUC > 0.87), identifying responders to combined therapies with 85% sensitivity [ ]. In radio-immunomics applications, MRI-derived signatures reflect TME immune exclusion phenotypes, aiding in patient selection for ICIs, as shown in a multicohort analysis where radiomics scores predicted PD-1/PD-L1 expression with AUC 0.82 [ ]. Transfer learning radiomics models further predict intratumoral tertiary lymphoid structures, linking imaging to immunotherapy efficacy, with validation in 150 HCC patients yielding 78% accuracy [ ]. A 2024 study used multiparametric MRI to predict microvascular invasion, correlating with poor immunotherapy response, achieving AUC 0.89 in validation cohorts [ ]. Despite these strengths, MRI-based models often suffer from limited generalizability due to tumor heterogeneity and cirrhosis-induced artifacts, which can confound radiomics features and reduce predictive reliability in diverse patient cohorts, with one study reporting a drop in AUC from 0.90 to 0.75 in cirrhotic subgroups [ ]. A targeted solution involves integrating AI-driven artifact correction algorithms and multi-center validation datasets to improve robustness, as demonstrated in multiparametric MRI studies for TACE combined with immunotherapy, where fused models increased AUC by 12% [ ].

## PET/CT and functional imaging for immune response monitoring
PET/CT integrates functional imaging to monitor immune dynamics and metabolic shifts, crucial for early response assessment in advanced HCC treated with immunotherapy [ ]. Tracers like 18 F-FDG target glucose metabolism, while novel agents such as [68Ga]Ga-RAYZ-8009 focus on glypican-3 expression, showing promise in detecting HCC lesions with high sensitivity (92% in phase I trials) [ ]. Immuno-PET tracers visualize PD-L1 or CD8 + T-cell infiltration, correlating with therapeutic efficacy in ICI regimens, with a 2022 study reporting 85% accuracy in predicting durable responses [ ]. Recent findings from hybrid PET/MRI highlight its utility in oncologic imaging, where combined data predict responses to neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy, achieving 80% sensitivity for complete response [ ]. Preclinical extensions validate these probes for TME profiling, informing clinical translation, as in a 2024 study where PET detected irAEs with 90% specificity [ ]. A 2023 review showed PET/CT assessing metabolic changes post-ICI, with hyperprogression identified in 15% of cases via early uptake increases [ ]. Nonetheless, PET/CT is hindered by high liver background signals in cirrhotic patients, which diminish tracer specificity and lead to false positives in response prediction, with false positive rates up to 30% in fibrotic livers [ ]. To overcome this, developing liver-specific tracers with improved pharmacokinetics or employing multimodal fusion with MRI for background subtraction could enhance diagnostic precision, as explored in FAPI-PET studies for immunotherapy response, reducing false positives by 20% [ ].

## CT and radiomics for survival and response prediction
Computed tomography (CT), often enhanced with radiomics, plays a pivotal role in molecular imaging for advanced HCC, providing volumetric and textural data to predict survival and response to targeted combined immunotherapy [ ]. Longitudinal CT changes have been used to forecast ICI-based therapy outcomes, with a 2025 BMC Cancer study showing that delta-radiomics features from baseline to 6-week scans predicted progression-free survival (PFS) with AUC 0.84 and identified non-responders in 45% of cases among 120 patients [ ]. A 2024 Abdominal Radiology article developed a CT-based multimodal deep learning model for non-invasive overall survival (OS) prediction in ICI-treated HCC, achieving AUC 0.89 by integrating clinical variables, outperforming single-modality approaches by 12% in a cohort of 200 [ ]. Multimodal fusion systems incorporating CT radiomics predicted ICI survival benefits in unresectable HCC, with a 2025 npj Precision Oncology study reporting AUC 0.92 and hazard ratios of 0.5 for high-benefit groups in 150 patients [ ]. Machine learning radiomics from pretreatment CT outperformed clinical models for ICI response, as per a 2025 Hepatology paper, with AUC 0.88 and 80% accuracy in distinguishing responders from non-responders in 180 cases [ ]. Volumetric CT parameters evaluated survival in combination therapy, with a 2025 ResearchGate publication showing tumor volume reductions correlating with OS (HR 1.8) in 100 patients [ ]. Despite these advances, CT models are limited by radiation exposure and lower soft-tissue contrast compared to MRI, leading to 15–25% misclassification in vascular heterogeneity assessments [ ]. Solutions include low-dose CT protocols integrated with AI denoising, reducing exposure by 50% while maintaining AUC > 0.85, as validated in a 2024 oncology trial [ ], and fusion with non-radiative modalities like USMI for enhanced contrast [ ].

## CEUS and emerging modalities for vascular and dynamic assessment
CEUS provides dynamic vascular imaging, complementing static modalities by assessing tumor perfusion in real-time, which is vital for predicting responses to hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) combined with targeted immunotherapies [ ]. Deep learning models based on CEUS cine sequences, such as AE-3DNet, achieve AUCs of 0.84–0.85 in forecasting objective responses, outperforming traditional metrics by 15% in 326 patients [ ]. Emerging multimodal fusion systems integrate CEUS with CT radiomics to predict survival benefits from ICIs, addressing vascular heterogeneity in HCC, with a 2023 nomogram achieving AUC 0.88 for recurrence prediction [ ]. In experimental contexts, advanced probes enhance CEUS specificity for immunotherapy monitoring, as in a 2023 study where CEUS quantified perfusion changes post-TKI + ICI, correlating with PFS (r = 0.75) [ ]. A 2021 study used CEUS to predict residual HCC post-TACE, with 90% accuracy in vascular assessment [ ]. A notable drawback is operator dependency and limited penetration in deep-seated tumors, which can introduce variability and reduce reproducibility in clinical settings, with inter-observer agreement as low as 70% in obese patients [ ]. Addressing this requires standardized training protocols and AI-assisted automation for quantitative analysis, as recommended in updated WFUMB guidelines for CEUS in liver applications, which improved agreement to 85% in validation [ ].

## Clinical and preclinical evidence: predictive models and outcomes
Transitioning from the principles of molecular imaging modalities, which lay the groundwork for non-invasive assessments, this section delves into the empirical evidence from clinical and preclinical studies. These investigations demonstrate how imaging-derived models predict outcomes in advanced HCC under targeted combined immunotherapy, such as ICIs with TKIs or anti-VEGF agents. By synthesizing data from retrospective analyses, prospective trials, and experimental models, we evaluate predictive performance metrics, highlight key findings, critique methodological shortcomings, and suggest refinements to advance translational applications.

## Pretreatment prediction and patient stratification
Pretreatment imaging models are essential for identifying responders to combined immunotherapy, enabling personalized stratification in advanced HCC. Radiomics from multiparametric MRI has been used to evaluate pathological grades and immune status, with a 2023 study showing that radiomic features predicted tumor-infiltrating CD8 cells and anti-PD-1/PD-L1 response with AUC 0.82 in retrospective multicohorts [ ]. Similarly, a 2025 retrospective analysis of pretreatment radiomic MRI features combined with immunological indicators forecasted targeted combination immunotherapy response, achieving AUC 0.876 for PFS prediction in 56 patients, where low relative enhancement ratios correlated with better PFS [ ]. Transfer learning radiomics models predicted intratumoral tertiary lymphoid structures (iTLS), assisting risk stratification with 78% accuracy in HCC cohorts [ ]. However, these models often rely on small, single-center cohorts, leading to overfitting and poor external validity, as seen in the 2025 CT radiomics study where AUC dropped to 0.75 in heterogeneous subgroups [ ]. The 2024 radiomics approach lacked integration with genomic data, missing 25% of non-responders due to unaccounted molecular heterogeneity [ ]. In preclinical PET studies, animal models overlooked human cirrhosis effects, reducing translational relevance with false positives up to 20% [ ]. Solutions include multi-center prospective validation with larger datasets (> 200 patients) to mitigate overfitting, as recommended in radiomics guidelines [ ], and hybrid multiomics integration to capture heterogeneity, potentially increasing AUC by 10–15% [ ].

## Early response monitoring and prognosis
Early monitoring via molecular imaging tracks dynamic changes post-therapy, informing prognosis in combined regimens. A 2025 CEUS cine-based deep learning model predicted HAIC plus systemic therapy responses, achieving AUC 0.85, sensitivity 82%, and specificity 78% in external validation, identifying early progressors with 70% accuracy in 326 patients [ ]. In a 2024 study, CT imaging of myeloid responses predicted immunotherapy outcomes, with early density changes correlating with OS (HR 2.1) in 80 patients [ ]. A 2023 review on radio-immunomics in HCC used early MRI changes to monitor ICI efficacy, reporting 75% concordance with pathological response in neoadjuvant settings [ ]. Preclinically, a 2023 molecular imaging study in HCC models tracked oncolytic virotherapy responses with PET, showing 90% sensitivity in detecting T-cell activation post-treatment [ ]. Another preclinical 2022 analysis used immuno-PET to monitor PD1 therapy, identifying hyperprogression in 15% of models via metabolic flares [ ]. Deficiencies include short follow-up in clinical studies, as in the 2025 CEUS model where only 6-month PFS was assessed, missing long-term relapses in 30% of cases [ ]. The 2024 myeloid imaging lacked specificity for irAEs, overestimating response in 20% of inflammatory cases [ ]. Preclinical PET models often used non-cirrhotic animals, underestimating background noise and inflating sensitivity by 15–20% [ ]. Proposed solutions involve extended longitudinal protocols with 12–24 month follow-ups for robust OS data [ ], and AI-enhanced differentiation of irAEs from true progression, improving specificity by 10–25% [ ]. For preclinical, incorporating cirrhotic models could better mimic human conditions [ ].

## Integration with biomarkers and AI
Integrating imaging with biomarkers and AI enhances predictive models, bridging clinical and preclinical insights. A 2025 biomarker review for HCC immunotherapy combined imaging with genomic profiles, predicting ICI response with 80% accuracy via NBR1 levels in stellate cells [ ]. A 2024 study on genomic biomarkers predicted combination therapy response using transcriptomic signatures integrated with radiomics, identifying responders with HR 0.45 for OS in 200 patients [ ]. In a 2025 machine learning radiomics model, pretreatment CT integrated with clinical biomarkers predicted ICI survival, with AUC 0.92 outperforming clinical models [ ]. Preclinically, a 2020 functional imaging study integrated PET with molecular markers in HCC models, predicting anti-PD1 response with 88% accuracy based on CD8 infiltration [ ]. A 2023 radio-immunomics paper fused MRI with biomarkers, simulating TME changes with 75% predictive fidelity in animal models [ ]. Shortcomings include incomplete biomarker panels in clinical integrations, as in the 2025 review where NBR1 alone missed 25% of resistant cases due to unaddressed heterogeneity [ ]. The 2024 genomic study lacked real-time AI, leading to static predictions that failed in 20% of dynamic responses [ ]. Preclinical fusions often ignored AI, resulting in manual biases and 15% error in TME simulation [ ]. Remedies encompass comprehensive multiomics panels for full heterogeneity capture [ ], and dynamic AI algorithms for real-time updates, boosting accuracy by 15–20% [ ]. In preclinical, AI-driven simulations could reduce biases [ ]. ## Radiomics and AI predictive modeling
The potential of radiomics and AI predictive modeling in HCC immunotherapy is evident, but specific application cases are sparse compared to other cancers like brain metastases. A 2024 study constructed an AI-driven multimodal imaging integration model for HCC immunotherapy response, using deep learning (CNN) to fuse PET, MRI, and USMI data, achieving AUC 0.92 in predicting ICI efficacy across multi-center cohorts of 150 patients, with validation showing 85% accuracy in validation sets [ ]. The model involved feature extraction from each modality, followed by fusion via attention mechanisms, but limitations included small sample size (n < 200) and high computational cost, potentially restricting accessibility [ ]. Another 2025 study developed a CT-based radiomics model for ICI response, constructing features from texture analysis and validating in 180 patients with AUC 0.88, but noted limitations in handling cirrhosis-induced variability, reducing performance by 10% in fibrotic subgroups [ ]. A 2025 deep learning radiomics model for TACE + ICI integrated multimodal data, achieving AUC 0.90, but lacked robust external validation, with overfitting in small training sets [ ]. Limitations of current AI applications in HCC include data scarcity, lack of standardized construction pipelines, and insufficient integration of multimodal data, leading to 15–25% prediction errors in heterogeneous tumors [ ]. To improve prediction accuracy, AI models can integrate multimodal imaging data via fusion techniques, as in the 2024 study where combining PET/MRI enhanced accuracy by 12% through shared feature spaces [ ].

## Standardization of probes and imaging endpoints
The standardization of probes and imaging endpoints is a crucial step for the translation of molecular imaging-based immunotherapy in HCC. Currently, there is a lack of standardization in the development and use of imaging probes, which makes it difficult to compare results across different studies. For example, different tracers for imaging immune checkpoints may have different binding affinities, pharmacokinetics, and imaging characteristics, leading to inconsistent results [ ]. In HCC, PET/SPECT probes are particularly challenging to standardize due to interference from high liver background signals in cirrhosis, as a 2023 study showed 30% false positives in fibrotic livers for FDG-PET [ ]. USMI faces limitations in cirrhotic backgrounds, with acoustic distortion reducing sensitivity by 20% in deep lesions [ ]. Standardization of imaging endpoints is also necessary. There is no consensus on the optimal imaging parameters and endpoints for assessing treatment response in HCC immunotherapy. For instance, the definition of objective response in imaging may vary between studies, which can affect the interpretation of treatment efficacy. Harmonization of immune biomarker assays, including those used in imaging, is needed to ensure accurate and reproducible results. This can be achieved through the integration of laboratory-specific protocols with standard operating procedures and assay performance benchmarks [ ]. Specific solutions include novel probe designs, such as GPC3-targeted [68Ga]Ga-RAYZ-8009, which reduced background noise by 25% in a 2025 phase I trial with 92% sensitivity for HCC detection [ ], or combined multimodal imaging to calibrate signals, improving specificity in cirrhosis by 15% [ ].

## Challenges in GMP manufacturing and regulatory approval
Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) manufacturing of imaging probes and immunotherapeutic agents faces several challenges. The production of high-quality, consistent, and safe imaging probes and cell-based immunotherapies requires strict control over the manufacturing process. For example, the production of radiolabeled nanoparticles for imaging needs to ensure the stability and purity of the product, as well as compliance with regulatory requirements [ ]. Regulatory approval for new imaging-based immunotherapies is also a complex and time-consuming process. The regulatory authorities require comprehensive preclinical and clinical data to demonstrate the safety and efficacy of these new therapies. The unique nature of molecular imaging-based immunotherapies, such as the use of novel probes and the combination of imaging and immunotherapy, poses challenges for regulatory evaluation. There is a need for clear regulatory guidelines and pathways for the approval of these innovative therapies to accelerate their translation into clinical practice.

## Biological complexity and heterogeneity of immune responses
The biological complexity and heterogeneity of immune responses in HCC present significant challenges for immunotherapy and molecular imaging. The tumor microenvironment in HCC is highly complex, consisting of various cell types, cytokines, chemokines, and metabolic factors that interact in a dynamic and often unpredictable manner [ ]. The immune response can vary between patients, and even within the same patient, different tumor regions may have different immune profiles. For example, the infiltration of immune cells, such as CD8⁺ T cells, Tregs, and macrophages, can vary spatially and temporally in the tumor. The heterogeneity of immune responses can lead to inconsistent treatment responses and make it difficult to develop universal immunotherapy strategies. Understanding the underlying mechanisms of immune response heterogeneity and developing strategies to overcome it are essential for improving the effectiveness of immunotherapy in HCC. Molecular imaging can play a role in characterizing this heterogeneity, but more research is needed to fully understand the complex biological processes involved.

## Strategies for theranostic development and clinical trial integration
Theranostic development, which combines diagnosis and therapy, holds great potential for HCC treatment. In the context of immunotherapy, theranostic approaches can use molecular imaging to guide treatment selection, monitor treatment response, and adjust therapy in real-time. For example, imaging-based theranostics can help in identifying patients who are likely to respond to a particular immunotherapy regimen and in detecting early signs of treatment failure or resistance [ ]. Clinical trial integration is also crucial for the development of new immunotherapies. Clinical trials need to be designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of new theranostic-based immunotherapies. This includes the selection of appropriate patient populations, the definition of endpoints, and the integration of imaging-based biomarkers. For example, in a randomized clinical trial assessing theranostic-guided corneal cross-linking for treating keratoconus, the theranostic software module was validated to confirm its accuracy in estimating treatment efficacy [ ]. Similar approaches can be applied in HCC immunotherapy trials to improve the development and evaluation of new therapies.

## Future perspectives and roadmap
As the evidence underscores the transformative potential of molecular imaging in HCC immunotherapy, yet reveals persistent gaps in standardization, generalizability, and integration, the path forward must innovate to overcome these barriers. Future efforts should pivot toward hypothesis-driven advancements that address biological heterogeneity through cutting-edge technologies, while fostering regulatory and clinical frameworks for rapid translation. By leveraging insights from recent reviews and trials, we propose a roadmap emphasizing AI-enhanced multimodal fusion, HCC-specific probe development, and adaptive trial designs, aiming to elevate predictive accuracy from current 70–85% levels to over 90% and personalize therapies for diverse patient subgroups.

## Advancing multimodal integration with AI to tackle heterogeneity
A primary shortcoming in existing studies is the failure to fully capture HCC’s spatial and temporal heterogeneity, as seen in a 2024 radio-immunomics analysis where MRI alone misclassified 25% of immune-excluded phenotypes due to unintegrated genomic data, resulting in suboptimal ICI response prediction (AUC 0.75 in heterogeneous cohorts) [ ]. Similarly, 2025 PET/CT studies overlooked cirrhosis-induced artifacts, leading to 20% false positives in TME profiling and underestimating response in 30% of advanced cases [ ]. Innovatively, we propose AI-driven multimodal fusion platforms that integrate PET/MRI/CEUS/CT with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to create dynamic “TME maps,” resolving heterogeneity at cellular resolution. This approach, inspired by a 2025 Nature study on HCC immune microenvironments that used spatial proteomics to map single-cell phenotypes, identifying multicellular connections in NASH-HCC with 85% resolution of immune niches [ ], could predict immunotherapy efficacy by identifying novel molecular subtypes (e.g., PTM-driven evasion), potentially increasing AUC to 0.95 through real-time adaptive modeling [ ]. To implement, federated learning algorithms could train models on decentralized datasets, addressing small sample biases (e.g., < 100 patients in 70% of radiomics studies) and improving generalizability by 15–20%, as demonstrated in a 2024 computational immuno-oncology framework [ ].

## Developing HCC-specific probes and theranostics for enhanced specificity
Probe standardization deficits, such as variable pharmacokinetics causing 15–30% inconsistency in PD-L1 detection across PET studies, hinder reliable monitoring, as noted in a 2025 Frontiers review where non-HCC-specific tracers failed in 25% of cirrhotic cases due to high liver background [ ]. Preclinical models exacerbate this by ignoring human etiology, with a 2022 study showing 20% overestimation of efficacy in non-cirrhotic animals [ ]. An innovative solution is engineering HCC-tailored nanoprobes targeting glypican-3 or MTFR2-integrated immune markers, enabling single-cell imaging to dissect TME niches. This could resolve 80% of heterogeneity issues, as projected in a 2025 Springer consensus on immunotherapy, where such probes enhanced specificity to 92% in pilot models [ ]. Theranostic hybrids, combining diagnostic imaging with therapeutic delivery (e.g., ICI-loaded nanoparticles), address regulatory delays by streamlining GMP, potentially shortening approval timelines by 12 months, per a 2025 Nature signaling pathways article [ ].

## Roadmap for clinical trials and regulatory adaptation
Short follow-ups and static models in trials limit prognostic utility, with a 2025 Gastroenterology article reporting only 50% long-term accuracy in emerging therapies due to unadaptive designs missing 30% of relapses [ ]. Operator dependency in CEUS further compounds errors, with 20% variability in a 2020 WFUMB guideline validation [ ]. Our roadmap advocates basket trials incorporating real-time imaging endpoints, adapting regimens based on AI feedback loops to boost response rates by 20–25%, as envisioned in a 2025 PubMed perspective on HCC immunotherapy [ ]. Regulatory adaptation via fast-track pathways for imaging-theranostics, supported by SITC 2024 visions, could accelerate adoption [ ].
