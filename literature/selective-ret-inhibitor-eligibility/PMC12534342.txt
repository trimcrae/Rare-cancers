Larger, controlled studies and real-world evaluations are needed to confirm these findings and inform broader clinical use.

## Key Points

 

## Introduction
Lung cancer remains the Leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with an estimated 125,070 deaths in the USA alone in 2024 [ , ]. Despite significant progress in the treatment of many other cancers, lung cancer continues to pose substantial clinical challenges. A commonly cited reason for its high mortality is the prevalence of late-stage diagnosis, when curative treatment options are limited. Although ongoing efforts aim to improve early detection through enhanced screening programs, there remains a critical and urgent need for more effective therapeutic strategies.
Historically, lung cancer has been treated with a combination of surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation. Early-stage disease (Stage I–II) is typically managed with surgical resection, sometimes followed by adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy. More advanced stages (Stage III–IV) require systemic therapy, including chemotherapy and radiation, with surgery pursued selectively [ ]. Over the past two decades, the treatment landscape for both non-small cell and small cell lung cancer (NSCLC and SCLC) has evolved significantly with the advent of molecularly targeted therapies and immunotherapies. The approval of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) for tumors harboring EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements marked a major milestone in precision oncology. In parallel, the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) such as nivolumab and pembrolizumab in 2015 further transformed care, both as monotherapy and in combination with chemotherapy [ – ]. These therapies have contributed to meaningful declines in lung cancer mortality [ ]. Randomized trials have shown that ICIs improve survival over chemotherapy while offering better tolerability [ – ]. For example, pembrolizumab improved median overall survival to 12.7 months versus 8.5 months with docetaxel in PD-L1-positive NSCLC, and was associated with fewer grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events (16% versus 35%) [ ]. Despite these advances, only a subset of patients (20%) derive durable benefit from immunotherapy [ ], highlighting the need for novel strategies, particularly those that target specific molecular alterations across tumor types.
Traditionally, treatment of advanced cancers has been largely histology-based, with drug regimens developed and approved according to the tumor’s tissue of origin. However, growing insight into cancer’s molecular mechanisms has fueled a precision medicine approach, utilizing “tissue-agnostic” therapies that target specific genomic alterations regardless of tumor location. Several actionable alterations, such as microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR), tumor mutational burden-high (TMB-H), neurotrophic tropomyosin receptor kinase (NTRK) fusions, BRAF V600E mutation, rearranged during transfection (RET) fusions, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) positivity, have been identified across multiple tumor types. These genetic changes are now the basis for tissue-agnostic therapies that can be matched to tumors on the basis of their molecular profile, often with improved survival outcomes and lower systemic toxicity compared with traditional chemotherapy and radiation [ ].
Since 2017, a total of eight tissue-agnostic drugs targeting molecular alterations in solid tumors have been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA): pembrolizumab, larotrectinib, entrectinib, dostarlimab, dabrafenib plus trametinib, selpercatinib, fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki, and repotrectinib (Fig. ). These approvals were based on clinical trials conducted in biomarker-defined populations across various tumor histologies, including patients with lung cancer. The aim of this analysis was to evaluate the available evidence on the efficacy and safety of FDA-approved tissue-agnostic therapies targeting specific molecular alterations—namely dMMR/MSI-H, NTRK gene fusions, TMB-H, BRAF V600E mutations, RET fusions, and HER2 positivity—in the context of lung cancer. 

## Literature Search Methods
We conducted a structured narrative review in July 2025 to provide an up-to-date synthesis of clinical trials, FDA approvals, and post-approval studies related to tissue-agnostic therapies in patients with specific genetic alterations, including dMMR/MSI-H, NTRK gene fusions, TMB-H, BRAF V600E mutations, RET fusions, and HER2 positivity. Sources included the FDA website (fda.gov) for approval announcements and indications, ClinicalTrials.gov for pivotal and post-approval studies, PubMed for peer-reviewed trial publications and real-world studies, and the Postmarketing Requirements and Commitments (PMRs/PMCs) database to verify the current status of post-approval obligations.
Key data points extracted included the date of tissue-agnostic FDA approval, trial dates and phases, and clinical endpoints such as objective response rate (ORR), median duration of response (mDOR), median progression-free survival (mPFS), overall survival (OS), and treatment-related or treatment-emergent adverse events (TRAEs/TEAEs). When available, the most recent and comprehensive trial results were prioritized to best reflect current evidence relevant to lung cancer. For example, while pembrolizumab was approved for dMMR tumors on the basis of data from 149 adults enrolled across 5 phase II trials, we referenced an updated analysis from a larger cohort of 233 adults reported in a subsequent publication. This review focuses specifically on extracting and reporting lung-cancer-specific data from the available clinical evidence.

## Findings
Since 2017, eight different drugs have received nine FDA-approved tissue-agnostic cancer indications (Fig. ). Among the clinical trials supporting these approvals, seven have since published updated results specific to patients with lung cancer [ – ]. In addition, we identified two post-approval trials and one retrospective analysis pooling data from 12 trials. Lung cancer-specific cohorts across these studies ranged in size from 4 to 247 patients. The primary efficacy endpoint across trials was ORR, reported in seven trials that included patients with lung cancer (Fig. ) [ – ]. These trials evaluated therapies targeting NTRK gene fusion (larotrectinib, entrectinib, repotrectinib), BRAF V600E mutations (dabrafenib with trametinib), RET fusions (selpercatinib), and HER2 positivity (fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki). Post-approval trial data were available for pembrolizumab (TMB-H) and selpercatinib (RET gene fusion), while updated lung cancer-specific results for other tissue-agnostic therapies were not yet available at the time of our search. The primary safety outcome, defined as the prevalence of TRAEs/TEAEs in patients with lung cancer, was reported in four trials, with rates ranging from 10 to 59.7% [ , , , ]. ## dMMR & MSI-H: Pembrolizumab and Dostarlimab
Deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) indicates a disruption in the DNA mismatch repair system, which is responsible for correcting replication errors during cell division. When this system fails, mutations accumulate, particularly in regions of repetitive DNA sequences known as microsatellites, resulting in microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) status and an overall high tumor mutational burden [ ]. Tumors harboring dMMR or MSI-H alterations tend to generate numerous immunogenic neoantigens, increasing their visibility to the immune system [ ]. These tumors are often heavily infiltrated by immune cells, especially cytotoxic T lymphocytes, suggesting an active immune response against tumor-associated antigens [ ].
Pembrolizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the Programmed Cell Death Protein 1 (PD-1), was first approved in 2014 for metastatic melanoma. In May 2017, it received additional accelerated approval, becoming the first FDA-approved tissue-agnostic therapy. This indication was for patients with previously treated, unresectable or metastatic solid tumors characterized by MSI-H or dMMR, who had no satisfactory alternative treatment options [ ]. This approval was based on pooled data from five clinical trials: KEYNOTE-012 [ ], KEYNOTE-028 [ ], KEYNOTE-016 [ ], KEYNOTE-158 [ ], and KEYNOTE-164 [ ], comprising 149 patients in total (including only one SCLC patient) at the time of FDA review. Of these, only Keynote-158 included patients with lung cancer. The final publication of KEYNOTE-158 described a larger cohort of 233 patients, including four patients with SCLC explicitly reported. The trial also included an unspecified number of patients with lung neuroendocrine tumors [ ], though efficacy outcomes were not disaggregated by cancer type. As a result, the clinical efficacy of pembrolizumab in dMMR/MSI-H lung cancer remains unclear [ ]. Our review of the published literature and clinicaltrials.gov did not identify additional trials reporting lung cancer-specific efficacy data for this tissue-agnostic indication. Real-world evidence is also limited to case reports [ ], which describe durable responses, but no cohort-level real-world data specific to dMMR/MSI-H NSCLC were found. These findings highlight the need for further studies to evaluate the role of pembrolizumab in the treatment of dMMR/MSI-H lung cancer.
Dostarlimab, another PD-1 monoclonal antibody, was initially approved by the FDA for the treatment of adult patients with dMMR recurrent or advanced endometrial cancer. In 2021, it received supplementary accelerated approval for the treatment of advanced dMMR solid tumors, regardless of tumor origin [ ]. This tissue-agnostic approval was based on results from cohort F of the GARNET trial [ ], though it is important to note that no patients with lung cancer were included in cohort F at the time of approval [ ]. While cohort E of the same trial included patients with NSCLC, this group was not restricted to individuals with dMMR tumors [ ]. Our review of the literature and ongoing trials did not identify any additional studies evaluating dostarlimab for tissue-agnostic indications that include lung cancer. No cohort-level real-world data were found for dostarlimab in dMMR/MSI-H lung cancer.
Together with the limited data available on pembrolizumab for dMMR/MSI-H lung cancer, these findings highlight a critical gap in the literature. Additional research is needed to determine the efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors, including pembrolizumab and dostarlimab, in lung cancer harboring dMMR/MSI-H alterations. The paucity of studies in this area is likely due, in part, to the rarity of dMMR/MSI-H mutations in lung cancer. One study reported that only 1.16% of patients with NSCLC were identified as dMMR/MSI-H [ ]. Despite their low prevalence, these cancers are associated with high morbidity and mortality, highlighting the importance of continued investigation. Better understanding the role of immunotherapy in this rare molecular subset may not only improve outcomes for affected patients but also offer broader insights into lung cancer biology and the potential for precision immunotherapy.

## TMB-H: Pembrolizumab
In June 2020, pembrolizumab received another FDA approval for the treatment of adults and children with TMB-H solid tumors, defined as tumors with ≥ 10 mutations per megabase. This approval was based on results from the KEYNOTE-158 trial, which evaluated pembrolizumab’s efficacy in patients with TMB-H tumors. TMB-H tumors tend to produce more neoantigens, novel proteins that enhance tumor immunogenicity and may trigger stronger anti-tumor immune responses. KEYNOTE-158 evaluated pembrolizumab across nine distinct cancer types, including SCLC, cervical, endometrial, and anal cancers. In the SCLC cohort (n = 34), the ORR was 29% (95% CI: 15–47) [ , ]. Additional evidence of efficacy comes from a pooled retrospective analysis of 12 phase I–II trials including 158 patients with TMB-H NSCLC, where pembrolizumab achieved an ORR of 30.4% [ ]. Real-world evidence is limited. In one cohort of 142 patients treated with first-line pembrolizumab, a TMB-H subgroup (n = 53) included 11 never-smokers, all of whom responded (ORR = 100%) with median OS of 27.95 months; this group showed enrichment for DNA damage response and repair (DDR) mutations [ ]. Larger observational studies of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in NSCLC similarly suggest that higher TMB is associated with better response and survival, although most cohorts included multiple ICIs [ ]. To date, no lung-specific cohort study has focused on pembrolizumab in TMB-H disease.
The prevalence of TMB-H is notably high in lung cancers. In one study involving 2589 patients across various tumor types, 40% of SCLC and 38–42% of NSCLC cases were classified as TMB-H [ , ]. These findings highlight the potential of pembrolizumab as an effective treatment option for patients with lung cancer with high tumor mutational burden, particularly in cases where standard therapies are limited or ineffective.

## NTRK Fusions: Larotrectinib, Entrectinib, Repotrectinib
Neurotrophic tyrosine receptor kinase (NTRK) genes encode the tropomyosin receptor kinase (TRK) family of proteins, which are involved in critical signaling pathways regulating cell growth, differentiation, and survival. Gene fusions involving NTRK1, NTRK2, or NTRK3 can lead to constitutive TRK activation, contributing to the development and progression of a wide range of solid tumors [ ].
Larotrectinib is a highly selective TRK inhibitor that received accelerated FDA approval in November 2018 for the treatment of advanced solid tumors harboring NTRK gene fusions. This approval was supported by results from three clinical trials—LOXO-TRK-14001 [NCT02122913], SCOUT [NCT02637687], and NAVIGATE [NCT02576431] [ , , ]—which enrolled a total of 55 adult and pediatric patients. Among these, only four patients with lung cancer (tumor histology unspecified) were included [ , ]. A subsequent 2022 pooled analysis of 20 patients with NTRK fusion-positive lung cancer from trials NCT02576431 and NCT02122913 demonstrated durable clinical responses to larotrectinib. Among the 15 evaluable patients, the ORR was 73% [ ]. The median PFS was 35.4 months (95% CI: 5.3–35.4) and median OS was 40.7 months (95% CI: 17.2–not reached) [ ]. TRAEs of grade ≥ 3 occurred in only 10% of patients [ ]. Real-world evidence is emerging. A Korean multicenter retrospective study including patients with lung cancer reported lower clinical efficacy for larotrectinib compared to phase I/II trials, potentially owing to false-positive or non-canonical NTRK fusions and co-existing driver mutations [ ]. However, the abstract did not report lung-specific ORR or survival outcomes [ ]. A prospective post-approval ON-TRK study is ongoing and includes a lung cancer cohort [ ].
Entrectinib is a multikinase inhibitor targeting TRK, ROS1, and ALK, which received accelerated FDA approval in 2019 for the treatment of advanced NTRK fusion-positive solid tumors. This approval was based on a pooled analysis of three phase 1 and 2 clinical trials: ALKA-372-001, STARTRK-1 and STARTRK-2 [ , ]. Entrectinib demonstrated durable responses across 10 NTRK fusion-positive tumor types, including NSCLC, sarcoma, mammary analogue secretory carcinoma, breast, thyroid, and colorectal cancers. In the initial analysis, only 10 patients with NSCLC were included [ , ]. A 2020 update expanded the cohort to 22 patients with NSCLC, revealing an ORR of 63.6% (95% CI: 40.7–82.8), a median DOR of 19.9 months (95% CI: 10.4–29.4), and a median OS that was not reached (95% CI: 20.8–not reached) [ ]. However, lung cancer-specific safety data were not reported. Real-world evidence remains limited. A multicenter real-world study from Turkey, which included patients with lung cancer, reported an ORR of 35.3% (95% CI: 14.2–62.7) with a median OS of 20.8 months (95% CI: 0–48.5), but lung-specific outcomes were not disaggregated [ ]. Similarly, a US Veterans Health Administration study identified 33 patients with NTRK fusions or rearrangements across tumor types; of the four patients treated with entrectinib, none achieved an objective response, including those with NSCLC [ ].
Repotrectinib is a next-generation TRK inhibitor designed to overcome resistance to earlier TRK inhibitors. Initially approved for ROS1-positive NSCLC, it received supplementary accelerated FDA approval on June 13, 2024, for the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic solid tumors with NTRK gene fusions [ , ]. This approval was based on the TRIDENT-1 trial (a multicenter, single-arm, open-label, multi-cohort trial) that assessed repotrectinib in both TRK inhibitor-naïve and TRK inhibitor-pretreated patients [ , ]. Among 21 patients with NSCLC included in the lung-cancer-specific cohorts, TRK inhibitor-naïve patients had an ORR of 62% (95% CI: 43–86), a 12-month DOR of 92% (95% CI: 76–100) and 12-month PFS of 64% (95% CI: 43–86). In TRK inhibitor-pretreated patients with NSCLC, the ORR was 42% (95% CI: 18–71), with a 12-month DOR of 44% (95% CI: 1–88) and 12-month PFS of 23% (95% CI: 0–49) [ , ]. However, Lung cancer-specific safety data were not reported. Real-world evidence remains limited. Only case-level data have been published, including one report of a patient with atypical carcinoid of the Lung achieving a sustained partial response lasting over 10 months [ ]. To date, no cohort-level real-world data are available for repotrectinib in NTRK fusion-positive lung cancer.
Despite these promising outcomes, NTRK fusions are rare in solid tumors, occurring in approximately 1% of all cancers [ ]. In NSCLC, the estimated prevalence of NTRK1/2/3 rearrangements is 1–2% [ , ]. Consequently, while the overall utility of NTRK-targeted therapies in lung cancer is limited by their low incidence, early studies indicate that patients with NTRK fusion-positive lung cancer may derive meaningful benefit from TRK inhibitor therapy. These targeted agents have shown favorable efficacy in appropriately selected patients, particularly when compared to outcomes reported with standard chemotherapy. While early safety profiles appear encouraging, the lack of lung cancer-specific safety data highlights the need for further investigation in this patient population.

## BRAF V600E Mutations: Dabrafenib plus Trametinib
BRAF V600E is a constitutively active mutation of the BRAF kinase that drives unregulated cell proliferation and differentiation, independent of upstream RAS activation. Initially targeted in melanoma, BRAF V600E has since been identified in multiple tumor types, including colorectal cancer (CRC), thyroid cancer, glioma, and NSCLC [ ]. However, responses to BRAF inhibition vary across cancers, reflecting the complexity of tumor biology. For example, while BRAF inhibitors produced strong responses in melanoma, their efficacy in CRC was limited, despite the same underlying mutation, due to compensatory EGFR activation [ ]. This discovery informed the development of combination therapies targeting both BRAF V600E and EGFR [ ].
The broader success of combination therapy led to the adoption of dual BRAF and MEK inhibition, targeting both upstream and downstream components of the MAPK pathway. The combination of dabrafenib (BRAF inhibitor) and trametinib (MEK inhibitor) demonstrated synergistic antitumor activity, an acceptable safety profile, and efficacy across multiple tumor types, including lung cancer [ ]. In 2017, this combination was approved for the treatment of metastatic NSCLC with BRAF V600E mutations [ ]. In 2022, this regimen received supplementary accelerated FDA approval for adult and pediatric patients (≥ 6 years) with unresectable or metastatic BRAF V600E mutant solid tumors who had progressed on prior therapies and had no satisfactory treatment alternatives [ ]. This approval was based on six clinical trials: BRF117019, CTMT212X2101, NCI-MATCH, COMBI-d, COMBI-v, and BRF113928 [ ]. Lung cancer-specific data came from cohorts B and C of the BRF113928 trial, which included 57 previously treated and 36 treatment-naïve patients with BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic NSCLC [ ]. A follow-up analysis with at Least 5-years of data showed that, in previously treated patients (cohort B, n = 57), the ORR was 68.4% (95% CI: 54.8–80.1), median PFS was 10.2 months (95% CI: 6.9–16.7), and median OS was 18.2 months (95% CI: 14.3–28.6) [ ]. In treatment-naïve patients (cohort C, n = 36), the ORR was 63.9% (95% CI: 46.2–79.2), median PFS 10.8 months (95% CI: 7.0–14.5), and median OS was 17.3 months (95% CI: 12.3–40.2) [ ]. Lung cancer-specific safety data were not reported. Real-world evidence supports the clinical effectiveness of dabrafenib plus trametinib in BRAF V600E–mutated lung cancer. In a French multicenter real-world cohort of 40 patients, median PFS was 17.5 months (95% CI: 7.1–23.0) and median OS was 25.5 months (95% CI: 16.6-not reached), with similar outcomes observed in both first-line and later-line settings [ ]. Findings from the IFCT-2004 BLaDE cohort also support effectiveness in routine practice: among patients with NSCLC receiving this combination as first-line therapy, the median PFS was 18.2 months (95% CI: 7.7–21.3) and median OS was 24.1 months (95% CI: 12.3–37.9) [ ]. Additionally, a multicenter comparative study showed superior outcomes with dabrafenib plus trametinib versus chemotherapy in the first-line setting, with higher ORR (67% versus 39%, p = 0.02) and longer median PFS (13.1 versus 6.1 months, p = 0.007) [ ].
As with other molecular alterations discussed in this review, BRAF V600E mutations are relatively rare in lung cancer, occurring in only 1–5% of cases [ ]. However, this subset is associated with particularly poor prognosis when treated with traditional chemotherapy, making the development and validation of targeted therapies such as dabrafenib plus trametinib especially important for improving outcomes in this population.

## RET Fusions: Selpercatinib
The RET gene encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase that plays a critical role in activating downstream RAS/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways. Owing to its regulatory function in cellular proliferation, oncogenic RET fusions, commonly found in thyroid carcinoma and NSCLC, can drive uncontrolled cell growth and cancer progression [ ].
Selpercatinib, a selective RET kinase inhibitor, was initially approved by the FDA in 2020 for the treatment of RET fusion-positive NSCLC. It received supplementary accelerated approval in 2022 for locally advanced or metastatic RET fusion-positive solid tumors in adults [ ]. This approval was based on data from the LIBRETTO-001 trial, which included 41 patients with RET fusion-positive tumors outside of NSCLC and thyroid cancer, and 343 patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC and thyroid cancer [ ]. An updated analysis of LIBRETTO-001 reported results from 69 previously untreated and 247 previously treated patients with NSCLC. In previously untreated patients, the ORR was 83% (95% CI: 72–91), median PFS was 22.0 months (95% CI: 16.5–24.9), and median OS was not reached (95% CI: 37.8-not reached) [ ]. In previously treated patients, the ORR was 62% (95% CI: 55–68), median PFS was 26.2 months (95% CI: 19.3–35.7), and median OS was 47.6 months (95% CI: 35.9–not reached) [ ].
A similar trial conducted in a Chinese population, LIBRETTO-321, assessed selpercatinib in patients with advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC [ ]. In the primary analysis of 47 patients with centrally confirmed RET fusions, the ORR was 69.2% (95% CI: 48.2–85.7) after a median follow-up of 9.7 months [ ]. A third study directly compared selpercatinib with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, a standard-of-care regimen, in patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC [ ]. Among 212 patients, selpercatinib demonstrated a significantly higher ORR of 84% (95% CI: 76–90) versus 65% (95% CI: 54–75) in the control group (p < 0.001). The median PFS was 24.8 months (95% CI: 16.9–not reached) with selpercatinib, compared to 11.2 months (95% CI: 8.8–16.8) with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy. The hazard ratio for progression or death was 0.46 (95% CI: 0.31–0.70, p < 0.001) [ ]. However, the rate of grade ≥ 3 AEs (regardless of treatment attribution) was higher in the selpercatinib group (70%) compared with 57% in the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group. While not necessarily treatment-related, this difference highlights the need for continued safety monitoring and individualized treatment planning. Real-world evidence is also available. A study using two US electronic health record databases reported that, in patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC treated with selpercatinib, the median real-world time to treatment discontinuation or death ranged from 12.1 to 22.4 months. The median time to next treatment or death ranged from 16.2 to 21.0 months, which are largely consistent with those observed in clinical trials [ ].
As with many other targetable mutations, RET fusions are relatively rare, occurring in approximately 1–2% of NSCLC cases [ ]. Although this low prevalence limits its applicability to a small subset of patients, selpercatinib has shown consistently favorable efficacy and survival outcomes in this group. These results suggest that selpercatinib may be a valuable therapeutic option for RET fusion-positive patients with NSCLC who have historically had limited targeted treatment options.

## HER2-Positive Solid Tumors
Fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki is an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) initially approved in 2019 for unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer. In April 2024, it received supplementary accelerated FDA approval for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive solid tumors who have received prior systemic therapy and have no satisfactory alternative treatment options [ ]. This approval was supported by data from several clinical trials, including DESTINY-PanTumor02 [ ], DESTINY-LUNG01 [ ], and DESTINY-CRC02 [ ]. The FDA’s Oncology Center of Excellence coordinated the review through Project Orbis, enabling simultaneous evaluation alongside regulators from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration, Brazil’s Health Regulatory Agency, Health Canada, and Singapore’s Health Sciences Authority.
Notably, the DESTINY-LUNG01 study evaluated fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki in patients with HER2-overexpressing or HER2-mutant NSCLC. This multicenter, open-label, phase 2 trial tested two dosing regimens: 5.4 mg/kg or 6.4 mg/kg. In the 5.4 mg/kg cohort (n = 41), the ORR was 34.1% (95% CI: 20.1–50.6), with a median PFS of 6.7 months (95% CI: 4.2–8.4) and median OS of 11.2 months (95% CI: 8.4–not reached) [ ]. The 6.4 mg/kg cohort (n = 49) showed an ORR of 26.5% (95% CI: 15.0–41.1), median PFS of 5.7 months (95% CI: 2.8–7.2) and median OS of 12.4 months (95% CI: 7.8–17.2) [ ]. However, the higher dose group experienced a greater incidence of grade 3 or higher TRAEs (53%), compared with 22% in the lower dose cohort (22%) [ ]. This difference was linked to more frequent treatment discontinuations, dose reductions and interruptions in the 6.4 mg/kg group. HER2 overexpression occurs in approximately 8–23% of NSCLC cases [ ]. The observed response rates in DESTINY-LUNG01 suggest that fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki is a promising targeted therapy option for HER2-positive patients with NSCLC. As one of the newest FDA-approved tissue-agnostic drugs, further research and post-approval studies are needed to better understand its efficacy and safety across different cancer types. No cohort-level real-world data have been reported for this drug in HER2-positive lung cancer.

## Conclusions
Since 2017, tissue agnostic drugs have emerged as promising treatment options across multiple cancer types, including lung cancer. Although indications for these therapies continue to expand, the degree of clinical benefit varies depending on factors such as tumor histology, molecular context, and overall mutational burden. Moreover, many targeted immunotherapies are associated with a distinct set of immune-related adverse effects (irAEs), which differ significantly from the cytotoxic effects of traditional chemotherapies. These irAEs require careful monitoring and prompt management to avoid serious treatment-related complications [ – ].
Of the eight tissue-agnostic drugs approved in the USA (across nine indications), all received accelerated approval, a regulatory mechanism designed to provide earlier access to innovative therapies for patients with advanced or metastatic disease who have exhausted standard treatment options. However, the clinical trials supporting these approvals were generally small in size, and in several cases, included no participants with mutation-positive lung cancer or other organ-specific tumors. Most trials relied on ORR as the primary efficacy endpoint. Several did not achieve a statistically significant improvement in OS at the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for lung cancer, likely due to limited statistical power. Since the majority of these approvals were based on surrogate outcomes such as ORR or PFS, the observed activity should be regarded as supportive rather than definitive for OS benefit.
To expedite access, regulators increasingly accept surrogate endpoints such as PFS and ORR [ ], even though these measures often lack a consistent and validated correlation with patient-relevant outcomes such as survival or quality of life [ , ]. Among 223 cancer drug indications approved by the FDA between 2003 and 2021, fewer than half (n = 95) included OS as an endpoint. Of those granted accelerated approval with immature OS data, subsequent follow-up showed a significant survival benefit in fewer than one-third [ ]. Furthermore, most tissue-agnostic approvals were based on single-arm phase 1 or 2 trials, which lack comparator arms and may overestimate treatment effects. Meta-analytic evidence suggests that absolute survival gains are generally modest, and reliance on single-arm or placebo-controlled designs can inflate efficacy estimates relative to active comparators [ ]. Therefore, there are important uncertainties in generalizing these findings to broader, more diverse populations with the same molecular alterations.
Although these therapies are considered “tissue agnostic,” emerging evidence suggests that tumor histology and microenvironment can influence therapeutic efficacy. For example, BRAF V600E inhibitors show markedly different outcomes in melanoma versus CRC, despite targeting the same mutation. To address these limitations, the FDA may impose PMRs and PMCs to confirm efficacy and safety across multiple tumor types. A search of the FDA Postmarketing Requirements and Commitments database identified 23 PMRs/PMCs linked to the nine tissue-agnostic indications. To date, three PMRs/PMCs have been fulfilled and two submitted, while the remainder are either ongoing or pending. Notably, none of the fulfilled or ongoing trials were found to specifically include lung cancer as a targeted enrollment group.
Our review identified only one phase III trial, not required under FDA PMR/PMC, that compared a tissue-agnostic therapy with standard-of-care [ ]. This study, evaluating selpercatinib in RET fusion-positive tumors, showed improved response rates and PFS in the NSCLC subset. However, it also reported a 70% incidence of grade ≥ 3 general adverse events (not necessarily treatment-related) [ ].
Among the eight FDA-approved tissue-agnostic drugs, only 10 trials reported efficacy data specific to biomarker-defined lung cancer subgroups (excluding post-approval or other trials), with sample sizes ranging from 4 to 247 patients per trial-cohort. Within these limited results, our review suggests that tissue-agnostic therapies targeting NTRK gene fusions (larotrectinib, entrectinib, repotrectinib), BRAF V600E mutation (dabrafenib plus trametinib) and RET fusions (selpercatinib) have shown encouraging response rates among patients with tumors previously considered untreatable, with preliminary PFS and OS benefits. Despite this, only four trials reported lung cancer-specific safety data [ , , , ]. Reported rates of treatment-related/emergent adverse events ranged from 10 to 59.7%, based on data from larotrectinib (NTRK gene fusions), selpercatinib (RET gene fusion), and fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki (HER2-overexpression) [ , , , ]. Real-world evidence is limited, highlighting the need for broader studies to evaluate both effectiveness and safety in more heterogeneous lung cancer populations outside of trial settings. In addition, acquired resistance to targeted therapies is an emerging concern, with resistance mechanisms such as secondary mutations or activation of bypass signaling pathways already documented [ ]. These findings highlight the importance of ongoing molecular monitoring and the development of combination or sequential treatment strategies to improve durability of response.
Despite strong recommendations from major guidelines, such as those from the European Society for Medical Oncology and the US National Comprehensive Cancer Network, for routine molecular testing [ ], disparities in access to next-generation sequencing may hinder the timely and consistent identification of actionable genomic alterations. Furthermore, the absence of universal standardization in testing methodologies, interpretation criteria, and reporting practices contributes to variability in patient eligibility for tissue-agnostic therapies across institutions and regions. While the prevalence of many targetable alterations in lung cancer is low, their cumulative clinical impact is substantial, particularly in lung cancer, which remains the leading cause of cancer-related death in many countries, including the USA [ ]. However, access to tissue-agnostic therapies is further complicated by the high costs associated with both comprehensive molecular profiling and the targeted treatments themselves. These financial barriers are especially pronounced in low- and middle-income countries and may exacerbate existing disparities in cancer care and outcomes.
In summary, on the basis of currently available evidence, FDA-approved tissue-agnostic therapies show promise for the treatment of patients with lung cancer with NTRK gene fusions (larotrectinib, entrectinib, repotrectinib), BRAF V600E mutations (dabrafenib plus trametinib) and RET fusions (selpercatinib). However, larger clinical trials and real-world studies are needed to further validate the efficacy and safety of these therapies specifically in the lung cancer population.
