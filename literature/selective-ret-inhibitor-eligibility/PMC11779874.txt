Mutations in the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene are frequently found in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Since curcumin and CB[2]UN support various medicinal applications in drug delivery and design, we investigated the effect of curcumin and CB[2]UN-based drugs in controlling EGFR-mutant NSCLC through a dodecagonal computational approach. Molecular docking studies revealed that the ligands curcumin (-6.9 kcal/mol) and CB[2]UN (-8.1 kcal/mol) bound more strongly to the EGFR-mutant NSCLC proteins with 2ITX and 2ITV, respectively. Molecular dynamics simulation (50 ns) investigation of protein-ligand complexes using RMSD, RMSF, Rg, and SASA indicated that curcumin and CB[2]UN with EGFR-mutant proteins are kinetically stable. In addition, MMPBSA/MMGBSA analysis confirmed the thermodynamic stability of each curcumin and CB[2]UN protein-ligand complex. Finally, KDeep absolute binding affinity calculations show energies of -6.13 kcal/mol and − 5.26 kcal/mol for 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN protein-ligand complexes, respectively. Thus, our dodecagonal strategy reveals that 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN are more likely to form protein-ligand complexes with more significant binding affinities and excellent stability throughout the 50 ns simulation time.

## Introduction
Cancer is a fatal disease that causes a significant number of deaths worldwide each year . Lung cancer is the second most common cancer in the world according to diagnosis rates. Approximately 85% of all cases of lung cancer are non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which continues to be the primary cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide . NSCLC oncogenic driver mutations have led to the development of medicines that can disrupt intracellular signaling pathways involved in carcinogenesis. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, for instance erlotinib or gefitinib, have supported favorable outcomes in those patients with enhanced NSCLC that hold EGFR mutations . EGFR signal transduction pathways support the growth of malignancies through several mechanisms, including impacts on cell cycle progression, tumor cell motility, suppression of apoptosis, angiogenesis, and metastasis – . The frequently utilized surgical resection procedures for lung cancer include high risk, recurrence, and complexity of the surgery .
In recent years, scientists have identified several chemical compounds that may be beneficial for lung cancer treatment. Curcumin (CUR), a naturally occurring substance produced from turmeric, has been demonstrated to have anti-tumor properties both in vitro and in vivo , . CUR has been reported as a potent anticancer candidate for chemotherapy due to its notable cytotoxic activity and apoptosis initiation capability against various human cancer cell lines . CUR has been demonstrated to suppress lung cancer cell growth and trigger apoptosis by altering the mitochondrial and lysosomal pathways dependent on reactive oxygen species (ROS) . Recently, Rudnik et al. have reported Co-loaded CUR and methotrexate nanocapsules with improved cytotoxicity against NSCLC cells, and Liang et al. evaluated both in vitro and in silico analysis of curcumin derivatives for their ability to target EGFR , . Jibrin et al. studied the in silico design, profiling, molecular docking computation, and drug kinetic model assessment of novel curcumin derivatives as potential anticancer agents . Fathima et al. reported the synthesis, structure determination, optical, molecular interaction, thermal, and anticancer activity studies, pharmacokinetic assessment, and in silico analysis of a monocarbonyl curcumin derivative . Maboudian et al. evaluated the effects of curcumin on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease using a bio-computational approach . However, they have not reported the thermodynamic and kinetic stability of the protein and ligand interaction. Another molecule consisting of cucurbit[n]uril homologues (CB[n], n = 5–10) is an acid-catalyzed condensation product of glycoluril and formaldehyde . Recent literature report reveals that study on CB[n]s have been rapidly growing which support various medicinal applications involving drug design, drug delivery, controlled drug release, biomolecular recognition, molecular machinery, and bioanalytical sensing – . Moreover, CB[n]s hold promises as being extremely biocompatible and non-toxic , .
Computational drug design is a powerful approach that combines chemistry, biology, informatics, and computational modelling to accelerate the discovery and development of new drugs. Hasanzade et al. reported mixed quantum mechanical/molecular dynamics simulations on the molecular mechanism for the encapsulation of the doxorubicin in the CB[n]s cavity and the impacts of diameter and protonation on loading and releasing of the anticancer drug . Ahmadian et al. reported molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation that offers molecular insight into the interaction between camptothecin and acyclic CB[4]s as useful nanocontainers in comparison with cucurbit[7]uril . Pandya et al. interpreted the nature of interactions in the inclusion complex of danofloxacin, a third-generation fluoroquinolone with cucurbit[7]uril, Ramalakshmi et al. studied the in silico approach of the effect of protein on the complexation of cucurbit[7]uril with N-(ferrocenylmethyl)aniline, and de Lima et al. reported the in silico study of the encapsulation of α-tocopherol and α-tocotrienol vitamins E into cucurbit[7]uril – . Singh et al. utilized, in silico techniques to identify a potentially selective inhibitor of Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 2 (CDK2). The thermodynamic study showed that the newly discovered selective ligand has a higher inhibitory potential against CDK2, which supports its development as a new CDK2 selective inhibitor . Bhardwaj et al. investigated the use of structural modification and computational treatments to alleviate the bitter taste associated with stevioside, a natural, non-caloric sweetener derived from the Stevia rebaudiana plant. By integrating experimental and computational methodologies, the study highlights the effectiveness of structure-based medication design in tackling taste-related issues . Singh et al. investigated the efficacy of natural compounds obtained from medicinal plants in treating bedaquiline-resistant tuberculosis (TB). Bedaquiline is a potent TB medication; however, its efficiency is declining due to the rise of resistant strains. In vitro studies demonstrated the potential anti-TB activity of specific natural compounds. The article recognized the challenges in developing natural compounds into effective treatments for tuberculosis, highlighting issues of standardization, toxicity, and bioavailability .
Biological processes, ranging from essential cellular functions to advanced physiological systems, result from intricate and finely regulated molecular interactions. A deep understanding of molecular interactions and their dependence on cellular and molecular structures forms the foundation of modern biology. A receptor molecule (frequently a protein) and ligand molecule interact specifically and selectively in a process known as molecular recognition. This recognition mechanism is crucial to numerous biological and chemical processes in living organisms and has significant applications in several disciplines, including materials science, biochemistry, and drug discovery . In computational drug design, it is crucial to understand how ligands bind to their targets – . Several software tools and packages are available for conducting molecular interaction research, including protein-ligand docking through protein-protein interaction analysis and beyond, such as AutoDock Vina, Maestro, Glide, etc . The most crucial step in molecular docking is choosing a ligand and a target molecule. Functional relevant mutations have been identified by gene enrichment through machine-learning approaches . The ToppGene suite is primarily designed for gene function prediction and prioritization based on their potential functional relevance . Hence, the identified genes after functional enrichment will be further subjected to a web-based networking platform such as STRING that facilitates the analysis of protein-protein interactions (PPIs) and functional associations between proteins. However, STITCH emphasizes the interaction between drugs and proteins , . The ligands and targets attained from the protein-drug interaction network (PDIN) after docking must be further supported by molecular dynamic simulations to evaluate the kinetic stability of the protein-ligand complexes . Additionally, the thermodynamic stability of protein-ligand complexes was determined by executing free energy estimation through Molecular Mechanics Poisson-Boltzmann Surface Area (MMPBSA) and molecular mechanics generalized born surface area (MMGBSA) studies. The free energy of protein-ligand interactions can be assessed through MMPBSA and MMGBSA techniques based on molecular dynamic simulations of the protein-ligand complex , .
Computer-aided drug design has proven effective and efficient for the identification of novel drug-like candidates against certain diseases. The present study utilized PPI, PDI network, and other computational methods to achieve this objective. Subsequently, the 2ITU, 2ITV, and 2ITX proteins were tested for analysis. Theoretically, all the NSCLC target proteins selected for this study had a half-life of 30 h. However, the effective half-life period of lung cancer protein is approximately 10 h . Hence, the real-time effective activity period is 2.5 times the half-life period of the protein, which is approximately 25 h. Therefore, we performed the molecular dynamic simulation studies for a simulation time of 50 ns. Thus, this manuscript emphasizes the effects of curcumin and CB[2]UN-based drugs in controlling EGFR kinase mutant NSCLC through molecular docking, ADMET, evaluation through molecular dynamic (MD) simulation, MMPBSA, MMGBSA analysis, and KDeep absolute binding affinity through a dodecagonal in silico computational approach. Our computational results confirmed that 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN were more likely to form protein-ligand complexes with more significant binding affinities and excellent stability throughout the 50 ns simulation time.

## Gene enrichment
Initially, 44,417 genes/mutations have been chosen which related to NSCLC. Subsequently, the top 20 genes, EGFR, KRAS, TP53, LRP1B, PIK3CA, STK11, BRAF, MET, KEAP1, RB1, KMT2D, ARID1A, NF1, KMT2C, ERBB2, FAT1, ALK, SMARCA4, ATM, and CDKN2A, were selected from the COSMIC database. On subjecting the top 20 genes to ranking based on functional resemblance using toppgene, they were recognized as having an average score value of 0.54 and above. This can provide insights into the biological processes or functions associated with the prioritized genes. Of these genes, 13 were utilized to construct protein-protein interaction network (PPIN) through bio-molecular networking, as depicted in Fig.  a. Of the selected genes, ERBB2, EGFR, PIK3CA, ALK, ATM, BRAF, CDKN2A, FAT1, KRAS, MET, NF1, RB1, and TP53 were found to be involved in the major mechanisms associated with NSCLC and are functionally relevant. We recognized that ERBB2 and PIK3CA had similar interactions with EGFR, with an average score of 0.999. Therefore, these genes were included in our study.

 

## Identification of drugs
Immune checkpoint inhibitors have become a significant advancement in NSCLC treatment. Essential drugs like Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab, and Atezolizumab are widely used in the controlling of lung cancer. Targeted therapies are used for patients with NSCLC with specific genetic mutations or alterations. EGFR inhibitors are one among them which can be used for EGFR-mutated NSCLC. These drugs target mutated EGFR proteins, inhibiting their activity and signalling pathways driving cancer growth. A PDIN (Fig.  b) of approved anti-NSCLC drugs showed that Afatinib, Crizotinib, Dacomitinib, Curcumin, Docetaxel, Gefitinib, Gemcitabine, Pembrolizumab, Paclitaxel, Doxorubicin, Atezolizumab, and Nivolumab had the highest average scores of 0.8 and above. Dacomitinib and Gefitinib exhibited the highest scores (approximately 0.999). All drugs included in the analysis were found to interact with the EGFR target gene.

## Molecular docking
Molecular docking analysis was performed using AMDOCK software. In docking studies, we identified protein-ligand interactions to determine whether the ligand interacts with the target in the required manner and whether it will be retained there for a longer time or sufficient time for drug action. We need to see how the structure will be different from the protein-ligand complex that has been obtained by docking. The Gibbs free energy (kcal/mol) implies complex stability and an equilibrium state in the protein-ligand binding process. In this study, molecular docking results displayed that CUR and CB[2]UN tend to bind to 2ITX and 2ITV target proteins with the lowest binding energy of -6.9 and − 8.1 kcal/mol, respectively (Table  ). Binding affinities measure the strength of molecular interactions. Higher negative values indicate stronger binding. Curcumin (-6.9 kcal/mol) and CB[2]UN (-8.1 kcal/mol) demonstrate notable binding affinities. Considering target specificity, kinetic, and thermodynamic factors enhances our understanding of the biological relevance of binding affinities and their implications for drug discovery and development.

## Molecular dynamic simulation
MD simulation was employed to calculate the ligand-target complexes. In the 2ITU-CUR complex (Fig. S2), Pi-alkyl interactions with Arg184 and Ile173 are observed within 10–30 ns of MD simulation. The Pi-alkyl interaction with Arg184 persisted for 40–50 ns during the simulation. The interactions concerning the π-electron cloud in the aromatic rings of CUR and the alkyl side chains of Arg184 and Ile173 are inspired by van der Waals and dispersion forces. Furthermore, there is a C–H bonding interaction with Arg184 seen from 20 to 30 ns. This could be attributed to the weak yet significant C-H…π interactions between CUR and Arg184. They are caused by van der Waals forces, a small positive charge on C-H bond, and the polarizability of aromatic ring. These interactions influence the overall binding affinity and specificity of the protein-ligand complex. Additionally, the interactions of alkyl groups with Arg184 and Ile173 during CUR binding are mostly influenced by van der Waals forces and the hydrophobic effect. In the context of CUR and the 2ITU protein residues, the alkyl groups present on the amino acid side chains (Arg184 and Ile173) are capable of interacting with the hydrophobic regions of CUR, comprising the methoxy groups and the aromatic rings. These forces involved to play a crucial role in ensuring the stability and specificity of the protein-ligand complex. Furthermore, the interactions between curcumin’s aromatic ring and Met176 involving Pi-sulfur are influenced by the polarizability of sulfur, along with weak electrostatic interactions and van der Waals forces. These interactions improve binding affinity and specificity.
Following the MD simulation of the ligand-target complex 2ITV-CUR (Fig. S3), Lys255 formed one Pi-cation and alkyl interaction within 40–50 ns. The complex is maintained by a Pi-cation interaction between the aromatic rings of CUR and the positive charge of Lys255. Van der Waals forces facilitate alkyl interactions, which promote optimal ligand positioning and hydrophobic effects. These interactions coordinate to enhance binding specificity and affinity. Additionally, between 40 and 50 ns, a Pi-Pi stacked bonding interaction with His188 was sustained. Pi-Pi stacking interactions between the aromatic rings of CUR and the aromatic ring system of His188 improve the stability and selectivity of the protein-ligand complex. The interactions yield a strong binding affinity, driven by van der Waals and electrostatic forces.
The results for the 2ITX-CUR complex (Fig. S4) indicate that the Asp74 and Pro76 residues established conserved H-bonding and Pi-alkyl interactions during the 10–50 ns MD simulation. The carboxylic acid group of Asp74 forms H-bonds with the -OH group of CUR, thus stabilizing the compound. The nonpolar side chain of Pro76 interacts with the aromatic rings of CUR through Pi-alkyl interactions, which make the rings less water-repellent. These interactions enhance binding affinity, specificity, and the overall stability of the complex.
The protein-ligand complex 2ITU-CB[2]UN (Fig. S5) maintained one C-H bond with Leu167 for 30–40 ns. The C-H bonds in Leu167 engage with oxygen atoms in CB[2]UN through weak C-H···O H-bonding. The overall binding affinity and specificity are stronger because of the polarizability of C-H bond, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals forces. Ala169 and Leu167 subsequently maintained an alkyl interaction for 30–50 ns. The alkyl interactions between Ala169 and Leu167 and CB[2]UN mostly account for hydrophobic effects and van der Waals forces.
In the 2ITV-CB[2]UN complex (Fig. S6), there is one alkyl interaction with the Val172 residue that remains consistent after 30–50 ns of simulation. The alkyl interactions of Val172 with CB[2]UN play a significant role in hydrophobic effects and van der Waals forces.
After 20–30 ns of simulation, the 2ITX-CB[2]UN complex (Fig. S7) maintained the H-bond interaction with Glu285 and one alkyl interaction with Arg286. The H-bonding between Glu285 and the carbonyl groups of cucurbituril significantly enhances binding affinity and specificity. Interactions between alkyl groups and Arg286 enhance hydrophobic effects, thereby providing additional stability to the protein-ligand complex. These interactions enhance the stability and selectivity of 2ITX-CB[2]UN complex. Additionally, detailed visualizations and elucidations of the protein-ligand interactions during the 50 ns simulation time are available in the supporting information.
The stability of protein-ligand complexes is frequently investigated using a 50 ns MD simulation. A 50 ns simulation may typically be sufficient to assess the stability of molecular complexes, depending on the system under study and the analyses, such as RMSD, RMSF, and Rg. RMSD measurements over 50 ns can reveal whether the substance achieves a stable conformation over time. If RMSD values remain stable throughout this duration, it implies that the complex has achieved stability and is not undergoing substantial structural changes, making more simulation time less necessary for stability evaluation. This method illustrates essential motions, including protein flexibility and ligand binding, facilitating the evaluation of complex stability through RMSD, hydrogen bonds, and additional interactions such as Pi-alkyl, C-H, and Pi-Pi. Additionally, we optimized the MD simulation duration to 50 ns for protein-ligand complexes, as indicated in published reports .

## MD simulation of 2ITU-CUR complex
The RMSD is the root-mean-square deviation. It is used to calculate the difference between the backbones of a protein from its primary structural conformation to its ultimate position. The RMSD versus time plot of 2ITU-CUR is shown in Fig.  a. A lower RMSD implies a greater stability of the 2ITU-CUR complex. Initially, a simulation time of less than 10 ns exhibited more variation. This may be due to the lower initial measurement of protein stability. From this plot, it is clear that there is not much variation in the protein structure after 10 ns of simulation time. Therefore, RMSD simulation confirmed that the protein maintained overall stability after 10 ns of simulation time. RMSF investigated the flexibility of residues in 2ITU. Figure  b shows the RMSF values of the 2ITU-CUR complex. The protein regions demonstrating higher levels of flexibility were determined. Initially, it was observed that a few residues fluctuated due to the initial perturbation associated with the start of the MD simulation, where the potential energy (PE) was partially converted to kinetic energy (KE). Residues corresponding to a few amino acids that experience unusual excitation associated with MD simulations are residue numbers close to 30 (Thr), 40 (Gly), 60 (Ala), 90 (Thr), 170 (Glu), 220 (Ser), 250 (Ala), and 280 (Glu). The stability of the MD simulation is a measure of the dynamic or KE stabilization. Therefore, the protein interaction with the ligand in the 2ITU-CUR complex was stable throughout the simulation time of 50 ns. The radius of gyration is a direct indicator of the stability of protein molecules over time. The radius of gyration versus time graph of 2ITU-CUR is shown in Fig.  c. This provides information on how a protein, or different amino acids move towards the center of the axis. We can see whether, at any simulation time up to 50 ns, the protein will be destabilized or stabilized to a large extent. This is a clear picture of the disturbance or perturbation distribution within a protein molecule.

 

Figure  c illustrates little variation in protein structure. This confirms that the protein interaction with the ligand is stable throughout the 50 ns simulation time. The solvent-accessible surface area of proteins is regarded as a key variable in protein folding and stability research. It is described as the surface characterized across a protein by the hypothetical center of a solvent sphere with the molecule’s van der Waals contact surface. SASA is the surface area of a biomolecule (protein) that is accessible to the solvent. A higher SASA value indicates the extension of the receptor macromolecules. Conversely, a lower SASA amplitude indicated greater compactness. The SASA versus time plot of 2ITU-CUR is shown in Fig.  d. The average SASA value of the 2ITU-CUR complex was 16389.21 nm2. Figure  d demonstrates that the 2ITU-CUR complex showed a more significant deviation between 0 and 20 ns simulation time, but after that point, only a slight variation in the protein structure was visible until 50 ns. Since the SASA plot displays the protein connection with the solvent environment and is dependent on the protein size, it can be concluded that the greater the level of solvent access, the lower the protein folding, and the more amino acids are available to interact with the ligand.

## KDeep protein-ligand binding affinity
The final protein-ligand complexes attained from the MD simulation were also subjected to KDeep calculations to assess the binding affinity and stability of the protein-ligand complexes. The KDeep computations for all CUR and CB[2]UN protein-ligand complexes are depicted as ΔGbind versus the simulation time plots in Fig. S9a and b, respectively. The KDeep computations for 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN protein-ligand complexes yielded higher absolute binding affinity of -6.13 kcal/mol at 35 ns and − 5.26 kcal/mol at 20 ns in the case of both CUR and CB[2]UN, respectively. The KDeep absolute binding affinity values measure the strength of the interaction between a molecule and a target protein. A lower value signifies enhanced binding affinity, which may result in increased drug potency. However, binding affinity is only one component that influences therapeutic efficacy; selectivity, pharmacokinetics, and toxicity are also important considerations. Curcumin exhibits a stronger binding affinity of -6.13 kcal/mol, indicating potential higher potency; however, its poor bioavailability restricts its overall effectiveness. Compared to curcumin, CB[2]UN has a weaker binding affinity of -5.26 kcal/mol. However, its unique properties may make up for this when it comes to drug delivery. This suggests that 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN are more likely to form stronger binding affinities and more stable protein-ligand complexes than other complexes with CUR and CB[2]UN.

## Discussion
The ERBB2 signalling pathway is an intricate and tightly controlled system that is essential for healthy cellular function and can promote cancer growth when activated abnormally. Numerous biological responses are ultimately brought on by the activation of ERBB2 signalling, including increased cell proliferation, improved cell survival and apoptosis (programmed cell death) resistance, control over cell invasion and migration, and influence on tissue remodelling and angiogenesis (the growth of new blood vessels) , . Like breast cancer and other cancer types, the ERBB2 (HER2) signalling pathway can also affect NSCLC. While NSCLC is frequently linked to different genetic variations, such as mutations in the EGFR gene, some NSCLC cases can also demonstrate overexpression or amplification of the ERBB2 gene, resulting in abnormal ERBB2 signalling. EGFR signal transduction pathways contribute to the growth of malignancies through several mechanisms, including effects on cell cycle progression, inhibition of apoptosis, angiogenesis, tumor cell motility, and metastasis , . The PIK3CA (Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-Bisphosphate 3-Kinase Catalytic Subunit Alpha) gene encodes the p110α catalytic subunit of phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), an essential enzyme involved in cellular signalling pathways. Breast, colorectal, and ovarian cancers are among the malignancies that frequently include PIK3CA gene mutations, which can result in dysregulated signalling through the PI3K pathway.
The mechanism of EGFR activation ratified the Src/CDK-like state of the inactive kinase and exhibited a cyclin-like mechanism of activation concerning asymmetric dimerization of the EGFR kinase domain . The L858R mutation was incompatible with its inactive conformation. This structure was established in complex with the small-molecule inhibitor lapatinib and was very analogous to that recently reported for an activation-resistant mutant of EGFR , . Somatic mutations in EGFR kinase that drive lung, and other cancers present an equivalent chance for intervention in these cancers, but with a critical discrepancy. In BCR-Abl, the mechanism of kinase activation is allosteric, and the kinase domain is unchanged from the wild-type kinase. Therefore, it is a single molecular target for drug development.
Curcumin’s effects on EGFR-mutant NSCLC extend beyond direct binding interactions. It regulates essential signaling pathways such as PI3K/AKT and MAPK, promotes apoptosis via mitochondrial dysfunction and caspase activation, interrupts the cell cycle at the G1 phase, and demonstrates antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties . It can also affect epigenetic mechanisms, including histone modifications and DNA methylation. The various actions enhance its potential as a therapeutic agent for EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Moreover, CBs provide potential therapeutic applications in EGFR-mutant NSCLC. These entities can directly interact with EGFR, serve as drug delivery vehicles, affect cellular uptake, and potentially alter cellular signaling pathways.
The dodecagonal strategy involves a comprehensive analysis of compounds through twelve distinct in silico methodologies, including gene enrichment, protein-protein interaction, identification of control drugs, protein-drug interaction studies, protein preparation, ligand preparation, molecular docking, ADMET assessment, MD simulations, binding affinity evaluations using MMGBSA, MMPBSA, and KDeep techniques.
This method is effective for drug discovery because it can accommodate various ligands and adapt to different target proteins. Gene enrichment analysis was conducted utilizing the ToppGene suite with bio-functionally relevant attributes . This method facilitates the identification of compounds in computer simulations, thereby expediting drug discovery. Consequently, the identified genes following functional enrichment will be analyzed using a web-based networking platform known as STRING, which enables the examination of protein-protein interactions (PPIs) and functional associations among proteins , . This in-silico method facilitates the prediction of compound effects without the need for laboratory experiments, thereby expediting the drug discovery process. The NCCN website identified the anti-NSCLC agents . A protein-drug interaction network was constructed utilizing the STITCH database to categorize the drugs that interact with the target . The drug that interacts with the target was identified and selected as a control drug. The preparation of proteins and ligands for in-silico analysis is a critical process that entails the generation of 3D structures for both the target protein and potential drug molecules.
The equilibrium between enthalpy and entropy influences the overall binding affinity. Enthalpy-driven interactions can lead to good binding, but entropy-driven factors may be more important, especially if the ligand loses a lot of its conformational freedom when it binds. Consequently, both enthalpy and entropy are essential factors in molecular docking. The primary objective of molecular docking is to predict a ligand’s binding mode and binding affinity to a protein . The process entails assessing the intensity of interaction and the compatibility of ligand with protein’s binding site. Molecular docking utilizes advanced scoring functions, distinguishing it from traditional docking methods. This study is appropriate due to its efficiency and capacity to yield valuable insights into drug-target interactions. ADMET analysis forecasts the behavior of a drug molecule within the human body, encompassing its absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity. ADMET analysis identifies compounds with favorable properties while reducing side effects, thereby expediting the drug discovery process.
For a process to be thermodynamically favorable, it is necessary for ΔG to be negative. This can happen when the reaction gives off heat (negative ΔH) or when it enhances disorder, which makes the situation entropically favorable at high temperatures (positive ΔS), or both. Conventional docking frequently simplifies the complex interaction between enthalpy and entropy in molecular binding. Although it can discern probable binding positions, it may not reliably estimate binding affinities. MD simulations are employed to evaluate the kinetic stability of protein-ligand complexes. Subsequently, the AmberTools platform in the Google Cloud computing system was employed to evaluate MD simulations of the complex. We conducted MD simulations for EGFR kinase mutant-selected lung cancer cell proteins (2ITU, 2ITV, and 2ITX) and ligands such as CUR and CB[2]UN to evaluate the thermodynamic and kinetic stability of protein-ligand complexes. The binding free energies of the protein-ligand complexes were evaluated through the MMPBSA and MMGBSA techniques. KDeep absolute binding affinity incorporates the change in Gibbs free energy, which is commonly utilized to evaluate the binding affinity of a ligand to a protein.
Based on the smallest route, biological network analysis used the STRING database, which assigns confidence scores to interactions, to determine the accuracy of each PPI prediction. Users can evaluate the quality of the evidence supporting a particular interaction using these scores. The average score provides an estimation of the overall dependability of the interactions linked to the protein or gene . A higher average score indicates that the interactions have more computational or experimental evidence, increasing their likelihood of being biologically significant. The physical or functional interactions between proteins in a biological system are graphically represented by PPIN . A key component of systems biology is the construction and analysis of PPI networks, which can provide insights into the organization and operation of biological systems . EGFR, ERBB2, and PIK3CA had an average score of 0.99.
Protein-drug interaction networks, which provide the connections between medications and their molecular targets, are essential for advancing drug discovery and development . The anti-NSCLC drugs such as Afatinib, Alectinib, Atezolizumab, Ceritinib, Crizotinib, Curcumin, Dacomitinib, Docetaxel, Doxorubicin, Gefitinib, Gilotrif, Gemcitabine, Imjudo, Lorlatinib, Methotrexate Sodium, Nivolumab, Pembrolizumab, Paclitaxel, Ramucirumab, Tepotinib Hydrochloride, and Zirabev displayed interactions with the gene EGFR. By simulating the dynamic behavior of protein-ligand complexes, researchers can elucidate key binding interactions, such as hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions, and electrostatic interactions, which can inform the rational design and optimization of drug candidates. Curcumin and CB[2]UN-based drugs were selected for the dodecagonal computational approach because of their complementary properties, including curcumin’s therapeutic potential and the molecular encapsulation capabilities of CB[2]UN. ## Dodecagonal strategy
The dodecagonal strategy is a study to recognize compounds in 12 different in silico analyses, such as gene enrichment, protein-protein interaction, identification of control drugs, protein-drug interactions, protein preparation, ligand preparation, molecular docking, ADMET, molecular dynamics, MMGBSA, MMPBSA, and KDeep protein-ligand binding affinity. The details are shown in Fig.  .

## Gene enrichment
Functionally important top 20 genes/mutations were selected from the COSMIC database. Primary screening of essential mutations was performed as training genes, and all other genes associated with NSCLC were used as the test gene set. Gene enrichment analysis was performed using the ToppGene suite with bio-functionally applicable attributes . The genes were then sorted based on their connection with each other. The genes with an average score of 0.54 and above have been utilized to construct protein-protein interaction networks through bio-molecular networking, STRING database (https://string-db.org/) , .

## Identification of control drugs
The anti-non-small cell lung cancer drugs were identified through the NCCN website . A protein-drug interaction network has been developed using the STITCH database (http://stitch.embl.de/) to classify the drugs interacting with the target . The drug interacting with the target was identified and chosen as a control drug.

## Protein preparation
The PDB format files of EGFR kinase mutant non-small lung cancer cell 3D structures of protein targets with PDB IDs of 2ITU, 2ITV, and 2ITX were selected from the RCSB Protein Data Bank. The resolution of protein targets for 2ITU, 2ITV, and 2ITX is 2.80 Å, 2.47 Å, and 2.98 Å, respectively. These protein files were subjected to Discovery Studio Visualizer 2021 software to remove all water molecules and to add polar hydrogen atoms. Finally, the protein data files were saved .pdb format for the docking analysis.

## Ligand preparation
The 3D structure of curcumin (CID: 969516) was recovered from the chemical database of PubChem. Another ligand with two glycoluril monomer units linked by the methylene bridges of cucurbituril (CB[2]UN) was optimized using the Gaussian 05 software package. The ligand files were written in .pdb file format.

## Optimization of the molecular docking analysis
The key concept in molecular docking is to forecast a ligand’s binding mode and binding affinity to a protein . It involves realizing the connection’s intensity and how the ligand fits into the protein’s binding site. The scoring function aims to calculate the binding free energy and predict the ideal binding configuration. Docking algorithm search techniques to investigate different ligand conformations and orientations inside the binding site of a protein. Molecular docking was performed using the AMDOCK software. The results were used to determine the lowest binding energies. The docked pose with the highest docking score was designated as the binding mode.

## Identification of potential pharmacokinetic properties
ADMET studies were predicted using the PreADMET online software. Key pharmacokinetic factors determine how a drug is absorbed, transported throughout the body, metabolized, and finally removed. Understanding pre-ADME variables is essential for optimizing the pharmacokinetic profile of a drug candidate , . This study incorporated both CUR and CB[2]UN in PreADMET software. Both molecules exhibited good ADMET properties. The resulting physicochemical signals were estimated using the BBB, CYP inhibition, Pgp inhibition, skin permeability, SKlogP, and SKlogS values. Drug candidates with improved solubility qualities can be selected using these values as a guide for compound optimization, thereby increasing the chances of success in drug development.

## Protein-ligand complex visualization
Discovery Studio Visualizer 2021 software was used to visualize the protein-ligand complexes. This software was employed to analyze the polar and hydrophobic interactions between the target and ligand, and to provide 2D and 3D visualizations.

## Molecular dynamics simulation
All molecular docking studies were assessed through molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of the complex using the AmberTools platform in the Google Cloud computing system. Protein and ligand topologies were constructed using ff19SB and AMBER force field-GAFF2, respectively. The solvated protein-ligand complex was then subjected to a 0.15 M salt (NaCl) concentration. MD simulations for EGFR kinase mutant-selected lung cancer cell proteins (2ITU, 2ITV, and 2ITX) and ligands such as CUR and CB[2]UN were performed to analyze the kinetic and thermodynamic stability of protein-ligand complexes. MD simulations were performed using an NPT (Number Pressure and Temperature) ensemble with an equilibrium time of 5 ns at 310 K and a pressure of 1 bar. The equilibrated protein-ligand complexes were subjected to MD simulation for 50 ns at 310 K and 1 bar pressure. The binding free energies of the protein-ligand complexes were analyzed using the MMPBSA and MMGBSA methods. The resulting protein-ligand complexes were evaluated for 50 ns to calculate the radius of gyration (Rg), root mean square deviation (RMSD), and root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) to confirm the stability and compactness of the structure. The solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) was accomplished using the PDBePISA server .

## KDeep protein-ligand binding affinity calculation
In addition to MD simulation studies, a parameter in the protein-ligand interaction affinity predictor tool was utilized to evaluate the binding affinity (ΔG), stability, and energetics of protein-ligand complexes . This prediction employs a 3D convolutional neural network and a machine learning technique. Deep convolutional neural network (DCNN)-based predictors of protein-ligand affinity include several pharmacophoric properties such as hydrophobic, hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor, positive and negative ionizable, and total excluded volume. KDeep absolute binding affinity (KABA) includes the change in Gibbs free energy, typically used to assess the binding affinity of a ligand to a protein. KABA was used to rank the most favorable candidates for further experimental confirmation.

## Conclusion
In this study, we investigated the effects of curcumin and CB[2]UN in controlling EGFR-mutated NSCLC using a dodecagonal in silico computational approach. The dodecagonal strategy recognized the compounds in 12 different in silico analyses, including gene enrichment, protein-protein interaction, identification of control drugs, protein-drug interactions, protein preparation, ligand preparation, molecular docking, ADMET, molecular dynamics, MMGBSA, MMPBSA, and KDeep protein-ligand binding affinity. Molecular docking results showed that CUR and CB[2]UN tended to bind to 2ITX and 2ITV target proteins with the lowest binding energies of -6.9 and − 8.1 kcal/mol, respectively. Furthermore, ADMET predictions of these compounds suggest a therapeutic molecule to modulate the EGFR mutation associated with NSCLC, which can be subjected to further in vitro and in vivo evaluations. The protein-ligand complexes from the MD simulation for 50 ns were analyzed using RMSD, RMSF, Rg, and SASA, which verified the kinetic stability of curcumin and CB[2]UN with EGFR-mutant proteins. Furthermore, MMPBSA/MMGBSA analysis validated the thermodynamic stability of each curcumin and CB[2]UN protein-ligand complex. Finally, KDeep absolute binding affinity calculations produced energies of -6.13 kcal/mol and − 5.26 kcal/mol for 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN protein-ligand complexes, respectively. Additionally, a limitation of this study is that conducting MD simulations for large protein-ligand complexes can result in significant costs, particularly for extended simulation durations or large structural ensembles. Thus, our dodecagonal strategy confirmed that 2ITX-CUR and 2ITV-CB[2]UN were more likely to form protein-ligand complexes with more significant binding affinities and excellent stability throughout the 50 ns simulation time.
