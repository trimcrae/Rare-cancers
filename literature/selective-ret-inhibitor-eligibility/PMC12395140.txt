## Abstract

## Introduction
The emergence of diverse resistance mechanisms after osimertinib therapy, including on-target epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations and off-target alterations, warrants investigation of novel therapeutics to overcome these challenges and improve patient outcomes.

## Methods
COMPOSIT was a French, retrospective, multicenter, cohort study of the effectiveness and tolerability of osimertinib in combination with other targeted therapies in patients with advanced EGFR-mutant (EGFRm) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who harbored other oncogenic drivers as primary or acquired resistance mechanisms. Real-world progression-free survival (rwPFS), overall survival (OS), and objective response rate (ORR) were the primary endpoints.

## Results
The study included 61 patients (63.9% women; median age, 61 years). Chemotherapy was administered to 26 patients (42.6%) before the combinations. The most frequently targeted resistance mechanisms were MET amplification (n = 40) and BRAF alterations (n = 11). Sixteen combinations of osimertinib with other targeted therapies were reported. Overall (except for 10 patients in clinical trials), median rwPFS and OS were 3.9 (95% CI, 2.9-5.2) and 9.8 months (95% CI, 6.8-14.8). Best ORR (n = 54) was 50% (95% CI, 33.0-72.8). In patients with MET amplification (n = 29), median rwPFS and OS were 4.9 (95% CI, 2.9-7.2) and 8.6 months (95% CI, 5.3-21.6). Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 15 patients (24.6%). No deaths were related to treatment.

## Conclusions
Combinations of osimertinib with other targeted therapies appeared to be feasible and safe and may offer clinical benefit to overcome resistance to osimertinib in EGFRm NSCLC, especially in patients with MET amplification.

## Implications for practice
The COMPOSIT study highlights the effectiveness and safety of osimertinib in combination with other targeted therapies for advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC for overcoming resistance mechanisms post-osimertinib. MET amplification was the most frequently targeted resistance mechanism post-osimertinib. This combination strategy offers an additional approach for managing resistant EGFRm NSCLC cases. Its real-world applicability suggests a pivotal shift toward multi-targeted therapy, potentially establishing osimertinib as a new treatment option for these patients.

## Introduction
Targeted therapies have revolutionized treatment strategies and outcomes of advanced non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) over the past 15 years. The most notable example in NSCLC is the targeting of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) pathway by EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs).
In non-Asian patients, EGFR gene mutations are found in 15% of advanced nonsquamous NSCLC cases; in Asians, incidence can reach 40%-60%. Osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR-TKI, has demonstrated superiority over earlier-generation inhibitors and is now the standard of care first-line treatment for EGFR-mutated (EGFRm) NSCLC. , However, as with previous EGFR-TKIs, after a median progression-free survival (mPFS) of 18.9 months, patients inexorably develop acquired resistance. Resistance mechanisms differ depending on whether osimertinib is given as first-line therapy or after the T790M resistance mutation. 
Identifying resistance mechanisms is crucial to guide therapeutic approaches in patients after osimertinib failure. Resistance mechanisms can be divided into 3 categories: on-target (involving EGFR), off-target (through alteration of bypass molecular pathway), and histological transformation. The most common on-target resistance mechanism is the EGFR C797S mutation, observed in ≤25% of cases with osimertinib as second-line therapy. , The most frequently identified off-target resistance mechanism is MET amplification, found in ≤25% of tumors progressing in patients taking osimertinib. Other infrequent but potentially druggable mechanisms include BRAF mutations and rearrangements, HER2 amplification, and other oncogenic fusions involving RET, ALK, or ROS1. , Histologic transformation is identified as a rare resistance mechanism with poor prognosis, particularly in NSCLC. 
The European Society of Medical Oncology guidelines on the management of EGFRm NSCLC recommend biopsy at disease progression to determine if new targetable alterations are present. In such cases, treatment with the relevant targeted agent is appropriate. However, there are no targeted agents approved for patients whose tumors have progressed on osimertinib. Strategies are being evaluated in clinical trials or during off-label use. Few studies have reported the outcomes of combination therapy in osimertinib-resistant advanced EGFRm NSCLC with identified molecular mechanisms. Some case reports and phase II studies highlight the importance of maintaining EGFR inhibition in subsequent lines of therapy. 
The exponential development of targeted therapies has led to the feasibility of several combinations to overcome resistance to osimertinib in clinical practice, after discussion in molecular board and for patients who are ineligible for clinical trials. The COMPOSIT study is a French national cohort, evaluating osimertinib combination with targeted therapies in patients with EGFRm NSCLC and other oncogenic driver alterations. The primary objectives of the study were to assess the effectiveness and tolerability of the combination of osimertinib with another targeted therapy in this patient population.

## Patients and methods

## Study design and eligibility criteria
The COMPOSIT study was a retrospective multicenter study conducted at 15 thoracic oncology centers in France. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (CEPRO) of the “Société de Pneumologie de Langue Française“ CEPRO 2022-004. Participating centers were responsible for obtaining patients’ consents.
The study included patients with advanced EGFRm NSCLC who received osimertinib between January 1, 2017 and April 30, 2021 and were subsequently treated with a combination of osimertinib with another targeted therapy in the course of their disease. Patients were identified from hospital charts and pharmacy records. Clinical, pathological, and biological data as well as treatment outcomes were collected using a dedicated case report form completed by investigators at each site.
The choice of combination therapy and dose regimens was decided by the attending physician after the local multidisciplinary tumor board. Patients who were included in clinical trials are described, but their efficacy and safety data were not provided. The data cutoff date was June 17, 2022.

## Study endpoints
The main endpoints included real-world PFS (rwPFS), overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and grade ≥3 adverse events (AEs) associated with the combination therapy. Treatment response was evaluated at each center according to investigator assessment or Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. There was no centralized imaging review. Imaging assessment and patient safety were evaluated in accordance with French regulations (Expanded Access Program) and standards of care. AEs were graded using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 4.0. 

## Histomolecular data
Resistance mechanisms to osimertinib were detected through routine clinical care using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) or DNA/RNA next-generation sequencing (NGS) on tissue biopsies. MET amplification was detected by FISH (MET/CEP7 ratio ≥2 or with ≥5 MET signals per cell , ) or NGS. When available, data from circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analyses (from cerebrospinal fluid, malignant pleural effusion, or plasma samples) were recorded.

## Statistical analyses
rwPFS was defined as the time from the initiation of the combination treatment to the date of radiologically confirmed progression-disease (PD), loss of follow-up, or death. OS was defined as the time from treatment introduction to death or last date of follow up. ORR was defined as complete response (CR) plus partial response (PR). Disease control rate (DCR) was defined as CR plus PR plus stable disease (SD).
Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival outcomes were compared using a log-rank test. Survival outcomes (PFS, OS) were calculated for patients with a minimum follow-up of 6 months in the absence of disease progression or death. Statistical analyses were performed using MedCalc, version 20.2 (MedCalc Software).

## Results

## Patients and histomolecular characteristics
Fifteen out of 33 surveyed French centers provided data for 61 patients who received combination of osimertinib with another targeted therapy. Among information obtained from 9 out of the 15 participating centers, 50 of the 874 (5.7%) patients treated with osimertinib during the study period received a combination of osimertinib. Sixty-five combinations of osimertinib with other targeted therapies were administered to the 61 patients; 4 patients received 2 treatment combinations. No patient received first-line combination therapy. Finally, 10 of 61 (16.3%) patients received the treatment combination in a clinical trial ( ).
Clinical and histomolecular characteristics are presented in . The median age of patients was 61 years (range, 29-85 years); 39 were women (63.9%), and 58 (95.1%) had stage IV disease at diagnosis. Most tumors were adenocarcinomas (n = 59; 96.7%).
 At diagnosis, exon 19 deletion was identified in 39 patients (63.9%). Concurrent mutations were observed in 14 patients (22.9%). Of these, 5 alterations were targetable, including 2 with de novo EGFR T790M mutations, 1 with HER2-amplification, 1 with MET amplification, and 1 with BRAF fusion. Other identified co-mutations, predominantly TP53 mutations, were not suitable for combination therapy ( ).
Twenty-six (42.6%) patients received osimertinib as first-line therapy with a median rwPFS of 9.0 months (95% CI, 8.0-12.7) and median OS of 28.8 months (95% CI, 17.8-29.2) ( ). Thirty-five (57.4%) patients received osimertinib as second line or more with a median rwPFS of 9.1 months (95% CI, 4.8-12.0) and median OS of 32.4 months (95% CI, 22.1-38.9) ( ). Among patients who received osimertinib as a second line or more, EGFR T790M mutation was identified in 27 (77.1%) patients before the introduction of osimertinib.
Treatments received before combination therapies are presented in . Combination therapy was administered as second-line treatment for 24 (39.3%) patients, as third-line treatment for 10 (16.4%) patients, and as a fourth-line treatment or more for the remaining patients ( ). Thirty-five patients (57.4%) received combination therapy without having received prior chemotherapy treatment.

MET amplification was the most targeted mechanism by combination therapies (n = 40; 65.6%) in patients receiving osimertinib as first line (n = 23) or second line or more (n = 17). The most frequent combination received was osimertinib plus crizotinib (n = 17; 42.5%). Techniques for detection of MET amplification are summarized in .

BRAF alteration (BRAF V600E mutation, n = 5; BRAF fusion, n = 6) was the second most frequent mechanism targeted. All mechanisms were observed in patients receiving osimertinib as second line or more. EGFR C797S mutation was observed in 4 patients receiving osimertinib as second line or more. For patients with BRAF V600E mutation, the therapy combinations were osimertinib plus dabrafenib (n = 2), osimertinib plus trametinib (n = 1), and osimertinib plus dabrafenib and trametinib (n = 2). All patients with BRAF fusion received a combination of osimertinib plus trametinib.

## Efficacy data

## General population
Analysis of rwPFS was conducted on 50 patients who received combination treatments. The analysis excluded 10 patients enrolled in a clinical trial and one patient with less than 6 months of follow-up. The median rwPFS was 3.9 months (95% CI, 2.9-5.2) ( ). The ORR (n = 54) was 50% (95% CI, 33.0-72.8); 1 patient achieved a CR (1.8%), and 26 patients achieved a PR (48.2%). Eleven patients (20.0%) had SD, and 16 patients (30.0%) had PD as the best response. Duration of exposure to the combination therapies, stratified by targeted genetic alterations, is shown in . Median rwPFS and ORR and DCR are presented in and for all different targeted molecular alterations.
 

## 
MET amplification population
In patients with MET amplification (n = 29), median rwPFS was 4.9 months (95% CI, 2.9-7.2) ( ). Among the 33 combinations of osimertinib with MET-targeted therapy, the best ORR was 63.6% (95% CI, 39.4-97.3); 1 patient (3.0%) achieved a CR, and 20 patients (60.6%) achieved a PR. Four patients (12.1%) had SD, and 8 patients (24.2%) had PD. The median rwPFS was longer with capmatinib and osimertinib (n = 8) than with crizotinib and osimertinib (n = 21); but the difference was not statistically significant (7.2 vs 3.9 months; hazard ratio [HR], 0.45 [95% CI, 0.20-1.01]; P = .054) ( ).

## 
BRAF pathway population
In patients with BRAF V600E mutation, median rwPFS of those treated with osimertinib plus dabrafenib and trametinib (n = 2) was longer vs patients treated with osimertinib and either dabrafenib or trametinib (n = 3; 8.2 [95% CI, 8.22-15.75] vs 3.1 [95% CI, 0.49-9.76] months). PRs only were observed when osimertinib was combined with dabrafenib and trametinib (n = 2). In patients with BRAF fusion (all treated with osimertinib and trametinib; n = 6), median rwPFS was 1.7 months (95% CI, 1.15-5.16). Only 1 patient (16.7%) had a PR.

## 
EGFR mutation resistance population
In patients with on-target EGFR resistance to osimertinib (n = 5), the median rwPFS was 2.33 months (95% CI, 1.45-3.62). Only one PR (20.0%) was observed when osimertinib was combined with afatinib to overcome EGFR G724S and T790M resistance mutations. No response was observed when EGFR C797S was targeted.

## Other targeted resistance population
One PR was observed in patients with RET fusion treated with osimertinib and pralsetinib (n = 2) and in 1 patient with ALK rearrangement treated with osimertinib and crizotinib. One patient was treated with osimertinib and erdafitinib targeting an FGFR1 amplification for 5.33 months with a best response of SD. Finally, 1 patient was treated with osimertinib and trastuzumab targeting an HER2 amplification for 3.19 months with PD.

## Survival data
Survival analyses were conducted on a total of 60 patients after excluding 1 with a follow-up less than 6 months. The median follow-up was 19.23 months (95% CI, 13.28-22.06). The median OS was 9.83 months (95% CI, 6.87-14.83). In the population of patients with MET amplification (n = 39), the median OS was 8.65 months (95% CI, 5.26-21.63) ( ).

## Tolerability of combination therapies
AEs of any grade were reported in 34 patients (55.7%). No deaths have been attributed to the combination therapy. Grade ≥3 AEs were reported in 15 patients (24.6%). The frequency of drug interruption due to AEs was 11.5% (n = 7). presents the frequency of AEs according to the molecule associated with osimertinib and describes the grade ≥3 AEs.
 

## Histomolecular mechanisms of resistance after combination therapies
Among the population of patients with available PFS data (n = 50), 46 patients had tumor progression with combination therapy at the time of data collection. Biopsies and/or ctDNA liquid analyses were performed in 18 (39%) patients. Histomolecular mechanisms of resistance after combination therapy were identified in 7 patients, while none were found in 11 patients.
In patients with treated MET amplification (n = 5), progression was associated with KRAS amplification, D1246N MET mutation, BRAF fusion + C797S EGFR mutation, EGFR amplification, and TP53 mutation + ERRB2 mutation + LIT mutation + FGFR2 mutation. Additionally, 1 patient with C797S EGFR mutation was found to have histologic transformation to small cell lung cancer and another patient with BRAF fusion showed TP53 mutation after progression with combination therapy. ## Discussion
This retrospective study included 61 patients who had previously been treated with osimertinib at 15 French thoracic oncology centers. To date, this represents the largest multicenter cohort studying the combination of osimertinib with other targeted therapies. We reported 16 distinct osimertinib combinations for 9 targeted resistance alterations, predominantly MET amplification and BRAF alteration. Regarding the efficacy of combination therapy, while the ORR was quite high (50%), both the median rwPFS and OS remained relatively short, less than 4 and 10 months, respectively.
Among patients with MET amplification, the median rwPFS was 4.9 months, and the ORR was 63.6%. The combination of osimertinib and crizotinib emerged as the prevalent choice, probably because crizotinib access was easier than for other drugs in France at the time of study. The efficacy of crizotinib has been corroborated by the literature. , , In the largest study (N = 38), the ORR was 48.6%, and median PFS was 5.0 months, consistent with our results. However, our results are inferior to recent studies published with MET inhibitors more active than crizotinib, including tepotinib and salvolitinib. Multiple ongoing and published clinical trials are probing dual MET and EGFR inhibition as a potential strategy to overcome acquired resistance with promising preliminary results. In the INSIGHT 2 study, which evaluated the efficacy of osimertinib and tepotinib in EGFRm NSCLC with MET amplification, the ORR in patients with ≥9 months’ follow-up was 54.5% (95% CI, 32.2-75.6) for MET amplification detected by FISH and 50.0% (95% CI, 24.7-75.3) for MET amplification detected by NGS. 
Gefitinib and tepotinib also demonstrated antitumor activity in EGFRm NSCLC with MET overexpression or MET amplification with an impressive PFS of 16.6 months in patients with MET amplification in the INSIGHT study. In the phase Ib TATTON trial, which evaluated osimertinib and savolitinib in EGFRm NSCLC with MET amplification, the median PFS in patients previously treated with osimertinib was 5.5 months (95% CI, 4.1-7.7), and the ORR was 33% (95% CI, 22-46). In the phase II SAVANNAH trail, in the subgroup with high MET amplification, the ORR was 49%, and the median PFS was 7.1 months. 
For BRAF V600E mutation, PRs were only observed when osimertinib was combined with both dabrafenib and trametinib. No response was observed when either dabrafenib or trametinib was used alone, as previously described in the treatment of patients with cancers carrying the BRAF V600E mutation. , The triple combination of osimertinib, dabrafenib, and trametinib showed encouraging efficacy in terms of ORR and PFS. Some case reports , presented similar results with this combination. In our study, some AEs (ie, fever grade 1, thrombocytopenia grade 4, pneumonitis grade 1) were reported with this triple combination, requiring a dose reduction. In a previous case report, 2 patients developed pneumonitis on TKI after exposure to the combination therapy, including a grade 5. 
The C797S mutation accounts for 20%-25% of resistance to osimertinib when used as second line or later and less than 10% when used as first line. , In our cohort, all patients received combination therapy to overcome C797S mutation resistance after second line or more osimertinib treatment. Four patients had triple mutations of activating EGFR, T790M, and C797S. Two were treated with osimertinib and a first-generation EGFR-TKI, which has shown brief success in patients with trans T790M and C797S mutations. An ongoing phase II trial is evaluating a cohort of patients receiving osimertinib and gefitinib after detection of C797S mutation as a resistance mechanism to osimertinib. Additionally, preliminary clinical evidence evaluated the use of concurrent osimertinib and gefitinib as first-line treatment of EGFRm NSCLC in a phase I/II study. In contrast, a cis configuration of T790M and C797S (on the same allele) notoriously obstructs the effective binding of both osimertinib and first-generation EGFR-TKIs to the EGFR kinase domain, thereby extensively limiting therapeutic options. Combination therapy with brigatinib and cetuximab also has been tested. , 
Oncogenic fusions have been described as a mechanism of acquired resistance. In our cohort, resistant oncogenic fusions were overcome with combination therapy for BRAF, RET, and ALK fusions in 6, 2, and 1 patients, respectively. All patients with BRAF fusion received a combination of osimertinib and trametinib, resulting in poor outcomes (median rwPFS 1.7 months and ORR 16.7%). Conversely, we reported encouraging responses with combination of osimertinib and pralsetinib, as previously described in 4 case reports. , , Recently, the combination of osimertinib and selpercatinib was reported to overcome RET fusion-positive EGFRm lung cancers in 14 patients. The efficacy and tolerability were promising, with a median treatment duration of 7.9 months and a response rate of 50%. 
Finally, we reported for the first time to date the combination of osimertinib and erdafitinib, which overcame FGFR1 amplification as acquired resistance to osimertinib. The AEs observed were grade 3 mucositis, skin dryness, and dry eyes.
The main clinical challenge with combination therapies is that efficacy also may lead to synergistic or additive toxicity. In our study, combination therapies presented an acceptable tolerability profile; only 11.5% of patients had treatment interruptions due to AEs. Nevertheless, as with all retrospective studies, it is crucial to interpret the data on tolerability with caution because AEs may be subject to underreporting in medical records.
Our study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study conducted in 15 French institutions, which could result in patient selection bias and missing data for tolerability. Patients had different baseline characteristics, but all received osimertinib as monotherapy before combination therapy. The demographics of our cohort were consistent with the French population described in the IFCT Biomarker France study. Notably, EGFR exon 19 deletion was more prevalent in our cohort (64%) than in the IFCT Biomarker France study (51%). However, our patients had extensive pretreatments and as previously known, tumors with EGFR L858R mutation manifest a reduced responsiveness to EGFR-TKI monotherapy. 
In our study, the prevalence of resistance mechanisms reflects the specific selection of patients with targetable resistance and may not represent their frequency in a general population. Patients with off-target resistance mechanisms may experience shorter PFS with osimertinib, suggesting that our study population may have poorer outcomes. Thus, in our combination cohort, the median rwPFS for osimertinib was similar as first- and second-line therapy and less than expected in the first-line setting. The rwPFS in second line and beyond was consistent with the PFS described in the AURA3 study (10.1 months). However, the rwPFS of osimertinib in the first line in our cohort was considerably lower than expected based on the FLAURA study (18.9 months). This is probably due to the high prevalence of MET amplification (n = 40; 65.5%) in our cohort. The onset of MET amplification bypass and EGFR C797X mutation appears to be mainly in the first year of osimertinib treatment. Moreover, with regard to OS, the detection of a targetable resistance mechanism seemed to be a factor of poor prognosis when osimertinib was given in first-line in our cohort (n = 26), the median OS was 28.8 months, which is much lower than in the FLAURA study (38.6 months). On the other hand, for patients treated with osimertinib in second line or beyond (n = 35), the median OS was 32.4 months which is slightly higher than in the AURA3 study (28.6 months).
Second, we highlight that all combination strategies remain investigational. The drugs were procured off-label, through the French Expanded Access Program (EAP), or clinical trials conducted by thoracic oncologists. Third, the resistance mechanism to osimertinib was locally determined using non-standardized technology (eg, different NGS, FISH, IHC). The choice of targeting a resistance mechanism identified was solely that of the referring center according to their practice. Last, only 18 patients were assessed for mechanisms of acquired resistance under combination therapy. Acquired resistance mechanisms to osimertinib plus MET-targeted therapy were complex and mediated by MET, EGFR, or KRAS alterations. , Our preliminary data seemed to be consistent with this.
As this is a real-world study, we aimed to describe all patients receiving osimertinib combinations in 15 French thoracic oncology centers. While safety and efficacy data for patients enrolled in trials are embargoed, it is notable that most patients received combination therapy outside of clinical trials, either off-label or through the French EAP, after Multidisciplinary Tumor Board review and informed consent. Among the 40 patients who received a combination targeting MET amplification as a resistance mechanism, only 25% were enrolled in a clinical trial. The reasons for non-enrollment warrant further investigation.
In conclusion, our study highlights the therapeutic challenges in developing strategies other than chemotherapy to overcome osimertinib resistance in EGFRm NSCLC. The strategy of directly targeting a resistance mechanism is demonstrated in our study; however, despite high response rates, the duration of response remains mostly short due to resistance often being polyclonal, not just a single mechanism. Hence, the interest in strategies that do not directly target a resistance mechanism. Although emerging strategies, without the need for biomarker pre-selection, were recently presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology congress in 2023, especially the amivantamab plus chemotherapy regimen, our findings provide real-world clinical evidence that the combined use of osimertinib and targeted therapy is generally well-tolerated and may provide benefits to patients with EGFRm NSCLC who have acquired molecular osimertinib resistance. Based on a deep understanding of the underlying molecular resistance mechanisms, multi-disciplinary Molecular Tumor Boards are necessary to select an adequate off-label combination therapy, when the patient is ineligible for clinical trials. Furthermore, given the diversity of resistance mechanisms, conducting comprehensive clinical trials to assess all potential combinations across varied clinical contexts poses a significant challenge. Real-world registries, such COMPOSIT, assume pivotal significance in unraveling these complexities, thereby offering invaluable insights to guide clinical decision-making amidst the evolving treatment landscape.
