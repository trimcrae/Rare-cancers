## Abstract

## Background
Selpercatinib is a selective RET inhibitor approved for treatment of RET-activated cancers. Adverse events (AEs) are manageable with dose modifications. This post hoc analysis characterized selpercatinib’s clinical safety profile after long-term follow-up in the safety population of LIBRETTO-001.

## Patients and Methods
LIBRETTO-001 is an ongoing phase I/II, single-arm, open-label trial (NCT03157128). Eligible patients were ≥18 years old with diagnosis of advanced/metastatic RET fusion-positive solid tumor, RET-mutant medullary thyroid cancer, or other RET-activated tumors. In phase I, patients received selpercatinib 20 mg QD or 20-240 mg BID; patients in phase II received 160 mg BID. The analyzed population comprised all patients who received ≥1 selpercatinib dose and were followed up until data cutoff (January 13, 2023).

## Results
For the 837 patients, median follow-up was 45.4 months (95% CI, 44.5-46.6); median time on treatment was 30.1 months (range 0.1-66.8). Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported in 76.2% of patients; most common events were hypertension (19.7%), ALT increased (11.8%), and hyponatremia (9.2%). Serious TEAEs were reported in 51.4% of patients. Most frequently reported any-grade AEs at <6 months of treatment were fatigue (36.6%), dry mouth (32.8%), and ALT increased (30.5%); at ≥24 months of treatment, these were edema (63.2%), diarrhea (60.7%), and fatigue (53.0%). Selpercatinib-related TEAEs leading to reduced dosage were reported in 39.3%, those leading to treatment interruption were reported in 47.1%, and those leading to discontinuation were reported in 4.3% of patients.

## Conclusion
Long-term treatment with selpercatinib is feasible. AEs are manageable with dose modifications, allowing most patients to continue safely on therapy.

Keywords: selpercatinib, non-small cell lung cancer, medullary thyroid cancer, RET inhibitor, toxicity

Awareness of potential adverse event timing during selpercatinib treatment and patient monitoring is key to managing toxicities. This article reports on the clinical safety profile of selpercatinib after long-term follow-up in the LIBRETTO-001 study.

## Implications for practice
Results of this post hoc analysis of LIBRETTO-001 demonstrate that long-term treatment with selpercatinib is feasible. This analysis with additional patients and additional follow-up time shows that adverse events (AEs) occur throughout treatment. These events are reversible or manageable with dose modification, allowing most patients to continue safely on therapy. Awareness of potential AE timing during selpercatinib treatment and patient monitoring is key to managing toxicities.

## Introduction
RET is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase, and alterations in the RET gene are involved in the pathogenesis of several cancer types. The RET proto-oncogene is generally activated by either fusions or mutations. RET fusions are oncogenic drivers in 1%-2% of non-small-cell lung cancers (NSCLC) and up to 10% of papillary thyroid cancers.  RET mutations occur in 70% of medullary thyroid cancers (MTCs). These alterations result in overexpression and ligand-independent activation of the RET protein as well as downstream pathways involved in cell proliferation and survival. , 
Selective RET inhibitors such as selpercatinib and pralsetinib have been developed to potently inhibit RET in tumors while sparing other kinase and non-kinase off-targets that can contribute to significant toxicity. Selpercatinib is a first-in-class, highly selective, and potent RET inhibitor approved in multiple countries for the treatment of RET-activated cancers. , It has demonstrated clinically meaningful and durable antitumor activity in patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC, , , including patients with brain metastases and patients with RET-activated thyroid cancers, among other tumor types. 
Adverse events (AEs) are generally manageable with dose modifications. After extended follow-up in the pivotal LIBRETTO-001 trial, 4% of patients discontinued treatment due to a treatment-related AE. The long duration of treatment with newer targeted treatments such as selpercatinib, which can extend to many years, makes understanding longer term toxicity and tolerability critically important. This post hoc analysis further characterized the clinical safety profile of selpercatinib after long-term follow-up in the safety population of the LIBRETTO-001 study.

## Methods
LIBRETTO-001 is an ongoing registrational, phase I/II, single-arm, open-label trial conducted at 65 sites in 12 countries (NCT03157128). , Eligible patients were ≥18 years of age if permitted by regulatory authorities, with a diagnosis of an advanced or metastatic RET fusion-positive solid tumor (eg, NSCLC, thyroid, pancreas, colorectal, etc.), RET-mutant MTC, or other tumor with RET activation (eg, mutations in other tumor types or other evidence of RET activation), additional eligibility criteria included Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2, QTc interval of ≤470 ms, and adequate organ function.
In the phase I dose-escalation portion, patients received selpercatinib 20 mg once daily or doses ranging from 20 to 240 mg twice a day. All patients in the phase II portion received selpercatinib at the recommended phase II dose of 160 mg twice a day. Patients with dose reductions were permitted to re-escalate once AEs had been resolved ( ). Treatment with selpercatinib beyond progressive disease was permitted if it was felt that the patient would derive ongoing benefit. The trial was conducted following Good Clinical Practice guidelines, in line with principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and all applicable country and local regulations. The protocol was approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each investigative site, and all patients provided written informed consent.
 The analyzed safety population comprised all patients enrolled who received at least one dose of selpercatinib and were followed up until the data cutoff (January 13, 2023). Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were defined as AEs that started (or worsened) on or after the first administration of the study drug, regardless of attribution to drug. The reported AE term was assigned a standardized preferred term using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, version 21.0. AEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.03. Serious AEs were defined as any AE that resulted in one of the following outcomes: death, initial or prolonged inpatient hospitalization, a life-threatening experience (ie, immediate risk of dying), persistent or significant disability/incapacity, congenital anomaly/birth defect, or important medical events that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalization but may jeopardize the patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the other outcomes listed. Events were summarized using descriptive statistics, and time-to-event analyses were conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method; 95% CIs for incidence rates were calculated based on Poisson distribution. Logistic regression was used to compare the average selpercatinib dose overall per patient stratified by responders and non-responders. A Cox model was used to calculate hazard ratios and 95% CI for the impact of mean daily dose as a continuous variable on duration of response and progression-free survival.

## Results

## Baseline characteristics
A total of 837 patients were included in the overall long-term safety cohort, including those with RET fusion-positive NSCLC (n = 362), RET-mutant MTC (n = 324), and RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer (n = 66) (Supplementary ). The median time on treatment was 30.1 months (range 0.1-66.8) for patients in the overall cohort and 84.3% of patients were on treatment ≥6 months, with 74.9% on treatment ≥12 months, 59.1% on treatment ≥24 months, and 48.2% on treatment ≥36 months. A total of 744 patients started the study at selpercatinib doses of 160 mg twice a day, and another 62 patients from the phase I portion eventually received 160 mg twice a day. Median age in the overall cohort was 59 years (range 15-92) ( ); 50.9% of patients were male, 68.3% were White, and 62.6% had an ECOG performance status of 1 or 2. The mean number of prior systemic regimens received was 1.8 overall (SD 1.9). Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were similar across cancer types.
 

## Treatment-emergent AEs
TEAEs regardless of causality are shown in . After long-term follow-up (median 45.4 months), grade ≥3 TEAEs were reported in 76.2% of patients overall. The most frequently reported (≥5%) grade ≥3 events were hypertension (19.7%), increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT; 11.8%), hyponatremia (9.2%), increased aspartate aminotransferase (AST; 8.7%), diarrhea (5.9%), and lymphopenia (5.9%). The profile of grade ≥3 TEAEs was similar across the NSCLC, MTC, and thyroid cancer populations. Serious TEAEs were reported in 51.4% of patients. A total of 38 patients (4.5%) died within 28 days of the last dose due to a TEAE, and 10 patients (1.2%) died >28 days after the last dose due to a TEAE. Only one patient experienced a fatal AE felt to be related to selpercatinib by the investigator (pneumonitis/acute respiratory failure).
 

## Treatment modifications and discontinuations
In the overall safety cohort, 42.7% of patients reported TEAEs leading to reduced dosage and 67.7% reported TEAEs leading to treatment interruption ( ). The median time to dose reduction in the overall cohort was 2.5 (95% CI, 2.1-2.8) months. TEAEs related to selpercatinib leading to reduced dosage were reported in 39.3% of patients. The most common events were increased ALT (7.8%), increased AST (6.5%), and prolonged QTc interval on electrocardiogram (3.1%). The median time to dose interruption in the overall cohort was 5.2 (95% CI, 3.9-6.5) months. TEAEs related to selpercatinib leading to treatment interruption were reported in 47.1% of patients, with increased ALT (10.0%), increased AST (9.4%), and hypertension (5.3%) being the most reported events. Dose re-escalation was reported in 10.6% of patients overall.
 Among the different tumor types, the median time to dose reduction was 2.1 (95% CI, 1.8-2.8) months in patients with NSCLC, 3.2 (95% CI, 2.5-5.1) months in patients with MTC, and 2.5 (95% CI, 1.2-3.7) months in patients with RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer. Median time to dose interruption was 3.7 (95% CI, 2.1-5.4) months in patients with NSCLC, 8.0 (95% CI, 5.2-11.1) months in patients with MTC, and 6.0 (95% CI, 2.2-17.7) months in patients with RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer.
Overall, TEAEs leading to permanent discontinuation were reported in 9.6% of patients. Increased ALT (0.7%), fatigue (0.6%), increased AST (0.5%), and sepsis (0.5%) were the most reported events. TEAEs related to selpercatinib leading to permanent discontinuation were reported in 4.3% of patients. The most common selpercatinib-related events were increased ALT (0.6%), ascites (0.4%), increased AST (0.4%), and fatigue (0.4%).

## Events of interest
Several AEs were determined to be of interest early in the selpercatinib development program. Any-grade treatment-emergent AST and ALT elevations based on laboratory value abnormalities were reported in 61.3% and 56.7% of all patients, respectively. Grade ≥3 events were reported in 10.4% and 12.3% of all patients, respectively. The median time to first onset was 4.7 weeks (range 0.7-227.9) for AST elevations and 4.4 weeks (range 0.9-186.1) for ALT elevations. Any-grade treatment-emergent alkaline phosphatase elevations were reported in 42.6% of patients, with grade ≥3 events reported in 4.4% of patients. The median time to first onset was 4.0 weeks (range 0.7-253.4). Any-grade treatment-emergent bilirubin elevations were reported in 30.8% of patients, with grade ≥3 events reported in 2.9% of patients. The median time to first onset was 8.1 weeks (range 0.6-252.1). There were no reports of liver failure assessed as related to selpercatinib.
Any-grade treatment-emergent hypertension was reported in 43.1% of patients and grade ≥3 events were reported in 20.1% of patients overall. The median time to first onset was 4.1 weeks (range 0.1-235.7); 30.2% of events were related to selpercatinib. Overall, 28.8% of patients initiated an antihypertensive treatment. Serious hypertension events were reported in 1.0% of patients overall.
Treatment-emergent QTc prolongation events of any grade were reported in 21.1% of patients and grade ≥3 events were reported in 4.9% of patients overall (Supplementary ). The median time to first onset was 4.1 weeks (range 0.1-189.3). QTc prolongation events related to selpercatinib were reported in 16.8% of patients overall, and only one serious event (0.1%) was reported in a patient with MTC. There were no reports of selpercatinib-related torsades de pointes, ventricular fibrillation, or ventricular flutter. One event of sudden death (time to event 12.2 months) and one event of supraventricular tachycardia (time to event 11.2 months) were reported.
Of any-grade hypersensitivity TEAEs, 4.2% of patients reported events leading to dose interruption, 3.9% reported events leading to dose reduction, and 0.4% reported events leading to selpercatinib discontinuation. Any-grade hypersensitivity TEAEs were reported in 13.9% of patients who received prior immunotherapy (n = 201) versus 3.1% of patients with no prior immunotherapy (n = 636; Supplementary ). Median time to first onset was 1.7 weeks (range 1.0-39.7) versus 4.4 weeks (range 0.7-203.9), respectively. Events were considered related to selpercatinib in 12.4% of patients who received prior immunotherapy versus 2.0% of patients with no prior immunotherapy. Among patients who received prior immunotherapy, the time from the last dose of immunotherapy to first occurrence of hypersensitivity with selpercatinib was ≥24 months in 86.4% (95% CI, 80.9-90.4) of the overall cohort. Among those who received prior immunotherapy, the last hypersensitivity event was reported as resolved in 89.3% of patients; among those with no prior immunotherapy, the last event was reported as resolved in 85.0% of patients.
Any-grade hypersensitivity TEAEs were reported in 8.1% of patients who received prior chemotherapy (n = 369) versus 3.8% of patients without prior chemotherapy (n = 468; Supplementary ). Median time to first onset was 1.8 weeks (range 1.0-39.7) versus 3.1 weeks (range 0.7-203.9), respectively. Events were considered related to selpercatinib in 7.0% of patients who received prior chemotherapy versus 2.6% of patients with no prior chemotherapy. Prior multi-kinase inhibitor (MKI) use was also evaluated for patients in the MTC and RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer cohorts. Any-grade hypersensitivity TEAEs were reported in 1.9% of patients who received prior MKIs (n = 211) versus 5.0% of patients without prior MKIs (n = 179). Median time to first onset was 33.3 weeks (range 23.3-203.9) versus 24.1 weeks (range 0.9-112.1), respectively. Events were considered related to selpercatinib in 0.5% of patients who received prior MKIs versus 2.8% of patients with no prior MKIs.
Other newly identified AEs of interest following selpercatinib’s initial approval included interstitial lung disease (ILD) and chylous effusion. ILD of any grade was reported in 2.6% of patients in the overall cohort, with grade ≥3 events reported in 0.5% of patients (Supplementary ). The median time to first onset was 47.5 weeks (range 0.1-188.0), and events related to selpercatinib were reported in 0.8% of patients. Six patients reported an ILD event leading to dose interruption, one patient reported an event leading to dose reduction, and one patient reported an event leading to selpercatinib discontinuation. A total of 3 (0.9%) serious events were reported, all occurring in patients with MTC. Treatment-emergent events of chylous effusions of any grade were reported in 3.9% of patients overall (chylothorax 2.2%, chylous ascites 1.9%), with 1.3% of patients reporting an event leading to dose interruption and 1.0% reporting an event leading to dose reduction. Median time to first onset of chylous ascites was 103.3 weeks (range 15.7-186.7); 3.0% of events were considered related to selpercatinib. A total of 13 (1.6%) serious events were reported in the overall cohort.
Hypothyroidism TEAEs were reported in 16.2% of patients overall, with all events being grade 1 or 2, and rates were similar between patients with NSCLC and those with MTC or RET fusion-positive thyroid cancer (Supplementary ). Median time to first onset was 16.1 weeks (range 0.1-226.4), and events related to selpercatinib were reported in 8.0% of patients. Two patients reported an event leading to dose reduction, and there were no events leading to dose interruption or selpercatinib discontinuation. Only one patient reported a serious event, which was considered related to selpercatinib and occurred in the NSCLC cohort.
The following incidences of any-grade treatment-emergent abnormal hematologic lab tests were reported (Supplementary ): decreased lymphocyte count (55.1%), decreased white blood cell count (50.7%), decreased platelets (40.3%), decreased hemoglobin (33.8%), and decreased neutrophil count (26.4%). The most commonly reported grade ≥3 events overall were decreased lymphocyte count, reported in 21.6% of patients, followed by decreased hemoglobin (4.0%), and decreased platelets (3.8%).
Median time to first occurrence for select AEs in the overall cohort was 3.5 (95% CI, 2.9-4.3) months for diarrhea, 4.1 (95% CI, 3.5-4.6) months for edema, and 2.7 (95% CI, 1.9-3.3) months for fatigue; for hypertension, median time to first occurrence was 4.1 weeks (range 0.1-235.7). Frequencies of any grade and grade 3-4 TEAEs by duration of selpercatinib treatment are presented in . The most commonly reported select any-grade AEs at <6 months of treatment were fatigue (36.6%), dry mouth (32.8%), and ALT increased (30.5%); at 6 months-1 year, these were diarrhea (40.9%), edema (40.9%), and fatigue (38.7%); and at 1-2 years, these were edema (53.1%), fatigue (51.7%), and diarrhea (49.7%). At ≥2 years of treatment, the most reported events were edema (63.2%), diarrhea (60.7%), and fatigue (53.0%). Hypertension was the most reported grade 3-4 event at <6 months (13.7%), 6 months-1 year (14.0%), 1-2 years (18.6%), and ≥2 years (23.3%).
 

## Discussion
The selective RET inhibitor, selpercatinib, has shown high response rates in patients with RET-activated cancers. However, treatment outcomes may be compromised by AEs if they result in discontinuation. In this post hoc analysis of the safety population of LIBRETTO-001 with 18-months longer follow-up than previously reported (median 45.4 months), results demonstrate a consistent safety profile with previous reports. , , TEAEs related to selpercatinib were associated with low rates of discontinuation (4.3% overall). Incidence of AEs varied over time and were manageable with dose adjustments. Of the overall cohort, 42.7% of patients had dose reductions and 67.7% experienced treatment interruption.
Given the long-term disease control seen in a subgroup of patients and resultant long-term exposure to drug, awareness of which potential AEs can occur at which time during treatment with selpercatinib and monitoring of patients while on treatment is key to managing toxicities and optimizing treatment outcomes. This analysis with additional patients and additional follow-up time shows that AEs occur throughout treatment. Of the AEs were determined to be of interest, the most reported any-grade events within the first 6 months on treatment were fatigue, dry mouth, and increased ALT, whereas after 2 years of treatment, most of patients reported edema, diarrhea, and fatigue. Hypertension was the most reported grade 3-4 event across time points, potentially due to off-target inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor. Those AEs initially identified during the selpercatinib development program all occurred fairly early on during treatment, but the more recently identified events of interest tended to occur later (ie, ILD, chylous effusions, and hypothyroidism). In the current study, increased ALT and AST as well as QT prolongation were the most common events leading to dose reduction, and ALT/AST and hypertension most commonly led to treatment interruption. The selpercatinib safety profile is characterized by AEs that can be monitored and managed with dose modifications, allowing most patients to continue treatment. Current recommendations for dose modifications for these AEs of interest based on US prescribing information are presented in . 
While QTc prolongation events related to selpercatinib were reported in 16.8% of patients overall, only one serious event (0.1%) was reported. Like other FDA-approved drugs, the frequency of QTc prolongation does not necessarily correlate with the onset of clinical symptoms or the need to make clinical decisions to address it. It is therefore important to monitor electrolytes and assess patients’ symptoms of dizziness, syncope, palpitations, and loss of consciousness, as well as any concomitant medications associated with cardiac abnormalities or those that could result in potential drug interactions. 
Edema is another common side effect of selective RET inhibitors, though it is often peripheral. In this study, approximately one-third of patients experienced any grade of edema, with a median time to occurrence of 31.4 months. In a case report of a patient with NSCLC, a gastrointestinal AE of small bowel edema was reported after 9 months of treatment which resolved after treatment interruption; selpercatinib was then re-administered with dose reduction without relapse. 
In addition to the chylous effusion events seen in LIBRETTO-001, there have been published case reports of patients experiencing these events following treatment with RET inhibitors. , The mechanism of the chylous effusion is not completely understood. Common methods of management discussed in the case reports included dose interruptions/reductions, drainage (often multiple), and nutritional consults which included low fat, high protein diets. In a recent retrospective analysis of a pan-tumor cohort treated with selpercatinib, 7/96 patients developed a chylous effusion. Events resulted in dose reduction in 47% of patients; however, there were no treatment discontinuations. Timely diagnosis and assessment of a differential diagnosis is critical to mitigate serious events as well as to prevent misinterpreting the event as a sign of disease progression.
RET inhibitors are also associated with pneumonitis and ILD. , In the present study, incidence of ILD was low overall (2.6%) and is lower than what is seen with other tyrosine kinase inhibitors and checkpoint inhibitors currently in use for metastatic or locally advanced NSCLC. In a recent analysis that evaluated the class effect of tyrosine kinase inhibitors using data from the FDA AE reporting system, selpercatinib did not show an ILD signal of disproportionality, whereas the largest number of reported cases came from the epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor osimertinib. 
Erectile dysfunction has been reported in patients receiving selective RET inhibitors. While 16.7% of patients in this study reported any-grade events, these may be underdiagnosed as patients may not proactively bring this up to their healthcare provider.
Hypersensitivity reactions to immunotherapies are an important consideration in patient management; symptoms may include fever, rash, or arthralgia/myalgia. Prior treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) has been shown to increase the risk and/or severity of hypersensitivity events in patients who subsequently receive RET inhibitors. This was seen in the current study, as rates of hypersensitivity were higher in patients previously treated with immunotherapy versus those who were not (13.9% vs 3.1%). McCoach et al reported that 17 of 22 hypersensitivity events related to selpercatinib occurred in prior ICI-treated patients. Despite a greater number of treatment-related hypersensitivity events with selpercatinib among patients who received prior ICI compared with those who did not, data showed that selpercatinib was safely administered regardless of prior treatment status, with close monitoring being key for timely identification and treatment. These events can be managed and often reversed with dose modification and supportive care. Current guidance recommends suspending selpercatinib until the event resolves while initiating steroid treatment, followed by incremental increases in dosage if treatment is tolerated without recurrent hypersensitivity. 
This study is strengthened by utilizing the longest length of follow-up to date for LIBRETTO-001, with patients on study treatment up to 5.5 years as well as the largest sample size to date for selpercatinib across multiple tumor types. However, it is not possible to extrapolate findings from a single-arm trial such as this to real-world settings, and further studies will be necessary to evaluate the safety of selpercatinib in clinical practice. Two ongoing randomized clinical trials have reported data that align with these results. In the LIBRETTO-431 phase III study, treatment with selpercatinib led to significantly longer progression-free survival than platinum-based chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab among patients with advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. In the LIBRETTO-531 phase III study, which evaluated selpercatinib versus MKIs in patients with advanced or metastatic RET-mutant MTC, selpercatinib showed superior progression-free and treatment failure-free survival. Both trials have reported a similar toxicity profile to that seen in LIBRETTO-001. , 

## Conclusion
Long-term treatment with selpercatinib is feasible. AEs should be monitored and are reversible or manageable with dose modification, allowing for most patients who experience these events to continue safely on and benefit from therapy. Long-term monitoring will continue to further characterize these findings.
