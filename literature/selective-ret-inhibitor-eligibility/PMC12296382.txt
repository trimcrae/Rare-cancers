Oncogenic RET fusions arise from chromosomal rearrangements resulting in RET proteins’ C-terminus splicing with the N-terminus of the fusion partner. Most lack a transmembrane domain and the constitutive activation of RET tyrosine kinase leads to activation of several intracellular downstream signaling cascades, including RAS/MAPK, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, JAK/STAT, and PKA/PKC [ ].

RET fusions are rare in colorectal cancer and a comprehensive genomic and clinicopathological analysis involving 891 patients with RET fusions confirmed the prevalence of colon adenocarcinoma at 0.18% (60/32998) and rectal adenocarcinoma at 0.01% (1/6672) respectively [ ]. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) identified NCOA4 (Nuclear receptor coactivator 4)- and CCDC6 (Coiled-coil domain containing 6)- as the most frequent fusion partners at 68% and 14% respectively. These fusions are mutually exclusive with other mutations [ ] and are more prevalent in microsatellite instability (MSI)-high tumors in the right colon, high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and programmed death-ligand 1 negative tumors [ , ]. Selective RET inhibitors, including selpercatinib are FDA and EMA approved for common RET-fusion positive cancers and in the agnostic setting [ ].
We present a case of a gentleman in his mid-sixties with oligometastatic colon cancer harboring a NCOA4-RET fusion detected by whole exome sequencing (WES).

## Case report
A Vietnamese gentleman in his mid-sixties attended the emergency department (ED) due to a left sided facial droop, left arm weakness and a one-week history of increasing falls. One year previously, he had completed six months of adjuvant FOLFOX (5-flurorouracil plus oxaliplatin) for a near-obstructing poorly differentiated stage IIIC (pT4bpN2b) sigmoid adenocarcinoma with proficient mismatch repair (pMMR) on immunohistochemistry. A magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) brain revealed a solitary mass in the right frontal lobe (4 × 3.8 × 4.1 cm) with significant vasogenic oedema, effacement of the right lateral ventricle and a 7 mm midline shift ( ). A successful gross total resection was performed via craniotomy, confirming oligometastatic colon cancer with signet ring features. Adjuvant stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) to the surgical cavity (27.5 Gy/5 fractions) was completed six-weeks later.
 This was an unusual metastatic site and prompted consideration of an oncogenic driver mutation. The subsequent case review at a molecular tumor board (MTB) meeting following completion of WES testing identified a high TMB, 13.2 Mut/Mbp and a NCOA4-RET fusion on the resected brain tissue. While on surveillance, multifocal dural recurrence was confirmed (Refer to ) four months later. There was further rapid dural progression identified on the Gamma-Knife SRS planning imaging, and he underwent hippocampal-sparing whole brain radiotherapy with a boost to gross disease of 35 Gy in 10 fractions instead. Two months later, MRI brain imaging demonstrated significant improvement, and no evidence of spinal cord involvement.
A further two months later in January 2022, he commenced initial systemic therapy with selpercatinib as part of an expanded drug access program. Three months later this was withheld for two weeks due to grade 3 transaminitis. During this time, he experienced loss of sensation in his legs, reduced hearing, and walking difficulties. An MRI brain confirmed further dural progression and re-irradiation was not recommended given the short interval. Following resolution of transaminitis, he restarted selpercatininb at 50% dose, resulting in a rapid improvement in clinical symptoms and radiologically confirmed until a tonic-clinic seizure in September 2022 prompted palliative partial-brain radiotherapy for new brain metastases. Despite no extracranial recurrence worsening neurological symptoms led to his death two months later, marking 21 months after his diagnosis of oligometastatic colon cancer.

## Discussion
This case highlights the intracranial efficacy of the selective RET inhibitor selpercatinib in a patient with metastatic colon cancer harboring a NCOA4-RET fusion, and the subsequent response upon re-challenge at a reduced dose. Patients with RET fusion-positive tumors have a fourfold increased risk of death compared with RET fusion-negative patients [ ], with a median overall survival of 14 months, prior to the advent of selective RET inhibitors. In a similar case report vandetanib, a multikinase inhibitor, was shown to inhibit patient-derived tumor cell lines [ ]. However, these drugs are less commonly used due the availability today of more efficacious selective RET inhibitors with fewer off-target side effects.
The presence of a single brain metastasis represents a rare (2.1%) and unusual site of metastases in colorectal cancer in addition to a poor overall survival (OS). Patients who underwent metastasectomy with or without radiation or chemotherapy had to an improved OS when compared with those without surgery, 11.69 versus 5.28 months [ ]. Interestingly, this case highlights the need to consider the presence of a rare oncogenic driver mutations in cases with rare sites of metastases.
The phase II (NCT03157128) open-label basket trial, LIBRETTO-001 evaluated the tolerability and efficacy of selpercatinib in 45 heavily pretreated patients with non-lung or non-thyroid tumors harboring a RET fusion [ ]. This tumor agnostic trial included diverse cancers with NCOA4- (38%), CCDC6- (16%), and KIF5B- (9%) as the common RET fusion partners. Of the 41 patients evaluated for efficacy, treatment with selpercatinib at 160 mg twice daily resulted in an objective response rate (ORR) of 44% and median duration of response (mDOR) of 24.5 months. While the ORR was lower in the ten patients (22%) with colon cancer at 20%, it remains higher than trifluridine and tipiracil at 1.6% [ ] in the treatment refractory setting. The median time to response was 1.9 months (Interquartile range 1.7–2.0) and mDOR 9.4 months respectively in this cohort.
In our case selpercatinib was held with the emergence of grade 3 transaminitis and re-introduced at 50% dose two weeks later upon resolution to grade 1. In the tumor agnostic safety population, there were 16% grade 3 ALT and 11% AST treatment-related adverse events and drug -induced liver injury occurred in one patient (2%). Dural metastases progresses quickly while our patient was off treatment, but there was a rapid response to selpercatininb and sustained for four months when reintroduction at a lower dose. The potential benefit of upfront adjuvant selpercatinib following resection of oligometastatic site and its combination with immunotherapy in those with mismatch repair or high TMB remain unanswered questions.
In conclusion, this case underscores the importance of MTB discussions and NGS-WES in identifying rare oncogenic driver mutations and guiding the use of targeted therapies to enhance patient outcomes.
