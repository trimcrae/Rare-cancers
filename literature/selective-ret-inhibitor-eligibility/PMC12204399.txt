## Abstract
Precision oncology has transformed the treatment landscape for patients with advanced solid tumors. Tumor-agnostic therapies, those that have been approved based on genetic mutations or biomarkers across tumor histology types, are important examples of how the implementation of precision oncology can expand therapeutic options for patients, especially those with rare cancer types and treatment-refractory disease. In this review, we first discuss how advances in next-generation sequencing and molecular profiling have enabled the identification of shared actionable alterations. Subsequently, we explore the current landscape of tumor-agnostic therapies that have received approval from the Food and Drug Administration. We discuss the strengths and limitations of these therapies and evaluate the clinical trial data leading to their approval. In addition, we detail updated results from these clinical trials and additional observational studies reported after the approval. Several factors such as tumor histology, specific alteration types, and the presence of co-alterations are associated with the efficacy of these therapies. Also, challenges remain in understanding resistance mechanisms and predicting response. Looking ahead, we discuss how improved diagnostic tools, novel experimental strategies, and innovative trial designs may further advance the field of precision oncology and improve therapeutic options for patients.
Keywords: precision oncology, tumor-agnostic therapy, molecular profiling, targeted therapy, biomarker-driven treatment

## Implications for Practice
Precision oncology, where a treatment is chosen for a patient based on molecular features of their tumor rather than only their cancer type, is expanding the therapeutic options for patients. For solid tumors, advances in precision oncology have led to nine tumor-agnostic approvals, where a treatment is approved across tumor types based on genetic mutations or biomarkers. In this review, we evaluate the strengths and limitations of these tumor-agnostic therapies. We also explore the determinants of efficacy of these therapies and discuss how emerging diagnostic and experimental tools and novel trial designs may further advancements in cancer therapy.

## Introduction
The lifetime probability of being diagnosed with an invasive cancer is 41.6% for men and 39.6% for women. For many cancer types, precision oncology has increased the number of therapeutic options. In precision oncology, a treatment is chosen for a patient based on molecular features of their tumor, rather than just the site of origin or histologic type of their tumor. With thorough genomics and proteomics efforts improving understanding of cancer, advancement of molecular testing, and novel targeted drug modalities, precision oncology is poised to introduce many new options for patients with solid tumor types.
Large sequencing efforts have shown that there can be molecular heterogeneity between patient tumors from the same histology. These efforts have also revealed many common genomic aberrancies and oncogenic pathway activations within and across tumor types. , Since most cancers display shared hallmarks, it is not surprising that common molecular alterations and pathways that drive, or are the consequence of, these hallmarks would be widely present.
The development of drugs targeting shared alterations with success in multiple cancer types has led to several recent tumor-agnostic approvals. In 2017, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) gave the first tumor-agnostic approval of a drug for PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab. Subsequently, the FDA has granted eight other tumor-agnostic accelerated or full approvals.
In this review, we first discuss how molecular profiling may provide increased therapeutic options for patients. We describe the characteristics that make a target actionable and the benefits of molecular testing for clinical trial matching and enrollment. Subsequently, we briefly discuss the strengths and limitations of FDA-approved tumor-agnostic therapies. In addition, determinants of efficacy such as the interplay of histology and molecular alterations are explored. We conclude the review by discussing the future of tumor-agnostic approvals.

## Molecular profiling and targeted therapy
Molecular testing and next-generation sequencing (NGS) have transformed our understanding of the driver mechanisms of cancer. Previously, testing was limited to focused assays that typically only assessed a single gene or single alteration. However, NGS assays have been developed that allow for testing for multiple molecular alterations across a multigene panel. Sequencing efforts generally identify many genomic alterations, but only those that are actionable affect the treatment course of the patient. A genomic alteration can be considered actionable if it has either of the following characteristics :
(1) It predicts therapy response (sensitivity or resistance to therapy).
(2) It affects the function of a cancer-related gene and can be targeted with approved or investigational therapies.
(3) It is a specific eligibility criterion for enrollment into genotype-selected trials.
(4) It has demonstrated the ability to establish diagnosis or influence prognosis.
(5) It is a germline alteration that predicts drug metabolism and/or adverse effects.
(6) It is a germline alteration that predicts future risk of cancer or other disease. In this case, it is usually considered more actionable if prevention screening with early treatment is feasible.

## Clinical utility of genomic testing
Many studies exploring the utility of NGS-testing have found benefit for subsets of patients with advanced solid tumors of various histology. A systematic review of studies with patients receiving NGS-informed treatment revealed that 11 studies reported improved progression-free survival (PFS) and 16 publications had improved overall survival. However, there are caveats to this benefit. For example, one study found that NGS tended not to offer benefit for patients with poor performance status, rapidly progressing disease, and short life-expectancy. In addition, there can be many barriers to access and coverage for NGS testing both in the United States and globally. Nonetheless, expanded molecular testing improves the application of precision oncology and has been shown to provide clinical benefit.
Several professional bodies have reviewed the clinical utility of NGS. In 2022, an American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) consensus panel recommended patients with metastatic or advanced cancer should undergo genomic sequencing if the presence of an alteration has regulatory approval as biomarkers to guide treatment. Furthermore, they concluded that tumor-agnostic approvals provide a rationale for genomic testing for all eligible patients. In 2024, the European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) updated their recommendations for genomic testing. They used the ESMO Scale for Clinical Actionability of molecular Targets (ESCAT) considering cost-effectiveness and accessibility. They recommended the use of NGS for routine practice within specific cancer types where there is ESCAT level 1 evidence. It was also unanimously agreed to report ESCAT level II alterations to facilitate trial enrollment and drug development. ESMO also recommends carrying out NGS to detect tumor-agnostic alterations with matched therapies in patients with metastatic cancers.

## Tumor-agnostic approvals: strengths and limitations
Tumor-agnostic indications, where the approval of a drug is based on a molecular alteration rather than a histology, provide some of the greatest support for the use of expanded molecular testing to determine eligibility for patients with advanced solid tumors. The FDA has made 9 tumor-agnostic approvals for solid tumors ( and ). In this following section, we will briefly summarize the data behind these approvals. We further investigate the data supporting the approval across the histology types, incorporate updated efficacy data, and discuss limitations.
 

## MSI-H/dMMR
Microsatellite regions consist of short, repeated sequences that are prone to DNA replication errors. Cells have a system responsible for identifying and correcting errors at these regions called the mismatch repair system (MMR). There are 4 main genes involved in this system that play a critical role (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2). In cancer, mutations in MMR proteins or epigenetic silencing can lead to repair defects. The resulting hypermutated phenotype is referred to as high microsatellite instability (MSI-H). Deficiency of the MMR system (dMMR) can be tested for by IHC of the 4 proteins involved in the MMR system. Expression loss in any of the four MMR proteins indicates dMMR. Another method to test for MSI-H is to check for microsatellite instability directly in 5-8 designated loci using a PCR assay. Newer NGS panels can also test for microsatellite instability at many more loci. 
Deficiency in this system increases the mutation rate that promotes oncogenesis. Hereditary deficiency of this system leads to Lynch Syndrome and increases risk for colonic and extracolonic cancers. Sporadic deficiency is also seen in patients across cancer types. The increased mutation rate also leads to increased production of neoantigens that are hypothesized to elicit an immune response to cancer. Pembrolizumab and dostarlimab are immune checkpoint inhibitor therapies that have received tumor-agnostic approval from the FDA for patients with MSI-H/dMMR tumors.

## Pembrolizumab
Pembrolizumab is a humanized IgG4 anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody. The interaction of PD-1 on T-cells and PD-L1 or PD-L2 ligands on tumor cells leads to immune suppression. Checkpoint inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab, reactivate the antitumor response by disrupting these immunosuppressive signals. The accelerated approval of pembrolizumab for MSI-H/dMMR tumors was based on results from preliminary analysis of 5 trials across multiple histologic types. A pooled analysis showed an objective response rate (ORR) of 39.6%, and the FDA granted accelerated approval for Pembrolizumab based on these results. Subsequent publications have reported additional outcomes across solid tumor types. In KEYNOTE-164, pembrolizumab showed an ORR of 33% for patients with MSI-H/dMMR colorectal cancer that was treatment refractory. In an updated analysis of KEYNOTE-158, patient with MSI-H/dMMR cancers had an ORR of 34.3%. After incorporating this updated data and interim results from KEYNOTE-051 for pediatric patients, the FDA granted full approval in 2023 for Pembrolizumab for solid tumors with MSI-H/dMMR.
Though these results are promising, there has not been conclusive data across all solid tumor types. In KEYNOTE-158, pancreatic adenocarcinoma had an ORR of 18.2% and a median PFS of 2.1 months ( ). However, a subsequent case series reported an ORR of 48.4% in 31 evaluable MSI-H/dMMR pancreatic adenocarcinoma patients treated with checkpoint inhibitor therapy (61.3% pembrolizumab) with a median PFS of 26.7 months. 
 

## Dostarlimab
Dostarlimab is a humanized IgG4 antibody anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody that is an immune checkpoint inhibitor. In the initial analysis of 209 patients with dMMR recurrent or advanced solid tumors showed an ORR of 41.4% in the GARNET trial. Based on these results, the FDA granted accelerated approval for dostarlimab in dMMR advanced solid tumors. Subsequent updated analysis from the GARNET trial showed consistent benefit with 327 patients achieving an ORR of 44% and a median duration of response that was not reached (median follow-up was 27.7 months).
In the updated analysis, dostarlimab showed consistent benefit across multiple histology types ( ), and 25 patients from 12 different histologic types were pooled for the “Other Cancer” analysis reporting a response rate of 44%. It is unclear if consistent benefit would be obtained for all these histologic types given their low representation.

## TMB high
Mutations in coding genes of tumors can lead to the generation of neoantigens. There is great intra- and inter-cancer type variability in the number of mutations each tumor has. If a tumor has more mutations, then it likely has more neoantigens that could elicit an immune response. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) is a way to quantify and compare the number of mutations between tumors. It is usually measured as a number of mutations per megabase (mut/Mb).
Pembrolizumab has been FDA-approved for patients with tumors that have a high TMB (≥10 mut/Mb). In the KEYNOTE-158 trial, outcomes with pembrolizumab treatment were analyzed for 102 patients with high TMB. They had an ORR of 29%, whereas patients without high TMB had an ORR of 6% (N = 688). The FDA granted accelerated approval based on these results to treatment-refractory solid tumors with TMB-H. A subsequent pooled analysis of multiple trials of pembrolizumab showed that TMB was predictive of response. For patients with TMB-H tumors, the ORR was 31.4% as compared with 9.5% for patients without TMB-H tumors. Of note, this study used a cutoff of 175 mutations/exome that was shown to be similar to the 10-mut/Mb cutoff.
Though 10 mut/Mb is the cutoff of current approval, a limited exploratory analysis of 32 patients with TMB between 10 and 13 in KEYNOTE-158 found an ORR of just 13%. There was also great variability in the response rate across cancer types in KEYNOTE-158 ( ). Updated analysis with greater patient numbers may help better determine efficacy across cancer types.

## 
NTRK fusions
The neurotrophin receptor tyrosine kinase (NTRK) genes NTRK1-3 encode separate TRK proteins that are cell surface receptors with similar structural domains. Once activated, they are auto-phosphorylated leading to the activation of downstream proliferative pathways. In cancer, there are multiple mechanisms to activate the TRK proteins, but the most common mechanisms are fusions that lead to ligand-independent activation of the TRK kinase that promotes oncogenesis. 

## Larotrectinib
Larotrectinib is a highly selective small-molecule inhibitor of all 3 TRK proteins. In a pooled analysis of 3 trials, larotrectinib displayed an overall response rate of 75%. At 1 year, 71% of the responses were ongoing and 55% of the patients remained progression-free. A total of 12 adult and pediatric tumor types were represented ( ). The FDA granted accelerated approval to Larotrectinib for adult and pediatric malignancies that are metastatic or where surgical resection would result in severe morbidity. Multiple follow-up studies focused updated analysis of these trials across cancer types demonstrated benefit in NTRK-fusion positive solid tumors ( ). 

## Entrectinib
Entrectinib is a potent small-molecule inhibitor of the TRK, ROS1, and ALK proteins and was designed to penetrate the central nervous system. In a pooled analysis of 3 single-arm trials, 54 adult patients with advanced solid tumors with NTRK fusions were treated with entrectinib and the overall response rate was 57%. The median duration of response was 10 months. The FDA granted accelerated approval for entrectinib for solid tumors with NTRK gene fusion with advanced solid tumors that did not have known acquired mutation. Updated analysis with a larger cohort of 121 patients across 14 tumor types showed a response rate of 61.2%. Of note, the intracranial response rate in 11 patients with CNS disease was 63.6%. There were also individual updates published for specific histologic types showing consistent response ( ). 

## Repotrectinib
Repotrectinib is a next-generation TRK, ALK, and ROS1 inhibitor. It was designed to be compact and have a small binding interface to be able to overcome one of the most common mechanisms of resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors in solvent-front mutations. In preliminary analysis of the TRIDENT-I trial, 40 patients with NTRK fusions that were TKI-naïve had a 58% ORR. In addition, 48 patients previously treated with entrectinib, larotrectinib, or both agents had an ORR of 50%. The trial included mostly NSCLC patients, but it also included patients with salivary gland cancer, thyroid cancer, soft tissue sarcoma, and other cancer types. The FDA granted accelerated approval for repotrectinib for NTRK-fusion positive solid tumors based on these data. 
Both entrectinib and larotrectinib displayed efficacy across a wide range of histologic types with NTRK-fusion tumors, but this analysis was based on single-arm studies. It is unclear which agent should be used or preferred for solid tumors with NTRK fusions. Limited analysis and indirect comparisons have suggested potential advantage of larotrectinib, , but the results are not conclusive. Repotrectinib also showed promising activity in both patients that had previously been treated with entrectinib and larotrectinib and those that were TKI-naive. Further reporting of these results may help clinicians decide on sequencing of these agents.

## 
RET fusions
The rearranged during transfection gene (RET) is a receptor tyrosine kinase protein that is a proto-oncogene. It is involved in normal embryonic development. RET can become aberrantly activated in cancer with two main methods: (1) activating mutations in its kinase domain or (2) somatic rearrangements that lead to gene fusions. When expressed in preclinical models, these RET fusion proteins can promoter cell proliferation and survival. 

## Selpercatinib
Selpercatinib is a highly selective RET inhibitor that is also brain-penetrant. In the multicohort LIBRETTO-001 trial, selpercatinib had already shown efficacy in NSCLC and thyroid cancer. For NSCLC, 105 patients that had received platinum-based therapy had an ORR of 64%. For 39 treatment-naïve patients, the ORR was 85%. The tumor-agnostic cohort did not include NSCLC or thyroid cancer patients and consisted of 45 patients across 14 cancer types, and they had a combined 43.9% ORR. The FDA granted accelerated approval of selpercatinib in treatment-refractory advanced or metastatic tumor based on these data. An updated analysis with 52 patients in the tumor-agnostic cohort reported an ORR of 44.2%. 
Of the 14 cancer types in the tumor-agnostic cohort, 10 had less than or equal to 2 patients enrolled in the trial ( ). The updated analysis reported results for an expanded number of patients, but only the results for pancreatic cancer and colorectal cancer were reported in detail.

## BRAF V600E tumors
The BRAF gene encodes the B-Raf protein that is an important part of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway that stimulated cellular growth and survival. Downstream in the pathway to B-Raf is the MEK protein that is a key point in the MAPK pathway. Many tyrosine receptor kinases activate the MAPK pathway leading to proliferation and promoting cellular survival. The BRAF V600 mutations (especially V600E and V600K) lead to constitutive activation of the MAPK pathway that can be seen in many cancer types. 

## Dabrafenib and trametinib
Dabrafenib is a competitive inhibitor of RAF kinases that has potent activity against the BRAF V600 mutations. Trametinib is a potent inhibitor of the MEK protein. The combination was shown to be efficacious in melanoma that had either BRAF V600E or BRAF V600K mutations when compared in phase III trials against dabrafenib plus placebo or vemurafenib (a BRAF V600 inhibitor). A phase II trial of dabrafenib plus trametinib in metastatic NSCLC patients that were pretreated or treatment naïve showed an ORR of 68.4% and 63.9%, respectively. An analysis of 3 trials studying the efficacy of dabrafenib and trametinib in 131 adult and pediatric patient tumors with the BRAF V600E mutation had an overall ORR of 41%. The FDA granted accelerated approval based on this analysis. Updated analysis from the ROAR trial focused on rare tumors showed an ORR of >50% in 8 out of 9 histologic types. 
Of note, the approval is not for colorectal cancer where there is resistance to monotherapy BRAF inhibition. However, combination therapy of cetuximab, an inhibitor of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), with the BRAF inhibitor encorafenib has had efficacy and been FDA-approved in colorectal cancer. Given that there is known to be resistance in CRC, it will be important to report the efficacy of dabrafenib and trametinib in histologic types with low trial representation.

## HER2 overexpression
The human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) gene encodes a growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase. Heterodimerization with other members of the EGFR family leads to autophosphorylation of tyrosine residues and activates cell proliferation, survival, and tumorigenesis. It is overexpressed, mutated, and amplified across various cancer types. 

## Trastuzumab deruxtecan
Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) that is composed of trastuzumab, an antibody targeting HER2, that is attached to the payload deruxtecan, a potent topoisomerase I inhibitor. T-DXd binds to cells with HER2 expressed on the surface, and internalization of the ADC leads to cleavage of the linker and release of its cytotoxic payload. In addition, the deruxtecan can also be released to nearby cells leading to a bystander effect. The ADC also functions by disrupting HER2 signaling and can lead to antibody-dependent cellular toxicity.
T-DXd demonstrated efficacy in HER2-positive (IHC 3+ or IHC2+/ISH+) metastatic breast cancer showing a response rate of 60.9% in a cohort of 184 patients that had previously been treated. In the DESTINY-CRC02 trial, 64 patients with HER2 IHC 3+ metastatic colorectal cancer with previous treatment had an ORR of 46.9% in patients treated with the 5.4-mg/kg dose of T-DXd. In the DESTINY-Lung01 trial, 17 patients with metastatic nonsmall cell lung cancer with HER2 IHC 3+ tumors treated with T-DXd had a response rate of 52.9%. In the multihistology phase II DESTINY-PanTumor02 trial, 111 patients from diverse histologic types with HER2 IHC 3+ at enrollment had a response rate of 51.4% when treated with T-DXd. , The FDA granted approval to T-DXd for patients with metastatic HER2 IHC 3+ solid tumors. In updated analysis of 267 patients from DESTINY-PanTumor02 with HER2 IHC 3+ or 2+ by local or central review, the ORR was 37.1%. Patients with central review HER2 IHC 3+ (N = 75) had an ORR of 61.3%. 
An important consideration of these results is the appropriate cutoff for HER2 expression. In breast cancer, T-DXd has been approved across the spectrum of HER2 expression: HER2 positive (HER2 IHC 3+ or HER2 IHC 2+/ISH+), HER2-low (HER2 IHC 2+/ISH− or HER2 IHC 1+), and, most recently, HER2 ultra-low (HER2 IHC 0 with membrane staining). , The current FDA tumor-agnostic approval is only for HER2-positive tumors that are IHC 3+. In metastatic CRC and other GI tumors, effect has been limited in lower thresholds for HER2 expression, but in gynecologic malignancies, there has been a signal at lower HER2 expression levels. , , This difference warrants further study. 

## Determinants of outcomes in precision oncology
Recognizing disease characteristics associated with decreased response can help tailor therapies for individual patients as more options become available. There has also been a framework (ETAC-S) developed to assess clinical trial data from novel therapies to determine tumor-agnostic potential. Here, we discuss a few major determinants of efficacy to consider in precision oncology.

## Histology
Though analysis leading to approval of these agents is based on pooling cancer types, response rates do differ between the histology types to these agents even when they have the same alteration ( ). One of the clearest examples of this was seen in trials of vemurafenib. Though in melanoma this agent had great efficacy with the BRAF V600E mutation with a response rate of 81%, in colorectal cancer, the response rate is much lower at 5%. However, researchers studied this phenomenon in preclinical models that showed that EGFR-mediated reactivation of MAPK can contribute to insensitivity. Thus, this led to combinations with EGFR inhibitors and ultimately FDA approval of the combination of encorafinib and cetuximab. Recognizing histologic types with lower efficacy and studying the mechanisms of resistance can offer therapeutic opportunities.

## Alteration types
The approvals from the FDA are based on having a particular alteration, and sometimes, these can exist on a spectrum that are associated with efficacy. One example outside of these tumor-agnostic indications is targeting exon 20 mutations in EGFR NSCLC. In a study of poziotinib, a TKI, for patients with exon 20 insertions, it was found that the location of the insertion showed difference in efficacy. If the insertion was in the near-loop region, the ORR was 46% versus being 0% if the insertion was in the far-loop region. Studies have shown significant variability between HER2 inhibitors in their activity against different exon 20 alterations. Thus, further analysis on the spectrum of alterations in patients treated with tumor-agnostic agents may help uncover difference in efficacy.

## Co-alterations
These actionable alterations also do not occur in isolation, and co-alterations within the tumor can affect the response. For example, in a phase II trial of trastuzumab and pertuzumab for HER2-amplified heavily pretreated colorectal cancer, the overall response rate was 32%. However, the ORR was 40% in the 56 patients that were KRAS wild-type and 8% if they had a KRAS mutant. For KRAS-mutated lung adenocarcinoma, co-alteration of STK11 is associated with worse outcomes and resistance to immune-checkpoint inhibitor therapy. For entrectinib and larotrectinib, knowing if a patient’s tumor has a solvent-front mutation in addition to the NTRK fusion is important since these mutations confer resistance. Increased use of biomarker testing and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) will likely help elucidate other important co-alterations that determine efficacy as well as mechanisms of acquired resistance.

## Intersection of histology and alterations
The combination of histologic type and alterations can also determine efficacy. For example, in a basket study of the pan-HER kinase inhibitor neratinib, multiple cancer types and different mutation types of HER2 were included. In comparing efficacy, there was large variability based on the combination of histologic type and mutation. This is also seen in the T-DXd results across tumor types and HER2 amplification level. Whereas T-DXd showed efficacy in breast cancer with tumors with HER2 IHC 1+, in colorectal cancer, there was much less efficacy in HER2 IHC 2+/ISH+ versus HER2 IHC 3+. 

## Conclusion and future of tumor-agnostic studies
Tumor-agnostic approvals have given many solid tumor patients access to multiple therapeutic options. This is especially true for more rare tumor types or those that have the actionable alteration less frequently. In most of these trials, the patients with advanced tumors had been exposed to previous treatments, and these agents still showed efficacy. This is especially seen in the case of repotrectinib, offering an option for patients with NTRK-fusion cancers that have been treated with either, or both previously tumor-agnostic approved TKIs in entrectinib and lorlatinib. In addition, further analysis from the trials that led to FDA approval consistently showed continued efficacy.
Though these agents have shown promising efficacy, their approvals and indications do have limitations. As we discussed, small numbers of patients enrolled in certain histologic types can prevent confident assessment of efficacy. In addition, more research needs to be done to elucidate the determinants of efficacy including histologic type, alteration type, and presence of co-alterations. New diagnostic and experimental tools can help in this effort. Incorporating longitudinal ctDNA testing during trials offers a noninvasive way to monitor for important co-alterations and potential resistance mechanisms. In addition, generating preclinical models, such as patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models, can help research these resistance mechanisms. Novel strategies such as coclinical trials can help rapidly evaluate mechanisms of resistances in patients that are reflected in these models and develop potential therapeutic strategies. 
These 9 tumor-agnostic approvals have expanded the treatment landscape for many treatment-refractory advanced solid tumors, and there are many more promising targets being evaluated in multihistology trials ( ). We are hopeful that these efforts will continue to expand effective options for both common and rare tumor types and improve the lives of patients with these advanced cancers.
