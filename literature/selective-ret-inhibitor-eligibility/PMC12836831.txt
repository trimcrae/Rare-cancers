## Abstract

## Background
This study investigates the clinical characteristics, targeted therapy outcomes, co-mutations, and prognosis of lung cancer patients with EGFR exon 19 (ex19) L747 mutations in Northwest China, providing insights for treating these rare mutations.

## Methods
We retrospectively analyzed advanced NSCLC patients with EGFR ex19 L747 mutations, identified from January 2020 to August 2024 at Xijing Hospital, using next-generation sequencing for mutation detection. Comprehensive clinical data, including molecular profiles, treatment regimens, and efficacy, were evaluated, accompanied by three illustrative case studies.

## Results
Among ten patients (70.0% female, median age 56.5 years, 80.0% nonsmokers), three had L747P and seven had L747_P753delinsS mutations. Common co-mutations included TP53 (50.0%), RB1 (10.0%), MED12 (10.0%), BRCA1 (10.0%), SMARCA4 (10.0%), and HER2 (10.0%). Treatment involved EGFR-TKIs combined therapy, achieving an objective response rate (ORR) of 20.0%, median progression-free survival (mPFS) of eight months (95% CI, 5-not reached), and median overall survival (mOS) of 32 months (95% CI, 21-not reached). In the L747_P753delinsS group (n = 7), third-generation EGFR-TKIs produced one CR, with an ORR of 14.3%, mPFS of 12 months (95% CI, 6-not reached), and mOS not reached (95% CI, 23.3-not reached). Two L747P patients with TP53 co-mutations receiving afatinib showed PFS of 1.5 and 15 months; those with L747_P753delinsS mutations and TP53 co-mutations on third-generation EGFR-TKIs had PFS of 12 and 16 months.

## Conclusions
EGFR ex19 L747 mutations, particularly L747P, are rare and exhibit heterogeneous responses to varying generations of EGFR-TKIs. L747P shows limited response to first- and third-generation TKIs but improved outcomes with afatinib. L747_P753delinsS mutations favor third-generation TKIs. Larger studies are needed for validation.

## Background
 Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related incidence and mortality globally. According to GLOBOCAN, it accounted for 2.48 million new cases and 1.81 million deaths worldwide in 2022, representing 12.4% of total cancer diagnoses and 18.7% of cancer-related deaths [ ]. Notably, in China, lung cancer caused 1.06 million new cases and 0.73 million deaths in 2022, which constituted 42.7% and 40.1% of the global burden, respectively [ ]. For patients diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), especially those with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene serves as an important therapeutic target. While EGFR mutations occur in 15–20% of LUAD in Caucasian populations, their prevalence in Asian patients is notably higher, reaching 50–60% [ ]. EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have been established as the standard treatment for advanced NSCLC with EGFR-sensitive mutations, such as exon 19 (ex19) deletions and L858R mutations, accounting for 85–90% of all EGFR-driven cases [ , ]. However, rare mutations like the L747 subtype remain insufficiently explored. Unlike classical ex19 deletions, these non-classical variants, specifically L747P and L747_P753delinsS, exhibit distinct therapeutic profiles and clinical outcomes, warranting their classification as a separate clinical entity requiring tailored management.
Specifically, mutations like L747P and L747-P753delinsS exhibit complex and varied interactions with TKI binding sites, leading to challenges in therapeutic applications. These variants are often categorized under the P-loop and α-C helix compression (PACC), highlighting the need for alternative therapeutic approaches [ ]. The scarcity of these mutations in clinical trial populations further exacerbates the evidence gap, complicating clinical decision-making. While some reports suggest resistance to first-generation TKIs, others indicate a variable response, influenced significantly by concurrent mutations such as TP53 or HER2. Such findings underscore an urgent need for comprehensive investigation and novel therapeutic approaches tailored to these mutations. Therefore, detailed characterization of these uncommon mutations from classical ex19 deletions is crucial for optimizing clinical strategies and advancing precision medicine.
In this study, we investigate the clinical and molecular characteristics, and treatment outcomes of NSCLC patients harboring EGFR ex19 L747 mutations at a single center in Northwest China. By delving into this rare mutation subset, we aim to provide valuable insights that can inform and refine therapeutic strategies, with the potential to guide more personalized and effective treatment protocols for these challenging cases. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist.

## Methods

## Patients and data collection
A retrospective analysis of patients diagnosed with NSCLC by histopathological confirmation at our institution was conducted between January 2020 and August 2024. Patients harboring EGFR ex19 L747-related mutations and receiving EGFR-TKI treatment were included. Comprehensive clinical and molecular data, including demographic characteristics, treatment history, and response to targeted therapy, were retrospectively collected from electronic medical records. This study was conducted in accordance with the declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethical Review Board of Xijing Hospital, Air Force Medical University (No. KY20202105). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to biopsy, covering both genetic testing and future research use.

## Staging and efficacy evaluation
At initial diagnosis, the size of assessable lesions and the clinical stage were determined by positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT), enhanced CT, cranial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and bone emission computed tomography (ECT), following the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system. Treatment efficacy was assessed every six weeks following the initiation of EGFR-TKI therapy, in accordance with the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 [ ]. A complete response (CR) was defined as the disappearance of all target lesions. A partial response (PR) required at least a 30% reduction in the sum of target lesion diameters from the baseline. Stable disease (SD) was observed when changes did not meet criteria for PR or progressive disease (PD), with PD marked by a minimum 20% increase in the sum of diameters of target lesions, an absolute increase of at least 5 mm, or the appearance of new lesions. The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion of patients achieving either CR or PR to treatment. The final follow-up date was Dec 31, 2024. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the initiation of EGFR-TKI treatment until the first occurrence of disease progression or death from any cause, whichever came first. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the initiation of EGFR-TKI treatment to death from any cause.

## DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing
The processes of DNA extraction, library preparation, and sequencing were conducted at Nanjing Geneseeq Technology Inc., a laboratory certified by the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA) and the College of American Pathologists (CAP). Genomic DNA was extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples using the QIAamp DNA FFPE Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Plasma samples were processed by centrifugation at 1800 g for 10 min to separate the plasma; cell-free DNA (cfDNA) was then isolated using the QIAamp Circulating Nucleic Acid Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). For pleural effusion samples, centrifugation at 2500 g for 15 min was used to separate the supernatant from floating cells, after which cfDNA extraction was performed on the collected supernatant. Genomic DNA from white blood cells, serving as a normal control, was extracted with the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). The quality of the extracted DNA was assessed using the Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA), and quantification was carried out using a Qubit 3.0 fluorometer (Life Technologies, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). Sequencing libraries were generated using the KAPA Hyper Prep Kit (KAPA Biosystems, Wilmington, Massachusetts, USA), with procedures optimized based on the specific sample type. Hybridization capture enrichment was applied using either the Pulmocan™ 139-gene lung cancer panel or the GeneseeqPrime™ 425-gene pan-solid tumor panel (Nanjing Geneseeq Technology Inc., Nanjing, China). The enriched libraries underwent sequencing via the Illumina Hiseq4000 platform in alignment with the manufacturer’s protocols.

## Data processing and mutation calling
Sequencing data were demultiplexed and then processed for quality control on FASTQ files using Trimmomatic [ ]. Qualified data with a QC score above 15 and without additional N bases were mapped to human genome 19 (hg19) using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA-mem, v0.7.12; https://github.com/lh3/bwa/tree/master/bwakit). The Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) was employed to recalibrate base quality scores and perform local realignment around insertions and deletions (indels) [ ]. Picard (http://broadinstitute.github.io/picard/) was used to remove PCR duplicates. VarScan2 was employed to detect single-nucleotide variations (SNVs) and indel mutations [ ], with variant calling thresholds established based on the previous study [ ]. Fusions were identified by DELLY using default parameters [ ]. Both mutations and fusions were verified using the Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV) [ ]. Copy number variants (CNVs) were calculated using copy number values adjusted by sample ploidy using CNVkit with a cutoff of fold change ≥ 1.6 and ≤ 0.6 for gain and loss, respectively [ ].

## Statistical analysis
Continuous variables were presented as medians with ranges, while categorical variables were expressed as counts with percentages. Kaplan-Meier survival analyses were performed and compared using the log-rank test, and hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated by Cox proportional hazards model. Diagrams were generated using R version 4.3.3.

## Results

## Patient demographics and baseline clinical characteristics
In this retrospective study, we identified ten patients with NSCLC characterized by EGFR ex19 L747-related mutations. Among them, three patients carried L747-A750 > P (L747P) missense mutations, while seven possessed L747_P753delinsS non-frameshift indel mutations. All patients were diagnosed with stage IV disease, with four categorized as stage IVA and six as stage IVB (Table  ; Fig.  A). The metastatic sites and comprehensive treatment regimens of each patient were described in Table  . The cohort comprised three male patients (30.0%) and seven female patients (70.0%), with a median age at onset of 56.5 years (range, 43–65 years). Notably, common co-mutations were identified in six patients, with TP53 being the most prevalent, present in 50.0% of the patients. Other observed co-mutations included BRCA1, RB1, MED12, SMARCA4, and ERBB2, each occurring at a frequency of 10.0% (Table  ; Fig.  A). All patients received EGFR-TKIs treatment: gefitinib (n = 3), afatinib (n = 2), osimertinib (n = 2), and almonertinib (n = 3). Additionally, seven patients also received chemotherapy including pemetrexed, nab-paclitaxel or pemetrexed/platinum (Table  ; Fig.  A).
 

## Treatment response and survival outcomes
During the follow-up, one patient achieved CR, one had PR, one experienced PD, and seven maintained SD as their best response to initial treatment (Fig.  A). Four patients died. The ORR for the cohort was 20.0%, with a median PFS (mPFS) of eight months (95% CI, 5-not reached months) and a median OS (mOS) of 32 months (95% CI, 21-not reached months, Fig.  B). Patients treated with third-generation EGFR-TKIs generally demonstrated longer survival compared to those receiving other generations of EGFR-TKIs (Fig.  C and D).
For the subgroup of seven patients harboring L747_P753delinsS mutations, the ORR was 14.3% (1/7). The mPFS for this subgroup was 12 months (95% CI, 6-not reached months), while the mOS was not reached, with a 95% CI of 23.3-not reached months (Fig.  E and F). Within this group, five patients were treated with third-generation EGFR-TKIs, all achieving SD as their best response, resulting in an mPFS of 12 months (95% CI, 8-not reached months, Fig.  G). The remaining two patients were treated first-generation EGFR-TKIs, one achieved a confirmed CR (P06) while the other achieved SD (P08), with PFS of 13 and 5 months, respectively. Additionally, within the L747_P753delinsS group, the patients with concurrent TP53 mutations treated with third-generation EGFR-TKIs showed PFS of 12 (P04) and 16 (P05) months, respectively (Table  ).
In contrast, the three patients with L747P mutations demonstrated different outcomes. The mPFS for this subset was five months (95% CI, 1.5-not reached months, Fig.  E), and the mOS was 26.5 months (95% CI, 21-not reached months, Fig.  F). Two patients in this group received afatinib. The first (P01), who had a concurrent a TP53 splice mutation, achieved PR with a PFS of 15 months and an OS of 21 months. The second (P03), who carried a TP53 C238Y mutation, experienced PD with afatinib, with a PFS of just 1.5 months. The third patient (P02) received a first-generation EGFR-TKI, achieving SD, with a PFS of five months and an OS of 32 months (Table  ; Fig.  H).

## Typical cases analysis

## Efficacy of second-generation TKIs in a complex mutation landscape
A female (P01) was diagnosed with stage IVB LUAD (Fig.  A), complicated by concurrent bone and lymph node metastases. Comprehensive genetic analysis of peripheral blood revealed an EGFR ex19 L747P missense mutation with a variant allele frequency (VAF) of 14.9%. The mutational landscape was further characterized by concomitant alterations in TP53 (VAF 7.0%), MED12 (VAF 12.9%), BRCA1 (VAF 1.0%), and SMARCA4 (VAF 6.6%) (Fig.  A). The patient initially received afatinib monotherapy, achieving PR in the primary lesions. However, due to suboptimal control of bone metastases, the treatment was intensified to afatinib combined with pemetrexed, and carboplatin, which was administered every three weeks for six cycles. Subsequently, maintenance therapy with afatinib and pemetrexed was continued for an additional two cycles until the occurrence of PD, as evidenced by CT (Fig.  B). The therapeutic strategy resulted in a PFS of 15 months and an OS of 21 months. Despite the challenging mutational profile, this case illustrates the potential efficacy of second-generation TKIs like afatinib in managing complex EGFR-mutant LUADs.
 

## Low abundance of L747P mutation
Another female patient (P02) was diagnosed with stage IVA LUAD, presenting with multiple lung and pleural metastases. Genetic testing of pleural effusion samples showed a low abundance EGFR ex19 L747P missense mutation with a low VAF of 0.8%, and no additional co-occurring mutations were detected (Fig.  A). This relatively low VAF likely reflects the lower tumor cellularity inherent to pleural effusion samples, rather than indicating a low clonal burden within the tumor biology itself. The initial treatment regimen comprised pemetrexed and cisplatin combined with gefitinib, along with four sessions of hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy. This approach resulted in SD and a PFS of five months. Upon disease progression, the treatment regimen was shifted to a second-line therapy with osimertinib combined with pemetrexed and nedaplatin. This adjustment led to a substantial extension in PFS to 12 months and achieved an OS of 32 months. This case underscores the efficacy of adaptive therapeutic strategies in managing low abundance EGFR L747P mutations in advanced LUAD.

## EGFR L747_P753delinsS and HER2 amplification
A 49-year-old woman (P06) with stage IVA LUAD with pleural metastases in January 2020, harboring an EGFR L747_P753delinsS mutation in peripheral blood. The initial therapy consisted of four cycles of pemetrexed and carboplatin from March to July 2020, in conjunction with gefitinib from March to November 2020. This treatment regimen induced CR with a PFS of 13 months. However, disease progression occurred in April 2021, manifesting as brain metastases characterized by multiple tumors in the bilateral frontal lobes and the left temporo-occipital lobe as shown by MRI (Fig.  C and D). After receiving cranial radiotherapy, the patient was shifted to second-line treatment with osimertinib from May 2021 to May 2022, achieving PR in the lungs and CR in the brain (Fig.  E and F), which extended PFS by an additional 12 months. In May 2022, lesions in the lower pole of the left kidney confirmed metastatic LUAD, and NGS revealed EGFR L747_P753delinsS mutation (VAF 13.3%), ERBB2 p.G776V (VAF 20.3%), ERBB2 p.V777L (VAF 0.4%), ERBB2 amplification (copy number 8.2-fold), and TP53 p.V173L (VAF 4.7%). A third-line regimen comprising osimertinib combined with pemetrexed and carboplatin yielded SD with a PFS of four months. In September 2022, CT scan indicated enlargement of the renal lesion, prompting the initiation of fourth-line treatment with osimertinib and trastuzumab, achieving a PFS of ten months. As of July 2023, imaging showed progression of pulmonary lesions, with a reduction in the size of the renal lesion. The overall assessment was PD, and the treatment was adjusted to a fifth-line regimen of osimertinib combined with nab-paclitaxel, which resulted in a PFS of ten months. On January 31, 2024, further enlargement of the left kidney lesion was observed. The patient was transitioned to sixth-line treatment using renal radiotherapy combined with trastuzumab, which leading to ongoing disease control. At the last follow-up, the patient remained alive, with a survival duration exceeding 59 months. This case highlights the complexity and challenges associated with managing advanced LUAD, particularly in the context of concurrent EGFR L747_P753delinsS mutation and HER2 overexpression.

## Discussion
Our investigation into EGFR L747 mutations within NSCLC patients revealed heterogeneous treatment responses. Notably, while second-generation TKIs like afatinib showed promise, complexities emerged when dissecting co-mutational impacts such as TP53 involvement, further complicating therapeutic pathways and outcomes. Crucially, variants such as L747P and L747_P753delinsS are structurally distinct from classical ex19 deletions and are classified within the PACC subclass. This structural distinction underpins the unique drug sensitivities and clinical outcomes observed in our study. The heterogeneity in treatment regimens reflects the real-world challenges of managing these rare mutations, necessitating strategies adapted to patient-specific needs and evolving therapeutic standards.

## Clinical relevance of EGFR ex19 L747 mutations
EGFR-TKIs are approved for classical and certain rare EGFR mutations, though drug sensitivity varies depending on the structural impact of specific mutations. The MD Anderson classification system categorized EGFR kinase domain mutations into four subclasses: classic-like, T790M-like, PACC mutations, and ex20 insertion [ ]. Among these, EGFR ex19 L747 mutations were categorized under the PACC subclass, known for disrupting TKI binding.
Although uncommon, L747 mutations are clinically significant clinical implications. Data from global repositories such as COSMIC and GENIE report that these mutations occur at low frequencies between 1% and 6% [ , ]. In a retrospective analysis of 3,648 patients with EGFR mutation patients, only 12 (0.59%) were identified with either EGFR L747P or L747S mutations. Another study revealed that among 16,715 NSCLC patients with EGFR mutation, 30.8% had atypical EGFR mutations, with L747P accounting for 1.2% of these and associated with poorer clinical outcomes [ ]. Thus, recognizing the less explored L747 mutations as distinct from classical ex19 deletions can significantly impact clinical decision-making and therapeutic outcomes.
In China, studies have shown a higher prevalence of L747_P753delinsS (6–35%), frequently co-occurring with TP53 or RB1 mutations. A multicenter-retrospective study of advanced NSCLC patients with EGFR ex19 delins mutations among 4,666 EGFR-mutated cases, with L747_P753delinsS representing 34.8%, L747_A750delinsP comprising 13.1%, and L747_T751delinsP making up 5.8% of cases. Concurrent mutations commonly included TP53 (45.8%), PIK3CA (11.6%), and RB1 (16.7%) [ ]. An analysis of 1,607 NSCLC patients in Taiwan revealed that the most frequent deletions within ex19 were delE746-A750 (65.9%), followed by delL747-P753insS (8.1%) and L747-T751 (5.8%) [ ]. Zhou et al. [ ]. showed that L747_P753delinsS accounted for approximately 6.1% of ex19 deletions within the EGFR L747 mutation spectrum.

## Therapeutic responses of different EGFR-TKIs to EGFR ex19 L747 mutations
The therapeutic efficacy of third-generation TKIs is well-documented for common EGFR mutations like ex19 deletions and L858R mutations (19del/L858R) [ – ]. However, their applicability to EGFR ex19 L747 mutations is less defined, mainly because these mutations are often excluded from clinical trials. The MD Anderson classification provides treatment guidance for rare EGFR mutations, yet comprehensive data on the epidemiological characteristics and resistance mechanisms specific to L747 mutations remain limited. The summarized efficacy of various TKIs on these mutations is detailed in Table . Four studies indicated that these mutations confer resistance to first-generation TKIs [ – ], while one suggested potential sensitivity [ ]. Regarding second-generation TKIs, five studies supported sensitivity [ , , – ]. For third-generation TKIs, two studies reported sensitivity to osimertinib [ , ], whereas one suggested resistance [ ].
 Notably, in cases where L747P is accompanied by TP53 Q331, dual resistance to osimertinib and gefitinib was observed [ ], while L747P patients without TP53 mutations respond favorably to osimertinib [ ]. This suggests that TP53 co-mutations may lead to poor efficacy of EGFR-TKIs. Further functional researches on EGFR L747P are needed to clarify its biology and optimize treatment strategies.

## Analysis of patients with EGFR L747 mutation in our center
Our center’s experience with L747 mutants presents diverse TKI responses, underscoring the real-world complexity of treating these mutations. Our analysis of three EGFR L747P cases (all female) showed varied TKI responses: afatinib achieved PR (PFS: 12 months) and PD (PFS: 1.5 months, with TP53 mutation), while gefitinib yielded SD (PFS: five months). Osimertinib demonstrated superior efficacy (PFS: 12 months) in L747P. Among seven L747_P753delinsS cases, third-generation TKIs showed an mPFS of 12 months vs. first-generation of nine months. One HER2 + case responded to osimertinib/trastuzumab (PFS: ten months) but progressed on afatinib. L747 mutations warrant second/third-generation TKIs, with TP53 co-mutations and HER2 alterations influencing outcomes.
These clinical observations highlight the potential necessity of second or third-generation TKIs for managing L747P mutations and point towards the influence of TP53 co-mutations and HER2 alterations on treatment outcomes. Particularly, TP53 co-mutations appear to adversely affect TKI efficacy, while HER2 alterations may require targeted combination therapy strategies. This underscores the importance of personalized therapeutic approaches and the need for comprehensive molecular profiling to inform treatment decisions. Our findings reinforce the value of incorporating novel TKIs and targeted therapies into treatment regimens for optimizing clinical outcomes in this challenging patient population. The observed heterogeneity in treatment regimens reflects the complexities of the real-world clinical setting, where therapeutic decisions are necessarily guided by patient-specific factors such as co-mutations (e.g., HER2 amplification) and prior lines of therapy. While this variability introduces confounding factors that limit definitive survival interpretation, it underscores the critical need for flexible, personalized approaches in managing rare mutations like EGFR ex19 L747, for which standardized protocols remain undefined.

## Future directions and unmet research needs
Despite expanding our understanding of EGFR L747 mutations, the retrospective design and limited cohort size of our study necessitate validation through broader prospective analyses. Furthermore, the emergence of first-line chemo-osimertinib as a new standard of care shifts the clinical context for interpreting monotherapy outcomes. Nevertheless, given the observed suboptimal response of L747P (PACC subclass) to third-generation TKIs, it remains critical to ascertain whether the addition of chemotherapy effectively mitigates resistance or if alternative strategies, such as afatinib-based regimens, offer superior benefit. Prospective, multicenter research could elucidate L747 mutation-specific pathways and treatment responses, offering clarity on survival outcomes and resistance patterns. Additionally, emergent trials exploring novel drug combinations and co-targeting strategies promise to deepen strategic arsenals against these challenging mutations. Exploration of biomarkers for predicting TKI responses could be invaluable, potentially transforming management strategies and improving patient outcomes.

## Conclusions
EGFR ex19 L747 mutations represent a rare subset characterized by therapeutic sensitivity profiles distinct from classical deletions. L747P exhibits intrinsic resistance to first- and third-generation TKIs but improved outcomes with second-generation TKIs such as afatinib, while L747_P753delinsS shows preferential sensitivity to third-generation TKIs. As the standard of care shifts toward chemo-osimertinib, it remains critical to validate whether these new combinations, or specific TKI sequencing, offers the best survival outcomes for these non-classical variants.
