## ABSTRACT
The first global approval of a microsatellite instability‐high solid tumors was a landmark in oncology, paving the way for targeted therapies approved globally. However, guidance remains limited regarding the biological and methodological conditions under which such designs, statistical borrowing, are most effective. We retrospectively evaluated the feasibility of tumor‐agnostic development using Bayesian modeling based on the objective response rate (ORR) as the primary endpoint, focusing on single‐agent molecular targeted therapy (MTT) in Japan. Using Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) approval documents, we identified MTTs approved for ≥ 3 cancer types in Japan between 2001 and 2023. We analyzed whether their ORR in treatment lines without standard therapy exceeded thresholds using the beta‐binomial model (BBM) and hierarchical Bayesian model (HBM). Among 97 approved MTTs, 57 were for solid tumors or sarcomas, and 14 for ≥ 3 indications. Poly (ADP‐ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 antibody‐drug conjugate (HER2 ADC) consistently exceeded thresholds in both models. In contrast, mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitors showed generally consistent results across both models, although some exceeded the threshold while others did not, indicating considerable variability. This study evaluates single‐agent therapies using ORR as the primary endpoint; therefore, the findings may not apply to combination therapies or endpoints such as progression‐free survival or overall survival. Nevertheless, integrating Bayesian models and biological understanding can clarify when statistical borrowing is appropriate, particularly in biologically similar tumor types and improve interpretability and the strategic feasibility of basket trials in tumor‐agnostic development.
Keywords: basket trial, drug development, precision medicine, tumor‐agnostic

## Summary
What is the current knowledge on the topic?
○Basket trials using master protocols offer a promising path for drug development in rare and refractory cancers. However, the biological and methodological conditions under which these designs are most effective remain unclear.

What question did this study address?
○Could molecularly targeted therapies approved for a single indication have been developed using basket trials, based on efficacy data?

What does this study add to our knowledge?
○The study supports the feasibility of master protocols early in oncology development and shows that Bayesian modeling can clarify when statistical borrowing is appropriate, guiding tumor‐agnostic strategies.

How might this change clinical pharmacology or translational science?
○Early application of master protocols, combined with biologically informed statistical models, may reduce development timelines and costs, supporting pan‐tumor strategies and accelerating precision medicine implementation.

1.

## Introduction
The clinical development of anticancer drugs has evolved from cancer‐type‐specific approaches to biomarker‐based tumor‐agnostic development. In 2017, pembrolizumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, became the first drug approved in the United States (U.S.) for solid tumors with high microsatellite instability (MSI‐High) or mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR). Since then, several biomarker‐based tumor‐agnostic therapies for solid tumors have been approved, including neurotrophic receptor tyrosine kinase (NTRK) inhibitors in 2018, tumor mutation burden‐high (TMB‐High) in 2020, von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) in 2021, V‐raf murine sarcoma viral oncogene homolog B (BRAF) V600 inhibitors in 2022, rearranged during transfection (RET) in 2022, and human epidermal growth factor receptor type 2 (HER2)‐positive therapies in 2024. In Japan, similar tumor‐agnostic approvals have followed, including pembrolizumab for MSI‐High and TMB‐High tumors and entrectinib for NTRK fusion‐positive tumors.
Many of these approvals were supported by master protocols, which commonly use the objective response rate (ORR) as the primary endpoint in rare cancers lacking standard treatment options. As demonstrated in the recent report by Hirakawa et al., several regulatory guidelines and case studies utilizing master protocols have been introduced in the United States, the EU, and Japan to actively promote the broader implementation of master protocols [ ]. However, despite the increasing use of ORR as a surrogate endpoint in master protocols, understanding remains limited regarding the biological and methodological conditions under which tumor‐agnostic development using such designs, particularly those involving statistical borrowing, is likely to be effective.
In this study, we retrospectively analyzed molecularly targeted therapies (MTTs) initially developed for individual cancer types and subsequently approved for multiple indications in Japan. Using the beta‐binomial model (BBM) and hierarchical Bayesian model (HBM) [ , ], we estimated the posterior ORR and the probability of exceeding the threshold ORR for these drugs. In addition, we retrospectively examined the feasibility of tumor‐agnostic and efficient clinical development through basket trials using master protocols. Furthermore, we analyzed whether the benefits of basket trials in clinical development varied depending on the drug's mechanism of action and the characteristics of the target disease.
This study also aims to explore how the appropriateness of statistical borrowing may vary depending on biological coherence or heterogeneity. By applying BBM and HBM, we aim to establish a framework for identifying when tumor‐agnostic development is likely to be effective, particularly for therapies with biomarker‐driven and consistent efficacy across tumor types.

## Methods
This study focused on evaluating the antitumor effects of MTTs with specific targets. Using drug information from Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) reports, we compiled a list of MTTs approved in Japan from 2001—the year the first molecular targeted drug was approved—through December 2023. From this list, we extracted drugs approved for the treatment of ≥ 3 cancer types (solid tumors and sarcomas). Hematologic malignancies were excluded because of their small patient population, high degree of subdivision, and diverse treatment options. Immune checkpoint inhibitors were also excluded, as their broad utility has already been validated.
In oncology drug development, targeting functional changes based on rare genetic abnormalities and conducting comparative validation trials with overall survival or similar endpoints as primary outcomes is often challenging. As a result, therapies developed for such rare molecular subtypes are frequently approved based on ORR as the primary endpoint. In this study, we focused on MTTs approved for ≥ 3 cancer types in Japan. For each drug, we extracted the ORR in later‐line treatment settings where no standard therapy existed. Drugs already approved for tumor‐agnostic indications in solid tumors were excluded. ORR data for each drug were obtained from PMDA review reports at the time of approval in Japan. If ORR data were unavailable in the review reports, we retrieved them from related references cited in the reports. For drugs approved for combination therapy, we extracted the ORR for monotherapy. Next, for each indication of the extracted MTTs, we collected ORR data for existing chemotherapy or best supportive care, including placebo, in later‐line settings without standard therapy. These values were used as threshold response rates. The number of patients in clinical trials, trial phases, and changes in standard therapies were not considered. To assess efficacy across indications, we applied BBM and HBM. BBM evaluates each cancer type independently without sharing information across indications and is appropriate when drug response is expected to differ by tumor type. In contrast, HBM allows partial borrowing of information across tumor types, making it suitable when a common molecular mechanism is assumed. By comparing results from both models, we aimed to explore how model selection might influence efficacy interpretation in tumor‐agnostic development.
We estimated the posterior ORR values and the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold ORR. For this study, efficacy was defined as a posterior probability > 90% that the ORR exceeded the threshold ORR, and the analysis was conducted accordingly. This study was based exclusively on publicly available, non‐identifiable data from regulatory sources and did not involve human participants or animals; therefore, ethical approval was not required.

## Results
From 2001 to 2023, a total of 97 MTTs were approved in Japan as new active ingredient‐containing pharmaceuticals. The number of annual MTT approvals increased greatly after the first molecular‐targeted drug was approved in Japan in 2001, with a notable rise from 2012 onwards and a peak in 2021. Among the 97 approved drugs, 57 were indicated for the treatment of solid tumors and sarcomas, whereas 2 were approved for both solid tumors and hematologic malignancies (Figure  ). Among the MTTs approved in Japan, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitors were the most prevalent, with non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) being the most frequently treated disease (Figures  and ). Of the 57 drugs approved for solid tumors and sarcomas, 14 were indicated for ≥ 3 cancer types and were classified into 8 MTT categories (Table  ).
 Excluding MTTs already approved for tumor‐agnostic indications in solid tumors, we calculated the posterior estimates of the ORR for poly (ADP‐ribose) polymerase (PARP), HER2, mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR), and VEGF inhibitors in later‐line settings without standard treatments. To evaluate efficacy, we determined the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate (ORR of existing treatments) using the BBM and HBM. The ORR for HER2‐positive salivary gland cancer and colorectal cancer, as well as VEGF inhibitors as monotherapy for NSCLC, could not be extracted and were therefore excluded from the analysis. In this study, efficacy was defined as a posterior probability > 90% that the ORR exceeded the threshold response rate for each indication. The analysis results are presented in Table  .
 3.1.

## 
PARP Inhibitors
For the PARP inhibitor olaparib, the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate for pancreatic cancer, HER2‐negative breast cancer, ovarian cancer, and prostate cancer ranged from 99.9% to 100% using BBM and 100% using HBM. Both models showed probabilities > 90%. Although niraparib is only indicated for ovarian cancer, its posterior probability of ORR exceeding the threshold response rate was 99.8% in BBM and 99.7% in HBM.

3.2.

## 
HER2 Inhibitors
For the HER2 inhibitor trastuzumab deruxtecan, the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate for HER2‐positive breast cancer, gastric cancer, and NSCLC were 100% in both BBM and HBM, confirming efficacy in all cases.
For HER2‐positive breast cancer, the posterior probabilities exceeded 90% for lapatinib and trastuzumab emtansine in both models. However, for trastuzumab and pertuzumab, the posterior probabilities were < 90% in both models. Notably, among HER2 inhibitors, differences were observed between antibody‐drug conjugate (ADCs) and other drug classes.

3.3.

## 
mTOR Inhibitors
For the mTOR inhibitor everolimus, the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate varied by cancer type. For hormone receptor (HR)‐positive breast cancer, both BBM and HBM yielded probabilities of 100%. However, for neuroendocrine tumors, the probabilities were 84.6% in BBM and 91.3% in HBM, whereas for renal cell carcinoma, they were 0% in BBM and 0.1% in HBM. For temsirolimus, the posterior probabilities for renal cell carcinoma were > 90% in both models.

3.4.

## 
VEGF Inhibitors
For the VEGF inhibitor bevacizumab, the posterior probability of ORR exceeding the threshold response rate was > 90% for ovarian cancer in both models. However, for cervical cancer, the probabilities were 86.4% and 95.3% in BBM and HBM, respectively. For HER2‐negative breast cancer, malignant glioma, and hepatocellular carcinoma, the probabilities were < 90% in both models. For sunitinib, the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate for gastrointestinal stromal tumor, renal cell carcinoma, and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor were 100% in both BBM and HBM. For lenvatinib, the probabilities were 100% for hepatocellular carcinoma, thymic carcinoma, and thyroid cancer in both models. However, for endometrial cancer, the probabilities were < 90% for both models. For regorafenib, the posterior probabilities of the ORR exceeding the threshold response rate were > 90% for gastrointestinal stromal tumors in both BBM and HBM. However, for hepatocellular carcinoma and colorectal cancer, the probabilities were < 90% in both models. For sorafenib, the posterior probabilities of ORR exceeding the threshold response rate were > 90% for renal cell carcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma in both BBM and HBM. However, for hepatocellular carcinoma, the probabilities were < 90% in both models.

3.5.

## Statistical Model Differences and Clinical Considerations
Cases such as the mTOR inhibitor everolimus in neuroendocrine tumors and the VEGF inhibitor bevacizumab in cervical cancer showed that model choice can affect outcomes: HBM yielded higher posterior probabilities than BBM. These results suggest that the differences between BBM and HBM may emerge in tumor types with biological heterogeneity or lacking well‐defined biomarkers. Some indications had probabilities below 90% despite approval, highlighting the limitations of relying solely on ORR‐based models. These cases may reflect clinical benefit demonstrated through other endpoints or in combination therapy. ORR‐based evaluation should be interpreted alongside broader clinical context, including treatment setting and endpoint selection.

4.

## Discussion
This study retrospectively analyzed the feasibility of tumor‐agnostic development using master protocols by analyzing the ORR of PARP, HER2, mTOR, and VEGF inhibitors approved in Japan between 2001 and 2023. We used BBM and HBM to estimate the posterior probability that the ORR exceeded predefined thresholds, demonstrating how statistical borrowing may support development decisions in basket trials.
PARP inhibitors showed consistent efficacy across multiple breast cancer susceptibility gene (BRCA)‐mutated tumor types. Importantly, both BBM and HBM yielded posterior probabilities > 90%, indicating robust efficacy within each individual tumor type and across tumor types when borrowing information using HBM. This suggests that PARP inhibitors may be suitable for both indication‐specific development and basket trial strategies. These results align with characteristics of previously approved tumor‐agnostic drugs (Table  ), which often target molecularly defined subtypes with low prevalence, such as BRCA‐mutated tumors, where conventional trial designs may be challenging due to small patient populations and difficulty in accrual [ ]. PARP inhibitors exemplify how biologically coherent targets can support flexible development strategies across tumor types. Although PARP inhibitors are known to be effective in HRD‐positive cancers beyond ovarian and breast cancers, including prostate and pancreatic cancer, the feasibility of a master protocol for HRD‐positive tumors remains uncertain due to limited data availability.
 In our HER2 analysis, the ADC trastuzumab deruxtecan demonstrated strong and consistent efficacy across HER2‐positive tumors, with 100% posterior probabilities in both BBM and HBM. Notably, this agent was recently approved by the FDA based on a basket trial targeting HER2‐positive rare tumors (Table  ). Although HER2 is expressed in multiple solid tumors, major cancers have established treatment options, and regulatory guidelines often require survival endpoints rather than ORR alone. Given its biomarker‐driven efficacy and broad tumor coverage, a dual development strategy combining indication‐specific trials for common cancers and basket trials for rare ones may be appropriate, supported by both BBM and HBM findings.
mTOR is a crucial regulator of cell growth, division, and protein synthesis, and its excessive activation contributes to cancer progression. Meanwhile, VEGF is a key pro‐angiogenic factor that promotes the formation of new blood vessels and plays a critical role in supporting tumor growth by enhancing vascular supply. Unlike PARP and HER2 inhibitors, mTOR and VEGF inhibitors lack biomarker‐driven specificity. In our analysis, although these agents demonstrated relatively high ORR in some indications, their ORR values were lower and more variable across tumor types than those of PARP and HER2 inhibitors. Among the 30 indications for mTOR and VEGF inhibitors analyzed, 11 failed to exceed the thresholds in both models. The wide variability in response across tumor types for the same agent, and the finding that approximately one‐third of indications had posterior probabilities below 90% in both models despite being approved therapies, suggest that ORR as a single measure may not adequately capture treatment efficacy in biologically heterogeneous tumors. On the other hand, among 17 of the 30 indications for mTOR and VEGF inhibitors exceeded the efficacy threshold in both BBM and HBM. Notably, gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) and renal cell carcinoma showed posterior probabilities exceeding the threshold in both models across multiple VEGF inhibitors. These findings suggest that even within biologically heterogeneous populations, when tumors share common therapeutic targets or pathway‐level mechanisms, both BBM and HBM can yield consistent results. Interestingly, everolimus (mTOR) for neuroendocrine tumors and bevacizumab (VEGF) for cervical cancer, the probability of trial success differed between BBM and HBM. Specifically, whereas BBM results showed posterior probabilities < 90%, HBM results were > 90%. Although this may suggest that HBM can enhance signal detection through information borrowing, these are only 2 cases, and the findings are insufficient to draw generalized conclusions. Further investigation is needed to clarify the underlying biological and statistical factors.
To improve the feasibility of tumor‐agnostic development, it is essential to integrate not only statistical robustness but also biological plausibility and clinical relevance. BBM suits heterogeneous tumor responses, as it assumes no information sharing across tumor types and aligns with scenarios of biological heterogeneity, whereas HBM is appropriate when biological coherence or shared mechanisms justify statistical borrowing. Understanding the characteristics of both models is critical, and applying HBM should consider factors, such as biological commonality, treatment context, regulatory requirements, and trial feasibility. Figure  illustrates this conceptual framework, clearly linking biological evaluation to the selection of either BBM or HBM and the corresponding trial design strategy. Based on our findings, master protocols may be most effective when applied to: (1) rare tumors or subtypes lacking standard therapy, (2) settings with prior knowledge of biomarker‐driven efficacy, and (3) tumor types that share similar molecular mechanisms across indications. These findings suggest that integrating Bayesian approaches with biological understanding can enhance the interpretability and utility of basket trials. This framework could also support regulatory discussions on tumor‐agnostic development using ORR as a surrogate endpoint in rare tumor settings.
 This study has some limitations worth noting. It focused only on ORR in later‐line settings and did not account for trial phases, sample sizes, or dosages. It also included only monotherapy data and did not assess the impact of combination regimens, which may influence response rates. Although all drugs included in this study were ultimately approved, several showed posterior probabilities below 90% in certain tumor types. These discrepancies may reflect clinical benefits supported by endpoints other than ORR, such as progression‐free or overall survival, or by efficacy demonstrated in combination regimens. Since our analysis was limited to monotherapy ORR and excluded combination settings and alternative efficacy endpoints, such differences highlight a methodological limitation of this study. The analysis was also restricted to drugs approved in Japan between 2001 and 2023, which may introduce historical or regional bias. As a retrospective study of approved drugs, it is subject to success bias and may not reflect failed or ongoing programs. These factors should be considered when interpreting the findings and their relevance to current development strategies.

5.

## Conclusion
This study provides evidence that Bayesian modeling using BBM and HBM can effectively support tumor‐agnostic development by identifying when statistical borrowing is appropriate. Therapies with well‐defined molecular biomarkers (e.g., BRCA mutations and HER2) showed consistent efficacy across models, reflecting biological coherence. In contrast, agents with broad, non‐specific mechanisms (e.g., mTOR and VEGF) showed variable results, limiting the utility of borrowing. These findings suggest that integrating Bayesian approaches with biological understanding can enhance the interpretability and utility of basket trials. This framework may help inform more efficient trial design and decision‐making in future tumor‐agnostic drug development.
