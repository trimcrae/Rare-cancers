PM refers to physicians tailoring treatment to patients based on their unique genetic, environmental and lifestyle factors, moving beyond the traditional ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach to personalised care. Governments worldwide have incorporated PM into their strategic planning. In 2015, the US launched the ‘Precision Medicine Initiative’ as a national research project. European countries, including the UK, France, Denmark and Spain, have designed specific strategic plans and initiatives to reinforce PM implementation. Similarly, Asian countries such as Japan and Singapore are also advancing various projects and initiatives to promote PM. Meanwhile, in 2016, the Ministry of Science and Technology of China also issued a special guide for PM research in 2016, namely the China PM Initiative, which emphasises the development of PM, focusing on malignant tumours as the entry point and clinical application as the guidance.
Cancer and the genome are particularly closely related. Over the years, the development of antitumour drugs has gradually transitioned from traditional cytotoxic drugs to molecularly targeted therapies. Current efforts of oncology in PM, in particular, genomic targeted therapies, are focused on expanding indications, overcoming drug resistance and optimising treatment plans. Numerous successful molecularly targeted drugs have emerged, such as crizotinib, an inhibitor of the mesenchymal epithelial transition and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK). Imatinib was the first ALK inhibitor specifically designed based on the molecular structure of its target, approved by the Food and Drug Administration before crizotinib entered the market. The emergence of imatinib, as the first molecular drug for tumours, sparked the development of drugs targeting key molecular characteristics of tumours. Imatinib was first approved by the Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) in 2002. Since then, China has made significant efforts to accelerate the approval and innovation of oncology PM. Up to December 2020, NMPA has approved a total of 49 tumour-targeting drugs on the market, including 14 domestic original drugs. Around the same time, the Ministry of Science and Technology of China also issued a special guide for PM research in 2016, known as the China PM initiative. However, genome-targeted oncology drugs are associated with very high costs in China. For instance, in the second-line treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the cost of atezolizumab ranges between US$14 638 and US$21 879 per person per year, which is far beyond the affordability of an average working-class family in China. A recent economic evaluation of oncology PM in China reported that the reported incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICER) of imported programmed death-1 (PD-1)/PD-ligand 1 inhibitors for the treatment of solid tumours were more than three times the per capita gross domestic product (GDP), rendering them not cost-effective in the Chinese context. The listed target anticancer medications (such as rituximab, trastuzumab and imatinib) in the Zhejiang Provincial reimbursement scheme successfully promoted access and potentially improved patients’ affordability. This suggests that the cost-effectiveness profile of gene-drive drugs warrants further exploration in China. More and more genomic oncology drugs have obtained approval from the NMPA of China. However, the cost-effectiveness of these expensive drugs in such a highly populated, low- and middle-income country is not well understood. This study will be the first to systematically review and quantify the pooled cost-effectiveness profile of PM in China.
The cost-effectiveness of genomic oncology drugs is influenced by various factors, including the prevalence of certain gene alleles, the severity of disease, the accuracy of tests, the related costs and so on. Therefore, it is crucial to map the cost-effectiveness profiles of genome-driven oncology drugs within the context of China. A comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis on this subject could bring the government and society about the economic thinking of target anticancer medications. Furthermore, such a study could provide policy-relevant information for decision-makers and researchers considering investments in oncology drugs.

## Objectives
This protocol describes a study aimed at summarising the cost-effectiveness profiles of genome-driven oncology drugs approved by the NMPA from 2002 to 2023. This study will employ a systematic review and meta-analysis to identify and quantify the sources of value heterogeneity.

## Methods and analysis

## Guideline
The review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols guidelines. The study has been registered in PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (No. CRD42024537386).

## Search strategy
The process of systematic search is conducted in two stages. First, relevant drugs were identified from the China NMPA databases, limiting the search to genome-target oncology drugs which were approved between 1 January 2002 and 31 December 2023. This search strategy, detailed in , employs a combination of keywords on the cost-effectiveness study of oncology precision. Search terms include ‘cost effectiveness OR cost utility OR cost benefit OR economic evaluation’ and ‘Genome-Driven Oncology Drugs (such as rituximab, trastuzumab and imatinib)’. Second, the search strategy (in the English and Chinese versions, respectively) was applied to identify relevant studies in English language databases, including MEDLINE (Oivd) and Embase (Oivd), as well as Chinese language databases, including Chinese Biomedical Literature Database, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, China Science and Technology Journal Database and Wanfang Database. ## Eligibility criteria
The inclusion criteria are based on compliance with the PICOS. Particularly, we will identify Patient (P): cancer patients treated in China; Intervention (I): all the studies with genome-driven oncology drugs approved by NMPA from 2002 to 2023 as an intervention were included for the meta-analysis; Comparator (C): any other targeted or non-targeted conventional therapy; Outcomes (O): costs, life-years (Lys), disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), ICER, net monetary benefit (NMB). ICER and NMB have been routinely used by health technology assessment agencies around the world to summarise the results of economic evaluations of health interventions. The ICER represents the incremental cost of the comparator in contrast to the current treatment, divided by the incremental benefit of the comparator relative to the current treatment, and the NMB is better suited for ranking large numbers of interventions. Study design (S): cost-effectiveness analysis, cost-utility analysis, systematic review on these studies and so forth.

## Data extraction
Data will be independently extracted by two reviewers using Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft Corporation): (1) study characteristics, including author names, publication year and types of funders; (2) population characteristics of the study, including target population types, age distribution, sex and disease types, etc; (3) characteristics of oncology drug treatment, including usage and dosage regimens, treatment cycles and types of drug therapies; (4) features of the analytical model employed, including model type, time horizon, discount rates applied to costs and outcomes and the perspective adopted in the analysis; (5) consideration of equity in health burden and benefits in decision-making; and (6) health-economic outcomes (costs, Lys, DALYs, QALYs, ICER and NMB).

## Outcomes and prioritisation
The extraction of economic parameters associated with genome-driven oncology drugs and the comparators as outcomes of PICOS. ICER serves as the primary outcome of this study.

## Risk of bias and quality assessment
Economic evaluations bias will be used to evaluate potential biases in economic evaluations. It is directed towards model-based economic evaluations, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview of the biases that could occur in both model- and trial-based economic evaluations. This model encompasses a total of 22 biases, of which 11 are specific to model-based economic studies. Each item will be graded as ‘no’, ‘yes’, ‘partly’, ‘unclear’ or ‘not applicable’. This decision was made to specifically evaluate the sources of heterogeneity and bias within the structure and model of economic evaluations related to PM. 

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses will be conducted by Stata Software V.16. Results are considered statistically significant at p<0.05 for meta-analysis and p<0.1 for the subgroup analysis and meta-regression. The robust comparative efficiency research (COMER) approach will be applied to combine cost-effectiveness estimates by pooling NMBs.

## Subgroup analysis and meta-regression
Subgroup analysis will be conducted based on the classification of drug mechanisms (such as targeting v-raf murine sarcoma viral oncogene homolog B1, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 and epidermal growth factor receptor) to identify better trade-offs between effectiveness and costs in pharmacology-relevant subgroups compared with the entire genome-driven oncology drug. Heterogeneity was tested by the I2 statistics, with I2<50% indicating moderate heterogeneity. If I2 ≥50%, there was statistical heterogeneity among the results. We will estimate pooled NMB through a random effects model based on the Laird method and DerSimonian. 
The univariate random-effects meta-regressions will be used to evaluate the durability and definitiveness of the impact of the following variables on NMB, incremental costs or incremental QALYs within each genome-driven oncology drug. These variables include target population (paediatric, adult and elderly), the perspective of economic evaluation, year of publication, type of funders, conflict of interest, intervention/comparator, study results and type of study. Furthermore, to assess publication bias, we will conduct Egger’s test to objectively assess the funnel plot asymmetry. 

## Confidence in cumulative evidence
Two reviewers (DZ and QJ) will independently evaluate the evidence quality, and disagreements between reviewers will be resolved by the third reviewer.
