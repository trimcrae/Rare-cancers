## Introduction
The pleomorphic variant is the least common and consequently, the least known subtype of liposarcomas. It represents <5 % of all liposarcomas and develops during late adulthood.

## Presentation of the case
We report the case of a 77-year-old male who presented with pain in the right iliac fossa and right inguinal region. A CT scan revealed a stony lesion at the level of the cecum. A right hemicolectomy was performed, and the pathology report was positive for pleomorphic liposarcoma. Consequently, he was referred for adjuvant radiotherapy.

## Clinical discussion
Molecular analysis has validated the distinction of liposarcoma subtypes, although histological subtype remains the reliable prognostic parameter in daily practice, recent evidence suggests that genetic profiles may eventually influence risk stratification of individual patients.

## Conclusion
The pleomorphic subtype of liposarcomas is infrequent, and they are very rare in the cecum, so it is currently recommended that patients be evaluated by a multidisciplinary team to make a therapeutic decision as it is a subtype of very aggressive behavior, before initiating any type of oncologic treatment.

## Introduction
Liposarcoma is a tumor of mesodermal origin derived from adipose tissue, it is the most frequent of the sarcomas and the localization in the cecum is rare. The World Health Organization (WHO) subclassifies it according to its histopathological characteristics into five groups: atypical lipomatous neoplasm/well-differentiated liposarcoma [NLA/LBD], dedifferentiated, myxoid, myxoid-pleomorphic and pleomorphic. They can be the result or not of mutations or aberrations in some genes such as MdM2, CDK4 [ , , ]. Searching in google scholar, ScienceDirect and PubMed, using the terms “primary colonic liposarcoma” and “colonic liposarcoma”, we found 11 cases worldwide of liposarcoma in the cecum including 3 myxoid and 8 well-differentiated histological types reported in the world literature. The subtypes are histologically and by molecular analysis distinct from each other, as they vary in biological behavior, ranging from low-grade subtypes that are not metastatic as atypical lipomatous neoplasm/well-differentiated liposarcoma [ALN/LBD] to high-grade liposarcomas with metastatic potential as pleomorphic liposarcoma [ , ]. The pleomorphic variant is the least common and consequently, the least known subtype of liposarcomas. It represents <5 % of all liposarcomas and develops during late adulthood [ ]. Given the rarity of these lesions, there is less cytogenetic and molecular data pertaining to this form of liposarcoma. Cytogenetically, they exhibit genetic rearrangements of complex structures that result in numerous gains and losses similar to other high-grade sarcomas [ , ]. Dysregulation of several tumor suppressor pathways such as P53, Rb1, NF1 commonly occur in this subtype, the only exceptions appear to be pleomorphic liposarcomas that have occurred in association with NLA/LBD. Pleomorphic liposarcomas show two related, clearly distinguishable histologic patterns that can coexist within the same tumor, as both share a disordered growth pattern and an extreme degree of cellular pleomorphism, the more common pattern resembling undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, containing giant lipoblasts with bizarre scalloped, hyperchromatic nuclei, many of which have deeply acidophilic cytoplasm with eosinophilic hyaline droplets [ , , ]. The second pattern of the pleomorphic subtype is composed primarily of sheets of pleomorphic giant cells associated with smaller mononuclear forms; these lipid-rich cells are highly vacuolated along lipoblasts that are easy to identify (in contrast to those of the first type of pleomorphic liposarcoma). Depending on the relative proportions of the two cell populations, this form of liposarcoma can be either a highly anaplastic tumor or a small clear cell tumor resembling a carcinoma or melanoma [ , ]. Tumors with a plethora of small round clear cells called the epithelioid variant of pleomorphic liposarcoma have been identified and are important because of their close similarity to adrenocortical carcinoma. Although these tumors may express keratin, actin, desmin and S-100 protein, they do not express inhibin or manifest ultrastructural features of steroid-producing tumors [ , ]. Pleomorphic liposarcomas are completely high-grade malignant, with local recurrence and metastatic rates of up to 30 % to 40 % with an overall 5-year survival of 55 % to 65 %. At present the treatment of choice remains complete surgical resection and adjuvant treatment with radiotherapy in cases of high-grade sarcomas and positive residual sarcomas. Medical oncology offers more and more alternatives from classical cytotoxic therapies or with drugs such as Palbociclib for tumors with CDK4 amplification [ , , ]. This work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [ ].

## Case report
A 77-year-old male patient came to the outpatient clinic presenting with pain in the right iliac fossa that radiated to the inguinal region and ipsilateral testicle, with a three-week history. He denied weight loss, hemoptysis, changes in bowel habits, bleeding, or urinary symptoms. Regarding family history, his father died of lung cancer, his sister died of cervical cancer, one brother is living with multiple myeloma, and another sister is alive with breast cancer. The patient had a significant history of smoking, consuming 20 cigarettes daily, and underwent a total thyroidectomy for papillary thyroid carcinoma in 2021. On physical examination, his abdomen was tender to mid and deep palpation in the right iliac fossa, with continuous pain but no signs of peritoneal irritation, palpable masses, megaliths, or inguinal lymphadenopathy. Control laboratory tests were ordered and results were within normal parameters. No tumor markers were requested. An abdominal tomography with double contrast revealed a stromal-dependent tumor on the lateral wall of the cecum with surrounding fat infiltration ( , ). A colonoscopy was performed, which showed no evidence of an intraluminal tumor lesion ( ). The ascending colon was mobilized from the peritoneum, revealing a stony lesion at the level of the cecum, strongly adhered to the abdominal wall and involving the pelvic wall and right spermatic cord ( ). The lesion was exposed, and a sample of perilesional tissue was taken for intraoperative study, which identified undifferentiated malignant cells. Consequently, a right hemicolectomy was performed with a latero-lateral ileocolic anastomosis using a mechanical linear stapler, isoperistaltic, along with resection of the spermatic cord and right testicle via an open technique. A radical right orchiectomy was also performed to reduce the risk of locoregional recurrence. No other organs in contact with the tumor were resected. The definitive pathology report confirmed a pleomorphic liposarcoma ( ), featuring yellow/grayish areas, necrotic regions, high mitotic activity (33 mitoses per 10 high-power fields), epithelioid pleomorphic cells with intracellular inclusions, and areas of dense lymphocytic infiltration. The tumor exhibited a histologic grade of 8 (differentiation: 3, mitotic activity: 3, and necrosis: 2), lymphovascular invasion, and a positive circumferential margin. Immunohistochemical staining showed that the tumor cells were negative for CK AE1/AE3, H-caldesmon, CD34, CD31, MDM-2, CDK-4, SOX-10, MART-1, Desmin, DOG-1, and Myogenin. The average Ki-67 proliferation index was 70 %. Based on these results, poorly differentiated carcinoma, adrenal gland carcinoma, melanoma, angiosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, and gastrointestinal stromal tumor were ruled out. According to the TNM classification for intestinal sarcomas, the tumor was classified as Stage C IIIB T4a N0 M0 (American Joint Committee on Cancer [AJCC] Staging System for Soft Tissue Sarcoma of the Abdomen and Thoracic Visceral Organs). The patient was referred for adjuvant radiotherapy.

## Discussion
Molecular analysis, immunohistochemistry and hybridization techniques have improved the ability to validate and distinguish the distinction of liposarcoma subtypes, as well as genetic profiles may eventually influence the risk stratification of individual patients. According to Miao [ ], in 2015, Chinese subjects of the Han race, used another type of diagnostic technique related to the evaluation of MDM2 and FPGS genes, which are linked and associated with the presence of liposarcoma, the latter being linked to a greater susceptibility to the presence of the tumor, as it apparently acts directly on the regulation of folates [ ]. On the other hand, Weaver (2008) [ ] stated that evaluating the presence of MDM2 allows differentiating large masses in the abdomen with other more frequent pathologies; however, for MDM2 and CDK4 it is negative in pleomorphic subtype since they show absence of MDM2 gene amplification through molecular studies [ , ]. The characteristics that should follow to consider the diagnosis of liposarcoma, is that most occur in deep soft tissues, in contrast some benign tumors such as lipomas, which occur in superficial soft tissues, however a liposarcomas exhibit unusual features or combine unaccounted patterns, so pleomorphic liposarcoma is a high-grade sarcoma with a variable number of lipoblasts and with a complex genomic profile, currently the use of p16 has been described in the differential diagnosis of adipocytic neoplasms, especially when differentiating the group of well-differentiated/differentiated liposarcomas from lipomas and other groups of liposarcomas, including pleomorphic liposarcoma [ , ]. The reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 12 and 16, which characterizes most liposarcomas, results in the expression of a number of fusion transcripts that play an important role in oncogenesis [ , ]. Traditionally, great emphasis has been placed on the identification of lipoblasts for the diagnosis of liposarcoma, as the overall pattern and cellular components become the most important determinants in making the diagnosis as to histologic subtype. In the context of a liposarcoma, the lipoblast is a neoplastic cell that to some extent recapitulates the differentiation cascade of normal adipose cells, the first cells arise as pericapillary adventitial cells that closely resemble fibroblasts, these spindle cells, endowed with a large endoplasmic reticulum, slowly acquire fat droplets first at the poles of the cell and then throughout the cytoplasm. As it accumulates in the cytoplasm, the cell loses its endoplasmic reticulum and assumes a round shape, at the same time the nucleus gradually disappears and is pushed to one side of the cell. Pleomorphic cells with the characteristics of lipoblasts already described, have no equivalent in the normal fat differentiation sequence, the criteria that have been used to identify and diagnose these lipoblasts include the following: 1) a hyperchromatic notch or strongly scalloped nucleus, 2) lipid-rich droplets in the cytoplasm, 3) appropriate histologic background [ , , ]. Imaging studies are useful in the diagnosis of pleomorphic liposarcomas, among these, computed axial tomography and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are the most useful, when differentiating a well differentiated liposarcoma from a pleomorphic liposarcoma according to bone destruction and invasion of adjacent tissues [ ]. In MRI, these lesions have a density higher than that of normal fat (close to that of water and muscle) [ , ]. When we use MRI, we can differentiate specific adjacent tissues and, therefore, make a tumor staging. In addition, plain radiography and ultrasound are valid diagnostic tools to use in the nature of the tumor [ , ]. In terms of treatment, anthracyclines, specifically doxorubicin, is usually the most active systemic treatment in this type of sarcoma, however, there are some other targeted therapy options that can be considered such as trabectedin or pazopanib. Another strategy that has taken a relevant role in the decision making process of systemic treatment is next generation sequencing, which allows us to identify in some cases, molecular alterations to provide targeted therapies, for example, identify fusion genes such as NTKR for treatment with Larotrectinib or Entrectinib, the RET gene for treatment with Selpercatinib or even identify the tumor mutational load with >10 mutations/megabase and offer treatment with immunotherapy such as Pembrolizumab or Nivolumab +/− Ipilimumab [ , ]. Specifically speaking of our case report, the patient is currently undergoing adjuvant treatment with external radiotherapy to the surgical bed (right iliac fossa), completing 18 sessions of 180 Gy with Vmat technique, guided by image, in addition to 14 sessions of 2.25 Gy to the right scrotum, in addition to previous preparation of the rectum and bladder, with a scheduled dose of 50.4 Gy/28 sessions to the right iliac fossa and 45 Gy/20 sessions to be completed. In general terms, radiotherapy (RT) is mainly used adjuvantly in high-grade sarcomas, although it can be administered as primary treatment preoperatively or postoperatively. Total RT doses are always determined based on tissue tolerance. Newer RT techniques, such as brachytherapy, intraoperative RT (IORT) and intensity-modulated RT (IMRT), have given better treatment results in patients with soft tissue sarcoma, since the main advantage of IMRT is its ability to more closely contour the high-dose radiation volume, thus minimizing the volume of high-dose radiation to surrounding normal tissues [ , , ]. In addition, image-guided techniques may allow target volumes to be reduced, further minimizing toxicity. If an R1 or R2 resection is anticipated, the use of clips is recommended in areas of high risk of recurrence, although their marking is generally recommended in all cases that do not involve amputation. In any case, an oncological consultation should always be held to determine the appropriate treatment for each patient [ , ].

## Conclusion
The pleomorphic subtype of liposarcomas is infrequent, and they are very rare in the cecum, so it is currently recommended that patients be evaluated by a multidisciplinary team to make a therapeutic decision as it is a subtype of very aggressive behavior, before initiating any type of oncologic treatment.
