## Abstract

## Background
Autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss (ADNSHL) is characterised by highly clinical and genetic heterogeneity. A variant of POU4F3 is considered the pathogenic cause of ADNSHL, specifically designated as autosomal dominant nonsyndromic deafness 15 (DFNA15). The aim of this study was to explore the genetic variants and molecular pathogenic mechanism for a Chinese pedigree with DFNA15.

## Methods
A Chinese family with deafness underwent physical and otoscopic examinations, hearing measurements, and whole-exome sequencing. Sanger sequencing and segregation analysis were used to confirm the variants in the patients and healthy controls. The pathogenicity of the variants was detected using molecular modelling, immunoblotting and immunofluorescence experiments.

## Results
The patients showed bilateral, late-onset, progressive and sensorineural hearing loss without any other systemic diseases. Whole-exome and Sanger sequencing revealed a novel pathogenic variant, c.288dup, within the POU4F3 gene in the large Chinese family, which entirely cosegregated with the disease phenotype. The variant produced a truncated protein without functional domains, as determined using modelling, and theoretically destroyed the function of POU4F3. Immunofluorescence experiments revealed that the subcellular localisation of the mutant protein differed from that of wild-type protein, which could damage its normal function. In addition, immunoblotting experiments indicated that the mutant had lower protein expression than the wild type.

## Conclusions
We first identified the frameshift variant (c.288dup, p.Ile97Hisfs*75) of POU4F3 associated with DFNA15. This study enriches the mutation spectrum of POU4F3 associated with hearing impairment and would help to illustrate the molecular pathogenic mechanism of POU4F3.

## Background
Hearing loss (HL) is one of the most frequent sensory disabilities in humans, with a prevalence of 1 per 1000 newborns [ ]. This is attributable to both genetic and environmental factors; approximately 50% of all cases are due to genetic factors [ ]. HL can be classified into syndromic HL and nonsyndromic hearing loss (NSHL) based on clinical features. Approximately 70% of all hereditary cases are assigned to NSHL [ ]. NSHL encompasses four hereditary models involving autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X chromosome-linked, and mitochondrial inheritance [ ]. NSHL exhibits extremely high genetic heterogeneity. More than 50 causative genes and 80 loci have been reported for autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss (ADNSHL) (http://hereditaryhearingloss.org/). However, the genetic aetiology of most sensorineural HL remains unclear. Furthermore, the application of traditional Sanger sequencing to discover pathogenic variants is extremely expensive and time-consuming because of the hundreds of candidate genes associated with hereditary HL. Next-generation sequencing overcame these disadvantages, because it is characterised by high throughput and low cost, making it possible to screen nearly all variants and genes associated with hereditary HL.
In 1998, POU4F3 was first described as a disease-causing gene for autosomal dominant nonsyndromic deafness 15 (DFNA15) and was mapped to chromosome 5q31-33[ ]. This gene is expressed specifically in the cochlear and vestibular hair cells [ , ]. It encodes a member of the POU family of transcription factor, comprising 338 amino acids and containing two highly conserved DNA-binding domains: a POU-specific domain and a POU homeodomain, with 179–256 and 277–333 amino acids, respectively [ ]. Both the POU domains play important roles in the function of POU4F3 as a transcriptional regulator. Functional studies have revealed that POU4F3 is essential for the maturation, differentiation, and survival of hair cells but not for the fate of inner ear hair cells [ , ]. POU4F3 is one of the most frequent pathogenic genes in ADNSHL in humans, variants of which result in bilateral, late-onset, and progressive deafness affecting all frequencies [ – ]. In addition, vestibular impairments were also observed in some patients, but the morbidity was low, and the clinical presentations were mild and liable to be ignored [ , ]. To date, 34 heterozygous variants, located mostly in the DNA-binding POU domains of POU4F3 have been reported to lead to DFNA15, with highly variable manifestations in the age of onset, severity of hearing impairment, progression course, and audiogram configuration [ – ].
In the present study, we identified variants in a Chinese family with ADNSHL using whole-exome sequencing (WES) and Sanger sequencing. A novel frameshift variant c.288dup (p.Ile97Hisfs*75) in the POU4F3 gene (NM_002700) was identified as the disease-causing factor for DNFA15, characterised by late-onset progressive hearing impairment. Moreover, we explored the effect of the variant on the predicted protein domain architecture, three-dimensional (3D) structure, protein expression level and subcellular localisation of POU4F3.

## Materials and methods

## Subjects
This study followed the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, and obtained the written informed consents from all participants or guardians. The experimental protocol was authorized by the Ethics Committee of Hunan University of Medicine. A four-generation Chinese family with hereditary HL was recruited in this study (Fig.  ). All 6 patients in this family had a presumable autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance on the basis of the family member’s statements. They were all subject to clinical examinations except for Case Ⅰ-1 in Department of Otorhinolaryngology Head and Neck Surgery, the Affiliated Hospital of Hunan University of Medicine. The medical history was acquired from all participants. After detailed physical and otoscopic examinations, all the individuals received hearing measurements, including pure tone audiometry (PTA), acoustic emittance, distortion product to acoustic emission (DPOAE), vestibular evoked myogenic potentials (VEMP), and auditory brainstem response (ABR) on the basis of standard protocols. Other disorders or ototoxic drug history which can affect auditory were ruled out. The hearing levels were classified as normal range (< 25 dB), mild HL (26–40 dB), moderate HL (41–70 dB), severe HL (71–90 dB), and profound HL (> 90 dB) in accordance with the criteria of American Speech–Language–Hearing Association [ ].
 

## Whole-exome sequencing
Peripheral blood was obtained from the current available cases (Ⅱ−2, Ⅱ−3, Ⅱ−5, Ⅲ−5, and Ⅲ−11) and the several unaffected members (Ⅰ−2, Ⅱ−1, Ⅱ−4, Ⅱ−6, Ⅱ−7, Ⅲ−2, Ⅲ−6, Ⅲ−7, Ⅲ−9, Ⅲ−12, and Ⅳ−3) for the variant detection. Genomic DNA (gDNA) was isolated from peripheral blood leukocytes of all participants as described in the manufacturer’s instructions (Tiangen Biotech Co. Ltd, Beijing, China). WES on two affected individuals (Ⅱ−2 and Ⅲ−11) was performed by the SierraVast Biomedical Technology Co. Ltd-Shanghai, China. Following the manufacturer’s protocol, the exome library was constructed using at least 1.5 μg of genomic DNA for each sample. The eligible genomic DNA was randomly fragmented into an average size of 500 bp by sonication, and the DNA library was pooled and hybridized for aggregation of exons using Agilent SureSelectXT Human All Exon V7 adherence to manufacturer’s suggestions (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA). Subsequently, the captured fragments were amplified, purified and conducted paired-end sequencing in Illumina Novaseq sequencer (Illumina, CA). The average sequencing depth was more than 100 × for each sample. Using Fastx-tools (Hannon Lab, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory) (http://hannonlab.cshl.edu/fastx_toolkit/), low quality reads were removed. The qualified reads were aligned to the hg19/GRCh37 human reference genome sequence (v2009). The single nucleotide variants (SNVs) and insertion–deletions (indels) in the regions of segmental duplications and unassigned chromosomes were ignored in the downstream analyses. After removing the duplicated reads and recalibrating the base quality scores, SNVs and indels were first called using Haplotyper of Sentieon (v202112.05, Home-Sentieon|Enable Precision Data for Precision Medicine) [ , ] for each individual and then jointly called for all of the participants. Then, variant quality score recalibration and hard filter methods were applied to obtain the high-quality variant calls for SNVs and indels. The identified SNVs and indels were annotated with SnpEff software, and online databases, such as ClinVar, OMIM, GnomAD and HGMD. Subsequently, we preferentially screened out for shared variants in the known deafness-causing genes for the two samples. Guideline of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) was utilized to interpret the pathogenicity of the variants, including “pathogenic”, “likely pathogenic”, “uncertain significance”, “likely benign”, and “benign”.

## Sanger sequencing of the implicated gene
By employing the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification and Sanger sequencing, the variants identified in the deafness-related genes via WES were validated. The primers for the potential causative variants were designed with the Primer3 online software (http://bioinfo.ut.ee/primer3/) and synthesized by Genewiz Biotechnology Co., Ltd. PCR amplification was carried out in a 25-μL reaction mixture with 60 ng of genomic DNA, 1.0 μL of each of the forward and reverse primers, and 12.5 μL of 2 × Taq Master Mix (Huiling Biotech Co. Ltd, Shanghai, China). Thermocycling was performed as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturating at 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 56 to 62 °C for 30 s, extension at 72 °C for 30 s, and final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. The PCR products were validated by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis and silver staining, and purified with the CyclePure Kit (OMEGA; Bio-Tek, Doraville, GA). Sanger sequencing was bidirectionally carried out on an ABI PRISM 3730 automated sequencer (Applied Biosystems). The sequencing data were blasted online (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). Two hundred ethnicity-matched unrelated normal controls were sequenced in the same way.

## Molecular modelling
The three-dimensional (3D) molecular model of the human WT and MUT of POU4F3 protein was constructed utilizing SWISS-MODEL, an online homology modelling software (http://swissmodel.expasy.org/workspace/). The intact protein sequence data used in 3D molecular modelling were from the NCBI GenBank (NP_002691.1). We visualized the data from the homology models utilizing Swiss-PdbViewer 4.1.

## Plasmid construction
Overexpression plasmid of human POU4F3 gene (NM_002700) named POU4F3 pcDNA3.1-3xFlag-N (G125684) and POU4F3 288dup gene named POU4F3 p.Ile97fs pcDNA3.1-3xFlag-N were obtained commercially from Youbio Biotech Company (Changsha, China). All the sequences of the plasmid were sequenced and verified by HonorGene (Changsha, China).

## Immunofluorescence
HeLa cells are epithelioid cells with a flat and stretched morphology, which facilitates the clear observation of subcellular localisation of proteins under a fluorescence microscope. This is suitable for verifying subcellular structural alterations caused by mutants. The Hela cells were seeded onto cell crawls of 24-well plates, and when the cells grew to 30–45%, the POU4F3 plasmids were introduced into the Hela cells using Lipo8000 (Beyotime Biotechnology, C0533) transfection reagent. The samples were collected after 36 h of transfection. The cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) three times. Subsequently, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 15 min and washed with PBS. The cells were permeabilized with 0.1% phosphate buffered saline with Tween-20 (PBST) for a period of 10 min. The primary antibody (Flag antibody, EpiZyme, LF303, 1:100) was incubated at 4 °C for overnight. The secondary antibody (goat antimouse antibody, Immunoway, 1:200), was then incubated at room temperature for 1 h. DAPI-stained nuclei were incubated at room temperature for 5 min, and then sealed with 50% glycerol sealer. To prevent movement, the edges of the coverslips were fixed with nail polish. Images were obtained using a laser confocal microscope (LSM980, Carl Zeiss AG).

## Western blot analysis
HEK293T cells are derived from human embryonic renal cells, which have a high transient transfection efficiency (up to 90% or more). They are able to express exogenous proteins in a rapid and large amount. This makes them suitable for verifying the effect of mutants on protein expression levels. The Protein was extracted from the wild-type or mutated POU4F3 plasmids transfected HEK293T cells by RIPA lysis buffer (YSD0100, Yoche) containing protease inhibitor Cocktail (BL507A, biosharp), and the samples were performed ultrasonic treatment. The total proteins (20 μg) were separated in 12.5% concentration of SDS–PAGE gel in Western Running Buffer, and transferred the protein to PVDF membrane. The transferred PVDF membrane was sealed by 5% skim milk at room temperature for 1 h, and incubated with the primary antibody (POU4F3 antibody, Proteintech, 21,509–1-AP, 1:1000; Flag antibody, EpiZyme, LF303, 1:1000; Actin antibody, Abbkine, KTD101-EN, 1:1000) at 4℃ overnight. The secondary antibody (Goat anti-Rabbit/Mouse IgG (H + L) HRP, Abways, AB01, 1:10,000) was incubated at room temperature for 1 h. We detected the protein band with ECL chromogenic solution by enhanced chemiluminescence (ChemiDoc MP Imaging System, Bio-Rad).

## Results

## Clinical manifestation
A four-generation Chinese family presented with nonsyndromic hearing impairment with an autosomal dominant inheritance mode. Six members had similar manifestations of hearing impairment. All patients showed bilateral, late-onset, progressive and sensorineural HL; however, the age at onset varied greatly among these patients, ranging from 17 to 45 years. For example, the proband (Ⅲ−11) recalled that the onset age of HL was 17, and symptoms became more severe over time. PTA examination revealed that the proband had moderate hearing impairment and a downsloping audiogram. The proband wore hearing aids to improve his hearing level; however, their effectiveness was undesirable. PTA examination of all patients in this Chinese family revealed moderate-to-profound hearing impairment. The audiograms were flat to downsloping configurations affected all frequencies (Fig.  ). In addition, the cases II-3, II-5, and the proband usually complained of tinnitus. Notably, a 6-year-old boy in this family (Ⅳ−3) also presented with tinnitus signs at times, but PTA examination showed that his hearing was unaffected at present. The speech recognition scores (SRS) of the affected members were unaffected by intellectual disability. VEMP detection and vestibular bithermal caloric tests showed no obvious dysfunction, although the proband occasionally complained of vertigo. The tympanometry test results of all the participants were entirely healthy. ABR detection in affected individuals was consistent with PTA, revealing moderate-to-profound sensorineural hearing impairment. None of the affected individuals passed DPOAE detection at most or all frequencies on either side of the ears. The examinations results for all unaffected individuals were normal.
 

## WES identified a novel POU4F3 frameshift variant
An average of 11.2 gigabases (Gb) was obtained from the two samples using WES, of which 99% passed the quality evaluation. All qualified raw reads were aligned to the human reference sequence hg19. Single-nucleotide polymorphisms were excluded from the dbSNP137, 1000 Genomes Project, HapMap8 databases and YanHuang1 (YH1) project with a 0.5% cutoff for minor allele frequency [ – ]. We preferentially screened the shared variants in known deafness-causing genes in the two patients. Finally, considering the autosomal dominant mode and clinical features in this family, as well as the evaluation of variant pathogenicity from ACMG, only one heterozygous frameshift variant, c.288dup (p.Ile97Hisfs*75), in exon 2 of POU4F3 was identified. According to the ACMG standards and guidelines, the variant was considered pathogenic and classified as PM2, PVS1 and PP1.

## Validation of causative variants
The heterozygous variant c.288dup in the POU4F3 gene (Fig.  ) was observed in 5 surviving patients and a healthy child (IV-3) using Sanger sequencing; however, it was absent in the other 11 healthy participants in this Chinese family. These findings suggested that POU4F3 c.288dup cosegregates with the deafness phenotype in the family. Owing to hearing impairment caused by variants of POU4F3, which is usually present at a late age, it was still too early for the individual IV-3 to develop HL. Sanger sequencing was performed on 200 ethnically matched normal controls, and the variant was not observed in any of them. The c.288dup variant led to a frameshift variant form the 97th amino acid and created a truncated protein comprising only 170 amino acids, whereas the normal protein consisted of 338 amino acids. The mutant protein lacked both the POU-specific domain and the POU homeodomain of POU4F3 (Fig.  ). In addition, the variant c.288dup was novel, because it had neither been reported in dbSNP, HGMD, Clinvar, or gnomAD before. These results revealed that the variant in POU4F3 was a pathogenic cause for ADNSHL in this Chinese family, rather than a rare polymorphism.
 

## Structural modelling
A 3D molecular model of POU4F3 was constructed for the structural analysis of WT/Mut proteins to predict mutant pathogenicity based on the crystal structure (Fig.  ). The constructed models of the WT and MUT proteins matched the target sequence of POU4F3 (WT residues 1–338, MUT residues 1–170). According to our model, there was 45.03% sequence identity between the target and template proteins, and the constructed molecular model covered 98.2% of the target sequence. We discovered that the mutant POU4F3 structure was incomplete compared to the wild-type protein structure when using Swiss-Pdb Viewer 4.1 software to analyse wild and mutant structures of POU4F3 protein.

## Effect of the POU4F3 variant on the subcellular localisation of protein
Wild-type and mutant (p.Ile97Hisfs*75) POU4F3 plasmids were overexpressed in HeLa cells. The 3X FLAG tag was fused to the N-terminus of the POU4F3 protein, and the subcellular localisation of POU4F3 was detected using immunofluorescence analysis with the FLAG antibody. As illustrated in Fig.  , the wild-type POU4F3 protein was uniquely located in the nucleus, whereas the mutant POU4F3 exhibited a pronounced fluorescence signal in the cytoplasm and a negligible or absent signal in the nucleus. The aforementioned results demonstrated that POU4F3 p.Ile97Hisfs*75 impeded or rendered POU4F3 ineffective as a functional transcription factor in the nucleus, thereby contributing to the pathogenicity of the variant in patients.
 

## Mutant reduced the protein expression of POU4F3
Wild-type and mutant POU4F3 plasmids were introduced into HEK293T cells to detect the expression of mutant POU4F3 protein, and the cells were incubated for 48 h to express the plasmids. The cells were lysed, and the proteins were collected. Western blot analysis was performed using anti-POU4F3, anti-FlAG, and anti-β-actin antibodies. The results (Fig.  ) demonstrated that the wild-type POU4F3 protein was normally expressed at the correct molecular weight in HEK293T cells. In contrast, a weak band was observed with the mutant POU4F3 protein (p.Ile97Hisfs*75), revealing that the expression level of the mutant protein was significantly lower than that of the wild-type protein. The mutant POU4F3 protein might produce a truncated protein with 18 kDa. Reduced expression and truncated structure of the mutant protein could contribute to the pathogenicity in the Chinese family with deafness.
 

## Discussion
Hair cells (HCs) in the inner ear are vital for hearing. HCs convert mechanical vibrations into acoustic electrical signals, which can then be transferred to the auditory cortex through spiral ganglion neurons [ , ]. Damage to HCs of mammals’ cochlea can be a primary cause for sensorineural hearing impairment, and the majority of the hair cell damage is everlasting and irreversible [ – ]. Approximately 50% of deafness cases are caused by genetic factors. Several common deafness genes, including SLC26A4, GJB2, GJB3, and mtDNA12SrRNA, have been identified in Chinese patients [ , ]. POU4F3, also known as BRN3C, was identified in a Jewish Israeli family with ADNSHL in 1998. It is considered a causative gene of DFNA15 [ ]. POU4F3 plays a crucial role in the maturation, differentiation, and maintenance of HCs in the cochlea [ , ]. The targeted deletion of the POU4F3 gene caused loss of all HCs in the cochlea and vestibular system of Brn-3c −/− mice, resulting in complete deafness and balance impairment [ , ]. To date, 34 heterozygous variants of POU4F3 have been reported to lead to DFNA15, of which 30 were discovered in East Asian populations (14 in China, 13 in Japan, and 3 in Korea); the remaining 4 variants were found in Israel (1), the Netherlands (2), and Brazil (1). This revealed that the pathogenic variant in POU4F3 is a significant contributor to ADNSHL, particularly in East Asian populations [ ]. Accordingly, the identification of POU4F3 variants should be a conventional examination for the diagnosis of genetic hearing impairment, particularly in East Asian populations.
In the current study, we discovered a novel frameshift variant (c.288dup) in the POU4F3 gene in a Chinese family with an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern using a combination of WES and Sanger sequencing. These patients exhibited moderate-to-profound sensorineural HL without any other clinical symptoms. Variations in the age at onset and severity of HL in the family revealed a high heterogeneity of the variant.
POU4F3 belongs to the POU superfamily of transcription factors, which link directly to specific DNA sequences to regulate the expression of target genes [ ]. The POU superfamily comprises 14 members, each of which contains two highly conserved DNA-binding domains: the POU-specific domain (amino acids 179–256), and the POU homeodomain (amino acids 277–333), which are the major functional elements [ ]. The variant c.288dup (p.Ile97Hisfs*75) of POU4F3 identified in this Chinese family led to a frameshift variant from the 97th amino acid and generated a truncated protein comprising only 170 amino acids, whereas the normal protein consisted of 338 amino acids. The variant lost both the POU-specific domain and the POU homeodomain (Fig.  ), resulting in the mutant protein lacking DNA-binding capacity, thus causing hearing impairment. We investigated the subcellular localisation of mutant protein compared to that of a wild-type protein control to reveal the functional effect of the novel variant on POU4F3 protein. Using immunofluorescence staining, we discovered that normal proteins were uniquely mapped to the nucleus, whereas mutant proteins were immobilised primarily in the cytoplasm of Hela cells (Fig.  ). POU4F3 harbors two nuclear localisation signals (NLS): a monopartite NLS (amino acids 274–278) and a bipartite NLS (amino acids 314–331) [ ]. Both NLSs play a significant role in trafficking cytoplasmic POU4F3 into the nucleus, and their loss leads to cytoplasmic localisation. As POU4F3 proteins are transcription factors, they are unable to perform their functions until they enter the nucleus and bind to DNA. The variant c.288dup resulted in the absence of the both NLSs, thereby causing alterations in the localisation of mutant POU4F3, which would affect the expression of downstream target genes associated with inner ear hair cell functions, such as Nr2f2, Lhx3, Bdnf, Ntf3, Caprin1, MYO6, and Gfi1 [ ]. A previous study revealed that POU4F3 protein could activate both the Bdnf and Ntf3 promoters, suggesting it may be a key regulatory element of neurotrophic gene expression [ ]. Subsequently, Tornari C et al. found that POU4F3 regulated the LIM domain transcription factor Lhx3 in the auditory system, but not in the vestibular system of HCs [ ]. In 2014, Nr2f2, an orphan thyroid nuclear receptor, was identified as a novel target gene, which may be associated with hair cell development and survival [ ]. Although the molecular mechanisms underlying HL related to POU4F3 variants are not well-understood, previous studies have indicated that haploinsufficiency is the most plausible mechanism [ , ]. Freitas et al. discovered that a complete deletion of POU4F3 led to ADNSHL [ ]. Mutai et al. discovered a frameshift variant of POU4F3 in a Japanese ADNSHL family that generated a prolonged mRNA owing to the lack of a stop codon in the mRNA open reading frame [ ]. Overlong mRNAs may decompose in cells. Western blot analysis revealed that the expression level of the mutant protein p.Ile97Hisfs*75 was much lower than that of the wild-type protein.
The frameshift variant produced a truncated protein, thereby reinforcing haploinsufficiency. We believe that this variant makes POU4F3 a haplotype that can exclusively be interpreted as haploinsufficient. Moreover, the alteration in POU4F3 mutant protein localisation in this study also supports the haploinsufficiency mechanism.
The primary clinical manifestation of DFNA15 is accompanied by postlingual onset and progressive nonsyndromic sensorineural hearing impairment [ , ]. However, there are many differences in the ages of onset, rates of progression and vestibular defects among DFNA15 patients [ ]. For instance, in a Jewish family, affected individuals showed progressive HL with onset ages ranging from 18 to 30 years [ ]. Unlike the Jewish family, some members of a Dutch family with deafness showed vestibular defects [ ]. In this study, the earliest onset age of HL in affected patients was 17 years without vestibular dysfunction (III-11). Intriguingly, a 6-year-old boy in this family (Ⅳ−3) displayed tinnitus signs sometimes; however, PTA examination showed that his hearing was unaffected at present, possibly because it was still too early for him to experience HL. Kitano et al. revealed that POU4F3-related hearing impairments are generally middle- or high-frequency hearing impairments [ ]. In this Chinese family, the audiograms of patients with deafness had flat to downsloping configurations, affecting all frequencies, and the symptoms became increasingly severe with age. These clinical differences suggested the possibility of involvement by other hereditary or environmental factors.

## Conclusions
In summary, we identified a novel variant of ADNSHL in a Chinese family. This is the 34th DFNA15 family globally that contains a novel c.288dup variant in the POU4F3 gene. This variant causes POU4F3 protein dysfunction. This study enriches the mutation spectrum of POU4F3 associated with hearing impairment and helps illustrate the molecular pathogenic mechanism of POU4F3. Furthermore, variants of POU4F3 are not a rare cause of ADNSHL and routine detection of POU4F3 is essential for the diagnosis of hereditary hearing impairment in the future, especially in East Asian populations.
