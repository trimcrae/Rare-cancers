## Abstract
The therapeutic landscape for non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harbouring epidermal growth factor (EGFR) gene mutations is undergoing significant transformation. For classical EGFR mutations such as exon 19 deletion and exon 21 L858R mutation, combination strategies in the first-line setting, based on the results of the MARIPOSA (lazertinib and amivantamab) and FLAURA 2 (platinum-based doublet chemotherapy and osimertinib) trials, provide promising outcomes. Compared to osimertinib monotherapy, they potentially delay both the onset of molecular resistance to treatment and the intracranial progression of the disease. Selecting the best first-line option should take into consideration patient and genome-related factors as well as the burden of the disease including the presence of central nervous system metastases. Beyond first-line therapy, novel agents—including antibody-drug conjugates, bispecific antibodies, and T-cell engagers—have emerged as innovative options for pretreated patients with EGFR-mutated disease. Optimising the treatment sequence in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC is crucial to ensure the best survival outcomes along with the best treatment tolerance and quality of life. Predictive biomarkers are strongly needed as well as biomarker-based escalation and de-escalation clinical trials.

## Key Points

 

## Introduction
Sustained proliferative signalling following oncogene activation is a key hallmark of cancer [ ]. In 2004, Lynch and colleagues showed that primarily small, in-frame, somatic deletions (exon 19) or amino acid substitutions (exon 21) in the tyrosine kinase (TK) domain of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), conferred enhanced sensitivity to gefitinib [ ]. Classical activating EGFR mutations (exon 19 deletion and exon 21 L858R mutation) are seen in 46.6% and 16.3% of patients in Asia and the Western countries, respectively [ ]. Their prevalence is higher in women (43.7% vs 24.0% in men), nonsmokers (49.3% vs 21.5% in past/current smokers) and patients with adenocarcinoma (38.0% vs 11.7% in other histologies) [ ]. Oncogenic EGFR driver mutations have been detected in 18% of normal lung tissue samples and air-pollution–derived particulate matter measuring ≤ 2.5 μm (PM2.5) promotes EGFR-driven NSCLC by recruiting lung macrophages and inducing interleukin-1β release [ ]. The treatment landscape of EGFR-driven non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is rapidly advancing from first- to second- to third-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), administered as monotherapy or combined with chemotherapy. Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) and bispecific antibodies (BsAbs) have joined the war against EGFR-driven lung cancer that is now being “smartly” targeted as early as the initial disease stages. Although survival outcomes of EGFR-mutated NSCLC have significantly improved with newer-generation TKIs and new combinations compared to platinum-based chemotherapy (PBC) and first-generation TKIs, acquired resistance to treatment remains inevitable [ , ]. In addition, the “immigration” of osimertinib, the standard-of-care frontline treatment of advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC into the earlier non-metastatic setting has highlighted the need for other EGFR-targeting treatments in the first-line metastatic setting [ – ]. Optimisation of the first-line treatment is essential to improve survival, tolerance and quality of life. In this review, we summarise the current therapies for advanced EGFR‑mutated (classical exon 19 deletion and exon 21 L858R mutation) NSCLC and address the ongoing debate on the best therapeutic sequence. Owing to constraints on manuscript length, we were unable to include a discussion of uncommon EGFR mutations.

## Evolution of Treatment Landscape in EGFR-Mutated NSCLC

## Definition and Types of EGFR Alterations
Epidermal growth factor receptor also known as ErbB1 or HER-1 is a transmembrane receptor within the receptor TK family. The EGFR family includes four members: EGFR, HER2, HER3 and ErbB4. Each member is composed of an extracellular ligand-binding domain, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular kinase domain [ ]. Upon ligand binding, these receptors form homodimers or heterodimers, such as EGFR-HER2, thus triggering phosphorylation of tyrosine residues on their C-terminal tail and activating downstream signalling pathways. Unlike the wild-type receptor, the mutated EGFR receptor promotes ligand-independent dimerisation and activates key signalling pathways including MAPK, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, PLC gamma/PKC and the JAK/STAT pathways, leading to enhanced deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) synthesis, cell migration and cell proliferation [ ]. Alterations in EGFR, such as mutations (10–50%), amplification (5–15%) and overexpression (40–80%), contribute to the development of lung cancer. The most common EGFR mutations include small in-frame deletions around the conserved LREA motif in exon 19 (residues 747–750) and the L858R point mutation in exon 21, which together account for over 90% of all EGFR kinase mutations [ ]. Additionally, point mutations in exon 18, predominantly at G719 account for about 5% of all EGFR mutations [ ].

## Classical EGFR Activating Mutations

## First- and Second-Generation TKIs
In lung adenocarcinoma, mutations in the kinase domain of EGFR confer increased sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors [ ]. Fifteen years ago, significant responses to the first-generation TKI gefitinib prompted multiple frontline trials comparing single-agent EGFR-TKIs with PBC in advanced NSCLC with common EGFR mutations, namely exon 19 deletions and exon 21 L858R mutations [ – ]. These studies improved objective response rate (ORR) and median progression-free survival (mPFS) but did not significantly extend median overall survival (mOS). Progression from first- to later-generation inhibitors aims to overcome acquired resistance and enhance blood-brain barrier penetration and central nervous system (CNS) efficacy—an essential goal given that up to 50% of patients develop brain metastases within five years and 39% present with intracranial disease at diagnosis [ ].
The ARCHER 1050 study demonstrated the superiority of the second-generation (2nd-gen) EGFR-TKI dacomitinib compared to gefitinib in terms of PFS (mPFS 14.7 vs 9.2 months, respectively, hazard ratio [HR] 0.59) [ ]. There was no OS benefit with afatinib, another 2nd-gen EGFR-TKI, compared to gefitinib, despite improvement of PFS, ORR and time to treatment failure (TTF) [ ].

## Third-Generation TKIs: The FLAURA Era
Osimertinib, an irreversible third generation (3rd-gen) EGFR-TKI, was evaluated in the AURA3 study to target a key mechanism of acquired resistance—namely the T790M secondary mutation in exon 20- found in over 50% of tumours initially sensitive to first- and 2nd-gen EGFR-TKIs [ ]. Following its favourable efficacy in the second-line setting and subsequent regulatory approval, the Phase 3 FLAURA trial was designed to assess osimertinib as a first-line treatment in EGFR-mutated NSCLC [ ]. With a mPFS of 18.9 versus 10.2 months (HR 0.46) and a mOS of 38.6 vs 31.8 months (HR 0.80) along with an improved toxicity profile and an enhanced intracranial activity compared to erlotinib and gefitinib, FLAURA established the role of osimertinib as the preferred first-line option in EGFR-mutated NSCLC [ ]. The fact that only 30% of patients in the control arm received osimertinib at progression prompted the debate on the best treatment sequence in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC [ ]. The Phase 2 APPLE trial compared up-front osimertinib versus a sequential approach (gefitinib followed by osimertinib at progression) in 156 treatment‑naïve EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients. While OS was comparable between arms (high crossover rate), up-front osimertinib led to a significantly reduced risk of brain progression (HR 0.54) compared to the sequential strategy [ ]. Interestingly, the clearance of plasma circulating-tumour DNA (ctDNA) (detected in 60% of patients) at 4 and 8 weeks after treatment start correlated with a longer PFS regardless of the up-front treatment. Conversely, the lack of ctDNA clearance was correlated with poor survival outcomes [ ]. Additional 3rd-gen EGFR-TKIs including aumolertinib, furmonertinib, lazertinib, and rezivertinib, have also been approved outside of Europe [ ]. They are listed in Table . Asandeutertinib (TY-9591), another 3rd-gen EGFR-TKI achieved an intracranial ORR of 92.8% versus 76.1% in the Phase 2 ESAONA study of EGFR-mutated, EGFR-TKI-naïve patients with brain metastases [ ].

 

## Beyond FLAURA

## Resistance is Inevitable
Resistance mechanisms to osimertinib can be divided into 3 main subgroups: EGFR-dependent/on-target (10%), EGFR-independent/off-target (40–50%) and unknown (40–50%) mechanisms of resistance [ , ]. They are illustrated in Fig. . Detecting and targeting an acquired resistance mechanism often requires a new biopsy (liquid, tissue or both) with a comprehensive molecular analysis [ ].
 

## On-Target Resistance
The EGFR-dependent mechanisms refer to the occurrence of novel unique or multiple EGFR mutations (e.g., C797S, S768I, L718V, L792H/V, G796S/C and other) [ ].
The EGFR C797S mutation arises as an acquired resistance mechanism during treatment with 3rd-gen EGFR-TKIs where serine replaces cysteine at position 797, thus disrupting drug binding. To overcome this, fourth-generation TKIs, including allosteric inhibitors and mutant selective covalent TKIs have been developed (e.g., BLU-945, TRX-221, BDTX-1535, BAY 2927099, H002, JIN-A02) and are being evaluated in early-phase clinical trials [ ]. The context in which the C797S develops in relation with other EGFR alleles impacts the response to subsequent treatments [ ]. First-generation EGFR-TKIs such as gefitinib do not require C797 to inhibit EGFR and can be combined with osimertinib as an effective strategy in patients with acquired C797S mutations as long as T790 is either wild-type or mutated but in a trans conformation with C797S [ ].

## Off-Target Resistance
The EGFR-independent mechanisms refer to the activation or upregulation of an alternative pathway such as amplifications in MET (7–15%) or HER2 (1–2%), mutations in HER2 (1%), BRAF (3%), KRAS (3–4%) or PIK3CA (7%), fusions of genes like ALK, RET or BRAF (1–8%) as well as amplifications in proteins involved in cell-cycle control like CCND1, CCND2 or CCNE1 (10%) [ ].
Lineage plasticity is another mechanism of resistance where tumour cells undergo phenotypic changes under selective pressure from targeted therapies or immunotherapy [ ]. Through activation of specific transcription factors like ZEB1 or TWIST-1 and signalling from pathways like transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), tumour cells can acquire mesenchymal features (epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition [EMT]) that confer drug tolerance, reduce dependence on EGFR signalling, increase motility, invasiveness and resistance to apoptosis [ , ]. Histological transformation into small-cell or squamous-cell lung cancer is another feature of lineage plasticity and accounts for approximately 15% of all resistance mechanisms. When phenotype transformation is suspected, a tissue biopsy is recommended whenever feasible [ ]. In a retrospective study, 25% of patients with a triple-positive mutation of EGFR, TP53 and RB1 presented with de novo or transformed small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) [ ]. Compared to de novo SCLC, transformed SCLC has a more aggressive behaviour and a shorter median PFS and OS with chemotherapy [ ].

## MET Amplification
Mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) oncogene alterations (mainly amplification) are the most common mechanism of resistance to osimertinib and affect 15% of patients who progress on first-line osimertinib [ ]. The rationale of combining MET and EGFR inhibitors in EGFR-mutated NSCLC is appealing and many studies have evaluated the combination of EGFR-TKIs with either crizotinib, savolitinib or tepotinib, in later lines [ – ]. Objective response rates ranged between 29 and 68% and mPFS between 4 and 16.6 months [ – ]. Recently, the combination of savolitinib, a highly selective MET-TKI, and osimertinib was superior to PBC in a Phase 3 randomised trial (SACHI) in EGFR-mutated patients who progressed on frontline EGFR-TKI and had a MET amplification [ ]. Median PFS time was longer both in the 3rd-gen EGFR-TKI–naïve and the intention to treat population (6.9 vs 3.9 m, HR 0.32 and 8.2 vs 4.5 m, HR 0.34, respectively) [ ]. Telisotuzumab vedotin, an antibody drug conjugate (ADC) targeting MET, was evaluated in combination with erlotinib in second-line c-MET overexpressing EGFR-mutated disease. The ORR was 30.6% and 52% overall and in c-MET high patients, respectively [ ]. It was also evaluated in combination with osimertinib after EGFR-TKI and PBC, in 38 c-MET overexpressing patients, with an ORR of 50% and a mPFS of 7.4 months [ , ]. Telisotuzumab adizutecan (ABBV-400), another c-Met protein targeting ADC, achieved an ORR of 63%, regardless of c-Met expression, in 41 EGFR-mutated patients who progressed on EGFR-TKI and PBC [ ].
Amivantamab, a bispecific EGFR-MET antibody, was evaluated in two Phase 1 and 1b studies—CHRYSALIS and CHRYSALIS 2—in combination with lazertinib, a 3rd-gen EGFR-TKI. Results were promising in the osimertinib-resistant cohort (after osimertinib and PBC, mPFS 4.5 months, mOS 14.8 months), the cohort with uncommon EGFR mutations and the treatment-naïve cohort. In the latter cohort (20 patients), the ORR was 100% and the mPFS was not reached after 33.6 months of follow-up [ – ].

## The VEGF-VEGFR Pathway
In EGFR-mutated NSCLC, aberrant EGFR signalling promotes vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression through hypoxia-independent pathways, and elevated vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) levels contribute to resistance against EGFR-TKIs [ ]. Combining anti-VEGFR therapy (bevacizumab, ramucirumab) with EGFR-TKIs enhances clinical outcomes and a meta-analysis of eight randomised trials (n = 1528), resulted in a 37% reduction in the risk of progression with no benefit in OS and with increased grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs), thus, highlighting the need for further studies with novel-generation EGFR-TKIs [ ]. The RELAY Phase 3 trial of erlotinib plus ramucirumab or placebo in untreated EGFR-mutated metastatic NSCLC showed no survival advantage with the combination (OS HR 0.98) [ ]. The Phase 2 RAMOSE trial (NCT03909334) tested an up-front addition of ramucirumab (VEGFR2 antibody) to osimertinib. Median PFS time increased from 15.6 to 24.8 months (HR 0.55), albeit with more grade 3 hypertension rates in the VEGFR-inhibitor arm (22.6%) [ ].
The EGFR-mutated tumours are labelled as “immune-cold” tumours and generally show poor responses to immunotherapy [ ]. Several biological factors may contribute to this resistance, including a lower tumour mutational burden, reduced PD-L1 expression (in some subsets), and a less inflamed tumour microenvironment. Additionally, EGFR-mutated NSCLC often presents in never-smokers, who typically exhibit a less immunogenic tumour profile. Most clinical trials evaluating immune checkpoint inhibitors have excluded patients with driver mutations. In a subgroup analysis of a small number of patients with EGFR mutations (124 of 1202) in the IMpower150 trial, atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, carboplatin and paclitaxel (ABCP) was superior to bevacizumab plus carboplatin and paclitaxel in terms of PFS (mPFS 9.7 vs 6.1 months) and OS (mOS not estimable vs 18.7 months) [ ]. A Phase 3 study of ABCP (ATTLAS), followed by maintenance with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab versus pemetrexed plus carboplatin or cisplatin (PC) followed by pemetrexed maintenance, demonstrated a benefit in ORR (69.5% vs 41.9%) and mPFS (8.48 vs 5.62 months, HR 0.62) in the ABCP versus PC regimens with comparable mOS times (20.63 vs 20.27 months, HR 1.01) [ ]. The previous findings suggest that inhibition of VEGF pathway could be the key to sensitise EGFR-mutated tumours to immune checkpoint inhibitors.
Ivonescimab (AK112/SMT112) is a first-in-class bispecific antibody targeting PD-1 and VEGF, with enhanced tumour microenvironment avidity due to its tetravalent structure [ ]. It potentially delays immune resistance through inhibition of the VEGF pathway. The HARMONi-A trial (in China) in EGFR-mutated NSCLC post-EGFR-TKI failure showed improved mPFS with ivonescimab plus chemotherapy compared to placebo plus chemotherapy (7.1 vs 4.8 months, HR 0.46). The benefits were consistent in subgroups of patients with brain metastases and those progressing on 3rd-gen TKIs. Grade ≥ 3 treatment-related AEs were more frequent in the ivonescimab arm (61.5% vs 49.1%) but were mainly due to chemotherapy. Immune-related grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 6.2% versus 2.5% of patients, and VEGF-related AEs in 3.1% versus 2.5% of patients treated with ivonescimab versus placebo [ ]. The HARMONi trial is the ongoing global randomised, double-blind, multicenter, Phase 3 study of ivonescimab or placebo combined with pemetrexed and carboplatin in patients with EGFR-mutated locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC who have progressed on EGFR-TKI treatment (NCT06396065). The latest results were recently presented at the World Conference on Lung Cancer (WCLC) 2025, with a mPFS of 6.8 versus 4.4 months (HR 0.52) consistent across predefined prognostic subgroups and geographic regions and a favourable trend for OS with a HR of 0.79 (p = 0.057, long-term follow-up in North America and Europe still immature) [ ].

## Other Non-Genetic Mechanisms of Resistance
Resistance can arise from drug-tolerant persister (DTP) cells without a genetic mechanism of resistance. Similar to some bacteria, these cells enter a reversible, dormant, slow-cycling state with chromatin remodelling (epigenetic modifications such as the upregulation of the histone demethylase KDM5A), metabolic reprogramming and anti-apoptotic pathway activations [ ]. They can survive drug therapy long enough to resume growth and re-establish a drug-sensitive population (drug-tolerant expanded persisters) upon drug discontinuation [ ]. They can also drive disease relapse/progression through diverse mechanisms of resistance [ , ]. Drug-tolerant persister cells are either present as a subclonal population before treatment start and primed to become tolerant because of intrinsic epigenetic properties (Darwinian clonal selection upon drug-exposure) or a drug-induced population after a random cell fate decision (Larmackian induction) or both [ , ].
Epigenetic modifications such as DNA methylation, histone modifications and non-coding RNA can regulate gene expression without altering the DNA sequence, thus reducing sensitivity to EGFR inhibitors [ , ]. They mainly influence the EGFR mutation status but can also impact bypass activation, EMT and drug efflux [ ].

## FLAURA 2
In a Phase 3 trial, adding pemetrexed and carboplatin chemotherapy to gefitinib in patients with treatment-naïve advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC, significantly prolonged the median PFS (16 vs 8 months) and the OS (not reached vs 17 months) albeit with increased grade 3 toxicities (51% vs 25%) [ ]. The NEJ009 study of gefitinib alone versus gefitinib with chemotherapy reported similar results [ ]. These data supported the hypothesis that the addition of a PBC may extend the benefit provided by osimertinib alone. FLAURA 2 is a Phase 3 trial that randomised 557 treatment-naïve patients with advanced EGFR-mutated (exon 19 deletion or L858R mutation) NSCLC to receive osimertinib with or without pemetrexed and platinum [ ]. The trial reached its primary endpoint with a median investigator-assessed PFS of 25.5 and 16.7 months in the combination arm and monotherapy arm, respectively (HR 0.62) [ ]. The combination was associated with post-progression benefits over osimertinib alone in the time to the first subsequent therapy (30.7 months vs 25.4 months; HR 0.73), second PFS (PFS2) (HR 0.70) and time to second subsequent therapy (HR 0.69) [ ]. The median OS was 47.5 versus 37.6 months in the combination versus monotherapy arm, respectively (HR 0.77), the longest in a global Phase 3 study showing OS benefit in this population [ ]. This 10-month survival advantage occurred despite the fact that 72% of patients in the monotherapy arm had received PBC upon disease progression, thus suggesting that combining osimertinib and chemotherapy from the onset may be the preferred treatment option [ ].
The combination provided the greatest incremental mPFS benefit (8.8 months overall) for patients with L858R mutation (24.7 vs 13.9 months) and those with CNS metastases at baseline (24.9 vs 13.8 months)—factors known to confer a poorer prognosis [ ]. Overall survival benefit was consistent across predefined subgroups including patients with CNS metastases and L858R mutations [ ]. In an exploratory OS analysis presented at The European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) in 2025, the survival benefit favoured the combination versus the monotherapy arm regardless of baseline negative prognostic factors (L858R mutation, CNS disease, liver or bone metastases, TP53 co-mutation, detection of plasma EGFR-mutated ctDNA) and was consistent with the benefit for the overall population [ ]. Patients with undetected plasma EGFR-mutated ctDNA and those with TP53 wild-type status had a better 3-year OS than the rest (in both arms) [ ].
In the CNS full analysis set, the combination arm was superior with a HR for CNS progression or death of 0.58 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.33, 1.01) and 0.67 (95% CI 0.43, 1.04) in patients with and without baseline CNS metastases (trend towards lower risk of developing new CNS disease) [ ]. In the post hoc CNS evaluable-for-response set (presence of ≥ 1 measurable target CNS lesion), CNS ORRs were 88% versus 87% in the combination versus monotherapy arm, respectively (patients received osimertinib in both arms). However, 48% versus 16% of patients in the combination versus monotherapy arm had CNS complete response [ ].
In the combination arm, 77% of the patients completed 4 cycles of carboplatin or cisplatin and there was a long chemotherapy-free period with a median duration of total exposure to osimertinib and pemetrexed of 30.5 and 8.3 months (median of 11 cycles), respectively [ ]. The combination was more toxic than osimertinib alone with 70% versus 34% grade ≥3 AEs (mainly hematologic and gastrointestinal events), and 46% versus 27% serious AEs, respectively. Discontinuation and dose interruptions of osimertinib occurred in 12% versus 7% and 43% versus 19% of patients in the combination arm versus the monotherapy arm, respectively, but had a minimal overall impact on actual exposure to osimertinib [ , ]. The safety profile of the combination arm was consistent with the established profiles of the individual agents. The onset of most AEs in the combination arm was highest during the induction period and diminished over time. Health-related quality of life was maintained in the combination arm but quality-of-life data were not collected beyond the primary analysis so the long-term effect of pemetrexed exposure on quality-of-life is unknown [ ].
Acquired resistance mechanisms in plasma were fewer in the combination arm but broadly similar between treatment arms and comparable to FLAURA results (exploratory analysis) with MET amplification being the most common resistance mechanism (12% vs 11% vs 16% in the combination arm vs osimertinib arm vs osimertinib in FLAURA). No emerging resistance mechanisms were detected in plasma and tumour-specific TP53 status at baseline, while being a negative prognostic factor in both arms, did not influence the PFS benefit of the combination versus the monotherapy arm [ ].
The strengths of FLAURA 2 include a robust well-powered design with a strong comparator arm that is the current standard-of-care, a clinically meaningful OS and PFS improvement that is consistent across different subgroups and a well-validated chemotherapy backbone. Limitations mainly concern the increased toxicity and its potential impact on quality of life (no mature quality of life data published yet). A cost-effectiveness analysis from the perspective of the USA health care system was not in favour of the combination arm but found more favourable cost-effectiveness in patients with L858R mutations and those without baseline CNS metastases [ ].
Similarly to FLAURA2, aumolertinib, a 3rd-gen EGFR-TKI was tested alone or in combination with chemotherapy in patients with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC in presence of tumour suppressor genes including TP53 and RB1 (negative prognostic factors). The ACROSS 2 trial met its primary endpoints with a mPFS of 19.8 versus 16.5 months in the combination and the monotherapy arms, respectively (HR 0.55). No new safety signals were observed [ ].

## MARIPOSA
The MARIPOSA Phase 3 trial compared the combination of intravenous (IV) amivantamab and oral lazertinib to osimertinib or lazertinib monotherapy in 1074 treatment-naïve advanced EGFR-mutated (exon 19 deletion or L858R) NSCLC patients. The mPFS time was longer in the combination arm than in the osimertinib arm (primary endpoint) (23.7 vs 16.6 months, HR 0.7). The subgroup analysis did not reveal any special groups with better benefit from the combination treatment versus osimertinib. As expected, mPFS time was longer in patients with exon 19 deletions (not estimable vs 18.5 months) compared to those with exon 21 L858R mutations (18.4 vs 14.8 months), in Asian (27.5 vs 18.3 months) compared to non-Asian populations (19.1 vs 15.2 months) and in patients without a history of brain metastases (27.5 vs 19.9 months) compared to those with brain metastases (18.3 vs 13 months) [ ]. Median PFS was improved in patients with and without TP53 co-mutations (18.2 vs 12.9 months and 22.1 vs 19.9 months, respectively), with detectable ctDNA at baseline (20.3 vs 14.8 months), with and without ctDNA clearance at cycle 3 Day 1 (24 vs 16.5 months and 16.5 vs 9.1 months, respectively), with and without baseline liver metastases (18.2 vs 11 months and 24 vs 18.3 months) [ ]. The ORR was similar (86% vs 85% in the combination and osimertinib monotherapy arms, respectively) with a longer median duration of response in the combination arm (25.8 vs 16.8 months) [ ]. Median PFS after first subsequent therapy was 30.4 months with the combination therapy (not estimable with osimertinib) [ ].
Overall survival (OS) data were recently presented at the European Lung Cancer Congress 2025 and showed a significant benefit (mOS unreached vs 36.7 months, HR 0.75 [95% CI 0.61, 0.92], median follow-up 37.8 months) in the combination versus osimertinib arm, respectively [ ]. There was a 9% absolute gain in OS at 36 months and it was predicted to exceed one year (exponential distribution) [ ]. The curves crossed at 1 year due to a small excess of early deaths that did not meet proportional hazards assumption. The benefit was seen across all subgroups but there was a trend towards better outcomes in patients with exon 19 deletion and those with brain metastases at baseline. The combination improved intra-cranial (IC) PFS in the latter group (median IC-PFS 25.4 vs 22.2 months, HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.61–1.02) but had the same IC-response rate as osimertinib. Of note, the complete intracerebral response rate was not reported. Close monitoring of brain relapse was only done in patients with brain metastases at baseline. The trial therefore cannot demonstrate a CNS protective effect. The late separation of curves and similarity of IC-PFS and duration of response (DoR) suggested a longer duration of response for existing brain metastases rather than delay in occurrence of new brain metastases or late CNS reseeding.
Known resistance mechanisms, namely MET amplification (4.4% vs 13.6%; p = 0.017) and other EGFR resistance mutations (C797S, L718X, G724X, 0.9% vs 7.9%; p = 0.014) were significantly less frequent in the combination arm versus osimertinib. Acquired TP53 resistance mutations and paired TP53/RB1 loss of function resistance mutations, usually associated with small-cell transformation were also numerically lower with the combination versus osimertinib [ ]. These findings suggest that the combination of lazertinib and amivantamab decreases the emergence of EGFR- and MET-dependent mechanisms of resistance.
However, this benefit comes at the price of significant toxicities with 75% of the patients in the combination arm experiencing grade ≥ 3 AEs compared to 43% of the patients treated with osimertinib [ ]. The main AEs occurred early, were dermatologic events (paronychia 68% vs 28%, rash 62% vs 31%), infusion-related reactions (63% vs 0%) or increased venous thromboembolic events (37% vs 9%) with a probable impact on quality of life during the “honeymoon” period of the disease course. The most common serious AEs were pulmonary embolism (6% vs 2%), pneumonia (4% vs 5%) and deep vein thrombosis (3% vs < 1%) [ ]. Duration of toxicity is still unknown and the daily management of dermatologic toxicities from the combination is a major constraint to the patient and the oncologist.
The first interim analysis of Cocoon, a randomised Phase 2 study, showed a significantly reduced incidence and severity of dermatologic AEs in patients treated with the combination of lazertinib and amivantamab who used a prophylactic dermatologic regimen including oral doxycycline or minocycline twice daily for 12 weeks, daily application of topical clindamycin 1% lotion on the scalp, chlorhexidine 4% on the nails and ceramides-based non-comedogenic moisturiser on the body and face. Grade ≥ 2 dermatologic events in the first 12 weeks were halved (76.5% and 38.6% before and after the Cocoon regimen, respectively). Grade 3 dermatologic events were also reduced (from 8.8% to 4.3%) and there were fewer treatment discontinuations both for dermatologic AEs (1% vs 4%) and any AEs (11% vs 19%) [ ].
With 62% (vs 33%) of thromboembolic events occurring within the first 4 months of treatment in the MARIPOSA trial, primary prophylactic anticoagulation is now recommended for the first 4 months of the treatment combination [ ].
Subcutaneous (SC) amivantamab in combination with lazertinib in patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC that progressed after osimertinib and PBC, was non-inferior to IV amivantamab. Fewer patients in the SC group experienced infusion-related reactions (13% vs 66%) and VTE (9 vs 14%). The risk of VTE among patients receiving prophylactic anticoagulation was 7% in the SC therapy group and 12% in the IV therapy group, compared to 17% and 26%, respectively, in patients who did not receive anticoagulation [ ]. Subcutaneous amivantamab every 4 weeks along with lazertinib achieved a response rate consistent with that of SC amivantamab every 2 weeks along with lazertinib with a 5-fold reduction in administration-related reactions compared with IV amivantamab every 2 weeks in MARIPOSA [ ].

## Emerging Treatments in EGFR-Mutated NSCLC

## Bispecific Antibodies
Bispecific antibodies (BsAbs) are an emerging therapeutic strategy in EGFR-mutated NSCLC. These antibodies are engineered to simultaneously bind two different targets, offering potential advantages over traditional monoclonal antibodies and TKIs, especially in the context of treatment resistance [ ]. Either in monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, they represent a novel and multifaceted approach in the management of EGFR-mutated NSCLC, with the potential to reshape therapeutic strategies beyond conventional TKIs by addressing both tumour-intrinsic and immune-related resistance mechanisms.
In addition to the previously mentioned amivantamab targeting EGFR and MET, and ivonescimab targeting PD1 and VEGF, other BsAbs are being developed and tested in the setting of progression after frontline EGFR-TKIs.
PM8002/BNT327, a BsAb targeting PD-L1 and VEGF-A, was studied in combination with chemotherapy in 64 patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC and achieved an ORR of 54.7% [ ]. The magnitude of benefit increased within the subgroup of high PD-L1 expression (ORR 92.3%) compared to the PD-L1-negative subgroup (ORR 46.4%). Thirty-nine patients (60.9%) experienced grade ≥ 3 treatment-related AEs. Grade ≥ 3 immune-related AEs were reported in 4 patients (6.3%). The safety profile was overall manageable with a low rate of treatment discontinuation [ ].
Cadonilimab, a BsAb targeting PD-1 and CTLA4, is being evaluated in a Phase 2 trial in China, in combination with pemetrexed and anlotinib for treatment of elderly patients with T790M-negative advanced non-squamous NSCLC following resistance to EGFR-TKIs (NCT06277674).

## Antibody-Drug Conjugates
Antibody-drug conjugates have emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy, combining the precision of targeted therapy with the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy [ ]. A variety of tumour antigens are being investigated as potential targets for ADCs following progression on 3rd-gen EGFR-TKIs, either as monotherapy or in combination with other agents [ ].
HER3 belongs to the EGFR family of receptor tyrosine kinases. Its expression is associated with poor outcomes in metastatic NSCLC notably when it is co-expressed with HER2 or EGFR [ ]. Approximately 83% of early-stage NSCLC and 90% of advanced EGFR-mutated tumours express HER3 as a resistance mechanism following prior EGFR-TKI treatment [ ]. Patritumab deruxtecan (HER3-DXd), an anti-HER3 ADC, is the most advanced ADC under investigation in patients with EGFR mutations progressing on osimertinib and PBC. In the Phase 1–2 HERTHENA-Lung01 study, HER3-DXd achieved an ORR of 30% and a mPFS of 5.5 months in heavily pretreated patients, including those with brain metastases (cranial ORR of 37%) [ ]. HER3-DXd was designated as a breakthrough therapy by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) after failure of 3rd-gen EGFR-TKI and chemotherapy. However, the license application was voluntarily withdrawn in May 2025 following the primary analysis of the Phase 3 HERTHENA-Lung02 trial that evaluated HER3-DXd versus PBC in a second-line setting after osimertinib and that failed to demonstrate a statistically significant benefit in OS [ ]. Although statistically significant, mPFS was numerically similar in both arms (5.8 vs 5.4 months, HR 0.77) [ ]. Median intracranial PFS was 5.4 vs 4.2 months, respectively [ ].
Beyond HER3, other targets are also being explored, namely Trophoblast Cell Surface Antigen 2 (TROP2), a transmembrane glycoprotein that functions as a signalling molecule promoting tumour cell proliferation, survival and metastasis. As TROP2 is overexpressed in 64–75% of squamous NSCLC and 23–64% of lung adenocarcinoma, irrespective of oncogenic driver mutations, it represents a valuable therapeutic target for ADC development in lung malignancies [ ].
Datopotamab Deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) is a TROP2-directed monoclonal antibody with an exatecan derivative (topoisomerase I inhibitor) payload, a tetrapeptide-based linker subjected to enzymatic cleavage and a drug-antibody ratio (DAR) of 4 [ ]. The TROPION-lung01 is a Phase 3 randomised trial that enrolled 605 pretreated patients with and without actionable genomic alterations (AGAs), and irrespective of TROP2 expression. Seventy-four percent of patients in the study had non-squamous histology [ ]. Compared to docetaxel, dato-DXd significantly improved mPFS (4.4 vs 3.7 months, HR = 0.75), but not mOS, a dual primary endpoint in the study (12.9 vs 11.8 months, HR 0.94, p = 0.530). Patients with non-squamous NSCLC had the most meaningful clinical benefit (mOS 14.6 vs 12.3 months, HR 0.84). Increased survival benefit was observed among patients with AGAs, especially in EGFR-mutated disease (mOS 15.6 vs 9.8 months, HR 0.65), thus highlighting the need to confirm these results in a larger EGFR-mutated cohort [ ]. A pooled analysis of the TROPION-Lung01 and TROPION-Lung05 studies with a total of 117 patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC, reported an ORR of 42.7%, including 4.3% complete responses [ ]. Median PFS and OS were 5.8 and 15.6 months, respectively. The most common grade ≥ 3 AEs in the Dato-DXd arm were stomatitis (7%), anaemia (4%) and interstitial lung disease (ILD) (4%). On June 23, 2025, the FDA granted accelerated approval to Dato-DXd for adults with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC who have received prior EGFR-directed therapy and platinum-based chemotherapy. In the Phase 2 ORCHARD platform study, the combination of osimertinib and Dato-Dxd administered at 2 dosages of 4 and 6 mg/kg in 69 patients with advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC whose disease progressed on first-line osimertinib, resulted in similar ORRs (4/6 mg: 43/36%) but increased mPFS with the 6 mg/kg dosage (4/6: 9.5/11.7 months). Grade ≥ 3 AEs related to any treatment (4/6 mg: 34/56%) and ILD/pneumonitis (4/6 mg: any grade, 3/15%; grade ≥ 3, 3/6%) occurred more frequently in the 6-mg cohort [ ]. TROPION-Lung14 (NCT06350097) is an ongoing Phase 3, open-label, multicentre, randomised study that will compare osimertinib to osimertinib and Dato-DXd 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks as first-line treatment for EGFR-mutated NSCLC [ ]. TROPION-Lung 15 (NCT06417814) in another ongoing Phase 3 study of Dato-DXd ± osimertinib versus PBC in EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC after progression on osimertinib [ ].
Sacituzumab tirumotecan (Sac-TMT; also known as SKB264/MK-2870) is another TROP2-directed monoclonal antibody with a belotecan-derivative (topoisomerase I inhibitor) payload, a linker subjected to both extracellular pH-sensitive and intracellular enzymatic cleavage and a drug-antibody ratio (DAR) of 7.4 [ ]. Recent Phase 1–2 trials, KL264-01 (43 patients) and SKB264-II-08 (64 patients) revealed promising results in pretreated patients with advanced NSCLC [ ]. The KL264-01 study enrolled 22 EGFR-mutated and 21 EGFR wild-type NSCLC patients and reported an ORR of 40% with a mPFS of 6.2 months [ ]. Post hoc subgroup analyses revealed better outcomes in the EGFR-mutated subset (ORR 55% and mPFS 11.1 months) that were independently supported by results from SKB264-II-08 (ORR 34% and mPFS 9.3 months) [ ]. The presence of EGFR activating mutations increased the internalisation and activity of sac-TMT [ ]. The most common treatment-related AEs were haematologic toxicities (30% and 35% patients with grade ≥ 3 anaemia and neutropenia, respectively). Diarrhea and ILD occurred in 4.4% and 1.1% of patients, respectively [ ]. Based on the previous results, sac-TMT was granted an FDA breakthrough therapy designation in December 2024, in patients with advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC after progression on EGFR-TKI and PBC. In March 2025, the Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) granted marketing authorisation to sac-TMT for the treatment of adult patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC following progression on an EGFR-TKI and PBC. The approval was supported by the updated results of the randomised Phase 2 SKB264-II-08 (Opti-TROP-Lung 03) study in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC progressing on EGFR-TKI and PBC. The ORR (45.1% vs 15.6%), mPFS (6.9 vs 2.8 months, HR 0.3) and mOS (not reached for both groups, HR 0.49) favoured Sac-TMT over standard docetaxel [ ]. Results of the OptiTROP-Lung04 Phase 3 trial of Sac-TMT versus pemetrexed plus PBC in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC progressing after EGFR-TKI therapy (3rd-gen or 1st–2nd-gen without T790M mutation) were recently presented at the European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) 2025 meeting. Median PFS was 8.3 versus 4.3 months in the sac-TMT versus the chemotherapy arms, respectively (HR 0.49). Overall survival was significantly improved with a HR of 0.6 and 65.8% versus 48% of patients alive at 18 months, in the Sac-TMT versus chemotherapy arms, respectively [ , ]. Sac-TMT is currently being compared to chemotherapy in two ongoing Phase 3 trials of patients with advanced EGFR-mutated previously treated NSCLC with AGAs including activating EGFR mutations (MK2870-004, NCT06074588) and with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC after progression on prior EGFR-TKIs (MK2870-009, NCT06305754). Of note, ocular surface toxicity (mostly grades 1–2) has recently been reported in 7.1% of the patients treated with sac-TMT at doses of 4 mg/kg or 5 mg/kg every 2 weeks. Artificial tears for prophylaxis, symptomatic treatment of mild dry eyes and ophthalmologic evaluation are indicated if worsening ocular symptoms occur or do not resolve.

## T-cell Engagers in EGFR-Mutated NSCLC
In addition to exploring the VEGF pathway to transform EGFR-mutated NSCLC tumours into “immune-hot” tumours, the use of T-cell engagers (TCEs) could be a promising solution. They are bispecific molecules that simultaneously bind to a tumour-associated antigen and to receptors like CD3, CD28 on T cells, thereby redirecting T cells to kill cancer cells independently of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) presentation [ ]. REGN7075 is a first-in-class costimulatory EGFR-CD28 BsAb, designed to provide costimulatory signals to enhance T cell activity in EGFR-expressing tumours. REGN7075 triggers CD28 upon binding, thus releasing the costimulatory signal to activate T cells. The tumour cell becomes an antigen-presenting cell. Having demonstrated encouraging preliminary results in patients with non–immuno-responsive microsatellite stable colorectal cancer with mostly grade 1–2 AEs including 1 grade 1 cytokine-release syndrome, REGN7075 is currently being evaluated with or without cemiplimab in a dose expansion cohort of patients with solid tumours including lung cancer (NCT04626635) [ ].

## Vaccines
The EGFR mutations generate tumour-specific antigens that are potentially targetable by personalised peptide-based, DNA and messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) cancer vaccines as monotherapy or more interestingly in combination with TKIs or immunotherapy [ ]. ABO2013 is a mRNA-based cancer vaccine targeting EGFR exon 19 deletion, L858R and T790M mutations that generated a strong adaptive cytotoxic T-cell response against EGFR-mutated tumour cells when injected intramuscularly in murine models [ ]. In a first-in-human dose-escalation study (ChiCTR2300071001), ABO2013 monotherapy (20 patients) or in combination with sintilimab (10 patients) was well tolerated without dose-limiting toxicities (DLT) or grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs. Strong neoantigen-specific immune responses and promising anti-tumour activities were reported [ ].

## Aligning Treatments in the Advanced Setting: What is the Best Sequence?
The treatment sequence in EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC is a “luxury problem” thanks to the multitude of available options, which often lead to heated scientific discussions. The main objective of improved outcomes (PFS, OS) should be in balance with quality of life, cost-effectiveness, later available lines and patient preferences. ## First-Line Treatment Options

## Up-front Combination Versus Monotherapy
Despite being the most commonly prescribed first-line treatment worldwide, osimertinib monotherapy remains suboptimal with around 20% of patients presenting with primary resistance (tumours enriched in TP53 missense alterations and MET amplification) as well as a few patients (13% in FLAURA) remaining on osimertinib for longer than 4.5 years [ , ]. Up-front combination therapy extends OS by 10–12 months by delaying the emergence of acquired resistance mechanisms and should be the standard of care in first-line settings for EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC. The survival improvement observed with osimertinib plus PBC and lazertinib plus amivantamab extends across all prognostic subgroups including those with TP53 alterations and detectable ctDNA. There are no current predictive biomarkers to select patients for either option. Results from FLAURA 2 emphasise the importance of up-front chemotherapy (median total exposure of 8.3 months vs 30.5 months for osimertinib) in improving survival outcomes including CNS control when compared to osimertinib monotherapy (10-month OS benefit despite 72% of patients receiving PBC at disease progression) [ ]. In MARIPOSA, the magnitude of OS benefit was statistically predicted to exceed 1 year, therefore the mOS results of FLAURA 2 appear to be more convincing. In addition, crossover to amivantamab was not allowed at disease progression. Unlike osimertinib and PBC, the CNS protective effect was less clear with the combination of lazertinib and amivantamab. Finally, while treatment tolerance improves after the initial 4 months in FLAURA 2, the toxicity profile of lazertinib and amivantamab as well as the necessity of time-consuming and sometimes expensive supportive care measures, make it the least favourable option according to patient preferences. For all these reasons regarding the risk-benefit ratio as well as patient preferences, we prefer treating EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients with up-front osimertinib and PBC rather than amivantamab and lazertinib. The latter combination might be a better option in patients with de novo MET amplification or overexpression who are prone to develop primary resistance to osimertinib (approximately 2–8% of tumours) [ ]. In addition, since osimertinib is now used in the adjuvant, neoadjuvant and locally advanced settings, the combination of lazertinib and amivantamab will naturally take its place as the first-line treatment option in initially non-metastatic patients who relapse on osimertinib [ – ]. In our opinion, osimertinib monotherapy still retains a role in the first-line setting because it provides the best safety profile among treatment options. Patients consult their oncologist every 3 months during this “honeymoon” phase. Osimertinib monotherapy remains an option in older, fragile patients and good prognostic disease features such as the absence of EGFR mutation in the plasma and TP53 wild-type status.

## Local Consolidative Therapy
Local treatment with either surgery or radiation therapy might improve disease control by eradicating pre-existing resistant sub-clones as those that start to emerge following drug-induced resistance. Results of the PTR-1 randomised Phase 2 trial comparing afatinib with or without primary tumour resection at 12 weeks with maximal regional control and optional treatment of distant metastasis with radiotherapy, were presented at WCLC 2025 [ ]. The 2-year PFS rate was 27.8% versus 8.3% (HR 0.48, 95% CI [0.25–0.93]). Patients with L858R mutations achieved a lower HR (0.38) compared with exon 19 deletions (0.6). Major pathologic response rate was higher in the exon 19 deletion group but did not correlate with PFS [ ]. NorthStar is a Phase 2 randomised trial of osimertinib with or without local consolidative therapy after a 6- to 12-week induction with osimertinib in patients with advanced NSCLC and TKI-naïve EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R mutation (95.2–96.4%) or acquired T790M mutation in the absence of prior 3rd-gen TKI (3.6–4.8%) [ ]. Only 29% of the patients had oligometastatic disease (0–5 metastases). Radiation, surgery or both were performed in 58.9%, 32.1% and 9% of the cases, respectively. There were no grade 4–5 local consolidative therapy-related AEs. Progression-free survival was significantly improved in the experimental arm versus osimertinib alone (mPFS 25.3 vs 17.5 months, HR 0.66) and across disease burden groups. Complete consolidation therapy (vs partial) was a better predictor of PFS than the initial number of metastases. Of note, 59% of patients with polymetastatic disease underwent complete consolidation therapy and had a mPFS of 27.9 months vs 14.5 months for those with partial therapy [ ]. Further studies including data on OS and local treatment in the era of up-front combination therapy are needed.

## Treatment Options in Line 2 and Beyond
Despite the efficacy of first-line treatment options, resistance eventually occurs. A fludeoxyglucose-18 (FDG) positron emission tomography (PET) scan is often indicated at progression when oligo progressive disease is suspected. Liquid and tissue biopsies, when feasible, are also useful to identify potential mechanisms of resistance. The presence of a molecular tumour board is very helpful in this situation to interpret the molecular results and orient patients to clinical trials.

## Oligo Progressive Disease (Local Progression)
Upon progression, 77% of patients have oligo progressive disease with a limited number of progressing sites [ ]. Several retrospective studies have reported an additional median PFS of 6.7–10 months after local interventions for EGFR-mutated NSCLC [ , – ]. In a recent retrospective study of 81 patients, 2% had in-field recurrence (progression within the radiation treatment field) only, 72% had out-of-field recurrence only, and 12% had both [ ].

## Resistance-Matched Treatment
Second-line and later treatments can be tailored to target an underlying resistance mechanism. One example is the addition of a MET-targeting therapy to osimertinib in patients with a MET amplification following progression on osimertinib monotherapy [ ].
Another example of a resistance-matched treatment is that of methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) loss that occurs at baseline and at disease progression (acquired) (15% and 15% of patients treated with osimertinib in the first-line setting in the ELIOS Phase 2 trial) [ ]. Methylthioadenosine phosphorylase is often co-deleted with CDKN2A/B (common co-mutations at baseline that do not increase at progression), thus leading to accumulation of MTA (methylthioadenosine), which can inhibit protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5), a methyltransferase involved in gene expression regulation [ ]. The PRMT5 inhibitors such as oral TNG462 and AMG193 are currently being evaluated in many solid tumours including NSCLC (NCT05732831) [ ].
Since the EGFR C797S mutation mainly disrupts binding of 3rd-gen inhibitors, adding gefitinib to osimertinib may be effective in patients with acquired C797S mutation with T790M or if both C797S and T790M are in trans [ ].
The previously mentioned ORCHARD trial (NCT03944772) is a Phase 2, open-label platform study focused on evaluating personalised second-line treatments for patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC who progress after first-line osimertinib [ ]. Treatment selection is guided by resistance mechanisms identified through tumour or plasma biopsy analysis. The trial aims to tailor therapies to individual tumour profiles and enhance outcomes after targeted therapy failure [ ].
Transformed small-cell lung cancer (5–15% of EGFR-TKI resistance mechanisms) is a rare disease with poor prognosis and limited data both on the efficacy of adding immune checkpoint inhibitors to etoposide and platinum and the utility of continuing the EGFR-TKI along with the chemotherapy. In a recent international retrospective study of 25 EGFR-mutated patients with small-cell transformation, mPFS was 2, 5, and 2 months for those who received chemotherapy alone (12 patients), chemotherapy plus immune checkpoint inhibitors (6 patients), and chemotherapy plus EGFR-TKI (5 patients), respectively [ ]. Median OS was 9.5, 10 and 8 months for patients who received chemotherapy alone, chemotherapy plus immune checkpoint inhibitors, and chemotherapy plus EGFR-TKI, respectively [ ]. The addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors or EGFR-TKIs did not improve survival outcomes [ ]. An interval of more than a year between NSCLC diagnosis and small-cell transformation conferred better outcomes [ ].

## Platinum-Based Chemotherapy Alone, with Osimertinib, Amivantamab or Ivonescimab
Platinum-based doublet chemotherapy was the treatment usually offered after progression on osimertinib monotherapy with a median PFS of 4.2–5.5 months [ , , ]. In patients with non-CNS progression on first-line osimertinib, continuing osimertinib along with PBC was associated with improved PFS (mPFS 8.4 vs 4.4 months, HR 0.43), longer CNS PFS in patients without baseline CNS metastases (CNS protective effect) and numerically improved OS (mOS 15.9 vs 9.8 months, HR 0.71). These results support pursuing osimertinib as a backbone treatment through treatment lines [ ]. MARIPOSA-2 is a Phase 3 study that randomised 657 patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC who progressed on osimertinib, to receive either amivantamab-lazertinib-chemotherapy, chemotherapy or amivantamab-chemotherapy. The dual primary endpoints, PFS of amivantamab-chemotherapy and amivantamab-lazertinib-chemotherapy versus chemotherapy, were reached (6.3 vs 8.3 vs 4.2 months, respectively) with HRs of 0.48 and 0.44, respectively [ ]. Median intracranial PFS was also improved (12.5 and 12.8 vs 8.3 months for amivantamab-chemotherapy and amivantamab-lazertinib-chemotherapy vs chemotherapy, HR 0.55 and 0.58, respectively). Haematologic AEs occurred less frequently in the amivantamab-chemotherapy arm compared to the amivantamab-lazertinib-chemotherapy arm. Interruptions of any study agent, mainly due to haematologic AEs, were reported in 77, 65 and 33 percent of patients in the amivantamab-lazertinib-chemotherapy, amivantamab-chemotherapy and chemotherapy arms, respectively [ ].
Another option is the addition of ivonescimab to PBC, as mentioned previously, based on the HARMONi-A and HARMONi study results with a median PFS of 7.1 and 6.8 months, respectively [ , ].
Platinum-based doublet chemotherapy alone or with ivonescimab can also be used following first-line treatment with lazertinib and amivantamab.

## Amivantamab and Lazertinib
As mentioned earlier, acquired resistance mechanisms in plasma to osimertinib and chemotherapy were fewer but broadly similar to osimertinib alone and comparable to FLAURA results with MET amplification being the most common resistance mechanism [ ]. The combination of amivantamab and lazertinib is an option following chemotherapy and osimertinib based on the results of the cohort A in CHRYSALIS 2 (patients pretreated with TKIs including osimertinib and PBC) with a median PFS of 4.5 months [ ].

## Atezolizumab, Bevacizumab, Paclitaxel and Carboplatin
The previously mentioned four-drug regimen is a potential option after first-line treatment with osimertinib with a reported mPFS of 8.48 months [ ].

## Treatment Options in Line 3 and Beyond

## TROP 2 ADCs
Treatment options after PBC and EGFR-TKI(s) include TROP2 ADCs such as datopotamab-deruxtecan (TROPION-Lung01 study, mPFS 5.8 months in patients with EGFR mutations) and sacituzumab tirumotecan (OptiTROP-Lung03 study, mPFS 6.9 months) [ , ]. These agents could also eventually move upstream to a second-line setting when combined with osimertinib (TROPION-Lung15) or after confirmation of the results of OptiTROP-Lung04 in a western population (MK2870-009, NCT06305754) [ , , ].

## EGFR-TKI Rechallenge
The EGFR-TKI rechallenge is another third-line option with an ORR, a disease control rate and an mPFS of 23.1%, 53.9% and 3.4 months, respectively, in a small Japanese retrospective study [ ]. OCELOT is an ongoing Phase 2 study evaluating a rechallenge with osimertinib in third-line setting after PBC (NCT04335292).

## Docetaxel With or Without Ramucirumab
Docetaxel, with or without ramucirumab, continues to be a standard-of-care treatment option for non-squamous NSCLC after PBC, with an mPFS of 4.5 months for docetaxel alone versus 3 months for the combination, and a mOS of 10.5 for docetaxel alone versus 9.1 months for the combination (HR 0.86) [ ]. Of note, only 2% and 3% of the patients who received docetaxel with ramucirumab and docetaxel with placebo harboured EGFR mutations, respectively [ ].

## How do we Move Forward?
Selecting the best treatment option in the first line setting in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC is essential to ensure patients have the longest survival outcomes along with the best tolerance profile. Predictive and prognostic biomarkers are the key to tailor treatments. Positive plasma ctDNA and a high tumour fraction most often reflect a high burden of disease and thus justify the need for a more intensive treatment. In addition, plasma ctDNA is a dynamic tool to identify patients at high risk of progression following initiation of a new treatment and then tailor subsequent treatments. Uncleared ctDNA as early as 3 weeks on treatment, a negative predictor of outcome on osimertinib in FLAURA and AURA3, could justify the addition of other treatments such as platinum-based chemotherapy as a treatment escalation strategy, currently assessed in the on-going PACE-Lung single-arm investigator-initiated Phase 2 trial (NCT05281406) [ ].
The EGFR-mutated NSCLC could be treated in the same way as Hodgkin’s lymphoma. Patients would receive intensified regimens (osimertinib-chemotherapy or lazertinib-amivantamab, that are similar to escalated BEACOPP regimen in Hodgkin’s lymphoma) or osimertinib monotherapy (similar to ABVD regimen in Hodgkin’s lymphoma) depending on the initial presence of aggressive disease features mostly related to a high tumour burden. Early ctDNA clearance (at 3–4 weeks), like the early interim PET/CT scan in Hodgkin’s lymphoma, would serve as a biomarker to de-escalate or intensify treatment [ , ].
Translational research programmes are also a key element to establish the best treatment strategy. Paired tissue and liquid biopsies before treatment, at best response and at progression help understand the different disease patterns (initially/secondary aggressive disease, indolent disease). They help identify and characterise drug tolerant persister cells that emerge in tumours initially sensitive to treatment, that can either produce drug-sensitive progeny or evolve towards irreversible, acquired resistance mediated by acquired genomic alterations [ ]. Lastly, they help identify mechanisms of resistance to treatment and potential ways to target/bypass them [ ]. Patient-derived lung cancer organoids can serve as in vitro cancer models for “therapeutic screening” and selection of the best treatment sequence [ ].
In the era of artificial intelligence, multidimensional deep learning models combining genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, radiomics, pathomics and other emerging “omics” could be the future method for disease assessment and subsequent treatment selection by providing a holistic view of tumour behaviour [ ].

## Conclusion
The therapeutic arsenal of EGFR-mutated NSCLC is now richer than ever. Treatment intensification with up-front combinations delays resistance and extends OS compared to osimertinib monotherapy. Selecting the optimal first-line treatment in EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC remains complex due to the lack of robust biomarkers. It should take into consideration many factors including patient preferences. These decisions will become easier with the help of translational research and artificial intelligence along with the emerging therapeutic options. The ultimate objective of curing advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC is no longer a distant dream.
