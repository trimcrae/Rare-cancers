## Introduction
Diverse clinicopathologic presentations of mesothelial tumors are recognized in the most recent 2021 WHO classification including well-differentiated papillary mesothelial tumor (WDPMT), mesothelioma in situ (MIS), and diffuse pleural mesothelioma (DPM). This diagnostic spectrum requires clear distinction between DPM, an aggressive tumor, from the indolent WDPMT and preinvasive MIS. Distinguishing reactive mesothelial hyperplasia from epithelioid DPM and chronic fibrous pleuritis from sarcomatoid or desmoplastic DPM is a common challenge in the diagnosis of DPM. In both settings, demonstration of pathogenic genomic alterations associated with DPM can support a diagnosis of malignancy in small biopsy and cytology specimens. – In recent years, ancillary testing to detect molecular alterations has advanced our ability to accurately diagnose mesothelial proliferations in many cases.
Homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/p16INK4a by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) is detected in 50% to 75% of DPM but not in benign mesothelial proliferations. , , , – There are limitations with FISH assays including cost, turnaround time, technical limitations, and availability, particularly in resource-poor locations. CDKN2A/p16INK4a is located at the 9p21 locus with proximity to the 5-methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) gene, which encodes a key enzyme of the methionine salvage pathway. Due to proximity of these genes, co-deletion of MTAP gene occurs in approximately 90% of tumors with homozygous CDKN2A/p16INK4a deletions. MTAP immunohistochemistry (IHC) has been shown in many studies to be a reliable and reproducible marker for distinguishing DPM from benign reactive mesothelial proliferations. – , – MTAP IHC also may have an important role as a biomarker for therapeutic guidance by predicting sensitivity to inhibition of PRMT5 or MAT2A. , – 
In our experience, interpretation of MTAP IHC with the EPR6893 (ab126770; Abcam) monoclonal antibody (mAb) is challenging in many cases. Because accurate interpretation of MTAP IHC results can be critical in the diagnosis of mesothelial tumors, and because variations in antibody performance may impact interpretation in clinical practice, we designed this study to compare the performance of 2 MTAP mAbs. Herein, we investigate the performance of MTAP mAbs EPR6893 and 1813 (NBP2–75730, Novus Biologicals) in clinical samples of DPM and correlate MTAP expression by IHC with 9p21 copy number status determined by allele-specific analysis of NGS data and by CDKN2A FISH in a subset of tumors with discrepant IHC and NGS findings that had material available for FISH testing.

## Materials and Methods

## Patient Selection
Following Institutional Review Board approval at the study institution, patients with DPM were retrospectively identified through a search of institutional pathology archives. Specimens included in the study comprised consecutive clinical samples of DPM diagnosed at the study institution between January 2014 and January 2021, with tissue available for IHC and prior clinical NGS data available. Two thoracic pathologists (CAFA and JLS) rereviewed all cases to adjudicate tumor classification and histologic subtype according to 2021 WHO Classification and to determine tumor purity. Demographic data were extracted from the medical record.

## Targeted DNA Sequencing
All DPM specimens underwent targeted NGS by MSK-IMPACT (detailed methodology described previously , ) to serve as a reference for 9p21 locus status prior to performing MTAP IHC on each tumor. In brief, the MSK-IMPACT assay is a hybridization capture-based NGS platform that sequences the entire coding region and selects noncoding regions of up to 468 genes, including CDKN2A and CDKN2B genes. While MTAP is not specifically targeted in these capture panels, assessment of the 9p21 region can be inferred using covered genes and tiling probes via Fraction and Allele-Specific Copy Number Estimates from Tumor Sequencing (FACETS). , Results of FACETS analysis were correlated with tumor purity, estimated by review of hematoxylin and eosinestained slides corresponding from the block selected for molecular testing. Low tumor purity for this study was defined as <20%, because the recommended minimum tumor purity for optimal FACETS performance is 20%. , 

## Allele-specific Copy Number Analysis
FACETS is an allele-specific copy number analysis pipeline that includes BAM file postprocessing steps starting with library size and GC-normalization, followed by joint segmentation of total and allele-specific signals, and integer copy number calls. FACETS performs a systematic enumeration of allelic-specific read counts from all heterozygous and homozygous SNP loci as well as pseudo-SNPs (nonpolymorphic loci) along target intervals. Integer copy number calls of major and minor alleles of 9p21 were reviewed to distinguish copy number states with the following criteria: diploid state (2,1), loss of heterozygosity (LOH) (1,0), copy number neutral LOH (≥ 2, 0), and homozygous deletion (0,0). All copy number plots were manually reviewed and re-analyzed with refitting parameters as needed. 

## Immunohistochemistry
MTAP IHC was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue (from the same tissue block used for NGS in all cases) using MTAP mAb clone EPR6893 (ab126770; Abcam) at 1:1000 dilution (0.9 μg/mL) and MTAP mAb clone 1813 (NBP2–75731; Novus Biologicals) at 1:2500 (0.2 μg/mL). Positive and negative staining with both antibodies was evaluated with appropriate control tissue. Staining for both clones was performed on all cases using Leica Bond-3 autostaining system (Leica), heat-based antigen retrieval, a high pH buffer solution (AR9640; Leica, Bond Epitope Retrieval Solution 2, 30 minutes), 30-minute primary incubation time, and a polymer detection system (DS9800; Leica, Bond Polymer Refine Detection).
Two thoracic pathologists (CAFA and JLS) interpreted cytoplasmic MTAP expression in tumor cells as retained, lost, or equivocal. Interpretation of each antibody was performed separately for each case, with pathologists blinded to their interpretation for the other antibody and also blinded to 9p21 gene status by NGS. Presence of positive cytoplasmic MTAP staining in stroma and/or immune cells was required in each case to serve as a positive internal control. Retained MTAP expression was defined as immunoreactivity in the cytoplasm of tumor cells that were stronger or similar in intensity to the staining seen in positive internal control. MTAP expression was considered lost when there was a complete absence of immunoreactivity in the cytoplasm of tumor cells (whole tumor or subset) with positive staining preserved in internal control across the tested tissue. Heterogenous staining was defined as staining that is not uniform throughout tumor and internal control in a single tissue section. As previously described, any loss of staining present in a discrete tumor cell population with positive internal control was considered to possibly represent subclonal loss and considered MTAP loss for this study.
Equivocal interpretations included cases with weak “blush” cytoplasmic staining in tumor cells, heterogenous staining of both tumor and internal control, and/or poor internal control (either weak or absent or heterogenous in the tested tissue) ( ). Although several issues contributed to equivocal interpretations in some cases, the predominant issue was documented to categorize the equivocal cases. MTAP IHC with mAB EPR6893 was repeated in all cases with an equivocal interpretation with that antibody.

## Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization
FISH was performed on 9 of 12 cases with discrepant MTAP IHC and NGS findings. Material was not available for FISH in 3 discrepant cases.
FFPE tissue sections of 4-mm thickness with tumor areas marked were used for FISH analysis following standard protocols. CDKN2A (9p21) FISH probe of 222 kb, labeled in orange, was used in combination with a centromere-specific probe for chromosome 9 (CEP9), labeled in green (both from Abbott Molecular). Signal analysis was performed in combination with morphology correlation, with at least 50 interphase cells within the marked tumor area evaluated. Selection of tumor cells for analysis was primarily on those with at least 2 internal control signals of the CEP9 probe. Homozygous deletion of CDKN2A gene was recorded if cells showed no signals for the CDKN2A gene and 2 or more signals for the internal control probe CEP9 ( ). Representative FISH images were captured using a Zeiss fluorescence microscope coupled with Metasystems ISIS software.

## Statistical Analysis
Contingency tables were designed to compare overall performance of MTAP mAbs and copy number calls using Cohen’s Kappa agreement statistics according to the following criteria: 0.01 to 0.2 slight agreement, 0.21 to 0.4 fair agreement, 0.41 to 0.6 moderate agreement, 0.61 to 0.8 strong agreement, and 0.81 to 1.0 almost perfect. All the above analyses used an alpha value of 0.05 to denote statistical significance and were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0 (IBM).

## Results

## Cohort Characteristics
Fifty-six DPM were identified for IHC and NGS profiling. Patient demographics and histologic features of DPM in the study cohort are provided in . Patients were predominantly male (80%; n = 45), with a median age of 72 years (range, 44–87). Tumors included 47 (84%) epithelioid, 7 (13%) biphasic, and 2 (3%) sarcomatoid DPM.

## Next-Generation Sequencing Findings and Tumor Purity in Overall Cohort
NGS profiling of 56 DPM revealed a median of 2 somatic mutations per sample. The median sample coverage was 633x (range: 207–956x) and the median tumor purity was 30% (range, 10%–85%) with 38 cases (68%) showing tumor purity of greater than 20%. Overall, copy number analysis using FACETS demonstrated homozygous deletion of 9p21 in 25 (45%) DPM.

## Evaluation of MTAP Expression With mAb EPR6893 in DPM
Of 56 DPM tested with mAb EPR6893, 19 cases (34%) showed equivocal interpretations, whereas 17 (30%) and 20 (36%) DPM exhibited retained and loss of cytoplasmic MTAP expression, respectively ( ). Distribution of cases with equivocal, retained, and loss of MTAP expression by histologic subtype included 16, 16, and 15 epithelioid DPM; 2, 1, and 4 biphasic DPM; and 1, 0, and 1 sarcomatoid DPM, respectively. MTAP expression (whether equivocal, retained, or lost) in biphasic tumors was the same in both epithelioid and sarcomatoid components. Equivocal interpretations included weak “blush” cytoplasmic staining in 10 cases, poor internal control in 6 cases, and heterogeneous staining in 3 cases ( ). MTAP IHC with mAb EPR6893 was repeated in all 19 equivocal cases. Interpretation with mAb EPR6893 remained equivocal after repeat IHC in all cases tested.

## Evaluation of MTAP Expression With mAb 1813 in DPM
There were no equivocal interpretations in all 56 cases of DPM tested with mAb 1813. Twenty-one cases (37%) showed retained cytoplasmic MTAP expression in tumor cells with mAb1813, whereas 35 (63%) exhibited MTAP loss in tumor cells ( ). MTAP expression (whether retained or lost) in biphasic tumors was the same in both epithelioid and sarcomatoid components. One case with MTAP loss by IHC and homozygous deletion by FACETS demonstrated a subclonal immunohistochemical staining pattern with loss of MTAP expression restricted to a subset of tumor cells. Distribution of cases with retained and loss of MTAP expression by histologic subtype included 20 and 27 epithelioid DPM; 1 and 6 biphasic DPM; and 0 and 2 sarcomatoid DPM, respectively.

## Performance of MTAP mAb 1813 Compared With MTAP mAb EPR6893 in DPM
There were no equivocal cases with mAb 1813. MTAP mAb 1813 showed stronger immunoreactivity overall, reducing nonspecific “blush” background staining often seen with mAb EPR6893, and improvement in intensity of staining of positive internal control, facilitating better distinction in interpreting loss or retained expression ( , and ). Fifteen (79%) and 4 (21%) cases that were equivocal with mAb EPR6893 showed loss and retained MTAP expression with mAb 1813, respectively. The case with subclonal loss of MTAP expression with mAb 1813 was an epithelioid DPM, initially interpreted as equivocal with mAb EPR6893 ( , ).

## Correlation of MTAP (mAb 1813) Expression in DPM with 9p21 Copy Number Status Determined by FACETS and CDKN2A FISH
Results of 9p21 copy number analyses using FACETS and FISH are shown in . Copy number by FACETS was analyzed for all samples, including 38 cases (68%) with greater than 20% tumor purity and 18 cases (32%) with tumor purity of 20% or less, which is the recommended minimum tumor purity for optimal performance.
FISH was performed in a subset of 9 (of 12) cases that showed discrepant MTAP IHC and NGS findings. Three discrepant cases did not have material available for FISH testing. Eleven (92%) of the discrepant cases had low tumor purity resulting in limited sensitivity for FACETS.
Overall, 32 (91%) of 35 cases that showed MTAP loss by IHC with mAb 1813 showed concordant 9p21 homozygous deletion by either FACETS or CDKN2A FISH, whereas 3 (9%) were discordant with homozygous 9p21 deletion not detected by FACETS. In a single case with additional tissue available, FISH also confirmed the lack of CDKN2A homozygous deletion ( ).
Of 21 cases that showed retained MTAP expression by IHC with mAb 1813, 20 (95%) cases were concordant with no 9p21 deletion detected by FACETS and 1 (5%) case was discordant with homozygous 9p21 deletion detected by FACETS. Unfortunately, additional tissue was not available in this case for orthogonal FISH testing to confirm the 9p21 deletion by FACETS ( and ).
In this entire cohort, MTAP IHC with clone 1813 showed excellent performance using combined NGS/FISH results as the gold standard. The agreement was near perfect (κ = 0.85; 95% CI, 0.71–0.99; P < .001). MTAP IHC was 96% sensitive, 86% specific, and 93% accurate for 9p21 homozygous deletion.
Discrepancies between NGS-based copy number calls and MTAP expression by IHC using mAb1813 were mainly observed in samples with low tumor purity (≤20%) ( , ). Particularly, 10 of the 11 DPM with discordant MTAP loss by IHC with mAb 1813 but no 9p21 deletion detected by FACETS had low tumor purity. In contrast to absence of 9p21 deletion by FACETS, FISH confirmed the presence of CDKN2A homozygous deletion in 7 of 8 discordant low-purity cases with residual material for testing, supporting the MTAP loss result by IHC.
As expected, correlation with NGS results was optimal in samples with tumor purity of greater than 20% and agreement was near perfect between MTAP IHC findings and 9p21 copy status by FACETS for these cases (κ = 0.94; 95% IC, 0.84–1.0; P < .001). A single case in this subset showed MTAP IHC loss and no 9p21 homozygous deletion by FACETS; however, biallelic loss of CDKN2A was confirmed by FISH.

## Discussion
We show that MTAP IHC with mAb 1813 performs better than mAb EPR6893 as a marker for detecting homozygous loss of 9p21 in DPM. In our experience, mAb 1813 shows stronger, cleaner, and more homogeneous cytoplasmic staining/loss of staining with good quality of internal control staining across all tissue samples compared with mAb EPR6893, facilitating improved scoring of MTAP expression by IHC with no equivocal interpretations. Improved immunoreactivity with mAb 1813 did not decrease assay specificity, as highlighted by molecular assay results. Although discrepancies between NGS and MTAP IHC were observed mainly in samples with low tumor purity, MTAP IHC using mAb 1813 showed very good sensitivity and specificity for demonstrating 9p21 homozygous deletion detected in DPM when FACETS and CDKN2A FISH results were combined. Our findings suggest that MTAP IHC and NGS may be complementary and highlight the potential role of MTAP IHC in low-purity samples where NGS may have limited sensitivity for the detection of 9p21 homozygous deletion and in low-resource settings where FISH may not be available.
MTAP IHC has been established as a reliable surrogate marker for detecting homozygous deletion of CDKN2A in tumors and has been increasingly used clinically to help distinguish DPM from benign mesothelial proliferations such as chronic fibrous pleuritis or reactive mesothelial hyperplasia. – , – , , , Although consistent definitive interpretation is important for any biomarker used to confirm a diagnosis of a malignant tumor, this is especially true in the setting of mesothelial proliferations given the clinical impact of a diagnosis of DPM due to its aggressive behavior and poor survival. , , With the addition of MIS as a new entity in the 2021 WHO classification, it is essential that MTAP IHC reliably detects homozygous deletion of CDKN2A, because the diagnosis of MIS depends on biomarkers to detect pathogenic alterations to confirm malignant transformation in histologically benign-appearing mesothelial cells.
Our findings show that an antibody clone is important in the performance and interpretation of MTAP IHC. Although mAb EPR6893 has been used in prior studies, , the findings of this study suggest that mAb 1813 may be superior for clinical use due to improved interpretation. In our experience, mAb EPR6893 is unreliable in many cases due to blush background staining, heterogenous staining, and/or poor staining of positive internal control resulting in equivocal interpretations. In the current study, MTAP expression by mAb EPR6893 was equivocal in 34% of cases. Similarly, other authors describe challenges with the interpretation of mAb 2G4 in up to 55% of cases due to heterogenous staining and/or lack of internal positive control. , , 
MTAP is a cytoplasmic protein, and scoring of cytoplasmic immunolabeling can be challenging in samples with variable and heterogeneous immunoreactivity secondary to preanalytical factors, such as tissue fixation or processing, and analytical variables such as uneven antigen retrieval across tissue samples. These issues can be improved or exacerbated depending on the quality, affinity, and avidity of mAbs used. Although variables other than antibody clones can impact the performance of IHC assays, it is unlikely that other variables led to the substantial number of cases with equivocal IHC interpretations with mAb EPR6893 given all cases with equivocal cases remained equivocal upon repeat staining with mAb EPR6893 and all equivocal interpretations were resolved with use of mAb 1813 under the same conditions/variables other than antibody clone.
Most equivocal interpretations with mAb EPR6893 in the current study were related to “blush” cytoplasmic staining or lack of adequate positive internal control staining. In both situations, it is difficult to reliably interpret the immunostain with certainty. It is possible that faint staining could be interpreted as loss of expression, but in the absence of stronger positive staining in benign internal control cells, it would be best to consider faint staining to be equivocal to avoid misinterpretation. On the other hand, MTAP loss cannot be interpreted in areas lacking internal control. Of note, lack of definitive positive control staining was the only reason for equivocal MTAP IHC interpretations in biphasic and sarcomatoid DPM in this study. Additionally, faint staining by tumor cells in areas rich in inflammatory cells with poor internal control was seen in some samples of epithelioid DPM and interpreted as equivocal due to heterogeneous staining. In the absence of adequate contrast between tumor and stroma, these geographic variations can lead to an incorrect interpretation of MTAP status and possibly a misdiagnosis of mesothelioma.
Our results confirm prior observations that MTAP IHC strongly correlates with homozygous deletion of 9p21 evaluated by NGS. , Notably, concordance between IHC and NGS was dependent on tumor purity, with high concordance being restricted to those with >20% tumor purity. This is expected given the limited sensitivity of NGS-based copy number analysis for low-purity samples. Furthermore, an accurate distinction between heterozygous and homozygous deletions has important clinical significance for DPM where homozygous deletion of CDKN2A is the validated diagnostic and prognostic biomarker. Implementation of allele-specific or SNP-based copy number analysis pipelines aids in distinguishing copy number states in this regard. Herein, implementation of FACETS allowed the distinction of homozygous deletion of 9p21 from other copy states and facilitated correlation with MTAP IHC expression using mAb 1813 in cases with sufficient tumor purity.
A critical observation in this study is that although the FACETS pipeline is optimized to detect copy number alterations in a wide range of tumor purity states, integer copy number calling based on modeling of log-ratio and allelic log-odds ratio values can be imprecise for allelically balanced alterations such as homozygous losses when the cellular fraction is low. SNP arrays, which can provide allele-specific copy number states, also require relatively high tumor purity for accurate performance. This emphasizes the potential utility of high-quality IHC assays in evaluating samples with limited tumor content, because assessment of copy number alterations by FACETS may have limited sensitivity when tumor purity is low. This is demonstrated in our study where FISH (a more sensitive assay for low-purity settings) was able to detect CDKN2A homozygous deletion in nearly all low-purity samples where FACETS did not detect 9p21 homozygous deletion. Notably, most of these low-purity cases with CDKN2A homozygous deletion also showed MTAP loss IHC using mAb 1813, thus supporting the utility of MTAP IHC in low-purity samples. Interestingly, 1 case showed MTAP loss in the absence of CDKN2A homozygous deletion by FISH and FACETS ( ). This finding could be the result of noncontiguous minute deletions on 9p21 similar to fragmentation of 3p21, which is a recurrent finding in mesothelioma. In rare cases, detection of these CDKN2A microdeletions (<200 kb) is suboptimal with long FISH probes (222 kb), whereas smaller probe designs (e.g., 57 kb) can be more effective in demonstrating short homozygous deletions. Alternatively, selective MTAP deletion, epigenetic repression, or posttranslational modifications cannot be excluded as these are prevalent mechanisms of MTAP loss in other tumor types. – 
NGS detected homozygous deletion in 1 DPM case showing retained MTAP expression with mAb 1813. A main limitation of the current study is that the NGS panel used does not include the MTAP gene. While MTAP gene is included in the 9p21 region, MTAP gene may not be always deleted with CDKN2A, one of the targeted genes used for 9p21 copy number analysis. Given that MTAP gene is co-deleted with the CDKN2A gene in up to 91% of mesotheliomas using FISH, approximately 10% of DPM with homozygous deletion of CDKN2A are expected to show retained expression of MTAP, which correlates with our observations in this study.
Another limitation of this study is the limited number of tumors with sarcomatoid histology. Unfortunately, to include matched normal tissue for sequencing (which is required for FACETs), the study was limited to cases of mesothelioma at the study institution with clinical sequencing data available and tissue for IHC available. Given the low frequency of sarcomatoid mesothelioma, there were not a large number of cases with pure sarcomatoid histology available for this study.
In summary, MTAP IHC is increasingly used as a surrogate marker to detect 9p21 homozygous deletion in mesothelioma, but in our experience, interpretation with mAb EPR6893 can be difficult. The findings of this study show that the interpretation of MTAP IHC is improved when mAb 1813 is used, significantly reducing equivocal interpretations. Furthermore, MTAP IHC correlates well with molecular assays for the evaluation of 9p21 locus deletion and has the potential to complement NGS and FISH, particularly in low-purity samples.
