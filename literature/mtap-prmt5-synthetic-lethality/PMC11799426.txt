## Abstract
Copy number alterations (CNAs) play a fundamental role in cancer development and constitute a potential tool for tailored treatments. The CNAs recognition in formalin fixed paraffin embedded (FFPE) material for diagnostic purposes has relied for years mainly on fluorescence in situ hybridization. The introduction of other procedures, such as Next-Generation Sequencing has dramatically improved CNAs discovery at genome-wide level. The detection of CNAs by NGS in FFPE material is, nonetheless, a complex issue, which still requires validation studies. Herein, the CNAs detection by a widely used NGS assay (Oncomine Comprehensive Assay plus®, OCA+) were evaluated in 14 FFPE samples mirroring diagnostic daily practice and compared to a whole-genome assay. OCA+, a targeted DNA panel, showed lower CNAs detection sensitivity and equal specificity for gains and losses. According to proprietary software pipeline, OCA+ accurately identified gains characterized by CN ≥ 5,2. No significant threshold maximizing the difference between true and false positive losses was found. Orthogonal FISH tests validated seven CNAs characterized by CN gain ≥ 6 or complete loss. Considering the CNAs growing significance in precision medicine, our findings further prompt towards a robust validation of NGS detection in FFPE materials.

## Introduction
Single nucleotide variants (SNVs) and copy number alterations (CNAs) are considered the main type of DNA alterations underlying the phenotypic heterogeneity of human cancer. Multiple recurrent chromosomal gains and losses characterize nearly all serous ovarian and breast cancers, a significant proportion of lung and head-and-neck squamous cell carcinomas, as well as endometrioid tumors of the serous subtype. These alterations potentially represent a powerful tool for diagnosis and personalized therapy in cancer medicine . Next-generation sequencing (NGS) comprehensive gene panels potentially allow to explore CNAs at genome-wide level in somatic tests, but the biases introduced by formalin fixation , , eventual limited percentages of tumor cells (TCs%) in formalin fixed paraffin embedded (FFPE) material and/or their deviation from diploid state , as well as the non-trivial issues related to the lack of standardized procedures in NGS protocols/analysis pipelines, affect reliable and reproducible CNAs quantification in diagnostic daily practice. In amplicon-based technologies, the initial genomic DNA is directly amplified by PCR using target-specific primers to obtain a pool of amplicons that are digested, adapter-ligated, purified and then sequenced by means of Ion semi-conductor sequencing . In capture-based chemistry, conversely, DNA is firstly fragmented, adapter-ligated and PCR amplified, then selected “targeted” genes are captured by hybridization. In this latter case sequencing-by-synthesis (SBS) chemistry and 2-channel fluorescence dyes detection are performed on “paired-end” reads method, while in Ion semi-conductor sequencing approach SBS exploits the detection of hydrogen ions released during the polymerization of DNA , . Despite these differences, in both approaches CNAs are quantified assuming that the depth of coverage is proportional to the initial copy number of corresponding genomic segment and by B-allele frequencies , . The current molecular diagnostic procedure in our laboratories relies on Oncomine Comprehensive Assay Plus® (OCA+, Thermo Fisher). It delivers results within a turn-around-time and cost structure compliant with the requirements for NGS analyses set by the National and Regional healthcare systems. This assay not only identifies SNVs, tumor mutational burden, and microsatellite instability but also detects chromosomal gains, losses, and loss of heterozygosity (LOH) exploiting an amplicon-based technology. Indeed, the short-read sequencing allowed by capture-based assay would be the preferred choice for CNA detection in FFPE samples to minimize issues associated with fragmented DNA, as also robustly demonstrated in-silico . However, it is worth considering the potential use of Oncomine’s amplicon-based technology for CNA discovery, as it may be more effective for DNA alignment.
Recently, a comparative analysis of CNV detection using OCA + and other NGS methods or FISH was reported, demonstrating good concordance between the copy numbers detected by OCA+ in three ERBB2-amplified breast cancer samples and 16 additional FFPE tumor samples . However, the reference methods used for validating the OCA + results were not fully detailed, leaving room for a more comprehensive evaluation of the CNV detection metrics of OCA+ in a diagnostic context.
Since the assessment of OCA+ accuracy in FFPE material obtained in daily practice conditions must be considered a milestone, its performance was investigated in 14 samples from diagnostic routine and results were compared to those obtained using the genome-wide assay OncoScan® (OCS) , which has been already successfully validated for CNAs detection in FFPE samples , . Potentially “druggable” alterations were investigated by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH).

## Results
 The sample cohort analyzed herein included 14 FFPE samples (8 surgical specimens and 6 biopsies) obtained from 14 carcinoma of different origin. Histological diagnosis, sample type, DNA integrity numbers (DINs), 260/280 ratios as well as quantity assessments are reported in Table  . Overall, the DNAs obtained showed a suitable quality for NGS applications based on DINs and 260/280 ratios. In line with the acceptable DNA quality, all obtained libraries successfully passed the Ion Reporter software 5.18.4. (IRS) controls requested for subsequent analyses.
 

## Genomic segmentation
IRS divided genome into contiguous segments of similar ploidy using a circular binary segmentation (CBS) algorithm. Log2 ratio for copy number (from Copy number variation pipeline data, Thermofisher) and log odds (from Ion Torrent variant caller data, Thermofisher) for variant allele frequency of observed population were calculated for each segment (representative example of this analysis is shown in Fig.  , panels A and B). The obtained values were used by a proprietary algorithm for estimating the TCs% in the sample to calculate CNAs in tumor cells (TCs). CNAs for each of the identified genomic segments are then reported for total and minor alleles (Fig.  , panel C).
 

## TC%
IRS successfully estimated TC% in 11 out of 14 samples but failed in the remaining three samples (A051, A053, and A062; Table  ). In these cases, the TC% determined by the pathologist was used for CNA evaluation, following IRS guidelines (Ion Reporter 5.18 User Guide: https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0024776_IonReporter5_18_UG.pdf). Overall, a low, non-significant correlation (R = 0.2, Pearson correlation coefficient) was observed between the TC% estimated by IRS and the pathologist’s evaluation.

## CNAs
A simplified example of genomic alterations detected by OCA+, their associated p-values and copies number is show in Table  . In addition to single gene detection, CNAs can be reported as associated with the entire chromosome arm gain or loss. The reliability of single gene calls was investigated through a comparison with OCS. OCS was chosen as a validation tool since it is a “wide genome” platform with probes spread throughout the genome, uses molecular inversion probes (MIPs) specifically designed for degraded DNA  and covered all the genes investigated by OCA+ (plus additional, up to about 900, cancer-associated genes). Based on OCS design, gain, loss or LOH true conditions were established for all the 345 genes found in the OCA+ Browser Extensible Data (BED) files. In that, all OCA+ call with p-value < 0,05 will be considered (hereafter named “standard” analysis).
 

## OCA+ CNAs standard analysis metrics
The distribution of sample-by-sample sensitivity and specificity for gain and loss across the whole dataset are shown in Fig.  A (red and blue boxplot, respectively). When considering gain events, a positive correlation between TC% and sensitivity was observed (R = 0,62 Pearson correlation coefficient). Both gain and loss detection specificity are greater than 0,95 in all samples. On the contrary, loss recognition sensitivity showed low performance (and, consequently, LOH detection as well). In detail, a total of 261 gains with CNAs > 3 and p-value < 0,05 were identified by OCA+ (Supplementary Table ). The results showed that true positive (TP) (n = 186) were characterized by a copy number (CN) mean equal to 6,7 (range 3–38) and a p-value mean equal to 10− 5 (range: 0–4,35 × 10− 3) while false positive (FP; n = 75) had CN and p-values means equal to 4,9 (range: 3–8) and p-value mean equal to 1,8 × 10− 3 (range 0–4,97 × 10− 2). Two tailed Mann–Whitney test was performed to compare the distributions of the CN for TP or FP detections in the whole cohort, and they were proved to be significantly different (p-value 7 × 10-7, Fig.  b). Receiving operating curve (ROC) analysis (Fig.  C) indicated that a CN threshold ≥ 5,2 maximized the OCA+ ability to detect TP gains overall. Among 138 calls with CN ≥ 5,2, OCA+ identified 116 TP and 22 FP. Interestingly, by applying the threshold recommended by HER2 gene amplification guidelines (equal to 6 copies) , , the TP/FP ratio increased up to 5-fold (of 72 cases, 60 were identified as TP and 12 as FP, Supplementary Table ). Notably, in the latter situation, 4 out of 12 (33%) FP involved the STK11, a tumor suppressor gene encoding an evolutionary conserved serine/threonine kinase (Liver Kinase B1—LKB1) located at 19p13.3 that is usually deleted or inactivated by mutations. OCA+ identified 99 losses (CN < 2, p-values range 0–6,48 × 10-3) divided in 68 TP and 31 FP. Differently from gains, despite sensitivity reached ≥ 90%, as evidenced by the small Area Under Curve (Fig.  D, Supplementary Table ), no significant threshold maximizing the difference in TP and FP for CN loss distribution was identified. The very small amount of LOH retrieved by OCA+ (13 LOH) prevents further analysis. OCA + missed the detection of some genes or regions with CNAs identified by OCS. Because the OCS assay is primarily designed to detect large regions encompassing multiple genes—many of which are not covered by OCA+ it was challenging to obtain reliable metrics for false negative data. Therefore, all the gains detected by OCS missed by OCA+ are provided in Supplementary Table . Specifically, this table details the 145 CNA regions that contain at least one of the 354 genes detectable by OCA+, which was not called as altered by the OCA+ assay. Consistent with OCA+’s ability to detect CNAs with a high number of copies, only two missed regions containing an OCA+ gene exceeded a copy number of 4 in the OCS analysis.
 

## OCA+ CNAs extended analysis metrics
As described in Table  , it is worth noting that OCA + assay did not always report alterations for all the genes mapping on chromosome arms for which, conversely, gain/loss variations were reported. This was, in all likelihood, due to the intrinsic features of the proprietary algorithm (e.g. CNV Transition Penalty or Smoothing parameters), which assign different weight and significance to gene- or arm- level output. Prompt by the notion that chromosome imbalances are often associated with a gene-dosage effect that involves genes located in the affected regions, we repeated the comparison analysis between OCA+ and OCS extending the gain/loss information to all the genes mapping to the arm for which a significant CNAs was reported. As such, a substantial improvement in OCA + sensitivity for gains and loss was achieved (Fig.  A). However, at the same time, this caused an expected decrease in specificity and a greater number of FP calls. As described for standard analysis, the distributions of CN were significant different between TP and FP (two tailed Mann-Whitney, p-value 2 × 10-16, Fig.  B). However, compared to the TP 60 gains with CN ≥ 6 revealed by standard analysis, the extended one retrieved only further 8 TP amplified genes but none of them had diagnostic or predictive significance for response to therapy (Supplementary Table ).
 

## Clinical relevance of CNAs
All CNAs characterized by CN ≥ 6 or complete loss found by OCA+ with potential application in patients’ clinical management are listed in Table  . NTRK1, ERBB2, FGFR1, and FGFR2 gains are potentially “druggable” targets with specific inhibitors while the CCND1 gain, when coupled with those of FGF3/FGF4/FGF19 on chromosome 11q13, strongly promotes gefitinib resistance in non-small cell lung cancers lines . The potential actionability of the identified targets according to ESMO Scale for Clinical Actionability of molecular Targets (ESCAT) classification , was reported in Table  . FGFR1 (A055 and A063), FGFR2 (A064) NTRK1 (A056) and CCND1 (A061) gains were confirmed by FISH while ERBB2 gain (A053) by chromogenic in situ hybridization. Interestingly, despite the absence of differences between TP and FP loss distribution, CDKN2A bi-allelic loss (A061 and A063) was confirmed by FISH (Table  ). Remarkably, OCA+ can discriminate between small amplification, or small loss from large genomic region CNAs due to chromosomal polysomy (Figs.  and ). This distinction is not merely speculative, since gene-specific amplifications or deletions are more likely to be crucial for cell survival and, where actionable, likely more sensitive to target therapies than “aspecific” polysomy.
 

## Discussion
Among the opportunities offered by advanced NGS technology, the evaluation of DNA gains and losses could be an additional tool for diagnosis and precision medicine. In particular, OCA + assay offers the possibility to detect simultaneously multiple CNAs at genome-wide level although, to the best of our knowledge, its accuracy has not yet been documented in a “real-world” setting where diagnostic procedure is run on FFPE samples, where several factors associated with sample quality (e.g. DNA GC content) and the presence of normal cells – , , make CNAs assessment particularly challenging. The main objective of present work was to assess the CNAs detection metrics of OCA + and its associated analysis software IRS in a set of FFPE samples that mirrored the daily diagnostic practice of a referral cancer center in the era of precision medicine. Positive (i.e. presence of gain or loss and LOH) and negative (i.e. absence of gain or loss and LOH) conditions were established using WGA OCS as reference, in which CNAs discovery relies on the MIPs hybridization to DNA target region . Each region is 20 base-pairs wide, making OCS largely independent of DNA quality . In addition, OCS is a WGA method where the number of MIPs (3,35 × 105) excess of 1 logarithm those of OCA+ amplicons (~ 1,3 × 104) , allowing more accurate CNAs quantification also in heterogeneous FFPE samples. In dedicated experiments, where tumor heterogeneity was obtained by the titration with normal DNA of “copy number events” (previously determined by FISH in FFPE samples), OCS showed concordant results across TCs% ranging from 40 to 80% .
Overall, the results herein provided sustain the notion that (i) CNAs discovery in FFPE samples is influenced by the TCs%; (ii) gain detection is more accurate than loss predictions; and (iii) TP gain calls showed higher number of copies than FP gain calls, suggesting the higher ability of OCA+ to detect big amplifications. In our FFPE cases, in fact, OCA+ showed an acceptable gains-detection sensitivity in samples characterized by the higher TCs% while (as witnessed by their positive correlation index), with few exceptions, loss and (consequently) LOH events, were lost in almost all cases. These results could be explained considering several factors: (i) the miscellaneous origin of samples, (ii) their biological intrinsic variability and (iii) technical limits of NGS platform. In fact, if all the extracted DNA were characterized by comparable quality/amount and gave reliable libraries (thus suggesting that pre-analytical conditions did not significantly impact on NGS CNAs metric), on the other hand low TCs% corresponded to overall reduced OCA+ sensitivity. This observation implies that FFPE samples with few TCs (such as small biopsies or tumors characterized by limited tumor bulk) might not be suitable for CNAs detections. The sensitivity-drop might be justified by the different number of probes used for genomic segmentation (3,35 × 105vs. 1,3 × 104), leading to more robust statistic estimation by the CBS algorithm in OCS than OCA+.
Overall, OCA+ retrieved 261 gains and 99 losses across the 14 samples. Both were characterized by the same frequencies of TP and FP calls, but TP gains showed significantly higher CN than FP ones. Notably, CN ≥ 5.2 (or, more stringently, CN > 6) can be considered as a technical threshold able to maximize the distinction between TP and FP. Notably, this is in line with expected “biological” threshold: in HER2 guideline where women are treated with Trastuzumab when HER2/centromeric ratio is > 2 or when at least 6 copies of HER2 are detected , . For loss detection, the presence of non-tumor cells could “mask” the deletion of one or two alleles in pathologic ones, rendering their discovery by NGS particularly challenging. In addition, the additional analysis of the entire chromosomal arms calls by IRS (here named “extended analysis”) resulted in increased sensitivity and specificity, albeit generated a large increase of FP calls with a CN ranging from 3 to 6. These outcomes suggested that the ranges between 3 and 5 CN gain or 0–1 loss must be considered with caution especially if coupled with few TCs%.
Among the TP gains (gain ≥ 6) and losses (< 0,5), we validated by chromogenic in situ hybridization (CISH) and FISH those potentially actionable by target therapies. All the analyzed alterations (ERBB2, NTRK1, FGFR1 and FGFR2) were confirmed.
Although tumor suppressor genes loss cannot be directly targeted, in some cases, they might be indirectly exploited for therapies as in the case of CDKN2A deletion, leading to CDK4/6 activation. CDK4/6 inhibitors palbociclib/ribociclib gave disappointing results in clinical trials as single agents – , but might led to significantly longer progression-free survival when used in combination with other drugs, as suggested by preliminary evidence in patients with PIK3CA-mutated, hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer .
Notably, in our cases, the CDKN2A loss was coupled with that of the close tumor suppressor CDKN2B (encoding p15Ink4b) and methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) genes. Interestingly, protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) was identified as a synthetic lethal target for cancers harboring homozygous loss of the MTAP gene in a phase I/II study, where the PRMT5 inhibition in MTAP deleted patients, induced objective clinical responses .
Although none of the identified CNAs in our series could be to date recognized as histotype-specific or tumor driving event, their use in precision medicine is a challenging task and might offer a significant contribution to the complex decision that will be taken by multidisciplinary context as Molecular Tumor Boards, where the neoplasm molecular features are discussed to propose the most appropriate treatment , . The major limitation of the present paper could be represented by the use of OCS as a comparative method. In fact, although OCS uses higher number of probes, it cannot be considered as a pure “reference” method since, similarly to OCA+, no normal CN counterpart is used as internal reference; thus, tumor heterogeneity and normal cell contamination might in part affect the output.
In conclusion, the present results further prompt towards a validation of NGS in extended cohorts of selected histotypes, in virtue of the increasing importance that CNAs will have in the context of personalized medicine.

## Materials and methods
Samples series Fourteen samples were obtained from as many patients enrolled in our Institute from April 2020 to March 2021 and screened in our department (Department of diagnostics innovation, Fondazione IRCCS Istituto Nazionale dei Tumori, Milan, Italy) by OCA+ -based SNVs assay in the context of the procedures of the Institutional Molecular Tumor Board . This study was performed according to the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics committee of Fondazione IRCCS Istituto Nazionale Tumori Milano (INT 277/20). Informed consent was collected from every patient. The sample cohort (Table  ) included 8 surgical specimens (A051, A060, A061, A062, A063, A064 A065 and A066) and 6 biopsies (A052, A053, A055, A056, A057 and A059).
DNA extraction and quality controls (QCs) assessment Depending on expected tumor fraction, 2 or 3 5 μm representative FFPE sections were used for DNA extraction. Tumor area was micro-dissected, when possible, whereas in case of small biopsies all sections were subjected to DNA extraction. DNA was obtained through digestion with Proteinase-K 200 µg/ml (Sigma-Aldrich, catalogue: P2308-100MG) overnight at 56 °C followed by a Proteinase-K blocks for 10 min at 95 °C. DNA quality/quantity was checked by means of 4200 TapeStation System (Agilent, catalogue: G2991AA), NanoDrop ND-1000 v 3.3.1 and Qubit High Sensitivity dsDNA kit (Thermofisher, catalogue: Q32851). DNA fragmentation was estimated through the DNA Integrity Number (DIN) algorithm . Optimal DIN values (median 4.8; range: 2.7–6.5) were observed for all but two samples (A061 and A063, Table  ), whose DIN value was marginally lower than 3, in theory considered as the quality threshold to obtain a reliable sequencing . The median absorbance ratio 260/280 was 1.4 (range: 1.17–1.56), slightly lower than expected (about 1.6–1.8 for DNA), in line with an extraction from FFPE material. All samples had sufficient DNA amount for library preparation (Table  ).

## NGS library preparation and sequencing
Libraries were generated according to the manufacturer’s instructions, starting from 20ng of DNA and then quantified by Ion Library TaqMan Quantification kit [Thermofisher, catalogue 4468802]. Each sample was sequenced at 5 pM final concentration on Ion GeneStudio S5 System using 550 chips, to obtain at least 20-million reads output corresponding to a medium coverage ~ 2000X. QCs of sequencing output was evaluated by means of the Coverage Analysis plugin on the Torrent Suite 5.12.3, Thermofisher). All our samples exhibited both uniformity and on-target metrics > 90%, with null coverage in less than 1% of the reads, ensuring negligible coverage variations across the library.
BAM files were automatically uploaded from the Torrent Suite to the Ion Reporter software 5.18.4. (IRS, Thermofisher) and analyzed with the workflow Oncomine Comprehensive Plus – w2.2 – DNA.
All samples showed a MAPD (Median of the Absolute value of all Pairwise Differences) < 0,5.
The first CNAs estimation is made by IRS with Thermofisher proprietary Variability Control Informatics Baseline (VCIB) algorithm. VCIB uses a computationally inferred baseline (created using at least 48 diverse samples) to assess the correct log2 ratios for each amplicon in input sample data. Then, genome is divided into contiguous segments of similar ploidy levels by circular binary segmentation (CBS) algorithm, and the Log odd ratios of variant allele frequency of observed population of single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) are calculated for each segment. These results are then used for inferring the TCs% of the sample and the ploidy of each genomic segment. Finally, the TCs% is used to adjust the copy number results by a proprietary algorithm (IRS software user guide).
Whole Genome Array Oncoscan® (OCS) assay For each sample, 80 ng of DNA were processed according to manufacturer’s protocol. The final amplicon smaller fragments were hybridized onto the OCS, stained and washed using the GeneChip™ Fluidics Station 450 (Thermofisher, catalogue: IC 13134) and then loaded into the GeneChip™ Scanner 3000 7G (Affymetrix), where array fluorescence intensity was finally acquired to generate array images (DAT files). Array fluorescence intensity (CEL) files were automatically generated from DAT files by the Affimetrix GeneChip™ Command Console (AGCC) Software version 4.0. Quality metrics and CNA calls were generated using the Chromosome Analysis Suite (ChAS) software (Thermofisher) using default settings. Briefly, to evaluate the quality of CNA calls performed by OCS, the following parameters were evaluated: (i) MAPD (PASS ≤ 0.3), which measures the variability in log2 ratios by looking at the pair difference of all probes and outputs the median value, (ii) SNP QC (PASS ≥ 26), SNP quality control, which estimates the distributions of homozygous AA, heterozygous AB and homozygous BB alleles and calculates the distance between them, (iii) Waviness-SD (PASS ≤ 0.12), the global measure of variation of microarray probes that is insensitive to short-range variations and focuses on long-range variations. Elevated waviness with good MAPD and SNP QC metrics can nonetheless occur in samples with many copy number changes. Overall, 10/14 samples profiled passed quality check, 1 sample was classified as acceptable (A059: MAPD & Waviness SD passed, SNP QC failed) while 3/14 presented “failed” quality control (A063, A064, A065 Waviness SD passed, SNP QC and MAPD failed. Since all those samples showed a MAPDs slightly exceeding 0,3 and passed Waviness-SD, they were anyway included in the analyses.

## Statistical analysis
To evaluate the performance of CNA calls (gain, normal or loss), only common genes between OCA + and OCS panels were selected for the analysis. OCS output was set as a comparative method for the statistical analysis, either in paired-samples or over the entire cohort. In the first case, sensitivity and specificity-score were used to establish the rate of TP and true negative (TN) calls, respectively, returned by panels. In details: (i) sensitivity (recall) is the measure of the correctly predicted positive cases over all the positive cases across the dataset; (ii) specificity is the number of correctly predicted negative cases of the total negative instances in the dataset. The Pearson’s correlation coefficient was considered as correlation indexes. Mann–Whitney test was used to evaluate the association between the distribution of the number of copies associated to TP and FP calls . Standard ROC test was performed to identify optimal threshold for OCA+ accuracy. Delong test was performed to assess AUC significance. All the analyses described above were performed in R software (version 4.2.3).

## FISH
FFPE samples were analyzed by FISH as previously described . Briefly, the following commercial probes: FGFR1 (ZytoLight® SPEC FGFR1/CEN 8 Dual Color Probe catalogue: Z-2072-200 ); FGFR2 (ZytoLight® SPEC FGFR2/CEN 10 Dual Color Probe catalogue: Z-2122-200 ); NTRK1 ( ZytoLight® SPEC NTRK1 Dual Color Break Apart Probe, catalogue: Z-2167-200 ); CCND1 (Vysis CCND1 Dual Color Break Apart Rearrangement FISH probe Abbott Molecular, catalogue: 05N38-020 ); CDKN2A (Vysis CDKN2A / CEP 9 FISH Probe Kit Abbott Molecular catalogue: 05J51-001 ) were hybridized according to manufacturer’s instructions. A minimum of 50 nuclei were analyzed using Leica DM 6000B (Wetzlar, Germany) microscope at 100x magnification and the appropriate fluorescence filters. The images were captured using Cytovision software (v. 7.0 Leica).
