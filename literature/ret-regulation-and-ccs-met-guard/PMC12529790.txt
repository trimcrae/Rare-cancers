## Objective
This study aims to develop a ceRNA network associated with the chromosomal passenger complex (CPC) and identify a prognostic signature in lung cancer, the most diagnosed globally, to better understand the molecular mechanisms underlying tumor progression.

## Methods
The study used R packages and publicly available databases to conduct multi-omics In-silico analyses on CPC. These tools facilitated gene expression profiling, prognostic assessment, exploration of miRNA (microRNA), lncRNA (long non-coding RNA), transcription factor interactions, and pathway enrichment analysis. Molecular docking tools were used to evaluate binding affinities of CPC proteins with tobacco carcinogens, selective Aurora kinase B inhibitors, FDA-approved chemotherapeutics, and natural compounds. Immune landscape analysis was conducted using the SPRING viewer to visualize immune cell subpopulations in NSCLC, validated by correlation analysis using the GSCA database.

## Results
The study reveals that CPC genes—BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB—are overexpressed in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and are associated with poor overall survival, especially in smokers. A dysregulated ceRNA axis involving lncRNA TMPO-AS1 and miRNA-hsa-let-7b-5p was identified, along with transcription factor E2F1, which shows a strong correlation with the CPC genes. Notably, TMPO-AS1 and E2F1 are positively correlated, while hsa-let-7b-5p is negatively correlated with the CPCs, contributing to tumor progression. Downregulation of hsa-let-7b-5p is linked to poorer outcomes, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. Nicotine and NNK show stable binding, suggesting potential roles in activating the CPC pathway and contributing to LUAD progression. CPCs have a strong binding affinity with Hesperidin, a natural bioflavonoid, compared to known chemotherapeutic agents like docetaxel and paclitaxel. CPC genes are negatively correlated with CD4⁺ T cells, indicating a role in promoting an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.

## Conclusion
Lung adenocarcinoma patients have poorer prognosis due to higher levels of CPCs, TMPO-AS1, and E2F1. A sponge complex between TMPO-AS1 and hsa-let-7b-5p may contribute to the tumor progression, and targeting CPCs with natural compounds could offer therapeutic potential.

Highlights
The overexpression of chromosomal passenger complex genes, AURKB, BIRC5, CDCA8, and INCENP is significantly associated with poor prognosis in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), particularly among smokers.
The competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) axis, which involves the long non-coding RNA TMPO-AS1 and the miRNA hsa-let-7b-5p, regulates the expression of these CPC genes. TMPO-AS1 shows a positive correlation with CPC genes, while hsa-let-7b-5p shows a negative correlation.
Survival analysis indicates that the combined expression of CPC genes, TMPO-AS1, hsa-let-7b-5p, and E2F1 may serve as a reliable prognostic biomarker panel for LUAD in smokers.
Hesperidin exhibits a strong binding affinity to CPC proteins, particularly AURKB, when compared to Barasertib, Docetaxel, and Paclitaxel, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic agent.
The overexpression of CPC genes, E2F1, and TMPO-AS1 in LUAD is strongly associated with reduced infiltration of CD4⁺ T cells, indicating their role in promoting an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.

## Introduction
Lung cancer remains a major global health concern, with 20 million new cases and 9.7 million deaths reported in 2022, according to the World Health Organization [ ]. Approximately 85% of lung cancer is classified as NSCLC, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) being the most common subtypes [ ]. Among these, Lung adenocarcinoma, has emerged as the most diagnosed cancer in younger men and women. Despite advances in treatment, the overall 5-year survival rate for lung cancer remains low at 18%. However, early diagnosis can substantially improve outcomes, with survival rates rising to around 55% and up to 40% with effective treatment strategies [ ]. Notably, over 70% of NSCLC patients are diagnosed at an advanced-stage, rendering them ineligible for surgical intervention [ ]. This challenge is largely attributed to difficulties in early detection and lack of effective therapeutic targets for patients with NSCLC [ ]. Therefore, accurate identification of potential gene biomarkers and effective candidate drugs are essential for reducing mortality rates among the patients. Recent advances in gene expression profiling, driven by high-throughput sequencing technologies, have enabled the discovery of genetic signatures associated with early-stage lung cancer. These developments have laid the foundation for the identification of novel biomarkers that hold promises for enhancing diagnostic precision and guiding targeted therapies in NSCLC.
Additionally, studies are increasingly examining the role of non-coding RNAs and epigenetic modifications, which may provide new paths for targeted therapies and improve the precision of diagnostic tools. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), including miRNAs and lncRNAs, play crucial roles in gene regulation and have been implicated in the progression and development of NSCLC [ ]. They can function as oncogenes or tumor suppressors, influencing cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis [ ]. Understanding the specific functions and mechanisms of these non-coding RNAs in tumor progression could lead to the development of novel biomarkers for early detection and new therapeutic targets for more personalized treatment approaches.
Our research group has previously explored the regulatory axis involving hsa-let-7b-5p, TMPO-AS1, and E2F1 in the context of lung cancer, highlighting downstream targets such as FOXM1, MAD2L1, NCAPG, and NUSAP1 [ – ]. Building on these findings, the current study extends this axis to investigate its regulation of a critical molecular regulator of chromosomal integrity, the chromosomal passenger complex (CPC), which consists of Aurora B kinase, INCENP, Survivin, and Borealin, offering novel insights into mitotic control as it is crucial for genomic stability, regulating processes during both nuclear and cytoplasmic division [ ]. It ensures the accurate segregation of duplicated chromosomes during mitosis, prevents chromosome damage during cytokinesis, and manages proper cytoplasmic division to avoid tetraploidization [ ]. Aneuploidy and chromosomal instability (CIN) are prevalent in various human cancers, including lung cancer, but whether this is a result of CPC dysregulation remains uncertain. Several new gene expression targets are currently under investigation for combating cancer, particularly those associated with chromosomal instability, which is frequently linked to defective cell cycles and regulatory checkpoints, such as cyclin/cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs). In the context of the cell cycle, cyclins and CDKs oversee four significant checkpoints: G0/G1, S phase, G2, and the M phase spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC). Abnormalities in these checkpoints can lead to improper chromosomal segregation and mitotic catastrophe, contributing to tumor aggressiveness [ – ].
The role of CPC genes and their noncoding RNAs in cell cycle progression could lead to novel treatment options for various cancer types. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the prognostic role of CPC genes in LUAD patients using non- coding RNA-based therapeutics, including miRNAs and lncRNAs. The study employed In- silico methods to determine the ceRNA network associated with CPC genes and its role in the prognosis of LUAD patients. By utilizing In-silico methods, deeper insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer progression can be gained, ultimately facilitating the development of more targeted and effective treatment strategies. By utilizing computational approaches, the study further explored the interaction of CPC proteins with various therapeutic agents, including known chemotherapeutics, natural compounds, and selective inhibitors. Additionally, the immune landscape of NSCLC to assess immune cell infiltration patterns and their potential association with CPC gene expression was investigated, ultimately aiding the identification of novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets for more effective and personalized treatment interventions.

## Results

## CPC genes exhibit prognostic significance in lung adenocarcinoma
Given the role of CPC genes in cancer progression, we sought to investigate the clinical relevance of the CPC genes across TCGA cancers. We first analyzed their expression profiles using the UALCAN database and found that these genes are significantly overexpressed in tumor tissues compared to normal across most malignancies, as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1A–D. Consistent with this, pan-cancer analyses using the Firehose Broad GDAC and TIMER databases also revealed elevated expression of CPC genes across various cancer types (Supplementary Fig. 2A–D and 3A–D), suggesting their potential oncogenic roles. Further, OncoMX database analysis confirmed significantly high expression of CPC genes in lung cancer, with log2 fold changes of 4.32 for BIRC5, 3.54 for CDCA8, 1.59 for INCENP, and 4.03 for AURKB (Supplementary Table 1). Furthermore, to explore their prognostic significance, we utilized the KM Plotter database to analyze survival outcomes in NSCLC patients. Our results indicated that higher BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB expression in tumor tissues correlated with poor patient prognosis and was significantly associated with reduced overall survival (OS) based on hazard ratios (HR) and confidence intervals (CI) as follows: BIRC5 (HR = 1.49, CI: 1.33–1.68, P = 2.9e−11), CDCA8 (HR = 1.59, CI: 1.41–1.79, P = 2.2e−14), INCENP (HR = 1.38, CI: 1.22–1.55, P = 1.4e−07), and AURKB (HR = 1.78, CI: 1.58–2.01, P < 1e−16), as shown in Fig.  A–D. As shown in Table  , overexpression of CPC genes substantially reduces patient survival. For example, low BIRC5 expression was associated with a median survival of 86 months (~7 years), while high expression reduced survival to 51.27 months (~4.2 years), indicating a strong correlation between elevated BIRC5 expression and poor prognosis in NSCLC patients. 
Similarly, high expressions of CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB also correlated with decreased survival. Given the clinical relevance, we examined CPC gene expression in histological subtypes and other clinicopathological factors. In LUAD patients, elevated expression of BIRC5 (HR = 1.96, CI: 1.65–2.34, P = 1.5e−14), CDCA8 (HR = 1.66, CI: 1.39–1.98, P = 1e−08), INCENP (HR = 1.57, CI: 1.32–1.86, P = 2.8e−07), and AURKB (HR = 2.11, CI: 1.77–2.52, P < 1e−16) was significantly associated with poor OS (Fig.  E–H). However, no significant association was observed in LUSC patients: BIRC5 (P = 0.97), CDCA8 (P = 0.87), INCENP (P = 0.84), and AURKB (P = 0.073), as depicted in Supplementary Fig. 4A–D and Table  . The magnitude of survival differences in LUAD patients further underscores the prognostic value of CPC genes. For instance, BIRC5 showed a 2.1-fold difference in OS (112.67 vs. 52 months), CDCA8 had a 1.7-fold difference (106 vs. 59 months), INCENP showed a 1.6-fold reduction (110.27 vs. 66 months), and AURKB exhibited the most significant impact with a 2.3-fold difference (117.33 vs. 49.43 months). Consequently, we found that the expression of CPC genes was significantly associated with both males and females LUAD patients (Table  and F g.  I–P). Similarly, the expression of CPC genes was significantly associated with the poor survival outcome of smokers with LUAD (BIRC5: HR = 1.85, CI: 1.42–2.41, P = 4.1e−06), (CDCA8: HR = 1.69, CI: 1.29–2.2, P = 9.5e−05), (INCENP: HR = 1.96, CI: 1.5–2.57, P = 5.5e−07), (AURKB: HR = 2.1, CI: 1.61–2.75, P = 3.3e−08) (Fig.  Q–T). A decline in survival outcome of approximately 1.5- to 2-folds from low expression cohort to high expression cohort in smoker LUAD patients was noted (Table  ).
Notably, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 4E–P, we found that the KM plotter analysis with similar clinicopathological characteristics in LUSC did not correlate with the CPC gene expression. To further corroborate our results a multivariate analysis taking mean expression of all the four genes was carried out to assess the prognostic significance of the CPC complex as a whole and the survival plots showed significant overexpression of these associated with poor OS (HR = 1.63, P = 6.6e−16, low expression = 93 months, high expression = 48 months), OS + LUAD (HR = 1.92, P = 1.6e−13, low expression = 110.27 months, high expression = 52 months), OS + LUAD + Males (HR = 1.73, P = 4.9e−06, low expression = 103 months, high expression = 46 months), OS + LUAD + Females (HR = 2.14, P = 6.8e−08, low expression = 116 months, high expression = 68.67 months), and OS + LUAD + Smokers (HR = 1.92, P = 4.3e−08, low expression = 95 months, high expression = 54.3 months), whereas insignificant results were found in the cases of OS + LUSC (HR = 1.07, P = 0.49, low expression = 58 months, high expression = 52.97 months) and OS + LUAD + Non-Smokers (HR = 1.55, P = 0.15, low expression = 76 months, high expression = 52 months) as shown in Table  and Supplementary Fig. 5A–G.

## Expression of CPC genes in human lung adenocarcinoma
To further explore their role in cancer conditions, we next assessed the expression patterns of CPC genes specifically in LUAD patient samples. Analysis using the UALCAN database revealed that tumor from LUAD patients had greater levels of BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB gene expression, with an approximately ninefold increase (P < 0.05) when compared to normal tissues (Fig.  A–D). Further, the log fold change in expression levels of BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB genes in tumor samples was analyzed using ENCORI (Log FC: 14.08, 9.16, 2.60, 11.93, respectively) as shown in Fig.  E–H. Additionally, to ensure an unbiased comparison, we normalized the sample size using R packages (Normal = 59, Tumor = 59), the box plots revealed a significant overexpression of CPC genes in LUAD tumor tissues compared to normal tissues (P < 0.05) as shown in F g.  I–L. Furthermore, Single-cell transcriptome analysis using TCGAnalyzeR revealed elevated Log FC values for BIRC5 (3.50), CDCA8 (2.18), INCENP (1.15), and AURKB (3.06), indicating their mRNA transcript upregulation in LUAD tumor cells as shown in Fig.  M–P. The data suggests that these genes could play a significant role in the progression of LUAD. Further, UALCAN was used to evaluate CPC gene expression in LUAD across various clinicopathological parameters, including smoking status as shown in Fig.  Q–T, gender, tumor stage, and nodal metastasis (Supplementary Fig. 6A–L). The results showed a significant upregulation (P < 0.05) of all CPC genes, particularly in male smokers, as well as in patients with advanced stages and positive nodal metastasis, reinforcing their potential clinical significance. 
Notably, tobacco smoke remains a leading cause of lung carcinogenesis, with nicotine and its derivative NNK recognized as key carcinogens. To assess their potential impact on mitotic regulation, molecular docking was performed with CPC proteins. The binding affinities (kcal/mol) observed for nicotine were: BIRC5 (−5.3), CDCA8 (−4.5), INCENP (−4.1), and AURKB (−6.0); and for NNK: BIRC5 (−5.5), CDCA8 (−4.4), INCENP (−3.3), and AURKB (−6.6) (Supplementary Table 2). Further, the interaction profiling revealed predominant π–π stacking and van der Waals forces (Supplementary Fig. 7A–H), suggesting that these tobacco-derived compounds may contribute to chromosomal instability and mitotic disruption in LUAD through direct CPC targeting, possibly underlying the altered expression patterns observed in smokers.

## CPC genes are involved in biological processes and metastasis
Next, to understand how dysregulation of CPC genes may influence cellular behavior, we investigated their role in key biological processes and metastatic potential in LUAD. The CancerSEA database revealed a significant correlation between gene expression and various biological processes involved in LUAD. BIRC5 displayed a significant correlation with the cell cycle (R = 0.80), DNA damage (R = 0.68), proliferation (R = 0.64), DNA repair (R = 0.61), invasion (R = 0.47), and EMT (R = 0.30). CDCA8 was significantly associated with the cell cycle (R = 0.59), proliferation (R = 0.54), DNA damage (R = 0.39), DNA repair (R = 0.33), and invasion (R = 0.33). INCENP showed a significant association with the cell cycle (R = 0.32) only, while AURKB was involved in the cell cycle (R = 0.69), DNA damage (R = 0.56), DNA repair (R = 0.52), proliferation (R = 0.50), invasion (R = 0.43),EMT (R = 0.31), Stemness (R = 0.25), and metastasis (R = 0.23) (Supplementary Fig. 8A–D). These results indicate that an increased level of CPC genes in LUAD cells facilitates metastasis and the transition from epithelial to mesenchymal cells, thereby achieving resistance to available therapies. Moreover, the TNMplot database confirmed significant upregulation of BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB in metastasis, with P values of BIRC5: 1.09e−89, CDCA8: 5.17e−135, INCENP: 5.37e−20, and AURKB: 8.73e−84 (Supplementary Fig. 8E–H). These findings suggest that CPC genes could serve as biomarkers for early LUAD detection and prognosis. Their upregulation positions them as promising targets for therapies aimed at inhibiting metastasis, improving patient outcomes, and enhancing the efficacy of existing treatments by overcoming resistance.

## Regulatory network of lncRNAs, miRNAs, and CPC genes identified
Furthermore, emerging evidence highlights the key role of competitive endogenous RNA networks in orchestrating gene expressions in cancer by enabling the crosstalk between lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs. In this loop, the lncRNAs and mRNAs, competitively binds to the shared miRNA, modulating its availability. Given the dynamic overexpression of CPC genes in LUAD, we hypothesize that post-transcriptional regulation via the ceRNA network may contribute to this dysregulation. Hence, to determine the miRNAs associated with CPC genes in LUAD patients, the miRNet database was used and a total of 41 miRNAs were found in the network. The UALCAN database was used to analyze their expression level in LUAD samples, where we aimed to identify downregulated miRNAs, indicating a plausible direct regulation between both. The downregulated miRNAs with a significant P value (<0.05) were selected for further analysis (Supplementary Table 3).
Interestingly, only 3 out of 41 miRNAs: hsa-let-7b-5p, hsa-let-7g-5p, and hsa-mir138-5p were found to be significantly downregulated in LUAD. Further correlation analysis of these miRNAs (hsa-let-7b-5p, hsa-let-7g-5p, and hsa-mir138-5) with the CPC genes using the ENCORI database revealed that, only hsa-let-7b-5p negatively correlated with all the genes: BIRC5 (R = −0.361), CDCA8 (−0.362), INCENP: (−0.309), and AURKB: (−0.448), as shown in Supplementary Table 4 and Fig.  A–D. Surprisingly, using the concentric circle analysis in the miRNet database, only hsa-let-7b-5p was shown to be in direct proximity with all the CPC genes (Supplementary Fig. 9A), strengthening our data. Consequently, to further assess this correlation, binding affinities between hsa-let-7b-5p and the CPC were analyzed using miRWalk database, which revealed highly favorable binding affinities ranging from −18.6 to −21.6 kcal/mol across BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB transcripts. Further, complementary structural interaction predictions using the RNA22v2 algorithm confirmed stable folding energies, with the minimum free energy of the heteroduplex structures falling between −15.40 and −18.10 kcal/mol, as shown in Table  . 
These thermodynamic values suggest that hsa-let-7b-5p can effectively bind and potentially repress these mRNAs post-transcriptionally. However, this analysis did not confirm canonical binding in all cases, these interactions should be considered putative and subject to future experimental validation. Additionally, the comparison of miRNA levels in tumor vs. normal lung tissue by using CancerMIRNome and UALCAN databases, revealed significantly lower hsa-let-7b-5p levels in LUAD tumors, suggesting its downregulation may contribute to CPC genes upregulation and tumor growth as shown in Fig.  E, F. To further validate these findings, we also analyzed the TCGA-LUAD data by normalizing the number of tumors and normal sample (n = 46 each) using R software. The boxplot consistently demonstrated significant downregulation of hsa-let-7b in LUAD samples as shown in Fig.  G. Consistent with our expression analysis showing significant downregulation of hsa-let-7b-5p in LUAD, previous studies have similarly reported reduced levels of hsa-let-7b-5p in multiple cancer types, suggesting its conserved tumor-suppressive role across malignancies [ – ]. We also examined the expression level based on smoking history using UALCAN database, as shown in Fig.  H. Furthermore, to evaluate its diagnostic potential, we analyzed the ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic) curve using the CancerMIRNome database. The AUC (Area Under the Curve) value for hsa-let-7b-5p in LUAD was found to be 0.91. An AUC value close to 1.0 indicates high accuracy in distinguishing between tumor and normal samples, suggesting that hsa-let-7b-5p may serve as a highly reliable diagnostic biomarker as shown in Fig.  I. Notably, we found significant downregulation of let-7b (HR = 0.71, CI = 0.53-0.95, P = 0.021) associated with poor prognosis as shown in Fig. J. Therefore, the above results indicate that inducing the levels of hsa-let-7b-5p can downregulate the CPC genes and potentially work as a tumor suppressor.
Next, we explored the regulatory molecule (lncRNA) influencing hsa-let-7b-5p functioning. Using the Enrichr database and lncHUB dataset we identified 69 lncRNAs associated with CPC genes. After careful evaluation of the expression level of each lncRNA using the UALCAN database, we found that 38 out of 69 lncRNAs, as mentioned in Supplementary Table 5, were significantly upregulated in LUAD tumor samples. Further, correlation analysis of upregulated lncRNAs with CPC genes using ENCORI database led to the selection of 7 strongly associated lncRNAs (R > 0.200) (Supplementary Table 6). The study aims to analyze the regulatory mechanism of the CPC genes and their role as prognostic markers, as it is well known that these genes play an important role in the cell cycle regulation and upregulation associated with poor prognosis; hence, only the lncRNA strongly associated with the cell cycle and poor prognosis of LUAD patients was chosen for further analysis. As shown in Supplementary Table 7, only TMPO-AS1 was found to be strongly associated with the cell cycle (z score = 5.815), and its overexpression was associated with poor prognosis of LUAD patients (HR = 1.5, CI = 1.29–1.74, P = 7.2e−08) and LUAD+ smoker patients (HR = 2.34, CI = 1.41–3.88, P = 0.00066) (Fig.  K, ). Further, differential expression analysis of TMPO-AS1 across different databases, such as ENCORI and UALCAN, as presented in Fig.  M, N, showed its significant overexpression in tumor conditions as compared to normal tissues, suggesting its potential oncogenic properties like those of the genes. Aligned with our finding of TMPO‑AS1 upregulation in LUAD, multiple previous studies have also reported significant TMPO‑AS1 overexpression in lung adenocarcinoma, supporting its oncogenic role in tumor proliferation, invasion and miRNA suppression [ – ]. Similarly, we also analyzed the TMPO-AS1 expression levels in TCGA-LUAD data by normalizing the number of tumors and normal sample (n = 59 each) using R software and the boxplot demonstrated significant upregulation of TMPO-AS1 expression in LUAD samples as shown in Fig.  O. Further using UALCAN database, TMPO-AS1 expression analysis based on different clinico-pathological parameters showed significant upregulation of TMPO-AS1 associated with advanced LUAD stages, males and smoker patients (Supplementary Fig. 10A–C).
The correlation analysis of TMPO-AS1 with CPC genes and hsa-let-7b-5p using the ENCORI database showed a strong positive correlation between TMPO-AS1 and the genes (BIRC5: R = 0.596, CDCA8: R = 0.631, INCENP: R = 0.557, and AURKB: R = 0.611), whereas a strong negative correlation between TMPO-AS1 and hsa-let-7b-5p (R = −0.277), as shown in Fig.  P–T was observed. These correlation values indicate that TMPO-AS1 has a strong positive association with CPC genes, suggesting that its overexpression may drive their upregulation, which is critical in cell cycle regulation. Conversely, its strong negative correlation with hsa-let-7b-5p suggests that TMPO-AS1 might act as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), functioning to sponge and inhibit this tumor-suppressive miRNA. This hypothesis is strengthened by the folding energy score of −12.10 kcal/mol predicted by RNA22v2, as listed in Table  , indicating a thermodynamically stable TMPO-AS1–hsa-let-7b-5p duplex. Such interaction could impair the miRNA's availability to bind and suppress its mRNA targets, including key mitotic regulators such as AURKB, CDCA8, INCENP, and BIRC5. These findings highlight the potential role of TMPO-AS1 as a key player in the tumorigenic processes of LUAD, especially in smoker patients. To further corroborate our findings, the correlation between the CPC genes and TMPO-AS1 was also analyzed using the OncoDB database, and similar results were found. Also, the network analysis using the miRNet database showed a direct association between the CPC genes, hsa-let-7b-5p and TMPO-AS1 (Supplementary Fig. 10D–H).

## E2F1 identified as a key transcriptional activator of CPC genes in LUAD
According to previous literature, transcription factors regulate gene expression by interacting with promoter and enhancer regions. Utilizing the Enrichr database, the E2F4 and E2F1 transcription factors were identified as being associated with all the CPC genes, as shown in Supplementary Table 8. E2Fs play a crucial role in regulating cell activity by mediating the transition between different phases of the cell cycle and influencing tumor growth. The E2F family consists of eight members: E2F1, E2F2, E2F3, E2F4, E2F5, E2F6, E2F7, and E2F8. A regression correlation was established with all E2Fs and BIRC5/CDCA8/INCENP/AURKB through the TIMER, ENCORI, and OncoDB databases, revealing a positive correlation between the E2Fs and these genes, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 11A-D and Supplementary Tables 9–11. To identify the most significant transcription factor with strong prognostic value in LUAD smokers, all E2Fs were analyzed using the KM Plotter database. The selection criteria were based on consistent significance (P < 0.05) across OS, FP, and PPS. Overexpression of E2F1, E2F4, E2F7, and E2F8 was significantly linked to poor survival in lung cancer patients. Because the article focuses on the adenocarcinoma subtype of lung cancer, further histological analysis revealed that only E2F1 (HR = 1.53, CI = 1.29–1.81, Log P = 1.1e−06, Fc = 1.6), E2F4 (HR = 1.55, CI = 1.3–1.84, Log P = 5.5e−07, Fc = 1.5), and E2F8 (HR = 1.26, CI = 1.07–1.5, Log P = 0.0073, Fc = 1.2) were particularly significant in LUAD and not in LUSC (Supplementary Table 12). Further clinicopathological analysis assessed overall survival in LUAD patients based on stage, gender, and smoking history. Among all E2Fs, only E2F1 consistently exhibited significant overexpression linked to poor survival in LUAD smokers (HR = 1.63, CI = 1.25–2.12, Log P = 0.00026, FC = 1.7) for both males (HR = 1.34, CI = 1.06–1.7, Log P = 0.013, FC = 1.4) and females (HR = 1.89, CI = 1.43–2.49, Log P = 4.2e−06, FC = 1.6) patients (Fig.  A–E and Supplementary Table 12). These findings imply that E2F1 could function as a potential biomarker for prognostic evaluation in LUAD smokers. Future research should focus on exploring the underlying molecular mechanisms of E2F1 role in LUAD and its interactions with other key genes, and developing targeted therapies that modulate E2F1 expression could provide new avenues for enhancing patient outcomes in this specific subset of lung cancer. 
Furthermore, differential expression analysis using the UALCAN database confirmed significant E2F1 overexpression in LUAD (n = 515) compared to normal (n = 59) samples (Fig.  F). Similarly, a normalized sample analysis using R packages, contrasting normal (n = 59) with tumor (n = 59) samples, demonstrated E2F1 overexpression in LUAD samples (Fig.  G). Additionally, LUAD patients with a smoking history (Fig.  H) and males compared to females (Fig.  I), exhibited E2F1 upregulation in smoker patients and males, respectively. To complete the regulatory mechanism, correlation analysis revealed that E2F1 positively correlates with BIRC5 (R = 0.706), CDCA8 (R = 0.746), INCENP (R = 0.657), AURKB (R = 0.736), and TMPO-AS1 (R = 0.581), while a negative correlation with hsa-let-7b-5p (R = −0.365) was observed (Fig.  J–O). The CPC-E2F1 correlation was further investigated using the ClusPro 2.0 server. ClusPro docking models were evaluated based on their weighted scores, a composite of van der Waals, electrostatic, and desolvation energies used to rank biologically plausible binding conformations. All CPC genes exhibited highly favorable interaction scores with E2F1, supporting the hypothesis of stable and biologically relevant complex formation. The weighted scores (lowest energy/center) obtained were CDCA8 (−1069.8/−1013.1), AURKB (−981.8/−924.9), BIRC 5 (−916.9/−827.7), and INCENP (−848.2/−723.8), as listed in Supplementary Table 13. The resulting docked models were further visualized using PyMOL software, revealing complementary binding interfaces and stabilizing interactions across all complexes. The E2F1–BIRC5 complex displays multiple stabilizing contacts involving ARG 106 and GLU 301, suggesting a stable interaction interface. E2F1 binding to CDCA8 is mediated by a network of residues including GLU 301, VAL 258, MET 263, ARG 66, ILE 284, ASN 52, LYS 48, and ASP 277, reflecting a combination of polar and electrostatic interactions. The interaction with INCENP involves key residues such as TYR 825, ASP 822, VAL 821, ILE 265, LYS 266, TYR 815, PRO 268, THR 271, VAL 262, and MET 263, highlighting a mix of hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions. In particular, the E2F1–AURKB complex consists of hydrogen bonding between HIS 133, GLU 301, LEU 297, ASP 294, LYS 266, TYR 145, and VAL 262, suggesting a strong polar interaction that potentially contributes to complex stability (Fig.  A–D). Furthermore, to investigate whether the inverse association between hsa-let-7b-5p and E2F1 is supported by direct miRNA–mRNA interaction, we examined the binding potential of hsa-let-7b-5p with E2F1. Using miRWalk, the predicted binding affinity was found to be −22.4 4 kcal/mol, while RNA 22 v 2 reported a folding energy of −13.3 kcal/mol, indicating a thermodynamically feasible interaction, as shown in Table  . 

## Heterogeneous model with CPC genes: its role
In cancer biology, a heterogenous model refers to the variability in expression levels, signaling pathways, and regulatory mechanisms, leading to different therapeutic responses. And hence, co-expressed genes associated with the CPC genes were identified using the Enrichr database. Listed are the top 10 co-expressed genes for individual target genes, BIRC5: AURKB, CCNB2, CDC20, CENPA, HJURP, KIF20A, KIF2C, RRM2, TPX2, and TROAP. CDCA8 co-expressed genes: AURKB, CDC20, CDC45, CDCA5, CENPA, KIF2C, KIFC1, PLK1, TPX2, and ZWINT. INCENP co-expressed genes: CAD, CHAF1A, GSG2, KHSRP, KIFC1, LMNB2, NCAPD2, RECQL4, and SSRP1. AURKB co-expressed genes: BIRC5, CDC20, CDC45, CDCA5, CDCA8, KIF2C, KIFC1, PLK1, RRM2, and TROAP. The regression correlation between these genes was validated using the TIMER database (Supplementary Fig. 12A–D), and a positive correlation was found between them, indicating that these genes may participate in common biological pathways involved in tumor growth and progression and can be considered for future research prospects. These co-expressed genes may influence several critical biological pathways, including cell cycle regulation, apoptosis, and DNA repair mechanisms. For instance, pathways involving mitotic spindle assembly and chromosomal segregation may be significantly affected, given the roles of genes like CPCs.

## CPC genes and their potential therapeutic targets
After establishing the role of CPCs, as well as miRNA-hsa-let-7b-5p and lncRNA TMPO-AS1, as potential predictive biomarkers in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), we aimed to further explore the therapeutic potential of targeting these CPC proteins in LUAD. To achieve this, we conducted a molecular docking analysis to assess the interaction profiles of key CPC genes with a selection of ligands relevant to cancer treatment. The compounds chosen for this analysis were significant in cancer therapy and had the potential to modulate gene functions. These included natural compounds known for their anti-cancer properties, such as hesperidin and quercetin, which exhibit anti-proliferative, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant effects. Additionally, we included paclitaxel and docetaxel, widely used chemotherapeutic agents that target microtubules and are specific to the G2/M phase of the cell cycle. Finally, we incorporated barasertib, a selective Aurora kinase inhibitor that has shown promise in preclinical models by specifically targeting AURKB and inhibiting its activity.
Our molecular docking analysis revealed distinct interaction profiles of CPC proteins—AURKB, BIRC5, CDCA8, and INCENP—with the selected ligands. These findings, summarized in Table  , provide insights into the binding affinities of each ligand and their potential as therapeutic agents for targeting CPC proteins in LUAD. Among all the tested ligands, hesperidin displayed the strongest binding affinity for AURKB (−9.4 kcal/mol), supported by a network of conventional hydrogen bonds, π–π stacking, and π–alkyl interactions, suggesting high structural complementarity and binding stability. In comparison, barasertib, the reference compound for AURKB inhibition, showed a relatively lower affinity (−7.0 kcal/mol) while forming multiple hydrogen and carbon–hydrogen bonds, as illustrated in Fig.  A–E. For BIRC5, hesperidin again demonstrated significant binding (−7.0 kcal/mol) through hydrogen bonds with SER77 and ASN64, as well as π–alkyl interactions and van der Waals forces, indicating a high level of engagement at the survivin-binding pocket. Barasertib and docetaxel exhibited moderate affinities (−6.3 and −6.8 kcal/mol, respectively), while quercetin formed a relatively stable complex (−6.7 kcal/mol). Similarly, hesperidin exhibited strong binding to CDCA8 (−7.1 kcal/mol), involving hydrogen bonds, π-cation and van der Waals interactions, indicating a robust interaction within the catalytic pocket. Paclitaxel, although a chemotherapeutic drug, showed comparable affinity for CDCA8 (−7.3 kcal/mol) via multiple π–alkyl and hydrogen bonding interactions with specific residues. Docetaxel also demonstrated stable binding to CDCA8 (−6.6 kcal/mol), characterized by π-stacking and polar interactions with relevant residues. Further in the case of INCENP, hesperidin (−5.7 kcal/mol) exhibited a favorable interaction network, forming conventional hydrogen bonds with critical residues, in addition to π–alkyl and van der Waals forces. Barasertib (−5.0 kcal/mol) and quercetin (−5.0 kcal/mol) showed fewer interactions, while compounds like docetaxel (−5.3 kcal/mol) and paclitaxel (−5.7 kcal/mol) exhibited enriched contact networks involving π–cation and carbon–hydrogen bonds, which could potentially modulate INCENP's activity. These findings highlight that hesperidin can form biologically significant interactions with INCENP. Overall, the results suggest that hesperidin consistently exhibited the most favorable binding profiles among CPC components, indicating its promise as a broad-spectrum natural modulator of mitotic regulators. The diverse range of stabilizing interactions, including hydrogen bonding, π-systems, and hydrophobic contacts, affirms its potential to compete with or surpass conventional inhibitors like barasertib and chemotherapeutics such as paclitaxel or docetaxel in CPC-targeted therapeutic strategies. 

## Immune cell infiltration pattern highlights a potential immunosuppressive role of CPC Genes
Tumor-infiltrating immune cells play an essential role in shaping the tumor microenvironment (TME), influencing tumor progression, therapeutic resistance, and clinical outcomes. The dynamic interactions between cancer cells and infiltrating immune cells contribute to tumor heterogeneity, often leading to the creation of immunosuppressive niches that allow for immune evasion. Alterations in the intrinsic gene expression of tumors can affect patterns of immune cell infiltration, ultimately impacting immune surveillance. To investigate the immunological relevance of CPC gene expression, a spring viewer database was utilized to visualize immune cell subpopulations in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells using a trajectory-based immune landscape, as shown in Fig.  A. Distinct immune cell clusters were identified, and when these were overlaid with gene expression patterns, all four CPC genes demonstrated strong to moderate expression across major immune compartments. Among these, BIRC5 showed a strong correlation with immune cells, likely due to its dual role in inhibiting apoptosis and modulating immune responses, including T cell survival and dendritic cell function [ – ]. Whereas AURKB, CDCA8, and INCENP primarily involved in mitotic regulation, may exhibit moderate correlations in immune cell signaling. This suggests that CPC genes play a role in immune evasion and tumor progression, as illustrated in Fig.  B–E. Additionally, the expression levels of E2F1, TMPO-AS1, and hsa-let-7b were examined. E2F1 and TMPO-AS1 were found to be expressed within immune clusters, while no expression of hsa-let-7b was detected in any cell cluster, as shown in Fig.  F–H. To statistically validate these observations, a correlation analysis was performed between CPC genes, along with E2F1 and TMPO-AS1, and immune cell infiltration in LUAD samples. The results revealed a significant negative correlation with CD4+ T cells, follicular B helper T cells (Tfh), T helper cells (Th2), natural killer T cells, and CD8+ cell infiltration (Supplementary Fig. 16A). Furthermore, the combined gene set expression score exhibited a strong negative correlation with CD4+ T cells (R = −0.57) (Supplementary Fig. 16B, C), suggesting that the overexpression of CPCs, E2F1, and TMPO-AS1 could lead to the suppression of adaptive immune cell infiltration, particularly CD4+ T cells, highlighting a potential mechanism of immune evasion in LUAD. 

## Discussion
CPC is a critical component of cell division, ensuring equal segregation of duplicated sister chromatids during M phase of the cell cycle [ ]. Its dynamic and cellular localization is essential for understanding the link between chromosome segregation and cytokinesis. Master regulators, such as inner centromere protein (INCENP), forms a complex with Aurora B kinase, both of which are vital for proper cell division [ ]. The CPC accumulates at the inner centromeres, which is necessary for establishing effective microtubule attachment to mitotic chromosomes [ ]. Moreover, the CPC regulates the timing of nuclear envelope reformation (NER), chromosome decondensation, and the completion of cytokinesis during mitotic exit [ ]. Recent findings underscore CPC’s critical role in coordinating these events, further establishing it as a central regulator of genomic stability. Consequently, dysregulation of Aurora B has been linked to increased genomic instability and tumorigenesis, directing current efforts to target this kinase for anti-cancer therapies. Anti-microtubule drugs, such as paclitaxel, and Aurora B inhibitors show promise in cancer treatment due to their ability to inhibit microtubule function and prevent tumor cell division [ ]. However, further research is needed to determine the effective dosage of these inhibitors and identify efficient biomarkers for solid tumors. The search for such biomarkers is complicated by the inherent complexity and heterogeneity of cancer, which stems not only from the dysregulation or overexpression of various genes and cellular pathways but also from diverse factors such as variable tumor microenvironments, genetic mutations, and therapeutic resistance [ ]. This complexity is particularly evident in lung cancer, where diagnosis and prognosis often rely on mutational biomarkers such as EGFR, KRAS, ALK, ROS1, MET, BRAF, ERBB2, and RET. However, these biomarkers have limitations in terms of specificity and sensitivity [ ]. In contrast to this, gene expression-based biomarkers, including miRNAs and lncRNAs, have shown potential for early diagnosis and improved prognosis [ ].
In this study, we integrated multi-omics analyses to identify the prognostic and therapeutic significance of CPC genes: BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, and AURKB within a regulatory ceRNA network, providing a comprehensive view of their role in LUAD, especially among smokers. Our findings highlight the oncogenic potential of CPC genes in LUAD, underscored by their significant overexpression and strong association with poor patient survival. This dysregulation aligns with previous literature suggesting that CPC components not only regulate mitotic events but, when dysregulated, can drive chromosomal instability and promote tumor aggressiveness. Notably, we observed this effect to be highly significant in smoker cohorts, suggesting that environmental carcinogens such as nicotine and NNK may enhance CPC activity through direct molecular interactions, as supported by our docking studies.
A key strength of our study lies in uncovering the TMPO-AS1/hsa-let-7b-5p ceRNA axis, which offers novel insight into the post-transcriptional regulation of CPC genes. The downregulation of hsa-let-7b-5p, a tumor-suppressor miRNA, and its negative correlation with CPC gene expression suggest a loss of post-transcriptional repression contributing to CPC upregulation. On the other hand, TMPO-AS1, a lncRNA significantly overexpressed in LUAD, likely serves as a molecular sponge that sequesters hsa-let-7b-5p, further promoting CPC expression. Additionally, the stable binding interactions within this ceRNA network underscore its functional significance and therapeutic potential. These findings are consistent with and extend our previous work on the hsa-let-7b-5p/TMPO-AS1/E2F1 regulatory axis, where we reported the involvement of downstream effectors such as FOXM1, MAD2L1, NCAPG, and NUSAP1 in lung cancer progression [ – ]. However, the current study is the first to integrate this regulatory axis with the CPC complex, suggesting that the same ceRNA circuit may broadly control multiple mitotic effectors to drive LUAD progression and immune suppression in smokers.
Transcription factors play a pivotal role in regulating gene expression by binding to specific promoter regions of gene sequence and modulate its transcriptional activity [ ]. Further strengthening this ceRNA axis, our study found E2F1 as a master regulator of CPC genes. Our analyses demonstrate a strong positive correlation between E2F1 and CPC genes-AURKB, BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP expression, along with its association with poorer prognosis, particularly in smokers. Furthermore, molecular docking between E2F1 and CPC proteins supports that E2F1 may enhance CPC gene expression both transcriptionally and through direct interaction. Notably, E2F1 is found to be negatively correlated with hsa-let-7b-5p, thereby suggesting a broader regulatory feedback loop that reinforces CPC activity in LUAD.
Further, in the search for novel therapeutic strategies, we explored the potential of targeting CPC proteins using both established chemotherapeutics and naturally derived compounds. Paclitaxel and docetaxel, widely used in NSCLC therapy, act by stabilizing microtubules, thereby arresting cells in mitosis [ ]. Despite their efficacy, these agents are often associated with drug resistance and systemic toxicity, necessitating the introduction of alternative or complementary strategies. Similarly, Barasertib, a selective Aurora kinase B inhibitor, is associated with narrow therapeutic application [ ]. To address these limitations, we investigated hesperidin, a natural flavonoid found in citrus fruits, known for its anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic, and anti-inflammatory effects in various cancers, including lung cancer [ ]. Unlike traditional chemotherapeutics, hesperidin exhibits lower toxicity and has been reported to induce cell cycle arrest, making it a promising candidate for cancer therapy [ ]. Quercetin, another plant-derived compound included in our analysis, has also shown to target key regulators of the cell cycle and induces chemosensitivity in cancer cells [ ]. In support to this, our molecular docking results highlighted the strong binding affinity of hesperidin, particularly to AURKB and other CPC proteins, compared with the conventional drugs.
Beyond molecular regulation, our immune landscape analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between CPC gene expression and immune cell infiltration, particularly CD4⁺ T cells. This suggests that CPC overexpression may also contribute to immune evasion, which is a hallmark of advanced stages, highlighting CPC genes as a potential immune checkpoint activity. Altogether, this study uncovers a tightly regulated axis involving TMPO-AS1, hsa-let-7b-5p, E2F1, and CPC genes that is dysregulated in LUAD. This integrated regulatory network contributes to tumor proliferation, immune suppression, and poor prognosis. Despite the comprehensive nature of our integrative bioinformatics analysis, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The analysis is based on publicly available datasets and computational tools which, although robust, may carry inherent biases or inconsistencies due to differences in data acquisition, processing pipelines, or sample diversity. The absence of experimental validation further limits the ability to confirm the functional relevance of some predicted interactions. Additionally, while our analytical pipeline incorporated standard normalization and statistical testing procedures, more advanced statistical controls, such as batch effect correction, covariate modeling, and formal multiple testing adjustments, were not applied. Some sources of technical or biological variability may therefore remain unaccounted for. Nevertheless, the focused, hypothesis-driven nature of our study, along with cross-validation of key findings across multiple independent databases (including UALCAN and KM Plotter), helps to mitigate these limitations and lends greater confidence to our results. Ultimately, the strength of in silico analysis lies in its ability to generate biologically meaningful hypotheses and provide a foundational framework for future experimental validation.

## Conclusion
The study highlights the importance of CPC genes and their regulatory molecules, hsa-let-7b-5p and TMPO-AS1, as prognostic biomarkers in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) patients. Targeting these genes could lead to the development of new therapeutic strategies for improving patient outcomes. By modulating their expression, we may be able to influence tumor progression and enhance the efficacy of current treatment options. Personalized medicine approaches can be designed by incorporating these biomarkers into diagnostic panels, allowing for tailored interventions based on the specific profiles of individual patients. Moreover, our study supports the therapeutic potential of natural compounds like hesperidin as promising modulators of CPC proteins. Additionally, the study found a negative correlation between CPC gene expression and the immune landscape. This suggests that higher levels of CPC gene expression may inhibit the CD4+ T cells induced adaptive immunity. The dysregulation of CPC genes contributes to increased proliferation, invasion, and metastatic potential in LUAD. Furthermore, the combined expression of CPC genes, TMPO-AS1, hsa-let-7b-5p, and E2F1 may serve as a reliable prognostic biomarker panel for LUAD patients.

## Methods

## Employed databases
This study employed a variety of publicly available databases to comprehensively analyze gene expression, regulatory networks, and the prognostic relevance of CPC genes and associated non- coding RNAs in lung cancer as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 17. UALCAN (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu) [ ] was used for TCGA- based gene expression profiling across various cancers, providing clinicopathological correlations that aid in biomarker identification. TIMER 2.0 (http://timer.cistrome.org) [ ] facilitated the exploration of differential gene expressions across TCGA cancers. Firehose Broad GDAC (https://gdac.broadinstitute.org/#) [ ] aggregates and standardizes TCGA genomic data for large- scale cancer dataset analysis. ENCORI (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) [ ] enabled the analysis of differential expressions of genes and miRNAs, RNA- RNA interactions, and regulatory networks in cancer, while TCGAnalyzer (http://tcganalyzer.mu.edu.tr) [ ], a platform based on transcriptome and scRNA- seq, allowed for a deeper exploration of gene expression in TCGA datasets. Additionally, CancerSEA (http://biocc.hrbmu.edu.cn/CancerSEA/) [ ] characterized single- cell functional states to explore gene involvement in various biological processes, and TNMplot (https://tnmplot.com/analysis/) [ ] compared gene expression between normal, tumor, and metastatic tissues. For survival analysis, we used the Kaplan- Meier Plotter (KM Plotter) (https://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php?p=background) [ ], which integrates gene expression with survival data to evaluate overall survival, first progression, and post- progression survival. We utilized miRNet (https://www.mirnet.ca) [ ], a tool for miRNA- centric network analysis, and CancerMIRNome (http://bioinfo.jialab-ucr.org/CancerMIRNome/) [ ], which specializes in cancer- associated miRNA expression profiling. Associations between lncRNAs, transcription factors, and CPC genes were identified using Enrichr (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/) [ ] and lncHUB (https://maayanlab.cloud/lnchub) [ ]. Moreover, OncoDB (https://oncodb.org) [ ] served as a comprehensive resource for integrated cancer genomic and clinical data. miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de) [ ] and RNA22v2 (https://cm.jefferson.edu/rna22) [ ] were employed to predict miRNA-mRNA, miRNA-TF, and miRNA-lncRNA interactions, supporting ceRNA network construction. SPRING Viewer database (https://kleintools.hms.harvard.edu/tools/springViewer_1_6_dev.html?datasets/Zilionis2019/human/NSCLC_all_cells) [ ], developed by Harvard Medical School, was used to visualize scRNA-seq data from NSCLC samples using a k-nearest neighbors (kNN) graph to explore gene expression across immune and non-immune cell types. GSCA was used to assess pathway activity and immune correlations in LUAD using GSVA, a non-parametric algorithm that quantifies pathway enrichment across samples (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/GSCA/#/) [ ].

## Expression analysis of CPC genes associated with LUAD and smokers
A pan-cancer analysis was conducted by using publicly available databases such as UALCAN, TIMER 2.0, and Firehose Broad GDAC. Further mRNA expression of CPC genes in LUAD was analyzed using UALCAN and ENCORI. To analyze the normalized gene expression of CPC genes, data was acquired from TCGA-LUAD utilizing the TCGAbiolinks (V_2.3.4.1) R package within RStudio (V_4.4.2). The script used TCGAbiolinks to query, download, and prepare transcriptome profiling data (RNA-Seq, STAR—Counts workflow). FPKM-unstranded normalized expression data was extracted from the resulting SummarizedExperiment object using GDCprepare, along with associated clinical information. The Ensembl ID for AURKB ("ENSG00000178999"), Survivin ("ENSG00000089685"), Borealin ("ENSG00000134690") and INCENP ("ENSG00000149503") was precisely identified using the biomaRt (V_2.62.1) R package, which is connected to the Ensembl database (hsapiens_gene_ensembl dataset) for gene annotation. The dataset was subsequently filtered using dplyr (V_1.1.4) to include only "Primary Tumor" and "Solid Tissue Normal" samples, and a balanced subset of 59 samples from each group was created using a set.seed(123) for reproducibility. A Wilcoxon rank-sum test was then performed to statistically assess the differential expression of CPC genes between these two distinct groups. The findings were visually represented through a boxplot generated with ggplot2 (V_3.5.2), which included overlaid jittered points, annotated sample counts, and the calculated p-value to clearly highlight the statistical significance. Furthermore, the transcriptome analysis was performed using TCGAnalyzer database. The expression of CPC genes based on different clinicopathological characteristics such as gender, lymph node status, stages and smoking history, were examined in tumor samples from lung cancer patients by using the UALCAN database. To explore the impact of smoking-related carcinogens, molecular docking of CPC proteins with NNK (PubChem CID: 123980) and Nicotine (PubChem CID: 89594) were performed. Protein and ligand structures were prepared using UCSF Chimera and AutoDock Tools 1.5.7, followed by docking simulations via AutoDock Vina. Further, CancerSEA, and TNMplot were employed to assess the involvement of CPC genes in various biological processes and metastasis.

## Prognostic significance of CPC genes
The Kaplan–Meier Plotter was used to evaluate univariate parameters such as overall survival (OS), first progression (FP), and post-progression survival (PPS) in lung cancer patients, specifically those with lung adenocarcinoma and lung squamous cell carcinoma. The analysis also considered clinicopathological characteristics, including gender and smoking history. Additionally, a multivariate analysis was conducted using the mean expression of CPC genes across the parameters. Patients were categorized based on their gene expression levels, using the median to divide them into low expression and high expression groups. The data retrieval employed the following terms: "Cancer: LUAD" and "Gene symbols: BIRC5, CDCA8, INCENP, AURKB (STK12)." The associated Affymetrix IDs included 202094_at, 221520_s_at, 219769_at, and 209464_at, respectively. Furthermore, data acquisition utilized various Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets, including GSE102287, GSE14814, GSE157011, GSE19188, GSE29013, GSE30219, GSE31210, GSE3141, GSE31908, GSE37745, GSE43580, GSE4573, GSE50081, GSE68465, GSE77803, GSE8894, and TCGA.

## Regulatory ceRNA network of CPC genes
The miRNA network associated with CPC (AURKB, BIRC5, CDCA8, and INCENP) was constructed using the miRNet database. Correlations between CPC genes and miRNAs were validated through ENCORI, while miRNA expression profiles were assessed using CancerMIRNome, UALCAN, and R packages (with normalized samples). Prognostic relevance of these miRNAs in lung cancer was evaluated via the KM Plotter. Subsequently, lncRNAs targeting CPC genes were identified using Enrichr and their expression levels in LUAD were analyzed using UALCAN. Correlation analyses between lncRNAs, CPC genes, and miRNAs were further performed through ENCORI database. Cell cycle–associated lncRNA was further screened using the lncHUB database and its involvement in ceRNA network formation was analyzed through miRNet. Differential expression and prognostic relevance of the lncRNA was subsequently evaluated using UALCAN, OncoDB, and the KM Plotter. Survival analysis and Expression profiles of miRNA and lncRNA has been previously described in our earlier work [ ], and reused in this study for comparative and contextual purposes. Furthermore, the interaction between hsa-let-7b-5p vs. CPC and TMPO-AS1 was analyzed using miRWalk and RNA22v2 databases to predict their binding and folding affinities, evaluating the stability and potential regulatory impact of the miRNA on mRNA and lncRNA expression.

## Transcription factor and heterogeneity associated with CPC genes
The transcription factors associated with the CPC genes were determined using the Enrichr database. Further, correlation analysis between the transcription factor and CPC genes was performed using TIMER, ENCORI, and OncoDB databases. The prognostic relevance of E2F transcription factors (E2F1–E2F8) in LUAD patients was assessed using the KM Plotter, with the following Affymetrix IDs: E2F1: 2028_s_at, E2F2: 207042_at, E2F3: 203693_s_at, E2F4: 202248_at, E2F5: 221586_s_at, E2F6: 203957_at, E2F7: 228033_at, and E2F8: 219990_at. Additionally, differential expressions and pathological status of the TF were analyzed using the UALCAN database. Further, the correlation between the regulatory network: miRNA, lncRNA and transcription factor was analyzed using ENCORI. The binding affinities between the CPC genes and E2F1 were further analyzed using ClusPro 2.0 server. ClusPro weighted score, is a composite scoring function used to rank predicted docking poses. The top-ranked models with weighted scores of >700 was selected. The best docked complexes were further visualized for interaction analysis using Pymol software. Additionally, to construct a comprehensive heterogeneous model of LUAD, co-expressed genes of the target molecules were identified using Enrichr and TIMER databases.

## Therapeutic targeting of CPC genes
Molecular docking was performed to investigate the interaction profiles between CPC proteins: AURKB (PDB ID: 4AF3), BIRC5 (PDB ID: 2RAW), INCENP (PDB ID: 2BFY), and CDCA8 (PDB ID: 2RAW), and selected ligands, including selective AURKB inhibitor, Barasertib (Pubchem CID: 11497983), natural compounds (Hesperidin, PubChem CID: 10621; Quercetin, PubChem CID: 5280343) and chemotherapeutic agents (Paclitaxel, CID: 36314; Docetaxel, CID: 148124). The 3D structures of protein targets were retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB), while ligand structures were obtained from the PubChem database in SDF format. Ligand structure minimization and protein structure cleaning was done using UCSF Chimera. Protein preparation including removal of water molecules, addition of polar hydrogens, and assignment of Kollman charges was performed using AutoDock Tools 1.5.7. The active site was defined by constructing a grid box around the binding pocket based on structural cues. AutoDock Vina was then used to perform molecular docking simulations, calculating binding affinities (in kcal/mol) for each ligand–protein complex. Ten docking poses were generated per ligand, and the top-ranked conformation with the lowest binding energy was selected. Binding interaction analysis of the selected conformations, such as hydrogen bonds, π–π stacking, π–alkyl interactions, van der Waals forces, and unfavourable steric contacts was conducted using AutoDock Tools. Finally, the best-docked complexes were visualized in Discovery Studio to generate 2D interaction diagrams.

## Immune cell infiltration analysis
To investigate the immunological relevance of CPC genes, we analyzed immune cell infiltration using trajectory-based immune mapping and correlation analyses. Single-cell immune landscape visualization was performed using the SPRING viewer database, which groups immune cell populations based on transcriptional similarity specific to NSCLC. Gene expression patterns of CPC genes, along with regulatory molecules (E2F1, TMPO-AS1, and hsa-let-7b-5p), were overlaid onto these immune clusters to assess their distribution across immune compartments. Subsequently, we performed immune cell correlation analysis using the GSCA database for LUAD specific data to statistically validate the expression vs. immune infiltration relationship. Finally, a gene set variation analysis (GSVA) score was computed for the combined expression profile of CPC genes, E2F1, and TMPO-AS1, and its association with immune cell infiltration was assessed.

## Statistical analysis
This study analyzed gene expression differences between tumor and normal lung tissues using both In-silico tools and R-based statistical approaches. An online gene set analysis model was employed to explore associations between gene expression and patient prognosis. In parallel, RNA sequencing data from the TCGA-LUAD dataset were processed in R, where log₂-transformed expression values were analyzed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (P < 0.05). Visualization was performed using the ggplot2 package, highlighting differential expression patterns of CPC genes, transcription factors (TFs), miRNAs, and lncRNAs. To further account for potential confounding factors, the study extended its analysis beyond this script by evaluating AURKB expression in relation to clinicopathological parameters, including cancer stage and gender. Expression trends and survival outcomes were independently validated using platforms such as UALCAN and KM Plotter, providing a robust, multi-dataset assessment of AURKB’s role in LUAD. A comprehensive summary of all databases and tools used in the study, including their specific purpose, data type, and underlying statistical model is provided in Supplementary Table 14.
