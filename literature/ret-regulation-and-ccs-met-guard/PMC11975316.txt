## Abstract

## Introduction
Moderate vitamin A levels during pregnancy are strongly related to normal embryonic development in both animal models and population studies. Abnormal development of urinary tract system is linked to either an excess or a shortage of vitamin A. The relationships among maternal vitamin A deficiency prior to conception, moderate vitamin A supplementation during pregnancy, and abnormal urinary system development in offspring are unclear.

## Methods
By creating preconception and preconception + pregnancy vitamin A insufficiency mouse models, we investigated whether moderate vitamin A treatment during pregnancy may reduce the prevalence of CAKUT and increase distant vitamin A levels in offspring, as well as any potential pathways involved.

## Results
We effectively established a prepregnancy vitamin A-deficient mouse model by providing a particular diet with or without vitamin A for 4 weeks. The offspring of the hypovitaminosis A model group presented a greater proportion of neonatal urinary tract developmental malformations. Abnormalities in ureteral bud emergence and key molecules during renal development, such as p-Plcγ and Ret, may be the primary causes of offspring development of CAKUT as a result of mothers’ hypovitaminosis A. Normal vitamin A diets, on the other hand, may help mitigate the teratogenic consequences of prepregnancy hypovitaminosis A, as well as defects produced by ureteral budding and major molecular changes.

## Conclusion
In contrast, the administration of normal vitamin A feeds during pregnancy may ameliorate the teratogenic effects of prepregnancy hypovitaminosis A to a certain extent and may also ameliorate the abnormalities associated with ureteral budding and key molecular changes.

## Vitamin A Level Assay
To measure serum all-trans-vitamin A levels via ACQUITY UPLC H-class/Xevo TQ-XS (Waters, Milford, MA, USA) by via the retinol standard (CDAA-210005, Anpel), serum was collected from model rats fed a special diet for 4 weeks, E11.5 days of gestation, at delivery, and at 7 weeks of age in each group of offspring.

## Phenotypic Analysis
Following CO2 anesthesia in each group of pregnant mice at E11.5, E12.5, and E15.5, the number and location of ureteral buds were observed, along with the number of branches. The urinary system was dissected from five groups of neonatal mice, and the position, morphology, and number of kidneys, the morphology and number of ureters, and the morphology of the bladder of the newborn pups were observed under a fluorescence microscope. CAKUT definition and classification refer to this literature [ ].

## RT-PCR
On E11.5, embryonic kidneys were separated into five groups, and total RNA was extracted via the RNeasy Mini Kit. Reverse transcription of the same mass of RNA was used to generate cDNA, which was subsequently amplified and subjected to real-time PCR analysis. Each group consisted of three parallel reaction tubes with three samples, all of which were kept under light protection. The primers used were shown in online supplementary Table 1 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000541289).

## Tissue Stained by Immunofluorescence in Embryonic Kidneys
Five groups of E11.5-day embryos were divided under an anatomical microscope. Sections that had been formaldehyde-fixed and paraffin-embedded were stained with anti-E-cadherin (GB12082, Servicebio, China, 1:200) and contained with Ret (3223S, Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA, 1:200) p-Akt (9271, Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA, 1:200), p-Erk1/2 (4370S, Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA, 1:200), and p-Plcγ (8713, Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA, 1:200) antibodies. The nuclei were labeled with DAPI (G1012, Servicebio, China).

## Staining Using RNAscope
Anatomical microscopy was used to isolate five groups of E11.5-day embryos, which were then formaldehyde-fixed using the RNAscope Multiplex Fluorescent Reagent Kit v2 (323100, Advanced Cell Diagnostics, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Probed were used: Mm-Ret (431791-C2, Advanced Cell Diagnostics, USA), positive control probe (320881, Advanced Cell Diagnostics, USA), and negative control probe (320871, Advanced Cell Diagnostics, USA).

## Detection of Apoptosis and Proliferation in Embryonic Kidney Tissue
Under an anatomical microscope, formaldehyde-fixed and paraffin-embedded slices from five groups of E11.5-day embryos were examined. Proliferation was identified by immunohistochemistry with anti-Ki67 (GB121141, Servicebio, China, 1:500) staining, and a TUNEL apoptosis detection kit (C1089, Beyotime, China) was used to detect cell death.

## H3K4me3 Cut-Tag
To produce libraries, follow the manufacturer’s instructions with the Novoprotein® CUT&Tag® 4.0 High-Sensitivity Kit (N259-YH01, Novoprotein, Shanghai, China). Novogene carried out library sequencing and data analysis.

## Data Analysis
With the use of Prism 10 software and SPSS, all the statistical data in this study were produced and analyzed. The findings were evaluated using the mean ± standard variance, with p < 0.05 indicating a difference that was statistically significant and p > 0.05 representing no statistically significant distinction (NS). Two-sided unpaired t tests were used for between-group data, and χ2 tests were used for assessing count data.

## Results

## Effective Establishment of Female Mouse Models with Inadequate Vitamin A Levels
Female mice that received no vitamin A diet, a low-vitamin A diet, or a normal vitamin A diet for 4 weeks before conception were successfully modeled as complete vitamin A deficiency, partial vitamin A deficiency, or normal vitamin A. Each week during the modeling period, the body weight and dietary intake of the mice were measured, and there was no difference between the groups (shown in b, c). After 4 weeks of modeling, the serum vitamin A levels of the mice in each group were detected via Acquity UPLC H-class/Xevo TQ-XS. The levels of vitamin A in the model mice, which were completely and partially deficient in vitamin A, were significantly lower than those in the normal group (0.50 ± 0.12 vs. 0.47 ± 0.07 vs. 1.49 ± 0.09 μmol/L, p = 0.0156; p < 0.0009) (shown in d).
After the serum vitamin A levels in the mice were measured via UPLC at E11.5 and P0.5, it was found that all the groups were vitamin A-deficient, and there was no difference in vitamin A levels at E11.5. On P0.5, however, the serum vitamin A levels in the model group substantially increased to normal levels and slightly decreased compared with those in the CON group (shown in e).
The body weights of the newborn mice did not differ significantly among the groups (shown in f), nor did the length of the top rump of the embryos at E11.5 or E12.5 (shown in g, h). The number of offspring in the neonatal and early embryonic phases did not differ ( i); however, the female mice in the SVAD group had a somewhat longer gestation cycle by 0.5–1 day (shown in j).

## The Offspring of Female Model Mice with Inadequate Vitamin A Levels Had a Higher Incidence of CAKUT
When comparing the neonatal kidney phenotype of the offspring mice in each group, the incidence of CAKUT in the TVAD offspring was significantly greater than that in the CON offspring (27.94% [19/68] vs. 1.79% [1/56], p < 0.0001), with the incidence of CAKUT in the male offspring being slightly greater than that in the female offspring (33.33% [12/36] vs. 21.88% [7/32], p = 0.2932) and the incidence of CAKUT in the SVAD offspring was slightly greater than that in the CON offspring (11.90% [5/42] vs. 1.79% [1/56], p = 0.0387). Additionally, normal vitamin A supplementation during pregnancy somewhat reduced the incidence of CAKUT in the offspring, with the incidence of CAKUT in the TVAS offspring being 8.11% (3/37) and the incidence in the SVAS offspring being 7.14% (3/42), both of which were slightly greater than that in the CON offspring (shown in a). In TVAD offspring, the CAKUT phenotype was 10.81% (8/74) for duplicated collection systems and 14.86% (11/74) for collection system expansion according to the specified phenotype (shown in b).
 There were no significant differences in body weight, kidney weight, renal symmetry by weight or kidney weight/body weight ratio, or sex between the offspring in each group and the offspring of those with normal kidney phenotypes at P0.5 or P10 (shown in online suppl. Fig. 1). At six postnatal weeks, the body weight, kidney weight, and kidney weight/body weight ratio of the offspring in each group did not differ significantly; nevertheless, the male offspring generally weighed more than the female offspring did (shown in online suppl. Fig. 1).
An UPLC assay of vitamin A levels in pup mice in each group at 7 weeks postnatally revealed that while all of the levels were above normal, the levels in the TVAD group were significantly lower than those in the CON group. However, there was no difference in the levels of vitamin A between the TVAS, SVAD, and SVAS groups and the CON group. No sex differences were observed (shown in c).

## The Vitamin A-Deficient Offspring of Pregnant Mice Presented Phenotypic Abnormalities in Ureteral Buds at the Embryonic Stage
When observing the ureteric bud emergence phenomenon at E11.5, the percentages of ectopic ureteric buds emerging in TVAD and SVAD were 8.33% (4/48) and 5.26% (3/57) higher, respectively, than those in the control group 1.20% (1/83); in contrast, the percentages of ectopic ureteric buds emerging in TVAS and SVAS decreased to 1.96% (1/51) and 2.27% (1/44), respectively (shown in d).
Common nephric duct (CND) length measurements in each group revealed that at E11.5, the CND lengths in the TVAD and SVAD groups tended to be shorter than those in the normal group (197.00 ± 8.23 vs. 180.80 ± 10.62 vs. 229.40 ± 9.74 μm, p = 0.0147; p = 0.0022), whereas the CND lengths in the TVAS and SVAS groups recovered and were comparable to those in the normal group (shown in e). At E12.5, there was no difference in the CND lengths among any group (shown in f). On E12.5, there was no discernible difference in the number of ureteric bud branches among the groups (shown in g). On E15.5, there was no discernible difference in the length of kidney among the groups (shown in h).

## During the UB Development Stage, p-Plcγ Activity Increased, whereas Ret Expression Decreased
At E11.5, RT-PCR was used to assess the expression of key molecules related to renal development and vitamin A metabolism. Ret expression was downregulated in the TVAD group (shown in a), but there were no significant changes in other kidney development-related molecules except Eya1, and vitamin A metabolism-related molecules Aldh1a2 increased (shown in online suppl. Fig. 2). In addition, the ureteric bud tissue in the TVAD group presented similarly downregulated Ret expression, as shown by RNAscope (shown in b, d) and immunofluorescence staining (shown in c, e). Ret promoter area activation was substantially lower in the TVAD group than in the CON group, according to H3K4me3 Cut-Tag analysis of chromatin promoter region activation in the two groups (shown in f). The molecular changes in key phosphorylated molecules downstream of Ret, p-Akt/p-Plcγ/p-Erk1/2, were observed via immunofluorescence staining. These changes suggested that the expression level of p-Plcγ was greater in TVAD, whereas the expression of TVAS, SVAD, and SVAS was similar to that in the control group (shown in a, b). In the TVAD, SVAD, and CON groups, there was no apparent difference in the expression of p-Akt or p-Erk1/2 (shown in online suppl. Fig. 3).
 Increased apoptosis of ureteric bud tissue on day E11.5 in TVAD group according to immunofluorescence TUNEL staining (shown in c, e) correlates with the phenotype of CND shortening, while there was no significant difference in the proliferation indices in the E11.5-day ureteric bud according to immunohistochemical Ki67 staining (shown in d, f).

## Discussion
By administering a particular diet deficient in vitamin A to mice for 4 weeks before conception, a mouse model of preconception vitamin A deficiency was effectively established. The number of ureteral buds emerging in the early stage of renal development was abnormal, as were the key molecules of Ret and p-Plcγ in metanephric development. In this model, the proportion of newborn urinary malformations was increased in offspring, and abnormalities in key molecule changes and ureteral bud emergence could be slightly mitigated by supplying a normal vitamin A diet during pregnancy.
The World Health Organization states that a serum retinol level of 1.05 μmol/L is normal for vitamin A, whereas a level of less than 0.70 μmol/L is indicative of vitamin A deficiency [ ]. In the present study, female rats consumed specific diets containing low or no vitamin A starting at 5–6 weeks of age. After 4 weeks, the serum vitamin A level of the female rats approached the standard of vitamin A deficiency. While vitamin A levels in all groups of female rats after birth significantly rebounded, vitamin A levels in female rats with varying prepregnancy levels dramatically decreased during gestation at E11.5. Because the fetus cannot produce vitamin A on its own, it must rely on the circulation of the mother to cross the placental barrier [ ]. Therefore, maternal vitamin A status can directly affect fetal vitamin A levels, and maternal vitamin A levels and fetal vitamin A levels are significantly positively correlated [ ]. Consistent with our findings, another study revealed that maternal plasma retinol levels drastically decrease from day 9 to day 14 of embryonic development [ ]. It is worth noting that in our experiments, we also found that in the prepregnancy vitamin A-deficient group, given normal vitamin A diet (4,000 IU/kg) during pregnancy, the mice consumed 3–4 g/day during pregnancy, which is equivalent to an intake of 12–16 IU/day, much lower than the upper limit recommended in the literature [ ]. Nonetheless, simultaneous testing of their vitamin A levels at day E11.5 revealed that they were comparable to those of mice in the whole normal diet group, reinforcing the complexity of the organism’s regulation of vitamin A levels in early pregnancy, and the fact that early intake should be done with extreme precaution. We found that at 7 weeks of age, the serum vitamin A concentrations of offspring born to CON mothers who continued to receive a normal vitamin A diet were ≥1.05 μmol/L, whereas the serum vitamin A concentrations of offspring born to TVAD mothers who were given a special no vitamin A diet were also ≥1.05 μmol/L but lower than those of littermates born to CON mothers. These findings are based on long-term observations regarding the impact of maternal vitamin A levels on offspring vitamin A levels. On the other hand, normal vitamin A dietary feeding during pregnancy significantly increased the vitamin A concentration reserve of offspring mice to the level of the normal group, suggesting that normal vitamin A intervention during pregnancy can significantly improve the vitamin A reserve level of offspring mice in vivo.
Vitamin A insufficiency poses a significant risk to pregnant women, nursing mothers, and infants since it is one of the three primary micronutrient deficiency illnesses worldwide [ ]. Research indicates that proper levels of vitamin A in mothers are closely linked to the development of organs during the embryonic stage. According to research on animals, a severe vitamin A shortage during pregnancy can be fatal to the fetus, whereas a modest deficiency is linked to developmental abnormalities of the hindbrain, spinal cord, eyes, heart, kidneys, lungs, and limbs [ , , ]. Maternal excess or vitamin A insufficiency might increase the risk of kidney abnormalities in the baby [ – ]. Compared with mothers with normal maternal vitamin A (1.45 ± 0.32 μmol/L), neonates with maternal vitamin A deficiency (0.94 ± 0.26 μmol/L) had a reduced kidney volume (114 ± 33 mL/m2 vs. 184 ± 44 mL/m2, p < 0.01) in a population-based study [ ]. Another population study [ ] reported similar results, revealing a positive connection (r = 0.48, p = 0.001) between maternal blood vitamin A levels and fetal bilateral kidney volume. A mild vitamin shortage has also been shown in a rat model to cause young mice to have fewer renal units [ ]. In line with the population study [ , ], the hypovitaminosis A pregnant mice model in this study had no effect on the body weight or top-rump length of the newborn offspring or on the mother’s pregnancy. However, the percentage of offspring with abnormal urinary system development increased significantly, and there was a sex difference: male offspring had a greater likelihood of developing renal developmental malformations. These findings were comparable to those of the population of mothers deficient in vitamin A, where a higher percentage of male offspring than female offspring had abnormal urine development [ ]. In the present research, we examined whether moderate vitamin supplementation during pregnancy could help avoid and control CAKUT in offspring by reducing the proportion of offspring with CAKUT from vitamin A-deficient female rats to some extent through the consumption of a diet with a normal vitamin A content prepregnancy.
Retinoic acid is an active form of vitamin A that has physiological effects on the body. By stimulating the formation of homo and heterodimers of retinoid X and/or retinoic acid receptors, retinoic acid can control the transcriptional levels of genes. Retinoids have been shown to regulate approximately 500 genes, most of which influence the development of the embryo [ ]. Renal malformations in mice, including Rara/Rarb knockout animals [ ], can result from abnormalities in the retinoic acid signaling system. It has been demonstrated that Ret signaling, an essential pathway controlling metanephric development, influences renal development by downregulating Ret expression in hypovitaminosis A [ ]. The results of the present investigation indicate that the downregulation of Ret expression in the ureteral bud tissue of the TVAD group may be a major contributing factor to the development of renal malformations in offspring caused by maternal hypervitaminosis. Additionally, H3K4me3 Cut-Tag analysis revealed that the peak of the Ret promoter region in the TVAD group was significantly lower than that in the CON group.
This study has certain limitations. First, we only measured the maternal serum vitamin A concentration; we did not measure the vitamin A content in the developing kidneys or embryos, which is not directly related to fetal vitamin A levels. Second, there is no sex difference in the mechanism of maternal hypovitaminosis A, even though males are more likely to suffer from the neonatal CAKUT phenotype. Third, very few previous studies have focused on vitamin A overdose and have found possible teratogenicity. In our study, we have not further explored the effect of different levels of vitamin A supplementation during pregnancy on the kidneys, so follow up with further research to clarify.
In summary, we were able to successfully create a preconception vitamin A deficiency mouse model in this study by feeding the mice a specific diet with either low or no vitamin A for 4 weeks. We also discovered that the offspring of the maternal hypovitaminosis A model group had a greater proportion of neonatal urologic malformations and that abnormalities in ureteral bud emergence during early renal development and abnormalities in key molecules involved in metanephric development, such as Ret and p-Plcγ, might be the primary causes of CAKUT in the offspring of the mother hypovitaminosis A model group, whereas normal vitamin A during pregnancy could be the main cause of CAKUT. In contrast, the administration of normal vitamin A feeds during pregnancy may ameliorate the teratogenic effects of prepregnancy hypovitaminosis A to a certain extent and may also ameliorate the abnormalities associated with ureteral budding and key molecular changes.
