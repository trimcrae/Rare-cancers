## ABSTRACT
Low‐density lipoprotein receptor‐related protein 11 (LRP11) is reported to be overexpressed in various cancers; however, its functional role in lung adenocarcinoma remains poorly understood. This study aimed to elucidate the tumor‐promoting function of LRP11 in lung adenocarcinoma. We assessed the expression and function of LRP11 in lung adenocarcinoma cell lines through both silencing and overexpression experiments. RNA sequencing was performed to identify genes associated with LRP11 expression. The clinical relevance was evaluated using public datasets (The Cancer Genome Atlas and the Singapore Oncology Data Portal). LRP11 was overexpressed in lung adenocarcinoma cells and promoted their proliferation in vitro. RNA sequencing identified multiple genes negatively correlated with LRP11, all of which contained predicted C/EBPβ binding motifs in their promoter regions. Clinically, high LRP11 expression was associated with poor prognosis in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. In conclusion, LRP11 promotes lung adenocarcinoma progression by enhancing cell proliferation and modulating transcriptional activity. These findings suggest that LRP11 may serve as a potential therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker in lung adenocarcinoma.

This study demonstrates that LRP11 promotes lung adenocarcinoma progression by enhancing cell proliferation and modulating gene expression. High LRP11 expression was associated with poor prognosis, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target and prognostic biomarker.

Furthermore, the proportion of LRP11‐high cells among lung cancer cell lines was significantly higher than that in other cancer types (13/198 vs. 30/958; p = 0.020, chi‐squared test). Among the four major histological subtypes of lung cancer, adenocarcinoma exhibited a higher LRP11 expression, although the differences were not statistically significant (mean z‐scores: adenocarcinoma 0.5861, squamous cell carcinoma 0.4582, small cell carcinoma 0.3976, and large cell carcinoma 0.2452) (Figure  ).
RT‐PCR analysis showed that all seven LUAD cell lines examined in this study (A549, Calu‐3, H1650, HLC‐1, LC‐2/ad, PC9, and VMRC‐LCD) exhibited higher LRP11 expression compared to the immortalized non‐neoplastic bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS‐2B (Figure  ). In the TCGA LUAD cohort, analysis of 57 paired tumor and adjacent normal lung tissue samples showed a trend toward higher LRP11 expression in tumor tissues (mean TPM: 1.078 vs. 0.958), although the difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.1743, paired t‐test; Figure  ). Reanalysis of a publicly available single‐cell RNA‐seq dataset of non‐small cell lung cancer (including LUAD and squamous cell carcinoma) revealed that LUAD‐specific subsets, such as CXCL1 and LAMC2 cancer clusters [ ], exhibited significantly higher LRP11 expression compared with the normal alveolar cluster (Fisher's exact test and Wilcoxon rank‐sum test, p < 0.001). In contrast, squamous cell carcinoma‐associated clusters, including CDKN2A, proliferating, and SOX2 cancer clusters, showed lower LRP11 expression levels.

## 
LRP11 Enhances Proliferation of Lung Adenocarcinoma Cell Lines In Vitro
Next, we explored the function of LRP11 in LUAD cells. Among the seven LUAD cell lines used in this study, A549 and H1650 cells, which exhibited high LRP11 expressions, were selected for knockout and knockdown experiments, respectively. PC9 and VMRC‐LCD cells, which showed relatively low LRP11 expression, were chosen for forced expression experiments.
To investigate the effect of LRP11 silencing, we generate LRP11
−/− sublines of A549 cells using the CRISPR/Cas9 system. Three LRP11
−/− sublines (A549_dLRP11_#3, #4, and #5) and two control sublines (A549_mock_#2 and #3) were established. Genome sequencing confirmed a frameshift mutation in LRP11 followed by a premature stop codon (Figure  ). Immunoblotting and immunofluorescence staining demonstrated decreased LRP11 expression in these LRP11
−/− sublines (Figure  ), although the interpretation of the immunoblotting results was complicated by high background signals. Among these, A549_dLRP11_#3 and #5 and mock_#3 were used in subsequent in vitro experiments, while A549_dLRP11_#4 and mock_#2 were used only for RNA‐seq. Cell proliferation assays showed that LRP11 silencing suppressed cell growth (Figure  ), and wound‐healing assays revealed that LRP11 ablation also inhibited cellular migration (Figure  ). We also performed LRP11 knockdown using siRNA in H1650 cells and observed a reduction in cell proliferation (Figure  ). Re‐expression of LRP11 in A549_dLRP11_#3 rescued the growth inhibition caused by LRP11 silencing (Figure  ), and a similar trend was observed in A549_dLRP11_#5 (Figure  ). These findings suggest a direct role for LRP11 in promoting LUAD cell proliferation.
 To confirm the effect of LRP11 on proliferation, we conducted overexpression experiments. Forced expression of LRP11 in PC9 and VMRC‐LCD cells was performed using the gene expression vector pEBM‐LRP11, and its overexpression was confirmed by quantitative RT‐PCR and immunoblot analysis (Figure  ). Both PC9 and VMRC‐LCD cells exhibited enhanced proliferation following LRP11 overexpression (Figure  ). Wound healing assays demonstrated that LRP11 overexpression promoted the wound closure ability of PC9 cells (Figure  ).
 Collectively, these experiments involving silencing and overexpression of LRP11 in four distinct LUAD cell lines indicate that LRP11 contributes to malignant traits in LUAD by promoting cancer cell proliferation and migration. No significant activation of caspase‐3 was observed in association with LRP11 modification, suggesting a limited effect of LRP11 on apoptosis (Figure  ).
To assess whether LRP11 influences cell cycle progression, we conducted flow cytometric analysis (Figure  ). In A549 cells, LRP11 knockout did not significantly alter cell cycle distribution (A549_dLRP11_#3), and only a minimal shift toward the G2/M phase was observed in another knockout clone (A549_dLRP11_#5), which did not correlate with the results of proliferation assays. Similarly, overexpression of LRP11 in PC9 cells had no discernible impact on cell cycle phase distribution. Collectively, these findings indicate that LRP11 does not play a significant role in cell cycle regulation in LUAD cells.
We also examined whether LRP11 influences epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT), given its potential relevance to cell migration. Quantitative RT‐PCR analysis revealed partial alterations in EMT marker expression following LRP11 knockout in A549 cells; however, these changes were not consistent with the migration assay results, and substantial clonal variability was observed (Figure  ). In PC9 cells, LRP11 overexpression induced modest changes in several EMT‐related transcription factors, but a clear EMT phenotype was not evident (Figure  ). These findings suggest that the migration phenotype associated with LRP11 is not attributable to consistent modulation of EMT.

## Gene Expression Changes Associated With LRP11 Expression
To explore the mechanisms underlying the tumor‐promoting role of LRP11 in LUAD, we performed RNA‐seq on PC9 and A549 cells. In LRP11‐overexpressing PC9 cells, we identified 544 DEGs, of which 158 were positively correlated and 386 were negatively correlated with LRP11 (Figure  ). Similarly, in LRP11‐silenced A549 cells, 805 DEGs were detected, including 422 upregulated and 383 downregulated genes (Figure  ). By examining genes that fluctuated in relation to LRP11 in both PC9 and A549 cells, we identified seven genes that were negatively correlated with LRP11: C1R, DEFB1, GBP2, LOXL4, MRPL18 (a pseudogene), PDZK1IP1, and UBA7 (Figure  ). In contrast, we observed no genes elevated alongside LRP11, except for the pseudogene MT1P1.
 To explore possible regulatory mechanisms, we searched for transcription factor binding motifs in the promoter regions of negatively correlated genes. Notably, predicted C/EBPβ binding sites were identified in all six promoters (Table  ), suggesting that LRP11 may influence transcriptional regulation via pathways involving C/EBPβ.

## 

LRP11
 Is Related to Poor Prognosis in the Lung Adenocarcinoma
Finally, we investigated public LUAD datasets to validate the tumor‐promoting function of LRP11 in a clinical setting. We analyzed data from two independent LUAD cohorts: TCGA and OncoSG. The TCGA dataset revealed a significantly shorter overall survival in the high LRP11 expression group than in the low LRP11 expression group (Figure  ). Similar results were observed in the OncoSG cohort (Figure  ). LRP11 expression tended to increase with clinical stage progression, with stage III and stage IV cases exhibiting significantly higher LRP11 expression than stage I cases (Figure  ). Notably, among patients with stage I LUAD, LRP11 expression did not correlate with prognosis. However, in patients with stage II–IV LUAD, those with high LRP11 expression had a significantly poorer prognosis (Figure  ). These findings suggest a pivotal role for LRP11 in the later stages of LUAD progression. Among the six genes (C1R, DEFB1, GBP2, LOXL4, PDZK1IP1, and UBA7) identified as correlated with LRP11 via RNA‐seq analysis, only UBA7 showed a significant correlation in clinical samples (Figure  ).

## Discussion
In the LDLR family, which includes LRP11, some members such as LRP5, LRP6, and LRP8 are known to promote the progression of various cancers through the regulation of diverse intracellular signaling pathways [ ]. However, the role of LRP11 in cancer remains largely unclear. Although previous studies have implicated LRP11 in the progression of cervical, prostate, and liver cancers [ , , ], its involvement in lung cancer has not yet been elucidated. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first to demonstrate the role of LRP11 in lung cancer. In this study, we demonstrated that LRP11 is overexpressed in LUAD cell lines compared with BEAS‐2B non‐neoplastic bronchial epithelial cell line. It should be noted, however, that BEAS‐2B is an immortalized bronchial cell line and may not accurately reflect the characteristics of alveolar epithelial cells, which are considered the presumed origin of LUAD. To address this limitation, we also analyzed paired LUAD and adjacent normal tissue samples from the TCGA dataset and observed a trend toward higher LRP11 expression in tumor tissues, although the difference was not statistically significant. Consistently, reanalysis of a public single‐cell RNA‐seq dataset further supported this trend, showing higher LRP11 expression in LUAD clusters compared with normal alveolar epithelial cells. We demonstrated LRP11's growth‐promoting role through both overexpression and knockdown experiments using LUAD cell lines. One limitation of this study is the absence of in vivo experiments to validate the function of LRP11. Nevertheless, we found that higher LRP11 expression is significantly associated with poor prognosis in two independent LUAD cohorts, suggesting a potential contribution of LRP11 to LUAD progression. Another limitation of this study is the genetic heterogeneity among the LUAD cell lines used. For instance, A549 cells harbor a KRAS mutation, while PC9 cells carry an EGFR alteration [ ]. These differences in oncogenic driver mutations may influence intracellular signaling and potentially modulate the functional effects of LRP11.
We identified LRP11 as a protein highly homologous to HAI‐1, a membrane‐anchored serine protease inhibitor [ ]. Both proteins share three domains: MANEC, PKD, and LDLRA. LRP11 is shorter due to the absence of two Kunitz domains. In contrast, LRP11 possesses only the LDLRA domain among the three core domains of the LDLR family: LDLRA, EGF‐like, and YWTD [ ]. This structural distinction suggests that the functions of LRP11 differ from those of other LDLR family proteins. We previously reported that the PKD domain of HAI‐1 is essential for the proper folding and chaperone function of target proteases [ ]. Additionally, it is known that the MANEC domain structure of HAI‐1 resembles the PAN/apple domain family, which plays active roles in intra‐ and intermolecular interactions [ ]. Given the close structural similarity between LRP11 and HAI‐1, knowledge of HAI‐1's domain functions might be useful for elucidating the functions of LRP11.
Although LRP11 has been reported as a poor prognostic factor in several cancers [ , ], little is known about the mechanism by which it promotes tumor progression. Wang et al. reported that the expression of cell cycle‐related proteins such as CDK2/4, cyclin D1/E1, MMP‐2/9, and VEGF correlates with LRP11 in cervical cancer [ ]. However, in our study, LRP11 expression did not alter cell cycle progression or apoptotic cell death pathways in LUAD cells. These findings suggest that the proliferative effects of LRP11 may be mediated through mechanisms other than direct regulation of the cell cycle or apoptosis.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate transcriptomic changes directly associated with LRP11 modulation. Notably, our RNA‐seq analysis found no overlap between previously reported genes and those associated with LRP11 expression. This discrepancy suggests that LRP11 may exert distinct roles depending on the primary organ, cancer type, and experimental context. Our transcriptomic analysis revealed a set of genes consistently downregulated in response to LRP11 expression. While the specific targets may vary across cancer types, we found that all genes negatively correlated with LRP11 shared predicted C/EBPβ binding motifs in their promoter regions. This suggests that LRP11 may influence transcriptional programs via pathways involving C/EBPβ, a transcription factor known to integrate inflammatory and proliferative signals in cancer cells [ ]. Notably, Sun et al. reported that LRP11 physically interacts with MAPK13/p38δ, a component of the signaling cascade upstream of C/EBPβ activation [ ]. These findings raise the possibility that LRP11 regulates gene expression through modulation of the MAPK‐C/EBPβ axis. Although the exact mechanism by which LRP11 influences gene transcription remains to be elucidated, our data suggest that LRP11 contributes to LUAD progression by altering gene expression patterns associated with cancer cell proliferation and migration. Further investigation is needed to determine whether LRP11 functions through direct transcriptional regulation, modulation of signaling cascades, or other epigenetic mechanisms.
Among the genes negatively correlated with LRP11 expression, UBA7 is particularly notable due to its established tumor‐suppressive role in lung cancer [ , ]. UBA7 encodes an E1 enzyme essential for ISGylation, a ubiquitin‐like protein modification system involved in both cancer regulation and immune responses [ ]. In LUAD, UBA7 expression is frequently downregulated, and restoration of UBA7 has been shown to inhibit tumor cell proliferation through ISGylation‐dependent degradation of cyclin D1 [ , ]. In our analysis, UBA7 showed a consistent negative correlation with LRP11 in both cell lines and clinical LUAD datasets, suggesting that it may be one of the key downstream effectors of LRP11. While the mechanism of this regulation remains to be clarified, the inverse relationship between LRP11 and UBA7 expression may partly account for the tumor‐promoting role of LRP11 in LUAD.
Although immune‐related mechanisms were not investigated in the present study, several previous reports have suggested a potential link between LRP11 expression and tumor immunity, including associations with PD‐L1 expression, reduced infiltration of immune cells, and, conversely, enhanced antitumor responses in tumor‐infiltrating T‐cells [ , , ]. The relevance of LRP11 to tumor immunity in LUAD remains unclear and warrants further investigation.
In summary, our study demonstrated the tumor‐promoting function of LRP11 in LUAD and suggested its involvement in transcriptional regulation. LRP11 may serve as a promising therapeutic target and potential biomarker for predicting tumor aggressiveness.
