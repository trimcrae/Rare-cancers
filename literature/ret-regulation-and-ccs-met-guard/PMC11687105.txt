## Abstract
Lung cancer (LC) is a highly malignant and metastatic form of cancer. The global incidence of and mortality from LC is steadily increasing; the mean 5-year overall survival (OS) rate for LC is less than 20%. This frustrating situation may be attributed to the fact that the pathogenesis of LC remains poorly understood and there is still no cure for mid to advanced LC. Methylation at the N6-position of adenosine (N6mA) of RNA (m(6)A) is widely present in human tissues and organs, and has been found to be necessary for cell development and maintenance of homeostasis. However, numerous basic and clinical studies have demonstrated that RNA m(6)A is deregulated in many human malignancies including LC. This can drive LC malignant characteristics such as proliferation, stemness, invasion, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. Intriguingly, an increasing number of studies have also shown that eliminating RNA m(6)A dysfunction can exert significant anti-cancer effects on LC such as suppression of cell proliferation and viability, induction of cell death, and reversal of treatment insensitivity. The current review comprehensively discusses the therapeutic potential of RNA m(6)A and its underlying molecular mechanisms in LC, providing useful information for the development of novel LC treatment strategies.
Keywords: Lung cancer, M(6)A, Epigenetics, Target therapy, Tumorigenesis

## Introduction
Lung cancer (LC), originating from the bronchial mucosal epithelium, is one of the most common malignant cancer types in humans. Among all cancer types, LC ranks second and first in terms of morbidity and mortality, respectively [ ]. LC can be mainly divided into two subtypes: non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC). The former accounts for approximately 85% of the total number of LC cases and can be further divided pathologically into adenocarcinoma (LUAD), squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), and large cell carcinoma (LLCC). Among these, LUAD is the most common subtype, accounting for approximately 35% to 40% of all NSCLC [ ]. LC is a common disease caused by multiple factors, however, its specific pathogenesis remains unclear. Currently, most tumor biologists believe that LC formation can be attributed to a combination of genetic and environmental factors [ ]. A susceptibility locus identified in chromosome 6q23-25 of some family groups of LC patients suggests genetic inheritance may be a factor [ ]. The three main susceptibility sites for LC caused by gene mutations or variations are located on chromosomes 15q25, 5p15, and 6p21 [ – ]. The most common gene mutations in LC tissues mainly include epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), Kirsten rat sarcoma (K-Ras), echinoderm microtubule-associated protein-like 4 (EML4), anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK), rearranged during transfection (RET), C-Ros oncogene 1 (ROS1), cellular-mesenchymal to epithelial transition factor (c-MET), and V-Raf murine sarcoma viral homolog B kinase (BRAF) [ ]. It is worth noting that among these mutations, EGFR mutations are the most common, with a mutation rate as high as 30% in NSCLC [ ]. Among EGFR activating mutations, the exon 19 deletion mutation and exon 21 point mutation (L858R), accounting for approximately 85%‒90% of all mutations in the EGFR kinase domain [ ]. Insertion mutations in exon 20 account for approximately 1%‒10% of all EGFR mutations and represent the third most common type of EGFR mutations [ ]. As a result, the development of targeted drugs represented by EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) (EGFR-TKI) has been widely used in clinical neoadjuvant chemotherapy for LC [ ]. Additionally, there is evidence showing that the incidence of K-Ras mutations in patients with LUAD can reach 40%, while that in smoking patients with NSCLC increases to 60% [ ]. Although studies have preliminarily revealed that K-Ras mutation activation is involved in the occurrence and progression of LC [ ], there is still no targeted drug similar to EGFR-TKI for clinical treatment.
Smoking is regarded as the most common cause of LC [ ]. Among the many potential environmental factors, air pollution is a major risk factor for LC in non-smokers [ ] and can include vehicle exhaust [ ], gases released from burning solid fuels [ ], smoke from high-temperature cooking [ ], and interior decorations and gases such as formaldehyde [ ] and xylene [ ] released from decorations. There is evidence that LC may be triggered by occupational exposure to hazardous chemicals such as asbestos [ ], ozone [ ], and silica [ ]. Several studies have demonstrated that psychological stress may account for the initiation and development of LC. For example, a study in the Watch the Spot Trial revealed that the size of lung nodules was positively correlated with the degree of mild, moderate, or severe emotional distress or anxiety in patients [ ]. Approximately 33.0%‒77.2% of patients with LC suffer from depression and/or anxiety symptoms [ – ]. Another study using mice with Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) showed that inducing depression increased plasma corticosterone and adrenaline levels in the blood, which was accompanied by accelerated tumor growth and increased radioresistance. In vitro studies have shown that β2-adrenergic receptor (β2-AR) agonists can block radiation-induced LLC-1 cell apoptosis and cell viability reduction [ ]. This suggests that psychological stress or emotions are closely related to LC progression and treatment efficacy.
Tissue biopsy is currently the gold standard for the diagnosis of LC, with an accuracy rate of approximately 87% [ , ]. Non-invasive liquid biopsy is a new, minimally invasive diagnostic method that has not yet been widely used in clinical practice. The more common blood-borne biomarkers include circulating tumor cells (CTCs), circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), microbial DNA (mbDNA), microRNA (miRNA/miR), and circulating exosomes [ ]. Surgical resection is considered a radical cure for early-stage LC, and approximately 30% of LC patients are suitable for curative resection [ ]. Treatment strategies for intermediate and advanced LC mainly include chemotherapy, radiotherapy, molecular targeted therapy, or immunotherapy [ ]. Unfortunately, approximately 50% of patients are diagnosed with LC recurrence and metastasis within five years of completing treatment [ ]. Chemotherapy using platinum-based drugs is the standard treatment for SCLC and most advanced NSCLCs [ , ]. Targeted drugs for LC include EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and ALK TKIs. Among these, osimertinib is generally used to treat NSCLC patients with EGFR mutation [ ]. Erlotinib, gefitinib, and afatinib can also be used in lung cancer patients with EGFR mutations (exon 19 deletion or L858R mutation) [ – ]. ALK-TKIs mainly include crizotinib, alectinib, and brigatinib [ , ]. Among these, alectinib and crizotinib can significantly prolong the progression-free survival (PFS) of patients with ALK mutations [ ]. Brigatinib is well tolerated by patients, and new secondary ALK mutations are rare during treatment [ ]. Dabrafenib and trametinib can be used to treat NSCLC patients with BRAF (V600E) mutations [ , ]. Immunotherapy using immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has also been applied to treat NSCLC [ ]. Commonly used immunotherapy regimens for LC mainly include single drugs (such as pembrolizumab, cemiplimab, atezolizumab, nivolumab, and ipilimumab) and drug combinations (such as pembrolizumab + platinum drugs, atezolizumab + platinum drugs, atezolizumab + bevacizumab + platinum [ – ].
Increasing laboratory and clinical data show that abnormal changes in DNA and RNA epigenetic modifications are closely related to the occurrence and progression of cancer [ ]. Among these, an imbalance of methylation occurring at the N6 position of adenosine (N6mA) on RNA (m(6)A) can affect the processing, splicing, localization, translation, and activity of RNA. This can promote the activation of oncogenes and the inactivation of tumor suppressor genes, ultimately leading to the occurrence, progression, and drug resistance of many cancers including LC [ ]. M(6)A, which determines RNA function and fate, mainly depends on three key regulatory factors: methyltransferases (writers), demethylases (erasers), and RNA-binding proteins (readers). Abnormal expression or functional imbalance of the above regulatory factors has been shown to be closely related to occurrence, progression, and treatment resistance in LC. For example, chronic exposure to heavy metal ions such as Cr(VI) can up-regulate METTL3 (a writer) expression, promoting normal lung epithelial cell (BEAS-2B) transformation and carcinogenesis [ ]. This oncogenic effect may be attributed to the increased expression of METTL3, which activates the key regulators of tumor stemness, Oct4 and Klf4 [ ]. It has been shown that silica can induce increased expression of ALKBH5 (an eraser) in mouse lung tissue fibroblasts. Up-regulated ALKBH5 promotes pri-miR-320a-3p to dem(6)A and fails to mature into miR-320a-3p, thereby losing its inhibitory effect on FOXM1 mRNA, ultimately promoting pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Considering that idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrosing interstitial pneumonia, the terminal stage of diffuse lung parenchymal disease, and a risk factor for LC, ALKBH5/miR-320a-3p/FOXM1 may play an important role in the occurrence of LC by promoting fibrosis in occupational lung disease [ ]. Treatment of human bronchial epithelial Beas-2B cells with a potential carcinogen nitrosamine 4-(methylnitrosamino)−1-(3-pyridyl)−1-butanone (NNK) can induce a significant increase in intracellular IGF2BP1 (a reader) expression and down-regulate the global m(6)A level. High expression of IGF2BP1 can promote proliferation, colony formation, and increase apoptosis resistance in Beas-2B cells. IGF2BP1 gene silencing can lead to tumor suppressive effects. It has been suggested that the carcinogen NNK may promote LC by affecting IGF2BP1-m(6)A [ ].
Overexpression of METLL3 [ ], fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO) (an eraser) [ ], and α-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase B homolog 5 (ALKBH5) (an eraser) [ ] in LC cells can promote cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Overexpression of YTHDF1 (a reader) in LC cells can promote mitophagy and induce chemotherapy resistance [ ]. Taken together, the ongoing discovery of the molecular mechanisms regulating RNA m(6)A modification in the occurrence, progression, and drug resistance of LC has provided new direction for the comprehensive treatment of LC.

## Overview of m(6)A

## M(6)A and m(6)A regulators
M(6)A is a dynamic reversible methylation modification process that occurs at the N6 position of RNA adenosine. It was first discovered in the mRNA of Novikoff hepatoma cells in 1974 [ ]. Follow-up studies have found that m(6)A not only occurs in coding RNA, but also commonly occurs in many types of non-coding RNA (ncRNA) such as long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), microRNA, circular RNA (circRNA), and ribose somatic RNA (rRNA) [ – ]. RNA m(6)A is a common mechanism that eukaryotic cells use for the post-transcriptional regulation of genes [ , ]. In mRNA, m(6)A sites often localize in the 5′-untranslated regions (5′-UTRs), near the stop codons, in the 3′-untranslated regions (3′-UTRs), and within long internal exons. The most common m(6)A consensus sequence is RRACH (R = G/A/U, A = m(6)A, H = U/A/C) [ , ]. M(6)A can be installed by the methyltransferase complex, removed by the demethylase, and recognized and bound by the m(6)A-binding protein [ , ]. Methyltransferase complex (MTC) mainly consists of METTL3, METTL14, Wilms tumor 1 associated protein (WTAP), Vir-like m6A methyltransferase associated protein (VIRMA/KIAA1429), RBM15, and zinc finger CCCH domain containing protein13 (ZC3H13). METTL3 can form a stable heterodimeric complex with METTL14, which catalyzes methyl transfer and promotes RNA substrate recognition. WTAP does not have methyltransferase activity and is responsible for localizing the METTL3-METTL14 heterodimer to nuclear speckles and enhancing their catalytic activity [ ]. The remaining members have the function of regulating MTC activity. METTL16 is an m(6)A methyltransferase that is independent of MTC and mainly catalyzes the m(6)A site located in the 3'-UTR region of small nuclear RNA, U6 snRNA, and other non-coding RNAs (such as lncRNAs) [ ]. Three types of m(6)A demethylases have been identified: FTO, α-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase B homolog 5 (ALKBH5), and ALKBH3. FTO mainly acts as an m(6)A demethylase and can bind to mRNA, snRNA, and tRNA. ALKBH5 is mainly localized in the nucleus, and its loss impairs RNA metabolism and mRNA export and assembly. Readers mainly include the superfamily proteins YT521-B homology domain family proteins (YTHDFs), insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA binding proteins (IGF2BPs), and heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (HNRNPs). Among these, the YTHDFs include YTHDF1/2/3 and YTH domain containing 1 (YTHDC1/2) [ – ]. IGF2BPs include IGF2BP1/2/3, which can directly recognize and bind to m(6)A-marked mRNA through hnRNP-K homology (KH) domains [ ]. HNRNP particles generally exist in the form of tetramers and include hnRNP (C1)C2, hnRNP(A2)3B1, and hnRNP(A1)3B2 [ – ].

## The biological functions of m(6)A
M(6)A plays a decisive role in RNA transcription [ ], splicing [ , ], stability [ ], localization [ , ], translation [ – ], and metabolism [ – ], thereby achieving post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression. Studies have shown that RNA m(6)A modifications are involved in multiple cell processes including the circadian rhythm, cell cycle, differentiation, growth, survival, proliferation, aging, and death. The changes in the above-mentioned cell functions caused by RNA m(6)A abnormalities can have significant impacts on the physiological functions of the human body at multiple levels. Based on existing studies, this mainly includes the following: (i) hematopoietic stem cell [ , ] and germ cell development [ , , ]; (ii) brain, nerve [ ], and embryonic development [ – ]; (iii) circadian rhythm [ ]; (iv) immunity and fat metabolism [ ]; and (v) tissue homeostasis [ ], genomic DNA stability [ ], tumorigenesis, and tumor progression and drug resistance [ – ] (Fig.  ).
 Specifically, the role of METTL3 in regulating hematopoiesis has been verified in mouse and zebrafish embryonic hemogenic endothelial (HE) cells. After endothelial-specific knockout of the METTL3 gene, the intracellular Notch mRNA m(6)A level was reduced and its stability was enhanced. Additionally, protein expression was increased, leading to excessive activation of Notch signaling, ultimately inhibiting the differentiation of HE cells into hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs) [ ]. This study reveals the indispensable function of METTL3-mediated RNA m(6)A in mouse hematopoiesis. METTL3 mutants are embryonic lethal in animals. Mechanistically, METTL3 mutations can lead to reduced overall m(6)A levels in gametes, causing early oocyte development arrest, sperm maturation defects, significantly reduced sperm motility, and significantly reduced female and male hormone levels [ ]. ALKBH5 is highly expressed in male mouse testes compared to other tissues or organs including the heart, brain, liver, kidney, spleen, lung, and gonadal fat pads. ALKBH5-deficient male mice exhibit increased global mRNA m(6)A levels, which can lead to significant changes in expression of spermatocyte development factors including DNA methyltransferase-1 (up-regulation) and ubiquitin-like with PHD and RING finger domains-1 (down-regulation). This activates the p53 apoptotic signaling network to induce apoptosis of mid-meiotic spermatocytes, ultimately inducing testicular atrophy and significantly impairing fertility in mice [ ]. The above findings suggest that m(6)A abnormalities may affect human fertility.
Numerous studies have confirmed that abnormal regulation of RNA m(6)A modification is involved in the occurrence and development of various cardiovascular diseases, including myocardial hypertrophy [ – ]. For example, METTL3 knockout can effectively inhibit cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in vitro and in vivo and loss of function of the METTL3-m(6)A axis leads to maladaptive cardiomyocyte remodeling and post-stress and aging-induced cardiac dysfunction [ ]. During the process of myocardial hypertrophy or under the stimulation of myocardial hypertrophic factors, global m(6)A levels in myocardial cells increase significantly. MRNA m(6)A levels of hypertrophy-related kinases (such as MAP3K5, MAP4K6, and MAPK14) and regulatory factors (such as IKB) are also elevated. Overexpression of METTL3 can activate MAP3K5/6/14 and promote cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. This study suggests that increased mRNA m(6)A leads to compensatory cardiac hypertrophy, whereas decreased RNA m(6)A leads to eccentric remodeling and dysfunction of cardiomyocytes. Similarly, METTL14 down-regulation has been found to protect mice from cardiomyocyte hypertrophy via suppressing protein phosphatase PHLPP2 mRNA m(6)A and activating Akt [ ]. Another study revealed increased expression of YTHDF2 (a reader) in cardiac tissue could effectively reduce cardiac hypertrophy by recognizing and binding to m(6)A-labeled Myh7 (β-myosin heavy chain) mRNA and promoting its translation [ ]. These findings indicate that RNA m(6)A plays an important role in maintaining cardiac homeostasis.
RNA m(6)A is critical for maintaining cardiac homeostasis. FTO is highly expressed in adult neural stem cells (NSCs) and neurons, and its deletion can impair the proliferation and neuronal differentiation of NSCs, resulting in a decrease in brain volume and weight, and ultimately impairing learning and memory [ ]. FTO is ubiquitously expressed in some tissues or organs involved in regulating energy metabolism such as the liver, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, heart, kidney, and pancreas [ ]. FTO up-regulation in human adipocytes is closely associated with increased obesity [ ]. One study showed that FTO controls the alternative splicing of Runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1T1) pre-mRNA by regulating m(6)A levels around the splicing site, thereby promoting adipocyte differentiation and ultimately adipogenesis [ ]. METTL3 and global m(6)A levels are significantly elevated in the liver tissue of type 2 diabetes (T2D) patients. Hepatocyte-specific METTL3 gene silencing can reduce m(6)A -marked Fatty acid synthase (Fasn) mRNA and overall Fasn mRNA, resulting in blocked fatty acid synthesis and enhanced insulin sensitivity. On the contrary, overexpression of METTL3 can enhance fatty acid synthesis and reduce insulin sensitivity [ ]. This suggests that a METTL3-m(6)A/Fasn axis is involved in regulating insulin sensitivity and may be a potential therapeutic target for T2D.
In addition to maintaining normal physiological function of human somatic cells and participating in the maintenance of homeostasis of tissues and organs, abnormal RNA m(6)A can lead to the occurrence, progression, and treatment resistance of malignant tumors. For example, METTL3 promotes the m(6)A-BHLHE41-CXCL1/CXCR2 axis to recruit immunosuppressive myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) into the tumor microenvironment (TME) to inhibit anti-tumor immune responses mediated by CD8( +) and CD4( +) T cells. This promotes colorectal cancer (CRC) occurrence in the ApcMin/ + mouse model. Alternatively, METTL3 knockout can reduce MDSCs, increase the number of CD8( +) and CD4( +) T cells, and inhibit CRC growth [ ]. In addition, a hypoxic environment can stimulate breast cells to overexpress ALKBH5, which can induce the stability of core pluripotency factor NANOG mRNA through m(6)A demethylation [de-m(6)A], enhancing breast cancer stem cell proliferation and ultimately promoting tumor formation [ ]. YTHDF1 overexpression can up-regulate FOXM1 expression in an m(6)A-dependent manner, which promotes EMT and accelerates the metastasis and invasion of breast cancer cells [ ]. The m(6)A removal [de-m(6)A] of USP7 mRNA induced by up-regulation of FTO can induce resistance of LC cells to gefitinib and accelerate the growth of transplanted tumors in LC nude mice [ ].

## The potential role of m(6)A in LC therapy

## Targeting writers
Studies have shown that writers including METTL3, METTL14, VIRMA, and METTL16 are usually overexpressed in LC. This can promote the translation and expression of oncogenes or inhibit tumor suppressor genes in LC cells, thereby leading to tumorigenesis, cell proliferation, migration and invasion, as well as cell death resistance [ – ]. On the contrary, down-regulation or gene silencing can exhibit anti-cancer effects [ ]. In LC cells, the abnormal RNA m(6)A regulated by writers mainly involves signaling pathways including enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2), AKT (protein kinase B), Bcl-2 family proteins, eukaryotic translation initiation factor, and various non-coding RNAs (Table  ).

 

## EZH2 signaling pathway
EZH2 is a histone lysine N-methyltransferase that inhibits the transcription of target genes by trimethylating lysine 27 of histone H3 (H3K27me3). It participates in regulation of the cell cycle, cell senescence, DNA damage repair (DDR), and cell differentiation and is closely related to tumorigenesis [ ]. EZH2 is commonly overexpressed in various cancers, including LC, and is significantly inversely associated with poor prognosis [ ]. In the early stages of cancer, EZH2 is often mutated and highly activated [ ]. Targeted inhibition of EZH2 has become one of the most promising strategies in cancer treatment [ ]. Chen et al. found that EZH2 participates in promoting LC cell invasion. Mechanistically, METTL3 overexpression in LC cells can increase EZH2 mRNA m(6)A and up-regulate its protein level, promoting LC cell proliferation and metastasis. Consequentially, simvastatin, an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor can reduce METTL3 to attenuate EZH2-mediated pro-cancer effects [ ]. In another study, Murai et al. further demonstrated that EZH2 overexpression can promote the progression of SCLC by inhibiting the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) signaling pathway [ ]. This study found that when wild-type TGF-β type II receptor (TβRII) was overexpressed in SCLC cells, TGF-β inhibited cell growth in vitro and tumor formation in vivo by inducing apoptosis. However, compared to normal lung epithelial cells and normal lung tissues, most SCLC cells and tissues exhibited low expression of TβRII. This may be attributed to the fact that overexpression of EZH2 in SCLC cells can lead to epigenetic silencing of TβRII expression, ultimately leading to the inhibition of TGF-β-mediated apoptosis [ ]. Nevertheless, the presence of a METTL3-m(6)A/EZH2/TGF-β-TβRII axis that is involved in promoting LC aggressiveness requires further investigation.
EZH2 binds to DNA methyltransferase 1 (DNMT1) and recruits it to DNA to regulate DNA methylation, thereby affecting the transcription of target genes such as transcription factor JunB [ , ]. It has been shown that JunB overexpression caused the loss of intercellular adhesion and cytoskeletal reorganization, increased cell motility and invasion, and played a promoting role in mediating cancer metastasis [ ]. TGF-β1 promotes cell migration, invasion, and EMT by up-regulating JunB [ ]. In LC cells, METTL3 overexpression increased m(6)A methylation and the stability of JunB mRNA, thereby up-regulating its protein level. METTL3 knockdown reduces JunB mRNA m(6)A methylation and stability, which impairs TGF-β-induced cell morphology transition, cell migration, and EMT. Overexpression of JunB partially rescued the inhibitory effect of METTL3 knockdown in the EMT phenotype [ ]. Taken together, the above studies suggest that there may be a METTL3-EZH2-TGF-β/JunB axis in LC cells that plays an important role in LC progression by promoting cell invasion and metastasis. TCGA database analysis showed that the expression levels of EZH2 and solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) mRNA were significantly increased in LC tissues [ ]. However, the regulatory relationship regarding EZH2 and SLC7A11 expression may be tissue specific. For example, in hepatocytes, EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 modification can inhibit the expression of SLC7A11 [ ]. In tongue squamous cell carcinoma (TSCC) cells, EZH2 can up-regulate SLC7A11 through inhibiting the expression of miR-125b-5p, thereby inhibiting erastin-induced ferroptosis [ ]. Ji et al. found that SLC7A11 protein expression was significantly increased in LC tissues and negatively correlated with poor prognosis [ ]. SLC7A11 gene silencing in LC cells can inhibit cell proliferation, invasion, and colony formation and delay the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice. This cancer-promoting effect may be related to SLC7A11-induced intracellular metabolic remodeling [ ]. In another study, overexpression of SLC7A11 in LC cells effectively inhibited ferroptosis and promoted cell proliferation. Mechanistically, METTL3 overexpression promoted m(6)A and the stability of SLC7A11 mRNA in an YTHDF1-dependent manner, thereby increasing SLC7A11 protein levels, ultimately inhibiting ferroptosis and promoting the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. This finding coincided with the discovery by Yu et al. that SLC7A11 inhibits ferroptosis and plays a cancer-promoting role in TSCC cells [ ]. Taken together, this evidence suggests that there may be a METTL3-m(6)A-EZH2-SLC7A11 axis contributing to LC development through inhibition of ferroptosis.
Homeobox A1 (HOXA1) is a member of the HOX gene family and is considered an oncogenic factor [ ]. Under septic conditions, lncRNA UCA1 levels were increased in lung cells and interacted with EZH2 to up-regulate HOXA1 expression, ultimately increasing the progression of sepsis-induced pneumonia in rat models [ ]. HOXA1 is overexpressed in NSCLC cells, which can promote cell proliferation and gefitinib resistance [ ]. VIRMA overexpression can target the 3'-UTR of HOXA1 mRNA to enhance its m(6)A methylation and stability, thereby increasing its protein expression and ultimately promoting NSCLC cell proliferation. VIRMA depletion inhibited the proliferation and migration of gefitinib-resistant LC cells, delaying the growth of LC transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. Therefore, there may be a lncRNA UCA1-EZH2/VIRMA-m(6)A-HOXA1 axis that plays a promoting role in the occurrence, progression, and drug resistance of LC. Phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) is a tumor suppressor that antagonizes the cancer-promoting effects mediated by the phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate kinase (PI3K)/AKT signaling pathway [ ]. The expression of PTEN is significantly reduced in LC cells, resulting in the promotion of cell survival, growth, and migration [ ]. Feng et al. reported that inhibition of METTL3 expression in LC cells mediated by the chemotherapy drug β-elemene can reduce PTEN mRNA m(6)A and enhance its stability, increase PTEN protein levels, and ultimately inhibit cell proliferation. Alternatively, METTL3 overexpression was found to antagonize the β-elemene-induced decrease in PTEN mRNA m(6)A modification and promoted its degradation, leading to a decrease in protein levels and ultimately accelerating the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. Studies have confirmed that overexpression of lncRNA UFC1 in LC cells can bind to EZH2 and mediate its accumulation in the PTEN gene promoter region, leading to H3K27 trimethylation and inhibiting PTEN expression, thereby promoting NSCLC progression [ ]. This suggests that a METTL3-EZH2-lncRNA UFC1-PTEN abnormality may be involved in promoting the progression of LC. Elevated EZH2 expression in LC cells increased the levels of autophagy-related proteins such as ATG5 and LC-3I/3II, inducing autophagy and promoting cell proliferation [ ]. Liu et al. found that in LC cells, METTL3 positively promoted autophagy by increasing key genes in the autophagy pathways (such as ATG5 and ATG7). They also found that β-elemene directly targeted and bound to the S-adenosinemethionine binding domain of METTL3. The investigators concluded that β-elemene reduced autophagy by inhibiting METTL3-mediated RNA m(6)A. This ultimately reversed cell resistance to gefitinib and effectively inhibited the growth of LC transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. However, the role of a METTL3-m(6)A-EZH2-autophagy axis in promoting LC cell growth and drug resistance requires further study.

## The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway
The membrane HGF receptor c-Met is a receptor tyrosine kinase whose activation mediates epithelial and endothelial cell survival, proliferation, motility, invasion, and angiogenesis [ ]. The expression level of c-Met is elevated in NSCLC cells and promotes cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ , ]. The histone deacetylase inhibitor (HDACi) chidamide can reduce c-Met mRNA m(6)A by down-regulating the expression of METTL3 and WTAP, thereby decreasing c-Met protein expression and enhancing the sensitivity of LC cells to the c-Met inhibitor crizotinib. Further, combined treatment with chidamide and crizotinib reduced NSCLC cell viability and inhibited cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and colony formation, promoted apoptosis, and overcame drug resistance [ ]. In LC cells, excessive activation of c-Met can promote cell proliferation through the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Inhibiting c-Met can effectively block the activation of the above signaling pathway and decrease tumor angiogenesis, delaying progression [ , ].
RNA binding motif 10 (RBM10) is a nuclear RNA-binding protein (RBP) that regulates alternative splicing of primary gene transcripts [ ]. RBM10 normally functions as a tumor suppressor, however, it may exert oncogenic effects due to genetic mutation [ , ]. The lncRNA MALAT1 is highly expressed in NSCLC and negatively correlated with poor patient prognosis; it is therefore regarded as a cancer-promoting factor [ ]. RBM10 expression was shown to be significantly down-regulated in NSCLC tissues. RBM10 directly bound to the lncRNA MALTA1, recruiting METTL3 to increase MALTA1 m(6)A levels. This reduced its stability, which inhibited the activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway and ultimately suppressed cell invasion and migration. As such, RBM10 gene silencing exerts cancer-promoting effects [ ]. Therefore, in LC cells, there may be an RBM10/METTL3-MALAT1-PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis that can inhibit the invasion and migration of NSCLC. AKT1 (a protein kinase B α subunit) is generally overactivated in LC, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, distant metastasis, and drug resistance [ ]. METTL3 and AKT1 protein levels were significantly increased in NSCLC tissues and exhibited a positive correlation. AKT1 mRNA m(6)A levels are significantly up-regulated in NSCLC tissues. AKT1 is positively correlated with chemotherapy resistance and negatively correlated with poor prognosis in NSCLC patients. This suggests that METTL3-mediated AKT1 mRNA m(6)A may be involved in LC drug resistance [ ]. Studies have shown that in mesenchymal stem cells, insufficient METTL3 expression can inhibit AKT1 expression by down-regulating m(6)A-marked AKT1 mRNA levels, thereby promoting adipogenesis in mesenchymal stem cells and ultimately enhancing chemotherapy resistance in acute myeloid leukemia cells [ ]. This suggests that targeted inhibition of METTL3 or AKT1 in the treatment of LC may involve a METTL3-m(6)-AKT1 axis, which mediates the induction of drug resistance in LC cells by MSCs in the TME or other organs in the body, greatly reducing efficacy.
Focal adhesion kinase (FAK) has been reported to promote LC cell migration, proliferation, and invasion by activating the downstream AKT signaling pathway [ , ]. Further, Fraser extracellular matrix complex subunit 1 (FRAS1) gene silencing in LC cells leads to down-regulation of FAK and inhibits cell migration and invasion [ ]. FRAS1 overexpression has been shown to enhance or contribute to the malignant phenotype of various cancer cells, including NSCLC [ ]. High expression of FRAS1 is significantly negatively correlated with poor prognosis in NSCLC patients [ ]. Overexpression of METTL3 in LC cells can enhance FRAS1 mRNA m(6)A. M(6)A-marked FRAS1 mRNA can be recognized by YTHDF1, enhancing its stability and promoting translation. This FRAS1 overexpression further enhances NSCLC cell viability. FRAS1 silencing or YTHDF1 silencing can attenuate NSCLC cell proliferation, colony formation, and tumor growth induced by METTL3 overexpression in vitro and in vivo [ ]. Therefore, there might be a METTL3-m(6)A-FRAS1-FAK-AKT axis with cancer-promoting effects in LC cells. Nevertheless, more experiments are needed to validate the above speculation. Secreted frizzled-related protein 2 (SFRP2), as a downstream target protein of the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, plays an indispensable role in mediating the survival of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) derived from various normal tissues [ – ]. SFRP2 is a member of the SFRP family, which also serves as a negative regulator of the Wnt signaling pathway and often exhibits allelic loss or promoter methylation in LC, resulting in suppressed expression [ ]. In vitro studies have shown that down-regulation of SFRP2 expression in LC cells promotes cell migration and invasion. Mechanistically, METTL3 overexpression in LC cells enhanced SFRP2 mRNA m(6)A and reduced its stability and protein levels, thereby activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and ultimately promoting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ ]. This implies that in LC cells, SFRP2 may act as a tumor suppressor, and abnormal epigenetic modification can promote LC progression. PTEN-induced kinase 1 (PINK1) is a protein kinase that has been shown to interact with the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis to promote cancer cell survival, growth, stress resistance, and cell cycle progression [ ]. PINK1 and the E3 ubiquitin ligase Parkin work together to sense mitochondrial functional status and mark damaged mitochondria, thereby initiating mitophagy mediated by PINK1-Parkin [ ]. Decapping protein 2 (DCP2) is a major mRNA decapping enzyme present in eukaryotic cells that regulates pre-mRNA processing, nuclear export, translation initiation, and mRNA decay [ – ]. Insufficient expression of DCP2 can lead to chemotherapy resistance in SCLC [ ]. Mitophagy is significantly enhanced in chemotherapy-resistant SCLC cell lines [ , ]. Mechanistically, overexpression of METTL3 leads to an increase in DCP2 mRNA m(6)A levels and acceleration of its degradation, resulting in down-regulation of DCP2 protein levels. Reduced DCP2 expression further increases the stability of PINK1 and Parkin mRNA and increases their protein expression, thereby increasing mitophagy and reducing mitochondrial damage. This ultimately leads to SCLC chemotherapeutic drug resistance and a faster growth rate of SCLC drug-resistant cell transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. Therefore, there may be a METTL3-DCP2-PINK1/Parkin axis that can crosstalk with the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway to affect mitophagy, thereby regulating LC cell sensitivity. MAP3K2 (MEKK2) is a serine/threonine kinase that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, and apoptosis by activating various signaling pathways such as p38MAPK, JNK, AKT, or ERK [ – ]. MAP3K2 is highly expressed in NSCLC tissues. Overexpression of VIRMA in NSCLC cells up-regulated MAP3K2 expression and activated the JNK/MAPK signaling pathway, ultimately promoting cell proliferation, migration, proliferation, and gefitinib resistance. VIRMA knockdown had the opposite effect. Mechanistically, overexpression of VIRMA promoted MAP3K2 expression in an m(6)A-dependent manner, activating JNK and ultimately promoting the growth and drug resistance of LC cells [ ]. The transcription factor Twist is a key driver of EMT and is overexpressed in a variety of cancers, including LC [ ]. A variety of signaling pathways including AKT, STAT3, MAPK, Ras, and Wnt can activate Twist. Activated Twist is involved in angiogenesis, invadopodia, extravasation, chromosomal instability, apoptosis resistance, tumor cell stemness, and drug resistance [ , ]. In NSCLC cells, METTL14 knockdown inhibited the AKT signaling pathway by reducing Twist expression, thereby up-regulating E-cadherin and inhibiting N-cadherin expression, ultimately reducing cell proliferation and migration [ ].
Death-associated protein kinase 2 (DAPK2), a member of the DAPK family and a tumor suppressor, is a Ca(2+)/calmodulin-regulated serine/threonine kinase that mediates apoptosis [ , ]. However, the tumor suppressor function of DAPK2 can be inhibited by AKT-mediated phosphorylation at the Thr(369) site [ ]. Jin et al. found that DAPK2 expression was low in cancer tissues of NSCLC patients with a history of smoking. This may significantly enhance the proliferation and migration of LC cells by activating the NF-κB signaling pathway. Mechanistically, CSE-induced NSCLC cells can promote METTL3 expression and increase DAPK2 mRNA m(6)A levels, reduce its stability and protein expression, and ultimately promote the migration of NSCLC cells. Overexpression of DAPK2 can significantly inhibit the migration of NSCLC cells. Therefore, CSE can induce down-regulation of DAPK2 through METTL3-m(6)A to activate the NF-κB signaling pathway, ultimately accelerating metastasis and invasion in NSCLC [ ]. Studies have also shown that in LC cells, activation of NF-κB can promote autophagy and enhance chemotherapy resistance [ ]. Further, autophagy can in turn activate non-classical NF-κB signaling pathways to promote LC cell survival and proliferation [ ], thus forming a positive feedback loop. Therefore, there may be a METTL3-m(6)A-DAPK2-NF-κB axis that promotes LC progression. In addition, the AKT signaling pathway is generally overactivated in LC cells. Therefore, METTL3-m(6)A and AKT may both negatively regulate the tumor suppressor effect of DAPK2 [ ]. The expression of death associated protein kinase 3 (DAPK3), another member of the DAPK family, is significantly down-regulated in LC. This leads to promotion of cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ ]. DAPK3 expression is negatively correlated with VIRMA. In LC cells, VIRMA overexpression can lead to increased m(6)A of DAPK3 mRNA and YTHDF2/3-dependent degradation, thereby reducing its protein level and ultimately promoting the proliferation, migration, and invasion of NSCLC cells. Exogenous introduction of DAPK3 or VIRMA gene silencing can significantly weaken the chemotherapy resistance of LC cells and effectively delay the growth and metastasis of LC transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ].

## B cell lymphoma protein-2 (Bcl-2) family proteins
It has been shown that the pro-survival protein Bcl-2 is highly expressed in NSCLC. Bcl-2 gene silencing can significantly reduce the number of metastatic nodules of LC cells and inhibit the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice with LC. Mechanistically, METTL3 promotes the stability and translation of Bcl-2 mRNA by enhancing its m(6)A level in vivo, ultimately promoting cell viability and migration. Accordingly, METTL3 knockdown inhibits Bcl-2 expression, induces NSCLC cell proliferation and migration, and promotes apoptosis [ ]. The tumor suppressor E3 ubiquitin ligase F-box and WD repeat domain-containing7 (FBXW7) belongs to the F-box protein family, which can promote proteasome-mediated degradation of oncoproteins such as Mcl-1, a Bcl-2 pro-survival family member [ ]. In a study by Wu et al., FBXW7 and METTL3 exhibited low expression in cancer tissues of LC patients and was positively correlated with poor prognosis. In LC cells, insufficient expression of FBXW7 can promote the proliferation, metastasis, and invasion of cancer cells. Mechanistically, METTL3 overexpression promoted FBXW7 mRNA m(6)A, enhanced mRNA stability, and up-regulated protein expression. FBXW7 overexpression reduced the expression of Mcl-1 and c-Myc in LUAD cells and exerted a tumor suppressor effect [ ].

## Eukaryotic translation initiation factor
Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit h (eIF3h) is an important translation initiation factor in human cells that is overexpressed in LC [ ]. Choe et al. found that METTL3-eIF3h-mediated mRNA cyclization in LC cells enhanced mRNA translation and promoted tumorigenesis in nude mice. The METTL3-eIF3h interaction forms a complex that leads to conformational changes in polyribosomes to form dense polyribosomes, whereby METTL3 promotes the translation of a large number of oncogenic mRNAs such as Bromodomain-containing protein 4 (BRD4). The enhancement of METTL3 pro-translational ability can induce oncogenic transformation of normal somatic cells, such as NIH-3T3 cells and mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs), and promote the invasiveness of LC cells. Depletion of METTL3 or disruption of METTL3-eIF3h complex formation can inhibit LC cell tumorigenesis and sensitize cells to translational inhibitors, resulting in reduced size of mouse xenograft tumors [ ]. Additionally, the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E type 2 (EIF4E2) inhibited translation by competing with EIF4E for binding to the mRNA 5' cap structure [ – ]. In LC cells, overexpression of METTL16 induced coupling with EIF4E2 in the cytoplasm, resulting in the inability of EIF4E2 to bind to the mRNA 5' cap structure. This facilitated EIF4E-cap recognition and ultimately promoted the expression of oncogenic proteins such as procollagen Lysine-2-Oxoglutarate-5-Dioxygenase 2, PLOD2. METTL16 gene silencing weakened the above process and induced cell cycle arrest, inhibited proliferation, colony formation, and migration, and delayed the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice [ ]. This study suggests that in LC cells, there may be a signaling pathway in which METTL16 determines the fate of mRNA through a non-m(6)A pathway, leading to the promotion of cancer progression.

## Non-coding RNA
Expression of the tumor suppressor miR-33a is down-regulated in NSCLC [ ]. MiR-33a can directly target and bind to the 3'-untranslated region (3'-UTR) of METTL3 mRNA to reduce METTL3 protein levels, ultimately inhibiting cell attachment-dependent growth and proliferation in NSCLC cells [ ]. Kang et al. found that miR-33a also decreased the proliferation and invasion of LC cells by targeting the inhibition of cullin-associated NEDD8-dissociated protein 1 (CAND1) [ ]. This suggests that miR-33a may have multiple regulatory targets in LC cells upon which it exerts tumor suppressor effects. Nevertheless, it remains unknown whether miR-33a affects CAND1 to exert anti-LC activity through METTL3-m(6)A. In LC tissue, there is a negative correlation between miR-338-5p and METTL3 expression. Insufficient expression of miR-338-5p leads to the up-regulation of its target gene METTL3, resulting in increased m(6)A and stability of c-Myc mRNA and up-regulation of its protein level. This ultimately induces LC cell proliferation and migration. Overexpression of miR-338-5p inhibited expression of METTL3 and decreased proliferation, colony formation, migration, and invasion [ ]. Similarly, overexpression of miR-600 in LC inhibited METTL3 expression, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation, survival, and angiogenesis. Mechanistically, miR-600 targeted and bound METTL3 mRNA, inhibiting its expression. This decreased the activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and down-regulated β-catenin, Cyclin D1, EGFR, Tafazzin, and DNA methyltransferase 3 alpha (DNMT3A), ultimately delaying LC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion as well as inhibiting angiogenesis and promoting apoptosis [ ]. As an oncomiR, miR-663 is generally overexpressed in LC cells. MiR-663 gene silencing activated the P53 up-regulated modulator of apoptosis (PUMA)/Bcl-2 binding component 3 and B cell translocation gene 2 (BTG2)/P53 dual signaling pathways. This induced an increase in mitochondrial outer membrane permeability and sequential polarization, ultimately leading to LC cell death and delaying the growth of LC xenografts in nude mice [ ]. In another study, METTL3 overexpression in LC cells increased the level of miR-663 by promoting pri-miR-663 m(6)A, decreasing the suppression of suppressor of cytokine signaling 6 (SOCS6) to promote cell migration and invasion as well as the growth of transplanted LC cells in nude mice. Overexpression of SOCS6 effectively decreased the growth-promoting effect of LC cells induced by high expression of miR-663 [ ]. Elevated SOCS6 targets the promotion of mitochondrial fission and induces apoptosis [ ], suggesting the presence of a METTL3-m(6)A-miR-663-SOCS6-mitochondria axis in LC cells with cancer-promoting effects.
The cancer-promoting factor lncRNA DGUOK-AS1 is overexpressed in a variety of cancers including breast and cervical cancer [ , ]. DGUOK-AS1 is up-regulated in NSCLC and promotes tumor cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. In contrast, DGUOK-AS1 gene silencing produces a tumor suppressor effect. Transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily m, member 7 (TRPM7) is a bifunctional membrane protein with ion channel and kinase activities. Its expression is up-regulated in NSCLC cells, which promotes angiogenesis and cell proliferation [ ]. TRPM7 overexpression abolished the inhibition of cell growth and metastasis induced by DGUOK-AS1 gene silencing. In NSCLC cells, METTL3 overexpression promoted TRPM7 mRNA m(6)A and stability. Further, DGUOK-AS1 recruited METTL3/IGF2BP2 to recognize m(6)A-marked TRPM7 mRNA and increased TRPM7 protein levels, ultimately accelerating cell proliferation and metastasis [ ]. The oncogenic lncRNA RMRP is often overexpressed in a variety of cancer tissues including LC [ , ]. Transforming growth factor beta receptor 1 (TGFBR1) is overexpressed in NSCLC tissues and is positively correlated with RMRP expression. In LC cells, overexpression of RMRP and TGFBR1 promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of NSCLC cells. Overexpression of METTL3 in LC cells accelerated RMRP m(6)A and stability. RMRP can interact with transcription factor Y-box-binding protein-1 (YBX1) and bind to the TGFBR1 promoter to activate TGFBR1 transcription, thereby up-regulating TGFBR1 protein expression and ultimately activating the TGFBR1-SMAD2/3 signaling pathway [ ]. This suggests that there may be a METTL3/m(6)A-lncRNA-RMRP/TGFBR1/SMAD2/3 axis that promotes LC progression. LncRNA DLGAP1-AS2 is overexpressed in the cancer tissues of NSCLC patients and is negatively correlated with tumor stage and poor prognosis [ ]. In LC cells, DLGAP1-AS2 overexpression enhanced aerobic glycolysis. Accordingly, DLGAP1-AS2 knockdown effectively inhibited NSCLC cell-derived tumor growth. Mechanistically, METTL3 enhanced the stability of DLGAP1-AS2 by up-regulating m(6)A. The resulting overexpression of DLGAP1-AS2 promoted the stability of c-Myc mRNA and increased its protein level in an YTHDF1-m(6)A-dependent manner. This promoted aerobic glycolysis and ultimately accelerated the growth of LC xenograft tumors in nude mice [ ]. There is evidence that overexpression of c-Myc enhances DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs), genetic instability, and tumorigenesis [ ]. H2A histone family member X (H2AX) is responsible for maintaining genome stability and is known to initiate early DNA double-strand break repair by activating various signaling cascades. DSBs induced by γ-ionizing radiation promote c-Myc to form foci and colocalize with γ-H2AX [ ]. Therefore, c-Myc may be involved in inhibiting γ-H2AX-mediated DSB repair and promoting aberrant DNA synthesis and activation of the DNA damage response [ , ]. H2AX phosphorylation is an early indicator of DNA double-strand break formation, and the DNA damage response and may serve as a novel biomarker for monitoring genotoxic events that drive cancer development and tumor progression. Carbon ion radiotherapy is a radical non-surgical treatment with a high local control rate and no serious adverse events [ ]. Xu et al. found that H2AX was significantly up-regulated in NSCLC cells treated with carbon-ion radiation. Phosphorylated H2AX (p-H2AX) promoted the survival of cancer cells after radiotherapy [ ]. In addition, METTL3 levels and its mediated m(6)A were increased in NSCLC cells treated with carbon ion radiotherapy. METTL3 overexpression increased the m(6)A-marked H2AX mRNA levels and inhibited its degradation, increasing its protein levels. Therefore, the increase in H2AX mRNA m(6)A mediated by METTL3 overexpression can up-regulate its protein level and activation via phosphorylation. This leads to reduced sensitivity of NSCLC to carbon ion radiotherapy, enhancing DNA damage repair, and ultimately promoting the survival of NSCLC cells [ ].

## Targeting readers
Readers can recognize and bind m(6)A-marked RNAs, regulating their fate and biological functions. The expression of readers in LC tissues exhibits different patterns. For example, multiple types of readers including YTHDF1, YTHDF2, IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, IGF2BP3, and HNRNPA2B1, are often overexpressed in NSCLC tissues, where they can function to promote LC cell proliferation, survival, migration, invasion, and metastasis and are significantly negatively correlated with poor patient prognosis. Loss of expression of other members such as YTHDC2 in LC can promote tumorigenesis, cell proliferation, and migration, as well as EMT and drug resistance. Therefore, such readers may exhibit antitumor activity in LC(Table  ).

 

## PI3K/ mTOR/AKT signaling pathway
Family with sequence similarity 83 member D (FAM83D) is reportedly up-regulated in various cancers, including LC and is negatively correlated with poor prognosis in patients with cancer [ ]. Yin et al. found that FAM83D gene silencing inhibited NSCLC cell proliferation, EMT and invasion, and reduced cisplatin resistance. Consistently, patients with NSCLC with low FAM83D expression benefit the most from chemotherapy. FAM83D activates the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR)/AKT signaling pathway. Pharmacological treatment with AKT or mTOR inhibitors has been shown to restore the tumorigenic phenotype induced by FAM83D overexpression [ ]. However, in another study, Zhao et al. demonstrated that high levels of YTHDF2 in LC cells recognized m(6)A-labeled FAM83D mRNA and promoted its degradation, inhibiting protein expression and thereby reducing TGFβ1-SMAD2/3 pathway activation. FAM83D overexpression or YTHDF2 knockdown led to activation of the TGFβ1-SMAD signaling pathway, consequently inhibiting cell proliferation and induction of apoptosis and promoting cell migration, EMT, and invasion [ ]. Therefore, YTHDF2 promoted LC metastasis by negatively regulating the FAM83D-TGFβ1-pSMAD2/3 axis. This suggests that, in addition to activating the tumor suppressor effect mediated by TGFβ1-SMAD, the reduced expression of YTHDF2 also activates the tumor-promoting effect mediated by other signaling pathways. Nevertheless, the above studies imply that the role of FAM83D in LC remains controversial and more in-depth research may be still needed. Consequently, when developing anticancer drugs targeting readers-m(6)A, comprehensive considerations are needed to avoid adverse factors. The mTOR/AKT signaling pathway integrates a variety of extracellular signals involved in cell survival, proliferation, migration, metabolism, and angiogenesis. Its overactivation plays an important role in promoting tumor resistance [ – ]. In LC cells, hypoxia has been confirmed to increase the expression of YTHDF2, which can induce phosphorylation and activation of mTOR and AKT, thus promoting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. On the contrary, YTHDF2 gene silencing inhibited the activation of mTOR and AKT, thereby decreasing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion and significantly decreasing the growth of xenograft tumors in nude mice [ ].
Inhibitor of DNA-binding/differentiation-3 (ID3) belongs to the ID family of proteins and contains a helix-loop-helix (HLH) structure. ID3 lacks a DNA-binding domain but can bind to other HLH proteins and target transcription factors, thereby inhibiting their binding to DNA. Thus, ID3 alone or in synergy with other ID family members mediates tumor neovascularization and promotes cell proliferation, invasion, and stemness in a variety of human cancers, including LC [ ]. Different from the above functions, Wang et al. found that ID3 exerted a tumor suppressor effect in LC cells. They found that the expression of ID3 and YTHDC2 was significantly reduced in LC tissues. Overexpression of ID3 in NSCLC cells induced apoptosis. Mechanistically, YTHDC2 overexpression led to recognition and binding to m(6)A marked ID3 mRNA, enhancing its stability and increasing ID3 protein levels. This ultimately inhibited the proliferation, invasion, migration, and promotion of apoptosis in LC cells, reversing NSCLC cisplatin resistance [ ]. This study suggests that ID3 mRNA m(6)A mediated by YTHDC2 may play a key role in regulating chemotherapy resistance in LC cells. There is also evidence that inhibition of ID3 expression in LC cells can lead to activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [ ]. Therefore, it is suggested that an YTHDC2-m(6)A-ID3-PI3K/AKT axis may be involved in promoting the regulation of LC progression. K-homology splicing regulatory protein (KHSRP) plays an important role in regulating RNA splicing, RNA transport, RNA editing, and mRNA stability and degradation [ ]. As a result, it is involved in the occurrence and development of various cancers, including LC [ , ]. In LC cells, KHSRP overexpression specifically bound to HNRNPC (C1/C2) to form a complex, thereby activating the JAK-STAT1 signaling pathway and ultimately inducing LC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [ ]. Nevertheless, the above study did not determine whether HNRNPC activates the JAK-STAT1 signaling pathway by regulating m(6)A. There is evidence that AKT inhibits the mRNA decay function mediated by KHSRP by inducing phosphorylation of Ser193, which binds to 14–3-3ζ [ ].
Nuclear factor erythroid-2-related factor-2 (NFE2L2/NRF2) regulates the expression of intracellular antioxidant response element (ARE) -mediated antioxidant genes such as NAD(P)H dehydrogenase, quinone 1 (NQO1), glutamate-cysteine ligase modifier subunit (GCLM), and heme oxygenase (decycling) 1 (HMOX1) [ ]. Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap 1) is a negative regulator of NRF2 and is inactivated under oxidative stress conditions [ ]. Thus, the Keap1-NRF2 signaling pathway is responsible for regulating intracellular redox homeostasis. NRF2 induces cytoprotective gene expression and provides protection against hyperoxic lung injury, whereas inhibition of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway increases (mouse) lung inflammation and alveolar permeability, concomitant with a reduction in NRF2 target genes such as NQO1 and HMOX1. Inhibition of PI3K/AKT down-regulated hyperoxia-stimulated NQO1 and HMOX1 expression in lung epithelial cells and alveolar macrophages. Accordingly, PI3K/AKT inhibition aggravated lung injury and inflammation in NRF2 knockout mice [ ]. Therefore, there may be a PI3K/AKT-NRF2 axia in lung cells or stromal cells involved in regulating redox balance and pneumonia. It has been shown that high expression or mutation activation of NRF2 can promote LC cell survival, proliferation, and apoptosis resistance by inducing metabolic reprogramming [ ]. Gene silencing of NRF2 can eliminate the above-mentioned cancer-promoting effects [ , ]. Patients with high YTHDF1 expression at high altitudes (under hypoxic conditions) have a better clinical prognosis [ ]. The expression of YTHDF1 is significantly reduced in drug-resistant (cisplatin/DDP) patients. Knockdown of YTHDF1 significantly promoted an increase in tumor weight and volume of transplanted tumors in nude mice, improved the translation efficiency of tumor Cyclin D1, and promoted the proliferation and metastasis of tumor cells. In LC cells, knockdown of YTHDF1 reduced Keap1 mRNA m(6)A levels and protein expression, increased the expression of NRF2 and aldehyde-keto reductase AKR1C1, activated the antioxidant system mediated by the NRF2-AKR1C1 signaling pathway, and ultimately promoted cisplatin chemotherapy resistance [ ]. This study suggests that in a hypoxic environment, highly expressed YTHDF1 may increase protein expression by stabilizing m(6)A-marked Keap1 mRNA, inhibiting activation of the NRF2-AKR1C1 antioxidant system, reducing resistance to chemotherapeutic drugs, and resulting in a better prognosis for NSCLC patients. BRD4 can directly bind to the Keap1 promoter to activate its transcription and reduce the stability of NRF2, thereby inhibiting the expression of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) at the transcriptional level in SCLC cells. This ultimately inhibited the growth of transplanted LC tumors in nude mice [ ]. G6PD is widely present in various tissue cells of the human body. It is a key rate-limiting enzyme in the glycolytic pentose phosphate bypass pathway. Its overexpression can provide more ribose 5-phosphate and NADPH to cancer cells and promote cell growth [ , ]. The expression of G6PD is significantly increased in LC and accelerates cell growth. In LC cells, overexpressed YTHDF2 directly binds to the m(6)A site of the 3'-UTR of G6PD, promoting the translation of G6PD mRNA and ultimately enhancing cell proliferation. Down-regulation of YTHDF2 reduces glycolysis in cancer cells by inhibiting G6PD mRNA translation and suppressing G6PD protein expression, inhibiting tumor cell proliferation [ ]. Activation of the Keap1-NRF2 signaling pathway can not only be combined with the AKT signaling pathway to reduce endotoxemia-induced acute lung injury [ ] or lipopolysaccharide-induced acute lung injury (ALI) [ ], but can also attenuate resistance of LC cells to the AKT inhibitor (MK2206) [ ]. Taken together, these findings suggest that Keap1-NRF2-AKT may be involved in the occurrence, development, and treatment resistance of LC.

## Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway
Axis inhibition protein 1 (Axin1) can serve as a multidomain scaffolding protein for key regulator of multiple signaling pathways including β-catenin, JNK, MAPK, and TGF-β, thereby mediating their activation or inhibition [ ]. Of note, Axin1 serves as a negative regulator of the β-catenin signaling pathway in a variety of cancer cells, including LC [ – ]. Overexpressed YTHDF2 in LC cells bound to m(6)A-labeled Axin1 mRNA and promoted its degradation, thereby reducing its protein level, activating Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, and ultimately promoting cell proliferation and migration [ ]. Exogenous introduction of Axin1 inhibited the cancer-promoting effects caused by YTHDF2 overexpression. Discs, large homolog associated protein 5 (DLGAP5, also called HURP) is regarded as an oncogenic protein and high expression of DLGAP5 in aggressive NSCLC tissues is correlated with shorter patient survival [ ]. In LC cells, genetic silencing of DLGAP5 inhibited cell proliferation [ ]. Shi et al. found that HNRNPC-positive EVs (extracellular vesicles) from the serum of NSCLC patients can be taken up by NSCLC cells, resulting in an increase in HNRNPC levels. In vivo, HNRNPC-positive EVs can significantly accelerate the proliferation and metastasis of NSCLC cells in nude mice. Mechanistically, overexpression of HNRNPC led to an increase in DLGAP5 mRNA m(6)A levels and increased the level of DLGAP5 protein [ ]. Studies have shown that DLGAP5 gene silencing impairs the activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting cancer cell migration and invasion [ ]. However, there remains insufficient evidence to conclude that DLGAP5 can promote LC progression through the Wnt signaling pathway.
Twist1 is a helix-loop-helix (HLH) domain-containing transcription factor that is closely related to the initiation, angiogenesis, stemness, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and chemoresistance of various tumors including LC [ ]. Cui et al. found that IGF2BP3 overexpressed in LC cells recognized and bound m(6)A-labeled Twist1 mRNA, enhancing its stability and increasing its protein expression. Knockdown of Twist1 effectively weakened the enhancement of cell viability, proliferation, migration, and invasion induced by IGF2BP3 overexpression [ ]. Similarly, in another study, Twist1 is highly expressed in LC tissues compared to adjacent tissues. Twist1 gene silencing was found to lead to down-regulation of β-catenin expression and suppress cell EMT in LC cells (SPC-A-1 and NCI-H1299) [ ]. These studies suggest that the existence of an m(6)A-dependent IGF2BP3-Twist1-β-catenin axis in LC cells that is involved in NSCLC progression. In addition, Cylindromatosis lysine 63 deubiquitinate (CYLD) is a deubiquitinase [ ] that blocks the activation of multiple polyubiquitin-dependent pathways such as the NF-κB signaling pathway. This inhibits cell growth, proliferation, migration, and invasion as well as inflammation. Therefore, CYLD is regarded as a tumor suppressor protein [ – ]. The expression of CYLD is significantly down-regulated in LC tissues and significantly negatively correlated with poor prognosis in LC patients. In LC cells, YTHDC2 recognizes and binds to m(6)A-marked CYLD mRNA, enhancing its stability and ultimately increasing its protein level. YTHDC2 knockdown promotes the degradation of CYLD mRNA and simultaneously activates the NF-κB signaling pathway. Therefore, abnormal reduction of YTHDC2 in LC cells may promote cell proliferation, migration, and EMT through the CYLD/NF-κB axis [ ]. It has been shown that CYLD inhibits activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to anti-LC activity [ ]. Studies have also shown that the loss of CYLD in multiple myeloma (MM) cells enhances the invasiveness of MM through activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Therefore, the CYLD-Wnt pathway may also exist in LC cells, and its manipulation may be a promising therapeutic strategy.
SLC7A11 (also called xCT) is the catalytic subunit of the XC− system (The XC− system is a sodium-independent cystine/glutamate antiporter complex formed by SLC7A11, which is connected to the regulatory subunit SLC3A2 through a disulfide bond). The XC− system can take in extracellular cystine and pump out intracellular glutamate, thereby providing raw materials for the production of intracellular antioxidant GSH to maintain the redox balance and other life processes in the cell. When the activity of the XC− system is inhibited, the cell lacks cystine and cannot produce GSH, resulting in the inactivation of Glutathione Peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and the accumulation of toxic lipid ROS, which in turn promotes lipid membrane and protein oxidative damage and triggers ferroptosis [ , ]. Ma et al. found that most LC patients showed both down-regulation of YTHDC2 and increased SLC7A11 in cancer tissues; the expression of the two had a significant negative correlation. Overexpression of YTHDC2 in LC cells significantly inhibited the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice. In LC cells, up-regulation of SLC7A11 expression led to high levels of intracellular cystine uptake, redox balance, and lipid metabolism, thereby promoting tumor growth. Mechanistically, the up-regulated YTHDC2 preferentially bound to m(6)A-marked SLC7A11 mRNA, promoting SLC7A11 mRNA decay and down-regulating SLC7A11 protein levels. This ultimately inhibited cystine uptake and downstream antioxidant processes, and significantly delayed the growth of transplanted tumors in nude mice. Moreover, XC− system inhibitors such as erastin or piperazine erastin have also been shown to significantly inhibit the growth of LUAD-derived mouse xenografts (PDX) [ ]. This suggests that there is an YTHDC2-m(6)A-SLC7A11 axis in LC cells that can promote progression. Other studies have shown that silica dust SiO2 activated the Wnt5a signaling pathway in the macrophages of lung tissue. This led to down-regulation of SLC7A11 and increased lipid oxidation, resulting in macrophage redox imbalance and ferroptosis, ultimately resulting in pulmonary fibrosis. This suggests that SLC7A11 may promote tumorigenesis by affecting tumor immunity [ ]. The expression of miR-376c in NSCLC is significantly lower than in normal or adjacent tissues, and it inhibits the migration and invasion of NSCLC cells [ ]. EVs produced by Ecs (endothelial cells) contain miR-376c. Under co-culture conditions, EC-Evs efficiently delivered miR-376c to NSCLC cells. MiR-376c directly targeted YTHDF1 in NSCLC cells, down-regulating the expression of YTHDF1, thereby impairing the Wnt/β-catenin pathway and reducing migration and invasion. YTHDF1 overexpression reversed EC-Evs-induced Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibition and EVs-miR-376c-induced tumor-promoting effects in NSCLC cells [ ].

## Non-conding RNA
Overexpression of the oncomiR miR-21-5p has been demonstrated in various cancers, including LC [ ]. High expression of miR-21-5p promoted cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis resistance and has been implicated in tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis [ ]. In NSCLC cells, miR-21-5p undergoes a large number of m(6)A modifications, and its overexpression can significantly accelerate cell proliferation and migration [ ]. Up-regulation of HNRNPA2B1 can be used as an independent prognostic marker in NSCLC patients, as it is significantly associated with distant metastasis and poor survival [ ]. Low expression of lncRNA MEG3 or elevated expression of miR-21-5p is also often predictive of a poor prognosis in NSCLC patients [ ]. In vitro, knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 impairs LC cell proliferation and metastasis. Mechanistically, HNRNPA2B1 recognized m(6)A-modified lncRNA MEG3 and reduced its stability, decreasing its inhibitory effect on miR-21-5p and up-regulating PTEN expression to inactivate PI3K/AKT signaling, which ultimately inhibited cell proliferation and invasion [ ]. Cancer stem cells (CSCs) exhibit strong self-renewal capabilities and drug resistance, and are believed to contribute to the development and chemotherapy resistance of various cancers, including lung cancer [ ]. RNA m(6)A has been shown to be a key mechanism by which cancer cells acquire stemness, promoting tumor growth [ ]. Rong et al. found that HNRNPA2B1 expression was up-regulated in lung CSCs. HNRNPA2B1 knockdown inhibited cell stemness, proliferation, migration, and LC xenograft tumor growth in mice. Mechanistically, elevated HNRNPA2B1 promotes the maturation and expression of miR-106b-5p by recognizing the m(6)A sequence on pri-miR-106b. Consequentially, the increased expression of miR-106b-5p in LC cells led to targeting and silencing of secreted frizzled related protein 2 (SFRP2) and activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which enhanced the stemness of LC cells and promoted cell growth and proliferation, thereby accelerating LC progression [ ]. MiR-320b is regarded as a tumor suppressor, as it inhibits the progression of LC by inhibiting proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis in LC cells [ ]. Studies have shown that in LC cells, overexpressed miR-320b targeted and inhibited the transcription factor hepatocyte nuclear factor 4γ (HNF4G), thereby down-regulating the expression of its target gene IGF2BP2 [ ]. Thymidine kinase 1 (TK1), a cytosolic enzyme involved in pyrimidine metabolism, catalyzes the addition of a γ-phosphate group to thymidine. TK1 mediates the generation and maintenance of deoxyribonucleotide dTTP, which is required for DNA replication and DNA damage recovery, thereby preventing DNA damage-induced cell death [ ]. Huang et al. found that miR-320b was underexpressed in LC tissues, while m(6)A-modified TK1 mRNA levels were significantly increased. Moreover, overexpression of TK1 in LC cells promoted cell invasion and angiogenesis. IGF2BP2 recognizes m(6)A-modified TK1 mRNA and increases TK1 protein levels [ ]. Therefore, in LC cells, there may be a miR-320b/HNF4G/IGF2BP2-m(6)A/TK1 axis that inhibits cancer progression. Additionally, it has been shown that TK1 is overexpressed in the tumors of patients with LC and is significantly negatively correlated with poor prognosis. Furthermore, gene silencing of TK1 in LC cells has been shown to effectively impair cancer cell growth and metastasis [ ]. Transcription factor activating enhancer binding protein 4 (TFAP-4) activated IGF2BP1 transcription and promoted its expression in LC cells, thereby promoting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion and inhibiting apoptosis. In contrast, IGF2BP1 gene silencing resulted in tumor suppressor effects. A study using nude mouse LC cell-derived tumors confirmed that TFAP4 knockdown inhibited tumor growth by down-regulating IGF2BP1 and TK1 [ ]. Mechanistically, TFAP4-mediated IGF2BP1 overexpression stabilized m(6)A-labeled TK1 mRNA and up-regulated its protein expression. Decreased expression of IGF2BP1 in NSCLC led to down-regulation of TK1 through a reduction in TK1 mRNA m(6)A [ ]. Taken together, the results of these studies suggest that an IGF2BP1/2- m(6)A-TK1 axis may be involved in promoting LC progression.
LncRNA MALAT1 (also called NEAT2) is one of the most conserved lncRNAs in human tissues and is up-regulated in a variety of cancer tissues, including LC. MALAT1 is considered a cancer-promoter as it promotes the proliferation, migration, invasion, and inhibition of apoptosis in tumor cells [ , ]. Overexpression of MALAT1 in NSCLC led to targeting and up-regulation of autophagy related protein 12 (ATG12), thereby promoting LC cell proliferation. IGF2BP2 recognizes and binds to m(6)A-modified MALAT1, enhancing its stability and up-regulating ATG12 protein levels [ ]. Overexpression of lncRNA LCAT1 has been shown to promote the proliferation and metastasis of LC cells. LCAT1 and IGF2BP2 expression is significantly increased in LC tissues and their expression is positively correlated [ ]. In LC cells, LCAT1 combined with IGF2BP2 to form a complex, increasing the stability of IGF2BP2. IGF2BP2 in the complex enhanced the stability of cell division cycle 6 (CDC6) mRNA in an m(6)A-dependent manner, up-regulating CDC6 protein levels and ultimately promoting the proliferation and metastasis of LC cells. Therefore, the LCAT1-IGF2BP2-CDC6 axis exerts a pro-oncogenic role [ ]. The expression level of LncRNA ZNRD1-AS1 in LC is significantly lower than in adjacent tissues. In LC cells, lncRNA ZNRD1-AS1 gene deletion promoted cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. YTHDC2 bound to m(6)A-modified ZNRD1-AS1 and promoted its stability. Overexpression of ZNRD1-AS1 led to targeting and inhibition of miR-942, thereby increasing miR-942 target protein tensin 1 (TNS1) levels and ultimately inhibiting LC cell proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis and reducing xenograft tumor growth [ ]. Therefore, the YTHDC2-ZNRD1-AS1-miR-942/TNS1 axis dysfunction may be involved in promoting LC progression.
CircRNA SFMBT2 is overexpressed in a variety of cancer tissues, including LC, promoting cell proliferation and metastasis [ – ]. The Hippo/YAP signaling pathway is involved in regulating organ size, tissue homeostasis, and tumorigenesis in mammals. YAP is reportedly overexpressed in cancer tissue samples from LC patients, leading to inhibition of PTEN expression in a Hippo pathway-dependent manner. This induces the activation of the mTOR/AKT signaling pathway, ultimately inhibiting autophagy, and promoting cell proliferation [ ]. Xu et al. found that SFMBT2 was underexpressed in NSCLC tissues. In LC cells, SFMBT2 gene silencing promoted cell proliferation and migration. Conversely, SFMBT2 overexpression inhibited cell proliferation, viability, migration, and EMT. Mechanistically, overexpression of YTHDF2 in LC cells led to recognition and binding to m(6)A-labeled SFMBT2, accelerating the degradation of SFMBT2. This led to activation of the Hippo/YAP signaling pathway and ultimately enhanced the proliferation and migration of NSCLC cells [ ].

## Others
Small ubiquitin-like modifier-1 (SUMO-1) belongs to the SUMO protein family, which includes four subtypes: SUMO1, SUMO2, SUMO3, and SUMO4. SUMOylation is a reversible post-translational protein modification where the SUMO protein attaches to a target lysine; this occurs through a series of enzyme systems to form a covalent link. SUMOylation thus regulates the function of proteins, participating in the regulation of DNA damage repair, the immune response, carcinogenesis, cell cycle progression, and apoptosis [ – ]. In LC cells, SUMO1 overexpression promoted colony formation, proliferation, invasion, and cell cycle progression. Mechanistic analysis showed that YTHDF2 can undergo SUMOylation, which significantly increases its binding to m(6)A-marked RNA and changes the gene expression profile in cells, ultimately enhancing the above-mentioned malignant characteristics of LC cells [ ].

## Targeting erasers
Eraser-related proteins such as FTO and ALKBH1 are generally overexpressed in LC tissues. These proteins promote cell growth, proliferation, migration, and invasion, and are negatively correlated with poor prognosis and poor survival [ , ]. There are seemingly contradictory conclusions about the expression of ALKBH5 in LC tissues. ALKBH5 is generally overexpressed in NSCLC tissues, promoting cell growth, proliferation, migration, and invasion, and reducing apoptosis in vitro. However, some studies have shown that ALKBH5 expression is significantly down-regulated in metastatic NSCLC tissues, which can induce cell EMT, migration and invasion, resulting in a poor overall survival rate [ ]. Accordingly, ectopic expression of ALKBH5 has been shown to inhibit NSCLC cell proliferation, invasion, migration, and EMT [ ] (Table  ).

 

## Cellular myelocytomatosis oncogene (c-Myc) signaling pathway
Overactivation of the oncoprotein c-Myc enhances metabolism and promotes cell growth, death resistance, metastasis, drug resistance, and cancer cell stemness maintenance via promoting cell cycle kinesin and anti-apoptotic protein expression [ ]. In LC tissues, c-Myc is often overexpressed and negatively associated with patient survival [ ]. It has been shown that liver kinase B1 (LKB1) is often lost in LC cells, leading to the overexpression and activation of the transcription factor myeloid zinc finger1 (MZF1). Up-regulated MZF1 further activates c-Myc expression and promotes LC progression [ ]. In one study, overexpression of FTO in LC cells enhanced MZF1 mRNA de-m(6)A and increased its stability, thereby increasing MZF1 protein levels and enhancing the viability and invasion of LC cells [ ]. This suggests that a LKB1/FTO-m(6)A/MZF1/c-Myc axis may be involved in regulating LC progression.
Wingless-type MMTV integration site family (Wnt) is a secreted glycoprotein that interacts with receptors of the Frizzled family, including lipoprotein receptor-related protein 5/6 (LRP5/6), Ror2, and Ryk. Activation of Wnt signaling cascades can regulate a diverse range of processes including cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, polarity, asymmetric cell division, and stem cell renewal. Wnt levels are increased in various cancer tissues including LC [ ]. Increased Wnt has been positively correlated with the expression of β-catenin, Cyclin D1, and c-Myc. Studies have confirmed that the expression of FTO is down-regulated in LC tissues, which may be attributed to Wnt signaling induction of the histone methyltransferase EZH2 to combine with β-catenin. EZH2 and β-catenin form a complex that binds to the LEF/TCF binding element in the FTO promoter region, through which EZH2 enhances H3K27me3 and inhibits FTO expression. Consequentially, down-regulation of FTO expression can significantly increase c-Myc mRNA m(6)A and translation in a YTHDF1-dependent manner, thereby promoting glycolysis and ultimately accelerating cell proliferation, invasion, and LC xenograft growth in nude mice [ ]. The incidence of Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homologue (K-Ras) mutation in NSCLC patients is approximately 36%. K-Ras mutated LC cells can be divided into three subgroups based on the gene mutations that occur simultaneously with K-Ras: K-Ras/LKB1, K-Ras/P53, and K-Ras/CDKN2A/2B. Therefore, K-Ras mutant LC is highly heterogeneous [ ]. Among these, LKB1 is regarded as a tumor suppressor and is involved in regulating various biological processes including cell polarity, cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and energy metabolism [ ]. Approximately 70% of K-Ras-mutated NSCLC cases also harbor LKB1 deletions. This type of LC (with K-Ras mutation and LKB1 deletion: KL) usually exhibits greater metastatic spread and drug resistance than cases with neither mutations nor deletions [ ]. ALKBH5 mRNA is overexpressed in KL LC tissues or cells, where global m(6)A levels are significantly reduced and closely related to disease progression and poor survival rates in KL patients. In KL cells, LKB1 deletion induced DNA hypermethylation of the CTCF binding motif in the ALKBH5 promoter, up-regulating ALKBH5 expression. Restoration of LKB1 expression has been shown to effectively reverse this effect. The reduction of m(6)A levels caused by ALKBH5 overexpression promoted expression of the oncogenic factors SOX2, SMAD7, and c-Myc through a YTHDF2-dependent pathway, ultimately leading to the migration and invasion of LC cells [ ].
Ubiquitin-specific protease 7 (USP7) belongs to the deubiquitinating enzyme (DUBs) family. Its main function is to hydrolyze Ub to deubiquitinate the target protein and extend its half-life. USP7 overexpression promotes the occurrence and progression of various human cancers by interacting with oncogenic proteins such as MDM2, MDMX, β-catenin, NF-κB, and FOXP3 [ – ]. USP7 is overexpressed in the cancer tissues of NSCLC patients. Exogenous introduction of USP7 into LC cells has been shown to accelerate cell proliferation and colony formation. Mechanistically, FTO enhanced the stability of USP7 mRNA and increased USP7 protein levels by promoting de- m(6)A of USP7 mRNA, ultimately promoting cell growth [ ]. Studies have shown that USP7 can mediate deubiquitination of c-Myc to extend its half-life, which promotes EMT and drug (cisplatin) resistance in LC cells [ ]. This suggests that there may be a FTO-m(6)A/USP7/c-Myc axis that promotes LC progression and drug resistance. The cytidine deaminase APOBEC3B (A3B) is a DNA cytosine deaminase that is widely expressed in normal tissues and organs of the human body. APOBEC3B (A3B) mediates base substitution of viral DNA in infected cells, thereby inhibiting viral replication and transcription. Therefore, it plays an important role in viral defense. A3B is an endogenous inducer of somatic mutations, as it converts cytosine in single-stranded DNA to uracil, causing chromosomal instability [ ]. Overexpression of A3B in LC may lead to somatic DNA mutations and is closely related to shorter overall survival. Mechanistically, overexpression of FTO in LC cells increased the m(6)A level of A3B mRNA to enhance its mRNA stability and up-regulate its protein expression, ultimately increasing the intracellular DNA mutation rate [ ]. This cancer-promoting mechanism was verified in a study where the mutagen and carcinogen arsenic was shown to promote DNA damage in LC cells through activation of the FTO/A3B signaling pathway [ ]. There is evidence that overexpression of A3B induces base substitutions in the c-Myc gene, causing genome instability. This suggests that A3B overexpression may increase the risk of cancer [ ]. The lncRNA PVT1 is regarded as a cancer-promoting factor, and high expression levels have been shown to promote tumor cell metastasis, invasion, and resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy [ – ]. Tokgun et al. found that overexpression of c-Myc in LC cells can lead to increased levels of lncRNA PVT1 and mediate accelerated cell proliferation [ ]. Overexpression of the lncRNA PVT1 in LC cells has been attributed to ALKBH5-mediated promotion of cell proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis through binding to lncRNA PVT1 and enhancing its stability [ ]. This suggests that in LC cells, a c-Myc/ALKBH5/PVT1 axis may be involved in cancer progression and treatment resistance.

## Signaling pathways involved in reprogramming glucose metabolism
Reprogramming of glucose metabolism is one of the hallmarks of cancer. The glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1) is a key regulator of glucose metabolism. In LC cells, the lncRNA GAS6-AS1 is often overexpressed. GAS6-AS1 directly interacts with the transcription factor E2F transcription factor-1(E2F1) and inhibits E2F1-mediated GLUT1 transcription, inhibiting glucose metabolism reprogramming and ultimately delaying LC progression [ ]. E2F1 is a key transcription protein factor involved in various biological processes including cell proliferation and apoptosis [ ], oxidative metabolism [ ], and cell senescence [ ]. E2F1 is overexpressed in the cancer tissues of NSCLC patients. It can induce the increased expression of neural epidermal growth factor-like 2 (NELL2) and enhance the migration and invasion of NSCLC cells. Mechanistically, FTO overexpression up-regulated the expression of E2F1 by promoting E2F1 mRNA de-m(6)A, leading to activation of NELL2 transcription. In nude mice, this has been shown to increase cell viability and promote migration, invasion, growth, and metastasis of LC xenograft tumors [ ]. The above studies suggest that there may be a GAS6-AS1/FTO-m(6)A/E2F1/GLUT1 axis which promotes the progression of LC through the reprogramming of glucose metabolism.
The transcription factor Forkhead Box M1 (FOXM1) belongs to the Forkhead box (Fox) family. Its high expression in actively dividing cells may result from up-regulation of cyclins (such as Cyclin A2, Cyclin B1, Cyclin E, and Cyclin F) and cyclin-dependent kinase-1 (Cdk1), which drives cell cycle progression. FoxM1 overexpression is found in many tumors, including NSCLC [ ]. Intermittent hypoxia (IH) caused by obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is reported to be closely related to cancer progression [ , ]. A study from Wang et al. found that under conditions of intermittent hypoxia, FOXM1 and ALKBH5 protein levels increased while global m(6)A levels decreased in mouse LC cells and subcutaneous LC tissues. Functionally, knockdown of ALKBH5 in LC cells significantly inhibited cell proliferation and invasion under IH conditions. Mechanistically, loss of ALKBH5 expression up-regulated m(6)A levels in FOXM1 mRNA and reduced the translation efficiency of FOXM1 mRNA, resulting in reduced FOXM1 protein levels. Exogenous introduction of FOXM1 partially impaired the tumor suppressor effect caused by ALKBH5 knockout [ ]. This suggests that LC progression may be promoted through the IH-ALKBH5-m(6)A-FOXM1 axis [ ]. There is evidence that FOXM1 directly binds to the promoter regions of GLUT1 and Hexokinase 2 (HK2), regulating and promoting the expression of two sugar metabolism-related genes at the transcriptional level, ultimately promoting aerobic glycolysis and cell proliferation [ ].

## Signaling pathways triggering autophagy
Autophagy is a double-edged sword with regard to the occurrence and progression of various tumor types. In most cases, autophagy exerts a protective effect on cancer cells by conferring resistance to apoptosis and therapy [ ]. The ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2C (UBE2C) is an ubiquitinase that catalyzes protein degradation in the 26S proteasome. It is considered an oncogene due to evidence that it promotes carcinogenesis by increasing cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis [ , ]. Aberrant activation of UBE2C in cancer tissues of LC patients is often inversely associated with poor prognosis [ ]. UBE2C is up-regulated in NSCLC cells, leading to inhibition of autophagy, increased cell proliferation, metastasis, and invasive growth. Disruption of UBE2C-mediated autophagy inhibition was shown to attenuate cell proliferation, colony formation, and invasive growth of NSCLC. Therefore, blocking the UBE2C-autophagy inhibition axis prevented NSCLC progression. Mechanistically, up-regulation of ALKBH5 leads to demethylation of the m(6)A on UBE2C mRNA and subsequent stabilization of its expression. This causes up-regulation of UBE2C expression and inhibition of autophagy, ultimately promoting the proliferation, metastasis, and invasion of NSCLC cells [ ]. UBE2C gene silencing can lead to a significant increase in the phosphorylation level of Yes-associated protein (YAP) (Ser127) in LC cells, which may accelerate the degradation of YAP in the cytoplasm and inhibit cell proliferation [ , ]. YAP is a main core effector of the Hippo signaling pathway. It mainly regulates target gene transcription by binding to transcriptional associate enhanced domain (TEAD1-4)/non-TEAD transcription factors (such as SMAD, RUNX1/2, p63/p73, or ErbB4). YAP is involved in various biological effects including cell proliferation and differentiation [ ]. YAP expression is increased in NSCLC cells, leading to cancer progression, drug resistance, and metastasis; it is also negatively correlated with poor prognosis [ , ]. In LC tissues, YAP expression is negatively correlated with ALKBH5 expression, and ectopic expression of ALKBH5 can effectively inhibit LC cell proliferation, invasion, migration, and EMT. Mechanistically, overexpression of ALKBH5 leads to a reduction in the m(6)A level of YAP mRNA in NSCLC cells, which promotes YAP mRNA degradation and down-regulates YAP protein in a YTHDF2-Argonaute 2 system-dependent manner. Furthermore, ALKBH5 also reduces YAP activity by activating the miR-107/Large tumor suppressor kinase 2 (LATS2) axis in a HuR-dependent manner. Finally, ALKBH5-mediated inhibition of YAP expression and activity was shown to delay the growth and metastasis of LC xenografts in nude mice [ ]. Additionally, YAP overexpression inhibited PTEN and activated the mTOR/AKT signaling pathway in a Hippo pathway-dependent manner, promoting LC cell proliferation. Accordingly, gene silencing of YAP induces autophagy. The autophagy inhibitor 3-methyladenine (3-MA) can block autophagy induced by YAP deficiency and promote the proliferation of LC cells in vitro and in vivo, indicating that high YAP expression inhibits autophagy in LC cells, thus promoting tumor progression [ ]. Alternatively, another study reported that YAP gene silencing inhibited CytoMix-induced autophagy in NSCLC cells and decreased cell invasion. This result indicates that YAP may play a key role in facilitating NSCLC progression by regulating autophagosome biogenesis and maturation [ ].
Intriguingly, the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-37 has been shown to alleviate pro-inflammatory macrophage infiltration, tissue damage, and collagen deposition by inhibiting the YAP-NLRP3 signaling pathway [ ]. Further, IL-37 is thought to be a potential tumor suppressor as it can impair LC cell EMT by inhibiting IL-6 activation of the STAT3 signaling pathway [ ]. Kim et al. found that expression of the IL-37 protein in alveolar epithelial cells (AEC) and alveolar macrophages in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) was significantly reduced. In vitro experiments have confirmed that IL-37 can inhibit the activation of the TGF-β1 signaling pathway and impair the proliferation of lung fibroblasts. IL-37 also significantly enhanced Beclin-1-dependent autophagy in IPF fibroblasts, thereby reducing inflammation and collagen deposition in mouse lungs. This effect was reversed by treatment with the autophagy inhibitor 3-MA [ ]. This suggests that reduction of IL-37 may be associated with the progression of IPF, as IL-37-induced inhibition of TGF-β1 signaling enhanced autophagy in IPF fibroblasts. The expression of IL-37 in LC tissues was also significantly reduced. Overexpression of IL-37 in LC cells significantly reduced the expression of ALKBH5 and down-regulated Wnt5a/b protein expression. IL-37 also induced a significant increase in global RNA m(6)A levels in LC cells [ ]. In addition, IL-37 may inhibit LC tumor growth by regulating the m(6)A of cancer-promoting factors such as Wnt5a/b mRNA and promoting degradation. It has also been reported that during the lung inflammatory injury induced by particulate matter 2.5 (PM2.5), increased expression of Wnt5a can trigger autophagy in macrophages (MH-S cells), activating the NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1β release in alveolar MH-S cells. Accordingly, Rapamycin has been shown to block this effect [ ]. This indicates that autophagy mediated by the Wnt signaling pathway may be involved in LC occurrence and progression.
Increasing evidence suggests that autophagy enhances the stemness of CSCs [ ]. Of note, one study showed that autophagy in LC cells enhanced CSC stemness by degrading ubiquitinated P53 [ ]. P53 is a tumor suppressor protein that can maintain the balance between self-renewal and differentiation as well as maintain the stability of the internal environment of the organ. The incidence of P53 mutations or deletions in LC is approximately 50%‒85% [ ]. Gain-of-function (GOF) mutations in P53 further enhance cancer cell stemness [ ]. The expression of p53 in LC is positively correlated with ALKBH5 [ ]. Mechanistically, P53 mutation (mutP53) in LC cells promotes the transcription and expression of ALKBH5, resulting in high expression of ALKBH5 in NSCLC-derived CSCs. This results in down-regulation of the global m(6)A level and ultimately enhances CSC stemness. MutP53 knockout or inhibition of the transcriptional activity of p53 reduced the expression of ALKBH5 and the malignant characteristics of CSCs such as increased cell proliferation, invasion, and tumor formation [ ]. Knockout of ALKBH5 increased global m(6)A and E-cadherin levels and reduced the stem cells markers Nanog and Oct4, thereby inhibiting the stemness of CSCs. MutP53 can also inhibit E-cadherin expression and promote EMT and stemness by up-regulating paired related homeobox 1 (PRRX1) [ ]. This suggests that the presence of a mutP53-ALKBH5-PRRX1 axis in P53 mutant LC cells may promote cancer cell stemness, invasion, and migration. In addition, Loging et al. found that compared to wild-type P53, the ability of P53 mutants (R248W and D281G) to inhibit the transcription of the tumor suppressor tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 3 (TIMP3) gene was increased more than ten times, showing that the mutP53 has strong cancer-promoting capacity [ ]. Together, these findings suggest that there may be a mutP53-m(6A)-autophagy axis in LC CSCs that maintains self-renewal, tumorigenesis, and progression. TIMP3 is a protease that is highly expressed in normal lung tissue and can promote the resolution of inflammation after lung injury and IPF [ , ]. TIMP3 expression is reduced in LC and is positively correlated with poor patient prognosis [ ]. ALKBH5 overexpression promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and reduced apoptosis in vitro, whereas knockdown of ALKBH5 inhibited tumor growth in vivo. In LC cells, elevated ALKBH5 expression reduced TIMP3 mRNA stability and protein production. On the contrary, ALKBH5 gene silencing increased the half-life of TIMP3 mRNA and increased its RNA stability, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation and metastasis and increasing apoptosis. In addition, ALKBH5 silencing in mouse LC xenografts inhibited tumor growth rate [ ].
TIMP3 has been reported to interact with Beclin-1 to regulate autophagy in both cancer and normal cells. TIMP3 gene silencing can weaken autophagy and inhibit apoptosis [ ]. It is unknown whether TIMP3 can regulate autophagy caused by radiotherapy and chemotherapy in LC cells. TIMP3 expression has been reported to be regulated by TGF-β [ ]. In IPF lung fibroblasts, autophagy is involved in promoting the production of collagen 1α2 and fibronectin induced by TGF-β1. By inhibiting autophagy in fibroblasts, TGF-β1-induced collagen and fibronectin biosynthesis is significantly reduced, improving IPF [ ]. Inhibiting autophagy and inactivating the TGF-β1 signaling pathway in LC significantly inhibited tumor growth [ ]. The TGF-β pathway has been shown to activate the SMAD cascade signaling to induce cancer cell EMT [ ]. Activation of the TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway promoted LC cell invasion [ , ]. Sun et al. found that ALKBH5 expression was significantly reduced in metastatic NSCLC. ALKBH5 overexpression inhibited TGF-β-induced LC cell EMT, invasion, and metastasis, whereas ALKBH5 knockdown promoted the corresponding phenotypes. Mechanistically, ALKBH5 overexpression decreased YTHDF1/3-mediated TGFβR2 and SMAD3 mRNA stability and eliminated YTHDF2-mediated SMAD6 mRNA degradation, thereby inhibiting TGF-β-induced NSCLC cell EMT and invasion. This suggests that in LC cells, there is an ALKBH5-TGFβR2/SMAD3/6 axis that controls TGF-β-induced NSCLC cell EMT [ ].

## Targeting m(6)A in the tumor microenvironment (TME)
The TME is a heterogeneous ecosystem that contains cancer cells, immune cells, stromal cells, cytokines, chemokines, extracellular matrix, and vascular networks. Communication between tumor cells and the TME can achieve immune evasion and induce angiogenesis, drug resistance, invasion, and metastasis. It has been shown that METTL3 overexpression in LC cells can promote the expression and secretion of pro-tumorigenic chemokines including CXCL1, CXCL5, and CCL20 by elevating c-Myc mRNA m(6)A and increasing c-Myc protein levels [ ]. METTL3 overexpression can also increase PD-L1 mRNA m(6)A and reduce its stability, producing an anti-inflammatory TME in LC. Accordingly, gene silencing or knocking out METTL3 induced LC TME reprogramming to produce a pro-inflammatory TME. This resulted in enhance effects of immunotherapy (anti-PD-1) in a mouse model of LC. Clinically, NSCLC patients exhibiting low METTL3 expression have a better prognosis when receiving anti-PD-1 therapy [ ]. Therefore, abnormal RNA m(6)A can also produce a favorable survival environment for tumor cells by regulating the TME, ultimately promoting progression and therapeutic resistance.

## Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs)
CAFs, which can accelerate tumor progression by producing various cytokines or growth factors, are the main stromal cells found in the TME [ ]. CAFs regulate the inflammatory microenvironment by promoting the expression of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8 in NSCLC, thereby promoting cancer [ ]. Rac family small GTPase 3 (RAC3) is a member of the RAS superfamily of small GTP-binding proteins, and its overexpression in LC cells can promote invasion and metastasis [ ]. Chen et al. found that human LC tissue-derived CAFs up-regulated METTL3 expression in NSCLC cells through VEGF-A secretion, thus promoting RAC3 mRNA m(6)A and stability and elevating RAC3 protein levels. This further activated the AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway and promoted NSCLC cell migration. METTL3 knockdown in NSCLC cells significantly weakened CAFs-induced cell migration and invasion and inhibited LC xenograft growth in nude mice [ ]. This study reveals that the CAF-METTL3-m(6)A-RAC3 axis promotes LC cell metastasis.
The collagen type X alpha 1 (COL10A1) is a secreted short-chain collagen that is highly expressed in various cancer types, including LC, and induces extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling to promote cancer progression [ , ]. COL10A1 promotes cancer cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, invasion, EMT, and angiogenesis [ , ]. Li et al. found that COL10A1 expression was significantly increased in CAFs derived from the plasma of LUSC patients, and that it inhibited oxidative stress-induced apoptosis and promoted LUSC cell proliferation under co-culture conditions. Mechanistically, increased expression of METTL3 in CAFs promoted LC cell proliferation and inhibited apoptosis by promoting COL10A1 mRNA m(6)A. Knockdown of COL10A1 in CAFs blocked CAF-CM-induced LUSC cell growth and apoptosis inhibition [ ]. Gene silencing of METTL3 in CAFs resulted in a significant increase in the expression and secretion of IL-18, whereas overexpression of METTL3 had the opposite effect. Co-culture of METTL3 gene-silencing CAFs with NSCLC cells significantly reduced the secretion of PD-L1 by the latter. NSCLC cells treated with medium containing CAFs resulted in a significant decrease in granzyme B and perforin levels in CD8( +) T cells [ ]. METTL3 knockout in CAFs further exacerbated CD8( +) T cell activation. Mechanistically, CAF-derived IL-18 induced immunosuppression by activating the NF-κB signaling pathway in NSCLC cells. This shows that METTL3 acts as a major effector against NSCLC immunosuppression by driving the NF-κB pathway [ ]. Therefore, METTL3 may regulate the production of IL18 by CAFs and induce immune suppression in LC the TME.

## Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs)
Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are the most abundant bone marrow-derived cell population found in the TME. TAMs can be divided into the anti-tumor M1 phenotype and the pro-tumor M2 phenotype, based on their function and molecular phenotype. M1-TAMs activate type 1 immunity, have strong antigen presenting capacity, and can produce a variety of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12, IL-23, CXCL9, CXCL10, and TNF-α to exert antitumor effects. M2-TAMs mainly activate type 2 immunity and secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines including IL-10, TGF-β, CCL17, CCL18, CCL22, and CCL24, which are generally considered to promote tumor initiation and progression [ ]. Tumor cells can induce the non-classically activated anti-tumor M1-TAMs phenotype to transform into the activated immunosuppressive M2-TAMs phenotype by recruiting TAMs and reshaping the gene expression of TAMs. In other words, TAMs have functional plasticity and M2-TAMs can become M1-TAMs after re-education by tumor cells [ ].
The infiltration of M2-TAMs was shown to be significantly increased in cancer tissues of nivolumab-resistant LC patients. In vitro, M2-TAMs induced LC cells to express and secrete immunosuppressive factors such as IL-10, TGF-β1, and CCL3. This increased resistance to cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) cytotoxicity, ultimately promoting cell proliferation, invasion, and migration [ ]. In vivo, M2-TAMs significantly increased the metastasis of LC cells injected through the tail veins of mice. Mechanistically, M2-TAMs significantly enhanced METTL3 expression and global RNA m(6)A levels in LC cells. Gene silencing of METTL3 significantly reversed M2-TAMs inhibition of the efficacy of CTL CD8( +) T cell killing in LUAD cells. Therefore, M2-TAM can promote immune resistance in LUAD cells by enhancing METTL3-mediated m(6)A [ ]. As such, METTL3 is a potential therapeutic target for reversing immune resistance. This research also provides new clues for the mechanisms by which M2-TAMs promote immune resistance in LUAD. Cigarette smoke extract (CSE)-induced production of extracellular vesicles (EVs) by M2-TAMs can enhance the malignant characteristics (such as proliferation, migration, and invasion) of NSCLC cells in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, circRNA EML4 in CSE-induced M2-TAMs-derived EVs is transported to NSCLC cells where it interacts with ALKBH5, reducing its distribution in the nucleus and inhibiting its RNA demethylation function. This enhances intracellular suppressor of cytokine signaling 2 (SOCS2) mRNA m(6)A levels and up-regulates its protein level, promoting the activation of the Janus kinase (JAK)-signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) signaling pathways. This ultimately promoted the growth and metastasis of transplanted LC in nude mice [ ]. EVs produced by CSE-induced EML4 gene-silenced M2-TAMs can significantly delay the growth of NSCLC xenografts in nude mice. These studies indicate that CSE-induced M2-TAMs-derived EML4-positive EVs can promote NSCLC progression through the ALKBH5-m(6)A-SOCS2/JAK-STAT3 axis. For patients with a history of smoking, smoking-induced accumulation of M2-TAMs in the TME may promote LC progression through the above-mentioned pathways and is a prognostic marker for a poor outcome [ ]. Colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) belongs to the class III receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK III) family. It promotes macrophages differentiation into M2-TAMs, which in turn induces tumor immunosuppression [ ]. CSF1R is overexpressed in LC tissues, which results in cancer cell migration and invasion. Mechanistically, overexpression of METTL14 leads to enhanced CSF1R expression in LC cells. CSF1R knockdown blocked the pro-proliferative and metastatic effects induced by METTL14 overexpression [ ]. Zhang et al. found that the small molecule CSF-1R inhibitor PLX 3397 (PLX) administered alone inhibited tumor growth in tumor-bearing mice while significantly reducing M2-TAMs infiltration in tumor tissue and increasing the number of M1-TAMs. Compared to PLX or cisplatin alone, the combination of PLX and cisplatin achieved greater tumor growth inhibition and reduced M2-TAMs infiltration [ ].

## Other cell types in the TME
Expression of circNDUFB2 is significantly down-regulated in NSCLC tissues. As a tumor suppressor, its expression is negatively correlated with malignant characteristics [ ]. Overexpression of circNDUFB2 can effectively inhibit LC cell growth and metastasis. Mechanistically, circNDUFB2 acts as a scaffold, enhancing the interaction between the E3 ubiquitin ligase TRIM25 and IGF2BP1/2/3 to form the ternary complex TRIM25/circNDUFB2/IGF2BPs, which facilitates the ubiquitination and degradation of IGF2BPs. Gene silencing of METTL3/14 has been found to reduce circNDUVB2 m(6)A and increase it expression, thereby destroying the interaction of circNDUFB2/IGF2BPs and blocking the ubiquitination and degradation of IGF2BPs. In addition, circNDUFB2 overexpression can also stimulate LC cells to secrete CXCL10, CXCL11, CCL5, and IFNβ by activating retinoic acid inducible gene-I (RIG-I). This leads to the recruitment of CD8( +) T cells and dendritic cells and activation of the anti-tumor immune response, ultimately inhibiting LC growth [ ]. In vitro, HNRNPC knockdown attenuated LC cell proliferation, colony formation, invasion, and migration. In vivo, HNRNPC knockdown inhibited LC growth and metastasis. This result may be related to the high level of CD8( +) and CD4( +) T cell infiltration induced by HNRNPC knockdown [ ].
The density of four tumor infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) subpopulations (CD8( +), FOXP3( +), PD-1( +), and CD45RO( +)) was significantly increased in the cancer tissue stroma of LC patients with high YTHDF1 and YTHDF2 expression. Silencing of the YTHDF1 and YTHDF2 genes in LC cells cultured in vitro significantly up-regulated the expression of PD-L1, thus inducing immune suppression. Therefore, inhibition of YTHDF1 and YTHDF2 expression may be used as a potential target to change the tumor immune microenvironment in LC and improve immunotherapy response [ ]. IGF2BP2 is specifically highly expressed in a subpopulation of LUAD cells (called LUAD_IGF2BP2) and a subpopulation of endothelial cells (called En_IGF2BP2). IGF2BP2-positive exosomes secreted by the LUAD_IGF2BP2 subgroup were taken up by the En_IGF2BP2-subgroup in the TME, thereby increasing IGF2BP2 in target cells. This led to recognition of m(6)A-labeled fms-like tyrosine kinase 4 (FLT4) mRNA and promotion of its stability, activating the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway and ultimately promoting angiogenesis and metastasis [ ]. This result suggests that different subpopulations of LC cells may use the TME to transmit RNA m(6)A information to promote their own progression. High expression and secretion of IL-6 is generally found in tumor tissues or adjacent tissues. IL-6 promotes the abnormal proliferation of normal tissue cells and peripheral blood vessel formation by changing the TME, leading to the occurrence and metastasis of malignant tumors [ ]. LUAD most commonly metastasizes to the liver (38% to 44%) [ ]. In vitro, IL-6 can activate METTL3 transcriptional activation and expression in hepatocytes, enhance the m(6)A levels of total intracellular RNA, and elevate the m(6)A level and translation of YAP1 mRNA. This activates the YAP1/TEAD signaling pathway and ultimately creates an inflammatory microenvironment, which promotes LUAD cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT. Peptide 17-mediated blockade of the YAP1 signaling pathway effectively inhibited the formation of an IL-6-driven inflammatory microenvironment and impaired the proliferation, migration, invasion, EMT, and metastasis of LC cells. Therefore, the inflammatory microenvironment caused by IL-6 regulated the malignant manifestations of LC cells and assisted in LC metastasis to the liver. This suggests a potential new strategy for the early diagnosis or treatment of LC [ ].

## Current clinical application status of targeting RNA m(6)A in LC
In general, study on the clinical diagnostic value of RNA m(6)A in various cancers including LC tends to be based on bioinformatics analysis of network medical database mining or/and clinical specimen analysis, or/and basic experimental verification [ ]. Some studies have also demonstrated that RNA m(6)A analysis can be used to judge therapeutic efficacy [ ] and predict prognosis [ ]. However, as yet, there has been no relevant clinical trial study. Current investigation on the application of targeting m(6)A in the treatment of LC mainly focuses on developing various specific inhibitors or antagonists of m(6)A regulatory factors, but they usually remain at the preclinical trial level. The reasons for the current dilemma are largely based on four aspects. First, the regulatory mechanism for RNA m(6)A is unclear. In recent years, new modulators or mechanisms have been continuously discovered [ ]. Therefore, considering the complexity of the m(6)A regulatory network, new multi-target inhibitors with high specificity are urgently needed. Second, it has shown that m(6)A writers, readers and erasers not only play key roles in RNA m(6)A, but also exert other biological effects independent of their m(6)A activity [ ]. As such, evaluating their anti-cancer effects solely by blocking their activities to govern RNA m(6)A may be insufficient. Third, although some inhibitors or antagonists have exhibited considerable anti-cancer activity, they have been gradually found to act on other key regulators of physiological processes, implying that there may be a variety of side effects. Fourth, their drugability (e.g. bioavailability, pharmacodynamics, and pharmacokinetics) remains unclear and requires more in-depth and comprehensive evaluation. Nevertheless, changes in the expression or activity of the three types of RNA m(6)A regulators themselves can indeed impact the malignant characteristics of LC cells and the growth of xenografts in vivo. Given the dispersion and relative independence of the results obtained by various research centers or groups, we suggest that a comprehensive assessment of the global RNA m(6)A signaling pathways within LC cells is needed. On this basis, a network landscape of tumor-specific RNA m(6)A regulatory factors is established, illustrating the key nodes and dissecting sequential medication standards for intervening in RNA m(6)A and ultimately leading to identification of the key signal pathway regulatory hubs.
Notably, it appears contradictory that m(6)A writers (e.g. METTL3) and erasers (e.g. FTO and ALKBH5) are overexpressed in LC cells; overexpression of both can promote cell proliferation and invasion and metastasis in vitro or in vivo. One possible explanation is that the writers accelerate cancer progression by increasing RNA m(6)A, while erasers facilitate cancer development by decreasing RNA m(6)A. Thus, these two types of m(6)A regulators may promote LC progression through different signaling pathways. This further validated from another perspective that the occurrence and progression of LC mediated by abnormal RNA m(6)A is highly complex. Therefore, the use of eraser or writer inhibitors alone may not achieve the desired anti-tumor effect. As such, multi-target biotherapy based on m(6)A will be one of the most promising strategies for effectively controlling malignant tumors, including LC.

## Conclusions and perspectives
In this review, we discuss in depth the close relationship between abnormalities in m(6)A-mediated RNA modification and the occurrence and progression of LC. We also reveal that a molecular mechanism network, in which abnormal RNA m(6)A and the transcript products of related genes are involved in regulating LC cell proliferation, migration, invasion, metastasis, and apoptosis resistance (Fig.  ). We hypothesize that the key nodes in the network composed of these signaling pathways are potential intervention and treatment targets in LC. On the one hand, increased global m(6)A levels or overexpression of related regulatory factors (including writers, readers, and erasers) in LC cells can activate cancer-promoting signaling pathways such as c-Myc, Wnt/β-catenin, and PI3K/mTOR/AKT. This can promote cell cycle progression, angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, or immunosuppression. On the other hand, some m(6)A regulatory factors such as YTHDC2 or ALKBH5, can promote LC cell occurrence, proliferation, migration, EMT, and drug resistance when insufficiently expressed in LC tissues. Exogenous introduction or endogenous activation of their expression has been shown to have a tumor suppressor effect. In addition, studies continue to reveal that abnormal function or expression of protein factors including writers [ ] and readers [ ] may lead to cancer promotion in an m(6)A-independent manner. It is worth noting that the establishment and remodeling of the TME on which tumor cells rely for survival is also inseparable from the imbalance of the m(6)A regulatory network in cancer cells and surrounding tumor stromal cells or infiltrating immune cells.
 A large number of studies have confirmed that various cells types including LC cells, CAFs, TAMs, and even normal somatic cells can use RNA m(6)A as an information and material exchange medium with LC cells. In this way, they regulate tumor matrix composition and remodeling through various cytokines and chemokines, recruit cells from surrounding or distant tissues and organs, and ultimately establish a TME that is conducive to the survival and development of tumor cells. Blocking the m(6)A-mediated connection between these cells is a potential promising strategy for the treatment of LC. Nevertheless, more research into to the role of the communication network established by m(6)A between LC cells and other non-cancerous cells such as dendritic cells and natural killer lymphocytes, adipocytes, or nerve cells in LC is required. Although a large number of inhibitors and agonists targeting m(6)A regulatory factors have emerged [ , ], these remain in the preclinical stage (Table  ). This may be due to their off-target effects, toxic side effects, and unclear bioavailability and metabolic mechanisms. We believe that with the gradual clarification of the molecular mechanisms and physiological functions of m(6)A and a clearer outline of the m(6)A regulatory network combined with the cross-application of computer drug design and artificial intelligence, targeting m(6)A may become a powerful tool for treating LC.
