## Introduction
 FAT4 (FAT Atypical Cadherin 4), an atypical cadherin family member, is the human ortholog of the Drosophila Fat gene [ ].It was first discovered for its function in regulating planar cell polarity (PCP) during Drosophila wing morphogenesis [ , ].The protein exhibits calcium-dependent adhesive properties and is essential for embryonic development, tissue homeostasis maintenance, and multisystem diseases. FAT4 modulates cell proliferation, polarity establishment, immune microenvironment, and tissue morphogenesis by activating the Hippo signaling pathway, inhibiting yes-associated protein (YAP)/transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ) nuclear translocation, and regulating pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin [ – ]. FAT4 is extensively expressed in renal tissue, epithelial cells, neurons, cardiomyocytes, and the lymphatic system [ ]. Abnormal FAT4 expression (promoter hypermethylation, mutation, or deletion) is significantly correlated with developmental anomalies (including Van Maldergem syndrome and Hennekam syndrome) and numerous malignancies. FAT4 is identified as a crucial tumor suppressor gene in tumors. Its inactivation accelerates tumor progression by disrupting cell adhesion, facilitating epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and activating pro-oncogenic signaling pathways (Hippo/YAP, Wnt/β-catenin) [ , ]. Clinical translational studies have demonstrated FAT4’s potential as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target. In malignancies such as lung, gastric, and colorectal cancers, FAT4 promoter methylation (at sites cg25879360, cg26389756) or mutation correlates with high tumor mutation burden (TMB), poor prognosis, and chemotherapy resistance [ , ]. Targeting FAT4 can enhance sensitivity to conventional chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil [5-FU]), and its activator (jujuboside A) inhibits metastasis in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) by modulating the FAT4-Hippo-YAP axis [ ]. Metformin ameliorates neuronal migration defects induced by FAT4 mutation through autophagy activation, offering an intervention strategy for genetic brain malformations [ ].Nonetheless, fundamental challenges persist, including the complexity and variability of its mechanisms. The molecular network through which FAT4 modulates cell behavior via various pathways (PCP, Hippo, and Wnt) has not been systematically integrated, especially regarding its roles in organ development (kidney branching morphogenesis) and immune microenvironment regulation. FAT4 is a key regulatory molecule across various fields. Enhancing fundamental and clinical research on FAT4 will improve the precise diagnosis and treatment of developmental diseases and tumors, offering theoretical support for novel targeted strategies.

## Biological characteristics of FAT4

## Molecular structure
FAT4 is a member of the human Fat family. The cytoplasmic domain of FAT4 demonstrates higher conservation compared to other members [ ].The FAT4 gene is located on human chromosome 4q28.1, comprises 17 known exons, and has a transcript length of approximately 16 kb. The FAT4 gene encodes a substantial protein comprising 4924 amino acids, predominantly expressed in epithelial and mesenchymal tissues [ ].The molecular structure of the FAT4 protein comprises 34 cadherin repeats, 2 laminin G domains (LamG), and 3 epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like domains [ , ].

## Functional mechanisms
Recent studies have demonstrated that FAT4 participates in maintaining PCP and inhibiting tumor cell proliferation. It can suppress tumor growth by activating the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway [ ] and inhibiting the Wnt signaling pathway. FAT4 exerts its tumor-suppressive effect on various systemic cancers through multiple pathways and mechanisms [ ], as illustrated in Fig. . FAT4 exhibits calcium-dependent adhesive properties, binding ligands such as Dachsous (Dchs) to regulate intercellular adhesion and PCP, thus maintaining tissue morphogenesis and development [ ]. The primary physiological function of FAT4 is to preserve cellular homeostasis and tissue architecture; its abnormal expression is significantly associated with developmental disorders and diseases (Fig. ).

 

## Mechanisms in tissue development
FAT4, a member of the cadherin family, modulates the development of the kidneys, craniofacial structures, brain, bones, heart, lymphatic system, and vertebrae through various pathways (Table  ).

## Kidney and ureter development
FAT4 performs various regulatory roles in mammalian kidney development. In a mouse model with Fat4 deficiency, FAT4 negatively regulates the RET-GFRA1-GDNF signaling pathway by directly binding to the RET receptor tyrosine kinase (utilizing its cadherin repeat domain), impacting ureteric bud (UB) branching morphogenesis.Its deficiency may affect the development of kidney and urinary tract abnormalities [ , ]. Moreover, the absence of FAT4 or its ligand Dachsous 1(DCHS1) causes proliferation of the mesenchymal nephron progenitor reservoir, known as the condensed mesenchyme (CM). The FAT4 protein non-autonomously binds the ligands DCHS1/2 within the CM, thereby restricting the self-renewal of nephron progenitor cells (NPCs) and precisely regulating the progenitor pool size. This process may entail YAP dysregulation or reliance on Six2 [ ].FAT4 integrates UB-derived Wnt9b signaling with stromal signals (such as those regulated by Sall1) to synchronize the renewal-differentiation balance of NPCs [ , ]. Moreover, FAT4, together with Dchs1 signaling, modulates Brg1/SWI/SNF complex activity, preserving renal mesenchymal homeostasis [ ]. FAT4 deficiency hinders directed cell division, tubule elongation, and cilia orientation by disrupting the PCP pathway and non-canonical Wnt signaling, resulting in cystic kidney disease [ , ]. Therefore, FAT4 is a key regulatory molecule coordinating UB branching, progenitor fate determination, nephron morphogenesis, and cystic lesion prevention.

## Craniofacial development
The FAT4 mutation is associated with Van Maldergem syndrome, which is characterized by severe craniofacial malformations, abnormal neuronal migration, and skeletal dysplasia.During the differentiation of osteoblasts in mice, FAT4 and DCHS1 mediate the neuronal guidance system through the planar cell polarity (PCP) pathway, and their functional disruption affects neuronal migration path errors and craniofacial skeletal patterning defects [ – ]. The Fat4 gene is specifically expressed in the medial nasal prominence mesenchyme. In the pathogenesis of orofacial clefts (OFC), indicating its potential role in modulating the development of midline upper lip structures and the left/right laterality of clefts. However, genetic association analyses do not fully support the laterality patterns observed epidemiologically [ ]. Moreover, FAT4 gene variants have been identified in non-syndromic OFC cases; however, the synergistic mechanism with CASP9 in pathogenesis requires clarification [ ].Molecular pathway studies demonstrated that the transcription factor Six1 enhances FAT4 expression by directly binding to the Fat4 promoter, co-regulating genes such as Bmp4, Fgf18, and Fgfr2, which collectively contribute to mouse embryonic mandible formation, offering a basis for deciphering the genetic network of OFC [ ].

## Brain development
In the development of the mouse brain, FAT4 and DCHS1 collaboratively regulate brain development by modulating neural progenitor proliferation, tube closure, and apical constriction [ ]. Their deficiency results in periventricular neuronal heterotopia (PH) [ – ]. Defects in both mechanisms alter progenitor morphology and disrupt neuronal migration [ , ].The Fat4-Dchs1 complex is enriched at membrane juxtaposition sites of apical adherens junctions in cortical progenitors; its absence disrupts this structure [ ] and may lead to pituitary developmental anomalies [ ].

## Bone development and metabolism
FAT4, as a Hippo pathway regulator, directly affects mesenchymal stem cell osteogenic differentiation and bone remodeling by regulating YAP/TAZ nucleocytoplasmic shuttling [ , ].In mouse sternum morphogenesis, FAT4 and its ligand DCHS1 form a PCP signaling module that precisely regulates the spatial arrangement of pre-chondrogenic mesenchyme by coordinating cell polarity, directional migration, and mechanical signal transduction [ ]. FAT4 deficiency specifically inhibits osteogenic differentiation in alveolar bone development, dependent on the transcription factor SOX6 [ ]; simultaneously, it enhances the osteogenic potential of odontogenic stem cells by regulating IL-6 expression, underscoring its potential in bone regeneration therapy [ ].

## Heart development
FAT4 regulates heart development in mice through the DCHS1-FAT4 signaling pathway.This pathway functions as a “molecular tether” for intercellular adhesion, serving a dual role during a crucial postnatal developmental period: suppressing cardiomyocyte proliferation and regulating cellular hypertrophy. The molecular mechanism involves the activation of the Hippo pathway, which inhibits the transcriptional activity of the effector Yap1. The absence of Fat4 results in uncontrolled Yap1, which induces abnormal cardiomyocyte proliferation and hypertrophy, leading to pathological cardiac remodeling [ , ].

## Lymphatic system development
FAT4 is essential in human lymphatic system development, especially during lymphatic valve morphogenesis.FAT4 mutation, as a direct downstream target gene of the transcription factor GATA2 [ ], impairs valve development, causing lymphatic reflux obstruction, which is the core pathological mechanism of Hennekam syndrome [ ]. Mutations in FAT4 or CCBE1 can independently or synergistically cause this syndrome [ – ].Furthermore, the FAT4 mutation is associated with Van Maldergem syndrome, indicating its allelic disease nature [ ].

## Vertebral development
FAT4 and DCHS1 constitute a receptor-ligand pair that regulates cell behavior during mouse vertebral development by regulating PCP and inhibiting the Hippo pathway effector YAP/TAZ activity, thus regulating cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation. Additionally, it exerts direct regulatory functions during mouse vertebral formation, independent of the YAP/TAZ pathway [ ].

 

## Tumor suppressive mechanisms of FAT4 in neoplastic diseases
FAT4 is a conserved core tumor suppressor present in various cancers, inhibiting tumorigenesis in over 30 tumor types (Table  ; Fig.  ).

## Gynecological malignancies
Gynecological malignancies (cervical, ovarian, and endometrial cancers) pose a significant global health burden for women due to high incidence and mortality rates [ ].The expression or mutation of the FAT4 gene in these tumors affects tumor cell suppression, proliferation, and invasion.

## Cervical cancer (CC)
CC is a malignancy that originates from the cervix. As one of the most common reproductive tract malignancies in women worldwide, its onset is primarily associated with chronic infection by high-risk human papillomavirus [ , ]. In cervical cancer, FAT4 activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway (in a β-catenin-dependent manner), inhibits N-glycosylation modification of PD-L1, obstructs its membrane localization, and facilitates degradation. This weakens tumor immune escape capability and enhances cytotoxic T lymphocyte anti-tumor activity, thus modulating the tumor immune microenvironment [ ]. This finding reveals a novel function for the FAT4/Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in modulating the cervical cancer immune microenvironment, offering a certain basis for immunotherapy strategies for CC.

## Ovarian cancer (OC)
OC has the highest mortality rate among female reproductive system malignancies, with epithelial carcinoma representing the most common subtype (accounting for 80%). The FAT4 gene mutation is common in ovarian cancer, representing one of the most frequent genetic alterations in primary ovarian tumors and occurring with high frequency in colorectal cancer tissues that have metastasized to the ovary [ ].FAT4 functions as a potential tumor suppressor in ovarian cancer. The inactivation (mutation or downregulation) relieves inhibition on Hippo pathway effectors YAP/TAZ and synergistically activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, collectively promoting tumor cell proliferation and invasion, and metastasis [ ].

## Endometrial cancer (EC)
EC is a common gynecological malignancy that arises from the endometrial epithelium, with endometrioid adenocarcinoma constituting the predominant histological type (> 80%). Based on extensive next-generation sequencing (NGS) analysis of tumor samples from patients with EC, FAT4 was identified as one of the genes exhibiting the highest prevalence of somatic mutation [ ].At the molecular level, FAT4 functions as a tumor suppressor in endometrial cancer. Its functional inactivation (due to USP51-mediated deubiquitination deficiency) alleviates the inhibition of the core Hippo pathway effector YAP/TAZ, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [ ].

## Lung cancer (LC)
LC, the malignancy with the highest global mortality rate, is histologically dominated by NSCLC (85%). FAT4 is a core tumor suppressor gene in LC, demonstrating anti-cancer effects through dual mechanisms: Directly inhibiting EMT, cellular proliferation, and metastasis and modulating the immune microenvironment by regulating ROS/NF-κB and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathways [ ]. Genetic evidence demonstrates that FAT4 is frequently mutated in smoking-related LC [ ]; animal models verify that Fat4 deletion accelerates lung adenoma formation [ ]; in SCLC, its mutation positively correlates with high TMB [ ]. Whole-genome analysis identifies FAT4 as a core driver gene in NSCLC, orchestrating tumor growth, metastasis, and immunosuppressive microenvironments [ ]. Epigenetically, 39% of lung adenocarcinomas demonstrate FAT4 promoter hypermethylation (CpG sites cg25879360 and cg26389756), resulting in gene silencing [ , ].These diverse mechanisms implicate FAT4 in the initiation, progression, and therapeutic intervention of lung cancer.

## Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)
HCC constitutes 90% of primary liver cancers, predominantly arising from chronic liver disease progression. FAT4 is often mutated and silenced in HCC; its functional deficiency directly promotes malignant phenotypes. Specific mechanisms: (1) In hepatitis B virus-associated HCC, FAT4 is synergistically downregulated with TP53 [ ]; knockout experiments confirm it promotes cancer cell proliferation and invasion; (2) FAT4 loss promotes tumorigenesis through a tertiary cascade (disrupting cell adhesion → inducing EMT → remodeling microenvironment), with the primary mechanism involving activation of the PAK6/WNT/β-catenin pathway [ ];3. miRNA-93-5p serves as an upstream inhibitor of FAT4; targeting this axis can activate antitumor immunity; patients with high FAT4 expression exhibit improved survival and enhanced immune infiltration [ ]. FAT4 deficiency accelerates HCC progression through the PAK6/WNT/β-catenin pathway, providing novel targets for personalized therapy (early gene repair/late pathway blockade or immune modulation).

## Gastric cancer (GC)
GC is a major cause of cancer-related deaths globally, with adenocarcinoma accounting for 0% [ ].FAT4 is a commonly mutated oncogenic gene in GC [ – ]. The mutation correlates with high TMB and can serve as an independent favorable prognostic marker and predictor of immunotherapy efficacy [ , ]. FAT4 exerts its tumor-suppressive function by maintaining cell adhesion [ ]. Its function is frequently suppressed due to epigenetic silencing resulting from promoter hypermethylation [ – ], a feature associated with GC risk in tissue and peripheral blood [ ].FAT4 deletion/silencing primarily drives malignant phenotypes through two pathways: (a) Promoting YAP nuclear translocation, thereby accelerating cell cycle progression and enhancing invasion [ ]; (b) activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which mediates EMT and unregulated proliferation [ , ]. Additionally, it serves as a crucial component of the Wnt/PI3K-Akt pathway [ ]. FAT4 expression is downregulated by miR-93-5p (which inactivates the Hippo pathway by targeting and suppressing mRNA [ ]) and by UBE4B (which inhibits autophagy through ubiquitin-mediated degradation [ ]).The inactivation of FAT4 and its regulatory network represents a potentially significant factor in GC progression, suggesting its possible utility as a therapeutic target and biomarker [ ].

## Colorectal cancer (CRC)
CRC, the third most common cancer and second leading cause of cancer-related death globally, is a major public health burden. Gene analysis utilizing multi-source samples (primary lesions, peritoneal metastases, cfDNA) and the TCGA database indicates that FAT4 is a commonly mutated gene in CRC [ – ]. FAT4 expression is markedly diminished in CRC tissues compared to non-cancerous tissues and suppresses tumor cell invasion, migration, and proliferation by inducing autophagy [ ]. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that FAT4’s tumor-suppressive function is primarily achieved through dual pathways:
Autophagy-EMT Regulatory Axis: AT4 activates autophagy and partially inhibits EMT by suppressing P13K activity through PI3K/AKT/mTOR and PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β signaling pathways [ ].
EMT-dependent Metastasis Suppression: FAT4 inhibits EMT-driven metastasis by disrupting the interaction between Wnt/β-catenin and Hippo/YAP1 signaling pathways [ ].
Additionally, FAT4 is negatively regulated by miR-106b-5p. This miRNA enhances CRC cell migration and invasion capabilities by targeting and suppressing FAT4 [ , ].
FAT4 forms a multi-pathway tumor-suppressive network by synergistically modulating autophagy and EMT inhibition. Its expression loss activates the PI3K-Wnt-Hippo signaling hub, facilitating CRC malignant progression, exacerbated by the negative regulation of miR-106b-5p. These findings establish FAT4’s significance as a prognostic biomarker and provide a theoretical framework for formulating combination therapeutic strategies targeting PI3K/Wnt/Hippo pathways.

## Urinary tract malignancies
Urinary system malignancies include various subtypes. FAT4 inactivation, through Hippo-YAP1 activation, cytoskeleton-calcium signaling disruption, and mutation enrichment, functions as a core tumor suppressor gene that facilitates the onset, progression, and drug resistance in Wilms tumor, urothelial carcinoma, prostate cancer, and testicular embryonal carcinoma.

## Renal malignancies
Wilms tumor (WT) is a prevalent embryonic tumor in children, closely associated with abnormal metanephric blastema development. FAT4 deficiency models indicate that Hippo pathway inactivation results in YAP1 dephosphorylation and nuclear translocation, thereby activating proliferation genes such as TGF/CYR61, which drives abnormal expansion of NPCs (mimicking human WT precursor lesions). FAT4 deficiency and p-YAP1 accumulation may function as novel biomarkers for aggressive WT, indicating therapeutic potential in targeting the YAP1-TEAD (TEA domain family members) complex [ ].

## Urinary tract (upper + bladder + urethra) malignancies
Urothelial carcinoma (UC) is a urinary system malignancy with high recurrence rates in China. Preliminary studies that utilized whole-exome sequencing of a Chinese cohort of 112 patients with UC identified FAT4 as one of the most commonly mutated genes [ ].This finding was validated in paired urine-tissue samples from high-grade UC (HGUC) [ ]. Deeper analysis of TCGA multi-omics data further revealed that the high TMB subtype demonstrates significant mutation enrichment in key tumor suppressor genes (TP53 and PIK3CA) and cell adhesion-related genes such as FAT4. These traits indicate that patients in this subtype may benefit from immunotherapy [ ].Notably, co-mutation of FAT4 and the microtubule-binding protein MACF1 was confirmed to be significantly correlated with high-risk UC. Its mechanism likely involves synergistic disruption of cytoskeletal stability, facilitating tumor metastasis [ ], thereby offering a novel perspective for understanding UC invasion and metastasis.

## Prostate cancer (PC)
PC is a prevalent genitourinary malignancy in men, characterized by age-related, racial, and genetic susceptibility. Integrated genomic and co-expression network analysis revealed that copy number variation-differential expression genes associated with FAT4 downregulation occupy central hub positions in regulatory networks and are specifically enriched in calmodulin signaling pathways [ ]. FAT4 deficiency may impair intercellular adhesion, thereby facilitating PC progression by activating pro-migration signals within the calcium signaling pathway. This finding provides a theoretical basis for intervention strategies targeting the calcium signaling pathway.

## Testicular germ cell tumors (TGCT)
TGCT are prevalent tumors in young men, with the embryonal carcinoma subtype exhibiting significant aggressiveness. By integrating TCGA multi-omics data (gene expression, alternative splicing, and mutation profiles), researchers found that FAT4 mutations are specifically enriched only in the embryonal carcinoma subtype [ ].This finding offers a molecular basis for the clinical diagnosis and precise treatment of patients with TGCT.

## Esophageal cancer
Esophageal cancer is primarily categorized into esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC, 90%), esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), and rare types. Its occurrence is associated with multiple factors, including smoking and alcohol consumption. A previous study demonstrated that FAT4 mutation can simultaneously disrupt Hippo and Notch signaling pathways and impair histone modifier gene function, collectively promoting ESCC development [ ].Targeted sequencing of patients with EAC revealed a 100% FAT4 mutation rate (n = 16) [ ]. Genetic susceptibility analysis confirmed that the FAT4 gene non-synonymous variants rs1014867 (p.Pro4972Ser) and rs1039808 (p.Ala807Val) significantly increase esophageal cancer risk (odds ratio [OR] = 2.3) [ ].

## Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA)
CCA is a highly aggressive malignancy that originates from the bile ducts, categorized anatomically into intrahepatic (iCCA), perihilar (pCCA), and distal (dCCA) types, with pCCA and dCCA collectively termed extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (exCCA). Different CCA subtypes exhibit prognosis-associated specific mutation profiles: in exCCA, pCCA demonstrates significantly higher mutation frequencies in PIK3CA, FAT4, KDM6A, MDM2, and TCF7L2 compared to dCCA, whereas TP53 and KRAS mutation frequencies are significantly lower in pCCA than dCCA. This molecular heterogeneity offers a key theoretical basis for precise subtyping and targeted therapy in patients with CCA [ ].

## Gastrointestinal neuroendocrine carcinoma (GI-NEC)
GI-NEC is a highly aggressive tumor derived from gastrointestinal neuroendocrine cells, belonging to the poorly differentiated, high-proliferative subclass of neuroendocrine neoplasms, characterized by high proliferative activity, early metastasis, and poor prognosis. Rectal neuroendocrine carcinoma (Rectal NEC) is the most prevalent but most prognostically heterogeneous subtype within GI-NEC. FAT4 mutation is a key driver in GI-NEC. In the rectal subtype (highest incidence), FAT4 mutation rates are extremely high [ ], facilitating malignant progression through JAK-STAT pathway activation. Whole-genome analysis (n = 143) demonstrated that FAT4 follows an evolutionary path of “whole-genome doubling (WGD) → clonal mutation → subclonal mutation“ [ ], resulting in significant genomic heterogeneity across various anatomical sites of GI-NEC. The FAT4 mutation and JAK-STAT pathway activation define the molecular features of rectal GI-NEC, whereas WGD-driven clonal evolution represents a common mechanism across GI-NEC sites.

## Breast cancer (BC)
BC is the most prevalent malignancy in women. Molecular mechanism studies confirm that the atypical cadherin FAT4 is a core tumor suppressor factor in breast cancer [ , ].Functional studies demonstrated that shRNA-mediated FAT4 gene silencing markedly promotes triple-negative breast cancer progression, and its expression loss is directly correlated with promoter hypermethylation [ ]. Multicenter evidence further substantiates FAT4’s tumor-suppressive function in breast cancer [ , ].The regulatory mechanisms are as follows: (1) The Src oncoprotein suppresses FAT4 mRNA expression by inducing actin depolymerization; FAT4 knockdown enhances BC progression by activating Hippo effector YAP/TAZ [ ]; (2) long noncoding RNA ZFHX4-AS1 activates the Hippo pathway by targeting and inhibiting FAT4; its silencing can alleviate transcriptional repression and induce apoptosis [ ]. Breast cancer cell (BCC) genomic analysis identified FAT4, along with TMEM98 and GPR64, as a novel membrane protein target [ ].FAT4 expression in BC tissue is 3.5-fold lower than that in normal tissue (P < 0.001), and its methylation level positively correlates with clinical stage (OR = 4.2). Cross-hierarchical evidence collectively confirms its translational potential as a therapeutic target. Paired sample analysis demonstrated that tsRNA-26,576 is significantly expressed in breast cancer tissue (upregulated 3.5-fold, P < 0.001), facilitating oncogenic effects by enhancing cancer cell proliferation and migration and inhibiting apoptosis. Functional intervention revealed that tsRNA-26,576 inhibitor effectively upregulates tumor suppressors FAT4 and SPEN [ ], collectively establishing its dual significance as a therapeutic target and predictive marker.

## Melanoma
Melanoma is a malignancy arising from melanocytes, characterized by its aggressiveness. It occurs in the skin, mucosa, uvea, and internal organs. FAT4 acts as a cross-species conserved tumor suppressor in melanoma, regulated by dual mechanisms: In ocular subtypes, diversified histone deacetylation inhibitors enhance m6A modification by acetylating methyltransferase-like 14, thereby promoting FAT4 expression in an m6 A-YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA-binding protein 1-dependent manner, establishing an epigenetic axis [ ]; in conjunctival and mucosal subtypes, frequent inactivation mutations and downregulation are significantly associated with poor prognosis [ , ].Human and canine mucosal melanoma studies further confirm the high mutation frequency of this gene [ , ].FAT4’s dual inactivation mechanisms collectively drive malignant progression.

## Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC)
OSCC is the most common oral malignancy. In OSCC, the FAT4 gene, as a core regulator of the PCP pathway, drives malignant progression by preserving cancer stem cell characteristics. Studies demonstrated that in 4-nitroquinoline 1-oxide (4-NQO)-induced mouse OSCC models, tumor stem cell clone populations (LSCC) harbor high-frequency FAT4 mutations, resulting in PCP pathway dysregulation. This impairs cell polarity regulation and enhances cancer stem cell self-renewal capacity [ ].This mechanism elucidates the critical role of FAT4 inactivation, through disruption of PCP pathway integrity, in the progression of OSCC (especially cancer stem cell maintenance).

## Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)
DLBCL is the most common aggressive subtype of adult non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), often termed the “number one killer of adult NHL.” In DLBCL, the FAT4 gene mutation exhibits a dual function: (1) Independent positive prognostic marker: Genomic analysis of 148 patients revealed that individuals with FAT4 mutations experienced significantly extended progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) [ ]; (2) FAT4 mutation frequency, a relapse-associated driver factor, was significantly higher in the relapse group than in the non-relapse group, indicating its potential as a relapse screening marker and therapeutic target for relapse [ ].This paradoxical phenomenon suggests that FAT4 function may be disease-stage dependent: Mutations at diagnosis could preserve genomic stability, whereas during relapse, pathway inactivation may facilitate treatment resistance.

## Primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL)
PCNSL is an aggressive NHL that originates in immune-privileged sites such as the brain, meninges, spinal cord, or eyes. The predominant majority exhibit DLBCL morphology, making it a specific anatomical subtype of DLBCL rather than a distinct histological type. In PCNSL, NGS analysis of 31 samples revealed that FAT4, a key gene regulating cell adhesion and cytoskeleton, frequently experiences mutation or dysregulation [ ].Targeting FAT4 or its pathway due to its role in mediating cell polarity and migration may surmount blood-brain barrier penetration challenges and remodel the tumor microenvironment, presenting new strategies for individualized therapy.

## T-cell lymphoma
Extranodal natural killer/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) and anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) are rare subtypes of aggressive T-cell lymphoma. ALCL can be categorized into ALK-positive and ALK-negative subtypes. In T-cell lymphoma, FAT4 gene abnormalities demonstrated subtype-specific mechanisms: ENKTL subtype: Targeted sequencing identified FAT4 as a significant recurrent somatic mutation target al.ongside the JAK-STAT pathway, indicating its cooperative involvement in pathogenesis [ ]; ALCL subtype: FAT4 and RUNX1T1 high-frequency mutations synergistically promote malignant phenotype transformation and treatment resistance. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that FAT4 inactivation facilitates tumor proliferation, invasion, and enhances chemotherapy resistance by activating β-catenin signaling and Hippo pathway effector YAP1 nuclear translocation [ ].The relatively high mutation frequency and pathway regulatory role of this gene in these two aggressive subtypes suggest its potential relevance as a candidate therapeutic target in T-cell lymphoma, though further functional validation and clinical correlation studies are warranted to establish its broader applicability.

## Burkitt lymphoma (BL)
BL, termed the “fastest-growing human tumor” because of its high proliferative rate. Whole-genome sequencing of familial cases revealed specific germline variants in the FAT4/DCHS2 genes. The proteins encoded by both genes are involved in regulating the Hippo signaling pathway, which is crucial in controlling tissue growth, maintaining stem cell activity, and organ size homeostasis [ ].

## Pediatric-type follicular lymphoma (PTFL)
PTFL is an uncommon subtype of FL, markedly distinct from the adult type in clinical, pathological, and molecular features. NGS analysis of 4 PTFL patients found FAT4 gene mutations in all cases [ ], indicating its role as a molecular marker or driver for PTFL; however, its precise biological function and downstream pathways remain unclear.Clarifying its mechanism may contribute to a better understanding of pediatric-specific lymphoma pathogenesis and could potentially inform future targeted therapy development.

## Splenic marginal zone lymphoma (SMZL)
SMZL is a slow-growing small B-cell NHL predominantly involving the spleen. In SMZL research, NGS analysis targeting pediatric-type follicular lymphoma (PTFL) identified FAT4 mutations present in all four samples studied [ ], suggesting its potential role as a molecular signature or driver factor in PTFL. However, the specific biological functions and downstream pathways require clarification.

## Ewing sarcoma
Ewing sarcoma is a malignant bone and soft tissue tumor prevalent in children/adolescents. Its hallmark molecular feature is the EWS-FLI1 fusion gene (85%–90% of cases), which is the primary oncogenic driver. In Ewing sarcoma, FAT4 is a transcriptional target of the oncogenic driver EWS-FLI1, modulating the expression of the secreted peptide C4orf48/NICOL through Hippo signaling activation. C4orf48 serves as a cofactor for NELL2, connecting the FAT4-Hippo and NELL2 signaling pathways, thus representing a candidate dependency that may be involved in tumor survival maintenance, pending further functional and clinical validation [ ].

## Angiosarcoma
Angiosarcoma is a rare aggressive malignancy that originates from vascular/lymphatic endothelium, commonly found in skin, soft tissue, liver, breast, and bone. In angiosarcoma, FAT4 is predominantly expressed in tumor endothelial cells. This high expression state significantly correlates with shortened patient OS and can serve as a poor prognostic marker [ ]. Targeting FAT4 or its signaling pathway is a potential therapeutic strategy, although specific mechanisms necessitate comprehensive investigation.

## Sporadic medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
Sporadic MTC is a relatively uncommon neuroendocrine malignancy that originates from thyroid parafollicular C-cells. In sporadic MTC, FAT4 was identified as a somatic high-frequency mutation gene (alongside FAT1). Knockdown experiments demonstrated that its downregulation significantly enhances cancer cell proliferation ability [ ].This functional gain-of-function phenotype suggests that FAT4 acts as a tumor suppressor, and its inactivation promotes tumorigenesis by alleviating proliferation inhibition.

## Leukemia
Leukemia is a group of highly heterogeneous, aggressive hematologic malignancies characterized by an overall poor prognosis. In leukemia, the FAT4 gene mutation exhibits cross-subtype conservation. Whole-exome sequencing in chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML) revealed that ≥ 10% of patients have FAT4 somatic mutations, signifying that it is a new high-frequency mutation gene [ ].Targeted sequencing in acute myeloid leukemia (AML) independently verified FAT4 mutation presence, validating its broad involvement in leukemogenesis [ ].The high mutation frequency of this gene in aggressive subtypes (CMML/AML) indicates its potential involvement in malignant progression by regulating cell proliferation and differentiation blockade, although specific signaling pathways remain unelucidated. The FAT4 mutation may function as a potential diagnostic marker and therapeutic target, offering a new direction for leukemia precision medicine.

## Kaposi sarcoma (KS)
KS is a malignancy closely associated with Kaposi sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV) infection. FAT4 functions as a tumor suppressor and is inhibited by KSHV by downregulation of miR-31. Reduced FAT4 expression enhances endothelial cell migration ability, synergizing with ETS1/2 release to facilitate malignant endothelial cell dissemination and stimulate tumor lesion development. This may provide a novel therapeutic strategy for Kaposi sarcoma [ ].

## Soft tissue sarcoma (STS)
STS is a cancer that originates from mesenchymal tissues and can occur in any soft tissue site in the body. In soft tissue sarcoma, FAT4, a key Hippo signaling pathway gene, was identified as a high-frequency mutation gene in a 3-methylcholanthrene (MCA)-induced mouse model [ ].The inactivation of this pathway facilitates sarcoma development by impairing the Hippo pathway’s CRE function in regulating cell proliferation and tumor suppression, offering a new direction for targeted therapy.

## Multiple myeloma (MM)
MM is an incurable hematological malignant tumor characterized by clonal plasma cell abnormal proliferation, predominantly affecting older adult populations. In newly diagnosed Chinese patients with MM, the FAT4 gene was identified as a prevalent genetic aberration target: Fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis revealed a high rate of single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) occurrence, whereas PCR-Sanger sequencing validated the presence of somatic mutations [ ].The prevalence of this gene anomaly (SNP/mutation) in the Chinese MM population indicates its potential role in disease pathogenesis, establishing a foundation for mechanistic studies.

## Philadelphia-negative myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN)
Philadelphia-negative MPN specifically denotes myeloproliferative tumors that are Philadelphia chromosome-negative (lacking the BCR-ABL fusion gene). In Philadelphia-negative MPN, NGS identifies FAT4 gene somatic mutations in approximately 3%–8% of patients [ ]. Although not a high-frequency event, this mutation indicates a specific molecular subtype. Monitoring its status may facilitate prediction of disease progression (transformation to high-risk subtypes or fibrosis aggravation), establishing a basis for precise prognosis assessment and intervention.

## Adrenal-derived pheochromocytoma
In the rat adrenal pheochromocytoma cell line PC12 in vitro model, pituitary adenylate cyclase-activating polypeptide induces PC12 cell neurite outgrowth driven by CRMP2 dephosphorylation through Pac1 receptor activation of the cAMP/PI3K-Akt/Ca²⁺ pathway. FAT4, as an early response factor, integrates this signaling network to modulate neurite morphology. The expression level correlates with tumor differentiation state, offering a new mechanism for neuroendocrine signal transduction [ ].

## Neuroblastoma (NB)
NB is the most common extracranial solid malignancy in pediatric patients, with stage 4 patients exhibiting a markedly poor prognosis. Researchers systematically mapped mutation profiles in 11 stage 4 patients with NB using targeted NGS. FAT4 is a high-frequency mutation gene in stage 4 neuroblastoma. This gene offers a novel avenue for integrating genomics with clinicopathological data, potentially enhancing NB diagnostic classification models [ ].

## Pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP)
PMP is characterized by extensive peritoneal dissemination of mucinous tumors and jelly-like ascites. Surgical excision is difficult and susceptible to recurrence, often originating from low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasms (LAMN). Whole-exome sequencing of five patients with LAMN identified FGFR2, APC, SMAD2, and FAT4 as high-frequency mutations [ ].Future studies require organoid models to confirm the regulatory role of mutant genes on mucus secretion and investigate targeted therapeutic strategies.

## Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)
BCC is the most common skin malignancy. It originates from epidermal basal keratinocytes, with significantly higher incidence in Caucasians than in Asians. Research of BCC in Asian populations utilizing whole-exome sequencing identified FAT4 as a high-frequency mutation gene (involved in cell polarity regulation), thereby addressing a deficiency in non-Caucasian genomic data [ ].

## Pancreatic acinar cell carcinoma (ACC)
Pancreatic ACC accounts for 1%–2% of pancreatic tumors, exhibits high aggressiveness, and lacks effective treatments. Gene sequencing revealed that this uncommon, highly aggressive tumor often harbors BRCA2 and FAT4 mutations [ ], establishing a molecular basis for targeted therapy.

## Glioblastoma (GBM)
GBM, known as the “king of brain cancer,” is highly aggressive. A serum multi-omics analysis identified a biomarker panel composed of EGFR, FAT4, and BCOR (prediction accuracy 64%) [ ], thereby establishing a basis for targeted therapy. This model provides decision support for personalized targeted therapies (EGFR inhibitors).

## Malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM)
MPM is a rare aggressive malignancy that originates from pleural mesothelial cells, accounting for approximately 81% of all mesotheliomas. It is often diagnosed at an advanced stage and has a very poor prognosis. Researchers identified FAT4 as a high-frequency mutation gene through TCGA/GEO data analysis [ ]. The inactivation of this factor may impair cell polarity and facilitate tumor progression. Future studies require functional experiments to validate their mechanism and investigate targeted intervention strategies.

 

 

## Non-tumor diseases
FAT4 plays a significant role in tumor-related diseases and acts as a core regulator of cell polarity in non-neoplastic conditions. The functional impairment disrupts tissue homeostasis (ciliary assembly, angiogenesis, and ECM metabolism), thereby contributing to the pathogenesis of various non-tumor diseases (Table ).

## Steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS)
SRNS denotes nephrotic syndrome (NS) that does not respond to conventional steroid therapy and is the second most common cause of end-stage renal disease in pediatric patients. Whole-exome sequencing studies in patients with SRNS identified variants in five genes, including FAT4 (MYH9 and MAFB), Whole-exome sequencing studies in patients with SRNS have identified FAT4 as one of several candidate genes harboring potentially pathogenic variants, suggesting its possible role as a susceptibility locus or correlated disease marker [ ].

## Coronary artery disease (CAD)
Impaired angiogenesis in CAD arises from endothelial progenitor cell (EPC) dysfunction. Research identified elevated miR-31 expression in EPCs of patients with CAD, which appears to simultaneously suppress FAT4 (a regulator of cell polarity) and thromboxane A2 receptor (TPA2R), thereby inhibiting EPC activity. FAT4 may function as a regulatory hub within this pathway; its downregulation appears to relieve inhibition on miR-720, leading to decreased miR-720 levels and potentially enhancing the angiogenic capacity of EPCs. These findings suggest that the miR-31-FAT4-miR-720 axis may represent a candidate regulatory network associated with vascular repair impairment in CAD, with FAT4 serving as a potential mechanistic mediator within this pathway. However, whether FAT4 functions as a causal gene, a susceptibility locus, or merely a correlated marker of EPC dysfunction requires validation through genetic association studies in large cohorts and functional experiments manipulating FAT4 expression in vivo [ ].

## Microvascular complications
The pathological basis of diabetic microvascular complications (including retinopathy and nephropathy) is attributed to pericyte dysfunction. Research demonstrated that high glucose-induced METTL3 overexpression significantly suppresses the expression of PKCη, FAT4, and PDGFRA through YTHDF2-mediated transcript degradation, disrupting pericyte-endothelial cell interaction and causing microvascular leakage. The METTL3-YTHDF2-PKCη/FAT4/PDGFRA signaling axis serves as a key hub regulating pericyte function, offering a new direction for developing targeted epigenetic modification strategies for microvascular protection [ ].

## Van Maldergem syndrome (VMLDS)
VMLDS is a rare genetic disorder characterized by craniofacial malformations, limb defects, intellectual disability, and deafness. Studies indicate that pathogenic variants in the cadherin genes DCHS1 or FAT4 may cause multi-organ developmental anomalies by disrupting cell polarity and cilia assembly [ ].This finding establishes the basis for genetic diagnosis of VMLDS; however, the precise mechanism by which variant genes impair ciliary function requires mechanistic investigation.

## Melanocytic nevus
Melanocytic nevi are benign tumors composed of localized melanocytes; their malignant transformation involves multiple gene mutations. Microdissection analysis demonstrated that invasive melanoma exhibits homozygous CDKN2A deletion, whereas in situ and invasive components possess NF2, FAT4, and KDR mutations. FAT4 inactivation can impair spatial cell positioning, synergizing with CDKN2A deletion to promote invasive transformation. This genetic profile establishes a basis for early diagnosis and risk assessments in melanoma [ ].

## Severe otitis media (OM)
Severe OM refers to recurrent aggressive phenotypes (chronic suppurative otitis media with tympanic membrane lesions or mastoiditis), prevalent in genetically susceptible populations. Exome sequencing revealed that severe OM is associated with coding variants in a gene set related to hair cell stereocilia morphology (including FAT4 and GRXCR1) [ ]. FAT4 may increase OM risk and severity in genetically susceptible individuals by inducing ciliary dysfunction.

## Idiopathic hypereosinophilia (IHE)/idiopathic hypereosinophilic syndrome
IHE manifests as persistent eosinophilia (> 1.5 × 109/L) without organ damage. The advancement to idiopathic hypereosinophilic syndrome (IHES) entails organ dysfunction. FAT4 is a candidate gene involved in eosinophil generation pathways, potentially modulating their proliferation, differentiation, and survival. However, its mechanism of action requires clarification to support targeted therapy [ ].

## Ménière’s disease (MD)
MD is characterized by endolymphatic hydrops, manifesting as episodic vertigo, variable hearing loss, tinnitus, and aural fullness. Immunogenomic sequencing of Chinese patients with sporadic MD identified FAT4 as an essential candidate pathogenic gene [ ].Its variants may contribute to autoimmune inflammation induced by inner ear pressure imbalance, potentially by regulating immunity (inflammatory cytokine release).

## Cherubism
Cherubism is a rare autosomal dominant condition marked by symmetric multicystic enlargement of the jawbones in childhood. Its core pathogenesis involves Tankyrase-induced abnormal PARsylation modification. Although the FAT4 functional abnormality is not the primary causative factor, FAT4, as a tankyrase target, may affect the phenotype by exacerbating osteoblast differentiation disorder [ ].This finding clarifies the disease’s genetic heterogeneity and provides new therapeutic ideas for targeted regulation of bone remodeling (inhibiting Tankyrase).

## COVID-19
Severe COVID-19 mechanisms involve immune dysregulation and tissue fibrosis. Bioinformatics analysis indicates that FAT4 is a potential target gene for SARS-CoV-2 viral miRNAs. The downregulation in infected cells may contribute to virus-associated fibrosis and immune regulatory pathways, although this mechanism requires experimental validation [ ].

## Endogenous bornavirus-like nucleoprotein elements (EBLNs)
EBLNs are host genome elements homologous to the Bornavirus nucleoprotein gene. Research indicates that significant suppression (> 80%) of EBLN1 in human oligodendrocytes leads to a significant downregulation of FAT4 (a cell polarity-regulating cadherin), confirming that EBLN1 positively influences FAT4 through transcriptional regulation. FAT4 downregulation is the core hub for the cellular phenotype (characterized by proliferation inhibition, G2/M phase arrest, and increased apoptosis) induced by EBLN1 silencing. The mechanisms primarily involve (1) Hippo pathway dysregulation leading to cell cycle disorder, (2) MAPK signaling abnormality, (3) p53-mediated DNA damage response, and (4) apoptosis pathway activation. This molecular cascade may offer a new perspective for understanding how viral elements regulate glial cell homeostasis [ ].

## Cystic lung disease
Cystic lung disease is characterized by thin-walled air-filled cavities in the lungs. Its coexistence with lymphedema indicates a common genetic basis. A familial study reported a 57-year-old female patient with cystic lung disease comorbid with lymphedema; her offspring carried a FAT4 variant, and she also presented with lymphedema [ ].The familial study confirmed this genetic association, necessitating additional experimental validation of the mechanism.

## Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)
GDM, a common metabolic disorder during pregnancy, Current research indicates that increased methylation of the FAT4 promoter in offspring caused by maternal hyperglycemia may represent a potential epigenetic mechanism affecting the formation of metabolic programming. However, whether this constitutes a true transgenerational molecular basis beyond direct exposure in one generation still requires validation through multigenerational cohort studies and animal models demonstrating heritable epigenetic states. Current preventive strategies appropriately focus on modifiable maternal factors—specifically early pregnancy screening, individualized glucose management, and postpartum metabolic follow-up—as the primary means of interrupting this hypothesized pathological chain and potentially reducing long-term metabolic risk in offspring, with FAT4 considered more as a candidate mechanistic marker rather than an established causal target [ ].

## Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)
Cystic kidney disease is characterized by renal cyst formation, with ADPKD being the most common type. Studies indicate that pathogenic FAT4 variants are the genetic etiology in some patients [ , ].During kidney development, FAT4 deficiency regulates YAP/TAZ signaling to equilibrate ureteric branching and stromal differentiation. In mature kidneys, it cooperates with non-canonical Wnt signaling via the PCP pathway to preserve renal tubule structure.FAT4 defect induces PCP dysregulation, leading to renal tubule polarity disorder and lumen dilation, which directly facilitates cyst formation [ , ], thereby providing a basis for anti-cystic therapy aimed at the PCP/Wnt pathway.

## Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD)
The pathological basis of intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is thought to involve an inflammation-induced metabolic imbalance of the extracellular matrix (ECM). Within the inflammatory microenvironment, FAT4 appears to function as a scaffold protein, potentially facilitating the connection between Interleukin 32 (IL-32) and phosphorylation of the Hippo pathway kinases MST1/2. This interaction may contribute to the activation of downstream signaling cascades. Activation of the Hippo-YAP pathway is associated with inhibition of YAP nuclear translocation, downregulation of ECM synthesis genes (such as COL2A1 and CAN), and upregulation of catabolic enzymes (including MMP13). Collectively, these alterations are implicated in the degradation of the nucleus pulposus structure. Consequently, targeting the FAT4-IL-32-MST1/2 axis represents a potential strategy that may help mitigate IDD progression [ ].

 

## Clinical translation – therapy
FAT4 regulates pivotal Hippo–YAP and Wnt pathways, functioning as a prognostic indicator and therapeutic target to reverse resistance and potentiate chemotherapy in lung, gastric, and colorectal cancers, and indicates translational potential for guiding immunotherapy decisions in neuroendocrine carcinomas and for drug repositioning in neuronal heterotopia (Table ).

## Lung cancer
FAT4 modulates NSCLC progression by coordinating PCP and Hippo signaling pathways. The novel FAT4 activator jujuboside A (JUA) impedes MAPK pathway-induced metastasis by activating the FAT4-Hippo-YAP axis [ , ] and inhibiting EMT by inducing autophagy in ceritinib-resistant ALK-positive NSCLC models [ ]. These findings confirm FAT4’s dual value as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target, highlighting JUA’s clinical translational potential for addressing resistance and metastasis through the FAT4 pathway.

## Gastric cancer
FAT4, as a key tumor suppressor, modulates GC biology through dual pathways: FAT4 deficiency facilitates Yap nuclear translocation and stimulates downstream pro-oncogenic target genes (proliferation/migration factors) by reducing phosphorylated Yap levels. Simultaneously, it synergizes with aberrant Wnt/β-catenin pathway activation, significantly enhancing GC cell resistance to 5-FU, cisplatin, oxaliplatin, and paclitaxel. FAT4 expression level positively correlates with drug sensitivity to chemotherapy and can act as a biomarker for personalized chemotherapy regimen selection (high-expression patients preferentially respond to regimens containing these drugs). Identifying FAT4 expression can optimize GC chemotherapy decisions. Future research should investigate combination strategies targeting YAP or Wnt pathways to reverse FAT4 deficiency-associated resistance, offering clear guidance for individualized GC chemotherapy [ ].

## Colorectal cancer
FAT4 is a key tumor suppressor gene that regulates 5-FU chemotherapy sensitivity. Upregulating FAT4 increases CRC cell sensitivity to 5-FU [ ]. FAT4 regulates chemotherapy sensitivity through the Hippo signaling pathway. Upregulated FAT4 (via gene transfection or epigenetic regulation) restores Hippo pathway function, expediting YAP/TAZ phosphorylation and degradation, consequently blocking pro-survival signals and increasing CRC cell sensitivity to 5-FU [ ].Targeting FAT4 expression (developing epigenetic regulatory drugs) holds promise as a novel combination therapy to overcome 5-FU resistance among patients with CRC.

## Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC)
Caution is warranted regarding hyperprogressive disease (HPD) during immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) treatment for LCNEC. A literature report described the first HPD event in a patient with carcinoma of unknown primary origin presenting as LCNEC: Atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1) treatment achieved > 12 months of response, but subsequent tumor progression occurred. Switching to serplulimab (anti-PD-1) resulted in HPD within 3 weeks. Comparative tumor genomic sequencing before each ICI treatment revealed marked dynamic changes in mutation regions of the FAT4, SMARCA4, CYLD, CTNNB1, and KIT genes (including a novel deletion in CTNNB1 exon 3). This case highlights the potential warning value of evolving gene mutation profiles during ICI treatment and underscores the urgent need for large-sample validation [ ].This finding suggests that mutations in genes such as FAT4 may serve as potential biomarkers to inform ICI selection, potentially aiding in the individualized choice of immune checkpoint inhibitors, though prospective validation is required.

## Periventricular heterotopia (PH)
PH, one of the most common cortical developmental malformations, is mechanistically linked to mutations in the cadherin ligand-receptor pair genes DCHS1 and FAT4, clinically manifesting as abnormal neuronal migration and positioning. In a recent study, patients with PH-derived human neuronal progenitor cells (hNPCs) and isogenic cell lines harboring DCHS1 or FAT4 mutations were transplanted into mouse brain models. Combined single-cell RNA-seq analysis revealed no significant changes in autophagy-related gene expression levels. Notably, metformin effectively rescued the neuronal migration defects induced by FAT4 mutation by activating the AMPK-dependent autophagy pathway, promoting clearance of migration-obstructing proteins (paxillin), and significantly enhancing the migration capacity of PH patient-derived hNPCs. This study is the first to identify metformin as a potential therapeutic agent for FAT4-related PH, providing a pharmacological intervention strategy based on “drug repurposing” for genetic brain malformations [ ].

 

## Conclusion
FAT4, a core member of the atypical cadherin family, coordinates multiple pathway signals (including Hippo, Wnt/PCP, and RET) through its conserved extracellular domains (34 cadherin repeats, 2 LamG, and 3 EGF domains). It precisely regulates embryonic development (kidney branching morphogenesis, neuronal migration, and lymphatic valve formation) and maintains tissue homeostasis. Its inactivation (via mutation/hypermethylation/miRNA inhibition) drives EMT, genomic instability, and pro-oncogenic gene expression by relieving inhibition on YAP/TAZ and β-catenin, establishing it as a tumor suppressor gene spanning over 30 tumor types. In solid tumors such as LC and GC, FAT4 deficiency mediates chemotherapy resistance (to 5-FU/cisplatin) and immunosuppressive microenvironment formation. Specific CpG site hypermethylation (cg25879360/cg26389756) serves as a prognostic marker. Beyond oncology, the FAT4 mutation causes developmental defects such as PH and Van Maldergem syndrome. Metformin rescues neuronal migration defects through AMPK-autophagy pathway activation. However, three major controversies exist: (1) Tissue-specific paradox mutation associated with good prognosis in DLBCL, high expression associated with poor prognosis in angiosarcoma. Genetic studies in DLBCL patients have found that those carrying FAT4 mutations have significantly prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). The frequency of FAT4 mutations is markedly higher in the relapse group compared to the non-relapse group.while in angiosarcoma, high expression levels are significantly associated with shorter overall survival;(2) pathway interaction complexity (dynamic regulation of Hippo/Wnt/PCP multi-axes).FAT4 regulates various tumor diseases such as lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric cancer, colorectal cancer, and breast cancer through multiple pathways including Hippo, Wnt, and PCP. It can regulate a single pathway independently or interact between pathways. For example, in the regulation of colorectal cancer, it can inhibit EMT-driven metastasis by interfering with the interaction between the Wnt/β-catenin and Hippo/YAP1 signaling pathways.(3) clinical translation gap (lack of prospective trials and standardized testing).In terms of clinical translation, it only involves genetic testing after current disease-related treatments, or studies on ex vivo cancer cell tissues and animal experiments, without prospective clinical trials or standardized testing.
Future efforts should prioritize developing single-cell spatial multi-omics maps to resolve spatiotemporal mechanisms, advancing CRISPR/AAV-based gene therapies, designing umbrella/basket trials stratified by FAT4 status for combination targeted therapies, and developing peripheral blood methylation-based dynamic monitoring tools. The long-term goal is to enable multi-dimensional precision intervention integrating “epigenetics, genetics, and immunological strategies.”
