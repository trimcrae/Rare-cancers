## OP-BST-01: DIAGNOSTIC ACCURACY OF THE WHO CLASSIFICATION FOR FINE-NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY OF SOFT TISSUE TUMORS: A RETROSPECTIVE STUDY

S Bishnoi, A A Khan, S Ahuja, C Ahluwalia, S Ranga; Department of Pathology, Vardhman Mahavir Medical College and Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Soft tissue tumors present diagnostic challenges due to their histopathological diversity. Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) is a minimally invasive technique for initial evaluation, but interpreting these cytological samples is complex. The World Health Organization (WHO) has proposed a new classification system to standardize soft tissue cytopathology. Materials and Methods: A retrospective observational study was conducted on 203 soft tissue FNA samples collected from January 2022 to June 2023. Samples were stained with Papanicolaou and Giemsa, and reviewed by two pathologists. Histopathological correlation was performed on 117 cases. Samples were categorized according to the WHO classification: Nondiagnostic (ND), Benign, Atypical, Soft Tissue Neoplasm of Uncertain Malignant Potential (STNUMP), Suspicious for Malignancy (SFM), and Malignant. Results: The distribution of cases was: ND (3.4%), Benign (62.5%), Atypical (4.9%), STNUMP (5.4%), SFM (13.8%), and Malignant (9.9%). The risk of malignancy (ROM) was 33.3% for ND, 1.2% for Benign, 40% for Atypical, 25% for STNUMP, 80% for SFM, and 100% for Malignant. Sensitivity and specificity were highest when SFM and Malignant categories were considered positive for malignancy. Sensitivity was 77.27%, specificity was 97.83%, and diagnostic accuracy was 93.86%. The interobserver agreement was moderate (Cohen’s kappa = 0.454). Discussion: The WHO classification system provides a reliable framework for diagnosing soft tissue tumors with high sensitivity, specificity, and diagnostic accuracy. The system’s structured categories help standardize reporting and improve clinical decision-making. Discrepancies in ROM values between studies highlight the need for further research to refine the system. Conclusion: The WHO classification for soft tissue cytopathology significantly enhances diagnostic accuracy and standardization in reporting FNA samples. Our study supports the system’s reliability and effectiveness, advocating for its continued development and implementation in clinical practice.

## OP-BST-02: INVESTIGATING NERVE PATHOLOGY IN LEPROSY: A CYTOLOGICAL STUDY

Bhavna Langan1, Sonal Sharma1, Preeti Diwaker1, Chander Grover2; 1Department of Pathology, UCMS and GTB Hospital, Delhi, India, 2Department of Dermatology, UCMS and GTB Hospital, Delhi, India

Introduction: Leprosy is a chronic granulomatous disease which primarily involves the peripheral nerves and is characterised by hypoesthetic skin lesions, thickened peripheral nerves with the presence of Mycobacterium leprae. It occurs in a spectrum ranging from most severe (lepromatous) to least severe form (tuberculoid). In cases with only nerve involvement, confirmation is done with nerve biopsy, which is an invasive and time-consuming procedure and can lead to nerve damage. Hence, a safer option is needed. Fine needle aspiration (FNA) has emerged as a valuable diagnostic tool in detecting nerve involvement in leprosy. Due to limited literature documenting cytological findings of peripheral nerves in leprosy, we conducted this study to fill the gap regarding the same. Aim and Objectives: To study the role of FNAC in the evaluation of peripheral nerve involvement and to compare cytomorphological features with skin cytology as well as histopathological findings of cutaneous nerves in leprosy. Materials and Methods: Clinically diagnosed and skin biopsy proven consenting adults having thickened nerve were taken. After clinical examination, FNAC from peripheral nerves and skin lesions was performed. The smears were stained with May Grunwald Giemsa (MGG) and Fite stains. Haematoxylin & Eosin stained skin biopsy slides were retrieved from the archives and reviewed. Observation and Results: The MGG and Fite stained slides from thickened nerves and skin lesions showed evidence of necrosis, granuloma, macrophages, giant cells, neutrophils and lymphocytes. Conclusion: The cytomorphological findings in peripheral nerves of patients with leprosy were consistent with the findings of skin cytology as well as histopathology.

## OP-BST-03: DIAGNOSTIC ACCURACY OF FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY IN SPINDLE CELL LESIONS OF SOFT TISSUE TUMORS WITH HISTOPATHOLOGY CORRELATION

Oshine Kachroo, Anita P Javalgi; Department of Pathology, SDM College of Medical Sciences and Hospital, Dharwad, Karnataka, India

Introduction: Spindle cell lesions are an integral part of soft tissue tumors, where accurate cytological grading plays a vital role in determining the treatment strategies and better prognosis. However, correlation between clinical presentation, imaging studies and histopathological findings is essential for a definitive diagnosis. Aim and Objective: To grade the soft tissue spindle cell lesions into low grade and high-grade tumors on the basis of its cytological features and to determine the utility of FNAC for the diagnosis of soft tissue spindle cell lesions and correlate with histopathological evaluation. Materials and Methods: Hospital based cross sectional study done from June 2021 to July 2024. The clinical and laboratory data was extracted from electronic medical records. The FNAC slides were retrieved from archives and these were reviewed by pathologist with single blinding for cytological grading of lesions. Available histopathology cases were also reviewed and ancillary test done when required. Relevant statistical analysis was done. Observation and Results: A total of 84 cases were reported as soft tissue spindle cell lesions in the study period. Out of these, 67 (79.76%) cases were low grade and 17 (20.23%) cases were high grade tumors. Most common age group was between 31-40 years with female predominance. Histopathological follow up was seen in 41 cases, of which 29 (70.73%) cases showed a positive correlation. The overall sensitivity and specificity were 76% and 54% respectively. Conclusion: The diagnostic capabilities of FNAC in soft tissue spindle cell lesions reveal promising results with commendable correlation with histopathological findings.

## OP-BST-04: PREOPERATIVE CYTOLOGY: A KEY TOOL IN PLANNING SURGICAL RESECTION OF PEDIATRIC ADIPOCYTIC TUMORS

Arti Khatri1, Dimple Chaudhary1, Abha Singh1, Parveen Kumar2; 1Department of Pathology, Chacha Nehru Bal Chikitsalaya, Delhi, India, 2Department of Pediatric Surgery, Chacha Nehru Bal Chikitsalaya, Delhi, India

Introduction: Adipocytic lesions in children are rare, accounting for approximately 10% of soft tissue tumors. The scarcity of studies on pediatric adipocytic tumors calls for focused cyto-histopathological research. Aims and Objectives: This study aims to: (1) Enumerate common adipocytic lesions in the pediatric population over five years. (2) Evaluate the synergistic role of cytopathology and histopathology in diagnosing these lesions. Materials and Methods: A retrospective analysis was conducted on fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of lipomatous lesions in pediatric patients from January 2019 to January 2024, in the Department of Pathology at Chacha Nehru Bal Chikitsalaya. Cases were evaluated for age, gender, site, and clinico-pathological correlation. FNAC findings were correlated with histopathological results wherever available. Observations and Results: Forty cases of adipocytic lesions were diagnosed via FNAC, with 35 undergoing excision and histopathological correlation. The age range varied from infancy to 12 years, with a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.2. Most cases were diagnosed as lipomas based on cytology. However, in some cases, the presence of spindle cells and skeletal muscle bundles led to a diagnosis of benign adipocytic lesions, prompting histopathological evaluation to rule out lipofibromatosis and intramuscular lipoma. Histopathological examination revealed: lipoma (n=18, 51.44%), lipofibromatosis (n=6, 17.6%), Lipofibromatosis like neural tumour (n=1,2.8%) intramuscular lipoma (n=4,11.4%), hamartomatous adipocytic lesion (n=3, 8.5%), lipoblastoma (n=2, 5.7%), and spindle cell lipoma (n=1, 2.8%). Conclusion: The synergy between cytopathology and histopathology plays a crucial role. The presence of mature adipocytes, skeletal muscle bundle, or spindle cells can hint towards specific diagnosis such as lipoma, lipoblastoma, lipofibromatosis etc. Some of these lesions have a propensity for local recurrence, making complete surgical excision with wide margins crucial for better management. Therefore, preoperative FNAC allows surgeons to prepare for the extent of resection required, thereby improving surgical outcomes and reducing the likelihood of recurrence.

## SKIN: OP-S-01: FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION OF CUTANEOUS METASTASIS – A 15-YEAR STUDY

Nongmaithem Priyadarshini Devi, Khuraijam Sucheta Devi, Sushma Khuraijam, Khagokpam Ambala Devi, Mutum Reeta Devi, Babina Sarangthem; Department of Pathology, Regional Institute of Medical Sciences, Imphal, Manipur, India

Introduction: Cutaneous metastasis is a rare clinical entity with an incidence of less than 0.7 to 0.9% among cancer patients. Dissemination to the skin can be via blood stream, lymphatics, direct or by seeing after surgical procedures. Cutaneous metastasis usually develops months to years after the diagnosis of primary tumor. Aim of the study is to evaluate the role of FNAC as a diagnostic tool of cutaneous metastatic nodules. Materials and Methods: A retrospective review of cytopathological records from Pathology department RIMS, Imphal for 15 years, from January 2010 to August 2024, was done, and 71 cases of cutaneous metastasis were encountered. Results: Out of 71 patients diagnosed with cutaneous metastasis from known to unknown primary tumor sites, 32 were male and 39 were females with age ranging from 16 to 80 years. Patients presented with firm, painless, nodular, cutaneous swelling for FNAC. The most common primary sites of malignancy to metastasize were lungs in males and breast and ovary in females. Adenocarcinoma was the most common cytomorphological type observed. The common sites of metastasis were chest wall, abdominal wall and back. Conclusion: FNAC is minimally invasive, safe, rapid and reliable method for the diagnosis of Cutaneous metastasis.

## THYROID: OP-T-01: COULD LIQUID-BASED CYTOLOGY BE CONSIDERED AS POTENTIALLY MORE EFFECTIVE ALTERNATIVE TO CONVENTIONAL SMEARS FOR THE EVALUATION OF THYROID FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY?

Rashmi Rajora1, Poonam Abhay Elhence1, Vikarn Vishwajeet1, Deepak Vedant1, Ramkaran Chaudhary2, Pushpinder Khera3, Amit Goyal4, Darwin Kaushal5; 1Department of Pathology and Lab Medicine, AIIMS, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India, 2Department of General Surgery, AIIMS, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India, 3Department of Diagnostic and Interventional Radiology, AIIMS, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India, 4Department of ENT-Otorhinolaryngology, AIIMS, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India, 5Department of ENT-Otorhinolaryngology, AIIMS, Bilaspur, Chhattisgarh, India

Introduction: Liquid-based cytology (LBC) presents an alternative approach to Conventional smears (CS) in thyroid Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) for diagnosis and pre-operative triaging of patients. Aims and Objectives: (1) Ascertain the diagnostic performance of LBC for thyroid nodules. (2) Compare the cytomorphological features of LBC versus CS. Materials and Methods: 140 patients with thyroid nodules were subjected to FNA, and smears prepared using “Split sample method” to mitigate sampling bias. Final diagnosis was rendered according to The Bethesda system for reporting thyroid cytopathology. Statistical analysis involved calculation of “p-value” using Chi square test and Fisher’s exact test, with significance level of p < 0.05. Observations and Results: Out of 140 patients, CS provided satisfactory diagnosis in 131 cases compared to 108 cases for LBC. The agreement in diagnosis between CS and LBC was noted in 72.41% cases (101), with 41.43% (85) cases being benign and remaining 11.43% (16) cases being malignant. LBC exhibited sensitivity and specificity of 91.3% and 97.4% respectively, with positive predictive value of 87.5% when compared to CS. Notably, LBC demonstrated higher number of Category I cases due to low cellularity, scant fragmented colloid and less lymphocytic impingement in follicular clusters. Another disadvantage was presence of singly scattered follicular cells with shrunken and hyperchromatic nuclei, resembling malignant cells, resulting in higher number of cases being grouped as Category V cases. The advantages of LBC included haemorrhage-free, clean background which lead to better visualization of architectural and cytological features of malignant cells like papillae, swirls, intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions and grooves. It also allowed for single-slide examination, saving time and effort. Conclusion: LBC smears can serve as a valuable supplementary tool, alongside CS, for the cytological diagnosis of thyroid nodules. Moreover, LBC offers an added advantage of facilitating further ancillary tests, if required.

## OP-T-02: CYTONUCLEAR SCORING AS A COMPLEMENTARY TOOL IN THE PRESURGICAL DIAGNOSIS OF INDETERMINATE FOLLICULAR PATTERNED THYROID NEOPLASMS

Deeba Khan, Nauris Qamar, Hena A Ansari, Kiran Alam, Mohammad Khalid; Department of Pathology, Jawaharlal Nehru Medical College, AMU, Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: Thyroid tumors with ‘intermediate’ cytological features often yield aspirates with overlapping cytomorphology. To this group belong adenomas, NIFTP, FVPTC and to complete the list, WDT-UMP. Similarity in features may cause false positive or negatives diagnoses. This can change the direction of management. Since molecular testing is not widely available due to cost and technical issues, this study aims to identify specific cytologic features using a modified cytonuclear scoring system to better differentiate these overlapping lesions. Aims and Objectives: To study the cytopathological features of follicular patterned lesions of thyroid and differentiate them using a modified cytonuclear scoring system. Materials and Methods: We analysed fine needle aspiration (FNA) smears of 14 cases- 5 adenomas, 6 infiltrating FVPTC, 2 NIFTP, 1 WDT UMP (histopathological diagnosis as gold standard). 26 control cases were evaluated to establish the range of scores. Each specimen was assessed for cellularity, cytoarchitectural patterns, nuclear features (enlargement/irregularity/overlapping, nuclear grooves, pseudoinclusions, chromatin pattern). Composite score was given to each case using a modified cytonuclear scoring format by scoring each feature. Cases were assigned into diagnostic categories (non-neoplastic/ benign/neoplastic/ malignant.) and their initial Bethesda category was re-evaluated. Observation: Data suggested that most cases of follicular adenomas showed moderate cellularity and mild nuclear changes (score ≥ 5-9). Good cellularity, microfollicular pattern, irregular nuclei and pale chromatin were most frequently detected in aspirates from FVPTC and NIFTP (Score 10 or more). Conclusions: Incorporating these findings, this study suggests that specific nuclear features aid in diagnosis of indeterminate follicular patterned thyroid neoplasms, helping to identify the possibility of FVPTC/ NIFTP and pick out cases which need further testing This approach is further enhanced with the above mentioned scoring system, thereby increasing diagnostic accuracy.

## OP-T-03: UTILITY OF GLYCEROL SALINE REHYDRATION IN HAEMORRHAGIC FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY SMEARS FROM THYROID

S Joseph, S Pathuthara, S Dhende, S Dubal, M Bal; Department of Cytopathology and Surgical Pathology, Tata Memorial Hospital, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

Background: Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of thyroid lesions are often haemorrhagic causing difficulty in screening and interpretation and may lead to erroneous diagnoses. Liquid based cytology has been proved useful for processing haemorrhagic samples but is not cost-effective and not suitable for Rapid On-site Evaluation (ROSE). Air-dried smears are known to minimise cell loss compared to wet fixation. Objective: To evaluate the utility of Glycerol saline rehydration technique (GSRT) on air-dried smears in haemorrhagic thyroid FNACs. Materials and Methods: This is a prospective study of 50 haemorrhagic thyroid aspirates. From each sample one extra air-dried smear was prepared and treated with 10% glycerol in normal saline for 10 seconds (GSRT) and subjected to Pap staining along with the routine conventional wet fixed smears (CP). Visibility, quality of nuclear cytoplasmic staining and overall background preservation were evaluated and graded as “Excellent” (+++) “Good” (++) and “Fair” (+). Quality index (Q.I.) for each parameter and diagnosis made with CP + GSRT and CP were compared. Results: Visibility of diagnostic cells in the GSRT was excellent in 94%, Good in 6% while with CP it was 14% and 57% respectively. Fair visibility was noted in 29% of cases with CP. The nuclear cytoplasmic and background Q.I. was 0.98, 0.96, 0.94 respectively with GSRT and 0.99, 0.97, 0.96 respectively with CP. A definite diagnosis could be offered in 72% cases with CP while when combined with GSRT it was increased to 82%. Granular pattern of colloid was observed in few cases with GSRT. Conclusions: Well visualized cytomorphology of the diagnostic cells present in the haemorrhagic smears can be achieved with GSRT with minimal cell loss compared to CP. GSRT on haemorrhagic thyroid aspirates does not affect cellularity or staining but increases the diagnostic accuracy.

## OP-T-05: FROM AMBIGUITY TO ACCURACY: REDEFINING INDETERMINATE THYROID LESION DIAGNOSIS WITH HBME-1 AND CD117 IMMUNOCYTOCHEMISTRY

Surabhi Chaudhary1, Preeti Diwaker1, Priya Suneja1, Sonal Sharma1, Vipin Arora2; 1Department of Pathology, UCMS and GTB Hospital, Delhi, India, 2Department of Otorhinolaryngology, UCMS and GTB Hospital, Delhi, India

Introduction: Indeterminate category thyroid lesions (Atypia of undetermined significance, Follicular neoplasm and Suspicious of malignancy) constitute 10-25% of all thyroid aspirates. Immunocytochemistry has been explored in making the definitive diagnosis in these lesions. However, the utility of combination of HBME1 (positive marker for thyroid malignancy) and CD117 (negative marker for thyroid malignancy) has not been explored in indeterminate thyroid category lesion. Aim and Objectives: To assess the utility of combination of immunocytochemical expression of HBME-1 and CD117 in the diagnosis of indeterminate category thyroid lesion of follicular origin. Materials and Methods: Thyroid aspirates reported as indeterminate lesions over a period of 3 years were evaluated. All these smears were reviewed and classified according to The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC). The cell cluster of interest in Pap-stained smears were marked, and same slides were used for immunocytochemistry with HBME-1 and CD117 after destaining. Immunocytochemistry was performed in cases in which histopathological follow-up was available. The cytoplasmic expression of HBME-1 and CD117 was considered positive. The results of immunocytochemistry were evaluated in the marked cell cluster. Observation and Results: Of total 76 cases diagnosed as indeterminate category lesions, histopathological follow-up was available in 30 cases. The sensitivity and specificity of combination of HBME-1 and CD117 in indeterminate category lesions was found to be significantly higher as compared to individual sensitivity and specificity of HBME-1 and CD117. Conclusion: The use of a combination of positive and negative immune markers for thyroid malignancy enhances the diagnostic accuracy of immunocytochemistry for making a definitive diagnosis in indeterminate category thyroid lesions. Based on our study results, in cases with conflicting immunocytochemistry results, adding an extra positive and/or negative marker could potentially resolve the diagnostic uncertainty.

## OP-T-07: ASSESSMENT OF THYROID FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATES USING 2023 BETHESDA SYSTEM: IMPACT OF NIFTP RECLASSIFICATION

N Sureka, C Ahluwalia, S Ahuja, N Madan, M Agrawal, S Ranga; Department of Pathology, Vardhman Mahavir Medical College and Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Thyroid nodules are common, with about 5% being malignant, necessitating accurate evaluation to avoid unnecessary surgeries. Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) is a safe, reliable, accurate, cost-effective and minimally invasive diagnostic tool to evaluate thyroid lesions. The 2023 Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) refines FNAC’s diagnostic accuracy and risk stratification for thyroid lesions. Objective: This study aimed to analyze and classify thyroid FNAs according to the 2023 TBSRTC, focusing on the impact of NIFTP reclassification on the risk of malignancy (ROM) and diagnostic accuracy. Materials and Methods: This retrospective study classified thyroid FNAs performed at a tertiary care center from January 2023 to June 2024 using the 2023 TBSRTC and compared it with histopathological diagnosis wherever available. FNAC slides were reviewed and reclassified according to the 2023 TBSRTC categories by two cytopathologists. Statistical analysis included sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV and diagnostic accuracy. Results: The study included 387 patients (female-to-male ratio of 4:1), with 61.5% benign, 9% AUS, 7.5% FN, 8% SFM, and 6% malignant cases. Histopathological correlation was available for 190 cases, revealing 126 benign and 64 malignant lesions. Cyto-histological concordance was observed in 71% of cases. Considering NIFTP as malignant, the ROM for benign, FN and SFM categories was 10%, 38% and 69.2%, respectively. Diagnostic accuracy metrics showed sensitivity decreasing and specificity increasing when NIFTP was considered malignant. The highest sensitivity (88.2%) was achieved when AUS, FN, SFM and malignant categories were considered positive. The highest diagnostic accuracy (85.4%) was observed when SFM and malignant categories were considered positive. Conclusion: FNAC, guided by the 2023 TBSRTC, is a reliable diagnostic tool for thyroid lesions, offering high sensitivity and specificity. The reclassification of NIFTP as benign enhances risk stratification and diagnostic accuracy, reducing unnecessary surgeries and aiding in effective clinical management. Molecular testing in indeterminate categories may further improve diagnostic precision.

## OP-T-09: REVISITING CATEGORY III/ATYPIA OF UNDETERMINED SIGNIFICANCE OF THE BETHESDA SYSTEM FOR REPORTING THYROID CYTOPATHOLOGY IN PROGNOSTICATING A MALIGNANT OUTCOME

C M Gurusiddaiah, Ranjini Kudva; Department of Pathology, Kasturba Medical College, Manipal, Karnataka, India

Introduction: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) is a rapid, cost-effective, and safe outpatient procedure for diagnosing thyroid nodules. Introduction of The Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) has enhanced the uniformity and clinical significance of FNAC. It standardizes the reporting and predictability in diagnosis and guiding appropriate management strategies. Category III of TBSRTC, also known as Atypia of Undetermined Significance (AUS) is the most disputed category since its inception. Objective: To study the impact of subcategorization of AUS category of thyroid FNAC in predicting a malignant outcome on thyroid surgical pathology. Methods: Retrospective analysis of all thyroid nodule FNACs at Kasturba Hospital, Manipal in 2022 and 2023 classified as ‘AUS’ which were triaged to surgery and their surgical pathology. Observation: Of 1409 FNACs conducted in the study period, 69 (4.89%) were categorized under AUS, of which 39 (56.5%) were in observe/ follow up loss group and 30 (43.47%) of them underwent surgical resection of thyroid (target group). On surgical pathology in the target group, 10 (33.33%) were benign and 20 (66.66%) turned out to be malignant. FNAC subcategorization of AUS into ‘nuclear’ atypia or ‘other’ was studied in the target group and found that nuclear atypia was significantly higher (16; 80%) in confirmed malignant cases on surgery and ‘other’ namely architectural atypia was higher in benign conditions (7; 70%). On further nuclear scoring of ‘nuclear atypia’ ranging from 0-3, a score of 2 or more (15) favoured a malignant outcome and less than 2 (3) favoured a benign outcome. Most commonly encountered, malignancy was papillary thyroid carcinoma (14) and benign disease was follicular nodular disease (6). Conclusion: Subcategorization of AUS category of TBSRTC better predicts the outcome of thyroid nodules. In our study, nuclear atypia is a better predictor of malignancy than architectural patterns. Further evaluation of nuclear scoring presages malignancy and envisions data to support the restructuring of TBSRTC categories.

## OP-T-010: CATEGORISATION OF THYROID LESIONS BY FNAC ACCORDING TO BETHESDA SYSTEM AND ITS HISTOPATHOLOGICAL CORRELATION WHEREVER POSSIBLE

Vartika Singh, Pooja Agarwal, Garima Dundy; Department of Pathology, S. N. Medical College, Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: The Bethesda system is a standardized way of categorizing and reporting thyroid cytology which helps to guide further management and treatment of thyroid swellings. Aim and Objective: (1) To categorize the thyroid lesions on FNAC using the Bethesda System of Reporting Thyroid Cytology. (2) To calculate the percentage of lesions in each category. (3) To assess the effectiveness of thyroid FNAC by histological correlation wherever possible. Introduction: Identification of non-pathogenic fungal contaminants is of utmost importance in order to ensure diagnostic accuracy in cytology. Aim and Objectives: To identify the non-pathogenic contaminant fungi in cytopathology specimens.
