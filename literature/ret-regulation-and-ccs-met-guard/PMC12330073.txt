## Abstract
Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1), the core rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis, has transcended its classical metabolic regulatory role and emerged as a multi-dimensional hub in tumour biology. This review systematically delineates the dynamic regulatory networks of PFK-1 isoforms (PFKP, PFKL, PFKM) in cancer: epigenetic remodelling drives tissue-independent expression reprogramming; post-translational modification networks confer metabolic–signalling dual functions; and the dynamic nature of its subcellular localization facilitates noncanonical roles, such as intranuclear transcriptional regulation. These mechanisms collectively orchestrate hallmark oncogenic processes, including tumour proliferation, metastatic invasion, cell death evasion, angiogenesis, immune escape, and metabolic reprogramming. In clinical translation, PFK-1 isoform expression profiles, modification states, and subcellular dynamics exhibit robust correlations with cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic response. The isoform-specific modification networks unveil novel targets for developing diagnostic biomarkers and tissue-selective therapeutic strategies. This work not only reestablishes the central role of PFK-1 in tumour metabolic plasticity but also offers a fresh perspective for overcoming cancer treatment challenges.

## Introduction
Metabolic reprogramming is a defining hallmark of malignant tumours [ ]. The "Warburg effect," characterized by augmented glycolysis under aerobic conditions, equips tumour cells with rapid energy production and biosynthetic precursors [ ]. PFK-1, the central rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis, catalyses the irreversible phosphorylation of fructose-6-phosphate (F-6-P) to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (F-1,6-BP), which governs glycolytic flux and dictates cellular fate. This enzymatic activity underscores the pivotal role of PFK-1 in tumour metabolic plasticity. Emerging studies have demonstrated that three tissue-specific PFK-1 isoforms, platelet-type (PFKP), liver-type (PFKL), and muscle-type (PFKM), exhibit profound expression heterogeneity and functional diversification in cancers. In addition to their canonical metabolic roles, these isoforms orchestrate multi-dimensional regulatory networks through transcriptional reprogramming, epigenetic modulation, post-translational modifications (PTMs), and dynamic subcellular localization, collectively driving metabolic adaptability and malignant progression. The intricate interplay between metabolic and signalling cascades positions PFK-1 as a linchpin in oncogenesis [ , ].
Conventional perspectives posit that the tissue distribution of PFK-1 isoforms is developmentally programmed: PFKP predominates in platelets and proliferative tissues, PFKL governs hepatic metabolic homeostasis, and PFKM sustains elevated glycolytic activity in skeletal muscle. However, recent studies have demonstrated substantial changes in these tissue-specific expression patterns in malignant tumours. For example, in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), PFKP upregulation is closely tied to hypoxic niches [ ], while within prostate cancer (PCa), PFKL expression is androgen dependent, with fluctuations linked to castration resistance [ ]. Intriguingly, increased PFKM activity in ovarian cancer (OC) correlates with S-nitrosylation-enhanced tetramer stability [ ]. This tissue-independent expression shift indicates that tumour cells commandeer PFK-1 isoenzyme expression to balance metabolic adaptability with microenvironmental demands.
Recent investigations into PFK-1 have revealed its functional expansion in cancer biology beyond canonical glycolytic regulation. Emerging evidence highlights the dynamic subcellular localization of PFK-1 as a novel regulatory dimension. For example, PFKP undergoes Cyclin D3/CDK6-mediated phosphorylation to facilitate nucleocytoplasmic shuttling, enabling its nuclear interaction with c-Myc to coactivate CXCR4 transcription and drive metastatic progression in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (T-ALL) [ , ]. During the recurrence of ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS), PFKL undergoes dynamic relocalization from the nucleoli to the plasma membrane. This subcellular redistribution has been identified as an early event linked to enhanced metabolic adaptation in tumour progression [ ]. PTM networks further fine-tune PFK-1 isoform functionality—for example, phosphorylation at the Y64 residue of PFKP activates VEGF signalling to potentiate angiogenesis [ ]. Furthermore, PFK-1 integrates metabolic reprogramming with oncogenic signalling pathways (e.g., the PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, and Wnt/β-catenin pathways), critically regulating hallmark cancer processes, including proliferation, apoptosis evasion, metastatic invasion, and immune escape. These insights challenge the traditional view of metabolic enzymes as cytoplasm-confined entities, underscoring the functional versatility of PFK-1 in cancer.
Despite significant advancements in PFK-1 research, its clinical translation faces multifaceted challenges. This review systematically synthesizes the multi-dimensional regulatory mechanisms of PFK-1 isoforms in cancer and their clinical implications. We focus on the synergistic interplay between metabolic dependencies and noncanonical functions in shaping hallmark cancer phenotypes while evaluating their translational potential as diagnostic biomarkers, prognostic indicators, and therapeutic targets. Our analysis not only redefines the understanding of tumour metabolic heterogeneity but also provides a conceptual foundation for developing precision therapies targeting PFK-1, offering novel strategies to circumvent therapeutic resistance in oncology.

## Molecular regulatory networks of PFK-1 isoforms in cancer
PFK-1 isoforms orchestrate tumour progression through multi-dimensional regulatory networks in cancer. At the transcriptional level, their expression is regulated by hypoxia-inducible HIF-1α and epigenetic mechanisms such as N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification and DNA methylation. PTMs, including Y64 phosphorylation and ubiquitination, induce conformational changes that mediate crosstalk between enzymatic activity and signalling functions, establishing pro-oncogenic metabolic signalling hubs. Dynamic subcellular localization confers noncanonical functionalities: nuclear-localized PFKP activates CXCR4 transcription, perimitochondrial PFKL enhances lipid metabolic adaptation, and nucleoplasmic PFKM coordinates DNA repair processes (Figs. and ).
 

## Transcriptional and epigenetic regulation
The expression profiles of PFK-1 isoenzymes are pivotal in driving cancer initiation and progression, with their regulatory networks encompassing interactions with transcription factors and epigenetic remodelling.
The transcriptional regulation of PFKP may be hypoxia driven since hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBO) suppresses PFKP transcription via HIF-1α degradation [ ]. Additionally, PFKP activates the MAPK/ERK pathway through a direct interaction with ERK2, increasing the stability of the transcription factor c-Myc. c-Myc accumulation further amplifies PFKP transcription, establishing a self-reinforcing positive feedback loop [ ]. CaMKK2 also directly regulates PFKP transcription via nuclear translocation, modulating cancer cell metabolism and proliferation [ ]. Epigenetically, DNA methylation profiling identified PFKP as a methylation marker in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), suggesting that its epigenetic silencing may contribute to early tumourigenesis [ ].
The regulatory network of PFKL exhibits heightened epigenetic complexity. TAp73 activates PFKL transcription by binding specific response elements in its promoter, driving glycolysis in colorectal cancer (CRC) [ ]. The PFKL promoter is also regulated by SREBF1 binding, which promotes HCC progression [ ]. Inhibition of CBP/EP300 with SGC-CBP30 reduces H3K27ac and H3K9ac enrichment at the PFKL promoter, demonstrating that suppressing CBP/EP300-mediated transcription impairs LSCC cell viability and glycolysis [ ]. m6A modification critically regulates PFKL: YTHDF3 enhances PFKL mRNA stability by recognizing m6A sites, forming a positive feedback loop with EFTUD2 to accelerate HCC progression [ ]. Depletion of the chromatin remodeller DPY30 suppresses CRC by diminishing H3K4me3 modification at the PFKL promoter [ ].
The transcriptional regulation of PFKM is exemplified in oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), where the hypoxia-driven promoter SV1 is induced under hypoxic conditions. This activation triggers NF-κB signalling, which subsequently modulates PFKM transcription in an NF-κB-dependent manner, leading to upregulated PFKM expression [ ]. Additionally, the m6A methyltransferase METTL16 stabilizes PFKM mRNA through an IGF2BP3-dependent mechanism, thereby enhancing glycolytic flux in HCC [ ]. In osteosarcoma (OS), NAT10 reduces PFKM mRNA stability by suppressing YTHDC1-mediated m6A recognition, highlighting a context-specific regulatory axis [ ].
Collectively, the transcriptional and epigenetic regulatory networks of PFK-1 exhibit marked heterogeneity and environmental responsiveness. These findings not only deepen the understanding of cancer metabolic plasticity but also provide a theoretical framework for targeting isoform-specific regulatory nodes in cancer therapeutics.

## PTMs and protein interactions
The PTM networks of the PFK-1 isoforms confer functional plasticity beyond their canonical metabolic roles, with distinct modification signatures and interaction patterns across cancer types, forming multifaceted regulatory hubs.
PFKP phosphorylation has been identified as a critical node in cancer cell signal transduction. In glioblastoma (GBM), AKT-mediated phosphorylation of the Ser386 residue in PFKP inhibits TRIM21 E3 ubiquitin ligase-dependent ubiquitination and degradation, thereby increasing PFKP protein stability [ ]. EGFR signalling activation induces KAT5-mediated acetylation at lysine 395 (K395) of PFKP, which promotes its interaction with EGFR and subsequent membrane translocation. This is followed by EGFR-driven phosphorylation of the Y64 residue, leading to PI3K/AKT pathway activation and increased F-2,6-BP synthesis [ ]. Concurrently, Y64 phosphorylation of PFKP is essential for AKT-mediated phosphorylation of β-catenin at Ser552 and its nuclear translocation [ ]. The ubiquitination regulatory network intricately modulates PFKP stability. In breast cancer (BC), ubiquitination has dual functions: the E3 ubiquitin ligase HRD1 suppresses tumour progression by mediating PFKP ubiquitination-dependent degradation [ ], whereas the deubiquitinating enzyme USP5 drives triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) progression through PFKP stabilization [ ].
The functional diversity of PFKL likely stems from its unique protein interaction networks and multifaceted PTMs. In HCC, DNAAF5 directly binds PFKL and recruits the deubiquitinase USP39 to form a ternary complex that stabilizes the PFKL protein, thereby amplifying glycolytic flux and tumour progression [ ]. Conversely, in OC, PTGES3 interacts with PFKL via PGE2 production to induce allosteric inhibition of its enzymatic activity [ ]. Ubiquitination-mediated regulation further diversifies PFKL functionality: USP14 stabilizes PFKL through deubiquitination to increase glycolysis and drive oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) invasion [ ], whereas Tiam1 suppresses PFKL ubiquitination-dependent degradation, sustaining its protein stability to promote glycolytic reprogramming and malignancy in BC models (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-468 cells) [ ]. Paradoxically, PDLIM2 promotes PFKL ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation to suppress glycolysis; however, M2 macrophage-derived exosomes deliver miR-222-3p to downregulate PDLIM2 expression, indirectly stabilizing PFKL and fuelling chemoresistance in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) [ ]. Phosphorylation also critically regulates PFKL activity. Under energy stress, p38 kinase phosphorylates PFKL at Ser159, increasing fatty acid oxidation (FAO) and proliferation in HCC [ ].
PFKM modification signatures provide compelling evidence for the cancer-specific PTM networks of PFK-1. In OC cells, NOS1-mediated S-nitrosylation at cysteine 351 (Cys351) of PFKM stabilizes its tetrameric structure and enhances its enzymatic activity [ ]. Recent studies revealed that lactate-mediated lysine 678 (K678) lactylation in endometrial cancer (EC) markedly elevates PFKM activity, promoting glycolytic flux and tumour microenvironment angiogenesis to accelerate malignant progression [ ].
These findings collectively demonstrate how isoenzyme-specific PTM networks exert profound impacts on cancer malignancy—via precisely orchestrated mechanisms involving conformational dynamics and protein‒protein interactions—that extend beyond their canonical metabolic roles. Therapeutic targeting of critical modification hotspots or disruption of oncogenic protein complexes may offer novel avenues for developing tissue-selective therapeutic strategies.

## Subcellular localization and functional diversification
The dynamic subcellular localization of PFK-1 isoenzymes provides a spatial regulatory basis for their functional diversity. Their nucleocytoplasmic shuttling, membrane localization, and organelle interaction patterns exhibit distinct biological significance in tumour progression.
PFKP dynamically regulates its subcellular localization through PTM-mediated conformational changes. In T-ALL, the phosphorylation of PFKP at Ser386 by the Cyclin D3/CDK6 complex induces tetramer disassembly and exposes its nuclear localization signal (NLS), facilitating nuclear translocation [ ]. Nuclear PFKP forms a transcriptional complex with c-Myc, directly binds to the CXCR4 promoter, and activates its expression to drive leukaemia cell invasion and metastasis [ ]. Notably, PFKP nuclear localization is significantly correlated with a poor prognosis in T-ALL patients, suggesting that its nuclear function is a biomarker for therapeutic resistance. Furthermore, in GBM, KAT5-mediated acetylation of PFKP at Lys395 promotes its interaction with EGFR and subsequent membrane translocation, activating the PI3K/AKT signalling cascade [ ].
PFKL subcellular redistribution is mechanistically linked to tumour metabolic plasticity. In DCIS of the breast, PFKL predominantly localizes to nucleoli in nonrecurrent premalignant lesions but relocates to the plasma membrane in recurrent cases [ ]. This spatial shift is strongly correlated with increased glycolytic flux and increased recurrence risk. In HCC, under glucose-deprived conditions (hypoglycolytic stress), p38 phosphorylates PFKL Thr331. This modification inactivates the canonical enzymatic function of PFKL by converting its tetramers to monomers. Consequently, phosphorylated PFKL binds and phosphorylates the lipid droplet protein PLIN2, translocating to mitochondria-proximal regions to promote lipid droplet-mitochondria tethering and FAO, thereby supporting tumour cell survival under nutrient scarcity conditions [ ]. This metabolic‒spatial coupling mechanism offers novel insights for targeting the tumour metabolic microenvironment.
PFKM engages in noncanonical regulatory roles through compartment-specific interactions. In GBM, cytoplasmic PFKM interacts with the tubular motor protein KIF11 to drive tumour invasion and maintain GBM cytoskeleton assembly, whereas nuclear PFKM maintains genomic stability in tumour cells through its interaction with NBS1 [ ]. In OC, NOS1-mediated S-nitrosylation of PFKM at Cys351 stabilizes its tetrameric structure and induces mitochondrial membrane potential depolarization to promote chemoresistance [ ]. Additionally, in EC, lactate-induced PFKM K678 lactylation enhances substrate-binding affinity to drive glycolysis while remodelling the angiogenic tumour microenvironment through VEGF secretion [ ], revealing a hierarchical regulatory network linking metabolic enzyme modification, localization, and function.
To summarize, the dynamic subcellular localization of PFK-1 isoenzymes transcends the traditional paradigm of metabolic enzymes confined to the cytoplasm. Their nuclear transcriptional regulation, membrane-associated signalling, and organelle crosstalk provide multi-dimensional therapeutic targets for cancer metabolism. Future studies integrating spatial metabolomics and single-cell trajectory analysis will elucidate the interplay between localization dynamics and tumour heterogeneity, advancing the development of precision intervention strategies.

## PFK-1 and multidimensional associations with tumour malignant phenotypes
The involvement of PFK-1 in malignant tumour phenotypes spans multiple critical dimensions. Among the established hallmarks of cancer, PFK-1 is intricately linked to “sustaining proliferative signalling”, “resisting cell death”, “activating invasion & metastasis”, “inducing or accessing vasculature”, “avoiding immune destruction”, “deregulating cellular metabolism”, and “nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming” across diverse malignancies while intersecting with key signalling pathways (detailed molecular mechanisms are summarized in Tables , , ).
 

## Sustaining proliferative signalling
Sustained proliferative signalling, a core cancer hallmark, involves persistent activation of pro-growth pathways via autocrine growth factors, receptor overexpression, or mutation to bypass normal cell division controls (Fig.  a). Emerging evidence highlights PFK-1 as a central regulator of tumour proliferation through metabolic reprogramming and signalling crosstalk.
 PFKP drives malignant progression via multi-dimensional signalling interplay. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), PFKP overexpression enhances aerobic glycolysis to promote metastasis [ ] and may predict gefitinib sensitivity through cell cycle gene modulation [ ]. In clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), PFKP (the dominant PFK isoform [ ]) sustains glycolysis and pentose phosphate pathway activity to fuel proliferation [ ]. Melanoma patients with high PFKP expression exhibit reduced survival, with PFKP directly accelerating tumour cell proliferation [ ]. In GBM, PFKP promotes tumour growth via AKT-mediated stabilization [ ], EGFR-driven Y64 phosphorylation [ ], and HIF-1α-dependent glycolytic reprogramming [ ]. BC studies revealed that Wnt signalling upregulates PFKP to drive proliferation [ ], whereas HRD1-mediated PFKP ubiquitination and degradation suppress cancer cell proliferation [ ]. In CRC, chronic stress activates the β2-AR/PKA/CREB1 axis to induce PFKP expression, accelerating CRC cell proliferation and tumour growth [ ]. PCa models have demonstrated that YY1 [ ] and SOX2 [ ] transcriptionally regulate PFKP to promote tumour growth and therapy resistance. Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) studies have shown that lncRNA XIST inhibition upregulates miR-142-5p to suppress PFKP expression, thereby inhibiting proliferation and inducing apoptosis [ ]. Additionally, PFKP promotes proliferation in HCC [ ], OC [ ], pancreatic cancer (PC) [ ], cervical cancer (CC) [ ], and OSCC [ ] via glycolysis.
PFKL critically contributes to dysregulated proliferation across cancers. In TNBC, PFKL compensates for the increase in glycolysis; dual inhibition of PFKFB3 and PFKL synergistically suppresses tumour clonogenicity [ ]. Notably, PFK-1 expression in BC predominantly involves PFKP rather than PFKL [ ]. In CRC, TAp73 transcriptionally activates PFKL to drive proliferation [ ], whereas the herbal compound worenine inhibits tumour growth via PFKL suppression [ ]. HCC studies have identified multiple PFKL regulatory axes: the miR-34c-3p/MAGI3 axis [ ], the DNAAF5/USP39 complex [ ], and YTHDF3-mediated m6A modification [ ] upregulate PFKL to increase proliferation, whereas miR-338 [ ], A20 [ ], and ZNFDF4-AS1 [ ] inhibit PFKL to attenuate tumour growth. In lung cancer (LC), high PFKL correlates with endocrine therapy resistance and poor prognosis [ ], whereas Trichinella spiralis secretions downregulate PFKL to reduce proliferation [ ]. LSCC models have shown that PFKL enhances glycolysis and DNA repair to drive cisplatin resistance and proliferation [ ]. PCa progression is associated with PFKL upregulation in castration-resistant disease [ ]. In contrast to previous studies, in CC, KLF7 negatively regulates PFKL, and KLF7 knockdown restores PFKL expression to inhibit proliferation [ ].
PFKM governs tumour proliferation through glycolysis modulation, epigenetic regulation, and pathway activation. In BC, PFKM SNPs are correlated with early-onset risk, and their overexpression fuels proliferation via glycolytic upregulation [ ]. Mechanistically, in oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma models, SV1 activates NF-κB to induce PFKM-driven tumour progression [ ], whereas PFKM serves as a prognostic hub gene in tongue cancer/OC by rewiring metabolism [ ]. The PFKM-C351S mutant significantly reduces cancer cell proliferation rates [ ]. Recent work in OS has revealed that the NAT10-YTHDC1 axis stabilizes PFKM mRNA to sustain glycolysis-dependent proliferation [ ].
In conclusion, PFK-1 orchestrates tumour proliferation through integrated metabolic reprogramming, epigenetic regulation, and signalling crosstalk (e.g., PFKM-mediated DDR, PFKL-associated epigenetic modifications, and PFKP phosphorylation cascades). These findings collectively establish PFK-1 as a multifaceted orchestrator of cancer hallmarks, providing a molecular foundation for glycolysis-targeted anticancer strategies.

## Resisting cell death
Tumour cells evade apoptosis by dysregulating apoptotic proteins (e.g., Bcl-2 inhibition, p53 mutation) or altering damage-sensing mechanisms, enabling survival under genetic or external stress and contributing to tumourigenesis and chemoresistance (Fig.  b). PFK-1 counteracts this phenotype through multifaceted regulatory roles. Studies have revealed a synthetic lethal interaction between PFK-1 and ATP synthase in cancer cells, underscoring the metabolic indispensability of PFK-1: while PFK-1 inhibition alone does not directly induce cell death, its combination with ATP synthase inhibitors markedly sensitizes cancer cells to metabolic stress, suggesting that PFK-1 sustains glycolytic flux to support survival under metabolic challenges [ ].
PFKP enhances resistance to programmed cell death via anti-apoptotic and autophagy-modulating mechanisms. In LC, PFKP overexpression activates the NF-κB pathway to upregulate ABCC2, promoting chemotherapeutic drug efflux and cisplatin resistance in NSCLC [ ]. PFKP-mediated glycolytic reprogramming also suppresses the radiotherapy-induced DNA damage response (DDR) by inhibiting the cGAS/STING pathway, whereas PFKP knockout restores radiosensitivity and triggers apoptosis [ ]. In GBM, PFKP knockdown induces cell death and prolongs survival in tumour-bearing mice, indicating its role in maintaining metabolic adaptability [ ]. CRC studies have shown that PFKP ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation suppress tumour growth [ ], whereas NF-κB signalling activation by PFKP confers 5-fluorouracil resistance [ ]. Additionally, PFKP sustains β-catenin signalling in HCC to enhance cancer stem cell traits (e.g., ALDH1 and CD133), thereby reducing apoptotic susceptibility [ ]. In leukaemia, PFKP upregulation diminishes sensitivity to mitochondrial-targeted agents (e.g., venetoclax) by amplifying glycolytic flux, fostering chemoresistance [ ].
PFKL facilitates apoptosis evasion by augmenting glycolysis, DNA repair, and metabolic adaptability. In HCC, 125I irradiation suppresses PFKL via miR-338 upregulation, attenuating the Warburg effect and inducing apoptosis [ ]. PFKL silencing similarly reduces glycolysis to promote apoptosis and radiosensitivity [ ]. In LSCC, PFKL overexpression sustains glycolytic activity to enhance DNA repair and cisplatin resistance, whereas PFKL depletion elevates mitochondrial ROS production and apoptosis [ ]. Oesophageal cancer models have demonstrated that penfluridol inhibits PFKL activity to activate the AMPK/FOXO3a/BIM axis, triggering apoptosis [ ]. Furthermore, EGR1-mediated PFKL suppression in HCC impairs tumour proliferation, reinforcing the role of PFKL in cell survival [ ].
PFKM promotes resistance to apoptosis through maintaining genomic stability and modulating the immune microenvironment. In GBM, nuclear PFKM interacts with NBS1 to activate the DDR pathway, enhancing genomic stability and enabling DNA repair-mediated apoptosis evasion [ ]. In CRC, PFKM stabilization via the TLR5/AKT2 axis drives M1 macrophage polarization, enhancing immune-mediated tumour clearance and indirectly suppressing survival [ ]. Overall, these mechanisms highlight the significant role of PFKM in protecting tumours against cell death through metabolic adaptation and genomic stability control.
Evidently, PFK-1 isoforms orchestrate apoptosis resistance through metabolic rewiring, DNA repair, and microenvironment crosstalk: PFKP dominates chemotherapeutic efflux and radioresistance; PFKL reinforces glycolysis-dependent DNA repair; and PFKM maintains survival advantages via genomic stability and immune reprogramming. Targeting PFK isoforms may reverse apoptotic evasion and enhance therapeutic sensitivity, offering novel strategies to overcome treatment resistance.

## Activating invasion and metastasis
Invasion and metastasis involve tumour cells acquiring migratory capacity via epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT), secreting proteases to degrade the extracellular matrix, and collaborating with stromal fibroblasts and immune cells to breach tissue barriers and disseminate to distant organs [ ].
PFKP drives tumour invasion and metastasis through metabolic adaptation, EMT regulation, and stemness maintenance. In NSCLC, PFKP overexpression enhances aerobic glycolysis, cell migration, and invasion via the circLDLRAD3/miR-497-5p regulatory axis [ , , ]. Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) studies reveal that PFKP activates the MAPK pathway through an ERK2/c-Myc positive feedback loop, promoting angiogenesis and metastasis while associating with chemoresistant invasive phenotypes [ ]. In GBM, EGFR/AKT-mediated Y64 phosphorylation of PFKP facilitates β-catenin nuclear translocation to augment cell migration and invasion [ ]. In BC models, Wnt3A stimulation transactivates the PI3K/Akt pathway via EGFR, inducing PFKP S386 phosphorylation to stabilize PFKP and increase tumour invasiveness [ ]. Additionally, PFKP sustains β-catenin signalling to reinforce cancer stemness in HCC [ ], while its knockdown suppresses migration and invasion in CRC [ ]. PFKP further promotes EMT under nutrient stress in OSCC and synergizes with PDK1 to regulate chemoresistance and migration in bladder cancer [ , ] or directly drives invasion in other contexts [ ].
PFKL orchestrates metastasis through dynamic glycolysis modulation, protein stability regulation, and interactions with key effector genes. In TNBC, PFKL compensates for PFKFB3 inhibition to sustain migratory capacity [ ]. OSCC patients with high PFKL expression exhibit larger tumours, advanced TNM stages, and poor outcomes, with PFKL enhancing glycolysis-dependent proliferation and invasion [ ]. In HCC, PFKL upregulation promotes glycolysis and migratory capacity, whereas A20 (a key E3 ubiquitin ligase) [ ] and Lnc ZNF674-AS1 [ ] suppress PFKL activity to attenuate malignant invasion. Similarly, NSCLC studies have linked PFKL overexpression to metastasis, which is driven by circDHTKD1/IGF2BP2-mediated stabilization of PFKL to sustain glycolysis-dependent aggressiveness [ , ]. In PCa, PFKL upregulation is correlated with disease progression and biochemical recurrence, with PFKL knockdown significantly inhibiting invasiveness [ ].
PFKM promotes invasion and metastasis via metabolic reprogramming, epigenetic modification, and protein interactions. In GBM, cytoplasmic PFKM binds kinesin KIF11 to increase invasiveness [ ]. OC studies have revealed NOS1-mediated S-nitrosylation at PFKM Cys351, which stabilizes tetrameric PFKM to amplify glycolysis and metastasis—a phenotype suppressed by the C351S mutant [ ]. OS models have demonstrated that NAT10/YTHDC1 stabilizes PFKM mRNA via m6A modification to drive glycolysis-dependent migration and invasion [ ].
Thus, PFK-1 isoforms synergistically drive tumour invasion and metastasis through multiple mechanisms, including metabolic reprogramming, signalling pathway activation (e.g., MAPK/Wnt/β-catenin), cancer stemness maintenance, and EMT induction (Fig.  c). These processes enhance migratory capacity and therapeutic resistance by reshaping the Warburg effect and metabolic plasticity. These insights elucidate the molecular basis of tumour metabolic adaptability and provide a rationale for developing antimetastatic therapies targeting PFK-1.

## Inducing or accessing the vasculature
Tumours induce angiogenesis by secreting pro-angiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and fibroblast growth factor (FGF) to stimulate endothelial cell proliferation and migration, forming a neovasculature to provide a nutrient supply. Additionally, tumours may adopt vascular mimicry or integrate into the host vasculature to form mosaic vessel systems, bypassing conventional angiogenesis [ ].
PFKP, a pivotal glycolytic regulator, drives tumour angiogenesis directly via metabolic pathways or indirectly by modulating angiogenic factors (e.g., VEGF and c-Myc). In HNSCC, PFKP interacts directly with ERK2 to activate the MAPK/ERK pathway, stabilizing c-Myc. c-Myc accumulation reciprocally enhances PFKP transcription, establishing a PFKP/c-Myc/ERK2 positive feedback loop that upregulates pro-angiogenic factors (e.g., VEGF) to fuel neovascularization [ ]. BC studies have revealed the nonmetabolic role of PFKP in VEGF regulation: EGFR activation induces Y64 phosphorylation of PFKP, which amplifies VEGF transcription via the AKT/HIF-1α axis, stimulating tubulogenesis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) [ ]. Studies employing GBM models have revealed a bidirectional PFKP–VEGF regulatory mechanism: PFKP upregulates VEGF via EGFR signalling, whereas VEGF enhances PFKP S386 phosphorylation to increase enzymatic activity, perpetuating angiogenesis [ ]. These findings establish PFKP as a central orchestrator of tumour angiogenic niches through integrated metabolic rewiring (e.g., glycolytic flux modulation) and signalling (the MAPK and AKT/HIF-1α pathways), providing a molecular rationale for anti-angiogenic combination therapies.
The direct role of PFKL in tumour angiogenesis has not been explicitly documented. PFKM lactylation is implicated in EC angiogenesis via microenvironment modulation [ ], but emerging evidence suggests that PFKM may indirectly influence angiogenic pathways through metabolic reprogramming. In GBM, inhibiting PFKM enhances the efficacy of bevacizumab, an anti-angiogenic drug, suggesting potential synergy with vascular microenvironment remodelling [ ]. In CRC, PFKM increases glycolysis in tumour cells, promoting the polarization of tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs) to the M1 type. M1 TAMs secrete anti-angiogenic factors (e.g., IL-12 and TNF-α), inhibiting angiogenesis. This process may indirectly regulate the angiogenic balance, but its exact molecular mechanisms need experimental validation [ ]. Currently, the direct regulatory networks of PFKL and PFKM in angiogenesis remain unclear. Future research combining metabolomics with vascular endothelial interaction models is needed to explore their potential functions.
In summary, PFK-1 isoforms play divergent roles in tumour angiogenesis (Fig.  a): PFKP dominates via metabolic-signalling coupling to drive neovascularization and reinforce pro-angiogenic feedback loops, whereas PFKL and PFKM likely modulate angiogenic balance indirectly through metabolic reprogramming or immune microenvironment crosstalk. While the pro-angiogenic mechanisms of PFKP are well characterized, the endothelial interaction patterns of PFKL/PFKM remain elusive. Future studies integrating multiomics analyses and functional validation models are essential for mapping the angiogenic landscape of PFK-1 and identifying targets for metabolism‒anti-angiogenesis combination therapies.
 

## Avoiding immune destruction
Tumour cells evade immune surveillance by downregulating major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules, activating immune checkpoints (e.g., the PD-L1/PD-1 axis), and recruiting immunosuppressive cells (e.g., Tregs and MDSCs), enabling persistent survival despite host immune defences [ ].
PFKP reprograms the tumour immune microenvironment through multiple mechanisms to promote immune evasion. In LUAD, PFKP drives HIF-1α-dependent glycolytic reprogramming, polarizing the tumour microenvironment towards an "immune desert" phenotype characterized by reduced CD8 + T-cell infiltration. Concurrently, PFKP suppresses cGAS/STING pathway activity, impairing radiotherapy-induced DDR and immune recognition [ ]. GBM studies have revealed that PFKP upregulates PD-L1 via EGFR signalling to inhibit T-cell function and facilitate immune escape [ ]. In uveal melanoma (UM), PFKP overexpression is correlated with increased glycolysis and immune evasion [ ]. PC data suggest a positive association between PFKP and PD-L1 expression, implicating PFKP in immune checkpoint regulation [ ]. Research on HNSCC has demonstrated that PFKP targeting suppresses glycolysis and enhances antitumour immunity, further validating its pro-evasive role [ ]. Studies indicate that high PFKP expression in HCC is correlated with an immunotherapy response. It may modulate immune cell infiltration by altering glycolysis, although further research is needed to confirm this mechanism [ , ]. Notably, Xu et al. reported that PFKP upregulation in AML enhances glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation, sensitizing leukaemia stem cells to chemotherapeutics (e.g., Ara-C and DOX). Simultaneously, PFKP promotes MYC degradation, augmenting macrophage/NK cell-mediated immune surveillance and reducing immune evasion, thereby rendering AML cells susceptible to immune clearance [ ].
The direct role of PFKL in tumour immune evasion has not been experimentally validated. However, emerging evidence suggests that PFKL regulates immune homeostasis: its Ser775 phosphorylation serves as a metabolic‒inflammatory nexus critical for innate immune activation and early glycolytic induction, modulating macrophage function and systemic inflammatory responses [ ].
PFKM exhibits context-dependent immunomodulatory effects. In PC, PFKM upregulation enhances glycolysis, induces PD-1 + T-cell infiltration, and elevates PD-L1 expression to foster an immunosuppressive niche. PFKM knockdown reverses this phenotype by reducing PD-1 + T cells and PD-L1 levels [ ]. Moreover, in CRC, PFKM stability is linked to immune phenotype switching [ ]: C5aR1 inhibition stabilizes PFKM via the TLR5/AKT2 pathway, promoting the polarization of TAMs to the pro-angiogenic M1 type and enhancing T-cell antitumour immunity. However, this mechanism primarily involves immune activation rather than immune evasion.
The above findings indicate that PFK-1 isoforms play subtype-specific immunoregulatory roles (Fig.  b): PFKP acts as a metabolic‒immune nexus, sculpting immunosuppressive niches via glycolytic reprogramming, checkpoint modulation, and immune cell exclusion; meanwhile, PFKM displays dual functionality, either enhancing PD-L1-mediated evasion or activating antitumour immunity. While the tumour-specific immune roles of PFKL remain uncharacterized, current evidence underscores the pivotal role of PFK-1 in metabolic‒immune crosstalk. These findings illuminate the direct and indirect contributions of PFK-1 to immune evasion, offering a rationale for combining PFK-1 targeting with immunotherapies.

## Deregulation of cellular metabolism
Tumour cells undergo metabolic reprogramming characterized by enhanced glycolysis (Warburg effect), glutamine dependency for biosynthetic precursors, and increased lipogenesis to fuel rapid proliferation. This metabolic dysregulation is driven by oncogenes (e.g., MYC and HIF-1α) and signalling pathways such as Hippo, whereas aberrant energy metabolism promotes tumour progression via microenvironment remodelling (e.g., lactate accumulation and immunosuppressive metabolite secretion). As a glycolytic gatekeeper, PFK-1 drives tumour metabolic dysfunction through unique molecular mechanisms, while pulsatile PFK-1 activity stabilizes PKM2 dimers to accumulate glycolytic intermediates [ ].
PFKP, the most ubiquitously expressed isoform of PFK-1, plays a central role in tumour metabolic reprogramming, with its dysregulated expression and functionality closely linked to cancer cell metabolic dysfunction. By promoting glycolysis and aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect), PFKP supplies energy and biosynthetic precursors to fuel malignant progression. This mechanism has been extensively documented across diverse malignancies, including LC [ ], OC [ ], BC [ ], GBM [ ], CRC [ ], OSCC [ , ], gastric cancer (GC) [ ], PC [ ], leukaemia [ ], HCC [ ], cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) [ ], PCa [ ], CC [ ] and HNSCC [ ], and it intersects with multiple signalling pathways. In LC, PFKP mediates hypoxia-adaptive metabolism via the HIF-1α axis [ ]. In PCa, Wnt3A stimulation transactivates the PI3K/Akt pathway through EGFR, inducing PFKP S386 phosphorylation to increase stability and glycolysis [ ]. Glioma studies have demonstrated that AKT-mediated S386 phosphorylation stabilizes PFKP [ ], whereas EGFR coactivation amplifies glycolytic flux via PI3K/AKT signalling [ ]. Altered PFKP subcellular localization further modulates glycolytic activity, restricting the transition of nonstem glioma cells (NSTCs) to stem-like phenotypes (GSCs) [ ]. Notably, PFKP dynamically regulates metabolic flux in CRC through β-AR/PKA/CREB1 pathway activation [ ] and a K688 lactylation-mediated negative feedback loop [ ].
The functional versatility of PFKP in metabolic dysregulation is governed by diverse PTMs, including acetylation [ ], phosphorylation [ ], lactylation [ ], and ubiquitination [ ]. In addition to playing canonical glycolytic roles, PFKP performs nonglycolysis-related functions in NSCLC. Under glucose starvation (GS), PFKP interacts with AMPK to promote its mitochondrial localization, phosphorylates acetyl-CoA carboxylase 2 (ACC2), and activates long-chain FAO. This process sustains ATP/NADPH levels to alleviate energy and redox stress, supporting cancer cell survival. Crucially, this regulation is independent of the enzymatic activity of PFKP but relies on domain-specific metabolite interactions [ ] (Fig.  ).
 PFKL contributes to metabolic dysregulation through hypoxia-responsive pathways. In BC, PFKL upregulation is correlated with HIF-1α/NF-κB activation, reflecting hypoxia-driven reprogramming [ ]. A shift from PFKL to PFKP dominance during BC progression markedly enhances glycolytic output [ ]. PFKL also promotes glycolysis in TAMs, potentially shaping metabolic adaptability in the tumour microenvironment [ ]. Subcellular redistribution of PFKL (e.g., plasma membrane localization in recurrent DCIS) is associated with increased glycolysis and recurrence [ ], whereas allosteric dysregulation via mutations impairs enzymatic activity and metabolic fitness in BC cells [ ].
In HCC, the miR-338/PFKL axis [ ], A20–PFKL interaction [ ], YTHDF3-mediated m6A modification [ ], and EGR1-mediated transcriptional repression of PFKL [ ] collectively regulate glycolysis. In CRC, TAp73 transcriptional activation [ ] and DPY30 epigenetic regulation [ ] drive PFKL-dependent Warburg effects. The metabolic plasticity of PFKL is further evidenced by its role in lipid droplet lipolysis under energy stress [ ] and allosteric inhibition-mediated suppression of glycolysis/TCA cycling [ ]. PFKL also modulates metabolic dysregulation in GBM [ ], OSCC [ ], and LC [ ].
PFKM dysregulation perturbs tumour glucose metabolism via unique PTMs. In BC, PFKM SNPs are correlated with early-onset risk, potentially promoting carcinogenesis through glycolytic upregulation [ ]. CC studies link PFKM mRNA downregulation to HPV-induced metabolic reprogramming, thus impacting energy metabolism and proliferation [ ]. GC models have shown that compound 7f (a trimethoxy flavonoid-salicylate derivative) suppresses PFKM to reduce lactate production and glycolysis [ ]. NF-κB signalling upregulates PFKM in ESCC to amplify glycolysis [ ]. PTMs—including NOS1-mediated S-nitrosylation in OCs [ ] and lactylation in ECs [ ]—further modulate PFKM activity and glycolytic flux, driving tumour progression.
In summary, PFK-1 drives tumour metabolic dysfunction through subtype-specific regulatory networks. Its multi-dimensional regulation reveals tumour metabolic heterogeneity and supports precision therapy strategies targeting glycolytic nodes. Future research should focus on understanding the synergistic effects between subtypes and metabolism‒epigenetic interaction networks.

## Nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming
Nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming in cancer refers to mechanisms that regulate gene expression through epigenetic modifications (e.g., DNA methylation, histone modifications, and chromatin remodelling) without altering DNA sequences, thereby promoting tumourigenesis and malignant progression [ ]. PFK-1 isoforms contribute to cancer development via diverse epigenetic regulatory mechanisms.
PFKP is predominantly regulated through DNA methylation dynamics and noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs, miRNAs), with significant clinical correlations. In clear cell sarcoma (CCS), PFKP upregulation is associated with promoter hypomethylation [ ], whereas hypermethylation-induced epigenetic silencing in PCa suppresses glycolysis and impedes tumour progression [ , ]. In BC, PFKP expression is dually regulated by lncRNAs and miRNAs: the lncRNA WFDC21P inhibits PFKP enzymatic activity via direct binding [ ], and miR-140-5p potentially targets PFKP to modulate its expression [ ]. Similarly, in PC, the lncRNA XIST regulates the miR-142-5p‒PFKP axis to control PFKP expression, thereby influencing tumour metabolism and proliferation [ ]. m6A modification further governs PFKP regulation. In AML, R-2HG suppresses PFKP expression by inhibiting FTO-mediated m6A demethylation, attenuating glycolytic flux [ ].
PFKL is epigenetically modulated through histone and RNA modifications. In CRC, DPY30 sustains H3K4me3 enrichment at the PFKL promoter to drive transcriptional activation [ ]. HCC studies revealed that YTHDF3 stabilizes PFKL mRNA by recognizing m6A-modified sites, forming a feedforward loop to accelerate glycolysis [ ]. In NSCLC, circDHTKD1 recruits the m6A reader IGF2BP2 to stabilize PFKL mRNA, promoting glycolysis [ ]. PFKL m6A modifications are also correlated with aggressiveness in PCa [ ].
PFKM functionality and expression are profoundly influenced by diverse epigenetic modifications, including S-nitrosylation, m6A methylation, and lactylation. For example, in OC, NOS1-mediated S-nitrosylation of PFKM stabilizes its tetramer structure, enhancing activity and promoting glycolysis and tumour growth [ ]. In EC, lactylation of PFKM at K678 increases its activity, increasing glycolytic flux and tumour progression [ ]. Additionally, m6A methylation in liver cancer stabilizes PFKM mRNA through METTL16 [ ], whereas in OS, NAT10 regulates PFKM expression by modulating m6A methylation, thereby controlling glycolysis [ ]. miRNA regulation also plays a role in the epigenetic control of PFKM. In HNSCC, miRNAs target PFKM expression, influencing glycolysis and tumour prognosis [ ].
These studies systematically demonstrated that PFK-1 isoforms shape tumour metabolic plasticity through multiple epigenetic mechanisms, including DNA methylation, histone modifications, RNA methylation, and PTMs. However, their cross-omics regulatory networks and targeted intervention strategies remain underexplored. Future research should integrate epigenomic–metabolic profiling and clinical translation studies to elucidate the precise regulatory logic of PFK-1 in tumour epigenetic reprogramming.

## Clinical translational value of PFK-1
Given the pivotal role of PFK-1 in diverse cancer types, it is unsurprising that significant interest exists in targeting PFK-1 for therapeutic benefits. The central involvement of PFK-1 in tumour metabolic reprogramming further positions it as a multifaceted therapeutic target with high clinical translational potential. Current research highlights its clinical value primarily through the development of diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers.

## Diagnostic potential of PFK-1 as a cancer biomarker
The diagnostic biomarker potential of PFK-1 isoforms in oncology is prominently exemplified by PFKP across diverse malignancies. In LUAD, Chen et al. [ ] demonstrated significant upregulation of PFKP expression in patient serum through quantitative proteomic profiling, suggesting its utility as a circulating biomarker. Kisiel et al. [ ] developed a 6-gene methylated DNA marker (MDM) panel (including PFKP) for HCC detection, which achieved exceptional diagnostic performance (AUC: 0.96; sensitivity: 95%; specificity: 92%). PFKP hypermethylation not only enhances HCC discrimination accuracy but also enables early-stage tumour identification via plasma-based target-enabled long-quantitative allele-specific (TELQAS) assays, substantiating its role in early HCC detection. In ccRCC [ ], integrative transcriptomic‒proteomic analysis revealed PFKP as a consistently upregulated gene in tumour tissues versus adjacent normal tissues, with independent validation through the CPTAC database reinforcing its specificity for ccRCC. OC studies further corroborated the diagnostic importance of PFKP [ ], highlighting its pancancer biomarker potential.

## Prognostic potential of PFK-1 as a cancer biomarker
The prognostic implications of PFK-1 isoforms across malignancies are multifaceted. PFKP overexpression [ ] is strongly correlated with adverse outcomes in diverse cancers [ , ]. In patients with LC, a hypoxia-associated signature enables multigene risk modelling for survival prediction [ , ], with significant associations with clinical features such as lymph node metastasis and histological grade [ ]. ROC analysis confirmed the power of PFKP to discriminate between malignant and normal lung tissues [ ]. For ccRCC, Wang et al. [ ] validated a six-differentially expressed gene (DEG) panel (including PFKP) as an independent prognostic indicator for overall survival, and elevated PFKP expression positively correlated with immune cell infiltration levels, suggesting that its prognostic role may involve modulation of the tumour immune microenvironment [ ]. In CC, PFKP overexpression is correlated with shorter overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS), and when LDHA is also overexpressed, the prognosis is even worse [ ]. Similarly, in BC—particularly TNBC [ , , , ]—elevated PFKP mRNA levels are positively associated with tumour size, nodal spread, and advanced staging, underscoring its critical role in disease progression [ ]. PFKP overexpression also predicts a poor outcome in HNSCC [ , ], HCC [ ], CRC [ ], and melanoma [ ]. Intriguingly, the nuclear localization of PFKP, rather than alterations in its expression level, is associated with unfavourable outcomes in T-ALL patients [ ]. In contrast, PCa exhibits paradoxical PFKP downregulation tied to disease progression [ ], potentially mediated by hypermethylation-driven silencing [ ].
PFKL has novel prognostic value: its overexpression predicts inferior BC outcomes [ , ], with hypoxia-associated mechanisms implicated [ ]. In DCIS, the nucleolar-to-membrane relocalization of PFKL is associated with an increased recurrence risk, and its subcellular distribution may serve as a key parameter in machine learning-based prediction models [ ]. LSCC [ ] and LC [ , ] data corroborate the negative survival correlations of PFKL.
Unlike PFKP, PFKL overexpression directly worsens PCa prognosis and biochemical recurrence [ , ], highlighting isoform-specific clinical behaviours.
PFKM has emerged as a hub RNA-binding protein (prhRBP) in tongue cancer, where metabolic reprogramming influences patient outcomes [ ]. HNSCC miRNA profiling predicts PFKM as a survival-related gene, potentially improving prognosis via glycolysis/gluconeogenesis pathway modulation [ ]. In BC [ ], SNPs within the PFKM gene region significantly correlate with early-onset risk, suggesting that genetic variations in PFKM may drive carcinogenesis by increasing glycolytic flux.

## PFK-1 as a predictive biomarker for cancer treatment response
The heterogeneity and complexity of cancer necessitate diverse and personalized therapeutic strategies. "Treatment response" refers to the extent of tumour regression or stabilization following anticancer interventions (e.g., surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy), a critical metric for evaluating therapeutic efficacy. Emerging evidence implicates PFK-1 in predicting cancer treatment responsiveness.
For example, in LC, PFKP expression levels are significantly correlated with sensitivity to multiple chemotherapeutic agents, suggesting its utility as a predictor of drug response [ , ]. In HCC, PFKP—a gene linked to hepatic metabolism and immune microenvironment modulation—exhibits distinct mRNA and lncRNA expression patterns between patients with partial response (PR)/stable disease (SD) and those with progressive disease (PD). These differences position PFKP as a potential stratification marker for first-line combination immunotherapy, with its transcriptional variations likely reflecting aberrant metabolic‒immune crosstalk that influences therapeutic outcomes [ , ]. Furthermore, Choi et al. [ ] demonstrated that the PFKL rs2073436C > G polymorphism is significantly associated with a worsened chemotherapy response and survival in NSCLC patients treated with paclitaxel-cisplatin regimens, highlighting the potential of PFKL genotyping to predict clinical outcomes in chemotherapy recipients.
In summary, the clinical translational value of PFK-1 has evolved from singular metabolic regulation to a multi-dimensional intervention framework. While PFK-1-targeted therapies are currently in preclinical validation, accumulating evidence underscores their indispensable role in oncology. Future strategies should prioritize the development of tissue-selective inhibitors and immunometabolic combination therapies by integrating subtype-specific modification networks, spatial metabolomics, and immune microenvironment profiling, thereby overcoming therapeutic resistance barriers in solid tumours.

## Limitations and suggestions for future research
Despite significant advances in understanding the role of PFK-1 in cancer, several limitations remain [ ]. First, current research focuses predominantly on single isoforms and lacks systematic analysis of how PFKP, PFKL, and PFKM synergize, compete, or compensate within tumours. Studies have revealed that the expression ratios of these isoforms exhibit substantial heterogeneity and dynamic changes across different tumour types and even within distinct regions or stages of the same tumour [ ]. This complexity complicates the precise delineation of the specific contributions and inter-regulatory relationships of each isoenzyme. Consequently, future work should employ conditional gene knockout/knock-in models, isoform-selective inhibitors, and single-cell multiomics technologies to elucidate functional redundancy, compensatory mechanisms, and synergy among isoforms dynamically. Particular attention should be given to the dynamic reprogramming of isoenzyme expression profiles during tumour evolution and its association with therapy resistance [ – ].
Second, the tissue-specific functional differentiation and compensatory mechanisms among isoenzymes remain incompletely understood. For example, while PFKP exhibits tumour-suppressive properties in PCa due to hypermethylation-mediated silencing, it functions as an oncogenic driver in most solid tumours. This apparent paradox suggests that its functionality may be constrained by tissue-specific interaction networks, indicating a dependency on local interactomes and epigenetic contexts. Consequently, universal targeting strategies may prove ineffective or even detrimental. Future studies should therefore focus on delineating the molecular basis driving tissue-specific functional divergence, such as identifying protein complexes that specifically bind to PFK-1 isoenzymes within distinct organ microenvironments or characterizing chromatin states and noncoding RNA networks regulating isoenzyme expression in carcinoma cells across tissues [ ].
In addition, PFK-1 acquires nonmetabolic functions through post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation and lactylation; for example, PFKP Y64 phosphorylation activates VEGF signalling. However, current inhibitors primarily target the enzymatic active site and thus fail to selectively block its signal transduction capabilities. The lack of modification site-specific inhibitors has hindered the development of "function-selective intervention" strategies. Similarly, there were no specific and high-affinity compounds that targeted PFK-1 until recently. These findings suggest that the concept that PFK-1 is essential for tumour growth remains contentious, particularly given its critical role in normal human tissues (e.g., PFKL in the liver). Therefore, for precision therapeutic strategies, the focus should shift to targeting key modification sites or regulatory nodes of PFK-1 to develop tissue-selective inhibitors. For example, small-molecule inhibitors or antibody-based drugs could be designed to specifically inhibit PFKP S386 phosphorylation or PFKM Cys351 S-nitrosylation, thereby blocking their pro-tumourigenic functions. Additionally, exploring combination therapies, such as pairing PFK-1-targeted metabolic inhibitors with PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors, may offer a novel approach to overcome resistance to immunotherapy [ ].
Furthermore, while discoveries such as nuclear PFKP promoting CXCR4 transcription and perimitochondrial PFKL regulating lipid metabolism demonstrate distinct functional roles of PFK-1 isoforms at different subcellular locations, these findings are highly dependent on immunofluorescence and cellular fractionation techniques. However, such methods face limitations in real-time tracking of dynamic localization changes within in vivo tumour contexts, thereby leaving the mechanisms underlying microenvironmental influences on localization elusive. To address this gap, future studies could integrate spatial multiomics technologies—particularly by combining spatial metabolomics (MALDI-MSI) with single-cell ATAC-seq—to delineate three-dimensional maps of PFK-1 localization, post-translational modifications, and chromatin interactions within the tumour microenvironment.
Finally, regarding clinical translation, although elevated PFKP levels in the blood circulation of LUAD patients indicate substantial diagnostic potential, current detection methods such as ELISA exhibit insufficient sensitivity for samples with low abundance and cannot distinguish between isoenzyme subtypes while being susceptible to interference from nonneoplastic factors (e.g., inflammation). Consequently, developing platforms based on mass spectrometry or high-sensitivity immunoassays to quantitatively measure specific isoenzymes and their key modifications in body fluids represents a promising direction. On the other hand, preclinical studies predominantly rely on cell lines and murine models, yet their metabolic microenvironments (e.g., hypoxia gradients, stromal cell interactions) differ significantly from those of humans; notably, blood lactate concentrations are markedly elevated in patients with solid tumours [ ]. This divergence may lead to underestimation of the influence of the PFK-1-mediated microenvironment on tumour progression. Future research should therefore adopt patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models and humanized immune system mouse models to more accurately recapitulate the metabolic landscape of human tumours.
In summary, overcoming the existing limitations in PFK-1 research in oncology necessitates deep interdisciplinary integration. Consequently, future studies should prioritize the following: the use of spatial multiomics technologies to resolve dynamic isoenzyme networks; the development of function-selective inhibitors that target post-translational modifications and protein interactions; and the exploration of precision therapeutics based on PFK-1 isoenzyme subtyping. Systematic elucidation of the tissue specificity and functional diversity of PFK-1 isoenzymes in tumour metabolic plasticity will not only deepen the fundamental understanding of cancer biology but also provide transformative strategies and tools to overcome current bottlenecks in targeted therapy, thereby enabling authentic "precision metabolic intervention".

## Conclusion
This review systematically delineated the multi-dimensional regulatory networks of PFK-1 in cancer and its clinical translational potential. Research on PFK-1 has fundamentally redefined its role beyond that of a canonical glycolytic rate-limiting enzyme, revealing its multifaceted regulatory mechanisms and functional plasticity in tumourigenesis and metabolic reprogramming. The PFK-1 isoforms (PFKP, PFKL, and PFKM), through transcriptional/RNA-level regulation (e.g., HIF-1α-driven adaptation and m6A-mediated RNA stability control), dynamic PTMs (Y64 phosphorylation/S-nitrosylation/lactylation), and subcellular compartmentalization (nucleocytoplasmic shuttling/mitochondrial interactions), not only couple glycolytic flux modulation with epigenetic remodelling, DNA damage repair, and immune microenvironment reprogramming but also reveal the noncanonical regulatory functions of PFK-1 in cancer. This functional plasticity explains how tumour cells achieve metabolic adaptability and therapeutic resistance in dynamic microenvironments, establishing PFK-1 as a critical nexus that integrates metabolic flexibility and oncogenic signalling. In addition to driving the Warburg effect, these mechanisms synergistically promote malignant progression by coordinating hallmark cancer processes, including uncontrolled proliferation, apoptosis evasion, metastatic invasion, angiogenesis, immune escape, and metabolic dysregulation.
Clinical data analysis further revealed that the expression patterns of PFK-1 were significantly correlated with patient outcome across multiple cancer types. Moreover, PFK-1 may serve not only as a circulating biomarker but also to enable discriminative diagnosis of cancer status or progression phases through characterization of its methylation profiles (e.g., PFKP hypermethylation in HCC) and subcellular localization dynamics (e.g., PFKL membrane translocation in DCIS recurrence). Concurrently, subtype-specific modification networks (such as K678 lactylation in PFKM isoforms) demonstrate potential for identifying therapeutic targets to advance precision oncology strategies.
Although clinical interventions targeting PFK-1 continue to face challenges such as tissue-specific functional paradoxes, metabolic redundancy, and the absence of modification site-specific inhibitors, promising strategies may lie in integrating spatial multiomics technologies to decipher dynamic isoenzyme networks, developing tissue-selective inhibitors directed at key regulatory nodes, and exploring synergistic therapies with immune checkpoint blockade. Such integrated approaches could enable precision metabolic intervention, thereby offering a novel therapeutic paradigm to overcome resistance bottlenecks in cancer therapy.
In summary, carcinogenesis and tumour progression constitute a multifactorial and dynamic process. As a pivotal regulatory node within this network, PFK-1 studies have not only deepened our comprehension of tumour biology but also opened novel avenues for precision oncology. Future investigations should integrate mechanistic exploration with clinical validation to advance PFK-1-targeted therapeutic applications, thereby offering innovative strategies to overcome current therapeutic limitations in cancer management.
