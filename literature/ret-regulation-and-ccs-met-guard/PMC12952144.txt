## Abstract
The Notch signaling pathway, an evolutionarily conserved mechanism governing cell-cell communication, plays a pivotal and multifaceted role in shaping the tumor microenvironment (TME). This review provides a comprehensive overview of the core components and signaling mechanisms of the Notch pathway, encompassing both canonical and non-canonical signaling cascades. It further examines the dual functionality of Notch in tumorigenesis, functioning as either an oncogene or a tumor suppressor. A central focus of this review is the detailed investigation of the molecular mechanisms through which Notch signaling modulates key cellular constituents of the TME. Recent advances are systematically summarized, with emphasis on the role of Notch in regulating immune cell differentiation and function, angiogenesis, modulating cancer-associated fibroblasts, and maintenance of cancer stem cells. To facilitate clinical translation, the review highlights emerging therapeutic strategies targeting the Notch pathway, including γ-secretase inhibitors and modulators, monoclonal antibodies against ligands and receptors, small molecule inhibitors, and combination therapies integrating immune checkpoint inhibitors, chimeric antigen receptor immune cell therapy, cancer vaccines, and oncolytic viruses. Collectively, this work offers a systematic synthesis of recent progress in understanding the diverse roles of Notch signaling within the TME, emphasizing the therapeutic potential of modulating Notch signaling to reprogram the TME and enhance anti-tumor immunity.
Keywords: Notch signaling, Tumor microenvironment, Immune cells, Cancer-associated fibroblasts, Immunotherapy

## Introduction
 The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a dynamic and complex ecosystem comprising diverse cellular and non-cellular components, including tumor cells, immune cells, stromal cells, vascular endothelial cells, and the extracellular matrix (ECM). Accumulating evidence indicates that the TME plays a pivotal role in tumor biology [ , ]. Among these constituents, the ECM serves as a key structural and functional scaffold, providing not only mechanical support for tumor tissues but also regulates key tumor cell behaviors, such as adhesion, migration, and differentiation. Concurrently, stromal cells such as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) contribute to tumor progression by secreting growth factors and cytokines, as well as remodeling the ECM to create a permissive niche for tumor growth [ – ]. Moreover, tumor cells and immune cells engage in a continuous state of dynamic equilibrium characterized by mutual competition. Tumor cells evolve multiple immune evasion mechanisms to resist immune surveillance, while immune cells adapt their functional phenotypes and effector activities in an ongoing effort to recognize and eliminate malignant cells [ ].
Studies have demonstrated that the TME facilitates tumor invasion and metastasis by promoting angiogenesis and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [ , ]. Meanwhile, it fosters an immunosuppressive milieu and confers therapeutic resistance through multiple mechanisms, including immune checkpoint-mediated suppression of anti-tumor immunity, as well as hypoxia, acidosis, and physical barriers that diminish the efficacy of various therapies [ , ]. These findings collectively highlight the central involvement of the TME in tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. The regulation of these intricate processes involves the coordination of multiple signaling pathways, notably the Notch signaling pathway [ ]. Beyond its well-established functions in development and tissue homeostasis, Notch signaling mediates intercellular communication within the TME, thereby modulating tumorigenesis and immune escape [ ].
The Notch signaling pathway, as a highly conserved mechanism of intercellular communication, exerts significant influence in tumor biology [ – ]. Depending on the cellular context, activation of the Notch pathway can lead to either oncogenic or tumor-suppressive outcomes, underscoring its dual nature, and emphasizing the need for a comprehensive understanding [ ]. For instance, in colorectal cancer (CRC), the Notch signaling pathway has been identified as a crucial molecular switch that drives tumor metastasis, primarily through remodeling of the TME [ – ]. In contrast, Notch pathway predominantly acts as a tumor suppressor in small cell lung cancer (SCLC) by regulating key biological processes such as neuroendocrine differentiation, cell proliferation, EMT, and chemoresistance [ ]. Similarly, in advanced prostate cancer, emerging evidence suggests that the Notch signaling exerts tumor-suppressive effects by inhibiting neuroendocrine differentiation and reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment, thereby restraining tumor progression [ ].
Collectively, Notch signaling represents a central hub for cell-cell communication with the TME [ ]. Therefore, a systematic investigation of the precise regulatory mechanisms underlying Notch signaling in the TME holds substantial scientific and translational significance. This review aims to provide a comprehensive summary of recent advances in Notch signaling research within the TME and to explore potential therapeutic approaches targeting Notch signaling.

## Overview of the Notch signaling
The Notch signaling pathway is highly conserved across evolution, and its signal transmission relies on direct cell-to-cell contact. This pathway not only regulates tumor cells proliferation, differentiation and survival, but also plays a central role in shaping an immunosuppressive microenvironment and promoting angiogenesis by mediating bidirectional communication between tumor cells and various immune cells within the TME [ , ]. To establish a molecular foundation for subsequent discussions, this section provides a systematic exposition of the core components and structural features of the Notch signaling pathway, followed by an analysis of its mechanisms of activation and signal transduction (Fig. ).
 

## The composition and structure of the Notch signaling
The core components of the Notch signaling include receptors, ligands, transcription factors, and downstream target genes. The structure of each component determines its functional specificity [ ]. In mammals, four Notch receptors (Notch1-4) have been identified, all of which are type I transmembrane proteins composed of an extracellular domain (NECD), a transmembrane domain (TMD), and an intracellular domain (NICD) [ ]. The NECD contains multiple epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like repeat sequences and negative regulatory regions (NRR). The NICD includes a RAM domain, ankyrin (ANK) repeat sequences, a nuclear localization signal (NLS), and a PEST domain associated with protein degradation [ ].
Ligands are classified into two families: Delta-like ligands (DLL1, DLL3, DLL4) and Jagged ligands (JAG1, JAG2). Both families contain the conserved DSL (Delta/Serrate/LAG-2) domain essential for specific receptor binding [ ]. Notably, JAG ligands possess an additional cysteine-rich region, a structural distinction that contributes to functional divergence. For example, DLL1 is primarily involved in regulating cell fate decisions during differentiation, whereas JAG1 plays a prominent role in angiogenesis [ , ].
The central transcription factor in the canonical pathway is the CSL protein (CBF1/RBP-Jκ). In the absence of activation, CSL functions as a transcriptional repressor by recruiting co-repressor complexes. Upon pathway activation, it undergoes a conformational switch to become a transcriptional activator. The primary downstream targets of CSL are members of the Hes and Hey families of basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factors. These effector genes regulate the expression of downstream functional genes, thereby modulating key biological processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis [ ].

## Classical Notch signaling transduction
The classic Notch signaling pathway is CSL-dependent and requires three precise cleavages of the receptor to initiate signal transduction [ ]. The Notch receptor is initially synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum, where it undergoes glycosylation modifications before being transported to the Golgi apparatus. The furin-like convertase cleaves the receptor at the S1 site, generating a heterodimer consisting of the extracellular domain and the transmembrane-cytosolic fragment, which is subsequently trafficked to the cell membrane [ ]. Upon binding of ligands expressed on adjacent cells, a disintegrin and metalloproteinase (ADAM) family metalloproteinase (primarily ADAM10 or ADAM17) cleave the receptor at the S2 site, releasing some extracellular fragments. The remaining transmembrane-cytosolic intermediate (NeXT) is subsequently cleaved at the S3 site by the γ-secretase complex to produce the active Notch intracellular domain (NICD) [ ]. NICD translocates to the nucleus via its nuclear localization signal. Within the nucleus, the RAM domain of NICD specifically binds to the CSL protein, a sequence-specific DNA-binding factor that is already anchored at target gene promoters.​ This binding recruits mastermind-like (MAML) co-activators and histone acetyltransferases, forming a ternary transcriptional activation complex. This complex displaces co-repressor proteins bound to CSL, thereby relieving transcriptional repression and initiating the expression of target genes such as Hes and Hey. This cascade ultimately mediates the regulation of diverse cellular functions [ ].

## Non-classical Notch signaling transduction
The non-classical Notch signaling pathway operates independently of the CSL transcription factor and exhibits distinct activation and effector mechanisms compared to the canonical pathway. It does not require CSL-mediated transcription and, in certain contexts, can be activated in a ligand-independent manner without undergoing the full sequence of three proteolytic cleavages [ ]. The primary mechanism involves direct physical interactions between the Notch receptor or its fragments and components of other signaling pathways, thereby modulating key signaling axes such as Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT, and JAK/STAT. For instance, NICD can bypass CSL and directly engage with other transcriptional regulators or signaling hubs. A key example is the interaction between NICD1 and the IκB kinase-α (IKKα) signalosome, which leads to the activation of the NF-κB pathway, a mechanism observed in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells [ ]. Additionally, NICD3 can interact with IKKα homodimers, leading to the activation of the alternative NF-κB pathway in T-ALL [ ]. It is worth noting that the functions of non-classical pathways in tumors have a dual nature. For instance, non-classical activation of Notch1 has been shown to sustain the proliferative capacity of melanoma cells, whereas Notch3 exerts anti-angiogenic effects in tumors by inducing apoptosis of tumor endothelial cells [ ].
In conclusion, the Notch signaling pathway exerts precise control over cellular functions through a unique structural organization and two distinct modes of signal transduction. A comprehensive understanding of these mechanisms not only enhances our insight into the regulation of fundamental biological processes but also provides a critical theoretical basis for the development of targeted therapies for cancer.

## The dual role of Notch signaling in tumor development
The dual role of the Notch signaling pathway in cancer is illuminated by its context-dependent functions in regulating cell fate decisions, particularly in balancing stemness and differentiation. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Notch activation is instrumental in maintaining CSCs and promoting chemoresistance, underscoring its oncogenic potential [ ]. This contrasts with its function in tissues like the skin epidermis, where Notch acts in concert with transcription factors like AP-2 to orchestrate terminal differentiation; the loss of Notch or its collaborators leads to suppressed differentiation and can result in hyperproliferation, revealing a tumor-suppressive role [ ]. This speculative distinction, based on whether Notch signaling maintains a stem-like phenotype or drives terminal differentiation, may partly explain its contradictory effects in different tumor types. Consequently, it is evident that Notch signaling exhibits dual functionalities in tumorigenesis.
An increasing number of studies have confirmed that the Notch signaling pathway exerts complex functions within the TME, where its dysregulation can either promote or suppress tumorigenesis. These effects are primarily mediated through the regulation of EMT, induction of cancer stem-like cell (CSLC) properties, modulation of cell proliferation, and mediation of chemotherapy resistance, ultimately influencing tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic response [ – ] (Table ).
 

## Oncogenic functions
The Notch signaling pathway contributes to tumor progression through multiple mechanisms [ ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that Notch signaling plays a critical role in regulating EMT. For example, Sun et al. reported that the epigenetic regulator MYBBP1A acts as the upstream driver by suppressing IGFBP4 expression in HCC. The downregulation of IGFBP4, in turn, relieves its inhibition on Notch1 signaling. This activation of Notch1 promotes the EMT, thereby enhancing the migratory and invasive capabilities of HCC cells and driving metastasis. The MYBBP1A/IGFBP4/Notch1 axis thus represents a critical pathway and a novel therapeutic target in HCC [ ].
Furthermore, aberrantly activated Notch signaling is recognized as a key regulator in CSLC traits [ ]. Recent studies have shown that CD146 upregulates the expression of Notch ligand JAG2 via activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby positively modulating CSLC characteristics in the TME of HCC and enhancing the self-renewal capacity and chemoresistance of liver cancer cells. Consequently, dual targeting of CD146 and JAG2 may offer a promising therapeutic strategy for HCC [ ].
In addition, an increasing number of studies have revealed that the Notch signaling pathway drives tumorigenesis by regulating cell proliferation across various tumor types [ ]. For instance, in CRC, Notch2 activates tumor progression through a non-canonical pathway. It directly promotes the transcription of TCF7L2, a key transcriptional regulator. The upregulation of TCF7L2, in turn, drives the expression of its downstream oncogenic targets, including MYC and JUN, thereby enhancing malignant behaviors such as uncontrolled proliferation and invasion [ ]. According to the study reported, the intracellular domain of Notch1 (NICD) has been shown to cooperate with the chromatin remodeling factor SMARCA4 in T-ALL to activate MYC transcription, facilitating malignant proliferation [ ]. Similarly, in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), abnormal activation of Notch1 has been confirmed to drive uncontrolled tumor cell proliferation. Notably, Notch2, Notch3, and their ligand JAG1 are significantly overexpressed in juxtaglomerular cell tumors, resulting in widespread Notch signaling activation and sustained abnormal cell proliferation [ ]. Additionally, in retinoblastoma, the methyltransferase METTL14 mediates N6-methyladenosine modification of the long non-coding RNA LINC00340. This epitranscriptomic regulation enhances LINC00340’s stability and oncogenic function. The upregulated LINC00340, in turn, exerts its tumor-promoting effects by inhibiting the Notch signaling pathway, thereby facilitating RB cell proliferation and suppressing apoptosis [ ].
More importantly, the Notch pathway plays a pivotal role in mediating chemoresistance. For example, GALNT5 interacts with MYH9 to activate Notch signaling, suppressing the DNA damage response and thereby increasing resistance of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) to the FOLFIRINOX regimen [ ]. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Feng et al. demonstrated that CRTC1 binds to Notch1 and activates the Notch1/Akt signaling axis, leading to upregulation of PD-L1 expression, promotion of immunosuppression, and resistance to immunotherapy [ ]. Furthermore, NRN1 promotes tumor growth via the Notch1/Akt pathway [ ].
Indeed, Notch signaling also contributes to the pathogenesis of other tumor types. The cooperative regulation of plectin by Notch1 and p63 disrupts cytoskeletal integrity and cellular homeostasis in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, thereby promoting carcinogenesis [ ]. Therefore, in embryonal tumors with multilayered rosettes (ETMR), single-cell transcriptomics reveals a distinct cellular hierarchy. Notch signaling mediates critical receptor-ligand interactions between the neural stem-like cells and more differentiated neuronal populations within this hierarchy. This intercellular communication promotes tumor progression by maintaining stemness and driving proliferation, establishing Notch as a key oncogenic pathway in ETMR pathogenesis [ ].

## Tumor suppressor functions
Notch signaling also exhibits tumor suppressor functions in certain contexts. It has been reported that Notch1 acts as a tumor suppressor in the skin epithelium through a non-cell-autonomous mechanism. Loss of Notch1 compromises the integrity of the epidermal barrier, induces a wound-like microenvironment, recruits immune cells, activates fibroblasts, and promotes angiogenesis, thereby providing proliferative signals to tumor-initiating cells and ultimately facilitating the occurrence of papillomas and squamous cell carcinomas [ ].
Additionally, Notch1 functions as a downstream tumor suppressor effector of p53 by negatively regulating Rho signaling components such as ROCK1/2 and MRCKα, thereby suppressing malignant proliferation of keratinocytes and limiting the expansion of stem cell populations. Disruption of Notch signaling impairs normal epithelial differentiation and, in cooperation with oncogenic Ras signaling, promotes the progression of invasive squamous cell carcinomas [ ]. Similarly, FRMD3, an endogenous activator of Notch signaling, enhances Notch pathway activity by promoting the degradation of Disheveled-2 and disrupting its interaction with CK1, FOXK1/2, and NICD. This regulatory axis maintains the differentiation of breast epithelial cells towards the luminal lineage, and consequently suppresses the occurrence of TNBC [ ].
Notably, studies in SCLC have demonstrated that Notch signaling inhibits the neuroendocrine differentiation program driven by hASH1 by activation of the HES1 transcription factor. Concurrently, it upregulates p21(waf1/cip1) and p27kip1, leading to G1-phase cell cycle arrest, thereby effectively restraining tumor cell proliferation and reducing tumorigenic potential [ ].
Furthermore, in cutaneous tumorigenesis, Notch1 exerts tumor-suppressor effects by inhibiting β-catenin signaling and suppressing sustained expression of Gli2. Research has revealed that the loss of Notch1 can synergistically activate these two oncogenic pathways, leading to the formation of basal cell carcinoma-like lesions and significantly increasing susceptibility to chemically-induced skin tumors [ ].
Interestingly, in bladder cancer, the Notch1 pathway primarily functions as a tumor suppressor, a role supported by convergent genetic and functional evidence. Recurrent inactivating mutations and copy number loss of NOTCH1 are frequently observed in patient tumors. Consistent with this, experimental activation of Notch1 signaling inhibits the proliferation of bladder cancer cells, whereas genetic ablation of pathway components promotes tumorigenesis in murine models [ ]. Mechanistically, Notch1 activation induces the expression of dual-specificity phosphatases, leading to the dephosphorylation and inactivation of the oncogenic drivers ERK1/2, thereby restraining tumor growth [ ].
In conclusion, the Notch signaling pathway can either promote or inhibit tumorigenesis depending on the biological context (Fig.  ). Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of its dual functionality is essential for the development of targeted treatment strategies in oncology.
 

## Notch signaling in the tumor microenvironment
The TME constitutes a complex and dynamic ecosystem surrounding tumors, comprising diverse cellular components, such as immune cells, vascular structures, fibroblasts, and cancer stem cells (CSCs), all of which crucially influences tumor progression and response to therapy. Notch signaling serves as a key regulatory pathway in modulating these cellular elements within the TME, as elaborated in the following sections (Table  ).
 

## Notch signaling in immune populations
Immune populations within the TME are primarily composed of myeloid and lymphoid lineage cells. Notch signaling exerts significant regulatory effects on both cell types, acting as a critical mediator in their differentiation and in the modulation of their functional phenotypes (Fig.  ).
 

## Tumor-associated macrophages
Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) derive from peripheral monocytes and are recruited into tumor sites through chemotactic signals, including colony stimulating factor-1 [ ]. Within the TME, TAMs serve as a key cellular population that significantly influences tumor development [ ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that TAMs display abundant surface expression of Notch receptors, and in cases where Notch signaling is activated, there is a marked increase in the production of immunosuppressive mediator molecules [ , ].
In epithelial ovarian cancer, the Notch/RBPJ/CXCL2 signaling axis in endothelial cells regulates CD44-mediated cholesterol depletion in monocytes, thereby promoting the differentiation of TAMs and facilitating the establishment of an immunosuppressive TME [ ]. Furthermore, Ye et al. demonstrated that Notch signaling regulates CCR2-independent TAMs in HCC via the β-catenin/WNT axis [ ]. In an orthotopic PDAC mouse model, Yan et al. observed that inhibition of Notch gene expression reduced tumor volume and diminished macrophage infiltration, suggesting that targeting the Notch pathway may enhance immunotherapeutic efficacy in PDA patients [ ]. In gastric cancer, a critical crosstalk between tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and cancer cells is mediated through DLL3-Notch signaling. Co-culture experiments demonstrate that TAMs activate DLL3-Notch1/Notch2 signaling in gastric cancer cells, which promotes cancer cell proliferation and creates a feedback loop that stimulates cytokine production (IL-1β, IL-12, IL-10) by macrophages. This reciprocal signaling establishes a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment that drives disease progression [ ]. In contrast, in gastric cancer, the SIRT1/NICD axis promotes the secretion of macrophage-derived exosomes that suppress nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) expression in CAFs, restore T cell function, and augment anti-PD-1 therapeutic responses [ ]. Similarly, JAG2 knockout was found to inhibit tumor growth in preclinical NSCLC models, with the underlying mechanism involving activation of protective anti-tumor T cell response through Notch1/2-dependent expansion of immunostimulatory macrophages signaling [ ]. However, downregulation of TCF21 in NSCLC promotes TAM-mediated immunosuppression via Notch signaling, leading to impaired CD8 + T cells activity and reduced cytotoxic capacity [ ]. Collectively, these findings highlight that modulation of the Notch signaling pathway can reprogram the TME and represents a promising therapeutic strategy to improve the effectiveness of cancer immunotherapy.
TAMs can differentiate into two phenotypically distinct subsets: the M1 phenotype, characterized by pro-inflammatory properties and the ability to augment anti-tumor immunity, and the M2 phenotype, which exhibits anti-inflammatory features and facilitates tumor development [ ]. The M2 polarization of TAMs has been well established as a critical contributor to the malignant advancement and metastatic spread of breast cancer. Mechanistically, Linc00514 enhances STAT3 phosphorylation, upregulates JAG1/Notch signaling, stimulates the secretion of IL-4 and IL-6 by breast cancer cells, and ultimately drives M2 polarization of TAMs [ ]. In B-cell lymphoma cells and murine models, CREBBP/EP300 mutations reduce H3K27 acetylation, thereby promoting M2 polarization of TAMs via the FBXW7-Notch-CCL2/CSF1 axis and facilitating tumor progression [ ]. Lin et al. demonstrated that inhibition of the DLL4-Notch2 signaling pathway suppresses EMT and prevents macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype, leading to TME reprogramming and enhanced efficacy of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy [ ]. The inherent complexity and heterogeneity of TAMs pose substantial challenges in fully elucidating their functional mechanisms and in designing effective therapeutic strategies [ ]. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis of TAM heterogeneity within the TME is crucial for developing targeted approaches to reprogram TAMs and improve the outcomes of cancer immunotherapies.

## Tumor-associated neutrophils
Neutrophils serve as a primary defense mechanism in innate immunity and are involved in a broad spectrum of inflammatory responses. Growing evidence indicates that the TME recruits neutrophils and drives their differentiation into tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) [ ]. TANs constitute a key cellular component within the TME and display functional plasticity by transitioning between anti-tumor and pro-tumor phenotypes [ ]. Notably, recent studies demonstrated that the Notch signaling pathway plays a critical regulatory role in shaping the diverse functions of TANs in the TME [ , ]. In high-grade serous ovarian cancer, JAG2+ TANs promote Notch signaling activation, facilitating RBPJ-dependent differentiation of naive CD4 + T cells into regulatory T (Treg) cells, leading to immune escape and resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy [ ]. Moreover, JAG2+ TANs contribute to interleukin-8 (IL-8)-driven immune evasion in epithelial ovarian cancer by partially suppressing CD8 + T cell cytotoxicity, which correlates with poor clinical outcomes and immune tolerance in epithelial ovarian cancer [ ]. Neutrophil extracellular traps, released by activated neutrophils, are intricate reticular structures composed of DNA fibers, histones, and antimicrobial proteins, and play a protective role in antimicrobial defense [ , ]. In EGFR-mutant lung cancer, NETs induce EMT through activation of Notch1 signaling, leading to increased migratory and invasive capabilities of tumor cells [ ]. Additionally, aberrant Notch signaling in CRC cells has been shown to recruit neutrophils and trigger transforming growth factor β (TGF-β)-mediated signaling cascade, ultimately promoting CRC metastasis [ ]. Overall, these results indicate that modulating the functional polarization of TANs through targeting of the Notch signaling pathway could serve as a potential strategy to enhance the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy.

## Myeloid-derived suppressor cells
Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) constitute a heterogeneous population of immature myeloid progenitors that suppress host immune responses, facilitate immune evasion, and promote tumor initiation and progression [ ]. Substantial evidence demonstrates within the TME, the Notch signaling pathway regulates MDSCs through multiple molecular mechanisms [ ]. First, activated Notch/RBP-J signaling in myeloid cells drives lactate metabolism, which in turn regulates MDSCs development via the Hes1/MCT2/c-Jun axis [ ]. Consistently, inhibition of Notch signal transduction has been shown to suppress the generation of monocytic MDSCs [ ]. Recent studies have reported that in a Notch3-dependent T-ALL transgenic mouse model, aberrant Notch signaling and excessive IL-6 production by leukemic T cells contribute to the expansion of MDSCs [ , ]. In breast cancer, MDSCs enhances the stem-like properties of tumor cells through crosstalk between the IL6/STAT3 and NO/Notch signaling pathways, thereby promoting tumorigenesis [ ]. Additionally, ROS/Notch/Nodal signaling has been implicated in mediating the promotion of circulating tumor cells dissemination and metastasis by polymorphonuclear myeloid-derived suppressor cells (PMN-MDSCs) in breast cancer [ ]. In summary, given the multifaceted role of Notch signaling in regulating the immunosuppressive functions of MDSCs in the TME, therapeutic strategies aimed at this pathway hold strong potential to reprogram MDSCs, alleviate immunosuppression, and serve as a promising approach for combination immunotherapy.

## Dendritic cells
Dendritic cells (DCs) are among the most potent antigen-presenting cells in the TME. They not only orchestrate lymphocyte-mediated immune responses but also undergo functional reprogramming that can contribute to tumor progression [ ]. The Notch signaling pathway regulates the fate of multiple immune cell types, including DCs, mediates intercellular communication among immune cells [ , ]. For example, JAG2, which activates DC-stimulated natural killer (NK) cells, directly enhances NK cells cytotoxicity, interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) production, and proliferative capacity, thereby augmenting anti-tumor immunity [ ]. Feng et al. demonstrated that RBPJ-mediated Notch signaling promotes DCs maturation, migration, and antigen presentation, and enhances T cell activation, thus reinforcing the anti-tumor immune response [ ]. In human lung tumors, expression of JAG and Delta-like ligands on DCs is significantly correlated with PD-1 and Notch receptors expressed by effector memory T cells [ ]. Notch1 signaling in Lin-CD45RA- DC precursor cells from ovarian cancer cell lines drives their differentiation into plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), while suppressing the development of myeloid dendritic cells (mDCs) [ ]. In activated inflammatory DCs within ovarian tumors, special AT-rich sequence-binding protein 1 upregulates the secretion of tumor-promoting factors such as Galectin-1 and IL-6 through the Notch1 pathway, contributing to an immunosuppressive microenvironment [ ]. In inflammation-associated CRC, Notch2 signaling promotes terminal differentiation of conventional DCs (cDCs), upregulates CCR7 expression, enhances DCs migration, and strengthens the cross-presentation of antigens to CD8 + T cells, thereby inhibiting tumor progression [ ]. In osteosarcoma patients, overexpression of RBP-J counteracts the suppressive effects of miR-133a on LPS-induced DC maturation and activation, suggesting a potential therapeutic target for future immunotherapeutic strategies [ ]. DCs expressing DLL4 can reciprocally enhance mRNA expression of Notch ligands, including DLL1, DLL4 and JAG1, thereby guiding Th1 and Th17 cell differentiation and promoting anti-tumor immunity [ , ]. In the context of adoptive immunotherapy for leukemia, engineering DLL4 expression on DCs can mitigate the graft-versus-host disease toxicity in donor T cells while generating leukemia-specific T cells, enabling effective adoptive immunotherapy [ ]. Collectively, these findings indicate that Notch signal transduction plays a pivotal regulatory role in DC-mediated tumor immunity and may provide a foundation for developing novel anti-cancer immunotherapies.

## CD8 + T cells
As a critical component of the adaptive immune system, CD8 + T cells play a central role in controlling tumor progression [ , ]. The Notch signaling regulates the function of CD8 + T cells in the TME through multiple mechanisms. For example, specific deficiency of Notch2 in CD8 + T cells compromises anti-tumor immunity, whereas activation of the Notch signaling enhances tumor-suppressive responses [ ]. O’Hara et al. demonstrated that Notch signaling during CD8 + T cell activation establishes a chromatin landscape favorable for Gata3 binding at lineage-specific gene loci, CD8 lineage-specific gene loci, thereby promoting robust CD8 + T cell effector function [ ]. Early studies have shown that surface-immobilized DLL1 and conditioned medium derived from stromal cells can effectively induce the differentiation of CD8 + early T cells progenitors. Moreover, mouse primitive bone marrow cells co-cultured with DLL1-expressing OP9 cells give rise to Notch-dependent cDCs, which hold therapeutic potential for anti-tumor applications [ , ]. Notch signaling also contributes to immune evasion by mediating MED1-regulated PD-L1 expression, leading to suppression of CD8 + T cell cytotoxicity and facilitating the progression of oral squamous cell carcinoma [ ]. In the lung cancer microenvironment, upregulated DLL1 expression within the Notch pathway promotes the accumulation of IFN-γ–producing CD8 + T cells, resulting in sustained vascular normalization and tumor regression [ ]. Interestingly, comprehensive analyses in lung adenocarcinoma reveal that Notch signaling may induce functional exhaustion of CD8 + T cells by downregulating Fas ligand and perforin [ ]. In liver cancer, nuclear translocation of the Notch2 intracellular domain diminishes CD8 + T cell activity. Furthermore, the RBPJ transcriptional complex contributes to acquired drug resistance in HCC by impairing CD8 + T cell function and accelerating T cell exhaustion [ , ]. In a genetically driven mouse model of acute T-cell lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma (T-ALL), Notch1 activation in CD8 + T cells drives the malignant transformation of normal T cells into leukemic cells [ ]. However, studies using immunodeficient mouse model suggest that T-cell leukemia development may require additional oncogenic events beyond Notch1 activation [ , ]. Previous research has identified CD69 + CD103 + CD8 + tissue-resident memory T cells as key mediators of enhanced anti-tumor immunity in CRC, where their presence correlates with improved clinical outcomes. Nevertheless, the NOTCH signaling pathway can promote CRC progression by inducing PD-1 expression, thereby inhibiting both cytotoxic and non-cytotoxic functions of CD8 + T cells [ , ]. Conversely, exosomes secreted by CD8 + T cells regulate PLP2 expression and subsequently modulate the Notch signaling axis, leading to downregulation of Ki-67 and multiple EMT-related molecules, thus suppressing estrogen-induced endometrial carcinogenesis [ ].

## CD4 + T cells
CD4 + T cells serve as central mediators of both innate and adaptive immunity responses [ ]. Upon antigen stimulation, naïve CD4 + T cells differentiate into distinct functional subsets, including T helper 1 (Th1), Th2, Th17, follicular helper T (Tfh), and regulatory T (Treg) cells. Studies have demonstrated that the transcriptional repressor RBP-Jκ, in cooperation with Ikaros (a regulator of gene transcription), directly suppresses Hes1 expression, thereby regulating the development of CD4 + T cells [ ]. Dai et al. successfully induced adult bone marrow progenitor cells to differentiate into antigen-responsive CD4 + Th cells in vitro by using engineered stromal cells expressing DLL1 and major histocompatibility complex class II molecules [ ]. Beyond governing CD4 + T cell development and differentiation, Notch signaling also modulates their effector responses. Friec and colleagues revealed that the interaction between CD46 and JAG1 is a critical mechanism for initiating and regulating human Th1 immunity. This signaling axis not only drives IFN-γ production in effector Th1 cells but also controls their transition to IL-10-producing regulatory phenotypes, which is essential for establishing balanced and effective immune response [ ]. Moreover, Notch signaling influences the functional activities of differentiated CD4 + Th subsets and Treg cells. Mechanistically, the DLL4/Notch signaling enhances PI3K-dependent signaling downstream of the T cell receptor and CD28 co-stimulation, thereby increasing the sensitivity of CD4 + T cell to antigenic stimuli [ ]. Research from the past three years highlights the pivotal role of CD4 + T cells in promoting anti-tumor immunity [ , ]. Activation of Notch1 signaling during the priming phase of CD4 + T cells can reprogram their transcriptional network, enhance antigen response responsiveness, and significantly improve the anti-tumor efficacy and helper function of CD4 + CAR-T cells in vivo [ ]. In gastric cancer patients, the Notch signaling pathway shapes the tumor immune microenvironment and contributes to cancer progression through modulation of Treg and Th17 cells [ ]. In lung adenocarcinoma, elevated Notch1 signaling promotes IL-22 production by CD4 + T cells via the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathway, thereby facilitating tumor cell invasion and disease advancement [ ].
Treg cells plays a dual role in tumorigenesis: they maintain immune homeostasis while simultaneously suppressing anti-tumor immunity within the TME, thus enabling immune evasion and tumor progression [ , ]. Zhong et al. showed that the long non-coding RNA HOXA-AS2 upregulates JAG1 expression by inhibiting miR-302a, thereby promoting Treg cells proliferation and immune tolerance and accelerating glioma progression [ ]. Similarly, other studies have demonstrated that the downregulation of the Notch signaling pathway reduces the proportion of TCF1 + Treg cells subsets lung adenocarcinoma patients, suggesting its potential utility in early patient stratification [ ]. However, Ortiz-Martínez et al. reported that the Notch pathway regulates FOXP3 expression in breast cancer, and Notch downregulation is associated with increased Treg cell infiltration, particularly in triple-negative/basal-like tumor subtypes [ ].
Given that the Notch signaling pathway regulates CD4 + T cells in all aspects from development and differentiation to effector functions, precise targeting of this pathway is expected to modulate T cell immune responses, providing novel strategies to enhance the efficacy of CAR-T therapy and reversing tumor immune suppression.

## B cells
B cells within the TME exhibit substantial functional heterogeneity. Distinct B cell subpopulations can either promote anti-tumor immunity through antibody production and activation of other immune cells or facilitate tumor progression by contributing to immune evasion and suppression [ , ]. Notch signaling modulates the role of B cells in tumorigenesis through multiple mechanisms [ , ]. For example, Notch signaling can redirect early Pax5-deficient B cell precursors toward T cells or NK cells fates. Concurrently, the synergistic activity of Pax5 and Ebf1 regulates Notch signaling, a process that closely resembles the developmental trajectory of early thymocytes [ , ]. In Burkitt lymphoma, the Notch signaling exerts dual and context-dependent functions. On one hand, Notch1 intracellular domain participates in transcriptional regulation in B cells, suppresses the t(8;14) chromosomal translocation, and inhibits c-myc oncogene activation, thereby acting as a tumor suppressor. On the other hand, upon activation of anti-apoptotic signaling pathways, Notch1 intracellular domain can drive quiescent B cells into the cell cycle and stimulate their proliferation, exerting an oncogenic effect [ , ]. RBP-J, the transcription factor central to Notch signaling, has also been shown to regulate the IRF4-miR-155 regulatory network at the genetic level, sustaining miR-155 expression and thereby promoting tumorigenesis and cancer progression [ ]. In classical Hodgkin lymphoma, Notch1 interferes with the dedifferentiation of malignant B cells by antagonizing the essential transcription factor E2A and the early B-cell factor [ ]. In multiple myeloma, Notch1 expression is significantly upregulated during the lymphoid differentiation of myeloma B cells, the transition from hematopoietic stem cells to plasma cells, and even at pre-malignant stages, indicating its broad influence on B cells development [ ]. In previous research, Arima et al. identified a role for the Notch signaling pathway contributes to the creation of an immune-privileged tumor microenvironment in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma through modulation of Treg and Th2 cells responses [ ]. Beyond B cell-derived lymphomas, the Notch1-IL-1β/HIF-2α-educated B cells signaling enhance the migration and invasion capacities of tumor cells in renal cell carcinoma microenvironment [ ]. Overall, the Notch signaling pathway serves as a key factor in regulating B cell development and differentiation, exhibiting both oncogenic and tumor-suppressive functions across various cancers. Future investigations into its subtype-specific regulatory mechanisms may provide valuable insights for the design of novel therapeutic strategies targeting B cell-mediated tumor immunity.

## NK cells
Natural killer (NK) cells serve as a primary defense mechanism against tumor development and play a pivotal role in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment through cytotoxic activity and cytokine secretion [ ]. The Notch signaling pathway modulates NK cell development, thereby influencing their tumoricidal capacity and ultimately contributing to the regulation of tumor initiation and progression [ ]. Evidence indicates that the maturation process of NK cells is crucial for their anti-tumor effector functions. Yang et al. demonstrated that the deletion of B cell lymphoma 9 (BCL9) in NK cells activates the GATA1-mediated Notch-IL18R1 signaling axis, enhancing the maturation and anti-tumor activity of CD11b + CD27- NK cells [ ]. In a previous study on pediatric medulloblastoma, overexpression of the Notch signaling was associated with reduced NK cell infiltration and correlated with poorer clinical outcomes [ ]. Although current research on the interplay between the Notch pathway and NK cells in the context of cancer remains limited, emerging findings suggest significant potential for both mechanistic investigation and future clinical translation.

## Notch signaling in cancer-associated fibroblasts
CAFs are a central component of the TME in various malignancies [ ]. They contribute to tumor initiation and progression through multiple mechanisms, including ECM remodeling, promotion of EMT, and induction of immunosuppressive conditions [ ]. The Notch signaling constitutes a key regulatory network within the TME, capable of modulating CAF activation, phenotypic plasticity, and the secretion of matrix-remodeling molecules, thereby influencing tumor stromal architecture [ ]. Procopio et al. found that in cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, CSL can inhibit the activation of CAFs. Loss or downregulation of CSL leads to CAF activation and promotes keratinocyte-derived tumorigenesis, with concomitant p53 deficiency further enhancing CAF gene expression and stimulating proliferation of both stromal and cancer cells [ ]. Another study revealed that CSL downregulates the expression of the ULK3 kinase, reduce autophagy in dermal fibroblasts, and attenuates the tumor-promoting properties of CAFs. Additionally, CSL interact with other transcriptional repressors as part of broader chromatin regulatory complexes, bind to specific genomic loci, and thereby modulates tumor progression [ , ]. The interplay between Notch signaling and CAFs has also been documented in lung cancer. Evidence suggests that the Notch ligand DLL1 induces the production of WISP-1 in CAFs, promoting tumor cell migration and invasion [ ]. Intriguingly, in a murine lung cancer model, targeting the Notch ligands JAG1 and JAG2, coupled with induced Notch activation in neighboring CAFs, was shown to effectively inhibit the metastasis of lung cancer [ ]. In TNBC, recent findings indicate that Notch signaling synergizes with NF-κB (p65) through mutual activation to amplify the tumor–CAFs–inflammation network, thereby driving TNBC metastasis [ ]. Conversely, CAFs can reciprocally regulate Notch activity. The functional peptide segment, MIB1-223aa, secreted by CAFs enhances Notch signal transduction by activating the DLL4, thus promoting the malignant progression of TNBC [ ]. Similarly, single-cell transcriptomic analysis in breast cancer have shown that DLL1+ tumor cells recruited CAFs in an IL6-dependent manner, and Notch2/3-expressing CAFs subsequently enhance the secretion of Wnt ligands. This paracrine signaling reinforces Wnt/β-catenin pathway activity in DLL1+ CSCs, facilitating metastasis and increasing resistance to radiotherapy [ ]. Furthermore, the CAF-S4 subset promotes cancer cell invasion in three-dimensional matrices via the NOTCH signaling pathway, correlating with a higher likelihood of advanced distant metastasis in patients [ ]. In conclusion, while current understanding of the role of Notch signaling in tumor stroma, particularly in CAFs, remains incomplete, accumulating evidence highlights its critical involvement in tumor-stroma crosstalk (Fig. ). With ongoing advances in research, therapeutic targeting of the Notch pathway holds promise as a strategy for inhibiting tumor metastasis.
 

## Notch signaling in cancer stem cells
CSCs are a critical component of the TME, characterized by their capacity for self-renewal and sustained proliferation. They drive tumor initiation and progression while maintaining intratumoral heterogeneity [ ]. The Notch signaling pathway is closely implicated in the maintenance, expansion, and metastatic potential of CSCs. It promote the expression of tumor-associated markers, enhance CSC self-renewal capacity, and contributes to rapid tumor growth and invasive phenotypes [ ]. Ojha et al. demonstrated that in glioblastoma, Notch signaling regulates reverse electron transport and the consequent NAD⁺/NADH balance, thereby mediating metabolic reprogramming that supports CSCs proliferation [ ]. In a related study, inhibition of Notch1 expression in glioblastoma led to cell cycle arrest in CSCs and a reduction in their population, further underscoring the pivotal role of Notch signaling in CSC regulation [ ]. In breast cancer, the Notch pathway influences CSCs through multiple mechanisms. For example, Notch3 acts as a key mediator of PD-L1 overexpression in CSCs. The interaction between the Notch3 and JAG1 establishes a positive feedback loop that amplifies Notch3 signaling activity, thereby promoting immune evasion [ , ]. Additionally, the Notch pathway regulates the breast CSC pool by modulating self-renewal signaling cascades and embryonic transcription factors, which contributes to chemotherapy resistance and metastatic dissemination [ ]. RNA sequencing studies in NSCLC have revealed that S-nitrosylation of Notch1 enhances its interaction with the deacetylase UCHL1, increasing Notch1 protein stability and augmenting the oncogenic functions of CSC [ ]. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), Notch1 has been shown to enhance tumor sphere formation, secondary colony formation, and tumorigenicity of liver cancer stem cells (LCSCs), thereby increasing tumor invasiveness and recurrence risk [ ]. Moreover, Notch1 plays a crucial role in maintaining the stem-like properties of CD44v6+ LCSCs [ ]. In a prostate cancer animal model, Notch1 serves as a primary downstream effector in CSC regulation and is significantly correlated with host survival duration [ ]. Furthermore, analysis of CSC-specific signaling signatures has identified Notch1 upregulation as an early event in CRC. Notably, upon CSC inhibition, the downregulation of Notch1 precedes changes in all other gene expressions, highlighting its upstream regulatory position [ ]. Collectively, the extensive regulation of CSCs by the Notch signaling pathway underscores its critical involvement in tumor development. Targeting this pathway may not only provide a promising therapeutic strategy but also offer potential for early cancer detection, thereby opening new avenues for improved cancer management.

## Notch signaling and tumor vasculature
Tumor angiogenesis represents a critical hallmark of cancer, supplying the essential nutrients and oxygen required for the growth and metastatic of tumors [ , ]. Notch signaling is recognized as a key regulatory mechanism in tumor angiogenesis, as it governs the specification, proliferation, differentiation, migration, and adhesion of endothelial cells, which are the primary cellular components directly involved in tumor angiogenesis [ , ]. This review subsequently summarizes recent advances in the mechanisms through which Notch signaling regulates angiogenesis within the TME.

## Promote angiogenesis
A recent study has demonstrated that the NICD1-RBPJ signaling pathway upregulates USP5, leading to stabilization of STAT3 and enhanced secretion of pro-angiogenic factors, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), thereby promoting angiogenesis in esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma [ ]. He et al. revealed that activated Notch1 mediates the lncRNA TRPM2-AS/PABPC1/NUMB signaling axis, thereby promotes angiogenesis in gallbladder cancer [ ]. Similarly, Ahn et al. demonstrated that activated Notch/CD133 signaling upregulates SORT1, a potential candidate oncogene, which drives angiogenesis and systemic metastasis in HCC [ ]. In TNBC, JAG1 enhances the secretion of exosomal lncRNA MALAT1, thereby targeting the miR-140-5p-JAG1/VEGFA axis and facilitating microenvironmental angiogenesis [ ]. Additionally, Valerio and colleagues demonstrated that overexpression of ALDH1A1 in melanoma cells regulates tumor angiogenesis by activating the IL-8/Notch signaling cascade [ ]. Qiang et al. reported that ALDH2 inhibits the NOTCH1/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, hinders the expression of HIF-1α and VEGFA, and consequently suppresses angiogenesis in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [ ]. Another study further demonstrated that inhibiting HIF-1α in tumor cells and VEGF-induced DLL4 expression in endothelial cells can suppress tumor growth and angiogenesis [ ]. Given the multifaceted role of Notch signaling in driving angiogenesis across various tumor types, anti-angiogenic strategies targeting this pathway may be effectively combined with VEGF inhibitors in the future to achieve superior control of tumor progression and metastasis.

## Anti-angiogenesis
Emerging evidence continues to expand our understanding of the anti-angiogenic functions mediated by the Notch signaling pathway. For instance, Jia and colleagues demonstrated that the KLF15/VASN axis in endothelial cells suppresses angiogenesis through activation of the DLL4/Notch1 signaling pathway [ ]. Another study found that angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors promote angiogenesis in male mice by inhibiting the notch signaling pathway via Sp1/Sp3-mediated regulation [ ]. Fan et al. identified Caspase-4/11 as a novel negative regulator of Notch1 signaling, acting through inhibition of γ-secretase activity, thereby blocking Notch1 signal transduction and promoting angiogenesis [ ]. Yan et al. found that the histone demethylase KDM4D binds to the JAG1 promoter, upregulates VEGFR3, and inhibits the Notch signaling pathway, consequently promoting angiogenesis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma [ ]. Collectively, the Notch signaling pathway plays a bifunctional regulatory role in tumor angiogenesis. On one hand, it promotes angiogenesis through multiple alternative pathways. On the other hand, it inhibits de novo blood vessel formation mainly via the DLL4/Notch1 axis. Notably, the final effect of Notch signaling in tumor angiogenesis is highly context-dependent, and further research is needed to clarify its complex regulatory network.

## Combination immunotherapy strategies targeting the Notch signaling pathway
The Notch signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in modulating tumor growth, survival, and therapeutic response by regulating fundamental metabolic processes. Its involvement in the TME has increasingly garnered attention in cancer research. Consequently, Notch-directed therapeutic strategies have emerged as a major focus of investigation, with numerous agents currently under evaluation in preclinical studies and ongoing clinical trials for cancer therapy (Table  ).
 

## Inhibitors targeting the Notch signaling pathway

## γ-Secretase inhibitors and modulators
Aberrant activation of Notch signaling, associated with several cancer types, is mediated by the release of NICD through γ-secretase-dependent cleavage of the Notch receptor [ ]. Recent studies have demonstrated that activation of the γ-secretase/Notch signaling axis plays a pivotal role in driving tumor progression and the development of drug resistance. γ-Secretase inhibitors (GSIs), which target this pathway, represent promising therapeutic candidates for reversing tumor advancement and are currently under extensive investigation [ ]. Although broad-spectrum GSIs have shown anti-tumor activity across multiple cancer types, their clinical translation has been hindered by significant challenging [ ]. Several GSIs, including BMS-986115 and MK-0752, were discontinued during phase I clinical trials due to dose-limiting toxicities [ , ]. To date, only RO4929097, nirogacestat (PF-03084014) and AL101 have progressed to phase II trials, however, their therapeutic efficacy has remained modest. In the phase III DeFi trial (NCT03785964) evaluating nirogacestat in adult patients with desmoid tumors, the agent demonstrated significant clinical benefit, improving both progression-free survival and patient-reported outcomes, thereby achieving key patient-centered endpoints [ ]. In 2023, nirogacestat became the first systemic therapy for desmoid tumors approved by the U.S. food and drug administration (FDA). Beyond its application in desmoid tumors, nirogacestat is currently in clinical development for ovarian granulosa cell tumors (NCT05348356) and is being investigated in combination regimens for multiple myeloma (NCT05556798). Crenigacestat (LY3039478) has been evaluated in multiple phase I and phase II clinical trials as monotherapy or in combination with various anticancer agents for advanced or metastatic solid tumors, as well as for relapsed or refractory non-Hodgkin lymphoma and CLL [ – ]. Nevertheless, its clinical efficacy has been limited. Further research is needed to fully elucidate its therapeutic potential, refine treatment strategies and enhance response rates.
To overcome the toxicity issues associated with non-selective γ-secretase inhibition observed in clinical trials, γ-secretase modulators (GSMs) have emerged as a more selective and potentially safer therapeutic alternative [ ]. Unlike γ-secretase inhibitors, which broadly suppress enzymatic activity, GSMs selectively alter the cleavage specificity of γ-secretase by fine-tuning its catalytic function [ ]. Initially developed for Alzheimer’s disease, this pharmacological approach has also shown promise in oncology [ ]. Preclinical evidence indicates that MRK-560, a PSEN1-targeted modulator of the γ-secretase complex, effectively reduces processing of mutant Notch1 and induces cell cycle arrest in T-ALL models, while avoiding the gastrointestinal toxicity commonly associated with GSIs [ ]. These findings suggest that GSMs may serve as a viable alternative to GSIs in specific oncologic contexts. However, their overall efficacy and safety profile require further validation through comprehensive preclinical studies and well-designed clinical trials.

## Antibodies against ligands and receptors
Although GSI demonstrate significant potential in clinical trials, they exhibit certain limitations due to their inability to discriminate between different Notch receptor subtypes, resulting in pan-Notch inhibition [ ]. In contrast, the use of monoclonal antibodies that specifically target individual Notch receptors or ligands represents a more selective and potentially superior therapeutic strategy [ ]. The Notch signaling pathway comprises five known ligands and four receptors. While the precise functional mechanisms of these components remain incompletely understood, their experimentally validated associations with specific diseases support their recognition as promising therapeutic targets [ ]. Treatment with CTX014, a humanized anti-JAG1/2 blocking antibody, effectively reprograms MDSCs by targeting the JAG1/2-NFκB-p65 signaling axis within the TME, leading to reversal of immunosuppression and improved efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [ ]. Antibody-drug conjugates targeting DLL3 have shown therapeutic potential in preclinical models of SCLC and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC), prompting the initiation of subsequent clinical trials [ ]. The first clinical results, published in 2017, showed that rovalpituzumab tesirine induced objective responses in a subset of patients with SCLC or LCNEC, with response rates correlating with DLL3 expression levels; however, manageable but notable drug-related adverse events were observed [ , ]. Despite these initial signs of activity, later phase II and III trials failed to meet their primary efficacy endpoints [ ]. In patients with relapsed or refractory SCLC, rovalpituzumab tesirine—administered as monotherapy or maintenance therapy—did not confer a survival benefit and exhibited a less favorable safety profile compared to standard chemotherapy [ , ]. Combination strategies with chemotherapy or immune checkpoint inhibitors were discontinued due to increased toxicity and limited incremental efficacy [ ]. Although overall clinical outcomes fell short of expectations, complete remissions were documented across multiple studies, indicating that DLL3-targeted therapy may still hold promise in molecularly defined patient subpopulations. Next-generation DLL3-targeting agents, such as Tarlatamab (NCT03319940), FZ-AD500 (NCT06424665) and HPN328 (NCT04471727) [ ], are currently under evaluation in ongoing clinical trials. Furthermore, the DLL3-targeted antibody–drug conjugate DB1314 has demonstrated relatively robust antitumor activity in preclinical models [ ]. Tarlatamab, a DLL3-directed bispecific T-cell engager, has shown efficacy in the treatment of SCLC and neuroendocrine prostate cancer, with promising improvements in survival outcomes among patients with previously treated SCLC [ , ]. In May 2024, tarlatamab received the first U.S. approval for the treatment of adult patients with extensive-stage SCLC who experienced disease progression following platinum-based chemotherapy [ ]. In a Phase II DeLLphi-301 trial, tarlatamab demonstrated durable antitumor activity in heavily pretreated SCLC patients. Approximately one-quarter of patients achieved a progression-free survival exceeding 9 months, and median overall survival surpassed 14 months—findings considered clinically meaningful [ ]. Results from the phase III DeLLphi-304 trial further indicate that for SCLC patients whose disease progresses during or after platinum-based chemotherapy, tarlatamab treatment is associated with prolonged overall survival compared to chemotherapy [ ]. When combined with a PD-L1 inhibitor as maintenance therapy, it demonstrates controllable safety and substantial antitumor effects, providing strong rationale for the ongoing phase 3 trial (NCT06211036) [ ].
Notch1 plays a pivotal role in tumorigenesis across multiple cancer types and is regarded as a promising therapeutic target in oncology. Brontictuzumab, a monoclonal antibody specifically targeting Notch1, has been evaluated in clinical trials involving patients with solid tumors (NCT03031691 and NCT01778439) and lymphoid malignancies (NCT01703572) [ ]. The anti-Notch2/3 monoclonal antibody tarextumab has been investigated in clinical trials for advanced solid tumors, including stage IV pancreatic cancer and stage IV SCLC. However, overall, tarextumab has demonstrated limited therapeutic efficacy and has not met anticipated clinical endpoints [ , ].

## Transcriptional complex inhibitor
Aberrant activation of the Notch signaling pathway and its associated genetic mutations are closely linked to tumor initiation, maintenance, and therapeutic resistance [ ]. To date, multiple therapeutic strategies have been developed, including monoclonal antibodies targeting Notch ligands and receptors, as well as small-molecule GSIs that block proteolytic cleavage of the Notch receptor [ ]. However, research on small-molecule therapeutics targeting the downstream core components of this pathway—specifically, the Notch transcriptional activation complex—remains relatively limited [ ]. CB-103 is an orally bioavailable small-molecule inhibitor that directly targets the Notch transcriptional activation complex [ ]. Evidence demonstrates that CB-103 effectively suppresses Notch signaling in primary human T cells from patients with acute lymphoblastic leukemia, as well as in various human tumor cell lines dependent on Notch activity [ ]. Concurrently, it induces cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, thereby inhibiting cellular proliferation. Phase I/II clinical trials have been conducted in patients with advanced solid tumors and hematological malignancies (NCT03422679). In these studies, CB-103 exhibited a manageable safety profile, favorable tolerability, and clear evidence of biological activity, although its antitumor efficacy as monotherapy was modest [ ].

## ADAM inhibitors
ADAM is a family of transmembrane proteins that regulate extracellular proteolysis at the cell surface, thereby modulating cellular interactions and cell-matrix adhesion [ ]. Notably, ADAM10 and ADAM17 mediate the proteolytic cleavage of the Notch receptor at the S2 site, a critical step that is significantly enhanced upon ligand binding [ ]. In this process, activation of Notch1 signaling is driven by ADAM17, which cleaves the Notch receptor to release the NICD, enabling its nuclear translocation and subsequent initiation of downstream gene transcription [ ]. ZLDI-8 is an ADAM17-targeting inhibitor that suppresses Notch protein cleavage, leading to downregulation of proteins involved in cell survival, anti-apoptosis, and EMT [ ]. Studies have shown that ZLDI-8 significantly enhances the sensitivity of HCC cells to sorafenib, etoposide, and paclitaxel [ ]. Subsequent in vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that ZLDI-8 downregulates integrin β1 and β3 expression in a Foxc2-dependent manner, providing a promising novel therapeutic approach for HCC treatment [ ]. INCB7839 is a dual protease inhibitor targeting ADAM10 and ADAM17 that has undergone phase I–II clinical evaluation in patients with previously treated solid tumors and breast cancer (NCT04295759 and NCT02141451). However, results from the phase I trial indicated that monotherapy with INCB7839 was associated with dose-limiting toxicities, including deep vein thrombosis, as well as adverse events such as fatigue and nausea.

## Combined immune checkpoint inhibitors with Notch inhibitors
Immune checkpoints serve as critical regulators of immune responses, normally preventing tissue damage and autoimmunity by modulating excessive immune activation [ ]. However, this protective mechanism is frequently exploited by cancer cells to evade immune surveillance and mediate immune escape. The identification and characterization of key checkpoint molecules, such as PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4, have established a robust theoretical foundation for the development of next-generation cancer immunotherapies [ ]. The successful application of immune checkpoint blockade has fundamentally reshaped the therapeutic paradigm for malignant tumors [ ]. However, the emergence of drug resistance under monotherapy underscores the necessity of developing combination strategies. Aligned with established principles of cancer therapy, the integration of immune checkpoint inhibition with other therapeutic modalities represents a critical and inevitable approach to enhancing clinical efficacy and broadening its applicability [ ]. CTLA4, the first immune checkpoint receptor to be clinically targeted, is specifically expressed on the surface of T cells and plays a critical regulatory role during the early phase of T cell activation [ ]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that inhibition of CTLA4 significantly enhances vaccine-induced anti-tumor immune responses in multiple models of low immunogenic tumors [ ]. PD-1, an inhibitory receptor expressed on T cells, plays a central role in the pathway of cancer adaptive immune resistance through its interaction with the ligand PD-L1 [ ]. In gastric cancer, activation of this pathway has been shown to be mediated by Notch signaling, which promotes tumor immune escape by upregulating PD-L1 expression [ ]. Furthermore, recent evidence demonstrates that synthetic Notch agonists, such as PDL1-SNAGs or HER2-SNAGs, effectively activate the Notch signaling pathway in CD8 + T cells co-cultured with tumor cells expressing the corresponding target biomarkers [ ]. In preclinical animal models, Notch1 inhibition not only increases tumor sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors but also strengthens anti-tumor immune responses by promoting lymphocyte infiltration [ ]. Elevated expression of JAG1 in patients with TNBC is frequently associated with increased macrophage infiltration and impaired T-cell function [ ]. Notably, the combination of PD-1 antibody and Notch inhibitor has been shown to significantly suppress tumor growth in preclinical breast cancer models, indicating its potential as a therapeutically valuable strategy [ ]. These findings collectively support the rationale for targeting Notch as an immunogenic driver and highlight the therapeutic potential of combining Notch inhibition with immune checkpoint blockade to elicit robust anti-tumor immunity (Fig. A).
 

## Combined chimeric antigen receptor immune cell therapy
Immunocytes engineered to express chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) have become a breakthrough therapy for treating blood cancers, and their applications are expanding to solid tumors, infectious diseases, and autoimmune diseases [ ]. T cells, due to their ability to proliferate and persist in the body, can provide medium to long-term efficacy and are currently the preferred choice for generating CAR-T cells [ ]. As the most advanced cell adoptive immunotherapy in clinical practice, CAR-T therapy accounts for nearly half of the global cancer cell therapy pipeline [ ]. Structurally, CAR consists of an extracellular antigen recognition region (typically in the form of a monoclonal antibody scFv) and an intracellular signaling region (including the CD3ζ activation domain and one or more co-stimulatory domains), which are connected by a hinge region and a transmembrane region [ ]. Additionally, the new generation of CAR designs also integrate functions such as secreting interleukins to enhance their proliferation, infiltration, persistence, and killing activity [ , ]. JAG1, a critical ligand in the Notch signaling pathway, has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in oncology [ ]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that anti-JAG1 antibodies exert robust antitumor activity in in vivo models of TNBC, ovarian cancer, and CRC, with no significant toxicity observed [ , ]. These findings provide compelling evidence supporting the targeting of JAG1 for the treatment of aggressive malignancies. Building upon this foundation, the development of CAR-T cell therapy directed against JAG1 holds promise for further enhancing its therapeutic potential in cancer immunotherapy. Studies have demonstrated that primary T cells expressing the anti-JAG1 B5 (VH-VL)−3G CAR can specifically recognize target antigens in vitro, become activated, and effectively eliminate JAG1-positive cancer cells [ ]. However, given that JAG1 is also expressed in certain normal tissues such as cardiac muscle cells, the in vivo application of these CAR-T cells may pose a risk of on-target, off-tumor toxicity [ ]. Therefore, engineered T cells incorporating combinatorial antigen recognition circuits—such as synthetic Notch (synNotch) receptor technology—are required to assess their therapeutic potential in vivo [ ]. The synNotch receptor enables inducible expression of effector proteins upon recognition of specific antigens. This approach has been validated to significantly improve tumor specificity of CAR-T cells and enhance their sustained antitumor activity across multiple cancer models [ , ]. Another study has demonstrated that the use of DLL4-targeted CAR-T cells to specifically eliminate DLL4-positive tumor cells can render HER2-positive breast cancer sensitive to chemotherapy [ ]. This combinatorial approach not only exhibits sustained antitumor efficacy but also demonstrates a favorable safety profile. CAR immune cell therapy targeting key receptors and ligands of the Notch signaling pathway not only demonstrates potential in eliminating tumor cells but also has the capacity to overcome drug resistance through modulation of the TME (Fig. B). When combined with synNotch receptor technology, this approach provides a well-defined developmental pathway for addressing on-target, off-tumor toxicity and achieving safe and effective cancer therapy.

## Combined cancer vaccines and oncolytic viruses
The cancer vaccine functions by introducing tumor-associated antigens to stimulate and direct both cellular and humoral immune responses in the body. This process facilitates the development of durable immune memory, thereby achieving the objective of suppressing tumor progression and preventing disease recurrence [ ]. Oncolytic viruses (OVs) can directly lyse tumor cells while sparing normal cells and indirectly enhance anti-tumor immunity by releasing antigens and activating inflammatory responses in the tumor microenvironment [ , ]. The use of OVs as monotherapy or in combination with other strategies has been supported by preclinical and clinical studies [ ] (Fig. C). However, their application has some limitations, such as the penetration of OVs into tumors and the host’s antiviral immune response, which have hindered the widespread clinical application of oncolytic virotherapy [ ]. The treatment outcome depends on the balance between the host’s antiviral response and the anti-tumor immunity induced by OVs, but the biological mechanisms influencing this balance remain unclear. Talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) is a genetically engineered, live attenuated herpes simplex virus type 1, designed to exert anti-tumor effects through selective intratumoral replication and the induction of systemic antitumor immunity [ ]. In October 2015, it became the first oncolytic viral therapy approved by the U.S. FDA for the treatment of unresectable cutaneous, subcutaneous, and nodal lesions in patients with melanoma recurring after initial surgical intervention [ ]. Intratumoral administration of T-VEC is considered to mediate anti-tumor effects through the induction of tumor cell lysis and the release of tumor-associated antigens, leading to the activation of a systemic immune response and the generation of tumor antigen-specific T-cell immunity [ ]. Research indicates that in patients with advanced melanoma, T-VEC therapy demonstrates superior overall survival outcomes compared to granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor therapy [ ]. Oncolytic herpes simplex virus (oHSV) infection induces high expression of JAG1 in infiltrating myeloid cells, leading to widespread activation of the Notch signaling pathway throughout the TME [ ]. Notch-activated macrophages secrete increased levels of CCL2, which further drives the expansion of MDSCs. Pharmacological inhibition of Notch signaling effectively reversed this immunosuppressive cascade and elicited CD8 + T cell-dependent anti-tumor immune memory, resulting in significant therapeutic benefits [ ]. Currently, the clinical benefits of OVs monotherapy remain limited. The combination of OVs with tumor antigen-targeted vaccines holds significant potential to activate and expand tumor-reactive cytotoxic T lymphocytes, either through the immunostimulatory effects mediated by OVs or via the coordinated presentation of tumor-derived epitopes by MHC class I molecules and viral epitopes by MHC class II molecules [ ].

## Conclusion and future perspectives
The Notch signaling plays a central role in the TME, functioning not as an isolated cellular event but as a complex, multicellular, and context-dependent regulatory network. It profoundly influences tumor initiation, progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance [ ]. This review highlights the dual roles of Notch signaling as either an oncogenic driver or a tumor suppressor in various cancer contexts [ ]. More importantly, this pathway fosters an immunosuppressive TME by reprogramming the functions of TAMs, TANs, MDSCs, T cells, and B cells, thereby facilitating immune escape. Additionally, Notch pathway participates in tumor angiogenesis by regulating cancer stem cell behavior and plays a critical role in the activation and functional diversification of CAFs [ , ]. The multifaceted involvement of Notch signaling across tumor cells, immune populations, and stromal components underscores both its biological complexity and the challenges associated with its therapeutic purposes [ ].
Current therapeutic strategies targeting the Notch pathway mainly include GSIs, monoclonal antibodies against Notch receptors or ligands, and inhibitors of the Notch transcription complex. Notably, given the dual role of Notch in regulating anti-tumor immunity, combining Notch-targeted therapies with immunotherapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cell therapy, cancer vaccines, and OVs, represents a promising approach for reshaping the TME and enhancing anti-tumor efficacy [ ].
Overall, the growing understanding of Notch signaling within the TME has shifted the therapeutic paradigm from a narrow focus on “killing tumor cells” to a broader strategy centered on “remodeling the TME.” This evolution has not only revealed novel mechanisms for overcoming immunosuppression and improving treatment responses through modulation of immune cells, stromal cells, and angiogenesis, but also established a crucial theoretical foundation and identified highly promising intervention points for developing combination therapies [ ].
Indeed, translating Notch pathway research into clinical applications requires more precise and systematic approaches. Based on the findings summarized in this review, it is suggested to utilize techniques such as single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics to dissect the activity and functional heterogeneity of Notch signaling across distinct cell subpopulations within the TME at the single-cell resolution [ , ]. Furthermore, it is essential to investigate the interplay between Notch signaling and key processes, such as metabolic reprogramming and ECM remodeling in the TME [ ]. More importantly, organoid co-culture systems and humanized mouse models should be utilized to study the Notch-mediated regulation in physiologically relevant, human-like TME [ ].
Furthermore, it is recommended to identify and validate key downstream effector molecules of Notch signaling in different TME through high-throughput screening and proteomics techniques, and to elucidate their functions in mediating tumor-stromal interactions and immune evasion [ ]. A systematic assessment of crosstalk between Notch and other pivotal signaling pathways, including Wnt, Hedgehog, and JAK-STAT, is also warranted [ ]. Exploring the synergistic or antagonistic interactions among these pathways will provide critical insights for designing rational combination therapies.
In translational medicine, efforts should prioritize the development of next-generation Notch-targeted agents with enhanced specificity and reduced toxicity. Strategies may include engineering Notch inhibitors with TME-selective delivery, such as via nanocarrier systems or tumor cell/matrix-specific antibodies, to minimize off-target effects [ ]. Moreover, the therapeutic potential of Notch pathway activators should be explored in tumor types characterized by Notch inactivation, thereby expanding the scope of applicable inactivation [ ]. Concurrently, personalized treatment strategies must be developed alongside the identification of predictive biomarkers through large-scale clinical studies to enable precision application of Notch-targeted therapies.
Despite its considerable promise, Notch pathway research faces several limitations, including the need for robust clinical validation, the complexity of resistance mechanisms, and the lack of longitudinal data on long-term efficacy [ ]. Therefore, in-depth investigation of molecular mechanisms underlying resistance to Notch-targeted therapies is imperative. Equally important is the exploration of combinatorial strategies involving co-targeting of resistance-related pathways or reversal of microenvironmental remodeling to overcome therapeutic resistance. Key challenges remain, including compensatory activation of alternative signaling pathways, TME-driven resistance, and gastrointestinal toxicity associated with Notch inhibition [ ]. Potential solutions may involve intermittent dosing regimens, advanced nano-delivery platforms, and co-targeting of resistance mechanisms. Ultimately, progress in this field will depend on strengthened interdisciplinary collaboration and data sharing among basic scientists, clinicians, and computational biologists, accelerating the translation of fundamental discoveries into tailored and effective anti-cancer therapies.
In conclusion, targeting the Notch signaling pathway within the TME remains a dynamic and highly promising area of oncology research. Extensive further investigation is required to fully elucidate the intricacies of Notch signaling and its impact on the tumor immune landscape. Most critically, future advances will rely on integrating deeper mechanistic insights, more precise targeting technologies, and innovative clinical trial designs. The ultimate goal is to achieve individualized cancer therapy by harnessing this evolutionarily conserved developmental pathway as a powerful weapon against malignancy.
