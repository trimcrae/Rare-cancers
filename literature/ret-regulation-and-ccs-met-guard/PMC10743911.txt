MULTIPLEX HR-HPV GENOTYPING IN CERVICAL PRECURSOR LESIONS BY LAB DEVELOPED TEST (LDT)

Suseela Kodandapani, N Lavanya, Harveen Gulati, Sujatha, T Subramaniyeshwara Rao; Department of Pathology, Molecular Oncology and Surgical Oncology, Basavatarakam Indo American Cancer Hospital and Research Institute, Hyderabad, Telangana, India

Background: High risk-HPVs, are ~14 genotypes, cause several types of cancer. HPV infection prevalence in young adult females < 30 years is 30% - 80%. The time lag between HPV infection and cancer occurrence is 2-4 decades, making them an appropriate target for screening and early detection to implement preventive measures. Present HPV detection methods are costlier and specific genotyping is available only for 6 genotypes. So, we developed LDT of MULTIPLEX PCR initially for HPV 16 & 18, extending it to 17 more HPV genotypes. Methodology: By using HPV-type specific primers conventional PCR was performed on HPV DNA from LBC samples. This is a multiplex PCR, where in one PCR testing 19 genotypes can be detected by using E6/E7 primers, on gel electrophoresis, confirmed by Sanger sequencing. LDT multiplex PCR could detect co-positive HPV genotypes also. Results: Previously we did a pilot study of 100 cervical precursor lesions using LDT only HPV 16 and 18 were detected (26.9% & 5.4%), with a sensitivity of 92.8% and specificity of 96.4% respectively. LDT was extended and standardized further for 12 HR-HPV and 4 LR-HPV and studied on 66 samples. HR-HPV genotypes detected were 16, 18, 33, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68 and LR-HPV were 6, 11, 42, 43 & 44. Of the 21 positive cases, 17 cases came for screening and 4 were post-treatment cases. Co-positive HPV genotypes found along with HR-HPV 16 and 18 were 56, 66, 68 and LR-HPV genotypes were 42, 43& 44. Conclusion: It’s an ongoing study, once completed this HR-HPV multiplex PCR test can be offered as an HPV screening test for a wide range of populations as a cost-effective and sensitive test done with a short turnaround time.

COMPARATIVE STUDY OF RAPID, ECONOMIC ACETIC ACID, PAPANICOLAOU (REAP) OVER CONVENTIONAL PAPANICOLAOU STAIN IN ORAL CYTOLOGY

Tummidi Santosh1, Soutrik Sur1, Santosh Kumar Mondal1, Gitismita Naik2, Debanshu Ghosh3, Deepika Pandey1, Ajay Mallick3; 1Department of Pathology and Lab Medicine, AIIMS, Kalyani, West Bengal, India, 2Department of Community and Family Medicine, AIIMS, Kalyani, West Bengal, India, 3Department of ENT, AIIMS, Kalyani, West Bengal, India

Introduction: Most of the studies on the efficacy of rapid staining have been done in the case of cervical cancer screening. There is less information in the literature to establish a conclusive statement on the efficacy of REAP in oral cytology. Aims and Objective: Our objectives will be to assess the efficacy of REAP stain in oral cytology as an alternative to conventional Papanicolaou stain. Comparing the cost-effectiveness of the staining procedures, intensity of nuclear and cytoplasmic staining of oral smears, visibility of cytoplasmic, nuclear and chromatin structures in both the staining procedures and long-term (6 months) color preservation. Materials and Methods: Study design - Our study was a cross-sectional, prospective study. Oral scrape cytology smears were collected by a trained pathologist, and two slides were prepared from every patient (PAP stain and REAP stain). Interpretation was done for nuclear and cytological staining quality was done. Results: The mean age of presentation was 48 years (4th decade) and with male predominance (M:F::3:1). Tobacco intake (47%) was the most common addiction. Tongue was the most common site (18%) to be involved in the study. Burning sensation was noted in 76% cases. Whitish lesion with irregular margins was seen in 48% of cases. The REAP stain was superior in quality for cytoplasmic and nuclear features (3+ →:well stained in REAP). Bacterial colonies were seen in 38% of cases followed by candida in 13%. 55% of cases were benign and only 2.1% of cases were malignant. Discussion and Conclusion: Rapid, Economic, Acetic acid Papanicolaou stain is advantageous over conventional Papanicolaou stain because it is cost-effective and rapid. It can be used as a better alternative to conventional Papanicolaou stain in oral cytological studies.

A CYTOMORPHOLOGICAL SPECTRUM OF BENIGN THYROID LESIONS – ONE YEAR CASE STUDY

Swarnima Raturi, Purvi Patel, Hansa Goswami; Department of Pathology, B. J. Medical College and Civil Hospital, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India

Introduction: Thyroid lesions are common in India with female predominance. The incidence of clinically apparent thyroid swellings is about 4-5%. It is difficult to distinguish between neoplastic and non-neoplastic thyroid lesions clinically. Excising all thyroid lesions is impracticable. FNAC being reliable, is often used to evaluate thyroid lesions along with clinical and radiological examinations. The clinical value of FNAC is based on its ability to identify benign thyroid lesions. Thereby, reducing the overall thyroidectomy rate. Benign thyroid cytopathology results are divided into Benign follicular nodules, thyroiditis and other entities according to TBSRTC. The most common lesion is Benign follicular nodule. Nodular Goiter is the most commonly sampled lesion by FNAC. Aim: To report the cytomorphological spectrum of benign thyroid lesions according to age and gender. Materials and Methods: This study was conducted in Civil Hospital, Ahmedabad for a duration of one year. Patients of all age and both genders were included. Before FNAC, proper history, clinical examination, and other concerned investigations were noted. Most common complaint was neck swelling. Under aseptic precautions, FNAC was performed. Gross appearance of the aspirate was noted. Squash smears were prepared, fixed with methanol and stained with H&E and PAP stain. Some were air dried and stained with MGG stain. The slides were examined microscopically. Reporting was done according to TBSRTC. Results: Among 169 cases, 88.7% were females. Most commonly affected age group was 41-50 years (23.6%). Most common lesion was benign follicular nodule which accounted for 75.1% of all cases. Conclusion: FNAC can give accurate diagnosis with minimum complications and avoids unnecessary surgeries. It differentiates solid from cystic lesions. It can be recommended as the single effective sensitive tool for majority of benign thyroid lesions.

THE CYTO-HISTOLOGICAL CORRELATION AND RISK STRATIFICATION OF SALIVARY GLAND NEOPLASM DIAGNOSED BY CYTOLOGY WITH APPLICATION OF THE MILAN SYSTEM AND UTILISATION OF A PANEL OF IMMUNOCYTOCHEMISTRY MARKERS

Pooja Dwivedi1, Madhu Kumar1, Manish Chandra2; 1Department of Pathology, King George’s Medical University, Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh, India, 2Department of Otorhinolaryngology, King George’s Medical University, Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: Salivary gland tumours are not very common. These comprise nearly 2-6.5% of the head and neck region tumours. Salivary gland tumours are known to have a heterogenous profile with variable clinical presentation, histopathology and malignancy rate. Aims and Objectives: Application of MILAN SYSTEM for salivary gland cytology. Utility of a panel of immunocytochemistry markers in diagnosing salivary gland neoplasms with cyto-histological correlation and risk stratification. Materials and Methods: This was a prospective study carried out in which a total of 60 patients were enrolled in the study. FNAC smears, cell blocks prepared with standard techniques and staining procedures. Immunocytochemistry (ICC) was performed on the cell block. Immunocytochemical stains p63, CK, PAN-CK, CD117, S-100 and SOX-10 were used for differentiation of lesions. Histopathology was studied if the patient underwent for excision of salivary gland lesions. Discussion and Results: Almost 60 cases were studied under FNAC and cell block evaluation as well as ICC, among those cell blocks of patients, 5 (8.33%) were inadequate, 8 (13.33%) were non-neoplastic, 27 (45%) were benign, 0 were neoplasms of uncertain malignancy potential, 1 (1.67%) was suspected of malignancy and 19 (31.67%) were malignant. Histopathological diagnosis was confirmed in 47 cases. Out of these 24 (51.06%) were benign and 23 (48.93%) were malignant. Malignancy rate for Milan Category I, II, III, IVa, IVb, V and VI was 0%, 0%, 100%, 24%, 50%, 80% and 84.6% respectively. The study showed that malignancy risk stratification could be further improvised by using cell block with ICC as a complementary diagnostic modality. Conclusion: The present study was carried out to assess the usefulness of the Milan System for reporting salivary gland cytology results. The findings of this study with ICC use showed that the Milan system is useful in malignancy risk stratification in salivary gland tumours.

CYTOMORPHOLOGICAL STUDY OF SACRAL LESIONS WITH HISTOPATHOLOGICAL CORRELATION: FIVE YEAR EXPERIENCE IN A TERTIARY CANCER INSTITUTE

G Sabarish Krishnan, Samanta Satarupa, Majal Shah, Priti Trivedi; Department of Oncopathology, Gujarat Cancer Research Institute, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India

Introduction: Primary bone tumours and metastatic bone disease can occur in the sacrum. Radiologic findings on this site are often not specific enough. FNAC as a first-line investigation can help in differentiating primary benign and malignant bone tumours from metastatic deposits. Aim: To analyse cytomorphological features of sacral lesions and to correlate cytology with corresponding histopathology. Materials and Methods: Cases of sacral lesions from the past five years (2018-23) were retrieved from hospital medical records. Only cases with adequate material both for cytological and histopathological diagnosis were included. Radiological findings, demographic details, and past history of known malignancies were retrieved. Results: Our cases ranged in age from 3 to 69 years. Out of a total of 19 cases, there were 11 females and 8 males. A single case signed out as a dermoid cyst on cytology was diagnosed as an enterogenous cyst on histopathology. Two cases diagnosed as Giant cell-rich lesions on cytology were signed out as Giant cell tumours on histopathology. Six cases diagnosed as chordoma on cytology were diagnosed as the same on histopathology after immunohistochemistry (IHC). A single case diagnosed as chondroid neoplasm on cytology was signed out as chondrosarcoma on histopathology. Out of the four cases, which were diagnosed as malignant round cell tumour (MRCT) on cytology, two cases were signed out as Ewing sarcoma, one case as BCOR-CCNB3 rearranged sarcoma and one case as round cell undifferentiated sarcoma on histopathology after IHC. Three cases diagnosed as metastatic carcinoma were signed out as same on histopathology. A single case diagnosed as a malignant germ cell tumour on cytology was diagnosed as a yolk sac tumour on histopathology. A single case signed out as suspicious for malignancy on cytology was diagnosed as metastatic leiomyosarcoma after IHC. Conclusion: Hence, Cytology when correlated with available clinical and radiological details can help in clinching the diagnosis of sacral lesions.

A SYSTEMATIC REVIEW OF CYTOMORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES OF SALIVARY GLAND LESIONS BASED ON THE MILAN SYSTEM OF REPORTING IN A TERTIARY CARE CENTER AND COMPARISON WITH THE HISTOMORPHOLOGICAL FINDINGS IN NEOPLASTIC LESIONS

Aayushi Guru, Shambhavi, Parul Rajpoot; Department of Pathology, Shyam Shah Medical College, Rewa, Madhya Pradesh, India

Background: Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of the salivary glands is a well-established technique that aids in pre-operative identification of abnormalities and to distinguish between neoplastic and non-neoplastic salivary gland lesions. Milan system for reporting of salivary gland cytology uses a standardized tiered system for categorization of salivary gland lesions. Aims and Objectives: The present study aims to classify the FNAC of a patient with a salivary gland lesion into the Milan system and to see how well it correlates with the final HPE report of the excised specimen of the neoplastic lesion (wherever possible) received would invariably be subjected too. Materials and Methods: The present prospective analytical study was done in the Department of Pathology, Shyam Shah Medical College, Rewa, M.P. A total of 84 FNAC was performed, examined, and classified according to the Milan system and histopathological findings wherever possible are correlated. Results: In the present study, a total of 84 cases of salivary gland lesions were included and categorized under the Milan system of salivary gland cytology. 5.9% of the smears in our study were non-diagnostic (Category I). The most common category was IVa comprising benign neoplastic lesions with 36.8%. Non-neoplastic lesions (Category II) were seen in 25% of cases, whereas 14% of the lesions belonged to category IVb (suspicious for malignancy). Malignant lesions (Category VI) comprised 8.3% of all the cases. The overall concordance rate between the cytological and histopathological diagnoses was 83% (70 out of 84 cases), with the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value at 75%, 96%, 85%, and 91%, respectively. Conclusion: The most common and least common category of salivary gland lesion was Category IVa and Category IVb, respectively. The adoption of the Milan classification system for reporting salivary gland FNAC is a critical step in categorizing these lesions for risk stratification and enhancing the communication among clinicians and pathologists, the ultimate result being improved patient care and management.

CORRELATION OF CYTOLOGY, BIOCHEMISTRY AND CBNAAT IN PLEURAL FLUID DIAGNOSIS

Garima Yadav, Sudha Iyengar, Rajesh Gaur; Department of Pathology, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Effusions are classified clinically as transudative or exudative. In India, the most common cause of pleural effusion is Tuberculosis followed by malignant effusion and a very few due to synpneumonic effusion. Aims and Objectives: To determine the diagnostic utility of CBNAAT, cytological, biochemical parameters in the evaluation of pleural effusions and find the most common non-malignant cause of the effusion. Materials and Methods: A retrospective study done in GRMC Gwalior of 100 cases on those meeting inclusion criteria. After consent, pleural fluid samples collected were sent for CBNAAT, biochemistry (protein, sugar, LDH) and cytology. Exudate Effusions were classified as Reactive, Tubercular, Malignant and Bacterial infection. Results: Out of 100 cases studied, the majority were exudates (76%) than transudates (24%). Out of exudates, most patients showed reactive effusion (45%), followed by CBNAAT-positive cases of Tuberculosis (17%), then malignancy (9%) were least were due to infectious causes (bacterial)- 5% in 18-95 years age group and male preponderance. Multiple symptoms were observed, with various symptoms found overlapping. Cough was most commonly seen, followed by fever, dyspnea, weight loss and loss of appetite. Reactive effusion showed mainly mild effusion, Malignancy showed severe effusion while Tuberculosis showed moderate effusion. Right effusion (46%) was more common than left effusion (38%). CBNAAT was positive in only 17% of cases. Lymphocytic predominance was seen in 88% of cases. Conclusion: Our study demonstrated that the most common cause of pleural effusion in our setting was Reactive effusion, followed by Tuberculosis. All three diagnostic techniques- CBNAAT, cytology and biochemistry should be used in tandem with clinical history and examination, to arrive at a diagnosis, so proper and timely treatment can be started.

UTILITY OF ENDOBRONCHIAL ULTRASOUND GUIDED TRANSBRONCHIAL NEEDLE ASPIRATION (EBUS-TBNA) CYTOLOGY IN THE DIAGNOSIS OF MEDIASTINAL LYMPHADENOPATHY

Amrapali Samadder, Vandana Raphael, Evarasalin Marbaniang; Department of Pathology, NEIGRIHMS, Shillong, Meghalaya, India

Introduction: Diagnosis of mediastinal lymphadenopathy is a challenging task for all clinicians as numerous benign and malignant conditions can present as a mass lesion at the very site. Recently, the development of Endobronchial Ultrasound-guided Transbronchial Needle Aspiration (EBUS- TBNA) has offered the operator a real-time visualization of the lymph nodes and surrounding structures. Real-time EBUS- TBNA has shown to increase the diagnostic yield in this context. Objective: This descriptive study was conducted to test the utility of EBUS-TBNA cytology in the diagnosis of mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Materials and Methods: Consecutive patients referred for EBUS-TBNA of mediastinal lymphadenopathy when they were clinically/ radiologically diagnosed with at least 1 mediastinal lymphadenopathy>1 cm on CT scan were included in the study. Rapid On-Site Evaluation (ROSE) was done at the time of the procedure using toluidine blue. Cytological smears deemed to be adequate were sent for routine staining by May Grunwald Giemsa staining (MGG) and Papanicolaou staining (PAP) and examined by cytopathologists. Biopsy samples were also taken from the same site and examined separately by the histopathologists. The results of the TBNA and TBNB samples were compared. Results: Thirty- five patients (24 males, 11 females) of mean age 52 years (Range- 16-82 years) underwent EBUS- TBNA. The procedure was diagnostic in 24 patients (68.6%); positive samples were 20 (10 infectious, 10 malignant), negative samples were 4 (1 benign, 3 reactive) and inadequate samples were obtained in 11 patients. Both the cytological and histopathological results matched in 21 patients (60%), the cytopathological study was inadequate but histopathology showed confirmed diagnosis in 4 cases (11%), the adequate cytopathological diagnosis was given but histopathology showed an inadequate sample in 3 cases (9%) and both the studies showed inadequate samples in 7 cases (20%). In these 35 cases, ROSE was performed in 15 cases, out of which only 1 case was reported as inadequate, whereas in the rest 20 cases, 8 cases were reported as inadequate. Conclusion: EBUS-TBNA is a safe and reliable method for sampling and early diagnosis of mediastinal lymphadenopathy and the use of ROSE increases diagnostic yield.

ROBINSON’S CYTOLOGICAL GRADING OF INVASIVE DUCTAL BREAST CARCINOMA AND CORRELATION WITH HISTOPATHOLOGICAL GRADING IN A TERTIARY CARE CENTRE: A FIVE YEARS RETROSPECTIVE STUDY

Khushboo Sinha, Sudha Iyengar, Reema Bhushan, Rajesh Gaur; Department of Pathology, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women in Indian women accounting for 14% of all cancers in women. The incidence rates in India began to rise in the third decade and peaked at ages 50-64 years. Aim: To study Invasive Ductal Breast Carcinoma, Robinson’s cytological grading on preoperative FNAC smears in cases of breast carcinoma and correlate it with modified Bloom Richardson histopathological grading. Materials and Methods: A retrospective 5-year study from June 2018 to June 2023 was carried out in the Department of Pathology, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior. FNA was performed from different sites of the breast lump and smears were stained with Giemsa and Papanicolaou stain and evaluated for cytological grading according to Robinson grading system. Paraffin-embedded, hematoxylin and eosin-stained slides were evaluated for histological grading with the Modified Bloom-Richardson grading method. Results: In this study, as per Robinson’s grade majority of the cases belong to grade II (50.8%) with 176 cases next was grade I (32.5%) with 112 cases followed by grade III (16.7%) with 58 cases. Histological Grade according to Nottingham modification of Scarff Bloom Richardson grading majority of tumors were grade II tumors (56.83%) with 129 cases, 50 cases were under grade I (22.03%) and 48 cases were classified as grade III (21.14%). The concordance rate was high in grade II (87.5%) with absolute concordance being 78% (p< 0.005). Most of the tumors were in the postmenopausal and perimenopausal women with mean age of presentation was 51.3 years. Conclusion: Robinson’s cytological grading in fine needle aspiration smears in breast carcinoma is the simpler, feasible grading method and can be used as a prognostic factor for better management of patients. This provides relevant information regarding the aggressiveness of tumor and to plan treatment preoperatively.

THE ROLE OF USG AND CT-GUIDED FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY (FNAC) IN DIAGNOSING MALIGNANT SPACE OCCUPYING LESIONS (SOL) OF THE LIVER

Yengkhom Daniel Singh, Khuraijam Sucheta Devi; Department of Pathology, Regional Institute of Medical Sciences, Imphal, Manipur, India

Introduction: The liver is a common site for primary and secondary tumors. The primary indication of fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of the liver is a diagnosis of single or multiple space-occupying lesions (SOL). The adjunct of USG and CT-guided FNACs have added to the armoury for diagnosing hepatic SOLs with negligible complications. Aim/Objective: This study aims to evaluate the role of USG and CT-guided FNACs in delineating primary versus metastatic malignancies of the liver in our region. Materials and Methods: We conducted a hospital-based prospective study in the Department of Pathology for a period of 3 years, enrolling a sum of 49 cases who either underwent USG or CT-guided FNAC of the liver. Conventional smear staining procedures were performed using May Grunwald Giemsa (MGG) stain. Observation/Results: A total of 49 guided FNACs were performed, which comprised of 6 CT-guided and 43 USG-guided FNACs among 26 (53.06%) males and 23 (46.94%) females. The mean age of all the patients was 57.8 years (Males = 57.85 years; Females = 57.74 years). A total of 35 (71.43%) malignant, 7 (14.29%) benign and 1 (2.04%) high-grade dysplastic lesions were diagnosed. However, 6 (12.24%) FNACs were deemed inadequate/undiagnostic. The number of Metastatic malignancies was equal to Primary malignant liver neoplasms, comprising 17 (34.7%) cases each and Primary Hepatocellular carcinoma constituted 15 (30.6%) cases. Conclusion: It is possible to render a cytopathological diagnosis in sufficiently sampled lesions. However, difficulties still arise in delineating Dysplastic Nodule and Hepatic Adenoma from a Well Differentiated Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC). Similarly, it is a challenge to differentiate Poorly Differentiated HCC from a Metastatic malignancy. USG/CT-guided FNAC is a well-established diagnostic modality for patients with suspected primary and secondary malignancies.

EVALUATION OF THE MILAN SYSTEM FOR REPORTING SALIVARY GLAND CYTOPATHOLOGY (MSRSGC): 5-YEAR RETROSPECTIVE STUDY IN A TERTIARY CARE CENTRE

Rishabh Chotrani, Sudha Iyengar, Rajesh Gaur; Department of Pathology, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) is the most effective, minimally invasive procedure for evaluation of salivary gland lesions. The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (MSRSGC) developed in 2018 and recently revised in 2023 has overcome challenges posed by the lack of a standardized, tiered diagnostic framework by which salivary gland FNA were reported. It is widely used for diagnosis and management according to the risk of malignancy (ROM) in different categories. Aim: To study the spectrum of salivary gland cytology and assess the accuracy along with the risk of malignancy by classifying lesions using the revised Milan system (2023) and to correlate with histopathological findings. Materials and Methods: In this retrospective study, FNAC performed for all salivary gland lesions over a period of five years were retrieved. All cases were categorized according to MSRSGC and compared to histopathological follow-up during the period from 1st August 2018 to 31st July 2023. Results: A total of 226 cases were evaluated cytologically, and histological follow-up was done in available cases. The distribution of cases into different categories was as follows: Category I – 8 (3.5%), Category II– 94 (41.6%), Category III– 4 (1.7%), Category IV–92 (40.7%), Category V–3 (1.5%) and Category VI-25 (11%). Maximum cases were found in category II (non-neoplastic) diagnosed as sialadenitis. Malignant cases were mostly seen in males in the age group of 60-70, with mucoepidermoid carcinoma being the most common case. Conclusion: Application of MSRSGC has immense value for standardization of reporting of salivary gland FNAC leading to better clinical and surgical management. Our data corresponds to the studies done worldwide and recommends the use of MSRSGC for future diagnostic purposes.

ULTRASOUND GUIDED FNAC-SPECTRUM OF LESIONS IN 3 YEARS

Richa Pathak, Prakriti Gupta, Sudha Iyengar, Rajesh Gaur; Department of Pathology, Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Ultrasound-guided Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) is a safe, simple, rapid and cost-effective technique which yields better diagnostic material from lesions which are deep-seated, small, vaguely palpable and are in close proximity to major vasculature in comparison to routine fine needle aspiration procedure. Aim: To study the spectrum of lesions at different sites with the usage of Ultrasound (USG) guided FNAC. Materials and Methods: This retrospective study was conducted in the Department of Pathology at Gajra Raja Medical College, Gwalior M.P. The study included all samples of USG-guided FNACs of different organs which were received at the Cytology section of our department over a period of Three years from August 2020 to July 2023. Results: A total of 408 USG-guided FNAC were performed over a period of 3 years, out of which 198 (49%) were Males and 201 (51%) were females. Cases were categorised as Inconclusive, inflammatory, benign tumors, suspicious for Malignancy and malignant lesion and their rates were 16.4%, 23.5%, 9.8%, 1%, and 46.5%, respectively. The most common site was the Lymph node (23.5%), followed by the liver (13.7%), gallbladder (13.5%) and lung (13.5%). Conclusion: FNAC coupled with a diagnostic imaging technique such as USG or Computed Tomography Scan improves the diagnostic yield and a definite diagnosis can be reached in most of the cases. Our study supports the use of guided FNAC in deep-seated lesions of various anatomic sites as it is a simple and minimally invasive tool in rapid evaluation and early diagnosis for better and timely management of deep-seated and unapproachable lesions.

STUDY OF CYTOLOGICAL FINDINGS OF LYMPHOCYTIC THYROIDITIS AND ITS CORRELATION WITH ANTI-TPO ANTIBODY LEVEL AND TSH LEVELS

Shreya Pandey, Prakash Dive, S Sudhamani; Department of Pathology, Dr. D.Y. Patil School of Medicine, Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

Introduction: Lymphocytic thyroiditis is an autoimmune disorder characterized by lymphocytic infiltration of the thyroid gland. It is the second most common thyroid disorder diagnosed on FNAC. It is frequently seen in women. It can be associated with hypothyroidism, euthyroidism, and even hyperthyroidism. Background: Micronucleus (MN) is a small extranuclear body found within the cytoplasm of cell which can be identified by light microscopy. It is formed during cell division due to failure of incorporation of whole chromosomes or their fragments and acts as a biomarker of chromosomal breakage, genome instability and cancer risk. Cytological analysis of fluids is a cost-effective technique helping in evaluating the cause of effusion. MN scoring can be done to identify malignant effusions. The number of micronuclei was counted per 1000 cells by microscopy under oil immersion (X1000) in the Giemsa stained slides and scoring was done. The following criteria was used for the detection of Micronuclei:[1] 1)Diameter of MN - around 1/3 to 1/16 of diameter of the primary nucleus. 2)Shape, color, and texture of MN should be similar to those of nucleus. 3)Should be in the same plane of focus as main nucleus. 4)Round to oval shaped with no actual contact with nucleus. 5)Intensity of staining similar to that of nucleus. 6)Cells lying singly were preferred. Smears with necrotic cells, degenerated cells and overlapping cells were exempted from counting. Cells without cytoplasm were not evaluated. Mimickers such as apoptotic fragments, superimposed lymphocytes and staining artifacts were excluded. It took around 15-20 mins per slide screening. Results and Observations: Quantitative variable such as number of micronucleated cells were expressed as mean with standard deviation. Differences in mean value between the groups were tested for statistical significance by student t-test and it was found to be statistically significant. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was done to determine the cut-off point between the two groups. Conclusion: MN scoring is simple, cost-effective easy means for the evaluation of fluids. There is a proportional relationship between MN counts and malignancy in cytological samples of pleural and ascitic fluid effusions.
