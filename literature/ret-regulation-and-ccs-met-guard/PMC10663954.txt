For this purpose, genomic data obtained from Illumina iSelect 4K quail SNP chip were utilized. After implementing various quality control steps, genotype data from a total of 875 birds for 2,015 SNP markers were used for subsequent analyses. The Bayesian analyses were performed using hibayes package in R (version 4.3.1) and Gibbs sampling algorithm. The results of the analyses showed that Bayes A accounted for 11.43, 11.65, and 11.39% of the phenotypic variance for body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio, respectively, while the variance explained by Bayes B was 7.02, 8.61, and 6.48%, respectively. Therefore, in the current study, results obtained from Bayes A were used for further analyses. In order to perform the gene enrichment analysis and to identify the functional pathways and classes of genes that are over-represented in a large set of genes associated with each trait, all markers that accounted for more than 0.1% of the phenotypic variance for each trait were used. The results of this analysis revealed a total of 23, 38, and 14 SNP markers associated with body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio in Japanese quail, respectively. The results of the gene enrichment analysis led to the identification of biological pathways (and candidate genes) related to lipid phosphorylation (TTC7A gene) and cell junction (FGFR4 and FLRT2 genes) associated with body weight gain, calcium signaling pathway (ADCY2 and CAMK1D genes) associated with feed intake, and glycerolipid metabolic process (LIPC gene), lipid metabolic process (ADGRF5 and ESR1 genes), and glutathione transferase (GSTK1 gene) associated with feed conversion ratio. Overall, the findings of this study can provide valuable insights into the genetic architecture of growth and feed consumption traits in Japanese quail.

## INTRODUCTION
According to FAO, total world food demand will increase by 35 to 56% between 2010 and 2050 and the world population is expected to reach 9 billion by 2050 ( ). The greatest global challenge for this and the next generations is to produce enough food to eradicate hunger, amid shrinking arable land, climate change and pressures on natural resources. To overcome these challenges, sustainable strategies must be put in place to produce and supply enough nutritious and safe food for a growing population, and the exploration of alternative protein sources has gained considerable attention, with a focus on animal breeding. Among the various animal species, the Japanese quail has emerged as a promising candidate due to its economic attractiveness and unique characteristics, making it a suitable model for studying poultry-related traits. Quail has been recognized as one of the most promising species for future income-earning opportunities worldwide ( ) and has the potential to significantly contribute to long term sustainable food production ( ).
Recently, a variety of statistical methods have been proposed to infer the genomic regions and causal genes associated with economically important traits in different species ( ). Within this context, Bayesian approaches have been shown to yield higher accuracies than those produced by linear models and it is indicated that some Bayesian models are proper, primarily due to their ability to more appropriately model the architecture of quantitative trait loci (QTL) effects within the genome ( ). Bayesian methods hold substantial influence on animal breeding, for several reasons. First, in the context of genome-wide association study (GWAS), where the number of markers often exceeds the number of observations, traditional least squares methods fall short in estimating marker effects accurately. In such scenarios, Bayesian models, capable of simultaneously incorporating multiple markers, provide a solution to this challenge ( ). Second, the Bayesian framework allows for the consideration of varying marker variances, a more realistic assumption compared to other methodologies. This flexibility enhances the model's capability to capture the complexity of genetic effects ( ). Third, Bayesian methods provide a means to assess and quantify uncertainty in all unknowns, offering a comprehensive understanding of the underlying genetic architecture ( ).
The performance of 2 widely and complementary Bayesian methods including Bayes A ( ) and Bayes B ( ) were evaluated in the current study. The main differences between these models are assumptions concerning the distributions of genomic marker effects. While the traditional linear models assume that all SNPs effects draw from the same normal distribution and hence all markers have small effects ( ), Bayes A assumes that the variances of marker effects are different and some SNPs have a large effect ( ) and the prior distribution in BayesB assumes the variance of markers to equal zero with probability π, and the complement with probability (1 − π) follows an inverse distribution ( ). investigated the performance of Bayes A and B methods to identify genomic regions and candidate genes associated with egg weight in Rhode Island Red. Their results revealed that these methods can be successfully used for detecting genomic regions associated with this trait. Nine markers were reported to be associated with egg weight in this study, located on chromosomes 1, 3, 5, and 20, and the candidate genes that were overlapped with these regions of interest were identified to be related in the egg production process through the albumen protein formation, fatty acids metabolism, and eggshell formation.
Body weight and feed consumption traits are the most important traits in quail, therefore identification of genomic loci for these traits can be essential to enhance genetic improvement by marker-assisted selection ( ). Therefore, the article aims to use a genome-wide association study (GWAS) based on Bayesian statistical methods to identify genomic loci and candidate genes associated with body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio in Japanese quail. The integration of Bayesian-based GWAS and bioinformatics analysis that performed in the current study offers a comprehensive and powerful approach to understand the genetic architecture and biological mechanisms associated with these economically important traits in Japanese quail.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Animal Samples, Traits Records, and Genotyping
The data used in this study included phenotypic and genotypic information related to feed intake (FI), body weight gain (BWG) between 10 and 15 d of age, and feed conversion ratio (FCR) of a large F2 cross with 875 Japanese quail individuals that have been previously described by details ( ). In summary, founder eggs were obtained from birds of 2 lines (line A and B), which took place at INRA, Nouzilly (France). Twelve males from founder line A (B) were mated to 12 females from founder line B (A) to produce the F1 generation. From this generation, 17 males and 34 females were selected, and 1 male was mated with 2 females, resulting in 875 F2 individuals. These individuals belonged to 34 full-sib families and 17 paternal half-sib families, with approximately the same family size. Rearing and feeding of the quails was divided into 3 periods. In period 1, hatchlings were weighed on the first day of life and raised in groups in floor pens until they were transferred on d 8 of life to the metabolic cages. Period 2 was an adaption period to the cages until d 10. From d 10 to 15 (period 3) individual feed intake was measured and total excreta were sampled. At d 15 the animals were slaughtered. At the beginning and the end of the excreta collection period, on d 10 and 15, respectively, the quails were weighed and body weight gain (BWG) in this period was calculated. Feed conversation ration (FCR) was calculated as feed intake (FI) within this 5-day period divided by BWG. Descriptive statistical parameters and heritabilities are provided in . The heritabilities of the traits were estimated by using mixed linear animal models. 
One mL blood sample was collected from each bird using EDTA-K tubes, and DNA extraction was performed using DNA Purification Kit (Promega). The genotyping of the samples was conducted using the 4K Illumina iSelect chip. In the present study, the genotyping data were first subjected to data mining and editing for quality control. Initially, animals with a genotype call rate of less than 99% were excluded from further analysis. Next, from 3,986 SNP markers available, the SNP markers with a minor allele frequency (MAF) of less than 0.01, genotype call rates below 99%, and outlier departure from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (P value <10−5) were excluded from subsequent analysis. Furthermore, the SNPs on the sex chromosomes Z or W and in the linkage group (LG) LGE22C19W28_E50C23 or E64 (information obtained from the C. japonica reference genome assembly (NCBI GCA_001577835.1)) were also rejected. The PLINK software version 1.9 was used for quality control purposes. This filtering resulted in a total of 2,015 SNP markers of 875 samples for further analysis. All of the 875 birds (including 428 males and 447 females) used in the present study successfully passed through various stages of quality control. The principal component analysis (PCA) was employed to identify how birds allocated to their population in this study. PCA was performed using the prcomp function in the R version 4.0.3 package (http://cran.r-project.org), which considers the total variance in the data and alters the original variables into a smaller set of linear compounds. After data editing, the data were converted to binary format using R software version 4.0.3 for further analysis.

## Whole-Genome Association Analysis Based on Bayesian Methods
Various Bayesian approaches can be employed in GWAS ( ). In the Bayesian method A, a conditional distribution is considered for marker effects, which follows a t-distribution. The t distribution resembles a normal curve, but it is more “spread out” because there is more uncertainty in the t distribution and the probability of identifying markers with large effects increases. This distribution leads to a multistage model: the first stage at the level of SNP effects and the second stage at the level of variance of SNP effects. To perform the aforementioned analyses in the current study, the hibayes package ( ) in R (version 4.3.1) was used along with the Gibbs sampling algorithm for posterior distribution inference of marker effects ( ). Marker effects were inferred using Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) with 41,000 iterations (1,000 burn-in iterations and 40,000 subsequent iterations) ( ). Finally, to depict the significant marker distributions at the genome level, Manhattan plots were generated for different traits using the SNPEVG software version 2.3 ( ).

## Gene Annotation and Gene Enrichment Analysis (GEA)
Genes located in the genomic regions overlapped with identified significant SNPs associated with different traits were obtained from the Ensembl genome browser using BioMart software (https://www.ensembl.org/biomart/) based on the latest reference assembly of Japanese Quail genome assembly (Coturnix_japonica_2.0). Gene function was determined using Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM) (at http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/omim/) and other online databases such as GeneCards (http://www.genecards.org) and UniProtKB (http://www.uniprot.org) were also utilized.
In our study, the phenotypic variance of each trait and the proportion of the phenotypic variance explained by each SNP, estimated using HiBayes package in R v. 4.3.1 ( ). Next, for the gene enrichment analysis, all the SNPs that explained more than 0.1% of the phenotypic variation for the respective traits were identified, and then a search was conducted using the Galgal6 reference genome of chicken for all the genes reported in the regions of interest using Genome Data Viewer (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/gdv/) database. It is useful to compare regions of interest in quail to the corresponding areas in chicken, as the chicken genome is better annotated. To identify corresponding homologous genes or orthologous regions in the quail genome and chicken genome, we performed a BLAT search using the UCSC Genome Browser on the Chicken Mar. 2018 (Galgal6) Assembly as a reference (https://genome.ucsc.edu/cgi-bin/hgBlat). We used quail genomic sequences as queries and aligned them against the chicken genome to pinpoint regions with high sequence similarity using BLAT search. BLAT on DNA is designed to quickly find sequences of 95% and greater similarity of length 25 bases or more (https://genome.ucsc.edu/). Nearby genes within a flanking distance of 500 kb from each region were acquired. This distance has been selected as previously considered by and . The GO2MSIG software ( ) was employed to analyze the gene functional categories and metabolic pathways. GO2MSIG integrates information from multiple databases such as Gene Ontology (http://www.geneontology.org/), KEGG pathways (http://www.genome.jp/kegg), Panther (http://www.pantherdb.org) ( ), and DAVID annotation (https://www.david.ncifcrf.gov/) ( ).

## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The PCA scatterplots of the birds in our study has been shown in . The results revealed that all sampled birds are appropriately grouped, and no evidence of stratification was observed in our study, which is consistent with findings reported by . The first 2 principal components account for approximately 2.1 and 1.9% of the total variance, respectively. 
The key advantages of Bayesian approaches lie in their ability to account for large-scale SNP effects and multiple genes in GWAS using a single joint model, providing a practical solution for estimating numerous unknown parameters ( ). The performance of Bayesian methods A and B was first compared for explaining the phenotypic variance of different traits in this study. The results revealed that Bayes A accounted for higher percentages of phenotypic variance explained than Bayes B across the examined traits, consisting body weight gain (11.43 vs. 7.02%), feed intake (11.65 vs. 8.61%), and feed conversion ratio (11.39 vs. 6.48%). This is likely due to the genetic architecture of the traits and the assumption behind the Bayesian approaches used in the current study. The Bayes A assumes that all SNPs have genetic effects which considers almost an equal share of variance for different markers whereas Bayes B assumes that only a small proportion of SNPs have an effect ( ). With this point in mind that our results revealed a limited amount of phenotypic variation has been explained with a large number of SNP markers, it seems the contribution of each marker to the phenotypic variance of the traits is low and as a result, Bayesian A method better explain the architecture of studied traits. This observation was in agreement with where they reported that the Bayes A method performed better in explaining additive genetic variance for egg weight-related traits in Rhode Island Red breed compared to Bayes B method. The results obtained from Bayesian method A were utilized for further analysis in the study. depicts the Manhattan plots showing the associations for body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio traits using Bayesian method A. 
The BioMart system in ENSEMBL was used to retrieve the gene overlapped with the genomic regions harboring SNPs explained more than 0.1% of phenotypic variance ( ). The results of the analysis revealed that body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio in Japanese quail were associated with 23, 38, and 14 SNP markers, respectively. Our primary results indicated that some regions did not overlap with any annotated gene in Japanese Quail genome assembly. This observation may be due to various factors including the genome assembly version, especially in nonhuman species ( ), and the possibility of insufficient marker density used in our study. However, it is important to note that these genomic regions may encompass noncoding sequences, potentially affecting the regulation of distant target genes, as highlighted in previous studies ( ). Furthermore, although, the medium density of SNP chips has been widely used in different studies in Japanese Quail (e.g., ; ; ), however, it seems the detection of significant SNPs associated with the traits of our interest can be improved by increasing the density of markers and further research on phenotypic associations would help understanding the role of each region.
For the gene enrichment analysis, a search was conducted on the latest genomic version of chicken (Galgal6), as the genome of Japanese Quail is closely related to that of the domestic chicken ( ) and the chicken genome is better annotated. Our results revealed that the regions of interest for body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio traits were overlapped with 84, 156 and 82 genes, respectively. Study of these genes by more details showed that some of those were shared among traits, for instance, 42 genes were common between body weight gain and feed intake traits, following 16 genes shared between body weight gain and feed conversion ratio. This observation was in agreement with the results obtained in previous studies showing high genetic correlation between these traits in Japanese quail ( ; ). Next, to determine the underlying biological functions of the genes overlapped with the regions of interest, the Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was used and the genes were evaluated for their effects on biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF) for each trait. The results obtained from gene enrichment analysis led us to the identification of several biological pathways and different candidate genes directly or indirectly associated with the traits of interest ( ), that some of the pathways (and their corresponding candidate genes) are briefly described functionally in details below. 
The present study discovered the lipid phosphorylation pathway as a significant biological pathway associated with body weight gain ( ). The TTC7A gene included in this pathway has been previously reported to be associated with growth performance traits. reported that TTC7A plays a key role in muscle glycogen in Jingxing yellow chickens. The protein encoded by TTC7A is part of complexes responsible for phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase (PI4K) coding on the plasma membrane. Muscle glycogen and glucose are primary components in the glucose metabolism cycle, contributing to maintaining normal metabolism in skeletal muscle cells. Additionally, glycogenolysis affects meat quality through glycolytic processes. Another significant pathway identified to be associated with body weight gain in the current study is the cell junction pathway, which is a fundamental aspect of cellular physiology. This pathway plays a critical role in connecting the cytoskeleton of one cell to another or to extracellular proteins on the plasma membrane surface. This pathway has also previously reported as one of the most important pathways affecting growth traits in Rhode Island Red ( ). The candidate genes identified within this pathway are FGFR4 and FLRT2. FGFR4 is a receptor tyrosine kinase protein and a member of the FGFR gene family. It serves as a crucial regulator of myogenic differentiation and muscle regeneration after injury ( ; ). Moreover, reported that FGFR4 plays a fundamental role in systemic lipid metabolism and glucose homeostasis.
In terms of feed intake trait, our results revealed the calcium signaling pathway, which has also been previously reported to be associated with this trait ( ). Two candidate genes including ADCY2 and CAMK1D, were identified within this pathway. ADCY2 encodes a protein from the adenylate cyclase family, responsible for catalyzing the formation of cAMP. reported ADCY2 as one of the candidate genes associated with feed intake in Nelore cattle through a genome-wide association study (GWAS). The CAMK1D gene is a member of the protein kinase A and calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CAMK) family and belongs to the serine/threonine kinases subfamily. These proteins involve in various processes, including the regulation of granule exocytosis, transcriptional regulation of CREB-dependent genes, aldosterone synthesis, neutrophil differentiation and activation, and erythroleukemia cell apoptosis ( ). demonstrated that the CAMK1D gene plays a crucial role in feed consumption and the development of obesity in mice.
One important pathway associated with the feed conversion ratio is the glycerolipid metabolism pathway. The LIPC gene as a candidate gene identified in this pathway, encodes a hepatic triacylglycerol lipase (LIPC) that is primarily synthesized and secreted by liver cells, with some production by macrophages and other tissues. This protein plays a significant role in lipoprotein metabolism ( ). It is also considered as a member of the triglyceride lipase family and is involved in the hydrolysis of triglycerides ( ). Hepatic lipase has a crucial role in the metabolism of HDL and LDL cholesterol. Recent GWAS studies have found a relationship between genetic variations in the LIPC gene and HDL cholesterol levels ( ). Another pathway identified in current study related to the feed conversion ratio is the lipid metabolism process. The ADGRF5 gene, which belongs to the G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) gene family, has been identified to be highly expressed in the endothelial cells of various organs in mice in this pathway and reported to be associated with feed efficiency ( ). Another candidate gene in this pathway is ESR1, a member of the ESR gene superfamily, which contains receptors for small molecules such as steroids, thyroid hormones, retinoids, and oxysterols ( ). The ESR gene family is believed to play a key role in regulating processes such as growth, reproduction, and normal physiology ( ). The role of ESR1, along with ESR2, in reproductive performance in poultry has been extensively studied ( ; ). ESR1 is also reported as a candidate gene associated with reproductive and egg-laying traits in chickens ( ). Studies based on GWAS have indicated that the ESR1 gene is an important role to regulate feed efficiency in pigs ( ). Finally, our results identified the glutathione transferase activity pathway as another significant pathway related to the feed conversion ratio. GSTK1 is one of the candidate genes identified in this pathway. GSTK1 is an important component of the glutathione antioxidant system and plays a crucial role in hypoxia adaptation and feed conversation ( ; ). Another genome-wide survey conducted using RNA-Seq technology revealed, GSTK1 as one of the most important genes affecting hypoxia adaptation in chicken embryos (Gallus gallus) ( ).

## CONCLUSIONS
A GWAS based on Bayes A and B statistical methods was applied to identify genomic regions and candidate genes associated with economically important traits of body weight gain, feed intake and feed conversion ratio in Japanese quail. Our results revealed that these methods can be successfully used for detecting genomic regions associated with these traits, although the Bayes A method performed better in explaining phenotypic variance compared to Bayes B. This result shows that the genetic architecture of these traits is such that probably a large number of genes with small effects control these traits. The results of GWAS studies showed that 23, 38, and 14 SNP markers are associated with body weight gain, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio traits in Japanese Quail, respectively. Next, nearby genes within a flanking distance from each region of interest were acquired and combined for functional analysis. The results of gene enrichment analysis uncovered the biological pathways (and candidate genes) related to lipid phosphorylation (TTC7A gene) and cell junction (FGFR4 and FLRT2 genes) associated with body weight gain, calcium signaling pathway (ADCY2 and CAMK1D genes) associated with feed intake, and glycerolipid metabolic process (LIPC gene), lipid metabolic process (ADGRF5 and ESR1 genes), and glutathione transferase (GSTK1 gene) associated with feed conversion ratio. In general, according to the biological function of the pathways identified in this research, it seems that these genes play an important role in the phenotypic variation of these traits in Japanese quail, however, further studies considering a higher sample size and SNP density markers will be needed to confirm our results. Furthermore, some of the pathways and genes that previously reported to be associated with our interested traits were briefly described functionally in the present study, however, many noncoding regions and uncharacterized genes also resided within these regions that cannot be dismissed.
