## Abstract
Kidneys play a crucial role in maintaining homeostasis within the body, and this function is intricately linked to the vascular structures within them. For vascular cells in the kidney to mature and function effectively, a well-coordinated spatial alignment between the nephrons and complex network of blood vessels is essential. This arrangement ensures efficient blood filtration and regulation of the electrolyte balance, blood pressure, and fluid levels. Additionally, the kidneys are vital in regulating the acid-base balance and producing hormones involved in erythropoiesis and blood pressure control. This article focuses on the vascular development of the kidneys, summarizing the current understanding of the origin and formation of the renal vasculature, and the key molecules involved. A comprehensive review of existing studies has been conducted to elucidate the cellular and molecular mechanisms governing renal vascular development. Specific molecules play a critical role in the development of renal vasculature, contributing to the spatial alignment between nephrons and blood vessels. By elucidating the cellular and molecular mechanisms involved in renal vascular development, this study aims to advance renal regenerative medicine and offer potential avenues for therapeutic interventions in kidney disease.
Keywords: kidney development, renal development, renal stem cell, stem cell, vascular, VEGF

## Overview of Renal Development
Mammalian kidney development progresses from the pronephros, through the mesonephros, to the metanephros. The pronephros and mesonephros are temporary organs that function only during embryogenesis, whereas the metanephros are responsible for adult function. The metanephros emerge through complex synthesis involving the ureteric bud (UB), which eventually extends from collecting ducts within the kidney to a portion of the bladder. Metanephric mesenchymal (MM) cells are responsible for nephron formation. Specifically, glial cell line–derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) secreted by MM cells induces the sprouting of UB from the Wolffian duct. Furthermore, on receiving Wnt family member 9b, secreted by the UB, MM cells undergo mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition. Once conversion is initiated, MM cells begin producing their own Wnt4, further promoting the conversion process. Conversely, the UB, which carries the GDNF receptor rearranged during transfection, undergoes branching and elongation in response to stimulation with GDNF secreted by MM cells (Figure , A and B). The iterative process of branching and elongation of the UB, along with the epithelialization of the MM, leads to the formation of the metanephros. As MM proliferate, the epithelialized structure gradually changes its shape. Cells that gather adjacent to the tip of the UB differentiate into nephron progenitor cells, which then differentiate into nephrons. These nephron progenitor cells aggregate to form cap structures. Subsequently, this cap structure forms the renal vesicles. The renal vesicle develops into a comma-shaped body, followed by an S-shaped body. As epithelialization progresses, the precursors undergo morphological changes and evolve into C-shaped and S-shaped bodies. The S-shaped body comprised Bowman's capsule epithelial cells, podocytes, proximal tubules, Henle's loops, and distal tubules (Figure C).
 During the S-shaped stage, podocytes express all isotypes of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and maintain their expression until they mature into glomeruli. The upper part of the S-shaped body becomes a distal tubule that invades the UB. Subsequently, the UB undergoes differentiation into the ductus deferens and ureter, supported by stromal cells. Intricate interactions among these diverse cells contribute to the formation of the complex three-dimensional structure of the kidney.

## Role of SIX2, Wnt, and Foxd1-Positive Cells in Renal Development
The nephron, the renal unit, possesses the capabilities of both self-replication and differentiation, derived from a multipotent SIX2-positive progenitor cell population that maintains a pool of nephron progenitor cells. Various transcription factors, including SIX1, PAX2, WT1, and SALL1, were identified for the specific identification and maintenance of SIX2-positive cells. Therefore, SIX2 is essential for maintaining the precursor-cell state. Several of these transcription factors bind to SIX2 to form complexes related to maintaining the state of nephron progenitor cells. – The lack of SIX2 prevents normal nephron formation and substantially affects renal function. Furthermore, cap mesenchymal cells lacking the transcription factor, SIX2, not only lose their self-renewal capability but also differentiate prematurely into nephrons. , Another transcription factor, EYA1, binds SIX2 and facilitates its translocation into the nucleus. EYA1-deficient cap mesenchymal cells exhibit a phenotype similar to that of SIX2-deficient cells, but fail to maintain the self-renewal capability of nephron progenitors and prematurely differentiate into nephrons. 
The renal stroma, as defined by Foxd1 expression, plays a crucial role in embryonic kidney development in mesenchymal-derived cells. Most stromal cells, excluding smooth muscle covering structures, such as the ureter, are derived from self-replicating multipotent progenitor cells regulated by the transcription factor Foxd1. Distinct pools of nephron (SIX2+) and stromal progenitor cells (Foxd1+) arise from Osr1+ progenitor cells. SIX2+ renal stromal progenitors are surrounded by Foxd1+ cells. At this stage, the fate of the nephrons was determined within the SIX2+ cells. While the fate of most Foxd1+ cells is also determined within the stroma, a small number of cells are believed to transition to SIX2+/Foxd1− nephron progenitor. Furthermore, Foxd1 stromal progenitors is necessary for normal renal arterial system formation. In addition, Foxd1 directs mesenchymal stem cells to differentiate into various renal stromal components, including fibroblasts, pericytes, vascular smooth muscle cells, and glomerular mesangial cells, all of which are essential for kidney function. , 
Wnt11 is genetically essential for kidney ureteric branching morphogenesis. Evidence linking Wnt11 to ureteral morphogenesis suggests that Wnt11 functions in this process. First, Wnt11 is expressed at the tips of the ureteric epithelium where branching morphogenesis occurs, indicating its potential involvement in the regulation of branching morphogenesis. Furthermore, Wnt11 expression is directly correlated with an induced increase or decrease in the tip of the ureter. , Wnt11 is necessary for the development of the collecting duct and is likely part of a reciprocal signaling mechanism between the ureteric epithelium and posterior renal stroma. This regulates normal ureteric branching morphology by modulating the Ret/Gdnf signaling pathway. 
These elements collectively coordinate the timing and location of kidney development and maturation, which are essential for the formation of a healthy kidney.

## Origin of Vascular Development of Kidney and Renal Organoids
The presence of endothelial cell (EC) precursors has been demonstrated in the kidney, with Osr1-positive cells in the early intermediate mesoderm giving rise to flk-1–positive cells, which are the precursors of renal ECs. One proposed concept to explain renal angiogenesis suggests that renal blood vessels are formed by a combination of angiogenesis and vasculogenesis processes. – Blood vessels arise in situ from endogenous endothelial progenitor cells at the periphery of the kidney through vasculogenesis (Figure B). Simultaneously, large vessels and medullary endothelial branches from exogenous sources formed through angiogenesis (Figure A). Finally, these endogenous and exogenous vascular ECs combine to form renal blood vessels (Figure C). More recently, endogenous angiogenesis has been supported by studies identifying ECs and wall cell progenitors internalized within MM. – However, regarding angiogenesis of exogenous origin, the entry of exogenous vessels into the kidney begins between E11.5 and E12.5. – Furthermore, studies involving chimeric mice and interspecies transplants of embryonic kidneys indicated that renal vessels are formed through a combination of angiogenesis and vascularization. , Therefore, renal macrovessels originate from the branches of the vascular plexus of the aorta and vena cava, whereas small vessels appear to differentiate and connect to macrovessels.
 Renal organoids derived from human embryonic stem cells exhibited vascularization in the early stages of culture, but regressed rapidly as culture time progressed. Renal organoids induced from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) successfully differentiate into UB, MM, and vascularization within kidney organoids, including nephrons derived from MM (Figure D). Notably, the UB formation explanation by Takasato et al. 2015 was later confirmed to have generated GATA3+ distal tubules of the nephron, but not the UB. Furthermore, these organoids are transcriptionally similar to human fetal kidney. However, the presence of a lumen was not confirmed in the blood vessels within kidney organoids.
Maggiore et al. successfully produced human kidney organoids by introducing inducible E26 transformation-specific translocation variant 2 into human iPSC lines and combining them with nontransgenic counterparts. These organoids exhibited the closest cellular identity to human renal endothelium and achieved extensive endothelialization. Furthermore, endothelialized kidney organoids demonstrate the formation of mature nephron structures, particularly the development of glomerular and vascular subtype nephrons along with the fenestrated endothelium. 
These studies highlight potential defects in the vasculature of renal organoids and signify progress in comprehending and enhancing the vasculature of cultured kidney tissue. , 

## Understanding and Application of Organoids in Vascular Studies
Renal organoids offer a promising platform for understanding and modeling renal vasculature, providing valuable translational insights into several key areas.
Renal organoids are particularly valuable for studying disease mechanisms. Diseases, such as diabetic nephropathy, in which changes in the renal microvasculature are critical, can be modeled using these platforms to understand how diseases affect the renal vasculature. 
Organoids derived from patient-specific iPSCs play a crucial role in genetic disorder research, enabling researchers to explore the mechanisms underlying diseases that involve genetic mutations affecting the renal vasculature, such as polycystic kidney disease. 
Renal organoids may serve as innovative tools for drug screening. These models allow a more accurate evaluation of drug effects, toxicity, and off-target effects than traditional 2D cell cultures, particularly when screening drugs that target the renal vasculature. 
Renal organoids have promising potential in regenerative medicine. They serve as foundational elements in the bioengineering of renal tissues, supporting the development of functional renal structures that can be integrated into the existing vascular systems post-transplantation. 
Renal organoids provide a powerful tool for studying the renal vasculature in health and disease, facilitating drug development, personalized medicine approaches, and advancements in regenerative therapies for kidney diseases. These models bridge the gap between basic research and clinical applications and pave the way for improving patient outcomes in renal medicine.

## Molecules Related to Renal Vessels

## VEGF
The VEGF pathway is an essential signaling pathway that regulates vascular development. The VEGF signaling pathway comprises subtypes of amino acids 121, 145, 165, 189, and 206, which belong to the VEGF-A, VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, and VEGF-E families. The receptors involved in this pathway include VEGFR-1 (flt-1), VEGFR-2 (KDR/flk-1), and VEGFR-3 (flt-4). , During renal development, VEGF-A is expressed in the MM at early developmental time points and is associated with the expression of endothelial progenitor cell markers. Moreover, Loughna et al. reported the presence of EC precursors in the early stages of mouse kidney formation, highlighting their ability to respond to VEGF-A and participate in the formation of glomerular capillaries. VEGF-A is produced by nephron precursors and serves as a chemoattractant for ECs to enter glomeruli. Mice lacking VEGF-A produced by podocytes exhibit embryonic lethality, reduced ECs, hypodifferentiation, and abnormal glomeruli with degeneration, resulting in renal failure. , Furthermore, drugs targeting VEGF induce proteinuria and glomerular damage, emphasizing the substantial role of VEGF-A signaling in crosstalk between podocytes and ECs, which form functional glomeruli from development to maturity.
Recently, a novel VEGF-A secretion pathway, involving the secretion of VEGF-A through small extracellular vesicle (sEV) within renal tubular cells damaged by AKI, was identified. Renal tubular cell-derived sEV-VEGF-A promotes EC proliferation. Furthermore, transplant therapy using sEV carrying VEGF-A facilitated the repair of peritubular capillaries and inhibited the transition from AKI to CKD. Therefore, there is a potential for new AKI treatments using sEV-VEGF-A.
Receptors found on glomerular ECs and tubular cells are also found on peritubular capillaries. , Understanding the physiological and pathological roles of the factors that regulate angiogenesis in the kidney will lead to the development of new therapeutic strategies for kidney diseases.

## Angiopoietins
In the kidney, Angpt-1, Angpt-2, Tie-1, and Tie-2 are expressed in the early stages of mouse metanephrosis and consist of the UB epithelium and MM. , – The levels of Angpt-1, Angpt-2, Tie-1, and Tie-2 peak shortly after birth, and all four genes are expressed in the adult kidney. Phosphorylated Tie-2 is detected in mouse kidneys during the late stages of pregnancy and persists throughout postnatal development until adulthood. In addition, Tie-2 has been suggested to be crucial for the formation of glomerular capillaries. , 
Angpt-1 is expressed in the renal stromal mesenchyme, differentiated tubular epithelium, and differentiated/mature podocytes. , , A decline in Angpt-Tie signaling leads to increased vascular permeability, exacerbation of inflammation, and a reduction in ECs, ultimately contributing to the onset of diabetic complications. Moreover, inhibiting Angpt-Tie-2 vascular signaling promotes injury, whereas Tie-2 activation may prevent such damage. These findings strongly suggest that maintaining normal Angpt-Tie-2 receptor Tie signaling is crucial for preventing and treating diabetic complications.

## Semaphorins
Semaphorins are guidance proteins that play a crucial role in organ formation and diseases. – Class 3 semaphorins and their receptors are regulated during kidney development. Functional gain and loss experiments with semaphorin 3A (SEMA3A) demonstrated the necessity of podocyte differentiation and establishment of the glomerular filtration barrier. , Furthermore, SEMA3A acts as a negative regulator of ECs movement and survival, contributing to the patterning of renal vasculature. – However, SEMA3C serves as a positive regulator of morphogenesis in ECs and UB. , Furthermore, excessive expression of SEMA3A accelerates kidney injury in various renal diseases through the c-Jun N-terminal kinase signaling pathway, suggesting that targeting SEMA3A signaling is a potential therapeutic approach for kidney injury. In addition, a new role for semaphorins in promoting vascular permeability in AKI has been demonstrated. However, it is still unclear how this phenomenon affects renal hemodynamics or systemic circulation, and if so, how it influences them. Therefore, semaphorins regulate vascular patterning and the interaction between podocytes and ECs, thereby playing a role in the development of renal vasculature and onset of kidney diseases. Understanding the signaling pathways of semaphorins contributes to understanding development and provides insights into pathological conditions.

## Notch and Wnt Signaling
The molecular mechanisms underlying renal vasculature regulation have garnered increasing attention, yet remain incompletely understood. Key signaling pathways, such as Notch and Wnt, play crucial roles in angiogenesis and maintenance of vascular integrity within the kidneys.
The Notch signaling pathway is fundamental for vascular development and maintenance in various tissues, including the kidneys. Notch receptors (Notch1–4) interact with ligands (delta-like ligands, DLL1–4, and Jagged1–2) to regulate EC fate determination, proliferation, and vessel maturation. In renal vasculature, Notch signaling was implicated in balancing angiogenic sprouting and vessel stabilization. Recent studies have highlighted its role in modulating EC behavior and angiogenic responses, specifically within the renal microvasculature. , 
Conversely, the Wnt signaling pathway, which is traditionally associated with embryonic development and tissue homeostasis, also plays a pivotal role in renal vasculature regulation. Wnt ligands bind to Frizzled receptors and coreceptors, such as LRP5/6, initiating a cascade that regulates EC behavior, vessel formation, and remodeling. In kidneys, Wnt signaling was implicated in vascular patterning during nephrogenesis and maintaining renal microvascular stability in adulthood. Wnt signaling is also involved in pathological conditions affecting renal vasculature, such as diabetic nephropathy, suggesting potential therapeutic targets. 
Insights into the Notch and Wnt signaling pathways underscore their pivotal roles in angiogenesis and vascular maintenance in the kidney. Ongoing research on novel molecular mechanisms, including microRNA (miRNA) and epigenetic modifications, can deepen our understanding of renal vascular disorders and unveil potential therapeutic strategies.

## Role of Noncoding RNAs in Vasculature Development
miRNAs and long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) play crucial regulatory roles in vascular development and maintenance by modulating gene expression at various levels, thereby affecting the complex processes involved in angiogenesis.
miRNAs typically bind to the 3ʹ-untranslated region of target mRNAs, leading to translational repression or mRNA degradation. Several miRNAs have been identified as the key regulators of renal vascular development. miRNA 126 targets negative regulators of the VEGF pathway, thereby promoting angiogenesis and enhancing EC function. miRNA 126 improves vascular integrity, enhances EC survival in the kidney, and contributes to normal renal vascular development.
lncRNAs are longer than 200 nucleotides and can influence gene expression through diverse mechanisms, such as chromatin remodeling, transcriptional regulation, and post-transcriptional processing. In the renal vasculature, specific lncRNAs are implicated in the regulation of EC function and angiogenesis. Metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 interacts with chromatin-modifying complexes and RNA-binding proteins to promote EC migration and vascular growth, thereby regulating the expression of genes involved in these processes. These noncoding RNAs are integral components of extensive networks that regulate renal vascular development.

## Diseases Affecting the Renal Vascular System
Common diseases affecting renal vasculature include hypertension, diabetic nephropathy, and arteriosclerosis. These conditions affect the structure and function of kidney vessels in distinct ways, as elucidated by recent studies on their pathophysiology and potential therapeutic targets.
Hypertension is a major cause of renal vascular diseases. Prolonged high BP damages the vascular walls of the kidneys and promotes endothelial dysfunction and fibrosis. This leads to reduced renal blood flow and deterioration of kidney function. Recent studies have focused on developing therapies targeting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, such as angiotensin II receptor blockers and aldosterone receptor antagonists, showing efficacy in mitigating renal vascular damage associated with hypertension. 
Diabetic nephropathy is a common kidney disease in patients with diabetes. Sustained high blood glucose levels damage the kidney microvasculature, particularly the glomeruli, causing glomerulosclerosis and proteinuria. Pathophysiological studies have highlighted the roles of inflammation and oxidative stress in diabetic nephropathy, suggesting that interventions targeting these pathways are potential new treatment strategies. The activation of inflammatory pathways, such as the NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 inflammasome and NF-κB in promoting cytokine production, contributes to the progression of diabetic nephropathy. 
Arteriosclerosis involves narrowing of the renal arteries and arterioles because of lipid and inflammatory cell deposition, which restricts blood flow to the kidneys. This reduction in renal blood flow impairs kidney function. There are molecular targets involved in controlling inflammatory responses and maintaining EC function within arteries, offering potential avenues for novel therapies targeting arteriosclerosis. 
Understanding the distinct effects of hypertension, diabetic nephropathy, and arteriosclerosis on the renal vasculature through recent research provides insights into the development of targeted therapies aimed at preserving kidney function and mitigating the progression of these diseases.

## Genetic and Epigenetic Factors Affecting Renal Vessels
Gene and epigenetic modifications play crucial roles in renal vascular development and subsequent disease risk. , Specific genes that influence the structure and function of the kidney vasculature have been identified. VEGF-A is pivotal in vascular formation. Similarly, genes such as ANGPT1 and ANGPT2 are involved in EC maturation and vascular stability. Mutations and genetic polymorphisms, such as angiotensin-converting enzyme, regulates vascular contraction, which may increase the risk of hypertension. Epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation and histone modification, are equally important. These modifications regulate gene expression and influence renal vascular formation and disease progression. Certain epigenetic patterns have been implicated in the increased risk of kidney disease. Understanding genetic and epigenetic changes has the potential to lead to considerable advancements in personalized medicine for renal vascular disease treatment.

## Effects of Aging on the Renal Vascular System and Susceptibility to Vascular Diseases
The effects of aging on renal vasculature are profound, and this is a major factor in the increased susceptibility of the kidney to vascular diseases. Renal vasculature undergoes various structural and functional changes during aging. First, there are changes in the vascular structure. As individuals age, the vascular walls of the kidneys become thicker and less elastic, causing the blood vessels to become stiffer and less flexible. These changes significantly affect the function of blood vessels, reducing their ability to regulate blood flow and pressure within the vessels. 
Furthermore, blood vessel function declines with age. Kidney blood vessels are lined with ECs that play a crucial role in the regulation of vascular contraction and dilation. However, with age, endothelial function declines, and the flexibility of blood vessels is impaired. This makes it more challenging, thereby increasing the likelihood of hypertension and other circulatory issues. A decline in repair mechanisms is another hallmark of vascular aging in kidneys. The ability of blood vessels to repair damage and inflammation is impaired, leading to accumulation of damage in the vessel walls. This elevates the risk of atherosclerosis and other vascular diseases. , 
Renal vascular aging presents a substantial challenge, highlighting the importance of research aimed at understanding, preventing, and treating the condition. 

## Flow Regulates Blood Vessel Formation
ECs covering the inner surface of blood vessels can sense blood flow and elicit cellular responses involving changes in morphology and function by intracellularly transmitting this information. In kidney organoid cultures under static conditions, there are few blood vessels, but when cultured under high-flow conditions, glomerular vascular formation is promoted (Figure A). However, the lumen of the blood vessels was not confirmed. In studies involving the transplantation of kidney organoids, a higher level of vascular formation occurs compared with in vitro cultivation. Notably, vessels formed within transplanted organoids contribute to the establishment of glomerular capillary networks. , , The enhancement of vascular growth and maturity in post-transplant organoids raises the question of whether this improvement can be attributed to an increase in oxygen supply, augmented nutrient availability, signals originating from the ECs themselves, or a combination of these factors. These mechanisms should be further elucidated for a comprehensive understanding of vascular development in transplanted organoids (Figure B).
 Using zebrafish, blood flow was reported to play a crucial role in the involvement of glomerular capillaries. Glomerular primordia are incorporated and undergo remodeling in the presence of blood flow. Conversely, when blood flow is halted, blood vessels surround the renal primordia. These findings suggested that blood flow regulates glomerular capillary formation during kidney development. Therefore, it is essential to elucidate the conditions and fundamental mechanisms of blood flow necessary for the development of glomerular capillaries in kidneys (Figure C).

## Differences between Rodents and Humans
Rodent and human vascular development share fundamental processes, such as angiogenesis and vasculogenesis, which are crucial for the formation of complex vascular networks. Both species use common signaling pathways, such as VEGF, to regulate these processes. However, substantial differences exist; rodents, with their shorter gestational periods and accelerated development, progress through vascular formation at a faster pace than humans. , This disparity particularly affects the timing and organization of vascular structures in complex organs, such as the brain and placenta. Species-specific differences in angiogenic responses influence the efficacy of therapeutic interventions targeting vascular disorders. Therefore, although rodent models are indispensable for understanding basic vascular biology, careful consideration of these interspecies differences is crucial for applying research findings to human clinical contexts and developing effective treatments.

## Conclusions and Perspectives
Research on the renal vascular system has advanced using cutting-edge techniques, such as transgenic, systemic, and conditional knockout approaches. However, substantial unresolved issues persist, particularly regarding the formation mechanism of the 3D structure of renal vessels and the precise spatiotemporal connection of these vessels to the nephrons. The elucidation of such challenges holds the potential for further advancements in future renal studies. A deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying renal vascular formation is expected to lead to substantial breakthroughs in both scientific and medical fields.
