## Abstract
Advanced sarcomas have limited treatment options after standard therapy, and therefore we investigated the efficacy and safety of sintilimab plus anlotinib in this setting. Patients age 18 to 75 years with advanced sarcomas and prior systemic therapy were enrolled. Patients with untreated, primary chemotherapy–resistant tumor types, such as alveolar soft-part sarcoma and clear-cell sarcoma, were also included. Patients received sintilimab 200 mg (day 1) and anlotinib (8, 10, or 12 mg investigator-chosen, day 1–14) every 3 weeks. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR). Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), disease control rate (DCR), and adverse events (AE). The predictive value of tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) was explored. A total of 42 patients were enrolled, and 40 (95.2%) patients were non–alveolar soft-part sarcoma. The ORR and DCR were 30.9% [95% confidence interval (CI), 16.4%–45.5%] and 76.2% (95% CI, 62.8%–89.6%), respectively, with a median follow-up duration of 15.4 months, and the median PFS was 5.0 months (95% CI, 2.8–10.2). The median OS was not reached. The most common AEs included elevated lactate dehydrogenase (28.57%), hypoproteinemia (21.43%), and increased thyroid-stimulating hormone (21.43%). The most common ≥ grade 3 AEs were hypertension (4.76%) and hyponatremia (4.76%). Two serious AEs (one hepatitis and one intestinal perforation) were recorded. The ORR in TLS-positive patients (n = 7) was significantly higher than that in TLS-negative patients (n = 28; 71.4% vs. 25.0%, P = 0.033). Therefore, sintilimab plus anlotinib demonstrated promising antitumor activity with manageable toxicity in advanced sarcomas, particularly among TLS-positive patients.

## Introduction
Sarcomas are a heterogeneous group of solid tumors that arise from mesenchymal tissue. They can be broadly categorized into those originating from soft tissues, such as adipose, muscle, vascular, and neural/nerve sheath tissues, and those from osseous tissue ( ). These tumors are relatively rare, accounting for only 1% of adult and 15% of pediatric malignancies ( ). The prognosis for patients with metastatic sarcomas is particularly grim, with only 15% surviving beyond 5 years ( ).
Anthracycline-based chemotherapy remains the standard first-line treatment for locally advanced and metastatic sarcomas. Nonetheless, there is no established second-line treatment for patients whose disease progresses after initial anthracycline therapy. In recent years, multikinase angiogenesis inhibitors (MAI) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) have emerged as two promising therapeutic strategies for locally advanced or metastatic sarcoma. Several studies have evaluated the efficacy and safety of combining MAIs with ICIs in patients with advanced sarcomas ( – ). However, the majority of these studies included patient cohorts that were heavily biased toward alveolar soft-part sarcoma (ASPS; refs. – ). The clinical efficacy of this combination treatment in patients with non-ASPS, therefore, requires further investigation. Moreover, recent evidence suggests that the presence of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) in advanced soft-tissue sarcomas (STS) may serve as a predictive biomarker for identifying patients who could benefit from treatment with pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab plus cyclophosphamide ( , ). However, to date, the predictive value of TLS for the combined treatment of MAIs with ICIs in patients with sarcoma has not been documented.
Therefore, we designed a prospective, single-arm, phase II trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sintilimab, a selective anti–PD-1 mAb, in combination with anlotinib, a multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, in patients with locally advanced or metastatic sarcoma, with a specific emphasis on non-ASPS cases. This study pioneers the investigation of TLS as a potential predictive biomarker for therapeutic response to combined antiangiogenic and immune checkpoint blockade therapy.

## Materials and Methods

## Study design and patients
Eligible patients were 18 to 75 years of age, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 to 1, and with a life expectancy of at least 3 months. They were required to have histologically and radiologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic sarcoma. Eligible patients were also required to provide either archived tumor tissue from a metastatic or locally advanced lesion, or a newly obtained core needle or excisional biopsy specimen. Additionally, they must have experienced disease progression during or after at least one line of anthracycline-based chemotherapy. [Treatment-naïve patients with primary resistance to chemotherapy, such as ASPS and clear-cell sarcoma (CCS), were allowed]. Key exclusion criteria included prior treatment with MAIs or ICIs.
A total of 42 patients were enrolled in this study. Tumor tissue samples were collected for routine pathologic evaluation. After enrollment, each patient provided 10 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections (3 μm in thickness) mounted on adhesive-coated slides, which were used for IHC and two-plex immunofluorescence (IF) staining to evaluate TLS. The remaining specimens were stored at room temperature in the pathology biobank of Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute.
The trial protocol was compliant with the Good Clinical Practice guidelines (as defined by the International Council on Harmonisation), and the study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shandong Cancer Hospital. All participants provided written informed consent prior to the initiation of treatment. The study was registered at Chictr.org (ChiCTR2100055071).

## Treatment
Sintilimab (Innovent Biologics) was administered intravenously at a dosage of 200 mg on day 1, once every 3 weeks. Sintilimab treatment was not continued past 24 months, and the dose was not adjusted during the study.
Anlotinib (Chia Tai Tianqing) was administered orally every 3 weeks from day 1 to day 14. The initial dose of anlotinib was 8, 10, or 12 mg, decided by the investigator based on the patient’s physical condition. If a grade 3 or 4 adverse event (AE) occurred, the anlotinib dose could be reduced at the investigator’s discretion.
Patients discontinued treatment if any of the following occurred: the patient decided to withdraw informed consent, unacceptable toxicity occurred, disease progression occurred according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 criteria, or the patient’s physical condition worsened so that the treatment could not continue.

## Outcomes
The primary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR). The secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), disease control rate (DCR), and treatment-related AEs (TRAE). In the exploratory analysis, we assessed the association between TLS and treatment response. Tumor response was evaluated according to RECIST 1.1 criteria, classifying responses as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease. ORR was calculated as the percentage of CR and PR during treatment, and DCR was calculated as the percentage of CR, PR, and SD. PFS was defined as the duration from the first dose to progressive disease (as assessed using RECIST 1.1 criteria), death due to any cause, or change to other treatment for personal reasons, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the duration from the date of the first dose to death from any cause. TRAEs were evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events 5.0 criteria.
Patients who were event free or were lost to follow-up were censored at the time of the last visit.

## TLS detection

## IHC
Tumor samples were subjected to chromogenic IHC and two-plex IF to assess the presence of TLS. FFPE tumor tissues were sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm. After deparaffinization in xylene and rehydration through graded ethanol and water, antigen retrieval was performed using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid buffer under high pressure for 3 minutes, followed by several washes with PBS. Sections were then incubated with primary antibodies against CD3 (Qiming Gene Technology, OG-J040) and CD20 (Ventana Medical Systems, 760-2531) for 20 minutes. After gentle washing with PBS, the slides were incubated with horseradish peroxidase–labeled anti–mouse CD20 polymer for 16 minutes. Chromogenic detection was performed using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (Leica Biosystems, DS9800), and nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin (Dako, Agilent Technologies, S3301). Slides were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, mounted using EcoMount (Biocare Medical, EM897L), and scanned at high resolution using a NanoZoomer digital slide scanner (Hamamatsu Photonics). All staining procedures were conducted using the Dako Autostainer system (Agilent Technologies), and CD3/CD20 detection was visualized using the EnVision detection system (Dako). Tumor regions were delineated by an experienced pathologist using HALO 10 image analysis software (Indica Labs) based on hematoxylin and eosin–stained reference slides. TLS detection was then performed using an integrated classifier that identified TLSs with a minimum surface area of 60,000 µm2. CD3+ and CD20+ cells within the tumor and TLS regions were quantified using a validated cell detection algorithm built into the HALO platform. TLS positivity was defined as the presence of at least one CD3+/CD20+ lymphoid aggregate containing more than 700 cells within the annotated tumor area. All slide evaluations were independently reviewed by two pathologists who were blinded to clinical outcomes. Discrepancies were resolved through joint review.

## Two-plex IF
The FFPE human tumor samples were cut into 3-µm-thick sections. The sections were deparaffinized with xylene and subjected to antigen retrieval for 20 minutes at 98°C in sodium citrate buffer. After cooling at room temperature, the sections were placed on 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 minutes to reduce the background reaction, rinsed with PBS buffer, and then incubated in normal goat serum for 30 minutes to block nonspecific protein–protein interactions. For the CD20 and CD3 two-plex staining, a tyramide system amplification (TSA) was used. The sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with CD3 antibody (Zuocheng Bio, ZA0053, 1:100) and then with the horseradish peroxidase–conjugated polymer at room temperature for 30 minutes, followed by incubation with the TSA reagent for 15 minutes. This protocol was repeated for the CD20 antibody (Zuocheng Bio, ZA0397, 1:50) and corresponding polymer incubations. The dilutions used for the TSA are 1:100 for TSA AF488 and 1:100 for TSA AF594, as performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Nuclear DNA was labeled with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; Sigma, D9542) for 5 minutes. After mounting with fluorescent antiquenching tablets, the sections were scanned using Pannoramic MIDI II (3DHISTECH).

## Statistical analysis
According to clinical trials cited in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines for subsequent lines of treatment for advanced or metastatic sarcoma, the ORRs for various treatment regimens are as follows: 4% for eribulin ( ), 9.9% to 11% for trabectedin ( , ), 4% to 6.9% for dacarbazine ( , ), 5% for ifosfamide ( ), 12% for gemcitabine plus dacarbazine ( ), 12.5% for gemcitabine plus vinorelbine ( ), 16% for gemcitabine plus docetaxel, and 6.5% for pazopanib ( ). According to these clinical trial data, the ORR of second-line and later-line monotherapies for sarcoma is between 4% and 11%, and the ORR of combination therapy is between 12% and 16%.
This study hypothesizes that the combination of sintilimab and anlotinib will increase the ORR to 20%. The statistical assumptions are as follows: a one-sided α level of 0.05 and power of 90%, with an anticipated ORR increase from 5% to 20%. Considering a 15% dropout rate, the study aims to enroll a total of 40 participants.
Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS version 27.0 (IBM, RRID: SCR_002865). Clinical and demographic characteristics of study patients were presented using descriptive statistics. Survival was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. The χ2 test and the Fisher exact test were used to compare the difference in ORR between the patients with and without TLS. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The raw data supporting the findings of this study are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

## Results

## Patient characteristics
Between December 2021 and April 2024, a total of 52 participants were screened, and 42 patients were enrolled in the study ( ). The baseline characteristics are summarized in . The representativeness of the enrolled patients is shown in Supplementary Table S2.
 The median (range) age was 54.5 (18–72) years. Major subtypes included dedifferentiated liposarcoma (seven cases, 16.7%), synovial sarcoma (SS; five cases, 11.9%), leiomyosarcoma (three cases, 7.1%), CCS (three cases, 7.1%), fibrosarcoma (three cases, 7.1%), and osteosarcoma (three cases, 7.1%). The most common metastatic sites were lungs (42.9%), followed by lymph nodes (21.4%), liver (16.7%), and bones (11.9%). Four (9.5%) patients received sintilimab plus anlotinib as the first-line therapy, 33 (78.6%) patients as the second-line therapy, four (9.5%) patients as the third-line therapy, and one (2.4%) patient as the fourth-line therapy. Anlotinib was initially administered at a dose of 8 mg to 8 patients, 10 mg to 12 patients, and 12 mg to 22 patients, respectively. Thirty-six (85.7%) patients had metastatic disease, and the remaining six (14.3%) patients had locally advanced disease. All locally advanced cases were assessed by investigators and deemed unsuitable for curative-intent surgical intervention or definitive radiotherapy.
At the data cutoff date of October 1, 2024, after a median follow-up of 15.4 months [95% confidence interval (CI), 6.7–24.0], 12 patients were still receiving treatment and 30 patients discontinued treatment. Among the 30 patients who ceased treatment, 3 discontinued because of TRAEs, 6 withdrew their informed consent after their condition improved and subsequently received local treatment such as surgery or radiotherapy, and 21 discontinued because of disease progression ( ). Sixteen patients had died.

## Efficacy
Of 42 patients evaluable for objective response (OR), 1 (2.38%) achieved a CR, 12 (28.57%) achieved a PR, and 19 (45.24%) achieved SD; the ORR and DCR were 30.9% (95% CI, 16.4%–45.5%) and 76.2% (95% CI, 62.8%–89.6%), respectively. The best percentage change from the baseline of target lesions is shown in . The changes in tumor burden from baseline according to RECIST 1.1 in the patients are shown in . The median follow-up time was 15.4 months (95% CI, 6.7–24.0), and the median PFS was 5.0 months (95% CI, 2.8–10.2; ). The median OS was not reached ( ). The 12-month OS rate was 62.6% (95% CI, 47.3%–77.9%).
 Among patients with non-ASPS, the ORR was 27.5% (95% CI, 16.1%–42.8%), the DCR was 75% (95% CI, 59.8%–85.8%), the median PFS was 4.5 months (95% CI, 2.6–8.1; ), and the median OS was not reached ( ). The 12-month OS rate for patients with non-ASPS was 60.6% (95% CI, 44.7%–76.5%).

## Safety
All patients experienced TRAEs ( ). The most common TRAEs included elevated lactate dehydrogenase (28.6%), hypoproteinemia (21.4%), increased thyroid-stimulating hormone (23.8%), abnormal T wave on electrocardiogram (16.7%), and hyperuricemia (19.0%). The most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were hypertension (4.8%) and hyponatremia (4.8%). Two serious TRAEs were recorded: one case of hepatitis and one case of intestinal perforation. No treatment-related deaths occurred.
 TRAEs led to dose reduction of anlotinib in two patients owing to grade 1 hypersomnia and grade 2 nausea, respectively. One patient discontinued sintilimab treatment because of grade 3 autoimmune hepatitis.

## Exploratory analysis of TLS as a biomarker in sarcomas
Among the 42 enrolled patients, seven were excluded from the exploratory analysis because of insufficient tumor content, which did not meet the evaluation criteria. Consequently, TLS assessment was performed on the remaining 35 patients ( ). Of these, seven (20%) patients were TLS positive: two cases of angiosarcoma (AS), one case of ASPS, one case of fibrosarcoma, one case of undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS), and two cases of CCS. The ORR was significantly higher in TLS-positive patients (N = 7) than in TLS-negative patients (N = 28, 71.4% vs. 25.0%, Fisher exact test: P = 0.033).
 

## Discussion
This study evaluated the efficacy and safety of combined sintilimab and anlotinib therapy in chemotherapy-pretreated advanced sarcomas, with a particular focus on non-ASPS cases. The trial demonstrated an ORR of 30.9% (95% CI, 16.4%–45.6%). The median PFS was 5.0 months (95% CI, 2.8–10.2), and the median OS was not reached; the 1-year OS rate was 62.6%. Within the non-ASPS subgroup, the ORR and DCR were 27.5% (95% CI, 16.1%–42.8%) and 75% (95% CI, 59.8%–85.8%), respectively. The combination regimen presented a favorable safety profile, with hypertension and hyponatremia being the most common grade ≥3 AEs, each occurring in 4.8% of patients. The ORR of patients with TLS was significantly higher than those without (71.4% vs. 25.0%, P = 0.033), suggesting TLS as a potential predictive biomarker for sintilimab plus anlotinib therapy.
Patients with various sarcoma subtypes commonly exhibit unique biological behaviors and varying responses to treatment ( , ). ASPS is a rare subset of STS that is typically refractory to chemotherapy ( ). Nevertheless, previous studies have demonstrated a favorable therapeutic response to the combination of MAIs and ICIs in patients with ASPS, with the ORR ranging from 57% to 75% when employing different MAIs and ICIs ( , , ).
Recently, a phase II study investigating the combination of durvalumab and pazopanib in patients with non-ASPS reported encouraging outcomes. Among the 47 participants in that study, only 2 (4.3%) were diagnosed with ASPS. The researchers observed that 100% (2/2) of patients with angiosarcoma, 75% (3/4) with UPS, 50% (2/4) with desmoplastic small round cell tumor, 50% (1/2) with endometrial stromal sarcoma, and 50% (2/4) with SS achieved an OR ( ). Similarly, in our study, only 5% (2/42) of patients had ASPS. Our findings confirmed that non-ASPS sarcoma subtypes also responded favorably to the combination regimen. Specifically, 100% (2/2) of patients with AS, 100% (2/2) with undifferentiated spindle cell sarcoma, 50% (1/2) with myofibroblastic sarcoma, and 40% (2/5) with SS achieved an OR.
However, it is worth noting that several previous phase II trials evaluating the efficacy of MAIs combined with ICIs in patients with UPS have shown disappointing results. Specifically, the ORR was 0% (0/5) for the axitinib plus pembrolizumab regimen, 0% (0/6) for the nivolumab plus sunitinib regimen, and 0% (0/5) for the anlotinib plus TQB2450 regimen ( – ). These results seem to differ substantially from the reported ORR of 75% in the study of durvalumab plus pazopanib ( ). In our study, only one patient with UPS was included, which was insufficient to draw any conclusion. Consequently, further studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to thoroughly investigate the efficacy of the MAIs plus ICIs combination therapy in UPS.
AS is a rare malignancy that affects the inner lining of blood vessels and lymph vessels. In a phase II study that included seven patients with AS, the combination of nivolumab and sunitinib produced a response rate of 42.9% (3/7; ref. ). Similarly, in both our study and the study of durvalumab plus pazopanib, all four patients with AS achieved an OR ( ). Moreover, it is particularly noteworthy that one patient with AS achieved a CR in our study and in the study of nivolumab plus sunitinib ( ). In our study, a CR was achieved in a patient with cutaneous AS, whereas a PR was observed in a patient with retroperitoneal AS. Despite the small number of AS cases enrolled in each study, all available evidence suggested promising results of MAIs plus ICIs in the treatment of AS.
Given that more than half of patients with non-ASPS did not achieve an OR following treatment with MAIs plus ICIs, there is an urgent need to develop a robust predictive biomarker to aid in the selection of proper patients. Recently, TLS, which is ectopically formed aggregates of B-cell follicles, follicular dendritic cells, and T cells within the tumor microenvironment, has been demonstrated to play a pivotal role in anticancer immunity across various tumor types ( ). Petitprez and colleagues ( ) conducted a comprehensive analysis of transcriptomic data from more than 600 STSs, identifying a subgroup characterized by elevated expression of a B cell–related gene signature ( ). IHC analyses revealed that this subgroup of sarcomas was marked by the presence of intratumoral TLS. Additionally, a retrospective analysis of biopsy specimens from 47 patients participating in the SARC028 study showed that this B cell–related gene signature was a strong predictor of response to the anti–PD-1 antibody pembrolizumab, suggesting that TLS could serve as a robust biomarker for personalized immunotherapy in patients with sarcoma ( ). Subsequently, a multicohort phase II study of pembrolizumab in combination with low-dose cyclophosphamide was conducted in patients with advanced STS, with a cohort selection based on TLS presence. The ORR in the TLS cohort was 30% (95% CI, 14.7–49.4), whereas the ORR in the unselected cohorts was only 2.4% (95% CI, 0.1–12.9; ref. ). This indicates that selecting patients based on TLS presence may be an effective strategy to enrich the correct population for chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy.
In the study investigating the combination of durvalumab and pazopanib, the researchers found that the positive rate of TLS was notably low in their cohort, with only 3 of 47 patients exhibiting TLS positivity (PR, n = 2; SD, n = 1). In multivariate analysis, they ultimately identified CD20+ B-cell infiltration as the independent prognostic factor for PFS. Notably, the CD20+ B cell is a critical component of TLS ( ).
In this study, we conducted the first investigation evaluating the prognostic significance of TLS in predicting therapeutic outcomes of combination therapy with MAI and ICI. Our analysis revealed a TLS positivity rate of 20%, which is consistent with the prevalence rates reported in prior sarcoma studies ( , ). Further analysis revealed that the ORR in the TLS-positive cohort was significantly higher than that in the TLS-negative cohort (71.4% vs. 25.0%, P = 0.033). This finding suggests that TLS may serve as a promising predictive biomarker for treatment efficacy in patients receiving sintilimab–anlotinib combination therapy.
Although most of the ORs among TLS-positive patients were observed in sarcoma subtypes already known to be responsive to ICIs—such as ASPS, AS, and UPS—this overlap does not diminish the potential predictive value of TLS. Rather, it underscores a strong internal consistency between histology-based sensitivity and TLS-based stratification, supporting the robustness and biological plausibility of our findings.
Encouragingly, an OR was also observed in a TLS-positive patient with CCS, a rare subtype that is typically resistant to chemotherapy and has shown limited response to ICI–MAI combinations. To date, only two prospective trials investigating ICI–MAI therapy have enrolled patients with CCS. In a study evaluating nivolumab plus sunitinib in advanced STS, the ORR in CCS was 18.2% (2/11; ref. ). In a separate phase II trial of durvalumab plus pazopanib, no responses were observed in patients with CCS (0/2; ref. ). In our study, three patients with CCS were enrolled: two were TLS positive and one was TLS negative. The ORR among TLS-positive patients with CCS was 50% (1/2). Although the sample size is small, this result is noteworthy given the historically limited treatment options for CCS and suggests that TLS may have potential as a predictive biomarker for combination immunotherapy in this challenging subtype. These findings underscore the necessity for larger-scale investigations to further elucidate the efficacy of ICI–MAI regimens in CCS and the predictive value of TLS in this subtype.
The safety profile of sintilimab plus anlotinib has been evaluated in prospective clinical trials involving various tumor subtypes, such as cervical cancer, non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), endometrial cancer, and biliary tract cancer ( – ). In a phase II study that assessed the efficacy and safety of anlotinib as monotherapy in advanced STS, the drug was administered at a fixed initial dose of 12 mg. Nonetheless, 14% (24/166) of the patients experienced AEs necessitating dose reduction ( ). In subsequent studies examining the combined use of sintilimab and anlotinib across different tumor types, the proportion of patients requiring dose reduction during the study ranged from 15.0% to 65.2% when anlotinib was administered at an initial fixed dose of 12 mg ( , – ) and was 26.2% when anlotinib was administered at an initial fixed dose of 10 mg ( ). Due to the high rate of dose reduction observed in these studies, our study employed an individualized dosing regimen for anlotinib, tailored to each patient’s physical factors. Fortunately, only 5% (2/42) of patients in our study required dose adjustment in response to AEs. Additionally, the incidence of grade 3 or higher TRAEs in our study (28.6%) was also lower compared with the rates reported in other trials of sintilimab and anlotinib in advanced NSCLC (28.6%) and endometrial cancer (50.0%; refs. , ). The most commonly reported TRAEs of sintilimab plus anlotinib were hypothyroidism, encompassing clinical hypothyroidism and increased thyroid-stimulating hormone, and elevated hepatic enzymes. However, the rates of hypothyroidism (16.7%) and alanine aminotransferase/aspartate aminotransferase elevation (4.8%) in our study were lower than those observed in other trials of sintilimab and anlotinib in advanced NSCLC (38.1% and 61.9%, respectively) and cervical cancer (33.3% and 35.7%, respectively), suggesting that individualized dosing of anlotinib may mitigate potential harm to the thyroid gland and liver.
Several limitations of our study must be acknowledged. First, the sample size of our study was relatively small, making it difficult to draw definitive conclusions about the efficacy of treating a specific tumor type. Second, the follow-up period was not long enough to adequately evaluate long-term toxicities. Third, our study population might have selection bias, as the inclusion criteria precluded patients with other indolent histologies who were primary refractory to anthracycline-based chemotherapy. Fourth, anlotinib, one of the agents used in this study, is currently approved for use only in China and is not commercially available in other countries, which may limit the global generalizability of our findings. Lastly, most ORs observed in this study occurred in sarcoma subtypes known to be responsive to either ICIs or anlotinib based on prior studies. As this was a single-arm trial without a comparator group, we cannot determine whether the observed activity is attributable to the combination regimen or to one of the agents alone. Therefore, future randomized controlled trials are needed to clarify the relative contribution of each agent and to confirm the efficacy of the combination therapy in specific sarcoma subtypes.
This study demonstrates that the combination of sintilimab and anlotinib is effective and well tolerated in patients with advanced sarcomas previously treated with chemotherapy, particularly in specific subtypes such as ASPS, AS, undifferentiated spindle cell sarcoma, and SS. The presence of TLS seems to be a valuable predictive biomarker for MAI combined with ICIs, potentially offering a more personalized treatment approach for patients. Future studies are warranted to validate these findings and further investigate the role of TLS as a predictor of response to ICI-based therapy in patients with sarcoma.
