## Abstract

## Background
Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) represents the most prevalent sort of thyroid malignancy, and its incidence has been consistently increasing worldwide. Although most PTC cases exhibit indolent behavior, a subset demonstrates aggressive characteristics, leading to recurrence, metastasis, and poor clinical outcomes. This study aims to unveil the molecular mechanism of PTC and identify potential biomarkers for its early diagnosis and individualized treatment.

## Methods
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were utilized for the analysis of alterations in cellular composition within PTC tissues, with a particular focus on differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in every cell population. Co-expression gene module analysis of tumor-associated epithelial cells (Tumor-epi) was carried out via the high-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA). Subsequently, multiple machine learning (ML) algorithms, including Random Forest (RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Boruta feature selection algorithm (Boruta), and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), were leveraged to refine and select three pivotal genes, XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1, for the construction of a diagnostic model. Finally, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed in the PTC cell line (BCPAP) and normal thyroid epithelial cells (NC) to validate the expression of these hub genes.

## Results
A notable elevation in epithelial cell percentage was detected in tumor tissues. Further analyses revealed that Tumor-epi cells exhibited distinct molecular characteristics and biological functions potentially linked to PTC invasiveness and immune evasion. Through hdWGCNA, co-expression gene modules were identified within Tumor-epi cells, which reflect their putative role in PTC pathogenesis. The diagnostic model, based on the three selected genes (XPR1, SH3RF1, TLE1), demonstrated high discriminatory power in distinguishing PTC from normal thyroid tissue [area under the curve (AUC) >0.90]. qRT-PCR validation in the BCPAP and NC confirmed that XPR1 was significantly upregulated, whereas SH3RF1 and TLE1 were downregulated in PTC (all P<0.001), consistent with computational predictions. Moreover, these genes were found to be linked to the immune cell distribution in the tumor immune microenvironment, suggesting their involvement in mechanisms of immune escape.

## Conclusions
This study presents new perspectives on the molecular pathogenesis of PTC and highlights the crucial role of epithelial cells in tumor progression. The three identified genes, namely XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1, serve as prospective diagnostic biomarkers and may facilitate diagnostic refinement and personalized treatment strategies for PTC. Additionally, their correlation with the immune microenvironment offers a new avenue for exploring the mechanisms of tumor immune evasion.

Keywords: Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), machine learning (ML), single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), high-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA), diagnostic biomarkers

## Introduction
Thyroid cancer (TC) is among the most frequently occurring malignancies in the endocrine system ( ). According to the GLOBOCAN 2022 database of the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) under the World Health Organization (WHO), it has the seventh highest incidence, and the fifth among females, who account for approximately 75% of all TC cases globally ( ). Its worldwide occurrence has shown a continuous upward trend over the last forty years ( ). In 2022, the National Cancer Center of China indicated an estimated 466,100 new TC cases in China, making TC the third leading malignancy by incidence. In females, TC accounted for 341,200 cases, also ranking third ( ). Histologically, TC can be broadly classified into three major subtypes: (I) differentiated thyroid carcinoma (DTC), including papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC), and Hürthle cell carcinoma; (II) medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC); and (III) anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC). Among these, PTC constitutes approximately 85–90% of all TC cases ( , ). Although most PTC cases are well-differentiated with infrequent occurrences of local invasion, recurrence, or both regional and distant metastatic spread, a subset of tumors, specifically aggressive histologic variants such as tall cell, columnar cell, and diffuse sclerosing PTC, demonstrates aggressive behavior, manifesting in local recurrence, distant dissemination, or poor clinical outcomes ( ). Therefore, identifying new biomarkers is pressing to unravel the molecular pathogenesis and guide the customized therapeutic strategies for PTC.
TC progression involves a multifaceted interaction of genetic, epigenetic, and microenvironmental influences. Common genetic alterations include BRAF mutations, RET/PTC rearrangements, and RAS mutations, which activate important signaling pathways like MAPK and PI3K/Akt, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion ( - ). Epigenetic modifications, including aberrant DNA methylation and dysregulated non-coding RNA expression, also influence thyroid tumorigenesis ( , ). The tumor microenvironment featuring immune evasion, angiogenesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling also further contributes to tumor growth and metastasis ( ). Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a pivotal mechanism by which tumor cells acquire invasive and metastatic capabilities, is crucial in TC progression ( , ). Furthermore, the emergence of resistance to targeted therapies underscores the potential activation of compensatory signaling cascades during treatment, enabling tumor cells to circumvent pharmacologic inhibition ( ). Elucidating these molecular mechanisms is essential for introducing novel diagnostic biomarkers and targeted therapeutic approaches, with the ultimate aim of ameliorating prognosis and improving clinical management in the TC population.
Our study thoroughly analyzed public single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data to delineate the cellular landscape of PTC. By clustering epithelial cell populations, our study identified tumor-associated epithelial cells (Tumor-epi) and normal epithelial cells (Normal-epi) and subsequently screened for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between these subsets. High-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA) was employed to uncover gene modules significantly linked to Tumor-epi. Seventy putative hub genes were noted after module genes and DEGs were intersected. To evaluate their diagnostic potential, feature selection, and model construction were enabled by multiple machine learning (ML) algorithms, ultimately yielding a diagnostic prediction model for PTC. Our study presents novel perspectives on the molecular mechanisms of PTC and lays the groundwork for innovating diagnosis and treatment approaches. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-aw-2244/rc).

## Methods

## Data acquisition and processing
Public GSE241184 and GSE33630 datasets were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/, accessed February 5, 2025). The former comprises scRNA-seq data from one normal thyroid sample and one PTC sample and was utilized to examine the heterogeneity of epithelial cells across normal thyroid and PTC tissues ( ). scRNA-seq data were analyzed via the Seurat package 5.1.0. Only cells expressing between 500 and 6,000 genes and having mitochondrial gene content below 25% were encompassed to enhance biological relevance. Data were normalized via the NormalizeData function to reduce technical noise, followed by principal component analysis (PCA). The Harmony package 1.2.3 mitigated batch effects inherent to scRNA-seq dissociation artifacts. Dimensionality reduction was performed via unsupervised clustering and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE), and cell clustering was achieved using the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions, thereby enabling effective delineation of cellular subpopulations.
The GSE33630 dataset, based on the GPL570 platform, includes transcriptomic data from 49 PTC samples and 45 normal thyroid tissues. Additionally, transcriptomic data from thyroid carcinoma (THCA) patients were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/, accessed on February 5, 2025), comprising tumor samples from 507 patients and matched normal tissues from 58 individuals. Our study employed TCGA-THCA as the training set ( ) and GSE33630 as the validation set ( ). The analysis of data obtained from publicly available databases did not require institutional ethics approval. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

## Trajectory inference and cell-cell communication (CCC) analysis
Macrophage pseudotemporal trajectories were analyzed through the reduceDimension function within the Monocle package 2.34.0. To infer intercellular communication networks between Tumor-epi and other cellular subsets, the CellChat package 1.6.1 was run with its predefined settings according to the suggested pipeline ( ).

## Enrichment analysis
DEGs between Tumor-epi and Normal-epi were identified using the FindMarkers function in Seurat. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses, as well as gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), were enabled by the clusterProfiler package 4.14.4. The results were visualized utilizing the tidyverse package 2.0.0.

## hdWGCNA
The hdWGCNA package 0.4.0 ( ) was employed to identify genes associated with the Tumor-epi cell population. Briefly, the MetacellsByGroups function generated metacells for every sample and cell cluster, with each metacell representing an aggregation of 25 individual cells. Cells of interest (i.e., Tumor-epi) were subsetted from the Seurat object and grouped according to subcell type and sample origin. Metacell construction was performed based on Harmony-reduced dimensions. Parameters were set such that each metacell aggregated 25 cells, with a maximum of 10 overlapping cells permitted per metacell. Subsequent analyses followed the standard hdWGCNA workflow, including sequential execution of TestSoftPowers, ConstructNetwork, ModuleEigengenes, ModuleConnectivity, and RunModuleUMAP functions using default settings. Interaction network analysis was carried out via the HubGeneNetworkPlot function.

## ML-based hub gene identification
Our study leveraged four widely used ML algorithms: Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Boruta feature selection algorithm (Boruta), support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), and Random Forest (RF) for hub gene identification ( ). Each of these algorithms offers unique advantages in gene selection and is well-suited to identifying key features associated with disease states. XGBoost, an efficient gradient-boosting framework based on decision trees, is particularly adept at handling high-dimensional data. Boruta is a feature selection algorithm built upon the RF framework, designed to identify all relevant features by assessing their importance. SVM-RFE identifies the most discriminative genes by recursively eliminating the least relevant features. Meanwhile, RF enhances classification accuracy by aggregating multiple decision trees with recognized robustness against noise and overfitting. The XGBoost analysis was enabled by the “xgboost” package 1.7.8.1; Boruta analysis via the “Boruta” package 8.0.0; SVM-RFE analysis through the “caret” package 7.0.1; and RF analysis via the “randomForest” package 4.7.1.2. The intersecting genes identified across these algorithms were subsequently utilized for the prediction model development. The “timeROC” package 0.4 generated receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the evaluation of the diagnostic efficacy of each gene. Validation was performed using the GSE33630 dataset. Additionally, for nomogram construction and calibration curve plotting, our study utilized the “rms” package 7.0.0, whereas model prediction accuracy was examined through decision curve analysis (DCA) via the “rmda” package 1.6.

## Immune microenvironment analysis
CIBERSORT 0.1.0 was leveraged to characterize the immune landscape. The relative percentages of 22 immune cell sorts were estimated across all TCGA-THCA samples utilizing the LM22 signature matrix ( ). Visualization of the immune cell composition was performed through stacked bar plots, grouped boxplots, and correlation heatmaps. Group-wise comparisons were made via the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and the link of gene expression to immune cell infiltration was evaluated through Spearman correlation analysis.

## Cell lines and cell culture
The PTC cell line (BCPAP) and normal thyroid epithelial cells (NC) were purchased from Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Procell, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco, USA). All cells were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2, and the culture medium was replaced regularly to ensure optimal cell growth.

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis was performed with a reverse transcription kit (MedChemExpress, USA). qRT-PCR was carried out on a PRISM 7500 Real-Time PCR Detection System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (MedChemExpress, USA). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) served as the internal control, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primers used for qRT-PCR were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., China, with the following sequences: XPR1 (forward: TGGTGTTACTACGCTGCGAC; reverse: CACTGAAGGCCAGTTTAAGGTC), SH3RF1 (forward: ATACCTCAGTTACCATGTGCCA; reverse: TCCACTTGTCTTCGCAAAATGAT), and TLE1 (forward: TCACCCTTCGGGACTTCAG; reverse: GAGCACTAGACAGCGCAAGAA).

## Statistical analysis
Our statistical analyses and visualization were enabled by R 4.4.2. The relations between continuous variables were examined utilizing Pearson’s and Spearman’s correlation coefficients. For inter-group variation analysis, non-parametric tests including Wilcoxon rank-sum and Kruskal-Wallis were employed, with Dunn’s procedure applied for multiple comparisons. A two-tailed P<0.05 suggested statistical significance.

## Results

## Single-cell sequencing reveals alterations in cellular composition of PTC tissue
Following quality checks and batch effect correction, 16,897 high-quality cells expressing 22,559 genes were retained, forming a robust dataset for downstream analyses of cellular populations and tissue heterogeneity. presents the gene expression profile of the retained cells. A strong positive correlation (r=0.95) was observed between the total gene expression and the number of genes detected per cell, with mitochondrial gene content generally below 10% ( ). Using unsupervised dimensionality reduction and clustering algorithms, our study annotated and integrated cellular identities. Automated annotation via the Single-cell Recognition (SingleR) algorithm identified 20 distinct cellular clusters. Subsequent manual curation refined these into seven principal cell types: macrophages, monocytes, B, endothelial, epithelial, T, and tissue stem cells ( ). DEGs for each cluster were identified and visualized using volcano plots ( ). Comparative analysis of PTC versus normal thyroid tissues revealed a marked increase in epithelial cell abundance in tumor samples ( ). Epithelial cells are pivotal to EMT, which elevates tumor invasiveness and metastatic potential ( , ). Therefore, this study focused subsequent investigations on elucidating how epithelial cells influence PTC.
 

## Single-cell analysis reveals epithelial cell heterogeneity in PTC tissues
To unveil the alterations in epithelial cells between normal thyroid and PTC tissues, epithelial cells were extracted and clustering analysis was performed based on highly variable genes specific to this cell population. This analysis identified eight distinct epithelial cell clusters ( ). Notably, clusters 0 and 7 were significantly enriched in PTC tissues and exhibited hallmark malignant transcriptional signatures, including activation of the p53 and KRAS signaling pathways. Therefore, these clusters were classified as Tumor-epi, representing the malignant epithelial cell population in PTC. In contrast, cluster 1 was predominantly derived from normal thyroid tissues and was therefore classified as Normal-epi, corresponding to non-malignant thyroid follicular cells ( ). These findings suggest that specific epithelial cell subsets are possibly pivotal in PTC incidence and advancement. Differential expression analysis was subsequently conducted across Tumor-epi and Normal-epi cohorts, with results visualized via volcano and heat maps ( ). Functional enrichment analysis on highly expressed characteristic genes unraveled the possible molecular mechanisms underlying the involvement of Tumor-epi cells in tumorigenesis and progression. GO analysis demonstrated notable enrichment in “actin filament organization”, “wound healing”, as well as “regulation of apoptotic signaling pathway” ( ). KEGG analysis further demonstrated the enrichment of several cancer-related signaling pathways in tumor tissues, particularly those associated with “proteoglycans in cancer”, the “p53 signaling pathway”, “focal adhesion”, and “lysosome” ( ). GSEA confirmed the marked enrichment of KRAS and p53 signaling pathways in Tumor-epi ( ).
 

## Pseudotime trajectory and CCC analyses
Pseudotime trajectory analysis of epithelial cells delineated the putative origin and developmental progression of Tumor-epi cells during thyroid carcinogenesis. The inferred lineage topology exhibited a branched structure emanating from the Epi-1 subpopulation, rather than from Normal-epi cells. Along pseudotime, the trajectory diverged into two distinct terminal branches, predominantly occupied by Normal-epi and Tumor-epi cells, respectively. This hierarchical organization indicates that the Epi-1 subpopulation may represent a stem-like or progenitor epithelial cell compartment. Therefore, PTC tumor cells are more plausibly derived from malignant transformation within this progenitor pool (Epi-1), rather than directly from Normal-epi cells, which aligns with the cancer stem cell hypothesis ( ). The CellChat framework was employed to further explore the intricate intercellular communication networks involving Tumor-epi cells. The results demonstrated that Tumor-epi cells exhibit extensive communication capacities and engage in dense interactions with various cell types ( ). Notably, Tumor-epi cells displayed significant crosstalk with macrophages through multiple ligand-receptor pairs, including APP-CD74 and MIF-(CD74 + CXCR4), implicating that they possibly modulate the tumor immune microenvironment and contribute to immune evasion ( ). In addition, a comparative analysis of signaling pathways, including EGF, MIF, and FN1, revealed distinct variations across Tumor-epi and Normal-epi in both target cell type selection and signal transduction intensity ( ). Tumor-epi cells showed a pronounced tendency to establish complex communication networks with immune cells, stromal cells, and other tumor-associated cell populations through these signaling axes, thereby promoting tumor proliferation, migration, and immune escape. The alterations in these signaling networks underscore their critical roles in the tumor microenvironment and may offer novel therapeutic targets for TC.
 

## Identification of Tumor-epi-associated modules via hdWGCNA
hdWGCNA, renowned for its reliability in single-cell transcriptomic network analysis, was carried out to delineate co-expressed gene clusters in Tumor-epi populations. Network construction employed a soft threshold (β=5) to achieve scale-free properties ( ), yielding 15 biologically relevant gene modules ( ), with visualized module eigengene (ME) expression profiles ( ). To elucidate the activity patterns of these modules across Tumor-epi and Normal-epi, our study examined the expression of their respective MEs. Notably, several modules exhibited marked differential expression between the two cell populations ( ). The yellow, turquoise, green, and blue modules demonstrated elevated expression in Tumor-epi cells, with the yellow module showing the highest average expression, suggesting a potential association with the malignant state. In contrast, the brown, cyan, red, and magenta modules were more active in Normal-epi cells, indicating their possible roles in maintaining epithelial homeostasis. These differential activation patterns underscore the biological relevance of the identified modules. The expression features of the tumor- and normal-associated modules were further characterized, and inter-module correlations were investigated ( ).
 

## Identification of Tumor-epi signature genes using multiple ML algorithms
Based on the hdWGCNA findings, genes from the yellow module intersected with the DEGs between Tumor-epi and Normal-epi cells, yielding 70 candidate genes ( ). To identify the most informative biomarkers, feature selection was performed through four ML algorithms, encompassing XGBoost, Boruta, SVM-RFE, and RF, using data from the TCGA cohort. In the XGBoost model, the importance of each gene was quantified via the gain metric and the top 10 genes were selected ( ). The Boruta algorithm ranked genes based on their importance in an RF model, identifying the top 10 most relevant features ( ). SVM-RFE performed with 10-fold cross-validation enabled the selection of 25 genes according to average ranking ( ). RF analysis provided a comprehensive ranking of all candidate genes, from which 5 genes with a mean decrease in Gini index greater than 5 were selected ( ). The overlap among the four algorithms was visualized via Venn diagrams, with three identified key genes shared across all methods: XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 ( ).
 

## Construction of an ML-based predictive model for PTC
ROC curve analyses were conducted to assess the diagnostic performance of XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 in the TCGA-THCA cohort. All three genes demonstrated excellent discriminatory capacity for distinguishing PTC from normal thyroid tissues, with area under the curve (AUC) values exceeding 0.90 ( ). Using the optimal cutoff determined by the Youden index, XPR1, selected as the representative gene, achieved an accuracy of 89.9%, a sensitivity of 88.9%, and a specificity of 98.3%. Validation using the independent GSE33630 dataset further confirmed the robustness of these findings, with all three genes maintaining strong diagnostic performance. Among them, XPR1 exhibited the highest accuracy (96.8%), followed by TLE1 (93.6%) and SH3RF1 (89.4%) ( ). To further illustrate the expression patterns underlying this diagnostic performance, the expression distributions of these genes were examined in the TCGA cohort. XPR1 expression was significantly elevated, whereas SH3RF1 and TLE1 were significantly reduced in PTC tissues compared with normal controls (all P<0.001), thereby reinforcing their validity as diagnostic biomarkers ( ). To enhance clinical applicability, a nomogram incorporating XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 was constructed to predict the risk of PTC. The nomogram enables individualized risk estimation based on gene expression scores ( ). Calibration curves showed consistent forecast and real outcomes ( ), while DCA ( ) indicated a positive net clinical benefit at various thresholds. Therefore, our findings establish a diagnostic model based on XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1. Although fine-needle aspiration (FNA) remains the gold standard, this molecular signature demonstrates substantial translational potential as an adjunctive tool to improve diagnostic accuracy, particularly in nodules with indeterminate cytology, thereby guiding personalized clinical management.
 

## Relationship between XPR1, SH3RF1, TLE1 and the tumor microenvironment
To unravel the relationship between XPR1, SH3RF1, TLE1, and the immune microenvironment, CIBERSORT was employed for infiltration estimation of 22 immune cell sorts in all TCGA-THCA samples. The percentages of immune cells were visualized ( ). Comparative analysis using boxplots revealed significant differences in immune cell distribution across sample groups ( ). Notably, M0 and M1 macrophages, regulatory T, resting and activated dendritic, and resting mast cells were markedly elevated in tumor tissues. Subsequently, interrelations among the 22 immune cell types were analyzed within the tumor microenvironment ( ). Building upon this quantitative immune landscape, our study further investigated the correlations between the expression of XPR1, TLE1, and SH3RF1 genes and specific immune cell types ( ). The results demonstrated significant associations between these genes and multiple immune cell subsets, implicating their involvement in tumor immune evasion and highlighting their potential as targets for future immunotherapeutic interventions.
 

## qRT-PCR validation of hub gene expression
To experimentally validate the bioinformatic findings, qRT-PCR was performed in the BCPAP and NC. The relative messenger RNA (mRNA) expression of the three hub genes (XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1) was quantified using GAPDH as the internal control. The results demonstrated that XPR1 was significantly upregulated in BCPAP cells compared with NC, whereas SH3RF1 and TLE1 were both markedly downregulated in BCPAP cells relative to NC (all P<0.001, ). These findings were consistent with our computational predictions and support the robustness of the identified hub genes as potential diagnostic biomarkers for PTC.
 

## Discussion
The prevalence of PTC has risen markedly worldwide over decades ( ). This trend is largely attributed to the widespread cancer screening and the increased use of imaging modalities including neck ultrasonography. These advancements have facilitated the early detection of many asymptomatic and low-risk PTC cases. However, such increased detection has not led to a commensurate improvement in survival outcomes ( ). Although PTC is generally considered an indolent malignancy, a subset of patients remains at risk of metastasis, which contributes significantly to recurrence and poor prognosis ( ). Consequently, the identification of critical molecular markers is essential not only for improving diagnostic precision of disease and prediction of metastatic potential but also for informing personalized therapeutic strategies.
In our study, scRNA-seq data of PTC and adjacent normal thyroid tissues were thoroughly analyzed, revealing marked alterations in cellular composition. In particular, a significant increase in epithelial cell proportion was observed in tumor tissues, underscoring their pivotal role in tumor progression. With the focus on epithelial cells, clustering analysis was performed to identify Tumor-epi and Normal-epi. DEGs between these groups were visualized using volcano and heat maps, and highly expressed genes in Tumor-epi cells were subjected to functional enrichment analysis. GO analysis indicated their enrichment in migration-related processes like cytoskeletal remodeling and cell adhesion, localized predominantly to cell-matrix and adhesion structures, and were functionally associated with enzyme inhibition, cadherin binding, and kinase regulation. Collectively, these findings point toward enhanced cellular motility and invasiveness. KEGG analysis demonstrated notable enrichment of upregulated genes in the “proteoglycans in cancer” pathway, the p53 signaling pathway, focal adhesion, and lysosomal pathways. Prior studies have shown that proteoglycans modulate tumor cell invasion, proliferation, and migration by regulating cell-extracellular matrix interactions ( ). In hepatocellular carcinoma, focal adhesion proteins have been identified as tumor suppressors that influence cell adhesion, migration, and prognosis ( ). Furthermore, lysosomal and p53 signaling pathways have been increasingly recognized for their roles in cancer biology ( - ). GSEA further demonstrated activation of the KRAS and p53 signaling pathways in Tumor-epi cells. Notably, although BRAF mutations are the predominant drivers in PTC, this study revealed significant activation of the KRAS and p53 signaling pathways in Tumor-epi cells, underscoring the heterogeneity of signaling pathways in PTC. As a member of the RAS family, KRAS integrates the BRAF-activated MAPK cascade (RAF-MEK-ERK), thereby modulating cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Even in the absence of upstream stimuli, KRAS mutations can maintain constitutive activation, thereby promoting PTC initiation and progression ( ). Concurrently, p53 mutations disrupt cell-cycle regulation and apoptotic pathways, leading to genomic instability and enhanced tumor invasiveness, features that are particularly pronounced in advanced or high-grade PTC subtypes. Therefore, the molecular mechanisms underlying PTC entail the stepwise accumulation of multiple signaling pathways, rather than exclusive dependence on BRAF. This interpretation is consistent with established biological principles and aligns with the findings of the present study ( ). Integrating the results from GO, KEGG, and GSEA analyses, our study systematically delineates the involvement of upregulated genes in Tumor-epi cells in key biological processes and signaling pathways, particularly those related to tumor cell migration, invasion, transcriptional regulation, metabolic reprogramming, and immune evasion. These mechanisms appear to be crucial in the initiation and progression of PTC. Moreover, the enrichment of KRAS and p53 pathways reinforces their significance in PTC and identifies them as promising therapeutic targets.
Pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed the distinct developmental trajectory of Tumor-epi cells. Through CCC analysis, Tumor-epi cells exhibited markedly stronger communication capacity than Normal-epi cells, engaging in more extensive interactions with various cell types. Among the signaling pathways analyzed, EGF, MIF, and FN1 were selected due to their established roles in modulating tumor proliferation, migration, and immune evasion within the tumor microenvironment ( , , ). Comparative analysis of these pathways highlighted notable differences in signal transduction dynamics between Tumor-epi and Normal-epi cells, unraveling the molecular mechanisms of tumor progression.
Through hdWGCNA and ML, three key genes were ultimately identified: XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 ( ). XPR1 is currently recognized as the sole phosphate exporter in mammals and is crucial in intracellular phosphate homeostasis ( ). XPR1 expression is markedly upregulated in ovarian epithelial carcinoma, tongue squamous cell carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma, where it contributes critically to tumor pathogenesis ( - ). In PTC, elevated expression of XPR1 has been associated with poorer prognosis, potentially by promoting tumor progression through activation of the BRAF-ERK-p53 signaling pathway ( ). SH3RF1, or POSH, is a multifunctional protein comprising four SH3 domains and an N-terminal really interesting new gene (RING) domain. It has been shown to negatively regulate FAT1 protein levels by inhibiting its ubiquitination ( ). Given that FAT1 is overexpressed in various malignancies, including lung and TCs, and is closely linked to unfavorable clinical outcomes ( ), it was hypothesized that SH3RF1 may participate in the progression of TC by modulating the stability of FAT1. TLE1 is a transcriptional corepressor that exerts its function through interactions with transcription factors and serves as a key modulator in multiple signaling pathways. In hepatic tissues, suppression of TLE1 activates the NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting hepatocarcinogenesis ( ). Nevertheless, how TLE1 influences TC remains elusive and warrants further investigation. Based on these three hub genes, an ML-based diagnostic model was constructed for predicting PTC. Validation in an independent cohort proved the robust accuracy and stability of the model. In addition to the computational analyses, we validated the expression of the three hub genes in BCPAP cells and NC by qRT-PCR. The results showed that XPR1 was significantly upregulated in PTC, whereas TLE1 and SH3RF1 were downregulated, which was consistent with our scRNA-seq and bulk transcriptomic findings. This experimental evidence enhances the robustness of the diagnostic model and underscores the potential clinical value of these genes as biomarkers for PTC. Clinically, this three-gene signature demonstrates potential as an adjunctive molecular assay for thyroid nodules with indeterminate cytology (Bethesda categories III and IV). Integration of these biomarkers into preoperative FNA testing may improve risk stratification and reduce the incidence of unnecessary thyroidectomy. Furthermore, CIBERSORT enabled the assessment of the relationships between XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 and immune cell population composition within the tumor immune microenvironment. Notable links of these genes to various immune cell subsets indicate their involvement in immune evasion and their potential as targets for immunotherapeutic intervention.
Despite new perspectives on the molecular underpinnings of PTC and the identification of XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 as potential regulators of tumor immune escape, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the scRNA-seq analysis relied on a limited number of biological replicates (one tumor vs. one normal), which may restrict the representativeness of the identified cellular landscape. To overcome this limitation, large-scale bulk transcriptomic cohorts (TCGA and GSE33630) were integrated, and in vitro experimental validation using qRT-PCR was performed to ensure the robustness and generalizability of the identified biomarkers. Second, clinical validation of these three genes remains insufficient, particularly regarding their diagnostic applicability and predictive accuracy in patients with PTC. It is important to note that the nomogram was constructed using RNA-seq-derived fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) values to represent normalized gene expression levels. As clinical qRT-PCR assays typically provide relative expression estimates (2⁻ΔΔCt), direct clinical implementation would require assay-specific recalibration of the model in future translational studies. Before their establishment as viable biomarkers, further in vitro experiments are essential for the verification and characterization of their biological functions. Future studies will focus on elucidating the regulatory mechanisms and clinical relevance of XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 in PTC progression. Third, the present study primarily relied on publicly available datasets, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Future research incorporating multi-center clinical samples will be crucial for independent validation and refinement of these results, thereby enhancing their translational potential. Collectively, our study introduces novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets for the early diagnosis and personalized management of PTC. Nevertheless, translating these genetic findings into clinical practice, particularly for prognostication and treatment response prediction, remains an important challenge. Future work should further investigate the complex interplay between these genes and the immune microenvironment to inform the innovative immunotherapeutic strategy formulation.

## Conclusions
The heterogeneity of epithelial cell subpopulations in PTC was systematically characterized by integrating single-cell transcriptomics, hdWGCNA, and multiple ML algorithms. Our study identified a Tumor-epi subset and delineated its unique transcriptional features and functional pathways. Furthermore, XPR1, SH3RF1, and TLE1 were pinpointed as key driver genes. A diagnostic model built on these three genes demonstrated excellent discriminatory performance across multiple cohorts (AUC >0.90) and revealed potential mechanisms of immune evasion. This multidimensional integrative framework not only elucidates the molecular basis of PTC onset and advancement but also lays a theoretical foundation for diagnostic refinement and targeted therapeutic strategies.
