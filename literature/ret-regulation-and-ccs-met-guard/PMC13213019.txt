While targeted RNA-based next-generation sequencing (NGS) has enhanced diagnostic precision through fusion gene detection, its clinical utility remains limited for dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS) due to the inability to identify MDM2 copy number variations - a critical diagnostic feature. This limitation is particularly clinically relevant as morphologically atypical DDLPS cases may undergo targeted RNA NGS testing. Through retrospective analysis of 150 patients undergoing RNA NGS and validation with TCGA-SARC data, we developed a novel screening algorithm for DDLPS using RNA NGS data, specifically the number of fusion events within the 12q13–15 chromosomal region and MDM2 transcript levels. Both metrics were significantly elevated in DDLPS compared to non-DDLPS cases in our institutional cohort (p < 0.001). ROC analysis established optimal diagnostic thresholds: ≥3 fusions in 12q13-15 region achieved 100% sensitivity (95% CI: [73.53%-100%]; 12/12) and 95.65% specificity (95% CI: [90.78%-98.39%]; 132/138). Similarly, MDM2 RNA expression ≥ 100 TPM showed 100% sensitivity (95% CI: [63.1%-100%]; 8/8) and 97.37% specificity (95% CI: [90.8%-99.7%]; 74/76). The algorithm also performed robustly in the TCGA-SARC data, although with a slightly different optimal cutoff, likely due to differences in gene panels, depth of sequencing, gene coverage, and bioinformatics pipelines. This study presents an effective strategy for screening DDLPS and potentially other MDM2-amplified sarcomas.

## Introduction
Diagnosing soft tissue sarcomas and bone tumors has long posed challenges for pathologists due to their rarity and diversity. In recent years, identifying driver gene fusions has significantly enhanced the diagnosis of soft tissue sarcomas and bone tumors. Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) and reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) methods are routinely used to detect gene fusions but are limited to known events and can identify only a few rearranged gene pairs per test.
RNA-based next-generation sequencing (NGS) provides high-throughput, parallel sequencing capabilities, enabling the comprehensive analysis of multiple fusion genes with exceptional sensitivity – . However, routine RNA-based NGS analyses focus solely on detecting driver fusion genes and lack the capability to assess copy number variations (CNVs), making it challenging to identify CNV-driven sarcomas such as MDM2-amplified STS like dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS). In diagnostically challenging cases of DDLPS lacking classical morphological features, pathologists may initially delay conventional MDM2 amplification tests, such as FISH and immunohistochemistry (IHC), and instead primarily rely on RNA-based NGS to identify diagnostically significant fusion genes. In the absence of CNV data, conventional RNA NGS may potentially lead to diagnostic confusion, for example, low-grade DDLPS harboring HMGA2 arrangement may be misdiagnosed as aggressive angiomyxoma through routine RNA fusion analysis, especially in the presence of stromal mucin-rich background.
In this study, we evaluate and validate an RNA NGS analytical framework that employs the spatial clustering of fusion transcripts within 12q13-15 chromosomal region and the quantification of MDM2 RNA expression level as a primary screening method to identify DDLPS and potentially other MDM2-amplified sarcomas.

## Materials and methods

## Study design and data collection
This study retrospectively analyzed RNA-based NGS and CNV data from patients diagnosed with bone and soft tissue tumors at the Second Xiangya Hospital between January 1, 2022, and May 1, 2024 (N = 150). RNA fusion and CNV data from The Cancer Genome Atlas Sarcoma (TCGA-SARC, N = 206) and The Skin Cutaneous Melanoma (TCGA-SKCM, N = 367) project were also utilized in this analysis. The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board at the Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (Approval No. LYEC2024-K0126). Based on the study’s retrospective design and complete anonymization of participant data, the Committee granted a waiver of informed consent.
All samples in our institutional cohort were procured from treatment-naïve tumors at the time of biopsy or complete resection, with the exception of two post-chemotherapy specimens (Case 20, Ewing sarcoma; Case 53, clear cell sarcoma-like tumor of the gastrointestinal tract).

## Clinical diagnostic NGS procedures
All NGS procedures were conducted in the Molecular Pathology Laboratory at the Second Xiangya Hospital. The detailed clinical diagnostic NGS procedures are outlined below.

## Isolation of DNA and RNA
Tumor cellularity was assessed by reviewing hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained slides, evaluated by two or more pathologists. Tissues with ≥ 20% tumor cellularity were selected for further analysis. The actual tumor cellularity of included samples ranged from 40% to 95% (median 90%), with the majority (90.7%, 136/150) having ≥ 80% cellularity. No significant correlation was observed between tumor cellularity and the number of fusion events or MDM2 TPM values (p > 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. ). Tumor DNA was extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens using the TIANamp FFPE DNA Kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA extraction was performed using the Novogene RNA Extraction Kit (Novogene, Tianjin, China). DNA and RNA quantities were measured with a Qubit 3.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Additionally, DNA purity was assessed with a NanoDrop ND-1000 Spectrophotometer (NanoDrop, Waltham, MA, USA). The quality of the nucleic acids was determined using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). For downstream processing, samples were considered acceptable if the DNA exhibited an OD260/280 ratio exceeding 1.6, and the RNA had a DV200 of at least 30%.

## DNA NGS and CNV analysis
DNA libraries were constructed using the Solid Tumor 1238-gene Mutation Detection kit (Novogene, Tianjin, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Supplementary Table ). Sequencing was performed on the NextSeq 550Dx platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The sequencing data were analyzed with an in-house Molecular Diagnostics Management System. This analysis aimed to identify various alterations, including single nucleotide variants (SNVs), insertions/deletions (INDELs), CNVs, and fusions. The CNV analysis pipeline was developed using CNVkit (version 0.9.11) to analyze DNA NGS data from paired tumor and matching blood samples, allowing for more accurate identification of copy number variations by comparing tumor genomic alterations to the normal genomic background . Subsequent visualization of the CNV results was performed using an R script to facilitate interpretation and presentation of the data.

## RNA NGS, fusion detection, and transcript qualification
Hybrid capture-based targeted RNA NGS was performed using the clinical RNA fusion panel (Supplementary Table ), consisting of either 507 genes for 66 patients or 1166 genes for 84 patients (Novogene, Tianjin, China). In this process, 100 ng of total RNA was reverse transcribed using a random primer mix. The synthesized cDNA underwent end-repaired and was subsequently used to generate sequencing libraries according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The enriched libraries were sequenced on the NextSeq 550Dx platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA).
The sequencing depth achieved for each panel was as follows: for the 507-gene panel (n = 66), the mean number of total reads per sample was 8.34 million (range: 3.87–14.31 million); for the 1166-gene panel (n = 84), the mean number of total reads per sample was 43.5 million (range: 18.0–177.4.0.4 million) (Supplementary Table ). These depths exceeded the minimum requirements (≥ 3 million reads for the 507-gene panel and ≥ 12 million reads for the 1166-gene panel), ensuring adequate coverage for reliable fusion detection and MDM2 expression quantification.
The sequencing data were subsequently analyzed with an in-house pipeline to identify fusion transcripts, utilizing the STAR aligner (version 2.7.11a) for alignment and Arriba (version 2.4.0) for fusion detection , . The 507-gene panel requires over 3 million high-quality sequencing reads, while the 1166-gene panel needs at least 12 million reads to ensure adequate gene coverage. Transcript qualification was performed using the Python package HTSeq (version 2.0.5) , and transcripts per million (TPM) were used to normalize gene expression.
For the purpose of counting fusions in the 12q13-15 chromosomal region, a fusion event was included if at least one of the partner genes mapped to 12q13-15.

## TCGA sarcoma and TCGA skin cutaneous melanoma (TCGA-SKCM) data source
The clinical data of the TCGA sarcoma (TCGA-SARC) project were downloaded from the supplementary data of the original research paper . RNA fusion and CNV data were obtained from the Sarcoma TCGA PanCancer data available on the cBio Cancer Genomics Portal (https://www.cbioportal.org/study/summary?id=sarc_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018) and Skin Cutaneous Melanoma TCGA PanCancer data available on the cBio Cancer Genomics Portal (https://www.cbioportal.org/study/summary?id=skcm_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018) , . The GISTIC (Genomic Identification of Significant Targets in Cancer) score was used to represent the frequency and significance of the CNV in specific genome regions . In TCGA-SKCM dataset, a case was classified as “MDM2-amplified” if the putative copy-number alteration value was ≥ 1, as determined by the GISTIC algorithm—a threshold that denotes copy-number gain in the cBioPortal database.
The detailed RNA fusion detection pipeline of the TCGA Pan Cancer Atlas was described in a previous article . In brief, RNA fusion calling was performed using STAR-Fusion, integrating its results with EricScript and the BREAKFAST algorithm. This pipeline further filters fusion candidates based on their relative abundance and removes low-confidence calls using a blacklist .

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Immunohistochemical staining was conducted on FFPE tissue sections using the MaxVisionTMHRP-Polymer anti-Mouse/Rabbit IHC Kit using antibodies specific to MDM2, CDK4, and MelanA. The primary antibody for MDM2 was a monoclonal mouse antibody (Catalog: MAB-0774, Fuzhou Maixin Biotech.Co.,Ltd, China). CDK4 was detected with a monoclonal mouse antibody (Catalog: RMA-0771, Fuzhou Maixin Biotech.Co.,Ltd, China). The primary antibody for MelanA was a monoclonal mouse antibody (Catalog: MAB-0275, Fuzhou Maixin Biotech Co.,Ltd, China).

## Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)
Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was performed on 3-µm formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections using a break-apart probe targeting the EWSR1 locus (22q12; LBP Medicine, Cat# F.01194-01) and a dual-color MDM2 Gene-Specific Probe (CSP)/Chromosome Enumeration Probe 12 (CEP12) probe (LBP Medicine, Cat# F.01017-01) according to the manufacturer’s protocols (FISH-Tissue Implementation Kit, Guangzhou LBP Medicine Science & Technology Co., Ltd., China). For signal interpretation, 200 morphologically intact tumor nuclei with non-overlapping borders and homogeneous DAPI staining were evaluated per case. EWSR1 rearrangement was considered positive if > 15% of nuclei exhibited split signal patterns (1F1R1G/1F1R/1F1G), whereas fused (2 F) or adjacent signals (< 2 signal diameters apart) defined negative status. MDM2 amplification was assessed by MDM2 (red)/CEP12(green) ratio (≥ 2.0 = amplified; <2.0 = non-amplified).

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses and visualizations were performed using R version 4.3.2. Continuous variables were summarized as median (interquartile range [IQR]) unless otherwise specified. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare the number of fusions and MDM2 RNA expression levels between the DDLPS and non-DDLPS groups. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to evaluate associations between fusion counts and continuous variables, including GISTIC scores, log2-transformed copy number values, and MDM2 RNA expression levels to ensure robustness against outliers. To evaluate the performance of fusion events and MDM2 RNA expression in distinguishing DDLPS from non-DDLPS cases, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using the pROC package (version 1.18.5) in R. Optimal cutoff values were determined by maximizing the Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1). For all diagnostic performance metrics (sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value [PPV], negative predictive value [NPV]), 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using the exact binomial method (Clopper-Pearson) via the binom.test function in R. The 95% CI for the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was computed using DeLong’s method as implemented in the ci.auc function of the pROC package. A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Fusion events in 12q13-15 as a potential marker for DDLPS
In this study, we retrospectively reviewed 150 sarcoma cases that underwent targeted RNA NGS. Based on morphological features, initial histopathological assessments classified Cases 1, 2, and 3 as solitary fibrous tumor, undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, and myxofibrosarcoma, respectively (Table  ; Fig.  D, and Supplementary Fig.  ). Subsequent DNA-based NGS analysis revealed MDM2 and CDK4 amplification in all three cases (Fig.  A-C). These molecular findings were further validated by IHC analysis, with representative images from Case 2 showing nuclear positivity for both MDM2 and CDK4 (Fig.  E, F). Definitive diagnosis of DDLPS was established through combined molecular evidence and exclusion of alternative lineages via negative staining for SMA, HMB45, MelanA, S100, Myogenin, CD117 and others (data not shown). Notably, fusion events clustered within the 12q13-15 chromosomal region were observed in all three index cases, despite the absence of driver gene fusions (Fig.  G-I). These observations led us to hypothesize that fusion events clustering in the 12q13-15 region may serve as a potential marker for DDLPS. To further investigate this hypothesis, we extended our analysis to an additional 147 sarcoma cases that had undergone targeted RNA sequencing.

 

 

Although Cases 4–6 were not initially diagnosed as DDLPS based on morphological features, definitive diagnoses were established through FISH detection of MDM2 amplification, which was prompted by the retroperitoneal localization of the tumors. In the remaining 144 cases, supplementary FISH testing identified MDM2 amplification in eight sarcomas (Cases 7–14), of which six (Cases 7–12) were reclassified as DDLPS after excluding definitively differentiated sarcomas (Table  ).
We next investigated the recurrent fusion events, and identified the CTDSP2::DNM3OS fusion in 4 out of 12 DDLPS cases (Table  ), but it was not detected in any of the 138 other sarcoma cases. The MIR-214 cluster was found to be embedded within the DNM3OS locus as previously characterized (Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, all 12 confirmed DDLPS cases (Cases 1–12) exhibited ≥ 3 fusion events within the 12q13-15 chromosomal region, despite lacking lipogenic differentiation (Supplementary Fig.  ). Circos plot analysis revealed 12q chromosomal region as the predominant site of intra- and inter-chromosomal rearrangements in DDLPS, followed by 1q chromosomal region (Fig.  A). This rearrangement clustering was absent in non-DDLPS cases (Fig.  B). A clear distinction emerged in 12q13-15 fusion burden, with all DDLPS cases consistently showed ≥ 3 fusions, whereas most non-DDLPS cases (excluding Cases 13–18) exhibited < 3 fusions (Fig.  C). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve established ≥ 3 fusions in 12q13-15 chromosomal region as the optimal diagnostic threshold, yielding 100% sensitivity (95% CI: [73.53%−100%]; 12/12), 95.65% specificity (95% CI: [90.78%−98.39%]; 132/138), and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.994 (95% CI: [0.984–1.984] (Fig.  D). The confusion matrix confirmed the model’s strong performance, with an overall accuracy of 96.00% (144/150), a positive predictive value (PPV) of 66.67% (95% CI: [40.99%−86.66%]; 12/18), and a negative predictive value (NPV) of 100% (95% CI: [97.24%−100%]; 132/132) (Fig.  E).

 

## Challenges in diagnostic specificity of fusion events
A total of six cases (Cases 13–18) exhibited ≥ 3 fusion events in the 12q13-15 region but did not meet the diagnostic criteria for DDLPS. Case 13 (36-year-old female) presented clustered four fusion events in 12q13-15 (Figs.  C and A), with imaging features suggestive of both osteogenic and osteolytic sarcoma (Fig.  D). However, core needle biopsy limitations precluded definitive microscopic confirmation of osteogenic differentiation (Fig.  B). FISH analysis revealed MDM2 amplification (Fig.  C), and integration with radiographic findings led to the reclassification as dedifferentiated variant of low-grade central osteosarcoma. Case 14, a unique case exhibiting malignant melanocyte differentiation (Fig.  H) and pulmonary metastasis (Supplementary Fig.  B), harbored 3 fusion events in 12q13-15 (Figs.  C and E), with FISH confirming MDM2 amplification (Fig.  G). Additionally, EWSR1 break-apart FISH results were negative (Supplementary Fig.  A). The initial diagnosis of Case 14 was uncertain until MRI revealed dermal involvement (Supplementary Fig.  C, D). Ultimately, it was diagnosed as malignant melanoma, despite the absence of epidermal lesion. Case 15, in spite of harboring 14 fusion events in 12q13-15 (Figs.  C and I), did not exhibit MDM2 amplification by FISH (Fig.  K) or DNA-NGS (data not shown) and was diagnosed as parosteal osteosarcoma based on CT features (Fig.  L) and H&E stains (Fig.  J). In Case 16, 17, and 18, the original diagnostic classifications were maintained based on the absence of MDM2 amplification (Table  ).

 

## Diagnostic value and limitations of MDM2 RNA expression levels for DDLPS
In 84 of 150 cases, we expanded our RNA-seq NGS gene panel from 507 to 1166 genes, incorporating MDM2 gene in the updated panel. Among these, all 8 DDLPS cases exhibited high MDM2 expression (Transcript per million (TPM) ≥ 100), whereas other sarcoma cases typically had TPM values below 100, except for cases 19 and 20 (Fig.  A). The optimal threshold (≥ 100 TPM), determined by ROC curve analysis, yielded a sensitivity of 100% (95% CI: [63.06%−100%]; 8/8) and a specificity of 97.37% (95% CI: [90.82%−99.68%]; 8/8), with an AUC of 0.993 (95% CI: [0.981–1.981]) (Fig.  B). The confusion matrix further validated the model’s strong performance, demonstrating an overall accuracy of 97.56% (82/84), a PPV of 80.00% (95% CI: [44.39%−97.48%]; 8/10), and a NPV of 100% (95% CI: [95.14%−100%]; 74/74) (Fig.  C). Case 19 harbored a driver fusion event, SLMAP::BRAF (Fig.  A), and was diagnosed as an unclassified sarcoma of the jaw with BRAF translocation . It was histologically characterized by osteoclastic giant cells and the absence of definitive pathological differentiation (Fig.  B). The osteoclastic multinucleated giant cells in case 19 exhibited positive MDM2 expression by IHC staining (Fig.  C), while showing non-amplified MDM2 in DNA NGS (Fig.  D). The MDM2 expression should be attributed to the osteoclastic giant cells rather than amplified status of MDM2. Case 20, a classic Ewing sarcoma with EWSR1::ERG fusion (Fig.  E), was evaluated for MDM2 amplification status using both DNA NGS and FISH. DNA NGS performed on the post-therapy biopsy sample showed no evidence of MDM2 amplification (Fig.  H), and FISH analysis confirmed the absence of MDM2 amplification in both pre- and post-chemotherapy biopsy samples. Despite the lack of genomic amplification, MDM2 RNA and protein upregulation was observed in post-chemotherapy samples (Figs.  A and F and G). This suggests that elevated MDM2 expression in this case may be attributable to treatment-related factors rather than true genomic amplification. This evaluation demonstrates that the MDM2 RNA expression threshold (≥ 100 TPM) should be interpreted with caution in the post-treatment setting. The RNA NGS analytical framework (either the fusion events in 12q13-15 ≥ 3 or MDM2 RNA TPM value ≥ 100) results of 150 sarcomas in our institutional cohort are summarized in Fig.  .

 

 

 

Consistent with these observations, integration of the RNA NGS–based screening strategy with confirmatory MDM2 testing (FISH or DNA NGS) enabled the reclassification of several cases in our cohort. As summarized in Table  , a total of 8 tumors with MDM2 amplification were reassigned diagnoses based on combined molecular and clinicopathological evaluation, including 6 cases ultimately classified as DDLPS.

## Validation of the fusion events and RNA expression diagnostic strategy in TCGA-SARC cohort
To validate our diagnostic strategy for DDLPS, we analyzed 50 MDM2-amplified DDLPS cases and 156 non-DDLPS cases (absence of MDM2 amplification) from the TCGA-SARC dataset. DDLPS exhibited significantly higher number of rearrangements in the 12q13-15 region compared to the non-DDLPS cohort (P < 0.001), with a cut off value of ≥ 2 fusion events demonstrating robust diagnostic performance (sensitivity 94.00% (95% CI: [83.45%−98.75%]; 47/50); specificity 99.36% (99.36% CI: [96.48%−99.98%]; 155/156); AUC: 0.978 (95% CI: [0.949–1.949])). (Fig.  A). Additionally, DDLPS displayed significantly higher MDM2 RNA expression levels than non-DDLPS cases (P < 0.001), where a TPM ≥ 80 threshold achieved perfect discrimination (100% sensitivity/specificity, AUC: 1.00) (Fig.  B).

 

## Rearrangements correlated with amplifications in 12q13-15
Analysis of 50 DDLPS cases with MDM2 amplification from the TCGA-SARC dataset revealed that rearrangements and amplifications were most frequently observed on chromosome 12q15 (median fusion count: 4; IQR: 2–6), with lower frequencies on 12q14 (median fusion count: 3; IQR: 1–4) and 12q13 (median fusion count: 1.5; IQR: 0–3) (Supplementary Fig.  ). Correlation analysis showed a strong positive relationship between the number of fusion events and the amplification levels (GISTIC score) within the same chromosomal bands, with 12q15 exhibiting the highest frequency of both fusion events and the amplification levels (Fig.  C). Furthermore, a significant positive correlation was observed between the frequency of rearrangements in 12q13-15 and MDM2 RNA expression levels in these cases (Fig.  D).
Considering that MDM2 amplification can occur in a subset of malignant melanoma , and noting that Case 14 in our cohort exhibited MDM2 amplification, we expanded our analysis to include malignant melanoma cases from the TCGA-SKCM dataset. Among 59 malignant melanoma cases with MDM2 amplification, rearrangement and amplification events occurred more frequently in 12q15 (Supplementary Fig.  A). Additionally, there was a significant positive correlation between rearrangements frequency in 12q13-15 and MDM2 amplification levels in these 59 cases, while no such relationship was observed in 308 cases of MDM2 non-amplified malignant melanoma (Supplementary Fig.  B).

## Discussion
Underdiagnosis of DDLPS in clinical practice can be attributed to three main factors: (1) histologic absence of lipogenic differentiation components, (2) predominance of histologically low-grade dedifferentiated tumor morphology, and (3) non-retroperitoneal tumor localization – . In our retrospective cohort of 150 cases, most DDLPS cases were initially misdiagnosed as other tumors because of the absence of atypical lipomatous tumor/well-differentiated liposarcoma components. By analyzing the patterns of passenger fusion events in the RNA-based NGS data of these 150 cases, we found that ≥ 3 fusion events in the 12q13-15 region were highly effective in distinguishing DDLPS from other sarcomas, achieving an overall diagnostic accuracy of 96.00% (144/150). Among the 18 cases with ≥ 3 fusion events in the 12q13-15 region, FISH or DNA NGS confirmed MDM2 amplification in 14 cases. Of these, 12 cases were diagnosed as DDLPS, while the remaining two were diagnosed as dedifferentiated variant of low-grade central osteosarcoma and malignant melanoma, respectively (Table  ). As a result, when considering MDM2-amplified sarcomas, the overall diagnostic accuracy increased to 97.33% (146/150). Our diagnostic strategy that employs the spatial clustering of fusion transcripts within 12q13-15 region also performed well in the TCGA-SARC dataset, although the cutoff value was lower (≥ 2 fusions), likely reflecting differences in sequencing platforms, library preparation methods, and bioinformatics pipelines applied to the TCGA data . The small discrepancy in diagnostic sensitivity (94.00% in the TCGA dataset vs. 100% in our institutional cohort) may be attributed to methodological differences in the fusion-calling algorithms employed.
MDM2 expression can be quantified using RNA NGS data, however, these expression profiles are often underutilized in clinical diagnostics. Notably, in the TCGA-SARC dataset, MDM2 expression shows perfect sensitivity and specificity (both 100%) for distinguishing DDLPS from other sarcomas. In our institutional cohort, however, caution is required in two scenarios: neoplasms with abundant osteoclastic giant cells and post-chemotherapy sarcomas. Osteoclastic giant cells in giant cell tumor of bone and chondroblastoma are moderately to strongly positive for MDM2 . For instance, Case 19, a sarcoma of the jaw with a BRAF translocation, showed numerous osteoclastic giant cells resembling those found in giant cell tumors of bone, which may explain the observed MDM2 overexpression in an MDM2 non-amplified sarcoma. Additionally, MDM2 gene upregulation is a known mechanism of chemotherapy resistance , . In our study, Case 20 is a classic Ewing sarcoma with EWSR1::ERG fusion, exhibited MDM2 upregulation in post-chemotherapy samples. In previous study, MDM2 gene expression was significantly higher in chemotherapy-treated metastases compared to chemo-naïve primary tumors in testicular germ cell tumors . MDM2 overexpression may also indicate a more aggressive tumor phenotype, potentially linked to chemotherapy resistance and increased recurrence risk – . Thus, although the dual-marker approach (fusion count plus MDM2 RNA expression) flagged this post-treatment case for FISH validation, the discordance observed highlights the need for cautious interpretation of RNA expression, particularly in treated tumors.
DDLPS is characterized by recurrent focal amplifications within the 12q13-15 chromosomal region , . Chromosomal rearrangements, including both inter- and intra-chromosomal events involving chromosome 12, are also observed in DDLPS , . Fusion events clustered in 12q13-15 region are evident in DDLPS cases, suggesting their potential as a diagnostic marker. Notably, both amplification and fusion events occur in close proximity within these chromosomal regions, and a strong positive correlation between these phenomena was observed (Fig.  C and Supplementary Fig.  ). This may be explained by chromothripsis and seismic amplifications, which could drive these genomic alterations. It has been reported that all cases of DDLPS exhibit chromothripsis, followed by the formation of circular extrachromosomal DNA. This DNA undergoes multiple rounds of recombination, ultimately resulting in highly complex amplicon structures with high copy number levels and multiple rearrangements, a phenomenon referred to as seismic amplifications, and this process primarily concentrated on chromosome 12 , . Chromothripsis, first described in 2011, involves the fragmentation of one or a few chromosomes into tens to hundreds of 0.1–10 Mb fragments, which are subsequently randomly re-ligated, creating massive, clustered genomic rearrangements . During chromothripsis and circularization, flanking rearrangements at the edges of broken segments occur first and are supported by high read counts due to subsequent repetitive amplification. While chromothripsis leads to oscillating copy number states, it does not inherently cause high-level amplifications. Instead, circular recombination generates lots of internal rearrangements within the amplicon, characterized by lower read counts. The number of internal rearrangements correlates with the number of CNV states, contributing to the complexity of these amplicons . These findings provide a compelling explanation for the strong correlation observed between 12q13-15 fusion events and 12q13-15 amplifications.
Besides DDLPS, chromothripsis is also observed in skin melanoma, affecting approximately 70% of cases , . Furthermore, 18.8% of skin melanoma cases exhibit seismic amplification . Among 367 melanoma cases from the TCGA-SKCM dataset, 59 exhibited MDM2 amplification, with amplification levels correlating with the number of rearrangements in 12q13-15 region (Supplementary Fig.  ). This positive correlation may also be attributed to chromothripsis and seismic amplifications. In patients with malignant sarcoma presenting without lesions in the skin or mucosa, whose samples test positive for HMB45, MelanA, and S100, the most critical differential diagnosis is clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue. During RNA NGS analysis, it is essential to focus not only on EWSR1-related rearrangements but also on other seemingly inconsequential fusions. Clustering of structural variations in 12q13-15 region likely indicates MDM2 amplification, which may suggest a diagnosis of metastatic melanoma. For example, Case 14 is a non-DDLPS MDM2-amplified sarcoma. Although no lesions of the skin epidermis were observed in Case 14, discussions during the molecular tumor boards led to diagnosis of malignant melanoma based on the absence of specific rearrangements involving clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue. MDM2 amplification has been linked to metastatic malignant melanoma and hyperprogression during immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy , but is not associated with clear cell sarcoma of soft tissue. In fact, this case represents primary melanoma regression with evident metastases, which has been frequently reported in previous literature – .
Recent studies have expanded our understanding of chromosome 12 chromothripsis beyond MDM2-amplified sarcomas. Valle-Inclan et al. reported chromothripsis in parosteal osteosarcomas involving chromosome 12 without high-level amplifications, while Saoud et al. described low-grade fibro-osseous lesions with isolated chromosome 12 chromothripsis lacking MDM2/CDK4 amplification , . This spectrum is exemplified by our Case 15—a parosteal osteosarcoma with complex rearrangements in chromosome 12, potentially indicative of chromosome 12 chromothripsis. Interestingly, case 15 lacked MDM2 amplification by FISH and DNA-NGS, and with a low MDM2 RNA expression (TPM value: 41.12). These observations suggest that chromosome 12 chromothripsis can occur independently of subsequent amplifications, representing a potential early event in tumorigenesis. Clinically, detection of clustered 12q13-15 fusions should prompt MDM2 FISH confirmation, but absence of amplification does not exclude isolated chromothripsis, which may still carry diagnostic significance.
The DNM3OS gene is located in the chromosomal region 1q24.3 and encodes the MIR199A2-MIR214 microRNA cluster , . In a previous study, rearrangements involving DNM3OS were identified in 11 of 51 cases of DDLPS . These rearrangements were believed to promote tumor cell proliferation and contribute to DDLPS progression, and they were not observed in ALT/WDLPS. In our study, DNM3OS fusion events were identified in 4 out of 12 cases of DDLPS, with all fusions involving the partner gene CTDSP2 (Table  ). Morphological evaluation indicated that DNM3OS fusions were present in both high-grade and low-grade DDLPS, exemplified in Case 10, a low-grade DDLPS harboring the CTDSP2::DNM3OS fusion (Supplementary Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). Given that only one previous study has reported DNM3OS fusions in DDLPS, further investigation is needed to clarify its functional role and significance in DDLPS.
Our approach leverages existing RNA NGS data to opportunistically screen for MDM2 amplification, primarily serving to rule out DDLPS or MDM2-amplified sarcoma with high sensitivity and NPV. Screen-positive cases—based on either 12q13–15 fusion count or MDM2 expression, necessitating confirmatory FISH or DNA-based testing. This method complements direct MDM2 amplification testing by flagging cases that might otherwise be overlooked, particularly when classic DDLPS features are absent. Although the two RNA-based parameters individually achieve near-ceiling diagnostic performance, they offer complementary utility: fusion count is universally applicable across RNA panel designs, while MDM2 expression provides orthogonal evidence when coverage permits.
This study has several limitations. First, the targeted RNA sequencing panels used in our institutional cohort were not uniform: a 507-gene panel was applied to 66 samples, while an expanded 1166-gene panel was used for the remaining 84 samples. Although both panels provided coverage of the 12q13-15 region for fusion detection, the 507-gene panel did not include MDM2, restricting MDM2 expression analysis to the 1166-gene panel cohort. This heterogeneity may have influenced the sensitivity of fusion detection, particularly in the smaller panel, and could affect the generalizability of the optimal cutoff value (≥ 3 fusions) established in this study. Given the variability in clinical RNA panel designs across laboratories, each institution should validate its own threshold based on panel composition, sequencing depth, and bioinformatics pipelines. Second, the number of DDLPS cases in our institutional cohort (n = 12) is relatively small, which may widen confidence intervals and affect precision. To address this, we validated our findings in the independent TCGA-SARC cohort (50 DDLPS, 156 non-DDLPS). The consistent diagnostic performance across cohorts supports the generalizability of our approach. Nevertheless, future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to refine this strategy.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, we established and validated an RNA NGS analytical strategy for screening MDM2-amplified tumors. By incorporating either the number of fusion events within the 12q13-15 region or MDM2 RNA expression levels, this approach enables efficient identification of cases that warrant confirmatory testing. When combined with MDM2 FISH, this strategy facilitated the reclassification of previously misdiagnosed tumors, including multiple cases of DDLPS in our cohort.
