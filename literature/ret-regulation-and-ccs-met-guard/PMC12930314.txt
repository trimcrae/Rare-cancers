3.3

## Effects of O3 Exposure on Pathological
Changes in the Hematopoietic System
Next, the hematopoietic
pathology was examined systematically. The results revealed distinct
O3 concentration-dependent alterations across hematopoietic
compartments ( A–F). For bone marrow, the density of nucleated cells was
significantly decreased ( C). Pathological sections showed that with increasing
the O3 exposure concentration, myeloid hematopoietic cells
gradually became sparse, disorganized, and scattered, accompanied
by a reduction in their distribution area; meanwhile, expansion of
cellular space and increased lipid droplet accumulation were observed.
An increase in the number of megakaryocytes was also detected, which
was consistent with the results of quantitative assessment ( B,C). For the spleen,
in the 0.50 ppm exposure group, the white pulp exhibited an irregular
structure, and the boundary between white pulp and red pulp was gradually
blurred. Quantitative analysis revealed an increase in red pulp area
accompanied by a decrease in white pulp area, leading to an elevated
red pulp-to-white pulp area ratio ( E–F). These splenic alterations are
highly indicative of extramedullary hematopoiesis, a compensatory
response to impaired bone marrow function. Collectively, these findings
indicate that exposure to ambient O3 induces structural
alterations in the bone marrow and spleen. These results provide direct
morphological evidence supporting 0.50 ppm of O3-induced
anemia. However, underlying molecular mechanisms need to be further
explored.
 
3.4

## Effects of O3 Exposure on Transcriptional
Alterations in Anemia
The bone marrow is the major hematopoietic
organ in adults; thus, the mRNA expression profiles were sequenced
(Figure S5). Transcriptomic analysis of
the 0.50 ppm group identified 564 DEGs, with 313 upregulated genes
and 251 downregulated genes ( A). GSEA showed that compared with the control, genes
were significantly enriched in Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, Th17 cell differentiation, and hematopoietic
cell lineages after O3 exposure ( B). However, there was no significant
change in the proportion of immune cells in bone marrow between the
control group and the O3 exposure group ( C). While total immune cell
proportions remained unchanged, functional activation was evidenced
by enriched T cell pathways.
 KEGG pathway analysis was also performed ( D), and the results
showed that Th1
and Th2 cell differentiation (mmu04658), Th17 cell
differentiation (mmu04659), hematopoietic cell lineage (mmu04640), T cell receptor signaling pathway (mmu04660),
and antigen processing and presentation (mmu04612)
were the most significantly enriched pathways. In addition, GO enrichment
analysis revealed that O3 exposure led to distinct immune-inflammatory
perturbations ( E–G and Table S3). BP terms were
mainly immune-related pathways ( E), including adaptive immune response (GO:0002250), immune response (GO:0006955), immunoglobulin-mediated immune regulation (GO:0016064), T cell receptor signaling pathway (Go:0050852), antigen processing and presentation of exogenous peptide antigen
via MHC class II (GO: 0019886), and peptide antigen
assembly with MHC class II protein complex (GO:0002503).
Consistently, CC were highlighted in significant enrichment
in immunoglobulin complex (GO:0019814) and MHC class
II protein complex (GO:0042613) ( F). MF terms were mainly MHC class
II protein complex binding (GO:0023026) and antigen
binding (GO:0003823) ( G). Consistent with GSEA results, the predominant DEGs
clustered within hematopoietic regulation pathways, particularly those
governing myeloid cell differentiation and lymphoid lineage commitment.
This multilevel convergence strongly implicates the O3-induced
disruption of bone marrow immunoregulation, potentially establishing
a proinflammatory microenvironment that contributes to anemic pathogenesis.

3.5

## Identification of the Most Immune-Related
Genes
Further, DEGs participating in O3-induced
immune-inflammatory dysregulation in bone marrow microenvironments
were filtered, and 50 IR-DEGs were identified ( A), comprising 45 upregulated and 5 downregulated
genes (Table S4).
 After GO enrichment analysis, IR-DEGs mainly participated
in adaptive immune response (GO:0002250), T cell receptor
signaling pathway (Go:0050852), positive regulation
of T cell activation (GO:0050870), immune response (GO:0006955), and antigen processing and presentation (GO: 0019882) ( B). KEGG pathway analysis also demonstrated that IR-DEGs were predominantly
enriched in three key immunological pathways, Th1 and Th2
cell differentiation (mmu04658), Th17 cell differentiation (mmu04659), and antigen processing and presentation (mmu04612) ( C). Notably, 30 core genes exhibiting significant pathway interconnectivity
were identified using KEGG pathways containing >10 genes (selection
threshold) ( D). After PPI network and Degree analysis, cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated
protein 4 (Ctla4), Cd247 molecule (Cd247), interferon
γ (Ifng), Cd3e molecule (Cd3e), lymphocyte
cell-specific protein-tyrosine kinase (Lck), Cd3g
molecule (Cd3g), and C–C motif chemokine ligand 5 (Ccl5) were
considered as the hub genes ( E).

3.6

## Prediction of Transcription
Factors
After that, upstream transcription factors (TFs)
governing the expression
of IR-DEGs were identified. NetworkAnalyst analysis identified 22
evolutionarily conserved TFs exhibiting pan-regulatory potential but
only 6 targeting to more than 3 IR-DEGs, including chromodomain helicase
DNA binding protein 1 (Chd1), Jun proto-oncogene,
AP-1 transcription factor subunit (Jun), transcription
factor 12 (Tcf12), NK2 homeobox 1 (Nxi1), nuclear respiratory factor 1 (Nrf1) and Jund proto-oncogene, AP-1 transcription factor subunit (Jund) ( A and Table S5). Comparative analysis
with DEGs profiles identified Jund as the significantly
dysregulated TF, forming a key transcriptional hub governing immune-inflammatory
gene networks (Figure S6).
 To confirm transcriptomic findings, hub gene expression
patterns
were validated by qRT-PCR. Quantitative analysis revealed significant
O3-induced overexpression of Jund (1.15-fold, p = 0.008), Ccl5 (1.12-fold, p = 0.005), Ifng (1.26-fold, p =
0.045), and Lck (1.20-fold, p =
0.033), while other genes showed nonsignificant alteration (p > 0.05) ( B). The expression profile of seven hub genes (Ctla4, Cd247, Ifng, Cd3e, Lck, Cd3g, and Ccl5), along with transcription factor Jund, exhibited
concordant upregulation with RNA-seq data ( B,C). To further explore the association
between these genes and phenotypic data, Spearman correlation analysis
was performed for target genes and hematopoietic indices (Figure S7). The results showed that Jund was significantly negatively correlated with erythroid indices (RBC
count, p < 0.05; absolute/relative reticulocyte
counts, p < 0.05) and CFU-GEMM (p < 0.05), while being significantly positively correlated with
Lym (p < 0.05). Ccl5 exhibited
a correlation trend consistent with that of Jund,
but Lck was only significantly negatively correlated
with HGB (p < 0.05). For Ifng, its correlation with hematopoietic indices did not reach statistical
significance (showing a trend consistent with those of Jund and Ccl5). These findings suggest that O3-induced target genes (Jund, Ccl5, Lck, Ifng) expression changes
are associated with hematopoietic regulation, promoting lymphocyte
proliferation and activation while suppressing erythroid lineage differentiation
and maturation. To validate this regulation at the protein level,
key molecules (Jund, Ifng, Ccl5, and Lck) were analyzed using ELISA.
The results showed elevated protein levels both systemically (in serum)
and in a tissue-specific context (bone marrow cells), confirming the
production and secretion of functional proteins ( D,E).

## Discussion
Previous studies indicated
that ambient O3 exposure
may be related to anemia, while the results remain controversial.
,,
 To address this knowledge gap,
we employed an animal exposure model to provide more explicit evidence.
After 28 days of O3 (0.50 ppm) exposure, significant reductions
in erythroid parameters (RBC, HGB, HCT, and Ret) and hematopoietic
colony-forming capacity (CFU-GM, CFU-GEMM, and total colony-forming
counts) were observed, which can promote the occurrence of anemia.
Meanwhile, the increase of Lym indicated that O3 activates
the immune system. Mechanistically, transcriptomic profiling identified Jund as a key transcription factor to regulate a network
of immune-inflammatory-related DEGs, including Ifng, Lck, and Ccl5. Collectively,
these findings provide critical experimental evidence supporting the
epidemiological hypothesis that O3 exposure to enhances
the risk of anemia.
Anemia, characterized by decreased hemoglobin
concentration or
impaired oxygen-carrying capacity of erythrocytes,
,
 requires comprehensive evaluation using erythrocytic indices for
classification. As a critical indicator
for assessing peripheral blood composition and bone marrow hematopoietic
function, hematological analysis in the present study revealed that
O3 decreased erythroid peripheral blood parameters (RBC,
HGB, HCT) in a dose-dependent manner, while no obvious erythrocytic
morphological adaptations were observed. This finding is consistent
with previous evidence from animal models and epidemiological investigations. In contrast, others reported transient O3-induced elevation
of erythroid parameters, but the discrepancy
may be due to (1) physiological adaptations in specific populations,
specifically that compensatory erythropoietic mechanisms in high-altitude
residents under chronic hypoxia may maintain oxygen saturation; (2) exposure paradigm differences, specifically
that our study employed long-term exposure in normal mice rather than
specialized models. Ret dynamics is a sensitive indicator of bone
marrow hematopoietic activity and erythropoiesis.
,
 Notably, our results showed significant decreases in both relative
and absolute counts after O3 exposure, which was similar
to previous reports.
,
 This confirms that exposure to
ambient O3 can lead to impaired erythroblast maturation
and diminished medullary erythropoietic cells. Emerging evidence underscores
the critical role of immune-inflammatory dysregulation in anemia. Consistent with this, the present study found
that O3 exposure significantly increased Lym, aligning
with observations from a recent cross-sectional study in a rural population. Furthermore, the significant negative correlation
between lymphocytes and the relative/absolute counts of reticulocytes
and RBC is likely attributable to immune cells driving inflammation
(Figure S4A,D,E), thereby disrupting erythropoiesis. Collectively, these lines of evidence indicate
that O3-induced anemia may arise from lymphocyte-driven
immune dysregulation, which interferes
with the production and functional integrity of RBC.
Further
investigation is warranted to determine whether immune-inflammatory
activation impairs erythropoiesis by disrupting the differentiation
of early hematopoietic progenitor cells. Hematopoietic stem and progenitor
cells (HSPCs) colony-forming assays are instrumental in assessing
this process. HSPCs reflect the differentiation potential through
the multipotent CFU-GEMM stage (generating mixed colonies containing
granulocytic, erythroid, monocyte-macrophage, and megakaryocytic components),
progressing to BFU-E and CFU-GM. Previous
studies have indicated that air pollutants (e.g., benzene, formaldehyde,
particulate matter, nitrogen dioxide) impaired colony-forming capacity
of bone marrow nucleated cells (BMNCs) derived BFU-E, CFU-GM, and
CFU-GEMM.
,−

 In the present study,
O3 significantly reduced CFU-GM, CFU-GEMM, and total colony
counts in murine bone marrow, which is consistent with previously
published findings,
,
 suggesting O3-induced
erythroid suppression originates from disrupted upstream hematopoietic
regulatory. To elucidate the causes underlying impaired hematopoietic
differentiation, BMNCs were quantified, and significant depletion
was observed in the 0.50 ppm exposure group. Combining with our observation
of BMNC depletion with prior research evidence,
,
 the observed colony deficits likely arise from O3-induced
exhaustion of primitive HSPCs pools or impaired HPC proliferative
capacity. Crucially, while bone marrow serves as the primary hematopoietic
organ containing HSCs, the spleen harbors a minor HSC population capable
of extramedullary hematopoiesis under stress conditions.
,
 Histopathological analysis revealed a decreased hematopoietic area
in the bone marrow and structural disorganization in the spleen, providing
tissue-level explanations for anemia pathogenesis. Taken together,
these multilevel perturbations in the hematopoietic process can be
important incentives related to O3-induced mice anemia.
Emerging evidence indicates that immune-mediated inflammatory responses
represent a key mechanism through which environmental exposures induce
hematopoietic damage.
,,−

 Previous single-cell transcriptomic studies have
suggested that benzene can trigger hematopoietic stem cell toxicity
via immune-related gene networks. In
this study, 30 core DEGs involved in immune–inflammatory networks
were identified in bone marrow cells. These findings are consistent
with our earlier conclusions from studies on NO2/PM2.5 exposure regarding the involvement of immune inflammation
in hematopoietic regulation,
,
 yet also reveal pollutant-specific
patterns of alteration. These differences may stem from variations
in the chemical properties of different pollutants, heterogeneity
of their molecular targets within the hematopoietic system, and distinct
regulatory effects under different exposure parameters.
Jund, a member of the AP-1
transcription factor family, is a key regulator of immune–inflammatory
processes and modulates multiple hub genes, including Ccl5, Lck, and Ifng. Existing studies have confirmed that Jund plays an important role in fine-tuning hematopoietic lineage differentiation.
,
 This characteristic also explains why the “moderate change”
in Jund transcript levels observed in this study produces significant
functional effects, suggesting that its role depends not on drastic
alterations at the single-gene level but on coordinated interactions
within a downstream gene network. Specifically, multiple hub genes
downstream of Jund disrupt hematopoietic homeostasis
through multidimensional mechanisms, ultimately leading to anemia.
Among these, Ccl5, a critical regulator of immune
cell recruitment and hematopoietic niche homeostasis, is significantly upregulated in the bone marrow and peripheral
blood of anemia patients. In inflammatory
contexts, Ccl5 disrupts niche stability and skews
hematopoietic commitment toward megakaryocytic–erythroid progenitors. The present study also revealed a strong correlation
between Ccl5 expression and lymphocyte abundance,
suggesting synergistic interactions between lymphocytes and Ccl5. Lck promotes helper T cell differentiation
and IFN-γ secretion. As a central
inflammatory cytokine, IFN-γ directly inhibits erythroid colony
formation, contributes to erythrocyte deficiency, and reprograms the
transcriptional profile of hematopoietic stem cells. This observation aligns with established mechanisms such
as mercury exposure suppressing hematopoietic stem cell proliferation
via Ifng
 and mycobacterial
infection shortening red blood cell lifespan through Ifng. Notably, we validated the coordinated
upregulation of these genes at both the mRNA and protein levels, further
supporting their roles in the O3-induced hematopoietic
toxicity.
It should be noted that this study also has several
limitations.
First, the current CFC assay fails to assess lymphoid progenitor evaluation
(CFU-PreB), limiting comprehensive assessment of O3 effects
on lympho-myeloid differentiation balance. Future studies should incorporate
lymphoid-specific colony assays to fully elucidate the effects of
O3 lineage-selective impacts on HSPCs niches. Furthermore,
future work should establish multiscenario O3 exposure
models (hyperoxia, normoxia, hypoxia) to clarify their underlying
mechanisms on blood-oxygen homeostasis.

5

## Conclusions
This study established
the O3 exposure animal model,
integrating multidimensional evaluations including peripheral parameter
abnormalities, HSPC differentiation disorders, hematopoietic tissue
heterogeneity, and transcriptome sequencing, to systematically elucidate
the toxicological pathway through which O3 induces anemia
in mice via the immune-inflammatory axis. These findings not only
provide theoretical evidence for O3-induced hematopoietic
damage but also offer experimental evidence for targeted prevention
of environmental exposure-associated hematological disorders.
