## Highlights
•CircBTBD7-420aa is a novel protein encoded by hsa_circ_0000563.
•CircBTBD7-420aa, as an E3 ubiquitin ligase, promotes the degradation of SLC3A2 through the ubiquitin-proteasome system, thereby regulating the progression of atherosclerosis.
•CircBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes can target vascular smooth muscle cells by targeting osteopontin.

## Summary
Circular RNAs are associated with cardiovascular disease, including coronary artery disease, but the mechanisms have not been completely elucidated. We found a new protein, circBTBD7-420aa, encoded by hsa_circ_0000563. Our results suggest that circBTBD7-420aa may inhibit the abnormal proliferation and migration of human coronary artery smooth muscle cells by promoting SLC3A2 degradation through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. In addition, we constructed engineered exosomes loaded with circBTBD7-420aa that can target vascular smooth muscle cells by modifying peptide fragments targeting osteopontin. This study suggests that circBTBD7-420aa may inhibit the progression of atherosclerosis and serve as a new target for the diagnosis and treatment of coronary artery disease.

As the major cause of morbidity and mortality around the world, coronary artery disease (CAD) is a common cardiovascular disease. Although there have been significant improvements in diagnostic models and therapeutic choices over the past few decades, there is an urgent need to explore novel molecular diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for CAD. Over the past decade, plenty of studies have shown that circular RNAs (circRNAs) play a vital role in the occurrence and development of CAD. , , Recently, the protein-coding function of circRNAs has been identified in heart, but the expression and function of protein encoded by circRNAs in CAD remain uncertain.
Recently, gene therapy has emerged as a novel and powerful treatment method for CAD. However, the lack of specificity and low bioavailability of the present delivery methods limit its therapeutic effects. As therapeutic carriers and diagnostic markers of CAD, exosomes have caught extensive attention because of their certain properties of good biocompatibility, low toxicity, and immunogenicity. , , The therapeutic potential of native exosome and bioengineered exosome have been only minimally studied in the context of CAD. Targeted delivery of circRNAs and their functional products via the bioengineered exosome platform represents a promising approach for CAD therapy, but no related study is currently available.
In our previous studies, we identified a down-regulated circRNA, hsa_circ_0000563 (also termed circBTBD7), in stenosed segments of human coronary artery lesions and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from subjects with CAD, suggesting that circBTBD7 may be a biomarker of CAD. In the present study, the expression level of circBTBD7 was down-regulated in the oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL)–induced abnormal proliferation and migration model of human coronary artery smooth muscle cells (HCASMCs). Overexpression of circBTBD7 with plasmid suppressed the abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs. In addition, a 420-amino-acid (aa) peptide encoded by circBTBD7 was identified. Further validations demonstrated that circBTBD7-420aa was responsible for the functions of circBTBD7 by interacting with the SLC3A2 protein and activating the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. Furthermore, targeted HCASMC delivery of circBTBD7-420aa via the bioengineered exosome platform has been constructed successfully.

## Methods

## Study subjects
This study included human coronary artery samples, an abnormal proliferation and migration model of HCASMCs induced by ox-LDL, an atherosclerosis animal model, and a bioengineered exosome platform. The study scheme has been summarized as 6 main stages: stage 1, to characterize the existence and subcellular distribution of circBTBD7; stage 2, to verify the translatability of circBTBD7; stage 3, to explore the function of circBTBD7-420aa encoded by circBTBD7 in vitro; stage 4, to validate the mechanism underlying the function of circBTBD7-420aa; stage 5, to confirm the function of the protein encoded by circBTBD7 in vivo; and stage 6, to construct the bioengineered exosomes platform in which there was targeted HCASMC delivery of circBTBD7-420aa. The Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University approved all experimental protocols of the present study, according to the approved guidelines, and the animal experiments conducted in this study were approved by the Laboratory Animal Committee of Nanjing Medical University (no. IACUC-2211034). All procedures performed during the study were meticulously carried out following the institutional ethical guidelines for the care and maintenance of laboratory animals, ensuring compliance with ethical standards throughout the research process.

## Tissue preparation, cell culture, and atherosclerosis animal model construction
The human coronary artery samples were harvested from the autopsies of 4 donors, supported by the informed consent of their families. 
HCASMCs were sigma-derived and cultured in smooth muscle medium containing 2% fetal bovine serum, 1% smooth muscle cell growth supplement, and 1% penicillin and streptomycin (1101, ScienCell). The abnormal proliferation and migration model of HCASMCs was established with stimulation of ox-LDL (Yiyuan Biotechnologies) at a concentration of 50 mg/L for 24 hours. 
Human embryonic kidney epithelial cell line (HEK-293T, HEK-293F) (The National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures) was cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin and streptomycin. The culture environment was a constant temperature incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2.
A total of 18 6-week-old male ApoE–/– mice were purchased from the Charles River for the study. They were randomly assigned into 3 groups, with 6 mice in each group: a negative control group, an empty lentivirus group, and a circBTBD7-420aa lentivirus group. The negative control group was fed with a normal diet, whereas the second and third groups were fed with a high-fat diet containing 20% fat and 11.25% cholesterol for a duration of 4 months. Lentiviral vectors expressing circBTBD7-420aa-flag was cotransfected into HEK-293T cells with lentiviral packaging constructs psPAX2 and pMD2.G (VSV-G) in a ratio of 4:3:2, resulting in the production of lentiviruses. The lentiviruses were subsequently harvested and concentrated. Ultracentrifugation was used for viral concentration. Each mouse was injected with a 200-μL suspension (1 × 108 transducing units (TU)/mL of empty lentivirus or circBTBD7-420aa lentivirus) through the tail vein. After 2 months of atherogenic diet feeding, the mice were fully anaesthetized and sacrificed.

## Fluorescence in situ hybridization
Paraffin sections of human coronary artery samples were sliced after embedding, then retrieved by a spreader, and baked at 62 °C for 2 hours. The subsequent procedures included dewaxing and dehydration; digestion; prehybridization; washing, blocking, and incubating the second antibody; incubating with 4,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole for 8 minutes in the dark, and then mounting.

## Sanger sequencing, RNase R, and actinomycin D treatment
To corroborate the presence of circBTBD7 in HCASMCs, convergent and divergent primers were designed to conduct polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using the complementary DNA (cDNA) and genomic DNA of HCASMCs. The circBTBD7 sequence was achieved by means of divergent primers sent to Tsingke for the analysis of Sanger sequencing. RNase R (Geneseed) was able to degrade almost all linear RNAs, but not circular ones. After extracting the total RNA from HCASMCs, it was divided equally into 2 parts: one was digested with RNase R (20 U/μL), and the other was not. After RNase R was added to the RNA, it was incubated at 37 °C for 30 minutes, and then 2 mg/mL actinomycin D (Merck) was added into the cell culture medium. After treatment with actinomycin D and RNase R, quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted to estimate the relative abundance of linear BTBD7 and circBTBD7.

## RNA extraction and qRT-PCR
Total RNA from cells was extracted using TRIzol (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and then the concentration and purity of all RNA were evaluated by the NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). A reverse transcription kit (Vazyme) was used to synthesize cDNA. Quantitative PCR was done using a qRT-PCR kit (Vazyme) on a CFX96 real-time PCR system (Bio-Rad). Relative expression of genes was determined using 2−ΔΔCt and normalized to the level of GAPDH. All primers are shown in .

## Nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction
The PARIS kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was intended for the separation of nucleic and cytoplasmic fractions. Concisely, HCASMCs were lysed on ice with Cell Fractionation Buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 10 minutes. Then, after centrifugation at 4 °C and 500g for 3 minutes, the upper layer of supernatant was harvested as the cytoplasmic fraction, and the pellets were lysed by Cell Disruption Buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) to collect the nuclear fractions. Ultimately, the nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA was extracted in accordance with the instructions.

## Plasmid construction and small interfering RNA transfection
The full length of circBTBD7 cDNA was synthesized and cloned into the pEX-3 vector (GenePharma) using the Xhol and EcoRI sites, which contains flanking sequences to induce circularization. CircBTBD7-mut, circBTBD7-420aa-flag, circBTBD7-420aa-flag-mut, and SLC3A2 cDNA were synthesized and cloned into a pLVX-puro vector (Tsingke). These vector sequences are shown in . Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting SLC3A2 were designed and synthesized by RiboBio. The siRNA sequences are shown in . Plasmids were transfected into HCASMCs or HEK-293T using TransIntro EL transfection reagent (TransGen).

## Western blot
Cells were collected, and proteins were extracted using radio immunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Beyotime) supplemented with protease inhibitor at 4 °C. Protein concentration was then measured by a bicinchoninic acid assay kit (Beyotime) and denatured at 95 °C for 10 minutes after the dilution of 5× loading buffer. The same quantities of protein samples were added to sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis gels and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes, which were then blocked with Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 buffer containing 5% skim milk for 2 hours at room temperature. Target proteins were detected with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight, and the next day the polyvinylidene difluoride membranes were washed by Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 and incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 2 hours at room temperature. Finally, the target protein bands were visualized by ECL reagent (Beyotime). The antibodies used were as follows: BTBD7 (ABclonal), SLC3A2 (ABclonal), Flag (ABclonal), Ub (R&D Systems), K48-Ub (Abcam Technology), K48-Ub (Abcam Technology), immunoglobulin G (ABclonal), V5 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) tag, and β-actin (Sigma-Aldrich).

## Proliferation and migration assays
The 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) and Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) (Biosharp) assays were conducted to evaluate HCASMC proliferation. For the EdU assay, HCASMCs were seeded in a 12-well plate with EdU reagent for 3 hours, and cell viability was then measured via an EdU kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For the CCK-8 assay, 3,000 cells were inoculated per well in 96-well plates. CCK-8 was performed to monitor the viability of cells at the appropriate time. Optical density 450 values were determined by a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).
For cell migration assays, 2 × 104 cells were seeded into the upper chambers. HCASMCs that migrated into the lower chambers were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 5% crystal violet. Random fields were captured, and the number of HCASMCs was counted. For the wound-healing assay, HCASMCs were plated to a 12-well plate. When the cell confluence reached 95%, we scratched vertical lines in the plate. Summarily, the plates were recorded at 0 hours and 24 hours by a microscope (Olympus).

## Coimmunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry
Immunoprecipitation (IP) and coimmunoprecipitation were conducted by means of a Pierce Classic Magnetic IP/Co-IP Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cells were lysed with cold lysis buffer, and the supernatant was collected after centrifugation at 12,000 revolutions per minute (rpm) at 4 °C for 10 minutes. Each sample contained a total of 1,000 µg of protein were incubated with specific IP antibody at 4 °C on a rotating platform overnight. The corresponding immunoglobulin G antibody was used as the control. Pierce Protein A/G Magnetic Beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were added to the antigen-antibody complexes and incubated at 4 °C for 3 hours. After washing with phosphate buffered saline and tween-20 the target antigen-antibody complexes were eluted with Elution Buffer, followed by Western blot or mass spectrometry analysis (BiotechPack).

## Immunofluorescence staining
HCASMCs were inoculated on glass coverslips and then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes at room temperature and permeabilized with phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.2% Triton X-100. Cell blockage was accomplished by 5% bovine serum albumin. Then, the cell slides were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibody (anti-Flag and anti-SLC3A2), followed by incubation with the corresponding fluorescent secondary antibody under light-proof conditions. Finally, cell nuclei were stained with 4,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole. Fluorescence was observed by laser confocal microscopy (Olympus).

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
The reporter vector constructions were carried out by inserting a full-length, truncated internal ribosome entry site (IRES) into dual-luciferase reporter vectors. HEK-293T cells were transfected with constructed reporter vectors, and the relative luciferase activities were detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System Kit (Yeasen) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Histopathology and immunohistochemistry
The aortic tissues from the mice were carefully dissected, removed, and subsequently fixed in 4% formaldehyde overnight at a temperature of 4 °C. Following fixation, the samples underwent a sequential dehydration process using ascending concentrations of alcohol solutions, followed by clearing with xylene. The dehydrated tissues were embedded in paraffin wax and sectioned into slices with a thickness of 5 μm. Hematoxylin and eosin staining and Masson staining were used to observe plaque morphology and collagen fibers. For immunohistochemistry experiments, antigen retrieval was performed using citric acid, followed by the blockade of endogenous peroxidase activity. After blocking in 3% bovine serum albumin in phosphate-buffered saline, the tissue sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-SLC3A2 antibody (1:200, 15193-1-ap, Proteintech), followed by incubation with a secondary antibody conjugated to horseradish peroxidase. Detection was subsequently performed using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine.

## Modification of circBTBD7-420aa–loaded exosomes with a peptide moiety targeting osteopontin
To explore the future clinical application of circBTBD7-420aa, we generated circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes that targeted the vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) phenotypic switching with a peptide moiety targeting osteopontin (OPN) (Leu-Arg-Ser-Lys-Ser-Arg-Ser-Phe-Gln-Val-Ser-Asp-Glu-Gln-Tyr-Pro-Asp-Ala-Thr-Asp-Glu). This involved transfection of HEK-293F cells with the MysPalm (myristoylation-palmitoylation) scaffold protein cicBTBD7-420aa open reading frame (ORF) fusion expression plasmid to obtain a universal exosome-LinkerA containing circBTBD7-420aa; preparation of a VSMC-targeted peptide (OPN-TP-LinkerB) recombinant protein with phenotypic switching and high expression of OPN; and incubation and connection of the universal exosomes of circBTBD7-420aa-LinkerA with OPN-TP-LinkerB to obtain the engineered exosomes of OPN-TP-circBTBD7-420aa loaded with circBTBD7-420aa within the membrane and displaying OPN-binding peptides on the membrane surface.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as the mean ± SEM from at least 3 experiments. Student’s t-test (2-sided) was applied for comparisons between 2 groups, and 1-way analysis of variance was performed for comparisons across multiple groups. Data normality was verified to ensure the correctness of parametric testing using Shapiro-Wilk test. GraphPad Prism version 8.3.0 (GraphPad Software) was used for all analyses, and a P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## CircRNA characterization of circBTBD7
CircBTBD7 was a circular RNA formed by the covalent connection of 2 exons of the host gene BTBD7 located on chromosome 14 ( ), and sequence alignment showed that circBTBD7 is highly homologous among several species ( ). Next, the results of the fluorescence in situ hybridization experiment indicated that circBTBD7 was abundantly expressed in smooth muscle cells in human coronary arteries ( ). The results revealed that the PCR product of circBTBD7 exhibited the expected band position on gel electrophoresis. The gel at this position was excised and subjected to Sanger sequencing, which confirmed the accurate back-spliced junction site ( ). Furthermore, the stability of circBTBD7 in HCASMCs was investigated. RNase R was implemented to digest the whole RNA, and it was observed that the level of circBTBD7 was almost unchanged, whereas the corresponding BTBD7 messenger RNA (mRNA) expression was significantly down-regulated ( ). The results of half-life assays suggested that the half-life of circBTBD7 exceeded 24 hours, whereas that of BTBD7 mRNA was about 5 hours in HCASMCs, indicating that circBTBD7 was more stable than linear BTBD7 ( ). In addition, RNA nucleocytoplasmic isolation experiments observed that circBTBD7 was prominently located in the cytoplasm ( ). 

## Verification of circBTBD7’s translatability
Multiple databases predicted that circBTBD7 contained the same ORF ( ), which strongly hinted that circBTBD7 maybe encode an undetected protein, and we named it circBTBD7-420aa ( ). CircBTBD7-420aa shares a 388-aa sequence (1-388 aa) with its corresponding linear mRNA translated protein. However, circBTBD7-420aa has a distinct 32-aa peptide (389-420 aa) next to the spanning junction of the ORF that is distinct from its corresponding linear mRNA translated protein ( red sequence, ). Next, we constructed empty vector, circBTBD7-420aa-flag, circBTBD7-420aa-mut-flag plasmids using pLVX-puro vector to authenticate the existence and activity of circBTBD7-420aa, and Western blot recognized circBTBD7-420aa-flag (lane 2) ( ). In addition, we found that the BTBD7 antibody (A18206, ABclonal) recognized the same immunogen sequence (ASNYPHSCSPRVGGNSQAQQTFIGTSSYSQQGYGCESKLYSLDHGHEKPQDKKKRTSGLATLKKKFIKRRK, 5-75aa) in circBTBD-420aa ( ). 
To further validate the endogenously expressed circBTBD7-420aa, lysate of HCASMCs was used to perform Coomassie blue staining experiments; subsequently, gels located at 25 to 80 kDa were subjected to mass spectrometry analysis, and the results unveiled that total 7 peptides matched circBTBD7-420aa—notably, one of which was a unique sequence (LLDPGLK) ( , ). Also, we applied AlphaFold 2.0 software (DeepMind) to predict the structure of circBTBD-420aa ( ). Because circRNAs lack free ends, they were found to be mainly dependent on IRESs or N6-methyladenosine modifications to accomplish the translation. We found 2 potential IRESs (IRES-1:53-202 and IRES-2:1096-1233) in the circRNAdb database, and the 2 IRESs were validated by dual-luciferase assays, respectively ( ). Concisely, these data collectively demonstrate that circBTBD-420aa indeed derives from circBTBD7 in HCASMCs.

## CircBTBD7 inhibits ox-LDL–induced abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs, mainly through circBTBD7-420aa
The expression of circBTBD7 was down-regulated in the ox-LDL–stimulated groups by the qRT-PCR experiment, and Western blot results hinted that the relative level of circBTBD7-420aa was down-regulated as well ( ). Compared with the control group, the expression of BTBD7 was not significantly changed in the ox-LDL–stimulated group ( ). Moreover, the immunofluorescence experiment showed that circBTBD7-420aa was located in the cytoplasm and nucleus ( ). Given that circBTBD7 may be a novel independent biomarker for diagnosing CAD and may affect the occurrence and development of CAD, we further investigated the biological functions of circBTBD7 and circBTBD7-420aa in HCASMCs. CircBTBD7 could perform its position as a noncoding RNA or a coding protein. To ascertain which form is indispensable for inhibiting the abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs, we obtained HCASMCs transfected with circBTBD7, circBTBD7-mut, circBTBD7-420aa, and circBTBD7-420aa-mut. The results of the CCK8 and EdU assays demonstrated that the proliferation abilities of HCASMCs treated by ox-LDL were inhibited by overexpression of circBTBD7 or circBTBD7-420aa, not circBTBD7-mut or circBTBD7-420aa-mut ( and ). ox-LDL–stimulated HCASMCs transfected with circBTBD7 or circBTBD7-420aa displayed a decreased migration rate by Transwell (Corning Incorporated) and wound-healing assays ( and ). Putting these results together, we concluded that circBTBD7 could not inhibit the abnormal phenotype after the start codon of the ORF is mutated from ATG to ATT. We can deduce that circBTBD7 inhibits HCASMC proliferation and migration by virtue of circBTBD7-420aa. 

## CircBTBD7-420aa inhibited ox-LDL–induced abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs, mainly through SLC3A2
To further explore the potential molecular mechanisms of circBTBD7-420aa in inhibiting ox-LDL–induced abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs, we performed IP experiments in circBTBD7-420aa-FLAG–overexpressing HCASMCs. The eluates were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, silver staining, and mass spectrometry analysis to determine circBTBD7-420aa potential binding partners ( ). First, we identified the presence of specific sequences of circBTBD7-420aa, and then, among the differentially expressed proteins, we found that SLC3A2 was a possible target related to proliferation ( , ). Furthermore, we corroborated that circBTBD7-420aa is indeed bound to SLC3A2 by co-IP experiments ( ). The results of immunofluorescence staining in HCASMCs transfected with circBTBD7-420aa-flag also exhibited the colocalization of circBTBD7-420aa with SLC3A2 ( ). Meanwhile, the characteristics of the combination of circBTBD7-420aa and SLC3A2 are successfully predicted through the swMATH website (https://www.swmath.org/software/17207) ( ). The results of Western blot showed that SLC3A2 was up-regulated in the ox-LDL–treated HCASMCs ( ) and verified the knockdown efficiency of siRNA targeting SLC3A2 ( ). Subsequently, EdU, CCK8, Transwell, and wound-healing assays determined the ability to suppress the proliferation and migration of knockdown of SLC3A2 ( and , and ). To confirm the biological function of circBTBD7-420aa dependent on SLC3A2, we determined that SLC3A2 was down-regulated in response to overexpression of circBTBD7-420aa ( ). We found that the proliferation and migration of ox-LDL–treated HCASMCs were halted by overexpressing circBTBD7-420aa; however, SLC3A2 overexpression could restore the reduction in proliferation and migration ability caused by the up-regulation of circBTBD7-420aa via CCK-8, EdU, Transwell, and wound-healing assays ( and , and ). 

## CircBTBD7-420aa directly interacted with SLC3A2 and impaired its stability via boosting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway
Because it was determined that circBTBD7-420aa exerts a biological role through SLC3A2, we further explored how circBTBD7-420aa affects the expression of SLC3A2. We applied the protein synthesis inhibitor cycloheximide (20 μg/mL) with or without circBTBD7-420aa for different times and indicated that the half-life of SLC3A2 was significantly shortened by overexpressing circBTBD7-420aa ( ). The degradation of SLC3A2 had significantly reduced after MG132 (20 μmol/L) inhibition of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway but not after chloroquine (20 μmol/L) inhibition of the lysosomal pathway ( ). These results suggest that SLC3A2 was degraded through the ubiquitin-proteasome system. The UbiBrowser website also predicted the ubiquitination site of SLC3A2 ( ). Subsequently, we further confirmed that SLC3A2 could function as a substrate to bind ubiquitin through IP experiments in HEK-293T cells ( ), and the level of ubiquitination of SLC3A2 was increased after overexpression of circBTBD7-420aa ( ). There are different types of polyubiquitin chains because of the various lysine residue sites of ubiquitin linkage, the most common of which are K48 and K63. Proteins with K48-linked polyubiquitination are commonly targeted for degradation by the proteasome, whereas K63 plays a role in regulating lysosomal function. Therefore, we further confirmed that the ubiquitinated chain type of SLC3A2 was K48 ( ); thus, it was concluded that SLC3A2 linked to the K48 ubiquitination chain was degraded by the proteasome. 

## CircBTBD7-420aa inhibits the progression of atherosclerosis in vivo
To further investigate the effect of circBTBD7-420aa on atherosclerosis and its effect on the expression of SLC3A2 in vivo, we constructed an ApoE–/– mouse atherosclerosis model. We divided 18 ApoE–/– mice into 3 groups: 1 control group and 2 high-fat diet–induced atherosclerosis groups. After 4 months of high-fat feeding, we administered either empty vector lentivirus or circBTBD7-420aa lentivirus via tail vein injection at a frequency of once every 2 weeks for a total of 2 months. Tissue samples were then collected for hematoxylin and eosin and Masson staining. The results showed that the degree of occlusion and collagen fibers in the empty vector lentivirus group were significantly more severe compared to the control group, whereas injection of circBTBD7-420aa lentivirus ameliorated this phenomenon ( ). These findings suggest that circBTBD7-420aa can partially inhibit the progression of atherosclerosis. Furthermore, immunohistochemistry analysis revealed a decrease in the expression level of SLC3A2 in the aorta of mice injected with lentivirus ( ). Based on the aforementioned results, we can conclude that circBTBD7-420aa may promote the degradation of SLC3A2, thereby regulating the abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs, ultimately inhibiting the advancement of atherosclerosis. 

## Construction of circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes
After elucidating the roles and mechanism of circBTBD7-420aa in CAD, we constructed circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes. The constructed plasmid (circBTBD7-420aa-MP peptide plasmid [p547-plasmid] was double-digested by Smal and Not1 and detected by gel electrophoresis. The fragment sizes of the enzyme digestion measured by gel electrophoresis were consistent with the expectation: 3,142 base pairs and 4,158 base pairs ( and ). In addition, as shown in , the results of Sanger sequencing validation were in line with expectations. Next, we used the NanoFCM assay (NanoReady FC-3100, Life Real, Hangzhou) to detect the particle size distribution and concentration of the exosomes derived from the p547-plasmid HEK-293F cells transfected, and the results showed that circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes (p547-Exo) matched the particle size distribution and concentration of exosomes ( ). Furthermore, NanoFCM assay results indicated that the positive rate of p547-Exo containing circBTBD7-420aa and OPN-targeted peptides was 45.2% ( ). According to the Western blotting results of V5 tag, it can be seen that compared with the negative control exosomes, the exosomes were enriched more after circBTBD7-420aa loading in p547-Exo ( ). In summary, we successfully constructed engineered exosomes loaded with circBTBD7-420aa targeting VSMCs. 

## Discussion
In the present study, we have reported the physiologic role and mechanism of a novel protein encoded by translatable circRNAs involved in the occurrence and development of CAD. Our main findings are as follows: The expression level of circBTBD7 is a marked reduction in the ox-LDL–induced abnormal proliferation and migration model of HCASMCs. Furthermore, we identified that circBTBD7 has a translational function, and the resultant protein circBTBD7-420aa inhibits the excessive proliferation and migration of HCASMCs and alleviates the progression of atherosclerosis. Mechanistically, circBTBD7-420aa acts as an E3 ubiquitin ligase to promote the degradation of SLC3A2 by the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Furthermore, the engineered exosomes targeting VSMCs were constructed based on the function of circBTBD7-420aa.
CAD is one of the major causes of morbidity and mortality around the world. The pathologic roles of circRNAs in CAD have not been comprehensively explored. Some circRNAs can be identified as new biomarkers for CAD, , and some circRNAs are involved in CAD via targeting miRNA and binding RNA-binding protein (RBP). However, whether the proteins or peptides encoded by circRNAs play a biological function in the progression of CAD is not yet known. In this study, previously published results showed that the expression of circBTBD7 was significantly down-regulated in coronary samples with severe coronary artery stenosis. 
CircBTBD7 was validated as a circRNA in PBMCs of CAD subjects, and its expression level in PBMCs of CAD subjects was significantly lower than in those of controls. In addition, a significant negative correlation between circBTBD7 and CAD was observed through univariate and multifactorial regression analyses. CircBTBD7 may be a protective factor and an independent predictor of CAD. However, how circBTBD7 plays a role in CAD caught our attention. We further identified circBTBD7-420aa, which translated from the spanning junction ORF of circBTBD7. Surprisingly, circBTBD7 can suppress the proliferation and migration of HCASMCs by means of circBTBD7-420aa. Thus far, to our knowledge, this is the first protein encoded by circRNA to be identified to play a pivotal role in CAD, which means that our study is trailblazing.
To gain more insights into circBTBD7-420aa in CAD, we identified SLC3A2 as its downstream target protein. The SLC3A2 gene encodes for the heavy chain of a cell surface, a transmembrane protein named CD98hc. Antecedent studies have focused on the contribution of SLC3A2 in the ferroptosis phenotype. Xiang et al found that SLC3A2 degradation promoted ferroptosis in vascular endotheliuml and aggravated the progression of atherosclerosis. Recently, it has been shown that SLC3A2 can take part in cell proliferation. Baumer et al concluded that the CD98hc–/– mice and cells showed a lesser proliferative rate, and Dong et al indicated that the down-regulation of SLC3A2 could inhibit human aortic VSMC proliferation and promote apoptosis. We found that the expression of SLC3A2 was up-regulated in the abnormal proliferation and migration model of HCASMCs; additionally, we found that the knockdown of SLC3A2 could alleviate the proliferation and migration of HCASMCs.
Ubiquitination is one of the common forms of posttranslational modification, and the ubiquitin-proteasome system is a pathway for scavenging short-lived proteins in eukaryotic cells. , The UbiBrowser database predicted that SLC3A2 has multiple ubiquitination sites. It has been justified that CD98 could be degraded through the ubiquitination pathway, so we inferred that circBTBD7-420aa might perform as an E3 ubiquitin ligase while SLC3A2 acts as a substrate. In this study, circBTBD7-420aa serves as an E3-like ubiquitin ligase responsible for transferring ubiquitin from E2 to substrate SLC3A2. Classically, E3 ubiquitin ligases have 3 protein families: RING, HECT, and RING-between-RING, but circBTBD7-420aa does not appear to possess one of these structures, which brings distinctive proof to the exploration of E3 ubiquitin ligases. In the treatment of diseases, the target protein of E3 ubiquitin ligases will be degraded by the proteasome, which has become a key target for drug discovery. Currently, the principle of the widely used proteolysis-targeting chimera is that it can induce target protein degradation, which indicates that circBTBD7-420aa has the potential to become a new therapeutic drug for coronary artery disease. However, the exact mechanism underlying the ubiquitination of SLC3A2 via binding circBTBD7-420aa needs further study.
Accumulating studies have elaborated the mechanism of CAD from multiple perspectives and provided promising treatment strategies for CAD. One study found that CAD risk variants at 15q26.1 reduce the expression of FES (FES proto-oncogene, tyrosine kinase), affecting the atherosclerotic lesion size and promoting its progression. Zeng et al showed that circMAP3K5, as a microRNA22-3p sponge, inhibited the abnormal proliferation of HCASMAs through TET2 (teneleven translocation-2) and reduced intimal hyperplasia. Our study confirmed that injecting circBTBD7-420aa lentivirus into the tail vein can improve the degree of vascular occlusion and collagen fibrosis in an ApoE–/– mouse atherosclerotic model and inhibit the progression of atherosclerosis. circBTBD7-420aa is a functional protein encoded by circRNA, and regulating the function or expression of circBTBD7-420aa can be a potential target for CAD therapy. We have focused on exosomes that can be used as delivery vehicles for bioactive substances. Poor targeting affects the therapeutic effect of exosomes on cardiovascular diseases. In recent years, surface modification of exosomes has become an effective way to improve their cardiac targeting, including modification of extracellular vesicles with cardiac-targeting peptide , and modification of extracellular vesicles with monocyte membranes, , among others. In combination with the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis, we constructed engineered exosomes loaded with circBTBD7-420aa targeting vascular smooth muscle. circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes can exert anti-CAD effects by targeting VSMCs (high OPN expression) that occur in the phenotypic transformation during atherosclerosis. Our results provide a new strategy for the targeted therapy of CAD. Further studies will demonstrate the efficacy and safety of circBTBD7-420aa-Exo for CAD in vivo and in vitro.
This study suggests that circBTBD7-420aa regulates the progression of atherosclerosis by promoting the degradation of SLC3A2 through the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Since 2017, more and more studies have focused on novel functional proteins encoded by circRNA. , , A recent study showed that nonclassical translation of circRNA can drive effective antitumor immunity, suggesting that vaccination using tumor-specific circRNA can be used as an immune strategy against malignant tumors. However, proteins or peptides encoded by circRNA have not been found to have biological functions during atherosclerosis. We found that the protein encoded by circBTBD7, circBTBD7-420aa, inhibits the abnormal proliferation and migration of HCASMCs in vitro and inhibits the progression of atherosclerosis in vitro. Because of the property that circRNA’s covalently closed ring structure can be protected from exonuclease-mediated degradation, circBTBD7-420aa is more likely to play a role as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for CAD. With the gradual deepening of the research of engineered exosomes in the treatment of diseases, , , it provides a safer and more effective transport carrier for the clinical transformation of circBTBD7-420aa. Considering the biosafety and targeting of engineered exosomes, we constructed engineered exosomes loaded with circBTBD7-420aa targeting vascular smooth muscle to solve these problems.

## Study limitations
First of all, as a newly discovered protein, circBTBD7-420aa has no commercial antibody, and we have not customized its specific antibody, which makes it impossible for us to do immunohistochemistry experiments on coronary artery specimens to investigate the differential expression of circBTBD7-420aa. Second, circBTBD7-420aa affects the proliferation and migration of VSMCs via SLC3A2. As a part of ferroptosis, does SLC3A2 affect ferroptosis and thus give rise to changes in proliferation and migration? Finally, although we successfully constructed circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes, we did not validate their role in vitro and in vivo because of the limited space of the paper. This requires further exploration. These issues will be addressed in future studies.

## Conclusions
The protein encoded by circBTBD7, circBTBD7-420aa, may promote the degradation of SLC3A2 through the ubiquitin-proteasome system, thereby inhibiting the excessive proliferation and migration of HCASMCs and alleviating the progression of atherosclerosis. Moreover, we successfully constructed circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: Although numerous studies have shown that circRNAs play a crucial role in the occurrence and development of CAD, the expression and function of the proteins encoded by circRNAs in CAD are unclear. This study suggests that circBTBD7-420aa, a protein encoded by circBTBD7, may inhibit the progression of atherosclerosis and be a new target for the diagnosis and treatment of CAD.
TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: The treatment of CAD is still a subject of great concern. We need to refine the mechanism evidence to elucidate the effect of circBTBD7-420aa on atherosclerosis as well as the role and safety of circBTBD7-420aa engineered exosomes. This will provide a new therapeutic strategy for the treatment of CAD.
