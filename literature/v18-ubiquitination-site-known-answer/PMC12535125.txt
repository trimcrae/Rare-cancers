## Abstract
As a member of the hemoglobin family, γ-globin is usually persistently expressed at high levels to perform the oxygen-carrying function during fetal development. In recent years, related gene editing clinical trials have confirmed that reactivation of γ-globin expression is a promising therapeutic strategy for treating β-hemoglobinopathies, including β-thalassemia and sickle cell disease. Human γ-globin expression is finely regulated by multiple mechanisms, and a deeper understanding of the mechanisms will help develop drugs that target its activation. With the advancement of biotechnology, epigenetic modifications (EMs) and protein post-translational modifications (PPTMs) have shown irreplaceable roles in the regulation of γ-globin expression. Therefore, this review will comprehensively summarize the regulatory mechanisms of EMs and PPTMs on γ-globin to provide new ideas for the treatment of β-hemoglobinopathies.

## Background
Γ-globin is the main component of fetal hemoglobin (HbF), which is essential for erythropoiesis. Encoded by the HBG1 and HBG2 genes, γ-globin is highly expressed during the fetal period. It assembles with α-globin to form HbF, which exhibits high oxygen affinity, thus meeting the critical oxygen transport requirements of the fetus in the low-oxygen intrauterine environment [ ]. After birth, the expression of the γ-globin gene undergoes critical, precise temporal regulation. And γ-globin expression gradually decreases while β-globin expression increases. A thorough understanding of the regulatory mechanism of γ-globin gene expression is of significant clinical importance, as its continuous or reactivated expression after birth can effectively alleviate the pathological symptoms of β-hemoglobin disorders [e.g., sickle cell anemia (SCD) and β-thalassemia (β-thal)]. The gene therapy strategies for reactivating γ-globin expression mainly fall into two categories: 1. virus-mediated gene addition: utilizing lentiviral vectors (LV) to deliver therapeutic γ-globin genes or enhancer elements primarily in vitro to patients’ CD34⁺ hematopoietic stem cells, achieving sustained expression through genomic integration, and 2. gene editing: employing CRISPR-Cas9 or base editors (ABE/tBE) to target and disrupt γ-globin inhibitory factors [such as the B-cell lymphoma 11A (BCL11A) enhancer) or directly activate the HBG promoter, thereby reactivating endogenous γ-globin expression. These strategies have progressed from basic research to clinical application [ – ]. Currently, the FDA has approved two gene therapies, exagamglogene autotemcel (exa-cel) and lovotibeglogene autotemcel (lovo-cel), for the treatment of SCD. Lovo-cel uses lentiviral vectors to deliver modified β-globin genes into cells, while exa-cel utilizes CRISPR technology to inactivate the BCL11A gene, thereby reactivating the synthesis of normal fetal hemoglobin and preventing the polymerization of sickle hemoglobin.
β-thal and SCD are inherited β-hemoglobinopathies caused by β-globin gene defects, leading to chronic anemia of variable severity and affecting millions globally [ ]. Globally, it is estimated that around 515,000 infants are born with SCD each year. Of these, about 30,000 individuals need regular blood transfusions to manage their condition [ – ]. It is reported that 80 to 90 million people are carriers of the β-thal gene worldwide, accounting for 1.5% of the global population [ ]. β-thal encompasses a spectrum of autosomal recessive genetic diseases caused by pathogenic variants in the β-globin gene, including point mutations, small-fragment deletions, or insertions, which result in significantly reduced or absent β-globin chain synthesis. Alterations in globin stoichiometry (β/α < 1) due to this molecular defect can induce the potentially harmful aggregation of free α-globin chains, which in turn results in ineffective erythropoiesis, peripheral hemolytic anemia, and secondary iron overload [ ]. SCD arises from a single-nucleotide mutation in codon 6 of the HBB gene, which causes glutamic acid to be substituted by valine at position 6 of the β-globin chain, leading to the formation of abnormal hemoglobin S (HbS). In the deoxygenated state, HbS tends to form polymers when deoxygenated, which alters the structural integrity of red blood cells and induces cellular deformation. This pathological process ultimately leads to microvascular occlusion crises, chronic hemolysis, and ischemic damage to end organs [ ]. In both SCD and β-thal, fetal hemoglobin (HbF) levels play a major role in determining disease severity [ – ].
Understanding the regulatory mechanisms of γ-globin expression will help identify new strategies for the treatment of β-hemoglobinopathies. Protein post-translational modifications (PPTMs) refer to a series of chemical modifications that occur after protein synthesis. PPTMs can affect the function, stability, localization, and interaction of proteins. Epigenetic modifications (EMs) involve the fine regulation of gene expression without altering the DNA sequence itself. With the development of epigenetics and proteomics, an increasing amount of evidence shows that PPTMs such as phosphorylation, acetylation, ubiquitination, methylation, and glcnacylation, and EMs including DNA methylation, histone modifications, and non-coding RNAs play important roles in regulating γ-globin expression. This review mainly summarizes the regulatory mechanism of γ-globin expression by EMs and PPTMs (Fig. ), to provide a relevant theoretical basis and new ideas for the treatment of β-hemoglobinopathies.
 

## Production and main function of γ-globin

## γ-globin production
The γ-globin coding genes (HBG1 and HBG2) are the major β-like genes that are expressed in most fetuses. They are located within the β gene cluster on human chromosome 11p15 (Fig. A). HBG1 and HBG2 differ only in which amino acid is located at position 136 (alanine or glycine). And they encode proteins consisting of 146 amino acids of Gγ and Aγ globins, respectively [ ]. γ-globin is structurally similar to β-globin and consists of eight α-helices that bend to form a pocket to accommodate heme molecules [ ]. The synthesis of γ-globin occurs mainly during hematopoiesis in the fetal stage. In the early stage of fetal development, hematopoiesis occurs mainly in the liver and spleen, and γ-globin protein expression gradually increases with the differentiation and maturation of hematopoietic stem cells. The γ-globin gene begins to be expressed in the liver at 5 weeks of gestation, reaches its peak at 10 weeks of gestation, but is silenced after birth [ ]. During the fetal period, γ-globin gradually increases and combines with α-globin to form HbF, and HbF is a tetramer composed of two α-globin chains and two γ-globin chains.
 

## γ-globin function
In the fetal period, γ-globin is one of the major components of hemoglobin and is usually combined with α-globin to form HbF. Relative to normal adult hemoglobin (Hemoglobin A, HbA), the substitution of Ser 143 in the γ chain of HbF with histidine in the β-globin chain decreases the binding ability of HbF to 2,3-BPG. 2,3-BPG reduces the oxygen affinity of hemoglobin, and HbF is less sensitive to 2,3-BPG [ ]. Therefore, HbF exhibits a higher affinity for oxygen under the same partial pressure of oxygen. This characteristic enables HbF to acquire oxygen efficiently from maternal circulation, thereby fulfilling fetal oxygenation requirements. In addition, HbF is more alkali resistant than HbA [ ]. HbF is the predominant hemoglobin from early pregnancy to 1 to 2 months after birth, after which HbA becomes dominant [ ]. In adults, γ-globin expression is usually suppressed and results in a decrease in the levels of HbF to less than 1% [ ]. Figure B summarizes the functions of γ-globin in different conditions.
Clinical, genetic, and biochemical studies have consistently highlighted the advantages of increased HbF levels, caused by increased γ-globin gene expression, in patients with β-thal and SCD [ ]. In β-thal, an increase in the expression levels of γ-globin can partially compensate for the deficiency of β-globin, thereby alleviating the imbalance of α/β globin peptide chains [ ]. Research has demonstrated that the levels of HbF are a significant indicator in patients with severe β-thal [ ]. For SCD, HbF can inhibit the formation of aggregates of deoxygenated HbS, which confers several clinical benefits to patients, such as reducing intravascular hemolysis, alleviating pain and tissue damage caused by vaso-occlusion, and mitigating endothelial injury [ ]. The severity of β-thal and SCD is influenced by the co-inheritance of HbF expression and α-thalassemia [ ]. Natural cases serve as examples of the phenotype resulting from the compound heterozygous state of HbS and pancellular hereditary persistence of fetal hemoglobin (S-HPFH) [ ]. In this scenario, each red blood cell contains not only HbS but also roughly 10pg of HbF. As a result, the polymerization of deoxy-HbS is almost completely inhibited, leading to a normal phenotype or just very mild hemolysis in these individuals. If HPFH coexists with β-thal, it can reduce thalassemia by decreasing the unused α-globin chains and their precipitated forms [ ]. Moreover, by recreating the specific genetic variations associated with HPFH in CD34+ hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) through gene editing technology, it can simulate the natural HPFH mutations and achieve an increase in the expression of γ-globin in these cells [ ]. Therefore, HPFH-associated mutations show great therapeutic potential for β-thal. Excessive α-globin chains have a significant impact on the anemia phenotype and clinical severity of patients with β-thal or SCD. Genetic variations in the α-globin genes that lead to reduced or absent α-globin synthesis are closely associated with milder clinical manifestations in patients with β-thal or SCD [ , ].
Currently, certain drugs such as hydroxyurea (HU) are available to stimulate the expression of the γ-globin gene and the synthesis of HbF. HU is currently the most widely used small-molecule drug in β-hemoglobinopathies, which plays a critical role in inducing HbF expression. HU is primarily employed as an HbF-inducing agent for patients with SCD [ ]. Clinical data demonstrated that HU treatment significantly reduces the incidence of acute pain crises, alleviates symptoms, and effectively prevents organ damage. Furthermore, long-term follow-up studies confirmed that HU therapy can substantially lower patient mortality rates [ ]. However, HU’s efficacy varies among individuals, and some patients may experience dose-limiting myelosuppressive side effect [ ]. Additionally, due to its inability to balance the production of α- and β-globin chains and its limited effectiveness in patients with low baseline levels of HbF, the application of HU in β-thal remains somewhat restricted. In summary, reactivation of γ-globin is an effective therapeutic strategy closely related to the treatment of β-hemoglobinopathies.

## Regulation of γ-globin expression by EMs
Epigenetics refers to the changes in gene expression that occur when the DNA sequence itself remains unchanged [ ]. The EMs mainly include DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA regulation, which play key roles in the regulation of γ-globin expression. Epigenetic therapeutics currently in clinical trials for SCD and β-thalassemia are catalogued in Supplementary Table 1.

## DNA methylation modification
DNA methylation refers to the transfer of methyl groups to specific DNA sequences by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) [ ]. DNA methylation is recognized by the methyl cytosine-binding domain (MBD) family, which tends to bind to DNA sequences containing methylated cytosines [ ].

## Hypermethylation of the γ-globin promoter
Increased DNA methylation of the γ-globin gene promoter leads to γ-globin silencing during adult erythropoiesis [ ]. Ploeg et al. [ ] analyzed the methylation status of the γ-globin promoter by digesting the DNA with methylation-sensitive restriction endonucleases and established a correlation map between DNA methylation levels of the γ-globin promoter and its expression during fetal development. Meanwhile, a study of three human fetal liver and three bone marrow samples by Mabaera et al. revealed changes in DNA methylation of the β-globin and γ-globin promoter regions during the transition from fetus to adult, confirming a negative correlation between promoter DNA methylation levels and stage-specific expression of the γ-globin gene [ ]. Additionally, it has been reported that the methylation level of the +17 CpG site in the γ-globin promoter is negatively correlated with the level of HbF expression [ ]. Similarly, Busslinger et al. [ ] showed that methylation of nucleotides at positions −760 to +100 in the 5’ region of the γ-globin gene prevents its transcriptional process. High DNA methylation of the γ-globin gene promoter inhibits γ-globin expression in fetal liver erythrocytes [ ]. In addition, DNMT1, a major epigenetic regulator, inhibits the expression of human γ-globin by maintaining promoter CpG methylation [ ]. Furthermore, the transcriptional repression mechanism of DNA methylation is mainly achieved through the binding of MBD proteins and their related co-repressor complexes [ , ]. Also, Shang et al. [ ] revealed that the MBD2a-NuRD complex localizes to the methylated HBG promoter, promotes interaction with BCL11A, and forms a large inhibitory complex containing protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5), leading to γ-globin gene silencing.

## Hypomethylation of the γ-globin promoter
In the γ-globin promoter region, hypomethylation is also crucial for its expression regulation. Mabaera [ ] investigated the methylation status of the γ- and β-globin promoters in erythroid cells from human fetal liver and adult bone marrow. The study revealed that during the differentiation of erythroid precursor cells, the CpG sites within the upstream γ-globin promoter became hypomethylated, followed by remethylation during later erythropoiesis. This transient hypomethylation of the γ-globin promoter appears to be the temporary reactivation of γ-globin gene expression, potentially explaining the reappearance of HbF production during early stages of adult erythropoiesis. This finding is further supported by Singh et al., who showed that as erythroid differentiation progresses, the hypermethylated regions of the γ-globin promoter are progressively demethylated in the liver of the early fetus [ ]. Meanwhile, Bao et al. [ ] delineated a hypomethylation pattern around the CpG sites around the locus control region (LCR) HS4-HS3 region of the β-globin cluster and the γ-globin promoter in patients with β0/β0-thal, in contrast with healthy controls, and indicated that low methylation is correlated with elevated levels of HbF. The precise mechanisms underlying the switch of β-globin genes from fetal to adult expression in humans remain to be fully elucidated. Furthermore, recent studies showed that the hypomethylation pattern within the globin promoter might facilitate the binding of certain methylation-modifying factors that mediate the looping of the globin promoter [ ]. Gong’s team identified a missense mutation (c.2633G>A, S878F) in the DNMT1 gene from 1142 patients with β-thal. This mutation was found to reactivate γ-globin expression via a dual mechanism that reduced methylation of the γ-globin gene promoter and diminished recruitment of the core repressor complex [ ].
In addition, MBD2 is also involved in the regulation of the γ-globin gene. Knockdown of MBD2 activates γ-globin gene expression in transgenic mice carrying human β-globin cluster genes and in human erythroid progenitor cells [ , ]. It is generally believed that disruption of DNA methylation appears to be one of the mechanisms that attenuate γ-globin gene silencing in both primate and human β-globin locus transgenic mouse models [ – ]. However, recent studies have employed Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras (PROTACs) to specifically degrade BCL11A. Following the depletion of BCL11A, the transcription of γ-globin gene was rapidly upregulated, while the promoter’s 5-methylcytosine levels remained unchanged. These findings suggested that BCL11A-mediated suppression of HBG1/2 is independent of promoter CpG methylation, indicating that CpG methylation may not be the primary mechanism underlying HBG1/2 repression [ ]. But the study mentioned DNA methylation may act independently of the transcription factor BCL11A to repress fetal hemoglobin production [ ].
The correlation between γ-globin promoter methylation and HbF silencing offers a theoretical foundation for utilizing DNA methylation inhibitors as a therapeutic strategy for β-hemoglobinopathies by inducing the activation of HbF. 5-Azacytidine significantly increases HbF levels in anemia models of baboons [ , ] as well as in patients with SCD [ ] and β-thal [ ]. However, bone marrow toxicity and potential carcinogenic risk restrict its clinical application for the treatment of β-thal or SCD to date [ ]. Decitabine, as an analogue of 5-Azacytidine, shows favorable tolerability and significant HbF induction in clinical trials for SCD [ ] and β-thal [ ]. But this drug remains unapproved due to concerns about the thrombocytosis-associated risks. While demethylating agents have shown promise in treating β-hemoglobinopathies, their clinical utility is limited by toxic side effects. Currently, research on DNMT inhibitors has shifted focus toward optimizing administration strategies [e.g., oral combination with tetrahydrouridine (THU) [ ]] and developing a new generation of highly targeted drugs (e.g., GSK3482364) [ ] to balance efficacy and safety, thereby facilitating clinical translation.

## Histone modifications
Histones are the main components of chromatin, which encapsulate DNA to form chromatin particles. Another important epigenetic mechanism that determines gene transcriptional activity is the histone modifications within chromatin. Histone modifications refer to chemical alterations (such as methylation, acetylation, etc.) that occur at specific locations on histone molecules [ ]. These modifications regulate chromatin plasticity through the dynamic addition or erasure of specific histone residues by the related chromatin-modifying enzymes. Increasing evidence showed that histone modifications play an important role in modulating γ-globin expression.

## Histone methylation modification
Histone methylation is one of the important modifications occurring on lysine (Lys) and arginine (Arg) residues of histones, which is mainly catalyzed by protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) and related family members (including SET domain and non-SET domain proteins) [ ]. In Fig. A, γ-globin gene expression is shown to be modulated by histone methylation. It has been demonstrated that the PRMT5 protein induces DNA methyltransferase 3 alpha (DNMT3A) to bind with the γ-globin gene promoter through a symmetrical dimethylation modification of the third arginine of histone H4. This modification catalyzes the methylation of CpG sites in this region, resulting in the silencing of the γ-globin gene in erythrocytes [ ]. In addition, Friend of PRMT1 (FoP), a chromatin factor that serves as a target for methylation of PRMT1 and PRMT5, is also associated with the silencing of the γ-globin gene. It was found that knocking down the FoP protein leads to an increase in the expression of the γ-globin gene in cultured adult erythroid progenitors [ ]. The enzymatic activity of PRMT1 may mediate the silencing of the γ-globin gene by enhancing the enhancer activity of the control region in the β-globin locus [ ]. Xu et al. [ ] found that WD Repeat Domain 5 (WDR5) could induce histone H3K4me3 on the promoter of the human γ-globin gene, thereby inhibiting the transcription of the γ-globin gene.
 Lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1/KDM1A) has emerged as a critical epigenetic repressor of γ-globin expression. LSD1 associates with some inhibitory factors, as components of the Direct Repeat Erythroid Definitive (DRED) complex. And LSD1 knockdown resulted in enhanced HbF and γ-globin mRNA synthesis [ , ]. Understanding the role of relevant histone methylation in the regulation of the γ-globin gene may facilitate increasing HbF production by modulating the activity of associated modifying enzymes, thereby rendering it a potential therapeutic target. LSD1 inhibitors have been demonstrated to reactivate γ-globin expression in human cells, mouse, and baboons as well as alleviate anemia in a transgenic sickle cell mouse model [ – ]. Meanwhile, euchromatic histone lysine methyltransferases 1 and 2 (EHMT1/2) inhibitors, such as UNC0638, have been shown to reduce the repressive histone H3K9me2 modification at the γ-globin promoter and to promote the formation of a loop between the LCR and the γ-globin promoter, subsequently inducing γ-globin gene expression [ ]. However, UNC0638 exhibits suboptimal in vivo pharmacokinetic properties, which may limit its utility as a therapeutic agent. Moreover, several small-molecule inhibitors targeting PRMT5 and PRMT1 have been developed and are currently advancing into clinical trials; however, they have not yet been applied for the treatment of hematologic diseases [ ].

## Histone acetylation modification
Histone acetylation affects gene transcription by altering the binding force between histone proteins and DNA. The level of histone acetylation is dynamically regulated by histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and histone deacetylases (HDACs). Histone acetylation regulates the expression of the γ-globin gene through multiple mechanisms (Fig. B). With the developmental transition of the human β-globin gene cluster region, histone acetylation also changes dynamically [ ]. During the fetal period, hyperacetylation keeps the γ-globin gene active [ – ]. Hyperacetylation of histone H3 and H4 in the promoter region of the γ-globin gene is associated with the up-regulation of γ-globin gene expression [ ]. In addition, deletion of erythroid transcription factors or epigenetic coregulators such as BCL11A, Kruppel-like factor 1 (KLF1), HDAC1, and HDAC2 can enhance histone acetylation at the γ-globin gene promoter, thereby upregulating γ-globin expression [ ]. Aerbajinai et al. [ ] found that thalidomide could enhance the expression of the γ-globin gene by promoting histone H4 acetylation.
HDACs play a complex role in the regulation of γ-globin gene expression. HDACs inhibit the expression of γ-globin. Moreover, the Nucleosome Remodeling and Deacetylase (NuRD) complex, which contains subunits such as HDAC1/2 and MBD3, suppresses the expression of the γ-globin gene [ ]. In addition, BCL11A and ZBTB7A (also known as LRF) are the main transcriptional repressors of the human γ-globin gene. They remodel the nuclear structure and recruit the NuRD complex to the γ-globin promoter, resulting in chromatin remodeling and gene silencing. However, they independently shut down HbF expression through different NuRD-related pathways. Inhibiting BCL11A or ZBTB7A can increase the expression of the γ-globin gene [ – ]. These findings provide a solid foundation for targeted therapy of hemoglobinopathies. However, HDACs activity does not always correlate with γ-globin repression. Dai et al. [ ] found that SIRT1 (Silent Information Regulator 1) promotes the looping of the LCR of the β-globin gene cluster to the HBG promoter, inhibits the expression of HBG transcription repressors, and indirectly increases histone acetylation in the HBG promoter, thereby inducing the expression of γ-globin. The function of HDAC9 in the expression of the γ-globin gene is different from that of traditional HDACs. It mainly manifests as an activating effect [ ]. HDAC9 and HDRP (histone deacetylase related protein, a splice variant of HDAC9) have opposite regulatory effect on γ-globin gene expression [ ]. However, further research is still needed to clarify the relevant mechanisms.
Furthermore, adenosine-2’,3’-dialdehyde (Adox) enhances the H4 acetylation level at the γ-globin promoter in adult erythrocytes of bone marrow through the inhibition of PRMT5 [ ]. Moreover, histone deacetylase inhibitors could activate the γ-globin gene through multiple mechanisms. The effect of butyrate on γ-gene expression was found to be partially mediated by inhibiting histone deacetylase [ ]. Vorinostat is an HDAC inhibitor that has been found to inhibit α-globin while inducing γ-globin production [ ]. Meanwhile, HDAC1 and HDAC2 inhibitors can activate the GATA-binding protein 2 (GATA2) through histone acetylation to enhance γ-globin gene expression [ , ]. Additionally, several studies have shown that histone deacetylase inhibitors can induce γ-globin gene expression by enhancing histone H4 acetylation through related signal transduction pathways [ , – ]. The therapeutic application of HDAC inhibitors is challenged by two major factors: First, their non-specific effects may lead to toxic reactions; second, the incomplete elucidation of the distinct yet overlapping roles of HDAC1, HDAC2, and HDAC3 in erythropoiesis suggested that non-selective inhibition may disrupt normal physiological processes, potentially elevating treatment risk.

## Histone phosphorylation
In addition to acetylation and methylation, phosphorylation is another important modification of histones. MAPK-mediated phosphoacetylation of histone H3 can regulate gene expression [ ]. Qian et al. [ ] showed that Plastrum testudinis (PT) can enhance the acetylation of histone H3 and H4, as well as the phosphorylation of H3, through the p38 MAPK pathway, and these modifications play an important role in γ-globin expression. Additionally, PT could enhance the phosphorylation of p38 MAPK, which is associated with increased γ-globin mRNA accumulation and HbF production. Meanwhile, it has been found that phosphorylated tyrosine kinase molecules can migrate to the nucleus and stimulate the binding of targeted transcription factors to promoter elements, thereby regulating gene expression [ ]. The p38 MAPK pathway is primarily regarded as activating the γ-globin promoter through this mechanism (Fig. C). Moreover, research has demonstrated that the activation of the MAPK pathway facilitates the induction of HbF activity by several drugs, including valproate, butyrate, thalidomide, and trichostatin-A [ , , ]. However, the correlation between γ-globin gene expression and histone phosphorylation within its promoter remains unclear. It is expected that with the improvement in the targeted genome and epigenome editing platforms in the future, the related mechanisms can be further clarified to fully exploit their therapeutic potential.

## Non-coding RNAs
Non-coding RNAs are functional RNA molecules with low potential for translating proteins, which commonly include microRNA (miRNA), long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), and circular RNA (circRNA). They are important regulators of cellular processes, biomarkers for disease prognosis, and potential therapeutic targets. Numerous studies in recent years have shown that non-coding RNAs play a significant role in the regulation of γ-globin expression. Figure depicts the molecular mechanism by which non-coding RNAs modulate γ-globin expression.
 

## miRNA
miRNA is a class of non-coding RNA ranging from 19 to 25 nucleotides in length that regulates target gene expression by inducing translational repression or inducing mRNA degradation. miRNA can directly bind to the 3’-untranslated region of γ-globin mRNA and inhibit its expression. Azzouzi et al. [ ] used in vitro assays demonstrated that overexpression of miR-96 reduces γ-globin expression by approximately 50%, whereas knockdown of endogenous miR-96 resulted in an increase in γ-globin expression by about 20%. BCL11A is a transcriptional repressor of HbF that directly represses the activity of the γ-globin promoter [ ]. Several studies have shown that miRNAs can directly target BCL11A and thus participate in the regulation of the γ-globin gene, such as miR-486-3p [ ], miR-210 [ ], miR-30a [ ], miR-190b-5p [ ], and miR-6747-3p [ ]. Moreover, Ma et al. [ ] revealed that miR-23a and miR-27a form a feedback loop with the transcription factors KLF3 and Specificity protein 1 (SP1), thereby positively regulating γ-globin synthesis. Sun et al. [ ] found that miR-223-3p can target LIM Domain Only 2 (LMO2) and promote the formation of the LMO2/LDB1/GATA1/TAL1 complex, and this complex is able to downregulate the expression level of γ-globin through affecting the LCR. Other miRNAs such as miR-26b, miR-15a, and miR-16-1 can induce γ-globin expression by targeting MYB [ , ]. In addition, miRNAs can indirectly regulate γ-globin expression by binding to other key transcription factors, including KLF1, GATA-1, and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) [ , ]. miRNAs are implicated in the post-transcriptional regulation of key transcription factors associated with γ-globin gene expression. The direct interaction between miRNAs and these transcription factors holds significant potential for the effective induction of HbF. It is anticipated that future advancements may enable miRNAs to precisely target and modulate γ-globin-related transcription factors, thereby enhancing the precision of gene editing technologies. However, the targets of miRNAs remain largely elusive, which currently hinders the implementation of therapeutic strategies for SCD and β-thal.

## lncRNA
In recent years, lncRNAs have attracted increasing attention and have been shown to play a key regulatory role in normal erythropoiesis [ , ]. Higashi et al. [ ] found that an lncRNA located within 4.4 kb to 5.2 kb downstream of the β-globin coding region has the ability to suppress fetal γ-globin gene expression. Jia et al. [ ] reported high average expression levels of lncRNA NR_001589 and uc002fcj1 in the high HbF group and noted that the two lncRNAs were positively correlated with HbF level. Furthermore, down-regulating the lncRNA (HMI-LNCRNA) derived from the intergenic region of the HBS1L-MYB gene can enhance the expression of HBG, but the specific mechanism still needs to be explored [ ]. BGLT3 is an lncRNA located near the γ-globin gene and acts as a positive regulator of γ-globin transcription [ ]. Ma et al. [ ] showed that lncRNA HBBP1 promotes γ-globin production by enhancing the expression of ETS transcription factor 1 (ELK1) in HUDEP-2 cells. Moreover, Bao et al. [ ] discovered that RP11-196G18.23 binds to the ERF promoter and subsequently recruits DNMT3A to mediate hypermethylation of the ERF promoter, which inhibited ERF-induced inactivation of γ-globin, and ultimately facilitated γ-globin expression. These studies have elucidated the synergistic role of lncRNAs in the regulation of γ-globin expression, providing a critical foundation for the development of gene therapy strategies based on RNA-targeted interventions.

## circRNA
circRNA is a newly discovered class of non-coding RNA, named for its unique circular structure. CircRNAs play important roles in regulating gene expression, cellular function, and a variety of biological processes. Currently, investigations into circRNA within the hemoglobin field remain in their infancy. It can inhibit miRNA, acting as a sponge, and thereby regulate the expression of γ-globin. Our previous study found that circLCOR(5,6,7) expression is positively correlated with HbF, β-globin, and γ-globin mRNA levels. Further analysis showed that circLCOR(5,6,7) is able to inhibit the expression of several predicted miRNAs and induce the activation of γ-globin in pediatric β-thal [ ]. Moreover, another study from Yang et al. revealed that circLBR(7,8) inhibited SOX6 transcription via sponge miR-19b-3p, increasing HbF levels in β-thal [ ]. Nevertheless, the effects and mechanisms of most circRNAs on γ-globin expression are unknown and have not been studied. Currently, in the field of hereditary β-hemoglobin disorders, research on circRNAs has mainly focused on their ceRNA effect, but the specific mechanisms still require in-depth exploration. In addition, exploring the interaction between circRNAs and RNA-binding proteins involved in the regulatory mechanism of the β-globin gene cluster holds great potential for the treatment of hemoglobin disorders [ ]. We expect that with further research on circRNAs, they will be applied in the diagnosis and treatment of hereditary β-hemoglobin diseases.

## Regulation of γ-globin expression by PPTMs
PPTMs play a vital role in functional proteomics by governing protein functions such as enzyme activity, redirecting cellular protein localization, enhancing or attenuating interactions with other proteins, and marking proteins for degradation [ ]. PPTMs mainly contain phosphorylation, acetylation, ubiquitination, methylation, and O-Glcnacylation modification, which are associated with γ-globin expression (Supplementary Table 2).

## Ubiquitination modification in γ-globin
Ubiquitination is a key post-translational modification process that regulates the stability, interaction, and biological function of proteins by covalently linking ubiquitin molecules to target proteins. This process requires the synergistic action of ubiquitin-activating enzyme (E1), ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme (E2), and ubiquitin ligase enzyme (E3). The mechanisms underlying the ubiquitination and degradation of γ-globin remain incompletely understood. However, the ubiquitin–proteasome system has been partially verified to play an indirect regulatory role in this process. Several previous techniques have been used to identify relevant ubiquitination sites. For instance, the ubiquitin residue immunoprecipitation mass spectrometry technology developed by Xu et al. provided a methodological reference for identifying ubiquitinated lysine modification sites [ ]. Marotti et al. [ ] directly identified the ubiquitination site (Lys165) of the G protein α subunit (Gpa1) by mass spectrometry and verified the critical role of this site in ubiquitination through mutation experiments. It is anticipated that the ubiquitination modification sites of γ-globin can be identified by CRISPR mutagenesis in combination with next-generation sequencing (NGS), Western blotting, and mass spectrometry.

## PPTMs in non-γ-globin

## Phosphorylation modification
Phosphorylation is a universal and reversible modification of serine (Ser), threonine (Thr), and tyrosine (Tyr) amino acid residues that is catalyzed by kinases. In erythropoiesis, the erythrocyte-specific activator Erythroid Kruppel-like factor (EKLF) is essential for initiating high levels of β-globin expression in adult [ – ]. Ouyang et al. [ ] showed that the presence of EKLF is insufficient to activate the β-globin promoter, and its function is regulated by phosphorylation state. When EKLF lacks phosphorylation, it will lead to the lack of appropriate protein–protein contacts, which may reduce the competitive advantage of the β-globin promoter relative to the γ-globin promoter. Studies have shown that the lack of EKLF phosphorylation leads to an increase in the expression levels of γ-globin in transgenic mice, and γ-globin expression takes longer than normal to close during mouse development [ , ]. KLF1 has been identified as a critical regulator through knockout mouse models, suggesting that the phosphorylation of EKLF may serve as a promising target for gene therapy of β-hemoglobinopathy.
Furthermore, Oridonin induces CREB1 phosphorylation by activating p38 MAPK signaling in erythroid progenitor cells, which further promotes histone hyperacetylation and increases γ-globin expression [ ]. Hahn et al. [ ] found that the enhancement of eIF2α phosphorylation may promote the expression of HbF by enhancing the translation efficiency of γ-globin mRNA. However, the underlying mechanism of action remains incompletely elucidated. Notably, the DNMT1 mutation (c.2633G>A, S878F) involves the substitution of a nucleophilic serine with an aromatic phenylalanine. This substituted serine can be phosphorylated through post-translational modification, which is correlated with increased DNMT1 protein stability. Furthermore, the interaction between DNMT1 S878F and BCL11A, along with other proteins, is weaker. The finding implicated that the kinase-mediating phosphorylation at DNMT1 S878F site could be a candidate inhibitory target, providing a potential new avenue for pharmacologic recapitulation of HPFH [ ].

## Acetylation modification
Acetylation plays an important role in biological processes such as chromatin stability, protein–protein interactions, cell cycle control, cell metabolism, nuclear transport, and actin nucleation. Nuclear factor erythroid 4 (NF-E4) is a globin-specific transcription factor that activates the γ-globin gene and competitively silences β-globin gene expression [ ]. Zhao et al. [ ] showed that NF-E4 is acetylated on its specific lysine residue (Lys43) by the acetyltransferase PCAF. This acetylation modification prolongs the half-life of NF-E4, prevents its degradation through the ubiquitination pathway, and reduces the interaction between NF-E4 and HDAC1, thereby improving the activation ability of the γ-globin promoter. Furthermore, acetylation of EKLF enhances its interaction with the SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex, resulting in the maximization of the activation potential of EKLF at the β-globin promoter, thereby inhibiting γ-globin expression [ ]. These findings highlight the critical role of acetylation and deacetylation among PTMs in regulating γ-globin expression. Additionally, the acetylation of NF-E4 is anticipated to be effectively evaluated as a promising target for enhancing HbF levels in patients with SCD and β-thal.

## Methylation modification
Methylation is catalyzed by specific enzymes, such as lysine methyltransferases (KMTs) and PRMTs. PRMT5 is essential for embryonic stem cell function, adult hematopoietic cell maintenance, and hemoglobin gene conversion [ ]. The catalytic activity of PRMT5 can be regulated by protein binding and PPTMs [ , ]. Nie et al. [ ] showed that coactivator-associated arginine methyltransferase 1 (CARM1) promotes the homodimerization of PRMT5 by methylation at the Arg-505 site, which in turn inhibited γ-globin expression in erythroleukemia cells. Additionally, Wang et al. [ ] have shown that PRMT1 blocks the translation of γ-globin mRNA, and this process requires the participation of Dead-Box Polypeptide 3 (DDX3). What is more, PRMT1 can induce arginine methylation of DDX3, enhancing its stability and preventing proteasomal degradation [ ]. Therefore, the interaction between PRMT1 and DDX3 may serve as a potential post-translational modification mechanism to regulate the expression of γ-globin.

## Ubiquitination modification
Ubiquitination also indirectly regulates the expression of γ-globin gene by modulating the activity of certain transcription factors. Under hypoxic condition, the activity of prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) is decreased, leading to the accumulation of Hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF1α) and the formation of a dimer with persistently expressed HIF1β. The HIF1α-HIF1β complex promotes γ-globin expression by binding to BGLT3 and subsequently recruiting transcription activators that facilitated chromatin opening and enhanced long-range interactions between the γ-globin gene and its upstream enhancers. On the contrary, under hyperoxia state, oxygen-dependent PHD hydroxylates the HIF1α protein, which is ubiquitinated by the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex and subsequently degraded by the proteasome to inhibit γ-globin expression [ ]. The loss of VHL protein in red blood cells activates a widespread HIF-dependent gene expression program, including induction of HbF expression. Consequently, the PHD-VHL-HIF oxygen-sensing pathway represents a promising therapeutic target for β-hemoglobinopathies [ , ]. One approach to target epigenetic repressors of γ-globin repression is through the specific degradation of these proteins. Studies have demonstrated that the deubiquitinating enzyme BRCA1-associated protein-1 (BAP1) protects the DRED scaffold protein NCoR1 from ubiquitin-mediated degradation. Notably, the knockdown of BAP-1 leads to the ubiquitination and degradation of NCoR1, which in turn triggers robust reactivation of γ-globin expression and increases HbF levels [ ]. Furthermore, the development of PROTACs, which specifically recruit E3 ubiquitin ligases to degrade target proteins, has been proposed as a strategy to target not only epigenetic co-repressors but also alternative transcriptional repressors [ ]. For instance, the rapid depletion of BCL11A mediated by PROTAC can induce the reactivation of HBG1/2 [ ]. However, practical applications for γ-globin reactivation using these approaches remain to be further validated.

## O-Glcnacylation modification
O-Glcnacylation is dynamically regulated by O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and O-GlcNAcase (OGA) and plays a regulatory role in erythropoiesis. Parker et al. [ ] found that O-Glcnacylation can stimulate the formation of the GATA-1-FOG-1-NuRD inhibitory complex to silence γ-globin expression. Blocking O-Glcnacylation can inhibit the formation of this complex and maintain γ-globin expression. Moreover, researchers fuse OGT and OGA with inactive Cas9 endonuclease (dCas9), and interesting results of γ-globin expression were found. When OGA-dCas9 targeted to the -1 to -223 bp region adjacent to the mRNA start site of the γ-globin promoter, a significant increase in γ-globin expression was observed. However, when OGT-dCas9 targeted the region surrounding γ-globin transcription start site, the inhibitory effect is significantly enhanced [ , ]. These results suggest that O-GlcNAcylation, as a novel PPTMs, could affect γ-globin expression.

## Conclusions and Prospects
Activation of the γ-globin gene is one of the core strategies for the treatment of β-hemoglobinopathy. Currently, clinical drugs and gene editing technologies for patients with β-hemoglobinopathies primarily focus on activating γ-globin gene expression to compensate for the absent β-globin by forming HbF in conjunction with α-globin. Elevated HbF levels can suppress abnormal hemoglobin aggregation, decrease hemolysis, extend red blood cell survival, and thereby alleviate patient symptoms. Nonetheless, these therapeutic strategies possess inherent limitations.
The regulatory mechanism of γ-globin gene expression is a finely tuned and complex network system. The expression of γ-globin is associated with gene polymorphism, gene transcription and post-transcriptional regulation, protein translation, and modification. Numerous experimental studies have gradually revealed that EMs and PPTMs play important roles in regulating γ-globin expression. EMs can regulate the expression of γ-globin by directly interacting with its promoter or by indirectly interacting with specific transcription factors, while PPTMs mainly affect the expression of γ-globin by directly modifying γ-globin itself or indirectly influencing the modification of specific transcription factors. Drugs have been developed for β-hemoglobinopathies based on epigenetic modifications. Likewise, the development of PPTMs, which are anticipated to regulate sustained γ-globin activation through the modification of key inhibitors via corresponding kinase pathways, represents a promising candidate for gene therapy in β-hemoglobinopathies. In-depth analysis of these mechanisms not only elucidates the pathological basis of β-hemoglobinopathies but also provides multi-level targets for clinical intervention, such as DNMTs, HDACs, and other key transcription factors. Nevertheless, most models are typically based on the primary effects of HbF inducers, such as global DNA hypomethylation induced by DNMT inhibitors, which are limited by non-specificity and toxic side effects. Key challenges, including targeting specificity, durability, and safety, need to be addressed urgently.
Despite these advances have been made, there is still a lack of in-depth study on mechanisms regarding the regulation of γ-globin expression, particularly the effect of PPTMs on γ-globin expression through non-γ-globin manner. Additionally, novel mechanisms of circRNA regulating γ-globin expression remain largely unknown. Therefore, at present, drugs that activated γ-globin by targeting EMs and PPTMs have not been well developed. This greatly limits the improvement in treatment methods for β-hemoglobinopathies patients. However, with the advancement of molecular biology technologies in the future, we believe researchers will be able to elaborate more comprehensively works on the regulatory mechanisms of the γ-globin gene and better understand the mechanisms of HbF-inducing drugs. A comprehensive understanding of γ-globin will facilitate the development of therapies based on gene editing technology or the targeted drugs that activated its expression. We expect that in the future, β-hemoglobinopathies can be cured by targeting specific molecules or mechanisms to manipulate γ-globin gene expression.
