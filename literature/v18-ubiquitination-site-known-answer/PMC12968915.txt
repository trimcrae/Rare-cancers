## Background
Hypoxia inducible factor 2α (HIF2α) is a major oncogenic driver in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). HIF2α is upregulated by von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) gene loss-of-function mutations in ccRCC. Deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) have emerged as key regulators of oncoprotein stability, but which DUBs sustain HIF2α accumulation in ccRCC remains unknown. Our study sought to systematically identify DUBs that stabilize HIF2α regardless of VHL status.

## Methods
We selected three ccRCC cell lines with distinct VHL gene statuses, and 15 DUB inhibitors were applied to screen for DUBs that regulate the degradation of the HIF2α protein in ccRCC regardless of VHL gene status. Coimmunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and mass spectrometry (MS) were used to verify the interactions between DUBs and the HIF2α protein.

## Results
In our study, the PR-619 inhibitor caused a decrease in the HIF2α protein level in 786-O, OS-RC-2, and SW839 cells, and MG132 treatment increased HIF2α protein expression after PR-619 treatment. Furthermore, the MS, Co-IP and Western blotting results revealed that ubiquitin-specific peptidase 10 (USP10) is a DUB that binds to HIF2α and that depletion of USP10 leads to HIF2α downregulation through promotion of HIF2α ubiquitination. We also confirmed that USP10 expression was upregulated in kidney cancer and had a positive correlation with HIF2α expression in RCC patient samples.

## Conclusions
Our results suggest that USP10 promotes HIF2α stability.

## What is the implication, and what should change now?
• HIF2α-USP10 co-blockade upfront prevents both VHL-independent and mutation-driven resistance.

## Introduction
Hypoxia-inducible factor 2 alpha (HIF2α) regulates the aggressiveness of various types of tumors, especially clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) ( , ). The HIF2α inhibitor belzutifan was used in a phase III, multicenter, open-label, active-controlled trial (NCT04195750) ( ). The enrolled participants all failed to respond to treatment with both a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitor and a VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) ( ). Compared with everolimus, the HIF2α inhibitor showed a notable advantage in terms of progression-free survival and objective response rate (ORR) in advanced ccRCC patients ( ). There was a 21.9% ORR in the HIF2α inhibitor group ( ). On the basis of the importance of the HIF2α protein in ccRCC, it is worthwhile to thoroughly study the factors regulating the stability of the HIF2α protein in ccRCC ( , , , ).
The stability of the HIF2α protein is influenced by a variety of factors, including gene mutations, the regulation of enzyme activity, and drug interventions ( - ). The abnormal accumulation of the HIF2α protein in ccRCC is due primarily to mutations in the VHL gene (in more than 70% of cases) and the loss of function of the von Hippel-Lindau tumor suppressor (pVHL) ( ). The function of pVHL is to facilitate the proteasomal degradation of HIFs by targeting the α-subunits of HIFs ( ). The amount of HIF2α protein that translocates to the cell nucleus and activates hypoxia-related genes, such as the EPO gene, may contribute to the onset of related diseases ( , ).
Ubiquitin-specific proteases (USPs) regulate the expression of various oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes through deubiquitination and play a significant role in the initiation, progression, drug resistance, and immunotherapy of cancer ( - ). USP7 positively regulates the tumor suppressor p53 by deubiquitinating MDM2. USP2 promotes tumor immune evasion by deubiquitinating and stabilizing PD-L1, which hinders T-cell cytotoxicity mediated by PD-1 ( ). USP11, on the other hand, inhibits cell proliferation and DNA damage-induced cell cycle checkpoints by deubiquitinating and stabilizing key proteins such as Mgl-1 and p21 ( , ). In recent years, significant progress has been made in the development of USP1, USP7, USP8, USP24, and USP48 inhibitors ( - ). For example, the small-molecule compound IU1 selectively inhibits USP14 via spatial occlusion, preventing the C-terminus of the substrate ubiquitin from entering the active site of USP14 ( ). Research indicated that by inhibiting specific USPs, the sensitivity of cancer cells to a variety of anticancer drugs can be increased, thereby circumventing drug resistance ( , ). Furthermore, the integration of USP inhibitors with current anticancer therapies provides new strategies for cancer treatment. Long-term use of HIF2α inhibitors may lead to drug resistance, partly due to mutations in the key amino acids in the binding domain of HIF2α with the inhibitors. Therefore, it is necessary to further investigate the mechanisms of drug resistance and develop new inhibitors or combination therapeutic strategies to overcome this issue.
In our study, to find the therapeutic potential of USPs in ccRCC, we undertook 15 USPs inhibitors to understand the molecular mechanisms of the HIF2α protein degradation, providing a theoretical basis for the treatment of renal cancer. We confirmed that USP10 promotes HIF2α protein stability regardless of VHL gene status in ccRCC. Our data showed that inhibitors of USP10 can enhance the sensitivity to clinical HIF2α inhibitors. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://tau.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tau-2025-aw-778/rc).

## Methods

## Cell lines and cell culture
The 786-O (RRID: CVCL_1051), OS-RC-2 (RRID: CVCL_1626), SW839 (RRID: CVCL_3604), and ACHN (RRID: CVCL_1067) human kidney cancer cell lines were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA). These cell lines were cultivated in RPMI-1640 (Gibco-Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco-Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), and these cell lines were cultured at 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 in a humidified incubator.

## Reagents
ML323 (cat#S7529), ML364 (cat#S6748), EOAI3402143 (cat#S6877), XL117A (cat#S8967), DUBs-IN-2 (cat#E0389); Spautin-1 (cat#S7888), IU1 (cat#S7134), GSK2643943 (cat#S6878), AZ1 (cat#S8904), MF-094 (cat#E1098), LDN-57444 (cat#S7135), TCID (cat#S7140), PR-619 (cat#S7130), BAY 11-7082 (cat#S2913), b-AP15 (cat#S4920), and MG132 (cat#S2619) were all purchased from Selleckchem (Shanghai, China).

## Western blotting (WB)
WB was performed as previously described. In brief, cells were lysed with immunoprecipitation (IP) buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors, after which the protein concentration was quantified by a BCA protein quantification assay. Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) was performed with 10% SDS-PAGE (cas#P2012, NCM Biotech, Shanghai, China), and a BioTraceTM NT Nitrocellulose Transfer Membrane (cas#66485, Pall, Inc., New York, USA) was used to transfer proteins from the gel to the membrane for detection. The membranes were blocked with 5% milk for 1 hour at room temperature, washed three times with Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST), and then incubated at 4 ℃ overnight with antibodies of interest (HIF2α, ab207607, RRID: AB_2618694, 1:1,000, Abcam, Shanghai, China; USP10, 19374-1-AP, RRID: AB_10858617, 1:1,000, Proteintech Group, Inc., Wuhan, China, and β-actin, #4967, RRID: AB_330288, 1:1,000, Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China). The membranes were subsequently washed three times with TBST and incubated with secondary antibodies [anti-mouse IgG #ZB-2305, RRID: AB_2747415, 1:2,000; anti-rabbit IgG #ZB2301, RRID: AB_2747412, 1:2,000; Beijing Zhongshan, China) for 1 hour at room temperature, after which the protein immunoreactive signals were visualized with a western film processor.

## MG132 assays
786-O, OS-RC-2, SW839, and ACHN cells were seeded in 6-cm dishes, and after 24 hours, the cells were treated with PR-619 (3 µM), MG132 (20 µg/mL), or PR-619 (3 µM) plus MG132 (20 µg/mL) for 24 hours. Cell protein lysates were lysed as described for WB, and the HIF2α protein level in whole-cell lysates (WCLs) was detected by WB.

## Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry/mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) assays
The cell protein lysates were lysed as described for WB. The cell lysate was incubated with an anti-HIF2α antibody for 4 hours at 4 ℃. Subsequently, the cell lysates were washed and detected by PTM BIO (Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China) for HIF2a directly or not directly interacting with proteins.

## Transfection
786-O and OS-RC-2 cells were transfected with USP7, USP9X, USP10, USP15, and USP39 oligo RNAs using LipofectamineTM 3000 (#L3000015, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Shanghai, China). The oligo RNA sequences for USP7, USP9X, USP10, USP15, and USP39 are shown in Table S1. After 48 hours of transfection, the change in HIF2a protein expression in 786-O and OS-RC-2 tumor cells was detected by WB.

## Cell Counting Kit-8 assay
Cell proliferative capacity was evaluated using CCK8 kit (#C0042, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells were transfected with USP10 oligo RNAs for 48 hours, then, triplicate sets of 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells were plated at 4×103 cells/well in 96-well culture plates. After 48 hours, Subsequently, 20 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added into each well, and the plates were incubated for 3 hours at 37 ℃. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader, and then measured by a Microplate Autoreader (Bio-Tek Instruments). The growth rate was calculated by the following formula: growth rate = optical density (OD) value in USP10 oligo RNAs groups/average OD value in the control group ×100%.

## Colony formation assay
For the clonogenic assay, 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells were transfected with USP10 oligo RNAs for 48 hours, then, cells were seeded in 6-well plates (1,000 cells/well). After 7 days of culture in a humidified incubator, visible colonies were stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (w/v, Sigma, Shanghai, China) to determine colony-forming ability under two-dimensional conditions.

## Datasets
The Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2 (GEPIA2) platform is a renowned and frequently referenced tool for examining gene expression. It utilizes data from both tumor and normal samples sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project. The URL for accessing this resource is http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index. GEPIA2 was used to analyze USP10 expression in RCC, and box plots were generated to visualize the results. Furthermore, the relationship between the expression of USP10 and that of EPAS1 (HIF2α) was assessed via the ‘Correlation Analysis’ component of the platform, and P≤0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. We used the non-log scale for calculations and the log-scale axis for visualization, and our study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation from at least three independent experiments. Comparisons between two groups were evaluated by two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for multiple comparisons. P≤0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## HIF2α inhibition with PR-619 reduced HIF2α protein expression regardless of VHL status in RCC
HIF2α has been identified as a key transcription factor involved in RCC progression. To screen potential deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) that regulate HIF2α protein stability independent of VHL in RCC, we purchased numerous specific DUB inhibitors ( ). The OS-RC-2, 786-O, SW839, and ACHN cell lines were chosen because of their representative EPAS1 genotypes—missense mutation, truncating mutation, unknown, and wild type, respectively ( ). As shown in , 15 USP inhibitors were used to treat OS-RC-2 cells, and the WB results revealed that the ML364, DuBs-IN-2, GSK2643943, LDN-57444, and PR-619 inhibitors decreased HIF2α protein levels in cells. Furthermore, ML364, DuBs-IN-2, GSK2643943, LDN-57444, and PR-619 inhibitors were applied to assess their effects on HIF2α protein stability in 786-O and SW839 cells. The WB results revealed that only PR-619 decreased the HIF2α protein in all three cell lines (OS-RC-2, 786-O and SW839 cell lines) ( ). Our data shown in indicate that PR-619 (which mainly inhibits USP2, USP7, and USP10) reduces HIF2α protein levels independent of VHL gene status in RCC.
 

## USP10 stabilizes HIF2α protein and promotes RCC cell growth
To confirm the mechanism by which PR-619 promotes HIF2α protein degradation, MG132 was added to the 786-O, OS-RC-2, SW839, and ACHN cell lines, and ubiquitin-mediated degradation was assessed. As shown in , MG132 impeded HIF2α protein degradation in all cell lines, and the combination of MG132 and the PR-619 inhibitor partly restored HIF2α protein levels, which were reduced by the PR-619 inhibitor alone. According to the library, USP1, USP2, USP4, USP7, USP8, USP9X, USP10, USP14, USP15, USP19, USP20, USP24, USP28, USP47, and USP48 can all be inhibited by PR-619 ( ). To determine the precise USB related to HIF2α protein stability in our study, we planned the HIF2α IP-MS to identify proteins that directly or indirectly interact with HIF2α ( ). The MS results revealed that USP3, USP5, USP7, USP9, USP9X, USP10, USP11, USP15, USP16, USP22, USP25, USP39, and USP46 interact with the HIF2α protein in 293T cells ( ). As shown in , we speculated that the 5 USPs (USP2, USP9X, USP10, USP15, and USP39) were the USPs most likely to regulate HIF2α protein deubiquitination on the basis of the results shown in .
 To determine which USPs affect HIF2α protein levels, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting 5 USPs (USP2, USP9X, USP10, USP15, and USP39) were designed and transfected into 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells (Table S1). shows the effects of USP2, USP9X, USP10, USP15, and USP39 knockdown on HIF2α protein levels in 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells, respectively. We observed that only USP10 knockdown reduced the protein level of HIF2α in 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells ( ). To investigate the functional role of USP10 in RCC, we transfected 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells with three independent siRNAs targeting USP10. WB results confirmed knockdown efficiency of USP10, with all three siRNAs markedly reducing USP10 protein levels compared to control siRNA-transfected cells ( ). Functional characterization by CCK-8 assay revealed that USP10 silencing significantly attenuated cell viability in both 786-O and OS-RC-2 cell lines. The proliferative capacity was substantially diminished following USP10 depletion ( , P<0.0001). Furthermore, colony formation assay demonstrated that USP10 knockdown suppressed the clonogenic survival of 786-O and OS-RC-2 cell lines. The number of colonies formed was drastically reduced in USP10-silenced groups relative to control in both 786-O and OS-RC-2 cell lines ( ). Collectively, our data indicated that USP10 stabilizes HIF2α protein and USP10 depletion impairs RCC cells proliferation and clonogenicity.
 Our data in , USP10 expression was elevated in kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), and kidney chromophobe (KICH). Additionally, high USP10 expression was associated with high HIF2α expression in KIRP (P<0.001, R=0.36), KIRC (P=0.02, R=0.13), and KICH (P<0.001, R=0.39) ( ). We performed an IP assay to assess whether USP10 decreases HIF2α protein ubiquitination in 786-O and OS-RC-2 cells. As shown in , after transfection with USP10 siRNAs, the HIF2α protein ubiquitination level in 786-O cells increased. In summary, our results highlighted that USP10 regulates HIF2α protein levels through the deubiquitination pathway.
 

## Discussion
Owing to its overexpression and increased activity, HIF2α is a key oncogenic driver in RCC ( ). VHL mutation has been reported to be the main cause of HIF2α accumulation in RCC ( ). Beyond the VHL-mediated degradation pathway, emerging evidence indicates that HIF2α stability is governed by multiple layers of regulation. Int6 was reported to induce proteasome inhibitor-sensitive HIF2α degradation that is to be both hypoxia- and pVHL-independent ( ). Mitochondrial E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 1 (MUL1) is the other E3 ligase involved in HIF1α degradation in Zachary A Bacigalupa’s report ( ). Wang et al. identified K394, K497, and K503 of HIF2α as key ubiquitination sites for ubiquitin-protein ligase E3B (UBE3B), and UBE3B depletion suppressed breast tumor growth and lung metastasis both in vitro and in vivo ( ). However, stabilization mechanisms extend beyond mere escape from degradation—mTORC2-dependent translation and Nox oxidase-p22phox signaling actively promote HIF2α protein synthesis in VHL-null RCC, coupling biosynthetic and proteolytic control ( ). Additionally, DUBs including USP7, USP10 (as shown in our results), and USP37 counteract ubiquitination, directly enhancing HIF2α stability. Collectively, these findings underscore that HIF2α levels represent a dynamic equilibrium between translation, VHL-dependent and VHL-independent degradation, and DUB-mediated stabilization. Future work should dissect the relative contributions of these pathways in distinct RCC subtypes to identify combined strategies targeting both HIF2α production and turnover.
Research reports on pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma have revealed that in highly “immune-cold” tumors, HIF 2α inhibitors can reprogram the immune microenvironment and inhibit tumor growth ( ). In 2024, a clinical study on renal cancer (NCT04195750) was reported in The New England Journal of Medicine: among patients with advanced renal cancer who had not benefited from immunotherapy, those treated with HIF2α inhibitors had a clinical ORR of approximately 20% (82/374). However, long-term use of HIF 2α inhibitors may lead to drug resistance, partly due to mutations in the key amino acids in the binding domain of HIF 2α with the inhibitors ( ). Therefore, it is necessary to further investigate the mechanisms of drug resistance and develop new inhibitors or combination therapeutic strategies to overcome this issue. By blocking the function of HIF 2α, these inhibitors can suppress the growth and metastasis of renal cancer cells and hold great promise for application.
In our study, we planned USP inhibitors treatment and Co-IP experiments, and we found that USP10 served as a DUB for HIF2α RCC cell lines. The process of targeted protein degradation through ubiquitination is a complex molecular mechanism. USP37 has also been reported to act as a DUB for HIF2α regardless of VHL gene status ( ). In our study, we demonstrated that USP10 knockdown could decrease HIF2α ubiquitination levels in cell lines, which likely resulted from USP37 knockdown and decreased HIF2α ubiquitination levels ( ). To our knowledge, our results and those of Hong et al. revealed two DUBs involved in HIF2α deubiquitination in RCC cell line ( ). While our current study focused on ubiquitination, investigating how the USP10-HIF2α axis influences tumor glycolysis, lipid metabolism, or immune cell recruitment in the RCC microenvironment could inform combined metabolic and immunotherapeutic strategies.
We detected USP10 expression in RCC tissues, and our results were consistent with the increased expression of USP10 in RCC. USP10 was reported to play a significant role in pulmonary disease models by enhancing cell migration, regulating Smad4 protein expression, modulating autophagy through deubiquitination, and influencing tumor resistance to apoptosis induction ( - ). USP10 promoted hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation via the deubiquitination and stabilization of YAP/TAZ, and USP10 was identified as a DUB in the treatment of esophageal cancer ( ). Furthermore, targeting the interaction of USP10-N1ICD complex may attenuate chemoresistance in pancreatic cancer ( ). In our study, USP10 knockdown via three independent siRNAs ( ) significantly reduced viability and clonogenic survival in 786-O and OS-RC-2 RCC cells ( ), demonstrating that USP10-mediated HIF2α stabilization is essential for RCC proliferation. However, it is also urgent to validate USP10 expression levels in larger ccRCC patient cohorts and determine its prognostic value and correlation with clinical outcomes in patients undergoing HIF2α inhibitor therapy in our future work.
Currently, Wu-5, P22077, HBX19818, and spautin-1 are reported as USP10 inhibitors ( ). Wu-5 is a newly discovered USP10 inhibitor that increases the sensitivity of AML cells to the FLT3 inhibitor crenolanib by directly interacting with and inactivating USP10 ( ). The inhibitor of spautin-1 is a small molecule that suppresses the deubiquitinating activity of USP10 and USP13, leading to increased ubiquitination of Beclin1 and subsequently reducing the level of autophagy in cancer cell lines ( ). Given the clinical emergence of resistance to HIF2α inhibitors due to mutations in the HIF2α binding domain, our findings suggested that combining HIF2α inhibitors with USP10 inhibitors (such as Wu-5 or Spautin-1) may overcome this resistance. Future studies will evaluate the synergistic anti-tumor effects of this combination in both in vitro and in vivo.

## Conclusions
In our study, we demonstrated that USP10, a DUB, can deubiquitinate the HIF2α protein regardless of VHL gene status in RCC.
