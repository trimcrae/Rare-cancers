Death receptor 4 (DR4; also called TNFRSF10A or TRAIL-R1) is a cell surface protein, which functions as a pro-apoptotic protein that transduces TRAIL death signaling to trigger apoptosis. DR4 expression is positively regulated by MEK/ERK signaling and thus can be downregulated by MEK/ERK inhibition. This study thus focused on determining the effects of AKL-TKIs on DR4 expression and the underlying mechanisms. Three tested ALK-TKIs including APG-2449, brigatinib and alectinib effectively and preferentially inhibited Akt/mTOR as well as MEK/ERK signaling and decreased cell survival in ALK-mutant (ALKm) NSCLC cells with induction of apoptosis. This was also true for DR4 downregulation, which occurred even at 2 h post treatment. These ALK-TKIs did not affect DR4 protein stability, rather decreased DR4 mRNA expression. In parallel, they promoted degradation and reduced the levels of Fra-1 and c-Jun, two critical components of AP-1, and suppressed AP-1 (Fra-1/c-Jun)-dependent transcription/expression of DR4. Hence, it appears that ALK-TKIs downregulate DR4 expression in ALKm NSCLC cells via facilitating Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation and subsequent AP-1 suppression. Our findings thus warrant further investigation of the biological significance of DR4 downregulation in ALK-targeted cancer therapy.

## Introduction
Death receptor 4 (DR4; also named TRAIL-R1 or TNFRSF10A), is a cell surface receptor for tumor necrosis factor-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL) and transduces extrinsic apoptotic signaling upon binding to TRAIL in a similar way as its sibling, death receptor 5 (DR5), does. This process involves trimerized DR4 (or DR5) interaction with the adaptor protein, Fas-associated death domain (FADD), which further recruits caspase-8 through the death effector domain of FADD, resulting in caspase-8 activation and eventually apoptosis , . Immune cells such as cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cells can produce and secrete TRAIL. Thus, one salient mechanism by which immune effector cells eradicate malignant cancer cells in vivo is by ligating endogenous TRAIL generated by these immune cells with its receptors on cancer cells and subsequent induction of apoptosis of cancer cells , , .
Although DR4 is in general considered to be a pro-apoptotic protein as discussed above, DR4 expression is positively regulated by oncogenic MEK/ERK signaling through AP-1-mediated activation of gene transcription, as our lab has previously reported . Upon inhibition of this signaling, such as with MEK inhibitors, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or MET inhibitors, DR4 expression was in fact downregulated , , . Moreover, enforced downregulation of DR4 via gene knockdown even sensitized some cancer cells to TRAIL or the EGFR-TKI, osimertinib , , suggesting the possible anti-apoptotic function of DR4. It was previously reported that genetic suppression of Fas, another well-known death receptor, even sensitizes EGFR mutant (EGFRm) NSCLC cells to erlotinib, likely due to the inhibition of NF-κB signaling . Other studies suggested that TRAIL death receptors including DR4 are involved in mediating TRAIL-induced generation of inflammatory cytokines such as CCL2/MCP1, IL-8 and CXCL1 in TRAIL-resistant cancer cell lines by promoting the formation of a “FADDosome” complex that primarily consists of caspase-8, FADD and RIPK1 and subsequently activating NF-κB signaling , . This finding is basically in agreement with a previous report showing that TRAIL death receptors activate NF-κB-dependent cytokine secretion . Consequently, the secreted inflammatory cytokines will inactivate immune cells, generating a tumor-supportive immune microenvironment that favors tumor growth , .
Anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) mutations, particularly gene rearrangements or fusions, occur in 3-7% of NSCLC and have been clinically associated with absence of smoking, younger age, and adenocarcinoma histology . The most common such genetic aberration is the echinoderm microtubule-associated protein-like 4 (EML4)-ALK fusion. Subsequent biological studies that demonstrated ALK fusions as driver mutations have greatly spurred the development of TKIs targeting ALK fusions. To date, ALK-TKIs have rapidly evolved from the first (e.g., crizotinib), to second (e.g., ceritinib, alectinib and brigatinib) and to the current third generation (e.g., lorlatinib) . The availability of these ALK-TKIs has led to significant clinical survival benefit in patients with ALK-mutant (ALKm) lung cancer . APG-2449 is a novel oral multi-kinase inhibitor that primarily inhibits ALK, ROS1, and FAK , . It belongs to third generation ALK-TKI category and is currently in clinical testing (www.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT03917043).
As a cell surface receptor protein, ALK shares with other cell surface receptor proteins such as EGFR and MET in the positive regulation of cell survival and growth primarily through activation of both PI3K/Akt/mTOR and Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathways . Accordingly, inhibition of ALK, e.g., with an ALK-TKI, will lead to inactivation of these signaling pathways. For this reason, we speculated that ALK-TKIs might have the capacity to downregulate DR4 expression in ALKm NSCLC cells given their inhibitory effect on MEK/ERK signaling. Thus, the current study focused on determining whether APG-2449 and other ALK-TKIs reduce DR4 levels and demonstrating the underlying mechanisms in ALKm NSCLC cells.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
APG-2449 was provided by Ascentage Pharm (Rockville, MD). Alectinib and brigatinib were purchased from MedChemExpress (Monmouth Junction, NJ). PS341 was originally provided by Millennium Pharmaceuticals (Cambridge, MA). Human recombinant TRAIL was purchased from PeproTech, Inc. (Rocky Hill, NJ). ERK1/2 and S6 antibodies were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc (Santa Cruz, CA). DR5, p-ERK1/2, Akt, p-Akt, p70S6K, p-p70S6K, 4EBP1, p-4EBP1, p-S6, Fra-1, p-Fra-1, c-Jun, p-c-Jun, c-Fos, p-c-Fos and FosB antibodies were all purchased from Cell Signaling Technology, Inc (Beverly, MA). DR4 antibody (B-N28) was purchased from Cell Sciences (Canton, MA). Other reagents and antibodies were the same as described previously .

## Cell lines and cell culture
H3122 and H2228 were purchased from ATCC (Manassas, VA). Other cell lines used in this study were described in our previous study , . Gene authentication was not done for these cell lines. Mycoplasma was detected annually or upon receiving using Lonza MycoAlert Mycoplasma Detection Kit (VWR; Suwanee, GA) to ensure all cell lines were mycoplasma negative. All cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 containing 5% fetal bovine serum except for H2228 that was cultured in RPMI 1640 containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 293T that was cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 95% air.

## Cell viability assay
Cell numbers in 96-well plates were measured by the sulforhodamine B (SRB) assay as previously described .

## Detection of apoptosis
The effects of the given drug treatments on apoptosis were evaluated by annexin V staining/flow cytometry using the annexin V/7-AAD Apoptosis Detection Kit (BD Biosciences; Franklin Lakes, NJ) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Detection of protein cleavage by Western blot analysis was also used as an additional indication of apoptosis.

## Western blot analysis
Preparation of whole-cell protein lysates and subsequent Western blotting were essentially the same as described previously , .

## Protein stability assay
Protein degradation rate was measured using cycloheximide (CHX) chase assay as previously reported .

## RNA extraction and reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
The procedures for RNA extraction and RT-qRCR were described previously . Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as an internal normalization control. The primers used are listed as follows: forward 5′-TTGCATCGGCTCAGGTTGT-3′ and reverse 5′-GAGACGAAAGTGGACAGCGA-3′ for DR4; and forward 5′-TGCACCACCAACTGCTTA-3′ and reverse 5′-GGATGCAGGGATGATGTTC-3′ for GAPDH.

## Reporter plasmids and luciferase activity assay
All reporter constructs used in this study for luciferase assays were the same as described previously .

## Gene silencing using small-hairpin RNA (shRNA)
Fra-1 shRNA (shFra-1) #1 (TRCN0000019541) and #2 (TRCN0000019542) and c-Jun shRNA (shJun) (TRCN0000010366) in pLKO.1 lentiviral vectors were purchased from Millipore Sigma (St. Louis, MO) and used as recommended by the manufacturer. The cell lines were selected with puromycin post infection and gene silencing effects were evaluated by Western blotting.

## Statistical analysis
The statistical significance of differences between two tested groups was analyzed with two-sided unpaired Student's t tests (for equal variances) or with Welch's corrected t test (unequal variances) by use of Graphpad InStat 9 software. Results were statistically significant at P < 0.05.

## Results

## ALK-TKIs show selectivity against the growth of ALKm NSCLC cells with induction of apoptosis
We first checked the selectivity of the three different ALK-TKIs, APG-2449, alectinib and brigatinib against the growth of the two ALKm NSCLC cell lines, H3122 and H2228. Both cell lines responded well to these 3 ALK-TKIs but minimally to the third generation EGFR-TKI, osimertinib ( A). In contrast, the three EGFRm cell lines, HCC827, PC-9 and H1975, were very sensitive to osimertinib, but resistant to these ALK-TKIs, particularly APG-2449 ( B). The A549 cell line has wild-type (WT) ALK and EGFR genes and was insensitive to the tested ALK-TKIs and osimertinib ( B). These data indicate the selectivity of these ALK-TKIs against ALKm NSCLC cells. Although APG-2449 was almost ineffective against the growth of the EGFRm NSCLC cell lines, both alectinib and brigatinib exerted some degree of activity in decreasing the survival of these EGFRm NSCLC cell lines ( B), suggesting the greater selectivity of APG-2449. These three ALK-TKIs increased cleavage of caspase-3 and PARP ( C) and annexin V-positive cell population ( D) in H3122 cells, indicating the induction of apoptosis. 

## ALK-TKIs exert varied effects on suppressing MEK/ERK and Akt/mTOR signaling in ALKm NSCLC cells
We next compared the effects of these ALK-TKIs on modulation of signaling downstream of ALK in ALKm NSCLC cells. Alectinib and brigatinib, but not APG-2449, effectively reduced phosphorylated ERK1/2 (p-ERK1/2) levels in both H3122 and H2228 cell lines ( A). Conversely, these three ALK-TKIs consistently decreased the levels of p-Akt, p-p70S6K, p-S6, and p-4EBP1. Interestingly, both brigatinib and alectinib (but not APG-2449) also decreased the levels of p-ERK1/2, p-Akt, p-P70S6K, p-S6 and p-4EBP1 in PC-9 cells. These three ALK-TKIs did not modulate the phosphorylation of these proteins in A549 cells with WT ALK gene. These data again suggest that APG-2449 has a better selectivity than alectinib and brigatinib toward mutant ALK. Time-course analysis for the cells treated with APG-2449 or alectinib showed that both agents starting at 2 h post treatment effectively decreased the levels of p-Akt, p-p70S6K, p-S6 and p-4EBP1; these suppressive effects remained up to 24 h ( B), indicating a rapid and sustained effect on suppressing Akt/mTOR signaling. Alectinib, but not APG-2449, exerted a similar kinetic activity in decreasing the levels of p-ERK1/2, indicating a rapid and lasting suppressive effect on MEK/ERK signaling by alectinib as well. 

## ALK-TKIs effectively decrease the levels of DR4 in ALKm NSCLC cells
Given that DR4 is positively regulated by MEK/ERK signaling and its expression is suppressed by MEK inhibitors, EGFR-TKIs or MET inhibitors , , , we then logically questioned whether ALK-TKIs have similar effects on altering DR4 levels. Hence, we further detected DR4 in ALKm NSCLC cells exposed to ALK-TKIs. APG-2449, alectinib and brigatinib all decreased the levels of DR4 in the ALKm cell lines, H3122 and H2228, but not in A549 cells without ALK mutation. However, alectinib and brigatinib, but not APG-2449, decreased DR4 levels in the EGFRm PC-9 cells as well ( A). These ALK-TKIs could decrease DR4 levels in H3122 cells even at 0.1 µM ( B). DR4 reduction induced by both APG-2449 and alectinib occurred early at 2 h and was sustained at 24 h post treatment ( C). Although DR5 is the sibling protein of DR4, these ALK-TKIs only weakly or minimally decreased DR5 levels, which also occurred late after 8 h post treatment ( ). Thus, it is clear that these ALK-TKIs effectively decrease DR4 levels in ALKm NSCLC cells. 

## ALK inhibitors decrease DR4 levels primarily through suppressing DR4 gene transcription in ALKm NSCLC cells
DR4 protein is post-translationally regulated via MARCH8-mediated protein degradation , . EGFR-TKIs decrease DR4 levels in part by facilitating protein degradation as our lab recently demonstrated . To determine whether ALK-TKIs decreased DR4 levels via a similar mechanism, we examined their effects on DR4 in the presence of the proteasome inhibitor, MG132. In H3122 cells, MG132 failed to rescue DR4 reduction induced by these ALK-TKIs although it did partially rescue DR4 reduction induced by osimertinib in PC-9 cells ( A). Similar results were also generated with another proteasome inhibitor, PS-341, in H3122 cells ( B). In H2228 cells, both MG132 and PS-341 also failed to rescue DR4 reduction induced by these ALK-TKIs (Fig. S1A). We noted that MG132 in fact effectively decreased DR4 levels in ALKm NSCLC cells. Thus, we conducted detailed analysis of MG132 modulation of DR4 in these cell lines. While MG132 did not decrease or increase DR4 levels in PC-9 cells, it potently decreased DR4 in both H3122 and H2228 cells in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. S1B). PS-341 clearly increased DR4 levels in PC-9 cells, but did not apparently decrease DR4 levels in H3122 or H2228 cells (Fig. S1C). However, both proteasome inhibitors consistently increased DR5 levels in both PC-9 and H3122 cell lines (Figs. S1B and C). 
We further analyzed the effects of APG-2449 and alectinib on modulating DR4 stability by conducting CHX chase assays. As presented in C, the DR4 degradation rate in cells treated with either APG-2449 or alectinib was comparable with that in cells exposed to DMSO, indicating that these ALK-TKIs do not alter DR4 stability. We lastly determined whether these ALK-TKIs suppress DR4 mRNA expression by conducting RT-qPCR and found that both APG-2449 and alectinib significantly decreased DR4 mRNA levels ( D), indicating transcription suppression.

## ALK-TKIs reduce the levels of Fra-1 and c-Jun via promoting Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation in ALKm NSCLC cells
Given that DR4 is regulated by AP-1 at transcriptional levels , we wondered whether ALK-TKIs inhibit DR4 expression through AP-1 inhibition. To this end, we first determined whether ALK-TKIs affect the levels of AP-1 component proteins in ALKm NSCLC cells. As shown in A, all ALK-TKIs at concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 1 µM effectively decreased the levels of not only phosphorylated, but also total, Fra-1 and c-Jun in H3122 cells. These ALK-TKIs decreased the levels of p-c-Fos, but not c-Fos and FosB proteins. Time-course analysis also showed that both APG-2449 and alectinib effectively decreased the levels of p-Fra-1, Fra-1, p-c-Jun and c-Jun within the tested period ranging from 2 h to 24 h ( B). Hence, it is clear that ALK-TKIs rapidly and effectively reduce the levels of both Fra-1 and c-Jun. 
Given that the phosphorylation of Fra-1 and c-Jun affects their protein stability , , we then questioned whether ALK-TKIs decreased the levels of Fra-1 and c-Jun via facilitating protein degradation. To this end, we assessed the rescue effects of MG132 on the reduction of Fra-1 and c-Jun induced by these ALK-TKIs. Indeed, the reduction of Fra-1 and particularly c-Jun was largely prevented in cells exposed to MG132 in comparison with cells treated with DMSO solvent control ( C). Moreover, both Fra-1 and c-Jun were degraded much faster in APG2449-treated cells than in DMSO control cells ( D and E). Collectively, we concluded that ALK-TKIs facilitate proteasomal degradation of Fra-1 and c-Jun.

## ALK-TKIs inhibit AP-1 transcriptional activity and decrease DR4 expression through suppressing AP-1-mediated transcription of the DR4 gene
We next checked whether the ALK-TKIs indeed suppress AP-1 transcriptional activity. Thus, we transfected AP-1 luciferase reporter construct into H3122 cells followed by treatment with APG-2449 or alectinib in the absence and presence of TPA stimulation. As shown in A, both ALK-TKIs not only significantly decreased the basal level of AP-1 activity, but also TPA-stimulated AP-1 activity, indicating that these ALK-TKIs indeed inhibit AP-1 activity. We further determined the effects of these ALK-TKIs on reporter constructs carrying the DR4 promoter region with an active or WT AP-1 binding site and a mutant AP-1 site, respectively, in H3122 cells. Transfection of the DR4 promoter with WT AP-1 reporter construct generated higher luciferase activity than did the DR4 promoter harboring mutant AP-1 construct. Both APG-2449 and alectinib significantly reduced luciferase activity in cells transfected with DR4 promoter with WT AP-1 reporter construct, but not in cells transfected with DR4 promoter harboring mutant AP-1 construct ( B). These findings indicate suppression of AP-1-dependent DR4 transcription. To assess the direct involvement of Fra-1 and/or c-Jun in mediating DR4 expression, we knocked down Fra-1 and c-Jun singly and in combination using two different shRNA targeting each of them. We found that knockdown of either one alone did not cause DR4 reduction; however, co-knockdown of both Fra-1 and c-Jun robustly decreased DR4 levels ( C). Thus, inhibition of Fra-1/c-Jun-mediated AP-1 by co-knockdown of Fra-1 and c-Jun is sufficient to cause DR4 reduction, suggesting the direct involvement of these proteins in the positive regulation of DR4 expression ( D). 

## Sustained pretreatment of ALKm NSCLC cells with ALK-TKIs impairs the ability of TRAIL to induce apoptosis
DR4 is known to be a receptor for the death ligand, TRAIL, for transducing intracellular extrinsic apoptotic signaling, resulting in apoptosis . Both H2228 and particularly H3122 cells were responsive to TRAIL ( A). Thus, it was logical to determine the impact of DR4 downregulation induced by ALK-TKIs on cell response to TRAIL. Hence, we first pre-treated H3122 or H2228 cells with different ALK-TKIs for 6 h and then switched to TRAIL for an additional 12 h after washing away the ALK-TKIs. Under such conditions, TRAIL-induced apoptosis was not apparently altered as evaluated by the cleavage of PARP and caspase-3 and detection of annexin V-positive cells (Figs. S2A and B). When the pretreatment time was increased to 12 h, we started to detect higher levels of uncleaved forms of caspase-3 and PARP in cells pre-treated with ALK-TKIs than in those pre-treated with DMSO, in which both PARP and caspase-3 were almost converted to the cleaved forms (Fig. S2C). This was also true when the pre-treatment time was increased to 24 h ( B). Via annexin V-staining and flow cytometry, we detected significantly fewer apoptotic cells in ALK-TKI-pretreated cells exposed to TRAIL than in control cells treated with TRAIL ( C). Therefore, it appears that sustained pretreatment of ALKm NSCLC cells with ALK-TKIs compromises the ability of TRAIL to induce apoptosis. 

## Discussion
In this study, we used three ALK-TKIs including APG-2449, alectinib and brigatinib. While both alectinib and brigatinib are approved drugs for the treatment of ALKm NSCLC, APG-2449 is a third generation ALK-TKI under clinical investigation. All three ALK-TKIs showed reasonable selectivity and comparable activities against the growth of tested ALKm NSCLC cell lines including induction of apoptosis. Although APG-2449 also possesses inhibitory activity against FAK and ROS1 , it actually exhibited better selectivity than brigatinib and alectinib against ALKm NSCLC cells given that both brigatinib and alectinib exerted much more potent effects than APG-2449 in decreasing the survival of EGFRm NSCLC cells and in suppressing the MEK/ERK and PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling with downregulation of DR4 in the EGFRm PC-9 NSCLC cell line. Another clear difference between APG-2449 and the other two ALK-TKIs is that APG-2449 only weakly suppressed ERK1/2 phosphorylation, suggesting its weak activity against MEK/ERK signaling.
Similar to EGFR-TKIs such as osimertinib , these ALK-TKIs preferentially decreased the levels of DR4 in comparison with their minimal reduction of DR5 levels. Both alectinib and brigatinib clearly decreased DR4 levels in PC-9 cells, suggesting that these ALK-TKIs may possess some activity against mutant EGFR. In fact, brigatinib has been shown to bind and inhibit mutant EGFR . While osimertinib downregulates DR4 levels in EGFRm NSCLC cells through both inhibiting AP-1-dependent transcription and facilitating MARCH8-mediated proteasomal degradation of DR4 secondary to MEK/ERK inhibition, these ALK-TKIs decrease DR4 levels primarily via suppressing AP-1-mediated transcription of DR4 in ALKm NSCLC cells in a mechanism similar to that of MET inhibitors in the negative regulation of DR4 expression in MET-amplified NSCLC cells . One interesting finding in this study is that APG-2449 reduced DR4 levels as effectively as alectinib and brigatinib did albeit with minimal or no activity in inhibiting ERK1/2 phosphorylation in the tested ALKm NSCLC cell lines, clearly suggesting an MEK/ERK inhibition-independent downregulation of DR4.
Albeit with varied activity against ERK1/2 phosphorylation, the three tested ALK-TKIs showed consistent effects in rapidly and effectively decreasing the levels of both Fra-1 and c-Jun and suppressing AP-1 transcriptional activity as did to DR4. Moreover, we have demonstrated that ALK-TKIs reduce the levels of Fra-1 and c-Jun through facilitating their proteasomal degradation evidenced by the rescue of Fra-1 and c-Jun reduction with proteasome inhibition and facilitation of Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation rates. In this study, we also detected reduction of phosphorylated Fra-1 (S265) and c-Jun (S73) levels. It is known that Fra-1 phosphorylation at S252 and S265 by ERK1/2 increases protein stability and leads to accumulation of Fra-1 in cancer cells , . In addition, the ERK1/2-dependent Fra-1 phosphorylation and stabilization enhances c-Jun stability by promoting heterodimerization with c-Jun, although c-Jun S73 phosphorylation is not involved in this process . Thus, it is logical to suggest that ALK-TKIs such as brigatinib and alectinib inhibit MEK/ERK signaling, leading to destabilization of Fra-1 and c-Jun and eventual reduction of their levels with suppression of AP-1 (Fra-1/c-Jun) activity ( D). However, this does not explain the effect of APG-2449 on the induction of Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation since it induced little or no inhibition of ERK1/2 phosphorylation. Hence, it is likely that APG-2449 induces ERK1/2-independent Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation and subsequent AP-1 suppression. Further study on understanding the mechanism by which APG-2449 induces ERK1/2-independent degradation of Fra-1 and c-Jun and AP-1 suppression is needed and warranted. Nonetheless, these ALK-TKIs inhibit Fra-1/c-Jun-mediated AP-1 transcriptional activity via induction of Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation.
Despite of varied activities against ERK1/2 phosphorylation, these ALK-TKIs shared similar effects in inhibiting the phosphorylation of Akt, p70S6K and S6, indicating effective suppression of the PI3K/mTOR/p70S6K signaling axis. Whether suppression of this signaling axis contributes to induction of Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation, suppression of AP-1 activity and subsequent downregulation of DR4 expression ( D) needs to be studied.
DR5 expression is also positively regulated by the Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway with a different underlying mechanism involving CHOP/ILK-mediated gene transcription, as our lab demonstrated previously , . The tested ALK-TKIs apparently exert weak effects on decreasing DR5 levels in ALKm NSCLC cells since sustained treatment was needed even for the weak reduction although some of the ALK-TKIs (i.e., alectinib and brigatinib) markedly inhibited MEK/ERK signaling. Hence, MEK/ERK signaling plays a less dominant role in the regulation of DR5 expression in these ALKm NSCLC cells.
In this study, only sustained pre-treatment of ALKm NSCLC cells with these ALK-TKIs impaired the ability of TRAIL to induce apoptosis. A previous study showed that silencing of DR5, but not DR4, in Huh-7 hepatocellular carcinoma cells attenuates TRAIL-induced apoptosis . Similarly, knockdown of DR5, but not DR4, abolished apoptosis induced by TRAIL or the combination of TRAIL with GGTI-298 (a geranylgeranyltransferase I inhibitor) in NSCLC cells as our lab previously reported . These findings suggest that DR5 plays a critical role in mediating TRAIL-induced apoptosis. Given that certain reductions in DR5 levels began only after a sustained treatment (e.g., after 12 h), it is very likely that DR5 reduction by sustained pre-treatment with ALK-TKIs may contribute to compromised effects on TRAIL-induced apoptosis. TRAIL/DR5 ligation represents an important mechanism by which immune cells such as T and natural killer cells to eliminate cancer cells , , . If ALK-TKIs function through activation of endogenous TRAIL/DR-mediated immune defense system to kill ALKm NSCLC cells beyond its direct effect on inducing apoptosis of cancer cells, sustained treatment of patients harboring ALKm NSCLC with an ALK-TKI may impair such a mechanism due to DR5 reduction, contributing to emergence of acquired resistance to ALK-targeted therapy. Similarly, there will be a concern if immunotherapy is used in combination with the ALK-targeted therapy. In this regard, the biological significance of DR4 downregulation needs to be further studied.
Although ALK-TKIs decrease DR4 expression, all tested ALK-TKIs also effectively induced apoptosis in ALKm NSCLC cells. The impact of DR4 downregulation induced by ALK-TKIs on ALK-targeted lung cancer therapy is unclear. A previous study from our lab with EGFR-TKIs in EGFRm NSCLC cells suggested that DR4 downregulation is tightly associated with cell response to EGFR-TKIs and rebound upregulation of DR4 may be associated with emergence of acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs . Moreover, DR4 expression is significantly associated with worse survival of NSCLC patients . Provocatively, our study even suggested an anti-apoptotic function of DR4 in EGFRm NSCLC cells since DR4 knockdown significantly sensitized the cells to osimertinib and TRAIL in terms of cell number reduction and apoptosis induction . Hence, the current mechanistic study warrants further investigation of the biological significance of DR4 downregulation in ALK-targeted therapy.
In conclusion, the current study has revealed that ALK-TKIs reduce DR4 levels in ALKm NSCLC cells while effectively decreasing their survival and inducing apoptosis. Mechanistically, this study suggests that ALK inhibitors downregulate the expression of DR4 in ALKm NSCLC cells primarily via facilitating ERK1/2-dependent and -independent Fra-1 and c-Jun degradation and subsequent AP-1 suppression. Our work warrants further investigation to unravel the biological significance of DR4 downregulation in ALK-targeted therapy.
