However, its expression and impact on cancers beyond the initially studied contexts remain unclear. This study focused on Lm-γ2F protein secretion and its role in non–small-cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), where EGFR signaling plays a pivotal role in malignancy progression. Lm-γ2F secretion was confirmed in serum-free conditioned medium from six NSCLC cell lines by Western blot analysis and further validated in NCI-H1650 cells. Hypothesizing that Lm-γ2F functions as an EGFR ligand, its effects in NSCLC cells lacking EGFR mutations were explored. In EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cell lines, Lm-γ2F overexpression significantly enhanced cell growth, survival, motility, and invasiveness through EGFR signaling activation compared with controls. Conversely, no effects were observed in VMRC-LCD cells lacking EGFR expression. Additionally, increased membrane-type 1 matrix metalloproteinase expression was detected in Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cells. In vivo, these cells exhibited elevated metastatic activity in a lung metastasis model. These findings suggested that ectopic Lm-γ2F expression contributes to malignant progression in NSCLC cells without EGFR mutations. Furthermore, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors may suppress metastasis in these contexts. This study provides novel insights into the oncogenic role of Lm-γ2F in NSCLC, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target to mitigate tumor progression and metastasis.

On the contrary, the Lm-γ2 chain is often expressed as a monomer (Lm-γ2m) at the front of invasive cancers, including non–small-cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC). , , Miyazaki et al reported that the N-terminal fragment of Lm-γ2 was detected at the leading edge of NSCLC tissues. Furthermore, high Lm-γ2m expression levels have been associated with poor survival, lymph node metastasis, advanced tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stages, and aggressive tumor phenotypes in multiple cancers, including NSCLC, as demonstrated through meta-analyses of public databases. , , , , 
A previous study, conducted using a prospective multicenter cohort, showed that Lm-γ2m is present in the serum of patients with cancer and can predict hepatocellular carcinoma occurrence in patients who have achieved sustained virologic response to hepatitis C virus infection. Additionally, a retrospective cohort study suggested that high serum levels of Lm-γ2m could serve as a risk factor for predicting distant metastases in patients with early-stage hepatocellular carcinoma. Collectively, these findings highlight the clinical significance of Lm-γ2m as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in cancer progression.
Beyond its role as a cancer biomarker, Lm-γ2m functions as a bioactive molecule. Moon et al reported that Lm-γ2m induces epithelial-mesenchymal transition in NSCLC, thereby enhancing metastatic potential. Furthermore, Lm-γ2m promotes resistance to gemcitabine by modulating epithelial-mesenchymal transition and ATP-binding cassette transporters in pancreatic ductal carcinoma. Additionally, it facilitates epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) membrane localization by preventing EGFR protein degradation via ubiquitination. 
Lm-γ2m acts as an EGF-like growth factor. , Domain III of the short arm in Lm-γ2 chain contains an EGF-like repeat domain. This domain is released when Lm-γ2m is cleaved by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), including membrane-type 1 MMP (MT1-MMP). The released fragment functions as a paracrine EGF-like growth factor, promoting malignant progression by activating EGFR signaling in both cancer cells and surrounding stromal cells. , Thus, Lm-γ2m plays a multifaceted role in cancer progression, from tumor initiation to metastasis and drug resistance. However, its activity depends on MMP cleavage, which serves as a rate-limiting factor in regulating its malignant potential. , 
The Lm-γ2 fusion gene (Lm-γ2F) was originally identified in SKOV-3 ovarian cancer cells and results from chromosomal translocation, in which a portion of the γ2 chain gene (LAMC2) fuses with the nuclear receptor NR6A1. Lm-γ2F comprises the short arm of the γ2 chain, containing the EGF-like domain, and a unique 13–amino acid sequence at the C-terminus derived from the antisense strand of NR6A1, which has no homology to known cellular proteins. Given that Lm-γ2F structurally resembles the Lm-γ2 cleavage fragment generated by MT1-MMP, it does not require proteolytic processing for EGFR-ligand activity, and thus activates EGFR more efficiently than Lm-γ2m. This suggests that Lm-γ2F may play a crucial role in malignant progression. However, the cancer types that express Lm-γ2F and its functional consequences remain largely unexplored.
Many driver gene mutations, including EGFR mutations, contribute to cancer initiation and serve as key targets for molecular therapies in NSCLC. , , However, approximately 30% of NSCLC cases lack detectable driver gene mutations, rendering molecular-targeted therapies ineffective. , Moreover, the mechanisms underlying carcinogenesis and malignant progression independent of driver gene mutations remain poorly understood. Recent studies have increasingly demonstrated that tumor development and progression, in the absence of genetic mutations, are driven by interactions within the tumor microenvironment and regulated by epigenetic modifications. , 
Given these insights, the ectopic expression of Lm-γ2F may function as a driver gene, supporting NSCLC cell growth and survival while promoting malignant behaviors, such as invasion and metastasis. This study was designed to investigate the role of ectopic Lm-γ2F expression as an EGFR ligand in the progression of NSCLC cells lacking EGFR mutations and explore the potential of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) in suppressing their malignant progression.

## Materials and Methods

## Antibodies and Inhibitors
Anti–Lm-γ2 mouse monoclonal antibodies (mAbs; D4B5) were gifted by Professor Kaoru Miyazaki (Yokohama City University, Yokohama, Japan). Anti–Lm-γ2F mouse mAb (23B1) was developed in the laboratory. Anti-EGFR rabbit (D38B1), anti–phosphorylated EGFR Tyr1068 rabbit (D7A5), anti-AKT1/2 rabbit (C67E7), anti–phosphorylated AKT1/2 Ser473 rabbit (D9E), anti–extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) mouse (3A7), and anti–phosphorylated ERK rabbit (D13.14.4E) mAbs were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA), while anti-V5 tag mAbs (M215-3) were procured from MBL (Tokyo, Japan). Anti–MT1-MMP rabbit polyclonal antibody was purchased from Millipore (Burlington, MA). Gefitinib was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO).

## Cells and Culture
The NSCLC cell lines PC-9, NCI-H1650, NCI-H1975, EKVX, RERF-LC-KJ, and VMRC-LCD were obtained from the Japanese Collection of Research Bioresource Cell Bank (National Institute of Biomedical Innovation, Health and Nutrition, Osaka, Japan). SKOV-3 cells for human ovarian cancer were acquired from ATCC (Manassas, VA). The cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium and minimal essential medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were maintained in 5% CO2 at 37°C. All cell lines were checked for mycoplasma contamination by PCR.

## Enhanced Expression of Lm-γ2F in NSCLC Cells
LAMC2-NR6A1 gene inserted into the pENTR/D-TOPO vector was generously provided by Dr. Daisuke Hoshino (Kanagawa Cancer Center Research Institute, Yokohama, Japan). The LAMC2-NR6A1 entry clone was integrated into pLenti6/V5-DEST using Gateway technology (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA).
EKVX, RERF-LC-KJ, and VMRC-LCD cells stably expressing LAMC2-NR6A1 chimeric fusion protein (Lm-γ2F) were generated using a lentiviral protein expression system. Cells were maintained in blasticidin (5 μg/mL for EKVX or 2 μg/mL for RERF-LC-KJ and VMRC-LCD) according to the manufacturer's instructions (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

## SDS-PAGE and Western Blot Analysis
NSCLC cells were lysed using radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer containing protease inhibitors (Millipore) and PhosSTOP (Sigma-Aldrich). Cells grown to confluency in a culture dish were incubated in serum-free conditioned medium (SF-CM) for 48 hours. SF-CM was collected and then centrifuged at 162 × g for 3 minutes. The supernatants were collected and again centrifuged at 20,000 × g for 30 minutes. The supernatants were collected, dialyzed against ice cold water, and concentrated using a freeze dryer. Protein lysates and CM proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes after SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions. After blocking with 3% skim milk, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight at the indicated concentration ( ). The membranes underwent another incubation with horseradish peroxidase–labeled secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. The bands were detected using chemiluminescence using EzWestLumi plus (ATTO) and visualized using Chemidoc Imaging System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA). 

## Two-Dimensional and Three-Dimensional Cell Growth Assays
EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cells were seeded at 1.0 × 104 and 5.0 × 104 cells/well, respectively, in 24-well plates and cultured at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 7 days. Cell counts were determined using Trypan blue on days 3, 5, and 7. For gefitinib-treated cultures, cells were incubated with gefitinib (500 nmol/L for EKVX and 250 nmol/L for RERF-LC-KJ) for 5 days.
For three-dimensional cultures, collagen gels were prepared by mixing collagen, 10 × minimal essential medium, and neutralization buffer in 24-well plates, followed by polymerization at 37°C. Next, 1.0 × 105 cells of EKVX or RERF-LC-KJ were added to the gel and further polymerized at 37°C. Finally, EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cells were incubated in growth medium containing 10% and 20% FCS, respectively. After trypsin treatment, cell numbers were determined using Trypan blue and microscopy.

## Spheroid Assay
Poly-(2-hydroxyethyl methacrylate) (poly-HEMA)–coated 96-well round-bottom plates were prepared with 25 μL of 5 mg/mL poly-HEMA in 95% ethanol. EKVX or RERF-LC-KJ cells were added at 1.0 × 104 cells/well and incubated in growth medium containing 10% FCS at 37°C in 5% CO2 for 8 days. Spheroid diameters were measured using ImageJ software version 1.54g (NIH, Bethesda, MD; https://imagej.net/ij), and growth activity was determined using the following calculation: V = (W2 × L)/2 (where V is tumor volume, W is tumor width, and L is tumor length). 

## Migration, Invasion, and Migration Velocity
Migration and invasion assays were performed as previously described. , Transwell chambers were used for migration assay, whereas Matrigel-coated transwell chambers were used for invasion assay. FCS-containing medium was added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant, and 1.0 × 105 cells in 0.1% FCS-containing medium were seeded in the upper chamber for both assays. After incubation of 16 and 20 hours for migration and invasion assays, respectively, at 37°C in 5% CO2, the cells that migrated to the lower filter were stained with 1% crystal violet dissolved in 20% methanol. The number of migrating cells was counted under a microscope at ×200 magnification, and the average of four fields of view was quantified.
For random migration assay, EKVX cells were seeded at 1.0 × 104 cells in a 24-well plate. Images were acquired every 20 minutes using a time-lapse microscope at ×200 magnification for 24 hours. ImageJ was used to quantify the migration distances of 20 cells per hour for 1 hour after seeding. Migration velocity was calculated by dividing the distance traveled by time.

## Xenograft
The metastatic potential of Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cells was examined using a lung experimental metastasis model in 7-week–old BALB/c nude mice. First, EKVX transfectant cells (Lm-γ2F #1) at 5 × 106 cells were suspended in 200 μL of phosphate-buffered saline and injected into the tail vein. After 13 weeks of injection, mice were scarified, lung tissue was excised, and foci on the lung surface were visually counted. The tissue was prepared as a formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded section, and metastasis foci were observed and counted using hematoxylin and eosin staining under a microscope. All animal experiments were approved by the Institute of Science Tokyo (D2022020-2) and were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals at the Institute of Science Tokyo.

## Statistical Analysis
Data are presented as means ± SD or ± SEM and are representative of at least three independent experiments. Statistical analysis was performed via unpaired t-tests or one-way analysis of variance to calculate the P values using IBM (Armonk, NY) SPSS Statistics version 29.0.0.0.

## Results

## Lm-γ2 and Lm-γ2F Are Detectable in NSCLC Cells
To screen Lm-γ2 and Lm-γ2F proteins secreted from NSCLC cells ( ), Western blot was performed using antibodies D4B5 and 23B1 in SF-CM obtained from the five NSCLC cells. D4B5 mAb recognizes the short arm of Lm-γ2, called DIII, whereas the 23B1 mAb specifically binds to a 13–amino acid polypeptide unique to Lm-γ2F ( A and ). Western blot using D4B5 antibody under reducing conditions showed that NSCLC cells produced multiple laminin proteins, with molecular weights of 140, 100, and 85 kDa, which corresponded with the Lm-γ2 chain and its related fragments, Lm-γ2′ and Lm-γ2x ( B). , Next, to specifically detect Lm-γ2F protein, specific 23B1 monoclonal antibody was successfully established. Immunoreactivity was analyzed using recombinant Lm-332γ2, Lm-γ2, and Lm-γ2F proteins, as shown in . 
Western blot using 23B1 mAb demonstrated a major 85-kDa and a minor 80-kDa band in the SF-CM of NCI-H1650 cells. In contrast, only the 80-kDa form was detected in SKOV-3 ovarian carcinoma cells that harbored the Lm-γ2F gene fusion gene ( B). The 85-kDa band may presumably be a glycosylated form of the 80-kDa form.

## Establishment of NSCLC Cells Stably Expressing Lm-γ2F
Lm-γ2F is known to promote malignant potential in ovarian carcinoma cells through EGFR signaling activation. However, its role in lung cancer progression is not well documented. Given the importance of EGFR signaling in lung cancer progression, Lm-γ2F was introduced in NSCLC cells carrying wild-type EGFR to examine whether ectopically expressed Lm-γ2F contributed to malignant progression in NSCLC cells devoid of EGFR mutations.
NSCLC, EKVX, and RERF-LC-KJ cells were used to generate Lm-γ2F–expressing cell lines, as they lack Lm-γ2F but express wild-type EGFR. VMRC-LCD cells that do not express EGFR were used as a negative control ( A). C-terminally V5-tagged Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX, RERF-LC-KJ, and VMRC-LCD cells were generated, and Lm-γ2F protein expression was confirmed in their SF-CM via Western blot using mAbs D4B5, 23B1, and V5 ( B). 

## Lm-γ2F Expression Promotes Cell Proliferation and Survival by Activating EGFR Signaling
The effect of Lm-γ2F expression on NSCLC cell proliferation was examined under both two-dimensional culture plate and three-dimensional collagen gel conditions.
In two-dimensional cultures, the proliferation activities of control and Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX, RERF-LC-KJ, and VMRC-LCD cells were compared. As a result, Lm-γ2F expression significantly enhanced the proliferation of EGFR-positive EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cells compared with the control ( , C and D). In contrast, Lm-γ2F expression had no effect on the growth of EGFR-negative VMRC-LCD cells ( E).
To determine whether Lm-γ2F–induced cell proliferation is dependent on EGFR signaling activation, both EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cells were treated with gefitinib, an EGFR inhibitor. Gefitinib treatment abrogated the growth-promoting effect of Lm-γ2F expression, indicating that Lm-γ2F expression promotes NSCLC cell proliferation through the activation of EGFR signaling ( , F and G). Lm-γ2F expression also promoted the proliferation of EKVX and RERF-LC-KJ cells under three-dimensional collagen gel conditions ( ).
Furthermore, the role of Lm-γ2F in cell survival was examined by spheroid formation, which evaluates anchorage-independent growth. Consistent with the proliferation results, Lm-γ2F expression significantly improved spheroid formation in EGFR-positive NSCLC cells ( , J and K), suggesting that Lm-γ2F confers resistance to anoikis in NSCLC cells by activating EGFR signaling.

## Lm-γ2F Expression Enhances Cell Motility and Invasiveness of NSCLC Cells
Transwell migration assay with EKVX cells was performed to examine the involvement of Lm-γ2F in NSCLC cell motility. Forced expression of Lm-γ2F significantly increased the cell motility of EKVX cells ( A), whereas Lm-γ2F expression did not affect motility in VMRC-LCD cells that do not express EGFR ( B). 
Migration velocity was also measured and compared between control cells and Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX using time-lapse microscopy. Forced expression of Lm-γ2F in EKVX cells exhibited a twofold higher migration velocity than control cells ( C and ). These results suggested that Lm-γ2F expression up-regulated NSCLC cell motility.
Furthermore, the involvement of Lm-γ2F expression in invasion and metastasis was analyzed using Matrigel-coated transmigration assay. Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cells had significantly higher invasiveness compared with control cells ( D). The involvement of EGFR signaling was verified in Lm-γ2F–induced invasiveness, and it was found that treatment of EKVX cells with gefitinib abolished the invasiveness. These results clarified that Lm-γ2F promotes NSCLC invasiveness by activating EGFR signaling ( E).
To further analyze the mechanism by which Lm-γ2F expression induced NSCLC invasiveness, secreted and membrane-type MMPs were analyzed in the presence/absence of Lm-γ2F expression in EKVX cells. First, SF-CMs obtained from control and Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cells were subjected to gelatin zymography to screen for secreted forms of MMPs, which revealed that EKVX cells secreted pro-MMP2 (64 kDa) and pro-MMP9 (90 kDa) in SF-CM. Although secretion of both MMPs was induced by Lm-γ2F expression, their active forms were not observed ( A). Thus, MT1-MMP expression was analyzed using MT1-MMP polyclonal antibody on Western blot in control and Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cell lysates. Although its expression level was lower than that in the positive control HT1080 cells, forced expression of Lm-γ2F induced MT1-MMP expression compared with that of control ( B). Interestingly, MT1-MMP expression was significantly decreased in the treatment of EKVX cells with gefitinib ( C). These findings suggested that Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX transfectant cells enhance MT1-MMP expression through EGFR signaling activation. 

## Lm-γ2F Activates EGFR and Its Downstream Signaling Pathways
To investigate whether Lm-γ2F activates EGFR and its downstream signaling molecules in lung cancer cells, the expression and phosphorylation of mitogen-activated protein kinase/ERK1/2 and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/AKT1/2 was examined using Western blot. Western blot analysis revealed that EGFR, ERK1/2, and AKT1/2 phosphorylation levels were elevated in Lm-γ2F–expressing EKVX cells compared with control cells ( D).
Taken together, Lm-γ2F expression activated mitogen-activated protein kinase/ERK and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/AKT signaling pathways involved in the promotion of NSCLC cell proliferation, survival, motility, and invasion.

## Lm-γ2F Expression Enhances the Metastatic Potential of Lung Cancer Cells in Vivo
In this study, Lm-γ2F expression promoted NSCLC progression in vitro. To confirm the role of Lm-γ2F expression in vivo, the metastatic potential of NSCLC cells was compared using an experimental lung metastatic mouse model ( A). Lm-γ2F expression significantly increased fivefold the number of metastatic foci in the lung compared with control cells ( , B and C). Furthermore, pathologic analysis with hematoxylin and eosin staining showed that Lm-γ2F expression promoted metastatic foci in lung tissue ( D). 
These results indicated that Lm-γ2F is a key player in acquiring metastatic potential in NSCLC cells in vivo.

## Discussion
To date, many mutations in oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes have been identified that play critical roles in cancer development and malignant progression. , , A significant proportion of lung cancers harbor EGFR mutations, and in such cases, treatment outcomes have substantially improved with the use of molecularly targeted therapies, such as EGFR-TKIs. , , However, approximately 40% of patients with lung cancer lack EGFR mutations, and yet develop metastases to distant organs. This highlights a gap in the understanding of the molecular mechanisms driving malignancy in EGFR wild-type lung cancer.
In this study, Lm-γ2F, which comprises the short arm of the laminin γ2 chain originally identified in ovarian cancer cells, was expressed in NSCLC cells. This study revealed that ectopically elevated Lm-γ2F promoted the malignant progression of NSCLC cells without EGFR mutation by constitutively activating EGFR signaling, as in NSCLC cells with EGFR mutation.
Previous pathologic studies have reported up-regulated expression of the Lm-γ2 chain as a monomer at the invasive front of NSCLC tissues. However, these studies used antibodies that recognize both the Lm-γ2 chain and Lm-γ2F, suggesting that the presence of Lm-γ2F may have been inadvertently detected in these studies. This highlights the need for more specific detection techniques to distinguish between Lm-γ2 chain and its fusion forms, such as Lm-γ2F, for a better understanding of their distinct roles in cancer progression.
Lm-γ2F may act as a driver gene in EGFR wild-type NSCLC, enhancing tumor proliferation, survival, motility, invasion, and metastasis through EGFR signaling activation. EGFR signaling induced by Lm-γ2F, as well as downstream mitogen-activated protein kinase/ERK and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/AKT pathways, was effectively suppressed by EGFR-TKIs, such as gefitinib. Consequently, gefitinib significantly reduced the proliferation, motility, and invasion of NSCLC cells expressing Lm-γ2F.
Although EGFR mutations such as Del19 or L858R have been widely studied in NSCLC, leading to the development of various small-molecule and antibody-based EGFR inhibitors, EGFR ligands have not been extensively developed. However, heparin-binding EGF-like growth factor has been identified as an EGFR ligand involved in the acquisition of malignant traits in breast, gastric, and ovarian cancers. , Similarly, amphiregulin, an EGFR ligand produced by EML-ALK fusion gene–positive NSCLC cells, has been reported to contribute resistance against ALK inhibitors. , These findings suggest two important possibilities. First, the ectopic expression of Lm-γ2F may act as a driver of malignant progression in EGFR wild-type NSCLC. And second, EGFR-TKI treatment may serve as an effective therapeutic strategy in EGFR wild-type NSCLC cases where Lm-γ2F is aberrantly expressed.
Lm-γ2F differs from EML4-ALK, ROS1, and RET fusion genes in that it is a secreted protein. , These fusion proteins promote malignancy in lung cancer through the constitutive activation of tyrosine kinase. , , , In contrast, Lm-γ2F promotes the constitutive EGFR activation as EGF-like growth factor. Therefore, Lm-γ2F is the first fusion gene of a secreted protein in lung cancer.
Lm-γ2F, a fusion protein, arises from the LAMC2 gene, encoding the laminin γ2 chain, fused to the antisense strand of an intron within a nuclear receptor gene, specifically NR6A1. Unlike the native Lm-γ2 chain, Lm-γ2F is secreted extracellularly in a truncated form that lacks domains I/II downstream of its EGF-like domain. This truncation enables Lm-γ2F to act as an EGFR ligand without requiring MMP-mediated processing. Furthermore, Lm-γ2F exhibits higher expression levels than conventional EGF-like growth factors, such as amphiregulin, accumulating extracellularly and functioning as a more potent EGFR ligand. As shown in an experimental lung metastasis model using EKVX cells, Lm-γ2F expression significantly enhances lung metastasis and promotes metastatic lesion growth, thereby strongly supporting its role in driving the malignant progression of NSCLC.
In conclusion, the present findings strongly suggested that ectopically expressed Lm-γ2F contributes to the malignant progression of EGFR wild-type NSCLC through EGFR activation ( A). Importantly, the ability of gefitinib to suppress the malignant progression induced by Lm-γ2F underlines its potential as a therapeutic agent for these cases ( B). Combining EGFR-TKIs with conventional anti-cancer drugs may provide synergistic benefits, including the suppression of metastasis. Future studies should aim to explore the broader applicability of targeting Lm-γ2F in other EGFR wild-type cancers and develop specific strategies to detect and inhibit this novel oncogenic driver.
