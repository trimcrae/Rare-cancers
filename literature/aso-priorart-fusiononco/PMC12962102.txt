## Summary
Bromodomain-containing protein 9 (BRD9), an essential subunit of the switch/sucrose non-fermentable (SWI/SNF) remodeling complex, functions as a chromatin reader that recognizes acetylated lysine (Kac) residues to modulate chromatin structure and gene expression. Dysregulated BRD9 contributes to cancer cell proliferation, metastasis, and drug resistance, making it a promising therapeutic target. However, BRD9 also exerts a tumor-suppressive function in a context-dependent manner. A comprehensive elucidation of the ambivalent role and underlying mechanism of BRD9 in oncogenesis is urgently needed. In this review, we present a detailed overview of the current knowledge regarding the structure, dysregulation, and biological functions of BRD9 in cancer. We then systematically summarize the oncogenic roles and tumor-suppressive functions of BRD9 in different contexts. Finally, we explore the potential of BRD9 as an innovative therapeutic target and discuss its implications in clinical oncology, providing insights into emerging directions for novel cancer treatment strategies.

## Introduction
Epigenetic dysregulation, mediated through aberrant histone post-translational modifications, DNA methylation patterns, non-coding RNA interactions, and chromatin structural reorganization, serves as a fundamental driver of tumorigenesis and cancer progression. Distinct from genetic mutations, these dynamic and reversible processes regulate gene expression programs that govern malignant transformation and metastatic dissemination. The epigenetic regulatory machinery comprises three functionally coordinated components: modulators responsible for establishing (writers), interpreting (readers), and removing (erasers) epigenetic signatures. 
Bromodomain-containing proteins (BRDs) constitute an evolutionarily conserved class of epigenetic readers that decode ε-N-lysine acetylation marks via stereospecific recognition through their bromodomains. This molecular recognition enables BRDs to orchestrate aberrant epigenetic regulation by recruiting transcriptional regulators and chromatin remodeling complexes. Contemporary research reveals their dual regulatory role in oncogenesis, exhibiting context-dependent tumor suppressive or oncogenic activities. For example, bromodomain and extra-terminal (BET) member BRD4 regulates transcriptional addiction by recognizing and activating super-enhancers, amplifying oncogenic drivers (e.g., MYC) in leukemias and solid tumors, while BRD7 demonstrates context-dependent tumor suppressor activity by transcriptional silencing oncogenic genes in nasopharyngeal and hepatic carcinomas. , This functional dichotomy extends beyond individual BRD members-several BRD isoforms exhibit functional ambivalence, dynamically shifting between oncogenic and tumor-suppressive roles depending on cellular microenvironmental cues. A systematic understanding of context-specific BRD interactomes across tumor subtypes is key to developing tumor-selective epigenetic therapies with reduced off-target effects.
The mechanistic complexity of BRD proteins becomes particularly evident when examining their paralog-specific integration into chromatin remodeling machinery. For instance, BRD4 is a well-characterized transcriptional regulator that functions by interacting with hyper-acetylated histone regions along the chromatin. In contrast, BRD7 and BRD9 exert their distinct pathogenic functions through their specific recruitment into the switch/sucrose non-fermentable (SWI/SNF) complexes, also known as BRG1/BRM-associated factor (BAF) complexes. These multi-subunit chromatin remodelers are mutated in ∼25% of human cancers and function as key regulators of chromatin accessibility and transcriptional reprogramming. Notably, mammalian SWI/SNF complexes are divided into three broad subfamilies with distinct organizations: (1) canonical BAF (cBAF), (2) non-canonical BAF (ncBAF/GBAF), and (3) polybromo-associated BAF (PBAF) ( ). Within this architectural hierarchy, BRD9 is recruited to ncBAF complexes through binding to SMARCA4/BRG1 or SMARCA2/BRM, , whereas BRD7 is uniquely integrated into the PBAF complex. Despite sharing 32% similarity in overall structure and 72% similarity in the bromodomain, BRD7 and BRD9 exhibit distinct functional roles within their respective complexes. Intriguingly, their activities may be antagonistic, suggesting divergent regulatory mechanisms that contribute to chromatin remodeling and transcriptional control. 
Emerging evidence suggests that BRD9 exhibits context-dependent oncogenic and tumor-suppressive activities in malignant neoplasia. , Aberrant BRD9 activation or overexpression drives tumorigenesis by orchestrating hallmark oncogenic processes, including sustaining proliferative signaling, enhancing invasion-metastasis cascades, driving cancer stemness and drug resistance, reprogramming metabolic pathways, and enabling microenvironmental remodeling. Conversely, BRD9 loss-of-function in specific cellular contexts disrupts the expression of tumor suppressor genes and compromises genomic stability, thereby promoting malignant transformation. The current review systematically examines the dualistic nature of BRD9, elucidating its structural architecture, physiological functions, and tissue-specific molecular mechanisms. We further review the molecular basis of BRD9’s dual roles in cancer and the therapeutic implications of emerging strategies, particularly small-molecule inhibitors and their translational progress.

## Dysregulation of bromodomain-containing protein 9 in human cancers

## Genomic alterations of bromodomain-containing protein 9
The BRD9 gene is a critical player in oncogenesis, driven by recurrent genomic alterations across malignancies that primarily encompass three molecular subtypes: (1) somatic mutations, including missense, truncating, and frameshift variants; (2) copy number alterations (CNAs) featuring both amplifications and deletions; and (3) structural rearrangements resulting in oncogenic gene fusions ( A). Understanding these alterations is essential for elucidating BRD9’s role in cancer biology and for developing targeted therapeutic strategies. 

## Mutation
Pan-cancer analyses via the cBioPortal webtool (https://www.cbioportal.org/) platform reveal BRD9 genomic alterations with tissue-specific alteration patterns. The highest frequencies of amplification-driven mutations were observed in lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), and adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) ( B), consistent with the previous investigation. Further supporting this, mutational architecture analysis delineates 106 somatic mutations (78.3% missense and 11.3% truncating) with domain-specific hotspots in the bromodomain (D167G/Y disrupting acetyl-lysine recognition) and DUF3512 domain ( C), in line with the findings of Chen et al. Clinically, D167 variants show no significant prognostic impact in uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC). In addition, computational structural modeling predicts that the Q479H mutation induces conformational changes potentially destabilizing the structural stability and function of BRD9, though functional validation in Sézary syndrome pathogenesis remains pending. 

## Amplifications or deletions
Recurrent CNAs involving the BRD9 gene are widely identified, profoundly disrupting its expression and functional roles in tumorigenesis. Amplification of BRD9 has been linked to increased cellular proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, highlighting its oncogenic potential. For example, in gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs), inhibition of BRD9 not only reduces cell proliferation but also enhances the efficacy of imatinib, a standard therapeutic agent, indicating that BRD9 amplification may contribute to treatment resistance. Furthermore, BRD9 amplification is associated with poor prognosis in patients with melanoma, mesothelioma (MESO), and liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), emphasizing the need for further investigation into the therapeutic implications of targeting BRD9 in tumors exhibiting such genomic alterations. Additionally, BRD9 deletions are observed in neoplastic tissues. It has been found that p.H467 and p.L468 are deleted in BRD9, which may lead to SWI/SNF recruitment impairment in Sézary syndrome.

## Fusions
BRD9 gene fusions occur in specific cancer types and are associated with distinct clinicopathological and molecular characteristics. In gallbladder carcinoma (GBC), the TPPP-BRD9 fusion, resulting from the juxtaposition of the TPPP gene and BRD9, has been reported as a recurrent event. Notably, TPPP-BRD9-positive GBCs frequently arise from preinvasive lesions (e.g., pyloric gland metaplasia with high-grade dysplasia) and exhibit a female predominance (Female: Male = 7:1). In B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (BCP-ALL), the BRD9-NUTM1 fusion is identified in the KMT2A-wild-type subgroup, particularly among infants with NUTM1 rearrangements (NUTM1-r). This fusion involves variable breakpoints in BRD9 (e.g., exons 3, 8, and 14) and is linked to favorable outcomes, with a 2-year event-free survival of 80% in NUTM1-r cases. While rare, BRD9-NUTM1 represents a molecularly defined subtype of infant BCP-ALL, highlighting its potential prognostic relevance. Pan-cancer analyses using the cBioPortal web tool on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset reveal that the BRD9-PMS2 fusion existed in two patients with osteosarcoma, which may exert oncogenic effects through the truncation of the PMS2 tumor suppressor. In contrast, BRD9-TERT fusions are observed in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), which remain biologically uncharacterized. Notably, truncating mutations in TERT, a proto-oncogene typically activated by gain-of-function alterations, suggest potential context-dependent oncogenic mechanisms requiring functional validation. , These findings underscore the heterogeneous roles of BRD9 fusions in malignancies, with implications for molecular subtyping and prognostic stratification. Although TPPP-BRD9 and BRD9-NUTM1 demonstrate clear clinicopathological associations, the functional consequences of BRD9-TERT and BRD9-PMS2 fusions remain speculative. A systematic characterization of BRD9 fusion partners, their structural configurations, and downstream pathways is imperative for delineating oncogenic mechanisms.

## Upstream regulatory factors of bromodomain-containing protein 9
To date, the regulatory networks governing BRD9 expression remain poorly characterized. Current evidence indicates that a multifaceted regulatory system may involve both transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms ( A). At the transcriptional level, double knock-in oncogenic PIK3CA (H1047R) and KRAS (G12V) mutations are demonstrated to elevate BRD9 mRNA levels to induce neoplastic transformation with undefined downstream transcriptional mediators. At the post-transcriptional level, microRNAs (miRNAs) critically modulate BRD9 expression via direct 3′ untranslated region (UTR) interactions. For example, miR-140-3p, frequently downregulated in LUSC, acts as a tumor suppressor by targeting BRD9 mRNA. Beyond miRNAs, the m6A demethylase FTO post-transcriptionally regulates BRD9 by stabilizing its mRNA through 3′UTR demethylation, thereby blocking YTHDF2-dependent degradation. This mechanism drives BRD9 expression upregulation and promotes malignant progression in HIF2αlow/− clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). Furthermore, Inoue et al. demonstrated that SF3B1 mutations induce aberrant exonization of a BRD9 intronic sequence, resulting in poison exon inclusion that disrupts the open reading frame and activates nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. Consequently, BRD9 transcripts exhibit accelerated degradation (reduced half-life) and fail to produce functional full-length BRD9 protein. Although these findings illuminate key regulatory nodes (e.g., transcriptional activation, miRNA interactions, RNA methylation, and splicing), how BRD9 expression is regulated—specifically in terms of promoter activity, tissue-specific regulators, and potential autoregulatory loops—remains poorly defined. Comprehensive mapping of BRD9’s interactome and cis-regulatory architecture will be essential to advance the therapeutic targeting of this epigenomic modulator. 

## Expression and prognosis of bromodomain-containing protein 9 in cancer
Aberrations in BRD9 expression levels represent a hallmark of BRD9’s involvement in cancer biology. To investigate BRD9 expression profiles, we utilized the TIMER2.0 (https://compbio.cn/timer2/) and UALCAN (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/index.html) online platforms. The analyses reveal that the mRNA ( B) and protein ( C) levels of BRD9 are significantly upregulated in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), while downregulated in UCEC. Interestingly, although BRD9 transcriptional levels are elevated in certain cancers, such as colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), LIHC, and HNSCC, protein expression in these cancers shows either no significant change or even downregulation ( B and 3C). This discrepancy suggests regulation by post-translational modifications or tissue-specific degradation, implying BRD9’s context-dependent role as either a tumor promoter or suppressor across cancers.
Further analysis shows a significant correlation between BRD9 expression and cancer progression across multiple malignancies. Using the UALCAN platform, we found that elevated BRD9 expression levels are strongly associated with advanced histological grades in diverse cancer types, including bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), as well as COAD, HNSCC, KIRC, LIHC, LUSC, LUAD, READ, and UCEC ( A). Moreover, Kaplan-Meier survival analyses using the GEPIA2 platform (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) underscore the prognostic relevance of BRD9. High BRD9 expression predicts shorter overall survival in patients with sarcoma (SARC), ACC, MESO, and HCC ( B). Interestingly, contrary to its oncogenic role in the aforementioned cancers, higher BRD9 expression levels are associated with improved overall survival in brain lower-grade gliomas, suggesting a context-dependent function of BRD9 ( B). These data suggest BRD9 as a prognostic biomarker or therapeutic target for patient risk stratification and treatment guidance. 

## Structure of bromodomain-containing protein 9 and its functions

## Structure
The conserved structural fold of BRDs comprises 110 amino acids arranged in a left-handed four-helix bundle consisting of αZ, αA, αB, and αC helices. The inter-helical αZ-αA and αB-αC loops form a hydrophobic pocket essential for recognizing the acetyl-lysine modification on histones ( A). In 2012, Filippakopoulos et al. identified 61 BRDs in 46 diverse proteins, categorized into eight subfamilies based on sequence similarity ( B). The members of these subfamilies, particularly II, comprising BET proteins, have gained much attention in BRD research because of their linkage to human cancer pathogenesis and significant druggability potential. Although the BET family has been identified as highly druggable, other subfamilies, particularly IV, also exhibit substantial drug target potential. 
BRD9, a member of the bromodomain family IV, comprises 597 amino acids. Structurally, BRD9 contains a bromodomain that selectively recognizes acetylated lysine (Kac) residues, a feature that is essential for its function as an epigenetic reader ( C). The bromodomain of BRD9 is responsible for mediating interactions with acetylated histones (e.g., H3K27) and other acetylated proteins (e.g., RAD54), thereby influencing chromatin dynamics and gene expression. , In addition to containing the characteristic bromodomain spanning amino acids 153–223, BRD9 features an uncharacterized domain DUF3512, which is crucial for the integrity of the SWI/SNF complex. Beyond recognizing Kac on histones H3 and H4, BRD9 bromodomain exhibits high affinity for butyrylated lysines (Kbu) and propionylated lysines (Kpr). Notably, BRD9 can identify the doubly acetylated histone H4K5acK8ac, the dipropionylated H4K5prK8pr, and accommodate the larger butyrylated lysine side chain at H4K5buK8bu. , Two crucial amino acid residues, Tyr106, termed the “gatekeeper,” and the core residue Met92, are essential for binding the Kbu histone sequence. Mutations in either residue abolish the ability of BRD9 bromodomain to bind this unique ligand. Recently, Yin et al. demonstrated that BRD9 selectively recognizes methylarginine (Rme) epigenetic marks via a bromodomain structural motif (Tyr173, Ile 176, and Tyr215), which is structurally adjacent to but different from its Kac binding pocket.

## Biological functions of bromodomain-containing protein 9
BRD9’s functional diversity stems from its interactions with various proteins, enabling the regulation of diverse signaling pathways. One of the most notable interactions is with the androgen receptor (AR) in prostate cancer, where BRD9 enhances AR-dependent gene expression, thereby promoting tumor progression. In addition to its interaction with AR, BRD9 also associates with other key regulatory proteins, such as CTCF, BRD4, and SS18-SSX, to influence chromatin structure and gene expression. , , Therefore, BRD9 plays a significant role in various biological processes, including stem cell pluripotency, cell cycle progression, DNA damage response (DDR), and ribosome biogenesis ( D).

## Gene transcription
BRD9 functions as a core epigenetic reader within the ncBAF chromatin remodeling complex, executing a bimodal regulatory mechanism: (1) Through stereospecific recognition of histone Kac marks, it recruits ATP-dependent chromatin remodelers (e.g., SMARCA4/BRG1) to establish nucleosome-depleted regions, thereby priming transcriptional accessibility; (2) Concurrently, BRD9 directly scaffolds transcriptional coactivators (e.g., MED1, BRD4, and MYC) at target gene promoters, synergistically enabling RNA polymerase II recruitment and transcriptional activation. This dual functionality of chromatin remodeling and direct transcriptional machinery assembly establishes BRD9 as a master coordinator governing the transcriptional activation of target genes. Studies have shown that BRD9 enhances the expression of oncogenes by occupying their promoter regions and modulating chromatin modifications, such as H3K27ac. , In addition to gene promoters, research also reported that BRD9 can bind to the specific enhancer of genes, remodeling the chromatin accessibility and driving gene transcription. , , Furthermore, BRD9 cooperates with the BET protein BRD4 and ISGF3 complex components (e.g., STAT1, STAT2, and IRF9) to orchestrate gene expression, highlighting BRD9 as a central regulator of the transcriptional landscape. Although the precise regulatory mechanisms remain unclear, BRD9 is implicated in the transcription of numerous genes involved in cell cycle progression, inflammatory and immune responses, cell adhesion, as well as DNA replication and repair in the context of various malignancies, including acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and multiple myeloma (MM). 

## Stem cell pluripotency
In naive murine embryonic stem cells (mESCs), BRD9 safeguards pluripotency by forming the ncBAF complex with GLTSCR1/GLTSCR1L, which binds to genomic sites containing CTCF, Sp5, and Sp1 motifs, stabilizing chromatin architecture and activating pluripotency transcriptional networks. This regulatory axis is evolutionarily conserved, as BRD9 ablation in human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) disrupts the pluripotency-differentiation equilibrium by impairing self-renewal and lineage specification. The defects in the differentiation of hESCs into the three germ layers can be rescued by supplementing TGF-β, NODAL, Activin A, and WNT3A. Mechanistically, BRD9 forms a complex with BRD4, P300, SMAD2/3, and β-catenin to regulate the expression of pluripotency genes and the activity of TGF-β/Nodal/Activin and Wnt signaling pathways, which is achieved by modulating the deposition of H3K27ac on associated genes. Paradoxically, while indispensable for somatic reprogramming, BRD9 inhibition enhances reprogramming efficiency via the suppression of MN1 and ZBTB38, transcriptional repressors that gatekeep pluripotency acquisition, undercovering its context-dependent functional duality.
In addition to its established functions in mESCs and hESCs, BRD9 has been demonstrated as indispensable for maintaining physiological hematopoiesis across multiple developmental stages. Genetic ablation of BRD9 recapitulates this phenotype in fetal systems, impairing hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) stemness and compromising erythroid maturation and B cell development. , Strikingly, paradoxical lineage-specific effects emerged: BRD9 depletion suppresses myeloid differentiation in fetal models yet enhances this process in adult counterparts. , Integrated multiomics map BRD9’s stage-specific chromatin occupancy and its role in orchestrating transcriptional programs, establishing the developmental-stage-dependent regulatory mechanism of the ncBAF complex in hematopoiesis.

## Cell cycle
Emerging studies have highlighted the critical role of BRD9 in governing cell cycle progression. , BRD9 depletion triggers cell-cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase, characterized by diminished S-phase progression, concomitant upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21, and suppression of cyclin E expression, thereby underscoring its essential role in promoting the G1/S transition checkpoint in AML. This phenotype is further supported by findings in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, where BRD9 inhibition impairs proliferation and disrupts lineage differentiation, potentially via the dysregulation of GATA1-mediated transcriptional networks that coordinate cell cycle exit and commitment. Mechanistically, BRD9 regulates cell cycle transitions by modulating chromatin accessibility at key regulatory loci. It recruits ncBAF components, GLTSCR1/GLTSCR1L, to acetylated histones at enhancers of CDK4/6 and E2F target genes, facilitating their transcriptional activation. , Additionally, BRD9 interacts with STAT5 and SMAD2/3 signaling hubs, linking extracellular signals to chromatin remodeling and cell cycle-related gene expression. In MM, BRD9 degradation with dBRD9-A disrupts MYC-driven ribosome biogenesis, indirectly impairing protein synthesis-dependent cell cycle progression and causing G1 cell-cycle arrest. These findings collectively manifest BRD9 as a multifaceted epigenetic regulator of cell cycle dynamics, with context-dependent roles in both normal and malignant cellular contexts.

## DNA damage response
Mounting evidence establishes BRD9 as an orchestrator of DDR by coordinating both chromatin-based regulatory programs and direct repair machinery engagement. In colorectal cancer (CRC), BRD9 interacts with GLTSCR1 to stabilize the ncBAF complex, which regulates chromatin accessibility at the promoter region of non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)-associated genes. GLTSCR1 deficiency disrupts ncBAF assembly, triggering BRD9 ubiquitination and its degradation. This aberrant chromatin remodeling enhances DNA accessibility at NHEJ repair loci, promoting genomic instability and CRC development by improving NHEJ efficiency. Similarly, BRD9-ncBAF maintains chromatin architecture by colocalizing with CTCF, preserving topologically associating domains critical for lineage specification. BRD9 loss amplifies the expression of myeloid-differentiation genes and downregulates DNA repair as well as replication-associated genes, linking ncBAF dysfunction to hematopoietic disorders and leukemia progression. Following DNA damage, BRD9 operates beyond chromatin remodeling to orchestrate homologous recombination (HR) repair in ovarian cancer by directly binding to acetylated lysine 515 (K515) on RAD54, thereby facilitating RAD51-RAD54 complex assembly. Depleting BRD9 sensitizes cancer cells to olaparib and cisplatin, elucidating BRD9 as a synthetic lethal target in HR-proficient cancers. 

## Ribosome biogenesis
Dysregulated ribosome biogenesis and hyperactivated translational programs critically drive oncogenic transformation and tumor progression. Over the past decade, causal links between dysregulated ribosome biogenesis and cancer risk have been solidified. This biological vulnerability has established ribosome biogenesis as a promising focus for therapeutic intervention in cancer. Notably, recent studies by Sima et al. and Kurata et al. reported that BRD9 governs ribosome biogenesis in human cancers. Pharmacologic or genetic ablation of BRD9 disrupts ribosome biogenesis and impairs translational function, ultimately leading to cell death. Mechanistically, BRD9 colocalizes with BRD4, ncBAF complex, and MYC at promoter regions of ribosome biogenesis genes. By sustaining the expression of key target genes involved in nucleolar function and ribosomal assembly, BRD9 serves as a critical regulator of cancer cell survival and growth. 

## Tumor-promoting role of bromodomain-containing protein 9 in human cancers
Recent research has systematically delineated how BRD9 governs the development of disease, with a primary focus on its oncogenic roles in human cancers. Accumulating evidence from both in vitro and in vivo models has conclusively established BRD9 as a pivotal epigenetic regulator orchestrating multifaceted oncogenic programs spanning tumorigenesis, progression, and therapeutic resistance. These include but are not limited to the dysregulation of proliferative signaling, metastatic dissemination, stemness maintenance, chemoresistance pathways, metabolic adaptation, and dynamic crosstalk in the tumor microenvironment. Herein, we present a comprehensive dissection of BRD9-driven oncogenic circuitry, providing an in-depth exploration of the molecular mechanisms underlying the oncogenic potentiation attributed to BRD9 ( ). 

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9-mediated proliferation and anti-apoptosis
Uncontrolled cell proliferation and resistance to cell death are hallmarks of cancer. BRD9 emerges as a multifaceted driver of tumorigenesis, activating proliferative signaling and apoptotic resistance through interconnected molecular mechanisms. Functioning as both a transcriptional regulator and chromatin remodeler, BRD9 recruits FOXP1 to activate CST1-dependent PI3K/AKT signaling, contributing to tumor outgrowth in gallbladder cancer. In synovial sarcoma, the SS18-SSX fusion protein sustains oncogenic transcription programs by recruiting the BRD9 bromodomain to acetylated chromatin. In addition, as a core ncBAF subunit, BRD9 synergizes with BET proteins to facilitate AR binding at prostate-specific loci, amplifying hormone-driven proliferation in prostate cancer. Apart from the canonical chromatin interactions, BRD9 engages in non-canonical signal transduction by recognizing symmetrically dimethylated arginine 391 (R391me2s) on AKT1 through an aromatic cage within its bromodomain. This cross-talk is enzymatically licensed by PRMT5-mediated R391me2s modification, which disrupts the autoinhibitory interaction between AKT1’s pleckstrin homology domain and kinase domain. , Subsequently, AKT1 directly phosphorylates EZH2 and impairs H3K27me3 deposition on genomic loci, thereby activating the transcription of downstream target genes that promote breast cancer cell survival and ultimately drive tumor progression. , 
Concurrently, BRD9 maintains apoptotic suppression by upregulating TUFT1 expression to block PUMA-mediated cell death in GISTs. Clinically, BRD9 overexpression correlates with advanced disease stage and reduced 5-year survival in patients with HCC and CRC, functional studies further demonstrate its essential role in synovial sarcoma survival and therapeutic response across prostate cancer, rhabdoid tumors, and AML, This mechanistic duality—activating proliferative cascades and silencing apoptotic pathways—positions BRD9 as a pivotal node for targeted intervention in chromatin-driven malignancies.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9-mediated metastasis
The migration and invasion of tumor cells are critical processes that enable metastasis, which accounts for over 90% of cancer mortality. The role of BRD9 in tumor metastasis is equally important, as it influences various cellular processes that facilitate the spread of cancer cells from the primary tumor to distant sites. BRD9 has been shown to promote cell migration and invasion in lung cancer with an unknown mechanism, which may interact with specific transcription factors to modulate the expression of proteins involved in the metastatic cascade. Furthermore, by influencing the expression of EMT markers, BRD9 promotes the transition of epithelial cells to a mesenchymal phenotype, which is characterized by increased motility and invasiveness. This transition is crucial for the detachment of cancer cells from the primary tumor and their subsequent invasion into surrounding tissues. Notably, BRD9 also activates metastasis-associated signaling networks in a cancer type-specific manner. In HCC, it recruits P300 to deposit H3K27ac marks at the TUFT1 promoter, amplifying TUFT1-AKT signaling. Whereas in HIF-2α-negative ccRCC, BRD9 transcriptionally upregulates Notch1 and Hes1 to activate Notch pathway-mediated metastasis. 
The above mechanistic insights establish BRD9 as a promising therapeutic target across malignancies. Suppression of BRD9 exerts potent anti-metastatic effects through distinct pathway disruptions: (1) ablation of BRD9 with miRNA decreases cell invasiveness, (2) targeted disruption of the BRD9-P300 interaction attenuates TUFT1-mediated metastatic dissemination in hepatocellular carcinoma, and (3) strategic blockade of the BRD9-Notch signaling axis impedes ccRCC progression by altering tumor-stroma crosstalk. BRD9’s role in diverse metastatic pathways identifies tissue-specific vulnerabilities, providing a basis for precision therapies that target its regulatory functions.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9-mediated cancer stemness and drug resistance
Cancer stem cells (CSCs) represent a small subset of the neoplastic subpopulation that drives tumor progression through their dual capacity for unlimited self-renewal and multilineage differentiation, serving as critical contributors to drug resistance. Emerging evidence indicates BRD9 as a key epigenetic regulator of CSC plasticity beyond its established roles in ESCs and HSCs. In pancreatic and breast cancer models, BRD9 sustains CSC self-renewal capacity and chemoresistance by epigenetically activating TGF-β/Activin/Nodal signaling cascades. Notably, pharmacological BRD9 inhibition suppresses tumorigenic potential and reverses gemcitabine resistance. Complementary mechanistic studies have demonstrated that BRD9 cooperates with SMAD2/3 to regulate 3D chromatin architecture, facilitating ncBAF complex-mediated enhancer-promoter looping and transcription of stemness genes in CSCs of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. In addition to solid tumors, the regulatory role of BRD9 in CSC maintenance extends to hematological malignancies. For instance, BRD9 depletion disrupts CTCF-mediated chromatin insulation at myeloid differentiation loci, inducing open chromatin configurations that drive terminal differentiation through epigenetic remodeling in AML. 
Beyond stemness regulation, BRD9 directly modulates drug resistance through diverse mechanisms. , It was found that BRD9 promotes resistance to EGFR inhibitors by binding to chromatin and transcriptionally upregulating key genes, including ALDH1A1, ROS1, and DIRC3. Pharmacological inhibition of BRD9 with GNE-375 effectively suppresses the emergence of drug-tolerant persister cells in EGFR-mutant PC9 models, demonstrating its potential to counteract adaptive therapeutic resistance. In ovarian cancer, BRD9 overexpression confers resistance to PARP inhibitors and cisplatin by enhancing HR efficiency. Conversely, BRD9 depletion induces synthetic lethality with these agents. Mechanistically, BRD9 interacts with acetylated K515 on RAD54, stabilizing the RAD54-RAD51 complex to facilitate DNA repair. 

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9-mediated metabolic reprogramming
Metabolic reprogramming allows tumor cells to adapt to the microenvironment and sustain rapid growth. BRD9 is engaged in this metabolic shift, influencing the glycolytic pathway and other metabolic processes that are essential for tumor progression. In colon adenocarcinoma, BRD9 acts as a master regulator of glycolysis by epigenetically coordinating H3K27ac modifications on the promoters of key glycolytic genes (e.g., ENO2 and ALDOC), leading to their increased expression and consequently enhanced glycolytic activity. This metabolic adaptation not only supports the energy demands of rapidly dividing cancer cells but also contributes to the production of biosynthetic precursors necessary for cell growth. Furthermore, Lv et al. demonstrated that BRD9 functions as a metabolic checkpoint reprogramming cellular metabolism to support tumor growth in prostate cancer. Mechanistically, BRD9 recruits the nuclear transcription factor-Y A-subunit (NFYA) to induce PYGL expression, which directs glucose utilization through the pentose phosphate pathway, generating NADPH and promoting the clearance of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Such epigenetic regulation ensures that cancer cells can maintain high levels of glycolytic activity, even in the presence of low oxygen conditions, a phenomenon known as the Warburg effect. 
Dysregulation of lipid metabolism is one of the most prominent metabolic alterations in cancer. SREBP1 is a crucial regulatory factor controlling the expression of rate-limiting enzymes of lipid synthesis. Through screening 200 types of epigenetic drugs for their ability to downregulate SREBP1 expression, BI-7273, a BRD9 inhibitor, was screened and found to decrease SREBP1 expression. BI-7273 was further validated to attenuate lipid accumulation in HepG2 cells through the AKT/mTOR/SREBP1 signaling axis. However, the mechanism by which BRD9 activates this signaling pathway to increase the expression of SREBP1 still requires further investigation. Moreover, Kurosawa et al. confirmed that BRD9 contributes to hepatic lipid synthesis by interacting with Pregnane X receptor (PXR), leading to the enhanced transcriptional activation of PXR. By modulating these pathways, BRD9 enables tumor cells to thrive under varying nutrient conditions, thereby enhancing their survival and proliferative capacity. , Targeting BRD9 to disrupt tumor metabolic adaptations presents a promising strategy, underscoring its key role in cancer metabolic reprogramming.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9-mediated microenvironmental remodeling
The tumor microenvironment (TME) consists of many different cellular and noncellular components that contribute to tumor growth, invasion, metastasis, and response to therapy. Currently, increasing studies reveal that BRD9 functions as a significant player in the TME, influencing both tumor progression and immune response.

## Involvement of bromodomain-containing protein 9 in angiogenesis
Angiogenesis, the process of new blood vessel formation, is a critical physiological response involved in tumor growth. Recent research has highlighted the importance of various proteins and complexes in modulating angiogenesis, with particular attention paid to BRD9. , BRD9 is intricately involved in the regulation of several key signaling pathways that are critical for angiogenesis, including the Notch and Hedgehog pathways. These pathways play essential roles in endothelial cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation, all of which are necessary for the formation of new blood vessels. For instance, BRD9 can activate the Notch signaling pathway, which is crucial for maintaining the balance between endothelial cell proliferation and differentiation. The Hedgehog signaling pathway, which is often suppressed in diabetic wounds, can also be positively regulated by BRD9 activity, facilitating the transition from an inflammatory state to one conducive to regeneration and angiogenesis. By regulating these pathways, BRD9 can significantly impact endothelial cell behavior and the overall angiogenic response. Therefore, therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing BRD9 activity or targeting its regulatory effects on these signaling pathways may provide novel approaches to improve angiogenesis in various pathological conditions, particularly in the context of impaired wound healing. Understanding the multifaceted roles of BRD9 in these signaling networks is essential for developing effective interventions that promote vascular regeneration.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9 participates in ECM remodeling
As a structurally dynamic meshwork critical for tissue homeostasis and pathological transformation, ECM reprogramming affects tumor formation, invasion, metastasis, and therapy resistance. BRD9 alteration drives biomechanical ECM dysregulation in diverse disease contexts. , , In uterine fibroids, BRD9 overexpression correlates with collagen deposition. Pharmacological inhibition of BRD9 reduces the accumulation of ECM components (e.g., COL13A1, COL16A1, and COL17A1), induces apoptosis, and arrests cell cycle progression by reprogramming the pathological epigenome and epitranscriptome of pro-fibrotic genes. In intervertebral disc degeneration, BRD9 exacerbates TNF-α-driven ECM degradation by activating the NOX1/ROS/NF-κB axis, which promotes matrix degradation and gasdermin D-mediated pyroptosis. Consistently, overexpression of BRD9 is correlated with poor prognosis in patients with melanoma. Pharmacological inhibition using the dual BRD9/7 inhibitor TP-472 suppresses tumor growth partially by downregulating the transcription of genes encoding various ECM proteins, including integrins, collagens, and fibronectins. However, the anti-tumor effect of TP-472 cannot be attributed exclusively to BRD9 inhibition, as it also suppresses BRD7 and lacks supporting genetic validation (e.g., BRD9 knockdown). Development and application of isoform-specific inhibitors or genetic tools are essential to define the unique functions of BRD9 versus BRD7. Collectively, these findings indicate that BRD9 serves as a vital ECM regulator in different cellular contexts, although its upstream regulators (e.g., hypoxia and cytokine signals) and tissue-specific effector networks remain incompletely characterized.

## Regulatory role of bromodomain-containing protein 9 on immunity
Recently, increasing studies indicate that BRD9 can function as a prognostic biomarker associated with immune infiltrate in ccRCC and HCC. The involvement of BRD9 in the immune landscape of tumors is multifaceted, influencing the activation of macrophages, the functionality of T cells, and the interactions with immune checkpoint molecules. Understanding these mechanisms provides insights into potential therapeutic strategies targeting BRD9 to enhance anti-tumor immunity.

## Macrophages
Macrophages are a critical cell type of the innate immune system that responds to microbial ligands by inducing a transcriptional program of proinflammatory and effector genes. Their biological roles have been well characterized in the context of cancer. BRD9 is an important regulator of macrophage activation by controlling these transcriptional events. , , Research demonstrates that BRD9 collaborates with BRD4, FOXP1, and the ISGF3 complex (composed of STAT1, STAT2, and IRF9) to orchestrate macrophage activation, upregulating inflammatory genes in response to microbial ligands (e.g., endotoxin lipid A) or interferon signaling pathways. , Intriguingly, BRD9 also antagonizes glucocorticoid receptor (GR)-mediated anti-inflammatory responses by competing with GR at shared genomic loci. Depleting BRD9 enhances GR occupancy at inflammatory genes, amplifying GR-dependent transcriptional repression. This functional duality suggests BRD9 as both a pro-inflammatory coactivator and a GR antagonist. These findings position BRD9 as a dynamic modulator of macrophage activation and its inhibitors as potential glucocorticoid adjuvants. Yet, whether BRD9 drives tumor progression via macrophages remains unclear, necessitating future single-cell multi-omics and in vivo CRISPR studies to unravel its role in the tumor microenvironment.

## T cells
Although Tregs are essential for maintaining immune tolerance, neoplastic systems paradoxically co-opt their immunosuppressive functions to establish tumor-protective microenvironments that evade cytotoxic T cell surveillance. Foxp3, a member of the Forkhead transcription factor family, is a critical regulator that orchestrates the molecular processes involved in Treg cell differentiation and function. To identify Foxp3 regulators in Treg cells, Loo et al. performed a lentiviral CRISPR sgRNA library and identified BRD9-containing ncBAF complex as a specific regulator of Foxp3 and a subset of its target genes. Ablation of BRD9 results in decreased Foxp3 expression and impaired Treg function, compromising Treg cell function in inflammatory bowel disease and tumor immunity in CRC. Therapeutically targeting BRD9 to modulate Foxp3 expression could inhibit Treg functionality, potentially restoring anti-tumor immunity.
Beyond modulating T cell activity through its effects on Treg function, studies have also shown that elevated BRD9 expression is significantly correlated with the increased infiltration of both CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells in HCC. Moreover, recurrent mutation of BRD9 is associated with dysregulated chromatin remodeling or transcriptional control, possibly synergizing with oncogenic signaling (e.g., MAPK, NF-κB, and NFAT) to promote T cell malignancy in Sézary syndrome. However, the precise mechanism of BRD9 in T cell transformation remains uncharacterized. The relationship between BRD9 and T cell responses is complex, as BRD9 not only supports Treg function but may also affect the balance between Tregs and effector T cells, thereby influencing overall immune responses against tumors.

## Immune checkpoint molecules
The interaction between BRD9 and immune checkpoint molecules is another critical aspect of its role in the tumor immune microenvironment. , Immune checkpoints, such as PD-1 and CTLA-4, are crucial for regulating T cell activity and maintaining immune tolerance. Research reveals positive correlations between BRD9 expression and eight immune checkpoint genes—including PD-L1, PD-L2, PD-1, CTLA4, LAG3, TIM-3, TIGIT, and SIGLEC15—in prostate adenocarcinoma, pheochromocytoma, and paraganglioma. Moreover, BRD9 has the potential to effectively forecast the prognosis of patients with melanoma undergoing anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. In addition, BRD9 was found to be upregulated in malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) compared to normal mesothelial cells. The BET inhibitor JQ1 reduced PD-L1 expression in MPM cells, as well as PD-L1 and LAG-3 expression in CD8+ T-lymphocytes. Whether BRD9 blockade directly interrupts the PD-L1/PD-1 axis, however, remains to be determined. Together, targeting BRD9 is possible to reduce the expression of immune checkpoints, thereby enhancing T cell responses against tumors and improving patient outcomes.
Collectively, BRD9 regulates the tumor immune microenvironment through macrophage activation, T cell function, and immune checkpoint interactions, making it a promising therapeutic target for enhancing anti-tumor immunity.

## Tumor-suppressive roles of bromodomain-containing protein 9 in human cancers
Although BRD9 upregulation has been shown to promote tumorigenesis via various pathways, , , recent evidence suggests a paradoxical role for BRD9 as a crucial tumor suppressor in multiple malignancies ( ). BRD9 governs important functions in regulating the transcription of tumor suppressor genes and preserving genomic integrity, highlighting its multifaceted contributions to cancer biology. 

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9 deficiency leads to tumorigenesis
Pan-cancer analyses indicate that BRD9 exhibits tissue-specific tumor-suppressive functions, which are proposed to operate through coordinated mechanisms involving both the preservation of accessible chromatin states at tumor suppressor loci and the counteraction of oncogenic transcription factor activity. It has been demonstrated that BRD9 functions as a critical tumor suppressor in Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC), which is caused by the integration of Merkel cell polyomavirus and persistent expression of Large T antigen and Small T antigen (ST). ST forms a complex with MYCL and EP400, leading to the activation of the histone demethylase LSD1, which represses key neuronal differentiation genes, such as ATOH1, and promotes tumorigenesis. In opposition to this oncogenic mechanism, BRD9 serves as a core component of the ncBAF complex to act as a central regulatory counterforce. Mechanistically, the pharmacological inhibition of LSD1 leads to the recruitment of BRD9 to the promoters of suppressed genes, including those in the bone morphogenetic protein signaling and neuronal differentiation pathways. This recruitment facilitates increased chromatin accessibility, leading to the reactivation of tumor-suppressive transcriptional programs. Loss of BRD9, as demonstrated by CRISPR-Cas9 screening, partially confers resistance to LSD1 inhibition, underscoring its essential role in mediating the anti-tumor effects. Thus, BRD9 antagonizes LSD1-driven transcriptional repression, restores differentiation signals, and suppresses MCC cell growth. These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting the LSD1-BRD9 axis in MCC and possibly other cancers.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9 depletion promotes tumor proliferation and metastasis
BRD9 is a potent suppressor of tumor progression, particularly in cancers harboring SF3B1 mutations, where it functions as a critical tumor suppressor. The tumor-suppressive role of BRD9 is intimately linked to its essential function as a core component of the ncBAF chromatin remodeling complex, which regulates gene expression programs critical for cellular homeostasis. The underlying mechanism involves SF3B1 mutations disrupting normal RNA splicing. Specifically, mutant SF3B1 misrecognizes a cryptic branchpoint within an intron of the BRD9 pre-mRNA, which leads to the aberrant inclusion of a “poison exon” into the mature BRD9 transcript. This aberrantly spliced mRNA is then targeted for degradation via the nonsense-mediated decay pathway, resulting in a significant loss of BRD9 protein expression. Consequently, the assembly and function of the ncBAF complex are compromised. Chromatin profiling reveals that BRD9 depletion causes the loss of the ncBAF complex at specific genomic loci, including those associated with the architectural protein CTCF. This augments the expression of genes involved in key cellular processes such as cell growth, adhesion, and migration, ultimately promoting tumor cell survival, proliferation, and metastasis. Experimental restoration of correct BRD9 splicing, using tools such as antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) to block the poison exon inclusion, successfully reverses these effects, inhibits tumor growth, and validates BRD9’s crucial tumor-suppressive activity. In addition, using ASOs to treat spinal muscular atrophy and other genetic diseases also succeeded, suggesting that ASO-based therapies may offer an effective strategy for targeting spliceosomal mutations in cancer. By restoring BRD9 expression and function, this therapeutic approach holds great potential for addressing cancers driven by SF3B1 mutations and other splicing-related abnormalities.

## Bromodomain-containing protein 9 inactivation contributes to genomic instability
DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair pathways function as critical safeguards against genomic instability, a hallmark of carcinogenesis primarily driven by germline mutations in DSB repair genes. Among these pathways, NHEJ and HR play central yet mechanistically distinct roles in preserving genome integrity. NHEJ, an error-prone repair mechanism, directly ligates DNA ends with minimal homology requirements, introducing insertions, deletions, or chromosomal rearrangements that paradoxically fuel oncogenic transformation. In contrast, HR ensures high-fidelity repair by utilizing sister chromatid templates to restore DNA lesions with nucleotide precision. Although HR deficiency predisposes cells to tumorigenesis, advanced malignancies frequently exploit hyperactivated HR pathways to evade genotoxic therapies, illustrating its dual role in cancer biology. Increasing studies identified BRD9 as a key regulator of both NHEJ and HR, underscoring its importance in preserving genomic stability and its potential function in cancer progression. , 
Recently, research has shown that BRD9 partners with GLTSCR1 to assemble the ncBAF, which restricts NHEJ-mediated genomic instability. GLTSCR1 deficiency triggers BRD9 degradation, aberrantly opening chromatin at NHEJ-associated gene promoters to drive error-prone repair and CRC progression. Thus, BRD9 safeguards genome stability, restricting cancer development by limiting mutagenic NHEJ. This contrasts with the oncogenic role of BRD9, where it often promotes pro-tumorigenic transcription programs. Analogously, BRD9 promotes HR by binding acetylated K515 on RAD54, facilitating RAD51 recruitment for error-free repair in ovarian cancer. However, BRD9 overexpression confers therapy resistance through hijacking the hyperactivation of the HR repair pathway. Pharmacological inhibition or genetic ablation of BRD9 induces HR deficiency, thereby restoring therapeutic sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents. When combined with PARP inhibitors, this therapeutic approach effectively sensitizes tumors to overcome chemoresistance, presenting BRD9 as an attractive candidate for precision oncology interventions. Together, cellular context, the genomic landscape, and specific complex partnerships are the key determinants that should be systematically evaluated to understand the conditions under which BRD9 functions as an oncogene or a tumor suppressor. Elucidating how these factors influence BRD9’s role will provide critical insight into its context-dependent behavior in tumor biology.

## Expanding the perspective: comparison with the homologous protein bromodomain-containing protein 9 and bromodomain-containing protein 7
While BRD9 exhibits context-dependent tumor-suppressive roles, its closest homolog, BRD7, is established as a canonical and potent tumor suppressor across diverse cancer types (e.g., nasopharyngeal carcinoma, breast cancer; and ovarian carcinoma ), where it is frequently downregulated or lost. Both BRD7 and BRD9 possess a conserved N-terminal bromodomain that recognizes acetylated lysine residues on histones, tethering their respective complexes to acetylated chromatin. , However, their incorporation into functionally distinct chromatin remodeling complexes underpins their major differences. In general, BRD9 functions as a component of the ncBAF complex, whereas BRD7 is a component of the PBAF complex. Mechanistically, BRD7 functions as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting key oncogenic pathways (e.g., β-catenin and PI3K/AKT , ), inducing apoptosis, and stabilizing the p53 tumor suppressor network. 
The comparison between BRD7 and BRD9 underscores a critical principle: homology does not equate to functional redundancy. The decisive factor for their divergent roles in tumorigenesis likely lies in their association with specific macromolecular complexes, PBAF versus ncBAF, which dictate their chromatin localization, interacting partners, and ultimate transcriptional output. In contrast to BRD7, which functions as a more stable sentinel against transformation, the tumor-suppressive function of BRD9 is tightly coupled to specific genetic backgrounds (e.g., SF3B1 mutations) or cellular states, and can be subverted into an oncogenic driver upon overexpression or in different cancer types. In the context of tumor suppression, BRD9’s anti-cancer mechanism specifically manifests through inhibiting the expression of oncogenic factors and maintaining accessible chromatin states at tumor suppressor gene loci. , Although the carcinogenic effects of BRD9 have been extensively studied across numerous cancer types, a deeper understanding of its tissue-specific tumor-suppressing function offers new avenues for developing more precise cancer treatment strategies.

## The potential of bromodomain-containing protein 9 as a therapeutic target

## Progress in the development of bromodomain-containing protein 9 inhibitors
Given its significant role in chromatin remodeling and gene expression regulation, BRD9 has emerged as a promising therapeutic target for malignancies. The development of BRD9 inhibitors has significantly progressed ( ). Initial efforts focused on discovering compounds that can disrupt the interaction between BRD9 and Kac on histones, which is critical for the recruitment of the ncBAF chromatin remodeling complex. As one of the earliest BRD9 inhibitors, LP99 established a critical foundation for subsequent investigations, leading to the development of enhanced derivatives (e.g., I-BRD9 and GSK6776). , , , In the past few years, a large number of proteolysis-targeting chimera (PROTAC)-based BRD9 degraders with improved potency and selectivity have emerged, such as dBRD9, VZ518, CFT8634, Compound 2 and 9. Concurrently, molecular glue degraders (MGDs) for therapeutic target proteins are arising as a strategy in drug discovery. Hughes et al. developed AMPTX1, a potent and selective BRD9-targeted glue degrader that recruits E3 ligase DCAF16, yet shows no cytotoxicity against AML cells in their study. Intriguingly, Byun et al. recently discovered that ZZ7, a BRD9-targeted MGD, exhibits significantly superior cell growth inhibition in synovial sarcoma cells. Together, these developments are crucial as they not only provide tools for investigating BRD9 biology but also hold therapeutic potential for patients with cancer. 
Preclinical studies indicate that BRD9 inhibitors (e.g., BI-7273) and PROTAC degraders (e.g., CW-3308) display potent antitumor activity in xenograft models of either AML , or synovial sarcoma. Furthermore, the discovery of natural BRD9 inhibitors, exemplified by polyphenolic derivatives with bromodomain-binding activity, has expanded the pharmacological landscape for epigenetic therapy development beyond synthetic compounds. As research continues, the focus will likely shift toward optimizing these inhibitors for clinical use, assessing their safety and efficacy in human trials, and exploring combination therapies that leverage BRD9 inhibition alongside other treatment modalities. However, considering that BRD9 displays 72% sequence similarity with BRD7 in the bromodomains, the development of BRD9-specific inhibitors is imperative. , 

## Combination therapy strategies involving bromodomain-containing protein 9
The therapeutic potential of BRD9 extends beyond monotherapy, as combination strategies have shown promise in enhancing treatment efficacy across various cancer types ( ). It has demonstrated that BRD9 inhibitors sensitize cancer cells to immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs) by downregulating critical survival pathways associated with MYC and Ikaros proteins in MM. Furthermore, the combination of the BRD9 inhibitor and MGD-28, a novel cereblon-dependent degrader for Ikaros proteins, exhibits pronounced synergetic efficacy in MM. These combination approaches not only improve the therapeutic index of IMiDs but also address the challenge of drug resistance that often complicates MM treatment. 
In addition to IMiDs, BRD9 inhibition has been found to enhance the efficacy of imatinib, a frontline therapy for GISTs. The mechanism involves the upregulation of PUMA-dependent apoptosis via TUFT1/AKT/GSK-3β/p65 axis, which is critical for promoting cell death in response to imatinib treatment. Similarly, targeting BRD9 with I-BRD9 sensitizes ovarian cancer to PARP inhibition and cisplatin. Furthermore, Weisberg et al. demonstrated that the pharmacological degradation of BRD9 mediated by either QA-68 or dBRD9-A exhibits synergistic effects with standard ALL therapies, such as the anthracyclines daunorubicin and doxorubicin. This combinatorial efficacy has been observed across multiple disease models, including ALL, MM, and various B-ALL cell lines, underscoring the therapeutic potential of BRD9 as a combinatorial strategy to enhance the efficacy of standard-of-care regimens in ALL and MM treatment.
Notably, the development of PROTACs that target BRD9 for degradation represents a novel approach to enhance the therapeutic window of existing treatments. These degraders can selectively eliminate BRD9, thereby disrupting its oncogenic functions and enhancing the sensitivity of cancer cells to overcome ligase-mediated resistance in clinical settings. Furthermore, combining BRD9 inhibitors with immunotherapies or other targeted agents opens new therapeutic avenues, particularly for cancers with BRD9 overexpression or dependency.

## Targeted bromodomain-containing protein 9 therapy in clinical trials
The clinical translation of BRD9-targeted therapies is progressing to evaluate their safety and efficacy. In a Phase I study of FHD-609 (NCT04965753), a selective BRD9 degrader, preliminary pharmacodynamic analyses confirmed dose-dependent BRD9 degradation and transcriptional modulation in tumor tissues at tolerated doses, supporting its therapeutic relevance in synovial sarcoma and SMARCB1-deficient malignancies. However, FHD-609 displayed limited efficacy in human patients and cardiotoxicity signals, including QTc prolongation and new-onset repolarization defects, causing the termination of the clinical trial in Phase I. These findings align with recent reports on CFT8634 (NCT05355753), an oral BRD9 bifunctional degrader, which exhibits comparable efficacy and safety concerns, notably QTc prolongation and T-wave inversion in clinical trials. Recently, the CFT8634 clinical trial has been terminated due to a lack of sufficient efficacy as a monotherapy. Inspiring, the novel BRD9 degrader ZZ7 exhibits potent anticancer effects while minimizing side effects in cardiomyocytes in vitro, underscoring the urgent need for further investigation in vivo and in clinical trials. 
Although these studies validate the feasibility of selectively targeting BRD9 via protein degradation, clinical responses remain modest. To address the unmet need in synovial sarcoma, future BRD9-directed strategies should prioritize combination therapies with existing anticancer agents or innovative delivery systems, such as antibody-degrader conjugates, to enhance tumor-specific targeting. Crucially, the observed cardiac risks necessitate rigorous safety monitoring in future trials. Further optimization of therapeutic windows, through either mechanistic refinement or synergistic combinations, will be critical to realizing the clinical promise of BRD9-targeted degradation.

## Conclusion
This review provides a comprehensive summary of current insights into the biological functions of BRD9, an essential subunit of the ncBAF complex and a chromatin reader that specifically binds Kac, Kbu, Kpr, and Rme modifications. The dysregulation of BRD9 expression and function has been linked to a wide range of human cancers, emphasizing its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target. Herein, we discuss the upstream regulatory factors influencing BRD9 expression and evaluate its prognostic significance in diverse malignancies. However, the precise mechanisms controlling its activity remain elusive, highlighting the need to delineate its full regulatory network for targeted therapy development.
Distinct from previous reviews, we provide a comprehensive analysis of BRD9’s paradoxical oncogenic and tumor-suppressive roles, emphasizing the context-dependent mechanisms that dictate its functional outcomes. Although recent studies have expanded our understanding of BRD9, the molecular basis of its activity, including its downstream targets and regulatory networks, remains largely uncharacterized. Beyond its known chromatin roles, further investigation is needed to identify BRD9’s interacting partners and non-histone substrates, which could reveal novel functional pathways. Given BRD9’s dual functionality, a critical challenge for therapeutic development lies in defining the specific cellular contexts in which its activation or inhibition yields the most beneficial effects.
To address these unanswered questions, we advocate the use of integrative experimental approaches, combining multi-omics profiling with advanced model systems such as patient-derived tumor organoids, patient-derived xenografts, and BRD9-specific conditional knockout mice. These platforms provide powerful tools to dissect the precise role of BRD9 in carcinogenesis and its broader biological implications. Moreover, the development and preclinical evaluation of BRD9-specific inhibitors, along with emerging PROTAC-based degraders and MGDs, represent promising strategies for therapeutic exploitation. Continued progress in these areas is expected to deepen our understanding of BRD9’s mechanistic roles and pave the way for its clinical application as a therapeutic target across multiple cancer types.
