## Abstract
Malignant tumors, commonly referred to as cancer, are pathological conditions distinguished by the unregulated growth and infiltration of malignant cells into adjacent tissues or remote organs. This uncontrolled cell proliferation results in continuous tumor cell division and proliferation in the body. The tumor invades surrounding tissues and spreads to other body parts through the bloodstream or lymphatic system. This forms distant metastases that can influence several systems and organs, seriously affecting the health and life of patients. Effective treatment methods are still lacking owing to their complex mechanism of action. With the continuous development of precision medicine, research on the correlation between extrachromosomal circular DNA (eccDNA) and malignant tumors has become a hot topic. The eccDNA is a circular DNA molecule independent of chromosomes, more stable, and less susceptible to nuclease degradation. Increasing evidence has shown that eccDNA has a function in malignant tumor heterogeneity, invasiveness, evolution, and chemical resistance. It drives tumor heterogeneity so that cancer cells can quickly adapt to treatment plans and environmental changes. Compared with linear chromosomal DNA, eccDNA has an open structure, carries active histone modifications, and can facilitate long-range gene interactions, significantly improving the transcriptional activity of genes and playing an essential regulatory role in disease progression. It also serves as a biological marker for diagnosing and predicting malignant tumors, thus attracting increasing attention. This article reviews its role, mechanism, and value in malignant tumors, providing a new perspective for diagnosing and treating this disease.

## Introduction
Malignant tumors, also known as cancer, are characterized by abnormal cell proliferation that is uncontrolled, invasive, and metastatic [ ]. Their hallmarks include rapid cell proliferation, abnormal tissue structure, marked cellular pleomorphism, increased nuclear division figures, and the capability to invade surrounding tissues and organs. They can spread to other body parts through the blood or lymphatic system, forming distant metastases [ ]. Epidemiological studies illustrate that malignancy is a main cause of death globally, with millions of deaths annually. In developed countries, the cancer occurrence and death rates of cancer are high, whereas in developing countries, the cancer incidence is gradually increasing owing to changes in economic conditions and lifestyle factors [ ]. Cancer incidence and mortality rates are related to various factors, including genetic predisposition, environmental exposures, dietary patterns, and infectious agents. Cancer development is correlated with various factors, including smoking, alcohol consumption, obesity, exposure to carcinogens, and infection with the human papillomavirus (HPV) [ , ]. As a genetic disorder, the etiology of cancer is diverse, including the dysfunction of tumor suppressor genes and overstimulation of oncogenes. The oncogene amplification of oncogenes is a prevalent molecular change in malignancy and has an important function in tumorigenesis. It offers selective growth advantages to cancer cells by overexpressing oncogenes and functional factors (e.g., enhancers) [ , ]. Therefore, identifying key clinical diagnostic and therapeutic cancer targets is particularly urgent.
It has been demonstrated that tumor-associated extrachromosomal circular DNA (eccDNA) has negative effects on human health and quickens malignant behavior [ ]. Cancer cells contain these circular molecules, which are categorized according to their size and number of copies. The initial category is referred to as eccDNA, specifically microDNA, with a typical size of less than 10 kb. By contrast, the subsequent category is known as extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA), exemplified by double minutes (DMs) [dm], which typically exceed 1 Mb in size. [ ]. There have been a number of investigations into the potential of eccDNA as biomarkers for cancer research and therapy, on the basis of their relationship to cancer biology. The detection of DM structures in childhood malignancies provides preliminary proof that ecDNA elements play a vital role in tumorigenesis [ ]. Moreover, whole-genome sequencing (WGS) results for several types of cancer indicate that ecDNA amplification increases the oncogenes copy number in various cancers [ ]. These observations suggested that ecDNA may accelerate cancer progression. Previous investigations illustrated that the ecDNA generation, particularly that of ecDNA-carrying oncogenes, is a powerful and strong gene amplification mechanism. Hung et al. provided evidence to support the notion that ecDNA harboring oncogenes within cancer cells exhibits a pronounced tendency to aggregate in clusters that are closely connected with regions of intense transcriptional activity. This intriguing phenomenon has been termed the ecDNA hub. [ ]. This localized ecDNA clustering promotes new enhancer–promoter interactions and oncogene expression. These enhancers strongly and vigorously promote intercellular heterogeneity among oncogenic drivers. While the chromosomal transcription hub relies solely on local cis-regulatory elements, chromatin conformation data, and clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) interference, the ecDNA hub has the potential to incorporate trans-regulatory elements sourced from various ecDNA molecules [ ]. These findings suggest that ecDNA can participate in the pathological process of cancer by regulating oncogene transcription and cancer cell heterogeneity.
Frontier exploration of ecDNA has indicated its critical role in tumorigenesis and progression [ ]. It has been established that ecDNA in tumors is huge enough to comprise one or numerous complete genes, especially oncogenes [ ]. The amplification of ecDNA-mediated oncogenes has been approved for many cancer types. Fortunately, the fast development of biotechnology has allowed the use of several techniques to identify and build ecDNA. These techniques offer the possibility of highlighting ecDNA structures and assuming their role in tumorigenesis [ ]. Various investigations have revealed the properties of ecDNA and its essential function in tumorigenesis in neuroblastoma (NB), glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), ovarian (OC), colon, and breast cancers [ ]. Studies have found that eliminating ecDNA can reduce amplified oncogenes on ecDNA, restoring the tumor phenotype [ ]. Furthermore, abundant evidence shows that eccDNA in tumor cells has strong mutability, significantly promoting the occurrence and development of tumors [ , ]. EccDNA released into the extracellular fluid is also considered as a biomarker for early diagnosis of cancer [ ]. The quantity, type, and mechanism of action of eccDNA in different cells and its potential biological functions remain unclear. This study illustrates a comprehensive summary of the many forms of eccDNA, elucidates the underlying mechanisms involved in their creation, and highlights their regulatory role in advancing research and application in cell physiological activities as well as pathological mechanisms.

## The research history and characteristics of eccDNA

## The origin of eccDNA
Genome-derived circular DNA, such as eccDNA, plays a crucial role in the carcinogenesis of normal cells and the phenotypic changes observed in cancer cells. In normal cells, eccDNA formation does not appear to be significantly influenced by antimetabolic anticancer drugs or ionizing radiation [ ]. However, in cancer cells, eccDNA can contribute to the rapid evolution and heterogeneity of tumors by amplifying oncogenes, facilitating tumor progression and resistance to treatment. This highlights eccDNA as a promising target for therapeutic intervention in specific cancers. These circular DNA molecules were first discovered in 1965 by observing wheat embryos and pig spermatozoa using electron microscopy [ ]. Similar circular DNA molecules were found outside the chromosomes in samples of NB cells in the same year but unlike the former, which appeared in pairs [ ]. In 1972, Charles observed numerous circular DNA molecules with a size of 0.2–2 μm in HeLa cells [ ]. In 1978, it was discovered that these small circular DNA molecules carried the dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) gene, mediating methotrexate (MTX) resistance in mouse cells resistant to the drug [ ]. Subsequently, researchers found that oncogenes (e.g., MYCN and EGFR) amplification was in the form of eccDNA in different samples [ ], and several carcinogens and DNA replication inhibitors could promote the eccDNA formation of eccDNA [ ]. Despite the identification of a close association between oncogenes and eccDNA, the lack of advanced technological capabilities has hindered comprehensive investigations into the mechanisms underlying their generation and their significance in the onset and progression of tumors. The structure and genetic features of eccDNA were gradually illustrated only after the advent of high-throughput sequencing, which was coupled with the further development, maturation, and improvement of chip microarray analysis and second-generation sequencing technology. Shibata et al. discovered smaller circular DNA molecules than those previously detected in mouse and human cells [ ]. Turner et al. assessed 17 malignant cell types using WGS, structural modeling, and cytogenetic analysis. They discovered that eccDNA presented in about 50% of malignant cells, with significant variations in eccDNAying among different cancer types [ ]. Moller et al. confirmed that these circular DNA molecules originated from chromosomes by sequencing muscle tissue cells extracted from 16 healthy men [ ]. Wu et al. used scanning transmission electron microscopy to observe circular eccDNA in tumor samples, and subsequent high-throughput sequencing revealed that this eccDNA had a loose structure, high openness, and high transcriptional activity [ ]. In the same year, Morton et al. discovered that the circular structure of eccDNA could aid enhancers in noncoding regions to regulate oncogenes expression in eccDNA, resulting in stronger transcriptional activity of eccDNA oncogenes than linear chromosome genes [ ]. Gradually, eccDNA has been a research hotspot owing to its intricate biological activities in numerous disease states (Fig.  ).
 

## The eccDNA classification
Currently, eccDNA research is not yet in-depth, there is no unified eccDNA naming, and many concepts are easily confused. EccDNA is typically classified according to its length [ ] (Table  ). EccDNA less than 1 kb includes four types: small polydisperse circular DNA (spcDNA), episomes, telomeric circles (T circles), and microDNA [ ] (Fig.  ). These types have small molecular weights, generally cannot carry complete genes, and are not observable under optical microscopes [ ]. Among them, spcDNA, T circles, episomes, and microDNA are commonly present in cells, with T circles mainly found in cells undergoing alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT). EccDNA greater than 1 kb includes DMs, chromosomes, and ecDNA [ ]. These types have larger molecular weights, carry one or more genes, and can directly be observed under optical microscopes. Among many types, only DMs appear in pairs, whereas other eccDNA types appear as irregular monomers under electron microscopy [ ], but their formation mechanisms remain unclear.
 

## The formation mechanism of eccDNA
Early studies have suggested that eccDNA formation is related to short repeat sequences and regulated by the detachment of short repeat sequences from chromosomes to form small DNA loops [ ]. However, further research has shown that eccDNA formation process was also related to chromosomal DNA replication, where partial fragments of DNA were erroneously dissociated. Double-strand breaks occur during the replication process, and these fragments are circularized into eccDNA through homologous recombination mechanism repair (HRR) and nonhomologous end-joining mechanism (NHEJ) repair mechanisms [ , ]. Meanwhile, the chromosomes were repaired through the HRR mechanism (Fig.  ). Moreover, the introduction of chromosomal replication inhibitors did not result in a significant reduction in eccDNA levels [ ]. Moreover, eccDNA can undergo replication independently of chromosomal replication [ ]. Consequently, there remains a contentious debate regarding the potential association between DNA replication and the production of eccDNA. Moreover, specific proteins are essential for eccDNA formation. For example, the lack of a DNA mismatch repair protein (MSH3) causes a decrease in microDNA formation [ ], and the use of drugs to inhibit key homologous recombination proteins (BRCA1) also results in a reduction in eccDNA levels [ ], indicating that the formation mechanisms of eccDNA are diverse.
 EccDNA less than 1 kb, including spcDNA and microDNA, does not depend on chromosomal replication. SpcDNA may also be generated during the transposon formation, which is associated with a specific DNA ligase involved in nonhomologous end-joining during double-stranded breaks. MicroDNA is a unique nonrepetitive sequence from mouse and human cell lines and is thought to be a nucleosome apoptosis product of nucleosome apoptosis [ ]. However, several studies have shown that microDNA did not appear in apoptotic cells. Therefore, its specific mechanism remains unclear [ ]. The T circles are believed to originate from directly detached telomeres or molecular recombination between telomeres [ ]. The episome mechanism remains unclear, but several research studies have suggested that episomes can gradually amplify and enlarge in the presence of protein inhibitors, leading to chromosomal sequence loss of chromosomal sequences until large DMs are formed [ ]. DMs and ecDNA formation mechanisms in eccDNA types larger greater than 1 kb are complex and involve processes, including the breakage–fusion–bridge cycle (BFB), translocation–deletion–amplification model, retrotransposition, episome amplification, and chromosomal proliferation (Table  , Fig.  ). Through the BFB mode in organism reconstruction, they start with abnormal BFB during mitosis, followed by chromosomal breaks, DNA damage, chromosomal missegregation, and micronuclei formation, ultimately leading to chromosomal proliferation and small fragment chromosomal detachment. The process of cyclic BFB results in the accumulation of numerous fragmented chromosomes that carry genetic material. These fragments then undergo gene amplification, leading to the formation of DMs [ ]. Episomes were further amplified to obtain DMs and ecDNA, precursors of DMs, and ecDNA precursors [ ].
 

## The eccDNA regulatory functions in cellular physiology
Eukaryotic cells contain a substantial quantity of eccDNA that exhibits notable levels of replication and transcription efficiency. In-depth research on eccDNA’s role in many higher animal cells’ physiological regulation is still limited and lacking. However, in many plants and viruses, eccDNA has been demonstrated to play critical regulatory roles, including acting as gene carriers, participating in dosage compensation, influencing cell aging, and regulating noncoding RNA expression. The eccDNA has plasmid-like functions and can increase the transcription efficiency of specific genes when eccDNA molecules are transferred into specific cells [ ], serving as gene carriers. According to recent reports, peripheral blood cells extracted from patients carrying the hepatitis C virus (HCV) contain homologous DNA sequences for HCV [ ]. Compared with the human chromosomal gene library, no homologous sequences are found in the existing current gene library, suggesting that these DNA sequences may be located in eccDNA. After invading the body, the encoded proteins Epstein–Barr nuclear antigen 1 (EBNA1), Kaposi sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV), and latency-associated nuclear antigen (LANA) from Epstein–Barr virus (EBV) and KSHV can circularize their original linear DNA, generating genome-like eccDNA molecules. These remain latent in B lymphocytes entering progeny cells with cell proliferation [ ]. The genome of HPV persists within basal epithelial cells in the form of eccDNA, undergoing replication in a manner similar to eccDNA replication.
The eccDNA can have a function in gene expression dosage compensation. In instances where genes are lost within an organism, a compensatory mechanism is observed wherein another gene undergoes amplification to ensure the overall gene count remains constant. This mechanism occurs in yeast strains lacking the hta1-htb1 gene, which amplifies hta2-htb2 through the eccDNA formation [ ]. Hta1-htb1 and hta2-htb2 are allelic gene pairs. In strains lacking hta1-htb1, the eccDNA number increased, including hta2-htb2 gene sequences such as hta2-htb2, to compensate for the decrease in hta1-htb1. Several researchers have revealed that eccDNA can provide better adaptive environmental capabilities for aging cells. Histone acetyltransferase Gcn5 attaches eccDNA to the nuclear pore complex during cell mitosis, causing exponential eccDNA increase during yeast cell proliferation [ ], ultimately forming a gene heterogeneity library that can quickly adapt to the environment without elimination. These studies have shown that the level of eccDNA accumulation may be related to the adaptive ability of biological cells after aging. EccDNA indirectly affects cell function expression through transcription factor formation, acting as a molecular sponge. MicroDNA, smaller than DMs and ecDNA, is more widely distributed. It cannot encode genes but transcribes noncoding RNA fragments [ ]. MicroDNA encodes microRNA (miRNA) and small interfering RNA (siRNA), two functional regulatory small RNAs that can be transcribed into mature sequences and regulate cell gene expression [ ]. Large molecular weight eccDNA has been shown to carry many mRNA-containing oncogenes.

## EccDNA and malignant tumors
While rare in healthy cells, eccDNA is abundant in malignant cells. The count of eccDNA copies varies among the types of malignancies, with the highest occurrence in GBM. EccDNA is an oncogene amplification carrier, and its role in promoting tumor initiation and progression has been extensively studied in vitro and in vivo. EccDNA-facilitated oncogene amplification has been identified as a tumor progression promoter.

## The eccDNA molecular mechanisms in malignant tumors
Many cellular processes rely on eccDNA, such as oncogene amplification, chromosomal rearrangements, gene fusions, epigenetic adjustments, histone modifications, gene compensation, nucleosome accessibility, long-range chromatin interactions, and signaling pathways regulated by eccDNA (Table  ).
 

## Oncogene amplification
The process of amplification in eccDNA has a notable influence on the expression of oncogenes. This amplification can lead to the reintegration of oncogenes into the homogeneously staining regions (HSR) of chromosomes, thereby influencing the accessibility of DNA and participating in the stabilization of oncogene expression. EccDNA is mainly formed by oncogene amplification, and there is a positive feedback regulatory loop between the high eccDNA gene expression of genes encoded by eccDNA and their accumulation. Studies have shown that the eccDNA gene suppression may decrease the eccDNA integration of eccDNA into cytoplasmic micronuclei [ ].

## Gene fusions
Gene fusions have been extensively found in the eccDNA of solid tumors, including medulloblastoma (MB), leukemia, multiple myeloma, and gastric cancer. The BCR–ABL1 fusion gene has been reported in ecDNA and the (9;22) (q34; q11) translocations, especially in chronic myeloid leukemia during imatinib treatment [ ].

## Epigenetic modifications and histone modifications
A wide range of biological activities are regulated by epigenetic changes, which also change the accessibility of eccDNA and chromatin. Chemical chromatin alterations, gene compensation, interactions between chromatin, and topological reorganization are all examples of these changes [ ]. Histone activation indicators (H3K4me1 and H3K27ac) are more abundant in eccDNA than repressive markers (H3K9me3 and H3K27me3). Low levels of repressive histone markers and high levels of activation histone markers were seen in the eccDNA of glioma cells throughout the mid-term investigation. In addition, research has demonstrated that eccDNA is crucial in histone gene compensation [ ].

## Remote chromatin interaction
Chromatin connections facilitated by eccDNA can be evaluated by the combination of circular chromosome conformation capture (4C) and high-throughput sequencing technologies. Previous investigations illustrated that eccDNA enhances active long-range chromatin interactions, and even super-long-range chromatin contacts have been detected [ ].

## Nucleosome accessibility
Topological investigations reveal that eccDNA is best packed as nucleosome and circular chromatin units. By contrast, their chromosomal DNA does not display a highly structured chromatin structure. The unique structure of eccDNA helps increase the accessibility of eccDNA genes to the transcription machinery [ ].

## EccDNA-mediated signaling pathways
Bystander effects and adaptive responses can be triggered by eccDNA signaling in the presence of oxidative stress. Radiation from ionizing sources can trigger cell death in nearby cells, as well as DNA damage, oxidative stress, eccDNA production, and other changes. Bystander cells change in nuclear shape appearance, promote actin polymerization, activate nucleolar organizer regions, and increase double-strand break levels, similar to the oxidative stress response in irradiated cells [ ].

## The impact of eccDNA on malignant tumor behavior and progression
Large DMs and eccDNA are almost absent in normal cells but play crucial roles in tumor initiation and progression [ ]. The types and quantities of eccDNA can contribute to increased genetic heterogeneity within tumors, which in turn can lead to issues such as gene integration and enhanced drug resistance, ultimately altering cell phenotypes. EccDNAs have been identified in a variety of tumors, including neuroblastoma (NB), colon cancer, gastric cancer, and ovarian cancer (OC), where they play a significant role in regulating tumor behavior. The following section summarizes the regulatory functions of eccDNA in these cancers [ ] (Fig.  ).
 

## The mechanism of high-efficiency transcription of eccDNA
The eccDNA harbors topologically associating domains (TADs), which aid in the transcriptional expression of genetic information contained within eccDNA. Oncogenes encoded by eccDNA have the highest expression levels in the tumor transcriptome [ ]. Enhancers in the eccDNA promoter region of eccDNA can significantly increase transcription levels. Oncogenes on eccDNA can interact with one or more enhancers, leading to aberrant oncogenes expression [ ]. In GBM, the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) encoded by eccDNA is upregulated, and multiple enhancers in the amplified noncoding region can promote EGFR transcription [ ]. Single enhancer interference by RNAi produces a compensatory enhancer effect, maintaining EGFR expression and promoting tumor cell proliferation [ ]. Similarly, DMs in OC cells have a sequence that specifically binds to the nuclear matrix, and adjacent genes, such as MYCN and EIF5A, have higher transcription levels, promoting tumor cell growth [ ]. In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), eccDNA is related to carcinogenesis, mitogen-stimulated protein kinase pathways, GTPase-correlated activities, and cytoskeleton functions [ ]. Furthermore, eccDNA enrichment is one of the characteristics of HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer, which highly expresses fusion transcripts from the HPV promoter and oncogenes. This process plays a driving role in carcinogenesis and immune evasion.

## The regulatory role of eccDNA in tumor cells
The dynamic exchange between eccDNA and chromosomes also increases genomic instability. Using WGS technology to compare NB and normal blood samples, it was discovered that most chromosome rearrangement sites coincided with the eccDNA sequence sites. EccDNA can enter the chromosome genome through hybrid and chromosome ring formation, blocking tumor suppressor gene expression and promoting oncogene expression [ ], suggesting that eccDNA genetic information may reenter the chromosome and reshape its genome. EccDNA can transcribe oncogenes, cell cycle regulatory genes (cdk4, pdgfrα, egfr, eif5a, her2, klf5, kras, and erbb3), and drug resistance-related genes (dhfr, erbb2, nras, mycn, and mdm2), activating different signaling pathways and promoting malignant cell proliferation and tumor development (Table  ). Some drugs can reduce and alter the quantity and size of eccDNA, inducing cell apoptosis. Previous studies have shown hydroxyurea can cause cad and dhfr gene loss on DMs, leading to DM formation after cell mitosis and degradation into smaller fragments [ ].
 The quantity of eccDNA from parent to offspring cells is unstable, indicating that oncogenes amplification in each tumor cell may vary. This leads to increased heterogeneity within tumors [ ]. Developing antitumor drugs that inhibit eccDNA formation and amplification could become a novel direction for reducing tumor proliferation [ ]. Investigations illustrated that eccDNA in tumor cells can enter the circulatory system and be an essential indicator for early tumor diagnosis [ , ]. Clinical cases have reported that eccDNA blood amount decreases after tumor resection, with a reduction in base-pair sequence length. This phenomenon is considered a sign of initial cure [ ]. Furthermore, several studies have used immune marker-containing eccDNA in peripheral blood from patients with coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) for diagnosis and to explore the pathogenesis of COVID-19 [ , , ]. EccDNA in maternal blood during fetal pregnancy can evaluate fetal health issues [ ]. These findings suggest that eccDNA has potential applications in tumor detection, treatment evaluation, and pathogenesis research.

## The regulatory mechanisms of eccDNA in tumor drug resistance
EccDNA mediated by tumor drug resistance mechanisms may be diverse [ ]. EccDNA carrying egfr is associated with tumor drug resistance, and multiple egfr mutations in tumors promote egfr VIII emergence [ ]. Researchers added an egfr tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) to tumor cells with egfr VIII mutation, and the tumor cells underwent apoptosis under the drug action. Nevertheless, following a cessation of the medication for a duration of 2 weeks, it was shown that tumor cells harboring the EGFR would resume their growth. This phenomenon may be credited to the potential capability of eccDNA comprising EGFR to evade targeted pharmacological interventions via certain mechanisms [ ]. Inhibiting eccDNA formation can also reduce the number of dhfr copies and restore MTX drug sensitivity [ ]. Drug resistance genes generally achieve tumor cell resistance mechanisms, reducing drug sensitivity. Earlier investigations illustrated that colon malignant HT29 cells develop drug resistance under high-dose MTX treatment, which may be related to the high dhfr expression carried by eccDNA [ ].
While targeted therapy has shown promise in treating patients with certain indications, it is susceptible to the onset of drug resistance, posing challenges in sustaining long-term effectiveness. EGFR gene amplification commonly occurs in the chromosomes and DMs of various tumor cells, such as non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), glioma, GBM, gastric adenocarcinoma, breast cancer, and others [ , ]. Nathanson et al. confirmed that GBM can develop resistance to erlotinib by eliminating the active mutant EGFRvIII on eccDNA. After stopping the drug for a while, EGFRvIII can be detected again on eccDNA, restoring the drug sensitivity of tumor cells [ ]. The discovery of this mechanism may help optimize targeted therapy dosing regimens. The development of drug-resistant clones is an essential mechanism of tumor drug resistance. Through WGS and genetic analysis of 17 different tumor types, eccDNA was detected in all tumors, but abundance varied significantly between them [ ]. Further studies have shown that oncogenes and drug-resistance gene amplification are more common in eccDNA than chromosomes. EccDNA amplification can more effectively elevate the copy number of oncogenes and tumor heterogeneity, inducing tumor drug resistance.

## The correlation between eccDNA and various malignant tumors
Numerous investigations illustrated that eccDNA has a significant function in numerous malignant tumors. For instance, microDNA can control gene expression, eccDNA can act as an auxiliary diagnosis and prognosis prediction biomarker, and eccDNA can carry oncogenes and promote their amplification (Fig.  ).
 

## NB
In 1983, Kohl et al. first positioned the N-MYC gene in NB ecDNA, the first study on ecDNA carrying oncogenes [ ]. A complete NB chromosome eccDNA circularization map was then generated, which verified the presence of the n-Myc gene amplification on NB ecDNA. In addition, the structure of n-Myc amplicons was analyzed via short reads and nanopore sequencing. Chromatin landscapes were analyzed using chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq), assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (ATAC-seq), and high-throughput chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C), which revealed two distinct types of amplicons [ , ].

## GBM
Evidence suggests that glioblastoma cells carrying ecDNA display enhanced invasiveness and radiation resistance and that EGFR is increased on ecDNA in GBM [ ]. By analyzing patient-derived models and clinical samples from patients with GBM who were subjected to EGFR TKIs, researchers discovered that glioblastoma cells rely on high-level ecDNA amplification for EGFR, c-Myc, and n-Myc, among other genes. The resistance to TKIs was enhanced when ecDNA encoding EGFRvIII was lost. In glioblastoma cells, EGFRvIII ecDNA was found again in high quantities after TKI treatment was stopped [ ]. Subsequent investigations illustrated that oncogenes, including EGFR, KIT proto-oncogene (KIT), platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA), and erbb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ERBB2), are positioned on ecDNA in GBM and are greatly amplified, promoting cancer [ ].

## Esophageal carcinoma
Researchers collected eccDNA extracted from three surgically corresponding ESCC tissue samples and studied differentially expressed eccDNA-related genes by high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatics analysis. A total of 184,557 eccDNAs were determined, primarily derived from 5′ and 3′ untranslated regions (UTRs), with few coming from exons, introns, LINE, or Alu repeat regions. The differential expression of 16,031 eccDNAs was found between ESCC and the corresponding neighboring epithelium, including 10,126 upregulated eccDNAs and 5905 downregulated eccDNAs. This confirmed ESCC-genome eccDNA existence, explored eccDNA differential expression, and illustrated eccDNA potential mechanisms in ESCC [ ].

## Colon cancer
On the basis of their observations in colon malignant HT29 cells, researchers have concluded that high-dose MTX treatment frequently causes a notable rise in the count of copies of the DHFR gene, which is dependent on ecDNA. As a result, HT29 cells acquire the ability to resist MTX. The second round of treatment outcomes in a significant reduction in cell resistance, and the loss of ecDNA carrying the DHFR gene is caused by the drug withdrawal. This finding provides more evidence that ecDNA can lead the second stage of colorectal cancer (CRC) treatment [ ]. DNA-PKcs of the key NHEJ protein can also promote DHFR ecDNA amplification. Inhibiting DNA-PKcs can remove DHFR ecDNA and improve MTX cell sensitivity [ ]. Additionally, researchers found two different ecDNA populations in the NCI-H716 cell line, demonstrating their heterogeneity in colon malignant cells and effectively constructing their molecular structures [ ].

## Gastric cancer
Utilizing the Circle-seq approach, scientists demonstrated that eccDNA abundance in gastric cancer tissue (GCT) is abnormally higher than in normal adjacent tissue (NAT). GCT comprised more eccDNA copies carrying oncogene fragments than NAT in individual patients. On the basis of FANTOM5 project data combined with The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database analysis of GC-specific eccDNA carrying enhancers (eccEnhancer), the presence of eccEnhancer was associated with poor GC prognosis. Through KEGG analysis, GC-specific eccDNA carrying pre-miRNA (eccmiR) was enriched in various cancer-related signaling pathways. The eccmiR genetic advantage also promotes host cell proliferation and invasive characteristics [ ].

## NSCLC
A study selected three surgically corresponding NSCLC tissues and NSCLC cell lines (H1299, A549, and H460) as subjects and validated upregulated candidate eccDNA encoding PLCG2 through high-throughput eccDNA sequencing and bioinformatic analysis. Plasmid transfection, RNA interference, real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT–PCR), and western blotting were employed for the verification of the PLCG2 level, and the outcomes illustrated that eccDNA from PLCG2 was upregulated and associated with a poor prognosis in NSCLC cells. PLCG2 was identified as an oncogene through eccDNA sequencing and may become a new NSCLC diagnosis and prognosis evaluation biomarker [ ].

## Hypopharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (HSCC)
Cisplatin (DDP) resistance is an essential element in reducing chemotherapy efficacy and causing local recurrence and lymph node metastasis in HSCC [ ]. Researchers used HSCC cell lines FaDu and the DDP-resistant cell line FaDu/DDP for eccDNA sequencing and whole transcriptome sequencing. The results showed that more than 10,000 eccDNAs were purified and sequenced from the two cell lines, with the eccDNA size distribution of 0.01–1000 kb. The combined analysis of eccDNA and transcriptome sequencing results showed that several overexpressed genes are transcribed entirely or partially from eccDNA-linked sequences rather than linear genomic DNA. Further screening and validation identified the encoded gene RAB3B, which can promote HSCC resistance to DDP by inducing autophagy [ ].

## Breast cancer
The oncogene HER2 (a part of the receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) and EGFR families) is amplified in about 20% of breast cancer cases [ ]. Studies have reported the presence of DM amplification in about 30% of HER2-positive tumors. These tumors can have a direct response to HER2 treatment [ ]. Even in cases of acquired resistance to anti-HER2 therapy because of HER2 expression loss, the count of HER2-carrying DMs remains constant in numerous resistance models. Therefore, clinical and preclinical research results illustrate that HER2-harboring DM loss resulting in decreased HER2 expression is unlikely an anti-HER2 therapy resistance mechanism [ ].

## Ovarian cancer
The researchers have discovered five novel matrix attachment regions (MARs) in a 682 kb DM in human OC cell line UACC-1598 through sequencing and bioinformatics analysis [ ]. These MARs interact with the nuclear matrix in vitro and are all related to oncogenes amplification (mainly n-Myc and eukaryotic translation initiation factor 5A2 (EIF5A2)) from eccDNA.

## Medulloblastoma (MB)
MB has an adverse prognosis, partly because of the absence of successful detection techniques [ ]. Using a Circle map, researchers were able to determine the genetic features of eccDNA in MB tissue and matched cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples, which they then compared with normal samples. Tumor and CSF samples from patients with MB tested positive for eccDNA. There were genes in MB that were associated with enriched control eccDNA. Gene density was inversely proportional to the genomic distribution of eccDNA. EccDNA in CSF had a similar distribution to matched MB tissue but differed in expression between tumors and normal tissue [ ]. Therefore, eccDNA-related central genes in CSF can serve as MB diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers.

## EccDNA and the treatment of malignant tumors
Therapeutic approaches reducing the level of eccDNA in cancer cells can downregulate the gene expression associated with the invasive phenotype and drug resistance in advanced cancer. Considerable in vitro and in vivo preclinical evidence suggests that drug treatment can lead to DM loss, thereby reducing DM-form oncogenes (MDR1, n-Myc, and c-Myc). These alterations can further improve treatment sensitivity [ ]. Recent investigations illustrated that eccDNA has potential applications in cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment. The eccDNA provides a new method for clinical applications in three main areas. First, eccDNA can be secreted into the peripheral blood system [ ] and may act as a potential prognostic biomarker for many malignancies, including ovarian [ ], cervical [ ], and NSCLC [ ]. For example, eccDNA was utilized in liquid biopsies for thyroid malignancy as a novel source of plasma genotyping [ ]. The second aspect is improved drug sensitivity after eliminating the eccDNA-related oncogenes. This exclusion offers novel adjuvant chemotherapy options [ , ]. The third aspect is the oncogene transfer and initiation of tumor formation by extracellular vesicles (EVs) with eccDNA. Thus, EV identification and targeting seem to have potential cancer treatment applications [ ].
Various treatment methods, including standard chemotherapy and targeted therapy, have been extensively employed in treating malignant tumors. However, resistance to treatment remains a global challenge, ultimately leading to pathological features, including tumor recurrence and a poor prognosis [ ]. Complex cellular and molecular processes involve resistance development mechanisms, including various factors and signaling pathways [ ]. However, thorough research is needed to explore the elements that detect cancer susceptibility to DHFR amplification. Clinical evidence suggests a close link between genomic rearrangement regions (such as focal amplification sites in the human cancer genome) and acquiring oncogene function mutations. Cancer treatment resistance may be caused by these mutations. As a cytogenetic marker of focal genome amplification, circular DNA elements are present in cancer cells. Depending on the circumstances, the amount of these elements with oncogenic mutations may increase. The most common types of neurological tumors that exhibit this feature are GBM and low-grade gliomas. RTKs frequently undergo mutations, often resulting in driver mutations. Stimulating mutations in EGFR and PDGF receptor alpha (PDGFRa), two members of RTK family, are primarily present in DM structures [ ]. These extrachromosomal mutations are a driving force for enhancing treatment resistance by increasing tumor heterogeneity [ ].
Moreover, gene editing technologies such as CRISPR–Cas9 provide a direct approach to targeting eccDNA by disrupting its formation or amplifying its degradation. This technology can be used to specifically target the DNA sequences responsible for eccDNA formation, potentially halting its role in oncogene amplification. By using CRISPR-based systems to edit or excise eccDNA from the genome, it is possible to reduce its oncogenic influence and, in turn, impair tumor progression. However, challenges remain in precisely targeting only the eccDNA without affecting the surrounding genomic regions, as well as ensuring the long-term stability and safety of such gene editing interventions. Small molecules targeting the stabilization of eccDNA or its interaction with regulatory elements could be employed to reduce the transcriptional activity of oncogenes. These molecules might work by disrupting the supercoiled structure of eccDNA, making it more prone to degradation, or by preventing its integration into the genome. By targeting the interactions between eccDNA and critical transcriptional enhancers or promoters, small molecules could prevent the overexpression of oncogenes that drive tumorigenesis. Such compounds, when used in combination with existing treatments, may offer a powerful therapeutic approach. Combining these strategies with conventional chemotherapy or immunotherapy may enhance treatment efficacy by preventing or overcoming resistance mechanisms associated with eccDNA. For example, tumors that harbor large amounts of eccDNA may exhibit resistance to conventional therapies owing to the amplification of genes involved in drug resistance. By specifically targeting eccDNA, these new strategies could bypass several of the limitations of current therapies and resensitize tumors to chemotherapy or immunotherapy. Moreover, the combination of gene editing or small molecule therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors or CAR-T cell therapies could offer a synergistic effect, amplifying the immune response against tumor cells. As the understanding of eccDNA biology improves, the development of these combined therapeutic strategies could represent a paradigm shift in the treatment of cancers with high eccDNA burden.

## Conclusions and future directions
The presence and significance of eccDNA are currently undeniable. EccDNA in human malignant cells is different from healthy chromosomal DNA. It offers novel visions into the disordered three-dimensional structure of the cancer genome and epigenome, as well as the invasiveness of specific tumor cells. This article reviews recent research progress on eccDNA and raises several questions and challenges. Thus, upcoming investigation on eccDNA may follow the subsequent directions. For instance, the specific processes of mRNA transcriptional regulation mediated by eccDNA and the potential role of additional epigenetic modifications in eccDNA biology are still unknown. Additionally, the biological occurrence and circulation mechanisms of eccDNA remain elusive. EccDNA and chromosomal DNA differ significantly in terms of their location, topological structure, and biology. Advances in high-throughput sequencing technologies have not only improved our ability to detect eccDNA but also enhanced our capacity to monitor tumor evolution in real-time. These technologies, including next-generation sequencing (NGS) and single-cell sequencing, allow for highly sensitive detection of eccDNA fragments, even at low concentrations, providing a more comprehensive view of tumor heterogeneity and genomic alterations. As a result, researchers and clinicians can now gain deeper insights into the dynamic changes occurring within tumors, such as genetic mutations, copy number variations, and the amplification of oncogenes carried by eccDNA. This real-time monitoring capability is crucial for understanding how tumors evolve in response to treatments, and can help identify early signs of resistance or relapse, potentially leading to more timely adjustments in therapeutic strategies. Further investigation into oncogene amplification by eccDNA and the function of eccDNA in cancer development necessitates the immediate development of novel research instruments, such as enhanced high-throughput sequencing analysis techniques [ ]. Earlier investigations have primarily concentrated on the function of oncogenes while neglecting the controlling factors that regulate their expression. Upcoming research should explore how to shut off the switches that activate these oncogenes. With its complex structure, high frequency in malignancies, and remarkable capacity to reintegrate into the genome, eccDNA acts as a vehicle for oncogene amplification. Perhaps in future therapeutic approaches it might be possible to harness the functionality of eccDNA as a vector [ ]. Furthermore, these eccDNA molecules may be essential in tumor progression, medication resistance, and poor prognosis; hence, they constitute a significant restriction to the creation of innovative cancer treatments. The mutation processes in tumors and the heterogeneity of important cancer genes must be better understood [ – ].
Clinical implications in cancer research, treatment, diagnosis, and prognosis may arise from the fact that the presence of abundant eccDNA expressing oncogenes is essential for fostering rapid tumor growth. Liquid biopsy, which detects eccDNA in circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), offers a noninvasive method for early cancer detection and monitoring therapeutic responses. By analyzing blood samples, liquid biopsy allows for the detection of eccDNA without the need for invasive tissue biopsies, which can be difficult and risky, especially in patients with inoperable tumors. The ability to track eccDNA in circulation also enables clinicians to assess tumor burden and detect minimal residual disease after treatment, thus offering a powerful tool for real-time monitoring of treatment progress and the detection of recurrence. This method has the potential to significantly improve early diagnosis and prognosis assessment in various types of cancer. Additionally, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and big data analysis into eccDNA detection could lead to personalized treatment strategies, enabling clinicians to rapidly assess treatment efficacy and adjust therapies accordingly. AI algorithms can analyze large-scale sequencing data, identify patterns in eccDNA amplification or mutation, and predict how tumors will respond to different treatments. Big data approaches can further integrate clinical, molecular, and imaging data, helping to build more accurate predictive models for individual patients. These models can guide clinicians in selecting the most appropriate therapies on the basis of the specific eccDNA profile of a patient’s tumor, potentially improving outcomes by tailoring treatments to the genetic and epigenetic characteristics of the cancer. As these technologies continue to evolve, they will likely play an increasingly important role in precision medicine, offering new avenues for personalized cancer therapy. To track the progression of cancer, a liquid biopsy can be performed using eccDNA released into the bloodstream by tumor tissues [ , , ]. Eradicating eccDNA from malignant cells can suppress tumor growth, reduce treatment resistance, and inhibit recurrence, offering a promising therapeutic approach. In late-stage OC, subcytotoxic doses of hydroxyurea decrease the count and dissemination of DMs, promoting the loss of drug-resistance genes carried by extrachromosomal amplifications and enhancing drug sensitivity. Similarly, gemcitabine effectively depletes eccDNA in OC cells, thereby inhibiting tumor proliferation [ ]. The therapeutic response of certain tumors was improved by fractionated radiation, according to Sanchez et al., since it sped up the loss of the multidrug-resistant gene MDR1, which is carried by ecDNA in tumor cells [ ]. In colon malignant cells, silencing BRCA1 suppresses homologous recombination, reduces extrachromosomal amplification in MTX-resistant cells, and enhances the effectiveness of tumor chemotherapy [ ]. TKIs help eradicate mutated eccDNA in glioblastoma and low-grade gliomas [ ].
eccDNA may become a novel target for targeted cancer therapy in the future. It can also be used in combination with traditional radiation, chemotherapy, or conventional anticancer medications to enhance the efficiency and synergistic effects of tumor treatment. The unique structure and role of eccDNA in tumor development make it a potential novel target to develop drugs [ , ]. Investigating the heterogeneity levels of eccDNA within tumors and between tumors can tailor personalized treatment plans for individual patients with cancer [ ]. The eccDNA structure comprises 20–50 enhancers and other regulatory components. To decrease oncogene function, researchers are seeking a novel category of therapeutic medicines that can suppress these regulatory components [ ]. Immunohistochemistry on tumor tissue paraffin sections, as well as fluid and blood specimen examination and more stringent experimental studies, will allow us to predict the differential expression of eccDNA and its correlation with cancer prognosis in the future [ ]. An intriguing possibility is that, owing to its lack of chromosomal protection, eccDNA may activate the innate immune pathway as an endogenous antigen. During mitosis, it is secreted into the cytoplasm, stimulating the cyclic GMP–AMP synthase pathway and the innate immunity [ ]. Additionally, investigations illustrated that eccDNA strongly stimulates innate immunity via the STING signaling pathway [ ]. Additionally, eccDNA may have a function in antitumor vaccines. Therefore, as research on eccDNA continues, it is believed that it will soon be incorporated into human WGS. Further analysis of its potential functions and mechanisms will have a substantial function in cellular physiological regulation, tumor diagnosis, and treatment. It will open up new perspectives in human tumor biology and indicate new directions for the development of tumor diagnosis and treatment. Following radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, new therapies targeting eccDNA may bring revolutionary changes to cancer treatment.

## Conclusions
Investigations illustrated that eccDNA is abundant in higher animal cells, but research on its physiological regulatory functions and role in the cell is still limited. However, recent investigations illustrated that eccDNA has a crucial regulatory function in the occurrence and development of malignant tumors. With the development of high-throughput sequencing and advanced microscopy imaging technologies, it has been proven that eccDNA can carry many oncogenes with efficient transcriptional capabilities, promoting tumor growth and progression. EccDNA detected in peripheral blood has also become an early detection marker for tumor diseases. EccDNA is an emerging oncology research hot topic, and eccDNA-mediated oncogene amplification is more common than previously thought. However, many questions remain unanswered owing to the absence of updated and comprehensive techniques to investigate the specific function of eccDNA in controlling physiological and pathological processes. For example, how do tumor cells dynamically regulate eccDNA numbers to achieve optimal survival conditions? It is still unknown whether eccDNA also carries tumor suppressor genes, which may be related to the mechanism of tumor resistance to body recognition and elimination. EccDNA floats freely between cells, allowing it to become a cancer treatment target and opening new immune response mechanisms and research directions for forming body antigens. As the eccDNA study continues, it is believed to be included in human WGS shortly. Further analysis of its potential functions and mechanisms will have a vital function in cell physiological regulation, tumor diagnosis, and treatment. It will open new horizons in human tumor biology, pointing to new tumor diagnosis and treatment development directions.
