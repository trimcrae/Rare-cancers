## Background
Extracellular vesicles (EVs), which are secreted by all types of body cells, are small vesicles with phospholipid bilayers [ ]. EVs, including micro vesicles and exosomes, are biological nanocarriers that transport proteins, DNA, RNA, lipids, and other biological molecules. These types of EVS are different in size, morphology, biologic content, surface markers, and origin [ ]. As shown in Fig.  , in the endosomal–lysosomal system, early endosomes (EE) develop into late endosomes (LEs) which are intracellular organelles called multivesicular bodies (MVBs) [ ]. During MVB maturation, intraluminal vesicles (ILVs) are formed inside MVBs by inward invagination of the EE membrane. In other words, MVBs contain ILVs, and ILVs contain lipids, proteins, DNA, RNA, etc. MVBs can have three destinations: (1) they can fuse with lysosomes (leading to degradation of ILVs content); (2) they can participate in signal generation; and (3) they can fuse with the plasma membrane to release ILVs in the form of exosomes [ ]. It is worth mentioning that the two membranes should contain soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor adhesion protein receptors, which are vital for fusion occurrence [ , ]. Furthermore, Ras-associated GTP-binding protein 27a (Rab27a) and Rab27b control the release of exosomes [ ].

 

For the first time, exosomeswere discovered in the sheep reticulocytes in 1970, but a new horizon was opened up when G.Raposo found that B lymphocytes can secrete exosomes in 1996 [ , ]. Between 1996 and 2001, other cell types that could secrete exosomes, such as dendritic cells and intestinal epithelial cells, were identified [ , ]. In 2000, scientists understood that exosomes released during reticulocyte maturation, bind to fibronectin via integrin [ ]. Exosome-based immune therapy was introduced in 2001 [ ]. The role of exosomes in apoptosis, along with immune modulation and cell-to-cell interaction, was understood in 2002 and 2004, respectively [ , ]. In 2005, exosomes were introduced as nanovesicular vaccines according to their ability to carry major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules, costimulatory molecules, heat shock proteins, and native tumor antigen [ ]. Numerous studies have revealed that immune cells such as B cells and natural killer cells (NKs) can secrete exosomes that have similar characteristics to the cells of origin [ ]. Even macrophages release exosomes carrying pathogen-related antigens after exposure to a specific antigen [ ]. Nowadays, exosomes are obtained from a variety of sources, like different cell types, stem cells, cancer cell lines, biofluids, etc. They have broad applications in cargo delivery, regenerative medicine, cosmetics, dermatology, cancer therapy, and personalized medicine. This review, focuses on different kinds of biofluids that can be a source for exosome isolation. Moreover, the characteristics, isolation methods, and applications of these exosomes will be clarified based on recent reports. Afterwards, the clinical trials involving exosomes derived from biofluids will be described, whether they are ongoing or completed. Finally, the challenges and limitations that researchers face in isolating and using exosomes will be explained.

## Properties and functions of exosomes

## Properties
The exosomes are biological nanovesicles ranging between 30 and 200 nm in size [ ]. These endogenous nanoplatforms contain a variety of biological molecules such as heat shock proteins (Hsp 60, 70, and 90), messenger RNA (mRNA), microRNA (miRNA) (in greater abundance than mRNA), long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), cytoskeletal proteins (i.e., actin, tubulin, and cofilin), enzymes (ATPase), cytoplasmic proteins, amino acids, mitochondrial DNA, and single-stranded DNA [ , ]. Exosomes contain cholesterol, sphingolipids, phosphoglycerides, ceramides, short and long saturated fatty acid chains, and saccharides (full of mannose and glycan) [ ]. Furthermore, they have many surface protein markers, including CD90, CD73, and CD105 (MSC surface antigens), GTPases, annexins, flotillin, Rab2 and Rab7 (membrane transport and fusion proteins), CD9, CD63, CD81, and CD82 (tetraspanins), lipid related proteins and phospholipases, alix, tumor susceptibility gene 101 (proteins involved in MVB synthesis), platelet-derived growth factor receptor, lactadherin, and the integrin [ ]. Annexins, flotillin, GTPases, Rabs, and ADP ribosylation factors are bound to the membrane lipid of exosomes and thus participate in transport and fusion functions. Tetraspanins and the integrin family are involved in fusion, cell migration, and adhesion. MHC-II functions to promote T-cell specific responses [ ]. Some of these markers, such as heat shock proteins (Hsps), tetraspanins, membrane transport and fusion proteins, alix, integrin, etc., which take part in the process of vesicle formation and secretion, are common in all exosome types, and others are specific based on the extraction source [ ]. For instance, placenta-specific enzyme placental-type alkaline phosphatase is a specific surface protein of placenta exosomes [ ]. While B cell marker CD20 is a specific surface marker of B cell exosomes [ ]. Even the contents of exosomes (proteins, miRNAs) also reflect their origin, and for this reason, examining these two components is a way to diagnose some diseases.

## Functions
Exosomes exhibit diverse immunomodulatory functions based on their cellular origin. They regulate immune responses. For example, B-cell-derived exosomes B-cell-derived exosomes bearing MHCII activate CD4⁺ T-cells [ ], while dendritic cell (DC)-derived exosomes with MHC I promote CD8⁺ T-cell responses, with enhanced efficiency from mature versus immature DCs [ ]. Pathogen- or tumor-associated antigens carried by exosomes (e.g., from infected macrophages/endothelial cells or tumor cells) can trigger immune responses against infections and cancers [ ], likely via spreading MHC/antigen or by direct interaction with T cells [ ]. Due to the points mentioned, exosomes can be used for vaccine development against viral diseases and even cancer. For instance, Kuate et al. explored the use of exosome-based vaccines against SARS-CoV. They generated exosomes containing a chimeric SARS-CoV spike protein (SGTM) by transfecting 293T cells. In a rat model, the exosomal vaccine required two doses to induce similar antibody levels as a single dose of an adenoviral vector vaccine. However, priming with the exosomal vaccine followed by an adenoviral booster produced neutralizing antibody titers exceeding those seen in SARS patient serum. This combined vaccine approach shows promise for developing effective SARS-CoV immunizations [ ]. A clinical trial (NCT04491240) demonstrated safety of inhaled MSC-derived exosomes in COVID-19 patients. Moreover, engineered exosomes functionalized with CpG DNA activate DCs, showing promise for cancer immunotherapy. A combination of tumor associated antigens, antibodies, and exosomes (as adjuvants) is a possible approach for cancer vaccine development. In this regard in a research, murine melanoma B16BL6 cells were transfected with a plasmid harboring gene of the fusion streptavidin-lactadherin (ST-LA) protein. The generated exosomes which expressed ST-LA, were incubated with biotinylated CpG DNA (an immune stimulatory DNA). As a result, engineered exosomes are bound to CpG DNA due to the high affinity of ST for biotin. This platform, which could efficiently deliver to the DCs of mice and activate them, was introduced as a potential tool for cancer immune therapy [ ]. Conversely, exosomes can suppress immunity by activating T-reg or inhibiting CD8⁺/CD4⁺ /NK cell proliferation and treat inflammatory/autoimmune diseases [ ].
Exosomes also promote angiogenesis through vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression [ , ], and miRNA-121 expression [ , ]. Saliva exosomes can promote angiogenesis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells through SMAD6/BMP2 pathway activation [ ]. In fact, the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2O (UB), which is one of the main mRNAs of salivary exosomes, decreases levels of SMAD6 through its degradation. SMAD6 degradation causes bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2) activation, and the protein induces angiogenesis [ – ]. Furthermore, exosomes facilitate wound healing by stimulating cell migration, proliferation, collagen production, and re-epithelialization via HGF/IGF1/NGF/SDF1-dependent activation of Akt/Erk/Stat3 pathways [ , ]. Additional roles include intercellular communication, tissue regeneration, homeostasis maintenance, and regulatory mRNA delivery [ ].

## Exosome sources
Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) possess self-renewal and multi-lineage differentiation capabilities (into adipogenic, osteogenic, chondrogenic tissues), express surface markers (CD73, CD90, CD105), and can be derived from various tissues (adipose, bone marrow (BM), dental pulp, umbilical cord, Wharton’s jelly (WJ)) [ ]. However, their properties such as gene expression, secretome, differentiation, proliferation, therapeutic effect vary based on source tissue and culture conditions [ – ]. While offering therapeutic benefits primarily via paracrine secretion of bioactive factors including growth factors, chemokines, cytokines, interleukins, interferon following intravenous or local administration [ ] MSC therapy faces substantial challenges. These include tumor genesis risk due to the similar growth factors and regulators involved in both cancer cells and stem cells [ ], low engraftment [ ], poor viability/proliferation post-transplant [ ], foreign body response [ ], risk of thrombogenic events with intravascular delivery [ ], undesirable immune responses [ , ],, pathogen transmission risk [ , ], and unwanted differentiation [ ]. Critically, research indicates the therapeutic effects of MSCs are mediated predominantly by their paracrine secretion, not direct cell replacement [ ], with extracellular vesicles (EVs), especially exosomes, being key effectors. MSC-derived exosomes demonstrate similar therapeutic efficacy to the parent cells themselves [ , ], suggesting exosome therapy could replace cell-based approaches. Beyond stem cells, exosomes are also sourced from diverse bio fluids (amniotic fluid, saliva, urine, bile, WJ, breast milk, seminal plasma, plasma, platelet-rich plasma, ear fluid, synovial fluid, and malignant ascites). Like MSC-exosomes, their biological content is origin-dependent and characterized by NTA, FC, WB, and TEM [ ].

## The benefits of bio fluid-exosomes over cell-derived exosomes
Bio fluid-derived exosomes have several advantages compared to cell-derived exosomes. Potential disease biomarkers are frequently present in low concentrations and can be obscured by higher amounts of proteins in body fluids. Furthermore, exosomes are encapsulated in a lipid bilayer and exhibit high stability under storage conditions. Bio fluid-derived exosomes can transmit information that can be utilized for determining the existence or progress of disease along with optimum treatment strategies. The isolation of the exosomes from bio fluids effectively eliminates most high-abundance proteins, thus substantially simplifying the sample complexity. Moreover, the non-invasive nature of the sampling of the most bio fluids facilitates easier exosome collection. These characteristics render them an optimal choice for biomarker analysis [ , ]. In case of cancer treatment, liquid biopsy is less expensive than tissue biopsy. Liquid biopsy also allows for detection of substances shed from several tumor sites. Body fluid can be collected serially for screening and evaluating therapeutic response. Therefore, bio fluid-derived exosomes are better choices for cancer diagnosis than tissue-derived exosomes. Consequently, exosome-based liquid biopsy has undergone clinical trials, received approval, and entered the market. ExoDx™ Lung (ALK) is the initial exosome-based liquid biopsy that identifies EML4-ALK mutations in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients, with 88% sensitivity and 100% specificity. The ExoDx Prostate IntelliScore (EPI) is an FDA-approved device that assesses the risk of prostate cancer. EPI surpasses the standard of care and aids in the early diagnosis of prostate cancer. MedOncAlyzer 170 is an innovative liquid biopsy technology capable of detecting both exosomal RNA and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) in a single assay. MedOncAlyzer 170 is able to detect mutation at all stages of various cancer types [ ].

## Exosome isolation methods
Some techniques have been developed to separate exosomes based on differences in size, surface markers, density, and molecular weight [ ]. Although, methods based on ultracentrifugation (UC) are the gold standard of exosome isolation [ ], other methods such as size exclusion chromatography (SEC), density gradient centrifugation (DUC), immune affinity capture (IA), ultrafiltration (UF), and poly ethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation have been used for obtaining exosomes from diverse sources.

## Ultracentrifugation (UC)
Ultracentrifugation (UC) (Fig.  ) isolates exosomes through sequential centrifugation steps based on density and size [ ]. An initial low-speed centrifugation removes cell debris and apoptotic bodies, followed by increased speed to separate larger vesicles, culminating in exosome precipitation at 100,000–200,000×g [ ]. There are four factors that affect the method: acceleration (g), lower rotor (k) factor, centrifuge time, and viscosity of the sample. The acceleration (g) determines the separation efficiency. The lower rotor (k) factor, indicates the relative pelleting efficiency of a rotor at maximum speed and leads to shorter centrifuge time. The centrifuge time is determined by the viscosity of the sample and the g value. Longer centrifuge time can result in higher quantities of obtained exosomes, but it may also lead to the co-precipitation of non-exosomal proteins, reducing the overall purity. Therefore, centrifuge time should be optimized to balance the yield of exosomes with the purity of the sample [ ]. Advantages of UC include its chemical-free nature, applicability to large sample volumes, reproducibility, and relatively high yield. However, limitations encompass high equipment costs, scalability challenges, and potential exosome damage from extreme centrifugal forces [ ].

 

## Density gradient centrifugation (DUC)
UC (Fig.  ) is a technique used to enhance the yield and purity of exosomes. The most commonly used gradient mediums for this method are sucrose and iodixanol. The use of an ultracentrifuge and density gradient aids in the formation of exosome pellets. The drawbacks of the DUC method were previously addressed in the UC method [ ]. In the DUC method, samples are mixed with gradient medium (sucrose or iodoxinol), and the mixture is centrifuged with high forces for 16 h. As a result, exosomes are enriched in the 1.1–1.18 g/mL density region. The method boasts high purity, thanks to its efficient extraction of exosomes. This effectively avoids any protein contaminants. In fact, research has shown that exosomes obtained through DUC have a higher level of purity compared to those obtained through differential centrifugation or one-step precipitation kits [ ]. There are several challenges associated with obtaining exosomes through the DUC method. These include a time-consuming preparation procedure, being labor intensive, a low yield of the final product, and necessity to remove the gradient material [ ].

 

## Size exclusion chromatography (SEC)
Size Exclusion Chromatography (SEC) (Fig.  ) separates bio fluid components based on size using columns packed with porous polymers (e.g., Sephadex, Sepharose, Biogel P, Sephacryl). Larger particles pass the column faster due to limited pore access, resulting in exosomes eluting after larger contaminants [ ]. Separation efficiency depends on hydrodynamic radius, particle shape, and molecular weight [ ]. Subsequent fractions elute non-exosomal proteins, yielding purified exosomes with reduced contamination [ ]. SEC specifically separates plasma proteins from exosomes [ ], offering rapid processing (~ 20 min/sample) [ ] and scalability advantages. However, SEC typically yields low exosome recovery, necessitating large sample volumes [ ], and cannot resolve exosomes from similarly sized micro vesicles. Though coupling with immunocapture methods mitigates this limitation [ ]. Critically, SEC preserves exosome integrity better than UC avoiding shear-force-induced morphological damage or aggregation [ ].

 

## Poly ethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation and immune affinity capture (IC)
In the PEG precipitation method (Fig.  ), a polymer is utilized to modify the solubility of exosomes, resulting in the formation of exosome aggregates. The subsequent step involves the precipitation of these aggregates through a low-speed centrifugation process [ ]. The method has a high yield, but it lacks a purity. This is because of the co-precipitation of soluble non-exosomal proteins, immunoglobulins, viral particles, and immune complexes [ , ]. To overcome this low purity, IC has been combined with PEG precipitation. The basis of IC is separating exosomes using their surface markers, such as tetraspanins. Samples should be incubated with magnetic beads coated with antibodies [ ]. These antibodies are able to bind to specific surface markers on exosomes. By using the IC method in conjunction with DUC, a more pure end product can be achieved [ ]. One disadvantage of the IC method is that antibodies are very expensive. Instead, using, for example a CD9 antibody can isolate the exosomes with the marker and so exclude CD9− exosomes. So the exosome yield will be low in the IC method [ , ].

 

## Immune affinity capture (IA)
The IA method is based the separation of exosomes through the expression of surface marker proteins using antibodies (Fig.  ). This can be achieved by incubating the sample with the magnetic beads or gold-loaded ferric oxide nanocubes, which are coated with antibodies against the surface proteins [ , ]. IA is commonly used in combination with other methods such as UF in order to improve the purity. IA provides exosomes with high purity. However, there are few challenges associated with IA method [ ]. The IA selectively isolates a subset of marker-specific exosomes, which may not represent the entire exosomal population. This method yields fewer exosomes and the integrity of exosomes can be compromised if antibodies are not easily removed post-isolation. Furthermore, the IA technique is expensive, necessitating substantial quantities of antibody-conjugated beads, which restricts its application mainly to small sample sizes.

 

## Ultrafiltration (UF)
UF is based on the use of membranes with particular pore diameters to isolate particles of a size range (Fig.  ). One or more filters, with a molecular weight cut-off of 10 kDa or 100 kDa, are commonly used to isolate particles larger than 30 nm [ ]. UF is a cost-effective, rapid, and labor-intensive method [ , ]. However, there are some challenges such as contamination with non-exosomal free-floating humoral peptides, poor RNA quality, low yield and purity of isolated exosomes [ ]. ## Emerging technologies in exosome research and extraction from body fluids
Obtaining and studying pure exosomes in clinical applications is difficult due to several limitations of conventional procedures, such as low yield, low purity, lengthy processing times, being expensive, and challenges in standardization. Thus, researchers have created various microfluidic devices to isolate, identify, and analyze exosomes from body fluids. Microfluidics is the technology of systems that operate small amounts of fluids within micro scale channels, thereby decreasing the analysis time while increasing its efficiency. There are two types of exosome isolation by microfluidic devices: physical property-based and immunoaffinity-based [ ]. The isolation approaches utilized in microfluidic platforms for exosome analysis include immune affinity, membrane-based filtration, nanowire trapping, acoustics, lateral displacement, and viscoelastic flow [ ] (Fig.  ).

 

Each biofluid possesses unique content and physical features, necessitating the assessment of various extraction methods to determine the most effective procedures for each particular case. For instance, in case of serum or plasma exosomes, before isolating exosomes, it is important to consider a few pre-analytical factors that can affect the yield and content of exosomes, as well as interfere with downstream uses. These factors include the time between blood collection and exosomes isolation, the kind of blood collection tubes used, the transport conditions, the temperature and storage periods, the centrifugation process, the fasting status of the individual, and any physical exercise undertaken [ – ]. Moreover, lipoproteins exist in great quantities in blood and are significant impurities in exosomes obtained from this fluid. Distinguishing lipoproteins from exosomes is challenging due to their similar sizes. Existing literature indicates that collecting blood in a fasting state is preferable. However, it is important to interpret findings with caution. Furthermore, ongoing attempts are being undertaken to enhance the purity of exosome isolation and to separate lipoproteins from exosomes. One of these efficient methods is acoustofuidic-based separation or agarose gel electrophoresis. Due to their negative charge, exosomes, HDL, VLDL, and LDL particles have the ability to be displaced when an electric field occurs. Therefore, lipoproteins and exosomes can be distinguished by their size and zeta potential using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis with tris–acetate-EDTA. In addition, lipoproteins are stained blue using Sudan Black B, a method used to identify triglycerides. During electrophoresis, the initial blue leading band consists of HDL, while the final leading band comprises a combination of LDL and VLDL. The exosomes are positioned between these two bands [ – ].
When the exosomes are isolated from saliva, immediate standardization of sample collection is necessary due to the reported dependence of saliva collection and composition on multiple factors. These variables include the location in the oral cavity, the type of salivary gland (parotid, submandibular, sublingual, or minor salivary glands), oral cavity diseases, smoking, drinking, and food intake. The timing of saliva collection for isolating exosomes can introduce variation due to potential changes in saliva content according to the circadian cycle. Additional research is required to investigate the effects of different methods of collecting saliva, both with and without stimulation, on the isolation of exosomes. Furthermore, it is necessary to examine the influence of blood contamination resulting from tooth brushing or oral cavity injuries. When isolating exosomes from saliva, it is important to consider the presence of cells and solid contaminants, which can be removed through low-speed centrifugation or filtration. The viscosity of the collected material may require dilution in PBS. Additionally, the large quantity of immunoglobulin and amylase may cover up the presence of smaller quantities of proteins. Two methods commonly employed to eliminate amylase include affinity adsorption and the use of affinity chromatography columns in conjunction with filter systems [ – ].
A significant obstacle in isolating exosomes from urine is the elimination of the Tamm-Horsfall glycoprotein (THP) or uromodulin, which tends to be the most prevalent protein found in urine. This protein has the ability to capture or attach to exosome, causing the exosomes to co-precipitate during low-speed centrifugation. Such centrifugation is typically performed to separate cell debris and other impurities. Additional techniques were employed to extract THP, including the use of different chaotropic reagents to induce denaturation, precipitation with NaCl to separate the salt, the incorporation of ZnSO4 to stimulate THP oligomerization and facilitate sedimentation, the utilize of a salting-out CHAPS lysis buffer, sucrose gradient separation, or the sole utilization of dithiothreitol (DTT) to disrupt the cysteine-cysteine interactions [ – ]. In summary, biofluids can be used for exosome isolation, but specific considerations must be applied to achieve pure exosomes with high yield.

## Exosome characterization
Exosome characterization is crucial to discover their functions in several biological processes and diseases pathogenesis. Each method offers distinct advantages and limitations, highlighting the complexity of exosome analysis and the need for optimized protocols to ensure accurate and reproducible results.

 

## Biofluid exosomes for diagnosis and treatment of diseases

## Clinical applications of amniotic fluid mesenchymal stem cell exosomes (AFMSC-Exo)
Amniotic fluid (AF) in the uterine, surrounds the fetus and helps freely motion of it in the uterine. It also protects the fetus from physical collision [ ]. The amniotic sac, next to the umbilical cord, is one of the safest sources for harvesting human stem cells [ ]. AF can be obtained by some methods such as amniocentesis [ ], cesarean [ ] or third trimester amnio-reduction [ ]. Amniotic fluid-derived stem cells (AFSCs) are non-tumorigenic, require minimally invasive collection, and raise no ethical concerns [ ]. These stem cells express human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-ABC marker which is related to immune-modulation in the body leading to less immunogenicity [ ]. Amniotic fluid mesenchymal stem cells (AFMSCs ) can differentiate to different kinds of cells such as oocyte like cells, hepatocytes, osteocytes, cardio myocyte-like cells, chondrocytes, neural cells, adipocytes and smooth muscle, making them an appropriate candidate for related diseases treatment [ , ].

## AFMSC-Exo for diseases treatment
Research has been shown that exosomes can lessen cardiomyocyte apoptosis and cardiac fibrosis [ ] and enhance angiogenesis and cardiac regeneration [ ]. So exosome therapy is an effective key for cardiac fibrosis [ ], and cardiac fibrosis has a critical role in almost all types of heart disease [ , ]. S Bollini and co-workers investigated human AFMSCs (hAFMSCs) cardio protective effect in Wistar rats a model of acute myocardial infarction [ ]. In this research, systemic injection of hAFMSCs and their conditioned medium, resulted in infarct size reduction. They noted that this reduction may be due to thymosin beta4 (an actin binding monomer) secretion which is a cardio protective and proangiogenic agent [ ]. According to the efficiency of hAFMSCs in cardio protection, Jiajia Hu et al. studied the potency of hAFMSCs-derived exosomes on cardiac fibrosis improvement [ ]. They received AF samples from pregnant women and isolated the exosomes by sequential centrifugation method. Results showed that injection of these exosomes to cardiac fibrosis rats, decreased collagen I level and α smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) protein level (marker of myofibroblast activation). Activated myofibroblasts secrete extracellular matrix (ECM) which causes fibrosis. The exosome injection could decrease ECM deposition. Conversely, exosomes injection caused vessel regeneration by increasing the expression levels of HIF-1α and VEGF.
The reason for the creation of fibrotic scars in wounds is actually the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts through activation of transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ) signaling pathway [ , ]. Another important point is that, cell proliferation and cell migration are essential in the wound closure process [ ]. hAFMSCs not only stimulate fibroblast migration and proliferation, but they can also inhibit myofibroblast formation thus preventing scar fibrosis [ , ]. Zhang and co-workers obtained exosomes from hAFMSCs (hAFMSCs-Exo) and investigated their function in wound healing [ ]. According to their research, hAFMSCs-Exo contain high amounts miR-22-3p, miR-27a-3p, miR-21-5p and miR-23a-3p which inhibit TGFβ pathway. Furthermore, α-SMA expression was lower in rats treated with hAFMSCs-Exo compared to the control group. Vessels and neuro regeneration were improved by exosome therapy. Hence, they concluded that hAFMSCs-Exo is an efficient wound healing agent that does not cause scar formation (Fig.  ).

 

The other possible application of hAFMSCs exosomes is osteoarthritis (OA) treatment [ ]. MSCs-derived exosomes contain ECM enzymes that regulate ECM balance. This makes MSCs-exo a potential agent for cartilage replacement [ ]. It has been verified that M1 macrophages in the synovial tissues of OA prevent the differentiation of MSCs to chondrocytes but M2 macrophages stimulate this differentiation [ ]. TGFβ, which is an immunosuppressive agent [ ], can increase the chondrogenesis and collagen II production [ ]. Zavatti1et al. compared efficiency of AFSCs and AFMSCs derived exosomes (with hAFMSCs-Exo) on rats with knee OA (KOA) and understood that treatment with hAFMSCs-Exo could renovate cartilage quicker than treatment with hAFMSCs, due to the participation of M2 in exosome therapy [ ]. The pain tolerance threshold of both treated groups was similar and higher than untreated OA rats group. H&E staining of negative control (healthy rats), positive control (OA rats with no treatment) and treated samples, showed that OARSI grade [ ] was 5 in positive control and it reached to 2.5 with cell therapy. Exosome therapy decreased OARSI grade to 2, making it a prior method for OA management.
hAFMSCs-Exo potential for stroke treatment was evaluated by Castelli et al. in an in vitro stroke model [ ]. They concluded that the neuroprotective effect of these exosomes is due to their miRNA cargos. For example, miR-154 activates the Wnt signaling pathway that is essential in neuronal survival and synaptic structure maintenance [ , ]. miR-27a controls neuro inflammation [ ], and miR-22-3p [ ], miR23a [ ] and miR27a [ ] are involved in neuroprotection by anti-apoptotic effects. The outcome gotten from this study emphatically pointed toward the neuroprotective impacts of the hAFMSCs-Exo within the in vitro stroke model by survival path way activation through miRNAs.
Non-obstructive azoospermia (NOAZ), the most problematic and complex type of infertility in men, arises from genetic/epigenetic abnormalities or hormonal disorders causing defective spermatogenesis [ ]. As OCT3/4, a transcription factor critical for early spermatogenesis, is reduced in azoospermia [ ]. In a recent study, researchers investigated that intratesticular injection of hAFMSCs-derived exosomes (10–40 µg) in rat models increased OCT3/4-positive cells and significantly improved sperm concentration, motility, and morphology.
Separately, AFSCs protect against chemotherapy-induced premature ovarian failure (POF) [ , ]. Ovarian granulosa cells (GCs) which are vital for oocyte survival and follicle development, are the most frequent cell type encountered by AFSCs after injection [ ]. AF exosomes were shown to deliver miR-146a and miR-10a, inhibiting apoptosis in damaged GCs and preventing ovarian atresia in chemotherapy-treated mice [ ]. Knockdown of these miRNAs reversed exosomal anti-apoptotic effects in vitro, while miR-10a delivery via liposomes similarly suppressed apoptosis. Collectively, these findings indicate that hAFMSCs-derived exosomes hold therapeutic potential for NOAZ and ovarian protection. As exosomes are an EV subgroup and amniotic fluid EVs have been applied in congenital diaphragmatic hernia [ ] and skeletal muscle defects [ ] hAFMSCs-exosomes represent a promising strategy for these conditions.

## AFMSC-Exo for diseases diagnosis
Exosomes obtained from hAFMSCs can not only be used for diseases treatment, but also for diagnosis. For example, in the amniotic fluid exosomes collected from pregnant women with prenatal diagnosis of congenital hydronephrosis the amount of has-miR-300 and has-miR-299-5p are reduced. Therefore, these miRNAs can be used as a good marker for the diagnosis of kidney fibrosis in conjunction with congenital hydronephrosis [ ]. In some cases, miRNAs are not the only tool used for diagnosis, the number of exosomes per ml of AF can be another tool for diagnosis. For instance, in the pregnant women with twin-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS) the number of exosomes in 1 ml of AF has been reported to be decreased significantly with a mean concentration of 5.3*109 and 26.8*109 particles/ml in the case and control groups, respectively. Additionally, exosomes extracted from TTTS AF had a small amount of RNAs (0.1–1.1 ng per 109 particles). Consequently, AF exosomes have a main role in replicating the condition of pregnancy and the fetus [ ]. The expression of 1252 mRNAs, 256 miRNAs and 1090 lnRNAs RNAs in exosomes of AF of pregnant females with a healthy fetus (control) is different with those in women with a ventricular septal defect (VSD) fetus (case). In a study, the researchers established a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network [ ] including 46 mRNAs, 11 miRNAs and 47 lncRNAs. Subsequently hub RNAs in the ceRNA network were verified by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Hub ceRNA network contained 5 hub mRNAs (ROCK1, PTP4A1, FMR1 SUCO and MYT1L), 3 miRNAs (miR-129-5p, miR-497-5p and miR-144-3p), and 4 lncRNAs. The 4 lncRNAs could regulate the expression of 5 mRNAs by binding to 3 miRNAs. The interactions of the hub RNAs could help them to investigate the role of the ceRNA network in the pathogenesis of VSD. The 3 miRNAs inhibit downstream transcription factors of the Wnt/ß-catenin pathway and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway [ , ] and miR-144-3p regulates the canonical Notch signaling pathway [ ]. Furthermore, elevated ROCK1, regulating cardiomyocyte apoptosis, and cardiac-development genes (FMR1, MYT1L) in VSD exosomes confirm their diagnostic potential [ , ].

## Clinical applications of Wharton’s jelly mesenchymal stem cell exosomes (WJMSCS-Exo)

## WJMSCS-Exo for diseases treatment
WJ, the mucoid connective tissue surrounding umbilical vessels [ ], contains collagen types I, III, and V alongside glycosaminoglycans including such as keratan sulfate, heparan sulfate, chondroitin-4-sulfate, chondroitin-6-sulfate, dermatan sulfate, heparin and high molecular weight hyaluronic acid [ ]. Hyaluronic acid, the most abundant glycosaminoglycan in WJ, can protect fibroblasts and collagen fibrils from pressure and preserve tissue geometry by creating a gel layer around cells [ ]. Besides containing hyaluronic acid, WJ has a high amount of MSCs.
It has been established that the culture medium of WJ MSCs can improve skin wounds both in vitro and in vivo (in mice) [ ]. Jeschke and co-workers conducted a comprehensive study on the wound healing potential of exosomes derived from WJ [ ]. Based on their study, WJ contains high quantities of isoform 2 of filamin protein (a protein which is enriched in exosomes) [ ]. Besides, exosomes surround fibroblasts and adhere to the cell surface within 30 min, after which most of these intact exosomes internalize the fibroblast cells. In the next part of the experiment, fibroblasts were treated with α2-MC, exosomes (rich of α2-MC), or DMEM (control) to investigate cell migration, cell proliferation, and cell viability; the results of this part of the study are shown in Table  . Therefore, WJ-derived exosomes can help wound repair due to their high amounts of α2-MC, which promotes cell migration, proliferation, and viability. In vivo studies in rat wound models demonstrate that topical application of WJ-derived exosomes substantially accelerates wound closure and collagen deposition relative to control treatments.

 

In another research, paclitaxel was loaded into WJ-derived exosomes (PAC-exo) by electroporation. Then, HeLa cells were treated with exosomes (control) or PAC-exo. The apoptosis percentage was higher in the PAC-exo group compared to the control, due to the enhanced expression of pro-apoptotic genes (caspase 3, caspase 9, and BCL-2-associated X protein) and decreased expression of anti-apoptotic genes (Snail, Slug). Therefore, this group of researchers introduced WJ-derived exosomes as an efficient drug delivery system for cervical cancer treatment [ ].
Due to the neuroprotective ability of WJ-derived exosomes, they are a good candidate for topical treatment of neurodegenerative disorders. For example, in an in vivo study, WJ-derived exosomes were used to treat glaucoma. It was concluded that exosome therapy resulted in retinal ganglion cell survival in the retinal ganglion cell layer and glia cell activation in the retina and optic nerve, compared to the control group [ ].
Zhdanova et al. examined effect of the exosomes obtained from human WJ on spatial memory of olfactory bulbectomized mice (a model of the sporadic form of Alzheimer’s disease) [ ]. After intranasal administration of the labeled exosomes, they were accumulated in the hippocampus and neocortex, structures that play an important role in learning and memory processes and are affected by Alzheimer’s disease. Intranasal administration is a way to bypass the blood-brain barrier (BBB) [ ]. The loss of olfactory bulbs causes spatial memory loss, and WJ exosomes prevented spatial memory loss. The positive effect of exosomes on memory was assumed to be due to the higher expression of neprilysin, which degrades β-amyloid aggregates [ ]. β-amyloid is a neurotoxin that causes the loss of synapses and the death of neurons, which subsequently results in memory loss [ ]. Therefore, these researchers developed a non-invasive method to deliver exosomes to the brain and prevent memory loss in a mouse model of Alzheimer’s disease.
In a comprehensive study, Gupta and coworkers, developed an injectable form of WJ derived exosomes and tested its content [ ]. The results are demonstrated in Table  . Moreover, they compared the content value of WJ with other biologics, and this comparison was based on their own studies and those of other researchers. For instance, the amount of growth factors was higher in WJ than in umbilical cord arteries [ ]. The expression level of genes involved in wound healing and immune response is higher in WJ than in BM tissue [ ]. The content of hyaluronic acid is higher in WJ than that in amniotic fluid [ ]. Besides, platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and BM are devoid of hyaluronic acid. Based on these points, WJ is an appropriate choice for use in the field of regenerative medicine.

 

More recently, Bavarsad et al. have used WJ-derived exosomes for the treatment of liver fibrosis [ ]. In the liver, fibrosis inflammation causes an excessive deposition of the ECM, which in turn induces cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma [ , ]. Pre-treatment of umbilical cord MSCs (UC-MSCs) with TGFβ1 inhibits the activation of the TGFβ1/Smad pathway. In this pathway, TGFβ1 regulates the expression of ECM proteins, such as collagen1, fibronectin, and α-SMA by phosphorylating Smad. The final consequence of TGFβ1/Smad pathway activation is the promotion of liver fibro genesis [ ]. In the mentioned research, it was revealed that the expression level of ECM proteins decreased after the treatment of activated LX-2 cells with exosomes and decreased even more after pretreatment of WJ-MSCs with TGFβ1.

## Clinical applications of platelet rich plasma exosomes (PRP-Exo)
PRP is a small portion of blood plasma with a high platelet concentration of 1.2 × 1012 platelets/mL [ ]. PRP is rich in molecular mediators such as growth factors (platelet-derived growth factor, vascular endothelial growth factor, TGFβ, insulin-like growth factor 1) and anti-inflammatory cytokines but poor in blood cells [ – ]. It is deeply rooted in the fact that the granular part of PRP is the main source of these molecular mediators [ ], indicating that exosomes encapsulate growth factors and cytokines. So, PRP-Exo, which are full of platelet derived exosomes, can be used in tissue regeneration due to their critical role in the healing effect of PRP. PRP-Exo have been shown to enhance the proliferation and migration of MSCs [ ], exhibit the anti-apoptotic effects in osteonecrosis rat models [ ] and promote re-epithelization in diabetic rats with ulcer [ ].

## PRP-Exo in diseases treatment
Hair loss is a common complaint among both male and female patients seeking treatments for this issue around the world [ ]. In a research PRP-Exo were used to induce hair dermal papilla cells (HDPCs) [ ]. According to previous studies, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity in dermal papilla reaches maximum it’s in hair the anagen phase of the growth cycle and decreases to minimum its amount in catagen the phase [ ]. Furthermore, most studies show that higher ALP activity correlates with deeper hair follicles [ ]. Another important factor promoting the anagen phase and positively influencing hair growth is the expression of the versican gene. In addition to stimulating the anagen phase, proteoglycans help maintain this phase and regulate immune responses [ ]. Taghiabadi and co-workers thus, used RT-PCR to observe expression of the ALP and versican genes after PRP-Exo treatment. They observed that PRP-Exo significantly upregulated ALP and versican expression in HDPCs. They also compared the effects of human adipose stem cell derived exosome (h-ADSC-Exo) and PRP-Exo treatment on HDPCs viability and gene expression profiles. Versican and ALP mRNA expressions were both increased compared to control group and expression levels were higher in human h-ADSC-Exo group compared to the PRP-Exo group. Additionally, cell viability and proliferation were higher in the h-ADSC-Exo group compared to the PRP-Exo group. Thus, while the efficacy of PRP-Exo in promoting hair growth is evident, h-ADSC-Exo demonstrated superior outcomes in this study. Nevertheless, further comprehensive studies are warranted.
Due to their abundance of growth factors and their ability to accelerate angiogenesis and re-epithelialization, PRP (and PRP-Exo) are beneficial in chronic wound healing [ , ]. In a study by Chun Guo, PRP-Exo, formulated into a hydrogel disc, was applied to a diabetic rat model to promote wound repair [ ]. The results indicated that sodium alginate hydrogel loaded with PRP-Exos outperformed both PRP-loaded sodium alginate hydrogel and the control (untreated) group in healing diabetic wounds. They also investigated the mechanism underlying this effect. According to previous research, Yes-associated protein (YAP) takes part in skin regeneration, fibroblast proliferation, re-epithelialization, and collagen deposition [ , ]. Upon YAP knockdown in fibroblasts, cell proliferation and migration were inhibited leading the researchers to conclude that PRP-Exo-induced wound healing is mediated via YAP activation, which enhances collagen synthesis. Later, XU et al. designed a study to investigate the role of other genes in PRP-Exo-induced wound healing [ ]. Mice were used as an in vivo wound healing model and were divided into three groups which were treated with PBS, PRP, or PRP-Exo injection near the wound site. PRP-Exo treatment resulted in faster wound healing and smaller scar formation. According to their research, ubiquitin-specific protease 15 (USP15) as a member of the de-ubiquitinases family is responsible for the wound healing effect of PRP based on the high level of USP15 expression in the PRP-exo-treated group. De-ubiquitinases can remove ubiquitin from proteins and, therefore, stabilize them, and in this way accelerate re-epithelialization [ – ].
Liu et al. showed that the Wnt/ß-catenin signaling pathway is responsible for OA treatment by intra-articular injection of PRP and PRP-Exo to OA animal models (rabbits) [ ]. Based on previous studies, they knew that the Wnt/ß-catenin signaling pathway activates and develops OA [ ]. The pathway also tempts upregulation of the RUNX2 gene, which stimulates the expression of factors catabolic to the cartilage ECM and is over-expressed in human osteoarthritic cartilage [ ]. They also compared the OA treatment potency of PRP and PRP-Exo. The results are shown in Table  . According to these observations, they confirmed that both PRP-Exo and PRP inhibited OA progression, but the effect of PRP-Exo was apparently superior to PRP by chondrocyte count and the OA Research Society International (OARSI) scoring system [ ]. Zhang and coworkers just formulated a thermo-sensitive hydrogel based on poloxamer407 and poloxamer188, referred to as PRP-Exo. This PRP-Exo incorporated gel was used for subtalar OA treatment in mice. The formulation was able to inhibit the apoptosis and hypertrophy of chondrocytes and induce their proliferation, thus decreasing the subtalar of OA development rate after application of the designated formulation. In vitro studies showed that the hydrogel could improve both mouse BMMSCs and chondrocyte proliferation and migration, as well as induce differentiation of BMMSCs to chondrocytes [ ].
Han et al. conducted a comprehensive review on the mechanisms, modes of administration, and efficacy of PRP-Exo in the treatment of the osteonecrosis of the femoral head (OFH). They analyzed 17 research studies (10 focusing on mechanisms and 7 on operational methods). They concluded that PRP-Exo are able to treat the OFH by stimulating angiogenesis, inhibiting inflammatory responses in necrotic lesions, promoting osteogenesis and preventing glucocorticoid- induced apoptosis. The authors emphasized that PRP-Exo can be utilized as an adjunctive therapy for core decompression along with stem cells and bone grafts [ ].

 

CD36 on the platelet surface helps the adhesion of platelets to the collagen of the flowing blood [ ] and platelet aggregation [ ]. CD36 also provides lipids for macrophage progression to foam cell formation and atherogenesis [ ]. PRP-Exo could decrease CD36 in platelets and macrophages of a mouse model of carotid artery thrombosis by enhanced ubiquitination and proteasome degradation. Therefore, PRP-Exo can prevent the atherothrombosis process [ ]. In addition to being effective in healing wounds and OA, PRP-Exo can also be used in other fields such as muscle injury [ ] and acute kidney injury [ ].
It was confirmed that the level of miR-223, miR-339, and miR-21, which are associated with platelet activation, was increased in PRP-Exo before arterial thrombosis. Thus PRP-Exo are suitable biomarker for prediction of atherothrombosis [ ].

## PRP-Exo in diseases diagnosis
Recent research has shown that exosomes which are isolated from platelet play a crucial role in the generation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). These exosomes assist in activation and adherence of neutrophils, and also enhance the inflammatory reaction following tissue damage. This process could potentially trigger the development of sepsis [ – ]. Moreover, in the context of sepsis, exosomes have been observed to cause myocardial dysfunction [ ]. Notably, the generation of platelet exosomes dramatically rises during sepsis [ ]. The expression of miR-15b-5p and miR-378a-3p has been observed to increase in platelet exosomes during sepsis. This leads to the activation of the Akt/mTOR-related autophagy pathway in neutrophils, causing an excessive synthesis of NET and worsening the severity of sepsis. Therefore, miR-15b-5p and miR-378a-3p have been identified as possible biomarkers for diagnosing sepsis [ ].

## Clinical applications of plasma exosomes (P-Exo)

## P-Exo for disease treatment
Jiang et al. isolated P-Exo from C57BL/6 mice in order to evaluate their efficacy in the treatment of cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury. The production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the mitochondria increases after stroke, leading to the loss of mitochondrial membrane integrity, and thus apoptosis in normal cells [ ]. ROS also degrades tight junction proteins of the BBB (TJPs) [ , ]. Hsp70, which exists on the exosome membrane, decreases the production of ROS and therefore is beneficial in the treatment of neurodegenerative disease [ ]. Their studies revealed that Hsp70 not only prevents ROS production and thus inhibits apoptosis and activates TJPs, but it also interacts with endothelial Toll-like receptor 4 of human brain microvascular endothelial cells (HBECs). The latter one facilitates the lateral migration and diapedesis of P-Exo across the BBB [ ]. Thus, they could provide a new method for targeted therapy and protection against ischemia-reperfusion injury.
P-Exo have been shown to have a strong ability to protect the heart in models of myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury [ ]. A cardio protective signaling pathway, which involves toll-like receptor (TLR) 4 and several kinases, has been discovered in cardiomyocytes. This pathway leads to the development of the cardio protective protein HSP27. These findings highlight the HSP70/TLR4 communication axis as a crucial element in exosome-mediated cardio protection.
Hypertension is the other illness that can be treated with P-Exo. Otani et al. isolated P-Exo from both hypertensive rats (HRs) and normotensive rats (NRs) [ ]. The P-Exo of HRs (HRE) was found to remarkably increase the systolic blood pressure in NRS. Similarly, P-Exo of NRs (NRE) could decrease the systolic blood pressure in HRs. In addition, NRE was shown to efficiently improve the structure and function of cardiovascular tissues in NRs and HRs, suggesting the promising therapeutic efficiency of P-Exo in treating hypertension.

## P-Exo for diseases diagnosis
P-Exo have been extensively investigated as non-invasive biomarkers for the diagnosis of various diseases. P-Exo are a suitable biomarker for the diagnosis of several types of cancers, such as colorectal cancer (CRC), which is one of the most frequent cancers worldwide. Notably, the concentration of plasma glypican1 positive exosomes (GPC1-positive P-Exo) in the both cancerous tissues and plasma of CRC patients is significantly higher than in healthy individuals, making GPC1-positive P-Exo a promising diagnostic marker. The diagnostic value increases further when GPC1-positive exosomes are co-analyzed with specific miRNAs such as miR-96-5p, miR-149 [ , ]. Moreover, miR-125a-3p, an exosomal miRNA, has been identified not only as a suitable biomarker for CRC diagnosis but also for its ability to distinguish early stage CRC, providing valuable insight into early diagnosis [ ]. Similarly, miR-21, either alone or in combination with other miRNAs has also been suggested as an appropriate biomarker for CRC diagnosis [ ]. In the context of breast cancer, studies have demonstrated that miR-1246 is significantly up regulated in most breast cancer cell lines compared to normal mammary epithelial cells [ , ]. Therefore, Yan Zhai et al. designed a probe based on Au nanoparticles in order to detect miRNA-1246 in plasma exosomes of breast cancer patients [ ]. The prepared probe, which was functionalized with DNA to target miR-1246, could enter into the exosomes isolated from 40 µL of patient’s plasma sample and thus a fluorescent signal could be detected by spectrophotometry. They indicated that miRNA-1246 is a potential biomarker of breast cancer. P-Exo also can be a marker for tumor progression or for the efficacy of the utilized treatment (Table  ).
Beyond cancer, P-Exo also hold potential as biomarkers for other diseases, including cardiovascular and neuropsychiatric disorders. For instance, Zhang et al. explored the expression level of various circular RNAs (circRNAs), lncRNAs, miRNAs, and RNAs in CHD patients [ ]. The results indicated that hsa_circ_0000038 and hsa_circ_0001360 were overexpressed in P-Exo of CHD patients compared to those in P-Exo of healthy participants, suggesting that these circRNAs are potential biomarker for CHD diagnosis.
Major depressive disorder (MDD) is another disease that can be diagnosed by P-Exo. P-exo can permit trough BBB and thus suggest brain-related knowledge. In a recent study, researchers combined deep learning algorithms with surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) to analyze P-Exo for MDD diagnosis [ ]. Their method utilized 28,000 exosome SERS signals to generate prediction results for each sample. The system achieved a sensitivity of 91.4% and a specificity of 88.6%, accurately predicting the disease state of 70 test samples that had not been used during training. Furthermore, the diagnostic scores correlated well with the severity of depression, highlighting the potential of this innovative, sensitive and specific protocol based on P-Exo for the diagnosis of MDD (Fig.  ).

 

 

## Clinical applications of milk exosomes (Milk-Exo)
Mammalian breast milk contains proteins, peptides, fatty acids, oligosaccharides, and immune-regulating components [ ]. Breast milk is a good source for obtaining exosomes, owing to its low cost, high availability, and biocompatibility. What draws attention to the use of milk exosomes (milk-Exo), however, is that they are scalable. Furthermore, crude ingredients for the isolation of these exosomes can be readily manufactured in industrial quantities [ ]. There are a few sources for obtaining milk-Exo that are shown in Table  .

 

## Milk-Exo for diseases treatment
Since exosomes are composed of both hydrophilic and hydrophobic components in their underlying layer, they can act as carriers for both hydrophilic and lipophilic small molecule drugs. Furthermore, like other drug delivery systems, they preserve drugs from enzymatic degradation and increase their solubility [ ]. They also have the capability to be attached to one or more tumor-recognizing ligands for target therapy [ ]. All of these features make exosomes a potential nanoplatform for drug delivery. The clinical applications of human milk exosomes are showed in Fig.  .
Milk-Exo have been used for the delivery of anticancer drugs/components (listed in Table  ). Milk-Exo can also be used for small interfering RNA (siRNA) delivery. siRNA inhibits the expression of the gene of interest (that causes disease) by cleaving the corresponding mRNA or limiting its translation [ ]. For example, Gupta et al. could successfully deliver AKT, MAP, and KRAS siRNAs to lung cancer cells by bovine milk-derived exosomes. They could also deliver VEGF siRNA to lung, breast, pancreatic, and ovarian cancer cells. Additionally, they loaded exosomes with SUR and BCL2 siRNAs and delivered them to breast cancer cells. This research group also decorated exosomes with folic acid [ ].
It has been demonstrated that milk-Exo can be stable against degradation in the acidic environment of the stomach or the basic environment of the gut. Moreover, milk-Exo can pass through the gastrointestinal barrier, making them suitable for oral delivery. With this in mind, milk-Exo can be used for medical imaging if they are attached to fluorescent dyes, contrast agents, or radioactive agents [ ]. For instance, in a research, goat milk exosomes were labeled with 99 mTc (technetium). After intranasal administration, intraperitoneal injection, or intravenous injection of the described platform into mice, single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) and computed tomography (CT) were attained. The results showed that intravenous administration established fast blood clearance (< 4 min) and mostly hepatic uptake. In contrast, intraperitoneal administration led to three- to four-fold longer circulation times compared to the intravenous route, probably because of their slower incorporation into the bloodstream. Lastly, intranasal instillation presented probe accumulation in the mouse head (nose), perhaps due to the retention of the developed platform during the breeding of the animal [ ].

 

 

## Milk-Exo for diseases diagnosis
There are not many studies that have utilized milk exosomes for diagnosing diseases. However, Qin et al. studied the effect of human milk exosomes on epithelial mesenchaymal transition (EMT) in breast cancer cell line [ ]. It was discovered that milk exosomes were taken up by both of normal epithelial breast cells and breast cancer cells. Moreover, TGFβ2 was found to be over expressed in breast milk exosomes during weaning process, leading to EMT in both malignant and normal breast cells. These findings suggest that milk exosomes provide a non-invasive biomarker for diagnosing breast cancer in women.

## Clinical applications of exosomes of malignant Ascites (MA-Exo)
Just like stem cells, tumor cells can secrete exosomes. These exosomes mediate communication between tumor cells, stromal cells, and distant target cells [ ]. In addition to enhancing tumor growth, metastasis occurrence, and angiogenesis promotion, cancer exosomes can induce drug resistance in tumor cells [ ]. Recently, exosomes have been derived from malignant ascites (MA) of cancer patients (MA-Exo)and are ready for clinical trials [ ].

## MA-Exo for diseases treatment
Exosomes derived from MA-Exo have induced antitumor immunity in animals [ , ]. So, MA-Exo can be used as a vaccine for cancer immune therapy. Although MA-Exo induces an immune response, its immunogenicity is poor. Dai and co-workers isolated MA-Exo from people with advanced colorectal cancer. They deduced that treatment with MA-Exo plus granulocyte–macrophage colony stimulating factor (GM-CSF), an adjuvant that induces an immune response, stimulates a tumor-specific antitumor cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) response [ ]. Exosomes secreted from tumors, can transfer tumor antigens to dendritic cells (DC) and DC can present these antigens to T cells thus activating CTL. In a research, resting T cells were mixed with DCs which were primed with MA-Exo of ovarian cancer patients. The results showed that MA-Exo could present tumor antigens to DCs, thus inducing tumor-specific cytotoxicity [ ]. It has been confirmed that heat treatment before exosome isolation induces more immunogenicity [ ]. Heat stress can stimulate Hsps and thus stimulate innate and adaptive immune responses [ ]. Heat-treated and untreated MA-Exo of gastric cancer patients were compared. Heat-treated exosomes contained a higher concentration of Hsp70 and Hsp60. Therefore, heat treatment caused increased exosome-induced immunogenicity, likely due to chemokine activation. As a result DC and T cells are activated by chemokine-induced chemo attraction [ ]. In a more recent study, exosomes were isolated from a very aggressive murine T-cell lymphoma, and intraperitoneal injection of these exosomes was found to stimulate cellular and humoral immune responses, resulting in tumor rejection. They also deduced that immune memory induction, interferon gamma production, and T helper 1 response involvement were responsible for the tumor rejection [ ].

## MA-Exo for diseases diagnosis
MA-Exo can be analyzed for their RNA content to diagnose cancer. For instance, a research group examined the miRNA profile of MA-Exo from 165 ascites samples to identify a potential biomarker for diagnosing peritoneal carcinomatosis in gastric cancer (GC), which has a poor prognosis [ ]. The study revealed a significant decrease in the expression level of miR-181b-5p in GC patients compared to the control group, suggesting the potential of MA-Exo in diagnosing GC.
In another study, exosomes were isolated from MA of patients with ovarian cancer [ ]. These exosomes were reported to be enriched with miR-6780b-5p. Additionally, the expression level of miR-6780b-5p was found to be linked to EMT development and tumor metastasis in ovarian cancer patients. This experiment provides evidence to the capability of MA-Exo in predicting the prognosis of ovarian cancer.

## Clinical applications of salivary exosomes
Saliva is a diluted fluid and is composed of 99% water. In addition to water, saliva contains electrolytes, immunoglobulins, proteins, enzymes, mucin, urea, ammonia and exosomes [ ].

## Salivary exosomes for diseases treatment
Several studies have investigated the use of salivary exosomes for disease treatment. Periodontitis, a chronic inflammatory disease, results in tooth loss, especially in its severe phase. Therefore, early diagnosis of periodontitis, is crucial to prevent the destruction of teeth. Salivary exosomes have the potential to facilitate the regeneration of dental pulp by upregulating the expression of certain proteins, stimulating angiogenesis, regulating the communication between epithelial and MSCs, and improving the functional capabilities of dental pulp cells. These findings suggest that salivary exosomes could be valuable therapeutic approaches in the future [ ].
Diabetes is the other disease which have been treated with salivary exosomes. A research investigation was conducted to evaluate the therapeutic effectiveness of salivary exosomes in improving diabetes and treating xerostomia, a consequence of salivary gland dysfunction, in diabetic rats. After 5 weeks of administering salivary exosomes, the salivary gland tissues were dissected and subjected to histological and ultrastructural examination. The findings demonstrated that salivary exosomes decreased blood glucose levels and improved the functionality of salivary glands in the exosome-treated rats compared to the control group. The anti-inflammatory activity of salivary exosomes was validated by observing the downregulation of NFκB/p65 and TNFα gene expression. The researchers determined that salivary exosomes have the potential to be an innovative therapeutic tool for xerostomia and salivary gland dysfunction in diabetic patients [ ].

## Salivary exosomes for diseases diagnosis
While the exosomes obtained from saliva can be used in the treatment of some illnesses, their primary application is in the detection of various oral and systemic diseases [ – ]. The main advantage of this diagnostic method is that saliva collection is completely non-invasive. Some of the diagnostic applications of salivary exosomes are summarized in Table  .

 

## Clinical applications of urinary exosomes (U-Exo)
Urine, which is collected non-invasively, contains proteins, exosomes, and small molecules [ ]. Therefore, it can be an ideal alternative to plasma for detecting specific biomarkers for disease diagnosis [ ]. Urinary exosomes (U-exo) are secreted into urine by kidney cells [ ]. Pisitkun et al. performed proteomic analysis of U-exo [ ]. 295 proteins, of which 73 were associated with membrane trafficking, were recognized mainly in the endosomal pathway. LC-MSMS analysis identified small GTP-binding proteins, including proteins in the Rab, ARF, Rho, and Ral families. Additionally, 10 cytoskeletal and motor proteins, and 48 integral membrane proteins were the other detected proteins. Furthermore, 20 proteins related to renal or other systemic illnesses. Some of these proteins are listed in Table  . 

## U-Exo for diseases treatment
U-Exo have been discovered to improve the kidney function in diabetic rats. Intravenous administration of U-Exo could successfully decrease the urine volume, urinary microalbumin excretion, podocyte and tubular epithelial cell apoptosis, while enhancing glomerular endothelial cell proliferation in diabetic rats. These beneficial effect is likely attributed to their content which includes growth factor, TGFβ1, angiogenin and bone morphogenetic protein-7 [ ].

## U-Exo for diseases diagnosis
Although urine is a good choice for liquid biopsy, the use of U-Exo for diagnosis is limited due to the lack of a standard method for exosome isolation. For instance, Channavajjhala et al. utilized different methods, including UF, UC, ExoQuick-TC, and treatment with dithiothreitol coupled with UF, for obtaining exosomes from human urine. They concluded that UF was the best method due to the lowest particle size, the highest amount of obtained exosomes per ml, and an appropriate polydispersity index, a measure of the heterogeneity of a sample based on size [ ], compared to other methods [ ]. Also in a recent approach, Cho and co-workers indicated that size exclusion chromatography (SEC) combined with UF is better than UF, UC, and precipitation for U-Exo isolation in terms of purity and exosome concentration [ ].
U-Exo have been extensively used for the diagnosis of various illnesses, some of which are listed in Table  . Studying miRNA and protein content of urinary exosomes will open a new avenue for diagnosing diseases. In addition to the application of U-Exo in the diagnosis of illnesses, they can also be utilized for differential diagnosis of related diseases. For example, urinary tract infection (UTI) and asymptomatic bacteriuria (ABU) are two similar diseases. In contrast to UTI, ABU should not be treated with antimicrobial agents except in pregnancy and in patients who will undergo traumatic urologic interventions [ , ]. Therefore, differential diagnosis of UTI and ABU becomes important in order to prevent the development of multi-drug-resistant microorganisms. WB analysis revealed that the Akt signaling molecule and CD9, an exosome marker, in the U-Exo of UTI patients were higher than the U-Exo of ABU patients, proving that they are reliable biomarkers for the differential diagnosis of UTI and ABU [ ]. In contrast to the potential application of U-Exo for diagnosis, prognosis, and progression studies of various illnesses, these exosomes have not been used for the treatment of diseases.

 

The prostate specific antigen (PSA) is not a suitable marker for PCa diagnosis. Because it is specific to prostatic tissue but it is not specific to malignant prostatic tissue and increases in benign prostatic hyperplasia. Thus, providing a reliable biomarker is vital for diagnosis of PCa. Bio-Techne’s ExoDx Prostate IntelliScore (EPI) test received FDA Breakthrough Designation on June 17, 2019. Afterward, there was no need for invasive biopsies for diagnosis of PCa, and early detection of PCa could be done solely through urine sampling. The kit predicts the likelihood of high grade PCa on an initial prostate biopsy. The sensitivity of the test is about 92% and it avoids 34% of unnecessary biopsies. This kit contains all reagents for the quantification of three exosome-derived RNA biomarkers (ERG, PCA3 and SPDEF) by quantitative PCR [ , ]. The other kit that has been received FDA breakthrough designation in 2020, is the miR Sentinel PCC4 Assay. This kit evaluates small non-coding RNAs to determine the risk of aggressive PCa in men over 45 years old. The test sensitivity is 98.5% and its prognostic sensitivity in distinguishing clinically non-significant from clinically significant prostate cancers is 83% [ , ].

 

## Clinical applications of bile exosomes
It has been confirmed that both hepatocytes and cholangiocytes release exosomes in bile fluid. These exosomes accumulate in the intrahepatic bile ducts and interact with cholangiocyte cilia. These biliary exosomes, which have specific markers (CD63 and Tsg101), take part in intercellular communication through this interaction and thus stimulate intracellular signaling. When normal rat cholangiocytes were exposed to these exosomes, the exosomes attached to cilia [ ]. The suppression of the ERK signaling pathway and over expression of miR-15 A result in inhibition of cholangiocyte proliferation [ , ]. Biliary exosomes inhibit ERK signaling pathway and decrease the expression of miR-15 A in normal rat cholangiocytes via interaction with the cilia. Thus these exosomes increase cholangiocyte proliferation [ ].

## Bile exosomes for diseases diagnosis
It has been found that the content of biliary exosomes in a pathologic situation is altered and analysis of the contents of these exosomes can be a potential key for diagnosis and prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma (CCA). For example, increased expression levels of the two lnRNAs (ENST00000588480.1 and ENST00000517758.1) are potential biomarkers for the detection of CCA along with differentiating between well and poor prognosis patients. The reason is that these lnRNAs target the p53 signaling pathway which promotes cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, apoptosis, etc [ ]. Additionally, miR-483-5p and miR-126-3p are the two biliary exosomal miRNAs which do better than serum carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 in the diagnosis of CCA. But the mechanism of their regulatory function in CCA is not well known. Recently another experiment revealed that bile exosomal miR-200c-3p, and miR-200a-3p are independent predictors of CCA and that they are valuable for predicting cancer recurrence [ ]. Also, the expression level of miR-451a and miR-3619-3p have been up regulated in patients with biliary tract cancer and utilizing these two miRNAs together revealed better potential for cancer diagnosis. miR-451a is an oncogene which is involved in proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis [ ]. Moreover, high expression level of miR-3619-3p (an oncogene in various cancers) in the bile reflects a poorer prognosis for biliary tract cancers [ ]. Interestingly, a novel circular RNA (circ-CCAC1) in biliary exosomes plays an important role in CCA progression by enhancing angiogenesis, and disrupting vascular endothelial barriers [ ]. These studies suggest that exosomes from bile can be used for prognosis and diagnosis of biliary tract malignancy. To the best of our knowledge, it should be noted that, in addition to diagnosis, suppressing the expression of some of these RNAs could be an effective therapeutic approach for the treatment of CCA.

## Clinical application of seminal plasma exosomes (SP-Exo)
Seminal plasma, the non-cellular part of human semen, originates from the secretions of the prostate, epididymis, and seminal vesicles [ ]. Exosomes of seminal plasma (SP-Exo), in which prostasomes and epididymosomes are the most abundant components, impact sperm function and regulate the female reproductive tract, as well as contributing to embryonic development [ – ]. SP-Exo increases intracellular calcium which leads to better motility of sperm in the vaginal acidic medium [ , ]. Moreover, SP-Exo regulates sperm capacitation and the acrosome reaction, both vital for fertilizing the oocyte [ ]. Additionally, SP-Exo contains proteins and miRNAs that have the potential to detect male infertility, diagnose asthenozoospermia, detecting azoospermia, diagnosis of PCa, and even for treatment of a few viral infections. In the following, role of the SP-Exo in diagnosing or treatment of these diseases will be explained.

## SP-Exo for diseases treatment
Exosomes derived from healthy human semen have been found to possess the ability to inhibit HIV-1 infection and transmission [ ]. This is attributed to their ability to disrupt viral reverse transcriptase function and suppress viral RNA gene expression [ ]. The process of reverse transcription and viral gene expression is dependent on the activity of transcriptional activator (Tat). The HIV-1 long terminal repeat (LTR), which consists of modulatory elements, enhancers, promoters, and the Tat-activating region (TAR), is responsible for regulating the transcription of proviral DNA. TAR binds to nuclear Tat, which in turn increases the activity of host polymerase II (Pol II). In other words, nuclear Tat is crucial for the transactivation of the HIV-1 L promoter, resulting in the transcription of high levels of viral mRNA by Pol II. The LTR also contains binding sites for transcription cofactors such as Sp1 and NFKB, which, upon binding, induce transcription activity of the LTR. In a study conducted by Welch and colleagues, the mechanism by which semen exosomes impair viral reverse transcriptase activity, was elucidated. The results showed that exosomes hinder NFKB activation, inhibit the binding of NFKB and Sp1 to the LTR, disrupt the binding of Pol II to the HIV-1 promoter, and block Tat protein-mediated LTR activation. Their research demonstrated the potential of semen exosomes in the treatment of HIV infection [ ].

## SP-Exo for diseases diagnosis
There are two types of azoospermia, including secretory azoospermia (SA) and obstructive azoospermia (OA). In SA, there are no or few sperm in the testicles. In obstructive azoospermia, there is no deficiency in spermatogenesis [ , ]. Thus, it can be treated by obtaining sperm from a testicular biopsy for assisted reproductive treatment [ ]. Therefore, distinguishing between SA and OA is important. To this end, a research group obtained exosomes from the seminal plasma of healthy people, SA and OA patients. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis suggested a multiplex model for discriminating SA and OA. The AUC of ROC curve was the highest in the case of miR-31-5p compared to the other 8 miRNAs, with the sensitivity and the specificity of 92.9 and 90% for predicting the origin of azoospermia, respectively. When detection of miR-31-5p was combined with FSH concentration in blood, the sensitivity for distinguishing the type of azoospermia reached 100%. The research group also suggested determining the expression levels of miR-539-5p and miR-941 in SP-Exo for documentation of the presence of residual sperm in the testis [ ].
The other men’s fertility-related dysfunction is asthenozoospermia, which is described as a reduction in sperm motility. Hong et al. studied the expression levels of PIWI-interacting RNAs (piRNAs) in the SP-Exo of people with asthenozoospermia and compared them with those in normal individuals. According to the qRT-PCR analysis, piR-1207, piR-2107, piR-5937, and piR-5939 were considerably reduced in exosomes from asthenozoospermia patients compared to the control. ROC curve analysis showed AUC values of 0.845 for piR-1207 and 0.93 for piR-2107. Also, the sensitivity percentages were 70% and 85%, and the specificity percentages were 95.2% and 90% for piR-2107 and piR-1207, respectively. Based on their research these piRNAs are correlated with asthenozoospermia and their reduction in SP-Exo can be a potential biomarker for diagnosis of the illness. They also showed that dysfunction of the mitochondrial protein, MitoPLD (also known as Zucchini or PLD6), is the reason for the reduction of piRNAs in asthenozoospermia patients [ ]. MitoPLD has been proposed to function as an endonuclease to generate the 5’ ends of piRNAs [ ].
Varicocele is a condition that results in male infertility and is associated with hypoxia [ ]. Hypoxia leads to Sertoli cell damage and is responsible for varicocele-induced infertility [ ]. Seminal exosomal miR-210-3p has been reported to be enhanced in patients with grade II and III varicocele. Since miR-210-3p is associated with a reduction in sperm count and the reduction of seminal Inhibin-B expression, detecting this miRNA can be an effective procedure for varicocele diagnosis [ ].

## Clinical applications of ear fluid exosomes
There was no research involving exosomes isolated from ear fluids until recent years, however, a few studies have recently been conducted to isolate and utilize exosomes from ear fluids. The first study successfully isolated exosomes with an approximate size of 200 nm from middle ear effusion (MEE) using the Exoquick-TC kit [ ]. The exosomes were collected from patients with chronic otitis media (COM). Then the miRNA and protein content of the exosomes were analyzed. The miR-223-3p, miR-451a, miR-16-2p, miR-320e, and miR-25-3p were the most abundant miRNAs in MEE. Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) predicted that these miRNAs regulate 442 target genes involved in the upregulation of a large host of IL-8 mediated cellular processes. Some of these processes include neutrophil degranulation, CXCR1/2 mediated signaling (related to antimicrobial defense), lymphocyte and monocyte chemo attraction, and NF-kB pro inflammatory up regulation (results in angiogenesis and inflammation). Additionally, proteomics analysis revealed that regulators of the innate immune system (Mucin5B, heat shock proteins, and BPI fold-containing family B member 1 (BPIFB1) and typical neutrophil mediators (such as myeloperoxidase and neutrophil elastase) were proteins which were specific to MEE. The most abundant miRNA in MEE, miR-223-b, takes part in neutrophil maturation, and differentiation, modulation of chemotaxis, and granule secretion [ ]. This fact together with potential neutrophilic source of detected proteins suggests a neutrophilic source for MEE.

## Ear fluid exosomes for diseases diagnosis
Won et al. isolated exosomes from inner ear explants of Wistar rats for the first time [ ]. After separating the inner ear from the skull, inner ear cell cultures were prepared using organ explants. Then the cells were treated with ototoxic drugs (cisplatin or gentamicin), and the exosomes isolated from the treated cells were compared with those of the control group (untreated cells). A remarkable reduction in the amount of exosomal protein and in the number of particles per cubic centimeter was observed in the treated groups compared to the control. Furthermore, there was an apparent difference in the protein expression profiles of the two groups. Proteomic analysis showed that the amount of some proteins was decreased (ribosomal protein S13, ribosomal protein L10, and Acan) and, in contrast, the amount of some proteins was increased (Tmem 33, Pgm1, Eif3f, Rps24, Cct8, Hsd17b4, Aldh3a1, Ddost, Aldh3a1, Eif3c, Luc7l2, and Acadvl) in the intervention group. These observations collectively confirm that inner ear exosomes serve as potential biomarkers. However, up to date, there have been no studies to confirm that exosomes can be isolated from inner ear fluid.

## Clinical applications of synovial fluid exosomes

## Synovial fluid exosomes for diseases treatment
In a comprehensive study, exosomes were obtained from synovial fluid of different types of inflammatory arthritis, including rheumatoid arthritis (RA), ankylosing spondylitis (AS), gout, and OA patients. They demonstrated that the number of exosomes in 1 milliliter was the lowest in the synovial fluid of OA patients and was the highest in AS patients. Joint inflammation leads to cartilage and bone destruction through osteoclast activation in RA patients [ ]. In the early stage of OA, the activity of osteoclasts and osteoblasts increases by 190% and 96%, respectively. This in turn increases bone remodeling and bone loss [ ]. In this study, osteoclast differentiation was examined by investigating internalization of synovial exosomes into macrophages. The entry of exosomes plays a critical role in regulating osteoclast differentiation. The human macrophages were treated with synovial exosomes of RA, AS, gout, and OA patients. After conducting TRAP staining, it was found that exosomes isolated from RA patients had the highest potential to induce osteoclast differentiation. Thus, the researchers concluded that synovial exosomes potentially induce osteoclast differentiation in RA [ ].

## Synovial fluid exosomes for diseases diagnosis
There are several lncRNAs that have been found to be highly associated with OA, including GAS5, PCGEM1, and HOTAIR [ – ]. However, while there were no significant differences in the levels of lncRNA PCGEM1 in plasma samples between OA patients and controls, the relative expression of exosomal lncRNA PCGEM1 in synovial fluid was significantly higher in late-stage OA compared to early OA, and also significantly higher in early OA compared to controls. Furthermore, there was a proportional increase in the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Arthritis Index (WOMAC) scores with an increase in lncRNA PCGEM1 levels. These observations suggest that it may be possible to detect and track the progression of OA by evaluating the amount of lncRNA PCGEM1 present in synovial exosomes [ ]. In a recent study, researchers collected synovial fluid samples from both healthy individuals and patients with OA. From these samples, they isolated exosomes. Through microarray analysis, they found that the expression of miR-214-3p was significantly lower in the exosomes of OA patients compared to those from healthy individuals. This particular miRNA has been shown to inhibit chondrocyte apoptosis and promote its proliferation. The study also demonstrated that this miRNA can reduce cartilage degeneration and inflammation. Therefore, exosomes derived from synovial fluid may have potential for use in treating OA [ ]. Furthermore, Kun et al. conducted a study on the differences in exosome cargo between individuals with KOA and healthy individuals. Their findings revealed that the exosomes from end-stage KOA patients contained a higher amount of pro-inflammatory cytokines compared to those from healthy subjects and early-stage KOA patients. The study also showed that exosomes play a role in stimulating lymphocyte chemotaxis through the expression of chemokines such as CCL2, CCL3, CCL15, CXCL8, CXCL9, and CXCL10. This suggests that exosomes may inhibit chondrocyte proliferation, leading to joint inflammation and cartilage degeneration [ ].

## Biofluid exosome based personalized medicine
Exosomes obtained from bio fluids, can be examined for their protein, nucleic acid, and lipid composition, offering specific information on an individual’s health condition and disease mechanisms [ , ]. The utilization of exosomes as biomarkers and their therapeutic potential in readily accessible body fluids has the capacity to revolutionize individualized diagnostics, prognostics, and treatments in various clinical applications [ ]. Biomarkers are crucial in precision medicine for illness diagnosis and personalized medicine. Exosomes, as biomarkers and therapeutic vehicles, should be stored in bio banks. Standardization of techniques and quality control (QC) criteria is essential for isolating exosomes and ensuring high-quality preparations. The International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) has formed a subcommittee for scientific rigor and reproducibility. Applying QC measures, monitoring exosome purity, and employing good standards can expedite exosome discovery in personalized medicine [ ]. It has been confirmed that autologous exosomes derived from body fluids are potential carriers for efficient targeted drug delivery with no immune response [ ]. An ongoing clinical trial has been designed for treatment of colorectal cancer by autologous malignant effusion derived exosomes. These autologous exosomes are promising immune therapy vaccine [ ].

## Regulatory guidelines for exosome manufacturing
The regulatory framework for exosomes can be classified into two main approaches. The first approach reveals the influence of exosome content on body physiology and the second one investigates the procedures used to isolate exosomes. Regulatory standards for exosomes differ worldwide, based on their features. The standards in USA and Europe are based on the effect of exosomes on physiological functions. However, these standards are based on the type of isolation procedure in the Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan [ ]. 
Most of the EV/stem cell–based therapies are presently being overseen by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (US FDA)’s Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER). According to US regulations, exosome products are governed by the Public Health Service (PHS) Act Sect. 351, which regulates human cells, tissues, and cell- and tissue‐based products (HCT/P). After reports of adverse effects in patients treated with exosome-based products, the US FDA issued a public safety notification. As of October 2023, the US FDA had issued six warning letters about exosome-based products. Exosome-based products are regulated as drugs, devices, and/or biological products under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C) Act and/or the PHS Act and are subject to additional regulations, including appropriate premarket review [ – ].
There is a lack of official approval for injected exosome products due to the variability and impurity of exosome preparations. Besides, the mechanism of action (MOA) of exosomes is poorly understood, impeding the advance of quality control processes. Under GMP regulations, physical controls on exosome quality are still basic. Progressive analytical methods can be utilized to explore exosome composition and purity in future, possibly resulting in the stronger regulatory oversight similar to biological products. According to existing regulatory measures and policy approach, clinical trials should be performed on exosomes to check their MOA. A more inclusive quality monitoring is needed to recognize the multifaceted exosome content and the restrictions of present testing procedures. To confirm quality and safety, regulatory units should develop standards for specialized information about cellular action and drug effects, including MOA. Industry standards for manufacturing of active cellular products should also be developed, including automatic procedures and purification techniques [ ].

## Limitations and challenges of clinical application of biofluid exosomes
Despite the efficiency and advantages of exosomes, as described in the previous sections, there are limitations to their use. One of the main challenges is selecting a safe source that will result in the isolation of effective exosomes with minimal immunogenicity [ ]. There are challenges associated with exosome isolation. For example, traditional isolation techniques are laborious and can compromise exosomal membranes. Moreover, commercially available kits, despite their efficacy, are excessively costly for large-scale isolation [ ]. Exosomes are often present in very low concentrations in bio fluids, making it hard to achieve adequate amounts for large-scale therapeutic applications [ , ]. On the other hand, obtaining body fluids in large volumes is challenging. The impurity of bio fluid-derived exosomes is another hurdle for large-scale pro.
duction of exosomes [ ]. Moreover, variability in source and isolation techniques can lead to batch-to-batch consistency. Hence, improving scalability is critical for advancing the use of bio fluid exosomes in diseases treatment.
Another challenge is that the method of isolation can affect the results of clinical intervention, concentration, purity, and size of exosomes. For instance, miRNA profiles are different, depending on the isolation methods used [ , ]. This challenge becomes more significant because the recommendations made in the International Society of Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) guidelines are unclear and leave the isolation of EVs to the discretion of researchers [ ]. The isolation of exosomes from body fluids can present several problems. Body fluids contain many components that cause contamination and should be removed during exosome isolation [ , ]. One of these contaminants is soluble proteins, such as albumin, immunoglobulins, and matrix metalloproteases (MMPs), all of which are abundantly present in body fluids [ , ]. Therefore, it is preferable to isolate the exosomes from a specific cell type. Although mycoplasma and other microorganisms can also release EVs, this can affect the results of exosome characterization, cargo analysis, and functional studies [ ].
Additionally, one important challenge is that the optimal conditions for cell culture are in contrast to the optimal conditions for exosome isolation. Therefore, both the ideal conditions for cell growth and the ideal conditions for exosome isolation must be carefully selected [ ]. The passage number of cells can also affect the cargo and potency of the exosomes. In higher passage numbers, the potency of the exosomes obtained may decrease [ ]. The age of the donor is another variable in determining the efficiency of exosomes. The exosomes derived from older donors possess lower efficacy [ ]. Even the number of seeded cells can affect the function and cargo of exosomes. Therefore, it is important to use the same seeding procedure to ensure reproducibility [ ].
On the other hand, regardless of the route of administration and cell source, systemically injected exosomes are quickly removed from the body by the macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system (RES) [ , ]. Less than 5% of the injected dose of exosomes remains in the blood circulation 3 h after injection [ ]. Also, the half-life of exosomes applied topically, for example, to the skin or ocular surface, may be even shorter due to rapid clearance of vesicles by sweat or tears [ ]. However, hydrogels can be used to load a relatively small amount of exosomes, while still creating the desired therapeutic effect and maintaining it for a certain period. This is because hydrogels can prevent the early clearance of entrapped exosomes [ ]. Another way to overcome this challenge is to introduce polyethylene glycol (PEG) on the surface of exosomes to prolong their circulation time [ ].
Beyond the technical and biological challenges, the clinical translation of exosome-based diagnostics faces significant ethical considerations. In contrast to acceptable outcomes, safety and side effects of exosome therapy are not completely understood. Based on clinical trials the feasibility and the short-term safety of autologous exosomes administration have been confirmed [ ]. However, the application of autologous exosomes for gene editing or drug delivery necessitates careful consideration of significant ethical concerns, particularly informed consent and data privacy. Patients undergoing gene therapies must be comprehensively informed about potential long-term outcomes, including heritable effects. Moreover, exosome-mediated CRISPR therapies introduce risks such as off-target editing and broader bioethical questions concerning human genome modification. Implementing rigorous guidelines for the ethical use of exosome technologies is vital to ensure patient safety and protecting genetic privacy [ ]. The concerns for allogeneic exosome-based products will be more rigorous. It has been indicated that exosomes facilitate pathogenesis by transporting diverse infectious agents derived from pathogens. Viral infections, for example, induce host cell exosome biogenesis and release. These vesicles, originating from pathogen-infected host cells contain proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids that can promote infection by epigenetically suppressing host defenses or inducing pro-pathogen gene expression [ ]. Thus, the application of allogeneic exosomes for diseases treatment increases the risk of pathogen transmission.
It should be mentioned that commercialization of exosome formulations requires more rigorous GMP practices for exosome manufacturing, including exosome source selection, standardized cell culture techniques, downstream exosome isolation and quality assessment protocols [ ]. Therefore, given that exosomes offer many advantages and applications in both the diagnosis and treatment of diseases, it seems more useful to conduct further studies on them to better address these few challenges.

## Conclusion
According to numerous papers, exosomes can be easily extracted from bio fluids. Depending on the type of source from which the exosomes are obtained, they can have therapeutic, diagnostic, or both applications. Among them, they can be used in the treatment of liver and heart diseases, wound dressing, muscle injury, neurodegenerative diseases, the diagnosis and treatment of various cancers, OA, etc. However, their clinical translation faces significant scalability hurdles, such as high production costs, low exosome yield and purity, batch variability, and stringent GMP requirements for large-scale manufacturing. There are also unresolved ethical challenges, such as long-term safety uncertainties, informed consent complexities for genetic therapies, genetic privacy risks, and potential pathogen transmission in allogeneic products. In the case of some of these biofluids, such as, semen, bile, synovial fluid, and ear fluid, more studies are needed to discover the exact composition of these exosomes and, as a result, their applications. It is probably necessary to overcome these limitations through further research in the future, ultimately bridging the gap between clinical studies on exosomes and the development of exosome-based therapeutic products and methods.
