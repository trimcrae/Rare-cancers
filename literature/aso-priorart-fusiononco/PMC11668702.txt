## Abstract
Patients with chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) frequently develop resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as imatinib. In this study, we explored the role of the insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) signaling pathway in CML and imatinib resistance. An analysis of IGF-1 gene expression using public databases revealed elevated levels of insulin-like growth factor-binding proteins in patients with chronic-phase CML. Further research revealed that IGF-1-related genes were upregulated in patients who were unresponsive to imatinib, suggesting that IGF-1 signaling plays a role in the resistance mechanism. Furthermore, we evaluated the efficacy of linsitinib, a selective insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor (IGF-1R) inhibitor, in inhibiting the growth of CML cell lines, including imatinib-resistant cell lines, and observed a notable decrease in cell viability and an increase in cytotoxicity. The combination of imatinib and linsitinib reduced cell viability and increased caspase-3/7 activity in imatinib-resistant cells. Moreover, silencing of IGF-1R by small interfering ribonucleic acid increased the sensitivity of CML cell lines to imatinib, indicating that IGF-1R could be a strategic target for overcoming resistance. These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of linsitinib and that IGF-1R inhibition may improve the treatment outcomes of patients with imatinib-resistant CML.

## Introduction
Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a myeloproliferative disorder characterized by the t(9, 22) translocation, thereby leading to the creation of the breakpoint cluster region::Abelson murine leukemia viral oncogene homolog 1 (BCR::ABL1) gene fusion. This condition evolves into three clinical stages: chronic phase (CP), accelerated phase (AP), and blast phase (BP) [ , ]. The oncogenic fusion gene encodes the BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase protein, and its increased activity results in the continuous activation of downstream signaling pathways [ ]. These changes collectively lead to leukemogenesis. Considering the pivotal role of BCR::ABL1 in the pathogenesis of CML, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) targeting this fusion protein have become selective and effective treatment strategies [ ]. The advent of BCR::ABL1-targeting TKIs has markedly enhanced the prognosis of patients with CML [ ]. Imatinib has transformed CML treatment by markedly enhancing patient outcomes and increasing patient survival rates. [ ]. Despite the effectiveness of imatinib and other Abelson tyrosine kinase inhibitors (ABL TKIs) in treating CML, some patients eventually develop resistance to these drugs [ ]. Resistance can develop through different mechanisms such as point mutations in the BCR::ABL1 kinase domain or the activation of alternative signaling pathways [ ]. Therefore, exploring alternative therapeutic strategies for addressing cases of TKI-resistant CML is crucial.
The insulin-like growth factor (IGF) system is pivotal for cellular growth, differentiation, and survival [ ]. IGF-1 shares nearly 50% of its amino acid sequence with insulin and induces a comparable hypoglycemic effect [ ]. The insulin-like growth factor family, which includes ligands (e.g., IGF-1, IGF-2, and insulin), receptors (e.g., insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor [IGF-1R], mannose-6-phosphate/IGF-2R, and insulin receptor [IR]), and IGF-binding protein 1–6 (IGFBP 1–6), have a pivotal role in normal human physiology and various disease states [ ]. The IGF-1R is a highly potent receptor with antiapoptotic, prosurvival, and potentially transformative properties [ ]. The IGF-1R, a tyrosine kinase receptor, is the main mediator of IGF-1 signaling. This receptor triggers various internal pathways such as the PI3K/Akt and rat sarcoma/rapidly accelerated fibrosarcoma/mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase/extracellular signal-regulated kinase (Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK) pathways [ ]. Pathways involving IGF-1 are crucial for cell proliferation and survival. Dysregulation of IGF-1 signaling has been implicated in the development and progression of various cancers [ ].
In this study, we assessed the potential involvement of IGF-1 signaling in imatinib-resistant CML cells. We investigated the expression levels and activation status of key components of the IGF-1 signaling pathway in imatinib-sensitive and imatinib-resistant CML cell lines. Additionally, we examined the efficacy of the IGF-1R inhibitor linsitinib in suppressing CML cell growth, and we explored whether combination treatment with imatinib and linsitinib could overcome ABL TKI resistance in CML cells.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
All reagents used in this study were of analytical grade. Linsitinib, a selective inhibitor of the IGF-1R, was purchased from Selleck Chemicals (Houston, TX, USA). Linsitinib stock solutions were prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide and stored at − 20 °C. Imatinib, a specific inhibitor of BCR::ABL1, was provided by Novartis Pharma AG (Basel, Switzerland). Additional reagents were acquired from Merck KGaA (Darmstadt, Germany).

## Cell culture
The chronic myeloid leukemia cell line K562 was acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). Additionally, T315I-mutant Ba/F3 cells and wild-type BCR::ABL-transfected Ba/F3 cells were obtained, as described previously. [ ]. Imatinib-resistant K562K562 IR cells were established through culture with a low imatinib concentration [ ]. Ponatinib-resistant K562 (K562 PR) cells were also characterized [ ]. K562 PR cells were established through N-ethyl-N-nitrosourea (ENU) mutagenesis screening and were cultured with ponatinib [ ]. Ba/F3 cells were maintained in a standard RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% (vol/vol) fetal calf serum and 10% (vol/vol) conditioned medium from the WEHI-3B cell line (as a source of IL-3). All cell lines were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 (RPMI 1640) medium, supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS), 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and 1% glutamine, at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide (CO2).

## Data collection and processing
To identify differentially expressed and spliced transcripts in CML, messenger ribonucleic acid (RNA) profiles of peripheral blood mononuclear cells from five patients with chronic-phase chronic myeloid leukemia (CP-CML) and five healthy volunteers were generated through deep sequencing using the NextSeq 500 system (GSE100026 dataset; Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) [ ]. In patients with CP-CML who were undergoing frontline imatinib treatment, an inability to achieve an early molecular response (EMR), defined as BCR-ABL1 > 10% (International Scale [IS]) at 3 months, was indicative of a poor prognosis. This factor was evaluated using the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE130404 in which patients with EMR failure (n = 13) were compared with patients with EMR success (n = 83) [ ]. Data were obtained from the GEO. GEO2R uses GEOquery and limma to perform differential expression analysis using original submitter-supplied processed data tables as input. The edgeR is an R/Bioconductor software package for differential analyses of sequencing data in the form of read counts for genes or genomic features used, according to user guide instructions. The RNA-Seq data analysis software for academic research was procured from BxINFO LLC (Shinagawa-ku, Tokyo, Japan). Additionally, RNA-Seq data visualization was conducted using the open bioinformatics web tool RaNA-Seq.eu) [ ].

## Cell viability assay
The cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 4 × 103 cells or 8 × 103 cells per well. Cells were treated with imatinib and/or linsitinib for 72 h, after which cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo Laboratories, Kumamoto, Japan) or determined using the Trypan Blue Staining assay (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. After 72 h of culture, samples were examined using the EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) or the TC10 Automated Cell Counter (Bio-Rad). Proliferation rates were reported as percentages relative to that of the control and untreated cells.

## Determination of caspase 3/7 activities
Caspase activity in CML cells was evaluated using the Caspase-Glo 3/7 Assay Kit (Promega Corporation, Madison, WI, USA), based on the manufacturer’s protocol. CML cells were treated with imatinib, linsitinib, or a combination of these at various doses. After 48 h of incubation, the Caspase-Glo 3/7 reagent was dispensed into each well in equal amounts. Luminescence was then measured using the EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer) to determine the activity of caspase 3/7.

## Cytotoxicity assays
The CML cells were treated with imatinib and/or linsitinib at various concentrations. Cytotoxicity was assessed by measuring the release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) from damaged cells using a cytotoxicity LDH assay kit with water-soluble tetrazolium salt (Dojindo Laboratories). After 48 h of treatment, LDH levels were quantified using the EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer), following the manufacturer's protocols.

## Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction analysis (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted from CML cells with the RNAqueous-4PCR Kit (Life Technologies Japan Ltd., Minato-ku, Tokyo, Japan) and reverse transcribed using the First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (OriGene Technologies, Rockville, MD, USA). Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was conducted using the Roche LightCycler 2.0 system (Roche Diagnostic GmbH, Minato-ku, Tokyo, Japan). Human IGF-1R, IGFBP2, IGF-1 and β-actin expression was quantified using the SYBR Green PCR Kit (Roche), following the manufacturer's instructions. Specific PCR primers were purchased from Takara Bio Inc. (Otsu, Shiga, Japan).

## Small interfering RNA and small interfering RNA transfection
Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting the IGF-1R was acquired from Merck KGaA (Darmstadt, Germany). K562 PR cells were transfected with the IGF-1R siRNA via electroporation. In brief, cells were electroporated in 400 μL of culture medium at a density of 2 × 107/mL in electroporation cuvettes, combined with 1 nmol of siRNA. Electroporation was performed via the Gene Pulser II System (Bio-Rad) set to 0.2 kV and 950 μF capacitance. Fifteen minutes postelectroporation, cells were diluted in culture medium and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 and 92% humidity. Gene expression in the transfected cells was assessed via RT-PCR. After 48 h, the cells were treated with imatinib and analyzed.

## Mitochondrial membrane potential assay
The mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was evaluated using a mitochondrial staining kit (Merck KGaA), following the manufacturer's protocol. The cells were thereafter treated for 48 h with or without imatinib and/or linsitinib. JC-1 monomers and aggregates were measured using the EnSpire Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer).

## Statistical analyses
All data were analyzed using Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Each experiment was performed three times. The paired Student’s t-test was used to calculate the significance of the differences between two groups. When one of the groups in this study was considered the control group, data were analyzed using Dunnett’s test as a post hoc test, followed by an analysis of variance with an alpha of 0.05 (n ≥ 3). Significance was expressed as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, or ****p < 0.0001.

## Results

## Gene expression of IGF-1 in CML and imatinib resistance
We investigated IGF-1 expression using publicly available databases to elucidate the influence of IGF-1 on CML. Figure  A illustrates the results of volcano plot analysis, obtained from the GEO database. In addition to the volcano plot, a dendrogram was generated, which allowed the hierarchical clustering of gene expression patterns (Fig.  B). IGFBPs have a central role in modulating the oncogenic actions of IGFs mediated by IGF-1R signaling [ ]. Figure  C shows the upregulation of IGFBP2 and IGFBP7 expression in patients with CML-CP [ ]. These results indicate a possible role for these genes in CML progression. The expression levels of IGFBP3 and IGFBP4 were conversely lower in patients with CML-CP. No significant alterations were detected in the expression of IGF-1R or IGF-1. We subsequently examined the expression of IGF-1-related genes in patients who did not achieve an EMR to imatinib treatment. The expression levels of IGF1, IGFBP2, IGFBP3, and IGFBP7 were higher in the EMR failure group than in the control group, as shown in Fig.  D [ ]. By contrast, the expression of IGF-1 and IGF-1R remained unchanged. This finding indicated that the IGF-1 signaling pathway may have a role in the emergence of imatinib resistance in CML.
 

## Linsitinib suppressed CML cells, including cells resistant to ABL TKIs
Linsitinib is a selective inhibitor of the IGF-1R. The expression level of the IGF-1R was further investigated in the TKI-resistant CML cell lines K562 IR and K562 PR. K562 IR cells did not harbor BCR::ABL1 point mutations. In contrast, K562 PR cells were heterogeneously characterized by the absence of exon 4 in the ABL kinase domain [ ]. IGF-1R expression was significantly increased in both the resistant lines compared with that in the parental K562 cell line (Fig.  A). Furthermore, we demonstrated a decrease in IGF1 gene expression in K562 IR and K562 PR cells. Moreover, we observed an increase in IGFBP2 gene expression in K562 IR cells; however, no significant change in expression was detected in K562 PR cells (Fig.  A). The β-actin gene served as the control for gene expression comparisons. The therapeutic potential of linsitinib was also assessed. Linsitinib effectively inhibited the cell viability of K562, K562 IR, and K562 PR cells. In addition, linsitinib treatment increased caspase-3/7 activity and cytotoxicity (Fig.  B, D, E). Subsequent investigations demonstrated that following linsitinib administration, the Ba/F3 BCR::ABL and Ba/F3 T315I cells exhibited diminished viability, whereas Ba/F3 cells remained unaffected (Fig.  C). These findings suggested that linsitinib is a promising therapeutic strategy for overcoming CML resistance.
 

## Imatinib activity against CML cells
The efficacy of imatinib was evaluated in several CML cell lines. Cell viability was evaluated following a 72 h incubation period, while cytotoxicity was assessed after 48 h of treatment. These time points were selected to capture the treatment's effects on cell viability. K562 cells exhibited a dose-dependent reduction in viability after imatinib treatment, accompanied by a significant increase in cytotoxicity. However, K562 IR cells were resistant to imatinib, whereas K562 PR cells showed only a partial response to imatinib treatment (Fig.  A). Cytotoxicity also increased in a dose-dependent manner (Fig.  B). The effect of imatinib on Ba/F3 BCR::ABL cells was also examined, and a notable reduction in cell viability and increased cytotoxicity were observed (Supplemental Fig. 1A). However, the T315I mutant Ba/F3 T315I cell line, which harbors a mutation that confers resistance to TKIs, exhibited high resistance to imatinib (Supplemental Fig. 1A). Cytotoxicity increased at high concentrations of imatinib (Supplemental Fig. 1B). These results are consistent with the known resistance profiles of BCR:ABL mutants, especially the T315I mutation, which poses a significant clinical challenge in the treatment of CML.
 

## Efficacy of imatinib and linsitinib against CML cells, including ABL TKI-resistant cells
In our investigation of combination therapy, we assessed the effects of imatinib and linsitinib cotreatment on CML cell lines. The combination treatment significantly reduced the proliferation of K562, K562 IR, and K562 PR cells (Fig.  A). This finding was accompanied by an increase in caspase-3/7 activity and in cytotoxicity, indicating enhanced induction of apoptosis (Fig.  B, C). Furthermore, cotreatment with imatinib and linsitinib decreased the viability of Ba/F3 BCR::ABL cells (Fig.  A). Of note, Ba/F3 T315I mutant cells, which are typically resistant to imatinib monotherapy, also exhibited a significant reduction in proliferation and increased caspase 3/7 activity and cytotoxicity when exposed to the combination treatment. MMP (i.e., ΔΨm) is a global indicator of mitochondrial function [ ]. We investigated the effect of the combination treatment on MMP, which is defined as the difference in electrical charge across the inner mitochondrial membrane and is crucial for mitochondrial function and apoptosis regulation (Fig.  D). Treatment with imatinib and linsitinib significantly reduced MMP levels, further corroborating the induction of apoptosis.
 

## Knockdown of IGF-1R enhances sensitivity to imatinib
To further investigate the role of IGF-1R in imatinib resistance, we used IGF-1R siRNA to selectively suppress IGF-1R expression in K562 PR cells, which are characterized by elevated IGF-1R levels. Using electroporation, we successfully transfected K562 PR cells with IGF-1R siRNA. Subsequent RT-qPCR analysis confirmed a significant reduction in IGF-1R expression in siRNA-transfected cells, compared with that of the controls (Fig.  A). After IGF-1R suppression, siRNA-transfected K562 PR cells exhibited an enhanced response to imatinib treatment compared with that of mock-transfected cells (Fig.  B). Furthermore, imatinib treatment increased caspase-3/7 activity and cytotoxicity in IGF-1R siRNA-transfected cells (Fig.  C, D), indicating that suppressing the IGF-1R sensitizes cells to imatinib.
 

## Discussion
In this study, we evaluated the efficacy of linsitinib, a selective IGF-1R inhibitor, against ABL TKI-resistant CML cells. The emergence of resistance to TKIs, particularly in cases of mutations such as T315I, necessitates the development of alternative treatment strategies. Our findings demonstrated that linsitinib effectively inhibited cellular proliferation and induced apoptosis in imatinib-resistant CML cell lines, including cells harboring the T315I mutation. These results suggested that targeting the IGF-1R signaling pathway may represent a viable approach to overcome resistance in CML therapy.
Analysis of publicly available GEO datasets revealed that the IGF-1 signaling pathway is upregulated in CP-CML cells, especially in patients who do not achieve an EMR to TKI therapy. The lack of an EMR is linked to poor prognosis and disease progression. This finding implies that IGF-1 signaling is a significant factor in the progression of CML and resistance to TKI treatment. Additionally, the involvement of IGF-1 signaling in various cancers, such as lung, breast, colorectal, and prostate cancers, has been extensively documented, indicating its wider relevance in oncogenesis [ ]. The IGF-1R has a regulatory role in tumor survival by influencing cell cycle progression, apoptosis, and differentiation [ ].
Linsitinib, an investigational oral IGF-1R inhibitor, has demonstrated efficacy in various oncological clinical trials [ ]. In the context of CML, we have elucidated its potential to overcome ABL TKI resistance. Our investigation demonstrated that linsitinib, used for monotherapy and used in combination with imatinib, significantly reduced cell viability and induced apoptosis in imatinib-resistant CML cells, including cells harboring the T315I mutation. This combination therapy reduced MMP, indicating mitochondrial dysfunction and the initiation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. The T315I mutation confers resistance to first- and second-generation TKIs; therefore, our findings suggested that IGF-1R inhibition by linsitinib is a viable therapeutic strategy to overcome this resistance.
Previous studies [ ] have demonstrated that IGF-1 signaling is a significant event in blast crisis transformation, providing a mechanism for elucidating the activity of IGF-1R and hemopoietic cell kinase inhibitors in impeding CML-BP phenotypes. Autocrine IGF-1 signaling is a potential mechanism driving the transition from CP to BP. The acquisition of IGF-1 signaling is an important event in the transformation to BP and provides a novel mechanism explaining the activities of IGF-1R and Hck inhibitors in blocking BP phenotypes [ ]. Our findings revealed the elevated expression of the IGF-1 gene in the parental K562 cell line, while its expression remained low in both K562 IR and K562 PR cell lines. Further analysis demonstrated that the combination of the IGF-1R inhibitor linsitinib with imatinib effectively suppressed the growth of imatinib-resistant chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) cells, including those harboring the T315I mutation. The clinical utility of IGF-1R inhibitors such as linsitinib is not limited to CML. The inhibition of IGF-1R has shown antitumor effects in various cancers by promoting apoptosis and inhibiting cell proliferation [ ]. Thus, IGF-1R inhibition represents a potentially efficacious therapeutic approach for malignancies refractory to conventional treatments, including TKI-resistant CML [ ].

## Conclusion
This study demonstrated that IGF-1R signaling has a critical role in the survival of TKI-resistant CML cells. Inhibition of this pathway by linsitinib, particularly when used in combination with TKIs, may improve outcomes in patients with resistant CML. Additional clinical trials are required to evaluate the efficacy of linsitinib in CML and its broad potential for oncological applications. These results suggested that targeting IGF-1R signaling could provide a novel therapeutic approach for overcoming TKI resistance in CML, potentially enhancing treatment options and outcomes in patients resistant to standard therapies.
