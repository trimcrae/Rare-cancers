## Introduction
 A wide range of pathological conditions are associated with alterations in cellular proteostasis [ – ]. The accumulation of misfolded or aberrant proteins can disrupt normal cellular functions, triggering a cascade of abnormal physiological states and ultimately contributing to tissue damage and disease pathogenesis. Over recent decades, substantial research has been dedicated to inhibiting overactive or disease-causing proteins through interventions at the genomic and transcriptomic levels, exemplified by the development of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies and small-molecule inhibitors [ – ]. Nevertheless, these conventional strategies are constrained by their reliance on the presence of specific, druggable binding sites—a requirement that many pathogenic proteins, particularly those involved in aggregation-prone disorders, do not fulfill [ – ]. Furthermore, the need for high inhibitor concentrations and prolonged target engagement increases the risk of off-target effects and potential toxicity [ – ]. In contrast, the endogenous ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) represents a natural regulatory mechanism that maintains proteostatic balance by selectively tagging and degrading damaged or unwanted proteins [ – ]. Harnessing the UPS to facilitate targeted degradation of disease-related proteins offers a promising therapeutic alternative, enabling the direct removal of pathogenic effectors rather than merely inhibiting their activity, and thereby opening new avenues for reversing disease progression.

 
Proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs, Fig. ) represent a paradigm-shifting approach in targeted cancer therapy, offering a novel mechanism of action that fundamentally differs from traditional small-molecule inhibitors [ – ]. These ingeniously designed heterobifunctional molecules comprise three essential components: a target protein ligand, an E3 ubiquitin ligase ligand, and a flexible linker connecting these two moieties [ – ]. This unique molecular architecture enables PROTACs to act as molecular bridges, recruiting the UPS to specifically tag and degrade target proteins [ – ]. The mechanism of action involves the formation of a ternary complex between the PROTAC, the target protein, and the E3 ubiquitin ligase, leading to polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of the target protein [ – ]. This innovative approach offers several groundbreaking advantages over traditional therapies [ – ]. First, PROTACs can target proteins that were previously considered “undruggable” due to the absence of well-defined binding pockets, thereby expanding the druggable proteome. Second, they operate catalytically, meaning a single PROTAC molecule can induce the degradation of multiple target protein molecules, potentially reducing the required therapeutic dose and minimizing off-target effects. Third, by eliminating the target protein rather than merely inhibiting its function, PROTACs can overcome drug resistance mechanisms that often plague conventional therapies, such as target protein overexpression or mutation.

 

Although PROTACs represent an attractive therapeutic paradigm due to their high efficiency and reduced potential for drug resistance, several key challenges hinder their clinical translation (Fig. ) [ – ]. Their characteristically high molecular weight and poor solubility limit cell permeability and oral bioavailability. Furthermore, the lack of inherent targeting capability often leads to off-target accumulation and potential systemic toxicity. Another significant limitation is the “hook effect”, where high concentrations of PROTACs result in the formation of inactive binary complexes rather than productive ternary complexes, thereby impairing target protein degradation. To overcome these limitations, advanced drug delivery systems offer a promising strategy [ – ]. Nanocarrier-based systems, including polymeric nanoparticles (NPs), liposomes, and ligand-functionalized vehicles, can significantly improve the physicochemical properties and pharmacokinetic profiles of PROTACs. These systems not only enhance drug solubility and promote cellular internalization but also allow for spatiotemporally controlled release within the tumor microenvironment (TME). By reducing renal clearance and minimizing non-specific exposure, such strategies help increase systemic exposure to malignant tissues while limiting off-target toxicities. For instance, liposomal encapsulation has been demonstrated to prolong blood circulation half-life, enhance physicochemical stability, and promote enhanced permeability and retention (EPR)-mediated passive targeting in tumor regions. Additional active targeting strategies, through the conjugation of specific ligands or antibodies, can further refine tissue specificity and therapeutic precision. Thus, the development of sophisticated delivery platforms constitutes a critical pathway toward realizing the full therapeutic potential of PROTACs.

 
This review aims to provide a comprehensive and up-to-date overview of the rapidly evolving field of nano-PROTACs in disease therapy (Fig.  ). We will systematically examine the design principles that guide the development of these innovative therapeutic agents, exploring various nanocarrier systems and their unique advantages. The review will elucidate the intricate mechanisms of action that underlie the therapeutic efficacy of nano-PROTACs, including their ability to overcome drug resistance and target traditionally undruggable proteins. Furthermore, we will examine their diverse therapeutic applications across different disease types, highlighting recent preclinical and clinical advancements. Finally, the review will address the current challenges in the field, including issues related to biocompatibility, scalability, and regulatory considerations, while discussing future perspectives such as the integration of advanced technologies like stimuli-responsive systems and theranostic approaches. Through this comprehensive analysis, we aim to provide researchers and clinicians with a deeper understanding of nano-PROTACs, highlighting their potential to transform disease therapy and improve patient outcomes. As we stand at the forefront of this exciting field, nano-PROTACs represent not just an incremental advancement, but a revolutionary approach that could redefine our strategies for combating disease. The convergence of targeted protein degradation and nanotechnology in nano-PROTACs offers unprecedented opportunities for precision medicine, potentially paving the way for more effective and personalized disease treatments with reduced side effects and improved therapeutic outcomes.

## Design and mechanism of nano-PROTACs
Nano-PROTACs represent a transformative integration of nanotechnology and PROTAC technology, addressing key limitations of conventional PROTACs through enhanced delivery and targeting capabilities [ ]. This innovative platform comprises three core components: (1) a nanocarrier delivery system, (2) the PROTAC molecule (target protein ligand, E3 ubiquitin ligase ligand, and linker), and (3) optional tumor-specific targeting ligands. The nanocarrier system, which encapsulates or conjugates the PROTAC molecule, provides protection against degradation and enables targeted delivery, while targeting ligands (e.g., antibodies or peptides) enhance tumor specificity through recognition of cancer cell surface markers.
The mechanism of action of nano-PROTACs involves three sequential phases: delivery, intracellular action, and protein degradation [ ]. During delivery, the nanocarrier system facilitates tumor accumulation via passive (EPR effect) or active (ligand-receptor interactions) targeting while protecting the PROTAC molecule from degradation. Following cellular internalization, typically through endocytosis, the PROTAC molecule is released in response to intracellular stimuli (e.g., pH changes or enzymatic activity). The intracellular action phase involves the formation of a ternary complex, where the PROTAC molecule simultaneously binds to the target protein and recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase, initiating polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of the target protein. This process disrupts critical cancer signaling pathways, leading to cancer cell death through apoptosis or cell cycle arrest.
Various nanocarrier systems have been developed for nano-PROTACs, each offering distinct advantages [ ]. Polymeric NPs, particularly those based on biodegradable polymers like PLGA or PEG, provide excellent biocompatibility and controlled release properties. Lipid-based nanocarriers, including liposomes and solid lipid NPs, offer high drug loading capacity and enhanced cellular uptake. Inorganic NPs, such as gold and mesoporous silica NPs, provide unique optical and magnetic properties for theranostic applications. The selection of nanocarrier systems depends on the physicochemical properties of the PROTAC molecule, desired release kinetics, and specific therapeutic requirements. Recent advancements in hybrid nanocarrier systems have further expanded the potential applications of nano-PROTACs in cancer therapy.
This integration of nanotechnology with PROTAC technology has significantly advanced the field of targeted protein degradation, enabling precise control of PROTAC molecule delivery and release while enhancing tumor-specific targeting. Ongoing research focusing on the development of advanced nanocarrier systems and novel targeting strategies is expected to further optimize the therapeutic potential of nano-PROTACs in cancer treatment.

## The application of nano-PROTACs in disease treatment

## Nano-PROTACs targeting anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)
ALK is a receptor tyrosine kinase that plays a key role in regulating cellular growth and differentiation. Genomic alterations such as translocations, amplifications, or activating mutations in ALK are strongly associated with multiple cancers, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), neuroblastoma, and anaplastic large cell lymphoma, rendering it a significant therapeutic target [ ]. In a notable advance for targeted protein degradation, researchers have developed nano-Click-formed PROTACs (nano-CLIPTACs, Fig. ), a tumor-specific platform designed to overcome central limitations of conventional PROTACs, including off-target effects, suboptimal pharmacokinetics, and the hook effect [ ]. This system employs two bioorthogonally reactive precursors—an ALK-targeting ligand (W4, ALE-TCO) and a CRBN-recruiting moiety (Z2, POM-TZ)—which were individually encapsulated into cyclic RGDfC-peptide-modified liposomes (cRGD-LPs) to enable integrin αvβ3-mediated tumor targeting. In the ALK-positive H3122 NSCLC model, the precursors exhibited rapid intracellular self-assembly via an inverse electron demand Diels-Alder (IEDDA) reaction, forming the active PROTAC WZ42 with a transformation efficiency exceeding 81.9% within 15 min. The resulting nano-CLIPTACs induced potent, dose-dependent degradation of EML4-ALK fusion protein with a DC₅₀ of 175.37 ± 53.24 nM, significantly outperforming both the unencapsulated precursors (DC₅₀ = 359.03 ± 48.29 nM) and the pre-assembled WZ42@cRGD-LPs (DC₅₀ = 620.10 ± 176.90 nM). Critically, the system showed no hook effect up to 10 µM, attributed to the controlled intracellular release of precursors from liposomes, which maintained optimal ternary complex formation. In H3122 xenograft model, treatment with W4@cRGD-LPs (5 mg/kg) and Z2@cRGD-LPs (4.34 mg/kg) every two days led to 77.4% tumor growth inhibition, accompanied by a 30-fold higher intratumoral concentration of WZ42 compared to the unformatted control. Immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry confirmed significant reductions in ALK and p-ALK expression, decreased Ki-67 proliferation marker, and elevated TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells. Safety was validated by the absence of body weight loss or histopathological toxicity in major organs. Together, this platform demonstrates that spatially controlled intracellular synthesis of PROTACs via tumor-targeted nano-assembly enhances degradation efficacy, overcomes the hook effect, and minimizes off-target toxicity—providing a robust and clinically promising strategy for precision protein degradation.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting B-Cell lymphoma extra large (BCL-XL)
BCL-XL, a key anti-apoptotic protein in the B-cell lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) family, critically regulates the intrinsic apoptotic pathway and controls cellular life-cycle [ , ]. In 2025, Zhang et al. developed an integrated protein degradation strategy that merges PROTAC technology with siRNA delivery (Fig. ) [ ]. The researchers constructed a cationic liposomal system co-encapsulating the BCL-XL-targeting PROTAC molecule DT2216 and BCL-XL-specific siRNA (siRNA2), with fluorinated polyethyleneimine (F-PEI) serving as a nucleic acid condensing agent to enhance cellular uptake and endosomal escape. In vitro experiments using human NSCLC H1299 cells demonstrated that the optimized formulation (PC concentration: 3.38 mg/mL; F-PEI/siRNA mass ratio: 30) achieved exceptional synergistic effects: while DT2216-loaded liposomes alone reduced BCL-XL protein levels, the combination with siRNA2 further enhanced degradation efficiency, resulting in near-complete protein ablation. This dual-action system induced significant apoptosis, with the co-delivery liposomes achieving an apoptosis rate of 15.7% and reducing cell viability to 61.0%, substantially outperforming DT2216 alone (89.4% viability) or DT2216-loaded liposomes (83.1% viability). In vivo evaluation in H1299 tumor-bearing BALB/c nude mice (n = 5 per group) receiving peritumoral injections every three days for three weeks revealed potent tumor growth inhibition, with the co-delivery group showing minimal tumor volume increase during the third week compared to substantial growth in the DT2216-only group. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed significantly reduced expression of angiogenesis markers (VEGF, CD31, CD34) and proliferation marker Ki-67 in treated tumors. Importantly, the co-delivery system modulated the tumor immune microenvironment, increasing infiltration of CD4+ (by approximately 2.1-fold) and CD8+ T cells (by approximately 2.8-fold) while promoting polarization of tumor-associated macrophages toward the anti-tumor M1 phenotype (iNOS+ cells increased by approximately 2.5-fold; CD206+ M2 macrophages decreased correspondingly). The system demonstrated excellent biocompatibility with no significant body weight changes or histopathological lesions in major organs, and hemolysis tests confirmed blood compatibility (< 5% hemolysis rate). This platform exemplifies an adaptable nanomedicine strategy that overcomes solubility limitations of PROTACs while enabling synergistic multi-level intervention through simultaneous gene silencing and protein degradation.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting BMI1 proto-oncogene, polycomb ring finger (BMI1)
The BMI1 protein serves as a core component of the Polycomb repressive complex 1 (PRC1), where it facilitates epigenetic silencing through histone H2A ubiquitination. It plays an essential role in maintaining stem cell pluripotency and self-renewal, and its overexpression is closely associated with tumorigenesis, therapy resistance, and unfavorable prognosis in multiple cancers, making it an attractive target for anticancer therapeutics [ , ]. To enhance immunotherapy efficacy in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), Wu et al. (2023) developed a novel injectable nanocomposite hydrogel system (PP-RC-H) that integrates chemo-immunotherapy with targeted protein degradation through a sophisticated dual-delivery mechanism (Fig. 6) [ ]. This thermosensitive PLGA-PEG-PLGA-based hydrogel was engineered to encapsulate two distinct nanoformulations: imiquimod-loaded CaCO₃ NPs (RC, ~ 35 nm) for immune activation, and cancer cell membrane-camouflaged mesoporous silica NPs (PepM@PacC, ~ 210 nm) co-loaded with a BMI1-directed peptide-based PROTAC and paclitaxel. In SCC7 HNSCC tumor-bearing C57BL/6 mouse models, intratumoral injection of PP-RC-H (20 mg/kg every two days for two weeks) demonstrated controlled degradation and sustained release of therapeutic payloads over 7–10 days. The RC NPs effectively neutralized the acidic TME (increasing pH from ~ 6.8 to ~ 7.4 within 2 h) while releasing R837 to promote M2-to-M1 macrophage repolarization (increasing M1 macrophages from ~ 30% to > 70%) and dendritic cell maturation (enhancing mature DCs from 19.9% to 40.5%). Concurrently, the biomimetic PepM@PacC NPs facilitated homologous targeting to cancer cells, with 3.8-fold higher cellular uptake in SCC7 cells compared to normal oral keratinocytes. Intracellular glutathione (GSH, 10 mM) triggered dissociation of the PROTAC peptide from the mesoporous silica framework, achieving efficient BMI1 degradation (> 80% reduction) while simultaneously releasing paclitaxel for apoptosis induction. This multi-mechanistic approach resulted in potent tumor growth inhibition (~ 85% reduction in tumor volume compared to controls) and significant suppression of lung metastasis (~ 75% reduction in metastatic nodules). Immunological profiling revealed enhanced T-cell infiltration with increased CD8+/CD4+ T cell ratios (~ 2.5-fold) and favorable modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment. Comprehensive safety assessment showed no significant body weight changes, normal blood biochemistry parameters, and absence of histopathological lesions in major organs, confirming the favorable biosafety profile of this integrated nanomedicine approach for overcoming HNSCC treatment resistance .

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting bromodomain containing 4 (BRD4)
BRD4, a prominent member of the bromodomain and extraterminal (BET) protein family, functions as an epigenetic reader that binds acetylated lysine residues on histones to modulate transcriptional activation and elongation. It is critically involved in processes such as cell cycle regulation, inflammatory response, and oncogenesis, and has thus emerged as a compelling therapeutic target for diverse cancers, including both hematologic and solid tumors [ , ]. In 2022, an innovative nano-PROTAC platform named CREATE (Fig. ) was developed to address BRD4 overexpression in lung cancer through a dual-targeting strategy. The system integrates pH/GSH-responsive DS-PLGA copolymers with engineered lung cancer cell membranes (CRV-LLCM), forming a biohybrid delivery vehicle capable of simultaneous targeting of both Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) cells and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) [ ]. Under the acidic (pH 5.0–6.5.0.5) and high-GSH (2–10 × 10−³ M) conditions of the TME, CREATE undergoes controlled disassembly, leading to the rapid release of the encapsulated BRD4-directed PROTAC molecule, dBET6. This triggered release promoted efficient degradation of BRD4 and induced caspase-3-dependent apoptosis in both target cell types, as demonstrated in vitro using LLC cells and IL-4-induced M2-like macrophages (Raw 264.7 and BMDMs). In vivo, CREATE administration (5 mg kg−¹ dBET6 equivalent, intravenous injection every 2 days) resulted in significant tumor growth inhibition—reducing tumor volume to approximately 1/30 of the saline control group in a subcutaneous LLC allograft model (C57BL/6 mice). In an orthotopic lung cancer model, CREATE treatment led to a 90% survival rate at 18 days, compared to nearly 100% mortality in control groups. Immunofluorescence and flow cytometry analyses confirmed a substantial reduction in TAMs (CD206⁺ cells) and cancer cells (TTF1⁺), accompanied by increased infiltration of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells and decreased angiogenesis markers (CD31⁺/α-SMA⁺). No significant body weight loss or histopathological abnormalities in major organs were observed, indicating a favorable safety profile. By concurrently engaging cancer cells and reprogramming the immune microenvironment, CREATE represents a substantial advance in lung cancer therapy and offers a promising combinatorial strategy for enhancing PROTAC efficacy through nanotechnology-enabled TME remodeling.

 
In 2022, Gao et al. introduced POLY-PROTAC—an innovative nanoscale platform (Fig. ) designed to enhance the efficacy and specificity of targeted protein degradation in cancer treatment [ ]. The system was constructed using an amphiphilic diblock copolymer covalently conjugated with VHL-based BRD4 PROTACs (e.g., ARV771 or MZ1) via reduction-sensitive disulfide bonds, which self-assembled into uniform micellar NPs with an average hydrodynamic diameter of 55 nm. The NPs were further functionalized with an MMP-2-cleavable PEG corona (GPLGLAG peptide) and acid-ionizable 2-(diisopropylamino)ethyl methacrylate (DPA) units, enabling sequential responsiveness to extracellular MMP-2, acidic pH (6.0–6.5.0.5), and intracellular GSH (10 mM). In vitro studies using MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells demonstrated that MMP-2 pretreatment enhanced cellular uptake of the sheddable NPs (PGD7) by approximately 2.8-fold compared to non-sheddable controls (PDA7), as quantified by flow cytometry. The NPs also exhibited improved penetration in 3D multicellular spheroids, with a ~ 3.0-fold higher fluorescence intensity at a depth of 50 μm. Under reductive conditions mimicking the intracellular environment (10 mM GSH, pH 6.0), over 50% of ARV771 was released within 4 h. Western blot analysis confirmed efficient BRD4 degradation with DC₅₀ values as low as that of free ARV771 (e.g., 1.9-fold lower for PGD7 than its non-sheddable counterpart), and proteasome inhibitor MG132 abolished this effect, validating a UPS-dependent mechanism. Cell viability assays (CCK-8) revealed IC₅₀ values of 0.25 µM for PGD7, comparable to free ARV771 (0.17 µM) and significantly lower than the BRD4 inhibitor JQ1 (7.43 µM). In vivo, following intravenous administration (10 mg/kg ARV771 equivalent) in MDA-MB-231 tumor-bearing BALB/c nude mice, the PGD7 NPs showed 2.0-fold higher tumor accumulation at 36 h post-injection compared to non-sheddable PDA7 NPs, as evidenced by fluorescence imaging. Treatment with PGD7 NPs every 3 days for five cycles led to ~ 50% tumor growth inhibition and significant prolongation of survival relative to free ARV771 or PBS controls. Immunoblot and immunohistochemical analyses of tumor tissues confirmed 80% BRD4 degradation and 1.5-fold higher caspase-3 activation in the PGD7 group, indicating enhanced apoptosis induction. No significant body weight loss or histopathological abnormalities in major organs were observed, supporting the biosafety of the formulation. To further augment tumor-specific delivery, the authors implemented a bioorthogonal pretargeting strategy using DBCO-loaded, pH-activatable PED NPs administered prior to azide-functionalized POLY-PROTAC (N₃@PGD7) NPs. This approach enhanced intratumoral ARV771 accumulation by 3.9-fold and 1.9-fold compared to free ARV771 and N₃@PGD7 alone, respectively, as quantified by HPLC. When combined with photodynamic therapy (PDT; 671 nm laser, 400 mW/cm², 5 min), the PED + N₃@PGD7 + Laser regimen induced 95% tumor regression and extended survival by 40% at 100 days, with synergistic activation of caspase-3 and depletion of BRD4 confirmed by Western blot and IHC. By integrating stimuli-responsive nanomaterials with precision protein degradation, the POLY-PROTAC platform represents a robust and translatable strategy to overcome the pharmacokinetic and specificity limitations of conventional PROTACs.

 
The emergence of two-dimensional nano-PROTACs (2D-nano-PROTACs, Fig. ) marks a substantial progression in targeted protein degradation therapeutics. In 2022, Liu et al. developed 2D-PROTACs (denoted as T-ARV), a platform based on ultra-thin tantalum telluride (TaTe₂) nanosheets loaded with the PROTAC molecule ARV-825 [ ]. The nanosheets, synthesized via liquid-phase exfoliation, exhibited a lateral size of 200 nm and a thickness of 1 nm, providing a large specific surface area for high-density ARV-825 loading via π-π stacking and van der Waals interactions. This structural design significantly increased the local concentration of PROTAC molecules and enhanced the spatial proximity between BRD4 and the E3 ubiquitin ligase, thereby promoting ternary complex formation and augmenting UPS-mediated degradation. In vitro studies using oral squamous cell carcinoma (CAL-27) cells demonstrated that T-ARV (at 100 µg/mL TaTe₂ and 5 µg/mL ARV-825) induced significantly stronger degradation of BRD4 and its downstream effector c-MYC compared to free ARV-825, as quantified by Western blot. Under near-infrared (NIR) irradiation (808 nm, 1.5 W/cm², 3 min), T-ARV exhibited potent photothermal (photothermal conversion efficiency: 62.3%) and photodynamic (generating •O₂⁻ and ¹O₂) effects, leading to synergistic tumor cell killing with > 95% reduction in viability. In vivo, using CAL-27 tumor-bearing nude mice, T-ARV (2 mg/kg TaTe₂, 50 µg/kg ARV-825) administered intravenously achieved efficient tumor accumulation, as visualized via PT-OCT imaging with micron-scale resolution, and showed peak intratumoral distribution at 12 h post-injection. When combined with NIR irradiation (1.5 W/cm², 8 min), T-ARV treatment led to near-complete tumor regression, with tumor volume reduced to < 10% of the initial size by day 14, significantly outperforming free ARV-825 or TaTe₂ alone. Immunohistochemistry and TUNEL staining of excised tumor tissues confirmed marked BRD4 and c-MYC degradation and extensive apoptosis in the T-ARV + Laser group. Systemic safety was validated by stable body weight, normal hematological parameters, and absence of histopathological damage in major organs. By integrating high-efficiency protein degradation, multimodal therapy, and real-time imaging within a single nanoplatform, 2D-PROTACs exemplify a versatile and translatable strategy for image-guided precision oncology, bridging advanced material design with targeted proteolysis technology.

 
In 2023, Wang et al. reported a significant advance in precision medicine with the development of NAP (Fig. )—a NIR photoactivatable nano-PROTAC platform enabling spatiotemporal control over protein degradation [ ]. The system was constructed through molecular self-assembly of an amphiphilic conjugate composed of the ARV-771 linked to a NIR photosensitizer (phosphorbide a, PA) via a ¹O₂-cleavable thioketal (TK) linker. This design yielded uniform spherical NPs (80 nm in diameter) with a critical micelle concentration of 2.21 ± 0.36 µM, indicating favorable self-assembly and colloidal stability under physiological conditions. Following intravenous administration in MCF-7 tumor-bearing mice, NAP exhibited enhanced tumor accumulation through the EPR effect, with peak fluorescence intensity observed at 8 h post-injection—a 13.2-fold increase over background. Upon localized NIR irradiation (670 nm, 300 mW/cm², 5 min) at the tumor site, the photosensitizer generated ¹O₂, which cleaved the TK linker and triggered the release of active ARV-771. This process was confirmed by HPLC, showing 85% release efficiency within 12 h post-irradiation. In vitro studies using MCF-7 cells demonstrated that NIR irradiation restored the proteolytic function of NAP, leading to dose-dependent degradation of BRD4 (65–97% at 0.5–5.5 µM) and other BET family proteins, without affecting BRD4 levels in the dark. This degradation was dependent on the UPS and VHL E3 ligase, as confirmed by inhibition and knockdown experiments. The combination of BRD4 degradation and PDT resulted in synergistic anticancer effects, with an IC₅₀ value of 0.15 ± 0.01 µM for NAP + NIR, significantly lower than that of NCP (0.64 ± 0.13 µM) or ARV-771 alone (0.32 ± 0.05 µM). In vivo, NAP + NIR treatment led to a marked reduction in tumor volume (337 mm³ at day 16) compared to ARV-771 (810 mm³), NCP + NIR (895 mm³), or PBS controls (1635 mm³), corresponding to a tumor inhibition rate of 83.4%. Western blot and immunohistochemical analyses confirmed significant BRD4 degradation only in the NAP + NIR group. H&E and TUNEL staining revealed extensive tumor cell apoptosis, while no significant body weight loss or organ damage was observed, indicating a favorable safety profile. By integrating photodynamic activation with targeted protein degradation, the NAP platform enables precise, externally controllable proteolysis within the TME, effectively addressing limitations of conventional PROTACs such as off-target toxicity and poor spatiotemporal control. This work establishes a promising paradigm for image-guided, personalized degradation-based therapies in oncology and beyond.

 
In 2023, Liu et al. developed ARV@PDSA, a GSH-responsive nano-PROTAC system (Fig. ) designed to overcome the poor bioavailability and off-target risks of conventional PROTACs [ ]. The platform was constructed using redox-sensitive poly(disulfide amide) (PDSA) polymers to encapsulate ARV-771 with a high loading efficiency of 85.3% and a loading capacity of 6.8%. The resulting NPs exhibited uniform spherical morphology (118 nm in diameter) and demonstrated excellent colloidal stability under physiological conditions. In vitro studies using human cervical cancer HeLa and murine melanoma B16F10 cell lines revealed that ARV@PDSA significantly enhanced cellular uptake and promoted rapid, dose-dependent degradation of BRD4. The DC₅₀ of ARV@PDSA for BRD4 was 21.5 nM, lower than that of free ARV-771 (32.9 nM). This was accompanied by downregulation of the oncoprotein c-Myc, with a DC₅₀ of 35.7 nM for ARV@PDSA compared to 51.9 nM for free ARV-771. The system also induced GSH depletion, which amplified c-Myc-related ferroptosis and G1-phase cell cycle arrest, leading to enhanced cytotoxicity and inhibition of colony formation in both 2D and 3D tumor spheroid models. In vivo, ARV@PDSA showed improved tumor accumulation in HeLa and B16F10 subcutaneous xenograft mouse models, with a 2.7-fold higher fluorescence intensity in tumors compared to free dye 24 h post-injection. Systemic cytokine analysis showed decreased TGF-β and increased IFN-γ levels, indicating reversal of immunosuppression. This work establishes a sequentially activated, intracellular-targeted delivery strategy for PROTACs, highlighting a promising combinatory approach to overcome ICB resistance in refractory colorectal cancer.

## Nano-PROTACs targeting cyclooxygenase-1/2 (COX-1/2)
COX-1/2 are key enzymes that catalyze the conversion of arachidonic acid to prostaglandins, which play critical roles in inflammation, pain, and cellular homeostasis. COX-1 is constitutively expressed in most tissues and supports physiological functions, while COX-2 is inducible and associated with pathological processes such as inflammation and tumor progression, making both enzymes significant therapeutic targets for anti-inflammatory and anticancer drugs [ , ]. Capitalizing on the modular design of nano-PROTAC systems, Zhang et al. developed SPNCOX (Fig. ), an advanced activatable platform that targets COX-1/2 using a peptide-based PROTAC (CPP) conjugated to a photodynamic semiconducting polymer core (PCB) via a CatB-cleavable linker [ ]. Targeting COX-1/2 was strategically selected due to its central role in prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) synthesis, which fosters an immunosuppressive TME by recruiting regulatory immune cells including M2 macrophages, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), and regulatory T cells (Tregs). SPNCOX operates through a dual mechanism: it facilitates ubiquitin–proteasome-dependent degradation of COX-1/2, thereby inhibiting PGE₂ production and mitigating immunosuppression, while the polymer core acts as a photosensitizer that generates cytotoxic ¹O₂ under 808 nm laser irradiation (0.3 W cm−²), inducing PDT and ICD. This combined action promotes robust antitumor immunity through metabolic interference and localized photoimmunotherapy. In vitro studies using 4T1 murine breast cancer cells demonstrated that SPNCOX treatment led to a significant reduction in COX-1 and COX-2 expression by 92.0% and 89.1%, respectively, and decreased extracellular PGE₂ levels by 90.9%, as confirmed by immunofluorescence and ELISA. Flow cytometry analysis revealed a substantial increase in cytotoxic T cells and granzyme B⁺ T cells in tumors, along with a decrease in immunosuppressive cells: Tregs by 82.9%, M2 macrophages by 36.2%, and MDSCs by 72.7%. The ratio of effector T cells to Tregs increased by 13.3-fold, indicating a profound reprogramming of the TME. Moreover, SPNCOX exhibited excellent biosafety, with no significant changes in body weight or histopathological alterations in major organs. A tumor rechallenge study further confirmed long-term immunity, with SPNCOX-treated mice showing significantly delayed tumor growth and improved survival. As the first PROTAC-based platform designed to simultaneously disrupt immunosuppressive metabolic pathways and enhance photodynamic antitumor responses, SPNCOX establishes a novel paradigm in immunotherapy and underscores the potential of stimuli-responsive nanoformulations for precision modulation of the TME.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH)
DHODH is a mitochondrial enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting step of de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis, playing an essential role in nucleotide synthesis and cellular proliferation. This work establishes BPNpro as a versatile and stimuli-responsive platform for precise ferroptosis induction, highlighting the potential of combining protein degradation with nanotechnology to advance targeted cancer therapy.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)
EGFR is a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor that plays a central role in regulating cellular proliferation, survival, and differentiation. Dysregulation of EGFR—through overexpression, mutation, or gene amplification—is a well-established driver in numerous cancers, rendering it a major target for therapeutic intervention [ , ]. In a notable advance, Zhang et al. developed an intracellular self-assembling nano-PROTAC platform (Fig. ) to overcome persistent challenges of conventional PROTACs [ ]. This system consists of two peptide-based precursors, PPOI and PE3, which are activated by the high GSH concentrations in tumor cells (e.g., A549 and LNCap lines) to undergo Cu⁺-catalyzed click reactions and self-assemble into fibrillar nanostructures via hydrogen bonding. In vitro, these nanoassemblies enabled dose-dependent degradation of multiple oncogenic targets—including EGFR and androgen receptor (AR)—with efficiencies up to 95% and sustained effects over 72 h, as confirmed by immunoblotting and flow cytometry. The formation of stable polynary complexes (e.g., EGFR₂: Nano-PROTAC: VHL) with a stoichiometry of 2:1 was validated by microscale thermophoresis (MST) and molecular docking, demonstrating positive cooperativity (α = 1.24) and effective bypass of the hook effect across a wide concentration range (5–50 µM). In vivo, following intravenous administration (2.5–10 mg/kg, q.o.d. for five doses) in A549 and LNCap xenograft mouse models, the nano-PROTACs exhibited dose-dependent tumor accumulation and retention for up to 72 h, leading to significant suppression of tumor growth (TGI: 81.4% at 5 mg/kg in A549 model) and target degradation up to 79%. Immunofluorescence and immunoblot analyses of tumor tissues confirmed dose-dependent reductions in EGFR and AR levels. The platform also showed excellent biosafety, with no significant body weight loss, histopathological abnormalities in major organs, or alterations in serum biochemical markers (ALT, AST, ALP, TP, ALB, CRE). This self-assembling nano-PROTAC strategy provides a generalizable and precise platform for targeted protein degradation, with high therapeutic efficacy and a favorable safety profile, highlighting its strong potential for clinical translation.

 
In 2024, Ma et al. reported a novel redox-responsive micellar system self-assembled from a folate-PEG-PROTAC conjugate (Fig. ) [ ]. This construct was designed by tethering a folate-modified PEG chain to an EGFR-directed PROTAC (PRO) via a reducible disulfide linkage, forming spherical micelles (50 nm by TEM, 202 nm by DLS) with high drug loading (80%) and negative surface charge (−7.1 mV). In vitro, in FR-positive HCC-827 and PC-9 cells, the micelles (MPRO) exhibited efficient cellular uptake via folate receptor mediation, as confirmed by flow cytometry and fluorescence imaging, and released active PROTAC under reducing conditions (10 mM DTT), leading to dose-dependent EGFR degradation and potent antiproliferative effects (IC₅₀: 72 nM in HCC-827, 591 nM in PC-9). In contrast, the non-cleavable control (MPRO-C) showed significantly reduced activity (IC₅₀: 588 nM and 8.07 µM, respectively). In vivo, following intravenous administration (15 mg PRO eq./kg, every other day) in HCC-827 xenograft mice, MPRO demonstrated enhanced tumor accumulation (peaking at 24 h) and superior tumor growth inhibition (TGI: 79.6%) compared to free PRO (TGI: 48.1%) and MPRO-C (TGI: 58.2%). Immunohistochemistry and western blot confirmed significant EGFR degradation in tumor tissues, accompanied by reduced Ki67 expression and enhanced apoptosis. The micellar system also exhibited excellent biosafety, with no significant body weight loss, normal histology in major organs, and unaltered serum biochemical markers. This FA-PEG-PROTAC micelle platform represents a robust and tumor-selective strategy to enhance the delivery and efficacy of protein degradation therapeutics, with strong potential for clinical translation.

 
In a 2025 study, Fang et al. developed silica-based nano-PROTACs (SiPROTACs, Fig. ) functionalized with multiple surface ligands designed to target and degrade transmembrane EGFR [ ]. The NPs, approximately 50 nm in diameter, were synthesized via the Stöber method and surface-modified with both CRBN E3 ligase ligands and EGFR-targeting ligands (gefitinib derivatives) via silanol chemistry, followed by liposomal coating (DOPC/cholesterol/DSPE-PEG) to enhance colloidal stability. In EGFR-driven NSCLC cell lines (HCC-827 and PC-9), SiPROTACs induced significant EGFR degradation at concentrations as low as 50 nM, as confirmed by western blot analysis, outperforming conventional small-molecule PROTAC PRO C8, which required 500 nM for detectable degradation. The degradation was mediated via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, as pretreatment with the proteasome inhibitor MG-132 or the competitive EGFR inhibitor gefitinib abolished the effect. Cellular uptake studies in HCC-827 cells using Dil-labeled SiPROTACs showed time-dependent internalization, with significant fluorescence observed after 24 h. In vivo, using female BALB/c nude mice bearing HCC-827 xenografts, SiPROTACs (administered at 15 mg/kg gefitinib-equivalent dose via tail vein every other day for 6 doses) exhibited prolonged tumor accumulation for over 48 h, as visualized by DIR-based NIR imaging. This was attributed to the EPR effect. Treatment with SiPROTACs resulted in significant tumor growth inhibition (average tumor weight: 0.166 g vs. 0.332 g in PBS group), comparable to PRO C8, and induced apoptosis and reduced proliferation. Importantly, SiPROTACs showed no significant body weight loss or organ toxicity, as evidenced by serum biochemical markers (BUN, CRE, ALT, AST) and H&E staining of major organs, indicating a favorable safety profile. These results underscore the potential of nanoscale PROTAC delivery systems to enhance degradation efficacy and therapeutic specificity. Furthermore, this platform technology may be adaptable to other membrane-associated oncoproteins, opening new avenues for targeted protein degradation in NSCLC and other malignancies.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting Estrogen receptor alpha (ERα)
ERα is a ligand-activated nuclear transcription factor that mediates estrogen signaling and influences key physiological processes including development, metabolism, and reproduction. Its abnormal activation or expression is closely linked to breast cancer and other hormone-dependent diseases, establishing ERα as a major target for therapeutic interventions such as selective receptor modulators and degraders [ , ]. In 2023, Cheng et al. reported an innovative microneedle-based delivery platform (Fig. ) designed to overcome central challenges in PROTAC administration, including poor solubility and lack of tissue specificity [ ]. The system co-encapsulated an ERα-directed PROTAC (ERD308) and the CDK4/6 inhibitor Palbociclib within pH-responsive MPEG-poly(β-amino ester) (MPEG-PAE) micelles, which were integrated into a dissolvable hyaluronic acid-based microneedle patch for localized and sustained release into breast tumor tissues. In vitro studies using ER-positive MCF-7 breast cancer cells demonstrated that ERD308 released from the micelles effectively degraded over 90% of ERα, as confirmed by immunofluorescence and western blot analysis. Co-encapsulation with Palbociclib resulted in enhanced anti-proliferative effects, with an IC₅₀ of 20.75 nM for the combination micelles (ERD_Pal), significantly lower than Palbociclib alone (IC₅₀ = 120.22 nM). These findings highlight the potential of transdermal microneedle systems to enhance the delivery efficiency, sustained release, and therapeutic profile of PROTACs, offering a targeted, minimally invasive alternative for cancer treatment. This approach may significantly broaden the applicability of protein degradation therapies in oncology.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting excision repair cross-complementing group 1/xeroderma pigmentosum complementation group f (ERCC1/XPF)
The ERCC1/XPF heterodimer is a structure-specific endonuclease essential for nucleotide excision repair (NER), where it catalyzes the 5’ incision of damaged DNA. Beyond NER, this complex also facilitates the repair of DNA interstrand crosslinks and helps maintain genomic integrity, rendering it an attractive therapeutic target in cancers with dysregulated DNA repair mechanisms—such as those resistant to platinum-based chemotherapy [ , ]. Cisplatin resistance remains a significant obstacle in oncology, frequently mediated by enhanced DNA damage repair activity. To address this, Wang et al. (2025) developed an integrated nanotherapeutic platform, termed HFn-NERiP-Pt(IV) (Fig. ), which combines a GSH-activated PROTAC molecule (NERiP) with a ferritin-based nanocage for coordinated delivery of a Pt(IV) prodrug and targeted degradation of the nucleotide excision repair (NER) complex proteins ERCC1/XPF [ ]. Pharmacokinetic analyses revealed that the half-life of HFn-NERiP-Pt(IV) was extended to 4.9 h, a substantial improvement over free cisplatin, with 4.9-fold higher platinum accumulation in tumor tissues. Safety assessments showed stable levels of liver (ALT, AST) and kidney (BUN, CREA) function markers, confirming reduced off-target toxicity. By concurrently disabling a key DNA repair mechanism and delivering a cytotoxic platinum agent within a single NP, this approach exemplifies a synergistic strategy to reverse chemoresistance. Moreover, the modular design offers potential adaptability for targeting other resistance-related proteins, highlighting broad translational implications for combination nanotherapies in precision oncology.

## Nano-PROTACs targeting hexokinase-2 (HK-2)
HK-2, a pivotal glycolytic enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate, is frequently overexpressed in tumors to sustain aerobic glycolysis and support rapid cancer proliferation. ## Nano-PROTACs targeting indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO)
IDO is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting step of tryptophan degradation via the kynurenine pathway, playing a pivotal role in promoting immune tolerance and tumor immune evasion. Its overexpression in the TME inhibits T-cell function and enhances regulatory T-cell activity, establishing IDO as an attractive target for immunotherapeutic interventions [ , ]. In 2021, Zhang et al. developed a photoactivatable semiconducting polymer nano-PROTAC, designated SPNpro (Fig. ), which synergistically combines targeted protein degradation with photodynamic immunomodulation [ , ]. The system is constructed from a NIR-absorbing semiconducting polymer core conjugated via a CatB-cleavable peptide linker to a PEG-functionalized PROTAC moiety. CatB, a lysosomal protease overexpressed in various malignancies including melanoma, prostate, and breast cancer, serves as a tumor-specific trigger to release the active PROTAC within the TME. Under NIR irradiation (808 nm, 0.3 W/cm²), the polymer core generates cytotoxic ¹O₂, inducing ICD and providing localized PDT. Concurrently, CatB-mediated cleavage enables site-specific PROTAC release, leading to ubiquitin-proteasome-dependent degradation of IDO. This dual mechanism enhances antitumor immunity by simultaneously elevating immunogenicity through PDT and counteracting immunosuppression via IDO elimination. In vitro studies using IFN-γ-stimulated 4T1 murine breast cancer cells demonstrated that SPNpro treatment resulted in approximately 90% reduction in IDO protein levels and about 70% decrease in kynurenine (Kyn) content, as confirmed by immunofluorescence and HPLC analyses. These effects were abrogated upon pretreatment with the proteasome inhibitor epoxomicin or the neddylation inhibitor MLN4924, validating the UPS-dependent mechanism. In vivo evaluation in 4T1-tumor-bearing BALB/c mice showed that intravenous administration of SPNpro (200 µL, [PCB] = 200 µg/mL) followed by NIR irradiation led to significant inhibition of both primary and distant tumor growth, with tumor volumes reduced by approximately 90% compared to saline controls. Immunophenotyping of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes revealed a 2.2-fold increase in CD8⁺ T cells and a 3.0-fold enhancement in granzyme B expression in SPNpro-treated mice relative to control groups, indicating potent activation of antitumor T-cell immunity. Moreover, SPNpro treatment resulted in a 10.9-fold decrease in IDO expression and a 77.2% reduction in intratumoral Kyn levels, confirming effective immunometabolic reprogramming. Histological and biosafety assessments showed no significant changes in body weight or organ morphology, supporting the high biocompatibility of SPNpro. The modular architecture of this platform also allows for the targeting of other immunosuppressive proteins, underscoring its adaptability and potential for broader application in precision immuno-oncology and combination therapies.

 
In 2024, Choi et al. developed a carrier-free light-triggered PROTAC nanoassembly (LPN, Fig. ) for photodegradation of IDO [ ]. The LPN was formed by self-assembly of prodrug conjugates containing an IDO-targeting PROTAC (NLG919 and a VHL-binding peptide), a CatB-cleavable linker (KRR), and the photosensitizer verteporfin (VPF), achieving a high drug loading of 80.76%. In CT26 murine colon carcinoma cells (high CatB expression), LPN treatment (5 µM, 12 h) under light irradiation (40 mW, 500 s) induced a 10.68-fold increase in ROS and significantly reduced both IDO expression and the kynurenine/tryptophan (Kyn/Trp) ratio. These effects were abolished by inhibitors of CatB, neddylation, or the proteasome, confirming the enzyme-triggered and UPS-dependent mechanism. In CT26 tumor-bearing BALB/c mice, intravenous injection of LPNs (3 mg kg−¹ VPF equivalent) resulted in enhanced tumor accumulation via the EPR effect. With NIR irradiation (200 mW, 15 min) at 3 h post-injection, LPN treatment achieved 100% complete tumor regression within 8 days. Immune profiling revealed a 1.28-fold increase in tumor-infiltrating cytotoxic T cells, a 1.26-fold increase in mature dendritic cells, and a reduction in regulatory Tregs to 57.2% of the VPF (+ L) group levels. The LPN platform also demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no significant body weight loss or organ damage observed even at doses up to 15 mg kg−¹. This carrier-free nanoplatform effectively combines photodynamic therapy with precision protein degradation, offering a targeted combinatorial strategy with enhanced efficacy and reduced off-target risks.

## Nano-PROTACs targeting mesenchymal to epithelial transition factor (MET)
The MET protein, a receptor tyrosine kinase encoded by the proto-oncogene c-MET, binds primarily to hepatocyte growth factor (HGF). Dysregulation of MET signaling contributes critically to tumor progression by enhancing cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis, thereby establishing it as a valuable therapeutic target in oncology [ , ]. In 2025, Jin et al. developed a covalent nano-targeting chimera—named Nano-APTAC (Fig. )—by conjugating protein-specific aptamers to graphene oxide (GO) via amide bonds [ ]. Using HeLa and A549 cell lines, the authors demonstrated that GO-Met chimeras (targeting the membrane protein Met) and GO-RelA chimeras (targeting the cytoplasmic protein p65) induced 52% and significant degradation of their respective targets within 24 h at concentrations of 80 nM and 200 nM, respectively. This modular platform allows for programmable design by simply exchanging aptamers, enabling degradation of proteins regardless of localization. The strategy also mitigates single-pathway overload and enhances targeting specificity, offering a versatile and adaptable tool for precision oncology.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT)
NAMPT is the rate-limiting enzyme in the NAD⁺ salvage pathway and plays a vital role in cellular energy metabolism and redox balance. Its overexpression is associated with a range of pathologies, including cancer, inflammatory conditions, and metabolic syndromes, positioning it as an attractive target for pharmacological intervention using inhibitors or degraders [ , ]. In 2024, Wang et al. developed a dual-programmable semiconducting polymer nano-PROTAC system named SPNFeP (Fig. ), which integrates sonodynamic therapy (SDT) and ferroptosis induction for precision cancer immunotherapy [ ]. This platform overcomes the challenge of spatiotemporal control in PROTAC activation, particularly in deep-seated tumors. SPNFeP comprises a semiconducting polymer (PFODBT) as a sonosensitizer, ferrocene as a ferroptosis inducer, and a NAMPT-targeting PROTAC molecule, co-assembled via nano-precipitation with a ROS-cleavable polymer (DSPE-TK-PEG). Under ultrasound irradiation (1.0 W/cm², 10 min) in the presence of H₂O₂ (100 µM), the system generates both ¹O₂ (via sonodynamic processes) and ·OH (via Fenton chemistry), leading to 93.2% release of PROTAC from the NPs. This ROS-amplified activation induced significant cytotoxicity in CT26 cells, reducing cell viability to 23% under combined US and H₂O₂ treatment. The combination of SDT and ferroptosis also triggered ICD, as evidenced by increased CRT exposure (≥ 5.6-fold), ATP release (3.5-fold), and HMGB1 release. In subcutaneous and 2 cm tissue-covered bilateral CT26 tumor mouse models, a single intravenous injection of SPNFeP (350 µg/mL) followed by US irradiation at 24 h led to complete tumor eradication in both primary and distant tumors, with a 100% survival rate over 55 days. The treatment also induced potent immune activation, increasing CD3⁺/CD8⁺ T cell infiltration in primary tumors to 33.3% and reducing MDSC populations to 26.8%, confirming enhanced antitumor immunity. Mechanistically, SPNFeP-mediated NAMPT degradation and subsequent STAT3 downregulation were confirmed by western blot and immunofluorescence in tumor tissues. The system also exhibited good biosafety, with no significant changes in body weight, blood parameters, or organ histology. This work introduces a programmable and combinatory nano-PROTAC platform that leverages dual ROS sources for spatiotemporally controlled PROTAC activation, ferroptosis, and immunotherapy, offering a versatile and translatable strategy for treating deep-seated and immunologically cold tumors.

 
In 2024, He et al. introduced a novel prodrug strategy termed “drugtamer-PROTAC conjugation” (Fig. ), which addresses major limitations of conventional PROTACs such as poor tumor selectivity and off-target toxicity [ ]. The conjugate AS-2 F-NP was constructed by covalently linking a nucleolin-targeting aptamer AS1411, two fluorouridine (Fdu) nucleotides—structurally mimicking thymidine—and a NAMPT-targeting PROTAC NP via a GSH-cleavable disulfide linker. In MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells, AS-2 F-NP demonstrated enhanced cellular uptake via macropinocytosis, with flow cytometry confirming specific recognition and confocal microscopy revealing rapid internalization. Under reducing conditions mimicking the tumor microenvironment, 63.7% of NP was released within 2 h. The conjugate exhibited superior NAMPT degradation (DC₅₀ = 18 nM, Dmax >90%) compared to NP alone (DC₅₀ = 70 nM) and the non-targeted control CRO-2 F-NP (DC₅₀ = 133 nM). It also showed enhanced cytotoxicity (IC₅₀ = 46 nM) relative to free NP (116 nM), FU (1016 nM), or their combination, and induced apoptosis in 68.4% of cells at 100 nM. In MDA-MB-231 xenograft mouse models, intravenous administration of AS-2 F-NP (10 µM/kg) every other day resulted in > 92% TGI after 19 days, outperforming the combination of FU and NP (TGI = 78.8%) and reducing tumor volume significantly without body weight loss. Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence confirmed decreased NAMPT and Ki67 expression, increased TUNEL-positive cells, and downregulation of p65 and Erk in tumor tissues. The conjugate also showed improved tumor targeting and retention in fluorescence imaging, with minimal accumulation in normal organs and no signs of organ damage in H&E-stained sections. By integrating aptamer-mediated targeting, GSH-responsive release, and synergistic NAMPT degradation with Fdu cytotoxicity, this drugtamer-PROTAC platform enables precise, tumor-specific co-delivery and enhanced therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting tumor protein p53 (p53)
The tumor suppressor p53 plays a critical role in maintaining genomic integrity by regulating key cellular processes including cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, and programmed cell death. Dysregulation or mutation of p53 is closely associated with tumor development, progression, and resistance to conventional therapies [ , ]. In 2025, Wang et al. developed an innovative cuproptosis sensitizer named CuS-MD@CS (Fig. ), which leverages a pH- and NIR laser-dual-responsive nano-PROTAC platform [ ]. This system was constructed using copper sulfide NPs (CuS) as a carrier to load the PROTAC molecule MD-224, followed by surface coating with chondroitin sulfate (CS) to enhance tumor targeting via CD44 receptor-mediated endocytosis. Under the acidic TME and 808 nm laser irradiation, CuS-MD@CS rapidly released copper ions and MD-224. The copper ions not only directly triggered cuproptosis but also consumed intracellular GSH and generated ROS via a Fenton-like reaction. Meanwhile, the released MD-224 promoted ubiquitin-proteasome-dependent degradation of MDM2, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that suppresses p53, leading to p53 upregulation. Elevated p53 expression subsequently inhibited glycolysis (e.g., via GLUT1 downregulation), enhanced mitochondrial respiration, and further reduced GSH levels—collectively sensitizing A549 lung cancer cells to cuproptosis. In vitro studies using A549, 4T1, and PC3 cell lines demonstrated that CuS-MD@CS + laser treatment resulted in a synergistic antitumor effect, with an IC₅₀ of 37.13 µg/mL in A549 cells and a combination index (CI) as low as 0.237 at 200 µg/mL. The treatment also induced 36.6% GSH depletion, 41% reduction in glucose uptake, and significant DLAT oligomerization—a hallmark of cuproptosis. Apoptosis rates reached 33.37% under laser irradiation, as confirmed by Annexin V/PI staining. In vivo evaluations in A549 tumor-bearing BALB/c nude mice (n = 5 per group) showed that CuS-MD@CS + laser treatment led to near-complete tumor eradication, with > 90% tumor growth inhibition compared to the PBS control. Biodistribution studies revealed a 2.0-fold higher tumor accumulation of CuS-MD@CS over non-targeted controls at 24 h post-injection. Safety assessments, including hematological, serum biochemical, and H&E staining of major organs, indicated no significant systemic toxicity, confirming the high biocompatibility of the nanosystem. These findings establish CuS-MD@CS as a novel PROTAC-based cuproptosis sensitizer that effectively combines metabolic reprogramming with dual-modal cell death induction, offering a promising combinatorial strategy for lung cancer treatment.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting E1A-binding protein p300 (p300)
The p300 protein is a critical transcriptional coactivator that plays a central role in regulating gene expression. It modulates chromatin architecture through histone acetylation and participates in diverse cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Dysregulation of p300 has been closely linked to the pathogenesis of various diseases, especially cancers [ , ]. In a landmark 2024 study, Zhang et al. addressed a critical therapeutic challenge in advanced prostate cancer by developing a novel peptide-based PROTAC (Fig. ) targeting the p300 protein, leveraging AI-guided drug design and a nano-selenium delivery system [ ]. This strategy focused on degrading p300—a key transcriptional coactivator implicated in both AR-dependent and AR-independent prostate cancer progression, including castration-resistant (CRPC) and neuroendocrine (NEPC) subtypes. The PROTAC was constructed using a 15-amino acid peptide (sequence: CPWIWDGDNKDDNSTDGGGSGGGTSPEGPWAWLWP) that binds the cysteine-histidine-rich 1 (CH1) domain of p300, linked to an MDM2-recruiting moiety. This design not only promoted ubiquitin-proteasome-dependent degradation of p300 but also displaced p53 from MDM2, enhancing p53-mediated tumor suppression. The construct was conjugated to nano-selenium particles (178 nm) to improve stability and cellular uptake, exhibiting a serum half-life of 10.19 h and efficient tumor accumulation via the EPR effect. In vitro evaluations in prostate cancer cell lines—including AR-positive (C4-2, CWR22Rv1), AR-negative (PC-3, DU145), and NEPC models (NE1.3, H660)—demonstrated that the p300 PROTAC induced dose-dependent p300 degradation (≥ 80% at 1 µM), suppressed proliferation (IC₅₀ values ranging 0.5–2.5 µM), and promoted apoptosis (up to 40% increase in Annexin V-positive cells). Specificity was confirmed by overlap analysis of differentially expressed genes following p300 knockdown and PROTAC treatment, showing ~ 50–70% overlap in regulated pathways. In vivo, the p300 PROTAC (5 mg/kg, i.p., every other day) significantly inhibited tumor growth in both CWR22Rv1 (AR-positive) and PC-3 (AR-negative) xenograft models (n = 5/group), achieving > 60% tumor growth inhibition versus controls. Immunohistochemistry confirmed reduced p300 and Ki-67 expression and increased TUNEL-positive cells in treated tumors. Biodistribution studies showed sustained drug accumulation in tumor tissue over 72 h, and toxicity assessments in BALB/c mice (15 mg/kg, 3 weeks) revealed no significant changes in liver/kidney function markers or histopathology, supporting its favorable safety profile. This work not only validates p300 degradation as a broad-spectrum strategy for advanced prostate cancer but also exemplifies the synergy of AI-assisted peptide design and nanocarrier delivery in overcoming the limitations of conventional small-molecule PROTACs, offering a versatile therapeutic template for targeting transcription factors in heterogeneous malignancies.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting p21-activated kinase 4 (PAK4)
In a groundbreaking study, Xu et al. identified PAK4 as a promising therapeutic target in renal cancer through multi-omics analysis of clinical samples and systematic investigation of oncogenic signaling pathways. PAK4 was found to be significantly overexpressed in renal cancer patients and plays a critical role in shaping the immunosuppressive TME [ , ]. Leveraging artificial intelligence-assisted peptide drug design, the research team developed a novel peptide-based PROTAC molecule (Fig. ), termed PpD, specifically targeting PAK4 for degradation [ ]. To overcome the inherent delivery limitations of peptide drugs, the authors engineered a nano-selenium-based delivery platform, which facilitated efficient in vivo transport and demonstrated a favorable pharmacokinetic profile with a half-life of 9.6 h in BALB/c mice, significantly enhancing tumor accumulation through the EPR effect. This innovative agent exhibited remarkable selectivity, inducing highly specific degradation of PAK4 (with a DC₅₀ of approximately 200 nM in 786-O and Caki-1 renal carcinoma cell lines) without affecting the homologous kinases PAK1, PAK2, or PAK3. The anti-tumor efficacy of PpD was rigorously validated across a comprehensive set of preclinical models. In vitro, PpD potently inhibited cancer cell proliferation with IC₅₀ values of 305.9 nM in 786-O cells and 532.2 nM in Caki-1 cells. In vivo, using 786-O xenograft models in BALB/c nude mice, treatment with PpD (2.5 mg/kg, administered intraperitoneally every two days) resulted in a significant suppression of tumor growth, reducing final tumor weight by approximately 60% compared to the control group. Notably, in immunocompetent Renca syngeneic mouse models, PpD monotherapy demonstrated comparable efficacy to PD-1 antibody treatment and significantly enhanced the infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells into the tumor microenvironment, increasing their proportion from 11.63% in controls to 25.12%. Furthermore, PpD demonstrated robust therapeutic potential in clinically relevant models, including patient-derived organoids (PDO) and mini-patient-derived xenografts (mini-PDX), where it effectively inhibited tumor growth at low micromolar or nanomolar concentrations (e.g., IC₅₀ of 471.4 nM in a PDO model), outperforming existing PAK4 inhibitors. Mechanistic studies confirmed that the anti-cancer effects operate through a dual mode of action: direct suppression of tumor cell proliferation via PAK4 degradation and potentiation of anti-tumor immunity by remodeling the immune microenvironment, as evidenced by RNA-seq analysis revealing enrichment in immune-related pathways such as TNF signaling and interferon response. Comprehensive toxicity assessments, including serum biochemical analysis and histopathological examination of major organs, confirmed no significant adverse effects, supporting its favorable safety profile. This work not only provides a targeted therapeutic strategy that synergizes intrinsic oncogenic inhibition with immune activation but also showcases the successful convergence of AI-based drug discovery and nanotechnology for cancer treatment, establishing a new paradigm for the development of selective and efficacious precision oncology therapeutics.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting Src homology region 2 (SHP2)
SHP2 is a non-receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase that plays a critical role in multiple signaling pathways, including RAS-MAPK, JAK-STAT, and PI3K-AKT, regulating cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Gain-of-function mutations and overexpression of SHP2 are frequently associated with various cancers and developmental disorders, making it an attractive therapeutic target for small-molecule inhibitors and emerging degraders [ , ]. In 2023, Zhang et al. reported a checkpoint-targeting nano-PROTAC termed NPRO (Fig. ), which integrates a photosensitizer (protoporphyrin IX, PpIX) with a caspase 3-cleavable peptide linker and an SHP2-directed PROTAC module [ ]. This system was systematically evaluated in both murine breast cancer 4T1 cells and immunocompetent 4T1 tumor-bearing mice. Under 660 nm light irradiation (30 mW cm−², 6 min), NPRO generated singlet oxygen, leading to significant photodynamic tumor cell killing and ICD, as evidenced by a 76.0% reduction in nuclear HMGB1 fluorescence intensity. Critically, phototherapy-induced caspase 3 activation triggered the cleavage of the DEVD peptide linker, releasing the active PROTAC (aPRO), which promoted ubiquitin-proteasome-dependent degradation of SHP2. This degradation was highly efficient, reducing SHP2 levels by 72.8% in M2 macrophages and 95.3% in CD8⁺ T cells, as quantified by immunofluorescence. Functionally, SHP2 degradation disrupted immunosuppressive CD47-SIRPα and PD-1-PD-L1 signaling, repolarizing M2 to M1 macrophages (M1 proportion increased to 32.7%) and enhancing CD8⁺ T cell cytotoxicity (45.2% of T cells became Granzyme B⁺). In vivo, NPRO (administered intravenously at a dose based on 1 mM PpIX equivalent) exhibited efficient tumor accumulation, as confirmed by fluorescence imaging, and when combined with light irradiation, led to profound tumor growth inhibition. Immune profiling of treated tumors revealed a 3.7-fold increase in the M1/M2 macrophage ratio and elevated populations of tumor-infiltrating CD3⁺/CD8⁺ T cells (33.0%) and Granzyme B⁺/CD8⁺ T cells (43.7%). Moreover, NPRO treatment enhanced systemic immune activation, indicated by increased serum levels of IL-6 (4.0-fold) and IFN-γ (2.6-fold). The nanoformulation also demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no significant body weight loss or histopathological abnormalities in major organs observed over 14 days. By conjugating the PROTAC peptide to a self-assembled nano-selenium or similar carrier, the system improved intracellular delivery and tumor-specific activation, showcasing a robust and activatable platform that synergizes phototherapy with targeted protein degradation to remodel the tumor immune microenvironment.

 

## Nano-PROTACs targeting signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3)
STAT3 is a key transcription factor that regulates processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and immune response. Its constitutive activation is closely linked to tumor development, metastasis, and treatment resistance across various cancers, establishing it as a prominent target for novel anticancer strategies [ , ]. In 2024, Wang et al. developed a liposomal STAT3-degrading PROTAC prodrug (Fig. ) that specifically targets hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) stem cells (CSCs) through a dual mechanism of chemical reprogramming and immune activation [ ]. The prodrug, inS3-TEG-VL-TK, consists of a STAT3-binding peptide (inS3) linked to a TK-caged VHL E3 ligase ligand via a tetraethylene glycol (TEG) spacer, optimized for ternary complex formation. This construct was encapsulated in cRGD-modified cationic liposomes with an average diameter of ≈ 103 nm, achieving a high loading efficiency of 88.6%. In vitro studies using hepa1-6- and huh7-derived CSCs demonstrated that the liposomal formulation (Lip@inS3-TEG-VL-TK) enhanced cellular uptake by over 4-fold compared to the free PROTAC, facilitated lysosomal escape via the proton sponge effect, and was selectively activated by elevated intracellular ROS to induce STAT3 degradation. At a concentration of 3.2 µM, the system achieved near-complete STAT3 depletion, as confirmed by western blot and immunofluorescence, and reduced the expression of stemness markers (CD44 and CD133) by approximately 25–29%. Functional assays revealed potent inhibition of CSC spheroid formation (82% reduction) and migration (85% reduction). In immunocompetent mice bearing hepa1-6-luc-derived tumors, intravenous administration of Lip-cRGD@inS3-TEG-VL-TK (dose equivalent to 5.8% w/w PROTAC loading) led to significant tumor accumulation and suppression, with final tumor volumes of 0.20 cm³ and weights of 0.20 g, compared to 1.72 cm³ and 1.4 g in the control group. The treatment also extended median survival to 60 days. Immune profiling showed increased infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells (25.3%) and mature dendritic cells (CD80⁺/CD86⁺, 26.65%), along with elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IFN-γ increased by 280% and 150%, respectively), while reducing immunosuppressive Tregs by 27.25%. The ROS-responsive prodrug design minimized on-target/off-tumor toxicity, as evidenced by stable body weight and normal histology in major organs. Moreover, the liposomal formulation improved pharmacokinetics, extending the blood circulation half-life to 7 h and enhancing tumor-specific accumulation. This work illustrates a targeted, activatable nano-PROTAC strategy that effectively eliminates CSCs and reprograms the tumor immune microenvironment, offering a potent and safe therapeutic modality for HCC.

 

## Challenges and prospects
While nano-PROTACs (Table  ) have demonstrated remarkable potential in preclinical studies, their successful translation to clinical practice hinges on addressing multifaceted challenges—while distinctly differentiating their mechanistic and functional advantages over conventional PROTACs. Unlike small-molecule PROTACs, which are plagued by poor aqueous solubility, limited bioavailability, and off-target effects stemming from passive systemic distribution, nano-PROTACs leverage engineered nanocarriers (e.g., lipid NPs, polymeric micelles, inorganic hybrids) to achieve transformative improvements: active tumor targeting via the EPR effect or ligand-receptor interactions, spatiotemporally controlled release responsive to TME cues (e.g., pH gradients, GSH levels, overexpressed enzymes), and optimized pharmacokinetic profiles with prolonged circulation half-lives. This targeted delivery paradigm not only amplifies on-target protein degradation efficacy but also mitigates the “hook effect” and reduces on-target/off-tumor toxicity—addressing core shortcomings of conventional PROTACs through design-driven innovation.
Yet, the structural and functional complexity of nano-PROTACs introduces unique barriers to clinical advancement. Scalability and reproducibility remain paramount hurdles: the synthesis of heterobifunctional PROTAC molecules, coupled with precise nanocarrier engineering (e.g., controlling drug loading efficiency, colloidal stability, and surface functionalization), demands rigorous control over batch-to-batch variability. While advanced manufacturing techniques such as microfluidics or continuous-flow reactors offer pathways to standardization, aligning these processes with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards—essential for clinical-grade production—requires further optimization of scalable fabrication workflows and quality control metrics.
Regulatory challenges are equally formidable, as nano-PROTACs represent a hybrid class of therapeutics that merges biologics-like targeting with small-molecule degradation mechanisms, creating gaps in existing regulatory frameworks. Agencies such as the FDA and EMA lack tailored guidelines for evaluating these entities, necessitating comprehensive characterization of both the PROTAC component (e.g., degradation specificity, off-target proteomic effects) and the nanocarrier (e.g., biodegradability, long-term biodistribution, immunogenicity). Critical unresolved questions include defining acceptable thresholds for nanocarrier accumulation in non-target organs, assessing the risk of accelerated blood clearance (ABC phenomenon), and standardizing methods to evaluate stimuli-responsive activation in clinical settings. Additionally, the integration of novel E3 ligases or bioorthogonal chemistry requires rigorous assessment of immunogenicity and unintended proteome-wide perturbations, underscoring the need for collaborative efforts between researchers, regulatory bodies, and industry to develop risk-based guidelines that balance innovation with patient safety.
Toxicity and biocompatibility testing must extend beyond conventional rodent models to ensure clinical relevance. Comprehensive toxicological evaluations should include assessments of hematological, hepatic, and renal function, immunotoxicity (e.g., complement activation, cytokine release), and histopathological analysis of major organs—with special attention to carrier-related risks such as NP accumulation in the reticuloendothelial system and chronic inflammatory responses. The development of immunocompetent models and PDXs will better recapitulate human physiological responses, enabling more accurate prediction of on- and off-target toxicity. Furthermore, as nano-PROTACs are increasingly explored in combination with other therapies (e.g., immunotherapy, radiotherapy), studies on synergistic toxicity and drug-drug interactions are imperative to inform safe clinical use.
Looking forward, several strategic directions hold promise for advancing nano-PROTAC technology. The design of multi-stimuli-responsive systems—capable of sequential activation in response to cascading TME cues (e.g., pH followed by ROS or enzymes)—will further enhance spatial-temporal precision, confining PROTAC activity to diseased tissues. Integration of theranostic functionalities (e.g., NIR-II imaging, photoacoustic tracking) will enable real-time monitoring of drug delivery, target engagement, and therapeutic efficacy, supporting personalized treatment adjustments. Expanding the repertoire of tissue-specific E3 ligases (e.g., FEM1B, RNF114) will minimize on-target toxicity in healthy tissues, while engineering nanocarriers to co-deliver synergistic agents (e.g., siRNA, chemotherapeutics) or modulate the TME (e.g., normalize vasculature, reduce hypoxia) will amplify antitumor effects. From a translational and pharmacoeconomic perspective, justifying the high development costs of nano-PROTACs requires demonstrating clear clinical benefits over existing therapies—such as improved response rates in drug-resistant cancers or reduced adverse events. Early engagement with health technology assessment bodies and payers to evaluate cost-effectiveness, particularly in combination regimens, will be critical for market access. Clinical trial designs should prioritize patient stratification based on target protein expression, E3 ligase availability, and TME characteristics to maximize therapeutic response, while establishing robust biomarkers of target engagement and degradation efficacy will facilitate regulatory endorsement and clinical adoption as the first nano-PROTAC candidates advance to Phase I trials.

 

## Conclusions
Nano-PROTACs represent a paradigm-shifting convergence of targeted protein degradation and nanotechnology, fundamentally expanding the boundaries of precision medicine. This review has comprehensively chronicled the rapid evolution of this field—from foundational concepts to a diverse toolkit of nanoformulations engineered to target a broad spectrum of oncoproteins and disease-relevant biomolecules. By ingeniously addressing the core limitations of conventional PROTACs—poor pharmacokinetics, inadequate tumor selectivity, and the hook effect—through rationally designed nanocarriers, this platform has demonstrably enhanced the therapeutic index of protein degradation in preclinical models, unlocking the potential to target “undruggable” proteins and overcome drug resistance.
The transformative power of nano-PROTACs stems from their distinct mechanistic and functional advantages over conventional PROTACs. Unlike free small-molecule PROTACs, which rely on passive diffusion and suffer from suboptimal tissue penetration, nano-PROTACs enable active targeting via surface functionalization (e.g., ligands, antibodies, cell membrane coatings) and spatiotemporally controlled release through intrinsic TME cues or external triggers (e.g., light, ultrasound). This design not only improves tumor accumulation and cellular internalization but also confines proteolytic activity to the disease site, minimizing systemic toxicity and expanding the range of tractable therapeutic targets.
As the field matures, future advancements will likely embrace greater complexity and intelligence: multi-stimuli-responsive systems for precision activation, theranostic integration for real-time treatment monitoring, and tissue-specific E3 ligase engineering to enhance selectivity. The synergistic potential of nano-PROTACs with other therapeutic modalities—including immunotherapy, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy—further positions them as pivotal components of next-generation combination regimens for heterogeneous malignancies.
However, the journey from bench to bedside demands concerted efforts to overcome translational hurdles. Scalable manufacturing must be optimized to meet GMP standards, while standardized characterization protocols and predictive preclinical models (e.g., immunocompetent PDXs) are essential to de-risk clinical development. Regulatory frameworks require refinement to address the unique properties of nano-PROTAC hybrids, and pharmacoeconomic considerations must be proactively addressed to ensure that the advanced efficacy of these therapies translates into accessible, cost-effective options for patients.
In conclusion, nano-PROTACs are more than an incremental advancement—they embody a revolutionary approach to disease treatment that merges the catalytic power of targeted protein degradation with the precision delivery of nanotechnology. By addressing unmet needs in drug solubility, bioavailability, and selectivity, they offer a versatile platform to tackle intractable diseases, from drug-resistant cancers to neurodegenerative disorders. Sustained innovation in material science, drug design, and translational strategy—coupled with interdisciplinary collaboration across academia, industry, and regulatory bodies—will undoubtedly propel nano-PROTACs to the forefront of next-generation therapeutics, ultimately fulfilling the promise of truly personalized and curative medicine.
