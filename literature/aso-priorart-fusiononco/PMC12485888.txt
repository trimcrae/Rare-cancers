## ABSTRACT
A low molecular compound originally developed as an anexelekto inhibitor, TP‐0903, has been highlighted as a promising therapeutic agent for treating chronic lymphocytic leukemia, solid tumors, and drug‐resistant AML. We investigated the in vitro effects of TP‐0903 on a myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)‐derived cell line (MDS‐L) and two myeloid leukemia cell lines. TP‐0903 effectively inhibited cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in all three cell lines. In MDS‐L cells, the PI3K/AKT and JAK/STAT3 pathways were inhibited, suggesting that this may be partly due to decreased direct interactions with hepatocyte growth factor receptor, commonly known as MET. Regarding its effect on the cell cycle, TP‐0903 was found to impact the DNA damage response and cell cycle‐related factors, particularly those centered around Aurora kinases. In MDS‐L cells, inhibition of Aurora A phosphorylation led to decreased levels of BORA, which in turn suppressed polo‐like kinase 1 activation. This suppression hindered mitosis initiation, resulting in cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase. Additionally, chromosomal misregulation caused by Aurora A inhibition appeared to impair cell division and contribute to cell death. Gene expression profiling of MDS‐L revealed changes in the ferroptosis‐related genes, including HMOX1 and transferrin, along with elevated levels of reactive oxygen species and intracellular iron accumulation. These findings suggest the activation of an atypical ferroptosis pathway mediated through the TGF‐β1/SMAD3 signaling pathway. Overall, these data indicate that TP‐0903 may offer a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of refractory hematological malignancies.

TP‐0903 exerts the antitumor effects through diverse cell‐specific mechanisms, including apoptosis, ferroptosis, and cell cycle disruption. These insights underline the potential of TP‐0903 as a therapeutic agent for hematological malignancies and emphasize the need for further research to fully elucidate the molecular targets of TP‐0903 and optimize its clinical applications.

## The Inhibition of AXL and MET Interaction by TP‐0903 May Affect Signaling
AXL cooperates with other receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), including epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), and hepatocyte growth factor receptor (MET), contributing to the pathogenesis of certain diseases [ , , , ]. Phosphorylation of MET was slightly increased in TP‐0903‐treated MDS‐L cells (Figure  ). Protein levels of EGFR and HER2 were extremely low or undetectable. HER2 and MET expression levels did not differ significantly from controls, while EGFR was undetectable (Figure  ). RTK proteins were not detected in HL‐60 or K562 cells.
 AXL and phosphorylated MET were co‐localized in MDS‐L cells in both control and TP‐0903‐treated groups with no significant differences (Figure  ). Using PLA, we observed a significant reduction in the interaction between phosphorylated MET and AXL in the TP‐0903 group, indicated by decreased dot count and fluorescence intensity per cell (p < 0.01) (Figure  ). No significant changes were seen between AXL and phosphorylated HER2 (Figure  ). These results suggest that TP‐0903 disrupts the interaction between AXL and phosphorylated MET, potentially affecting downstream signaling.

## 
TP‐0903 Induces G2/M Arrest Through the Activation of the DNA Damage Response
Since TP‐0903 has been reported to affect the cell cycle, we performed a cell cycle analysis of TP‐0903‐treated MDS‐L, HL‐60, and K562 cells, which revealed an accumulation of cells in the G2/M phase (Figure  ). Morphological observations and quantification of mitotic cells (mitotic index) were conducted using May–Grünwald–Giemsa staining (Figure  ). Large multinucleated cells were observed in all three cell lines, with K562 cells displaying distinct donut‐shaped nuclei. Notably, the CD34+ cells did not exhibit any significant morphological changes following TP‐0903 treatment. The mitotic index decreased in MDS‐L cells treated with 50 nM TP‐0903 compared to controls (2.5% vs. 6.2%), and a similar trend was observed in HL‐60 (2.5% vs. 5.0%) and K562 (1.8% vs. 4.8%) cells treated with 25 nM TP‐0903. TP‐0903 treatment increased phosphorylation of histone H2AX at Ser139 (γH2AX), indicating activation of the DNA damage response, which initiates DNA repair, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis (Figure  ).
 In MDS‐L cells, cell cycle modulation was mediated via the TGF‐β1/SMAD3 signaling pathway (Figure  ). In HL‐60 cells, TGF‐β1 expression remained unchanged, whereas CDKN1A was significantly upregulated (Figure  ). Both MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells exhibited marked reductions in c‐MYC and Cyclin A2 levels, indicative of Cyclin A/CDK1 complex inhibition and mitotic arrest (Figure  ). Conversely, K562 cells showed no alterations in these factors, suggesting the involvement of distinct mechanisms in G2/M arrest.

## 
TP‐0903 Inhibits Aurora A Activity, Disrupting the Cell Cycle
After demonstrating that TP‐0903 inhibits cell cycle progression, particularly during the M phase, we next investigated whether TP‐0903 affects aurora kinases and PLK1, both of which play key roles in mitosis and cytokinesis.
PLK1 localizes to centrosomes and spindle poles and is crucial for the G2/M phase transition by supporting centrosome maturation and spindle formation [ ]. In this study, phosphorylated PLK1 expression decreased across all three cell lines, while total PLK1 levels varied by cell line (Figure  ). Aurora A, which promotes PLK1 phosphorylation to initiate mitosis, showed unchanged total expression, but phosphorylated Aurora A and B levels were significantly reduced (Figure  ). Aurora A activity is critical during mitosis; it decreases significantly in the G1 phase and localizes to centrosomes during prophase, microtubules during metaphase, and the midzone during cytokinesis [ , ]. Following TP‐0903 treatment, the number of MDS‐L cells undergoing normal division decreased during both metaphase and anaphase. Additionally, a significant increase in abnormal metaphase cells (misaligned cells) was observed in both MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells (Figure  ). K562 cells predominantly displayed nuclear irregularities, resulting in fewer mitotic cells and complicating detailed evaluation.
 Among the tested cell lines, MDS‐L cells exhibited a significant reduction in phosphorylated Aurora A and a marked elevation in cells with abnormal Aurora A localization. To further investigate whether TP‐0903 inhibits Aurora A activity, we evaluated MDS‐L shAXL cells (Figure  ). These cells showed suppressed Aurora A phosphorylation (Figure  ) and displayed structural abnormalities, including centrosome immaturity and spindle misalignment (Figure  ). These findings suggest that TP‐0903 strongly inhibits Aurora A activity by targeting this kinase, particularly in MDS‐L cells.
 The scaffold proteins, BORA and TPX2, which enable Aurora A to phosphorylate PLK1 during the G2/M phase, exhibited distinct expression patterns among cell lines. BORA kinase forms a complex with Aurora A in the G2 phase, activating PLK1 during mitosis. Activated PLK1 promotes BORA degradation, establishing a feedback loop critical for proper cell division [ , ]. Conversely, TPX2 stimulates microtubule formation during mitosis, stabilizing and activating Aurora A, thereby ensuring proper spindle localization [ , ]. The expression of BORA and TPX2 decreased in MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells (Figure  ). Conversely, K562 cells showed no change in BORA expression, despite increased TPX2 expression (Figure  ). These results suggest that BORA in MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells and TPX2 in K562 cells work together with Aurora A and PLK1 to regulate G2/M arrest. This regulation ultimately leads to the inhibition of mitosis and cell death.
To determine whether the G2/M arrest induced by TP‐0903 was primarily due to Aurora A inhibition or involved other kinases, we conducted comparative experiments using the selective Aurora A inhibitor MLN8237. Growth inhibition was observed in all three cell lines, with IC50 values of 145.9 ± 42 nM (48 h) for MDS‐L, 416.9 ± 268.5 nM (72 h) for HL‐60, and 6070.1 ± 198.6 nM (48 h) for K562. Furthermore, a significant increase in the number of G2/M phase cells was observed (Figure  ), demonstrating that G2/M arrest was specifically attributable to Aurora A inhibition.

3.5

## 
TP‐0903 Induces Ferroptosis in MDS‐L Cells
To investigate the effects of TP‐0903 on gene expression, we performed RNA sequencing on MDS‐L, HL‐60, and K562 cells (Figure  ). Consistent gene expression patterns were observed within each cell line group and validated by qPCR. We identified several genes that were commonly altered in MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells (Table  ; Figure  ); however, ferroptosis‐related gene changes were observed exclusively in MDS‐L cells. Ferroptosis is a form of programmed cell death characterized by the accumulation of lipid peroxides in an iron‐dependent manner. We observed increased ROS generation in TP‐0903‐treated MDS‐L cells (Figure  ), which was consistent with the results of the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (Table  ). Given mitochondrial dysfunction in MDS‐L cells, ROS generation likely occurs predominantly outside the mitochondria. We hypothesized that ROS production was driven by ferroptosis through the TGF‐β1/SMAD3 signaling pathway [ , ]. We observed a marked increase in the intracellular iron levels in TP‐0903‐treated MDS‐L cells (Figure  ). Ferroptosis‐related genes such as HMOX1, FTH1, and FTL were upregulated, while GPX4, which suppresses ferroptosis, showed no significant changes (Figure  ). These findings suggested that the Fenton reaction was enhanced, leading to ROS generation and ferroptosis (Figure  ). Additionally, the increased expression of genes that promote lipid peroxidation (Figure  ) further supported the induction of ferroptosis. Ferroptosis was suggested to be potentially activated in the HL‐60 cells (Figure  ); however, no changes were observed in the expression of ferroptosis‐related genes in the K562 cells. These data collectively indicate that TP‐0903 induces a non‐canonical ferroptosis pathway [ ] in MDS‐L cells through TGF‐β1/SMAD3 signaling, leading to cell death.
 To confirm that ferroptosis was the primary mechanism of cell death, rescue experiments were conducted by adding ferroptosis inhibitors (Ferrostatin‐1 and Liproxstatin‐1) to TP‐0903‐treated MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells. Cell viability was restored in MDS‐L cells, but not in HL‐60 cells (Figure  ). These results suggest that ferroptosis plays a critical role in TP‐0903‐induced MDS‐L cell death.

## Discussion
TP‐0903 is a novel multikinase inhibitor that has demonstrated efficacy in CLL [ , ], solid tumors [ , , , ], and resistant AML [ , , ]. This study explored its antitumor effects in MDS and leukemia cell lines and found that TP‐0903 effectively inhibited cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in all tested cell lines. Notably, the apoptotic pathways induced by TP‐0903 varied among the cell lines. Although TP‐0903 inhibited the PI3K/AKT pathway in all the cell lines tested, the MAPK/ERK pathway remained unchanged. TP‐0903 also inhibited the JAK/STAT3 pathway in MDS‐L and K562 cells. In MDS‐L cells, TP‐0903 increased phospho‐MET expression while disrupting the MET‐AXL interaction. These findings suggest that TP‐0903‐mediated AXL suppression enhanced reactive MET activity and contributed to its anti‐proliferative effects. However, the functional significance of MET in this context remains unclear and warrants further investigation. Elevated levels of γH2AX indicated activation of the DNA damage response. Although p53 is commonly involved in DNA damage signaling, its role here is unlikely, given that MDS‐L cells harbor TP53 mutations [ ], HL‐60 cells lack TP53, and K562 cells showed no significant TP53‐related changes.
A particularly notable difference among the cell lines was in the Aurora A cell cycle‐related signaling responses. In MDS‐L and K562 cells, TP‐0903 significantly inhibited Aurora A activity, which regulates the cell cycle and signaling pathways such as PI3K/AKT, WNT/β‐catenin, and JAK2/STAT3 [ , , ]. The inhibition of Aurora A likely contributed to the observed reduction in STAT3 phosphorylation. These observations provide mechanistic insights into the action of TP‐0903.
In MDS‐L cells, the TP‐0903‐mediated inhibition of Aurora A phosphorylation decreased Cyclin A/CDK1 and BORA levels, disrupted PLK1 activation, and blocked mitotic initiation. BORA phosphorylation by Cyclin A/CDK1 triggers Aurora A‐mediated PLK1 activation. The inhibition of this pathway causes G2/M arrest and apoptosis. The disruption of chromosome alignment during metaphase caused by Aurora A inhibition, and the resulting abnormalities in the early stages of mitosis, are considered to contribute to cell death. HL‐60 cells showed minimal changes in Aurora A phosphorylation, with cell cycle alterations likely caused by AKT suppression and DNA damage response. PLK1 expression initially increased at 1 h after TP‐0903 treatment but decreased by 24 h. Given the shorter doubling time of HL‐60 cells (24 h vs. 48 h in MDS‐L), PLK1 likely accumulated early and was gradually degraded.
In contrast, K562 cells exhibited a unique response to Aurora A inhibition, demonstrating a marked increase in TPX2 expression. Since TPX2 activates and stabilizes Aurora A, its overexpression can cause spindle defects and mitotic arrest. Additionally, it disrupts nuclear reassembly after cell division, resulting in abnormal daughter cells with characteristic donut‐shaped nuclei [ ]. This phenomenon was specific to K562 cells, indicating a distinct mechanism of cell cycle disruption.
RNA‐sequencing revealed increased expression of TGF‐β‐related genes in MDS‐L and HL‐60 cells, implicating this pathway in regulating cell cycle progression. However, K562 cells, driven by the BCR‐ABL fusion gene, showed no changes in TGF‐β signaling, likely due to the BCR‐ABL‐mediated suppression of this pathway.
TP‐0903 also induces ferroptosis through TGF‐β1/SMAD3 signaling [ , ], as indicated by increased ROS and intracellular iron accumulation. Notably, elevated expression of ferroptosis‐related genes, such as HMOX1 and transferrin, suggested the activation of an atypical ferroptosis pathway [ ]. The results of the rescue experiments also suggest that ferroptosis may be the primary mechanism of TP‐0903‐induced cell death in MDS‐L cells. Ferroptosis may serve as a complementary mechanism in cancer therapy, particularly in leukemia differentiation treatment [ ]. Collectively, our findings suggested that TP‐0903 exerts its antitumor effects through diverse cell‐specific mechanisms, including apoptosis, ferroptosis, and cell cycle disruption. These insights highlight the potential of TP‐0903 as a therapeutic agent for hematological malignancies and emphasize the need for further research to fully elucidate the molecular targets of TP‐0903 and optimize its clinical applications.
