## Abstract

## Objective
This study aimed to investigate the expression and functions of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) in circulating leukocytes related to type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).

## Methods
We conducted microarray analysis and RNA sequencing on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from newly diagnosed T2DM patients and healthy controls. Differentially expressed lncRNAs, mRNAs, and miRNAs were identified using an absolute log2(Fold Change) of ≥ 0.585 cutoff and a P value < 0.05. A competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network was constructed, and functional enrichment analyses (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO)) were performed. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was used to validate the expression of selected lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs. Correlations with clinical parameters were assessed, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate their potential as biomarkers. Dual-luciferase and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were used to validate the interactions within the ceRNA network.

## Results
A total of 531 DElncRNAs, 328 DEmRNAs, and 42 DEmiRNAs were identified in PBMCs from T2DM patients compared to healthy controls. The ceRNA network included 24 differentially expressed lncRNAs, 11 miRNAs, and 18 mRNAs. Functional enrichment analyses indicated that the differentially expressed mRNAs were mainly associated with pancreatic secretion, lipid metabolism, atherosclerosis, type I diabetes mellitus, and fluid shear stress. Furthermore, qRT-PCR validation confirmed the significant downregulation of prostate pancer associated non-coding RNA 1 (PRNCR1) and somatostatin receptor 3 (SSTR3), and the upregulation of miR-642a-5p. PRNCR1 and miR-642a-5p were positively correlated with triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), and homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), while negatively correlated with leptin and insulin. Conversely, SSTR3 was positively correlated with leptin and insulin, while negatively correlated with total cholesterol (TC), TG, LDL-C, HbA1c, FPG, and HOMA-IR. ROC curve analysis suggested that the expression of miR-642a-5p and SSTR3 could differentiate T2DM patients from healthy controls. Dual-luciferase and RIP assays confirmed that PRNCR1 could sponge miR-642a-5p, which in turn regulated SSTR3, and PRNCR1 knockdown was demonstrated to significantly downregulate SSTR3.

## Conclusion
These findings suggest that PRNCR1 may contribute to insulin secretion disorders in T2DM by regulating the miR-642a-5p/SSTR3 axis, and gained new insight into the potential mechanisms and pathways involved in T2DM.

## Introduction
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a global health crisis that is predicted to affect 578 million by 2030 and 700 million adults by 2045 worldwide [ ]. The major causes of T2DM are environmental and genetic factors, resulting in insufficient insulin secretion from beta cells or insulin resistance, but the mechanism is still unclear [ ]. Moreover, studies have demonstrated that the main characteristics of T2DM are malfunction, dedifferentiation, and apoptosis of β cells [ – ] and that impaired insulin secretion is a key contributor to chronic hyperglycemia in T2DM patients [ ]. Long-term progression can lead to the pathogenesis of several associated complications, including nephropathies, retinopathies, neuropathies, and cardiovascular diseases [ ]. Thus, T2DM imposes a substantial economic burden on affected families and has become an important public health problem. In recent years, great progress in the diagnosis and therapeutic treatment of T2DM and its complications has been made; however, the efficacy of these therapeutic strategies in preventing disease progression have not been completely satisfactory. Therefore, further understanding the precise molecular mechanisms and identifying new noninvasive biomarkers are essential for the early diagnosis and treatment of T2DM.
The functions of lncRNAs in regulating gene expression are related to their structure and subcellular localization. In cytoplasm, lncRNAs and target mRNAs are linked through miRNA, which engage in competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks [ ] and play a crucial role in many diseases. Numerous previous studies suggested that differentially expressed lncRNAs such as SNHG5, LINC-P21, HOTAIR, HCG27_201, GAS5, GHRL-3:2, and GHRL-3:3, played important roles in the pathogenesis of diabetes [ , ]. Notably, although the expression profiles of lncRNAs and lncRNA-related ceRNA network have been studied in T2DM patients, there are still some shortcomings in the intricate regulatory mechanisms in the progression of T2DM. Therefore, new molecular insights into lncRNA-mediated regulatory networks in T2DM are needed.
In this study, we conducted a microarray profile to identify the aberrantly expressions of lncRNAs, mRNAs, and miRNAs in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from T2DM patients to explore their association with diabetes risk factors. Furthermore, we prioritized prostate cancer-associated non-coding RNA 1 (PRNCR1) for mechanistic investigation based on its role in the PRNCR1/miR-642a-5p/somatostatin receptor 3 (SSTR3) ceRNA network. These results provide new insights into the potential mechanisms and pathways involved in T2DM, as PRNCR1 represents a novel candidate with a pivotal regulatory role in the ceRNA network.

## Materials and methods

## Participants and clinical measurements
This study protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Provincial Hospital (ethical approval number: K2023-03–037), which was established in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, and all participants volunteered to participate and signed an informed consent form.
The participants were divided into two groups: 32 patients with T2DM, and 30 healthy normal individuals. All T2DM patients were diagnosed for the first time according to the World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines [ ] and past medical history and had no history of any other endocrine disease, cancer, cardiovascular disease, liver damage, rheumatic immune system diseases. Clinical measurements, such as fasting plasma glucose (FPG), glycated hemoglobin (HbAlc), fasting triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), were tested via a standard protocol in the hospital. The concentrations of serum insulin, adiponectin, and leptin were detected via radioimmunoassay kit (HY-10069) and ELISA kits (HY-D0046 and HY-D0040) according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Beijing Sino-UK Institute of Biological Technology, China). The homeostatic model assessment index of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated via the following equation: fasting insulin (µIU/L) × fasting glucose (nmol/L)/22.5 [ ].

## Sample collection and total RNA extraction
Anticoagulant whole blood was collected with a BD Vacutainer Cell Preparation Tube (CPT) in the morning after an overnight fast of 10 h, and PBMCs were isolated via the CPT methods. Total RNA from PBMC was extracted via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration of RNA was determined via a NanoDrop ND-1000 Spectrophotometer (OD 260 nm, Thermo Scientific, USA). The integrity of the RNA and gDNA contaminants was further assessed by standard denaturing agarose gel electrophoresis.

## LncRNA and mRNA microarrays and data analysis
ArraystarHuman LncRNA Microarray V5.0 was designed by Arraystar Inc. (MD, USA), and it covered more than 39,317 lncRNAs and more than 21,174 coding transcripts in human genome. The lncRNA and mRNA expression profiles of PBMCs were screened via Human LncRNA Microarray following the manufacturer’s guidelines. Briefly, each RNA sample was amplified and transcribed into fluorescent complementary RNAs (cRNAs) throughout the entire length of the transcripts without 3’ bias via a random priming method (Arraystar Flash RNA Labeling Kit, Arraystar). The labeled cRNAs was purified, and the concentration and specific activity (pmol Cy3/µg cRNA) were measured via a NanoDrop ND-1000. Then, 1 µg of each labeled cRNA was fragmented and hybridized onto the lncRNA expression microarray. The hybridized arrays were washed, fixed and scanned via an Agilent Microarray Scanner (part number G2505C), and Agilent Feature Extraction software (version 11.0.1.1) was used to analyze the acquired array images. After quantile normalization of the raw data via the GeneSpringGX v12.1 software package (Agilent Technologies), the differentially expressed lncRNAs (DElncRNAs) and mRNAs (DEGs) between the two groups were identified through P value/FDR filtering and fold change filtering. The DElncRNAs and DEGs with an absolute log2(Fold Change) of ≥ 0.585 and a significant P value of < 0.05 were identified through Volcano Plot filtering and selected for further analysis. The threshold (absolute log2(Fold Change) of ≥ 0.585) helps uncover genes with novel or atypical roles in specific biological processes, providing a more comprehensive candidate pool for follow-up mechanistic work. Hierarchical clustering was performed via R package to reveal distinguishable lncRNA and mRNA expression profiles. All microarray hybridization and collection of expression data were performed by KangChen Biotech (Shanghai, China).

## MiRNA sequencing and data analysis
miRNA sequencing was performed by KangChen Biotech on Illumina Small RNA Sequencing Platform (San Diego, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. After 3’ and 5’ adapters were ligated to total RNAs from PBMCs, the ligation products were reverse transcribed into cDNA, and ~ 135–155 bp fragment were selected to generate the miRNA sequencing library. The size distribution and quantity of sequencing library were subsequently determined on Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies), and the library was finally sequenced via Illumina NextSeq 500 sequencer. The raw reads were analyzed via routine algorithms (KangChen Biotech). Differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs) between the two groups were identified via edgeR (version 3.18.1) package with an absolute log2(Fold Change) of ≥ 0.585 and P values of < 0.05, and visualized via volcano plots and clustered heat map.

## Construction of the CeRNA network
The flow chart of the ceRNA network constructed in this study is shown in Fig. . Based on the ceRNA hypothesis, lncRNA from the same host gene presented the same expression trend. Thus, the co-expression target genes between the DElncRNAs and DEGs were filtered via the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC), with a PCC ≥ 0.9 and P < 0.05. miRcode and StarBase were used to search for DElncRNAs-binding miRNAs that intersected with DEmiRNAs to determine lncRNA-miRNA pairs. The targeted mRNAs by the DEmiRNAs were predicted via the miRDB and TargetScan and intersected with DEGs to identify miRNA-mRNA pairs. The selected lncRNA-miRNA pairs, miRNA-mRNA pairs and lncRNA–mRNA pairs were subsequently overlapped to construct the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA network, which was visualized via Cytoscape software [ ].
 

## Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) and gene ontology (GO) pathway enrichment analysis
To better understand the pathogenetic mechanisms of T2DM, GO analysis and KEGG analysis were performed to analyze the DEGs. The GO analysis provides a vocabulary of biological functions (http://geneontology.org/), comprising three components: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). KEGG is a database resource that stores information about gene and protein annotation and is often used to explore potential pathways. The potential function and underlying mechanism of the DEGs were evaluated via the Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) [ ]. Significant terms were selected based on a threshold of P < 0.05.

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) validation
qRT-PCR was carried out to validate the selected genes. In brief, cDNA was prepared via reverse transcription of lncRNA/mRNA using SuperScript III Reverse Transcriptase (Invitrogen), and miRNA using M-MuLV Reverse Transcriptase (Enzymatics, USA). Then, qRT-PCR was performed via 2×PCR master mix (Arraystar, USA) on a QuantStudio 5 Real-time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). The sequences of the primers are shown in Table  . All PCR primers were obtained from Invitrogen, Shanghai, China. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) or U6 was used as the internal reference gene for the lncRNA/mRNA and miRNA levels respectively. The relative gene expression level was normalized to the internal gene expression level and calculated via the relative expression method (2−ΔΔCt).
 

## Cell culture and transfection
The hepatoblastoma cell line HepG2 (HB-8065, American Type Culture Collection, Manassas, VA, USA) was maintained in dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM, Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Invitrogen). The cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C. To knock down PRNCR1 expression, small interfering RNA si-PRNCR1 (5’-CAGTTTGATTAGGGAGGCACATTTA-3’) was designed and synthesized by Hanbio Tech (Shanghai, China). A scramble siRNA (5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3’) was used as the negative control (si-NC). Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. After 48 h, the cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

## Nuclear/cytoplasmic RNA extraction
The cytoplasmic and nuclear lncRNA fractions of HepG2 cells were isolated via a Cytoplasmic and Nuclear RNA Purification Kit (Aidlab, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Specifically, HepG2 cells were collected and lysed with Lysis Buffer RLN. After centrifugation, the cytoplasmic fraction in the supernatant and the nuclear fraction in the pellet were obtained. The pellet was subsequently resuspended, and the cytoplasm was mixed with RLT Plus. Then, cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions were load onto RNA columns and washed with RW buffer. Finally, both cytoplasmic and nuclear RNA were eluted with RNase free water.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
The wild-type target site sequences of PRNCR1 or SSTR3 3’UTR, which are inversely complementary to miR-642a-5p, were predicted and mutated. Then, PRNCR1 (305 nt) or SSTR3 (305 nt) 3’UTR were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) and cloned into the psiCHECKTM-2 Vector (Promega, USA) to generate PRNCR1 or SSTR3 wild-type (Wt) and mutant-type (Mut) dual-luciferase reporter plasmids. Thereafter, the luciferase reporters were co-transfected with miR-642a-5p mimics or NC mimics into 293 T cells using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, the firefly and renilla luciferase activities were measured following the manufacturer’s instructions using a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega), and the light. intensity was measured with an Orion II Microplate Luminometer (Berthold Detection. Systems GmbH, Pforzheim, Germany). The relative luciferase activity was evaluated by normalizing the firefly activity to the renilla activity.

## RNA binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP)
For the Argonaute-2 (Ago2) RIP assay, the binding of Ago2 with lncRNA PRNCR1 and miR-642a-5p was assessed via the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The cells were lysed with RIPA lysate and incubated with magnetic beads conjugated with human antibody against Ago2 (Proteintech) or normal mouse IgG (Millipore). After the proteins in the complex were removed, qRT-PCR was performed to quantify PRNCR1 and miR-642a-5p expression.

## Statistical analysis
All the statistical data were analyzed via SPSS v23.0. Data with a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Two-tailed Student’s t-test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were performed to determine the statistical significance of differences between groups. Pearson’s correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between gene expressions and the clinical measurements. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to evaluating the diagnostic performance of lncRNA and mRNA. P < 0.05 indicates statistical significance.

## Results

## Demographic and clinical characteristics of participants in this study
Data regarding the participants’ demographic characteristics are summarized in Table  . There were no significant differences in BMI, age, or sex (P > 0.05), but there were significant differences in TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, adiponectin, leptin, fasting insulin, HbA1c, FPG, and HOMA-IR (P < 0.05).
 

## Identification of DElncRNAs, DEmiRNAs, and DEGs
To identify the regulatory network of mRNA and lncRNA that play critical roles in T2DM, microarray and sequencing were performed to profile the expression of lncRNAs, mRNAs and miRNA of PBMC from three pairs of patients with T2DM and healthy controls. The principal component analysis (PCA) analyses revealed that all genes, DElncRNAs, DEmiRNAs, and DEGs had a relatively scattered distribution between patients with T2DM and healthy controls (Fig.  A-C), which verified that the samples are correctly separated. After applying an absolute log2(Fold Change) of ≥ 0.585 and P < 0.05 as cutoff values, a total of 22,332 lncRNAs were detected, compared with those in healthy controls, 394 were upregulated, and 265 were downregulated in T2DM patients. A total of 173 upregulated and 155 downregulated mRNAs were identified among the 15,943 detected mRNAs in the T2DM patients. In addition, a total of 42 DEmiRNAs were found, including 24 upregulated and 18 downregulated DEmiRNAs. Heatmaps (Fig.  D-F) and volcano dot plots (Fig.  G-I) showed the expression profiles of DElncRNAs, DEGs and DEmiRNAs after normalization. The raw data of this study have been deposited in the GEO repository (GEO accession GSE281291), which are publicly accessible at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE281291.
 

## Functional and pathway enrichment analyses of DEGs
To gain further insights into the molecular functions of these differentially expressed mRNAs involved in T2DM, GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were performed. The results showed that the upregulated mRNAs were enriched mostly in supramolecular complex for cellular component, cell fate commitment for biological process, and serine-type endopeptidase activity for molecular function (Fig.  A). In addition, KEGG pathways analysis revealed that the top 10 enriched signaling pathways were salivary secretion, dilated cardiomyopathy, hippo signaling pathway, melanogenesis, pancreatic secretion, endocrine and other factor-regulated calcium reabsorption, human papilloma virus infection, basal cell carcinoma, proteoglycans in cancer, and adherens junction (Fig.  C). For the downregulated mRNAs, the most enriched GO terms were ficolin-1-rich granule membrane for cellular component, negative regulation of MAPK cascade for biological process, and pattern recognition receptor activity for molecular function (Fig.  B). Moreover, KEGG pathway analysis revealed enrichment of the c-type lectin receptor signaling pathway, fluid shear stress and atherosclerosis, hematopoietic cell lineage, cGMP-PKG signaling pathway, and progesterone-mediated oocyte maturation (Fig.  D). The global enrichment rate of GO and KEGG pathway analyses were shown in Supplementary Tables 1 and Supplementary Table 2, respectively.
 

## Construction and dissection of the CeRNA network
By integrating the lncRNA-miRNA pairs, miRNA-mRNA pairs and lncRNA-mRNA pairs, a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network was constructed on the basis of the established theory. In total, the ceRNA network was composed of 107 edges and 53 nodes, including 24 DElncRNAs, 11 DEmiRNAs, and 18 DEGs (Fig.  A).
 GO and KEGG functional enrichment analyses were subsequently conducted to better understand the molecular mechanisms of the 18 DEGs involved in the ceRNA network. As shown in Fig.  B, BPs were significantly enriched in myosin binding, GTP-dependent protein binding, G protein activity, and cytoskeletal protein binding. MFs were significantly enriched in amelogenesis, anatomical structure formation involved in morphogenesis, and negative regulation of viral process. CCs were significantly enriched in cytoplasmic vesicle, intracellular vesicle, and the plasma membrane. The KEGG analysis revealed that the DEGs were mainly enriched in pathways such as pancreatic secretion, lipid and atherosclerosis, type I diabetes mellitus, and fluid shear stress and atherosclerosis (Fig.  C).

## qRT-PCR validation of lncRNA, DEmiRNA, and DEG expression
Some differentially expressed genes (Table  ) related to T2DM were subsequently selected from the lncRNA-related networks based on insights gained from previous studies for validation by qRT-PCR. Compared with those in healthy controls, the expression levels of lncRNA AC021491.4, hsa-miR-642a-5p, hsa-miR-30c-2-3p, and tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily 10B (TNFRSF10B) were upregulated, whereas those of lncRNA PRNCR1, BCR-ABL1-independent long non-coding RNA (IRAIN), hsa-miR-1268b, SSTR3, and insulin-like growth factor I receptor (IGF1R) were downregulated (Fig.  A). The results of qRT-PCR were highly consistent with those of the microarray analysis and sequencing (Fig.  B-D).
 

## Diagnostic efficiency of LncRNAs and mRNAs for T2DM
To examine the diagnostic efficiency of the selected lncRNAs and mRNAs for T2DM, ROC curve analysis was performed, and the AUC was calculated. As illustrated in Fig.  A-F, the AUC of PRNCR1 was not statistically significant, whereas the diagnostic efficiency of other RNAs for T2DM was better. The AUC of AC02149.12 was 0.8266 (95% CI:0.7258 to 0.9274, P < 0.0001), that of IRAIN was 0.8945 (95% CI:0.8192 to 0.9698, P < 0.0001), that of TNFRSF10B was 0.9615 (95% CI:0.9203 to 1.000, P < 0.0001), that of SSTR3 was 0.9868 (95% CI:0.9674 to 1.000, P < 0.0001), and that of IGF1R was 0.9713 (95% CI:0.9384 to 1.000, P < 0.0001). The results suggested that lnc-AC02149.12, IRAIN, TNFRSF10B, SSTR3, and IGF1R might be used to differentiate patients with T2DM and healthy controls.
 

## Correlation analysis of the expression of lncRNAs, MiRNAs and mRNAs and the clinical characteristics of participants
Correlation analysis was performed to examine whether lncRNAs, miRNAs and mRNAs expression was associated with the clinical characteristics of T2DM patients. As shown in Table  , TC was significantly negatively correlated with IGF1R and SSTR3 and positively correlated with miR-30c-2-3p. TG was significantly negatively correlated with IRAIN, IGF1R and SSTR3 and positively correlated with miR-642a-5p and TNFRSF10B. HDL-C was significantly negatively correlated with TNFRSF10B and positively correlated with IRAIN and IGF1R. LDL-C was significantly negatively correlated with IGF1R and SSTR3 and positively correlated with PRNCR1, miR-642a-5p, miR-30c-2-3p and TNFRSF10B. Adiponectin was significantly negatively correlated with IRAIN and IGF1R. Leptin was significantly negatively correlated with AC02149.1, PRNCR1, miR-642a-5p, miR-30c-2-3p and TNFRSF10B and positively correlated with IRAIN, IGF1R and SSTR3. Insulin was significantly negatively correlated with miR-642a-5p and TNFRSF10B and positively correlated with IRAIN, miR-1268b, IGF1R and SSTR3. HbA1c, FPG and HOMA-IR were negatively correlated with IRAIN, IGF1R and SSTR3 and positively correlated with AC02149.1, miR-642a-5p, miR-30c-2-3p and TNFRSF10B. HbA1c and HOMA-IR were positively correlated with PRNCR1, whereas HbA1c was negatively correlated with miR-1268b independently.
 

## PRNCR1 modulates SSTR3 via sponge adsorption of miR-642a-5p
We conducted post-hoc power analyses in this study. The corresponding post-hoc power values (α = 0.05) were approximately 100% for PRNCR1, 98.8% for miR-642a-5p, and 100% for SSTR3. Thus, our sample size was more than adequate to detect the observed differences in PRNCR1, miR-642a-5p and SSTR3. In the ceRNA network, SSTR3, as a ciliary G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR), mediates somatostatin action on beta cells and modulates insulin secretion in islets [ ]. Furthermore, PRNCR1 has not been reported in T2DM, and was chosen for further investigation. Several studies have suggested that lncRNAs in the cytoplasm can act as miRNA sponges that regulate mRNA expression. The results of the nuclear/cytosol fractionation assay revealed that the lncRNA PRNCR1 was predominantly expressed in the cytoplasm of HepG2 cells (Fig. A); thus, PRNCR1 may function as a ceRNA by competitively binding miRNAs. The potential biding sites between PRNCR1 or SSTR3 and miR-642a-5p were subsequently predicted (Fig. B). The results of the dual-luciferase reporter assay revealed that the relative luciferase activity of cells co-transfected with the PRNCR1-Wt and miR-642a-5p mimic was significantly lower than that of cells co-transfected with the PRNCR1-Mut and miR-642a-5p mimic (Fig. C). In addition, compared with the SSTR3-Mut reporter, the miR-642a-5p mimic apparently decreased the relative luciferase activity in the SSTR3-Wt reporter group (Fig. D). These results confirmed the direct binding between PRNCR1 or SSTR3 and miR-642a-5p. Furthermore, the RIP results revealed that PRNCR1 and miR-642a-5p were more abundant in anti-AGO2 than anti-IgG (Fig. E and F). In addition, PRNCR1 knockdown (Fig. A) was demonstrated to significantly downregulate SSTR3 mRNA (Fig. B). Collectively, these results demonstrate that PRNCR1 serves as a sponge of miR-642a-5p to modulate SSTR3 expression.
 

## Discussion
As one of the largest and most populous countries in the world, China is a main region of the fast-rising T2DM global epidemic [ ]. Although T2DM is characterized by both insulin resistance and dysfunction of pancreatic beta cell, the specific underlying mechanisms are not yet clear. Recently, emerging evidence has revealed that the aberrant expression of lncRNAs is distinctly associated with the occurrence and development of T2DM and its complications [ ]. However, few studies have investigated lncRNAs in circulating leukocytes involved in T2DM. In a very recent report, significantly differentially expressed circulating lncRNAs were identified in T2DM patients via RNA sequencing and can be considered diagnostic markers or regulators of T2DM [ ].
In the present study, microarray technology was used to detect lncRNA and mRNA expression profiles in the PBMCs of freshly diagnosed diabetic patients and healthy controls. In total, 659 differentially expressed lncRNAs and 328 differentially expressed mRNAs, as well as 42 differentially expressed miRNAs, were identified. GO and KEGG analyses revealed that the DEGs were notably enriched in pancreatic secretion and cGMP-PKG signaling pathway, which are relevant for treating diabetes [ ]. Then, the expression levels of candidate lncRNAs (AC021491.4, IRAIN and PRNCR1), miRNAs (hsa-miR-1268b, hsa-miR-642a-5p and hsa-miR-30c-2-3p), and mRNAs (TNFRSF10B, IGF1R and SSTR3) were subsequently validated via a large sample qRT-PCR experiment. The results of qRT-PCR were highly consistent with those of the microarray analysis. The results of correlation analysis indicated that IRAIN, miR-642a-5p, TNFRSF10B, IGF1R and SSTR3 were closely correlated with LDL-C, Leptin, Insulin, HbA1c, FPG and HOMA-IR in patients with T2DM and healthy controls, which suggested that these differentially expressed RNAs might be involved in lipid metabolism, insulin secretion, and glucose regulation. Furthermore, the ROC analysis indicated that AC021491.4, IRAIN, TNFRSF10B, SSTR3 and IGF1R had good sensitivity and specificity and can be used as potential blood biomarkers to distinguish T2DM patients from nondiabetic patients.
On the basis of the ceRNA theory, the experimental data overlapped with the predicted data from the bioinformatic analysis, and a lncRNA-associated ceRNA network in T2DM was constructed. This strategy ensures the validity and reliability of the ceRNA regulatory mechanisms involved in T2DM. GO and KEGG pathway analyses revealed that the functions of DEmRNAs in the ceRNA network were significantly correlated with focal adhesion, proteoglycans in cancer, pancreatic secretion, T cell receptor signaling pathway, lipid and atherosclerosis, leukocyte transendothelial migration, and natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity. These findings provide a new understanding of the pathogenic mechanism of T2DM.
LncRNAs can function as ceRNA or natural microRNA sponges to protect mRNAs from miRNA-mediated suppression [ ]. PRNCR1 is located on chromosome region 8q24 from chr8:127,079,873 − 127,092,600, plus strand. It is also named as Prostate Cancer Associated Transcript 8 (PCAT8). PRNCR1 acts as androgen receptor (AR) coactivator lncRNAs, and successively binds to AR and cause transactivation of AR-mediated genes, thereby promoting the transcriptional activity of the androgen receptor [ ]. Thus, the aberrant expression of PRNCR1 may contribute to the development and progression of androgenic and AR related disorders. Recent studies have found that PRNCR1 is significantly up-regulated in breast cancer [ ], polycystic ovary syndrome [ ], gastric cancer [ ], colon cancer [ ], etc. Nevertheless, how PRNCR1 participates in the pathogenesis of T2DM has rarely been described. In the present study, PRNCR1 was downregulated and found to be localized in the cytoplasm, suggesting that it potentially functions as a miRNA sponge. Bioinformatics analyses confirmed the interaction between PRNCR1 and miR-642a-5p and between miR-642a-5p and SSTR3. Furthermore, the results of the dual-luciferase reporter and RIP assays confirmed that the lncRNA PRNCR1 functions as a miR-642a-5p sponge and that SSTR3 is a target gene of miR-642a-5p. Functionally, PRNCR1 acts as a negative regulator of miR-642a-5p, which in turn reduced SSTR3 expression in T2DM patients. However, ROC analysis (AUC = 0.6283, P = 0.08) indicated that PRNCR1 is unlikely to serve as a robust circulating biomarker. Nevertheless, its coordinated expression within the miR-642a-5p/SSTR3 ceRNA network points to a significant role in T2DM-related transcriptional regulation. Therefore, we selected PRNCR1 for mechanistic studies based on its regulatory centrality and novelty, rather than on any diagnostic promise. MiRNA are endogenous, small non-coding RNAs of 19 to 25 nucleotides in length that suppress mRNA expression by binding to the 3’-untranslated region (UTRs) of specific genes. One such miRNA is miR-642a-5p, which is a potential tumor suppressor that can inhibit the vitality, migration, invasion, and EMT of colorectal cancer [ ] and pancreatic cancer [ ] tumor cells while promoting apoptosis. In this study, for the first time, miR-642a-5p was shown to be upregulated in T2DM.
SSTR3 is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCRs) named somatostatin receptor (SSTR) 1–5. Multiple laboratories have employed immunocytochemical methods involving the use of different antibodies to detect the pattern of SSTR3 expression in human islet cells, with variable results [ ]. However, the localization of SSTR3 is restricted to primary cilia, specialized cell-surface projections that have been identified on pancreatic islet cells and function as cellular sensors, hormone regulators, and cell-cell communications devices [ ]. Furthermore, SSTR3 is expressed predominantly in ciliary beta cells and mediates the suppression of insulin secretion by somatostatin [ ]. Intriguingly, the expression of SSTR3 was lacking in the somatostatin cells and PP cells of islets from T2DM patients, which suggest a reduced automatic feedback inhibitory effect of somatostatin on somatostatin cells and pancreatic polypeptide (PP) cells, resulting in increased hormonal paracrine activity [ ]. Being the primary source of endogenous glucose production, the liver is one of the essential insulin target organs. The HepG2 cell line is currently being used in several studies involving hepatocyte modeling to study insulin signaling or the molecular mechanisms of metabolic processes [ ]. HepG2 cells provide a tractable system to test miRNA-mediated regulation of SSTR3 without confounding factors from pancreatic microenvironments. Additionally, the downregulated expression of SSTR3 was also identified in patients with T2DM in the current study. Prior studies have indicated that SSTR3 mediate the inhibitory effects of somatostatin on insulin, glucagon, and PP secretion [ , ]. Thus, the downregulation of SSTR3 may lead to uncontrolled insulin, glucagon, and PP secretion, resulting in T2DM. Overall, PRNCR1 influences SSTR3 expression by acting as a miR-642a-5p sponge, which contributes to the progression of T2DM. Moreover, the specific roles of PRNCR1 still need further study to elucidated the underlying pathogenesis of T2DM.
In addition, this study also has some limitations. When searching for lncRNAs that can “sequester” miRNAs, analyzing miRNAs solely based on DEGs may not be appropriate. This is because even miRNAs that do not exhibit expression changes in T2DM could still be bound and inhibited by lncRNAs. Screening for significant miRNAs through DEGs analysis may indeed exclude some potentially relevant miRNAs, thereby limiting the comprehensiveness of the study. More comprehensive analytical strategy is needed to identify potential miRNA-lncRNA interactions. Of course, strengths of this study include the use of a beta cell model to validate these findings in future studies.

## Conclusions
In this study, the expression profiles and functional networks of lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs were comprehensively analyzed in the PBMCs of patients with T2DM. Moreover, PRNCR1 can function as a ceRNA by regulating miR642a-5p-dependent SSTR3 expression, which contributes to the progression of T2DM. These findings help to elucidate the potential mechanisms and new pathways of insulin disorders in patients with T2DM. Even so, further studies are needed to elucidate the exactly mechanism of PRNCR1/miR-642a-5p/SSTR3 ceRNA network in T2DM.
