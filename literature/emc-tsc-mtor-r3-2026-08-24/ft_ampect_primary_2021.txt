pub-id-type="pmid">37890128</pub-id></related-article><abstract><title>PURPOSE</title><p>Malignant perivascular epithelioid cell tumor (PEComa) is a rare aggressive sarcoma, with no approved treatment. To our knowledge, this phase II, single-arm, registration trial is the first prospective clinical trial in this disease, investigating the safety and efficacy of the mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitor <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus (AMPECT, <ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="https://www.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT02494570" ext-link-type="uri">NCT02494570</ext-link>).</p><sec><title>PATIENTS AND METHODS</title><p>Patients with malignant PEComa were treated with <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus 100 mg/m<sup>2</sup> intravenously once weekly for 2 weeks in 3-week cycles. The primary end point was objective response rate evaluated by independent radiology review. Key secondary end points included duration of response, progression-free survival, and safety. A key exploratory end point was tumor biomarker analysis.</p></sec><sec><title>RESULTS</title><p>Thirty-four patients were treated (safety evaluable), and 31 were evaluable for efficacy. The overall response rate was 39% (12 of 31; 95% CI, 22 to 58) with one complete and 11 partial responses, 52% (16 of 31) of patients had stable disease, and 10% (3 of 31) had progressive disease. Responses were of rapid onset (67% by week 6) and durable. Median duration of response was not reached after a median follow-up for response of 2.5 years, with 7 of 12 responders with treatment ongoing (range, 5.6-47.2+ months). Twenty-five of 31 patients had tumor mutation profiling: 8 of 9 (89%) patients with a <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutation achieved a confirmed response versus 2 of 16 (13%) without <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutation (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; .001). The median progression-free survival was 10.6 months (95% CI, 5.5 months to not reached), and the median overall survival was 40.8 months (95% CI, 22.2 months to not reached). Most treatment-related adverse events were grade 1 or 2 and were manageable for long-term treatment. No grade ≥ 4 treatment-related events occurred.</p></sec><sec><title>CONCLUSION</title><p><italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-Sirolimus is active in patients with malignant PEComa. The response rate, durability of response, disease control rate, and safety profile support that <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus represents an important new treatment option for this disease.</p></sec></abstract><counts><fig-count count="3"/><table-count count="3"/><equation-count count="0"/><ref-count count="30"/><page-count BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro"><title>INTRODUCTION</title><p>Perivascular epithelioid cell tumors (PEComas) are mesenchymal neoplasms, composed of histologically and immunohistochemically distinctive epithelioid cells.<sup><xref rid="b1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="b2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref></sup> Most PEComas are clinically benign and do not metastasize, but malignant PEComas demonstrate local invasion and/or metastatic spread. Malignant PEComas are classified as an ultrarare soft tissue sarcoma (STS) with an estimated annual incidence of ≤ 1/1,000,000 population,<sup><xref rid="b3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref></sup> arise most commonly at visceral sites (especially renal, uterine, and gastrointestinal), and have a female predominance.</p><boxed-text id="B1" position="anchor" orientation="portrait"><sec sec-type="context-box"><title>CONTEXT</title><p>
<list list-type="simple"><list-item><p>
<bold>Key Objective</bold>
</p></list-item><list-item><p>Malignant perivascular epithelioid cell tumor is a rare aggressive soft tissue sarcoma, with no approved treatment. To our knowledge, the AMPECT study is the first prospective clinical trial in this disease and evaluated the efficacy and safety of the novel mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitor <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus.</p></list-item><list-item><p>
<bold>Knowledge Generated</bold>
</p></list-item><list-item><p>The overall response rate to <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus was 39%, with one complete and 11 partial responses, exceeding the prespecified lower-bound objective response rate of 15% below which the regimen would be considered no more active than standard doxorubicin-based chemotherapy. Responses were of rapid onset and durable. Given the aggressive natural history of the disease not known to spontaneously regress, the responses are most likely due to antitumor activity of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus. The median progression-free survival was 10.6 months, and the progression-free survival rate at 6 months was 70%, significantly exceeding the benchmark (14%) that is considered potentially active in advanced soft tissue sarcoma.</p></list-item><list-item><p>
<bold>Relevance</bold>
</p></list-item><list-item><p><italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-Sirolimus may offer an important benefit and a new treatment option in a rare and aggressive sarcoma, perivascular epithelioid cell tumor.</p></list-item></list>
</p></sec></boxed-text><p>Malignant PEComa has no approved treatment. Although often treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy regimens, these have shown modest benefit.<sup><xref rid="b4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref></sup> Some patients with PEComas benefited from treatment with mTORC1 inhibitors (including sirolimus, everolimus, and temsirolimus), as described in case reports and retrospective analyses.<sup><xref rid="b4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>-<xref rid="b9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref></sup> PEComas commonly have loss-of-function mutations in or deletions of <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic> or <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic>.<sup><xref rid="b10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref></sup> In addition, PEComas often show evidence of mTORC1 activation with phosphorylation of p70S6K and ribosomal protein S6 by immunohistochemistry (IHC).<sup><xref rid="b11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref></sup> Aberrant mTORC1 signaling is a key driver of cell proliferation and tumor formation,<sup><xref rid="b12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref></sup> suggesting that mTORC1 inhibition may be a promising therapeutic approach for PEComas.</p><p>The orally available mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitors sirolimus and everolimus have variable absorption, often require therapeutic drug monitoring, and have incomplete target suppression.<sup><xref rid="b13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>-<xref rid="b15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref></sup>
<italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-Sirolimus (nanoparticle albumin–bound sirolimus; ABI-009, formerly known as <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-rapamycin) is a novel intravenous (IV) mTOR inhibitor with significantly higher tumor growth inhibition, higher intratumoral drug accumulation, and greater mTOR target [phospho-S6 (pS6)] suppression compared with oral inhibitors, as demonstrated in preclinical models.<sup><xref rid="b16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref></sup></p><p>To our knowledge, this trial (AMPECT) is the first prospective clinical trial in advanced malignant PEComa. Herein, we describe the safety and efficacy of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus in patients with this disease.</p></sec><sec sec-type="patients|methods"><title>PATIENTS AND METHODS</title><sec sec-type="subjects"><title>Patients</title><p>Eligible adults (age ≥ 18 years) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status score ≤ 1, had not previously received an mTOR inhibitor, and had a histologically confirmed diagnosis of either metastatic or locally advanced (ineligible for surgery) malignant PEComa and measurable disease according to the RECIST, v1.1. Histology was assessed locally in each institution at enrollment and subsequently confirmed by central review at the Dana-Farber/Harvard Cancer Center (J.L.H.). Pathologic confirmation of PEComa was based on characteristic histologic features and evidence of melanocytic (HMB-45 and/or melan A) and smooth muscle (smooth muscle actin and/or desmin) differentiation by IHC; PEComas with either marked nuclear atypia or pleomorphism combined with mitotic activity were considered malignant.<sup><xref rid="b1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref></sup></p><p>Eligible patients had adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function, including an absolute neutrophil count of ≥ 1.5 × 10<sup>9</sup>/L, platelets of ≥ 100 × 10<sup>9</sup>/L, a hemoglobin level of ≥ 9 g/dL, a bilirubin level of ≤ 1.5 × upper limit of normal, and a serum creatinine level of ≤ 1.5 × upper limit of normal.</p></sec><sec><title>Trial Design and Treatment</title><p>In this multicenter, open-label, phase II registration study, patients received <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus 100 mg/m<sup>2</sup> IV over 30 minutes once on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. A maximum of two dose reductions to 75 and 56 mg/m<sup>2</sup> were permitted for toxicity. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicities, or patient preference.</p></sec><sec><title>End Points and Statistical Analysis</title><p>The primary efficacy end point was overall objective response rate (ORR) by 6 months, evaluated by independent radiology review. The primary analysis was preplanned to occur when the last enrolled patient had been treated for 6 months. The sample size estimation assumed an observed 30% ORR and a sample size of 30 patients, which would exclude values &lt; 15% for the lower bound of the 95% CI. Secondary end points included duration of response (DOR), progression-free survival (PFS), PFS at 6 months (PFS6), overall survival (OS), and safety. A key exploratory end point evaluated the association of tumor mutational and biomarker analyses with clinical response.</p><p>The DOR, PFS, and OS reported here are based on an additional 1.5-year follow-up after the primary analysis date.</p></sec><sec><title>Assessments</title><p>All patients receiving at least one dose of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus were evaluable for toxicity. All adverse events (AEs) were collected from the time the patient signed informed consent until 28 days after the last dose of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus. AEs were graded by National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v4.03 and were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities.</p><p>Patients were evaluated by contrast-enhanced computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging if computed tomography was contraindicated every 6 weeks for the first year and then every 12 weeks until disease progression. Patients evaluable for efficacy had ≥ 1 dose of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus and centrally confirmed PEComa.</p><p>Tumor response was evaluated by RECIST v1.1 by investigators and independent review by two radiologists and an adjudicator, who were unaware of the investigators' assessment.</p><p>Patients were followed for survival every 12 weeks until death, loss to follow-up, or study closure.</p></sec><sec><title>Biomarker Study Methodology</title><p>Targeted exome next-generation sequencing using the OncoPanel test (Center for Advanced Molecular Diagnostics, Brigham, and Women's Hospital, Boston, MA)<sup><xref rid="b17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref></sup> was performed to assess mutations, copy number changes, and translocation events in approximately 500 genes. In addition, pS6, phospho-4EBP1 (p4EBP1), SPARC, %Ki67, and percent of cleaved caspase 3 were assessed by IHC, and <italic toggle="yes">TFE3</italic> translocation by fluorescence in situ hybridization.</p><p>Mutational and biomarker analyses were blinded with respect to clinical outcome.</p><p>A multivariable analysis was conducted using Pearson correlation to correlate clinical response with altered genes and the following biomarkers: TFE3, phospho-AKT, pS6, p4EBP, SPARC, Ki67, and cleaved caspase 3.</p></sec><sec><title>Trial Oversight</title><p>The study was approved by the institutional review board of each participating site and was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonization requirements for Good Clinical Practice and with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent before the initiation of the study.</p><p>Independent Data Monitoring Committee meetings were convened when 14 and 26 patients had completed ≥ 1 cycle of therapy and reported no concerns regarding the safety of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus requiring study modification or intervention.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="results"><title>RESULTS</title><sec sec-type="subjects"><title>Patient Characteristics and Demographics</title><p>A total of 35 patients were enrolled between April 2016 and November 2018 at nine community and academic centers across the United States; 34 of 35 patients were treated with at least one dose of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus, and 31 were evaluable for efficacy (two did not have PEComa on central pathology review; one did not have sufficient tissue for review). The response rate, durability of response, disease control rate, and toxicity profile support that <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus may represent an important new treatment option for patients with advanced malignant PEComa.</p><p>It is inherently difficult to perform randomized studies for ultrarare indications; thus, this study was a single-arm phase II study to estimate the response rate. There are no prior prospective trials in patients with malignant PEComa, but STS response rates are low and typically &lt; 20%.<sup><xref rid="b18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>-<xref rid="b20" ref-type="bibr">20</xref></sup> For example, in a recent study that included doxorubicin as control for an unselected patient population of STS, the response rate was 11.9%.<sup><xref rid="b21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref></sup> Although the small sample size in the present study provided a wide confidence interval for the 39% ORR, given the aggressive natural history of the disease not known to spontaneously regress, the responses are most likely due to antitumor activity of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus.</p><p>Therapeutics yielding PFS rates of ≥ 40% at 3 months and ≥ 14% at 6 months are considered to be potentially active in advanced STS.<sup><xref rid="b22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref></sup> A retrospective analysis of cytotoxic chemotherapy in malignant PEComa described a median PFS of 3.2-5.4 months.<sup><xref rid="b4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref></sup> In the current study, <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus significantly exceeded these benchmarks with PFS rates at 3 and 6 months of 79% and 70%, respectively. Although patients with tumors with <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutations had a longer PFS and OS compared with those without <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutations, we cannot differentiate between this being an effect of <italic toggle="yes">nab-</italic>sirolimus or potentially reflecting variations in clinical behavior of these genotypes. Overall, these outcomes for a targeted therapy are promising and demonstrate the importance of studying the molecular genetics of each type of sarcoma and other cancer types.</p><p>A subset of malignant PEComas are associated with mutations (inactivation or deletions) of <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic> or <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic>, negative regulators of the mTOR signaling pathway. Retrospective analyses of patients with advanced PEComa treated with mTOR inhibitors sirolimus, temsirolimus, or everolimus showed evidence of antitumor activity,<sup><xref rid="b4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>-<xref rid="b8" ref-type="bibr">8</xref></sup> suggesting that mTOR inhibitors may improve outcomes compared with chemotherapy and tyrosine kinase inhibitors. <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-Sirolimus is a novel albumin-bound mTOR inhibitor characterized by high tumor uptake and mTOR target suppression and may enhance tumor penetration and accumulation via albumin receptor–mediated (gp60) endothelial transcytosis.<sup><xref rid="b23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="b24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref></sup> Albumin has long plasma half-life and broad binding affinity and accumulates in tumors, areas of inflammation, and tissue remodeling, making it an ideal candidate for drug delivery.<sup><xref rid="b23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="b25" ref-type="bibr">25</xref></sup>
<italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-Paclitaxel was the first therapeutic agent using this technology and was shown to have greater drug delivery, safety, and efficacy compared with conventional solvent-based paclitaxel in different various solid tumors.<sup><xref rid="b26" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="b27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> Similarly, <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus has a distinct pharmacologic profile and pharmacokinetics compared with sirolimus and other mTOR inhibitors,<sup><xref rid="b14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="b28" ref-type="bibr">28</xref></sup> although a direct clinical comparison has not yet been performed.</p><p>The female prevalence in this study is consistent with known epidemiology of this disease. The relatively high ORR in patients with primary uterine PEComas is consistent with that of the overall study population and contrasts with retrospective reports of lower sensitivity of uterine PEComas to mTOR inhibitors.<sup><xref rid="b4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref></sup></p><p>No new safety signals were observed despite relatively high doses of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus versus those reported with other mTOR inhibitors.<sup><xref rid="b13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="b14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="b29" ref-type="bibr">29</xref></sup> The high degree of AE resolution and the ability of patients to continue on therapy for &gt; 2 years suggest that <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus is manageable for long-term treatment. On the basis of the pharmacokinetic and safety profile of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus in a phase I study,<sup><xref rid="b28" ref-type="bibr">28</xref></sup> as well as the safety and efficacy in the present study, therapeutic drug monitoring is not required with IV <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus.</p><p>To our knowledge, AMPECT is the first trial to prospectively evaluate treatment outcome and exploratory correlation with mutational status and biomarkers in malignant PEComa. <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic>- or <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic>-inactivating mutations were seen in 56% (14 of 25) of patients. <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutations were a strong positive predictive factor for response to <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus in these patients, which is consistent with the role of the TSC protein complex in mTORC1 regulation and warrants further studies for the role of <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus in other tumors with <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic>-inactivating mutations. Extensive previous studies have shown that inactivation or loss of either <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic> or <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> has similar effects on the activation of mTORC1.<sup><xref rid="b30" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup> Thus, it remains unclear as to why <italic toggle="yes">TSC2</italic> mutations and not <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic> mutations were associated with response in this study. This analysis is limited by the small number of patients (n = 5) with <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic>-mutant tumors. Of the four patients with <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic>-mutant tumors that did not develop RECIST responses, three had SD, two for at least 12 weeks, suggesting that <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus might have provided some, but incomplete, antitumor activity in this setting. Other possible explanations could be that the nature of the <italic toggle="yes">TSC1</italic> mutation led to incomplete inactivation of the TSC1/TSC2 complex or other coincident mutations in these tumors such as mutations in <italic toggle="yes">TP53</italic> may contribute to different tumor behavior and outcome (Fig <xref rid="fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>D). No significant differences were identified in pharmacokinetic parameters of Cmax or area under the curve in these patients that could account for differences in outcome.</p><p>The absence of pS6 staining, which reflects lack of mTORC1 activation, was a strong negative predictor of response to <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus. <italic toggle="yes">RB1</italic> mutation was also uncommon in responders. Since the total number of patients with mutation and biomarker analysis was relatively small (n = 25), further study of these biomarkers is warranted.</p><p>Although other mTOR inhibitors have been used off-label for treatment of advanced malignant PEComa, to our knowledge, the AMPECT study is the first prospective study in this disease and provides evidence that <italic toggle="yes">nab</italic>-sirolimus may offer an important benefit in a rare and aggressive sarcoma for which there are no approved therapies.</p></sec></body><back><ack><title>ACKNOWLEDGMENT</title><p>We thank all the patients and their families who participated in this phase II study; Amin Nassar, MD, for preparation of the co-Mut analysis; and Krinio Giannikou, PhD (postdoctoral fellow), and Kathryn Lasseter (laboratory technician) for the analysis of biomarker samples.</p></ack><fn-group content-type="presented-at"><title>PRIOR PRESENTATION</title><fn specific-use="presented-at" fn-type="presented-at"><p>Presented in part at the American Society of Clinical Oncology Annual Meeting, May 31-June 4, 2019, Chicago, IL and June 4-8, 2020 (virtual), and the Connective Tissue Oncology Society Annual Meeting, November 13-16, 2019, Tokyo, Japan and November 18-21, 2020 (virtual).</p></fn></fn-group><fn-group content-type="supported-by"><title>SUPPORT</title><fn specific-use="supported-by" fn-type="supported-by"><p>Supported by Aadi Bioscience.
