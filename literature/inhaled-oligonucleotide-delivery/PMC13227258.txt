## Abstract
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are globally prevalent pathologies driven by dysregulated signaling networks. Circular RNAs (circRNAs), a class of covalently closed non-coding RNAs produced by back-splicing mechanism, in which a downstream 5′ splice site joins an upstream 3′ splice site, have emerged as critical regulators of CVD pathogenesis. This review examines their lifecycle, including biogenesis, nuclear export mechanisms, subcellular localization, and degradation, along with their functional roles in microRNA sequestration, protein interactions, transcriptional regulation, and polypeptide translation. Recent advances in circRNA engineering are also discussed, including in vitro synthesis strategies, delivery platforms, vaccine development, gene editing, and aptamers (synthetic high-affinity nucleic acid ligands). Furthermore, we explore the latest findings that focus on how circRNAs modulate these core CVD-related pathways. The unique expression patterns and engineering potential of circRNAs render them valuable candidates for biomarker and therapy development. This review connects the fundamental biology of circRNAs to recent technological advances, offering a roadmap for leveraging the functional interaction between circRNAs and signaling pathways in CVD precision medicine.

## Introduction
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), characterized by multiorgan vascular pathologies, pose a significant threat due to high mortality, recurrence, and complications [ ]. Despite advancements in pharmacological and interventional therapies, the persistent rise in CVD morbidity and mortality underscores critical gaps in targeting the molecular drivers of disease progression [ ]. Central to CVD pathogenesis are dysregulated signaling pathways [ , , ]. The Transforming Growth Factor-Beta (TGF-β) pathway regulates fibrosis and inflammation. Its dysregulation promotes cardiac fibrosis through fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix overproduction [ ]. The Wingless-related integration site (Wnt)/β-catenin pathway is essential in development but contributes to pathological cardiac remodeling and vascular calcification in adults [ , ]. Nuclear Factor-kappa B (NF-κB) coordinates innate immunity and is central to vascular inflammation. Its chronic activation drives endothelial dysfunction and atherosclerosis by upregulating adhesion molecules and cytokines [ , , ]. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR (PAM) signaling supports cardiomyocyte survival under physiological conditions but its hyperactivation leads to pathological hypertrophy and impaired cardiac function [ , , ]. The Hippo/Yes-associated protein (YAP) pathway regulates cardiomyocyte proliferation and tissue repair. Dysregulation of YAP/TAZ promotes arrhythmogenic remodeling and impaired angiogenesis [ , , , ]. Collectively, these pathways form an intricate signaling network that balances tissue homeostasis and disease progression, presenting both challenges and opportunities for therapeutic innovation.
Circular RNAs (circRNAs), a class of covalently closed, single-stranded RNA molecules generated via back-splicing of pre-mRNA transcripts, have recently emerged as pivotal regulators of cellular signaling with distinct metabolic and functional properties [ ]. Functionally, circRNAs operate through diverse mechanisms, including miRNA sponge, protein interaction, polypeptide translation, RNA interaction and DNA interaction. Advances in circRNA engineering, including in vitro synthesis via ribozyme-based circularization, lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-based delivery systems optimized for cardiac targeting, circRNA vaccines encoding pathogenic and circRNA-developed tools for gene editing, and aptamers, have further expanded their therapeutic potential [ , , ].
Recent studies have illuminated circRNAs as critical modulators of CVD-associated signaling pathways. For the TGF-β pathway, A. Bibi et al. have revealed that circPVT1 promotes CF activation by sponging miR-30a-5p and miR-125b-5p [ ]. In the case of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, Huang et al. have demonstrated that circNfix suppresses post-MI repair by sponging miR-214 to upregulate Gsk3β and promoting Ybx1 ubiquitination, thereby inhibiting cardiomyocyte proliferation and angiogenesis [ ]. Regarding the NF-κB pathway, Kong et al have reported that circ-Sirt1 attenuates vascular inflammation by enhancing SIRT1 expression through sequestration of miR-132/212, leading to suppression of NF-κB activity [ ]. For the PI3K/AKT pathway, a study conducted by Yang et al. has discovered that circ-Amotl1 confers cardioprotection by scaffolding PDK1 and AKT1 to facilitate nuclear translocation of pAKT [ ]. Finally, within the Hippo/YAP pathway, Wang et al. have shown that N6-Methyladenosine (m6A)-modified circ-ZNF609 was found to regulate cardiac repair by modulating Yap expression [ ].
This review examines the roles of circRNAs in CVD signaling pathways. Firstly, we outline the circRNA lifecycle, including biogenesis, functions, localization, and degradation ( ). Subsequently, we highlight engineering breakthroughs, including synthesis, delivery systems, vaccines, genome editing and aptamers ( ). Furthermore, we categorize recent advances in major signaling pathways implicated in CVDs and emphasize the role of circRNAs in modulating these pathways. Finally, we discuss current challenges and future directions. By integrating molecular mechanisms with therapeutic strategies, this work aims to advance circRNA-based interventions for CVD treatment.
 

## Overview of circRNAs

## Metabolism of circRNAs: from biogenesis, nuclear export, functions to degradation
This section provides a foundational overview of circRNA biology, encompassing their biogenesis, unique characteristics, and metabolism, which sets the stage for understanding their intricate roles in cardiovascular pathophysiology.

## Characterizations of circRNAs
CircRNAs originate mainly via back-splicing. Their lack of 5′ caps and 3′ polyadenylated tails confers significantly greater stability than linear mRNAs [ , ].It is reported that circRNAs originate from various genomic regions, including exons, introns, 5′ untranslated regions (UTRs), 3′ UTRs, and antisense sequences [ ]. Notably, comparative genomic analyses have revealed that a subset of circRNAs, particularly those derived from coding exons, exhibits significant sequence conservation across species, suggesting evolutionary pressure and potential functional importance. For instance, the well-characterized circRNA CDR1as (ciRS-7) displays high conservation between humans and rodents, facilitating functional studies in model organisms [ ]. They are classified into four main categories: intronic circRNAs (ciRNAs), exon–intron circRNAs (EIciRNAs), exonic circRNAs (ecircRNAs), and mitochondria-encoded circRNAs (mecciRNAs) [ ]. The majority of endogenous human circRNAs are composed of multiple exons and predominantly localize to the cytoplasm, whereas EIciRNAs and ciRNAs remain localized within the nucleus [ ]. In contrast, ciRNAs are more prevalent and widely expressed across human cells [ ]. EIciRNAs differ from ecircRNAs in key aspects, including their greater length, nuclear localization, higher tissue specificity, and predominance in brain tissues. In the cardiovascular system, approximately 90 % of cardiac circRNAs originate from constitutive exons, whereas only 10 % derive from alternatively spliced exons [ ].
Mitochondria-encoded circRNAs (mecciRNAs), first identified by Zhao et al. in 2019, are emerging as a research hotspot. They are unique in that they originate from the mitochondrial genome and can reside not only in mitochondria but also in the cytosol and extracellular space. Unlike nuclear-derived circRNAs, mecciRNAs are produced independently of conventional splicing mechanisms, as mitochondria in higher eukaryotes lack introns and linear splicing events [ , , ]. Beyond hepatic roles in nonalcoholic hepatitis [ ], notably, a recent study has found that exogenous mecciRNA degradation is crucial in heart failure, which is mediated by SUPV3L1 (suppressor of var1, 3-like protein 1) /endoribonuclease ELAC2 (elaC ribonuclease Z 2) [ ]. To distinguish from mecciRNAs, mitochondrial circRNAs are transcribed from nuclear DNA and then transported to mitochondria. Zheng et al. have been reported that CircSamd4, a mitochondrial circRNA, induces cardiac repair after myocardial infarction by blocking mitochondria-derived ROS output [ ]. These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of mecciRNA or mitochondrial circRNAs for cardiovascular disorders.
Collectively, the defining features of circRNAs, including their enhanced stability, subtype-specific localization (nuclear, cytoplasmic, or mitochondrial), differential species conservation, and tight spatiotemporal regulation, render them promising candidates as disease biomarkers, precision therapeutic targets, and novel pharmacological agents.

## Four biogenesis models of circRNAs
CircRNAs are predominantly generated through the back-splicing of pre-mRNA, a process governed by multiple mechanisms that collectively contribute to their biogenetic diversity. Two canonical models are most prevalent: intron pairing-driven circularization and RNA-binding protein (RBP)-mediated circularization. In contrast, two other models, though less common: lariat-driven circularization. The key distinctions among these models lie in their core machinery, corresponding to base-pairing, RBPs, splicing intermediates, or non-mRNA RNA precursors, and in their resultant circRNA subtypes. This mechanistic diversity is relevant to cardiovascular diseases, as the predominant biogenesis pathway in a specific cardiac cell type can determine the circRNA’s response to pathological stimuli including oxidative stress or hypoxia. For instance, the upregulation of specific RNA-binding proteins, including Quaking, in heart failure can drive the production of pro-survival or pathological circRNAs, while alterations in splicing fidelity during ischemia may promote lariat-derived circRNAs with distinct functions. Therefore, understanding the specific biogenesis model of a cardiovascular disease-associated circRNA is crucial for elucidating its pathological role and therapeutic potential.

## Regulatory factors in circRNA biogenesis

## RBPs
The formation of circRNAs through these models is not spontaneous but is precisely controlled by a suite of regulatory factors. The molecular mechanisms underlying circRNA formation remain an active area of investigation. A central role is played by RBPs, which orchestrate the back-splicing process through modulation of pre-mRNA structure and splicing kinetics. For instance, QKI and RNA-binding Motif Protein 20 (RBM20) regulate the generation of cardiac-specific circRNAs by influencing alternative splicing events. QKI, a key regulator of vascular smooth muscle cell phenotype, modulates muscle-specific splicing programs that are critical for maintaining cardiomyocyte contractility [ , , ]. Similarly, RBM20 facilitates exon exclusion from Titin pre-mRNA, enabling the production of RBM20-dependent circRNAs. The Titin-derived circRNAs establish back-splice motifs that are necessary for SRSF10-mediated splicing of cardiac muscle genes [ , ]. These findings collectively highlight the critical roles of RBPs in the regulation of circRNA biogenesis.

## Epigenetic regulation and linear splicing competition regulated circRNA biogenesis
Beyond the involvement of RBPs, multiple additional regulatory layers contribute to circRNA biogenesis. Epigenetic modifications, including m6A methylation, histone modifications, and promoter-associated epigenetic changes, have been shown to influence back-splicing. These modifications can mark specific loci near the start and stop codons of linear mRNAs or modulate host gene transcription [ ]. In addition, kinetic competition between canonical linear splicing and circularization plays a crucial role in circRNA abundance [ ].

## The nuclear export and subcellular localization of circRNAs
CircRNAs exhibit distinct biological functions that are tightly linked to their subcellular localization. Following their biogenesis in the nucleus, many circRNAs are exported to the cytoplasm to exert regulatory roles. Recent studies have revealed several mechanisms that govern the nuclear export of circRNAs, including length-dependent nuclear export [ ], m6A modification-dependent pathways [ , ], and extracellular exosome-mediated trafficking [ , ]. Notably, a specialized circRNA export mechanism has been characterized that involves the coordinated activity of Ran-GTP, exportin-2, and insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA binding protein 1 (IGF2BP1). IGF2BP1 binds directly to circRNAs and recruits both Ran-GTP and exportin-2, suggesting that circRNA export resembles protein trafficking more than canonical mRNA export [ ].
The balance between nuclear retention and cytoplasmic localization of circRNAs is tightly regulated by nuclear factors. For instance, depletion of exportin-4 (XPO4) results in nuclear accumulation of ecircRNAs, triggering R-loop formation, DNA damage, and downstream phenotypes such as infertility and neurodevelopmental disorders [ ]. During neural differentiation, adenosine (A)-rich circRNAs translocate from the nucleus to the cytoplasm under the regulation of poly(A)-binding protein PABPC1 and nuclear basket protein TPR [ ]. Additionally, RNA helicases such as Hel25E and its human homologs UAP56 and URH49, mediate length-dependent export of circRNAs. Post-translational modifications of transport machinery further fine-tune circRNA localization. For example, attachment of small ubiquitin-like modifier protein (SUMO-ylation) to DDX39B enhances the nuclear retention of circNCOR1 [ ].
In conclusion, the nuclear export patterns of circRNAs remain incompletely understood. Aberrant nuclear accumulation of specific circRNAs has been shown to promote genomic instability, including R-loop-mediated DNA damage [ ], while mislocalized circRNAs in the cytoplasm may compromise their roles in signaling pathway modulation. Future investigations should aim to elucidate whether the imbalanced subcellular distribution and defective nuclear export of circRNAs contribute to the disruption of cellular homeostasis and overall cellular fitness.

## Molecular functions of circRNAs
CircRNAs exert versatile molecular functions in disease regulation through multiple mechanisms [ , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , ], including acting as miRNA sponges, functioning as protein sponges, scaffolds, decoys, or recruiters, mediating transcriptional regulation, participating in polypeptide translation, and engaging in RNA or DNA interactions ( ).
 

## MicroRNA sponge
A well-characterized molecular function of circRNAs is their role as miRNA sponges. Most circRNAs localized in the cytoplasm contain multiple miRNA response elements (MREs) and act as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), thereby regulating gene expression by competitively binding miRNAs at specific sites [ ]. In this context, circRNAs often demonstrate stronger miRNA-binding capacity than other RNA species such as mRNAs, lncRNAs, or pseudogenes [ ]. Recent cardiovascular studies have provided compelling examples of circRNA-mediated miRNA sponging, including circHMGCS1, circ_0008362, circMAP3K5, circCHSY1 and hsa_circ_0001402 ( ). These examples underscore the therapeutic potential of circRNA-miRNA axes in CVDs.
Although extensively studied, rigorous validation of circRNA-miRNA binding sites remains essential. Such sites should be confirmed using both bioinformatic predictions and experimental assays. Additionally, expression levels of circRNAs and miRNAs require quantification across physiological and pathological states to clarify regulatory relationships.

## Protein sponge, scaffold, decoy, recruiter
Beyond sponging miRNAs, a more direct functional repertoire of circRNAs involves their dynamic interactions with proteins. CircRNAs regulate protein–protein interactions via multiple mechanisms, including scaffolding, sequestration, subcellular redistribution, and complex formation. These actions directly impact signaling cascades, cellular processes, and disease phenotypes [ , , , ].
CircRNAs can act as protein sponges by binding to specific proteins. Li et al. found that circHIPK3 binds DRP1, enhancing mitochondrial fission and ROS, thus inducing VSMC necroptosis and atherosclerotic vulnerable plaques [ ]. Sun et al. showed 5-Methylcytosine (m5C)-modified circCCNL2 binds FXR2, reducing free FXR2-CDKL3 interaction to inhibit PASMC proliferation and PH progression [ ]. CircRNAs may also act as protein scaffolds, stabilize or enhance protein interactions. In the cardiovascular context, CircYap further bridges TPM4 and ACTG, attenuating actin polymerization and cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Moreover, as protein decoys, mimicking or occupying protein-binding sites to alter downstream signaling. In cardiac pathology, circCacna1c sequesters Hnrnpf in the cytoplasm, attenuating RIPK1-mediated necroptosis and reducing infarct size in MI [ ]. Similarly, circSSR1 sequesters YTHDF1, enhancing the m6A-dependent translation of SSR1 and triggering endoplasmic reticulum stress in pulmonary hypertension [ ]. As recruiters or adaptors, circRNA can guide proteins to specific subcellular compartments or modulating their activity. CircHelz facilitates nuclear translocation of YAP through NFATc2, promoting cardiac fibroblast proliferation [ ]. These adaptor roles demonstrate circRNAs’ versatility in spatially and functionally rewiring protein networks.
In conclusion, circRNAs orchestrate protein networks through scaffold, decoy, and adaptor mechanisms, offering novel avenues for therapeutic intervention. However, several challenges hinder progress in fully elucidating their molecular functions. A major unresolved question is how circRNAs achieve functional specificity, and how these roles are influenced by chromatin state, metabolic conditions, or transcriptional regulators. Technical limitations persist in studying low-abundance circRNAs in vivo, including resolving circRNA-protein structural conformations, and capturing dynamic subcellular redistribution. Addressing these knowledge gaps is critical for transitioning from descriptive studies to mechanistic insight and therapeutic innovation.

## Competing with pre-mRNA splicing
CircRNAs are generated through back-splicing, a non-canonical splicing event competing with conventional linear splicing, therefore influencing pre-mRNA splicing and mature mRNA production. An increase in circRNA generation can lead to a reduction in linear mRNA output, potentially impacting gene expression levels. The competition between circRNA biogenesis and canonical splicing was first established by Ashwal-Fluss et al., who showed that the splicing factor Muscleblind (MBL) binds its own pre-mRNA to promote circMbl formation at the expense of its linear transcript [ ]. This competitive mechanism extends to human diseases, including non-small cell lung cancer, in which circ_0007386 biogenesis competes with CRIM1 linear splicing to promote tumor progression [ ].
However, this competitive mechanism remains less explored than other circRNA functions, especially in the cardiovascular field, which may be due to the technical challenges in unequivocally distinguishing the effects of circRNA production from the concomitant reduction of its cognate linear mRNA in functional assays. Despite this, its potential role in cardiovascular disease is significant. The cardiovascular system relies on precise splicing regulation of key genes, including those encoding sarcomeric proteins such as the titin gene (TTN), ion channels, and various metabolic enzymes, where the balance of alternative splicing isoforms is critical for normal function. Disruption of this balance is a hallmark of disease. Therefore, investigating whether circRNA biogenesis competes with pre-mRNA splicing may reveal novel regulatory mechanisms in CVD. Future research should focus on the molecular determinants of this competition and its therapeutic implications.

## Transcription regulator
Some circRNAs, particularly EIciRNAs, have been shown to regulate transcriptional and post-transcriptional gene expression by interacting with small nuclear ribonucleoprotein U1 (snRNP U1) and RNA polymerase II. For instance, circEIF3J and circPAIP2 localize in the nucleus and participate in transcriptional enhancement of their parental genes via these interactions [ ]. Furthermore, a recent study by Su et al. identified a class of MRE circRNAs, which interacted specifically with the RNA-binding protein Gawky under conditions of copper stress. MRE circRNAs facilitated the detachment of Gawky from chromatin and promoted its aberrant cytoplasmic accumulation, thereby impairing the recruitment of RNA polymerase II to transcriptionally active gene loci. These findings suggest that the MRE circRNA-RBP-chromatin axis may constitute a conserved regulatory mechanism for gene expression in eukaryotic cells [ ].

## CircRNA interactions with RNA
Certain circRNAs directly bind to RNA molecules, thereby influencing a range of pathophysiological processes [ ]. While the regulation of mRNA stability by circRNAs is typically mediated through indirect mechanisms, via ceRNA activity or RBP scaffolding. Emerging evidence suggests that circRNAs can also directly modulate mRNA stability and translation [ ]. For example, Wang et al. demonstrated that circCAMSAP1 enhanced SERPINH1 expression by stabilizing its mRNA through direct binding to the 3′UTR, thereby promoting nasopharyngeal carcinoma tumorigenesis. Singh et al. identified hundreds of mRNA-interacting circRNAs across cell types of βTC6 (pancreatic β-cells), C2C12 (myoblasts), and HeLa (cervical cancer cells) [ ]. To date, direct circRNA-mRNA interactions have been primarily characterized in cancers, with limited exploration in cardiovascular contexts. With the progress of identification methods, in-depth research on circRNA interactions with RNA in the cardiovascular field can be carried out.

## CircRNA interactions with DNA
Recent studies have unveiled a novel regulatory mechanism by which circRNAs interact with DNA to form circRNA-DNA hybrid structures, known as circR-loops [ ]. These triple-helix structures consist of an RNA-DNA hybrid and a displaced single-stranded DNA (ssDNA). Initially identified in the human mitochondrial genome, circR-loops have since been detected in plants such as Arabidopsis, Populus trichocarpa, and Zea mays [ ]. CircR-loops have been implicated in diverse molecular processes, including modulation of alternative splicing, disruption of DNA replication fidelity, chromatin remodeling, centromere destabilization, and interactions with non-coding RNA networks, collectively contributing to genomic instability and are increasingly associated with cancer and respiratory diseases [ , ].
Importantly, research has begun to link circR-loops to cardiovascular pathophysiology, especially pulmonary hypertension. Song et al. provided the first evidence that nuclear circ-calm4 forms R-loops with the promoter of the COMP gene, suppressing its expression and inducing ferroptosis in pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) [ ]. In parallel, Liu et al. discovered that super-enhancer-associated circLrch3 formed R-loops with its host gene Lrch3, modulating pyroptosis in PASMCs, and exacerbating the progression of pulmonary hypertension [ ]. These discoveries begin to bridge a previously unaddressed gap in the field of cardiovascular circR-loop biology, revealing their mechanistic contribution to disease.
Despite these advances, the functional repertoire of circR-loops remains largely unexplored in the broader cardiovascular context. Future research should focus on developing methodologies tailored to their unique structural and biochemical characteristics. This includes new techniques for isolation, purification, amplification, and stabilization, as well as the establishment of standardized criteria for their identification and classification. Such developments will enable the discovery of previously unrecognized circR-loops that may play critical roles in regulating gene expression, genomic architecture, and disease pathogenesis.

## Polypeptide translation
Some circRNAs contain open reading frames (ORFs) and can function as templates for translation, a discovery that was first reported in 2017 [ ]. Unlike conventional mRNAs that undergo cap-dependent translation, circRNAs lack both a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly(A) tail and are thus translated through cap-independent mechanisms, predominantly via IRES [ ] or by m6A-mediated initiation [ ]. IRES elements are specialized RNA sequences that recruit the 40S ribosomal subunit to the mRNA in a cap-independent manner. Chen et al. used a high-throughput split-eGFP circRNA reporter assay to identify approximately 17,000 putative IRES-containing sequences [ ].
Moreover, m6A, the most common internal RNA modification in mammalian mRNAs, has been shown to facilitate ribosome recruitment. For example, Timoteo et al. demonstrated that YTHDF3 and eIF4G2 modulated the translation of circ-ZNF609 [ ], while Yang et al. showed that even a single m6A site was sufficient to initiate translation, necessitating eIF4G2 and m6A reader YTHDF3, with positive regulation by methyltransferase METTL3/14 and inhibition by FTO [ ]. Notably, some endogenous circRNAs can even encode full-length intact functional proteins, such as β-catenin [ ]. CircRNAs that fail to initiate proper translation may be degraded via the nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) pathway [ , ]. When a circRNA contains an EJC downstream of the stop codon at the back-splice junction (BSJ), it is targeted for degradation. However, if elongating ribosomes displace the EJC, the circRNA can evade NMD and remain stable. Certain circRNAs that lack stop codons undergo rolling-circle translation, continuously generating protein products [ ].
In oncology, several circRNAs have been found to encode cryptic peptides that elicit anti-tumor immune responses. Huang et al. discovered that circFAM53B encoded non-canonically translated HLA-I-binding peptides, which effectively prime naive CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, activating antigen-specific anti-tumor immunity [ ]. In the cardiovascular domain, Du et al. found that circ_0036176 harbored an IRES element and an ORF of 627 nucleotides, encoding the Myo9a-1608 protein, which prevented the progression of cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Conversely, Xu et al. reported that the translation of circular neuroligin RNA (circNlgn) into Nlgn173 contributed to doxorubicin-induced cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Du et al. further demonstrated that Nlgn173, derived from circNlgn, combined with the promoters of ING4 and SGK3 (C8orf44-SGK3). This interaction led to decreased cardiomyocyte viability, enhanced cardiac fibroblast proliferation, and excessive collagen deposition, key features of pathological cardiac remodeling [ ].
Nevertheless, the field of circRNA translation remains in its infancy, and many critical questions remain. Technical challenges hinder the detection of circRNA-derived peptides and proteins. Current proteomic methods lack the sensitivity required to reliably identify low-abundance or cryptic translation products. Moreover, the molecular details of internal ribosome recruitment, including the potential involvement of A-to-I RNA editing and other mRNA modifications beyond m6A, are still not fully defined. These gaps underscore the urgent need for more sensitive analytical platforms, combined with computational and bioinformatics tools, to decode the structural and functional landscapes of circRNA-mediated translation [ ].

## Cell-to-cell communication
Building upon the diverse intracellular roles of circRNAs, their functional repertoire extends beyond single-cell autonomy to mediate intercellular signaling. This communication is primarily facilitated through their packaging into exosomes, a subtype of extracellular vesicles (EVs) ranging from 40 to 160 nm in diameter, with an average size of approximately 100 nm. These vesicles, which are enclosed within a lipid bilayer membrane, are secreted by most eukaryotic cells [ ]. CircRNAs have been found to be abundantly and stably expressed in exosomes, underscoring their potential role in long-range signaling [ ].
Currently, four primary mechanisms have been proposed to explain how circRNAs are selectively packaged into exosomes. 1) RBP-mediated mechanism: Specific RNA-binding proteins facilitate circRNA entry into exosomes. For instance, hnRNPA2B1 promotes the packaging of circCCAR1, circ-CDYL, and circNEIL3 into exosomes [ , , ]. KHDRBS3 mediates the exosomal sorting of circ_0088300 [ ]. Additionally, protein complexes within exosomes play regulatory roles; circRHOBTB3 is sorted through interaction with SNF8, a component of the ESCRT-II complex [ , ].
2) miRNA-mediated regulation: miRNA abundance influences circRNA sorting by competing for shared binding sites. Stimulation with miR-7 reduces exosomal abundance of circCDR1as [ , ]. miRNAs can also promote intracellular circRNA breakdown, thereby reducing material available for export. 3) m6A modification: m6A methylation influences circRNA conformation, interactions with exosomal proteins, and stability, indirectly affecting exosomal inclusion [ ]. Notably, m6A modification of circ-CDYL promotes its exosomal sorting, suggesting cooperation between epitranscriptomic regulation and RBP-mediated pathways [ ]. 4) Stress-induced modulation: Under cellular stress, changes in exosome composition and release alter the type and abundance of exosomal circRNAs. Stress pathways modulate both cargo selection and secretion dynamics, serving as an adaptive intercellular communication mechanism in pathophysiological conditions.
Emerging studies have begun to reveal the functional importance of exosomal circRNAs. In the cardiovascular context, exosome-derived circ_0001785 has been reported to attenuate endothelial dysfunction and delay atherogenesis via the miR-513a-5p/TGFBR3 axis, underscoring its therapeutic potential [ ]. Nevertheless, the specific mechanisms governing circRNA sorting into exosomes within cardiovascular cell types, represent a significant knowledge gap. Key challenges include identifying cardiovascular-specific RNA-binding proteins, clarifying stress-induced export signals, and determining whether conserved pathways, including m6A, operate similarly in the heart and vasculature. Elucidating these mechanisms will be essential for leveraging exosomal circRNAs as biomarkers or engineered therapeutics in cardiovascular diseases.

## Degradation of circRNAs

## RNAse family-mediated circRNA degradation
The degradation of circRNAs is a crucial aspect of their metabolic regulation and functional lifespan. Unlike linear RNAs, circRNAs are generally more resistant to exonuclease activity due to their covalently closed structure; however, specific endonucleases mediate their degradation under defined conditions. RNase H1 has been shown to degrade circRNAs that form circRNA-DNA hybrid structures (circR-loops). In addition, circRNAs are globally degraded by RNase L under innate immune activation, such as after poly(I:C) stimulation or viral infection. Moreover, a significant advancement was reported by Chen et al., who found that in normal cells, the endonucleolytic enzyme DIS3 selectively degraded circRNAs across the transcriptome by identifying U-rich motifs, independent of the canonical exosome complex. This discovery uncovered a previously unrecognized pathway for circRNA degradation and introduced the possibility of regulating circRNA stability through sequence engineering [ ].
In the cardiovascular context, recent research has identified mecciRNAs as key regulators of mitochondrial function. Liu et al. demonstrated that the mitochondrial RNA helicase SUPV3L1, in conjunction with the endonuclease ELAC2, mediated the rapid degradation of mecciRNAs. These circRNAs regulated mitochondrial ROS and mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) activity. In models of heart failure (HF), impaired mecciRNA degradation leads to mitochondrial dysfunction. Notably, exogenous supplementation of mecciRNAs in murine models rescued cardiac phenotypes, highlighting their therapeutic potential in mitochondrial and cardiovascular disorders [ ]. Notably, this pathway may be particularly relevant in cardiovascular tissues, given the high metabolic demands and susceptibility to oxidative stress in cardiomyocytes. Whether degradative enzymes such as DIS3, RNase L, or SUPV3L1 are enriched or dysregulated in failing hearts or atherosclerotic vessels remains an open question that warrants further investigation.

## MiRNA/protein complex-mediated degradation
Recent findings suggest that miRNAs may participate in the destruction of circRNA, a process which is facilitated by Argonaute2 (Ago2), an active slicer enzyme. For instance, circRNA CDR1as can interact with several miRNAs. In the case of miR-671, CDR1as binds to it forming a double-stranded structure. Ago2 then recognizes and cleaves this structure, resulting in its degradation [ ]. In the spinal nucleus, circRNA-Filip1l sponges miRNA-1224, which is also degraded in an Ago2-dependent manner [ ]. These findings demonstrate that miRNA-mediated circRNA degradation represents a distinct, physiologically significant, and evolutionarily conserved regulatory mechanism.
In addition to Ago2, GW182 is implicated in circRNA degradation. Depletion of GW182 leads to the accumulation of steady-state circDati and circLaccase2 transcripts, while having little effect on most nascent circRNAs. GW182 proteins comprise several functional domains: an Ago-binding domain (ABD), a ubiquitin-associated domain (UBA), a glutamine-rich domain (Q-rich), a middle region (Mid), an RNA-recognition motif (RRM), and a C-terminal region (C-term). The ABD of GW182 is reported to mediate its interaction with Ago proteins in the RNA interference (RNAi) pathway and the Mid-domain of GW182 also contributes to circRNA degradation [ ].

## Epigenetic and structural degradation of circRNA
M6A methylation is the most prevalent epigenetic modification and plays a key role in regulating RNA stability and degradation. The first evidence of circRNA degradation mediated by m6A mutation was reported by Park et al. in 2019. Their study showed that YTHDF2 recognized and interacted with m6A sites on circRNAs, resulting in RNA degradation [ ]. More recent studies have found that circ3823, which was degraded by YTHDF3 and ALKBH5, in cooperation with YTHDF2 in colorectal cancer, may serve as a novel therapeutic target [ ].
Structure-mediated RNA decay (SRD) is another mechanism regulating the degradation of highly structured RNAs, particularly those with a 3′ untranslated region forming complex secondary structures in vivo. This process involves two RBPs: UPF1 and G3BP1. The regulation of circRNAs is contingent upon the RNA-binding and helicase activities of UPF1, as well as the RNA-binding capacity and phosphorylation at serine 149 (S149) of G3BP1. Although both proteins associate with structured and less structured circRNAs, the extent of their binding varies, and the underlying reasons for this variability remain poorly understood [ ].

## External and systemic factors-mediated circRNA degradation
External factors, such as the gut microbiota (GM), can influence circRNA degradation. The GM is capable of converting various dietary nutrients into trimethylamine (TMA) [ ]. A significant portion of TMA is oxidized to trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) by hepatic flavin-containing monooxygenase (FMO) [ ]. High-sugar and high-fat diets murine models have been shown to alter GM composition, elevate TMAO levels, and modulate circRNA expression profiles. Subsequent studies revealed that certain circRNAs may serve as sensitive signaling molecules in response to TMAO, indicating that TMAO may affect circRNA synthesis and degradation [ ]. In addition to intracellular degradation, circRNAs can also be eliminated from cells through EVs, including exosomes and microvesicles.

## CircRNA engineering: Advances in vitro synthesis, delivery systems, vaccine development, potential aptamers, and gene editing

## In vitro synthesis of circRNAs

## Circularization techniques
CircRNA production relies on a range of circularization techniques. Traditional methods, such as the permuted intron–exon (PIE) system, utilize group I self-splicing introns but often leave residual exon-derived sequences, which may trigger immune responses. To achieve scarless circularization, ribozyme-based methods using the Tetrahymena thermophila group I intron have shown promise. Cui et al. reported 80 % efficiency both in vitro and in vivo using this ribozyme alongside a split GFP reporter and optimized IRES [ ]. Enzymatic strategies, including those using T4 RNA ligases, generate homogeneous circRNAs but struggle with long precursor transcripts [ ]. He et al. engineered a thermostable T7 RNA polymerase mutant (G788A), achieving over 95 % cyclization efficiency and reducing dsRNA byproducts [ ]. Hybrid methods, such as group II intron-mediated PIE and RtcB-driven in-cell circularization, further expand scarless synthesis capabilities, though length limitations remain [ , ].

## Control of efficiency and quality
CircRNA functionality depends on both efficient circularization and structural integrity. While chemical/enzymatic methods are restricted to in vitro use, ribozymes are compatible with both in vitro and in vivo applications [ ]. Fukuchi et al. revolutionized circRNA translation by engineering m7G-capped circRNAs (cap-circ mRNAs), which recruited ribosomes 50 times more efficiently than IRES-dependent systems. Hybridization with m7G-capped oligonucleotides further enhanced cell-type-selective expression, minimizing immunogenicity through the use of m1Ψ modifications [ ]. Quality control methods include qPCR to assess circularization efficiency, capillary electrophoresis or nanopore sequencing for purity analysis, and next-generation sequencing (NGS) to detect structural anomalies [ ]. Scalable strategies for removing non-functional or incomplete sequences remain critical for therapeutic development.

## Research bottlenecks and practical challenges
Despite significant progress, several practical bottlenecks impede the translational path of synthetic circRNAs. Scalability is a primary concern, as moving from laboratory-scale synthesis to Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)-level production of high-purity, homogeneous circRNA populations presents major technical and economic hurdles. Off-target effects and immunogenicity remain substantial risks; while modifications like m1Ψ can minimize innate immune sensing, unintended immune activation by residual linear RNAs or byproducts must be rigorously controlled. Furthermore, achieving cell-type-specific delivery in vivo without off-target accumulation and ensuring efficient and predictable translation across different tissues are unresolved challenges. Addressing these bottlenecks is crucial for harnessing the full therapeutic potential of circRNAs.

## Advanced delivery system of circRNA

## LNPs-based delivery
Beyond efficient circRNA synthesis, advanced delivery systems are essential for evading immune clearance and achieving targeted tissue expression. Lipid nanoparticles represent promising carriers due to their structural stability and controllable release profiles. Systems including solid lipid nanoparticles, nanostructured lipid carriers, and cationic lipid amphiphiles have been widely applied for RNA delivery in cancer models including lung cancer [ ] and prostate cancer [ ], inflammatory diseases such as sepsis [ ], and degenerative disorders including intervertebral disc degeneration [ ]. Soroudi et al. further highlighted LNP-based mRNA delivery strategies for CAR T-cell therapy and CRISPR/Cas genome editing in CVDs, underscoring their clinical translational potential [ ]. However, no successful applications of circRNA-loaded LNPs have been reported in the context of cardiovascular diseases.

## Exosomes-based delivery
Exosomes have emerged as natural delivery vehicles with high biocompatibility and low immunogenicity. They have been utilized for circRNA delivery in models of sepsis [ ] and multiple cancer types including breast and pancreatic cancers [ ] through surface modification and cargo-loading optimization. In cardiovascular applications, exosome-based systems have shown promise for other RNA therapeutics. For instance, stem cell membrane-camouflaged exosome-mimicking nanocomplexes delivered miRNAs for myocardial infarction repair [ ], while IMTP-modified milk exosomes successfully delivered miR-146a to attenuate myocardial ischemia–reperfusion injury by suppressing NF-κB signaling [ ]. Despite these advances, exosome-mediated circRNA delivery has not yet been successfully demonstrated in cardiovascular models.

## Current challenges and future perspectives
Notably, studies reporting successful delivery systems for circRNAs in the cardiovascular context remain remarkably scarce. Against this backdrop, we have systematically summarized the currently available successful delivery systems [ , , , , , , , , , , ], which are tailored for cardiovascular settings ( ). Both LNPs and exosomes face significant challenges for cardiovascular circRNA delivery. For LNPs, key limitations include achieving sufficient cardiac-specific targeting beyond passive accumulation, minimizing off-target effects in highly perfused organs, and reducing potential immunogenicity while ensuring endosomal escape efficiency. Exosomes confront challenges related to scalable production, batch-to-batch consistency, and efficient circRNA loading without compromising vesicle integrity. Additionally, both systems must overcome the hemodynamic environment of the heart and circulating nuclease activity to maintain circRNA stability. Current RNA delivery successes in CVDs have been achieved with other nucleic acid therapeutics. Intramyocardial delivery of modRNA-LNPs enhanced transfection in cardiac fibroblasts and endothelial cells [ ], while VEGF-C mRNA-loaded LNPs promoted cardiac lymphangiogenesis and functional recovery after myocardial infarction [ ]. Developing efficient and heart-targeted delivery systems tailored for circRNA therapeutics remains an essential direction for future research.
 

## Development of circRNA vaccines
mRNA vaccine technologies have rapidly progressed in recent years, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic [ , ]. However, linear mRNA vaccines are limited by their inherent instability, relatively low translational efficiency, and innate immunogenicity [ ]. With the growing understanding of circRNA mechanisms, particularly the use of internal ribosome entry sites (IRESs) and open reading frames (ORFs) for polypeptide translation, along with the progress of in vitro circRNA synthesis, circRNA-based vaccines have emerged as a promising platform. They offer advantages in safety, stability, simplified manufacturing, and scalability [ ].
In designing circRNA vaccines, in addition to optimizing structural stability, translational efficiency, and specific interactions with target molecules, it is crucial to address immunogenicity, safety, and broad-spectrum protection against viral variants. Enhancing circularization methods is key to minimizing immunogenicity. Among various synthesis methods, circRNAs produced using T4 RNA ligase demonstrate the lowest immunogenicity. Further refinements, such as incorporating unstructured elements at the flanking ends to avoid complex secondary structures or adding modifiable homologous spacers have been suggested to improve translational efficiency and safety.
Several studies have explored the potential of circRNA vaccines in treating a wide range of diseases. For example, circRNAs have been engineered to encode SARS-CoV-2 receptors, leading to the development of the circRNA-RBD-Delta vaccine to combat COVID-19 [ ]. Huang et al. discovered that circFAM53B noncanonically translated cryptic antigenic peptides capable of binding to HLA-I and stimulating naive CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in an antigen-specific manner, eliciting strong anti-tumor immune responses [ , ].
Increasing evidence supports the efficacy of RNA-based therapeutic platforms across diverse disease contexts, including type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and cystic fibrosis. However, there is no direct studies have been conducted in CVD models to date, with most advances emerging from oncology and virology. Key challenges for cardiovascular application include cell-specific delivery, safety in chronic diseases, and sustained efficacy in relevant microenvironments. Substantial biological differences, such as distinct immune microenvironments between tumors and atherosclerotic plaques, potential variations in antigen presentation, and the current absence of preclinical vaccination studies specifically for cardiovascular diseases, further complicate direct extrapolation. Direct translation of these findings to cardiovascular contexts, necessitates rigorous experimental and preclinical validation. Nonetheless, given the expanding insights into circRNA roles in CVDs, circRNA-based vaccines represent a promising future direction for cardioprotective therapy.

## CircRNA as a potential aptamer
CircRNAs have emerged as promising aptamer candidates due to their covalently closed structure, which provides enhanced stability, reduced immunogenicity, and distinct three-dimensional folding properties that enable precise molecular interactions. Recent advances highlight their potential in both therapeutic and diagnostic applications. In therapeutic contexts, Chen et al. developed double-stranded circRNA (ds-cRNA) aptamers delivered via adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) to inhibit PKR in Alzheimer’s disease models, leading to significant improvements in cognitive function in mice. Similarly, spleen-targeted LNPs loaded with ds-cRNA effectively reduced PKR-mediated inflammation and epidermal hyperplasia in psoriasis, demonstrating their potential for immune modulation [ ].
In the realm of RNA dynamics monitoring, Yuan et al. introduced the “circDucker” biosensor, a circRNA-based aptamer system enabling high-contrast, spatiotemporal visualization of endogenous RNA translocation, including mitochondrial lncRNA transport between the nucleus and cytoplasm [ ]. Together, these studies underscore the dual utility of circRNA aptamers in modulating disease pathways and enabling ultrasensitive, real-time molecular detection. However, precise structural validation and sequence-specific targeting remain crucial for advancing their clinical translation.

## CircRNA as a tool for gene editing
Synthetic circRNAs are emerging as modular and programmable tools for gene editing, capitalizing on their intrinsic stability and capacity to scaffold complex molecular systems. Su et al. engineered circRNAs encoding an endoplasmic reticulum-targeted Cas13 (erCas13). When co-delivered with single guide RNAs (sgRNA), these circRNAs effectively degraded viral RNA in infected cells [ ]. Liang et al. expanded this approach by designing synthetic circRNAs that harbored tandem CRISPR RNA (crRNA) arrays and reverse transcriptase template-primer binding sites (RTT-PBS), enabling Cas12a-based prime editors to simultaneously target and edit four distinct genomic loci in human cells [ ]. These advancements emphasize circRNAs’ versatility as synthetic platforms capable of orchestrating genome editing machinery, spatially organizing subcellular structures, and mediating complex biochemical transformations.

## CircRNAs regulate key signaling pathways in CVDs
CircRNAs have emerged as critical regulators in cardiovascular pathophysiology by intricately modulating key signaling pathways [ , ]. This section integrates recent advances across several major pathways, including TGF-β, Wnt/β-catenin, NF-κB, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, Hippo/YAP, FUS/VEGF-A, p53γ, Pink1/FAM65B and the ubiquitination pathway and their functional interaction with circRNA ( ).
 

## CircRNAs and the TGF-β signaling pathway

## TGF-β signaling
Transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1), a prototypical member of a large family of structurally related proteins, is activated through ligand-induced oligomerization of serine/threonine receptor kinases. This activation leads to the phosphorylation of cytoplasmic signaling molecules, Smad2 and Smad3, in the TGF-β/activin pathway [ , ] or Smad1/5/9 in the bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) pathway [ , , , ]. TGF-β signaling maintains tissue homeostasis and mediates injury repair by regulating cell proliferation, migration, phenotypic plasticity, metabolic adaptation, and immune surveillance across diverse cell types [ ].
TGF-β signaling plays a central role in myocardial fibrosis through diverse regulatory mechanisms. For example, coagulation factor XI activates BMP7, which suppresses the BMP-SMAD1/5 axis and reduces inflammatory and fibrotic gene expression in heart failure models [ ]. Additionally, acetate and acetyl-CoA synthetase 2 (ACSS2) stabilize TGF-β signaling via ALK5/SMAD2/4 acetylation, promoting endothelial-mesenchymal transition and fibrosis [ , ]. In inflammation and recovery contexts, TGF-β superfamily members contribute to cardiac repair and inflammatory regulation. BMP9 enhances lymphatic drainage and mitochondrial bioenergetics after myocardial infarction through DECR1-mediated organ crosstalk [ ]. Furthermore, eosinophil-derived cationic proteins promote vascular calcification via the BMPR‐Smad1/5/8‐Runx2 axis in aortic stenosis, underscoring the role of BMP signaling in inflammation-driven pathology [ ].

## Translational advances and challenges
Clinically, the TGF-β superfamily has shown potential in diagnostics and prognostics. For instance, Masukar et al. identified GDF3, a cardiac-derived cytokine from the TGF-β family, as a key mediator secreted by cardiac stromal cells that promotes fibrosis post-MI. GDF3 was proposed as a biomarker for adverse cardiac remodeling, with ongoing clinical investigation (NCT01113268) [ ]. Despite significant progress, several key challenges remain. The cell-type-specific responses to TGF-β signaling, such as the distinct roles of pericytes in infarct vasculature maturation and the plasticity of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), are not yet fully elucidated, especially in the context of coordinated disease progression. Clinical translation is further complicated by the dual nature of TGF-β signaling, which can exert both protective and pathological effects. Addressing this paradox will require refined approaches, including the development of SMAD-binding aptamers, modulation of downstream effectors, and patient stratification based on pathway activity.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the TGF-β signaling
Restoring physiological TGF-β signaling through therapeutic intervention is a developing field of rigorous investigation, with growing evidence highlighting circRNAs as pivotal regulators of TGF-β networks across CVDs ( ). In MI, Cai et al. demonstrated that circ-Ttc3 exerted cardioprotective effects in a TGFβ1-induced MI model by sequestering miR-15b-5p, thereby upregulating Arl2 expression. This mitigated hypoxia-induced ATP depletion and cardiomyocyte apoptosis, indirectly attenuating TGFβ1-driven fibrotic remodeling [ ]. Cardiac fibrosis, characterized by fibroblast activation and phenotypic transition. Liu et al. showed that hypoxia significantly upregulated CircHIPK3 in CFs, where it sponged miR-152-3p to activate the TGF-β2 axis, promoting CF proliferation, migration, and differentiation [ ]. In atrial fibrillation (AF), Gao et al. identified hsa_circ_0004104 as a key regulator. Its significant downregulation in both in vitro and in vivo AF models exacerbated fibrosis by dysregulating miR-30 and miR-3280, thereby disrupting the crosstalk between the MAPK and TGF-β pathways [ ]. Zhang et al. further delineated the role of CircCAMTA1 in AF, showing that its knockdown alleviated AngII-induced atrial fibrosis by sequestering miR-214-3p, leading to suppression of TGFBR1 expression [ ]. Additionally, a study by Wei et al. revealed that circ_0079480 enhanced TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling in human atrial fibroblasts (HAFs) by sponging miR-338-3p, which elevated THBS1 expression and accelerated Ang II-induced fibrotic progression [ ].
 In AS, circRNAs exhibit divergent roles in vascular injury. Wang et al. reported that circ_CHMP5, elevated in the serum of AS patients and in ox-LDL-treated HUVECs, exacerbated endothelial injury by sponging miR-516b-5p, thereby activating the TGFβR2 pathway [ ]. Conversely, Ye et al. demonstrated that circCOL1A suppressed TGF-β signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) by sponging miR-30a-5p, leading to upregulation of SMAD1, which promoted phenotypic switching and contributes to plaque instability [ ]. In aortic valve disease, circANKRD36 regulated EndMT and cellular heterogeneity by interacting with miR-599, thereby inhibiting TGF-β pathway activation and reducing osteogenic differentiation and calcified nodule formation in valve interstitial cells (VICs) [ ]. In pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), Su et al. identified circ-Ntrk2 as a hypoxia-induced driver of vascular remodeling that sponged miR-296-5p, preventing its binding to the TGF-β1 3′UTR, thus enhancing TGF-β1 translation and activating the TGF-β1/p38 MAPK axis [ ]. Finally, in HF, Zhang et al. showed that circ_0062389 knockdown ameliorated cardiomyocyte apoptosis by modulating the TGF-β1/Smad3 axis, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target [ ].
In conclusion, circRNAs have emerged as critical regulators of TGF-β signaling in CVDs. Current research on circRNA-mediated regulation of TGF-β signaling in CVDs reveals several key limitations. First, although most studies focus on circRNAs functioning as miRNA that indirectly modulate downstream effectors such as Smad3 or THBS1, direct interactions between circRNAs and upstream components of the TGF-β pathway, including ligands such as TGF-β1/2/3, receptors including TGF-βR2, or early signaling adaptors including Smad4, remain largely unexplored. This limits our understanding of how circRNAs may directly influence TGF-β ligand-receptor interactions or pathway activation. Second, the isoform-specific regulatory roles of circRNAs toward distinct TGF-β subtypes are poorly characterized. For instance, it remains unclear whether circRNAs selectively amplify pro-fibrotic TGF-β1 or counteract anti-inflammatory TGF-β3, hindering insights into their context-dependent regulatory functions. Third, the temporal dynamics of circRNA-TGF-β interactions are largely neglected. TGF-β signaling transitions from adaptive to maladaptive across disease states, such as acute injury to chronic fibrosis. Collectively, these gaps hinder the development of circRNA-targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring TGF-β homeostasis. Future research should prioritize mechanistic investigations, including circRNA-TGF-β ligand/receptor interaction assays, isoform- and stage-specific functional validations, comprehensive multi-pathway crosstalk mapping, and exploration of feedback and degradation pathways. These efforts will be critical to fully unlock the therapeutic potential of circRNAs in TGF-β-driven cardiovascular pathologies.

## CircRNAs and the Wnt/β-catenin pathway

## Wnt/β-catenin signaling
The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway regulates gene expression and cellular homeostasis by controlling the stability and nuclear translocation of β-catenin. In the absence of Wnt ligands, a cytoplasmic destruction complex, containing APC, AXIN, CK1, and GSK-3β, phosphorylates β-catenin, leading to its ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation [ , ]. Upon binding of Wnt ligands such as Wnt3a to Frizzled receptors and LRP5/6 co-receptors, the destruction complex dissociates, enabling β-catenin to accumulate and enter the nucleus. There, it associates with TCF/LEF transcription factors to activate target genes including MMPs and c-Myc [ ]. Beyond its roles in development and tissue homeostasis [ , ], this pathway is increasingly implicated in cardiovascular diseases, influencing angiogenesis, fibrosis, and cardiomyocyte survival. Dysregulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling contributes significantly to pathological remodeling in the heart, highlighting its dual role in both physiological and pathological contexts.
The Wnt/β-catenin pathway plays critical yet dual roles in cardiovascular development and disease. In vascular biology, Wnt5a regulates cholesterol homeostasis and plaque progression in atherosclerosis, while Wnt5a-enriched extracellular vesicles promote aortic valve calcification via osteogenic differentiation [ , ]. In cardiac pathology, persistent Wnt/β-catenin activation drives fibrosis and maladaptive remodeling, such as through LRP6-mediated regulation of profibrotic ligands in pressure overload and FOSL1/2-induced collagen synthesis in pulmonary hypertension [ , ]. Conversely, stage-specific Wnt activation supports cardiomyocyte differentiation from human pluripotent stem cells, highlighting its potential in cardiac regeneration and tissue engineering, though precise spatiotemporal control remains a challenge for therapeutic translation [ ].

## Translational advances and challenges
To date, clinical applications targeting the Wnt/β-catenin pathway remain largely confined to oncology. Inhibitors such as BC2059, PRI-724, and LGK974 have demonstrated efficacy in preclinical and clinical trials for malignancies including multiple myeloma and acute myeloid leukemia, primarily by blocking nuclear β-catenin accumulation or disrupting Wnt ligand secretion [ , , , ]. While the Wnt pathway has been extensively investigated in oncology, neuroscience, and developmental biology, relatively less research has been conducted in the context of cardiovascular medicine. Despite growing evidence implicating Wnt signaling in fibrosis, calcification, and cardiac regeneration, cardiovascular applications have lagged behind. This translational gap is partly attributed to the mechanistic complexity of the Wnt pathway and the necessity for spatiotemporal precision, particularly in the setting of cardiac tissue engineering. Advancing this field requires the development of cardiovascular-specific Wnt modulators, deeper investigation into Wnt-inflammatory crosstalk, and refinement of targeted therapies to mitigate Wnt-driven fibrosis and hypertrophy. Addressing these challenges is essential to bridge the gap between preclinical promise and clinical utility in CVD.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling
Recent studies have underscored the regulatory role of circRNAs in modulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in CVDs, particularly in AS and MI, positioning them as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets ( ). In the context of MI, Huang et al. investigated the function of circNfix in β-catenin regulation. Through chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) and electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), they demonstrated that the transcription factor Meis1 interacted with the E2 region within a super-enhancer at the circNfix locus. CircNfix functioned as a sponge for miR-214, leading to upregulation of Gsk3β and subsequent inhibition of β-catenin activity. Furthermore, RNA pulldown and luciferase reporter assays revealed that circNfix promoted Ybx1 ubiquitination via Nedd4l, resulting in suppression of cyclin A2/B1, ultimately impairing cardiomyocyte proliferation and angiogenesis [ ]. These findings highlight the cardioprotective effects of circNfix knockdown following MI. In the setting of myocardial fibrosis, Li et al. demonstrated that circARAP1 exacerbated cardiac fibrosis during myocardial ischemia–reperfusion injury (MI/RI) [ ]. Gain-of-function experiments revealed that circARAP1 modulated the miR-379-5p/KLF9 axis, thereby activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and promoting fibrotic remodelling. Similarly, Ji et al. elucidated the pro-fibrotic role of circNSD1 in both in-vivo and in-vitro MI models [ ]. CircNSD1 functioned as a sponge for miR-429-3p, resulting in the upregulation of Sulfatase 1 (SULF1), which subsequently activated the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade.
 Most current studies focus on circRNAs as miRNA sponges in regulating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway in CVDs. However, this narrow mechanistic perspective overlooks other potential functions, including direct RNA-protein interactions and epigenetic modulation of Wnt pathway components. Clinically, while preclinical models support circRNA-Wnt crosstalk, translational gaps persist due to limited validation in human tissues, inadequate characterization of tissue-specific expression patterns, and a lack of longitudinal studies. Additionally, the cell type-and stage-specific roles of circRNAs remain poorly understood, as many investigations rely on simplified or single-cell models, potentially limiting translational relevance and therapeutic precision. Future research should aim to explore multi-faceted mechanisms of circRNA action, including their roles in phase separation and translational regulation, and to perform spatially resolved single-cell analyses to map circRNA-Wnt interactions in vivo. Further, the development of targeted delivery systems, such as nanoparticle-mediated therapies, is essential for selectively modulating pathological circRNAs and advancing their clinical applicability in Wnt-driven cardiovascular disease.

## CircRNAs and the PAM signaling pathway

## PAM signaling
The PAM pathway is a highly conserved signaling cascade that plays a fundamental role in regulating cellular survival, growth, and metabolic homeostasis. In the cardiovascular system, this pathway governs cardiomyocyte viability, hypertrophic responses, angiogenesis, and inflammatory modulation, positioning it as a central mediator of cardiac physiology and pathophysiology [ , , ]. Among the three AKT isoforms, AKT1 and AKT2 are predominantly expressed in cardiac tissue and are involved in both adaptive and maladaptive responses to cellular stress [ , , ]. Dysregulation of PAM signaling impairs redox balance, autophagic flux, and mitochondrial dynamics, thereby contributing to the progression of CVDs such as ischemic injury and HF [ , , ].
The PI3K/AKT pathway critically regulates cardiovascular cell survival, metabolism, and remodeling. After myocardial infarction, its activation enhances cell survival and angiogenesis, with interventions such as PI3K inhibition or activation improving outcomes [ , ]. In atherosclerosis, it regulates endothelial and macrophage functions, for instance, through OASL maintaining eNOS activity, while nutrient excess promotes foam cell formation via mTOR-PI3K/AKT [ , ]. In remodeling, it shows dual roles: suppressing hypertrophy in some contexts yet contributing to electrical dysfunction in Brugada syndrome [ , ]. In cardiotoxicity, PI3K/AKT activation can be either protective or harmful, highlighting its context-dependent nature [ , ]. Fine-tuned modulation of this pathway offers broad therapeutic potential across cardiovascular diseases.

## Translational advances and challenges
The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway represents a central regulator of cell survival, metabolism, and proliferation with broad therapeutic implications in cardiovascular diseases. Although several PI3K or AKT inhibitors originally developed for oncology are being repurposed to treat pathological hypertrophy and vascular remodeling [ , , ], clinical translation remains challenging due to the pathway’s context-dependent roles: it supports repair and angiogenesis in endothelial and cardiomyocyte contexts, yet exacerbates fibrosis and plaque instability in fibroblasts and inflammatory cells. Notably, clinical studies have identified a leucine-mediated threshold in protein intake that activates mTOR in monocytes/macrophages, driving atherosclerosis, highlighting nutrient-pathway crosstalk as a modifiable risk factor (NCT03946774 and NCT03994367). Systemic inhibition also carries risks of metabolic disturbances including insulin resistance, underscoring the need for tissue-specific modulation. Future efforts should prioritize pathway-selective inhibitors, combination regimens to maximize efficacy and minimize toxicity, and biomarker-guided patient stratification.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the PAM signaling
Emerging evidence indicates that circRNAs play a critical role in the regulation of CVDs by modulating the PAM pathway through diverse mechanisms ( ). In AS, circZBTB46 has been shown to bind hnRNPA2B, stabilizing PTEN, which in turn suppresses AKT/mTOR signaling, thereby inhibiting vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation and exacerbating plaque formation [ ]. Similarly, circ_0040414 attenuates inflammatory responses in chronic HF by sponging miR-186-5p to upregulate PTEN, leading to AKT inhibition and promoting cardiomyocyte survival through anti-inflammatory signaling [ ]. In the context of angiogenesis, hsa_circ_0004543 knockdown enhances oxLDL-induced proliferation of endothelial cells (ECs) via activation of the PI3K/AKT/NOS3 pathway. Conversely, circ_0002984 promotes VSMC migration in AS by sponging miR-181b-5p, leading to VEGFA upregulation and subsequent activation of PI3K/AKT signaling [ , ]. Notably, circ-Amotl1 exhibits cardioprotective properties by directly binding PDK1 and AKT1, thereby facilitating the nuclear translocation of phosphorylated AKT, which attenuates doxorubicin-induced cardiomyopathy [ ].
 CircRNAs exert multifaceted regulatory roles on the PAM pathway across various cardiovascular pathologies. However, significant challenges remain in translating these findings into therapeutic applications. One major limitation lies in the cell type-specific effects of circRNAs: for example, certain circRNAs suppress AKT/mTOR signaling in VSMCs to exacerbate AS, while others activate the same pathway in ECs to confer cardioprotection. This apparent inconsistency underscores the need to delineate the context-dependent regulation of the PAM pathway by circRNAs. Additionally, current studies have predominantly focused on miRNA-dependent mechanisms, overlooking other regulatory modes such as direct circRNA-protein interactions, which may exert broader influence over the pathway. Additionally, the lack of integration between circRNA activity and upstream signaling events, including growth factor receptor activation, limits understanding of how extracellular signals shape circRNA-PAM networks in dynamic physiological environments. To address these gaps, future studies should prioritize mapping circRNA expression patterns across cardiovascular cell types, developing targeted delivery platforms to minimize off-target effects, and investigating circRNA-mediated feedback loops. Validation in physiologically relevant in vivo models will be essential for advancing circRNA-based interventions targeting the PAM pathway in CVDs.

## CircRNAs and the Hippo signaling pathway

## Hippo signaling
The Hippo signaling pathway is an evolutionarily conserved regulatory network that orchestrates tissue homeostasis, organ size, and cell fate decisions by regulating mammalian cell proliferation, apoptosis, and differentiation [ , , , , , , ]. At the core of this pathway are the transcriptional coactivators YAP and TAZ, whose activity is tightly controlled by a kinase cascade involving MST1/2 and LATS1/2. Phosphorylation of MST1/2, in complex with SAV1, activates LATS1/2 and MOB1, which then phosphorylate YAP/TAZ at critical residues such as Ser127 and Ser381. Dysregulation of Hippo-YAP/TAZ signaling is implicated in a range of diseases, from cancer to cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis, underscoring its emerging therapeutic relevance.
The Hippo-YAP/TAZ pathway plays multifaceted roles in cardiac and vascular remodeling. In the heart, it regulates hypertrophy, fibrosis, and regenerative capacity. YAP activation promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation but also contributes to fibrotic matrix deposition, and its inhibition, such as with verteporfin, reduces fibrosis without fully restoring function [ , , ]. In vascular biology, Hippo signaling mediates pulmonary hypertension through TAZ-RUNX2-driven osteogenic transdifferentiation and influences thrombotic events via JCAD-mediated PI3K/Akt activation [ , ]. Additionally, the pathway is involved in arrhythmogenesis, where LATS1/2 inactivation disrupts calcium handling and promotes fibrotic remodeling in the sinoatrial node [ ]. These findings highlight the context-dependent functions of Hippo-YAP/TAZ and its potential as a therapeutic target in diverse cardiovascular conditions.

## Translational advances and challenges
Recent clinical trials highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting the Hippo signaling pathway in CVDs. For instance, in patients with aortic aneurysms, activation of the Hippo pathway has been associated with reduced autophagy and elevated oxidative stress in aortic wall tissues (NCT03211000). These findings suggest that Hippo inhibition, either pharmacologically through agents such as verteporfin or through autophagy-enhancing compounds like rapamycin, may mitigate aortic wall degeneration and offer a novel therapeutic strategy for aneurysm management. Another clinical trial explored propranolol’s efficacy in vascular tumors, linking its anti-angiogenic effects to YAP suppression (NCT02334930). These results support the potential of β-blockers as therapeutic agents for Hippo-driven vascular remodeling. While these studies highlight the translational relevance of Hippo signaling, further validation is required in patient populations with common comorbidities to better assess safety, specificity, and therapeutic window.
Despite these advances, critical gaps persist. Current research largely centers on YAP/TAZ effectors, neglecting upstream Hippo kinases such as MST1/2 or non-canonical mechanisms, including NF2/AMOT-YAP interactions during cardiomyocyte lineage specification. Clinical translation is also hampered by preclinical models that often fail to account for complex patient phenotypes, such as coexisting metabolic disorders. Furthermore, studies are often limited by insufficient validation in human tissues. Future efforts should prioritize single-cell multi-omics approaches to resolve the spatial and temporal dynamics of Hippo signaling across cardiac and vascular cell types. Engineering cell type-specific therapeutic interventions, such as YAP acetylation inhibitors or circRNA-targeted nanotherapies, will be crucial to enhancing precision and minimizing off-target effects in cardiovascular applications.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the Hippo signaling
Mounting evidence reveals a reciprocal interaction between the Hippo signaling cascade and non-coding RNAs including circRNAs. While several microRNAs, such as miR-130a and miR-21, have been shown to regulate YAP/TAZ activity in cardiovascular contexts [ , ], the regulation of Hippo signaling by circRNAs is an emerging area of study ( ). Shao et al. reported that circRNA cerebellar degeneration-related protein 1 antisense (Circ-CDR1as) was upregulated in diabetic cardiomyopathy (DbCM). Mechanistically, circCDR1as activated the Hippo pathway by inhibiting MST1 ubiquitination, thereby enhancing FOXO3 transcriptional activity and promoting cardiomyocyte apoptosis [ ]. Wang et al. found that circ-ZNF609 is upregulated in myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury. Knockdown of circ-ZNF609 improved CM survival and proliferation by modulating the crosstalk between Hippo-YAP and Akt signaling. Using M6A methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (meRIP-qPCR), the authors confirmed that circ-ZNF609 regulated m6A modification of YAP mRNA. RIP and RNA pull-down assays demonstrated that circ-ZNF609 knockdown reduced YTHDF3 expression altering the binding dynamics of YTHDF1/2 and modulating YAP translation. These findings identify circZNF609 as a promising therapeutic target for I/R injury [ ]. Pang et al. observed that circHelz expression was elevated in murine hearts post-MI and in TGFβ-treated CFs. CircHelz interacted with YAP to facilitate its nuclear translocation, an effect regulated by NFATc2, a transcriptional activator that enhances CF proliferation [ ].
 Although these studies expand our understanding of circRNA-Hippo interactions, several critical gaps remain. Firstly, most mechanistic insights focus on post-translational regulation of YAP/TAZ, with limited exploration of transcriptional or epigenetic modulation of upstream Hippo components such as MST1 or LATS1. Second, the interaction between Hippo and complementary pathways (e.g., PI3K/AKT) is often noted but rarely dissected mechanistically, leaving key signaling intersections unresolved. Finally, the translational relevance of these findings is constrained by the predominant use of rodent models, necessitating validation in patient-derived cells or tissues, particularly in the context of comorbid conditions such as diabetes. Future studies should leverage multi-omics technologies to map dynamic circRNA-Hippo networks across cell types and pathological stages. CRISPR-based functional screens will be instrumental in identifying novel circRNA effectors with therapeutic potential in CVD.

## CircRNAs and the NF-κB signaling pathway

## NF-κB signaling
The NF-κB signaling pathway is a central regulator of inflammation, cell survival, and proliferation in CVDs [ ]. Activation of NF-κB occurs through two main pathways: the canonical and non-canonical arms, with the canonical pathway primarily implicated in inflammatory and immune responses [ , , ]. Canonical NF-κB signaling is triggered by pro-inflammatory stimuli such as TNF-α, IL-1, and LPS, which bind receptors like TNFR1 or TLR4, initiating downstream signaling cascades [ , , , , , ]. Following TNF-α binding, TNFR1 recruits TRADD and RIP1 via its cytoplasmic death domain, forming a complex with TRAF2/5 and cIAPs, which promotes K63-linked polyubiquitination of RIP1. This cascade ultimately liberates the p65/p50 heterodimer, enabling nuclear translocation and transcriptional regulation of pro-inflammatory genes [ , , ]. Dysregulation of this pathway promotes pathological inflammation, making it a critical therapeutic target in CVDs.
The NF-κB signaling pathway plays a central role in vascular inflammation, myocardial injury, and fibrotic remodeling. In atherosclerosis, it promotes endothelial activation and plaque instability through mechanosensitive and epigenetic mechanisms [ , , , ]. During myocardial injury, NF-κB drives inflammatory cytokine production, oxidative stress, and immune dysregulation, with interventions targeting upstream regulators such as ZBP1 or miR-146a delivery showing protective effects [ , , , , ]. In fibrosis, NF-κB mediates crosstalk between immune and stromal cells, for example, through m6A modification feedback in monocytes or macrophage-specific CCR2 signaling, highlighting its broad involvement in matrix remodeling and arrhythmogenesis [ , ]. The pathway’s dual roles in both initiating and sustaining cardiovascular inflammation make it a compelling yet complex target for therapeutic intervention.

## Translational advances and challenges
Clinically, the NF-κB signaling pathway has emerged as a pivotal mediator of inflammation in cardiovascular diseases, with growing therapeutic implications. For instance, the MAGiC-ART trial (NCT04017936) demonstrated that IL-1 blockade with anakinra significantly reduced systemic and cardiac inflammation in patients with cardiac sarcoidosis, highlighting the feasibility of targeting upstream NF-κB activators. Similarly, a randomized controlled trial (NCT05552586) revealed that high-dose melatonin markedly suppressed NF-κB activation and downstream proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting, correlating with improved clinical outcomes such as reduced myocardial injury and shorter intubation time. Despite these promising results, clinical translation remains challenging due to the pleiotropic roles of NF-κB in immune regulation, tissue repair, and cellular homeostasis. Pathway-specific inhibition risks impairing essential physiological functions, including host defense and stress adaptation. Future efforts should focus on tissue-specific drug delivery systems, context-dependent dosing strategies, and patient stratification based on inflammatory biomarker profiles to maximize efficacy while minimizing systemic side effects.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the NF-κB signaling
CircRNAs have emerged as critical regulators of NF-κB signaling in CVDs, often through miRNA sponging mechanisms ( ). In AS, Kong et al. identified circ-Sirt1 as a suppressor of neointimal hyperplasia in VSMCs, where its downregulation enhanced TNF-α-induced NF-κB p65 acetylation and activity. Mechanistically, circ-Sirt1 sequestered miR-132/212, thereby upregulating SIRT1 and reducing NF-κB-mediated transcription [ ]. Similarly, Liu et al. reported that circ_0026218, elevated in AS serum and ox-LDL-treated ECs, mitigated endothelial dysfunction by sponging miR-188-3p, thus suppressing the TLR4/NF-κB signaling and maintaining vascular homeostasis [ ]. Huang et al. demonstrated that circ-RELL1 attenuates endothelial inflammation by targeting miR-6873-3p to downregulate MyD88, a key upstream adaptor of NF-κB [ ]. Zhang et al. linked circ_0003575 to reduced TRAF6 expression through miR-63 sponging, alleviating endothelial dysfunction [ ]. In macrophages, He et al. found that circSCAP promoted oxidative stress and lipid accumulation by elevating PDE3B via miR-221-5p sponging, further activating NF-κB signaling under atherosclerotic conditions [ ].
 In AMI, Zhai et al. showed that circ_0060745 knockdown reduced inflammatory cytokine production (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) by inhibiting NF-κB activation, highlighting its biomarker and therapeutic potential [ ]. Chen et al. reported that knockdown of circROBO2 decreased CM apoptosis by reactivating miR-1184, which downregulates TRADD, a TNF/NF-κB signaling adaptor, suggesting the circROBO2-miR-1184-TRADD axis as a novel target in AMI [ ]. Song et al. identified circTLK1 as a driver of infarct expansion in myocardial I/R injury. CircTLK1 sponged miR-214 to upregulate RIPK1, enhancing TNF/NF-κB signaling and promoting CM apoptosis. Mechanistic studies, including luciferase and RIP assays, validated in vivo, support this regulatory axis [ ]. Si et al. have reported that circDhx32 promotes cardiac inflammatory responses in the mouse cardiac ischemia–reperfusion injury model. Mechanistically, circDhx32 binds to FOXO1 to regulate the AdipoR1-AMPK-NF-κB signaling pathway.
These findings highlight the context-dependent role of circRNAs in regulating NF-κB signaling across cardiovascular pathologies. While miRNA sponging remains the predominant mechanistic theme, alternative roles, such as direct protein binding or epigenetic modulation, are underexplored. Furthermore, the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of circRNA expression across cell types and disease stages limits translational potential. Most studies are restricted to in-vitro or preclinical models, necessitating rigorous validation in human tissues and clinical cohorts. Future work should focus on integrating single-cell transcriptomics, spatial multi-omics, and functional genomics to map circRNA-NF-κB networks and uncover novel therapeutic targets. Translating these discoveries into effective therapies will depend on cell-specific delivery systems, biomarker-guided interventions, and comprehensive human validation.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the Pink1/ FAM65B signaling
Dysregulated autophagy is a hallmark of several cardiovascular pathologies, yet the regulatory roles of circRNAs in this process remain incompletely understood [ , , , , ]. A recent study identified autophagy-related circular RNA (ACR) as a critical modulator of the Pink1-FAM65B signaling axis, which governs mitochondrial quality control and cardiomyocyte survival [ ]. In I/R injury models, ACR is significantly downregulated in cardiomyocytes. Mechanistic assays, including RNA pull-down and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP), revealed that ACR binds DNA methyltransferase Dnmt3B, preventing its recruitment to the Pink1 promoter. This inhibition of Dnmt3B-mediated DNA methylation reactivates Pink1 transcription and downstream FAM65B phosphorylation. Furthermore, phosphomimetic FAM65B (S46D) recapitulated the anti-autophagic effects of ACR in vitro, confirming its mechanistic relevance [ ]. Future studies should evaluate whether ACR exerts conserved roles in human ischemic cardiomyopathy and explore combination strategies involving circRNA delivery and kinase activation for enhanced therapeutic efficacy.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the FUS/VEGF-A signaling
The FUS/VEGFA signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in angiogenesis, cellular survival, and tissue regeneration. In cardiovascular contexts, FUS, an RBP, interacts with VEGFA to promote EC proliferation and vascular remodeling, processes that are vital to post-infarction repair. The circRNA CircFNDC3B, derived from the FNDC3B gene, directly binds FUS to enhance VEGFA transcription and signaling. In MI, circFNDC3B is downregulated, resulting in impaired angiogenesis and increased cardiomyocyte apoptosis. Restoration of circFNDC3B via AAV9 delivery stabilizes FUS-VEGFA interactions, promoting neovascularization and cardioprotection. Notably, this effect operates independently of the PI3K pathway, suggesting a distinct therapeutic axis [ ]. Interestingly, in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), its upregulation correlates with lymphangiogenesis and metastasis through the same FUS-VEGFA axis [ ], reflecting the dualistic nature of circRNA function across tissue types.

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the p53γ signaling
The p53γ signaling pathway, regulated by hnRNPA1, plays a crucial role in suppressing VSMC proliferation and phenotypic switching [ , ]. Shuai et al. identified circSMAD3 was downregulated during vascular injury, acting by recruiting the E3 ubiquitin ligase WD76, which ubiquitinates hnRNPA1, thereby relieving its inhibition of p53γ. Transcriptomic and morphological analyses confirmed that circSMAD3 overexpression suppresses VSMC proliferation and maintains contractile phenotype. In vivo, circSMAD3 delivery reduced neointimal formation and preserved vascular integrity. Rescue experiments revealed that hnRNPA1 overexpression reverses the protective effects of circSMAD3. These results position circSMAD3 as a promising therapeutic candidate for vascular remodeling. Future work should focus on optimizing circSMAD3 delivery systems and assessing combinatorial strategies in models of AS [ ].

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the ubiquitination signaling
CircHIPK3 has been implicated in multiple signaling pathways, including TGF-β1 and cAMP-PKA-Ca2+, and is a critical regulator of cardiovascular repair mechanisms [ , , ]. It influences cell proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis, and also plays a protective role in cardiac aging by modulating the ubiquitination pathway [ ]. Deficiency of circHIPK3 accelerates CM senescence and worsens cardiac function. Mechanistically, circHIPK3 serves as a scaffold for the E3 ubiquitin ligase β-TrCP, promoting the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of the RBP HuR, which in turn reduces p21-driven senescence. In CM-specific conditional knockout (CKO) mice, circHIPK3 depletion impaired cardiac function. Ubiquitination was validated through co-immunoprecipitation and cycloheximide chase assays. Additionally, umbilical mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes (UMSC-Exos) enriched with circHIPK3 restored cardiac performance by attenuating CM senescence, highlighting its translational potential in aging-related cardiovascular dysfunction [ ].

## CircRNAs regulate CVDs by modulating the crosstalk between multiple pathways
For instance, circ-0001283 was shown to promote cardiac hypertrophy by binding MYL3 and activating both PI3K/Akt/mTOR and ERK signaling pathways, thereby enhancing autophagic activity and pathological growth of cardiomyocytes [ ]. Similarly, circDhx32 exacerbates inflammatory responses during cardiac ischemia–reperfusion injury by competitively binding FOXO1 and suppressing AdipoR1-AMPK-NF-κB signaling, revealing a regulatory axis that connects RNA modification with innate immunity [ ]. Furthermore, m6A-modified circ-ZNF609 serves as a key molecular link between Hippo-YAP and AKT pathways, and its downregulation promotes cardiomyocyte repair following ischemic injury [ ]. The coordination of these pathways by circRNAs underscores their role as higher-order regulators capable of synchronizing diverse biological processes such as metabolic homeostasis, inflammatory response, and cell survival. This integrative function highlights the potential of circRNAs to fine-tune complex pathophysiological outcomes in CVDs, offering novel insights into the molecular orchestration of cross-pathway communication in disease settings.

## Discussions and future perspectives
Research over the past decade has illuminated the multifaceted roles of circRNAs in CVDs, establishing them as dynamic regulators of key signaling pathways, which govern critical processes such as angiogenesis, apoptosis, fibrosis, and cardiac remodeling. However, translating these discoveries into clinical applications remains challenging due to incomplete mechanistic understanding and technical limitations.
For mechanistic understanding, a critical challenge lies in deciphering the theory for the context-dependent duality of circRNA functions, where some circRNAs exhibit opposing roles in different CVDs or at various stages of the same disease. For example, CircHIPK3 promotes cardiac fibrosis by enhancing autophagy and apoptosis via the miR–20b–5p/ATG7 axis under ischemic conditions, yet attenuates inflammatory fibrosis in myocarditis models, potentially through the miR-338/FoxA1 pathway. Similarly, CircANRIL induces apoptosis in atherosclerosis, limiting fibrotic cap formation, yet may facilitate disease progression under hyperlipidemic conditions. These opposing functions are governed by spatiotemporal factors such as cell type, disease stage, and microenvironmental signals. Future studies should employ single-cell circRNAomics and spatial transcriptomics to resolve context-specific circRNA interactomes within pathological niches, facilitating the rational design of conditionally activated circRNA-based therapeutics.
In terms of technical limitations, one of the primary challenges is the accurate detection of circRNAs due to their overlapping sequences with linear mRNAs. Next-generation sequencing (NGS), particularly long-read sequencing platforms such as Oxford Nanopore, facilitated the identification of circRNAs by resolving BSJs. Notably, single-cell sequencing technology is causing a revolution in the study of transcriptional expression at cellular resolution, revealing unanticipated heterogeneity within cardiomyocytes, pericytes and fibroblasts, and revealing a unique subpopulation of cardiac fibroblasts. Farah et al. have revealed that cardiac cell types in the developing human heart form spatially organized communities with distinct subpopulations,are deciphering the heterogeneity of the heart. In the research of circRNA, Recent advances in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA) technologies, coupled with sophisticated deep learning-based algorithms, have revolutionized the investigation of circRNAs at unprecedented resolution and scale. In terms of circRNA research in cardiovascular field, recent innovations including combinatorial barcoding in microfluidic platforms coupled with strand-specific RNA-seq, have enabled cell-type-specific circRNA mapping in complex cardiovascular tissue. Single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH) techniques with split-probe designs targeting BSJs have improved spatial circRNA visualization in fixed tissues. However, real-time tracking in live cardiomyocytes remains elusive. Novel CRISPR-based imaging approaches, such as fluorescently tagged Cas13d variants, may soon allow dynamic visualization of circRNA behaviors during cardiac injury and regeneration.
Another critical frontier is targeted circRNA manipulation. Traditional RNA interference methods, including siRNAs and shRNAs, suffer from off-target effects due to overlapping exonic sequences with linear transcripts. In terms of overexpression, plasmid-based expression systems remain the norm, but emerging in-vitro circularization methods using permuted intron–exon ribozymes, such as Twister ribozyme-mediated circularization, now allow scalable production of synthetic circRNAs. These constructs require rigorous validation via Sanger sequencing, RNase R resistance, and northern blotting to exclude contamination from linear transcripts. Notably, engineered circRNAs containing optimized IRES elements achieved greater translation efficiency than their linear counterparts in cardiomyocytes, underscoring their potential for sustained therapeutic protein expression in ischemic heart disease. However, therapeutic delivery of circRNAs remains a major bottleneck. While AAVs offer high efficiency, they face constraints related to cargo size and pre-existing immunity. LNP systems tailored for cardiac tropism present a promising non-viral alternative under active investigation.
Mechanistically, most functional studies focus on the ceRNA hypothesis, whereby circRNAs act as miRNA sponges. Yet, quantitative modeling suggests that due to their low copy numbers, most circRNAs exert minimal effects on miRNA availability. Beyond sponging, circRNA-protein interactions have emerged as critical modulators of signaling. Advanced methods such as CLIP-seq and cryo-electron microscopy are now being leveraged to unravel these interactions. Additionally, AI-powered prediction models achieve great accuracy in predicting circRNA-protein interactions, accelerating mechanistic insight and target discovery. From a clinical standpoint, the translation of circRNAs into diagnostics and therapeutics faces two major hurdles: biomarker validation and therapeutic implementation. While promising candidates, including circZNF609 in coronary artery disease and circ_0001900 in atherosclerosis, their clinical utility is constrained by several inherent challenges. These include low abundance in bodily fluids, high tissue specificity limiting generalizability, and the lack of robust, standardized detection protocols. The CIRCULATE consortium recently proposed quantification guidelines, advocating for digital PCR (dPCR) and orthogonal methods. Further efforts are essential to develop highly sensitive and specific assays capable of reliably quantifying circRNAs in clinical samples.
On the therapeutic front, the clinical trial by Shanghai Ruijin Hospital is investigating HM2002, a VEGF-encoding circRNA delivered via myocardial injection in patients with ischemic heart failure undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) (NCT06621576). Parallel efforts in circRNA-based vaccines, such as the COVID-19 candidate, demonstrated higher neutralizing titers than mRNA vaccines in primates.
Furthermore, the translational development of circRNA-based therapeutics must carefully consider potential off-target effects and immunogenicity. Exogenous synthetic circRNAs can potentially activate innate immune sensors such as RIG-I or PKR, leading to unintended interferon responses and cytotoxicity. Meticulous circRNA design, including the avoidance of open reading frames and immunostimulatory motifs, combined with advanced purification methods to remove double-stranded RNA contaminants, is crucial for mitigating these risks and developing safe therapeutic applications.
Looking ahead, the integration of spatial transcriptomics with single-cell circRNA profiling will illuminate circRNA localization across cardiovascular microenvironments. Machine learning models trained on these datasets could identify patient-specific circRNA targets, enabling precision therapeutics. Advances in synthetic biology, including tRNA-based circularization systems that permit in vivo circRNA generation and advancing LNP and exosome systems, may overcome the barriers of synthesis and delivery. As circRNA research converges with gene editing, prime editing guides embedded within circRNAs could offer scarless genome correction in cardiomyocytes. While significant challenges remain, the synergy of high-precision gene tools, scalable biomanufacturing, and AI-driven target prediction positions circRNAs to reshape the landscape of cardiovascular therapeutics in the coming decade.
