## Background
The role of telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) in bone metabolism remains poorly defined. This study investigates its function in osteoporosis (OP) pathogenesis and its therapeutic potential.

## Methods
TERT expression was analyzed in clinical OP specimens and ovariectomized (OVX) mice. Mechanisms were probed using RNA-seq, gain/loss-of-function studies, and pathway inhibition. Exosomes derived from TERT-overexpressing BMSCs (TERT-Exo) were functionalized with a bone-targeting aptamer (Apt-TERT-Exo) and evaluated in OVX mice.

## Results
TERT was downregulated in OP, correlating with impaired osteogenesis. TERT was identified as a novel upstream activator of the BMP4/Smad1 pathway, essential for its pro-osteogenic effect. Apt-TERT-Exo exhibited enhanced bone accumulation and systemic administration effectively restored trabecular bone mass in OVX mice by promoting BMSC osteogenesis.

## Conclusion
TERT is a novel upstream regulator of osteogenesis via BMP4/Smad1. The Apt-TERT-Exo platform represents a promising targeted, cell-free therapeutic approach for osteoporosis.

## The translational potential statement
The Translational Potential of this Article.The Apt-TERT-Exo platform presents a targeted, cell-free strategy for osteoanabolic therapy. Its design addresses key translational challenges by enabling precise delivery to bone tissue, potentially minimizing systemic side effects and offering a promising new avenue for treating osteoporosis.

## Introduction
Osteoporosis (OP), a systemic skeletal disorder characterized by compromised bone strength and microarchitectural deterioration, leading to an increased risk of fractures [ , , ]. Currently, OP imposes a significant burden on patients' quality of life and the healthcare system [ , ]. Epidemiological data indicates that the prevalence of OP is as high as 30% - 50% in individuals over the age of 50, with its incidence on the rise due to the aging population [ ]. Clinically, the diagnosis of OP primarily relies on bone density measurements. While anti-resorptive agents (e.g., bisphosphonates) and anabolic drugs (e.g., teriparatide) partially restore bone mass, their long-term application faces critical challenges [ , ]. Anti-resorptive agents fail to restore lost bone mass, while anabolic drugs exhibit transient efficacy and carry risks of osteosarcoma and cardiovascular events with prolonged use [ ]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop novel and safer therapeutic strategies for improving OP.
Telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT), as the catalytic subunit of the telomerase complex, is historically recognized for its telomere-lengthening role in cell immortalization and tumorigenesis [ , ]. However, emerging evidence reveals that TERT exhibits non-canonical functions independent of telomerase activity, including regulation of stem cell differentiation, metabolic reprogramming, and stress adaptation [ ]. Nevertheless, the precise role of TERT in bone metabolism, particularly in the context of osteoporosis, remains contentious and incompletely understood, with reported effects on osteogenesis varying considerably across different experimental models and cellular contexts. In OP, dysregulated TERT expression is implicated in impaired osteogenic commitment of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), contributing to pathological bone loss. Notably, TERT has been shown to physically interacts with the SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling factor BRG1 and mediates chromatin remodeling via ATPase activity during MSCs differentiation, replacing the repressive histone markers (H3K27me3) at the Runx2 promoter [ , ]. This remodeling enables recruitment of transcriptional coactivators (e.g., p300), driving osteogenic commitment [ ]. It further stabilizes mitochondrial electron transport chain components (Complexes I/III) and enhances Nrf2-Keap1 antioxidant signaling, thereby suppressing oxidative stress-induced osteoblast apoptosis [ , ]. Further mechanistic studies revealed that TERT forms an intranuclear complex directly with β-catenin through its N-terminal and C-terminal structural domains, enhancing Wnt3a-induced β-catenin nuclear translocation and target gene transcription. Strikingly, this pro-osteogenic effect persists even in TERT mutants lacking telomerase activity [ , ]. These findings suggest that its ability to regulate cell function makes TERT a potential therapeutic target. This ambiguity surrounding TERT's function in bone homeostasis underscores the critical need to elucidate its precise molecular mechanisms and regulatory networks in OP, which is essential for evaluating its potential as a viable therapeutic target.
In this study, we sought to clarify the role of TERT in OP pathogenesis and its therapeutic potential. We found that TERT plays a crucial role in promoting osteogenic differentiation of stem cells. This finding is highly relevant to the clinical need for promoting bone formation to improve OP. Currently, clinical treatments for OP mainly focus on inhibiting bone resorption, while drugs that promote bone formation are relatively scarce [ ]. TERT's regulation of osteogenic differentiation offers a new perspective for OP treatment. Additionally, our study deciphers BMP4/Smad1 signaling as the central axis through which TERT governs osteogenic commitment. It has been reported that BMP signaling regulates bone homeostasis through Smad-dependent (classical) versus non-dependent (TAK1-MAPK) pathways. Wu et al. indicated that BMP-2/7 synergistically activates osteogenesis-related genes (e.g., COL1A1, BSP) by phosphorylating Smad1/5/8 in concert with Runx2 [ ]. However, Kamiya et al. found that conditional knockdown of BMPR1A increases bone mass in mice by a mechanism involving inhibition of osteoclastogenesis, suggesting that BMP signaling negatively regulates bone resorption [ ]. Crucially, TERT deficiency induces aberrant methylation of BMP-Smad effector genes (Smad5, Runx2) [ ], whereas BMP-9 potentiates osteogenesis in TERT-immortalized stem cells via ALK1/BMPRII-mediated Smad1 phosphorylation [ ]. Innovatively, TERT agonist TA-65 synergizes with BMP-2 to accelerate fracture healing [ ]. These findings highlight the importance of TERT in bone regeneration. Although BMP signaling is a well-established regulator of bone formation, BMP4 crosstalk with TERT has not been reported in skeletal biology.
Despite these mechanistic insights, systemic delivery of TERT faces formidable challenges, including off-target effects and rapid clearance. Exosomes, as endogenous nanovesicles, have emerged as a transformative drug delivery platform owing to their innate biocompatibility, barrier-penetrating capacity, and low immunogenicity [ , ]. These extracellular vesicles exhibit unparalleled versatility in cargo encapsulation, efficiently transporting small-molecule therapeutics alongside complex nucleic acid payloads (e.g., siRNA, mRNA) while preserving bioactivity, a critical advantage that bridges gene therapy and precision medicine [ , ]. Notably, their intrinsic ability to traverse biological barriers has been exemplified in central nervous system targeting, where exosome-delivered siRNA achieved high gene silencing efficiency in hippocampal neurons through blood-brain barrier penetration [ , ]. Moreover, the therapeutic potential of exosomes is further amplified through surface modification [ , ]. By conjugating tissue-specific ligands to exosomal membrane proteins (e.g., Lamp2b fusion), targeted delivery to pathological sites can be achieved with spatial precision [ ]. For instance, surface engineering of exosomes with bone-homing peptides (e.g., DSS6 motif) has been shown to enhance skeletal accumulation by 4.7-fold compared to unmodified counterparts [ , , ]. Having established a platform for designing multifunctional scaffolds incorporating engineered exosomes, we have demonstrated the efficacy of exosomes as efficient carriers for nucleic acid delivery to promote extracellular matrix remodeling and scaffold-tissue integration [ ]. We hypothesize that the targeted delivery of pro-osteogenic factors via a similar exosome-based strategy could effectively rejuvenate bone formation in OP. This targeting strategy not only improves therapeutic payload concentration in osteoporotic bone but also concurrently minimizes off-target effects.
Herein, we explored the molecular mechanisms of TERT in OP, and further constructed an exosome-based therapeutic system for precise treatment of OP. Firstly, we discovered that the expression of TERT was downregulated in patients with age increased. Besides, we also observed downregulation of TERT expression in ovariectomized (OVX) mice. After activating TERT expression, the bone mass loss was significantly inhibited, indicating that TERT may positively regulated bone homeostasis. Moreover, we found that TERT regulated osteogenic differentiation through the BMP4/Smad1 pathway, while independent of osteoclastogenesis. For the treatment of OP, we firstly combined aldehyde group-modified aptamer on the surface of exosomes to engineer bone target exosomes (Apt-Exo). After loading TERT lentivirus, the obtained Apt-TERT-Exo could be targeted to accumulate in bone tissue, increasing bone mass and bone microarchitecture strength, thereby providing a new theoretical basis for OP treatment. By integrating molecular mechanism exploration with advanced nanomedicine design, this work not only redefines the biological significance of TERT beyond telomere maintenance but also charts a roadmap for next-generation OP therapies, a paradigm shifts from symptomatic management to stem cell-centric regeneration.

## Materials and methods
2.1

## Correlation analysis between TERT and OP
Using GWAS summary statistics of skeletal-related traits, including all-cause fracture (PMID: 30158200) and BMD at the trunk, arms, and legs (PMID: 39349818), we extracted SNPs within the TERT gene region (approximately ± 50 kb, hg19, chr5:1, 253, 262-1, 353, 262) from these GWAS datasets. Specifically, this region contained 899 SNPs associated with all-cause fracture and 360 SNPs associated with BMD at the trunk, arms, and legs, with 275 SNPs overlapping across all four traits. Cross-phenotype meta-analysis was performed using the CPASSOC method (PMID: 25500260) with genetic association summary statistics from the four BMD traits. The input for CPASSOC was an M × K summary statistics matrix, where M represents the number of SNPs shared across phenotypes and K denotes the number of phenotypes (in this study, M = 275, K = 4). The SHet function of CPASSOC was utilized to conduct cross-phenotype association analysis under default settings. The output of the SHet test was a vector of p value for the M SNPs. For the most significant SNPs, potential eQTL annotations were further investigated using the eQTLGen consortium dataset (PMID: 34475573).

2.2

## scRNA-seq
The single-cell transcriptome of the samples was downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) under accession numbers GSE169396 and GSE147287. The data were analyzed using the R package 'Seurat'. During data preprocessing, we created a Seurat object for each sample with the following initial thresholds: genes expressed in at least 3 cells and cells expressing at least 200 genes. Then, quality control was performed by filtering cells based on the following criteria: the number of detected genes per cell between 400 and 4,500, the percentage of mitochondrial genes (MT-) less than 25%, and the percentage of ribosomal genes (RP) greater than 1%. To correct for batch effects among different samples, we applied the Harmony algorithm (implemented in the R package 'harmony') using the 'RunHarmony' function with the sample identity as the batch variable. The filtered and batch-corrected data were then normalized, and 2000 highly variable genes were identified. After scaling the data, we performed principal component analysis (PCA) and selected the top 30 principal components for subsequent analysis. The data were further processed for dimensionality reduction using the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) and t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) algorithms. Cell clustering was performed using the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions with a resolution of 0.3. Cell types were identified by using marker genes in the Cell Marker Database (http://xteam.xbio.top/CellMarker).

2.3

## Collection of human samples
Patients who needed THA underwent preoperative detection of bone density and were then divided into the mild OP group (<60 years old) and the severe OP group (>60 years old) following the standard of the National Osteoporosis Foundation. The Bioethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (no.2024-698) approved this study of human samples, and the patients signed informed consent forms. The samples were stored at −80 °C before further analysis.

2.4

## OVX model establishment
All animal experiments were performed as per the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Soochow University (ECSU-201700041). Eight-week-old female mice were anesthetized via inhalation of isoflurane and positioned prone on a 37 °C thermostatic surgical station. After shaving the surgical field (L1-L3 vertebral region), the skin was disinfected three times with 10% povidone-iodine followed by 75% ethanol under sterile conditions. A 1.5 cm midline dorsal incision was made to expose the costovertebral angles. The dorsal muscle layer was incised at the projection site of the subrenal fat pad, and the fat-encased ovarian complex (ovary and uterine horn) was gently exteriorized. The oviduct isthmus distal to the ovary was doubly ligated with 6-0 silk sutures, followed by ovarian excision. The identical procedure was performed contralaterally. Muscle and skin layers were closed separately with 6-0 absorbable sutures. Postoperatively, mice were housed individually and maintained on a 37 °C heating pad until full recovery.

2.5

## Co-immunoprecipitation
Cells were collected and lysed with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime). After centrifugation at 4 °C, 14,000×g for 15 min, the supernatant was immediately transferred to a new EP tube. An aliquot was reserved as input control. Protein A + G magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2108) were pre-incubated with Antibodies or control IgG (Beyotime, A7028; 50 μg/mL) for 1 h at room temperature with rotation. The antibody-bound beads were then incubated with equal amounts of total protein lysates overnight at 4 °C with gentle rotation. After centrifugation, the supernatant was removed, and another 15 μL of loading buffer (Beyotime) was added before boiling at 95 °C for 10 min. The following steps for detection were identical to those used in the Western blotting method.

2.6

## Molecular docking and site-directed mutagenesis
The amino acid sequence of TERT was retrieved from UniProt, and its three-dimensional structure was predicted using AlphaFold3. The experimental structure of BMP4 was obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB). Predicted models were evaluated using PyMOL, and the conformation with the highest predicted confidence score was selected as the final structure for docking. Global protein-protein docking was performed using HDOCK under default parameters. Resulting complexes were visualized and analyzed in PyMOL.

2.7

## Isolation and characterization of BMSC-exo
According to the instructions of the exosome-specific medium, a staged culture method was adopted to collect BMSCs supernatant. The specific protocol was as follows: when P3 cells reached 50% confluency in culture medium, the original medium was removed and replaced with a new medium (50% DMEM/F12 complete medium + 50% exosome-specific medium) for continued culture. When cell confluency reached 70%, the medium was switched to exosome-specific medium, and cells were placed back into the incubator. Upon achieving 90%-100% confluency, the cell supernatant was collected. BMSC-Exo were isolated from the supernatant using ultracentrifugation, quantified via a BCA assay kit, and stored at −80 °C for long-term preservation.
A small aliquot of the BMSC-Exo sample was resuspended in PBS buffer. The suspension was applied onto a pre-vacuum-treated copper grid and dried under an infrared lamp for 30 min. The sample was stained with 1% phosphotungstic acid, gently rinsed with double-distilled water to remove excess stain, dried under an infrared lamp, and observed under TEM (Hitachi H7800, Japan) at 80-120 kV to examine the morphological features of Exo. The particle size distribution of BMSC-Exo was analyzed using dynamic light scattering (DLS) and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA, Particle Metrix, Meerbusch, Germany), respectively. Western blot was performed to detect the expression levels of marker proteins, such as Calnexin (Affinity, AF5362), CD63 (Invitrogen, PA5-100713) and CD81 (Abcam, ab109201).

2.8

## Statistical analysis
Data were presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analysis (GraphPad Software, USA) was evaluated using GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, USA). For comparisons among three or more groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied, followed by Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired, two-tailed Student's t-test. A probability value (p) less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results
3.1

## TERT is markedly decreased in human and mouse bone tissues
To investigate whether the TERT gene plays a role in bone mass maintenance in humans, we extracted the genetic summary data of this gene region (approximately 50 kb of the gene region, hg19, chr5: 1, 253, 262-1, 253, 262) for various bone-related traits, including all-cause fracture and bone mineral density (BMD) in the trunk, arms, and legs. We performed cross-phenotype meta-analysis with CPASSOC software to identify the TERT polymorphisms associated with human BMD. Of the 275 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in this region, 2 SNPs were identified to be associated with bone-related traits at a significant level of 1.82 × 10−4 (Bonferroni correction). SNP rs13174919 represented the top signal (p = 1.69 × 10−5) ( A). The phenotype-specific association results showed that SNP rs13174919 was significantly associated with all-cause fracture (β = −2.56 × 10−2, SE = 7.49 × 10−3, p = 4.50 × 10−4), and trunk BMD (β = 1.89 × 10−2, SE = 6.88 × 10−3, p = 6.26 × 10−3) while the relationship between rs13174919 and BMD in arms and legs was weak ( B). In eQTL annotation, SNP rs13174919 (G > C) was found to be associated with TERT expression in blood (β = 4.63 × 10−2, SE = 1.90 × 10−2, p = 1.50 × 10−2) from the eQTLGen consortium dataset ( C). This suggests that the identified SNP may influence TERT expression levels, potentially contributing to its observed association with bone-related traits. 
To investigate the expression of TERT in human bone tissue, femoral heads were collected after total hip arthroplasty from clinical patients across different age. Our results showed that, compared to younger patients, the bone microstructure of bone samples from older patients was much looser ( D). More importantly, the expression of TERT of bone samples from older patients were lower ( E). A Pearson correlation analysis between the quantified TERT IOD values and the corresponding BMD measurements for each individual revealed a significant positive correlation ( F, ). These findings provide compelling clinical evidence for the role of TERT in OP development.
Subsequently, to confirm the expression of TERT was closely related to OP, we established mouse OVX model by removing both ovaries. After 6 weeks, significant mouse trabecular bone loss was observed ( G–K). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) ( L) and tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) staining ( ) also exhibited the decreased cancellous bones and abnormal osteoclastogenesis. Corresponding to the findings in human tissue, decreased TERT expression was also observed in OVX mice ( M, ). Moreover, western blot ( ) and immunofluorescence staining ( N, ) results showed that the expression of TERT was obviously decreased in BMSCs from OVX mice. These results demonstrate that TERT was positively correlated with OP.

3.2

## TERT enhanced osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs
To clarify the cellular localization and function of TERT, we first employed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis on femoral head tissue from GEO database. After filtering and normalizing the GSE169396 dataset and GSE147287, 13 cell types were identified, including B cells, dendritic cells, endothelial cells, erythrocytes, macrophages, mast cells, mesenchymal stromal cells, monocytes, neutrophils, osteoblasts, plasma cells, smooth muscle cells, and T cells ( A). Dot plots and violin plots indicated that among the various subgroups, TERT has the highest expression abundance in mesenchymal stromal cells that co-express RUNX2 ( B–D), ALPL and COLA1 ( ), suggesting changes in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) function and possible effects on osteoblast activity. 
Given that bone loss in osteoporosis is associated with downregulation of TERT expression, we investigated whether enhancing its expression could ameliorate osteoporotic phenotypes. Cycloastragenol (CAG), a TERT activator, was administered intraperitoneally (10 or 20 mg/kg) to OVX mice. After 8 weeks, Micro-CT analysis revealed denser cancellous bone architecture in CAG-treated groups ( E), particularly at 20 mg/kg group, with significantly increased bone volume fraction (BV/TV), trabecular number (Tb.N), and thickness (Tb.Th), alongside reduced trabecular separation (Tb.Sp) compared to OVX controls ( G–J). These data indicate that TERT activation by CAG promotes bone formation, further validated by H&E ( F) and Masson staining ( ).
To elucidate the mechanistic basis of TERT in bone homeostasis, we conducted in vitro studies with CAG. Biocompatibility assessment via CCK-8 assay confirmed negligible cytotoxicity of CAG at ≤0.5 μM in both BMSCs and bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMMs) ( ), thus 0.25 μM and 0.5 μM doses were selected for subsequent experiments. In BMSCs, CAG dose-dependently upregulated TERT expression ( K and O) and enhanced osteogenic proteins expression (RUNX2, OPN, COL1, OCN) ( L and 2P-S). ALP and alizarin red staining corroborated TERT-mediated osteogenic differentiation ( M−N, 2T-U).
To future explore the role of TERT in regulating osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, we introduced its specific siRNA (siTERT) during osteoblastic induction ( ). ALP staining showed that decreased expression and lower activity of ALP in siTERT BMSCs after 7 days ( A and C). Alizarin red staining was also decreased after induction for 21 days ( B and D). The expression levels of osteogenic markers were analyzed, and downregulation of COL1, RUNX2, OPN and OCN was verified by western blot ( E–I). Conversely, CAG treatment did not alter osteoclast formation in RANKL-induced BMMs ( J–M), suggesting that TERT lacks significant regulatory effects on osteoclastogenesis. Taken together, these results demonstrate that TERT significantly promotes osteogenic function. 

3.3

## TERT promoted osteogenesis via BMP4/Smad1 pathway
To elucidate the molecular mechanism by which TERT regulates osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, TERT-overexpressing (OE-TERT) BMSCs model was established. Optimal transduction efficiency was achieved at MOI 50, as determined by dose-dependent fluorescence imaging (MOIs: 25, 50, 100) ( ). At this MOI, western blot quantification confirmed robust induction of TERT expression ( ). Then, we performed RNA sequencing. Heatmap analysis identified 3321 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between OE-NC and OE-TERT groups ( A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment revealed that these DEGs were predominantly associated with positive regulation of gene expression in ossification, osteoblast differentiation, biomineral tissue development, and bone development ( B). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis indicated that OE-TERT potentially enhanced osteoblast differentiation and bone mineralization via TGF-β and PI3K-Akt signaling, while modulating cell adhesion and migration through cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, ECM-receptor interaction, and focal adhesion pathways ( C). The TGF-β pathway exhibited the most pronounced upregulation, suggesting its specific activation by OE-TERT. Crucially, BMP4 and Smad1, key components of the TGF-β pathway, are pivotal regulators of osteogenesis. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) further confirmed significant downregulation of TGF-β/Smad signaling in OE-TERT versus OE-NC groups ( D). The volcano plot showed Bmp4 as a significantly upregulated transcript in the OE-TERT group ( ). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction via STRING identified BMP4 as the core hub in the TERT-regulated osteogenic differentiation network ( ). 
Given these findings, we hypothesized that BMP4 mediates TERT-driven osteogenesis. To test this, we first examined BMP4 and p-Smad1 expression in BMSCs from OE-TERT and TERT-knockdown (siTERT) BMSCs models. OE-TERT significantly increased BMP4 and p-Smad1 levels versus OE-NC controls ( E–H), whereas siTERT reduced their expression compared to siNC ( I–L). Immunofluorescence staining confirmed a significant increase in intracellular BMP4 protein levels, localized predominantly to the cytoplasm, in TERT-overexpressing BMSCs ( M). Immunofluorescence also confirmed nuclear translocation of p-Smad1 in OE-TERT cells ( N), demonstrating TERT-dependent activation of the BMP4/Smad1 axis. Molecular docking technique showed that TERT chain bands Arg193, Arg386, Ala190, Arg29, Arg6, Lys212, Arg30 form hydrogen bonding interactions with Arg70, Ser351, Leu17, Arg69, Asn350, Tyr233, Pro187, His354, Thr376 of protein BMP4 ( O). Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) further validated the binding interaction between TERT and BMP4 ( P). To determine whether BMP4 is required for TERT's osteogenic effects, we inhibited BMP4 with DMH-1 in OE-TERT cells. DMH-1 completely abrogated the enhanced osteogenic differentiation induced by TERT overexpression ( Q–V). Collectively, these results establish BMP4 as a downstream effector mediating TERT's pro-osteogenic function.

3.4

## Preparation, characterization and bone-targeting modification of BMSC-Exo
Mouse BMSCs were cultured until 90%-100% confluency, and BMSC-derived exosomes (BMSC-Exo) were isolated via ultracentrifugation ( A). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed typical cup-shaped morphology of BMSC-Exo with a homogeneous size distribution of BMSC-Exo (≈120 nm) ( B and C). Western blot confirmed high expression of exosomal markers (CD63, CD81) and absence of endoplasmic reticulum contaminant Calnexin ( D), validating successful isolation of BMSC-Exo. Biocompatibility was assessed by Live/Dead staining and cytoskeletal (F-actin) visualization. BMSCs were incubated with varying concentrations of BMSC-Exo. Live/dead staining showed negligible cytotoxicity across all groups ( ), and cytoskeletal staining further demonstrated normal cell adhesion and spreading ( E). CCK-8 assays revealed that 50 μg/mL BMSC-Exo significantly enhanced BMSCs proliferation after 3 days, with no additional effect at 100 μg/mL. At day five, both 50 and 100 μg/mL groups sustained pro-proliferative effects, albeit at reduced rates ( F). 
To confer bone targeting capability, an aldehyde-modified aptamer was covalently conjugated to surface amines of BMSC-Exo via Schiff base formation, generating bone-targeting Apt-Exo. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) exhibited a characteristic -C=N- bond absorption peak at 1581 cm−1 ( G), confirming successful conjugation. To determine the bone targeting effect of aptamer, DiR-labeled Apt-Exo was cultured with mouse BMSCs. As shown in H, pronounced co-localization with the cytosolic signal of BMSC, but minimal overlap with BMDM ( ), confirming good BMSC targeting effect.

3.5

## Osteoinductive activity of Apt-TERT-Exo
The osteogenic potential of Apt-TERT-Exo was evaluated. Western blot further showed that Apt-TERT-Exo markedly upregulated osteogenic proteins (COL1, RUNX2, OCN) ( I–L). ALP staining demonstrated that TERT-Exo (TERT-loaded non-targeted exosomes) enhanced osteogenic differentiation versus Apt-NC-Exo (targeted empty exosomes) groups, while Apt-TERT-Exo (targeted + TERT-loaded) exhibited maximal activity ( M and O). Alizarin red staining confirmed the highest mineralized nodule formation in the Apt-TERT-Exo group ( N and P), indicating potent stimulation of BMSCs differentiation.
Besides, we further explored the effect of BMSC-Exo for OP improvement. The mouse OVX-induced OP was established after 6 weeks. OVX-induced osteoporotic mice received weekly intravenous injections for 6 weeks ( A and B). We also measured the bone targeting property of DiR-labeled Apt-Exo via tail vein injection. After 12 h, DiR-labeled Apt-Exo obviously accumulated within femurs and tibias compared to Exo. In contrast, the fluorescence signals in the major non-skeletal organs were comparable between the two groups, indicating no increased accumulation due to aptamer modification ( C). Micro-CT analysis revealed that NC-Exo (non-targeted empty exosomes) mildly attenuated bone loss, while TERT-Exo (non-targeted TERT-loaded) and Apt-NC-Exo (targeted empty) groups showed increased trabecular bone formation. Notably, Apt-TERT-Exo generated the densest cancellous architecture ( D). Trabecular parameters (elevated BV/TV, Tb.N, Tb.Th; reduced Tb.Sp) further validated its potent bone-regenerative capacity ( E–H). H&E staining corroborated maximal trabecular bone density in the Apt-TERT-Exo group ( I). Histological analysis revealed markedly enhanced RUNX2 expression in Apt-TERT-Exo group versus controls. Furthermore, the upregulation of the osteogenic mediator BMP4 and p-Smad1 was observed ( J–L). The quantitative analysis also revealed a significant increase in the positive staining area for RUNX2, BMP4, and p-Smad1 in the bones of OVX mice treated with Apt-TERT-Exo compared to the vehicle-treated OVX control group ( ), indicating activation of the BMP4/Smad1 signaling pathway during bone regeneration. To evaluate the safety of BMSC-Exo after tail intravenous injection, the visceral organs including heart, liver, spleen, lung and kidney, were collected after 6 weeks. Visceral organs in all groups exhibited normal structure ( M), demonstrating good safety of BMSC-Exo in vivo. In addition, to assess the potential impact of Apt-TERT-Exo therapy on bone resorption, we performed TRAP staining from OVX mice treated with either vehicle or Apt-TERT-Exo. Quantitative analysis of osteoclast surface per bone surface (Oc.S/BS) in the secondary spongiosa revealed no significant increase in osteoclast parameters in the Apt-TERT-Exo group compared to the OVX control ( ). This result indicates that the significant bone mass restoration induced by Apt-TERT-Exo was driven primarily by its anabolic action on bone-forming cells, without a concomitant increase in osteoclast-mediated bone resorption at the tissue level. 
To evaluate the durability of the therapeutic effect following cessation of treatment, we conducted a treatment-withdrawal study. OVX mice were administered Apt-TERT-Exo for 4 weeks. Subsequently, one cohort continued therapy for an additional 2 weeks (Continuous group), while a parallel cohort entered a 2-week treatment-free observation period (Withdrawal group). Analysis of trabecular bone microstructure in the distal femur via micro-CT at the 6-week endpoint demonstrated that bone mass in the Withdrawal group remained significantly elevated compared to the PBS control. However, the bone mass in this group was moderately lower than that in the Continuous treatment group ( ). This result indicates that the anabolic effect of Apt-TERT-Exo is partially sustained after a finite treatment period, suggesting a durable, though not permanent, positive impact on bone remodeling.

## Discussion
This study establishes TERT as a pivotal orchestrator of bone homeostasis through BMP4/Smad1 signaling and pioneers a targeted exosome platform for OP therapy. We demonstrate that a marked dysregulation of TERT expression in clinical bone tissue samples from OP patients and OVX mice models, which was mechanistically linked to impaired osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. Importantly, we identified a previously unrecognized non-canonical regulatory axis wherein TERT orchestrates osteoblast lineage commitment through direct modulation of the BMP4/Smad1 signaling cascade, independent of its telomere-elongating activity. Building upon this mechanistic basis, we engineered a novel therapeutic strategy utilizing BMSCs-derived exosomes loaded with lentiviral encoded TERT (TERT-Exo). Systemic delivery of exosomes via tail vein injection resulted in significant amelioration of bone loss in OVX mice, as evidenced by restored trabecular bone volume fraction and enhanced bone formation markers. These findings collectively define the functional paradigm of TERT in skeletal metabolism by establishing its role as a therapeutically actionable target. The efficacy of our exosome-based delivery platform not only provides critical insights into bone-targeted precision medicine, but also establishes a robust framework for developing RNA/protein combinatorial therapies against metabolic bone disorders.
Extensive studies have established the pivotal role of TERT in regulating fundamental cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, and survival across diverse cell types, with its dysregulated expression implicated in multiple pathological conditions. Choi et al. found that even in the absence of telomerase reverse transcriptase activity, TERT still significantly enhanced the proliferation of keratinocytes and induced quiescent hair follicle stem cells to enter into the active trichogenetic phase through activation of a Myc/Wnt-related developmental program [ ]. This effect is independent of the telomere lengthening mechanism, suggesting a non-classical function of TERT in the regulation of stem cell function. In addition, investigations in stem cell biology have revealed TERT's dual capacity to preserve telomere integrity and enhance self-renewal capabilities, a paradigm that exhibited conceptual alignment with our findings regarding TERT-mediated osteogenic lineage specification in BMSCs. Critically, prior work failed to resolve key contradictions: while TERT enhances osteogenesis in young BMSCs, it paradoxically inhibits differentiation in aged niches due to context-dependent modulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling [ ], and although BMP4 induces TERT expression [ ], reciprocal regulation remained unverified. Our multi-modal evidence bridges these gaps: genome-wide association analyses identified a significant association between the TERT locus and BMD, providing population-level genetic validation; clinical bone specimens from OP patients showed strong positive correlation between TERT levels and BMD; and TERT knockout in BMSCs suppressed osteogenesis, conclusively establishing its non-canonical role in bone metabolism.
Furthermore, the BMP4/Smad1 signaling pathway activates the phosphorylation of Smad1/5/8 through ligand-receptor specific recognition (BMPR-I/II complex), which in turn forms a transcriptionally active complex with Smad4, regulates the expression of key osteogenic genes, such as Dlx2, OSX, and Osteocalcin, and drives the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs [ ]. Notably, this pathway can restore osteogenic differentiation potential under conditions of aging or pathological stress (e.g., cadmium exposure) through epigenetic reprogramming (e.g., regulation of chromatin openness), providing a targeted intervention strategy for OP treatment [ , ]. In line with these findings, our functional studies definitively establish TERT as a novel upstream regulator of the BMP4/Smad1 signaling axis in BMSCs. We demonstrated that TERT overexpression rescues BMP4 expression and p-Smad1 activation, while its deficiency suppresses this pathway. Crucially, the pro-osteogenic effect of TERT was completely abolished upon pharmacological inhibition of BMP4, establishing a direct functional link. While the precise molecular mechanism by which TERT governs BMP4 expression—whether through direct transcriptional regulation, chromatin remodeling, or other post-transcriptional processes—remains an important subject for future investigation, our data conclusively position TERT within the established BMP4/Smad1 osteogenic cascade. This newly identified regulatory role of TERT significantly advances our understanding of its function in bone metabolism.
The current treatment paradigm for OP relies on pharmacological interventions (e.g., bisphosphonates, RANKL inhibitors) and physical therapy, but these approaches still face a triple challenge: (1) long-term use of bisphosphonates leads to paradoxical increases in bone fragility (risk of atypical fracture HR = 3.2), and RANKL inhibitors result in a rapid loss of BMD after discontinuation of the medication (12-month lumbar spine BMD decreased by 6.7%) [ ]; (2) anti-sclerostin antibodies may induce cardiovascular events (risk of myocardial infarction HR = 1.9), and denosumab interferes with immune surveillance function (risk of pneumonia OR = 1.8) [ ] and (3) existing therapies are unable to reverse epigenetic senescence in MSC (H3K27me3-mediated silencing of the osteogenic genes), and it is difficult to overcome target pathway redundancy (double knockdown of LRP5/6 still retains 50% bone formation activity). In contrast, our study pioneers a transformative strategy employing TERT-Exo, with further precision achieved through bone-homing aptamer conjugation [ ]. This dual-optimized platform (designated Apt-TERT-Exo) capitalizes on the innate biocompatibility, immune-evasive property and cargo-protective capacity of exosomes, enabling targeted TERT mRNA/protein delivery, circumventing systemic immune activation and neutralizing antibodies associated with conventional viral vectors (e.g., AAV serotype-dependent immunogenicity) [ , ].
Mechanistically, lentiviral-driven TERT expression in donor BMSCs facilitated high-efficiency packaging of functional TERT transcripts into exosomal lumens, while aptamer surface functionalization conferred robust skeletal tropism. In vivo biodistribution analysis via NIR imaging revealed rapid Apt-TERT-Exo accumulation in distal femora within 12 h post-injection, exhibiting greater signal intensity versus untargeted exosomes. Therapeutically, Micro-CT quantification demonstrated significant restoration of trabecular bone architecture in OVX mice after 6 weeks treatment. Histomorphometric validation showed an increase in bone formation rate relative to vehicle-treated models, corroborated by upregulated osteogenic markers. Furthermore, our histomorphometric analysis revealed that the Apt-TERT-Exo treatment did not increase osteoclast activity, as evidenced by unchanged osteoclast surface parameters compared to the OVX control. This finding suggests that the robust restoration of bone mass is primarily attributable to a direct anabolic stimulation of osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs, rather than an indirect effect mediated through the suppression of bone resorption. This predominantly anabolic profile distinguishes our Apt-TERT-Exo platform from some current therapies and may contribute to a favorable safety and efficacy balance, as it avoids the potential pitfalls associated with excessive suppression of bone turnover. Crucially, Apt-TERT-Exo administration elicited no hepatorenal toxicity, underscoring the platform's exceptional safety profile. This therapeutic superiority stems from three synergistic innovations: (1) aptamer-mediated bone targeting minimizes off-organ exposure, reducing ectopic calcification risks associated with systemic BMP delivery [ , , ]; (2) exosomes as natural nanovesicles significantly prolong the translational activity of encapsulated TERT mRNA through a unique bilayer membrane structure and endogenous biomolecule modifications, outperforming siRNA/nanoparticle systems [ ] (3) beyond delivering TERT, exosomal cargos (e.g., miR-29b-3p, ALP); synergistically enhance osteoanabolic and anti-osteoclastic pathways [ ].

## Conclusion
In conclusion, this study substantially advances the theoretical framework for understanding TERT in OP pathogenesis. Our findings redefine TERT as a metabolic rheostat coordinating skeletal homeostasis, transcending its canonical telomere maintenance role [ ]. Crucially, we identified BMP4/Smad1 signaling as the central axis mediating TERT's pro-osteogenic effects. TERT overexpression enhancing Smad1 phosphorylation and BMP4 co-stimulation synergistically amplifying TERT-driven osteogenic transcription. These findings redefine TERT as a chromatin-modifying rheostat that integrates extracellular BMP4/Smad1 cues with epigenetic reprogramming of osteoprogenitors, thereby bridging a critical knowledge gap in skeletal biology. Moreover, we developed Apt-TERT-Exo to target bone tissue and precisely activated TERT expression in BMSCs, promoting osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and improving OP. Clinically, this strategy holds particular promise for treatment-refractory OP subtypes characterized by impaired BMSCs differentiation capacity, yet this work immediately advances a clinically actionable strategy that shifts the therapeutic paradigm from anti-resorptive to anabolic precision medicine.
