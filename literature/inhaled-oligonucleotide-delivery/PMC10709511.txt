## Introduction
Cell-based bone regenerative therapy exhibits considerable potential in the treatment of bone defects caused by trauma, disease, and congenital anomalies. The periosteum, a fibrous membrane covering the outer surface of bone, plays a crucial role in bone formation and regeneration by sourcing osteoprogenitor cells. The remarkable osteogenic potential of periosteal cells (PCs) has led to the effective clinical implementation of PC-based regenerative therapies and tissue engineering. The abundance of progenitor cells in cultured PCs is well established; however, the heterogeneity of the cell population and its impact on bone regeneration remain uncertain. In this study, we aimed to characterize the heterogeneity of cultured PCs via single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) and to examine their osteogenic potential in vivo.

## Methods
Human PCs cultivated using the tissue explant method were utilized in this study. scRNA-seq and real-time PCR were performed to examine the cellular heterogeneity and osteogenic capacity of the cultured PCs. Experimental bone formation by the cultured PCs was examined using the rat model of subcutaneous implantation.

## Results
ScRNA-seq analysis showed that the cultured PCs were categorized into three cell types (osteoprogenitor cells, mesenchymal stem cells, and fibroblasts) with specific gene expression patterns. In addition, the cellular population and osteogenic capacity differed between the central and peripheral regions in the culture dish. The PCs in the central region showed higher osteogenic potential than those in the peripheral region.

## Conclusions
This study revealed the diversity of the composition of the PCs and their distinct osteogenic capabilities in different regions in the culture dish. The findings may provide promising prospects for the development of more efficacious regenerative therapeutic applications using cultured PCs in the future.

## Introduction
Bone regeneration therapy is a medical approach that addresses the unmet medical needs associated with bone defects caused by injuries, diseases, and congenital anomalies [ ]. The process of bone regeneration comprises three fundamental components (cells, scaffolds, and growth factors), which play crucial roles in developing therapeutic approaches to promote bone healing and regeneration [ ]. Cell-based transplantation therapy, including the use of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), has garnered significant interest in bone regeneration, and its effectiveness is widely recognized [ ]. Alternative cell types, such as induced pluripotent stem cells and periosteal cells (PCs), have also been investigated for their potential in bone regenerative therapy [ , ].
The periosteum, a connective layer covering the surface of bone, consists of enriched pluripotent progenitor cells that play important roles in bone formation and regeneration [ ]. Previous studies have identified distinct pluripotent progenitor cell subpopulations within PCs based on the expression of the surface markers, the gene expression profiles, and the functional characteristics [ , ]. In vitro studies have explored the differentiation potential and osteogenic properties of cultured PCs, revealing their capacity to generate mineralized bone-like tissue [ ]. Furthermore, in vivo investigations have demonstrated the remarkable regenerative capacity of PCs in bone fracture healing and callus formation, highlighting their significance in skeletal tissue repair [ ]. PC-based regenerative medicine and tissue engineering have demonstrated high bone regenerative potential in the clinical setting [ ]. We have previously shown the effectiveness of autologous PC transplantation in bone regeneration therapy for bone defects caused by oral trauma and periodontal diseases [ , , ].
Similar to MSCs, PCs are recognized as a heterogeneous and diverse cellular population. In one study, conventional cell cloning analysis was used to demonstrate the distinct cellular heterogeneity and functional divergence of PCs; 83 different clonal cultures of freshly isolated human PCs derived from the mastoid periosteum biopsies of four donors were generated, and the heterogeneity was evaluated using flow cytometry and genome-wide microarrays [ ]. Instead of conventional methods for heterogeneity analysis, single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) methods have recently revolutionized transcriptome research and significantly contributed to the identification and classification of distinct cell types and states within a heterogeneous population [ , ]. Significant progress has been made in characterizing MSCs using scRNA-seq techniques, thus increasing our knowledge about the heterogeneity and plasticity of these cells in tissue regeneration [ ]. However, despite these advancements in MSC research, a comprehensive understanding of the heterogeneity of cultured human PCs remains elusive.
In this study, we aimed to characterize the cell populations of cultured PCs using scRNA-seq. Furthermore, the possible distinctions in the cellular composition and osteogenic potential between the central and peripheral regions in the culture dish were examined. Understanding the heterogeneity and region-specific characteristics of cultured PCs could optimize the utilization of these cells in bone regeneration therapy.

## Materials and methods
2.1

## Isolation and culture of human PCs
Human periosteum was dissected, and the PCs were cultivated, as reported previously [ , ]. Briefly, a small piece of periosteum (5 × 10 mm) was dissected during the extraction of an impacted wisdom tooth under local anesthesia after obtaining informed consent from the patient ( ). The tissue was rinsed with cold phosphate-buffered saline, cut into small pieces (1 × 1 mm) on a clean bench, and placed directly in a 60 mm tissue culture dish (TPP, Trasadingen, Switzerland). The tissues were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere (95 % air and 5 % CO2); the basal medium for PCs (KOJIN BIO, Saitama, Japan) was supplemented with 10 % serum, penicillin (100 IU/ml), and streptomycin (100 mg/ml). Subsequent medium changes were performed twice a week for up to four weeks until subconfluent multilayered PCs were obtained ( ). The cells were incubated with an osteogenic differentiation medium (MSCgo™ Osteogenic XS; Sartorius, Beit HaEmek, Israel) for an additional week to evaluate the osteogenic potential.
This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and was performed with the approval of the ethics committee of Niigata University Medical and Dental Hospital (Approval No: 2021-0018 and G2022-0001).

2.2

## scRNA sequencing and analysis of cultured human PCs
The scRNA sequencing was performed on cultured PCs dispersed by trypsinization. Single cells were encapsulated with emulsion droplets on the Chromium Controller platform (10x Genomics, CA, USA). Library construction was performed using the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3′ Reagent Kits v3.1 (10x Genomics), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The sequencing was conducted using a DNBSEQ-G400 sequencer (MGI Tech, Shenzhen, China), and a set of bioinformatic analysis pipelines was performed using the Cell Ranger Single Cell Software version 7.0. Data were visualized using the Loupe Browser software (10x Genomics). The quality control of library DNA and sequence data was conducted by electrophoresis and UMI counts from barcode rank plot. The batch effects were corrected by using the mutual nearest neighbors algorithm in the Cell Ranger Single Cell Software. The normalization and dimensionality reduction of the data set were performed with default parameters of analysis software. The Louvain algorithm was used for clustering the gene expression profiles of the cells.
For the single-cell analysis of the central and peripheral regions, half of the radius of culture dish, which is 1.5 cm, was marked concentrically from the center. Subsequently, the cells that were located inside and outside the mark were scraped by using a cell scraper and collected separately for subsequent processes of analysis. Each dataset from the central and peripheral regions was merged and re-clustered to analyze the whole cell population in the culture dish.

2.3

## Alkaline phosphatase assay and alizarin red staining
Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity was determined using the TRACP & ALP double-stain kit (Takara Bio Inc., Shiga, Japan), according to the manufacturer's instructions, and the mineralization was determined using alizarin red staining. The cells were fixed with 4 % PFA and stained with alizarin red. Bright-field imaging was performed using a microscope (BZ-X710; Keyence, Osaka, Japan).

2.4

## Real-time quantitative PCR
Total RNA was isolated from the cultured PCs using the RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen) and synthesized to cDNA using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara Bio Inc.). The amplification was performed on a QuantStudio real-time PCR system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., MA, USA) using PowerU SYBR Green Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The expression level of each gene was normalized with that of GAPDH using the ΔΔCt method, as described previously [ ]. The primer sets designed in Primer-BLAST and synthesized by Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. are listed in . 

2.5

## Ectopic bone formation in the rat subcutaneous implantation model
A rat subcutaneous implantation model was used to assess ectopic osteogenesis of the PCs. Eight-week-old male nude rats (F344/N Jcl rnu/rnu; CLEA Japan, Inc., Tokyo, Japan) weighing 230–250 g were used. The surgery was performed under general inhalation anesthesia with nitrous oxide and oxygen. The cultured PCs were peeled, inserted into a titanium mold, and surgically implanted into subcutaneous sites in the rats (approximately 3 × 106 number of cells/each group). All the animals were sacrificed at four and eight weeks after the operation, followed by sampling for analysis. The rats were maintained in accordance with the guidelines of the National Institutes of Health's Guide for Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (approval number, SA01275). This study conformed to the ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) guidelines.

2.6

## Histological preparation and staining
The samples were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde and decalcified with 0.5 M EDTA for 28 days. The paraffin blocks were cut into 4-μm-thick sections using a microtome, and alizarin red staining was performed, as mentioned in section .

2.7

## Microcomputed tomography
Ectopic bone formation in the rat models was evaluated using the CosmoScan GX microcomputed tomographic (μCT) imaging scanner (Rigaku Co., Tokyo, Japan) at an X-ray tube voltage, current, and slice thickness of 90 kV, 88 μA, and 50 μm, respectively. The field of view was 25 mm in diameter and 21 mm in height, with a 512 × 512 matrix; therefore, the size of each pixel was 50 mm. The images were reconstructed using the three-dimensional (3D) software Analyze 12.0 (AnalyzeDirect Inc., KS, USA). Additionally, the volume of calcified substances was measured using the software.

2.8

## Statistical analysis
All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The data were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance followed by post hoc tests for multiple comparisons. GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) was used for all the statistical analyses. A p-value of ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results
3.1

## Cellular heterogeneity and the highly enriched population of osteoprogenitor and MSCs in the cultured PCs
scRNA-seq analysis was conducted using cells expanded from the biopsy of a volunteer via the explant culture method ( A). Based on the gene expression levels of NT5E (CD73), THY1 (CD90), proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2), activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4), S100 calcium-binding protein A4 (S100A4), and vimentin (VIM), the cultured PCs were clustered into three different cell types: osteoprogenitor cells, mesenchymal cells, and fibroblasts. The osteoprogenitor cells dominantly expressed the osteogenic cell makers BMP2 and ATF4, the MSCs expressed the stem cell makers CD73 and CD90 and the proliferative marker PCNA, and the fibroblasts expressed the fibroblast makers S100A4 and VIM ( B, C, and D). The expression of other osteogenic cell-related genes was relatively low, while periosteal cell makers, which paired related homeobox 1 (PRRX1) and gremlin 1 (GREM1), were consistently expressed in all cell types ( ). These results suggested that the cultured PCs exhibited cellular heterogeneity with a particular enrichment of osteoprogenitor and MSCs. 

3.2

## Regional differences in the cellular population using scRNA-seq analysis
PCs cultivated using the tissue explant method exhibit distinct differences in thickness and stiffness between the central and peripheral regions within the culture dish [ ]. Notably, increased ALP activity and alizarin red staining were observed in the central region of the culture dish compared to that in the peripheral region ( A), indicating the possible distinction in cellular composition and osteogenic potential between the central and peripheral regions. 
Therefore, further scRNA-seq analysis was carried out to differentiate the cellular populations in the two regions. After completing the standard four-week culture period with a regular complete medium, the PCs were divided into two experimental groups bordered by 1.5 cm concentrically from the center of the culture dish: the central region (referred to as the C group) and the peripheral region (referred to as the P group). An extra week of culture was performed with an osteogenic differentiation medium after the standard four-week culture with a regular complete medium to validate the osteogenic potential of the PCs in these specific regions. Consequently, the differentiated PCs were divided into two additional groups: those from the central region with osteoblastic differentiation (the C-d group) and those in the peripheral region with osteoblastic differentiation (the P-d group; B). The Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) plots showed that the C and P groups consisted of the three distinct cell types (osteoprogenitor cells, MSCs, and fibroblasts), and the osteoprogenitor cells appeared to be more densely populated in the C group compared to the P group ( C upper). The additional culturing in an osteoblastic differentiation medium led to a considerable expansion of the osteoprogenitor cell population. The C-d group exhibited a substantially higher abundance of osteoprogenitor cells than the P-d group, suggesting the site-specific features of the cell population in the central and peripheral regions ( C lower). Furthermore, the expression levels of some specific genes were compared between the groups to provide a more comprehensive characterization of the four groups ( ). The expression levels of THY1 (CD90), BMP2, and ATF4 tended to be higher in the C group than in the P group. Conversely, the expression level of S100A4 tended to be higher in the P group than in the C group. Notably, the expression levels of both BMP2 and ATF4 were markedly elevated in the C-d group compared to those in the C group, indicating the greater abundance of osteoprogenitor cells in the central region of the culture dish. Other osteogenic cell-related genes expressed at a low level, while periosteal cell makers PRRX1 and GREM1 observed to be consistently expressed in all groups ( ). Taken together, these results suggested the variances in cellular composition between the regions of the cultured PCs, with the central region exhibiting a greater osteogenic potential. 

3.3

## Higher osteogenic differentiation potential of the cultured PCs in the central region
Quantitative PCR was performed to compare the gene expression levels associated with osteogenesis among the groups ( ). In the C-d and P-d groups, where osteogenic differentiation was induced, the level of each gene tended to be elevated to a greater extent than those in the non-differentiation-induced groups (C and P). Moreover, the C-d group exhibited higher levels of each gene than the P-d group. These findings indicated the osteogenic potential of the cultured PCs, particularly in the central region. 
Ectopic osteogenesis was performed in a rat model to determine the osteo-inductive capacity of the PCs in the central region in vivo. Alizarin red staining of the implanted tissue demonstrated significant calcified nodule formation in the differentiation-induced groups (C-d and P-d), and the formation was denser in the central region (C-d) compared to that in the peripheral region (P-d; A and ). A similar trend was confirmed in the morphological analysis and volumetric measurement using Micro-CT ( B). Taken together, these results suggest that the central region of the cultured PCs possessed significant osteo-inductive potential.

## Discussion
The periodontium plays a crucial role in bone formation and regeneration by storing progenitor cells. In this study, we detected the cellular heterogeneity of cultured PCs using scRNA-seq methods. Furthermore, regional differences in the cellular population and their osteogenic properties were characterized both in vitro and in vivo. These findings hold promise for advancements in the development of more potent cellular regenerative therapies utilizing PCs.
The periosteum is a specialized fibrous layer composed of fibroblasts, osteoblasts, stem/progenitor cells, and pericytes; the cellular heterogeneity and functional divergence of PCs have been extensively examined [ , ]. Conventional single-cell lineage analysis using the cloning method has provided evidence suggesting the coexistence of functionally distinct cell populations in the periosteum [ , ]. For the last decade, scRNA-seq technologies have revolutionized the fields of medicine and molecular biology by providing high-resolution views of single-cell heterogeneities [ ]. Recently, Ding et al. [ ] visualized the transcriptional landscape of human PCs derived from the jawbone using scRNA-seq analysis. The PCs in their study were isolated from discarded bone slices after orthognathic surgery and cultured by using the enzymatic digestion method. The cells were grouped based on the expression levels of specific genes; the cluster of progenitor cells was the most dominant, followed by fibroblasts and a small cluster of pericytes [ ]. The cellular profiles of the clusters and the dominancy of the progenitor cells in their study were similar to the findings of the current study. The lack of a pericyte cluster in the present study may be due to differences in the methods used for the isolation and/or cultivation of the PCs between the two studies.
This study demonstrated the dominancy of osteoprogenitor cells and the bone formation capacity of PCs in the animal model, suggesting the pivotal roles of PCs in bone repair and regeneration. In 2015, Chan et al. [ ] identified and characterized a unique stem cell population, named skeletal stem cells (SSCs), which commit only to the osteogenic lineage in mouse bones. SSCs are roughly divided into two niches (bone marrow-derived and periosteum-derived) and have been extensively studied for years [ ]. Periosteal-derived SSCs have recently gained significant attention based on the recognition of their distinguished roles in bone development, homeostasis, and regeneration [ , , ]. In 2018, Debnath et al. [ ] demonstrated the transcriptional signatures of periosteal SSCs that differed from those of other SSCs and MSCs via transcriptional analysis. Additionally, they discovered that periosteal SSCs play important roles in both intramembranous and endochondral ossification, which are considered as independent processes caused by different types of MSCs. More recently, Tsukazaki et al. [ ] reported that periosteal SSCs cooperatively orchestrate the intramembranous and endochondral pathways for the proper development and maintenance of bone growth. The functions of periosteal SSCs remain poorly understood because the precise cellular markers of these cells have not been specified so far; nonetheless, their advantageous roles in bone regeneration remain undebated. Further research and a better understanding of the role of periosteal-derived SSCs in skeletal formation are warranted.
The PCs in this study were cultivated using the explant culture method from a small segment of the periosteum. In our previous microscopic study, PCs presented with a multilayered structure and an expanded extracellular matrix (ECM) distributed among the layers, with a thickness of >50 μm in the central region and <20 μm in the peripheral region [ , ]. Furthermore, the stiffness of the multilayered PCs, measured using atomic force microscopy, was significantly higher in the central region (5.59 ± 3.24 kPa) compared to that in the peripheral region (4.35 ± 2.69 kPa) [ ]. Recent studies have shown that external mechanical cues, including the stiffness of the surrounding ECM, can influence the differentiation of progenitor cells [ ]. According to Sun et al., the stiffness of the matrix affects the osteogenic outcome of hMSCs through mechanotransduction events mediated by integrins [ ]. Piezo1/2, the mechanosensitive ion channels in mesenchymal or osteoblast progenitor cells, act as key force sensors required for bone development and osteoblast differentiation [ ]. Recently, Liu et al. reported that Piezo1 is highly expressed in periosteal stem cells and promotes osteogenic factors via the Yes-associated protein and β-catenin complex [ ]. Thus, cell behavior could be affected by the microenvironment around the cells, including the mechanical stimuli. A deeper understanding of the effect of mechanical stimuli on the microenvironment of PCs will contribute to establishing more efficient bone regenerative therapies with these cells.
In this study, the cultured PCs demonstrated an osteogenic potential, and the incubation of these cells in osteoblastic differentiation medium dramatically increased the expression of osteogenic-related genes. Furthermore, ectopic bone formation was enhanced in the osteoblastic differentiation groups compared to that in the non-differentiation groups. As a modality of cell transplantation in bone regenerative therapy, the beneficial effect of pre-differentiated cells with osteo-inductive factors has been proposed previously [ ]. In the study by Peters et al. [ ], the local application of osteogenic pre-differentiated progenitor cells proved more effective than undifferentiated MSCs in the treatment of delayed bone healing in vivo. In 2015, Cai et al. [ ] reported that the transplantation of pre-treated MSCs in an osteogenic medium containing 10 mM sodium β-glycerophosphate for 10 days contributed to alveolar bone regeneration in a rat model. In addition, the effect of a pre-culture period with osteogenic media on bone formation by MSCs was examined by Castano-Izquierdo et al. [ ], wherein MSCs cultured with osteogenic media were implanted into the rat cranium for different time periods (4, 10, and 16 days). Histomorphometric analysis demonstrated that the MSCs cultured with osteogenic media for 4 days showed highest bone formation, and longer pre-culture periods led to a progressive reduction in osteo-induction. Thus, employing pre-osteodifferentiated PCs for cell transplantation therapy could be considered as a prospective option. However, additional fundamental studies, such as those exploring the optimal incubation period with an osteogenic medium, must be conducted. Furthermore, ectopic bone regeneration along with orthotopic bone regeneration should be examined in the future for applying in clinical sets. Additionally, long-term observations with increasing the number of samples in animal experiments would be required to strengthen our hypothesis of this study.

5

## Conclusion
This study revealed the heterogenicity and high osteogenic potential of cultured PCs. The findings indicate that further research using these cells holds great promise for advances in bone regenerative medicine.
