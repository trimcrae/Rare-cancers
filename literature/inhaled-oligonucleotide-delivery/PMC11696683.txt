Mitochondria-related genes (Mito-RGs) play important roles in multiple diseases. However, the role of mitochondria-related genes in MIRI remains largely unknown. The GSE67308 dataset from the GEO database was utilized to identify MIRI-related gene modules through WGCNA. Meanwhile, differential expression analysis was conducted to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the GSE61592 dataset. Next, candidate Mito-RGs related to MIRI were screened by Venn analysis. Thereafter, a myocardial hypoxia/reperfusion (H/R) H9C2 cell model and a mouse ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) model were established to verify the expression level of glycine decarboxylase (Gldc) in MIRI in vitro and in vivo. Based on data from the GEO database, Gldc levels were notably upregulated in murine MIRI samples, compared to the control group. RT-qPCR and western blot confirmed that Gldc levels were obviously elevated in the heart of I/R mice and H/R-exposed cardiomyocytes. Moreover, the deficiency of Gldc notably increased the viability and reduced the apoptosis and inflammatory responses in H9C2 cells exposed to H/R. Meanwhile, Gldc downregulation significantly reduced p-NF-κB p65, Bax and cleaved caspase 3 levels and elevated p-Akt and Bcl-2 levels in H9C2 cells exposed to H/R. The ROC curve analysis further demonstrated that Gldc gene exhibited good diagnostic value for MIRI. Collectively, Gldc deficiency could attenuate H/R injury in cardiomyocytes in vitro through activating Akt and inactivating NF-κB signalings. These data suggested that GLDC may serve as both a diagnostic and therapeutic target for MIRI.

## Introduction
Myocardial ischemia is a serious condition in the heart, it is often caused by reduced or stopped blood perfusion to the heart , . Once the blood flow to the heart is clogged, the cardiac muscle’s oxygen and nutrient supply will decrease and cardiac muscle energy metabolism will be disturbance, ultimately resulting in heart dysfunction , . Severe myocardial ischemia can lead to myocardial infarction (MI), which is a leading cause of death worldwide – . Thrombolytic therapy and percutaneous coronary intervention are the primary treatment methods for patients with MI , . These interventions can restore blood flow to the heart timely . However, the recovery of ischemic myocardial blood flow often inevitably lead to myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI) , . In brief, MIRI is characterized as a combined cardiac injury that occurs during the ischemia and reperfusion processes . Evidence has shown that early diagnosis of MI can ensure timely treatment initiation, thereby reducing mortality and improving the prognosis of patients . Thus, uncovering novel biomarkers related to MIRI may be helpful for enhancing diagnosis and treatment of MI.
Mitochondria are key organelles in cells, participating in multiple essential cellular functions, including redox homeostasis, ATP production, apoptosis and autophagy , . Mitochondria make up approximately 30% of cardiomyocyte volume and play a crucial role in maintaining cardiac homeostasis and myocardial cell survival , . MIRI can lead to ATP depletion, excessive production of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) and damage to DNA and proteins, eventually resulting in myocardial cell death . Multiple lines of evidence indicated that mitochondrial dysfunction may be an important mechanisms underlying cardiac cell injury during MIRI , . Recently, mitochondria-related genes (Mito-RGs) have been implicated in the development of heart diseases including MIRI , . However, the specific role of Mito-RGs in MIRI remains largely unknown.
Glycine decarboxylase (GLDC) plays a role in glycine metabolism in mammalian cells , . It has been shown that glycine is involved in some important metabolic processes in mitochondria, exerting a crucial role in maintaining redox homeostasis . However, the role of GLDC in MIRI has not been explored previously. In this study, we identified Gldc as a hub Mito-RG related to MIRI progression. Compared to normal samples, the expression level of Gldc was remarkably upregulated in MIRI samples. Meanwhile, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis further confirmed the diagnostic value of Gldc for MIRI. Suggesting that Gldc may serve as a potential diagnostic marker in MIRI.

## Materials and methods

## Data collection
The GSE61592, GSE67308, GSE130217, GSE97320 and GSE66360 data files were acquired from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database.
The GSE61592 dataset contained 3 heart samples from mice subjected to I/R injury (MIRI group) and 3 heart samples from mice that were not undergone any surgery (normal group), and Illumina MouseWG-6 v2.0 expression beadchip was used for data processing. The GSE67308 dataset contained 8 heart samples from mice subjected to I/R injury (MIRI group) and 8 heart samples from mice that were not received any surgery (normal group), and Agilent-014868 Whole Mouse Genome Microarray 4 × 44 K G4122F was used for data processing. The transcriptome profiles of 6 heart samples from mice subjected to I/R injury (MIRI group) and 6 heart samples from mice subjected to sham operation (normal group) were collected from GSE130217 dataset, which was processed on Illumina NextSeq 500 (Mus musculus).
The GSE97320 dataset contained 6 peripheral blood samples of 6 individuals including 3 patients with acute MI and 3 healthy people, and GSE66360 dataset contained 99 circulating endothelial cell samples of 99 individuals including 49 patients with acute MI and 50 healthy controls, Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array was used for data processing.
1140 Mito-RGs were downloaded from the MitoCarta3.0 database (http://www.broadinstitute.org/mitocarta) (table S1)

## Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
The “WGCNA” package in R language was utilized to screen key modules . Firstly, utilizing data from the GSE67308 dataset, the top 25% of genes screened by analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used for WGCNA. Next, the Pearson’s correlation coefficient between each gene was calculated. Meanwhile, the optimal soft threshold β was then determined to ensure that the network conform to the standard of scale-free coexpression network. The adjacency matrix was then transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). Following this, the gene significance and module significance were calculated for evaluating the significance of genes and clinical information and analyzing the relationship between modules and traits. Finally, modules related to clinical traits were identified and the differential genes in these modules were screened.

## Differential expression analysis
The data from the GSE61592 dataset were analyzed for screening differential expressed genes (DEGs). The DEGs were identified by the “limma” package (version 3.52.4) in R language with |log2FC| > 0.5 and FDR < 0.05 as screening threshold .

## Function enrichment analysis
The Gene Ontology (GO) function annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genome (KEGG, www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html) enrichment analysis were conducted using the “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.7.1.2) in R language with p value < 0.05 as the thresholds. GO analysis included biological process (BP), molecular function (MF) and cellular component (CC).

## Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks
The interaction between each protein was predicted by the STRING database (https://string-db.org/,version 11.0). Meanwhile, the PPI networks were visualized by the Cytoscape software (version 3.7.2) . Additionally, the PPI network was also constructed by using the online GeneMANIA database (http://www.genemania.org).

## Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)
According to the median target gene level, samples were grouped into high-Gldc level (H-Gldc) and low-Gldc level (L-Gldc) groups. The enriched signaling pathways between these two groups were identified using both GSEA and GSVA. The GSEA was conducted using the R package “clusterProfiler” (version 4.7.1.2) with p value < 0.05 as the threshold. The GSVA was conducted using the m2.cp.reactome.v2023.1.Mm.symbols and m5.go.v2023.1.Mm.symbols datasets downloaded from the Molecular Signature Database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) with |LogFC|>0.5 and p-value < 0.05 as the threshold.

## Immune cells profile
The relative proportion of 22 immune cells were calculated using the CIBERSORT (version 0.1.0) software . Additionally, ssGSEA algorithm from the “GSVA” (version 1.46.0) in R package was utilized to calculate the abundance of 28 immune cells between H-Gldc and L-Gldc groups . Meanwhile, 64 types of immune cells between two groups were assessed using the “Xcell” (algorithm) in R package .

## Murine MIRI models
All the animal experiments were complied with the guidelines of the Tianjin Medical Experimental Animal Care and performed in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. Animal protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Yi Shengyuan Gene Technology (Tianjin) Co., Ltd. (protocol number YSY-DWLL-2023254). A total of 24 C57BL/6 mice (8-week-old) were purchased from Beijing Huafukang Biotechnology Co., LTD. After 3 days of adaptive feeding, animals were deprived of food for 12 h before surgery, but not of water. After that, all mice were randomly grouped into 2 groups: sham and I/R groups (n = 12 per group).
Mice in the I/R group were anesthetized with isoflurane inhalation, and the heart of each mouse was exposed through a thoracotomy. The isoflurane anesthesia exposure consisted of 5 min for induction (3%, 3 L/min) and 5 min for maintenance (1.5%, 0.8 L/min). Next, the left anterior descending coronary artery was ligated with an atraumatic suture for 40 min, followed by 12 h of reperfusion. For the sham group, animals received the same surgery procedure without any ligation. After the reperfusion, all mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation under anesthesia, and the heart tissue of each mice was collected.

## 2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining
A TTC staining kit (S19026, Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co., LTD) was used to evaluate the myocardial infarct size. Firstly, heart tissues were washed with 0.9% NaCl and then subsequently frozen at -20 °C for 20 min. Next, the heart tissues were continuously cut into 5 sections with the same thickness. All sections were stained with 1% TTC for 10 min in darkness at 37 °C. Thereafter, images were photographed under a digital camera. The infarct area was analyzed using the Image-J software. The myocardial infarct area appeared pale white.

## Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining
Hearts were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, and sliced into 4 μm sections. Thereafter, sections were stained with hematoxylin (ZLI-9613, Beijing Zhongshan Jinqiao Biotechnology Co., LTD) and eosin solution (ZLI-9610, Zhongshan Jinqiao) according to themanufacturers’ instructions. Finally, sections were sealed with neutral resin and observed using an optical microscope.

## Masson’s trichrome staining
The Masson’s trichrome staining kit (G1340, Solarbio) was used to stain fibrotic tissue according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The stained sections were sealed with neutral resin and observed using an optical microscope.

## Real time-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was isolated from heart tissues using the Redzol Reagent (FTR-50, SBS Genetech Co., Ltd.) and then reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Surescript™ First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (QP056, iGeneBio). Next, the mRNA levels of GLDC were determined using RT-qPCR with the 2×SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix kit (None ROX) (MPC2203026, Servicebio). GAPDH was served as an endogenous control. The primers used in this study were listed in Table  . Meanwhile, the Gldc, Collagen I and Collagen III mRNA levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. 

## Western blot
Protein concentration was measured using the Bradford protein concentration detection kit (AR-145, Wuhan Boster Biological Technology, LTD). The proteins were fractionated via 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies including anti-Gldc (ab97625, 1:750), anti-GAPDH (ab9485, 1:1000), anti-NF-kB p65 (ab207297, 1:1000), anti-p-NF-κB p65 (ab239882, 1:1000), anti-AKT (ab8805, 1:1000), anti-p-AKT (ab38449, 1:1000), anti-Bcl-2 (ab32124, 1:1000), anti-Bax (ab32503, 1:1000), anti-Cleaved Caspase-3 (ab2302, 1:1000), followed by incubation with the Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 790, ab175781, 1:10000) secondary antibody. All antibodies were obtained from Abcam. Blots were then detected by using the ECL kit, and results were analyzed using the Image J software.

## Cell culture, transfection and treatment
H9C2 cells (Procell) were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO2.
To silence Gldc expression in cells, Gldc specific small interfering RNA (siRNA) sequences were purchased from (GUIJI Biotechnology Co., LTD). Approximately 105 H9C2 cells were transfected with 50 pmol of siRNA negative control, Gldc siRNA1 or Gldc siRNA2 using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (2343152, Invitrogen).
To mimic the myocardial hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) injury in vitro, H9C2 cells were cultured in DMEM without glucose and serum and maintained in an anaerobic chamber (95% N2 and 5% CO2) at 37 °C for 4 h. Next, cells were then cultured in complete DMEM containing glucose and serum and maintained under normal culture conditions with 95% air and 5% CO2 for 1, 6, 12 and 24 h. Cells in the control group were cultured under normoxic conditions.

## Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
H9C2 cells were seeded onto a 96-well plate overnight. After treatments, cells were treated with CCK-8 reagent (10 µL) for 2 h. Next, the absorbance value of each well was measured using a microplate reader (Bio-tek) at 450 nm.

## Flow cytometry
The Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis kit (AP101-100-kit, MULTI SCIENCES) was used for evaluating cell apoptosis. 105 cells were re-suspended in 500 µL of 1×Binding Buffer and then stained with 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 10 µL of PI for 15 min in darkness. The analysis of apoptotic cells was performed using the BD FACSCalibur™ Flow Cytometer (BD Biosciences).
The mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) in cells was assessed using the mitochondrial membrane potential assay kit with JC-1 (C2006, Beyotime) via flow cytometry, according to the manufacturer`s instructions. Finally, the results were analyzed using a BD FACSCaliburTM Flow Cytometer (BD Biosciences).
The Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) assay kit (S0033S, Beyotime) was utilized to determine ROS production in cells using the fluorescent probe 2′, 7′-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA). DCFH-DA can readily cross the cell membrane and enter the cells. Once inside, it is hydrolyzed by intracellular esterases to produce DCFH, which cannot permeate the cell membrane, thus facilitating the loading of the probe into cells. Meanwhile, intracellular ROS can oxidize the non-fluorescent DCFH, resulting in the formation of fluorescent 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescein (DCF). Intracellular ROS levels were detected by measuring the DCF fluorescence intensity. The results were analyzed using a BD FACSCaliburTM Flow Cytometer (BD Biosciences).

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The levels of IL-6, TNF-a, MDA and LDH in the supernatant of H9C2 cells were assessed by using the ELISA kits (ml102828, ml002859, ml077384, ml003416, Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). The levels of LDH (J24590) and CK-MB (J24765) in the heart tissues of mice were evaluated by the ELISA kits (GILED BIOTECHNOLOGY).

## Statistical analyses
The potential drug used for the treatment of MIRI was predicted using the DREIMT database (http://www.dreimt.org/, version 1.1.1). T test was employed for comparing the difference in gene levels and immune cell infiltration between two groups. TISDB database (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) was utilized for calculating the correlation between gene and immune infiltrating cells; and other results were analyzed using the correlation function ‘cor’ in R using the ‘pearson’ method. All bioinformatics analysis were performed using R software (https://www.r-project.org/, version 4.2.2). For experimental results, statistical significance was analyzed by unpaired Student t test or or one-way ANOVA test using GraphPad Prism (version 9.5). p-value < 0.05 was regarded as significant statistically.

## Results

## Identification of candidate Mito-RGs associated with MIRI
To screen MIRI-related gene modules in the GSE67308 cohort, WGCNA was performed. After determining soft threshold power of β = 6, 15 gene modules were clustered (Fig.  A,B). The blue module exhibited the strongest negative correlation with MIRI, the turquoise module had the strongest positive correlation with MIRI (Fig.  C), and a total of 2377 genes were screened within these two modules.
Furthermore, the DEGs between the normal and MIRI groups in the GSE61592 cohort were analyzed using the “limma” package in R language. Compared to normal group, a total of 4139 DEGs were screened in the MIRI group, including 2258 upregulated genes and 1881 downregulated genes (Fig.  D).
The overlap among the 2377 MIRI-related genes, 4139 DEGs and 1140 Mito-RGs was assessed using a Venn diagram, resulting in the identification of 11 hub genes including Coq10a, Mrpl55, Bdh1, Gldc, Mthfd2, Alas2, Bcat2, Slc25a45, Ucp3, Cbr3 and Armcx1 (Fig.  E and Table ).
GO and KEGG analyses were then performed on these 11 hub genes. These hub genes were involved in 13 pathways, including “biosynthesis of cofactors” and “glycine, serine and threonine metabolism” (Table ), with the top 10 pathways listed in Fig.  F. Additionally, 87 GO terms were identified, comprising including 58 BP (e.g. quinone metabolic process and small molecule catabolic process), 22 MF (e.g. pyridoxal phosphate binding and vitamin B6 binding) and 7 CC (e.g. mitochondrial matrix and organellar large ribosomal subunit) (Fig.  G,I, Table ).

 

## Gldc gene was selected as a hub Mito-RG associated with MIRI
GeneMANIA database was used to construct the PPI network based on the proteins encoded by these 11 hub genes (Fig.  A), resulting in the identification of a total of 245 interaction sites among these genes (Table ).
Meanwhile, a PPI network involving proteins encoded by these 11 hub genes was constructed using the STRING database (minimum required interaction score > 0.4 as a threshold) and visualized by the Cytoscape software. The PPI network consisted of 4 nodes and 3 edges, with Gldc interacting with other 3 genes, indicating that Gldc may serve as a hub gene (Fig.  B). Thus, Gldc was selected for further analysis to explore its role in MIRI.

 

## Validation of the expression and clinical value of Gldc gene
To further validate the expression levels of Gldc between murine MIRI and normal samples, GSE61592 and GSE130217 cohorts were utilized. The data presented in Fig.  A,B showed that Gldc levels were strongly elevated in MIRI samples compared to normal controls in GSE61592 and GSE130217 cohorts. Next, the GSE61592 and GSE130217 datasets were merged after removing batch effect. A ROC curve was drew to assess the diagnostic value of Gldc in MIRI based on the merge datasets. The AUC of the ROC curve was 0.778, suggesting that Gldc may serve as a potential diagnostic marker for MIRI (Fig.  C).
Additionally, data from the HPA database showed that GLDC mRNA and protein levels were extremely low in normal human heart muscle tissues (Fig.  D,E). Given the direct association between MIRI and MI , the expression levels of GLDC validated in MI tissues from patients with acute MI and normal samples from healthy controls in the MI-related dataset GSE97320. The data showed that GLDC levels were remarkably upregulated in MI samples than that in normal controls (Fig.  F). A ROC curve was then drew to assess the diagnostic value of GLDC in MI using data from the GSE66360 dataset. The AUC of the ROC curve was 0.605, suggesting GLDC may also serve as a potential diagnostic marker for MI (Fig.  G).
Furthermore, the “oncoPredict” package (version 0.2) in R language was used for predicting drug sensitivity based on data from the GSE66360 dataset . The GLDC level exhibited a significant negative correlation with the IC50 values of 25 drugs such as AZD2014_1441 (mTOR inhibitor), LY2109761_1852 (TGF-β receptor type I/II inhibitor), AZD1208_1449 (PIM inhibitor) and AZD3759_1915 (EGFR inhibitor) (Fig.  H,I and Table ).
According to the median GLDC level in MI samples in GSE66360 dataset, MI samples were grouped into high-GLDC level (H-GLDC) and low-GLDC level (L-GLDC) groups. Subsequently, differential expression analysis was performed to screen DEGs between H-GLDC and L-GLDC groups. GLDC gene and top 40 DEGs between two groups were selected as potential therapeutic targets. Meanwhile, these targets were employed for predicting the potential drugs using the DREIMT database (|tau| scores > 99 and FDR<0.05 as thresholds). In total, 515 drugs with potential anti-MI effects were identified and are listed in Fig.  J and Table .

 

## Biological function analysis of gldc gene in MIRI
According to the median Gldc levels in MIRI samples from the GSE67308 dataset, MIRI samples were grouped into H-Gldc and L-Gldc groups. DEGs were screened between two groups and GSEA and GSVA were then performed. The results of GSEA showed that DEGs were involved in 73 signaling pathways such as “ECM-receptor interaction-Mus musculus”, “NOD-like receptor signaling pathway-Mus musculus”, “Toll-like receptor (TLR) signaling pathway-Mus musculus”, “TNF signaling pathway-Mus musculus (house mouse)”, “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway-Mus musculus” and “NF-kappa (κ) B signaling pathway-Mus musculus” (Fig.  A,B and Table ). The results of GSVA showed that DEGs were involved in 197 pathways such as “Reactome_activated_TAK1_mediates_p38_MAPK_activation” and “interleukin_4_and_interleukin_13_signaling” (Fig.  C and Table ).

 

## Immune cell infiltration in H-Gldc and L-Gldc groups
The role of Gldc in the immune microenvironment of murine MIRI samples was then investigated. Utilizing the CIBERSORT algorithm, the relative levels of 22 immune cells in each sample were analyzed in the GSE67308 cohort (Fig.  A). Compared to the L-Gldc group, the proportions of mast cells and neutrophils were elevated and the proportions of memory B cells and follicular CD4 T cells were markedly declined in H-Gldc group (Fig.  B). Meanwhile, Gldc levels were positively correlated with the proportions of memory CD8 T cells, mast cells and gamma delta T cells and was negatively correlated with the proportions of memory B cells (Fig.  C). Meanwhile, the Xcell algorithm and TISIDB database were also used to analyze the correlation between GLDC expression and the abundance of immune cells in MIRI samples in the GSE67308 cohort. The results of Xcell algorithm revealed that Gldc gene was positively correlated with B cells and class switched memory B.cells (Fig.  D). In the TISIDB database, Gldc gene was positively correlated with activated B cells and immature B cells (Fig.  E). The correlation between each immune cell in H-Gldc and L-Gldc groups was shown in Fig.  F.

 

## Gldc level was elevated in heart tissue of I/R mice and H/R cardiomyocytes
Next, to validate the Gldc level both in vivo and in vitro, a mouse myocardial I/R model in vivo and a H/R model using H9C2 cells in vitro were established. The results of TTC staining showed that compared to the sham group, I/R operation remarkably increased the myocardial infarct area of mice (Fig.  A). Additionally, evidence has shown that myocardial ischemia/reperfusion can induce myocardial injury and fibrosis , . Histological analyses using H&E and Masson staining were conducted to observe the histological condition and fibrosis in heart tissue of mice. The results showed that compared to the sham group, obvious inflammatory infiltration, disordered arrangement of myocardial cells and the increased range of myocardial fibrosis were observed in the I/R group (Fig.  B), suggesting that I/R surgery significantly induced myocardial injury and fibrosis in mice. Furthermore, LDH and CK-MB are essential cardiac biomarkers used to evaluate myocardial injury . Elevated levels of LDH and CK-MB were observed in heart tissue of I/R mice (Fig.  C), which indicated cardiac injury in I/R mice. Additionally, we found that the mRNA levels of fibrosis markers, collagen I and collagen III, were notably elevated in heart tissue of I/R mice (Fig.  D), indicating the presence of cardiac fibrosis in I/R mice. Meanwhile, p-NF-κB p65 levels were notably increased and p-Akt levels were remarkably reduced in heart tissue of mice subjected to I/R (Fig.  E,F), confirming that myocardial ischemia/reperfusion leads to cardiomyocyte apoptosis and inflammation. These findings indicated that a mouse I/R model was successfully established. As shown in Fig.  G,H, the mRNA and protein levels of Gldc in heart tissue were notably increased in the I/R group compared to the sham group.
Furthermore, compared to the control group, H9C2 cells subjected to 4 h of hypoxia followed by reoxygenation periods of 6, 12 and 24 h exhibited approximately 50% loss of cell viability (Fig.  I). Moreover, 4 h of hypoxia and 12 h of reoxygenation notably elevated the percentage of apoptotic cells to 44.97%, in contrast to 11.61% in the control group (Fig.  J). In the following experiments, H9C2 cells were subjected to 4 h of hypoxia followed by 12 h of reoxygenation. The mRNA and protein levels of Gldc were notably increased in H/R-exposed H9C2 cells compared to the control group (Fig.  K,L).

 

## Downregulation of Gldc protected against H/R injury in H9C2 cellsin vitro through activating Akt and inactivating NF-κB signalings
Next, we explored the role of Gldc in H/R injury in vitro. As shown in Fig.  A,B, Gldc siRNA1 strongly reduced Gldc levels in H9C2 cells, compared to the si-NC group. Moreover, compared to H/R + si-NC group, Gldc siRNA1 remarkably declined Gldc expression in H9C2 cells exposed to H/R (Fig.  C). Meanwhile, we performed assays to evaluate the effects of si-NC and Gldc siRNA1 on normal H9C2 cells. As shown in Supplementary Fig. A,B, Gldc siRNA1 did not significantly affect the viability or apoptosis of H9C2 cells. Additionally, Gldc siRNA1 had no obvious effects on MMP, ROS production or the levels of IL-6, LDH, MDA and TNF-α in H9C2 cells (Supplementary figure C–E). These results suggested that Gldc siRNA1 exhibits no obvious effect on H9C2 cells.
Significantly, Gldc siRNA1 enhanced cell ability and suppressed apoptosis in H/R-exposed H9C2 cells (Fig.  D,E). Furthermore, mitochondrial function can be evaluated by measuring mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production . We conducted flow cytometry to assess MMP and ROS production in H9C2 cells. As shown in Fig.  F,G, compared to control group, H/R exposure notably diminished MMP and elevated ROS production in H9C2 cells; however, Gldc siRNA1 effectively reversed these changes (Fig.  F,G). Downregulation of Gldc also obviously reduced levels of IL-6, TNF-a, MDA and LDH in H9C2 cells exposed to H/R, compared to HR + si-NC group (Fig.  H). These results showed that Gldc downregulation could improve mitochondrial function and suppress inflammatory responses in H/R-exposed H9C2 cells.

 

Furthermore, compared to control group, H/R exposure notably elevated levels of p-NF-κB p65, Bax and cleaved caspase 3 and reduced levels of p-Akt and Bcl-2 in H9C2 cells; however, these protein levels were reversed by Gldc siRNA1 (Fig.  A–F). Collectively, downregulation of Gldc could protect against H/R injury in H9C2 cells through activating Akt and inactivating NF-κB signalings.

 

## Discussion
Mito-RGs can function as potential therapeutic targets in various diseases such as cancer, heart and skeletal diseases , , . Zhang et al. constructed a six Mito-RG signature (including APOOL, HIGD1A, MAOB, BCAP31, SLC44A2, and CHPT1), and demonstrated its predictive ability for esophageal carcinoma prognosis . Peng et al. showed that the aberrant expression of Mito-RGs, including Pdk4, Hmgcs2, Decr1, and Ivd, was related to abnormal cardiac function in diabetic cardiomyopathy . Guo et al. found that Mito-RG mitofusin-2 was highly expressed in intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) and showed good diagnostic performance in IVDD . Shu et al. reported that Mito-RG NDUFB3 is highly expressed in sepsis and may act as a therapeutic targets in sepsis . These findings all confirmed the important role of Mito-RGs in different diseases.
In the current study, we explored the role of Mito-RGs in MIRI. Analysis of the GSE61592 and GSE130217 datasets revealed that Gldc levels was strongly elevated in MIRI samples from mice, compared to control group. Meanwhile, Gldc gene exhibited a good diagnostic value for MIRI. Additionally, the GSE97320 dataset, which included human peripheral blood samples from MI patients and healthy controls, was also utilized to verify the GLDC levels in MI. The GLDC levels were greatly increased in MI samples compared to control group, suggesting that GLDC is upregulated in response to myocardial injury caused by MI. Furthermore, RT-qPCR and western blot assays confirmed that Gldc levels were notably elevated in heart tissue of I/R mice and H/R-exposed cardiomyocytes. Moreover, inhibition of Gldc obviously suppressed the apoptosis and inflammatory responses and improve mitochondrial function in H/R-exposed H9C2 cells, suggesting that downregulating Gldc could attenuate H/R injury in cardiomyocytes. These results indicated that GLDC might function as a candidate therapeutic target for MI.
Next, to further explore the potential signaling pathways affected by Gldc gene, GSEA analysis was performed. The results revealed that Gldc gene might closely related to glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, inflammation-related signaling pathways and cell survival-related signaling pathways. MIRI could lead to significant metabolic disorders including amino acid metabolism , . For example, the abnormal branched chain amino acid catabolism could render the heart vulnerable to MIRI . Moreover, Liu et al. found that isoproterenol (ISO) induced-MI could affect the glycine, serine and threonine metabolism . GLDC has been recognized as an oxidoreductase, exerting an important role in regulating glycine metabolic process . These findings suggested that Gldc might be involved in MIRI via affecting amino acid catabolism, and this assumption deserved to be further confirmed in the future.
Myocardial ischemia and reperfusion processes can lead to cell apoptosis and excessive inflammatory response and cause myocardial injury , . During MIRI, some inflammation-related pathways are activated, such as TLR4/NF-kB, JAK/STAT, Rho/Rock and NLRP3 inflammation signaling pathways – . Suppressing these inflammation-related signaling pathways could alleviate MIRI – . Additionally, PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, a central pathway controlling cell growth, also participates in MIRI process . Activation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway could protect cardiomyocytes from MIRI , . Thus, targeting inflammation- and cell survival-related pathways may ameliorate MIRI. Our results indicated that downregulation of Gldc could notably reduced the phosphorylation of NF-κB and increased the phosphorylation of Akt in H9C2-exposed H9C2 cells, suggesting that Gldc may be participated in MI progression via affecting NF-κB and Akt signaling pathways. Collectively, Gldc deficiency may protect against H/R injury in cardiomyocytes in vitro through activating Akt and inactivating NF-κB signalings.
Evidence has been shown that immune system exerts a crucial role in the development of MIRI , . During MIRI, infiltrated immune cells (e.g. T cells, neutrophils and macrophages) are involved in tissue injury and repair and cardiac remodeling – . Excessive neutrophils infiltrate injured cardiac tissue can promote myocardial injury . Conversely, activated regulatory T cells may attenuate MIRI . In this study, we found that the proportions of neutrophils were elevated, and the proportions of B memory cells, and follicular CD4 T cells were declined in the high-Gldc level group, which were consistent with previous studies , . These results showed that Gldc may affect immune cell infiltration during MIRI. Thus, we speculated that Gldc may be participated in MIRI progression via regulating immune cell infiltration; and this assumption still needs to be further investigated in the future.
Long-term ischemia and hypoxia in cardiac tissue can cause tissue necrosis, ultimately leading to heart failure and death in patients. Therefore, restoring coronary blood flow is crucial for improving patient outcomes. Paradoxically, burst reperfusion can also cause tissue damage, exacerbating the initial ischemic injury . Thus, circulating blood cells play a significant role in myocardial ischemia and hypoxic injury , . Evidence suggested that T lymphocytes cells or platelets may be crucial instigators of acute MIRI , , indicating that certain blood cells may promote myocardial damage during reperfusion. In this study, we found that Gldc was significantly upregulated in the heart tissue of I/R mice. This upregulation may be attributed to myocardial damage resulting from the re-influx of circulating blood into the ischemic area, and we will verify this conclusion in follow-up experiments.

## Conclusion
In this study, we found that Gldc level was obviously upregulated in heart tissue of I/R mice in vivo and H/R-exposed cardiomyocytes in vitro. The ROC curve confirmed that Gldc gene exhibited a good diagnostic value for MIRI. Moreover, Gldc deficiency could attenuate H/R injury in cardiomyocytes in vitro through activating Akt and inactivating NF-κB signalings. These data showed that GLDC may serve as a potential therapeutic target for MIRI. Our present work primarily focused on the role of Gldc in the MIRI progression in vitro; thus, future in vivo studies are necessary to investigate the role and molecular mechanism of Gldc in MIRI.
