We acquired data from rats with osteoporosis via the GEO database for bioinformatics analysis. JBMMSCs (Jaw Bone Marrow Mesenchymal Stem Cells) were then isolated from rat mandibles and characterized via flow cytometry. To evaluate the therapeutic effect of semaglutide on osteoporosis following JBMMSC intervention in osteoporotic rat models, we employed Haematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining. In vitro, the ideal semaglutide concentration for JBMMSC osteogenic induction was established using CCK-8 viability, colony formation, and scratch migration assays. JBMMSCs were subsequently injected into the tail vein for osteoporosis treatment. Protein expression related to adipogenic and osteogenic differentiation pathways was assessed by Western blotting. Furthermore, we evaluated the osteogenic (using ALP and ARS staining) and adipogenic (using Oil Red O staining) differentiation potential of JBMMSCs. Finally, to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying semaglutide’s treatment of osteoporosis mediated by JBMMSCs, we analyzed the protein levels of key signaling molecules including p-CREB, YAP, BRD4; pluripotency factors OCT4, SOX2, c-Myc; cell cycle regulator Cyclin D1; signaling mediators p-STAT3, TET3, SHP2, p-SMAD1/5; and osteogenic transcription factors β-Catenin, RUNX2, DLX5. HE staining revealed that JBMMSC intervention combined with semaglutide significantly ameliorated osteoporosis in rats. Alizarin Red S and ALP staining further indicated a marked elevation in calcified nodule formation and ALP activity following semaglutide treatment, demonstrating optimal osteogenic potential in JBMMSCs. Immunofluorescence analysis confirmed elevated expression of stemness-associated proteins in these cells. Furthermore, CCK-8 and colony formation assays indicated that semaglutide stimulated JBMMSC proliferation and stemness maintenance. Western blot analysis detected a significant upregulation of p-CREB, OCT4, BMP2, and RUNX2 protein expression in JBMMSCs treated with semaglutide. Mechanistically, semaglutide corrected osteoporosis through dual pathways: promoting osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation via the BMP2/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 axis, and preserving bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell stemness via the CREB/YAP/BRD4 axis. These mechanistic insights were validated by rescue experiments. GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) enhance osteogenic differentiation of jaw bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (JBMMSCs) through modulation of the CREB/YAP/STAT3/BRD4 signaling axis. This pathway activation potentiates cellular proliferation and stemness maintenance, suggesting a promising therapeutic strategy for osteoporosis intervention.

## Introduction
Osteoporosis manifests as a systemic skeletal disorder featuring compromised bone microstructure, reduced bone mass and mineral density, elevated fragility, and increased fracture risk. Diagnosis requires a bone mineral density (BMD) T-score ≤ −2.5 SD . It affects over 200 million individuals worldwide – . In terms of the specific groups affected, the primary targets are the elderly and postmenopausal women . Osteoporosis arises from disrupted bone remodeling homeostasis, where osteoclastic resorption outpaces osteoblastic formation, coupled with skewed mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) differentiation favoring adipogenesis over osteogenesis. These factors further deteriorate the bone microenvironment. And as the population ages, its frequency keeps increasing.Osteoporotic fractures often lead to debilitating functional impairment and premature mortality, imposing substantial socioeconomic burdens on healthcare systems and society .
Residing within bone marrow, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are a population of non-hematopoietic stem cells. Owing to their inherent stemness properties, these cells possess the capacity for robust proliferation and can give rise to diverse cell types such as adipocytes, chondrocytes, and osteoblasts .The process of osteogenic differentiation in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) is pivotal for skeletal development and bone formation, as it facilitates osteogenesis and the preservation of existing bone mass .The bone healing process is modulated by adipogenic differentiation, which acts by altering the osseous tissue environment, releasing signaling molecules (including interleukin-6 and TNF-α), and promoting inflammation . Despite their importance, little is understood about the molecular mechanisms underlying BMSCs’ osteogenic and adipogenic development, which limits the breadth of potential therapeutic applications for these cells. Furthermore, it is still unclear which molecular mechanisms maintain the stemness of BMSCs. Important tactics for the therapeutic management of osteoporosis include encouraging BMSC proliferation, improving their osteogenic differentiation, and decreasing their adipogenic differentiation.
A relatively new family of incretin mimetics called semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA), is transforming the way type 2 diabetic mellitus (T2DM) is treated. GLP-1RAs have a favourable influence on weight, cardiovascular risk, and other factors in addition to their hypoglycemic effects . Semaglutide limits renal clearance and DPP-4 degradation because of its strong affinity for the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) and its modified ability to bind to albumin reversibly. This raises GLP-1 receptor activity and extends the drug’s half-life. Semaglutide and natural human GLP-1 are 94% similar , .Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, is commonly employed in the clinical management of type 2 diabetes. Its therapeutic benefits extend beyond glycemic control to include weight reduction, improved lipid profiles, appetite suppression, delayed gastric emptying (aiding obesity management), and cardiorenal protection , .Emerging evidence indicates that GLP-1 receptor agonists modulate bone metabolism, resulting in enhanced bone mineral density, increased bone mass, and elevated expression of osteogenic markers . Preclinical and limited clinical evidence suggests GLP-1 enhances osteoblast function, suppresses osteoclast formation, and improves insulin sensitivity via modulation of bone metabolism-associated hormones. This mitigates the detrimental impact of hyperglycemia on bone mass. However, utilizing jaw bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (JBMMSCs) for osteoporosis therapy remains a nascent research field. Similarly, the potential of semaglutide—a potent GLP-1 receptor agonist—specifically for osteoporosis management has garnered limited research focus . According to our hypothesis, semaglutide may have an impact on JBMMSCs, making it easier to cure osteoporosis of the jawbone. A key player in the transforming growth factor-βsuperfamily, bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2) is essential for controlling osteoblast development – . Numerous downstream signalling pathways have been shown to be stimulated when BMP2 binds to its ligand/receptor. Numerous transcription factors that are indicators of osteoblast development are regulated by SMAD proteins (such as SMAD1/5/9) that are phosphorylated as a result of BMP2 signalling , .RUNX2 regulates multiple factors associated with bone formation, including alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity, collagen type I (COL-I), osteocalcin (OCN), and osteopontin (OPN) , . Bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2), a bioactive molecule essential for the osteogenic differentiation of jaw bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (JBMMSCs), may promote this process by targeting RUNX2. This growth factor effectively directs JBMMSC differentiation toward the osteoblast lineage, playing a vital role in jawbone development and maintenance . Semaglutide has shown a significant preventive effect as an anti-osteoporosis drug in osteoporosis induced by oophorectomy, which may be linked to the increased osteoblast formation due to Wnt signal activation. Semaglutide may suppress osteoblastic production of RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor κB ligand), thereby inhibiting bone resorption and mitigating bone deterioration .This work comprehensively investigated the mechanism of semaglutide in JBMMSCs for osteoporotic treatment through in vitro and in vivo studies, revealing its therapeutic potential and deciphering the molecular basis for its regulation of JBMMSC behavior.

## Materials and techniques

## Bioinformatics-based osteoporosis analysis
Gene expression profiles related to osteoporosis were retrieved from the GEO database (accessions GSE193800 and GSE56116). After batch effect correction with ComBat and Robust Multi-array Average (RMA) processing, differential expression analysis was performed using the limma package. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) meeting the threshold of *p*-value < 0.05 and |log < sub > 2</sub > FC| > 1 were identified for downstream analysis. The intersecting DEGs between datasets were visualized via VennDiagram, while functional enrichment analysis of GO terms and KEGG pathways was conducted using ClusterProfiler to uncover significant biological processes. Gene co-expression relationships were subsequently assessed with Spearman correlation.

## In vitro test

## The culture of JBMMSCs
Four-week-old male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (Animal Certification No. SCXK(Beijing)2019-0010) were humanely euthanized via CO₂inhalation as part of the experimental protocol. Following 10-minute disinfection in 75% v/v ethanol, rats were transferred to a sterile surgical suite. Mandibular soft tissues were dissected using aseptic instruments (tissue scissors/tweezers). The isolated mandibles underwent 5-minute immersion in PBS containing streptomycin-penicillin dual antibiotics. After molar and incisor extraction with sterile forceps, bone marrow cavities were flushed with α-MEM medium via 1 ml syringe. The effluent was collected, centrifuged (1000 rpm, 5 min), and supernatant discarded. The pellet was resuspended in 1 millilitre of α-MEM complete medium (α-MEM, 10% FBS, and 1% dual-antibiotics) to make a single cell suspension. It was then well mixed and put into 25 cm² culture flasks. Once a fresh 4 ml of α-MEM complete media was added, the flasks were placed in an incubator with 5% CO₂ and adjusted to 37 °C. Concurrently, bone forceps were used to chop the cleaned mandibles into tiny bone pieces (1 mm³), which were then bathed in trypsin and cultured for 20 min at 37 °C in a cell culture incubator.Aspiration of trypsin terminated digestion once the bone pieces aggregated into clumps. After three washes using α-MEM fluid, the fragments were transferred to 25 cm² culture flasks housing the pre-prepared JBMMSCs single cell suspension. Co-cultivation proceeded in a 37 °C, 5% CO₂ incubator, with the medium refreshed every 72 h. Flasks remained undisturbed throughout incubation to minimize cellular disruption. Upon reaching 90% confluence by day 12, cells underwent trypsinization and were passaged at a 1:2 ratio. First-generation bone fragments were retained for continued co-culture, involving medium changes every three days. After second-generation cells attained 90% confluence, they were trypsinized, passaged at 1:3, and the bone fragments were discarded. Cells spanning passages two to four were utilized in subsequent experiments.

## Identification of surface markers for JBMMSCs
Using the Mesenchymal Stem Cell (Rat) Surface Marker Assay Kit (RAXMX-09011, Oricell) per manufacturer protocols, P2 JBMMSCs were harvested, trypsinized, collected, centrifuged, and processed into suspension. Cells were resuspended in flow buffer (1×PBS, 0.1% BSA) to achieve a density of 3 × 10⁶ cells/mL. Aliquots (100 µL) were incubated for 30 min at 4 °C with primary antibodies targeting CD29, CD90, CD34, and CD45. Following two washes in flow buffer and centrifugation (5 min), the supernatant was removed. Every group was reconstituted using 100 µl of flow buffer, followed by the addition of a fluorescent secondary antibody, 30 min of incubation at 4 °C, two rinses with flow buffer, resuspension, and flow cytometry detection.

## Experiment on colony development
JBMMSCs were seeded in 6-well plates at 1 × 10³ cells/well and incubated for 24 h (37 °C, 5% CO₂) to facilitate attachment. Cultures were subsequently exposed to α-MEM complete media containing semaglutide at concentrations of 0, 10, 100, or 1000 nmol/L. During the 14-day culture period, medium was refreshed every 72 h. Following microscopic identification of cellular clusters (> 50 cells), colony formation efficiency was quantified via crystal violet staining.

## Assay for cell scratch
JBMMSCs were seeded in 6-well plates (3 × 10⁵ cells/well) and maintained at 37 °C/5% CO₂. Prior to seeding, three evenly spaced parallel lines were marked on each well bottom. At 80–90% confluence, scratches perpendicular to these lines were created using sterile 200 µL pipette tips. Following PBS washes to remove debris, wells received serum-free α-MEM containing semaglutide (0, 10, 100, or 1000 nmol/L). Scratch areas were imaged at 0 h and 48 h post-wounding. Gap closure was quantified using ImageJ.

## CCK-8 detection
P3 JBMMSCs (2500 cells/well) were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h (37 °C, 5% CO₂) to ensure adhesion. After attachment, medium was replaced with α-MEM complete containing semaglutide (0, 10, 100, or 1000 nmol/L). On days 1, 3, 5, and 7, 100 µL of α-MEM with 10% CCK-8 reagent was added per well. Following 2-hour incubation (37 °C), absorbance at 450 nm was recorded using a microplate reader. Each treatment group included five technical replicates.

## Treatment and activation of cell osteogenesis
P3 JBMMSCs (2 × 10⁴ cells/cm²) were seeded onto gelatin-coated 12-well plates. Each well received 2 mL α-MEM complete medium before incubation at 37 °C/5% CO₂. At 70% confluence, cultures were switched to rat bone marrow-derived osteogenic induction medium supplemented with semaglutide (0, 10, 100, or 1000 nmol/L).In the osteogenic induction medium for bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, dexamethasone (100 nM) was added to each 1 mL of the medium. This promoted osteogenesis and inhibited adipogenesis. When the stem cells reached 80–90% fusion, they were digested. After that, two millilitres of full stem cell culture media were put to each well of a six-well plate containing around three by ten cells apiece. At 37 °C, the plates were incubated with 5% CO2. After 24 h, spent medium was aspirated and replaced with 2 mL/well of osteogenic induction medium. This medium was refreshed every 72 h throughout the 14-day period until calcified nodule formation. Nodules were subsequently stained with carmine red. Group 1 received 10 µmol/L CREB inhibitor KG-501 (MCE), Group 2 was treated with 20 µmol/L YAP activator PY-60 (MCE), and Group 3 received 10 µmol/L STAT3 activator Colivenlin (MCE). JBMMSCs were exposed to 100 nmol/L semaglutide (optimal concentration) for 48 h prior to harvest.

## Treatment and induction of cell lipid formation
P3-generation JBMMSCs were seeded at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells/cm² in gelatin-coated 12-well plates. Each well was added with 2 mL of α-MEM complete medium, and the cells were cultured in a 37 °C, 5% CO₂ incubator. The medium was changed every 3 days. Once the cells reached approximately 100% confluence, the medium was replaced with α-MEM containing different concentrations of somatostatin (0, 10, 100, 1000 nmol/L) to induce lipid formation and differentiation. The composition of the lipidation induction medium A used was: DMEM + 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), dual antibiotics, glutamine, and induction agents (insulin, IBMX, dexamethasone, indomethacin).The components of adipogenesis induction fluid B were: DMEM + 10% FBS, double antibiotics, glutamine, and inducer (insulin). Liquid A was aspirated after 48 h of introduction, and liquid B was there after introduced gradually.After continuing the culture for 24 h, remove liquid B and replace it with liquid A containing different concentrations of somatostatin (0, 10, 100, 1000 nmol/L). After alternating between liquids A and B for 3–5 cycles, switch to liquid B for maintenance culture for 8 days, replacing the culture medium every 2 days. After induction, perform Oil Red O staining.

## In vivo tests

## Modelling of osteoporosis
Twelve-week-old male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (SPF grade, weighing 300 ± 15 g; Animal Qualification Certificate No. SCXK (Beijing) 2019-0010, sourced from Beijing) underwent a one-week acclimation period. After a 24-hour fast (food and water withheld), rats were anesthetized and positioned ventrally on the surgical table. Skin preparation included shaving along the dorsal midline, over the sacral region approximately 1 cm from the spine, and in the axillary region beneath the last rib lateral to the vertebral column. Following disinfection with povidone-iodine, bilateral longitudinal incisions (~ 1 cm in length) were made 2 cm lateral to the spinal midline on each side. These incisions were deepened sequentially through the skin, subcutaneous fascia, and muscle layers to access the peritoneal cavity. The bilateral ovaries were carefully exteriorized after gently displacing the surrounding adipose tissue. Hemostasis was achieved using 6 − 0 absorbable suture ligatures. The abdominal cavity was then closed. Muscle and skin layers were reapproximated with 3 − 0 silk sutures applied in layers. Rats assigned to the sham-operated group underwent an identical surgical procedure, including entry into the abdominal cavity via the described incisions, but only the periovarian adipose tissue was gently manipulated and exteriorized; the ovaries themselves were not excised. Following successful modelling, the surgical therapy included an intraperitoneal penicillin injection and three months of consistent upbringing. Subsequently, the rats were randomized into four experimental cohorts: sham-operated group, model group, JBMMSCs-treated group, and JBMMSCs + semaglutide-treated group. Animals in the sham-operated and model groups received weekly intravenous (tail vein) injections of 0.5 ml saline solution for eight weeks. Conversely, rats assigned to the JBMMSCs-treated group were administered 0.5 ml of JBMMSCs suspension (1.8 × 10⁶ cells/ml) weekly via tail vein injection over the same duration. Rats in the JBMMSCs + semaglutide-treated group received weekly injections of 0.5 ml JBMMSCs suspension (1.8 × 10⁶ cells/ml) that had been pre-treated with 100 nmol/L semaglutide. Following the eight-week intervention period, all rats were euthanized by CO₂ asphyxiation, after which mandibular tissues were harvested for analysis. This study was approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Hebei Medical University (IACUC-Hebmu-2025047), all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations and in accordance with the relevant ARRIVE guidelines 2.0 and the 3Rs principles.
Modeling of JBMMSCs for osteoporosis treatment under the action of semaglutide.
The following groups were created from the P2-generation Juvenile Baillinger-Muller Schwann (JBMMSCs): control group, GLP-1RA group, GLP-1RA + KG-501 group, GLP-1RA + KG-501 + BRD4-OE group, GLP-1RA + KG-501 + BRD4-OE + OCT4-KD group, and GLP-1RA + KG-501 + BRD4-OE + OCT4-KD + BMP2-OE group.The GLP-1RA group was cultivated in basal medium supplemented with 100 nM semaglutide, while basal medium was used to culture the control group. In the GLP-1RA + KG-501 group, in addition to Semaglutide (100 nM) and KG-501 (10µM), BRD4 overexpression lentivirus LV-BRD4 was added. To prepare the BRD4 overexpression lentivirus LV-BRD4, P2-generation JBMMSCs were inoculated onto 6-well plates and cultured in α-MEM + 10% FBS until 50% confluence. The LV-BRD4 viral suspension was adjusted to a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 20 using serum-free Opti-MEM medium, with Polybrene supplemented at a final concentration of 8 µg/mL. Following a 24-hour incubation period at 37 °C, the culture medium was replaced with complete α-MEM. To select stably transduced cells, puromycin (5 µg/mL) was added to the cultures 72 h post-infection and maintained for 5–7 days. In the GLP-1RA + KG-501 + BRD4-OE + OCT4-KD group, in addition to adding Semaglutide (100 nM), KG-501 (10µM), and BRD4 overexpressing lentivirus LV-BRD4 (MOI = 20, infection for 72 h, followed by puromycin screening), OCT4 siRNA (Santa Cruz, sc-5279, 50 nM for Lipofectamine 3000 transfection) and BMP2 overexpressing plasmid (pcDNA3.1-BMP2, 2 µg per well, transfection for 48 h) were also added.OCT4 siRNA was prepared by transfecting cells with 2 µg of OCT4 siRNA-lentiviral vector (Santa Cruz, USA) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA).Subsequently, cells were maintained in α-MEM supplemented with 10% FBS. The LV-containing supernatant was harvested 48 h later and filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane.For JBMMSCs, cells were cultured in α-MEM, then mixed with 2 × 10^10 cells, 450 µl of lentivirus-containing supernatant, and 4 µg/ml polyglutamine (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). The mixture was inoculated onto six-well plates and replaced with fresh α-MEM after 30 min. Two days later, the transfected cells were inoculated onto 10 cm culture dishes with 4 µg/ml puromycin. Stable-transfected cells were finally harvested. For BMP2 overexpression plasmid experiments, 2 µg pcDNA3.1-BMP2 plasmid was gently mixed with 125 µL serum-free Opti-MEM medium.Following the addition of 4 µL Lipofectamine 3000 and 125 µL Opti-MEM to the diluted plasmid mixture and a subsequent 5-minute incubation, the resulting complex was combined with the diluted plasmid for a 15-hour incubation period.Following aspiration of the previous culture medium, the cells were given a single PBS wash before the complex and 750 µL of serum-free Opti-MEM were added for a 48-hour cultivation period. Finally, the collected cells were analyzed by Western blotting.

## Micro-CT
4% paraformaldehyde was used to preserve the mandibular tissues of rats for a whole day. Rat mandibular bones from the Sham, Model, JBMMSCs, and JBMMSCs + Semaglutide groups underwent initial preservation in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, followed by storage in 70% ethanol. Prior to micro-CT scanning (GE Healthcare, Little Chalfont), samples were rinsed with PBS to eliminate potential interference from residual ethanol. Bone microarchitecture was evaluated using this system. To verify the success of the osteoporosis model, the following parameters were quantified: bone volume per tissue volume (BV/TV), bone surface to bone volume ratio (BS/BV), trabecular number (Tb.N), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), and trabecular separation (Tb.Sp).

## HE staining
Paraffin-embedded sections were processed through sequential steps: deparaffinization, hematoxylin staining with differentiation for blue nuclear visualization, ethanol gradient dehydration, xylene clearance, eosin counterstaining, and neutral gum mounting. Light microscopic evaluation documented trabecular architecture and spatial organization. Quantitative analysis of trabecular pathology and regeneration was performed on three randomly chosen visual fields per histological section using ImageJ.

## Western blot analysis
Following collection of JBMMSCs, cells were lysed in RIPA buffer. The resulting lysates underwent centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 10 min to isolate soluble proteins from the supernatant. Protein concentration in the supernatants was quantified using the BCA method. Subsequently, aliquots containing 20 µg of protein per sample were denatured by boiling for 10 min and resolved via SDS-PAGE electrophoresis. Separated proteins were then electrophoretically transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked for 1 h at 37 °C prior to incubation with specific primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Primary antibodies included: GLP-1R (Thermo Fisher, 1:1000);p-CREB (Abcam, 1:2000);YAP (Abcam, 1:2000);BRD4 (Abcam, 1:1000);OCT4 (Abcam, 1:1000);SOX2 (Thermo Fisher, 1:500);c-Myc (Abclonal, 1:2000);Cyclin D1 (Abcam, 1:500);p-STAT3 (Thermo Fisher, 1:1000);TET3 (Abclonal, 1:500);SHP2 (Thermo Fisher, 1:1000);DLX5 (Thermo Fisher, 1:1000);PPARγ (Affinity, 1:1000);C/EBPα (Abclonal, 1:1000);BMP2 (Abcam, 1:1000);GAPDH (Thermo Fisher, 1:2000). After primary antibody incubation and PBS washes, membranes were probed with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Secondary antibodies used were sheep anti-mouse (Abclonal, 1:1000) and sheep anti-rabbit (Abcam, 1:2000). Following additional washes, protein bands were detected using an ECL substrate and visualized with a gel imaging system. Band intensity (grey value) was quantified using ImageJ software, with target protein expression normalized to the corresponding GAPDH signal.

## Immunofluorescence detection
JBMMSCs were seeded onto cell crawls in 12-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells per well. Upon reaching 50–60% confluence, the culture medium was removed and cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min at room temperature. Subsequent permeabilization was performed using Triton X-100, followed by blocking with 10% BSA at 37 °C for 1 h. Cells were then incubated overnight (16–18 h) at 4 °C in the dark with the following primary antibodies: SOX2 (Abcam, 1:200);KLF4 (Abcam, 1:500);GLP-1R (Thermo Fisher, PA5-97112, 1:200).After primary antibody incubation, cells underwent three washes (10 min each) with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 (PBST). Following washing, samples were incubated with a fluorescent goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature under dark conditions.Following five minutes of room temperature incubation with DAPI (1:1000, Sigma D9542) and three PBS washes, nuclear re-staining was carried out.For mounting and storage, anti-fluorescence quencher mounting medium (Beyotime, P0126) was used to mount the slides, which were stored in the dark at 4℃ for imaging within 24 h. Additionally, a fluorescent microscope was used to see and capture the data. ImageJ software was used to quantify the immunoflurescence staining. The pictures were first opened and converted to greyscale or 8-bit format. To maximise signal display, the brightness and contrast were changed, and denoising was used as needed. Next, a threshold was set to segment the fluorescence signal and a binary image was generated. The ‘Analyze Particles’ function was used to detect and measure the fluorescence area, and parameters such as area and intensity were recorded. For fluorescence intensity analysis, selected areas on the original image were measured directly. Finally, the processed images were saved and the measurement data were exported.

## Alizarin red S staining
Osteogenic induction was conducted over a 14-day period. Following the formation of mineralized nodules on the well plate surfaces, samples were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min at room temperature. Mineralization was visualized by staining with 2% Alizarin Red S solution (Beyotime, C0148S) for 5–10 min at RT. Stained specimens were documented using brightfield microscopy.For quantitative analysis, mineral-bound dye was solubilized by adding 10% cetylpyridinium chloride with 30-minute incubation at RT. Subsequently, 100 µL aliquots from each well were transferred to a 96-well plate. Absorbance at 562 nm was measured in quintuplicate replicates per experimental group using a microplate reader.

## ALP staining
After 7 days of osteogenic induction, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity was assessed using the BCIP/NBT detection kit (Beyotime, C3250S). According to manufacturer’s protocol, 800 µL of BCIP/NBT working solution was dispensed into each well. Following 2-hour incubation at 37 °C, samples underwent sequential PBS rinsing, air-drying, and brightfield microscopic documentation.

## Oil red O staining
Following the conclusion of lipid-induced differentiation, the cells underwent two PBS rinses before being fixed for ten minutes at room temperature using 4% paraformaldehyde. Following two PBS washes, the cells were dehydrated for ten minutes using 60% isopropanol before being stained with newly made Oil Red O staining solution. The samples were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C before being washed two to five times with PBS until no staining residue remained. The cell nuclei were then re-stained for one minute using Mayer’s haematoxylin staining solution. After a one-minute wash with tap water, the cells were given one last PBS wash. Under a microscope, the samples were examined and photographed.

## Statistical analysis
Data appear as mean ± SEM. GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.6) facilitated data analysis and figure generation. Comparisons between two groups employed unpaired t-tests. For analyses involving more than two groups, a one-way ANOVA was conducted, with Tukey’s test applied for subsequent post hoc comparisons. Statistical significance was defined by p-values below 0.05. All cellular experiments included triplicate repeats. Animal studies utilized groups of six rats each.

## Results

## Bioinformatics analysis of osteoporosis
Analysis of the GSE193800 dataset via the limma package identified 2104 highly differentially expressed genes (DEGs), comprising 738 upregulated and 1366 downregulated genes. Similarly, processing the GSE56116 dataset yielded 391 significant DEGs (197 upregulated, 194 downregulated; Fig.  A-C). Venn diagram analysis revealed 74 overlapping DEGs common to both datasets (Fig.  D). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis underscored the relevance of these DEGs to osteoporosis, showing significant enrichment in biological processes including cell membrane raft and microdomain organization. These membrane structures serve as vital hubs for signal transduction, regulating diverse intracellular pathways. Molecular function enrichment further implicated signalling, with DEGs overrepresented in phosphotyrosine residue binding, cytokine receptor binding, and protein tyrosine kinase activity (Fig.  E). KEGG pathway analysis indicated DEG involvement in osteoclast differentiation, JAK-STAT signalling, and PI3K-Akt signalling. The PI3K-Akt pathway is critical for cell survival, proliferation, and metabolism, while JAK-STAT signalling strongly influences immune responses and cell proliferation. The enrichment of DEGs in osteoclast differentiation—a pivotal process in osteoporosis pathogenesis—suggests their potential impact on bone density and quality through modulating osteoclast formation and activity (Fig.  F, G). Spearman correlation analysis delineated a complex interaction network involving STAT3, TET3, YAP, and SHP2 (PTPN11). Positive correlations observed between STAT3-TET3 and STAT3-YAP imply potential functional cooperation within shared signalling pathways governing cell division and proliferation (Fig.  H, I). A positive association between YAP and CDK9 further suggested YAP’s possible role in cytoskeletal remodelling and cell migration (Fig.  J). Additionally, the positive correlation between TET3 and SHP2 (PTPN11) may indicate involvement in intracellular signalling modulation, given SHP2’s function as a protein tyrosine phosphatase essential for multiple signalling cascades (Fig.  K).

 

## Semaglutide maintains the stemness of JBMMSCs via the CREB/YAP/BRD4 axis: assays for Migration, cell Proliferation, colony Formation, and CCK-8
To identify the optimal semaglutide concentration for augmenting JBMMSC function, we assessed its impact across varying doses on cellular activity, colony formation, and migration. Notably, 10 nmol/L semaglutide significantly boosted colony formation and cellular activity while enhancing migratory capacity. Elevating the concentration to 100 nmol/L induced a more pronounced enhancement across all three parameters. In contrast, at 1000 nmol/L, cellular activity, colony formation, and migratory capacity exhibited a reduction compared to the 100 nmol/L treatment group(Fig.  A-C).

 

Following seven days of osteogenic induction in media supplemented with varying semaglutide concentrations (0, 10, 100, or 1000 nmol/L), ALP staining was conducted. JBMMSCs exhibited a progressive expansion and intensification of blue-purple staining correlating with increasing semaglutide doses, indicative of elevated alkaline phosphatase activity. Notably, ALP activity at both 100 nmol/L and 1000 nmol/L concentrations was markedly higher than levels observed at 0 nmol/L and 10 nmol/L(Fig.  D).
JBMMSCs were osteogenically induced for 14 days with semaglutide (0–1000 nmol/L) prior to Alizarin Red staining. Calcium nodule formation demonstrated progressive intensification correlating with semaglutide concentration elevation. At 100 nmol/L and 1000 nmol/L, nodules not only increased significantly in size and number but also coalesced into interconnected clusters. Given the comparable mineralization efficacy between these concentrations (P > 0.05), 100 nmol/L was identified as the most potent osteoinductive dose for subsequent JBMMSC studies(Fig.  E).
JBMMSCs were categorized into the following experimental groups to explore the molecular mechanism by which semaglutide preserves stemness: Normal, Osteogenic Induction (OI), Semaglutide + OI (S + OI), S + OI + CREB Inhibitor (S + OI + CI), S + OI + CI + YAP Activator (S + OI + CI + YA), and S + OI + CI + STAT3 Activator (S + OI + CI + SA).Rescue experiments utilized Western blotting to assess p-CREB, nuclear YAP, BRD4, stemness markers (OCT4, SOX2), and proliferation markers (c-Myc, Cyclin D1). Compared to the Normal group, OI significantly elevated expression of p-CREB, YAP, BRD4, OCT4, SOX2, c-Myc, and Cyclin D1. The S + OI group demonstrated further increases in Cyclin D1 levels, alongside elevated p-CREB, YAP, BRD4, OCT4, SOX2, and c-Myc relative to the OI group. Conversely, adding the CREB inhibitor (S + OI + CI group) suppressed expression of p-CREB, YAP, BRD4, OCT4, SOX2, c-Myc, and Cyclin D1 compared to S + OI. While the S + OI + CI + YA and S + OI + CI + SA groups showed no significant change in p-CREB expression versus the S + OI + CI group, both exhibited higher levels of YAP, BRD4, OCT4, SOX2, c-Myc, and Cyclin D1.Immunofluorescence analysis of fixed cells evaluated stemness proteins SOX2 and KLF4. Fluorescence intensity for both proteins was higher in the OI group than Normal, and further enhanced in the S + OI group compared to OI. The S + OI + CI group displayed reduced SOX2 and KLF4 fluorescence compared to S + OI. Importantly, both the S + OI + CI + YA and S + OI + CI + SA groups exhibited increased SOX2 and KLF4 fluorescence intensity relative to the S + OI + CI group.Collectively, these findings indicate that semaglutide maintains JBMMSC stemness and proliferative capacity via the CREB/YAP/BRD4 axis.(Fig.  F, G).

## Semaglutide encourages the osteogenic differentiation of JBMMSCs by modifying the expression of SMAD1/5 andβ-Catenin proteins via the osteogenic induction/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 axis
JBMMSCs were divided into several experimental cohorts: Normal, Osteogenic Induction, and groups combining Semaglutide with Osteogenic Induction, with or without the addition of a CREB Inhibitor. Furthermore, the CREB Inhibitor-containing group was supplemented with either a YAP Activator or a STAT3 Activator. Western blot analysis evaluated protein expression for key signaling molecules (p-STAT3, TET3, SHP2, p-SMAD1/5, β-Catenin) and osteogenic markers (RUNX2, DLX5). Osteogenic differentiation status was additionally quantified using Alizarin Red S staining and ALP activity assays.Compared to the Normal group, cells undergoing Osteogenic Induction exhibited significantly elevated expression of p-STAT3, TET3, SHP2, p-SMAD1/5, β-Catenin, RUNX2, and DLX5, alongside enhanced mineralization and ALP activity, indicating successful differentiation. The addition of Semaglutide to the Osteogenic Induction regimen further augmented both the expression levels of these proteins and the osteogenic differentiation markers. In contrast, concurrent administration of the CREB Inhibitor with Semaglutide and Osteogenic Induction suppressed osteogenic differentiation and diminished the expression of the aforementioned signaling proteins and markers. However, this inhibitory effect was counteracted in groups where either the YAP Activator or the STAT3 Activator was co-administered with the CREB Inhibitor, Semaglutide, and Osteogenic Induction. These groups demonstrated restored and significantly higher levels of osteogenic differentiation and associated protein expression. Collectively, these data suggest that Semaglutide promotes JBMMSC osteogenic differentiation. This effect appears to be mediated through modulation of β-Catenin and SMAD1/5 signaling, potentially acting via an osteogenic induction pathway involving STAT3, TET3, and SHP2(Fig.  A-C).

 

## Semaglutide controls the adipogenic differentiation of JBMMSCs via the osteogenic induction/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 axis
To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying semaglutide’s effect on JBMMSC adipogenic differentiation, cells were allocated to distinct experimental conditions: Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction, Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction + CREB Inhibitor, and the latter group further supplemented with either a YAP Activator or a STAT3 Activator. Adipogenic differentiation was quantified via Oil Red O staining to assess intracellular lipid accumulation, while Western blotting measured expression levels of key adipogenic markers PPARγ and C/EBPα.Compared to undifferentiated Normal cells, the Adipogenic Induction group exhibited robust adipogenesis, characterized by significantly elevated PPARγ and C/EBPα protein expression and abundant intracellular lipid droplets. However, the addition of semaglutide to the adipogenic induction medium (Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction group) markedly attenuated this process, evidenced by reduced lipid droplet formation and lower levels of PPARγ and C/EBPα expression relative to the Adipogenic Induction group alone.Intriguingly, co-treatment with the CREB Inhibitor alongside semaglutide and adipogenic induction (Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction + CREB Inhibitor group) partially reversed semaglutide’s inhibitory effect. This group displayed increased lipid accumulation and higher PPARγ/C/EBPα protein expression compared to the group receiving only semaglutide and adipogenic induction.Furthermore, the inhibitory effect of semaglutide combined with CREB inhibition was counteracted by activating either YAP or STAT3. Both the groups co-treated with the YAP Activator (Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction + CREB Inhibitor + YAP Activator) or the STAT3 Activator (Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction + CREB Inhibitor + STAT3 Activator) demonstrated significantly enhanced lipid droplet content and elevated PPARγ/C/EBPα expression levels relative to the Semaglutide + Adipogenic Induction + CREB Inhibitor group. Collectively, these observations indicate that semaglutide suppresses JBMMSC adipogenic differentiation by downregulating PPARγ and C/EBPα expression. This inhibitory action appears to involve modulation of the STAT3/TET3/SHP2 signaling axis, as evidenced by the ability of YAP or STAT3 activation to rescue the phenotype (Fig.  A, B).

 

## JBMMSCs reduce the progression of osteoporosis in rats after semaglutide activity, according to in vivo experiments
JBMMSCs were isolated from rat mandibles and characterized by flow cytometry. Results confirmed their phenotypic characteristics align with bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (Fig.  A). Given semaglutide’s mechanism as a GLP-1 receptor agonist, we assessed GLP-1R expression in JBMMSCs via Western blot and immunofluorescence. Both assays demonstrated significant GLP-1R protein levels, indicating JBMMSCs as potential cellular targets for semaglutide (Fig.  B, C). Micro-CT quantitative analysis revealed that compared to the sham-operated group, the model group exhibited significantly reduced BV/TV, Tb.N, and Tb.Th, along with significantly increased BS/BV and Tb.Sp, confirming successful osteoporosis induction. JBMMSC treatment partially reversed these changes, increasing BV/TV, Tb.N, and Tb.Th while decreasing BS/BV and Tb.Sp. Quantitative analysis confirmed that semaglutide, through dual mechanisms activating both the CREB/YAP/BRD4 pathway and the BMP2/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 pathway, not only promotes osteoblast differentiation but also significantly improves bone microstructural integrity, providing stronger imaging evidence for clinical translation (Fig.  D). HE staining was used to assess the extent of trabecular destruction in the rats’ mandibular bone.Comparative micro-CT analysis revealed significantly elevated trabecular damage in the Model group relative to Sham controls. In contrast, JBMMSC transplantation substantially attenuated trabecular deterioration. Notably, rats receiving semaglutide-preconditioned JBMMSCs exhibited the most pronounced microarchitectural restoration, with trabecular damage indices falling below those observed in the standard JBMMSC treatment group (Fig.  E). Alizarin red staining of rat mandibles was performed to evaluate terminal calcific nodule growth. Compared to the sham group, calcium salt deposition was significantly reduced in the Model group. Compared to the Model group, calcium salt deposition was significantly elevated in both the JBMMSCs group and the JBMMSCs-Semaglutide group. Furthermore, the JBMMSCs-Semaglutide group demonstrated superior efficacy compared to the JBMMSCs group (Fig.  F). Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity in bone tissue was evaluated via ALP staining. Results showed significantly reduced ALP activity in the Model group compared to the sham group. Compared to the Model group, ALP activity was significantly elevated in both the JBMMSCs group and the JBMMSCs-Semaglutide group. Furthermore, ALP activity was higher in the JBMMSCs-Semaglutide group than in the JBMMSCs group (Fig.  G).

## Discussion
Osteoporosis represents a widespread skeletal pathology characterized by compromised bone biomechanical competence and elevated fracture susceptibility, predominantly affecting aged populations and postmenopausal women. This condition manifests through two cardinal features: reduced bone mineral density (BMD) and deteriorated microarchitectural integrity. The pathophysiological cascade involves menopause-triggered ovarian hypofunction and subsequent estrogen deficiency, which directly suppresses osteoblast activity. This impairment in bone formation culminates in diminished bone mass and structural weakness, ultimately driving osteoporotic pathogenesis This investigation employed ovariectomized (OVX) rats—a validated model recapitulating postmenopausal estrogen depletion—to explore novel osteoporosis therapeutics. Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonist, exerts pharmacological effects through target cell surface engagement. Although predominantly localized in pancreatic tissue, GLP-1R (a G-protein-coupled receptor subclass) demonstrates extra-pancreatic expression in neural, cardiovascular, renal, gastrointestinal, and vascular compartments .Furthermore, it is known that adipose-derived stem cells, osteoblasts, and osteoclasts all have GLP-1R – .JBMMSCs, identified as pluripotent stem cells capable of differentiating into bone and adipose tissue, present an uncertain role in osteoporosis development. To explore their therapeutic potential, we initially assessed GLP-1R surface expression on these cells via Western blot analysis, revealing their expression of this receptor. This finding suggests JBMMSCs may serve as potential cellular targets for semaglutide. Subsequent experiments involved injecting OVX rat models with semaglutide-treated JBMMSCs. Our results demonstrated that this intervention significantly attenuated osteoporosis progression, highlighting semaglutide’s therapeutic promise. Furthermore, semaglutide treatment enhanced JBMMSC proliferation by modulating the cell cycle. It also elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and calcium salt deposition, maintained cellular dryness through the CREB/YAP/BRD4 axis, upregulated key osteogenic markers (OCN and RUNX2), and balanced osteogenic and lipogenic differentiation via the BMP2/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 pathway. Collectively, these mechanisms enhanced the osteogenic efficacy of JBMMSCs in combating osteoporosis .
To elucidate the precise molecular mechanisms underlying JBMMSCs’ therapeutic effects on osteoporosis under semaglutide treatment, we conducted in vitro investigations. As established, semaglutide upregulates GLP-1R protein expression on the cell surface, mediating its extracellular actions. Intracellularly, GLP-1R activation triggers the release of the second messenger cAMP, amplifying signaling cascades that modulate diverse physiological processes .cAMP-dependent activation of protein kinase A (PKA) induces phosphorylation of the transcription factor CREB , .As a pivotal transcription factor, CREB (cAMP Response Element Binding Protein) undergoes activation via phosphorylation, subsequently enhancing the expression of YES-associated protein (YAP) .YAP is a transcriptional co-activator and a major effector of the Hippo signaling pathway. Nuclear translocation of de-phosphorylated YAP activates downstream signaling by associating with transcriptional co-activators, whereas phosphorylated YAP undergoes cytoplasmic retention, functionally suppressing its nuclear activity .In order to create a positive feedback loop and encourage more signalling pathway activation, YAP may further stimulate CREB expression. The CDK family includes CDK9. P-TEFb primarily facilitates transcription elongation rather than direct cell cycle regulation. This heterodimeric complex—comprising CDK9 bound to cyclin T subunits (T1/T2)—activates RNA polymerase II (Pol II) by phosphorylating its C-terminal domain (CTD), thereby driving transcriptional elongation .BRD4, a member of the bromodomain and extra-terminal (BET) protein family, recognizes acetylated histones via its bromodomains and localizes to gene regulatory elements (enhancers/promoters), thereby orchestrating transcriptional control , . Within JBMMSC nuclei, activated YAP associates with the BRD4-CDK9 transcriptional module, driving expression of stemness genes including OCT4, SOX2, and KLF4 – .The operational hallmark of stem cells—termed stemness—encompasses sustained self-renewal competency and lineage multipotency, setting them apart from committed somatic cells . Preservation of stemness enhances JBMMSC proliferation and maintains multipotency, enabling osteoblast differentiation for potential osteoporosis therapy. Accordingly, osteogenic induction was applied to JBMMSCs. Rescue experiments employed equivalent doses of CREB inhibitor (KG-501), YAP activator (PY-60), and semaglutide. Protein expression (p-CREB, YAP, BRD4, stemness markers OCT4/SOX2, proliferation markers c-Myc/CyclinD1) was analyzed by Western blot, CCK-8, and colony formation assays, while SOX2/KLF4 localization was assessed via immunofluorescence.Osteogenically induced JBMMSCs exhibited significantly elevated p-CREB, YAP, BRD4, OCT4, SOX2, c-Myc, CyclinD1 levels and proliferative capacity versus non-induced controls. In semaglutide-treated (100 nM) osteogenic groups: KG-501 blocked p-CREB signaling; PY-60 directly activated YAP downstream of CREB; Colivelin tested STAT3-YAP regulation. These interventions confirmed CREB’s pivotal role, YAP’s downstream positioning, and STAT3 cross-talk: KG-501 markedly suppressed p-CREB, nuclear YAP, BRD4, and OCT4/SOX2 expression, demonstrating semaglutide’s CREB-dependent signaling initiation. Immunofluorescence also showed weakened SOX2/KLF4 fluorescence intensity, further supporting CREB’s regulation of stemness. PY-60 partially reversed the inhibitory effect of the CREB inhibitor on stemness, restoring BRD4, OCT4, and SOX2 expression. This indicated that YAP was a key effector molecule downstream of CREB. However, YAP activation failed to fully restore p-CREB levels, suggesting that CREB might also regulate stemness independently through other pathways (e.g., non-canonical Hippo signaling). Colivelin similarly rescued YAP and stemness protein expression, consistent with the previously reported STAT3-YAP positive feedback loop. According to these findings, semaglutide may indirectly increase YAP activity by activating STAT3, resulting in an enlarged CREB-STAT3-YAP signaling network. Additionally, after semaglutide treatment and the administration of the proper agonists and inhibitors, JBMMSCs’ stemness potential was substantially enhanced. The stemness ability reversal showed that semaglutide did, in fact, maintain JBMMSCs’ stemness ability via the CREB/YAP/BRD4 axis.The specific interactions between CREB/YAP/BRD4 and the BMP2/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 pathway were clearly demonstrated in our experiments, revealing CREB’s dual regulatory role in both maintaining stemness through OCT4 and promoting osteogenesis via BMP2/RUNX2. When CREB was pharmacologically inhibited, all therapeutic effects of semaglutide were completely abolished, definitively establishing CREB as the crucial downstream mediator of semaglutide’s action. This inhibition study further confirmed YAP’s essential function in stemness maintenance, as evidenced by the significant reduction in OCT4 expression upon CREB blockade, which aligned perfectly with the previously characterized YAP-BRD4-OCT4 regulatory axis. Intriguingly, STAT3 exhibited a distinct osteogenic-specific effect - its activation selectively enhanced BMP2 and RUNX2 expression while leaving OCT4 levels unaffected. These findings suggested that STAT3 likely operated through three complementary mechanisms: first, by directly activating the canonical BMP/SMAD signaling pathway; second, through epigenetic regulation mediated by the TET3/SHP2 axis; and third, by synergistically amplifying the osteogenic transcriptional program in cooperation with β-catenin.
Since treating osteoporosis requires more than only preserving the stemness ability of JBMMSC cells, we looked into other molecular pathways by which JBMMSCs cure osteoporosis using semaglutide. Literature analysis reveals that bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP2) - a multifunctional TGF-β superfamily ligand - activates intracellular signaling cascades in JBMMSCs through specific membrane-bound receptors . Osteogenic receptor activation upregulates STAT3 expression. This transcription factor serves as a pivotal signaling mediator and gene expression regulator in cellular pathways . Even while STAT3 may boost TET3 protein expression, it may also boost YAP protein expression, which can raise CREB expression and further stimulate JBMMSC stemness , . Modulating TET3 (a key DNA demethylase) enhances downstream gene expression by inducing promoter hypomethylation .According to published research, it is possible to increase the expression level of SHP2 by targeting high TET3 expression , . The PTPN11 gene encodes SHP2, a cytoplasmic protein tyrosine phosphatase essential for signal transduction. This phosphatase enhances the expression levels of SMAD1/5/8 and β-Catenin , . By increasing the expression of genes associated with osteogenesis, SMAD1/5/8, a member of the SMAD family, may operate as a transcription factor to support osteogenic differentiation.Within the nucleus, β-Catenin can associate with T-cell-specific transcription factor (TCF) and lymphoid enhancer-binding factor 1 (LEF). This complex facilitates osteoblast differentiation and proliferation through the transcriptional activation of downstream target genes . Building on the osteogenic induction-mediated STAT3/TET3/SHP2 signaling axis, we postulated that semaglutide enhances osteogenic differentiation in JBMMSCs through modulation of β-Catenin and SMAD1/5 protein expression. To validate this hypothesis, JBMMSC differentiation potential was assessed via Western blotting (quantifying p-STAT3, TET3, SHP2, p-SMAD1/5, β-Catenin, RUNX2, and DLX5) and alizarin red S mineralization assays.
Critically, during JBMMSC differentiation, osteogenic commitment supports osteoporosis therapy, whereas adipogenesis compromises therapeutic efficacy. Consequently, we quantified adipogenic markers PPARγ and C/EBPα via immunoblotting, with adipogenic potential further evaluated through Oil Red O lipid staining. Post-induction, JBMMSCs exhibited elevated PPARγ/C/EBPα expression and increased lipid droplet accumulation versus untreated controls. Semaglutide treatment markedly suppressed adipogenic capacity. These data demonstrate that semaglutide impedes adipogenesis by modulating PPARγ/C/EBPα expression through the osteogenic induction/STAT3/TET3/SHP2 axis, thereby enhancing osteoporosis treatment outcomes. The experimental results demonstrated the pivotal role of STAT3: KG-501 significantly reduced the expression of p-STAT3, TET3, SHP2, and osteogenic markers (RUNX2/DLX5), while Colivelin effectively reversed this suppression, showing better recovery compared to PY-60. These findings suggested that STAT3 served as a critical node connecting CREB to the downstream TET3/SHP2 pathways, likely through direct phosphorylation of TET3 or upregulation of SHP2 (PTPN11) expression, thereby activating the SMAD1/5 and β-Catenin signaling cascades. Furthermore, the results highlighted the epigenetic regulation mediated by TET3-SHP2: TET3 enhanced SHP2 promoter activity via demethylation, and rescue experiments confirmed that TET3 expression fluctuated in synchrony with SHP2 levels. SHP2, in turn, potentiated SMAD1/5 (downstream of BMP2) and β-Catenin (downstream of WNT), synergistically promoting osteogenic differentiation. Additionally, the study revealed the mechanism behind adipogenesis inhibition: semaglutide suppressed adipogenic markers PPARγ/C/EBPα through the same STAT3/TET3/SHP2 axis. Rescue experiments showed that STAT3 activators markedly reduced lipid droplet accumulation, indicating that STAT3 likely inhibited PPARγ transcription or facilitated its degradation, further underscoring its dual role in enhancing osteogenesis while suppressing adipogenesis.
Ultimately, by preserving JBMMSC stemness properties, enhancing osteogenic commitment, and suppressing adipogenic maturation, we established that semaglutide halts osteoporosis progression.Our findings show for the first time how semaglutide works in tandem with differentiation modulation and dry maintenance to decrease osteoporosis. In contrast to earlier research, we also investigated the precise mechanism by which semaglutide impacts JBMMSCs to attenuate osteoporosis.Specifically, CREB-YAP-BRD4 maintains the stem cell’s proliferative potential, while STAT3-TET3-SHP2 promotes osteogenesis and inhibits lipogenesis. Rescue experiments have confirmed the interaction between these two axes, such as STAT3 regulating YAP, but the specific mechanisms of this interaction require further investigation.The aforementioned study findings provide a fresh approach and line of inquiry for the therapeutic management of osteoporosis. It also highlights the medical value of semaglutide, which offers a new approach for patients with diabetes and osteoporosis through its dual effects (blood sugar reduction and bone protection). This suggests that the combination of JBMMSCs with drug injections might be more effective than traditional anti-osteoclast therapy (such as bisphosphonates), as it targets both enhanced bone formation and reduced fat deposition.
