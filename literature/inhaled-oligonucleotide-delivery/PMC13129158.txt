## Abstract

## Background
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is resistant to therapy and carries high mortality. Ferroptosis is a promising therapeutic target in which long non-coding RNAs may be involved. This study aimed to investigate the regulatory role of the PTTG3P-miR-142-5p-IGF2BP3 axis in ferroptosis during HCC, revealing the potential mechanisms by which this axis influences HCC initiation and progression.

## Methods
Bioinformatics analysis revealed the expression and prognostic significance of PTTG3P in HCC. Concurrently, overexpression and knockdown models of PTTG3P, miR-142-5p, and IGF2BP3 were established to detect intracellular levels of ferroptosis regulatory factors. Gene interactions were explored via western blot, quantitative real-time PCR, and luciferase reporter assays. Finally, in vivo experiments validated the role of the PTTG3P-miR-142-5p-IGF2BP3 axis in tumorigenesis.

## Results
PTTG3P was upregulated in HCC and associated with poor prognosis. PTTG3P was a molecular sponge for miR-142-5p, leading to IGF2BP3 derepression and modulation of ferroptosis proteins, NRF2, SLC7A11 and GPX4. PTTG3P overexpression in HepG2 cells increased ferroptosis resistance, while PTTG3P knockdown in Huh7 cells sensitized these cells to ferroptosis. Additionally, the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis influenced tumor growth in a xenograft mouse model.

## Conclusion
The PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis is a master regulator of ferroptosis in HCC. PTTG3P is a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) which sustains IGF2BP3-mediated ferroptosis resistance. Targeting the axis sensitizes HCC to ferroptosis and is potentially a novel therapeutic target to combat treatment resistance.

## Introduction
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 75%-85% of primary liver cancers and ranks third in cancer-related deaths worldwide [ , ]. 5-year survival rates remain below 20% despite the availability of targeted therapies, such as sorafenib and lenvatinib, due to intrinsic and acquired drug resistance [ ]. The elucidation of molecular mechanisms underlying HCC progression and therapeutic resistance is urgently needed.
Ferroptosis describes iron-dependent regulated cell death, characterized by lethal lipid peroxide (LPO), and may bypass apoptosis to overcome chemoresistance in HCC [ – ]. However, cancer cells often develop adaptive mechanisms to evade ferroptosis, particularly through RNA-mediated regulatory networks [ , ]. Long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs) may act as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) which modulate MicroRNA (miRNA) activity and regulate gene expression and cellular homeostasis [ , ]. ceRNA networks orchestrated by LncRNAs may influence sensitivity to ferroptosis [ , – ]. LncRNA pituitary tumor-transforming 3 pseudogene (PTTG3P) has been shown to affect proliferation, migration and apoptosis in a range of cancers. PTTG3P is overexpressed in HCC and expression has been correlated with aggressive clinicopathological characteristics, including increased tumor burden, advanced TNM staging and shorter overall survival. PTTG3P overexpression (PTTG3P-OE) enhanced cell proliferation, migration and invasion in vitro and enhanced the tumorigenicity of in vivo xenografts. PTTG3P silencing was shown to have antitumor effects, reversing oncogenic phenotypes in cells and in animal models [ ]. However, the involvement of PTTG3P in ferroptosis remains unexplored in HCC.
Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 3 (IGF2BP3) is an m6A reader protein which regulated the ferroptosis genes, SLC7A11 and GPX4, in HCC [ ], lung adenocarcinoma [ ] and colorectal cancer [ ] and enhanced malignancy. IGF2BP3 enhanced HCC resistance to ferroptosis by stabilization of NRF2 mRNA [ ] and promoted angiogenesis by m6A modulation of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGFA) expression [ ]. However, the interaction of IGF2BP3 with ceRNA networks during ferroptosis regulation remains unexplored. LncRNA/miRNA crosstalk via ceRNA networks is known to affect gene expression in cancer [ ]. The tumor modulating activity of miR-142-5p has been acknowledged [ ] but little is known about its involvement in ferroptosis or interaction with PTTG3P and IGF2BP3 in HCC. PTTG3P was identified as a molecular sponge for miR-142-5p during the current bioinformatic analysis and dual-luciferase reporter assays. Database screening allowed 16 putative target genes of miR-142-5p, including IGF2BP3, to be identified. Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses showed miR-142-5p to be associated with pathways of ferroptosis, particularly sphingolipid [ , ] and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling [ – ]. m6A post-transcriptional modification has previously been shown to affect signaling [ , ] and cancer cell ferroptosis [ – ]. The m6A reader activity of IGF2BP3 is known to stabilize transcripts of the system xc- component, SLC7A11 [ ], which may contribute to maintenance of redox homeostasis and oppose ferroptotic cell death [ ]. We hypothesize that the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 regulatory axis is a master switch controlling HCC ferroptosis susceptibility.
The role of PTTG3P in modulating ferroptosis sensitivity by miR-142-5p sponging and IGF2BP3 derepression was examined in the current work. PTTG3P was established as a ferroptosis suppressor in HCC, a novel ceRNA mechanism involving the miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3/SLC7A11 axis was delineated and the potential for increasing the sensitivity of HCC to ferroptosis-inducing therapies by targeting this axis was explored. The current findings contribute to the understanding of ferroptosis regulation by RNA and suggest targets for overcoming HCC therapy resistance.

## Materials and methods

## Bioinformatics analysis
LncRNAs/miRNA interactions were predicted from the online databases, ENCORI (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/index.php) and LncTar (http://www.cuilab.cn/lnctar) and miRNA target genes predicted using the online tools, ENCOR, miRDB (http://www.mirdb.org/), miRTarBase (https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/), TarBase (http://diana.imis.athena-innovation.gr/DianaTools/index.php) and TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org). GO (https://geneontology.org) and KEGG (https://www.kegg.jp) analyses were performed in R package “Cluster Profile” to investigate biological functions and pathways. Interactions were validated by the Database of Interacting Nucleic Acids and Nucleotides (DINNA, http://bioinfo.szbl.ac.cn/dinna/). Gene expression in HCC was correlated with that in normal liver tissues from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga), Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) datasets.

## Antibodies and chemicals

## Antibodies
Rabbit monoclonal primary antibodies were used for Western blotting (WB) as follows: IGF2BP3 (A4444, ABclonal Technology; 1:2000), GAPDH (HA721136, HUABIO; 1:10,000), GPX4 (ET1706-45, HUABIO; 1:10,000), SLC7A11 (HA721868, HUABIO; 1:1000), NRF2 (R1312-8, HUABIO; 1:1000) with HRP-conjugated Goat anti-Rabbit secondary IgG (H + L) (S1002, Report Technology; 1:6000).

## Chemical reagents
Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (D8371, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was used to solubilize 10 µM erastin (17754, Cayman, USA) or 2 µM ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1, 17729, Cayman, USA) for ferroptosis experiments with a vehicle control of 0.1% DMSO.

## Tissue samples
64 paired pathologically confirmed HCC and adjacent non-tumor liver samples (from regions at least 3 cm distal to the tumor margin) were obtained from patients undergoing surgical resection at the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery, Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University (Hebei, China) between January 2023 and October 2024. Specimens were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately after resection and stored at -80 ℃. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Chengde Medical University Affiliated Hospital (CYFYLL2023316, approved on March 16, 2023) and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The first patient underwent surgery on March 24, 2023, and the enrolled patients ranged in age from 45 to 75 years (median age: 57 years).

## Plasmid construction, oligonucleotide synthesis
Human PTTG3P and IGF2BP3 overexpression (PTTG3P-OE/ IGF2BP3-OE) plasmids were constructed by Public Protein/Plasmid Library (PPL, China) with full-length coding sequences (CDS) cloned into (pcDNA3.1(+) or pCDH-CMV) mammalian expression vectors under the control of a CMV promoter. HPLC-purified siRNA duplexes (GenCefe BiotechCo. Ltd., jiangsu, China) were designed for PTTG3P knockdown (PTTG3P-KD): siPTTG3P-619 (5´-GCUCUGACAUAGAUAUUUATT-3´/5´-UAAAUAUCUAUGUCAGAGCTT-3´) and IGF2BP3-KD: siIGF2BP3-1326 (5´-GCAGGAAUUGACGCUGUAUAAdTdT-3´/5´-UUAUACAGCGUCAAUUCCUGCdTdT-3´). A control scrambled siRNA (5´-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3´/5´-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3´) was also synthesized. miR-142-5p mimic (5´-CAUAAAGUAGAAAGCACUACU-3´/5´-UAGUGCUUUCUACUUUAUGUU-3´) and 2´-O-methylated inhibitor (5´-(mA)(mG)(mU)(mA)(mG)(mU)(mG)(mC)(mU)(mU)(mU)(mC)(mU)(mA)(mC)(mU)(mU)(mU)(mA)(mU)(mG)-3´) plus scrambled controls were synthesized by GenCefe BiotechCo. Ltd., jiangsu, China. 20 µM oligonucleotides were resuspended in nuclease-free water and quality verified by mass spectrometry.

## Cell culture
Human liver cancer cell line HepG2 (CL-0103), together with hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines Hep3B (CL-0102), Huh7 (CL-0120) and the immortalized hepatic cell line, THLE-2 (CL-0833), (Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd, Wuhan, China) were cultured in DMEM (Huh7, C11995500BT, ThermoFisher, USA), MEM (HepG2 and Hep3B, PM150410, Procell, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, C2880-0500, VivaCell, China) or in specialized complete medium (THLE-2, CM-0833, Procell, China), according to the manufacturer’s protocol at 37 ℃ in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Cells were thawed from low-passage stocks and subcultured every 48 h. Transfections were carried out using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (L3000015; Invitrogen, USA), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Mycoplasma-free status and cell line authenticity were confirmed by bimonthly PCR testing.

## Cell viability assay
Cell viability was assessed by Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, K1018, APExBIO, USA), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were transfected for 48 h in 6-well plates, trypsinized and seeded into 96-well plates at 5 × 103 cells/well overnight, before treatment with 10 µM erastin, 2 µM Fer-1 or 0.1% DMSO control for 24 h. Cells were incubated with CCK-8 solution for 2 h at 37 ℃ and absorbance measured at 450 nm by hemocytometer (JSH-TH-2, JSHBIO, China).

## Measurement of lipid peroxide (LPO) and lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS)
Intracellular LPOs were measured by Fluorometric Assay Kit (E-BC-F003, Elabscience®, Wuhan, China) and intracellular ROS by Reactive Oxygen Species Assay Kit (CA1410, Solarbio, Beijing, China). Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 3.5 × 105 cells per well, cultured overnight to 70–80% confluency, transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 and incubated for 24 h before experimental treatment with erastin or Fer-1 for 24 h under standard culture conditions. Culture medium was removed, cells washed twice with serum-free medium working solution and 500 µL per well 2 µmol/L C11-BODIPY in serum-free medium or 1 mL per well 1:6000 dilution of DCFH-DA in serum-free medium added for incubation at 37 ℃ for 30 min in the dark. Cells were washed 3 times with serum-free medium to remove excess probe and imaged under a fluorescence microscope ((ECHO REVOLVE, San Diego, USA) with FITC filters. Quantification of green fluorescence intensity (representing oxidized probe) was conducted with ImageJ software (NIH) and normalized to the vehicle control. Results are means of three independent replicates.

## Measurement of ferrous ions (Fe2+), reduced glutathione (GSH) and malondialdehyde (MDA)
Three biomarkers were measured to assess redox homeostasis and iron metabolism: GSH by DTNB-based assay (BC1175), MDA by thiobarbituric acid reaction (BC0025) and Fe2⁺ by TPTZ chromogenic detection (BC5415), according to manufacturers’ protocols (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Cells were plated in 10 cm culture dishes at a density of 3 × 106 cells per dish, allowed to reach 70–80% confluency overnight and transfection performed with Lipofectamine 3000. Cells were treated 48 h post-transfection with erastin or DMSO for 24 h under physiological culture conditions, trypsinized and counted with a hemocytometer (JSH-TH-2, JSHBIO, China) before resuspension in PBS. Cell lysates were prepared in Reagent 1 by ice-bath sonication. GSH was detected at 412 nm, MDA by dual-wavelength detection at 532 nm and 600 nm and Fe2⁺ TPTZ at 593 nm by Multiskan FC microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

## WB analysis
Proteins were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (AIWB-012; Affinity Biosciences, China) supplemented with phosphatase inhibitor (K1015; APExBIO, USA) and protease inhibitor (K1007; APExBIO, USA) cocktails. Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay (P1511, APPLYGEN, China) and samples mixed with 5× SDS-PAGE loading buffer (AIWB-0025, Affinity Biosciences), followed by denaturation at 95 ℃ for 10 min. Equal quantities of protein were separated on 4–20% gradient SDS-polyacrylamide precast gels (ET12420Gel; ACE, China), transferred to PVDF membranes (0.2 μm, ISEQ00010, 0.45 μm, IPVH00010, Millipore, USA) by rapid transfer system with 20× transfer buffer (BR0003-01; ACE, China) for 45 min. and membranes blocked with blocking buffer (AIWB-004; Affinity Biosciences, China) for 10 min at room temperature. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (see Antibodies and Chemicals) overnight at 4 ℃, washed 3 times for 10 min each with 10× TBST buffer (G0004-1 L, Servicebio, China), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at RT and signals developed with ECL substrate (BL520B, Biosharp, China) on a chemiluminescence imaging system. Protein separation was calibrated by Prestained Protein Ladder (10–180 kDa, 26616, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) or Tri-color Protein Marker (10–250 kDa, ZS-PR24001-250ul, Zhongshi Gene Technology (Tianjin) Co., Ltd., China).

## RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis and real-time PCR
Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed as follows: total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (15596026, Invitrogen, USA), treated with DNase I to remove genomic DNA and cDNA synthesized by reverse transcription of mRNA using FastKing RT Kit (with gDNase) (KR116; TIANGEN, China) and of LncRNAs by lnRcute LncRNAs First-Strand cDNA Kit (KR202; TIANGEN, China). Total RNA was isolated for miRNA analysis by miRcute miRNA Isolation Kit (DP501; TIANGEN, China) and reverse transcribed with 2× miRNA RT Buffer Kit (RK215; TIANGEN, China). qPCR amplification was carried out on a Roche cobas z480 system using 2× SYBR Green qPCR Mix (AH0104; SparkJade, China) under standardized cycling conditions. Primer sequences were: PTTG3P (F: CAGGCATCCTTGTGGCTACA, R: TCTGGTAGCTTTAGGTAAGGATGTG), miR-142-5p (F: GCGGCGGCATAAAGTAGAAAGC, R: ATCCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGG), IGF2BP3 (F: ACCTATTCCAGTAAGGACCAAGC, R: GGCCATTTCATCAGGGATATAG), GAPDH (F: GGAAGCTTGTCATCAATGGAAATC, R: TGATGACCCTTTTGGCTCCC) and U6 (F: CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA, R: AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT). GAPDH was used as internal control for mRNA/LncRNAs and U6 for miRNA. Fold change in gene expression was calculated using the 2−∆∆CT method. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

## Luciferase reporter assay
The wild-type (WT) PTTG3P sequence containing the predicted miR-142-5p binding site was synthesized and cloned into the pmirGLO Dual-Luciferase vector, downstream of the firefly luciferase gene. A mutant (MUT) construct with 4-nucleotide substitutions in the seed region was generated by site-directed mutagenesis. A 200-bp fragment of the IGF2BP3 3’UTR containing the WT or MUT binding site was cloned into the pmirGLO vector to enable examination of miR-142-5p binding. HEK293T cells were co-transfected with *miR-142-5p* mimics or negative control (NC) plus WT or MUT reporter constructs. Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were quantified 48 h post-transfection by Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (E1910, Promega, USA) and activity normalized to Renilla luciferase.

## In vivo experiments
SPF grade BALB/c-nude male mice (n = 20), aged 3–4 weeks, 15–20 g, were purchased from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Production License No.: SCXK [Su] 2023-0009, Jiangsu, China; ) and housed in SPF-grade facilities at the Central Laboratory, Chengde Medical University Affiliated Hospital. All animal procedures were performed in accordance with guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, Chengde Medical University (CYFYLL2025008, approved on December 9, 2024, conducted from May 6, 2025, to June 3, 2025). Mice were housed with 12-h light/dark cycle and access to sterilized food and water ad libitum. 1 × 107 HepG2 cells suspended in 200 µL PBS were subcutaneously injected into the right axillary flank region near the back of each mouse, and tumors measured weekly using calipers (n = 5). Tumor volume was calculated: Volume = [(long diameter) × (short diameter)2]/2. Mice were sacrificed at the end of the experiment, tumors excised, weighed and photographed before fixing in 4% paraformaldehyde for histology or snap-freezing in liquid nitrogen for molecular analysis. Humane endpoints were defined in accordance with institutional and international guidelines. Mice were euthanized if tumor volume exceeded 1500 mm3, if ulceration or necrosis occurred, or if body weight loss exceeded 20%. Animals showing severe distress, impaired mobility, or respiratory difficulty unrelieved by care were also humanely sacrificed. Euthanasia was performed by CO2 inhalation followed by cervical dislocation, and death was confirmed by the absence of heartbeat and respiration.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.1.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation from at least three independent experiments. Inter-group comparisons were made by two-tailed Student’s t-test for 2 groups and by one-way or two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s or Bonferroni’s post hoc tests for multiple groups. For paired samples, normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed data were analyzed with a paired Student’s t-test, and non-normal data with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis was performed and correlated with Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Sample sizes were determined based on previous studies and preliminary experiments to ensure a statistical power of at least 80%. A value of p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001).

## Results

## PTTG3P was overexpressed in HCC and correlated with poor prognosis
Bioinformatics analysis of the UALCAN and Kaplan-Meier Plotter databases showed upregulated PTTG3P expression in primary HCC compared with normal liver tissues (Fig.  A). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis correlated high PTTG3P expression with shorter overall survival (Fig.  B), suggesting that PTTG3P may be an oncogenic factor and a prognostic marker. RT-qPCR analysis of 64 paired HCC samples demonstrated higher PTTG3P expression in tumor tissues than in adjacent non-tumor liver tissues (Fig.  C). PTTG3P overexpression was strongly associated with advanced TNM stage and larger tumor size but not with demographic (age/gender), biochemical (AFP) or metastatic (vascular invasion/distant lesions) characteristics (Table ), implying a tumor-intrinsic role in HCC progression. When compared to the immortalized hepatocyte control (LO2), the expression of PTTG3P was markedly elevated in the HCC cell lines HepG2, Huh7, and Hep3B, as determined by RT-qPCR (Fig.  D). These results suggest that PTTG3P is a biomarker for poor prognosis in HCC and an oncogenic factor for cancer progression. PTTG3P expression was correlated with poor patient outcome and molecular mechanisms were investigated to illuminate the potential as a therapeutic target.
 

## PTTG3P regulated ferroptosis susceptibility in Huh7 and HepG2 cells
PTTG3P expression was knocked down in Huh7 cells by siRNA and overexpressed in HepG2 cells by plasmid transfection and impacts on cell viability assessed by CCK-8 assay. PTTG3P-OE cells were resistant to the cytotoxicity induced by the ferroptosis stimulator, erastin, and this effect was partially alleviated by co-treatment with the ferroptosis inhibitor, Fer-1 (Fig.  A). PTTG3P knockdown reduced cell viability, an effect that was enhanced by erastin treatment and opposed by Fer-1 co-treatment (Fig.  B). These results indicate a role for PTTG3P in ferroptosis resistance.
 Quantitative analysis of LPO using C11-BODIPY581/591 staining showed increased levels in PTTG3P-KD Huh7 cells compared with NC-KD controls. This effect was partially reversed by addition of Fer-1 and erastin treatment increased LPO in NC-KD cell (Fig.  C). Assessment of intracellular ROS levels in PTTG3P-OE or PTTG3P-KD HepG2 and Huh7 cells showed lower basal ROS levels in PTTG3P-OE relative to NC-OE cells. ROS levels increased in PTTG3P-OE cells treated with erastin (Fig.  D). PTTG3P-KD cells had elevated basal ROS and these levels were reduced by Fer-1 treatment (Fig.  E). Assessment of levels of ferroptosis markers, GSH, Fe2⁺ and MDA, in HepG2 and Huh7 cells showed the PTTG3P-OE status to be associated with increased GSH and decreased Fe2⁺ and MDA (Fig.  F-H). By contrast, PTTG3P-KD status was associated with reduced GSH and elevated Fe2⁺ and MDA, effects that were amplified by erastin treatment (Fig.  I-K). WB analysis showed upregulation of ferroptosis regulators, SLC7A11, GPX4 and NRF2, in PTTG3P-OE cells (Fig.  L) and downregulation in PTTG3P-KD cells (Fig.  M). These findings, derived from gain- and loss-of-function studies in HepG2 and Huh7 cells, suggest a regulatory role for PTTG3P in redox homeostasis and ferroptosis.

## PTTG3P was a molecular sponge for miR-142-5p in HCC
LncRNA-miRNA interactions were predicted from the LncTar and ENCORI databases and miR-142-5p suggested as a putative downstream target of PTTG3P, after consultation of relevant literature (Fig.  A). Binding between PTTG3P and miR-142-5p was indicated by bioinformatics analyses and a sponging relationship suggested (Fig.  B). Examination of WT and MUT luciferase reporter constructs showed that co-transfection with the miR-142-5p mimic reduced luciferase activity in cells with the WT reporter but not in those with the MUT reporter. These results are consistent with the view that miR-142-5p interacts with the PTTG3P 3’-UTR region (Fig.  C). RT-qPCR analysis of 64 paired HCC and adjacent non-tumor tissues showed upregulation of PTTG3P (Fig.  D) and downregulation of miR-142-5p in HCC samples (Fig.  E). Pearson correlation analysis showed negative correlation between PTTG3P and miR-142-5p in HCC (Fig.  F). This finding supports the existence of a functional interaction of PTTG3P and miR-142-5p in hepatocellular carcinogenesis.
 

## miR-142-5p modulates ferroptosis in Huh7 and HepG2 cells
Treatment of HepG2 cells with the miR-142-5p mimic reduced viability relative to treatment with the NC mimic and decreased viability was accentuated by addition of erastin in both transfected cell-types. Co-treatment with Fer-1 partially rescued cell viability in both groups, particularly for cells transfected with miR-142-5p mimic (Fig.  A). Assessment of LPO levels showed a decrease in Huh7 cells treated with miR-142-5p inhibitor compared with those treated with NC inhibitor and this decrease was partially reversed by erastin, indicating that miR-142-5p inhibition partially protects against ferroptosis-induced LPO (Fig.  B). Cells transfected with miR-142-5p mimic had elevated basal ROS compared with NC-mimic controls, an effect that was attenuated by Fer-1 co-treatment (Fig.  C). Cells transfected with miR-142-5p inhibitor had lower basal ROS than NC-inhibitor controls (Fig.  D). These data demonstrate that miR-142-5p overexpression disrupts cellular redox homeostasis and the resulting oxidative stress enhances sensitivity to ferroptosis. Cells transfected with miR-142-5 mimic also had increased intracellular Fe2+ and MDA and reduced GSH, effects that were accentuated by erastin exposure (Fig.  E-G). WB analysis showed downregulation of ferroptosis regulators, SLC7A11, GPX4 and NRF2, with miR-142-5p mimic and upregulation with miR-142-5p inhibitor (Fig.  H-I). These phenotypic and molecular changes were consistent with those observed for PTTG3P-KD or PTTG3P-OE cells, indicating a role for miR-142-5p in mediating PTTG3P regulation of ferroptosis.
 

## miR-142-5p targeted IGF2BP3 and modulated ferroptosis in HCC
Target predictions from the TargetScan, miRDB, ENCORI, miRTarBase, DIANA and TarBase databases allowed identification of 16 high-confidence target genes, one of which was IGF2BP3 (Fig.  A). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis indicated the association of miR-142-5p with sphingolipid and MAPK signaling in the context of ferroptosis (Fig.  B). IGF2BP3 was functionally enriched in these pathways, particularly through its regulation of SLC7A11. This finding suggests the involvement of IGF2BP3 in ferroptosis sensitivity in HCC.
 Analyses of the miRDB, ENCORI and TargetScan databases were performed to identify binding sites of miR-142-5p on IGF2BP3 and bioinformatics prediction revealed two conserved binding sites in the 3’ UTR of IGF2BP3. The 625–632 site had optimal binding energy and central location within the 3’ UTR and was the focus of further experiments (Fig.  C). Dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed with IGF2BP3-WT and IGF2BP3-MUT constructs of the predicted miR-142-5p binding site from the IGF2BP3 3’-UTR. Co-transfection of HEK293T cells with miR-142-5p mimic and IGF2BP3-WT reporter reduced luciferase activity compared with the NC-mimic control (Fig.  D). This suppressive effect was abolished with the IGF2BP3-MUT reporter. These results are consistent with the view that miR-142-5p binds the 3’ UTR of IGF2BP3, leading to effects on ferroptosis. Analysis of TCGA database showed IGF2BP3 expression to be higher in HCC tissues than in normal liver tissues (Fig.  E). High IGF2BP3 expression levels were associated with poor patient prognosis (Fig.  F). IGF2BP3 may thus have potential as a therapeutic target in HCC.

## Functional characterization of IGF2BP3 in HCC ferroptosis regulation
Measurements of IGF2BP3 expression in HepG2, Hep3B and Huh7 cells showed the highest levels in Hep3B and the lowest in HepG2 cells (Fig.  A). Hep3B cells were used for IGF2BP3-KD and HepG2 for IGF2BP3-OE experiments. IGF2BP3-KD was shown to reduce cell viability, an effect that was enhanced by erastin treatment and reversed by Fer-1 co-treatment (Fig.  B). Experimental results indicated an impact of IGF2BP3 on redox homeostasis. IGF2BP3-KD increased LPO levels compared with NC-KD and treatment with Fer-1 reduced LPO levels in IGF2BP3-KD cells (Fig.  C). IGF2BP3-OE cells had reduced basal ROS, an effect that was partially reversed by erastin (Fig.  D). By contrast, IGF2BP3-KD cells had increased ROS and levels were reduced by Fer-1 (Fig.  E). IGF2BP3 thus exerts a dual influence over LPO and ROS generation during ferroptosis. IGF2BP3 is suggested to be a suppressor of ferroptotic death in HCC. IGF2BP3-KD cells also had elevated intracellular Fe2+ and MDA and reduced GSH (Fig.  F-H). WB analysis showed upregulation of SLC7A11, GPX4 and NRF2 in IGF2BP3-OE cells (Fig.  I) and downregulation in IGF2BP3-KD cells (Fig.  J). The impact of IGF2BP3 manipulation on ferroptosis recapitulated the effects of PTTG3P modulation and suggest that IGF2BP3 is a downstream effector in the regulatory cascade.
 

## Control of HCC ferroptosis by a PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 regulatory axis
Experiments investigating the impact of overexpression and knockdown of PTTG3P and miR-142-5p on IGF2BP3 expression assessed by WB showed that PTTG3P-KD decreased and PTTG3P-OE increased IGF2BP3 levels (Fig.  A). Similarly, the miR-142-5p mimic reduced and the miR-142-5p inhibitor enhanced IGF2BP3 expression (Fig.  B). These results suggest a reciprocal regulatory relationship among PTTG3P, miR-142-5p and IGF2BP3.
 Rescue experiments were performed in which cells co-transfected with PTTG3P-OE and miR-142-5p mimic were found to have lower IGF2BP3 levels than PTTG3P-OE alone. Co-transfection of PTTG3P-OE with IGF2BP3-OE produced a greater increase in IGF2BP3 levels than PTTG3P-OE alone. Cells co-transfected with PTTG3P-KD and miR-142-5p inhibitor had higher IGF2BP3 levels than with PTTG3P-KD alone and co-transfection of PTTG3P-KD with IGF2BP3-KD produced a greater reduction of IGF2BP3 than PTTG3P-KD alone. These results suggest synergistic effects of PTTG3P and IGF2BP3 on IGF2BP3 expression. WB analysis of SLC7A11, GPX4 and NRF2 showed expression patterns consistent with those of IGF2BP3, indicating regulation by the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p axis (Fig.  C-D). These findings suggest a ceRNA regulatory axis in which PTTG3P competitively binds miR-142-5p to sustain IGF2BP3 expression, which in turn transcriptionally regulates SLC7A11, GPX4, and NRF2. The activity of this axis modulates cellular susceptibility to ferroptotic death (Fig.  E).

## In vivo validation of the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis
Subcutaneous xenograft tumors were established in nude mice from HepG2 cells transfected with different constructs (Fig.  A). PTTG3P-OE increased tumor volume and weight compared with NC-OE. Co-overexpression of miR-142-5p and PTTG3P partially reversed the increase in tumor weight and volume, indicating mitigation of the tumor-promoting effects of PTTG3P by miR-142-5p. By contrast, co-overexpression of IGF2BP3 and PTTG3P enhanced tumor growth, suggesting that IGF2BP3 amplifies the effects of PTTG3P on tumor progression (Fig.  B-C). The upregulation of IGF2BP3 produced by PTTG3P was shown to be reversed by miR-142-5p mimic and augmented by PTTG3P-OE and IGF2BP3-OE by WB analysis of excised tumor tissue (Fig.  D). RT-qPCR analysis confirmed that PTTG3P and miR-142-5p affect IGF2BP3 mRNA in a bidirectional manner, consistent with results at the protein level (Fig.  E). The in vivo findings are consistent with the results of in vitro experiments and support the pathophysiological relevance of the ceRNA network in HCC tumorigenesis.
 

## Discussion
LncRNAs are thought to have many diverse roles in different cancer types. Ning et al. curated a database of 1057 associations of 531 LncRNAs with 86 cancer types [ ] and this resource has been expanded to form Lnc2Cancer3.0 which encompasses thousands of associations [ ]. Such databases facilitate the exploration of LncRNAs function, regulation and potential as biomarkers. LncRNAs have context-dependent activities in cancer progression, functioning as tumor suppressors or oncogenes. For example, LncRNAs, CASC2, is a tumor suppressor in breast cancer and inhibits TGF-β signaling [ ] whereas LncRNAs, HOTTIP, has an oncogenic role in various cancer types and is associated with poor clinical outcomes [ ]. Other examples of pathways related to cancer that are affected by LncRNAs include APAL activation of PLK1 which regulates mitosis [ ] and the effects of PCAT7 on ErbB/PI3K/Akt signaling which drives breast cancer progression [ ]. LncRNAs also influence therapeutic responses. Berberine has pro-apoptotic effects on colorectal cancer by upregulating the tumor suppressor, CASC2, and inhibiting Bcl-2 expression [ ] and the oncogenic CASC7 drives glycolytic metabolism in esophageal cancer [ ]. Thus, LncRNAs have dual clinical significance as predictive biomarkers and therapeutic targets to enhance treatment outcomes.
LncRNAs have been shown to both promote and inhibit ferroptosis in cancer cells via diverse mechanisms and may thus be therapeutic targets. The LncRNAs tumor suppressors, P53RRA5 and HEPFAL [ ], enhance the sensitivity to ferroptosis by acting through p53 and SLC7A11 and oncogenic LncRNAs, OIP5-AS1 [ ] and LUCAT1 [ ], confer resistance to ferroptosis. Many mechanisms are involved, including miRNA sponging, epigenetic modifications and interactions with RNA-binding proteins. Crosstalk among LncRNAs and miRNAs expands the ferroptosis regulatory network and the H19/miR-19b-3p/FTH1 axis shows that LncRNAs fine-tune iron metabolism and oxidative stress responses. PTTG3P integrates m6A-dependent regulation via IGF2BP3 with ceRNA activity by contrast with other LncRNAs involved in ferroptosis, such as HEPFAL or OIP5-AS1, suggesting a broader role in post-transcriptional control. Future studies might explore whether PTTG3P-targeted antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) are synergistic with sorafenib in overcoming resistance.
The oncogenic LncRNAs, PTTG3P, has been recognized to have consistent overexpression and functional importance in tumor progression and exerts many synergistic effects. In its role as ceRNA, PTTG3P sequesters tumor-suppressive microRNAs, including miR-383 in HCC, releasing oncogenic targets from post-transcriptional repression [ ]. PTTG3P also activates the oncogenic pathways, PI3K/AKT [ ] and Notch1 [ ] and its interaction with RNA-binding proteins, such as ILF3 [ ], stabilizes pro-tumorigenic transcripts, forming a malignant feed-forward loop. PTTG3P was identified as a novel modulator of ferroptosis in HCC during the current work, giving mechanistic insights into its established association with poor clinical outcomes. PTTG3P and miR-142-5p expression were inversely correlated in clinical specimens, emphasizing the relevance of the regulatory axis. The role of miR-142-5p as a tumor suppressor is well-documented, suggesting that sequestration by PTTG3P may account for the oncogenic potential. The PTTG3P/miR-142-5p axis appears to be a promising prognostic indicator and a potential therapeutic target in HCC management.
IGF2BP3 is well-established as an m6A reader protein and concerned with promotion of ferroptosis resistance in many cancers, including HCC. The underlying molecular mechanisms include the stabilization of ferroptosis mRNAs with impacts on SLC7A11, GPX4 and RRM2 expression via m6A - dependent binding [ – ]. Second, IGF2BP3 activates the NRF2 and TFAP2A transcription pathways to regulate the expression of ferroptosis genes [ , , ]. Third, IGF2BP3 regulates iron and LPO metabolism to maintain cellular redox homeostasis. The IGF2BP3 and NRF2 regulatory axis is acknowledged to influence the HCC response to sorafenib treatment [ ]. IGF2BP3 also collaborates with LncRNAs LINC00942 to form an m6A - dependent stabilization network for SLC7A11 mRNA, leading to treatment resistance in tumor cells [ ]. IGF2BP3 was identified as a downstream effector of the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p regulatory axis by the current work and this finding bridges the gap between the ceRNA regulatory network and ferroptosis activity. IGF2BP3 has been shown to have similar ferroptosis regulatory activities in lung cancer and colorectal cancer [ ] and the present study provides more detailed mechanistic insights into this regulation. The coordinated modulation of SLC7A11, GPX4, and NRF2 by the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis suggests that this pathway may constitute a key defense against lipid peroxidation, potentially accounting for the high incidence of therapeutic resistance observed in this malignancy.
The current in vitro and in vivo results suggest that targeting the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis may be a potential strategy to overcome therapy resistance in HCC. Restoration of miR-142-5p may mitigate the tumor-promoting effects of PTTG3P and recent progress in RNA therapeutics suggests that miRNA replacement therapy may be viable [ ]. Inhibition of IGF2BP3 sensitized this malignancy to ferroptosis, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target in combination with ferroptosis inducers [ ]. Such findings give a theoretical basis for development of new targeted therapies for HCC. The current study demonstrates that a LncRNAs-miRNA network converges on the m6A reader protein, IGF2BP3, to control ferroptosis contributing to the complexity of this pathway’s regulation. Similar regulatory mechanisms may operate in malignancies other than HCC where IGF2BP3 is overexpressed [ ], increasing the understanding of m6A modification in tumor development and progression.
This study possesses a certain degree of innovation. A novel PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 regulatory axis that governs ferroptosis sensitivity in HCC is presented and gives insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying HCC progression and therapeutic resistance. LncRNAs, PTTG3P, functions as a molecular sponge for miR-142-5p to maintain IGF2BP3 expression and influence expression of ferroptosis regulators, SLC7A11, GPX4 and NRF2. These results advance the understanding of RNA-mediated ferroptosis regulation in HCC pathogenesis. We acknowledge several limitations to the current study. First, the direct interaction between PTTG3P and miR-142-5p has been confirmed solely through luciferase assays. Future studies should employ RNA precipitation or in situ hybridization experiments to strengthen the evidence for their binding. Secondly, detecting only basic ferroptosis markers may not provide a comprehensive assessment of ferroptosis status. Future studies should incorporate GPX4 enzyme activity assays or lipidomics analysis to furnish more direct and comprehensive evidence for ferroptosis occurrence. Third, IGF2BP3 may regulate transcripts involved in ferroptosis other than SLC7A11 and these potential targets remain to be explored. Fourth, the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis may crosstalk with other cell death pathways and such interactions require elucidation. Fifth, there are challenges for the clinical translation of the current findings, particularly as regards delivery of RNA-based therapeutics and further preclinical studies are necessary. Sixth, the primary findings of this study are based on observations from specific cell lines (HepG2, Huh7, Hep3B). Although I designed the experiments rationally based on baseline expression levels and supplemented them with rescue experiments for validation, genetic heterogeneity exists among different hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines. Future studies should further validate the universality of these conclusions in additional cell lines and in vivo models.

## Conclusion
We identify a novel PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 regulatory axis that modulates ferroptosis sensitivity through a ceRNA mechanism. These findings advance the understanding of this malignancy’s biology and highlight potential therapeutic targets within this axis. Notably, IGF2BP3 inhibition sensitizes these tumors to ferroptosis, particularly in combination with ferroptosis inducers, offering a potential strategy to overcome treatment resistance. Future studies may give more information regarding relevant molecular mechanisms, explore combinatorial therapies targeting the PTTG3P/miR-142-5p/IGF2BP3 axis alongside conventional treatments to improve clinical outcomes and validate therapeutic potential in preclinical and clinical settings. A foundation for development of novel anti-HCC precision medicine by exploiting ferroptosis susceptibility is presented.
