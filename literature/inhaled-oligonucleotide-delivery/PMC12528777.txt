## Abstract

## Background
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is marked by progressive lung scarring with no existing cure, emphasising the need for new therapeutic targets. Current evidence suggests that cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) mitigates lung fibroblast proliferation via the protein kinase A pathway, but the impact of exchange proteins directly activated by cAMP 1 (Epac1) on IPF remains unexplored.

## Objective
To investigate the role of Epac1 in IPF progression.

## Methods
We examined lung samples from IPF patients and controls, and from a bleomycin-induced mouse model of pulmonary fibrosis. The effects of Epac were analysed in knockout mice and through modulation using viral vectors. The Epac1-specific small compound inhibitor AM-001 was evaluated in vitro using lung fibroblasts from patients with IPF, in vivo in bleomycin mice and ex vivo in IPF precision-cut lung slices.

## Results
Increased Epac1 expression was observed in lung tissues from IPF patients, fibrotic fibroblasts and bleomycin-challenged mice. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 with AM-001 decreased proliferation in normal and IPF fibroblasts, and reduced expression of profibrotic markers such as α-smooth muscle actin, transforming growth factor-β/SMAD family member 2/3, and interleukin-6/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 pathways. Epac1-specific inhibition consistently protected against bleomycin-induced lung injury and fibrosis, suggesting significant therapeutic potential. Global gene expression profiling indicated a reduced profibrotic gene signature and neddylation pathway components in Epac1-deficient fibroblasts and human-derived lung cells. Mechanistically, the protective effects may involve inhibiting the neddylation pathway and preventing neural precursor cell expressed, developmentally downregulated 8 (NEDD8) activation, which in turn reduces the degradation of forkhead box protein O3 by NEDD8. Additionally, these effects may be enhanced while also limiting the proliferation of lung-infiltrating monocytes.

## Conclusions
Our findings demonstrate that Epac1 regulates fibroblast activity in pulmonary fibrosis, and that targeting Epac1 with the pharmacological specific inhibitor AM-001 offers a promising therapeutic approach for treating IPF disease.

## Introduction
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a severe and progressive age-related disease that results in respiratory failure and, eventually, death [ – ]. Despite extensive research efforts in experimental and clinical studies, the incidence of IPF continues to rise with increasing morbidity, with a median survival of 3.8 years [ ]. To date, only two therapeutic options have emerged, which slow disease progression and do not reverse the existing fibrosis. Current paradigms of pulmonary fibrosis (PF) include prominent events in both injury and repair [ ], in which chronic or unresolved injury initiates a tissue response characterised by the recruitment of inflammatory cells, concomitant activation and proliferation of fibroblasts (FBs) to profibrogenic myofibroblasts, and exaggerated extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition that distorts the pulmonary architecture and compromises lung function [ ]. Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)/SMAD family member 3 (SMAD3) signalling is considered a “master switch” in the regulation of fibrosis [ – ] and predisposes older adults to the development of IPF [ – ]. We and others have reported that counteracting TGF-β signalling prevents and attenuates PF [ – ]. Increasing evidence suggests that a positive feedback loop between interleukin-6 (IL-6) and TGF-β may be associated with PF [ – ]. Owing to the complexity and multicellular pathophysiology of PF, alternative pharmacological therapies to reverse this disease are needed.
Cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) is an important second messenger that regulates many physiological and pathophysiological processes via two main direct effectors, protein kinase A (PKA) and exchange proteins directly activated by cAMP, Epac1 and Epac2 [ – ]. For many years, PKA has been described as the “classic” effector responsible for diverse cAMP-mediated functions. For fibrosis formation, various studies have shown that cAMP inhibits FB activation through the PKA pathway [ – ]. Given the emerging role of Epac1 in modulating inflammatory and fibrotic responses in other organ systems, investigating it in the context of lung fibrosis offers a promising avenue for identifying new therapeutic targets [ ]. Evidence that Epac1 and TGF-β1 trigger acute protein kinase B (AKT) phosphorylation suggests that the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) axis may regulate TGF-β receptor signalling in lung fibrosis [ ]. Previous studies have demonstrated that forkhead box protein O3 (FoxO3a) is hyperphosphorylated and inhibited by AKT in IPF, which correlates with severe fibrotic outcomes [ ]. FoxO3a dysregulation and degradation drive FB-to-myofibroblast transdifferentiation, which is associated with IPF progression. Experimental models have revealed that FoxO3 knockout (KO) mice are more vulnerable to bleomycin (BLM)-induced PF and exhibit increased mortality, underscoring the essential protective function of intact FoxO3 signalling in inhibiting the cell cycle and preventing PF [ , ]. Moreover, studies suggest that pharmacological activation of FoxO3a using UCN-01 shows significant promise in reversing the IPF myofibroblast phenotype and effectively reducing fibrosis in vivo [ ], making it a potential therapeutic avenue worth pursuing.
While Epac1 plays a role in regulating AKT activity, the specific mechanisms through which Epac1 may contribute to the pathogenesis of IPF, including its potential influence on mediating FB proliferation and differentiation beyond the classical PKA pathway, have not been thoroughly investigated. Addressing these knowledge gaps and elucidating the role of Epac1 in the pathogenesis and progression of IPF could potentially reveal novel therapeutic avenues and directly respond to the urgent need for more effective IPF treatments.
In the present study, we examined the role of Epac1 and its mechanism of action in PF progression using both genetic and pharmacological approaches. Our findings demonstrate that inhibiting Epac1 using a short hairpin RNA (shRNA)-mediated lentiviral knockdown (KD) or a small molecule inhibitor AM-001 effectively suppresses the growth of isolated IPF-FBs and mitigates BLM-induced PF in mice, both in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, Epac1 blockade inhibited key signalling pathways activated by TGF-β-induced SMAD2/3 and IL-6/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) signalling pathways. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 with AM-001 restored FoxO3a expression, a crucial regulator of FB activation, by preventing its interaction with neural precursor cell expressed, developmentally downregulated 8 (NEDD8) and inhibiting its degradation via the neddylation pathway.

## Methods
The authors declare that all supporting data are available within the article and the supplementary material.

## Patient and sample collection
In this study, RNA, protein and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections were obtained from human IPF patients and healthy control donors. Human lung tissue was obtained from patients with IPF (n=8) who underwent lung transplantation, while lung explant samples from healthy failed donors (n=8), initially considered for organ donation but deemed unsuitable for transplant, were obtained from the Organ Transplant Program of the University General Consortium Hospital of Valencia. All samples were archived and anonymised (supplementary tables S1 and S2). The study protocol was approved by the local research and independent ethics committee of the University General Consortium Hospital of Valencia (CEIC/2013). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant before inclusion in the study.

## Ex vivo model of human precision-cut lung slices
The use of human lung tissues for precision-cut lung slices (PCLS) was approved by the Biosafety and Ethics Committees of Laval University and the Institut Universitaire de Cardiologie et de Pneumologie de Québec (IUCPQ) (CER #22390). Three human lung biopsies were obtained from patients with PF. The tissues were perfused with 2% (m/V) low-melting agarose (Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) through the visible vessels and bronchi. The tissues were then sectioned into 400-µm-thick slices using a Compresstome VF-310-0Z Vibrating Microtome (Precisionary, Natick, MA, USA). These lung slices were sequentially placed individually in a 24-well plate containing 1 mL of medium and allowed 2 days to recover before treatment initiation. The slices were cultured as replicates for 10 days in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 units·mL−1 penicillin, 0.1 mg·mL−1 streptomycin, 0.25 mg·mL−1 amphotericin B and 0.03 mg·mL−1 gentamicin. The medium was replaced every 2 days with AM-001 (20 µM) or vehicle (DMSO) for 10 days. Next, the lung slices were fixed in formaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and processed for Masson's trichrome staining and immunofluorescent labelling or snap-frozen to assess variations in protein expression by Western blotting. Lung sections from three different patients were used. Detailed information regarding the clinical information of the patients included in this study is presented in supplementary table S3.

## Reagents
The non-hydrolysable Epac1 preferential agonist Sp-8-(4-chlorophenylthio)-2′-O-methyl-cAMP was purchased from BioLog (Farmingdale, NY, USA; catalogue number: C052). This compound is abbreviated here as 8-CPT. The Epac1 inhibitor AM-001 was synthesised using previously published methods [ ]. Pevonedistat (MLN4924) is a potent and selective inhibitor of the NEDD8-activating enzyme (NAE) with a half-maximal inhibitory concentration of 4.7 nM, obtained from MedChemExpress (Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA; catalogue number: HY-70062). All the reagents used in this study are listed in supplementary table S4.

## Immune phenotyping by flow cytometry
After systemic perfusion with 10 mL of PBS through the right ventricle, mice lungs were collected, cut into small pieces (<0.5 cm) and enzymatically digested in 10 mL of RPMI (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 5% FBS (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA), 0.5 mg·mL−1 DNase I (grade II, from bovine pancreas; Roche, Penzberg, Germany) and 0.2 mg·mL−1 collagenase IV (Gibco) for 1 h at 37°C. The samples were passed through a 70-µm cell strainer, washed twice with RPMI-1640 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and further purified by density gradient using a Percoll gradient (Pharmacia, Piscataway, NJ, USA). Single-cell suspensions were labelled for viability (Live/Dead blue; Thermo Fisher Scientific) and washed in fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) buffer (2% FBS and 2 mM EDTA in PBS), then Fc receptors were blocked with anti-CD16/CD32 (clone 93; BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA). Staining for surface markers was performed on ice for 45 min, washed with FACS buffer (2% FBS and 2 mM EDTA in PBS), and fixed/permeabilised using the FOXP3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Finally, the samples were labelled with intracellular and extracellular markers on ice for 45 min. Samples were acquired using a 5-laser Cytek Aurora cytometer (Cytek Biosciences, Fremont, CA, USA) and analysed using FlowJo 10.8.2 software (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA. The antibodies used are provided in supplementary table S5.

## Statistical analysis
For in vitro studies involving primary lung FBs, all quantitative reverse-transcriptase (qRT)-PCR and Western blot analyses were performed using at least three independent experiments (biological replicates), each derived from three independent biological replicates. PCLS experiments were performed using lung sections from three different patients. Each experiment was conducted at least in triplicate to ensure robustness. The results are expressed as mean±sem. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 10.3.0, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), and data are presented as mean±sem. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05. The Shapiro–Wilk test was first conducted to assess data normality. For comparisons between two independent groups, an unpaired t-test was used for normally distributed data, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was applied for nonnormally distributed data. When comparing more than two groups, a one-way ANOVA was performed if data met normality assumptions, followed by Tukey's multiple comparisons test for pairwise comparisons or Dunnett's test when comparing against a control group. For nonnormally distributed data, the Kruskal–Wallis test was used, followed by Dunn's post hoc test for multiple comparisons. To assess the effects of two independent variables, a two-way ANOVA was performed, with post hoc tests conducted when significant interactions were detected.

## Results

## Epac1 expression is upregulated in the lungs of patients with IPF and mice challenged with BLM
To determine the contribution of Epac proteins to PF pathogenesis, we first examined Epac1 expression in the lungs of patients with IPF (65±10 years old, n=8) and matched healthy control donors (62±10 years old, n=8). We found that Epac1 mRNA expression level was higher in the IPF lungs compared to healthy control donors ( ), whereas Epac2 expression did not change (supplementary figure S1a). Additionally, co-immunostaining showed that ɑ-smooth muscle actin-positive (ɑ-SMA+) FBs (green) strongly expressed Epac1 (red) in fibrotic areas and fibroblastic foci in IPF lung samples compared with healthy controls ( and supplementary figure S1b). Similarly, increased Epac1 protein levels were observed in hyperproliferating FBs isolated from the lungs of IPF patients (IPF-FBs) expressing α-SMA and cyclin D1 compared to normal human lung fibroblasts (NHL-FBs) ( and supplementary figure S1c). Finally, Epac1 expression was also upregulated in the BLM mouse model of PF, following a single intratracheal instillation of BLM (4 U·kg−1) in mice, after 28 days (supplementary figure S1d, e). These data suggest that increased Epac1 expression is a feature of PF and led us to investigate the role of Epac1 in PF development.
 

## Epac1 regulates lung FB growth and activation
Lung FBs are central mediators of pathological fibrotic accumulation in the ECM, and their proliferation occurs in response to prolonged injury and inflammation [ ]. We first used a gain-of-function approach using an adenovirus encoding Epac1 (Ad.Epac1) to assess the effects of Epac1 overexpression on proliferation and profibrotic gene expression in TGF-β1-activated NHL-FBs. As expected, Ad.Epac1 induced Epac1 overexpression without affecting Epac2 expression (supplementary figure 2a, b). Interestingly, Epac1 overexpression potentiated NHL-FB proliferation cultured in high concentration serum (10% S) ( ). Moreover, Epac1 overexpression potentiated TGF-β1 mRNA levels in TGF-β1-stimulated NHL-FBs ( ), and upregulated the expression of profibrotic genes, including collagen type 1 ɑ1 (COL1A1), collagen type 3 ɑ1 (COL3A1) and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) ( ). These data suggest that Epac1 is a key regulator of lung FB growth and fibrotic pathway activation in response to stress stimuli.
Next, NHL-FBs were stimulated with the Epac1-specific agonist 8-CPT to confirm these results. NHL-FB stimulation with 8-CPT promoted the expression of TGF-β1 and profibrotic genes, such as COL1A1, COL3A1 and CTGF ( ). Markedly, the addition of TGF-β1 significantly potentiated the effects of 8-CPT on fibrosis-related gene expression ( ). Finally, we validated these results using the shRNA-mediated Epac1 KD (shEpac1) approach in NHL-FBs, which effectively reduced Epac1 expression without affecting Epac2 expression ( and supplementary figure S2c, respectively). Surprisingly, Epac1 KD reduced TGF-β1 ( ) and COL1A1, COL3A1 and CTGF mRNA levels ( ) in NHLF-FBs stimulated with TGF-β1. Remarkably, IPF-FBs exhibited higher Epac1 mRNA levels compared to NHL-FBs ( ). Additionally, Epac1 KD reduced the proliferative activity of both NHL-FBs and IPF-FBs cultured in 10% S ( ). Notably, α-SMA and IL-6 mRNA levels were reduced in Epac1-depleted IPF-FBs and NHL-FBs ( , n). Altogether, these data demonstrate that Epac1 regulates the growth and activation of primary human lung FBs.

## Selective pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 with AM-001 blocks lung FB proliferation and the expression of profibrotic markers in vitro
Inhibition of Epac1 activity with the AM-001 compound (supplementary figure S3a) effectively prevented cAMP-induced Epac1 activation in IPF-FBs, as measured with an Epac1-based bioluminescence resonance energy transfer sensor ( and supplementary figure S3b). Remarkably, AM-001 treatment selectively reduced Epac1 mRNA levels in IPF-FBs, while having no effect on nonactivated, non-TGF-β1-stimulated NHL-FBs (supplementary figure S3c). Notably, AM-001 significantly repressed NHL-FB proliferation induced by a high concentration of serum and TGF-β1 ( ), as well as profibrotic markers and TGF-β1 upregulated TGF-β1 mRNA expression ( ). We next knocked down Epac2 expression using a small interfering RNA (siRNA) approach and confirmed efficient Epac2 KD on Epac2 at the mRNA and protein expression level compared to IPF-FBs transfected with control siRNA (supplementary figure S3d, e). By contrast, Epac2 KD did not affect Epac1 mRNA levels (supplementary figure S3f). We further investigated the specificity of Epac1 inhibition by treating Epac2-depleted IPF-FBs with AM-001. While IPF-FBs exhibited elevated mRNA levels of profibrotic markers (TGF-β1, COL1A1, COL3A1 and CTGF) relative to control FBs, siRNA-mediated KD of Epac2 alone did not significantly alter their expression (supplementary figure S3g). Consistent with Epac1-dependent effects, AM-001 treatment significantly reduced profibrotic gene expression in control siRNA-transfected IPF-FBs but failed to exert additional suppression in siEpac2-depleted cells (supplementary figure S3g). These findings confirm that the antifibrotic effects of AM-001 are specifically mediated through Epac1. These results further support the specific effects of the AM-001 inhibitor toward Epac1. Additionally, we tested the effect of CE3F4, another pharmacological inhibitor of Epac1 [ ], and, as expected, we found that CE3F4 mimicked the inhibitor effects of AM-001 on NHL-FB proliferation and the fibrosis markers (supplementary figure S3h–j). These findings further confirm that selective pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 effectively prevents the activation and proliferation of lung FBs.
 

## Characterisation of the Epac1 transcriptome involved in profibrotic pathways in NHL-FBs
To identify Epac1-regulated genes, we performed bulk RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) on three experimental groups of NHL-FBs ( ): 1) Control group: NHL-FBs stimulated with TGF-β1 to mimic profibrotic activation, serving as the baseline reference; 2) AM-001 treatment group: NHL-FBs stimulated with TGF-β1 for 48 h in the presence of the Epac1-selective inhibitor AM-001; and 3) Epac1 KD group (shEpac1): NHL-FBs infected with Epac1-targeting shRNA for 72 h, followed by stimulation with TGF-β1 for 48 h. A total of 166 and 182 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between the control and activated NHL-FBs treated with shEpac1 or AM-001, respectively ( ). More specifically, the expression of 62 genes was significantly upregulated, whereas that of 120 genes was downregulated in AM-001 NHL-FB-treated cells ( ). Comparative analysis of DEGs identified genes commonly upregulated and downregulated by both Epac1 inhibition strategies (pharmacological and genetic). Additionally, the analysis revealed distinct gene expression patterns specific to each inhibition method, highlighting unique transcriptomic signatures in activated NHL-FBs treated with AM-001 or shEpac1 ( , supplementary figure S4a, b and supplementary table S6). Reactome and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed on significantly DEGs identified in both activated NHL-FBs treated with AM-001 and shEpac1-depleted NHL-FBs stimulated with TGF-β1 to identify key biological pathways regulated by Epac1 inhibition. This dual approach aimed to consistently identify DEGs regulated by Epac1 inhibition, enhancing the reliability of our findings and minimising potential nonspecific effects associated with either method alone. As anticipated, the results of the Gene Ontology (GO) and Reactome pathway analysis indicated that critical gene signatures predominantly relate to processes such as ECM and extracellular structure organisation. These processes include collagen synthesis, ECM organisation, and involvement in fibroblast growth factor receptor 1 (FGFR1), tenascin C (TNC) and TGF-β signalling and the neddylation pathway ( and supplementary figure S4a, b). Furthermore, the KEGG pathway analysis showed that the downregulated DEGs were strongly associated with pathways, including the proteasome and PI3K-AKT signalosome ( ). As predicted by gene set enrichment analysis, most genes with downregulated expression following Epac1 inhibition were associated with collagen formation, biosynthesis and trimerisation ( and supplementary figure S4a, b). Next, we analysed our RNA-seq dataset ( ) using the ENCODE and ChIP Enrichment Analysis libraries to identify the top 50 transcription factors potentially regulating the Epac1-associated transcriptome. Notably, FoxO3a emerged as the top-ranked transcription factor (supplementary figure S4a). FoxO3a has previously been shown to attenuate lung fibrosis by suppressing FB activation and reducing ECM deposition [ ]. Previous studies have shown that FoxO3a is under the control of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway and is implicated in the differentiation of lung FBs and the hyperproliferation phenotype [ , ]. Consistent with these studies, we found that Epac1 overexpression reduced FoxO3a mRNA levels in NHL-FBs (supplementary figure S4c). In contrast, Epac1 silencing restored FoxO3a expression in IPF-FBs, while having no effect on FoxO3a expression in nonactivated NHL-FBs (supplementary figure S4d).

## Epac1 inhibition with AM-001 prevents TGF-β1-induced profibrotic STAT3, SMAD2/3 and AKT signalling in NHL-FBs and IPF-FBs
Treatment of IPF-FBs with the TGF-β1 receptor inhibitor SB431542 significantly reduced Epac1 mRNA levels compared to the nonactivated or non-TGF-β1-stimulated IPF-FBs ( ), confirming that TGF-β1 induced Epac1 expression in IPF-FBs. In addition, AM-001 exerted a strong antiproliferative effect on serum-induced IPF-FB proliferation ( ).
 Next, we evaluated the effects of AM-001 on TGF-β1 transcripts in IPF-FBs. As expected, we observed that TGF-β1 mRNA levels were higher in IPF-FBs than in NHL-FBs. Interestingly, AM-001 treatment abolished this increase without affecting basal TGF-β1 levels in non-stimulated NHL-FBs ( ). Taken together, these findings suggest that AM-001-mediated reduction of TGF-β1 levels may play a role in regulating Epac1 expression. Previous studies have identified lung epithelial cells as a major source of TGF-β1 production, and their interactions with FBs through this signalling pathway are thought to contribute significantly to IPF disease development and progression [ , ]. To further investigate whether TGF-β mediates Epac1 expression in IPF-FBs, we cultured IPF-FBs in conditioned media from TGF-β1-activated healthy human small airway epithelial cells. The results demonstrated a significant increase in Epac1 protein expression compared to non-stimulated cells (supplementary figure S4e, f), suggesting an epithelial–FB crosstalk in Epac1 regulation and a reciprocal regulatory relationship and feedback response between TGF-β levels and Epac1. Notably, this effect was reduced by AM-001 treatment of IPF-FBs compared to vehicle-treated cells (supplementary figure S4e, f). Furthermore, AM-001 treatment significantly reduced the mRNA expression levels of α-SMA and IL-6 in IPF-FBs ( , e). Consequently, the protein levels of phosphorylated STAT3 (p-STAT3), SMAD2/3 (p-SMAD2/3) and AKT (p-AKT) were markedly decreased in AM-001-treated IPF-FBs, indicating the suppression of key fibrotic signalling pathways ( , g). Our results indicate that Epac1 concurrently activates key pro-inflammatory and profibrotic signalling pathways, including IL-6/STAT3, TGF-β/SMAD2/3 and AKT signalling, in IPF-FBs.

## AM-001 blocks endogenous FoxO3a degradation through neddylation in IPF-FBs
Neddylation-like ubiquitination is a reversible post-translational modification that plays a crucial role in controlling substrate degradation and is catalysed by the successive enzymatic cascade of NEDD8-activating enzyme E1 (NAE1), NEDD8-conjugating enzyme E2 and substrate-specific NEDD8-E3 ligases [ – ]. Based on our RNA-seq analysis, we identified neddylation-related genes and FoxO3a as being up- and downregulated in NHL-FBs, respectively ( –i). Next, we analysed the neddylation pathway in IPF lung samples and found that ubiquitin conjugating enzyme E2 M (UBE2M) and NEDD8 mRNA expression were significantly increased, whereas NAE1 expression remained unchanged ( ). Conversely, FoxO3a mRNA levels were significantly reduced in the lungs of IPF patients ( ). Of note, we observed an abundant NEDD8 protein expression (red) in IPF fibrotic lung tissues co-stained with the myofibroblast marker α-SMA (green), while FoxO3a (red) protein expression was reduced ( , respectively, and supplementary figure S5a, b). We next confirmed that UBE2M and NEDD8 protein levels were significantly augmented, while FoxO3a protein level was reduced in IPF-FBs compared to NHL-FBs ( ). Further, we examined the effects of AM-001 on the neddylation pathway and FoxO3a levels in AM-001-treated IPF-FBs. Among the neddylation-related genes, NAE1, NEDD8 and UBE2M mRNA levels in IPF-FBs were significantly diminished by AM-001 ( ), as well as UBE2F and UBA3 transcripts (supplementary figure S6a, b). Conversely, FoxO3a mRNA expression was restored in AM-001-treated IPF-FBs ( ). Similar reductions in neddylation-related gene expression were observed in Epac1-depleted IPF-FBs, although the decrease in UBE2F levels was not significant (supplementary figure S6c–g). Consistently, we found that TGF-β1 treatment of NHL-FB increased both FoxO3a phosphorylation at Thr32 (pFoxO3aThr32) and active form of NEDD8 levels ( ), as well as the UBE2M level (supplementary figure S6h), while the total protein level of FoxO3a was reduced ( ). In IPF-FBs, the basal levels of pFoxO3aThr32 and the active form of NEDD8 were augmented, accompanied by reduced FoxO3a expression ( ). These findings suggest that activated NEDD8 may promote FoxO3a neddylation and degradation of the pFoxO3aThr32 form, and that Epac1 inhibition may reverse this effect, thereby preserving FoxO3a expression.
 To validate this assumption, we examined whether the ubiquitin-like protein NEDD8 was involved in the degradation of FoxO3a proteins in the context of IPF. As expected, UBE2M (supplementary figure S6h) and the activated form of NEDD8 protein levels were increased in NHL-FBs stimulated with TGF-β1 and in IPF-FBs ( ), whereas AM-001 reversed this effect by reducing NEDD8 and restoring total FoxO3a levels in IPF-FBs ( ). In addition, we found that a selective inhibitor of NEDD8-activating enzyme E, MLN4924 (pevonedistat), mimicked the effect of AM-001 on the expression of profibrotic markers in IPF-FBs ( ). Importantly, co-treatment of IPF-FBs with MLN4924 and AM-001 reduced pFoxO3aThr32 levels and restored total FoxO3a expression. These findings suggest that pFoxO3aThr32 degradation is regulated through NEDD8-dependent interaction and neddylation ( ). Consistently, we observed that haemagglutinin (HA)-tagged NEDD8 interacts with endogenous FoxO3a in IPF-FBs pretreated with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 ( ), while this interaction was prevented by the addition of AM-001 ( ). Interestingly, the inhibition of NEDD activation with MLN4924 in Epac1-activated IPF-FBs (8-CPT treatment) partially disrupted the NEDD8–FoxO3a interaction, though not as effectively as AM-001 ( ). These findings suggest that Epac1 promotes NEDD8 expression, leading to FoxO3a neddylation and subsequent degradation in IPF-FBs, whereas AM-001 treatment reverses this effect, stabilising FoxO3a expression ( ). Overall, our results suggest that the proteasome-mediated degradation of FoxO3a is regulated by the NAE1–NEDD8 axis in IPF-FBs.
TGF-β regulates the FGFR signalling cascade and upregulates the TNC-related pathway in lung FBs [ – ]. Both FGFR1 and TNC contribute to ECM remodelling and fibrotic progression and are significantly upregulated in human IPF lung samples compared to healthy lung tissue (supplementary figure S7a, b). Given their established roles in fibrosis, we investigated the impact of Epac1 inhibition on these pathways. TNC, highly expressed in fibrotic lung tissue, particularly within fibroblastic foci, is induced by TGF-β1, reinforcing its role in fibrotic remodelling. Similarly, FGFR1 activation in IPF promotes FB proliferation and ECM deposition. Our transcriptomic analysis identified TNC and FGFR1 as key targets of Epac1 inhibition, prompting further validation. Indeed, FGFR1 and TNC mRNA expression levels were significantly reduced in AM-001-treated or Epac1-depleted IPF-FBs, confirming the regulatory role of Epac1 in these fibrotic pathways (supplementary figure S8a–d).

## Epac1 deficiency protects mice from BLM-induced PF
Next, we sought to determine the impact of Epac1 genetic depletion on PF development following BLM administration. In mice, BLM aerosolisation induces lung injury, leading to a fibroproliferative response and increased production of profibrotic cytokines, such as TGF-β and IL-6 [ , , ]. To assess this, global Epac1 KO mice (12–14 weeks old) and littermate wild-type (WT) mice were randomly assigned to receive a single intratracheal instillation of BLM (4 U·kg−1) and were evaluated 28 days post-treatment ( ). We first validated the successful deletion of Epac1 in Epac1 KO mice, as evidenced by a significant reduction in Epac1 mRNA and protein expression in the lungs, with no compensatory changes in Epac2 levels ( ). BLM treatment specifically upregulated Epac1 expression in the lungs of WT mice, while Epac2 levels remained unchanged (supplementary figure S9a, b). Remarkably, hallmarks of PF formation, such as ECM deposition, Ashcroft histopathology, TGF-β1 and fibrotic markers, were all significantly reduced in BLM-treated Epac1 KO mice compared with BLM-treated WT mice ( –f).
 At the molecular level, NEDD8 mRNA and protein expression levels assessed by qRT-PCR and immunostaining were significantly decreased compared to BLM-treated WT mice ( , h and supplementary figure S9c). Remarkably, FoxO3a mRNA levels were higher in BLM-treated Epac1-KO mice compared to BLM-treated WT mice ( ). The impact of Epac1 deficiency on FoxO3a protein expression was further confirmed by immunofluorescence staining ( and supplementary figure S9d). Finally, we quantified the active form of NEDD8 and pFoxO3aThr32 and p-SMAD2/3 levels by immunoblotting and observed significantly elevated NEDD8 protein levels as well as pFoxO3aThr32 and p-SMAD2/3 in BLM-treated WT mice but lower in BLM-treated Epac1 KO mice ( , l). Consistently, FGFR1 and TNC mRNA expression levels were downregulated in BLM-treated Epac1 KO mice (supplementary figure S9e, f). Consistent with our in vitro findings, IL-6 mRNA expression was significantly reduced in the lungs of Epac1 KO mice challenged with BLM compared to WT mice, further confirming the contribution of Epac1 to IL-6-mediated profibrotic signalling in vivo (supplementary figure S9g). Taken together, these results demonstrate that Epac1 is critical in lung fibrosis development, and its inactivation provides significant protection against PF.

## Therapeutic effect of AM-001 on PF progression
We first evaluated the effect of AM-001 on WT control mice. The control mice were randomly assigned to receive either a vehicle or AM-001 (10 mg·kg−1) via intraperitoneal injection every other day for 14 days. The treatment with AM-001 did not result in significant weight loss and had no notable impact on lung fibrosis or collagen content, as assessed by Masson's trichrome staining and hydroxyproline content, respectively (supplementary figure S10A–C). Additionally, the mRNA expression levels of Epac1, Epac2, the profibrotic markers, neddylation pathway markers and FoxO3a remained unchanged in the lungs of AM-001-treated mice in comparison to the vehicle-treated animals (supplementary figure S10D–I). Moreover, analyses of the effects of AM-001 on the right ventricle revealed no significant differences in Epac1 and Epac2 mRNA expression levels between the AM-001-treated and vehicle-treated groups. Similarly, right ventricular (RV) hypertrophy and remodelling, as assessed by the Fulton Index, along with the expression of hypertrophy and profibrotic RV mRNA markers, remained unchanged following AM-001 treatment (supplementary figure S10j–n). Next, we tested the therapeutic effects of AM-001 in a murine model of BLM-induced PF. BLM-challenged mice (28 days post-instillation) were randomly assigned to receive either vehicle or AM-001 (10 mg·kg−1) via, intraperitoneal injection every other day for 14 days ( ). Markedly, AM-001 treatment reduced fibrotic lesions and lowered hydroxyproline content in BLM-treated mice compared to vehicle-treated controls ( , c). Additionally, AM-001 treatment markedly decreased the mRNA expression levels of profibrotic markers ( ), indicating AM-001 in vivo antifibrotic effects. Remarkably, AM-001 treatment reversed the BLM-induced effects by significantly decreasing the mRNA expression levels of NAE1, UBE2M and NEDD8 ( ). Consistently, co-immunostaining and immunoblotting analyses revealed that AM-001 lowered NEDD8 expression and restored FoxO3a ( –h; supplementary figure S11a, b). Additionally, AM-001 reduced Epac1 expression and decreased p-FoxO3a and p-SMAD2/3 levels in the lungs of BLM-treated mice, compared to vehicle-treated controls ( –h). Furthermore, AM-001 reduced FGFR1 expression and tended to decrease TNC mRNA levels (supplementary figure S11c, d). Importantly, IL-6 mRNA expression levels were significantly reduced in the lungs of BLM-treated mice following AM-001 treatment, further confirming the inhibitory effects of Epac1 blockade on IL-6 signalling in vivo (supplementary figure S11e).
 Next, we examined the effect of AM-001 on pulmonary vascular remodelling secondary to BLM-induced PF [ ]. Compared to vehicle-treated BLM mice, AM-001 significantly reversed distal pulmonary artery vascular remodelling, as assessed by morphometric analysis (supplementary figure S12). Additionally, AM-001 improved RV function and alleviated RV remodelling, as evidenced by decreased RV systolic pressure and reduced RV hypertrophy, as indicated by a lower Fulton Index and the downregulation of hypertrophic genes (supplementary figure S13a–c). Finally, histological analysis showed that AM-001 treatment reduced RV fibrosis and cardiomyocyte size, compared to the vehicle-treated group (supplementary figure S13d–g). Taken together, these findings demonstrate that Epac1 inhibition by AM-001 effectively reduces PF progression and subsequent RV remodelling.

## AM-001 as a modulator of immune lung cells in BLM-treated animals
To decipher the mechanism underlying the inhibition of BLM-induced PF mediated by AM-001, we performed spectral flow cytometry to analyse immune cell populations in the lungs of control and BLM-treated mice following the administration of AM-001. We evaluated the main leukocyte populations, including myeloid and T-cells. The analysis revealed a distinct pattern of immune cell composition between BLM-induced fibrosis and AM-001 nonfibrotic mice ( and supplementary figure S14).
 The immune cell composition of the lungs showed a decrease in the percentage of neutrophils and macrophages following AM-001 treatment ( ). This is consistent with previous findings suggesting that the depletion of macrophages using clodronate-loaded liposomes in BLM-induced PF in mice suppresses the pathology of the disease [ ]. Looking into the distinct macrophage subsets in the lungs, we observed that, while alveolar macrophages were markedly reduced in BLM-treated mice independent of the AM-001 administration, there was a marked increase in the number of recruited macrophages ( ). Interestingly, the percentage of recruited macrophages was significantly decreased in the AM-001-treated group, indicating that the 3-week treatment after the induction of PF had a marked effect on lung infiltration by monocyte-derived cells. Further characterisation of recruited macrophages showed that BLM mice exhibited significantly higher percentages of proliferating M2 macrophages compared to AM-001-treated and control groups ( ). In addition, we observed a significant upregulation in the expression of major histocompatibility complex class II (MHC-II) molecules in M2 macrophages from fibrotic mice, facilitating the interaction with T-cells and promoting cytokine secretion. These results suggest that, mechanistically, AM-001 prevents PF in part by limiting the proliferative capacity of lung-infiltrating monocytes ( ).
Characterisation of the T-cell-mediated immune response in the lungs revealed a significant increase in the percentages of both CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells in the lungs of BLM-treated mice ( ). However, tissue-resident CD4+ T-cells were significantly reduced in the AM-001-treated group, with similar cell percentages as control nonfibrotic mice. Further evaluation of the T-cell function indicated an overall reduction of the activation marker CD25 that achieved significant differences in CD4+ T-cells ( ). This suggests that AM-001 treatment modulates the T-cell response by reducing the ability of CD4+ T-cells to respond to effector cytokines. Because CD25 controls T-cell proliferation and differentiation in T-cells, we next evaluated the expression of the Ki67 proliferation marker. Our results indicate that AM-001 treatment significantly reduced the proliferative capacity of CD4+ T-cells. Further phenotypic characterisation revealed the upregulation of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) by CD4+ T-cells in the lungs of BLM-treated mice ( , e). PD-1 is an inhibitory receptor expressed by T-cells during activation that is associated with fibrotic lung disease in humans, and its upregulation in CD4+ T-cells has been associated with PF following BLM administration in mice [ ].
Together, these findings demonstrate that AM-001 treatment modulates the immune landscape of BLM-induced fibrotic lungs by reducing neutrophil and macrophage infiltration, limiting the expansion of proliferating M2 macrophages and attenuating monocyte-derived macrophage recruitment. Additionally, AM-001 suppresses T-cell activation and proliferation, contributing to its immunomodulatory and antifibrotic effects.

## AM-001 treatment reverses fibrosis in an ex vivo IPF human-derived PCLS model
Human PCLS, three-dimensional tissue explants, provide a representative ex vivo platform model with preserved tissue lung architecture and viability in culture [ ]. Therefore, we evaluated the potential therapeutic effects of AM-001 in human IPF disease mechanisms, Epac1 downstream signalling and the expression of fibrotic markers in IPF-PCLS ( ). IPF human-derived PCLS remained viable after 10 days of AM-001 treatment, as assessed by MTT assay (supplementary figure 15a). Markedly, the fibrotic lesions were evaluated using Masson's trichrome staining, which revealed a significant reduction in collagen deposition in AM-001-treated PCLS compared to the vehicle-treated group ( ). Additionally, quantification of fibrosis severity using the Ashcroft scoring system showed a marked decrease in ECM deposition in AM-001-treated PCLS ( ), indicating its antifibrotic effects in human-derived lung fibrosis tissue. Additionally, AM-001 reduced NEDD8 protein expression (red) in IPF human-derived PCLS, as observed in co-staining with α-SMA (green). Conversely, FoxO3a (red) was restored in AM-001-treated PCLS ( , e), further supporting its antifibrotic effects. Subsequently, IPF human-derived PCLS treated with AM-001 showed lower protein levels of the fibrotic protein markers α-SMA, fibronectin, matrix metallopeptidase 2, type 1 collagen (COL1), type 3 collagen (COL3) and total SMAD2/3, a trend reduction of NEDD8 and p-FoxO3a, and higher total FoxO3a protein levels compared to vehicle-treated IPF human-derived PCLS ( , g). Overall, our data suggest that AM-001, by inhibiting Epac1, exerts pleiotropic effects on IPF-FBs, effectively suppressing FB activation and ECM deposition. AM-001 reduces NEDD8 expression, preventing FoxO3a degradation, while simultaneously blocking TGF-β1/SMAD2/3, IL-6/STAT3 and p-AKT signalling, thereby attenuating inflammation, fibrosis progression and lung remodelling in IPF. Our findings identify AM-001 as a promising therapeutic approach for PF ( ).
 

## Discussion
PF is primarily driven by the pathological remodelling of lung tissue, where increased TGF-β levels prompt FBs to differentiate into myofibroblasts, enhancing matrix production and deposition [ – ]. However, the role of Epac1 in PF remains elusive, and previous literature has reported conflicting results, possibly influenced by varying experimental stimuli [ – ].
Our data indicate that Epac1 plays a crucial role in profibrotic gene expression and FB activation and proliferation in PF. These findings suggest that targeting Epac1 with AM-001 may provide a novel therapeutic strategy to reduce fibrosis and FB-driven disease progression. Moreover, accumulating evidence suggests that Epac1 plays an important role in airway inflammation and cell proliferation [ , ]. Huang
et al. [ ] demonstrated that, while prostaglandin-mediated inhibition of collagen expression in primary human fetal lung FBs was exclusively mediated by PKA, Epac1 was responsible for inhibiting fetal FB proliferation. However, there are some controversies regarding the role of Epac1 in cell proliferation, migration and fibrosis [ , , , ]. Depending on the cell type and age, and its differentiation state, some studies have reported an inhibitory role [ , ]. Other studies have shown opposite results [ ]; thus, Epac1 appears to be critical for the integration of pro- and antifibrotic signals and the regulation of FB function depending on stress stimuli [ ]. Interestingly, Epac1 and PKA mediate the opposing effects of cAMP on PKB/AKT regulation, which may provide a potential mechanism for the differential cell type-specific effects of cAMP [ ]. Therefore, the apparent cellular effects of cAMP may vary depending on the relative cellular abundance and compartmentalisation of Epac1 and PKA, as well as stress inducers. While our study primarily focuses on the role of Epac1 in PF, we acknowledge the complex interplay between the cAMP-PKA and cAMP-Epac1 signalling pathways in regulating fibrotic processes. The balance between these pathways appears to be context-dependent, with PKA often exerting opposing or compensatory effects when Epac1 signalling is inhibited. Although we did not directly assess PKA activation in response to Epac1 inhibition, our findings align with prior studies suggesting that the relative activation of PKA and Epac1 may fine-tune fibrosis progression depending on microenvironmental cues. Future studies exploring the compensatory role of PKA in the absence of Epac1 will be crucial to fully delineate the mechanistic contributions of cAMP-mediated signalling in PF. Additional studies will be needed to determine whether Epac1 inhibition affects the expression or activity of specific phosphodiesterase (PDE) isoforms in FBs, potentially altering cAMP compartmentalisation and signalling. Indeed, cAMP presents the particularity of being highly regulated from its production at the membrane to the activation of its intracellular targets. This modulation of cAMP signalling involves a large family of PDEs, which catalyse the hydrolysis of cAMP into 5′-AMP [ ]. PDEs are key actors in limiting the spread of cAMP and appear critical for the formation of dynamic microdomains that confer specific responses to various signals [ ]. Interestingly, the pharmacological inhibition of PDE4 subtypes increases intracellular levels of cAMP and decreases fibrosis, the release of pro-inflammatory molecules and recruitment of inflammatory cells in the lung, suggesting PDE4 as a potential target for the treatment of IPF [ ]. With regards this, nerandomilast (also named BI 1015550), a selective and preferential PDE4B inhibitor, displays both anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects in preclinical models of lung fibrosis [ ]. In addition, similar to AM-001, nerandomilast inhibits TGF-β1-induced myofibroblast transformation and the expression of ECM proteins and fibroblast growth factor in lung FBs from patients with IPF [ ]. Phase 3 clinical trials are under investigation in patients with IPF and other types of progressive PF [ , ].
At first glance, the profibrotic effect of Epac1 activation contradicts the protective effect of nerandomilast in IPF. Indeed, one could speculate that nerandomilast, which reduces the activity of PDE4B and thus increases intracellular levels of cAMP, could promote Epac1 activation and its deleterious effect in lung cells. Accumulating evidence indicates that Epac1 protein signalling is highly compartmentalised and exerts its biological function in combination with scaffolding proteins such as β-arrestin, PKA and PDEs [ ]. Depending on the composition of the Epac1 signalosome, this cAMP-binding protein mediates a specific cellular effect. For instance, PDE4D5 recruitment to the β2-adrenergic receptor by β-arrestin prevents cardiomyocyte hypertrophy through a pathway involving inhibition of Epac1 and Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II [ ]. At present, it is still unknown whether PDE4B influences Epac1 activity in lung cells. Further investigation is needed to understand if PDE4B is part of the Epac1 signalling pathway in basal and disease conditions.
Compelling evidence indicates that Epac1 inhibition may be an effective approach for treating cancer and cardiovascular diseases [ ]. Importantly, Epac1 KO mice exhibit decreased cardiac fibrosis and improved cardiac function under stress conditions such as chronic isoproterenol infusion. In line with this, we showed here that Epac1 KO mice are protected against BLM-induced PF. Therefore, the identification of pharmacological modulators against Epac1 is important for elucidating the role of Epac1 in disease conditions [ ]. AM-001 selectively inhibits Epac1 catalytic activity in cultured cells [ ]. A recent study has shown that AM-001 exerts cardioprotective effects against myocardial ischaemia/reperfusion injury and prevents chronic β-adrenergic activation, induced cardiac inflammation and fibrosis [ ]. Nonetheless, the precise role of AM-001 Epac1 inhibitor in lung diseases and PF has not been explored and remains unclear. In this study, mechanistically, AM-001 targets key pathways involved in IPF, such as the TGF-β-SMAD2/3, IL-6/STAT3 and phospho-AKT/pFoxO3a signalling pathways. We found that Epac1 expression was increased in BLM-treated WT mice and that AM-001 treatment reduced Epac1/TGF-β1, collagen and IL-6 gene expression and signalling, which could be related to FoxO3a pathway activation and TGF-β signalling suppression, thus reversing lung fibrosis. FoxO3a has been shown to play a key role in fibrogenesis, and its expression is reduced in IPF [ ]. FoxO3a, a target of PKB/AKT via phosphorylation that results in its cytoplasmic accumulation and subsequent degradation, has been implicated in the differentiation and hyperproliferation of lung FB [ , ]. Additionally, FoxO3a KO mice display enhanced susceptibility to BLM and increased fibrosis and mortality [ ]. We validated this result and showed that FoxO3a expression is downregulated in IPF patients and animal models of PF, while FoxO3a phosphorylation is increased.
Neddylation is a reversible and important post-translational modification that targets protein degradation [ ]. This process involves NAE (a heterodimer comprising subunits NAE1 and UBA3), NEDD8-conjugating enzyme E2 (UBC12) and substrate-specific E3s [ – ]. NEDD8 is first adenylated and activated by E1-NAE, then NEDD8-loaded NAE is transferred to one of the two NEDD8-conjugating enzymes, UBE2M or UBE2F, through a trans-thiolation reaction [ , ]. However, the effects of NEDD8-mediated neddylation and degradation of FoxO3a and its function in PF remain unclear. Notably, the expression and phosphorylation of FoxO3a by Epac1 via AKT activation were inhibited by AM-001 and the NEDD8 inhibitor MLN4924. These findings suggest that NEDD8 is involved in FoxO3a neddylation and subsequent degradation. NEDD8 targets proteins and affects their stability, subcellular localisation, conformation, and function [ ]. It has been reported that FoxO3a is regulated predominantly by post-translational modifications, including phosphorylation and ubiquitination [ ]. Consistently, we showed that NEDD8 interacts with FoxO3a and may trigger its subsequent proteasomal degradation. Additionally, MLN4924 and AM-001 suppressed these effects and subsequently, the expression of profibrotic markers. The combination of MLN4924 and AM-001 potentiated this effect. Our findings indicate that pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 and thus the neddylation pathway effectively inhibits IPF-FB activation and the PF phenotype. While ourstudy, primarily focused on FBs, future investigations into the effects of Epac1 inhibition on lung airway, alveolar and vascular cells will be critical to our understanding of the broader implications of Epac1 signalling in lung homeostasis.
Resident myeloid cells continuously sample the tissue microenvironment that triggers inflammatory responses to tissue injury and trauma and pave the way for the progressive recruitment of circulating myeloid cells, such as neutrophils and inflammatory macrophages [ ], and represent a key factor in many diseases, including IPF [ – ]. Epac1 is expressed in immune and hematopoietic cells, and its activation promotes monocyte adhesion and polarisation and enhances chemokine-induced migration [ ]. Our results indicate that Epac1 inhibition with AM-001 decreased neutrophil and monocyte-derived macrophage infiltration in the lungs, which is associated with a reduced pathology in BLM-induced PF [ ]. Interestingly, AM-001 treatment also reduced the number of proliferating M2 macrophages that facilitate PF progression [ ]. These BLM-induced pathogenic lung macrophages that exhibit increased proliferation rates also express higher levels of MHC-II [ ], which induce cytokine production in CD4+ T-cells [ ]. Epac1 also regulates TGF-β1 signalling in CD4+ T-cells by regulating p-STAT3 and SMAD4 [ ] in a subset of PD-1-expressing CD4+ T-cells [ ]. Our data indicate that AM-001 treatment reduces the number of CD4+PD-1+ T-cells in the lungs. The anti-inflammatory properties of Epac1 inhibitors further emphasise the importance of Epac1 inhibition as a promising new therapeutic strategy for respiratory diseases associated with excessive and persistent inflammatory responses [ , , ].
Finally, for translational novel therapy and to closely mimic in vivo PF conditions and evaluate the effects of AM-001, we applied disease models to ex vivo human-derived IPF-PCLS that conserved the anatomical architecture of the lung tissue microenvironment of the fibrotic lung and ECM. A recent study demonstrated there is a consistent progression of fibrosis throughout tissue culture in IPF-PCLS [ ], marked by an increase in the expression of pro-inflammatory, profibrotic and ECM deposition-related factors. In human-derived IPF-PCLS, AM-001 reduces the progression of fibrotic lesions, collagen deposition and ECM remodelling, as well as myofibroblast activation. Of particular interest, NEDD8 and p-FoxO3a were decreased in AM-001-treated IPF human-derived PCLS compared to the control vehicle. Considering the limitations and variability in clinical presentations among PCLS donors, we cannot definitively determine whether the observed effects reflect the prevention or attenuation of fibrosis. However, our results indicate that AM-001 effectively reduces fibrosis in IPF-PCLS.
Single-cell RNA-seq studies have established an “IPF cell atlas” from IPF lung tissue samples and identified diverse cell populations, including epithelial cells, vascular cells, FB subtypes and immune cells with macrophage subsets, that contribute to the pathological progression of IPF [ – ]. In this study, we primarily focused on IPF-FBs and immune cells to evaluate the role of Epac1 and its specific inhibitor, AM-001, in PF. However, future research should further investigate the cell-specific molecular changes in Epac1, FoxO3a and the neddylation pathway, and the effects of AM-001 across the various cell populations identified in the IPF cell atlas. In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of Epac1 with AM-001 effectively suppresses fibrotic activation in IPF-FBs in vitro, reduces lung fibrosis in vivo and attenuates fibrosis in ex vivo cultured IPF-PCLS. These results provide compelling evidence that targeting the Epac1 pathway could be a viable therapeutic strategy for IPF.
