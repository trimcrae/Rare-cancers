## Abstract
Innumerable pathogens including RNA viruses have catastrophic pandemic propensity, in turn, SARS-CoV-2 infection is highly contagious. Emergence of SARS-CoV-2 variants with high mutation rate additionally codifies infectious ability of virus and arisen clinical imputations to human health. Although, our knowledge of mechanism of virus infection and its impact on host system has been substantially demystified, uncertainties about the emergence of virus are still not fully understood. To date, there are no potentially curative drugs are identified against the viral infection. Even though, drugs are repurposed in the initial period of infection, many are significantly negative in clinical trials. Moreover, the infection is dependent on organ status, co-morbid conditions, variant of virus and geographic region. This review article aims to comprehensively describe the SARS-CoV-2 infection and the impacts in the host cellular system. This review also briefly provides an overview of genome, proteome and metabolome associated risk to infection and the advancement of therapeutics in SARS-CoV-2 infection management.
Keywords: SARS-CoV-2, Variants, Omics, Alternative medicines

## Introduction
Innumerable pathogens including RNA viruses have catastrophic pandemic propensity, in turn, severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus-2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection is highly contagious that has a catastrophic effect on the world’s demographics and resulted in multitudinous deaths worldwide. The SARS-CoV-2 surge emanates airborne respiratory infection spanning across the globe. The outrageous spread of SARS-CoV-2 riveted the World Health Organization (WHO) to proclaim it as a global pandemic on March 11, 2020. In fact, the past outbreaks of mild respiratory diseases manifested as infectious agents and are emerged as consequential health crisis since 1940s ( ). Additionally, severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus (SARS-CoV) ( ) and Middle East Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus (MERS-CoV) outbreaks brought primitive edges to active research on pandemics and overwhelmed the healthcare system. On this view, the membrane enveloped viruses caused such outbreaks opportune for broad-spectrum antiviral targets and ravaged human health ( ).
The viral transmission primarily occurs through a nosocomial route that increases the degree of infection frequency in humans. Moreover, it has been predicted in past pandemics of human CoV (hCoV), indeed, a predominance of transmission detected between person to person and their relatives. Likewise, the nosocomial transmission of hCoV through an infected individual is a common route for high-risk airborne infection, which is predicted as 13–21% and 22–39% in MERS and SARS cases, respectively ( ). These airborne infections also transmit through poor ventilation, which conferred importance of ventilation-dependent infectious aerosols' dilution. Recently, Li et al. described the importance of Wells-Riley equation as the distribution probability in steady state concentrations of infectious particles in an environment evident for rapid spread of SARS-CoV-2 ( , ). Recently, the assessment of SARS-CoV-2 aerosol concentration with the comparison of space-time dependent formula and classical Wells-Riley’s model evidenced for the identification of high-risk individual cases in a non-equilibrium concentration cloud at heterogeneous environment ( , ). Albeit, the concentration, circulation and ventilation conditions of infectious particle of SARS-CoV-2 is dreadful.
The spread of unfamiliar SARS-CoV-2 variants with highly deleterious mutations has been reported lately in several countries. These variants' emergence with high effect mutations codifies additional infectious ability which contributes in virus adaptation and also confers fitness advantages which arisen clinical imputations to human health ( ). Recently, SARS-CoV-2 B.1.1.529 variant, Omicron with 15 mutation in receptor-binding domain that substantially increase the risk of intrinsic severity ( ). Accordingly, surveillance and characterization platforms of genetic and antigenic changes of sets of mutations in variants, and their impact on transmissibility and infectivity correlates to possible updates of emerging variants. At present, SARS-CoV-2 variants lineage nomenclatures have been procured by open access databases, that includes, Global Initiative on Sharing All Influenza Data (GISAID) ( ), Nextstrain, and Pango ( ). In addition, the variants' classification varies by geographic distribution. Hence, WHO has asserted the criteria of variants, which have been designated as variant of interest (VOI), variant of concern (VOC) and variant under monitoring (VUM). At first, the variant with higher transmissibility, severity and immune and therapeutic escape in a community or population with an increasing number of case reports over time is considered as VOI. Second, the variant meets the VOI definition and is associated with intense disease severity, critical clinical illness with the necessity to provide therapeutic measurements designated as VOC. Third, foreseeable risk of suspecting genetic changes in a variant classified as VUM ( ). The spread of the SARS-CoV-2 variant goes beyond a degree of disease severity that is often described by people’s illness, and deaths, which are ambiguous. Consequently, the far-ranging negative impact imparts to human beings is uncertain, and the multitude of effects is foreseeable.
The clinical association of SARS-CoV-2 severity has reported with co-morbid risk factors which are being related with higher mortality purview of age and sex. However, clinical risk factors associated with SARS-CoV-2 severity is not well understood due to variance of infection rate between the age and sex( ). Childrens are less likely to develop severe symptoms than adults, but the infected children usually showed up acute respiratory infections including fever (49.6%) and cough (45.5%) and extra respiratory symptoms such as diarrhea (9.4%) and vomiting (7.3%) ( ). The critical illness of SARS-CoV-2 to cellular system includes multiple organ injury, coagulopathy, and respiratory-related ailment ( , ). Moreover, pathological conditions in patients are prompt to deteriorate immune system. For example, the infection activates both cellular and humoral immunity such as regulation of CD8 + /CD4 + T cell ratios, MHC class I and II molecules and IgM, IgA, and IgG antibodies ( ). Heretofore, the staunch host immune protection against the infection is yet to be known. ( ). The impaired exercise and diffusing levels of carbon monoxide (DLCO) also involved in the ruination of intra-alveolar diffusion pathway ( ). Likewise, the infected individuals are reported with specific pulmonary abnormalities. In recuperated individuals, the persistence of chronic infection develops irreversible lung disease ( ). Therefore, it is necessary for more efficient strategies on (1) screening of pre-symptomatic and asymptomatic individuals (2) prevention of spread and (3) treatment methods. Currently, mathematical models help out comparing strategies, for example, (1) identification of an area or group of individuals uncertain to infection, and (2) determination of the orbit of action for the urgent research ( ). These mathematical modeling improves understanding of complexities of disease ( ). In this review, we discussed the clinical and epidemiological features of infection and countermeasures against the infection.

## Effect of SARS-CoV-2 infection on cellular receptors
The pathogenesis and infectivity of SARS-CoV-2 is dependent on sustainability of virus in harsh cellular surroundings. The functional activity of a virus is basically in three stages; 1) dynamic entry which includes attachment, fusion and uncoating, 2) ability to replicate and translate the essential cues (genome and protein) and 3) exit which includes assembly, maturation and release. The SARS-CoV-2 virus attachment and fusion of host membrane receptor is determined by surface-anchored spike (S) protein of the virus. At initially, the virus surface protein fused with the host membrane receptor, Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2(ACE2) ( ) which has been recognized as the S protein binding receptor (
 ). Other than S protein, several receptors such as arginine-glycine-aspartic (RGD) motif and Dipeptidyl-peptidase 4 (DPP4) also being extensively studied for host interaction. Moreover, the pathogenesis and infectivity of virus accelerated through activating the host clathrin-mediated endocytosis and, likely, endocytic trafficking regulators of SNX27 for internalization ( ). Generally, the human cells have two forms of ACE2; 1) membrane ACE2 (mACE2) and 2) soluble form of ACE2 (sACE2) in which mACE2 has the receptor binding site for S protein. The fusion of S protein to hACE2 expedites higher angiotensin II (Ang II) production and activation of downstream Ang II/AT1R (Angiotensin II receptor type 1) axis and that instigates inflammation in cellular system ( ). Notably, the elevated levels of sACE2 forms have been evidenced in disease severity through increasing the membrane anchored protein, ADAM17′s activity. This cascade of protein interaction increased the cellular susceptibility over SARS-CoV-2 entry. Moreover, there are reports which suggest the correlation of elevated sACE2 levels with their protective effect. These sACE2 inhibits the interaction of S protein with mACE2. The availability of ACE2 increase the fusion of S protein, in contrast, low-level expression of ACE2 on the cell surface has defiance co-morbid conditions such as cardiovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), diabetes and renal disease ( ) (

## Deleterious effect of comorbidities in SARS-CoV-2 patient’s.

Diabetes mellitus

Individuals with diabetes mellitus (DM) are poor at prognostication of SARS-CoV-2 infection due to compromised arms of the immune system. SARS-CoV-2 infection is associated with T-cell exhaustion, it is abnormally differentiated in DM individuals ( ). Reduced levels of pathogen-specific memory CD4 + cells are hostile to humoral immune responses ( ). Numerous studies have reported that ACE2, furin and TMPRSS2 signal axis mediates pathological process of SARS-CoV-2. In SARS-CoV-2, furin cleaves S protein at the furin-cleavage site ( ); notably, plasma furin levels are associated with insulin pro-receptor maturation that reflects levels of active insulin receptors in DM individuals ( ). The immune response dysregulation with atypical receptor regulation could lead to higher SARS-CoV-2 infectivity.

Cardiovascular disease

The mechanism behind the RAS and cardiovascular system is well known, and ACE2 gene disruption resulted in higher angiotensin II that led to cardiac contractility defects ( , ). Patients with cardiovascular risk factors including hypertension, hypotension, and bradycardia have higher mortality risk for SARS-CoV-2 infection ( ). Hypertension patients are prone to viral infection due to innate immune response attenuation, which is critical to viral pathogenesis when compared with non-hypertensives ( ). Also, hypertension patients with SARS-CoV-2 presented with increased high-sensitivity CRP, procalcitonin and IL-6, which indicates enhanced inflammatory response in SARS-CoV-2 infection. Interestingly, patients’ undergone renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system blockers (RAAS blocker) treatment had a less inflammatory profile in comparison with non-angiotensin II receptor blocker/ ACE inhibitors' treatment ( , ).
In one hand, the ubiquitous RGD motif is a binding protein that facilitates cell attachment and entry by interacting with integrin heterodimers, which activates phosphatidylinositol-3 kinase (PI-3 K) or mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK). RGD motifs are either parallel or sequentially bind to integrins, this interaction is feasible to Integrin-mediated viral endocytosis ( ) On the other hand, the host Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP4) or cluster of differentiation 26 (CD26) interacts with the S1 domain of S protein that influences rapid spread of infection. According to clinical reports, endocytosis is a common route for virus infectivity, blocking of clathrin mediated endocytosis reported reducing the viral internalization ( , ). Neuropilin-1 (NPR-1) also serves as a cellular receptor to facilitate virus entry, which increases the virus infectivity through tropism. Mutations in the NPR-1 b1 domain reported preventing NPR-1-S1 interaction ( ) which would be a potential strategy against the infection. Beyond the known host receptors, LDLRAD3 (LDL scavenger-receptor family), TMEM30A (Transmembrane protein), CPLX1 (Member of complexin/synaphin family), CA9 (Transmembrane protein and a tumor marker) and CLEC4G (C-type lectin family) are identified as functional receptors for the SARS-CoV-2 S protein N-terminal domain. These receptors actively mediate the virus entry and infection in an ACE2- independent way ( ).
The innate immune response triggers the first defense line cells such as dendritic cells (DC), macrophages, neutrophils and Natural Killer (NK) cells against the viral entry. The degree of immune cell migration frequency is higher in Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of infected individuals. In comparison, of macrophages and neutrophils percentage in patients reflected higher in mild and myeloid DCs (mDCs), plasmacytoid DCs (pDCs), T and NK cells lower in severe cases ( ). Several reports have confirmed that the innate lymphoid cells are lower in older patients with higher disease severity. In addition, NK cells are reported to significantly decrease in severe cases, hence, NK cell depletion crucial in immune pathogenesis of infection. Moreover, the CD84/NK group 2 member A (NKG2A), a heterodimeric inhibitory receptor expressed by NK cells that bind to non-classical HLA class I molecules and inhibits cytokine secretion. This is how the SARS-CoV-2 infection basically overruled the host immune response ( ). At overall, several SARS-CoV-2 related studies showed the involvement of pathogenesis and associated humoral and cellular immunity. However, the development of effective drug and vaccines requires better understanding of host immune responses and immune evasion.

## Genomic evidences for virus infection and re-infection
The positive-sense ssRNA genome of the SARS-CoV-2 contains a potential repertoire of expressed antigenic proteins. However, the severity of SARS-CoV-2 not only depends on genetic variation in the virus, the genetic predisposition in individuals also increase the infection rate. For example, recent report on Spanish and Italian genome wide analysis of human chromosome, clusters rs11385942 with GA or G insertion-deletion variant and cluster rs657152 A or C SNP at locus 3p21.31 and locus 9q34.2 are found to be related with SARS-CoV-2 induced respiratory failure ( ). Orlova et al. reported the SNP rs657152 association with risk factor is population-specific which are evidenced in Caucasians in Eastern Siberia where the probability of viral load in patients with SNP rs657152 variant is not associated with infectivity ( ). In addition, the locus 3p21.31 comprises SLC6A20, LZTFL1, CCR9, FYCO1, CXCR6, and XCR1 genes that are functionally relevant to susceptibility of SARS-CoV-2 infection. The risk frequency at the locus is reportedly higher in patients receiving mechanical ventilation than supplementation with O2. Though the variants of specific locus that are risk to SARS-CoV-2, frequency of risk alleles may vary among individuals to population ( ).
The meta-analysis on ABO blood group distribution revealed that among patient’s blood group A is highly prone ( ) and blood group O ( , ) have protective effects against infection. Apart from that, amino acid absorption mechanism also alters during the infection which has been reported that sodium-imino acid transporter 1 (SLC6A20) and sodium-dependent neutral amino acid transporter B(0)AT1 (SLC6A19) are highly expressed in intestinal enterocytes and kidney cells. Notably, he SLC6A20 transporters functionally interact with ACE2 for amino acid absorption ( ). In addition, the genetic variation associated with immunodeficiency in an individual could also increase the chance of SARS-CoV-2 infection. Van Der Made et al. reported that a rare genetic variation in X-chromosomal TLR7 in young male patients shafts down-regulation of downstream of type I interferon signaling pathway and these genetic predisposition contracts to SARS-CoV-2 infection ( ). Ideally, analysis of specific genetic variants highlights association of individual's disease severity. However, to what extent the degree of specific genetic factors implies predisposition of individuals, and thus risk to SARS-CoV-2 infection is not fully understood.

## Protein evidence of infection
Approximately 30-kb genome of SARS-CoV-2 encodes 14 open-reading frames (ORF) in which ORF-1a and ORF1ab encodes polyproteins and are auto-proteolytically processed to 16 non-structural proteins (NSP1-NSP16) that configures replicase-transcriptase complex (RTC), consists multiple enzymes. Papain like protease (NSP3), protease (NSP5), NSP7-NSP8 primase complex, Primary RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (NSP12), a helicase triphosphatase (NSP13), an exoribonuclease (NSP14), endonuclease (NSP15) and N7 and 2′O- methyltransferases (NSP10 and NSP16) have reported to composed RTC. 13 ORFs are expressed at the 3′ end of the genome that includes four structural proteins such as spike (S), envelope (E), membrane (M) and nucleocapsid (N) and 9 putative accessory proteins ( ). The transmembrane S protein is a 150 kDa class I fusion protein that found in the outer portion of a virus, which contains highly glycosylated α-helical coiled structure. It has two N terminal spaces which are liable for binding (S1 subunit) and fusion (S2) to the host receptor. The E proteins are small integral membrane proteins which are prominent in infected cells during viral replication. The M protein is a structural membrane of virus which is involved in cross-species transmission. The N proteins are binding to the viral genome, which forms a shell-like structure around the nucleic acid ( ).
The ORF8 has hypervariable genes, which are one of the notable beta coronavirus proteins that rapidly evolves and facilitates virus adaptation in the host. The special crystal structure of ORF8 with separate non-covalent interface and covalent disulfide linked dimer form a unique assembly of virus which makes rapid evolving accessory proteins mediated immune evasion through down-regulation of MHC-I ( , ). The NSP1 protein is produced in infected cells, which are essential for suppression of host translation of SARS-CoV-2 that induce mRNA degradation. Thus, it inhibits cell-intrinsic immune responses such as the interferon signaling pathway (Sa Ribero et al., 2020). NSP8 and NSP9 are essential for virus replication by binding to signal recognition particles and disrupt trafficking of protein to the cell membrane upon infection ( , ). NSP16 (2′-O-methyltransferase) forms part of RTC and promotes mRNA translation, viral replication and immune evasion ( ). These viral proteins are evidence for higher infectivity and immune evasion in host system.

## Metabolomics evidence of infection
In general, metabolomics approach applies to measure and characterize the metabolomic profile by the tools of analytical chemistry. The metabolite profile or metabotype reflects the metabolic status of a cellular system. The metabolomic analysis is a valuable method to deal with the effect of viral infection and how it alters the metabolic cues of an infected cell. In many clinical reports, SARS-CoV-2 patients are evidenced with the metabolic signatures of co-morbid disease and conditions, for example, obesity, diabetes and cardiovascular disease. These comorbid conditions are a barrier to the development of potential drugs against the infection, because of alteration in the metabolites of the host. In this context, Blasco et al. reported the alteration in tryptophan-nicotinamide pathway and cytosine metabolism in the infected patients ( ). Generally, the tryptophan-nicotinamide pathway consists of two parts; 1) tryptophan to quinolinic acid and 2) quinolinic acid to N1 methyl-2-pyridone-5-carbamide and N1-methyl-4-pyridone-3-carboxamide. These metabolites are reported to be a signature of inflammatory signals of tryptophan-kynurenine metabolism which is indirectly regulated by epithelial immunity associated ACE2 dependent changes in the host ( , ). The cytosine metabolite of cytosine metabolism belongs to the pyrimidine class, and they significantly change metabolism, such as pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis. The cytosine level is reportedly higher in infected patients due to the coupling between viral particle synthesis and host cellular metabolism, which helps the virus to escape from the immune system. ( ). Similarly, the report on serum metabolomic and lipidomic profiling of infected individuals could be a potential marker. The lipidomic profiling of infected serum by Bruzzone et al. revealed the dysregulation of lipoprotein, triglyceride level, VLDL subclasses of lipid and apolipoprotein composition. As an alternative strategy, the host cellular system activates metabolism that induce excessive ketone bodies such as 3- hydroxybutyric acid and acetoacetic acid and other metabolites succinate and pyruvate as energy source, but these metabolites are reported to be involved in mitochondrial dysfunction and are considered as oxidative stress markers. Notably, porphyrin levels of patients are reported to implies at oxidative stress and liver damage ( ). The strong association of immunity cues such as proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines with metabolites that are involved mainly in amino acid and NAD+ metabolism reported in an immunometabolism of infected individuals ( , ).
The metabolite profiling of mild infected severely infected and recovered individual’s revealed alteration in the respiration related metabolites such as malate, 2-oxoglutarate and aspirate. In addition, dysregulation in metabolites of glucose metabolism and TCA cycle metabolism is also crucial for SARS-CoV-2 infection course which consume large amounts of ATP for the replication and unwinding of RNA duplex ( ). Moreover, the host metabolic reprogramming of metabolites in urea cycle such as arginine and ornithine levels are significantly reduced in the sera of patients which is crucial for disease progression ( ). The metabolite arginine can either be converted to ornithine or urea by enzyme arginase or to nitric acid and citrulline by nitric oxide synthase ( ). The nitric acid and its derivatives are reported to affect the S protein fusion with its cognate receptor, ACE2 ( ). This effect of nitric acid is recognized as potential treatment in SARS-CoV-2 patients, where it has been inhaled by the infected individual and approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration in May 2020 in a phase 3 clinical study (PULSE-CVD19–001). Therefore, metabolite analysis of various infection stages could be a viable approach against SARS-CoV-2 infection. As a note, the future course of infection would be determined by the metabolite changes of host system as well as the clinical characteristics of virus that would determine the infectivity and virulence.

## Co-infection and secondary infections effect in SARS-CoV-2 infected patients and recuperated individual
Patients infected with SARS-CoV-2 and recuperated individual can develop severe illness due to the development of Co- and secondary infections necessitating intensive care. A case report from China highlights co-detection of respiratory virus in individuals whose chest CT report evinced a sort of common feature ground-glass consolidation of respiratory viruses in lungs thus evidenced subsidiary difficulty on viral detection ( ). Moreover, coinfection of Influenza virus A and B in SARS-CoV-2 patients report from Spain divulged clinical treatment and improvements of patients. Further, patients treated in combination of Lopinavir and Ritonavir, oral Hydroxychloroquine and Oseltamivir have reported the patients' improvement, yet diagnosis of super-infections necessitates effective antiviral drugs ( ). Additionally, new onset of manifestation and prevalence of gastrointestinal symptoms and lung injury are more pronounced in SARS-CoV-2 patients ( ). Besides, perioperative data of SARS-CoV-2 patients with acute abdomen concurrent to the gastrointestinal symptoms in SARS-CoV-2 infected patients. Parallelly, it surges the emergency of abdominal surgery and modulates associated SOFA score and D-dimer prognostic factors ( ). Likewise, Metapneumovirus ( ), Rhinovirus/enterovirus and Adenovirus significantly reported as virus coinfection ( ). However, the prominent evidences are emerging over bacterial and fungal coinfection in the SARS-CoV-2 patients. The case report of Adult SARS-CoV-2 patient has evidenced with Burkholderia cepacia critical to bacterial dependent pneumoniae, which indeed evinced superimposed bacterial infection ( ). Recent systematic review and meta-analysis evidenced a significant rate of bacterial coinfection in 3338 of 3506 patients aged from 2 to 71 years, of which considerably 281 patients are confirmed with bacterial infection. In addition, a random meta-analysis of patient populations reported 3.5 % bacterial co-occurrence, and 14.3 % secondary infection. In spite, 6.9 % of the patient population significantly affected with bacterial infection, consistently, 5.9 % and 8.1 % are reported to hospitalized and critically ill individuals, respectively ( ). Moreover, a retrospective study on patients admitted to intensive care units (ICU) evidenced the bacterial and auxiliary respiratory virus as coinfections and super-infections. Among the median and quartile aged ICU patients', acute respiratory failure is common and are not related with atypical bacterial or viral infection, thus evidenced the distribution of respiratory associated bacterial coinfection related to Mycoplasma species, Haemophilus influenzae, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Streptococcus pneumoniae ( ). In fact, observational study of low-rate laboratory dependent bacterial infection in SARS-CoV-2 patients, Klebsiella pneumoniae, ventilator-associated Enterobacter cloacae, central line associated Candida albicans, Enterococcus spp. and P. aeruginosa and urinary catheter associated Escherichia coli are identified as source for secondary infections ( ). A recent multicenter study reported an incidence of ventilator-assisted pneumonia to 50% in patients with SARS-CoV-2 ( ). Subgroup studies of ICU patients reported greater proportions of bacterial infection rather than hospitalized and hospitalized/ICU mixed patients ( ). Therefore, prior and rapid diagnosis of bacterial coinfections are imperative to narrow the detection (e.g., metagenomic Next Generation Sequencing) and aid in lighting the current situation along with novel therapeutic interventions. A countable number of studies concretely designed for fungal co- and super-infections. First case of fungal coinfection has been reported for cavity pulmonary mucormycosis in ICU SARS-CoV-2 patients. In fact, secondary bronchial aspirate, microscopic and cranial and thoracic CT scan examination of that patient imparted rapid growth of candy-like structure due to certain molds ( ). Moreover, invasive aspergillosis occupies 30 % of ICU SARS-CoV-2 patients ( ). Bloodstream infections in critically ill patients are rather scarce and been the second most common secondary infection incidences ranging from 3.4 % to 50 % ( ). In the treatment of COVID19 guidelines the corticosteroids have an important part which has the impact on development of secondary infection. The secondary infection patients administration with the corticosteroids significantly reduced the likelihood of mortality ( ). The severity of infection increased rather than the SARS-CoV-2 but also by secondary and coinfection of bacterial and fungal species. Therefore, early detection could permit prophylaxis of the frequency of bacterial and fungal infections. Identification of pathogens responsible to Co- and secondary infection and possible risk factors may lead to development of new strategies for therapy against secondary infections.

## Effect of viral infection in host olfaction and gustation
A multi-center observational study revealed that the olfactory and gustatory dysfunctions are primary symptoms of SARS-CoV-2 infection and their post infection in individuals. In addition, growing evidence of SARS-CoV-2 neurotropism, invasion and their virulent allures' dissection of neuroanatomical areas too. A meta-analysis estimated the omicron variant global prevalence of olfactory dysfunction, which is being 6.6 % of adults infected with the omicron variant ( ). An autopsy report of SARS-CoV-2 patient suggested that the ACE2 expression in non-neuronal cells rather than on neurons increased the degree of severity. The non-neuronal choroid plexus (ChP) epithelial cells are the boundary of blood-CSF-Barrier which separates capillary leakage of stroma from the CNS and attributes high expression of ACE2 level ( , ). Similarly, an organoids model study reported the over-expression of ACE2 and TMPRSS2 in lipoprotein-producing clusters of ChP cell which exacerbated severity of virus invasion into CNS, intimately on infants too ( , ). The SARS-CoV-2 infection alters the function of olfactory epithelium, which is generally gets modulated during aging process ( ). The anatomical proximity region between neurons and olfactory mucosa (Neural-mucosal interface) within pharynx adroit hyposmia or anosmia and dysgeusia or ageusia in SARS-CoV-2 patients. This neural-mucosal interface is a port of viral entry to the CNS ( ). Moreover, SARS-CoV-2 enwrapped sustentacular cells of the olfactory endothelium, a player of both epithelial and neuroglial function that permits impaired function of olfactory receptor neurons. Although, in some SARS-CoV-2 patients, the CNS region is not directly connected to the olfactory mucosa, there may be an additional mechanism for entry to the CNS, possibly via axonal transport. Recently, neuropilin-1 (NPR1) expression on the olfactory epithelium has been identified as an additional route for viral invasion to the CNS. ( , ). The recovery rate of olfactory and gustatory function in people recuperating from SARS-CoV-2 infection is still unknown. Moreover, current clinical studies on significance of olfactory and gustatory dysfunction are controversial, thus only based on self-report in lieu of rhino logical report ( ). The general prevalence of clinical status of olfactory and gustatory impairment remains to be further enlightened.

## Impact of multi drug resistance in SARS-CoV-2 patients
Frequent antibiotic usage in patients infected with SARS-CoV-2 threatens to antimicrobial resistance. SARS-CoV-2 copped rising risk factors that associated with multi drug resistant dependent bacterial and fungal infections. The increasing number of multidrug-resistant microorganisms and our waning breadth to annihilate virus infection render us to challenge more during this pandemic. The antimicrobial resistance varied substantially based on the bacterial and fungal infections. The recent review on antibiotic resistance reported that the highest prevalence of resistance is reported for stenotrophomonas spp, Acinetobacter spp and Klebsiella spp to colistin and carbapenems. The patients in intense care unit, receiving IL-6 inhibitors and having diabetes are most associated to presence of antibiotic resistance isolates. The Klebsiella spp have multidrug resistance and resistant to third generation cephalosporin. The Escherichia coli have carbapenem resistance and Proteus spp have multidrug resistance ( ). Recent report from India coinciding with MDR-associated secondary infections, in which, 60% case fatality is observed due to candidemia caused by Candida auris in ICU patients. Among the ICU patients, predominantly C. aurins, C. albicans and C. tropicalis are reported as source of candidemia, in fact, those isolates are resistant to a sort of antifungal agent fluconazole, voriconazole, amphotericin B, 5- flucytosine and even to the combination ( , ). Moreover, in vitro trials of anti-microbial and immunomodulatory drugs have been reported against SARS-CoV-2. However, with insufficient concentrations of drugs, these remains ineffective in in vivo studies ( ). Randomized clinical trials of hydroxychloroquine, an anti-malarial drug, reported ineffective in hospitalized and non-hospitalized individuals ( ). Moreover, sufficient higher concentrations are prone to adverse effects and toxic to the in vivo system ( ). The specific drug efficacy and safety in the prophylaxis and treatment of SARS-CoV-2 is not yet identified. Despite conflicting efficacy for drugs with significant benefits have been denied by multitudinous cohort and randomized clinical trials which suggesting that subsidiary well-validated assays are required.

## Targeted therapeutic management and developments
At present, with distinguished knowledge on possible utility of drug repurposing could prevent the rising cases of SARS-CoV-2. In the earlier, passive antibody administration has been more effective in susceptible individuals, which increased the SARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibodies when utilized for disease prophylaxis. The troublesome aspect of selecting passive antibodies for therapy is temporal efficacy variation. The efficacy of antibody administration is stable shortly after onset of symptoms and delayed administration has no benefit on prophylaxis ( ). The antibody-dependent enhancement of immunity aggravates SARS-CoV-2 infection along with modification of structural and genome patterns, which makes it much more difficult for identification of novel therapies. Therefore, development of small molecule inhibitors could interfere in vital mechanisms of viral entry and propagation (
 ). The pathogenesis of SARS-CoV-2 turns hyperactivates immune cells such as macrophages, natural killer cells to release the chemo and cytokines. Elevation of such interleukins and cytokines are, which induces hyper innate inflammatory response during infection course eventually leads to inflated levels of cytokines, which is a plausible reason for multiple-organ damage ( ). In addition, activation of mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAP Kinases) cascade and activator protein − 1 (AP-1) by S-protein mediates release of IL-8 in infected cells, which also mitigates treatment complications ( ). Likewise, P13K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway also reported against SARS-CoV-2; these pathways involved in host inflammatory response, cell survival and proliferation. Foreseeable studies on identification of inhibitors against SARS-CoV-2 implicating host cellular pathways could reduce exacerbation of infection ( ). Phytochemicals have been proven to inhibit SARS-CoV-2 replication in vitro ( ). These druggable phytochemicals have been reported for potential activity against SARS-CoV-2, notably, 5, 7, 3′, 4′-tetrahydroxy-2′-(3, 3-dimethylallyl) isoflavone predicted to bind with catalytic region of viral 3-chymotrypsin-like cysteine protease (3CLpro), which is a control enzyme of viral replication ( ). Similarly, phytochemicals such as Abietane (Lycopus europaeus L. (Lamiaceae)), Berbamine (Berberis amurensis Rupr. (Berberidaceae)), Catechin gallate (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze (Theaceae)) have also been reported against 3CLpro, that restrict viral entry and replication ( ). Therefore, investigating efficacy of small molecule inhibitors, including phytochemicals against SARS-CoV-2 could reduce the therapeutic gap.

## Vaccine strategies against SARS-CoV-2.
The severity of SARS-CoV-2 infection is heterogeneous that depends on various variables including ethnicity and comorbidities. Moreover, previous SARS-CoV-2-exposed-individuals developing protection over subsequent infection are unclear. An efficacious vaccine might have the capability to protect and prevent mortality of an infected person is a crucial efficacy endpoint ( ). The beneficial effect of such vaccines on people could be observed based on their efficacy in older adults (∼ >60 years). Wide coverage of vaccines at high-risk people to infection would have a prominent effect upon disease endpoints. Vaccines that only reduce transmissibility of virus but not affect any clinical course could be a valuable strategy; alternatively, assessment of multiple parallel vaccine candidates in an adaptive trial design against a single placebo group could also sufficiently increase efficiency ( ). Though, comparison of vaccines within different populations is essential to standardize and quantify efficacy endpoints.

## Alternative medicines as a strategy to mitigate therapeutic scarcity
The practice of complementary and alternative medicines (CAM) involves different approaches and resources. Traditional alternative medicine may include Acupuncture, Ayurveda, Homeopathy, Naturopathy, Chinese or Oriental medicine etc. Recent studies indicate renewed interest from multiple sources on CAM towards management of infectious diseases especially COVID-19 ( , , , ). Multiple alternatives available such as traditional Chinese medicines in treatment of COVID has been document by multiple evidences ( , ; ). Similarly, Indian traditional Ayurveda has been attempted in management of COVID ( , ). Also, other systems such as Siddha etc. have also been implicated in treatment of COVID ( , ). Siddha medicine uses specific polyherbal formulations for the treatment of viral diseases including COVID ( , ). Traditional food contains multiple spices, herbs etc. that play role in managing infectious disease infections ( , ). Recent advancement in molecular characterization revealed active components of spices and herbs with antiviral properties ( , ). Natural products such as honey ( ) and natural bio-actives as adjuvant therapy in multiple health conditions, including COVID. ## Future prospective
The relentless emergence of SARS-CoV-2 variant with remarkable severity would stigmatize global health of humans; thus, measurement, monitoring and understanding of emerging variants' transmissibility and high risk of mortality could significantly increase our knowledge to reduce the susceptibility of immune response to SARS-CoV-2 infection and re-infection. SARS-CoV-2 variants' genomic surveillance is arduous and rudimentary toil. However, obstruction in therapeutic development could surge the peak of infected individuals. Besides, higher population density fuels insufficient vaccinations and allows variant evolution; therefore, regional restrictions could apparently reduce SARS-CoV-2 impact globally. Even though animal models have accounted for mutation associated study and therapeutic interventions, how it would be translatable to humans is still unclear. Inception of specific mutations in SARS-CoV-2 proteins aside from S protein besides might direct interposition of therapeutic developments. In addition, the majority of the population prompts re-, super- and co- infection of various respiratory related microbial infections along with SARS-CoV-2. Considering the recent development in precision medicine through natural resources with active components having antiviral properties, it is of utmost important to characterize newer compounds with direct impact of SARS-CoV-2 targets for effective therapeutic strategies for future outbreak, if any.
