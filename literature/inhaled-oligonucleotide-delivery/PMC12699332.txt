## Abstract
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, interstitial lung disease lacking an efficient drug to reverse it. Thus, there is an urgent need to elucidate the complex pathogenesis of IPF and identify new therapeutic targets. It has been revealed that the pathophysiology of IPF is a highly orchestrated process that includes multiple cell types in which the contribution of endothelial cells (ECs) has attracted researchers’ attention. However, although the involvement of ECs in fibrosis has been recognized, the underlying key molecules driving these changes are not well defined. In this study, we revealed that von Willebrand factor (VWF), a marker of damaged ECs, and endothelial dysfunction are positively correlated with IPF progression on the basis of reanalysis of gene expression profiles of patients with IPF. Next, we discovered that VWF deficiency attenuated fibrosis in experimental models, including human cell lines (in vitro) and mice (in vivo). Mechanistically, VWF deficiency inhibited endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, regulated vascular abnormalities, and limited M2 macrophage infiltration, which were achieved, at least in part, by the inhibition of Wnt signaling. Our findings provided evidence for the pivotal role of ECs in IPF and revealed that VWF might be a driving factor of endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, suggesting that VWF can be developed as a potential therapeutic target against IPF.
Keywords: von Willebrand factor, endothelial cells, endothelial dysfunction, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, Wnt signaling

## Clinical Relevance
Our findings demonstrate that endothelial dysfunction and von Willebrand factor (VWF) expression are positively correlated with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Genetic VWF deletion attenuates fibrosis in experimental models, including human cell lines (in vitro) and mice (in vivo). Mechanistically, VWF deficiency inhibits endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, regulates vascular abnormalities, and limits M2 macrophage infiltration. These effects are achieved, at least in part, by the inhibition of Wnt signaling. These findings help us better understand the pivotal role of endothelial cells in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and provide evidence that VWF is a therapeutic target in pulmonary fibrosis.

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), a chronic, progressive, and irreversible interstitial lung disease characterized by lung scarring, is associated with high mortality and low 5-year survival rates ( , ). The pathogenesis of IPF involves continuous damage to the lung epithelium; activation of immune cells that release large amounts of inflammatory factors, resulting in a hyperinflammatory state at the site of injury; and activation of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, which excessively secrete extracellular matrix, leading to aberrant lung remodeling ( – ). Despite advances over the past decades, the pathological mechanism of IPF remains poorly understood ( , ). Although antifibrotic therapies slow disease progression, they cannot reverse pulmonary fibrosis ( , ). Therefore, the identification of new therapeutic targets for IPF is urgently needed. Compared with other cell types, the involvement of lung endothelial cells (ECs) in IPF pathogenesis has scarcely been studied, making their contribution to disease progression difficult to determine. Assessing the role of ECs in IPF may accelerate therapeutic development.
In the lung, 30% of cells are ECs, which provide a tight endothelial barrier to the lung tissue ( , ). In addition to forming a vascular barrier, ECs control coagulation, vascular tone, and immune responses through the expression of adhesion molecules ( , ). An imbalance in these physiological processes can lead to endothelial dysfunction. In the context of IPF, ECs play immunomodulatory roles by recruiting immune cells through chemokine secretion and adhesion molecule expression ( , ). Moreover, ECs are a potential source of myofibroblasts via endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT), a process characterized by endothelial dysfunction leading to an imbalance in angiogenesis, thereby contributing to the progression of IPF ( – ). These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of improving endothelial function and inhibiting EndoMT to alleviate IPF, although the mechanisms of action involved are less well understood.
The glycoprotein von Willebrand factor (VWF) is stored in α-granules and Weibel-Palade bodies of platelets and ECs, respectively ( ). Although platelets release VWF predominantly upon platelet activation from α-granules, ECs release VWF from Weibel-Palade bodies in a combined basal and regulated manner ( ). Injured ECs release VWF from Weibel-Palade bodies. Some viewpoints considered VWF as a marker of damaged ECs because the plasma VWF concentrations are almost exclusively derived from ECs ( ). In a study of associations between the endothelial coagulation system and the inflammatory state in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, plasma VWF concentrations were found to be elevated during exacerbations and significantly decreased during clinical stability ( ). However, the precise role of EC-derived VWF in IPF pathogenesis remains unknown.
In this study, we hypothesized that VWF concentration regulates IPF progression by affecting endothelial function and EndoMT. We sought to elucidate the mechanisms by which VWF regulates the functional state of ECs and its role as a potential therapeutic target in IPF. We used bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and a knockout (KO) mouse model to demonstrate that VWF deficiency improves endothelial function in lung fibrosis and attenuates fibrosis through Wnt signaling. Our results highlight the importance of ECs in the progression of IPF and provide insight into the potential of targeting ECs for the treatment of IPF. Importantly, this study supports the use of VWF as a therapeutic target for the treatment of IPF.

## Methods
Extensive descriptions of all methods are provided in the data supplement. Here, we provide details on the in vivo study.

## Bleomycin-induced IPF Model
To establish a murine model of bleomycin-induced IPF, wild-type and Vwf-KO mice received 1.8 mg/kg bleomycin (Selleck, S1214) via intratracheal injection. Control mice received the same volume of sterile saline. After 17 days, the mice were killed, the inflated left lung was fixed with a buffered 10% formalin solution, and the right lung tissues were used for qPCR and Western blotting.

## Histology and Immunohistochemistry
Paraffin-embedded fixed lung tissues were cut into 4-μm slices. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson’s trichrome, and the degrees of pulmonary fibrosis and inflammation were assessed using Ashcroft and Szapiel scores ( , ). The criteria for grading lung fibrosis according to Ashcroft score is as follows: normal lung (grade 0), minimal fibrous thickening of alveolar or bronchiolar walls (grade 1), moderate thickening of walls without obvious damage to lung architecture (grade 2–3), increased fibrosis with definite damage to lung structure and formation of fibrosis bands or small fibrous masses (grade 4–5), severe distortion of structure and large fibrous areas (grade 6–7), and total fibrous obliteration of the field (grade 8). The criteria for grading lung inflammation according to Szapiel scores with some modification is as follows: no alveolitis (grade 0); thickening of the alveolar septum by a mononuclear cell infiltrate, with involvement limited to focal, pleura-based lesions occupying less than 20% of the lung and with good preservation of the alveolar architecture (grade 1); a more widespread alveolitis involving 20–50% of the lung, although still predominantly pleural based (grade 2); severe alveolitis and fibrosis involving more than 50% of the lung, with occasional hemorrhagic areas within the interstitium and/or alveolus (grade 3); and total lesions and inflammatory cell infiltration of the lung (grade 4). For β-catenin (Abcam, ab224803) immunohistochemical staining, sections were sequentially incubated with primary and secondary antibodies.

## Statistical Analysis
The data are presented as the mean ± SD. All graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.0.0; GraphPad Software, Inc.). Two-tailed Student’s t tests were used for comparisons between two groups. Multiple groups were compared using one-way ANOVA. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Endothelial Dysfunction and VWF Expression Are Positively Correlated with IPF
To determine whether endothelial dysfunction is involved in IPF pathology, we reanalyzed a published transcriptome sequencing dataset of patients with IPF ( ). The expression levels of PTAFR (platelet-activating factor receptor), VCAM1 (vascular cell adhesion molecule 1), ACE (angiotensin I–converting enzyme), and IL2RA (IL-2 receptor subunit-α) were used to characterize changes in EC function in healthy individuals and patients ( ). Compared with healthy lungs, the expression levels of PTAFR, VCAM1, ACE, and IL2RA mRNA were increased in fibrotic lungs, implying endothelial dysfunction in IPF ( ). To generate a mouse model of IPF, we challenged wild-type mice with bleomycin, a drug widely used to study pulmonary fibrosis ( ). Next, we performed RNA-seq of lung tissues from mice with IPF. Consistent with human data, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed significant upregulation of pathways, including coagulation ( ), positive regulation of inflammatory response ( ), and leukocyte adhesion to vascular ECs in fibrotic lungs ( ). Because the endothelium is the supporting surface for the formation of procoagulant complexes and clotting ( ) and endothelial dysfunction regulates the coagulation process directly and timely, we extracted 53 core genes in the coagulation pathway identified by GSEA and displayed their expression changes using a heatmap ( ). Among these genes, VWF attracted our attention because high VWF concentrations imply endothelial dysfunction ( , ). Although the mechanistic role of VWF during pulmonary fibrosis is uncertain, it is reported that lung fibrosis is linked to increased EC activation, in which the gene expression of VWF was increased in different endothelial subpopulations ( ). Moreover, VWF concentration is increased in the plasma of patients with lung fibrosis ( ). We therefore investigate the mechanistic role of VWF during IPF. To assess endothelial dysfunction in the context of IPF, we measured plasma VWF antigen concentrations in fibrotic mice using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs). Plasma VWF antigen concentrations in IPF mice were significantly elevated compared with those in normal mice, as detected by ELISA ( ). Likewise, analysis of single-cell RNA-seq data from 51 healthy individuals and 56 patients with lung fibrosis ( ) revealed high VWF concentrations in patients with IPF ( ). These results suggest that endothelial dysfunction and VWF concentrations are positively correlated with the pathological progression of IPF.
 

## VWF Deficiency Alleviates Bleomycin-induced Pulmonary Fibrosis in Mice
To investigate the relevance of VWF in pulmonary fibrosis progression in vivo, we analyzed bleomycin-challenged Vwf-KO and wild-type mice 17 days after instillation ( ). Complete Vwf-KO was confirmed by genotyping of mouse tail (see Figure E1A in the data supplement) and lung tissue (Figure E1B) samples. Consistent with the data of patients with IPF, Vwf expression levels were significantly elevated in the fibrotic lungs of wild-type mice (Figure E1B), indicating that VWF was involved in IPF progression. Wild-type mice were more susceptible to bleomycin administration, showing higher mortality ( ) and lung coefficients ( ). Conversely, Vwf deficiency reduced the bleomycin susceptibility of mice, resulting in significantly better survival ( ) and lower lung coefficients ( ). H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining showed no fibrotic areas or inflammatory cell infiltration in the lung tissues of saline-treated wild-type and Vwf-KO mice ( ). However, bleomycin-treated wild-type mice had significant disruption of alveolar structure, collagen deposition, and inflammatory cell infiltration in lung tissues, suggesting severe fibrosis ( ). Compared with these mice, bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice showed attenuated fibrosis and inflammation ( and E1C), according to Ashcroft and Szapiel scores ( ). Moreover, measurement of hydroxyproline content in lung tissues similarly showed that VWF deficiency inhibits collagen accumulation during fibrosis, as evidenced by a markedly decrease in hydroxyproline concentrations in bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice compared with wild-type mice ( ). These results indicate that the absence of VWF inhibits collagen deposition during pulmonary fibrosis and promotes the resolution of fibrosis.
 

## VWF Deficiency in ECs Suppresses EndoMT In Vitro and In Vivo
EndoMT in ECs is a process in which ECs lose their endothelial characteristics and acquire a mesenchymal-like phenotype and increased expression of mesenchymal genes promotes extracellular matrix deposition and accelerates fibrosis ( – ). We performed immunofluorescence staining using lung tissues from mice to investigate the role of VWF in EndoMT in vivo. Bleomycin stimulation induced EndoMT in wild-type mice during fibrosis, as evidenced by the colocalization of CD31 and α-SMA and the decreased expression of CD31 and increased expression of α-SMA ( ). Importantly, the colocalization of CD31 and α-SMA and the expression of α-SMA were significantly decreased in Vwf-KO mice ( ). Quantification similarly showed that the number of CD31+α-SMA+ double-positive cells in the fibrotic lungs of Vwf-KO mice was significantly lower than that of wild-type mice, suggesting that Vwf deficiency inhibits EndoMT during IPF ( , right panel). Consistently, the expression of endothelial markers, including Cdh5, Kdr, and Pecam1, was markedly reduced in bleomycin-treated wild-type mice but increased in fibrotic Vwf-KO mice ( ). In addition, Vwf deficiency dramatically inhibits the expression of fibrotic genes, including Col1a1, Col3a1, and Fn1, during fibrosis formation ( ). Moreover, GSEA of RNA-seq data from bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice showed substantial inhibition of Gene Ontology terms related to “collagen biosynthetic process,” “focal adhesion,” and “matrix metalloproteinases” (Figures E2A–E2C). The expression levels of mesenchymal genes in these pathways were considerably downregulated in Vwf-KO mice compared with those in wild-type mice (Figures E2D–E2F). These results also indicate, to some extent, that EndoMT is inhibited in the absence of VWF. We then conducted in vitro experiments using human pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells (HPMECs) to determine the direct effects of VWF in the regulation of EndoMT. We first determined whether bleomycin treatment stimulates EC activation, promotes VWF secretion, and upregulates mesenchymal gene expression. After 48 hours of continuous stimulation with 1 μg/ml bleomycin, HPMECs were significantly activated, as evidenced by significantly increased VWF mRNA concentrations (Figure E3A) and markedly elevated VWF concentrations in supernatants (Figure E3B). Notably, bleomycin induced EndoMT in vitro, resulting in the high expression of COL1A1, one of the mesenchymal genes (Figure E3C). Next, we determined the effects of VWF deficiency on mesenchymal gene expression in ECs. HPMECs were transfected with VWF siRNA to silence VWF expression or scrambled siRNA as a control. VWF siRNA treatment resulted in an ∼90% reduction in VWF mRNA (Figure E3D). As expected, in the scrambled siRNA treatment group, bleomycin administration induced significant increases in the expression of fibrotic genes, including ACTA2, COL1A2, and TIMP1 ( ). In contrast, the expression of these mesenchymal genes was substantially decreased in VWF-deficient HPMECs ( ). Moreover, Western blot analysis showed that the concentrations of collagen I and α-SMA were significantly elevated after bleomycin stimulation and were markedly decreased in VWF-deficient HPMECs ( ). Collectively, these results suggest that VWF deletion in ECs suppresses EndoMT and inhibits the expression of mesenchymal genes during EndoMT.
 

## VWF Deficiency Regulates Vascular Abnormalities during IPF
Vascular abnormalities, including vascular remodeling and the increased vascular permeability accompanying IPF progression, are recognized as distinctive features of fibrotic lungs ( – ). We thus investigated the role of VWF in the regulation of vascular remodeling and vascular permeability during IPF. We assessed pulmonary artery remodeling and pulmonary microvascular remodeling using H&E staining and immunohistochemical staining for CD31, respectively. In fibrotic lungs of wild-type mice, the media fractional thickness of pulmonary arteries and the microvessel density (MVD) were significantly increased ( ). In contrast, VWF deficiency reduced the media fractional thickness and MVD ( ), suggesting the regulation of vascular remodeling by VWF during IPF. Permeability of the endothelial barrier is considered to be an indicator of vascular function. Cell number in BAL fluid (BALF) represents the endothelial permeability. We then measured the cell numbers in BALF to compare the endothelial permeability between wild-type and Vwf-KO mice. The increased cell counts in wild-type mouse BALF indicates augmented endothelial permeability, whereas the decreased cell counts in Vwf-KO mouse BALF indicates lower endothelial permeability ( ). In summary, the absence of VWF regulates the vascular abnormalities, including vascular remodeling and increased vascular permeability, during IPF.
 

## Absence of VWF Inhibits Wnt Signaling during Fibrosis
To explore the mechanism of action of VWF in fibrosis progression, we performed KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway enrichment analysis using the RNA-seq data. Compared with bleomycin-treated wild-type mice, Vwf-KO mice had 244 upregulated and 336 downregulated differentially expressed genes ( ). KEGG enrichment analysis of the downregulated differentially expressed genes revealed that VWF deficiency during fibrosis regulates several fibrosis-related pathways, including Wnt, TGF-β (transforming growth factor-β), and PI3K (phosphoinositide 3-kinase)-Akt signaling pathways ( ). Wnt signaling not only is essential for maintaining EC function but also is a key signaling pathway in fibrosis formation and resolution ( – ). The heatmap in shows the expression levels of Wnt ligands, receptors, agonists, and antagonists in each group of mice. The expression of genes that activate Wnt signaling was significantly elevated in bleomycin-challenged wild-type mice, whereas the expression of Wnt inhibitors was suppressed ( ). In contrast, the expression of positive regulatory mediators of Wnt signaling was markedly decreased in bleomycin-challenged Vwf-KO mice, whereas the expression of Wnt antagonists was increased ( ). To further confirm the regulatory role of VWF in Wnt signaling pathways, we examined the expression of β-catenin, a central Wnt signaling protein, in the lung. Immunohistochemistry showed markedly decreased β-catenin accumulation in bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice compared with bleomycin-treated wild-type mice ( ). Moreover, qPCR revealed a significant reduction in Wnt9b ( ) and Wnt10a ( ) in bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice compared with bleomycin-treated wild-type mice. Likewise, reanalysis of the Gene Expression Omnibus dataset accession number GSE181508 of mouse ECs in lung fibrosis revealed that Vwf concentrations in ECs were positively correlated with β-catenin concentrations (Figure E4). These results demonstrated that VWF deficiency suppresses Wnt signaling in IPF.
 To further validate the relevance of VWF and Wnt signaling in ECs, we performed in vitro assays using HPMECs. As expected, bleomycin stimulation activated ECs to secrete WNT10A, whereas VWF knockdown markedly inhibited Wnt ligand secretion from HPMECs ( ). Using qPCR, we detected several other Wnt ligands. Consistent with our in vivo results, VWF knockdown inhibited the expression of WNT4 and WNT9A in HPMECs compared with scrambled siRNA-treated HPMECs ( ), suggesting suppression of Wnt signaling by VWF deficiency. Moreover, to define the correlation between VWF and Wnt signaling well, we performed an in vitro assay using Wnt inhibitor MSAB, a compound that inhibits Wnt signaling by degrading β-catenin. Detection of the ACTA2 expression level showed that inhibition of mesenchymal genes by VWF was eliminated by the Wnt inhibitor, implying that inhibition of fibrotic gene expression by VWF is dependent on regulation of Wnt signaling ( ). Collectively, VWF deficiency ameliorates bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis by inhibiting Wnt signaling.

## VWF Deficiency in ECs Indirectly Affects Macrophage Infiltration via Wnt Signaling during Fibrosis
One strategy for fibrosis resolution is the reduction of macrophage infiltration, especially of M2 macrophages, a macrophage that promotes fibrosis by secreting profibrotic mediators ( , ). EC-derived chemokines are important mediators regulating monocyte adhesion during disease ( ). Our reanalysis of a published gene expression microarray dataset of murine ECs during lung fibrosis revealed that the expression levels of both β-catenin and Ccl2, an important chemoattractant for macrophages, were significantly elevated in ECs under fibrotic conditions ( ). Moreover, β-catenin expression was positively correlated with Ccl2 expression ( ), implying that Wnt signaling regulates macrophage migration and adhesion by affecting chemokine expression in ECs. Because the absence of VWF inhibits Wnt signaling during IPF, we speculated that VWF-KO leads to a reduction in M2 macrophage infiltration by inhibiting chemokine expression, which may be another reason for the attenuation of fibrosis in Vwf-KO mice. GSEA of RNA-seq data from bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice showed significant inhibition of Gene Ontology terms related to “macrophage migration” ( ) and “positive regulation of macrophage chemotaxis” ( ), suggesting that Vwf deficiency during IPF regulates macrophage migration and chemotaxis. Moreover, we assayed M2 macrophage infiltration in the lungs of each group of mice using flow cytometry. In agreement with the GSEA and qPCR results, the degree of M2 macrophage infiltration was significantly increased in the lungs of bleomycin-treated wild-type mice, whereas the proportion of M2 macrophages in the lungs of bleomycin-treated Vwf-KO mice was only half that of wild-type mice ( and E5), indicating that Vwf deficiency suppresses profibrotic macrophage infiltration.
 In summary, VWF deficiency reduces chemokine expression in ECs by inhibiting Wnt signaling, thereby suppressing macrophage chemotaxis and infiltration into the lungs. This may be a viable way in which VWF deficiency in ECs can inhibit IPF progression.

## Discussion
The ultimate pathological event in pulmonary fibrosis is the disruption of the alveolar structure and excessive deposition of extracellular matrix. This complex process involves various cells, including fibroblasts, pericytes, immune cells, epithelial cells, and ECs ( , , – ). Recent studies have defined dysregulated angiogenesis as central in the rupture of functional alveolar units ( ). However, the pathological role of ECs in IPF remains unclear. In this study, we identified VWF as a crucial molecule for maintaining endothelial function in IPF. Our results provide the first experimental evidence that VWF deficiency attenuates fibrosis in experimental models, including human cell lines (in vitro) and mice (in vivo). VWF deficiency inhibits EndoMT, regulates vascular abnormalities, and limits M2 macrophage infiltration at least in part through the inhibition of Wnt signaling. Our findings reveal the biological role of VWF in the maintenance of lung homeostasis and the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis and dissect the underlying mechanism of VWF deficiency–mediated protection from IPF in vivo and in vitro.
The alveolar epithelium is surrounded by a complex network of microvasculature that supports the alveoli during gas exchange ( ). Vascular ECs compose the inner layer of blood vessels and serve as protective barriers between blood components and connective tissues ( ). The integrity of the endothelial barrier is essential for regulating interstitial inflammation, coagulation, and vascular tone ( , ). Increased inflammation and abnormal coagulation due to ECs activation have been detected in patients with COVID-19 or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease ( , ). Although researchers have found that ECs are highly heterogeneous within fibrotic lungs and that dysfunctional ECs contribute to IPF pathology through interactions with macrophages and stromal cells ( ), a comprehensive assessment of whether endothelial dysfunction is accompanied by pulmonary fibrosis progression is still lacking. We analyzed whether endothelial dysfunction exists in patients with IPF from the perspective of different ECs functions owing to their diversity. The expression levels of representative molecules involved in coagulation, adhesion, vascular tone, and inflammation were all significantly elevated in fibrotic lungs. Consistent with this finding, representative ECs processes, including coagulation, inflammatory response, and leukocyte adhesion to vascular ECs, were aberrantly activated in mice with bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis. Thus, our results suggest that endothelial dysfunction accompanies fibrosis and that modulation of endothelial functional homeostasis may be a potential therapeutic strategy to facilitate fibrosis resolution.
Endothelial dysfunction implies abnormal ECs activation. Because plasma VWF is almost exclusively secreted from Weibel-Palade bodies in ECs, plasma VWF concentrations are recognized markers of endothelial dysfunction ( , ), although platelets release VWF predominantly upon platelet activation from α-granules ( ). As acute-phase reactants, increased plasma VWF concentrations have been observed in various diseases significantly associated with endothelial damage and inflammation ( ). IPF is a progressive lung disorder characterized by destructive inflammation and vascular endothelial dysfunction. In our study, bleomycin treatment resulted in substantially increased VWF antigen concentrations in the plasma of mice with fibrosis. Correspondingly, a reanalysis of single-cell RNA-seq data showed that VWF expression levels were higher in patients with pulmonary fibrosis than in healthy control subjects. Therefore, high VWF concentrations may contribute to aggravated inflammation during pulmonary fibrosis. Notably, the targeted blockade of VWF improves endothelial dysfunction and alleviates inflammatory responses, both in mouse models and in the treatment of patients with cardiovascular diseases ( – ). Moreover, improvement in endothelial function with aggressive drug therapy is associated with significantly reduced VWF concentrations in vivo ( ). Evidently, VWF is not only a marker of ECs injury but also an important molecule in maintaining endothelial homeostasis. However, whether VWF affects the pathological processes of pulmonary fibrosis remains unclear. Using Vwf-KO mice and RNA-seq, we explored whether Vwf deletion in vivo can 1) inhibit collagen deposition and ameliorate pulmonary fibrosis and 2) regulate the function of ECs in fibrosis. First, our results demonstrate that VWF deficiency reduced extracellular matrix deposition in lung tissue and attenuated pulmonary fibrosis in mice. After bleomycin administration, mortality and lung coefficients of Vwf-KO mice were significantly lower than those of wild-type mice. Bleomycin-treated wild-type mice showed histopathologically severe disruptions of alveolar structures, marked collagen deposition, and significantly elevated inflammation levels in lung tissues. In response to the same stimulus, Vwf-KO mice showed relatively intact alveolar structures and exhibited substantially reduced degrees of fibrosis and inflammation. ECs acquire mesenchymal cell markers through EndoMT and express profibrotic genes, which contribute to the development of pulmonary fibrosis ( – ). Our analysis of profibrotic gene expression, including Col1a1, Col3a1, and Fn1, in murine lung tissues revealed that Vwf deletion suppresses the expression of these mesenchymal genes. Similar results were observed in HPMECs in vitro. Silencing VWF expression using siRNA in HPMECs decreased the bleomycin sensitivity of ECs and prevented the expression of mesenchymal genes. These results suggest that VWF deficiency facilitates the resolution of pulmonary fibrosis and prevents the expression of mesenchymal genes and collagen deposition by inhibiting EndoMT. Second, we determined whether VWF deficiency regulates vascular abnormalities. We evaluated pulmonary artery remodeling and microvascular remodeling using H&E staining and immunohistochemical staining for CD31, and we found that VWF deficiency regulated vascular remodeling, as evidenced by reduced the media fractional thickness and MVD. Moreover, the permeability of the pulmonary endothelium was improved in the absence of VWF. These results indicate that the absence of VWF regulates vascular abnormalities, including vascular remodeling and increased vascular permeability, during IPF. In addition, because vascular hemodynamics is an important parameter for vascular performance, the pulmonary vascular hemodynamics should be measured in future work. Collectively, our results provide compelling experimental evidence that VWF deficiency drives the resolution of pulmonary fibrosis by regulating EndoMT and vascular abnormalities during IPF.
Organ fibrosis involves a complex network of signaling pathways, of which the Wnt pathway is a common profibrotic signaling pathway ( , ). Targeting the inhibition of Wnt signaling is a potential strategy for treating fibrosis ( , ). Furthermore, Wnt signaling is critical for ECs proliferation and functional maintenance. Wnt inhibitors significantly ameliorate endothelial dysfunction by reducing inflammatory cytokine production, monocyte adhesion, and endothelial permeability ( , ). In our study, genetic VWF deletion suppressed Wnt signaling during fibrosis both in vivo and in vitro, reducing the expression of Wnt ligands and inhibiting the expression of β-catenin, the core protein in Wnt signaling pathways. Importantly, the role of VWF in regulating expression of fibrotic genes was eliminated by Wnt inhibitor, implying that the regulation of VWF in fibrosis is dependent on regulation of Wnt signaling. The suppression of Wnt signaling in ECs inhibits chemokine secretion and monocyte adhesion ( ). Consistently, we found that β-catenin expression in ECs was positively correlated with CCL2 expression on the basis of sequencing data of ECs from control and fibrotic mice. This suggests that the activation of Wnt signaling regulates chemokine expression. Blocking the CCL2–CCR2 (CCL2 receptor) axis is a promising strategy for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis because CCL2 is involved in the regulation of macrophage migration ( ). In the present study, we found that VWF deficiency inhibited macrophage migration and chemotaxis in bleomycin-induced fibrosis of murine lungs according to GSEA using mRNA sequencing data. Moreover, genetic VWF deletion significantly suppressed CCL2 expression and limited M2 macrophage infiltration in the mouse model of lung fibrosis. Thus, VWF deficiency in pulmonary fibrosis reducing M2 macrophage infiltration is partly associated with the inhibition of Wnt signaling. Future studies should focus on exploring the direct regulatory relationship between ECs and macrophages and on elucidating the intrinsic mechanisms by which this correlation depends on Wnt signaling.
Although our study reveals the potential of targeting VWF for the treatment of IPF, it is necessary to discuss whether VWF could be a suitable potential therapeutic target for IPF compared with other established targets. Vascular abnormalities during IPF are of increasing interest to researchers ( ), and the mechanism of action of nintedanib, which targets VEGF to treat IPF ( ), highlights the importance of ECs during IPF. Although how lung ECs are involved in the pathogenesis of IPF is poorly understood, the results of existing studies are gradually supporting the idea that reestablishing ECs homeostasis and function may be a novel approach to treating pulmonary fibrosis diseases ( , ). Compared with nintedanib, targeting VWF regulated not only vascular remodeling and EndoMT but also several fibrosis-related pathways (including Wnt, TGF-β, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathways), suggesting the clinical value of targeted VWF for the treatment of IPF. However, the potential for clinical application of targeting VWF requires the discovery of new pharmaceutical compounds and further investigation. In addition, both patients and mice with IPF feature an imbalance between thrombosis and fibrinolysis ( ). Considering that VWF is an important component of the coagulation process, future research efforts should also focus on the role of VWF in thrombosis during IPF.

## Limitations
The present study has several limitations. First, the exact mechanism by which VWF inhibits Wnt signaling and the key intermediary molecules remain elusive, and it is necessary to investigate this issue in future work. Thus, further studies are needed. Second, considering the effect of VWF derived from other cell types, especially activated platelets, which release VWF predominantly at the site of injury, mice with conditional VWF-KO in pulmonary ECs are necessary to elucidate the mechanistic role of VWF in lung fibrosis. Furthermore, the development of drugs that target VWF for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis is urgently needed to evaluate the potential of targeting VWF for the treatment of IPF. IPF is a progressive disease with complex pathologic mechanisms. Although the bleomycin-induced model is the American Thoracic Society–recommended model as “the best-characterized animal model available for preclinical testing” ( ), it is essential to explore the molecular role of VWF in other models of pulmonary fibrosis to reflect the complexity of human IPF.

## Conclusions
In summary, our study revealed that endothelial dysfunction is a contributing factor to pulmonary fibrosis. Genetic deletion of VWF, a marker of damaged ECs, alleviates fibrosis both in vitro and in vivo. VWF deficiency suppresses EndoMT, regulates vascular abnormalities, and limits M2 macrophage infiltration to attenuate pulmonary fibrosis, which is achieved, at least in part, by inhibiting Wnt signaling. The results of our experimental study, together with a reanalysis of publicly available patient datasets, indicate that VWF is a key factor in the progression of IPF. These findings have implications for the development of new treatments for IPF and provide proof of concept for VWF as a potential therapeutic target.
