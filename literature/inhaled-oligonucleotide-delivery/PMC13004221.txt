## Abstract
The meniscus, a critical fibrocartilaginous structure in the knee joint that cushions load and stabilizes movement, suffers from poor self-healing potential following tears. This impaired repair not only fails to restore joint function but often progresses to osteoarthritis, posing significant clinical challenges. Regrettably, current therapeutic approaches, such as surgical suturing or partial resection, have limited efficacy in achieving functional regeneration of the meniscus. To address these bottlenecks, we developed a multifunctional composite hydrogel system integrating methacrylated silk fibroin (SilMA), cerium dioxide (CeO2) nanozymes and tetrahedral framework nucleic acid (tFNA)-miRNA-455. The SilMA hydrogel, leveraging photocrosslinking technology for on-demand solidification, offers injectability (enabling minimally invasive delivery), strong tissue adhesion and robust mechanical support—effectively bridging meniscal tear gaps and creating a scaffold for cell infiltration. Embedded CeO2 nanozymes act as potent reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavengers and nanozyme-mediated ROS clearance mitigates inflammation and fosters a regeneration-conducive microenvironment. Moreover, tFNAs serve as a biocompatible, stable delivery vector for miRNA-455, protecting the nucleic acid from degradation and ensuring its efficient cellular uptake. This targeted delivery drives chondrogenic differentiation of synovial mesenchymal stem cells (SMSCs), directly promoting fibrocartilage formation. This synergistic strategy unites structural reinforcement, immunomodulation and stem cell regulation, overcoming conventional carrier limitations (cytotoxicity and poor stability) and demonstrating significant potential for meniscal repair. Ultimately, it offers a promising solution for cartilage regeneration and meniscus function restoration, with broad implications for clinical translation.
Keywords: meniscus tears reconstruction, tFNAs, miRNA-455, CeO2 nanozymes, SilMA hydrogels

## Introduction
The meniscus is a crescent-shaped fibrocartilaginous pad situated in the knee joint that plays indispensable roles in joint stabilization, load-bearing, stress absorption and synovial fluid distribution [ , ]. Meniscal tears, which are among the most prevalent musculoskeletal injuries resulting from traumatic injury or degenerative pathology, often lead to joint dysfunction and accelerate the progression of osteoarthritis (OA) [ ]. Owing to its limited vascularity, the meniscus has poor intrinsic healing capacity [ ]. Currently employed clinical approaches for meniscal repair primarily include suturing techniques, partial meniscectomy and meniscal allograft transplantation (MAT) [ , ]. However, these surgical strategies face significant challenges, including low healing rates, donor shortages, poor graft matching, disease transmission risks, immune rejection and long-term graft degeneration [ ]. Therefore, new therapeutic approaches are essential for effective meniscal tear management.
Recently, meniscus in situ tissue engineering has emerged as an attractive therapeutic paradigm for restoring damaged meniscal tissue [ ]. Endogenous stem/progenitor cells are mobilized to the lesion, where they undergo differentiation and secrete extracellular matrix (ECM) components, which is critical for healing [ ]. Specifically, synovium-derived mesenchymal stem cells (SMSCs) are the main stem cell type involved in the response to knee injury and are essential for meniscal repair [ ]. Furthermore, SMSCs exhibit high proliferative capacity and strong chondrogenic differentiation potential, further demonstrating their importance in meniscal repair [ ]. Therefore, effectively and precisely regulating SMSC migration and chondrogenic differentiation is key to promoting meniscal healing [ ]. Previous studies often utilized exogenous growth factors such as CTGF, TGF-β3 and IGF-1 to induce SMSC differentiation [ ]. However, these factors suffer from poor stability, short in vivo half-lives and the need for continuous administration to maintain bioactivity, necessitating novel chondrogenic differentiation strategies [ ]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are noncoding RNAs that modulate gene expression via translational repression, deadenylation and posttranscriptional degradation of mRNAs [ , ]. miRNAs offer high specificity and efficiency, enabling precise targeting of specific genes and accurate control of cell fate [ ]. Substantial evidence indicates that miR-455-3p critically governs the chondrogenic commitment of MSCs, suggesting its potential value for meniscal repair [ ]. However, its clinical application is hampered by poor stability and susceptibility to degradation [ , ]. Thus, a safe and effective delivery system is needed to increase the therapeutic efficacy of miRNAs. Existing miRNA delivery technologies are broadly categorized into viral vectors (e.g. lentiviruses, adenoviruses) and non-viral systems (e.g. lipid nanoparticles, polymeric micelles, inorganic nanoparticles and exosomes), each with distinct pros and cons affecting tissue repair efficacy. Viral vectors, despite high transfection efficiency, have safety issues (e.g. immunogenicity, insertional mutagenesis and large-scale production difficulty), limiting clinical translation. Non-viral systems, though safer, face challenges like poor miRNA stability, low cellular uptake, off-target effects or burst release, compromising sustained and precise target tissue delivery [ , ]. Tetrahedral framework nucleic acids (tFNAs), first synthesized by Turberfield et al. in 2005, are 3D structures self-assembled from four specific single-stranded DNA sequences [ ]. tFNAs exhibit structural simplicity, excellent biocompatibility, efficient cellular uptake and lysosomal escape facilitated by nuclear localization signals, ensuring stable gene delivery and making them ideal carriers for miRNAs [ ]. Previous studies have confirmed the ability of tFNAs to regulate mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) differentiation [ , ]. Combining tFNAs with miRNAs thus enables safe delivery and precise regulation.
In addition, meniscal injury frequently triggers a complex immunoinflammatory pathological response [ , ]. Excessive inflammation accelerates cartilage degeneration and contributes to OA pathogenesis. Therefore, modulating the dysregulated postinjury proinflammatory microenvironment and the imbalance between anabolic and catabolic factors is beneficial for meniscal regeneration [ ]. An integrated oxidative stress–inflammatory axis driven by reactive oxygen species (ROS) and proinflammatory mediators (e.g. IL-1β, TNF-α and IFN-γ) exacerbates the changes in the local microenvironment and impedes meniscal healing [ ]. Consequently, developing hydrogel-based composite biomaterials capable of regulating this proinflammatory microenvironment is particularly necessary. Previous studies targeting ROS have focused primarily on diseases such as cancer and OA, with few reports specifically concerning the meniscus. Studies indicate that antioxidant strategies aimed at scavenging ROS to suppress inflammation hold promise for the treatment of meniscal injury [ ]. As emergent catalytic nanomaterials, nanozymes harness hybrid enzyme-nano properties to exhibit significant potential across biomedical domains [ ]. Among them, cerium oxide (CeO2) nanozymes possess reversible redox capabilities, high-density surface oxygen vacancies and a multienzyme mimetic system, demonstrating remarkable therapeutic potential in inflammation-related diseases through multidimensional regulatory mechanisms [ , ].
Herein, we developed a composite hydrogel system, ‘S-T-N’, loaded with CeO2 nanozymes and tFNA-miRNA-455-3p conjugates. This system integrates three functions: bridging meniscal tears, modulating the local proinflammatory microenvironment and promoting stem cell chondrogenic differentiation, collectively facilitating meniscal regeneration and repair ( ). The methacrylated silk fibroin (SilMA) hydrogel used in this study possesses excellent injectability and tissue adhesion. Upon photocrosslinking, it forms a robust structure with superior mechanical properties and a porous architecture, effectively bridging the meniscal tear gap; ‘suturing’ the separated fragments and facilitating cell proliferation, migration and tissue ingrowth. Concurrently, the embedded ROS-responsive CeO2 nanozymes exhibit potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capabilities, ameliorating the local regenerative microenvironment. Furthermore, tFNA-delivered miRNA-455-3p efficiently enters SMSCs, precisely promoting their differentiation toward meniscal fibrochondrocytes. This strategy holds promise for overcoming the clinical challenge of low healing rates postmeniscal injury, enabling true functional regeneration.
 

## Experimental section

## Preparation and characterization of tFNAs and tFNAs-miR455
Following our previously reported protocol, four predesigned single strands ( ; ) were self-assembled into tFNAs by incubation at 95°C (10 min) and then at 4°C (20 min). miR-455 was subsequently conjugated to S2 to generate S2-miR-455 ( ). Finally, tFNAs-miR455 were synthesized under the same self-assembly conditions. The tFNAs-miR455 were stored at 4°C in a buffer solution containing 10 mM Tris-HCl, 10 mM MgCl2, pH 8.0. The successful formation of both tFNAs and tFNAs-miR455 was systematically validated by 8% native PAGE, high-performance capillary electrophoresis (HPCE), DLS, AFM and TEM.

## Effects of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs

## Effects of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs proliferation
The proliferative effects of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs were evaluated by EdU staining and Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assays. For EdU staining, SMSCs were seeded into 24-well plates at 3 × 104 cells per well and, upon reaching ≈70% confluency, exposed to miR-455, tFNAs, or tFNAs-miR455. After 24 h, EdU staining was conducted according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Beyotime, China), and images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope. For the CCK-8 assay, SMSCs were plated into 96-well plates at 3 × 10³ cells per well and treated with miR-455, tFNAs or tFNAs-miR455 for 1, 3, 5 or 7 days. CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime) was then added and incubated for 2 h; the absorbance was subsequently measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## Effects of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs migration
The effects of tFNAs-miR455 on both the vertical and horizontal migration of SMSCs were evaluated using Transwell and scratch-wound assays, respectively. For the Transwell assays, 2 × 104 SMSCs suspended in 250 μL of medium were plated in the upper compartment (Corning, USA), while the lower compartment was filled with 750 μL of medium containing miR-455, tFNAs or tFNAs-miR455. Following 24 h of incubation, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (30 min), counterstained with 0.1% crystal violet (15 min) and examined by bright-field microscopy. For the scratch-wound assay, SMSCs were plated into 6-well plates at 3 × 104 cells per well (JET BIOFIL, Guangzhou, China). When the cultures reached ≈80% confluence, a sterile pipette tip was used to create a uniform scratch. Wound closure was monitored and photographed at 0, 12 and 24 h. The number of migrated cells and wound closure percentages were quantitatively determined with ImageJ.

## Effects of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs chondrogenic differentiation
The chondrogenic potential of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs was systematically assessed by qRT–PCR, immunofluorescence staining and Western blotting. The chondrogenic markers evaluated included COL II, ACAN, SOX9, COL I, FN1 and TNC. Chondrogenic induction was performed according to a previously published protocol [ ]. Detailed procedures for qRT–PCR, immunofluorescence and Western blotting are provided in the corresponding sections.

## Preparation and characterization of CeO2 nanozymes
CeO2 nanozymes were synthesized according to a previously reported method, and their successful preparation was confirmed by TEM, XRD, XPS and EDS [ ]. RAW264.7 macrophages were seeded into 6- or 24-well plates and treated with LPS in the presence or absence of CeO2 for 24 h, followed by qRT–PCR and immunofluorescence analyses. Similarly, chondrocytes were plated into 6- or 24-well plates and exposed to IL-1β (Novoprotein, Suzhou, China) with or without CeO2 for 24 h before qRT–PCR and immunofluorescence assays were performed.

## Preparation and characterization of SilMA hydrogels
SilMA hydrogel was purchased from Engineering For Life (EFL, China). Briefly, SilMA lyophilized powder was dissolved at 15% (w/v) in a 0.25% (w/v) lithium phenyl-2,4,6-trimethylbenzoylphosphinate (LAP) photoinitiator solution, with or without the incorporation of CeO2 nanozymes and tFNAs-miR455. The precursor solution was then exposed to UV light (25 mW cm−2) for 30 s to induce crosslinking. After lyophilization, the as-formed hydrogels were analysed via FTIR spectroscopy, SEM imaging and mechanical assays.

## Proteomic analysis
At the 24-week postmeniscal tear endpoint, regenerated meniscal tissue from the control and S-T-N groups was harvested for label-free proteomic profiling. Untargeted liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) analyses were performed by Shanghai Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s standard protocols. Proteins were first extracted and subjected to quality control by SDS–PAGE. Tryptic peptides were subsequently separated on an EASY-nLC 1200 UHPLC system coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Raw spectra were processed with Spectronaut™ software; DEPs were defined as those exhibiting a ≥2-fold increase or ≤0.5-fold decrease relative to controls. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted on the Majorbio Cloud Platform (cloud.majorbio.com).

## 
In vivo experiments
All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the PLA General Hospital (Approval number: 2024-X20-105). Sixteen-week-old male New Zealand white rabbits were anesthetized with isoflurane (R510-22-10, RWD Life Science) delivered through a veterinary vaporizer (R580, RWD Life Science) connected to a tight-fitting oxygen mask [ ]. Following anesthesia, the rabbits were shaved around the knee joint, and the skin was disinfected with povidone-iodine. A 3-cm anteromedial parapatellar incision was made through the skin and fascia. The medial collateral ligament was transected to open the joint cavity, and a scalpel was used to create a full-thickness tear in the body of the medial meniscus. Immediately after injury, 200 µl of the hydrogel solution was injected into the lesion with a 1-mL syringe and cross-linked in situ by 30 s of UV irradiation (25 mW cm−2) to seal the tear. At 12 or 24 weeks postsurgery, the animals were euthanized by inhalation of an overdose of isoflurane under general anesthesia, and the knee joints were harvested for further analyses.
Whole-joint MRI was performed to evaluate FC, TP, and meniscal integrity. Meniscal lesions were scored in a double-blinded manner using the whole-organ magnetic resonance imaging score (WORMS). Harvested menisci and joints were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 1 week at 4°C and then decalcified in 10% (w/v) EDTA-2Na, with the solution changed twice weekly until complete decalcification was confirmed by needle penetration. The samples were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a thickness of 6 µm. The sections were stained with H&E and safranin O/fast green according to the manufacturer’s protocols (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Immunofluorescence staining was also performed.

## Statistical analysis
All the data were collected from at least three independent experiments or repeated measurements. Differences among groups were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All the statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0. The results are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs) of the means.

## Results and discussion

## Synthesis, characterization and cellular internalization of tFNAs-miR455
Following our previously reported protocol, four predesigned single-stranded oligonucleotides were annealed to generate tFNAs ( ) [ , ]. miR-455-3p was then covalently conjugated to the 3′ terminus of strand S2, yielding miR-455-3p-functionalized tFNAs (tFNAs-miR455) using an identical self-assembly strategy ( ). Comprehensive physicochemical characterization confirmed successful fabrication. Eight percent polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) revealed discrete bands for both tFNAs and tFNAs-miR455 whose electrophoretic mobilities matched their predicted molecular weights, a finding corroborated by HPCE ( ). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) revealed a hydrodynamic diameter of 12.54 nm, and zeta potential analysis revealed a surface charge of −4.77 mV ( ). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) further demonstrated the expected triangular morphology with a uniform size distribution ( ). To evaluate serum and intracellular stability, tFNAs-miR455 were incubated in 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) or synovial mesenchymal stem cell (SMSC) lysates. The results revealed minimal degradation, confirming robust structural integrity under physiological conditions ( and ). Cellular internalization was quantified via confocal laser-scanning microscopy and flow cytometry after incubation with Cy5-labeled tFNAs-miR455. LSCM images revealed marked intracellular accumulation of Cy5-miR-455-3p ( and ). Similar results were obtained by flow cytometry, underscoring the superior delivery efficiency of the tFNAs ( ). Collectively, these data establish a solid foundation for subsequent in vitro and in vivo investigations.
 

## The protective effect of tFNAs-miR455 on SMSCs
Given that effective MSC-based repair of articular cartilage and meniscal defects hinges on robust cell proliferation, migration and chondrogenic potential, we systematically examined whether tFNAs-miR455 exerts protective and stimulatory effects on SMSCs [ , , ]. At the cellular proliferation level, 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) staining and CCK-8 assays demonstrated that tFNAs-miR455 elicited the highest proliferative response among all treatment groups, an effect attributable to the synergistic activity of miR-455-3p and the tFNAs ( ). Next, at the cellular migration level, Transwell inserts and scratch-wound assays were employed to evaluate longitudinal and lateral migratory capacities, respectively. Consistent with the proliferation data, tFNAs-miR455 significantly accelerated SMSC migration compared with either naked miR-455-3p or unmodified tFNAs ( ). Finally, we assessed the capacity of tFNAs-miR455 to drive both hyaline and fibrocartilaginous differentiation. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT–PCR) revealed significant upregulation of the Col II, Col I, Fn1 and Tnc transcripts in tFNAs-miR455-treated SMSCs ( ). Immunofluorescence and Western blot analyses further confirmed the robust induction of COL II and COL I at the protein level ( ).
 Collectively, these in vitro findings establish that tFNAs-miR455 substantially enhances SMSC proliferation, migration and chondrogenic differentiation, thereby providing a robust rationale for its translational application in meniscal tear repair.

## The anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory capacities of CeO2 nanozymes
CeO2 nanozymes were synthesized according to a previously published protocol and subjected to comprehensive physicochemical characterization [ , ]. TEM revealed that the agglomerated nanoparticles exhibited a well-defined cubic fluorite structure ( ). Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns matched the characteristic reflections of CeO2, and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) confirmed the coexistence of Ce³+ and Ce4+ oxidation states ( ). Elemental identity was additionally confirmed via energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, which indicated the successful formation of CeO2 nanozymes ( ).
 Chondrocytes, the sole resident cells of articular cartilage, are essential for cartilage homeostasis, whereas macrophages critically shape the joint immune microenvironment [ , ]. Given the reported anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and immunomodulatory properties of CeO2 nanozymes, we evaluated their bioactivity in both cell types. In RAW 264.7 macrophages, qRT–PCR revealed that CeO2 nanozymes significantly downregulated the expression of M1 markers (CD86, IL-1β and iNOS) and upregulated the expression of M2 markers (CD206 and ARG1) compared with those in the LPS-stimulated group ( ). Immunofluorescence further confirmed the reduction in iNOS expression and increase in ARG1 expression ( and ). In chondrocytes, CeO2 nanozymes increased anabolic gene expression (Col II, Acan) and suppressed catabolic marker expression (Mmp3, Mmp13) under IL-1β stimulation ( ). Immunofluorescence corroborated the increased COL II and decreased MMP13 protein levels ( ). In addition, excess ROS induce oxidative stress in chondrocytes and ultimately lead to cell death, whereas CeO2 nanozymes have been reported to possess potent ROS-scavenging capacity [ , ]. Using the DCFH-DA fluorescent probe, we found that IL-1β stimulation induced a pronounced ROS burst in chondrocytes, whereas CeO2 nanozyme treatment markedly attenuated ROS levels, thereby mitigating oxidative stress and protecting chondrocytes ( ).
Collectively, our findings demonstrate that CeO2 nanozymes exert potent anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and chondroprotective effects, providing a robust basis for their therapeutic application in meniscal tear repair.

## Preparation and characterization of S-T-N
The selection of methacrylated silk fibroin (SilMA) hydrogel is due to its unique physicochemical and biological properties tailored to address critical challenges in meniscal injury management, fulfilling requirements like bridging tears, providing mechanical support, facilitating cell/tissue ingrowth and maintaining structural stability. Compared to methacrylated hyaluronic acid (HAMA) and methacrylated gelatin (GelMA), SilMA offers distinct advantages: superior mechanical robustness and structural stability to withstand joint forces [ ]. In addition, SilMA has been reported to potentiate osteogenesis via the sustained release of parathyroid hormone (PTH) [ ]. On this basis, we hypothesized that SilMA could play a pivotal role in meniscal tear repair.

 shows the gelation profiles of the SilMA and SilMA@CeO2 hydrogels; both formulations were optically transparent. Scanning electron microscopy ( ) revealed that the pores of the SilMA hydrogels were larger than those of the SilMA@CeO2 hydrogels. Rheological analyses ( ) further demonstrated that the incorporation of CeO2 nanoparticles significantly enhanced the mechanical properties of the SilMA hydrogels. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) indicated the absence of new chemical bonds after gelation ( ). Biocompatibility was evaluated by coculturing lyophilized scaffolds with SMSCs and performing live/dead staining ( ), which confirmed excellent cytocompatibility.
 A Transwell-based coculture model was subsequently used to assess the regulatory effects of CeO2 and tFNAs-miR455 released from the scaffold on SMSCs. Consistent with our previous findings, CeO2 and tFNAs-miR455 liberated from the S-T-N scaffold robustly promoted chondrogenic differentiation ( ). Overall, we fabricated an S-T-N scaffold that integrates superior physicochemical properties with pronounced biological activity, providing a solid foundation for in vivo investigations.

## S-T-N Enhanced the endogenous repair capacity of meniscus in rabbits
The blood supply of the meniscus is divided into three zones with sequentially decreasing healing capacity [ ]. Owing to its limited vascularization, the white zone has negligible self-healing potential, making the treatment of tears in this region particularly challenging [ ]. Here, we established a rabbit model of transverse tear in the medial meniscal white zone to investigate the biological effects of the composite hydrogel system ‘S-T-N’ for meniscal tear reconstruction ( ). As shown in , the hydrogel was injected into the tear site for in situ adhesion, whereas the control group received no treatment. Three and 6 months postintervention, the meniscus was harvested for subsequent radiological, gross and histopathological analyses. To evaluate the differences in meniscal tear reconstruction and joint protection efficacy among the groups, we conducted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based analyses ( ). The coronally oriented 2D MR images revealed well-defined tearing in the meniscal triangular zone of the control group at both the 3- and 6-month timepoints, accompanied by pronounced inflammatory signals and severe articular cartilage degradation. In contrast, the S-T-N treatment group presented neomeniscal tissue regeneration in the triangular zone as early as 3 months postintervention, with progressive maturation leading to complete anatomical restoration by the study endpoint. Notably, the S-T-N group exhibited significantly attenuated intra-articular inflammatory signals and maintained pristine cartilage surface smoothness ( ). These findings collectively highlight the dual-functional regeneration capacity and microenvironment-modulating ability of the composite hydrogel system, which results in both structural reconstruction and biological remodeling of the injured meniscus.
 Macroscopic assessment of the meniscus and corresponding femoral condyle (FC) and tibial plateau (TP), as shown in , revealed that the medial meniscal white-zone tear in the control group failed to demonstrate any self-healing capacity, resulting in localized cartilage erosion at both the FC and TP. In contrast, the pure silk fibroin hydrogel group exhibited partial tear healing, with reduced tear dimensions observed. Remarkably, the S-T-N treatment group exhibited near-total closure of the meniscal defect, indicating successful repair of transverse white-zone meniscal injury. Furthermore, the articular cartilage surfaces of the FC and TP in the S-T-N group remained intact and smooth, with no evidence of secondary damage, effectively mitigating the risk of OA development. To further evaluate the histological characteristics of the reconstructed meniscus, we performed hematoxylin & eosin (H&E) staining, toluidine blue staining and collagen immunohistochemical staining ( ). Consistent with the macroscopic observations, the S-T-N treatment group demonstrated superior meniscal repair outcomes in terms of histological staining compared with the other groups ( ). The treated samples exhibited complete healing at the tear site, along with well-organized alignment of both type I and type II collagen fibers ( ). In addition, safranin O-fast green staining of the FC and TP revealed marked cartilage degeneration and significant depletion of glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) in the blank control group. In contrast, the S-T-N composite hydrogel group demonstrated superior chondroprotective efficacy, with preserved cartilage integrity and GAG retention ( ). These histological findings corroborated the gross morphological observations, further validating the therapeutic potential of the hydrogel system. These findings were also further corroborated by the Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI) scoring system ( ) and the biomechanical testing ( ).
 Taken together, the S-T-N system achieves therapeutic synergy through the integration of three core functions, with distinct yet complementary roles of CeO2 nanozymes and miR-455-3p: The SilMA scaffold provides a three-dimensional microenvironment to facilitate cell infiltration and ECM deposition, bridging meniscal tear gaps and stabilizing the repair site; tFNAs enable sustained release of miR-455-3p, directly promoting chondrogenic differentiation of MSCs in the avascular meniscal zone—a key process for functional fibrocartilage regeneration; CeO2 nanozymes scavenge ROS to mitigate oxidative stress-induced chondrocyte apoptosis in adjacent articular cartilage, ameliorating the proinflammatory microenvironment and preserving cartilage integrity. CeO2 and miR-455-3p exert effects via distinct but cooperatively reinforcing pathways without direct molecular cross-regulation: CeO2 improves the regenerative microenvironment by scavenging ROS and reducing inflammation, enhancing MSCs’ survival and activity to indirectly support miR-455-3p’s efficacy; miR-455-3p directly modulates cellular fate decisions by targeting chondrogenic genes (e.g. SOX9, COL2A1), driving MSCs toward a fibrocartilaginous lineage—augmented by SilMA’s structural support and CeO2-maintained low-ROS microenvironment. Their synergy manifests in the temporal and spatial coordination of microenvironment improvement (CeO2) and cellular differentiation (miR-455-3p), collectively addressing the dual challenges of avascular meniscus regeneration and OA progression. This synergistic combination of structural support (SilMA), genetic regulation (tFNAs-miR455) and oxidative protection (CeO2 nanozymes) thus addresses both the regenerative challenges of the avascular meniscus and the degenerative cascade in OA.

## Proteomic profiling reveals the potential mechanisms by which S-T-N promotes meniscal tear repair
We employed label-free proteomics to elucidate the mechanisms underlying S-T-N-mediated meniscal tear repair. Venn analysis revealed 7945 proteins shared between the control and S-T-N groups, with 65 unique to the S-T-N group and 53 unique to the control group ( ). Volcano and hierarchical clustering analyses revealed 534 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs; 205 upregulated and 329 downregulated in S-T-N) ( ). Notably, meniscus-relevant ECM proteins such as COL1A1 and COL6A1 were significantly upregulated in S-T-N. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that these DEPs were associated with biological processes including ‘regulation of cell population proliferation’ and ‘regulation of cell migration’, as well as various cellular components and molecular functions ( ). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis revealed significant enrichment of PI3K-Akt signaling ( ) and then the results of western blot were also further corroborated ( ). Reactome enrichment highlighted ‘collagen biosynthesis and modifying enzymes’, ‘collagen formation’ and ‘ECM organization’ ( ). Protein–protein interaction mapping identified hub proteins—including COL1A1, COL4A1, COL4A2 and COL6A2—that are central to meniscal ECM assembly ( ). Collectively, our proteomic data provide mechanistic insights into how S-T-N promotes meniscal tear repair.
 Herein, we integrate tFNA-based miRNA delivery with ROS-responsive nanoenzymes to achieve synergistic regulation. As an efficient miRNA carrier, tFNAs stably load and protect miRNA from degradation, enabling precise delivery to target cells for regulated expression of anti-inflammatory or pro-regenerative genes. Meanwhile, ROS-responsive nanoenzymes scavenge excessive ROS in the pathological microenvironment, normalizing oxidative stress to create a favorable milieu for miRNA function. This integration overcomes limitations of single-function systems: standalone miRNA delivery often suffers instability in high-ROS conditions, while ROS-scavenging nanoenzymes lack gene-regulatory capacity; together, they synergize to enhance therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, we established a rabbit model of medial meniscal white zone transverse tear to evaluate the repair effects of the S-T-N composite hydrogel system, with observations conducted at 3 and 6 months postintervention, which demonstrated promising neomeniscal tissue regeneration, attenuated inflammation and preserved cartilage integrity. However, we acknowledge that the differences in knee joint characteristics between rabbits and humans, as well as the current observation period limited to 6 months (which may not fully reflect long-term repair stability under prolonged physiological stresses), represent important limitations that need to be concerned. In future work, we will extend the observation period to longer durations (e.g. 12 months or more) to assess the long-term stability of regenerated meniscal tissue, and further explore the use of larger animal models with knee joint features more analogous to humans (such as non-human primates or goats) to systematically evaluate the impact of species-specific differences on repair outcomes, thereby providing more robust evidence for the clinical translation of the S-T-N system.

## Conclusion
We constructed a novel tFNA-miRNA delivery system integrated within a CeO2-loaded SilMA hydrogel to promote meniscal repair, denoted S-T-N. This system synergistically combines the structural stability and cellular uptake efficiency of tFNAs, the chondrogenic potential of miRNA-455-3p, the antioxidant/anti-inflammatory properties of CeO2 nanozymes, and the mechanical support of SilMA hydrogels to address critical challenges in meniscal healing. S-T-N significantly enhanced miRNA stability and delivery efficiency, enabling precise regulation of SMSCs. The system promoted SMSC migration to injury sites and directed their differentiation toward fibrochondrogenic lineages, which are essential for functional meniscal regeneration. Concurrently, CeO2 nanozymes mitigated oxidative stress and inflammation, creating a pro-regenerative microenvironment. Furthermore, the SilMA hydrogel provided mechanical reinforcement and bridged meniscal tears, facilitating tissue integration. Overall, S-T-N represents a breakthrough strategy for achieving structural and functional meniscal regeneration, with significant clinical translational potential in regenerative medicine.
