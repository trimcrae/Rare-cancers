## Abstract

## Background
Protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs) play key roles in β-cell function and diabetes development. PTPN2 is a candidate gene for type 1 diabetes (T1D) that negatively regulates JAK/STAT signalling. However, the impact of PTPN2 deficiency on the differentiation and functionality of human stem cell-derived somatic metabolic cells remains unclear.

## Methods
PTPN2 expression in β cells from T1D organ donors and during the differentiation of human stem cell-derived islets (SC-islets) was evaluated using single-cell RNA-Sequencing (scRNA-Seq) datasets. We differentiated CRISPR-Cas12a genome-edited PTPN2-deficient H1 human embryonic stem cells (H1-hESCs) into SC-islets, and scRNA-Seq was performed. The maturation and functionality of PTPN2-deficient SC-islets were assessed by implantation under the kidney capsule of NOD-SCID mice.

## Results
scRNA-Seq analysis showed that PTPN2 expression was increased in β cells from recently diagnosed T1D and decreased in long-standing T1D organ donors compared with controls. Conversely, we found that PTPN2 expression was decreased at the early stages of SC-islet differentiation and reconstituted at the later stages, suggesting a developmental dynamic. PTPN2 deficiency exacerbated interferon-induced inflammatory signalling in stem cells and differentiated somatic metabolic cells. Interestingly, PTPN2 deficiency increased hedgehog signalling and reduced SC-islet differentiation efficiency in vitro. In addition, PTPN2-knockout SC-islets exhibited reduced glycaemic control after implantation in vivo, mediated by reduced endocrine cell identity and enhanced interferon signalling.

## Conclusions
Our study postulates a key role of PTPN2 in preserving β-cell function during inflammatory and metabolic stress in SC-islets.

## Background
Inflammation-mediated pancreatic β-cell destruction is a hallmark of type 1 diabetes (T1D), resulting in diminished β-cell mass and insulin production [ – ]. During insulitis, type I and type II interferons activate the JAK/STAT signalling pathway in β cells via a cascade of tyrosine phosphorylation, exacerbating cytokine production, immune cell infiltration in the pancreas, and β-cell dysfunction [ ]. Baricitinib is a pharmacological inhibitor of JAK proteins 1 and 2 used to treat rheumatoid arthritis. Repurposing the drug in a phase 2 clinical trial with recently diagnosed T1D patients showed that baricitinib improved human C-peptide levels during mixed meal insulin secretion [ ]. This indicates that the pharmacological inhibition of JAK/STAT signalling preserves β-cell function in patients with T1D.
The superfamily of protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs) regulates tyrosine phosphorylation implicated in several pathways, including JAK/STAT signalling. Classical PTPs share highly similar catalytic mechanisms conserved in receptor- and non-receptor-types [ ]. PTPN2 and PTPN22 are non-receptor PTPs and known risk genes for T1D [ , ]. Lack of PTPN2 function caused by single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) rs2542151 and rs1893217 results in a higher risk of developing T1D and earlier disease development [ ]. Complete depletion of PTPN2 in mice results in severe autoimmunity and premature lethality [ ]. Furthermore, specific PTPN2 deletion in T cells of non-obese diabetic (NOD) mice causes earlier insulitis and increased diabetes incidence [ ], suggesting the importance of PTPN2 in autoimmune diabetes.
We and others previously demonstrated that PTPN2 decreases JAK/STAT signalling in human and rodent β cells [ – ]. PTPN2 deficiency enhances calcium-dependent ER stress response in human β cells [ ]. Recent studies have shown that PTPN2 deficiency reduces mitochondrial respiration, and the expression of genes involved in insulin secretion in cytokine-treated murine islets [ ]. These results indicate that PTPN2 regulates key pathways involved in β-cell function under inflammatory and ER stress. Here, we show that PTPN2 expression is decreased in β cells from long-standing T1D organ donors and during SC-islet differentiation. In addition, PTPN2 inhibits the JAK/STAT pathway as an anti-inflammatory protein in pluripotent stem and somatic metabolic cells. PTPN2 regulates stress responses in implanted SC-islets while preserving the endocrine cell identity.

## Methods
The present work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

## Ethical statement
The experimental procedures were approved by the Belgian Regulations for Animal Care, and the animal protocols underwent approval from the Commission d’Etique du Bien-Être Animal (CEBEA), Faculté de Médecine, Université Libre de Bruxelles (Dossiers No. 732N and 802N). All animal experiments were performed in strict compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. H1-hESCs (WiCell, Madison, WI) are registered with hPSCreg for use in the European Union.

## Mice
Non-obese diabetic (NOD)/Severe-Combined-Immunodeficiency (SCID) mice were housed and inbred in the animal facility of ULB (Brussels, Belgium) according to the National Institutes of Health (NIH) guide for the care and use of laboratory animals. All experimental procedures were approved and performed following the Commission d’Ethique du Bien-être Animal of the ULB (reference 732N and 802N). The number of mice used was decided based on own previous experiments. The housing of all mice was under semi-barrier conditions, and the animals were fed sterile food and water ad libitum. Mice were screened for the onset of diabetes by evaluating the glucose levels in blood (AccuCheck; Roche Diagnostics, Vilvoorde, Belgium). For anaesthesia, we used 4–5% isoflurane with a gas flow rate of 1.0–1.5 LPM and maintained using 1–3% isoflurane. Reflexes were checked before starting and during the procedure. After the surgery, a buprenorphine injection (0.05 mg/kg in PBS) was administered intramuscularly. Explanted kidneys were collected at the time of nephrectomy. Mice were euthanized using CO₂ inhalation following standard ethical guidelines.

## Stem cell proliferation and colony formation analysis
PTPN2 knockout stem cells were generated by CRISPR-Cas12a as previously described [ , ]. Trypan Blue 0.4% (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to quantify the cell viability with the bright field function of LUNA-FL™ Dual Fluorescence Cell Counter (Logos Biosystems).
For the colony-forming assay, the PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ stem cells were dissociated into single-cell suspension with Gentle Cell Dissociation Reagent (STEMCELL Technologies). 20,000 cells were seeded in Matrigel-coated 6-well plates in technical triplicates in the presence of Y-27632 ROCK pathway inhibitor (STEMCELL Technologies). The medium was changed every day. After two weeks, the colonies were fixed with 4% PFA solution containing 0.5% crystal violet. The colony number and stained area were quantified with CellProfiler [ ].

## H1-hESCs differentiation into SC-islets
CRISPR-Cas12a-edited H1-hESCs were differentiated into SC-islets (20–50% insulin-positive cells) as previously described [ , ]. Briefly, PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ clones were dissociated into a single-cell suspension using Accutase. 2 million cells were seeded in 6-well plates and 250,000 cells in 8-wells culture slides. Cells were differentiated using a 7-stage protocol. The first four stages of the differentiation, until pancreatic progenitor stage, was conducted in monolayer. Subsequently, cells were dispersed into a single-cell suspension, and 1 million cells were seeded into wells of 24-Aggrewells plates for the final stages of differentiation.

## SC-islet implantation in NOD-SCID mice
1000 or 3000 SC-islets PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ (control) were implanted under the kidney capsule of random NOD-SCID mice and allowed to mature for 16 weeks. Once SC-islets reached maturity, two doses of 150 µg/g of STZ were intraperitoneally injected to destroy the murine β cells. One week after the second STZ injection, the engrafted kidney was removed to induce diabetes. Human C-peptide levels were measured at week 8. Glycemia was measured at weeks 8, 12 and 16, and daily after STZ treatment until 3 days after diabetes was detected. Blood samples were collected from the tail tip and glycemia was measured using a glucometer (Accu-Check Performa, Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Additionally, 10–15 drops of blood were collected in capillary EDTA tubes (Sarstedt) for the measurement of human C-peptide using the Ultrasensitive human C-peptide ELISA (Mercodia, Cat#10–1141-01).

## Hepatocyte-like cell differentiation and cytokine treatment
H1 PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ stem cells were differentiated according to a previously published protocol [ , ]. Briefly, the cells were dissociated into single cells using Gentle Cell Dissociation Reagent (STEMCELL Technologies) to seed them on laminin-521-coated plates and reach optimal confluency. The cells were differentiated into definitive endoderm, hepatocyte progenitors and eventually hepatocyte-like cells. The efficiency of the differentiation was measured using a human albumin ELISA kit (Sigma-Aldrich). After differentiation, the cells were treated with IFN-γ (50 U/µL, PeproTech) for 1 h and the cell lysates were collected for western blotting.

## Western blotting
Immunoblotting was performed using the antibodies specified in Supplementary Table S2. Protein samples were collected in cell lysis buffer for western blotting (Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA). Equal quantities of proteins were resolved by 10% SDS PAGE and immunoblotted as indicated [ , ]. All blots were quantified using ImageJ® software (Version: 2.3.0/1.53q) [ ]. The intensity values of the protein bands were normalized by the values of housekeeping protein GAPDH or β-actin.

## ATAC-Seq and bulk RNA-Seq sample preparations and bioinformatic analysis
Upon 70% confluency, the stem cells were treated with IFN-γ (1000 U/mL) for 24 h. A total of 50,000 cells were used for ATAC-Seq. The remaining cells were frozen as a pellet for later processing for RNA-Seq.
The samples for ATAC-Seq were immediately processed. Resuspension buffer was prepared using 10 mM Tris–HCL (pH 7.5), 10 mM NaCl, 3 mM MgCl2, and nuclease free water. This buffer was used to prepare the cell lysis and wash buffer. The cells were washed with PBS and pelleted by centrifugation. Afterwards, the cells were lysed with the lysis buffer prepared using the resuspension buffer with 0.1% v/v NP-40, 0.1% v/v Tween-20 and 0.01% v/v Digitonin. To complete the cell lysis, the cells were incubated on ice for 3 min. 1 mL of wash buffer was added before gently inverting the tubes 3 times. The wash buffer was also prepared using the resuspension buffer with 0.1% v/v Tween-20. To separate the cytoplasm (supernatant) and the nuclei (pellet), the samples were centrifuged at 500 g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully discarded. The open chromatin sites were selected with the Nextera DNA library kit using Tagment DNA buffer (Illumina, San Diego, USA). The transposition reaction was added to the pellet and mixed by gentle pipetting. Afterwards, the mix was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min at 112 g. The DNA was isolated using Qiagen MinElute reaction CleanUp mix according to the manufacturer’s guidelines (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany).
The pelleted cells for RNA-Seq were used to isolate RNA using a Qiagen Mini RNeasy kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines.
The samples for ATAC-Seq and Bulk RNA-Seq were sequenced by the BRIGHT core facility (Brussels, Belgium). The results were further analysed by HOMER [ ] or DESeq2 [ ]. The volcano plots were made with Prism, GraphPad (GraphPad Software 9, La Jolla, CA). Features were filtered based on p-value < 0.05 and log2FoldChange < 1.
Rank-Rank Hypergeometric Overlap (RRHO) analysis of β cells was used to compare the PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ stem cells to differentially expressed genes in EndoC-βH1 cells control or PTPN2 silencing [ ] by the RRHO package in RStudio.

## scRNA-Seq sample preparation and bioinformatic analysis
scRNA-Seq datasets were obtained from the human pancreatic analysis program [ ]. 3 healthy and 3 single-autoantibody positive organ donors were used as controls and compared with 3 early and 2 late T1D donors (Supplementary Table S1). The donors were selected based on the highest number of β cells, gender, age and BMI. Publicly available datasets were used to perform the analysis on SC-islets, human islets [ , ] and murine pancreata [ ] during autoimmune diabetes development. Each analysis was performed separately.
The fastq files were mapped to the human genome (GRCh38) with cell ranger v7. The Seurat v5 package was used in RStudio to filter out the low quality of cells, the doublets, the ambient RNA, and integrate the data of the respective analysis. The data was normalized by SCTransform and the cell populations were annotated based on previously published datasets [ , ]. Heatmaps were generated with DoHeatmap on the average expression of the PTPs. Violin plots were generated by the Seurat package.
SC-islets were generated and dispersed as previously described [ ]. The single cells were fixed with the 10X genomics fixation kit (CG000478). The fixed cells were processed and prepared by the 10X genomic flex kit and sequencing was performed by the Australian Genome Research Facility (AGRF). The resulting sequencing reads were aligned to the hg38 reference dataset (GRCh38) and demultiplexed using CellRanger. For data analysis, raw counts were filtered based on the number of genes (nFeature_RNA), and the percentage of mitochondrial genes (percent.mt). Cells with less than 100 features (nFeature_RNA), over 1% mitochondrial genes (percent.mt), and genes expressed in less than 2 cells were excluded from the analysis. The remaining cells were converted into a “sce” object using the SingleCellExperiment package v1.12.0 to facilitate doublet removal. To remove doublets from the dataset, an unsupervised approach was used with the scDBlFinder package v1.4.0. Finally, the filtered data was analysed using Seurat v5 in RStudio.
The remaining 7407 and 7704 cells of PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ SC-islets, respectively, were normalized using the SCTransform function with the top 1,000 variable features. This function returns the Pearson residuals from regularized negative binomial regression. After normalization, the dimensions were reduced by Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). In UMAP, each dot represents a cell positioned based on its PCA values. The above steps were carried out separately for the PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ samples and then merged into a single dataset using the merge function from Seurat. Clusters were annotated in a supervised manner based on the cell signature markers in the literature [ ]. Further bioinformatic analysis was performed on the merged dataset.
To perform in-depth analysis of the separate cell types, the corresponding cluster was selected in a subset to compare gene expression and elucidate subclusters. The corresponding cell type cluster was subset from the entire dataset using the subset function from Seurat. Differential gene expression was performed for the subclusters using the FindAllMarkers function with default parameters in the standard Seurat pipeline. The top 10 genes obtained using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test are presented in dot, violin, or feature plots per cluster. Volcano plots and bar charts were generated using Prism software (GraphPad Software 9, La Jolla, CA). Features were filtered based on p-value < 0.05 and log2FoldChange < 1. Pathways were selected by an adjusted p-value higher than 0.05.
To assess the interactions between the ligand and receptor molecules, CellPhoneDB [ ] was used for cell communication analysis. The input data was extracted and generated according to the documentation of CellPhoneDB [ ]. Significant mean and cell communication statistics (p-value < 0.05) were calculated based on the interaction and the normalised cell matrix achieved by Seurat normalisation.

## Bulk RNA-sequencing of implanted SC-islets
Human embryonic H1 stem cells were differentiated towards SC-islets and implanted under the kidney capsule of NOD/SCID according as described [ , ]. The grafted tissues were collected during survival nephrectomy, snap frozen, and stored at – 80 0C. Total RNA was isolated using RNeasy kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany). mRNA was sequenced by NovoGene (Munich, Germany). The reads were mapped to the human (GRCh38) and mouse genome (mm39). Volcano plots were generated with Prism, GraphPad (GraphPad Software 9, La Jolla, CA), pathway analysis was performed using GSEA (UC San Diego and Broad Institute) and cell types were estimated by EnrichR (Ma’ayan lab).

## Histology and immunofluorescence
Grafted SC-islets were fixed with formalin for 24 h and embedded in paraffin, cut into 7 μm sections and prepared for confocal microscopy as described previously [ , , ]. Immunoblotting was performed using the antibodies specified in Supplementary Table S2. The islet-like structures were imaged on Scanning Resonant AX-R Confocal microscope (Nikon Europe B.V., Amstelveen, The Netherlands). The images were processed, and intensities measured using ImageJ® software (Version: 2.3.0/1.53q) [ ]. The fraction of cells expressing insulin was measured by immunofluorescence staining as previously described [ , , ].

## Statistical analysis
The results are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Student t test was used for comparisons between two groups. Differences among groups were assessed with two-way ANOVA or repeated-measures ANOVA. Statistical analyses were assessed with Graphpad Prism software (GraphPad Software 9, La Jolla, CA). Differences were regarded as statistically significant if *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, or ***P < 0.001.

## Results

## PTPN2 expression is decreased in β cells during T1D
We aimed to investigate the expression of PTPs during the development of autoimmune diabetes. Single-cell transcriptomic datasets were selected from matched healthy, single-autoantibody positive, recently diagnosed T1D (early), and long-standing T1D organ donors (late) from the human pancreatic analysis program (HPAP [ ]) (males, age 20 ± 15, BMI 23 ± 14, n = 2–3, Supplementary Table S1). Long-standing T1D was determined upon a disease duration of longer than 1 year. After data integration, β cells (INS+), α cells (GCG+), δ cells (SST+), ductal cells (KRT19+), acinar cells (CPA1+), stellate cells (COL1A1+), and endothelial cells (PECAM1+) were detected (Fig.  A–C). PTP expression was determined in the cell types ranked on their expression in β cells (Fig.  D). PTPRN and PTPRN2 were the most expressed receptor-type PTP genes in endocrine cells, including β cells. Among the non-receptor PTPs, PTPN4, PTPN1, PTPN11, PTPN2, PTPN12, represented the top-five highly expressed in β cells (Fig.  E). The sum of the average expression of receptor type (PTPR) and non-receptor type (PTPN) PTPs tended to increase in β cells of early T1D and decrease in late T1D (Fig.  F). The single-cell expression of PTPN1, PTPN2, PTPN11, PTPRA, PTPRF, and PTPRK exhibited a similar pattern in β cells (Fig.  F, Supplementary Figure S1A). In α cells, the average expression of receptor and non-receptor types of PTPs decreased in late T1D compared with healthy organ donors. The expression tended to increase in single-autoantibody positive and early T1D donors (Supplementary Figure S1B and C), suggesting an early compensatory upregulation of phosphatase activity in response to immune stress. However, our current dataset does not allow us to determine whether this increase in PTPN2 expression is causally linked to α-cell dysfunction or represents a secondary response to immune activation. This pattern is consistent with reports that α cell dysfunction preceding diabetes onset in single auto-antibody positive individuals [ , ].
 The temporal shift of the PTPN2 expression during early and late T1D suggests an adaptive role for PTPN2, in which its upregulation may counteract inflammatory signalling. In line with this, inflammatory signalling similar to early diabetes, induces PTPN2 expression in EndoC-βH1 cells [ ]. However, oxidative stress induced by inflammation can inhibit PTPN2 activity in the autoimmune pancreas [ ], and chronic immune stress could lead to PTPN2 depletion [ ], inducing β-cell sensitivity in T1D.
We confirmed these results in an autoimmune diabetes mouse model [ ] and assessed the changes in PTP expression during insulitis [ ]. Single-cell RNA-Seq dataset of female 8-, 14-, and 16-week-old NOD-mice represents the transition from peri-insulitis to insulitis in most of the islets [ ]. We found an even distribution of β cells (Ins1+, Ins2+), α cells (Gcg+), δ cells (Sst+), ductal cells (Krt19+), acinar cells (Cpa1+), stellate cells (Col1a1+), and endothelial cells (Pecam1+), defined by their respective gene markers (Supplementary Figure S1D-F). Heatmaps of the average expression of PTPs showed that Ptprn and Ptprn2 were highly expressed in endocrine cells, which is in line with the human dataset. Ptpn11, Ptpn1, and Ptpn2 were highly expressed in all cell types, except for acinar cells (Supplementary Figure S1G). An increase in Ptprn, Ptprf, Ptpn11, and Ptpn1 expression levels was observed in the β-cell cluster in 16-week-old mice compared with 8- and 14-week-old NOD mice (Supplementary Figure S1H). A similar pattern was observed in the average expression of non-receptor type (PTPN) or receptor type (PTPR) PTPs (Supplementary Figure S1I). PTP expression in the α cells also showed an increase for 16-week-old mice compared with the other groups (Supplementary Figure S1J).

## PTPN2 expression is dynamic in β-like cell differentiation
Mature β cells can lose their identity and dedifferentiate to a precursor state under inflammatory stress during T1D progression [ ]. We hypothesized that the expression levels of PTPs can also be affected by β-cell (de) differentiation. Thus, we compared PTP expression by single-cell RNA-Seq in human SC-islet differentiation (stages 5–7) [ ], matured SC-islets after implantation in NOD/SCID mice (for 1 and 6 months) [ ], and islets from cadaveric donors [ ]. The SC-islets from this study has been reported to contain 30–50% of insulin positive cells at stage 7. The glucagon-positive proportion increases from 10 to 40%. The functionality of these cells was determined by insulin perifusion in which the SC-islets show a glucose-stimulated insulin secretion after 1 week of in vitro maturation [ ]. β-like cells (INS+), α-like cells (GCG+), δ-like cells (SST+), enterochromaffin-cells (EC cells) (FEV+), and endocrine progenitors (FOXA1+) were detected in the analysis (Fig.  G–I). PTPRN, PTPRN2, PTPN11, PTPN3, and PTPN18 were the highest expressed PTPs in β cells and β-like cells (Fig.  J). PTPN2 decreased from stage 5 toward implanted β-like cells (Fig.  K). The protein expression of several PTPs, including PTPRK, PTPRM, PTPN3, PTPN11, PTPN12, PTPN6, and PTPN2, decreased in stage 1 and was activated again later in the differentiation process (Fig.  L). PTPN2 levels were significantly decreased in stages 1, 3, and 7 compared with the stem cells (Supplementary Figure S1K). In stage 1, definitive endoderm formation is induced by ActivinA (ActA) and varying concentrations of CHIR99021. We tested both molecules and found that CHIR99021, a β-catenin-mediated WNT activator, was sufficient to decrease PTPN2 expression in stage 1 (Supplementary Figure S1L). This suggests that WNT signalling activation may repress PTPN2 during early development/ definitive endoderm induction. PTPN2 was not significantly reduced in α-like cells during the SC-islet differentiation, suggesting cell specificity (Supplementary Figure S1M). Overall, our data indicate that PTPN2 expression is decreased in β cells in established T1D and at early stages of SC-islet differentiation. PTPN2 is induced in β cells in early diagnosed T1D, pre-diabetic NOD mice, and later stages of SC-islet differentiation, suggesting a temporally dynamic, cytokine-dependent response, which could be related to inflammatory signalling [ ].

## PTPN2 deficiency enhances interferon-mediated inflammatory signalling
We previously showed the anti-inflammatory role of PTPN2 in β cells and SC-islets is mediated by the dephosphorylation of interferon-induced STAT1 signalling [ ]. Here, we determined whether this regulatory mechanism is restricted to β-like cells or broadly conserved across undifferentiated and differentiated cells. We used gene-edited PTPN2 knockout embryonic stem cells by CRISPR-Cas12a (Fig.  A) [ ]. PTPN2−/− stem cells exhibited a higher colony number compared with control cells (Fig.  B), in line with the previously described role of PTPN2 in cell proliferation [ ]. We studied PTPN2-mediated gene regulation in undifferentiated stem cells exposed to IFN-γ for 24 h by ATAC-Seq and RNA-Seq. The results of ATAC-Seq were compared with RNA-Seq performed on the same samples. IFN-γ-induced STAT1 phosphorylation was enhanced by PTPN2 knockout (Fig.  C). PTPN2 deficiency led to fewer regions of open chromatin and fewer differentially expressed genes (DEGs) compared to control, in cells without IFN-γ treatment (Supplementary Figure S2A and B).
 IFN-γ increased the number of open chromatin sites compared with the control condition, which was exacerbated by PTPN2 deficiency (Fig.  D). Inflammatory-related genes (STAT1, IFIT3, and DUSP4) showed enhanced open chromatin in PTPN2−/− cells. 5 motifs were increased by IFN-γ treatment in PTPN2−/− cells; ISRE, IRF1, IRF2, IRF3, and FOS, whereas only two motifs were significantly regulated in PTPN2+/+ cells: EGR1 and WT1 (Fig.  E). Pathway analysis indicated an upregulation of the innate immune response, IFN-γ response by PTPN2 depletion, and cytokine exposure (Supplementary Figure S2C).
STAT1 and UBA7 were found to be upregulated in all datasets under cytokine treatment (Fig.  F). 16 genes overlapped in the ATAC-Seq and RNA-Seq for the PTPN2−/− stem cells, related to apoptosis (KREMEN1, GLRX) or inflammation (AIM2, NRG1) (Fig.  F). RNA-Seq also confirmed the amplified inflammatory signalling in HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C, IFI35, IFI6, and IFIT3 genes in PTPN2−/− stem cells (Fig.  G). Rank-Rank hypergeometric overlap (RRHO) analysis indicated high similarity in IFN-γ-treated PTPN2 knockout stem cells and PTPN2 knockdown EndoC-βH1 β cells [ ] (Fig.  H). IFN-γ treatment in PTPN2−/− stem cells induced several pathways involved with the immune response, such as the cellular response to type II interferon and innate immune response with a high normalized enrichment score (NES). An increase was also observed in response to cytokines and type II interferon with a lower NES in PTPN2+/+ compared with PTPN2−/− cells (Fig.  I and Supplementary Figure S2D).
We aimed to determine the role of PTPN2 in IFN-γ response in distinct metabolically active somatic cells. PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ stem cells were differentiated into hepatocyte-like cells [ , ], showing the induction of albumin expression in which PTPN2−/− cells had an earlier and higher albumin induction compared with PTPN2+/+ cells (Supplementary Figure S2E). In line with stem cells and β cells, an increase in interferon-induced STAT1 phosphorylation was observed in PTPN2-deficient hepatocyte-like cells (Supplementary Figure S2F). Our data suggest that the regulation of the IFN-γ response and STAT1 signalling by PTPN2 is not cell type-dependent.

## PTPN2 deficiency affects the hedgehog signalling pathway in SC-islets.
In the scRNA-Seq analysis, we observed decreased PTPN2 expression from stage 5 to NOD/SCID implanted cells during SC-islet differentiation (Fig.  L and Supplementary Figure S1K). To further define the role of PTPN2 in β-like cell generation, we differentiated PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ human embryonic stem cells into SC-islets [ ] and validated gene knockout at stage 7 by western blotting (Fig.  A). No changes in insulin expression or differentiation markers were observed during the SC-islet differentiation in PTPN2 knockout and control cells (Fig.  B and Supplementary Figure S3A). Immunostaining of insulin-positive cells at stage 7 showed approximately 20–50% of cells are insulin-positive, while 2–5% are glucagon- or somatostatin-positive, however, no difference between PTPN2-deficient and control β-like cells was observed (Fig.  C). We performed a deeper transcriptomic analysis using single-cell RNA-Seq at stage 7 of differentiation. Cell cluster annotation revealed pancreatic endoderm (SOX9+, FOXA1+), α-like cells (GCG+), δ-like cells (SST+), early (INS+), and late β-like cells (INS+, MAFB+, SLC2A1+) defined by their respective gene markers (Fig.  D-E). Insulin expression was observed in β-, α-, and δ-like cells which can be expected before the complete maturation of SC-islets. Early and late β-like cells were distinguished based on the expression of maturity genes, such as MAFB and SLC2A1 (encoding for GLUT1). scRNA-seq allowed us to define the maturation stages of β-like cells (e.g., early versus late β-like cells) based on a larger panel of genes, compared to immunostaining which only detected insulin (Fig.  C). A slightly lower number of late β-like cells (26% PTPN2−/− vs. 37% PTPN2+/+) and an increased number of pancreatic endoderm cells (18% PTPN2−/− vs. 10% PTPN2+/+) were observed in PTPN2−/− SC-islets. The remaining cell types exhibited similar percentages in PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ SC-islets (Fig.  D).
 Early β-like cells showed two subclusters with high endocrine identity (INS+, NKX6.1+, and CHGA+), or EC cell identity (TPH1+, LMX1A+, SLC18A1+, and low INS) (Fig.  F–G). The subclusters had a similar distribution among PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ SC-islets (Fig.  F). The volcano plot shows changes in the DEGs associated with notch signalling (DLK1, SOX9), and apoptosis (AOC, GSN) (Fig.  H). Endocrine pancreas development was downregulated in PTPN2−/− SC-islets (Fig.  I), whereas GO pathway analysis indicated the alteration of pathways related to neuron development (Fig.  J). The SC-islet and neuron differentiation share high similarity in transcription factors and gene targets [ ], such as SYT11, which were modulated by PTPN2 deficiency (Fig.  H and J).
Late β-like cells showed two subclusters with differential maturity defined by the expression of INS, MAFB, and SLC2A1 and a comparable fraction over PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ cells (Fig.  K–L). Similar to early β-like cells, PTPN2 deficiency in late β-like cells affected the expression of several genes related to notch signalling (DLK1, HES1, SOX9) (Fig.  M). SLC2A1 expression (encoding for GLUT1) was significantly reduced in PTPN2-deficient late β-like cells compared with controls (Fig.  L). The ERK1-ERK2 cascade and hedgehog signalling were upregulated in response to PTPN2 deficiency. As hedgehog signalling is known to impair β-cell identity and insulin secretion, further investigation is warranted to determine if its upregulation in PTPN2-deficient cells drives functional impairment or dedifferentiation. Furthermore, endocrine pancreatic development was downregulated in PTPN2−/− SC-islets (Fig.  N). The GO pathway analysis indicated the modulation of the chromosome region and chromosome regulation (Fig.  O), indicating a possible effect of PTPN2 deficiency on histones and chromatin remodelling.
α-like cells showed three separate subclusters with differences in the expression of GCG, INS, ARX, and NKX2.2 (Supplementary Figure S3B-C). PTPN2-knockout SC-islets had a lower number of double hormonal α-like cells expressing insulin and glucagon (34% PTPN2−/− vs. 45% PTPN2+/+) and a slightly increased number of precursor α-like cells expressing NKX2.2 (17% PTPN2−/− vs. 10% PTPN2+/+) (Supplementary Figure S3B). PTPN2-deficiency affected similar genes in α-like cells as for early and late β-like cells, as shown in the volcano plot (Supplementary Figure S3D). The oestrogen response was upregulated, whereas E2F targets were downregulated in PTPN2−/− cells. GO pathway analysis indicated the modulation of chromosome organization by PTPN2 depletion (Supplementary Figure S3E-F).
In δ-like cell, three subclusters were identified (Supplementary Figure S3G-H). PTPN2 deficiency affected gene expression in δ-like cells, similar to the observations in the other endocrine cell types (Supplementary Figure S3I). In the highest significantly modulated pathways, we noticed the upregulation of the oestrogen response, which was also observed in early β-like and α-like cells (Supplementary Figure S3J). Regulatory pathways, such as DNA binding and regulation of RNA binding, were modified by PTPN2 deficiency in the GO pathway analysis (Supplementary Figure S3K).
Pancreatic endoderm cells showed four subclusters, defined as ductal-like cells by KRT19+, endocrine progenitors by SOX9+, acinar-like cells by CPA1+, and proliferating cells by MKI67+ (Supplementary Figure S4A-B). PTPN2 deficiency affected similar genes as for the other endocrine cell types, such as DDX3Y, TRIM4, and TUSC1 (Supplementary Figure S4C). A significant decrease in pancreatic β-cell specification was observed in the pathway analysis, while the hedgehog signalling was upregulated due to PTPN2 deficiency (Supplementary Figure S4D). The GO pathway analysis indicated changes in the ECM composition of SC-islets due to the extracellular matrix structural constituent and the external encapsulating structure following PTPN2 deficiency in the pancreatic endoderm cells (Supplementary Figure S4E).
Cell-to-cell communication analysis of endocrine cells indicated activation by PTPN2 deficiency in the connection of WNT4 with SFRP1 or FZD3_LRP5 by PTPN2 deficiency in δ-like cells to other endocrine cells. An increase in insulin binding to its receptor was observed in PTPN2-deficient early β-like cells (Supplementary Figure S4F).
Our data suggest that PTPN2 affects the expression of genes in the different endocrine cell types following SC-islet differentiation, such as DDX3Y, TRIM4, SYT11, DLK1, and TUSC1. These genes have been implicated in the immune response in other cell types or β-cell functionality [ – ]. PTPN2 deficiency reduced the efficiency of β-like cell generation and increased the hedgehog signalling pathway in late β-like cells and pancreatic endoderm.

## Functionality is decreased in mature PTPN2-deficient SC-islets implanted in NOD/SCID mice
Our transcriptomic analysis showed differences in the PTPN2−/− SC-islet maturation genes and pathways but not major changes in the overall number of insulin-positive cells. We next studied the in vivo maturation post-implantation of SC-islets under the kidney capsule of NOD/SCID mice (Fig.  A). To evaluate the effect of different numbers of SC-islets during maturation, we implanted 1000 or 3000.
 PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ SC-islets were implanted from independent but parallel differentiations and with comparable insulin-positive cells (≈30%) between the genotypes. The maturation of the graft can be estimated by the reduced glycaemic levels in mice corresponding to the “humanized” glucose levels (around 5.5 mM) [ ]. At the lower graft dose (1000 SC-islets), mice implanted with PTPN2−/− SC-islets maintained significantly higher glycemia than controls at weeks 8 and 12, indicating delayed functional maturation of the PTPN2-deficient grafts (Fig.  B). In contrast, with 3000 SC-islets, mice in both groups achieved humanised glycemia before week 16, and glycemic levels converged between genotypes by that time, reflecting a graft mass–dependent SC-islet function effect (Fig.  B). The levels of human C-peptide at 8 weeks were lower in mice implanted with 1000 SC-islets as compared to 3000 but were similar between PTPN2−/− or PTPN2+/+ cells (Fig.  C). This suggests that the reduction in glycaemic levels in PTPN2−/− SC-islets is mediated by a lower maturation/functionality and not graft survival. Moreover, fasted human C-peptide levels at 12 and 16 weeks showed no differences between PTPN2-knockout and control SC-islets (Fig.  D). The endogenous β cells were ablated with streptozotocin (STZ) 16 weeks after implantation. PTPN2-deficient SC-islets showed reduced glycaemic control after STZ treatment compared with PTPN2+/+ SC-islets (Fig.  E). Graft retrieval by nephrectomy led to hyperglycaemia, indicating the efficient destruction of endogenous β cells by STZ. The area under the curve shows a tendency for higher overall glycemia in PTPN2−/− grafted SC-islet cells compared to PTPN2+/+ (Fig.  E). Quantification of insulin fluorescence intensity in the grafts showed a significant decrease in insulin content in PTPN2-deficient SC-islets (Fig.  F). Importantly, this decrease was not accompanied by changes in the relative proportions of endocrine cell types within the grafts (Supplementary Figure S4G), indicating that the reduction reflects impaired β-cell identity or function rather than loss of β-cell number.
To gain mechanistic insights into these functional impairments, we performed bulk RNA-Seq on the retrieved grafts. The transcriptomic analysis showed a decrease in markers for human β-cell functionality, such as INS, IAPP, ABCC8, GCK, ISL1, and CHGA in PTPN2−/− SC-islets compared to controls (Fig.  G). Furthermore, α-cells markers (GCG, IRX, MAFB, and TTR), δ-cell marker (SST), and transcription factors related to pancreatic development (NKX2-2, NKX6-1, FOXA2, NEUROD1, PDX1, and PAX6) were reduced in PTPN2-knockout compared with control grafts. Interestingly, an increase in the human inflammation markers (STAT1, IFIT1, IFIT3, IRF2, PIAS3, BCL2, and HLA-H) was observed in PTPN2−/− graft (Fig.  G). Pathway analysis indicated an upregulation in the interferon α and γ response and downregulation of β-cell identity, regulation of hormone levels, and hormone secretion (Fig.  H), confirming the reduced functionality and cell identity of PTPN2-knockout SC-islets. The presence of inflammatory transcripts in the graft is consistent with activation of both graft-intrinsic and host innate immune pathways. Given that NOD/SCID mice lack adaptive immunity such as T and B cells, the enhanced inflammatory response in PTPN2−/− grafts likely involved murine innate immune cells, such as macrophages and dendritic cells. Their recruitment can amplify the local stress on the graft, contributing to impaired glycaemic control. To determine the environment and possible infiltration of murine immune cells during the maturation of the human SC-islets, we mapped the sequenced reads to the mouse genome and noticed at least 15% of the samples contained mouse reads (Fig.  I). Plasmacytoid dendritic cells, neutrophils, and macrophages were detected in the grafted tissue from both PTPN2−/− and PTPN2+/+ SC-islets, (Fig.  J), which likely contributes to the cytokine-mediated activation of STAT1 signalling [ ]. Our data suggest that PTPN2 regulates the anti-inflammatory response in SC-islets while preserving the endocrine functionality to maintain normoglycemia.

## Discussion
The use of stem cell-derived islets as β-cell replacement therapy for T1D is providing promising results. A phase 1–2 clinical study has shown insulin independence in established T1D patients after the implantation of SC-islets (Zimislecel—former VX-880) [ ]. Modulating SC-islets to obtain immune-resilient cells would enhance the treatment by bypassing the immune response. We demonstrated that PTPN2 regulates interferon-mediated JAK/STAT signalling in pluripotent stem and somatic metabolic cells and contributes to the maintenance of functionality in SC-islets implanted in NOD/SCID mice under inflammatory stress.
We found transcriptional activation of several PTPs in β cells at the early stages of T1D and downregulation in established T1D, suggesting a coordinated modulation. In line with these findings, an increase of PTPN2 expression has been previously observed in the synovial tissues of patients with rheumatoid arthritis compared with non-immune-induced arthritis (osteoarthritis) [ ]. Possibly, an enhanced inflammatory-mediated adaptive response increases PTPN2 expression in patients with an autoimmune disease. Consistent with this, we previously observed the activation of PTPN2 expression in β cells by IL-10 and IFN-γ [ ].
We observed that the negative regulation of JAK/STAT signalling by PTPN2 is not β-cell specific. PTPN2 deficiency in pluripotent stem cells and hepatocyte-like cells also exacerbated interferon-induced STAT1 activation, indicating that this anti-inflammatory mechanism is conserved across different undifferentiated and metabolic cells. Similar results were observed in THP-1 monocytes, in which STAT1 phosphorylation was increased after PTPN2 silencing and IFN-γ exposure [ ]. The JAK/STAT signalling impacts several autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis and T1D, which can link the established role of PTPN2 inactivation in the different autoimmune pathologies [ , ].
In diabetes development, β-cell dedifferentiation has been shown to be induced by inflammation [ , ]. The expression of PTPN2 was decreased at the early stages of SC-islet differentiation mediated by the GSK3β kinase inhibitor, CHIR99021, which prevents the phosphorylation/inactivation of β-catenin during definitive endoderm induction. These results indicate that changes in PTPN2 expression can be mediated by the phosphorylation of β-catenin/WNT signalling. WNT signalling is associated with the differentiation of the pancreas [ ] and is reduced during definitive endoderm induction. Additional studies are required to establish whether WNT signalling is involved in PTPN2-reduced expression and β-cell dedifferentiation in T1D.
We observed that PTPN2 and other PTPs were re-expressed in stage 3–4 of SC-islet differentiation, suggesting an adaptive response. However, the expression of PTPN2 in the SC-islets was lower compared with that in the human islets. The SC-islet differentiation protocol bypasses several development pathways resulting in a lower maturation compared to human islets [ ]. We showed that PTPN2-deficient SC-islets exhibited a reduced differentiation efficiency as assessed by the number of late β-like cells compared with controls. Together, our results indicate that PTPN2 contributes to β-cell maturation by buffering cytokine and interferon signalling during differentiation. In the absence of PTPN2, sustained STAT1 activation shifts differentiating cells toward a stress-responsive transcriptional state, which constrains their ability to fully acquire β-cell identity [ , ]. This mechanistic link supports the increase in interferon-responsive genes and reduction in β-cell maturation markers observed in PTPN2−/− SC-islets. Notably, individuals carrying loss-of-function PTPN2 variants develop T1D at an earlier age [ ], supporting the idea that PTPN2 influences β-cell resilience under immune-mediated stress. During differentiation, hedgehog signalling is inhibited to promote the commitment of the pancreatic lineage. Murine β cells with activated hedgehog signalling exhibit reduced expression of β-cell markers [ ]. We found that PTPN2 deficiency increased hedgehog signalling in late β-like cells with a significant reduction in the expression of SLC2A1 (encoding for GLUT1), which could contribute to decreased β-cell functionality. However, the overall decreased differentiation efficiency did not significantly impact the number of insulin-positive SC-islet cells, as previously observed [ , ]. SOX9 functions downstream of both Hedgehog [ ] and Notch signalling [ ], whereas DLK1 is a well-established inhibitor of the Notch pathway [ ]. The interplay between these networks is complex and central to SC-islet differentiation. Given that SOX9 integrates signals from both pathways, the reduction in Notch activity observed in PTPN2-knockout cells likely contributes to the accompanying decrease in SOX9 expression. SOX9 is critical for pancreatic progenitor specification, and its expression must decline as cells transition toward a β-cell identity. However, SOX9 also plays an essential role in mature β-cell function, where its loss impairs insulin secretion and splicing regulation [ ]. Thus, reduced SOX9 expression in PTPN2-deficient SC-islets may contribute to impaired maturation and functionality. Importantly, these transcriptional differences were observed within the same annotated cell types, supporting a cell-intrinsic regulatory effect rather than a change in cluster composition.
SC-islets require maturation in vivo to achieve a glucose-stimulated insulin response to levels comparable to those of primary islets [ ]. A limited number of implanted SC-islets diminished the capacity of PTPN2−/− β-like cells to induce “humanized” glycaemic levels in NOD/SCID mice. In addition, glycaemic control was impaired when PTPN2−/− grafted SC-islets were the only source of insulin production. In line with our results, the deficiency of MANF, an ER stress-inducible protein, reduced β-like cell functionality but did not induce major effects on SC-islet differentiation [ ]. Interestingly, the 48 kDa isoform of PTPN2 is located in the ER and regulates the unfolded protein response in β cells [ ]. It is conceivable that ER-associated proteins have a limited impact on SC-islet differentiation but are relevant for β-like cell functional maturation.
The deletion of TYK2, another T1D candidate gene like PTPN2, diminished SC-islet differentiation efficiency mediated by reduced STAT3 activation and NGN3 expression [ ]. STAT3 is also a PTPN2 target [ ], but PTPN2 deficiency did not change STAT3 or NGN3 expression in SC-islets. These results suggest that independent mechanisms are regulated by TYK2 and PTPN2 in SC-islet differentiation.
The observed normalization of glycaemic control at week 16 represents reflects cell function compensation in PTPN2−/− grafts, but not complete restoration of normal per-cell function. At week 16, the total insulin secretory output is sufficient for both genotypes to achieve human glycaemic levels, effectively masking the underlying functional deficit in PTPN2-deficient SC-islets. Following STZ treatment, when glycemia relies solely on the human graft, mice receiving PTPN2−/− SC-islets display impaired glycaemic control, unmasking the intrinsic defect. Supporting SC-islet dysfunction, PTPN2−/− grafts exhibit reduced insulin staining intensity by immunofluorescence and diminished endocrine/β-cell gene expression, alongside enrichment of interferon signalling and HLA-associated transcripts. The increased HLA expression levels in PTPN2-deficient SC-islets can enhance innate immunogenicity, thereby reducing graft survival in NOD-SCID mice. This result suggests that PTPN2 deficiency cannot only enhance T-cell activation but also the innate response against pathogens related to T1D (i.e. enterovirus) [ ].
A limitation of our study was that we did not assess the role of PTPN2 in SC-islets with different genetic backgrounds. In addition, human-induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSC) generated from somatic cells of patients with T1D and associated SNPs in PTPN2 [ ] will further clarify the signalling affected in response to cytokines. The PTPN2 variants affecting mRNA stability or protein structure have been associated with early-onset T1D [ ]. The use of these hiPSCs will allow us to assess how patient-specific PTPN2 affects SC-islet maturation and cytokine responsiveness.
In our analysis of healthy, sABB, early, and late T1D, we have selected male individuals to exclude sex-related variability. Further studies are required to stratify donors and study the expression and activity of PTPN2 in patient subgroups.

## Conclusions
Our study shows that PTPN2 is a key protein in β-cell function and autoimmune diabetes. Prolonged and/or increased active PTPN2 in β cells can provide beneficial effects under inflammatory stress. Moreover, strategies aiming to maintain or enhance PTPN2 expression in SC-islets can increased inflammatory resistance and restrain residual proliferative capacity, which can be advantageous for encapsulation-devices and transplantation in β-cell replacement therapies in T1D.
