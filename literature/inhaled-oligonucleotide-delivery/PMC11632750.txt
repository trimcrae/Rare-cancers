From the onset of COVID-19 pandemic, significant strides have been made in treatment strategies to manage the viral infection. This includes three small-molecule antivirals (nirmatrelvir – ritonavir, remdesivir and molnupiravir) and 11 monoclonal antibodies that have been launched on the market [ ]. Despite these advancements, the constant emergence of new SARS-CoV-2 variants poses challenges to the efficacy of current treatments. Moreover, recent coronavirus (CoV) outbreaks suggest COVID-19 may not be the last. Future CoV pandemics underscore the need for more potent antivirals, especially fast-acting and mutation-resistant broad-spectrum ones.
The anti-SARS-CoV-2 drugs have been developed thus far to obstruct various stages in the replication cycle, including receptor recognition and virus entry (via spike inhibitors), pp1a/b cleavage (via protease inhibitors), RNA synthesis (via RdRP inhibitors), and virion assembly (via nucleocapsid inhibitors) [ ]. These strategies targeting viral proteins against SARS-CoV-2 have shown success, but the numerous mutations in protein-coding sequences bring challenges, so strategies focusing on conserved viral nucleic acids are drawing researchers’ attention.
The life cycle of SARS-CoV-2 involves key phases centred on protein translation [ , ]. Entering the host cell, it releases genomic RNA (gRNA) and translates it into polyproteins, which are cleaved to form replication-transcription complex (RTC) for genome replication and sub-genomic mRNA (sg-mRNA) transcription, then translated into proteins for new virion assembly [ , ]. Translational inhibition is thus an alternative strategy to combat its infection. SARS-CoV-2 relies on the host cell machinery for its translation. Strategies can be used to block translation initiation and elongation by interfering with host translation machinery or viral mRNA. Viral mRNA-targeted ones are preferred as they can be based on conserved elements and not interfere with the host.
SARS-CoV-2 hijacks the host’s translation machinery upon entering the host cell, which is a crucial early step. The gRNA is elaborately designed to ensure this machinery is solely dedicated to synthesizing viral components. It serves as the template for viral protein production and contains highly conserved RNA elements and untranslated regions (UTRs) that are crucial for regulating translation and viral replication [ ]. These can interact with protein, DNA, or other RNA to modulate their activities, and their functions are determined by their conserved nucleotide sequences and the complex three-dimensional structures they form [ ].
This review aims to discuss the current understanding of the translation mechanism of SARS-CoV-2, highlighting the crucial role of the conserved RNA elements and UTRs in its regulation, and also the strategies for targeting its mRNA to fight protein translation.

## SARS-CoV-2 genome architecture
SARS-CoV-2 possesses a positive single-stranded RNA [ss(+)RNA] genome of 29,903 nucleotides (nt) in length, capped at the 5 end and polyadenylated at the 3 end ( ). The genome is composed of at least 12 open reading frames (ORFs), bordered by a 5 UTR of 265 nt and a 3 UTR of 239 nt [ ]. The first two-thirds of the genome is occupied by two overlapping ORFs, ORF1a and ORF1b. These ORFs encode polyproteins pp1a/b, which are subsequently proteolytically processed into 16 non-structural proteins (NSPs).
 
The remaining third of the genome contains other ORFs that encode for structural and accessory proteins [ ]. Specifically, it includes four structural proteins: the nucleocapsid (N), envelope (E), membrane (M), and spike (S) proteins. Additionally, it encodes at least six accessory proteins (ORF3a, ORF6, ORF7a, ORF7b, ORF8, and ORF10), as per the current annotation (reference genome NC_045512.2).

## SARS-CoV-2 life cycle
The life cycle of SARS-CoV-2 involves multiple key steps: cell entry, genome uncoating, gRNA translation, transcription and replication, structural protein production, and finally, new virion assembling and releasing [ , ] ( ).
 
SARS-CoV-2 attaches to host cell membrane via its S protein interacting with cell-surface receptor angiotensin converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) [ , ]. It infiltrates the cell through two primary mechanisms [ , ]. One involves proteases activation for the fusion between the viral and host cell membranes, a process known as cell surface entry. Cellular proteases like transmembrane serine protease 2 (TMPRSS2) or furin cleave the S protein to separate receptor binding domain (RBD) and S2, activating S2’s membrane-fusion capacity and starting the membrane-fusion process [ , , ]. The other is referred to as endosomal entry. It involves virion endocytosis. Within the endosome, the protease cathepsin L (CatL) triggers the fusion of the viral and endosomal membranes [ , , ].
Following the membrane fusion, the viral RNA genome is liberated into the cytosol, where it undergoes uncoating to enable translation, replication and transcription. The host translation machinery, including ribosomes and translation factors, are recruited. The ORF1a/b on the gRNA serves as an mRNA template for the synthesis of pp1a and pp1ab. Proteases in NSP3 and NSP5 cleave pp1a and pp1ab into a total of 16 NSPs, with pp1a generating NSP1–11 and pp1ab producing NSP1–10 and NSP12–16 [ , ]. The swift release of NSP1 instigates the cessation of host mRNA translation for viral protein synthesis [ , ]. Concurrently, other NSPs may assemble into RTCs to facilitate viral RNA synthesis [ ]. This takes place within double-membrane vesicles (DMVs), which are rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER)-derived membranous replication organelles induced by the virus [ , ].
The membrane-bound RTCs catalyse the synthesis of new gRNAs and the series of sg-mRNAs encoding the structural and accessory proteins of the virus. The structural proteins, namely S, E, and M, are inserted into endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and follow a secretory pathway into ER-Golgi intermediate compartment (ERGIC) [ , , ]. The newly synthesized gRNAs can be translated to produce additional NSPs, act as a template for further RNA synthesis, or be packaged into new virions. The assembly of new virions begins with the encapsulation of gRNAs with N proteins, forming encapsulated genomes that bud into the ERGIC by interacting with the membrane-inserted structural proteins. This interaction results in the acquisition of a lipid bilayer with S, M, and E proteins.
Once assembled, the mature virions are transported to the plasma membrane via smooth-walled vesicles. These vesicles merge with the plasma membrane at the cell surface, leading to the release of the virions through exocytosis [ , , ].

## SARS-CoV-2 RNA synthesis
The RTC of SARS-CoV-2 synthesizes both sg-mRNAs and new gRNA. This process involves the production of negative-sense full-length and a corresponding set of sg-mRNA intermediates. These intermediates subsequently serve as the templates for the production of positive-sense gRNA and sg-mRNAs [ , , ].
The synthesis of negative-stranded RNA is a process of discontinuous transcription controlled by transcription-regulating sequences (TRSs). These sequences include the TRS-leader (TRS-L) at the 3 end of the leader sequence and the TRS-body (TRS-B) preceding each viral gene in the body [ , , ]. When the TRS-B copy hybridizes with the TRS-L during the synthesis of negative-stranded RNA, discontinuous transcription takes place. More specifically, in the RTC, the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp), that is, NSP12, initiates the synthesis of the negative strand by moving along and copying the genome from the 3 end. Upon encountering a TRS-B, the RdRp either continues elongation or switches the template to TRS-L. This switch in polymerase template leads to discontinuous transcription, resulting in the fusion of the leader-body. Positive-stranded mRNAs are then transcribed from these fused negative-stranded intermediates. Consequently, all SARS‐CoV‐2 mRNAs, including sg-mRNAs and gRNA, share a common leader sequence, followed by a variable RNA length to form 5 UTRs. ORF-7a and ORF-8 have the shortest 5 UTRs with only the leader sequence. ORF-S, ORF-3a, ORF-E, and ORF-N have 1–8 nt more. ORF-M, ORF-6, and ORF-7b have longer 5 UTRs of 119 nt, 230 nt, and 151 nt. The gRNA 5 UTR is the longest at 265 nt ( ).

## SARS-CoV-2 protein translation
Once in the cytoplasm, SARS-CoV-2 immediately translates the ORF1a/b to generate pp1a and pp1ab, which are proteolyzed into early NSPs [ , , ]. The host 80S ribosome assembles at the translation initiation site (TIS) and then moves along to translate the genome from the 5 end, producing pp1a until it reaches the ORF1a termination codon. For ORF1b translation, a −1 programmed ribosomal frameshift (PRF) is required just before the ORF1a termination codon [ , , ], enabling read-through to form a pp1a/b fusion via a programmed −1 frameshift stimulation element (PFSE) [ ]. The ribosome stalls and retreats by one nucleotide when crossing the PFSE.
In the late phase of infection process, structural and accessory proteins are translated from the set of sg-mRNAs for virion assembly.
To ensure the exclusive dedication of the translation machinery to the synthesis of viral components, the host cellular translation is selectively deactivated shortly after viral intrusion. SARS-CoV-2 employs a multifaceted strategy, mediated by various NSPs including NSP1, NSP7, and NSP8, to seize control of the translation machinery and hinder host protein synthesis [ ].
Among the early proteins expressed post-infection, NSP1, the most N-terminal product of ORF1a, is a crucial virulence factor in coronaviruses [ , ]. It plays a pivotal role in suppressing host gene expression through a unique, dual-pronged strategy. NSP1 has three domains: N-terminal, central linker, and C-terminal. The C-terminal of NSP1 binds to and blocks the mRNA entry channel of the 40S ribosomal subunit, disabling its translation [ , , ]. When bound to the ribosome, NSP1 further induces RNA modification of a capped mRNA, resulting in accelerated cellular mRNA decay in SARS-CoV-2 infected cells [ ]. This shutoff mechanism is specific for host mRNA to favour viral mRNAs [ , , , ]. Viral transcripts resist both NSP1 translation inhibition and NSP1-guided RNA degradation [ , ]. This resistance, or evasion, to NSP1 inhibition is facilitated by the stem-loop1 (SL1), which is situated near the 5 extremity within the leader sequence of both gRNA and sg-mRNAs [ , , ] ( ). The interaction between the N-terminal domain of NSP1 and SL1 liberates the mRNA accommodation channel [ ].

## Translation initiation
Translation is a meticulously controlled process of mRNA decoding, which can be segmented into four stages: initiation, elongation, termination, and ribosome recycling. The initiation phase is the rate-determining step that facilitates the formation of functional ribosomal complexes at the initiation codon [ ].
Most capped and polyadenylated cellular and viral mRNAs employ the conventional cap-dependent scanning model to kickstart translation [ ]. The cap interacts with the eukaryotic initiation factor (eIF) 4F complex consisting of eIF4E, eIF4G, and eIF4A. eIF4G interacts with eIF3 to attract 43S complex which contains 40S subunit, eIFs and the ternary complex (the association of eIF2 with GTP and Met-tRNAiMet). The 43S complex scans the 5 UTR to the cognate AUG start codon. This process is catalysed by the RNA and ATP-dependent helicase activity of eIF4A, which unfolds the RNA structures found within the 5 UTR. On recognizing the TIS and releasing eIFs, the 60S subunit is recruited to form the 80S ribosome for elongation.
SARS-CoV-2, like other coronaviruses, is thought to have a 5 -capped and 3 -polyadenylated ss(+) RNA genome serving as an mRNA template for translation [ ]. Its sg-mRNAs, synthesized through discontinuous nested transcription, also carry 5 cap and 3 poly(A) tail, akin to the gRNA. However, despite the capping of its mRNA, translation initiation is not solely or not at all facilitated by the canonical mechanism [ ].
In fact, during the initial phase following viral intrusion, the swiftly produced viral NSP1 protein inhibits the canonical cap-dependent translation of cellular mRNAs [ , , ]. Additionally, infected cells actively reduce cap-dependent translation to thwart the translation of viral proteins, in response to the dual challenge of NSP1 translation inhibition and NSP1-guided RNA degradation [ ]. Beyond these points, there are significant observations suggesting that the translation mechanism also plays a crucial role. These findings provide further insight into the complex interplays between the virus and the host cellular machinery.

## Initiating translation: the role of 5 UTR

## 5 UTR and ribosome scanning
5 UTR is a crucial nucleotide stretch that ribosome scans in search of the starting codon. Its inherent properties, such as length, RNA folding, and upstream AUG (uAUG) codon, can influence the initiation efficiency [ ]. Typically, the length of the 5 UTR is a critical factor, with the optimal length not exceeding 200 nt, and perhaps not exceeding 70 nt being the most ideal [ ]. An unstructured 5 UTR is advantageous for scanning, thereby boosting initiation efficiency [ ]. The existence of uAUG and short upstream ORF (uORF) within the 5 UTR can affect the translational efficiency of the primary gene because some ribosomes may alternatively translate the uORF before reaching the main reading frame [ ].
In the context of SARS-CoV-2, it has been observed that the 5 UTR, particularly in the gRNA, does not lend itself well to traditional scanning methods ( ). The gRNA 5 UTR is the longest at 265 nt, highly structured with five stem-loops (SLs), SL1 to SL5 and potential SL4.5 between SL4 and SL5 [ ]. SL5, where ORF1a start codon is embedded, forms a complex structure with a stable four-way junction ( ). All sg-mRNA 5 UTRs share SL1, SL2, and SL3 of the leader sequence [ , ]. These SLs may complicate translation, especially ribosome recruitment and scanning ( ). Also, the gRNA 5 UTR has a ten-codon conserved uORF starting at uAUG107. Moderate reduction in SARS-CoV-2 translation due to the uORF was confirmed in rabbit reticulocytes lysate (RRL) [ ]. Other uORFs initiating at near-cognate initiation codons were also found [ , ]. One is at CUG59, 10 nt upstream of the TRS leader, on all the sg-mRNAs.
 
RNA positive-stranded viruses commonly have stable structures that block ribosome scanning translation, so viruses evolve alternative strategies, giving viral mRNA a competitive edge. Condé L’s research shows that SARS-CoV-2 gRNA translation is efficient and competitive despite secondary structures and uORFs [ ], suggesting additional translation-initiation features.
Two studies have conflicting results on SARS-CoV-2 translation initiation [ , ]. Slobodin B posits that the gRNA 5 UTR drives cap-independent translation via internal ribosome entry site (IRES), dependent on eIF4A and eIF4G, and NSP1 enhances it, but the sg-mRNA 5 UTRs are cap-dependent [ ]. Condé L argues for cap-dependent translation initiation, independent on the poly(A) tail [ ]. Reason for the divergence is still unclear. Both used Renilla luciferase as the reporter gene, its AUG context and UTR structures, including 5 UTR and 3 UTR, differ from those of SARS-CoV-2 mRNA. The AUG of SARS-CoV-2 is in the remote stem of SL5, downstream of the four-way junction structure [ ]. Its AUG context, including its downstream nucleotide stretch, may contribute to the formation of the SL5 structure and potentially influence translation initiation. The 3 UTR and its interaction with 5 UTR can also regulate or enhance translation [ , ]

## 5 UTR and noncanonical translation initiation

## Leaky scanning
In the canonical scanning model, ribosomes scan mRNA from 5 cap for initiation codon. If the first AUG context is suboptimal, ribosomes may scan further downstream for an alternate, called leaky scanning [ ]. In mammals, optimal initiation codon context has G at + 4 and purine at −3 (with the initiation codon corresponding to nt + 1 to + 3) [ ]. For SARS-CoV-2, the gRNA 5 UTR has uAUG at 107 with weak context (ugcAUGc) ( ). This implies potential for efficient leaky scanning, but start codon selection can be swayed by mRNA’s secondary structure, like downstream (14 nt or slightly further) loop near initiation codon enhancing recognition [ ]. The conserved uORF starting at uAUG107 in the SARS-CoV-2 gRNA 5 UTR overlaps with SL4 and SL4.5. It’s unclear if SL5 promotes uAUG107 recognition, but ribosomal profiling shows the ribosome recognizes and pauses at it, indicating it can be translated by the host ribosome [ , ].
According to Condé L and Slobodin B, SARS-CoV-2 mRNA translation initiation depends on eIF4A [ , ]. Its helicase activity unfolds the 5 UTR RNA structures, enabling leaky scanning through the uAUG107 for continued scanning and reinitiation at ORF1a/b. Ribosomes translating the short uORF can resume scanning and reinitiate on ORF1a/b but need time to regain eIFs. The RNA structures and short uORFs in the 5 UTR influence the proportion reaching ORF1a/b, thereby regulating translation initiation efficiency [ ].

## 5 UTR and noncanonical translation initiation

## Internal ribosome entry
When cap-dependent translation is suppressed, translation not relying on the 5 cap structure, like through IRES, becomes a major way to support the robust expression of viral mRNA [ ]. IRESs are highly organized and complex RNA structures that can directly interact with the 43S complex to recruit the ribosome near the start codon in a cap- and eIF4E-independent manner [ ]. This makes IRES-containing mRNAs have lower requirements for the set of eIFs. Based on this property, viruses can establish their translational dominance strategies by inactivating those factors implicated in cellular translation.
The AUG start codon of SARS-CoV-2 gRNA is embedded in SL5, a four-way junction often seen in some viral IRESes [ ], with conserved IRES motifs like U-turns [ , ]. SL5 has two (Y)UNR-type and one GNRA-type U-turns (N is any nucleotide, R is purine, and Y is pyrimidine) ( ) [ , ]. The U-turn is for RNA-RNA recognition [ ]. For SARS-CoV-2 gRNA, the U-turns in SL5 May promote interactions with the host ribosome through U-turns in rRNAs [ ], aiding the gRNA to directly recruit the ribosome.
For capped SARS-CoV-2 mRNA to have IRES, its cap-independent translation initiation is important [ ]. Cap-dependent and independent translation initiation of viruses can cooperate ( ) [ , ]. When cap-dependent is blocked under stress, IRES-dependent becomes dominant [ ], helping the mRNA resist translation attenuation, especially at the initial stage of infection, when the interferon response is caused by the virus intrusion [ ]. The IRES in the 5 UTR of SARS-CoV-2 gRNA allows the virus to overcome the response when cells downregulate translation to limit infection, promoting ORF1a/b translation and generating early NSPs to counter the host defence.

## Initiating translation: the role of 3 UTR
Three structures in the SARS-CoV-2 3 UTR are identified [ , ], namely the bulged stem-loop (BSL), the stem-loop 1 (sl1, which is in lowercase here to distinguish it from the SL1 in the 5 UTR), and hypervariable region (HVR) with a stem – loop II-like motif (s2m) and an octa-nucleotide motif (ONM) of 5 -GGAAGAGC-3 ( ). Their conformations are thought to be complex and dynamic [ ]. BSL can be extended or pseudo-knotted with the apical loop of sl1. Huston et al.’s in-vivo probing data support the extended conformation as likely dominant [ ]. The pseudoknot conformation is confirmed by NMR spectroscopy [ ] and in-vivo SPLASH assay [ ], and considered non-dominant. The two conformations are mutually exclusive and are proposed to function as switches. The HVR is not well-conserved overall, but its s2m and ONM are [ ]. The s2m of SARS-CoV-2 is a 41-nt (29728–29768) highly conserved motif in the terminal of HVR, with two base substitutions (U5C and G31U) from SARS-CoV ( ) [ ]. It forms alternative secondary structures in different studies and has a GUAC palindrome-containing terminal loop of varying sizes ( ) [ , , , , , ]. The GUAC palindrome in the terminal loop mediates the formation of a homo-dimeric RNA-RNA kissing complex, which can convert to a stable duplex with the help of the N protein ( ) [ ].
 
 
The 3 UTR of mRNA is crucial for post-transcriptional regulation, affecting stability and translation [ , , ]. Its interactions with microRNA (miRNA) and RNA binding proteins (RBPs) are critical determinants [ , ]. In addition to those interactions, the proposed functions of SARS-CoV-2 3 UTR in translation involve viral mRNA circularization.

## miRNA binding
Studies found host miRNA binding sites in the 3 UTR of SARS-CoV-2, like s2m for hsa-miR-1307-3p, downstream for miR-34a-5p and miR-34b-5p, and terminus for miR-760-3p [ , ]. These miRNAs control the production of various interleukins and interleukin receptors [ , ]. Hsa-miR-1307-3p binding is affected by s2m dimerization. The s2m element serves as a molecular switch, changing from dimerization to binding with hsa-miR-1307-3p [ ]. TargetScan shows that in addition to interleukins, many other host mRNAs like TMPRSS2 and ribosomal proteins (RPs) also have hsa-miR-1307-3p binding sites [ ]. The sequestration of hsa-miR-1307-3p by s2m may boost the production of these target proteins, inducing overactive immune response, helping viral entry and translation.
Also, increasing evidence shows that host miRNA can affect the multiplication of RNA virus by binding to its genome [ ]. The impact of miRNA depends on the location of the binding site and structural changes. If the binding site is in the secondary structure of RNA or the binding site of RBP, the corresponding miRNA may have a great influence on the expression or function of this genome [ , ]. Among them, some RNA viruses have an uncanonical miRNA-viral RNA relationship that positively regulates replication by increasing the stability of the viral RNA or its secondary structure [ ]. The effects and mechanisms of host miRNA on the translation of SARS-CoV-2 need further studies.

## Ribosome recruitment
Research by Goebel et al. shows that while HVR and ONM aren’t crucial for mouse hepatitis virus (MHV) RNA synthesis, the HVR impacts viral pathogenesis [ ], suggesting it may play a role in other crucial processes like protein translation. SARS-CoV s2m forms a unique tertiary structure (PDB:1XJR) including two perpendicular RNA helix axes, creating a negatively charged tunnel binding two magnesium ions [ ]. It is similar to an rRNA 530 SL that attaches to the RNA oligomer binding folds of RPs and eIF1A, proposed to play a role in recruiting host translational machinery [ ]. In SARS-CoV-2, s2m has two base substitutions altering RNA secondary structure [ ], but the GNRA-like motif (GAGUA) remains in the apical loop of one secondary structure alternative ( ), a structural element involved in organizing rRNA structure for ribosome recruitment [ ].

## mRNA circularization
Genome cyclization is crucial in RNA virus replication [ , ], ensuring that only the genome carrying the complete UTRs interacts with the replication machinery [ ]. Ziv et al [ ] and Li et al [ ] found a long-range interaction for SARS-CoV-2 genome circularization between its 5 and 3 UTRs. This cyclization opens 5 SL3 with TRS-L and disrupts the 3 UTR triple helix junction [ ], hinting at regulating discontinuous transcription like in MHV [ ].
mRNA 5 -3 cross-talk is also thought to enhance translation initiation by promoting terminating ribosome shunting and/or stimulating eIF activity [ , , ]. In cap-dependent translation, the interaction of eIF4E, 4 G, and poly(A)-binding protein (PABP) causes mRNAs to pseudo-circularize. In IRES-mediated translation, similar long-range interactions are seen between the 3 UTR and IRES [ ].
Generally, the formation of RNA 5 –3 cross-talk complexes involves a series of RNA-RNA, RNA-protein, and protein-protein interactions [ , , ]. RBPs often bind to both ends to stimulate translation initiation. For SARS-CoV-2, the circularization of gRNA through end-to-end base pairing between 5 SL3 and the 3 UTR triple helix junction is determined [ , ], but this pairing in sg-mRNA is still waiting to be investigated. Besides end-to-end base-pairing, the insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 1 (IGF2BP1) has been identified as a factor that interacts with both the 5 UTR and 3 UTR [ , , ], potentially ensuring mRNA circularization and stimulating translation, similar to how IGF2BP1, by binding to the HCV 5 UTR and/or HCV 3 UTR, recruits eIF3 and enhances HCV IRES-mediated translation [ ].
It’s also noted that a novel Gamma-interferon-Activated Inhibitor of Translation (GAIT)-like regulon in the 3’UTR was reported as the Sarbecoviral Pan-End Activating RNA (SPEAR) element [ ]. It is a 39-nt cis-element (29811–29849, NC_045512.2) in the HVR region ( ). ORF10 internal initiation is critical for SPEAR-mediated induction, disrupting the HVR secondary structure to facilitate SPEAR formation. The SPEAR element and tRNA synthetase Sarbecoviral RNA-Interacting (TASRI) complex are hijacked for global viral translation enhancement.
To sum up, mechanisms underlying the SARS-CoV-2 translation initiation await further studies. Cap-dependent scanning and cap-independent internal ribosome entry may cooperate against host defence ( ). In the early stage of infection, when cap-dependent translation is blocked, IRES-dependent translation is needed for NSP production to counter the host defence. In the later stage, cap-dependent translation may be required for structural protein production. In both translation models, both UTRs may be key for gRNA and sg-mRNAs to hijack the host ribosome and start translation. In cap-dependent scanning, the eIF4F complex bound to the 5’ cap recruits the 40S ribosome and mediates mRNA circularization through the interaction of eIF4E, 4 G and PABP. 5’UTR SLs block ribosome scanning and need to be unfolded by eIF4A ( ). In cap-independent internal ribosome entry, the 5’UTR may take advantage of its secondary structures to recruit translation components [ ]. The 5 or 3 crosstalk via long-range base pairing or potential interaction of macromolecules such as IGF2BP1 can promote mRNA circularization and translation ( ). The highly conserved 5’UTR SL5 and 3’UTR s2m both have structures that can interact with RP, rRNA and eIFs. The 3 UTR recruits translation components, a likely mechanism in the 5 or 3 communication during SARS-CoV-2 translation, as in other ss(+) RNA viruses like West Nile virus (WNV) [ , ]. Relying on the secondary structures of both UTRs and end-to-end crosstalk, gRNA can start translation effectively through cap-independent internal ribosome entry [ ]. For sg-mRNA, without SL4 and SL5, its ability to recruit ribosomes internally may be weakened. Whether the 5’UTR of sg-mRNA, that is, SL1–3, can directly recruit ribosomes and mediate sg-mRNA cyclization needs to be explored, and the role of IGF2BP1 in mediating SARS-CoV-2 mRNA cyclization and its effect on the two translation mechanisms also need to be finally determined.

## PRF
After gRNA translation starts, the host ribosome elongates pp1a along ORF1a to its stop codon. PFSE between ORF1a and ORF1b makes the ribosome retreat one nucleotide for readthrough to ORF1b. The PFSE of SARS-CoV-2 includes an attenuator hairpin (AH), a slippery sequence (SS, U_UUA_AAC), and a downstream stimulatory structure ( ) [ , , ]. ORF1a has a stop codon there. When recognized, the ribosome shifts to −1 frame at SS (UUU_AAA_C) and starts ORF1b translation. AH is a negative regulator of PRF [ , , ], helping maintain proper frameshift efficiency for the balanced expression of ORF1a and ORF1ab, which is key to viral infectivity.
 
PFSE is found in CoVs as a three-stemmed pseudoknot. In SARS-CoV-2, Bhatt and other researchers found the PFSE priming for frameshifting is a similar pseudoknot, forming an H-type with Stems 1 and 2 coaxially stacked and Stem 3 perpendicular ( ) [ , , ]. Studies also revealed its conformational variability depending on sequence context ( ) [ , , , ]. Huston et al. suggested a dominant in vivo structure with a variable putative stem 3 region forming various long-range interactions outside the PFSE ( ) [ ]. The canonical three-stemmed pseudoknot conformation is a minority. Schlick et al. and Dey et al. stated that the PFSE can be an H-type or HL-type pseudoknot or a three-way junction, sharing Stems 1 and 3, with flexible Stem 2 involved in conformation switches ( ) [ , ], and the mutants suppressing switches stop frameshifting. In Lan et al.’s in-cell models, the PFSE folds into at least two alternative conformations (clusters 1 and 2) [ ]. In both, the sequence downstream of SS that is supposed to form canonical Stem 1 pairs with a 10-nt sequence upstream of AH to form an Alternative Stem 1 (AS1) ( ). These studies show the dynamics and plasticity of the SARS-CoV-2 PFSE structure, indicating the importance of conformational transitions in frameshifting and the potential of new therapeutic strategies based on certain RNA fold distributions.

## SARS-CoV-2 translation inhibitors
Strategies to inhibit viral translation may involve obstructing the initiation and elongation processes via hurdling the function of the implicated ribosome, mRNA, and/or translation factors. SARS-CoV-2, as an obligate parasite, utilizes the host cellular ribosome and translation factors. Antivirals that target the host translation machinery often induce interference in the overall cellular translation. Therefore, strategies targeting viral mRNA are typically favoured. The SARS-CoV-2 gRNA, which is engineered to take over the cellular translation machinery, contains multiple conserved RNA elements that control translation. These elements’ function is dictated by the conserved nucleotide sequences and their high-order structures [ ]. Their high conservation reduces the potential for drug resistance, thus increasing the potential effectiveness of antiviral drugs. Therefore, our focus is on the RNA-based strategies predicated on translation inhibition.

## Targeting viral RNA cleavage
Degrading viral RNA to eliminate the template for SARS-CoV-2 translation is viewed as a potential strategy for the development of antiviral drugs. Numerous RNA-degrading strategies exist, including antisense oligonucleotide (ASO), small interfering RNA (siRNA), CRISPR/Cas system, and catalytic nucleic acid [ ] ( ). Potential strategies targeting the shared 5 leader sequence can induce targeted cleavage of both sgRNA and sg-mRNAs, reducing overall viral protein translation ( ).

## ASO
ASOs are small synthetic single-stranded oligonucleotides that can target specific RNA sequences for gene silencing. They can be designed as gapmers for RNase H-dependent cleavage, or as a steric block to modulate RNA splicing and suppress translation [ ]. Gapmers with RNase H-competence usually have a central DNA ‘gap’ flanked by modified nucleotides like locked nucleic acid (LNA) [ ], whose ribose ring is locked by connecting the C(2 )- and C(4 )-atoms. DNA hybridizing with RNA activates RNase H-dependent RNA degradation. LNA promotes nuclease resistance and target binding.
Several studies have shown that ASOs can be potential therapeutics against SARS-CoV-2. Baldassarre et al. proposed that LNA gapmers targeting the 5 UTR can activate RNase H-mediated cleavage of SARS-CoV-2 RNA [ ]. Dhorne-Pollet et al. found that LNA gapmers targeting ORF1b, 5 UTR and N can significantly reduce the viral genome copies in cell infection model [ ]. Qiao et al. provided evidence that 2´constrained ethyl (cEt) gapmers for cleavage of gRNA serve as potent inhibitors of cellular infection by multiple SARS-CoV-2 variants [ ]. Su X et al. engineered a chimeric ASO, comprising a 2 -O-methoxyethyl (2 MOE)-modified ASO and a 5 ‐phosphorylated 2 ‐5 ‐poly(A)4, to degrade SARS-CoV-2 RNAs through RNase L, inhibiting the virus and its mutants [ ].

## siRNA
siRNA mediates post-transcriptional gene silencing (PTGS) by degrading specific mRNA [ ] and is becoming a therapeutic approach [ , ]. Many attempts, as already reviewed by Zhang Y et al. [ ], have been made to apply siRNAs against SARS-CoV-2 [ ], mainly targeting S and RdRP. The 5 UTR is also a potential target [ , , ]. For example, Idris et al. found siRNAs targeting 5 UTR SL1, RdRp, and Hel genes had strong repression in Vero E6 cell and mouse model [ ]. Saify Nabiabad et al. made Apt-LNPs-siRNA with RBD aptamer-modified lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) and siRNA targeting N [ ]. This aptamer-siRNA chimera has dual functions of inhibition and neutralization, showing anti-SARS-CoV-2 activity in human lung cell line. In a clinical case, inhaling 10 mg Apt-LNPs-siRNA nebulized daily for 6 days improved the lung condition of a 36-year-old man with severe SARS-CoV-2.

## CRISPR/Cas13
CRISPR/Cas system is a nucleotide-specific RNA-guided genome editing tool and has been used to cleave viral genomes. Among diverse CRISPR/Cas systems, CRISPR/Cas13, a Class II type VI system targeting ssRNA cleavage, has been highlighted as a potential therapeutic approach for SARS-CoV-2 infection [ ]. Abbott et al. developed a CRISPR/Cas13-based strategy termed PAC-MAN to target multiple CoV species simultaneously. PAC-MAN effectively degraded SARS-CoV-2 RNA in a human lung epithelial cell model [ ]. Wang et al. used bioinformatics to identify conserved S sequence for CRISPR/Cas13a targeting [ ]. Fareh et al. engineered a CRISPR/Cas13b-based system to target S and N transcripts to suppress SARS-CoV-2 variants [ ]. Zeng et al. stated that Cas13d delivered via LNPs can reduce CoV infections in primary human airway epithelium air-liquid interface cultures [ ].

## Other RNA-cleavage strategies
Nucleic acid therapeutic technologies such as siRNA, ASO, and CRISPR/Cas are crucial for responding rapidly to viral threats. Targeting conserved regions can reduce viral mutational escape, but there are still challenges like stability, immunogenicity, and delivery. Modular nucleic acid catalysts like ribozymes and DNAzymes provide an alternative. Artificial catalysts made of single-stranded synthetic xeno-nucleic acid oligonucleotides (XNA) expand the catalyst’s capabilities and can cleave long structured RNA under physiological conditions. Site-specific RNA endonuclease XNAzymes can be rapidly developed to target SARS-CoV-2 RNA cleavage and inhibit viral infection [ ].
Besides, the monoclonal antibody 3D8 single-chain variable fragment (3D8 scFv), an antiDNA antibody with unspecific nucleic acid-hydrolysing activity, has been found to target SARS-CoV-2 and other coronaviruses in vitro, demonstrating broad-spectrum antiviral activity [ ].

## Targeting viral RNA element function
The RNA genome of SARS-CoV-2, though it mutates frequently, harbours conserved sequences and structures that are crucial for protein translation, in both coding and noncoding regions. These offer unique interaction sites for cellular functional molecules and are potential targets for selective ligands [ ]. Approaches like ASOs and RNA-binding small molecule (SM) inhibitors have been utilized ( and ) [ ]. Structure-recognizing SMs that modify the RNA conformational structure are a beneficial adjunct to sequence-recognizing ASOs. 

## 5 UTR targeting
The 5 UTR of SARS-CoV-2 gRNA is highly structured and has at least five SLs (SL1–5), which promotes ORF1a/b translation and NSP synthesis [ ]. The common 5 leader sequence including SL1–3 in the nested set of SARS-CoV-2 mRNA may enhance the antiviral efficacy of inhibitors, because a single inhibitor targeting this region can potentially affect all viral mRNAs ( ). Except for SL4.5, these features are also in the 5 UTR of other CoVs [ ] and can be utilized as vulnerabilities for broad-spectrum therapeutics.

## Targeting SL1
SARS-CoV-2 strategically utilizes NSP1 to block host protein synthesis and exploits SL1 in the 5 UTR to bypass NSP1 suppression for viral translation [ , , ]. This dependence on SL1 exposes a vulnerability in SARS-CoV-2, indicating a potential therapeutic strategy [ ]. ASOs are among the well-studied ones. These ASOs form stable hybrids with SL1 for steric blocking. Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are also engineered as antisense RNAs [ ].
A variety of chemically modified ASOs targeting SL1 have been investigated. LNA ASOs can inhibit SARS-CoV-2 translation and replication in vitro and protect transgenic mice from SARS-CoV-2-caused death [ ]. Uniform 2 MOE ASOs targeting the 5 UTR are inhibitors of multiple variants’ infection, and the two most active target SL1 [ ]. A LNA mixmer, which is an ASO with a combination of LNA and DNA nucleotides, shows nanomolar effectiveness in inhibiting SARS-CoV-2 variant replication in human cells and mice [ ]. Peptide-conjugated phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomers (PPMOs) and cell-penetrating peptide -conjugated antisense peptide nucleic acids (PPNAs) were both found to reduce SARS-CoV-2 titres in cell cultures [ , ].
Besides ASOs, SL1-targeted SM has also been studied. Zafferani et al. found that three dimethylamiloride (DMA) analogs (DMA-132, DMA-135, and DMA-155) reduced the SARS-CoV-2 titre in Vero E6 cells by targeting conserved structured elements at the viral 5 -end, with SL1 as the preferred target for DMA-135 [ ].

## Targeting other 5 SLs
The initiation efficiency of translation is fundamentally reliant on the structures at the 5 ends of the mRNA to recruit translation machinery, applicable to both cap-dependent and cap-independent mechanisms [ , , ]. The cap-dependent scanning mechanism necessitates RNA helicases, such as eIF4A, to unravel RNA structures within the 5 UTR [ ]. The stability and accessibility of these RNA folds to eIF4A can thus impact initiation efficiency. Conversely, the cap-independent mechanism demands specific RNA topologies provided by the 5 ends of the mRNA template, serving specific RNA motifs, especially single-stranded structural ‘modules’. These modules and their corresponding nucleotide sequences act as recognition sites for translation machinery, including the ribosome and eIFs [ ]. These distinct high-order structures offer opportunities for specific recognition by artificial ligands, facilitating potential therapeutic applications. In the context of SARS-CoV-2, despite inconsistent experimental findings concerning the translation initiation mechanism, a reliance on eIFs such as eIF4A and eIF4G has been confirmed [ , ]. Theoretically, the recognition sites of structured 5 UTR, like U turns in SL5, for these eIFs can be potential targets for antiviral therapeutics ( ). Zafferani et al uncovered that SL4, SL5a, and SL6 (downstream of SL5 in extended 5 -end) can all bind to the DMA analogs, which helps inhibit SARS-CoV-2 proliferation [ ].
IGF2BP1 binds SARS-CoV-2 RNA ends and promotes translation ( ) [ , , ]. It interacts with ‘CACA’ motif in SL2–3, SL4 and 3 UTR triple helix junction [ , ]. ASOs for SL2–3 and SL4 were used to disrupt interaction with IGF2BP1, reducing SARS-CoV-2 RNA yield in huh7.5.1 cells by 50% [ ].
Besides, Yao et al. demonstrated that the host cellular RNA-binding protein 24 (RBM24) specifically binds to the SL4 GUGUG element in the 5 UTR of SARS-CoV-2, thereby inhibiting 80S ribosome assembly and subsequently suppressing polyprotein translation and SARS-CoV-2 replication [ ]. The anti-translation effect of RBM24 may result from its obstruction of the 40S ribosomal subunit scanning for the AUG codon or its direct interference with the access and assembly of ribosomal subunits. This study also suggests the SL4 GUGUG element’s potential significance in 80S ribosome assembly and as a possible target for SARS-CoV-2 inhibition. Future antiviral treatments could potentially involve the induction of RBM24 overexpression in vivo or the delivery of RBM24 or other ligands to the SL4 GUGUG element.

## Frameshifting targeting
PRF is an evolutionary mechanism that optimizes genome coding potential by facilitating translation of overlapping reading frames [ ]. CoVs use it to balance ORF1a and ORF1b expression [ , , ], while human cells rarely use it, making it a potential target for antiviral drug development.
PRF is contingent upon the flexibility of PFSE and its capacity to engage with the ribosome. Modifying the PFSE structure can inhibit frameshifting and viral replication. The primary focus of the discovery of RNA-targeting SMs has been on the PFSE. Some compounds like 1,4-diazepane derivative MTDB [ ], merafloxacin [ , ], geneticin [ ], and aminoquinazoline derivatives [ ] have been recognized as potential inhibitors, which have been extensively reviewed by other authors [ , , , ]. The ASO strategy is also used, like Zhang et al.’s LNA ASOs (mixmers) that can inhibit the function of PFSE and reduce the replication of SARS-CoV-2 in A549-ACE2 cells at a concentration of 100 nM [ ]. Using 3D antisense modelling, PRF3p, a phosphorodiamidate morpholine oligonucleotide (PMO), was optimized to target Stem 3 in the PFSE, breaking the pseudoknot and stopping the frameshift, so as to stop the virus from replicating in cells [ ].
Frameshifting in SARS-CoV-2 also involves interaction with the AH sub-region [ , ]. Haniff et al. found a drug-like SM that binds and stabilizes the AH structure, reducing frameshifting efficiency, and it was further modified into a ribonuclease targeting chimera (RIBOTAC) molecule via linked with a RNase L-recruiting module [ ].

## 3 UTR targeting
The regulatory role of SARS-CoV-2 3 UTR led to the exploration of its druggability, with the s2m element getting more attention. Proposed functions for s2m element include hijacking of host protein synthesis [ ], regulation via host miRNA [ ] and RNA recombination events via genomic dimerization [ ]. Jiang et al. found no change in viral infection and fitness in hamster models upon s2m deletion [ ]. But they acknowledged the study’s limitations and didn’t rule out the possibility that s2m could still be important depending on the cell line and the host.
According to Imperatore et al., SARS-CoV-2 s2m can have three conformations because of dimerization through the GUAC palindrome in the terminal loop: monomeric, homo-dimeric kissing and duplex [ ]. The monomer binds host miRNA, but the dimer doesn’t [ ]. Also, there is a GAGUA sequence overlapping the GUAC palindrome, forming a GNRA-like terminal loop in one reported secondary structure of the s2m ( ), related to the recruitment of the 43S complex via interaction with RP, rRNA or eIFs [ ]. The dimerization turns this loop into a paired stem, thus hindering the recruitment. So, s2m may act as a molecular switch, changing from dimerization to binding miRNA and the translational machinery ( ).
Due to its high conservation and proposed roles in viral translation and replication [ , , ], s2m is deemed as a promising target for potential anti-CoV drugs by some researchers [ ]. A s2m-targeting agent can target all the SARS-CoV-2 mRNAs to enhance antiviral effects ( ).
Aldhumani et al. investigated s2mG35U as a potential drug target by docking a library of FDA-approved drugs [ ]. Aminoglycosides and polymyxins were found to have extensive contacts with s2mG35U at the same position where magnesium ions are bound in the crystal structure [ ]. Enzymatic probing of the docking ligands showed that polymyxin binding changes the conformational profile of the RNA.
Lulla et al designed several LNA ASOs that can successfully remodel the s2m structure despite its high structural compactness [ ]. The particular ASO designs described by the authors may offer suitable lead compounds for further development as anti-SARS-CoV-2 therapeutics.
Li and Sczepanski identified high-affinity L-DNA aptamers for the s2m [ ]. The optimized L-C1t and L-C3t aptamers selectively bind to s2m and cause significant conformational changes, thereby blocking protein-s2m interactions. The ‘cross-chiral’ nucleic acid recognition mode leads to highly selective binding by relying on both the sequence and structure of the RNA target. Due to its intrinsic nuclease resistance and advantageous binding properties, L-aptamer represents a promising approach for developing RNA-targeted therapeutics.
Besides s2m-targeting, SPEAR-targeting PMO markedly reduced SARS-CoV-2 titre by blocking SPEAR-protein interaction, suggesting a pan-sarbecoviral therapeutic modality [ ].

## Conclusions
Protein translation is crucial in the whole in-cell life process of SARS-CoV-2. After being released and uncoated in the cytoplasm, gRNA is immediately translated to produce early proteins NSPs to prepare the infected cell for viral takeover. In the later stage of the infection process, the set of sg-mRNAs are translated to obtain structural proteins for virion assembly ( ). Inhibiting viral protein translation is thus a potential anti-SARS-CoV-2 approach. Due to the fact that SARS-CoV-2 uses host translation machinery, to selectively block viral translation, direct targeting of the viral mRNA, including gRNA and sg-mRNA, is a better option.
Current anti-SARS-CoV-2 translation-targeting strategies mainly involve targeting viral RNA cleavage and to hinder viral RNA element function. Targeting viral RNA cleavage gets more attention, with RNA-degrading strategies such as ASO (gapmer), siRNA, CRISPR/Cas13, XNAzyme, and anti-DNA antibody ( ). As the gRNA of SARS-CoV-2 is the common template for ORF1a/b translation, sg-mRNA transcription, and new gRNA replication, its targeted cleavage can simultaneously block these processes, thereby enhancing the antiviral effect. However, there are still challenges like specificity, stability, immunogenicity, and delivery, and no corresponding drug has obtained clinical approval yet.
Targeted intervention strategies for viral mRNA functional elements rely on the understanding of virus translation. The translation mechanism of SARS-CoV-2 is poorly understood, but its dependence on several conserved elements has been preliminarily shown. Its UTRs (5 UTR and 3 UTR) may hijack the host ribosome for translation initiation. SLs1–5 in the 5 UTR mediate this, and s2m in the 3 UTR may affect it. For ORF1a/b translation, PFSE-mediated PRF is required. Researchers are exploring anti-SARS-CoV-2 strategies by targeting the functions of these elements with SM ligands and ASOs ( and ). Their primary focus is on the PFSE and SL1, while s2m and other UTR SLs, including SL4 and SL5 in the 5 UTR, are less explored. To develop novel anti-SARS-CoV-2 drugs, more researches on these elements are needed.
SARS-CoV-2 s2m forms alternative secondary structures in different studies ( ), considered complex and dynamic [ ]. The size and flexibility of the apical GUAC loop and adjacent internal loop affect the formation and conversion of the kissing dimer to duplex [ ], likely fine-tuning its function as a molecular switch for precise regulation of translation and virus adaptability. But it’s uncertain whether all the reported structural alternativity is due to s2m dynamics. SL4 and SL5 may be crucial for cap-independent ribosome entry into SARS-CoV-2 mRNA. Renilla luciferase was used as the translation initiation reporter [ , ], but the constructs lacking complete SL5 have different AUG context and UTR structures from SARS-CoV-2 mRNA, so further studies are needed to accurately understand the mechanism. When using constructs, those with complete regulatory elements, such as the extended 5 UTR including SL4, SL5 and even downstream SLs of start codon AUG, and the 3 UTR including s2m are preferable.
