To view a copy of this licence, visit http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/.Osteoarthritis (OA) is a common joint disease associated with the aging of the population, and it reduces the quality of life of patients. It is characterized by the destruction of articular cartilage and the secretion of inflammatory cytokines. Owing to the unclear pathogenesis of OA, current treatment methods have significant limitations. Oxidative stress has been revealed to play an important role in the development of OA. Our experiments indicated that the levels of GSH decreased and the level of MDA increased in chondrocytes, which induced ferroptosis in chondrocytes in OA. We also revealed that ferroptosis was the main mechanism of cartilage destruction caused by the addition of the ferroptosis activator erastin and the ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1. NOX1 is the main modulator of oxidative stress by increasing the generation of reactive oxidative species (ROS). We suppressed the expression of NOX1 in chondrocytes through cell transfection. The expression of collagen II and MMP13, and the secretion of IL-1β and TNF-α were reversed. An increase in the mitochondrial membrane potential and a decrease in the level of intracellular ROS indicate an improvement in oxidative damage. Additionally, we determined the effect of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway on NOX1-mediated chondrocyte injury. We found that NOX1 inhibited the expression of Nrf2/HO-1, but the activation of Nrf2 improved the oxidative damage to chondrocytes in vivo and vitro. This study revealed that NOX1-mediated oxidative stress induces chondrocyte ferroptosis by inhibiting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Our findings contribute to revealing the pathogenesis of OA, providing targets for drug design and optimizing the clinical treatment of OA.

## Introduction
Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative disease characterized by joint pain, stiffness, deformity and limited movement . The prevalence rate of OA has been reported to be more than 50%, and it has increased with the increase in the overall age of the global according to global epidemiology research . OA is the main musculoskeletal cause of mobility inconvenience for elderly individuals . There are many pathogenic factors for OA, including genetic predisposition, age, obesity, and uneven joint arrangement. Because the pathogenesis of OA is still unclear and because people typically do not give attention to this disease early, patients choose total joint replacement surgery to improve the pain associated with OA and to resolve the irreversible degradation of cartilage after the failure of conservative management . Articular chondrocytes are the main cell type in articular cartilage and are responsible for the generation and maintenance of the extracellular environment of cartilage. They are strictly regulated to maintain the dynamic equilibrium between growth, differentiation and apoptosis, and contribute to the proper circulation of cartilage matrix biosynthesis and degradation under normal physiological conditions . Determining the pathological changes in articular cartilage contributes to analyzing the pathogenesis and optimizing the treatment plans for OA.
Oxidative stress is a main pathological change in the development of OA . Oxidative damage is involved in the disturbance of chondrocytes in OA . The expression of chondrogenic markers including Sox9, collagen II and aggrecan, decreases in the oxidative state . Measurement of synovial fluid indicated that articular cartilage in OA patients was in a state of peroxidation with elevated levels of malondialdehyde and decreased glutathione peroxidase . Oxidative changes in the joint result in the generation of a large amount of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Mitochondria play an important role in the regulation of ROS generation . ROS lead to lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial depolarization in chondrocytes in OA . Iron participates in the maintenance of chondrocyte homeostasis and induces oxidative stress-mediated ferroptosis in OA. Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of programmed cell death that is modulated by the composition of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) . Under the action of divalent iron or ester oxygenase, unsaturated fatty acids are highly expressed on the cell membrane and perform lipid peroxidation to induce chondrocyte ferroptosis . The activation of GPX4 could improve the degradation of the extracellular matrix induced by ferroptosis . Ferroptosis promotes chondrocyte apoptosis and induces extracellular matrix degradation . Exploring the upstream target and improving the state of oxidative stress are potential processes to prevent the deterioration of OA.
NADPH oxidase (NOX) mediates the oxidative damage of chondrocytes in OA . NOX family proteins reduce oxygen molecules to superoxide negative ions through NADPH-dependent single electron reduction, which is the main source of ROS and is the only enzyme that directly produces ROS in the body . NOX1, NOX2, NOX3 and NOX5 mainly convert oxygen into superoxide. NOX4, DUOX1 and DUOX2 mainly convert oxygen into hydrogen peroxide . NOX-induced ROS generation is involved in the development of OA . NOX activation is involved in the oxidative damage to chondrocytes induced by the secretion of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, interleukins or damage-associated molecular patterns . NOX1 is a main modulator of oxidative stress and is responsible for the catalytic one-electron transfer of oxygen to generate superoxide or hydrogen peroxide. NOX1 is associated with leucine-rich anion channels to provide a pathway for the influx of superoxide to the cytoplasm . NOX1 also induces ROS damage by inhibiting the activity of antioxidant enzymes in inflammation . NOX1 knockout reportedly decreases the severity and incidence of experimental arthritis in mice . On the basis of the above evidence, we speculate that NOX1-mediated oxidative stress plays an important role in the oxidative damage of chondrocytes during the deterioration of osteoarthritis. Our research aims to reveal the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis, identify targets for drug design, and contribute to the clinical treatment of OA. NOX1 is a potential pathological and therapeutic marker that mediates oxidative damage to induce chondrocyte ferroptosis during the development of OA.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
SD rates were purchased from housed in the Department of Laboratory Animal Science of China Medical University. Monosodium iodoacetate (MIA) was obtained from Sigma–Aldrich. Antibodies against collagen II (1:1000; cat. no. ab34712), MMP13 (1:3000, cat. no. ab39012), SLC7A11 (1:1000, cat. no. ab175186), ACSL4 (1:10,000, cat. no. ab155282), FTH1 (1:1000, cat. no. ab183781), TFRC (1:1000, cat. no. ab214039), NOX1 (0.1 μg/ml, cat. no. ab131088), Nrf2 (1:1000, cat. no. ab92946) and HO-1 (1:5000; cat. no. ab68477) were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA). Antibodies against GPX4 (1:1000; no. 59735) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology, Inc. Additionally, β‑actin (1:2000; 66009‑1‑Ig) antibodies and horseradish peroxidase‑conjugated anti‑mouse and anti‑rat secondary antibodies (both 1:2000; SA00001‑1 and SA00001‑15) were obtained from ProteinTech Group, Inc. Nrf2 agonist dimethyl fumarate (DMF) was purchased from Merck, Germany. Erastin and ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) were purchased from Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (1:1000 means that 1 ml of antibody is added to 1000 ml of TBST).

## Extraction and culture of primary chondrocytes
Four-week-old SD rats were euthanized by cervical dislocation under inhalation anesthesia with isoflurane. Then the rates were soaked in 75% ethanol to maintain relative sterility, transferred to a super clean workbench, and their limbs were fixed. Femur and tibia of rats were separated under sterile conditions. Fascia, muscle, and connective tissue were removed as much as possible. Cartilage tissues from the femoral condyle and tibial plateau were collected and cut into tissue blocks smaller than 1 mm3. These tissues were rinsed twice with PBS solution containing 100 U/ml streptomycin sulfate and 100 mg/ml penicillin. After standing for 5 min, the upper liquid and suspended tissue were removed. Three times the volume of 0.25% trypsin was added to digest cartilage tissue on a constant temperature shaker at 37 ℃, and vibrate for 15 min. After standing at 4 ℃ for 5 min, the supernatant was removed, 0.2% collagenase II was added on a constant temperature shaker at 37 ℃, and shake for 45 min. Growth medium was added to terminate digestion and the suspension passed through the 200 mesh filter screen in turn. The filtrate was collected and centrifuged at 1200 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the cells were resuspended with DMEM medium (Hyclone, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (HyClone; Cytiva), 100 U/ml streptomycin sulfate and 100 mg/ml penicillin, and then cultured in a 5% CO2 cell incubator at 37 ℃. Three passage chondrocytes were used for experiments.

## Cell viability
Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8, APExBIO) was used to measure the activity of chondrocytes after MIA treatment to determine the optimal concentration of MIA. Chondrocytes were plated into 96‑well plates at 5 × 103 cells/well in 100 µl medium/well. Different concentrations of MIA including 1 µM, 2.5 µM and 5 µM were added into chondrocytes for 24 h treatment in a humidified incubator. CCK‑8 solution was added at 10 µl/well, and the cells were incubated in incubator for another 1 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was used to represent cell viability.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
Rat IL-1β and TNF-α ELISA kit was purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology. The supernatant of chondrocytes was collected by centrifugation and stored in − 80 ℃ refrigerator. The relative protein concentrations of IL-1β and TNF-α were calculated by measuring the absorbance at 450 nm. All experiments were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Western blotting
Western blotting was used to detect the protein contents of matrix protein. The cleaned cells were lysed on ice using radioimmunoprecision (Ripa) assay buffer (Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology) and phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride with the ratio of 1:1000. The lysed samples were collected in the centrifuge tubes and sufficient centrifugated. The supernatant was applied for calculating protein concentration by using BCA assay kit (Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology). The quantified proteins were stored in − 80 ℃ refrigerator. SDS-PAGE was performed to resolve the proteins with different molecular weight and polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes were used to transfer these proteins. These membranes were blocked and incubated overnight in corresponding primary antibodies. Then, we washed them with TBST and incubated with secondary antibodies for 1.5 h. Enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) system (UVP Inc., CA, United States) was used for protein content visualization. ImageJ software was used to calculate the optical density and relative protein expression levels normalized to β-actin.

## Glutathione (GSH) and malondialdehyde (MDA) measurement
GSH assay kit (S0053) and lipid peroxidation MDA assay kit (S0131S) were purchased from Beyotime. All experiments were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The supernatant of the cells to be tested was fully mixed with GSH working solution and reacted at room temperature for 60 min. The GSH content was determined according to the calculation formula, based on a pre drawn standard curve. Chondrocytes were fully lysed by Ripa solution, and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was collected for MDA measurement.

## Ferroptosis staining
FerroOrange (Dojindo, Japan) was used for fluorescence imaging of Fe2+ in living cells. FerroOrange was mixed with DMSO to prepare 1 mmol/L FerroOrange solution, and diluted with serum-free culture medium to 1 μmol/L. Chondrocytes were washed with serum-free culture medium, covered by 1 μmol/L FerroOrange solution and incubated at a 5% CO2, 37 ℃ environment. Fluorescence microscope was used to observe cell ferroptosis.

## Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) measurement
MMP measurement of chondrocytes was performed by using JC-1 (Beyotime Biotechnology). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, chondrocytes were stained with JC-1 working solution and cleaned with buffer solution. Flow cytometry (CytoFLEX System B3‑R3‑V3) was used to detect and CytExpert 2.3 (both Beckman Coulter, Inc.) was used to analyze the relative levels of MMP. The measurement of MMP of chondrocytes was at 24 h after MIA treatment.

## ROS measurement
Reactive oxygen species assay kit (DCFH-DA, Beyotime Biotechnology) was used to measure ROS level in chondrocytes. DCFH-DA was diluted with serum-free culture medium at 1:1000 to a final concentration of 10 μmol/L. Cell culture medium was removed and diluted DCFH-DA was added to cover chondrocytes sufficiently. Then, the cells were incubated at 37 ℃ for 20 min and washed three times with serum-free cell culture medium to fully remove DCFH-DA. The change of intracellular ROS was determined by laser confocal microscope and fluorescence microplate reader. The measurement of ROS of chondrocytes was at 24 h after MIA treatment.

## Cell transfection
NOX1‑small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) were designed and synthesized by SynBio‑Tech (Suzhou) Co., Ltd. Lipofectamine™ 3000 reagent (Invitrogen; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) was used to transfect NOX1-siRNA into chondrocytes for incubating 48 h according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Western blotting was performed to determine the relative protein expression of NOX1 after transfection to represent the transfection rate. The cells were used in the experiments 2 days after transfection at 37 ℃.

## Animal experiments
Sprague–Dawley (SD) male rats (6 weeks old, 200–220 g) were purchased from the Department of Laboratory Animal Science of China Medical University. They were fed in an environmental of 20–26 °C with constant temperature, 40–70% relative humidity, ≤ 14 mg/m3 ammonia concentration, ≤ 60 dB (A) noise and a 12/12 h alternating light and dark cycle. Before the experiment, all rats were adapted to the new feeding environment for 2 weeks. SD rats were randomly divided into three groups (n = 7 each group): sham group (50 μL saline), MIA group (0.5 mg MIA dissolved into 50 μL saline) and DMF group (50 mg/kg DMF after MIA treatment three times a week). After 4 weeks of above treatment, all rats were sacrificed and the cartilage from the femoral condyle and tibial plateau were collected under inhalation anesthesia with isoflurane. Serum was collected for Elisa test. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University and were performed according to the laboratory and animal welfare guidelines.

## Histological staining
Cartilage tissue in knee joint of SD was collected and fixed with formaldehyde. These tissue samples were dehydrated with alcohol, transparent with xylene, and then embedded in paraffin. The samples with paraffin were sliced and stained with hematoxylin–eosin (HE). The number and morphology of chondrocytes, such as vacuoles and chromatin condensation, are used to evaluate the cartilage.

## Statistical analysis
The results involved in this study were carried out three times of independent experiments biologically, and were analyzed by GraphPad software. Two-sided t-test or one-way analysis of variance followed with Tukey post-hoc test was used to determine statistical significance, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Ethics approval and consent to participate
All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University (no. [2021]460) in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. All animal experiments were performed according to laboratory and animal welfare guidelines and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University (approval no. 2019014).

## Results

## MIA induced ferroptosis in chondrocytes in a cell OA model
To simulate OA at the cellular level, we cultured chondrocytes in media containing different concentrations of MIA including 1 µM, 2.5 µM and 5 µM and determined the optimal concentration of MIA. The results indicated that 5 μM MIA inhibited chondrocyte activity after 24 h of treatment, accounting for 60% of the control group, which is an appropriate concentration for mechanistic research and subsequent verification (Fig.  A). We detected the levels of IL-1β and TNF-α, which indicate the inflammatory response of chondrocytes induced by MIA (Fig.  B, ). Additionally, the protein expression of collagen II and MMP13 was associated with chondrocyte damage after MIA treatment (Fig.  D, ). To reveal the role of ferroptosis in the development of OA, we first measured GSH and MDA levels. GSH is involved in the prevention of ferroptosis and MDA is associated with lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis activation. As shown in Fig.  F, , the level of GSH decreased and the level of MDA increased in chondrocytes after treatment with 5 μM MIA. GPX4, SLC7A11, ACSL4, FTH1 and TFRC are marker proteins related to ferroptosis. The protein expression of GPX4, SLC7A11 and FTH1 decreased, and that of ACSL4 and TFRC increased (Fig.  H, ). FerroOrange staining also indicated that MIA induced ferroptosis in chondrocytes (Fig.  J). 

## Ferroptosis mediated chondrocyte damage
On the basis of the above results, we aimed to determine the role of ferroptosis in the development of OA. We first added the ferroptosis inducer erastin (10 μM) to the chondrocyte medium. The expression of collagen II decreased, and that of MMP13 increased, which indicated the presence of chondrocyte damage (Fig.  A, ). Additionally, we added the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 (10 μM) to the chondrocyte medium supplemented with MIA. As shown in Fig.  C, , the inhibitory effect of MIA on the expression of collagen II and MMP13 was reversed after Fer-1 treatment, which indicated that ferroptosis was essential for MIA-induced chondrocyte damage. The expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11 in Fig.  shows the positive effect of erastin and negative effect of Fer-1 on ferroptosis. 

## NOX1-mediated oxidative damage is involved in the MIA-induced ferroptosis of chondrocytes
NOX1-mediated oxidative stress plays an important role in ferroptosis. The protein expression of NOX1 increased in chondrocytes treated with MIA (Fig.  A, ). To test the effect of NOX1, we transfected cells to inhibit NOX1 expression by adding NOX1-siRNA (Fig.  C, ). Both IL-1β and TNF-α secretion were lower after NOX1 inhibition than after MIA treatment alone (Fig.  E, ). Increased collagen II and decreased MMP13 expression also indicated that blocking NOX1 could improve chondrocyte damage (Fig.  G, ). Thus, we aimed to determine the role of NOX1 in oxidative stress-associated ferroptosis. As shown in F g.  I, , weakening NOX1 expression increased GSH and decreased MDA levels. Ferroptosis in chondrocytes was reversed (Fig.  K). Additionally, detection of the mitochondrial membrane potential and intracellular ROS levels indicated that MIA caused mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative damage in chondrocytes, which could also be improved by inhibiting NOX1 expression (Fig.  L, ). 

## NOX1 induced oxidative damage in chondrocytes by inhibiting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway
The Nrf2/HO-1 pathway plays an important role in the regulation of oxidative stress. As shown in Fig.  A, , the expression of Nrf2 and HO-1 decreased after MIA treatment and was reversed after NOX-1-siRNA transfection. NOX-1 can mediate oxidative damage by inhibiting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Then, we added DMF (25 μM) to the chondrocytes. The expression of GPX4, SLC7A11 and FTH1 increased, and the expression of ACSL4 and TFRC decreased (Fig.  C, ). Ferroptosis staining also indicated that activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway inhibited the MIA-induced ferroptosis of chondrocytes (Fig.  E). Additionally, JC-1 and DCFH-DA measurements revealed that Nrf2 activation improved the mitochondrial membrane potential and intracellular ROS level (Fig.  F, ). 

## Intra-articular injection of a Nrf2 agonist improves the progression of OA
For the animal experiments, we constructed a rat model of OA by injecting MIA into the articular cavity. As shown in Fig.  A, MIA induction caused cartilage destruction and matrix loss, which could be reversed after DMF treatment. Additionally, the serum IL-1β and TNF-α levels also indicated that DMF improved the inflammatory response in OA rats treated with MIA (Fig.  B, ). These results suggest that the activation of Nrf2 can attenuate inflammatory damage and improve cartilage tissue in OA. 

## Discussion
With the age of the overall population increasing, an increasing number of elderly people suffer from OA, which seriously influences their quality of life. Most patients neglect early symptoms and receive inadequate treatment, leading to a continuous deterioration of their condition. Patients with OA either accept joint replacement surgery or wait for their condition to deteriorate before undergoing joint replacement surgery because of a lack of understanding of the pathogenesis of OA. Additionally, drug treatments mainly include oral glucosamine and intra-articular injection of sodium hyaluronate, which do not fundamentally inhibit the process of cartilage destruction and inflammation deterioration . Given this context, we aimed to explore the pathological state of chondrocytes in the pathogenesis of OA and reveal pathogenic target genes.
Oxidative stress plays an important role in the pathogenesis of OA by increasing the generation of ROS . Mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to the oxidative damage of chondrocytes, resulting in matrix degradation of the articular cartilage . ROS accumulation also accelerates the senescence of chondrocytes . Moreover, inhibiting mitochondrial ROS generation can deactivate the NLRP3 inflammasome and reduce the secretion of inflammatory cytokines to alleviate OA . Articular injection of exogenous antioxidants has been shown to prevent the occurrence and delay the deterioration of OA . Ferroptosis plays an important role in oxidative stress-mediated damage to chondrocytes. It has been revealed that ROS-induced ferroptosis occurs in chondrocytes and that ferroptosis inhibitors can reverse OA development in vitro and in vivo . Our study also revealed that ferroptosis is an important form of programmed death in chondrocytes during OA deterioration. Fer-1 addition obviously improved the degree of oxidative damage to chondrocytes. There findings indicate that oxidative stress-ferroptosis is a necessary pathological change in chondrocytes and the pathogenesis of OA. The regulation of iron ions inside and outside the mitochondria involved in oxidative stress-mediated ferroptosis is a potential direction for further research.
NOX1 is an important intracellular initiator of the oxidative stress response. In addition to directly inducing ROS generation, NOX1 activation clearly suppresses the activity of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase and catalase . To identify the upstream target, we first measured the expression of NOX1 in chondrocytes after MIA treatment. NOX1 expression clearly increased, and silencing NOX1 reversed mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative damage in chondrocytes. In an animal model of OA, the expression of NOX1 in cartilage tissues was consistent with the results at the cellular level. NOX1 leads to a pathological state of oxidative stress, mediating cartilage degeneration and the inflammatory response during OA development. Additionally, we explored the signaling pathways involved in this process. Nrf2 mediates antioxidant signals to eliminate intracellular ROS. Nrf2 induces the transcription of many protective genes, such as heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), glutathione-S-transferases and NADPH quinone oxidoreductase 1 to resist oxidative stress damage . The Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is involved in anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects in OA . Recent studies have revealed that multiple drugs upregulate Nrf2 expression to ameliorate OA , . The activation of Nrf2 contributes to reducing the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines and inhibiting the degradation of the extracellular matrix . Additionally, Nrf2 expression can reverse NOX1-mediated ROS production in adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells . High NOX1 activity-induced oxidative stress and cell apoptosis are related to the inhibition of Nrf2 activation and nuclear translocation . The Nrf2 signaling pathway is essential for protection against NOX1-associated oxidative damage. However, the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is classic for regulating redox balance. NOX1 overexpression may induce oxidative damage while inhibiting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. The effect of NOX1-Nrf2/HO-1 on ferroptosis is not inevitable. We added DMF to activate the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, after which the ferroptosis of chondrocytes improved. Silencing NOX1 also increased the expression of Nrf2 and HO-1. We conclude that NOX1-induced ferroptosis of chondrocytes via inhibition of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is specific to OA development. Our study also revealed that the activation of Nrf2 contributes to reversing chondrocyte ferroptosis induced by NOX1-mediated oxidative stress, which provides evidence for OA prevention and treatment. Intra-articular injection of targeted NOX1 inhibitors to activate the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway can optimize treatment plans and improve therapeutic effects of OA ( ).
There are several limitations in our experiments. MIA treatment of chondrocytes simulates the acute damage, which may not fully recapitulate the chronic progression of OA. MIA mainly targets chondrocytes. It has been reported that an increased load on subchondral bone is also associated with clinical OA pain . A previous study indicated that NOX4 is the major component of joint inflammation and oxidative stress in OA . Our study validated the important role of NOX1 induced oxidative damage in chondrocyte ferroptosis in vitro. In vivo experimental verification of the importance of NOX1 and NOX4 in the OA process may need to be improved. Additionally, the roles of NOX1 and Nrf2/HO-1 in oxidation–reduction reactions are established, which indicates their inevitable connection. In vivo experiments on NOX1 knockout mice will increase the credibility of our conclusions.
