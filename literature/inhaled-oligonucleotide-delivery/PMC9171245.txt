## Abstract
Increasing circular RNAs (circRNAs) are involved in the progression of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). However, circRNA biogenesis and circRNA-mediated crosstalk between mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals in IPF remain obscure. In this study, a novel circRNA-ankyrin repeat domain 42 (ANKRD42) from peripheral blood of patients with IPF, which participated in pulmonary fibrosis through the close communication of mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals, was identified. Mechanistic studies revealed that the heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein L (hnRNP L) activated the circANKRD42 reverse splicing biogenesis. The biogenetic circANKRD42 sponged miR-324-5p to promote the AJUBA expression, which blocked the binding between phosphorylated yes-associated protein 1 (YAP1) and large tumor suppressor kinase 1/2 (LATS1/2), leading to increased YAP1 entering the nucleus. circANKRD42 also sponged miR-136-5p to promote the YAP1 translation. Accumulating YAP1 in nucleus bound to TEAD, which initiated the transcription of genes related to mechanical stiffness. Finally, the therapeutic effect of circANKRD42 was evaluated in mice and the association between circANKRD42 and clinicopathological features was analyzed in IPF patients. Our findings supported that circANKRD42 is a promising biomarker and a potential therapeutic target related to cytoskeleton tension for IPF treatment.
Keywords: pulmonary fibrosis, circRNA, RNA reverse splicing, cytoskeleton tension, hnRNP L, YAP1, AJUBA

## Introduction
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and usually lethal disorder of unknown etiology and has limited therapeutic options. The damage to the alveolar epithelial cell, which releases inflammatory and fibrotic factors, is followed by the differentiation of fibroblast into myofibroblast, uncontrolled myofibroblast proliferation and high migration, and extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, leading to alveolar structure destruction. Patients eventually die of respiratory failure. Therefore, understanding the molecular mechanism of the differentiation of fibroblast into myofibroblast and blocking the myofibroblast proliferation and migration should be done to ameliorate the development of IPF. Recent research found that the dysregulation in mechanical properties occurs in the fibroblast from patients with IPF, leading to the alterations in cellular morphology, stiffness, adhesion, and migratory capacity. However, the communication of these mechanical properties signals with biochemical signals to promote pulmonary fibrogenesis is obscure. 
Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a subpopulation of endogenous noncoding RNAs that are produced from pre-mRNAs through the back splicing of exons and differ from mRNAs in their production, structure, and turnover. Thus, circRNAs have unique cellular functions and potential biomedical applications. Tsitsipatis et al. reported that the interaction of circRNA-PCNX with AUF1 selectively prevents AUF1 binding to p21 mRNA, leading to enhanced p21 mRNA stability and p21 protein production and suppressed cell growth. circRNA-VGLL3 markedly enhances new bone formation with upregulated bone mineral density, bone and tissue volume, and trabeculae number and increases new bone generation by modulating the microRNA (miRNA)-dependent integrin alpha5 expression. This phenomenon indicates that circRNA-VGLL3-engineered, adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells may be effective potential therapeutic targets for bone regenerative medicine. circRNA-SCAR, a mitochondrial circRNA that drives metaflammation, is associated with steatosis-to-nonalcoholic steatohepatitis progression. Some cytoplasmic circRNAs can be effectively translated into detectable peptides. Yang et al. discovered that consensus m6A motifs are enriched in circRNAs and that a single m6A site is sufficient to drive translation initiation. In addition, circRNAs have higher stability than their linear counterparts and can be easily detected in plasma, saliva, and exosomes. , , Therefore, with the development of detection and characterization methodologies, circRNAs can be potential biomarkers or therapeutic targets for numerous diseases. For example, Ju et al. developed a prognostic tool on the basis of four circRNAs to improve the prognostic stratification for patients with radically resected stage II/III colon cancer. circRNA-HECTD1 and circRNA-ZC3H4 are validated to promote the pulmonary macrophage activation leading to silicosis. , In fibrosis, circPSD3 alleviates hepatic fibrogenesis by targeting the miR-92b-3p/Smad7 axis. circUbe3a from M2 macrophage-derived small extracellular vesicles mediates myocardial fibrosis after acute myocardial infarction. 
However, the cell perceives the change in the microenvironment through not only soluble signals but also mechanical properties cues, such as cytoskeletal biomechanics or ECM stiffness, especially in fibrogenesis. The cellular mechanical properties, such as stiffness, may serve as novel biological markers of cell phenotypes, reflecting disease processes and cell states. Recent studies revealed protective effects of circYap upon pressure overload via binding with tropomyosin-4 and gamma-actin decreasing actin polymerization in cardiac fibrosis. But in general, detailed studies on the understanding of circRNA-mediated mechanical stiffness signal are limited, especially in the pathogenesis and therapeutic measures for IPF. Our previous study first reported the different circRNA profiles in the peripheral blood samples of patients with IPF by using a circRNA microarray. Among these differentially expressed circRNAs, 38 are upregulated and 29 are downregulated. From the 67 dysregulated circRNAs, Li et al. selected six circRNAs with high absolute fold change and low p values (circANKRD42, circCDC27, circZMYM2, circGRHPR, circTADA2A, and circARHGAP26) to confirm our circRNA microarray results. Further, they demonstrated that circTADA2A relieves IPF via sponging miR-526b and miR-203. However, circRNA biogenesis and circRNA-mediated crosstalk between mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals in IPF remain obscure. In this study, the upregulated has-circ-100906, termed circANKRD42 because it is derived from its host gene ankyrin repeat domain 42 (ANKRD42), is selected to investigate its reverse splicing processing mechanism, circANKRD42-mediated crosstalk between mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals, and usefulness in the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis. We attempt to disclose the circANKRD42 biogenetic mechanism and reveal a complex circRNA-mediated interplay between mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals in pulmonary fibrosis.

## Results

## circANKRD42 functions as profibrotic factor to promote pulmonary fibrogenesis
Based on the microarray analysis from the plasma of patients with IPF, circANKRD42 has remarkably higher expression compared with other transcripts. So circANKRD42 expression was re-detected in peripheral blood samples from 25 patients with IPF by using quantitative RT-PCR (qRT-PCR) to verify the results of the microarray analysis. The circANKRD42 in patients with IPF was significantly higher than that in the matched controls ( A). Then, 5 ng/mL transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1) was used to activate the differentiation of fibroblast (MRC-5 cell) into myofibroblast to build a cell model of pulmonary fibrosis. Compared with the normal control group, circANKRD42 expression gradually increased in the TGF-β1-treated group, and the highest expression level was observed at 72 h in MRC-5 cells ( B). Thus, circANKRD42 was selected for further study.
 The myofibroblast migration and proliferation and ECM deposition were tested to elucidate the circANKRD42 function during lung fibrogenesis. First, a scratch-wound-healing assay was performed to analyze the effect of circANKRD42 on myofibroblast migration ( C). Images of the Incucyte S3 live-cell analysis system depicted that the TGF-β1 group had faster cell migration rate than the normal group. The circANKRD42 knockdown (si-circANKRD42) reduced the migration ability of the myofibroblast induced by TGF-β1, whereas the circANKRD42 overexpression (RP) enhanced the myofibroblast migration ability. The real-time cell growth technology further monitored the effect of circANKRD42 on myofibroblast proliferation and migration ( D and 1E). The growth curve exhibited that the circANKRD42 knockdown inhibited myofibroblast proliferation and migration, and circANKRD42 overexpression promoted the myofibroblast proliferation and migration compared with the TGF-β1. Finally, the western blot further verified the effect of circANKRD42 on fibrotic proteins ( F). This result demonstrated that the circANKRD42 knockdown group had a rapid decrease in α-SMA, vimentin, and collagen I levels compared with the TGF-β1 group. circANKRD42 overexpression induced a rapid increase in α-SMA, vimentin, and collagen I levels. These findings suggested that circANKRD42 could function as a profibrotic factor to promote the differentiation of fibroblast into myofibroblast, myofibroblast proliferation and migration, and ECM deposition.

## Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein L (hnRNP L) promotes the reverse splicing of circANKRD42 biogenesis
Specified primers were designed for PCR to determine the reverse splicing site of circANKRD42. PCR products were sequenced and analyzed using the Sanger sequencing. The detection elucidated that circANKRD42 produced from the reverse splicing from the third to eighth exon in ANKRD42 pre-mRNA located in chromosome 11, and its length was 797 nt ( A). As shown in B, divergent primers amplified the circANKRD42 from cDNA, but not genomic DNA (gDNA). RNase R treatment and actinomycin D assay were performed to further confirm the stability of circANKRD42 ( C and 2D). To explore the underlying mechanism of circANKRD42 biogenesis processing, we examined RNA-binding proteins to uncover RNA splicing factors. A specific biotin-labeled circANKRD42 probe was designed to perform an RNA pull-down assay. Silver staining results showed the enrichment of several bands of proteins. The hnRNP L was one of the binding proteins of circANKRD42 ( E). Given that the hnRNP L is an alternative RNA splicing factor, it was selected for further study. After the RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay by using hnRNP L antibody, qRT-PCR further indicated that the hnRNP L protein bound the pre-ANKRD42 ( F). Then, hnRNP L gain- and loss-of-function studies were performed to study the role of hnRNP L. After silencing hnRNP L, circANKRD42 decreased and ANKRD42 increased. After overexpressed hnRNP L, circANKRD42 increased, but ANKRD42 had no change ( G). We speculated that overexpressed hnRNP L increased total ANKRD42, but most of them were spliced into circANKRD42. So ANKRD42 had no change. Dual-luciferase reporter assay indicated that si-hnRNP L reduced fluorescence intensity of circANKRD42, indicating that hnRNP L regulated circANKRD42 ( H). The myofibroblast proliferation and migration were repressed after silencing hnRNP L ( I). Fibrotic proteins decreased under si-hnRNPL action and increased under overexpressed hnRNP L ( J). Rescue experiments indicated that si-circANKRD42 reversed the increased trends of collagen I, vimentin, and α-SMA levels caused by overexpressed hnRNP L ( K). These findings suggested that hnRNP L promoted the reverse splicing of circANKRD42, leading to the control of the profibrotic effect of circANKRD42.
 

## circANKRD42 serves as a sponge of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p
The intracellular localization of circRNA determines its regulatory fibrogenesis mechanism. Thus, the RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (RNA FISH) assay and nucleocytoplasmic separation were performed to investigate the circANKRD42 location. The images of immunofluorescence revealed that circANKRD42 was preferentially localized within the cytoplasm and only a little in the nucleus. circANKRD42 knockdown did not cause circANKRD42 translocation ( A). Nuclear and cytoplasmic fraction experiments confirmed the location of circANKRD42 ( B). Given that circANKRD42 was mainly in the cytoplasm, circANKRD42 was hypothesized to act as miRNA sponge to promote the pulmonary fibrosis process. Thus, the potential miRNAs bound to circANKRD42 were predicted using the miRanda and TargetScan algorithms. The intersection of databases analysis showed that miR-324-5p, miR-136-5p, miR-374a-3p, miR-330-5p, and miR-653-3p were the top five miRNAs that had higher binding value than other miRNAs. miR-324-5p had two binding sites, and miR-136-5p had three binding sites with circANKRD42. The other three miRNAs had only one binding site. The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay verified that miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics reduced the activity of luciferase. When the predicted binding site of circANKRD42 was mutated, miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics did not affect the luciferase activity ( C), indicating that miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p bound to circANKRD42. The RIP for argonaute-2 (AGO2) was conducted to demonstrate that endogenous circANKRD42 and miR-324-5p/miR-136-5p pulled down were specifically enriched by qRT-PCR analysis ( D). Next, we evaluated the expression and function of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p in pulmonary fibrosis. Their expression levels gradually decreased with increased TGF-β1 treatment time ( E). Expression of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p also decreased in the blood of 25 IPF patients ( F). RNA-FISH was conducted to detect the overlap of circANKRD42 and miR-324-5p in MRC-5 cells. They mainly located in the cytoplasm ( G). The above results suggest that circANKRD42 can bind miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. So miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p were selected for further research.
 The western blot analysis revealed that the expression levels of α-SMA, vimentin, and collagen I were suppressed by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics and enhanced by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors ( H and 3I). Similarly, the myofibroblast proliferation and migration decreased under the treatments of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics and increased under the treatments of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors ( J and 3K).
Finally, rescue experiments were designed to confirm whether the circANKRD42 profibrotic function depended on miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. Data exhibited that the interfering circANKRD42 expression group reduced collagen I, vimentin, and α-SMA compared with the TGF-β1 group. However, miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors reversed the decreased trends of these proteins and considerably increased in the expression of these proteins ( A). Similarly, the expression levels of these proteins were promoted by the circANKRD42 overexpression and considerably decreased by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics ( B). Furthermore, rescue experiments showed that miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors reversed the myofibroblast proliferation and migration decrease caused by circANKRD42 knockdown. miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics reversed the myofibroblast proliferation and migration increase caused by the circANKRD42 overexpression ( C and 4D).
 

## circANKRD42 directly targets AJUBA and YAP1, depending on miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p
miRNAs exert their regulatory functions through specific interactions with their target genes. miRWalk was first used to predict the target signal pathways of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. We found that the Hippo signal pathway was the target pathway for not only miR-324-5p but also miR-136-5p. Then, TargetScan, miRanda data, and miRbase were used to explore the target genes of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. The intersection of databases analysis showed that the target genes of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p were AJUBA and YAP1, respectively. Considering AJUBA and YAP1 were the key genes in Hippo signal pathway, subsequently, the dual-luciferase reporter gene assay was performed to confirm that AJUBA and YAP1 were the target genes of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p ( A). The expression levels of AJUBA and YAP1 increased with prolonged TGF-β1 treatment ( B). Western blot results indicated that AJUBA and YAP1 levels were reduced by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics, respectively, and enhanced by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors, respectively ( C). The expression levels of AJUBA and YAP1 proteins were increased by circANKRD42 overexpression and decreased by circANKRD42 knockdown ( D). Immunofluorescence images demonstrated that p-YAP1 was mainly located in the cytoplasm in the normal group and that YAP1 was mainly located in the nucleus in the TGF-β1 group. The circANKRD42 knockdown group had enhanced p-YAP1 expression in the cytoplasm, and the circANKRD42 overexpression group had promoted YAP1 expression in the nucleus compared with the TGF-β1 group. This finding suggested that YAP1 translocated from cytoplasm to nucleus after YAP1 dephosphorylation in pulmonary fibrosis, which could be strengthened by circANKRD42 overexpression and weakened by circANKRD42 knockdown ( E). Rescue experiments showed that the suppression of si-circANKRD42 on AJUBA and YAP1 expression was partly reversed by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors and that the induction of circANKRD42 overexpression on AJUBA and YAP1 expression was partly reversed by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics ( F and 5G). These results proved that the circANKRD42 regulation on AJUBA and YAP1 depended on miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. And miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p target to AJUBA and YAP1’s rescue experiments proved that roles of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p depended on AJUBA and YAP1, respectively ( H and 5I). Then, the large tumor suppressor kinase 1/2 (LATS1/2), an upstream kinase of YAP1 that could phosphorylate YAP1, was explored. Results demonstrated that LATS1/2 and p-LATS1/2 were increased by circANKRD42 knockdown and decreased by circANKRD42 overexpression ( J). Rescue experiments displayed that the miR-324-5p inhibitor suppressed LATS1/2 and p-LATS1/2 and reversed the increasing trend caused by circANKRD42 knockdown. The miR-324-5p mimic promoted LATS1/2 and p-LATS1/2 and reversed the decreasing trend caused by circANKRD42 overexpression ( K). This finding suggested that circANKRD42 suppressed LATS1/2 and p-LATS1/2, depending on miR-324-5p.
 

## circANKRD42 enhances the mechanical stiffness, depending on miR-324-5p, miR-136-5p, and YAP1
Atomic force microscope (AFM) is a visual tool to study the mechanical stiffness. Then, we employed AFM quantitative measurement to examine the cell stiffness of fibroblast in response to TGF-β1. And Young’s modulus, also known as tensile modulus, is a physical quantity that measures the resistance of material to deformation and the stiffness of material. So the changes of mechanical stiffness were measured through Young’s modulus by using the AFM. Young’s modulus value increased in TGF-β1 group compared with the normal group. Knockdown circANKRD42 induced its decrease, and overexpressed circANKRD42 caused its increase ( A). F-actin is an important component of cytoskeleton, which takes part in mechanical, such as cytoskeleton, tension. Western blot demonstrated that F-actin was reduced by circANKRD42 knockdown and increased by overexpression, respectively ( B). miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics and inhibitors respectively inhibited and enhanced the F-actin expression ( C). The immunofluorescence of the cytoskeleton showed that the circANKRD42 knockdown and overexpression respectively decreased and increased the cytoskeleton tension. The rescue experiments of immunofluorescence images depicted that the inhibition of si-circANKRD42 on the cytoskeleton tension was partly reversed by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p inhibitors and that the induction of circANKRD42 overexpression on the cytoskeleton tension was partly reversed by miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics ( D). The rescue experiments of western blot further confirmed this point and proved that the circANKRD42 enhancement on the cytoskeleton tension depended on miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p ( E). The overexpression of YAP1 considerably increased the F-actin and reversed the decreasing trend of F-actin caused by the circANKRD42 knockdown ( F), which revealed that the circANKRD42 enhancement on the cytoskeleton tension depended on YAP1. Myo1c can sense cellular mechanical properties and function as tension-sensitive anchor or transporter. Western blot uncovered that circANKRD42 knockdown decreased Myo1c expression and overexpressed circANKRD42 increased Myo1c expression compared with the TGF-β1 group ( G).
 

## circANKRD42 is evaluated as a potential therapeutic target for pulmonary fibrosis
The potential of circANKRD42 as a therapeutic target was evaluated in bleomycin (BLM)-treated mice model of pulmonary fibrosis and IPF patients. Because the circANKRD42 was screened from the peripheral blood samples of patients with IPF and had a low degree of homology in mice, we first validated the mouse model is relevant to the proposed mechanism. In mouse fibroblast L929 cells, qRT-PCR results demonstrated that overexpressed humanized circANKRD42 decreased miR-136-5p and miR-324-5p ( A). The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay was performed to confirm that miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p were also the target genes of circANKRD42 in mice ( B). Western blot results illustrated that AJUBA and YAP1 decreased under miR-324-5p and miR-324-5p mimics action, respectively ( C). Rescue experiments displayed that overexpressed circANKRD42 increased AJUBA and YAP1 expression, but miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p mimics suppressed AJUBA and YAP1 expression and reversed the increasing trend caused by overexpressed circANKRD42 ( D). The findings in L929 cells corroborated the human fibroblast results, which suggest that the mouse model is relevant to the proposed mechanism. Hence, we can use mice model to further confirm the proposed mechanism.
To improve the convincing mechanism and overcome the low degree homology of circANKRD42 in mice, according to the similar study, the overexpressed circANKRD42 was packaged into the adenovirus vector to spray into the mouse lung ( A). Meanwhile, we mutated ATGGAG and GGATGC to CTTGCG and TGCTTC in the full-length sequence of circANKRD42, which were the binding sites of miR-136-5p and miR-324-5p, respectively. We synthesized circANKRD42 mutant packaged into the adenovirus vectors and sprayed to the lungs of mice. qRT-PCR data illustrated that circANKRD42 increased in mice with overexpressed circANKRD42. Expression of miR-136-5p and miR-324-5p decreased in overexpressed circANKRD42 group. There was no significant change in the circANKRD42 mutation group compared with the control group ( B). The results of MicroCT imaging system for small animal (PerkinElmer, USA) demonstrated that fibrotic changes occurred in the left lower lung of mice in overexpressed circANKRD42 group. The degree of fibrosis in overexpressed circANKRD42 + BLM group was significantly higher than that in BLM + circANKRD42 NC group. The degree of fibrosis in circANKRD42 mutation + BLM group was similar to that in the BLM + circANKRD42 NC model group and had no significant change compared with the control group ( C). H&E and Masson images revealed that excess collagen was deposited, and the alveolar wall became thicker in overexpressed circANKRD42 group than those in sham + circANKRD42 NC. The degree of fibrosis in circANKRD42 mutation + BLM group was similar to that in the BLM + circANKRD42 NC group and had no significant change compared with the control group ( D and 7E). Overexpressed circANKRD42 promoted profibrotic proteins containing collagen I, vimentin, and α-SMA and enhanced mechanical force signals, such as LATS1/2, F-actin, AJUBA, and YAP1. However, there was no significant change in circANKRD42 mutation group ( F and 7G). Finally, we synthesized the mimics of miR-136-5p and miR-324-5p packaged into the adenovirus vectors and sprayed to the lungs of mice. The results of MicroCT imaging system for small animal, lung function analysis, H&E, and Masson showed that the mimics of miR-136-5p and miR-324-5p attenuated collagen deposition, improved the forced vital capacity (FVC), and had significant therapeutic effects ( H–7K). In vivo experiment results coincided with in vitro results and reflected the potential of circANKRD42 as a therapeutic target for pulmonary fibrosis.
 Finally, we assessed clinical significance of circANKRD42 in patients with IPF. qRT-PCR was conducted to examine circANKRD42 expression in plasma from healthy people. It is very interesting that circANKRD42 expression levels increased with age ( A). It is reasonable to infer that, probably because IPF is an age-related disease. The receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) between circANKRD42 and FVC showed that the sensitivity and specificity values are 60.0% and 93.3% in IPF patients, respectively ( B). The area under the ROC curve was 0.769. ROC curve between circANKRD42 and DLco showed that the sensitivity and specificity values are 80.0% and 53.3% in IPF patients, respectively. The area under the ROC curve was 0.649 ( C). Moreover, we employed the RNA scope assay to detect the location of circANKRD42, observing the circANKRD42 mainly located in the cytoplasm in IPF patient’s lung samples ( D). The findings indicated the potential value of circANKRD42 as a biomarker or therapeutic target for IPF.
 

## Discussion
circRNAs participate in the development and progression of a variety of human diseases through their involvement in the sequestration of miRNAs or proteins, modulation of transcription, interference with splicing, and translation to produce polypeptides. However, their biogenesis and how they process the interchange between physical and biochemical signals are not fully understood. In this study, we elucidated circANKRD42 biogenesis, which mediated pulmonary fibrosis progression by facilitating the differentiation of fibroblast into myofibroblast. Mechanistic studies revealed that the upstream hnRNP L activated the circANKRD42 biogenesis by promoting RNA reverse splicing. Increasing circANKRD42 mediated the crosstalk between mechanical stiffness and biochemical signals through at least two signal pathways. First, the circANKRD42-sponged miR-136-5p targeted YAP translocation via the dephosphorylation of Yap. Second, the circANKRD42-sponged miR-324-5p targeted AJUBA to block the binding between p-Yap and p-LATS, resulting in increased Yap entering the nucleus. Increasing Yap in nucleus bound to TEAD, which initiated genes related to mechanical properties transcription, such as F-actin and Myo1c ( D).
The vast majority of circRNAs are derived from coding sequences, making their biogenesis compete with the production of linear isoforms. Thus, the altered circRNA biogenesis contributes to multiple hallmarks of disease. , , However, the principles of biogenesis are currently not fully understood in detail. Recent reports indicate that splicing factors, such as splicing factor proline- and glutamine-rich, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (hnRNPs) and Quaking, participate in regulating circRNA biogenesis. , Among these splicing factors, hnRNP L, which belongs to the hnRNP family, is found to affect RNA splicing, packaging, and RNA half-life through 3ʹ UTR binding. For instance, by interacting with the histone methyltransferase SETD2, hnRNP L can mediate the crosstalk between the transcription and the splicing machinery. A genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 knockout screen once has identified hnRNP L as dependent to prostate cancer by directly regulating a set of RNA splicing, including circRNA splicing. Furthermore, hnRNP-L-regulated circRNA genes in prostate cancer are significantly more enriched than those in normal prostate. In pulmonary fibrosis, whether hnRNP L functions as splicing factor has been unreported. Our earlier study revealed that hnRNP L can form an RNA-protein complex with lncITPF to target ITGBL1, leading to aggravated lung fibrosis. In the present study, we identified that hnRNP L, a splicing factor, could be involved in fibrogenesis by controlling circRNA biogenesis.
The most frequently described regulation of circRNAs acts as miRNA sponge to regulate the target gene. , Accordingly, the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network has potential implications for understanding pulmonary fibrosis. Within the circRNA nucleotide sequence, a circRNA should contain multiple miRNA-binding sites and has multiple functions. Thus, the regulatory network is not restricted to only one circRNA or miRNA. For example, lncCyrano, circCdr1as miR-7, and miR-671 form a regulatory network, in which lncCyrano represses miR-7 via the target-directed miR-671 degradation and enables circCdr1as accumulation in the mouse brain. A circular RNA profiling by using the RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analyses of ribosomal RNA-depleted total RNA reveals that circHIPK3 regulates cell growth by sponging nine miRNAs (i.e., miR-124, miR-152, miR-193a, miR-29a, miR-29b, miR-338, miR-379, miR-584, and miR-654) with 18 potential binding sites. circ949 and circ057 form a regulatory network with lnc865 and lnc556 to regulate simultaneously the miR-29b-2-5p targeting STAT3 translocation from cytoplasm to nucleus, which promotes pulmonary fibrogenesis. Astilbin alleviates pulmonary fibrosis through the regulatory network of two circRNAs (i.e., circRNA-949 and circRNA-662) and a miRNA (miR-29b-2-5p) with their targeted gene STAT3. Our work here uncovered that circANKRD42 simultaneously served as a sponge of miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p. Through controlling miR-324-5p and miR-136-5p, circANKRD42 further directly targeted AJUBA and YAP1 to worsen pulmonary fibrosis.
In addition to chemical stimulation, mechanical cues play a substantial role in regulating lung fibrogenesis. Outside the cell mechanics changes, such as cytoskeletal biomechanics, substratum hardness, cell mechanical distribution, or ECM stiffness, that the cell was cultured change will lead to a change in the elasticity of tissue. Wu et al. once demonstrated that elevated mechanical tension activates a TGF-β signaling loop in alveolar type II cells, which drives the periphery-to-center progression of lung fibrosis. Considering that compelling evidence showed that altered mechanical properties are major drivers of tissue fibrogenesis by promoting the mechano-activation of fibroblasts, a class of drugs known as “mechano-therapeutics” have been proposed on the basis of the mechanobiology of tissue fibrosis. However, how circRNA coordinates these mechanical responses is unknown. YAP, as a nuclear sensor of mechanical signal exerted by the cell mechanics changes, is necessary for the ability of cells to respond to mechanical signals. Elosegui-Artola et al. once showed that the force applied to the nucleus directly drives YAP nuclear translocation by decreasing the mechanical restriction of nuclear pores to molecular transport. Exposure to a stiff environment leads cells to establish a mechanical connection between the nucleus and the cytoskeleton, allowing forces exerted through focal adhesions to reach the nucleus. YAP inhibitors can inhibit YAP nuclear localization via the induction of YAP phosphorylation and cytoplasmic retention, reducing fibrosis markers in activated human lung fibroblast and in the bleomycin mouse model of pulmonary fibrosis. Targeting mechanics-induced fibroblast activation through the CD44-RhoA-YAP pathway can ameliorate crystalline silica-induced silicosis. The Kindlin-2 depletion activates Hippo/YAP signaling and alleviates renal fibrosis in Kindlin-2 knockout mice with unilateral ureteral occlusion. YAP activity increases in the respiratory epithelial cells of the lungs of patients with IPF. YAP and TAZ function as mechano-activated coordinators of the matrix-driven feedback loop that amplifies and sustains lung fibrosis. YAP1/Twist promotes fibroblast activation and lung fibrosis conferred by the miR-15a loss in IPF. Thus, targeting the YAP signal pathway may be a potential strategy to ameliorate lung fibrosis. Here, we demonstrated that circANKRD42 knockdown decreased the YAP1 expression and blocked the YAP1 translocation from cytoplasm to nucleus via the induction of YAP1 phosphorylation, which reduced the mechanical stiffness caused by cytoskeletal biomechanics and fibrotic markers. AJUBA is a positive regulator of YAP, which executes multiple cellular functions, including cell adhesion, motility, and mechanical properties. Rauskolb et al. reported that increasing and decreasing cytoskeletal tension respectively increases and decreases Drosophila wing growth. These changes in growth depend genetically on the AJUBA. Our work discovered that circANKRD42-sponged miR-324-5p targeted AJUBA to repress the binding between p-Yap1 and p-LATS, leading to increased Yap1 translocation into the nucleus. Increasing Yap in the nucleus promoted the genes related to mechanical stiffness transcription, such as Myo1c and F-actin. In addition, circANKRD42 also sponged miR-136-5p to promote YAP1 translocation.
In conclusion, circANKRD42 is a profibrotic factor and promotes the differentiation of fibroblast into myofibroblast and myofibroblast proliferation and migration via the AJUBA/YAP1-mediated cytoskeletal tension. Our in vivo and in vitro findings and the clinical data from patients with IPF supported that circANKRD42 is a promising biomarker and a potential therapeutic target related to the mechanical stiffness for IPF treatment.

## Materials and methods

## Patient samples
Blood samples from 25 IPF patients, diagnosed in accordance with the American Thoracic Society (ATS) and European Respiratory Society (ERS) criteria for IPF, and age-matched healthy controls were collected at the Affiliated Hospital of Binzhou Medical University (Binzhou and Yantai, China). The basic information of patients is shown in . All patients provided written informed consent, and ethical consent was granted from the Committee of Binzhou Medical University.
 

## Cell culture
Human fetal lung fibroblast cell line MRC-5 was purchased from American Type Culture Collection. The cells were maintained in advanced minimum essential medium and supplemented with 10% newborn calf serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin at 37°C under a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 95% air. Digestion and passage were performed until 80% confluence of cells. The cells were administered with 5 ng/mL TGF-β1 for different times in accordance with the experimental requirements.

## qRT-PCR
Total RNA was isolated from cells or tissues using TRIzol reagent. cDNA synthesis was performed using a PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit following the manufacturer’s instructions. The qRT-PCR was performed using an SYBR Green PCR Master Mix kit on the Rotor Gene3000 real-time PCR system. The reaction system was 20 μL and conditions were as follows: predenaturation at 95°C for 30 min and PCR amplification for 45 cycles at 95°C for 15 s, 60°C for 10 min, and 95°C for 15 s. Primers of circANKRD42 were 5ʹ-GGAGAGACCCAGTGATGTGG-3ʹ and 5ʹ-TCATCCTGGGCTGTCAGATTTGCTC-3ʹ.

## Western blot
The cells were lysed with radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis solution. Total protein lysate samples containing 20 μg of protein were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes, and blocked with 5% skim milk in Tris-buffered saline and Tween 20 (TBST) containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.6), 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% Tween 20 for 1.5 h at room temperature. The membranes were washed thrice with TBST buffer, incubated at 4°C overnight with specific antibodies, washed again with TBST, and incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled immunoglobulin G (IgG) for 1.5 h at room temperature. The membranes were further washed with TBST, incubated with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent, exposed, and subsequently stripped. The item number and brand of the antibody were listed in .

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
The dual-luciferase detection kit was purchased from Guangzhou Ruibo Biological. The MRC-5 cells were planted in 96-well plates at a density of about 70% and transfected with luciferase reporter gene plasmid (Firefly: Renilla: transfection reagent = 200:4:250) and RNA. After 72 h of co-transfection, the old medium was discarded. Cell samples were washed with 100 μL 1× PBS, 100 μL 1× passive lysis buffer added, and the mixture shaken for 15 min at room temperature on a shaker for lysis. Five microliters supernatant and 50 μL of pre-mixed LAR II were added to a white opaque 96-well microtiter plate. After 2 s, the mixture was measured. Then, in each well was added 50 μL pre-mixed Stop&Glo Reagent and measured.

## RNA pull-down
circANKRD42 was transcribed using T7 and SP6 RNA polymerase in vitro and then by using the RNeasy Plus Mini Kit and treated with TURBO DNase I. Purified RNAs were biotin labeled using Biotin RNA Labeling Mix. The biotinylated circANKRD42 and its antisense RNAs were mixed and incubated with cell lysates. Then, avidin magnetic beads were added to each binding reaction, and the mixtures were incubated at room temperature. Finally, the beads were washed, and the retrieved proteins were resolved in gradient gel electrophoresis followed by silver staining.

## RNA-FISH assay
RNA-FISH probes, including circANKRD42, U6, and 18S FISH probes, were synthesized by Ribo Bio Technology (Guangzhou, China). MRC-5 cells were inoculated on a circular slide, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, washed with PBS, and punched with 0.5% Triton X-100. After incubation at 4°C for 3 min, pre-hybridization solution was dropped. Then, the denatured probe mix was added to the culture well and placed in hybridization oven at 37°C in the dark overnight. The next day, the hybridization probe solution was aspirated at 42°C and the slides were rinsed sequentially with 2× saline sodium citrate (SSC) and 1× SSC and washed three times for 5 min each. We added 150 μL DAPI solution, stained for 6 min at room temperature, and washed with PBS. Fluorescence was observed by a laser confocal microscope. The FISH probe sequence of circANKRD42 and miR-324-5p was hsa_circANKRD42 (CY3 labeled): 5ʹ-gaagacactctttgccacatcactgggt-3ʹ and hsa_miR-324-5p (fluorescein isothiocyanate [FITC] labeled): 5ʹ-accaatgccctaggggatgcg-3ʹ.

## RNA scope assay
The RNA scope probe targeting circANKRD42 was designed and synthesized by Advanced Cell Diagnostics, and detection of circANKRD42 was performed according to the steps of BaseScope Detection Reagent Kit v2-RED User Manual. The images were acquired with 3DHISTECH’s Slide Converter. The probe information was as follows (hsa_circANKRD42: a 1ZZ probe named BA-Hs-ANKRD42-circRNA-E8E3 targeting 1863-1108 of NM_182603.4).

## RIP assay
The RIP kit was purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Beijing, China). RIP lysis buffer and protease inhibitor cocktail were added to cell samples. The supernatant was collected by centrifugation. We mixed 200 μL supernatant and 800 μL lysis buffer as a RIP reaction system. AGO-specific antibody or corresponding IgG was added to the reaction system, respectively. After incubation at 4°C overnight, 20 μL Dynabeads Protein G was added. Beads were collected after incubation for 2 h at 4°C and washed five times. RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, reversed transcription by PrimeScript RT reagent Kit, and qPCR performed. The reaction conditions of qPCR were as follows: predenaturation at 95°C for 30 s and PCR amplification for 50 cycles at 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 34 s.

## Real-time cellular proliferation and migration analysis
We seeded 5 × 104/mL cells in E-plate for proliferation analysis and CIM plate for migration analysis, respectively. The CIM plate contains the upper chamber and the lower chamber. We added 30 μL serum-free medium into the upper chamber. We added 165 μL serum medium into the lower chamber. The parameters of RTCA DPlus instrument were set (ACEA Biosciences, Hangzhou, China). Then, cell samples were put in the instrument, which can automatically record a real-time cellular proliferation or migration curves. Cell index representing the amount of proliferation and migration cells was calculated with RTCA software from ACEA Biosciences (San Diego, CA).

## Wound healing test
We seeded 5 × 104 MRC-5 cells in each well of a 96-well plate. When the cells grew to above 95%, a dividing line was drawn by using Incucyte S3 scratcher (Sartorius, Michigan, USA). The scratched cell residues were washed away with PBS, and fresh medium was added. Then, the cell samples were put in the Incucyte S3 instrument at 37°C under a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. The videos were recorded automatically every 2 h by Incucyte S3 instrument. After 90 h, the images were exported and quantitatively processed according to the instrument software.

## Cytoskeleton staining
The cell samples were seeded on circular slides, placed in 24-well plates, and fixed with paraformaldehyde for 15 min. After washed with PBS, the cell samples were punched with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min. Then, 200 μL of 1% 488-phalloidin was added for 30 min. After washed with PBS, DAPI was added for 7 min. Finally, cytoskeleton was observed by using laser scanning confocal microscopy.

## Animal model and ethics statement
C57BL/6 mice with an average body weight of 20 ± 5 g were purchased from Jinan Pengyue Laboratory (Jinan, China) Experimental Animal Breeding Company. Animal experiments were carried out according to the guidance of the Animal Experiment Ethics Committee of Binzhou Medical University. The mice were divided into different groups (10 mice in each group) according to the experimental requirements. The BLM-treated mouse was administered 5 mg/kg BLM, which was sprayed into the lungs using a Penn-Century MicroSprayer (Penn-Century, Wyndmoor, PA, USA). The experimental mice were administered 1.0 × 1012 vg/mL adenoviral-circANKRD42, adenoviral-circANKRD42 mutation, or mimics of miR-136 and 324-5p, which were also sprayed into the lungs using a Penn-Century MicroSprayer. The sham group only received the same amount of normal saline as the control group. On the 28th day, all mice were sacrificed. The lung tissue was collected and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for further study.

## H&E and Masson staining
The lung tissues were fixed overnight with 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated with an automatic dehydrator, and embedded in paraffin. After sectioning, 4-μM sections were stained with H&E or a modified Masson trichrome staining kit. We randomly selected the field of view to obtain under the microscope.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v.19.0 software. Data are presented as the mean ± SD of at least three independent experiments. Unpaired Student’s t test was used for experiments comparing two groups, whereas one-way ANOVA with Student-Newman-Keuls post hoc test was applied for experiments comparing three or more groups. Statistical significance was considered at p < 0.05.
