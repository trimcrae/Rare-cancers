In China, the disease exhibits a similar epidemiological profile, with a 5-year survival rate of approximately 50% and a 10-year survival rate of only 25%, underscoring its poor prognosis . While the etiology of DCM remains heterogeneous and incompletely understood, approximately 30–50% of cases are linked to genetic mutations, with secondary contributors including viral infections, autoimmune responses, metabolic dysregulation, and environmental toxins. Emerging research has begun to unravel molecular mechanisms underlying DCM pathogenesis. A 2024 study identified the long non-coding RNA DCRT as a critical regulator of mitochondrial metabolism, demonstrating that its downregulation in both human DCM tissues and murine models exacerbates cardiac dilation and contractile dysfunction . Mechanistically, DCRT stabilizes the interaction between polypyrimidine tract-binding protein 1 (PTBP1) and NDUFS2 mRNA, preventing aberrant exon skipping and preserving mitochondrial complex I integrity, thereby mitigating oxidative stress and improving systolic performance. While this discovery offers a potential therapeutic avenue, the broader pathophysiological landscape of DCM remains fragmented , . For instance, the interplay between genetic predispositions, epigenetic modifications, and environmental triggers is poorly defined, and the role of immune-mediated myocardial injury in non-familial DCM cases requires further validation.
Programmed cell death (PCD), a genetically regulated process essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis, encompasses apoptosis, autophagy, and necroptosis, which eliminate damaged or dysfunctional cells through distinct molecular pathways. Recent studies have increasingly linked dysregulated PCD to the pathogenesis of DCM, a condition characterized by ventricular dilation and impaired systolic function. A 2023 study demonstrated that excessive cardiomyocyte apoptosis, driven by upregulated PD-1/PD-L1 signaling in cardiac tissue, exacerbates myocardial remodeling in DCM by suppressing T-cell-mediated immune surveillance and promoting inflammatory fibrosis – . Another 2024 investigation revealed that PDCD4 (programmed cell death protein 4) overexpression in DCM patients amplifies mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress, accelerating cardiomyocyte loss through the miR-21/PTEN/Akt pathway, as evidenced by both in vitro models and human biopsy analyses . Furthermore, a 2023 multi-omics study identified necroptosis activation via RIPK3-MLKL signaling as a critical contributor to DCM progression, with pharmacological inhibition of this pathway restoring cardiac function in preclinical models by reducing infarct size and inflammatory cytokine release .
While research on PCD in DCM has been conducted, the specific mechanisms remain unclear. Utilizing public transcriptome sequencing data, we conducted a systematic analysis of the differential expression patterns of 18 PCD types in DCM, identifying that ferroptosis plays a pivotal role in the onset of DCM. Through extensive screening of ferroptosis-related genes, we highlighted the significant role of SLC40A1 in DCM and demonstrated through basic experiments that it significantly influences glutathione (GSH) levels in cardiomyocytes. Further investigation revealed that the expression of SLC40A1 is regulated by the transcription factor IRF9. This study elucidates the mechanism of ferroptosis in DCM cardiomyocyte injury and its regulatory network, offering a novel research direction for further exploration of the pathological processes in DCM.

## Methods

## Source of data
Initially, our literature review identified 18 types of programmed cell death. These include Ferroptosis, Methuosis, Entosis, Entotic cell death, Parthanatos, Anoikis, Alkaliptosis, Autophagy, Paraptosis, Necroptosis, Apoptosis, NETosis, Cuproptosis, Pyroptosis, Netotic cell death, Lysosome-dependent cell death, Oxeiptosis, and Immunogenic cell death. Subsequently, we gathered relevant data from path databases and literature searches for further analysis. Data pertaining to dilated cardiomyopathy heart samples were extracted from the GEO database. Our search revealed two sequencing datasets for further study: GSE57338, which includes left ventricular samples from 82 DCM patients and 136 healthy controls, and GSE141910, which comprises left ventricular samples from 161 DCM patients and 161 healthy controls. The overall process of this study is shown in Fig.  .

 

## PCD score
In this study, raw expression matrices of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and healthy control samples were downloaded from the GEO database (GSE141910 and GSE57338). To account for technical variations across different sequencing platforms and batches, raw counts were converted to Transcripts Per Million (TPM) and subsequently log2-transformed. The sva R package (specifically the ComBat function) was employed to computationally remove known batch effects between datasets prior to integration. While comprehensive clinical metadata (such as medication history) was incompletely annotated in the original public repositories, we strictly matched the age and sex distributions between the DCM and healthy control cohorts to minimize potential confounding variables during differential analysis. Based on this normalized and batch-corrected foundation, we utilized the Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) method to quantitatively assess the enrichment levels of 18 programmed cell death (PCD)-related gene sets, thereby generating continuous activity scores for each sample.

## Potential gene functions analysis
To systematically examine the functional characteristics and potential regulatory mechanisms of differential cell death genes, this study employed a multi-dimensional bioinformatics analysis approach. Initially, the ClusterProfiler tool was utilized to conduct functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes using GO (Gene Ontology) and KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes). Biological processes with significant changes were identified through a statistical significance threshold of P < 0.05, encompassing biological processes, molecular functions, cellular components, and key metabolic and signal transduction pathways. Subsequently, an interaction network of proteins encoded by differential genes was constructed using the STRING database to thoroughly explore potential functional correlations among these genes. Based on this network, the degree analysis method was applied to identify key genes with the highest connectivity within the interactive network.

## Single cell analysis
The single-cell sequencing dataset was sourced from the GEO database under accession GSE145154, comprising 4 healthy myocardial tissue samples and 8 DCM myocardial tissue samples. Single-cell transcriptomic data underwent comprehensive processing utilizing the R-based Seurat analytical framework (v4.0). Stringent quality control procedures were applied: cells expressing fewer than 200 or more than 2500 detected genes, cells with > 20% mitochondrial gene expression, and cells with > 3% erythrocyte-associated gene expression were systematically excluded. Furthermore, potential doublets were identified and removed using the DoubletFinder R package with a standard expected doublet rate of 7.5%. After strict quality control, a total of 38,426 high-quality single cells were retained for downstream analysis.
To eliminate sample-specific and batch-associated technical artifacts across the 12 distinct specimens, data underwent systematic normalization via the NormalizeData function, followed by the identification of the top 2000 highly variable features using the FindVariableFeatures algorithm. We then applied the standard Seurat integration workflow using the FindIntegrationAnchors and IntegrateData functions. The integrated features underwent scaling transformation using ScaleData, followed by principal component analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction. Cellular clustering was accomplished through graph-based community detection employing the FindNeighbors function with k = 20 nearest neighbors, followed by cluster identification using FindClusters with a resolution parameter of 0.5. The clustering results were visualized using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE). Developmental trajectories were reconstructed utilizing the Monocle3 analytical framework, with cardiomyocyte cell populations designated as the root node for pseudotime calculation. Trajectory-associated genes displaying significant correlation patterns were identified through the graph_test function, applying stringent statistical criteria of q-value < 0.01 and Moran’s I statistic > 0.05. Intercellular communication networks based on ligand-receptor interactions were inferred and analyzed using the CellChat R package.

## In vivo dilated cardiomyopathy model
Male Sprague-Dawley rats (body weight 200–220 g, age 8–10 weeks) were purchased from Charles River Laboratories (Wilmington, MA, USA). Rats were housed under standard conditions (12-hour light/dark cycle, 22 ± 2 °C, 50 ± 10% humidity) with free access to food and water. Doxorubicin hydrochloride (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# D1515) dissolved in 0.9% sodium chloride was administered via intraperitoneal injection. Rats were randomly divided into control group (n = 3) receiving saline and doxorubicin-treated group (n = 3) receiving doxorubicin at 1 mg/kg twice weekly (Monday and Thursday) for 6 weeks (cumulative dose: 12 mg/kg). Body weight was recorded weekly. Transthoracic echocardiography was performed under isoflurane anesthesia (2–3%) at baseline, 3 weeks, and 6 weeks using a Vevo 2100 ultrasound system to measure left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD), ejection fraction (LVEF), and fractional shortening (FS). At 4–8 weeks after final doxorubicin injection, rats were euthanized by CO2 inhalation followed by cervical dislocation.

## Primary neonatal rat cardiomyocyte isolation and culture
Primary neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) were isolated from 1 to 3 day-old Sprague-Dawley rat pups (Charles River Laboratories) by enzymatic digestion. Neonatal hearts were excised, atrial tissue removed, and ventricular tissue minced into 1–2 mm3 pieces in ice-cold HBSS⁻ (Gibco, Cat# 14185-052). Tissue underwent 8–12 rounds of enzymatic digestion (15–20 min each, 37 °C) with collagenase II (250 U/mL; Worthington Biochemical, Cat# LS004176) and pancreatin (60 µg/mL; Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# P3292). Supernatant from each round was collected into 10% FBS-containing medium to inactivate enzymes. Cell suspensions were pooled, filtered through 100-µm mesh (Corning, Cat# 352360), centrifuged at 300×g for 5 min at 4 °C, and resuspended in plating medium (DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, 0.1 mM BrdU; Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# B5002) to inhibit fibroblasts. Viable cells (> 85%, trypan blue exclusion) were seeded at 1 × 106 cells per well in 6-well plates pre-coated with fibronectin (10 µg/mL; Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# F1141). After 24 h attachment at 37 °C/5% CO2, medium was replaced with differentiation medium (DMEM/F12 with 5% FBS, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, no BrdU). NRCMs were treated with doxorubicin (1–2 µmol/L) for 24–48 h with PBS as vehicle control.

## EdU incorporation assay
To assess the effect of SLC40A1 overexpression on cardiomyocyte proliferative activity in the dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) model, an EdU incorporation assay was performed using a commercial EdU Cell Proliferation Kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cardiomyocytes were seeded onto coverslips in 24-well plates and transfected with the SLC40A1 overexpression plasmid or corresponding control vector. After establishment of the DCM model, cells were incubated with 10 µM EdU for 2 h at 37 °C. The cells were then washed with PBS, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min at room temperature. After reaction with the fluorescent azide working solution, nuclei were stained with DAPI. Images were captured under a fluorescence microscope, and the proportion of EdU-positive cells was quantified by counting EdU-positive nuclei relative to total DAPI-positive nuclei in at least five randomly selected microscopic fields. Each experiment was repeated independently at least three times.

## TUNEL assay
Apoptosis of cardiomyocytes was assessed utilizing a terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay kit in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cultured cardiomyocytes were grown on glass coverslips in 24-well plates, subjected to the designated experimental conditions (DCM modeling with or without SLC40A1 overexpression), and subsequently washed with PBS. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature and then permeabilized using 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 min on ice. After rinsing, the cells were incubated with the TUNEL reaction mixture, containing terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase and fluorescein-dUTP, for 1 h at 37 °C in a humidified dark chamber. Following the TUNEL reaction, the nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole) for 5 min. The coverslips were mounted on glass slides, and images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope. The apoptotic rate was determined by calculating the ratio of TUNEL-positive nuclei (green fluorescence) to the total number of DAPI-stained nuclei (blue fluorescence) across at least five randomly selected microscopic fields per sample. All experiments were conducted in triplicate.

## AAV-mediated in vivo and in vitro gene overexpression
To achieve highly efficient and stable overexpression of Slc40a1 in both primary neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) and the rat myocardium, recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) vectors were utilized, given their robust and specific tropism for cardiac tissue. The AAV9 vector carrying the full-length rat Slc40a1 coding sequence (AAV9-SLC40A1) driven by a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, alongside the corresponding negative control empty vector (AAV9-NC), were designed, constructed, and packaged by Hanbio Biotechnology (Shanghai, China).
For the in vitro cellular model, cultured NRCMs were infected with AAV9-SLC40A1 or AAV9-NC at an optimal multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 1 × 105 viral genomes per cell. Following 24 h of viral infection, the culture medium was refreshed, and the cells were incubated for an additional 48 h to ensure optimal target gene expression prior to doxorubicin treatment.
For the in vivo rat DCM model, adeno-associated viruses were delivered systemically. Rats randomly assigned to the overexpression groups received a single injection of AAV9-SLC40A1 or AAV9-NC via the tail vein at a precise dose of 1 × 1011 viral genomes (vg) per rat, diluted in 200 µL of sterile PBS. To allow for stable, widespread, and steady-state myocardial expression of the transgene, the doxorubicin-induced modeling was initiated 3 weeks post-AAV injection. The overexpression efficiency in both cultured NRCMs and excised rat myocardial tissues was stringently verified at the mRNA and protein levels via qRT-PCR and Western blotting, respectively.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
To validate the transcriptional regulation of SLC40A1 by IRF9, a dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed in HEK293T cells. The putative promoter region of human SLC40A1 (− 2000 to + 50 bp relative to the transcription start site) was PCR-amplified and cloned into the pGL3-Basic vector (Promega) upstream of the firefly luciferase gene to create the wild-type reporter plasmid (pGL3-SLC40A1-WT). Site-directed mutagenesis was applied to alter the predicted IRF9-binding motifs (GAAA sequences), resulting in the mutant construct (pGL3-SLC40A1-Mut). HEK293T cells (ATCC) were cultured in 24-well plates and co-transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 500 ng of either the wild-type or mutant reporter plasmid, 50 ng of the pRL-TK Renilla luciferase control vector (Promega), and varying concentrations (0–1.5 µg) of a pcDNA3.1-IRF9 overexpression plasmid or IRF9-specific siRNA (Santa Cruz Biotechnology). After 48 h of transfection, cell lysates were harvested, and luciferase activities were measured utilizing the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega) on a GloMax Navigator microplate luminometer. The firefly luciferase activity was normalized to the Renilla luciferase activity to account for variations in transfection efficiency. Three independent experiments were conducted, each with quadruplicate technical replicates.

## Measurement of malondialdehyde (MDA) levels
To evaluate the extent of intracellular and tissue lipid peroxidation, malondialdehyde (MDA) levels were quantified using a commercial Lipid Peroxidation (MDA) Assay Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat# MAK085) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cultured cardiomyocytes or homogenized myocardial tissues were lysed on ice using the provided lysis buffer, followed by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove insoluble debris. The supernatant was collected, and the protein concentration was determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for subsequent data normalization. The sample extract was then reacted with thiobarbituric acid (TBA) at 95 °C for 60 min to form an MDA-TBA adduct. After cooling on ice, the absorbance of the colorimetric product was measured at 532 nm using a microplate reader. All samples were analyzed in triplicate, and the relative MDA concentrations were calculated and normalized to the total protein content (expressed as nmol/mg protein).

## Measurement of glutathione (GSH) levels
The intracellular redox status was assessed by measuring the levels of glutathione (GSH), a key endogenous antioxidant, using a commercial Glutathione Colorimetric Assay Kit (BioVision, Cat# K261). Following the specified experimental treatments, cells or tissue samples were homogenized in cold GSH assay buffer and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatant was collected and deproteinized using 5% sulfosalicylic acid to prevent enzymatic degradation of GSH. After neutralizing the samples, the extracts were incubated with the reaction mixture containing 5,5′-dithiobis (2-nitrobenzoic acid) (DTNB), which reacts with GSH to generate a yellow-colored product (TNB). The optical density was monitored at a wavelength of 412 nm utilizing a microplate reader. Assays were performed with three independent technical replicates per biological sample.

## Experimental design and statistical analysis
All in vitro cellular assays (e.g., EdU, TUNEL, qRT-PCR, Western blot) were performed with at least three independent biological replicates (n ≥ 3), and each biological replicate included three technical replicates to ensure reproducibility. For in vivo animal studies, rats were randomly assigned to experimental groups (n = 3 per group) using a computer-generated randomization sequence. To minimize observation bias, data collection and analysis for key functional outcomes (such as echocardiographic measurements and histological quantification) were conducted by independent investigators who were strictly blinded to the experimental group assignments. All quantitative data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance between two groups was evaluated using the two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test, while multi-group comparisons were assessed using One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test, utilizing GraphPad Prism software. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Result

## Ferroptosis-related pathways are significantly dysregulated in DCM
GSE141910 comprises datasets of left ventricular samples from 161 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and 161 healthy controls. Utilizing GSE141910, we conducted a differential expression analysis to investigate the primary mechanisms influencing the onset and progression of DCM. Our analysis identified 1348 genes with significantly increased expression and 1852 genes with significantly decreased expression (Fig.  A). Subsequent KEGG functional enrichment analysis of these differentially expressed genes revealed striking associations with signaling pathways related to programmed cell death, including the P53 signaling pathway, apoptosis, and notably, ferroptosis (Fig.  B, p.adjust < 0.05). This pronounced enrichment strongly implies that the perturbation of programmed cell death (PCD) networks is a critical feature of DCM pathology.

 

To further delineate which specific modes of cell death are predominantly altered, we employed Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) to evaluate 18 distinct programmed death signatures within the GSE141910 cohort. A comprehensive differential analysis of these PCD pathways indicated that the enrichment patterns of Ferroptosis, Methuosis, and Entosis were significantly altered in DCM compared to healthy controls, exhibiting distinct change shifts in their pathway activity scores (Fig.  C). To robustly validate these bioinformatic findings, we performed a parallel external verification using an independent dataset, GSE57338. The verification analysis corroborated our initial results, demonstrating that the pathway signatures of Ferroptosis, Methuosis, and Alkaliptosis were indeed significantly differentially enriched between the DCM and control groups (Fig.  D). Considering the consistent and prominent perturbation of the ferroptosis signature across multiple cohorts, we postulate that the dysregulation of ferroptosis-related signaling and iron metabolism plays a pivotal role in DCM.

## SLC40A1 is a key ferroptosis factor in DCM
To identify the ferroptosis related genes involved in DCM, we conducted a cross-analysis of the differential ferroptosis related genes in two datasets. Our analysis revealed that six genes were downregulated in DCM, while one gene was upregulated (Fig.  A). In the protein interaction analysis of all ferroptosis genes, we discovered that SLC40A1, a gene with increased expression in DCM, occupies a crucial regulatory position within the interaction network. Consequently, we hypothesize that SLC40A1 may influence ferroptosis in DCM cardiomyocytes (Fig.  B). To verify the elevated expression of the SLC40A1 gene in DCM, we established a DCM model using rat cardiomyocytes. The expression levels of SLC40A1 were assessed in this cell model, revealing that both mRNA and protein levels of SLC40A1 were indeed elevated in DCM (Fig.  C and D).

 

## DCM single-cell analysis
To elucidate the functional role of SLC40A1 in DCM cellular networks, we conducted single-cell RNA-sequencing analysis using the Seurat framework on the GEO145154 dataset, encompassing 4 healthy cardiac tissue specimens and 8 DCM cardiac tissue samples. Following stringent quality control procedures, we employed established marker genes to perform dimensionality reduction and clustering, successfully identifying seven distinct cellular populations: T lymphocytes (CD3D), macrophages (CD68), B lymphocytes (MS4A1), endothelial cells (PECAM1), smooth muscle cells (ACTA2), cardiomyocytes (TNNT2), and fibroblasts (COL1A1) (Fig.  A–B). Notably, expression profiling revealed elevated SLC40A1 levels across macrophages, smooth muscle cells, and cardiomyocytes (Fig.  C). To investigate the functional implications of differential SLC40A1 expression, we stratified cardiomyocytes into high- and low-expression cohorts using the median expression threshold within this cell population. We subsequently examined the intercellular communication patterns between these cardiomyocyte subsets and neighboring cell types. Our analysis revealed enhanced ligand–receptor interactions between SLC40A1-high cardiomyocytes and macrophages, fibroblasts, as well as endothelial cells, with particularly robust communication strength observed within the TWEAK signaling cascade (Fig.  D). Furthermore, we reconstructed cardiomyocyte developmental trajectories using Monocle3, establishing all cardiomyocyte clusters as the trajectory root and applying graph_test with q < 0.01 to identify trajectory-associated genes. Pseudotime analysis demonstrated a progressive increase in SLC40A1 expression along the developmental continuum, suggesting its functional importance during later stages of cardiomyocyte maturation or stress response mechanisms (Fig.  E).

 

## SLC40A1 mitigates pathological cardiac remodeling and dysfunction via ferroptosis-suppressing mechanisms
To investigate the functional role of SLC40A1 in dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), we established an in vitro DCM cell model and induced the overexpression of SLC40A1. First, we evaluated cardiomyocyte proliferation and cell death. The EdU incorporation assay (Fig.  A) demonstrated that SLC40A1 overexpression significantly promoted DNA synthesis and enhanced cell proliferation compared to the DCM model group (p < 0.001). Additionally, the TUNEL staining assay (Fig.  B) revealed that the elevated apoptosis rate induced by the DCM condition was significantly ameliorated in the SLC40A1 overexpression group (p < 0.0001), indicating a strong cardioprotective effect.

 

Given that SLC40A1 is a critical iron exporter and potentially regulates ferroptosis, we further investigated its impact on intracellular redox homeostasis and lipid peroxidation. Western blot analysis showed that SLC40A1 overexpression upregulated the protein expression of GPX4 (Fig.  C), a central endogenous inhibitor of ferroptosis. Consistent with this, the accumulation of malondialdehyde (MDA), a major marker of lipid peroxidation, was significantly attenuated upon SLC40A1 overexpression (Fig.  D, p < 0.01). Furthermore, SLC40A1 overexpression markedly restored the depleted intracellular glutathione (GSH) levels in DCM cells (Fig.  E, p < 0.0001). Collectively, these findings strongly suggest that SLC40A1 exerts its protective role in DCM by enhancing the GSH/GPX4 antioxidant axis, reducing lipid peroxidation, and ultimately suppressing ferroptosis.
To further validate the cardioprotective effects of SLC40A1 in vivo, we established a rat model of DCM and performed adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated overexpression of SLC40A1 in the myocardium. Macroscopic examination and morphological analysis of the excised hearts revealed that the DOX-induced DCM model group exhibited typical morphological characteristics of dilated cardiomyopathy. However, AAV-mediated overexpression of SLC40A1 remarkably attenuated these macroscopic pathological alterations, preserving ventricular wall thickness, restricting excessive chamber dilation, and restoring the overall structural integrity of the myocardium (Fig.  A). Successful gene delivery was confirmed by Western blot, which demonstrated that SLC40A1 protein expression in the myocardial tissue was significantly elevated in the DCM+SLC40A1-OE group (Fig.  B, p < 0.05).

 

Consistent with our in vitro findings, we evaluated ferroptosis-related markers in the cardiac tissues. The expression of the ferroptosis-inhibiting protein GPX4 was significantly downregulated in the DCM group but was partially restored following SLC40A1 overexpression (Fig.  C, p < 0.05). Furthermore, the elevated myocardial MDA levels observed in the DCM rats were significantly decreased (Fig.  D, p < 0.01), while the depleted GSH levels were markedly replenished in the SLC40A1-OE group (Fig.  E, p < 0.01), confirming that SLC40A1 also effectively inhibits lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis in vivo.
Finally, we assessed the therapeutic impact of SLC40A1 on global cardiac function using echocardiography. The DCM rats exhibited severe left ventricular systolic dysfunction, as evidenced by profoundly reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and fractional shortening (LVFS), along with extensive ventricular dilation indicated by increased left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) and end-systolic diameter (LVESD). Strikingly, SLC40A1 overexpression significantly improved contractile function, restoring both LVEF (Fig.  F, p < 0.01) and LVFS (Fig.  G, p < 0.01). Concomitantly, it mitigated structural remodeling, leading to a substantial decrease in both LVEDD (Fig.  H, p < 0.05) and LVESD (Fig.  I, p < 0.01). Taken together, these in vivo data compellingly demonstrate that SLC40A1 overexpression alleviates pathological cardiac remodeling and preserves systolic function in DCM, at least in part, by suppressing myocardial ferroptosis.

## SLC40A1 expression is regulated by IRF9
To investigate the aberrant expression mechanism of SLC40A1 in dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), we initially identified transcription factors regulating SLC40A1 using the Remap database, identifying 15 potential candidates. Differential expression analysis of these factors revealed that 2 genes were highly expressed and 3 were under-expressed in DCM samples (Fig.  A). Given the typical positive nature of transcriptional regulation, we focused on highly expressed IRF9 and STAT6 for further study. Subsequent co-expression analysis demonstrated a significant relationship between SLC40A1 and IRF9 (Fig.  B). JASPAR database predictions confirmed a binding site for IRF9 in the SLC40A1 promoter region (Fig.  C). To validate IRF9’s regulatory effect on SLC40A1, a dual luciferase reporter assay was conducted, showing that IRF9 significantly enhances SLC40A1’s fluorescence activity (Fig.  D). Additionally, functional experiments indicated that IRF9 overexpression impacts GSH levels, an effect partially reversible upon SLC40A1 knockdown (Fig.  E). IRF9 upregulated the expression of SLC40A1 by binding to the promoter region of SLC40A1, further increased the level of GSH, and ultimately inhibited ferroptosis in DCM cells (Fig.  F).

 

## Discussion
This study systematically elucidates the critical involvement of programmed cell death (PCD) networks in the pathogenesis of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). Through comprehensive bioinformatic analysis, single-cell transcriptomics, and experimental validation, we identified ferroptosis as a predominantly altered cell death mode in DCM. Crucially, we discovered that the iron exporter SLC40A1 is significantly upregulated in DCM and acts as a potent endogenous suppressor of ferroptosis. Mechanistically, we demonstrated for the first time that the transcription factor IRF9 directly regulates SLC40A1 expression. Enhancing this IRF9-SLC40A1 axis effectively restored glutathione (GSH) levels, mitigated lipid peroxidation, and ultimately preserved cardiac function in vivo and in vitro.
Our analysis identified three prominently altered forms of programmed cell death (PCD) in DCM: ferroptosis, methuosis, and alkaliptosis. Among these, ferroptosis—an iron-dependent process driven by lipid peroxidation and GPX4 inactivation—has recently garnered significant attention in cardiovascular pathology. Previous studies have demonstrated that ferroptosis-related genes, such as MAPK3, are involved in ischemia-reperfusion injury mechanisms that may overlap with DCM pathophysiology . Additionally, FSP1, a crucial ferroptosis suppressor, modulates oxidative stress, though its precise cardiac-specific functions and direct links to DCM remain under active investigation. In contrast, methuosis (characterized by excessive macropinosome vacuolization) and alkaliptosis (a pH-sensitive pathway driven by NF-κB signaling) have been primarily studied in cancer models. To date, there is no direct evidence elucidating their roles or potential therapeutic applications in cardiomyocytes. Given the robust relevance of iron metabolism to cardiac injury, our study strategically focused on the ferroptosis regulatory network in DCM progression.
Given the prominent role of ferroptosis in DCM, our differential expression analysis identified seven key ferroptosis-related genes, with six being downregulated. Among these, LPCAT3 and STEAP3 exhibited the most significant impact on the regulatory network. LPCAT3 is a critical enzyme that enriches cellular membranes with peroxidation-prone polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), a central prerequisite for ferroptosis execution – . Concurrently, STEAP3 acts as a metalloreductase that regulates cellular iron uptake and intracellular labile iron pools, which catalyze lipid peroxidation , . While recent studies—such as those utilizing cardiac-specific Fth knockout mice—have implicated iron dysregulation in cardiomyopathy, direct evidence linking LPCAT3 or STEAP3 specifically to DCM pathogenesis remains limited. Although these downregulated targets warrant future investigation regarding their specific contributions to myocardial lipid remodeling and iron homeostasis, our current functional validation primarily focused on the single prominently upregulated target, SLC40A1.
As the central focus of our study, SLC40A1 (solute carrier family 40 member 1), which encodes the highly conserved transmembrane protein ferroportin (FPN1), is currently the only known cellular iron exporter in mammals , . Its fundamental physiological role is to facilitate the efflux of intracellular ferrous iron (Fe2+) into the circulation, thereby tightly regulating the intracellular labile iron pool (LIP) and systemic iron homeostasis. Given that excessive intracellular iron acts as a potent catalyst for the Fenton reaction and ensuing lipid peroxidation, SLC40A1 has emerged as a crucial negative regulator of ferroptosis – . Previous studies across various pathological models have demonstrated that the downregulation or impairment of SLC40A1 severely compromises cellular iron output, leading to severe iron overload and dramatically accelerating cellular ferroptosis. For instance, inhibiting SLC40A1 in renal tubular epithelial cells exacerbates diabetic nephropathy by triggering massive lipid peroxidation, whereas its preservation or pharmacological induction effectively impedes erastin-induced ferroptosis , . In the specific context of cardiovascular diseases, the regulation of SLC40A1 presents a highly complex and context-dependent paradigm. Recent literature indicates that during acute ischemic stress, such as in the initial phase of myocardial infarction, aberrant overexpression of SLC40A1 can paradoxically exacerbate cardiomyocyte apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction by causing severe intracellular iron deficiency . Conversely, in conditions characterized by chronic oxidative stress and intracellular iron accumulation—such as chronic intermittent hypoxia-induced cardiac dysfunction—cardiomyocyte-specific overexpression of SLC40A1 has been proven to significantly diminish cardiac iron accumulation, attenuate reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, and protect against mitochondrial damage .
Building upon these complex regulatory mechanisms, our study provides unprecedented insights into the specific role of SLC40A1 in dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). Through integrated single-cell transcriptomics and experimental validation, we discovered that SLC40A1 is significantly upregulated in surviving DCM cardiomyocytes. Rather than mediating iron deficiency, our functional assays confirmed that this adaptive upregulation serves as a potent, compensatory cardioprotective mechanism designed to expel lethal iron excess. Overexpressing SLC40A1 markedly restored depleted intracellular glutathione (GSH) levels, upregulated GPX4 expression, and attenuated MDA accumulation, collectively suppressing ferroptosis. Most importantly, we advanced the current paradigm by identifying the transcription factor IRF9 as the direct upstream activator of SLC40A1. This finding reveals a novel IRF9-SLC40A1 signaling axis that dynamically responds to pathological stress, offering a promising therapeutic target for mitigating ferroptosis-driven cardiac remodeling and preserving systolic function in the failing heart.
Crucially, our findings advance current understanding by proposing a targeted, cardiac-intrinsic defense mechanism rather than relying on systemic iron depletion. While traditional strategies for ferroptosis-related cardiac injury use systemic iron chelators that often cause off-target anemia, we demonstrated that upregulating the specific cellular iron exporter SLC40A1 restricts the intracellular labile iron pool locally and safely. From a translational perspective, this paradigm shift highlights the IRF9/SLC40A1 axis as an ideal therapeutic target. Developing IRF9 agonists or utilizing AAV-mediated SLC40A1 gene therapy could offer highly precise interventions for DCM, effectively halting ferroptosis-driven cardiac remodeling while bypassing the hematological side effects associated with conventional anti-ferroptotic drugs.
Several limitations of this study must be acknowledged. Firstly, while reporter assays suggest IRF9 transcriptionally activates SLC40A1, direct evidence of their physical interaction at the chromatin level is lacking; future studies utilizing ChIP-qPCR are necessary to confirm IRF9 binding to the endogenous SLC40A1 promoter. Secondly, our mechanistic validations relied on doxorubicin-induced models, which generate profound systemic oxidative stress. This may elicit off-target effects that do not fully capture the progressive pathophysiology of human spontaneous or genetic DCM. Thirdly, ferroptosis regulation in human cardiac tissue involves complex spatiotemporal dynamics and multi-cellular microenvironments that cannot be perfectly replicated in vitro. Further investigations using genetic DCM murine models and human clinical samples are warranted to fully elucidate the complex interplay of the IRF9/SLC40A1 axis and other cell death pathways, such as methuosis and alkaliptosis, in the failing heart.
In conclusion, our work identifies SLC40A1 as a key ferroptosis regulator that exerts a cardioprotective role in DCM, and further establishes IRF9 as its upstream transcriptional activator. These findings provide a new perspective for understanding PCD remodeling in DCM and suggest the IRF9/SLC40A1 axis as a promising therapeutic target.
