## Abstract

## Introduction
Atopic dermatitis (AD), allergic rhinitis (AR), and bronchial asthma (BA) are major allergic diseases in childhood. Pediatric allergic diseases are characterized by the “atopic march,” where two or more allergic conditions can occur either simultaneously or sequentially. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) serve as fine regulators of gene expression, capable of modulating the clinical manifestations of allergic diseases posttranscriptionally. We investigated miRNAs commonly expressed in these three allergic diseases to enhance the understanding of their development and management.

## Methods
We collected serum samples from subjects diagnosed with AD, AR, and BA as well as from healthy controls at Korea University Anam Hospital. Their miRNA expression patterns were analyzed using microarray technology. Additionally, we examined the allergic inflammatory response of miRNA through an allergic animal model.

## Results
A total of 68 subjects were enrolled in the allergy group, consisting of 42 with AD, 13 with AR, and 13 with BA, while 10 children participated as controls. Microarray analysis revealed that miR-4497 expression levels were consistently downregulated in these three allergic disease groups. Following mast cell activation and miR-4497 transfection, reduced levels of macrophage-derived chemokines (MDCs) were observed. Furthermore, levels of IL-4, MDC, and methacholine Penh were significantly decreased in a miR-4497-treated mouse model.

## Conclusions
MiR-4497 expressions were consistently downregulated in pediatric subjects with AD, AR, and BA. Allergic inflammation was significantly reduced in human mast cell-1 transfected with miR-4497 and in the treated mouse model. Further research is needed to elucidate the epigenetic mechanisms by which miR-4497 modulates allergic diseases and to explore its potential as a noninvasive biomarker for diagnosis and treatment.

Keywords: MicroRNA, Allergic disease, Microarray, MiR-4497, Macrophage-derived chemokine, Atopic dermatitis, Allergic rhinitis, Bronchial asthma

## Introduction
Atopic dermatitis (AD), allergic rhinitis (AR), and bronchial asthma (BA) are major allergic diseases in childhood. Pediatric allergic diseases are characterized by the ‘atopic march,’ where two or more allergic conditions can occur either simultaneously or sequentially, emphasizing the need for more individualized management [ ]. Allergic inflammation results from complex interactions of genetic and environmental factors, involving various signal transmitters and inflammatory substances [ – ]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs), a class of small noncoding RNAs, act as fine regulators of gene expression, capable of modulating the clinical manifestations of allergic diseases posttranscriptionally. Although various miRNAs that are upregulated or downregulated are known to be involved in each allergic disease [ ], the miRNAs consistently expressed in these three allergic diseases remains unclear.
It is essential to identify the miRNAs that are commonly overexpressed or downregulated in allergic diseases and to understand their roles in allergic inflammatory responses. This knowledge could facilitate their potential use as diagnostic markers or target molecules for new biological treatment approaches [ – ]. Additionally, these commonly expressed miRNAs in childhood allergic diseases could play a significant role in preventing the allergic progression, such as “atopic march” in children.
Previous studies have suggested that miRNAs influence Th2 polarization, Treg cell suppression capacity, and immunoglobulin class switching in B cells in relation to allergic diseases [ ]. However, the exact mechanisms of allergic inflammatory response involving miRNA are not yet well understood. The aim of this study was to investigate the expression patterns of miRNAs in pediatric allergic patients and to explore how miRNAs influence allergic inflammatory responses through in vitro and in vivo experiments.

## Materials and Methods

## Study Participants
We enrolled subjects with allergic diseases, such as AD, AR, or BA, who visited the Department of Pediatrics at Korea University Anam Hospital for this study. AD was diagnosed based on the criteria by Hanifin and Rajka and is characterized by chronic or recurrent itching and typical eczematous dermatitis [ ]. AR was defined as having current symptoms of rhinitis, such as sneezing, rhinorrhea, nasal stuffiness, or itching, unrelated to a common cold during the previous year [ ]. BA was diagnosed based on typical asthma symptoms (wheezing, dyspnea, and chronic cough) occurring within the past 12 months, with variable expiratory airflow limitation confirmed by either a positive bronchodilator response or bronchial hyperresponsiveness [ ]. Subjects exhibiting two or more allergic diseases simultaneously were excluded from the study group. The control group consisted of non-atopic healthy children without a history of allergic disease who visited the pediatric outpatient clinic for a general checkup. Additionally, subjects with chronic diseases, infectious diseases, or conditions that may affect the immune responses were excluded from the study.

## RNA Extraction and Microarray Analysis
Blood samples were collected from the subjects, and the miRNA expression profiles in the serum samples of both the study and control groups were measured using the Affymetrix microarray analysis.

## Functional Analysis of Target MiRNA Using Human Mast Cell-1
To confirm the immune modulatory response of the target miRNA, we conducted a cell experiment. On the first day of the experiment, human mast cell (HMC)-1 were activated using phorbol myristate acetate (50 ng/mL) and ionomycin (1 µg/mL). On the fourth day, miRNA (100 pmol/2 mL) was transfected into the activated HMC-1 cells. In detail, total RNA, including miRNA, was extracted from transfected HMC-1 cells using miRNeasy Mini Kit (QIAGEN®, Seoul, Korea) and quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific®, Seoul, Korea). Reverse transcription was performed with 3 µL of RNA using the TaqMan™ MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (cat. No. 4366596, Thermo Fisher Scientific®, Seoul, Korea), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Quantitative PCR was conducted in duplicate using TaqMan™ Fast Advanced Master Mix (cat. No. 4444557, Thermo Fisher Scientific®, Seoul, Korea) and a TaqMan™ probe specific for miR-4497 (cat. No. 4427975, Life Technologies®, Seoul, Korea) on a qPCR system (e.g., Applied Biosystems 7500). Relative miR-4497 levels were normalized to U6 snRNA using the ΔΔCt method. On the sixth day, the changes in cytokine secretion pattern were compared with those of the activated control.

## Measurement of Cytokines and Airway Resistance in an Allergy Animal Model
After 1 week of acclimation, 5-week-old female BALB/c mice were intraperitoneally sensitized with 50 μg of ovalbumin (OVA) twice at 2-week intervals. On days 21, 22, and 23 following the start of the sensitization, the mice were administrated 100 μg of OVA intranasally. For miR-4497 treatment, control oligonucleotide and miR-4497 were dissolved in endotoxin-free water. One nanomole of oligos from both the control group and the miR-4497 group was intraperitoneally injected into the mice on the 24th day. To evaluate cytokine levels, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected from the mouse lungs twice with saline. The collected BALFs were analyzed for IL-4, IL-2, IFN-γ, IL-12, and macrophage-derived chemokine (MDC) using the RayBio ELISA kit for mouse, following the manufacturer’s protocols. The enhanced pause (Penh) values were measured on the 24th day after sensitization. For this, aerosolized methacholine was nebulized in increasing concentrations (0, 6.25, 12.5, 25, and 50 mg/mL) through an inlet of the main chamber for 3 min. Airway responsiveness was subsequently monitored for 3 min using OCP-3000 (All Medicus, Gyeonggi-do, Korea). Penh values were recorded and averaged for 3 min after each nebulization.

## Statistical Analysis
Statistical analysis, including summarization, background correction, and normalization, was conducted using Affymetrix Power Tools software. The p value, which indicates the presence or absence of expression in each probe set, was calculated using the detected above background method; a detected above background p value <0.05 was considered significant. An independent Student’s t test was utilized to compare the mean miRNA expression levels, while the Benjamini-Hochberg method was applied to adjust for the false discovery rate (FDR). Continuous variables between the two groups were compared using either the independent Student’s t test or the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using χ2 or Kruskal-Wallis tests. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were carried out using R software version 3.3.3.

## Functional Analysis of MiR-4497
MiR-4497, which exhibited a significant difference between the allergy and control groups in the microarray analysis, has not been previously reported to be associated with allergic diseases. Therefore, a cell experiment was conducted to investigate the role of miR-4497 in allergic inflammation. An inflammation model utilizing HMC-1 was established, and miR-4497 was transfected to compare cytokine secretion patterns ( a). We measured miR-4497 expression levels using qRT-PCR following transfection. The results confirmed a significant upregulation of miR-4497 in the treated cells compared to the control group, as presented in online supplementary Figure S1. After mast cell activation and interaction with miR-4497, a significant difference in MDC was observed between the negative control, positive control, miR-control, and the miR-4497-treated group (3.71 ± 1.0 pg/mL, 105.57 ± 3.1 pg/mL, 100.19 ± 2.5 pg/mL, and 82.38 ± 2.1 pg/mL, respectively, b).
 

## Role of MiR-4497 in an Asthma Mouse Model
We established an asthma model to assess the roles of miR-4497. Following the administration of miR-4497 in a mouse model and subsequent methacholine inhalation, the OVA + miR-4497 model showed a significant decrease in Penh levels compared to the asthma model (OVA) and the miR-control model (p = 0.0081, ). Furthermore, an analysis of cytokines in the BALF of each mouse revealed a trend toward reduced levels of IL-4 (2.51 pg/mL vs. 6.43 pg/mL, adj p = 6.082E−07), MDC (9.55 pg/mL vs. 14.74 pg/mL, adj p = 7.693E−05), and IFN-γ (4.36 pg/mL vs. 9.50 pg/mL, adj p = 8.524E−08) in the miR-4497-treated group compared to the miR-control group, although IL-2 (2.71 pg/mL vs. 3.25 pg/mL) and IL-12 levels (616.64 pg/mL vs. 583.33 pg/mL) remained unchanged. ## Discussion
Although allergic diseases are common in childhood, many aspects of their pathogenesis remain incompletely understood. Recently, alongside the genetic and environmental factors contributing to the development of these diseases, there has been increasing attention on the role of miRNAs, which regulate gene expression posttranscriptionally [ , ]. MiRNAs involved in various pro-inflammatory mechanisms related to allergic diseases, influencing the innate immune response in the keratinocytes of AD, showing differential expression in AR, and promoting smooth muscle cell proliferation in BA. Furthermore, miRNAs participate in regulating Th1 and Th2 polarization in cells such as basophils, eosinophils, T/B cells, and mast cells [ – ]. Some miRNAs are either upregulated or downregulated to initiate and sustain allergic inflammatory responses [ ]. The Th1/Th2 imbalance is crucial in the development of allergic disease, with miRNAs playing a significant role in the regulation of allergic immune responses [ , ].
In the present study, miR-4497 exhibited a consistent pattern of downregulation in the AD, AR, and BA groups. Although miR-4497 has not been previously linked to allergic diseases, there have been some reports regarding its role in the suppression or activation of gene expression. For example, it has been shown to be involved in NF-κB signaling and to modulate keratinocyte injury [ ]. Additionally, one study indicated that miR-4497 may enhance the secretion of TNF-α under both basal and lipopolysaccharide-induced conditions [ ]. Furthermore, its levels were significantly elevated in the breast milk of individuals with type 1 diabetes compared to the control group, suggesting a potential impact on the immune system of newborns [ ].
In the present study, we identified 26 genes in miRTarBase and 38 genes in CyTargetLinker as target genes of miR-4497 (online suppl. Table S4). Among these target genes, cyclin F (CCNF), vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A), bone morphogenetic protein 8A (BMP8A), and Src homology 2 (SH2) domain-containing adaptor protein B1 (SH2B1) were potentially associated with allergic diseases. CCNF is a critical regulator of the cell cycle, linked to DNA damage, class 1 MHC-mediated antigen processing and presentation, and the innate immune system [ , ]. Increased airway smooth muscle hyperplasia and collagen deposition were found in asthmatic mice overexpressing VEGF-A, and VEGF-A overexpression enhanced IL-4 and IL-13 production as well as eosinophilic inflammation in asthmatic mice [ ]. BMP8A is a member of the transforming growth factor-β superfamily [ ]. Significantly downregulated expression levels of the SH2B1 gene were associated with the CD4+/CD8+ ratio of T lymphocytes in canine AD [ ].
We assessed the expression levels of miRNAs previously linked to allergic diseases such as let-7, miR-15a, miR-7, miR-19a, miR-21, miR-24, miR-27, miR-124, miR-125b, miR-126, miR-133b, miR-142, miR-143, miR-145, miR-146, miR-149, miR-155, miR-181, miR-221, miR-323, miR-151a, miR-203, miR-223, miR-365, or miR-375 [ – ]. However, we did not observe significant differences in the expression levels of these known miRNAs associated with allergic diseases. It is important to note that miRNAs can exhibit diverse expression patterns within the same disease, influenced by factors such as disease severity, treatment status, and progression [ , ]. While some miRNAs (such as let-7b-5p, miR-24-3p, and miR-485-3p) showed notable differences in expression levels compared to the control group, the majority of miRNAs previously associated with allergic diseases did not demonstrate significant variations in their expression levels in the present study.
Allergic diseases are recognized as heterogeneous conditions influenced by the complex interactions of cellular and molecular mechanisms [ , ]. Recently, these diseases have been categorized based on their pathomechanisms, emphasizing the importance of personalized treatment strategies tailored to specific endotypes [ ] and biological modification of immune responses for improved and individualized control is crucial. MiRNAs, which are relatively stable and easily obtained from various body fluids such as serum, urine, and saliva [ ], hold promise as biomarkers for more refined classification of allergic diseases and the implementation of effective treatment approaches. As previously mentioned, miRNAs can exhibit diverse expression patterns even within the same disease context, depending on disease status. Understanding the mechanisms underlying these variations in miRNA expression patterns related to disease progression, severity, and endotype is essential for utilizing miRNAs as treatment biomarkers in clinical settings.
Five to ten percent of patients with allergic diseases experience severe exacerbation, and current treatment strategies do not achieve satisfactory results [ ]. As a new alternative for their management, it is necessary to develop a treatment strategy that targets the modification of the immune response according to the disease status of the patient, and miRNA has the potential to be a target molecule for this purpose [ , , ]. With a better understanding of the biological functions of miRNAs, more studies on the regulation of miRNA expression can lead to the development of more effective treatment methods for allergic diseases in the future.
MDC levels are typically elevated in the skin of patients with AD, where this Th2 chemokine plays a significant role in the immune mechanisms of allergic diseases [ ]. In the present experiment using HMC-1 cells, we observed a decrease in MDC levels following miR-4497 transfection. This finding suggests that MDC could potentially serve as a target molecule for the modulation of allergic diseases through the action of miR-4497.
This study had several limitations. First, we identified one common miRNA that had not previously associated with three phenotypes of allergic disease. The expression patterns of miRNAs can vary based on demographic characteristics such as race and age, even within the same disease group [ , ]. Our study subjects primarily consisted of Korean children and adolescents, and the severity or treatment status of the disease was not adequately reflected due to the small sample size. As a result, our findings may not be broadly applicable to all patients with allergic diseases, highlighting the need for further research to comprehensively understand the role and clinical relevance of miR-4497 in allergic diseases.
Second, due to the small injection dose and the study’s focus on observing phenotypic and biological effects rather than direct quantification, qRT-PCR was not performed in the in vivo context. Instead, the observed outcomes (e.g., suppression of inflammation or related markers) strongly support that miR-4497 was successfully delivered and biologically active.
Third, allergic diseases are characterized by their complexity and heterogeneity [ ]. Although we categorized the subjects in this study into AD, AR, and BA groups based on standard diagnostic criteria, it is important to recognize that these conditions may have diverse underlying causes, presenting a challenge in categorizing them under a single disease group. Lastly, despite our efforts to enroll patients with a single clinically distinct allergic disease, practical constraints limited the inclusion of individuals with other subclinical allergic conditions. Nevertheless, miR-4497 emerged as a novel biomarker associated with three distinct allergic diseases (AD, AR, BA) in the present study. Although further validation is needed, miR-4497 appears to play a common role in the mechanisms underlying these three allergic diseases, indicating its potential as a shared biomarker for managing allergic diseases. Additionally, given that miRNAs are stable and can be tested noninvasively, there is significant potential for their use as biomarkers for monitoring allergic diseases as well.
The commonly downregulated levels of miR-4497 in children diagnosed with AD, AR, and BA corresponded with decreased allergic inflammation following transfection into inflammatory cells. While these findings were validated through cell experiments, further research, including the identification of target genes, is essential to elucidate the mechanisms by which miR-4497 influences T-cell function activation in allergic diseases. Additional studies are needed to provide valuable insights into the epigenetic mechanisms contributing to the development of allergic diseases and to explore their potential as noninvasive biomarkers for these conditions.
