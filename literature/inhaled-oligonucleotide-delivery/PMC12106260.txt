## Abstract
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play critical roles in the process of lung tissue injury and repair which abnormal repair leads to disease including fibrosis, yet the physiopathology remains elusive. Here, we identified the lncRNA SYISL as a key regulator that is markedly upregulated in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) patients and bleomycin (BLM)-induced murine fibrotic lungs. Inhibition of SYISL significantly attenuates TGF-β1-driven fibroblast myofibroblast transition (FMT), a process confers to tissue injury repair and regeneration. Which demonstrates SYISL interaction with miR-23a function as a potent suppressor of fibrotic activation. Mechanistically, SYISL acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) that directly binds miR-23a, thereby derepressing TRIO and F-actin binding protein (TRIOBP) via targeting its 3' untranslated region (UTR). Knockdown of TRIOBP amplifies the anti-fibrotic effects of miR-23a mimics while abolishing the pro-fibrotic activity of miR-23a inhibitors, establishing TRIOBP as a downstream effector of the SYISL/miR-23a axis. In vivo, intratracheal delivery of SYISL-targeting shRNA via adeno-associated virus (AAV) robustly reduces collagen deposition, hydroxyproline content, and expression of fibrotic markers in BLM-induced mice. Our findings elucidate a lncRNA-driven regulatory circuit in which SYISL promotes pulmonary fibrosis by sequestering miR-23a to elevate TRIOBP expression, nominating this axis as a novel therapeutic target for IPF.

## Introduction
Lung diseases are the leading cause of mortality worldwide. Current therapy mainly relies on short-term pharmacotherapy and lung transplantation with limited sustained clinical benefit. Stimulating alveolar fibroblasts could increase the lung's capacity to undergo self-repair. Nevertheless, fibroblast expansion in a diseased lung is frequently handicapped by the lack of fibroblast-active cues from the dysfunctional surrounding microenvironmental/niche cells. Consequently, impaired lung repair often provokes excessive scar formation and fibrosis. IPF is a devastating chronic lung disease characterized by the excessive activation of lung fibroblasts and the abnormal deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), leading to progressive lung dysfunction and a high mortality rate [ ]. The pathogenesis of IPF involves complex interactions between epithelial and mesenchymal cells following persistent lung epithelial injury and fibroblast activation [ – ]. which underscores the need for a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying lung fibrosis to inform effective clinical interventions. Fibroblasts are critical in fibrotic lung tissue as they can transition to myofibroblasts, a process influenced by mechanical factors, and extracellular matrix proteins, which is pivotal in asthma-related bronchial airway wall remodelling [ ] as well as for wound repair and fibrogenesis in IPF [ ].
The TGF-β signaling pathway plays a central role in promoting fibroblast-myofibroblast transition (FMT), a critical process in fibrogenesis, through the synergistic regulation of canonical Smad-dependent and non-canonical pathways. In the canonical TGF-β/Smad pathway, TGF-β1 binds to TGF-β receptor type II (TβRII) on the cell membrane, which recruits and phosphorylates TGF-β receptor I (TβRI/ALK5), forming an active receptor complex. Activated TβRI subsequently phosphorylates receptor-regulated Smad proteins (R-Smads, e.g., Smad2/3), enabling their oligomerization with the common mediator Smad4 [ , ], The Smad complex translocates to the nucleus, where it cooperates with transcription factors such as AP-1 and SP1 to bind Smad-binding elements (SBEs) in the promoters of key fibrotic genes. This transcriptional activation upregulates α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), a hallmark of myofibroblasts that enhances cellular contractility, as well as ECM-related genes including collagen I (COL1 A1) and fibronectin (FN1), thereby driving pathological ECM deposition [ , ].Beyond the Smad pathway, TGF-β activates multiple non-canonical signaling cascades to amplify fibrogenesis. For instance, TGF-β stimulates the ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK pathways, which phosphorylate transcription factors like c-Jun and ATF2 to enhance pro-fibrotic gene expression [ ], Additionally, TGF-β stabilizes β-catenin via the Wnt pathway, facilitating its nuclear translocation and subsequent activation of Snail1 and Twist, transcription factors that promote FMT [ ]. TGF-β further induces collagen deposition through the Nox2-dependent PI3 K/Akt pathway and activates JAK2/STAT3 signaling to upregulate connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) and α-SMA, thereby stimulating collagen secretion [ ]. Notably, TGF-β establishes a self-reinforcing regulatory loop by inducing microRNAs such as miR-424, which suppresses Slit2 to enhance Smad3 phosphorylation and sustain pro-fibrotic gene expression [ ].
lncRNAs are a class of transcripts over 200 nucleotides in length with low coding capacity that participate in a variety of pathological and physiological processes [ – ]. Increasing evidence highlights the important role of miRNAs in the context of pulmonary fibrosis [ – ]. For instance, the therapeutic potential of miRNA mimics to enhance miRNA levels, with miR-29 being considered a strong candidate for the treatment of PF [ ]. Additionally, the loss of miRNA 140 (miR-140) is a key risk factor for radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis, having a profound impact on the reprogramming of fibroblasts and macrophages [ ]. LncRNAs and miRNAs play indispensable roles in the fibrotic processes of various organs, including the lung, liver, kidney, and myocardium, and due to their profound impact, they have become promising therapeutic targets in fibrotic pathophysiology. LncRNA NEAT1 has been demonstrated to promote PF by participating in the miR-9-5p/TGF-β signaling pathway [ ]. PTAR is associated with the promotion of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis in ovarian cancer, attributed to its regulation of the miR-101/ZEB1 axis [ ]. Recent studies have emphasized the complex relationship between lncRNAs and fibrotic diseases [ – ]. Although some studies have described the expression profile of lncRNAs in rat bleomycin (BLM)-induced pulmonary fibrosis models [ ] and identified several functional lncRNAs as potential targets for the treatment of lung fibrosis [ – ], a comprehensive understanding of their regulatory mechanisms and biological functions in IPF is still evolving.
SYISL is a conserved lncRNA involved in muscle differentiation in mice, pigs, and humans, and it has been shown to facilitate muscle atrophy through the miR-23a-3p/miR-205-5p/miR-103-3p [ ]. However, the role and molecular mechanisms of SYISL in the progression of IPF remain to be elucidated.

## Materials and methods

## Cell cultures
Primary human lung fibroblast PHLFs, primary mouse lung fibroblast (PMLFs), and HEK293 T cells were cultured in DME/F-12 or DMEM at 37 °C and 5% CO2. The medium contained 10% FBS (fetal bovine serum), 100 units/ml penicillin, 100 g/ml streptomycin, and 1 mM sodium pyruvate. Media were changed every three days.

## Isolation of PHLFs
The isolation and culture process of PHLFs is as follows: In brief, lung tissues from healthy donors were cut into small pieces approximately 1 mm in diameter and then immersed in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (Gibco, Life Technologies Corporation) containing collagenase II (2 mg/mL), trypsin (2.5 mg/mL), DNase I (2 mg/mL), penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 μg/mL) at 37 °C on a shaker for 12 h. Subsequently, they were washed with Dulbecco's phosphate-buffered saline. The cells were then centrifuged at room temperature at 500 g for 5 min, and High-glucose complete medium (containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 μg/mL) (Gibico, Life Technologies) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The clinical studies were approved by the Henan Provincial Chest Hospital Medical Research Ethics Committee (No.2020–03–06).

## Isolation of PMLFs
PMLFs were isolated as described [ ]. In brief, 3-day-old mice were euthanized with anesthetics and cleaned with 75% ethanol. The lung tissue was collected and washed twice with sterile PBS before being cut into 1 mm3 pieces. The pellet was resuspended in DMEM medium containing 15% FBS and cultured in 10-cm culture dishes at 37 °C after centrifugation (600 g, 5 min). After digestion and filtration, the attached fibroblasts were collected after 4–5 days of incubation for subculturing or other assays.

## Mouse model
Male C57BL/6 mice aged 6–8 weeks with an average body weight of 18–23 g were selected. Under anesthesia, a single intratracheal administration of BLM (3 mg/kg) was performed. C57BL/6 mice were allowed to acclimate for one week before the intratracheal administration of either 02.5 × 1011 µg of AAV-shSYISL or AAV-NC (HanBiO Technology, Shanghai). An experimental pulmonary fibrosis model was established 28 days later. Animals were randomly assigned to each experimental group. Assessors were unblinded to group allocation. All animal procedures were approved and performed by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at Henan Normal (IACUC, SMKX-2118BS1018).

## Reverse transcription qPCR (RT-qPCR) and western blot
For RT-qPCR, total RNAs from cultured cells with different treatments were extracted with Trizol Reagent. Following RNA isolation with TRIzol, all RNA samples were treated with RNase-free DNase I (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 30 min at 37 °C, followed by purification using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen), which incorporates an on-column DNase digestion step to ensure complete genomic DNA removal. cDNA was synthesized by M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase (M1708, Promega), RNase-free DNase I (Thermo Fisher Scientific). RT-qPCR was conducted using a SYBR green kit (11201ES03, Yeasen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Each sample and experiment were tested in triplicate. Delta CT values of the target gene were normalized to GAPDH or U6. The data were evaluated by the 2−ΔΔCt method. The employed primers are listed in Supplementary Table .
For western blot, proteins were separated on polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes, and immunoprobed with specific antibodies. The proteins were detected using a chemiluminescence reagent kit purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific. The imager station captured the images (Odyssey Software Version 5.2, LI-COR Biosciences). The following antibodies were used: TRIOBP (16124–1-AP, Proteintech), GAPDH (AF7021, Affinity), α-SMA (ab124964, Abcam), Type I collagen (14695–1-AP, Proteintech), Fibronectin (15613–1-AP, Proteintech), Vim (10366–1-AP, Proteintech).

## Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Masson's trichrome staining and immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Lung tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin to prepare 5-μm tissue sections. The tissue sections were deparaffinated and rehydrated in xylene and different concentrations of ethanol. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson’s trichrome staining were performed according to the instructions of the kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China), and histopathological changes were observed under a microscope. For IHC, the steps of immunohistochemical staining were as follows: lung sections were dewaxed transparently and treated with 3% H2O2 dropwise for 10 min; distilled wash with water and immerse slides in 0.01% sodium citrate solution, microwave to boiling, 50% goat serum blocked 30 min, overnight incubation with primary antibody. The following day, incubate the horseradish peroxidase-labeled antibody for 30 min at 37 °C before developing with DAB chromogen. After staining the nuclei with hematoxylin for 5 min, the sections were rinsed with running water for 10 min. Five visual fields were randomly selected for evaluation in each section after being sealed with neutral balsam.

## Immunofluorescence staining
Cells were seeded into 24-well cell culture plates. After treatment, cells fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde were washed three times for 5 min, blocked with 1% goat serum for 1 h at room temperature, incubated with the required antibody, washed three times in PBS, incubated with secondary antibody, washed three times in PBS again, treated with DAPI to dye the nuclei, and finally washed three times in PBS.

## Hydroxyproline assay
The total hydroxyproline content in wet lung tissue was determined by using a hydroxyproline assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, AO303-1) and following the manufacturer's protocol. The concentration of hydroxyproline in the right lung was calculated (µg/mg of wet tissue).

## Luciferase reporter assay
In luciferase reporter gene assays, oligonucleotides harboring SYISL (human and mouse), TRIOBP (human and mouse), and deletion sequences were amplified and cloned into a vector. To investigate the impact of miR-23a on SYISL and TRIOBP, reporter genes for SYISL, TRIOBP, and the deletion fragments were constructed. The luciferase constructs and miR-23a mimic were transfected into HEK293 cells using Lipofectamine 3000. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, luciferase activity was measured with a dual luciferase assay system (YEASEN, catalog #11402ES60) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

## EdU incorporation assay
RiboBio Corporation (Guangzhou, China) provided an Apollo567 in vitro Imaging Kit, which was used for the EdU incorporation assay. The cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde (4%), permeabilized with Triton X-100 (0.2%), and stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, 5 μg/ml) and Apollo fluorescent dyes after being cultured with EdU (10 M) for 2 h.

## Analysis of cell survival and migration
For analysis of cell survival, the Cell-Light EdU DNA cell proliferation kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) was used to detect the ability of cell proliferation following the manufacturer’s protocol. For analysis of cell migration, transwell assay was also used to analyze cell migration. Serum-free medium was used to resuspend the cells, and containing FBS medium (10%) was used in the lower chambers. Calculating the cell numbers from five random fields allowed for the staining, imaging, and analysis of the migrated cells.

## Statistics and analysis
The results in the control and experimental groups were analyzed by GraphPad software 9.0. The Shapiro–Wilk normality test was used to test normal distribution. The results were analyzed by the Mann–Whitney U test for comparisons between two groups when sample data were not normally distributed, and by unpaired Student’s t-test for comparisons between two groups with normal distribution. Data are presented as mean ± SD and were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

## Results

## SYISL inhibition blocks TGF-β-mediated lung FMT
To investigate whether SYISL participates in TGF-β-mediated activation of pulmonary fibroblasts into myofibroblasts—a process that drives fibrogenesis—we first demonstrated that human SYISL (hSYISL) is significantly upregulated in lung tissues from IPF patients compared to healthy controls (Fig.  A). Consistently, murine SYISL expression was markedly elevated in bleomycin-induced fibrotic lungs (Fig.  B, C). Primary human (PHLFs) and mouse (PMLFs) lung fibroblasts were isolated (Fig.  D), and shRNA-mediated hSYISL knockdown in PHLFs robustly suppressed TGF-β1-induced expression of FN1, COL1 A1, and α-SMA (Fig.  E, F). Functional assays revealed that hSYISL silencing attenuated fibroblast proliferation (Fig.  G), collagen gel contraction (CGC) capacity (Fig.  H), and vimentin (VIM) expression (Fig.  I). Parallel experiments in PMLFs confirmed that SYISL knockout abolished TGF-β1-induced upregulation of Fn1 and Acta2 (Fig. J, K, O), while impairing fibroblast migration (Fig.  N), proliferation (Fig.  L), and CGC activity (Fig.  M). These findings collectively demonstrate that SYISL depletion disrupts TGF-β-driven myofibroblast differentiation by attenuating both phenotypic switching (α-SMA) and ECM hypersecretion (FN1/COL1 A1), thereby identifying SYISL as a critical regulator of FMT.
 

## SYISL overexpression accelerated TGF-β-induced lung FMT
To elucidate the functional consequences of SYISL overexpression on lung FMT, we performed PMLFs to overexpress SYISL and observed a significant upregulation of fibrotic genes (Fig.  A, B), which was accompanied by enhanced TGF-β1-driven proliferation (Fig.  C), migration (Fig.  D), and collagen gel contraction (CGC) capacity (Fig.  E). Parallel experiments in PHLFs recapitulated these findings, demonstrating that SYISL overexpression amplifies TGF-β1-induced activation across functional endpoints (Fig.  G-K). To dissect SYISL’s functional domains, we employed UNAFold-based structural predictions, partitioning SYISL into three segments (1–391 bp, 1–800 bp, and 801–1181 bp; Figure A, B). Strikingly, full-length SYISL and its truncation variants—particularly the 1–800 bp segment—synergistically potentiated TGF-β1-induced expression of α-SMA,COL1 A1,and FN1 in PMLFs (Figure C-G), with the 1–800 bp fragment exhibiting the most robust pro-fibrotic activity. Collectively, these data establish that SYISL overexpression not only exacerbates TGF-β-driven myofibroblast differentiation but also localizes its core functional domain to nucleotides 1–800, providing mechanistic insights into its role as a central orchestrator of FMT.
 

## SYISL drives lung FMT by functioning as a ceRNA to sponge miR-23a
Using RegRNA 2.0, we predicted that SYISL harbors a conserved miR-23a binding site (Fig.  A), which was validated by dual-luciferase assays demonstrating that mutation of this site abolished the interaction between SYISL/hSYISL and miR-23a (Fig.  B). Silencing SYISL or hSYIS in PHLFs and PMLFs lung fibroblasts significantly elevated miR-23a levels (Fig.  C, D) and counteracted TGF-β1-induced miR-23a suppression, whereas SYISL overexpression exacerbated this suppression (Fig.  E). Notably, miR-23a mimics inhibited TGF-β1-driven SYISL upregulation, while miR-23a inhibitors amplified it (Fig.  F). we observed a significant downregulation of miR-23a in IPF patients compared to healthy controls(Fig.  G) Functional studies revealed that miR-23a mimics suppressed TGF-β1-induced FN1, COL1 A1, α-SMA, VIM, TRIOBP expression (Fig.  H, I, L; Figure A-B, F), fibroblast migration (Supplementary Figure D), proliferation (Fig.  J, Figure E), and collagen gel contraction (CGC)(Fig.  K, Figure C). Conversely, miR-23a inhibitors potentiated TGF-β1-induced fibrotic gene expression (Figure G-H, A-B), migration (Figure J), proliferation (Figure K, C), and CGC (Figure I, D), while upregulating α-SMA and VIM (Figure L, E). Critically, SYISL/hSYISL knockdown synergized with miR-23a mimics to suppress fibrotic gene expression (Fig.  M, Figure M) and blunted the pro-fibrotic effects of miR-23a inhibitors (Figure N, F). Collectively, these data establish SYISL as a ceRNA that sequesters miR-23a, thereby derepressing TRIOBP and amplifying TGF-β-driven FMT, a mechanism central to IPF pathogenesis.
 

## MiR-23a modulates lung FMT by directly targeting TRIOBP
Using the TargetScan database, we identified a perfectly conserved miR-23a binding site within the 3'UTR of TRIOBP in both human and murine (Fig.  A), dual-luciferase assays showing that mutating this site abolished the miR-23a-TRIOBP interaction (Fig.  B). Silencing TRIOBP significantly attenuated TGF-β1-induced suppression of miR-23a in PHLFs (Fig.  C) and PMLFs (Fig.  D), while miR-23a mimics inhibited and miR-23a inhibitors enhanced TGF-β1-driven TRIOBP upregulation (Fig.  E). TRIOBP protein levels are significantly elevated in patients of IPF (Fig.  F), and TRIOBP knockdown suppressed TGF-β1-induced expression of fibrotic markers (FN1, COL1 A1, and α-SMA) (Fig.  G-K) and impaired fibroblast proliferation (Fig.  L) and collagen gel contraction (CGC) (Fig.  M) in both PHLFs and PMLFs (Figure A-F). Critically, TRIOBP depletion synergized with miR-23a mimics to further reduce TGF-β1-induced fibrotic gene expression (Fig.  N) and counteracted the pro-fibrotic effects of miR-23a inhibitors (Fig.  O). Collectively, these findings establish that SYISL orchestrates a ceRNA network in which it competitively binds miR-23a to derepress TRIOBP, thereby amplifying TGF-β1-driven FMT —a mechanistic axis central to pulmonary fibrogenesis.
 

## AAV-mediated delivery of SYISL-shRNA alleviate BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis
To evaluate the therapeutic potential of SYISL targeting in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), we established a bleomycin (BLM)-induced murine pulmonary fibrosis model and administered SYISL-shRNA via intratracheal delivery of adeno-associated virus serotype 6 (AAV6) seven days post-BLM instillation (Fig.  A). SYISL knockdown robustly suppressed BLM-driven upregulation of fibrotic genes (Acta2, Col1a1, Fn1) and TRIOBP (Fig.  B, D), concomitant with reduced hydroxyproline content (Fig.  C), a biochemical hallmark of collagen accumulation. Strikingly, SYISL silencing resulted in a marked upregulation of miR-23a (Fig.  E). Masson’s trichrome staining revealed diminished collagen deposition in SYISL-silenced lungs, while immunohistochemistry demonstrated marked reductions in α-sma, Col1a1,Vimentin and Triobp protein levels (Fig.  F). Collectively, these data demonstrate that AAV6-mediated SYISL silencing effectively attenuates BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis, thereby nominating SYISL as a novel therapeutic target for IPF.
 

## Discussion
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) critically regulate lung tissue repair, with dysregulated expression driving pathological fibrosis. Here, we identify SYISL, a conserved lncRNA markedly upregulated in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and bleomycin-induced fibrotic lungs, as a pro-fibrotic driver whose inhibition attenuates TGF-β1-driven fibroblast-myofibroblast transition (FMT)—a process central to aberrant tissue remodeling. Mechanistically, SYISL functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) that sequesters miR-23a, derepressing TRIOBP via direct targeting of its 3'UTR. Intratracheal delivery of SYISL-targeting shRNA via adeno-associated virus (AAV) in mice robustly reduces collagen deposition, hydroxyproline content, and fibrotic marker expression in bleomycin-challenged lungs. we unveils the SYISL/miR-23a/TRIOBP axis as a master regulatory circuit in pulmonary fibrosis in IPF.
Recent research highlights the critical role of fibroblasts in the pathogenesis of IPF, even though their heterogeneity poses challenges to therapeutic targeting. The successful clinical trials of pirfenidone and nintedanib, FDA-approved antifibrosis agents, highlight the importance of fibroblasts in pulmonary fibrosis [ , ]. Our study focuses on fibroblasts, utilizing SYISL as a key lncRNA to understand its role in fibrosis.
Several studies have explored the involvement of lncRNAs in IPF. LncRNA MALAT1 promotes EMT through phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3 K) p85 regulation by acting as a ceRNA for miR-503, contributing to silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis. [ ]. LncRNA PCF promotes epithelial cell proliferation by directly targeting miR-344a-5p, while silencing PCF alleviates pulmonary fibrosis in rats [ ]. LncRNA ATB activated by TGF-β promotes EMT in silica-driven lung fibrosis by binding to miR-200c [ ]. Emerging evidence suggests that miRNAs play a crucial role in the initiation and progression of IPF and can be targeted for therapeutic intervention [ ]. Our previous studies identified miR-29b as a critical anti-fibrotic effector in IPF through targeting regulation of TRIOBP [ ]. Many studies have demonstrated the protective role of miR-23a in various cancers, such as hepatocellular carcinoma [ ], breast cancer [ ], ovarian cancer [ ], and lung cancer [ ]. However, the role of miR-23a in IPF is not fully understood. Our study demonstrates that lncRNA SYISL contributes to lung fibrosis through regulation of the expression of TRIOBP by competitively binding to miR-23a.
Increasing evidence suggests that interactions between lncRNAs and miRNAs play a critical role in PF [ , , ]. lncRNA PFAR as a new pro-fibrotic molecule [ ] that acts as a ceRNA of miR-138 during lung fibrosis [ ]. SNHG16 promoted pulmonary fibrosis by targeting miR-455-3p to regulate the Notch2 pathway, which might provide a novel insight into the pathologic process of lung fibrosis [ ]. LncRNA PFRL contributes to the progression of lung fibrosis by modulating the reciprocal repression between miR-26a and Smad2 [ ]. SNHG20, in contrast to miR-490-3p expression, is elevated in TGF-β1-treated fibroblast cell lines and contributes to decreased levels of miR-490-3p [ ]. In the current study, we used PHLFs and PMLFs to examine the role of lncRNAs by constructing a ceRNA network for lung fibroblast. Previous research has revealed that SYISL can participate in biological processes by acting as a ceRNA, binding to miRNAs [ ], and it was discovered that both hSYISL and SYISL can interact with miR-23a. Here, we verified that SYISL knockdown attenuated TGF-β1-induced fibroblast activation, while miR-23a inhibitor reversed this effect. Surprisingly, a positive relationship between SYISL and TRIOBP was observed in lung fibrosis, and it was concluded that SYISL could regulate TRIOBP expression via the modulation of miR-23a to affect lung fibroblast activation.
we highlight a novel non-canonical TGF-β regulatory axis involving lncRNA SYISL, which acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) to sequester miR-23a, thereby relieving miR-23a-mediated repression of TRIOBP and amplifying pro-fibrotic gene expression (Fig. ). Therefore, it can be speculated that inhibiting the expression of SYISL may provide a new approach to the treatment of PF.
 While this mechanism represents a significant advance in understanding TGF-β-driven fibrosis, it is important to acknowledge that SYISL may participate in additional regulatory networks within the TGF-β signaling landscape. For example, SYISL could potentially interact with chromatin modifiers or other RNA-binding proteins to modulate fibrotic responses, or it may cross-talk with other TGF-β-associated pathways. Future studies are warranted to explore these possibilities and to delineate the broader functional repertoire of SYISL in fibrogenesis.
