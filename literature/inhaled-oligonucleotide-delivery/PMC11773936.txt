## Abstract
Oxidative stress-associated proximal tubular cells (PTCs) damage is an important pathogenesis of hypertensive renal injury. We previously reported the protective effect of VEGFR3 in salt-sensitive hypertension. However, the specific mechanism underlying the role of VEGFR3 in kidney during the overactivation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system remains unclear. In the present study, hypertensive nephropathy was established by angiotensin II (Ang II). We found that VEGFR3 was highly increased in PTCs of Ang II-infused mice. Activation of VEGFR3 mitigated renal dysfunction, pathological damage, and oxidative stress in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice. Moreover, we found that VEGFR3 restored mitophagy deficiency induced by Ang II both in vivo and in vitro to alleviate oxidative stress injury in PTCs. Furthermore, in vitro experiment demonstrated that VEGFR3 improved abnormal mitophagy by enhancing PARKIN mitochondrial translocation. LC-MS/MS and Co-IP assays identified HSPA1L as the interacted protein of VEGFR3, which promoted the mitochondrial translocation of PARKIN. Mechanistically, VEGFR3 disorder domain bound to HSPA1L, and crotonylation modification of HSPA1L at K130 by VEGFR3 was required for mitophagy regulation in the context of Ang II-induced PTCs. Finally, the protective effect of VEGFR3 on mitophagy and oxidative stress were attenuated by transfection K130 (HSPA1L-K130R) mutant plasmid in vivo and in vitro. These findings indicated that VEGFR3 alleviated oxidative stress by promoting PARKIN-dependent mitophagy pathway via regulating HSPA1L crotonylation at K130 site in Ang II-induced PTCs, which provided a mechanistic basis for the therapeutic target in hypertensive renal injury.

## Introduction
Hypertension is the leading cause of cardiovascular death and a major risk factor for chronic kidney disease (CKD) [ ]. Approximately 850 million people globally are affected by CKD, with > 80% of them hypertensive [ ]. Nephron loss in hypertensive nephropathy is caused not only by damage to the glomerulus, but also by proximal tubular cells (PTCs) damage, which induces morphological changes and irreversible kidney dysfunction, ultimately leading to end stage renal disease [ , ]. Oxidative stress contributes to a range of physiological and pathological processes in hypertensive kidney end-organ damage [ – ]. Thus, alleviating PTCs oxidative stress injury has potential clinical significance for the treatment of hypertensive renal injury. However, the pathological mechanisms are not well understood. A detailed study of the molecular mechanism and pathway of PTCs oxidative stress injury is needed to develop a targeted treatment for hypertensive nephropathy.
Vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 3 (VEGFR3), a crucial member of the VEGF family, is produced in response to various stresses such as hypoxia, oxidative stress, or mitochondrial stress [ – ]. As a tyrosine kinase receptor, VEGFR3 has the basal amount of autophosphorylation, and the activation of VEGFR3 by its ligand VEGFC further enhances its tyrosine phosphorylation, which is crucial for its ability to target downstream effect or to regulate the function of interacting proteins [ , ]. Despite most notable vascular roles, VEGFR3 has also been linked to noncanonical immunoregulatory and protecting against oxidative damage roles [ – ]. We previously discovered VEGFR3 promoting autophagy under high salt stress to ameliorate salt-sensitive hypertension [ ]. Recently, the member of VEGF family newly assumed a distinct impact in nonvascular tissues through mitophagy regulation [ ]. Whether VEGFR3 alleviated oxidative stress via regulating mitophagy and its role in hypertensive kidney injury is yet to be identified. Mitophagy, an essential mitochondrial quality control mechanism that eliminates damaged mitochondria and the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), promoting renal PTCs survival and preserving renal function during hypertensive nephropathy [ ]. The best described pathway of mitophagy is driven by the enzyme 3 (E3) ubiquitin ligase PARKIN pathway and mitophagy receptor pathway [ ]. Targeting mitophagy may provide an effective potential treatment strategy for hypertensive renal injury [ , ].
Protein posttranslational modifications (PTMs) play essential roles in modifying protein function and thus influence numerous biological processes [ ]. It is now well established that the key mitophagy machinery undergoes extensive PTMs such as phosphorylation, methylation, ubiquitination, and acetylation (Kac) [ , ]. The aberrant PTMs are becoming more crucial in the progression of CKD, and histone lysine acetylation is one of the most well-studied PTMs [ ]. Lysine crotonylation (Kcr) was newly explored to be an evolutionarily conserved PTM related to the regulation of mitochondrial function [ , ]. Subsequent studies showed that Kcr shares transferases and deacylases with Kac [ , ]. Previous study has revealed that VEGF increased target protein acetylation through activating acetyltransferase, suggesting that VEGF may also play a key role in the regulation of Kcr [ ]. In the context of kidney diseases, histone Kcr was observed in both healthy and diseased kidneys, high levels of Kcr contribute to the protection from acute kidney injury [ ]. While histones are the most abundant crotonylated proteins, nonhistone protein Kcr is poorly understood [ ]. Recently, few reports have demonstrated the key role of crotonylated nonhistone protein in regulating mitophagy to influence the progression of cardiovascular disease and chemoresistance in cancer cells [ , ]. However, regarding the functional roles of nonhistone protein Kcr during hypertensive kidney injury remain limited. Heat shock protein family A member 1 like (HSPA1L) belong to heat shock protein 70s family, possesses a highly conserved domain structure crucial for protein stabilization, cell proliferation, and apoptosis, as well as cell signal transduction [ , ]. Previous studies reported that HSPA1L contributes to mitophagy, leading to mitochondrial quality control and resulting in reduced mitochondrial ROS (mtROS) production [ ]. High-content genome-wide RNAi screens identified HSPA1L activates mitophagy by binding and translocating PARKIN to the damaged mitochondria [ ], nevertheless, how it plays, are still enigmas. Studies indicated PTMs affect the activity, structure, and function of HSPA1L [ , ]. Our LC-MS/MS analysis showed that HSPA1L with a higher score is expected to be an important interacting protein of VEGFR3 and the crotonylation modification lysine site was identified in HSPA1L. However, the functional elucidation of non-histone HSPA1L crotonylation in the regulation of mitophagy remains unclear.
In the present study, we investigated how VEGFR3 regulated PTCs oxidative stress in a mitophagy manner via interacting with HSPA1L. We showed that VEGFR3-HSPA1L regulated mitophagy by promoting the mitochondria translocation of PARKIN. Critically, we found that crotonylation of HSPA1L at K130 was essential for its function in Ang II-induced PTCs, as well as Ang II-induced hypertensive mice. Our findings describe a novel and critical role of VEGFR3 in the regulation of HSPA1L crotonylation to enhance mitophagy and overcoming oxidative stress to protect against hypertensive renal injury.

## Materials and methods

## Animal experiments
All animal experiments were approved by the institutional research ethics committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (SYDW2022-075). Procedures were performed in compliance with the National Institutes of Health Guidelines (Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals) and the guidelines from Directive 2010/63/EU of the European Parliament on the protection of animals were used for scientific purposes.
Eight to ten-week-old male mice on a C57BL/6J background weighing 20–25 g were purchased from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. All mice were feed on a standard mouse diet and water. To induce hypertensive nephropathy model, mice were anesthetized with 3% isoflurane and infused with Ang II (1000 ng/kg·per min) for 14 days with osmotic minipumps (Alzet MODEL 2002; DURECT, Cupertino, CA) as previous description [ ]. According to the instruction, for subcutaneous placement, a small incision is made in the skin between the scapulae. Using a hemostat, a small pocket is formed by spreading the subcutaneous connective tissues apart. The pump is inserted into the pocket with the flow moderator pointing away from the incision. The skin incision was closed with sutures. Recombinant VEGFC protein (VEGFCc156s, 69.4 ng/kg·per min) continuously infusion was used as treatments, which specifically activated VEGFR3 but not VEGFR2. All mice were implanted with osmotic minipumps and were randomly divided into the following groups: Control mice received saline (Control + Saline) or VEGFC (Control + VEGFC) for 14 days. Hypertensive mice infused with Ang II (Ang II + Saline) or co-administrate with recombinant VEGFC protein (Ang II + VEGFC) for 14 days. For AAV9-mediated HSPA1L-WT or HSPA1L-K130R in mice, the adeno-associated viruses were generated by HanBio (Shanghai, China). 100 µL of AAV9 carrying the HSPA1L-WT or HSPA1L-K130R plasmids (1 × 1012virus genome/ml) were injected into the renal pelvis of mice. For renal pelvis injection, mice were anesthetized and the left kidney was exposed through the flank incision. The renal pelvis was exposed, and AAV9 particles were injected into the renal pelvis using a 31-gauge needle. 2 weeks after AAV9 transfection, osmotic pump was implanted subcutaneously to infuse Ang II and VEGFC simultaneously.

## Cells and cell treatment
Human kidney proximal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2 cells, ATCC, Rockville, MD, USA) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium combined with F-12 Ham nutrient mixture (DMEM: F-12, 1:1 mixture) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). HK-2 cells were assigned to 48 h of treatment with a vehicle or 10−6 M Ang II. In separate groups of cells, an VEGFR3 inhibitor, MZA51 (10 µM), or an VEGFR3 antagonist, VEGFC (10 ng/mL), or an inhibitor of mitophagy, 3-MA (5 mM), were added to the culture medium 3 h before treatment with the vehicle or Ang II. HK-2 cells were switched to serum-free medium for 12 h before exposure to the standard culture conditions. The dose of Ang II and doses of pharmaceutical chemistry in this series of experiments were selected on the basis of earlier reports. Drugs were dissolved in phosphate buffer saline (PBS) or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). Cells in control group were treated with equal amounts of vehicle (PBS and DMSO).

## Reagents and antibodies
Recombinant VEGFC protein (ab52146), antibodies including Anti-VEGFR3 (ab27278), Anti- kidney injury molecule 1 (KIM-1, ab316854), Anti-SQSTM1 (ab109012), Anti-TOMM20 (ab56783), Anti-LC3 (ab192890), 3-nitrotyrosine (ab61392) PARKIN (ab15494) and donkey Anti-mouse IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 488, ab150105) and KIM-1 ELISA kits (ab213477) were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). SOD1 (67480-1-Ig) and 8-oxo-2’-deoxyguanosine (8-oxo-dG, 15125-1-AP) antibodies were purchased from Proteintech (USA). ACTB (AF7018), GAPDH (AF7021), VDAC1 (AF5478), HSPA1L (DF6662), PARKIN (AF0235), PINK1 (DF7742) antibodies and Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–conjugated immunoglobulin G (S0001) were acquired from Affinity Biosciences (OH.USA). Anti-pan-Kcr (PTM-502) was obtained from JingjiePTMBioLab Co. Ltd (China). Human Ang II (A9525), 3-MA (M9281) and MAZ51 (676492) were from Sigma-Aldrich (Milwaukee, WI, USA). Beyotime (Jiangsu, China) provided the Cy3-labeled Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) (A0516), 4′,6-diamidino- 2-phenylindole (DAPI, C1005), MitoSOX kits (S0061M), JC-1 kits (C2003S), FLAG (AF2852) and HA (AF2858) antibodies. Bimake (USA) provided the anti-Flag (B26101) and anti-HA (B26201) immunomagnetic beads. HE staining kit (G1120) and Masson staining kit (G1340) were from Solarbio Science &Technology (Beijing, China). DAB Horseradish Peroxidase Color Development Kit (ZLI-9018) was from KitZSGB-Bio (Beijing, China). Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China) provided creatinine (CRE, C011-2-1) and urea nitrogen (BUN, C013-2-1) ELISA kits.

## Blood pressure measurement
Blood pressure (BP) was measured via Tail-cuff method in awake mice. The Softron Biotechnology noninvasive blood pressure acquisition system for mice (BP-2010 A, Softron Biotechnology, Beijing, China) was used for all measurements. After 3 days baseline BP measurements, BP was recorded every 2 days from 1 to 5 PM. The procedures were conducted in a designated quiet area, where mice acclimatized for 30 min before measures began. Mice were handled gently and placed into a restrainer of appropriate size which were preheated to 34 °C. Mice were warmed and acclimatized for 5 min prior to BP recordings. BP values were determined from the pressure traces by 2 independent, blinded investigators.

## Biochemical analysis and renal tissue harvest
At the endpoint of the experiment, mice were placed in diuresis cages and urine was collected from individual mouse over a period of 24 h. The collected urine was centrifuged and stored frozen at −80 °C until measurement. RNA from urinary sediment cells was extracted as previously described [ ]. Briefly, the pellet was washed with sterile PBS solution and recentrifuged at 13,000 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Trizol was then added to the pellet, and RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and quantitative real-time PCR proceeded as described above. The primer of KIM-1was the following: F: 5′‐ATGAATCAGATTCAAGTCTTC‐3′ and R:5′-TCTGGTTTGTGAGTCCATGTG‐3′. Mice were euthanized at the end of the experiment by CO2 inhalation and then cervical dislocation. Blood samples were collected from abdominal aorta. The plasma was isolated for later measurements of CRE and BUN. The kidney was removed and cut longitudinally. Half of the kidney was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and the other half frozen in liquid N2, and stored at −80 °C.

## Histopathology
For morphological analysis, paraffinized-embedded kidney Sect. (4 mm) were stained by HE and Masson according to manufacturer’s instructions. Tubular injury was scored from kidney sections stained with HE using described methods, which was morphologically evaluated by two independent examiners who were blinded to the animal groups and semi-quantitatively scored based on the degree of tubular damage as described previously [ ]. And the collagen positive fibrotic kidney area was calculated as an index of renal fibrosis using Image J (free download from http://rsbweb.nih.gov/ij/). Renal fibrosis was scored as published previously [ ], and the scores were presented as a percentage area-fraction relative to the total area analyzed.

## Immunohistopathology
Deparaffinized kidney sections mounted on slides were re-hydrated followed by heat-induced antigen retrieval, blocked endogenous peroxidase activity and permeabilized with 0.1% TritonX-100 solution. Following a 1 h blocking step in 5% bovine serum albumin, renal tissues were incubated at 4℃ overnight with VEGFR3 antibody (1:200) antibody. The slides were washed and incubated with biotinylated secondary antibody for 30 min at room temperature. DAB Horseradish Peroxidase Color Development system was used for visualization followed by nuclear counterstaining staining with hematoxylin. Finally, a light microscope was used to photograph images.

## Immunofluorescence
HK-2 cells permeabilized and block as described above, cells were immunolabeled with the PARKIN (1:200) double staining with TOMM20 (1:100). Incubation of these primary antibodies occurred overnight at 4 °C. Alexa Fluor 488 (1:200) and 594 (1:200) labeled secondary antibodies were incubated for 1 h at room temperature. Samples only incubated with secondary antibody were used as negative controls. Finally, slides were incubated with DAPI for visualization of nuclear. Immunofluorescence was imaged using fluorescence confocal microscopy.

## Western blotting and immunoprecipitation
Renal cortex tissue and HK-2 cells separately were homogenized in RIPA buffer with a protease inhibitor and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail, respectively. Mitochondria were isolated from renal tissue or HK-2 cells using the Mitochondria Isolation Kit, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For immunoprecipitation analysis, cells were transfected with the indicated plasmids for a specified time. The lysates were incubated with Anti-Flag or Anti-HA immunomagnetic beads 4℃ overnight. Following the beads were washed 3 times with lysis buffer and resuspended in SDS–PAGE loading buffer for western blot analysis. Lysates were subjected to SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. After blocking for 1 h at 37 °C with 5% (w/v) non-fat milk, the membranes were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the subsequent primary antibodies: KIM-1 (1:1000), VEGFR3 (1:1000), 3-nitrotyrosine(1:1000), 8-oxo-dG (1:1000), and SOD1(1:2000), Flag(1:1000), HA (1:1000), Kcr (1:200), PARKIN(1:1000), PINK1(1:200), SQSTM1 (1:1000), LC3 (1:2000), VDAC1(1:1000), TOMM20(1:3000), ACTB (1:3000) and GAPDH (1:3000). After being washed with TBST 3 times, membranes were incubated with a secondary antibody labeled with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody 1 h at room temperature and visualized with an ECL Prime chemiluminescent kit. Densitometry was calculated with ImageJ software.

## Plasmid transfection
The murine plasmids expressing a full-length VEGFR3, truncated domain constructs Ig-like domain (IGI), protein kinase domain (KD) and disordered domain (DIS) with a Flag-tag sequence at the 3ʹ tail were purchased from GeneChem (Shanghai, China). HA-tagged HSPA1L plasmids and HA-tagged HSPA1L-K130R (lysine130 mutated to arginine) were obtained from HanBio (Shanghai, China). Plasmids sequences are shown in Table S1. Cells were transfected using Lipo3000 reagent, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, in separate tubes,4 µl Lipo3000 reagent and 5 µl plasmid DNA (2 µg) were diluted into 125 µl Opti MEM respectively, then 4ul P3000 reagent were added into diluted DNA and mixing well. The diluted DNA with P3000 reagent was added to the diluted Lipofectamine 3000 reagent and incubated at room temperature for 20 min, then 250 µL of the resulting complex was added to cells in a 6-well plate. Cells treatments were performed after 24 h post-transfection.

## Knock down of HSPA1L expression by siRNA
The siRNA targeting HSPA1L were used to knock down expression of HSPA1L. The scramble siRNA (Si-NC) and si-HSPA1L were generated by General Biosystems (Shanghai, China). After seeding into 6-well plates, HK-2 cells were transfected with 100 nM of Si-NC or Si-HSPA1L using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). The sequence of HSPA1L siRNA: 5′-GAUCAUAAGAGAAAGGAAUtt-3′.

## Detection of mitophagy
Mitophagy was examined based on co-localization of mitochondria and lysosomes. HK-2 cells were incubated with red fluorescent Mitotracker Red and green fluorescent Lysotracker Green for 30 min. Confocal images were obtained with a Zeiss LSM 610 microscope (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). Quantification was performed with Image-J co-localization analysis.

## MitoSOX and JC-1 staining
Mitochondrial ROS were detected using the fluorescent probe MitoSOX, following the manufacturer’s protocols. Images were obtained with a confocal laser scanning microscope and analyzed with ImageJ software. Mitochondria membrane potential staining was performed on HK-2 cells according to manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, after treatment with Ang II, the HK-2 cells were labeled with JC-1 (300 nM) for 20 min at 37 °C. Subsequently, fluorescent images were acquired with a confocal microscope (Zeiss).

## Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
The cells with different treatment were fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde immediately after being harvested and then the cells were fixed with 1% osmic acid solution for 2 h and dehydrated with a graded concentration of ethanol. The cells were embedded using a mixture of an embedding agent and acetone. Ultrathin sections were cut using a Reichert Ultracut S ultramicrotome (Science Service, Munich, Germany), and lead citrate was used for contrast. Images were captured using an EM 10 CR electron microscope (H-7650; Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) and analyzed by an independent blinded investigator.

## Statistical analysis
All data are presented as means ± standard error of mean (SEM), and all the data were acquired from at least three independent experiments. Data were analyzed and graphed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to determine the normality of the data. Two-tailed Student’s t-test was performed to compare the differences between 2 groups with the normally distributed data. Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare data between two groups with non-normal distributed values. One-way ANOVA followed by a Tukey’s multiple comparison test was performed to examine the differences among multiple groups. The Sidak multiple comparison test in two-way ANOVA was suitable for multiple factors analysis. P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Activation of VEGFR3 attenuates Ang II-induced hypertensive renal injury
To investigate whether VEGFR3 plays a role in Ang II-induced hypertensive nephropathy, we first evaluated the expression of VEGFR3 in renal tissue of hypertensive mice induced by Ang II infusion. Immunohistochemistry assay and immunoblotting results indicated that the level of VEGFR3 was substantially elevated in PTCs (Fig.  A and B). To test the effect of VEGFR3 activation on Ang II-induced hypertensive nephropathy, the mutant VEGFC protein analogue was used, which is a specific activator of VEGFR3. Experimental paradigm is shown in Fig.  C. After Ang II stimulation, noninvasive tail-cuff measurement showed that SBP was significantly elevated, whereas the elevated SBP was decreased from the 10th day of VEGFC treatment and almost back to the baseline level on the 14th day (Fig.  D). Interesting, the results showed that 24 h urinary excretion of albumin and urinary mRNA levels of KIM-1 began to decrease on the 6th day with VEGFC treatment before any effect of VEGFC on blood pressure (Fig.  E and F). To further conform the protect effect of VEGFR3 activation on tubular damages, expression levels of KIM-1 in renal cortex were detected. The result showed protein levels of KIM-1 were significantly increased in Ang II group compared to that in control group, which were almost completely abolished by VEGFC treatment (Fig.  G and H). Similarly, plasma CRE (Fig.  I) and BUN (Fig.  J) levels induced by Ang II stimulation were decreased in VEGFC treatment group at the endpoint. Taken together, these results indicated that VEGFR3 activation exerted beneficial effect on kidney injury in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice.
 

## Activation of VEGFR3 decreases renal oxidative stress levels and restored mitophagy in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice
High throughput transcriptome sequencing was performed on the kidney of mice subjected to osmotic pump implanted subcutaneously to infuse Ang II or saline. GO enrichment analysis showed that, compared with the control group, regulation of oxidative stress-induced cell death, establishment of protein localization to mitochondrion, positive relation of response to external stimulus and mitochondrial transport were significantly increased in Ang II-induced hypertension group (Fig.  A). mtROS is the major source of cellular reactive oxygen species that cause oxidative stress [ ]. Next, we detected the renal mtROS levels by MitoSOX fluorescent probe. As shown in Fig.  B and C, higher oxidative stress levels were observed in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice compared with control group, which was reversed by VEGFC treatment. Mitophagy is an important pathway to eliminate mtROS and alleviate cellular oxidative stress. We previously demonstrated that VEGFR3 enhanced autophagy exerted anti-inflammatory effect in salt-sensitive hypertension [ ]. In this study, we speculate whether VEGFR3 might regulate mitophagy to alleviate hypertensive kidney injury in hypertensive mice induced by Ang II. We extracted mitochondrial fractions from the renal cortex of mice to detected the expression of SQSTM1 and LC3 II/LC3 I in mitochondria. The results showed that Ang II induced impaired mitophagy in the kidney of Ang II-induced hypertensive mice, which was reversed by VEGFC treatment (Fig.  D and F). Simultaneous detecting of several oxidative stress-related markers including 3-nitrotyrosine, 8-oxo-dG and SOD1 were performed on the renal tissues from each group. Higher oxidative stress-related proteins and lower antioxidant protein were observed in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice compared with control mice and these performances were markedly blunted in Ang II +VEGFC group mice (Fig.  E and G). We performed HE-staining to assess the degree of renal tubular injury. The result showed that the tubular injury score was significantly increased in Ang II group, which was decreased by VEGFC treatment (Fig.  H and J). Likewise, VEGFC treatment mice had less renal fibrosis than mice with Ang II infusion as detected by Masson staining (Fig.  I and K). Collectively, these data suggested that VEGFR3 played a protective role in hypertensive renal injury which might be explained by decreasing renal oxidative stress levels and restoring mitophagy.
 

## VEGFR3 inhibits Ang II-induced oxidative stress injury in HK-2 cells
To determine whether VEGFR3 was involved in cell response to oxidative stress, we examined the expression of VEGFR3 in Ang II-induced HK-2 cells. We investigated the effect of different concentrations of Ang II on VEGFR3 expression, thereby determining that Ang II at a concentration of 10−6 M significantly induced VEGFR3 expression (Fig.  A). Consistently, time-dependent activation of VEGFR3 in HK-2 cells was observed upon Ang II stress (Fig.  B). Mitochondrial damage is a primary cause of mtROS generation. The result showed VEGFR3 activator VEGFC (10 ng/ml) significantly depressed the production of mtROS induced by Ang II, while VEGFR3 inhibitor MAZ51 (10 µM) further increased mtROS (Fig.  C and D). Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was another indicator of mitochondrial function. We observed that VEGFC also significantly prevented Ang II-induced reduction of MMP in HK-2 cells (Fig.  C and E). To further investigate the effect of VEGFR3 on Ang II-induced oxidative stress injury in cells, we overexpressed Flag-tagged VEGFR3 in HK-2 cells. Lower oxidative stress-related proteins 3-nitrotyrosine and 8-oxo-dG, as well as higher levels of antioxidant protein SOD1were observed in FLAG-VEGFR3 cells compared with FLAG-Vector after Ang II stimulation (Fig.  F and G). Of note, MAZ51 abolished the protective effect of VEGFC in Ang II-induced HK-2 cells, which confirmed that VEGFC exerted its anti-oxidative stress effect via VEGFR3 activation (Fig.  H-J). These data collectively indicated VEGFR3 protected Ang II-induced HK-2 cells through decreasing mitochondrial ROS production, inhibiting mitochondrial damage and reducing oxidative stress injury.
 

## VEGFR3 improves abnormal mitophagy by enhancing PARKIN mitochondrial translocation
Previous studies showed mitophagy machinery was impaired in response to Ang II [ , ]. To study the direct effect of Ang II on mitophagy in PTCs, we extracted mitochondrial fractions from HK-2 and detected the expression of SQSTM1 and LC3 in mitochondria to evaluate mitophagy levels. The result showed that Ang II exposure enhanced mitophagy in the initial stages, which was detected as early as 12 h, peaked at 24 h and dropped gradually at 36 h. As shown in Fig.  A, the ratio of LC3-II/LC3-I were dramatically decreased after Ang II treatment for 48 h. Notably, protein levels of LC3-II/LC3-I were downregulated concomitant with the increasing of SQSTM1 (Fig.  A-C). Based on the observation, the cells were treated with Ang II for 48 h in subsequent studies. HK-2 cells overexpression FLAG-VEGFR3 or Vector control were incubated with Ang II. Immunoblotting results observed that the protein levels of LC3-II/LC3-I in mitochondria were restored by overexpressing VEGFR3. Moreover, we noticed that FLAG-VEGFR3 HK-2 cells displayed decreased SQSTM1 protein levels upon Ang II treatment compared to FLAG-Vector cells (Fig.  D and E). Representative TEM images shown that overexpressed of VEGFR3 increased the mitophagosome formation and decreasing the accumulation of damaged mitochondria in Ang II-treated HK-2 cells (Fig.  F). We also performed immunofluorescence for Lysotracker and Mitotracker, which showed the same result with immunoblotting (Fig.  G and H). Since we observed that VEGFR3 restored mitophagy in Ang II-induced HK-2 cells, we asked which pathway of mitophagy could be affected by VEGFR3. HK-2 cells pretreated with VEGFR3 inhibitor MAZ51 following by Ang II incubation, the data showed that MAZ51 treatment resulted in comparable amounts of mitochondrial BNIP3 and FUNDC1. Notably, the levels of PARKIN in mitochondrial were significantly decreased by MAZ51 treatment, despite enhancement in levels of PINK1 (Fig.  I and K). Immunofluorescence result confirmed that the colocalization of PARKIN and TOMM20 was reduced by Ang II stimulation in HK-2 cells, while this colocalization was improved in cells pretreated with VEGFC. Consistent with the immunoblotting results, a further deterioration of colocalization of PARKIN and TOMM20 was observed with MAZ51 treatment (Fig.  J and L). Collectively, we found that VEGFR3 enhanced PARKIN mitochondrial translocation to improve abnormal mitophagy induced by Ang II. As mitophagy can ameliorate the deleterious consequences of mitochondrial dysfunction, proteins regulating PARKIN translocation could be useful drug targets for increasing mitochondrial quality control.
 

## VEGFR3 interacts with HSPA1L to alleviate oxidative stress injury by regulating PARKIN-dependent mitophagy in vitro
To identify downstream substrates of VEGFR3, we performed immunoprecipitation of VEGFR3 followed by mass spectrometry analysis. Since oxidative stress and establishment of protein localization to mitochondrion were significantly increased in the Ang II-induced hypertensive mice, we focused on proteins related to mitophagy and oxidative stress. Given that HSPA1L is the main sensor and regulator of cell oxidative stress, we were thus interested in the VEGFR3-HSPA1L module. The interaction between VEGFR3 and HSPA1L was confirmed by coimmunoprecipitation (Fig.  A). Previous study reported that HSPA1L is a regulator upstream of mitophagy [ ]. Therefore, we asked whether the regulation of mitophagy by VEGFR3 was modulated by HSPA1L. We transfected HK-2 cells with siRNAs to block the expression of HSPA1L. Indeed, the HK-2 cells overexpression FLAG-VEGFR3 restored the mitochondrial localization of PARKIN with Ang II stimulation, while knock-down of HSPA1L decreased the colocalization of PARKIN and TOMM20 to a similar degree to FLAG-Vector cells (Fig.  B and C). These results were verified by immunoblotting (Fig.  D and E). Moreover, si-HSPA1L aggravated Ang II-induced mitophagy deficiency and abolished the protective effect of VEGFR3 on mitophagy (Fig.  F and G). These data suggested that VEGFR3 interacted with HSPA1L to enhance PARKIN mitochondrial localization, whereby improved Ang II-induced mitophagy deficiency in HK-2 cells. To further investigate whether HSPA1L could regulate cell oxidative stress via mitophagy, 3-MA, a pharmacological mitophagy inhibitor was used. VEGFR3 overexpression enhanced mitophagy meanwhile inhibiting oxidative stress in the presence of Ang II (Fig.  H-L). Upon treated with si-HSPA1L, the changes of mitophagy proteins were no longer significant and the differences of oxidative stress-related proteins became smaller with no significant between Vector and VEGFR3 overexpressing cells. Moreover, 3-MA significantly enhanced oxidative stress levels in si-NC cells, which reached to a similar degree as that in si-HSPA1L cells. Taken together, these findings suggested VEGFR3 interacted with HSPA1L to alleviate oxidative stress injury by regulating PARKIN-dependent mitophagy.
 

## Crotonylation modification of HSPA1L at K130 by VEGFR3 is required for mitophagy regulation
Post-translational modifications are a common property for many proteins that can regulate mitophagy. Crotonylation, a posttranslational modification of lysine residues, plays an important role in regulating cellular functions in both physiological and pathological states. VEGFR3 and HSPA1L were co-expressed in HEK-293T cells, and HSPA1L was enriched by immunoprecipitation followed by immunoblotting with the specific antibody against crotonylated lysine sites. Indeed, overexpression of VEGFR3 promoted the crotonylation of HSPA1L (Fig.  A). Based on the mass spectrometry results from the UniProt database, the crotonylation modification at lysine 130 (K130) was identified in HSPA1L (Fig.  B). We generated point mutation at K130 site to arginine (Arg, R) (named K130R), and transfected the site mutation into HK-2 cells. The crotonylation of HSPA1L was validated by immunoblotting with a specific antibody against crotonylated lysine following the HSPA1L pulldown. Furthermore, the replacement of K130 with arginine dramatically reduced the crotonylation level of HSPA1L, indicating that K130 is convincingly the crotonylation site of HSPA1L (Fig.  C). Compared to the wild-type control (HA-WT), the mutation (HA-K130R) could significantly decrease the levels of PARKIN in mitochondria as well as reducing LC3 lipidation, accompany with the accumulation of SQSTM1 in mitochondria and aggravate cellular oxidative stress levels under Ang II stimulation (Fig.  D and E). K130 is evolutionarily conserved from Mus musculus to Homo sapiens (Fig.  F). We next assessed whether VEGFR3 induced mitophagy via crotonylated modification of HSPA1L at K130. We further overexpressed VEGFR3 together with HA-WT or HA-K130R incubated with Ang II. Notably, the K130R mutation significantly blocked VEGFR3-induced PARKIN and LC3 lipidation mitochondrial translocation, and enhanced the levels of oxidative stress proteins (Fig.  G and H). These data indicated that crotonylation of K130R in HSPA1L by VEGFR3 played a critical role for mitophagy.
 To further understand the details of the VEGFR3-AMPK interaction, we performed a domain-mapping experiment. VEGFR3 is composed of Ig-like (IGI), Protein kinase (KD) and Disordered (DIS) domains (Fig.  I). We transfected HEK-293 T cells with plasmids expressing a full-length VEGFR3 and one of various VEGFR3 truncated sequences together with plasmids expressing HA-HSPA1L. After 24 h, the cells were used for immunoblotting analysis. Interestingly, HSPA1L interacted predominantly with DIS domain, but negligibly with the IGI or KD domains (Fig.  J). These data indicated that HSPA1L interacted with VEGFR3 by binding to the DIS domain. Taken together, our findings have provided the first evidence that crotonylation of HSPA1L at K130 by VEGFR3 is critical for mitophagy machinery and response to oxidative stress in PTCs.

## The protective effect of VEGFR3 is attenuated in hypertensive mice with AAV-HSPA1L-K130R
To further confirm the protective effect of VEGFR3 on hypertensive kidney injury via crotonylation of HSPA1L at K130, AAV9 carried with HSPA1L-WT or HSPA1L-K130R were injected into the renal pelvis of mice. 2 weeks after AAV9 transfection, Ang II co-administered with VEGFC infusion via osmotic minipumps was used to establish an in vivo model in HSPA1L-WT and HSPA1L-K130R mice for another 2 weeks. The result showed HSPA1L-K130R mutation reversed the effect of VEGFR3 on lowering blood pressure (Fig.  A). Crucially, the protect effect of VEGFR3 on renal function were inhibited in HSPA1L-K130R mutant mice. Compared with HSPA1L-WT mice, urinary mRNA levels of KIM-1, as well as the serum levels of CRE and BUN were increased significantly in HSPA1L-K130R mutated mice (Fig.  B-D). Moreover, HE staining demonstrated that tubular injury scores were higher in HSPA1L-K130R mice compared with HSPA1L-WT mice (Fig.  E). As shown in Fig.  F, renal fibrosis was also deteriorated by K130R mutation. To evaluate whether the regulation of VEGFR3 on Ang II-induced mitophagy damage through crotonylation of HSPA1L at K130 in vivo, the levels of mitophagy proteins were measured. We noticed that the expression level of LC3 II/LC3 I was significantly decreased and the level of SQSTM1 was increased in mitochondrial lysates of renal cortex tissue in HSPA1L-K130R mice (Fig.  G and H). Furthermore, higher oxidative stress-related proteins (Fig.  I and J) and oxidative stress levels (Fig.  K and L) were observed in HSPA1L- K130R mice compared with HSPA1L-WT. Together, these results demonstrated that in hypertensive mice with AAV-HSPA1L-K130R the protective effect of VEGFC was weakened.
 

## Discussion
Here, we found that activation of VEGFR3 alleviated oxidative stress damage and restored mitophagy deficiency in PTCs caused by Ang II. In addition, we demonstrated that VEGFR3 improved abnormal mitophagy by enhancing PARKIN mitochondrial translocation. Moreover, we discovered that HSPA1L, which is an mitophagy-associated non-histone protein, was regulated by VEGFR3. We further uncovered the molecular mechanism by which VEGFR3 ameliorated hypertensive renal injury by regulating HSAP1L crotonylation. Mechanistically, we provided the first evidence that VEGFR3 disorder domain bound to HSPA1L, which was a target of VEGFR3-dependent crotonylation via K130 (Fig.  M). These observations highlight the importance of the VEGFR3-HSPA1L axis in overcoming oxidative stress injury induced by Ang II, which present a therapeutic target for addressing hypertension and its progression to CKD.
Recently, the concept of tubular-centric view of hypertensive nephropathy has emerged [ , , ]. PTCs are crucial for maintaining renal homeostasis which is critical for both tubular and glomerular functions [ , ]. However, there has been no obviously effective approach to prevent tubular cells loss from metabolic stresses. In the present study, we observed upregulation of VEGFR3 in the proximal tubule cells from mice with Ang II-induced hypertensive renal injury. Our RNA-sequencing analysis showed that Ang II activated the biological process of cellular response to oxidative stress-induced cell death in the kidney of hypertensive mice. These findings suggest that VEGFR3 may play a crucial role in mediating PTCs from oxidative stress and progressive fibrosis in Ang II-induced hypertensive nephropathy. Previous studies reported that the activation of VEGFR3 by VEGFC could protect kidney from inflammation and fibrosis [ , ]. However, the evidence supporting an important role of VEGFR3 in Ang II-induced hypertension from previous studies remain indirect because cause and effect relationship of high blood pressure and renal injury has not been established previously. Here, we demonstrated activation of VEGFR3 alleviated hypertensive renal injury before any effect on blood pressure, which indicated that the tissue benefits of VEGFR3 beyond blood pressure control alone, also including improvement in proteinuria, and reduction in oxidative stress and fibrosis. The improvement of oxidative stress damage of PTCs by VEGFR3, in turn, contribute to lower high blood pressure. Consistently, clinical trials have shown that RAAS blockers reduce urinary protein levels and demonstrated reno-protective effect through blood pressure-dependent and blood pressure-independent mechanisms, including through the attenuation of pro-inflammatory and profibrotic pathways [ , ].
Mitochondrial damage is a common feature of kidney diseases [ ]. Damaged mitochondria fail to maintain the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane and are the main source of ROS in cells [ ]. The renal tubules actively reabsorb filtered initial urine, which requires abundant energy, and the renal tubules are therefore rich in mitochondria [ ]. Damaged mitochondria fail to supply sufficient ATP, causing an energetic deficit, resulting in atrophy or dedifferentiation of kidney tubule cells [ ]. Previous studies have reported that Ang II, a major component of the RAAS, induced oxidative stress and inflammatory responses by stimulating the formation of ROS [ ]. In the present study we found that activation of VEGFR3 significantly depressed the production of mtROS induced by Ang II stimulation, while VEGFR3 inhibitor further increased mtROS. We also observed that VEGFR3 reduced oxidative stress-related markers including 3-nitrotyrosine and 8-oxo-dG, increased the antioxidant SOD1 activity in Ang II injured PTCs. These results suggested that VEGFR3 inhibits oxidative stress and CKD progression by restraining mtROS.
Mitophagy, a selective autophagic process that removes mitochondria is critical for maintaining mitochondrial quality control, promoting renal PTCs survival and preserving renal function during hypertensive nephropathy [ , ]. Mitophagy deficiency has been proposed to have a pathogenic role in various kidney diseases, including diabetic nephropathy, hypertension-associated kidney disease by increasing ROS production and causing mitochondrial damage [ , ]. Previous studies showed mitophagy machinery was impaired in response to Ang II [ , ]. In the present study, we found that Ang II exposure enhanced mitophagy in the initial stages and damaged mitophagy in the advanced stages. In agreement with our findings, studies in unilateral ureteric obstruction revealed that following stimulation with Ang II mitophagy was damaged in cultured HK-2 cells or primary PTCs [ ]. On the contrary, Du et al. showed Ang II induced mitophagy activity in macrophages [ ]. Nevertheless, all of these studies mentioned above demonstrated blockade of autophagy by conditional ATG5 deletion or Atg5 heterozygote deficiency, aggravated tissue damage induced by Ang II. In addition to the genetic models, pharmacological enhancement of mitophagy has also been shown to be beneficial. In diabetic kidney disease, CoQ10 administration restores mitophagy and improves kidney function [ ]. Our results demonstrated that mitophagy were impaired in both PTCs and kidney exposed to Ang II, which could be reversed by overexpression of VEGFR3 or exogenous VEGFC. In addition, we explored the specific signal transduction pathway regulated by VEGFR3 in response to hypertensive renal injury. We found that VEGFR3 enhanced mitophagy by affecting PARKIN, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, mitochondria localization. Furthermore, we demonstrated that PARKIN-dependent mitophagy played a renal protection role by attenuating oxidative stress injury in PTCs.
We further investigated the molecular mechanism of VEGFR3 in protecting PTCs from oxidative stress damage and identified an important role of VEGFR3-HSPA1L module in mediating mitophagy pathways in the kidney. HSPA1L, has 91% homology to HSPA1A, which is a protective protein whose expression is elevated after stress [ , ]. However, the function and regulation of HSPA1L are remain largely unknown. Previous studies reported that HSPA1L could selectively promote PARKIN translocation activity to damaged mitochondria without affected the levels of PINK1 protein accumulation [ , ]. Nevertheless, the detail molecular interaction mechanism is unclear. In this study, we conformed that HSPA1L enhanced PARKIN mitochondrial localization to alleviates mitophagy dysfunction induced by Ang II. Importantly, crotonylation modification at lysine 130 of HSPA1L, was identified by LC-MS/MS analysis and was confirmed by Co-IP assays. Studies have revealed that Kcr plays a critical role in certain pathophysiological processes by influencing protein structure, protein interactions, localization, and modulation of protein stability [ , , ]. It has been reported that regulating the modification of histone crotonylation contribute to delay the progression of kidney fibrosis [ ]. However, the mechanisms of non-histone protein crotonylation remain to be explored. Recently study has revealed that inhibiting the crotonylation of metabolic enzyme Cox4i1, leading to mitochondrial damage and the subsequent distal tubular cell apoptosis in AKI [ ]. Thus, different lysine modifications might exert diverse roles. In the present study, we firstly discovered that HSPA1L, which is a non-histone protein, crotonylation at K130 is regulated by VEGFR3. Interestingly, we found that K130 crotonylation is indispensable for enhancing the mitochondrial localization of PARKIN. Moreover, our studies showed that crotonylation of HSPA1L enhanced mitophagy and inhibiting oxidative stress damage induced by Ang II. Conversely, the K130R mutation of HSPA1L inhibits mitophagy by affecting the localization of PARKIN. In addition, the K130R mutation of HSPA1L sensitizes PTCs to oxidative stress damage.
Mechanistically, domain mapping experiment proved HSPA1L binding to VEGFR3 disordered domain (DIS) to mediate the interaction. Intrinsically DISs are defined by heterogeneous conformational ensembles, rather than a singular structure, comprise 37–50% of the human proteome [ ]. Despite the absence of a defined 3D structure, DISs are essential for cellular processes ranging from transcriptional control and cell signaling to subcellular organization [ ]. Yet the link between this sequence and molecular function is less well defined needs to be further investigated in the future. In vivo, we show that mutation of HSPA1L at K130 (K130R) aggravates mitophagy defects, subsequently leading to oxidative stress injury, thus ultimately enhancing the effect of hypertensive renal injury in K130R mice treated with VEGFC. Collectively, our study proposes a novel mechanism for elucidating the role of crotonylation regulation in Ang II-induced hypertensive renal injury.
In present, treatment of CKD resulting from hypertension is challenging, and the prognosis is difficult to determine [ ]. Our study identifies a potential target to treat kidney injury, besides the VEGFR3 mentioned above. Existing therapeutic agents for treating PTMs exhibit beneficial outcomes in diverse disease states, among which is kidney damage [ ]. It is promising to note that several agents targeted at epigenetic modifiers are currently in clinical application or in the trial phase, with some already showing encouraging results from retrospective analyses [ , ]. Nonetheless, most of what we know about these modifiers in relation to the kidneys comes from the negative impacts of agents [ ]. Here, our founding suggested that HSPA1L crotonylation at K130 provides a theoretical basis for the design of treatment options and targeted drugs. Moreover, VEGF inhibitors are the common antineoplastic agents which is drug class exhibits the strongest association with hypertension [ ]. Blood pressure is more difficult to control in patients with tumor complicated with hypertension due to renal function injury [ , , ]. The discoveries in our present study have unique clinical relevance and physiological significance.
Limitations of our work include that the effect of pharmacological VEGFR3 agonist and inhibitor were examined using a recombinant VEGFC protein or MAZ51, mice with a specific knockout of VEGFR3 in PTCs would be warranted to mitigate the risk of off-target effect. Although LC-MS/MS analysis identified the crotonylation modification at lysine 130 (K130) in HSPA1L interact with VEGFR3, we did not explore the roles of other Kcrs in this study. Future LC-MS/MS analysis, systematic proteomics profiling of lysine crotonylation in hypertensive kidney and related experiments are needed to better understand the correlational and causal relationships between different Kcrs during hypertensive kidney injury. Last, the mechanism by which VEGFR3 disorder domain interact with HSPA1L to enhanced the crotonylation levels of K130 was not investigated in the current study, further research is required.

## Conclusion
In summary, we have identified a novel regulatory mechanism whereby VEGFR3 alleviates Ang II-induced hypertensive renal injury by promoting PARKIN-dependent mitophagy via VEGFR3 disorder domain interact with HSPA1L to regulating crotonylation of HSPA1L at K130 site, thereby mitigating oxidative stress damage in PTCs. These observations demonstrate the potential utility of targeting VEGFR3 and HSPA1L-K130 crotonylation for treatment of hypertensive renal injury.
