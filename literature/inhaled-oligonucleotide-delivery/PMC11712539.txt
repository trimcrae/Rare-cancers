## Abstract
Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is increasingly recognized for its link to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), though the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. Histone lysine demethylase 6B (KDM6B) may either prevent or promote organ fibrosis, but its specific role in IPF is yet to be clarified. This study aimed to investigate the function and mechanisms of KDM6B in IPF and the exacerbating effects of OSA. We assessed KDM6B levels in lung tissues from IPF patients, IPF mouse models, and a dual‐hit model combining OSA‐associated intermittent hypoxia (IH) with bleomycin (BLM) or TGF‐β1. We evaluated pulmonary fibrosis, myofibroblast activation, and oxidative stress. KDM6B levels were elevated in lung tissues from IPF patients and BLM‐treated mice, as well as in TGF‐β1‐stimulated myofibroblasts. Importantly, IH significantly worsened BLM‐induced pulmonary fibrosis and TGF‐β1‐induced myofibroblast activation, further amplifying KDM6B expression both in vivo and in vitro. Inhibition of KDM6B reduced pulmonary fibrosis and decreased fibroblast activation and migration in IPF and dual‐hit models. Mechanistically, KDM6B inhibition led to decreased NOX4 expression and reduced oxidative stress. KDM6B plays a critical role in promoting pulmonary fibrosis and mediating the exacerbating effects of OSA on this condition. Our findings identify KDM6B as a novel potential therapeutic target for IPF.

Lysine‐specific demethylase 6B (KDM6B) is a potential therapeutic target for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)‐exacerbated IPF. The present findings suggest that KDM6B expression is significantly elevated in the lung tissues of IPF patients and bleomycin‐induced lung fibrosis mouse model. Intermittent hypoxia, a key clinical feature of OSA, exacerbates bleomycin (BLM)‐induced pulmonary fibrosis and further increases KDM6B levels. Inhibition of KDM6B effectively mitigates pulmonary fibrosis and reduces fibroblast activation and oxidative stress mediated by NADPH oxidases 4 (NOX4).

## Effect of KDM6B inhibition on BLM‐induced pulmonary fibrosis
To examine the therapeutic potential of KDM6B inhibition in PF, we administered the specific KDM6B inhibitor GSK‐J4 (10 mg/kg/day) intraperitoneally to BLM mice for 21 days. KDM6B inhibition notably mitigated body weight loss in the fibrosis models (Figure  ). H&E and Masson's trichrome staining displayed reduced collagen deposition in PF mice (Figure  ). Additionally, GSK‐J4 treatment significantly decreased the lung tissue HYP content and the expression of fibrosis markers, including FN1, α‐SMA, and COL1A1, and NOX4 expressions (Figure  ). These results indicate that KDM6B inhibition effectively ameliorates BLM‐induced PF and OS.

3.3

## 
KDM6B ameliorates lung fibroblast activation and NOX4 upregulation
During the initiation of PF, epithelial cell injury and inflammatory responses trigger the production of the pro‐fibrotic cytokine TGF‐β1, a key step in transitioning fibroblasts into myofibroblasts, which increases collagen synthesis in injured cells. To elucidate the role of KDM6B in this context, we treated the MLg with TGF‐β1 and observed elevated KDM6B mRNA and protein levels in treated cells compared with mock‐treated control cells (Figure  ). Notably, the H3K27 methylation level was decreased upon TGF‐β1 treatment (Figure  ), and this trend was reversed by the GSK‐J4 (Figure  ). TGF‐β1‐induced fibroblast activation was attenuated by GSK‐J4 treatment, as well (Figure  ). Also, GSK‐J4 diminished the fibroblast migration rate induced by TGF‐β1 (Figure  ). Furthermore, GSK‐J4 reduced the intracellular NOX4 expression and ROS levels stimulated by TGF‐β1 (Figure  ), suggesting that KDM6B inhibition can counteract TGF‐β1‐induced OS in lung fibroblasts and regulate the NOX4‐ROS axis in fibrosis.
 
3.4

## Exacerbating effect of IH on BLM‐induced PF in mice
In our study on the effects of IH on BLM‐induced PF, mice were intratracheally administered either BLM or saline and exposed to IH or normoxic conditions. Over the 21‐day experimental period, BLM‐treated mice exhibited significant weight loss, which was further exacerbated by IH (Figure  ). Histopathological examination on day 21 revealed fibrotic lesions in BLM‐treated mice, characterized by alveolar wall thickening, inflammatory cell infiltration, and collagen deposition, as shown by H&E and Masson's trichrome staining (Figure  ). Quantitative analysis confirmed these findings, with increased fibrosis area and HYP content (Figure  ). IH further intensified BLM‐induced fibrosis and elevated the expression of fibrosis markers, including Follistatin‐like 1 (FSTL1)—a novel biomarker associated with fibrosis , and NOX4 mRNA levels (Figure  ).
 
3.5

## 
IH‐aggravated TGF‐β1 effects on fibroblast activation and NOX4 expression
To assess the impact of IH on TGF‐β1‐induced fibroblast activation, MLg cells were co‐exposed to IH and TGF‐β1. The expression levels of fibrosis‐related genes and proteins were significantly upregulated in the TGF‐β1‐treated group, compared with the control group, with IH further enhancing these effects (Figure  ). Additionally, IH increased the activation and migratory capacity of TGF‐β1‐induced fibroblasts (Figure  ) and elevated NOX4 mRNA and ROS levels (Figure  ), indicating that IH synergistically exacerbates the effects of TGF‐β1.
 
3.6

## Therapeutic implications of KDM6B inhibition in IH‐exacerbated PF

Given the exacerbating effects of IH on BLM‐induced PF and TGF‐β1‐induced fibroblast activation, we further investigated KDM6B's role. Elevated KDM6B mRNA and protein levels in the BLM combined with the IH (IH + BLM) treatment group suggested its pathogenic involvement in vivo (Figure  ). Moreover, GSK‐J4 administration in a mouse model of IH‐exacerbated BLM‐induced PF resulted in increased body weight, reduced lung collagen deposition, and decreased HYP content (Figure  ). In IH + BLM mice, the fluorescence intensity of α‐SMA in lung tissues was significantly higher than in BLM‐induced PF mice. Inhibition of KDM6B with GSK‐J4 markedly reduced this intensity (Figure  ), suggesting that KDM6B inhibition may lower α‐SMA expression. Similar trends were observed in the transcription and protein levels of fibrotic markers (Figure  ). Furthermore, GSK‐J4 treatment led to a decrease in NOX4 mRNA levels, indicating KDM6B's role in the fibrotic mechanisms induced by IH (Figure  ).
 
3.7

## 
KDM6B inhibition attenuates IH‐aggravated TGF‐β1‐induced fibroblast activation, migration, and OS

To confirm KDM6B as a potential therapeutic target for the worsening of IH‐induced PF, we exposed the MLg cells to both IH and TGF‐β1. Inhibiting KDM6B significantly reduced the transcriptional and protein levels of fibrosis markers in mouse fibroblasts stimulated by this combination (Figure  ). MLg cells treated with GSK‐J4 under IH + TGF‐β1 condition exhibited notably lower migratory capacity compared with those exposed only to IH + TGF‐β1 (Figure  ), indicating that KDM6B inhibition mitigates the combined effects of IH and TGF‐β1 on fibroblast migration.
 Furthermore, GSK‐J4 treatment significantly decreased NOX4 expressions and ROS levels in cells exposed to IH and TGF‐β1 (Figure  ). These findings suggest that KDM6B inhibition can alleviate OS and NOX4 expression in MLg cells exposed to IH and TGF‐β1. By reducing fibroblast activation, migration, and OS, KDM6B inhibition could potentially provide a beneficial intervention for treating pulmonary fibrosis intensified by IH, especially in the context of BLM‐induced fibrosis (Figure  ).

## DISCUSSION
IPF is a chronic, progressive lung disease characterized by poor prognosis and a complex, poorly understood etiology. It likely results from intricate interactions among various cell types and signaling pathways. Current treatments for PF remain suboptimal. KDM6B has been implicated in the regulation of fibrosis across multiple organs, displaying both promoting and inhibiting effects that vary by organ. For example, KDM6B inhibition protects cardiac structure and collagen levels in mice after myocardial infarction. Conversely, in renal fibrosis, KDM6B inhibition is associated with increased ECM deposition, suggesting an anti‐fibrotic role. In our study, using a mouse model of BLM‐induced PF, we observed upregulated KDM6B expression (Figure  ). In vitro, fibroblast models under fibrotic conditions also showed increased KDM6B expressions following TGF‐β1 stimulation (Figure  ). GSK‐J4, a histone demethylase inhibitor, raises H3K27me3 levels by inhibiting KDM6B. , Our findings indicate that KDM6B plays a significant role in BLM‐induced PF and fibroblast activation, contributing to ECM deposition, and propose it as a potential therapeutic target (Figures  and ). The upregulation of KDM6B in lung tissues of IPF patients suggests its promotive role in lung fibrosis. Recent studies have employed transcriptomic analysis to investigate the differences between IPF patients and a control group. A total of 3499 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between the two groups, including KDM6B. The expression of KDM6B in the lungs of mice with bleomycin‐induced pulmonary fibrosis was markedly elevated and exhibited a positive correlation with fibronectin and α‐SMA. These findings further substantiate the assertion that KDM6B plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis and progression of IPF.
OS plays a crucial role in driving pulmonary fibrosis, with NOX4 being essential for fibrogenesis and myofibroblast tissue repair. Elevated OS levels have been confirmed in the lungs and blood of IPF patients. , Research highlights NOX4's specific role in mediating myofibroblast functions; TGF‐β1‐induced differentiation, ECM production, and contractility of lung myofibroblasts rely on NOX4‐generated ROS, particularly hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). This ROS production not only promotes myofibroblast differentiation and collagen deposition but also establishes a positive feedback loop through the TGF‐β1/Smad2/3 signaling pathway, contributing significantly to IPF development. In IPF patients, NOX4 expression correlates with profibrotic markers such as α‐SMA, FN1, and procollagen. TGF‐β1‐induced NOX4‐dependent ROS production enhances fibroblast migration and profibrotic myofibroblast phenotype. In vitro studies show that KDM6B upregulates NOX4 transcription in TGF‐β1‐exposed lung fibroblasts, leading to increased ROS levels. Conversely, inhibiting KDM6B reduces NOX4 expression and OS (Figure  ). KDM6B also influences the trimethylation of H3K27me3, impacting gene regulatory functions. It demethylates H3K27me3 at promoter regions, enhancing NOX4 expression and influencing cell proliferation and migration. Further investigation is needed to elucidate KDM6B's role in fibrosis through NOX4 and its effects on other lung cell types, such as epithelial cells.
Recent findings have indicated that modifications at other amino acid sites of histone H3 are also involved in PF. The disruptor of telomeric silencing 1‐like (DOT1L) is a characterized histone methyltransferase in mammals, which methylates lysine‐79 of histone H3 (H3K79) and participates in the epigenetic regulation of gene transcription. , , In addition to histone methyltransferases, another study has shown that histone deacetylase (HDAC) modification of histones is linked to the altered gene expression profiles in PF. Reduced HDAC3 levels in IPF fibroblasts lead to hyperacetylation of H3K9 and increased expression of COL1A1, ACTA2, and p21, suggesting that HDAC3 connects epigenetic mechanisms to the fibrotic microenvironment.
OSA characterized by periodic breathing pauses during sleep, leads to IH, hypercapnia, and fragmented sleep. Hypoxia is a significant inducer of fibrosis or fibrogenesis, especially in age‐related diseases. , , Animal models demonstrate that IH increases OS and exacerbates BLM‐induced PF, through a yet unknown mechanism. Our study confirms that IH worsens BLM‐induced PF in mice, likely via OS pathways. However, mice exposed to IH did not show any significant fibrotic changes (Figure  ). Supporting evidence from other studies indicates that IH + BLM mice exhibited more severe PF symptoms, with reduced antioxidant enzyme expressions, such as superoxide dismutase and glutathione peroxidase. One study found that on day 21, the IH + BLM group had more pronounced fibrosis than the BLM group, although no differences were noted on day 4. Additionally, ROS and HIF‐1α levels were higher in the IH + BLM group, correlating positively with HYP content. While IH increased ROS in lung tissue, it did not lead to significant fibrosis, possibly due to lower ROS levels compared with the BLM group. Other research suggests that IH can result in weight loss and heightened PF, with increased COL1A1 and COL3A1 mRNA levels observed after 28 days of IH exposure in mouse lung tissue, as well as an increased protein deposition in the lung parenchyma. While slight variations in hypoxic conditions exist, findings consistently show that IH exacerbates BLM‐induced PF and is associated with OS, with short‐term IH alone not causing fibrosis. These results indicate that the development of pulmonary fibrosis due to IH likely depends on the duration of exposure. Overall, while IH enhances OS and promotes fibrosis progression, the degree of impact may be influenced by the length and severity of IH exposure.
Cell experiments in the context of IH and IPF are limited. In our in vitro model, exposing pulmonary fibroblasts to both IH and TGF‐β1 resulted in increased transcription of fibrosis markers and enhanced cell migration (Figure  ), aligning with in vivo findings that indicate IH exacerbates PF symptoms. In vitro experiments showed that IH caused an increase in ROS, but no statistically significant difference in NOX4 transcript levels compared with controls, and we considered that one of the reasons for this is that although NOX4 is an important ROS‐generating enzyme under IH conditions, the source of intracellular ROS may not be limited to NOX4. In addition, the frequency, duration, and degree of intermittent hypoxia, as well as the timing of the experimental assays, may affect NOX4 expression. However, the degree of NOX4 and ROS elevation caused by IH alone appears insufficient for significant cellular activation and migration. Nevertheless, IH may accelerate the progression of PF under certain conditions.
KDM6B emerges as a potential therapeutic target for IPF patients comorbid with OSA. Our study demonstrated that IH significantly upregulated KDM6B and NOX4 levels in PF (Figures  , and ) and inhibition of KDM6B alleviated the exacerbation of BLM‐induced PF pathology in mice exposed to IH (Figure  ), suggesting a crucial role for KDM6B in this context. Additionally, IH appears to activate NOX4 transcription induced by BLM, leading to increased OS. Inhibition of KDM6B mitigated this increase, alleviating further progression of fibrosis, fibroblast activation, migration, and OS (Figures  and ). OS is a hallmark of both IH and BLM exposures. , It is hypothesized that one pathway through which OSA worsens IPF involves OS. Studies show that IH can upregulate NOX4 expression in various tissues linked to diseases like pulmonary arterial hypertension and diabetes. , , Furthermore, TGF‐β1 has been shown to induce NOX4 expression in multiple cell types, and knockdown of NOX4 using siRNA has been demonstrated to reduce BLM‐induced PF in mice. This suggests that NOX4 is a potential therapeutic target for IPF, particularly in patients with OSA. Our research highlights the need to understand the intricate mechanisms underlying PF and the potential for targeted therapies.
In conclusion, our study demonstrates that IH exacerbates BLM‐induced PF in vivo, with IH further activating the transcription and expression of KDM6B in lung tissues. Inhibiting KDM6B mitigates the combined fibrotic effects of IH and BLM, suggesting that KDM6B might be involved in a key mechanism by which IH exacerbates PF. The TGF‐β1‐KDM6B‐NOX4 axis, influenced by IH, elevates OS, promoting fibroblast activation and ECM secretion, which leads to pulmonary fibrosis. By inhibiting KDM6B, we observed reduced NOX4 levels and OS, resulting in decreased fibroblast activation and migration, ultimately alleviating PF pathology. Further research is necessary to clarify the connections between TGF‐β1 and KDM6B, as well as KDM6B's role in upregulating NOX4. Future studies using KDM6B gene knockout models and cellular overexpression will help confirm KDM6B's involvement in PF and its exacerbation by IH.
