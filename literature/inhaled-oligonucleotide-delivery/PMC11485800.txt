The terms on which this article has been published allow the posting of the Accepted Manuscript in a repository by the author(s) or with their consent.Aim: To assess mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) as carriers for HIF-1α siRNA-loaded nanoparticles (NPs) for targeted therapy of experimental choroidal neovascularization (CNV).
Materials & methods: A poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA)-core/lipid-shell hybrid NP was designed. The transfection efficacy of MSCs with the hybrid NPs was assessed. Mice were intravenously injected with MSCs after laser photocoagulation and CNV was assessed at 7 days post-injection.
Results & conclusion: The transfection efficiency of hybrid NPs into MSCs was 72.7%. HIF-1α mRNA expression in 661w cells co-cultured with MSC-hybrid-siRNA NPs was significantly lower. Intravenous delivery of MSC-hybrid-siRNA NPs greatly reduced CNV area and length. Intravenous injection of MSC-hybrid-siRNA NPs achieved therapeutic efficacy in reducing CNV area. The MSC-mediated homing enabled targeted inhibition of ocular angiogenesis.

3.2.

## Isolation & culture of MSCs
Primary MSCs were isolated from mouse bone marrow and cultured. After initial seeding, irregular round or polygonal cells were observed, forming colonies by days 1∼3. By day 9, cells reached 80% confluence and were passaged. Passaged MSCs exhibited a uniform spindle-shaped morphology. The cultured MSCs demonstrated osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation potential, forming calcified nodules after 19 days in osteogenic medium ( B) and intracellular lipid droplets after 7 days in adipogenic medium ( C). Undifferentiated control MSCs grew densely without evidence of calcium deposits or lipid accumulation ( A).
 Flow cytometry confirmed the MSC phenotype with positive expression of CD105 and CD90 and negative expression of hematopoietic markers CD34 and CD45 ( D). MSCs are predominantly in the G1 stage of the cell cycle (84.76%) ( E).

3.3.

## The effect of HIF-1 α siRNA NPs on proliferation & apoptosis of MSCs
The molar ratio of cationic N/P ratio could influence the proliferation and apoptosis of MSCs. The CCK-8 test results revealed that there was no difference in proliferation when the N/P ratio was less than 5. However, at N:P ratios of 8 (N/P 8:1) and 10 (N/P 10:1), a significant decrease in the proliferation was observed to be 1.44 ± 0.05 and 1.31 ± 0.14, p < 0.01; A). The cell apoptosis results by flow cytometry were consistent with the CCK8 results. The apoptotic cells were obvious in the N/P 8:1 and N/P 10:1 group ( B). Therefore, the N:P ratios of 5 was applied in the following experiments.
 
3.4.

## In vitro HIF siRNA transfection of MSCs by hybrid-siRNA NPs
To mimic in vivo conditions, hybrid-siRNA NP uptake by MSCs was evaluated in the presence of serum using Fam-labeled siRNA at a final siRNA concentration of 100 nM. Blank MSCs and naked siRNA showed minimal fluorescent signal, likely due to serum nuclease degradation of unprotected siRNA ( A). In contrast, strong Fam fluorescence was observed in MSCs treated with hybrid-siRNA NPs, indicating enhanced transfection efficiency compared with naked siRNA. Transfection with Lip 2000 also produced bright fluorescence but required serum-free conditions. Flow cytometry quantification demonstrated hybrid-siRNA NPs achieved a 72.7% transfection efficiency in MSCs, comparable to Lip 2000 (79.7%) ( B).
 To identify the characteristics of transfected MSCs, HIF-1α mRNA levels in MSCs were quantified by real-time PCR. Compared with the blank group, there was no significant changes of HIF-1α expression in both the hybrid-siRNA (0.90 ± 0.24 vs. 1.02 ± 0.24, p = 0.437) and Lip2000-siRNA (0.80 ± 0.23 vs 1.02 ± 0.24, p = 0.653) treatment groups. The phenotype and cell cycle of transfected MSCs did not change. Flow cytometry confirmed the transfected MSC phenotype still showed positive expression of CD105 and CD90, and negative expression of CD34 and CD45. MSCs were still mainly in the G1 stage of the cell cycle (92.36%) ( ).
 
3.5.

## In vitro suppression of HIF-1α mRNA expression & the pathway underlying the effects of MSCs loaded with hybrid SiRNA NPS on hypoxic 661W cells
661W cells exposed to hypoxic conditions (100 μm CoCl2) were divided into four experimental groups:Control: 661W cells alone.
Blank: Co-culture with unmodified MSCs.
Free-siRNA: Co-culture with MSCs transfected with siRNA only.
Hybrid-siRNA: Co-culture with MSCs loaded with HIF-1α siRNA hybrid NPs.
Lip2000-siRNA: Co-culture with MSCs loaded with Lip 2000-complexed HIF-1α siRNA.

After 48 h of co-culture, HIF-1α mRNA levels in 661W cells were quantified by real-time PCR. Compared with the control group, HIF-1α expression was significantly reduced in both the hybrid-siRNA (0.62 ± 0.1 vs. 1.00 ± 0.09, p = 0.001) and Lip2000-siRNA (0.46 ± 0.02 vs 1.00 ± 0.09, p < 0.001) treatment groups. In contrast, co-culture with unmodified (blank group) and transfected with siRNA (free-siRNA group) MSCs did not alter HIF-1α mRNA levels compared with control (0.91 ± 0.09 vs. 1.00 ± 0.09, 1.07 ± 0.15 vs 1.00 ± 0.09, p > 0.05). The extent of HIF-1α knockdown was comparable between the hybrid-siRNA and Lip2000-siRNA groups (p = 0.053) ( A).
 The exact mechanism(s) underlying the effects of MSCs on 661W cells remains unclear. 661W cells were exposed to hypoxic conditions and non-contacting co-cultured with MSCs isolated from EGFP Reporter mice in different groups. After 24 h, all groups showed EGFP fluorescent signal in 661W cells, likely due to the secretion of MSC cells. Cy5 fluorescence was observed in 661W cells treated with MSCs loaded with HIF-1α siRNA hybrid NPs and Lip 2000-complexed HIF-1α siRNA, indicating effective transfection. ( B). Connexin 43 (Cx43) is a transmembrane protein to form gap junctions effecting direct intercellular communication. In the Lip2000-siRNA group, Cx43 protein expression increased in 661W cells (0.096 ± 0.016 vs 0.061 ± 0.013, P = 0.021), indicating the importance of Cx43 channel function in Lip 2000- HIF-1α siRNA in intercellular transfer ( C).

3.6.

## In vivo targeted noninvasive treatment of CNV by MSCs loaded with hybrid-siRNA NPs
A laser-induced CNV mouse model was used to evaluate the in vivo therapeutic effect of intravenous MSC-hybrid-siRNA NPs ( A). Fluorescent choroidal flat mounts revealed overlap of DiD-labeled MSCs with isolectin B4-stained CNV lesions, indicating MSC homing to the neovascular sites ( A).
 Intravenous MSCs loaded with different siRNA formulations were divided into six experimental groups:▪Control: intravenous saline alone.
▪Naked-MSCs: intravenous blank MSCs alone.
▪MSCs-siRNA: intravenous MSCs transfected with siRNA only.
▪MSCs-hybrid NPs: intravenous MSCs loaded with hybrid NPs only.
▪MSCs-Lip2000-siRNA: intravenous MSCs loaded with Lip 2000-complexed HIF-1α siRNA.
▪MSCs-Hybrid-siRNA: intravenous MSCs loaded with HIF-1α siRNA hybrid NPs.

Quantitative analysis showed a significant reduction in CNV area in eyes treated with MSCs-hybrid-siRNA compared with saline controls (66003.1 ± 20462.03 μm2 vs. 110938.97 ± 13792.7 μm2, p = 0.04). Blank MSCs, MSCs-siRNA, MSCs-hybrid NPs and MSCs-Lip2000-siRNA groups showed no significant therapeutic effect compared with control group (122363.85 ± 18574.71 μm2; 124537.87 ± 25685.71 μm2; 92554.18 ± 54419.58 μm2, p > 0.05) ( B & C), although there was a trend toward a decreased CNV area in MSCs-Lip2000-siRNA group. H&E-stained retinal sections confirmed the flat mount findings, with decreased CNV length only in the MSC-hybrid-siRNA group (107.27 ± 10.82 μm vs. 186.12 ± 41.78 μm, p = 0.021; A & B). However, no significant difference was observed in CNV thickness (38.85 ± 12.22 μm vs. 55.27 ± 29.7 μm, p = 0.158) ( A & B). Further analysis showed that HIF-1α mRNA in CNV lesions following MSCs-hybrid-siRNA treatment was reduced compared with saline controls (0.65 ± 0.07 vs. 1.01 ± 0.14, p = 0.031) ( C).
 
3.7.

## The biosafety of MSCs loaded with hybrid-siRNA NPs via intravenous injection
No significant pathological changes were observed in H&E-stained sections of major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys) (Supplementary Figure S2A). Additionally, no fluorescent signal from DiD-labeled MSCs was detected in these tissues (Supplementary Figure S2B), suggesting the MSC-hybrid-siRNA NPs did not accumulate in off-target organs. Overall, systemic safety evaluations revealed no obvious detrimental effects of intravenous MSC-hybrid-siRNA administration on the health and organ morphology of the mice.

## Discussion
HIF transcription factors are attractive therapeutic targets for co-regulating multiple downstream angiogenic pathways. Previously, Zhang et al. demonstrated that intravitreal injection of HIF-1α shRNA-loaded PLGA NPs inhibited experimental CNV [ ]. However, potential surgical risks and retinal toxicity remain limitations of intravitreal delivery. Hou et al. reported the ability of systemically administered MSCs to specifically home to CNV lesions without recruitment to other organs, which was also observed in the current study [ ]. We previously showed that MSCs carrying HIF-1α siRNA-loaded PLGA NPs could suppress HIF-1α expression in hypoxic RPE cells in vitro [ ]. However, low drug loading, high production costs and lack of in vivo data hindered the translational potential [ ]. Therefore, this study aimed to optimize an MSC-mediated biodegradable hybrid nanoparticle system for targeted delivery of HIF-1α siRNA and investigate its efficacy in both in vitro and in vivo CNV models.
siRNA delivery remains a major challenge for clinical translation due to its hydrophilicity and negative charge [ ]. Naked siRNA can be degradated by serum nuclease. NPs could be used to protect siRNA and increase the drug carrying capacity of MSCs. PLGA, a biodegradable and non-toxic polymer approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 1969 [ ], has been widely used in drug delivery systems (DDS) [ ] and explored for various biomedical applications [ ]. Our previous study showed that PLGA NPs loaded with HIF-1α siRNA (314.1 nm diameter) could effectively silence target gene expression in vitro, but suffered from low encapsulation efficiency and high production costs [ ]. To overcome these limitations and enable in vivo application, we designed a lipid-polymer hybrid NP system comprising a PLGA core and a cationic lipid shell to enhance siRNA loading via electrostatic interactions. This approach reduced the amount of siRNA needed per formulation to 20 μg, compared with 100 μg in our previous study [ ]. However, it has been shown that different N/P ratio influences the efficiency of the gene delivery system and cytotoxicity, and the optimal N/P ratio differs between cell lines [ , ]. Therefore, it is important to select an optimal N/P ratio. In this study, it showed the highest transfection efficiency and relatively lower cytotoxicity at an N/P ratio of 5. The hybrid NPs achieved a transfection efficiency of 72.7%, surpassing that of PLGA NPs alone (67.3 ± 5.2) [ ] and comparable to the commercial reagent Lip 2000, while avoiding its cytotoxicity. The concept of multi-functional nanoplatforms for co-delivery has gained traction [ ], and our hybrid system offers a versatile platform for incorporating diverse payloads [ ]. The PLGA core provides structural stability and facilitates cellular uptake. In vitro, co-culture of 661W cells with MSCs loaded with hybrid siRNA NPs significantly reduced HIF-1α mRNA levels by 40%, consistent with our previous findings [ ]. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of our Trojan horse-inspired biomimetic delivery system and warrant further in vivo investigation.
Intercellular transfer has been shown to occur through direct cell-cell contacts (i.e., tunneling nanotubules (TNTs)) or through microvesicles (including Evs.) from donor cells [ ]. In this study, these hybrid siRNA NPs had no remarkable toxicity effect on MSCs without affecting MSC as carrier cells. MSCs loaded with hybrid siRNA NPs has little effect on the HIF-1α mRNA levels, cell cycle and surface marker expression of MSCs. Cellular internalization is one of the techniques used for drug loading. Drug-encapsulated NPs could be encompassed through endocytosis. Then, the delivery of MSCs with drug is facilitated through an evasion process [ ]. Panyam et al. first demonstrated that NPs formulated with PLGA can leave the endosomal portion [ ]. Pascucci et al. showed that liposomal NP can been used to deliver therapeutic cargos to MSCs through endocytosis and release their cargos [ ]. Much evidence suggest that the positive effects of stem cell therapy may be mediated by the paracrine mechanism of MSCs, such as nanoscale microvesicles [ ]. We found that in the MSCs transfected by hybrid NPs and Lip 2000 groups, 661W cells were observed EGFP+ secretion co-localized with loaded siRNA from MSCs through non-contact co-culture. Gap junctions are specialized intercellular channels formed called connexins. Cx43 is a predominant gap junction protein [ ]. Up-regulation of Cx43 could be observed only in 661W cells co-cultured with MSCs loaded the Lip 2000-siRNA. Based on our results, in vitro, the interactions between 661W cells and MSCs transfected by hybrid NPs was the paracrine pathway, while MSCs transfected by Lip 2000 was both the paracrine pathway and gap junction.
The current study demonstrated that intravenous administration of MSCs loaded with hybrid-siRNA NPs significantly inhibited CNV area by 40.5% and HIF-1α mRNA expression in a laser-induced murine model without detectable organ toxicity. The MSC-based systemic delivery vector provided a less invasive therapeutic approach, possessing both active targeting to CNV lesions and anti-angiogenic bioactivity. Recent advances in NP delivery systems have enabled systemic administration with enhanced treatment effects, such as photo-targeted [ ] and cell membrane-cloaked biomimetic NPs [ ]. However, synthetic nanocarriers can exhibit “non-self” properties resulting in immune clearance by the reticuloendothelial system [ ]. Physiological barriers also hinder the efficient penetration and delivery of NPs [ ]. In contrast, endogenous cells like MSCs possess natural membranes recognized as “self”, allowing them to evade biological barriers and minimize immunogenicity when carrying therapeutic cargo [ , ]. MSCs are also highly responsive to hypoxic cues present in ischemic CNV lesions [ ]. Furthermore, allogeneic MSCs can be safely delivered without triggering a major immune reaction [ ]. Interestingly, although Lip2000 transfected MSCs can inhibit the expression of HIF-1α mRNA in vitro, it did not seem to have a significant therapeutic effect on CNV in vivo. Lip2000 is a cationic liposome based transient transfection reagent, peak transient expression of which is generally seen 24–72 h following transfection [ ]. Our previous studies have found that the effect of MSCs loaded with HIF-1α siRNA PLGA NPs can last for 7 days [ ]. In this study, CNV was observed 7 days after intravenous administration of MSCs. It was demonstrated that Lip2000 mediated siRNA transport can lead to increased apoptosis in endothelial cells [ ]. Thus, the ineffective transfection of Lip2000 may be related to its short duration of action or alter MSCs function. Further research was needed.
Here we confirmed the ability of MSCs to specifically migrate to CNV without accrual in other organs. Although MSCs alone slightly increased CNV area, the difference was not statistically significant, consistent with prior reports [ ]. Hou et al. also demonstrated the feasibility of MSCs as Trojan horse vectors by using PEDF gene transduced MSCs [ ]. Notably, nanoparticle-loaded live cells can achieve higher accumulation at target sites compared with cell membrane-coated NPs, likely due to more active trafficking of intact cells to inflammatory regions [ ]. Integration of advanced nanomaterials with cell-based platforms can amplify the overall therapeutic impact by synergizing the strengths of both strategies while overcoming their individual limitations.
Despite the promising efficacy observed in this study, several limitations should be addressed in future investigations. While the laser-induced Bruch's membrane photocoagulation model is widely used to simulate CNV, it primarily mimics the chronic pathology of wet AMD. However, CNV can arise from various etiologies, including pathological myopia, trauma and infectious diseases, necessitating the evaluation of this therapeutic approach in other relevant experimental models [ ]. Furthermore, the mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of MSC-based nanodrug carriers remain to be fully elucidated. Additional studies are needed to unravel the molecular pathways involved and establish optimal clinical dosing regimens in vivo. Scalability poses another challenge for the clinical translation of cell-based therapies. The development of standardized protocols for large-scale production of MSC-nanoparticle systems is crucial. Exploring innovative strategies to directly load NPs into cells in vivo could potentially circumvent manufacturing bottlenecks and streamline the translation process.

5.

## Conclusion
A HIF-1α siRNA-loaded PLGA@lipid hybrid NP system was developed and demonstrated enhanced cellular uptake by MSCs without significantly altering their phenotype. Exploiting the homing ability of MSCs, systemic administration of these hybrid siRNA-loaded MSCs inhibited pathological angiogenesis in a laser-induced CNV murine model. Further studies elucidating the mechanisms governing MSC migration and therapeutic efficacy could accelerate the clinical translation of this targeted nanomedicine approach for ocular neovascular diseases.
