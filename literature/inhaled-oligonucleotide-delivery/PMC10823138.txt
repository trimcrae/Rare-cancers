## Purpose
The roles and mechanisms of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) in T helper 2 (Th2) differentiation from allergic asthma are poorly understood. We aimed to explore a novel lncRNA, LincR-protein phosphatase 2 regulatory subunit B' gamma (PPP2R5C), in Th2 differentiation in a mouse model of asthma.

## Methods
LincR-PPP2R5C from RNA-seq data of CD4+ T cells of asthma-like mice were validated and confirmed by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, northern blotting, nuclear and cytoplasmic separation, and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Lentiviruses encoding LincR-PPP2R5C or shRNA were used to overexpress or silence LincR-PPP2R5C in CD4+ T cells. The interactions between LincR-PPP2R5C and PPP2R5C were explored with western blotting, chromatin isolation by RNA purification assay, and fluorescence resonance energy transfer. An ovalbumin-induced acute asthma model in knockout (KO) mice (LincR-PPP2R5C KO, CD4 conditional LincR-PPP2R5C KO) was established to explore the roles of LincR-PPP2R5C in Th2 differentiation.

## Results
LncR-PPP2R5C was significantly higher in CD4+ T cells from asthmatic mice ex vivo and Th2 cells in vitro. The lentivirus encoding LincR-PPP2R5C suppressed Th1 differentiation; in contrast, the short hairpin RNA (shRNA) lentivirus decreased LincR-PPP2R5C and Th2 differentiation. Mechanistically, LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency suppressed the phosphatase activity of the protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) holocomplex, resulting in a decline in Th2 differentiation. The formation of an RNA-DNA triplex between LincR-PPP2R5C and the PPP2R5C promoter enhanced PPP2R5C expression and activated PP2A. LincR-PPP2R5C KO and CD4 conditional KO decreased Th2 differentiation, airway hyperresponsiveness and inflammatory responses.

## Conclusions
LincR-PPP2R5C regulated PPP2R5C expression and PP2A activity by forming an RNA-DNA triplex with the PPP2R5C promoter, leading to Th2 polarization in a mouse model of acute asthma. Our data presented the first definitive evidence of lncRNAs in the regulation of Th2 cells in asthma.

## INTRODUCTION
Asthma is the most common chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by airway inflammation, airway hyperresponsiveness, and reversible airflow obstruction. The symptoms of asthma include episodes of cough, wheezing, shortness of breath, and chest tightness. Worldwide, asthma was reported to affect estimated 262 million people in 2019 and cause 461,000 deaths. Allergic asthma is the most common asthma phenotype commonly associated with airway type 2 immune responses mediated by T helper 2 (Th2) cells, eosinophils, mast cells, basophils, group 2 innate lymphoid cells, and immunoglobulin E (IgE)-producing B cells. 
Multiple recent studies have demonstrated how epigenetic mechanisms contribute to allergic asthma and type 2 immune responses. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are newly emerging mediators of epigenetic factors; lncRNAs are transcribed RNA molecules > 200 nucleotides in length that lack protein-coding potential. The levels of several lncRNAs are remarkably different in the peripheral blood of asthmatic patients compared to those of healthy individuals and correlate with imbalances in key transcription factors (TFs) and cytokines; these lncRNAs act as biomarkers for phenotypes of asthma. A few studies have implied that lncRNAs participate in asthma pathogenesis by affecting macrophages, fibroblasts, or epithelial cells. 
In vitro, lncRNAs have been shown to modulate T-cell development, differentiation, and functions. These studies analyzed expression patterns of lncRNAs and functions of lncRNAs in specific inflammatory cells or toward certain molecules; however, direct evidence for specific functions of lncRNAs in Th2-skewed immunity in allergic asthma is limited. Therefore, lncRNAs in Th2 cells and asthma require additional specialized studies.
Previously, we have explored the profiles of microRNAs and lncRNAs in CD4+ T cells from an asthma model using next-generation sequencing, indicating the roles of lncRNAs in Th2 differentiation and asthma pathogenesis. Protein phosphatase 2 regulatory subunit B' gamma (PPP2R5C) belongs to the protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) regulatory subunit B family. PP2A has emerged as a key regulator of T-lymphocyte differentiation ; in particular, PP2A is required in Th1, Th2, Th9, Th17, and regulatory T (Treg) cells. Herein, we validated a novel lncRNA, LincR-PPP2R5C, which was highly expressed in asthma CD4+ T cells ex vivo and Th2 cells in vitro. Moreover, our study of knockout (KO) and CD4 conditional KO (cKO) mice provided definitive evidence for the role of LincR-PPP2R5C in allergic asthma, which was linked with the modulation of PP2A activity during Th2 cell differentiation.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Animals
Female C57BL/6J mice aged 6–8 weeks and weighing 20–25 g were purchased from the laboratory animal center, Nanjing Medical University (Nanjing, China) and housed as specific pathogen-free mouse colonies. LincR-PPP2R5C KO (LincR-PPP2R5C−/−, KO) and LincR-PPP2R5Cfloxed mice on the C57BL/6 background were generated by Cyagen Biosciences (#KOCMS170904JN1 and #TOS170904JN1; Santa Clara, CA, USA) as described in . CD4cre+/− mice were a gift from professor Xiaoming Wang and were housed and bred as specific pathogen-free colonies.
All animal experiments were performed according to the guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (Ministry of Health, Beijing, China, 1998) and guidelines of the Laboratory Animal Ethical Commission of Nanjing Medical University. All experimental protocols were evaluated and approved by the Animal Ethical Committee of Nanjing Medical University (approval number 20170911).

## Antibodies and reagents
Anti-GATA3 (ab199428), anti-T-bet (ab91103), anti-PP2A α+β (ab32065), and anti-PPP2R5C antibodies (ab94633) were purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Anti-PP2A-Cα/β (sc-80665), anti-p-PP2A-Cα/β (Tyr307) (sc-12615), anti-methyl-PP2A-Cα/β (sc-81603), and anti-demethylated-PP2A-C antibodies (sc-13601) were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Santa Cruz, CA, USA). DT-061 (S8774) was purchased from Selleck (Princeton, NJ, USA). Okadaic acid (OA; O9381, used at 10 nM) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA).

## Establishment of an acute asthma model
A 23-day model of acute asthma was established following a previously described protocol. Mice were sensitized with 4 mL/kg ovalbumin (OVA)-aluminium hydroxide suspension (2 mg/kg OVA and 80 mg/kg aluminium hydroxide; intraperitoneal) on Days 0, 7, and 14; anaesthetized; and challenged with 4 mg/mL OVA solution intra-nasally (administration of 12.5 μL per nostril) on Days 20, 21, and 22. Measurements were performed at 18 to 24 hours after the last challenge on Day 23

## CD4+ T-Cell purification
Mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of a mixture of 10 mg/kg xylazine (MTC Pharmaceuticals, Cambridge, ON, Canada) and 200 mg/kg ketamine hydrochloride (Rogar/STB, London, ON, Canada). Anesthetized mice were sacrificed by cervical dissociation. The spleen was removed and ground, and a single-cell suspension was prepared. CD4+ T cells from the spleen were sorted using CD4 (L3T4) microbeads (130-049-201; Miltenyi Biotec, Gaithersburg, MD, USA). Briefly, single-cell suspensions were incubated with CD4 (L3T4) microbeads, and magnetically labelled cells were flushed and collected.

## CD4+ T-cell differentiation in vitro

Naive CD4+ T cells were sorted from the spleen and lymph nodes by magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS) using a naive CD4+ T-cell isolation kit (130-104-453; Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany). Briefly, single-cell suspensions were incubated with biotin-antibody cocktails, which depleted non-T cells and memory CD4+ T cells. Intact naive CD4+ T lymphocytes were plated on 96-well plates precoated with 5 μg/mL anti-CD3e (85-16-0031-85; eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA) and 2 μg/mL anti-CD28 (16-0281-85; eBioscience). For Th1 polarization, naive CD4+ T cells were further stimulated with 20 ng/mL mouse interleukin (IL)-2 (96-212-12; PeproTech, Offenbach, Germany), 50 ng/mL mouse IL-12 (96-210-12; PeproTech), and 10 μg/mL anti-IL-4 (16-7041-85; eBioscience). For Th2 polarization, naive CD4+ T cells were stimulated with 20 ng/mL mouse IL-2 (212-12; PeproTech), 200 ng/mL mouse IL-4 (214-14; PeproTech), 10 μg/mL anti-interferon (IFN)-γ (85-16-7311-85), and 10 μg/mL anti-IL-12 (85-16-7123-85; eBioscience). Half of the volume of complete RPMI-1640 medium (16000-044; Gibco, Billings, MT, USA) containing 10% FBS (Gibco) was replaced with fresh medium to maintain the cytokine environment. A lentivirus (Blank, Enhancer, scramble, or Inhibitor) provided by RiboBio Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China) was added on Day 1 (multiplicity of infection, MOI = 50), and CD4+ T cells were harvested or analyzed on Day 5.

## Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)
FISH assays were performed using a RiboTM fluorescent in situ hybridization kit and RiboTM lncRNA FISH probe mix (RiboBio Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Briefly, cells seeded on chamber slides were fixed in 4% formaldehyde for 10 minutes and then washed with PBS. The cells were incubated with 20 μmol/L lncRNA FISH probe mix at 37°C overnight. After washing, the FISH preparations were counterstained with DAPI and observed by confocal microscopy using appropriate fluorescence filter sets (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). A lncRNA probe labelled with Cy3 was designed and synthesized by RiboBio Co., Ltd., and RiboTM U6 and RiboTM 18S were used as the reference controls for subcellular localization of the lncRNA.

## Chromatin isolation by RNA purification (ChIRP) assays
A Magna ChIRP RNA interactome kit (Millipore) was used for ChIRP assays according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 2 × 107 EL4 cells were cross-linked with 1% glutaraldehyde for 10 minutes, ultrasonically sheared into 100- to 200-bp DNA fragments in a 4°C water bath and hybridized with biotinylated DNA probes for 4 hours at 37°C; the samples were divided into odd and even groups. Next, the complexes were recovered using streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads. Finally, RNA-associated DNA was eluted, purified from the beads by treatment with RNase A and proteinase K, and analyzed by qRT–PCR. The probes for ChIRP assays are listed in .

## Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET)
The triplex-forming oligonucleotide (TFO) strand contained a 5-carboxytetramethylrhodamine (TAMRA) label on its 5′ end, and the corresponding triplex target site (TTS) was labelled with 5-carboxyfluorescein (FAM) on its 5′ end. Triplex formation was assessed in binding buffer (20 mM HEPES, pH 7.5; 10 mM MgCl2; and 50 mM sodium acetate) at a ratio of 1:5 (500 nM TTS:2500 nM TFO). The mixtures were annealed by incubation at 55°C for 10 minutes, followed by incubation at 37°C for 10 hours. The fluorescence intensities between 480 and 690 nm were measured by a BioTek Synergy microplate reader.

## PP2A immunoprecipitation phosphatase assay
The PP2A enzymatic activity of CD4+ T cells was assessed using a PP2A immunoprecipitation phosphatase assay kit (cat# 17-313; Millipore) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the supernatants of cell lysates were immunoprecipitated with an anti-PP2AC antibody (clone 1D6; Millipore) and protein A agarose slurry at 4°C for 1–2 hours with constant rocking. After washing, a phosphopeptide (amino acid sequence: K-R-pT-I-R-R) was added to the immunoprecipitated immune complexes as a substrate for PP2Ac and incubated at 30°C in a shaking incubator for 10 minutes. The supernatants (25 μL) were transferred onto a half-volume 96-well microtiter plate, and activity was assessed based on dephosphorylation of the phosphopeptide measured after the addition of 100 μL of malachite green phosphate detection solution for 15 minutes. Phosphate concentrations were calculated based on a standard curve created from serial dilutions of a standard phosphate solution.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 7 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) and SPSS 19.0 software (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). Immunofluorescence and immunohistochemical staining were analyzed with ImageJ (US National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). Statistically significant differences between 2 groups were determined by Student’s t-test, and differences among more than 3 groups were assessed by analysis of variance. Error bars represent the standard error of mean in all figures. Statistical significance was defined as follows: P < 0.05, P < 0.01, P < 0.01, and not significant.

## RESULTS

## LincR-PPP2R5C is induced at a high level in Th2 cells
We previously determined the mRNA and lncRNA profiles of CD4+ T cells in an acute model of asthma by RNA-seq analysis and constructed a lncRNA–mRNA co-expression network of differentially expressed mRNAs and lncRNAs. High-throughput analysis revealed that as compared with the control group, the asthma group had 36 significantly upregulated known lncRNAs and 98 significantly downregulated known lncRNAs ( ). In addition to these lncRNAs that were formally defined in the databases, several lncRNAs without annotations (novel lncRNAs) were identified by RNA-seq and filtering. A total of 4,602 novel lncRNAs were identified, including 7 downregulated and 8 upregulated novel lncRNAs ( ).
 To explore the robustness of the altered expression of novel lncRNAs, another parallel experiment with an acute asthma model was performed. We used qRT-PCR to verify the expression levels of differentially expressed lncRNAs with suitable RPKM ( , ), and TCONS_000056450 was expressed at high levels in CD4+ T cells in asthmatic mice, whereas low expression levels of this lncRNA were observed in control mice. This novel lncRNA sequence was found to be ~2,590 nucleotides in length; this lncRNA was determined to be located on chr12qF1 at 110362240–110364829 bp on the forward strand and was mapped upstream of the cis gene, serine/threonine-PP2A 56 kDa regulatory subunit gamma isoform (PPP2R5C or B56γ). Therefore, this lncRNA was designated LincR-PPP2R5C. Furthermore, expression of LincR-PPP2R5C was detected in the spleen, lymph nodes, and thymus gland ( ). To investigate whether LincR-PPP2R5C is expressed in vitro, naive CD4+ T cells were purified from mouse spleens and cultured for Th cell differentiation as described previously. The data indicated that LincR-PPP2R5C was expressed at a higher level in Th2 cells than in Th1 cells ( ). Next, the expression of LincR-PPP2R5C was evaluated by northern blot analysis of the spleen ( ). Furthermore, the results of nuclear-cytoplasmic RNA fractionation and fluorescence in situ RNA hybridization experiments indicated that LincR-PPP2R5C was primarily expressed in the nuclear compartment of lymphocytes ( , ). Overall, these data suggested that LincR-PPP2R5C was expressed at relatively high levels in CD4+ T cells in allergic asthma and in Th2 cells.

## LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency attenuates Th2 cell differentiation in vitro

An imbalance between Th1/Th2 cells is known to play a central role in the development of allergic asthma. Th2 cells secrete the Th2-type cytokines IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 to amplify type 2 inflammation, while Th1 cells secrete the Th1-type cytokines IFN-γ, IL-12, and tumor necrosis factor-α to limit type 2 inflammation. To investigate the roles of LincR-PPP2R5C in maintaining the Th1/Th2 paradigm, a scrambled lentivirus and shRNA-expressing lentiviral vectors targeting the LincR-PPP2R5C sequence (inhibitor) were constructed. The shRNA-expressing lentiviral vectors are extensively used for transduction of T cells. We isolated naive CD4+ T cells from mouse spleens by MACS and mixed them with this lentivirus at an MOI of 50 in the presence of cytokines and simultaneously induced T-cell activation and differentiation. After 5 days, we tested the inhibitory effects of this lentivirus on LincR-PPP2R5C expression in Th2 cells. As shown in , the LincR-PPP2R5C-specific shRNA lentivirus indeed resulted in an approximately 50% reduction in expression. The transcriptional levels of related genes in Th2 cells treated with the inhibitor were measured by qRT-PCR. When LincR-PPP2R5C was silenced, the transcriptional levels of IL-4 and IL-13 produced and released by Th2 cells and the key TF GATA3 were significantly suppressed in Th2 cells, while the levels of the type 1 cytokines IL-12 and the key Th1 TF T-bet were significantly increased ( ), suggesting that LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency was accompanied by reduced Th2 cell differentiation. The expression of cytokines and TFs at the protein level was determined by intracellular flow cytometry and western blotting; the data demonstrated that the expression level of the Th1 master TF T-bet was increased in Th2 cells after inhibitor administration and that the expression of the Th2 master TF GATA3 was decreased. Similarly, the expression level of IFN-γ was increased, while the level of IL-4 was decreased ( ). The levels of Th1/Th2-related cytokines in the supernatant were detected by ELISA, and the levels of IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 secreted by Th2 cells were decreased after inhibitor treatment. Additionally, naive CD4+ T cells from LincR-PPP2R5C KO mice were isolated and differentiated in vitro; the results indicated a weakened Th2 cell differentiation tendency for these cells compared with that for naive CD4+ T cells from wild-type mice ( ). Overall, LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency suppressed the differentiation of Th2 cells in vitro.
 

## LincR-PPP2R5C inhibits Th1 cell differentiation in vitro

Since the level of LincR-PPP2R5C was decreased in Th1 cells, we investigated whether upregulation of LincR-PPP2R5C antagonizes Th1 cell differentiation, therefore favouring Th2 cell differentiation. We constructed a blank lentivirus and lentiviral vectors encoding full-length LincR-PPP2R5C driven by the cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter (enhancer) for efficient transgene expression in mouse T cells. Naive CD4+ T cells were isolated and transduced with either the enhancer or blank lentivirus at an MOI of 50, followed by activation and differentiation. As shown in , mouse T cells were efficiently transduced by the lentiviral vectors. qRT-PCR analysis indicated that after administration of the enhancer, the levels of the type 1 cytokines IFN-γ and IL-12 were reduced, while the levels of the type 2 cytokines IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 were upregulated ( ); these results were verified by ELISA ( ). Furthermore, the level of the key Th1 TF T-bet was significantly decreased, while the level of the Th2 master TF GATA-3 was significantly increased ( ). Overall, these results indicated that LincR-PPP2R5C was a positive modulator of Th2 cell differentiation, preventing CD4+ T cells from differentiating into Th1 cells.
 

## LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency alleviates OVA-induced lung inflammation
Studies on topics related to lncRNAs that employ genetically modified mice to assess the alterations in CD4+ T-cell-dominated disease phenotypes are limited. In the present study, we constructed a LincR-PPP2R5C KO mouse strain to determine whether LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency alters type 2 inflammation in the airways in vivo in an extensively used acute asthma model established with OVA sensitization ( , ). The results suggested that airway hyperresponsiveness in the asthma model may be significantly alleviated in LincR-PPP2R5C KO mice compared with wild-type mice ( , ). Eosinophilic infiltration in the BALF was also decreased in KO mice ( ). Furthermore, LincR-PPP2R5C KO significantly reduced the level of IgE in the serum of asthmatic animals ( ). Decreases in the levels of IL-4 and IL-13 in the supernatants of lung tissue homogenates from KO mice demonstrated relief of airway inflammation ( , ). Furthermore, inflammatory cell infiltration in the lung parenchyma was comparably recovered in KO mice, and goblet cell hyperplasia and mucus secretion in the bronchial lumen were significantly relieved ( ). Moreover, the number of IL-4-producing CD4+ T cells in the lungs was reduced in KO mice ( ). In summary, LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency significantly alleviated type 2 inflammation in an acute asthma mouse model.
 

## cKO of LincR-PPP2R5C in CD4+ T cells attenuates OVA-induced lung inflammation
We then generated CD4+ T-cell-specific LincR-PPP2R5C-deficient mice by crossing CD4Cre mice with LincR-PPP2R5C floxed mice to obtain CD4Cre+ LincR-PPP2R5Cfl/fl mice (LincR-PPP2R5C cKO mice, cKO) ( ). CD4Cre- LincR-PPP2R5Cfl/fl mice were used as a control group (blank). Comparison with blank mice indicated that cKO mice exhibited relieved eosinophilia and airway hyperresponsiveness in the asthma model ( , ). cKO of LincR-PPP2R5C in allergic asthma mice significantly decreased the total IgE level to that of allergic asthma mice in the blank group ( ). Similarly, allergic cKO mice had reduced levels of IL-4 and IL-13 in the lungs compared with those in the blank group ( , ). cKO mice exhibited less inflammatory cell infiltration around the trachea and blood vessels and decreased goblet cell hyperplasia and mucus secretion beneath the bronchial lumen compared with those in the blank group ( ). Furthermore, comparison with blank mice indicated that the deletion of LincR-PPP2R5C in CD4+ T cells led to significant T-cell dysfunction ( ). These phenotypic alterations in cKO mice paralleled those in KO mice, suggesting that LincR-PPP2R5C influenced the phenotype of acute asthma in a CD4+ T-cell-dependent manner.
 

## LincR-PPP2R5C increases PPP2R5C expression through cis-regulatory activity
Long ncRNAs are often involved in the regulation of neighbouring protein-coding genes to exert cis-regulatory effects. We investigated whether the effect of LincR-PPP2R5C on CD4+ T-cell polarization was due to its influence on PPP2R5C expression. Interestingly, the qRT–PCR data shown in indicated that in the later period (Day 5) of Th2 cell induction, PPP2R5C mRNA expression was reduced by 24% in the context of LincR-PPP2R5C inhibition. The reduction was observed in Th2 cell derived from Linc-PPP2R5C KO mice Compared with wild-type mice by 27% as well ( ). However, overexpression of LincR-PPP2R5C using an enhancer had no effect on PPP2R5C ( ). Similarly, the expression level of PPP2R5C was increased on Day 3 and Day 5, but not on Day 2, in Th2 cells of Linc-PPP2R5C KO mice but not in those of wild-type mice ( ), suggesting that both inhibiting and knocking out LincR-PPP2R5C downregulated the expression of PPP2R5C.
 The results of the present study indicated that high levels of LincR-PPP2R5C were present in the nucleus, suggesting possible biological functions in the nucleus, such as an interaction with genomic DNA. Nuclear lncRNAs can bind to specific genomic loci to form RNA–DNA triplex structures. To determine whether LincR-PPP2R5C binds to the PPP2R5C promoter to regulate the expression of the PPP2R5C gene, we used 3 potential binding motifs (TFOs) of LincR-PPP2R5C and the corresponding binding sites (TTSs) upstream of the PPP2R5C promoter Ppp2r5c_1, which had a high binding potential according to LongTarget prediction ( , ). In brief, LongTarget constructed 48 RNA strands on 24 rule sets (6 Hoogsteen rule sets and 18 reverse Hoogsteen rule sets) in the region of the PPP2R5C promoter, LincR-PPP2R5C was aligned to each strand, the aligned regions were considered TFOs, and the best TFO (TFO1) was identified by assessing the stability of the generated triplexes. After shuffling the PPP2R5C promoter region sequence and LincR-PPP2R5C sequence, the above steps were repeated for each pair of shuffled sequences. Furthermore, a ChIRP assay was used to determine the exact location of the binding sites on the chromatin in the region from −2,000 bp to +99 bp of the transcriptional start site, which corresponded to the exact locations of lncRNA binding sites on the chromatin. As shown in , LincR-PPP2R5C interacted with the P4 region of the PPP2R5C promoter Ppp2r5c_1 in EL4 cells, corresponding to the location of TTS1 but not that of TTS2. Next, we synthesized predicted TFOs labelled with 5-TAMRA and TTSs labelled with fluorescein amidite (FAM). FRET analysis showed an increase in the fluorescence signal at 570–580 nm and a decrease at 520 nm in the TFO1/TTS1 group, but not in the TFO2/TTS2 or TFO3/TTS3 group, compared with the signals detected in the control ssRNA/TTS group ( , ), which were similar to the signals in the FENDRR/PITX2 positive control group ( ), indicating energy transfer from the fluorescein donor to the rhodamine acceptor and the formation of a triplex structure. These results validated the hypothesis that a triplex was formed between LincR-PPP2R5C and the PPP2R5C promoter region to upregulate the transcription of the gene.

## LincR-PPP2R5C promotes Th2 cell differentiation via PPP2R5C/PP2A
PPP2R5C is one of the variable regulatory subunits (B’/B56/PR61 family) of trimeric PP2A complexes. The PP2A holocomplex regulates key cellular processes, such as cell cycle progression, apoptosis, cellular metabolism, and migration, and the variable regulatory subunits determine the subcellular localization, substrate specificity, and catalytic activity of PP2A. Another regulatory subunit, PPP2R2A, was reported to enhance Th1 and Th17 cell differentiation through activation of the GEF-H1/RhoA/ROCK signaling pathway, and genetic PPP2R2A deficiency in mouse T cells was shown to reduce Th1 and Th17 cell differentiation. We evaluated the enzymatic activity of PP2A in Th2 cells to explore the possible effects of LincR-PPP2R5C KO on PP2A. As shown in , PP2A enzymatic activity was suppressed by 17% by LincR-PPP2R5C KO on Day 5, suggesting that LincR-PPP2R5C potentially may modulate the function of the PP2A holocomplex.
 PP2A is critical in many cellular functions of T cells, including cell survival, proliferation, activation, and differentiation. PP2A has been implicated in the regulation of the production of IL-2 and IL-17 by CD4+ T cells. Treg cell function depends on the presence of PP2A, and in the absence of PP2A, mice develop profound lymphoproliferation and autoimmunity. OA is a PP2A inhibitor that has been extensively used as a probe to identify cellular processes regulated by phosphorylation, DT-061 activates PP2A by selectively stabilizing specific PP2A holoenzymes. We administered OA and DT-061 to explore the effect of PP2A dysfunction on Th2 cell differentiation. As shown in , the enzymatic activity of PP2A in Th2 cells was significantly impaired by OA and promoted by DT-061. Next, we quantified the relative proportions of GATA3- and IL-4-positive Th2 cells by intracellular flowcytometry. The frequencies of GATA3-expressing cells and IL-4-producing cells were significantly decreased after OA administration under Th2-skewing conditions and increased after DT-061 administration ( ). Subsequently, we explored the functional consequences for Th2-related cytokines, including IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13. As shown in , the secretion of these cytokines into the supernatant was decreased under Th2 conditions. These data suggested that PP2A inhibition may impair Th2 cell differentiation. Impaired methylation invariably leads to a decrease in the binding of the regulatory subunit to PP2Ac, and the phosphorylation of PP2Ac at Y307 efficiently inactivates PP2A. In the present study, a decrease in the GATA3 level together with increases in the p-PP2A levels were observed after OA treatment ( ), while an increase in the m-PP2A and GATA3 levels were observed after DT-061 treatment ( ). Overall, these results indicated that LincR-PPP2R5C may promote Th2 cell differentiation by modulating PPP2R5C and PP2A activity.

## DISCUSSION
The functions of lncRNAs in Th2 cell differentiation in allergic asthma remain largely elusive. The present study identified a novel lncRNA, LincR-PPP2R5C, that was upregulated in CD4+ T cells from mice with acute asthma according to RNA-seq and qRT-PCR data. Then, northern blotting and FISH data confirmed the presence of LincR-PPP2R5C. Subsequently, in vitro T-cell induction was used to demonstrate that LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency suppressed Th2 cell differentiation. Mechanistically, LincR-PPP2R5C directly interacted with the PPP2R5C promoter region by forming a DNA–RNA triplex to upregulate PPP2R5C expression. Furthermore, PPP2R5C deficiency resulted in a malfunction in the phosphatase activity of the PP2A holocomplex, leading to the suppression of Th2 cell differentiation by naive CD4+ T cells, while activation of PP2A by an agonist promoted Th2 cell differentiation. Importantly, global LincR-PPP2R5C KO and conditional LincR-PPP2R5C KO in CD4+ T cells relieved airway hyperresponsiveness, pulmonary inflammation, airway remodeling, and Th2 cell infiltration. These findings provide in-depth mechanistic and translational insights into the pathways by which lncRNAs promote inflammation in asthma in a Th2 cell-dependent manner.
The Th1 and Th2 paradigms have provided a productive model for exploring the pathogenesis of allergic diseases. Growing evidence indicates that Th9, Th17, Th22, Tfh, Treg, and other non-Th2 CD4+ cells may contribute to asthma development ; however, Th2 cells are pivotal in the type 2 immune response, which is the most common asthma phenotype. Lentiviral vectors encoding LincR-PPP2R5C driven by the CMV promoter were used as an enhancer, and an shRNA-expressing lentiviral vector was used as an inhibitor in vitro because lentiviral vectors are able to transduce nondividing cells better than retroviral vectors. Moreover, transduction of lentiviruses with the CMV promoter results in more efficient transgene expression in mouse T cells when the cells are activated by γ-chain cytokines (IL-2) at the time of vector application. Therefore, we administered lentiviruses on Day 1 to monitor the influence of enhancement or suppression during the whole induction period. The results indicated that downregulation of LincR-PPP2R5C may decrease the production of IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13. Furthermore, the level of the Th2 master TF GATA-3 was reduced in Th2 cells.
Numerous types of mouse models of asthma have been classified according to the allergen and duration of the model. The allergens include OVA, house dust mite (HDM), and cockroach extract. We chose an acute asthma model sensitized with OVA based on the following considerations. First, unlike other allergens, OVA is a T-cell-dependent antigen commonly used as a model protein to investigate antigen-specific immune responses in mice. Importantly, the structure of OVA is clearly defined, and OVA use is characterized by good repeatability and reproducibility. The present study focused on T cells in asthma, and acute allergen challenge models have been extensively used to investigate pulmonary inflammation and AHR, while chronic asthma models mainly involve simulation of smooth muscle hypertrophy, subepithelial collagen deposition, and fibrosis. In the OVA-induced allergic asthma model, global LincR-PPP2R5C KO or cKO of LincR-PPP2R5C in CD4+ T cells suppressed Th2 pulmonary inflammation, suggesting that LincR-PPP2R5C may participate in asthma in a CD4+ T-cell-dependent manner. Additionally, the present study demonstrated that the suppression of IL-4 and IL-13 in the context of LincR-PPP2R5C KO was more significant than the suppression of IL-5 both in vivo and in vitro. We hypothesized that these effects can be explained by the fact that IL-4 and IL-13 share the same receptor, i.e., type II IL-4R, comprising IL-4Ra and an IL-13-binding chain (IL-13Ra1), which largely accounts for the overlap in the biological effects of IL-4 and IL-13. 
LncRNAs regulate gene expression through various mechanisms and have emerged as key modulators of both physiological and pathological processes. LncRNAs are generally classified into antisense lncRNAs, pseudogene lncRNAs, intronic lncRNAs, and intergenic lncRNAs. Most lncRNAs function via cis-acting mechanisms by targeting only adjacent chromatin regions due to the low abundance and instability of these RNA species. A few lncRNAs with high abundance and stability migrate, target, and exert trans-regulatory activity on distant chromatin regions. Considering the relative position with regard to the protein-coding gene sequences (intergenic), subcellular localization (mostly nuclear), and abundance (low) of LincR-PPP2R5C, we hypothesized that this lncRNA acts in cis. GATA3-AS1 is a cis-acting lncRNA that binds to the components of the MLL methyltransferase and forms a DNA–RNA hybrid (R-loop), thus tethering the MLL methyltransferase to the gene locus. Cis-acting lncRNAs have also been demonstrated to contribute to enhancer activity through lncRNA–DNA interactions. Nonetheless, this activity is likely to require proximity to the target promoter, implying that a lncRNA is transcribed proximally to its target gene. Using genome mapping and ChIRP assays, we demonstrated that LincR-PPP2R5C physiologically interacted with the P4 region of the PPP2R5C promoter. One of the potential mechanisms of these effects is direct binding to DNA and the formation of an RNA–DNA-DNA triple helix formed by weaker, non-Watson-Crick base pairs, i.e., Hoogsteen hydrogen bonds or reverse Hoogsteen hydrogen bonds between the Watson-Crick base-paired dsDNA and the third strand composed of RNA. Potential pairs of TFOs and the corresponding TTSs of the binding motifs were predicted by LongTarget. These predictions were supported by FRET analysis. However, the actual prevalence of triplex structures remains unknown due to difficulties in detecting them in vivo.
 
The content of PP2A is 1% of the total cellular protein, and PP2A accounts for the majority of serine/threonine phosphatase activity in mammalian cells. Considering the broad regulatory profile of PP2A, this protein is often dysregulated in human diseases, especially in immune inflammatory diseases. T cells from patients with systemic lupus erythematosus express abnormally high levels of PP2Ac, and high levels of PP2Ac in T cells promote the production of IL-17. High levels of PP2A were demonstrated to constrain inflammatory responses. Interestingly, other research has shown that decreased PP2A activity increases AHR in a HDM mouse model. PP2A expression and activity were found to be reduced in the PBMCs of steroid-resistant asthma patients, and knockdown or inhibition of PP2A reduced steroid responsiveness. As a result, the molecular mechanisms by which PP2A controls inflammation warrant further investigation. In the present study, activation of PP2A with DT-061 promoted Th2 polarization in vitro. We hypothesized that this discrepancy results from stimulus-dependent modulation of PP2A activity and substrate specificity governed by the expression of the B subunits, even though the role of expression in signal transduction has not been extensively studied. An agonist and inhibitor of PP2A showed strong stimulatory or suppressive effects on Th2 cell differentiation; however, we did not expand our findings to animal experiments because most PP2A inhibitors are toxic and have limited clinical utility. Moreover, OA and calyculin A increase the expression of nuclear factor-κB target genes, including proinflammatory mediators, which could exacerbate asthma due to the imperfect specificity of these compounds. Considering that chemicals targeting the regulatory B subunit have not been developed, we speculated that mediators of epigenetic modifications, such as lncRNAs, may be a novel therapeutic target in asthma.
There are some limitations to the present study. LncRNA genes have less conservation of the primary nucleotide sequence than coding genes. Among 200 human and mouse lncRNAs that were shown to be differentially expressed following activation of an immune response, there was less than 1% sequence conservation. Unsurprisingly, multispecies genome alignments (UCSC pslMap tool and chain alignments) did not identify any orthologous regions or overlap for spliced LincR-PPP2R5C in humans. However, some research indicates that conserved interspecies expression of lncRNAs does not necessarily correlate with function. Further investigations are required to determine the biological significance of LincR-PPP2R5C. Current experiment protocols are not enough to conclude that LincR-PPP2R5C is only involved in the Th2 differentiation. CRISPR/Cas9-mediated deletion, shRNA-expressing lentiviral and enhancer-carrying lentivirus were applied from Day 1–5 from naïve CD4+ T cells to Th1/Th2 cells, only to observe the final alterations of TFs and cytokines, confused the activation, proliferation, differentiation, and function stage of CD4+ T cells. Besides, although preview study has demonstrated that knockdown and overexpression of B56 subunit alters PP2A activity, we have not proved the causative roles of B56γ and PP2A in our experiment. In addition, we observed an impressive change in CD4+ T cells but a modest change in KO mice, which could result from the complex functions of lncRNAs. Mechanisms of LincR-PPP2R5C other than cis-regulatory activity have not been ruled out. On the other hand, we focused exclusively on the Th1 and Th2 paradigms of allergic diseases. Th9, Th17, Th22, Tfh, Treg, and other non-Th2 CD4+ T cells also play important roles in asthma. Knocking out LincR-PPP2R5C in CD4+ T cells would also alter these cells, which may explain why the effects of this KO approach were less impressive in vivo.
In conclusion, the present study revealed that LincR-PPP2R5C, a novel lncRNA, upregulated in asthmatic mice, regulated PPP2R5C expression by forming an RNA–DNA triplex, resulting in overactivation of PP2A and consequently influencing Th2 polarization. Global and cKO of LincR-PPP2R5C significantly reduced Th2 cell differentiation and relieved airway inflammation in a mouse model of acute asthma, suggesting that LincR-PPP2R5C and associated PPP25C/PP2A may be involved in Th2-dominant allergic diseases.
