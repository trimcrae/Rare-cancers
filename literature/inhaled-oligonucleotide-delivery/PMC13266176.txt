## Abstract
Lipid-formulated liposomes have been utilized for the transport of various biological substances, including mRNA molecules. Basically, large lipids play a crucial role in the structure and function of cell membranes. In protein synthesis, mRNA plays a crucial role in expressing the proteins. In this context, this natural process enables gene editing, protein replacement therapy, cancer immunotherapy, cellular reprogramming, and vaccinations. A range of biomaterials is utilized for mRNA transport, including polymers, lipid-based nanoparticles, and protein-mRNA complexes. Thus, the review aims to analyse the mRNA-lipid nanoparticles assigned with gene editing and the mechanisms to enhance immune responses to infectious diseases. A nonviral lipid nanoparticle approach for CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing has achieved up to 80% gene editing in vivo and 98% in vitro across various cancer cell types. MEVPCS-mRNA LNPs, as a preventative HIV vaccine, elicit comparable and highly specific immune responses via CD8+ T cells. Th1-type CD4+ T cell responses are notable in mice immunized with LNP-mRNA, indicating a substantial impact on Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Variations in pH, ionic strength, and freezing rate are essential for enhancing the lyophilization of mRNA lipid nanoparticles. The polydispersity index has risen from 0.1 to 0.3, and the LNP particle size in the Vibrating Membrane Nebulizer has increased from an average of 85 nm to 300 nm, suggesting notable structural degradation due to elevated shear stresses. The Laminar Fluid Ejection Device enhanced the stability of lipid nanoparticles. This enhances the immune response and effectively manages harmful microbes.
Keywords: Lipid nanoparticles, mRNA, Infectious diseases, Drug carriers, Immune response

## Introduction
Protein synthesis converts messenger RNA (mRNA) molecules into their corresponding proteins. This intrinsic mechanism facilitates several biological and therapeutic uses, including vaccinations, cancer immunotherapy, protein replacement therapy, cellular reprogramming, and genome editing. , Especially, mRNA therapeutics can prevent and treat various diseases. mRNA functions in the cell cytosol, avoiding the risk of genome mutagenesis associated with plasmid DNA. Furthermore, in vitro transcription (IVT) technology has simplified the management of mRNA stability and immunogenicity. In the drug delivery system, polymers, protein–mRNA complexes, lipid-based nanoparticles, and other biomaterials can transport mRNA. Primarily, the development of SARS-CoV-2 has raised global concerns about immunisations. Flexible and fast mRNA immunisations are sought; to generate strong immune responses and long-lasting protection, mRNA vaccines require rapid mRNA delivery to cells and high antigen expression. Thus, lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) must maximize their potential. Additionally, cell membranes contain mostly lipids, which are necessary for membrane integrity and biological processes. It is well known that lipid-formulated liposomes may carry mRNA molecules and other payloads. , , In LNP-mRNA vaccine development, mRNA component stability is crucial; however, the novel vaccine platform's primary drawback is cold-chain storage. Traditional vaccinations may be stored at 2°–8 °C in a refrigerator for 6 months, whereas mRNA-LNP vaccines must be frozen, making vaccine distribution in poor infrastructure challenging. Thus, lyophilisation, or freeze-drying, helps to preserve the vaccines by eliminating water. Notably, the LNPs prepared in PBS had less than 80% mRNA encapsulation after lyophilization with maltose as a lyoprotectant, but 5 mM Tris buffer maintained their encapsulation efficiency compared to fresh LNPs. Also, innovative continuous spin lyophilization or batch freeze-drying has shown the efficacy of lyophilising mRNA-LNP formulations with a reduction-sensitive ionisable lipid and 20% (w/v) sucrose. Importantly, mRNA vaccine formulation and lyophilisation must be improved. Currently, mRNA-LNP-based vaccine candidates represent a rapidly advancing platform for addressing various diseases; however, adherence to the precise technical processes is vital. Thus, the review aims to analyse the mRNA-lipid nanoparticles assigned with gene editing and the mechanisms to enhance immune responses to infectious diseases.

## LNP-mRNA roles in gene editing
Chemically specified RNA-delivery vehicles like LNPs have been studied for non-viral delivery. Recent genome editing studies show that LNPs can transport Cas9 mRNA and single-guide (sgRNA) into mice livers. Changes in LNP's size and lipid composition allowed it to transport Cas9 and sgRNA to mice's liver, lung, muscle, and brain. Pre-existing Cas9 protein immunity is a concern. The LNP approach for delivering the RNA-based CRISPR-Cas9 system into extensive skeletal muscle has not demonstrated efficacy due to the multitude of injections, immunological reactivity, in vivo safety concerns, and injection routes. Notably, the nonviral lipid nanoparticle method for CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing achieves 80% gene editing in vivo and 98% in vitro across several cancer cell types. In brief, the study used the following cell lines, such as murine glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), human serous ovarian adenocarcinomas Ovcar8 (OV8) and NCI-ADR (NAR), human colon carcinoma HCT116, and human lung adenocarcinoma A549 were tested for gene disruption utilizing L8-cLNPs. Incubation with sgGFP-cLNPs (1 μg/mL; 7 nM of total RNA) resulted in GFP fluorescence in 3–18% of cancer cells. L8-cLNPs perform precise gene editing in vitro with little toxicity in several cancer cell lines. Unlike sgGFP, cLNPs damaged the polo-like kinase1 (PLK1) gene in GBM 005 and OV8, resulting in 84% and 91% genomic editing, respectively. 
Notably, CRISPR-LNPs (cLNPs) targeting PLK1 reduced tumour growth and enhanced survival in two aggressive mouse cancer models following single or multiple doses. Single delivery of sgPLK1-cLNPs to the tumour bed of a murine GBM model resulted in almost 70% PLK1 gene editing, activated caspase 3 labelling, 50% longer median longevity, and 30% higher overall survival of 005 GBM-bearing mice. Many cancer treatments require daily administration, which lowers patient quality of life and increases toxicity and expense. Increase therapeutic efficacy, minimise treatment frequency, and circumvent dosage constraints for cancer medications by permanently altering tumour survival genes with CRISPR-Cas9. EGFR-targeted cLNPs can alter PLK1 genes by 80% in one injection, since dysregulation of PLK1 has been associated with the prognosis of many malignancies. PLK1 is crucial for the regulation of the cell cycle and the development of cancer. In mice with high-grade ovarian cancer and malignant ascites, two intraperitoneal EGFR-targeted sgPLK1-cLNPs decreased tumour growth and saved over 80%. , Besides this, the Cas9-RNP exhibits partial association with the exterior surface, whereas mRNA Cas9 demonstrates more effective integration within the core of LNPs. The distribution of anions across the surface of the Cas9-RNP is uneven, despite the complex possessing a net negative charge. 
In the context of LNP-mRNA's clinical relevance in cancer therapy, a recent study evaluated how prenatal and developmental biology can be used to overcome biological barriers to mRNA-LNP distribution to hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). LNP-mediated gene delivery is easier in the liver, where fetal hematopoiesis and HSCs develop, than in the postnatal bone marrow niche. A high-performing LNP from the similar study was used to test mRNA-LNP delivery to the fetal liver in gestation. However, the process of developing the novel method on LNPs is essential for yielding high-efficiency outcomes; thus, the dynamic iminoboronate ester linkage was used in a recent study to create H₂O₂-responsive, cationic ionizable lipidoids in a one-pot, three-component synthetic process. This technology allows for the production of a large library of dynamically covalent LNPs, which can then be screened for the transport of mRNA using high-throughput techniques. In addition, a recent study used inexpensive and commonly available chemicals to synthesize lipids with improved architectural diversity across lipid lengths quickly. Lipids with terminal branching groups allowed the study of linear and non-linear relationships between ionic liquid structure and function in lipid nanoparticle distribution. For liver protein and mRNA transport, LNP-mediated transfection is enhanced by Branched ENdosomal Disruptor (BEND) ionizable lipids. When used with Cas9 mRNA and the RNP complex for gene editing cargo and T cell transportation, BEND LNPs improved T cell transfection and liver editing.

## mRNA-LNP roles in induced immune responses
Lipid nanoparticles formulations often contain ionisable, helper, cholesterol, and PEGylated lipids. Most studies focus on ionisable lipids' role in RNA packing and endosomal escape. In this context, the MC3 DOPE generated more cytokines in THP-1 cells than C12–200 DOPE. 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine (DOPE) formulations may increase THP-1 cytokine production due to increased LNP absorption. DOPE's fusogenic properties as an assist lipid promote cargo movement and elevate expression and cytokine levels, relative to other formulations at the same dosage. According to a recent immunological study, the viral protease cleavage sites (VPCS) vaccine is the inaugural HIV vaccination strategy that elicits narrowly targeted immune responses to VPCSs, akin to those observed in HIV-highly exposed seronegative individuals (HESNs). In order to suppress the HIV infection, MEVPCS-mRNA lipid nanoparticles trigger specific immunological responses mediated by CD8+ T cells. Antigen-specific CD8+ central memory T cells generated by vaccination are enhanced by MEVPCS-mRNA LNPs. Epitopes from MEVPCS polypeptide units that are thought to represent HLA class I allele epitopes can trigger immunological responses mediated by MHC-I in CD8+ T cells in murine models. Recent research demonstrated that cationic lipid (ALC0307), cholesterol, phosphatidylcholine, and PEG-lipid (APCP) LNP-encapsulated nucleoside-modified mRNA may generate substantial epitope-specific CD8 T cell responses that become memory cells following intradermal and intramuscular vaccination. Thus, the interaction was adequate for the type I interferon receptor (IFN-I), also known as IFNAR. Subcutaneous delivery of mRNA vaccines encapsulated in 1,2-dioleoyl-3-trimethylammonium-propane (DOTAP) / DOPE may be required to inhibit type I interferon (IFN-I) signaling and activate CD8 T cell responses. The necessity of this procedure is assessed in this study. The effectiveness of an mRNA-LNP vaccine in stimulating CD8 T cell responses in patients with functioning IFN-I responses may depend on the composition of LNP. , 
Recent clinical studies have notably demonstrated the effectiveness of mRNA-LNP vaccines; research on therapeutic HIV vaccines is noticeably lacking, although preventive mRNA HIV vaccines have advanced significantly. Prophylactic immunological responses prioritize HIV-envelope-specific neutralizing antibodies, but therapeutic immunization necessitates significant Gag-specific polyfunctional CD8+ T cell responses. , Despite their importance, gut lymphoid tissues are commonly overlooked in preclinical and analytical research. There isn't much preclinical research that has examined mRNA-LNP formulations in connection to Gag-specific polyfunctional CD8+ T cell responses in mice or macaques. , , 
Significantly, mRNA vaccines using lipid nanoparticles induce humoral (antibody) and cell-mediated (T cell) protection. To improve antigen-specific cellular immune responses ( ), LNP may increase antigen absorption and endosome escape. Recent studies classified CpG Oligodeoxynucleotides (CPG ODNs) as Type A (D), B (K), and C. Full-phosphorothioate CpG-B ODN CPG 1018 has one or more CpG dinucleotides. Limited ability exists to boost IFN-α production, despite stimulating B cells and increasing TLR9-dependent NF-κB activation. In contrast, CpG 2395, a Class C, exhibited Class A and B capabilities, prompting pDC to generate IFN-α and activate B cells. Rabies virus (RABV) protection and humoral and cellular immune responses are better in mice with LNP-encapsulated CpG 1018 than with LNP and CpG 2395. Besides that, the SP-HBsAg mRNA-LNP vaccination in murine models demonstrated enhanced protection against hepatitis B virus (HBV) infections by eliciting a robust antigen-specific Th1-cell-biased immune response. Serum hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) concentrations in chronically infected mice were decreased by both vaccines. Mice with chronic hepatitis B (CHB) who were administered both mRNA vaccine candidates had the most robust CD8+ T-cell-mediated response and the lowest plasma levels of HBsAg. 
 On the other hand, in haematological malignancies, natural killer (NK) therapies are most beneficial. Solid tumour therapy is difficult. Cytokines released by malignant cells in the immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment (TME) inhibit NK cell recruitment and function. , TGFβ may reduce NK cell metabolism, cytokine activity, and degranulation; mRNA therapy is possible. NK cell transfection is difficult and restricted. According to recent research, LNPs transfected 75% more than electroporation (57%). The two injection methods were safe and achieved 99.5% cell viability. Enhancing LNPs fivefold enhanced geometric mean fluorescence intensity (gMFI) compared to electroporation, indicating a major change in enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP) expression. Using fluorescent microscopy, NK cells transfected with eGFP were examined. LNPs outperformed electroporation in signal strength and protein levels.

## mRNA-LNP formulation on infectious diseases
The mRNA-LNP plays a substantial function in infectious diseases; however, these lipid nanoparticles enhanced immune responses more than direct application of mRNA-based substances. In correlation with immune responses with mRNA-LNP modules, a recent study found that mRNACV2, a LNP-mRNA vaccine encoding the CysVac2 protein, was administered to mice to assess its efficacy. In chronic infection, the immunodominant Ag85B and the sulfur absorption route overexpressor CysD lead to the production of CysVac2. , Notably, protection against aerosol infection by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) was linked to increased numbers of vaccine-specific polyfunctional CD4+ T cells that produced IFNγ, IL-2, and TNF in mice given mRNACV2. In animal models, polyfunctional CD4+ T cells offer protection against MTB; however, this might not be the case in human beings. The study found that, in comparison to other investigations, there were more cytokine-producing CD4+ T cells and antigen-specific Ag85B:I-Ab+ T cells after CysVac2 adjuvanted vaccination. Mice immunized with LNP-mRNA exhibit robust Th1-type CD4+ T cell responses. Thus, the protection against MTB aerosol infection was associated with an increase in vaccine-specific polyfunctional CD4+ T cells that produced IFNγ, IL-2, and TNF in mice that received mRNACV2. Although polyfunctional CD4+ T cells protect animals against MTB, this may not always be the case in humans. , 
Recently, the research evaluated the pharmacokinetics and functional activity of three monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) as mRNA/LNP in mice infected with S. aureus. Single chain variable fragment-fragment crystallizable (ScFv-Fc) and immunoglobulin G (IgG) formats were used to evaluate the mAb trio to avoid HC and LC mispairing. The Cmax values of scFv-Fc and IgG forms varied from 10 to 22 μg/mL and 15 to 90 μg/mL, respectively, 24 h after delivery. Both forms were effective, and the triple mAb protected against S. aureus dermonecrosis. Non-human primates (NHPs) receiving triple IgG via mRNA had blood levels ranging from 2.9 to 13.7 μg/mL, with a terminal half-life of 11.8 to 15.4 days. In correlation with the above findings, to combat the Gram-positive bacterium S. aureus, the study initially amalgamated three mAbs that target the surface-expressed clumping factor A (ClfA) with four bi-component leukotoxins (LukSF, LukED, HlgAB, and HlgCB) and alpha toxin (AT). 
The mRNA-LNP GAS vaccine module Combo #5 comprises five highly conserved and protective detoxified group A streptococcus (GAS) antigens: trigger factor (TF), arginine deiminase (ADI), streptococcal C5a peptidase (SCPA), streptolysin O (SLO), IL-8, and protease. This study investigated the effects of Combo#5-TM mRNA-LNP on the humoral and cellular immune responses in mice, focusing on CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses, germinal center B cells, and memory B cells, all of which are critical for sustained antibody responses. The Combo#5-TM vaccination resulted in an increase in Tfh cells, which are critical for the proliferation of GC B cells and the production of antibodies. In GAS, long-lasting antibody responses need humoral and cellular responses from mice, including CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, GC B cells, and MBCs, which Combo#5-TM mRNA-LNP boosts. 
LNP-delivered mRNA from an acute HBV donor's bone marrow restores chronic HBV through core antigen-specific T cells. In contrast to standard therapy relapses, HBV resolvers had higher core- and pol-specific responses and maintained virological control. , Adeno-associated virus (AAV)-HBV mice following multi-antigen vaccination showed a substantial decrease in core-specific CD4+ and CD8+ responses compared to naive animals. In AAV-HBV models, vaccination antigen ratio changes may boost core-specific responses. Injecting lipid nanoparticles with mRNA into myocytes or antigen-presenting cells such as dendritic cells in draining lymph nodes post-intramuscularly, may boost immune responses and immunization. , In this context, recent research evaluated mRNA-LNP and systemic and mucosal immunity to phylogenetic group 2 influenza variants with Aic HA target antigen. The extensive cross-reactivity of cellular immunity reduces and prevents influenza. After antigen-re-stimulation in vitro, all vaccination groups, including one-dose PHC, significantly decreased CD127 on splenic T cells compared to the naïve group. 
Furthermore, a recent study shows nasal RSV·F IgA antibodies prevent upper and lower respiratory tract infections. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)-primed mice showed elevated IgA antibodies in their nasal compartment after receiving a single dose of LNP-formulated SMARRT.RSV.preF, but most naïve animals did not respond. In line with that, convalescent and naïve patients may have different mucosal IgA responses due to mRNA/self-amplifying (saRNA) vaccines augmenting pre-existing B-cells from viral infection or primary immunization via circulating antigen or cell migration. HIV-specific latency-reversing medicines reduce the latent HIV reservoir and aid antiretroviral virologic control. LNP X overcame all HIV transcription start and RNA processing problems when combined with an mRNA that expressed the first coding exon of HIV Tat's 72 amino acids, exceeding usual T cell mitogen-induced viral reactivation , ; however, the new Tat-LNP X reverses HIV latency better. Lack of LNP formulation disclosure and unknown biological properties of the Tat66 isoform employed in the experiment hindered direct comparisons of the two Tat-LNPs. Tat-LNP X's strong mRNA transport efficiency, which is compatible with its low transfection efficiency, may explain its superiority.

## Factors associated with formulation and application of mRNA-LNP
Scientific investigations on mRNA-LNP for drug development and diagnostics using optimal formulations. Physical and chemical characteristics are integrated for exact findings. Notably, a recent study evaluated the LNP-RNA vaccine in 10% w/v sucrose in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and showed good physical and functional preservation at −20 °C for at least 30 days. This temperature allows cold-chain vaccine delivery, as shown by Moderna's COVID-19 vaccine (mRNA-1273). In the LNP-RNA system, −20 °C offers optimum ice nucleation, progressive cooling, and protected particle structure while preventing RNA degradation by hydrolytic cleavage. , Lyophilization and storage of mRNA LNPs have been shown in several investigations, albeit without mechanistic analysis. Especially, recent research has shown that RNA LNPs can be lyophilized; however, the parameters that affect freeze-drying have not been studied. Optimizing lyophilization for mRNA LNPs requires including freezing rate, ionic strength, and pH fluctuations. Specifically, the effect of cryoprotectants in mRNA-LNP formulations with mpox vaccine candidates is that ten formulations with sucrose and trehalose or maltose ratios were produced. The glass transition temperature (Tg’) and collapse temperature (Tc) of each formulation ranged from −31.8 °C to −34.1 °C and − 27.0 °C to −32.0 °C. The initial trial lyophilization settings (20 vials/batch) for cryoprotectant selection assumed a freezing temperature below Tg’ and a primary drying temperature below Tc. The mRNA-LNP without cryoprotectants (C0) had a minor amount of white powder at the bottle's base after freeze-drying and an opaque appearance with many visible insoluble particles after being reconstituted with water. , , , 
Furthermore, the optimal LNP formulations were generated using a preference function to maximize desirability under certain conditions. RNA integrity should surpass 50%, size below 100 nm, PDI below 0.2, EE over 80%, and protein expression maximized. Desirability: function 1 reduces immunogenicity, whereas function 2 increases it. Desirability function 3 optimizes RNA integrity independent of in vitro assessment. On the other hand, shaking and mixing are also crucial in formulating mRNA- LNP, however, as shaking time rises, check the polydispersity index (PDI) and LNP size. Within two hours of shaking, LNP size grows dramatically; after four hours, it slows and stabilizes. In the first two to four hours of shaking, PDI rises dramatically before dropping. As the NanoFlowSizer found, shaking broadens the unimodal distribution of bigger LNPs by shifting the particle size distribution from narrow to bimodal. Low-mRNA concentration formulations are more susceptible to air entrapment during dilution or sample handling, which may explain LNP size and PDI fluctuations during shaking. 
In lipids, there are various types such as cationic or ionizable lipids; zwitterionic phospholipids; neutral co-lipids; mostly cholesterol; and PEGylated lipids in nanoparticle formulations that have been separated and measured using reversed-phase (RP) high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Most lipids including phospholipids, lack chromophores and require alternatives to UV detection. Mass Spectrometry (MS) offers exceptional selectivity and sensitivity, but its high cost, demanding maintenance, and requirement for professional operators make it unsuitable for quality control. Instead, charged aerosol detectors (CAD) and evaporative light scattering detectors may detect substances less volatile than the mobile phase, which is nebulized and evaporated to manufacture analytes. Unlike refractive index. , , Furthermore, good HEPES lipids were synthesised using hEPO mRNA to assess lipid efficacy in mice following a single intramuscular injection. The effectiveness and simplicity of measuring LNP potency using hEPO mRNA in mice have made it a popular model protein. Under regulated conditions, ionisable, phospholipid, cholesterol, and PEGylated lipid solutions were combined with hEPO mRNA in an aqueous acidification buffer to make Good HEPES lipid nanoparticles for mRNA delivery. This study demonstrated that Gen1 good lipids generated as much hEPO protein as industry standard SM-102 and MC3. Mice administered Gen1 LNPs generated higher hEPO than Gen2 lipids. Gen2 tail esters may accelerate lipid breakdown in vivo. , 
Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) combined with a multi-angle light scattering (MALS) detector and concentration detectors such as differential refractometers (dRIs) and UV photometers is an effective method for this type of characterization. It is common to define LNPs and other complex macro-entities utilizing SEC-MALS-UV-dRI. Researchers can evaluate sample concentration and size without a reference standard across the SEC peak. When the UV extinction coefficient and dn/dc values (or a certain refractive index increment) for binary conjugates are known, a multi-detector system can promptly measure the two constituent components in each eluting fraction. Additionally, the CM Nebulizer (Vibrating Membrane) exhibits a substantial increase in LNP particle size, rising from an average of 85 nm to 300 nm, alongside a change in the PDI from 0.1 to 0.3, indicating significant structural damage due to elevated shear stresses. The Laminar Fluid Ejection (LFE) Device exhibited superior LNP stability, evidenced by consistent particle size and minimal PDI changes (0.099 for mRNA and 0.117 for saRNA). 
Importantly, the LNPs-mRNA particle size and mRNA encapsulation are important quality factors that affect in vitro effectiveness. Combining orthogonal procedures that use distinct measurement concepts is recommended for validating complicated quality attribute measurements. This follows FDA and ICH Q2R2 guidance materials for LNP-mRNA formulation. , Besides nanomaterials and liposomes, analytical ultracentrifugation (AUC) has been utilized to size macromolecules. As sedimentation velocity separates particles, sedimentation coefficient distributions rely on the mass, density, and shape of each particle population in the sample.

## Discussion
In this review, we analyzed the mRNA-LNP-based drug modules that are improvements in gene editing, correlation to enhance the immune response, and activity against various infectious diseases and malignant tumors. However, multiple studies have shown the precise mechanism of LNP, and the important factors are links to accuracy, sensitivity, and stability. LNPs activate the immune system by engaging with pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) on antigen-presenting cells (APCs), including toll-like receptors (TLRs). Additionally, pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, are released as a result of the interaction between LNPs and PPRs, indicating the activation of an innate immune response ( ). 
 In strengthening the LNP formulation with mRNA for respiratory infections, a recent study reported that the titres generated by mucoadhesive films containing mRNA/LNP used as booster doses were comparable to those of a standard intramuscular vaccine. Furthermore, they generated local IgA titres, potentially improving immune protection against respiratory infections. In contrast, the efficacy of mRNA-LNP inhaled therapy is currently constrained by shear forces during nebulisation and the presence of lung mucus, indicating a need for further advancements. Nanoparticle modification using pulmonary surfactant lipids enhances infiltration through the lung barrier and reduces clearance from lung tissue. , Similarly, using mRNAs that encode protein adjuvants in LNPs instead of antigens might increase the immunogenicity of current vaccines. In contrast, protective vaccines that boost T-cell-mediated cellular immunity and humoral response are still challenging to develop. The LNP-encapsulated mRNA enhances immune responses in various viral infections. However, the T cells are activated by mRNA vaccines, which are modified to evade the immune system and are delivered via lipid nanoparticles for improved efficacy. 
On the other hand, the factors associated with the formulation of mRNA-LNP are crucial ( ), since LNP that carries mRNA into antigen-presenting cells is a critical challenge for mRNA vaccine development. Several ionizable lipid formulations with great encapsulation efficiency have been tested for several mRNA vaccines; thus, choosing one is crucial when deciding on LNP components. In stability conditions, the LNP-Luc formulation remained stable at 4 °C for at least seven days after freezing, indicating considerable stability. LNP-Luc retained its physical properties and mRNA transport efficiency in a cryoprotective buffer containing 12% tris-sucrose, unlike the new formulation ( ). According to Alameh et al., the LNP formulation may serve as an adjuvant for recombinant protein vaccines, facilitating the development of highly effective next-generation protein-based vaccines. Additionally, the study indicated that RIG-I/MDA5 and most TLRs do not recognise LNP. Mice without MyD88 and MAVS showed largely unchanged Tfh and GC B cell responses to rHA + eLNP immunization. Future research will examine LNP recognition and IL-6 control. Tfh and GC B cells showed reduced stimulation from mRNA-LNP vaccinations in MyD88-deficient mice. Similar to COVID-19 vaccinations, 1-methylpseudouridine-modified mRNA vaccines enhanced protein translation and decreased inflammation triggered by PAMP sensing. , , , 
 Therefore, innovative nucleic acid drugs target genetic and transcriptome disorders. mRNA and siRNA are used to regulate intracellular protein levels. After cytosolic transport, siRNAs activate the RNA interference (RNAi) pathway to silence specific post-transcriptional genes. In vitro mRNA can induce the synthesis of therapeutic proteins and antigens. In vivo RNA treatments face challenges with nuclease degradation, tissue distribution, and membrane transport, leading to their encapsulation in synthetic nanoparticles. , Thus, there are immense research prospects on NPs and transcriptomic studies. Among the NPs studied, LNPs are the most effective at carrying RNA. Furthermore, gene editing using LNPs is a recent advancement in treating malignant tumors and infectious diseases.

## Conclusion
This study evaluates the effectiveness of mRNA-LNP in gene editing with Cas9 techniques to enhance outcomes in clinical and diagnostic research. LNP-based drug delivery focuses on treating various infectious disorders and highlights the factors related to mRNA-LNP formation that enhance immune responses to these conditions. Current studies and findings are bridging the knowledge gap about mRNA-LNP.
