The role of UBE2C, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, in gastric cancer progression was investigated. Data from TCGA and GEO datasets revealed significant upregulation of UBE2C in gastric cancer tissues, with a negative correlation between UBE2C and ACSL4, a key regulator of ferroptosis. The expression of UBE2C was manipulated in gastric cancer cell lines, and silencing of UBE2C significantly reduced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Additionally, an increase in reactive oxygen species (ROS) and iron ion accumulation suggested that UBE2C inhibition could promote ferroptosis. Further experiments showed that UBE2C ubiquitinates ACSL4, leading to its degradation, which suppressed ferroptosis in gastric cancer cells. Rescue experiments confirmed that silencing ACSL4 reversed the effects of UBE2C on cell proliferation and migration. In vivo experiments with GSH treatment alleviated the tumor-promoting effects of UBE2C overexpression, further supporting the regulatory role of UBE2C in ferroptosis. These findings suggest that UBE2C-mediated inhibition of ferroptosis through the ubiquitination of ACSL4 contributes to gastric cancer progression, and targeting this pathway may offer a new therapeutic strategy for gastric cancer.

## Introduction
Gastric cancer (GC) is the fifth most commonly diagnosed malignancy globally [ ], with a notably poor prognosis; in China, the 5-year survival rate is approximately 70% [ , ]. Despite surgical resection being a standard treatment for patients eligible for surgery, local recurrence or distant metastasis occurs in approximately 60% of these patients [ ]. Furthermore, around one-third of patients present with peritoneal metastasis at the time of diagnosis, rendering them ineligible for surgical intervention [ ].
UBE2C, a critical E3 ubiquitin ligase, plays a central role in regulating the cell cycle by mediating the ubiquitination of key cell cycle proteins [ , ]. Numerous studies have highlighted UBE2C as an oncogenic protein involved in cancer progression. For instance, the UBE2C/CDH1/DEPTOR axis has been shown to regulate both cell cycle progression and autophagy, positioning UBE2C as a potential therapeutic target in cancers with Kras mutations [ ]. Additionally, the FOXM1-ZIC2-UBE2C-mTOR signaling pathway has been implicated in the progression of clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), marking UBE2C as a promising prognostic marker and therapeutic target [ ].
In this study, we aim to explore the role of UBE2C in gastric cancer by focusing on its interaction with potential regulatory sites. By investigating new mechanisms contributing to the pathogenesis of gastric cancer, we seek to uncover novel regulatory pathways that could be leveraged for therapeutic purposes, ultimately leading to more effective treatment options for this challenging malignancy. The findings from this research will not only enhance our understanding of the molecular dynamics at play in gastric cancer but also emphasize the broader relevance of ubiquitin-mediated regulation in cancer biology.

## Materials and methods

## Database analysis
The TCGA dataset included 375 gastric cancer and 391 normal tissue samples, while the GSE27342 dataset contained 80 gastric cancer and 80 matched non-tumor samples. Differential mRNA expression was analyzed using the Limma package (version 3.40.2), and the Benjamini-Hochberg method was applied to adjust p-values for multiple comparisons [ ]., with adjusted p-values used to minimize false positives. A threshold of adjusted p-value < 0.05 and log2 fold change > 1 or < −1 was applied to define significant mRNA differential expression. Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted using the ClusterProfiler package in R [ ]. The ggplot2 package was used to visualize the differential expression of genes, with statistical significance considered when p-values were < 0.05 [ ].

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Tumor sections were subjected to standard deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval procedures. Immunostaining was performed using Histostain™-Plus Kits (Bioss, SP-0022). Following blocking, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody targeting UBE2C (Abcam, ab252940) at a dilution of 1:500. Subsequently, sections were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature and developed using DAB. Hematoxylin was used for nuclear counterstaining.

## Prussian blue staining
Iron deposition was detected using the Prussian Blue Iron Stain Kit (YEASEN, 60533ES20), following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, paraffin-embedded tissue sections underwent standard deparaffinization and rehydration procedures as described in the IHC method. Sections were then incubated with an equal-volume mixture of 2% potassium ferrocyanide and 2% hydrochloric acid for 30 min, followed by thorough washing with distilled water.

## ROS detection
ROS generation was assessed using a standard ROS detection kit (Biyuntian, S0033M). Gastric cancer cells were treated with DCFH-DA at a concentration of 10 µM for 20 min at 37 °C, followed by PBS washing to remove unabsorbed DCFH-DA. ROS levels were quantified by fluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry (488 nm excitation and 525 nm emission).

## Cell culture and transfection
Human gastric cancer cell lines AGS, SNU-1, NCI-N87, HGC-27, and MKN-45, as well as the gastric epithelial cell line GES-1, were purchased from Procell (China) and cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. For overexpression experiments, plasmids for Myc-tagged ACSL4 and Flag-UBE2C were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen), following the manufacturer’s instructions. After 24 h, cells were treated with 50 µg/ml cycloheximide (CHX, Sigma-Aldrich) for the indicated time points, followed by lysis for Western blot analysis. Transfection efficiency was verified by Western blotting at 48 h post-transfection.

## ShRNA and SiRNA construction and transfection
Lentiviral shRNAs targeting UBE2C and a negative control (shNC) were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Lentiviral particles were prepared using a standard protocol. siRNAs targeting UBE2C and a negative control siRNA (siNC) were synthesized by GenePharma and transiently transfected into HEK293T or gastric cancer cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. siUBE2C: CCUGCAAGAAACCUACUCA.

## Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)
RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), and cDNA synthesis was performed with the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems). qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Gene expression was normalized to β-actin. Primer sequences are available upon request. Primer are as follows: UBE2C: F: GATGACCCTCATGGCAGTGG, R: GGGCTCAACCGAGGCTTAAT. ACSL4: F: GGCACGCGGTTCCTTTTT, R: AGCCGACAATAAAGTACGCAA. GAPDH: F: AATGGGCAGCCGTTAGGAAA, R: GCGCCCAATACGACCAAATC。.

## Western blot and immunoprecipitation
Cell lysates were prepared in RIPA buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit. 30ug protein amounts were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked and incubated with primary antibodies against UBE2C (Abcam, ab252940), ACSL4 (CST, #38493), FTH (Abcam, ab75973), and GAPDH (Abcam, ab8245), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Bands were visualized using the ECL detection system. For immunoprecipitation, cell lysates were incubated with anti-UBE2C or anti-ACSL4 antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by protein A/G beads. Immunocomplexes were washed, eluted, and analyzed by Western blot.

## Immunoprecipitation-coupled mass spectrometry (IP-MS)
Cell lysates were prepared 24 h post-transfection for immunoprecipitation using anti-UBE2C antibodies. Proteins were resolved by SDS-PAGE, stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue, and specific bands were excised for LC-MS/MS analysis. The spectra were analyzed with MaxQuant software, and only proteins identified by at least two unique peptides were considered for analysis.

## Ubiquitination assay
To assess the ubiquitination status of ACSL4, HEK293T or gastric cancer cells were transfected with HA-tagged ubiquitin (HA-Ub), along with either Flag-tagged UBE2C overexpression plasmid or UBE2C-targeting siRNA. For K48-specific linkage experiments, plasmids expressing HA-Ub-K48-only constructs (Addgene) were co-transfected. Cells were treated with 10 µM MG132 for 8 h before harvesting. Lysates were precleared and subjected to immunoprecipitation with anti-ACSL4 antibody (CST, #38493) overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with protein A/G agarose beads (Thermo Fisher) for 2 h. After washing, the bound proteins were resolved by SDS-PAGE and analyzed by Western blotting using anti-HA and anti-ACSL4 antibodies. Input samples were analyzed in parallel to verify protein expression.

## Cell viability assay
Cell proliferation was assessed using the CCK-8 assay (Dojindo). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates, treated as indicated, and CCK-8 solution was added at the specified time points. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## Transwell assay
In the Transwell assay, cells were seeded in the upper chamber with matrigel. After 24 h, non-invasive cells were removed with a cotton swab. The cells at the chamber’s base were fixed with methanol and stained with crystal violet. Five random fields were captured using a Leica microscope.

## Wound healing assay
Cells were plated in 24-well plates and cultured until confluence. Wounds were created using a 200 µl micropipette, followed by washing with PBS. Serum-free medium was added, and images were taken at 6-hour intervals over 24 h. The results were analyzed using ImageJ software.

## Immunofluorescence (IF)
Cells were fixed, permeabilized, and blocked before incubation with primary antibodies against UBE2C and ACSL4. After washing, cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were captured using a confocal microscope.

## ELISA detection
MDA levels were measured using the MDA assay kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Fe²⁺ content was determined using the iron ion detection assay kit (Solarbio, BC5410), and GSH and NADPH levels were quantified using commercial ELISA kits (COIBO BIO). Absorbance was measured at 450 nm for all assays.

## Xenograft tumor growth
Nude mice were subcutaneously injected with AGS wild-type or sh-UBE2C cells. GSH (MCE, HY-D0187) was administered by intragastric gavage at a dose of 100 mg/kg per day for 14 consecutive days [ ]. Tumor growth was monitored by measuring tumor dimensions with calipers, and tumor volume was calculated. Animals were euthanized using carbon dioxide inhalation, in accordance with institutional guidelines. Tumors were harvested for further analysis, following institutional animal ethics guidelines.

## Statistical analysis
All Western blot experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates and representative results are shown. Other in vitro assays were also conducted in triplicate unless otherwise stated. Data were expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA followed by post-hoc tests for multiple comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001). Analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0).

## Results

## High expression of UBE2C in gastric cancer and its association with ferroptosis
We first analyzed the transcriptomic data of gastric cancer and adjacent normal tissues from the TCGA and GEO (GSE27342) datasets. The counts of differentially expressed genes and the volcano plot are provided in Supplementary Tables 1 and Supplementary Fig.  . A total of 398 common differentially expressed genes were identified, including UBE2C and UBE2T from the ubiquitin ligase family (Fig.  A, B). Further validation in gastric cancer tissues and cell lines confirmed significant upregulation of UBE2C in both tumor tissues and cell lines (Fig.  C, D). To explore the potential mechanisms, we performed RNA sequencing on AGS cells with UBE2C knockdown, revealing 336 upregulated differentially expressed genes involved in ferroptosis and drug metabolism pathways (Fig.  D, E). Subsequently, we assessed ferroptosis-related markers in patient tissues. Immunofluorescence analysis showed decreased ROS levels in gastric cancer tissues, while Prussian blue staining indicated reduced iron accumulation in tumor tissues (Fig.  F, G).

 

## Silencing of UBE2C inhibits proliferation, migration, and ferroptosis in gastric cancer cells
We silenced UBE2C in two gastric cancer cell lines, AGS and NCI-N87, and confirmed the efficiency of knockdown at both the mRNA and protein levels (Fig.  A, B). Silencing of UBE2C significantly reduced the proliferation, clonogenic ability, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells (Fig.  C-F). Additionally, UBE2C knockdown led to an increase in ROS levels and iron ion content in gastric cancer cells (Fig.  G, H), suggesting that UBE2C inhibition may promote ferroptosis in gastric cancer.

 

## UBE2C mediates the ubiquitination and degradation of ACSL4
To explore the involvement of UBE2C in gastric cancer cell proliferation, immunoprecipitation (IP) assays were performed using UBE2C antibodies in gastric cancer cell lines, followed by protein analysis through SDS-PAGE, Coomassie Brilliant Blue staining, and LC-MS/MS (Fig.  A). This analysis identified ACSL4 as a possible binding partner of UBE2C (Fig.  B). In order to confirm this interaction, HEK293 cells were co-transfected with Flag-UBE2C and Myc-ACSL4 constructs, and reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation assays were carried out. The results demonstrated that both anti-Myc and anti-Flag antibodies successfully co-precipitated UBE2C and ACSL4, respectively (Fig.  C). Furthermore, endogenous co-immunoprecipitation of UBE2C and ACSL4 in gastric cancer cells using anti-UBE2C and anti-ACSL4 antibodies confirmed their interaction (Fig.  D). Confocal microscopy revealed that ACSL4, which is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, colocalized with UBE2C (Fig.  E). To further characterize this interaction, computational docking analysis was conducted using PyDock, which provided the highest-ranked predicted model of UBE2C-ACSL4 binding (Fig.  F). In addition, tissue sections from the patient cohort showed that samples with high UBE2C expression exhibited markedly lower levels of ACSL4 (Fig.  G).

 

## UBE2C regulates ACSL4 stability via ubiquitination in gastric cancer
To examine the role of UBE2C as an E3 ubiquitin ligase in the regulation of ACSL4, gastric cancer cells were used to assess the expression of ACSL4 in AGS cells overexpressing UBE2C and NCI-N87 cells with UBE2C knockdown. A negative correlation between UBE2C and ACSL4 expression was observed (Fig.  A). Moreover, ACSL4’s half-life was significantly shortened in cells with overexpressed UBE2C, while it was substantially prolonged when UBE2C was silenced, as demonstrated by cycloheximide (CHX) chase assays (Fig.  B). Treatment with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 led to a partial restoration of ACSL4 levels, reversing the reduction in ACSL4 caused by UBE2C knockdown, and also reversing the decreased ACSL4 expression associated with UBE2C overexpression (Fig.  C). These findings suggest that UBE2C regulates ACSL4 stability through its ubiquitination activity, leading to proteasomal degradation. To further determine the ubiquitin linkage involved in ACSL4 degradation, we performed ubiquitination assays using HA-tagged ubiquitin mutants. Compared to wild-type HA-Ub, overexpression of K48-only Ub significantly increased ACSL4 ubiquitination, while K63-only Ub had minimal effect. These findings suggest that UBE2C promotes ACSL4 degradation predominantly via K48-linked polyubiquitination. Consistently, in the input fractions, UBE2C knockdown led to increased ACSL4 protein levels, while K48-only Ub reduced ACSL4 expression. These data support a model in which UBE2C facilitates K48-dependent ubiquitin conjugation to ACSL4, targeting it for proteasomal degradation. (Fig.  D). These results indicate that UBE2C directly facilitates the ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of ACSL4.

 

## UBE2C-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of ACSL4 inhibits ferroptosis in gastric cancer
To investigate whether UBE2C regulates gastric cancer cell functions through the ACSL4 pathway, we performed rescue experiments. We found that silencing ACSL4 reversed the effects of UBE2C on gastric cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion (Fig.  A, B, C). Moreover, ACSL4 silencing significantly diminished the inhibitory effects of UBE2C on Fe²⁺ accumulation, MDA and 4-HNE levels (Fig.  D) and ROS generation (Fig.  E, F). Consistently, Western blot analysis showed reduced expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11 following UBE2C knockdown, which was significantly rescued upon ACSL4 silencing (Fig.  G). These findings suggest that UBE2C inhibits ferroptosis in gastric cancer cells by ubiquitinating and degrading ACSL4.

 

## GSH ameliorates the tumorigenic effect of UBE2C in gastric cancer in vivo
Given that GSH prevents the accumulation of lipid peroxides and inhibits ferroptosis by supporting GPX4 antioxidant function, regulating intracellular iron metabolism, and maintaining redox balance [ , ], we used GSH to counteract the ferroptotic effects of UBE2C in a mouse model. The results showed that tumors from UBE2C overexpression mice exhibited significantly smaller size and lower weight; however, GSH treatment led to a noticeable increase in tumor volume and weight (Fig.  A, B). Mice with UBE2C interference showed significant elevation in Fe²⁺ levels and MDA content, which were mitigated by GSH (Fig.  C, E). Additionally, GSH administration restored the significantly reduced GSH levels and NADPH content in UBE2C interference mice (Fig.  D, F). Furthermore, UBE2C interference led to an upregulation of FTH and ACSL4 protein levels in the tumors, which was suppressed by GSH (Fig.  G). These findings suggest that UBE2C promotes tumorigenesis in vivo by inhibiting ferroptosis pathways.

 

## Discussion
Ferroptosis, a distinct form of cell death, results from the iron-dependent accumulation of lipid peroxides, a concept first introduced by Dixon et al. [ , ]. This type of cell death is specifically marked by the buildup of lipid peroxides that is reliant on iron. ACSL4, an enzyme involved in converting fatty acids into fatty acyl-CoA esters, plays a pivotal role in lipid metabolism [ , ]. Notably, research demonstrated that ACSL4 is crucial for the induction of ferroptosis [ , ], while utilizing CRISPR-based genetic screening and analysis of ferroptosis-resistant cells, further established ACSL4 as a fundamental factor in this process [ ].
Considerable research has focused on understanding the biological mechanisms driven by UBE2C overexpression in gastric cancer. It has been demonstrated that UBE2C influences the stability of the NRF2 protein in gastric cancer cells. Specifically, UBE2C overexpression promotes the interaction between p62 and KEAP1 while inhibiting the binding between NRF2 and KEAP1 [ ]. Moreover, UBE2C is known to contribute to epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through the activation of the Wnt/β-catenin and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways, which are regulated by phosphorylation levels of Aurora-A [ ]. Additionally, overexpression of UBE2C has been linked to chromosomal instability, disrupting the cell cycle and contributing to the poor prognosis of intestinal-type gastric cancer [ ]. However, despite these findings, no studies have yet reported the involvement of UBE2C in ferroptosis in gastric cancer.
This study reveals a novel mechanism through which UBE2C regulates ACSL4 stability via ubiquitination, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis and promoting tumor cell survival in gastric cancer. Transcriptomic analyses of gastric cancer tissues and cell lines identified UBE2C as a key regulator of ACSL4 expression, with its overexpression inversely correlating with ACSL4 levels. The interaction between UBE2C and ACSL4 was further validated through immunoprecipitation, Western blotting, and RNA sequencing, confirming UBE2C’s role in regulating ferroptosis-related pathways. Notably, UBE2C was found to enhance gastric cancer progression by suppressing ferroptosis through the degradation of ACSL4. This finding highlights the importance of ferroptosis regulation in gastric cancer survival, suggesting that targeting ferroptosis pathways may offer a promising therapeutic strategy. Additionally, UBE2C inhibition increased ROS levels and iron accumulation, sensitizing gastric cancer cells to oxidative stress. These results propose that targeting UBE2C or modulating ferroptosis could be a potential therapeutic approach, particularly when combined with conventional therapies such as chemotherapy, providing a strategy to overcome treatment resistance in gastric cancer.
Furthermore, the in vivo experiments in this study, using a GSH-based ferroptosis rescue model, highlighted the potential of targeting the UBE2C-ACSL4 axis to promote ferroptosis and inhibit tumor growth in gastric cancer. The administration of GSH alleviated the effects of UBE2C overexpression, restoring redox balance and reducing tumor progression. This suggests that ferroptosis modulation, specifically through UBE2C inhibition, could be a valuable therapeutic approach for managing gastric cancer, particularly in cases with advanced or metastatic disease.
While this study provides significant insights into the molecular mechanisms driving gastric cancer progression, there are several limitations that should be addressed in future research. One limitation is the focus on UBE2C as a single target in regulating ferroptosis. Although UBE2C-mediated ubiquitination of ACSL4 plays a central role in the inhibition of ferroptosis, it is likely that other E3 ligases or signaling pathways are involved in modulating ACSL4 stability and ferroptosis in gastric cancer. Future studies could investigate the broader network of regulatory molecules involved in this process, including the potential cross-talk between UBE2C and other E3 ligases, kinases, and transcription factors. Additionally, the role of other ferroptosis regulators, such as GPX4, FTH, and ferroportin, should be explored in combination with UBE2C inhibition to better understand the full spectrum of ferroptosis regulation in gastric cancer.
In conclusion, this study adds valuable knowledge to the growing body of literature on the role of ferroptosis in gastric cancer and highlights the potential of UBE2C as a therapeutic target. By providing evidence for the regulatory role of UBE2C in ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis, the study opens new avenues for the development of novel therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating ferroptosis to treat gastric cancer. However, further studies are needed to explore the clinical implications of these findings and to identify additional therapeutic targets in the regulation of ferroptosis in gastric cancer.

## Limitations
This study did not explore whether other E3 ligases or regulatory pathways contribute to ACSL4 modulation following UBE2C knockdown. In addition, a GSH-only treatment group was not included in the in vivo experiments, limiting the interpretation of GSH’s independent effects.
