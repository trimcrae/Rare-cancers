## Abstract
Subarachnoid hemorrhage leads to a series of pathological changes, including vascular spasm, cellular apoptosis, blood–brain barrier damage, cerebral edema, and white matter injury. Microglia, which are the key immune cells in the central nervous system, maintain homeostasis in the neural environment, support neurons, mediate apoptosis, participate in immune regulation, and have neuroprotective effects. Increasing evidence has shown that microglia play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of subarachnoid hemorrhage and affect the process of injury and the prognosis of subarachnoid hemorrhage. Moreover, microglia play certain neuroprotective roles in the recovery phase of subarachnoid hemorrhage. Several approaches aimed at modulating microglia function are believed to attenuate subarachnoid hemorrhage injury. This provides new targets and ideas for the treatment of subarachnoid hemorrhage. However, an in-depth and comprehensive summary of the role of microglia after subarachnoid hemorrhage is still lacking. This review describes the activation of microglia after subarachnoid hemorrhage and their roles in the pathological processes of vasospasm, neuroinflammation, neuronal apoptosis, blood–brain barrier disruption, cerebral edema, and cerebral white matter lesions. It also discusses the neuroprotective roles of microglia during recovery from subarachnoid hemorrhage and therapeutic advances aimed at modulating microglial function after subarachnoid hemorrhage. Currently, microglia in subarachnoid hemorrhage are targeted with TLR inhibitors, nuclear factor-κB and STAT3 pathway inhibitors, glycine/tyrosine kinases, NLRP3 signaling pathway inhibitors, Gasdermin D inhibitors, vincristine receptor α receptor agonists, ferroptosis inhibitors, genetic modification techniques, stem cell therapies, and traditional Chinese medicine. However, most of these are still being evaluated at the laboratory stage. More clinical studies and data on subarachnoid hemorrhage are required to improve the treatment of subarachnoid hemorrhage.
Keywords: apoptosis, blood–brain barrier, brain edema, microglia, neuroinflammation, neuron, neuroprotection, subarachnoid hemorrhage, vasoconstriction, white matter injury

## Introduction
Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) is a clinical phenomenon characterized by a rupture of intracranial blood vessels that results in the direct influx of blood into the subarachnoid space. It is associated with a mortality rate and is a debilitating ailment with an intricate and multifaceted pathogenesis (Muehlschlegel, 2018; Ziu et al., 2024).
An expanding body of literature reveals the diverse functions of microglia, encompassing the maintenance of neural environmental equilibrium, support for neurons, mediation of apoptosis, involvement in immune regulation, and the exertion of neuroprotective effects (Subhramanyam et al., 2019; Jia et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022b). Microglia play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of SAH, thereby impacting both the onset and prognosis of SAH. Additionally, in the post-SAH recovery phase, microglia contribute to neuroprotection (Xia et al., 2021). Ongoing investigations aimed at modulating the activities of microglia hold considerable promise in mitigating post-SAH damage and offer novel targets and perspectives for the treatment of SAH.
This review synthesizes prior research delineating the involvement of microglia in SAH and furnishes an overview of the current progress in therapeutic strategies that target microglia in the context of SAH.

## Search Strategy
We conducted electronic searches of the PubMed database using the following keywords: “subarachnoid hemorrhage,” “microglia,” “microglia activation,” “vasoconstriction,” “neuronal apoptosis,” “neuroinflammation,” “blood–brain barrier,” “brain edema,” “white matter injury,” and “neuroprotection.” Various combinations of these keywords were employed to comprehensively retrieve full-text articles that were published in English between January 2000 and January 2024. Further screening of titles and abstracts was performed to ensure relevance to our research objectives, and if deemed pertinent, the entire article was accessed. Additionally, following detailed examination of the literature, we also included two reference articles (Giulian and Baker, 1986; Alliot et al., 1999). These studies were primarily conducted on mammals, such as rodents and humans. The majority of the selected literature (> 80% of all citations) was published between 2017 and 2024.

## Subarachnoid Hemorrhage
SAH is a prevalent, yet hazardous, neurological emergency that occurs within the subarachnoid space, typically arising from vascular rupture (Muehlschlegel, 2018; Tan et al., 2024; Ziu et al., 2024). This condition not only induces physiological and structural alterations to the nervous system but also exerts profound impacts on the patient’s quality of life and prognosis (Macdonald and Schweizer, 2017).
The principal symptom of SAH is the sudden onset of “the worst headache of one’s life.” Approximately 70% of patients experience headaches, with 50% characterizing them as a sudden, thunderclap-like headache reaching peak intensity within 1 minute of onset. Headache is the only symptom in approximately 50% of SAH cases. In the remaining cases, symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, transient or sustained loss of consciousness, or focal neurological deficits may be present (Chou, 2021).
The cerebral damage that results from SAH occurs in two distinct phases: the early brain injury (EBI) phase, which occurs within the initial 72 hours, and the subsequent delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) phase, which manifests between days 4 and 14 post-onset (Rabinstein, 2013). EBI denotes the direct brain injury and consequential pathophysiological changes that transpire within the initial 72 hours post-SAH, prior to the onset of cerebral vasospasm (Rass and Helbok, 2019). DCI is a complication of SAH and is also referred to as symptomatic cerebral vasospasm. It is marked by the appearance of fresh focal neurological deficits or a decrease in the GCS score of 2 or more points that is not attributable to other causes, such as hydrocephalus, rebleeding, or seizures (Koenig, 2012). Approximately one-third of patients with SAH experience delayed neurologic decline due to DCI 4–14 days post-bleeding. SAH triggers systemic responses that affect the pulmonary system (pulmonary edema and acute respiratory distress syndrome), cardiac function (arrhythmias and impaired contractile function), and disrupts blood and electrolyte equilibrium, potentially culminating in systemic inflammatory response syndrome (Koenig, 2012). The persistent activation of microglia, marked by mild reactive gliosis during disease progression post–brain injury, has emerged as a pivotal factor in DCI. This cellular process plays a critical role in the development of the complications of SAH, has long-term ramifications, and contributes to a spectrum of symptoms, including cognitive decline following SAH (Dodd et al., 2021b).

## Microglia
Microglia represent the brain’s resident immune cells and serve as the parenchymal macrophages of the brain and spinal cord. Similar to perivascular macrophages, they belong to the innate immune system and constitute 10%–15% of neuroglial cells. Microglia are pivotal immune cells that respond to various acute brain injuries. They interact with numerous other cells in the central nervous system (CNS), including neurons, astrocytes, and oligodendrocytes. They play crucial roles in maintaining the stability of the CNS microenvironment and supporting neuronal functions, and they participate in immune regulation (Rodríguez-Gómez et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2023). Microglia are busy and vigilant housekeepers in the adult brain (Nimmeriahn et al., 2005; Nguyen and Chauhan, 2023).
Microglia have long been believed to originate from the neural ectoderm, much like other resident glial cells and neurons in the CNS. The origin of microglia is closely associated with neural ectoderm differentiation and migration. However, it is now established that these cells are part of the mononuclear phagocyte system and are immune cells (Prinz et al., 2019). Hematopoietic stem cells are precursor cells with the potential for “primitive” and “definitive” erythroid and myeloid lineage differentiation. Currently, it is believed that hematopoietic stem cells are depleted before birth and establish and maintain definitive lymphoid and myeloid hematopoiesis throughout adulthood (Ginhoux et al., 2010). Microglia initially develop from c-KitloCD41lo progenitors, which arise from the yolk sac around embryonic day 7.25 and can potentially generate primitive erythrocytes (Ginhoux et al., 2010). Additionally, studies have shown that microglia are established early in embryonic development, appearing much earlier than other glial cells (Alliot et al., 1999; Herbomel et al., 2001).
Giulian and Baker (1986) were the first to use fluorescence-activated cell sorting to isolate microglia. Jung et al. (2000) established the C–X3–C motif chemokine receptor 1-green fluorescent protein mouse line, enabling in vivo visualization of microglia. Microglia were initially believed to be the only adult macrophage population that originated from early primitive hematopoiesis (Hoeffel et al., 2015; Perdiguero and Geissmann, 2016). Subsequently, it was found that embryonically derived microglia from early primitive hematopoiesis also expanded to other adult CNS macrophages within the blood–brain barrier (BBB). In peripheral barrier tissues, embryonically derived macrophages are gradually replaced by circulating blood monocytes (Bain et al., 2014). Microglia and barrier-associated macrophages of the CNS (Mrdjen et al., 2018), also known as CNS-associated macrophages (CAMs; Prinz et al., 2017), are the two main resident macrophage populations in the CNS; the latter is located outside the CNS parenchyma, in sites such as perivascular spaces, the meninges, and the choroid plexus (Kierdorf et al., 2019; Prinz et al., 2019). It is worth noting that the choroid plexus seems to be an exception within the CNS macrophage nests, as over time, monocytes derived from hematopoietic stem cells gradually become the contribution pool for stromal choroid plexus macrophages (Goldmann et al., 2016). Two notable features shared by microglia and CAMs are their common prenatal origin and longevity. Similar to microglia, CAMs are largely of embryonic origin, and both exhibit low self-renewal rates and persist for extended periods (Goldmann et al., 2016). They also share some marker genes, such as genes for the ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1, the colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor, and the fractalkine receptor (Jordão et al., 2019). Due to these extensive similarities between the two, microglia are often confused with CAMs during traditional bulk analyses. However, with the advancement of gene sequencing, it is now possible to clearly differentiate between microglia and CAMs based on their distinct transcriptomic features using single-cell RNA sequencing (Sousa et al., 2018).
Under normal conditions, microglia exhibit highly branched structures with three or four levels of branching. The branches rarely overlap, and this branched morphology is commonly denoted as “resting” microglia (van Dijk et al., 2016). With the occurrence of inflammation, infection, trauma, or other neurological disorders, microglia are rapidly activated, assuming an activated state characterized by enhanced phagocytic capabilities. Activated microglia display enlarged cell bodies and shortened processes, and they adopt a round or rod-shaped morphology. With further activation and modulation, these microglia lose their processes, adopting an amoeboid morphology indicative of heightened phagocytic functionality (Colonna and Butovsky, 2017; Xu et al., 2020a).
There are two subtypes of activated microglia: M1 (classical activation) and M2 (alternative activation). In addition, the M2 subtype can be further subdivided into M2a (regenerative and reparative), M2b (immune regulatory), and M2c (acquired deactivation) microglia (Ginhoux et al., 2010). The M1 and M2 subtypes of microglia exert cytotoxic and cytoprotective effects on neurons, respectively, contributing to a nuanced activation mechanism (Kwon and Koh, 2020).
Microglia possess the ability to sense changes in the external environment and subsequently adapt their roles within the internal microenvironment. However, an associated drawback is the potential for the overactivation of pro-inflammatory microglia. This causes or sustains chronic inflammation, resulting in prolonged neurological damage to primary neurons, axonal degeneration, and other processes associated with systemic inflammation (Hickman et al., 2018; Kwon and Koh, 2020; ).
 Crucially, microglia play a pivotal role in hematoma clearance and brain tissue repair (Chen et al., 2022a). In inflammatory responses, microglia often exhibit dual roles, with an initial detrimental impact during the early stages of EBI in SAH and subsequently assuming a protective role in the later stages of SAH (Chen and Wong, 2021).

## Microglia and Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

## Activation of microglia after subarachnoid hemorrhage
In recent years, new insights have been gained about microglia, unveiling their close association with the occurrence and progression of SAH (Chen and Wong, 2021; Chen et al., 2022a; ).
 

## Acute phase
Within the initial 24 hours following SAH, microglia manifest morphological indicators of activation (Chen and Wong, 2021). Markers linked to M1-type microglia, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), significantly increase (Chen and Wong, 2021). Studies have demonstrated the presence of activated microglia in the cortex within the first 24 hours post-SAH, with a gradual infiltration by monocytes observed only after 72 hours post-SAH. Particularly within the initial 48 hours post-SAH, the early neuroinflammatory response is predominantly instigated and intensified by resident microglia (Xu et al., 2019). In this acute phase, the activated microglia are primarily M1 microglia, which are pro-inflammatory. Their pro-inflammatory effects promote cell death, including the neurodegenerative apoptosis of neurons (Chen and Wong, 2021). The resultant neurodegenerative apoptosis and necrosis of neurons lead to the further release of inflammatory molecules (alarm molecules) such as the high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) that subsequently activate a substantial number of microglia. The activation of microglia induces the recruitment of blood-derived innate immune cells, increased BBB permeability, and additional neuronal loss (Zeyu et al., 2021).
An experiment that investigated the translocator protein in mouse brain tissue within 72 hours post-SAH revealed that the translocator protein co-localized with the microglial cell-specific marker ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1 at 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours after modeling. The translocator protein was implicated in the activation of microglia, with its expression initially increasing and subsequently decreasing over time. This aligns with the activation pattern of microglia post-SAH (Zhou et al., 2019).

## Subacute phase
Between 24 and 72 hours post-SAH, activated microglia undergo an acute upregulation of the p75 neurotrophin receptor, contributing to the monitoring of the temporal progression of microglial cell infiltration. Studies indicate a further escalation in the activation of microglia during this phase (Xu et al., 2019).
The turning point in the escalating trend of M1 microglia occurs on the third day post-SAH. Subsequently, the levels of M1 microglia begin to decline, while M2 markers begin to increase on the third day post-SAH (Xu et al., 2019; Chen and Wong, 2021; Chen et al., 2022a). During this period, the aggregation and activation of microglia exert a pro-inflammatory effect on the brain parenchyma, inducing neuronal apoptosis and resulting in prolonged neuronal damage (Chen and Wong, 2021).
Gris et al. (2019) found that systemic IL-6 levels rapidly increased post-SAH in the plasma of 13 patients with SAH, while their interleukin-10 (IL-10) levels decreased. The temporal changes in other inflammatory factors, such as intercellular adhesion molecule-1, basic fibroblast growth factor, interleukin-7, interleukin-12p40, and monocyte chemoattractant protein 4, were different between SAH patients with favorable and unfavorable outcomes. Graetz et al. (2010) confirmed elevated levels of IL-6 in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), extracellular fluid, and plasma of all patients with aneurysmal SAH (aSAH). The study also demonstrated that high levels of IL-6 and CRP in plasma were associated with a poor prognosis in SAH. Sarrafzadeh et al. (2010) further demonstrated that in patients with symptomatic aSAH, the levels of IL-6 in the CSF predicted the occurrence of DCI. Simon and Grote (2021) further confirmed that IL-6 levels rapidly increased post-SAH in both patients with SAH and experimental SAH models using clinical studies. Coulibaly and Provencio (2020) found a significant increase in the expression of IL-4 in the CSF post-SAH, and exogenous administration of IL-4 improved neurological deficits post-SAH, reduced apoptosis, and promoted transformation to the M2 phenotype, thus alleviating neuroinflammatory responses.
As immune cells in the CNS, microglia interact with other cells to respond to physiological conditions. They are also the phagocytic cells that clear plaques, neurofibrillary tangles, and cellular debris within the CNS (Prinz et al., 2019). Xu et al. (2021b) confirmed that CX3C chemokine receptor 1 is highly expressed in microglia.

## Delayed injury phase
Between the 4th and 6th days post-SAH, the morphological activation of microglia is observed to be less than the levels observed within the initial 24 hours but remains higher than the baseline levels. Subsequently, it gradually increases, reaching a second peak around the 14th day, and then gradually subsides around the 28th day. Microglia undergo dynamic changes between their initial activation (24 hours), return to baseline (3 days), and reactivation (14 days) phases (Xu et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2022a). Between the 14th and 28th days, the expression of IL-6, Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), and TNF-α by microglia increases (Timis et al., 2021). In the late stage post-SAH, there is an upregulation of M2-associated genes and anti-inflammatory responses (IL-4 and transforming growth factor-β) throughout the entire brain (Schneider et al., 2018). The expression profile of inflammatory cytokines mirrors the dynamic changes in microglial polarization. These immune modulators have been shown to disrupt the BBB, increase BBB permeability, and induce the recruitment of additional peripheral immune cells, thereby, triggering neuroinflammation and causing neuronal damage (Schneider et al., 2018; Timis et al., 2021).
Schneider et al. (2015) observed the aggregation of intracranial inflammatory cells in both human SAH specimens and mouse models of SAH, which persists from days 4 to 28 post-SAH. Through bone marrow chimeric mouse tracking, wherein all peripherally derived immune cells were traced, they verified the exclusive CNS intrinsic microglial origin of these inflammatory cells, which were in an activated state. This experiment elucidated the activation of microglia and their cytokine profile following SAH. The experiment also found that the number of neuronal cell deaths significantly decreased with the depletion of microglia, thus, showing that the accumulation of microglia leads to secondary brain damage post-SAH (Schneider et al., 2015). Patsouris et al. (2023) found that 1 month post-SAH, a gradual reduction in the aggregation of microglia or macrophages and neutrophil death led to the gradual restoration of tissue homeostasis. However, the study showed that the mRNA expression levels of IL-6 and TNF-α remained significantly elevated at 1 and 2 months post-SAH. This study confirms the incomplete resolution of intracerebral inflammation post-SAH and indicates that the resolution of inflammation and the restoration of tissue homeostasis impact the prognosis of SAH (Patsouris et al., 2023).
Microglia play dual roles of both causing and protecting against neuronal damage in various injury contexts (Weiland et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023). Additionally, microglia may have other roles that are yet to be investigated. For example, in a post-mortem study, Koopman et al. (2023) discovered that in the brain tissue of patients with aSAH, the surface area of microglia around blood vessels with small thrombus formation was approximately 40% larger than that of those around vessels without thrombus formation. The surface area of microglia significantly increased after aSAH, suggesting a close association between microglia and small thrombus formation post-aSAH (Koopman et al., 2023). Another study has also indicated that microglia interact with microvessels to regulate brain energy metabolism (Liu et al., 2020c).
After inducing SAH in intercellular adhesion molecule-1 and p-selection glycoprotein ligand-1 gene knockout mice, Atangana et al. (2017) demonstrated in a chronic cranial window model that microglia, a crucial component of the brain’s intrinsic immune system, undergo inside-out activation triggered by inflammatory reactions in microvessels post-SAH. This activation promotes neuronal cell death, contributing to secondary brain injury following SAH. The experiments also revealed a significant reduction in neutrophil-endothelial cell interactions within the initial 7 days post-SAH. This inhibitory approach to reducing neutrophil recruitment to the endothelium markedly decreased microglial cell aggregation and neuronal cell death (Atangana et al., 2017). These findings support the notion that the chemotactic factors and neurodegenerative effects of microglia persist for an extended period post-SAH. This implies that even patients with intact neurological function during this period may not be entirely immune to the impact of delayed brain injury.
The accumulation of unphosphorylated signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) in cerebral arteries plays a crucial role in regulating the genes associated with late-stage cerebral ischemia and related pathological processes post-SAH (Zhou et al., 2022b). Li et al. (2018) performed a study using a rat SAH model with tumor necrosis factor-stimulated gene 6 protein knockdown and found that the detrimental effects of microglia were accompanied by more neurological deficits. Recombinant human tumor necrosis factor-stimulated gene 6 protein significantly improved brain injury, reduced pro-inflammatory mediators, and shifted microglia polarization toward a more anti-inflammatory phenotype within 24 hours post-SAH. This effect was achieved by modulating the levels of the suppressor of cytokine signaling 3/STAT3 axis.
In a SAH model using APOE–/– and APOE wild-type (WT) mice, Wu et al. (2019) found that ApoE deficiency increased microglial cell activation and was associated with inflammatory damage to neurons. The study suggests that ApoE may play a protective role against EBI post-SAH by regulating the inflammatory response through modulation of microglial cell activation.
Yang et al. (2023b) discovered that exogenous recombinant human sestrin2 enhances the polarization of M2-like microglia and inhibits the number of M1-like microglia post-SAH. Recombinant human sestrin2 significantly alleviates inflammatory damage and oxidative stress post-SAH. Additionally, the study suggests that the protective effect of recombinant human sestrin2 is associated with the activation of the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) signaling pathway.
Shao et al. (2023) found that the expression of cyclic guanosine monophosphate–adenosine monophosphate synthase (cGAS) in rat brain tissue significantly increased post-SAH, peaking at 24 hours. Blocking cGAS using RU.521 induced a transition in microglial phenotype from M1 to M2, leading to decreased secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, and an elevation in the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10. RU.521 regulates microglial polarization after SAH via the cGAS/stimulator of interferon genes (STING)/nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathway, resulting in enhanced neurological function and diminished neuroinflammation.
Many questions regarding the role of microglia in EBI and DCI post-SAH, including its functions, such as phagocytosis, inflammation, and neurogenesis, remain unanswered. Research focused on microglia has been a prominent area within SAH studies, and the aforementioned investigations offer novel molecular targets for SAH treatment. Ongoing research efforts hold promise for advancing the understanding and treatment of SAH ( ).
 

## Microglia are involved in the pathophysiological process after subarachnoid hemorrhage

## Microglia and vasoconstriction
Vasospasm is a lethal complication that ensues after SAH. Previous investigations have unveiled the involvement of microglia and TLR4 expression in inducing vasospasm during both the early and late stages of SAH (Wang et al., 2022a).
Noteworthy findings indicate that curcumin and hydrogen sulfide can potentially alleviate vasospasm by inhibiting the TLR4 pathway, thereby inducing the polarization of microglia towards the M2 phenotype (Gao et al., 2019; Duan et al., 2020). Furthermore, the long non-coding RNA fantom3_F730004F19 and CSF peroxidase 2 are also considered promising therapeutic targets (Liu et al., 2023d; Peng et al., 2023b).
Akamatsu et al. (2020) underscored the critical role of microglial heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and inhaled carbon monoxide in efficiently clearing blood and hemoglobin post-SAH, consequently mitigating neuronal apoptosis, necrosis, vasospasm, and cognitive impairment.
Hepatoma-derived growth factor (HDGF), a growth factor that is associated with normal development and known for its participation in liver development and regeneration, has been investigated by Chung et al. (2022). Their findings indicated elevated levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in CSF and serum post-SAH. Treatment with recombinant HDGF antibodies before and after SAH suppressed the upregulation of these pro-inflammatory factors, with no significant change observed in serum IL-6 levels. SAH upregulated the protein expression levels of pro-brain-derived neurotrophic factor (pro-BDNF) and NF-κB, while treatment with recombinant HDGF antibodies significantly reduced the protein expression levels of pro-BDNF and NF-κB. Pre-treatment with recombinant HDGF antibodies significantly attenuated the proliferation of microglia and reduced the apoptosis of neuronal cells. The study suggests that blocking HDGF can alleviate early brain tissue inflammation post-SAH, reducing neuronal apoptosis and vasospasm (Chung et al., 2022).
M1 microglia play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of cerebral vasospasm. They release inflammatory products that not only contribute to parenchymal damage but also disrupt the BBB, promote edema, increase intracranial pressure, and reduce cerebral perfusion pressure, potentially leading to secondary ischemic injury. Additionally, M1 microglial activation may disrupt cerebral autoregulation, resulting in cerebral ischemia or congestion (Wu et al., 2022). The aforementioned studies provide novel research targets for elucidating the role of microglia in cerebral vasospasm following SAH. Further in-depth research to unravel the specific mechanisms is required, offering new avenues for the treatment of SAH by targeting microglia.

## Microglia and neuronal apoptosis
TLR4 plays a crucial role in initiating neuronal apoptosis following SAH by detecting various pathogen-associated molecular patterns from microglia. The activation of microglial TLR4 occurs through interactions with ligands such as HMGB1, hemoglobin, and heme, triggering downstream signaling pathways via adaptor proteins. This cascade leads to the production of inflammatory cytokines and oxidative metabolites, ultimately resulting in neuronal apoptosis and tissue damage (Mathy et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022a).
Moreover, TLR4 has the ability to identify various pathogen-associated molecular patterns generated by pathogens in laboratory settings, such as lipopolysaccharides, along with danger-associated molecular patterns manifested by diverse compromised cells within the organism. Additionally, M1 microglia, which have increased expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), are involved in actively transporting antigens and promoting the synthesis of cytotoxic agents (Koopman et al., 2023). Upon exposure to these stimuli, microglia can trigger an inflammatory cascade, resulting in the secretion of corresponding pro-inflammatory cytokines (Heinz and Schneider, 2022).
The receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE), a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily, is expressed in neurons and microglia. Li et al. (2014) observed a significant increase in RAGE mRNA expression 6 hours post-SAH, peaking at 12 hours. Notably, RAGE expression is elevated near the subarachnoid clot in the cerebral cortex, particularly in neurons and microglia. RAGE can bind to inflammatory ligands like HMGB1, the S100 protein family, amyloid-β, and macrophage antigen complex-1 and thereby promote M1 microglial polarization and the release of TNF-α and IL-6, which are associated with neuronal apoptosis.
Lu et al. (2018) employed a co-culture system involving primary neurons and microglia, along with a TLR4 knockout mouse model, to explore the interaction between peroxiredoxin 2 (Prx2) and TLR4 on microglia following SAH. The investigation revealed that Prx2 interacts with TLR4 to activate microglia through the TLR4/myeloid differentiation primary response 88 (MyD88)/NF-κB signaling pathway. This activation results in the expression and release of pro-inflammatory factors, ultimately leading to neuronal apoptosis. The study further observed a positive correlation between serum Prx2 levels in patients with SAH and Hunt-Hess grading. The presence of Prx2 in CSF suggests its potential utility as an indicator for assessing brain damage and prognosis.
The receptor-interacting protein 3 (RIP3) acts as a pivotal regulator in programmed necrosis. Yuan et al. (2019) observed an elevated expression of RIP3 in rat brains following SAH, peaking at 24 hours post-injury. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of RIP3 or TNF-α mitigated SAH-induced brain damage, whereas RIP3 overexpression exacerbated brain and neuronal injury. Using an in vitro SAH model, RIP3-mediated programmed necrosis was validated in primary neurons treated with conditioned media from oxyhemoglobin-activated primary microglia. The involvement of RIP3 in SAH-induced brain injury was established, with a close association with microglia.
In a study involving 46 SAH patients, Kaiser et al. (2019) identified a positive correlation between HO-1 mRNA levels in CSF and hemorrhage size. Elevated HO-1 mRNA levels, correlated with hemorrhage size, were linked to improved functional outcomes at discharge. The induction of HO-1 in microglia and the subsequent production of endogenous carbon monoxide constitute a crucial signaling pathway in blood-induced inflammatory responses, influencing the production of monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 by microglia and the extent of neuronal cell death (Kaiser et al., 2019).
Li et al. (2020) discovered an increased expression of NIMA-related kinase 7 (NEK7) in microglia and endothelial cells following SAH. The introduction of recombinant NEK7 protein worsened neurological impairment, brain swelling, neuronal cell death, BBB permeability, microglial clustering, and the activation of caspase-1 and mature interleukin-1β (IL-1β). Additionally, it activated the downstream NOD-like receptor pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) pathway, leading to the production of caspase-1 and IL-1β, thus, intensifying BBB permeability, microglial clustering, and neuronal cell death post-SAH. NEK7 emerges as a crucial factor in the activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes following SAH. This potentially occurs through the NEK7/NLRP3 signaling axis.
Microglia play a vital regulatory role in neuronal apoptosis following SAH and represent crucial molecular targets for SAH treatment. Nevertheless, further research is imperative to unveil the specific mechanisms governing the interplay between microglia and neuronal apoptosis in the context of SAH.

## Microglia and neuroinflammation
Inflammation is the biological response directed at eliminating noxious stimuli, eliminating damaged cells, and instigating processes for tissue repair (Leńska-Mieciek et al., 2023). Within the CNS, inflammation is predominantly orchestrated by microglia. They are activated in response to inflammatory stimuli, setting off a cascade of inflammatory reactions (Jin et al., 2022a). Microglia can manifest pro-inflammatory or anti-inflammatory characteristics, defined as the M1 and M2 phenotypes (Rodríguez-Gómez et al., 2020). M1 microglia are activated by interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor, and pathogen-associated molecular patterns and are pro-inflammatory. In contrast, M2 microglia contribute to inflammation resolution by modulating anti-inflammatory responses, fostering tissue repair, and facilitating remodeling. Following SAH, there is a surge in the microglia population, initially marked by a predominance of M1 marker expression, with a gradual transition towards the M2 phenotype (van Dijk et al., 2016; Colonna and Butovsky, 2017; Rodríguez-Gómez et al., 2020).
The neuroinflammatory response triggered by SAH extends from the frontal to posterior regions of the brain, is facilitated by the invasion of activated microglia, and affects all layers of the cerebral cortex. The correlation between neuronal cell death and neuroinflammation increases with the severity of the disease. Mitigating neuroinflammation across all layers of the cerebral cortex, which is responsible for long-term memory and motor function, may enhance the prognosis of patients with SAH (Yamada et al., 2022; Alves et al., 2024).
A previous study suggested that tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) is implicated in microglia-mediated neuroinflammation following SAH. Cerebral cavernous malformation 3/mammalian sterile 20-like kinase 4 (CCM3/MST4) has been shown to phosphorylate TRAF6 directly, inhibiting its ubiquitination and suppressing the inflammatory response (Song et al., 2016).
Peng et al. (2020a) found that rat brain tissue exhibited no significant alteration in CCM3/MST4 levels post-SAH, but a noticeable decline in CCM3 levels within microglia was observed. BV-2 cells treated with oxygenated hemoglobin showed decreased CCM3 protein levels, reaching their nadir at 6 hours post-treatment, while their MST4 protein levels remained relatively stable. Additionally, primary cultured microglia treated with oxygenated hemoglobin in an in vitro SAH model displayed diminished CCM3 expression and altered CCM3 subcellular localization. The overexpression of CCM3 correlated with reduced cell degeneration, enhanced neurocognitive function, diminished nuclear NF-κB p65 levels, and lower concentrations of inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β). The action of CCM3 overexpression on microglia through the NF-κB signaling pathway alleviated neuroinflammation and mitigated neurofunctional damage (Peng et al., 2020a).
Wu et al. (2021) reported a significant elevation in plasma soluble triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 1 (TREM1) levels following SAH. Treatment with the TREM1 inhibitor, LP17, resulted in a remarkable attenuation of post-SAH neurofunctional deficits, reduced brain water content, preserved BBB integrity, and mitigation of neuronal damage. LP17-mediated TREM1 inhibition caused a reduction in the transformation of microglia to the pro-inflammatory subtype, indicated by decreased levels of CD68, CD16, and CD86. Furthermore, it diminished the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps, as evidenced by reduced levels of citrullinated histone H3, myeloperoxidase (MPO), and norepinephrine. This ultimately led to a reduction in neuroinflammation, as indicated by reduced levels of IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α. To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, additional studies were conducted, which revealed that TREM1 activates downstream pro-inflammatory pathways by interacting with spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk). Inhibiting TREM1 via its interaction with Syk effectively mitigates the transformation of microglia to the pro-inflammatory subtype and the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps, thereby reducing neuroinflammation (Wu et al., 2021). Additionally, Xu et al. (2021a) corroborated the aforementioned role of TREM1. However, subsequent investigations revealed that TREM-1 triggers pyroptosis in microglia by activating the NLRP3 inflammasome, thereby further alleviating neuroinflammation after SAH. The multifaceted role of TREM1 is underscored by these findings.
These investigations imply that microglia play a pivotal role in neuroinflammation after SAH and offer potential avenues for therapeutic interventions in SAH ( ).
 

## Microglia, blood–brain barrier disruption, and brain edema
The BBB serves as a crucial physiological barricade in the human body. Its compromise facilitates the entry of inflammatory substances and plasma components into the brain parenchyma, exacerbating neuroinflammation and brain injury, ultimately leading to impaired brain function (Alahmari, 2021; Tian et al., 2023). Subsequent to SAH, the activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase pathway associated with TLR4/MyD88 signaling occurs, intensifying cytokine release and contributing to BBB disruption (Wang et al., 2023a). The activation of the calcium-sensing receptor is linked to neuronal deterioration, brain edema, and neurodegeneration. Inhibiting calcium-sensing receptor activation and NLRP3 inflammasome formation can be employed to ameliorate neurological dysfunction, brain edema, and BBB disruption after SAH (Wang et al., 2020a).
When blood vessels encounter pathological or physiological stressors, there appears to be a precise and close correlation between vascular activation, BBB disruption, and the transition of microglia to the M1-activated state (Bowyer et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2019). This idea was validated by Haruwaka et al. (2019). Current evidence indicates that pro-inflammatory M1 microglia contribute to BBB dysfunction and vascular leakage, while anti-inflammatory M2 microglia exert a protective effect on the BBB (Haruwaka et al., 2019). Specifically, during the early inflammation stages, activated microglia migrate around the blood vessels, relying on the C–C chemokine receptor type 5 to maintain the BBB phenotype. For instance, co-culturing mouse brain endothelial cells with resting microglia boosts the expression of the key tight junctional proteins occludin and ZO-1.27 in endothelial cells (Haruwaka et al., 2019). However, prolonged activated microglia-induced inflammation leads to BBB impairment (Kang et al., 2020). Microglia can also directly interact with endothelial cells, thereby influencing BBB permeability (Ronaldson and Davis, 2020).
Evidence from rodent models of cardiac arrest and subsequent cardiopulmonary resuscitation suggests that increased BBB permeability may arise from damage to the endothelial glycocalyx (Zhu et al., 2018a). Microglia are known to participate in the regulation of neuronal glycocalyx, influencing microglial immune responses, and phagocytosis (Linnartz and Neumann, 2013). Forsberg et al. (2018) observed the co-localization of ionized calcium-binding adaptor molecule-1-positive microglia with the glycocalyx marker ulex europaeus agglutinin I in vessel bundles. This indicates that activated microglia can interact with the vascular glycocalyx and, thereby, potentially disrupt vascular integrity.
Reports indicate that microglia play a role in modulating the immune response, dampening inflammation, and contributing to BBB repair (Subramaniam and Federoff, 2017). Microglia engaged in BBB protection and neurorepair have enlarged cell bodies and produce anti-inflammatory cytokines, extracellular matrix proteins, glucocorticoids, and other molecules (Subramaniam and Federoff, 2017). The shift of microglia from a quiescent state to a protective M2 phenotype is facilitated by signaling pathways involving IL-4 receptors, fragment crystallizable gamma receptors, or IL-10 receptors (Subramaniam and Federoff, 2017). Esposito et al. (2018) suggested that the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 signaling pathway might also influence microglial polarization towards a protective phenotype. In instances of ischemic preconditioning, increased vascular endothelial growth factor production enhances vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 expression in microglia, resulting in the branching of ionized calcium-binding adaptor molecule-1-positive microglia and elevated expression of M2 markers (Esposito et al., 2018). Activation of the IL-4 receptor initiates phagocytic mechanisms linked to tissue repair. Using a mouse model of intracerebral hemorrhage, He et al. (2020) demonstrated that IL-4 promotes microglial polarization towards an anti-inflammatory phenotype, thereby reducing brain infarction and edema and improving neurological outcomes.
Li et al. (2023b) observed that the expression of glycoprotein nonmetastatic melanoma protein B (GPNMB) significantly increased due to the inflammatory response after SAH. Administration of recombinant GPNMB alleviated brain edema, restored BBB integrity, and improved neurological function in SAH mice. GPNMB treatment notably elevated the expression of phosphorylated AMP-activated protein kinase while suppressing phosphorylated NF-κB (p-NF-κB), IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. Furthermore, the co-application of GPNMB and an adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) inhibitor resulted in reduced phosphorylated AMP-activated protein kinase intensity, reversed p-NF-κB, and decreased levels of the aforementioned inflammatory cytokines. GPNMB appears to regulate microglia function through the AMP-activated protein kinase/NF-κB signaling pathway, thus improving brain edema and neuroinflammation, protecting the BBB, and enhancing neurological function.
In the investigation of Yang et al. (2023a), dental pulp stem cell-conditioned medium was found to significantly alleviate brain edema and mitigate microcirculatory disturbances after SAH. Dental pulp stem cell-conditioned medium exerts its anti-inflammatory effects by reducing M2 microglia polarization induced by hemolysis products or CSF from patients with SAH through the IGF-1/protein kinase B (AKT) signaling pathway.
In conclusion, these findings unveil the intricate interconnections among BBB damage, neuroinflammation, and neurological dysfunction. These insights provide potential avenues for the development of innovative therapeutic strategies; however, additional research to corroborate and substantiate these discoveries is required.

## Microglia and white matter injury
SAH arises from sudden bleeding into the subarachnoid space and is associated with cerebral circulation disturbances, increased intracranial pressure, and hypoxia. The subsequent pathophysiological mechanisms, including the activation of neuroinflammation and neuronal cell apoptosis, have a fatal impact on the vulnerable white matter (Chen et al., 2022b). Evidence from patients with SAH and animal models suggests that abnormal white matter signals can be observed in early MRI following SAH (Liu et al., 2023c), even within the hyperacute period (within 4 hours). This indicates the presence of a white matter injury (WMI) (Reijmer et al., 2018). During the acute phase of brain injury, microglia rapidly respond and release damage-associated molecules that induce white matter damage. Microglia also play a crucial role in regulating the health and regeneration of myelin in the CNS (Toyota et al., 2019).
Pang et al. (2018) found that apolipoprotein E gene knockout (ApoE–/–) mice of the C57BL/6J lineage exhibited more extensive EBI 48 hours post-SAH. These mice displayed broader WMI and neuronal apoptosis than WT mice. These alterations were associated with increased expression of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase 2 (NOX2), a critical regulator of oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed abundant NOX2 expression in activated M1 microglia. The Janus kinase 2 (JAK2)/STAT3 signaling pathway, an upstream regulatory factor, was upregulated in WT mice and more activated in ApoE–/– mice. Treatment with the JAK2-specific inhibitor AG490 reduced NOX2 expression, oxidative stress, and inflammation in ApoE-deficient mice. The ApoE mimetic peptide COG1410 inhibited the JAK2/STAT3 pathway, thereby significantly suppressing the activation of M1 microglia and mitigating EBI by restraining M1 microglial activation. Thus, it alleviated oxidative stress and WMI after SAH (Pang et al., 2018). Additionally, Peng et al. (2019) demonstrated that the depletion of microglia via low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein-1 significantly attenuated WMI following SAH. Moreover, they confirmed that COG1410 could ameliorate brain edema, enhance the anti-inflammatory M2 microglial phenotype, alleviate WMI, and improve neurological function after SAH. Furthermore, the effects of COG1410 were reversed by low-density lipoprotein receptor–related protein-1 small interfering RNA (siRNA) or phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitors. This experimental evidence suggests that the ApoE mimetic peptide COG1410 may be a promising therapeutic agent for patients with SAH (Peng et al., 2019).
ApoE in humans is classified into three allelic variants: E2, E3, and E4, and it is associated with the progression of white matter lesions. Li et al. (2023a) demonstrated that ApoE4 exacerbates WMI after SAH by impairing the phagocytic activity of microglia. This effect was characterized by increased expression of CD16, CD86, and CD16/CD206 and decreased levels of arginase-1 and CD206. The detrimental impact on WMI and subsequent reduction in neurobehavioral function may be linked to oxidative stress-dependent mitochondrial damage in microglia following SAH.
Peng et al. (2023a) found that microglial inflammation is associated with demyelination and axonal injury, which manifest as a reduction in myelin basic protein, increased levels of degraded myelin basic protein, and increased levels of amyloid precursor protein. TLR4 gene knockout altered microglial polarization towards an anti-inflammatory phenotype and provided early protection to the white matter (24 hours post-SAH). This protection was evidenced by a reduction in toxic metabolites, preservation of myelin, decreased amyloid precursor protein accumulation, reduced white matter T2 high signal intensity, and increased fractional anisotropy (FA) values. TLR4 inhibition reduced the expression of MyD88 and phosphorylated NF-κB in microglia, thereby inhibiting M1 polarization and alleviating inflammation. Decreased TLR4 in microglia increased the preservation of neighboring oligodendrocytes. The results demonstrate a dual role of microglial inflammation in early WMI following experimental SAH (Peng et al., 2023a).
WMI can lead to profound cognitive and emotional impairments after SAH. The aforementioned studies provide insights into the intricate relationship between microglia and WMI following SAH. Safeguarding the integrity and connectivity of white matter presents a promising avenue to improve the prognosis of SAH, introducing novel targets and therapeutic strategies for its treatment.

## The Neuroprotective Role of Microglia
A growing body of research indicates that microglia release pro-inflammatory factors and cytotoxic substances leading to neuronal damage and apoptosis, as well as clear cellular debris through phagocytic activity, in response to CNS trauma. Furthermore, they release neurotrophic and anti-inflammatory factors, thereby mitigating neural damage, promoting tissue repair, and exerting neuroprotective effects (Nakajima and Kohsaka, 2004; Chen and Trapp, 2016; Gupta et al., 2023).
In the spectrum of biological activities, M2 microglia play a crucial role in diverse processes, including allergic reactions, clearance of parasites, inhibition of inflammation, tissue remodeling, and regulation of the immune system. Following SAH, the principal role of M2 microglia involves actively promoting the repair of injuries through the attenuation of inflammatory responses, and their emergence is particularly pronounced during the later phases of EBI (Xu et al., 2019; Chen and Wong, 2021).
During the early stages of EBI, microglial activation initiates the synthesis of various neuroprotective proteins, including neuroglobin and HO-1. These proteins reduce oxidative stress, thus protecting neurons (Sozen et al., 2011). Akamatsu et al. (2020) illustrated the neuroprotective function of HO-1 by genetically removing it from microglia. Furthermore, microglia increase the expression of metabotropic glutamate receptor 5 and suppress the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, thereby reducing neuronal apoptosis and brain swelling (Sozen et al., 2011). Moreover, following SAH, activated microglia also boost the levels of IL-4 and IL-17 and contribute to the clearance of cellular debris and hematoma via scavenger receptors (Galea et al., 2022).
TLR4 plays a dual role in brain injury, being involved in the injury process while also exerting an anti-inflammatory effect by regulating microglia to protect brain tissue integrity (Heinz and Schneider, 2022; Wang et al., 2023a). The TIR-domain-containing adapter-inducing interferon-β (TRIF) signaling pathway recruits adapter molecules in a process facilitated by TRAF3 and TRAF6. Through TRAF6 mediation, the TRIF-dependent pathway generates inflammatory cytokines in a cascade similar to the MyD88-dependent pathway. TLR4 effectively coordinates both “early” and “late” activation, with the MyD88 pathway controlling “early” NF-κB activation and TRIF determining “late” NF-κB activation. However, compared with TRAF6, the TRAF3-mediated TRIF signaling pathway can induce interferon-beta (IFN-β) production, demonstrating anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic effects. IFN-β also regulates the synthesis of other IFN-I, further suppressing the inflammatory response after EBI and providing neuroprotection (Häcker et al., 2006).
Cao et al. (2022) found that 48 hours post-SAH, the overexpression of triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2 (TREM2) led to an increase in CD206+/CD11b+ cells and IL-10 expression. The protein expression level of the anti-inflammatory factor IRAK3 significantly increased, MPO+ cell infiltration decreased, TNF-α and IL-1β expression was reduced, cell phagocytic function was enhanced, the inflammatory response was inhibited, rat neuronal apoptosis decreased, and neuronal survival increased. Conversely, knocking down TREM2 had the opposite effect. TREM2 plays a neuroprotective role by modulating neuroinflammation in microglia post-SAH, improving both short-term and long-term neurological deficits after SAH (Cao et al., 2022). Wang et al. (2020c) have also corroborated these findings.
Guo et al. (2023) identified a notable rise in estrogen-related receptor gamma (ERRγ) expression in the rat brain following SAH. After activating ERRγ using DY131 (ERRγ agonist), the expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha, Sirtuin 3, and B-cell lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) increased, and the expression of 4-hydroxynonenal and Bax decreased. This activation of ERRγ notably ameliorated both short-term and long-term neurological deficits 24 hours post-SAH in rats, concurrently reducing oxidative stress and neuronal apoptosis, thus exerting a neuroprotective effect.
Tang et al. (2023) found that recombinant growth arrest-specific 6 (rGAS6) promotes the extravasation of microglia and prevents the weakening of inflammatory responses by inhibiting or knocking down Axl and Rac1, thereby attenuating the early brain damage in mice after SAH. Wang et al. (2023b) demonstrated that microglia-secreted extracellular vesicles transmitted miR-140-5p into microglia to downregulate monocyte-to-macrophage differentiation, ultimately promoting neuroprotection in rats after SAH.
The above studies collectively indicate that microglia play a multifaceted and intricate role in providing neuroprotection after SAH. Further experimental data and clinical research are required to substantiate and definitively clarify these mechanisms.

## Novel Therapeutic Targets for Microglia in Subarachnoid Hemorrhage
While the inflammatory pathways triggered in microglia following SAH are intricate, considerable research has been undertaken. Nevertheless, a consensus on the precise mechanisms of microglia action remains elusive. Various conventional cell signaling cascades and partially validated pharmaceuticals are associated with these processes, ultimately improving the symptoms and prognosis of SAH. This section aims to comprehensively categorize the latest therapeutic targets for microglia, related medications, and emerging treatment methods, such as stem cell therapy and gene therapy, with the goal of providing a comprehensive overview ( ).
 

## TLR inhibitors
Research findings have elucidated the pivotal role of the TLR4 signaling pathway in the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying acute brain injuries, particularly SAH (Wang et al., 2022a). TLR4 is present on the surface of microglia, and it is a pivotal factor in orchestrating the immune response within the brain cellular milieu. Components intrinsic to this pathway, encompassing TLR4 pathway ligands, the receptor itself, and inflammatory cytokines, serve as discernible biomarkers that provide insights into the inflammatory milieu after SAH (Gao et al., 2019). Furthermore, entities such as the NLRP3 inflammasome and receptors akin to TREM1 intricately contribute to the TLR4 pathway, thereby initiating the cascade of inflammatory processes (Duan et al., 2020).
Following SAH, the activation of TLR4 induces an overproduction of pro-inflammatory mediators and an augmented expression of co-stimulatory molecules, consequently fostering neuroinflammation (Gao et al., 2019; Duan et al., 2020). Emerging evidence posits that the activation of TLR4, particularly within microglia as opposed to astrocytes or neurons, is indispensable for initiating brain injury and unfavorable cognitive outcomes (Duan et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022a). TLR4 assumes a pivotal role in the activation of microglia and the subsequent neuronal damage following SAH. Islam et al. (2022) suggest that the intracerebroventricular injection of chlorpyrifos diethyl lipid vesicles can potentially improve cognitive function in a murine SAH model. Targeting microglia TLR4 may prove to be an efficacious approach to mitigating SAH-related neuronal damage and safeguarding against cognitive dysfunction.
Resveratrol (RSV) is a naturally occurring polyphenolic compound renowned for its robust anti-inflammatory properties. Zhang et al. (2016) demonstrated a substantial inhibition of TLR4, HMGB1, MyD88, and NF-κB expression when RSV was administered post-SAH. At 24 hours post-SAH, RSV significantly attenuated microglial cell activation, thereby reducing inflammatory cytokines implicated in neuronal apoptosis, brain edema, and neurobehavioral impairment. However, the administration of RSV did not ameliorate brain edema and neurofunctional deficits at 72 hours post-SAH. Subsequent investigations suggested that RSV treatment partially mitigated SAH-induced EBI by suppressing the TLR4-mediated inflammatory signaling pathway. In a subsequent study, Zhang et al. (2017) corroborated that a 60 mg/kg injection of RSV significantly suppressed microglia activation and neutrophil infiltration post-SAH, thereby alleviating brain inflammatory responses, cortical cell apoptosis, brain edema, and neurobehavioral impairment. Additionally, the study unveiled a noteworthy inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation in the SAH + 60 mg/kg RSV group, implying that the effects of RSV involve a multifaceted mechanism that targets multiple pathways.
Duan et al. (2020) observed that the administration of sodium hydrosulfide significantly ameliorated cognitive dysfunction in rats following SAH. Simultaneously, sodium hydrosulfide reduced the expression of TNF-α, TLR4, and NF-κB p65 while also mitigating the nuclear translocation of NF-κB p65. These findings suggest that hydrogen sulfide may mitigate cognitive impairment in SAH-afflicted rats by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB pathway (Duan et al., 2020).
Wang et al. (2020b) determined that TREM2 is predominantly localized in microglia and exhibits a notable delayed upregulation after SAH. Suppression of TREM2 resulted in an elevation in pro-inflammatory mediator levels, heightened microglial activity, and exacerbated neurological dysfunction post-SAH. Notably, TLR4 knockout significantly augmented TREM2 expression, thereby mitigating neuroinflammation and improving neurological function. Correspondingly, there was a marked increase in soluble TREM2 aggregation in the CSF of patients with SAH according to the distinct clinical Hunt-Hess grade. TREM2 plays a pivotal role in orchestrating microglial polarization post-SAH, and its neuroprotective efficacy may be impeded by the overactivation of TLR4 in the early phases of SAH (Wang et al., 2020b). Hu et al. (2021b) subsequently validated and reinforced these findings, suggesting that targeting TREM2 in pharmaceutical interventions could be an innovative strategy for the treatment of SAH.

## Nuclear factor kappa B and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 pathway inhibitors
Upon activation of TLR4 receptors, M1 microglia initiate the NF-κB pathway via MyD88 adapter proteins and MyD88 binding. This activation results in the formation of IRAK and facilitates the synthesis of pro-inflammatory factors. Inhibition of the NF-κB pathway has been shown to diminish the release of inflammatory factors, thereby protecting the nervous system (Lu et al., 2022).
Peng et al. (2018a) discovered that escitalopram, traditionally employed as an antidepressant, significantly upregulates the expression of Sirtuin 1 while concurrently inhibiting the activation of NF-κB. This modulation results in the downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, along with the upregulation of the protective cytokine IL-10. Additionally, escitalopram markedly inhibits microglial activation, thereby exerting a neuroprotective effect through the suppression of neuroinflammation and the reduction of neuronal loss following SAH.
BMS-470539, a highly efficient and selective melanocortin-1 receptor (MC-1R) agonist, was investigated by Xu et al. (2020b). Treatment with BMS-470539 increased the levels of phosphorylated AMP-activated protein kinase and phosphorylated TANK-binding kinase 1, coupled with a simultaneous decrease in the levels of NF-κB, IL-1β, and TNF-α. This cascade of events resulted in the inhibition of microglial activation, the promotion of the transformation of CD206+ microglia, a reduction in neutrophil infiltration, a significant improvement in neurological functional deficits caused by SAH, and a notable enhancement in long-term spatial learning and memory abilities after SAH. BMS-470539 demonstrated robust neuroprotective effects (Xu et al., 2020b).
Liu et al. (2020a) uncovered the neuroprotective effects of Resolvin D1, which are mediated by the lipoxin A4 receptor/formyl peptide receptor 2. The study revealed that inhibiting hemoglobin could induce the pro-inflammatory polarization of microglia, and its interaction with the A4 receptor/formyl peptide receptor 2 could ameliorate hemoglobin-induced neuronal oxidative damage and death. The A4 receptor/formyl peptide receptor 2 interaction may play a dual role in the CNS. These findings present a promising therapeutic target (A4 receptor/formyl peptide receptor 2) and a potentially effective drug (Resolvin D1) for treating SAH.
Liu et al. (2023b) demonstrated that isoflurane modulation attenuates microglial activation and NF-κB expression in microglia post-SAH. Isoflurane conditioning and pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate, a selective NF-κB inhibitor, alleviated large artery vasospasm and microvascular thrombosis, thereby improving neurological deficits after SAH. The studies suggest that the protective effects induced by isoflurane conditioning against DCI are, at least in part, mediated by downregulating the NF-κB pathway.
Kajimoto et al. (2023) observed that SAH induces widespread microglia activation in the brain that can be inhibited by glibenclamide. The study suggests that glibenclamide inhibits brain edema, microglia activation, and excessive secretion of inflammatory cytokines, thereby significantly enhancing the prognosis of SAH.
Sun et al. (2024) demonstrated that intranasal administration of exogenous osteopontin improves neurological dysfunction, reduces neutrophil infiltration, decreases the expression of phenotypic and functional markers such as CD16 and TNF-α in pro-inflammatory microglia, increases the expression of CD206 and IL-10, and promotes an anti-inflammatory microglial phenotype. Further investigations also indicate the involvement of the STAT3 and NF-κB pathways in osteopontin-mediated regulation of microglial activation through integrin-FAK signaling, providing new targets for the treatment of SAH.

## Glycine/tyrosine kinase family
The non-essential amino acid glycine plays a role in neuroprotection following intracerebral hemorrhage through the modulation of the phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN)/AKT signaling pathway. Qin et al. (2019) observed that the phosphorylation of AKT at S473 is downregulated following SAH, a condition reversed by glycine treatment. Administration of glycine effectively mitigates neuronal cell death in SAH by activating the AKT pathway. Furthermore, glycine induces the upregulation of miRNA-26b, resulting in the downregulation of PTEN and subsequent AKT activation, ultimately inhibiting neuronal cell death. Notably, the neuroprotective effect of glycine can be attenuated by inhibiting miRNA-26b, PTEN, or AKT activation. Glycine treatment additionally hinders the M1-type microglia polarization induced by SAH, suppresses inflammation, mitigates post-SAH brain edema, and protects neurons. Subsequent investigations suggest that these effects are mediated through the miRNA-26b/PTEN/AKT signaling pathway. This suggests that it is a potential therapeutic target for mitigating SAH-induced damage.
Mer, a member of the TAM receptor tyrosine kinase family, plays a pivotal role in the resolution of inflammation. Tan et al. (2022) observed that Mer expression increases post-SAH; this is predominantly localized in microglia/macrophages. Treatment with MersiRNA amplifies the pro-inflammatory phenotype of microglia/macrophages and diminishes the anti-inflammatory phenotype, consequently exacerbating neurological deficits and brain edema following SAH. The downregulation of Mer inhibits the downstream, anti-inflammatory signal suppressor of cytokine signaling 1 (SOCS1)/suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 by reducing the phosphorylation of STAT. This study affirms the involvement of Mer in microglia/macrophage polarization and in the resolution of inflammation after SAH, with this mechanism being at least partially associated with the STATs/SOCSs pathway.

## NOD-like receptor pyrin domain-containing 3 signaling pathway inhibitors
The inflammasome plays a pivotal role as a crucial mediator in diverse vascular diseases, exerting its effect through the positive regulation of pro-inflammatory pathways. It is firmly established that the activation of NLRP3 contributes to the induction of IL-1β production and enhances the reactivity of microglia, both of which are pathological hallmarks of SAH. Studies have consistently demonstrated that NLRP3 inhibition yields a favorable prognosis, particularly evident at 1 and 5 days post-SAH (Du et al., 2020; Hu et al., 2023; Lai et al., 2023). Furthermore, NLRP3 inhibitors can forestall delayed cerebral vasospasm during the secondary injury phase and mitigate the sensory-motor deficits associated with SAH (Liu et al., 2023a).
During the EBI phase following SAH, fluoxetine has been shown to inhibit the activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes, mitigate necrotic cell death, and alleviate the disruption of the BBB and cerebral edema. Liu et al. (2018) provided evidence that fluoxetine effectively ameliorates BBB breakdown, reduces brain edema, and enhances neurological function post-SAH. These observed effects coincide with a reduction in the number of ionized calcium binding adaptor molecule 1-positive microglia/macrophages, an attenuation of neutrophil infiltration and cell death, diminished levels of inflammatory cytokines, a downregulation of TLR4 and MyD88 expression, and the facilitation of nuclear translocation of NF-κB p65.
Dodd et al. (2021a) demonstrated that the administration of MCC950, a selective inhibitor targeting NLRP3, leads to a reduction in neuronal apoptosis in mice. MCC950, along with other NLRP3 inhibitors, holds promise as a potential tool for the development of therapeutic drugs targeting EBI and DCI
Xia et al. (2021) determined that the Sirtuin 1 agonist SRT1720, through modulation of the reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway, induces a phenotypic shift in microglia towards the M2 type, thereby ameliorating EBI following SAH.
Hu et al. (2021a) found that INT-777 mitigates NLRP3-ASC inflammasome-mediated neuroinflammation during EBI after SAH. This is partially achieved through the Takeda G-protein-coupled receptor 5/cyclic adenosine monophosphate/protein kinase A signaling pathway. Early administration of INT-777, a selective Takeda G-protein-coupled receptor 5 agonist, holds promise as a potential therapeutic approach for treating EBI in patients with SAH.
Jin et al. (2022b) discovered that metformin inhibits neuroinflammation through an AMPK-dependent mechanism, exerting its neuroprotective effects by modulating the production of NLRP3-related pro-inflammatory factors and the activation of microglia.
Luo et al. (2023) demonstrated that suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid increases miR-340 expression while decreasing NEK7 levels in both in vivo and in vitro SAH models. MiR-340 directly targets NEK7, thereby inhibiting the NLRP3 signaling pathway. Knockdown of miR-340 or overexpression of NEK7 reversed suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid’s suppression of inflammation and apoptosis in microglia. The histone deacetylase inhibitor suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid improves microglial pyroptosis in SAH by upregulating miR-340 expression to suppress NEK7 signaling.
Lai et al. (2023) observed that a deficiency of the pleckstrin homology-like domain, family A, member 1 (PHLDA1) leads to a reduction in neuronal apoptosis and an improvement in neurological function after SAH. The study proposes that blocking PHLDA1 inhibits the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway following SAH. The inhibition of PHLDA1 may modulate microglial M1/M2 polarization by suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome signaling, consequently ameliorating SAH-induced brain injury.
Liu et al. (2023d) discovered that the knockdown of H19 significantly diminished the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome proteins 24 hours post-SAH in rats. This resulted in improved EBI, enhanced neurobehavioral deficits, reduced brain edema, and mitigated neuronal damage. H19 knockdown also alleviates cell pyroptosis induced by oxyhemoglobin and NLRP3-mediated inflammasome activation in primary microglia. H19 competitively binds to rno-miR-138-5p, subsequently upregulating NLRP3 expression during the inflammatory response post-SAH. In the EBI phase after SAH, H19 long non-coding RNA functions as a competitive endogenous RNA by acting as a sponge for rno-miR-138-5p in rats, thus promoting NLRP3-mediated cell pyroptosis.

## Gasdermin D inhibitors
Multiple lines of evidence suggest that the gasdermin D (GSDMD) family plays diverse roles in biological and pathological processes, encompassing cell differentiation, cell proliferation, cell death, mitochondrial homeostasis, antimicrobial defense, inflammation, and tumorigenesis. Among the GSDMD family members, particular attention has been paid to GSDMD. It is the first confirmed executor of pyroptosis and acts downstream of inflammatory caspases to regulate cellular inflammation (Wei et al., 2022; Ding et al., 2024).
Prior reports have indicated that dexmedetomidine (Dex) can inhibit SAH-induced cerebral edema, cell apoptosis, and neuronal damage. In their study utilizing a rat model of SAH, Wei et al. (2022) investigated the effects of Dex on pyroptosis during EBI following SAH and delineated the underlying mechanisms. Pyroptosis was observed in microglia during EBI post-SAH. Dex significantly attenuated pyroptosis in microglia by reducing the levels of the executor of pyroptosis, GSDMD, and suppressing the release of SAH-induced pro-inflammatory cytokines. Additionally, the PI3K inhibitor LY294002 reversed the Dex-induced reduction of GSDMD levels. Dex alleviated microglial pyroptosis during EBI following SAH via activation of the PI3K/AKT/GSK3β pathway.
Cai et al. (2023) unveiled LDC7559 as a recently identified inhibitor of GSDMD. Their investigation revealed a notable surge in microglial activation and neuronal pyroptosis following SAH, a phenomenon effectively mitigated by LDC7559 in both in vitro and in vivo settings. Administration of LDC7559 resulted in diminished neuronal apoptosis and enhanced behavioral functions. The levels of GSDMD and cleaved GSDMD were reduced by LDC7559 post-SAH. Conversely, nigericin, an activator of the NLRP3 inflammasome, amplified GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis and counteracted the favorable effects of LDC7559 on SAH-induced brain injury. Notably, treatment with LDC7559 did not markedly impact the expression of NLRP3 following SAH. In essence, LDC7559 can potentially suppress neuronal pyroptosis and microglial cell activation post-SAH by inhibiting GSDMD, thereby facilitating the restoration of brain function.

## Retinoic acid receptor alpha receptor agonist
In various CNS disorders, the activation of retinoic acid receptor alpha receptor (RARα) receptors has been demonstrated to have neuroprotective effects (Tian et al., 2022).
In their study, Tian et al. (2022) noted an increase in RARα, v-maf avian musculoaponeurotic fibrosarcoma oncogene homolog B (MAFB), and macrophage scavenger receptor 1 (MSR1) levels in rat brain tissues following SAH. Utilizing tamibarotene (Am80), a specific agonist, to activate RARα receptors relieved neurological deficits, decreased brain edema, and improved BBB integrity. Am80 increased protein kinase B (PKB or Akt) phosphorylation and suppressed NF-κB phosphorylation, leading to elevated MAFB and MSR1 expression, thus reducing neuroinflammation. The therapeutic effects of Am80 were impeded by AGN196996 (a specific RARα inhibitor), Msr1 siRNA, and LY294002, resulting in decreased Msr1 expression and Akt phosphorylation. In an in vitro SAH model, Am80 facilitated microglial polarization from the M1 to M2 phenotype and inhibited NF-κB nuclear transcription. Activation of RARα receptors mitigates neuroinflammation by promoting M1 to M2 microglial polarization and modulating the Mafb/Msr1/PI3K-Akt/NF-κB pathway. Therefore, RARα represents a promising therapeutic target for SAH treatment.

## Iron death inhibitors
Iron is the most abundant transition metal in the brain. Iron death is a newly discovered iron-dependent programmed cell death process associated with pathological cell death related to neurodegenerative diseases, brain hemorrhage, and traumatic brain injury (Deng et al., 2023). Among all types of brain cells, microglia have the highest iron storage capacity. Accumulation of iron affects the physiology of microglia and leads to neurodegeneration (Ryan et al., 2023). Ryan et al. (2020) demonstrated that induced pluripotent stem cell-derived microglia, grown in a three-cell culture system (including microglia, astrocytes, and neurons), are highly sensitive to iron and susceptible to iron death. Furthermore, iron overload leads to significant changes in the transcriptional state of microglia, contributing to neurodegeneration. Removing microglia from the three-cell culture system reduced lipid peroxidation in neurons, significantly delaying iron-induced neuronal death. A genome-wide clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeat screening was conducted. It identified new regulatory factors for iron death, including the vesicle transport gene SEC24B. These data collectively suggest that iron overload and iron death in microglia play a crucial role in neurodegenerative diseases and neuronal death.
Gao et al. (2022) established a mouse SAH model using the intravascular puncture method and simulated SAH in vitro using endothelial cells, BV2 microglia, RSL3, and hemin. The study confirmed that the accumulation of lipid peroxides, defects in the glutathione peroxidase 4/glutathione antioxidant system, elevated expression of arachidonate 15-lipoxygenase (ALOX15) in microglia and endothelial cells, and changes in iron death in microglia mitochondria substantiate the occurrence of iron death in vivo after SAH. Simultaneously, it was discovered that cryptotanshinone inhibits iron death and improves neurological function by downregulating ALOX15 expression. In in vitro experiments, the crucial role of ALOX15 in RSL3-induced iron death in endothelial cells was investigated. The study found that M2-type microglia are more sensitive to RSL3-induced iron death than M1-type microglia, and hemin may induce iron death in M2-type microglia by increasing ALOX15 levels and reducing glutathione peroxidase 4 levels. The in vitro experiments also confirmed the effects of cryptotanshinone. In summary, to our knowledge, this was the first study that demonstrated iron death in microglia and endothelial cells after SAH, with an elevation in ALOX15 levels promoting this process. Cryptotanshinone can inhibit iron death by downregulating ALOX15 expression.
Previous studies have found that iNOS/NO• plays a crucial role in promoting the survival of M1-type microglia by blocking iron death. Qu et al. (2022) discovered that L-N6-(1-iminoethyl) lysine (an iNOS inhibitor) promotes iron death in M1-type microglia by increasing the expression of iron death-related proteins and lipid peroxidation while inhibiting iNOS expression. This effect was reversed by the iron death inhibitor liprostatin-1. Inhibiting iNOS may improve outcomes after SAH by promoting iron death in M1-type microglia and reducing neuroinflammation.
Tao et al. (2022) established a SAH model through the specific knockout of microglia S100A8. Their study revealed a significant increase in S100A8 expression in the brain tissues post-SAH. Targeted inhibition of microglia S100A8 was shown to improve neurological function and reduce neuronal apoptosis in mice after SAH. Further mechanistic exploration indicated that the beneficial effects of depleting S100A8 during EBI might be attributed to autophagy-dependent inhibition of iron death in microglia. S100A8 could be a potential intervention target for microglia iron death in EBI following SAH.

## Transgenic technology
Transgenic technology is an evolving biomedical method that encompasses the modification or induction of the expression of specific proteins to elicit therapeutic effects. Its objective is to achieve targeted therapeutic outcomes. In recent years, transgenic technology has increasingly been applied in the field of neurological disorders (Sudhakar and Richardson, 2019).
Zhu et al. (2018b) discovered that an exogenous vascular growth factor, G-patch and FHA domains 1 (rh-AGGF1), significantly increased PI3K expression, AKT phosphorylation, and the levels of VE-cadherin, occludin, and claudin-5 after SAH. Simultaneously, there was a notable decrease in p-NF-κB p65 expression, albumin, MPO, TNF-α, and IL-1β. Conversely, suppressing endogenous AGGF1 within 24 hours post-SAH exacerbated BBB disruption, inflammatory response, and neuronal functional impairment. Furthermore, LY294002 hindered the protective effects of rh-AGGF1. AGGF1 is a promising therapeutic target for SAH treatment.
Xie et al. (2018) ascertained that recombinant human netrin-1 could mitigate brain edema, ameliorate neurological deficits, suppress microglia activation, concomitantly activate peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma, inhibit NF-κB, and decrease TNF-α, IL-6, intercellular adhesion molecule-1, and MPO levels post-SAH. Depletion of endogenous netrin-1 led to heightened expression of pro-inflammatory mediators and MPO, thereby exacerbating neuroinflammation and brain edema.
Liu et al. (2020b) demonstrated that miR-146a, acting through the interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 1/TRAF6/NF-κB or mitogen-activated protein kinase signaling pathways, downregulated the gene expression of hemoglobin-induced pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β) and phenotype-related genes (iNOS and CD86). This substantiates its anti-inflammatory activity in microglia, positioning it as a prospective therapeutic microRNA that negatively modulates inflammatory responses.
Qiu et al. (2023) demonstrated the attenuation of necrosis in microglia, mitigation of neuroinflammation, and improvement in neurological function in mice following SAH through adenovirus-mediated knockout of BCL-2 homologous antagonist killer (Bak1). The elimination of Bak1 in BV2 microglial cells reduced necrosis by downregulating the expression of phosphorylated mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein, subsequently leading to a decrease in the expression of pro-inflammatory phenotype genes. Disruption of Bak1 in BV2 microglial cells mitigated various immune and inflammatory pathways, concurrently improving cell damage by enhancing the expression of thrombospondin 1. The pivotal regulatory role of Bak1 in microglial necrosis and neuroinflammation post-SAH underscores its potential as a therapeutic target for SAH.
MiR-124, the most abundant microRNA in the CNS, plays a crucial role in microglia activation by targeting the protein CCAAT-enhancer-binding protein alpha. Chen et al. (2020) engineered a plasmid that specifically expressed CX3CL1 and CX3CR1. They found that the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 axis may confer protection post-SAH by facilitating the delivery of exosomal miR-124 to microglia. This orchestrated process alleviates microglia activation and neuroinflammation, presenting a novel therapeutic avenue for SAH.
Liao et al. (2020) noted that conditioned medium derived from microglia exposed to oxygenated hemoglobin induced neuronal apoptosis, leading to microglial activation and upregulation of CCR6, IL-1β, and TNF-α expression. Introducing CCL20 siRNA or CCR6 siRNA into the conditioned medium reduced the proportion of apoptotic neurons. Silencing CCL20 or CCR6 counteracted the impact of oxyhemoglobin on microglia, thereby enhancing the prognosis of SAH.
Peng et al. (2020b) discovered that the activation of STING exacerbates neuroinflammation by promoting microglia activation and polarization towards the M1 phenotype. This activation induces an elevation in M1 markers within microglia, including IL-1β, iNOS, IL-6, TNF-α, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, and the NLRP3 inflammasome. In contrast, the STING antagonist, C-176, exhibits robust anti-inflammatory effects. Further investigations suggest that the therapeutic impact of inhibiting STING is partially mediated by activating the AMPK signaling pathway, thereby alleviating inflammation induced by SAH. STING emerges as a novel and promising therapeutic target for SAH.
Wang et al. (2021) reporterd that treatment with JAK2/STAT3 siRNA (Si-JAK2/Si-STAT3) or overexpression plasmids demonstrated that Si-JAK2 and Si-STAT3 therapies alleviate cortical neuron apoptosis and necrosis, BBB disruption, brain edema, and cognitive dysfunction in experimental SAH rats. These therapeutic effects coincide with reduced phosphorylation of JAK2 and STAT3 proteins, decreased total levels of JAK2 and STAT3 proteins, and increased expression of the SOCS1 protein. Si-JAK2 and Si-STAT3 impede the transformation of M1 microglia and the release of pro-inflammatory factors (iNOS, IL-1β, and TNF-α), while concurrently enhancing the release of anti-inflammatory factors (arginase-1, IL-10, and IL-4), thus exerting neuroprotective effects.
Song et al. (2022) reported that overexpression of miR-340-5p in SAH mice and transfection of miR-340-5p into microglia resulted in a significant reduction in neurological deficits, brain edema, BBB damage, and neuroinflammation. MiR-340-5p mitigates EBI induced by SAH through the inhibition of STING.
The study from Qian et al. (2022) revealed that extracellular vesicles derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) transfer miR-140-5p into microglia, leading to the downregulation of activin receptor-like kinase 5 (ALK5) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase 2 (NOX2). This process inhibits the activation of M1 microglia in SAH mice, providing a genetic target for the treatment of SAH.

## Traditional Chinese medicine
Traditional Chinese medicines predominantly originate from natural plant sources. Owing to their plentiful and readily available nature, these herbal remedies are frequently perceived as being natural, non-toxic, or possessing low toxicity. Furthermore, they often manifest their effects through multiple targets and mechanisms. In recent years, investigating the protective and therapeutic effects of traditional Chinese medicines on neurological disorders has emerged as a prominent and growing area of interest (Ning et al., 2021; ).
 Curcumin, a natural phytochemical compound, has potential anti-inflammatory effects and the ability to modulate microglial cell phenotypes. Yuan et al. (2017) determined that curcumin was an inhibitor of microglia activation and matrix metalloproteinase-9 expression, thereby alleviating brain edema and mitigating BBB disruption following SAH. Gao et al. (2019) further suggested that curcumin may facilitate the transformation of microglia towards the M2 phenotype, potentially by inhibiting the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to a reduction in neuroinflammatory responses post-SAH.
Magnesium lithospermate B (MLB), a bioactive compound derived from Salvia miltiorrhiza (Danshen), has shown neuroprotective properties in various CNS injuries, as demonstrated by Peng et al. (2018b). Their study highlighted that MLB provides significant neuroprotection against SAH by suppressing neuroinflammation and cellular apoptosis. The neuroprotective mechanisms of MLB in SAH might involve modulation of the Sirtuin 1/NF-κB pathway. MLB, or modulation of the Sirtuin 1/NF-κB pathway, could offer a novel and promising therapeutic approach for SAH management.
Berberine, an isoquinoline alkaloid extracted from the Chinese herb Huanglian (Coptis chinensis), was found by Zhang et al. (2020b) to have anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective characteristics. Their research suggests that treatment with berberine markedly reduces microglial activation and the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Berberine confers beneficial outcomes in SAH-induced brain inflammation by inhibiting the HMGB1/NF-κB pathway, potentially via Sirtuin 1 activation.
Wang et al. (2020d) discovered that paeoniflorin, a natural monoterpene glucoside extracted from Paeonia lactiflora, demonstrates neuroprotective effects. Their study showed that paeoniflorin significantly ameliorates neurological deficits 72 hours post-SAH, resulting in reduced brain water content and Evans Blue extravasation. The protective effects of paeoniflorin are associated with the inhibition of microglial activation and neuroinflammatory responses. Furthermore, at 72 hours post-SAH, paeoniflorin treatment markedly reduced the Bax/Bcl-2, active caspase-3/NeuN, and terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling/4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole-positive cell ratios.
Zhang et al. (2021) demonstrated that luteolin, a compound with diverse biological functions, has beneficial effects on cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Luteolin significantly inhibits SAH-induced neuroinflammation, characterized by reduced microglial activation, diminished neutrophil infiltration, and suppression of pro-inflammatory cells. The study suggests that luteolin protects against SAH by inhibiting the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway, and this effect may be regulated by the activation of Nrf2.
Zhou et al. (2022a) demonstrated that alantolactone treatment alleviates neurological deficits, inhibits neuronal apoptosis and inflammatory responses, promotes the polarization of microglia towards the M2 phenotype, and activates the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. Alantolactone can mitigate neuronal and cerebral cortical damage in SAH rats by activating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway.
Pterostilbene, an analog of RSV, has been confirmed to be an effective activator of Sirtuin 1. Zhang et al. (2022) provided evidence that pterostilbene prevents SAH injury by activating the Sirtuin 1-dependent Nrf2 signaling pathway. Pterostilbene administration may serve as a therapeutic alternative for SAH.
Lin et al. (2022) revealed that cycloastragenol (CAG), the principal active component of Astragalus membranaceus, performs diverse biological functions. The study indicates that CAG significantly inhibits oxidative damage, the production of inflammatory mediators, the activation of microglia, and neutrophil infiltration induced by SAH. Treatment with CAG upregulates Sirtuin 1 expression and suppresses the acetylation levels of Forkhead box O1, NF-κB, and p53, thereby inhibiting subsequent oxidative, inflammatory, and apoptotic pathways. CAG emerges as a promising and effective candidate for SAH treatment.
Hong et al. (2023) discovered that eupatilin, the key bioactive component of the medicinal herb Artemisia asiatica Nakai (Asteraceae), possesses neuroprotective properties. Recent studies have demonstrated that the administration of eupatilin alleviates neural damage in rats after SAH, leading to reduced brain edema and BBB impairment. The research suggests that eupatilin improves SAH-induced EBI by modulating the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway in the rat model.
Sinomenine has been shown to possess neuroprotective effects in traumatic brain injury models. Sinomenine administration was found to enhance neurobehavioral function after SAH, reducing neuronal apoptosis and brain edema. Research suggests that Sinomenine supplementation protects against SAH-induced neuronal apoptosis, potentially by regulating glial cell inflammatory responses and the Nrf2 pathway (Fu et al., 2023).

## Stem cell therapy
The goal of stem cell therapy is to enable therapeutic cells to home in on the damaged areas of the brain, stimulating tissue repair and maintenance through paracrine effects. Stem cell therapy represents an emerging treatment strategy for SAH (Alessandrini et al., 2019).
Liu et al. (2019) investigated the effects of intravenous transplantation of 3 × 106 bone marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs) in SAH rats. Concurrently, the rats received ventricular injections of the Notch1 activation inhibitor N-[N-(3,5-difluorophenacetyl-L-alanyl)]-S-phenylglycine t-butyl ester and Notch1 siRNA. The results demonstrated that BMSC treatment alleviated neurobehavioral deficits, brain edema, and BBB disruptions associated with SAH. It caused a decrease in the expression of ionized calcium-binding adaptor molecule 1, CD68, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, while increasing IL-10 expression. Inhibition of the Notch1 signaling pathway post-SAH by N-[N-(3,5-difluorophenacetyl-L-alanyl)]-S-phenylglycine t-butyl ester or Notch1 siRNA improved neurobehavioral injury, BBB disruption, reduced brain edema, reduced neuronal degeneration, inhibited microglia activation, and reduced the production of pro-inflammatory factors. BMSCs also suppressed the enhancement of Notch1 signaling pathway factors and NF-κB phosphorylation post-SAH while upregulating Botch levels in the ipsilateral cerebral hemisphere. Knockdown of Botch in BMSCs abolished their protective effects on EBI and the inhibitory effect on Notch1 expression. BMSC treatment mitigated neurobehavioral deficits and inflammatory responses after SAH, possibly through the upregulation of Botch expression in brain tissue, thereby inhibiting the Notch1 signaling pathway (Liu et al., 2019). Zhang et al. (2020a) corroborated these findings and additionally identified the involvement of epigenetic regulatory factors in the modulation of microglia-mediated inflammation by BM-MSCs.
Han et al. (2021) showed that extracellular vesicles derived from MSCs (MSC-EVs) mitigate the expression of inflammatory cytokines in the frontal cortex and hippocampus 24 and 48 hours post-SAH. MSC-EVs inhibit NF-κB and activate AMPK to alleviate inflammation following SAH. The modulation of microglia polarization toward the M2 phenotype by MSC-EVs involves suppressing the expression of IL-1β, CD16, CD11b, and iNOS while enhancing CD206 and arginase-1. By suppressing neuroinflammatory responses in rat brain tissues post-SAH, MSC-EVs exhibit neuroprotective properties. These findings suggest the potential consideration of MSC-EVs as a therapeutic approach for early-stage SAH in the future.

## Other uncategorized treatments
Turan et al. (2018) discovered that progesterone treatment markedly diminishes the activation and proliferation of glial cells in the cerebellum and cortex while exhibiting no discernible impact on the brainstem. In a mouse model of SAH, progesterone therapy improved cerebral vasospasm, diminished the activation of glial cells, restored synaptic GluR1 localization, and enhanced neurobehavioral performance. These findings establish a theoretical groundwork for subsequent translational trials evaluating the efficacy of progesterone therapy in the context of SAH.
Li et al. (2019) demonstrated that high-dose minocycline treatment significantly improves neurological function scores following SAH and mitigates cerebral hemorrhage and edema. While low-dose minocycline reduces bleeding, its impact on neurological deficits and brain edema remains insignificant. Minocycline treatment prevents the activation of P2X4R post-SAH, thereby suppressing the downstream phosphorylation of p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase. The therapeutic mechanism of minocycline potentially involves inhibiting the effects of P2X4R on microglia. Chung et al. (2021) substantiated that minocycline can impede the proliferation of microglia and astrocytes, suppress the upregulation of TNF-α and IL-1β after SAH, and diminish SAH-induced neuronal death and cerebral vasospasm. Blecharz-Lang et al. (2022) illustrated that minocycline treatment following SAH significantly diminishes the phagocytic activity of microglia/macrophages, accompanied by reduced spatial interaction with neurons and decreased neuronal apoptosis. Minocycline markedly improves the overexpression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and neuronal cell death induced by SAH. Within clinically relevant time windows, minocycline treatment may represent a long-term beneficial approach for managing secondary brain injury following SAH.
Zuo et al. (2019) revealed that rosuvastatin treatment significantly mitigates brain edema following SAH and ameliorates both short-term and long-term neurological deficits post-SAH. Mechanistically, rosuvastatin elevates peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma and Sirtuin 6 levels; diminishes the expression of phosphorylated Forkhead box O3a, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α;inhibits microglia activation and neutrophil infiltration 24 hours after SAH. The induced retinoid X receptor activation by rosuvastatin reduces neuroinflammation post-SAH and improves neurological deficits. Its anti-neuroinflammatory effects are, at least partially, mediated through the regulation of the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma/Sirtuin 6/Forkhead box O3a pathway. Rosuvastatin may be a promising therapeutic strategy for managing patients with SAH.
Dienel et al. (2021) analyzed plasma samples from 82 subjects and affirmed that alpha-7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonists can alleviate neuroinflammation in male and female mice 24 hours after SAH and are associated with an improved prognosis. This results from the modulation of JAK2/STAT3 and interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase M activity, leading to a robust enhancement in neural/cognitive states. Cell culture experiments suggest the direct beneficial effects of alpha-7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonists on microglia. Alpha-7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonists, particularly galantamine, may offer therapeutic benefits to patients with aSAH.
Li et al. (2023a) found that inhibiting mitochondrial oxidative stress with mitoquinone enhances the phagocytic function of microglia. In summary, the protective effects of antioxidative stress and phagocytic activity present promising therapeutic approaches in the management of SAH.

## Conclusion
The pathogenesis of SAH is intricate, characterized by severe clinical symptoms and a grim prognosis. Microglia, which are integral components of the CNS, play a pivotal role in both the pathogenesis and prognosis of SAH. The mechanisms governing microglia actions are complex, involving a myriad of molecules and pathways. In this comprehensive review, we delineated the activation of microglia during SAH and their involvement in various pathological processes such as vascular spasm, neuroinflammation, neuronal apoptosis, BBB disruption, brain edema, and WMI. Furthermore, we delve into the neuroprotective role played by microglia during the recovery phase of SAH. Despite decades of exploration, the prognosis of SAH remains unsatisfactory, imposing a significant burden on patients and society. Ongoing research on SAH treatment is multifaceted. Within this review, we furnish a comprehensive classification and summary of recent therapeutic approaches targeting microglia in the context of SAH. Our objective is to contribute to the advancement of SAH treatment. This review is based on the consensus opinions of all authors and does not provide specific quantitative data or large-sample statistical analysis. Additionally, most therapeutic methods are still in the laboratory stage. More clinical research and data on human SAH need to be collected and tracked to lay the foundation for future clinical translation.
