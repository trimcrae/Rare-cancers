## Graphical abstract
 
This report discusses potential therapies for treating human coronaviruses (HCoVs) and their economic impact. Specifically, we explore therapeutics that can support the body’s immune response, including immunoglobulin (Ig)A, IgG and T-cell responses, to inhibit the viral replication cycle and improve respiratory function. We hypothesize that carbon quantum dots conjugated with S-nitroso-N-acetylpenicillamine (SNAP) could be a synergistic alternative cure for treating respiratory injuries caused by HCoV infections. To achieve this, we propose developing aerosol sprays containing SNAP moieties that release nitric oxide and are conjugated onto promising nanostructured materials. These sprays could combat HCoVs by inhibiting viral replication and improving respiratory function. Furthermore, they could potentially provide other benefits, such as providing novel possibilities for nasal vaccines in the future.

## Introduction
The ongoing global health and fiscal consequences of SARS-CoV-2 emphasize the urgent need for potential alternative therapies to mitigate its detrimental consequences. Concerns about the effectiveness of current treatments for novel infections have exposed the need for: (i) the development of new antiviral agents; and (ii) improvements to respiratory devices to achieve more consistent results. Even at an accelerated rate, the development of antiviral agents for this ravaging infection is toilsome, because the structural flexibility at the receptor-binding domain–angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (RBD–ACE2) interface permits the RBD to endure numerous mutations while maintaining high ACE2 affinity. , Additionally, the primary health-worsening effects of SARS-CoV-2 have a significant impact on patient morbidity and mortality. In addition to the imperative need for a vaccine to provide IgG and T-cell responses that offer significant immunity, there is a significant concern about highlighting nonspecific therapeutic strategies that can also support an IgA response, protecting the nasal mucosa from secondary infections. By April 2020, some 330 clinical trials were in progress worldwide to evaluate potential therapies against COVID-19, the disease caused by the virus.
Besides the rush to develop an effective drug candidate to treat SARS-CoV-1 infection, , a few novel therapeutic approaches were investigated. One such approach was nitric oxide (NO)-based treatment, which aimed to inhibit the viral replication cycle and reduce the synthesis of viral progeny at an early stage. Recent reports reveal that diverse macromolecules had been considered to modify virucidal agents to combat severe airflow obstruction. , However, these macromolecules were later replaced by biocompatible nanomaterials owing to their ability to maintain the activity, selectivity and stability of these agents. Studies have shown that metal or metal oxide nanoparticles, as well as polymeric nanoparticles aided with NO donors, have demonstrated an improvement in therapeutic properties and effective delivery. However, the toxicity of metal nanoparticles can cause adverse effects on the lungs, which is why carbon nanomaterials are now preferred. , Based on current developments, a new series of molecular nanoarchitectures called SNAP@CQD conjugates have been proposed to combat human coronaviruses. These structures are designed to be biocompatible and use carbon nanomaterials instead of metal nanoparticles to avoid potential toxicity concerns. The proposed conjugates are expected to enhance the antiviral activity and selectivity of modified virucidal agents, ultimately improving the treatment of severe airflow obstruction caused by human coronaviruses.

## Potential of SNAP and CQDs against SARS-CoV-1 infection
The human body produces NO internally by converting l-arginine to NO under the catalytic influence of the enzyme nitric oxide synthase (NOS). NOS exists in three isoforms: the neuronal isoform (nNOS), which produces NO to act as a neuronal messenger that regulates synaptic neurotransmitter release ; the macrophage or inducible isoform (iNOS), which activates after inflammatory or other injurious stimuli such as bacteria or viruses; and endothelial NOS (eNOS), which produces NO in the vasculature to regulate blood flow. , The antiviral ability and the dilation of blood vessels are largely dependent on the activity of eNOS and iNOS, which are delineated in this paper to better understand their roles in the context of SARS-CoV-1 infection. After inhalation, NO diffuses rapidly across the alveolar–capillary membrane into the subjacent smooth muscle of the pulmonary vessels to activate soluble guanylate cyclase ( 
). This enzyme mediates many of the biological effects of NO and is responsible for the conversion of GTP to cGMP. Increased intracellular concentrations of cGMP relax smooth muscle via different mechanisms. In addition to its pulmonary vasodilating effects, inhaled NO could lead to bronchodilation and has anti-inflammatory and antiproliferative effects. Locally released NO in the airways is followed by oxidation to stable and inactive nitrate in the pulmonary circulation. However, some NO is trapped by the blood and forms S-nitroso thiol (RSNO) in erythrocytes and plasma proteins for systemic bioavailable NO donors to provide systemic benefits. Furthermore, surface-grafting utilizing zwitterionic polymer brushes combined with NO release have already demonstrated excellent antibacterial and antithrombotic activity. , Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) circuits, which are combined with NO, have been demonstrated to enhance antifouling characteristics, improve the surface morphology, ensure easy release and exhibit antiviral characteristics owing to their remarkable antimicrobial properties and antithrombotic effects ( 
). The most promising approach to prevent complications and concurrently exhibit antiviral effects in ECMO treatments appears to be materials that use combinations of antifouling techniques that incorporate NO release and/or production. 
 
In pursuit of catering to lower and upper respiratory sabotages, NO-based virucidal drugs like INOmax™ and FabiSpray® , were introduced. Although nasal spray gNO was found to be effective as an antimicrobial as well as a virucidal agent, it also manifests I/R-induced lung injury and behaves as a causative agent of endotoxin-induced acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) in many cases. , Moreover, the stability and transport of this free radical under physiological conditions remain a challenge, because NO gets rapidly scavenged in the presence of molecular oxygen or oxyhemoglobin, which calls for a drug carrier capable of localized and sustainable NO release. Sustainable NO release requires an extremely stable NO donor to deliver the highly unstable NO radical, for example nitroprusside, organic nitrates, S-nitroso thiols, metal complexes, N-diazeniumdiolate and Nω-nitro-l-arginine methyl ester. RSNOs are a prevalent class of physiological compounds and constitute endogenous storage forms of NO; hitherto, S-nitroso-N-acetyl penicillamine (SNAP) and S-nitroso-N-acetylcysteine (SNAC) have been the focus. SNAP, in antiviral clinical trials, has been shown to be an active NO donor owing to its ease of synthesis and purification, along with its stability in comparison to primary S-nitroso thiols (e.g., S-nitroso glutathione). Additionally, the nontoxic and biocompatible nature of its degradation products is also an advantage. , Although SNAP is thermodynamically stable, it shows kinetic instability under physiological conditions triggered by pH, enzymes or reductants. Therefore, a drug carrier is of the utmost importance to ensure the kinetic stability of the RSNO, which can favor controlled drug release. Carbon quantum dots (CQDs), having a size of 10 nm with high surface-functionalization properties, gained prominence owing to their exceptional cell membrane permeability, high biocompatibility and low cytotoxicity. This is even supported by the massive surface-area:volume ratio of CQDs, which serves as a binding station for ligands and adds to their ability to intervene in the viral molecule and generate lethal combinations against HCoVs.

## Anti-inflammatory impact of NO on coronaviruses
The consequential defensive response that incapacitates pathogens and lays the premises for tissue repair is known as inflammation. Studies on the NO:ROS ratio indicate how practically every stage of inflammation is either promoted or inhibited. , In the case of SARS-CoV-2, lower levels of NO have shown a strong relationship to vasculature impairment. Coronaviruses typically infect the respiratory system by transmitting through the nasopharyngeal region and then progressing to the bronchioles and alveoli. This can cause extensive damage to the cardiovascular and pulmonary systems. NO plays a preeminent part in severe acute respiratory syndrome, because it primarily offers nasal mucosal defense , and improves arterial oxygenation, strengthening immunological support in the bronchioles. In addition to bronchodilation, NO also protects blood vessels from excess platelet activation , , and blood clotting by circulating cells and platelets during pathological processes and maintaining vascular homeostasis. , Furthermore, it prevents unwanted body injury through the production of key cytokines in immune cells during the inflammatory response, because the cytokine storm has an extreme inflammatory effect. , , NO also has the notable property of inhibiting cellular entry by reducing palmitoylation of (S) proteins, possibly via nitration of (S) proteins or by using peroxynitrite formed by the oxidation of NO with superoxide anions. Consequently, it is undeniable that the immune response mediated by NO plays a significant part in inhibiting viral entry. NO thus exerts an administrative influence on lymphocyte and monocyte operations, providing strong signal transduction between biological and vaccine-induced immunity. Comprehensively, the high infectivity rate of HCoVs depends on virus tropism and pathogenicity. These are governed by the expression and tissue distribution of entrance receptors that release genetic material for replication. On ingress, SARS-CoV uses the infectious RNA to act as mRNA in the cellular environment, leading to the fast translation of two massive open reading frames: ORF1a and ORF1b, which encode protease enzymes. These are co-translationally and post-translationally processed into the individual nonstructural proteins (nsps) that account for the viral transcription and replication complex. From the assessments reported previously, it was evident that NO has a direct effect on N protein or possibly affects one or both 3CL (3-chymotrypsin-like) cysteine proteases encoded in ORF1a in coronaviruses, bridging viral replication wherein they interact with N-encapsulated, freshly produced genomic RNA. , Among other possibilities, NO possibly affects the replication process by oxidizing amino acids of viral capsid proteins using in situ generated peroxynitrite. 

## Virucidal efficacy of CQDs
The efficacy of CQDs against viruses has been demonstrated in various studies. For instance, CQDs produced from boronic acid exhibited stronger antiviral effects than existing nanoparticle-based inhibitors, such as tannic-acid-modified Ag nanoparticles, against herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) infection. Similarly, boronic-acid-modified CQDs were found to be effective entrance inhibitors against HIV infection, with the boronic acid sites on CQDs reacting with the 1,2-cis diol sites on gp120 to form a tetravalent boronated diester cyclic complex. This suppresses the development of syncytium and exhibits excellent antiviral ability, with an IC50 value of 26.7 mg/ml. More intriguingly, CQDs derived from benzoxazine-monomer and glycyrrhizic acid have been found to strongly prevent viral infection against various lethal flaviviruses (Japanese encephalitis, Zika and dengue viruses) and nonenveloped viruses (porcine parvovirus and adenovirus-associated virus). Nitrogen-rich CQDs were also found to exhibit great antiviral activity against DNA and RNA viruses, such as Pseudorabies virus (PRV) and porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV), respectively, by triggering type I interferon responses. , Functionalized CQDs were tested as antiviral agents in combination with Duviral – a compounded medication designed to obstruct nucleoside reverse transcriptase activity, yielding increased antiviral efficacy. In a study investigating the feasibility of using drug-loaded GQDs as non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) to treat HIV, it was found that the conjugation of CQDs with CHI499 (a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor) greatly enhanced the antiviral efficacy (IC50 of 0.67 µg/mL vs IC50 of 0.09 ± 0.12 µg/mL, with and without GQDs). Moreover, in addition to acting as a drug-loading platform, CQDs also exhibited a certain level of antiviral activity. A similar trend of effective virucidal impact was observed when antisense locked nucleic acid (LNA) oligonucleotide functionalized CQDs were used as a gene-delivery platform for the treatment of Kaposi’s sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV) infection. With an understanding that quaternate cationic CQDs could act as an adjuvant to promote antigen presentation and induce a robust immune response, Huang and co-workers developed carbon composites by absorbing ovalbumin (OVA), as a model antigen, on the cationic charge-bearing CQDs. In comparison, it was observed that OVA-bonded CQDs yielded higher cellular uptake and secreted more OVA-specific IgG, ∼60-fold more than with OVA alone. In addition to cellular uptake and specific immune response, the composite demonstrated persistent antibody secretion that persisted for >8 weeks. Eventually, this led to the development of an antigen-OVA-based CQD platform for intranasal vaccination to trigger a specific immune response. The findings indicate that vaccine formulations based on CQDs, when administered intranasally, significantly enhance IgG and IgA production in local and distant mucosal regions, as well as induce more memory T cells. In another study, modified CQDs with ricin-toxin-binding subunit B (RTB) were found to have an immunoregulatory capacity when added to macrophages. Compared with RTB alone, the functionalized CQDs triggered an increase in NO production in macrophages, which then secreted more cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α and interleukin (IL)-6, which modulated immune activity. To combat HCoV-229E, amine-rich CQDs were developed and functionalized with boronic acid. Compared with nonmodified CQDs, the boronic-acid-functionalized and boronic-acid-doped CQDs showed a higher inhibition rate of HCoVs, with estimated EC50 values of 52 ± 8 mg/ml and 5.2 ± 0.7 mg/ml, respectively. 

## Therapeutics against HCoVs using SNAP@CQD
The involvement of NO in the nitrosation of thiols and amines in the extracellular matrix, and lipid peroxidation in the cell wall, has led to the development of NO-discharging materials that can simulate vital endogenous effects under specific conditions for the treatment of HCoVs. Owing to the promising potential of carbon nanoplatforms against a broad range of enveloped positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses and their low toxicity in humans, , using nanomaterials as a drug carrier seems appealing to achieve a twofold outcome. The therapeutic potential of CQD derivatives proposes the development of SNAP@CQD conjugates for the synergistic curative effect on human coronaviruses using nanoengineering approaches. In this regard, peptide chemistry can be incorporated for surface functionalization to develop SNAP@CQD. Peptides are inherent subunits of proteins and macromolecules responsible for diverse cellular tasks. Activation of SNAP or sustained NO release can be modulated by anchoring metals with suitable redox potential, such as copper nanostructures. , Although several approaches exist for the synthesis of hetero-doped CQDs, thermolysis of Na2[Cu(EDTA)] and hydroxylamine hydrochloride at 300 °C would result in the effortless synthesis of CQD-1. CQD-1, owing to its pendant –NH2, –OH and –COOH, can block invasion through interaction with spike proteins and provide a union point for functionalization, making it a strong therapeutic candidate against HCoVs. To provide a synergistic effect, SNAP will be anchored to the pendant –NH2 group via a peptide linkage. Boronic acid, owing to its properties in inhibiting viral entry by interacting with serine residues near the active site of 3CLpro, , and its Lewis acidic nature, which can adjust the pH level caused by the viral infection, can further functionalize CQD-1 with 4-amino boronic acid through a peptide linkage to develop CQD-2 and heighten the restorative gain. To provide a sustainable approach, CQD-3 can be synthesized by the thermolysis of Na2[Cu(EDTA)] and sugar molasses at 180 °C. It can be further functionalized with SNAP through a variety of flexible-to-rigid diamine linkers to generate CQD-4 ( 
), where sulfonic acid will also assist in inhibiting HSPG-dependent coronaviruses. 
 
Besides copper, platinum, a biocompatible transition metal, has demonstrated enhanced release of NO from SNAP ; and silver has a redox potential lying within Cu+/Cu2+ and Pt2+/Pt, which can additionally induce sustained NO release. This suggests the potential for developing derivatives of CQDs that can accommodate metal complexes and SNAP as a conjugate for an effective antiviral effect. Interestingly, hydroxyl derivatives of CQD, specifically CQD-5, can be synthesized from citric acid via the hydrothermal route, and further oxidized to 1,2-dicarbonyl via ruthenium-catalyzed oxidation, generating CQD-6. CQD-6 will be nanoengineered with 1,10-phenanthroline-5,6-diamine and 4-aminobenzyl-1,2-diamine. CQD-7 is particularly significant because it has pendant –NH2 groups for SNAP functionalization and chelating receptors for accommodating metal complexes. ## Concluding remarks and future outlook
The host cell undergoes significant biological changes as an outcome of the viral infection cycle, leading to cell damage. Therefore, a potent therapeutic strategy that inhibits viral adhesion to host cells and replication is necessary to reduce infection and propagation. Nanomedicinal theranostic devices are being touted as the future of therapeutics, and their role has been amplified in the wake of the recent COVID-19 pandemic. Based on the investigations and design herein, we anticipate conceptual insight into a series of SNAP-CQD conjugates to combat HCoVs. SNAP-entrenched CQDs have several benefits, such as easing cellular entry and providing thermodynamic stability for the sustained release of NO, which could protect against adverse vascular and pulmonary ramifications. Synergistically, the conjugate can block endocytosis by reducing palmitoylation through the action of NO and pendant groups in CQDs or can inhibit viral proliferation by blocking enzymes like 3CLpro. As a result, we hypothesize that SNAP and boronic-acid-nanoengineered CQDs carrying heteroatoms (S, N) and metal atoms (Cu, Ag and Pt) would have a lethal effect against HCoV infection. Owing to their sheer aerosolized release, they will shield the upper respiratory tract by enabling NO release in the region, thereby minimizing the serious side-effects of coronavirus by lowering vascular stress. From a broader perspective, insights into the mode-of-action and interaction of SNAP@CQD with viruses and its proteases could be the aim for future studies owing to the cutting-edge properties.
