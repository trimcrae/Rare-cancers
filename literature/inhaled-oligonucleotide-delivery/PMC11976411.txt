## Highlights
•GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 expression levels were decreased in mice subjected to IR injury.
•GAS6/Axl-mediated signaling machinery protects against myocardial IR injury likely through inhibition of mitochondrial injury, oxidative stress, and apoptosis.
•SIRT1 serves as a downstream effector for GAS6/Axl-initiated myocardial benefits.
•Cian, screened from natural product library, possesses potent cardioprotection efficacy via GAS6 activation.
•These observations molecularly define a feature of GAS6 that protects against myocardial IR injury.

## Introduction
Myocardial ischemia–reperfusion (IR) injury is a common medical issue contributing to the onset and progression of ischemic heart diseases (IHD). Growth arrest-specific gene 6 (GAS6), a vitamin K-dependent secretory protein, promotes cell proliferation and inhibits inflammation and apoptosis through binding with Tyro3, Axl, and Mertk (TAM) receptors.

## Objectives
Our study aimed to examine the effect of GAS6 pathways activation as a potential new treatment in myocardial IR injury.

## Methods
Gain- and loss-of-function experiments were utilized to determine the roles of GAS6 in the pathological processes of myocardial IR injury.

## Results
Our results revealed down-regulated levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in murine hearts subjected to IR injury, and cardiomyocytes challenged with hypoxia reoxygenation (HR) injury. GAS6 overexpression significantly improved cardiac dysfunction in mice subjected to myocardial IR injury, accompanied by reconciled mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. In vitro experiments also observed a protective effect of GAS6 in cardiomyocytes. SIRT1 was found to function as a downstream regulator for GAS6/Axl signaling axis. Through screening a natural product library, a polyphenol natural compound catechin was identified to exhibit a protective effect by turning on GAS6/Axl-SIRT1 cascade.

## Conclusions
Together, our findings indicate that GAS6 emerges as a potential novel target in the management of myocardial IR injury and other related anomalies.

## Introduction
Ischemic heart diseases (IHD) continue to be the main factor for disability and mortality worldwide. One of the primary causative cues for IHD is myocardial ischemic injury evoked by partial or complete loss of perfusion due to coronary artery occlusion and biochemical metabolic alterations , . Several drugs including cyclosporine and metoprolol have been shown to reduce infarct size, but they fail to clear the hurdle of concrete clinical end points in many large clinical trials , . Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is the classical effective measures to reopen occluded arteries to minimize myocardial ischemic injury . However, reperfusion treatment is often accompanied by the “second hit” injury, such as decreased myocardial systolic function and arrhythmias, which contributes to irreversible myocardial injury, namely ischemia–reperfusion (IR) injury , . During reperfusion, reactivation of electron transport chain and opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) lead to the devastating oxidative stress, inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and ultimately cell death , . Despite the perceived benefits of reperfusion procedure, there are limited options available to protect IHD patients from myocardial IR.
Growth arrest-specific gene 6 (GAS6), a vitamin K-dependent secretory protein, was initially identified in mouse fibroblasts . GAS6 is involved in a variety of biological processes, including cell vitality, immune regulation, vascular integrity, and remodeling via binding with Tyro3, Axl, and Mertk , , . Among these receptors, Axl shows the highest affinity with GAS6 (Axl > Tyro3 > Mertk) , . GAS6/Axl axis plays a key role in physiological and pathophysiological cardiovascular processes including congenital heart disease , valvular heart disease , myocardial fibrosis , and endothelial cell injury . Notably, it has been reported that GAS6 mRNA level is significantly decreased during liver IR injury, and GAS6 deficiency aggravates liver injury in mice . However, recombinant GAS6 administration inhibits IR-induced liver injury, as shown by reduced proinflammatory response and liver cell death . Nonetheless, whether GAS6 and its receptors (Axl) participate in the development of myocardial IR injury still remains elusive.
Silencing information regulator 1 (SIRT1) is an NAD+ dependent type III histone deacetylase that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, senescence, apoptosis, and metabolism , , . Several reports have unveiled an important cardiometabolic regulatory roles of SIRT1 , , . Our earlier study also revealed that melatonin inhibits oxidative stress-induced myocardial IR injury through SIRT1 signaling . Moreover, under vascular calcification, miR-34a inhibits cell proliferation and induces senescence through directly down-regulating Axl and SIRT1 . Nonetheless, the interplay between GAS6/Axl and SIRT1 in face of myocardial IR injury remains unclear.
Here, our present study aimed to evaluate the role of GAS6/Axl-SIRT1 in myocardial IR injury and screen a natural regulator targeting GAS6 from a natural product library as well as assess the efficacy of screened a natural regulator for activating GAS6 in myocardial IR injury. Taken together, our results favor a convincing cardioprotective property of targeting GAS6/Axl-SIRT1 signaling axis against myocardial IR injury, denoting its therapeutic potential in clinical settings of myocardial IR injury such as thrombolytic therapy, acute myocardial infarction, and heart transplantation.

## Methods

## Animals
Adult male BALB/c mice (22–25 g) aged 8–10 weeks were obtained from the animal center of Air Force Military Medical University (Xi’an, Shaanxi, China). All animal experiment protocols were performed in accordance with the guidelines of Animal Care and Use Committees at the Northwest University (Xi’an, Shaanxi, China, 2019012) and were in compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85–23, revised 2011). All mice had free access to food and water and were bred at 25°C in a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle.

## Materials
Natural product library was purchased from purchased from TargetMol Chemicals Inc (Boston, MA, USA). 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescin diacetate (DCF-DA) detection kit, Dihydroethidium (DHE) and 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) were purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Cyanine 3 (Cy3) was purchased from Servicebio Technology (Wuhan, Hubei, China).

## Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
ELISA was used to detect GAS6 in serum samples from IR mice or Sham mice. The measurement of GAS6 serum levels was performed in strict accordance with the kit instructions (Boster Biological Technology Co., Ltd, USA). Finally, the OD value was measured at 450 nm using the microplate reader. A standard curve was drawn to calculate the changes of its content.

## Mouse myocardial IR injury model
Anesthesia was performed with 2% isoflurane (3 L/min) prior to surgery and was maintained with 1.5 L/min during surgery. Mouse chest skin was disinfected with 75% alcohol, and left thoracotomy was performed to determine the anterior descending branch of the left coronary artery (LAD) in the right decubitus position. A slip-knot was tied 2–3 mm downstream of the anterior descending branch of left coronary artery with 6–0 suture. Then the heart was quickly placed back to the chest cavity, and excess air was gently squeezed out to avoid pneumothorax. After 30 min of ischemia, mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane again. The chest was then reopened and the long end of suture was gently pulled to loosen the noose for 3 or 24 h of reperfusion. Successful myocardial reperfusion is marked by change in color from pale to reddish in the ischemic area. Mice were put back into the cage and were inhaled room air for 3–5 min to resume normal mental activities. Sham group received similar operation procedure although the left coronary artery was not ligated ( A).

## Echocardiography evaluation
Transthoracic echocardiography was performed using an animal specific instrument (VisualSonics Vevo3100, VisualSonics, Toronto, ON, Canada) at 3 or 24 h following of reperfusion. Ejection fraction (EF), fractional shortening (FS), cardiac output (CO), stroke volume (SV), left ventricular diastolic volume (LVEDV), left ventricular systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricular anterior wall thickness of systole period (LVPWs), and left ventricular anterior wall thickness of diastole period (LVPWd) were measured. All measurements were averaged from five consecutive cardiac cycles and were carried out by three experienced technicians blinded to the identities of animal groups.

## Detection of myocardial infarction size and risk area
Myocardial infarct size was detected using Evans blue (Biotoppend, China)/Triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC, Sigma, USA) double staining. Firstly, the coronary artery was ligated again, and the abdominal aorta was injected with 2 mL of 2% Evans Blue, then the heart was immediately taken out. The heart was then sliced in the mold, and incubated in 2% TTC staining solution at 37°C for 20 min. Finally, the sections of hearts were fixed overnight in 4% paraformaldehyde, prior to imaging analysis.

## Adeno-associated viral delivery
Construction of recombinant adeno-associated virus (serotype 9) vector containing MCS promoter (AAV9-MCS-GAS6) (Genecarer Inc., Xi'an, China). AAV9-MCS was used as the negative control. Virus packaging was performed in AAV-293 cells, and then viral medium was collected at 48 and 72 h. Mice were delivered 200 μL virus liquid using tail vein injection, and were fed normally for 30 days prior to detection of GAS6 in myocardial tissues. In addition, mice underwent ischemia for 30 min and reperfusion for 3 h, prior to assessment of cardiac function, blood routine, and blood biochemistry.

## Immunohistochemistry staining
All tissue samples were fixed with formalin and embedded with paraffin. The 5 μm sections were mounted onto slides (Starfrost® Knittel Glass). For immunohistochemically analysis, antigen retrieval was carried out in a citrate buffer (10 mM, pH 6.0) or Tris–EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) at 95°C for 20 min. Briefly, sections were incubated overnight (4°C) with primary antibodies including GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 rabbit polyclonal antibody in a humidified chamber following blocking. Samples were developed using diaminobenzidine (DAB) solution, and tissue sections were counterstained with Mayer’s hematoxylin.

## Cell culture and the construction of an appropriate HR injury model
H9c2 and HL-1 cells were obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Rockville, MD, USA). GAS6 overexpressed (GAS6 OE) H9c2 cells were constructed by Cyagen Biosciences (Guangzhou, China). Cell culture conditions were followed as previously described . The culture medium was replaced every 3 days. At 80% confluence, cells were digested with 0.25% trypsin and cultured in a ratio of 1:3. Cells were ground into a single-cell suspension prior to centrifugation. Supernatants were discarded, and cells were rinsed with PBS prior to cell counting. When the confluence of H9c2 and HL-1 cells reached 80–90%, a serum-free culture medium was replaced. An anoxic chamber was applied to form the anaerobic environment (Longyue Company, Shanghai, China) with the less than 6%-8% (v/v) oxygen concentration. H9c2 cells following 12 h-hypoxia and 12 h-reoxygenation (H12R12) and HL-1 cells following 6 h-hypoxia and 6 h-reoxygenation (H6R6) were selected as the appropriate HR injury model ( B-C).

## Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) detection in cells
LDH levels were determined in culture medium using a commercially available kit (Nanjing jiancheng bioengineering institute, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Optical density was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Multiskan FC, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and data were presented as fold changes of the control.

## Construction of GAS6 overexpression/knock down in HL-1 cell lines
The pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-Puro plasmid (System Biosciences, LLC located at 2438 Embarcadero Way Palo Alto, CA 94303) underwent standard molecular cloning techniques for its creation. Plasmid PCDH-GAS6 (GAS6: NM_019521.2) was prepared using overlapping PCR, with pPCDH-control serving as the control group. The pGreenPuro shRNA lentiviral vector was generated by cloning specific oligonucleotides into the lentiviral vector following the manufacturer's instructions from System Biosciences. The target sequence of the GAS6 shRNA template oligonucleotides was 5-CCTGGCACTGATGGAAATCAA-3. All constructs were sequenced for verification. These vectors, along with 293TN cells, lentiviral vectors, and packaging and envelope plasmids, were co-transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 to produce the virus. The supernatant containing the virus was collected 2–3 days after transfection and used to infect cardiomyocytes. The stably expressing cells were screened using purinomycin and identified through qRT-PCR and Western blot analysis.

## Cell viability assay
Cells were treated with trypsin and the supernatant was removed and then centrifuged. The cells were then resuspended in PBS to create a single cell suspension. Next, they were incubated with the MuseTM Count & Viability Assay Kit (MCH100104, Merck&Millipore, Germany) for 5 min in the dark at room temperature. Cell viability was subsequently assessed using the MuseTM cell analyzer (Merck&Millipore, Germany).

## Apoptosis assay
A MuseTM Cell Analyzer was used according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. Briefly, cells were harvested and were stained with a MuseTM Annexin V & Dead Cell Kit (MCH100105, Merck&Millipore, Germany) in the dark prior to detection of living cells, early apoptotic cells, late apoptotic cells, and death cells. A TUNEL Apoptosis Detection Kit was used to evaluate apoptosis of cardiac tissues (Servicebio, USA) where tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm intervals. Nuclei were stained blue with DAPI, and TUNEL-positive cardiomyocyte nuclei (apoptotic cells) were stained green. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (NIKON ECLIPSE C1).

## ROS production assay
DCF-DA or DHE staining was employed to measure ROS generation. Cells were incubated with DCF-DA or DHE (10 μM, Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) in the dark at 37°C for 20 min. Fluorescence intensity was immediately recorded using a Fluorescence microscope (Invitrogen EVOS M5000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Visual fields were randomly photographed and fluorescence signals were quantified using the Image J software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

## Mitochondrial membrane potential measurement
The enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential detection kit (JC-1) from Beyotime Biotechnology in Shanghai, China was used to detect the mitochondrial membrane potential. When the mitochondrial membrane potential is high, JC-1 forms a polymer in the matrix of mitochondria, leading to a red fluorescence signal. Conversely, under low mitochondrial membrane potential, JC-1 remains a monomer and cannot accumulate in the mitochondria matrix, resulting in green fluorescence. Cells were stained with JC-1 for 20 min at 37°C and then visualized using a fluorescence microscopy (Invitrogen EVOS M5000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

## Co-immunoprecipitation assay
Interaction between Axl and SIRT1 was determined by Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP). Lysates were pretreated with protein A/G (Immunoprecipitation Starter Pack, GE Healthcare, New York, NY, USA) for 1 h at 4°C. Supernatant was transferred into a new centrifuge tube and was incubated with antibodies against SIRT1 overnight at 4°C. Protein A/G plus was immunoprecipitated overnight at 4°C to precipitate the immune complexes. The precipitated complexes were rinsed with a lysis buffer and were boiled for 3 min followed by immunoblotting.

## Luciferase construct and reporter gene assays
With the Ensembl database, EPD data maps, and literature analysis, the effective promoter core sequence of CAS6 gene was determined and cloned into the pGL3-basic reporter gene vector. 293T cells were inoculated into 24-well plates at a density of 2x104/mL. After 24 h, cells were instantaneously co-transfected with pGL3-GAS6, RTK and Lipofectamine 2000. Following 24 h transfection, 293T cells were washed with PBS, and medium was changed to a serum-free medium. After treatment with natural products for 3 h, cells were collected, and double luciferin assay was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions (Promega, Madison, WI, USA).

## Statistical analyses
All values are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed with GraphPad Prism version 8.01. Significant differences were evaluated using student’s t test or one-way ANOVA where appropriate. Multiple comparisons were performed using a Tukey’s HSD post hoc analysis. Differences were considered significant at P < 0.05.

## Results

## GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 expression levels were decreased in the hearts of mice subjected to IR injury
Ischemia model was constructed by 30 min of ischemia followed by 3 h (IR 3 h) or 24 h (IR 24 h) of reperfusion. Assessment of echocardiography in mice at 3- or 24 h following IR injury revealed overtly drop in EF, FS, CO, and LVEDV at both time points, while significant increases in LVPWd and LVPWs at 3 h following IR ( A). When compared with the IR 24 h group, mice challenged by IR 3 h displayed more pronounced cardiac dysfunction, as shown by marked decrease in SV and LVESV, the effect of which was absent in IR 24 h group ( A). Blood routine parameters (WBC, LYM, and MID) and blood biochemical CK-MB, TnI, LDH, and AST were drastically elevated at IR 3 h, while CKMB, TnI, LDH, AST, and BUN was declined at IR 24 h ( B-C). Histopathology was employed to discern myocardial structure. IR induced muscle fiber disorder, interstitial hypertrophy, and destruction of cellular structure ( D). TUNEL staining suggested that IR injury also increased the level of apoptosis at both IR 3 h or 24 h ( E). In addition, serum GAS6 levels were analyzed using ELISA kit in mice. We found that serum GAS6 levels were significantly declined following IR 3 h or 24 h ( F). We then evaluated GAS6/Axl and SIRT1 signaling in IR-challenged murine myocardium at different time points. As shown in G, qRT-PCR showed that mRNA levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 were overtly decreased at IR 3 h or IR 24 h. Similarly, Western blot results showed that protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 were overtly downregulated at IR 3 h ( H). A similar trend was observed using immunohistochemistry analysis ( I). To sum up, IR 3 h might evoke more pronounced myocardial injury, and was employed as an appropriate time point for subsequent experiments. 

## GAS6 overexpression alleviated IR injury-induced cardiac dysfunction in vivo
To clarify the role of GAS6/Axl signaling in myocardial IR injury, we constructed GAS6 overexpression (AAV9-GAS6) mice, which was validated by overtly up-regulation of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 using Western blot and qRT-PCR ( A-B). GAS6 overexpression increased EF, FS, CO, SV, LVEDV, LVESV, and decreased of LVPWd and LVPWs in IR injured-mice ( A). Blood routine and blood biochemical parameters exhibited that GAS6 overexpression reduced levels of WBC, LYM, MID, LDH, and AST while rose levels of GRA and ALB ( B-C). Consistently, GAS6 overexpression significantly reduced infarct size (white area, D). HE staining revealed improved myocardial injury in GAS6 overexpressed mice subjected to IR, in comparison with AAV9-Control + IR group ( E). These data suggest that GAS6 overexpression markedly improved cardiac function and myocardial structure in IR injury. 
Immunohistochemistry findings showed elevated levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in heart tissues of GAS6 overexpressed mice compared with the AAV9-Control mice in the face of IR challenge ( G), and a similar trend was observed in mouse cardiomyocytes using immunofluorescence staining ( H). Meanwhile, Western blot showed much higher protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in the heart tissues of AAV9-GAS6 mice compared with AAV9-Control mice under IR injury ( I). In addition, GAS6 overexpression significantly altered levels of canonical IR injury-related protein markers for apoptosis, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Compared with AAV9-Control + IR group, there were fewer TUNEL-positive cells (green) and up-regulated levels of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl2 in the AAV9-Control + IR group, indicating reduced apoptosis in cardiomyocytes ( F and 2I). GAS6 overexpression increased markers of antioxidant stress (Nrf2 and NQO1) as well as mitochondrial biomarkers (PGC-1α, TFAM, UCP2, and NRF1) in IR-injured mice ( I). These findings suggest a vital role for GAS6 in protecting against apoptosis, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction under myocardial IR injury.

## GAS6 overexpression protected against HR injury in vitro
Rat H9c2 and mouse HL-1 cells were subjected to hypoxia for 12 h and 6 h, respectively, followed by different reoxygenation durations (3 h, 6 h, and 12 h). HR injury decreased mRNA and protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in H9c2 ( A-B) and HL-1 cardiomyocytes ( C-D). Notably, H9c2 cells following 12 h-reoxygenation (H12R12) and HL-1 cells following 6 h-reoxygenation (H6R6) induced significant changes of mRNA and protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1, which was selected as the HR injury model. We next constructed H9c2 cells ( A-C) and HL-1 cells ( A-C) with GAS6 overexpression (GAS6-OE), validated using qRT-PCR, immunofluorescence, and Western blot. Moreover, GAS6-OE also prominently increased cell viability ( C), while decreased intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS, D), apoptosis ( E), and LDH release of culture medium ( F) in HR-injured H9c2 as well as HL-1 cells ( A-D). Mitochondrial membrane potential detection revealed more evident green JC-1 monomer in HR-challenged cells, indicating collapsed mitochondrial membrane potential ( G and E). However, the green JC-1 fluorescence was significantly decreased, and mitochondrial membrane potential was recovered in GAS6-OE + HR group ( G and E). Molecularly, GAS6-OE overtly increased fluorescence intensity of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in HR-injured cardiomyocytes ( H and F). In addition, GAS6-OE overtly up-regulated levels of Axl, SIRT1, Bcl2, Nrf2, HO-1, NQO1, PGC-1α, TFAM, NRF1, and UCP2, in HR-challenged group ( I and G). These data indicate that GAS6-OE distinctly inhibited HR injury in H9c2 or HL-1 cells, reminiscent of the in vivo results. 

## GAS6 deficiency worsened cardiomyocyte HR injury in vitro
GAS6 siRNA targeting H9c2 cells were synthesized to generate GAS6-deficiency cells. Western blot showed that GAS6 deficiency reduced protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in H9c2 cells ( A) and HL-1 cells ( A). qRT-PCR and immunofluorescence results showed that GAS6 deficiency overtly decreased levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 ( B-C and B-C). Subsequently, GAS6 deficiency drastically reduced cell viability ( A) while promoted intracellular ROS accumulation ( B), apoptotic rate ( C) and LDH release of culture medium ( D) in HR-challenged H9c2 cells. In addition, green JC-1 monomer fluorescence was stronger in GAS6 siRNA + HR cells compared with Control siRNA + HR group, indicating that GAS6 deficiency led to further collapsed mitochondrial membrane potential under IR stress ( E). Additionally, GAS6 deficiency reduced fluorescence intensity of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 in HR-exposed H9c2 cells ( F). Similarly, the decreased levels of GAS6, Axl, SIRT1, Bcl2, Nrf2, HO-1, PGC-1α, TFAM, NRF1, and UCP2, as well as increased Bax protein were also observed in GAS6 siRNA + HR cells ( G). Such effects were also found in mouse HL-1 cells ( A-G). These observations favor that GAS6 deficiency indeed aggravated cardiomyocyte HR injury through apoptosis, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial injury. 

## Axl was the key receptor for GAS6-evoked cardioprotection against cardiomyocyte HR injury
Axl, Tyro3, and Mertk siRNAs was treated in H9c2 cells with GAS6-OE and cells were then exposed to HR injury. Axl siRNA remarkably reduced cell viability, increased intracellular ROS accumulation and apoptosis in HR-challenged H9c2 cells with GAS6-OE ( A-C). Conversely, Tyro3 siRNA increased cell viability, reduced intracellular ROS accumulation and apoptosis in GAS6-OE + HR group. Mertk siRNA displayed little effects on cell survival, intracellular ROS, and apoptosis ( A-C). In GAS6-OE with HR-injured HL-1 cells, Axl siRNA displayed similar responses ( A-C). These results suggest that Axl deficiency nullified GAS6 overexpression-offered cardioprotection against HR injury. Hence, Axl plays a more predominant role in GAS6-offered cardioprotection compared with Mertk and Tyro3. 

## SIRT1 participated in the role of GAS6/Axl against myocardial HR injury
As mentioned earlier, SIRT1 was downregulated in myocardia from IR-challenged murine hearts ( G-I). Then, SIRT1 siRNA was applied to H9c2 or HL-1 cells with GAS6-OE. As shown in D-F and D-F, SIRT1 siRNA abrogated GAS6-OE-offered cardioprotection against HR injury, manifested as rises in cell death, intracellular ROS, and apoptosis. Furthermore, we found that Axl inhibition or SIRT1 inhibition significantly reduced expression of Axl and SIRT1 with little effect on GAS6 ( G). Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays revealed a direct interaction between SIRT1 and Axl on H9c2 cells with GAS6-OE ( H). Crucially, RNA-seq analysis between Control shRNA + HR and GAS6 shRNA + HR group were employed to further unveil the interplay between GAS6 and SIRT1. These data showed that |log2FoldChange| of SIRT1-SIRT7 was less than 0.5. Interestingly, among SIRTs family members, only SIRT1 was overtly down-regulated with a p value of 0.01 in the GAS6 shRNA + HR group ( A), which was verified using qRT-PCR ( B). Taken together, these results support a positive upstream regulation of GAS6/Axl signaling on SIRT1.

## Catechin, the natural regulator for activating GAS6, was screened from natural product library
Through literature and analysis from TargetMol, an organ protection drug library was constructed composed of 81 plant-derived medicinal agents with potential of activating GAS6 ( ). HL-1 cells were treated with these drugs at the concentration of 10 μM and then drugs with obvious cell toxicity were eliminated. qRT-PCR was used to identify the top 10 drugs effectively up-regulating GAS6 mRNA levels, including Catechin (CA, No.53), Hydroxy camptothecine (No.30), Isoeugenol (No.68), Ginkgolide B (No.2), Glycyrrhizin (No.36), Isoegugenyl acetate (No.69), 8-Gingerol (No.38), (−)-Epigallocatechin gallate (No.33), Eugenol (No.51), and Iithospermic acid (No.35) ( A and A). Next, a luciferase reporter vector containing GAS6 promoter was constructed to verify activation of GAS6 promoter by these 10 compounds in 293T cells ( B). Based on these results and the literature review, CA was ultimately chosen as the natural regulator for activating GAS6 to verify its efficacy in myocardial IR injury both in vitro and in vivo.
Toxicity of CA was first assessed in vitro in HL-1 cells. As shown, CA exhibited little effect on cell morphology within a concentration of 50 μM ( B). Then, 293T cells and HL-1 cells were treated with different concentrations of CA (2.5, 5, 10, 20 and 50 μM) for 3 h, and samples were collected for luciferase reporter gene assay, qRT-PCR, and Western blot analysis. CA significantly promoted activation of GAS6 promoter ( C) and up-regulated mRNA and protein levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 ( D-E).

## Catechin alleviated HR injury and H2O2 injury through GAS6/Axl pathway in HL-1 cells
HL-1 cells were treated with CA (2.5, 5, 10, 20, and 50 μM) for 3 h prior to HR injury ( D). Our data revealed that CA overtly restored HR-induced loss in cell viability, buildup in intracellular ROS, cell apoptosis, and levels of LDH in the culture medium ( A-D). Mitochondrial membrane potential detection revealed that CA treatment recovered mitochondrial membrane potential in HR-injured cells ( E). Then, we selected 5 μM CA for further study. Fluorescence staining showed that CA treatment significantly reversed the fluorescence intensity of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 following HR injury ( F). Similarly, Western blot confirmed that CA significantly increased levels of GAS6, Axl, SIRT1, Bcl2, Nrf2, NQO1, PGC-1α, TFAM, UCP2, and inhibited Bax protein in HR-injured cells ( G). These results indicate that CA significantly reduces HR injury in cardiomyocytes. Furthermore, HL-1 cells with GAS6 shRNA were treated with CA for 3 h prior to HR injury. GAS6 deficiency reversed CA-offered protection against HR injury, as shown by decreased cell viability ( A) and mitochondrial membrane potential ( E), together with increased intracellular ROS ( B), apoptotic rate ( C), and LDH levels in the culture medium ( D), as well as decreased levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 ( F-G). 
We also constructed H2O2-induced oxidative stress in HL-1 cells ( F). ## Catechin ameliorated cardiac dysfunction and myocardial structure in mice under IR injury
In order to examine the cardioprotective effects of CA in mice, mice were pretreated with 25 or 50 mg/kg CA prior to ischemic challenge (administered every two days, a total of three times, and given one more time before modeling, E). ## Discussion
The findings from our study disclosed a protective role of GAS6 in myocardial IR injury, expanding our knowledge of biological function of GAS6 beyond inflammation and hemostatic regulation in the settings of cancer and fibrosis , . Our findings highlighted that: 1) the levels of GAS6, Axl, and SIRT1 are significantly decreased in IR-damaged murine myocardium, and HR-challenged cardiomyocytes, and serum levels of GAS6 are also decreased in IR-challenged mice; 2) GAS6 overexpression mitigates IR-injured hearts and related pathophysiological alterations; 3) GAS6 deficiency aggravates HR-instigated cell injury; 4) Axl serves as a dominant molecule mediating GAS6-offered cardioprotection against I/R injury; 5) SIRT1 is a downstream modulator of GAS6/Axl axis in myocardial IR injury; 6) CA may serve as a natural regulator for activating GAS6 to protect against myocardial IR injury.
Myocardial IR injury commonly provokes the onset and progression of IHD , . Nonetheless, clinical outcome of strategies targeting IR injury remain dismal due to the complex and redundant machineries of myocardial IR injury including oxidative stress, apoptosis, and mitochondrial injury. In addition, the aforementioned culprit factors, appropriate timing of intervention is another vital factor , . Data from our current study noted that severity of pathophysiological disturbances and cardiac dysfunction peaked at 3 h following reperfusion, dictating a comprehensive intervention targeting pathological processes during this critical period.
Our current results revealed decreased GAS6 levels in IR injured mouse hearts, denoting possible clinical value of GAS6 in IHD. In particular, recombinant GAS6 displayed protective role against liver IR injury in GAS6-deficient and wild-type mice . GAS6 has also been shown to preserve against kidney IR , and acute lung injury . Along the same line, our earlier finding also noted a beneficial role of high GAS6 in brain IR injury . To discern the role of GAS6 in myocardial IR stress, overexpression or deficiency of GAS6 was utilized in murine IR models. Our data showed that GAS6 overexpression reduced cardiac injury in IR-challenged mice and HR-exposed cardiomyocytes, likely through regulation of oxidative stress (ROS and antioxidant mediators such as Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO1), preservation of mitochondrial function (increased PGC-1α, TFAM, NRF1, and UCP2) and inhibition of apoptosis (Bcl2/Bax ratio). Not surprisingly, GAS6 deficiency yielded the opposite results in the face of IR or HR challenge.
Notably, biological functions of GAS6 are mediated by the interaction with TAM receptors . Under liver IR injury, Llacuna and colleagues reported that Mertk but not Axl is more vital in mediating GAS6-offered hepatoprotective effects, manifested by elevated Mertk phosphorylation and unchanged Axl activation following IR injury . However, among TAM receptors (Mertk, Axl, and Tyro3), the most predominant protective effects of GAS6 described in various pathological processes appear to be attributed to GAS6 binding with Axl , , . Consistent with these findings, in our study, Axl plays a prominent role in myocardial IR courtesy of increased levels of Axl in AAV9-GAS6 mice. Our in vitro data revealed discrepant levels of Axl depending on the context of GAS6 expression. Furthermore, silencing of Axl (but not Tyro3 or Mertk) remarkably aggravated HR injury in cells with GAS6 overexpression. This compelling evidence further favors the likely obligatory role of Axl as the primary ligand mediating GAS6-mediated cardioprotection.
Our studies went on to denote a likely role for SIRT1 in GAS6-offered cardioprotection against myocardial IR injury. Considering the reported role of SIRT1 activation in myocardial IR injury , this prompted us to further look into the interplay between GAS6 and SIRT1. Our results revealed a critical role of SIRT1 as downstream signaling in GAS6/Axl-offered protection against myocardial IR injury. This is consistent with the observation that SIRT1 siRNA effectively reversed GAS6-OE-offered protection in cardiomyocytes challenged with HR. Co-IP assays also verified the interplay between Axl and SIRT1. Furthermore, RNA-seq revealed that only SIRT1 (but not other SIRTs such as SIRT2-SIRT7) was significantly down-regulated in GAS6 shRNA cells challenged by HR, which was verified by qRT-PCR. Thus, the likely interplay of GAS6/Axl-SIRT1 improves functional recovery and cell survival to protect against myocardial IR injury.
Natural compounds are valuable sources for drug discovery which can be developed into safe and efficacious medications , . We constructed an organ protection drug library composed of 81 plant-derived medicinal agents to identify promising reagent(s) capable of targeting GAS6 with optimal efficacy and safety. As a results, CA, a phenolic active compound extracted from a variety of plants such as green tea and black tea with various cardiovascular benefits , was finally chosen. Although earlier evidence did favor any action of CA or derivatives in myocardial IR injury , , our data provided unique benefit of CA against myocardial IR injury. Under either in vitro or in vivo setting, CA displayed benefits against myocardial IR insult, through inhibition of oxidative stress and apoptosis. These findings implicate translational and clinical utilities of CA in cardioprotection against ischemic heart diseases.
This study is not without limitations. We applied H9c2 and HL-1 cell lines but not primary cultured adult or neonatal cardiomyocytes in the in vitro experiments. Although the evidence for primary cardiomyocytes is more convincing, HL-1 and H9c2 cells are also used to study signal transduction, metabolism and transcriptional regulation, and a variety of pathological changes such as hypoxia, hyperglycemia, hyperinsulinemia, apoptosis and ischemia reperfusion in the heart , , , . These two cell lines can mutually verify the results of primary murine cardiomyocytes with great reliability and accuracy (in particular when gene transfection is involved).
Taken together, finding from our present work denoted GAS6/Axl-mediated signaling against myocardial IR injury likely through inhibition of mitochondrial injury, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. In particular, SIRT1 serves as a downstream effector for GAS6/Axl-initiated myocardial benefits. Moreover, CA screened from the natural product library exerts potent cardioprotective efficacy through activation of GAS6/Axl-SIRT1 signaling. Considering the high prevalence of myocardial IR injury, targeting GAS6 might represent an effective avenue in clinical settings of myocardial IR injury.
