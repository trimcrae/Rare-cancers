## Significance
Here, we demonstrate the pathophysiological role of TRPC3 in the development of neuropathic pain using TRPC3-knockout mice. TRPC3 deficiency suppressed pain hypersensitivity in a neuropathic pain mouse model. TRPC3 is mainly expressed in the spinal dorsal horn neurons. The knockdown of TRPC3 in spinal dorsal horn neurons alleviates neuropathic pain, whereas its activation induces acute mechanical hypersensitivity. Furthermore, mechanical hypersensitivity induced by phospholipase C, which is associated with the pathology of neuropathic pain, was suppressed in TRPC3-knockout mice. Our findings elucidate the TRPC3-mediated cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain and suggest that TRPC3 may be a therapeutic target for the effective treatment of this condition.

Neuropathic pain is a debilitating chronic condition mainly caused by peripheral nerve injury. However, the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying this condition remain unclear. Transient receptor potential canonical 3 (TRPC3), a TRP channel that is activated by downstream of the Gq-phospholipase C (PLC) axis, is expressed in the somatosensory system. Therefore, the present study investigated its pathophysiological role in neuropathic pain following peripheral nerve injury. Here, partial sciatic nerve ligation (pSNL) elicited mechanical and thermal hypersensitivity in wild-type mice, which was suppressed in TRPC3-KO mice. In situ hybridization revealed that TRPC3 is predominantly expressed in neurons in the spinal dorsal horn. Furthermore, spinal dorsal horn neuron-specific downregulation using miRNA attenuated pSNL-induced mechanical hypersensitivity. Spinal TRPC3 activation elicited acute mechanical hypersensitivity. Moreover, its genetic ablation reduced the mechanical hypersensitivity caused by spinal NK1R or PLC activation. These findings demonstrate that TRPC3 in spinal dorsal horn neurons facilitates the development of neuropathic pain. Therefore, TRPC3 may be a promising therapeutic target for neuropathic pain caused by peripheral nerve injury.

However, abnormally severe pain persists despite the absence of tissue injury in the context of chronic pain, which substantially impairs patient quality of life. Neuropathic pain is chronic pain primarily caused by peripheral nerve injury ( ). Peripheral and central sensitization play critical roles in neuropathic conditions. Peripheral sensitization occurs with the infiltration and activation of immune cells such as macrophages, neutrophils, and mast cells at the site of nerve injury ( ). In contrast, central sensitization occurs with the activation of spinal glial cells such as astrocytes ( ) and microglia ( ). These processes result in the amplification and prolongation of responses to nociceptive stimuli and the development of allodynia (where nonpainful stimuli such as tactile stimuli evoke pain) ( , ).
Transient receptor potential canonical 3 (TRPC3) is a Ca2+-permeable nonselective cation channel ( , ). It is functionally expressed in primary sensory neurons and may be involved in nociceptive responses ( , ). Additionally, this cation channel is involved in the proliferation and activation of peripheral immune cells, such as macrophages ( , ) and T cells ( ). TRPC3 expression has been observed in the spinal dorsal horn ( ), particularly in cultured primary spinal dorsal horn neurons ( ) and astrocytes ( ). Astrocytic TRPC3 mediates neuroinflammatory responses both in vitro and in vivo ( , ). Furthermore, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the introns of the TRPC3 locus are associated with the development of chronic pain in humans ( ), suggesting the involvement of TRPC3 in the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain.
The Gq protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling pathway plays an essential role in pain modulation in the nociceptor ( , ) and spinal cord ( ). TRPC3 is directly activated by downstream of Gq via diacylglycerol (DAG), which is produced by activated phospholipase C (PLC) ( ). TRPC3 is involved in signaling mediated by substance P ( ), ATP, protease ( ), endothelin-1, and bradykinin ( ), which elicit pain via GPCRs. This suggests that it may be implicated in pain modulation downstream of the Gq-coupled receptors; however, its exact mechanisms remain unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the pathophysiological role of TRPC3 in neuropathic pain following peripheral nerve injury. Here, we identified spinal dorsal horn neuron-expressed TRPC3 as an essential component for the development of neuropathic pain after peripheral nerve injury. Moreover, we showed that spinal TRPC3 activation elicits mechanical hypersensitivity and that TRPC3 coordinates the Gq–PLC–DAG axis of nociceptive signaling in the spinal dorsal horn.

## Results

## TRPC3 Was Required for Pain-Related Behavior Following Partial Sciatic Nerve Ligation.
Behavioral experiments were performed using naive wild-type (WT) and TRPC3-knockout (KO) mice to investigate the role of TRPC3 in sensory function under physiological conditions. TRPC3-KO mice exhibited normal mechanical and thermal sensitivity compared with WT mice ( ). In addition, to assess the sensitivity to chemical stimuli, we performed intraplantar administration of capsaicin or allyl isothiocyanate (AITC). TRPC3 deficiency did not affect the nociceptive responses induced by intraplantar capsaicin or AITC injection (SI Appendix, Fig. S1). To determine whether TRPC3 was required for the development of neuropathic pain after peripheral nerve injury, we generated a partial sciatic nerve ligation (pSNL) model ( ), a well-established neuropathic pain model ( ). WT mice showed mechanical hypersensitivity 3 d after pSNL treatment, whereas the development of pain-like behavior was suppressed in TRPC3-KO mice ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). In addition, thermal hyperalgesia was observed in WT mice 3 d after pSNL treatment, whereas TRPC3 deficiency reduced thermal hyperalgesia ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S2B). Recent studies have revealed several key mechanisms regulating neuropathic pain differ between sexes ( ). Therefore, we investigated whether TPRC3 is necessary for pSNL-induced hyperalgesia in female mice. Similar to male mice, mechanical hypersensitivity was also reduced in female TRPC3-KO mice after pSNL treatment (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 C and D). Moreover, the genetic ablation of TRPC3 suppressed mechanical hypersensitivity in a spinal nerve transection (SNT) model ( ), another neuropathic pain model (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 E and F).
 TRPC6, a member of the TRPC channel family, conjugates with TRPC3 to form functional heterotetramers ( ). As its involvement in neuropathic pain has been suggested ( ), we investigated whether TRPC6 is necessary for the development of neuropathic pain in TRPC6-KO mice. However, genetic ablation of TRPC6 did not affect pSNL-induced mechanical or thermal hyperalgesia ( ). These results indicate that TRPC3 plays a crucial role in the development of neuropathic pain following peripheral nerve injury, whereas TRPC6 has little effect on the pathogenesis of this condition.

## TRPC3 Deficiency Did Not Affect the Production of Inflammatory Cytokines.
Given the importance of spinal glial cell activation in the development of neuropathic pain ( , ), we assessed spinal glial cell activation through immunostaining. There was no significant difference in spinal microglial activation post pSNL between WT and TRPC3-KO mice. However, the delay of microglial activation was observed in TRPC3-KO mice, i.e., in WT mice, activation of microglia was observed at day 3 after pSNL in WT mice, whereas it was observed at day 7 after pSNL in TRPC3-KO mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 A and B). Unexpectedly, no significant astrocytic activation was observed in the spinal cord after pSNL treatment (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 C and D).
We further investigated whether pSNL treatment altered TRPC3 expression, and whether TRPC3 deficiency affected the expression of key molecules involved in pain development using quantitative RT-PCR. TRPC3 expression in the spinal dorsal horn (SI Appendix, Fig. S4A) did not increase after the pSNL treatment (SI Appendix, Fig. S4B). However, TRPC3 expression tended to be decreased (SI Appendix, Fig. S4G) in the L4 dorsal root ganglion (DRG) (SI Appendix, Fig. S4F). TRPC3 deficiency did not significantly affect the upregulation of the expression of the nerve injury marker ATF3 ( ) in the DRG (SI Appendix, Fig. S4H) or that of the key inflammatory cytokines TNFα, IL1β, and IL6 in the spinal cord and DRG (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 C–E and I–K) post pSNL treatment. These findings suggest that TRPC3 is not involved in neuroinflammatory responses in the DRG or spinal cord after peripheral nerve injury.

## TRPC3 in Peripheral Immune Cells Was Not Involved in pSNL-Induced Neuropathic Pain.
TRPC3 is expressed in both the peripheral and central somatosensory systems, as well as in peripheral immune cells ( ). Thus, we investigated whether its expression in peripheral immune cells contributes to the development of neuropathic pain using bone marrow (BM) chimeric mice. Flow cytometric analysis showed that >90% of blood cells derived from BM chimeric mice were replaced by donor mouse-derived GFP+ cells ( ). BM chimeric mice lacking TRPC3 only in the peripheral immune cells ( ; WTTRPC3-KO-BM) exhibited mechanical hypersensitivity similar to that of control mice (WTWT-BM) ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S5A). Conversely, BM chimeric mice expressing TRPC3 only in peripheral immune cells ( ; TRPC3-KOWT-BM) exhibited suppressed mechanical hypersensitivity compared with WTWT-BM mice ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S5B), suggesting that TRPC3 in the peripheral immune cells does not contribute to the development of neuropathic pain.

## TRPC3 Was Expressed in Both the Spinal Dorsal Horn and Dorsal Root Ganglia Neurons.
TRPC3/6/7 constitute a subgroup of the TRPC channel family and exhibit high-sequence homology ( ). However, the expression of this protein subgroup in the somatosensory system remains unclear. Therefore, we assessed TRPC3/6/7 expression levels in the spinal cord, DRG, and sciatic nerves using quantitative RT-PCR. TRPC3 expression was approximately four times higher in the spinal cord and two times higher in the DRG than that of TRPC6 ( ). In contrast, TRPC3 expression in the sciatic nerve was less than half of that of TRPC6 ( ). No significant difference was observed in the expression levels of TRPC6/7 in the spinal cord, DRG, and sciatic nerves between WT and TRPC3-KO mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S6). Considering that TRPC3-KO mice but not TRPC6-KO mice exhibited pain suppression in the pSNL model ( ), we hypothesized that TRPC3 functions in the spinal cord or DRG and contributes to the development of neuropathic pain. TRPC3 is expressed in nonpeptidergic nociceptors, but not in peptidergic fibers, myelinated fibers, or satellite glia in primary sensory neurons ( ). However, the types of cells that express TRPC3 in the spinal cord in vivo remain unclear.
 Aδ and C fibers convey noxious mechanical and thermal stimuli to the superficial layers (laminae I and II) of the spinal dorsal horn, and C, Aδ, and Aβ fibers convey innocuous mechanical stimuli to the deeper layers (laminae II–V) ( ). To investigate TRPC3 expression in the spinal dorsal horn, in situ hybridization using RNAscope was performed on spinal cord sections ( ). Trpc3 mRNA was abundantly expressed throughout spinal dorsal horn ( ). Dual in situ hybridization-immunohistochemistry staining revealed high colocalization of Trpc3 mRNA with the neuronal marker NeuN ( ) but little colocalization with the astrocyte marker GFAP ( ) in both of the superficial and deeper layers of the spinal dorsal horn. In L4 DRG, Trpc3 mRNA was primarily expressed in NeuN+ small-diameter neurons (SI Appendix, Fig. S7A), consistent with the previous report ( ). Although TRPC3 is reported to be expressed in neurons and astrocytes, whether microglia also express this cation channel remains controversial ( , ). In the spinal dorsal horn, limited Trpc3 mRNA expression was observed in TMEM119+ microglia (SI Appendix, Fig. S7B). These results indicate that both primary sensory and spinal dorsal horn neurons mainly express TRPC3 in the somatosensory system.

## Spinal Dorsal Horn Neuron-Specific TRPC3 Knockdown Ameliorated Neuropathic Pain.
Tissue- or cell-type-specific TRPC3 knockdown was performed using an adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector and miRNA to determine whether TRPC3 expressed in the somatosensory system is involved in the development of neuropathic pain. In vitro screening of miRNAs for their ability to reduce Trpc3 mRNA levels in Lenti-X 293 T cells overexpressing TRPC3 showed that miRNA #2 downregulated Trpc3 mRNA expression more effectively, with a decrease of approximately 80% observed ( ). Therefore, miRNA #2 was used for the in vivo experiments ( ).
 Spinal TRPC3 knockdown was performed through the intraspinal injection of AAVDJ-CMV-EmGFP-miRNA to determine whether spinal TRPC3 is involved in the development of neuropathic pain ( ). EmGFP fluorescence was observed in the spinal dorsal horn ( ). Spinal TRPC3 knockdown mice showed suppressed mechanical hypersensitivity after pSNL treatment compared to control mice ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S8A). As TRPC3 is also expressed in the DRG, including primary nonpeptidergic nociceptors ( ), it was knocked down through the intrasciatic injection of AAV6 in primary sensory neurons. Similarly, primary sensory neuronal TRPC3 knockdown mice were generated through the intrasciatic injection of AAV6-hSyn-mCherry-miRNA ( ). Fluorescence of mCherry was observed in the NeuN+ DRG neurons ( ), and approximately 40% of NeuN+ DRG neurons possessed mCherry fluorescence (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 B and C). Contrary to spinal TRPC3 knockdown mice, post-pSNL mechanical pain was not suppressed in primary sensory neuronal TRPC3 knockdown mice ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S8D). These results suggest that TRPC3 is required for neuropathic pain development in the spinal cord, but not in primary sensory neurons.
As TRPC3 is mainly expressed in spinal dorsal horn neurons ( ), we explored its potential role in the development of neuropathic pain in these neurons. TRPC3 in spinal dorsal horn neurons was knocked down via the intraspinal injection of AAV-expressing miRNA and mCherry under the control of the hSyn promoter ( ). Here, many of NeuN+ spinal dorsal horn neurons possessed mCherry fluorescence ( ). Moreover, spinal dorsal horn neuron-specific TRPC3 knockdown suppressed mechanical hypersensitivity post pSNL treatment ( and SI Appendix, Fig. S8E). To investigate the effects of gene delivery to nonneuronal cells, we injected AAV-CMV-DIO-EmGFP-miRNA into the spinal dorsal horn of Gfapcre mice to knock down TRPC3 in the spinal dorsal horn astrocytes. The knockdown of spinal dorsal horn astrocytic TRPC3 did not suppress pSNL-induced mechanical hypersensitivity (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 F and G), indicating that TRPC3 in the spinal dorsal horn neurons plays a critical role in neuropathic pain development.

## Spinal Activation of TRPC3 Produced Acute Mechanical Hypersensitivity.
Given the necessity of TRPC3 in the spinal dorsal horn neurons for neuropathic pain development, we hypothesized that TRPC3 activation in the spinal cord could induce pain. To test this hypothesis, we used GSK1702934A (GSK), a TRPC3 agonist ( ). We performed intraplantar GSK administration to investigate whether TRPC3 activation in primary sensory neurons could induce pain-like behavior ( ). Intraplantar injection of GSK did not elicit acute pain-like behavior or increase mechanical sensitivity ( ), indicating that TRPC3 activation in primary sensory neurons did not induce mechanical hypersensitivity. In contrast, intrathecal ( ) GSK administration reduced the withdrawal threshold to mechanical stimuli in a dose-dependent manner and increased mechanical sensitivity ( ). The increase in mechanical sensitivity disappeared 1 d postadministration ( ). Moreover, intrathecal GSK administration did not enhance mechanical sensitivity in TRPC3-KO mice ( ), suggesting that spinal TRPC3 activation induces mechanical hypersensitivity.
 

## Spinal Phospholipase C Activation Induced Mechanical Hypersensitivity via TRPC3.
As TRPC3 is directly activated by DAG by downstream of PLC, we explored the upstream regulators of TRPC3 under pain conditions. Substance P, a pronociceptive neurotransmitter, is released from primary afferent terminals to the spinal cord. It elicits pain-like behavior, mainly through the Gq protein coupled with NK1R ( ) ( ). Therefore, we hypothesized that it is an upstream regulator of TRPC3. We subsequently evaluated the involvement of TRPC3 in substance P-induced pain-like behavior. Intrathecal administration of substance P induced acute mechanical hypersensitivity, which was suppressed in TRPC3-KO mice ( ). This indicates that this neurotransmitter induces mechanical hypersensitivity via TRPC3 in the spinal cord.
Various Gq protein signaling pathways, including substance P-NK1R-Gq, transmit pain to the spinal dorsal horn ( ). We hypothesized that TRPC3 is involved in Gq protein activation-induced mechanical hypersensitivity in the spinal cord. To test this hypothesis, we used m-3M3FBS, a PLC activator ( ) ( ). Because the effect of spinal PLC activation by m-3M3FBS on pain sensation was unclear, mechanical sensitivity following intrathecal m-3M3FBS administration was assessed. Intrathecal administration of m-3M3FBS increased mechanical sensitivity in a dose-dependent manner at 1 d postadministration (SI Appendix, Fig. S9A). This persisted for 3 d and recovered on Day 7 (SI Appendix, Fig. S9B). This m-3M3FBS-induced mechanical hypersensitivity was suppressed in the TRPC3-KO mice ( ). These findings indicate that TRPC3 is activated by downstream of PLC and induces mechanical hypersensitivity in the spinal cord.

## Discussion
The present study revealed three key findings: i) TRPC3 in spinal dorsal horn neurons is essential for the development of neuropathic pain; ii) spinal TRPC3 activation induces mechanical hypersensitivity; and iii) mechanical hypersensitivity induced by the activation of spinal NK1R or PLC is mediated by TRPC3. In the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain, central sensitization leads to an enhanced response to noxious stimuli or generation of pain responses to nonnoxious stimuli ( ). As TRPC3 is a Ca2+-permeable cation channel, its activation by downstream of Gq-coupled receptors, such as NK1R, in dorsal horn neurons may promote Ca2+ influx and enhance neuronal activity and pain sensation during neuropathic pain development (SI Appendix, Fig. S10).

## Differences in the Functions of TRPC3/6 in Neuropathic Pain.
TRPC3/6 are members of the TRPC channel family and share high sequence homology. These channels are activated by DAG and function as heterotetramers ( ). However, only the deficiency of TRPC3 suppressed neuropathic pain, whereas TRPC6 deficiency had no effect in the present study ( ). Moreover, the TRPC3 expression level was approximately four times higher than that of TRPC6 in the spinal cord ( ), indicating that TRPC3 plays a more dominant role than TRPC6 in spinal function and in the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain.
Contrary to our findings, previous studies suggest that TRPC6 is involved in neuropathic pain. For instance, increased TRPC6 expression in the DRG was observed in a chronic constriction injury model ( ), and the knockdown of spinal TRPC6 using antisense oligodeoxynucleotides alleviated pain in models of diabetic peripheral neuropathy ( ) and spared nerve injury ( ). The discrepancies between these results and our findings may be due to differences in animal species, neuropathic pain models, and inhibition methods of TRPC6. To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the role of TRPC6 in neuropathic pain using TRPC6-KO mice.

## The Involvement of TRPC3 in pSNL-Induced Spinal Microglial Activation.
Microglial activation in the spinal dorsal horn after pSNL was delayed in TRPC3-KO mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S3). Considering the limited TRPC3 expression in spinal microglia (SI Appendix, Fig. S7), upstream signaling of microglial activation could be involved in this delay. Oligodendrocytes lineage cells (OLs) are implicated in neuropathic pain; after peripheral nerve injury, spinal OLs produce interleukin-33 (IL33), which activates microglia and promotes the development of neuropathic pain ( , ). Moreover, microglia and oligodendrocytes express LPLAT9, a platelet-activating factor (PAF)-producing enzyme ( ). PAF receptor is mainly expressed in microglia in the spinal cord and is involved in neuropathic pain ( ). Since oligodendrocytes express TRPC3 in rat brain ( ), spinal OLs may express TRPC3. Therefore, OLs-derived IL33 and PAF production may change in TRPC3-KO mice, which may lead to the delay of microglial activation after pSNL and contribute to pain suppression in addition to the mechanism via spinal dorsal horn neurons. Although its contribution is relatively small, further studies are needed to elucidate whether TRPC3 is expressed in spinal OLs and whether TRPC3 deficiency affects the production of IL33 and PAF from OLs and contributes to neuropathic pain via microglial activation.

## TRPC3 Expressing Cells in the Somatosensory System.
TRPC3 is functionally expressed in primary sensory neurons. In cultured primary DRG neurons, TRPC3 is activated via the FcγRI complex ( ), P2Y2, PAR2 ( ), ETA/B, and B1/2 receptors ( ), which mainly activate PLC signaling. In addition, TRPC3 is expressed in nonpeptidergic unmyelinated fibers in the DRG ( ). Consistent with this report, Trpc3 mRNA was primarily expressed in NeuN+ small-diameter neurons in L4 DRG. However, our findings revealed that TRPC3 in primary sensory neurons is not required for the development of neuropathic pain. TRPC3 is expressed in MrgprA3+ primary sensory neurons, a subset of itch sensory neurons ( ). Moreover, pruritogens elicit itching by exciting primary afferents, mainly via Gq protein activation ( ). These findings suggest that TRPC3 expression in primary sensory neurons may contribute to itch signaling rather than pain.
The activation of TRPC3 via intraplantar GSK administration did not elicit pain-like behavior in primary sensory neurons ( ). However, Liu et al. ( ) showed that the subcutaneous injection of GSK into the cheeks of mice elicited pain-like wiping behavior in the forelimbs. This discrepancy in results may be attributed to differences in the site of GSK administration. The plantar is innervated by lumbar DRGs, whereas the cheek is innervated by the trigeminal ganglia (TG). Thus, the expression pattern of TRPC3 may differ between DRG and TG neurons. Additionally, as the intracellular localization of TRPC3 in primary sensory nerves remains unclear, it is also possible that TRPC3 is not functionally expressed in the epidermal free nerve endings of DRG neurons; therefore, intraplantar GSK administration cannot stimulate primary sensory neurons. Thus, the exact patterns of TRPC3 expression in DRG neurons warrant further elucidation.
In vivo expression of TRPC3 in the spinal cord remains largely understudied. Previous studies reported TRPC3 expression in cultured primary spinal neurons ( ) and astrocytes ( ). In the present study, Trpc3 mRNA was expressed in neurons but not in astrocytes or microglia in the spinal dorsal horn of naïve mice. Additionally, TRPC3 is expressed in the cerebral cortex neurons under physiological conditions and rarely in astrocytes ( ). Moreover, TRPC3 played a crucial role in the development of neuropathic pain in spinal dorsal horn neurons but not in astrocytes. These findings suggest that in vivo spinal astrocytic TRPC3 expression is maintained at low levels and has no effect on pain modulation.

## Upstream Molecules of TRPC3.
TRPC3 is activated in conjunction with PLC and interacts with various Gq-coupled GPCRs via the Gq–PLC–DAG signaling axis. Given the involvement of the PLC in the development of neuropathic pain ( ), TRPC3 may contribute to the PLC activation-dependent mechanisms underlying neuropathic pain. Substance P, a well-known pain mediator, induces pain primarily through Gq signaling via NK1R ( ). Moreover, it is substantially involved in the pathology of neuropathic pain. Increased substance P levels were observed in the spinal cord of a rat neuropathic pain model ( ). In large-diameter myelinated primary afferents, which are generally glutamatergic and transmit innocuous touch stimuli, substance P is expressed after spinal nerve ligation ( ). In our study, intrathecal substance P injection-induced mechanical hypersensitivity was suppressed in TRPC3-KO mice. This implies that TRPC3 in spinal dorsal horn neurons is activated by downstream of substance P-NK1R and facilitates Ca2+ influx, leading to the increased activity of spinal dorsal horn neurons in neuropathic pain.
TRPC3 may also be involved in pain generation through other Gq-coupled receptors expressed in spinal dorsal horn neurons, such as Group I mGluRs (mGluR1/5). mGluR1/5 are involved in neuropathic pain development ( , ). Although a previous report suggested that TRPC3 does not contribute to Ca2+ influx downstream of mGluR1/5 in spinal neurons ( ), this study used siRNA for TRPC3 knockdown with an efficiency of approximately 30%, which may have been insufficient to precisely observe TRPC3-mediated Ca2+ influx. We intrathecally injected m-3M3FBS, a PLC activator, and demonstrated the involvement of TRPC3 in PLC activation-mediated mechanical hypersensitivity. This suggests that TRPC3 may function downstream of the Gq–PLC–DAG signaling axis and contribute to the development of neuropathic pain. However, further studies are needed to elucidate the downstream molecules of TRPC3 and the molecular mechanisms of TRPC3-induced activation of spinal dorsal horn neurons.

## Expression Levels of TRPC3 Under Neuropathic Pain.
In this study, TRPC3 expression levels did not change significantly in the spinal dorsal horn after pSNL treatment. However, an increase in upstream molecules of TRPC3 may lead to neuropathic pain. For example, the expression levels of NK1R ( ) and B1/2 ( ) increase in the spinal dorsal horn after pSNL treatment. This suggests that the Gq–PLC–DAG signaling cascade mediated by Gq-coupled receptors, such as NK1R and B1/2, is enhanced under neuropathic pain, which promotes the activation of TRPC3, potentially leading to increased excitability of spinal dorsal horn neurons.
Aoki et al. ( ) showed an association between the rs11726196 SNP in TRPC3 and chronic pain. This SNP is located in the TRPC3 intron and may be involved in transcriptional regulation. TRPC3 mRNA expression depends on the rs11726196 genotype (TT > GG) in most peripheral tissues, including those of the peripheral nervous system. Although it is not clear whether this SNP affects TRPC3 expression levels in the lumbar dorsal horn, it is possible that the TT genotype also increases TRPC3 expression in this tissue. This may enhance neuronal activity in dorsal horn neurons and contribute to the development of neuropathic pain. Further studies are required to determine whether this SNP affects TRPC3 expression in the lumbar dorsal horn and whether chronic pain is associated with this change in TRPC3 expression.
In summary, our findings reveal that TRPC3 expressed in spinal dorsal horn neurons acts as a key modulator of the development of neuropathic pain following peripheral nerve injury. These findings provide insights into the mechanisms underlying the development of neuropathic pain and identify TRPC3 as a therapeutic target for the treatment of long-lasting neuropathic pain.

## Materials and Methods
SI Appendix includes detailed methods.

## Animals.
All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines of the Kyoto University Animal Experimentation Committee and Japanese Pharmacological Society. All animal use and study protocols were approved by the Kyoto University Animal Experimentation Committee (approval numbers: 19 to 38, 19 to 39). All mice (8 to 12 wk of age, male; 20 to 30 g, female; 18 to 22 g) were housed at a constant ambient temperature of 22 ± 2 °C under a 12 h light/dark cycle and allowed water ad libitum. The number of used animals used was empirically determined and was similar to that used in previous reports ( ).

## Behavioral Tests.
Mechanical sensitivity was assessed by measuring the paw withdrawal threshold using calibrated von Frey filaments as described previously, with slight modifications ( , ). The thermal sensitivity of the paw was assessed using a Hargreaves radiant heat apparatus (IITC Life Science) as described previously, with slight modifications ( , ). The responses to noxious heat stimuli were assessed using a hotplate algesimeter (Ugo Basile, Gemonio, Italy).

## pSNL Model.
Surgery was performed as previously described, with slight modifications ( , ). Mice were anesthetized with a cocktail of three different anesthetic agents (0.3 mg/kg medetomidine, Nippon Zenyaku Kogyo; 4.0 mg/kg midazolam, Fuji Pharma; and 5.0 mg/kg butorphanol, Meiji Seika Pharma). One-third to half of the diameter of the right sciatic nerve at the upper thigh level was ligated tightly with a 9-0 silk suture.

## Generation of BM Chimeric Mice.
BM transplantation was performed as previously described ( ), with slight modifications. After irradiation, WT or TRPC3-KO recipient mice received an intravenous injection of 3.0 × 106 BM cells derived from GFP+ WT or TRPC3-KO donor mice.

## In Situ Hybridization (RNAscope).
RNAscope analysis was performed as previously described ( ), with slight modifications. Mice were killed by decapitation. The lumbar spinal cord and the L4 DRG were isolated and cryosectioned at a thickness of 20-μm. RNAscope was performed using the RNAscope Multiplex Fluorescent Reagent Kit v2 (Cat# 323110; Advanced Cell Diagnostics) and Probe-Mm-Trpc3-O3 (Cat# 572151; Advanced Cell Diagnostics) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## AAV Injection.
Intraspinal injections were performed as previously described ( ) with slight modifications. Under anesthesia, a glass micropipette backfilled with AAVDJ solution was inserted into the spinal dorsal horn, and 500 nL of the solution was microinjected using a custom-made pump.
Intrasciatic injection was performed as previously described ( ), with slight modifications. Under anesthesia, a glass micropipette backfilled with AAV6 solution was inserted into the sciatic nerve, and 1 μL of the solution was microinjected using a custom-made pump.

## Intrathecal GSK1702934A Administration.
Mice were lightly anesthetized with 1.5% inhaled isoflurane and given an intrathecal injection (5 to 50 pmol/5 μL, L5–L6) of GSK1702934A (Focus Biomolecules). Control groups were administered a vehicle (saline containing 10% DMSO and 1% Tween80). No spontaneous response, such as scratching, was observed after the injection of GSK1702934A (50 pmol). Mechanical sensitivity was assessed 1 h after injection, and the results were used as Day 0 results.

## Experimental Design and Statistical Analysis.
Statistical analyses were performed using Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA). Briefly, Welch’s t test was used for comparisons between single experimental and control groups. One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test or two-way ANOVA or two-way RM ANOVA with Bonferroni’s post hoc test was used for comparisons between multiple experimental groups; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data are presented as the mean ± SEM. The assessor was blinded to the treatment conditions.
