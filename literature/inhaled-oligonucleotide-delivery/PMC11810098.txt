The disease occurs primarily in elderly population, especially 50–70 years old. The incidence of IPF tends to increase with age [ ]. Clinical course of IPF is somewhat unpredictable and variable, but the median survival time after diagnosis is 3–5 years if untreated [ ]. Novel anti-fibrotic medications, such as pirfenidone and nintedanib, have given new hope to patients with IPF, but it remains a debilitating and fatal disease and IPF still has no cure [ ]. Therefore, the in-depth exploration of mechanisms and investigating diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets of lung fibrosis are extremely important.
Despite the comprehensive understanding of the exact pathophysiological mechanisms underlying IPF remains unclear, studies in the past decade have reached significant progress. The development of the fibrotic process is associated with alterations in many aspects of cellular and molecular physiology, including genetics, epigenetics, aging, epithelial cells damage, fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, cell-signaling pathways, extracellular matrix deposition, apoptosis, inflammation and immunity, metabolism, and autophagy [ ]. In the development of IPF, environmental exposure, aging, and genetic susceptibility may have a cooperative effect. Increasing evidence demonstrated a vital role for epigenetic changes in IPF [ ]. Epigenetic alteration can modify gene expression in the absence of revision in DNA sequence. The main epigenetic alterations include DNA methylation, RNA-based mechanisms, and histone modifications. With the development of sequencing technology, the various cells phenotype and molecular mechanisms related to epigenetics in fibrosis have become clearer [ ]. However, the m6A in the pathological process of IPF is largely unexplored.
N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the pervasive modification in eukaryotic RNAs, which is the central regulator of mRNA metabolism, including transcription, maturation, processing, translation, degradation, and stability [ ]. The m6A modification participates in many biological processes by affecting the expression of target genes [ ]. The reversible dynamic regulation of m6A modification includes methylation, demethylation, and recognition. M6A is catalyzed by methyltransferase writers, removed by m6A demethylase erasers, and recognized by reader proteins. Studies have shown that the dysregulation of m6A can influence many diseases, such as brain diseases and cancer [ ]. Several studies found that m6A in mRNA was associated with the fibrotic process [ , ]. The biological function of m6A in IPF needs to be illuminated.
In our present study, we delineated high‑throughput sequencing to profile the alterations of m6A modification and mRNA expression in peripheral blood of IPF patients. The expressions of identified m6A regulator and target gene were validated using MeRIP-qPCR and real-time PCR. Moreover, we established an animal model and a senescent model of A549 cells to explore the associated molecular mechanisms.

## Materials and methods

## Sample collection and ethics statement
Twenty-four healthy controls and 24 IPF patients were selected (both female: male, 7:17; age, 69 ± 9.4 vs. 67 ± 7.8 y ears; p > 0.05). IPF was diagnosed according to published criteria established by ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT [ ]. All human samples used in the present study were collected from First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University from 2022 to 2023. The 3 ml venous blood was harvested and stored at ‑80°C. On the basis of consistent background including gender and age, four healthy controls and four IPF patients were selected for high‑throughput sequencing. The remaining 20 healthy individuals and 20 IPF patients were used for further verification experiments. The present clinical study was approved by the Ethics Committee of First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (NO.KYLL-2023-028).

## MeRIP-seq and data analysis
The isolation of total RNA was performed with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and RNA concentration was assessed. Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation was conducted using the GenSeq® m6A MeRIP Kit (GenSeq Inc.). First, the m6A antibody was coupled to protein A/G magnetic beads, and RNA was randomly fragmented to 200nt. Second, the fragmented RNAs were incubated with bead-linked antibodies for 4 h at 4°C, and immunoprecipitated RNA fragments were eluted from the RNA/antibody complexes. Third, RNA libraries were prepared, and the libraries quality was verified with Agilent 2100 bioanalyzer. Finally, the libraries were sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq platform. After removing low-quality reads, the remaining clean reads were then aligned to the reference genome by STAR and Hisat2 software (v2.0.4). The methylated sites (peaks) and differentially m6A peaks were identified by MACS software and diffReps. GO and KEGG analyses were performed for functional analysis.

## RNA-seq and data analysis
The rRNA-depleted samples were used to prepare RNA libraries. After controlling the quality and quantity, libraries were sequenced with the Illumina Novaseq instrument. Cutadapt software (v1.9.3), hisat2 software (v2.0.4), and HTSeq software (v0.9.1) were used for further analysis.

## Real-time PCR and MeRIP-qPCR
The cDNA synthesis was made using the PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix (Takara, RR036A) and the expression levels of mRNAs were performed using a TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Takara, RR820A). The reaction conditions were maintained as follows: 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 20 s. Data were analyzed by 2−ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH as an endogenous reference gene. MeRIP-qPCR consisted of two parts: MeRIP and qRT-PCR. The experimental procedure was conducted as described previously. The modification differences were identified by %(IP/Input). lists the primer sequences. 

## Animal model
Sixteen healthy male C57BL/6 mice (aged 7–8 months) were randomly assigned to two groups: a control group (group C) and a pulmonary fibrosis group (group M), with eight mice in each group. The pulmonary models were prepared via intratracheal injection of bleomycin (BLM, 5 mg/kg) on the initial day, while the control groups were injected with saline. Mice were killed on days 28, and the lung tissues were collected for analysis. HE and Masson staining, and hydroxyproline content determination (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Company), was used to measure the degree of pulmonary fibrosis. The mRNA expressions of METTL14 and DDIT4 were determined by qRT-PCR. The m6A level of DDIT4 was measured by MeRIP-qPCR, as described previously. lists the primer sequences. The animal study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanxi Medical University (SYDL2023027).

## Cell culture and establishment of aging model
A549 cells were cultured in DMED (Wuhan Pricella Biotechnology) mixed with 1% penicillin-streptomycin and 10% FBS (ExCell Bio) at 37°C with 5% CO2. In this study, A549 cells were assigned to two groups: A549 7d group, and TGF-β1 7d group. A549 7d group was incubated in DMED supplemented with 5% FBS, and TGF-β1 7d group was treated with TGF-β1 (Sino Biological, 10 ng/ml) for 7 days. β-Galactosidase staining (Beijing Solarbio Science) was used to detect senescent cells. Finally, cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

## Real-time PCR and western blotting
The amount of METTL14 and DDIT4 was determined by qRT-PCR and Western blotting. Total RNA was extracted from lung tissues or A549 cells. The cDNA synthesis was made using M5 Super plus qPCR RT kit with gDNA remover (Mei5 Biotechnology Co.), and the expression levels of mRNAs were performed using 2×M5 hiPer Realtime PCR Super mix with Low Rox (Mei5 Biotechnology Co.). β‑Actin was used as an endogenous control. lists the primer sequences.
After extracting total protein from A549 cells, the concentration was calculated by the BCA protein assay reagent (Wuhan BOSTER Company). Total protein for each sample (30 µg) was separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred into a PVDF membrane. After blocking for 2 h, the PVDF membrane was incubated with anti-METTL14 (1:500; BOSTER), anti-DDIT4 (1:500; BOSTER), or a 1:10000 dilution of anti-β-actin (Wuhan ABclonal) at 4°C overnight. The membrane was then incubated with a secondary HRP-conjugated antibody (1:5000; BOSTER) for 2 h. The protein bands were visualized by SuperECL Plus (Dalian Bergolin Biotechnology) and calculated with ImageJ.

## Cell transfection with siRNA
The siRNA was transfected with Lipo3000 Transfection Reagent (Mei5 Biotechnology Co.) to interfere with METTL14. A549 cells were randomly divided into a normal group (transfected by nonsense sequence siRNA) and a positive siRNA group (transfected by siRNA). After 72-h intervention, the content of METTL14 and DDIT4 mRNA were measured by qRT-PCR, and the m6A level of DDIT4 were determined by MeRIP-qPCR. The experimental procedure was conducted as described previously.

## mRNA stability analysis
In normal A549 cells and METTL14 knockdown cells, transcriptional inhibitor actinomycin D (MedChemExpress, 5 μg/ml) was used to detect differences in the half-life of DDIT4 mRNA. After incubation with actinomycin D for 0 h, 0.5 h, 1 h, and 1.5 h, the cells were harvested for RNA extraction. The content of DDIT4 mRNA was determined by qRT-PCR.

## Statistical analysis
The results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. GraphPad Prism software (v 8.0.2) was utilized to analyze the differences between the two groups. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Overall features of MeRIP-seq results
We mapped the whole transcriptome-wide modifications of m6A using MeRIP-Seq. According to the results, 69,868 m6A modified peaks were found in the IPF group, and 67,483 peaks were observed in the healthy control ( ). The differences and overlaps in m6A modified peaks are demonstrated in . Among these, 36,951 m6A peaks were generally discovered in both groups. These results elucidated an obvious difference in m6A modification patterns between the two groups. Next, the abundance of m6A peaks between IPF and control group was analyzed with the norm of Fold change >2 and p < 0.00001. A total of 3756 peaks were significantly hypermethylated, which were related to 3087 genes, and 4712 were significantly hypomethylated among 3769 transcripts of genes ( ). The information of top 20 differentially modified peaks is depicted in .
 
The MetaPlotR [ ] was utilized to reveal the distribution patterns of peaks in mRNA gene locations. The result showed that peaks were mostly enriched in the coding DNA sequence (CDS) regions, stop codons, and start codons ( ). In order to search for consensus motifs deposited among the m6A peaks, the m6A methylomes of IPF and control groups were mapped using DREME software [ ]. The results identified that m6A mainly existed in the classical RRACH (R=A or G; H=U, A, or C) consensus sequence, which was in accord with the previous studies. The top five conserved motifs in the two groups are shown in . These results strengthened the credibility of the identified m6A peaks in IPF.

## Bioinformatics analysis of the identified differentiated m6A genes
With GO analysis, the m6A genes were divided into three parts of biological information: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC). Pathway analysis revealed that DEGs were mainly related to Wnt signaling pathway, cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, and ECM–receptor interaction ( ).

## Validation of differential expression of METTL14 and DDIT4
As depicted in , the expression of METTL14 was significantly decreased in IPF group (p < 0.0001). To explore the functional role of METTL14 in IPF, we selected DDIT4 for further study. According to the high‑throughput sequencing results, the m6A level of DDIT4 was decreased, but the mRNA level of DDIT4 was increased in IPF. The specific m6A modification site of DDIT4 was revealed by MeRIP-seq (chrom: chr10; txStart: 74034452; txEnd: 74035797; PeakID: MACS_peak_51941). ## Establishment of pulmonary fibrosis model
A well-alveolized normal histology was observed, and no morphological changes were observed in group C. Bleomycin instillation into mouse lung led to the destruction of normal lung architecture, the extensive proliferation of fibroblasts, and the significant deposition of fibrillar collagen ( ). Through HE, Masson staining, and hydroxyproline content determination, we found the degree of fibrosis was evident in the M group ( ). The mRNA expression of METTL14 was downregulated, and DDIT4 was upregulated in the M group (P<0.05) ( ). The m6A level of DDIT4 was downregulated in the model group (P<0.05) ( ).
 

## Establishing the senescent model of A549 cells
Using an inverted phase-contrast microscope, we observed that TGF-β1 intervention gradually transformed A549 cells from original polygonal shape to long spindle shape ( -1 and 2). The number of senescent cells was significantly increased in TGF-β1 7d group (P<0.05) ( -3,4 ,b). The mRNA expression of METTL14 was downregulated, and DDIT4 was upregulated in TGF-β1 7d group (P<0.05) ( ). Compared with A549 7d group, the protein level of METTL14 was decreased, and DDIT4 was increased in TGF-β1 7d group (P<0.05) ( ).
 

## METTL14 promotes DDIT4 expression by reducing m6A level of DDIT4 mRNA
The mRNA expression of METTL14 was significantly downregulated, and DDIT4 was significantly upregulated after the knockdown of METTL14 (P<0.05) ( ). The m6A level of DDIT4 was downregulated after siRNA interference with METTL14 (P<0.05) ( ). The knockdown of METTL14 prolongs the half-life of DDIT4 mRNA, thereby promoting DDIT4 mRNA stability ( ).
 

## Discussion
IPF usually occurs in elderly population. The incidence of IPF increases drastically with age [ , ]. The disease is characterized by a massive accumulation of ECM, distortion of normal lung architecture, and senescence of alveolar epithelial cells [ ]. The high morbidity and mortality of IPF impose a growing economic burden on families, communities, and global health care [ ]. While the recent novel antifibrotic drugs, such as nintedanib and pirfenidone, have provided treatment options for the patients with IPF, there is still no cure for the disease [ ]. Thus, the exploration of precise therapeutic targets for IPF is needed.
Although the mechanistic understanding of IPF is incomplete, recent advances have delineated significant alterations that lead to the development of pulmonary fibrosis. These alterations include repetitive damage to the alveolar epithelium, epithelial cell senescence, pro-fibrotic epigenetic reprogramming, excessive secretion of pro-fibrotic mediators, and ongoing proliferation of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts [ ]. Epigenetic alteration can alter gene expression in the absence of revision in DNA sequence. Multiple studies have demonstrated that epigenetic changes are increasingly recognized as a crucial factor in PF [ , ]. Epigenetic alterations include DNA methylation, RNA-based mechanisms, and histone modifications. Among them, the methylation modification of RNA and DNA is extremely vital epigenetic alterations [ ]. However, understanding the epigenetic regulation of pulmonary fibrosis is not straightforward.
M6A is a pervasive modification in eukaryotic RNAs, which is regulated by methyltransferase (writers) and demethylase (erasers). Most m6A sites occur at conserved motif RRACH (R=A or G; H=U, A, or C) [ , ]. Methyltransferase complexes consist of METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, METTL16, RBM15B, RBM15, VIRMA (also named KIAA1429), and ZC3H13 [ , ]. Among them, METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP are the catalytic central components. The reversible process of m6A is removed by demethylases including FTO and ALKBH5 [ ]. The m6A methylation is recognized by different m6A-binding proteins (readers), including YTHDF1/2/3, HNRNPC/G, HNRNPA2B1, YTHDC1/2, eIF3, and IGF2BP1/2/3 [ ]. Different m6A readers can carry out different biological functions. The m6A modifications are related to almost all mRNA metabolism, including transcription, processing, maturation, translation, degradation, and stability, which is critical for a variety of biological and pathological processes [ , ].
Numerous studies have shown that m6A is related to many diseases, including cardiometabolic diseases [ ], ischemic stroke [ ], neurological diseases [ ], steatotic liver disease [ ], retinal diseases [ ], and various types of cancer [ ]. There is evidence indicating that m6A methylation plays a crucial role in lung diseases [ ]. Several studies demonstrated the m6A methylation was associated with fibrotic process such as liver fibrosis [ ], renal fibrosis [ ], cardiac fibrosis [ ], and pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. M6A regulators and m6A-related genes are vital to the occurrence, progression, and prognosis of IPF [ , ]. In arsenite-related idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, H3K18 lactylation can promote the progression via YTHDF1/m6A/NREP [ ]. Research showed that m6A regulated lung fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition in pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Han et al. confirmed that m6A modification blocked miRNA-126 maturation, which activated the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway and caused fibrosis in lung tissue [ ]. However, the m6A methylation in IPF is largely unexplored. In the present study, we aimed to profile the alterations of m6A methylation in IPF.
In this study, we chose venous blood from IPF patients and healthy controls for high‑throughput sequencing. We obtained a panorama of m6A methylation in IPF using MeRIP-Seq. The IPF group showed increased peaks (3756) and decreased peaks (4712) ( ). The m6A methylation in different regions of mRNA often indicates different functions such as output, splicing, translation, degradation, and stability [ ]. As shown in the , m6A peaks were mostly enriched in the CDS regions, stop codons, and start codons, which is in accord with the previous report [ ]. Our current study also identified that m6A mainly exists in the classical RRACH consensus sequence. Through bioinformatics analysis, differential m6A genes were significantly related to cellular process, RNA metabolic, cell junction, and extracellular matrix structural constituents ( ). These genes were related to the ErbB signaling pathway, adherens junction, ECM–receptor interaction, and focal adhesion ( ). Therefore, the current evidence strongly implies that m6A methylation may regulate the expression of IPF-related genes. Using RNA-seq, 1518 differentially expressed genes were related to cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, Wnt signaling pathway, and ECM–receptor interaction ( ). Further associated analyses identified 382 hypermethylated peaks and 367 hypomethylated peaks in DEGs ( ).
When looking into the expressions of m6A related regulators in IPF, METTL14 was identified, and the downregulation of METTL14 might induce alterations of m6A methylation in IPF ( ). We used the bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis as a model system for further validation. Through HE, Masson staining, and hydroxyproline content determination, we found the degree of fibrosis was evident in the M group ( ). The mRNA expression of METTL14 was downregulated in M group ( ). TGF-β1 is a key regulator of IPF and an important profibrotic factor [ ]. TGF‑β1 is able to induce premature senescence in A549 cells [ ]. Our present study also identified that TGF-β1 intervention gradually transformed A549 cells from original polygonal shape to long spindle shape and the number of senescent cells increased significantly after treatment with TGF‑β1 for 7 days ( ). The expression of METTL14 was decreased in TGF-β1 7d group ( ). These results indicated that METTL14 was related to cell senescence and pulmonary fibrosis. In accord with our results, a previous study reported that knocking down METTL14 exacerbated liver fibrosis [ ]. Overexpression of METTL14 might inhibit replicative senescence and premature aging [ ].
To investigate the role of METTL14 in IPF, we selected hypo-up and hypo-down genes for bioinformatics analysis. These 367 genes were involved in collagen fibril organization, transforming growth factor beta activation, cell-matrix adhesion, secretory vesicle, and transmembrane transporter activity through GO analysis ( ). Further, pathway analysis depicted that these genes were connected with TNF, PI3K-Akt, and T cell receptor signaling pathways ( ).
Notably, we selected our interested genes including four hypo-down genes and six hypo-up genes in . Among them, ADRB2 activation prevented fibrosis [ ]. ZNRF3, RBX1, SRXN1, ANXA2, and CIRBP participated in fibrosis or played a vital role in the epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) [ , ]. GAS6 may play its role in fibrosis by macrophages accumulation and EMT [ ]. MMP9 was involved in EMT and fibroblast migration [ ]. FN1 was important component of the extracellular matrix (ECM). DDIT4 promoted cell senescence and fibrosis.
For these 367 genes, we comprehensively evaluated each gene through literature reading and IPF-related gene retrieval, and ultimately identified DDIT4, which is associated with senescence and fibrosis. DNA Damage-Inducible Transcript 4 (DDIT4), also named as Redd1, is induced by various stresses and has been studied in many diseases such as metabolic disorders, cancer, and autoimmune diseases [ ]. A previous study observed that DDIT4 was contained in 42% of fibrosis-related gene sets, which were annotated in the MSigDB database [ ]. It has been reported that REDD1/autophagy pathway could promote fibrosis in human SLE through NETs decorated with TF and IL-17A [ ]. Others have shown that levels of cardiac fibrosis, MMP-2, and REDD1 were upregulated in high-fat diet mice [ ]. Previous results indicated Redd1 promoted cardiac senescence with NF-kB signaling [ ]. In summary, DDIT4 is involved in autophagy and cell senescence, which are closely related to pulmonary fibrosis. A recent study has demonstrated that the overexpression of FTO reduced m6A levels of DDIT4, and then m6A reader YTHDF2 could not recognize the DDIT4 mRNA with lower m6A marks, thereby impeding the decay of DDIT4 mRNA [ ]. It has also been found that the m6A level of DDIT4 mRNA is closely related to its mRNA stability in prostate cancer cells and head-neck squamous cell carcinoma [ , ].
According to the qRT-PCR and MeRIP-qPCR ( ) results, our study also suggested that although the m6A level of DDIT4 was decreased, the mRNA level of DDIT4 was increased in IPF. It was further verified in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis ( ). At the cellular level, it was further confirmed that the amount of DDIT4 was increased in TGF-β1 7d group ( ). The content of DDIT4 mRNA was significantly upregulated, and the m6A level of DDIT4 was downregulated after siRNA interference with METTL14 ( ). The knockdown of METTL14 prolongs the half-life of DDIT4 mRNA, thereby promoting DDIT4 mRNA stability ( ). We speculate that the downregulation of METTL14 might inhibit the decay of DDIT4 mRNA by reducing the m6A modification level of DDIT4 mRNA, leading to high expression of DDIT4 mRNA and protein.
Taken together, we obtained a panorama of m6A methylation in IPF and identified 6856 differential m6A genes. The following joint analysis showed that there were 749 m6A peaks in DEGs. Moreover, we found that METTL14 might induce alterations of m6A methylation in IPF, and the downregulation of METTL14 might reduce m6A level of DDIT4 and then promote the mRNA level of DDIT4. Our study might provide a valuable reference for future investigations aimed to explore the diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets regarding m6A modifications in aging-related IPF.

## Limitation
However, there are some limitations in this study. First, we only detected the content of DDIT4 mRNA and the m6A level of DDIT4 after siRNA interference with METTL14 but did not further detect phenotypes associated with cell senescence and pulmonary fibrosis. Second, we did not further confirm the specific mechanism of the involvement of DDIT4 in aging-related IPF.
