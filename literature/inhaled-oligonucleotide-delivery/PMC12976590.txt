## ABSTRACT
Prostate cancer (PCa) remains a leading cause of cancer‐related morbidity and mortality among men, necessitating the elucidation of molecular drivers behind its progression. In this study, we demonstrated that CSDE1 was upregulated in PCa tissues, and its expression was associated with poorer progression‐free survival in patients with high Gleason scores. Functional analyses revealed that CSDE1 knockdown reduced PCa cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, while suppressing metabolic activity, as evidenced by reduced extracellular acidification rates (ECAR), oxygen consumption rates (OCR), and lactate production. At the mechanistic level, CSDE1 was found to be associated with RAC1 expression and RAC1‐related signaling markers, accompanied by changes in MAPK pathway phosphorylation. Genetic modulation of RAC1 partially reversed the cellular and metabolic effects associated with altered CSDE1 expression, supporting a functional role for RAC1 in CSDE1‐associated phenotypes. In addition, CSDE1 expression was accompanied by increased levels of the glycolytic regulators GLUT1 and LDHA, and pharmacological inhibition of MAPK signaling partially attenuated CSDE1‐associated glycolytic changes. In vivo models validated the oncogenic role of the CSDE1‐RAC1 axis in tumor growth. Together, these findings support CSDE1 as a key driver that enhances RAC1‐mediated MAPK signaling and metabolic profiles in PCa, suggesting that CSDE1 may serve as a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

## 
CSDE1 Promotes Prostate Cancer Cell Growth and Invasiveness
The efficiency of CSDE1 knockdown was confirmed by quantitative real‐time PCR and Western blot analysis in PC3 and DU145 cells (Figure  ). CSDE1 knockdown significantly reduced cell proliferation, as demonstrated by CCK‐8 growth curves, decreased EdU incorporation (Figure  ), and markedly impaired clonogenic capacity in colony formation assays (Figure  ). In addition, CSDE1 knockdown suppressed cell migration and invasion, as evidenced by reduced numbers of migrating and invading cells in Transwell assays and delayed wound closure in wound healing assays (Figure  ).
 Conversely, CSDE1 overexpression in PC3 and DU145 cells led to increased cell proliferation and enhanced EdU incorporation, accompanied by increased colony‐forming ability (Figure  ). CSDE1 overexpression also promoted migratory and invasive capacities, as indicated by increased Transwell migration and invasion and accelerated wound closure (Figure  ).
 These experimental results suggest that CSDE1 plays a tumor‐promoting role in the initiation and progression of prostate cancer.

3.3

## 
CSDE1 Expression Is Associated With RAC1‐Related Signaling in Prostate Cancer Cells
To explore signaling pathways associated with CSDE1, protein–protein interaction analysis via STRING and KEGG pathway enrichment identified multiple CSDE1‐related pathways, including the MAPK signaling pathway (Figure  ). In the TCGA prostate cancer dataset, CSDE1 mRNA expression was positively correlated with RAC1 mRNA expression (Figure  ). At the protein level, CSDE1 knockdown in PC3 and DU145 cells was accompanied by reduced RAC1 protein expression, decreased phosphorylation of the RAC1 downstream effector PAK, and reduced phosphorylation of RAF, MEK, and ERK, whereas total PAK, RAF, MEK, and ERK levels remained largely unchanged (Figure  ). Conversely, CSDE1 overexpression was associated with increased RAC1 protein levels and enhanced phosphorylation of PAK, RAF, MEK, and ERK in both cell lines (Figure  ). Coimmunoprecipitation assays revealed an association between CSDE1 and RAC1, and immunofluorescence analysis revealed overlapping cytoplasmic distributions of CSDE1 and RAC1 in PC3 and DU145 cells (Figure  ). To assess whether CSDE1 expression is associated with changes in RAC1 protein turnover, cycloheximide chase assays were performed. In both PC3 and DU145 cells, RAC1 protein levels decreased over time following cycloheximide treatment, with CSDE1 overexpression associated with a decrease in RAC1 protein turnover compared with that in vector controls (Figure  ). At the transcript level, actinomycin D chase assays revealed comparable RAC1 mRNA decay kinetics between vector control and CSDE1‐overexpressing cells, with no significant difference observed in RAC1 mRNA stability (Figure  ). In parallel, RNA immunoprecipitation assays demonstrated significant enrichment of RAC1 mRNA in CSDE1 immunoprecipitates compared with IgG controls in both PC3 and DU145 cells (Figure  ).
 Together, these findings support an association between CSDE1 expression and RAC1 protein turnover with corresponding changes in downstream signaling without a significant effect on RAC1 mRNA stability in prostate cancer cells.

3.4

## 
CSDE1 Expression Is Associated With Glycolytic Features in Prostate Cancer Cells
To investigate whether CSDE1 affects metabolic reprogramming in PCa, we analyzed data from the TCGA‐PRAD cohort. The analysis revealed that CSDE1 expression levels were positively correlated with overall glycolysis scores and were particularly associated with increased mRNA expression of key glycolytic regulators, LDHA and GLUT1 (Figure  ). CSDE1 knockdown markedly reduced LDHA and GLUT1 expression at both the mRNA (Figure  ) and protein levels (Figure  ). It also led to decreased lactate production (Figure  ). Conversely, CSDE1 overexpression led to elevated expression of both LDHA and GLUT1 (Figure  ) and increased lactate production (Figure  ). Consistent with these findings, extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) measurements revealed reduced glycolytic activity in PC3 and DU145 cells following CSDE1 knockdown compared to control cells (Figure  ). It also altered OCR profiles, indicating corresponding changes in cellular bioenergetic status (Figure  ). To examine the contribution of MAPK signaling to this metabolic phenotype, we treated CSDE1‐overexpressing cells with the MEK inhibitor U0126. U0126 effectively suppressed ERK1/2 phosphorylation and partially attenuated CSDE1 overexpression–associated increases in GLUT1/LDHA expression and lactate production (Figure  ). U0126 also partially reduced CSDE1‐associated ECAR elevation and produced corresponding effects on OCR phenotypes (Figure  ).
 Collectively, these results support that CSDE1 enhances glycolysis‐related activity in prostate cancer cells, and that this phenotype is at least partly dependent on MAPK pathway activity.

3.5

## 
RAC1 Is Required for CSDE1‐Associated Cellular and Metabolic Phenotypes in Prostate Cancer Cells
To further examine the involvement of RAC1 in CSDE1‐associated cellular and metabolic features, rescue experiments were performed in PC3 and DU145 cells. CSDE1 knockdown was associated with reduced RAC1 expression, decreased activation of RAS/MAPK signaling components, and reduced expression of the glycolytic regulators GLUT1 and LDHA; these changes were partially attenuated by RAC1 overexpression (Figure  ). Densitometric quantification of p‐RAF, p‐MEK, and p‐ERK further supported this trend: RAC1 overexpression partly restored the reduced MAPK phosphorylation caused by CSDE1 knockdown in both PC3 and DU145 cells (Figure  ). Functionally, RAC1 overexpression partially alleviated the reductions in cell proliferation and colony formation observed following CSDE1 knockdown (Figure  ) and partially restored migratory and invasive capacities (Figure  ). In parallel, the decrease in lactate production associated with CSDE1 knockdown was also attenuated by RAC1 overexpression in both cell lines (Figure  ). Conversely, RAC1 silencing in CSDE1‐overexpressing cells attenuated CSDE1‐associated increases in cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and lactate production (Figure  ).
 Together, these findings indicate that RAC1 is involved in CSDE1‐associated cellular and metabolic phenotypes in prostate cancer cells.

3.6

## In Vivo Validation of CSDE1–RAC1 Axis in Prostate Tumor Growth
To validate the roles of CSDE1 and RAC1 in tumorigenesis in vivo, a xenograft model was established by subcutaneously injecting PC3 cells into immunodeficient mice. Tumor growth was significantly reduced in CSDE1‐silenced group, as indicated by a decreased tumor volume and reduced tumor weight. Importantly, RAC1 overexpression partially reversed this inhibitory effect in CSDE1‐knockdown cells (Figure  ). Tumor weight is presented as mean ± SD, and individual mouse values are shown (Figure  ). One‐way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test showed that tumor weight was lower in the sh‐CSDE1 group than in the sh‐NC group (mean difference 151.0 mg, 95% CI 115.7–186.4; adjusted p < 0.0001). In CSDE1‐knockdown background, RAC1 overexpression increased tumor weight compared with sh‐CSDE1 tumors (mean difference 95.29 mg, 95% CI 59.96–130.6; adjusted p < 0.0001). However, tumor weight remained lower than that of sh‐NC controls (mean difference 55.75 mg, 95% CI 20.41–91.09; adjusted p = 0.0032). These results indicate a partial rescue in vivo (Figure  ). Tumor volume curves are shown as mean ± SD and were analyzed by two‐way ANOVA (Figure  ).
 Immunohistochemistry revealed reduced levels of RAC1, Ki‐67, p‐PAK, p‐MEK, p‐ERK, GLUT1, and LDHA in tumors from the CSDE1‐knockdown group, with these markers partially restored upon RAC1 overexpression (Figure  ). Western blot analysis of tumor tissue lysates confirmed these observations, further supporting the regulatory interplay between CSDE1 and RAC1 in vivo, which is consistent with the in vitro results (Figure  ).
On the basis of our in vitro and in vivo findings, we propose a model in which CSDE1 may enhance RAC1‐associated MAPK signaling and glycolytic activity, thereby contributing to prostate tumor growth (Figure  ).

## Discussion
Although most prostate cancers have favorable long‐term outcomes, patients with Gleason score 9–10 represent a high‐risk subgroup with markedly poorer clinical outcomes and a greater likelihood of recurrence and metastasis, underscoring the need to better define the molecular features associated with aggressive PCa [ ]. Previous studies have demonstrated that CSDE1, an RNA‐binding protein (RBP), exerts significant oncogenic effects on various types of malignancies. For example, in melanoma, CSDE1 promotes tumor cell invasion and metastasis by regulating posttranscriptional gene expression. In pancreatic, colorectal, and breast cancers, CSDE1 has been shown to be closely associated with the maintenance of cancer cell stemness, chemoresistance, and enhanced metastatic potential [ , , , , , , ]. However, the biological role and mechanistic relevance of CSDE1 in prostate cancer have not been systematically characterized.
In this study, we systematically evaluated CSDE1 expression and functional relevance in prostate cancer and examined its relationships with RAC1‐related signaling, MAPK pathway activity, and glycolysis‐related metabolic phenotypes. We found that CSDE1 is upregulated in prostate cancer tissues and is associated with shorter progression‐free survival in the high‐Gleason subgroup. Functionally, altering CSDE1 levels was accompanied by changes in cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, together with shifts in glycolysis‐related readouts (ECAR and lactate production) and accompanying changes in mitochondrial respiration (OCR), consistent with altered GLUT1 and LDHA expression. Collectively, these results extend prior observations to a prostate cancer context and support a link between CSDE1 and tumor growth–related phenotypes and glycolysis‐associated metabolic features, with potential translational relevance in high‐grade disease.
RAC1 plays a central regulatory role in processes such as cytoskeleton remodeling, cell migration, and malignant progression of tumors [ , ]. Its expression is increased in many cancer types [ , , , , ]. The RAS/MAPK pathway, as a core oncogenic network, is widely involved in the regulation of the proliferation, survival, and metastasis of various advanced malignant tumors, including PCa [ , , ]. Prior studies in melanoma and other tumors have suggested a link between CSDE1 and RAC1‐related signaling [ ]. However, in prostate cancer, systematic evidence is still limited regarding the expression features of CSDE1 and its functional relationship with RAC1/MAPK signaling. It also remains unclear whether these signaling changes are connected to metabolic phenotypes such as enhanced glycolysis.
To address these gaps, in our study, the modulation of CSDE1 expression in PCa cells was accompanied by changes in proliferative and invasive behaviors and altered phosphorylation of MAPK pathway components. Together with RAC1 rescue experiments and the attenuation of related phenotypes by MEK inhibition, these results support a functional role for RAC1 in CSDE1‐associated cellular phenotypes. Collectively, these findings support a functional association between CSDE1 expression, RAC1‐related signaling, and MAPK pathway activity in PCa models.
Tumor cells with high proliferative activity typically undergo metabolic reprogramming to meet increased biosynthetic demands [ , ]. Early‐stage or well‐differentiated tumors often rely more on oxidative phosphorylation. In contrast, in high‐grade disease, during progression, or under treatment pressure, increased glycolysis and altered lactate metabolism are more often linked to aggressive behavior and poor outcomes [ ]. In this context, glucose uptake mediated by GLUT1 (SLC2A1) and lactate production driven by LDHA have been repeatedly reported to correlate with metabolic adaptation and invasive phenotypes in prostate cancer [ , , ], Glycolytic enzymes are regulated by several signaling pathways, including the PI3K/AKT/mTOR, MAPK, and HIF‐1α pathways, which collectively facilitate metabolic adaptation to environmental changes [ , , , ]. However, it remains unclear whether RNA‐binding proteins (RBPs), as post‐transcriptional regulators, can serve as a “signal–metabolism” linker in prostate cancer and shape glycolytic phenotypes by influencing the expression of key metabolic regulators such as GLUT1 and LDHA.
In this study, the modulation of CSDE1 expression in PCa cells was associated with changes in glycolysis‐related phenotypes. High CSDE1 expression was associated with increased glycolytic activity, as reflected by elevated ECAR, OCR, and lactate production, together with increased expression of the glycolytic regulators GLUT1 and LDHA. Moreover, MEK inhibition attenuated CSDE1‐associated increases in GLUT1/LDHA and glycolysis‐related readouts. Collectively, these findings suggest that CSDE1 expression is associated with changes in RAC1‐related MAPK signaling and with glycolysis‐related phenotypes in PCa cells.
The TME is a key determinant of cancer progression and therapeutic resistance [ ]. In this study, TIMER analysis of the TCGA‐PRAD cohort showed that CSDE1 expression was positively correlated with estimated infiltration scores of multiple immune cell subsets; however, these results are based on computational deconvolution and should be considered exploratory. Future work will validate these associations in independent cohorts and experimental models.
Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although we observed an association between CSDE1 and RAC1, the precise molecular basis of this association—including whether it involves direct protein–protein interaction or RNA‐mediated mechanisms—remains to be clarified. Second, while our analyses suggested a correlation between CSDE1 expression and immune cell infiltration, we did not perform functional experiments to directly examine the role of CSDE1 in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment. Third, the clinical validation was based on a small paired tissue cohort (n = 12), and we did not perform multivariable survival analyses to assess whether CSDE1 provides independent prognostic information beyond established clinicopathologic factors. In addition, our experimental models primarily reflect hormone‐sensitive PCa cell lines and xenografts; validation in CRPC settings and patient‐derived models will be important in future studies.
In conclusion, this study demonstrated that CSDE1 expression is closely associated with aggressive cellular behaviors and glycolysis‐related phenotypes in prostate cancer. Our findings support a functional association between CSDE1 expression, RAC1‐related MAPK signaling activity, and metabolic features, suggesting that CSDE1 may participate in prostate cancer progression through the coordinated regulation of signaling and metabolic pathways. These findings expand the current understanding of the biological role of CSDE1 in prostate cancer and provide a rationale for further investigation of CSDE1 as a potential biomarker and therapeutic intervention target.
