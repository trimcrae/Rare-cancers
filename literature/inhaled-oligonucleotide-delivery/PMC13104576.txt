## Background
Hypertension-associated aortic dissection (HTN-AD) features intense immune activation and extracellular matrix disruption, yet the cellular programs that promote aortic-wall stabilization remain unclear. We aimed to identify macrophage states associated with vascular integrity and to determine whether these states could be mechanistically or therapeutically leveraged.

## Methods
Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on thoracic aortas from HTN-AD, Marfan syndrome-associated AD, and donor controls (n = 3 per group). Peripheral and tear-adjacent blood samples were analyzed in patients with HTN-AD, MS-AD, and healthy donors (n = 30 per group), with paired intraoperative tear-adjacent sampling performed when available (n = 10). Circulating monocytes and serum cytokines were profiled by flow cytometry and ELISA. Functional validation included TGF-β1 conditioning of human monocytes and murine macrophages and adoptive transfer experiments in a murine AD model.

## Results
A distinct SPP1high fibrogenic macrophage population was enriched in diseased aortas and transcriptionally bridged low-inflammatory macrophages and matrix-active states. Patients with HTN-AD displayed increased tear-adjacent non-classical monocytes and higher monocytic SPP1, correlating with serum TGF-β1 and a shorter ICU stay. TGF-β1 induced an SPP1-dependent fibrogenic program in macrophages while suppressing inflammatory markers. Myeloid Spp1 deficiency aggravated aortic dilatation and wall disruption, whereas adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-conditioned SPP1high macrophages reduced false lumen formation, improved collagen organization, decreased IL-1β and IL-6 expression, and enhanced survival.

## Conclusions
A TGF-β1–SPP1 axis drives the emergence of SPP1high fibrogenic macrophages that support structural stabilization of the dissected aorta. Targeting this reparative myeloid program may offer a mechanistically informed therapeutic strategy for acute aortic syndromes.

## Background
Aortic dissection (AD) is a catastrophic cardiovascular emergency, with an incidence of approximately 4–7.7 per 100,000 patient-years [ ], characterized by an intimal tear or intramural hematoma that separates the aortic wall layers [ ]. Despite advances in surgical techniques and perioperative care, in-hospital mortality remains as high as 17–26%, and many survivors experience substantial long-term morbidity [ , ]. Hypertension and heritable aortopathies such as Marfan syndrome together account for the majority of AD cases [ ], yet current therapies primarily target hemodynamic load rather than the local biological processes that determine whether the dissected aortic wall stabilizes or progresses to rupture. Increasing evidence implicates immune-cell-driven inflammation and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling as central determinants of AD progression [ , ], but the specific cellular and molecular programs that promote vascular stabilization remain poorly defined.
Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is essential for cardiovascular development and vascular homeostasis [ – ], and protective actions of TGF-β signaling have been documented in several aneurysm models [ ]. However, its effects are highly context-dependent, varying with disease stage, aortic segment, genetic background, and cellular target [ , ]. Classical, smooth muscle-centric paradigms can only partially explain the complex pathology observed at the time of dissection, when dense myeloid-cell infiltration, matrix degradation, and attempts at repair coexist in the same arterial segment [ ]. In particular, it remains unclear whether TGF-β-responsive myeloid programs promote a controlled, reparative form of fibrosis associated with improved organization of the dissected aortic wall, or instead exacerbate inflammation and matrix degradation in human AD.
Osteopontin (OPN), encoded by SPP1, is a matricellular protein with both immunoregulatory and profibrotic properties [ – ] and is highly expressed in vascular pathology. Across diverse inflammatory and fibrotic conditions, TGF-β signaling has been linked to OPN/SPP1 pathways [ , ], suggesting a hierarchical relationship between these two mediators. In patients with hypertension and vascular calcification, SPP1 has been reported to dampen inflammatory cytokine production by shaping monocyte-macrophage differentiation [ ], whereas in other settings SPP1⁺ macrophages have been implicated in pathogenic fibrosis [ ], tumor progression [ ], and atrial fibrillation [ ]. These apparently divergent observations underscore the strongly context-dependent nature of OPN/SPP1 in tissue remodeling and raise the possibility that, under specific conditions, OPN⁺ myeloid programs might be harnessed in a protective manner. Whether such a program exists in AD, how it is regulated by TGF-β1, and how it relates to clinical outcomes remain unknown.
Here, we sought to define TGF-β1-linked myeloid programs that may promote vascular stabilization in AD. We constructed single-cell transcriptomic atlases of human thoracic aorta spanning hypertension-induced AD (HTN-AD), Marfan syndrome-associated AD (MS-AD), and non-diseased controls, and integrated these data with peripheral immune phenotyping and in vitro TGF-β1 conditioning of human monocytes and murine macrophages. Using experimental models of hypertension-associated AD, we then functionally interrogated the impact of TGF-β1-induced macrophage states on aortic remodeling and survival. We hypothesized that engagement of a TGF-β1-SPP1-enriched myeloid program drives a low-inflammatory, matrix-active macrophage phenotype that supports fibrogenic repair and stabilization of the dissected aortic wall, thereby revealing a potentially targetable myeloid axis for therapeutic exploration in acute aortic syndromes.

## Methods

## Patient specimens
Human tissue use was approved by the Human Ethics Committee of Guangzhou First People’s Hospital (K-2019–156–02), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Normal aortas (n = 3) were obtained from heart-transplant donors, while diseased aortas included samples from patients with HTN-AD (n = 3) and MS-AD (n = 3) collected during surgical aortic replacement for single-cell RNA sequencing.
Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), CD14⁺ monocytes, and serum were collected from HTN-AD (n = 30), MS-AD (n = 30), and age-/sex-matched healthy donors (HDs, n = 30). Peri-dissection blood was obtained intraoperatively when applicable. Exclusion criteria included active infection, autoimmune disease, malignancy, prior aortic surgery, or corticosteroid/NSAID use. Delayed ICU discharge was defined as an ICU stay longer than 7.1 days, which corresponds to the mean ICU stay in the HTN-AD group (Additional file 1: Table S1). Clinical data were collected independently by blinded investigators. All procedures complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Single-cell RNA sequencing data preprocessing
FASTQ files were processed with Cell Ranger (v4.0.0, 10 × Genomics) and aligned to GRCh38. Resulting UMI matrices were analyzed in Seurat (v3.2.0) [ ]. Cells with low gene counts, high mitochondrial content (> 10%), or doublet-like profiles (> 6000 genes) were removed, yielding 97,288 cells and 29,828 genes. Counts were library-normalized and log-transformed; 2000 variable genes were selected for integration. The nine datasets were integrated using Seurat’s anchoring workflow, followed by dimensionality reduction, clustering, differential expression, SCENIC, functional enrichment, module scoring, trajectory inference, and NicheNet analysis, as detailed in Additional file 1: Supplementary Methods.

## Animal model, anesthesia, and euthanasia
Aortic dissection was induced in 4-week-old male C57BL/6 J mice maintained under specific-pathogen-free conditions with free access to food and water by administering 0.25% β-aminopropionitrile (BAPN, wt/vol) in the drinking water for 4 weeks. After BAPN treatment (day 29), mice were subcutaneously implanted with Alzet osmotic mini-pumps delivering angiotensin II (Ang II, 1000 ng/kg/min) for 7 days. During the Ang II infusion period, blood pressure was monitored by the tail-cuff method to confirm stable induction of the hypertensive AD model. Mice with failed pump implantation or signs of postoperative infection were excluded from further analysis according to predefined criteria.
Mice were anesthetized with inhaled isoflurane (1–2% in oxygen) for all survival surgeries and ultrasound examinations. Recombinant TGF-β1 (5 μg/mL, 200 μL per mouse) was administered via tail vein injection either at the onset of BAPN treatment (week 0) or at week 2, and then once weekly thereafter according to the experimental group.
For cell-transfer experiments, 1 × 106 TGF-β1-induced macrophages were injected intravenously at week 2. Survival was monitored daily, and thoracic aortas were harvested at the predefined experimental endpoint or upon death. For terminal tissue and cell collection, mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal administration of pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg) followed by cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional and national guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. Interventions and outcome assessments were conducted by separate investigators, with analysts blinded to group allocation. All reagents are listed (Additional file 1: Table S2). All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital (approval No. 0108–7–800-ZX(X)−019) and complied with Directive 2010/63/EU of the European Parliament and the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Animals were randomly assigned to experimental groups using a random number generator.

## Validation of adoptive transfer of SPP1high macrophages
Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) were generated from donor mice of the indicated genotypes (wild-type C57BL/6 J, Lyz2-IRES-mtagBFP2-Luc, and myeloid-specific Spp1 conditional knockout mice). For bone marrow isolation, donor mice were deeply anesthetized with intraperitoneal pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg) and euthanized by cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional and national guidelines. Bone marrow cells were flushed from femurs and tibias and cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and M-CSF (20 ng/mL) for 7 days to generate BMDMs.
For adoptive transfer, BMDMs derived from Lyz2-IRES-mtagBFP2-Luc mice were stimulated with TGF-β1 (5 ng/mL, 24 h) to obtain SPP1high macrophages, harvested, and resuspended in PBS. A total of 1 × 106 viable cells in 100 μL were injected intravenously via the tail vein into recipient mice with aortic dissection. Three days later, recipient mice were deeply anesthetized with intraperitoneal pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg) and euthanized by cervical dislocation for aortic cell isolation. Thoracic aortas were perfused with cold PBS, separated into lesion and tear-remote regions, and enzymatically digested to obtain single-cell suspensions. BFP⁺ cells were detected by flow cytometry, and their proportions among live singlets and among total aortic cells were quantified separately in lesion and tear-remote regions.

## Generation of myeloid-specific Spp1 conditional knockout mice
To generate myeloid-specific Spp1 conditional knockout (Spp1-CKO) mice, we crossbred mice carrying a floxed Spp1 allele (Spp1flox/flox, C57BL/6 J background; MODEL ORGANISMS, SHANGHAI, China) with Lyz2-Cre transgenic mice (C57BL/6 J background). Spp1flox/+;Lyz2-Cre+ offspring were obtained and intercrossed with each other to generate mice carrying Spp1flox/flox with myeloid-specific Cre expression (Spp1flox/flox;Lyz2-Cre+, referred to as Spp1-CKO) and Spp1flox/flox littermates lacking the Cre transgene (Spp1flox/flox;Lyz2-Cre−, used as controls). Genotypes were determined by PCR analysis of tail DNA before inclusion in experiments.

## Histology
Thoracic aortas were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, paraffin-embedded, sectioned (4 μm), and stained with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) or Masson’s trichrome to assess pathology and collagen deposition. Histological assessments, including lesion severity and fibrosis scoring, were performed by investigators blinded to group allocation.

## ELISA
Cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, TGF-β1/β2, OPN) were quantified using Quantikine ELISA kits (R&D Systems) following the manufacturer’s instructions and reported as absolute concentrations or fold-changes.

## SPP1high macrophage induction
Bone marrow cells were isolated from the femurs and tibias of 6-week-old C57BL/6 J mice and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 25 ng/mL recombinant murine M-CSF (rM-CSF) for 7 days to induce macrophage differentiation. After differentiation, cells were treated with 5 ng/mL TGF-β1, 0.1 μM angiotensin II, or 100 ng/mL OPN for 24 h. Culture media were refreshed every 3 days during the differentiation process.

## siRNA transfection
Mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages were seeded at a density of 1.0 × 105 cells/well in 6-well plates and transfected with siRNAs using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen, 13,778) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. An siRNA targeting OPN (siOPN) was used at a concentration of 20 nM. Scrambled siRNA (siScrambled) at a concentration of 20 nM served as a negative control. All siRNAs were obtained from Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd, China. siRNA-mediated knockdown efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR and Western blotting.

## Flow cytometry
Cells were collected, washed with PBS containing 2% FBS, and incubated with fluorophore-conjugated antibodies for surface marker staining at 4 °C in the dark. After washing, cells were resuspended in PBS with 2% FBS and analyzed using a flow cytometer. Data were analyzed with FlowJo software. The antibodies used for flow cytometry analysis are listed (Additional file 1: Table S3).

## Isolation of PBMCs and CD14⁺ monocytes
Peripheral blood was collected into EDTA tubes and processed within 2 h. PBMCs were isolated by density-gradient centrifugation using Ficoll-Paque PLUS and washed twice with PBS. For experiments requiring purified monocytes, CD14⁺ monocytes were positively selected from PBMCs using magnetic CD14 MicroBeads and LS columns (Miltenyi Biotec) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The purity of CD14⁺ monocytes routinely exceeded 90% as assessed by flow cytometry.

## RT-qPCR
Total RNA was isolated (TaKaRa MiniBEST, 9767), DNase I-treated, and reverse-transcribed (PrimeScript RT Master Mix, RR036A). qPCR was run with SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (RR820A) on QuantStudio 5. Relative expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method using β-actin as the reference; controls were set to 1. The primers used for qPCR analysis are listed (Additional file 1: Table S4).

## Western blotting
Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer. Protein lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, probed with primary and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and detected by chemiluminescence.

## Immunofluorescence staining
Paraffin sections were dewaxed, antigen-retrieved, blocked, incubated with primary antibodies overnight, followed by fluorescent secondary antibodies and DAPI counterstaining, and imaged on a ZEISS LSM800 microscope.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (v8). Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± SEM or median (IQR), as appropriate, while categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to assess the relationship between two continuous variables. For comparisons between two groups, the paired or unpaired Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test was employed, based on data distribution and study design. Comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test for normally distributed data. Data distribution was assessed prior to analysis, and appropriate parametric or nonparametric tests were applied accordingly. The diagnostic performance of key indicators was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and the area under the curve (AUC) was reported. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## An SPP1high cell population is enriched in aortic dissection
scRNA-seq was performed on aortic tissues from patients with HTN-AD (n = 3), MS-AD (n = 3), and heart-transplant donors as controls (n = 3), yielding 97,288 high-quality cells with an average of 1725 genes per cell (Additional file 1: Fig. S1A). Integrated analysis across all samples minimized batch effects and enabled the generation of a comparative cell atlas.
Unsupervised clustering based on canonical vascular markers (CD68 for monocytes/macrophages, CD3E for T cells, LUM for fibroblasts, ACTA2 for SMCs, PECAM1 for ECs, and KLRB1 for NK cells) identified major cell populations (Additional file 1: Figs. S1B, C and Fig.  A). Notably, a unique cell cluster coexpressing intermediate levels of CD68 and LUM was observed predominantly in samples from patients with HTN-AD and MS-AD (Figs. B and Additional file 1: Fig. S1D). This cluster was characterized by high expression of SPP1 (osteopontin) (Additional file 1: Figs. S2 and S3), which is associated with vascular inflammation [ ] and fibrogenic macrophage phenotypes [ ] (Fig.  C). 
Cell-type composition analysis showed that myeloid cells were the most abundant across all groups. A newly identified SPP1high cluster was markedly enriched in diseased aortas but virtually absent in controls, whereas T-, NK-, and B-cell populations were comparable across groups. In contrast, non-immune cells, particularly fibroblasts and SMCs, were significantly depleted in HTN-AD (Fig.  D). Stromal depletion suggests a pathogenic compromise of structural support, whereas expansion of SPP1high cell groups implicates a myeloid-remodeling program that merits mechanistic investigation.
To trace the hierarchical relationship, transcriptomic correlation analysis revealed that SPP1high cluster was transcriptionally closer to monocytes/macrophages (Fig.  E). This was further validated by immunofluorescence staining, showing increased CD68⁺vimentin⁺ double-positive cells in HTN-AD and MS-AD lesions (Fig.  F). Moreover, SPP1⁺ cells were more abundant at the tear site than in remote areas of the aorta (Fig.  G). Collectively, these findings define SPP1high as a distinct myeloid population in AD.

## SPP1high cluster exhibits an intermediate state between macrophages and fibroblasts
The SPP1high cluster exhibits an intermediate state between macrophages and fibroblasts, as evidenced by the expression of CD68, AIF1, BGN, and LUM at intermediate levels, alongside limited expression of the collagen gene COL1A1 and the myofibroblast marker ACTA2 (Fig.  A). Differential expression analysis revealed a unique transcriptional profile, characterized by the expression of inflammatory cytokines (IL1B, CCL3, CCL4, CXCL3); complement components (C1QB, C1QC, C3); and cell-surface proteins (HLA-DRA, CD74, FCER1A), similar to macrophages (Fig.  B). Enrichment analysis indicated their primary function in extracellular matrix (ECM) organization (Fig.  C). 
Consistently, the SPP1high cluster exhibits intermediate functional features between macrophages and fibroblasts. GSEA of differentially expressed genes shows that it upregulates ECM organization and connective tissue development pathways compared to myeloid cells, but downregulates immune response-related pathways. In contrast, when compared to fibroblasts, SPP1high cluster exhibits lower activity in ECM-related pathways and higher immune response activity (Fig.  D). SCENIC analysis identifies MEOX2 and GATA3—transcription factors associated with low-inflammatory polarization [ ] and known to exert fibrogenic effects in cardiovascular disease [ ]—as highly active regulons in this population (Fig.  E). These regulons likely drive the phenotypic characteristics of role in fibrosis.

## Low-inflammatory, matrix-active macrophages expand and connect transcriptionally to SPP1high states
Myeloid cells constituted the largest immune population in the aortic samples. Unsupervised clustering identified six macrophage clusters, one monocyte cluster, and one conventional-dendritic-cell (cDC) cluster (Additional file 1: Fig. S4A).
Among macrophages, Macro1 increased whereas Macro2 decreased in HTN-AD, with a similar but milder pattern in MS-AD; monocytes also rose in HTN-AD, consistent with recruitment from blood (Additional file : Fig. S4B). Marker analysis showed Macro2 was pro-inflammatory (CCL3/CCL4/TNF/IL1B), while Macro1 downregulated pro-inflammatory cytokines and upregulated ECM-remodeling genes (FN1, SPP1), enriching pathways that regulate fibroblast proliferation (Additional file : Fig. S4C, D), indicating a low-inflammatory, matrix-active (pro-fibrotic) program. Across macrophage subsets versus NC, canonical inflammatory genes (e.g., CCL3/CCL4/NFKB1/NLRP3/IL1B) were broadly downregulated; higher S100A8/A9 in monocytes and Macro1 suggested recent monocyte recruitment with retained differentiation potential (Additional file : Fig. S4C, E).
Focusing on SPP1high heterogeneity, seven SPP1high clusters with patient specificity were identified (Additional file 1: Fig. S5A, B); consistent with the biological paradigm that tissue-infiltrating monocytes give rise to macrophages, particularly in disease contexts [ ], monocytes were assigned as the root cells for pseudotime ordering. A force-directed graph and PAGA resolved myeloid and SPP1high populations into distinct subnetworks connected by Macro1–SPP1high7 (Additional file 1: Fig. S5C, D), with Slingshot inferring a concordant minimum-spanning-tree lineage (Additional file 1: Fig. S5E). Transcriptome correlation further supported Macro1–SPP1high7 similarity (Additional file 1: Fig. S5F). With monocytes as the root, diffusion pseudotime supported a trajectory from circulating monocytes to Macro1 macrophages and subsequent acquisition of SPP1⁺ fibrogenic features (Additional file 1: Fig. S5G, H), suggesting derivation from newly recruited, relatively low-inflammatory macrophages in AD tissue.

## TGF-β signaling is implicated in the transition of macrophages to an SPP1high state
Scatter-density plots indicate a positive association between SPP1 and TGFB1 in macrophages, prominent in HTN-AD and weak in NC (HTN-AD ρ = 0.24; MS-AD ρ = 0.043; NC ρ = 0.078) (Additional file 1: Fig. S6A).
To identify key drivers of macrophage reprogramming toward an SPP1high state in AD, we applied NicheNet to predict ligand-receptor interactions and their regulatory impact on differentially expressed genes between SPP1high macrophages and myeloid cells in AD [ ]. Among the predicted ligands, TGFB1 and TGFB2 exhibit the highest regulatory activity (Fig.  A), consistent with their established roles in AD and fibrosis [ , ]. Additional profibrotic ligands (SPP1, FGF1, BMP2) and proinflammatory signals (IL1B, IFNG, TNF) are also identified (Fig.  B). SPP1 was markedly upregulated across nearly all major cell types, whereas TGFB1 showed mild upregulation in SMCs, fibroblasts, ECs, and SPP1high macrophages, but was downregulated in T and NK cells. TGFB2 expression remains largely unchanged (Fig.  C). In MS-AD, a similar ligand signature is observed, sharing six top ligands with HTN-AD (Fig.  D–F). 
Analysis of downstream target genes revealed that ECM-related genes such as COL1A1, COL3A1, and POSTN, as well as anti-oxidative-stress-related genes such as SOD3, were markedly upregulated. In contrast, a small subset of genes involved in monocyte differentiation, inflammatory signaling, and antigen presentation was downregulated in AD samples (Fig.  G).
Beyond macrophage-to-macrophage SPP1high transition, ligand–target interactions contribute to transcriptional changes from healthy to diseased states in myeloid cells, revealing high regulatory activity of TGFB1 and SPP1 (Additional file 1: Fig. S7A–D). Collectively, these findings identify TGFB1 as a key regulatory cue in AD, particularly in myeloid cells, where its elevated expression may orchestrate both fibrotic and inflammatory reprogramming.

## Tear-adjacent enrichment of low-inflammatory monocytes with elevated OPN expression reflects the TGF-β1–OPN axis
Non-classical monocytes are recognized as a major source of low-inflammatory tissue macrophages after recruitment to inflamed sites [ ]. Consistent with this concept and with our single-cell data indicating a continuum from monocytes to low-inflammatory macrophages and ultimately SPP1high macrophages within AD lesions, we next examined circulating monocyte subsets and their clinical correlates (Fig.  A). Flow-cytometric t-SNE analysis of peripheral blood monocytes shows a reduced proportion of non-classical monocytes (CD14⁻CD16⁺⁺) in patients with HTN-AD and MS-AD compared with healthy donors (Fig.  B). Paired sampling further revealed that non-classical monocytes were significantly enriched in tear-adjacent aortic blood compared with peripheral blood in HTN-AD, but not in MS-AD (Fig.  C), consistent with scRNA-seq evidence of increased low-inflammatory monocytes/macrophages within HTN-AD lesions (Additional file 1: Fig. S4B). 
To explore whether systemic signals contribute to this low-inflammatory remodeling, we next assessed circulating TGF-β1 and OPN. Serum TGF-β1 concentrations are higher in HTN-AD than in MS-AD or healthy donors (Fig.  D), and within HTN-AD the frequency of non-classical monocytes positively correlates with serum TGF-β1 (Fig.  E). Serum OPN is also elevated, particularly in HTN-AD (Fig.  F). Notably, lower serum OPN levels in combination with TGF-β1 are associated with prolonged ICU stay (AUC = 0.85; Fig.  G). ICU stay was comparable across groups, indicating that the SPP1–ICU relationship reflects within-group variation in HTN-AD (Additional file 1: Table S1).
At the cellular level, CD14⁺ monocytes from patients with HTN-AD exhibited increased intracellular OPN expression compared with those from patients with MS-AD and healthy donors (Fig.  H). In paired samples from HTN-AD, monocytic OPN was higher in tear-adjacent aortic blood than in peripheral blood, whereas no such gradient was observed in MS-AD (Fig.  I). Across the HTN-AD cohort, serum TGF-β1 positively correlated with monocytic OPN protein (Fig.  J), and higher monocytic OPN was associated with shorter ICU stay (Fig.  K). Together, these findings support a TGF-β1-SPP1-linked monocyte program in HTN-AD that is associated with enrichment of non-classical monocytes at the dissection site and with more favorable clinical outcomes.
To further test whether TGF-β1 directly remodels monocyte phenotypes in HTN-AD, CD14⁺ monocytes from patients with HTN-AD were stimulated ex vivo with TGF-β1, angiotensin II (Ang II), a central renin–angiotensin effector in hypertension and HTN-AD, or recombinant OPN (Fig.  L). Both TGF-β1 and OPN, but not Ang II, increased the proportion of non-classical monocytes (Fig.  M). In parallel, TGF-β1 and OPN reduced the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of the pro-inflammatory costimulatory receptor CD86, increased expression of the chemokine receptor CX3CR1 (Fig.  N, O), and upregulated intracellular SPP1 in monocytes (Fig.  P). Together, these findings indicate that the TGF-β1–OPN axis shapes a low-inflammatory monocyte program in HTN-AD, reflected by elevated OPN expression and tear-adjacent enrichment.

## SPP1 (OPN) mediates TGF-β1-induced suppression of inflammation and promotes the transition toward SPP1high fibrogenic macrophages
To dissect how TGF-β1 drives the transition toward SPP1high fibrogenic macrophages, BMDMs were stimulated with TGF-β1, angiotensin II (Ang II), or recombinant OPN. TGF-β1 robustly induced Spp1/Opn at both the mRNA and protein levels, whereas Ang II had minimal effects (Figs. A–C and Additional file 1: Fig. S9). In parallel, TGF-β1 and OPN, but not Ang II, upregulated a matrix-producing program, as evidenced by increased Col1a1, Col3a1, Fn1, and Postn transcripts (Fig.  D–G). Immunofluorescence staining for CD68 and vimentin revealed a higher proportion of CD68+vimentin+ cells and a more elongated, fibroblast-like morphology in response to TGF-β1 or OPN compared with control or Ang II (Fig.  H). 
TGF-β1 also shifted macrophage polarization toward a low-inflammatory state, with reduced CD86 MFI and increased CD206 MFI; OPN recapitulated these effects, whereas Ang II had little impact (Fig.  I, J). To test whether SPP1 is required for these TGF-β1-driven changes, we performed loss-of-function experiments using Spp1-targeting siRNA (Additional file 1: Fig. S10). SPP1 knockdown (siOpn) did not substantially alter the basal phenotype but markedly attenuated the TGF-β1-induced fibrogenic shift, reducing CD68+vimentin+ colocalization and blunting the fibroblast-like morphological transformation, while having minimal effects in Ang II-treated cells (Fig.  K). Likewise, siOpn abrogated TGF-β1-mediated downregulation of CD86 and upregulation of CD206 (Fig.  L, M).
Together, these data indicate that SPP1/OPN is a critical effector of TGF-β1 in macrophages, required both for induction of an ECM-producing, fibrogenic program and for suppression of inflammatory co-stimulatory signals, thereby promoting the transition toward an SPP1high fibrogenic macrophage phenotype.

## Adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-induced fibrotic macrophages attenuates aortic inflammation and improves survival in AD mice
To investigate the function of SPP1 in TGF-β-induced fibrotic macrophages, in vivo adoptive transfer was performed in BAPN/Ang II-induced hypertensive AD mice, with continuous systolic blood pressure monitoring (Fig.  A and Additional file 1: Fig. S11). Prior to intervention, expression dynamics of TGF-β1 and OPN in the aortic arch were analyzed during AD progression to determine suitable treatment windows. TGF-β1 expression showed a continuous decline after AD induction, whereas OPN peaked at week 2 and subsequently decreased (Additional file 1: Fig. S12A, B). Accordingly, systemic TGF-β was administered at week 0 to compensate for early TGF-β1 loss, whereas adoptive transfer of TGF-β-conditioned SPP1high macrophages was performed at week 2 to coincide with peak OPN expression and matrix remodeling. 
Early administration of recombinant TGF-β1 at week 0 significantly improved survival in AD mice (Fig.  B). Furthermore, adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-preconditioned BMDMs at week 2 prolonged survival compared to non-conditioned macrophages, without increasing collagen deposition in the cardiac tissue, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney, as shown by COL1A1 and Masson’s trichrome staining (Figs. C and Additional file 1: Figs. S13 and S14). However, when the intervention schedule was reversed, with systemic TGF-β1 starting at week 2 or adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-preconditioned BMDMs at week 0, no survival benefit was observed (Additional file 1: Fig. S15). BFP-based in vivo tracking using Lyz2-IRES-mtagBFP2-Luc-derived SPP1high macrophages further demonstrated a significantly higher proportion of transferred cells within aortic lesion areas compared with tear-remote regions [ ], supporting their preferential homing to sites of vascular injury (Additional file 1: Fig. S16).
Ultrasound and gross examination showed reduced aortic dilation in the ascending aorta in both TGF-β1 and fibrotic macrophage treatment groups (Fig.  D, E), with significant reductions in diameter in the ascending and arch segments (Fig.  F, G), while no significant changes were observed in the descending aorta. Histological assessment using H&E staining revealed a reduction in dissection area in both the TGF-β1-treated and fibrotic macrophage-treated groups (Fig.  H), along with decreased aortic wall disruption (Fig.  I). Masson’s trichrome staining demonstrated improved collagen organization (Fig.  J) and a reduced incidence of rupture (Fig.  K). Cytokine analysis further showed significantly decreased IL-1β and IL-6 expression and increased TGF-β1 expression in aortic tissues after treatment (Fig.  L, M).
Moreover, 2–3-month echocardiographic follow-up in surviving mice revealed less aortic arch dilation after adoptive transfer of fibrotic macrophages (Additional file 1: Fig. S17), supporting sustained therapeutic benefit from fibrogenic macrophage remodeling.

## Adoptive transfer of fibrotic macrophages mitigates AD progression under SPP1-deficient conditions
To further probe the role of SPP1 in fibrotic macrophage-mediated protection, we next used mice with myeloid-specific Spp1 deletion (Spp1-CKO) and their Spp1flox/flox littermate controls. Under identical BAPN + Ang II induction, Spp1-CKO mice exhibited markedly worse survival than control mice (Fig.  A, B). This deterioration could not be rescued by systemic TGF-β1 administration (Fig.  C). In contrast, adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-induced fibrotic macrophages derived from Spp1flox/flox donors significantly improved survival in Spp1-CKO recipients (Fig.  D). 
Consistent with a requirement for SPP1-dependent ECM programs, Spp1-CKO BMDMs failed to upregulate matrix-related genes upon TGF-β1 or OPN stimulation (Additional file 1: Fig. S18), and adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-treated Spp1-CKO BMDMs did not confer protection (Fig.  E). Transthoracic echocardiography and gross examination further showed that Spp1-sufficient fibrotic macrophages partially attenuated aortic dilatation in Spp1-CKO mice, most notably in the ascending aorta and aortic arch (Fig.  F–H).
The protective effect of TGF-β1 was likewise SPP1 dependent, as systemic TGF-β1 treatment did not reverse Spp1-CKO-induced aortic expansion or false lumen formation (Additional file 1: Fig. S19). By contrast, adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-induced Spp1-sufficient fibrotic macrophages substantially rescued these structural defects, indicating that SPP1 is required for the beneficial effects of TGF-β1 in AD (Fig.  I–N). Adoptive transfer of TGF-β1-induced Spp1-sufficient fibrotic macrophages markedly improved aortic wall integrity in Spp1-CKO mice. H&E staining revealed severe medial disruption and false lumen formation in Spp1-CKO aortas, both of which were substantially attenuated after macrophage transfer (Fig.  I). Consistently, Masson’s trichrome staining showed disorganized matrix structure in Spp1-CKO aortas, whereas adoptive transfer restored matrix organization and significantly reduced false lumen area (Fig.  J–K). In parallel, AD-related outcomes shifted toward less severe phenotypes following macrophage transfer (Fig.  L). At the molecular level, elevated IL-1β and IL-6 expression in Spp1-CKO mice was blunted at both transcript and protein levels, while TNF-α and TGF-β/TGF-β1 showed minimal changes (Fig.  M–N). Together, these data indicate that SPP1 is required for the structural and immunomodulatory benefits of TGF-β1-conditioned fibrotic macrophages in AD.

## Discussion
In this study, we identify an SPP1high fibrogenic macrophage population that is enriched in hypertension-associated AD and is associated with features of improved aortic-wall integrity. Although macrophage heterogeneity and fibroinflammatory plasticity have been increasingly recognized in vascular biology, their specific contributions to acute aortic injury remain incompletely understood. By integrating single-cell transcriptomics with paired profiling of peripheral and tear-adjacent aortic blood, which provides rare access to local immune signatures in human AD, we show that higher TGF-β1 levels are associated with an expanded pool of low-inflammatory macrophages in vivo, and that TGF-β1 promotes the acquisition of SPP1+ fibrogenic features in monocytes/macrophages in vitro. In mouse models, engagement of this program was linked to reduced aortic dilatation and rupture, consistent with a stabilizing effect on the dissected aorta. The protection conferred by TGF-β1-conditioned macrophages was abolished in Spp1-deficient myeloid cells, supporting SPP1 as a required myeloid factor in this context.
TGF-β signaling has been implicated in both protective and deleterious vascular remodeling, and its effects appear to be context-dependent [ , ]. In our models, the beneficial consequences of engaging the TGF-β1–SPP1 program were most evident in the ascending aorta and aortic arch, regions that differ from other aortic segments in developmental origin and biomechanical loading. These observations raise the possibility that anatomical heterogeneity and local hemodynamic forces may influence whether TGF-β-responsive myeloid programs promote controlled, reparative fibrosis associated with improved organization of the dissected aortic wall, or instead exacerbate inflammation and matrix degradation. Such spatial considerations may partially account for previous divergent findings on TGF-β as either stabilizing or matrix-degrading in aortic disease [ , ].
Mechanistically, our findings support a model in which a TGF-β1-to-SPP1 axis contributes to the acquisition of fibrogenic-macrophage features. Although Ang II can upregulate both TGF-β1 and SPP1 in other cardiovascular contexts [ ], Ang II alone did not reproduce the TGF-β1-induced program in our study, suggesting distinct upstream requirements and signaling integration. The SPP1high macrophages described here also differ from the inflammatory SPP1+ macrophages reported to have pathogenic roles in atrial fibrillation [ ]. In AD, these cells display low-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and matrix-supportive characteristics, whereas in chronic fibrosis SPP1 expression has been linked to persistent extracellular matrix accumulation. More broadly, accumulating evidence suggests that SPP1 functions in a context-dependent manner, often amplifying inflammatory responses during acute immune activation, while contributing to tissue repair, vascular injury remodeling, and extracellular matrix organization in chronic or resolving injury settings [ ]. These functional differences likely reflect distinct tissue demands in acute versus chronic settings: in atrial fibrillation, diffuse inflammatory fibrosis disrupts electrical conduction and promotes arrhythmogenesis [ ], whereas in AD, focal extracellular matrix reinforcement at the tear margins may help contain wall stress and limit rupture risk. Consistent with this interpretation, studies of lung injury have suggested that moderate, well-organized fibrosis can contribute to structural containment rather than pathological remodeling [ ].
Our study also refines the understanding of fibrogenic-cell origin in the human aorta. Low-inflammatory CD14⁺ monocytes and CD14⁺ monocytes with high intracellular SPP1 were enriched in tear-adjacent aortic blood compared with peripheral blood. This finding suggests directed recruitment to the site of injury. Single-cell analysis and in vitro induction experiments are consistent with a model in which circulating monocytes give rise to low-inflammatory macrophages that can further acquire fibrogenic characteristics. This pathway differs from the fibrogenic populations derived from tissue-resident macrophages described in chronic fibrosis [ , ]. The features of the cells identified here are more compatible with the rapid and spatially restricted reparative needs of acute vascular injury rather than with the sustained extracellular matrix accumulation observed in chronic fibrotic remodeling.
From a translational perspective, our findings suggest that selectively harnessing TGF-β1-SPP1-dependent myeloid programs may provide a way to capture the beneficial aspects of reparative fibrosis while minimizing the risks associated with systemic TGF-β1 exposure. In our models, delivering TGF-β1-conditioned macrophages within a defined therapeutic window improved survival and reduced aortic damage without detectable increases in cardiac fibrosis, indicating that macrophage-based engagement of this pathway may help to focus fibrosis to sites of mechanical vulnerability. Nonetheless, the broader safety profile of such approaches, including potential off-target fibrotic effects in other organs and interactions with background cardiovascular medications, will require careful evaluation in future studies.
This study has several limitations. First, the clinical associations between TGF-β1, monocytic SPP1 expression, low-inflammatory monocyte subsets, and clinical outcomes are observational and based on a modest sample size, demonstrating association rather than causation and potentially subject to residual confounding from background medications and comorbidities. Second, while our data support a role for SPP1high macrophages in enhancing structural stability, currently available AD models lack cell-type-specific fate-mapping tools, precluding definitive in vivo proof of direct monocyte-to-SPP1high macrophage conversion. In addition, although adoptive transfer experiments support a functional role, murine models may not fully recapitulate the cellular complexity and disease heterogeneity of human aortic dissection. Third, while single-cell transcriptomic analyses provide high-resolution characterization of macrophage heterogeneity, transcriptional profiles do not fully capture post-transcriptional regulation and functional states. Finally, mechanistic experiments were primarily conducted in hypertension-associated AD models, which may limit generalizability to other etiologies and broader patient populations. Larger prospective clinical studies and further mechanistic investigations are warranted to validate the translational implications of targeting this pathway.

## Conclusions
Our findings suggest a potential TGF-β1-SPP1 axis that may contribute to vascular protection in AD through immune programming of SPP1high macrophages with fibrogenic features. These observations provide a context-dependent view of fibrosis and highlight a myeloid-derived cell population with translational potential for immunotherapy in acute aortic syndromes.
