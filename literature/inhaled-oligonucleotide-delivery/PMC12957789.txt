## Abstract

## Introduction
Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is a common enterovirus-induced illness primarily affecting children under 5 years of age. Enterovirus 71 (EV71) is a major causative agent associated with severe HFMD that can lead to fatal neurological complications. Despite available vaccines, no antiviral therapy with a clearly defined molecular mechanism has received approval. While most antiviral agents target viral components, host-targeting antiviral approaches remain underexplored. We first identified voxtalisib, a dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor, through compound screening as a potential inhibitor of EV71 replication; however, the underlying mechanism remains unclear.

## Objectives
To identify novel host-targeted antiviral candidates and to investigate the antiviral mechanism of voxtalisib against EV71 and other enteroviruses.

## Methods
Voxtalisib was first identified as a potent anti-EV71 compound through in vitro phenotypic screening. Follow-up experiments in rhabdomyosarcoma (RD) cells were used to assess the effect of voxtalisib on EV71 replication, and proteomic analysis identified its molecular targets. Voxtalisib’s antiviral activity was also assessed against Coxsackie B viruses (CVB3, CVB4-5, and CVB4-7) and Echovirus 11 (Echo11). In vivo, suckling ICR mice were treated with voxtalisib. Survival rates, viral loads, and histopathological changes were subsequently evaluated.

## Results
Proteomics analysis identified Ras-related nuclear protein (RAN) as a key host factor in EV71 replication. EV71 infection significantly upregulated RAN, while voxtalisib suppressed RAN. Mechanistically, RAN regulates nuclear–cytoplasmic transport of phosphorylated STAT1/2 (p-STAT1/2), affecting the interferon (IFN)-mediated antiviral response. Consequently, downregulating RAN expression enhanced the nuclear retention of p-STAT1/2 and upregulated interferon-stimulated genes expression, ultimately reducing EV71 replication. In vivo, voxtalisib improved survival, decreased viral loads, and alleviated organ damage in EV71-infected ICR suckling mice. Similar RAN-dependent p-STAT nuclear retention and antiviral effects were also observed against CVB and Echo11, confirming voxtalisib’s broad-spectrum antiviral potential.

## Conclusion
RAN is a novel antiviral host target that indirectly mediates EV71 replication by regulating the nuclear–cytoplasmic transport of p-STAT1/2. Voxtalisib effectively restores IFN–STAT signaling by modulating RAN, offering a promising host-directed antiviral strategy against enteroviruses.

## Introduction
Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) remains a significant global health concern, particularly in Asia, where it predominantly affects children under 5 years of age , , . HFMD is caused by various enteroviruses, most notably, enterovirus 71 (EV71), associated with severe neurological complications, including encephalitis and aseptic meningitis, and, in rare cases, mortality , , . Despite EV71 vaccination reducing prevalence, its efficacy is limited by viral diversity and immune evasion . No antiviral therapy with a well-defined mechanism is approved for HFMD, leaving treatment reliant on supportive care .
For decades, significant efforts have been devoted to developing direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) that target EV71 proteins, such as 2A, 2C, 3C, and 3D , . However, these approaches are often limited by narrow specificity, the rapid emergence of drug resistance, and a lack of broad-spectrum efficacy . In contrast, host-targeting antivirals (HTAs), which modulate host factors essential for viral replication, offer broader antiviral potential and a lower propensity for resistance , , , . Yet, no specific host-targeting antiviral therapy has been approved for HFMD treatment, primarily due to the lack of well-defined host targets.
RAN has emerged as a potential antiviral host factor of interest due to its critical role in regulating nucleocytoplasmic transport and cell cycle progression , , , , . In particular, enteroviruses, including EV71, exploit RAN-mediated pathways to facilitate the nuclear transport of viral proteins—such as 3D/3CD—and essential host factors, including heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein K (hnRNP K), thereby promoting viral replication , , . However, it remains unclear whether RAN affects host antiviral responses beyond its function in viral replication.
Given the critical role of nucleocytoplasmic transport in immune signaling, RAN may also modulate interferon (IFN)-mediated antiviral responses. In the IFN–signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT)1/2 pathway, phosphorylated STAT1/2 forms the interferon-stimulated gene factor 3 (ISGF3) complex, translocates into the nucleus, and induces the expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), which combat enterovirus infection by inhibiting viral entry, replication, and assembly , , , . Meanwhile, EV71 employs strategies to evade IFN signaling. For instance, its 2A and 3C proteases cleave host proteins involved in p-STAT1/2 nuclear translocation, diminishing ISGs expression and weakening antiviral immunity , , . Hence, RAN may play a dual role in EV71 pathogenesis: facilitating viral protein transport while modulating IFN–STAT signaling.
Through high-throughput screening, we identified voxtalisib (also called SAR245409 or XL765)—a phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) dual inhibitor used in cancer therapy , , —as a small-molecule compound with significant in vitro and in vivo antiviral activity against EV71.
Despite the growing interest in HTAs, the role of RAN in modulating IFN–STAT signaling and antiviral immunity remains uncharacterized. In this study, we investigated whether RAN functions as a key host immune regulator during EV71 infection by influencing p-STAT1/2 nuclear transport and ISGs expression. Furthermore, we assessed whether voxtalisib suppresses EV71 replication by restoring RAN-mediated antiviral responses and enhancing IFN–STAT signaling. Voxtalisib’s antiviral activity was also assessed against CVB3, CVB4-5, CVB4-7 and Echo11. By addressing these questions, this research aims to offer new insights that could lead to host-targeted antiviral strategies against HFMD and other enterovirus infections.

## Material and methods

## Cell culture, viruses, compounds, and antibodies
RD (human rhabdomyosarcoma), HeLa (human cervical cancer), and Vero (African green monkey kidney) cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMED; Cytiva HyClone, Logan, UT, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) and maintained at 37 ℃ in a 5 % CO2 incubator. All cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and tested for Mycoplasma contamination using a PCR-based Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Enterovirus strains, including EV71 (Xiangyang-Hubei-09 strain) (GenBank accession no. JN230523.1), Coxsackieviruses B3 (CVB3), B4-5 (CVB4-5), B4-7 (CVB4-7), and Echo11, were used. A small-molecule library comprising 6000 compounds was obtained from Selleck Chemicals (Cat: L1700; Houston, TX, USA). Leptomycin B was purchased from MedChemExpress (Cat: HY-16909; Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA). IFN-α was sourced from Novoprotein (Cat: C005; Suzhou, China). All compounds were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide for antiviral experiments. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-β-actin (Cat: 20536-1-AP; Proteintech), anti-RAN (Cat: 67500-1-Ig; Proteintech), and anti-EV71 VP1 (Cat: PAB7631; Abnova, Taiwan, China).

## RNA overexpression and RAN interference in EV71 infection
RAN overexpression in RD cells was achieved by transfecting the pcDNA3.1(+)-3 × Flag-RAN plasmid using Lipofectamine® 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) for 6 h. The medium was then replaced with fresh DMEM containing 10 % FBS. After 24 h, the overexpression efficacy was validated using western blotting. Subsequently, cells were infected with EV71 (multiplicity of infection [MOI] = 0.1) for 20 h.
For RAN knockdown, RD cells were transfected with siRNAs targeting the RAN mRNA sequence (Sangon, Shanghai, China) using Lipofectamine 2000. The siRNA sequences used in this study are provided in . After 24 h, knockdown efficiency was validated using western blotting, followed by infection with EV71 (MOI = 0.1) for 20 h. RAN and VP1 mRNA and protein expression levels were analyzed using RT-qPCR and western blotting, respectively.

## Plaque assay and median tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) assay
Plaque and TCID50 assays were conducted to quantify infectious viral particles, as described previously . Briefly, 1 × 106 Vero cells were seeded per well in 6-well plates, with three replicates per condition. Viral suspensions were prepared by performing ten-fold serial dilutions in DMEM containing 2 % FBS. After a 2-h of incubation at 37 ℃, the inoculum was replaced with DMEM containing 0.8 % low melting-point agarose (NeoFroxx GmbH, Einhausen, Germany) and 2 % FBS. The plates were incubated at 37℃ for 72 h, fixed with 4 % formalin, and stained with 0.5 % crystal violet (Biosharp, Shanghai, China) for 30 min. The plaques were counted manually.

## Mouse infection model
To evaluate the antiviral efficacy of voxtalisib, 2-day-old ICR mice (n = 10/group) were intraperitoneally injected with 8 × 106 plaque-forming units (PFU) of EV71. After 2 h, the mice were administered intraperitoneal injections of voxtalisib (0.25 mg/kg) daily, while the control group was administered a vehicle solution. Animal health was monitored daily, including weight loss, mobility, dehydration, and respiratory distress assessments. Upon a weight loss exceeding 25 %, severe dehydration, or significant distress, mice were humanely euthanized via CO2 inhalation. On day 2 post-infection, viral loads in tissues (the brain, heart, liver, spleen, and front legs) were measured using RT-qPCR, and pathological changes were assessed via hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Survival rate and body weight changes were monitored to assess the protective effects of voxtalisib.

## Compliance with ethics requirements
Pregnant ICR mice were obtained from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and maintained in a pathogen-free environment. All experiments involving mice were approved by the institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (WIVA38202103).

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0; GraphPad Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences among groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by appropriate post hoc tests, where applicable, and using Student’s t-test for pairwise comparisons. For survival analysis, Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated and compared using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. For body weight changes, a two-way repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to assess differences between groups over time, followed by Bonferroni’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value < 0.05.

## Results

## Voxtalisib inhibits EV71 replication in vitro

## High-throughput screening and cytotoxicity
In this study, a high-throughput cytopathic effect (CPE) screening assay was conducted, revealing that voxtalisib exhibited potent inhibitory activity against EV71-induced cytopathic effects ( A). The initial cell-based CPE assay revealed that voxtalisib significantly reduced the cytopathic effects of EV71 infection. Subsequent MTT assays in RD and HeLa cells indicated that the CC50 of voxtalisib was 6.533 μmol/L and 13.573 μmol/L, respectively ( B). In comparison, the CC50 values of ribavirin in the same cell lines were 104.455 μmol/L and 114.681 μmol/L, respectively ( C). Despite its lower CC50 than that of ribavirin, voxtalisib retained a favorable safety margin, as its effective antiviral concentrations were significantly lower than its cytotoxic threshold, suggesting a sufficient therapeutic window for further investigation. Consistently, MTT assays demonstrated minimal cytotoxicity at concentrations up to 0.8 μmol/L, further supporting the safety profile of voxtalisib at tis effective doses ( ).
 

## Antiviral efficacy in EV71-infected cells
To directly assess the effect of voxtalisib on viral replication, we examined the expression of VP1—a key structural component of the EV71 viral capsid—via western blot analysis. EV71-infected RD cells showed significantly elevated VP1 levels in the absence of treatment, confirming successful viral replication ( D). Meanwhile, increasing concentrations of voxtalisib (0.0064–4 μmol/L) decreased VP1 expression in a dose-dependent manner, with near-complete suppression at 4 μmol/L. In comparison, ribavirin treatment exhibited a weaker inhibitory effect on VP1 expression, requiring higher concentrations to achieve noticeable suppression.
To further assess the antiviral effect of voxtalisib, we performed immunofluorescence assays targeting EV71 double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) intermediates, a hallmark of viral replication . Untreated EV71-infected RD cells displayed intense dsRNA fluorescence signals, indicating robust viral replication ( E). However, following treatment with increasing voxtalisib concentrations, the dsRNA signals weakened in a dose-dependent manner. At 4 μmol/L, dsRNA expression was nearly undetectable.
Plaque assays were conducted to quantify the antiviral effect of voxtalisib at the level of infectious virus production. In untreated EV71-infected cells, a high density of viral plaques was observed, indicating efficient viral replication and infectivity. Voxtalisib treatment led to a dose-dependent reduction in plaque formation, with a nearly 90 % decrease at 4 μmol/L ( F). Therefore, to further quantify viral infectivity, a TCID50 assay was performed. Viral titers in untreated EV71-infected RD cells remained high. Voxtalisib treatment (0.032–4 μmol/L) significantly reduced viral titers, with near-complete suppression at 4 μmol/L (p < 0.0001; G).
To assess the kinetics of voxtalisib-mediated inhibition of EV71 replication, a time-course study was performed using western blot and RT-qPCR to monitor VP1 protein and viral RNA (vRNA) levels over time. In untreated EV71-infected cells, VP1 expression progressively increased, peaking 18–20 h post-infection (hpi) ( H). Voxtalisib (4 μmol/L) treatment significantly inhibited VP1 expression at 18 and 20 hpi, whereas ribavirin induced only partial suppression at these time points. Similarly, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that voxtalisib markedly reduced vRNA levels at 20 hpi, with a greater reduction than that with ribavirin (p < 0.0001; I).
These findings indicate that voxtalisib exhibits strong and prolonged antiviral activity against EV71, effectively inhibits viral replication at the protein and RNA levels, and outperforms ribavirin in multiple antiviral assays.

## Voxtalisib primarily inhibits EV71 during the post-entry phase
To assess whether voxtalisib exerts a direct virucidal effect, EV71 particles were pre-incubated with different voxtalisib concentrations at 4 °C for 1 h before infecting RD cells ( A). No significant difference was observed in EV71 viral genomic RNA levels, indicating no virucidal effects ( B). Additionally, no substantial differences were observed between the groups pre-incubated with and without voxtalisib, suggesting that it does not exert direct virucidal activity against EV71.
 To determine whether voxtalisib interferes with the viral attachment process, we conducted a binding assay in which EV71 was incubated with RD cells at 4 °C for 1 h, allowing virus-cell surface interactions to occur, followed by treatment with different concentrations of voxtalisib ( C). RT-qPCR analysis showed that vRNA levels remained relatively unchanged at most concentrations, excluding a slight decrease at 0.0064 μmol/L ( D). However, at the clinically relevant concentration (4 μmol/L), no significant reduction in viral binding was observed, suggesting that voxtalisib does not significantly impact EV71 attachment to host cell receptors.
To further examine whether voxtalisib interferes with the viral entry process, RD cells were first incubated with EV71 at 4 °C for 1 h to allow binding, followed by incubation with voxtalisib at 37 °C for 1 h to promote viral internalization ( C). At 0.8 μmol/L, voxtalisib significantly reduced vRNA levels compared with the untreated control (p < 0.05; E). However, at higher concentrations (4 μmol/L), this inhibitory effect was not significant. Thus, voxtalisib may exert a weak inhibitory effect on viral entry at lower concentrations but does not function primarily as an entry inhibitor.
Next, we investigated whether voxtalisib disrupts late-stage EV71 replication. RD cells were infected with EV71 for 2 h before treatment with voxtalisib ( F). The RT-qPCR analysis revealed a significant, dose-dependent reduction in EV71 vRNA levels in voxtalisib-treated cells, particularly at 0.032, 0.16, 0.8, and 4 μmol/L, confirming potent post-entry inhibition ( G). Notably, at 4 μmol/L, vRNA levels were reduced by over 90. Pharmacodynamic analysis showed an IC50 of 0.1578 μmol/L and a selectivity index (SI) of 41.4, suggesting that voxtalisib exerts strong antiviral activity with a favorable safety profile ( H).
These findings indicate that voxtalisib does not act as a direct virucidal agent, nor does it significantly interfere with viral binding or entry. Instead, it predominantly inhibits EV71 replication during the post-entry phase, likely by targeting viral transcription or translation processes.

## Voxtalisib inhibits EV71 replication by downregulating RAN
A proteomic analysis was conducted to investigate the targets of voxtalisib in the late invasion stage of EV71. Four experimental groups were established: control (M), infection (I), treatment (T), and drug (D). To refine candidate selection, we conducted a four-way Venn analysis integrating our proteomic data with disease-related gene sets. Specifically, the four datasets represented: proteins altered by EV71 infection (I vs. M), proteins modulated by voxtalisib during infection (T vs. I), proteins affected by voxtalisib in uninfected cells (D vs. M), and host genes associated with “HFMD” or “enterovirus” obtained from the GeneCards database. Among the differentially expressed proteins, RAN was identified as a common factor across multiple datasets ( A), suggesting its potential role in EV71 replication and voxtalisib’s antiviral mechanism.
 To confirm whether EV71 infection modulates RAN expression, RD cells were infected with increasing MOIs (0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, and 0.1), and RAN levels were analyzed via RT-qPCR and western blotting ( B). RAN mRNA and protein levels significantly increased in an MOI-dependent manner, indicating that EV71 infection directly induced RAN upregulation.
To assess the functional role of RAN in EV71 infection, RD cells were transfected with increasing amounts of a Flag-RAN overexpression plasmid (0.5, 1, and 2 μg) before EV71 infection (MOI = 0.1; C). RT-qPCR and western blot analysis revealed that RAN overexpression significantly increased EV71 vRNA and VP1 protein levels. Meanwhile, transfecting RD cells with three different siRNAs targeting RAN, followed by EV71 infection (MOI = 0.5; D) significantly reduced vRNA expression and VP1 protein abundance. This suggested that RAN was required for effective EV71 replication.
To investigate whether voxtalisib affects RAN expression, RD cells were treated with increasing concentrations of voxtalisib (0.0064–4 μmol/L) for 20 h ( E). RT-qPCR and western blot results demonstrated a concentration-dependent decrease in RAN expression, with the most significant reduction observed at 4 μmol/L. Additionally, treating RD cells with 4 μmol/L voxtalisib for different time intervals (0, 8, 12, 18, and 24 h) revealed progressive RAN mRNA and protein expression decline ( F), with the most significant inhibition observed at 18 and 24 h. These results suggest that voxtalisib suppresses RAN expression in a dose- and time-dependent manner.
Finally, we assessed whether voxtalisib treatment reduces RAN levels in EV71-infected cells ( G). RD cells were infected with EV71 (MOI = 0.1) for 2 h, followed by treatment with voxtalisib (0.0064–4 μmol/L) for 20 h. Voxtalisib significantly reduced RAN mRNA and protein expression in infected cells, correlating with decreased VP1 protein levels. These findings indicate that voxtalisib inhibits EV71 replication by downregulating RAN.
To further validate that RAN is essential for the antiviral activity of voxtalisib, we performed a rescue experiment using RAN knockdown. RD cells were transfected with si-NC or si-RAN for 24 h and subsequently infected with EV71 (MOI = 0.5), with or without voxtalisib treatment (4 μmol/L, 20 h). As shown in H, Western blot analysis revealed that RAN knockdown alone significantly reduced VP1 expression compared to the si-NC group. Notably, voxtalisib treatment did not further reduce VP1 levels in RAN-deficient cells, indicating that voxtalisib’s antiviral effect is largely dependent on the presence of RAN. These results provide functional evidence that RAN is a critical host factor for EV71 replication and that voxtalisib exerts its antiviral effect primarily through RAN downregulation.
Overall, these results demonstrate that EV71 infection induces RAN expression, which enhances viral replication, while voxtalisib suppresses RAN expression, reducing EV71 replication.

## Voxtalisib inhibits EV71 replication by restoring IFN–STAT signaling through RAN regulation

## Downregulation of RAN expression modulates p-STAT1/2 nuclear and cytoplasmic transport
Given the critical role of RAN in nucleocytoplasmic transport, we hypothesized that voxtalisib exerts its antiviral effects against EV71 by modulating the RAN-mediated distribution of phosphorylated STAT1 and STAT2 (p-STAT1/2). To test this, we performed nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation and western blot analysis in RD cells. EV71 infection significantly reduced nuclear p-STAT1/2 levels while increasing their cytoplasmic accumulation, indicating a potential viral immune evasion mechanism. However, voxtalisib treatment effectively restored nuclear p-STAT1/2 localization ( A).
 To further investigate whether IFN signaling amplifies voxtalisib-mediated effects, we treated RD cells with IFN-α and voxtalisib. Notably, co-treatment resulted in greater nuclear accumulation of p-STAT1/2 compared to that with IFN-α alone ( B). Additionally, RAN knockdown significantly decreased EV71 vRNA and VP1 protein levels, whereas RAN overexpression had the opposite effect. Voxtalisib-treated cells displayed a more significant reduction in viral replication than did ribavirin-treated cells, underscoring the potent antiviral activity of voxtalisib through RAN inhibition ( H). To determine whether voxtalisib-induced RAN downregulation occurs through the canonical PI3K/mTOR pathway, we conducted a comparative analysis using two well-established pathway inhibitors: LY294002 (targeting PI3K) and Rapamycin (targeting mTORC1). Western blot analysis confirmed that both inhibitors effectively suppressed their respective targets (p-PI3K and p-mTOR), indicating successful pathway inhibition. However, neither compound significantly altered RAN expression. In contrast, voxtalisib markedly reduced RAN protein levels, despite exerting only modest effects on PI3K/mTOR signaling components. These results suggest that RAN downregulation by voxtalisib is mediated through a noncanonical, PI3K/mTOR-independent mechanism ( ).

## Nuclear p-STAT1/2 accumulation boosts ISGs expression
ISGs play a crucial role in antiviral defense by amplifying IFN signaling. Given the observed nuclear retention of p-STAT1/2 following voxtalisib treatment, we examined its impact on ISGs expression. Heatmap analysis revealed that voxtalisib treatment significantly upregulated multiple ISGs, including but not limited to OAS2, OAS3, IFIT1, ISG20, and MX1 ( C) further supporting the hypothesis that RAN inhibition strengthens IFN-mediated antiviral responses.
To determine whether voxtalisib affects upstream interferon production, we measured IFN-α and IFN-β levels in EV71- or poly(I:C)-stimulated cells, with or without voxtalisib. Voxtalisib did not significantly alter IFN expression at either the mRNA or protein level under any condition ( ), suggesting its antiviral action acts downstream of interferon induction.

## Leptomycin B validation of voxtalisib’s antiviral mechanism
To further confirm that voxtalisib inhibits the nuclear export of p-STAT1/2 via RAN, we employed Leptomycin B (LMB), a chromosomal region maintenance 1 (CRM1)-specific inhibitor that blocks nuclear export. The experiment was divided into four groups: Control group, voxtalisib group, LMB group, and LMB + voxtalisib group. The Control group received DMSO at the same volume as voxtalisib, while the LMB and LMB + voxtalisib groups were treated with LMB (20 nM) for 4 h before sample collection. All groups were stimulated with IFN-α (10 ng/mL, 15 min before collection) to activate the IFN-STAT signaling pathway. Western blot analysis of nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions showed that in the presence of LMB, p-STAT1/2 exhibited increased nuclear accumulation, mimicking the effects of voxtalisib. Notably, combined treatment with voxtalisib and LMB did not further enhance nuclear retention of p-STAT1/2, suggesting that voxtalisib exerted its effect through the CRM1 pathway by modulating RAN ( D). These findings indicate that voxtalisib-induced RAN inhibition increases intranuclear p-STAT1/2 accumulation, preventing viral immune evasion.

## Voxtalisib does not affect nuclear translocation of IRF3, IRF7, or NF-κB
To determine whether voxtalisib modulates the nuclear translocation of other innate immune transcription factors, we assessed the localization of phosphorylated IRF3, IRF7, and NF-κB using nuclear–cytoplasmic fractionation. RD cells were treated under four conditions: mock (DMSO), voxtalisib (4 μmol/L, 20 h), IFN-α (10 ng/mL, 15 min), and voxtalisib + IFN-α (20 h pretreatment with voxtalisib followed by 15 min of IFN-α stimulation). As shown in E, IFN-α stimulation robustly induced nuclear accumulation of p-IRF3, p-IRF7, and p-NF-κB. In contrast, voxtalisib alone failed to promote the nuclear translocation or phosphorylation of these transcription factors. Additionally, voxtalisib did not further enhance their nuclear localization when combined with IFN-α, despite a significant reduction in RAN expression. These data suggest that voxtalisib does not regulate the IRF3/7 or NF-κB pathways. Its antiviral effect is therefore unlikely to involve these transcriptional responses.

## RAN overexpression reverses the antiviral effects of voxtalisib
To validate that RAN is the primary target of voxtalisib, we conducted rescue experiments by overexpressing RAN in EV71-infected RD cells. Overexpression of RAN significantly increased vRNA and VP1 protein levels, indicating enhanced EV71 replication. Moreover, RAN overexpression effectively reversed the antiviral effects of voxtalisib, restoring VP1 protein expression to levels comparable to those in untreated EV71-infected cells ( F). Consistent with these findings, plaque assays revealed that while voxtalisib treatment significantly reduced EV71 plaque formation, RAN overexpression reversed this effect, leading to increased viral infectivity ( G). These data confirm that the antiviral activity of voxtalisib is dependent on RAN inhibition, and targeting RAN is crucial in limiting EV71 replication.
Collectively, these findings establish that RAN plays a central role in EV71 replication by modulating the nucleocytoplasmic transport of p-STAT1/2. Voxtalisib inhibits RAN expression, preventing the nuclear export of p-STAT1/2, promoting ISGs activation, and enhancing IFN-mediated antiviral responses. Meanwhile, RAN overexpression abolishes the antiviral effects of voxtalisib, validating RAN as a critical target for antiviral intervention.
To further elucidate the mechanism by which voxtalisib inhibits EV71 replication, we investigated whether RAN physically interacts with phosphorylated STAT1 and STAT2. Co-immunoprecipitation assays in RD cells demonstrated that FLAG-tagged RAN co-precipitated with both p-STAT1 and p-STAT2, whereas no such interaction was observed in the IgG control group ( H). These results provide direct biochemical evidence for a physical association between RAN and p-STAT1/2, further supporting the model that voxtalisib enhances antiviral signaling by promoting RAN-dependent nuclear retention of these transcription factors.
To assess whether voxtalisib affects upstream immune evasion mechanisms, we examined the cleavage of MAVS and RIG-I by the EV71-encoded 2A and 3C proteases. As shown in , voxtalisib did not significantly inhibit the protease-mediated cleavage of MAVS or RIG-I, suggesting that its antiviral activity is more likely driven by RAN-mediated enhancement of IFN–STAT1/2 signaling rather than interference with viral immune evasion.

## Voxtalisib alleviates virus replication and pathogenesis in an EV71-infected mouse model
To corroborate our in vitro observations, 2-day-old suckling mice were intraperitoneally injected with EV71 (8 × 106 PFU), followed by daily treatment with 0.25 mg/kg voxtalisib, ribavirin (20 mg/kg) or vehicle for 8 days ( A). Voxtalisib significantly improved survival (∼80 %) and weight gain, outperforming ribavirin (∼30 % survival), which showed only partial protection ( B, C).
 To further evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of voxtalisib, we measured EV71 vRNA levels in the heart, liver, spleen, brain, and front legs. Voxtalisib-treated mice had significantly reduced viral loads across all analyzed tissues, demonstrating effective suppression of in vivo EV71 replication ( D–H).
H&E staining revealed significant histopathological changes in EV71-infected mice, particularly in the liver and spleen ( I). In the liver, hepatocyte edema increased, characterized by cell swelling and loose cytoplasmic staining. Mild vacuolar degeneration was observed, with a few small round vacuoles appearing in the cytoplasm. The liver sinusoids were also filled with numerous hematopoietic cells, although no obvious tissue necrosis was detected. In the spleen, necrotic cell fragments were present, alongside a few megakaryocytes in the red pulp and occasional granulocyte infiltration, with severe tissue edema. In contrast, following treatment with voxtalisib, the histology of multiple tissues was improved, demonstrating the protective role of voxtalisib in mitigating EV71-induced tissue damage. These findings suggest that voxtalisib treatment reduces mortality in EV71-infected mice and lowers EV71 vRNA levels and titers while alleviating infection-induced histopathological damage. These qualitative findings were further supported by histopathological scoring presented in .

## In vivo toxicity and pharmacokinetic evaluation of voxtalisib in suckling mice
To assess the in vivo safety of voxtalisib, 2-day-old ICR suckling mice were intraperitoneally administered voxtalisib at 0.25 mg/kg daily for six consecutive days. Behavioral and physiological parameters—including grooming behavior, activity level, fur condition, posture, and signs of distress—were monitored throughout the treatment period. No mortality or observable adverse effects were recorded.
To further evaluate organ toxicity, histopathological examination was performed on heart, liver, spleen, brain, and front leg muscle tissues collected at daily intervals (Day 0 to Day 6). H&E staining revealed no signs of necrosis, edema, or inflammatory cell infiltration in the heart, spleen, brain, or skeletal muscle. Mild hepatic steatosis was observed in some liver sections ( ).
 To characterize the pharmacokinetics of voxtalisib, liver samples were harvested at 0, 15 min, 30 min, 2 h, 4 h, 8 h, and 24 h after a single intraperitoneal injection (0.25 mg/kg). High-performance liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS) detected voxtalisib in liver tissues from 15 min to 8 h post-injection, with concentrations ranging from 0.0248 to 0.0305 ng/0.1 g ( ). No voxtalisib was detected at 0 min or 24 h. These results confirm rapid hepatic uptake and clearance of the drug in suckling mice.
Collectively, these findings support the in vivo safety profile of voxtalisib at 0.25 mg/kg in suckling mice and establish this dose as an appropriate therapeutic concentration for antiviral efficacy studies.

## Voxtalisib exerts broad anti-enteroviral properties
Finally, we investigated whether the in vitro RAN-targeting mechanism of voxtalisib is effective against other enteroviruses, including CVB3 ( A), CVB4-5 ( B), CVB4-7 ( C), and Echo11 ( D), using the same treatment procedure as for EV71 treatment. Voxtalisib treatment of all HeLa cells infected with the various CVB serotypes and Echo11 strains yielded a dose-dependent decrease in viral mRNA levels. TCID50 assays confirmed that voxtalisib significantly reduced viral titers dose-dependently ( E–H). To further validate the conservation of the RAN-STAT1/2 regulatory pathways across enteroviruses, we performed nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation and Western blot analysis in RD cells infected with CVB3 and Echo11. In line with our observations in EV71-infected cells, voxtalisib treatment enhanced nuclear accumulation of p-STAT1/2 and decreased RAN protein levels in both models ( ). These findings suggest that voxtalisib restores IFN–STAT signaling via RAN downregulation not only in EV71 but also in other clinically relevant enteroviruses. Overall, these findings suggest that voxtalisib possesses broad-spectrum activity against multiple enteroviruses.
 In addition, voxtalisib significantly inhibited RSV replication in vitro at concentrations ≥0.8 μmol/L, further supporting its potential as a broad-spectrum antiviral agent beyond enteroviruses ( ).

## Discussion
The results of this study suggest that voxtalisib potentially inhibits EV71 replication in vitro, primarily during the post-entry stage. Mechanistically, RAN inhibition reactivates the IFN signaling pathway and restricts EV71 replication. Moreover, voxtalisib exhibits dose-dependent antiviral activity against multiple enteroviruses beyond EV71, including CVB3, CVB4-5, CVB4-7, and Echo11, as evidenced by marked reductions in viral RNA levels and infectious titers. In vivo, voxtalisib significantly reduces the EV71 viral load and mitigates disease manifestation, validating its potential as an antiviral therapeutic.
Voxtalisib was identified as a highly promising HTA. Additionally, EV71 infection significantly upregulates RAN expression, while voxtalisib downregulates RAN expression regardless of EV71 infection status. RAN is a key GTPase in maintaining nuclear structure, regulating mRNA processing, mediating nucleocytoplasmic transport, and controlling the cell cycle , . Elevated RAN expression promotes the nuclear transport of EV71 3D, 3CD, and host hnRNPK proteins , enhancing viral replication. Our in vitro analyses confirmed that EV71 infection markedly upregulated RAN expression. Mechanistically, voxtalisib downregulated RAN expression in a dose and time-dependent manner, accompanied by a significant accumulation of p-STAT1/2 in the nucleus. In this way, IFN–STAT signaling was restored, and ISG expression, including OAS3, OAS2, and MX1, was upregulated, leading to EV71 suppression, which combat enterovirus infection by inhibiting viral entry, replication, and assembly , , , . Notably, combining voxtalisib with IFN⍺ further enhanced the antiviral immune response, highlighting the potential of voxtalisib as a novel HTA candidate , . Accordingly, we defined RAN as a critical target for EV71 infection and the antiviral activity of voxtalisib. This conclusion is further supported by our co-immunoprecipitation results demonstrating that RAN physically associates with p-STAT1/2 in RD cells, suggesting a direct regulatory role in STAT1/2 nuclear retention. Additionally, we confirmed that voxtalisib does not enhance IFN-α or IFN-β production under EV71 or poly(I:C) stimulation, suggesting its antiviral effect operates downstream of interferon induction. In addition to STAT1/2, we examined whether voxtalisib affects the nuclear translocation of IRF3, IRF7, and NF-κB. Our results showed that voxtalisib, either alone or combined with IFN-α, did not further increase the nuclear accumulation of these factors beyond that induced by IFN-α alone, despite reduced RAN expression. These findings suggest that voxtalisib acts selectively through the IFN–STAT1/2 pathways without broadly activating other innate immune pathways.
In this study, we further investigated the regulatory mechanism by which voxtalisib promotes the nuclear retention of p-STAT1/2. Although both voxtalisib and the CRM1 inhibitor LMB enhanced p-STAT1/2 nuclear accumulation, only voxtalisib significantly downregulated RAN expression. These results suggest that voxtalisib modulates nuclear export through a RAN-dependent but CRM1-independent mechanism. Collectively, these findings support a distinct mode of action for voxtalisib as a host-targeted antiviral agent that acts upstream of nuclear transport regulation. We confirmed that EV71 proteases 2A and 3C cleave MAVS and RIG-I, and that voxtalisib does not prevent this cleavage, suggesting that its antiviral effect is unlikely to be mediated by interference with viral immune evasion. This supports the notion that voxtalisib primarily exerts its effect through the RAN–STAT1/2 pathway. Notably, voxtalisib downregulated RAN expression independently of classical PI3K/mTOR signaling. While LY294002 and rapamycin effectively inhibited their respective targets (p-PI3K and p-mTOR), neither compound altered RAN levels. In contrast, voxtalisib significantly reduced RAN expression despite only modest inhibition of these upstream kinases. These findings suggest a distinct mechanism by which voxtalisib promotes p-STAT1/2 nuclear retention and enhances antiviral signaling. Finally, we confirmed the functional relevance of RAN by demonstrating that overexpression of RAN significantly reversed the antiviral effect of voxtalisib by restoring vRNA levels and VP1 expression to near-control levels, validating the critical role of RAN in EV71 replication. In line with this, RAN knockdown alone significantly suppressed EV71 replication, and voxtalisib failed to exert additional antiviral effects in RAN-deficient cells. These results provide further evidence that the antiviral activity of voxtalisib is RAN-dependent.
In vivo, voxtalisib improved survival, maintained weight gain, and reduced viral loads in multiple tissues of EV71-infected mice while alleviating liver and spleen damage. Notably, in parallel experiments, voxtalisib conferred greater protection in terms of survival and body weight compared to ribavirin (20 mg/kg), a commonly used antiviral agent against EV71. Given that severe HFMD involves systemic viral dissemination and immune-mediated pathology, these findings suggest that voxtalisib mitigates disease severity by limiting viral replication and inflammation. As current HFMD treatment is primarily supportive, voxtalisib shows promise as a potential antiviral therapeutic candidate, particularly for severe cases with neurological involvement, warranting further investigation . Importantly, the 0.25 mg/kg dose used in our suckling mouse model is much lower than the 30 mg/kg dose used in previous oncology studies , , . Nonetheless, HPLC–MS analysis showed that voxtalisib remained detectable in the liver from 15 min to 8 h post-injection, with full clearance by 24 h—closely matching earlier pharmacokinetic data. These results confirm the biological relevance and favorable safety of our dosing strategy in suckling mice.
Our findings highlight voxtalisib as a HTA that exerts broad-spectrum activity by modulating RAN , , a critical host factor for viral replication. To confirm the broader applicability of this mechanism, we tested voxtalisib in CVB3 and Echo11 infection models. It consistently enhanced nuclear p-STAT1/2 accumulation and reduced RAN expression, supporting the conservation of the RAN–STAT1/2 pathway across enteroviruses. Moreover, voxtalisib inhibited RSV replication in a dose-dependent manner, suggesting antiviral activity beyond enteroviruses. Targeting essential host factors like RAN provides a durable antiviral strategy with a lower risk of resistance and potential synergy with other treatments immune defense across diverse organisms . The conserved role of RAN in nucleocytoplasmic transport further supports its significance in antiviral defense. Overexpression of AtRAN1 in Arabidopsis thaliana promotes elongation growth and enhances resistance against Pseudomonas syringae DC3000 . This suggests that RAN-mediated nucleocytoplasmic transport is a key regulator of the balance between growth and defense. These cross-species findings further underscore the conserved function of RAN and provide a solid theoretical foundation for our host-targeted antiviral strategy based on modulating RAN activity , .
Despite these promising findings, our study has some limitations. First, although the suckling mouse model provides valuable insights into EV71 pathogenesis, it does not fully recapitulate the complexity of the human immune response. Future studies should assess the efficacy of voxtalisib in adult mouse models or non-human primates to enhance clinical relevance. Second, our study did not directly assess whether voxtalisib physically binds to RAN. While our data demonstrate that voxtalisib downregulates RAN expression and modulates the IFN–STAT1/2 signaling pathway, further studies—such as binding assays or target engagement profiling—are needed to confirm whether RAN is a direct molecular target of voxtalisib.

## Conclusion
The findings of this study confirm that EV71 infection upregulates RAN expression to promote viral replication and immune evasion while demonstrating the feasibility of inhibiting viral replication by downregulating RAN to block viral nuclear export. Specifically, voxtalisib restricts EV71 replication by inhibiting RAN and activating the IFN–STAT pathway, underscoring the potential of HTAs. voxtalisib can also be combined with IFN⍺ or other antiviral agents to enhance its efficacy and combat resistance. However, follow-up studies should further assess the safety profile of voxtalisib in various animal models and clinical contexts. Additionally, optimizing its administration route and dosage may improve its therapeutic efficacy and clinical feasibility. Beyond safety considerations, future investigations should explore the broader role of RAN in immune defense, particularly its function as a potential therapeutic target in emerging viral infections.
This research expands the scope of HTA drug development, reinforcing the importance of host-directed antiviral strategies. Moreover, evaluating combinatorial strategies for voxtalisib and existing immunotherapies could provide insights into potential synergistic effects for treating EV71 and other enteroviruses. Cumulatively, these insights offer a new strategy for developing broad-spectrum, resistance-limited, host-targeted antivirals and provide a solid preclinical foundation for future antiviral drug development.
