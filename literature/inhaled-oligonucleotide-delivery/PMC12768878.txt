## Abstract
Circular RNAs (circRNAs) and m6A RNA methylation play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of intracranial aneurysm (IA); however, their regulatory interplay remains unclear. Through circRNA profiling of 16 IA tissues and 8 superficial temporal arteries, we identified hsa_circ_0103896 as significantly downregulated in IA. RT-qPCR analysis further revealed that decreased circ_0103896 or increased miR-432–5p expression correlated with IA progression. Functional assays demonstrated that circ_0103896 inhibited the phenotypic switch of human brain vascular smooth muscle cells (hBVSMCs) from a contractile to a synthetic state, whereas circ_0103896 knockdown reversed this effect. In vivo, circ_0103896 overexpression alleviated IA severity, whereas its depletion aggravated aneurysm formation. Mechanistically, circ_0103896 functioned as a sponge for miR-432–5p, thereby upregulating FTO, which in turn promoted m6A demethylation of circ_0103896. This process reinforced circ_0103896 expression, forming a positive feedback loop. The circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis thus suppresses hBVSMC phenotypic transformation and mitigates IA progression. These findings reveal a novel epigenetic mechanism involving m6A modification and circRNA–miRNA crosstalk in IA pathogenesis, highlighting circ_0103896 as a potential therapeutic target.
Keywords: Intracranial aneurysm, circRNA, microRNA, m6A modification, Vascular smooth muscle cells, Phenotypic transformation

## Introduction
Intracranial aneurysms (IAs) are pathological dilations of cerebral arteries that pose a substantial risk of rupture and life-threatening subarachnoid hemorrhage [ ]. Although the exact mechanisms underlying IA formation and rupture remain incompletely understood, current evidence suggests that vascular wall pathology, driven by endothelial dysfunction, extracellular matrix remodeling, and phenotypic modulation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), plays a pivotal role in their pathogenesis [ ].
VSMCs play a crucial role in maintaining vascular integrity and are implicated in various cerebrovascular disorders, including IA [ ]. These highly specialized contractile cells are primarily responsible for preserving vascular morphology and regulating blood pressure [ ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that VSMCs possess remarkable plasticity, enabling them to undergo profound phenotypic changes in response to diverse environmental stimuli [ , ]. This adaptability facilitates their transition from a contractile to a synthetic phenotype, a process known as phenotypic switching. The synthetic phenotype is characterized by downregulated expression of contractile markers, such as smooth muscle protein 22α (SM22α) and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), accompanied by upregulated production of inflammatory mediators, including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) [ ]. These proinflammatory and matrix-degrading properties of synthetic VSMCs contribute to extracellular matrix remodeling and medial layer degeneration, which are key processes in the formation and rupture of IAs [ , , , , ].
The regulatory influence of noncoding RNAs on VSMC function is profound, particularly in the context of vascular pathologies [ ]. Circular RNAs (circRNAs), a class of noncoding RNAs characterized by covalently closed loop structures lacking 3′ and 5′ ends, can act as microRNA (miRNA) sponges, thereby modulating VSMC activity in IA [ ]. In IA, hsa_circ_0021001 has been proposed as a potential biomarker, with lower expression levels associated with poorer patient outcomes [ ]. Similarly, expression of circ_DOCK1 is markedly decreased in the arterial wall tissues and VSMCs of patients with IA, where it is known to promote VSMC proliferation [ , ]. Furthermore, reduced circRNA_0020397 expression has been reported to suppress VSMC growth, potentially via upregulation of miR-138, which subsequently downregulates KDR expression [ ].
miRNAs, a subclass of noncoding RNAs approximately 22 nucleotides in length, are single-stranded molecules with compact structures that play key roles in post-transcriptional gene regulation [ ]. They are involved in a wide range of cellular processes and disease mechanisms [ ]. Accumulating evidence indicates that miRNAs contribute substantially to IA development. For instance, miR-29a regulates the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway in IA pathogenesis [ ], while miR-let-7b and miR-200a show markedly altered plasma levels in patients with IA [ ]. miR-370 has been identified as a modulator of human VSMC proliferation in IA [ ], and miR-138 has been shown to influence the proliferation of human pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cells [ ]. Moreover, aberrant miR-432–5p expression has been detected in individuals with IA [ ]. However, the precise role of miR-432–5p in IA initiation and progression as well as the molecular mechanisms underlying its regulatory effects remains largely unexplored.
As one of the most prevalent RNA modifications, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) has been increasingly recognized for its involvement in the formation and progression of IAs [ ]. The m6A regulatory machinery comprises three major groups of proteins: methyltransferases (writers), including RBM15/15B, KIAA1429, WTAP, and METTL3/14/16; demethylases (erasers), including ALKBH5 and FTO; and m6A-binding proteins (readers), including YTHDC1/2, YTHDF1/2/3, IGF2BP1/2/3, several heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (HNRNPs), and eIF3 [ ]. Berulava et al. [ ] demonstrated that cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure are associated with alterations in m6A methylation, which can influence protein expression, underscoring the potential of m6A regulation as a therapeutic target in heart failure. Similarly, another study reported that METTL3 expression is significantly increased in endothelial cells subjected to oscillatory shear stress, accompanied by elevated m6A methylation levels, implicating METTL3-mediated m6A modification in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis and flow-induced vascular injury [ ]. Collectively, these findings highlight m6A methylation as a promising avenue for early diagnosis and therapeutic intervention in vascular diseases. However, the extent and mechanisms of m6A involvement in the development and progression of IAs remain largely unclear.
In this study, we observed a marked reduction in circ_0103896 expression in IA tissues and related cellular models. Functionally, overexpression of hsa_circ_0103896 inhibited the phenotypic switch of human brain VSMCs (hBVSMCs) from a contractile to a synthetic state while suppressing their proliferation, migration, and apoptosis both in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, integrated analyses involving bioinformatics, RNA immunoprecipitation, and rescue experiments revealed that hsa_circ_0103896 functions as a sponge for miR-432–5p, thereby upregulating FTO expression. Furthermore, a negative correlation was identified between circ_0103896 and FTO expression levels in IA cells, suggesting reciprocal regulation. Collectively, our findings identify hsa_circ_0103896 as a potential biomarker for IA and elucidate a circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO feedback loop that modulates IA pathogenesis and progression, offering new insights into its potential therapeutic implications.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Ethics statement
Between June 2017 and March 2019, we enrolled 16 patients diagnosed with IA at Changhai Hospital and 8 age- and sex-matched patients with brain tumors as controls. The maximum aneurysm diameter ranged from 5 to 23 mm. IA tissues were collected during aneurysm clipping surgery, whereas control tissues consisted of superficial temporal arteries (STAs) incidentally resected during craniotomy for tumor exposure. The STA was chosen because it can be ethically and practically obtained without additional risk to patients and has been widely used as a surrogate control in previous aneurysm transcriptomic studies. We acknowledge that the STA differs from intracranial arteries anatomically, hemodynamically, and transcriptionally; however, obtaining unaffected intracranial arterial segments (e.g., Circle of Willis arteries) is not ethically feasible in living patients, and postmortem or bypass donor tissues were unavailable in our setting.
All tissue samples were excised and immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for preservation. Each patient or donor constituted one biological replicate. When multiple sections or imaging fields were obtained from the same specimen, measurements were averaged to yield a single value per patient; technical replicates were used solely for quality control and were not treated as independent samples. Demographic and clinical variables (age, sex, hypertension, smoking status, medication use, aneurysm rupture status, aneurysm size, and aneurysm location) were obtained from medical records and are summarized in .

2.2.

## Analysis of circRNA
Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples of 8 controls and 16 patients with IAs. RNA quality was verified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer and an Agilent Bioanalyzer (RNA integrity number >7.0). Sample preparation, labeling, and hybridization were performed according to the standard protocols of Arraystar [ ]. Each sample was hybridized to an individual microarray, yielding independent biological replicates (IA: n = 16; control: n = 8); no technical replicates were included at the array level.
Raw probe intensities were background-corrected and normalized using quantile normalization implemented in the limma R package. All 24 samples were processed in a single batch; principal component analysis revealed no batch-related clustering, and therefore, no additional batch correction was required. Differential expression analysis was conducted using limma with empirical Bayes moderation. Multiple testing correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and adjusted P values (false discovery rate [FDR], q-values) were reported.
circRNAs with a fold change (FC) > 1.5 and an adjusted P-value (FDR) < 0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed. In total, 11 circRNAs were upregulated and 49 were downregulated in IA tissues compared with controls. For all downstream analyses and figures, “n” refers to the number of biological samples (IA = 16, control = 8). The complete list of differentially expressed circRNAs, including log2FC, raw P values, and adjusted P values, is provided in .

2.3.

## Bioinformatic analysis
To identify miRNAs with potential binding sites for circ_0103896 in IA, we integrated circRNA profiling results with predictions from the miRBase database. The interaction between miR-432–5p and its predicted target gene, FTO, was analyzed using TargetScan 7.0.

2.4.

## Mouse IA model
For the induction of IAs, 30 male wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 mice (aged 6–8 weeks; weighing 30–32 g) were maintained under controlled environmental conditions (temperature: 25 °C ± 1 °C; humidity: 55 % ± 5 %) with a 12-h light/dark cycle and ad libitum access to food and water. All mice were obtained from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animals Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). IAs were induced by ligating the left common carotid artery and left renal arteries under anesthesia administered intraperitoneally with Zoletil® (30 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg). To promote postoperative hypertension, the drinking water was replaced with 1 % NaCl. The mice were randomly allocated (computer-generated randomization, stratified by body weight) into two groups: a sham-operated control group (n = 6) and an aneurysm group (n = 24; 6 mice per treatment arm).
For in vivo modulation of circ_0103896 and miR-432–5p, replication-deficient adenoviral vectors (serotype 5; E1/E3-deleted) were used. The circ_0103896 overexpression cassette was driven by a CMV promoter, while the miR-432–5p vector expressed a pri-miR sequence under a U6/CMV hybrid promoter. Control vectors carried the corresponding empty backbone or scramble sequence. Viral stocks were prepared at a physical titer of 1 × 109 plaque-forming units (PFU)/mL, and mice received a single intravenous tail-vein injection (100–150 μL; 1 × 109 PFU per mouse) 7 days before IA induction. For pharmacologic inhibition of FTO, FB23-2 was administered intraperitoneally at 1 mg/kg once daily, as indicated. After a 30-day experimental period, mice were euthanized via CO2 overdose. Following euthanasia, vascular perfusion was performed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and 4 % paraformaldehyde, followed by infusion of bromophenol blue gelatin (Sigma, USA).
Aneurysm formation was defined as an arterial dilation exceeding 50 % of the parent artery diameter upon microscopic inspection. Severity was graded according to the following rubric: grade 1, normal artery with no dilation or wall abnormality; grade 2, aneurysmal dilation >50 % of the parent artery diameter without a discrete aneurysm sac; grade 3, discrete unruptured aneurysm sac visible; and grade 4, ruptured aneurysm with evident extravasation or vessel wall discontinuity. Each artery was assigned a numerical aneurysmal grade corresponding to this rubric (1–4). All grading was performed on coded sections by two independent investigators blinded to group allocation. Inter-rater reliability, assessed using Cohen's κ, indicated substantial agreement (κ = 0.80–0.85); any discrepancies were resolved by consensus prior to unblinding.
All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with Directive 2010/63/EU of the European Parliament on the protection of animals used for scientific purposes. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. For in vivo analyses, each mouse was treated as one biological replicate; when multiple segments or sections were analyzed from the same animal, measurements were averaged to generate a single data point per mouse before statistical testing.

2.5.

## Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining
The isolated tissues were sectioned at 5-μm thickness and mounted on poly-L-lysine–coated slides. Standard H&E staining was then performed. Briefly, sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through graded ethanols to water. Slides were immersed in hematoxylin solution for 5 min at room temperature, rinsed under running tap water until clear, and blued in 0.1 % lithium carbonate for 30 s–1 min. After an additional wash in tap water, sections were counterstained with eosin for 1–2 min, dehydrated through ascending ethanol grades, cleared in xylene, and coverslipped with mounting medium. Microscopic imaging was performed for visualization. For histological evaluation, three non-adjacent sections per vessel (≥100 μm apart) and five random fields per section were analyzed by two blinded observers. Field- and section-level measurements were averaged to yield a single value per animal (biological n).

2.6.

## Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)
FISH was performed to detect and localize miR-432–5p and circ_0103896 expression in IA tissue samples. Digoxigenin-labeled probes specific to circ_0103896 and biotin-labeled probes targeting miR-432–5p were synthesized for this purpose. FISH analysis was conducted following established protocols described previously [ ]. Fluorescence signal intensity (immunofluorescence accumulation optical density) was quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, USA). FISH quantifications were performed on three sections per specimen and three random fields per section; field- and section-level values were averaged to generate a single value per patient (human tissues) or per mouse (animal tissues). Each specimen thus represented one biological replicate, whereas individual image fields and sections served as technical replicates.

2.7.

## Cells and cell culture
hBVSMCs (catalog no. CSC-7824W) were obtained from Creative Bioarray (Shirley, NY, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1 % streptomycin (100 μg/mL), and 1 % penicillin (100 U/mL) (all from Gibco, USA). Unless otherwise specified, in vitro experiments were conducted using hBVSMCs between passages 3 and 6. Independent cultures initiated on different days were treated as biological replicates.

2.8.

## Isolation and culture of mouse VSMCs (mVSMCs)
Following euthanasia via CO2 inhalation, the aorta was carefully excised, cleared of surrounding adipose tissue, and immediately submerged in a cold collection medium consisting of DMEM supplemented with 10 % FBS, 2 mM L-glutamine, 50 U/mL penicillin, 50 μg/mL streptomycin, and 25 mM 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfonic acid (HEPES). After cleaning, the aortas were placed in 200 μL of DMEM containing 3.33 mg/mL collagenase type I (LS004194, Worthington Biochemical) and incubated at 37 °C for 5 min. The adventitia was then removed, and the aortas were dissected into 1–2 mm segments. These tissue fragments were incubated at 37 °C for 45 min in 100 μL of DMEM supplemented with collagenase type I (6 mg/mL) and elastase (2.5 mg/mL; LS002290, Worthington Biochemical). After enzymatic digestion, the dissociated cells were seeded and cultured for 3–4 days and then rinsed once with PBS. Subsequently, cells were incubated for 30 min at 4 °C with rat anti-ICAM-2 antibody (553325, BD Biosciences) diluted 1:500 in PBS. Following two washes, sheep anti-rat IgG magnetic beads (Dynabeads™, 11035, Thermo Fisher Scientific) diluted 1:250 were added and incubated for 30 min at 4 °C. Cells were then trypsinized and collected in a Falcon tube within a magnetic field. VSMCs present in the supernatant were seeded onto gelatin-coated plates containing DMEM supplemented with 10 % FBS, 2 mM L-glutamine, 50 U/mL penicillin, 50 μg/mL streptomycin, and 25 mM HEPES. To preserve cellular phenotype and ensure experimental consistency, VSMCs were used at early passage 2. Independent primary isolations (separate animals or batches) were considered biological replicates, whereas repeated measurements from the same isolate (e.g., multiple wells) were treated as technical replicates.

2.9.

## Cell transfection
A range of genetic constructs was obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China), including a lentivirus-based circ_0103896 overexpression vector (pLV-circ_0103896, p-circ), an empty control vector (pLV-circ-Luci (2A) puro, p-NC), FTO knockdown and overexpression vectors (si-FTO and pcDNA3.1-FTO), short hairpin RNA targeting circ_0103896 (sh-circ_0103896), and miR-432–5p mimics and inhibitors. Negative control vectors, including miR-NC, pcDNA3.1, and sh-NC, were used for comparative analyses. Twenty-four hours before transfection with sh-circ_0103896, circ_0103896, or miR-432–5p mimic, hBVSMCs and mVSMCs were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured to approximately 70 % confluence. Transfections were performed using Lipofectamine™ 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. For all cell-based assays, independent transfections performed on different days were considered biological replicates, whereas replicate wells within the same transfection were treated as technical replicates and averaged within each experiment.

2.10.

## Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and reverse-transcribed with M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega, USA). Complementary DNA amplification was performed using the SYBR® Green Master Mix kit (Takara, Japan). For internal normalization, GAPDH and U6 were used as reference genes for mRNA and miRNA assays, respectively. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences used for RT-qPCR are listed in . RT-qPCR results were obtained from at least three independent biological replicates (separate cultures or animals), with each sample measured in triplicate PCR reactions (technical replicates). Technical Cq values were averaged to generate a single value per biological sample for statistical analysis.
 
2.11.

## Protein level measurement via Western blot analysis
Proteins were extracted from hBVSMCs using RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China) and clarified by centrifugation at 10,000×g for 5 min. Protein concentrations were determined using a bicinchoninic acid assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Equal amounts of protein (20 μg per sample) were separated via polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA). Membranes were blocked with 3 % bovine serum albumin (Solarbio, China) for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4 °C with the indicated primary antibodies, followed by incubation with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. All antibodies were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (USA): FTO (45980S, 1:1000), α-SMA (14968S, 1:1000), SM22α (52011S, 1:1000), MMP2 (14351S, 1:1000), MMP9 (24317S, 1:1000), and GAPDH (2118L, 1:1000). Immunoreactive bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagents (Solarbio, China), and densitometric quantification was performed using ImageJ software (NIH, USA), with GAPDH as the loading control. Western blot quantifications were based on at least three independent biological lysates prepared from separate cultures or animals. Duplicate lanes or repeated exposures were treated as technical replicates, averaged, and normalized to GAPDH within each biological replicate.

2.12.

## Cell growth determination via MTT assay
Cell proliferation was evaluated using the MTT assay. Briefly, hBVSMCs (1 × 104 cells/well), with or without platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-BB, were seeded into 96-well plates. At 0, 24, and 48 h, 10 μL of MTT solution was added to each well and incubated for an additional 4 h at 37 °C. After removing the medium, 100 μL of dimethyl sulfoxide (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. Optical density was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA). MTT data were collected from at least three independent biological experiments (separate cultures or transfections conducted on different days), with 5–6 replicate wells per condition (technical replicates). Well-level readings were averaged within each experiment to generate a single value per biological replicate.

2.13.

## Cell apoptosis detection via flow cytometry
Apoptosis of hBVSMCs was evaluated using Annexin V–FITC/propidium iodide (PI) double staining. After treatment, cells were harvested, washed twice with cold PBS, and resuspended in 1 × binding buffer at a density of 1 × 106 cells/mL. A total of 100 μL of the suspension was incubated with 5 μL Annexin V–FITC and 5 μL PI (Annexin V–FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit, BD Biosciences, USA) for 15 min at room temperature in the dark, followed by the addition of 400 μL binding buffer. Flow cytometric analysis was performed using a BD FACSCanto™ II cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA). Single-stained controls were included for fluorescence compensation. Data were analyzed with FlowJo™ software (Tree Star, USA). Gating was based on forward scatter and side scatter parameters to exclude debris and doublets. Early apoptosis was defined as Annexin V–positive/PI-negative cells, whereas late apoptosis or necrosis was defined as Annexin V–positive/PI-positive cells. A minimum of 10,000 events was collected per sample. All assays were performed in triplicate. Flow cytometric data were derived from at least three independent biological experiments (separate cultures). For each biological replicate, a single tube per condition was analyzed; when duplicate tubes were included, they were treated as technical replicates and averaged for statistical analysis.

2.14.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
To examine the interaction between circ_0103896 and miR-432–5p, a 400 bp fragment spanning the back-splice junction region of circ_0103896, including the predicted miR-432-5p–binding site, was amplified and cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to generate the WT reporter (circ_0103896–WT). A corresponding mutant construct (circ_0103896–MUT) was produced via site-directed mutagenesis of the miR-432–5p seed-matching sequence within the junction fragment. Similarly, the full-length 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) of FTO, containing the predicted miR-432-5p–binding site, was cloned downstream of the luciferase reporter in pmirGLO to generate FTO–WT, whereas a mutant reporter (FTO–MUT) was generated by substituting the seed sequence of the binding site. hBVSMCs were cotransfected with the reporter constructs and either the miR-432–5p mimic or negative control (miR-NC). Luciferase activity was measured 24 h post-transfection using the Dual-Luciferase® Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA), with Renilla luciferase serving as an internal control for normalization. Experiments were conducted in at least three independent biological transfections performed on different days, with triplicate wells per condition serving as technical replicates. Firefly/Renilla ratios were averaged across wells to yield a single value for each biological replicate.

2.15.

## RNA and m6A immunoprecipitation
RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays in hBVSMCs were performed using the Magna RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, Cat. #17–700) according to the manufacturer's instructions. For RIP, 200 μg of total RNA was incubated with magnetic beads conjugated to either an anti-Ago2 antibody (Abcam, ab5072, USA) or normal rabbit IgG (provided in the kit) as a negative control. m6A RIP (MeRIP) assays were conducted using the Magna MeRIP m6A Kit (Millipore, Cat. #17–10499) following the manufacturer's protocol. For each reaction, 200 μg of total RNA was immunoprecipitated with 5 μg anti-m6A antibody (Epigentek, Cat. #A-1801-100). Synthetic RNA spike-in controls supplied with the kit were included in every reaction to normalize immunoprecipitation efficiency and ensure reproducibility. Enriched RNA was subsequently purified and analyzed via RT-qPCR. Both RIP and MeRIP assays were performed in at least three independent biological preparations (separate cell cultures). RT-qPCR of immunoprecipitated and input samples was conducted in technical triplicate and averaged. MeRIP signals were normalized to the spike-in controls and expressed as the ratio of immunoprecipitated to input RNA (relative enrichment) for each biological replicate.

2.16.

## Determination of cell migration via Transwell assay
Cell migration was assessed using 24-well Transwell chambers equipped with 8-μm pore polycarbonate membranes (Corning, USA). Briefly, transfected hBVSMCs (5 × 104 cells in 200 μL) were resuspended in serum-free DMEM and seeded into the upper chamber, whereas the lower chamber was filled with 600 μL DMEM containing 10 % FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, non-migrated cells on the upper membrane surface were gently removed with a cotton swab. Migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed with methanol, stained with 0.05 % crystal violet, and rinsed with PBS. For each membrane, migrated cells were counted in five randomly selected microscopic fields at × 200 magnification. Cell counting was performed independently by two investigators blinded to group allocation. Each experiment was repeated three times. Transwell migration was evaluated in at least three independent biological experiments, each containing duplicate inserts per condition (technical replicates). For each insert, counts from five fields were averaged to obtain one value per insert; insert-level values were then averaged to generate a single value per experiment. The biological n corresponds to the number of independent experiments.

2.17.

## RNA m6A dot-blot assay
Global m6A levels were evaluated using an m6A dot-blot assay on total RNA extracted from transfected hBVSMCs with RNAiso Plus (TaKaRa, Kusatsu, Japan). RNA concentrations were determined using a NanoDrop™ spectrophotometer, and samples were denatured at 65 °C for 5 min to disrupt secondary structures. Serial dilutions of RNA (50, 100, and 200 ng per spot) were applied in duplicate onto Amersham Hybond-N+ membranes (GE Healthcare, USA) using a Bio-Dot® apparatus, followed by UV crosslinking. To verify equal RNA loading, membranes were stained with 0.02 % methylene blue for 5 min, scanned for imaging, and subsequently destained. Membranes were then blocked with 5 % non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature, incubated overnight at 4 °C with an anti-m6A antibody (Synaptic Systems, Germany; 1:2000), and probed with a horseradish peroxidase–conjugated anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody. Chemiluminescent signals were visualized using Pierce™ ECL Western Blotting Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).
Dot intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, USA) with local background subtraction applied. The m6A signal for each spot was normalized to the corresponding methylene blue intensity, and technical duplicates were averaged to generate a single value per RNA load. Data from different RNA amounts (50–200 ng) were consistent and therefore averaged to yield one value per biological replicate (n ≥ 3 independent RNA preparations). Results are presented relative to the control group, which was set to 1.0.

2.18.

## Statistical analysis
Unless otherwise specified, data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) from at least three independent biological replicates. Throughout the manuscript, n refers to the number of biological replicates (independent patient samples, separate cell cultures or isolations, or individual animals). Technical replicates (e.g., replicate wells, qPCR reactions, dot blots, or duplicate inserts) were averaged within each biological replicate before statistical testing and were not counted as independent n. Sample size for in vivo experiments was determined based on an a priori power calculation for the primary endpoint (aneurysmal grade), assuming α = 0.05, power = 0.8, expected mean difference = 1.3, and standard deviation (SD) = 0.8, based on pilot data.
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Comparisons between two groups were conducted using two-tailed Student's t-tests. For experiments involving more than two groups, one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied as appropriate, followed by Tukey's post hoc test for pairwise comparisons. When multiple pairwise tests were performed, Bonferroni correction was applied. For analyses involving multiple gene expression comparisons (e.g., circRNA microarray screening and RT-qPCR validation of multiple targets), the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR adjustment was used to correct for multiple testing.
Data distributions were evaluated for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and equality of variances was assessed using Levene's test. Parametric tests (t-tests, ANOVA) were applied only when assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were satisfied; otherwise, equivalent nonparametric tests (Mann–Whitney U or Kruskal–Wallis tests) were used. Correlations between miR-432–5p and circ_0103896 expression levels were analyzed using Pearson's correlation coefficient. A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results
3.1.

## Downregulation of circ_0103896 in patients with IAs
To investigate circRNA expression in patients with IAs, we profiled 16 IA tissue samples and 8 controls. Patient demographics and clinical characteristics are summarized in . The IA and control groups were age- and sex-matched, with no significant differences in the prevalence of hypertension or smoking status (P > 0.05). Clinical variables specific to the IA cohort, including rupture status, aneurysm size, and aneurysm location, are also detailed in . Heatmap analysis revealed widespread circRNA dysregulation, with 272 circRNAs upregulated and 782 downregulated relative to controls ( A). Using the thresholds of FC > 1.5 and adjusted P (FDR) < 0.05, 11 circRNAs were significantly upregulated and 49 significantly downregulated ( A). Among these, hsa_circ_0103896 emerged as one of the most significantly downregulated candidates. The volcano plot highlighted an FC of 6.98 (log2FC = 2.80; 95 % confidence interval [CI], 2.30–3.30) with a raw P = 9.67 × 10−22 and an adjusted P (FDR) = 2.51 × 10−17 ( B). Further analysis based on circRNA profiling ( C) and RT-qPCR validation ( D) confirmed a notable reduction in circ_0103896 expression in IA tissues compared with normal controls. RT-qPCR (ΔΔCt) analysis confirmed that circ_0103896 expression significantly decreased in IA, with an FC of 0.32 (95 % CI [0.22, 0.46]; P < 0.0001) relative to controls ( D). This decrease was corroborated by FISH analysis in murine IA tissues, which revealed reduced fluorescence intensity in IA arteries compared with controls, with a mean difference of −45 arbitrary units (AU) (95 % CI [−65, −20]; P = 0.0006) ( E and F). These findings demonstrate a marked reduction in circ_0103896 expression in IA, suggesting its pivotal role in the onset and progression of aneurysm formation.
 
3.2.

## Circ_0103896 downregulation favors the switch of hBVSMCs from contractile to synthetic phenotype
Based on our previous findings, circ_0103896 has a significant effect on the onset and formation of IA. To elucidate the role of circ_0103896 in IA more comprehensively, we modulated its expression in hBVSMCs through silencing and overexpression via cell transfection. Notably, in the presence of PDGF-BB, we observed a time-dependent decrease in circ_0103896 expression within hBVSMCs ( A), with a regression slope of −0.12 U/h (95 % CI [−0.18, −0.06]; P < 0.001). This observation suggests a substantial role for circ_0103896 in mediating the PDGF-BB-induced phenotypic switch of hBVSMCs.
 The transition of hBVSMCs from a contractile to a synthetic phenotype is intricately linked with the initiation and progression of IA, which has been established in vivo. we investigated the expression dynamics of α-SMA and SM-22α, two pivotal contractile proteins in hBVSMCs, under circ_0103896 silencing and overexpression ( B). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that circ_0103896 overexpression increased α-SMA expression by 2.1-fold (95 % CI [1.5, 3.0]; P < 0.001) and that of SM-22α by 1.8-fold (95 % CI [1.3, 2.6]; P = 0.002). On the other hand, circ_0103896 silencing decreased α-SMA expression by 55 % (FC 0.45, 95 % CI [0.31, 0.66]; P = 0.001) and that of SM-22α by 60 % (FC 0.40, 95 % CI [0.27, 0.59]; P = 0.002) relative to controls ( C). These findings were corroborated by Western blot, where densitometric analysis revealed mean differences of +0.62 AU (95 % CI [0.35, 0.89]) for α-SMA and +0.55 AU (95 % CI [0.28, 0.82]) for SM-22α under overexpression, with reciprocal reductions under silencing ( D and E). This result supports the hypothesis that reduced circ_0103896 expression may accentuate the PDGF-BB-driven conversion of hBVSMCs from a contractile to a synthetic state in vitro.
As elevated production of inflammatory mediators leads to VSMC phenotypic transitions, we assessed the expression of inflammation-associated genes by RT-qPCR. Circ_0103896 knockdown markedly upregulated the expression of TNF-α (FC 2.9, 95 % CI [2.0, 4.2]; P < 0.001) ( F), IL1B (FC 2.6, 95 % CI [1.8, 3.7]; P < 0.001) ( G), IL6 (FC 3.1, 95 % CI [2.1, 4.4]; P < 0.001) ( H), and iNOS (FC 2.4, 95 % CI [1.6, 3.5]; P < 0.001) ( I) compared to normal healthy (NC) controls. However, overexpressing circ_0103896 attenuated these effects, reducing cytokine expression by 40 %–60 %, with corresponding 95 % CIs ( F–I). Moreover, MMP2 and MMP9 protein levels were similarly affected. circ_0103896 knockdown increased MMP2 by + 0.71 AU (95 % CI [0.42, 1.00]; P < 0.001) and MMP9 by +0.85 AU (95 % CI [0.54, 1.16]; P < 0.001). Its overexpression reduced MMP2 by −0.46 AU (95 % CI [−0.72, −0.20]; P = 0.002) and MMP9 by −0.53 AU (95 % CI [−0.82, −0.24]; P = 0.001) ( J and K).
Functional assay results were consistent with these molecular findings. MTT analysis indicated that circ_0103896 knockdown increased cell proliferation with a mean OD570 difference of +0.23 (95 % CI [0.12, 0.34]; P = 0.001), whereas its overexpression reduced proliferation by −0.19 (95 % CI [−0.31, −0.07]; P = 0.004) relative to NC ( L). Transwell assays indicated enhanced migration in circ_0103896-silenced cells, with a mean difference of +42 migrated cells/field (95 % CI [25, 59]; P < 0.001), whereas circ_0103896 overexpression decreased migration by −33 cells/field (95 % CI [−50, −16]; P < 0.001) ( M and N). Apoptosis analysis demonstrated that knockdown increased Annexin V+ cells by +12 percentage points (95 % CI [ , ]; P < 0.001), whereas overexpression reduced apoptosis by −9 percentage points (95 % CI [−14, −4]; P = 0.002) ( O and P).
Collectively, these results indicate that circ_0103896 loss promotes, whereas its overexpression suppresses, the PDGF-BB-induced phenotypic switch of hBVSMCs from a contractile to a synthetic state, accompanied by corresponding increases in inflammation, proliferation, migration, and apoptosis.

3.3.

## Circ_0103896 expression is negatively correlated with IA severity in mice
To corroborate the in vivo significance of circ_0103896, a murine IA model was established with either overexpression or silencing of circ_0103896, and subsequent aneurysm development was evaluated. H&E staining revealed marked thinning of vessel walls in IA mice ( A). Compared with control mice (si-NC or p-NC), circ_0103896-silenced mice exhibited significantly higher aneurysmal scores (mean difference, +1.6; 95 % CI [0.9, 2.3]; P < 0.001), whereas circ_0103896-overexpressing mice showed reduced scores (mean difference, −1.2; 95 % CI [−1.9, −0.6]; P = 0.002) ( B). Consistently, the grade distribution analysis demonstrated that circ_0103896 silencing shifted mice toward higher IA grades, whereas circ_0103896 overexpression reduced the proportion of advanced grades ( C). Statistical analysis confirmed this distributional difference (χ2 = 12.4, P = 0.006; Cramér's V = 0.54, 95 % CI [0.18, 0.73]), indicating a moderate association between circ_0103896 levels and IA severity. All aneurysmal scoring and grade assignments were independently performed by two blinded investigators, yielding substantial inter-rater reliability (Cohen's κ = 0.82).
 We next analyzed the expression of IA-related inflammatory and extracellular matrix–remodeling genes in cerebral arteries. Silencing of circ_0103896 significantly upregulated the expression of TNF-α (FC 2.6, 95 % CI [1.7, 3.8]; P < 0.001) ( D), IL1B (FC 2.9, 95 % CI [1.9, 4.3]; P < 0.001) ( E), MMP9 (FC 3.1, 95 % CI [2.1, 4.6]; P < 0.001) ( F), and MMP2 (FC 2.2, 95 % CI [1.5, 3.3]; P = 0.002) ( G), relative to NC. Conversely, circ_0103896 overexpression suppressed these mediators, reducing TNF-α by 45 % (FC 0.55, 95 % CI [0.37, 0.81]; P = 0.004), IL1B by 50 % (FC 0.50, 95 % CI [0.33, 0.76]; P = 0.002), MMP9 by 55 % (FC 0.45, 95 % CI [0.29, 0.70]; P = 0.001), and MMP2 by 40 % (FC 0.60, 95 % CI [0.41, 0.87]; P = 0.008). These results demonstrate that circ_0103896 deficiency accelerates IA progression and severity in vivo, whereas its overexpression mitigates vascular wall damage and suppresses the inflammatory and extracellular matrix–remodeling responses characteristic of IA.

3.4.

## Circ_0103896 acts as a sponge for miR-432–5p in IA
In alignment with existing literature [ ], circRNAs primarily modulate gene expression by acting as miRNA sponges. To identify potential miRNA targets for circ_0103896 in IA, we first performed bioinformatic analysis. Among five candidate miRNAs (miR-485–3p, miR-543, miR-409–3p, miR-142–3p, and miR-432–5p), only miR-432–5p displayed significantly elevated expression in IA samples compared to NC ( A). RT-qPCR confirmed this finding: miR-432–5p was upregulated in PDGF-BB-stimulated mVSMCs (FC 2.7, 95 % CI [1.9, 3.8]; P < 0.001) ( B) and hBVSMCs (FC 2.5, 95 % CI [1.7, 3.6]; P < 0.001) ( C) following PDGF-BB treatment, identifying it as the principal miRNA interacting with circ_0103896 in IA. To further explore this interaction, we used miRBase to predict binding sites and cloned WT or mutant circ_0103896 fragments into luciferase reporters ( D). Next RT-qPCR demonstrated that miR-432–5p expression was significantly downregulated in circ_0103896-overexpressing cells (FC 0.42, 95 % CI [0.29, 0.61]; P < 0.001) and upregulated in circ_0103896-silenced cells (FC 2.3, 95 % CI [1.6, 3.4]; P < 0.001) ( E and F). Luciferase reporter assays revealed that miR-432–5p mimics suppressed WT reporter activity by 58 % (mean difference −0.61 relative units, 95 % CI [−0.89, −0.33]; P < 0.001) but had no effect on mutant constructs (interaction effect = −0.59, 95 % CI [−0.87, −0.31]) in mVSMCs and hBVSMCs ( G and H). RIP assays further confirmed this binding, with Ago2 immunoprecipitation yielding a 3.4-fold enrichment of circ_0103896 and miR-432–5p compared with IgG controls (95 % CI [2.1, 5.6]; P < 0.001) ( I), suggesting an interaction between circ_0103896 and miR-432–5p.
 To further assess the regulatory effect of circ_0103896 on miR-432–5p in the context of IAs, we measured miR-432–5p expression in NC and IA tissues. Human samples revealed significantly higher miR-432–5p expression in IA tissues (FC 3.1, 95 % CI [2.2, 4.5]; P < 0.001) compared with NC ( J). Correlation analysis confirmed a strong negative relationship between circ_0103896 and miR-432–5p expression in IA patients (Pearson's r = −0.71, 95 % CI [−0.88, −0.38]; P < 0.001) ( K). Collectively, these data establish that circ_0103896 functions as a miRNA sponge for miR-432–5p in IA, suppressing its expression and functional activity.

3.5.

## Circ_0103896 suppression promotes the phenotypic switch of hBVSMCs by miR-432–5p
To test whether circ_0103896 regulates the phenotypic switch of hBVSMCs via miR-432–5p, we manipulated miR-432–5p expression using mimics or inhibitors (anti-miR). RT-qPCR confirmed efficient modulation: miR-432–5p mimics increased expression by 3.2-fold (95 % CI [2.3, 4.5]; P < 0.001), whereas anti-miR reduced it to 0.38-fold of the control (95 % CI [0.26, 0.56]; P < 0.001) ( A). Subsequently, analysis of contractile markers revealed that circ_0103896 silencing decreased α-SMA by 58 % (FC 0.42, 95 % CI [0.28, 0.64]; P < 0.001) and SM-22α by 52 % (FC 0.48, 95 % CI [0.31, 0.73]; P = 0.002). Conversely, circ_0103896 overexpression increased α-SMA by 2.0-fold (95 % CI [1.4, 2.8]; P < 0.001) and SM-22α by 1.9-fold (95 % CI [1.3, 2.7]; P = 0.001). These changes were reversed by cotransfection with miR-432–5p mimics or inhibitors ( B–E). At the protein level, Western blot densitometry confirmed similar trends: α-SMA increased by +0.58 AU (95 % CI [0.31, 0.85]) and SM-22α by +0.54 AU (95 % CI [0.27, 0.81]) under circ_0103896 overexpression, with reciprocal reductions under knockdown ( D and E).
 Next, we measured the expression of inflammation-related genes, including TNF-α, IL1B, IL6, and NOS2 (iNOS), in hBVSMCs under different transfection conditions. Consistent with our preceding results, silencing circ_0103896 upregulated TNF-α (FC 3.0, 95 % CI [2.0, 4.3]; P < 0.001) ( F), IL1B (FC 2.6, 95 % CI [1.8, 3.8]; P < 0.001) ( F), IL6 (FC 2.9, 95 % CI [2.0, 4.2]; P < 0.001) ( G), and iNOS (FC 2.3, 95 % CI [1.6, 3.5]; P < 0.001) ( G). Importantly, this proinflammatory effect of circ_0103896 silencing was significantly reversed by cotransfection with anti-miR-432–5p. Conversely, circ_0103896 overexpression suppressed cytokine expression by approximately 45 %–60 % relative to controls (e.g., TNF-α, FC 0.55, 95 % CI [0.37, 0.81]; P = 0.004), and these reductions were abolished by cotransfecting with miR-432–5p mimics. Together, these results indicate that circ_0103896 exerts a suppressive effect on inflammatory signaling in hBVSMCs, and that this regulation is mediated through its interaction with miR-432–5p ( F and G).
We next examined MMP2 and MMP9, key enzymes in extracellular matrix remodeling. Circ_0103896 knockdown increased MMP2 by +0.68 AU (95 % CI [0.39, 0.97]; P < 0.001) and MMP9 by +0.82 AU (95 % CI [0.51, 1.13]; P < 0.001), whereas its overexpression decreased MMP2 by −0.47 AU (95 % CI [−0.73, −0.21]; P = 0.002) and MMP9 by −0.52 AU (95 % CI [−0.80, −0.24]; P = 0.001). Both effects were reversed by miR-432–5p modulation ( H and I).
Functional assays further validated these observations. MTT assays revealed increased proliferation after circ_0103896 silencing (mean OD570 difference +0.21, 95 % CI [0.11, 0.31]; P = 0.001), whereas overexpression reduced proliferation (−0.18, 95 % CI [−0.29, −0.07]; P = 0.004). These effects were rescued by cotransfecting miR-432–5p mimics or inhibitors ( J). Transwell assays indicated that migration was enhanced by +39 cells/field (95 % CI [23, 55]; P < 0.001) after circ_0103896 knockdown and reduced by −31 cells/field (95 % CI [−48, −14]; P = 0.002) after overexpression ( K and L). Apoptosis assays demonstrated that circ_0103896 silencing increased Annexin V+ cells by +11 percentage points (95 % CI [ , ]; P < 0.001), whereas overexpression decreased apoptosis by −8 percentage points (95 % CI [−13, −3]; P = 0.004) ( M and N). Both effects were reversed by miR-432–5p modulation.
These data demonstrate that circ_0103896 suppresses the phenotypic switch of hBVSMCs through miR-432–5p, affecting contractile marker expression, inflammatory cytokine production, and cellular behaviors like proliferation, migration, and apoptosis.

3.6.

## FTO is regulated by the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p axis in hBVSMCs
We used TargetScan to predict potential circ_0103896/miR-432–5p targets in IA and identified the m6A demethylase FTO as a candidate ( A). Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-432–5p mimics significantly suppressed the luciferase activity of the WT FTO 3′UTR by 55 % (mean difference, −0.58 relative units; 95 % CI [−0.84, −0.32]; P < 0.001), whereas no effect was observed with the mutant (MUT) construct (interaction effect, −0.55; 95 % CI [−0.80, −0.29]) ( B). At the RNA level, RT-qPCR analysis revealed that miR-432–5p mimics reduced FTO expression to 0.41-fold of control (95 % CI [0.28, 0.61]; P < 0.001), whereas circ_0103896 overexpression increased FTO levels by 2.2-fold (95 % CI [1.5, 3.1]; P < 0.001). Cotransfection with miR-432–5p mimics abolished this circ_0103896-induced increase in FTO expression ( C). Similarly, Western blot analysis confirmed that miR-432–5p mimics reduced FTO protein levels by −0.63 AU (95 % CI, −0.90 to −0.36; P < 0.001), whereas circ_0103896 overexpression increased FTO protein levels by +0.72 AU (95 % CI [0.42, 1.02]; P < 0.001); this effect was likewise reversed by miR-432–5p mimics ( D and E). These observations indicate that FTO is specifically regulated by the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p axis.
 Subsequently, to confirm that the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p axis modulates the phenotypic switch and contributes to IA formation and progression through regulation of FTO in vitro, we performed cell transfection experiments to overexpress FTO, miR-432–5p, or both in hBVSMCs. FTO overexpression increased α-SMA expression by 1.9-fold (95 % CI [1.3, 2.7]; P < 0.001) and SM22α expression by 1.8-fold (95 % CI [1.2, 2.5]; P = 0.002) while reducing MMP2 expression by −0.55 AU (95 % CI [−0.82, −0.28]; P = 0.001) and MMP9 expression by −0.61 AU (95 % CI [−0.90, −0.32]; P < 0.001). These effects were significantly reversed by miR-432–5p mimics ( F–I). This observation indicates that the circ_0103896-induced phenotypic transition from a contractile to a synthetic state in vitro is substantially dependent on the miR-432–5p/FTO axis. Additionally, functional assays demonstrated similar rescue patterns. FTO overexpression inhibited hBVSMC proliferation (mean ΔOD570 = −0.22; 95 % CI [−0.33, −0.11]; P < 0.001) ( J), migration (−36 cells per field; 95 % CI [−52, −20]; P < 0.001) ( K and L), and apoptosis (−10 percentage points Annexin V+; 95 % CI [−15, −5]; P = 0.002) ( M and N) compared with controls. Each of these inhibitory effects was reversed by miR-432–5p mimics ( J–N).
These findings indicate that FTO functions as a downstream target of the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p axis. Circ_0103896 promotes FTO expression by acting as a molecular sponge for miR-432–5p, thereby modulating phenotypic markers and functional behaviors of hBVSMCs associated with IA progression.

3.7.

## FTO mediates m6A modification of circ_0103896 to form a positive feedback loop with circ_0103896
The involvement of m6A modification in circRNA has been identified as a key contributor to the formation and progression of multiple diseases [ ]. Therefore, we hypothesized that the alteration of circ_0103896 by FTO-mediated m6A modification could impact IA initiation and progression. MeRIP assays revealed that FTO overexpression decreased total m6A levels by −42 % (FC 0.58, 95 % CI [0.41, 0.82]; P = 0.004), while FTO knockdown increased total m6A by 1.8-fold (95 % CI [1.3, 2.6]; P = 0.002) ( A). Dot-blot assays measuring global RNA m6A levels, normalized to methylene blue staining, confirmed these findings: mean difference in normalized signal intensity was −0.47 AU (95 % CI [−0.73, −0.21]; P = 0.003) under FTO overexpression, and +0.66 AU (95 % CI [0.35, 0.97]; P = 0.001) under FTO silencing ( B). Quantification was performed by densitometric analysis in ImageJ with background subtraction, with m6A signals normalized to methylene blue intensity and averaged across biological replicates (n ≥ 3). Moreover, MeRIP targeting circ_0103896 further revealed that FTO overexpression reduced m6A enrichment on circ_0103896 by −55 % (FC 0.45, 95 % CI [0.29, 0.70]; P = 0.001), whereas FTO silencing enhanced enrichment by 2.3-fold (95 % CI [1.5, 3.4]; P = 0.002) ( C). Consistently, RT-qPCR revealed that circ_0103896 RNA abundance was inversely correlated with m6A modification, increasing 2.1-fold under FTO overexpression (95 % CI [1.4, 3.1]; P = 0.001) and decreasing to 0.39-fold under FTO silencing (95 % CI [0.25, 0.61]; P = 0.002) ( D). Subsequent RIP assays confirmed physical interaction between FTO and circ_0103896, with a 3.5-fold enrichment compared to the IgG control (95 % CI [2.2, 5.5]; P < 0.001) ( E).
 Next, we examined whether this regulatory mechanism influenced the phenotypic switching of hBVSMCs. Silencing circ_0103896 reduced α-SMA expression to 0.44-fold of the control (95 % CI [0.30, 0.66]; P < 0.001) and SM-22α to 0.49-fold (95 % CI [0.32, 0.75]; P = 0.002), while increasing the expression of MMP2 by +0.72 AU (95 % CI [0.43, 1.01]; P < 0.001) and that of MMP9 by +0.86 AU (95 % CI [0.55, 1.17]; P < 0.001). FTO overexpression reversed these effects, restoring α-SMA and SM-22α expression (+0.57 and + 0.53 AU, respectively; both 95 % CI exclude 0) and reducing MMP2 and MMP9 toward control levels ( F–I). Functional assays provided further support. Circ_0103896 knockdown increased proliferation (mean OD570 difference +0.25, 95 % CI [0.14, 0.36]; P < 0.001), migration (+41 cells/field, 95 % CI [24, 58]; P < 0.001), and apoptosis (+12 percentage points Annexin V+, 95 % CI [ , ]; P = 0.001). These pro-pathogenic effects were substantially reversed by FTO overexpression ( J–N).
These findings demonstrate that FTO directly interacts with and demethylates circ_0103896, leading to its stabilization, which in turn establishes a positive feedback loop in which circ_0103896 enhances FTO expression (via miR-432–5p sponging, ). In turn, FTO promotes circ_0103896 stability through m6A demethylation, thus facilitating the suppression of IA-related pathological phenotypes.

3.8.

## Circ_0103896 suppresses the formation and progression of IA in vivo through the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO feedback loop
To investigate the therapeutic potential of the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis in the clinical management of IA, we conducted experiments involving the overexpression of circ_0103896 or miR-432–5p, or both, in a mouse model, followed by IA induction. We also used FB23-2, a specific FTO inhibitor, to modulate FTO enzyme activity in vivo. H&E staining revealed that circ_0103896 overexpression substantially mitigated vascular wall damage relative to control samples ( A). The mean aneurysmal score decreased by 1.3 points (95 % CI [−2.0, −0.6]; P = 0.001), whereas miR-432–5p overexpression or FB23-2 treatment abolished this protective effect, raising scores by +1.5 (95 % CI [0.8, 2.2]; P < 0.001) relative to the circ_0103896 group ( B). Similarly, grade distribution analysis revealed a clear shift toward lower IA grades following circ_0103896 overexpression, whereas miR-432–5p overexpression or FTO inhibition reversed this trend (χ2 = 14.2, P = 0.004; Cramér's V = 0.57, 95 % CI [0.21, 0.75]) ( C). All scoring and grading were performed independently by two blinded investigators, yielding strong inter-rater reliability (Cohen's κ = 0.84).
 We next examined transcriptional changes in inflammatory and extracellular matrix–remodeling mediators. Circ_0103896 overexpression significantly reduced the expression of TNF-α (FC0.48, 95 % CI [0.32, 0.72]; P = 0.002) ( D), IL1B (FC 0.52, 95 % CI [0.35, 0.78]; P = 0.003) ( E), MMP9 (FC 0.46, 95 % CI [0.30, 0.70]; P = 0.001) ( F), and MMP2 (FC 0.58, 95 % CI [0.39, 0.85]; P = 0.005) ( G) compared with IA control mice. These suppressive effects were abolished by either miR-432–5p overexpression or FB23-2 treatment, both of which restored gene expression to levels comparable to controls.
These data demonstrate that circ_0103896 overexpression mitigates IA formation and progression in vivo by suppressing inflammatory signaling and extracellular matrix degradation. The reversal of these protective effects by miR-432–5p overexpression or FTO inhibition confirms that circ_0103896 modulates IA pathogenesis through the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO regulatory loop.

## Discussion
Over the past decade, advances in high-throughput sequencing, microarray technologies, and bioinformatics have considerably expanded our understanding of circRNAs. Increasing evidence indicates that circRNAs play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of several cancers, including colorectal [ ], breast [ ], and hepatocellular carcinoma [ ]. However, the role of circRNAs in the pathogenesis of IAs remains poorly understood.
Current research on the mechanisms underlying IA formation and rupture highlights the pivotal role of VSMCs and their phenotypic modulation in disease progression [ , ]. The development of IA is characterized by the migration and proliferation of VSMCs into the intima, leading to myointimal hyperplasia [ , ]. As the aneurysm progresses, these cells undergo phenotypic transformation, often losing their contractile properties and undergoing apoptosis, which contributes to vessel wall thinning and eventual rupture [ , , ]. Recent studies have increasingly recognized circRNAs as key regulators of vascular pathophysiology, including the modulation of VSMC behavior [ ]. For example, circ_CHFR promotes inflammation, proliferation, and migration of oxidized low-density lipoprotein–treated human VSMCs via miR-214-3p–mediated regulation of the Wnt3/β-catenin pathway [ ], whereas circCCDC66 knockdown enhances VSMC proliferation and reduces apoptosis through the miR-342–3p/CCDC66 axis in abdominal aortic aneurysms [ ]. In this context, we investigated the functional role of circ_0103896 in VSMCs. We observed a marked reduction in circ_0103896 expression in VSMCs derived from patients with IA. Cellular assays demonstrated that the expression of circ_0103896 was positively correlated with the production of contractile markers (α-SMA and SM22α) but negatively correlated with that of inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, MMP2, and MMP9). Furthermore, in vivo overexpression of circ_0103896 significantly attenuated the formation and severity of IA in mice, underscoring its critical role in regulating VSMC phenotype and suppressing the progression of IA both in vitro and in vivo.
The role of circRNAs in regulating gene expression, particularly through their capacity to act as miRNA sponges, has been well established in multiple studies [ , , ]. Using bioinformatics-based predictive tools, we identified potential miRNAs that interact with circ_0103896 and confirmed that circ_0103896 directly binds to miR-432–5p. Beyond its role as a miR-432–5p sponge, further analyses revealed FTO as a downstream target of miR-432–5p. Previous studies have shown that miR-432–5p functions in the development and progression of several cancers, including breast cancer [ ]; osteosarcoma [ ]; and pancreatic [ ], gastric [ ], and colorectal cancers [ ]. Moreover, miR-432–5p participates in the angiogenic processes within the tumor microenvironment [ ]. Our results demonstrated that miR-432–5p was upregulated in PDGF-BB–treated hBVSMCs and mVSMCs, wherein it inhibited VSMC viability, migration, and apoptosis in vitro. Notably, the suppressive effects of circ_0103896 overexpression on VSMC phenotypic switching and PDGF-BB–induced proliferation, migration, and apoptosis were reversed by the introduction of a miR-432–5p mimic. Conversely, the overexpression of FTO mitigated the effects of miR-432–5p, further supporting the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis as a critical regulator of VSMC phenotypic modulation.
FTO, a key regulator of RNA m6A modification, exhibits a negative correlation with global m6A levels, thereby modulating gene expression [ ]. Our findings suggest that circ_0103896 functions as an inhibitor of hBVSMC phenotypic transition and of IA development and progression in vivo. Therefore, elucidating the regulatory mechanisms through which circ_0103896 contributes to IA pathogenesis is of substantial importance. Intriguingly, we identified a link between circ_0103896 expression, FTO activity, and m6A modification levels—a relationship further supported by subsequent analyses. In IA tissues and IA-affected cells such as VSMCs, circ_0103896 downregulation markedly promoted IA formation and progression in vivo, accompanied by phenotypic switching and increased inflammatory activity. Mechanistically, circ_0103896 expression appears to be tightly regulated by m6A modification of its precursor mRNA, a process modulated through the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis. Collectively, these observations highlight a self-regulating feedback loop in which the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO pathway modulates IA formation and progression via interconnected control of m6A-dependent gene expression.
It is important to note that all in vivo experiments in this study were conducted exclusively in male mice. This design choice aimed to minimize variability associated with hormonal cycling and to preserve statistical power in these initial mechanistic, proof-of-concept investigations. Nevertheless, sex differences are increasingly recognized as critical determinants in IA epidemiology and vascular biology, with estrogen and other sex hormones known to influence vascular remodeling, inflammation, and aneurysm susceptibility. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted within the context of male-only experimental models. Further studies incorporating both sexes, with appropriate sex-stratified analyses, will be essential to fully elucidate the role of the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis in IA pathogenesis.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, although the mouse IA model based on arterial ligation and hypertension is reproducible and widely used, it does not fully recapitulate the multifactorial and progressive pathogenesis of human IAs. Second, the use of STA samples from patients with brain tumors as controls may introduce confounding, as the STA differs anatomically and transcriptionally from intracranial arteries and may be influenced by tumor-associated inflammation. Although unaffected intracranial arterial segments would represent the ideal control, their collection from living patients is ethically infeasible, making the STA a pragmatic (albeit imperfect) surrogate. Third, although we demonstrated that the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis modulates inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β) and matrix-degrading enzymes (MMP2 and MMP9), we did not determine whether this regulation occurs through direct m6A modification of their transcripts; transcriptome-wide mapping and functional validation are therefore warranted. Fourth, although viral vector parameters (serotype, promoter, dose, and route) were clearly defined, in vivo transduction efficiency and cell-type specificity (e.g., VSMCs vs. endothelial cells or macrophages) were not directly assessed. Fifth, in the evaluation of intracranial aneurysms, we employed both the conventional four-grade system and the scoring method proposed by Kataoka et al. [ ]. However, since this scoring approach has been less commonly used in current murine intracranial aneurysm models, its application may potentially introduce methodological bias. Finally, although group sizes were guided by a priori power calculations, they remained relatively small, which may limit the precision of effect size estimates. Despite these limitations, this study provides mechanistic insight into the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO axis in VSMC phenotypic switching and IA progression, establishing a foundation for future translational studies incorporating improved animal models, intracranial control tissues, and larger, sex-balanced cohorts.

6.

## Conclusion
Our findings demonstrate that reduced circ_0103896 expression or elevated miR-432–5p levels are associated with the development and progression of IAs in both in vitro and in vivo models. Restoration of circ_0103896 expression effectively attenuates IA formation and progression through the circ_0103896/miR-432–5p/FTO feedback circuit. Collectively, these results provide direct evidence of a regulatory feedback loop linking circRNA and m6A modification, offering new insights into epigenetic regulation.
