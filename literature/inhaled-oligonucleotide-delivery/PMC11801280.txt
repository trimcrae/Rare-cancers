Age-related hearing loss, one type of acquired sensorineural hearing loss, is largely responsible for this escalating global health burden. Noise-induced, ototoxic, and idiopathic sudden sensorineural are other less common types of acquired hearing loss. The etiology of these conditions is complex and multi-factorial involving an interplay of genetic and environmental factors. Oxidative stress has recently been proposed as a likely linking cause in most types of acquired sensorineural hearing loss. Short non-coding RNA sequences known as microRNAs (miRNAs) have increasingly been shown to play a role in cellular hypoxia and oxidative stress responses including promoting an apoptotic response. Sensory hair cell death is a central histopathological finding in sensorineural hearing loss. As these cells do not regenerate in humans, it underlies the irreversibility of human age-related hearing loss. Ovid EMBASE, Ovid MEDLINE, Web of Science Core Collection, and ClinicalTrials.gov databases over the period August 1, 2018 to July 31, 2023 were searched with “hearing loss,” “hypoxamiRs,” “hypoxia,” “microRNAs,” “ischemia,” and “oxidative stress” text words for English language primary study publications or registered clinical trials. Registered clinical trials known to the senior author were also assessed. A total of 222 studies were thus identified. After excluding duplicates, editorials, retractions, secondary research studies, and non-English language articles, 39 primary studies and clinical trials underwent full-text screening. This resulted in 11 animal, in vitro, and/or human subject journal articles and 8 registered clinical trial database entries which form the basis of this narrative review. MiRNAs miR-34a and miR-29b levels increase with age in mice. These miRNAs were demonstrated in human neuroblastoma and murine cochlear cell lines to target Sirtuin 1/peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1-alpha (SIRT1/PGC-1α), SIRT1/p53, and SIRT1/hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha signaling pathways resulting in increased apoptosis. Furthermore, hypoxia and oxidative stress had a similar adverse apoptotic effect, which was inhibited by resveratrol and a myocardial inhibitor–associated transcript, a miR-29b competing endogenous mRNA. Gentamicin reduced miR-182-5p levels and increased cochlear oxidative stress and cell death in mice – an effect that was corrected by inner ear stem cell–derived exosomes. There is ongoing work seeking to determine if these findings can be effectively translated to humans.

## Background
Hearing loss is the third leading cause of years lived with disability (Global Burden of Disease Study 2013 Collaborators, 2015). Age-related hearing loss (ARHL), the most common type of acquired sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL), is estimated to affect just over 60% of the population aged 50 years and older globally (Global Burden of Disease Study 2013 Collaborators, 2015). There is increasing evidence of an association between SNHL and cognitive decline in older adults (Deal et al., 2017), hence exacerbating the disability attributable to hearing loss. Hearing loss and tinnitus are the most common presenting symptoms in all types of SNHL Vertigo a non-auditory symptom is a presenting symptom in rarer types of SNHL e.g., Menieres disease, and in a proportion of Sudden SNHL patients (Ayub et al., 2019; Joshua et al., 2022).
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are on average 22-nucleotide-long fragments that modulate cellular processes such as cell proliferation, and survival through inhibition of mRNA and other downstream transcription factors. They have been found to be associated with congenital and acquired types of SNHL (Soldà et al., 2012; Nunez et al., 2020). Chen et al. (2019) concluded that there was clear evidence linking miRNAs to the pathogenesis of ARHL.
The cellular and sub-cellular mechanisms underlying ARHL and other acquired types of SNHL are poorly understood. However, oxidative stress a proposed important cellular pathogenetic mechanism in diseases of the highly metabolically active heart tissue has been investigated in the cochlea (De Leiris et al., 2006; Xie et al., 2022). Oxidative stress describes the production of highly metabolically active superoxide oxygen and hydroxyl ions known as oxygen free radicals and less reactive intermediates hydrogen peroxide and singlet oxygen collectively known as reactive oxygen species (ROS) in excess of the cell’s antioxidant capacity (De Leiris et al., 2006; Lushchak, 2014). The mitochondrion is the site of the majority of ROS metabolism (Lushchak, 2014). ROS are synthesized as a by-product of the citric acid cycle and electron transport chain process amongst others. They are degraded enzymatically primarily by mitochondrial superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione synthetase and non-enzymatically by cytoplasmic vitamins E and C and others (De Leiris et al., 2006). There is evidence pointing towards oxidative stress having a pathogenetic role in various types of acquired SNHL, namely ototoxic, noise-induced, and idiopathic sudden SNHL (Fetoni et al., 2013; Lai et al., 2020; Nunez et al., 2020). Oxidative stress alters the expression of miRNAs and miRNAs target oxidative stress adaptive mRNAs in inner ear cells (Safabakhsh et al., 2022). This narrative review appraises the literature to collate the increasingly recent evidence related to acquired sensorineural SNHL-associated oxidative stress miRNAs, their target genes, and gene pathways.

## Search Strategy and Selection Criteria
ClinicalTrials.gov, Ovid MEDLINE, Ovid EMBASE, and Web of Science Core Collection were searched for English language articles/entries or clinical trials for the period August 1, 2018 to July 31, 2023. The terms: “hearing loss,” “hypoxamiRs,” “hypoxia,” “microRNAs,” “ischemia,” and “oxidative stress” singly or in combination were used to search database titles and abstracts for text words or subject headings for MeSH terms. Titles and abstracts of identified entries were screened to select studies that matched the review’s aims. Sensorineural hearing loss interventional treatments identified by searching ClinicalTrials.Gov or the senior author’s file of registered ClinicalTrials.gov trials were selected for inclusion if there was evidence in the entry that they matched this review’s aims or if such evidence existed in the literature. Either full text of articles or complete clinical trial data entries were then reviewed to determine if they matched this review’s aim. The inclusion and exclusion criteria applied were as previously described (Safabakhsh et al., 2022). In brief, primary studies investigating audiometrically confirmed acquired SNHL in human participants or mammalian animals in which oxidative stress was documented by clinical features or objective measures. Also, mammalian animal and in vitro inner ear cell studies with induction or objective evidence of oxidative stress, hypoxia, or ischemia were included. Non-English language, secondary research, correspondence, or other publications that were not full manuscripts were excluded except clinical trial database entries. Data extracted in addition to publication identification details included study design, type of SNHL investigated, number and demographic characteristics of participants, oxidative stress methodology, dysregulated miRNAs, their target genes, and gene or functional enrichment pathway analysis findings. A Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) flow diagram was generated (Page et al., 2021).
A total of 222 articles or trial entries were identified by database, registry, and author file searches. A total of 169 duplicates were excluded and 53 article abstracts and registry entries were screened. 13 failed to meet the review’s inclusion criteria, and one was irretrievable. Data was extracted from 11 primary research articles and 8 registered clinical trial entries ( ). Of the primary articles, there were 3 investigations of age-related hearing loss, 3 idiopathic sudden sensorineural hearing loss, 2 drug-induced ototoxic hearing loss, 2 occupational solvent-induced hearing loss, 1 noise-induced hearing loss, and 1 investigated autoimmune hearing loss ( ). Of the clinical trials, 5 investigated ototoxic hearing loss and 3 investigated idiopathic sudden sensorineural hearing loss ( ).
 

## Evidence of miRNA Role in Cochlear Oxidative Stress
House Ear Institute organ of Corti (HEI-OC1) cells subjected to oxidative stress by incubation with hydrogen peroxide demonstrated increased ROS, apoptosis, and upregulation of miR-653-5p accompanied by downregulation of Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1) mRNA, protein and Long noncoding RNA H19 (LncRNA H19) expression (Xie et al., 2022). LncRNA H19 a greater than 200 nucleotide RNA is increasingly recognized as a competitive inhibitor of several miRNAs through which it affects several disease processes (Wang et al., 2020). Xie et al. (2022) found that Lentiviral LncRNA H19 transfection partly mitigated oxidative stress-induced HEI-OC1 cellular apoptosis by downregulating miR-653-5p. This in turn increased SIRT1 expression which promoted cell viability. An earlier study of oxidatively stressed HEI-OC1 cells reviewed by Safabakhsh et al. (2022) identified dysregulation of multiple miRNAs including mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and insulin-like growth factor 1 signaling pathway miRNAs miR-383, -208a, -377, -181d, -874, -29a, -17, and -200c that may be involved in cochlea oxidative stress-induced cell death. These and other miRNA pathways are being investigated for their role in the pathogenesis of acquired SNHL.

## Ototoxic Sensorineural Hearing Loss and Oxidative Stress
Gentamicin exerts its ototoxic effect by promoting oxidative stress in rat cochlea tissue and marginal cell cultures as demonstrated by gentamicin-induced tissue and cell increases in malondialdehyde and reduction in superoxide dismutase (SOD) a potent cellular antioxidant (Ding and Wang, 2022). Furthermore, they identified that miRNA-106A was upregulated in the serum of patients and cochlear tissue of rats treated with gentamicin and that this was inversely related to the level of connexin-43. Therefore, they concluded that miRNA-106A was a promotor of gentamicin-induced oxidative stress mediated ototoxicity. Gentamicin ototoxicity was significantly alleviated by treatment with a miRNA-106A antagonist and the mitigating effect of miRNA-106A antagonist was partly reversed by connexin-43 inhibition. The latter indicates that miR-106A downregulation of connexin-43 contributes to gentamicin ototoxicity.
Mouse inner ear stem cell–derived exosomes provide another gentamicin ototoxicity-induced oxidative stress mitigating strategy by reversing oxidative stress changes in malondialdehyde and SOD (Lai et al., 2020). The effect was dose-dependent and correlated with the up-regulation of miR-182-5p and the down-regulation of FOXO3 mRNA and protein expression reversing the actions of gentamicin. FOXO3 is proapoptotic and attenuation of its activity reduced HEI-OC1 apoptosis. Therefore, miR-182-5p/FOXO3 is another gentamicin-induced ototoxicity pathway worthy of further exploration (Ramkumar et al., 2021). Liu et al. (2021) identified that cisplatinum downregulated miR-455-5p expression leading to Phosphatase and Tensin homolog (PTEN) upregulation. PTEN subsequently deactivates the phosphoinositide-3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (Akt) pathway. The latter was associated with increased hair cell apoptosis. Furthermore, they replicated these in vitro findings in a mouse model and mitigated cisplatinum ototoxicity by pre-treatment with Resveratrol, a naturally occurring antioxidant with miRNA regulatory functionality. Resveratrol had the reverse effect to that of cisplatinum on miR-455-5p expression. The cisplatinum ototoxicity amelioration effect of upregulation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway has also been demonstrated with other compounds (Zhang et al., 2022). Several other anti-oxidative agents are under investigation as potential treatments for cisplatinum ototoxicity in registered clinical trials. One of these agents was found to reduce ototoxicity in patients undergoing cisplatinum treatment with a similar safety profile compared to placebo in a Phase 1 trial (NCT04262336) (Rischin et al., 2023). Several other trials are currently recruiting (NCT05628233), of unknown recruitment status (NCT01451853), or recently completed (NCT04226456). The results of all these trials have not yet been reported on the clinical trials register but the agents under investigation are N-acetyl cysteine, azasetron besylate, sodium thiosulphate, ebselen, and statins. The statin trial (NCT03225157) demonstrated a 53% odds ratio reduction in ototoxicity in atorvastatin users with no alteration in cancer survival rate in a cohort of cisplatin treated Head and Neck cancer patients, miRNA expression levels were not investigated in this study nor in any of the other identified registered clinical ototoxicity trials (Fernandez et al., 2021). However, a review by Climent et al. (2020) outlines that N-acetyl cysteine exerts its effect in part through the upregulation of miR-155.
Hydrogen therapy was not found to be effective in a small study of nasopharyngeal cancer patients (Kong et al., 2022). Sodium thiosulfate on the other hand was found to prevent ototoxicity in children undergoing cisplatinum treatment for hepatoblastoma (Brock et al., 2018). N-acetylcysteine also protected against aminoglycoside ototoxicity in end-stage renal disease patients through long-term hearing deteriorated in both the treatment and control groups (Vural et al., 2018). Therefore, the results from clinical trials of antioxidant treatments have been variable and the optimal antioxidant therapy regimen is yet to be determined. Additionally, different ototoxic agents appear to preferentially exert their deleterious effect through different pathways miRNA-106A/connexin-43 and miR-182-5p/FOXO3 in the case of gentamicin and miR-455-5p/PTEN/PI3K-Akt in cisplatinum ototoxicity. This suggests that ototoxin-specific therapies are required rather than a general antioxidant approach.
Ototoxicity can also be a consequence of occupational exposure to solvents, namely benzene, toluene, and xylene. Sisto et al. (2022) studied workers with exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and occupational noise with and without audiometric evidence of hearing loss. They found that miR-195-5p and miR-122-5p downregulation correlated with increased pure tone audiometry-measured hearing impairment. Distortion product otoacoustic emission, a measured reduction in cochlear function correlated with miR-497-5p and -92b-5p downregulation and miR-206 upregulation. It is difficult to ascertain the effect of VOCs and noise singly or combined on the identified correlations of miRNA dysregulation with hearing loss and cochlear functional reduction. The authors did not seek evidence of oxidative stress in the participants. However, in a previous study not directly investigating hearing loss, the authors also found that industrial painters with exposure to VOCs had a higher plasma level of miR-589-5p, miR-941, miR-146b-3p, and miR-27a-3p compared to controls (Sisto et al., 2020). These four miRNAs were statistically correlated with urinary 8-oxodGuo, a biomarker of DNA oxidative damage. These miRNAs also have established roles in other oxidative stress pathways – for instance, miR-589-5p is implicated in the regulation of the cyclo-oxygenase 2, a gene with far-reaching pro-inflammatory effects (Matsui et al., 2013). The relationship of VOC-associated miRNAs, hearing loss and oxidative stress deserves further study.

## Noise-Induced Hearing Loss, miRNAs, and Oxidative Stress
Noise especially in the forms of cumulative occupational exposure or acute blast trauma is an important cause of SNHL for which there is evidence of miRNA dysregulation. Ha et al. (2021) identified several differentially expressed miRNAs in the hippocampi of rats following noise exposure namely miR-758-5p, -210-5p, -370-5p, -652-5p, -3544, -128-1-5p, -665, -188-5p, and -874-5p increased, while miR-448-3p, -204-5p, and -204-3p decreased. These are implicated in regulating several genes involved in parallel and overlapping signaling pathways. Notably, miR-204-3p was found to have an anti-oxidative role (Tao et al., 2021), supporting Fetoni et al. (2013)’s finding that oxidative stress is involved in noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL).

## miRNAs in Oxidative Stress Mediated Hair Cell Loss in Age-Related Hearing Loss
Hao et al. (2019) identified that an allelic polymorphism of the LncRNA myocardial ischemia-associated transcript (MIAT) increased the risk of human ARHL. They demonstrated that miR-29b downregulated MIAT and cochlear SIRT1, and increased mitochondrial dysfunction and hair cell apoptosis with age in C57BL/6 mice similar to findings from a study by Xie et al. (2022). MIAT and miR-29b appear to act in a negative feedback loop since MIAT was demonstrated to have a similar action to LncRNA H19 but it increases SIRT1 and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1-alpha (PGC-1α) levels by downregulating miR-29b expression (Hao et al., 2019). LncRNA H19 increased SIRT1 expression by downregulating miR-653-5p (Xie et al., 2022). GM44593, another lncRNA that is upregulated in the aged cochlea of the C57BL/6 mouse ARHL model (Su et al., 2019), has also been demonstrated to inhibit the inner ear cell apoptotic and mitochondrial dysfunction effects of oxidative stress by switching down miR-29b (Li et al., 2022). Li et al. (2022) also demonstrated that miR-29b directly targets the WNK lysine deficient protein kinase 1 (WNK1) gene to downregulate WNK1 protein levels while Gm44593 has the reverse effect. GM44593 is postulated to have ARHL therapeutic potential based on their finding that oxidative stress induction increased GM44593 and WNK1 mRNA expression. MiR-29b mimic and inhibitor transfection of HEI-OC1 cells experiments also support a miR-29b role in ARHL (Xue et al., 2016). However, before LncRNAs can be considered for human therapeutic trials their carcinogenesis potential will need to be excluded or mitigated (Carlevaro-Fita et al., 2020).
SIRT1 has also been found to be downregulated by miR-34a in oxidatively stressed mouse and HEI-OC1 cell models. Furthermore, pharmacological activation of SIRT1 with resveratrol, as well as miR-34a inhibition, both reduced age-related cochlear cell loss and hearing thresholds in old mice (Xiong et al., 2019). Therefore, miR-29b and miR-34a are both overexpressed in response to oxidative stress and through downregulation of SIRT1/PGC-1α signaling in the aging cochlea. Resveratrol was also mentioned previously in this review to upregulate miR-455-5p which mitigated cisplatin ototoxicity via the PTEN-PI3K-Akt pathway (Liu et al., 2021). SIRT1 is a known activator of Akt and thus resveratrol may have an otoprotective role through both of these converging pathways (Yang and Dong, 2021).
Yang et al. (2021) demonstrated that inner ear administration of exosomes derived from cochlear progenitor cells can be used prophylactically to reduce ischemia-reperfusion cochlear hair cell loss in mice. MiR-21-5p, miR-26a-5p, and miR-181-5p were found to be upregulated in these cochleae. These findings were further supported by similar results in mice given exosomes derived from miR-21-transfected neural progenitor cells (Hao et al., 2022). However, there is so far no evidence that treatment with exosomes will be effective post injury, which limits its real-world therapeutic application.

## Sudden Sensorineural Hearing Loss, miRNAs, and Oxidative Stress
The etiology of idiopathic Sudden SNHL is unknown though there are two predominant theories - ischemic and viral. The sudden onset, higher occurrence with age, and clinical response to hyperbaric oxygen therapy support the ischemic theory (Joshua et al., 2022). Bioinformatic analyses suggest that MAPK pathway genes are likely targets of differentially expressed miRNAs identified in the blood of sudden sensorineural hearing loss (SSNHL) patients after the onset of hearing loss (Li et al., 2017; Nunez et al., 2020). Hypoxamirs are known to target the MAPK signaling pathway and experimentally induced SSNHL in guinea pigs is associated with miR (Gupta et al., 2018; Xie et al., 2021). Xie et al. (2021) identified that histone deacetylase 2 a transcription regulator known to be active in many diseases downregulates miR-204-5p. Furthermore, hypoxia-induced downregulation of histone deacetylase 2 in HEI-OC1 cells upregulates miR 204-5p which in turn downregulates apoptosis-inhibition genes Bcl-2 and Bcl-xl resulting in increased cochlear hair cell apoptosis. Notably, miR 204-5p was one of the differentially expressed miRNAs identified in the rat model of NIHL by Ha et al. (2021) as discussed above. Another phase 3 SSNHL clinical trial utilized a synthetic peptide inhibitor of MAPK8 as the active intervention agent (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT02561091). This intervention failed to improve hearing on day 28 compared to baseline in SSNHL patients, though it did show a significant and clinically meaningful improvement in a subpopulation of profound SSNHL patients (Staecker et al., 2019). A previous trial with the same sponsor also demonstrated improved hearing outcomes in severe to profound SSNHL patients (Suckfuell et al., 2014). Thus, there is complimentary evidence from multiple sources suggesting that MAPK signaling pathway hypoxamirs are important in the pathogenesis of SSNHL, particularly when the hearing loss is profound.
Elevated plasma levels of 5-hydroxytryptamine have been identified in 90% of SSNHL patients and miR-143 targets the 5-hydroxytryptamine 2B receptor (Drouet et al., 2020; Pang et al. 2021). However, a 5HT3 antagonist Sens-401 the active intervention, in a randomized controlled trial of 115 SSNHL participants was not superior to placebo (NCT03603314). Pioglitazone, a thiazolidinedione drug used for glucose control, modifies peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma a cellular transcription factor that regulates genes involved in the oxidative stress response (Paciello et al., 2018). Pioglitazone has been shown to mitigate cochlear hair cell loss related to gentamicin ototoxicity (Sekulic-Jablanovic et al., 2017). It was previously found to increase miR-24a and reduce coronary vessel intimal hyperplasia so improving blood flow (Hong et al., 2015). It is currently the subject of a Phase 3 trial for SSNHL (NCT03331627).
Various inhalational therapies aimed at combating hypoxia with air delivered by positive airway pressure (NCT04192656) and a hydrogen and oxygen mixture as an antioxidant (NCT04881435) are being assessed as treatments for SSNHL in ongoing clinical trials. However, Okada et al. (2022) recently reported that 3% inhaled hydrogen adjuvant therapy offered no advantage over oral prednisone and rescue intra-tympanic dexamethasone in SSNHL patients.

## Autoimmune Sensorineural Hearing Loss, miRNAs, and Oxidative Stress
Autoimmune SNHL accounts for less than 1% of hearing loss cases (Bovo et al., 2006). Zhang et al. (2019) proposed that upregulation of miR-8112 inhibits the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway genes and thereby contributes to the pathogenesis of autoimmune SNHL. They hypothesize that this signaling pathway can be linked to oxidative stress by findings from a study by Liu et al. (2016) that β-catenin deficiency in hair cells decreased the expression of antioxidant enzymes. This study however did not directly test oxidative stress in an autoimmune SNHL model, and our search did not yield any articles that did. The pathophysiology of this rare form of hearing loss remains largely unclear, and thus these studies revealed new miRNAs to study with respect to oxidative stress and autoimmune SNHL.

## Conclusions
The evidence highlights the role of miRNAs in cochlear oxidative stress, especially in acquired SNHL. Many included studies investigated the role of single selected miRNAs, predominantly miR-34a and miR-29b, in ARHL. Studies on medication-induced ototoxicity have prompted several promising clinical trials in ototoxin-specific therapies, while studies on occupational chemical ototoxicity have suggested miRNAs play a role in oxidative stress. The miRNAs involved in iatrogenic ototoxicity are dissimilar to those identified in occupational chemical ototoxicity. Additionally, idiopathic SSNHL research suggests intricate connections between miRNAs, oxidative stress, and the MAPK signaling pathway. Despite challenges in studying this enigmatic condition, clinical trials are being conducted into potential treatments including inhalational hydrogen therapy and pioglitazone. A comprehensive list of miRNAs investigated in our literature search can be found in . The heterogeneity of animal models, cell lines, and methods demonstrated in precludes a meta-analysis of the data. While one study measured hypoxamirs in a population of SNHL patients compared to controls (Ding and Wang, 2022), no studies were identified that investigated how hypoxamirs acted in humans. The animal and in vitro studies discussed in this review can pave the way for similar miRNA studies in humans.
