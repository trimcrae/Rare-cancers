3.2

## Mn Exposure Impairs Cognitive Function and
Disrupts AA Metabolism, Leading to Reduced 15d-PGJ2 Levels
To assess cognitive function, we first subjected mice to the Y-maze
test. Compared to controls, Mn-exposed mice exhibited a significantly
lower percentage of spontaneous alternation ( A, P < 0.05), indicating
an impairment in spatial working memory. No significant difference
in the total number of arm entries was observed between groups (Figure S3), suggesting that the cognitive impairment
was not attributable to altered locomotor activity. Consistent with
the behavioral deficit, neuroinflammatory responses were evident in
the striatum. Immunofluorescence analysis revealed microglial activation,
characterized by an increase in iNOS-positive cells and a decrease
in Arg1-positive cells ( B). These findings were corroborated by qPCR results ( C,D). To elucidate
the underlying molecular mechanisms, we performed RNA sequencing on
striatal tissues. Volcano plot illustrated the differentially expressed
genes in the two exposed groups compared to the control ( E,F). Weighted gene
coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) identified key modules of coexpressed
genes, among which the blue module demonstrated the highest positive
correlation with Mn exposure ( G,H). Functional enrichment analysis revealed that
genes within the blue module were significantly involved in fatty
acid metabolic process, neuroinflammatory responses, and microglial
cell activation ( I), thereby linking lipid metabolism dysregulation to the observed
neuroinflammation. Based on this transcriptomic evidence, we hypothesized
that Mn exposure alters the landscape of oxidized lipids in the striatum.
Targeted lipidomic analysis confirmed a significant decrease in the
level of 15d-PGJ2, a specialized pro-resolving mediator derived from
AA, in both exposure groups ( J,K). The pathway analysis showed that Mn exposure
broadly perturbed AA metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, and unsaturated
fatty acid biosynthetic ( L). An integrated analysis of genes and metabolites
further confirmed that the AA metabolic pathway was among the most
significantly altered by Mn exposure ( M).
 To validate the translational relevance of these
findings, we examined
key markers in a human population with occupational Mn exposure. Consistent
with our animal data, the expression of AA metabolism-related genes
(PTGS2, ALOX15, and ALOX5) was significantly upregulated, while the
expression of GPX4 was downregulated ( A–D, P < 0.05).
They are key genes for AA metabolism and are also involved in the
regulation of oxidative stress. E, P < 0.05). Combined
with these results, our study suggests that Mn exposure may affect
AA metabolism by interfering with the levels of genes such as PTGS2,
ALOX15, and related metabolites.
 
3.3

## MMP9 is Implicated as a 15d-PGJ2 Target in
Mn-Induced Neuroinflammation and Cognition Impairment
To
identify potential mediators linking Mn-induced neuroinflammation
to cognitive impairment, we screened for genes that might interact
with 15d-PGJ2. Bioinformatics analysis nominated MMP9 as a candidate
target ( F).
Molecular docking further supported this interaction ( G). The binding energy of 15d-PGJ2
to MMP9 protein was −8.2 kcal/mol, indicating that the two
have a binding capacity. This is consistent with the finding that
MMP9 is elevated in the peripheral blood of the Mn population. We
also discovered that Mmp9 was overexpressed in the striatum of Mn-exposed
mice ( H). Simultaneously,
we discovered a negative relationship between workers’ cognitive
function and serum MMP9 ( I), suggesting that MMP9 upregulation is associated
with cognitive impairment.

3.4

## Mn Induces Microglial Inflammation via the
AA Metabolism
To investigate the role of AA metabolism in
Mn-induced neuroinflammation, we utilized BV2 microglial cells. We
selected the optimal dose of MnCl2 (50, 100, 200 μmol/L)
by observing cell viability ( A). We assessed its effects on inflammatory responses
and AA metabolism. Mn exposure promoted a pro-inflammatory state,
elevating pro-inflammatory factors and suppressing anti-inflammatory
factors ( B).
Concurrently, Mn increased intracellular ROS levels ( C), and upregulated MMP9, as
confirmed by immunofluorescence staining and ELISA ( D,E). Mn also significantly
altered the expression of key genes of AA metabolism, upregulating
Ptgs1 and Ptgs2 while downregulating Ptgds and Alox15 ( F).
 Pharmacological inhibition of COX-2 with NS-398
attenuated Mn-induced
microglial inflammation ( G), normalized the expression of AA metabolism-related
genes ( H),
and reduced oxidative stress ( I). Furthermore, NS-398 pretreatment suppressed the
Mn-induced upregulation of Mmp9 mRNA ( J).

3.5

## 15d-PGJ2 Protects Against Mn-Induced Neurotoxicity
by Suppressing MMP9
To elucidate the mechanism underlying
the protective effect of 15d-PGJ2 against Mn-induced neurotoxicity,
we first investigated its impact on Mn-induced inflammatory imbalance
and oxidative stress. As shown in A,B, 15d-PGJ2 treatment significantly attenuated
the imbalance of inflammatory cytokines and excessive ROS production
in Mn-exposed BV2 microglia. Furthermore, we found that 15d-PGJ2 effectively
suppressed Mn-induced elevation of Mmp9 ( C), suggesting a potential association between
Mmp9 inhibition and the observed cytoprotective effects.
 To directly determine whether Mmp9 is a necessary
mediator of Mn-induced
neurotoxicity, we performed a loss-of-function experiment using siRNA.
Transfection with si-Mmp9 achieved a high knockdown efficiency ( D). Notably, Mmp9
deficiency significantly attenuated Mn exposure-induced inflammatory
responses ( E) and alleviated oxidative stress ( F), with protective effects consistent with
the mode of action of 15d-PGJ2.
Collectively, these findings
demonstrate that 15d-PGJ2 alleviates
Mn-induced neuroinflammation and oxidative stress by targeting Mmp9
and critically establish Mmp9 as a key downstream effector molecule
in Mn-mediated neurotoxicity.

## Discussion
This cross-sectional study
identified multidimensional cognitive
impairment in Mn-exposed workers, which correlated significantly with
urinary Mn levels. Inflammation is one of the molecular mechanisms
leading to cognitive impairment, and
neuroinflammation is an important mechanism of action for Mn-induced
neurotoxicity. Peripheral inflammatory markers may reflect cerebral
inflammation and predict clinical outcomes. The mechanism by which peripheral inflammation adversely affects
central nervous system and cognitive function could be that peripheral
inflammatory factors can penetrate the blood–brain barrier
and activate microglia, eventually leading to neuronal loss and cognitive
behavioral dysfunction.
,
 Pro-inflammatory cytokines
and neurological damage markers were significantly altered in Mn-exposed
workers (P < 0.05), and these peripheral biomarkers
correlated with both urinary Mn and cognitive performanceconsistent
with prior studies.
−

 Mediation analysis highlighted TNF-α as a key
mediating role in Mn-associated cognitive impairment.
In a mouse
model of Mn exposure, behavioral deficits in the Y-maze
were observed alongside microglia activation, indicating cognitive
impairment and neuroinflammation. Transcriptomic profiling of the
striatum indicated enrichment of hub genes in neuroinflammation and
fatty acid metabolism. Targeted lipidomics on the striatum further
revealed differentially expressed metabolites in the AA metabolic
pathway, with joint-pathway analysis indicating Mn-induced alternations
in both genes and metabolites in AA metabolism. Notably, 15d-PGJ2an
anti-inflammatory metabolite produced via the cyclooxygenase pathway
from PGD2was downregulated in
both exposure
groups. 15d-PGJ2 is known to attenuate neuroinflammation and microglial
activation in neurological disorders, including PD and AD, via PPARγ activation and NF-κB suppression. In recent years, a study developed magnetic
nanocarriers to deliver 15d-PGJ2 to enhance its targeting and anti-inflammatory
effects in cerebral hemorrhage, providing new ideas for clinical translation.

In the Mn-exposed population, key mRNAs
related to AA metabolism
were dysregulated, and serum 15d-PGJ2 were significantly reduced consistent
with the animal results. In order to elucidate the mechanisms associated
with neuroinflammation and to identify potential gene targets associated
with Mn exposure in the pathophysiology of cognitive impairment, our
study utilized the CTD and Gene Cards databases, combined with the
results of striatal transcriptional profiling in an animal model,
and through molecular docking, we identified MMP9 as apotential mediator
of Mn-induced neuroinflammation and cognitive impairment. MMP9 is
a zinc-dependent protease. As a key mediator of synaptic remodelling
and neuroinflammation, its level is closely related to cognitive function. Elevated peripheral blood MMP9 levels can disrupt
the integrity of the blood–brain barrier, leading to the infiltration
of inflammatory factors into the center, exacerbating neurological
damage and cognitive impairment. Targeted
inhibition of MMP9 is proposed as a therapeutic strategy in epilepsy-related
studies.

In BV2 microglial, Mn
exposure induced inflammatory activation,
altered AA metabolism gene expression, and upregulated Mmp9. Mn activates
microglia to provoke neuroinflammation and promotes oxidative stress
by increasing ROS production.
−

 COX-2 is the key rate-limiting
enzyme in AA metabolism, catalyzing the conversion of AA to prostaglandins. Treatment of BV2 cells with the COX-2 inhibitor
NS-398
,
 alleviated Mn-induced inflammation and downregulated
Mmp9. These results implicate AA metabolism in Mn-induced neuroinflammation,
potentially through Mmp9.
To clarify the functional role of
Mmp9, we performed Mmp9 knockdown
using small interfering RNA. Results attenuated Mn-induced pro-inflammatory
responses and ROS, confirming its functional role in neuroinflammation
and oxidative stress.
We further demonstrated that 15d-PGJ2
suppressed Mn-induced neuroinflammation
in BV2 cells, reducing inflammatory gene expression, Mmp9 secretion,
and ROS. 15d-PGJ2 mitigates ROS accumulation by upregulating antioxidant
enzymes via PPARγ activation, thereby interrupting the ROS–NF–κB
positive feedback loop that amplifies neuroinflammation. Since Mmp9 inhibition is known to be neuroprotective, our data suggest 15d-PGJ2 exerts its protective
effect partly through Mmp9 downregulation.
This study establishes
MMP9 as a central effector in Mn-induced
neuroinflammation and cognitive impairment. We show that 15d-PGJ2
alleviates Mn neurotoxicity by inhibiting MMP9 and restoring redox
and metabolic homeostasis. These findings clarify the role of AA metabolic
disruption in Mn neurotoxicity and highlight the 15d-PGJ2–MMP9
axis as a potential therapeutic target.
While we observed downregulation
of 15d-PGJ2 in both human and
mouse models after Mn exposure, the precise molecular mechanisms driving
this reduction remain unclear. We speculate that Mn may directly inhibit
key synthetic enzymes, such as PGD2 synthase or lipoxygenase, or promote
the degradation of 15d-PGJ2. Alternatively, Mn might interfere with
upstream substrates or transcriptional regulators of the AA metabolic
cascade. Future studies should explore the effect of Mn on the expression
and activity of enzymes involved in 15d-PGJ2 synthesis, and utilize
isotopic tracing to delineate the metabolic flux in this pathway.
Several limitations deserve consideration. First, the population-based
research component employed a cross-sectional design. While this design
effectively revealed strong associations between Mn exposure, neuroinflammation,
AA metabolic disorders, and cognitive decline, it cannot establish
clear causal temporal relationships. Other unmeasured confounding
factors may also influence the observed associations. Future studies
should employ longitudinal cohort designs or intervention studies
targeting high-risk populations, incorporating larger and more diverse
cohort groups. This approach will validate these findings, explore
potential finer individual variations and cognitive changes within
Mn exposure effects, and provide more definitive causal evidence for
Mn neurotoxicity. Second, although this study confirms the anti-inflammatory
effects of 15d-PGJ2 at the cellular level, its ability to reverse
Mn-induced cognitive behavioral deficits in vivo requires further
investigation. In vivo intervention with 15d-PGJ2 or its analogues
represents a critical next step to validate the therapeutic potential
of the pathway proposed in this study, which is the explicit direction
of our subsequent research. Finally, the intricate interplay between
the AA metabolic network and inflammatory signaling warrants further
investigation.

## Conclusion
In summary, this integrated
human and experimental study reveals
key pathways associated with Mn-induced cognitive impairment, implicating
neuroinflammation and disrupted AA metabolism as central components
of its mechanism. Our data indicate that Mn exposure is associated
with reduced levels of the endogenous anti-inflammatory mediator 15d-PGJ2
in both exposed workers and mouse models. This downregulation appears
to disturb homeostatic balance, contributing to microglial activation,
a shift toward pro-inflammatory lipid mediators, and subsequent cognitive
impairment. Furthermore, we identify MMP9 as a functionally active
participant in this process. Collectively, these findings suggest
that 15d-PGJ2 deficiency and dysregulated AA metabolism represent
important mechanisms in Mn neurotoxicity. This study not only advances
the mechanistic understanding of Mn-related cognitive impairment but
also highlights 15d-PGJ2 and MMP9 as potential targets for future
therapeutic strategies.
