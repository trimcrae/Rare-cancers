## Abstract
This review synthesizes a decade of advancements in nucleic acid therapeutics for liver diseases, incorporating bibliometric analysis and translational evaluation. The field has evolved from foundational viral vector engineering to precision genome editing and RNA-based modulation, with advancements in CRISPR-Cas9 and innovations in non-viral delivery systems. Our analysis highlights the concentrated research efforts made in metabolic disorders and hepatocellular carcinoma, and reveals an emerging emphasis on multifactorial pathologies. Although clinical milestones highlight important advancements in targeting strategies, significant challenges remain in immune compatibility and preclinical translation. The integration of computational modeling, human-relevant disease models, and combinatorial strategies places nucleic acid therapies in a unique position to tackle the evolving global liver disease burden through mechanism-driven interventions.
Keywords: Nucleic acid therapeutics, Liver diseases, Bibliometric analysis

## Introduction
Liver disorders, including metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD, formerly known as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease [NAFLD]) and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), remain a significant global health issue and contribute to approximately 2 million deaths annually. Over the past decade, obesity-driven conditions such as MASLD and its progressive form non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) have risen alarmingly. This increase is compounded by persistent challenges in managing genetic liver disorders and complications associated with viral hepatitis. Although conventional therapies, including pharmacologic agents, lifestyle modifications, and liver transplantation, have improved patient outcomes, their effectiveness is limited by challenges such as drug resistance, procedural risks, and insufficient donor availability, highlighting the urgent need for transformative therapeutic strategies. 
The development of nucleic acid therapeutics in the past decade has fundamentally reshaped the therapeutic landscape for liver diseases. Progress in RNA interference (RNAi), messenger RNA (mRNA) delivery, and CRISPR-based genome editing has enabled precise targeting of disease-causing genes, presenting transformative opportunities to address monogenic disorders (e.g., Wilson's disease and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency), as well as complex multifactorial diseases such as MASLD/NASH and HCC. Because of its distinctive vascular architecture and regenerative capacity, the liver is particularly well suited to nucleic acid interventions, as illustrated by several recent key achievements, including the approval of patisiran for the treatment of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in 2018 by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the clinical application of adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated gene therapies for hemophilia. , A comprehensive overview of the present clinical and preclinical development of nucleic acid-based therapeutics targeting liver-associated diseases is provided in . These therapeutics are categorized according to their anatomical and pathophysiological relevance. In addition, prominent drug candidates, molecular targets, and drug types are emphasized. However, despite these breakthroughs, substantial challenges persist, such as off-target effects of gene-editing tools, immune responses to viral vectors, and the limited predictive accuracy of preclinical models for human liver pathophysiology. 
 To systematically assess the evolution of this dynamic field, we performed a bibliometric analysis of 1973 peer-reviewed articles published between 2015 and 2024, retrieved from the PubMed database. This methodology quantifies research trends, delineates collaborative networks, and highlights groundbreaking advances, from lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-encapsulated siRNA delivery to base editing technologies, that have defined the past decade. Our analysis explored three pivotal questions:
1)In what ways have technological advancements in nucleic acid delivery (e.g., GalNAc conjugation, AAV capsid engineering) and genome editing tools (e.g., prime editing, CRISPR-Cas9 variants) influenced the trajectory of research in this field.
2)What disease-specific shifts have emerged, particularly regarding the transition from rare genetic disorders to multifaceted acquired liver diseases.
3)Which key translational challenges, such as mitigating immune-related toxicities or refining large-animal models for clinical extrapolation, persist.
By combining quantitative bibliometric insights with a critical evaluation of clinical milestones, this review underscores the intersection of molecular innovation and unmet clinical needs, providing a strategic framework to guide the development of safe and effective nucleic acid therapies tailored to address the diverse spectrum of liver diseases.

## Data and methods
2.1.

## Data collection
The PubMed database was used to identify peer-reviewed publications published between January 2015 and December 2024. A systematic search strategy was implemented using the following query: (nucleic acid therapeutics OR gene therapy OR genetic medicine OR RNA-based therapeutics OR DNA therapeutics OR nucleic acid drugs) AND (liver disease). Filters were applied to limit the results to English-language original research articles, resulting in a total of 1973 publications. Non-research articles (e.g., reviews, editorials) and duplicate entries were excluded systematically through automated screening processes.

2.2.

## Bibliometric analysis
Data mining and visualization processes were performed using the Citexs analytics platform. The analysis centered on key metrics, including annual publication trends, contributions by countries and institutions, author productivity, and journal distributions. Affiliation data extracted from PubMed records were standardized to resolve inconsistencies (e.g., “university of pennsylvania” vs “University of Pennsylvania”). Collaborative networks were mapped to identify leading research hubs, and institutions and authors were ranked based on publication volume.

2.3.

## Keyword and thematic analysis
Author-defined keywords and terms extracted from titles and abstracts were systematically examined to identify emerging research hotspots. High-frequency keywords (e.g., “hepatocellular carcinoma”, “AAV”, “CRISPR/Cas9”) were ranked, and temporal trends were assessed by dividing the study period into four distinct intervals (2015–2017, 2018–2020, 2021–2022, 2023–2024). Shifts in thematic focus were quantified by analyzing keyword ranking changes across these intervals.

2.4.

## Gene–disease association mining
The BioBERT biomedical language model was employed to systematically extract gene and disease entities from article abstracts. Gene entities were mapped to standardized Human Genome Organisation Gene Nomenclature Committee symbols, whereas disease entities were aligned with Medical Subject Headings terms. The statistical significance of gene–disease associations was determined using Fisher's exact test, with false discovery rate correction applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method.

2.5.

## Ethical and data quality considerations
To address potential biases from incomplete metadata, manual validation was performed for the top 50 authors and institutions. Ambiguous terms (e.g., “NAFLD” vs. “MASLD”) were reconciled using expert-curated ontologies. All analytical workflows, including keyword co-occurrence networks and temporal trend visualizations, were executed using open-source tools (VOSviewer, Gephi) to ensure reproducibility.

## Results and visualization
3.1.

## Annual publication trends
A total of 1973 publications on nucleic acid therapeutics for liver diseases were published between January 2015 and December 2024, corresponding to an average annual output of 198 publications. The field demonstrated exponential growth, reaching a peak of 293 publications in 2024. The most significant surge occurred in 2021, marked by a year-on-year growth rate of 42.59%, indicating a pivotal inflection point in research activity ( ).
 
3.2.

## Geographical and institutional contributions
The United States spearheaded global research efforts, contributing 31% of total publications (614 articles). This was followed by China (22%, 427 articles) and Italy (7%, 130 articles). These three countries collectively accounted for approximately 60% of the total outputs in the field, underscoring their substantial investments in translational research ( A). At the institutional level, University College London (UCL) and the University of Pennsylvania were recognized as leading research hubs, each contributing 27 studies ( B). UCL's research focused on optimizing AAV vectors for inherited metabolic disorders, whereas the University of Pennsylvania prioritized advancing CRISPR-Cas9 applications for HCC. Other prominent institutions included the University of California, Baylor College of Medicine, and Capital Medical University, which specialized in non-viral siRNA delivery systems for MASLD.
 
3.3.

## Leading authors and journals
Giuseppe Ronzitti (France) and Federico Mingozzi (France) were the most prolific authors, with research focusing on AAV-mediated therapies and immune modulation strategies. James M. Wilson from the University of Pennsylvania (USA) was ranked third and was recognized for his contributions to the development of liver-targeted AAV serotypes. More than half of the top 10 authors were affiliated with institutions in the USA or France, highlighting the regional dominance of these countries in the field ( ). Molecular Therapy–Methods & Clinical Development had the highest publication output (49 articles), with a primary focus on vector design, while high-impact journals such as Hepatology (impact factor: 17.43 in 2020) featured clinical trial outcomes and reviews ( ).
 
3.4.

## Keyword hotspots and thematic evolution
The most frequently mentioned keywords were HCC (805 mentions), liver (713 mentions), MASLD (683 mentions), and AAV (592 mentions). Early research (2015–2017) emphasized viral vector engineering and monogenic disorders, such as Wilson's disease. From 2018 to 2020, CRISPR-Cas9 and lipid nanoparticles emerged as prominent themes, driven by breakthroughs in non-viral delivery technologies. From 2021 onwards, over 60% of studies addressed complex acquired conditions such as MASLD/NASH and fibrosis, while studies focusing on precision medicine, including personalized therapy and biomarker development, increased by 215% ( ). The proportion of papers reporting viral vector studies declined from 55% to 34% of the total output between 2015 and 2024, whereas the percentage of studies describing non-viral systems increased from 18% to 47% during the same period. CRISPR-related publications increased 12-fold and were driven by key achievements such as the FDA approval of the first CRISPR-based therapy Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel; BLA: 125787) for the treatment of β-thalassemia in 2023. RNA-based therapeutics, particularly those that used siRNA and mRNA to target metabolic drivers, constituted 29% of research conducted after 2021.
 
3.5.

## Gene–disease associations and disease-specific research priorities
The genes SLC17A5 (167 studies), INS (161 studies), and TNF (157 studies) were the most extensively investigated. SLC17A5, which is associated with lysosomal acid lipase deficiency, was primarily studied in AAV-based gene replacement approaches. INS and TNF were identified as critical factors in MASLD pathogenesis, with preclinical models demonstrating that TNF knockdown reduced hepatic inflammation by 78%. CRISPR editing of SLC17A5 and ATP7B achieved correction rates exceeding 90% in genetic disorder models ( A). There were three main disease clusters: neoplastic disorders such as HCC (579 studies), metabolic conditions including MASLD/NASH (592 studies), and genetic pathologies such as Wilson's disease (185 studies). HCC research focused on oncogene silencing strategies using siRNA or CRISPR, with 19 clinical trials currently in progress. Most studies on MASLD investigated lipid metabolism regulators, whereas those focusing on genetic disorders primarily used AAV and CRISPR strategies, with 14 therapies advancing to late-stage trials ( B).

## Discussion
4.1.

## Therapeutic modalities in nucleic acid-based liver disease treatments
Over the past decade, groundbreaking advancements in nucleic acid-based therapeutics have transformed the treatment landscape for liver diseases. Innovations in RNAi, CRISPR-based genome editing, and targeted delivery platforms have accelerated therapeutic development and enabled precise modulation of disease pathways at the molecular level. These technologies, including siRNA-mediated gene silencing, ASO-driven mRNA modulation, and CRISPR correction of genetic defects, have contributed to addressing longstanding challenges in hepatic medicine, such as MASLD and HCC. The integration of these tools with advanced delivery systems, such as GalNAc conjugation and lipid nanoparticles, has enhanced liver-specific targeting while minimizing systemic toxicity, representing a paradigm shift from broad-spectrum pharmacotherapy to mechanism-driven precision medicine.
4.1.1.

## Clinical advances in RNAi and ASO technologies
RNAi and ASO technologies have emerged as leading strategies for gene silencing, offering targeted and durable suppression of disease-associated transcripts in liver disorders. RNAi-based therapies, particularly siRNAs, have shown significant efficacy in selectively degrading pathogenic mRNAs with high sequence specificity. A notable example is lumasiran, an siRNA targeting hydroxyacid oxidase 1 (HAO1), which has demonstrated favorable safety and efficacy profiles in patients with primary hyperoxaluria type 1 across Phase I/II and Phase III clinical trials. Treatment with lumasiran led to clinically meaningful reductions in urinary oxalate levels, confirming its therapeutic potential in this rare metabolic liver disease. Moreover, siRNA-based approaches are increasingly being explored for use in more complex hepatic conditions, including MASLD.
In parallel, ASOs have gained traction because of their chemical flexibility, stability, and potent mRNA degradation capabilities. For instance, bepirovirsen, an ASO that targets both hepatitis B virus (HBV) mRNA and pregenomic RNA, has shown encouraging results in the treatment of chronic hepatitis B, leading to sustained reductions in hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and HBV DNA levels in clinical trials. The development of ligand-conjugated ASOs, such as GalNAc-conjugated ASOs, has significantly enhanced their liver-specific delivery and potency, enabling efficient hepatocyte targeting while reducing systemic exposure and off-target effects. AZD2693, an ASO targeting PNPLA3, led to significant reductions in PNPLA3 mRNA and protein levels in primary human hepatocytes, resulting in decreased liver fat content and inflammatory markers in clinical trials. Similarly, ION224, an ASO targeting DGAT2, led to substantial improvements in MASH histological endpoints, with 44% of patients experiencing a relative reduction in liver steatosis of over 50%. The successful use of these ASO-based therapies not only confirms the efficacy of gene silencing but also highlights their potential in addressing complex metabolic liver diseases.

4.1.2.

## Progress and challenges in CRISPR-based gene editing
CRISPR technologies enable precise correction of mutations in monogenic liver diseases such as Wilson's disease and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. The CRISPR-Cas9 system has demonstrated high editing efficiency in preclinical models, enabling durable therapeutic outcomes through targeted genomic modifications. Recent advancements in base editing and prime editing technologies further broaden CRISPR's therapeutic scope. Base editors facilitate single-nucleotide corrections without inducing double-strand breaks, making them particularly suited for point mutations, while prime editors enable versatile edits, including insertions and deletions, to address complex genetic defects. 
Despite these breakthroughs, challenges persist. Delivery constraints, particularly the limited packaging capacity of viral vectors for large editor systems, necessitate ongoing optimization of non-viral delivery platforms. Immune responses to CRISPR components and off-target effects in hepatocytes remain critical concerns, although advancements in cell-specific promoters and engineered Cas9 variants show promise in mitigating these risks. The application of these tools to multifactorial liver diseases, such as MASH, represents an emerging frontier, with early studies highlighting their potential to modulate key pathogenic pathways. 

4.1.3.

## Gene replacement and augmentation
There have been significant advances in gene replacement therapies, particularly in the treatment of inherited metabolic disorders. AAV vectors have been extensively studied and optimized for the delivery of functional genes to hepatocytes. AAV-mediated therapies have shown success in preclinical and clinical studies, with some advancing to late-stage trials. For instance, AAV-based gene replacement approaches for lysosomal acid lipase deficiency have demonstrated the ability to restore enzyme activity and alleviate disease symptoms. The continuous improvement of AAV capsid engineering and promoter design has enhanced the efficiency and specificity of gene delivery, reducing the risk of immune responses and improving the therapeutic window. 

4.1.4.

## Evolution and optimization of delivery systems
The efficacy of nucleic acid therapeutics hinges on the capacity of delivery systems to navigate biological barriers while ensuring hepatocyte-specific targeting ( ). LNPs remain a cornerstone technology, leveraging ionizable lipids to encapsulate and protect RNA payloads, thereby achieving efficient hepatic delivery. Innovations in LNP formulations, such as pH-responsive lipid blends, have significantly improved endosomal escape and intracellular release. GalNAc conjugation continues to dominate oligonucleotide delivery, exploiting the high expression of asialoglycoprotein receptors on hepatocytes to enable potent, dose-efficient silencing of disease targets. 
 The use of emerging modalities, such as extracellular vesicles (EVs), offers distinct advantages, including inherent biocompatibility and natural tropism for specific cell types. EVs derived from hepatocytes or engineered to display targeting ligands show potential for precision delivery, particularly in fibrotic or inflamed liver microenvironments. However, scalability and cargo-loading limitations currently restrict their clinical use. Hybrid strategies, such as EV-LNP composites, aim to synergize the strengths of synthetic and biological systems, offering tunable delivery profiles with reduced immunogenicity. This strategic integration of delivery modalities, informed by disease pathophysiology and therapeutic payload, will drive the next wave of precision nucleic acid therapies.

4.2.

## Clinical challenges and translational barriers
The transition of nucleic acid therapeutics from preclinical validation to clinical utility is fraught with biological, technical, and regulatory complexities that necessitate rigorous interrogation. A critical barrier lies in the immunogenic profiles of delivery vectors, particularly AAVs, which exhibit pre-existing neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) in a significant subset of the population. This immunological hurdle was starkly evidenced in the Phase II trial of DTX301, an AAV8-based therapy for ornithine transcarbamylase deficiency, where NAbs abrogated transduction efficiency in 35% of enrolled patients, ultimately leading to trial discontinuation. To circumvent such limitations, innovative strategies such as engineered “immune-stealth” capsids and transient immunosuppressive regimens are under investigation, with early-phase trials demonstrating a 50% reduction in NAb-mediated neutralization through protease-cleavable decoy particles. 
Equally pressing are the disparities between preclinical models and human pathophysiology. Murine models, while instrumental in initial drug discovery, frequently fail to recapitulate the epigenetic heterogeneity and microbiome-driven metabolic crosstalk inherent to MASLD. This discordance was exemplified in recent ASO trials targeting transforming growth factor (TGF)-β1, in which results from rodent models predicted a 60% reduction in fibrosis, yet human cohorts exhibited no statistically significant improvement, a discrepancy attributed to interspecies variations in TGF-β receptor isoform expression. Emerging humanized organoid platforms, which integrate patient-derived hepatocytes, immune cells, and microbiota, now achieve >85% concordance with clinical outcomes in retrospective validation studies, offering a transformative tool for translational prediction. 
The therapeutic paradigm further grapples with the tension between durability and adaptability. While CRISPR-Cas9 editing enables permanent genomic correction, as exemplified by a >90% ATP7B restoration in Wilson's disease models, its static nature poses challenges in dynamic pathologies such as HCC, where clonal evolution necessitates adaptable interventions. In contrast, transient RNAi platforms, though tunable, face compliance barriers associated with repeated administration regimens, as evidenced by a 40% attrition rate in a long-term inclisiran extension study. Hybrid approaches, including photoactivatable Cas9 variants, now enable spatiotemporal control of gene editing, with preclinical HCC models demonstrating a 74% reduction in tumor burden through regulated MYC silencing. 
Regulatory frameworks struggle to harmonize rapid technological advances with patient safety imperatives. The FDA's 2024 mandate for 15-year post-marketing surveillance of AAV therapies highlights concerns over delayed genotoxicity, as evidenced by delayed hepatotoxicity in non-human primate studies of high-dose AAV-CRISPR vectors. This requirement has profound implications for trial design, as seen in the VERVE-101 trial for familial hypercholesterolemia, which now incorporates a 5-year monitoring protocol for off-target editing events. Concurrently, the termination of the ST-920 trial, a zinc finger nuclease-based therapy for Fabry disease, highlights the pitfalls of suboptimal transgene expression, with α-galactosidase A activity failing to exceed 5% of physiological levels despite optimized delivery. 
To navigate these challenges, a multidisciplinary paradigm is emerging. First, the adoption of organoid-microphysiological systems is bridging the preclinical-clinical divide. Second, modular delivery platforms, such as hybrid LNP-EV systems, enable dose titration and cell-specific targeting while mitigating immunogenicity. Finally, global regulatory harmonization initiatives are addressing CRISPR-specific challenges, with the European Medicines Agency and FDA jointly standardizing off-target assessment protocols to accelerate therapeutic development without compromising safety.
These advances collectively highlight the imperative for iterative innovation, where biological insights, engineering solutions, and regulatory agility converge to realize the transformative potential of nucleic acid therapies in hepatology.

4.3.

## Emerging strategies and future directions
To address the challenges faced by nucleic acid-based liver therapies and advance the field, several emerging strategies and future directions warrant further exploration. The integration of multi-omics technologies and artificial intelligence (AI) offers transformative opportunities. Through the analysis of molecular profiles of liver diseases using genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, researchers can identify key genes and pathways implicated in disease pathogenesis. This approach facilitates the development of more targeted and effective therapies. AI algorithms can further augment this process by predicting gene functions, designing optimized nucleic acid sequences, and modeling therapeutic outcomes, thus enhancing the efficiency and precision of drug development. For example, AI can be used to design potentially effective anticancer drugs by developing chemoinformatics or pharmacoinformatics models to predict molecular properties. An advanced deep learning framework, POLYGON, was developed through the integration of variational autoencoder, reinforcement learning, and random forest regression models to embed chemical spaces and iteratively generate novel molecular structures. The majority of the 32 generated lead compounds targeting mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 1 and mammalian target of rapamycin inhibited their activity at 1–10 μM and demonstrated reduced tumor cell viability in in vitro experimental validation. 
Additionally, combination therapies are emerging as a promising strategy to tackle complex liver diseases characterized by multiple pathological pathways. For instance, the combination of siRNAs targeting lipid metabolism regulators with ASOs targeting inflammatory mediators may yield synergistic effects in the treatment of NAFLD/NASH. Integrating nucleic acid-based therapies with traditional pharmacological agents or immune checkpoint inhibitors has the potential to enhance therapeutic efficacy and address treatment resistance. , Preclinical studies have demonstrated that combining siRNA ALG-125903 with ASO ALG-020579, alongside other anti-HBV agents, induced rapid and potent suppression of HBsAg in vivo.
Moreover, the continuous development of next-generation delivery systems is critical for improving the specificity and efficiency of nucleic acid delivery to the liver. Chemically modified GalNAc ligands and advanced nanoparticle formulations, such as those incorporating pH-sensitive or targeting ligands, can further enhance liver-specific uptake and reduce off-target distribution. Moreover, exploring non-invasive delivery routes, such as oral or inhalation administration, could improve patient compliance and convenience. The advancement of these delivery technologies will be instrumental in maximizing the therapeutic potential of nucleic acid-based therapies.
Finally, a more comprehensive understanding of the liver's unique immunological environment could inform the development of immunomodulatory strategies aimed at enhancing the tolerability and efficacy of nucleic acid therapies. Modulating immune responses to viral vectors or nucleic acid drugs using immunomodulatory agents or gene editing techniques could mitigate adverse immune reactions and enhance treatment outcomes. Additionally, leveraging the liver's immunotolerant properties to promote the acceptance of gene-edited cells or delivered nucleic acids constitutes a promising avenue for future research. , 

5.

## Conclusion and perspectives
Over the past decade, nucleic acid therapeutics have transitioned from experimental concepts to viable clinical strategies for liver diseases. This evolution has been driven by two key forces: advances in gene editing and RNA modulation technologies, and the persistent need for effective therapies against complex hepatic disorders such as MASLD and HCC. Among technological innovations, non-viral delivery platforms, notably GalNAc conjugates, LNPs, and EVs, have enabled more efficient and liver-specific delivery, while AI-assisted design tools have accelerated sequence optimization, delivery formulation, and therapeutic prediction.
Looking forward, clinical translation will depend on combination therapies that simultaneously target metabolic, inflammatory, and genetic pathways, and on the use of humanized models, such as patient-derived organoids and immune-competent chimeric mice, that better reflect human liver pathophysiology. These strategies will improve translational fidelity and therapeutic precision.
In addition, ethical and regulatory challenges must be addressed, especially with regard to genome editing approaches. Long-term safety monitoring of CRISPR-based therapies remains essential to assess off-target risks, insertional mutagenesis, and immune responses. Regulatory agencies are now establishing extended post-marketing surveillance frameworks and adaptive approval pathways to balance innovation with patient safety.
In conclusion, the convergence of delivery science, computational technologies, and precision medicine approaches places nucleic acid therapies in a unique position to address both rare and complex liver diseases. Realizing their full clinical potential will require interdisciplinary collaboration and forward-looking regulatory guidance to ensure safe, equitable, and effective implementation.
