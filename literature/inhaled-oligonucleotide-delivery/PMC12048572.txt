## Abstract
GTPase IMAP family member 8 (GIMAP8) plays a key role in pathophysiology of several malignancies. The objective of this current research endeavor was to investigate the prognosis value of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma and examine how it relates to immunity. Expression profiles associated with GIMAP8 and related clinical details were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas database, and we conducted survival analysis, enrichment analysis and immune infiltration studies. Additionally, we evaluated the effect of GIMAP8 on radiation resistance of tumor by in vivo and in vitro experiments. Our results showed that lung adenocarcinoma tumor tissues exhibited lower GIMAP8 levels compared to nearby normal tissues. Furthermore, decreased GIMAP8 expression strongly correlated with poorer OS. The expression of GIMAP8 is closely related to the formation of radiation resistance in tumor cells. GSEA identified multiple signaling pathways linked to GIMAP8, including immune-related, chemokine, cell adhesion molecule, and NF-κB signaling pathways. GIMAP8 expression strongly correlated with the expression of immune checkpoint molecules, tumor mutational burden, tumor neoantigen burden, immune cells, and tumor immune microenvironment. GIMAP8 was found to have an inhibitory effect on lung adenocarcinoma and was closely related to the immune response. Moreover, GIMAP8 may also influence radiation resistance in tumors.

## Introduction
Lung cancer poses a significant threat to human health, ranking among the most prevalent forms of malignancy and being the primary cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, emphasizing its grave impact – . Despite remarkable advancements in the early detection and therapeutic approaches for lung cancer, the prognosis for patients with metastatic lung adenocarcinoma remains grim, with a five-year survival rate hovering below 20%, largely hindered by treatment resistance . Thus, the search for novel treatment targets and potent clinical indicators for lung adenocarcinoma is imperative. Furthermore, further investigation into the fundamental mechanisms underlying lung adenocarcinoma is crucial.
The genes belonging to the human guanosine triphosphatase of immunity-associated protein (GIMAP) family are located on chromosome 7 and are intimately linked to the immune system . The GIMAP family of proteins is essential for regulating cell death . In hepatocellular carcinoma patients, GIMAP proteins were found to be downregulated at mRNA and protein expression levels in tumor tissues and blood, indicating their potential involvement in the pathophysiology of hepatocellular carcinoma . GIMAP8 is a member of the GIMAP family that was determined to be a prognostic biomarker abnormally expressed in endometrial and breast cancer tissues – . However, the association between GIMAP8 and lung cancer remains to be explored.
The current investigation assessed the levels and prognostic implications of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma and explored its relationship with immunity. GSEA was used to uncover signaling pathways linked to GIMAP8. GIMAP8 was found to be potentially responsible for radiation resistance in tumors. This research is expected to reveal new therapeutic targets for lung adenocarcinoma.

## Results

## GIMAP8 shows low expression in lung adenocarcinoma
The levels of GIMAP8 mRNA expression were different in 16 tumor types and associated normal tissues based on the analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data (Fig.  A). A subsequent investigation revealed that lung adenocarcinoma tissues expressed GIMAP8 at lower levels in contrast to nearby normal tissues (Fig.  B). Patients with lung adenocarcinoma were categorized into groups of high and low GIMAP8 expression based on median expression values. As shown on the Kaplan–Meier survival curve, high GIMAP8 group showed better OS than low GIMAP8 group (P < 0.001; Fig.  C). Further analysis of GIMAP8 protein expression was conducted using data from the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) database. The results revealed a marked decrease in GIMAP8 protein expression in lung adenocarcinoma tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Fig.  D). This phenomenon was also observed in the immunohistochemistry images (Fig.  E,F).
 

## GIMAP8 is involved in radiation resistance in lung adenocarcinoma
Lung adenocarcinoma is a challenging cancer to treat. The treatments include surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy . However, the presence of cancer and radiation resistance leads to a poor prognosis . Previous studies found that higher GIMAP8 expression was linked to better OS. Therefore, the present study hypothesis was that GIMAP8-low lung adenocarcinoma cells would be more resistant to radiation. Radiation-resistant models were established in A549 and H1299 cells to test this theory and to investigate the differences in GIMAP8 expression (Fig.  A). Colony formation assays revealed that A549-RR and H1299-RR cells exhibited higher colony numbers at the same irradiation dose compared to parental A549 and H1299 cells (Figs.  B,C), confirming the successful establishment of radiation-resistant models. Further detection of GIMAP8 mRNA expression revealed that GIMAP8 mRNA expression in A549-RR and H1299-RR cells was lower than that in parental A549 and H1299 cells (Fig.  D,E). The GIMAP8 protein expression results were similar to those for mRNA expression (Fig.  F,G). Subsequently, we developed a cell model overexpressing GIMAP8 (Fig. H,I) and measured the cell survival rate after irradiation. The results indicated that the survival rate of A549-RR cells was significantly higher than that of A549 cells after irradiation. However, when the cells overexpressed GIMAP8, their survival rate decreased (Fig.  J). Similar results were also observed in H1299 cells (Fig.  K). We established a GIMAP8 knockdown cell model using siRNA (Fig.  L,M) and assessed cell survival following irradiation. The results demonstrated that GIMAP8 knockdown increased cell survival compared to both A549 cells and A549-RR cells (Fig.  N). Similarly, consistent results were observed in H1299 cells (Fig.  O). Using A549 cells and A549-RR cells, we established tumor-bearing mouse models, with the mouse tumors shown in Fig.  P. Further examination of the expression of GIMAP8 mRNA and protein in mouse tumor tissues revealed that compared with A549, the expression of GIMAP8 in the tumor tissues of mice in the A549-RR group was decreased (Fig.  Q,R). Therefore, the study outcomes demonstrate that GIMAP8 expression negatively correlates with radiation resistance.
 

## GIMAP8 is a prognostic factor in lung adenocarcinoma
To analyze the impact of GIMAP8, patient age, gender, and tumor stage diagnoses on prognosis, we employed univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. The results indicated that GIMAP8 is a major prognostic factor for lung adenocarcinoma (Table ). An effective four-factor nomogram was constructed to determine the OS prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma patients according to multivariate Cox analyses (Fig.  A). Subsequently, we constructed a multi-indicator receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve to evaluate the accuracy of all models. The Area Under Curve alues for one-, three-, and five-year ROC curves were 0.72, 0.69, and 0.72 (Fig.  B). The one-, three-, and five-year calibration plots also demonstrated consistency between actual and predicted results (Fig.  C). In summary, an effective nomogram was used to make precise prognosis predictions in individuals with lung adenocarcinoma.
 

## Analysis of GIMAP8-related signaling pathways
To achieve a better understanding of the role of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma, GSEA was used to explore the pathways associated with GIMAP8. The analysis revealed significant associations between GIMAP8 expression and the chemokine signaling pathway, cell adhesion molecules, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions. Furthermore, GIMAP8 expression was demonstrated to correlate with immune-related signaling pathways (Fig.  A). For experimental validation, we selected three chemokines from the chemokine signaling pathway. Knockdown of GIMAP8 in both A549 and H1299 cell lines resulted in decreased expression of CXCL9, CXCL10, and XCL1 chemokines (Fig.  B,C). Overall, the study results revealed the possible signaling pathways associated with GIMAP8, which will provide a reference for the subsequent in-depth study of GIMAP8 and lung adenocarcinoma.
 

## Analysis of GIMAP8 protein interaction network and its correlation with tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), and tumor neoantigen burden (TNB)
To identify proteins that are closely associated with GIMAP8, we analyzed co-expression data sourced from TCGA database.The results of the protein–protein interaction (PPI) network showed that the following 10 proteins were closely related to GIMAP8: CARNS1, PADI3, PARP14, PADI2, CDKL5, GIMAP1, GIMAP4, GIMAP5, GIMAP6, and GIMAP7 (Fig.  A). Further studies were then conducted to evaluate the relationships between GIMAP8 and TNB, MSI, and TMB. The results revealed that GIMAP8 was markedly correlated with TMB (p < 0.01) and TNB (p < 0.05; Fig.  B,D) and not associated with MSI (p > 0.05; Fig.  C) in lung adenocarcinoma. Therefore, TMB and TNB may be important factors involved in the role of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma.
 

## Analysis of GIMAP8 correlation with immune cell infiltration and tumor microenvironment
Previous studies have found that GIMAP8 is involved in immune responses . Therefore, the connection between GIMAP8 and tumor microenvironment or immune cell infiltration was assessed. The GIMAP8 expression correlation with tumor microenvironment (including ESTIMATE, immune, and stromal scores) was evaluated using the ESTIMATE algorithm. GIMAP8 expression was markedly correlated with the ESTIMATE, immune, and stromal scores (all p < 0.001; Fig.  A–C). The examination of tumor immune infiltration revealed a strong correlation between GIMAP8 and infiltration of neutrophils, myeloid dendritic cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, and macrophages (all p < 0.001; Fig.  D–H).
 In addition, GIMAP8 showed a strong correlation (all p < 0.05) with activated dendritic cells, central memory CD8+ T cells, activated CD4+ T cells, and central memory CD8+ T cells in lung adenocarcinoma according to analysis conducted on the TIMER website (Fig. I).
Subsequent examination of the association between GIMAP8 and immune checkpoint molecules demonstrated that GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma was connected with immune checkpoint molecules, such CD27, CD28, CD40, CD80, and CTLA4 (all p < 0.05; Fig.  A). After that we validated the above results in model cells overexpressing GIMAP8. To better mimic the tumor microenvironment, we co-cultured the model cells with immune cells, namely Jurkat cells, and then examined the expression of CD27, CD28, and CD40 mRNA in the model cells. The results showed that compared with A549 cells, the expression of CD27, CD28, and CD40 mRNA was reduced in A549-RR cells. However, after overexpressing GIMAP8, the expression of CD27, CD28, and CD40 mRNA increased in both A549 and A549-RR cells, with a more significant increase observed in A549 cells after overexpression (Fig.  B). Similar results were observed in H1299 cells (Fig.  C).
 

## Discussion
Lung cancer stands as the most frequently occurring malignancy that results in mortality . Approximately 35–40% of lung cancer instances are lung adenocarcinoma cases, which is the most prevalent form of the disease . The three main therapies for lung cancer are chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and surgery. Despite new advances in treating lung cancer and understanding its pathogenesis, the OS in lung adenocarcinoma patients remains poor due to treatment resistance . A number of disorders have been linked to GIMAP8, which is part of the GIMAP familySPS:refid::bib55, including salt-sensitive hypertension , acute undifferentiated leukemia , breast cancer , and endometrial cancer . Nevertheless, research on GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma is lacking. Therefore, the current study sought to assess the prognostic and immunologic value of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma, which may provide a useful reference for lung adenocarcinoma treatment.
The present study findings indicated that lung adenocarcinoma tumor tissue showed lower expression of GIMAP8 than the neighboring normal tissue, which was linked to a poor OS. Further validation revealed that GIMAP8 expression was reduced in A549-RR and H1299-RR cells compared to parental A549 and H1299 cells. Moreover, in the tumor-bearing mouse model, the expression of GIMAP8 in tumor tissues of the A549-RR group was lower than that in the A549 group. Through the establishment of GIMAP8-overexpressing and knockdown cell models, we demonstrated that differential expression of GIMAP8 modulates radiation resistance in lung adenocarcinoma cells. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses revealed that GIMAP8 can serve as a primary prognostic factor for lung cancer. Nomogram comprising prognostic factors, including GIMAP8, was used to predict one-, three-, and five-year survival prognoses for lung adenocarcinoma patients. GIMAP8-related signaling pathways, such as those involving chemokines, cell adhesion molecules, Th17 cell differentiation, and the immune system, were identified using the GSEA. In addition, GIMAP8 expression was significantly associated with immune cells, TMB, TNB, immune checkpoint molecules, and tumor immune microenvironment.
The GIMAP8 was found to be critical in the development of inflammation . It was also identified as a regulator of basal immunity in Arabidopsis . In terms of the role of GIMAP8 in cancer, it was found that GIMAP gene expression was dysregulated in non-small cell lung cancer, where GIMAP6 and GIMAP8 expression levels were significantly reduced in tumor tissue . Additionally, breast cancer patients had lower levels of GIMAP8 mRNA expression in mammary glands than healthy women when GIMAP gene mRNA expression was detected . Similarly, the present study results showed that lung adenocarcinoma tissues expressed less GIMAP8 than the nearby normal tissues, and that this decreased expression was strongly linked to a worse OS.
Signaling pathways associated with GIMAP8 were identified using GSEA, including chemokine, cell adhesion molecule, NF-κB, and immune-related signaling pathways. Experimental validation demonstrated that knockdown of GIMAP8 in both A549 and H1299 cell lines led to decreased expression of multiple chemokines, including CXCL9, CXCL10, and XCL1. Numerous malignancies were found to be intimately associated with the chemokine signaling pathway, both in terms of their occurrence and progression , . The NF-κB signaling pathway is crucial for the initiation and management of cancer . However, these results are exploratory and although GSEA analysis has revealed signaling pathways that correlate with GIMAP8 expression, the precise mechanisms by which GIMAP8 regulates these pathways remain to be fully elucidated. These results provide important references and directions for future investigations.
Alignment diagram is widely utilized to display prognosis in patients with different types of cancers . In the present study, a validated alignment diagram for predicting OS in lung adenocarcinoma patients was constructed using the TCGA database. The ROC curves and calibration plots demonstrate the accuracy of our constructed alignment diagram in predicting prognosis.
The GIMAP8’s relationship to immunity was investigated in further detail with an emphasis on the tumor microenvironment, tumor immune infiltration, immune cell pathways, and immune checkpoint molecules. Tumor microenvironment and immune infiltration play essential roles in various tumors – . Immune cells and immune checkpoint molecules are considered ideal for treating cancer , . In the present study, GIMAP8 was markedly correlated with the ESTIMATE, immune, and stromal scores in the tumor microenvironment. Regarding immune infiltration, GIMAP8 displayed a significant association with CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, myeloid dendritic cells, macrophages, and neutrophils. Further analysis revealed that GIMAP8 was highly correlated with a variety of immune cells in lung adenocarcinoma, particularly activated dendritic cells, central memory CD8 T+ cells, central memory CD4+ T cells, and activated CD4+ T cells, suggesting that GIMAP8 may a crucial role in regulating tumor immune infiltration and may influence cancer initiation and progression by affecting the immune infiltration of tumors. However, these observations remain speculative at present, as the precise underlying mechanisms have not been elucidated. Further investigations are warranted to fully characterize the relationship between GIMAP8 and tumor immune infiltration, as well as to uncover the specific molecular mechanisms involved. In addition, we also observed that GIMAP8 was positively correlated with a variety of immune checkpoint molecules (including CD27, CD28, CD40, CD80 and CTLA4) in lung adenocarcinoma. Further validation of this result in lung cancer cells revealed that CD27, CD28 and CD40 expression was decreased in A549-RR cells compared to A549 cells, while CD27, CD28 and CD40 expression was elevated upon overexpression of GIMAP8. This correlation underscores the intimate relationship between GIMAP8 and immunological functions. Studies have shown that activation of immune checkpoint molecules CD27, CD28 and CD40 can directly or indirectly enhance anti-tumor immune responses and enhance the anti-tumor efficacy of immunotherapy – . In contrast, the expression of immune checkpoint molecules CD27, CD28 and CD40 was reduced in radiation-resistant cells, suggesting that there may be a potential relationship between GIMAP8, immune checkpoint molecules and tumor radiation resistance.
Some of the main highlights of the present study included the fact that the differential expression of GIMAP8 was determined based on the TCGA dataset, while its protein expression was verified using CPTAC analysis and HPA database. Furthermore, it was determined that GIMAP8 might be a factor involved in radioresistance generation in tumor cells, which was verified experimentally. On the other hand, additional analyses revealed that GIMAP8 was markedly associated with the tumor microenvironment, tumor immune infiltration, immune cells, and immune checkpoint molecules, suggesting that GIMAP was closely related to immunity.
This study has several limitations. First, the prognostic analysis lacked data on smoking history and treatment information. In addition to tumor biological factors, other variables such as smoking history and clinical medical records related to treatment institutions play crucial roles in determining the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma patients. However, due to incomplete documentation of these parameters in the original dataset, they could not be incorporated into the prognostic analysis. Second, our analysis revealed a close correlation between GIMAP8 and immune cell infiltration. However, we did not provide direct evidence for the role of GIMAP8 in tumor immune infiltration. In the future, a significant amount of experimental research will be needed to clarify the relationship between GIMAP8 and tumor immune infiltration. In addition, there was a lack of external validation using an independent cohort when assessing the predictive accuracy of the nomogram. In future studies, we plan to conduct a multicenter collaboration to obtain external validation data and assess the external applicability of the nomogram.

## Materials and methods

## Data mining using public databases
The differential expression levels of the GIMAP8 gene in various tumor tissues and their corresponding adjacent normal tissues were assessed using the TIMER2.0 database (http://timer.cistrome.org). Lung adenocarcinoma gene expression profiling matrices and clinical messages for 59 normal tissues and 483 tumor tissues were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas database (TCGA, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). R software (version 4.1.0) was used to normalize all gene expression profile matrices . GIMAP8 mRNA was then superimposed to generate GIMAP8-related single-gene expression and clinical data.

## CPTAC and the Human Protein Atlas (HPA)
An independent cohort in the CPTAC was used to explore GIMAP8 protein expression in healthy and lung adenocarcinoma tissues. The cohort consisted of 111 cancer samples and 111 matched healthy samples. Data on clinicopathology and protein expression were obtained from LinkedOmics database (http://www.linkedomics.org/) , . The immunohistochemical data for lung cancer and normal lung tissue were assessed utilizing the HPA database (http://www.proteinatlas.org/) .

## Kaplan–Meier(K-M) survival analysis
Based on the median expression value of GIMAP8 in lung adenocarcinoma patients, the patients were categorized into high-expression and low-expression groups. Survival analysis was conducted using Kaplan–Meier (K–M) plots and the log-rank test to compare the survival differences between the two groups, and to investigate the relationship between GIMAP8 expression and prognosis. We regarded the difference as statistically significant when p < 0.05.

## Cells
A549, H1299 and Jurkat cells, sourced from Shanghai Cell Bank (Shanghai, China), were cultured in DMEM or RPMI-1640 (Gibco, Hyclone) with 1% penicillin/streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum (Gimini). Incubation was at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2.

## Experiments on animals
Female BALB/c mice (4 weeks old, 20 g) were obtained from Huafukang Biotechnology (Beijing, China) and housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility at the Experimental Animal Center of the School of Public Health, Jilin University. The mice were provided with ad libitum access to food and maintained under controlled environmental conditions, with temperatures ranging from 22 to 25 °C and relative humidity of approximately 50%. To establish the NSCLC cell-derived xenograft model, A549 and A549-RR cells were subcutaneously injected into the right hind legs of the mice at a density of 1 × 107 cells suspended in PBS. At the conclusion of the experiment, the mice were humanely euthanized using carbon dioxide inhalation to minimize suffering, in compliance with the ethical guidelines approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jilin University and in accordance with the Regulations for the Administration of Laboratory Animals. This experiment was approved by the Experimental Animal Welfare Ethics Committee of the School of Public Health, Jilin University (approval number: 2024-03-005). All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations, as well as in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

## Irradiation
Radiation was applied to A549 cells at a dose rate of 1.0 Gy/min and to H1299 cells at a dose rate of 0.75 Gy/min using an X-ray generator (Model X-RAD320iX, USA). A total of five 6-Gy radiation doses were administered to the cells, resulting in a cumulative dosage of 30 Gy. The cells were passaged four or more times before performing further tests.

## Colony formation assay
Cells were seeded in a six-well plate and irradiated with X-rays at doses of 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 Gy the subsequent day. After 2 weeks, the cell colonies underwent three phosphate-buffered saline washes, each lasting 3 min. The cells were then fixed with fixative for 20 min and stained with Basic Violet 3 (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 20 min.

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)
RNA was extracted using TRIzol and reverse transcribed according to the instructions to produce cDNA (TaKaRa Reverse Transcription Kit, Dalian, China), then qRT-PCR was performed using TB-Green (Fluorescence Quantification Kit, TaKaRa). qRT-PCR primer sequences were shown in Supplementary Table .

## Western blotting
Protein samples were obtained from cells using Radio Immunoprecipitation Assay (RIPA) Lysis buffer. The bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay was used to calculate the protein content. Sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis was conducted, and samples were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride. Blocking was performed using a blocking solution, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C. After washing, the membranes were subjected to a 2-h secondary antibody incubation period, followed by further washing and detection using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit. The antibody information was shown in Supplementary Table .

## Plasmid transfection
Human pCDH-CMV-GIMAP8-GFP-Puro (GIMAP8OE) was generated in-house. The Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was used for transfections in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Small-interfering RNA
A small-interfering RNA (siRNA) of human GIMAP8 gene was designed and synthesized. The sequence is as follows: sense (5′-3′): GGAAGGUUGUGGUAAUU.
GA; antisense (5′-3′): UCAAUUACCACAACCUUCC. Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was used for transfections in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Co-culture model
A549 cells, A549-RR cells, H1299 cells and H1299-RR cells were inoculated into cell culture dishes (plates), and after the cells were adhered to the wall and extended, the same number of Jurkat cells were added to establish a co-culture model .

## Cell viability assay
Cells were seeded into 96-well plates. After adhesion and spreading, they were irradiated with 0, 4, and 8 Gy X-rays (at a dose rate of 1.0 Gy/min). The medium was changed 24 h post-irradiation, and then 10 μl of Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8, MCE, USA) was added to each well. After 2 h, the absorbance at 450 nm was measured by using a microtiter plate spectrophotometer (Tecan, Switzerland).

## GSEA
To further understand the pathways related to GIMAP8, gene enrichment analysis (GSEA) was implemented based on KEGG signaling pathway database (www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html) – using GSEA software (version 4.1.0) . The gene set database chosen was “c2.cp.kegg.v7.2.symbols.gmt”. The names of each gene were chosen as the expression dataset. Statistical significance was established with a p-value of < 0.05 and a normalized enrichment score of > 1.5.

## Regression analyses
In order to explore the possible association of GIMAP8 with the overall survival (OS) in lung adenocarcinoma patients in TCGA database, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to ascertain whether GIMAP8 and additional clinical characteristics (stage, sex, and age) could be used as independent factors associated with OS and to derive their hazard ratio (HR). Generally speaking, a gene with HR > 1 was considered risky and protective when HR < 1. When the p-value was < 0.05, all comparisons were deemed statistically significant.

## Nomogram construction
The R package “rms” was used to generate a nomogram construction with independent prognostic markers to further predict OS in patients with lung adenocarcinoma . After scoring each factor, the scores of all parameters were summed to calculate the total score. The performance of the alignment diagram was evaluated using calibration and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

## Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis
The STRING (https://string-db.org/) database was employed to conduct a PPI network analysis of the GIMAP8 gene, aiming to uncover potential associations between GIMAP8 and other genes.

## Correlation analysis of GIMAP8 with tumor neoantigen load, tumor mutation load, and microsatellite instability
Spearman’s correlation analysis was used to investigate the relationships between GIMAP8 gene expression and tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), or tumor neoantigen burden (TNB) – . Sangerbox program (http://www.sangerbox.com/tool) was used for all of these analyses . Each indicator was represented via a radar chart using the R package “fmsb”. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Analysis of GIMAP8 correlation with immune cell infiltration and tumor microenvironment
A correlation study was conducted between GIMAP8 and the degree of infiltration in six distinct immune cell types using the TIMER website (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/). The connection between GIMAP8 and tumor microenvironment comprising the immune, ESTIMATE, and stromal scores was evaluated using the ESTIMATE method , . Furthermore, immune cells, mismatch repair proteins, and immune checkpoint molecules were independently evaluated using Sangerbox tool against the expression levels of GIMAP8 in the TCGA database . A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Statistical analysis
The GraphPad Prism 8 program and R 4.1.0 software were used to statistically analyze the experimental data. The results were represented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test, with a p-value threshold of < 0.05 indicating significant differences in the experimental results.
