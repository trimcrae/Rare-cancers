## Introduction
Cardiac fibrosis is the main driver for adverse remodeling and progressive functional decline in nearly all types of heart disease including myocardial infarction (MI). The activation of cardiac fibroblasts (CF) into myofibroblasts is responsible for cardiac fibrosis. Unfortunately, no ideal approach for controlling CF activation currently exists.

## Objectives
This study investigated the role of Heat shock protein A12A (HSPA12A), an atypical member of the HSP70 family, in CF activation and MI-induced cardiac fibrosis.

## Methods
Primary CF and Hspa12a knockout mice were used in the experiments. CF activation was indicated by the upregulation of myofibroblast characters including alpha-Smooth muscle actin (αSMA), Collagen, and Fibronectin. Cardiac fibrosis was illustrated by Masson’s trichrome and picrosirius staining. Cardiac function was examined using echocardiography. Glycolytic activity was indicated by levels of extracellular lactate and the related protein expression. Protein stability was examined following cycloheximide and MG132 treatment. Protein-protein interaction was examined by immunoprecipitation-immunoblotting analysis.

## Results
HSPA12A displayed a high expression level in quiescent CF but showed a decreased expression in activated CF, while ablation of HSPA12A in mice promoted CF activation and cardiac fibrosis following MI. HSPA12A overexpression inhibited the activation of primary CF through inhibiting glycolysis, while HSPA12A knockdown showed the opposite effects. Moreover, HSPA12A upregulated the protein expression of transcription factor p53, by which mediated the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of glycolysis and CF activation. Mechanistically, this action of HSPA12A was achieved by acting as a scaffolding protein to bind p53 and ubiquitin specific protease 10 (USP10), thereby promoting the USP10-mediated p53 protein stability and the p53-medicated glycolysis inhibition.

## Conclusion
The present study provided clear evidence that HSPA12A is a novel endogenous inhibitor of CF activation and cardiac fibrosis. Targeting HSPA12A in CF could represent a promising strategy for the management of cardiac fibrosis in patients.

## Introduction
Cardiac fibrosis is a common feature of the advanced stage of nearly all types of heart diseases including myocardial infarction (MI) , , , . An early fibrotic response is crucial for post-MI healing. However, persistent or excessive fibrotic response results in pathological fibrosis, which can lead to several adverse outcomes, especially heart failure and even sudden cardiac death , , , , , . Unfortunately, no ideal cardiac fibrosis-controlling approach currently exists, partially due to the mechanisms underlying fibrosis development have not been fully understood.
Cardiac fibrosis is characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) generated by myofibroblasts, an activated or differentiated form of cardiac fibroblasts (CF). CF are the major type of cells in the adult heart and account for approximately 20% of the total cardiac cell population . After MI or other cardiac injury, CF are activated into myofibroblasts which secrete collagens and other ECM proteins for the development of cardiac fibrosis. MI is one of the most serious coronary artery diseases, and a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. With the advances of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and pharmacologic therapies, the prognosis of patients with MI was shown improved significantly . Though PCI can restore blood flow in narrowed arteries, it cannot completely prevent adverse cardiac remodeling caused by cardiac fibrosis , , , . Therefore, delaying or reversing ischemia-induced cardiac fibrosis to attenuate adverse cardiac remodeling and heart failure remains an important goal for MI treatment.
Fibroblast activation is regulated by multiple internal and external factors. Among them, glycolysis, a metabolic process that transforms glucose to lactate, has garnered increasing attention in recent years , , . Apart from energy provision, glycolysis also coordinates ancillary biosynthetic pathways to provide the elements essential for CF activation . Glycolysis is accomplished by a set of proteins including glucose transporters (e.g. Glucose transporter type 1 and type 4, GLUT1 and GLUT4), glycolytic enzymes (e.g. Hexokinase type II, HK-II; Pyruvate Kinase M2, PKM2; lactate dehydrogenase A, LDHA), lactate transporters (e.g. Monocarboxylate Transporter 4, MCT4) and their co-transporter CD147 (also named basigin). The expression of these glycolysis-related proteins is regulated by multiple transcriptional factors including p53. p53 serves as a potent suppressor of glycolysis by inhibiting expression of the glycolysis-related key proteins in cancer cells , .
Our previous studies have revealed that in renal cancer cells, the glycolysis activity is inhibited by heat shock protein A12A (HSPA12A) . HSPA12A is an atypical member of the heat shock protein 70 family. We have recently reported that HSPA12A protects against ischemic stroke and endotoxemic liver injury and promotes non-alcohol liver disease and obesity development , , , , . However, whether it can inhibit glycolysis to suppress CF activation and cardiac fibrosis is still unknown.
To address this question, we investigated the effects of HSPA12A on CF activation and MI-induced cardiac fibrosis. The results demonstrated that HSPA12A expression was enriched in quiescent CF but was dramatically decreased during CF activation. Ablation of HSPA12A in mice resulted in enhancement of the MI-induced cardiac fibrosis. Loss- and gain-of-function studies revealed that HSPA12A was required for inhibiting CF activation through suppressing glycolysis. Further analysis unraveled that HSPA12A acted as a scaffolding protein to bind p53 and ubiquitin specific protease 10 (USP10), thereby promoting the USP10-mediated p53 protein stability for glycolysis inhibition, which ultimately resulted in suppression of CF activation. Therefore, the findings indicate that HSPA12A functions as a novel suppressor of CF activation and serves as a new therapeutic potential for cardiac fibrosis management.

## Experimental animals
Conditional Hspa12a knockout mice were created using the Cre-loxP recombination system as described previously , . Briefly, the Hspa12a targeting vector was constructed using bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) retrieval methods . The region of the Hspa12a gene containing exons 2–4 was retrieved from a 129/sv BAC clone (BAC/PAC Resources Center, Oakland, CA) using a retrieval vector containing two homologous arms. Exons 2 and 3 were replaced by loxP sites flanking a PGK-neo cassette as a positive selection marker. To generate chimeric mice (Hspa12aflox/+), embryonic stem cells were injected into C57BL/6 blastocysts and followed by transfer into pseudo-pregnant mice. The chimeric mice were crossed with EIIa-Cre transgenic mice to delete the Hspa12a gene, The mice were bred at the Model Animal Research Center of Nanjing University and were maintained in the Animal Laboratory Resource Facility of the same institution. The animal care and experimental protocols were approved by Nanjing University’s Committee on Animal Care. All experiments conformed to the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication, 8th Edition, 2011). All experiments conformed to international guidelines on the ethical use of animals.
Mice (C57BL/6 background) were assigned randomly to all analyses. Investigators were blinded to the echocardiographic measurements and histological analysis. Investigators were not blinded to animal handling, sampling, and raw data collection.

## Ethics statement
All experiments involving animals were conducted according to the ethical policies and procedures approved by the ethics committee of the Faculty of Pharmacy, Cairo University, Egypt (Approval no. MI-2364).

## Myocardial infarction (MI)
MI was induced in 8– to 10–week-old, male wild type (WT) and HSPA12A knockout (Hspa12a−/−) mice by permanently ligating the left anterior descending coronary (LAD) as previously described , . Briefly, after anesthesia by inhalation of 1.5–2% isoflurane, mice were mechanically ventilated, chest opened, and LAD ligated. In sham-operated animals, the same surgical process was conducted except for the LAD ligation. Analgesia was conducted as our previous methods . For tissue collection, mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation after overdose anesthesia by pentobarbital sodium 150 mg/kg (intraperitoneal injection).

## Cardiac fibrosis
Three weeks after MI, the hearts of mice were cut into five equal pieces transversally. After fixed in 4% formalin at room temperature overnight, the tissue pieces were prepared for paraffin embedded-sections. For evaluation of cardiac fibrosis, Masson trichrome staining and picrosirius red staining were performed according to the previous methods . The area of fibrosis was quantified using Cellsens Dimention 1.15 software (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and expressed as percentage of ventricular area. The staining of picrosirius red was also observed using a polarimicroscope. For histological examination, the paraffin-embedded heart sections were also HE stained.

## Cardiac function
Cardiac function was examined 21 days after MI using echocardiography according to previous methods , . Briefly, mice were anesthesia by inhalation with 1–1.5% isoflurane. The hair of chest skin was removed using a depilatory agent. The mouse was subsequently tapped on the heated platform. Imaging was taken using the Vevo770 system equipped with a 35-MHz transducer (Visualsonics, Toronto, Canada). During image acquisition, the heart rate of the mice was maintained at 450–600 beats per minute (bpm). The examination was conducted by an observer blinded to the treatment. Parameters were obtained from M−mode and pulse Doppler tracings and averaged using four to five cardiac cycles.

## Isolation and culture of primary CF and cardiomyocytes
Primary CF and cardiomyocytes were isolated from hearts of neonatal rats (1–3 day-old). Briefly, Newborn Sprague–Dawley rats were anaesthetized by hypothermia and euthanized by decapitation. The hearts were then collected, trimmed, washed, and cut into small pieces of approximately 1–2 mm2 in size. Next, these tissue pieces were digested with type II collagenase (0.4 mg/ml) and pancreatin (0.6 mg/ml) at 37 °C. After the digestion was terminated by horse serum (25%, v/v), the cell precipitation was collected, resuspended in growth culture medium (DMEM, 5% FBS, 10% horse serum), and preplated for 2 h to allow the CF attachment. The unattached cell population, rich of cardiomyocytes, was collected and seeded in culture dishes pre-coated with 0.1% gelatin. CF were cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS and the antibiotics (100 U/ml penicillin and 0.1 mg/ml streptomycin). The cardiomyocytes were cultured in DMEM containing 5% FBS, 10% horse serum, and the same antibiotics mentioned above. When CF reached 80%-90% confluence, they were detached using 0.25% trypsin and passaged in a 1:3 ratio.

## HSPA12A overexpression and knockdown
To overexpress HSPA12A (Hspa12aOE), primary CF were infected with an adenovirus carrying the expression sequence of Hspa12a for 24 h, as described previously , . CF infected with empty adenovirus served as normal expression controls (NC). To knockdown HSPA12A expression (Hspa12aKD), primary CF were transfected with siRNA directed against rat Hspa12a mRNA for 24 h. CF transfected with Scramble RNA served as controls (NC). The siRNA sequences were listed in Supplementary .

## TGFβ1 treatment
After HSPA12A overexpression or knockdown for 24 h, CF were treated with TGFβ1 (10 ng/ml) for a further 24 h. In experiments of glycolysis inhibition, CF were treated with 2-DG (5 mM) or oxamate (5 mM) 30 min prior to TGFβ1 stimulation. In experiments of p53 and USP10 inhibition, CF were treated with PFTɑ (20 μM) and Spautin-1 (10 μM), respectively, 30 min prior to TGFβ1 stimulation.

## Examination of p53 protein stability
To block de novo p53 protein synthesis, CF were treated with CHX (0.1 mg/ml) for 2 h. To block p53 protein degradation, CF were treated with a proteasome inhibitor MG132 (20 μM) for 4 h. After CHX and MG132 treatment, the remaining or cumulative p53 level was examined by Western blotting and expressed as a percentage relative to those of the controls.

## Immunoblotting
For immunoblotting, myocardial infarcts were collected 7 days after MI and primary CF were harvested 24 h after TGFβ1 treatment. Proteins were extracted and separated by electrophoresis through 10% (W/V) SDS-polyacrylamide gels for immunoblotting as previously described , . The separated proteins were then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking with 5% fat-free milk for 2 h at 37 ℃, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 ℃ overnight. The blots for GAPDH or α-Tubulin served as controls for lane loading. Subsequently, membranes were washed and incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 2 h. The immunoreactive proteins were visualized by the High-sig enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents followed by quantification using scanning densitometry. The final results from each set of experiments were expressed as relative integrated intensities (compared with those of controls). Antibodies used in this experiment were shown in Supplementary .

## Co-immunoprecipitation
Co-immunoprecipitation was conducted to analyze the interaction between HSPA12A and other proteins as well as the effects of HSPA12A on p53 ubiquitination. Primary CF were overexpressed with flag-tagged HSPA12A. Following stimulation with TGFβ1 for 24 h, CF were harvested for protein preparation. After adjusting to the same concentration and volume, the whole-cell extracts were pre-treated with Protein A agarose for 2 h and centrifuged at 15,000 g for 7 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was then precipitated using anti-Flag, anti-Ubiquitin, or anti-IgG antibodies. Subsequently, the mixture was incubated with Protein A agarose at 4 °C for 6 h. The fractions were then analyzed by immunoblotting for p53, PPARγ, Hif1α, USP10 and HSPA12A as described previously , .

## Quantitative real-time PCR
The mRNA levels of genes were estimated by quantitative real-time PCR as previous methods , . Briefly, total RNA was prepared for cDNA synthesis followed by real-time PCR using the SYBR Green Master. The PCR results of 18sRNA gene expression served as internal controls. The primers used in this experiment were shown in Supplementary .

## Measurement of HK II activity, LDH activity and lactate levels
Levels of lactate and activities of LDH and HK II were assayed using the corresponding assay kits, according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Immunofluorescence staining
To visualize gene expression, immunofluorescence staining was conducted on frozen heart tissue sections or 4% PFA-fixed CF using the appropriate antibodies. Briefly, sections or fixed-cells were incubated with the indicated primary antibody (1:100) overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubated with Cy3- or Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody. DAPI was used to counterstain the nuclei. The staining was examined using a fluorescence microscope and quantified using Cellsens Dimention 1.15 software (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

## Statistical analysis
Results were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation and were analysed parametrically. Normality and homogeneity were tested using SPSS Statistics 27 before choosing the statistical test. The student’s two-tailed unpaired t-test, or two-way analysis of variance analysis followed by Tukey test as a post-hoc tests were used for data which were consistent with normality and homogeneity. Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis were used for data which were not consistent with normality and homogeneity with GraphPad Prism 9. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Sample size was not pre-estimated.

## Results

## HSPA12A displays high expression in quiescent CF, but the expression is decreased during CF activation
The expression pattern of HSPA12A in different cardiac cells is unknown. We thus compared HSPA12A expression in primary CF and cardiomyocytes that isolated from rat hearts. Unpassaged CF without any treatment, which are considered to be quiescent , displayed 3.6-fold higher level of HSPA12A protein expression than cardiomyocytes ( a). 
The high levels of HSPA12A expression in quiescent CF prompted us to investigate whether HSPA12A is involved in CF activation. To this aim, HSPA12A expression was examined in CF following activation by TGFβ1 treatment or culture passage according to previous studies , ( b). Compared to vehicle controls, TGFβ1-treated CF displayed decrease of HSPA12A expression but increase of the CF activation characters including αSMA and collagen III ( c-d). Similar results were obtained during culture passage-induced CF activation ( e).
To determine whether this is also the case in injured heart, HSPA12A expression was examined at day 7 post-MI ( f), because at this time the CF show significant activation as indicated by the upregulation of αSMA expression , , . Of note, at day 7 post-MI, murine infarcted myocardium showed an increased αSMA expression whereas decreased HSPA12A expression compared to sham controls ( g). Moreover, the primary CF that isolated from infarcted myocardium showed a decrease of HSPA12A expression at day 7 post-MI ( h). Our findings were supported by the results from Gene Expression Omnibus (GSE151834) , which demonstrated that Hspa12a mRNA expression displayed the top 1 decrease among 25 HSPs in MI hearts compared to sham controls ( ).

## HSPA12A ablation promotes CF activation and cardiac fibrosis after MI in mice
To determine the biological consequences of downregulated HSPA12A in cardiac fibrosis following injury, wild type (WT) and HSPA12A knockout (Hspa12a−/−) mice were subjected to MI ( a). The successful ablation of HSPA12A in hearts of Hspa12a−/− mice was confirmed by immunoblotting ( b). Following MI for 7 days, Hspa12a−/− hearts displayed higher αSMA protein expression and more αSMA+ cells than WT controls ( c-d). Also, the hearts of Hspa12a−/− mice displayed higher expression of Collagen III, Collagen I, and Fibronectin than WT hearts after MI ( c, S2a-b). In agreement, Hspa12a−/− mice exhibited larger areas of cardiac fibrosis than WT mice following MI for 21 days as indicated by Masson’s trichome staining ( e, S2c). More collagen maturation was detected in Hspa12a−/− hearts than WT controls after MI ( d). HE staining showed severer histological abnormalities of myocardium in Hspa12a−/− mice than in WT following MI for 21 days ( e). 
Fibrosis is detrimental for cardiac performance. Thus, echocardiography was carried out on mice to assess cardiac function. At 21 days after MI, Hspa12a−/− mice displayed worsened diastolic and systolic dysfunction than WT controls, as indicated by lower peak early filling to late diastolic ventricular filling velocity (E/A ratio), and lower ejection fraction (EF%) and fractional shortening (FS%) ( f-g). Moreover, the MI-induced adverse cardiac remodeling was aggravated in Hspa12a−/− mice compared to WT controls, as reflected by thinner left ventricle wall (decrease in IVSd) and larger left ventricular chamber (increases in LVIDd, LVIDs, LVVd, and LVVs) ( ).
Given that CF activation is beneficial for early healing post MI, mice survival was also monitored and recorded. Intriguingly, Hspa12a−/− mice exhibited improved survival compared with WT mice within 7 days post MI ( a), which could be due to fewer ventricular ruptures ( b). No further deaths were observed in either of the two groups from day 8 to day 21 after MI ( a), which was consistent with our previous reports .

## HSPA12A is required for suppressing CF activation
To determine the direct role of HSPA12A on CF activation, we examined the effect of HSPA12A on TGFβ1-induced myofibroblast characteristics in primary CF ( a). To this end, HSPA12A was overexpressed (Hspa12aOE) by infecting CF with an adenovirus encoding HSPA12A expression, and the CF infected with the corresponding empty control adenovirus served as negative controls (NC) ( b). Hspa12aOE CF showed significant attenuation of TGFβ1-induced upregulation of myofibroblast markers (αSMA, Collagen III, Ctgf and Fibronectin) compared to NC controls ( c-e, S5). Even non-TGFβ1-treated Hspa12aOE CF displayed lower expression of the myofibroblast markers than the non-treated NC controls. The data suggest that HSPA12A overexpression attenuated CF activation. 
To determine whether HSPA12A is required for inhibition of CF activation, HSPA12A expression was knocked-down (Hspa12aKD) by transfecting CF with an siRNA targeting Hspa12a mRNA or a scramble control RNA (NC) ( b). The TGFβ1-induced upregulation of αSMA and collagen III expression was enhanced in Hspa12aKD CF compared with their NC controls ( c, 3d, 3f). The data indicate that HSPA12A was required for inhibition of CF activation.

## HSPA12A suppresses CF activation via inhibiting glycolysis
Glycolysis is thought to be necessary for fibroblast activation , , . To test whether glycolysis is involved in the regulation of HSPA12A in CF activation, we measured extracellular lactate, the readout of glycolysis . As shown in a, TGFβ1 increased the extracellular lactate level of CF, and this increase was attenuated by HSPA12A overexpression but enhanced by HSPA12A knockdown when compared with their respective NC controls. In supporting this, the TGFβ1-induced upregulation of glycolysis-related proteins for glucose uptake (GLUT4), glycolytic catalysis (HK-II, PKM2 and LDHA), and lactate export (MCT4) were markedly attenuated by HSPA12A overexpression but further enhanced by HSPA12A knockdown when compared with their NC controls ( b-c, S6a-b). Moreover, TGFβ1 increased activities of LDH and HK II in CF, and the TGFβ1-induced increase of LDH activity was attenuated by HSPA12A overexpression ( c). Taken together, these data suggest that HSPA12A was required for inhibiting glycolysis in CF. 
To test whether glycolysis plays a role in the regulation of HSPA12A in CF activation, Hspa12aKD CF were treated with the glycolysis inhibitors 2-DG or oxamate (Oxa) to block glycolysis ( d). Either 2-DG or Oxa reversed the increase in extracellular lactate content of TGFβ1-treated Hspa12aKD CF ( e), indicating that these inhibitors abolished the HSPA12A knockdown-induced promotion of glycolysis. Of note, both 2-DG and Oxa abolished the HSPA12A knockdown-induced increases of αSMA and collagen III expression in TGFβ1-treated CF ( f-g). Collectively, the data suggest that HSPA12A controlled CF activation via modulating glycolytic activity.

## HSPA12A inhibits glycolysis by increasing p53 protein expression in CF
Next, we sought to address how HSPA12A inhibits glycolysis in CF. We recently reported that in renal cancer cells, HSPA12A inhibits glycolysis by decreasing CD147 protein expression . However, in CF, CD147 protein levels were not decreased by HSPA12A overexpression ( ), suggesting that HSPA12A may inhibit CF glycolysis through a CD147-independent mechanism.

## HSPA12A interacts with p53, increases its protein content and nuclear localization
Glycolysis is regulated by multiple transcription factors, including Hif1α, an activator of glycolysis; PPARγ, an activator/inhibitor of glycolysis; and p53, an inhibitor of glycolysis , , , . Intriguingly, in our mRNA sequencing of mice livers, HSPA12A overexpression resulted in increased Trp53 and Pparg mRNA expression but did not change Hif1α mRNA expression ( a). However, analysis of protein expression in CF demonstrated that, while HSPA12A overexpression increased p53 protein expression, it had no effect on PPARγ or Hif1α protein expression ( b). Consistently, p53 protein expression was decreased in CF with HSPA12A knockdown ( c). Further immunoprecipitation-immunoblotting analysis revealed that only p53 protein was recovered from the flag-tagged HSPA12A immunocomplexes that prepared from TGFβ1-treated Hspa12aOE CF, while neither Hif1α nor PPARγ proteins were recovered ( a). This suggests a direct interaction between p53 and HSPA12A in CF. Notably, TGFβ1 decreased p53 protein expression in CF, while this decrease was attenuated by HSPA12A overexpression ( b). Similarly, TGFβ1 decreased p53 nuclear localization, which was attenuated by HSPA12A overexpression ( c).

## p53 inhibitor abolishes the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of CF glycolysis and activation.
To determine whether p53 mediates the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of glycolysis and activation, CF were treated with a p53 inhibitor, PFTα, 30 min prior to TGFβ1 exposure. Treatment with PFTα alone showed no effect on extracellular lactate level of CF at baseline ( ). Hspa12aOE CF had reduced extracellular lactate content relative to the NC controls, but this reduction was reversed by PFTα treatment ( d). In agreement, Hspa12aOE CF showed downregulation of glycolysis-related proteins (GLUT4, HK-II, and PKM2) compared with the NC controls, whereas this downregulation was attenuated or completely prevented following administration with p53 inhibitor PFTα ( e). More importantly, PFTα abrogated the decreases of myofibroblast markers (αSMA and collagen III) in Hspa12aOE CF ( f-g). Together, these findings indicate that HSPA12A inhibited CF glycolysis and activation by increasing p53 protein expression.

## HSPA12A acts as a scaffolding protein to stabilize p53 protein by USP10

## HSPA12A increases p53 protein stability
To further investigate how HSPA12A increases p53 protein expression, we examined p53 transcription levels. Unexpectedly, HSPA12A did not change p53 mRNA levels either at the basal level or following TGFβ1 treatment ( a). This suggests that HSPA12A might increase p53 protein expression by increasing p53 protein stability. To test this hypothesis, Hspa12aOE CF were treated with cycloheximide (CHX) for 2 h to block de novo p53 protein synthesis. Notably, the remaining p53 protein content, which was expressed as percentage of those of the respective vehicle controls, was significantly higher in CHX-treated-Hspa12aOE CF than in CHX-treated-NC CF ( b). Given that proteasomal degradation is a major pathway regulating the levels of proteins , , CF were then treated with the proteasome inhibitor MG-132. We found that MG132 increased p53 protein levels in NC CF but not in Hspa12aOE CF ( c). Together, these data indicate that HSPA12A increased p53 protein stability in CF.

## HSPA12A acts as a scaffolding protein to bind USP10 and p53
p53 protein stability can be controlled by MDM2-mediated ubiquitination and USP-mediated deubiquitination ( d) , , . However, bioinformatics prediction (https://www. biocloud.net/) demonstrated that USP10 (a deubiquitinase), but not MDM2 (an E3 ubiquitin ligase), was among the top 20 potential binding proteins of HSPA12A ( ). This prediction was confirmed by immunoprecipitation-immunoblotting analysis, which showed that USP10 was present in HSPA12A immunoprecipitates prepared from TGFβ1-treated Hspa12aOE CF ( e). Considering that USP10 is a deubiquitinase for p53 deubiquitination, we then investigated the levels of p53 ubiquitination. To this aim, immunoprecipitates for ubiquitin was prepared from CF using anti-ubiquitin antibody. The immunoprecipitation-immunoblotting analysis demonstrated that less p53 was recovered in the ubiquitin-immunoprecipitates that prepared from Hspa12aOE CF compared to that from NC controls, suggesting HSPA12A reduced p53 ubiquitination in CF ( ). Taken together with the results showing a binding of HSPA12A to p53 ( a), the data indicate that HSPA12A served as a scaffolding protein for binding of both USP10 and p53 in CF.

## UPS10 meditates the HSPA12A-induced increase of p53 protein stability
To investigate whether UPS10 mediates the HSPA12A-induced increase of p53 protein stability, CF were treated with the USP10 inhibitor, Spautin-1, 30 min prior to TGFβ1 exposure , . Western blot analysis revealed that Spautin-1 reversed the HSPA12A-induced increase in p53 protein levels ( f-g), suggesting that the HSPA12A-induced increase of p53 protein stability was dependent on USP10. Remarkably, inhibition of USP10 with Spautin-1 reversed the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of glycolysis (reflected by levels of extracellular lactate and GLUT4 and HK-II expression) and downregulation of myofibroblast markers (αSMA and Collagen III) in TGFβ1-treated CF ( h-i, S12a-c).

## Discussion
The present study has revealed that HSPA12A acted as an inhibitor of CF activation and MI-induced fibrosis by suppressing glycolysis via its ability to increase the USP10-mediated p53 protein stability ( j). Therefore, HSPA12A has a therapeutic potential for management of cardiac fibrosis in patients.
Cardiac fibrosis is a common feature of almost all types of heart diseases and is one of the main drivers for progression of adverse cardiac remodeling , . In general, cardiac fibrosis is a scar formation process in response to injury and is characterized by deposition of excessive ECM proteins that are mainly generated by myofibroblasts, the activated form of CF. While the fibrotic response is beneficial for cardiac repair in the short term after injury, it can also result in adverse remodeling such as myocardial architecture distortedness, and contribute to the progression of arrhythmia, heart failure, and even sudden cardiac death , , , . Therefore, timely controlling cardiac fibrosis is predicted to change the clinical course and outcome of patients with cardiac injury . However, therapies that are effective in inhibiting pathological cardiac fibrosis by targeting CF activation are currently lacking .
HSPA12A is an atypic member of HSP70 family. We have demonstrated that HSPA12A is required for the development of diet-induced obesity and nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, and liver cancer growth and metastasis , , , . By contrast, HSPA12A shows protective effects on cerebral ischemic injury, hepatic ischemia/reperfusion injury, endotoxin-induced lung and liver injury, and renal cancer metastasis , , , , . Particularly, we have demonstrated that HSPA12A is a novel regulator of glycolysis in cancer cells , . Moreover, HSPA12A controls cerebral lactate homeostasis to maintain hippocampal neurogenesis and mood stabilization, as shown in our previous studies . In this study, we found that HSPA12A displayed high expression in quiescent CF but showed downregulation during CF activation, while ablation of HSPA12A in mice exaggerated MI-induced cardiac fibrosis. Intriguingly, though HSPA12A ablation exaggerated cardiac fibrosis and adverse remodeling at 21 days after MI, HSPA12A ablation reduced cardiac rupture and improved mouse survival within 7 days post-MI, suggesting that timely manipulating fibrotic response is indeed beneficial for MI repairment. These findings provide clear evidence that HSPA12A was a novel inhibitor of MI-induced cardiac fibrosis and HSPA12A may be involved in governing CF activation.
The activation or conversion of CF into myofibroblasts, the main source of ECM proteins and enzymes for matrix processing, plays a central role in the development of cardiac fibrosis , . After cardiac injury, the activation of CF is triggered by various extracellular signals, including neurohumoral mediators, cytokines, and growth factors (e.g. TGFβ1), which are released by damaged myocardium and immune cells . However, the intracellular events for transduction of these extracellular fibrogenic signals are not clearly understood. Interestingly, recent studies have demonstrated that enhanced glycolysis is necessary for the activation of pulmonary and renal fibroblasts and hepatic stellate cells through providing metabolic intermediates for collagen synthesis and providing lactate to activate latent TGFβ1 , , . Glycolysis is critical for both energy provision and coordinating ancillary biosynthetic pathways to provide the elements essential for activation of numerous fibroblast populations. Thus, targeting glycolysis in fibroblasts has been proposed as an anti-fibrotic strategy , . In this study, we found that HSPA12A was required for inhibition of CF activation and glycolysis, while inhibition of glycolysis reversed the CF activation that promoted by HSPA12A knockdown. Collectively, our findings indicate that HSPA12A suppressed CF activation by inhibiting glycolysis.
We have recently reported that in renal cancer cells, HSPA12A inhibits glycolysis by decreasing protein expression of CD147 , the co-transporter for lactate export. However, in this study, HSPA12A did not decrease CD147 protein expression in CF, suggesting that HSPA12A might control CF glycolysis through a CD147-independent mechanism. Glycolysis, the process that converts glucose to lactate, is accomplished by a set of proteins including glucose transporters (e.g. GLUT1, GLUT4), glycolytic enzymes (e.g. HK-II, PKM2, and LDHA), and lactate transporters (e.g. MCT4) together with the co-transporter CD147. We found that HSPA12A downregulated GLUT4, HK-II, PKM2 and LDHA expression in CF, suggesting that some transcriptional factors might mediate the HSPA12A-induced downregulation of these proteins. Previous studies have shown that among transcription factors, Hif1α activates, PPARγ activates and inhibits, and p53 inhibits glycolysis , , , . In this study, we made the findings in CF. (1) HSPA12A increased the protein expression of p53 but not of PPARγ or Hif1α; (2) Only p53 formed complexes with HSPA12A; (3) p53 inhibition abrogated the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of glycolysis of CF. Altogether, these data suggest that HSPA12A inhibits CF glycolysis by upregulating p53 protein expression.
Although HSPA12A increased p53 protein expression, there was no changes in p53 mRNA (Trp53) expression in CF, suggesting that HSPA12A might regulate p53 protein expression by increasing its protein stability. This hypothesis was confirmed by our further experiments using protein synthesis blocker CHX and proteasome inhibitor MG132. Evidence has shown that p53 is degraded through the ubiquitin–proteasome system, and USP10, a deubiquitinase, prevents p53 degradation . Notably, we found that USP10 bound with HSPA12A in CF, and inhibition of USP10 not only reversed the HSPA12A-induced upregulation of p53 protein levels, but also reversed the HSPA12A-induced inhibition of glycolysis and activation of CF. Taken together with the fact that HSPA12A also bound with p53, our data indicate that HSPA12A acted as a scaffolding protein for the binding of both p53 and USP10, by which promoting the USP10-mediated p53 protein stability, and ultimately leading to the inhibition of glycolysis-mediated CF activation. Scaffold proteins were defined as molecules that bind to at least two other signaling proteins . Scaffold proteins play critical roles in providing a platform for the bound proteins to promote their biological functions through coordinating their interaction, assemble, and cellular localization. In this study, we revealed that HSPA12A was a novel scaffold protein to bind to p53 and USP10 to regulate CF activation. When taken into account that HSPA12A increased p53 mRNA in liver but not in CF, HSPA12A may regulate p53 mRNA expression in a tissue-dependent manner.
However, the present study has some limitations. In animal studies, the role of HSPA12A was only examined in global Hspa12a−/− mice, future studies will use CF-specific HSPA12A targeted mice to further clarify the role HSPA12A in cardiac fibrosis in vivo. Also, though we identified the interaction of HSPA12A with p53 and UPS10, the specific binding sites should be characterized in future studies.
In conclusion, this study revealed that HSPA12A was a novel inhibitor of CF activation and cardiac fibrosis, and this action of HSPA12A was achieved by inhibiting glycolysis via promoting the USP10-mediated p53 protein stability as a scaffolding protein. Therefore, targeting HSPA12A in CF represents a promising strategy for the management of cardiac fibrosis.
