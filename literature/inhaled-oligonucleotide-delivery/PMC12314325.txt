## Highlights
•Analyses of our transcriptome profiling datasets identify periostin as a novel direct target of miR-150.
•Genes of POSTN are downregulated in human cardiac fibroblasts (HCFs) and mouse left ventricles treated with carvedilol.
•The protective actions of miR-150 in HCFs are mediated by the functional repression of POSTN.
•Postn ablation alleviates cardiac dysfunction and remodeling after myocardial infarction that are induced by miR-150 deletion.

## Summary
Our prior studies have revealed that miR-150-5p (miR-150) attenuated cardiac dysfunction in mice, which overexpressed a long noncoding RNA called myocardial infarction–associated transcript during myocardial infarction or harbored cardiac-specific abrogation of β-arrestin–mediated β1-adrenergic receptor signaling during chronic catecholamine stimulation. Although previous studies have shown the importance of miR-150 in heart failure, details surrounding its actions remain elusive in part because of (1) the lack of detailed mechanistic insight by which this small noncoding RNA induces myocardial protection and (2) the absence of definitive studies using appropriate mouse models to establish its direct functional relationship with key downstream targets. In the current study, we provide strong evidence that fibrotic periostin is a significant downstream target of miR-150 repression in ischemic mouse hearts. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to directly establish the functional link between miR-150 and periostin in murine myocardial infarction and primary adult human cardiac fibroblast activation.

Controlling the biogenesis of small noncoding RNAs (microRNAs [miRs]) in the heart plays a critical role in the development and progression of heart failure (HF). , , , , Because miRs can target multiple messenger RNAs within a disease pathway and thus can increase the robustness of the gene-regulatory network, miRs are being investigated as potential therapeutic targets for various diseases, , , , including HF, with some reaching clinical trial stages. , We identified that miR-150-5p (miR-150) was upregulated in the heart by the β-adrenergic receptor antagonist (β-blocker) carvedilol (Carv) through β-arrestin-mediated β1-adrenergic receptor (β1AR) protective signaling. Using systemic and cardiac-specific miR-150 knockout (KO) mouse models, we demonstrated that Carv/β1AR/β-arrestin-responsive miR-150 enhanced beneficial cardiac remodeling post–myocardial infarction (MI) in part by reducing cardiomyocyte (CM) apoptosis. , Additionally, cardiac-specific overexpression of miR-150 was shown to attenuate transverse aortic constriction (TAC)–induced HF, whereas miR-150 loss resulted in increased cardiac fibrosis post-TAC. This effect was accompanied by miR-150 downregulation in cardiac fibroblasts (CFs), rather than CMs, isolated from TAC mice, and miR-150 was found to inhibit mouse CF activation in vitro. Our further investigation revealed that the protective effects of miR-150 in CFs were partly due to the direct repression of profibrotic homeobox A4 (HOXA4) and small proline rich protein 1A (SPRR1A). , Moreover, we showed that genetic miR-150 overexpression improved cardiac function and reduced cardiac fibrosis in mice overexpressing the long noncoding RNA myocardial infarction–associated transcript (MIAT) after MI as well as in cardiac-specific G protein–coupled receptor kinase −β1AR transgenic mice (which show impairments in both β1AR/β-arrestin signaling and Carv-mediated miR-150 upregulation ) subjected to chronic catecholamine stimulation. Importantly, both circulating and cardiac miR-150 are downregulated in patients with various heart diseases , , , and in mouse models of HF. , , Additionally, miR-150, conserved between rodents and humans, is significantly associated with outcomes of patients with HF. Taken together, these findings underscore the clinical relevance of miR-150 in HF, supporting its potential as a therapeutic, diagnostic, and prognostic target. However, the specific mechanisms by which miR-150 regulates fibrogenesis, cardiac fibrosis, and ischemic HF remain to be fully elucidated.
Periostin (POSTN) is a highly conserved matricellular protein playing a critical role in the progression of systemic fibrotic diseases. Circulating POSTN expression is elevated in patients with MI. , Studies using inducible transgenic and systemic KO mouse models have demonstrated that Postn acted as a maladaptive regulator of ventricular remodeling, including fibrosis following chronic MI, and that Postn regulated genes involved in fibrosis, cell adhesion, and extracellular matrix in the heart. , 
Postn expression is increased in post-MI and post-TAC hearts, concurrently with the upregulation of fibrotic MIAT and the downregulation of antifibrotic miR-150. , Antioxidants such as cinnamaldehyde and curcumol have been shown to inhibit POSTN upregulation mediated by transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1), interleukin 13, and carbon tetrachloride, reducing fibrotic responses in different tissue types. , Moreover, miR-203-3p has been identified as a direct repressor of Postn, limiting ischemic CM injury in vitro. However, the cardiac regulatory mechanisms of POSTN by noncoding RNAs remain largely unexplored, and it is unknown whether profibrotic POSTN is regulated by Carv/β1AR/β-arrestin-responsive miR-150 in MI and maladaptive human cardiac fibroblast (HCF) function.
Here, we identify Postn as a novel downstream target of the MIAT/miR-150 axis through analysis of our transcriptomic datasets from MIAT KO and cardiac-specific miR-150 conditional KO mouse hearts. Our findings reveal that left ventricular (LV) POSTN is upregulated in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and that POSTN is a new direct target of miR-150 repression in vitro. Cell fractionation studies show the dramatic upregulation of Postn in both CFs and CMs isolated from mouse hearts post-MI, aligning with prior reports of the expression of POSTN genes in human and mouse CMs. , , Furthermore, we find that Carv, concurrent with miR-150 upregulation, downregulates Postn in mouse hearts. Using adult primary HCFs and a novel miR-150/Postn double KO mouse model, we also demonstrate the following: 1) POSTN is upregulated in HCFs subjected to hypoxia/reoxygenation but is downregulated by Carv in HCFs, a pattern that is inversely correlated with miR-150 expression; 2) the protective actions of miR-150 in HCFs are mediated by its direct functional repression of POSTN; and 3) Postn ablation in mice alleviates cardiac dysfunction, injury, apoptosis, and fibrosis following MI induced by miR-150 deletion. Our results directly establish a functional relationship between miR-150 and POSTN in HCF activation and post-MI remodeling in mice. These findings suggest that profibrotic POSTN serves as a key downstream effector of miR-150 in suppressing fibrogenesis and maladaptive cardiac remodeling, positioning the miR-150/POSTN axis as a promising therapeutic target for ischemic heart disease and pathological organ fibrosis.

## Methods

## Availability of data and materials
Our published microarray datasets were deposited in the Gene Expression Omnibus under the access codes of GSE177057 and GSE185396. , All other data are included in this paper and the . For the purposes of reproducing the results or replicating the procedures, the analytical methods and study materials will be available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. Other methods are provided in the .

## miR-150 KO and Postn KO mice as well as the generation of miR-150/Postn double KO mice
Systemic miR-150 KO mice were purchased from the Jackson Laboratory (007750), and their cardiac phenotypes post-MI were reported in our previous studies. , We also acquired a Postn KO mouse line (Jackson Laboratory, 009067). In the current study, Postn KO mice were bred with miR-150 KO mice to generate the novel miR-150;Postn double KO mouse line. All mice were maintained on a C57BL/6J background, and wild-type (WT) littermates were used as controls.

## Minimization of pain and distress of mice
All efforts were made to minimize discomfort, distress, pain, and injury. Animals were handled carefully and humanely before anesthesia by approved methods. To perform MI surgery on the heart, adult mice were anesthetized using isoflurane (1% to 4%, inhalant). The topical local analgesia drug, bupivacaine (a few small drops of 0.75% to 1%) was administered at the time of surgery. Sustained-release meloxicam (4 to 5 mg/kg, subcutaneous injection) and extended-release buprenorphine (3.25 mg/kg, Ethiqa XR: extended-release formulation, subcutaneous injection) were administered once to provide up to 72 hours of systemic analgesia. The animals were observed for the pinch toe reflex during the surgery. Following the surgery, the mice were monitored until they regained consciousness. Postoperative care included monitoring every 15 to 30 minutes following the surgery for 2 to 3 hours and then daily until the study endpoint for signs of distress, including difficulty with breathing, grooming, defecation, eating, and mobility.

## Human heart samples
LV samples of failing human hearts were collected from ischemic cardiomyopathy and nonischemic cardiomyopathy patients who were hospitalized with HFrEF and underwent orthotopic cardiac transplantation as previously described. LV tissues were dissected and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. The frozen samples were then stored in the specimen storage facility at the Indiana Clinical and Translational Sciences Institute located at Indiana University. Non-failing LV tissues were obtained from donor hearts not suitable for transplantation as well and were collected and stored in the same manner. Demographic characteristics of these LV tissue samples are provided in .

## Ethical approval
The animal experiments conducted as a part of this study complied with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the U.S. National Institutes of Health. Mice were euthanized by thoracotomy under 1% to 4% inhaled isoflurane. All experiments with mice were performed according to the protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the Indiana University School of Medicine (approval reference #21189). Eight to 16-week-old C57BL/6J mice of both sexes were used for this study. Genotype- and sex-matched mice were randomly assigned to experimental groups to mitigate the cage effect. Operators were blinded to mouse genotypes until the end of the analysis. All the procedures involving human samples were conformed to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the Guidelines for the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, as well as approved by the Indiana University Institutional Review Board (approval reference #08-018). All participants provided informed written consent before inclusion in the study.

## Statistics
Data are presented as the mean ± SEM (except in echocardiographic data that use the standard deviation because no clear variation bars are shown otherwise) from independent experiments with different biological samples per group. Triplicate experiments were performed for all biochemical and cell biology studies. The number of in vitro biological samples per group was 4 to 8. The number of mouse samples per group was 4 to 22. The number of human samples per group was 8 to 10. The exact sample size for each experimental group/condition is given as a number in each figure/table legend. To allow the direct evaluation of the distribution of the data, we present graphical data as scatter/dot plots. Normality was assessed with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The following statistical tests were used: unpaired 2-tailed Student’s t test for comparisons between 2 groups, 1-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey's post hoc test for multiple pairwise comparisons, 2-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test for comparisons between 2 groups with different treatments, and two-way repeated-measures ANOVA with Bonferroni’s post hoc test for groups over time. EndNote 20 was used for analysis with full reference citation.

## Results

## Postn is identified as a novel target of the MIAT/miR-150 axis in our transcriptome profiling analyses and is activated by maladaptive MIAT in mouse hearts
We previously reported that miR-150 overexpression ameliorated cardiac dysfunction and fibrosis post-MI caused by MIAT, directly establishing an in vivo functional link between MIAT and miR-150 in HF. To identify novel targets of the MIAT/miR-150 axis that promote HF, we performed transcriptome profiling in the left ventricles of 4 groups: WT vs MIAT KO and miR-150fl/fl vs cardiac-specific miR-150 conditional knockout (cKO). , Based on the correlation between genotype and transcript signatures, we filtered our array datasets that are accessible through the National Center for Biotechnology Information Gene Expression Omnibus Series (access numbers GSE185396 and GSE177057). , We identify 4 significantly dysregulated and potentially maladaptive genes that are downregulated in MIAT KO compared to WT ( and , I dataset) and upregulated in cardiac-specific miR-150 cKO compared to miR-150fl/fl ( and , II dataset). In silico prediction algorithms , , reveal that among these 4 genes, only Postn possesses a conserved miR-150 binding site ( and ). Consequently, we focused on POSTN as a novel regulatory target of miR-150. 

## POSTN is a direct target of miR-150 and is downregulated by the Carv/β1AR/β-arrestin/miR-150 axis
Although functional miR binding sites are commonly found in the 3’-untranslated regions (3’-UTRs) of target mRNAs, it has been reported that the miR targeting can also occur in the 5’-UTRs or coding regions. Using bioinformatic miR target prediction tools , , based on the evolutionary conservation of target sites for miR-150 across species, we have identified a putative conserved binding site for miR-150 in the coding region of POSTN genes in humans and rodents ( ), suggesting evolutionary conservation of miR-150 regulation of POSTN and their roles in both rodent and human systems. In addition to this conserved binding site, rat and mouse Postn genes have 2 and 3 additional putative binding sites, respectively, for miR-150 outside the coding region. To test whether human POSTN is a direct target of miR-150, we co-transfected miR-150 mimics and constitutively active luciferase (LUC) reporter constructs containing the conserved POSTN binding site. miR-150 overexpression results in a significant decrease in LUC activity from the WT POSTN reporter. However, mutation of the seed binding sites for miR-150 abolishes this decrease in LUC activity ( ). Postn was upregulated in post-MI mouse hearts, concurrent with fibrotic MIAT upregulation and antifibrotic miR-150 downregulation. , We have further shown that concurrent with miR-150 downregulation , and MIAT upregulation, LV POSTN is upregulated in patients with HFrEF ( ). Next, our cardiac cell fractionation studies have revealed that Postn expression at baseline is significantly higher in CFs than CMs, cardiac endothelial cells (CEs), and cardiac inflammatory cells (CIs). Postn is dramatically upregulated in CFs and CMs isolated from mouse hearts post-MI ( ), which is consistent with reports showing the expression of POSTN genes in human and mouse CMs , , as well as is similarly associated with MIAT expression. Together with previous in vitro studies , , and our in vivo studies reporting direct interaction between MIAT and miR-150, our data thus suggest that POSTN is a direct target of the MIAT/miR-150 axis.
Carv stimulated miR-150 expression in the heart acting through β-arrestin (β-arr)-mediated β1AR signaling. We have also shown that Carv/β1AR/β-arr-responsive miR-150 played a vital protective role in MI. Our data in ( ) suggest that miR-150 is a new upstream negative regulator of POSTN. This led us to examine whether Carv inversely regulates the novel target of miR-150, Postn in left ventricles of WT mice. Cardiac Postn is downregulated upon Carv stimulation when compared to the vehicle control (see WT in ). We next tested whether β-arr signaling is required for inhibition of Postn by Carv. The Carv-mediated inhibition of Postn that occurs in WT mice is not observed in hearts from β-arr1 or β-arr2 KO mice ( ). Because Carv is a β-arr–biased ligand for both β1AR and β2AR, , we then measured Postn expression in hearts from β1AR KO or β2AR KO mice. We show that the Carv-mediated inhibition of Postn observed in WT mice is blunted in hearts lacking β1AR ( ). These data indicate that Postn is regulated mainly by β1AR, which is consistent with our previous study showing the β1AR specificity of Carv-mediated miR-150 activation. 
Because Postn is downregulated in mouse hearts treated with Carv, we next tested whether POSTN is similarly regulated in HCFs treated with Carv. POSTN is downregulated in HCFs subjected to hypoxia/reoxygenation conditions after Carv treatment ( ) concurrent with miR-150 upregulation. Concurrent with the downregulation of miR-150, , we have also observed that POSTN is increased in HCFs after hypoxia/reoxygenation ( ). These findings are consistent with a report in post-MI hearts and our data in isolated CFs from ischemic myocardium ( ).
Given that POSTN is a direct target of miR-150 ( ), we next tested whether POSTN is downregulated by miR-150. Our protein analysis reveals POSTN upregulation in miR-150 KO mouse left ventricles compared to WT controls ( ). Conversely, our in vivo gain-of-function studies show that miR-150 overexpression decreases the expression of Postn in mouse left ventricles ( ). Thus, our findings suggest that Carv/β1AR/β-arr signaling regulates the miR-150/POSTN axis and that POSTN is a key downstream target of miR-150 in hearts and CFs.

## Profibrotic POSTN is a critical functional target of miR-150 during HCF activation
Because Postn expression is significantly enriched in CFs and upregulated in CFs isolated from ischemic mouse hearts ( ), and considering that miR-150 is downregulated in CFs from TAC mice and acts as an important negative regulator of CF activation, , we next focused on primary adult HCFs and conducted loss-of-function studies, as previously validated. , Although POSTN does not affect miR-150 expression, it is upregulated in HCFs transfected with antimiR-150 ( ). Consistent with our prior reports demonstrating the antifibrotic actions of miR-150 in HCFs, , we have shown that miR-150 knockdown increases the expression of fibrotic genes such as SNAIL and COL3A1 in HCFs ( and ) as well as promoting HCF proliferation ( and ) and migration ( and ). Conversely, we have observed that POSTN knockdown decreases HCF proliferation and migration compared to controls ( and ). This finding aligns with our data showing that HCFs with POSTN knockdown exhibit decreased mRNA levels of ACTA2 and COL5A3 as well as reduced protein levels of α-SMA and POSTN compared to controls ( ). 
Finally, to establish the functional linkage among miR-150, POSTN, and HCF activation, we applied an anti-miR/small interfering (siRNA)–based rescue strategy to validate the functional relevance of the new direct miR-150 target, POSTN. Consistent with our earlier observations ( and ), anti–miR-150 treatment promotes HCF proliferation and migration ( , , , and ) as well as enhances the expression of ACTA2 and COL5A3 and the levels of POSTN and α-SMA ( ). These effects of miR-150 knockdown are abrogated by siRNA against POSTN ( , , and ). Combined with our data revealing increased cardiac levels of POSTN in miR-150 KO mice ( ) and decreased cardiac expression of Postn in miR-150 transgenic mice ( ), our findings indicate that miR-150 exerts protective effects in part through direct functional repression of POSTN, a positive regulator of CF activation.

## Postn ablation in miR-150 KO mice largely corrects cardiac dysfunction post-MI mediated by miR-150 deletion
POSTN is a direct target of miR-150 in vitro ( ), miR-150 acts as a gatekeeper of beneficial CF function in part by inhibiting fibrotic POSTN ( and ), and their correlative cardiac actions after chronic MI are shown. , , However, an in vivo functional relationship between miR-150 and POSTN in the heart has not been established. To directly investigate their functional interaction in the heart, we bred miR-150 KO mice with Postn KO mice that had attenuated cardiac phenotypes only after chronic MI and generated a novel miR-150/Postn double knockout (DKO) mouse line. We first conducted permanent ligation of the left anterior descending artery in mice to induce MI. We observed that miR-150 KO mice exhibit normal cardiac function at baseline ( , and ) but respond differently to chronic MI. Consistent with a previous report, cardiac function is significantly compromised in miR-150 KO mice following chronic MI. First, MI significantly worsens the cardiac function of miR-150 KO mice at 4 weeks as indicated by a decreased cardiac output, ejection fraction (EF), fractional shortening (FS), stroke volume, and systolic LV anterior wall thickness as well as an increase in end-systolic volume (ESV) compared to those of WT controls ( , and ). miR-150 KO mice also display impaired cardiac function at 8 weeks post-MI, shown by a significant decrease in cardiac output, EF, FS, stroke volume, and systolic LV anterior wall thickness ( , and ). In contrast, WT littermate controls show less functional impairment at 4 weeks ( , and ) and 8 weeks following MI ( , and ).
To further test the hypothesis that miR-150 protects mouse hearts against chronic MI by repressing Postn, we next examined the responses to MI in the miR-150/Postn DKO mouse line. We show that cardiac function of miR-150/Postn DKO mice is indistinguishable from WT controls at baseline ( , and ). However, a significant improvement in cardiac function at 4 weeks after MI is observed in miR-150/Postn DKO mice compared to miR-150 KO mice, indicated by an increase in EF, FS, and systolic left ventricular posterior wall thickness as well as a decrease in end-diastolic volume, ESV, diastolic left ventricular internal diameter (LVID), and systolic LVID ( , and ). miR-150/Postn DKO mice also display enhanced cardiac function at 8 weeks post-MI as evidenced by a significant increase in EF and FS as well as a significant decrease in end-diastolic volume, ESV, diastolic LVID, and systolic LVID ( , and ) compared to those of miR-150 KO mice. We have not observed any difference in post-MI mortality between groups ( : see n for animal numbers per each group at week 0, week 4, and week 8 after MI).

## Sustained Postn ablation alleviates cardiac damage, inflammation, and apoptosis induced by miR-150 loss after chronic MI
We have previously reported that miR-150 KO mice exhibit excessive maladaptive remodeling following MI, including cardiac damage, inflammation, and apoptosis. To determine whether the repression of Postn mediates the major functions of miR-150 in vivo, we used miR-150/Postn DKO mice and assessed post-MI remodeling in comparison to miR-150 KO controls. Our findings indicate that miR-150/Postn DKO mouse hearts display a reduction in the loss of normal architecture and cellular integrity after 8 weeks of MI ( ). Next, we evaluated whether an improved cardiac inflammatory cell response contributes to the decreased structure disorganization observed in miR-150/Postn DKO mouse hearts following MI. Inflammatory markers Tnf and Il6 are downregulated in miR-150/Postn DKO mouse hearts ( and ) compared to miR-150 KO hearts post-MI. Furthermore, we observed decreased mRNA levels of HF markers Acta1 and Nppa ( and ) after 8 weeks of MI in miR-150/Postn DKO mouse hearts compared to miR-150 KO hearts.
Finally, we have found that miR-150/Postn DKO mouse hearts contain significantly fewer cleaved caspase-3-positive cells compared to miR-150 KO MI controls ( and ), indicating decreased apoptosis in miR-150/Postn DKO hearts post-MI. Our data further show that miR-150 KO MI hearts exhibit increased expression of apoptotic Bax and Trp53 compared to expression in WT controls, but miR-150/Postn DKO MI hearts display decreased expression of these apoptotic genes ( and ) compared to levels in miR-150 KO MI hearts. Our previous study also reported that miR-150 KO MI hearts had increased mRNA levels of Bax and Trp53 as compared to WT MI hearts, without affecting protein levels of BAX and p53. Altogether, our results indicate that sustained Postn downregulation attenuates adverse post-MI remodeling caused by miR-150 loss and that miR-150 is a functionally significant upstream negative regulator of Postn in the heart. 

## Loss of Postn in miR-150 KO mice blunts cardiac fibrosis after chronic MI observed following miR-150 deficiency
Postn was a key fibrotic gene after chronic MI. , To further determine the response of miR-150/Postn DKO mice to MI, we accordingly examined the degree of fibrosis using Masson’s trichrome staining and Picrosirius Red staining of the hearts at 8 weeks post-MI. As reported previously, we find a much greater degree of fibrosis in miR-150 KO hearts than in WT MI controls ( ). We next observe reduced fibrosis post-MI in miR-150/Postn DKO hearts compared to miR-150 KO hearts ( ). Next, miR-150 KO MI hearts display increased expression of fibrotic Ctgf, Col1a1, and Col6a1 ( ) compared to expression in WT controls, but miR-150/Postn DKO MI hearts exhibit decreased expression of these profibrotic genes ( ) compared to miR-150 KO controls. Collectively, our data shows for the first time that genetic deletion of Postn significantly alleviates adverse postinfarct remodeling mediated by miR-150 loss. 

## Discussion
In this study, we identify a direct functional interaction between miR-150 and POSTN as a novel regulatory mechanism relevant to both HCF activation and murine MI. Using primary adult HCFs, we show for the first time that miR-150 functionally inhibits profibrotic POSTN; specifically, the enhanced expression of POSTN in HCFs lacking miR-150 leads to a higher degree of CF activation ( and ). Moreover, using a new miR-150/Postn DKO mouse model, we show that the loss of Postn mitigates the impairment of LV function and excessive adverse remodeling following MI that is mediated by miR-150 deletion. The miR-150/Postn DKO partially rescues the chronic MI phenotype exhibited by miR-150 KO mice that show greater severity than WT MI ( , , , , ). Therefore, our findings indicate that POSTN is a critical ischemic stress-responsive target of miR-150 in both whole mouse hearts and HCFs and that miR-150 exerts protective effects on HCF activation and murine MI by repressing fibrotic POSTN.
POSTN is a matricellular protein, and circulating POSTN is increased in patients with MI. Cardiac Postn is also upregulated in post-MI and post-TAC hearts, concurrent with antifibrotic miR-150 downregulation. , Loss- and gain-of-function mouse studies have reported that Postn was a crucial maladaptive regulator in cardiac remodeling, including fibrosis after chronic MI. , Our filtering and validation analyses of array datasets lead us to discover POSTN as a novel target of miR-150 repression ( ). Our cardiac cell fractionation study reveals that Postn expression is higher in CFs than CMs, CEs, and CIs at baseline and that Postn is drastically upregulated in CFs isolated from mouse hearts post-MI ( ). Our current study also shows for the first time that POSTN loss blunts increased HCF activation mediated by miR-150 knockdown ( and ) and that Postn deletion alleviates cardiac damage, inflammation, apoptosis, and fibrosis post-MI observed following miR-150 loss ( , , , ). In this study, we also show that LV POSTN is upregulated in patients with HFrEF ( ), consistent with other studies to report the upregulation of circulating POSTN in patients with MI. , We also report that POSTN genes are downregulated in mouse hearts and HCFs by Carv ( ), concurrent with the upregulation of miR-150. , Therefore, our findings of Postn upregulation in CFs during MI ( ) further support that POSTN repression could be therapeutically beneficial for cardiac fibrosis and HF. Postn was shown to be regulated by miR-203-3p in mouse CMs. Except for this previous study, little is known about the regulation of POSTN by miRs in the heart. Given our findings that fibrotic POSTN is a critical functional target of miR-150 in primary adult HCFs ( and ) and whole mouse hearts ( , , , ) and that POSTN was regulated by miR-150 by direct interaction ( ), current and future targeted treatment options based on POSTN could be taken into consideration for MI patients with reduced levels of circulating miR-150.
The β1AR is primarily expressed in the heart, and β-arr–mediated β1AR signaling provides myocardial protection against catecholamine-induced stress. In our prior study, we found that the β-blocker Carv activated miR-150 through β-arr–mediated β1AR protective signaling. We also reported that miR-150 was a critical downstream mechanism by which β-arr–mediated β1AR signaling exerted protective effects. Combined with all the data presented here, we propose that the regulatory mechanisms of miR-150 upregulation, mediated by β-arr and β1AR, promote beneficial remodeling in the failing heart by suppressing CF activation and repressing fibrotic markers such as POSTN. We showed that systemic loss of miR-150 significantly compromised cardiac function and remodeling following MI by promoting cell death. In contrast, another study reported that systemic overexpression of miR-150 blunted murine MI by inhibiting monocyte migration. POSTN has been recognized as a key maladaptive regulator in ventricular remodeling, particularly fibrosis, after chronic MI. , Although prior studies have established a correlative relationship between miR-150 and POSTN in HF, the functional dynamics between them remains unclear due to: 1) a lack of mechanistic insight into how the miR-150/POSTN axis regulates HF; and 2) the absence of rigorous studies establishing their direct in vivo functional relationship in HF.
Our previous fractionation studies of different myocardial cell types revealed that miR-150 expression was significantly higher in normal CMs than in CFs at baseline and that its expression was not altered in stressed CMs, CFs, CIs, and CEs isolated from ischemic myocardium 1 week post-MI. Additionally, other studies have reported that cardiac-specific overexpression of miR-150 attenuated HF induced by TAC and that the loss of miR-150 resulted in increased cardiac fibrosis after TAC. miR-150 was downregulated in CFs (but not CMs) isolated from TAC mice. MiR-150 also negatively regulated mouse CF activation in vitro. Coinciding with POSTN upregulation, , , TGF-β1 or TGF-β/Smad signaling inhibited miR-150 expression. , Furthermore, miR-150 suppressed TGF-β in vivo in rat hearts and in primary CFs. TGF-β1 activation was directly correlated with POSTN upregulation, whereas POSTN itself mediated TGF-β responsiveness to SMAD signaling. POSTN acted as a key maladaptive regulator in fibrosis following chronic MI, partly by activating profibrotic markers including collagen type V. These findings support the notion that miR-150 exerts antifibrotic effects, whereas POSTN has profibrotic roles in the heart. Moreover, our prior studies provided additional experimental evidence supporting the protective roles of miR-150 in ischemic mouse hearts and HCFs. These studies showed that miR-150 inhibited fibroblast activation—including fibroblast proliferation and migration as well as myofibroblast accumulation—through repression of PCNA, CCNB1, CTHRC1, TNC, classical TGF-β1/SMAD signaling, and other profibrotic markers, such as α-SMA, CTGF, SRF, VIMENTIN, HOXA4, and SPRR1A. , Recently, we have also published findings using a novel fibroblast-specific miR-150 cKO mouse model. This model demonstrated that selective loss of miR-150 in adult myofibroblasts led to various cardiac structural and functional abnormalities associated with post-MI remodeling, as well as increased the cardiac expression of additional profibrotic markers including SNAIL and RUNX3. In all, this phenotypic study shows the myofibroblast-specific effects of miR-150 in the diseased heart. Here, we present new discoveries that establish the functional miR-150/POSTN axis in primary HCF activation and murine MI as well as report a signaling axis encompassing Carv, β1AR/β-arr signaling, miR-150, and POSTN.

## Study limitations
Although we report that miR-150 is a key negative regulator of primary adult HCF activation by functionally suppressing fibrotic POSTN and that systemic loss of Postn in mice attenuates maladaptive remodeling after chronic MI caused by miR-150 deficiency, miR-150 or POSTN levels in other cardiac lineages may also play an important role as supported by our prior study using CM-specific miR-150 cKO mice. A potential role of POSTN in CMs in addition to its well-known profibrotic actions is also indicated by our data, showing that Postn is drastically upregulated in CMs isolated from mouse hearts post-MI ( ). This finding is consistent with reports of POSTN upregulation in injured human and mouse CMs. , , The notion of the potential CM role of POSTN is also supported by previous studies showing that Postn overexpression in rats caused a decrease in CMs ; an injection of POSTN in rats suppressed the sodium current in ventricular CMs, modulating electrical remodeling ; and that POSTN promoted mouse CM apoptosis. Future cell type–specific mouse studies and detailed studies on the other actions of the miR-150/POSTN axis in all cell types (eg, measurements of physiological and functional CM parameters in primary CMs) are therefore warranted to fully elucidate the potential contribution of miR-150 or Postn expression in other cell types to maladaptive post-MI remodeling. Especially, whether Postn is a significant downstream target of CM or fibroblast miR-150 during apoptotic or fibrotic remodeling in murine MI and whether the miR-150/POSTN axis alters CM–CF interactions remain to be investigated and are beyond the scope of the current investigation. Our prior study showed that miR-150 KO mouse hearts exhibited neutrophil infiltration and CM apoptosis at 1 day after MI and that the protective roles of miR-150 during MI were partly attributed to direct repression of proapoptotic genes Egr2 and p2x7r in CMs. Because the healing process is largely complete by 8 weeks post-MI when we harvest hearts in the current study, additional immunohistochemical assessments and gene expression studies at earlier time points must be conducted to gain a comprehensive understanding of the sequence of events. For example, it remains elusive which cell types have died as well as what is the status of CMs, inflammatory and wound healing responses, and vascularization at multiple time points post-MI in the context of miR-150 and/or Postn deletion. Significantly, our functional rescue data in HCFs and mouse hearts establish a functional link between miR-150 and POSTN in HF, supporting that they are in a linear pathway. Nevertheless, miR-150 can also have other targets mediating distinct functions. We will thus explore additional new functional targets (ie, POSTN-independent mechanisms) in the heart by cross-referencing the gene signature from cardiac-specific miR-150 cKO mice with miR-150 target prediction analyses in our future studies. Last, we use a primary adult HCF model in the current study to bolster the translational implication. Although this human cell model is an appropriate human cell source for HF remodeling in vitro, the mode of action in mice could be different in human cells. We could use CFs isolated from the mice used in this study to confirm the miR-150/POSTN axis in CFs.

## Conclusions
Our data from studies using primary adult HCFs and a novel double loss-of-function mouse model show that miR-150 exerts a protective effect, in part, by directly repressing the profibrotic protein POSTN and that reductions in POSTN mitigate adverse remodeling associated with miR-150 loss following chronic MI. Although both miR-150 and POSTN have been associated with HF in human studies , and their correlative relationship in the heart has been observed, , , our current work with unbiased transcriptomic filtering analysis and a unique mouse model directly establishes the functional link between miR-150 and POSTN during both HCF activation and murine MI. This also defines a key mechanism through which miR-150 suppresses CF activation. Given that increased POSTN levels or decreased expression levels of miR-150 , , , contribute to various heart conditions, the actions of the miR-150/POSTN axis in fibroblasts and cardiac tissues may be broadly relevant across different types of cardiac stress. Thus, genetic or pharmacologic strategies to reduce POSTN or enhance miR-150 expression, thereby modulating CF activation, may represent promising adjuvant therapies for organ fibrosis and HF.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: Given our data showing that detrimental POSTN is a functional and direct target of miR-150, and the fact that there is an inverse correlation between miR-150 and POSTN during MI, upregulating miR-150 or downregulating POSTN could be a potential adjunctive approach to limiting peri-infarct damage and promoting recovery post-MI. The nonselective β-blocker, carvedilol via β-arr–mediated β1AR signaling, has been shown to enhance or repress the cardiac expression of miR-150 or POSTN, respectively. We thus anticipate that carvedilol and treatments based on POSTN (eg, cinnamaldehyde and curcumol, other known POSTN inhibitors) may enhance benefits in patients with MI, HF, and organ fibrosis that are associated with activated POSTN or reduced miR-150. This efficacy can be easily identified by circulating levels of increased POSTN or decreased miR-150 in the acute phase of the disease. This novel functional miR-150/POSTN axis can be also genetically manipulated by miR mimic-based overexpression, a strategy used in clinical trials.
TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: Although previous studies have reported modulation of miRs as a new therapeutic strategy, oligonucleotide approaches showed deleterious off-target effects (eg, nonspecific immune activation), warranting a better understanding of upstream signaling, downstream targets, and an optimal mode of delivery to safely pursue miRs as therapeutic modalities. Our discovery of a protective mechanism involving the β1AR/β-arr/miR-150/POSTN axis has important clinical implications in the development of much-needed therapies. Specifically, our research will provide the rationale for refining β1AR-selectivity of β-arr–biased ligands or allosteric activators that leverages protective mechanisms mediated by carvedilol, a weak β-arr–biased ligands for both β1AR and β2AR. Our study will also facilitate the development of new and effective miR-150 activators and POSTN inhibitors for the treatment of heart disease associated with fibroblast dysfunction.
