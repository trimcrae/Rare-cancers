## Abstract
Statins act as antifibrotic agents but their mechanism is unclear. Therefore, we aimed to evaluate the antifibrotic effects of rosuvastatin in a chronic kidney fibrosis model in vivo and transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)-stimulated Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells in vitro. Mice with unilateral ischemic reperfusion injury and contralateral nephrectomy (uIRIx) were administered vehicle or rosuvastatin (10 mg/kg/day by oral gavage) for four weeks and kidney fibrosis markers were analyzed. Moreover, control and homeobox protein Hox-A13 (HOXA13) knocked-down MDCK cells were stimulated with TGF-β1 (5 ng/ml) and then treated with rosuvastatin. The uIRIx mice developed severe tubulointerstitial fibrosis with increased α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), collagen I and uterine sensitization-associated gene-1 (USAG-1) expression, but rosuvastatin therapy attenuated these expression and improved fibrosis. Rosuvastatin also reduced Smad3 phosphorylation and increased Smad1/5/9 phosphorylation, both associated with bone morphogenetic protein-7 (BMP-7) signaling. TGF-β1-stimulated MDCK cells exhibited increased α-SMA, fibronectin, vimentin, and collagen 1 expression, which rosuvastatin reversed. In addition, TGF-β1-stimulated MDCK cells demonstrated increased USAG-1 expression without changes in BMP-7 expression. Gene knockdown using HOXA13 siRNA suggested rosuvastatin decreased USAG-1 expression by increasing HOXA13 expression. Our results demonstrate that rosuvastatin inhibits kidney fibrosis by activating BMP-7 signaling via upregulation of HOXA13 and downregulation of USAG-1.

## Introduction
The progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD) to end-stage kidney disease is caused by the gradual loss of viable nephrons, which are replaced by fibrotic scars. This fibrotic pathway involves the loss of kidney tubules, as well as accumulation of macrophages, lymphocytes, fibroblasts, and matrix proteins, including collagen, fibronectin, and laminin , . Transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) is the primary regulator of kidney fibrosis, and its role in kidney fibrosis has been extensively investigated. TGF-β1 mediates kidney fibrosis by promoting tubular deletion, apoptosis, and induction of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), which results in an increase in the number of myofibroblasts, elevated extracellular matrix (ECM) protein synthesis, and decreased ECM degradation – . Additionally, it plays a pivotal role in regulating inflammation and cell infiltration in diseased kidneys, contributing to progressive kidney fibrosis .
Bone morphogenetic protein-7 (BMP-7) is an endogenous growth factor that has several biological functions. Studies on BMP-7 knockout mice have demonstrated that BMP-7 is essential for proper kidney development, as the mice died shortly after birth from widespread kidney dysplasia . BMP-7 expression is downregulated following acute ischemic kidney injury, diabetic nephropathy , hypertensive nephrosclerosis , and unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) . In vitro experimental research has shown that exogenous BMP-7 could inhibit or reverse TGF-β1-induced EMT. It could also prevent or delay the development of fibrosis, reduce inflammation, and improve kidney function in a variety of kidney disease models . As a result, BMP-7 is recognized as a TGF-β1 antagonist with antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory properties . In particular, BMP-7 counteracts the profibrotic actions of TGF-β1 via the Smad1/5/9 signaling pathway , . Therefore, the balance between TGF-β1 and BMP-7 signaling is a determining factor of kidney fibrosis progression , suggesting that targeting TGF-β1 or restoring BMP-7 levels in CKD could have potential therapeutic benefits. However, BMP-7 regulation in the kidney is complex, as BMP-7 activity is not only dependent on its availability but also on a balance of various agonists and antagonists.
Uterine sensitization-associated gene-1 (USAG-1) is the most abundant BMP-7 antagonist expressed in adult kidneys, primarily in distal tubular epithelial cells . It binds to BMP-7 and inhibits its interaction with the receptor, thus negatively regulating BMP-7 activity in kidney injury models , . Studies on experimental acute and chronic kidney injury models have revealed that mice lacking USAG-1 (USAG-1−/− mice) are resistant to kidney injury and have preserved kidney function , . On the contrary, obstructed kidneys of wild-type mice showed prominent tubulointerstitial fibrosis; marked upregulation of tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukin-1β, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, TGF-β1, and type IV collagen expression; and markedly increased USAG-1 expression in distal tubular epithelial cells. In contrast, obstructed kidneys of USAG-1−/− mice exhibited only mild tubular dilatation and low activation of profibrotic and proinflammatory genes . Moreover, the obstructed kidneys of USAG-1−/− mice exhibited enhanced BMP-7 signaling while preserving the phosphorylation of Smad1/5/9 and reducing kidney fibrosis. Overall, enhanced USAG-1 secretion by damaged distal tubule cells seems to aggravate tubule injury, inflammatory cell infiltration, and interstitial fibrosis by affecting BMP-7 signaling pathway activity . Therefore, USAG-1 is a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of acute kidney injury (AKI) or CKD.
Statins are cholesterol-lowering drugs that have become the standard of care for both primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular diseases , . In addition to their lipid-lowering effects, statins have potential pleiotropic effects such as anti-inflammatory and antiproliferative effects, inhibition of isoprenoid synthesis, and improvement of endothelial function , . Preclinical data suggest that statins may exert beneficial effects on damaged kidneys by acting through antifibrotic pathways, but the underlying mechanism remains unclear . While statins have been demonstrated to affect several processes and signaling pathways involved in the progression of kidney fibrosis, few studies have investigated USAG-1 and BMP-7 mediated antifibrotic pathways . In addition, transcription factor homeobox protein Hox-A13 (HOXA13) negatively regulates USAG-1 expression to enhance BMP-7 signaling . Accordingly, the HOXA13/USAG-1/BMP-7 pathway has been identified as a novel therapeutic target for preventing kidney fibrosis progression .
In this study, we aimed to investigate the antifibrotic effect of rosuvastatin and elucidate the mechanism by which rosuvastatin exerts its antifibrotic effects via the HOXA13/USAG-1/BMP-7 pathway in unilateral ischemia/reperfusion injury (IRI) followed by contralateral nephrectomy (uIRIx) mice and Madin Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells. Our findings may provide a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of kidney fibrosis in CKD patients.

## Results

## Effects of rosuvastatin treatment on kidney function and albuminuria in uIRIx mice
Both vehicle- and rosuvastatin-treated uIRIx mice exhibited significantly lower hemoglobin levels than did normal control (11.9 ± 0.9 g/dL vs. 13.2 ± 0.9 g/dL, p < 0.001). However, there was no difference in the degree of anemia between the vehicle- and rosuvastatin-treated groups (Fig.  A). The uIRIx mice in the study demonstrated features of CKD. Compared with age-matched control mice, uIRIx mice exhibited a significant increase in serum creatinine levels (0.40 ± 0.07 mg/dL vs. 0.20 ± 0.01 mg/dL, p < 0.001). In contrast, rosuvastatin therapy improved kidney function, as demonstrated by decreased serum creatinine levels compared with those of the vehicle-fed group (0.24 ± 0.06 mg/dL vs. 0.40 ± 0.07 mg/dL, p < 0.001, Fig.  B). Furthermore, uIRIx mice had increased urinary albumin excretion and elevated systolic blood pressure (Fig.  C,D). Notably, rosuvastatin treatment effectively reduced both urinary albumin excretion and systolic blood pressure.
 

## Rosuvastatin treatment ameliorates tubulointerstitial fibrosis in uIRIx mice
We evaluated the degree of tubulointerstitial fibrosis in the kidneys of uIRIx mice, with or without four weeks of rosuvastatin treatment. Masson’s trichrome (MT) and Sirius red (SR) staining of vehicle-treated uIRIx mouse kidneys demonstrated a robust tubulointerstitial matrix and collagen deposition. In contrast, four weeks of rosuvastatin treatment considerably improved tubulointerstitial fibrosis (Fig.  A–C). Immunohistochemical staining revealed increased α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression and F4/80-positive inflammatory cell infiltration in the interstitium of vehicle-treated uIRIx kidneys. Quantitative analyses revealed that four weeks of rosuvastatin treatment effectively decreased the α-SMA positive area and F4/80-positive inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig.  D,E). Overall, these findings suggest that rosuvastatin treatment inhibits the infiltration of inflammatory cells and ameliorates tubulointerstitial fibrosis progression.
 

## Effect of rosuvastatin on expression of molecules associated with fibrosis and antifibrosis in uIRIx mouse kidneys.
Quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was performed to assess the mRNA levels of TGF-β1, collagen 1, BMP-7, USAG-1, and HOXA13 in kidney tissues. Figure  illustrates that the expression of TGF-β1 and collagen 1 mRNAs was markedly upregulated in uIRIx kidneys but dramatically reduced after four weeks of rosuvastatin treatment (Figs.  A,B). Moreover, uIRIx or rosuvastatin treatment had no effect on BMP-7 gene expression levels in the kidneys (Fig.  C). However, the expression of USAG-1 increased in vehicle-treated uIRIx kidneys and rosuvastatin treatment significantly reduced USAG-1 expression (Fig.  D). The transcription factor HOXA13 negatively regulates USAG-1 expression , and the expression of HOXA13 was significantly increased in the kidneys of rosuvastatin-treated uIRIx mice compared with that of vehicle-treated mice (Fig.  E). These data indicate that rosuvastatin may enhance the antifibrotic pathway, at least in part, by downregulating the USAG-1 expression via the upregulation of HOXA13 expression.
 In uIRIx kidneys, significant increases in fibronectin, α-SMA, collagen 1, and vimentin expression were demonstrated using western blot analysis, while E-cadherin expression was decreased (Fig.  A–F). Rosuvastatin treatment effectively reversed these changes and improved tubulointerstitial fibrosis. There was no significant effect on BMP-7 expression. However, there was a significant increase in USAG-1 expression in vehicle-treated uIRIx kidney, which decreased with subsequent rosuvastatin treatment. This resulted in a significant decrease in the USAG-1/BMP-7 ratio (Fig.  G–I). In vehicle-treated uIRIx mice, the expression of phosphorylated Smad3 (pSmad3) increased, while phosphorylated Smad 1/5/9 (pSmad1/5/9) decreased remarkably during the development of fibrosis in injured kidneys (Fig.  ). In contrast, rosuvastatin treatment significantly inhibited Smad3 phosphorylation and ameliorated kidney fibrosis. Moreover, the kidneys of rosuvastatin-treated uIRIx mice showed significantly increased expression of pSmad1/5/9, suggesting the possible maintenance of BMP-7 regulated pathways via the suppression of USAG-1 expression.
 

## Rosuvastatin prevents TGF-β1-induced EMT and fibrosis in MDCK cells
We investigated the impact of statin on molecules associated with EMT in TGF-β1-stimulated MDCK cells. Immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated α-SMA upregulation and E-cadherin downregulation in MDCK cells co-cultured with TGF-β1. However, these effects were effectively reversed by rosuvastatin co-treatment (Fig.  A). Consistent with this finding, western blot analysis demonstrated that rosuvastatin inhibited TGF-β1-induced effects, significantly increased E-cadherin protein levels, and decreased the expression of α-SMA. Moreover, fibronectin, collagen 1, and vimentin protein levels significantly increased in TGF-β1-stimulated MDCK cells in western blot analysis, but markedly decreased with co-treatment with rosuvastatin (Fig.  B–F).
 Quantitative RT-PCR and western blot analyses revealed that BMP-7 gene and protein expression was not altered by either TGF-β1 stimulation or rosuvastatin co-treatment (Fig.  A). In contrast, TGF-β1 stimulation significantly increased USAG-1 expression, whereas rosuvastatin co-treatment significantly decreased it (Fig.  B). Moreover, TGF-β1 stimulation decreased HOXA13 mRNA expression in MDCK cells, whereas rosuvastatin treatment significantly increased it (Fig.  C). To further investigate the role of HOXA13, we knocked it down using small interfering RNA (siRNA). Treatment with HOXA13 siRNA significantly reduced HOXA13 mRNA expression, confirming effective knockdown efficiency (Fig.  C). Quantitative RT-PCR and western blot analyses indicated that siRNA-targeted HOXA13 knockdown did not significantly change the BMP-7 expression (Fig.  A,D,E, hatched bar). However, the effect of rosuvastatin on USAG-1 expression was significantly reversed upon HOXA13 knockdown (Fig.  B,D,F, hatched bar). These results suggested that rosuvastatin acts on HOXA13 to modulate USAG-1 expression. To determine the effect of rosuvastatin on the activity of the Smad signaling pathway, we examined the levels of phosphorylated and total Smad in MDCK cells. Western blotting revealed that TGF-β1 significantly increased pSmad3 and decreased pSmad1/5/9 expression, indicating increased activity of Smad3 and reduced activity of Smad1/5/9 signaling (Fig.  A–C). However, significant downregulation of p-Smad3 and upregulation of pSmad1/5/9 expression following rosuvastatin co-treatment indicate that rosuvastatin treatment could suppress the TGF-β1-induced Smad-associated fibrotic pathway (Fig.  A–C). Moreover, HOXA13 knockdown significantly abrogated the effect of rosuvastatin on the expression of downstream profibrotic gene products, α-SMA and collagen 1, in TGF-β1-stimulated MDCK cells (Fig.  D–F, hatched bar). These results suggest that rosuvastatin enhances the BMP-7-dependent signaling pathway via HOXA13-USAG-1 to exert its antifibrotic effects.
 

## Discussion
Our results demonstrated that rosuvastatin treatment effectively attenuated tubulointerstitial fibrosis in a mouse model of uIRIx. It also inhibited TGF-β1-induced EMT and fibrosis in MDCK cells in vitro. Interestingly, the antifibrotic effect of rosuvastatin treatment was mediated not by the upregulation of BMP-7 expression but by the significant downregulation of USAG-1 expression via increased HOXA13 expression. Moreover, rosuvastatin administration improved kidney function, albuminuria, and systolic blood pressure in the uIRIx mice. Overall, our data suggest that targeting the HOXA13/USAG-1/BMP-7 signaling pathway with rosuvastatin may be a novel and effective therapeutic strategy for CKD.
In this study, we utilized a mouse model of CKD that mimics the transition from ischemic AKI-to-CKD using uIRIx, to corroborate and extend the findings reported for adenine-induced nephropathy. Compared to the adenine-induced nephropathy model, the uIRIx model offers distinct advantages for investigating renoprotective agents. First, uIRIx more accurately reflects the clinical pathogenesis of AKI-to-CKD transition, a scenario frequently observed in postoperative hypoperfusion, bleeding, dehydration, shock, and sepsis , . In contrast, adenine nephropathy is driven by crystal nephropathy and metabolic toxicity, which do not represent the most common etiologies of human CKD . Second, the uIRIx model predominantly induces tubulointerstitial fibrosis with preserved glomerular architecture in the early phase, thereby reflecting key features of fibrogenic remodeling commonly observed during human CKD progression , . Third, unlike the adenine model, which often leads to variable severity and off-target gastrointestinal and systemic effects , uIRIx produces a reproducible and localized injury, allowing for more consistent interpretation of therapeutic responses . Although the uIRIx model has been widely used to simulate the clinically observed transition from AKI to CKD, it predominantly reflects tubulointerstitial injury and does not fully recapitulate chronic kidney diseases of primary glomerular origin. Therefore, caution is warranted when generalizing these findings to glomerulocentric forms of CKD. Nevertheless, progressive tubulointerstitial fibrosis represents a common final pathway of kidney function decline across diverse CKD etiologies, supporting the relevance of this model for investigating fibrosis-driven CKD progression and therapeutic interventions.
In our current study, rosuvastatin treatment significantly improved kidney function, as evidenced by amelioration of tubulointerstitial fibrosis and reduced expression of fibronectin, type I collagen and TGF-β1 in the kidney. Furthermore, decreased α-SMA and TGF-β1 levels in rosuvastatin-treated mice support its inhibitory effect on myofibroblast activation and ECM production in fibrotic kidneys. In addition to these histological and molecular improvements, rosuvastatin exerted pleiotropic effects, including the attenuation of albuminuria, suppression of blood pressure elevation, and reduction of systemic and kidney inflammation. Reduction of albuminuria has been attributed, in part, to the antioxidative properties of statins . However, whether rosuvastatin treatment reduces oxidative stress in the kidneys of uIRIx mice remains to be determined. Notably, we observed a significant decrease in systolic blood pressure following rosuvastatin treatment. While it is known that blood pressure reduction may partially attenuate CKD progression, the extent of kidney recovery observed in our study exceeds what would be expected from hemodynamic modulation alone . In our experimental model, rosuvastatin treatment resulted in a reversal of inflammation and fibrosis in mice with established CKD. These findings suggest that rosuvastatin exerted pleiotropic effects—beyond its modest antihypertensive action—that directly promoted tissue repair and fibrosis resolution. Reversal of endothelial dysfunction—via enhanced endothelial nitric oxide synthesis and decreased reactive oxygen species production— has been proposed as a central mechanism in the blood pressure-lowering effect of statins – . This antioxidative action is thought to alleviate vascular oxidative stress and improve endothelial function. Moreover, downregulation of angiotensin II receptor subtype 1 by statins have been implicated in both blood pressure reduction and attenuation of albuminuria . Despite these pleiotropic effects of rosuvastatin, they do not appear to restore hemoglobin levels in the context of CKD. This may be attributed to their limited ability to enhance erythropoietin production or counteract inflammation-induced hepcidin elevation – .
Previous studies have shown that BMP-7 is implicated in regeneration of the kidney tissue upon injury by antagonizing TGF-β action, the ultimate profibrotic growth factor. Administration of BMP-7 and its mimetics exerted therapeutic effects in both experimental AKI and CKD models , . However, these effects have not been consistently reproduced , , and kidney BMP-7 expression does not always correlate with kidney disease progression, particularly in chronic kidney injury . Our results indicated no significant differences in BMP-7 expression between the control and uIRIx mouse kidneys. However, we found that USAG-1, major BMP-7 antagonist, expression was significantly increased in the kidney tissues of uIRIx mice. Both in vivo and in vitro experiments showed that rosuvastatin exerted its anti-fibrotic effect by suppressing USAG-1, and not by upregulating BMP-7 expression. Notably, rosuvastatin treatment led to increased phosphorylation of Smad1/5/9, a canonical downstream mediator of BMP-7 signaling that inhibits profibrotic gene expression and supports epithelial cell integrity. Thus, the observed activation of this pathway, in the absence of increased BMP-7 expression, suggests that rosuvastatin may indirectly facilitate BMP-7 signaling activity. Therefore, rosuvastatin may confer renoprotective activity by modulating the balance between BMP-7 antagonists and downstream signaling components, rather than by directly altering BMP-7 abundance . However, our interpretation of BMP-7 pathway activation is based on surrogate markers such as Smad1/5/9 phosphorylation, and not on direct modulation or quantification of BMP-7 activity and further studies focusing on BMP-7 pathway regulation, including its antagonists and downstream signaling are needed to confirm its functional role in mediating the observed effects of rosuvastatin. Sustained expression of USAG-1 as well as the increased USAG-1 to BMP-7 ratio was observed in uIRIx mouse kidney in our study. The observed sustained expression of USAG-1 in uIRIx mouse kidney may imply ongoing inflammatory process as implicated by increased infiltrations of F4/80-positive inflammatory cells. Such persistent elevation of USAG-1 may inhibit the actions of remaining BMP-7 and further worsen the injuries to kidney tubules leading to progressive tubulointerstitial fibrosis. Notably, treatment with rosuvastatin reduced intrarenal USAG-1 expression and downregulated fibrosis-related genes and proteins, resulting in improved tubulointerstitial fibrosis. Moreover, in vitro experiments using siRNA demonstrated that effect of rosuvastatin on USAG-1 expression was decreased by HOXA13 gene knockdown. These results further indicate that rosuvastatin negatively modulates USAG-1 expression by upregulating HOXA13 gene.
Systemic administration of BMP-7 may effectively reduce kidney fibrosis in various models of kidney fibrosis , , . However, concerns remain regarding potential off-target effects, including ectopic bone formation and vascular calcification, particularly with long-term exposure . These risks stem from BMP-7’s pleiotropic actions and widespread receptor distribution. Moreover, the biological activity of BMP-7 in the kidney is regulated by a complex interplay of ECM binding, activation by cleavage of the pro-domain, and modulation by various extracellular antagonists . In this context, drugs targeting USAG-1, that is expressed restrictively in distal tubular epithelial cells in adult kidney, offers a more selective approach. Inhibiting USAG-1 locally can restore BMP-7 signaling in a physiologic, kidney-restricted manner, minimizing systemic exposure and reducing the risk of adverse effects.
In conclusion, rosuvastatin attenuated the progression of kidney fibrosis by activating the BMP-7-mediated antifibrotic pathway in the uIRIx model. In vitro experiments demonstrated that rosuvastatin enhanced the antifibrotic pathway by downregulating a dominant BMP-7 antagonist, USAG-1, via HOXA13 upregulation.

## Methods

## Animal experiments
The FVB mice (5–6 weeks old; Orient Bio, Gyeonggi-Do, Korea) were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions in a temperature-controlled environment with a 12-h light/dark cycle. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Yonsei University College of Medicine, Seoul, Korea (IACUC approval number: 2021-0218). Animal research throughout the current study was completed and reported in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. All methods are reported in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. For the initial unilateral IRI and second-stage contralateral nephrectomy surgery, the animals were anesthetized by inhalation of 1–3% isoflurane, and their body temperature was regulated during surgery using a heated thermoplate (Jeung Do Bio & Plant Co, Korea). On day 0, unilateral IRI was induced by clamping the left kidney pedicle with an atraumatic vascular clamp (Fine Science Tools, Foster City, CA, USA) for 42 min, while the right kidney was kept intact. After 2 weeks, the right kidneys were removed under the same anesthesia and surgical protocols. The mice that underwent uIRIx were randomly assigned to either the control or rosuvastatin group. They were orally administered the vehicle (0.5% methylcellulose) or rosuvastatin (10 mg/kg) daily for four weeks, a regimen selected based on prior studies demonstrating its efficacy and tolerability in mice, while avoiding the toxicity and metabolic complications observed with higher doses or prolonged treatment durations – . At the end of the experimental period, mice were humanely euthanized under deep anesthesia with isoflurane (5% for induction, maintained at 3–4% until loss of pedal reflex and cessation of respiration). The kidneys were collected at the end of the experiments, fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution, and embedded in paraffin, as previously described . Blood samples were collected through cardiac puncture under deep anesthesia, and serum creatinine concentrations were determined using a Fuji Dri-Chem 4000i system (Fujifilm, Japan). The urinary albumin concentration was measured using an ELISA kit (catalog no. ab-108792, Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), while the urinary creatinine concentration was measured using a creatinine colorimetric kit (catalog no. 500701, Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA). The urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio was calculated as the urinary albumin concentration divided by the urinary creatinine concentration. Blood pressure was measured using a computerized non-invasive tail cuff system (BP-2000 Blood Pressure Analysis System; Visitech Systems, NC, USA). To minimize induced stress, the animals were accustomed to the instrument for four consecutive days prior to definitive measurements. This consisted of a minimum of 10 repeated measurements per mouse.

## Kidney tissue pathological analysis
To analyze kidney fibrosis, kidney sections were stained with Masson’s trichrome and Sirius red. The areas positive for Masson’s trichrome and Sirius red were evaluated relative to the unit area using MetaMorph image analysis software (Molecular Devices, CA, USA). Immunohistochemistry for α-SMA (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) and F4/80 (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) was performed to identify α-SMA-positive myofibroblasts and F4/80-positive macrophages. The quantification of these cells was based on the number of cells per high power field. Microscopic assessment was performed in a blinded manner, with 20 randomly selected fields from each slide section examined at 400 × magnification, as previously described .

## MDCK cell culture, RNA knockdown of HOXA13, and rosuvastatin treatment
The MDCK cells were obtained from the Korean Cell Line Bank (Seoul, South Korea) and were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA), 100 U/ml penicillin G, and 100 mg/ml streptomycin. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified environment with 5% CO2. To achieve HOXA13 knockdown, RNA interference (RNAi) was utilized as detailed previously . Specifically, sub-confluent MDCK cells were transfected with either Stealth RNAi Negative Control Med GC (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) or predesigned small interfering RNA targeting canine HOXA13 using Lipofectamine 2000 Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The small interfering RNA sequences targeting HOXA13 were: sense 5′-UUUGUUUGUAGCAUAUUCCCGUUCG-3′ and antisense 5′-CGAACGGGAAUAUGCUACAAACAAA-3′. Post-transfection, cells were cultured in serum-free medium for 24 h and then exposed to recombinant human TGF-β1 (5 ng/ml, R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) alone or TGF-β1 in combination with rosuvastatin (co-treatment), in 1% FBS-containing medium for 48 h. Rosuvastatin was used at 10 μM, a concentration selected based on preliminary dose-ranging experiments showing no significant cytotoxicity, while higher doses induced apoptotic morphology. Details are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

## Immunofluorescent staining of MDCK cells
The treated MDCK cells were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and immediately fixed with 4% formaldehyde diluted in PBS for 15 min at room temperature. This was followed by permeabilization with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 min. The cells were subsequently blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin for 30 min at room temperature. After blocking, the cells were incubated with corresponding antibodies overnight at 4 °C, and rhodamine-conjugated antibodies against rabbit IgG were used to detect primary antibodies. The immunostained cells were visualized using an LSM-780 META confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany) at 488 nm for GFP and 647 nm for rhodamine. Fluorescence intensity was assessed by examining at least five fields per section at 400 × magnification using MetaMorph digital image analysis.

## Quantitative RT-PCR analysis for USAG-1, BMP-7, HOXA13, TGF-β1 and collagen 1
Total RNA was isolated from kidneys or MDCK cells with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Life Technologies, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions, and then 2 μg total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, Bedford, MA, USA). The cDNA was then amplified in the ABI 7500 sequence detection system (Applied Biosystems, Bedford, MA, USA) using Power SYBR® Green PCR Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Bedford, MA, USA) with the following cycling conditions: 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s, 58 °C for 10 s, and 72 °C for 20 s. The sequences of primers were as follows: canine TGFβ-1 (forward: 5′-TGGACACGCAGTACAGCAA-3′, reverse: 5′-TAGTACACGATGGGCAGTGG-3′), canine collagen 1 (forward: 5′-GTGTGTACAGAACGGCCTCA-3′, reverse: 5′-TCGCAAATCACGTCATCG-3′), canine USAG-1 (forward: 5′-GGAGGCAGGCATTTCAGTAA-3′, reverse: 5′-TGTACTTGGTGGAACGCAGT-3′), canine BMP-7 (forward: 5′-CTGGTGTTCGACATCACAGC-3′, reverse: 5′-CATGAAGGGCTGCTTGTTCT-3′), canine HOXA13 (forward: 5′-GGGAGAAAGAAGCGCGTC-3′, reverse: 5′-CGTCGTGGCTGATATCCG-3′), and canine glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GADPH) (forward: 5′-TTCCACGGCACAGTCAAG-3′, reverse: 5′-ACTCAGCACCAGCATCAC-3′). Target gene expression was normalized to that of GADPH and quantitative analyses were conducted using the ΔΔcycle threshold method and StepOne Software version 2.2.2.

## Western blot analysis for fibrosis markers, USAG-1/BMP-7, and Smad signaling molecules
Total cell lysates were prepared by lysis of mouse kidney tissues or MDCK cells with radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and the protein contents were measured using the Bradford assay (Bio-Rad, 500–0006). Equivalent amounts of each protein extract were heat denatured in 5 × sample buffer (2% sodium dodecyl sulfate, 62.5 mM Tris, pH 6.8, 0.01% bromophenol blue, 1.43 mM mercaptoethanol, and 0.1% glycerol), separated on 12% polyacrylamide gels, and electrophoretically transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA). After blocking, membranes were treated with the appropriate antibodies, including E-cadherin (1:1000; BD, #610405, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA), fibronectin (1:5000; Santa Cruz, #sc-8422, Dallas, TX, USA), α-SMA (1:10,000; R&D, #MAB1420, Minneapolis, MN, USA), collagen 1 (1:1000; Santa Cruz, #sc-59772, Dallas, TX, USA), vimentin (1:5000, Abcam, #ab92547, Cambridge, UK), BMP-7 (1:1000, Abcam, #ab56023, Cambridge, UK), USAG-1 (1:1000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, #PA5-70641, Waltham, MA, USA), Smad3, pSmad3 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, #9520, Danvers, MA, USA), Smad1/5/9, pSmad1/5/9 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, #13820, Danvers, MA, USA), α-tubulin (1:10000, Abcam, #ab15246, Cambridge, UK), and actin (1:10000, Santa Cruz, #sc-8432, Dallas, TX, USA). Immunostaining was performed using chemiluminescent reagents (SuperSignal West Pico Luminol/Enhancer solution; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and Agfa medical X-ray film, and the band density was measured using NIH Image J software.

## Statistical analysis
Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical differences among the groups were determined using the Mann–Whitney U test. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows version 25.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Results of comparisons with P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.
