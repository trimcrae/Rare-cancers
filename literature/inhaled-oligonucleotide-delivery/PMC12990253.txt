## Abstract
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is associated with high morbidity and mortality, highlighting the urgent need for more effective treatments with fewer adverse effects. Small interfering RNA (siRNA) delivered via lipid nanoparticles (LNP) has shown both safety and therapeutic potential as a novel treatment strategy. Alox15 and TGF-β1 were both upregulated in the lung tissues of IPF patients and bleomycin-induced mice. LNP-delivered siRNAs demonstrated superior tissue distribution and efficacy in targeting molecules upregulated in IPF compared to naked siRNAs, and were used to treat bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice. Dual knockdown of Alox15 and TGF-β1 was achieved using LNP-delivered dual siRNAs, leading to significant improvements in lung function, reduced fibrosis severity, and decreased hydroxyproline content. Knockdown of either Alox15 or TGF-β1 alone also ameliorated pulmonary fibrosis, but the combined knockdown produced a more pronounced therapeutic effect. These results demonstrate that dual knockdown of Alox15 and TGF-β1 via LNP-delivered siRNAs effectively targets bleomycin-induced IPF, representing a promising therapeutic strategy for this disease.
Keywords: Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), Small interfering RNA (siRNA), Alox15, TGF-β1, Lipid nanoparticles (LNP)

## Introduction
Interstitial lung diseases (ILDs) comprise a diverse group of chronic lung conditions characterized by inflammation and fibrosis, with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) being the most common and notably severe. IPF, marked by increasing morbidity and mortality with age, presents a significant clinical challenge [ ]. Although existing antifibrotic therapies provide limited efficacy and are associated with significant costs, their lack of specificity often leads to severe systemic side effects [ , ]. The emergence of pulmonary fibrosis as a complication of COVID-19 further highlights the urgent need for improved therapies [ ]. Finding therapies that are more effective and have fewer side effects has become an urgent priority.
Ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, is increasingly recognized for its role in diseases like fibrosis [ , ]. In the context of IPF, Alox15 (15-lipoxygenase, 15lox) an enzyme involved in lipid metabolism and ferroptosis, contributes to oxidative damage and fibrotic progression by metabolizing arachidonic acid (AA) to generate 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), a compound that induces transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) expression [ ]. Elevated 4-HNE levels are associated with increased TGF-β1 mRNA and protein [ ], promoting fibrosis through epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). TGF-β plays a central role in fibrogenesis by driving fibroblast activation, excessive extracellular matrix deposition, and upregulating intracellular Fe2+5, all of which intersect with the ferroptotic pathway and potentially exacerbate pulmonary fibrosis. Targeting the specific contributions of Alox15 and TGF-β to ferroptosis in IPF enhances our understanding of disease mechanisms and may offer a therapeutic opportunity to halt fibrotic progression.
Small interfering RNA (siRNA) works by triggering specific post-transcriptional gene silencing. It promotes mRNA degradation, which leads to reduced expression of the corresponding proteins [ ]. However, siRNAs have a short half-life and are rapidly degraded by nucleases in the bloodstream. Their ability to reach target organs and cells is also limited. To address these challenges, lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) have been developed as carriers to enhance siRNA stability and improve cellular delivery [ , ]. In 2018, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved Onpattro™ (patisiran), the first LNP-encapsulated siRNA therapeutic [ ]. In recent years, as a safe way this technology has been applied to silencing key molecular players involved in other diseases including IPF [ , ] due to its high level of biocompatibility and safety. This innovative therapeutic approach holds potential for slowing fibrotic progression and improving outcomes in patients with IPF, offering new hope for those affected.
In this study, we evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of LNP-mediated silencing of Alox15 and TGF-β in the most widely used and best-studied animal model of IPF, the bleomycin model [ , ]. We discovered that the decrease in pulmonary fibrosis achieved through LNP-mediated RNAi targeting ferroptosis is attributed to enhanced RNAi uptake facilitated by LNPs, thereby obstructing ferroptosis and consequentially reducing the formation of ferroptosis metabolites like 4-HNE. Furthermore, our research explored the upregulation of Alox15 and TGF-β in IPF patients compared to controls, aiming to bridge the gap between preclinical models and clinical application. Through this comprehensive approach, we aim to advance the development of targeted RNAi-based therapies for IPF, addressing a critical unmet need in patient care.

## Material and methods
2.1.

## Reagents
The siRNAs were obtained from Integrated DNA Technologies (MI, USA). The DLin-MC3-DMA was obtained from Nanosoft Polymers (NC, USA). 1,2-dimyristoyl-rac-glycero-3-methoxypolyethylene glycol-2000 (DMG-PEG 2000) and 1,2-distearoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DSPC) were purchased from Avanti Polar Lipids (AL, USA). Insulin, transferrin, hydrocortisone, β-estradiol, l-glutamine, sodium selenite, HEPES, cholesterol, bleomycin, avertin, and pancuronium were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (MO, USA). Penicillin G, and Streptomycin were obtained from Corning Incorporated (VA, USA).

2.2.

## Preparation and characterization of lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)
LNPs composed of DLin-MC3-DMA, cholesterol, DMG-PEG2000, DSPC, and siRNA were synthesized by FormuRx Pharmaceuticals Co., Ltd. (Taipei, Taiwan) and prepared using microfluidic mixing. Briefly, siRNA was diluted in acetate buffer (pH 4.0), and lipid components were prepared in 95% ethanol at a molar ratio of 50:38.5:1.5:10. Using the NanoAssemblr Ignite instrument (Precision NanoSystems Inc., Vancouver, Canada) with a flow ratio of 1:3 (aqueous to organic) and a total flow rate of 12 mL/min, and the final N/P ratio was 3. Four groups of siRNA were encapsulated into LNPs (siRNA@LNPs) and ultimately formulated as follows: si-negative control (si-NC@LNP), si-Alox15 alone (si-Alox15@LNP), si-TGF-β1 alone (si-TGF-β1@LNP), and dual siRNA mixture of si-Alox15 and si-TGF-β1 encapsulated together (si-Alox15/si-TGF-β1@LNP). The siRNA@LNPs were then analyzed to determine siRNA encapsulation efficiency (EE%) and particle size. The siRNA encapsulation efficiency was assayed using a Quant-iT RiboGreen RNA assay kit (Thermo Fisher). Particle size, zeta potential, and Polydispersity Index(PDI) of the LNPs were measured by NaNo Partica SZ-100 nanoparticle analyzer (HORIBA, Ltd., Kyoto, Japan). The components of the LNPs and the characteristics of the siRNA@LNPs are described in . LNPs are stable for at least one month when stored at 4 °C. The stability analysis data are described in .

2.3.

## Animal models
Six-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were obtained from BioLASCO Taiwan Co., Ltd. (Taipei, Taiwan). All animal experiments were conducted under protocols approve by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Taipei Veterans General Hospital (Protocol IACUC number: 2022-087). The mice were housed under conditions of a 12-h light/dark cycle at 25 °C with humidity ranging from 40% to 60%. Allowed free access to water and laboratory chow. To induce pulmonary fibrosis, 2U/kg body weight of bleomycin diluted in 50 μL saline was given by intratracheal (i.t.) at day 0. The control group received the same volume of saline alone as the non-fibrotic mice. Mice were sacrificed on days 2, 4, 7, or 14 for further analyses. For administration of siRNA@LNPs, mice were randomly divided into five groups. The non-fibrotic control group received saline on day 0 and was subsequently treated with si-NC@LNP (0.125 mg/kg) on days 1, 4, and 7. The remaining groups received bleomycin on day 0, followed by treatment with four different siRNA@LNP formulations (i.t., diluted in 50 μL sterilized saline),including si- NC@LNP (0.125 mg/kg), si-Alox15@LNP (0.125 mg/kg), and si-TGF-β1@LNP (0.125 mg/kg), si-Alox15/si-TGF-β1@LNP (0.125 mg/kg each) on day 1, 4 and 7. The mice were sacrificed on day 14 for further analyses.

2.4.

## Measurement of lung function
To assess pulmonary functionality, lung function tests were performed before sacrifice. Mice were anaesthetized via intraperitoneal injection with avertin (240 mg/kg). Subsequently, pancuronium was administered intravenously (0.08 mg/kg) and a tracheotomy was performed to establish an airway access. Mechanical ventilation was facilitated using a computer-controlled piston ventilator (FlexiVent, SCIREQ, Montreal, Quebec, Canada). The tidal volume was set at 10 mL/kg at a frequency of 150 breaths per minute against 3 cm H2O positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP). The lungs were initially inflated by applying a pressure of 30 cm H2O for 3 s to reach total lung capacity, allowing the alveolar pressure to equilibrate. After conducting two total lung capacity maneuvers to standardize volume history, pressure and flow data (reflective of airway and tissue dynamics) were collected and used to calculate the airway resistance (Rrs), lung tissue elastance (Ers), static lung compliance (Cst) and inspiratory capacity (IC).

2.5.

## Hydroxyproline assay
Collagen levels in murine lung tissues were measured using the hydroxyproline colorimetric assay kit (ab222941, Abcam, United Kingdom) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, 10 mg of lung tissue was homogenized in 100 μL ddH2O, followed by mixing with 100 μL 10 N NaOH at 120 °C for 1 h, and subsequently neutralized with an equal amount of HCl. Afterward, the supernatant was dried in a 96-well plate and then mixed with reaction reagents. The absorbance was measured at 560 nm using a SpectraMax M5 multi-mode reader (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA, USA).

2.6.

## Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) analysis
Total RNA from animals or cells was extracted using the GENEzol reagent (Geneaid biotech, Taiwan) and then reverse transcribed using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using the EnTurbo™ SYBRGreen PCR SuperMix (ELK Biotechnology, China) on a StepOne™ Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Data were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with normalization to GAPDH by the manufacturer's software. All primers in this study were synthesized by Integrated DNA Technologies (Singapore). Primer sequences were listed in .

2.7.

## Hematoxylin-Eosin (H&E) and Picro Sirius Red staining
The lungs of mice were fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin (NBF) through tracheal instillation for 10 min and then immersed in NBF overnight. The lungs were dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and then sliced into 4-μm-thick tissue sections. Tissue sections were subjected to staining with H&E and Picro Sirius Red (Sigma-Aldrich). Images were captured with an Olympus Provis AX70 microscope equipped with a digital camera (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Collagen-rich areas appeared red in Picro Sirius Red stain, and quantification of the section images was performed using an image-analysis system. (Image-Pro Plus, Media Cybernetics, Silver Spring, MD).

2.8.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
The slides were hydrated using Xylene followed by a graded ethanol series. After immersion in citrate buffer and microwave treatment, sections were quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min and then blocked with 1% BSA for 10 min. Subsequently, sections were incubated with primary antibodies against Alox15 (ab80221, Abcam), 4-HNE (ab48506, Abcam), or TGF-β1 (IR112-457, iReal Biotechnology, Taiwan) overnight at 4 °C. The next day, tissues were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 30 min at room temperature, followed by development using DAB (K5007, Dako, Denmark) and staining with hematoxylin. Images were captured with an Olympus Provis AX70 microscope equipped with a digital camera. Subsequently, quantification of the section images was performed using Image-Pro Plus.

2.9.

## Western blot analysis
Tissue and cell lysates were prepared in a commercial lysis buffer (Corning) plus protease inhibitor cocktail. Bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (Pierce, Rockford, IL, USA) was used to determine protein concentration. Equal amounts of protein were loaded on 8-10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Non-specific binding was prevented by blocking with 5% milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature. The membranes were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Alox15 (ab244205, Abcam), 4-HNE (ab48506, Abcam), TGF-β1 (sc-130348, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA), E-cadherin (IR55-180, iReal Biotechnology), Vimentin (GTX100619, GeneTex, Taiwan), α-SMA (E-AB-34268, Elabscience, China), or GAPDH (2118, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA). Membranes were washed with TBST and then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature. The signals were detected with a chemiluminescent system (PerkinElmer Life and Analytical Sciences, Boston, MA, USA) and exposed to X-ray film (Amersham Pharmacia Biotech, Piscataway, NJ, USA). GAPDH was used as the internal control.

2.10.

## Cell cultures
Primary murine lung fibroblasts were isolated from the lungs of 4-week-old C57BL6/J mice lung and grown in DMEM medium (Corning) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Avantor, Inc., Radnor, PA, USA) containing 100 U/mL penicillin G and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Murine lung epithelial cells (MLE-12 cells) were obtained from ATCC (Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Corning) containing 4% FBS, 0.005 mg/mL insulin, 0.01 mg/mL transferrin, 30 nM sodium selenite, 10 nM hydrocortisone, 10 nM β-estradiol, 10 mM HEPES, 2 mM l-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin G, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. All cells were incubated in a 37 °C humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

2.11.

## Transfection of siRNA
MLE-12 cells (8 × 105 cells/well) and murine lung fibroblasts (1.5 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in 6-well plates before transfection. The siRNAs were mixed with TransIT-X2 ® (MIR 6000, Mirus Bio, Madison, WI, USA) diluted in Opti-MEM (Thermo Fisher) to a final concentration of 25 nM/well at room temperature for 20 min. The mixtures were then added to the cells and incubated for 24 h. The medium was replaced with serum-free medium containing 10 mU/mL bleomycin for additional 24 h. Cells were then collected for further analysis. The siRNA sequences were described in .

2.12.

## Cell viability assay
MLE-12 cells and murine lung fibroblasts were seeded at a density of 10,000 cells per well in 96-well plates overnight. The cells were then treated with various concentrations of LNPs and incubated for 24 h. All assays were performed by Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo Laboratories, Japan), and absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

2.13.

## Cellular uptake study
MLE-12 cells and murine lung fibroblasts were cultured in 6-well plates. The siRNA-Cy5 or siRNA-Cy5 encapsulated with LNPs (siRNA@LNP-Cy5) were diluted in 150 μL Opti-MEM. Cells were then treated with 50 nM siRNA-Cy5 or siRNA@LNP-Cy5 in serum free medium for 6 h. Cy5 signals were detected using flow cytometry (FACS-Calibur®, Becton-Dickinson, Immunofluorometry Systems, Mountain View, CA, USA). The mean fluorescent intensity (MFI) of Cy5 was analyzed using FlowJo software (Tree Star, Ashland, OR, USA). For live-cell fluorescence imaging, MLE-12 cells and murine lung fibroblasts were treated with 50 nM naked siRNA-Cy5 or siRNA@LNP-Cy5 for 6 h and then stained with Hoechst 33342. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (Axiovert 5, Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany).

2.14.

## Ex vivo imaging
For ex vivo imaging of LNPs uptake and delivery, saline (50 μL), naked siRNAs-Cy5 (0.25 mg/kg) or siRNA@LNP-Cy5 (0.25 mg/kg) were injected intratracheally into mice once after bleomycin treatment. 24 h after injection, the mice were sacrificed and important organs, including heart, lung, liver, spleen and kidney were collected. These organs were then immediately analyzed using the PhotonIMAGER Optima system (Biospace Lab, Paris, France).

2.15.

## Toxicological evaluation
Mice were treated with saline or siRNA encapsulated with LNPs (siRNA@LNP (i.t., 0.25 mg/kg) on days 1, 4, and 7. Body weight measurements were recorded on days 1, 4, 7, and 14 to monitor changes in health. The mice were then sacrificed on day 14, blood and organ specimens were harvested for further analysis. Complete blood count (CBC) analysis was performed using an automated hematology analyzer (Advia 2120, Bayer, MO, USA). Biochemical analyses of AST, ALT, BUN, CRE, and GLU were performed on serum using a chemistry analyzer (Hitachi 7080, Hitachi, Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Major organs (heart, lung, liver, spleen, and kidney) were collected for H&E staining.

2.16.

## RNA sequencing analysis
RNA sequencing data of IPF and normal human lungs were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, including GSE134692 (Normal group n = 26; IPF group n = 46) and GSE 150910 (Normal group n = 103; IPF group n = 103).

2.17.

## Statistical analysis
Values were reported as the mean ± standard deviation. Student's two-tailed unpaired t-test was used for comparing two groups, and one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post-hoc test was used for comparing multiple groups. Mann-whitney U test was used in RNA-sequencing analysis. P value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

## Results
3.1.

## RNA sequencing analysis uncovers elevated expression of ALOX15 and TGF-β1 genes in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis dataset
IPF is a complex disease with multiple mechanisms. In our quest to identify crucial markers of IPF, we employed RNA sequencing technology to analyze a dataset of IPF patients. Notably, we focused specifically on genes intersecting with the ferroptotic pathway, known to potentially exacerbate pulmonary fibrosis [ , ]. Our analysis unveiled a significant elevation in the expression levels of ALOX15 and TGF-β1 in IPF patients ( ), while other LOX family biomarkers displayed inconsistent changes ( ). These findings suggest a potential pivotal role of ALOX15 and TGF-β1 in the pathological processes underlying IPF.
 
3.2.

## IPF mice induced by bleomycin exhibit worsen lung function and pulmonary fibrosis
Based on the aforementioned findings, we conducted animal experiments to observe if similar gene expression changes occur in a bleomycin-induced IPF mouse model. Bleomycin was administered intratracheally on day 0, and animals were sacrificed for sampling and analysis on days 2, 4, 7, and 14. As the duration after administration increased, bleomycin-treated mice showed significant changes in lung function, including increased airway resistance and elasticity, as well as decreased lung compliance and inspiratory capacity compared to controls ( A). H&E staining demonstrated temporal changes in tissue morphology, with increasing areas and severity of honeycombing structures in bleomycin-treated mice, compared to controls. Collagen accumulation, a crucial indicator of lung fibrosis, was confirmed by Picro Sirius red staining and hydroxyproline analysis, showing an increase in total collagen content over time ( B–D). Together, these data suggest that bleomycin-induced IPF mice exhibit worsening lung function and pulmonary fibrosis.
 
3.3.

## IPF mice induced by bleomycin highly express Alox15 and TGF-β1
To further investigate the correlation between Alox15 and TGF-β1 with IPF, we performed biochemical analyses. The qPCR results indicated a significant increase in Alox15 expression during the early stages of fibrosis, while TGF-β1 expression significantly increased on day 7 ( E). Western blot analysis confirmed the mRNA data ( F). IHC staining revealed high expression levels of Alox15 and TGF-β1 at the sites of lung fibrosis. Furthermore, the downstream product of Alox15, 4-HNE, exhibited a concurrent increase with Alox15 expression ( G). Additionally, the downstream signaling of TGF-β1, including several markers of EMT implicated in inducing pulmonary fibrosis, displayed time-dependent increases (N-cadherin, Vimentin, and α-SMA) or decreases (E-cadherin) ( ). These findings collectively indicate a marked elevation in the expression of Alox15 and TGF-β1 during the progression of lung fibrosis.

3.4.

## Enhancing siRNA efficacy through LNP encapsulation
In pursuit of finding effective therapeutic approaches to inhibit Alox15 and TGF-β1, we utilized siRNAs to suppress mRNA expression. We designed siRNAs that potentially effectively inhibit Alox15 and TGF-β1 mRNA in both lung epithelial cells (MLE-12) and primary lung fibroblasts. Following transfection experiments, we identified the most potent siRNAs, namely si-Alox15-1 and si-TGF-β1-1 ( A and B). Upon selecting the effective sequences, we conjugated siRNAs with the fluorescent dye Cy5 and encapsulated them using lipid shells. Based on previous literature, Onpattro [ ], an approved siRNA LNP drug, employs a stable and effective lipid shell consisting of DLin-MC3-DMA, cholesterol, DMG-PEG2000, and DSPC in a ratio of 50:38.5:1.5:10. These components, along with siRNA, are therefore processed through microfluidics to produce siRNA@LNP.
 Cell experiments were conducted to evaluate the efficacy of the LNP carrier. Compared to naked siRNA, siRNA@LNP demonstrated significantly higher cellular uptake and fluorescence intensity ( C–E). Both naked siRNA and siRNA@LNP were administered via intratracheal injection into mice, and vital organs were collected 24 h later for ex vivo imaging and fluorescence intensity measurement. Results indicated that siRNA specifically targeted the lungs, and under the same dosage conditions, LNP encapsulation protected siRNA from degradation, effectively mitigating siRNA decay and prolonging its residence time in the target organs ( F). Together, these data suggest that this strategy holds promise for enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of siRNAs in treating pulmonary fibrosis.

3.5.

## Assessing in vivo LNP safety: biochemical indicators and vital organ health
To confirm the biocompatibility of LNPs, we introduced siRNA@LNP into MLE-12 cells and primary lung fibroblasts in multiple dosages, which did not impact cell growth ( ). In animal experiments, repeated intratracheal injections of siRNA@LNP compared to the saline group did not affect mouse weight gain ( A). There were no significant differences in blood cell counts, liver function, kidney function, or blood glucose levels ( B and C). H&E staining revealed no apparent signs of inflammation in vital organs (including heart, lungs, liver, spleen, and kidneys) of healthy mice after repeated administration ( D). These data indicate that LNP encapsulation has no significant impact on biochemical indicators and the health of vital organs, and it does not induce noticeable inflammatory responses or cytotoxicity.
 
3.6.

## LNP-mediated siRNA delivery alleviates pulmonary fibrosis in bleomycin-induced IPF model
Following the confirmation of drug stability, we evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of siRNA@LNPs in animal experiments. The experimental flow chart is shown in . Results indicated significant improvement in lung function post-siRNA@LNP treatment, with the dual siRNAs group showing the most significant improvements in lung resistance (Rrs), elasticity (Ers), compliance (Cst) and inspiratory capacity (IC). While single siRNA treatment targeting either Alox15 or TGF-β1 alone improved Rrs and Ers, they did not produce significant improvements in Cst or IC ( A). H&E staining of tissue slices revealed a reduction in fibrotic tissue formation in the treatment groups, accompanied by significant decreases in Picro Sirius red staining and hydroxyproline content ( B–D). While either Alox15 or TGF-β1 knockdown alone partially improved fibrosis, dual siRNA treatment showed superior efficacy, yielding the largest reductions in Picro Sirius Red–positive areas and hydroxyproline levels, clearly demonstrating the therapeutic advantage of combined knockdown. ( C–D). Western blot analysis revealed changes in EMT-related markers, including N-cadherin and vimentin, following siRNA treatment. Notably, dual siRNA treatment restored these markers to levels approaching those of the control group ( ). The qPCR measurements of Alox15 and TGF-β1 revealed effective inhibition of their expression ( E). Western blot confirmed the effective suppression of specific protein expression by siRNAs, with downstream 4-HNE decreasing alongside Alox15. The dual siRNAs group exhibited the most significant effects across all biomarkers ( F). IHC analysis demonstrated significant reductions in the tissue distribution of Alox15, 4-HNE, and TGF-β1 in the treatment groups compared to the control group ( G). Notably, dual siRNA treatment produced the most pronounced decrease in these markers, demonstrating that simultaneous inhibition of Alox15 and TGF-β1 is more effective and can be achieved through co-encapsulation within LNPs. These findings suggest that the inhibition of Alox15 and TGF-β1 expression through LNP-mediated siRNA delivery effectively alleviates pulmonary fibrosis and reduces the progression of IPF. This study provides new molecular targets and treatment strategies for IPF, with potential clinical applications.
 

4.

## Discussion
In this study, we present a novel therapeutic approach using two types of siRNA for inhibition and LNPs as carriers. This method enhances delivery efficiency and stability, providing an effective solution for IPF. Previous research has confirmed the feasibility of using RNAi to treat IPF. Recent studies on RNAi-based therapies for IPF have mainly focused on targeting single pathogenic mechanisms, such as the TGF-β signaling pathway or specific genes related to fibrosis [ , , ]. However, pulmonary fibrosis involves numerous inflammatory cytokines and drives complex mechanisms. Despite showing some success, targeting a single mechanism often fails to address the multifactorial nature of IPF comprehensively. Therefore, using LNPs to deliver multiple RNAi agents effectively may provide a more comprehensive solution. Our method offers a comprehensive treatment strategy. By targeting both ferroptosis and the TGF-β signaling pathway, we aim to disrupt the fibrosis pathways comprehensively. In our study, we found that this drug effectively inhibited two target genes in animal models. It was delivered efficiently to the lungs, extending the drug's residence time in the lungs. This achieved significant therapeutic effects, improving lung function deterioration and preventing tissue morphological changes. This comprehensive treatment approach may be more effective than single-pathway methods, offering new insights and potential therapeutic avenues for IPF.
Unlike previous studies, our research uses LNPs to simultaneously inhibit ferroptosis and the TGF-β signaling pathway. This strategy offers greater advantages compared to existing methods. We aim to address the complex interactions between oxidative stress and fibrogenesis, which are central to IPF pathophysiology. The dual knockdown strategy not only helps reduce oxidative stress responses in disease progression but also inhibits the activation of key fibrogenic signaling pathways. This approach inhibits the pathological process of IPF in multiple aspects. Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death characterized by lipid peroxidation, drives fibrosis in various organs, including the lungs. Inhibiting ferroptosis has shown promise in mitigating fibrotic responses and preserving lung function in preclinical IPF models [ ]. Similarly, dysregulated TGF-β signaling promotes fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition, contributing to pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. We analyzed the RNA sequence database from IPF patient samples and RNA and protein samples from animal models. This analysis revealed significant increases in both ALOX15 and TGF-β1 RNA and protein levels. These findings demonstrate a strong correlation between ALOX15, TGF-β1, and IPF. After successfully inhibiting these targets with the drug, the animal models showed excellent therapeutic effects. Improvements were noted in EMT proteins highly associated with IPF progression. These results underscore the importance of ferroptosis and TGF-β signaling pathways in IPF, highlighting the potential benefits of this combination therapy. Our study emphasizes the significance of this comprehensive treatment strategy and provides new insights into IPF therapy.
LNPs have become the focal point of current nucleic acid delivery research. LNPs not only protect mRNA from degradation but also prolong its release time. After extensive research, the technology of LNPs has matured, demonstrating good biological safety, making them an ideal clinical treatment method. LNPs suitable for drug delivery can be designed with different outer membrane compositions and proportions, particle sizes, or surface chemical compositions. These design changes can alter their pH or charge, addressing various challenges within the biological environment [ ]. Due to the success of COVID-19 vaccines, research on LNPs in medicine has rapidly advanced [ ]. Furthermore, we note that the LNPs used in our treatment approach have also been applied in the development of mRNA vaccines. Besides COVID-19, many candidate mRNA-loaded nanoparticle vaccines for preventing or treating various diseases have entered clinical trials. However, these LNP-based drugs may cause side effects, such as immunogenicity or toxicity reactions. Dosage and frequency also impact drug safety. These issues may arise in our therapeutic application as well. Therefore, potential safety issues must be carefully considered, and further experiments and clinical trials are necessary to determine the optimal treatment approach. Addressing LNP-based drug therapy for lung diseases, current research still lacks in vivo efficiency and targeting capability, particularly in delivering drugs to organs other than the liver. Effectively delivering drugs to the lungs remains a significant challenge [ ]. During blood transmission, drugs are easily metabolized by the body. Thus, scientists have attempted to deliver drugs via aerosolization. However, the aerosolization process can disrupt the nanoparticle structure, reducing efficiency [ ]. Moreover, lung-specific barriers, such as mucus layers and chemical barriers, hinder drugs from efficiently reaching target cells [ ]. In our animal models, LNP-encapsulated siRNA was delivered efficiently to the lungs via intratracheal injection without inducing inflammatory responses or cytotoxicity, as demonstrated by complete blood count analysis, serum chemistry analysis, and histological analysis. Further investigation is required to apply our findings clinically.
In conclusion, the results of this study demonstrate that inhibiting the expression of Alox15 and TGF-β1 by siRNA can effectively alleviate pulmonary fibrosis and improve the progression of IPF. This finding provides new molecular targets and treatment strategies for IPF therapy, with potential clinical applications. However, further research is still needed to determine the optimal treatment regimen and elucidate the treatment mechanism for better clinical application. Additionally, extending our discussion to include relevant literature on RNAi-based therapies for IPF and the challenges associated with LNPs in COVID-19 vaccine development enhances the contextual understanding and implications of our findings.
