## Highlights
•This review comprehensively summarized the research status of CSBV treatment.
•Outlined the current problems and deficiencies of control means against CSBV infection.
•Proposed some promising strategies for the enhancement and synergist of EYA efficacy.
•EYA as an effective agent could terminate CSBV infection in near future in apiculture.
•EYA offer scientific guidance and reference for the control of other bee viral diseases.
Keywords: CSBV, Apis cerana, Egg yolk antibodies, Prevention and treatment, Synergism

## Abstract
Chinese sacbrood virus (CSBV) is the most severe pathogen of Apis cerana, which leads to serious fatal diseases in bee colonies and eventual catastrophe for the Chinese beekeeping industry. Additionally, CSBV can potentially infect Apis mellifera by bridging the species barrier and significantly affect the productivity of the honey industry. Although several approaches, such as feeding royal jelly, traditional Chinese medicine, and double-stranded RNA treatments, have been employed to suppress CSBV infection, their practical applicabilities are constrained due to their poor effectiveness. In recent years, specific egg yolk antibodies (EYA) have been increasingly utilized in passive immunotherapy for infectious diseases without any side effects. According to both laboratory research and practical use, EYA have demonstrated superior protection for bees against CSBV infection. This review provided an in-depth analysis of the issues and drawbacks in this field in addition to provide a thorough summary of current advancements in CSBV studies. Some promising strategies for the synergistic study of EYA against CSBV, including the exploitation of novel antibody drugs, novel TCM monomer/formula determination, and development of nucleotide drugs, are also proposed in this review. Furthermore, the prospects for the future perspectives of EYA research and applications are presented. Collectively, EYA would terminate CSBV infection soon, as well as will provide scientific guidance and references to control and manage other viral infections in apiculture.

1.

## Introduction
Currently, China is the largest exporter of honey and royal jelly in the world, producing around 500,000 tons of honey per year ( ). As the most important pollinators of crops, honey bees are essential for the economy, sustainable agriculture and food security ( ). At the same time, honey bees are infected by various pathogens in actual breeding, such as Sacbrood virus (SBV) or Chinese Sacbrood virus (CSBV), deformed wing virus (DWV), acute bee paralysis virus (ABPV), and Kashmir bee virus (KBV). The most severe pathogen among them is CSBV, a small single positive-strand RNA virus with a diameter of 26 to 30 nm that affects Apis cerana (A. cerana), which can cause deadly infections in the entire bee colony and ultimately result in the death of all bees ( ; ). In recent years, Chinese Sacbrood disease (CSBD) has attracted widespread concern among beekeepers and researchers. The honeybee larvae that were infected could not pupate and shed their leathery endocuticle because 1-3 days old honey bee larvae are most susceptible to CSBV infection. As a result, ecdysial fluid with CSBV particles accumulated under their unshed skin. In the meantime, the color of infected larvae will change from white to pale yellow. Finally, they create a sac-like appearance which is the feature of this disease ( ). Despite showing no obvious physical symptoms, adult bees infected with CSBV have a shorter lifespan ( ). Therefore, there is an urgent need to investigate and explore a highly effective approach to CSBV infection control.
To date, a few approaches have been attempted to prevent and treat CSBV, such as the use of disinfectant, feeding royal jelly, RNA interference (RNAi), traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and egg yolk antibodies (EYA) antibodies. Although silver ion solution has greater anti-bacterial properties, however, viral infection in A. cerana cannot be cured by it ( ). Many honeybee products, including pollen, propolis, royal jelly, beeswax, and bee venom, have demonstrated potent antiviral effects at the early stages of viral cycle but have failed to show satisfactory effects following virus infection ( ). RNAi and double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) technologies have antiviral properties, but they are still in the early stages of laboratory research and unsuitable for clinical practice. TCM has a long history of being important in the treatment of various viral diseases ( ). Their anti-virus mechanism mainly improves resistance and tolerance to the virus through immune system without inactivating the virus directly. Nevertheless, TCM has drawbacks such as a long treatment cycle, unclear or uncertain mechanism as well as slow and subtle effects in the actual application ( ). Meanwhile, due to the strong TCM odor, A. cerana often refuses to feed on TCM or avoid them which makes practical breeding challenging. It is encouraging to note that specific EYA antibodies are being utilized more frequently in passive immunotherapy for infectious diseases, especially for viral diseases ( ; ). As an example, EYA have been used to treat CSBV infection, which show remarkable therapeutic and preventative effects against CSBV with approximately 97% protection rate ( ). Therefore, EYA offer a promising prospect for reducing CSBV infection in the near future.
In the past decade, some effective approaches have been established to detect CSBV, such as PCR or TaqMan PCR, loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), plaque reduction neutralization test, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The sensitivity and specificity of these methods are significantly improved, which offers the opportunity to detect CSBV infection early. Early detection makes it easier to control the CSBV infection quickly, which leads to optimal therapeutic results. At the same time, significant advancements and breakthroughs have been achieved in the research of CSBV. This study not only provides a thorough summary of current developments in CSBV but also provides a thorough analysis of issues and gaps in the area, giving researchers and beekeepers detailed background knowledge.

2.

## CSBV pathogen
2.1.

## Epidemiology of CSBV
Epidemiology research on CSBV was expanded when the sacbrood disease spread globally, paying particular attention to the transmission methods, pathophysiology, and the interaction between CSBV and host ( ). The CSBV can transmit through horizontal transmission, vertical transmission, and cross-reaction ( ). The horizontal spread among the members of the same generation is a significant way that can be divided into direct or indirect ways. In the former, the virus can spread from host to host directly, through the consumption of pollen, brood food, and honey, whereas the latter transmits the virus from host to host by an indirect route through the use of biological or physical vectors, such as infected host ( ), mites ( ), and contaminated beehives. Next, the bee offspring can be vertically infected with CSBV from mother via the eggs ( ). For instance, when the queens are positive for five viruses, which are black queen cell virus (BQCV), DWV, chronic bee paralysis virus (CBPV), KBV, and SBV, these viruses are also found in the offspring. Similarly, when the queens tested positive for BQCV and DWV, their offspring were also detected with only these two viruses ( ; ). The fertilized eggs, drone progeny and the worker, will be infected by CSBV through vertical transmission if the queens are infected ( ). However, not all progenies can be infected by infected queens that can produce about 30% virus-free progeny across several progeny classes. It implies that the efficiency of vertical transmission among infected queens is varied. Hence, controlling vertical transmission is a crucial strategy to prevent the spread of CSBV.
BQCV, DWV, Israeli acute paralysis virus (IAPV) and SBV viruses have been found in previous evolutionary studies to be disseminated by the cross-regional ( ; ; ). Moreover, in the co-cultured apiaries, the host-to-host transmission of viral infection, for example, AcSBV in A. cerana and A. mellifera, may play a significant role in viral disease transmission ( ). Hence, the understanding of cross-infection is important to monitor and control SBV infection in co-cultured apiaries ( ). The temporal fluctuation of virus predominance is synced to each other, causing the cross-infection of various species to occur periodically. A. mellifera may act as the AcSBV reservoir for SBV because of its low prevalence (0.63%) and pathogenicity as well as rarely caused serious disease or colony damage after infection. Contrarily, A. cerana infected with CSBV usually causes high mortality in the bee colonies. The susceptibility levels of A. cerana and A. mellifera to SBV are helpful in guiding beekeeping practices ( ). Apart from that, the virus prevalence is influenced by different seasons ( ). In comparison to autumn (26%/3%), the frequency of sacbrood in adults and pupae is higher in the spring (44%/13%) and summer (64%/18%) ( ). The seasonal variation could be caused by differences in bee vulnerability to the virus or environmental changes, including the pollen consumed by larvae. Therefore, early reinforcement of pathogen prevention and management in different seasons will be useful in controlling the seasonal spread of CSBV.

2.2.

## Subtypes of CSBV
The first complete genome sequencing of two strains of SBV (GenBank numbers: MG545286.1 and MG545287.1) was published in 2019. Both SBV strains exhibit the typical genome features of the Iflaviridae family ( ), in which the SBV strain that infects A. cerana is named CSBV ( ). CSBV genomic RNA contains 8863 nucleotides and a single large open reading frame (ORF), which encodes a 319.614 kDa polyprotein ( ; ). Domain analysis of CSBV polyprotein shows the order of the viral proteins to be identical to that of mammalian picornaviruses, with the four major structural proteins (VP1-VP4) in the N-terminal portion, and the nonstructural proteins (such as RNA helicase, chymotrypsin-like 3C protease, and RNA-dependent RNA polymerase) in the C-terminal portion of the polyprotein ( ). The structural proteins show a high variation in the amino acid sequences among different CSBV strains as well as exhibit excellent immunogenicity. Although they play an important role in the viral replication cycle and pathogenicity, their function, and the specific host cell interaction partner proteins are unclear ( ; ). The genome sequence of CSBV-GD-2002 is the first to be sequenced. There are currently 97 CSBV subtypes in total, with 40 subtypes found in A.cerana and 57 subtypes in A. mellifera, respectively. Each CSBV/SBV genome characteristic, GenBank number, strain number, and name, as well as a country source have been summarized in . In the aspects of physiological and biochemical characteristics, CSBV and AmSBV-UK-2000 are comparable, however their antigenicities are different. CSBV is highly homologous to AmSBV-UK-2000 with the highest divergence (19.3%) occurring between 2181 and 2221 base pairs. Under conditions of natural selection and immune pressure, it is possible for CSBV/SBV strains to be transferred between hosts, proving that these viruses have evolved independently ( ). For instance, AmCSBV-SDLY-2016 has the potential infection and cause the death of A. mellifera larvae by crossing the species barrier.

## Detection means of CSBV
3.1.

## Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)
Due to its high specificity and sensitivity, PCR is employed to detect the SBV infection, especially for low viral RNA copy numbers. PCR-based assays, including reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR), real-time PCR, ultra-rapid real-time PCR (URRT-PCR), and multiple RT-PCR, have been rapidly evolving over the past 20 years for the diagnosis of bee viral diseases ( ). The detail of the reaction conditions of each method is summarized in . They have replaced other techniques, such as ELISA, western blot, and agar gel immunodiffusion, because of their simplicity and rapidity. For example, RT-PCR method has identified seven honeybee viruses, which are CSBV, DWV, IAPV, BQCV, CBPV, ABPV, and KBV, from all bee samples. The prevalence rates were 90%, 81.25%, and 26.25% for CSBV, DWV, and IAPV, respectively ( ). Using the same method, it was discovered that about 80% of Thai apiaries in Northern Thailand were infected with DWV, ABPV, SBV, and KBV ( ). Real-time RT PCR is established for the simultaneous detection and quantitation of bee viruses. It has the advantages of shortening the detection time, cost-effective, rapid, reliable, and reproducible with high sensitivity and specificity ( ). Multiplex RT-PCR (mRT-PCR) can simultaneously detect seven diseases in contrast to real-time RT-PCR ( ). The results demonstrated that mRT-PCR test could generate good specificity, and sensitive and reliable results. It also saves time and cost by concurrently detecting seven viral agents. Thus, the multiplex PCR approach may have numerous applications in the identification of bee viruses in the future. The detection effectiveness of real-time PCR and URRT-PCR is impacted by different incubation times of cDNA in an effort to shorten the detection time. URRT-PCR is able to detect SBV within 22 min, including a 5 min RT process. It is concluded that URRT-PCR is one of the fastest SBV detection approaches and may be useful in detecting SBV and other viruses rapidly and accurately ( ).
 
3.2.

## TaqMan PCR
Due to the existence of SBV variants and strains in A. cerana, it is difficult to distinguish them from other viruses due to the same pathogenesis and genomes. This makes the virus identification process challenging and difficult. Additionally, the ignorance of AcSBV variation in detection approaches is an unwise step. The genome sequences suggest that conventional qPCR techniques might not be able to detect newly discovered A. cerana SBV variants (AcSBV). Accordingly, a TaqMan minor groove binder (MGB) probe fluorescence quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) assay was developed to identify SBV variants and strains. As compared to unmodified DNA, MGB probes have a higher melting temperature (Tm) and greater specificity and sensitivity, particularly when single-base mismatches occur in the MGB region. Simultaneously, shorter probes can be employed for hybridization-based tests due to the production of stable duplexes formed between DNA probes and MGB groups with single-stranded DNA targets ( ). 37 clinical samples are tested for the presence of CSBV using TaqMan PCR and the efficiency of this method is compared to clinical diagnosis, electron microscopy analysis, and conventional RT-PCR ( ). This is done using conserved regions of VP1 to design or construct an MGB probe. The results confirmed that TaqMan MGB-based probe fluorescence qRT-PCR is more sensitive in the detection of CSBV ( ), which may be helpful for CSBV testing, epidemiological investigation, and establishment of animal models ( ).

3.3.

## LAMP
Generally, controlling the illness progression and preventing viral dissemination require early diagnosis of CSBV. The RT-PCR method, which is used by most countries, is expensive, requires highly trained analysts, time-consuming (4–8 h), and has financial implications. Therefore, these restrictions can make it difficult to use such diagnostic tests effectively. The discovery of LAMP has provided a reliable substitute platform with improved sensitivity and specificity, rapid detection, no requirement for expensive reagents or instruments, and cost savings ( ). Due to the use of 2-3 pairs of primers (internal, external, and loop), these primers can detect up to 8 distinct sites on target DNA or RNA sequences. Thus, the sensitivity of LAMP for CSBV detection is equivalent to RT-PCR (1pg). The DNA denaturation stage, which produces more viral copies (109) in less time (< 1h), is eliminated by LAMP because the Bst DNA polymerase in LAMP exhibits strong strand displacement activity. At the same time, compared to other PCR techniques, its products can be detected much faster and sometimes only require analysis with the naked eye ( ). Aside from that, LAMP can be carried out at a constant and stable temperature, like a heater or incubator. The conventional RT-PCR and LAMP were compared in 31 clinical CSBV samples (as shown in ), and both assays showed the positive amplification. However, the former was quick, affordable, and simple ( ). Meanwhile, LAMP may be able to distinguish between cDNA samples from other viruses, indicating that this assay is as precise and accurate as RT-PCR for the identification of CSBV. Because it is amenable to resource-limited situations or devoid of expertise and infrastructure, especially in the low-and middle-income countries ( ), LAMP might be seen as a great option for the virus diagnosis ( ). LAMP has so far been used to diagnose conditions like CSBV, severe acute respiratory syndrome, MERS, and ZIKV accurately by detecting low copy number nucleic acids ( ; ).

3.4.

## ELISA
One of the most widely used clinical diagnostic tools for the detection and quantification of protein biomarkers is ELISA. Although various ELISA methods have been established to detect CSBV antigens, there are currently no commercially available ELISA kits ( ). Anti-CSBV biological agents could be developed to combat CSBV by using the CSBV VP2 protein as the antigen to synthesize monoclonal antibodies (mAb) ( ). Sandwich ELISA results showed that mAb has a high specificity for CSBV and does not cross-react with other bee viruses. This technique allows beekeepers to detect CSBD quickly as an early warning and monitor it in real-time ( ). Although sandwich ELISA method and RT-PCR have a coincidence rate of 90%, the detection limit of sandwich ELISA is 3.675 × 104 copies/μl. Hence, it is quite promising to enhance the sensitivity of ELISA further. To detect CSBV, for instance, antigen-capture ELISA (AC-ELISA) could be developed with 100% specificity and sensitivity ( ). The colloidal gold-based immunochromatographic (ICG) test strip should be developed during field epidemiological studies for on-site detection of CSBV infection. Without specialized equipment, this assay can be completed within 15 min, which offers the advantages of being ready to use, low-cost, fast, and time-saving. The serological ELISA assay gives quantitative detection and is now thought to be the best alternative to the ICG strip, which can only provide qualitative data with great sensitivity and detection capability. For instance, polyclonal antibodies (pAbs) and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were synthesized using recombinant VP1 and VP2 proteins of AcSBV to immunize mice. Western blot demonstrated that the reaction of pAb-rVP1/VP2 with homologous rVP1/VP2 was strong and vice-versa for heterologous rVP2/VP1. Subsequently, these antibodies are used to develop a rapid IC strip assay kit with colloidal gold coupled to pAb-rVP1 and pAb-rVP2 at the conjugate pad and the test line comprised of mAb-rVP1-1 and mAb-rVP2-13. Finally, pAb-rVP1/mAb-VP1-1 is chosen for validation since it demonstrates positive reactivity at a concentration as low as 1.38 × 103 copies. In view of this, this method provides results rapidly and not requires expertise, and is appropriate for field testing in actual bee breeding ( ).

4.

## Control of CSBV infection
4.1.

## Physical methods
Physicochemical approaches are well-known methods to manage viral diseases. In the field of apiculture, physical methods are frequently used, for example, routine apiary cleaning, hives replacement, feeding bees sugar syrup, and exposing hives to sunlight ( ) ( A). Although routine apiary cleaning methods and hives replacement can slow down the spread of disease, they have no substantial effect on the control of CSBV infection. Next, feeding bees sugar syrup containing herbal medicines does not directly inactivate the virus, but it does improve resistance to the CSBV via immune regulation ( ). Once CSBD breaks out, this approach has a subpar cure outcome for serious infection colonies. Again, prolonged exposure to sunlight can inactivate some viruses, but it cannot eradicate them completely. Other risk-free procedures, such as burning and burying, have also been documented for bees with severe infection. Burning and burying do not have a therapeutic effect on CSBV infection despite the fact that they can stop the transmission of CSBV. As a result of the drawbacks outlined above, the practical application of physical methods is severely constrained.
 
4.2.

## Chemical methods
Chemical disinfection, such as silver ion, lime cream, bleach powder, and other disinfectants, is the most widely used method in the disinfection of apiary ( B). The effectiveness of silver ion against natural Korea SBV (KSBV) infection in A. cerana was evaluated with respect to the powerful effect in killing bacteria, fungi, and viruses. In the Bosung-gun (Korea) apiary, 5 colonies were treated with sugar syrup that consisted of silver ions. The results strongly suggested that silver ions were good at preventing KSBV infection in A. cerana because the test group could survive longer (40 days) than the control group (21 days). However, the introduction of silver ions had the potential to contaminate honey and cause adverse reactions ( ; ). We should thus strengthen further study into the safe levels of silver residual in the bee products as well as the underlying antiviral mechanism. In addition, bee farms can be thoroughly disinfected with 10%-20% lime cream or 5% bleach powder, especially in the early spring and autumn each year. Despite having a mild disinfection effect, they have a number of drawbacks due to their pungent odor and chemical contact, which can affect the activity of bee colonies and ultimately reduce colony survival in beekeeping operations. Hence, future research should examine the green and environmental-friendly chemical disinfection approach.

4.3.

## Medicine applications
4.3.1.

## Traditional Chinese medicine
Numerous investigations demonstrated that viral illnesses treated with traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), such as influenza virus ( ), SARS-CoV ( ), and CSBV ( ), have satisfactory clinical effects ( C). Radix isatidis (R. isatidis) is the only TCM treatment for CSBV infection that has been described. After the treatment of CSBV-infected 3rd instar larvae with R. isatidis extract, the overall survival rate during an observation period from the 3rd day after CSBV inoculation to the 3rd day after R. isatidis extract treatment was 93.05%, which was higher than that of CSBV-infected larvae without R. isatidis extract, which was 43.05%. It was evident that R. isatidis extract increased the lifespan of CSBV-infected larvae ( ). The main cause was the adenosine and L-proline components from R. isatidis extract that could stop the replication of CSBV in A. cerana larvae and enhance the immune system ( ). Other TCM ingredients, including Cyrtomium Rhizome, Honeysuckle and Atractylodes, Licorice, have also been reported in Chinese literatures. These ingredients could offer a cure rate of more than 90%, which encourages further research into them to control CSBV infection ( ; ). However, TCM treatment for CSBV is a time-consuming, labor-intensive, slow action, and long course. Additionally, A. cerana frequently refuses to feed on TCM due to the strong odor, and this may destroy the whole bee colony ( ). Therefore, additional research is needed to consider a TCM formula or monomer that is more effective at controlling CSBV.

4.3.2.

## RNA interference
RNA interference (RNAi) is triggered by different dsRNA precursors that are converted into short-interfering RNA (siRNA) duplexes ( D). siRNA has the ability to stop viral reproduction by directing the recognition, cleavage, or translational repression of complementary single-stranded RNAs, such as mRNAs or viral genomic RNAs ( ). To date, RNAi has been suggested as an ideal approach to protect A. cerana from CSBV infection ( ). When second instar larvae were fed CSBV dsRNA, after 96 h, the mortality rates for larvae treated with dsSBV1 or dsVP1 + CSBV were 46.7% and 50%, respectively, but the morality rates for larvae fed with CSBV just or dsGFP + CSBV were 93.3% and 90%, respectively. The outcome was dsGFP, an unrelated dsRNA, did not protect larvae from CSBV infection, whereas two dsRNAs generated from CSBV (dsSBV1 and dsVP1) did. Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) must be produced and purified on a large scale, like the production of recombinant CSBV VP1 gene with genetic Escherichia coli ( ) and Bacillus thuringiensis ( ) to combat the infection effectively.
Alternatively, selecting a targeted CSBV gene is a critical step for RNAi to work. The interference effect of the CSBV RNA⁃dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) gene was more pronounced than that of structural gene VP1 because VP1 is not expressed at every stage, while RdRp gene is stably expressed during viral replication. Larval mortality was reduced by 60%, and the RdRp and VP1 genes were down-regulated by 85% and 78% respectively when 2 μg dsRdRp was fed to 2d-larvae for 3 days. It can be seen that the RdRp gene can serve as an RNAi target for the suppression of CSBV, which greatly broaden our understanding of virus-host interactions ( ). Although RNAi-based treatments have made significant progress, there are still a number of crucial issues that need to be solved in order to manage CSBV effectively. Firstly, numerous viral infections in bee colonies are a common occurrence ( ), which means that beekeeping practices might not always produce the expected results. Even if the purified virus came from bee samples, it still contains several types of viruses ( ) in field isolates such as CSBV, DWV, BQCV, ABPV, and KBV. Therefore, more research on the siRNA response to multiple viral infections is needed ( ). Secondly, in large-scale RNAi screening approaches, miRNA-like off-target effects are problematic, and many findings are false positive. In order to avoid false positive outcomes, indel screening must take this into account ( ). Thirdly, since dsRNA stability in different hosts may obviously vary, a detailed analysis of hemolymph components could be an essential strategy to design an effective RNAi ( ). Fourthly, the efficient uptake and optimum quantity of dsRNA should be carefully considered in the systematic application of RNAi ( ). In some cases, dsRNA uptake can occasionally be ineffective in the specific site of the host, so the role or interactions of siRNA from other pathogens with the host RNAi machinery should be further investigated. Finally, since the injection route is not a practical way in the bee culture, future studies should focus on developing approaches to enable large-scale production, efficient uptake in target organs, and persistence of dsRNA in vivo after oral application ( ).

4.3.3.

## Antibodies agents
Generally, antibody drugs or medications have been widely used to treat many diseases, such as cancer ( ), autoimmune diseases ( ), viral diseases ( ), and other illnesses. EYA, also named Immunoglobulin yolk (IgY), originates from oviparous animals and accumulates in the egg yolk, which is the evolutionary precursor of IgG presented only in mammals ( ). Due to the differences between IgY and IgG, such as their heavy chain sizes and antigenic differences, the term “IgY” is currently replacing “IgG” in the field of poultry ( ). IgY molecule has similar light chains to that of IgG but has a major difference in their heavy chains, i.e., IgG has three constant regions (CH1-CH3) while IgY has four constant regions (CH1-CH4) ( ). Due to structural differences and phylogenetic distance, IgY is more suitable for diagnostic purposes than mammalian antibodies since it does not react with specific components of human-conserved proteins ( ). Moreover, owing to the advantages of high yield and stability, cost-effectiveness, and noninvasiveness of IgY applications, IgY has been widely applied in passive immunization, especially in young animals in which adaptive immunity is not fully developed. Based on these advantages, EYA have been exploited in various aspects for its applications in research ( ), diagnosis ( ) and therapy ( ) ( E). Due to structural and evolutionary differences, EYA does not react with certain immune system components and displays greater avidity for mammalian conserved proteins ( ). In passive immunotherapy, EYA antibodies have been used for infectious diseases with the features of being safe, inexpensive, and without any known side effects, like anaphylactic reaction, liver function damage, gastrointestinal symptoms, and so on. Anti-CSBV antibody offers a 95-100% cure rate for CSBV infection with 25 antibody titers ( ). The prepared EYA offered 98.9-100% protection against CSBV infection after more than two years of use, and now it is being employed as a functional feed additive in Chinese apiculture to suppress CSBV outbreaks ( ).
In fact, many factors affect the therapeutic efficacy of EYA, for instance, antibody titer, extraction method, and administration route ( ; ). Consideration should be given to a number of loops, including the immunization interval, vaccination program, and adjuvant type, in order to achieve a high titer of EYA. The titer of EYA in eggs began increasing on day 14 after the first immunization, reaching a peak on day 42, and stayed high until the 140th day ( ). The level of EYA in graphene oxide-chitosan group was the highest in comparison with traditional adjuvants, such as Freund's adjuvant and white oil, whereas the traditional groups were relatively lower. To obtain high titer EYA, an appropriate extraction method is necessary. This includes the production equipment, extraction scale, cost, and the environmental impacts. Various extraction methods have been summarized by De Meulenaer and Huyghebaert, including water dilution, ammonium or sodium sulphate precipitation, polyethylene glycol precipitation ( ), dextran sulfate precipitation ( ), precooled propane and acetone ( ), and water dilution ultrafiltration ( ). In terms of EYA yield, purity and activity, water extraction is superior to other methods. While the PEG-precipitation method can be used to extract EYA with a stable titer and high specificity at a very low cost. Following PEG-precipitation, the produced EYA worked very well in many immunological assays ( ). Therefore, the most suitable extraction method should be chosen according to the actual circumstances of EYA production. Furthermore, administration routes of EYA play a decisive role in practical use. EYA are currently mostly administered through oral and are applied in apiculture. Despite orally administered antibodies being susceptible to denaturation by gastric acid in stomach and degradation by proteases, there is no detectable inactivation of EYA in aqueous solutions with high sugar concentrations. Because the digestibility and absorption of honey bees are different from animals, the high sugar contents in honey sacs and basic larval diet may enhance the stability of the EYA ( ). Nevertheless, EYA production is still plagued by a number of issues, like the potential risk of egg pathogens, high cost, instability, and lack of standardized procedures. Thus, a lot of effort must be made to make the EYA an effective medicine to prevent and treat CSBV infection.

4.3.4.

## Others
According to practical experiences, CSBV infection can still be controlled in a variety of ways ( F). For example, after a long period of ‘deseeding therapy’, the virulence and infective ability of CSBV are greatly reduced, increasing the larval immunity. The apiary location and its scientific management are also important for CSBV control. Honey bees should be kept as far away from pollution sources as possible, such as chemical plants, animal breeding farms, and other places with severe pollution, because conditional or nutritional stress can lead to death ( ). Again, feeding nutrients to CSBV-infected larvae, like adenosine, proline ( ), propolis ( ), and royal jelly ( ), can increase their immunity defense, but it still fails to control the CSBV epidemic. Hence, exploring a novel approach or medicine still remains an important research direction against CSBV infection.

5.

## Challenges and future prospects
5.1.

## Current EYA challenges
With regard to CSBV outbreak control, EYA has the benefits of fast action, a high cure rate, and rapid recovery. However, there are many limitations in its actual applications, including pathogens pollution, instability of EYA, high cost, and lack of standardized procedures. In actual chicken breeding, diseases such as Salmonella enteritidis ( ), Chlamydia gallinacea ( ), and Avian adenoviral may be transmitted horizontally or vertically through the eggs ( ). In contrast, the antigens in egg yolk (16/60) were found more frequently than albumen (5/60). Establishing highly efficient inactivation techniques is essential to prevent pathogens contamination of EYA production ( ). Because EYA is an egg-derived substance, it is more expensive than conventional agents. Moreover, EYA stability is greatly influenced by temperature, duration of heat treatment and cooling period, and moisture of the surrounding environment ( ), so the majority of EYA products are susceptible to enzymatic hydrolysis and corruption. Thus, the production process uses freeze-drying technology to remove antibodies, which further increases production costs. To reduce its cost, EYA preparation could be carried out through the development of novel extraction methods ( ), and using highly potent adjuvants ( ). Nevertheless, the lack of a regulated production process and method for evaluating quality has severely restricted the use of EYA products.

5.2.

## Further enhanced EYA bioactivity
In practical applications, EYA bioactivity could be further enhanced by combining with other compounds, determining the best administration route, and selecting the right dose ( ). Currently, most of the domestic products are in viscous form, which is very difficult to inject and absorb. Therefore, it could be explored in the form of whole egg powder or whole yolk powder using freeze-drying technology ( A). EYA power may be the most feasible way to include EYA for prophylactic treatment by incorporating antibodies into bee feeds ( ). Additionally, the suspension displayed low viscosity and good physical stability, suggesting that it may offer an opportunity to explore a novel dosage form of EYA ( ). Next, the appropriate route of administration of EYA is crucial for a favourable outcome ( ). Oral administration is the most practical approach and has been extensively applied to control pathogens ( ). Apart from that, it should be considered whether switching to a nebulized route in the future would achieve the same effect, while the cost will be reduced, taking less time, easier and more efficient ( ). Again, EYA coating or the preparation of novel product formulations is a feasible strategy to lengthen the duration and bioavailability of EYA in the gastrointestinal tract ( B). Activity loss of EYA was reduced to different extents ( ) by using methacrylic acid copolymer, chitosan-alginate ( ), and alginate/carrageenan hydrogels to encapsulate particular egg yolk. New hyperimmune EYA in livestock could be delivered site-specifically and released under regulated conditions via affordable encapsulation. Apart from that, the antiviral effect can be enhanced by the addition of herb powder ( ), immunologic stimulants ( ), microelements ( ) and vitamins ( ).
 
5.3.

## Development of novel antibody drugs
With the rapid development of antibody drugs, novel antibody drugs have been revolutionized over the past decade ( ). Bispecific antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), antibody conjugated nanoparticles (ACNPs), and other antibodies modified using novel genetic and chemical techniques can all result in homogenous therapies with new functions or improved therapeutic profiles ( ). First of all, ADCs are a relatively recent modification of antibodies created to combine the drug's capabilities with the selectivity of antibodies ( ; ) ( E). In the treatment of viral diseases, ADCs have shown a significant effect in reducing virus titers ( ). ADCs combine the benefits of chemical medicines with antibodies to create a single substance that has greater specificity, a long half-life, and high potency ( ). Engineering technology is always improving, and the range of ADCs for pharmaceuticals and their indications is expanding. ( ). However, the field has witnessed a number of technological vulnerabilities, such as the identification of antibody functional regions, and overall ADC stability ( ). Thus, novel ADCs or potential targets along with innovative payloads should be evaluated in the future. Second, nanomedicine has received a lot of attention as a formulation-based strategy to increase the bioavailability of medications ( ; ). The success and potential of both ADCs and nanotechnology are built upon by the new strategy known as ACNPs ( F). When compared to ADCs, ACNPs have a number of benefits, including regulated drug administration, drug structure preservation, decreased risk of secondary metabolites (if metabolism is uncertain), and maybe reduced toxicity ( ). Although the mechanisms of both targeted therapies are quite similar, conjugates can more readily penetrate cells when delivered as nanoparticles, which has clear benefits for the treatment of viral infections ( ). Thirdly, bispecific antibodies (bsAbs) have a higher binding selectivity than monospecific antibodies and can interact with two separate surface antigens ( ). For instance, the humanized mAb ibalizumab's Fab can target the human CD4 receptor, while a humanized mAb 10E8 variant can target the HIV-1 envelope MPER ( G). A bispecific antibody with incredibly powerful and widespread HIV-1 antiviral activity (up to 100% protection rate) is produced using the CrossMAb format ( ). There is already a wide variety of bispecific antibody formats that have mostly been used in the treatment of inflammatory and malignant diseases. According to the studies discussed here, bispecific antibodies have a wide range of capabilities and targeting mechanisms that can be used for viral immunotherapy. Therefore, there is a lot of room for the development of next-generation treatments and the application of similar techniques to other viruses ( ).

5.4.

## Exploitation of novel TCM monomer/formula
Exploiting novel TCM monomers or formulas can be a promising direction based on the significant anti-CSBV effect, such as adenosine and L-proline, from R. isatidis ( ). The obtained results could be used as a basis for further research into TCM in the discovery and development of novel antiviral medicines ( ), such as polysaccharides, forsythiaside, phillyrin and alkaloids ( C). For example, sulfated polysaccharides extracted from Schizymenia pacifica and phenylethanoid glycoside monomer extracted from Forsythia suspensa possess the potential to suppress HIV-1 ( , ) and infectious bronchitis virus, respectively ( ). In China, COVID-19 has been treated with TCM formulas such as Lianhua-Qingwen capsule (LQC), Ma-xing-shi-gan-tang and Jinhua Qinggan granules granules. LQC, which contains 13 herbal ingredients, has the most notable impact on the outbreak and spread of COVID-19. These include Gancao (Licorice), Hongjingtian (Rhodiola), Bohenao (Menthol), Dahuang (Rhubarb Tangute Rhubarb), Mahuang (Ephedra), Jinyinhua (Wild honeysuckle flower), Guanghuoxiang (Patchouli), Lianqiao (Forsythia suspensa), Shigao (Gypsum), Kuxingren (Bitter almond), Yuxingcao (Houttuyniae herba), Banlangen (Indigowoad root), and Mianmaguanzhong (Dryopteris crassirhizoma) ( ; ). In addition, they have been used to treat influenza with immune regulatory effects, which have been shown to have broad-spectrum antiviral effects. These examples may provide meaningful information for further development of TCM medications against CSBV.
To enhance the antiviral activity of TCM, some strategies have been developed for TCM monomer or formula, such as probiotic fermentation of TCM and nano-TCM. Through probiotics fermentation, bio transforming of TCM results in reduced harmful components and easier release of active ingredients or therapeutic substances. The macromolecule or polymer forms of active ingredients can be cut down into smaller molecules during fermentation. This enhances the adsorption of the active substances and encourages transmembrane transport. Consequently, TCM fermentation increases the health benefits by enhancing the interactions between microbial and herbal activities ( ). The incorporation of nanotechnology into TCM is an innovative strategy that can considerably improve the antiviral effect of TCM. The surface area of TCM will increase with nanotechnology, giving the medicine accrue plenty of physicochemical properties and biological activity that does not exist under normal conditions. The TCM therapeutic effects would be strengthened as a result of TCM nanotechnology. The time required for decoction will be reduced and improved the solubility and dissolution rate. Consequently, nano-TCM has enormous potential in developing novel TCM monomers or formulas with higher bioavailability, lower toxicity, and extra bio functions ( ). These methods might present a new opportunity for creating therapeutic agents to treat honey bee CSBV infections.

5.5.

## Exploration of nucleotide drugs
A minor modification can have a significant impact on the structures of nucleosides ( ). Theoretically, nucleoside analogues should resemble natural nucleosides to disrupt and stop the replication or other biological processes when they are identified by cellular or viral enzymes ( , ) ( H). They generally present in 5’-triphosphorylated form and target viral DNA or RNA polymerase that stops the synthesis of viral nucleic acid ( ). When contemplating the potential modifications to the nucleoside scaffold, several sites can be considered, such as the sugar moiety, aromatic heterocyclic base, glycosidic bond, and/or phosphate group of nucleotides ( ). Moreover, these modifications can be carried out in tandem, enabling a wide range of nucleoside structures and functions ( ). DENV, H1N1 influenza virus, Ebola virus and most recently SARS-CoV-2 virus, can all be treated with nucleoside or nucleotide analogs drugs today ( ; ). Nucleotide medications should therefore be able to prevent and treat CSBV infection. However, there are still several issues with nucleotide drugs such as the generation of resistant mutants ( ), direct host cell damage, and unidentified interactions with the target viral proteins. These issues should be further investigated in the future.

6.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, antibody therapy exhibits a good and positive development trend and a significant market potential for the control and treatment of CSBV infection. This review provides detailed background information to support and facilitate future research on the control of CSBV. Despite this field being confronted with many challenges, it is confirmed that yolk antibodies could terminate the CSBV infection in the future, which also serves as a valuable reference to researchers and beekeepers. Meanwhile, other effective approaches against CSBV infection, such as the development of novel antibody drugs, TCM monomer/formula, nucleotide drugs, and their synergistic study, are also prospected, which not only provides a new strategy for significant enhancement of EYA efficacy, but also offers a bright prospect for the control of other bee viruses.
