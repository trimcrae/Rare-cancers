## Abstract
The acute respiratory virus infection can induce uncontrolled inflammatory responses, such as cytokine storm and viral pneumonia, which are the major causes of death in clinical cases. Cyclophilin A (CypA) is mainly distributed in the cytoplasm of resting cells and released into the extracellular space in response to inflammatory stimuli. Extracellular CypA (eCypA) is upregulated and promotes inflammatory response in severe COVID-19 patients. However, how eCypA promotes virus-induced inflammatory response remains elusive. Here, we observe that eCypA is induced by influenza A and B viruses and SARS-CoV-2 in cells, mice, or patients. Anti-CypA mAb reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines production, leukocytes infiltration, and lung injury in virus-infected mice. Mechanistically, eCypA binding to integrin β2 triggers integrin activation, thereby facilitating leukocyte trafficking and cytokines production via the focal adhesion kinase (FAK)/GTPase and FAK/ERK/P65 pathways, respectively. These functions are suppressed by the anti-CypA mAb that specifically blocks eCypA-integrin β2 interaction. Overall, our findings reveal that eCypA-integrin β2 signaling mediates virus-induced inflammatory response, indicating that eCypA is a potential target for antibody therapy against viral pneumonia.
Keywords: cyclophilin A, integrin β2, leukocyte trafficking, cytokine, viral pneumonia

## Introduction
The acute respiratory virus infection, such as those caused by influenza A virus (IAV), influenza B virus (IBV), or severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), can induce uncontrolled inflammatory responses, such as cytokine storm and viral pneumonia, which are the major causes of death in clinical cases. , , Currently, the drug treatment options to control cytokine storm and viral pneumonia include steroids, intravenous immunoglobulins, inhibitors of JAKs, and antibody therapies, such as anakinra (interleukin [IL]-1 receptor antagonist) and tocilizumab (IL-6 receptor antagonist). , However, the patients with viral pneumonia cannot always acquire benefit from those drugs, especially for steroids. , , , , Similarly, the clinical trial reports indicate that neither anakinra nor tocilizumab shows survival benefit in patients with severe viral pneumonia, , indicating that virus-induced inflammatory response is complicated, and antibody against single pro-inflammatory cytokine might be insufficient for the treatment of viral pneumonia. Therefore, new therapies should probably focus on the identification of key upstream regulators to control pro-inflammatory cytokines production and leukocyte functions.
Cyclophilin A (CypA, encoded by PPIA), a peptidyl-prolyl cis/trans isomerase (PPIase), is an essential regulator of inflammation. Intracellular CypA (iCypA) is involved in promoting multiple cytokines production by regulating the RIG-I-like receptor-, IL-6-, and IL-1β-mediated signaling pathways, macrophage polarization, and TCR signaling. Meanwhile, CypA is secreted from cells in response to oxidative stress, lipopolysaccharide treatment, and injury, , , indicating that extracellular CypA (eCypA) might play an important role in aggravated disease process. Recent evidence indicates that the concentration of extracellular CypA (eCypA) in plasma is significantly higher in severe and critical COVID-19 patients than in those experiencing mild symptoms of disease. Moreover, our previous studies demonstrate that proteolysis targeting chimera targeting CypA controls virus-induced cytokine storm, and anti-CypA antibody reduces lipopolysaccharide-induced acute mouse pneumonia. These findings indicate that eCypA may be a catalyzer and potential therapeutic target for inflammatory disease.
So far, CD147 is the only known receptor for eCypA. eCypA binding to CD147 activates the ERK and NF-κB signaling pathways, leading to the production of various pro-inflammatory cytokines in some chronic inflammatory disease models. , , Anti-CD147 monoclonal antibody (mAb) or CD147 mutant inhibits eCypA-induced chemotactic activity, , , but the underlying mechanisms remain obscure. Several studies have shown that CD147 does not mediate all cellular events associated with eCypA. For example, CD147 knockdown did not affect eCypA-induced IL-8 expression in U937 cells and multiple eCypA-activated signals in vascular smooth muscle cells and endothelial cells. , Therefore, other receptors might mediate certain functions of eCypA.
In the present study, we identified integrin β2 as another potential receptor for eCypA and investigated the role of eCypA-integrin β2 signaling in regulating virus-induced inflammatory response. We found that blocking eCypA-integrin β2 complex by anti-CypA mAb alleviates viral pneumonia, which highlights the importance of anti-CypA antibody therapy against inflammatory diseases.

## Results

## eCypA is upregulated during virus infection
To investigate the relationship between eCypA and virus infection, the secretion of eCypA was determined in virus-infected patients, mice, and cells. The upregulation of eCypA was observed in serum of IAV-infected outpatients (children aged 3–14) compared with age-matched healthy donors ( A). Then the levels of eCypA were examined in virus-infected mouse and cell models. Mice were challenged with IAV, IBV, or SARS-CoV-2. We found that the concentration of eCypA in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) and/or serum of IAV- ( B), IBV- ( C), and SARS-CoV-2- ( D) infected mice was rapidly upregulated at 1 day post-infection (d.p.i.) and maintained high levels up to 7 d.p.i. In addition, eCypA in the cell culture supernatants from MHS (mouse alveolar macrophage), A549 (human lung epithelial cell), hVeC (human vascular endothelium cell), THP1 (human monocyte/macrophage), HL60 (human promyeloblast), and Jurkat (human T lymphocyte) were upregulated during IAV infection, especially in those from MHS and THP1 cells ( E). Similar results were observed for eCypA in the cell culture supernatant from IBV-infected cells ( F). These data demonstrate that the secretion of eCypA is upregulated during both influenza virus and SARS-CoV-2 infection.
 

## Anti-CypA mAb reduces the production of virus-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine and leukocyte infiltration in mouse lungs
Anti-CypA mAb was used to investigate whether eCypA is a potential therapeutic target for virus-induced inflammatory response. First, the specificity and effectiveness of anti-CypA mAbs were detected by immunoprecipitation assay. The result showed that anti-CypA mAbs could bind to CypA, but not to other cyclophilins ( ). Next, mice were infected with IBV ( A), IAV ( A), or SARS-CoV-2 ( A) and then treated with the anti-influenza drug oseltamivir (OSE), anti-CypA mAb, a combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb, or vehicle control (10% DMSO). At 2, 4, and 6 d.p.i., the production of 36 cytokines in BALF from IBV-infected mice was detected using ProcartaPlex Multiplex Immunoassays. The overall cytokine levels ( B), especially the levels of key pro-inflammatory cytokines (Th1 cytokines and chemokines) involved in viral pneumonia (tumor necrosis factor [TNF]-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2, and interferon [IFN]-γ) ( C) in the BALF of mice treated with anti-CypA mAbs or OSE+anti-CypA mAbs were significantly lower than those in mice treated with vehicle, or OSE. In contrast, anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 was upregulated following these treatments ( C). However, anti-CypA mAb had a slight effect on Th2 cytokines (IL-4 and IL-13) ( C). Meanwhile, the levels of major pro-inflammatory cytokines in IBV- ( D), IAV- ( B), or SARS-CoV-2- ( B) infected mice were further examined by ELISA or real-time PCR. The pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, CCL2, CXCL1, and CXCL10 were reduced in mice treated with anti-CypA mAb or OSE+anti-CypA mAb, which was consistent with the results shown in C. In addition, the effect of anti-CypA mAbs on leukocyte trafficking was investigated in mouse lungs using immunofluorescence. Less macrophage and neutrophil infiltration was found in the lungs of IBV-infected mice treated with OSE or anti-CypA mAb, with the least leukocyte infiltration observed in mice treated with the combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb ( E and 2F). These results demonstrate that blocking eCypA by anti-CypA mAb suppresses virus-induced pro-inflammatory cytokines production and leukocyte infiltration.
 

## Anti-CypA mAb alleviates virus-induced lung injury in mice
The effect of anti-CypA mAb on virus-induced lung injury was further investigated. The lungs of IBV-infected mice were imaged using micro-X-ray computed tomography (micro-CT), which is a standard tool used to observe the anatomical basis and pathophysiology of lung injury in live animals. The area of high-density shadows in the lungs of mice treated with OSE, anti-CypA mAb, or the combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb was smaller than that of the vehicle-treated mice at 4 and 6 d.p.i. The mildest pulmonary inflammation was observed in the lungs of mice treated with the combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb ( A). We evaluated pulmonary injury using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and pathological scores. Consistent with the results of micro-CT, IBV-, IAV-, or SARS-CoV2-infected mice treated with OSE or anti-CypA mAb showed less leukocyte infiltration, lung consolidation, and congested blood vessels ( B, C, and C), and the mice treated with the combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb showed the mildest pulmonary injury following IBV and IAV infection ( B and C). The effects of anti-CypA mAb on lung indices, weight changes, and survival rates were evaluated in IBV- and IAV-infected mice. The lung indices (100 × lung/body weight) of mice treated with OSE, anti-CypA mAb, or the combination of OSE and anti-CypA mAb were lower than those of vehicle-treated mice. The mice treated with OSE and anti-CypA mAb combination showed the lowest lung indices in IBV- ( C) and IAV- ( D) infected mice. Consistent with these results, mice treated with OSE, anti-CypA mAb, or their combination lost weight at a slower rate than vehicle-treated mice, with those that received the combination losing weight at the slowest rate ( D and E). In addition, these treatments, especially the combination treatment, improved the survival rates of mice infected with IBV. Only 6.67% (1 of 15) of the untreated (vehicle) mice survived this challenge. In comparison, 40% (6 of 15) OSE-, 33.33% (5 of 15) anti-CypA mAb-, and 73.33% (11 of 15) OSE/anti-CypA mAb combination-treated mice survived at 14 days post-IBV infection ( E), and the survival rates were similar to those in IAV-infected mice ( F). Together, blocking eCypA by anti-CypA mAb alleviates virus-induced murine lung injury, and the combination of anti-CypA mAb and antiviral drugs show synergistic effects.
 

## eCypA binds to integrin β2
The mechanisms of eCypA-induced inflammatory response are still not clear. An increasing body of evidence suggests that some functions of eCypA are independent of CD147. Therefore, we hypothesized that other receptors might be involved in eCypA-induced inflammatory response. Mass spectrometry was used to identify the potential membrane-binding proteins bound to CypA in leukocytes (THP1, HL60, and Jurkat). Among those candidate proteins ( ), we found that integrin β2, one of the most highly expressed integrins in leukocytes that mediate leukocyte adhesion and migration, might be a novel receptor of eCypA. To confirm the interaction between eCypA and integrin β2, His-tagged extracellular fragments of integrin β2 and CD147 were transiently expressed in HEK293 cells and purified using immobilized metal affinity chromatography ( A). The results of ELISA ( A) showed that CypA bound to integrin β2, and the affinity of CypA to integrin β2 is slightly higher than that of CypA to CD147. The specific blocking effect of anti-CypA mAb on CypA-integrin β2 interaction was confirmed by ELISA and immunoprecipitation assays. The results showed that anti-CypA mAb specifically blocked the binding of CypA to integrin β2, but not to CD147 ( B and 4C). Then the binding of CypA to CCL2-or Mn2+-activated integrin β2 in THP1 cells was determined with an immunoprecipitation assay. We observed a consistent binding affinity of CypA to activated integrin β2 ( B). Additionally, CypA R55 is crucial for PPIase activity, and R55A displayed lowest PPIase catalytic activity (0.1%). We found that the mutation of R55A did not decrease the binding affinity of CypA to integrin β2 ( D), indicating that the eCypA-integrin β2 interaction was independent of PPIase. In addition, the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and competitive SPR assays were performed to further confirmed the blocking effect of anti-CypA mAb on CypA-integrin β2 interaction. CypA had high binding affinity to anti-CypA mAb (KD = 0.295 nM) ( D). CypA bound to integrin β2 (KD = 206 nM) in a concentration-dependent manner ( E and 4F). Furthermore, anti-CypA mAb blocked CypA-integrin β2 interaction (KI = 14 nM) in a concentration-dependent manner ( G and 4H). Therefore, the anti-CypA mAb was used as a specific blocking reagent for CypA-integrin β2 interaction in the following experiments.
 Next, the binding domain of integrin β2 with CypA was analyzed by Alphafold V2.3.1. The results showed that CypA bound to the integrin epidermal growth factor (I-EGF) domains (I-EGF2, I-EGF3, and I-EGF4) but not with PSI/hybrid domain/I-EGF1 (PHE1) domain of integrin β2 ( C), which was confirmed by the co-immunoprecipitation assay ( D). Furthermore, the key binding sites of CypA with integrin β2 and anti-CypA mAb were analyzed by Alphafold V2.3.1 and ligPLUS. , , As shown in I, His (H)-70 and Lys (K)-76 of CypA might be the key residues for binding to both integrin β2 and anti-CypA mAb. The results of co-immunoprecipitation assay showed that H70A and double mutations (H70A and K76A) of CypA significantly decreased both CypA-integrin β2 ( J) and CypA-anti-CypA mAb ( K) interactions, and K76A had a slight effect on the binding activity of CypA to integrin β2 and anti-CypA mAb, indicating that integrin β2 and anti-CypA mAb could competitively interact with CypA. We also found that both IBV ( E) and eCypA ( F) upregulated integrin β2 expression. These results suggest that the eCypA-integrin β2 complex may play an important role in virus-induced inflammatory response.

## eCypA triggers integrin β2 activation and subsequent leukocyte trafficking
To investigate whether the binding of eCypA to integrin β2 triggered integrin activation, a specific antibody that recognizes activated CD11/CD18 (integrin αLβ2/αMβ2/αXβ2) was used. The flow cytometry results showed that eCypA upregulated the ratios of CD11/CD18-activated THP1 cells, which was significantly suppressed by anti-CypA mAb ( A). Talin and kindlin-3 are integrin co-activators that trigger integrins inside-out activation by binding to the integrin β tail in the cytoplasm. Thus, we next examined the interaction of integrin β2 with talin and kindlin-3 in eCypA-stimulated THP1 cells. Co-immunoprecipitation results showed that eCypA enhanced the binding of talin and kindlin-3 to the integrin β2 tail, and this effect was inhibited by anti-CypA mAb ( B). Additionally, the effect of eCypA on integrin activation was further confirmed in THP1-PPIA−/− cells ( A and S5B), indicating that eCypA-triggered integrin activation is independent of iCypA. These data demonstrated that the binding of eCypA to integrin β2 triggers integrin activation.
 Integrin β2 activation is crucial for leukocyte trafficking. Therefore, whether eCypA-triggered integrin activation was associated with leukocyte trafficking was examined. The adhesion and transendothelial migration results showed that both eCypA ( C and 5D) and IBV ( E and 5F) significantly triggered the adhesion of THP1, HL60, and Jurkat cells to the immobilized ICAM-1 substrate within 30 min and the transendothelial migration of those cells, which was restrained by anti-CypA mAb. Meanwhile, eCypA-induced leukocyte adhesion was confirmed in mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) ( C). Moreover, eCypA induced leukocyte adhesion and transendothelial migration independently of iCypA ( D–S5G). These results indicate that eCypA is able to trigger integrin activation and promote leukocyte trafficking.

## The integrin β2-FAK-GTPase axis is critical for eCypA-triggered leukocyte trafficking
We then investigated whether eCypA triggered leukocyte trafficking was dependent on integrin β2. When integrin β2 was knocked down in eCypA-stimulated THP1 cells using siRNA, we observed significantly decreased numbers of adherent cells ( A) and fewer cells migrated across hVeCs ( B), and the effect of anti-CypA mAb was eliminated ( A and 6B), indicating that eCypA-triggered leukocyte adhesion and transendothelial migration was dependent on integrin β2. Focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and GTPase are crucial for integrin-mediated leukocyte trafficking. , We then examined whether integrin β2 affected eCypA-triggered activation of FAK and GTPase. As shown in C, eCypA enhanced FAK phosphorylation and Rac1, CDC42, and RhoA activation, while the knockdown of integrin β2 dramatically reduced phosphorylation of FAK and almost failed to activate Rac1, CDC42, and RhoA, indicating that integrin β2 was critical for eCypA-triggered FAK, Rac1, CDC42, and RhoA GTPase activation. eCypA-induced FAK phosphorylation and GTPase activation were also confirmed in THP1-PPIA−/− cells ( A). In addition, we constructed the BSG (encoding CD147)-knockout THP1 (THP1-BSG−/−) cells to assess the effect of eCypA-CD147 binding on leukocyte trafficking. The absence of CD147 had little effect on eCypA-triggered adhesion ( B), transendothelial migration ( C), FAK phosphorylation ( D), and Rac1, CDC42, and RhoA GTPase activation ( D). These results suggest that eCypA-triggered leukocyte trafficking was mainly dependent on integrin β2 other than CD147.
 Next, the FAK inhibitor PF573228 (PF) was used to investigate whether FAK-GTPase signaling is critical for eCypA-triggered leukocyte trafficking. We observed that PF caused the reductions of eCypA-triggered THP1 cell adhesion ( D), and transendothelial migration ( E), and GTPase activation ( F), and the effect of anti-CypA mAb was eliminated ( D and 6E), indicating that eCypA-triggered leukocyte trafficking is dependent on FAK-GTPase activation. In addition, we investigated F-actin rearrangement, a downstream effect of GTPase activation, using phalloidin staining of THP1 cells. The immunofluorescence results showed that eCypA directly induced F-actin polymerization in THP1 cells, and anti-CypA mAb attenuated this effect ( G), further confirming the effect of eCypA on leukocyte activation. Overall, these data demonstrated that eCypA-integrin β2 binding triggers integrin activation and subsequent activation of FAK and GTPase, thereby facilitating eCypA-triggered leukocyte trafficking.

## The integrin β2-FAK-ERK-P65 axis is involved in eCypA-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression
Whether eCypA triggered pro-inflammatory cytokine production was further examined. We observed that both eCypA ( A) and IBV ( B) induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in THP1, HL60, and Jurkat cells, and anti-CypA mAbs attenuated these effects. Meanwhile, eCypA-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression was confirmed in BMDM ( C). In addition, eCypA induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression ( D and S7E), and ERK1/2 and P65 phosphorylation ( F) independently of iCypA. Next, the effect of integrin β2 on eCypA-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression was evaluated in ITGB2-knockdown THP1 cells using small interfering RNA (siRNA). The results of real-time PCR and western blotting assays indicated that ITGB2 knockdown reduced eCypA-triggered TNF-α, IL-1β, CCL2, and CXCL1 expression ( A), and ERK1/2 and P65 phosphorylation ( B). Notably, anti-CypA mAb inhibited these effects of eCypA in both wild-type (WT) and ITGB2 knockdown cells, indicating that eCypA-induced ERK-P65-mediated pro-inflammatory cytokine expression through both integrin β2-dependent and independent pathways. Then we investigated whether CD147 mediated eCypA-triggered pro-inflammatory cytokines. BSG knockout in THP1 cells reduced eCypA-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression ( G), and ERK1/2 and P65 phosphorylation ( H). These data indicated that both integrin β2 and CD147 were involved in eCypA-induced ERK-P65-mediated pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Furthermore, PF inhibited eCypA-triggered pro-inflammatory cytokine expression ( C), and the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 and P65 ( D) in THP1 cells, indicating that FAK was involved in eCypA-triggered pro-inflammatory cytokine production. These results demonstrate that the integrin β2-FAK-ERK-P65 axis is another pathway for eCypA-triggered pro-inflammatory cytokine production.
 

## Discussion
Many cytokines and host factors can aggravate virus-induced inflammatory response, leading to severe complications, such as viral pneumonia and cytokine storm. , , In the present study, we found that eCypA is upregulated upon viral infection in A549, hVeC, MHS, THP1, HL60, and Jurkat cells. Moreover, THP1 and MHS appeared to secrete more eCypA than other cells. We considered the possibly that the expression level of eCypA might depend on the ability of different cells to sense viral infection and trigger CypA production-associated pathways, such as STAT3 signaling pathway. , Furthermore, eCypA promotes viral-induced inflammatory responses, suggesting that eCypA is a potential target for the therapy of viral infection with excessive inflammatory responses. Additionally, an eCypA potential receptor, integrin β2, was identified. eCypA-integrin β2 signaling plays a dominant role in leukocyte trafficking and multiple pro-inflammatory cytokine production, and anti-CypA mAbs specifically blocking eCypA-integrin β2 interaction alleviates IAV-, IBV-, and SARS-CoV-2-induced pneumonia. These data provide an understanding of the molecular mechanisms by which eCypA promotes virus-induced inflammatory response.
Uncontrolled leukocyte trafficking is a major cause of tissue damage in inflammatory diseases. Leukocyte movement is complicated and precisely regulated in a step-by-step process called the leukocyte trafficking cascade. The integrin β2 subfamily (αLβ2, αMβ2, αXβ2, and αDβ2) has the most commonly expressed integrins in leukocytes and plays key roles in most steps of the leukocyte trafficking cascade. The activation of the β2 subfamily integrins is dependent on the selectin and chemokine signaling pathways. , , Activated β2 subfamily integrins bind to their ligands, triggering leukocyte movement. The ligands of β2 subfamily integrins are diverse, including typical ICAMs, some ECM molecules, atypical growth factors, hormones, venomous compounds, small soluble molecules, and pathogen membrane proteins. It is well established that the headpiece of integrin β2, including I-like (βA) domain and metal ion-dependent adhesion sites, mediate ligand binding. The I-EGF domains 1, 2, 3, and 4 are in the stalk region of integrin β2, which provides the link between the headpiece and transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains, and conveys the inside-out signal to change the conformation of the headpiece. In addition, it has been reported that engagement of I-EGF3 with its mAbs triggers integrin β2 activation. Similarly, we found that eCypA bound to the I-EGF domains of integrin β2 and triggered its activation. It is suggested that the binding of eCypA or other proteins with I-EGF domains might change the conformation of I-EGF domains, thereby leading to integrin β2 activation and leukocyte trafficking. These data reveal a previously unknown mechanism underlying eCypA-mediated leukocyte trafficking.
Several studies have suggested that CD147 plays important roles in eCypA-mediated leukocyte chemotaxis and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. , , However, at least one study has suggested that that CD147 can directly regulate intercellular adhesion pathways independently of eCypA. The binding of CD147 mAb to the CD147 molecule, which mimics natural ligand binding, may generate intracellular signals that activate the LFA-1/ICAM-1 intercellular adhesion pathway. Therefore, the roles of eCypA-CD147 interaction in leukocyte trafficking require further exploration. In this study, we found that integrin β2, but not CD147, played a key role in eCypA-triggered THP1 adhesion, transendothelial migration, and GTPase activation. Meanwhile, both integrin β2 and CD147 were involved in eCypA-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. This indicated that eCypA regulates leukocyte trafficking and multiple pro-inflammatory cytokine production in different pathways. However, we cannot exclude the possibility of a third ligand for CypA that could have an impact on inflammatory response. This is a limitation of this study.
The binding of eCypA to known or unknown receptors is a potential target for anti-inflammatory therapy of infections. Recent evidence demonstrates that anti-CD147 mAb inhibits SARS-CoV-2-induced inflammatory response by blocking eCypA-CD147 interaction. However, we cannot rule out the possibility that blocking CD147 might interfere with its other physiological functions. Moreover, integrin β2-deficient mice have severe defects in leukocyte recruitment and T cell activation, , indicating that targeting integrin β2 might impair host antiviral immune response. eCypA is secreted by host cells in response to inflammatory stimuli, such as viral infection. Therefore, targeting eCypA to block its binding to the receptors might be a better anti-inflammatory strategy, which does not affect the normal functions of iCypA. Our data showed that the anti-CypA mAb was able to inhibit virus-induced inflammatory responses by specifically blocking eCypA-integrin β2 interaction. Theoretically, strategies that target eCypA to block its binding to both integrin β2 and CD147 may get a better anti-inflammatory effect during viral infections, which needs to be further confirmed in future studies.
A systematic review of clinical studies revealed that oseltamivir is ineffective in reducing morbidity and symptoms when administered post-influenza virus infection. Therefore, therapeutic strategies for fighting severe respiratory virus infections should be aimed at viral replication and an excessive inflammatory response. We observed that both eCypA and integrin β2 were upregulated during infection with IBV. Anti-CypA mAb and knockdown of integrin β2 reduced virus-induced leukocyte trafficking and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. The combination therapy with anti-CypA mAb and oseltamivir showed strong synergistic therapeutic effects in mouse models of viral pneumonia and resulted in almost complete recovery of infected mice. These results suggested that the therapy combinations of anti-CypA mAb and antiviral drugs may be effective for the treatment of virus infections.
In summary, our study reveals another way for eCypA to promote inflammatory responses. eCypA binds to integrin β2, a leukocyte-specific integrin subunit, resulting in leukocyte trafficking and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. Additionally, anti-CypA mAb suppresses virus-induced excessive inflammatory responses and reduced tissue damage in mice, suggesting that blocking eCypA is a promising therapeutic strategy for individuals with severe viral pneumonia suspected to have an excessive inflammatory response. Furthermore, integrin β2 is mainly expressed in leukocytes. It also can be detected in some tissues, such as eye, skeletal muscle, and early embryo (https://www.genecards.org/cgi-bin/carddisp.pl?gene=ITGB2#expression), but its functions in these tissues are still not clear. Most viral infections are associated with tissue injury and leukocyte activation and trafficking. Therefore, the interaction of eCypA and integrin β2 may occur not only in respiratory viral infections but also in other viral infections, and targeting this complex may have broader anti-inflammatory effect.

## Materials and methods

## Mice
WT C57BL/6 mice were purchased from the Vital River Laboratory Animal Center (Beijing, China). Human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (hACE2) transgenic mice, which express human ACE2 driven by the human epithelial cell cytokeratin-18 (K18) promoter, used as an infection model for SARS-CoV-2, were purchased from Gempharmatech. The mice were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions at the Institute of Microbiology. Eight-week-old female and male mice were used for this study.

## Cell lines
MDCK, Vero E6, MHS, A549, hVeC, THP1, HL60, Jurkat, and 293T cells were obtained from ATCC. Short hairpin RNA (shRNA)-based knockdown of CypA in 293T cells has been previously described. 
PPIA-knockout THP1 (THP1/PPIA−/−) and BSG-knockout THP1 (THP1/BSG−/−) cells were generated using CRISPR-Cas9 (Ubigene Biosciences Co., Ltd., China). MDCK cells were used to determine influenza virus titrations. BMDMs were obtained as previously described. Briefly, bone marrow cells were isolated from aseptically dissected and flushed tibias and femurs of 6- to 8-week old C57BL/6 mice. Bone marrow cells were differentiated into BMDMs for 5–7 days in the medium with 20 ng/mL macrophage colony stimulating factor (M-CSF). All cell lines were tested for mycoplasma contamination using the MycoBlue Mycoplasma Detector kit (Vazyme, China) and maintained in DMEM or RPMI 1640 (GIBCO) supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS (GIBCO), 100 U/mL penicillin (Sigma), and 100 mg/mL streptomycin (Sigma) at 37°C under a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

## Serum from IAV-infected patients
Serum was obtained from IAV-infected outpatients (children aged 3–14, n = 10) and age-matched healthy donors (n = 8) at Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University. This study was approved by the ethics committees of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University (IEC-C-008-A08-V.05.1).

## Antibodies and proteins
The anti-CypA mouse mAb was generated from hybridoma by BGI Genomics. His-tagged CypA, integrin β2, and CD147 were transiently expressed in HEK293 cells and purified using immobilized metal affinity chromatography. Fc-tagged CypA and CypA-R55 were transiently expressed in HEK293 cells and purified using protein A affinity chromatography.

## Method details

## Adhesion assay
Flat-bottom 96-well microplates were coated overnight with 100 μL of 5 μg/mL ICAM-1 in coating buffer (PBS, 10 mM NaHCO3, pH 9.0) at 4°C, followed by incubation with 2% BSA in the coating buffer for 2 h at room temperature to block non-specific binding sites. Leukocytes were washed and resuspended in 0.2 mL of Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) (Thermo Fisher Scientific) or serum-free RPMI-1640 with eCypA (10 nM), eCypA+anti-CypA mAb (10 nM eCypA+50 nM anti-CypA mAb, preincubated at 37°C for 1 h), or other stimuli and added to ICAM-1-coated wells (2 × 105 cells/well). After 30 min of incubation (37°C, 5% CO2), nonadherent leukocytes were removed immediately by washing gently with PBS. The adherent leukocytes were resuspended in PBS and counted with an automatic cell counter (Thermo Fisher). These results are expressed as a percentage of adherent cells.

## Transendothelial migration assay
The Transwell upper chamber (3-mm pore, Millipore) was seeded with hVeC (0.2 mL DMEM containing 2 × 105 cells), and the lower chamber was added with 0.3 mL DMEM, followed by incubation for 16 h (37°C, 5% CO2). Then the DMEM medium of the lower chamber was changed to 0.3 mL serum-free RPMI 1640 medium with a different stimulus. The upper chamber’s DMEM medium was replaced with 0.2 mL serum-free RPMI 1640 medium containing eCypA (10 nM), eCypA+anti-CypA mAb (10 nM eCypA+50 nM anti-CypA mAb, preincubated at 37°C for 1 h), or other stimulus containing 5 × 105 leukocytes. After incubation for 12 h (37°C, 5% CO2), leukocytes migrating to the bottom surface were counted using an automatic cell counter. Results were expressed as the percentage of transendothelial migrated cells.

## CypA-integrin β2/CD147 binding assay
Purified integrin β2-His or CD147-His (1 μg/mL) were coated on 96-well plates in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) overnight at 4°C. After blocking for 1 h with 0.4% BSA in PBST at room temperature, different concentrations of CypA-Fc were added to the plates for 2 h at room temperature. Plates were subsequently washed three times and incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-human heavy chain antibody for 1 h at room temperature. After washing, plates were developed with TMB. The OD was measured at 450 nM. BSA was used as a negative control.
For CypA-integrin β2/CD147 interaction blocking assay, purified anti-CypA mAb was coated on flat-bottom 96-well microplates in PBS overnight at 4°C. After blocking for 1 h with 0.4% BSA in PBST at room temperature, CypA-Fc were added to the plates for 2 h at room temperature. Plates were subsequently washed three times and incubated with integrin β2-His or CD147-His for 2 h at room temperature. Plates were washed three times and incubated with HRP-conjugated anti-His antibody for 1 h at room temperature. After washing, plates were developed with TMB. The OD was measured at 450 nM. Isotype IgG1 was used as a negative control. Then, the blocking effect of anti-CypA mAb was confirmed by immunoprecipitation. The excessive CypA-Fc or human Fc protein was incubated with protein A agarose beads in PBS, and the redundant proteins were washed three times with wash buffer (300 mM NaCl, 20 mM HEPES, 1 mM EDTA, 1% Triton X-100, and 10% glycerin), and the complexes were incubated with or without mouse anti-CypA mAb in PBS. Then, the precipitation was incubated with integrin β2-His or CD147-His in PBS, and the redundant proteins were washed three times with wash buffer. The bound proteins were analyzed by immunoblot with anti-His, and anti-human heavy chain antibodies.

## Surface plasmon resonance
SPR and competitive SPR analyses were performed on Biacore T200 (Cytiva). In brief, integrin β2-His was immobilized to the surface of CM5 sensor chips (Cytiva, BR100530) by an Amine Coupling Kit (Cytiva, BR-1000-50). The bindings of CypA to anti-CypA mAb and integrin β2 were detected using the kinetic analysis/concentration series/direct binding mode. In the competitive SPR assay, the mixture of CypA (constant concentration) and anti-CypA mAb (increased concentrations in a gradient) was as an analyte. The flow rate was set to 30 μL/min, and the binding and dissociation time were 120 s and 300 s, respectively. The results were analyzed by Biacore evaluation software 3.2 to determine the affinity or inhibition constants.

## Integrin activation assay
A Mem-PER Plus kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to extract the membrane protein of THP1 cells according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Lysates were immunoprecipitated using integrin β2 antibodies. The binding of talin and kindlin-3 to activated integrin and GAPDH (loading control) were detected by immunoblotting.

## Rho GTPases activation assay
The activation of Rho GTPases was detected by a RhoA/Rac1/CDC42 activation assay using a biochem kit (cytoskeleton, BK030), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, THP1 cells were lysed, and the supernatants were used to perform a GST precipitation assay to detect GTP-bound RhoA (Rhotekin-RBD affinity beads), Rac1 (PAK-PBD affinity beads), and CDC42 (PAK-PBD affinity beads). GTP-RhoA, GTP-Rac1, and GTP-CDC42 levels were normalized to total RhoA, Rac1, and CDC42 levels, respectively.

## Virus and virus infection
The influenza B Guangxi/JZ1352/2018 (Victoria lineage) virus was isolated from patients in Jiangzhou, Guangxi, China, and was provided by Prof. Dayan Wang of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Influenza A/WSN/33 (H1N1) was obtained from cDNA. SARS-CoV-2 (hCoV-19/China/CAS-B001/2020; GISAID No. EPI_ISL_514256-7) was provided by Prof. Yuhai Bi of the Institute of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences.
Eight-week-old C57BL/6 mice were anesthetized with isoflurane via inhalation. Subsequently, the mice were inoculated intranasally with 100,000 PFU of IBV suspended in 30 μL of PBS, or 3,000 PFU of IAV suspended in 30 μL of PBS. hACE2 transgenic mice were intranasally inoculated with 100,000 PFU of SARS-CoV-2 suspended in 30 μL of PBS.
All experiments involving the SARS-CoV-2 strains were conducted in a biosafety level 3 (BSL3) laboratory. All animal experiments were approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Institute of Microbiology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (APIMCAS2023052) and complied with the Beijing Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethical Guidelines of the Beijing Administration Committee of Laboratory Animals. All mice were maintained in a barrier facility with free access to food and water.

## ProcartaPlex Multiplex Immunoassays and ELISA
BALF was obtained from the lungs of mice with 1 mL of PBS and collected after centrifugation. In addition, cell supernatants were collected after centrifugation. Cytokines in BALF were measured using a mouse ProcartaPlex Multiplex Immunoassays Kit (LAIZEE BIOTECH, EPX360-26092-901) and ELISA Kits (MULTISCIENCES). Cytokines were quantified in supernatants by ELISA Kit (MULTISCIENCES).

## RNA isolation and qPCR analysis
Total RNA was extracted from THP1, HL60, Jurkat, and BMDM using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Invitrogen). cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using oligo (dT) primers and M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega). cDNA was amplified by real-time PCR with SYBR Premix Ex Taq (TaKaRa) on an ABI 7500 detector (Applied Biosystems). The primers used are listed in . The detector’s Ct values were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

## Micro-X-ray CT
Mice were anesthetized with isoflurane via inhalation and then transferred to a micro-CT imaging system (PE Quantum FX, PerkinElmer). CT images were obtained and analyzed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The parameters: Voltage 90 kV, Current 180 μA, FOV 24 mm, Scan Technique 4.5 min.

## Histological analysis
Mouse lung tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The tissues were embedded in paraffin and sectioned. The sections were used for H&E staining and immunofluorescence histochemistry. For H&E staining, lung injury severity was scored in a blinded manner, as previously described. For immunofluorescence histochemistry, anti-F4/80 and anti-Ly6G mAbs were used to detect macrophages and neutrophils, respectively, as previously described. 

## Quantification and statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using Prism 9 (GraphPad) software and Microsoft Excel (2019). Comparisons were performed using the methods as indicated in the legends. p < 0.05 was considered significant (∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.001, #p < 0.05, or ##p < 0.01). In the survival study, Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated with Prism 9 and analyzed for statistical significance using the log rank test.
