## Abstract
Mutations in NHE6 (human gene name SLC9A6) lead to the rare X‐linked disorder Christianson syndrome (CS) characterized by intellectual disability, autism and ataxia. NHE6 is a Na+/H+ exchanger that regulates pH and ionic equilibria across endosomal membranes in a variety of tissues, although its non‐neuronal role is understudied. A common co‐morbidity in CS is low body weight, with body mass index in the 3–5th percentile range. In the present study, we use a Nhe6KO
 mouse model of CS to demonstrate a role for NHE6 in fat accumulation and glucose homeostasis. On both chow and a high‐fat diet, Nhe6KO
 mice displayed lower body weight and decreased fat accumulation in adipose tissue. By contrast, glycogen accumulation was strongly increased in liver of Nhe6KO
 mice on a high‐fat diet. Knockdown of NHE6 (NHE6 KD) in 3T3L1 adipocytes resulted in smaller lipid droplets, pointing to a cell autonomous role in fat accumulation. NHE6 KD adipocytes also exhibit dysregulation of insulin‐responsive uptake of fatty acids, glucose and Fe/transferrin, as well as the cell surface translocation of the corresponding transporters. Furthermore, we observed selective downregulation of key components of the insulin signalling pathway that could be restored using the Na+/H+ exchanger mimetic monensin or the V‐ATPase inhibitor bafilomycin, pointing to pH dysregulation as the underlying defect. These findings establish NHE6 as a novel contributor to energy metabolism with implications for CS patients.
 

## Key points

NHE6 is an endosomal Na+/H+ exchanger mutated in Christianson syndrome.

Nhe6 null mice accumulate less fat and weigh less than control animals.
In adipocytes, NHE6 knockdown reduces insulin‐stimulated fatty acid and glucose uptake.
NHE6 exerts proteostatic control over multiple components of the insulin signalling pathway.

Although BAT has low fat content, lipid droplets were clearly visible in tissue sections from WT mice (Fig.  ). By contrast, lipid droplets were small and scarce in BAT of Nhe6KO
 mice, as confirmed by quantification of the droplet area (Fig.  ). In white adipose tissue (WAT), the lipid droplet is large, unilocular and equivalent to adipocyte cell size as seen in representative H&E stains of inguinal and gonadal WAT (Fig.  ). WAT adipocyte size increases with obesity and is accompanied by comorbidities such as dyslipidemia and diabetes. Thus, it is noteworthy that lipid droplet size, on average, was significantly smaller in inguinal (Fig.  ) and gonadal WAT (Fig.  ) from Nhe6KO
 mice.
 Next, we considered whether the lower body weight in Nhe6KO
 mice could be compensated by a high‐fat diet (HFD) in which 60% of calories are derived from fat. Accordingly, 5‐month‐old mice were placed on HFD for 15 weeks and body weight was monitored weekly. Weight gain in control animals was significantly greater than in the Nhe6KO
 mice (Fig.  , inset), culminating in greater body weight and fat weight at 9 months (Fig.  ). Brown and white adipose tissue isolated from Nhe6KO
 animals weighed less than those from control (Fig.  ), as did the liver (Fig.  ). However, skeletal muscle and brain mass were not different (Fig.  ). H&E stains of brown and white fat (Fig.  ) showed significantly smaller lipid droplets (Fig.  ) in knockout mice. In summary, loss of NHE6 leads to a global decrease in fat accumulation that persists with HFD‐feeding.
 

## Nhe6 null mice show aberrant glycogen stores in liver and muscle
We considered whether the robust effects of NHE6 on body weight and fat mass were associated with changes in diet‐related glycogen storage and systemic glucose regulation. Adipose tissue is the major fat depot, whereas skeletal muscle and liver are responsible for the bulk of post‐prandial glucose clearance, which is stored as glycogen. H&E stains of skeletal muscle in both chow and HFD fed mice were similar in control and Nhe6KO
 animals (Fig.  ). To reveal glycogen content, tissues were stained with periodic acid‐Schiff in the absence (PAS) or presence of diastase (PASD). Glycogen stores appeared to be modestly reduced in skeletal muscle of Nhe6KO
 mice on HFD, relative to control animals (Fig.  ). H&E staining of liver was similar in both groups of animals in chow diet but revealed visibly reduced fat accumulation in Nhe6KO
 compared to control in HFD (Fig.  ). Unexpectedly, glycogen stores in liver of Nhe6KO
 animals showed diet‐dependent differences from control, being visually lower in chow but higher in HFD (Fig.  ). This reversal prompted the evaluation of serum glucose levels in both groups of animals as described in the Methods. Baseline fasted levels of serum glucose was significantly higher in Nhe6KO
 compared to control animals fed on chow (Fig.  ), but lower in HFD‐fed Nhe6KO
 mice (Fig.  ). Following i.p. injection, glucose clearance was similar in both Nhe6KO
 and control, but peak serum levels were lower in Nhe6KO
 animals on HFD. Although not substantially different, glucose handling showed diet‐dependent alteration in Nhe6KO
 animals that correlated with changes in liver glycogen.
 In liver, glucose transporter isoform 2 (GLUT2) is the glucose transporter that facilitates bi‐directional transport of glucose, enabling both glucose uptake for storage and release into the bloodstream. A HFD can significantly impact glycogen storage and GLUT2 expression (Jiang et al., ; Kim et al., ). We observed diet‐dependent changes in GLUT2 expression in liver tissue, with significant decrease in Nhe6KO
 under a HFD (Fig.  ). Downregulation of GLUT2 can impede both glycogen synthesis and mobilization, with severe deficiency leading to excessive liver glycogen in Fanconi–Bickel syndrome. Thus, our observations require more detailed investigation but point to a role for NHE6 in GLUT2 expression and liver glycogen storage.

## Differentiated adipocytes selectively upregulate Nhe6
To evaluate cell autonomous roles of NHE6 on lipid and glucose accumulation and insulin regulation, 3T3‐L1 fibroblasts were differentiated using an adipogenic cocktail (Zebisch et al., ). Successful differentiation into adipocytes was confirmed by Oil Red O staining of cells (Fig.  ) and mRNA induction of canonical adipocyte genes Slc2a4 (GLUT4), Adipoq (Adiponectin), Plin1 (Perilipin‐1) and Plin‐2 (Perilipin‐2) (Fig.  ). Adipocyte differentiation was accompanied by an almost three‐fold increase in NHE6 transcript (Fig.  ). Although the magnitude of NHE6 increase is not on the same scale as the adipocyte markers, the high rate of ion exchange (∼1500 ion s−1) associated with this class of transporter (Lee et al., ) means that small changes in expression can have large consequences in the limited microenvironment of the endosomal compartment. By contrast, transcript levels of the closely related NHE9 isoform decreased by ∼50% upon adipocyte differentiation (Fig.  ) suggesting an isoform‐selective role for NHE6 in adipocyte function.
 

## NHE6 knockdown reduces insulin‐dependent fat accumulation in adipocytes
We used either shRNA or siRNA to knockdown (KD) NHE6 in 3T3‐L1 cells, either before or after differentiation into adipocytes. In all cases, we confirmed that transcript levels of NHE6 decreased whereas NHE9 levels remained unchanged (Fig.  ). Similarly, expression of adipocyte markers was unchanged in NHE6 KD cells, except for a 1.5‐fold increase transcript levels for adiponectin, which is known to drive lipogenesis (Fig.  ). Despite the increase in adiponectin, Oil Red O staining of fat droplets was diminished in NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ). Electron microscopy confirmed that fat droplets were smaller and fewer in NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ). Thus, the in vivo findings from fat tissue in Nhe6KO
 mice were reproduced by NHE6 KD in 3T3‐L1 adipocytes, pointing to a cell autonomous effect.
Reductions in lipid content in NHE6 KD adipocytes could be the result of alterations in fatty acid uptake. Therefore, we monitored uptake of BODIPY‐labelled C12 fatty acid (2 µm for 15 min) in 3T3‐L1 adipocytes by confocal fluorescence microscopy. Mean fluorescence intensity in NHE6 KD was significantly diminished compared to control cells (Fig.  ). Insulin plays a crucial role in stimulating fatty acid uptake into adipocytes, driving the accumulation of fat. As expected, insulin treatment (100 nm for 20 min) increased fatty acid uptake in control adipocytes. However, fatty acid uptake in NHE6 KD adipocytes was not significantly elevated. Similar results were obtained by flow cytometry analysis of BODIPY C12 fluorescence. We observed time‐dependent fluorescence increase (15 min and 1 h) in both control and NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ). Cell associated BODIPY C12 fluorescence was lower for NHE6 KD, as seen by the left shift in peak distribution, with the difference increasing at the longer time point (Fig.  ). The percentage of cells showing high BODIPY C12 fluorescence (>2000 a.u.) was lower for NHE6 KD under all conditions (Fig.  ).
 CD36 is a scavenger receptor that shuttles between endosomes and the cell surface where it facilitates uptake of long chain fatty acid into adipocytes. The rate of fatty acid uptake is increased by insulin‐stimulated translocation of CD36 to the plasma membrane (Glatz & Luiken, ). We used a dual labelled construct of CD36 with exofacial V5 tag and cytoplasmic GFP (Fig.  ) transiently expressed in 3T3L1 adipocytes to quantify CD36 translocation in unpermeabilized cells, as previously reported (Chan et al., ). Basal levels of surface to total fluorescence ratio in tagged CD36 were similar in control and NHE6 KD cells (Fig.  ). Insulin treatment elicited cell surface translocation of CD36 in control cells but not in NHE6 KD, as seen by representative confocal images, as well as the V5/GFP fluorescence ratio (Fig.  ). In summary, fatty acid uptake is decreased and less responsive to insulin following the knockdown of NHE6.

## Insulin treatment increases NHE6 on the plasma membrane
A proteomic analysis of the adipocyte plasma membrane revealed NHE6 as a novel insulin‐responsive protein (Prior et al., ). As a first step toward probing the role of NHE6 in adipocytes, we compared the cellular localization of NHE6 under basal conditions and following insulin treatment. 3T3‐L1 cells with stable expression of NHE6 containing an external triple‐HA tag and cytoplasmic C‐terminal GFP marker (Fig.  ) were antibody labelled without permeabilization. NHE6 has been localized to early and recycling endosomes (Zhao et al., ), although a minor component may localize to the plasma membrane (Brett et al., ; Hill et al., ). Consistent with these findings, localization of NHE6‐GFP in 3T3‐L1 adipocytes was intracellular under basal conditions (Fig.  ). In the presence of insulin, there was a 2.5‐fold increase in cell surface NHE6 as revealed by punctate labelling of the extracellular HA epitope (Fig.  ). 3‐D visualization of confocal microscopy images reveals a small population of NHE6 that becomes surface membrane‐associated in response to insulin treatment (Fig.  ). The partial enrichment of NHE6 at the cell surface of insulin‐treated adipocytes that we observe closely replicates previous findings (Prior et al., ).
 

## NHE6 colocalizes with GLUT4
A consequence of insulin signalling is the insulin‐dependent increase of selected endosomal proteins at the cell surface (Larance et al., ; Piper et al., ; Tanner & Lienhard, ; Volchuk et al., ; Wardzala et al., ), most prominently the glucose transporter isoform 4 (GLUT4). In the absence of insulin, GLUT4 is distributed between endosomes, trans‐Golgi network, and a heterogeneous mix of endosomal sorting intermediates and specialized GLUT4 storage vesicles (Leto & Saltiel, ). We investigated co‐localization of NHE6 and GLUT4 under basal and insulin‐stimulated conditions in 3T3‐L1 adipocytes stably transfected with a cMyc‐GLUT4‐mCherry construct (Fig.  ) (Lim et al., ; Lorenzo & Bennett, ). We observed partial co‐localization of NHE6‐GFP and GLUT4‐mCherry signals in the basal state (Pearson's correlation = 0.32) (Fig.  ) consistent with a previous report (Prior et al., ). Following insulin treatment, the extent of co‐localization remained the same (Fig.  ). This finding is consistent with translocation of the co‐localized intracellular pool of NHE6‐GFP and GLUT4‐mCherry to the plasma membrane.

## NHE6 knockdown decreases insulin‐stimulated surface translocation of GLUT4
Previous studies in neurons and astrocytes showed that NHE6 regulates cell surface expression of transmembrane receptors and transporters (Ouyang et al., ; Prasad & Rao, ). In adipocytes, basal surface expression of Myc‐GLUT4‐mCherry, in the absence of insulin, is low both in control and NHE6 KD cells (Fig.  ). Treatment with insulin (100 nm for 30 min) elicited robust translocation of cMyc‐GLUT4‐mCherry to the plasma membrane in control cells; however, NHE6 knockdown drastically reduced GLUT4 surface expression relative to control (Fig.  ). Insulin‐independent translocation of GLUT4 in response to metformin (1 mm metformin for 24 h) was also reduced, albeit less drastically (Fig.  ). Metformin has been reported to enhance insulin sensitivity in adipocytes by increasing the surface expression of GLUT4. However, metformin did not enhance plasma membrane GLUT4 in NHE6 KD adipocytes treated with insulin, pointing to a common point of disruption in the GLUT4 translocation pathway (Fig.  ).
 Insulin enhances cell surface translocation of other membrane proteins, including the transferrin receptor (TfnR) (Tanner & Lienhard, ). Surface levels of TfnR under basal conditions, revealed by the binding of fluorescent transferrin (Tfn‐488) to non‐permeabilized adipocytes, were not significantly decreased in NHE6 KD cells, whereas insulin‐mediated increase was observed only in control cells (Fig.  ). Together, these findings indicate a broad requirement for NHE6 in the surface translocation of multiple insulin‐responsive proteins.

## NHE6 knockdown alters glucose uptake in adipocytes
The reduced insulin responsive translocation of GLUT4 in NHE6 KD adipocytes suggested defective activation of the insulin signal transduction pathway. The serine/threonine kinase Akt is a key effector of insulin‐stimulated GLUT4 translocation (Fig.  ) (Kohn et al., ). Akt activation alone is sufficient to stimulate GLUT4 translocation in the absence of insulin (Ng et al., ). We found that phosphorylated Akt (p‐AktSer473) levels were reduced by 67% in insulin‐treated NHE6 KD adipocytes without a change in total Akt protein levels (Fig.  ). We conclude that the activation of this branch of the insulin signalling pathway is impaired in the absence of NHE6.
 Insulin‐responsive 2‐deoxy‐d‐[1,2‐3H] glucose (2‐DG) uptake was monitored in control and NHE6 knockdown 3T3‐L1 adipocytes (Fig.  ). In control adipocytes, 2‐DG uptake was increased seven‐fold following acute treatment with insulin, as previously reported (Bryant et al., ). By contrast, only a two‐fold increase in 2‐DG uptake was observed in NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ), consistent with reduced insulin responsive signalling and surface expression of GLUT4 (Fig.  ). Unexpectedly, basal uptake of 2‐DG, in the absence of insulin, was increased four‐fold in NHE6 KD adipocytes. Because basal surface expression of GLUT4 was unaltered in these cells (Fig.  ), these observations suggest compensatory increase in other glucose uptake mechanisms. One probable mechanism may involve upregulation of the GLUT1 glucose transporter. Although mRNA levels of GLUT1 were unchanged (Fig.  ), protein expression of GLUT1 was increased in NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ). Thus, NHE6 has a selective effect on insulin stimulated surface trafficking of GLUT4. It is possible that other glucose transporters such as GLUT8 and SGLT2 (Scheepers et al., ; Xu et al., ) could also be upregulated in the absence of NHE6 and contribute to basal glucose uptake observed in 3T3L1 adipocytes.

## NHE6 regulates proteostasis in adipocytes
In addition to defective insulin signalling, the drastic reduction in surface expression of GLUT4 resulting from NHE6 knockdown could also be a result of reduced GLUT4 expression. We show that NHE6 KD 3T3‐L1 adipocytes express 44% less GLUT4 under basal conditions (Fig.  ) despite no significant change in GLUT4 transcript (Fig.  ). GLUT4 exists in discrete organellar pools within the cell, with ∼40% of the total residing in the endo‐lysosomal system (Bryant et al., ; Thong et al., ), suggesting that NHE6 regulates the itinerary of GLUT4 through this pool. NHE6 KD adipocytes also showed a 30% reduction in protein levels of the insulin receptor‐β (IR‐β) subunit (Fig.  ), even though transcript levels were increased by 24% (Fig.  ). Thus, reduction of GLUT4 and IR‐β proteins are a post‐transcriptional effect of NHE6 on the GLUT4 pathway.
 Next, we considered whether other membrane protein components of the GLUT4 storage vesicles (GSV) (Fig.  ) also show altered protein expression in response to NHE6 KD. One key component of GSVs is sortilin, a Vps10 domain containing protein that interacts with GLUT4 within the endosomal lumen and recruits components of the retromer (GGA1) and clathrin with its cytoplasmic tail (Shi & Kandror, ). Notably, GGA1 was reported to retain NHE6 in endosomes via an interaction with the NHE6 cytoplasmic tail (Ma et al., ). A proteomic analysis of GSVs revealed low density lipoprotein receptor‐related protein 1 (LRP1) to be another major, insulin‐responsive component (Jedrychowski et al., ). We found that NHE6 knockdown decreased protein levels of both sortilin (Fig.  ) and LRP1 (Fig.  ).
Fat accumulation requires uptake of long chain fatty acids through insulin‐regulated fatty acid transporters, including FATP1 in 3T3L1 adipocytes (Stahl et al., ). Here, we show that protein levels of FATP1 are reduced in NHE6 KD adipocytes (Fig.  ), consistent with reduced uptake of BODIPY C12 fatty acid shown in Fig.  . Taken together, our data suggest that NHE6 exerts proteostatic control over multiple components in the insulin responsive pathway in adipocytes.

## Chemical bypass of NHE6
The exchange of luminal protons for cations by the transport activity of NHE6 counters the proton pumping activity of the V‐ATPase to establish the pH balance of the endosome (Kondapalli et al., ; Prasad & Rao, ). Thus, loss of NHE6 results in hyper‐acidification of endosomal lumen by 1–2 pH units (Ouyang et al., ; Prasad & Rao, ). In adipocytes, NHE6 KD hyper‐acidified endo‐lysosomal compartments as indicated by increased acridine orange fluorescence (Brett et al., ) (Fig.  ). Treatment with the V‐ATPase inhibitor bafilomycin should abolish H+ pumping and alkalinize the endo‐lysosomal compartments. This is demonstrated by the disappearance of fluorescent puncta, resulting in diffused cytoplasmic distribution of acridine orange (Fig.  ).
 Lysosomal turnover of proteins requires acidic compartmental pH. Treatment with bafilomycin treatment restored insulin receptor (Fig.  ) and LRP1 protein (Fig.  ) in NHE6 KD adipocytes to levels similar to control, as seen by western blot analysis. The ionophore monensin is a chemical mimetic of Na+/H+ exchangers and has been shown to block lysosomal delivery of cargo and reroute proteins to the cell surface (Muro et al., ), effectively reversing the effect of NHE6 KD in many cell types (Ko et al., ; Prasad & Rao, , ). Addition of monensin (1 µm for 16 h) to NHE6 KD adipocytes was sufficient to restore insulin receptor IRβ (Fig.  ) and GLUT4 protein (Fig.  ) to levels similar to control. These findings point to the role of NHE6 in regulating proteostasis of the insulin‐responsive pathway.

## Discussion

## NHE6 selectively regulates trafficking and turnover of insulin‐responsive cargo
Endosomal pH plays a key role in regulating the trafficking and turnover of plasma membrane receptors and transporters. Our data point to multiple steps involved in adipocyte insulin‐stimulated glucose and fat uptake that are affected by loss of NHE6, as summarized by the schematic in Fig.  . Diminished activation of the insulin signalling pathway, as indicated by decreased phosphorylation of Akt, results in reduced exocytosis and delivery of cargo from the pool of insulin‐responsive storage vesicles to the plasma membrane, lowering uptake of glucose and fatty acids. Endosomal pH also directly impacts protein degradation by regulating the activity of luminal proteases and indirectly, by affecting trafficking to the lysosome. Hyper‐acidification of the endo‐lysosomal pathway, as a result of the loss of NHE6, enhanced the degradation of FATP1, GLUT4 and other components of GLUT4 storage vesicles such as LRP1 and sortilin. Consistent with the role of endo‐lysosomal pH, GLUT4 stability can be restored by agents that are known to alleviate hyperacidic compartmental pH (Prasad & Rao, , , ), such as monensin, a chemical mimetic for Na+/H+ exchange, and bafilomycin, a H+‐ATPase inhibitor, pointing to lysosomal turnover of cargo. We note that, in prior work, we have ruled out a role for the proteosome in endosomal NHE‐mediated protein turnover (Ko et al., ). The severe reduction in surface expression of GLUT4 in NHE6 KD adipocytes may be caused by a combined effect of reduced activation of the insulin signalling pathway (resulting from reduced expression of insulin receptor and Akt phosphorylation) and enhanced degradation of GLUT4 protein, although their relative contribution is not known. Intriguingly, NHE6 appears to selectively target the insulin responsive pathway, whereas levels of GLUT1 increased in the absence of NHE6, probably as compensation. We expect variable proteostasis susceptibility of vesicular cargo to NHE6 activity depending on the extent of co‐localization, compensatory effects of other pH regulators (such as NHE9 and PAC) and individual differences in protein half‐life. A comprehensive cataloging of proteins for which turnover could be altered by NHE6 may be required to better understand selectivity.
 In pre‐adipocytes, both ectopically and endogenously expressed GLUT4 localizes to endosomes with a half‐life of only 2 h (Shi & Kandror, ). However, in differentiated adipocytes, GLUT4 is sorted into small (<100 nm) GLUT4 storage vesicles that represent the major insulin‐responsive compartment. This not only increases its insulin responsiveness, but also protects GLUT4 from lysosomal degradation. What controls this proteostatic switch? We suggest that induction of NHE6 upon adipocyte differentiation counters the acidification of lumen pH by V‐ATPase and protects endosomal cargo from lysosomal degradation.

## NHE6 alters glucose handling and glycogen storage
Failure in insulin control of glucose transport is a chief component underlying insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes (Garvey et al., ; Klip et al., ). GLUT4 gene variants are rare (Stockli et al., ); therefore, understanding GLUT4 proteostasis could be key to our knowledge of metabolic disorders. GLUT4 expression is reduced in obese, prediabetic, insulin resistant and diabetic individuals (Cignarelli et al., ; Klip et al., ) and, based on the results from the present study, may be impacted by expression and activity of NHE6.
Biguanide drugs such as metformin are widely used to treat diabetes and work, in part, by increasing glucose uptake into adipose tissue (Kim & You, ). Specifically, metformin increases insulin‐independent plasma membrane expression of GLUT4 in adipocytes under basal conditions (Grisouard et al., ). Intriguing findings from worm and fly models show that eNHE mutants are resistant to metformin, probably through dysregulation of autophagy (Kim et al., ). Our observations revealed that metformin does not restore surface expression of GLUT4 in NHE6 knockdown adipocytes. Thus, similar to observations in C. elegans, NHE6 may be required for the ameliorating effect of metformin on GLUT4 in mammals. Identifying the factors that influence GLUT4 expression and trafficking could lead to a better understanding of insulin resistance in diabetes which is essential for more effective management of this disease.
In mouse models, heterozygous knockout of GLUT4 and conditional deletion in adipose tissue or skeletal muscle results in insulin resistance and propensity to develop diabetes (Abel et al., ; Rossetti et al., ). Intriguingly, fat‐specific insulin receptor knockout mice exhibit increased lean body mass and protection from glucose intolerance (Bluher et al., ; Minokoshi et al., ; Stenbit et al., ). Given our observation that adipocyte NHE6 KD decreased both GLUT4 and insulin receptor abundance, conferring insulin resistance at a cellular level, the glucose handling in whole body NHE6 knockout animals is difficult to interpret. Nhe6 null mice have lower body weight relative to control mice, but the same lean mass. Because mice were dosed per total body weight, Nhe6 knockout mice receive effectively less glucose than WT mice per lean mass. This may mask glucose intolerance, particularly in chow‐fed mice. Lack of a clear effect on glucose clearance could also point to opposing phenotypes from downregulation of both GLUT4 and insulin receptor in Nhe6KO
 animals and may require tissue‐specific knockouts and more careful analysis of glucose and insulin tolerance to clarify the underlying mechanisms.

## Implications for CS
The Nhe6KO
 mouse model of CS has been a useful tool to understand many of the pathophysiological effects of NHE6 loss. Similar to patients, the model shows a combination of neurodegenerative and neurodevelopmental features, including delayed brain growth and Purkinje cell loss (Xu et al., ), altered nociceptor excitability and hyposensitivity to pain (Petitjean et al., ). Except for a report on skeletal abnormalities (Schnyder et al., ), there is a lack of research on non‐neuronal phenotypes that could inform on CS pathology. In this context, our observations relating to age‐dependent fat accumulation defects in the Nhe6KO
 mouse phenocopy the age‐dependent low body mass index comorbid with CS (Christianson et al., ; Gilfillan et al., ; Schroer et al., ), and reveal potential problems with insulin responsiveness, fat and glucose handling in patients. In part, these defects are cell autonomous, and the underlying dysregulation of proteostasis probably contributes to lower fat accumulation. However, tissue‐specific dysregulation of glucose or fat uptake (e.g. in skeletal muscle or intestine) may also be regulated by NHE6 and warrants further investigation. Another example relates to the CNS, where GLUT4 is enriched at active synapses to support energy demands during sustained activity (Ashrafi et al., ). Therefore, a question of interest is whether NHE6 regulates glucose uptake at active synapses.
In conclusion, the present study makes the case for further physiological studies of NHE6 in non‐neurological systems, in line with the ubiquitous tissue distribution and broad functional role of endosomal Na+/H+ exchange. We show data leading to a testable hypothesis of a mechanism linking endosomal pH to glucose and energy metabolism in adipocytes, offering new insights into obesity and insulin resistance that should spur additional studies in the future.
