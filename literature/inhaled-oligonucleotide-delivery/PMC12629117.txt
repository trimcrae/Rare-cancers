CKAP4 (cytoskeleton-associated protein 4), a type II transmembrane protein, is mainly located in the endoplasmic reticulum and plasma membrane, and CKAP4 has been identified as a receptor for tPA (tissue-type plasminogen activator), SP-A (surfactant protein A), APF (antiproliferative factor), alginate exopolysaccharides, and DKK (Dickkopf) 1 protein. CKAP4 is involved in various physiological functions, including cell proliferation, cell migration, and stabilizing the structure of the endoplasmic reticulum. It has also been reported that CKAP4 is a protumor molecule to promote tumor progression in various cancers including lung, pancreatic, esophageal, and renal tumors. In addition to its role in cancer, recently, CKAP4 has also been implicated in cardiac remodeling. CKAP4 was upregulated in activated ventricular fibroblasts after ischemic injury and implying that CKAP4 is a marker for ventricular fibroblast activation. 
In a previous study, we found that CKAP4 plays a pivotal role in tryptase-induced atrial fibroblast proliferation, migration, and collagen synthesis. To further investigate the role of CKAP4 in atrial fibrosis and AF vulnerability, we use a CKAP4 knockdown mouse model of atrial fibrosis and found that CKAP4 promotes atrial fibrosis and enhances AF vulnerability by wingless/INT signaling pathway (WNT)/β-catenin pathway. Revealing the CKAP4/WNT/β-catenin pathway in the pathogenesis of atrial fibrosis and AF may identify new risk markers and therapeutic targets to prevent and treat AF and associated complications.

## Methods
The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

## Patients
Between December 2022 and December 2023, we prospectively enrolled 268 patients: 189 with drug-refractory AF undergoing catheter ablation and 79 control subjects undergoing ablation for routine supraventricular tachycardia or ventricular arrhythmias. The study cohort comprised 148 men and 120 women, aged between 18 and 85 years. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Tongji Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine (Approval No. (Tong) Ke Shen [2022-009]), and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects.
The patients’ characteristics are summarized in Table S1.

## Assessment of Serum CKAP4 Concentration and Degree of Atrial Fibrosis
Blood samples were collected upon admission and centrifuged within 30 minutes to separate plasma, which was immediately stored at −80 °C. Serum CKAP4 levels were measured using a human CKAP4 ELISA kit (Jingmei Biotechnology, Yancheng, China; Cat. JM-5854H1, 96 tests) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Each patient sample was assayed in triplicate, and the mean of valid replicates was used for analysis. Atrial fibrosis was assessed by a 3-dimensional electroanatomic mapping system (Carto) as described previously. , Voltage mapping was performed in sinus rhythm for all participants. In patients presenting with AF during the procedure, electrical cardioversion was performed after completion of pulmonary vein isolation, and the voltage map was subsequently obtained in sinus rhythm.

## Single-Cell and Single-Nucleus RNA-Seq Data Processing
We processed the raw count matrices from GSE238242 (human left atrial [LA] appendage, single-nucleus RNA-seq, and snRNA-seq) and GSE224959 (human atrial tissue, single-cell RNA sequencing, and enriched for atrial stromal cells) in parallel using Seurat. Cells/nuclei with low gene counts or high mitochondrial/ribosomal content were removed, and genes detected in <5 cells were excluded. After log-normalization and scaling, the most variable genes were selected for principal component analysis. We retained the top 40 principal components for graph-based clustering (resolution=0.8) and visualized the results in 2 dimensions by t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE). Clusters were annotated by known marker genes. CKAP4 expression was then overlaid on the embedding and quantified across cell types.

## Correlation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Pathway Enrichment Analysis
Left atrial appendage expression matrices from patients with AF were retrieved for 3 data sets (GSE79768, GSE41177, and GSE115574). For each data set, the Spearman rank correlation was computed between CKAP4 and all other genes using cor.test in R. Genes with ρ>0.5 and P<0.05 were considered significantly positively correlated and carried forward. Each resulting gene list was subjected to KEGG pathway enrichment analysis using the clusterProfiler R package, with the set of all expressed genes in that data set as background and the Benjamini-Hochberg method for multiple testing correction (adjusted P<0.05).

## Mice
Specific pathogen-free male C57BL/6J mice (8 weeks old, 20±2 g) were purchased from Shengchang Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China. Before experimentation, the mice were acclimatized to laboratory conditions (23 °C, 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle, 50% humidity, with ad libitum access to food and water) for 1 week. Mice were housed at a density of one per cage and were deprived of food for 24 hours before euthanasia by cervical dislocation.
All animal studies were approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Tongji Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine (Approval No. 20230626100). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Tongji Hospital and the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the National Institutes of Health (8th Edition, NRC 2011).

## Mouse Model of Atrial Fibrosis by Angiotensin II Infusion
Using the previously described methods, , mice were anesthetized intraperitoneally with pentobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg) and subcutaneously implanted with an osmotic minipump (Alzet 2004, United States) to infuse Ang II (750 ng/kg per min) for 28 days to induce atrial fibrosis in mice. As a control, saline was given to the mice at the same rate.

## Mouse Model of Atrial Fibrosis by Transverse Aortic Constriction
Transverse aortic constriction was performed as described. , In brief, mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane inhalation and maintained with 1% to 2% isoflurane by endotracheal intubation and a respirator. A 2 to 3-mm longitudinal cut was made in the proximal portion of the sternum, which allowed visualization of the aortic arch. The transverse aortic arch was ligated between the innominate and left common carotid arteries with an overlaying 27-gauge needle, followed by needle removal, leaving a discrete region of constriction. Sham-operated mice underwent the same procedure without aortic constriction.

## Echocardiography in Mice
Cardiac function was evaluated by transthoracic echocardiography using the Vevo 2100 (VisualSonics Corp, Toronto, Canada) equipped with an MS400 (30-MHz) transducer. During the procedure, mice were first anesthetized with isoflurane (1.5%) in oxygen (1 L/min), and then, the mice were restrained on a 37 °C plate without isoflurane to avoid a low heart rate. A parasternal long-axis image was acquired with appropriate positioning of the scan head so that the maximum left ventricular length could be identified, and LA size was assessed and measured at end-systole. Echocardiographic measurements were averaged from at least 3 separate cardiac cycles.

## AF Induction in Mice
Mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (3% for induction and 1.5%–2% for maintenance) while spontaneously breathing and positioned supine on a heating pad, and the body temperature was kept at 37 °C. Surface ECGs were obtained by subcutaneous placement of 27-gauge needles in the limbs and continuously recorded using an amplifier (AD Instruments) and LabChart 8 software. An octapolar 2F catheter electrode was inserted through the esophageal opening and positioned for appropriate atrial capture using a programmable stimulator, followed by measurement of atrial diastolic capture threshold. Standard clinical electrophysiological pacing protocols were used to induce AF as previously described. , Bipolar atrial pacing was performed at twice the threshold amplitude with a pulse width of 2 ms. To test AF susceptibility, burst atrial pacing was conducted for 15 s at 6 different cycle lengths (50, 40, 30, 25, 20, and 15 ms). A recovery time of 30 s was allowed between all pacing trains. AF was defined as a rapid, irregular rhythm lasting with irregular R-R intervals >1 s. The duration of AF from the end of the pacing train to the first regular atrioventricular conduction P-wave after fibrillation termination was measured, calculating the number of times and total duration of induced AF for each animal. If an AF episode persisted for 10 minutes, the procedure was terminated.

## Collection of Mouse Tissue and Histological Study
Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane and euthanized via cervical dislocation. Hearts were extracted quickly and dipped once in clean saline solution to remove excess blood. For immunoblot and Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qPCR) experiments, LAs were separated from the ventricles and right atria, stored in tubes, and flash frozen in liquid nitrogen. For histology, atrial tissues were dipped into another container with clean saline solution, fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for at least 24 hours, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4-µm thickness using a microtome. Immunohistochemistry was performed according to our previously described protocol. Picrosirius red and Masson trichrome staining was performed for the detection of collagen fibers in atrial tissue.

## Isolation and Culture of Rat Atrial Fibroblasts
Atrial tissue was separated from the hearts of male neonatal Sprague-Dawley rats (2–3 days old), and rat atrial fibroblasts were isolated and cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) as previously described by our group. , In brief, rats were euthanized by cervical dislocation and immersed in 75% alcohol for 10 seconds. The hearts were excised, thoroughly rinsed 3× to 4× with chilled PBS to remove blood, and the atria were minced into ≈1 mm³ tissue pieces. The cardiac tissues were then suspended in 0.125% trypsin and transferred to centrifuge tubes maintained in a 37 °C water bath for 10 minutes. After digestion, the supernatant and sediment were transferred into new centrifuge tubes. To halt digestion, DMEM with 20% FBS was added to the tube containing supernatant, and 0.125% trypsin was added to the sediment tube. After 10 minutes of oscillation digestion at 37 °C, repeated digestion steps were performed using trypsin; 2.5 mL of 0.125% trypsin and 2.5 mL of 0.125% collagenase II were added, and the resulting mixtures were transferred into new centrifuge tubes. This process was repeated 3×. After digestion, digestion was halted by adding Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) with 20% FBS to the tube containing the supernatant and centrifuging at 12 000g for 10 minutes at 4 °C. The pellet was resuspended in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and cultured at 37 °C for 60 minutes. Rat atrial fibroblasts were then isolated by differential adhesion and were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Second to third passage rat atrial fibroblasts were used for experiments.

## Immunohistochemistry and Immunofluorescence
Rat atrial fibroblasts were seeded onto coverslips placed in 6-well plates. After overnight adhesion, the cells were treated with Ang II (angiotensin II) at 1 μM for 24 hours to induce a fibrosis model. si-CKAP4 was then administered to evaluate its effect on fibrosis. The coverslips were washed 3× with ice-cold PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. Cells were incubated in PBS containing 0.1% Triton X-100 (Amresco) and subsequently stained overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies in 1% BSA. After washing 3× with PBS, secondary antibodies were added and incubated at 37 °C for 60 minutes. Cells were treated with ProLong Gold antifade reagent containing 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (Invitrogen) and imaged using a tricolor immunofluorescence microscope.

## Cell Migration Assay
The migration of rat atrial fibroblasts was assessed by the Transwell chambers and the scratch assay. A Transwell chamber (24-well, Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd., China) was used. The cells (1.5×104) were seeded in the upper chamber in serum-free DMEM medium. After incubation at 37 °C for 24 hours, the cells were removed from the upper chamber, and the cells were stained with 0.25% crystal violet for 15 min after fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde. The cells were counted under a microscope, and 5 random images were taken of each chamber.
For the scratch assay, primary rat atrial fibroblasts were seeded at a density of 5.0×104 cells/mL in 6-well plates. Once the cells reached 90% confluence, a single scratch was made using a sterile 200-μL pipette tip. Different markers were drawn at the bottom of the plate to ensure continuous monitoring of cell migration within the same field of view. Migration speed was quantified using ImageJ software, version 1.8.0.

## Flow Cytometry
Rat atrial fibroblasts were digested with trypsin and neutralized with serum-containing medium, centrifuged to discard the supernatant, and resuspended in precooled PBS for washing. Cells were fixed in 70% ethanol at 4 °C for 2 hours and then stained in the dark for 30 minutes with a solution containing propidium iodide (0.05 mg/mL), RNase A (1 mg/mL), and 0.3% Triton X-100. The percentage of cells in different phases of the cell cycle was measured using a flow cytometer, and data were analyzed with ModFit software (Becton Dickinson, United States).

## Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction Assay
Total RNA was extracted from mouse atrial tissues and cultured rat atrial fibroblasts using TRIzol (Takara, Dalian, China) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Prime-Script RT reagent kit (Takara). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction was performed using specific primers (Table S2) and Premix Ex Taq (probe qPCR), normalized to GAPDH expression. Samples were run on an ABI 7500 detector (Applied Biosystems) using standard polymerase chain reaction conditions (95 °C for 30 seconds, followed by 95 °C for 5 seconds and 60 °C for 34 seconds).

## Western Blotting Assay
Proteins were extracted from mouse atrial tissues and cultured rat atrial fibroblasts using RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors, and the protein concentration was detected and quantified by bicinchoninic acid protein assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS/PAGE and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. Blots were blocked with 5% nonfat milk in Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST) buffer at room temperature for 1 hour and then incubated with primary antibodies as specified. Blots were further incubated with horseradish peroxidase–conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 hour, followed by visualization using enhanced chemiluminescence reagent (ECL, Promega, United States). Densitometric analysis was performed using ImageJ software.

## Co-Immunoprecipitation Assay
Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) was conducted using the Classic Magnetic Protein A/G IP/Co-IP Kit as per the manufacturer’s instructions (Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.). Cells were first washed with cold PBS and then lysed on ice for 10 minutes with immunoprecipitation lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors (500 μL). Centrifugation was performed at 12 000 rpm for 20 minutes to obtain the supernatant, with a small aliquot collected for protein blot analysis (input group). To form antigen-antibody-magnetic bead complexes, corresponding primary antibody (10 μL) and pretreated protein A/G magnetic beads were added to the remaining supernatant and incubated on a rotator at room temperature for 2 hours. Under the influence of a magnetic rack (Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.), the antigen-antibody-bead complexes were settled at the bottom of the tube and subsequently washed 4× with lysis buffer. The complexes were mixed with 60 μL of 1×SDS-PAGE sample buffer and heated at 100 °C for 10 minutes. After the magnetic beads were drawn to the bottom of the centrifuge tube, the supernatant was collected for further SDS-PAGE analysis.

## Proximity Ligation Assay
Rat atrial fibroblasts were seeded on coverslips in 48-well plates at a density of 28×103 cells/cm2. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 minutes, washed with PBS, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 3 minutes, and then blocked with 2% BSA in PBS for 0.5 to 1 hour. The cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies at appropriate concentrations. Immunofluorescence was performed using the Duolink in situ Fluorescence kit (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Images were acquired with a confocal imaging system (Andor) and analyzed using ImageJ and GraphPad Prism software.

## Statistical Analysis
Data are presented as mean±SEM. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to check for normality of data distribution. Two-group comparisons were made using a 2-tailed Student t test, while multiple group comparisons were performed using ANOVA followed by a Tukey post hoc test with the software SPSS 17.0 (SPSS, United States). All experiments were repeated at least 3×, and a P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## The Serum Levels of CKAP4 Were Increased in Patients With AF and Associated With Left Atrial Fibrosis
We first evaluated the expression of CKAP4 in patients with AF (Table S1). ELISA analysis indicated that the expression level of CKAP4 in the peripheral blood of patients with AF was significantly higher compared with patients with sinus rhythm, indicating a potential involvement of CKAP4 in the pathophysiological processes underlying AF.
Subsequently, we investigated the association between serum CKAP4 levels and the degree of LA fibrosis, characterized by atrial scar percentage, in patients with AF. Correlation analysis revealed significant positive correlations between serum CKAP4 and regional scar percentages, including posterior wall (r=0.27; P<0.0001), anterior wall (r=0.16; P=0.02), apical region (r=0.27; P=1.2×10−4), isthmus (r=0.29; P=3.7×10−5), inferior wall (r=0.17; P=0.02), and total scar percentage (r=0.21; P=3.0×10−3; Figure S1A through S1G). These findings suggest that elevated CKAP4 levels may contribute to AF through promoting atrial fibrosis, highlighting CKAP4 as a potential biomarker for atrial fibrosis and structural remodeling in patients with AF.

## Single-Cell Analysis Reveals Fibroblast-Specific CKAP4 Upregulation in AF
To investigate the cell-type specificity and AF-associated regulation of CKAP4, we analyzed 2 human single-cell RNA-sequencing data sets. GSE238242 profiles LA appendage tissue using single-nucleus multiome analysis, while GSE224959 focuses on the stromal and immune landscape of AF using freshly isolated atrial samples.
Unsupervised clustering identified major cardiac cell types in both data sets (Figure S2A and S2F). CKAP4 was predominantly expressed in fibroblasts, as shown by feature plots highlighting CKAP4+ cells mainly in the fibroblast cluster (Figure S2C and S2H) and dot plots confirming that fibroblasts had the highest average expression and percentage of CKAP4-expressing cells (Figure S2D and S2I). Furthermore, stratified analysis revealed that CKAP4 expression in fibroblasts was significantly elevated in AF compared with sinus rhythm or control conditions in both data sets (Figure S2E and S2J). These findings, derived from 2 independent high-resolution human data sets, suggest that CKAP4 is selectively expressed by fibroblasts and upregulated in AF, supporting its potential role in atrial structural remodeling.

## Profibrotic Stimuli Upregulate CKAP4 In Vivo and In Vitro Fibrosis Models
To extend our findings implicating CKAP4 in atrial fibrosis, we examined CKAP4 expression in experimental mouse and cellular models derived from neonatal rat atrial tissue that was subjected to profibrotic stimulation. First, atrial fibrosis was induced in mice by transverse aortic constriction surgery or by chronic Ang II infusion via a subcutaneous minipump. The extent of LA fibrosis was examined by Picrosirius red and Masson trichrome staining. The expression of CKAP4 in LA tissue was detected by immunohistochemistry. Mice experiencing pressure overload stress from transverse aortic constriction or treated for 4 weeks with Ang II minipump infusion showed a significant increase in LA CKAP4 expression compared with control mice (Figure S3A and S3B).
Second, rat atrial fibroblasts were cultured as described , (Figure A), and dose-response experiments identified 1 μM as the optimal concentration to induce fibrotic activation , (Figure S3C and S3D). After being stimulated with Ang II (1 μM) for 24 hours, Western blotting and immunofluorescence analysis showed a significant increase in the expression of α-SMA, collagen I, collagen III, and vimentin in cultured rat atrial fibroblasts, as demonstrated in Figure (Figure B through G). The expression of CKAP4 in atrial fibroblasts was significantly increased after incubation with Ang II for 24 hours (Figure H). In contrast, Ang II treatment did not significantly affect CKAP4 expression in rat H9C2 cardiomyocytes (a rat cardiac myoblast cell line) (Figure S3E), suggesting that CKAP4 upregulation is specific to fibroblasts under profibrotic conditions.
 

## CKAP4 Promotes the Proliferation, Migration, and Collagen Synthesis in Cultured Rat Atrial Fibroblasts
Activation of fibroblasts and enhanced collagen synthesis are important features of atrial fibrosis. To demonstrate a causal association between CKAP4 and atrial fibrosis, cultured rat atrial fibroblasts were used, and cellular fibrosis was induced by Ang II; then, CKAP4 was knocked down or overexpressed.
First, we treated rat atrial fibroblasts with CKAP4-siRNA. As demonstrated in Figure A, cultured rat atrial fibroblasts were effectively transferred by CKAP4-siRNA. In addition, the expressions of CKAP4 were significantly decreased in CKAP4-siRNA–treated cells compared with controls (Figure B). Western blotting and immunofluorescence analyses revealed that the inhibition of CKAP4 decreased the expression of vimentin, collagen I, and collagen III in Ang II-stimulated rat atrial fibroblasts (Figure C through E). Moreover, the wound-healing assay demonstrated that the inhibition of CKAP4 significantly reduced the migration ability of Ang II-treated rat atrial fibroblasts (Figure F and G). Flow cytometry with PI staining also indicated that knockdown of CKAP4 could effectively inhibit the cell cycle progression of rat atrial fibroblasts promoted by Ang II (Figure S4).
 To further confirm the effect of CKAP4 on atrial fibrosis, the cultured rat atrial fibroblasts were treated with a CKAP4-Overexpression (OE) plasmid (Figure S5A). Similar to Ang II stimulation, overexpression of CKAP4 also increased the expression levels of collagen I and collagen III in rat atrial fibroblasts (Figure S5B and S5C). Moreover, the overexpression of CKAP4 enhanced the migration ability of rat atrial fibroblasts, and this effect was synergistically augmented with Ang II stimulation (Figure S5D).

## CKAP4 Knockdown Decreases Left Atrial Volume, Attenuates Atrial Fibrosis, and Reduces the Vulnerability of AF in a Mice Model of Atrial Fibrosis
Ang II infusion is the most commonly utilized approach to study atrial fibrosis in vivo. , To elucidate the influence of CKAP4 on atrial fibrosis and AF, mice were continuously infused with Ang II, and then, CKAP4 was knocked down using adeno-associated virus 9 vectors with a Flag tag. Immunofluorescence staining and qPCR showed significant expression of Flag protein and effective knockdown of CKAP4 in the atria of mice following tail vein injection of adeno-associated virus 9 (Figure S6A).
On the 28th day of infusion, echocardiography was performed. We observed an Ang II-induced enlargement in the LA cavity compared with controls, while the LA diastolic area was significantly decreased in CKAP4 knockdown mice (Figure A). Masson and Sirius Red staining demonstrated that the extent of atrial fibrosis increased in Ang II-infused mice, whereas the extent of atrial fibrosis was significantly decreased in CKAP4 knockdown mice compared with mice in the Ang II infusion group (Figure B and C). In addition, Western blot analysis showed that Ang II-infused mice exhibited higher expression levels of α-SMA, vimentin, Col1 (collagen type I), and Col3 (collagen type III) proteins, which were downregulated by CKAP4 knockdown (Figure S6B through S6E). Our results indicated that CKAP4 plays a pivotal role in atrial fibrosis and atrial remodeling.
 Next, to determine whether the attenuated atrial fibrosis induced by CKAP4 knockdown was associated with a lower threshold for AF, we conducted in vivo cardiac electrophysiological studies. Due to the absence of spontaneous arrhythmias throughout the electrophysiological examination, we used the transesophageal atrial burst pacing protocol, a widely used pacing protocol to induce AF (Figure A). After pacing, Ang II-infused mice showed a significantly increased susceptibility to AF, manifested as increased numbers of episodes and prolonged durations, compared with saline infusion. In contrast, mice in the CKAP4 knockdown group exhibited significantly attenuated AF episodes and durations, indicating that CKAP4 knockdown attenuated the susceptibility to AF (Figure B and C).
 

## CKAP4 Activates Rat Atrial Fibroblasts and Enhances the Vulnerability of AF via WNT/β-Catenin Signaling Pathway
We demonstrated that the expression of CKAP4 was increased in the LA tissue of mice and in Ang II-stimulated cultured rat atrial fibroblasts, and we also found that CKAP4 promotes atrial fibrosis and enhances the vulnerability of AF in mice. To unravel the mechanism behind CKAP4 promoting atrial fibrosis in AF, we retrieved transcriptomic sequencing data of the LA appendage from patients with AF from the Gene Expression Omnibus database. Based on the median value of CKAP4 expression in patients with AF, we divided them into high and low CKAP4 expression groups and identified DEGs between these groups. Subsequent enrichment analysis revealed that these DEGs were enriched in the WNT signaling pathway (Figure S7A).
Literature screening revealed that among WNT subfamily members, WNT1, WNT2, WNT3A, WNT4, WNT5A, and WNT11 are expressed in cardiac fibroblasts. – Therefore, we initially used immunoprecipitation to investigate which WNT proteins interact with CKAP4 in the fibrosis model. IP experiments demonstrated that only WNT3A and WNT5A interacted with CKAP4 in Ang II-stimulated rat atrial fibroblasts (Figure S7B). To further elucidate whether CKAP4 interacts with WNT3A or WNT5A, we performed Co-IP and proximity ligation assay in primary rat atrial fibroblasts isolated from neonatal rats. Co-IP demonstrated significant interactions between CKAP4 and both WNT3A and WNT5A (Figure A and B). Proximity ligation assay further confirmed these results (Figure C and D). Our results demonstrated that CKAP4 may exert its regulatory role through the WNT signaling pathway.
 β-Catenin is the central mediator of the WNT pathway. To further elucidate the regulatory effect of β-catenin on atrial fibrosis, the specific β-catenin/TCF pathway inhibitor and agonist SKL2001 were used to treat Ang II-stimulated cultured rat atrial fibroblasts and mice. As expected, β-catenin expression was increased in Ang II-stimulated rat atrial fibroblasts and was reduced when CKAP4 was knocked down (Figure A). Treatment with the β-catenin agonist SKL2001 reversed the decrease in collagen synthesis (Col1/Col3) and migration capacity caused by CKAP4 knockdown in rat atrial fibroblasts (Figure B through D).
 Similarly, β-catenin expression was also elevated in rat atrial fibroblasts overexpressing CKAP4 (Figure A), and the enhanced collagen synthesis and migration capacity induced by CKAP4 overexpression were suppressed by the β-catenin/TCF pathway inhibitor (Figure B through D). These findings indicate that the canonical WNT signaling pathway mediated by WNT/β-catenin is the key mediator of CKAP4-induced atrial fibrosis.
 To confirm the effect of β-catenin on atrial fibrosis and the vulnerability of AF in vivo, Ang II-infused CKAP4 knockdown mice were treated with β-catenin agonist SKL2001. Echocardiographic measurements revealed that the LA diastolic area was significantly increased in Ang II-infused CKAP4 knockdown mice (Figure A). Masson and Sirius Red staining demonstrated that atrial fibrosis was enhanced in SKL2001-treated CKAP4 knockdown mice (Figure B and C). In addition, Western blot analysis revealed that SKL2001 reversed the reductions in β-catenin, Col1, and Col3 expressions caused by CKAP4-KD (Figure D through F). After burst pacing stimulation, the addition of SKL2001 to the Ang II-infused CKAP4 knockdown mice showed a significantly increased susceptibility to AF compared with Ang II-infused CKAP4 knockdown mice (Figure B and C).

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrate that (1) serum levels of CKAP4 are increased in patients with AF and are associated with the extent of LA fibrosis; (2) the expression level of CKAP4 is found to be elevated in the mouse model of atrial fibrosis, as well as in cultured rat atrial fibroblasts; (3) CKAP4 promotes rat atrial fibroblasts migration, proliferation, and collagen synthesis; (4) CKAP4 knockdown attenuates LA fibrosis and reduces the vulnerability of AF in mice model of atrial fibrosis; and (5) as the downstream target, WNT/β-catenin pathway is required for the augmentation effect of CKAP4 in promoting atrial fibrosis and enhancing AF vulnerability. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate the essential role of CKAP4 in the pathogenesis of atrial fibrosis and AF. Our work reveals a previously unrecognized and important pathway of the CKAP4/WNT/β-catenin signaling axis cascade in the atrium that enhances atrial fibrosis to promote AF vulnerability.
CKAP4, a transmembrane protein, has been identified to be a cell surface receptor for tissue-type plasminogen activator, surfactant protein A, antiproliferative factor, alginate exopolysaccharides, and DKK protein. CKAP4 has been recognized as a serological marker of several tumors, and CKAP4 is expected to be a tumor therapeutic target. , , Besides its role in tumors, recent studies showed that CKAP4 played an important role in cardiovascular disease. Lyu et al found that CKAP4 participated in maintaining the integrity of endothelial cell adhesion junctions. Shi et al demonstrated that CKAP4 contributes to the progression of vascular calcification by modulating Yes-associated protein (YAP) phosphorylation and matrix metalloproteinase 2 MMP2 expression. Using single-cell sequencing, CKAP4 was identified as a novel marker for activated fibroblasts, and CKAP4 positively correlated with known myofibroblast markers in diseased hearts of murine and human. In our previous study, we demonstrated that knockdown of CKAP4 inhibited tryptase-induced atrial fibroblast proliferation, migration, and collagen synthesis. Here, we extend our previous results and show that CKAP4 promotes atrial fibroblasts migration, proliferation, and collagen synthesis and aggravates the extent of atrial fibrosis and atrial remodeling. CKAP4 can promote atrial fibrosis that forms the arrhythmogenic substrate for AF. Atrial fibrosis disrupts conduction between cardiomyocytes and generates regions of local conduction block and conduction heterogeneities, thereby favoring reentry and perpetuation of arrhythmias. , , Our results demonstrated that Ang II-infused mice showed a significantly increased susceptibility to AF, whereas attenuation of atrial fibrosis by CKAP4 knockdown was associated with a lower threshold for AF.
The WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway is a highly conserved pathway that plays crucial roles in various biological processes such as embryonic development, organogenesis, neurological development, inflammation, and heart development and regeneration. , The WNT signaling is closely related to myocardial fibrosis. – In this study, to clarify the role of the WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway in CKAP4-induced atrial fibrosis, first, we analyzed the data obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database and found that these DEGs were enriched in the WNT signaling pathway. Subsequently, we used Co-IP and proximity ligation assays and found that CKAP4 binds to WNT3A and WNT5A in Ang II-stimulated rat atrial fibroblasts.
β-Catenin is the central effector of the WNT signaling pathway and functions as both a component of the adherens junction, where it links cadherins to the cytoskeleton, and as a transcriptional activator. , Bai et al demonstrated that the accumulated β-catenin is associated with human atrial fibrosis and AF, whereas inhibiting β-catenin prevents the development of atrial fibrosis and AF. In this study, we have demonstrated that CKAP4 promotes atrial fibrosis and enhances the vulnerability of AF in culture rat atrial fibroblasts or a mouse model of atrial fibrosis, whereas the specific β-catenin inhibitor or agonist abolishes the profibrotic and proarrhythmic effects of CKAP4. Taken together, our results suggest that CKAP4 promotes atrial fibrosis and enhances the vulnerability of AF through the WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway.

## Limitations
There are some limitations to this study. First, in correlation analyses, we used raw P values rather than multiple testing–adjusted values due to small sample sizes, as adjustment would markedly reduce statistical power; findings require cautious interpretation and validation in larger cohorts. Second, our Co-IP and proximity ligation assay experiments confirm that CKAP4 can form a complex with WNT5A or WNT3A, but the specific binding sites of CKAP4, WNT5A, and WNT3A need further exploration. Besides, the WNT signaling pathway is a highly complex signal transduction pathway as it contains 19 different WNT proteins and other ligands, , and further studies were warranted to elucidate the role of other WNT proteins in CKAP4-induced atrial fibrosis. Finally, all of our experimental studies were conducted with rodent cells and animal models; therefore, potential differences in fibroblast signaling between human and rodent tissues should be considered when interpreting our findings.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, our results demonstrated for the first time that CKAP4 and WNT/β-catenin mediated signaling are fundamental to the progression of atrial fibrosis and the vulnerability of AF, thereby participating in the pathogenesis of AF. CKAP4 knockdown suppressed susceptibility to AF, which may be a novel target for the treatment of AF.
