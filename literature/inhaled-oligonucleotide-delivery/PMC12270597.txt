## Abstract
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a common arrhythmia in clinical. Its most important pathophysiological factor is atrial fibrosis. Transferrin receptor (TFRC) promotes ferroptosis by
facilitating iron uptake. Its role in AF is unknown. TFRC expression in Angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced AF mice was significantly upregulated. TFRC knockdown significantly reduced AF
occurrence. TFRC silence ameliorated myocardial fibrosis by inhibiting transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)/Smad2 pathway in vivo. TFRC interference reduced ferroptosis by
inhibiting lipid oxidation product generation in vivo. Ang II-induced HL-1 cardiomyocyte model was employed to simulate an in vivo situation. The in
vitro results were consistent with the in vivo results. Forkhead box O3 (FOXO3) was reported to protect atrium against fibrosis and participate in ferroptosis.
FOXO3 exerted transcriptional repressive activity by binding to TFRC promoter. FOXO3 overexpression protected HL-1 cells against ferroptosis, which was reversed by TFRC overexpression. In
summary, TFRC knockdown reduces AF occurrence by ameliorating atrial fibrosis through inhibiting cardiomyocyte ferroptosis under FOXO3 regulation.
Keywords: atrial fibrillation, atrial fibrosis, ferroptosis, forkhead box O3, transferrin receptor

## Introduction
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is one of the most common arrhythmias, and more than 33 million people worldwide suffer from arrhythmia, whose prevalence is expected to more than double in the next
40 years [ ]. AF is associated with major adverse cardiovascular events, such as heart failure, stroke, and myocardial infarction [ ]. AF increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, decreases quality of life, and increases mortality [ ]. Despite pharmacologic and
surgical treatments, more than 80% of patients continue to experience debilitating symptoms of AF [ ]. Studies have found that atrial structural
remodeling is the main pathological change in AF, while its main manifestation is atrial fibrosis [ ]. Therefore, clarifying the mechanism of myocardial
fibrosis in AF is of great clinical significance for treating AF. Ferroptosis is a type of cell death mainly mediated by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation [ ]. Recent studies have shown that ferroptosis aggravates liver fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, renal fibrosis, and myocardial fibrosis in AF, and targeted inhibition of ferroptosis
helps to inhibit tissue fibrosis [ , , , , ]. Ferroptosis is an attractive therapeutic target in myocardial fibrosis [ ].
Transferrin receptor (TFRC) encodes transferrin receptor protein 1 (TFR1), which promotes cellular iron uptake by importing iron from the extracellular environment into the cell and exerts a
role in promoting ferroptosis [ , ]. Knockdown of TFRC inhibited ferroptosis in rat cardiomyocytes [ ]. Mice lacking TFRC in the heart died during the second week of life and developed cardiomegaly, poor cardiac function, failure of mitochondrial
respiration, and ineffective mitophagy [ ]. Heterozygous TFRC deletion in mice ameliorated unilateral ureteral obstruction-induced renal fibrosis and
decreased the expression of ferritin, 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), TGFβ receptor type 1 (TGFβR1), and Smad2 in the kidney tissues [ ]. Therefore, we
conjectured that TFRC may be important in involving cardiac fibrosis in AF.
Forkhead box O3 (FOXO3) is a member of the FOXO transcription factors, which effectively represses the transcription of downstream genes [ ]. It has
been reported that FOXO3 was significantly downregulated in the cardiac tissues of patients with AF, and the upregulation of FOXO3 was involved in the protective effect of quercetin against
isoprenaline-induced myocardial fibrosis [ ]. FOXO3 overexpression remarkedly reversed the promotional effect of miR-122 on cardiomyocyte hypertrophy
[ ]. In addition, FOXO3 attenuated the inflammatory response by inhibiting ferroptosis [ ]. We hypothesized
that TFRC exerts effects in FOXO3-mediated cardiac fibrosis in AF.
To verify the above conjecture, we conducted a preliminary investigation on the TFRC expression in patients with AF and animal AF models in a public database. Further validation was performed
in the Angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced mouse AF model. We found that TFRC was significantly upregulated in the cardiac tissues of AF mice. The function of TFRC in AF mice and its effects on
atrial fibrosis and ferroptosis were explored by inhibiting TFRC expression in mouse hearts using viral vectors. Subsequently, a model of Ang II-induced mouse cardiomyocyte (HL-1 cells) was
also established to explore whether the effect of TFRC on ferroptosis was regulated by FOXO3.

## Materials and Methods

## Bioinformatics analysis
The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was utilized to
retrieve the AF-related datasets and download expression profiling data. The differential expression genes (DEGs) of these datasets were defined as genes with absolute
log2Foldchange >0.67 and P-value <0.05. The Venn diagram was used to show the intersection of DEGs. Gene ontology (GO) annotation was performed with the
mouse genome as background. Ferroptosis-related targets were collected and screened (criteria: relevance score >4; category=protein coding) via the Genecards database (https://www.genecards.org/). The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was available from the STRING database
(https://string-db.org/) and visualized using Cytoscape 3.7.1 software.

## Reagent
Ang II (A9290) was purchased from Solarbio (Beijing, China). For immunohistochemistry (IHC), 4-HNE antibody (Bioss, Beijing, China, bs-6313R), and goat anti-mouse IgG-horseradish peroxidase
(HRP) (ThermoFisher, Pittsburgh, PA, USA, #31460) were used. For western blot, TFRC antibody (Zenbio, Chengdu, China, 381603), transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) antibody (Zenbio,
250346), p-Smad2 antibody (Zenbio, 310079), Smad2 antibody (Zenbio, 200790), acyl-CoA synthetase long chain family member 4 (ACSL4) antibody (Zenbio, R24265), Glutathione Peroxidase 4 (GPX4)
antibody (Zenbio, 381958), solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) antibody (Zenbio, R26116), goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (Solarbio, SE134), goat anti-mouse IgG-HRP (Solarbio, SE131), and
GAPDH antibody (Zenbio, 380626) were used.

## Animal study
The flow chart of the animal experiment was shown in . Healthy male C57BL/6 mice at the ages of 8 weeks were obtained and randomly divided into 4 groups. TFRC shRNA and negative control shRNA were synthesized and inserted into the
pAV-U6-shRNA-CMV-RFP vector (labeled with red fluorescence) for virus packaging. The targeting sequence for TFRC shRNA and negative control shRNA were indicated below:
5′-ccgggaggagacagacaagtcagattcaagagatctgacttgtctgtctcctccttttt-3′ (TFRC shRNA); 5′-cccttctccgaacgtgtcacgtttcaagagaacgtgacacgttcggagaattttt-3′ (negative control shRNA). The 200
µl 5 × 1011 viral genome (per mouse) adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9) was delivered by tail vein injection [ ]. Mice in
the control and AF groups were injected with the same volumes of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). After 2 weeks of virus injection, mice were implanted with a subcutaneous miniosmotic pump
under isoflurane anesthesia (3% isoflurane for induction and 1.5% isoflurane for maintenance of anesthesia) for continuous delivery of Ang II (3 mg/kg/day) for 3 weeks to establish a mouse
model of AF [ ]. Next, AF was induced using burst atrial pacing of the right atrium in anaesthetized mice (isoflurane inhalation), and the surface
electrocardiogram (ECG) was recorded to detect AF [ ]. Five consecutive atrial burst pacing sessions were performed in each mouse to calculate the AF
induction rate (the number of times AF was induced/the number of times burst pacing was performed). AF duration was defined as the time interval between the initiation of AF and its
spontaneous termination. At the end, mice were euthanized under deep anesthesia by exsanguination and the left atrial tissue was collected for subsequent experiments. To confirm virus
delivery to the atrium of mice, red fluorescence intensity was observed in left atrial tissues under a fluorescence microscope.
 Mice were adaptive feeding for one week before the experiment. All mice were housed in a specialized facility with an ambient temperature of 20–26°C and a relative humidity of 40–60%. They
had free access to food and water. All animal experiments including the euthanasia method were approved by the Animal Experiment and Experimental Animal Welfare Committee of the Capital
Medical University (AEEI-2023-325) and followed the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

## Histological examination
Mouse left atrial tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 µm thick.

## Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining
The left atrial tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene, and hydrated with decreasing concentrations of ethanol (95%, 85%, and 75%). Then, after staining with hematoxylin (Solarbio)
for 5 min, tissues were stained with eosin (Sangon, Shanghai, China) solution for 3 min. Dehydration was performed using a series of ethanol (75%, 85%, and 95%), followed by incubation with
xylene to make them transparent, and mounted. The sections were observed under a microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) and photographed.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
After tissues were dewaxed and hydrated, antigen retrieval solution (1.8% 0.1 M citric acid and 8.2% 0.1 M sodium citrate, in distillated water of pH 6.0) was added to the tissues for
antigen retrieval. Sections were incubated with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 min to eliminate endogenous peroxidase activity. Then, 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) was used to block the
sections for 15 min. Tissues were incubated with primary antibody (1:100 dilution) in a humidified box at 4°C overnight, followed by incubation with HRP-labeled secondary antibody (1:500
dilution) at 37°C for 1 h in a humid chamber. Next, the chromogenic substrate diaminobenzidine (DAB) was used, and hematoxylin was used for counterstaining. Dehydration, transparency, and
sealing were done afterward. Finally, an Olympus microscope was used to observe sections and take photos.

## Masson staining
After routine deparaffinization and hydration, the left atrial tissue sections were stained with ponceau fuchsin dye for one minute and differentiated with 1% phosphomolybdic acid aqueous
solution for 5 min, followed by staining with aniline blue solution for 5 min without washing with water. Then, ethanol dehydration, xylene transparency, sealing, and examination under a
microscope were conducted.

## Prussian blue staining
Iron accumulation and distribution were determined by Prussian blue staining performed using a Prussian blue kit as described below. Tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene and
rehydrated in ethanol. A Prussian blue kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) was used to stain the tissue following the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequently, the tissues were then dehydrated
in ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted using neutral gum. The sections were observed and taken photos under a microscope (Olympus).

## Transmission electron microscope (TEM)
For TEM, the tissue samples were fixed with the electron microscope fixative (Servicebio) and stored at 4°C. Then the tissues were post-fixed in 1% osmic acid solution and dehydrated using
increasing alcohol concentrations. Then, an acetone/812 embedding agent (SPI Pharma, Wilmington, NC, USA) was used to embed samples. Thin (60–80 nm) sections were cut using an ultramicrotome
(Leica, Nussloch, Germany) and stained with 2% uranyl acetate saturated alcohol solution, followed by 2.6% lead citrate. Finally, the observation of mitochondrial ultrastructure was done
under a transmission electron microscope (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan).

## Cell culture
Mouse cardiomyocytes (HL-1 cells) were obtained from iCell. HL-1 cells were plated on cell culture dishes or well plates pre-coated with 0.02% (v/v) gelatin containing 5
µg/ml fibronectin and cultured in MEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum at 37°C in an incubator containing 5% CO2. Three siRNAs targeting TFRC (siTFRC#1,
siTFRC#2, and siTFRC#3) and the negative control siRNA (siNC) were synthesized. The sequences of siTFRCs were CGUGCUACUUCUAGACUAA (#1), GGAUCUUUAGUGAUUGUUA (#2), and GGAUAUGGGUCUAAGUCUA
(#3). The FOXO3 and TFRC CDS fragments were subcloned into pCDNA3.1 (+) to generate the overexpression plasmid, respectively. siRNAs/plasmids were transfected into HL-1 cells using a
prepared transfection reagent lipo3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) after the cells adhered to the wall. After transfection for 24 h, cells were incubated with 1 µM Ang
II for 24 h [ ], followed by the collection of the cells for further experiments.

## Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
CCK-8 cell proliferation detection kit (KeyGEN, Nanjing, China) was used for the CCK-8 assay. In brief, cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 5 × 103/well, transfected with
corresponding siRNA/plasmid for 24 h, and incubated with 1 µM Ang II for 24h. Cell viability was tested by adding 10 µl of CCK-8 reagent to each well and
incubating for 2 h in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37°C. Then, a microplate reader (Biotek, Winooski, VT, USA) was used to evaluate the OD value 450 nm.

## Western blot (WB) assay
RIPA lysis buffer (Solarbio) was used to extract the total protein from left atrial tissue samples and HL-1 cells. Then, the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method was performed to measure the
total protein concentration. For electrophoresis, a loading buffer (Solarbio) was added to dilute samples and heated in a boiling water bath for 5 min, and then 20 µl sample
solution was loaded onto 5% stacking gel and 10% or 12% separating gel. Proteins were then transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). For immunoblotting, membranes were
blocked using a blocking solution (Solarbio) for one hour at room temperature. Then membranes were incubated with primary antibodies diluted (Solarbio) in antibody diluent at 4°C overnight.
The concentrations of primary antibodies used were as follows: TFRC (1:500), TGF-β1 (1:500), p-Smad2 (1:500), Smad2 (1:500), ACSL4 (1:500), GPX4 (1:500), SLC7A11 (1:500), and GAPDH
(1:8,000). Subsequently, membranes were incubated for one hour at 37°C with HRP-labeled secondary antibodies with a concentration 1:3,000 in antibody diluent. After extensive washing, the
proteins were visualized by ECL western blotting substrate (Solarbio), and the membrane was then exposed to film in a darkroom. Tanon Image processing software was used to analyze the
optical density value of the target band.

## Real-time quantitative PCR (Rt-qPCR) assay
The total RNA of mouse left atrial tissues and HL-1 cells were extracted by using TRIpure reagent (BioTeke, Beijing, China) and reverse-transcribed using the All-in-One First-Strand
SuperMix (Magen Biotechnology, Guangzhou, China). For Rt-qPCR, SYBR Green (Solarbio) was used for gene expression analysis. The mRNA expression was normalized to mouse
Gapdh. The relative mRNA expression was calculated by the comparative threshold cycle method (2-∆∆Ct method). Primers used were listed as follows: mus Tfrc
F-CGCTCGTGGAGACTACTTC, mus Tfrc R-AGATACATAGGGCGACAGG, mus Col1a2 F-CCTAGCAACATGCCAATAT, mus Col1a2 R-CAGCAAAGTTCCCAGTAAGA, mus Col3a1 F-TGCCCACAGCCTTCTACACCT, mus Col3a1
R-ATAGCCACCCATTCCTCCCAC, and mus Gapdh F-CTGCACCACCAACTGCTTAGC, mus Gapdh R-CATCCACAGTCTTCTGGGTG.

## Lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) measurement
After the corresponding treatment, cells were collected and incubated with 2 µM C11-BODIPY581/591 (Maokang biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for 20 min. Next, cells were
washed with PBS two times, and fluorescence was detected by flow cytometry (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) after resuspending the cells.

## Glutathione (GSH) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX) measurement
Mouse left atrial tissues were homogenized in an ice water bath and centrifuged (2,500 rpm) for 10 min, followed by taking the supernatant for further measurement. HL-1 cells were lysed by
sonication and resuspended in PBS for further measurement. The BCA method was conducted for protein concentration determination. Then, the GSH levels and GSH-PX activities were determined by
a reduced GSH assay kit (A006-2) or GSH-PX assay kit (A005-1), and calculated according to the protocol provided by the supplier (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing,
China).

## Dual luciferase assay
HL-1 cells were co-transfected with the PGL3-enhancer vector containing different lengths of the TFRC promoter sequence (−760 ~ +35 and −1760 ~ +35) and FOXO3 overexpression plasmid or
control vector. Dual luciferase assay was performed using the dual luciferase reporter gene assay kit (KeyGEN). Firefly luciferase values were normalized to renilla luciferase activity and
plotted as relative luciferase activity of TFRC promoter (−760 ~ +35) + Vector group.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay
ChIP assay was conducted using the ChIP Assay Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). HL-1 cells were incubated with formaldehyde at a final concentration of 1% at 37°C for 10 min to make proteins
cross-linked to DNA. Samples were lysed, and DNA was sheared by sonication. A portion of the supernatant was used as the input DNA, and the remainder was used for immunoprecipitation with
FOXO3 antibody (Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA, 10849-1-AP) or IgG antibody. The purified DNA was used as the template for qPCR amplification using the Taq PCR MasterMix and specific
primers. The ChIP results were normalized to the input sample.
The enrichment values were normalized to the input sample. Primers used were listed as follows: Primer 1 F-tgggctacatgagatcaag, R-tcctctcccatctccccga, and Primer 2 F-tttttttttgccataagat,
R-ggaggttcttgctgtacag.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.5. The statistical data were presented as mean ± SD, and the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or unpaired
t-tests was used to evaluate statistical differences between groups. P<0.05 was used to define statistical significance.

## Results

## TFRC expression in AF models accessed by the GEO datasets
To find potential therapeutic targets for AF, we analyzed DEGs from the GEO database for three different species of AF datasets (GSE41177 for Homo sapiens; GSE126711 for
Rattus norvegicus; GSE121516 for Mus musculus). As shown in the venn diagram ( ), six intersection genes were screened for the following analysis. The main pathological change of AF is atrial fibrosis, while ferroptosis has been reported to contribute to atrial
fibrosis [ ]. We found that these intersection genes were involved in “cellular response to iron ion”, “iron ion transport”, and “cellular iron ion
homeostasis” ( ). We then found TFRC, the only gene related to ferroptosis, by taking the intersection of the six common genes and
ferroptosis-related genes ( ). TFRC gene expression was significantly increased in AF patients and rats/mice with AF accessed by the GEO
datasets ( ).
 

## TFRC interference reduces the incidence of AF in Ang II-induced mice
The flow chart of the animal experiment was shown in . AF inducibility in mice was assessed by burst pacing in the right atrium. The surface
ECG of mice ( ) showed that AF was induced in mice injected with Ang II, as evidenced by a fibrillatory baseline in the ECG and irregular RR
intervals. This result implied that the AF mice model was successfully established. As detailed in , Ang II increased the inducibility
of AF and AF duration in mice. In contrast, TFRC knockdown significantly suppressed the occurrence of AF. It was observed that the interference of TFRC inhibited Ang II increased
cardiomyocyte size and cardiomyocyte loss in mice left atrium ( ). These results suggested that the knockdown of TFRC inhibited mice AF. We
observed red fluorescence in the left atrial tissue of mice, indicating that the virus entered the left atrial tissue of mice ( ). The mRNA
and protein expression of TFRC assayed by Rt-qPCR ( ) and WB ( ) was upregulated in AF mice
left atrial tissues. This finding was consistent with the TFRC expression trend in GEO datasets. The knockdown efficiency of TFRC was verified by Rt-qPCR and WB ( ).

## TFRC interference reduces atrial fibrosis in Ang II-induced mice
Since atrial fibrosis is the major pathological change of AF, we explored the effect of TFRC knockdown on atrial fibrosis in AF mice by detecting a series of fibrosis markers. In masson
staining ( ), TFRC interference decreased the collagen deposition (blue staining) induced by Ang II. The interference of TFRC decreased the relative mRNA expression of Col1a2
(type I collagen) and Col3a1 (type III collagen) ( ). During atrial fibrosis, binding of TGF-β1 to the receptor
TGF-βRII promotes phosphorylation of its downstream transcription factor Smad2 followed by collagen synthesis [ , ]. We found that TFRC knockdown inhibited the protein expression level of TGF-β1 and the phosphorylation level of Smad2 in the left atrium of AF mice ( ).
 

## TFRC interference inhibits ferroptosis in the atrium of Ang II-induced mice
Iron is essential for ferroptosis [ ]. The Prussian blue staining ( ) showed that TFRC knockdown decreased the iron distribution and accumulation in the left atrium of Ang II-induced mice. It was observed that Ang II-induced mitochondrial morphological
changes and increased 4-HNE staining, which were the manifestations of ferroptosis. In contrast, TFRC interference promoted the restoration of mitochondrial morphology and inhibited the
4-HNE staining in AF mouse left atrial tissues ( ). Ferroptosis is mediated by iron-dependent lipid peroxide accumulation [ ], and GSH-PX utilizes GSH to detoxify lipid peroxidation [ ]. The GSH content and GSH-PX activity in the left
atrium of AF mice were significantly inhibited, whereas the knockdown of TFRC recovered their levels ( ). As visualized by the PPI
network ( ), ACSL4, GPX4, and SLC7A11 occupied the main position among these ferroptosis-related proteins, and TFRC interacts with the three
key proteins. After TFRC silencing, the ACSL4 protein expression was downregulated, and the protein expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4 was upregulated in the left atrial tissue of AF mice ( ).
 

## TFRC interference alleviates Ang II-induced mouse HL-1 cell damage, ferroptosis, and fibrosis
To test the role of TFRC in the HL-1 mouse cardiomyocyte cell line, TFRC-targeting siRNAs or a control siRNA were transfected into HL-1 cells. We verified the knockdown effectiveness of the
siRNA via Rt-qPCR and WB in HL-1 cells and found siTFRC#3 was the best ( ). Therefore, siTFRC#3 was further utilized to inhibit the expression of TFRC in cells. The mRNA and protein expression levels of TFRC were notably increased in Ang II-induced HL-1
cells ( ). The CCK-8 results showed that Ang II increased HL-1 cell death, while TFRC interference reversed this effect ( ). The knockdown efficiency of TFRC was verified by Rt-qPCR and WB ( ). In HL-1 cells treated with Ang II, TFRC
interference inhibited the lipid-ROS levels ( ), and increased the GSH contents and GSH-PX activities ( ). The relative protein expression level of ACSL4 was inhibited and relative GPX4 and SLCA11 expression were increased in Ang II-simulated HL-1 cells after TFRC knockdown ( ). In addition, the TFRC silence inhibited Col1a2 and Col3a1 relative mRNA expression in Ang II-induced HL-1
cells ( ).
 

## TFRC interference inhibits ferroptosis in Ang II-induced mouse HL-1 cells under the regulation of FOXO3
It has been reported that the FOXO3 expression was significantly inhibited in the heart tissue of AF patients, and the upregulation of FOXO3 participated in preventing isoprenaline-induced
myocardial fibrosis [ ]. In addition, FOXO3 reduced inflammatory response by inhibiting ferroptosis [ ].
Therefore, we explored the relationship between FOXO3 and TFRC in HL-1 cells. Dual luciferase assay showed that FOXO3 overexpression remarkedly inhibited TFRC reporter activity, and the
longer TFRC gene promoter (−1760 ~ +35) exhibited lower luciferase activity ( ). To consolidate this result, the ChIP experiment was performed to validate the binding of FOXO3 at the promoter regions of TFRC ( ).
Next, we found that FOXO3 overexpression notably increased the cell viability of HL-1 cells exposed to Ang II, while the overexpression of TFRC significantly counteracted this effect ( ). Quantitative analysis in showed that TFRC mRNA and protein expression was markedly decreased
in the FOXO3-overexpressing HL-1 cells under Ang II conditions. Importantly, FOXO3 overexpression notably decreased the lipid ROS generation and increased the GSH content and GSH-PX activity
in Ang II-induced HL-1 cells ( ). However, these effects were reversed by the TFRC overexpression. These results indicated that FOXO3
played a role in inhibiting Ang II-induced HL-1 cell damage and ferroptosis by suppressing the transcriptional activity of TFRC.
 

## Discussion
In the study, we investigated the effects of TFRC on AF, atrial fibrosis, and ferroptosis, and explored its mechanism of action. We selected multiple datasets related to AF in the GEO
database to analyze its DEGs and obtained potential hub genes of AF by taking intersections of DEGs. Iron homeostasis plays a part in the function of the heart, and both iron deficiency and
iron overload are harmful to the heart [ ]. We found that these hub genes might be related to biological processes such as cellular response to iron
ions, iron ion transport, and cellular iron cardiomyocyte ferroptosis ion homeostasis. Atrial fibrosis is one of the most prominent pathological changes in AF [ ], and ferroptosis, which is caused by iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation, is closely associated with myocardial fibrosis in AF [ ]. We
identified the gene of interest, TFRC, by taking the intersection of potential hub genes of AF with ferroptosis-related genes.
Ferroptosis differs from other types of cell death such as apoptosis, and it is characterized by the accumulation of iron-dependent ROS and lipid peroxidation [ ]. TFRC is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein that binds to transferrin and mediates endocytosis of iron-binding transferrin, promoting intracellular iron uptake [ ]. TFRC increased ferroptosis [ ] and is a specific ferroptosis marker [ ].
TFRC has been widely used as a target for antibody-mediated cancer therapy [ ]. In addition, the TFRC deletion played an important role in the
progression of obstructive nephropathy and diabetic kidney disease by inhibiting renal fibrosis [ ]. In the study, we reported that TFRC knockdown
inhibited the occurrence of AF, atrial fibrosis, and ferroptosis in mice with AF.
The surface ECG of AF mice is characterized by an irregular atrial rhythm without clear P waves combined with irregular RR intervals that lasted at least 1 s [ ]. It can be observed that the AF model was successfully constructed through the surface ECG results. TFRC was significantly upregulated in the atrial tissues of humans, rats, and
mice with AF by analyzing GSE41177, GSE126711, and GSE121516 expression profiles. A consistent TFRC expression trend was observed in the left atrium of Ang II-induced AF mice in our study.
After induction of AF by atrial burst pacing, we found that TFRC knockdown significantly reduced AF inducibility and AF duration in mice. Furthermore, TFRC was involved in influencing pressure
overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy [ ], and cardiomyocyte hypertrophy was also one of the pathological features of AF. TFRC knockdown reduced Ang
II-induced increase in cardiomyocyte size in the left atrium. We concluded that TFRC knockdown in mouse atrial tissues inhibited AF occurrence.
Masson staining was used to stain collagen fibers and evaluate atrial fibrosis. In the study, TFRC interference inhibited the deposition of collagen. The major collagen types in cardiac
fibrotic tissue were types I and III collagen [ ]. TFRC knockdown inhibited the types I (Col1a2) and III (Col3a1)
collagen mRNA expression in AF mouse atrial tissues. TGF-β1 is the most relevant profibrotic factor in myocardial fibrosis and is produced by multiple cell types including cardiomyocytes and
fibroblasts [ , ]. The binding of TGF-β1 to its receptor TGFβR1 activates the downstream phosphorylation of
Smad2 and promotes the synthesis of profibrotic proteins such as collagen [ ]. In the study, TFRC interference significantly inhibited the TGF-β1/Smad2
pathway activation in mice atrial tissues. In conclusion, the knockdown of TFRC in the atrial tissues of AF mice inhibited atrial fibrosis.
The occurrence of ferroptosis is iron-dependent, and we found that TFRC knockdown inhibited iron accumulation and distribution in mouse myocardial tissue. Mitochondrial morphological changes
and lipid peroxidation are manifestations of ferroptosis [ ]. 4-HNE is the most abundant cytotoxic lipid oxidation product, whereas GSH and GSH-PX
defend against lipid peroxidation [ ]. TFRC knockdown inhibited ferroptosis by promoting restoration of mitochondrial morphology in mouse atrial tissues
while decreasing the production of lipid peroxides and improving the GSH content and GSH-PX activity. In addition, TFRC knockdown inhibited ferroptosis by modulating the levels of several key
proteins associated with ferroptosis (ACSL4, GPX4, and SLC7A11) in atrial tissue. These results suggested that TFRC attenuated atrial fibrosis by inhibiting ferroptosis.
During the occurrence of ferroptosis, excess iron increases the production of ROS [ ], and the excessive ROS triggers lipid peroxidation and damages
the cell membrane, leading to cardiomyocyte death [ ]. On the other hand, iron-induced oxidative stress triggers the inflammatory response. Both
oxidative stress and inflammatory response exacerbate myocardial cell injury, and promote the remodeling of the extracellular matrix in the myocardium, driving myocardial fibrosis [ ]. Ferroptosis repressor xCT (SLC7A11 gene-encoded plasma membrane) alleviated angiotensin II-induced cardiac fibrosis and pathological cardiac remodeling
by inhibiting ferroptosis [ ]. It was reported that the inhibition of ferroptosis by ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1, a ferroptosis inhibitor) alleviated
myocardial fibrosis [ , ]. These studies indicated that ferroptosis is an attractive therapeutic target in
myocardial fibrosis. Considering that more severe forms of atrial fibrosis occur when fibrous tissue replaces zones of dead cardiomyocytes [ ], we
focused on forestalling cardiomyocyte death. We, therefore, conducted mechanistic studies in cardiomyocytes in vitro. The knockdown of TFRC has been reported to inhibit
ferroptosis in rat cardiomyocytes [ ]. This finding was in harmony with our study. In mouse cardiomyocytes stimulated with Ang II, TFRC interference
attenuated ferroptosis (manifesting as the reduction in lipid ROS content and improved ability to resist lipid peroxidation). Cardiomyocytes can promote myocardial fibrosis by producing
collagen and affecting collagen deposition [ ]. Our results were comparable to the results of Chen et al. [ ] that types I and III collagen mRNA expression were increased in Ang II-induced mouse cardiomyocytes (HL-1 cells). We found that TFRC interference inhibited collagen mRNA
levels in cardiomyocytes in vitro, which contributed to the inhibition of myocardial fibrosis.
FOXO3, a transcriptional repressor, has been reported to be significantly downregulated in cardiac tissues of AF patients and its upregulation was involved in the protective effect against
myocardial fibrosis [ ]. Importantly, it was also involved in the process of ferroptosis [ ]. We speculate that
FOXO3 is somehow related to TFRC during AF. It was found that FOXO3 binds to the promoter of TFRC through the prediction by using the Jaspar database. This was further confirmed by the results
from the dual-luciferase reporter and ChIP assays in HL-1 cells. The overexpression of FOXO3 protected HL-1 cells against Ang II-induced cell death, inhibited lipid ROS generation, and
promoted GSH and GSH-PX production. These effects were reversed by TFRC overexpression, suggesting that TFRC knockdown exhibited the inhibition of ferroptosis regulated by FOXO3 in
cardiomyocytes. We speculated that in cardiomyocytes in vivo, the involvement of TFRC in the progression of atrial fibrosis was also regulated by FOXO3. However, one
limitation was that the relationship between TFRC and FOXO3 has not been verified in vivo. Ultimately, this hypothesis will need to be further explored in future research.
In summary, our research shows that TFRC knockdown reduces the occurrence of AF by ameliorating atrial fibrosis through inhibiting cardiomyocyte ferroptosis. It is observed that TFRC
interference inhibited cardiomyocyte ferroptosis under the FOXO3 regulation. Our study provides a promising potential therapeutic target for the treatment of AF.
