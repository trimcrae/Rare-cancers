## Abstract
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) ranks as the third most common contributor to global mortality. Oxidative stress has been recognized as a critical driver of multiple interacting mechanisms in COPD development. This research investigated the potential of oxidative stress-related genes (OSRGs) biomarkers and their potential molecular mechanisms for COPD clinical diagnosis and treatment through bioinformatics analyses. As a result, 5 hub genes, CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, and ECT2, were yielded by LASSO, Boruta, and SVM-RFE, and the performance of the nomogram constructed based on hub genes was favorable. Correlation analyses between hub genes and oxidative stress biomarkers showed that MMP9 and MAPT genes had a high association with oxidative stress biomarkers. Immune cell infiltration identified follicular helper T cells, Γδ T cells, M0 macrophages, and CD8 T cells as significantly different in COPD. ROC of ECT2 and MMP9 showed a higher capability to discriminate COPD patients from normal samples. In addition, we collected clinical samples and analyzed the core gene expression, which revealed that the hub genes ECT2 and MMP9 had high discriminatory ability in the COPD samples. The epistasis of ECT2 and MMP9 was further verified by constructing animal models, pathological sections, qPCR, immunoblotting, immunohistochemistry, etc. The data indicated the crucial function of MMP9 in CSC-induced oxidative stress injury. Deprivation of MMP9 attenuated CSC-induced injury and promoted macrophage polarisation to M2 macrophages. MMP9 deprivation protected against CSC-induced injury, mainly related to the reduction of cell apoptosis, cell inflammation, and ROS injury in BEAS-2B. It promoted macrophage polarization from M1 to M2. In summary, we found ECT2 and MMP9 are related to oxidative stress in COPD, and MMP9 was related to cell apoptosis, cell inflammation, and ROS injury in BEAS-2B, and the macrophage polarization from M1 to M2.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent chronic illness and a major contributor to global mortality (Ryan et al. ; Christenson et al. ). It is marked by persistent airway inflammation triggered by exposure to harmful particles or gases. This leads to limited airflow and is associated with chronic bronchitis and emphysema (Ryan et al. ; Willemse et al. ). Smoking is the most decisive factor of COPD among all risk factors; 25% of persistent smokers eventually develop COPD(Postma et al. ; Spilling et al. ). Repeated exposure to about 4,000 chemicals in cigarette smoke (CS) can create a cycle of chronic cellular damage and repair. This causes notable modifications in the structure, gene expression, and functionality of alveolar epithelial cells, causing small airway lesions and alveolar destruction (Crystal et al. ; Salvi ; Varraso and Shaheen ). Available research has demonstrated correlations between COPD pathogenesis and inflammation (Barnes ; Brightling and Greening ), oxidative stress (Barnes ; Kirkham and Rahman ), protease-antiprotease imbalance(Hlapčić et al. ), and enhanced senescence of the structural cells (Barnes et al. ). Current pharmacologic treatments for COPD mainly apply antibiotics and hormones to reduce the inflammatory response, including phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors such as roflumilast and inhaled contortionists (Kahnert et al. ; Rosenberg and Kalhan ). However, the existing mainstream drugs are not effective in stopping disease development and lung function decline (Guo et al. ). Therefore, understanding the underlying mechanisms and identifying new targets for COPD treatment is essential.
The disbalance of antioxidants and oxidants causes oxidative stress (Ansari et al. ). It is generated during the metabolism and immune response, or stimulated by external oxidative stressors, with excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Yang et al. ). The lung is very susceptible to oxidative stress and, therefore, has many antioxidant defenses (Kirkham and Rahman ). Oxidative stress is an essential driver of multiple interacting molecular mechanisms in COPD pathogenesis (Barnes ; McGuinness and Sapey ). Evidence indicates increased oxidative and carbonyl stresses in COPD because of exposure to air pollution and CS, and endogenous ROS production in the lung (Barnes ; Kirkham and Rahman ). Oxidative stress accelerates lung senescence, exacerbates chronic inflammation, inflicts DNA damage, contributes to corticosteroid resistance, and stimulates fibrosis, emphysema, and the formation of autoantibodies (Neofytou et al. ; Gonçalves and Romeiro ). The antioxidants that can generate glutathione, such as N-acetylcysteine (Wang et al. ), carbocysteine (Nucera et al. ; Li et al. ), and erdosteine (Lei et al. ), have been proven to reduce exacerbations in COPD patients. Several naturally produced antioxidants (e.g., resveratrol and curcumin) have shown their potential in the treatment of COPD (Gonçalves and Romeiro ; Wang et al. ; Nucera et al. ; Li et al. ). Antioxidants like vitamin C can assist in mitigating inflammation levels and attenuating airway remodeling and lung damage, consequently slowing COPD progression (Lei et al. ). Furthermore, the function of antioxidant enzymes is frequently diminished in COPD patients (Du et al. ). The activation or supplementation of these enzymes contributes to the antioxidant capacity enhancement, thereby reducing oxidative stress and inhibiting inflammatory responses, thus mitigating lung damage. It is also imperative to enhance air quality and avoid exposure to environmental pollutants, thereby decreasing oxidative stress and alleviating COPD symptoms (Sin et al. ). Thus, reducing oxidative stress might be a practical treatment aspect for COPD.
Here, we investigated the potential of oxidative stress-associated genes as biomarkers, and their potential molecular mechanisms for COPD clinical diagnosis and treatment through bioinformatics. Oxidative stress-related genes were derived from the MSigDB database. GSE76925 was used for analyzing differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in COPD. Co-expression network and chromosomal localization analysis of the oxidative stress-related DEGs (DE-ORGs) were also performed. After identifying hub genes using machine learning algorithms, a nomogram was constructed. Then, their regulatory mechanism was predicted. Meanwhile, the effect of matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9) on CS-induced injury and macrophage polarization was detected. Our findings provide that gene signatures associated with oxidative stress may represent therapeutic and diagnostic targets for COPD.

## Materials and methods

## Gene expression data acquisition
We obtained a gene expression matrix from microarray data in the GEO database, comprising 40 healthy control and 111 COPD lung tissues, as the training set from the GSE76925 cohort (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE76925) (Team., R.C., R: A Language and Environment for Statistical Computing ) in the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The data was generated using the GPL10558 platform. Gene symbols converted from the probe IDs were converted by combining them with the corresponding annotation files of the sequencing platform. Similarly, a gene expression matrix from microarray data, including 23 COPD and 9 control lung tissues, was obtained from the GSE38974 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE38974) cohort (Wickham ) in the GEO database as the validation set. GPL4133 (Agilent) was used for sequencing. The downloaded dataset files already contained collated expression data, and the analysis did not involve preprocessing steps such as missing value handling, standardization, and normalization. We obtained 436 genes related to oxidative stress by searching the MSigDB database with specific keywords:
GOBP_RESPONSE_TO_OXIDATIVE_STRESS, GOBP_NEGATIVE_REGULATION_OF_RESPONSE_TO_OXIDATIVE_STRESS, GOBP_POSITIVE_REGULATION_OF_RESPONSE_TO_OXIDATIVE_STRESS, and GOBP_REGULATION_OF_RESPONSE_TO_OXIDATIVE_STRESS (Supplementary file 1).

## DEG identification and function analysis
The Limma package (version. 3.54.0) (Ritchie et al. ) in R script (version. 4.2.2) (Team., R.C., R: A Language and Environment for Statistical Computing ) screened DEGs in COPD based on P adj. ≤ 0.05 and |Log2 fold change (Log2FC)|≥ 0.5. DEGs visualization used Volcano plots via the ggplot2 package (Version. 3.4.1) (Wickham ). Then, the DE-ORGs were selected with the ggvenn package (Version 0.1.9). The clusterProfiler package (version 4.7.1.003) (Wu et al. ) was used for the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (Jin et al. ) and Gene Ontology (GO) (Resource ). P ≤ 0.05 indicates significant differences.

## Function and correlation prediction in hub gene
To understand the location of the DE-ORGs on the chromosomes, the chromosome localization information was provided by the NCBI Gene dataset and then visualized by the OmicCircos package (version 1.36.0) (Hu et al. ). Functions of DE-ORGs were then analyzed using GeneMANIA.

## Robust prediction of hub genes by machine learning
The hub genes were screened by the Boruta, support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), and Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithms. These analyses were performed by glmnet (version 4.1–4) (Shannon et al. ). The lowest penalty term (λ) was obtained by the tenfold cross-validation. When the Parameter family was set as binomial, lambda. min was obtained, and the corresponding genes were selected.
The maximum interval principle of SVM serves as the foundation for the sequence backward selection technique known as SVM-RFE. SVM-RFE was performed using caret (version 6.0–93). Genes were selected when model prediction accuracy was reached for the first time. The Boruta package in R (version 8.0.0) was employed to identify all pertinent features related to the dependent variable, based on shadow features and a binomial distribution. The genes whose importance was set as confirmed were selected.

The expression and risk coefficients were used to get each hub gene’s risk score, and the following formulas were used to determine each gene’s score as well as the overall score:

To evaluate the precision of the nomogram, we plotted a calibration curve utilizing the rms package. Decision curve analysis (DCA) for clinical utility was performed using the ggDCA package (version 1.2) in R. PRROC package (version 1.3.1) in R script(Keilwagen et al. ) constructed the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) for evaluating nomogram effectiveness. Hub gene correlations were analyzed in the training set (GSE76925) using Spearman correlation, and heatmaps and chord plots of gene expression correlation were constructed using the circlize (version 0..4.15) and corrplot (version 0.92)(Gu et al. ) packages in R script. The functional similarity of hub genes across GO-BP, GO-CC, and GO-MF dimensions was examined with GOSemSim (version 2.24.0)(Yu et al. ), and the similarity scores between genes were obtained. The hub genes were ranked by their average similarity scores, which were then visualized. Relevance scores for the hub genes with oxidative stress biomarkers were generated by the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). A heatmap was created with the ggvenn package (version 1.0.12). Network visualization was achieved with the Cytoscape (version 3.6.1) (Shannon et al. ). Genes and oxidative stress biomarkers were considered to be relevant when Relevance scores were > 7 and were considered to be highly relevant when Relevance scores were > 15.

## Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)
In the training set (GSE76925), Spearman correlation coefficients for all genes were computed. Then, all the genes were sorted by correlation coefficients from the largest to the smallest to get the list of associated genes for each hub gene. Then, GSEA analysis was conducted to explore the hub gene functionality using clusterProfiler (version 4.7.1.003) (Wu et al. ). The background gene sets included GO gene sets (c5.go.bp.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt, c5.go.mf.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt) and KEGG gene sets (c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1. Hs.symbols.gmt) from the MsigDB database. Significantly enriched results were determined at P < 0.05.

## Immune infiltration analysis
Gene expression data from the GSE76925 dataset was evaluated with the CIBERSORT algorithm. The Wilcoxon test compared immune cell infiltration between COPD and normal samples, defining significance as P < 0.05. Spearman correlation analysis examined associations among hub genes, immune infiltrating cells, and COPD. In addition, LASSO regression and correlation analyses were performed on the differential cells obtained in the training set GSE76925 using the ‘glmnet’ (version 4.1–4) package in R (Liu et al. ), and validated in the dataset for the expression of core immune cells in the disease group.

## Regulatory networks construction
Regulatory hub gene TF prediction was carried out using the JASPAR database on the NetworkAnalyst platform. To investigate how miRNAs regulate hub genes, we initially identified miRNAs influencing these genes through the Tarbase and mirTarbase databases on the NetworkAnalyst platform, followed by predicting associated lncRNAs via the miRNet database. The above interactive relationships were integrated to obtain the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory relationships. To comprehend the connection between chronic problems and diseases associated with the hub gene, the CTD database was first utilized for hub gene-related diseases, and diseases classified as respiratory tract diseases were selected to obtain a comparison of hub gene-disease relationships. The Drugbank database was utilized to explore potential drugs targeting the hub gene.

## Gene expression
The hub gene expression in the validation set (GSE38974) and training set (GSE76925) of the COPD and control groups was analyzed by the Wilcox test. To evaluate the hub gene’s ability to differentiate between COPD and control samples, false-positive and true-positive values based on hub gene expression and disease grouping information were calculated using pROC (Version 1.18.0) (Robin et al. ).

## Clinical sample collection
Peripheral blood was collected from 30 COPD patients (61.53 ± 11.50) enrolled in First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province, along with 30 health control (59.27 ± 13.95) between 2022 and 2023. The COPD patients and health control underwent bronchoscopy and venesection. COPD diagnosis followed the Global Initiative of Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD 2018) criteria (Mirza et al. ). The First People’s Hospital ethics committee in Yunnan Province verified the study (certification no. KHLL2021-KY1050, Clinical trial number: not applicable). Additionally, all participants gave their informed consent after being fully granted use of the samples before the gathering of specimens. ## CS-COPD model construction
C57BL/6 J mice (n = 12; male, 6–8 weeks, 18–22 g) (Silaikejingda Laboratory Animal Co.; No. SCXK(Xiang)2019–0004) were randomized into two groups (six in each). All procedures adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines and Yunnan’s provincial legislation for animal care, with approval from the Animal Care and Use Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province (No. YYLH017). Mice were exposed to CS of DAQIANMEN (Roasted tobacco, 14 mg carbon monoxide, 12 mg tar, 14 mg Carbon monoxide, 0.9 mg nicotine) for 2 h in a tempered glass box with 10 cigarettes per session, twice a day with 6 h intervals for 16 weeks (Liu et al. ). The study aimed to gather essential experimental data for upcoming intricate designs, justifying the limited sample sizes required by statistical validity. The study’s endpoint was marked by an incapacity to stand for up to 24 h, or standing only with great reluctance, along with an inability to eat or drink without sedation or anesthesia. After anesthesia via 1% sodium pentobarbital (i.p.; 150 mg/kg), mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation. Lung tissues were harvested for subsequent experiments after pulmonary function measurement. Lung slices were stained at room temperature with hematoxylin (G1004, Servicebio, China). After 1% alcohol hydrochloride (1 mL of strong hydrochloric acid mixed with 99 ml of 75% anhydrous ethanol) was used to distinguish the tissue slices for two seconds, Eosin staining (G1001, servicebio, China) was applied for one minute. After dehydration, the tissue pieces were made transparent in xylene for 30 min. After that, neutral glue was used to seal the slices, and they were examined under a microscope.

## Pulmonary function measurement
After model establishment, mice were given 1% sodium pentobarbital (i.p.; 40 mg/kg) to induce unconsciousness. The trachea was exposed using hemostatic forceps, the T-shaped port was cut out, and tracheal intubation was fixed. The Peak Inspiratory Flow, Minute Ventilation Volume, and Peak Expiratory Flow were measured with the AniRes2005 Animal Spirometry Instrument (Beijing Beilanbo Technology Co., China). The waveforms were stabilized and recorded for 2 min, and the data were exported.

## Preparation of CS condensate
To create the COPD model, cigarette smoke condensate (CSC) was created (Liu et al. ). The smoke of DAQIANMEN was dissolved in 10 mL of DMEM without serum (Pricellar, China), and a 100% CSC solution was obtained using a vacuum pump at a pressure of 0.03–0.04 Mpa and standardized by the absorbance at 320 nm. Following a 30-min incubation, the suspension’s pH was modified to 7.4 and subsequently passed through a 0.22 μm filter (Genebrick, NC, USA) to remove larger particles and microbes. The serum-free DMEM was used to dilute the concentration of CSC and added to the medium for cell stimulation 30 min after CSC preparation. The IC50 of CSC was calculated, and 5% CSC was used for the experiment.

## Cell culture and model establishment
Acquired from Pricella Life Science & Technology Co. in China, BEAS-2B, THP-1 underwent STR identification to rule out common contaminants. BEAS-2B was cultured in DMEM containing 1% Penicillin/ Streptomycin (Procella, China) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37°C with 5% CO2. Cells were transfected with the negative control and Lentiviral vector sh-MMP9 (Genomeditech, China) using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA). Fresh medium was supplied following the transfection 48 h later. To find the transfection efficiency, qPCR was employed. After adding CSE to DEME at varying concentrations and stimulating BEAS-2B for 48 h, the medium was replaced and the cells underwent two PBS washes. THP-1 was cultivated at 37 °C with humidified 5% CO2 in RPMI 1640 (Pricella, China) containing 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin, 0.05 mM β-mercaptoethanol (Invitrogen, CA, USA), and 10% FBS. Six-well plates were seeded with 1 × 106 cells, and 48 h later, 2 mL PMA (100 ng/mL, P1585, Merke, Germany) was applied to trigger the primary macrophages (M0 macrophages). Transwell inserts (24 mm, #3412, Corning, NY, USA) were placed into 6-well plates, and M0 macrophages were seeded with 1 × 106 cells into the upper chamber. Following incubation for 12 h, the lower chamber was inoculated with BEAS-2B cells (1 × 106 cells/well). After 48 h of incubation in RPMI1640 media, the cell suspension was removed from the upper and lower chambers when the confluence reached 80%.

## Statistical analysis
Bioinformatic analysis was performed using R software (version 4.1.2). Two-tailed P values were calculated unless specified with significance set at P < 0.05. Data analysis utilized SPSS version 20.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL), with normality assessed via the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Continuous variables that fulfilled normal distribution are represented by mean ± standard deviation. The comparative analysis across different groups was executed using one-way ANOVA and Tukey’s post-hoc test, with P ≤ 0.05 denoting statistical significance.

## Results

## The identification of DEGs in COPD
Through analysis with the GSE76925, 248 DEGs, including 204 down-regulated and 44 up-regulated genes, were found between the COPD sample and health control (adjusted P value ≤ 0.05 & |Log2FC|≥ 1, supplementary Table  ). The distribution and expression of TOP 10 up-regulated genes (CA3, FGG, HTR2B, CD19, DPYS, TNFRSF17, TM4SF19, COL10A1, COMP, CHIT1) and TOP 10 down-regulated genes (KLF5, SUMO1, ADI1, DNTTIP2, B3GNT5, ID4, PPIB, CD164, HMCN1, MED21) according to log2 (FC) were exhibited in Fig.  A-B. Then, GO enrichment analysis of DEGs was performed to understand their function in COPD. As a result, 449 enriched GO terms were identified: 56 in MF, 25 in CC, and 368 in BP. The most significant terms (with the minimum P value) included leukocyte migration, protein processing, peptide hormone processing, phosphatidylinositol-5-phosphate binding, regulation of microtubule nucleation, signaling receptor ligand precursor processing, de novo protein folding, heat shock protein binding, collagen-containing extracellular matrix, and protein stabilization. Genes such as PPIB, LEP, BCHE, PLA2G7, SH3PXD2B, DNAJB4, DNAJC2, and SRPX2 are involved in these pathways. Moreover, 8 KEGG pathways were enriched, including Primary immunodeficiency, Basal transcription factors, B cell receptor signaling pathway, Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Arachidonic acid metabolism, inflammatory mediator regulation of TRP channels, Ether lipid metabolism, and, alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism. Genes such as PLA2G4A, PLA2G2D, TNFRSF13B, CD79A, CD19, IL1RAP are involved in these pathways (Fig.  C-D, Table  , supplementary Table  and ).
 

## The identification of DE-ORGs
Then, the intersection of DEGs and ORGs was performed, and a VENN plot was constructed to obtain DE-ORGs. 8 DE-ORGs including CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, ECT2, PDE8A, HSPA1A, HIF1A, was obtained (Fig.  A, supplementary Table  ). PPP1R15B was found on Chromosome 1, ECT2 on Chromosome 3, HSPA1A and CA3 on Chromosome 6, HIF1A on Chromosome 14, PDE8A on Chromosome 15, MAPT on Chromosome 17, and MMP9 on Chromosome 20, and no gene was distributed in XY Sex chromosomes (Fig.  B). Subsequently, GeneMANIA was utilized to predict the hub-gene-associated genes and their functions. There were 20 genes with a total of 157 predicted links (Co-expression of 12.58%, Shared protein domains accounted for 10.16%, Physical Interactions accounted for 74.96%, and Genetic Interactions of 2.31%). These related genes (especially HSPA1A, MMP9, MAPT) were enriched in the regulation of pathways such as mitochondrion organization, apoptotic signaling pathway, mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, and apoptotic mitochondrial changes (Fig.  C).
 

## Screening of hub genes using machine learning algorithms
Feature genes were further selected using Boruta, LASSO regression, and SVM-RFE based on DE-ORGs in the training set (GSE76925), considering COPD as the outcome variable. First, the LASSO regression was applied. Eight DE-ORGs, including CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, ECT2, PDE8A, HSPA1A, and HIF1A, were chosen because their regression coefficients remained above zero with a minimal penalty term (λ) of 0.000317374 (Fig.  A-B, supplementary Table  ). SVM-RFE analysis was then used. The model prediction accuracy was highest for the first time when the number of characterized genes was 5. Additionally, the comparable genes were MMP9, ECT2, PPP1R15B, CA3, and MAPT (Fig.  C, supplementary Table  ). Boruta analysis was performed on 8 DE-ORGs. The importance of seven genes (CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, ECT2, HSPA1A, and HIF1A) was found to be Confirmed. The importance of PDE8A was defined as Tentative and therefore excluded (Fig.  D, supplementary Table  ). Additionally, 8 LASSO-characterized genes, 5 SVM-RFE-characterized genes, and 7 Boruta-characterized genes were intersected to produce the hub genes, which were then further characterized. Thus, 5 hub genes (CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, and ECT2) were identified (Fig.  E, supplementary Table  ).
 

## Construction and validation of the diagnostic model
Next, a nomogram of five hub genes was created to forecast the likelihood of COPD development. Each gene received an individual score, with total points computed by aggregating all scores. The risk of developing COPD was predicted using total points; the greater the total points, the higher the probability of developing COPD and the higher the likelihood of developing COPD (Fig.  A). The nomogram's prognostic value was assessed by the construction of the calibration curve. The outcome demonstrated that there was no discernible difference between the reference line and the nomogram's estimated probability (P = 0.0763, Fig.  B). Additionally, the area under the curve was 0.8459459, indicating that the created nomogram had a high degree of prediction accuracy (Fig.  C). DCA demonstrated a notably improved benefit rate of the constructed nomogram over the diagonal line (All) and the horizontal line (None), highlighting its additional advantages (Fig.  D).
 

## Relationship and regulation of hub genes
Spearman correlation analysis examined the relationships among the hub genes. ECT2 and PPP1R15B displayed a strong positive correlation (cor = 0.76, P < 0.05), whereas CA3 showed a notable negative correlation with ECT2 (cor = −0.41, P < 0.05). Additionally, MMP9 did not correlate lower with other genes (Fig.  A). The chord plot (Fig.  B) was then selected based on the results of |cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05. Functional similarity analysis based on GO-MF, GO-BP, and GO-CC dimensions was carried out to acquire similarity scores between genes to learn about the functional similarities of the hub gene. MAPT, ECT2, and CA3 were the top three genes with the greatest functional similarity (Fig.  C). To investigate whether oxidative stress indicators and the hub genes (including 8 biomarkers and 5 antioxidant biomarkers) are related, relevance scores between the hub gene and oxidative stress biomarkers were obtained using the GeneCards database. MMP9 was highly correlated (Relevance scores > 15) with TOS, ROS, and TOAC, and MPAT was highly correlated with TOS and TOAC (Fig.  D, supplementary Table  ). A correlation network was constructed between the genes and relevant oxidative stress biomarkers (Relevance scores > 7). 11 nodes (including 2 hub genes, 6 oxidative biomarkers, and 3 Antioxidant biomarkers) and 15 edges were obtained (Fig.  E). MMP9 was related to 8 oxidative stress biomarkers, and MPAT was 7 oxidative stress biomarkers.

## Regulation network prediction
The direct relationship between transcription factors (TFs) or noncoding (nc) RNAs and hub genes was evaluated to further predict the mechanism by which the hub gene was regulated in COPD. 31 TFs were predicted, and a network included 36 nodes (5 hub genes and 31 TFs) and 46 edges. Of these, FOXC1 was predicted to be related to 4 hub genes, i.e., PPP1R15B, CA3, MMP9, and MAPT (Fig.  A, supplementary Table  ). There were 229 miRNAs predicted for the 5 hub genes in the Tarbase database. 199 miRNAs were predicted for the 3 hub genes (ECT2, MMP9, PPP1R15B) in the mirTarbase database. miRNA-hub gene regulatory pairs were obtained by retaining the predictions consistent with those of both databases and consisted of the 3 hub genes (ECT2, MMP9, PPP1R15B) and 50 miRNAs. Next, a total of 228 lncRNAs related to 2 hub genes (ECT2, PPP1R15B) were predicted using the miRNet database. A network including 242 nodes (2 hub genes, 12 miRNAs, and 228 lncRNAs) was constructed (Fig.  B, supplementary Table  ). A network including 10 nodes (7 diseases and 3 hub genes) and 7 edges was constructed (Fig.  C, supplementary Table  ). Also, 3 hub genes (MAPT, MMP9, CA3) were predicted as related to 37 drugs, and a network including 47 nodes (7 diseases, 3 hub genes, and 37 drugs) was constructed (Fig.  D, supplementary Table  ).
 

## Hub gene expression in COPD
The hub gene expression was analyzed in GSE76925 and GSE38974. In the training set, GSE76925, CA3, MAPT, and MMP9 expression was significantly higher, while ECT2 and PPP1R15B were notably reduced in COPD patients (Fig.  A). While in the validation set GSE38974, 3 hub genes (CA3, ECT2, MMP9) showed consistent expression trends between the validation and training sets, and 2 of them (ECT2, MMP9) showed significantly differential expression (Fig.  C). All hub genes in GSE76925, as well as the ECT2 and MMP9 in the validation set GSE38974, had area under the curve values greater than 0.7 (Fig.  B, D), indicating that ECT2 and MMP9 had high discriminatory ability in COPD samples. Then, the mRNA expression in peripheral blood was detected. Compared with healthy controls, COPD patients had significantly increased MMP9 but significantly decreased ECT2 (Fig.  A-B). Since MMP9 was significantly expressed in GSE76925 and GSE38974, as well as in the in-house cohort. As shown in the above results, MMP9 was highly related to oxidative stress biomarkers and respiratory tract disease; MMP9 was selected for the subsequent experiments. The CS-induced COPD model was constructed, and HE was performed to evaluate lung tissue histopathology. As a result, the tissues from the control group showed intact alveolar lumens, no detachment of epithelial cells from the mucosa and necrosis, no hyperplasia of submucosal glands, and few inflammatory cells in the airway periphery were observed. There were no secretions in the tracheal lumen, basement membranes, or smooth muscle hyperplasia. While in the CS-COPD group, the alveolar septa were thickened, the basement membranes and smooth muscles of the bronchi were hyperplastic, the bronchial walls were thickened, there was a large number of inflammatory cells infiltrating, epithelial cell detachment or secretion was observed in the lumen of the trachea, and there were varying degrees of stenosis in the trachea (Fig.  A). The lung function was detected. The COPD group had lower minute ventilation volume, peak expiratory flow, and peak inspiratory volume (Fig.  B-D) as well as declined cytokines (Fig.  E-G). As presented in Fig.  H-K, the SOD, CAT, and GSH were reduced, while MDA was increased in the COPD group, suggesting the occurrence of OS injury in the CS-COPD model. Also, MMP9 expression was detected by immunohistochemistry and qPCR. MMP9 was located in the extracellular matrix and cytoplasm, and MMP9 was highly expressed in the COPD group (Fig.  L-N). Since MMP9 was highly positively related to M1 macrophages, we detected the M1 and M2 cell surface markers. The M1 macrophage markers, including CD86, iNOS, and MCP1, were all increased. In contrast, the markers of M2 macrophages, such as IL-10, ARG1, CD206, and CD163 were all reduced in the COPD group (Fig.  O-P), indicating the significant difference of M1 macrophage exerted in COPD.
 

## Deprivation of MMP9 alleviated the CSC-induced injury and promoted the macrophage polarization to M1 macrophage
To evaluate the appropriate concentration of CSC to stimulate the BEAS-2B, the freshly prepared 100% CSC was diluted to concentrations at 1%, 2%, 4%, 8%, and 15% in serum-free medium, and BEAS-2B was stimulated by different concentrations of CSC for 24 h. CCK-8 assessed cell viability. When the concentration of CSC reached 4%, the percentage of BEAS-2B viability decreased to 50%, and IC50 was calculated as 4.079%. Therefore, we chose 4% CSC to stimulate the BEAS-2B in subsequent research (Fig.  A). And the lentivirus was constructed and transfected to deprive the MMP9 expression in BEAS-2B. The transfection efficiency was detected with qPCR. As a result, sh-MMP9#3 showed better expression suppression and was selected for further detection (Fig.  B). The MMP9 level was up-regulated in CSC-BEAS-2B than in BEAS-2B but down-regulated when sh-MMP9 was transfected (Fig.  C-D). The cell viability was decreased in CSC-BEAS-2B compared to BEAS-2B, which was increased after the deprivation of MMP9 expression (Fig.  E). The cytokine levels were elevated in CSC-BEAS-2B than in BEAS-2B but decreased after the deprivation of MMP9 expression (Fig.  F-H). ROS content and oxidative stress marker levels were determined to assess the presence of oxidative stress injury induced by CSC. MDA and ROS levels were increased, whereas CAT, SOD, and GSH-Px levels exhibited a decrease following CSC stimulation. Upon depletion of MMP9 expression, a decrease was observed in MDA and ROS levels, whereas CAT, SOD, and GSH-Px levels showed an elevation (Fig.  -N). The apoptosis rate of BEAS-2B was increased in CSC-BEAS-2B compared to BEAS-2B, which was decreased when sh-MMP9 was transfected (Fig.  O-P). This data suggests the key role of MMP9 in CSC-induced oxidative stress injury.
Then, to detect the role of MMP9 in macrophage polarization, the co-culture of PMA-induced THP-1 cells with CSC-BEAS-2B was performed. THP-1 cells were suspension cells, showing a small round shape with regular morphology, and PMA-induced THP-1 cells were transformed into primary macrophages (M0 macrophages), which were irregularly polygonal in shape and grew adherently to the wall (Fig.  A). The positive rate of CD68 was increased after the induction of PMA (Fig.  B-C), which indicated the successful induction of M0 macrophage. Also, the expression of MMP9 in co-cultured macrophages was detected. As a result, MMP9 mRNA expression was increased in M0 that co-cultured with CSC-BEAS-2B compared to M0 or M0 that co-cultured with BEAS-2B, and decreased when MMP9 lentivirus was transfected in CSC-BEAS-2B (Fig.  D). The surface markers of M1 and M2 were all increased in M0 co-cultured with CSC-BEAS-2B, compared to M0 or M0 that co-cultured with BEAS-2B. In contrast, transfection of MMP9 lentivirus in CSC-BEAS-2B led to a decrease in M1 surface markers like CD86, iNOS, and MCP-1, while M2 markers such as IL-10, ARG1, CD206, and CD163 increased (Fig.  E-K, Supplementary Fig.  ). These results showed that deprivation of MMP9 alleviated the CSC-induced injury and promoted the macrophage polarization to M2 macrophage (Fig.  ).
 

## Discussion
COPD, primarily linked to environmental factors like smoking, has become a major global health concern (Labaki and Rosenberg ; Rabe and Watz ). COPD is marked by progressively limited airflow and strong lung inflammation because of exposure to harmful gases or particles (Hogg and Timens ). Currently, the diagnosis of COPD relies mainly on spirometry to recognize pulmonary dysfunction, and spirometry has significant limitations in the early detection of COPD, among others (Cheng and Solit ; Riley and Sciurba ). Despite significant advancements in COPD treatment through conventional lung volume reduction surgery and other techniques, there remain some unpredictable complications (Zhang and Li ). In addition, while some pulmonary rehabilitation programs like behavioral changes, education, exercise training, and personalized treatments, are considered effective in attenuating COPD, there are still patients who do not benefit from these treatments (Hartman et al. ). Oxidative stress is a critical factor in chronic inflammation and importantly involved in COPD development (Menzel ). A distinctive feature of COPD is that its symptoms do not subside when exposure to CS ceases. This suggests that factors beyond external triggers, such as autoimmunity or persistent infections, contribute to disease progression (Zhang et al. ; Davidson et al. ). The lungs produce ROS from two main sources: environmental factors like smoking and pollution, and cellular processes like enzymatic production by inflammatory cells and epithelial cells in response to pathogens or stimuli (Kirkham and Rahman ; Langen et al. ; He et al. ). ROS can be generated within cells through various mechanisms such as mitochondrial respiration (Fernández et al. ; Zhang et al. ; Chouchani et al. ). When generated near cell membranes, ROS oxidizes membrane phospholipids, initiating a cascade that forms lipid hydroperoxides, potentially deactivating enzymes and receptors, disrupting membrane function, and increasing tissue permeability (Gutteridge ). This can deactivate enzymes and membrane-bound receptors, disrupt membrane function, and enhance tissue permeability. Therefore, exploring the functions and mechanisms of oxidative stress-related genes in COPD may be valuable for diagnosing and treating COPD patients.
In this manuscript, 248 DEGs were found in GSE76925, which mainly enriched in processes associated with inflammation including leukocyte migration, Primary immunodeficiency, and in processes related to metabolism such as Ether lipid metabolism, Arachidonic acid metabolism, and alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism. Monocytes and lymphocytes work together to promote migration towards CCL5 and CXCR3 chemokines, resulting in elevated T-cells and macrophages in COPD patients(Costa et al. ). Venkata et al. (Allam et al. ) found that the immunomodulatory protein macrophage migration inhibitory factor inhibited the responses to glucocorticoids and enhanced the recruitment of neutrophils to the lungs. The TRP (Transient Receptor Potential) channels participated in toxin sensing as chemosensors and in lung inflammation and related diseases (like edema, asthma, COPD, and lung fibrosis) as mediators (Dietrich et al. ). These all indicated that the DEGs may participate in COPD development.
The intersection between differential genes and oxidative stress-related genes yielded 8 common genes, including CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, ECT2, PDE8A, HSPA1A, and HIF1A, which were distributed on different autosomes. Furthermore, 5 hub genes, CA3, PPP1R15B, MAPT, MMP9, and ECT2, were yielded by SVM-RFE, LASSO regression, and Boruta, and the performance of the nomogram constructed based on hub genes was favorable and instrumental in helping doctors and patients make personalized predictions of illness and, in turn, choose better treatment options. Carbonic anhydrase isozyme 3 (CA3) involved glutathiolation, which was found to increase during aging and under acute oxidative stress(Cabiscol and Levine ). Two heterodimeric eIF2α holophosphatases in mammalian cells, namely the constitutive PPP1R15B (R15B) and the stress-inducible PPP1R15A (R15A), regulate eIF2α phosphorylation at low levels by binding to the catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 1(Krzyzosiak et al. ). Julia et al.(Hülsmann et al. ) revealed that the PP1 regulator CReP/PPP1R15B dephosphorylated eIF2α to counteract the translational inhibition by stress kinases. This process regulates the integrated stress response at the signaling level and degrades misfolded proteins during stress. The 10 + 16 mutations of the microtubule-associated tau protein gene (MAPT) were linked to reduced ATP production through oxidative phosphorylation partial inhibition of respiration driven by complex I, and decreased mitochondrial NADH levels (Esteras et al. ). NOX4-mediated mitochondria-associated ROS increased MMP9 mRNA stability and affected cancer invasion(Mori et al. ). Xiao et al. found that the PI3k 110β/Aurora B pathway was suppressed by ROS accumulation, resulting in reduced levels of the centralspindlin subunits CYK4 and MKLP1, along with proteins involved in regulating downstream cytokines, namely CHMP2A, Cep55, Ect2, and RhoA(Xiao et al. ). The GSEA results indicated that CA3, PPP1R15B, ECT2, and MAPT were enriched in mitochondrion function, protein translation, and protein translational modifications such as Cytoplasmic Translation, Mitochondrial Gene Expression, Mitochondrial Translation, Oxidative Phosphorylation, Positive Regulation of Protein Polyubiquitination, Protein_N Linked Glycosylation via Histone Lysine N Methyltransferase Activity, N Methyltransferase Activity, Histone H3 Methyltransferase Activity, Lysine N Methyltransferase Activity, and Asparagine. MMP9 was enriched in processes related to inflammation, such as Antigen Binding, Immune Receptor Activity, and Cytokine Receptor Binding. Correlation analyses between hub genes and oxidative stress biomarkers showed that both MMP9 and MAPT genes had a high association with oxidative stress biomarkers, with both MMP9 and MAPT having the highest correlation with total oxidant status (TOS). TOS and total antioxidant capacity (TAOS) have been identified as significant indicators for evaluating redox equilibrium within living organisms (Sharebiani et al. ). This study revealed that MMP9 was strongly correlated with TOS and ROS. This finding indicates that oxidative stress, through its contribution to MMP9 expression, may potentially expedite the progression of structural lung damage. Oxidative stress (an increase in ROS) activates MMP9 transcription via several signaling pathways, including NF-κB, leading to lung tissue damage and airway remodeling (Sul and Ra ). This role is particularly important in COPD, where oxidative stress leads to heightened damage to the airway and alveolar walls and a persistent worsening of the inflammatory response (Racanelli et al. ). Therefore, regulating MMP9 expression to inhibit oxidative stress may effectively alleviate COPD symptoms and progression. MAPT (microtubule-associated protein Tau) is generally linked to neurodegenerative diseases (Strang et al. ), and the findings demonstrated a stronger correlation between MAPT and TOS and TAOS.MAPT may indirectly enhance cellular tolerance to oxidative stress by promoting microtubule stability and regulating cell migration and repair processes (Al-Dalahmah et al. ). Furthermore, MAPT may protect against COPD by modifying intracellular antioxidant enzyme activity and decreasing ROS accumulation (Korn et al. ). Consequently, modulating the expression or function of MAPT could potentially strengthen antioxidant defenses, mitigate the progression of COPD, and enhance lung function and patients’ quality of life. Immune cell infiltration found that CD8 T cells, Γδ T cells, follicular helper T cells, and M0 macrophages had infiltration differences in COPD. Among all cells, M1 macrophages displayed the strongest positive correlation with resting dendritic cells and the most pronounced negative correlation with activated dendritic cells. Conversely, MMP9 exhibited the highest positive correlation with M1 macrophages and the most significant negative correlation with activated dendritic cells. MMP9 showed strong negative correlations with activated dendritic cells but positive correlations with M1 macrophages. A key feature of inflammation in COPD is the heightened infiltration of T-lymphocytes, B-lymphocytes, macrophages, and neutrophils, particularly CD8 + T-lymphocytes, into the airways and lung parenchyma (Costa et al. ), which was a coincidence of our prediction.
Then, the hub gene expression was validated in GSE76925 and GSE38974. MMP9 levels were markedly higher in COPD tissues than in normal ones, while ECT2 expression was reduced, which was confirmed in the peripheral blood sample. ROC of ECT2 and MMP9 showed a higher capability to discriminate COPD patients from normal samples. This study identified a significant association between ECT2 and CD8 T cells, as well as its enrichment in mitochondrial function and protein translation. It has been demonstrated that free radicals produced by oxidative stress can compromise mitochondrial function, leading to disrupted energy metabolism and worsening airway inflammation and tissue damage (Bhatti et al. ). By enhancing mitochondrial function, ECT2 may help alleviate the harmful effects caused by oxidative stress, improving the energy supply and metabolic state of cells. Thus, ECT2 may regulate immune responses and promote tissue repair in COPD. Furthermore, ECT2 has been observed to regulate protein translation processes in immune cells, particularly T cells, thereby impacting their function (Justilien et al. ). In the context of COPD, the active involvement of CD8 + T cells is closely associated with airway inflammatory responses, and ECT2 may facilitate the growth and functional improvement of these immune cells, potentially providing protection against oxidative stress, reducing inflammation, and mitigating tissue injury(Villaseñor-Altamirano et al. ). Consequently, the function of ECT2 in oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, and protein synthesis may provide new molecular targets for COPD treatment, especially in modulating immune responses and cellular metabolism. MMP9 was selected for the subsequent experiment. As a member of the MMP family, it comprises five structural domains: the amino-terminal prepeptide, signal peptide, zinc-binding catalytic region, carboxy-terminal heme-like protein-binding region, and hinge region. The catalytic domains consist of the fibronectin II domain, the active site, and the zinc-binding region (Dofara et al. ; Atkinson and Senior ; Navratilova et al. ; Zhu et al. ). MMP9-TIMP1, an important group of protease-protease inhibitors in organisms, has been proven vital in developing COPD (Navratilova et al. ). In addition to degrading protein structures and resisting proteases, MMP9 can also alter cells by regulating matrix-binding fibroblast growth factors and cytokines and altering cellular function(Yang et al. ; Yao et al. ). Lungs of smokers and COPD individuals harbor an abundance of macrophages that release various cytokines like IL-1β, as well as MMPs such as MMP9 and MMP12 (Ohnishi et al. ; Shapiro ). Macrophage polarization occurs throughout the development of COPD, which is often acutely exacerbated by infection (Zhong et al. ; Jiao et al. ). Macrophage polarization to M1 is consistent with the development of inflammation (Le et al. ; He et al. ). Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection induces polarization of CS-exposed macrophages towards both M1 and M2 phenotypes. This leads to increased production of MMP12 and MMP9(Le et al. ; Chapman and Stone ). Sanja et al.(Dimic-Janjic et al. ) revealed that individuals with COPD exhibited elevated serum concentrations of TIMP-1, MMP-9, and a higher MMP-9/TIMP-1 ratio. This causes a disbalance favoring a pro-proteolytic environment. Zhu et al. demonstrated that Krt15 regulated EMT by influencing MMP-9 expression, consequently shielding lung tissue from apoptosis, inflammatory infiltration, and damage caused by CS (Zhu et al. ). This study found that exposing BEAS-2B to CSC caused a marked reduction in cell viability and an increase in oxidative stress indicators (e.g. MDA and ROS). This finding aligns with earlier studies indicating that CSC can trigger oxidative stress, causing cellular damage (Kume et al. ). To explore this further, the impact of MMP9 knockdown in BEAS-2B cells was assessed, revealing restored cell viability and decreased oxidative stress markers. This finding suggests that MMP9 may respond to CSC by mediating oxidative stress injury. This hypothesis was further supported by the reduced levels of ROS, MDA, and increased activities of antioxidant enzymes (e.g. GSH-Px, SOD, and CAT) after MMP9 deletion. Furthermore, the validation results of PCR and WB provide substantial evidence that MMP9 is crucial in CSC-induced macrophage polarization and oxidative stress. MMP9 deletion can alleviate CSC-induced injury and promote the polarisation of macrophages towards the M2-type, which is associated with anti-inflammatory properties. Collectively, MMP9 could be a valuable target for addressing oxidative stress and inflammation.
In summary, MMP and ECT2 were oxidative stress-related genes in COPD, with MMP9 linked to cell apoptosis, cell inflammation, and ROS injury in BEAS-2B, and M1 transition to M2. MMP9 has been shown to be directly related to macrophage migration as well as ECM degradation (Travnickova et al. ). Macrophage-secreted MMP9 has been shown to be involved in macrophage-to-fibroblast migration (Li et al. ). In COPD, the TLR2/MMP9 axis in lung monocytes has been shown to promote EMT in lung epithelial cells (Li et al. ). Although resident cells in the normal lung do not produce MMP-9, various forms of stimulation have been shown to induce MMP-9 production in bronchial epithelial cells, Clara cells, alveolar type II cells, fibroblasts, smooth muscle cells and endothelial cells (Atkinson and Senior ). This study investigated the potential of oxidative stress-related genes (OSRGs) biomarkers and their molecular mechanisms in the clinical diagnosis and treatment of COPD through bioinformatic analysis. The screened biomarkers, ECT2 and MMP9, play important roles in the oxidative stress response in COPD, and the potential mechanism of MMP9 in regulating apoptosis, inflammatory response, ROS damage and macrophage polarization shift from M1-type to M2-type in BEAS-2B cells was verified by sufficient experiments, the results showed that MMP9 plays a key role in CSC-induced oxidative stress injury. deprivation of MMP9 attenuated CSC-induced injury and promoted macrophage polarization to M2 macrophages. Therefore, the present study confirms the role of MMP9 on lung epithelial cells through oxidative stress injury and can demonstrate the role of MMP9 in macrophage polarization in epithelial cells, providing a new mechanism for intercellular communication in lung diseases and a theoretical basis for the potential application of MMP9 inhibitors in regulating oxidative stress as well as macrophage polarization, which may open new opportunities for COPD treatment.
