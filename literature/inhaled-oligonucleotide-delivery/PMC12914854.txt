This approach offers a platform for regenerative gene therapy through metabolic-epigenetic reprogramming.

## Introduction
Ischemic heart disease (IHD) remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, largely due to the irreversible loss of cardiomyocytes (CMs) and the heart's limited regenerative capacity [ ]. While current therapies aim to restore perfusion and limit infarct size, they fail to replenish functional myocardium, often leading to heart failure. Stimulating endogenous CMs proliferation remains a major unmet goal in cardiac repair.
Neonatal hearts possess a transient regenerative window during which CMs retain their ability to re-enter cell cycle and repair injury. This regenerative capacity is rapidly lost postnatally as CMs undergo terminal differentiation [ ], a process accompanied by a marked metabolic transition from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO) [ , ]. Growing evidence indicates that this metabolic shift is not merely coincident with CM maturation but actively contributes to the establishment of a repressive epigenetic landscape that silences pro-proliferative gene expressions and enforces a stable post-mitotic state. Accordingly, targeting this metabolic-epigenetic axis has shown promise in reactivating CMs proliferative competence. For example, inhibition of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (Cpt1) has been shown to increase intracellular α-ketoglutarate levels and activates histone lysine demethylase KDM5B, leading to reduced H3K4me3 enrichment at maturation-associated genes and promote CM dedifferentiation [ ]. However, global FAO suppression often results in lipid accumulation and metabolic toxicity [ ]. A more refined strategy involves targeting CD36, a fatty acid translocase upstream of mitochondrial oxidation, whose inhibition attenuates FAO flux while limiting lipotoxic stress [ ].
RNA interference (RNAi) has enabled several approved therapeutics [ , , , ], yet its broader clinical translation remains limited by instability, rapid clearance, immune recognition, and poor tissue retention [ , , ]. To address these challenges, nanocarrier-based systems have emerged as promising delivery vehicles. Among them, mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) offer high surface area and tunable surface chemistry for efficient siRNA loading, protection, and controlled release [ , , , ]. However, their lack of intrinsic targeting and rapid systemic clearance constrain their utility in extrahepatic tissues.
Platelets, in contrast, naturally home to sites of vascular injury and inflammation, making them attractive vectors for ischemic heart targeting [ , , , , ]. This injury-homing behavior is driven by endothelial activation, exposure of subendothelial matrix components, and local inflammatory signaling, which together promote platelet recruitment through coordinated engagement of platelet surface receptors and enable directed chemotaxis toward injured tissue. In addition, platelets possess a well-developed open canalicular system (OCS), an interconnected membrane network that facilitates the internalization, storage, and stimulus-responsive release of nanoscale cargo [ , ]. By harnessing both intrinsic injury-homing capacity and the OCS-mediated cargo loading capability, we designed an integrated platelet-engineered therapeutic platform that combines physiological targeting with nanoparticle-enabled nucleic acid delivery.
In this study, we developed a bioresponsive RNA therapeutic platform by loading CD36-targeting siRNA into MSNs (siCD36@MSNs) and subsequently encapsulating them within intact platelets via the OCS, yielding siCD36@MSNs@Plt. Leveraging this design, the siCD36@MSNs@Plt system enables site-specific delivery of CD36 siRNA to ischemic myocardium, resulting in effective metabolic gene silencing in vivo. In a murine I/R injury model, this strategy siCD36@MSNs@Plt reprogrammed lipid metabolism, reactivated cell-cycle entry, and enhanced cardiac functional recovery without inducing pathological lipid accumulation (see ).

## Results
2.1

## Cd36 upregulation is associated with metabolic maturation and loss of CM proliferative capacity
Transcriptomic profiling revealed a coordinated, stage-specific shift between lipid metabolic activity and cell-cycle programs during cardiac development. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis comparing embryonic hearts with postnatal hearts (neonatal and adult) demonstrated that genes enriched in embryonic hearts were predominantly associated with cell-cycle progression and proliferative processes, whereas genes enriched in postnatal hearts were linked to metabolic pathways, including fatty acid metabolism ( A). Conversely, comparison of adult hearts with embryonic and neonatal stages showed enrichment of oxidative and fatty acid metabolic processes alongside suppression of cell cycle-related pathways in the adult myocardium ( B). 
Consistent with these pathway-level changes, expression of lipid-handling genes increased progressively across developmental stages, with prominent upregulation of fatty acid transporters such as Cd36 and Fabp3 ( C). Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis further confirmed that Cd36 expression in cardiomyocytes increases from embryonic day 12.5 (E12.5) to E14.5 and is most pronounced at the neonatal stage ( D–F). Together, these data establish a developmental association between lipid metabolic maturation and CM proliferative arrest, identifying Cd36 as a maturation-associated fatty acid transporter that is functionally interrogated in subsequent sections.

2.2

## siCD36 loading preserves MSN structural integrity and enables efficient cargo encapsulation
Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) were synthesized and systematically characterized as carriers for siCD36 delivery. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed uniform, spherical nanoparticles with an average diameter of approximately 140 nm, which increased to ∼200 nm following siCD36 loading, indicating successful cargo incorporation ( A). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis further confirmed this size increase, with the hydrodynamic diameter shifting from 142.5 ± 1.3 nm to 197.5 ± 0.8 nm upon siCD36 loading ( B and C). Consistent with nucleic acid complexation, zeta potential measurements showed a pronounced surface charge inversion from −17.0 ± 0.1 mV for bare MSNs to +25.8 ± 0.1 mV following siCD36 incorporation ( D and E). This charge reversal is attributed to Ca2+-mediated electrostatic bridging between the negatively charged silica surface and the phosphate backbone of siRNA, resulting in stable surface complexation and overcompensation of surface charge [ ]. 
Textural properties of the MSNs were quantified by nitrogen adsorption–desorption analysis using the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method. The specific surface area was calculated to be 228.13 m2 g−1, indicating a highly porous architecture with substantial surface accessibility. Pore size distribution analysis revealed a predominant mesoporous structure (2-50 nm), with a most probable pore diameter of 31.40 nm ( ), supporting efficient siRNA accommodation within the MSN framework. siRNA loading efficiency was quantified by spectrophotometric measurement of unbound siCD36 in the supernatant. Under optimized conditions, siCD36@MSNs achieved a loading efficiency of approximately 20% ( ).
In vitro release studies demonstrated a rapid initial release of siCD36 from siCD36@MSNs within the first few hours, followed by a slower release phase ( F). Approximately 80% of the loaded siCD36 was released within 5 h, indicating a burst-dominated release profile. This release behavior aligns with the intended therapeutic context, as platelet homing and activation occur predominantly during the acute phase following I/R injury. Upon platelet accumulation and stimulation within the injured myocardium, rapid unloading of siCD36@MSNs is expected to facilitate efficient intracellular delivery during this critical therapeutic window.

2.3

## CD36 silencing reduces lipid accumulation and promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation
To assess the biological efficacy and target specificity of CD36 silencing, neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes (NMCMs) were treated with control formulations, scrambled siRNA-loaded MSNs (scrambled-siRNA@MSNs), or siCD36@MSNs. The scrambled-siRNA@MSNs group was included to exclude nonspecific effects of the nanocarrier system. Western blot analysis demonstrated efficient and specific reduction of CD36 protein levels in NMCMs treated with siCD36@MSNs, with CD36 expression reduced to 39.7% of control levels, whereas scrambled-siRNA@MSNs did not induce significant changes ( G and H). Immunofluorescence staining further confirmed CD36 downregulation at the cellular level ( I and J). In parallel, RT-qPCR analysis showed that siCD36 delivered via MSNs achieved target mRNA suppression comparable to that obtained using a commercial transfection reagent (Lipofectamine), confirming effective intracellular delivery ( ).
CD36 silencing markedly reduced fatty acid uptake, thereby limiting intracellular lipid loading, as evidenced by decreased BODIPY signal intensity, while scrambled-siRNA@MSNs had no effect ( K and L). Non-targeted metabolomics revealed reduced intracellular FADH2 levels following CD36 silencing, indicating suppressed mitochondrial FAO ( ). As CPT1 governs mitochondrial fatty acid entry in CMs, this metabolic signature is consistent with reduced CPT1-dependent FAO activity. This metabolic shift was accompanied by increased expression of Cyclin E1, a key regulator of the G1/S transition ( M and N). Moreover, EdU incorporation assays and propidium iodide (PI)-based flow cytometric analysis demonstrated enhanced DNA synthesis and increased cell-cycle entry specifically in response to CD36 silencing ( ). These results demonstrate that siCD36@MSNs induce CD36-specific metabolic reprogramming characterized by reduced lipid uptake and FAO, coupled with activation of CM cell-cycle progression.

2.4

## CD36 silencing reprograms lipid metabolic networks
To characterize the metabolic consequences of CD36 inhibition, we performed untargeted metabolomics analysis on siCD36-treated NMCMs. A total of 1793 metabolites spanning diverse chemical classes were detected, with lipids and lipid-like molecules representing 34% of the metabolome ( A). Principal component analysis revealed clear separation between siCD36-treated and control cardiomyocytes, indicating that CD36 silencing induces a distinct global metabolic state ( B). Differential metabolite analysis identified 272 significantly altered species (175 upregulated, 97 downregulated), with lipids and lipid-like molecules comprising the largest category of change (38% of differential metabolites; C and D). Pathway enrichment analysis highlighted primary bile acid biosynthesis, steroid biosynthesis, sphingolipid metabolism, and glutathione metabolism as the most significantly perturbed pathways ( E), suggesting coordinated remodeling of lipid handling and cellular redox status. 
Focused analysis of the lipid compartment revealed that glycerophospholipids (42%), prenol lipids (22%), fatty acyls (19%), and steroid derivatives (11%) were the most prominently altered subclasses ( F). Volcano plot visualization and hierarchical clustering confirmed systematic reorganization across multiple lipid categories rather than isolated pathway perturbations ( G and H). Quantitative assessment of lipid subclass abundance showed enrichment of prenol lipids and glycerophospholipids alongside depletion of sphingolipids in siCD36-treated cells ( I). These findings demonstrate that CD36 inhibition triggers comprehensive, systems-level reprogramming of cardiomyocyte lipid metabolism. This metabolic remodeling is consistent with reduced reliance on FAO and attenuation of lipotoxic stress, thereby establishing a permissive metabolic environment for CM cell-cycle re-entry.

2.5

## Platelet-mediated encapsulation enables bioresponsive siRNA release and preserves siCD36 bioactivity
To evaluate platelet-mediated encapsulation, siCD36@MSNs were co-incubated with isolated mouse platelets under physiological conditions. Transmission electron microscopy revealed MSNs localized within platelet-associated membrane invaginations consistent with the OCS, while preserving platelet ultrastructure and morphology ( A). Confocal microscopy further demonstrated co-localization of DiO-labeled platelets with Cy5-labeled siCD36@MSNs, confirming efficient nanoparticle internalization ( B). Consistent with this, the ultrastructural analysis showed that the encapsulated siCD36@MSNs remained stably retained within platelet membrane compartments without evidence of platelet activation for up to 24 h following co-incubation ( ). 
The protective effect of platelet encapsulation on siRNA stability was assessed under nuclease-rich conditions. Cy5-labeled siCD36@MSNs and siCD36@MSNs@Plt were incubated in RNase-containing mouse plasma at 37 °C for 4 h. Following centrifugation and resuspension in RNase-free PBS, fluorescence intensity was quantified as a surrogate for siRNA integrity. siCD36@MSNs@Plt retained significantly higher fluorescence than siCD36@MSNs alone, indicating reduced nuclease-mediated degradation and enhanced protection of the siRNA cargo conferred by platelet encapsulation ( ). Release kinetics of the encapsulated siRNA were subsequently examined. Fluorescence intensity measurements of Cy5-siCD36 revealed a burst-dominated release profile, with rapid release during early time points followed by a slower residual release phase extending to 24 h ( C). This release behavior is consistent with the early unloading profile observed in in vitro release studies. To assess stimulus responsiveness, siCD36@MSNs@Plt were exposed to increasing concentrations of thrombin (0-200 U/mL), resulting in a dose-dependent enhancement of siRNA release, with maximal release observed at 100 U/mL ( D). This concentration was therefore selected for subsequent experiments to model platelet activation at sites of vascular injury.
Platelet activation status following encapsulation was evaluated by flow cytometric analysis of the canonical activation markers CD62P (P-selectin) and PAC-1. No significant upregulation of either marker was detected in siCD36@MSNs@Plt compared with untreated platelets, indicating that OCS-mediated encapsulation preserves platelet quiescence prior to stimulation ( ). The functional integrity of platelet-mediated siCD36 delivery was evaluated using a Transwell system, in which siCD36@MSNs@Plt were placed in the upper chamber and NMCMs were cultured in the lower chamber ( E). Upon thrombin stimulation (100 U mL−1), a significant increase in transfer and cellular uptake of siCD36@MSNs released from platelets was observed compared with unstimulated conditions ( F), confirming that platelet activation enables on-demand cargo release and downstream cellular delivery. Consistent with enhanced uptake, Western blotting and immunofluorescence analyses revealed more pronounced CD36 knockdown in NMCMs exposed to thrombin-activated siCD36@MSNs@Plt relative to non-activated complexes ( G–J).
Effective CD36 silencing following platelet-mediated delivery was accompanied by reduced intracellular lipid accumulation ( K and L) and induction of Cyclin E1 expression ( M and N), indicating preservation of downstream metabolic and cell cycle-associated responses previously observed with siCD36@MSNs alone. Epigenetic analysis further revealed a reduction in global H3K4me3 levels ( O and P), consistent with chromatin remodeling associated with altered metabolic state following CD36 inhibition as previously reported [ ]. In parallel, expression of cell cycle-associated markers, including Ki67, phosphorylated histone H3 (pH3), and Aurora B kinase, was increased following thrombin-activated delivery ( Q–V). bioresponsive release of siCD36@MSNs while preserving siRNA stability, knockdown efficiency, and downstream metabolic and cell cycle-associated effects in vitro.

2.6

## Platelet-mediated delivery enhances circulation, immune evasion and cardiac targeting of siCD36 MSNs
To evaluate in vivo circulation behavior and cardiac targeting, mice subjected to I/R injury were intravenously administered PBS, siCD36@MSNs, siCD36@MSNs + Plt (co-administration of MSNs and platelets), or siCD36@MSNs@Plt at 30 min post-reperfusion. Biodistribution was assessed by in vivo fluorescence imaging (IVIS) at predefined time points. At 2 h post-administration, mice treated with siCD36@MSNs@Plt exhibited a markedly stronger fluorescence signal in the anterior cardiac region compared with those receiving siCD36@MSNs or siCD36@MSNs + Plt ( A). Ex vivo IVIS imaging of harvested organs further confirmed preferential accumulation of siCD36@MSNs@Plt in the injured myocardium relative to control formulations ( B). 
Analysis of major non-target organs revealed substantially reduced fluorescence signals in the liver, spleen, lung, and kidney in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group compared with siCD36@MSNs and siCD36@MSNs + Plt groups ( C). Quantitative assessment of IVIS signals across organs corroborated these findings, demonstrating that platelet-mediated encapsulation enhances cardiac targeting while simultaneously reducing off-target organ accumulation ( D). Histological analysis further supported the imaging results. Cy5-labeled siCD36 signals were predominantly localized within infarcted myocardial regions and co-localized with cardiac troponin T (cTnT), indicating effective delivery to cardiomyocyte-rich areas ( E). Quantification revealed a 4.1-fold increase in myocardial fluorescence intensity in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group compared with siCD36@MSNs alone, highlighting the critical contribution of platelet encapsulation to injury site–specific accumulation ( F).
To elucidate the mechanistic basis underlying the enhanced circulation and targeting conferred by platelet cloaking, we next evaluated innate immune interactions with platelet-coated and uncoated formulations. In I/R-injured mice at seven days post-treatment, circulating levels of complement components C3 and C4 showed no significant differences among treatment groups, indicating minimal systemic complement activation ( ). In parallel, following intravenous administration of Cy5-labeled siCD36-loaded nanomedicines, peripheral blood was collected after 24 h for macrophage isolation. Flow cytometric analysis revealed markedly higher uptake of uncoated siRNA@MSNs by macrophages compared with platelet-coated siCD36@MSNs@Plt ( ), demonstrating that platelet encapsulation substantially reduces macrophage-mediated phagocytic clearance in vivo.
Longitudinal IVIS imaging was performed one week after administration for longer-term distribution and clearance assessment. No detectable fluorescence signal was observed at this time point, indicating efficient systemic clearance of the siRNA payload and minimal long-term tissue retention ( ). Collectively, these results demonstrate that platelet-mediated encapsulation confers a functional self-recognition barrier that limits immune clearance, prolongs circulation, enhances early cardiac targeting, and facilitates efficient clearance of the delivered siRNA cargo, supporting the suitability of siCD36@MSNs@Plt for targeted in vivo RNA delivery following myocardial injury.

2.7

## Platelet-delivered siCD36 therapy promotes cardiac regeneration without inducing lipotoxicity
To assess in vivo therapeutic efficacy, C57BL/6 mice underwent sham or I/R surgery followed by intravenous administration of saline, siCD36@MSNs, or siCD36@MSNs@Plt. Heart tissues were collected four weeks after treatment for analysis. Heart weight-to-body weight (HW/BW) ratios were comparable among groups, indicating that increased CM content was not associated with pathological cardiac hypertrophy ( A). Wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) staining revealed reduced CM cross-sectional area in siCD36-treated hearts, with a more pronounced reduction observed in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group ( B and C). Western blot analysis confirmed effective CD36 knockdown in both treatment groups, with greater suppression achieved by platelet-mediated delivery ( D–G). Correspondingly, Cyclin E1 expression was highest in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group ( E–H), while levels of the maturation-associated histone mark H3K4me3 were most strongly reduced ( F–I). RT-qPCR analysis further showed upregulation of fetal-type CM genes and downregulation of maturity-associated genes following CD36 silencing ( J and K), consistent with a shift toward a less differentiated transcriptional state. 
Histological analyses further supported enhanced CM proliferative activity. Immunostaining for Ki67 and phosphorylated histone H3 (pH3) demonstrated an increased fraction of cycling cardiomyocytes in both siCD36-treated groups, with significantly higher levels in mice receiving siCD36@MSNs@Plt indicating that platelet-mediated siCD36 delivery effectively suppresses CD36 expression and enhances CM cell-cycle activity after I/R injury ( L–O). Furthermore, Oil Red O staining revealed no detectable intracellular lipid droplet accumulation in siCD36-treated hearts ( P). Consistently, immunohistochemical analysis of 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE) showed no increase in lipid peroxidation or oxidative damage following CD36 silencing ( ). These results demonstrate that targeted CD36 inhibition promotes cardiac regeneration without inducing pathological lipid accumulation or lipotoxic injury.
The durability of in vivo CD36 silencing was evaluated to determine whether the extent and duration of knockdown were sufficient to support the therapeutic course. Cardiac CD36 mRNA levels were comparable across groups at baseline (0 week) but were significantly reduced at 2 weeks post-treatment and remained suppressed at 4 weeks ( ), indicating sustained gene silencing throughout the critical post-infarction remodeling period.

2.8

## Platelet-mediated siCD36 delivery attenuates cardiac remodeling and restores ventricular function after I/R injury
To evaluate long-term therapeutic outcomes, hearts were examined four weeks after treatment. Masson's trichrome staining revealed extensive fibrosis in untreated I/R hearts, which was markedly reduced in siCD36-treated groups, with siCD36@MSNs@Plt showing the most pronounced reduction ( A). Quantitative analysis confirmed a significant decrease in collagen deposition, with siCD36@MSNs@Plt-treated hearts exhibiting the lowest fibrotic burden ( B). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining demonstrated preserved left ventricular anterior wall (LVAW) thickness in siCD36-treated groups compared with I/R controls, again with superior preservation in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group ( C and D). 
Functional recovery was also assessed by echocardiography. M-mode imaging demonstrated improved ventricular wall motion in mice treated with siCD36 formulations, particularly siCD36@MSNs@Plt ( E). Quantitative parameters showed significant restoration of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), fractional shortening (FS), and cardiac output (CO) in the siCD36@MSNs@Plt group compared with I/R controls and siCD36@MSNs alone ( F–H). Together these results demonstrate that platelet-mediated siCD36 delivery not only reduces adverse remodeling, as evidenced by decreased fibrosis and preserved wall thickness, but also restores cardiac contractile function.

2.9

## Platelet-mediated siRNA delivery exhibits a favorable safety profile
The standard therapeutic dose for adult mice (25 ± 2 g) is approximately 5 × 107 platelets per animal [ , ]. The systemic safety of siCD36@MSNs@Plt was evaluated in C57BL/6 mice following intravenous administration in both healthy and I/R-injured models. At 24 h post-treatment, coagulation parameters, including prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), thrombin time (TT), and fibrinogen (FIB), showed no significant differences between treatment groups ( ). Consistently, plasma levels of platelet activation markers P-selectin (CD62P) and soluble CD40L (sCD40L) remained unchanged in both normal and I/R-injured mice ( ), indicating the absence of unintended systemic platelet activation. Thromboelastography further confirmed preserved coagulation balance, with no evidence of hypercoagulability or thrombosis following treatment ( ).
Seven days after administration, platelet morphology and indices, including platelet count, platelet distribution width (PDW), mean platelet volume (MPV), and plateletcrit (PCT), remained within physiological ranges ( ). In parallel, screening for platelet-specific antibodies revealed no detectable immune response, suggesting preserved platelet homeostasis ( ). Systemic inflammatory responses were assessed by cytokine profiling, which revealed no significant induction of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, IFN-α, or IFN-γ following siCD36@MSNs@Plt treatment ( ). In addition, serum biochemical markers of hepatic, renal, and cardiac function, including ALT, AST, ALP, LDH, CK-MB, and creatinine, remained within normal ranges ( ), indicating no detectable organ toxicity.
Histopathological examination of the spleen, brain, liver, lung, and kidney at 4 weeks post-treatment revealed preserved tissue architecture with no evidence of microthrombus formation, vascular occlusion, or pathological injury across treatment groups ( ). Long-term systemic safety was further evaluated by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis in mice administered siCD36@MSNs@Plt at a dose of 1.5 mg/kg, corresponding to twice the standard experimental dose. Survival rates remained unchanged over a 28-day observation period in both healthy and I/R-injured mice, indicating that siCD36@MSNs@Plt did not induce significant adverse effects even at elevated siCD36 dosing levels ( ). These data demonstrate that siCD36@MSNs@Plt maintains coagulation homeostasis, preserves platelet integrity, does not elicit inflammatory or immune activation, and does not compromise major organ function. This favorable safety profile supports the translational potential of platelet-engineered siRNA delivery for myocardial repair.

## Discussion & conclusion
Metabolic manipulation of CMs has emerged as a promising strategy for cardiac regeneration, particularly through suppression of FAO to facilitate cell-cycle re-entry of post-mitotic CMs. However, global FAO inhibition, such as through CPT1 ablation, often associated with maladaptive lipid accumulation and pathological remodeling [ , , ], underscoring the need for more selective metabolic intervention. In this study, we demonstrate targeted silencing of CD36 in CMs, offers a refined alternative. RNAi-mediated CD36 knockdown delivered via platelet-engineered platform (siCD36@MSNs@Plt) attenuated FAO without inducing lipotoxicity, thereby enabling a coordinated metabolic-epigenetic shift that restored CM proliferative competence and improved cardiac function following I/R injury.
Efficient, organ-specific delivery remains one of the major bottlenecks for RNAi-based therapeutics, particularly in cardiac applications. While MSNs provide advantages in siRNA loading capacity and controlled release, their rapid systemic clearance and lack of intrinsic targeting substantially limit translational potential [ , , ]. Membrane-coated nanocarriers have partially addressed these challenges by improving circulation stability, but limited membrane yield, low scalability, and storage instability remain unresolved [ , , , , , ]. In this context, intact platelets offer a distinct and clinically relevant delivery strategy. Beyond serving as natural reservoirs for MSN loading, platelets possess injury-homing capability, can be readily obtained from peripheral blood, and are compatible with established clinical storage practices. Our findings show that platelet cloaking prolongs nanoparticle circulation, reduced off-target clearance, and conferred selective delivery to infarcted myocardium, achieving efficient CD36 knockdown in vivo.
Importantly, the therapeutic benefits of siCD36@MSNs@Plt extended beyond molecular gene silencing. Treatment resulted in reduced myocardial fibrosis, preservation of ventricular wall thickness, and improved contractile performance, as consistently validated through histological, molecular, and echocardiographic analyses. Notably, these regenerative outcomes were achieved without evidence of pathological lipid accumulation, thrombosis, or systemic toxicity, as supported by comprehensive safety profiling. The combination of robust therapeutic efficacy with a favorable safety profile positions siCD36@MSNs@Plt as a clinically relevant framework for precision gene therapy in ischemic heart disease.
Despite these encouraging findings, several challenges remain for translational advancement. Current platelet loading relies on passive uptake through the OCS, and future efforts may explore accelerated or surface-engineered strategies to further improve loading efficiency and batch-to-batch reproducibility. In addition, donor-to-donor variability and formulation stability during long-term storage will require systematic optimization to ensure clinical robustness. Platelet recruitment to injured myocardium is known to involve adhesion receptors such as P-selectin and GP1ba, but identification of dominant injury-homing receptors in future studies may enable development of simplified, receptor-mimetic delivery platforms with improved batch consistency and manufacturing scalability. Although NMCMs were employed for mechanistic in vitro studies, owing to their experimental accessibility and metabolic responsiveness, their intrinsic proliferative capacity differs from that of adult CMs. While this model enabled precise dissection of metabolic and epigenetic mechanisms, future validation in adult human CMs or engineered myocardial tissues will be important to further strengthen translational relevance.
The present study demonstrates efficient clearance of the siRNA payload and absence of overt organ toxicity within the relevant post-infarction window, however, detailed investigations into the long-term fate and biodegradation of the mesoporous silica carrier, including quantitative tracking of residual material and assessment beyond the remodeling phase, will be important to fully define long-term biosafety. Moreover, although no structural or functional cardiac abnormalities were detected, more comprehensive electrophysiological evaluations, such as ECG-based monitoring, will be valuable in future studies to assess long-term electrical stability, particularly for cardiac-targeted RNA therapies that modulate metabolic pathways. Moreover, while no structural or functional cardiac abnormalities were observed, metabolic reprogramming of cardiomyocytes may theoretically influence membrane lipid composition, ion channel function, or electrical stability. Accordingly, comprehensive electrophysiological evaluations, including ECG-based monitoring and assessment of arrhythmogenic susceptibility, represent an essential prerequisite for future clinical translation of cardiac-targeted RNA therapies that modulate core metabolic pathways. Finally, validation in large-animal models and exploration of temporally controlled or combinatorial gene-silencing strategies will be critical steps toward clinical translation.
In conclusion, we present a platelet-engineered RNAi therapeutic platform that integrates metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming to promote myocardial regeneration. By selectively targeting fatty acid uptake through CD36 silencing, siCD36@MSNs@Plt alleviates lipotoxic stress, primes cardiomyocytes for cell-cycle re-entry, and restores cardiac function following ischemic injury. This bioresponsive delivery strategy establishes a robust and translatable framework for advancing RNAi-based precision therapeutics in ischemic cardiomyopathy. Looking forward, the MSN-platelet hybrid platform may be expanded into a versatile gene delivery system capable of accommodating a broad range of nucleic acid modalities, including shRNA, mRNA, CRISPR-Cas components, and plasmid DNA. Such adaptability would enable tailored genetic and epigenetic interventions across cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular indications, highlighting platelet-mediated nanocarriers as a broadly applicable strategy for next-generation precision gene therapies.

## Methods and materials
4.1

## siCD36@MSNs synthesis and characterization
Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) were synthesized using a previously reported the sol-gel method [ ], which was briefly described in the supply. TEM (Thermo/Talos L120C) was used to evaluate morphology. Hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential were measured by DLS (ZetaView, Particle Metrix). A 4 M CaCl2 stock solution was prepared by dissolving 4.5 g anhydrous CaCl2 (MW 110.98, Sigma-Aldrich) in 10 mL RNase-free water, centrifuging to remove precipitates, and storing at 4 °C. Three siRNA duplexes targeting different regions of the murine CD36 gene were synthesized (Synthace),And the sequences were provided in the supply. The three siRNAs were mixed at a 1:1:1 M ratio to yield the siCD36 pool. For loading, MSNs (1 mg, dispersed in 100 μL RNase-free water) were mixed with pooled siCD36(150 μL, 150 μM) and CaCl2 (750 μL, 4 M) and incubated for 60 min at room temperature. siRNA-loaded MSN complexes were purified by sequential washing and centrifugation with RNase-free water, 70% ethanol, and 100% ethanol. Loading efficiency was determined by measuring unbound siRNA in the supernatant using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific). Loading efficiency = (Total siRNA - Free siRNA)/Total siRNA ∗ 100%. For release studies, siCD36@MSNs were suspended in PBS at 37 °C and aliquots were collected over time to quantify cumulative siRNA release. The antibodies and siCD36 product information used in this study can be referred in (Supplementary Information).

4.2

## Platelet isolation and drug loading
Mice platelets were isolated and purified using established protocols, which was briefly described in the supporting information. Approximately 5 × 107 platelets were co-incubated with siCD36@MSNs(180 μg siCD36)at 37 °C for 4-5 h with gentle agitation. The suspension was centrifuged at 800×g for 20 min at room temperature. Pellets were washed with Tyrode's buffer to remove free nanoparticles, re-centrifuged, and resuspended to obtain purified siCD36@MSNs@Plt complexes, which were used immediately.

4.3

## Neonatal cardiomyocyte culture
Primary neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes (P1 NMCMs) were isolated from C57BL/6 pups using the neonatal heart dissociation kit (Miltenyi, No. 130-098-373) following the manufacturer's instructions. Ventricles were minced into ∼1 mm3 fragments and enzymatically digested to yield a single-cell suspension. After differential adhesion for 15 min to remove fibroblasts, cardiomyocytes were cultured in DMEM (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS).

4.4

## Transwell assays
Cy5-siCD36@MSNs@Plt(100 nM Cy5-siCD36) were placed in the upper chamber and P1 NMCMs in the lower chamber. After 24 h, thrombin (100 U/mL) or PBS was added to the upper chamber for 12 h to stimulate nanoparticle release, and NMCMs were cultured for an additional 12 h. Internalization of Cy5-labeled siCD36@MSNs by NMCMs was quantified using confocal microscopy after MitoTracker staining. CD36 knockdown was assessed by Western blot and immunofluorescence. Lipid uptake was analyzed using the BODIPY probe. Cell-cycle markers (Cyclin E1, Aurora B, pH3, and Ki67) were assessed by Western blot or immunofluorescence.

4.5

## In vivo tracing and tissue-targeting specificity of siRNA@MSNs@Plt
To assess in vivo targeting and tissue distribution, Cy5-labeled siCD36@MSNs, siCD36@MSNs@Plt, or PBS (200 μL per mouse) were administered via intravenous injection 30 min after I/R surgery. Myocardial accumulation and systemic biodistribution were evaluated at 2 h post-injection using in vivo fluorescence imaging (IVIS). Following imaging, mice were euthanized and major organs, including heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney, were harvested for ex vivo IVIS analysis to quantify organ-specific fluorescence signals. Cardiac tissue sections were further subjected to immunohistochemical staining with anti–cardiac troponin T (cTnT; Abcam) to assess myocardial localization at the cellular level.

4.6

## Animal experiments and treatments
All animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (SRRSH202306045). Male C57BL/6 mice (7-8 weeks, 25 ± 2 g) were obtained from the Experimental Animal Center of the Zhejiang Academy of Medical Sciences. Myocardial I/R was induced by ligating the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery for 60 min with 8-0 silk suture, followed by reperfusion. Sham-operated mice underwent the same procedure without ligation. Thirty mins after reperfusion, mice were randomized to receive saline, siCD36@MSNs, or siCD36@MSNs@Plt via tail vein injection (siCD36:0.75 mg/kg, Plt:2∗1010/kg). Echocardiography (Vevo, VisualSonics, Japan) was performed 28 days post-surgery. Mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation, and hearts and major organs were collected for histology.

4.7

## Regeneration analysis
Cardiomyocyte cell cycle and maturation-related proteins (cyclin E1) and genes (Nppa, Nppb, Acta1, Myh7; Tnni3, Mylk3, Myocd) were analyzed by RT-qPCR and Western blotting. Epigenetic regulation was assessed via Western blotting targeting H3K4me3. To evaluate myocardial regeneration, heart tissue sections were stained with WGA, Ki67, and pH3. Concurrently, lipid deposition in cardiac tissue was examined using Oil Red O staining. Four weeks post-treatment, cardiac function was assessed by transthoracic echocardiography (Visual Sonics Vevo 770 system), with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS), and cardiac output (CO) calculated from M-mode tracings of six consecutive cardiac cycles. Subsequently, hearts were excised, sectioned, and subjected to H&E's, Masson's trichrome, and additional histological staining to assess tissue necrosis and collagen deposition.

4.8

## Broadly targeted lipidomics analysis
Cell samples were retrieved from a −80 °C freezer and thawed on ice. Heart tissue samples (20 mg) were homogenized in 1 mL of extraction solvent mixture (methanol:MTBE = 1:3, v: v) containing an internal standard. The homogenate was vortexed for 15 min, followed by the addition of 200 μL of water. After vortexing for an additional 1 min, the mixture was centrifuged at 12000 rpm at 4 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, 500 μL of the upper phase (supernatant) was collected and concentrated under vacuum. The resulting residue was reconstituted in 200 μL of mobile phase B and analyzed using an LC ESI-MS/MS system (UPLC, Shim-pack UFLC SHIMADZU CBM A system; MS, QTRAP System) at Wuhan Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

4.9

## Statistical analysis
Data are expressed as mean ± SD. Analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (v10.0). Two-tailed unpaired Student's t-tests were used for comparisons between two groups, and one-way ANOVA for multiple groups. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. Sample sizes (n) are indicated in figure legends.
