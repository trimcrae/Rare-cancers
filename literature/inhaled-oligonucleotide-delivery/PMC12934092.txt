## Abstract

## Background
Ferroptosis plays a crucial role in lung diseases, including bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), but its involvement in BPD pathogenesis and impact on pulmonary vascular development remain unclear. This study investigated ferroptosis in BPD and evaluated sodium propionate’s (SP) therapeutic potential in regulating ferroptosis and promoting pulmonary angiogenesis.

## Methods
Serum markers of ferroptosis and oxidative stress were measured in preterm infants (< 32 weeks) with and without BPD. BPD rats and human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) models were established through hyperoxia induction. Sprague-Dawley rats were randomly assigned to four experimental groups: normoxia control (CON), normoxia with sodium propionate treatment (CON + SP), hyperoxia-induced BPD model (BPD), and hyperoxia with SP treatment (BPD + SP). In vitro experiments, HUVEC cells were respectively treated with hyperoxia and SP, and transfected with siRNA to detect the role of the SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway. Ferroptosis was evaluated by measuring lipid peroxidation (LPO), malondialdehyde (MDA), reactive oxygen species (ROS), and glutathione (GSH). Angiogenesis-related genes (VEGFA, CD31) and angiogenic ability were evaluated by Western blot, RT-PCR and in vitro experiments.

## Results
Serum LPO and iron levels were significantly elevated in BPD infants. SP alleviated lung injury in BPD rats. SP significantly inhibited PTGS2 expression, decreased ROS, MDA, and LPO levels, and increased GSH in BPD models. It also enhanced mRNA and protein expression of SLC7A11, GPX4, VEGFA, and CD31. Silencing SLC7A11 or GPX4 counteracted SP’s protective effects in hyperoxia-exposed HUVECs.

## Conclusions
Ferroptosis contributes to BPD pathogenesis. SP effectively inhibits ferroptosis and promotes angiogenesis in experimental BPD models, suggesting a novel avenue for potential combination therapies in BPD treatment.

## Introduction
Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) is a prevalent chronic lung disease predominantly affecting preterm infants. BPD was first described and characterized by Northway in 1967 and defined as alveolarization, parenchymal fibrosis, and angiogenesis disorders. In a Chinese study involving 1,044 infants with a gestational age < 32 weeks, the prevalence of BPD was 78.2% [ ]. Similarly, a clinical study conducted in Tunisia from 2017 to 2021 reported a BPD prevalence of 37.7% among 231 neonates born at 26–31 weeks’ gestation [ ]. At birth, the lungs of preterm infants are in transition from the canalicular to the alveolar (saccular) stage of development. Preterm delivery disrupts normal lung maturation, increasing susceptibility to injury. BPD is a multifactorial disease influenced by incomplete lung development, prenatal and postnatal infections, nutritional deficiencies, and mechanical ventilation-induced injury. The increase of oxygen concentration can cause abnormal changes in neonatal lung tissue, including fibroblasts proliferation, leading to pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. High or moderate concentrations of oxygen carries significant risks, mainly related to ROS production [ ]. Redox homeostasis plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of oxygen-induced lung injury [ ]. Mechanical ventilation compromises cellular defense mechanisms of cells, induces inflammatory responses and cell damage, and cell death occurs [ ]. However, clinical practice shows that merely reducing invasive interventions (e.g., intubation) without optimizing the overall respiratory support strategy may not effectively reduce BPD incidence and could, in some contexts, adversely affect outcomes due to inadequate support [ ]. Therefore, exploring non-invasive or adjunctive therapies that can mitigate lung injury and promote lung development remains crucial. Emerging research implicates additional pathogenic factors in BPD development. A common cause of chronic lung disease in preterm infants includes disturbance of gut and airway microbiota, and preterm infants with chronic lung disease have a lower diversity of fecal microbiota than controls [ ]. The gut-distal organ axis of gut and distal organs have been well-established, and the influence of gut microbiota on infant lungs not only after birth, but also before and during birth cannot be ignored [ ]. In healthy term neonates, facultative anaerobes predominate during the first few days of life but change over time [ ]. The intestinal microbial colonization of preterm infants is different from that of full-term infants, and the intestinal microbiota of preterm infants, being influenced by maternal complications, delivery mode, antibiotics and breastfeeding [ ]. According to murine models and organoid systems, antimicrobial peptides are identified as the crucial mediators of the lung gut axis, the depletion of antimicrobial peptides can change the composition of gut microbiota, and the exogenous administration of antimicrobial peptides in mice can alleviate the symptoms of lung injury [ ]. Preclinical studies using rat models of BPD have demonstrated that probiotic interventions significantly attenuate characteristic disease manifestations [ ].
Ferroptosis, as a mode of cell death, has been shown to exist in BPD [ , ]. Light chain subunit solute carrier member 7 (SLC7A11), a multi-channel transmembrane protein, mediates lular cystine uptake in cells. Knockdown of SLC7A11 expression by drugs or gene knockout induces ferroptosis [ ]. In mammals, glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is a selenoenzyme that inhibits IL-1 induced NFκB activation in the cytoplasm [ ], GPX4 is also a classical ferroptosis defense gene [ , ]. Studies have shown that SLC7A11/GPX4 axis is an important signaling axis in ferroptosis signaling pathway [ ]. Overexpression of est-1 could alleviate BPD symptoms in A549 and mice through Nrf2/HO-1 [ ].
Sodium propionate(SP) is a short-chain fatty acid produced by gut microbiota metabolize dietary fiber. Previous studies from our group have shown that SP can ameliorate LPS-induced lung injury through the Nrf2 pathway [ ]. Nrf2 knockout mice showed lower resistance to increased Escherichia coli in the airways, especially under hyperoxia, but the NrF2-mediated antioxidant effect was enhanced after intervention with a respiratory probiotic mixture [ ]. According to previous reports, ingestion of SP through breast milk can significantly reduce airway inflammation in a mouse model of asthma [ ]. A study of 2,227 children linked longer breastfeeding to normative infant microbiome development [ ], a mechanism potentially underlying the protective effect of breastfeeding against BPD in preterm infants as shown in a meta-analysis [ ]. Oxidative stress and inflammation interact with each other in the pathogenesis of BPD. Anti-oxidation or anti-inflammation alone is not an effective therapy, and therapeutic drugs with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties will show certain effects [ ]. The anti-inflammatory effect of SP is unquestionable [ , ], However, SP regulating ferroptosis in BPD through SLC7A11/GPX4 signaling has not been studied, and more studies are needed to better understand how SP affects BPD.

## Materials and methods

## Clinical subjects and samples

## Subject population and study design
The subjects of this study were preterm infants less than 32 weeks of gestation who were admitted to the neonatal intensive Care Unit of Wuxi Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital (Wuxi, China) from January 2024 to January 2025. This observational study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Wuxi Maternal and Child Health Hospital. The study design was approved by the ethics committee of the hospital (2024-01-1024-24). This study obtained written informed consent from the guardians of the newborns to participate in the research. These preterm infants included two groups: non-BPD group (n = 32) and BPD group (n = 22). The definition of BPD was according to the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development in 2018 [ ]. The inclusion criteria for the control group included: (1) very preterm infants with gestational age of 24 to 32 weeks; (2) appropriate for gestational age; Exclusion criteria: BPD group and control group exclusion criteria: (1) severe congenital malformation; (2) severe disease at birth with poor prognosis; (3) abandoning treatment or transferring to another hospital.

## Data collection
Clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical record. After enrollment, maternal information, demographic details, and clinical data were collected for each infant. Prenatal data included maternal age, duration of pregnancy and delivery, pregnancy-related conditions, and prenatal treatment.

## Collection of plasma samples
Umbilical artery blood samples from subjects at delivery as well as peripheral blood samples on day 28 were collected and stored at 4℃ for no more than 24 h. After centrifugation, plasma samples were collected by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min and rapidly stored at -80℃.

## Enzyme‑linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The GPX4 and SLC7A11 ELISA kits (EH8916, ER1243, Fine Biotech, CHINA) were used to detect SLC7A11 and GPX4 levels in human serum. Serum was diluted and added directly to the test wells according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 50 µL of standard, blank or sample was added to each well and incubated at 37 ℃ for 45 min. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, and results were calculated from a standard curve.

## Detection of GSH
The content of GSH in serum of blood was detected according to the instructions of Nanjing Jianjian Bioengineering Research Institute (A006-1-1, Nanjing Jianjian Bioengineering Research Institute, Nanjing, China). According to the instructions of Nanjing Jianzhen Bioengineering Research Institute, the solvent storage solution and double distilled water of reagent four standards should be used in accordance with 1:9 dilution into reduced glutathione standard solvent application solution, take the reagent five reduced glutathione standard powder, with reduced glutathione standard solvent application solution 10mL dissolved into a concentration of 1 mmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution, Then 200 µL of 1 mmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution and 980 µL of standard application solution were used to prepare 20 µmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution. Serum 50 µL was mixed with reagent 200 µL, then centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 min, and the supernatant was used. A transparent 96-well plate was used for sample addition, which was divided into blank wells, standard wells and assay wells. 100 µL of reagent I, 25 µL of reagent III and 100 µL of reagent II were added to the blank wells, and 100 µL of 20 µmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution, 25 µL of reagent III and 100 µL of reagent II were added to the standard wells. 100 µL supernatant mixed with serum and reagent I, 25 µL reagent III and 100 µL reagent Ⅱ were added into the assay wells. The 96-well plate was gently shaken and mixed, and left for 5 min. The microplate reader was preheated in advance, and the absorbance of each well was measured at 405 nm and calculated according to the instructions.

## Detection of LPO
According to the instructions of Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Bioengineering(A106-1-3, Nanjing, China), first mix the matrix stock solution and matrix dilution solution in the lipid peroxide test kit at a volume ratio of 3:1 to form reagent-one application solution. Take 1.5 mL of capped centrifuge tubes and divide them into blank tubes, standard tubes and test tubes. Add 200 µL of anhydrous ethanol to the blank tubes, reagent Ⅲ (10 µmol/L) standard to the standard tubes, and 200 µL of serum sample to the test tubes. Add 650 µL of reagent - application solution to each centrifuge tube, then cover the tube and mix thoroughly. After thorough mixing, add 150 µL of reagent Ⅱ to each centrifuge tube, vortex to mix well, and incubate at 45 °C for 1 h at 4000 rmp. Centrifuge for 10 min, then transfer the supernatant to a transparent 96-well plate. Preheat the microplate reader to 37 °C in advance, and measure the absorbance of each well at a wavelength of 586 nm. Calculate according to the instructions.

## Detection of serum iron
According to the instructions of Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Bioengineering(A039-1-1, Nanjing, China), first pour the A and B powders of the kit into the C solution to obtain the iron chromogenic agent and store it at 4 degrees Celsius away from light. Take 1.5 mL of capped centrifuge tubes and divide them into blank tubes, standard tubes and test tubes. Add 500 µL of double-distilled water to the blank tubes, 500 µL of 2 mg/L iron standard application solution to the standard tubes, and 500 µL of serum to be tested to the test tubes. Subsequently, 1.5 mL of iron chromogenic reagent was added to each tube, mixed well, and then immersed in boiling water for 5 min. After cooling, it was centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 min. 1 mL of the supernatant was taken, and zero was adjusted using double-distilled water. The absorbance OD value of each tube was measured at a wavelength of 520 nm.

## Animal experiments

## Animal model
There were 16 healthy female Sprague-Dawley(SD) rats and 8 male rats, weighing 80-100 g, all from Shanghai Slek Laboratory Animal Co., LTD. These mice were allowed to obtain standard rodent food and water at will. The temperature of the experimental animal environment is controlled at 16-26 ℃, and the relative humidity is maintained at 30%-70%. The natural light cycle is simulated, and a 12-hour alternation of light and dark is adopted. The rats were placed in the quarantine room. An experimental plan was prepared before the research but was not registered. After the quarantine period, the female and male rats were paired in a 2:1 ratio and placed in cages. Two weeks after being placed in cages, the non-pregnant female and male rats were taken out, and two pregnant rats were placed in each cage until they gave birth. Four cages of rats were placed in hyperoxia, with one female rat and ten young rats in each cage, and one cage formed a group. Each rat is an experimental unit. The remaining female mice were exposed to normoxia and exchanged with the female mice in the hyperoxic chamber every day to prevent the female mice in the hyperoxic chamber from being poisoned by oxygen. The remaining rat pups served as the control group. Based on a modeling method previously established [ ], neonatal SD rats were randomly divided into the control group, the control drug administration group, the model group and the sp treatment group within 12 h after natural delivery. The oxygen concentration inhaled by rats in the model group and the sp treatment group was 85%O2, while that inhaled air (FiO2 21%) by rats in the control group and the control dosing group. Sodium propionate (A600882-0250, Sangon, Shanghai, China) was dissolved in 0.1 M PBS (E607008, Sangon, Shanghai, China) and subcutaneously injected intraperitoneally at a dose of 500 mg/kg once a day for 7 consecutive days. This dosage was selected based on previous studies. After the operation is completed, put it back into the hyperoxic chamber. The number of 10 newborn rats in each group is due to the fact that during the modeling process, there may be situations where the mother rats do not feed or step on them, resulting in a loss of the number of newborn rats. After the 14th day, the model was established. After the modeling was completed, the eyeball was removed and blood was collected under anesthesia with 1.5% isoflurane. After blood collection, rats were euthanized by cervical dislocation following the experimental protocol to remove the lung tissue from both sides. Animal Welfare Monitoring Protocol: Daily health checks were conducted, with specific criteria for pain and distress assessment (e.g., monitoring for labored breathing, significant weight loss (> 20%), reduced mobility, and inability to feed). Predefined humane endpoints were established, the attainment of which would necessitate early euthanasia. All procedures were performed under deep anesthesia to minimize suffering. All animal study procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Care and Use of Jiangnan University (Approval No. JN. No 20240430S0241230[216]). Xiaoqing chen will be aware of the grouping situation at different stages of the experiment (allocation, experiment implementation, outcome evaluation, data analysis). The criteria for the success of the hyperoxia-induced BPD mouse model are as follows: the mice show behavioral changes such as sluggish response, slow weight gain, dull and lackluster hair, cyanosis at the tip of the nose and rapid breathing. The development of alveoli was observed by HE staining. The development of alveoli was quantitatively evaluated by radial alveolar count (RAC) and respiratory membrane thickness. Pathological changes of BPD characterized mainly by alveolar block occur in lung tissue: enlarged alveolar cavities with reduced numbers, disappearance of normal alveolar structures, alveolar fusion, significantly widened alveolar septum, and disordered angiogenesis, etc. Select the rats that have been successfully modeled for experiments. SD rats are currently the commonly used animal for establishing hyperoxia models of BPD, and their breeding numbers are relatively stable. BPD is a chronic lung disease in newborns, usually caused by long-term ventilation, and further determined by the age of premature infants and their oxygen demand for supplementary oxygen. Animal models of BPD are usually established using 85% oxygen concentration. The BPD model has been verified and applied in SD rats [ ].

## Morphological observation of lung tissues
After the modeling of the rat pups was completed, the eyes were removed under anesthesia, and blood was collected to ensure a clear field of vision during thoracotomy. The skin of the rat’s thoracic cavity and neck was cut open. Surgical sutures were used to free the trachea, and the right lung was ligated. After making a intubation incision in the trachea, 4% paraformaldehyde was injected. When the left lung was filled, it was ligated and immersed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h. After paraffin embedding, continuous sections were made along the sagittal plane with a thickness of 4 μm. Radial alveolar count (RAC) : Under a light microscope, a vertical line is drawn from the center of the terminal bronchioles to the nearest connective tissue diaphragm. The number of alveoli on this vertical line is the RAC value, which reflects the number of alveoli contained in the terminal respiratory unit and serves as an indicator for detecting the degree of lung development. Eight slices are selected from each group. Each slice is counted three times and the average value is taken. Mean Linear Intercept (MLI) : Divide the length of a line drawn on the lung section by the total number of interceptions calculated within that line, the average alveolar diameter. Select 8 sections from each group, choose 3 fields of view for each section, and measure at least 10 alveoli in each field of view. Take the average. alveolar septal thickness (AST) is as follows: 8 sections are selected from each group, and 3 fields are randomly selected from each section. The AST in each field is measured and the average value is taken. To determine the immunoreactivity of ferroptosis marker protein CD31, lung tissue sections were immunostained, followed by detection of avidin-biotin complex signal with biotinylated secondary anti-body and 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) solution.

## Cell experiments

## Cell culture
HUVECs (iCell-h110, Cellverse Co., Ltd. China) were cultured in Endothelial Cell Medium (ECM) (Cat. No.1001. Sciencell, American), at 37 °C in 85% air and 5% CO2. Cultured HUVEC cells were subjected to hyperoxia induction and overexpression assays. To conduct the hyperoxia experiments, we used subconfluent cells (at 70% with approximately 350-400 cells/mm2) and placed them in sealed glass chambers filled with a gas mixture of 85% O2 and 5% CO2. The cells were then incubated at 37 °C for 48 h. To serve as a control, cells were maintained under normal air conditions, of 21% O2 and 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The gas and medium were regularly changed every 2 days. The siRNA sequence that targets human SLC7A11、GPX4 (siSLC7A11、siGPX4) was designed and synthesized by KeyGEN BioTECH (JiangSu, China) and transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 (L3000-015, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The plasmid DNA clones for human SLC7A11、GPX4 and the empty vector were purchased from KeyGEN BioTECH (JiangSu, China).
To examine the effect of SP on HUVECs under hyperoxic conditions, the cells were grouped into the Control group, Hyperoxia group, and Hyperoxia + SP group. The Control group was maintained in normal air. The Hyperoxia group was exposed to hyperoxia, while the Hyperoxia + SP group was pretreated with SP 2 h before exposure to hyperoxia. SP (0.6mM) was dissolved in ECM.

## Immunofluorescence staining
HUVECs were cultured overnight in 12-well plates at a density of 1 × 104/cm2. The cells were divided into four groups: The control group was placed in 21%O2, the control + SP group was placed in 21%O2 and SP, the hyperoxia group was placed in 85%O2, and the intervention group was placed in 85%O2 and SP. After the cell model was established, the cell culture plate was removed, the medium was removed, and the cell culture plate was gently washed three times with PBS. Each well was added 4% paraformaldehyde that could not pass the cell slide and fixed for 20 min. After the fixation, 4% paraformaldehyde was removed, gently washed three times with PBS, and the cells were permeabilized with 0.2% triton-x-100 (P0096-100 mL, Beyotime Biotechnology., shanghai, China). Triton-x-100 was removed at the end of permeabilization, washed gently three times with PBS, blocked by adding 5% goat serum (C0265, Beyotime Biotechnology., shanghai, China), and blocked for 30 min at room temperature. The antibody was diluted at a ratio of1:500 using the diluent of the primary antibody. After blocking, the blocking solution was removed, and the diluted antibody was added directly without washing. The amount of liquid could cover the cell slide, and the antibody was incubated at 4 ℃ overnight. The second day, the cell culture plate was removed, the primary antibody incubated overnight was collected, gently washed three times with PBS, added fluorescent secondary antibody (K1202, K1211, APExBIO Technology, American) diluted in PBS, incubated at 37 ℃ in the dark for 40 min, kept in the dark, removed fluorescent secondary antibody, gently washed three times with PBS, grabbed the cell slide with forceps, dropped Antifade Mounting Medium with DAPI (P0131-5 mL, Beyotime Biotechnology., shanghai, China), and removed the cell slide. The plates were overlaid on the Antifade Mounting Medium with DAPI and imaged using a confocal microscope (Nikon, Japan) after sealing with neutral resin (C0173-100 mL, Beyotime Biotechnology., shanghai, China).

The content of GSH in HUVECs was detected according to the instructions of Nanjing Jianjian Bioengineering Research Institute (A006-1-1, Nanjing Jianjian Bioengineering Research Institute, Nanjing, China). According to the instructions of Nanjing Jianzhen Bioengineering Research Institute, the solvent storage solution and double distilled water of reagent Ⅳ standards should be used in accordance with 1:9 dilution into reduced glutathione standard solvent application solution, take the reagent Ⅴ reduced glutathione standard powder, with reduced glutathione standard solvent application solution 10 mL dissolved into a concentration of 1 mmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution, Then 200 µL of 1 mmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution and 980 µL of standard application solution were used to prepare 20 µmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution. After collecting the cells, wash them 1 to 2 times with PBS, then centrifuge at low speed to collect the precipitated cells. Add 0.3 to 0.5 mL of reagent to suspend the cells, and break the cells by ultrasound or manual grinding. Take 0.1 mL of the broken cell suspension, add 0.1 mL of reagent and mix well. Centrifuge at 3500 rpm for 10 min, and take the supernatant for color development reaction. A transparent 96-well plate was used for sample addition, which was divided into blank wells, standard wells and assay wells. 100 µL of reagent I, 25 µL of reagent III and 100 µL of reagent II were added to the blank wells, and 100 µL of 20 µmol/L reduced glutathione standard solution, 25 µL of reagent III and 100 µL of reagent II were added to the standard wells. Add 100 µL of the supernatant of the samples, 25 µL of reagent Ⅲ and 100 µL of reagent Ⅱ to the test well. The 96-well plate was gently shaken and mixed, and left for 5 min. The microplate reader was preheated in advance, and the absorbance of each well was measured at 405 nm and calculated according to the instructions.

## Detection of ROS
The levels of reactive oxygen species of HUVEC cells were measured by DCFH-DA (E004-1-1, Nanjing jiancheng, nanjing, China), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. HUVEC cells were seeded onto 12-well plates and subjected to further treatment. Then diluted fluorescent probes were added, and incubated in a 37 °C cell culture incubator for 20-30 min. Finally, cells were washed three times with PBS and observed under a fluorescence microscope. The cellular fluorescence intensities were quantified using the Image J software.

## Lipid peroxidation probe
In order to detect cellular Fe2+, Lipid Peroxidation Probe (L248, Dojindo Laboratories, Japan) was used according to the manufacturer’s protocol. HUVEC cells were treated and then stained with the final concentration of 1 µmol/L FerroOrange for half hour at 37 °C. Images were obtained through Confocal microscopy and fluorescence intensity was measured by Image J.

## Tube formation assay
Tube formation assays were performed according to a published protocol [ ] with slight modifications. Briefly 10 uL of Matrigel (356234, Cornging, American) was coated onto each well of a 96-well plate and incubated at 37 °C for an hour until the coated membrane extract solidified. Next, differentiated progeny of HUVEC cells (1.25 × 104 cells) were added to the coated wells and incubated at 37 °C for 6 h. Images were taken with a microscope at 100×magnification. Tube formation ability was quantified at 100×magnification using Image J (Wayne Rasband, National Institute of Health, USA) and Angiogenesis Analyzer plugin (Gilles Carpentier, Universite Paris Est Creteil Val de Marne, France).

## Statistical analysis
Cohort characteristics were presented through descriptive statistics, including median, mean, standard deviation, frequency, and percentage. Data analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 software. For normally distributed continuous variables, t-tests or one-way ANOVA were employed, with ANOVA combined with Dunnett post-hoc test for secondary analysis of time-dependent data. Two-group comparisons between multiple groups were conducted using Tukey HSD test, and correlation plots were labeled. For non-normally distributed data, Mann-WhitneyU test was used. Categorical variables were analyzed using chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as applicable. Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.05.

## Result

## Exploration of markers related to serum ferroptosis in infants with non-BPD and BPD
Previous studies have shown that cord blood iron concentration in preterm infants tends to increase compared with full-term infants [ ]. It has been documented that the increase of oxidative metabolites (MDA) and the decrease of antioxidants (GSH, etc.) in preterm birth can cause adverse pregnancy outcomes [ ]. Compared to non-BPD controls (n = 34), BPD infants (n = 25) showed significantly elevated serum LPO and iron with depleted antioxidants (GSH, GPX4, SLC7A11) in both cord and day-28 blood samples(Fig.  ) (Supplementary Tables 1,2 for full cohort characteristics).
 

## Ferroptosis and impaired vascular development are present in the lungs of BPD rats
To investigate ferroptosis in the lung of BPD rats and the symptoms of impaired vascular development, we created a neonatal rat model of BPD. Schematic diagram of the experimental design and timeline for the neonatal rat model of BPD (Fig.  ). Figure A, B shows that neonatal rats exposed to hyperoxia had a lower body weight than control rats. HE showed ruptured alveoli and reduced alveolar number in neonatal rats exposed to hyperoxia (Fig.  C). Exposure to hyperoxia reduced RAC in rats compared with controls (Fig.  E).Mean Linear Intercept (MLI) and alveolar septal thickness (AST) can be seen in Supplementary Fig.  A, B. Neonatal rats exposed to 85%O2 had significantly lower GPX4 and SLC7A11 expression and increased PTGS2 content compared with the control group (Fig.  D, F, G). Compared with the control group, the levels of LPO and MDA in the serum of the hyperoxia exposed rats increased, while the levels of GSH and SOD decreased (Fig.  H-K). Compared with the control group, the BPD group showed a decrease in the contents of angiogenesis-related genes VEGFA and CD31 (Fig.  L-N). ELISA detection of GPX4 and SLC7A11 in rat serum indicated the same trend(Supplementary Fig. 2A, B). These results suggest ferroptosis and impaired vascular development in the lungs of neonatal rats exposed to hyperoxia.
 

## SP inhibits ferroptosis in the lungs of BPD rats
Intraperitoneal injection of SP increased the expression of antiferroptosis-related genes in the lungs of rats exposed to hyperoxia. Compared with the BPD group, the expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11 was increased, and the expression of PTGS2 was decreased in the SP intervention group (Fig.  A-E). SP reversed hyperoxia induced increases in LPO, MDA and 4-HNE levels (Fig.  F-H) and increased SOD, GSH, GPX4 and SLC7A11contents (Fig.  I-K, Supplementary Fig. 2C).
 

## SP alleviates hyperoxia-induced lung injury in rats
Intraperitoneal injection of SP prevented weight loss, alveolar simplification, and reduction in number in rats exposed to hyperoxia (Fig.  A-C, Supplementary. Figure 1C, D). The decreased expression of VEGFA and CD31 in the lungs of rats under hyperoxia condition was reversed by SP (Fig.  D-H). The immunohistochemical graph of CD31 shows the same trend(Supplementary. Figure 3).
 

## SP alleviates the impaired angiogenesis of HUVECs induced by hyperoxia
To explore the mechanism by which hyperoxia blocks vascular development in BPD, we used HUVECs to create a BPD cell model. Firstly, the concentration of SP added to HUVECs was explored, and it was found that 0.6 mM SP could increase the cell survival rate to a greater extent in the hyperoxia environment (Fig.  A). The expression of CD31 and VEGFA in HUVECs decreased under hyperoxia condition, which was alleviated by SP intervention (Fig.  B-H). In addition, the intervention of SP inhibited the endothelial-mesenchymal transition of HUVEC cells. Under hyperoxic conditions, the expression of a-SMA in the cells increased, and SP inhibited this trend(Supplementary. Figure 4A, B). Subsequently, in vitro angiogenesis experiments were carried out on the model cells, and it was found that the tube formation ability of HUVECs treated with hyperoxia was significantly lower than that of the control group, and SP intervention restored the angiogenesis ability of HUVECs (Fig.  ).
 

## SP inhibits hyperoxia-induced ferroptosis in HUVECs
SP intervention significantly improved the decreased expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11 in HUVECs treated with hyperoxia compared with those treated with hyperoxia, and SP also reduced the content of PTGS2 in hyperoxia cells (Fig.  A-E). At the same time, the content of LPO and ROS decreased and the content of GSH increased in HUVECs after SP treatment (Fig.  F-J).
 

## Effects of SLC7A11/GPX4 upstream and downstream knockout on ferroptosis and vascular development
To further illustrate the role of SLC7A11/GPX4 axis in the improvement of BPD by SP, we knocked down SLC7A11 and GPX4, respectively. First, the siRNA was validated. The siRNA with high knockdown efficiency was selected for treatment of cells (Figs.  and A-C). We found that after knocking out SLC7A11 and GPX4, SP intervention in cells exposed to hyperoxia did not have the effect of antagonizing ferroptosis and improving vascular development, PTGS2 was increased, and CD31 and VEGFA were decreased (Figs.  and D-E). After knocking down SLC7A11 and GPX4, the ROS expression and LPO expression of cells treated with SP intervention and hyperoxia model were not decreased (Figs.  and F, G and H-J), and the angiogenesis ability was not restored (Figs.  and ). Knockdown experiments of GPX4 and SLC7A11 in HUVECs further verified that SP inhibited ferroptosis through SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway in the hyperoxia cell model.
 

## Discussion
We systematically investigated the mechanism by which SP attenuates BPD by reducing ferroptosis. In this study, serum iron, LPO, SLC7A11, GPX4 and GSH levels were measured in non-BPD and BPD cord blood and peripheral blood samples. The results showed that serum antioxidant levels were decreased in BPD infants compared with non-BPD infants, while serum iron and ferroptosis marker LPO levels were increased. There is a positive correlation between iron content and ferroptosis [ ], ferroptosis has been shown to be present in BPD [ , ], the symptoms of BPD can be alleviated by inhibiting ferroptosis [ , ].
In the clinical cohort of this study, we did not observe statistically significant differences in serum markers related to ferroptosis between male and female infants(Supplementary. Figure 5). This might be related to the relatively small sample size of ours. However, a large number of studies have shown that male infants born at very low birth weight are more prone to lung diseases than female infants born at very low birth weight [ ]. The mechanism may be related to the increase of α -smooth muscle actin and the decrease of CD31 expression in male endothelial cells under hyperoxic conditions [ ]. The BPD model of male mice shows more obvious alveolar development arrest and a higher degree of inflammatory infiltration [ ]. Therefore, conducting gender-stratified analyses in larger-scale prospective cohorts in the future is crucial for revealing the complete role of ferroptosis in BPD. Considering the existence of gender-specific pathological mechanisms, exploring gender-dependent treatment strategies may become a more efficient way to treat BPD.
This clinical component of our study has several limitations. First, its design as a single-center, retrospective, observational study inherently carries the potential for biases, such as selection bias and information bias. Although we applied clear inclusion and exclusion criteria and extracted data directly from electronic medical records, unmeasured confounding factors (e.g., details of nutritional support, transfusion history, etc.) may have influenced the observed association between serum markers and BPD. Retrospective correlation analysis cannot establish causality; the precise temporal sequence and mechanistic relationship underlying the changes in serum ferroptosis markers observed in BPD infants require further elucidation through future studies and in-depth mechanistic experiments. Nonetheless, the preliminary clinical clues provided here, corroborated by subsequent rigorously controlled animal and cellular experiments, collectively support the hypothesis that ferroptosis plays a significant role in the pathogenesis of BPD.
Using complementary in vivo (neonatal rat) and in vitro (HUVEC) BPD models, we identified that hyperoxia exposure: (1) suppresses ferroptosis-inhibitory and angiogenic gene expression, and (2) can be effectively counteracted by SP treatment. These findings reveal SP’s protective mechanism via ferroptosis modulation, offering new therapeutic avenues for BPD.
Ferroptosis has been shown to be present in BPD [ , ], SLC7A11/GPX4 axis is an important signaling axis in ferroptosis signaling pathway [ ]. Cellular SLC7A11 is important for cystine import, glutathione production and antioxidant function. Ferroptosis occurred when SLC7A11 was knocked down [ ]. Recent studies have shown a potent role for SLC7A11 in regulating immune responses and oxidative stress in diabetic wounds [ ], The key factors in diabetes-induced chronic wounds lie in the dysfunction of inflammation and antioxidant defenses. GPX4 is classified into three isoforms, which are found in the cytoplasm, mitochondria, and nucleus. The loss of GPX4 not only causes ferroptosis, but also induces apoptosis and pyroptosis [ ]. GPX4 has an antioxidant effect and can reduce the impact of oxidative reactions in cells [ ]. GPX4 protects against lipid peroxidation, maintains cell membrane integrity [ ], and inhibits ferroptosis. GPX4 also has an important function in regulating mammalian embryonic development, protecting against radiation and oxidative damage, and GPX4 knockout can cause intrauterine death of mouse embryos [ ].
Oxidative stress and inflammatory response play important roles in the pathogenesis of BPD. Ferroptosis plays a crucial role in regulating oxidative stress and inflammatory response [ ]. Inhibition of ferroptosis significantly alleviates oxidative stress and alleviates disease states such as emphysema [ ], lung injury due to hemolysis [ ] And bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Blocking ferroptosis reduces the inflammatory response [ , ]. Inhibition of ferroptosis can alleviate sepsis-related lung injury [ ], reducing neuroinflammation in Alzheimer’s disease model through NRF2/GPX4 pathway [ ], And psoriatic inflammation [ ].
Hyperoxia decreased the expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4 in HUVEC cells. Our study found that SP intervention reversed the ferroptosis of HUVEC induced by hyperoxia and upregulated the expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4. SP can enhance the antioxidant capacity of cells [ , ], this is consistent with our results. Moreover, by knocking down the upstream and downstream genes of the SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway followed by SP intervention, the addition of SP after knocking down did not enhance the antioxidant capacity of the cells. These results suggest that SP may play a role in protecting cells from hyperoxia through the SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway. SLC7A11/GPX4 axis activation plays an important role in the suppression of BPD symptoms. SLC7A11 and GPX4 are two key targets for regulating ferroptosis, and inhibition of these factors triggers ferroptosis [ ]. In addition, our previous study showed that SP inhibited LPS-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice through NRF2/HO-1 pathway [ , ], NRF2 plays an important role in inhibiting oxidative stress and ferroptosis [ , ]. As for the concentration of SP in in vitro experiments, in the cellular model, SP exhibited a biphasic dose response. The protective effect peaked at 0.6 mM and diminished at higher concentrations (1.2 and 2.4 mM). This phenomenon is consistent with studies on other short-chain fatty acids, which indicate that higher concentrations can significantly influence cellular metabolism, intracellular pH, and epigenetic regulation—effects that may override or interfere with beneficial signaling pathways [ – ]. The specific mechanism underlying this biphasic effect in the endothelial model warrants further investigation.
PTGS2, also known as cyclooxygenase 2 (COX2), is the product of the metabolic pathway of arachidonic acid. Cyclooxygenase is the precursor of prostaglandin. Under normal circumstances, the content of prostaglandin is very low, but when the body has an inflammatory response, the content of prostaglandin increases, and the content of COX2, also known as PTGS2, will also increase. When PTGS2 knockout mice were used for LPS induced lung injury, compared with control wild type mice, PTGS2 knockout mice showed milder lung injury symptoms [ ]. The increase of PTGS2 not only indicates inflammation, but also is a marker gene of ferroptosis. By analyzing public databases and using quercetin interventions in a mouse model of BPD in lung epithelial cells, researchers found that quercetin, through the MAPK/PTGS2 pathway, inhibited ferroptosis and alleviated alveolar simplification of BPD [ ].
VEGFA is a star molecule in vascular development research. Various pathogenesis of BPD causes vascular growth disorders in the lung [ ]. Some studies have found that the decreased expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4 causes the down-regulation of VEGFA expression and inhibits HUVEC angiogenesis [ , ]. In addition, the increased expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4 also had the same up-regulation effect on CD31 expression [ , ]. This is consistent with the results of the present study.
In addition to directly regulating ferroptosis and angiogenic factors, the lung-protective effect of SP may also involve its regulation of hyperoxia-induced inflammatory responses. In this study, we observed a close association between inflammation and ferroptosis under hyperoxic exposure: the expression level of PTGS2, a classic inflammatory mediator, significantly increased simultaneously with ferroptosis markers such as elevated LPO and decreased GPX4, and SP intervention could reverse these changes at the same time. In another previous study of our research group, SP was used to intervene in BPD mice. ELISA experiments showed that SP reduced the levels of IL-17 A, IL-6, and TNF-α in serum, alleviated alveolar septal thickening and alveolar fusion, and promoted weight recovery [ ].
Although the inhibition of angiogenesis is very popular in the field of cancer research, the promotion of angiogenesis is still a key issue in the study of non-malignant and chronic diseases [ ]. As an important pathological mechanism of BPD, it is important to promote the development of blood vessels, and the increase of blood vessels can improve lung hypoplasia. But blood vessels do more than supply oxygen and nutrients, and blindly promoting blood vessel development is not a good option when blood vessel function is wrong [ ]. However, the current research on BPD does not seem to explore this Angle, which will become a direction of our future research.
Moreover, the biochemical relationship between SP’s structure as a short-chain fatty acid and its anti-ferroptosis activity remains unclear. Meanwhile, there are several other defense pathways of ferroptosis, and there may even be some defense pathways that have not been discovered yet, but we only examined one of them, and the complete molecular mechanism underlying SP-mediated ferroptosis protection requires further characterization.
Although this study provides experimental evidence that SP inhibits ferroptosis by regulating the SLC7A11/GPX4 axis and thereby alleviates BPD, we must evaluate this finding within a broader context. The translational potential of ferroptosis as a therapeutic target is inherently complex. Firstly, its pathological role is highly complex. For instance, in gastrointestinal diseases, ferroptosis can play completely opposite roles at different pathological stages [ ]. This suggests that in different stages of BPD or with different severity, a simple targeted therapy against ferroptosis may lack effectiveness and requires case-specific studies. Additionally, ferroptosis inhibitors (such as Ferrostatin-1, Liproxstatin-1) face poor pharmacokinetic properties and limited in vivo stability in clinical translation [ ]. Moreover, in other disease models (such as stroke models), the protective effects of ferroptosis inhibitors have not been consistently replicated [ ]. These contradictory evidence remind us that a single targeted strategy may not be sufficient to address this multifactorial disease. Therefore, the protective effect of SP in this study should be understood as a metabolite with multi-target potential, and some of its beneficial effects are achieved by regulating the important node of ferroptosis. Future research needs to utilize conditional gene knockout animal models to specifically regulate ferroptosis at different stages of BPD development, in order to more precisely evaluate its therapeutic value and strive to develop more targeted and stable regulators.

## Conclusion
The results of the present study suggest that intraperitoneal injection of SP in neonatal rats exerts a protective effect in BPD by inhibiting oxidative stress-induced ferroptosis. This effect is achieved by activating the SLC7A11/GPX4 axis(Fig.  ). SP can not only improve ferroptosis in BPD, but also improve vascular development in BPD.
