## ABSTRACT

## Introduction
The diagnosis of systemic transthyretin amyloidosis based on prostate biopsy is very rare, and we report this case with a literature review.

## Case Presentation
A 73‐year‐old male patient was referred to our department due to an increased serum prostate‐specific antigen level at 10.4 ng/mL while receiving benign prostatic hypertrophy treatment. Prostate needle biopsy was performed, and the pathology revealed transthyretin amyloid deposits in the prostate tissue without adenocarcinoma. Subsequent 99mTc pyrophosphate scintigraphy confirmed the accumulation in the myocardium. The patient was diagnosed with cardiac transthyretin amyloidosis.

## Conclusion
Amyloidosis is a systemic disease where amyloid is deposited in multiple organs, causing various dysfunctions. Considering both our case and previous reports, prostatic amyloidosis may frequently be associated with systemic involvement. The treatments including oligonucleotide therapeutic agents and transthyretin‐stabilizing drugs will be more effective with early diagnosis and early treatment, and various symptoms suspicious of systemic amyloidosis should not be missed.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining showed perivascular deposition of pale eosinophilic, homogeneous, and amorphous material, raising suspicion of amyloid deposition (Figure  ). Accordingly, further staining was performed. Direct‐fast‐scarlet (DFS) staining showed DFS‐positive deposits around the blood vessels (Figure  ). Immunohistochemical staining revealed that the deposits were positive for transthyretin (ATTR) (Figure  ), while they were negative for AL amyloid (κ and λ light chains), AA amyloid, and β2‐microglobulin. Based on the above findings, the patient was diagnosed with prostatic ATTR amyloidosis.
 Additional tests were conducted considering amyloidosis as a systemic disease. Cardiac ultrasonography revealed cardiac hypertrophy, and the ejection fraction was 65.2%. The patient was referred to cardiologists as cardiac amyloidosis was also suspected. A Technetium‐99 m pyrophosphatase (Tc99m‐PYP) study was conducted to investigate further systemic lesions. The study revealed accumulation in the myocardium with a quantitative visual score of 3. The Tc99m‐PYP scan demonstrated a myocardial PYP uptake greater than rib uptake (Figure  ). The patient was diagnosed with cardiac ATTR and referred to the clinical genetic department for further screening and treatment.
 A blood test for the ATTR gene was conducted after genetic counseling. The result revealed no germline mutation in the ATTR gene, and the patient was diagnosed with wild‐type ATTR (ATTRwt). Tafamidis treatment was planned in addition to carvedilol and furosemide.

## Discussion
Amyloidosis is a complex disorder caused by misfolded protein accumulation in various organs throughout the body [ ]. It is classified based on unique precursor proteins, such as immunoglobulin light (AL‐λ/AL‐κ) or heavy chains (AH), transthyretin (ATTR), leukocyte chemotactic factor 2 (ALECT2), serum amyloid A (AA), and semenogelin (ASem1), among others, thereby identifying the type and associated with specific organ tropism and disease course [ ]. Amyloidosis affects any body organ, whereas the heart is the most affected organ (70%), followed by the kidney, liver, gastrointestinal system, and soft tissue, which are involved in 60%, 20%, 15%, and 10% of cases, respectively [ , ].
Prostatic amyloidosis is a rare disease, with approximately 210 cases being reported to date (Table  ) [ , , , , , , , , , , , , ]. Gilani et al. investigated pathological findings in 150 patients with amyloidosis in the urinary tract and prostate [ ]. The most prevalent amyloid type in the prostate is ASem1, followed by ATTR and AL. PSA was measured and reported in only 13 cases, as far as our literature review. Of these, PSA level exceeded the reference range of 4.0 ng/mL in all cases, ranging from 4.95 to 170 ng/mL. The number of cases is limited; however, these cases indicate that PSA is frequently increased in prostatic amyloidosis and is discovered based on biopsy prompted by this increase. MRI was conducted in only three cases, with no specific imaging results in common. Further, prostatic amyloidosis sometimes causes hematuria and dysuria, resulting in urinary retention, and transurethral resection of the prostate was conducted as treatment in some cases [ , ]. As far as our literature review, no symptoms or imaging findings are specific to prostatic amyloidosis, and prostate biopsy is the only diagnostic tool. The question of which patients should be suspected of prostatic amyloidosis and the criteria for prostate biopsy warrant further discussion.
 ASem1 amyloid deposition is associated with localized amyloidosis, whereas ATTR is clinically important as cases were diagnosed with prostatic ATTR preceding systemic amyloidosis diagnosis [ ]. Nevo et al. have reported seven cases of prostatic amyloidosis. Among them, six underwent amyloid typing tests. The diagnosis of cardiac amyloidosis followed prostatic amyloidosis in four patients [ ]. Cardiac amyloidosis is the most prognostic factor of systemic amyloidosis as it causes cardiomyopathy and heart failure, arrhythmias, orthostatic hypotension, or sudden death due to severe conduction disorders [ ]. Tafamidis is an oral medication that binds to transthyretin, which prevents tetramer dissociation and amyloid genesis. Tafamidis treatment is currently approved for patients with ATTRwt and hereditary (ATTRv) cardiomyopathy. The efficacy of Tafamidis is greater in patients with earlier‐stage disease (New York Heart Association class I–II) [ ]; thus, early diagnosis is crucial for a better prognosis. Further, Patisiran, which is a new oligonucleotide therapeutic agent, is currently available for ATTRv [ ]. ATTRv is diagnosed based on genetic testing showing a germline mutation in the TTR gene; thus, early indication of the test with prompt genetic counseling may increase treatment options [ ]. These therapies are expected to be more effective with early diagnosis and treatment.
In this case, the patient demonstrated no symptoms suspicious of cardiac disorder, including shortness of breath or chest pain. However, additional ultrasound cardiography revealed cardiac hypertrophy, and Tc99m‐PYP scan resulted in a systemic amyloidosis diagnosis. Considering both our case and previous reports, prostatic amyloidosis may frequently be associated with systemic involvement. Therefore, various symptoms suspicious of systemic amyloidosis should not be missed.

## Conclusion
Prostatic ATTR often precedes systemic amyloidosis. A detailed inspection needs to be performed when confronted with prostate amyloidosis.
