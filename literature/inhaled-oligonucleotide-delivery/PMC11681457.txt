## Background
Sympathetic hyperactivity plays an important role in the initiation and maintenance of pulmonary hypertension. Carotid baroreceptor stimulation (CBS) is an effective autonomic neuromodulation therapy. We aim to investigate the effects of CBS on hypoxia‐induced pulmonary hypertension and its underlying mechanisms.

## Methods and Results
Rats were randomly assigned into 4 groups, including a Control‐sham group (n=7), a Control‐CBS group (n=7), a Hypoxia‐sham group (n=10) and a Hypoxia‐CBS group (n=10). Echocardiography, ECG, and hemodynamics examination were performed. Samples of blood, lung tissue, pulmonary arteries, and right ventricle were collected for the further analysis. In the in vivo study, CBS reduced wall thickness and muscularization degree in pulmonary arterioles, thereby improving pulmonary hemodynamics. Right ventricle hypertrophy, fibrosis and dysfunction were all improved. CBS rebalanced autonomic tone and reduced the density of sympathetic nerves around pulmonary artery trunks and bifurcations. RNA‐seq analysis identified BDNF and periostin (POSTN) as key genes involved in hypoxia‐induced pulmonary hypertension, and CBS downregulated the mRNA expression of BDNF and POSTN in rat pulmonary arteries. In the in vitro study, norepinephrine was found to promote pulmonary artery smooth muscle cell proliferation while upregulating BDNF and POSTN expression. The proliferative effect was alleviated by silence BDNF or POSTN.

## Conclusions
Our results showed that CBS could rebalance autonomic tone, inhibit pulmonary arterial remodeling, and improve pulmonary hemodynamics and right ventricle function, thus delaying hypoxia‐induced pulmonary hypertension progression. There may be a reciprocal interaction between POSTN and BDNF that is responsible for the underlying mechanism.

## 
CBS Attenuated PA Remodeling in HPH Rats
Coimmunofluorescence staining of Ki67 with vWF and α‐SMA was performed to visualize proliferating cells in the PA wall, and representative photomicrographs in lung tissue slices were shown in Figure  . The result showed that Ki67‐positive cells/total cells in PA wall of the Hypoxia‐sham group were higher than the Con‐sham group, whereas it was reduced in the Hypoxia‐CBS group (Figure  ). The WT% of pulmonary arteriole was analyzed by hematoxylin and eosin staining (Figures  and ). The degree of muscularization of pulmonary arteriole was calculated by double‐label immunofluorescence staining for vWF and α‐SMA (Figures  and ). Masson staining of lung tissue showed that hypoxia increased collagen levels in the lungs, whereas CBS ameliorated the increase (Figures  and ). After exposed to hypoxia for 6 weeks, WT% of pulmonary arteriole with 20 to 100 μm or 100 to 200 μm in diameter was increased markedly in rats (Figure  through ). Additionally, there was a significant increase in the proportion of fully muscular arteries and a reduction in the proportion of nonmuscular arteries in rats (Figure  ). However, all these parameters were attenuated by CBS treatment (Figures  through and ). Although there was no statistical difference in the P‐ERK1/2(phosphor‐extracellular signal‐regulated kinase 1/2)/ERK1/2 ratio among the 4 groups, CBS had a tendency to reduce P‐ERK1/2/ERK1/2 ratio induced by chronic hypoxia (Figure  through ). These data indicate that CBS could inhibit the SMC proliferation and reduce the WT% and the degree of muscularization of pulmonary arteriole, as well as collagen deposition in lung tissue of HPH rats partially by inhibiting ERK1/2 pathway.
 

## 
CBS Rescued RV Remodeling in HPH Rats
Hematoxylin and eosin staining of RV (Figure  ) and the data of cross‐sectional area (Figure  ) demonstrated that rats in the Hypoxia‐sham group had larger cross‐sectional area when compared with the Con‐sham group, which was markedly blunted in the Hypoxia‐CBS group. As shown in Figure  through , chronic hypoxia significantly increased RV/left ventricle+septum, RV/body weight ratio, and RVWT in HPH rats, which were all ameliorated after CBS treatment. Moreover, CBS could obviously inhibit the mRNA expression of myocardial hypertrophy‐related gene ANP (atrial natriuretic peptide) in HPH rats (Figure  ). As shown in the representative photomicrographs (Figure  ), the CVF of RV was assessed by Masson trichrome staining. CVF was increased markedly in the Hypoxia‐sham group compared with the Con‐sham group, but this increase was abolished by CBS treatment (Figure  ). Similar to the result of CVF, the protein expression levels of MMP2 (matrix metallopeptidase 2) and MMP9 were also increased in the Hypoxia‐sham group as compared with the Con‐sham group. However, the rats in the Hypoxia‐CBS group showed a reduction both in MMP2 and MMP9 expression compared with those in the Hypoxia‐sham group (Figures  and ). These data suggest that CBS could attenuate hypertrophy and fibrosis of RV in HPH rats.
 

## 
CBS Inhibited SNS Overactivation and Reduced Sympathetic Nerve Density Around PA in HPH Rats
TH‐positive nerve fibers in the connective and adipose tissue around PA trunks and bifurcations were identified using immunohistochemical staining, and typical photomicrographs were shown in Figure  . The TH‐positive innervation area was increased significantly in the Hypoxia‐sham group compared with the Con‐sham group, which was partially reversed after CBS treatment (Figure  ). The analysis of short‐duration HRV showed that both LF (Figure  ) and LF/HF ratio (Figure  ) were significantly increased, whereas HF (Figure  ) was markedly decreased in rats of the Hypoxia‐sham group. However, the changes of HF, LF, and LF/HF ratio were all partially reversed by CBS treatment. These data demonstrate that CBS could inhibit SNS overactivity and reduce the density of sympathetic nerve around PAs.
 

## 
DEGs Involved in HPH Were Identified by RNA‐Seq Analysis in Rats
To reveal the target molecule involved in the effects of CBS on an improvement in PA remodeling in HPH rats, intralobar PAs were used for mRNA high throughput sequencing. As shown in Figure  , after being exposed to hypoxic conditions for 6 weeks, 207 genes were upregulated and 216 genes were downregulated as compared with the Con‐sham group (Table  ). However, CBS partially reversed the expression of genes altered by hypoxia (Table  ). Then, Gene Ontology analysis of the DEGs reversed by CBS in HPH rats showed that their biological processes mainly focused on response to hypoxia, response to xenobiotic stimulus, and regulation of postsynaptic membrane potential (Figure  ). As shown in Figure  , the protein–protein interaction network was constructed to show the top hub genes of DEGs. The heat map also showed the 21 genes downregulated by CBS treatment (Figure  and Table  ). Among the 21 downregulated genes, BDNF and POSTN are closely involved in hypoxia‐induced PASMC proliferation. In addition, Vahitha et al. reported a significant increase in POSTN expression by proteomic analysis of lung tissue from patients with PH, which was consistent with our results of RNA‐Seq analysis in rat PAs. The relative mRNA expression levels of POSTN and BDNF were significantly elevated in the Hypoxia‐sham group when compared with the Con‐sham group, whereas they were reduced after CBS treatment (Figures  and ). Therefore, BDNF and POSTN would be further verified in our subsequent experiments.
 

## 
Norepinephrine Promoted PASMC Proliferation and HPH‐Associated Proteins Expression Via ERK1/2 Pathway
In vitro, primary PASMCs were isolated and cultured from Sprague–Dawley rats. The representative image of PASMC identification was shown in Figure  . First, PASMCs were treated with different concentrations of norepinephrine (0.01, 0.1, 1 μmol/L) for 48 hours. CCK8 assay results showed that norepinephrine induced a significant increase in cell viability at 0.1 μmol/L in PASMCs (Figure  ). Second, α‐adrenergic blocker prazosin or ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126 was used to treat PASMCs in combination with 0.1 μmol/L norepinephrine. As shown in Figures  and , both prazosin and U0126 inhibited cell viability stimulated by norepinephrine with a maximal effect at concentrations of 10 μmol/L and 160 μmol/L, respectively. Thus, the optimum concentration of norepinephrine at 0.1 μmol/L, prazosin at 10 μmol/L, and U0126 at 160 μmol/L were identified for the subsequent experiments.
 Next, to investigate whether ERK1/2 pathway was involved in norepinephrine‐induced PASMC proliferation, prazosin or U0126 was used to block norepinephrine and ERK1/2 pathway, respectively. As shown in (Figure  through ), it was demonstrated that norepinephrine increased PASMC viability (Figure  ) and the number of PASMC (Figure  ) as well as the protein expression levels of PCNA ( ), and P‐ERK1/2/ERK1/2 ratio (Figure  ), which could be inhibited by prazosin or U0126. In addition, PASMCs exhibited higher cell viability, number of cells, PCNA protein expression and P‐ERK1/2/ERK1/2 ratio under hypoxic conditions as compared with normoxic conditions (Figure  ). To investigate HPH‐associated proteins, the protein expression levels of POSTN, BDNF, phosopho‐tyrosine kinase receptor B (P‐TrkB), TrkB, and bone morphogenetic protein receptor, type II (BMPR2) were assessed under normoxic and hypoxic conditions (Figures  , through ). Hypoxia significantly elevated the protein expression levels of POSTN, BDNF, P‐TrkB, and TrkB, while reducing BMPR2 protein expression. Moreover, norepinephrine exaggerated these effects on the aforementioned protein expression. All these changes induced by norepinephrine were reversed by prazosin or U0126. These data suggest that ERK1/2 pathway participates in norepinephrine‐induced cell proliferation and HPH‐associated protein expression in PASMCs.

## Effect of

 Signaling in PASMC Proliferation
To investigate whether POSTN was involved in norepinephrine‐induced PASMC proliferation, adenovirus mediated POSTN silencing (Ad‐shPOSTN) and ‐shNC (adenovirus mediated negative control) adenovirus vector were constructed. Then, cells were transfected with either Ad‐shPOSTN or Ad‐shNC for 96 hours under normoxic conditions. It was shown that the protein expression of POSTN was decreased in the Ad‐shPOSTN group, with little change in the Ad‐shNC group compared with the control group (Figure  ). Next, PASMCs cultured in normoxic or hypoxic conditions were treated with 0.1 μmol/L norepinephrine or 10 ng/mL POSTN protein in combination with 0.1 μmol/L norepinephrine for 48 hours. Some cells were transfected with Ad‐shNC or Ad‐shPOSTN for 48 hours before being stimulated by 0.1 μmol/L norepinephrine for another 48 hours. As shown in (Figure  through ), norepinephrine increased PASMC viability, the number of PASMCs, and the protein expression of PCNA, and POSTN further enhanced these effects. However, both cell viability and numbers, as well as PCNA protein expression in PASMCs, were decreased in the norepinephrine + Ad‐shPOSTN group compared with the norepinephrine+Ad‐shNC group. These results indicate that POSTN is implicated in norepinephrine‐induced proliferation of PASMC.
 To further investigate the effects of POSTN on the expression of BDNF/TrkB signaling and BMPR2 induced by norepinephrine, the protein expression levels of BDNF, P‐TrkB, TrkB, and BMPR2 were detected. As shown in (Figures  , , through ), POSTN enhanced the effect of norepinephrine on the protein expression levels of BMPR2, BDNF, P‐TrkB, and TrkB. On the contrary, the norepinephrine+Ad‐shPOSTN group reversed the changes in protein expression of BMPR2, P‐TrkB, TrkB, and BDNF compared with the norepinephrine + Ad‐ShNC group. These data suggest that POSTN regulates the protein expression levels of BDNF, P‐TrkB, TrkB, and BMPR2. To further explore the relationship between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling, we treated PASMCs with siRNA targeting BDNF and found that BDNF and POSTN expression were significantly decreased (Figure  through ). Next, cell culture experiments with time course of BDNF treatment showed that BDNF upregulated POSTN expression (Figures  and ) and promoted PASMC proliferation (Figures  and ). Therefore, we hypothesized that there exists a positive feedback loop between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling in hypoxia‐induced PASMC proliferation.

## DISCUSSION

## Major Findings
In the present study, we have several main findings. First, CBS significantly inhibited remodeling of PA and RV, thereby improving pulmonary hemodynamics and RV dysfunction, thus delaying the progression of HPH. Second, CBS suppressed sympathetic tone, reduced both BDNF and POSTN mRNA expression in PAs of HPH rats. In addition, ERK1/2 activation was also shown to be suppressed after CBS treatment. Third, norepinephrine upregulated BDNF and POSTN protein expression and promoted cell proliferation in PASMCs, whereas this effect was abolished by prazosin or U0126. Last, knockdown of POSTN in vitro inhibited norepinephrine‐induced PASMC proliferation and BDNF protein expression under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. Conversely, knockdown of BDNF also resulted in inhibition of POSTN protein expression.

## 
CBS Rebalanced Autonomic Tone in HPH Rats
SNS overactivation is considered as an independent predictor of PH prognosis. A variety of studies have reported that the SNS is overactivated in multiple experimental PH models and patients with PH. , , , Sympathetic overactivity in PH is characterized by increased muscle sympathetic nerve activity and impaired HRV. The TF and HF components of HRV were reduced, whereas the LF/HF ratio was increased, suggesting a decrease in vagal tone and an increase in sympathetic tone in monocrotaline‐induced PH rats. In present study, chronic hypoxia exposure significantly increased sympathetic tone as assessed by increased TH positive sympathetic nerve staining area and LF component, as well as the LF/HF ratio. In addition, vagal tone was suppressed characterized by decreased HF component. CBS could partially reverse these changes and is a promising tool to rebalance autonomic tone.

## 
CBS Inhibited PA Remodeling in HPH Rats
HPH is a typical model that widely used in PH studies. , Hypoxia serves as a significant stimulus for PH development, with the carotid body being capable of sensing a decrease in PaO2 and subsequently leading to sympathetic hyperactivation. There are abundant sympathetic nerves in the connective and adipose tissue around PA trunks and bifurcations in human and rats. These nerve fibers are mainly composed of TH‐positive nerves. When the SNS was stimulated, the sympathetic nerves released a mixture of neurohormones, which resulted in upregulation of multiple neurohormone plasma levels such as norepinephrine, contributing to PH progression by interacting with receptors in the local tissues. Corresponding receptors, especially α‐adrenoreceptors, have been found to be expressed in pulmonary vessels. The study by Xiao et al. showed that hypoxia increased the expression of alpha1 adrenergic receptors in PAs and PASMCs of rats, and norepinephrine activated alpha1 adrenergic receptors, which could promote PASMC proliferation. , , Excessive proliferation of smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and deposition of extracellular matrix contribute to augmented WT% and degree of muscularization, which are indicative of hypoxia‐induced pulmonary vascular remodeling. Zhao et al. performed cervical sympathetic trunk transection in monocrotaline‐induced PH rats and found that local lung norepinephrine concentration and PA TH expression were decreased, PASMC proliferation and WT% were reduced, and pulmonary hemodynamics and right heart function were improved. These results were similar to those in our study, where CBS reduced TH‐positive sympathetic nerve density around PA trunks and bifurcations, inhibited SMC proliferation, and decreased WT% and muscularization degree of pulmonary arteriole in HPH rats. Consistently, our in vitro study suggested that prazosin inhibited norepinephrine‐induced PASMC proliferation under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. With the inhibition of PA remodeling by CBS, pulmonary hemodynamics parameters such as mPAP and RVSP were improved.

## CBS Inhibited RV Remodeling and Improved RV Function in HPH Rats
RV fibrosis and hypertrophy are the 2 main pathophysiological characteristics of cardiac remodeling. The increase of RV afterload in PH is initially countered with an adaptive hypertrophic response. However, the adaptive response will gradually shift into maladaptive remodeling of the RV, eventually causing RV failure. In the present study, chronic hypoxia increased RV cardiomyocyte cross‐sectional area, RVWT, weight ratio of RV/body weight and RV/(left ventricle+septum), and elevated ANP mRNA expression in the RV. In addition, CVF and the protein expression of MMP2 and MMP9 were also enhanced. These markers of RV hypertrophy and fibrosis were attenuated after CBS treatment in HPH rats.
RV function is one of the most important determinants for the prognosis of patients with PH. Pressure‐volume loop analysis is commonly used to assess load‐independent parameters of RV function. Our data showed that chronic hypoxia increased E
es, E
ed, and arterial elastance, indicating RV dysfunction. However, no significant difference was found in TAPSE between the Con‐sham group and the Hypoxia‐sham group, indicating that chronic hypoxia was a moderate stimulator for PH model establishment. The hypertrophy and fibrosis of the RV, as well as the deterioration of RV function induced by chronic hypoxia, could all be attenuated after treatment with CBS. CBS inhibited RV remodeling and improved RV function due to the following reasons. On the one hand, the afterload of RV was decreased with the reduction of mPAP and PVR, and thus inhibiting the cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. On the other hand, sympathetic activation was considered as a compensatory response to improve cardiac function during the early stage, but chronic sympathetic overactivation exacerbated the deterioration of heart failure. , CBS rebalanced the autonomic tone to interrupt this vicious cycle. These findings were consistent with our previous data in chronic heart failure canines, which showed that CBS improved cardiac performance and ameliorated cardiac remodeling by reducing the sympathetic activity. 

## Potential Mechanisms of CBS on Improving Pulmonary Arterial Remodeling
Chronic sympathetic overactivation is ubiquitously present in patients with PH. , , To investigate the effects of CBS on PA remodeling in HPH rats and its potential mechanisms, mRNA high throughput sequencing of intralobar PA was used to identify DEGs. As a result, BDNF and POSTN were identified as key genes associated with HPH. BDNF/TrkB expression was significantly elevated in hypoxic mouse lungs as well as in PAs of patients suffering from idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension. Furthermore, BDNF/TrkB axis has been shown to participate in promotion of epithelial–mesenchymal transition in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and PASMC proliferation in PH. POSTN is a 90 kDa extracellular matrix protein implicated in cell proliferation, tumorigenesis, and inflammatory response. It has been documented that POSTN is involved not only in cardiac hypertrophy, atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction, and heart failure but also serves as an indicator of the disease progression in patients with cardiovascular diseases. In addition, our data regarding PASMCs exposed to hypoxia align with previous findings that demonstrated high expression of POSTN in keloid fibroblasts and nonsmall cell lung cancer A549 cells under hypoxic conditions. Recent studies have shown that POSTN expression is markedly increased both in various experimental PH models and patients with PH. Moreover, POSTN is considered as a biomarker of PH progression. , 

The ERK1/2 pathway plays a central role in a wide variety of biological process, including cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, senescence, and apoptosis. Moreover, multiple lines of evidence have suggested that ERK1/2 activation is found in patients and animal models with PH. , In the in vivo study, CBS reduced both the mRNA expression levels of BDNF and POSTN and the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 induced by chronic hypoxia exposure. In vitro study showed that norepinephrine significantly promoted the protein expression of BDNF and POSTN but inhibited BMPR2 protein expression. BMPR2 mutation, accounting for around 70% to 80% of heritable cases, is reported to be involved in the occurrence and development of PH. In the study by Wang et al., it was found that knockout of BMPR2 in PASMCs had high proliferative and antiapoptotic properties. In addition, BMPR2 mutant rats were more susceptible to HPH and exhibited intrinsic RV dysfunction independent of PH. Moreover, the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 as well as cell proliferation were increased in PASMCs treated with norepinephrine. These changes were abolished by prazosin or U0126 under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. Based on the results of present study, it could be speculated that ERK1/2 pathway is an upstream HPH‐associated protein and plays an important role in PA remodeling. Interestingly, among the HPH‐associated proteins, there exists a positive feedback loop between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling. POSTN upregulated BDNF protein expression in norepinephrine‐induced PASMCs under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. A previous study has shown that BDNF expression and secretion were increased in PASMCs under hypoxic conditions that resulted in PASMC proliferation through autocrine effects. However, we treated PASMCs with siRNA targeting BDNF and found that POSTN expression was decreased. Moreover, PASMCs stimulated by BDNF showed that BDNF upregulated POSTN expression and promoted cell proliferation. Therefore, we hypothesize that there may be a positive feedback loop between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling during hypoxia‐induced PASMC proliferation with or without norepinephrine stimulation. The specific regulatory mechanism governing the interaction between POSTN and BDNF remains to be clarified. Norepinephrine serves as an initiating factor, accelerating their interaction, which explains part of why sympathetic activation leads to continuous progression of PH. Our study revealed that CBS inhibited sympathetic activation and subsequently reduced BDNF and POSTN expression via inhibition of ERK1/2 pathway. CBS interrupted the positive feedback loop between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling thus inhibiting PA remodeling and ameliorating HPH.
There are 3 limitations in this study. First, it is unclear whether CBS would interfere with other sympathetic neurotransmitters or adrenergic receptors, and it cannot be determined whether CBS would interfere with cholinergic receptors in HPH rats. Second, the mechanisms of CBS are complicated, and none of the experimental PH models could entirely simulate the complex pathophysiological characteristics of PH patients. Third, given the many comparisons with a significance level of 0.05 and the susceptibility of RNA‐seq analyses to type 1 error, there might be a possibility of spurious results in this study. Future studies using multiple types of animal models including transgenic animals would enable us to study the effects of CBS on the expresssion of POSTN and BDNF though ERK1/2 pathway in pulmonary vascular remodeling.

## CONCLUSIONS
To summarize, we demonstrated for the first time that CBS inhibits remodeling of the PA and RV, amends hemodynamics, and thus delays the progression of HPH. The underlying mechanism may be attributed to CBS inhibiting sympathetic tone and interfering with the positive feedback loop between POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling. Our work adds new insights and perspectives to CBS on the role of POSTN and BDNF/TrkB signaling in HPH and provides an experimental basis for the treatment of HPH.
