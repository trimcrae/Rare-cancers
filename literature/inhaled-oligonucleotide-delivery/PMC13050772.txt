This study evaluated the anti-inflammatory activity of ZLWH-38, a novel harmine derivative, in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophages and a murine ALI model. ZLWH-38 showed low cytotoxicity and effectively inhibited the production of nitric oxide and pro-inflammatory cytokines. In macrophages, it dose-dependently reduced the expression of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β and iNOS, and intracellular ROS levels. Mechanistic investigations indicated that ZLWH-38 interacted with glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK3β) and suppressed downstream NF-κB signaling. In female C57BL/6 mice subjected to LPS-induced lung injury, prophylactic administration of ZLWH-38 markedly attenuated histopathological damage, lowered the lung wet/dry weight ratio, and decreased neutrophil infiltration and cytokine levels in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid and lung tissue. These findings suggest that ZLWH-38 ameliorates ALI by blocking GSK3β and thereby repressing downstream NF-κB signaling to curtail inflammation, offering a novel rationale and mechanistic foundation for ALI therapeutics.

## Introduction
ALI commonly arises from pulmonary infection, sepsis, chemical inhalation, or trauma, and it triggers an immediate innate immune response characterized by massive cytokine release and recruitment of inflammatory cells, including neutrophils, to eliminate pathogens [ ]. Excessive cytokine production precipitates a systemic “cytokine storm,” in which hyperactive immunity and pathological hyperinflammation inflict self-tissue damage that may progress to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), with a mortality rate reaching 40% [ , ]. Therefore, effective therapeutic agents against ALI/ARDS are urgently required.
Pulmonary macrophages constitute the first defensive line against airborne invasion by engulfing pathogens, clearing necrotic debris and preserving lung homeostasis, and thus they critically shape ALI progression [ ]. Upon activation of pattern-recognition receptors, these macrophages engage downstream NF-κB signaling to release TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, MCP-1 and MIP-2, and to recruit neutrophils and monocytes from the periphery into the lung parenchyma [ ]. Overactivated macrophages further drive oxidative stress by upregulating NADPH oxidase and inducible nitric oxide synthase, thereby generating reactive oxygen and nitrogen species whose mitochondrial damage culminates in pulmonary tissue injury [ , ].
GSK3β, a serine/threonine kinase orchestrating cell differentiation, glycogen metabolism, protein synthesis, immune responses and cell death [ , ], governs ALI progression by modulating inflammatory and oxidative pathways in macrophages [ ]. In recent years, the critical regulatory role of GSK3β in inflammatory diseases such as ALI has attracted considerable attention. However, NF-κB inhibitors have limited clinical application due to their inability to fully block inflammatory pathways and their propensity to induce immune imbalance [ , ]. Therefore, GSK3β inhibitors have emerged as promising therapeutic alternatives. Li et al. demonstrated that the bursal pentapeptide BP5 regulates Nrf2 via the Akt/GSK3β/Fyn axis, while Lv et al. reported that xanthohumol modulates antioxidant defense and inhibits inflammatory pathways through the AMPK/GSK3β axis [ , ]. Both compounds exert protective effects against LPS-induced ALI by targeting GSK3β-related mechanisms. Wedelolactone suppresses soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) activity, thereby regulating the GSK3β-mediated NF-κB and Nrf2 pathways to attenuate inflammation and oxidative stress in LPS-challenged RAW264.7 cells and ALI mice [ ]. Compared to natural products such as wedelolactone and xanthohumol, the synthetic compound ZLWH-38 possesses a well-defined target site and circumvents common limitations of natural products, including batch-to-batch variability and difficulties in structural modification [ – ].
Harmine, isolated from the seeds of the medicinal plant Peganum harmala L., exhibits potent anti-inflammatory, antitumor, and neuroprotective activities [ ]. Harmine exhibits poor selectivity, targeting GSK3β in addition to DYRK1A, AChE, BChE, CDKs, and monoamine oxidase, which engenders unpredictable off-target toxicities (e.g., tremors and neurotoxicity) that limit its clinical application [ – ]. To circumvent this limitation, we rationally designed and synthesized a series of derivatives via Glide molecular docking, specifically targeting GSK3β as the key mediator of anti-inflammatory activity. Guided by this scaffold, we designed and synthesized a series of GSK3β-targeted harmine inhibitors, and the fourteen most potent derivatives were screened for cytotoxicity in RAW264.7 cells and for inhibition of LPS-induced NO and TNF-α release to identify candidates that combine low cytotoxicity with high anti-inflammatory potency. The lead compound, ZLWH-38, was advanced to in vivo evaluation in LPS-induced ALI mice, where it demonstrated marked anti-inflammatory efficacy. CETSA confirmed direct engagement of GSK3β by ZLWH-38, and subsequent mechanistic studies elucidated its anti-inflammatory mode of action.

## Method

## Reagents
ZLWH-38 was designed and synthesized by our research team [ ]. The NO detection kit was purchased from Beyotime (Shanghai, China). RNAiso Plus, PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix, and TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ were sourced from Takara (Beijing, China). The antibody against GSK3β, p-GSK3β, NF-κB p65, p-NF-κB p65, IκBα, p-IκBα and GAPDH were obtained from Affinity (Jiangsu, China). The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) detection kit was obtained from DAKEWE (Shenzhen, China), and the ROS detection kit was sourced from Elabscience (Wuhan, China). Flow cytometry antibodies PE-Cy7 anti-mouse CD45, Percp-Cy5.5 anti-mouse CD11b, APC-Cy7 anti-mouse CD11c, and FITC anti-mouse Ly6G were obtained from BioLegend (San Diego CA, USA).

## Cell Culture
RAW264.7 macrophages were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. For experiments, cells were pretreated with ZLWH-38 for 1 h, then stimulated with LPS at concentrations tailored to specific assays: 1 ng/mL for TNF-α, 10 ng/mL for IL-6 and NO, and 1 µg/mL for ROS, Western blotting, and RT-PCR. DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS served as the control. After 24 h incubation, cell culture supernatants were collected to determine NO, TNF-α, and IL-6 levels.

## Animals
Female C57BL/6 mice aged 6–8 weeks (Beijing HFK Bioscience Limited Company) were used. Animals were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with a 12 h light/dark cycle. All experimental procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command (approval number: Ethics Review No. [2024] 052).

## ALI Model
Mice were randomly assigned to seven groups (n = 8 per group): Control, LPS, LPS + dexamethasone (DEX; 5 mg/kg), LPS + harmine (50 mg/kg), and three LPS + ZLWH-38 dose groups (12.5, 25, and 50 mg/kg). Following induction of anesthesia with 1.5% isoflurane, 20 µL of LPS solution (5 mg/kg body weight in sterile PBS; Solarbio, Beijing, China) was administered via intratracheal instillation. One hour prior to LPS stimulation, mice received intraperitoneal injections of the respective agents: normal saline (Control), ZLWH-38 (12.5, 25, or 50 mg/kg), dexamethasone (5 mg/kg) [ – ]; or harmine (50 mg/kg) (50 mg/kg) [ , ], Dexamethasone and harmine served as positive controls. All mice were euthanized 6 h post-LPS administration.

## NO Levels
Cell models were established as described in Sect. . NO concentrations in cell culture supernatants were measured using an NO detection kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## ELISA
TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β concentrations in serum, lung homogenates, BALF, or cell culture supernatants were measured using mouse ELISA kits (DAKEWE, Shenzhen, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Optimal sample dilutions (1:4–1:5) were determined empirically to ensure concentrations remained within the detection range.

## Real-time PCR
Total RNA was extracted from cells and lung tissue using RNAiso Plus (Takara, Beijing, China) and reverse-transcribed to cDNA with PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, Beijing, China). Quantitative PCR was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Beijing, China) and the primer pairs listed in Table  under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 34 s, and a final melt-curve analysis with a 60 °C hold for 1 min and a 95 °C hold for 15 s. Relative mRNA expression levels were normalized to β-actin using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

 

## Harvest lungs and BALF
For BALF collection, mice were euthanized 6 h after LPS instillation. The trachea was cannulated and lavaged three times with 0.7 mL ice-cold sterile PBS. The pooled fluid was centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was used for ELISA analysis.

## Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) Staining
For histological analysis, the right upper lobe was excised, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 72 h, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 5 μm. Sections were stained with haematoxylin and eosin.

## W/D Weight Ratio
Pulmonary oedema was quantified by the wet-to-dry (W/D) weight ratio. Immediately after euthanasia, lungs were excised and weighed (wet weight, W), dried at 60 °C for 48 h, and reweighed (dry weight, D).

## Flow Cytometry
Flow cytometry was performed on BALF using a BD FACSCanto™ system (Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Neutrophils were identified as CD45 + CD11b+CD11c-Ly6G+ cells, and macrophages as CD45 + CD11b+CD11c+Ly6G- cells. The gating strategy is shown in Supplementary Fig. 12.

## ROS Staining
Intracellular ROS generation was quantified using a ROS Fluorometric Assay Kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China). Cell models were established as described in Sect. , after which cells were incubated with 10 µM DCFH-DA at 37 °C for 30 min. Fluorescence was measured by flow cytometry.

## Western Blotting
RAW264.7 cells were lysed in RIPA buffer, and protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay. Samples were resolved on 10% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking and washing three times with TBST buffer, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against GSK3β (1:2000), p-GSK3β (1:2000), NF-κB p65 (1:3000), p-NF-κB p65 (1:2000), IκBα (1:2000), p-IκBα (1:2000), and GAPDH (1:30000). Membranes were then washed three times with TBST buffer and incubated for 1 h at room temperature with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (1:10,000). Specific bands were visualized by enhanced chemiluminescence. Data were expressed as ratios of target protein to GAPDH, normalized to the LPS group (set to 1). All experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates (n = 3). Representative bands were selected based on signal clarity and minimal background; quantitative data represent the mean of three replicates.

## CETSA
RAW264.7 cells were seeded in 100-mm dishes and treated with ZLWH-38 for 6 h (vehicle: DMSO). Cells were harvested, aliquoted into PCR tubes, and heated in 3 °C increments (37–77 °C) for 3 min per step, followed by equilibration at 25 °C for 3 min and three freeze-thaw cycles between liquid nitrogen and a 25 °C PCR block. Lysates were cleared by centrifugation at 13,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C. Supernatants were mixed with loading buffer, heated at 95 °C for 10 min, and analyzed by western blot using an anti-GSK3β antibody. CETSA was performed in three independent replicates. Protein band intensities were quantified using ImageJ, pooled across experiments, and analyzed statistically.

## Statistical Analysis
All experiments were performed at least in triplicate. Data were analysed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 and are presented as mean ± SD. Inter-group statistical significance was first evaluated by one-way ANOVA to determine the overall difference among groups; subsequent pairwise comparisons were performed using Tukey’s multiple comparison test; p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Result

## Anti-Inflammatory Activity Screening of Harmine Derivatives
The cytotoxicity of 14 harmine derivatives previously synthesized by our team was first assessed in RAW264.7 cells via MTT assay. As shown in Table  , harmine and eight derivatives (ZLWH-38, ZLWH-40, ZLWH-52, ZLWH-60, ZLWH-62, ZLWH-64, ZLWH-66, ZLWH-68) exhibited > 70% cell viability at 10 µM, indicating low cytotoxicity. These compounds were therefore selected for further in vitro anti-inflammatory evaluation.

 

Figure  A and B show NO and TNF-α levels in culture supernatants from LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells. Among the eight low-cytotoxicity derivatives, ZLWH-38, ZLWH-40, and ZLWH-64 exhibited the strongest inhibitory effects on NO and TNF-α production.

 

Figures C and D present IC50 values for NO and TNF-α. All active compounds inhibited LPS-induced NO and TNF-α release in a dose-dependent manner. These results demonstrate that harmine derivatives possess significant in vitro anti-inflammatory activity, with ZLWH-38 showing the most potent effect. ZLWH-38 was therefore selected for further in-depth studies of its anti-inflammatory efficacy in vitro and in vivo.

## ZLWH-38 Alleviates LPS-induced Inflammatory Responses in vitro
To evaluate the anti-inflammatory effects of ZLWH-38, we treated LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells with the compound (2.5, 5, and 10 µM). As shown in Fig.  A and B, ZLWH-38 dose-dependently inhibited LPS-induced release of TNF-α and IL-6. It also suppressed mRNA expression of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and iNOS (Fig.  C-F).

 

## ZLWH-38 Inhibits NF-κB Signaling Pathway Activation
NF-κB signaling is a classic pro-inflammatory pathway crucial to inflammatory diseases. LPS stimulation significantly increased phosphorylation of NF-κB proteins p65 and IκBα in macrophages compared to control, while ZLWH-38 pretreatment markedly reduced their phosphorylation (Fig. ). These results indicate that ZLWH-38 exerts anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB pathway activation.

 

## ZLWH-38 Inhibits LPS-Induced ROS Production
Inflammatory responses and oxidative stress often coexist and drive disease progression. We therefore hypothesized that ZLWH-38 might possess antioxidant properties. LPS triggers oxidative stress via ROS formation under NF-κB control. As shown in Fig. , LPS exposure significantly increased ROS levels, reflected by elevated fluorescence intensity and an increased number of ROS-positive cells. Treatment with 10 µM ZLWH-38 markedly inhibited these LPS-induced elevations, restoring both parameters to baseline levels.

 

## ZLWH-38 Exerts Anti-Inflammatory Effects by Targeting GSK3β
ZLWH-38 (docking score: -5.964) bound via multiple interactions: the nitrogen atom on its pyridine ring and the adjacent amide bond each formed hydrogen bonds with Val135; the cyclohexane side chain exhibited a p-π interaction with Ile62; and the five-membered ring of the harmine core formed a unique π-sulfur bond with Cys199 (Fig.  A, B). For reference, harmine alone docked with a score of -4.954.

 

Western blotting detected GSK3β expression and phosphorylation in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells. ZLWH-38 (10 µM) significantly reduced GSK3β phosphorylation (Fig.  D, E). The Cellular Thermal Shift Assay (CETSA) evaluated ZLWH-38 binding to GSK3β by assessing thermal stability. As shown in Fig.  B, C, ZLWH-38 treatment increased GSK3β thermal stability compared to DMSO controls, indicating direct binding. Thus, GSK3β is a direct target of ZLWH-38.

## ZLWH-38 Attenuates LPS-Induced Pulmonary Injury and Inflammation in ALI Mice
ALI mouse models were established via intratracheal administration of LPS (5 mg/kg) to evaluate the in vivo protective effects of ZLWH-38. As shown in Fig.  A and B, LPS challenge resulted in markedly thickened alveolar septa, pulmonary congestion, extensive tissue damage, macrophage activation, neutrophil infiltration, and a significantly elevated lung W/D ratio, indicative of pulmonary edema. ZLWH-38 treatment (12.5, 25, and 50 mg/kg) alleviated lung inflammatory injury, as evidenced by only occasional alveolar bleeding, minimal inflammatory cell infiltration, and localized alveolar wall thickening. The highest dose (50 mg/kg) markedly ameliorated lung pathological lesions. Moreover, ZLWH-38 markedly decreased pulmonary levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β (Fig.  C-E). Overall, these findings demonstrate that ZLWH-38 attenuates pulmonary injury and inflammatory responses.

 

## ZLWH-38 Attenuates Neutrophil Infiltration in BALF of LPS-Induced ALI Mice
ZLWH-38 significantly reduced TNF-α levels in the BALF of LPS-induced ALI mice (Fig.  A). To assess pulmonary inflammatory cell infiltration, neutrophils (gated as CD45 + CD11b+CD11c-Ly6G+) in BALF were quantified. As shown in Figs.  B–C, LPS administration (5 mg/kg) caused substantial neutrophil infiltration into the lungs, while ZLWH-38 treatment dose-dependently reduced it. Cytokine changes in BALF mirrored neutrophil alterations.

 

## Discussion
From fourteen harmine derivatives with potent GSK3β kinase inhibition, eight displayed low cytotoxicity, and the lead compound, ZLWH-38, exhibited superior anti-inflammatory efficacy. In LPS-activated RAW264.7 macrophages, ZLWH-38 reduced NO, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β production, suppressed NF-κB phosphorylation, and curbed ROS generation. In the LPS-induced ALI mouse model, ZLWH-38 markedly attenuated pulmonary TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β release, decreased neutrophil infiltration in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, and alleviated pulmonary edema and tissue damage. These findings demonstrate that ZLWH-38 possesses robust anti-inflammatory and anti-ALI activity both in vitro and in vivo.
In acute kidney injury mice, intraperitoneal harmine at 50 mg/kg for five consecutive days diminishes LPS-elicited production of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β in serum and renal tissue [ ]. In sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction, oral administration of 50 mg/kg harmine for seven days significantly lowers myocardial TNF-α and IL-1β levels [ ]. In the present study, intraperitoneal dosing of 50 mg/kg harmine or 50, 25, and 12.5 mg/kg of its derivative ZLWH-38 reproduced these protective effects against LPS-induced ALI. At equivalent systemic exposure, ZLWH-38 matched harmine in anti-ALI efficacy yet surpassed it in suppressing cytokine, NO, and ROS generation in vitro while exhibiting lower cytotoxicity and superior targeted inhibition of GSK3β. These attributes position ZLWH-38 to enhance therapeutic efficacy and simultaneously reduce the toxic liabilities associated with harmine.
Direct pulmonary or systemic inflammatory insults convert quiescent alveolar macrophages into a pro-inflammatory phenotype that releases cytokines and chemokines to recruit neutrophils, monocytes, and adaptive immune cells into injured lung tissue. Among these infiltrates, neutrophils constitute the pathognomonic cellular signature of ALI, and their abundance in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid directly correlates with disease severity [ , , ]. Rapid yet controlled neutrophil influx from the circulation eliminates pathogens and alveolar debris, whereas excessive and sustained neutrophil accumulation discharges inflammatory mediators, including cytokines, ROS, proteases, NETs, and pro-coagulant molecules, that amplify lung injury and accelerate ALI progression [ ]. Targeting neutrophil trafficking, infiltration, and oxidative activity while enhancing NET clearance therefore represents a pivotal therapeutic strategy [ ]. The harmine derivative ZLWH-38 markedly suppresses LPS-driven pulmonary neutrophil infiltration and thus constrains neutrophil-mediated collateral tissue damage.
LPS activation of macrophages and neutrophils generates abundant reactive oxygen species that initially curtail infection, yet when overproduced oxidize membrane lipids and damage DNA while amplifying redox-sensitive inflammatory cascades driven by NF-κB, p38 MAPK, and AP-1 to promote cytokine expression [ , ]. RAW264.7 macrophages challenged with LPS exhibit marked ROS elevation and overt oxidative stress, whereas ZLWH-38 substantially attenuates ROS production and thus mitigates LPS-induced oxidative stress in these cells.
Under physiological conditions, nitric oxide is synthesized by nitric-oxide synthase through the oxidation of L-arginine and NADPH in the presence of molecular oxygen and cofactors including heme, FMN, FAD, and tetrahydrobiopterin [ ]. In LPS-driven inflammation, supraphysiological NO concentrations disrupt mitochondrial integrity and elicit cellular damage [ ]. Three NOS isoforms have been identified: endothelial NOS, neuronal NOS, and inducible NOS, all of which require calmodulin binding for activation [ ]. Nanomolar NO levels are produced by nNOS and eNOS upon calcium-triggered calmodulin engagement, whereas iNOS generates micromolar NO continuously after calmodulin binding, thus contributing to host defense yet al.so promoting proliferation arrest, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis [ , ]. ZLWH-38 markedly attenuates LPS-induced iNOS expression and NO production, thereby conferring cytoprotection through modulation of the iNOS pathway.
LPS engagement of TLR4 on macrophages activates NF-κB, the master transcriptional regulator of inflammation [ ]. In the resting state, NF-κB remains inactive in the cytosol through association with inhibitory IκB proteins, whereas inflammatory stimuli activate the IκB kinase (IKK) to phosphorylate and degrade IκB, thereby liberating NF-κB for nuclear translocation and transcriptional induction of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and iNOS [ ]. We demonstrate that, in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophages, the harmine derivative ZLWH-38 suppresses phosphorylation of NF-κB p65 and IκBα to block NF-κB signaling and consequently attenuate inflammation. However, the causal relationship between GSK3β targeting, NF-κB inhibition, and anti-inflammatory effects requires validation using GSK3β siRNA or genetic knockout models.
Our findings are limited to female C57BL/6 mice, a 6-hour LPS-induced ALI model, and evidence of ZLWH-38 targeting GSK3β from molecular docking, CETSA, and kinase inhibition assays. Future studies should therefore employ both sexes, extend observation periods, use GSK3β⁻/⁻ mice to confirm target dependency, and conduct pharmacokinetic and toxicity assessments and selective kinase profiling to evaluate off-target risks and translational potential.
Molecular docking and CETSA reveal that ZLWH-38 binds GSK3β and suppresses its kinase activity, and immunoblotting confirms that ZLWH-38 inhibits LPS-evoked phosphorylation of GSK3β, establishing this kinase as a direct target of the derivative. Collectively, ZLWH-38 appears to ameliorate ALI by blocking GSK3β and thereby repressing downstream NF-κB signaling to curtail inflammation, offering a novel rationale and mechanistic foundation for ALI therapeutics.
