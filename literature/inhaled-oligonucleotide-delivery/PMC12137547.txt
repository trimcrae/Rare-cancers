## Abstract
Breast cancer, the leading cause of death in females, is profoundly affected by cooperative changes of molecular and pathological characters in fatty acid metabolism. To investigate the core molecule of fatty acid metabolism in breast cancer progression, we performed in-silico analyses and found 2,4-Dienoyl-CoA reductase (DECR1) was overexpressed in breast cancer tissues with impaired prognoses of patients. In vitro experiments showed that the proliferation and migration of breast cancer cells were significantly inhibited after DECR1 knockdown. Multi-omics analyses and metabolite detection indicated that DECR1 knockdown induced cell ferroptosis, and inhibited the production of arachidonic acid (AA) with phosphatidylcholine (PC) accumulation, AA was previously identified as ferroptosis inducer via up-regulating ACSL4, accordingly we found that knocking-down DECR1 increased the expression of ACSL4 with ferroptosis inhibitor GPX4 and SCL7A11downregulation. Additionally, we found the expression of phospholipase A2, group XIIA (PLA2G12A), a key enzyme in metabolic regulation of PC to AA, was elevated in DECR1 knocking-down cells. Further, by computer aided virtual screening, we identified various compounds with DECR1 targeting properties, and verified-Erigoster B, exerts antitumor effects by promoting ferroptosis. In conclusion, DECR1 could be a candidate target for breast cancer, and the DECR1 inhibitor Erigoster B can be a potential drug to exert therapeutic effect on breast cancer.

## Introduction
Breast cancer (BC) is the most prevalent cancer in female individuals and has high mortality rates . Although extensive research on BC has been conducted, patients with advanced BC have limited treatment options and poor prognoses. Therefore, identifying novel and effective therapeutic targets for BC is urgent and critical to develop potential antitumor candidate drugs.
Metabolic reprogramming is considered a hallmark of cancers , which involves glycolysis , oxidative phosphorylation , amino acid metabolism , and fatty acid metabolism (FAM) . Generally, rapidly proliferating cells require large amounts of fatty acids to synthesize phospholipids to maintain membrane integrity. . Fatty acids serve as raw materials for mitochondrial ATP production and protein functional modifier . Hence, fatty acids play extremely important roles in tumors, and interference with FAM may become an promising strategy for tumor treatment. The plasma and organelle membranes are dense in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) . Degradation of PUFAs requires the assistance of auxiliary enzymes, including δ2, δ3-enoyl CoA isomerase and NADPH dependent-2, 4-Dienoyl-Coa reductase(DECR1) . However, the reaction of PUFAs with an excess of ROS causes cell damage and excessive lipid peroxide production, accompanied by ferroptosis induction , . Ferroptosis regulates the fates of various diseases, including cancers , osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease , and is regulated by lipid metabolism , . Indeed, lipid peroxidation induced by cellular oxidative stress is the foundation of ferroptosis . Research has assessed the impact of different fatty acid on inducing ferroptosis and has proved that only arachidonic acid (AA) enhances ferroptosis via the ACSL4-dependent pathway . In malignancies, the degradation of phosphatidylcholine (PC) into AA catalyzed by phospholipase A2 (PLA2) represents a crucial source of AA. PLA2, a prominent enzyme family, plays a pivotal role by hydrolyzing of phospholipids, thereby releasing free fatty acids. Major subtypes of PLA2 include secretory PLA2 (sPLA2) and cytoplasmic PLA2 (cPLA2), among which PLA2G12A (Phospholipase A2 Group XIIA) is a kind of sPLA2. PLA2G12A has emerged as a potential biomarker in colorectal cancer. Its expression level significantly correlates with time to recurrence and overall survival, with lower expression indicating a poorer clinical prognosis . However, mechanisms and targeting of fatty acids metabolic features in breast cancer remains to be explored.
In this study, we analyzed the functional role of DECR1 in BC progression and evaluated the potential therapeutic value of targeting DECR1 in BC. We proved that DECR1 was over-expressed in BC tissues compared with normal breast, and the expression of DECR1 predicts impaired prognoses of BC patients. Further analysis indicated that silencing DECR1 could inducing ferroptosis by elevating PLA2G12A mediated PC-AA metabolism. According to computer aided virtual screening, we identified various compounds with DECR1 targeting properties, and verified Erigoster B exerts antitumor effects by promoting ferroptosis. Collectively, we revealed how DECR1 manifest conductively to BC cells, with therapeutic value Erigoster B by targeting DECR1 to alleviate BC progression.

## Results

## DECR1 was overexpressed in clinical breast cancer samples and was associated with poor prognosis
To explore potential antitumor targets, we analyzed the expression of eighty-four FAM-related genes in breast cancer. Initially,19 genes were identified as hub genes according to the Functional Protein Interaction Network (Supplementary Fig. ). Subsequently, examination of the expression level of 19 genes in the GEO96058 dataset revealed that DECR1 exhibited significantly higher expression in BC tissues compared to in adjacent tissues (Fig. ). Additionally, survival analysis represented in TCGA and GEO96058 datasets indicated that elevated DECR1 expression correlated with poor prognosis of BC patients (Fig. B, C). These findings collectively suggest that DECR1 holds promise as a potential antitumor target.
 To verify the expression value of DECR1 in BC patients, we performed IHC analyses with tissue arrays constructed from 140 breast cancer specimens and 77 adjacent tissues, and found that DECR1 was overexpressed in BC tissues compared with adjacent tissues (Fig. ). Survival analyses suggested that the higher expression level of DECR1 indicates impaired prognosis (Fig. 1E, F) with an independently prognostic value in 8-year survival and 10-year survival (Fig. ). Collectively, these results suggest that DECR1 expression is upregulated in breast cancer and associated with poor prognosis.

## DECR1 influenced the malignant phenotype of breast cancer cells
To identify whether the molecules identified by bioinformatics analysis have the potential to be therapeutic targets in BC, we explored the influence of DECR1 knockdown on the malignant phenotype of BC cells. We assessed the expression of DECR1 in various breast cancer cell lines and ultimately selected the MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cell lines for subsequent research by online database UALCAN and western blot (Supplementary Fig. B, C). Furthermore, we established cell lines with stable knockdown of DECR1 via lentiviral transduction and the efficacy of DECR1 knockdown was demonstrated (Supplementary Fig. ). The proliferation (Fig. A, B), migration (Fig. ) and invasion (Fig. D, E) of breast cancer cells with suppression of DECR1 expression were significantly inhibited compared to those of control cells.
 Furthermore, we established a breast cancer orthotopic xenograft model by injecting differently treated breast cancer cells into the mammary fat pads of nude mice to determine the effect of DECR1 in vivo. After inhibiting DECR1 expression, the tumor growth rate decreased, and the tumor volume and size were significantly lower than those in the control group (Fig. ). Altogether, these results demonstrated that DECR1 promoted the progression of BC in vitro and in vivo.

## Targeting DECR1 promoted ferroptosis in BC cells
DECR1 is a fatty acid β-oxidation (FAO) coenzyme. Previous studies have demonstrated the link between DECR1 and PUFAs. DECR1 is a negative regulator of the androgen receptor in prostate cancer, and inhibition of DECR1 causes PUFAs accumulation in cells, enhancing cellular oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation and thus inducing ferroptosis. PUFAs-related lipid peroxidation is the characteristic marker and driver of ferroptosis. We explored whether the expression of DECR1 in BC cells influences the level of ferroptosis. First, TEM was used to determine whether the morphological changes in mitochondria after DECR1 knockdown were consistent with the characteristics of ferroptosis. The density of the mitochondrial membrane was concentrated, mitochondrial cristae were reduced in number or lost, the outer mitochondrial membrane was ruptured, and the nuclear size was normal after DECR1 knockdown (Fig. ). The above characteristics were consistent with those of ferroptotic BC cells. Therefore, we suspected that BC cells with inhibited DECR1 expression underwent ferroptosis.
 Accordingly, we measured the levels of intracellular ROS, mitochondrial ROS, and plasma Fe2+ levels in DECR1-knockdown MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells to indicate the level of ferroptosis. The mean fluorescence intensity indicated that compared to those in negative control cells, the levels of intracellular ROS (Supplementary Fig. ), mitochondrial ROS (Fig. B, C), and Fe2+ (Fig. D, E) in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells with DECR1 knockdown were significantly increased. Moreover, the content of MDA, a product that indicates the level of cellular lipid oxidation, was measured and found to be increased in BC cells with stable DECR1 knockdown (Fig. ). To clarify the precise role of ferroptosis in influencing the malignant phenotype of tumor cells, we further treated stable DECR1 knockdown BC cells with the ferroptosis inhibitor (Ferrostatin-1). Results indicated that Ferrostatin-1 significantly rescued the proliferation and migration inhibition caused by DECR1 knockdown (Supplementary Fig. ). Taken together, these findings strongly suggested that DECR1 knockdown could trigger ferroptosis in BC cells.

## DECR1-PLA2G12A-ACSL4 axis induced ferroptosis by mediating phospholipid metabolism accompanied by the accumulation of arachidonic acids
To further explore the underlying mechanisms by which DECR1 promotes the progression of breast cancer by inducing ferroptosis, transcriptomes were compared to identify the dysregulated genes in DECR1-knockdown compared to negative control MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells. Giving the crucial role of DECR1 in FAO and its profound impact on cell metabolism, we performed metabolomic analysis to identify metabolites with significant changes. By combining the results of this analysis with those of transcriptomic analysis, we found that the PC content was reduced but AA metabolism was elevated after DECR1 knockdown (Fig. ). Simultaneously, we further assessed the levels of PE and PE-AA metabolites, which indicated that the PE-AA levels did not show significant changes following DECR1 knockdown (Supplementary Fig. ). Therefore, we hypothesized that the metabolism of PC to free AA may play a more critical role in inducing ferroptosis. Integration analyses of metabolomics and transcriptomics, revealed increased PLA2G12A levels were observed in DECR1-knockdown BC cells (Fig. B, C). However, the upregulation of other PLA2 enzymes expression was less pronounced than that of PLA2G12A after DECR1 knockdown (Supplementary Fig. A, B). In addition, we quantified the levels of various phospholipids in BC cells with DECR1 knockdown using mass spectrometry (Supplementary Figs. and ). The content of PC was significantly decreased (Fig. D, E) but the AA content was increased (Fig. ) by inhibition of DECR1 expression.
 AA, a member of the Ω-6 PUFAs family, is widely distributed and exhibits critical biological activities in vivo. Under physiological conditions, AA is predominantly esterified to PC or PE (phosphatidylethanolamine). Under stimulation, phospholipase A2 (PLA2) is activated and catalyzes the hydrolysis of phospholipid diacyl groups, resulting in the liberation of AA in a free form and its release into cells. The AA metabolic pathways in the KEGG database show that an abundance of AA is generated from phospholipids under the catalytic action of PLA2G12A. Cells with knockdown of DECR1 exhibited an increase in the content of PLA2G12A. Further knockdown of PLA2G12A in BC cells (Supplementary Fig. C, D) was accompanied by a decrease in the intracellular AA content (Fig. ), and BC cells were protected from ferroptosis (Fig. ). Exogenous treatment with PLA2G12A protein at both 20 ng/mL and 40 ng/mL significantly increased intracellular ROS and Fe2+ levels, indicating that PLA2G12A functions as a key regulatory molecule in the induction of ferroptosis in tumor cells (Supplementary Fig. ). Besides, we also compared the binding capabilities of PC/PA2G12A and DECR1/PLA2G12A, and found that DECR1 and PLA2G12A bind more firmly, blocking the catalytic effect of PLA2G12A to PC (Supplementary Fig. ). To further investigate the potential competitive binding mechanism, we performed site-directed mutagenesis on the three residues exhibiting the strongest binding affinity (Lys42, Gln40, and Thr255), substituting each with alanine (Supplementary Fig. F, G). Subsequent molecular docking analyses of the mutated sites revealed a marked decrease in binding free energy at two residues—Gln40 and Thr255—compared to the wild-type, suggesting that these two amino acids may play critical roles in the interaction interface (Supplementary Fig. H, I). Previous study suggests that AA induce ferroptosis in tumors through ACSL4. We detected the expression of ferroptosis-related genes in BC cells with DECR1 knockdown and found that ACSL4 expression was significantly increased, accompanied by downregulation of ferroptosis key gene GPX4 and SLC7A11 expression (Fig. ). It was observed that the expression of ACSL4 and PLA2G12A was significantly upregulated at the protein level following DECR1 knockdown (Fig. ). Further knockdown of ACSL4 using siRNA in DECR1-depleted breast cancer cells (Supplementary Fig. ) led to a reduction in mitochondrial ROS and intracellular Fe2+ levels (Supplementary Fig. ), suggesting that ACSL4 may serve as a critical downstream effector mediating DECR1 knockdown-induced ferroptosis. These results suggested that targeting the DECR1-PLA2G12A-ACSL4 signaling axis in BC promoted the production of AA by regulating phospholipid metabolism, thus activating ferroptosis and inhibiting tumor progression.

## The DECR1 inhibitor Erigoster B exerts therapeutic potential in BC
We demonstrated that inhibition of DECR1 may be a promising therapeutic strategy for BC. Therefore, we searched for small molecules targeting DECR1 to preliminarily explore its potential as a candidate drug using computer-aided virtual screening and molecular docking approaches. The main amino acid residues in DECR1 that interacted with Erigoster B were GLY66, THR69, GLY70, LEU71, GLY72, ASN144, ALA145, ALA146, ASN148, ILE150, ILE195, THR196, THR197, LYS214, PRO240, GLY241, PRO242, ILE243, THR245 and ARG251. Moreover, Erigoster B formed hydrogen bonds with THR69, ASN144, THR196, LYS214, ILE243 and SER250, which played a crucial role in the binding of DECR1 and Erigoster B (Fig. A, B). However, the binding sites of Erigoster B in DECR1 were not completely consistent with those of NADP, and the Erigoster B binding mode was the only theoretical solution that had the best score compared to DECR1 (Fig. ). The binding ability of Erigoster B to DECR1 was demonstrated by SPR in vitro, and the results suggested that DECR1 captured on a COOH chip could bind Erigoster B with an affinity constant of 40.5 μM, as determined by localized SPR (LSPR) (Fig. ). Furthermore, we evaluated the antitumor effect of Erigoster B and found that it could inhibit the proliferation of BC cells (Fig. ).
 Furthermore, we tested the influence of Erigoster B on the migration and invasion of BC cells. Erigoster B treatment for 48 h resulted in significantly reduced cell migration and invasion. After preliminary evaluation of drug treatment, we found that the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of Erigoster B was 17.12 μM in MDA-MB-231 cells and 16.36 μM in BT474 cells. Based on the that, we selected concentrations (1 μM and 5 μM) that are non-impaired to cell viability for subsequent functional validation (Fig. F, G). Treatment with Erigoster B suppressed tumor growth in vivo, with an effect equal to that of paclitaxel (Fig. I, J). Compared to treatment with paclitaxel (PTX) alone, the combination of Erigoster B and PTX significantly suppressed tumor growth rate (Supplementary Fig. A, B). In addition, the in vivo safety profile of Erigoster B was evaluated. Administration of high-dose Erigoster B (45 mg/kg) in both wild-type and tumor-bearing mice did not result in significant weight loss or observable histopathological changes in major organs, indicating that Erigoster B exhibits good systemic tolerance and biosafety (Supplementary Fig. ). These findings indicate that Erigoster B exhibits therapeutic activity against BC cells and warrants further clinical investigation for BC treatment.

## Erigoster B induced breast cancer cell ferroptosis
Erigoster B is a phenolic acid compound and was first identified in a systematic study of the phytochemistry and active ingredients of Erigeron breviscapus. This compound has been proven to have antioxidant activity equivalent to that of scutellarin. We found that Erigoster B caused cell death, which could be rescued by ferroptosis inhibitors rather than inhibitors of other types of cell death (Fig. A-B), and various ferroptosis core genes expression showed differences compared to control group, including GPX4, SLC7A11 and ACSL4 (Fig. ). These results suggested that ferroptosis is the main mechanism by which Erigoster B induces BC cell death. As expected, Erigoster B treatment increased the levels of intracellular ROS (Fig. C, D), mitochondrial ROS (Fig. D, E), and Fe2+ (Fig. F, G), suggesting that Erigoster B may exert antitumor effects by inducing ferroptosis in breast cancer cells. Moreover, we found that treatment with Erigoster B in BC cells caused an increase in the AA content and a decrease in the PC content (Fig. ). Furthermore, we assessed the expression of relevant proteins following Erigoster B treatment. The results showed that treatment with Erigoster B significantly suppressed DECR1 expression while upregulating the protein levels of ACSL4 and PLA2G12A (Fig. ). These findings indicated that Erigoster B exhibits therapeutic activity against breast cancer cells and induces ferroptosis by regulating PC metabolism and then increasing the AA content via DECR1-PLA2G12A-ACSL4 axis. Thus, further clinical investigation of Erigoster B for breast cancer treatment is warranted.
 

## Discussion
Metabolic reprogramming is not only a hallmark of tumors but also provides a promising opportunity for BC treatment , . Lipid metabolism is crucial for the biosynthesis of cellular components and ATP production to maintain cancer cell survival and proliferation. In this study, we focused on another understudied aspect of lipid metabolism, lipid oxidation, to evaluate the potential of FAO-related genes as therapeutic targets. Several studies have shown that malignant progresses including angiogenesis, metastasis, and chemotherapy resistance can be controlled by using etoposide to block FAO , . Additionally, interfering with cellular lipid oxidation leads to cell death; for example, phospholipid peroxidation induces ferroptosis. ROS, superoxide, peroxides, and free radicals can trigger the oxidation of PUFAs and cause oxidative stress, an event that is the basis of ferroptosis . Recent studies have demonstrated that promoting TPD52-mediated lipid storage or inhibiting ATG5- and RAB7A-dependent lipid degradation protects against RSL3-induced lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis. These findings suggest that modulating the balance between lipid synthesis, storage, and degradation can influence ferroptosis susceptibility . Therefore, FAM provides more target strategies for BC treatment to induce ferroptosis , .
We performed bioinformatic analysis to evaluate the potential targets of FAM-related genes and found that DECR1 was significantly overexpressed in BC and associated with poor prognosis, indicating its promise as a therapeutic target in BC. DECR1, a rate-limiting factor in PUFAs oxidation, has also been shown to be overexpressed in human prostate cancer and associated with an increased risk of recurrence . Moreover, DECR1 knockdown selectively inhibited β-oxidation of PUFAs and dysregulation of fatty acid homeostasis in Decr1−/− mice . DECR1 was originally studied as a coenzyme in FAO. It participates in the mitochondrial metabolism of unsaturated fatty enoyl-CoA esters with double bonds in both even- and odd-numbered positions. In addition, it catalyzes the NADP-dependent reduction of 2,4-dienoyl-CoA to yield trans-3-enoyl-CoA . We found that DECR1 was abnormally overexpressed in BC, and inhibiting the expression of DECR1 showed antitumor effects in vivo and in vitro. Similarly, DECR1 has also been identified as a potential therapeutic target in tumors including prostate cancer and cervical cancer . However, in HER2 positive BC subtype, DECR1 may exert antitumor effects by reducing the expression of ErbB2/Neu . Consequently, the role of DECR1 under different types of malignancies remains to be clarified. This complex regulatory relationship may be influenced by various factors, including the tumor microenvironment, intracellular signaling pathways, and metabolic states. Therefore, further research is needed to comprehensively understand the mechanisms by which DECR1 contributes to tumorigenesis.
Indeed, DECR1 exhibits cancer type-specific functions influenced by metabolic demands, signaling pathways, tumor microenvironment, and genetic background. For example, prostate cancer cells depend on fatty acid oxidation, and high DECR1 expression supports this metabolic need . It is also regulated by androgen signaling and is upregulated after androgen deprivation therapy . Additionally, DECR1 may affect redox balance and cell survival pathways . In the tumor microenvironment, it may promote progression by modulating interactions with immune cells , and its role may vary due to metabolic heterogeneity . Genetic alterations and epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation, further contribute to its functional diversity across cancers . In summary, the functional differences of DECR1 across various tumor types result from the combined influence of metabolic demands, signaling pathways, tumor microenvironment, and both genetic and epigenetic regulation. To further explore the mechanism by which DECR1 promotes BC progression, we conducted transcriptomic and metabonomic analyses on BC cells with DECR1 knockdown and the corresponding negative control group. The results showed that after knockdown of DECR1, intracellular PC metabolism was active, accompanied by the activation of PLA2G12A, a key phospholipase A2 enzyme involved in lipid metabolism, which promoted the production and accumulation of AA. Notably, AA, rather than other fatty acids, has been shown to interact with IFNγ for synergistic induction of potent ferroptosis in tumor cells . As known, phospholipase A2 (PLA2) enzymes play a crucial role in hydrolyzing phospholipids, thereby influencing cell signaling and membrane dynamics . We noticed that, in addition to PLA2G12A’s significant role in lipid metabolism, other PLA2 enzymes also contribute. PLA2G6 hydrolyzes oxidized lipids, working with LPCAT3 to convert pro-ferroptotic signals into healthy phospholipids . iPLA2β prevents ferroptosis by eliminating redox lipid death signals, influencing cellular sensitivity to ferroptosis . Darapladib, an Lp-PLA2 inhibitor, enhances cancer cell sensitivity to ferroptosis by remodeling lipid metabolism iPLA2β-mediated lipid detoxification is crucial in p53-driven ferroptosis, independent of GPX4 . MBOAT1 and MBOAT2 inhibit ferroptosis by reshaping the cellular phospholipid profile . In conclusion, the distinct roles of PLA2 enzymes, such as PLA2G6, iPLA2β, and the MBOAT family, in modulating ferroptosis highlight their significance in lipid metabolism and cellular signaling. Targeting these enzymes could offer promising therapeutic strategies for enhancing cancer treatment efficacy and overcoming ferroptosis resistance.
This study, however, had limitations. First, the in vivo assay was performed with a cell line-derived xenograft model in nude mice. In vivo studies with breast cancer patient-derived organoid units would be more helpful to investigatethe critical role of DECR1 in different subtypes of BC. Additionally, the antitumor effects of the DECR1 inhibitor, Erigoster B need more validation by evaluating its ADME properties and toxicity. In conclusion, our results show that DECR1 is an actionable target and that its inhibitor Erigoster B is a promising therapeutic compound warranting further clinical investigation for breast cancer treatment.
In this study, we revealed the vital role of DECR1 in the progression of breast cancer and found that DECR1 was upregulated in breast cancer tissues and cells and that DECR1 expression was negatively correlated with patient prognosis. Functionally, the tumor-promoting effect of DECR1 was demonstrated in vivo and in vitro. Regarding the molecular mechanism, the activity of PLA2G12A was suppressed by DECR1, which interfered with the hydrolysis of PC to release AA, thus preventing ferroptosis in BC cells.

## Methods

## Gene expression analysis
The set of FAM-associated genes was obtained from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG; https://www.genome.jp/kegg/). Data for an independent cohort (TCGA-BRCA) containing 1096 cases was downloaded and imported into R (version 4.1) via The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) Biolink (https://bioconductor.org/biocLite.R). In this cohort, 1045 cases were retained for further analysis due to the availability of sufficient clinical follow-up data. Data for 3409 cases (GSE96058) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to validate the gene expression and prognostic value of DECR1 in breast cancer patients. For survival analysis, gene expression data were normalized and mean-centered. Kaplan‒Meier curves were plotted to show the value of the specified FAM-associated genes for predicting overall survival (OS). The outcome event was defined as “deceased”. The cut-off expression level of each gene was estimated by using the best separation algorithm according to the R package “survminer”. The log-rank test was used to analyze the data from the Kaplan‒Meier plots and results of univariate Cox regression analysis. The correlation between DECR1 expression and the OS rate was further evaluated by multivariate Cox regression analysis, and a nomogram was generated by using the “rms” package based on the multivariate Cox regression analysis results.

## Functional protein association network analysis
A functional association network of FAM genes was indexed by STRING (https://cn.string-db.org/). Subsequently, the functional association network was analyzed by Cytoscape (version 3.7.0, http://www.cytoscape.org/). Genes for which the degree, topological coefficient and neighborhood connectivity were all above the median score were selected and are listed in Supplementary Table .

## Tissue microarrays derived from clinical samples
A total of 140 breast cancer tissues and 77 adjacent paraffin tissues were collected based on the clinical and pathological information and the availability of complete follow-up information. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The Medical Ethics Committee of Shanghai Outdo Biotech Company granted ethical approval for the project (Grant Nos. SHYJC-CP-1901002; SHYJC-CP-1607006). The study was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsink.

## Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining
The tissue microarray was incubated at 4 °C overnight with a rabbit monoclonal anti-DECR1 antibody (Abcam, ab198848) at a 1:100 dilution. An EnVision system was used for immunohistochemical staining. Diaminobenzidine (Dako, Glostrup, Denmark) was used for visualization of antibody binding. The images were captured using a Patholigical imaging microscope (Leica DM2000). The staining intensity of DECR1 was calculated by Image-Pro Plus (version 6.0.0.260, USA).

## Cell culture
The human BC cell lines MDA-MB-231 and BT474 were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). All cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) or RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, 10437028) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco, 10378016) at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere.

## Functional assays
Cell viability: BC cell viability was evaluated using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) Cell Proliferation Assay Kit (Solarbio, CA1210). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1 ×105 cells/mL and allowed to adhere overnight. The BC cells with DECR1 knockdown or treated with varying concentration of Erigoster B (0, 3.125, 6.25, 12.5 25, 50, 100, 200 μM) for 48 h. After treatment, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to the medium, and the cells were incubated for another 2 hours. The OD was then measured at 450 nm using a multiwell spectrophotometer (microplate reader). Cell viability was expressed as a percentage of that of the control (untreated) cells. The experiment was performed in triplicate with setting 6 replicate wells for each condition.
Cell migration: When MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells were 100% confluent, a wound was created in the central region of each well using a 2 well silicone insert with a defined cell-free gap (Ibidi, Germany). The wounded areas were photographed at 0 and 24 h. Each experiment was repeated three times.
Cell invasion: Breast cancer cells were cultured in serum-free medium for 12 h. The cell concentration was adjusted to 4-5 × l05 cells/mL. Transwell chambers with a Matrigel-coated membrane (Corning, USA) were placed in 24-well plates. RPMI-1640 medium (500 μL) containing 10% FBS was placed in the bottom compartment, and 200 μL of the cell suspension was placed in the upper compartment. Then, the cells were incubated for 24 h. The cells on the lower surface of the membrane were fixed with glacial acetic acid for 15-30 min and stained with crystal violet for 30 min, and cells were counted. The experiments were repeated independently three times.

## Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis
The morphological characteristics of ferroptotic breast cancer cells were evaluated by TEM. In brief, a total of 2×104 cells were seeded onto a chambered cover glass. Samples were pre-fixed in 3% glutaraldehyde and post-fixed in 1% osmium tetroxide. And then gradual dehydration was achieved using a series of ethanol concentrations (30%, 50%, 70%, 80%, 90%, 95%, and 100%). Samples were infiltrated with acetone/Epon812 resin mixtures (3:1, 1:1, and 1:3) before embedding in pure Epon812 resin. Ultra-thin sections (60 ~ 90 nm) were prepared and mounted onto copper grids. Sections were stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Images were acquired using a JEM-1400FLASH transmission electron microscope. Initial observation was performed at 6000x magnification to assess cellular morphology changes.

## Transcriptome analysis
To investigate the role of DECR1 gene in breast cancer cells, we constructed stable knockdown cell lines of DECR1 in human breast cancer cell lines MDA-MB-231 and BT474. Specific shRNA sequences targeting DECR1 were designed and synthesized, cloned into lentiviral expression vectors, and then used to infect cells followed by selection of stable expressing clones. Total RNA was extracted from log-phase growing DECR1 knockdown cells and their control counterparts using Trizol reagent according to standard procedures. After quality assessment, the extracted RNA was used for library construction using the Illumina RNA Library Prep Kit. This process included mRNA purification, fragmentation, reverse transcription, end-repair of cDNA, A-tailing, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification. The quality of the libraries was assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer and Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer.
The constructed libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq platform using a PE150 strategy, which involves paired-end sequencing with a read length of 150 bp. The sequencing data were saved in fastq format, containing raw sequencing reads. Raw sequencing data were first subjected to quality control using FastQC to remove low-quality reads and adapter sequences. The featureCounts software package (version 1.5.0) was used to calculate the read counts for each gene, serving as a measure of gene expression. For differential gene expression analysis, we employed the “limma” package for R (version 4.1.2). Differentially expressed genes were screened based on a p-value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change | > 1.

## Untargeted metabolomics
For untargeted metabolomics, 1 × 107 cells were placed in Eppendorf (EP) tubes and resuspended in prechilled 80% methanol by vortexing well. Then, the samples were melted on ice and whirled for 30 s. After sonication for 6 min, the samples were centrifuged at 5000 rpm and 4 °C for 1 min. The supernatants were freeze-dried and dissolved in 10% methanol. Finally, the solutions were injected into the LC-MS/MS system for analysis. UHPLC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a Vanquish UHPLC system (ThermoFisher, USA) coupled to an Orbitrap Q ExactiveTM HF mass spectrometer (ThermoFisher, USA) at Novogene Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The raw data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 (CD3.1, ThermoFisher) to perform peak alignment, peak picking, and quantitation for each metabolite. These metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database, the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB; https://hmdb.ca/metabolites) and the LIPIDMaps database (http://www.lipidmaps.org/).

## Quantitative RT-PCR
Total RNA was extracted and purified using TRIzol reagent (Magen, R4801-01) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Next, reverse transcription was performed using a transcription kit (Vazyme, R122-01). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed using an ABI 7900 real-time PCR system, and all primers used for amplification of target genes are listed in the supplementary material (Supporting Information Supplementary Table ). Finally, data were analyzed using the 2-ΔΔCt method. The mRNA levels of the target genes were normalized to those of β-actin or GAPDH.

## Western blot
Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Protein concentration was measured using the BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 µg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% BSA, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against with Anti-β-actin (Abcam, England, ab6276), Anti-DECR1(Abcam, England, ab198848), Anti-ACSL4 (Santa Cruz, USA, sc-365230). Bound antibodies were detected with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech, USA) by ECL kit (Beyotime, China, P0018S). Densitometric analysis was performed using ImageJ.

## Animal experiments
Nude mice and BALB/c mice were purchased from Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. Experimental colonies were maintained at the experimental animal center of Southwest University under specific pathogen-free conditions. The animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Southwest University. The animals were housed in cages at 23 ± 2 °C and fed a standard laboratory diet and tap water throughout the experiments.
Orthotopic xenograft model: Mice were randomly assigned into treatment and control groups. Mice were anesthetized with 4% chloral hydrate, and 100 μL of a breast cancer cell suspension with 1×107 cells was injected into the mammary fat pad to establish the orthotopic xenograft model. For the tumorigenesis assay, knockdown negative control (NC) MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells, DECR1-knockdown (shDECR1) MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells were injected into nude mice, and 4T1 cells were injected into BALB/c mice. Different treatments were administered for approximately 7 days (n = 5 per group). The tumors were measured every two days, and the tumor volume was calculated as follows: tumor volume = (length × width2)/2. Animals were euthanized by CO2 inhalation followed by observation of rigor mortis. No analgesic drug was used for pain. All experiments were performed in accordance with French legislation concerning the protection of laboratory animals and in accordance with a currently valid license for experiments on vertebrate animals, issued by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Institute of Animal Science at the Southwest University (No. IACUC-20240229-11).

## Measurement of malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration, ROS level, and Fe2+ level
To evaluate ferroptosis in the groups, the MDA, ROS and Fe2+ levels were measured in each group. The intracellular MDA concentration, ROS level and Fe2+ level were assessed using a Lipid Peroxidation (MDA) Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, S0131S), an Intracellular ROS Assay Kit (Vigorous Biotechnology, R001), MitoSOX Red mitochondrial superoxide indicator (Invitrogen, M36008) and a FerroOrange Assay Kit (Dojindo, F374) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Computer-aided drug screening methods
Receptors for virtual screening: The protein structure of DECR1 was optimized using the Protein Preparation Wizard panel (Schrödinger 2021) by correcting the bond order, adding hydrogen atoms, distributing charges, and predicting protonation states (pH 7.0). The OPLS4 force field was used for constrained energy optimization to eliminate atomic conflicts in the structure with the root mean square deviation (RMSD) of heavy atoms converging to 0.3 Å, and the side chain positions were optimized to obtain a rational side chain structure.
Compound library preparation: The compounds from L6020 were processed with the LigPrep panel (Schrödinger 2021). First, the compounds were protonated and desalted with the Epik program at pH 7.0 ± 2.0 to generate tautomers and maintain the original atomic chirality. To ensure the conformational diversity of small molecules during the virtual screening process, up to 32 conformations of each small molecule were generated.
Molecular docking: The following pipeline was used for screening: Glide HTVS [high throughput virtual screening mode] → Glide SP [standard precision mode] → Glide XP [extra precision]. The protein structure was set as rigid, small molecules as flexible, and other parameters as the default values. The docked structures were subjected to energy optimization, and the structures with docking scores within the top 50% were retained for the next round of screening. Visual inspection was performed after duplicate removal and filtering for drug-like properties.
After the initial screen, approximately 15,000 small molecule compounds were obtained. After removing duplicate structures, 2497 compounds remained. Filtering was performed based on the predicted absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) properties using QikProp (specified criteria: 0 < H bond donor < 6, 150 < Mol Weight < 725). Compounds with a molecular weight of greater than 725 and an H bond donor count of greater than 6 were removed, resulting in a final set of 1258 compounds. Binary fingerprints of these 1258 compounds were then generated using Canvas, and hierarchical clustering based on the Tanimoto similarity metric was performed. The compound with the highest docking score (in kcal/mol, where a lower value indicates a higher affinity between the receptor and the ligand) was selected from each cluster. This process yielded a final set of 100 small molecule compounds representing different chemical structures (Supplementary Table ).

## Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay
Interactions between analytes and ligands were evaluated at room temperature with an OpenSPR instrument (Nicoya, Canada). First, according to the standard procedure, a COOH sensor chip was installed in the OpenSPR instrument. The buffer was run at the maximum flow rate and exhaust bubble parameter after the signal baseline was reached. Then, 10 mM glycine-HCl (pH 7.4) was injected onto the sensor chip surface. The chip was activated, and the prepared EDC and NHS solutions were injected to complete functionalization of the chip surface. Then, the sample was injected at a flow rate of 20 μL/min.
Finally, different concentrations of analytes were injected separately onto the surface of the ligand chip (the specific concentrations are provided in the experimental results), and the analyte was sampled at 20 µL/min. The binding time of the analyte to the ligand was 240 s, and the natural dissociation time was 360 s. A 200 μL volume of sample was flowed through the chip for 240 s at a constant flow rate of 20 μL/min in each cycle. The bound compounds were dissociated from the coating surface by treatment with acidic and alkaline solutions. After each measurement, small molecules and target proteins were dissociated by treatment with 10 mM glycine-HCl (pH 1.5). Finally, the kinetic parameters of the binding reactions were calculated and analyzed by Trace Drawer software (Ridgeview Instruments AB, Sweden).

## Targeted quantitative LC‒MS Analysis of Phosphatidylcholine (PC)
A total of 1×107 cells were collected, and a 70% methanol/water solution was added to grind and crush the cells. Then, trichloromethane solution was used for metabolite extraction. Chloroform-containing metabolic substances were further concentrated in a concentration apparatus. The concentrates were further resolubilized in 300 μL of isopropanol: methanol (1:1) solution (containing 100 ng/mL internal standard PC (15:0/18:1-d7)) and centrifuged for 12,000 rpm, 15 min, and the supernatant was retained for analysis. In addition, the PC (16:0-18:1) standard solution (500 ng/ml) was analyzed, with an internal standard concentration of 100 ng/ml.
Chromatographic analysis was performed on a Waters Acquity UPLC using an Acquity UPLC BEH Amide column (1.7 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm). The chromatographic separation conditions were as follows: column temperature, 45 °C; flow rate, 0.300 mL/min. Metabolomic analysis was performed with mobile phase A (H2O:acetonitrile 5 mM; NH4AC, 95/5, v/v) and mobile phase B (H2O:acetonitrile 5 mM; NH4AC, 50/50, v/v, pH adjusted to 8.0 with ammonia water) with the following elution gradient: 0–1 min, 0% B; 1–2 min, 50% B; 2–4 min, 100% B; 4-6 min, 0% B.
Furthermore, mass spectrometry analysis was performed on an AB SCIEX 5500 triple quadrupole (QQQ) -mass spectrometer using an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Mass spectrometry was performed with the following conditions: curtain gas, 35 arb; collision gas, 9 arb; ion spray voltage, 4500 V; temperature, 450 °C; ion source gas 1, 50 arb; and ion source gas 2, 50 arb. The final results were integrated using MultiQuant software, and metabolite contents were calculated using the internal standard method.

## Arachidonic acid content analysis
MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells with DECR1 knockdown and BC cells treated with Erigoster B (5 μM, 48 h) were collected. Then, 150-200 μL of PBS was added to every 106 BC cells for resuspension. Cells were lysed by ultrasonication, and the supernatant was tested with an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit following the manufacturer’s instructions. The Arachidonic Acid (AA) Assay Kit (E-EL-0051c) was obtained from Elabscience.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 20.0 software (SPSS Software Products). All data in this study are expressed as the mean ± SD of three independent experiments. Differences among more than two groups were examined for statistical significance using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). The Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to present the survival curves and log-rank test were used to estimate the differences of survival curves between two different groups. Cut-off values were calculated in R software (Version 4.1.0, Vienna, Austria) by the “BestSeparation” algorithm in “Survminer” package. Nomograms were constructed by R according to the “rms” package. Differences between two groups were examined for statistical significance using Student’s t test. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.
