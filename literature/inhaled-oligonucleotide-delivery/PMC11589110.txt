## Abstract

## Background
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) with complex molecular carcinogenesis represents a kind of prevalent neoplasm occurring in the liver. The objective of this study is to illustrate the function of baculoviral inhibitor of apoptosis repeat containing 5 (BIRC5) and underlying action mechanisms in HCC progression.

## Methods
Comprehensive bioinformatics methods were conducted to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs), cuproptosis-associated DEGs, and hub genes. The correlation between BIRC5 and immune cell infiltration, prognosis value was evaluated. The specific effects of BIRC5 silencing on HCC cells was validated by functional assays, and the impact on tumorigenicity and cuproptosis was also elucidated in vivo. Additionally, the effects of BIRC5 deficiency on PPAR pathway were determined using Oroxin A in vitro.

## Results
A total of 45 cuproptosis-associated DEGs and 9 hub genes were discovered through bioinformatics. Then 6 core genes were confirmed in Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1 cells with 4 genes upregulated and 2 genes downregulated. Therein, BIRC5 was positively correlated with the infiltration of CD8+ T cells, macrophages, and highly expressed BIRC5 exhibited poor prognosis of overall survival in HCC. Furthermore, BIRC5 deletion inhibited the PPARγ pathway, thereby restraining the malignant phenotypes of HCC cells and tumorigenesis in vivo. Additionally, silencing of BIRC5 contributed to the initiation of cuproptosis in HCC.

## Conclusions
BIRC5 silencing attenuated HCC through blocking PPARγ pathway and regulating cuproptosis, which may offer therapeutic implications against HCC.

## Introduction
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), as the most prevalent neoplasm of liver with constantly increasing incidence rates has posed serious threats to the health of human worldwide [ , ]. Even though liver transplantation, surgical resection, thermal ablation are considered as potentially curative therapies for HCC, up to 70% high recurrence rate of cases 5 years following treatment [ , ]. Furthermore, less than 30% of HCC patients are suitable for radical treatment at the time of initial diagnosis owing to the insidious onset of HCC [ ]. Therefore, it is of paramount importance to explore the complex pathogenesis against HCC development and find more novel effective targets in clinical treatment of HCC.
Cuproptosis, a unique form of cell death driven by copper is distinct from existing other regulated cell death pathways [ ]. Increasing number of researchers have linked cuproptosis to diverse tumors like lung cancers and primary liver cancer [ , ]. Furthermore, copper can modulate tumor cell characteristics, and excess copper will cause liver damage, thus copper homeostasis exhibits broader implications for the development of HCC [ ]. Besides, elevated alpha-fetoprotein levels and advanced tumor stages are relevant to high cuproptosis index values [ ]. Additionally, MELK regulates cuproptosis-related gene DLAT-mediated mitochondrial activity, thereby stimulating HCC carcinogenesis [ ]. Therefore, targeting cuproptosis offers a new lens in guiding treatment for patients with HCC.
Baculoviral inhibitor of apoptosis repeat containing 5 (BIRC5, also named survivin), is categorized to IAP family that is essential for regulation of cell mitosis and control of apoptosis during tumorigenesis and metastasis in cancer cells [ , ]. Distinguishingly high expression of BIRC5 in cancers makes it considered as a promising therapeutic candidate for cancer therapy. For example, BIRC5 may act as a potential biomarker for laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma [ ] and lung adenocarcinoma prognosis [ ], as well as prevention and early diagnosis of precancerous lesions to gastric cancer [ ]. Moreover, BIRC5 exerts a role of immunosuppressive in HCC [ ], and high expressed BIRC5 possibly increases the mortality of patients with HCC [ ]. Additionally, circANKRD52 accelerates HCC tumorigenesis through upregulation of BIRC5 expression via sponging off miR-497-5p [ ]. However, the specific role of BIRC5 in modulating cuproptosis of HCC remains, and the mechanisms behind BIRC5 in HCC are still not fully understood.
Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) consisting of three subtypes containing PPARα, PPARβ/δ, and PPARγ, which govern hepatic lipid homeostasis, immunity and inflammation response [ , ]. Depart from the well-known properties, emerging discoveries are also made in PPARs’ functions in the cancer field like inhibition of cell proliferation, regulation of apoptosis and tumor immune response, which has been proved in colorectal cancer [ ], ovarian cancer [ ] and prostate cancer [ , ]. Furthermore, in the pathophysiology of liver cancer, PPARγ-mediated effects facilitate growth arrest, cell senescence or clearance, which makes PPARγ a potential therapeutic target in attenuating hepatocarcinogenesis to reduce the global burden linked to liver cancers [ ]. However, the correlation of BIRC5 with PPAR in the development of HCC is still elusive.
Herein, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs), cuproptosis-related DEGs, and core genes were determined through the comprehensive bioinformatics methods. We then attempted to elucidate the function of BIRC5 and underlying action mechanisms on HCC, which may provide a meaningful reference for further understanding the complex pathogenesis involved in HCC and identification of the molecular targets regarding HCC therapeutics.

## Materials and methods

## Data sources
Two expression profiles by array taken “HCC” as a keyword, GSE74656 and GSE50579 were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database and further cuproptosis-related genes were screened through the cuproptosis online gene set (https://www.genecards.org/cgi-bin/carddisp). There were 5 adjacent normal tissue samples and 5 HCC samples in the GSE74656 (platform GPL16043, GeneChip PrimeView Human Gene Expression Array). Similarly, GSE50579 dataset contained 10 controls and 67 HCC tissue samples (GPL14550, Agilent-028004 SurePrint G3 Human GE 8 × 60 K Microarray). The interactive online tool Gene Expression Omnibus 2R (GEO2R, www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r) was utilized to analyze DEGs according to the thresholds of p < 0.05 and |logFC|≥ 2. The heatmap and volcano plot were plotted using Pheatmap, and ggplot2 packages in the R software (Version 3.6.3), and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis was performed the signal pathway enrichment analysis for all DEGs in the two datasets, respectively. Finally, a Venn diagram was depicted to screen overlapping genes among GSE74656, GSE50579 datasets and cuproptosis-related genes through the EVenn tool (http://www.ehbio.com/test/venn/#/).

## Intersection of DEGs and functional enrichment analysis
The functional roles of the overlapping DEGs were analyzed through GO annotation and KEGG using the Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery (DAVID, https://david.ncifcrf.gov/summary.jsp, version 6.8). The enrichment analysis results were visualized in the bubble plots using “Enrichment map” R package with p < 0.05 regarded as significant.

## PPI network construction and identification of the hub genes
The screened common DEGs (co-DEGs) were subjected to construct the PPI network through the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING, https://www.string-db.org, version 12.0) with a confidence score more than 0.4 as the threshold. Then the Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) were applied for visualization and the significant gene modules using MCODE plugin (degree cut-off = 2; node score cut-off = 0.2; k-score = 2; maximum depth = 100) to further screen core genes. The hub candidate genes were filtered with the connectivity degree ≥ 10, and the top 9 genes were identified.

## Analysis of hub genes
The expression of hub genes in the original sample data was selected as a variable for PCA and ridge map. The GO enrichment chord diagram was depicted to assess the relationship between hub genes and potential pathways, then the correlation analysis of identified hub genes was performed. The expression differences of hub genes were verified on the GEPIA2 website (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index). The ROC curve analysis was conducted with the AUC to assess the diagnostic accuracy. Finally, Kaplan–Meier Plotter (http://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php?p=background) was performed to evaluate clinical prognosis of hub genes in HCC.

## Immune-related characteristics
The SangerBox tool (http://sangerbox.com/home.html) was utilized to analyze hub genes expression differences based on UCSC (https://xenabrowser.net/) pan-cancer data. The relationship between hub genes expression and clinical outcome in human cancers was investigated using “survival” in the R package, and differences between curves were evaluated using the log-rank test with p < 0.05 considered significant. Timer is a comprehensive resource for the systematic analysis of associations between gene expression and immune cell infiltration features in tumors of UCSC, and immune score was evaluated through CIBERSORT analysis using Pearson's correlation coefficient with p < 0.05 indicated a significant correlation.

## Cell culture and transfection
The normal human liver cell line (L-O2) and human HCC lines (Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1) were obtained from iCell (Shanghai, China), then were cultured in DMEM medium (Hyclone, Logan, UT, USA) containing 10% heat-inactivated FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (Hyclone, Logan, UT, USA) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 concentration conditions.
BIRC5 knockdown in Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1 cells was achieved by transfection with BIRC5 siRNA. Briefly, Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1cells were seeded in 6-well plates until the cells reached 70–80% confluency in each well, they were transfected with 50 nM BIRC5 siRNA or a negative control using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Cat. no. 11668019, California, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. After 24 h, the cells were harvested and evaluated at mRNA levels. The siRNA sequences were designed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) and were listed in Supplementary Table 1. In addition, the cells were treated with PPARγ pathway activator Oroxin A (1 nM, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 2 h.

## Construction of stable cell lines
The lentiviruses knocking down BIRC5 expression (LV‐BIRC5-1 and LV‐BIRC5-2) and matched negative control lentiviruses (LV‐NC) were purchased from GenePharma. The SK-Hep-1 cells were infected with LV‐BIRC5 and LV‐NC, and Polyamine (GenePharma, 5 μg/mL) was added into to improve infection the efficiency. For the acquisition of stable cell lines, the cells were selected using 3 μg/mL puromycin (Vicmed, Xuzhou, China) 48 h after transfection for consecutive 2 weeks. The target sequences for LV-BIRC5 are provided in Supplementary Table 2.

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR).
Total RNA was extracted by splitting cells using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and cDNA synthesis was performed with PrimeScript RT Reagent kit (TaKaRa, Otsu, Japan). Then qRT-PCR was carried out with ABI7500 quantitative PCR instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and the reaction procedures were as follows: 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s. A comparative 2−ΔΔCt method was applied to quantify gene expression with β-actin used for normalization. The primer sequences used for qRT-PCR were synthesized by Shenggong Bioengineering Company (Shanghai, China) and displayed in Supplementary Table 1.

## Cell viability assay
The cells were seeded in 96-well microplates with a density of 1 × 103 cells/well at an incubator of 37 °C until the cells reached 80% confluence and then the medium was replaced with 2% FBS-supplemented DMEM per well for inoculation of 24, 48, 72 h. Next, 10 µL of diluted CCK-8 regents (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 2 h of inoculation, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm every hour on a microplate reader (VL0000D0, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) to determine the best culture time.

## Scratch assay
For the scratch assay to assess cell migration, a monolayer of cells was seeded into 6-well plates until growing up at 95% confluence, and scratch-wounds were created with a 200 µL sterile plastic tip in the cell layer, then incubated in serum-free medium for 24 h. The wounds were monitored and imaged under a microscope (IX71, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) at 0 and 24 h in situ and the migration rate was calculated according to formula: migration rate = (0 h scratch width − 24 h scratch width)/0 h scratch width) × 100%.

## Transwell assay
For the transwell assay to assess cell invasion, Matrigel (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA) was plated to each well before the cells (2 × 104 cells) were resuspended in the upper compartment with serum-free culture medium and the complete medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower compartment for 24 h. The residual cells were wiped off with cotton swabs on the upper chamber surface of the filter. Cells that migrated the underside of well were rinsed with PBS, then fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, stained by 0.2% crystal violet and counted under microscopy (Olympus).

## Flow cytometry
Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 105/well for 24 h. Then the cells were harvested by centrifugation, then washed, stained with 5 μL of Annexin V- FITC/ PI (Beyotime) in dark, and analyzed by flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA).

## Detection of pyruvic acid, α-ketoglutaric acid, and copper content
Briefly, the cells and blood samples were centrifuged, then the obtained supernatant and serum were placed into 96-well plates, followed by incubating with reaction reagents according to the manufacturer’s protocol of pyruvate acid (Catl. No. A081, BioVision, Milpitas, CA, USA) and α-ketoglutaric acid assay kit (BC5420, Solarbio, Beijing, China). Meanwhile, inductively coupled plasma-Optical Emission Spectrometer (ICP-OES, NexION 300X, Beijing, China) was adopted to determine the copper ion (Cu2+) content in the guidance of manufacturer’s instructions.

## Western blot analysis
The total protein was extracted, harvested, quantified, and separated by SDS-PAGE, then transferred onto PVDF membranes. After sealing with 5% skimmed milk for 2 h, the membranes were incubated with specific primary antibodies (Abcam) as following: (FDX1, ab108257, 1:1000; DLAT, ab172617, 1:1000; PPARγ, ab178860, 1:2000; FABP5, ab255276,1:1000; BIRC5, ab76424, 1:1000; β-actin, 4967S, 1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology) at 4 °C overnight. After incubation with the secondary antibody (Beyotime), the signals were detected by imaging system (Tanon 5200, Shanghai, China) with β-actin as an internal reference gene.

## Tumor xenograft assay
A total of twelve female BALB/c nude mice (6–8-weeks old, weighing 18 ± 20 g) were acquired from SPF Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and randomly divided into LV-NC group and LV-BIRC5 group with six mice in each group. Then 2 × 106 stably transfected SK-Hep-1 cells were subcutaneously injected into the dorsal surface of the mice. Tumor weight was measured every week and the tumor volume was accessed according to the following formula: V = (length × width2)/2. After 4 weeks, the mice were euthanized through inhalation of isoflurane (Rayward, Shenzhen, China) for 2–3 min, then the tumors, tissues, and blood samples were harvested. The animal experiments were approved by Ethics Committee of Zhujiang Hospital of Southern Medical University (Approval No. LAEC-2024-005) in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

## Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining
The tissues were fixed in the 4% paraformaldehyde, next carried out paraffin embedding procedures, and then cut in slices. The slices were subsequently immersed in xylene and gradient alcohol solution. Next, the slices were dyed with hematoxylin solution, and counterstained in the eosin solution. Following the dehydration and transparency, the sections were sealed with neutral gum, and imaged under a microscope (Olympus).

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Briefly, the paraffin sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, blocked with 3% H2O2 and incubated with Ki67 antibody (ab15580, 1:1000, abcam) overnight at 4 °C followed by secondary antibody incubation. The slides were stained with DAB chromogen solution (zli-9018; zsbio, Beijing, China) and counterstained by hematoxylin. Finally, the sections were balatasealed by glycerin gelatin and photographed using microscopy (Olympus).

## Statistical analysis
All data analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software 8.0 (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA) with measurement data expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical difference between two sets of data was determined by Student's t-test, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed among multiple groups subsequently followed by Tukey's test. The p-value < 0.05 was considered as statistical difference.

## Results

## Identification of DEGs and functional enrichment analysis
This investigation used GSE50579 and GSE74656 datasets. A total of 766 DEGs were screened from the GSE50579 with 388 upregulated and 378 downregulated genes, while GSE74656 comprised 428 DEGs including 152 upregulated and 276 downregulated genes (Fig.  A). The GSEA results revealed three significant enrichment pathways among the DEGs in GSE50579 and GSE74656 dataset with two identical pathways including drug metabolism cytochrome P450 and retinol metabolism (Fig.  B). The distribution of top 15 significantly upregulated and downregulated DEGs between control and tumor samples in the two datasets was visualized by heatmaps, respectively (Fig.  C). At the intersection of these two datasets, 177 DEGs were screened, and by cross-analyzing with cuproptosis-related genes, 45 co-DEGs were eventually identified (Fig.  D).
 To figure out the functions of 45 co-DEGs, GO and KEGG analysis were conducted. The top enriched 10 GO terms met the criteria with the lowest p value were screened for each category of cell component, including condensed chromosome kinetochore, biological process such as mitotic nuclear division and chromosome segregation, and molecular function like monooxygenase activity. (Figure S1A-C). As for KEGG analysis, the top 10 pathways with the lowest p value using KEGG enrichment analysis were presented such as cell cycle and mineral absorption (Figure S1D).

## Construction of PPI network and identification of the hub genes
A construction of the PPI network using the STRING was generated from 45 co-DEGs (Figure S2A). A tightly connected protein cluster was obtained through MCODE plugin, and the 9 hub genes were distinguished, including BIRC5, TOP2A, CCNB1, CCNA2, CDK1, ESR1, CDKN3, TYMS and UBE2C (Figure S2B).
Then the PCA analysis was carried out with two principal components (PC1 and PC2) effectively explained 91.2%, suggesting these hub genes could significantly distinguish HCC from normal individuals (Fig.  B). The GO enrichment chord diagram was generated to better understand the relationship between hub genes and enrichment pathways (Fig.  C). The correlation heat map showed that there was a moderate to strong association among selected hub genes (Fig.  D). Besides, ridgeline plots of the 9 hub genes were plotted with relatively dense distribution for each hub gene (Fig.  E). BIRC5 was selected the core gene in this study, and BIRC5 expression was significantly upregulated in HCC through GEPIA2 online website (Fig.  A). The AUC of BIRC5 were 0.939 and 0.960 in two datasets, respectively (Fig.  F). Additionally, lower of BIRC5 expression exhibited better prognosis of overall survival in HCC (Fig.  G).
 

## Correlation between BIRC5 and immune cell infiltration, prognosis value in pan-cancer
The Timer tool was utilized to analyze the relationship between expression of BIRC5 and different types of immune cell infiltration in HCC. The results suggested that BIRC5 was positively correlated with the infiltration of CD8+ T cells and macrophages (Figure S3A–C). Then BIRC5 expression exhibited a significant correlation with immune score for 33 cancer types (Figure S3D). Next, significant upregulation of BIRC5 was observed in 34 tumors by calculating the difference of BIRC5 expression between normal and tumor samples (Figure S3E). Finally, the results of prognosis analysis revealed that high expression of BIRC5 had poor prognosis in 21 tumor types, and BIRC5 low expression was with poor prognosis in 1 tumor type (Figure S3F). The above findings demonstrated that BIRC5 was highly expressed in several cancer tissues and might participate in the process of immune regulation.

## Quantification of hub genes
To verify our bioinformatics analysis results, we evaluated the expression of 6 hub genes in L-O2 and human HCC lines (Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1). The results demonstrated that compared with L-O2 cells, the expressions of BIRC5, CCNA2, TYMS and UBE2C were increased, while ESR1 and CDK1 decreased in HCC cells (Fig.  ).
 

## BIRC5 knockdown suppresses proliferation, invasion, migration and promotes apoptosis of HCC cells
To assess the accurate role of BIRC5 in HCC, the Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1 cells were transfected with si-NC and si-BIRC5-1, -2, and -3 for knockdown BIRC5. The expression of BIRC5 was obviously downregulated in SK-Hep-1 and Hep-3B cells, and si-BIRC5-1 was selected for further experiments owing to transfection of si-BIRC5-1 in HCC cells with the lowest expression (Fig.  A, Supplementary Table 3). Compared with si-NC group, BIRC5 knockdown significantly suppressed the cell viability, and also inhibited ability of migration and invasion (Fig.  B–D). Subsequently, we found that the apoptotic cell rate in si-BIRC5 group had an obvious increase than that in si-NC group (Fig.  E). Taken together, these results suggested that silencing of BIRC5 significantly restrained viability, migration, invasion and promoted apoptosis of HCC cells.
 

## Knockdown of BIRC5 affects the cuproptosis in HCC cells
To further explore BIRC5 potential in cuproptosis regulation, we detected the content of pyruvic acid and α-ketoglutaric acid in SK-Hep-1 and Hep-3B cells, and the levels of pyruvic acid and α-ketoglutaric acid were significantly upregulated when the two HCC cells transfected with si-BIRC5 (Fig.  A). Furthermore, the cellular Cu2+ level was higher in BIRC5-silenced HCC cells than that in the si-NC group (Fig.  B) Subsequently, the cuproptosis markers including FDX1 and DLAT protein expression were determined. We observed that BIRC5 knockdown led to an increase of FDX1 and DLAT expression in Hep-3B and SK-Hep-1 cells (Fig.  C).
 

## BIRC5 positively regulates the PPAR pathway in HCC cells
We intended to further unravel the mechanism of BIRC5 and the molecules of PPAR signal pathway including PPAR-γ and FABPP5 were examined. The expression of PPAR-γ and FABPP5 were lower in BIRC5 siRNA groups than the control groups (Fig.  ), indicating BIRC5 may be involved in the regulation of expression of PPAR signaling molecules.
 

## BIRC5 knockdown inhibits tumor growth in vivo
To further evaluate effect of BIRC5 on tumor growth in vivo, we established a BIRC5 knockdown tumorigenesis model. The results of qRT-PCR and western blot showed that the mRNA and protein levels of BIRC5 were significantly reduced in the LV-BIRC5-1 and LV-BIRC5-2 groups compared to the LV-NC group (Fig.  A, ). We selected LV-BIRC5-1 for subsequent experiments. We found that the body weight was increased, and the mice with less tumor weight were presented in the LV-BIRC5 group (Fig.  C–E). The HE staining results demonstrated a decrease in cell density of LV-BIRC5 group (Fig.  F). In addition, LV-BIRC5 group displayed significantly decreased levels of Ki67 (Fig.  G). Furthermore, the pyruvic acid and α-ketoglutaric acid levels were elevated in the LV-BIRC5 group (Fig.  H). Additionally, FDX1 and DLAT protein expressions were upregulated, while the expression levels of PPAR-γ and FABPP5 were reduced in LV-BIRC5 groups (Fig.  I, ). Collectively, these data suggested knockdown of BIRC5 exerted suppressive effect on tumor growth via cuproptosis and PPAR pathway in vivo.
 

## Downregulation of BIRC5 restrains the malignant phenotypes of HCC cells via PPARγ pathway
To further determine whether BIRC5 restrained the malignant phenotypes of HCC cells via PPAR pathway, the PPARγ pathway activator, Oroxin A, was employed. Oroxin A treatment attenuated the inhibitory effect of BIRC5 silencing on cell viability, migration and invasion capacity of SK-Hep-1 cells compared to the si-BIRC5 group (Fig.  A–C). Besides, the apoptotic cell rate was lower in si-BIRC5 + Oroxin A group than that in the si-BIRC5 group (Fig.  D). In addition, Oroxin A treatment enhanced the effect of BIRC5 knockdown treated downregulation of PPAR-γ and FABPP5 (Fig.  E). The above findings indicated that downregulation of BIRC5 attenuated malignant phenotypes of HCC cells through inhibition of PPARγ pathway.
 

## Discussion
HCC, a high degree of malignancy with poor prognosis is manifested as complex molecular carcinogenesis [ , ]. Further understanding underlying molecular mechanisms of HCC and identification of predictive biomarkers can be leveraged to improve the progression of HCC. Herein, we identified 45 cuproptosis-related genes through the comprehensive bioinformatics methods between GSE50579 and GSE74656 dataset, and then extracted 9 hub genes by construction of PPI network. Additionally, we revealed that BIRC5 silencing might exert potential role as an anti-oncogene to regulate HCC progression via PPARγ pathway and cuproptosis.
Among screened hub genes, CDK1-PLK1/SGOL2/ANLN pathway contributes to a disordered cell division in cell cycle in HCC cells [ ]. CCNA2 is upregulated with a high diagnostic value for HCC, which may serve as a direct molecular indicator in cirrhosis progression to HCC [ ]. Highly expressed ESR1 can attenuate occurrence and development of HCC by upregulating metabolism of cobalamin-associated A, and participate in metabolic reprogramming [ ]. TYMS protein expression is higher in HBV-positive HCC tissues, and has associations with poor survival in patients [ ]. A significantly higher expression of UBE2C is discovered in stage 2 and stage 3 of HCC tissues [ ]. In this study, upregulation of BIRC5, CCNA2, TYMS, UBE2C and downregulation of ESR1 and CDK1 were observed in HCC cells, whereas the elaborate mechanisms behind these genes in HCC development are worthy being further validated.
BIRC5, a multifunctional gene is elevated in solid tumors and involved in malignant behaviors of tumor cells. Higher BIRC5 expression level is linked to worse overall survival in breast cancer, but better overall survival for lung squamous cell carcinoma and ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma [ ]. Depletion of BIRC5 represses melanoma cell proliferation and metastasis through regulation of the β-catenin/HIF-1α/VEGF/MMPs pathway [ ]. Upregulated BIRC5 with a high mortality rate has been found in lung adenocarcinoma patients, as well as BIRC5 silencing restrained proliferation and enhanced pyroptosis in lung adenocarcinoma cells [ ]. Downregulation of BIRC5 exhibits a suppressive role of proliferation and migration in prostate cancer cells [ ]. Our findings revealed that silencing of BIRC5 repressed malignant phenotypes, promoted apoptosis of SK-Hep-1 and Hep-3B cells in vitro. Furthermore, we demonstrated BIRC5 deficiency restrained tumorigenesis in vivo.
PPARs have an impact on cancer progress, and potentially act as a cancer therapy option through versatile strategies. NUSAP1 protein is able to facilitate tumor proliferation and metastasis through the AMPK/PPARγ pathway activation in of breast cancer cells [ ]. TGFBI treatment increased the malignancy of cholangiocarcinoma cells by stimulating the ITGB1 dependent PPARγ signaling pathway [ ]. Moreover, ACOX2 acts as a tumor suppressor role in reducing the proliferation and metastasis potential of HCC through PPARα pathway [ ]. In addition, the elevated FNDC5 expression is found in HCC cells contributing to promoting M2 phenotype macrophage polarization, which is achieved by affecting the PPARγ/NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway [ ]. Through interaction with PPARγ, C/EBPα regulates FOXC1 to further modulate tumorigenesis in HCC [ ]. Herein, we found BIRC5 knockdown was also accompanied by decreased PPAR-γ and FABPP5, which was key molecules in PPAR pathway. Our results also elucidated that treated with Oroxin A, a PPARγ-specific activator in BIRC5-silenced SK-Hep-1 cells attenuated the inhibitory effect of BIRC5 depletion on malignant phenotypes and pro-apoptotic, indicating that downregulation of BIRC5 is responsible for HCC progression via inhibition of PPARγ pathway.
Targeting cuproptosis is recognized as a prospective direction for treatment of HCC diseases [ ]. DLAT is substantially aberrant expressed in HCC tissues, and directly linked with clinical stage or grade, as well as DLAT expression deficiency can effectively impede cuproptosis [ ]. Cuproptosis-related gene LIPT1 has been demonstrated to promote malignant phenotypes of hepatoma cells [ ]. It is found that depletion of PDXK facilities cuproptosis in HCC [ ]. FDX1 expression enhances the HCC cells’ (HepG2 and Hep3B) sensitivity to Cu2+, and downregulation of FDX1 promotes migrated and invasive capacity of tumor cells [ ]. We linked our findings regarding BIRC5 silencing to cuproptosis and revealed that knockdown of BIRC5 resulted in an increase of pyruvic acid, α-ketoglutaric acid and related cuproptosis markers expression in vitro and in vivo.
However, this study still has several potential limitations. Firstly, we solely focused on cuproptosis, while we could not exclude whether BIRC5 affected other forms of regulated cell death such as pyroptosis, ferroptosis, and disulfidoptosis in HCC, which should deserve comprehensive researches. Secondly, the effect of BIRC5 silencing on HCC only was verified in vivo and in vitro but lacked clinical evaluation. Additionally, the correlation between cuproptosis and PPARγ pathway in HCC progression still require further exploration.
To summarize, 45 cuproptosis-related genes and 9 hub genes were extracted through the comprehensive bioinformatics methods, which may play pivotal roles in regulating HCC. Additionally, we elucidated that downregulation of BIRC5 exerted a suppressive role responsible for HCC progression via inhibition of PPARγ pathway and cuproptosis. Collectively, findings of this study offer profound insights into the functions of BIRC5, and BIRC5 deletion may represent a potential therapeutic avenue for treatment of HCC.
