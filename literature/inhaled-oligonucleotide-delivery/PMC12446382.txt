## Highlights
•The 3rd South Asian Melioidosis Congress- 2023 held in Bangladesh in October 2023 was conducted with the primary aim to raise awareness on melioidosis.
•Following the congress, 9 melioidosis cases were reported to Ibrahim Medical College from different clinical settings of the country, with different clinical manifestations.
•This indicates the constant vigilance of the clinicians and microbiologists alike regarding high risk patients.

Melioidosis is a fatal infectious disease caused by the gram-negative soil dwelling bacterium Burkholderia pseudomallei. Bangladesh is considered a definite country for melioidosis owing to the isolation of the organism both from clinical samples as well as soil. Only around 100 cases of melioidosis have been diagnosed sporadically in the country for the past 60 years till October 2023 and about 90 % of these cases were detected in BIRDEM General Hospital. A dilemma existed for researchers worldwide whether the disease is actually rare in this part of the continent or whether it is the limitation of our clinicians and microbiologists keeping the disease neglected due to a lack of clinical and laboratory suspicion, knowledge gap and occasionally lack of adequate laboratory facilities. Consequently, to overcome these obstacles, a group of dedicated researchers began conducting workshops, seminars and finally arranged the 3rd South Asian Melioidosis Congress-2023 to disseminate knowledge on the fatality of the disease and ways to detect and manage the cases among the physicians all over the country. The outstanding outcome of these melioidosis centered educational sessions was the isolation and identification of nine melioidosis cases from different clinical settings of the country, other than BIRDEM. We present the nine melioidosis cases with different clinical manifestations. Interestingly, 33.3 % patients were non-diabetic, although two of the nine patients had other chronic illness, while a young patient of 28 years had no known co-morbidities at all. Eight of the nine patients recovered after six months treatment, while one patient unfortunately expired. The case series is expected to raise vigilance among the concerned physicians and microbiologists of the country to keep the suspicion of melioidosis at the back of their mind in high risk patients, thus improving the chance of early diagnoses and management.

## Introduction
Melioidosis was first diagnosed by the scientists Alfred Whitmore and C. S. Krishnaswami in Rangoon in 1911, among a group of morphine addict patients ( ). The causative organism, Burkholderia pseudomallei is an aerobic, gram-negative, non-fermenting, non-spore forming bacillus, widely distributed in the rhizosphere, soil and water. The organism is acquired by inoculation, inhalation and ingestion, owing to which agricultural workers and rural population are considered high risk groups in endemic regions ( ).
Melioidosis is considered highly endemic in countries like northeast Thailand, Singapore, Malaysia and northern Australia ( ). In Indian sub-continent, researchers have determined Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, India and Myanmar to be definite countries for melioidosis, considering the organism has been isolated from both clinical and environmental samples ( ; ; ). However, the true burden of the disease in South Asia is still under shadows due to lack of diagnostic facilities, clinical awareness, poor resources as well as inadequate funding.
The scenario in Bangladesh till now has been the detection of around 100 sporadic clinical cases in the past 60 years ( ). It is interesting to note that most of these cases were diagnosed in BIRDEM General Hospital where clinicians and microbiologists are well acquainted with the disease ( ; ). For the past three years, different measures have been taken to educate clinicians, microbiologists and laboratory technologists in order to diagnose cases of melioidosis and detect the causative organism in the laboratory in tertiary care hospitals on their own. With that perspective in mind, a four day workshop on melioidosis was organized by Department of Microbiology, Ibrahim Medical College and BIRDEM General Hospital, Department of Microbiology and Immunology, Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujib Medical University, Bangladesh Society of Infectious and Tropical Diseases, Bangladesh Society of Medical Microbiologists along with United States Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (US CDC) in 2021 where 113 participants were trained on the basics of this fatal disease and the minimum diagnostic criteria required to follow for the laboratory detection of B. pseudomallei. The minimum diagnostic criteria for the detection of B. pseudomallei strongly suggests that any non-Pseudomonas species, oxidase-positive, gram-negative bacillus isolated from any clinical specimen from a patient in an endemic area should be suspected to be B. pseudomallei. Furthermore, any oxidase-positive gram-negative bacilli that are sensitive to amoxicillin/clavulanic acid but resistant to aminoglycosides (such as gentamicin), colistin, and polymyxin should be classified as B. pseudomallei based on the antibiogram ( ). In 2023, the 3rd South Asian Melioidosis Congress- 2023, held in Bangladesh focused to create a platform for participants to share their knowledge, exchange views and ideas, gather experience and come out with vital recommendations needed to unmask the melioidosis havoc. Very successfully, following the congress, nine cases of melioidosis have been diagnosed in different clinical settings in the past nine months (November 2023- July 2024).
In perspective of Microbiology laboratory facilities of the country, any clinical sample brought to the lab for culture/sensitivity are inoculated in blood agar media and MacConkey agar media, considering culture to be the gold standard diagnostic test for melioidosis diagnosis. Blood culture is performed in automated culture systems (BD BACTEC Blood Culture System, USA). Culture of a gram-negative bacillus is suspected to be B. pseudomallei if it shows distinctive mauve coloured colony, that is oxidase positive and resistant to aminoglycosides and colistin ( ). The isolate is further confirmed by latex agglutination test (LAT) and polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The LAT utilizes a monoclonal antibody that interacts with exopolysaccharide of B. pseudomallei ( ). DNA was extracted from the latex confirmed colonies by boiling method and probe based real time PCR was conducted targeting the conserved gene TTS1 for further confirmation of isolates ( ).
Here, we discuss these nine melioidosis cases that have been diagnosed in tertiary care hospitals of the country other than BIRDEM, which reflects the fruition of the incessant planning and workshops to educate physicians all over the country on the importance of diagnosis and management of this neglected disease and thus preventing death from melioidosis.
Case-1: A 45-year-old diabetic male, farmer by occupation and residing in Narsingdi district, was admitted in December 2023, to the National Institute of Cardiovascular Diseases with high grade fever for four months. The patient also experienced dyspnea and coughing for a month, chest discomfort for the same period, and edema in both legs for ten days. A coronary angiogram along with stenting was performed two months ago in the same institute. General examination of the patient revealed mild anemia, dyspnea, high-grade fever, and bilateral leg edema. His total leukocyte count was 15.50/cumm, neutrophil-82 %, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)-115 mm in the first hour, fasting blood glucose (FBS)-11.7 mmol/L, random blood sugar (RBS)-13.9 mmol/L and serum creatinine-1.3 mg/dl. An echocardiography report demonstrated mild left ventricular systolic failure, with an ejection fraction 44 %. Blood culture yielded growth of gram-negative and oxidase positive bacilli, B. pseudomallei. The isolate was identified by colony morphology and biochemical tests followed by confirmation with monoclonal antibody based latex agglutination test for B. pseudomallei (Melioidosis Research Center, Khon Kaen, Thailand) and PCR. A single set of oligonucleotide primer (forward primer- 5`-CGTCTCTATACTGTCGAGCAATCG-3` and reverse primer 5` -CGTGCACACCGGTCAGTACTC-3) has been selected targeting the TTS1 gene. PCR parameters were used as described by , to generate a 115 base pair (bp) PCR product. The final PCR amplicon was observed in ethidium bromide stained 2 % agarose gel ( ). After successful treatment with standard antibiotic regimen (I/V meropenem for two weeks followed by co-amoxiclav for five months) of melioidosis, the patient’s condition was improved at follow-up visit after six months.
Case-2: A 44-year-old diabetic male, who runs a fruit selling business from a remote forest at Fatikchhari, Chittagong, complained of fever for three months, which was later associated with cough and chest pain. Fever was intermittent in nature with the highest recorded temperature being 104⁰F. Cough was initially dry but gradually became productive accompanied with chest pain. After 3 months, he developed multiple swellings on both sides of his forehead. Swellings were painful and cystic in consistency. He was admitted to Dhaka Medical College Hospital in January 2024, and treated for multiple abscesses by surgical intervention and injectable empiric antibiotics. After some relevant investigations, he was diagnosed with melioidosis and treated accordingly. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of the brain was suggestive of linear collection in right temporo-parietal region of scalp. Complete blood count revealed neutrophilia (85 %). Patient’s RBS and HbA1c were 23.70 mmol/L and 13.7 % respectively. Blood culture yielded B. pseudomallei. The patient was treated with intravenous meropenem for two weeks followed by oral amoxicillin+clavulanic acid for three months and his conditions were found improved at three and again at six months follow-up.
Case 3: A young expatriate aged 28 years hailing from Pabna district was admitted to Dhaka Medical College Hospital in January 2024 with fever and cough for one month. The patient also complained of breathlessness for 10 days and gave history of right lower limb fracture due to incidental cause. The patient resided in Malaysia for occupational purpose for the past 5 years. General examination featured no significant abnormality except raised temperature of 102⁰F. On the contrary, abdominal examination revealed hepatosplenomegaly and shifting dullness. His ESR was 109 mm in first hour and HbA1c was 7.4 %. Ultrasonography of whole abdomen showed two space occupying lesions in liver (larger one measuring 1.6 cm×1.7 cm) along with two space occupying lesions in spleen (larger one measuring 1.8 cm×1.6 cm) with mild splenomegaly. Chest X-ray demonstrated no abnormal findings. Sputum culture yielded B. pseudomallei. Following treatment with antibiotic regimen (I/V meropenem and oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) for two weeks as per sensitivity report, the patient was transferred to Orthopedics hospital for further management.He was advised to continue oral antibiotic for six months. The patient’s health condition is found to be improved at 6 months’ follow-up.
Case 4: In May 2024, a 54-year-old male from Habiganj district, who is a farmer by profession complained of fever for six months. Fever was intermittent in nature with the highest recorded temperature being 101⁰F. Patient is a diagnosed case of uncontrolled diabetes mellitus for 10 years, chronic kidney disease (CKD-stage IV) and hypertension. Along the course of fever, patient developed disorientation. He was admitted to a tertiary care hospital in Dhaka city. On examination, patient was severely anemic with chemosis present on both eyes. Glasgow Coma Scale score was E4M3V4 (eye response; motor response; verbal response) and plantar reflex was bilateral equivocal. The patient was diagnosed as a case of uremic encephalopathy due to Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) on CKD (stage IV) with severe anemia and was treated accordingly. His overall condition improved but the fever did not subside. Eventually his blood culture yielded growth of B. pseudomallei. As per his complete blood count report, Hb was 6.6 g/dl, ESR was 140 mm in first hour and total count of WBC was 10,300/cumm. Serum creatinine and RBS were 9.7 mg/dl and 9.5 mmol/L respectively. Ultrasonography of whole abdomen revealed bilateral renal parenchymal disease, left renal cortical cyst and suggestive of cystitis. His echocardiography was normal. Following intensive therapy with injectable meropenem, oral regimen with amoxicillin+clavulanic acid was continued for 6 months as continuation therapy. The patient has been followed up and although the patient showed signs of improvement, renal complications still persist.
Case-5: Just about two days following the diagnosis of case-4, another female hypertensive patient aged 77 years, hailing from Mymensingh district, was admitted to the same tertiary care hospital with the complaints of fever for two weeks and sudden syncopal attack. The fever was continuous in nature with highest recorded temperature being 101⁰F. The patient is a diagnosed case of AKI on CKD for the past eight years and was treated accordingly. Her investigation profile revealed, Hb-9.9 g/dl, ESR-39 mm in first hour, HbA1c-5.6 %, total count of WBC-2000/cumm, total platelet count-85,000/cumm and serum creatinine was 2.3 mg/dl. Incidentally, Dengue NS1 was found positive for this patient. In addition, blood culture revealed growth of B. pseudomallei. Intensive treatment with injectable meropenem started adjusting the dose accordingly owing to compromised renal function followed by oral amoxicillin+clavulanic acid in the continuation phase for 6 months. The patient’s symptoms have improved at 6 months’ follow-up.
Case-6: A hypertensive female housewife aged 62 years from Maulvibazar district, was admitted to a private hospital in Sylhet with the symptoms of fever, cough and dyspnea for a week. On admission her temperature was 103⁰F. Chest examination revealed crepitations and rhonchi. The patient was diagnosed as a case of diffuse parenchymal lung disease, diffuse cutaneous systemic sclerosis along with pulmonary hypertension (pulmonary artery systolic pressure-44 mmHg and ejection fraction-54 %) and was shifted to Intensive Care Unit. Soon she developed severe sepsis and blood culture yielded B. pseudomallei. Although, injectable meropenem was started, the patient unfortunately expired two days following isolation of the organism.
Case-7: In June 2024, a diabetic and hypertensive female housewife aged 59 years old was admitted to a private hospital in Dhaka. with the complaints of fever along with pain and redness in the right foot for 15 days. She gave a history of accidental fall from wheel chair about 15 days back, leading to the lacerated wound in her right foot. She was diagnosed of CKD and was on dialysis. Pus from the wound infection yielded B. pseudomallei. She received intravenous ceftazidime for one month followed by oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for three months with a successful recovery.
Case-8: A 48-year-old diabetic male, residing in Dhaka complained of fever for one month along with chest pain and dyspnea. Fever was intermittent with highest recorded temperature 102⁰F. Complete blood count shows Hb-11.5 g/dl, ESR-64 mm in first hour and total leukocyte count-10,700/cumm. RBS was 10.5 mmol/L while serum creatinine was 0.68 mg/dl. Blood culture revealed growth of B. pseudomallei resistant to aminoglycosides and colistin. Intensive treatment began with injectable meropenem for two weeks and followed by oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 3 months. He is doing well on follow-up.
Case-9: During the end of July 2024, a businessman, residing in Dhaka, aged 58 years was admitted to a private hospital in Dhaka with a history of fever for 21 days. Fever was associated with cough. The patient gave a travel history of visiting Cox’s Bazar one week before the onset of fever. The patient has diabetes. The complete blood count report revealed Hb-13.8 g/dl, ESR-87 mm in first hour and total leukocyte count-11,500/cumm. The patient’s HbA1c measured 10.5 %, while serum creatinine was 0.9 mg/dl. Chest X-Ray showed consolidation. Blood culture yielded B. pseudomallei. The patient is being treated with injectable meropenem for 10 days and then put on oral amoxicillin+clavulanic acid for three months. At present, the patient has recovered on follow-up.

## Discussion
Burkholderia pseudomallei is a highly pathogenic soil dwelling organism, that is commonly found during the flooding of rice paddy fields in the beginning of monsoon ( ). Bangladesh, being a highly populous agriculture-based country, is suspected to be a hub of the disease, although under diagnosed due to a lack of awareness and knowledge among clinicians and microbiologists alike.
The first case of melioidosis from the country was diagnosed in a British sailor in 1961, whose ship was stuck in a paddy field for 3 months in Chittagong following a cyclone ( ). From 1961 till 2023, only about 100 melioidosis cases have been diagnosed and out of these, 86 of the isolates have been identified in BIRDEM General Hospital, where the clinicians and microbiologists are well aware about the fatality of the disease ( ; ). Unfortunately, it was a drawback for the researchers in the country that although infected patients arrived from all over the country, reflecting the source of the organism to be widespread, the infection was being detected in one particular diabetic hospital only, situated in the capital. In an article published by , different measures were mentioned to raise awareness concerning melioidosis in Bangladesh ( ). The online workshop arranged in 2021 where International panelists such as Prof. David A. B. Dance and Prof. Chiranjay Mukhopadhyay attended to share valuable insights and to answer queries on the based on their years of experience on melioidosis, have helped created awareness among clinicians and microbiologists.
The 3rd South Asian Melioidosis Congress-2023 (3rd SAMC-2023) was held in Bangladesh in October 2023 ensured the presence of researchers and clinicians from different parts of the world engaged in expansive discussions regarding the most recent diagnostic innovations, research trends, practical challenges encountered and solutions adopted in various thematic areas of melioidosis. Very interestingly, the doctors and microbiologists who reported these nine melioidosis cases were present in the 3rd SAMC-2023, which reflects the increased rate of melioidosis case detection.
A total of nine melioidosis cases were referred to the Department of Microbiology, Ibrahim Medical College from October 2023 to July 2024, that were confirmed by latex agglutination test and PCR. However, there were more suspected cases that were also notified but owing to lack of detailed history and confirmed diagnoses or treatment summary, the cases could not be included in this case series.
In the nine cases reported, six were male and three were female. Among them, 33.3 % of the patients were involved in farming which increases the chance of acquiring the organism through soil exposure. Previously, 25 % of melioidosis patients reported in Bangladesh were agricultural workers ( ). One of the patients was an overseas construction worker from Malaysia who was deployed back to Bangladesh due to chronic fever for a month. All patients reported with febrile illness, along with abscess in three out of nine cases. Barai et al., has also reported that previously in Bangladesh, out of 68 melioidosis patients, 37 cases presented with fever and abscess ( ). Diabetes mellitus is the commonest risk factor associated with melioidosis ( ). While 88.2 % of melioidosis patients from Bangladesh were reported to be diabetic previously, our study found 33.3 % of the patients to be non-diabetic ( ). Three of these patients have chronic kidney disease and one had diffuse parenchymal lung disease as underlying immunocompromised conditions that may have activated melioidosis. However, no evidence of a chronic underlying condition was traced in the 28-year-old overseas construction worker. In a retrospective study conducted in Malaysia with 246 melioidosis cases, 63 % of the patients were found to be without an underlying chronic illness ( ). B. pseudomallei was isolated from blood in seven out of nine cases, and rest two isolates were detected from sputum and pus respectively ( ). In Bangladesh, the treatment was quite varied amongst the hospitals before ( ), however during the workshop we tried to advocate the recent consensus guideline ( ; ). This has been well reflected in managing all the cases across the centres. Eight out of nine patients were treated with injectable meropenem in the intensive phase, while five of the patients were treated with co-amoxiclav instead of co-trimoxazole in the eradication phase. According to Revision of the 2015 Darwin melioidosis treatment guideline ( ), co-trimoxazole is the drug of choice in eradication therapy unless contraindicated due to allergy or renal failure. In two of the five case, co-amoxiclav was prescribed as the patients are suffering from chronic kidney disease, while in the rest three cases, two were co-trimoxazole resistant by disc diffusion method (zone diameter <17 mm according to ). The rest one case (Case number 3), the concerned clinician reported the patient to have sulphur allergy, for which co-trimoxazole was avoided. 
Most of the nine cases have been reported from the north and northeastern region of the country ( ). This is in accordance with earlier detection of cases from these regions supposedly due to soil textures, rainfall patterns and seasonal growth of paddy in these areas. It was presumed that the lack of cases in south-western regions of Bangladesh could be due to a lack of diagnosis and/or the underreporting of cases ( ). However, researchers are now trying to figure out the soil patterns of these particular regions of the country, where due to extreme salinity and high pH, growth of B. pseudomallei may be suppressed in the soil, hence accounting for the lack of cases.

The researchers in Ibrahim Medical College and BIRDEM General Hospital are relentlessly working on case detection of melioidosis all over the country as well as detection of the causative agent, B. pseudomallei from environmental samples in order to figure out the true burden of this neglected tropical disease. In addition, a website will be developed recently to track down each case of melioidosis found in the country so that the clinician/microbiologist in charge of the patient can themselves provide the input of the melioidosis patient from their own district hospital. In this way, it will be easier to keep record of annual detection of melioidosis cases so that no single case will be missed or misdiagnosed and at the same time follow-up of all cases will be possible to ensure reduction of mortality rate.

## Conclusion
Till now, melioidosis was considered an extremely neglected and under recognized disease in Bangladesh. Years of collaborative work with national and international experts, workshops, seminars and congress have now commenced to unmask the true burden of the disease in this country. The national policy makers in healthcare system should take further steps to include melioidosis as a notifiable disease in order to create awareness among general population and thus reduce adverse outcomes.
