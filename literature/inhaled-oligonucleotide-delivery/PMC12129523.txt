BPIFB1 is a prognostic biomarker and mediated collagen synthesis in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

## Abstract
It has been demonstrated that bactericidal/permeability-increasing-fold-containing family B member 1 (BPIFB1) is highly expressed in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and is linked to a dismal prognosis; nevertheless, its molecular role and connection to lung function in IPF are still unknown. The Gene Expression Omnibus database provided the data. Functional enrichment analyses, Kaplan–Meier, Cox regression, and the Pearson correlation coefficient were applied. Studies and single-cell ribonucleic acid sequencing research verified the impact of BPIFB1 on IPF. BPIFB1 was highly expressed in differentiating ciliated cells and MUC5B + cells, and single-cell sequencing and transcriptome analysis revealed that it was up-regulated in the lungs of IPF; however, blood BPIFB1 expression was similar in the control group and IPF. In addition, we identified 24 genes that interact with BPIFB1 and the top 5 transcription factors that target these genes as reported by the hTFtarget database. Clinical samples show that in the IPF, BPIFB1 is inversely correlated with lung function. According to bioinformatics analysis, elevated levels of BPIFB1 expression might be correlated with lung fibroblast differentiation and collagen synthesis. BPIFB1 may be a useful indicator of the prognosis and severity of the IPF. BPIFB1 may be associated with lung fibroblast differentiation and collagen production.
Keywords: biomarker, BPIFB1, collagen, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, prognosis

## 1. Introduction
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a long-term, worsening, and fibrotic lung disease with an unclear pathogenesis.[ , ] A variety of exogenous and endogenous factors, such as autoimmune diseases, viral infections, cigarette smoke, inhalation of toxic substances, and free radicals, can lead to damage in alveolar epithelial cells and abnormal activation of immune cells (neutrophils, alveolar macrophages, lymphocytes, and eosinophils) and fibroblasts.[ ] These pulmonary cells secrete multiple pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic factors, thereby accelerating the epithelial-mesenchymal transition process. This promotes the transformation of alveolar epithelial cells into pulmonary fibroblasts, activates quiescent fibroblasts, and enhances the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts.[ ] Myofibroblasts further release large amounts of collagen and extracellular matrix (ECM), leading to excessive fibroblast proliferation and ultimately accelerating the progression of pulmonary fibrosis.[ ] The role of fibroblasts in IPF has been studied extensively, and these cells are considered to be responsible for excessive collagen and other extracellular matrix synthesis and deposition that occur in lung fibrosis; therefore, these cells play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.[ , ] The process engages multiple mechanisms. Some studies showed that calpain has been identified as a mediator in the production of Collagen I by enhancing the synthesis and activation of transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) in pulmonary fibrosis induced by bleomycin.[ ] In addition, in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis models, the NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in macrophages enhances collagen production and the advancement of pulmonary fibrosis.[ ] The complex disease mechanisms contributing to the progression of IPF mean effective treatments to cure such disorders are lacking. Patients with IPF have an extremely dismal prognosis with a 5-year survival rate of <40% and typically pass away within 2 to 3 years.[ ] Consequently, finding reliable biomarkers is essential for the early diagnosis of patients with a dismal prognosis.
Respiratory system components, including the pleura, nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, and lungs, have a direct connection to the external environment. When air enters the respiratory system, toxic substances and infectious microorganisms have the potential to cause respiratory disease. The first defense the body has against dangerous pathogens is innate immunity. Due to its structural similarity to the lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-binding protein and the bactericidal/permeability-increasing (BPI) protein, 2 innate immune components that identify and react to gram-negative bacteria, BPI-fold-containing family B member 1 (BPIFB1) potentially assists in innate immunity.[ ] Existing fibrotic responses are coordinated by innate and adaptive immune processes, according to studies.[ , ] BPIFB1 is abnormally expressed in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, gastric cancer, and other tumor tissues, where it regulates chronic infection and inflammation, suggesting its potential critical role in tumor development. Additionally, due to the structural similarity between BPIFB1 and LPS-binding proteins, BPIFB1 has a well-established function in sensing and responding to gram-negative bacteria. Consequently, it may modulate cystic fibrosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma, and other respiratory disorders.[ ] Herrera JA et al[ ] interrogated the fibrotic honeycomb airway cells and fibrotic uninvolved airway cells (distant from honeycomb airways and morphologically intact) in specimens from 10 patients with usual interstitial pneumonia. They found that BPIFB1 was the most significantly increased secretome-associated protein in usual interstitial pneumonia.[ ] Analysis of lung tissue from patients with IPF using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)[ ] indicates that BPIFB1 is predominantly and significantly expressed in the MUC5B-positive cell population. Gain-of-function MUC5B polymorphisms are among the highest risk factors associated with pulmonary fibrosis.[ – ] Sesé L[ ] showed in the study that air pollution and MUC5B genotype have an important impact on survival in patients with IPF. According to Zhang Q, MUC5B overexpression in honeycomb cysts and terminal bronchi causes defensive host dysfunction in the distal airway, which may contribute to the onset of IPF.[ ] In IPF lung tissue, BPIFB1 is one of the top 3 significantly increased genes, according to research by Li N et al.[ ] She did not, however, perform any experimental validation of the BPIFB1 function in an IPF or bioinformatics study. Further research is needed to determine how BPIFB1 affects the prognosis and lung function of patients with IPF.
Therefore, in this investigation, we evaluated BPIFB1 expression in IPF lung tissue and lung fibroblasts using a publicly available dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and explored the mechanism of BPIFB1 in IPF through bioinformatics analysis and experiments.

## 2. Materials and methods

## 2.1. Dataset processing
The workflow of our research is illustrated in Figure . As per the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), the following 13 datasets were chosen: 8 datasets (GSE32537, GSE47460–GPL14550, GSE47460–GPL6480, GSE53845, GSE110147, GSE150910, GSE48149, GSE10667) were derived from lung tissue samples; 4 datasets (GSE27957, GSE93606, GSE28042, GSE38958) were obtained from blood samples; and 1 single-cell sequencing dataset (GSE135893) was obtained from lung tissue samples. The “Affy” R software application was utilized to standardize matrix data.
 The clinical characteristics of each dataset are detailed in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P97. From GSE38958 and GSE93606 (blood), the percent predicted diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO% predicted) and the percent predicted forced vital capacity (FVC% predicted) were extracted. The lung tissue GSE32537 dataset was used to extract the St. George Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) scores, FVC% predicted, and DLCO% predicted. The predicted FVC%, FEV1%, and DLCO% were determined from the lung tissue dataset GSE47460. Transplant-free survival (TFS) was retrieved from the GSE28042 and GSE27957 datasets (blood). From the time of blood collection until (1) disease advancement, which was defined as a predicted reduction in FVC% of 10% or higher within 6 months; (2) mortality; or (3) censoring at the last contact, patients with IPF were monitored for progression-free survival (PFS). The GSE93606 served as the source for the PFS.
The R package “stats” (v.4.1.3, Pearson correlation analysis) was used to use these datasets and determine the association between lung function and BPIFB1.

## 2.2. Patients with IPF and healthy individuals
The Medical Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu Medical College revised and approved (No.: 2021CYFYIRB-BA-32-01) all procedures conducted in this study. All participants provided written informed consent. Transbronchial lung cryo-biopsy was used to obtain lung tissue from 20 patients (10 females and 10 males, ages 52–70) and 17 patients (7 females and 10 males, ages 55–65) who underwent pulmonary nodulectomy at the Chengdu Medical College’s First Affiliated Hospital’s Department of Respiratory and Thoracic Surgery between January 2021 and December 2022. For RNA extraction later on, some lung tissue was quickly stored in liquid nitrogen, while some lung tissue was preserved in 10% formalin for histological and immunohistochemical analyses. Following the 2022 international IPF guidelines for the diagnosis of interstitial pneumonia (IPF), patients who exhibit characteristics that are prominent on high-resolution computed tomography images or potentially represent a common form of IPF are eligible to be evaluated for IPF. Substance intoxication, occupational or family environmental exposures, and connective tissue illnesses with autoimmune features are excluded as other recognized etiologies of interstitial lung disease (ILD).

## 2.3. The determination of transcription factors (TFs) and partners associated with BPIFB1
The STRING database was used to generate the protein–protein interaction. The interaction between BPIFB1 and genes derived from STRING was validated using the GENEMANIA database, which is comprised of 100 publicly accessible gene expression databases. Target gene expression is influenced by TFs, which are crucial regulators. In this study. The TFs that target BPIFB1 and its companions were found using the hTFtarget database.

## 2.4. Analysis of scRNA-seq data
The GSE135893 dataset (lung) was computationally analyzed using the “Seurat” R package. Based on the “Seurat” R package, quality control was conducted on the dataset (percentage of mitochondrial gene < 20%, percentage of erythrocyte gene < 0.1%, percentage of ribosomal gene < 3%, and 200 < number of feature RNA < 5000). Principal component analysis was conducted using the Seurat RunPCA tool. Using the Seurat NormalizeData function, the scRNA-seq data were normalized. Using the first 30 principal components and the Seurat RunUMAP and FindClusters functions, the Louvain clusters and uniform manifold approximation and projection for dimension reduction were calculated. A resolution of 0.8 was established. The cluster markers were identified using the CellMarker and Seurat FindAllMarkers functions, with cell types being determined based on the markers of each cluster level following the datasets’ original article. The gene expression and distribution were graphically represented using the Seurat DotPlot, VlnPlot, and FeaturePlot functions, respectively.

## 2.5. In patients with IPF, the prognostic value of BPIFB1
Kaplan–Meier analysis and log-rank test were performed using the R software package “survival” to compare the differences in TFS and PFS between different groups. Based on the “surv_cutpoint” function of the ‘survminer’ R package, the optimal gene expression threshold for survival analysis was established. As per the “survivalROC” R report, an ROC curve was constructed to assess the predictive capability of BPIFB1.

## 2.6. Functional analysis
Moreover, the groups with high and low BPIFB1 expression underwent gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). Reactome gene sets were utilized to establish a correlation between BPIFB1 expression and biological function. A P-value < .05 and a false discovery rate < 0.25 were used to determine statistical significance for a gene set. According to the “GSVA” R package, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was used to calculate the infiltration score of 19 immune cells and the activity of 15 immune-related pathways (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P98).

## 2.7. Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and RNA extraction
TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, MA) was used to isolate total RNA from cells and tissues. To conduct reverse transcription, the PrimeScript RT Master Mix Kit (Takara, Dalian, China) was utilized. Utilizing a TB Green PreMix Ex Taq Kit (Takara, Dalian, China), messenger RNA (mRNA) levels were quantified. Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P99 has a list of primer sequences. Using the 2-△△Ct technique, the relative mRNA expression was standardized to the levels of glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase mRNA.

## 2.8. Reagents
The R&D system (Minneapolis, MN) supplied the recombinant TGF-β1. Sigma–Aldrich (Sigma–Aldrich, St Louis, MO) supplied mouse monoclonal antibodies targeting human α-SMA and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase, as well as a rabbit antibody specific to fibronectin (FN). Abcam (Cambridge, UK) supplied the rabbit monoclonal antibodies to Collagen I and Collagen III, as well as an antibody to BPIFB1.

## 2.9. Immunohistochemistry
Lung tissue sections were subjected to dewaxing, followed by rehydration using gradient ethanol. We extracted the antigen using a solution of ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid and microwave irradiation for 20 minutes. We inhibited endogenous peroxidase activity in the samples by treating them with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 minutes without light. The sections were incubated with 5% goat serum for 20 minutes to prevent nonspecific binding. We then incubated the sections overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (BPIFB1, Abcam, China). The following day, we incubated the tissue slices for the specified amount of time with a secondary antibody (goat anti-rabbit IgG, kit-5030, MXB Biotechnology). Then, 3,3′-diaminobenzidine staining was conducted. We subsequently subjected the sections to permeabilization, dehydration, and staining before microscopy. Ten randomly chosen fields of view from each section were assessed, accounting for the staining intensity and cell count, to determine the H score. H score = (proportion of cells stained weakly × 1) plus (proportion of cells stained moderately × 2) plus (proportion of cells stained strongly × 3).

## 2.10. Cell culture
From the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai), HLF cells, which are a human lung fibroblast cell line, were acquired. Following the addition of 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and antibiotics, DMEM was sterilized at 37 °C in 5% CO2/95% air. Phosphate-buffered saline was used twice to rinse the cells to eliminate FBS before they were cultured for a full day without serum to promote myofibroblast differentiation using TGF-β1.

## 2.11. Immunoblot analysis
After rinsing the cells in phosphate-buffered saline, they were lysed with CelLytic M (Sigma–Aldrich) supplemented with Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail and Protease Inhibitor Cocktail Complete (Roche, Germany). After a 15-minute incubation time at room temperature, the cell debris was removed by centrifugation, and the amounts were determined using a Thermo BCA protein assay kit. Cell extracts were electrophoresed under reducing conditions on a sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel before being transferred to a Polyvinylidene Fluoride membrane. After being treated with secondary antibodies conjugated with horseradish peroxidase, the membrane was generated with the GE Healthcare ECL Prime System. With the aid of Image J software (NIH, Bethesda, MD), signal intensities were assessed.

## 2.12. In vitro small interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection
BPIFB1 siRNA duplex (sense, 5`-GCACAGAGAUUUCAGUCUAGC-3`; antisense, 5`-UAGACUGAAAUCUCUGUGCGG-3`) was designed and purchased from Shanghai Sangon Co., Ltd. Shanghai Sangon Co., Ltd. also supplied non-targeting control siRNA. RNAiMax Lipofectamine transfection solution (Invitrogen) was utilized to transfect siRNA. HLF cells were cultured at a density of 4 × 104 cells/mL in multi-well plates containing Opti-MEM supplemented with 5% FBS. After 24 hours, cells were transfected with 20 nM of either BPIFB1 siRNA or control siRNA. Cells were cultured in Opti-MEM with or without 0.5 ng/mL of TGF-1 after being rinsed to remove FBS after 24 hours. After TGF-β1 treatment, samples were collected 24 hours and 48 hours later, respectively, for quantitative PCR and immunoblot analysis. The experimental design involved the division of the cells into 4 distinct groups: control siRNA, control siRNA plus TGF-β1, BPIFB1 siRNA, and BPIFB1 siRNA plus TGF-β1.

## 2.13. Cell viability analysis
After TGF-β1 treatment, samples were collected 24 hours, 48 hours, and 72 hours later, respectively, for cell viability analysis.To facilitate adhesion, the 4 groups of HLFs were inoculated at a density of 1 × 104 cells/well in 96-well plates (Corning, USA) and incubated for overnights. Then, the cell culture medium was substituted. A solution was prepared by mixing 10 µL of MTS reagent (Promega, USA) with 100 μL of culture medium and incubating the cells at 37 ℃ for 2 hours, per the manufacturer’s guidelines. With a 96-well plate reader, the absorbance in the culture medium’s supernatant was determined at an optical depth of 490 nm.

## 2.14. Cell transwell migration assay
In the DMEM medium, 5 × 104 cells/mL of each of the 4 HLF groups were resuspended. The lower chamber was then filled with 1 µL of completed DMEM medium, while the upper chamber was filled with 200 µL of cell suspension. The cells were coated with 4% paraformaldehyde and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet after being cultured for 24 hours. A microscopic examination and tally of the transferred cells were performed.

## 2.15. Statistical analysis
For statistical analysis, R soft (Version 4.1.3) and SPSS Statistics 25 (IBM SPSS) were utilized. For comparing continuous variables, one-way analysis of variance or Student t tests were utilized. GraphPad Prism 8 was used to visualize several statistical analyses. There was a bilateral test. Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine the relationship between DLCO% predicted and the mRNA expression of BPIFB1. Statistical significance was attained when the P-value was < .05.

## 3. Results

## 3.1. BPIFB1 expression in IPF
BPIFB1 is mostly expressed in the human stomach, salivary glands, and lung tissue, according to the National Center of Biotechnology Information Database (Figure S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P89, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/?term=BPIFB1). Twelve datasets were selected from the GEO database to investigate the expression of BPIFB1 in IPF. The results showed that patients with IPF had significantly higher levels of BPIFB1 expression in their lung tissue. However, in the remaining data, there was no difference in BPIFB1 expression between the TFS and non-TFS groups, the control and IPF groups, or between BPIFB1 expression in blood and lung tissue (Fig. [lung tissue], Figure S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P90 [blood]).
 

## 3.2. BPIFB1 partners and TFs
Ten protein-coding genes that interact with BPIFB1 have been found using the STRING database: BPIFB6, BPIFB3, BPIFA1, BPIFA2, BPI, CAMP, PRB2, BPIFA3, and KCNRG (Fig. A). The 2 most notable co-expression genes of BPIFB1 were IKBIP and BPIFA1, based on the GENEMANIA database (Fig. B). Twenty-four genes are interacting with BPIFB1. The first 5 TFs (GLIS1, ATF3, JMJD1C, USF1, and ZNF382) of BPIFB1 and the 24 associated genes were selected for additional research based on the hTFtarget database (Fig. C). The GO and KEGG analyses revealed a substantial relationship between antimicrobial humoral response, innate immune response, innate immune in the mucosa, innate immune system, immune system, and antimicrobial peptides with the first 5 TFs, BPIFB1, and the 24 interacting genes (Figure S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P91).
 

## 3.3. The scRNA-seq analysis
It was decided to perform the scRNA-seq analysis (GSE135893 [IPF = 12, control = 10]). Figure A–C shows that MUC5AC + high, MUC5B+, and differentiated ciliated cells were the main and most abundant cell types that expressed BPIFB1. Moreover, BPIFB1 levels in MUC5B + cells from patients with IPF were significantly higher (Fig. C). The expression of the first 5 TFs and the 24 genes that interact is shown in Figure B. Because GSE135893 did not include annotations for BPIFA3, BPIFC, BPIFB3, and BPIFB6, they were not displayed.
 

## 3.4. Correlation between BPIFB1 expression level and lung function or SGRQ scores
In GSE47460–GPL14550, BPIFB1 levels were substantially inversely correlated with FEV1% predicted, DLCO% predicted, and FVC% predicted (Table ). However, the correlation was weak, especially between BPIFB1 expression level and FVC% predicted and FEV1% predicted (<0.3) (Figure S4, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P92). Regretfully, there was no discernible correlation found between lung function and BPIFB1 expression in GSE93606, GSE32537, GSE38958, and GSE47460–GPL6480. In addition, there was no significant correlation between SGRQ scores and BPIFB1 expression (see Figure S5A, S5B, S5C, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P93 and Table ). We validated this measure because DLCO% predicted is one of the best indicators of survival in individuals with IPF who have severe disease.[ ] However, our clinical samples illustrated an inverse correlation between BPIFB1 mRNA levels and DLCO% predicted in patients with IPF (cor = −0.535, P = .027) (Figure S6, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P94).
 

## 3.5. Prognostic correlative analysis
Based on the optimal threshold for gene expression, which is described in detail in the techniques section, patients with IPF were divided into 2 groups. As shown in Figure A and B, individuals with robust BPIFB1 expression had a much shorter TFS time than those with low expression. Patients with substantial BPIFB1 expression in blood samples (GSE93606) had a significantly shorter PFS duration than patients with modest expression, as seen in Figure C.
 

## 3.6. Functional analysis
Patients with high and low BPIFB1 expression levels underwent a GSEA enrichment analysis (P < .05 and false discovery rate < 0.25) to determine the biological activities and pathways linked to BPIFB1. Since the GSE150910 dataset has the largest sample size, we selected it. The GSEA results (Figure S7, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P95) showed that the BPIFB high-expression group produced more collagen than both the low and high-expression groups.
Additionally, patients with high-expression BPIFB1 had higher lung scores for regulatory T cells (Treg), dendritic cells, macrophages, neutrophils, cytokinesis-cytokine receptor, and inflammatory response compared to patients with low-expression BPIFB1 (Figure S8, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/P96). These results showed a significant positive association between BPIFB1 and the inflammatory process, suggesting that higher levels of BPIFB1 could be linked to improved immunological function.

## 3.7. Experimental verification

## 3.7.1. BPIFB1 expression in clinical samples of IPF
Table displays the clinical samples’ initial clinicopathological features. We used RT-qPCR and immunohistochemical staining to detect BPIFB1 expression. Compared to healthy controls, patients with IPF had considerably higher levels of BPIFB1 expression (Fig. A and B). In addition, we examined BPIFB1 expression in HLF cells following TGF-β1 treatment. As shown, mRNA expression of BPIFB1 was significantly increased in TGF-β1-stimulated HLF cells (Fig. C). Additionally, BPIFB1 expression in HLF was assessed by Western blotting analysis (Fig. D). BPIFB1 protein was significantly overexpressed in HLF.
 

## 3.7.2. BPIFB1 stimulates ECM relative protein production
GSEA enrichment analysis predicted BPIFB1 could be associated with ECM production. Therefore, we tested the above hypothesis. Western blot analysis of protein levels in si BPIFB1-transfected cells showed that knockdown of BPIFB1 expression caused a decrease in TGF-β1-induced α-SMA protein, COL1A1, COL3A1, and FN expression in human lung fibroblasts (Fig. ).
 

## 3.7.3. BPIFB1 impacts the proliferation and migration of lung fibroblasts
Cell proliferation activity assays showed that TGF-β1 significantly enhanced fibroblast proliferation. Knockdown of BPIFB1 significantly inhibited TGF-β1-induced fibroblast proliferation (Fig. A). It has been reported in the literature that[ ] BPIFB1 can promote the metastasis of breast cancer cells. IPF considered a “tumor-like disease,” is caused by alveolar epithelial cell injury and impaired repair,[ ] with excessive proliferation and migration of activated lung fibroblasts.[ ] Thus, we investigated BPIFB1’s impact on lung fibroblast migration using transwell assay, and the findings revealed that BPIFB1 knockdown dramatically reduced TGF-β1-induced fibroblast migration (Fig. B).
 

## 4. Discussion
IPF is a persistent, life-threatening lung illness that exhibits the pathology and radiological characteristics of typical interstitial pneumonia. In this study, it was discovered that patients with IPF had significantly higher levels of the BPIFB1 gene in their lung tissue, but not in their blood. Concurrently, they were predominantly expressed in MUC5B + cells and differentiating ciliated cells, and scRNA-seq analysis revealed that BPIFB1 expression was substantially up-regulated in MUC5B + cells from patients with IPF. Furthermore, functionally relevant bioinformatics analysis (GSEA and ssGSEA analysis) revealed that individuals exhibiting elevated levels of BPIFB1 expression were more prone to experiencing a heightened inflammatory response compared to those with diminished levels of BPIFB1 expression. This increased collagen production ultimately led to a more unfavorable prognosis for the former group. The findings presented here align with the functional classification of BPIFB1 as reported in the UniProt database.[ ] Furthermore, our experimental results verified that BPIFB1, which is linked to the synthesis of collagen, the migration, and proliferation of human lung fibroblasts, is substantially expressed in IPF patients’ lung tissue.
One member of the BPI-fold-containing family is Long-palate Lung and Nasal Epithelium Clone 1, or BPIFB1.[ ]
BPIFB1, which is predominantly expressed in serous cells of the airway submucosal glands, goblet cells of the nasal cavity, and airway epithelium, comprises 484 amino acids and is composed of 16 exons and 15 introns.[ ] It has been widely accepted that these proteins are expressed exclusively in the respiratory tract.[ – ] It has been reported in the literature that BPIFB1 protein can recognize and specifically bind LPS components of Pseudomonas aeruginosa to reduce the activation effect of lipopolysaccharide on CD14 receptors on the target cell membrane surface and inhibit the overexpression of intracellular inflammatory factor TNF-α, to achieve the effect of regulating the inflammatory response.[ ] On the contrary, recent research has revealed that aberrant BPIFB1 expression is linked to numerous diseases, including cystic fibrosis-related severe pulmonary disease, vibrio cholerae infection, nonobese diabetes, and malignancies. BPIFB1 is a secreted protein that is associated with immunoregulatory function and shows antitumor effects.[ ] Unfortunately, no literature reports have been identified regarding the association between BPIFB1 and collagen synthesis in liver fibrosis or renal fibrosis.
Shum et al[ , ] identified anti-BPIFB1 antibodies in the sera of patients with ILD and connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (CTD-ILD), including undifferentiated CTD with ILD, scleroderma with ILD, and rheumatoid arthritis, and found BPIFB1 to be associated with the development of IPF in patients with type I autoimmune polyendocrine gland disease syndrome and their associated mouse models of autoimmune diseases. This was also demonstrated in our ssGSEA analysis. As identified using ssGSEA analysis, patients whose lung BPIFB1 expression was high were more likely than those with low-expression BPIFB1 to have elevated scores of dendritic cells, macrophages, neutrophils, Treg, cytokinesis-cytokine receptor, and inflammatory response. This may be because BPIFB1 can serve as an indicator of the host defense status of IPF patients. BPIFB1 is up-regulated in acute exacerbations or when high microbial burden occurs, which may explain the inverse association of BPIFB1 with DLCO% predicted in patients with IPF. However, the bioinformatics results showed that BPIFB1 was not associated with lung function in IPF, which may be due to the different sources of patients with IPF and the severity of IPF in patients. Second, different detection methods and sample sizes may also lead to the deviation of BPIFB1 expression in IPF tissues. To ascertain the relationship between BPIFB1 and lung function in patients with IPF, a large sample size of clinical trials is required.
The production of mucus is significantly influenced by the airways. Mucus protects the distal airways by acting as a physical barrier as well as having antibacterial properties.[ ] Mucus blocks and clogs the honeycomb airways during fibrosis, decreasing blood oxygen exchange and pathogen clearance. Two gel-forming mucus proteins, mucin 5B (MUC5B), which is most abundant in the airways, and MUC5AC, which is also discovered in low levels, maintain secreted mucus hydrogels.[ ] One of the biggest risk factors for pulmonary fibrosis is gain-of-function MUC5B polymorphisms.[ – ] Herrera JA uses the “secretome” dataset[ ] to determine that BPIFB1 is the most prevalent secretome-associated protein in fibrotic mucus based on the number of cellular infiltrates in the mucus. This aligns with our bioinformatics findings. Based on single-cell sequencing data, we found that cells expressing higher levels of MUC5B also have higher levels of BPIFB1. BPIFB1 and MUC5B were discovered by Herrera JA[ ] to be the most highly expressed proteins in honeycomb airway cells in pulmonary fibrosis. Our results supported the bioinformatics analysis in this article, which suggested a connection between BPIFB1 and the synthesis of collagen. Dysfunctional lung epithelial cells often cause pulmonary fibrosis by interacting with lung fibroblasts.[ ] Pulmonary fibrosis is characterized by excessive proliferation of lung fibroblasts, resulting in impaired lung function. Activated lung fibroblasts are the key effector cells of IPF.[ ] TGFβ1 has prominent effects on the lung fibroblast population, the effector cells responsible for the deposition of extracellular matrix in lung fibrosis.[ ] When HLF cells were exposed to TGF-β1, BPIFB1 expression rose significantly. Lung fibroblasts treated with TGF-β1 produced much less collagen, α-SMA, and FN via suppressing BPIFB1 expression. Furthermore, there was inhibition of fibroblast migration and proliferation. According to our theory, TGF-β1 activates fibroblasts and airway-ciliated cells by inducing fibroblasts to produce BPIFB1. Moreover, BPIFB1 promotes extracellular matrix remodeling, myofibroblast differentiation, and lung fibroblast activation. Consequently, the prognosis is worse for people with IPF who express high levels of BPIFB1. Though single-cell sequencing suggested that BPIFB1 was expressed in airway epithelial cells, our experiments revealed its expression in pulmonary fibroblasts as well. Inconsistency existed between the experimental results and the single-cell sequencing analysis above.This discrepancy may stem from differences in experimental methods and sample types. As a secreted protein, BPIFB1 produced by airway epithelial cells may influence the function of pulmonary fibroblasts. Specifically, BPIFB1 secreted by airway epithelial cells could potentially promote pulmonary fibrosis through its action on fibroblasts. Due to experimental limitations, we were unable to validate the interaction between airway epithelial cells and pulmonary fibroblasts. In future studies, we will focus on this aspect to conduct more in-depth investigations. Nevertheless, nothing is known about the molecular pathway between BPIFB1 and MUC5B, and research on the connection between BPIFB1 and pulmonary fibrosis has been published. Future studies with a deeper focus on fundamentals should validate BPIFB1’s unique role in IPF.
Several limitations apply to this investigation. Initially, comprehensive treatment data for patients is absent in studies, potentially impacting the assessment of the predictive efficacy of BPIFB1. Furthermore, the research was conducted using retrospective data obtained from the GEO database, with a comparatively limited number of samples in each dataset. Furthermore, the majority of the data sets utilized in this study failed to include noteworthy prognostic clinical parameters, including therapeutic interventions, underlying disorders, and so forth. Therefore, the predictive significance of BPIFB1 and its relationship with lung function remains limited. Long-term research with larger sample sizes is necessary to establish the therapeutic importance of BPIFB1. Future investigations should aim to study BPIFB1 whether play an important role in cell–cell communication among diverse cells types and tissues, including the lung tissue and pulmonary vessels.

## 5. Conclusion
BPIFB1 is a promising biomarker for predicting prognosis and severity in people with IPF. Inhibition of BPIFB1 attenuates TGF-β1-induced collagen production in fibroblasts. BPIFB1 represents a potentially effective target in the pursuit of elucidating the mechanisms underlying IPF and formulating therapeutic approaches.
