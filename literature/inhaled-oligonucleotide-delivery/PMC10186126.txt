## Abstract

## Purpose
Epithelial cystatin SN (CST1), a type 2 cysteine protease inhibitor, was significantly upregulated in asthma. In this study, we aimed to investigate the potential role and mechanism of CST1 in eosinophilic inflammation in asthma.

## Methods
Bioinformatics analysis on Gene Expression Omnibus datasets were used to explore the expression of CST1 in asthma. Sputum samples were collected from 76 asthmatics and 22 control subjects. CST1 mRNA and protein expression in the induced sputum were measured by real-time polymerase chain reaction, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and western blotting. The possible function of CST1 was explored in ovalbumin (OVA)-induced eosinophilic asthma. Transcriptome sequencing (RNA-seq) was used to predict the possible regulated mechanism of CST1 in bronchial epithelial cells. Overexpression or knockdown of CST1 was further used to verify potential mechanisms in bronchial epithelial cells.

## Results
CST1 expression was significantly increased in the epithelial cells and induced sputum of asthma. Increased CST1 was significantly associated with eosinophilic indicators and T helper cytokines. CST1 aggravated airway eosinophilic inflammation in the OVA-induced asthma model. In addition, overexpression of CST1 significantly enhanced the phosphorylation of AKT and the expression of serpin peptidase inhibitor, clade B, member 2 (SERPINB2), while knockdown using anti-CST1 siRNA reversed the trend. Furthermore, AKT had a positive effect on SERPINB2 expression.

## Conclusions
Increased sputum CST1 may play a key role in the pathogenesis of asthma through involvement in eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation through activation of the AKT signaling pathway, further promoting SERPINB2 expression. Therefore, targeting CST1 might be of therapeutic value in treating asthma with severe and eosinophilic phenotypes.

Keywords: Asthma, cystatin SN, eosinophils, inflammation, epithelium, ovalbumin, SERPINB2

## INTRODUCTION
Asthma is a chronic airway inflammatory disease with high morbidity worldwide and high mortality in low and lower-middle-income countries. , It is characterized by airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR), chronic airway inflammation, and reversible airflow restriction, causing symptoms including recurrent wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath. Numerous studies have suggested that airway epithelial allergic/eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation play key roles in the pathogenesis of asthma. , , 
Cystatin SN (encoded by CST1) is a member of the type 2 cystatin proteins superfamily that provides protective functions in a variety of human fluids and secretions, which can modulate the immune response and likely play a role in inflammatory disorders. Recently, CST1 has been reported to be upregulated in airway chronic inflammatory diseases, such as rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, , , allergic rhinitis, , , and asthma, especially in eosinophilic asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). A study by Yan et al. indicates that cystatin SN enhances eosinophil activation and infiltration through interleukin (IL)-5 in patients with chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps. Furthermore, we found that CST1 expression was significantly upregulated in asthmatic patients with severe and eosinophilic phenotypes. However, only a few studies have explored the underlying mechanism of CST1 in bronchial epithelial cells and the roles of CST1 in airway eosinophilic inflammation in asthma models. Therefore, the original point of this study was to further understand the potential regulatory mechanism of CST1 in asthma, namely, to discover the regulatory relationship between CST1, AKT signaling pathway, and serpin peptidase inhibitor, clade B, member 2 (SERPINB2).
The AKT signaling pathway was reportedly involved in various inflammatory diseases, such as allergic rhinitis, COPD, , , and asthma. , , Targeting phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT)/AKT could bring positive effects on treating diseases, such as suppressing asthma airway inflammation, attenuating atherosclerosis, and attenuating inflammatory response in rats with osteoarthritis. SERPINB2 is one of the T helper 2 (Th2) signature genes. However, the relationship between AKT signaling pathway and SERPINB2 expression in asthma has not been reported.
Our study observed that upregulated CST1 was positively correlated with eosinophilic inflammation and speculated that CST1 may be involved in Th2-mediated inflammation in asthma. SERPINB2 was significantly upregulated in bronchial epithelial cells treated with recombinant CST1 (rCST1). The AKT signaling pathway could be activated by the overexpression of CST1 in bronchial epithelial cells and ovalbumin (OVA)-asthma mouse through RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and subsequent experiments verification. Therefore, it is necessary to study the role of CST1 and its underlying mechanism in asthma, which may provide guidance for the precise treatment of asthma in the future.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Bioinformatic analysis of CST1 based on Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets
The expression of CST1 in the asthmatic airway epithelial brushing cells or induced sputum cells was obtained in the present study from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/); a total of seven datasets were used including GSE43696 (88 asthma cases and 20 healthy controls), GSE63142 (128 asthma cases and 27 healthy controls), GSE130499 (116 asthma cases and 38 healthy controls), GSE76262 (118 asthma cases and 21 healthy controls), GSE45111 (17 eosinophilic asthma, 12 neutrophilic asthma, and 18 paucigranulocytic asthma), and GSE137268 (15 healthy control, 16 paucigranulocytic asthma, 11 neutrophilic asthma, 17 eosinophilic asthma, and 15 mixed granulocytic asthma), and GSE4302 (28 healthy control, 42 asthmatics at baseline, 14 asthmatics after placebo treatment, 18 asthmatics after Flovent treatment). The GEOquery, BiocManager, limma, org.Hs.eg.db, ggplot2, and pheatmap packages in R language (version 4.0.3) were used in this study.

## Subjects
Participants initially diagnosed with asthma (without glucocorticoid treatment) were recruited from the first affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). Asthma was diagnosed according to the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) guidelines : symptoms of wheezing, cough, and dyspnea plus the provocative concentration of methacholine causing a 20% fall (PC20) of forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1) <8 mg/mL and/or ≥12% increase in FEV1 following inhalation of 200 μg salbutamol. After a baseline assessment of each enrolled asthmatic subject, patients began taking inhaled corticosteroids (ICS, fluticasone, or budesonide) with a prescription from the referring physician for four weeks. Patients with asthma were mainly non-severe asthma (accounting for 94.7%) in our study. Healthy controls had no respiratory or inflammatory illness and were recruited by advertisement. For each participant, we collected clinical information including the pulmonary function, fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO), blood eosinophils (EOS) %, and serum total immunoglobulin E (IgE). All participants provided written informed consent. The study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the ethics committee of the first affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University (2021[071]).

## Collection of induced sputum
Participants were asked to inhale atomized 4.5% sodium chloride solution to induce coughing, and the sputum was collected under the operation of respiratory physicians. The sputum samples were processed within 2 hours of collection for optimum cell viability. Then the sputum bolt was dissolved in 0.1% DL-Dithiothreitol (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 4 times its weight. The dissolved solution was filtered through a cell screen and centrifuged (1,000 g × 5 minutes; Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany), then the sputum cells in the sediment were collected in 1 mL of TRIzol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) for quantitative polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and western blotting examination and the sputum supernatant was stored in −80°C refrigerator for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) detection. The proportion of squamous cells <5% in induced sputum or less than 10 squamous cells at low magnification was considered qualified sputum specimens.

## Cell culture and treatment
Human primary bronchial epithelial (HBEpiC) cells (#3210; ScienCell, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and immortalized human lung epithelial (BEAS-2B) cells (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) were cultured at an air-liquid interface as previously described. HBEpiC cells were treated with or without rCST1 (100 ng/mL; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA). BEAS-2B cells were stimulated with or without cytokines IL-4, IL-13, and IL-17A (100 ng/mL; PeproTech, Cranbury, NJ, USA), stimulated with or without house dust mite (HDM; Greer Laboratories, Lenoir, NC, USA), treated with or without E64d (10 μM; GlpBio, Montclair, CA, USA), induced with SC-79 (4 μg/mL, AKT activator; GlpBio) or MK2206 (10 μM, AKT inhibitor; Selleck, Houston, TX, USA), and transfected with control vector or overexpression CST1 vector (GeneCodex, Wuhan, China), siRNA negative control or anti-CST1 siRNA (GeneCodex) using transfection reagent Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The target sequence of CST1 siRNA was: 5′-CCACCAAAGAUGACUACUA-3′. Twenty-four hours later, HBEpiC cells were harvested for RNA-seq, and BEAS-2B cells were harvested for subsequent quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) and western blotting detections.

## Murine model of allergic airway inflammation
Seven- to eight-week-old female C57BL/6 mice (GDMLAC, Foshan, China) were anesthetized with isoflurane (West Gene, Chengdu, China) before OVA solution sensitization. Mouse was sensitized with an intraperitoneal injection of OVA solution (100 μg in 100 μL saline; Sigma-Aldrich) mixed with Al(OH)3 as an adjuvant on days 0, 7, and 14. Mouse was challenged with intranasal administration of OVA solution (1 mg in 50 μL saline) on days 19, 20, 21, 22, and 23. As mice lack the CST1 gene, we used rCST1 referring to the description of Fukuoka et al., 2 hours before the OVA challenge on days 19, 20, 21, 22, and 23, rCST1 (12 μg in 40 μL; NovoProtein, Suzhou, China) or control saline were administered intranasally. Twenty-four hours after the last challenge of OVA, lung tissues were collected for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, periodic acid-Schiff staining (PAS), quantitative PCR, and western blotting detections. Inflammation was scored according to H&E staining as previously described. The bronchial alveolar lavage fluid (BALF) of the mouse was collected and centrifuged (980 rpm, 5 minutes; Eppendorf), then the supernatant was stored in a −80°C refrigerator for ELISA detection, and the precipitated cells were lysed with red blood cells lysis buffer and then resuspended with 0.9% saline to adjust the concentration to (0.5–1) × 106 cells/mL. Then, 10 μL of the above cell suspension was taken for the cell smear. The dried cell smears were stained by Liu stain solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions for cellular staining. The number of cells in BALF was visually counted by observers who were blinded under the microscope (Olympus BX63; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). Then, the percentage of cells was counted. Finally, the cell number was calculated according to the cell concentration, volume, and proportion. Animal experiments were approved by the ethics committee of the first affiliated hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University (2021000895).

## qRT-PCR
Total RNA from induced sputum cells treated human bronchial epithelial cells as well as lung tissues of the mouse were isolated using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Reverse transcribed using the Evo M-MLV RT Premix kit (AG, Changsha, China). The expressions of candidate genes were quantitatively determined using Biosystems Light Cycler 480 (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA) as standard procedures. The primers used are listed in . The gene expression was presented as log2 transformed and relative to the mean of the control group.
 

## ELISA
The protein levels of CST1 (CSB-EL006088HU) in the induced sputum supernatant of participants were measured using commercially available ELISA kits from CUSABIO (Wuhan, China). Meanwhile, the protein levels of IL-4 (MM-0051H1), IL-10 (MM-0066H1), IL-25 (MM-1954H1), IL-33 (MM-1743H1), thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP, MM-0260H1), IL-5 (MM-0050H1), IL-13 (MM-0062H1), SERPINB2 (MM-60461H1), IL-2 (MM-0055H1), IL-6 (MM-0049H1), IL-8 (MM-1558H1), IL-17A (MM-2117H1), interferon-γ (IFN-γ; MM-0033H1), and mucins 5ac (MUC5AC, MM-1278H1) in induced sputum supernatant, and Muc5ac (MM-0218M1) in the BALF of the mouse were measured using commercially available ELISA kits (MEIMIAN, Yancheng, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. All standards were measured in duplicate.

## Western blotting
CST1, p-AKT/AKT, and SERPINB2 protein levels in induced sputum cells, bronchial epithelial cells, and mouse lung tissues were measured by western blotting according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, total proteins were extracted from cells by protein lysates using radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Phygene, Fuzhou, China), which contains 1% phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (1 mM; West Gene) and 1% protein phosphatase/protease inhibitor mixture (Phygene). The total protein concentration was measured by the bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit (Phygene) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The final concentration of total protein containing 25% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis loading buffer (Phygene) varying from 1.5 to 3 µg/µL was acceptable. Then, 10 µL of the above protein samples was electrophoresed in 7.5% or 10% SDS gels (EpiZyme, Shanghai, China) at the voltage of 60 then 100 volts, and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (0.2 µm; Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) at the current of 220 mA (4°C for 2 hours). Subsequently, the PVDF membranes were incubated sequentially with 5% skim milk (at room temperature for 1 hour), the different primary antibodies (4°C for 24 hours), and developed with the horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (at room temperature for 1 hour) using enhanced chemiluminescence (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Finally, the bands were scanned through the chemiluminescence imaging analysis system (ImageQuant LAS 4000; Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan) and quantified using ImageJ software. Antibodies to anti-CST1 (1:500; ABclonal, Wuhan, China), p-AKT (1:1,000; Affinity, West Bridgford, UK), AKT (1:1,000; Affinity), SERPINB2 (1:500; Affinity), Tubulin beta (1:1,000; Affinity), glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH, 1:3,000; Affinity), and goat anti-rabbit IgG (H+L) HRP (1:4,000; Affinity) were used in this study.

## Statistical analysis
R language (version 4.0.3), SPSS software (version 25), and GraphPad software (version 8.3.0) were used for all statistical analyses. For normally distributed data, we calculated means ± standard deviation (SD) and used Student’s t-test to compare across the groups. For non-normally distributed data, we calculated medians with interquartile ranges and used non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney test) to compare across the groups. We analyzed the correlation using Spearman's rank-order correlation. The χ2 test was used for categorical measures. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and Youden’s index were used to determine the optimal cutoffs for sputum CST1 level in diagnosing asthma. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## RESULTS

## CST1 is upregulated in transcriptome profiles of airway samples from asthmatic patients with severe and eosinophilic phenotypes
Airway epithelial and induced sputum cells from epithelial brushings (GSE43696, GSE63142, and GSE130499) and induced sputum (GSE76262, GSE45111, and GSE137268) of asthmatic patients or healthy controls undergoing transcriptome expression profiles analysis were obtained from the GEO datasets. Through bioinformatical analysis of these datasets, we found that the mRNA expression of CST1 in asthmatic airway epithelial cells and induced sputum cells were more significantly upregulated in severe asthma ( ) and eosinophilic asthma ( ) compared to healthy controls ( ).
 

## CST1 mRNA and protein levels are increased in asthma and decreased after ICS treatment
We next recruited a total of 22 healthy controls and 76 asthmatic patients ( ). First, the mRNA levels of CST1 were detected in induced sputum cells by qRT-PCR. CST1 mRNA expression was more significantly increased in asthmatic subjects compared to healthy controls (P = 0.0077) ( ). In addition, the protein levels of CST1 in induced sputum supernatants were validated by ELISA. Also, results showed that CST1 protein levels were significantly upregulated in asthmatic patients (P < 0.0001) ( ), which was consistent with the mRNA expression results. In the ROC analysis of the protein levels of CST1 in induced sputum supernatants, the area under the curve was 0.7673 (P = 0.0001) ( ). For discriminating asthmatic patients from healthy controls, the optimal sputum CST1 cutoff was 1.515 ng/mL with a sensitivity of 77.63% (95% confidence interval [CI], 67.07%–85.54%) and a specificity of 68.18% (95% CI, 47.32%–83.64%). CST1 western blot results of induced sputum cells were more significantly increased in asthmatic patients than in healthy participants ( ). Furthermore, we found that CST1 mRNA expression was decreased after ICS (Flovent) treatment compared to placebo treatment in asthma based on the dataset of GSE4302 ( ), and that the same trend of CST1 protein expression in induced sputum supernatants was observed after 4 weeks of ICS treatment ( ). In contrast, there was no significant difference in CST1 expression in peripheral blood between asthmatics and healthy controls or in asthmatic patients before and after ICS treatment ( , ). These results suggest that CST1 expression may be specifically upregulated in asthmatic airway specimens, which was consistent with previous microarray data. Therefore, we presumed that elevated sputum CST1 might serve as a noninvasive diagnostic biomarker to monitor the treatment response of airway inflammation in asthma.
 

## Elevated CST1 expression is associated with airway eosinophilic/type 2 inflammation
It has been well known that FeNO, blood EOS, and serum total IgE are significant predictors for airway eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation in allergic asthma, , , while airway immune responses triggered by Th2/Th1 cytokines are known to contribute to allergic and nonallergic asthma. To further determine whether CST1 is involved in eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation in asthma, we analyzed the correlation between CST1 expression and these inflammatory indicators. Results showed that there were significant positive correlations between CST1 mRNA expression and FeNO (rs = 0.5761, P < 0.0001), blood EOS% (rs = 0.3004, P = 0.045), and serum total IgE level (rs = 0.3642, P = 0.0246) ( ). Consistently, positive correlations between CST1 protein levels and FeNO, blood EOS%, and serum IgE were also observed ( ). Furthermore, a similar trend between CST1 and Th2 cytokines (IL-4, IL-10, IL-25, IL-33, TSLP, IL-5, and IL-13) ( ), but not Th1 (IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and IFN-γ) or Th17 (IL-17A) cytokines ( ) in induced sputum supernatants was observed. Specifically, the CST1 protein level in induced sputum supernatants was significantly associated with the secretion of IL-4, IL-10, IL-25, IL-33, TSLP, IL-5, and IL-13 cytokines. Moreover, we found a negative correlation between CST1 and the pulmonary function of subjects ( ), while there was no significant correlation between CST1 and MUC5AC secretion in induced sputum supernatants ( ). These results suggest elevated CST1 might be related to airway eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation. Whether it affects airway mucin secretion requires further clinical cohort studies with a larger sample.
 

## Recombinant CST1 enhances airway eosinophilic inflammation infiltration in the OVA asthma model
In addition, we investigated the role of CST1 in the airway inflammation of asthma with eosinophilic phenotype using the OVA-induced mouse model. The mice were sensitized and challenged with OVA, and recombinant protein CST1 or saline was administered intranasally 2 hours before OVA challenges ( ). Compared to saline-treated controls, OVA sensitization and challenge resulted in the infiltration of inflammatory cells around the airways as assessed using H&E staining, airway inflammation scoring, and BALF cell counting ( ). In addition, the expression levels of Th2 inflammatory cytokines in the lung tissues were increased in OVA-induced allergic mice ( ). Recombinant CST1 in OVA-induced mice significantly enhanced lung inflammatory cell infiltration ( ), and specifically increased eosinophils in BALF ( ). Also, the expression levels of Th2 inflammatory cytokines, including IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, IL-25, and IL-33, were further increased in asthmatic mice treated with rCST1 (P < 0.05; ). However, lung PAS staining showed that rCST1 did not affect goblet cells hyperplasia in asthmatic mice ( ), nor did it affect Muc5ac mRNA and protein levels ( ). These data further suggest that CST1 might be involved in the pathogenesis of asthma by promoting airway eosinophil infiltration rather than affecting mucin secretion.
 

## CST1 regulates SERPINB2 expression via the AKT signaling pathway in airway epithelial cells
We further explored the potential mechanism of CST1 in bronchial epithelial cells. First, through RNA-seq on HBEpiC cells treated with or without rCST1 for 6 hours, a total of 435 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the 2 groups were obtained, including 227 upregulated and 208 downregulated genes, respectively ( ). Through Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis, we found that these downregulated genes mainly contributed to regulating cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions and the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway ( ), which has been reported to be involved in airway inflammation in asthma. Thus, we assumed the AKT signaling as the downstream pathway of CST1. Meanwhile, we found that SERPINB2, one of the Th2 signature genes in asthma, , , was significantly upregulated in HBEpiCs treated with rCST1 ( ). In addition, there was a significant positive correlation between CST1 and SERPINB2 in airway epithelial and induced sputum cells of asthma was observed based on transcriptome profiles ( ). Further validation showed that the expression of SERPINB2 in induced sputum supernatants of asthma was more significantly increased compared to healthy controls ( ) and positively correlated with CST1 expression ( ). Therefore, we assumed SERPINB2 to be the downstream molecule of CST1.
 Subsequently, we found that IL-4 could increase CST1 in BEAS-2B cells, whereas IL-17A induced a downregulation of CST1 ( ), which is consistent with a previous study. Therefore, we chose the cytokine IL-4 to treat BEAS-2B to simulate the asthma cell model. We found that IL-4 could induce the protein expression of CST1, p-AKT, AKT, and SERPINB2 in BEAS-2B cells ( ). In addition, the expression of CST1, p-AKT, and SERPINB2 were significantly upregulated in BEAS-2B cells stimulated with HDM at the concentration of 50 μg/mL ( ). These results suggest that CST1, AKT signaling, and SERPINB2 can be activated in allergic inflammation.
 Furthermore, we verified that overexpression of CST1 significantly increased the phosphorylation of AKT and the expression of SERPINB2 by establishing a CST1 overexpression vector to transfect BEAS-2B cells for 24 hours ( ). To further determine whether SERPINB2 participates in the AKT signaling pathway, the agonist of AKT (SC-79) was used. As indicated, SC-79 significantly increased the phosphorylation of AKT and the expression of SERPINB2 ( ). What is more, E64d, a chemical compound of CST1, significantly increased the expression of SERPINB2 and the phosphorylation of AKT in BEAS-2B cells ( ), while knockdown CST1 using CST1 siRNA significantly decreased p-AKT and SERPINB2 expression ( ). Meanwhile, the inhibitor of AKT (MK2206) significantly decreased the phosphorylation of AKT as well as the expression of SERPINB2 ( ). Therefore, these results indicate that CST1 may be involved in the AKT signaling pathway and promotes the expression of SERPINB2, showing a clear correlation between SERPINB2 and the AKT signaling pathway. Taken together, these results demonstrate that CST1 can promote SERPINB2 expression in bronchial epithelial cells via the AKT signaling pathway.
 

## Recombinant CST1 increases p-AKT and SERPINB2 expression in the OVA asthma model
OVA sensitization and challenge significantly increased p-AKT, AKT, and SERPINB2 expression in mice treated with OVA compared to those treated with saline, and rCST1 further increased the expression of p-AKT as well as SERPINB2 compared to mice treated with OVA alone ( ). These data are consistent with bronchial epithelial cell experiments, supporting the regulated role of CST1 in the AKT signaling pathway and SERPINB2 expression in asthma. Collectively, the above results suggest that CST1 might be involved in airway eosinophilia and Th2 inflammation in asthma by activating the AKT signaling pathway and promoting SERPINB2 expression.
 

## DISCUSSION
Type 2 asthma with eosinophilic inflammation is a common phenotype in asthma. Epithelial-derived molecules play a cardinal role in the pathogenesis of allergic/eosinophilic asthma driven by Th2 cytokines. From the GEO datasets, we initially found that CST1 expression was significantly upregulated in asthmatic airway epithelial and induced sputum cells, mainly in severe and eosinophilic asthma. Consistently, subsequent experiments verified that CST1 was significantly increased in sputum samples of asthmatic patients and BEAS-2B cells stimulated with IL-4 or HDM. Sputum CST1 was significantly correlated with airway eosinophilic inflammation and Th2 cytokines secretion. After ICS treatment, both the mRNA and protein levels of CST1 were significantly decreased in patients with asthma. Furthermore, rCST1 promoted eosinophilic inflammation response and Th2 inflammatory cytokine expression in OVA-induced asthma. Upregulated CST1 could activate the AKT signaling pathway and further promote SERPINB2 expression in vitro and in vivo. Therefore, we speculated that sputum CST1 might participate in the pathogenesis of asthma by involving eosinophilic/type 2 inflammation potentially through the AKT-SERPINB2 signaling pathway.
Recent studies have suggested that EOS contributes to the development of severe asthma or asthma exacerbation. , , George et al. found that CST1 was positively correlated with blood EOS in asthma. Yan et al. found that both the mRNA and protein expression of CST1 in polyp tissues from patients with CRSwNP were significantly, albeit weakly positively, correlated with tissue eosinophil percentages and FeNO levels. Diver et al.
 found that CST1 levels were strongly correlated with FeNO levels. In the present study, we found that the expression of CST1 was significantly increased in eosinophilic asthma in GSE45111 and GSE137268, and that both mRNA and protein levels of CST1 positively correlated with FeNO, blood EOS%, and serum total IgE levels. Subsequently, sputum CST1 was positively correlated with the secretion of Th2 cytokines (including IL-4, IL-10, IL-25, IL-33, TSLP, IL-5, and IL-13) in sputum, but not Th1/Th17 cytokines. We also found that IL-4 could increase CST1 in epithelial cells, whereas IL-17A induced a down-regulation of CST1. Based on these, we hypothesized that elevated CST1 in asthma might be involved in type 2 inflammation, which is consistent with the study of Kato et al.
 and Nocera et al. Also, we found decreases in CST1 mRNA and protein levels in asthmatic patients after ICS treatment, which provides further evidence for the results of previous research that the level of CST1 was significantly decreased in the nonallergic rhinitis with eosinophilia syndrome group after 2 weeks of treatment. However, there was no significant difference in CST1 expression between asthma and control groups in peripheral blood or between the ICS treatment and non-treatment groups in serum. This inconsistency with airway epithelial cells and induced sputum results may be because peripheral blood reflects the general condition of the body, while induced sputum and airway epithelial response are the local conditions of the airway rather than the systemic condition; thus, CST1 in airway specimens is more likely to be used as a biomarker to monitor the pathogenesis of airway inflammation in patients with asthma.
Based on the above results, we constructed an OVA-induced asthma model to further explore the role of CST1 in the pathogenesis of eosinophilic asthma. Our results showed that rCST1 significantly enhanced the infiltration of lung inflammatory cells and the EOS in BALF in OVA-induced mice as well as promoted the expression of Th2 inflammatory cytokines in the lung tissue, including IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, IL-25, and IL-33, which further validated the positive correlation between CST1 and Th2 cytokines. IL-4 plays a key role in Th2-type inflammation, and we found that the expression of CST1, p-AKT/AKT, and SERPINB2 could be induced to increase in bronchial epithelial cells in response to the cytokine IL-4 stimulation. Furthermore, rCST1 treatment of OVA-induced asthma increased Th2 cytokines including IL-4 and EOS. A plausible explanation for activated EOS and the release of Th2 cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 , is that there might be a CST1-mediated positive feedback loop that propagates EOS recruitment and activation in bronchial epithelial in asthma. To some extent, our data further suggest that CST1 may be involved in eosinophilic and type 2 inflammation triggered by Th2 cytokines in asthma in vivo, which enriched the previous evidence that upregulated CST1 could enhance eosinophil activation and infiltration through IL-5 in vitro. 
It has been reported that the expression of p-AKT could be increased and decreased after CST1 overexpression and silencing in hepatocellular carcinoma, and that CST1 overexpression contributed to the activation of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in estrogen receptor (ER)-breast cancer cells. Activation of the AKT signaling pathway is not only involved in asthma but also in cell survival, proliferation, apoptosis, activating signaling pathway mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) and cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), and localizing forkhead box class O (FOXO). Because we focused on airway inflammation in asthma, signaling pathways were further studied with sequencing results. RNA-seq results showed that rCST1 mainly contributed to the regulation of cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions and the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway in human primary bronchial epithelial cells. Our experiments verified that overexpression and knockdown of CST1 in BEAS-2B significantly affected the phosphorylation of AKT; CST1 overexpression increased p-AKT expression, while CST1 knockdown decreased p-AKT expression.
Meanwhile, we found that SERPINB2, a Th2 signature gene in asthma, , , was significantly increased in bronchial epithelial cells treated with rCST1 in RNA-seq, demonstrating that CST1 and SERPINB2 were positively correlated with both mRNA and protein levels; furthermore, CST1 had a positive effect on SERPINB2 expression. SERPINB2 and CST1 have been reported to be upregulated in mild asthma, but the relationship between CST1 and SERPINB2 expression in asthma has not been reported. Our study is the first to investigate the effect of CST1 on SERPINB2 expression.
In addition, our experiments showed that activation of the AKT signaling pathway could increase SERPINB2 expression, while inhibition of the AKT signaling pathway reversed the trend. Therefore, we hypothesized that SERPINB2 is one of the downstream molecules of the AKT signaling pathway. This study also found that CST1, AKT signaling, and SERPINB2 were activated in the asthmatic cell model of BEAS-2B cells stimulated with IL-4 or HDM. In short, our experiments showed that CST1 might contribute to airway eosinophilia and Th2 inflammation in asthma by promoting the expression of the Th2 signature gene SERPINB2 through the AKT signaling pathway.
There was no significant correlation between the expression of CST1 and MUC5AC in induced sputum supernatants. rCST1 did not affect the number of goblet cells and the expression of Muc5ac in OVA-asthmatic mice. Therefore, we speculated that elevated CST1 might be involved in the pathogenesis of asthma by activating eosinophil infiltration rather than by enhancing airway mucus secretion.
There are some limitations in our study. First, we did not identify and localize CST1 expression in bronchial biopsy specimens due to the failure to obtain the lower respiratory tract specimens from patients with asthma. Secondly, as mice lack the CST1 gene, we used rCST1 rather than generating transgenic mouse-expressing human CST1 to investigate the role of cystatin SN in OVA-induced asthma. Thirdly, the role of CST1 in airway mucin secretion and airway hyperresponsiveness in asthma needs to be further investigated. Finally, we did not investigate other classic downstream effects of the AKT signaling pathway, such as mTOR and CREB activation, FOXO localization, or the effect of the AKT signaling pathway in the bronchial epithelial cell function of asthma. All the above-mentioned limitations still need to be further studied in asthma.
Taken together, our findings suggest that upregulated CST1 may play a cardinal role in the pathogenesis of eosinophilic asthma by promoting airway eosinophilic and Th2 inflammation. Overexpression of CST1 enhances SERPINB2 expression in vitro and in vivo by activating the AKT signaling pathway. Therefore, targeting CST1 might be of therapeutic value in treating asthma with an eosinophilic phenotype.
