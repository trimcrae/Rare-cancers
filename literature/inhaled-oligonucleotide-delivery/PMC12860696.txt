## Background
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a type of interstitial lung disease that is critically influenced by epigenetic regulation and mitochondrial function during its pathogenesis and progression. Flavonoids possess significant anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, and among these compounds, quercetin has been shown to inhibit the progression of pulmonary fibrosis by modulating the expression of specific microRNAs (miRNAs) and alleviating oxidative stress associated with mitochondrial dysfunction. However, the precise underlying mechanism remains unclear.

## Methods
We established a murine model of pulmonary fibrosis via endotracheal nebulization with bleomycin, which resulted in uniformly distributed, severe fibrotic lesions in the lungs. Quercetin (Que) was administered via the intratracheal route, enabling targeted pulmonary delivery, reducing the required drug concentration, increasing bioavailability, and minimizing potential systemic toxicity. For the assessment of histopathological changes and collagen deposition in lung tissues, we used hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) and Masson's trichrome staining. In cell experiments, we stimulated A549 cells with transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) and transfected them with a miR-423–5p inhibitor to examine the expression levels of extracellular matrix (ECM) related proteins and the NDUFS7 subunit of mitochondrial complex I. Furthermore, we evaluated mitochondrial function by measuring reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) levels, and the mitochondrial membrane potential.

## Results
Our research revealed that quercetin regulated the level of miR-423–5p in mouse lung tissue, as well as the expression of the mitochondrial complex I subunit NDUFS7. After 15 days of endotracheal nebulization with quercetin, extracellular matrix remodeling and collagen deposition in mouse lung tissue were alleviated, and the pulmonary fibrosis induced by bleomycin was improved. In vitro experiments demonstrated that inhibiting miR-423–5p enabled the normal translation and expression of NDUFS7, and improves mitochondrial function, ultimately alleviating the ECM remodeling induced by TGF-β1.

## Conclusion
Targeting miR-423–5p to modulate NDUFS7 expression represents a potential therapeutic strategy for ameliorating mitochondrial dysfunction and suppressing pulmonary fibrosis.

## Introduction
Pulmonary fibrosis (PF) is a chronic and progressive interstitial lung disease characterized pathologically by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), which disrupts the normal lung architecture. The clinical prognosis of PF is extremely poor. Once diagnosed, the disease typically leads to irreversible respiratory failure and eventual death [ ]. Considering the complexity of its pathogenesis, no effective treatment strategies are currently available [ ]. Ongoing research continues to focus on improving and optimizing existing antifibrotic therapies, as well as exploring novel mechanisms and therapeutic targets for PF.
Oxidative stress is a key pathogenic mechanism in PF [ , ]. Excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) leads to redox imbalance, with intracellular ROS generated primarily via mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I and III [ ]. TGF-β overexpression induces a fibrotic phenotype accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction, a decrease in respiratory chain complex V, cristae deformation, and a surge in ROS production [ ]. As a core subunit of mitochondrial complex I, NDUFS7 plays a direct role in electron transfer, and impairment of its expression or function can directly lead to mitochondrial dysfunction [ ]. Mutations in NDUFS7 are among the major causes of complex I-deficient mitochondrial diseases, such as Leigh syndrome [ , ]. Several studies have reported that introducing NDUFS7 mutations into HEK293T cells suppresses cell proliferation, increases cell death, and enhances sensitivity to oxidative stress [ ]. However, the expression and role of NDUFS7 in PF remain unexplored.
MiR-423–5p is a key member of the microRNA (miRNA) family and is characterized by a mature sequence that is highly conserved across species, suggesting an evolutionarily conserved role in biological regulation. Under pathological conditions, miR-423–5p expression is frequently dysregulated and is markedly upregulated in diseases such as lung cancer, heart failure and PF [ , , ]. Functionally, miR-423–5p has been shown to activate the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by targeting MYBL2, thereby exacerbating hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction [ ]. Additionally, extracellular vesicle-delivered miR-423–5p can inhibit the differentiation of hepatic stellate cells, suggesting its potential involvement in liver fibrosis [ ]. Studies have also revealed that miR-423–5p expression is significantly elevated in fibrotic airway tissues and in TGF-β1-stimulated BEAS-2B cells. Overexpression of miR-423–5p promotes TGF-β1-induced EMT, whereas its knockdown attenuates this process [ ]. Nevertheless, the precise regulatory mechanisms through which miR-423–5p and mitochondrial oxidative respiratory chain complex-related subunits contribute to the pathogenesis and progression of PF remain unclear.
Que, a naturally occurring flavonoid widely distributed in plants, has multitarget and multipathway modes of action in terms of biological functions [ ]. Extensive studies have confirmed its notable bioactivities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antiapoptotic, immunomodulatory, and antifibrotic effects [ ]. In the context of oxidative stress regulation, Que activates the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)/antioxidant response element (ARE) signaling pathway, thereby increasing the expression and activity of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) [ ]. This contributes to the effective clearance of excess ROS and mitigates oxidative damage to cells and tissues. With respect to inflammatory responses, Que downregulates key proinflammatory signaling pathways, including nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), leading to reduced secretion of inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), thereby alleviating chronic inflammation [ , ]. Furthermore, Que modulates the expression of epigenetic regulators, particularly microRNAs (miRNAs). It has been shown to upregulate miR-16, miR-138–5p, and the let-7 family while downregulating miR-21, miR-155, and miR-29 [ , ]. This regulatory capacity underscores its potential therapeutic value in fibrotic diseases.
The present study aimed to investigate three key questions: first, is NDUFS7 expression impaired in PF? Second, can Que regulate the relative levels of miR-423–5p and the expression of NDUFS7? Third, is miR-423–5p involved in the translational regulation of NDUFS7 mRNA?

## Materials and methods
2.1

## GEO data analysis
We obtained gene expression data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, specifically the dataset GSE110147, which includes transcriptomic profiles of lung tissue samples from four groups: patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), combined IPF and NSIP, and healthy controls. The downloaded data were then analyzed in RStudio (version R4.4.1) to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs). A volcano plot was generated to visualize the distribution of DEGs according to their fold change and statistical significance. Additionally, a heatmap was constructed via the expression matrix of genes related to the mitochondrial oxidative respiratory chain, providing an intuitive representation of their expression patterns across the different sample groups.

2.2

## Construction and treatment of the pulmonary fibrosis mouse model
We used adult male C57BL/6J mice (8 weeks old, 20–22 g) as experimental subjects. A pulmonary fibrosis model was established via endotracheal nebulization: the mice were briefly anesthetized with isoflurane, after which a micronebulizer (Model HY-LWH03A, Beijing Yuansenkaidu Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) was inserted orally into the trachea, the outer diameter of the capillary tube of this micronebulizer: φ 0.6 mm. Press the plunger downward quickly, and the medicine can be directly atomized into an aerosol and delivered into the airway cavity. Both a bleomycin (BLM)-induced fibrosis model (2 mg/kg, GC15819, GLPBIO) and subsequent drug treatments were administered via this nebulization approach. Previous studies have indicated that, compared with intratracheal instillation, BLM delivery via nebulization leads to a more uniform distribution of fibrotic lesions in the lungs, with fibrotic damage progressively worsening over time. Starting on day 7 after BLM administration, Que (0.1 mg in 50 μL, the dosage selection was based on existing studies [ , ] and a preliminary experiment was conducted. ) was nebulized into the mouse lungs. Subsequent Que treatments were administered every two days until the end of the 21-day experimental period. Upon completion of the treatment, major organ tissues were harvested for pathological staining, and serum samples were collected for biochemical analysis.

2.3

## Cell culture and treatment
In this study, we utilized the A549 human lung epithelial cell line and the MRC-5 fibroblast cell line. The cells were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 % penicillin/streptomycin. They were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5 % CO2, and the medium was changed every 2–3 days. For experimental treatments, A549 cells and MRC-5 cells were exposed to 5 ng/mL [ ] and 10 ng/mL [ ] transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1; Cat. No. GMP-CA59, NovoProtein) for 24 h.

2.4

## Cell transfection
We transfected cells with the miR-423–5p mimic and the miR-423–5p inhibitor via a commercial transfection kit (siRNA-mate plus Transfection Kit, GenePharma) according to previously established methods. MiRNA expression levels were detected 24 h after cell transfection at 30 pmol/mL to confirm transfection efficiency, and protein levels were measured 48 h post-transfection.

2.5

## Pathological staining
Lung tissues were embedded in paraffin and serially sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm. For histological evaluation, two staining kits from Solarbio were used: the H&E Staining Kit (Cat. No. G1121) and the Masson Staining Kit (Cat. No. G1346). H&E staining was performed to assess general morphological alterations in the lung tissues. Masson's trichrome staining was employed to visualize and quantify collagen deposition. For each group of 6 mice, 3 consecutive paraffin-embedded lung tissue sections were prepared. Two independent and experienced pathologists, who were blinded to the experimental grouping performed the Ashcroft scoring and Masson staining quantitation and used Image J to determine the percentage values of collagen deposition.

2.6

## Western blot analysis
For sample preparation, either 20 mg of fresh lung tissue or 1 × 106 cells collected per well of a 6-well plate was used. Each sample was lysed in 200 μL of RIPA buffer. Lung tissue was homogenized by grinding and subsequently subjected to ultrasonic disruption on ice, while cell samples were directly sonicated on ice. After lysis, the samples were centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected for protein quantification via the BCA method. SDS loading buffer was then added to the protein extracts, which were denatured by heating at 95 °C for 5 min and then cooled on ice. Denatured proteins were separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS‒PAGE) and transferred to a PVDF membrane via a wet transfer system. The membrane was blocked with 5 % nonfat milk to reduce nonspecific binding. Following blocking, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the samples were incubated with enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein signals were detected via an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate and visualized via a chemiluminescence imaging system. Band intensities were quantified via ImageJ software.

2.7

## RT-qPCR
Total RNA was extracted from both tissue samples and cultured cells via TRIzol reagent. For miRNA analysis, cDNA synthesis was performed via the miRcute Enhanced miRNA cDNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit (Cat. No. KR211, TIANGEN), followed by quantitative real-time PCR via the miRcute Enhanced miRNA Fluorescence Quantitative Detection Kit (Cat. No. FP411, TIANGEN). Specific primers for miR-423–5p (5′-GAGGGGCAGAGAGCGAGA-3′) and the internal reference U6 small nuclear RNA were included in the reactions. All primers were synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. The relative expression of miR-423–5p was measured via a CFX Connect™ Real-Time PCR System, with U6 serving as the endogenous control for data normalization.

2.8

## Immunofluorescence analysis
Lung tissue sections were dewaxed and subjected to antigen retrieval via microwave heating in sodium citrate buffer. After cooling to room temperature, the sections were permeabilized with 0.1 % Triton X-100 for 10 min and blocked with 5 % bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min at room temperature. The sections were then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After the samples were washed with PBS, fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied, and the samples were incubated for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and after a final PBS wash, the sections were mounted and imaged via a confocal fluorescence microscope. The cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde, permeabilized, and then blocking. Subsequent steps—including blocking, primary and secondary antibody incubation, DAPI staining, mounting, and imaging—were performed identically to the protocol for the tissue sections.

2.9

## ROS fluorescence microscopy detection
A549 cells were cultured on glass coverslips until they reached an appropriate density for detection. In accordance with the instructions of the DCFH-DA probe detection kit, positive and negative controls were included to validate the experimental results. The DCFH-DA working solution was then added to completely cover the cells, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark to prevent probe photodegradation. After incubation, the cells were washed with serum-free medium to remove unbound probe and minimize background interference. Finally, the coverslips with adhered cells were transferred to a fluorescence microscope, and the fluorescence intensity in the green channel was measured. The level of intracellular ROS was assessed based on the intensity of the green fluorescence signal.

2.10

## Detection of ATP content, H2O2 content, and mitochondrial membrane potential
2x10^6 A549 cells and MRC-5 cells were collected for the detection of ATP and H2O2 contents. The ATP Content Chemiluminescence Assay Kit (Cat. No. E-BC-F002, Elabscience) and the H2O2 Content Detection Kit (Cat. No. E-BC-K102, Elabscience) were used according to the manufacturers’ instructions. After the cells were processed and the reaction systems were prepared as specified, luminescence signals (for ATP) and absorbance (for H2O2) were measured via a multifunctional microplate reader, simultaneously measure the protein content of the samples, and determine the standardized measurement values based on the protein content. The intracellular ATP levels and H2O2 production levels were calculated on the basis of the corresponding signal intensities. A549 cells were cultured on glass coverslips until they reached the appropriate confluency. A Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Detection Kit (JC-1) (Cat. No. E-CK-A301, Elabscience) was used according to the provided protocol. JC-1 working solution was added to completely cover the cells, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 20 min. After incubation, the cells were washed to remove excess unbound JC-1 dye. The coverslips were then transferred to a fluorescence microscope for observation. After setting the exposure time and fixing the threshold, the mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed by quantifying the ratio of red to green fluorescence intensity.

2.11

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
To validate the potential binding of miR-423–5p to the 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR) of NDUFS7, plasmids containing either the wild-type or mutant NDUFS7 3′-UTR sequence were constructed. These plasmids were cotransfected into A549 cells along with either the miR-423–5p mimic or the miR-423–5p inhibitor. After an appropriate incubation period for transfection and reporter expression, luciferase activity was measured via the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Cat. No. K1136; APEBIO). Firefly luciferase activity, which reflects the regulatory interaction between miR-423–5p and the NDUFS7 3′-UTR, was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity, which served as an internal control. The normalized firefly/Renilla luciferase ratio was used to assess the effect of miR-423–5p on NDUFS7 expression.

2.12

## Data processing and statistical analysis
All the numerical data are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs). For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was first performed to assess overall significance. If a significant difference was detected, Dunnett's t-test was used for post hoc comparisons between each experimental group and the control group. The following conventions were used to denote statistical significance: ∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01, ∗∗∗P < 0.001, and ∗∗∗∗P < 0.0001.

## Results
3.1

## Effects of Quercetin on Histopathological Changes and Collagen Production in C57BL/6J Mice Exposed to bleomycin
The volcano plot of the gene expression matrix from the GSE dataset is presented in A. B shows the chemical structure of Que and the overall study design. We evaluated the effects of Que on body weight, pulmonary edema, and histopathological alterations in C57BL/6J mice at 21 days post-BLM exposure. Que treatment alleviated ECM remodeling and diminished collagen fiber accumulation. Histological changes in the lung parenchyma were assessed via hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining combined with the Ashcroft scoring system, whereas interstitial collagen deposition was quantified as the collagen volume fraction (%) following Masson's trichrome staining ( C–E). Que significantly attenuated BLM-induced body weight loss and reduced the lung wet weight/body weight ratio, as shown in F. Collectively, our findings demonstrated that Que markedly reduced BLM-induced alveolar wall thickening, loss of alveolar space, and the production and deposition of collagen fibers within the lung interstitium. 

3.2

## The expression level of MiR-423–5p and its regulation
The mice received BLM via endotracheal nebulization. At 21 days post-exposure, H&E and Masson's trichrome staining revealed significant pulmonary pathology, including pronounced inflammatory infiltration, tissue damage, alveolar wall thickening, and fibrotic structural alterations. Increased collagen deposition within the mesenchymal compartment and pathological collagen production were also observed. RT-qPCR analysis revealed elevated miR-423–5p expression levels in the pulmonary fibrotic lesions of these mice. Compared with vehicle treatment, 15 days of Que treatment markedly reduced miR-423–5p expression in fibrotic mice ( A). In vitro, TGF-β1-treated alveolar epithelial cells presented notably increased expression of fibrotic markers, including fibronectin 1 (FN1), collagen type I alpha 1 (COL1A1), and alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), compared with untreated controls ( B). Concurrently, miR-423–5p expression mirrored this increase ( C). Furthermore, in vitro transfection of A549 and MRC-5 cells with a miR-423–5p mimic or inhibitor successfully modulated intracellular miR-423–5p levels. RT-qPCR confirmed that the inhibitor significantly downregulated miR-423–5p expression, whereas the mimic substantially upregulated it ( D–F). 

3.3

## Quercetin reduces extracellular matrix remodeling in lung tissues of mice with bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis
To determine whether Que mitigates BLM-induced ECM remodeling, we assessed the expression of FN1, COL1A1, α-SMA, and E-cadherin (E-ca) via western blotting (WB) ( A–E) and immunofluorescence ( F and E). WB analysis revealed significantly upregulated expression of FN1, COL1A1, and α-SMA in the lung tissues of BLM-exposed mice. Notably, Que treatment downregulated the expression of these proteins. Conversely, E-ca expression, which was decreased in the BLM group, was restored by Que treatment. 

3.4

## Quercetin regulates the expression of NDUFS7 in lung tissue
Whether Que alleviates PF by affecting the expression of NDUFS7, a subunit of mitochondrial complex I in the oxidative respiratory chain. In vivo, RT-qPCR, WB, and immunofluorescence analyses revealed decreased NDUFS7 expression in the PF model, whereas the protein expression level of NOX-4 was increased. However, Que intervention significantly reversed this BLM-mediated downregulation of NDUFS7 expression ( A‒E). 

3.5

## MiR-423–5p Inhibitor Regulates the Expression of NDUFS7 in A549 and MRC-5 Cells in Vitro
In vitro RT-qPCR experiments using A549 and MRC-5 cells revealed that NDUFS7 mRNA levels were significantly reduced in TGF-β1-induced cells. Transfection with a miR-423–5p inhibitor increased NDUFS7 mRNA levels ( A and B), indicating that miR-423–5p regulates NDUFS7 expression. Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed a significant difference in the Renilla luciferase/firefly luciferase (RL/FL) ratio after cotransfection of the wild-type NDUFS7 (WT-NDUFS7) plasmid with either a miR-423–5p mimic or inhibitor. In contrast, cotransfection with the mutant NDUFS7 (MUT-NDUFS7) plasmid did not significantly affect the RL/FL ratio ( C–F). Collectively, these results demonstrate that miR-423–5p knockdown increases mitochondrial NDUFS7 mRNA levels. Using a TGF-β1-induced cellular model, WB and immunofluorescence analyses revealed significantly increased NOX-4 expression alongside decreased NDUFS7 expression. Treatment with a miR-423–5p inhibitor reduced NOX-4 expression and reversed NDUFS7 downregulation ( G–K). Furthermore, significant differences in NOX-4 and NDUFS7 expression levels were observed between cells transfected with the miR-423–5p inhibitor negative control (NC) and those transfected with the miR-423–5p inhibitor. Our research also found that transfection with miR-423–5p inhibitor alleviated TGF-β1-induced ECM remodeling, including the abnormal expression of fibronectin and α-SMA ( A–E). These findings demonstrate that the miR-423–5p inhibitor regulates ECM remodeling by modulating the miR-423–5p/NDUFS7 pathway. 

3.6

## MiR-423–5p Inhibitor Regulates Mitochondrial Function and ROS production via the miR-423–5p/NDUFS7 pathway
Mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are well-established mechanisms involved in the pathogenesis of PF. In our study, WB revealed decreased expression of NDUFS7, a subunit of mitochondrial complex I, in TGF-β1-induced A549 cells. Concurrently, TGF-β1 treatment enhanced the fluorescence signal of the ROS marker DCFH-DA. Inhibition of miR-423–5p increased NDUFS7 expression and reduced ROS production ( A and B). Furthermore, TGF-β1 reduced the mitochondrial membrane potential in A549 cells. This reduction was reversed by transfection with the miR-423–5p inhibitor ( C and D). While TGF-β1 increased both ATP production and H2O2 levels, transfection with the miR-423–5p inhibitor reversed the inhibitory effect on NDUFS7 expression, restored mitochondrial electron transport function, decreased H2O2 production, and further increased ATP synthesis ( E and F).

## Discussion
Que, a flavonoid with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, has demonstrated efficacy in treating various clinical diseases in numerous animal model studies. However, its clinical application is significantly limited by poor water solubility, which makes it difficult to use in vitro studies, low bioavailability, rapid systemic clearance, metabolism, and enzymatic degradation [ , ]. Strategies to increase the bioavailability of Que include the use of nanocarriers or microemulsions [ , , ]. Optimizing administration routes can further improve its therapeutic utility and mitigate toxic side effects. For PF, in which lesions are localized to the lungs, endotracheal nebulization offers a distinct advantage over systemic routes such as oral gavage or intraperitoneal injection. Nebulization delivers the drug directly to the target site, bypassing systemic circulation and pulmonary redistribution, thereby improving its bioavailability and therapeutic effects [ ]. Future research should prioritize the use of nanocarriers or nucleic acid-based delivery systems to address the challenges associated with the solubility and bioavailability of Que. Targeted endotracheal nebulization of such formulations holds promise for enhancing Que distribution within the lungs while potentially reducing systemic exposure, paving the way for viable clinical trials.
MiRNAs are short non-coding RNAs that play crucial roles in post-transcriptional gene regulation. Dysregulation of specific miRNAs in PF, including profibrotic miRNAs (e.g., miR-21, miR-154, and miR-96) and antifibrotic miRNAs (e.g., miR-29, miR-30, and miR-26a), contributes to disease progression [ , , ]. Clarifying the pathophysiological roles of profibrotic miRNAs, particularly their impact on mitochondrial function, is essential for elucidating the pathogenesis of IPF. In the lung tissues of mice with chronic arsenic-induce PF, miR-21 is highly expressed. It downregulates PTEN, activates the AKT pathway, promotes glycolysis, disrupts mitochondrial energy metabolism, and ultimately induces PF [ ].​ MiR-29c inhibits apoptosis, maintains mitochondrial energy supply, preserves epithelial integrity, and alleviates PF [ ].​ Additionally, a study on lung epithelial cells from aged mice showed that miR-34a induces mitochondrial dysfunction (decreased membrane potential and ROS accumulation) by targeting SIRT1, thereby promoting cellular senescence and accelerating fibrosis [ ].​ In addition, miR-423–5p upregulation in cardiomyocytes induces mitochondrial membrane potential loss, reduces ATP content, and increases ROS production [ , ]. Notably, none of these studies clearly defined the effects of these miRNAs on mitochondrial complex I/III activity. Our in vitro studies confirmed that a miR-423–5p inhibitor suppresses TGF-β1-induced ECM component expression in epithelial cells and reverses the TGF-β1-mediated downregulation of NDUFS7.
Crucially, mitochondrial complex I, the primary source of mitochondrial ROS, generates excessive ROS when impaired [ , ]. As a mediator of mitochondrial dysfunction, NOX4's inhibition of mitochondrial complexes is exacerbated by excess mitochondrially derived ROS [ ]. Mitochondria-derived ROS can directly activate the TGF-β1/SMAD signaling pathway in lung fibroblasts, promoting proliferation and accelerating ECM deposition [ , , ]. In Alzheimer's disease (AD) and cerebral ischemia-reperfusion (I/R), deficiency of mitochondrial NDUFS7 expression directly impairs mitochondrial function, reduces ATP synthesis, and induces abnormalities in the mitochondrial membrane potential and structural damage. Excessively generated ROS further damage complex I structure, inhibit the expression of mitochondrial unfolded protein response (mtUPR)-related proteins, and impair mitochondrial quality control capacity, forming a vicious cycle [ , , ]. Our study demonstrated that BLM exposure and TGF-β1 treatment induced miR-423–5p overexpression, leading to abnormal mitochondrial complex I expression (specifically the NDUFS7 subunit) and excessive ROS production. The fibrotic microenvironment leads to damage to the mitochondrial structure and impairs normal oxidative phosphorylation function. Under the dual drive of mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming, total ATP production in key fibrotic cells increases. Significant downregulation of complex I likely activates glycolysis to maintain ATP supply [ ]. While the potential involvement of mitochondrial metabolic dysfunction and aggravated electron leakage requires further verification, miR-423–5p inhibition increased ATP production, reduced ROS, and reversed membrane potential loss, likely by ameliorating TGF-β1-induced mitochondrial dysfunction. This study has limitations. Further validation using miR-423–5p knockout mice and clinical lung samples is required. In addition, the low solubility of Que, as previously discussed, hinders confirmatory cell-based experiments.

## Conclusion
Que was shown to regulate the expression of miR-423–5p and reverse the downregulation of NDUFS7 expression in mouse lung tissue induced by BLM. Endotracheal nebulization with Que can effectively alleviate ECM remodeling and ultimately inhibits BLM-induced PF progression. Inhibition of miR-423–5p restored NDUFS7 expression. This restoration improved mitochondrial function by reducing ROS production, mitigating the decline in the mitochondrial membrane potential, and normalizing metabolic function (as evidenced by decreased H2O2 levels and increased ATP production). Overall, these data confirmed the regulatory effect of Que on the miR-423–5p/NDUFS7 axis in mouse lung tissue, and that in vitro regulation of this axis can ultimately improve ECM remodeling. Targeting this axis represents a potential therapeutic strategy for mitigating PF by improving mitochondrial function and reducing oxidative stress.
