Cell death contributes to the development and progression of chronic asthma. However, the novel copper-dependent cell death, cuproptosis, and especially cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial cells, its role, and underlying mechanisms in airway remodeling are unclear. Repetitive OVA exposure was used to establish a mouse model of chronic asthma. The human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B (BEAS-2B cells) was incubated with recombinant human IL-4 (rhIL-4) and TGF-β1 to simulated airway remodeling in vitro. Cuproptosis increased after repetitive OVA exposure in chronic asthmatic mice, evidenced by increased Cu2+ levels in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) and increased protein expression of cuproptosis-related genes (FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT), followed by increased airway responsiveness, lung inflammation, area of subepithelial fibrosis and mucus hypersecretion and expression of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)-related proteins (MMP-2, MMP-9, N-cadherin, vimentin and α-SMA). However, a cuproptosis blocker, the mitochondrion-targeted antioxidant, Szeto-Schiller (SS)-31, substantially inhibited the above mentioned processes. Meanwhile, we observed that proteins of the key regulator of cuproptosis (ferredoxin-1, FDX-1) and bronchial epithelial marker, Keratin 8/18, were partially colocalized in bronchial epithelial which demonstrated that OVA may mediate airway remodeling through cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial. Next, we simulated airway remodeling in vitro through coincubation of rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 in BEAS-2B cells and found that cuproptosis increased after rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 mediation, indicated by increased Cu2+ levels in supernatant and cytoplasm and increased protein expression of FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT, whereas addition of SS-31 (10 ng/mL) 30 min before rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 treatment attenuated cuproptosis. Moreover, our results revealed increased cell migration and protein expression of above EMT-related proteins in response to induction of rhIL-4 and TGFβ1. Furthermore, silencing FDX-1 significantly inhibited above mentioned responses. Cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial cells participates in airway remodeling in chronic asthma by EMT induction through FDX-1 regulation and may be a therapeutic target in chronic asthma.

## Introduction
Large epidemiological studies have demonstrated that chronic asthma is a major burden both on patients and society worldwide because of its recurrent attacks and low rate of full control . The pathological mechanisms of chronic asthma are attributed mainly to repetitive airway inflammation, airway hyperresponsiveness and airway remodeling, which manifests as inflammatory cell infiltration, smooth muscle hypertrophy and hyperplasia, goblet cell hyperplasia, hypertrophy of the submucosal mucus glands, subepithelial fibrosis and collagen deposition; these changes cause persistent deterioration of lung function and steroid resistance – . Thus, strategies to address chronic asthma, especially the pathological mechanism of airway remodeling, are urgently needed.
Accordingly, reports on the exact mechanisms of airway remodeling are not comprehensive; however, the airway epithelium is the first natural barrier to exposure to allergens and harmful substances, and its injury, death and destruction are initiating factors that induce asthma-associated pathological features . Impaired airway epithelial cells lead to the release of platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), serum endothelin-1 (ET-1), transforming growth factor-β (TGFβ), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), which promote the inflammatory response, airway epithelial basement membrane thickening, airway subepithelial fibrosis and smooth muscle cell hyperplasia, resulting in airway hyperresponsiveness, airway remodeling and airflow limitation , . Moreover, injured airway epithelium can induce epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through the secretion of various factors and signaling pathways; this process is characterized by the downregulation of epithelial cell markers (E-cadherin and occludin) and the upregulation of mesenchymal cell markers (N-cadherin, vimentin and α-SMA), which may occur during the development of airway remodeling in chronic asthma through the induction of persistent epithelial damage , .
Copper-dependent cuproptosis, a novel cell death mechanism first identified in 2022, is initiated by Cu2+ accumulation, is distinct from known death mechanisms such as apoptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis, and pyroptosis and is dependent on mitochondrial respiration, of which the key regulator is ferredoxin-1 (FDX-1). This occurs through the direct binding of Cu ions to lipoylated components of the tricarboxylic acid cycle during mitochondrial respiration, leading to the aggregation of lipoylated proteins and the subsequent downregulation of Fe-S cluster proteins, which causes toxic stress to the proteins and ultimately leads to cell death .
Copper, an essential cofactor for all organisms, is a key regulator of cellular signaling pathways and is involved in energy metabolism, including cytochrome C oxidase expression, oxidant-antioxidant balance, copper-zinc superoxide dismutase activity, and iron metabolism. Under normal conditions, the intracellular copper concentration remains very low, but copper can become toxic if the concentration exceeds a threshold . Clinical studies have demonstrated that copper levels in sputum, serum, toes and lung tissues are increased in asthmatic patients and are negatively correlated with lung function, such as forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) , . To date, the mechanisms of copper involved in asthma have been related mainly to inflammation, oxidative stress, imbalance of the oxidation-antioxidant system, immune dysfunction, ferroptosis, extracellular matrix metabolism, and disordered mitochondrial function – . Based on changes in copper levels mediate cuproptosis, the direct association of cuproptosis with airway remodeling has not been explored.
In the present study, we first investigated the role of cuproptosis in repetitive ovalbumin (OVA) exposure and rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 incubation-induced airway remodeling in vivo and in vitro and explored the underlying regulatory mechanisms. This research will help to clarify the importance of cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial cells in chronic airway inflammation and airway remodeling and provide new possible targets for the clinical treatment of chronic asthma.

## Methods

## Animals and ethics statement
Forty male wild-type C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks old, 15 ± 3 g, Weitong Lihua Experimental Animal Technology Co., Ltd) were used in this study. All the animals were housed in individually ventilated cages (25 ± 2 °C) with a 12 h light and 12 h dark cycle and allowed to acclimatize to laboratory conditions for 1 week before being used in the experiments. The mice were provided with water and food ad libitum. All protocols in this study were approved by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments of Hebei University (IACUC permit number: 202321XR). All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guideline and regulations.

## Experimental groups of animals
After acclimating for 1 week, male C57BL/6 mice were assigned to four groups (n = 10 per group) . Animal protocol was shown in Fig.  . 
Group A (control): The mice received equal amounts of saline via intraperitoneal injection on days 0, 7, and 14. Beginning on day 21, the mice received equal amounts of saline via inhalation for 30 min, three times a week, for 8 weeks.
Group B (OVA): The mice were sensitized via intraperitoneal injection of OVA/alum (20 μg of chicken OVA, grade V; Sigma, St. Louis, MO; adsorbed to 2 mg of Imject Alum, Thermo Scientific Pierce Biotechnology, Rockford, IL) in a total volume of 20 μL of saline on days 0, 7, and 14 and then challenged with 5% OVA via inhalation for 30 min, three times a week, for 8 weeks.
Group C (OVA + SS-31): 30 min before the inhalation of 5% OVA, 10 mg/kg cuproptosis blocker (mitochondrion-targeted antioxidant, SS-31; Yuanye Shengwu, Shanghai, China) was injected intraperitoneally, three times a week, for 8 weeks.
Group D (OVA + vehicle): 30 min before the inhalation of 5% OVA, 10 mL/kg PBS was injected intraperitoneally three times a week, for 8 weeks.

## Measurement of airway hyperresponsiveness
Mice were endotracheally intubated under isoflurane (3%-5%, nasal inhalation) and connected via the endotracheal cannula to a flexiVent system (SCIREQ Inc, Montreal, Canada). After initiating mechanical ventilation, the mice were subjected to deep lung inflation (DI; slow inflation to a pressure of 30 cmH2O held for 3 s) and mechanically ventilated at a tidal volume of 10 ml/kg and a respiratory frequency of 150 breaths/min for the measurement of pulmonary mechanics. Respiratory system resistance under single-frequency vibration [Rrs], airway resistance at fixed frequency [RN] and total respiratory system elastance [Ers] were measured in response to increasing doses of aerosolized methacholine (Mch: 0, 1.56, 3.125, 6.25, 12.5, 25 and 50 mg/ml) 24 h after the final inhalation of 5% OVA.

## Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid collection and cell classification
After measurement of airway hyperresponsiveness, bronchoalveolar lavage was performed by lavaging airways of mice thrice with 1 mL normal saline containing 0.01 mM EDTA via a tracheal cannula. The recovered lavage fluid was centrifuged at 252 g for 10 min at 4 °C. The numbers of eosinophils, neutrophils, and lymphocytes were determined by Wright staining under a microscope. The cell-free supernatant was collected and stored at -80 °C for subsequent cytokine assay.

## Copper assay kit for assessing Cu2+ and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) for BALF cytokines
Cu2+ levels in the BALF of the mice and in the cell supernatant and cytoplasm of the BEAS-2B cells were determined via a copper assay kit (Bioengineering Research Institute of Jiancheng, Nanjing, China). Cytokine levels (interleukin-4 (IL)-4, TGFβ1 and IL-1β) (Multi Sciences Biotech Co., Ltd) in the BALF of the mice were measured via individual ELISA kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

## Histological analysis of lung tissue
After BALF collection, the right lung was cut and fixed with 4% neutral phosphate-buffered formalin for 72 h. The fixed lung tissues were dehydrated, embedded in paraffin and sectioned. Tissue Sects. (4-μm thickness) were stained with hematoxylin–eosin (HE) to evaluate the general morphology and inflammation of the lung tissue and were stained with Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) and Masson’s trichrome to observe mucus secretion and subepithelial fibrosis in the lung tissue. Images were acquired with a light microscope (Zeiss, Inc., Germany).

## Dual immunofluorescence staining of lung tissue
Above mentioned tissue sections (4-μm thickness) were initially washed in PBS, recovered the antigen in sodium citrate, permeabilized using 0.1% Triton X-100 (Sigma), and then blocked with 4% bovine serum albumin (Gibico, USA). Immunofluorescence staining was developed using 1:200 rabbit anti-FDX-1 and 1:20 mouse anti-Keratin 8/18 primary antibody. The secondary antibodies included 1:350 TSA 620, and 1:350 TSA 520. FDX-1+ cells were observed in pink, and Keratin 8/18+ cells were observed in green. Dual (FDX-1 and Keratin 8/18) immunofuorescence-stained sections were imaged using a laser-scanning confocal microscope (Nikon Eclipse AX, Japan).

## Western blotting
Western blotting was performed as described previously . Briefly, total protein was extracted from lung tissues and BEAS-2B cells. Protein samples were heated to 100 °C for 10 min in SDS-PAGE loading buffer and separated on 10% and 15% polyacrylamide gels. The separated proteins were transferred at 200 mA to a PVDF membrane (Millipore, 21MA, USA) for 2 h at 4 °C. After the membrane was blocked in Tris-buffered saline (TBS) containing 5% nonfat milk, the blots were incubated with primary antibodies specific for FDX-1 (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), LIAS (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), DLAT (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), MMP-2 (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), MMP-9 (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:1000), E-cadherin (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), N-cadherin (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500), vimentin (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:500) and α-SMA (rabbit polyclonal antibody, 1:1000). All of the above antibodies were obtained from Proteintech, USA. Bands were detected via SuperECL Plus detection reagents (LI-COR, USA) and quantified via a Bio-Rad ChemiDOC imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA).

## Cell culture
The human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B (obtained from Meilun Biotechnology, Dalian, China) was cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Thermo-Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibico, USA) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) in an incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. When the cells reached 60–80% confluence, the BEAS-2B cells were incubated with recombinant human IL-4 (rhIL-4) and TGF-β1 (10 ng/mL, R&D Systems) for 24 h to stimulate airway remodeling in vitro. At 30 min before coincubation with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1, the BEAS-2B cells were exposed to a cuproptosis blocker (SS-31, 100 ng/mL; Yuanye Biotechnology Company, Shanghai, China).

## Cell viability measurement
Viability of BEAS-2B cells was determined with a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo, Shanghai, China), according to the manufacturers’ instructions, at different concentrations of SS-31 (0, 1, 10, 100, and 1000 ng/mL).

## SiRNA transfection to specifically silence FDX-1 of BEAS-2B cells
BEAS-2B cells were transfected with Lipofectamine® RNAiMAX Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 20 nM siRNA (RiboBio; China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Three separate FDX-1-specific siRNA sequences were used, and an unrelated control siRNA sequence was transfected using the same protocol, with no effect expected on FDX-1 expression. Knockdown of FDX-1 expression was confirmed at 48 h by western blotting.

## Measurement of cell migration
Migration of BEAS-2B cells was measured by wound healing experiments. Briefly, cell suspension was added to a 6-well cell culture plates, and the cells were allowed to adhere to the well and grow to greater than 90% confluence. Then, the medium was replaced with serum-free medium, and the cells were starved for 24 h. A 200 μL pipette tip was used to generate a wound in the cell monolayer perpendicular to the bottom line, and the dissociated cells were gently removed by washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Then, serum-free basic medium was added to the control group, and DMEM supplemented with rhIL-4 and TGF-β1 (10 ng/mL) was added to the experimental group. The cells were incubated in a cell incubator at 37 °C in 5% CO2 for 24 h. Changes in the wound area were observed under a microscope and photographed at 0, 12 and 24 h. ImageJ software was used to measure the area of the wound at each time point, which was compared with the area with that observed at 0 h, and the migration distance of each group was analyzed.

## Equipment and settings
All figures were processed by Adobe Illustrate. All images were processed by origin 2025 (except Figs.  and ). All western bands were processed by Image J.

## Statistical analysis
All the statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 21.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY). Differences between two groups were analyzed by two-sample Student’s t tests. p < 0.05 or p < 0.01 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Cuproptosis increased in chronic asthma model mice
To explore the effects of cuproptosis on chronic asthma, we established a chronic asthmatic mouse model through intraperitoneal injection and repeated nasal inhalation of OVA. We observed that cuproptosis increased after OVA exposure. As shown in Fig.  A, the Cu2+ concentration in the BALF was greater in the OVA group than in the control group. Moreover, we measured the protein expression of cuproptosis-related genes (FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT) in the lung tissues of the mice in the different groups. As shown in Fig.  B–E, the expression of these proteins was greater in the OVA group than in the control group. Research has shown that Cu2+ is highly dependent on mitochondrial respiration and that the application of mitochondrial antioxidants can reduce the sensitivity of cells to Cu2+, which can weaken the process of cuproptosis . Thus, our research adopted an cuproptosis inhibitor, the mitochondrion-targeted antioxidant SS-31, 30 min before nasal inhalation of OVA to further verify the role of cuproptosis in chronic asthma. Our results revealed that the use of SS-31 indeed inhibited the above mentioned increases in the Cu2+ level and protein expression of FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT, as shown in Fig.  B–E. 

## The cuproptosis blocker SS-31 could ameliorate repeated OVA exposure-induced airway hyperresponsiveness and pathological damage in the lung
Airway responsiveness and pathological damage in chronic asthma and the role of cuproptosis in above process were explored next. As shown in Fig.  A, the airway responsiveness increased in mice with chronic asthma. The concentration of methacholine required to double the initial airway resistance in the OVA group was 3.125 mg/ml, which was much lower than that in the control group (12.5 mg/ml). And the effect of cuproptosis on OVA-induced airway hyperresponsiveness was confirmed, as SS-31 addition was found to decrease the airway resistance compared with that in the vehicle group (6.25 mg/ml). 
Studies have confirmed that airway inflammation is the basis of the pathological characteristics of chronic asthma . Here, we illustrated the important role of cuproptosis in chronic airway inflammation. As shown in Fig.  B, OVA exposure led to epithelial cell injury and drop, inflammatory cell infiltration in the peribronchial and perivascular regions, alveolar space widening, smooth muscle layer and tracheal wall thickening. However, SS-31 application alleviated lung pathology, as indicated by decreased inflammatory cell infiltration and smooth muscle layer and tracheal wall thickening. In addition, shown in Fig.  C, the numbers of eosinophils, neutrophils, and lymphocytes in the BALF of the OVA groups were greater than those in the control group. However, SS-31 treatment reduced the number of these three types of inflammatory cells. Moreover, displayed in Fig.  D, compared with those in the control group, the levels of IL-4, TGFβ1, and IL-1β in the BALF of the mice increased after OVA exposure whereas SS-31 exposure reduced the release of the above mentioned three inflammatory factors, which was consistent with the changes in the inflammatory cells.

## The cuproptosis blocker SS-31 may ameliorate airway remodeling by EMT induction
Next, we explored the role and mechanism of cuproptosis in airway remodeling in chronic asthma. Masson and PAS staining were used to assess the subepithelial fibrosis and mucus secretion in the lung tissues. As shown in Fig.  A–C, compared with those in the control group, the area of subepithelial fibrosis and the level of mucus secretion increased in the OVA group. In contrast, the application of SS-31 decreased fibrosis and mucus secretion. Moreover, studies have demonstrated that EMT may occur during the development of airway remodeling through the induction of persistent epithelial damage . Thus, we next explored the effects of cuproptosis on EMT. As shown in Fig.  D and Supplementary Fig. 1, we measured the protein expression of EMT-related MMP-2, MMP-9, E-cadherin, N-cadherin, vimentin and α-SMA in the lung tissue of the mice and found that all the above proteins (except E-cadherin) were upregulated in the OVA group compared with those in the control group, whereas, SS-31 treatment before OVA induction downregulated the protein expression of these genes (except E-cadherin) in lung tissues. 

## Cuproptosis was increased in BEAS-2B cells incubated with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 and the addition of SS-31 could inhibit this process
Thus far, we concluded that cuproptosis participates in airway hyperresponsiveness, airway inflammation and airway remodeling in chronic asthma and is association with EMT induction in vivo. In addition, we observed that the proteins of ferredoxin-1 (FDX-1, the key regulator of cuproptosis) and Keratin 8/18 (bronchial epithelial marker) were colocalized in the bronchial epithelium in lung tissue sections which confirmed that OVA mediated airway remodeling through cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial, as shown in Fig.  . Moreover, the literature has confirmed that injury to and death of airway epithelial cells can induce the emergence of asthma-related pathological features, which increase Cu2+ uptake and cause Cu2+ accumulation, subsequently inducing cuproptosis . Thus, we next used a human bronchial epithelial cell line, BEAS-2B, as our next experimental cell line and simulated an airway remodeling model at the cellular level via incubation with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1. Consistent with in the vivo results, the results revealed that cuproptosis was also increased induced by rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 incubation in BEAS-2B cells. As shown in Fig.  B–G, the Cu2+ level in the supernatant and cytoplasm, and the protein expression levels of FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT in the BEAS-2B cells were greater than those in the control group. Moreover, different concentrations of SS-31 (0, 1, 10, 100, and 1000 ng/mL) were used to determine the effects on the viability of BEAS-2B cells. We found that 100 ng/mL SS-31 resulted in the highest viability, as shown in Fig.  A, and this concentration was used in our subsequent experiments. As expected, the addition of SS-31 also inhibited cuproptosis in vitro, as shown in Fig.  B–G. 

## Silencing of FDX-1 could attenuate cell remodeling in BEAS-2B cells
FDX-1 is the key regulator of cuproptosis-mediated Cu2+ toxicity . The in vitro results outlined above demonstrated that cuproptosis was increased by incubation with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 and that EMT was related to airway remodeling in vivo. However, the effects of cuproptosis on rhIL4- and TGFβ1-mediated EMT are still unclear. Thus, we next determined the role of cuproptosis in the rhIL-4- and TGFβ1-mediated changes in cell morphology and cell migratory function via wound healing experiments and EMT-related protein expression in BEAS-2B cells. As shown in Fig.  C, BEAS-2B cells contained more pseudopodia in the group treated with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 group than in the control group, as observed under a light microscope, which was the foundation of the subsequent process of cell migration. As expected, incubation with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 promoted the migratory function of BEAS-2B cells, as manifested by an increase in migratory distance at 24 h, as shown in Fig.  D–E. Moreover, similar to the results of cell migratory function, the protein expression levels of EMT-related MMP-2, MMP-9, N-cadherin, vimentin and α-SMA but no E-cadherin, in BEAS-2B cells were also upregulated in the group treated with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 compared with those in the control group, as shown in Fig.  F and Supplementary Fig. 2. However, FDX-1 knockdown by siRNAs, as shown in Fig.  A, B, dramatically decreased these processes compared with those in the scramble group, as shown in Fig.  C, F, which further confirmed the important role of cuproptosis in rhIL4- and TGFβ1-mediated EMT and cell remodeling through FDX-1 regulation. 

## Discussion
Numerous epidemiological studies have shown that chronic asthma is a significant burden worldwide because of its drug hyporesponsiveness . The 2023 GINA Global Strategy reports that with the widespread use of inhaled bronchodilators and corticosteroids (ICSs), airway hyperresponsiveness and chronic airway inflammation can be partially controlled in asthma patients; however, effective drugs and potential targets for reversing and relieving airway remodeling remain to be elucidated .
Recent studies have identified a new form of Cu-dependent cell death designated cuproptosis, which has generated renewed interest in the role of Cu homeostasis in asthma . Independent findings have shown that the copper toxicity significantly inhibits ciliary motion in the trachea and can eliminate a large percentage of the ciliary motion in the tracheal explants and lead to reductions in cell viability . Exposure to high concentrations of free Cu2+ (2 to 6 μM) causes hypercontraction of ACh-induced tracheal rings which contributes to calcium channels, NADPH oxidase, endothelial nitric oxide synthase and COX-mediated pathways . Short-term exposure to fine particulate matter (PM2.5)-bound Cu in individuals with asthma leads to a rapid decline in maximal midexpiratory flow (MMEF) and a significant increase in IL-23, promoting Th17 differentiation, disrupting the Th17/Treg balance, and leading to the development of asthma . In mice sensitized by intraperitoneal injection of ovalbumin, the intranasally infusion with CuO NPs exacerbated the development of asthma by increasing AHR, the inflammatory cell count in BALF, mucus secretion, serum IgE levels, the levels of allergic inflammatory markers, including IL-5 and IL-13, and ROS production in BALF . The above studies indeed demonstrated that exposure to high concentrations of Cu is closely associated with development of asthma. However, unlike the above studies, our study directly explored the relationship between cuproptosis and airway remodeling by adding a cuproptosis inhibitor in vivo and silencing FDX-1 (the key regulator of cuproptosis) in vitro rather than only focusing on copper, copper supplements, Cu transport protein 1 (CTR1) and/ or copper chelators.
In recent years, increasing amounts of epidemiological and experimental data have revealed significantly elevated Cu ion levels, Cu/Zn ratios and Cu/Se ratios in the sputum or lung tissues of patients with asthma, which are closely associated with the FEV1/FVC% and CD4/CD8 lymphocyte ratios and can be used as routine biomarkers for asthma risk in patients with recurrent asthma , . Studies confirmed that mice with pulmonary inflammation were susceptible to copper mediated oxidative damage; abnormal distributions of Cu ion may aggravate oxidative damage and inflammation, increased CD4/CD8 lymphocyte ratios and decreased lung function in asthma which illustrated Cu ion may be released from inflammatory bronchopulmonary tissues, not only from dead cells , . Changes in copper levels mediate cuproptosis; thus, to explore the role of cuproptosis in airway remodeling, we adopted the traditional method of repetitive inhalation of ovalbumin (OVA) to establish a chronic asthma mouse model. In our study, OVA exposure led to an increase in cuproptosis, as reflected by the increase in the level of copper ions in the BALF and the protein expression of cuproptosis-related genes (FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT). Moreover, airway resistance, lung inflammation, inflammatory cell infiltration (eosinophils, neutrophils and lymphocytes in the BALF), secretion of IL-4, TGFβ1, and IL-1β in the BALF, levels of secreted mucus, and thickening of subepithelial fibrosis increased compared with those in the control group, which confirmed the validity of chronic asthma model. However, challenge with HDM extract is another traditional way to establish an airway remodeling model . The methods outlined above primarily induce a type 2 immune response. However, human asthma is highly heterogeneous and is not always only due to Th2-driven airway inflammation, such as Th17/Treg imbalance. Whether cuproptosis plays a role in non-Th2-driven airway remodeling needs further research.
The literature has evidently shown that mitochondrial-targeted antioxidant represses cuproptosis through inhibition of copper ion carriers . Moreover, the mCuS@lm nanocages could protect cells from cuproptosis-induced damage through mitochondrial-targeted antioxidants . Thus, to directly explore the role of cuproptosis in airway remodeling, we used treatment with a cuproptosis blocker, Szeto-Schiller (SS)-31 (a mitochondrion-targeted antioxidant), and as expected, its addition inhibited outlined above to some extent confirming that cuproptosis participates in OVA-induced airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness and remodeling in chronic asthma. Studies demonstrate that SS-31 is a synthetic tetrapeptide that binds to the inner mitochondrial membrane, contributing to the prevention of oxidative stress, OxS, inflammatory response, apoptosis, vascular permeability and restoring mitochondrial function, thereby ameliorating ALI and protecting in vitro production of porcine embryos , .
Basic studies have demonstrated that EMT may occur during the development of airway remodeling through the induction of persistent epithelial damage , . In our study, we indeed found that the protein expression levels of EMT-associated MMP-2, MMP-9, N-cadherin, vimentin and α-SMA (but no E-cadherin) increased after repetitive OVA exposure relative to those in the control group. In addition, we observed that the proteins of ferredoxin-1 (FDX-1, the key regulator of cuproptosis) and Keratin 8/18 (bronchial epithelial marker) were colocalized in the bronchial epithelial in lung tissue sections through dual immunostaining which confirmed that OVA mediated airway remodeling through cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial.
In allergic asthma, IL-9 drives inflammation, and Th9 cell differentiation is governed by a complex, multi-layered regulatory network which centers on the synergistic action of TGFβ and IL-4 . As we all know, IL-4 level increases in the Th2 asthma and its exposure in vitro to stimulate model of asthma has been widely adopted . In addition, TGFβ1 is released in response to injury, inflammatory stimuli, and during bronchoconstriction to induce fibrosis and EMT in the pathophysiology of asthma . Thus, Next, to further investigate the effects of cuproptosis on EMT, we chose human bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B cells) to simulate airway remodeling by coincubation with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 , which was also the reason that we assessed IL-4, TGFβ1, and IL-1β secretion in the BALF in an animal model. Consistent with the in vivo results, cuproptosis was also increased, as indicated by the levels of copper ions in the supernatant and cytoplasm and the protein expression of FDX-1, LIAS, and DLAT in BEAS-2B cells incubated with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 compared with those in the control cells. Similarly, the addition of SS-31 (100 ng/mL, which was associated with the highest cell viability) inhibited cuproptosis. In addition, we measured EMT by observing migratory function and assessing EMT-related protein expression in BEAS-2B cells treated with rhIL-4 and TGFβ1. As expected, migratory function improved, and the protein expression levels of EMT-related genes (except E-cadherin) in BEAS-2B cells were increased rhIL-4 and TGFβ1 treatment. Moreover, silencing FDX-1 (the key regulator of cuproptosis) with siRNAs prevented the above responses, which further suggested that cuproptosis plays an important role in rhIL4- and TGFβ1-induced EMT and cell remodeling through FDX-1 regulation. However, the specific upstream and/or downstream signaling pathways of FDX-1 are still unclear. To date, the mechanisms of cuproptosis in various diseases involve mainly mitochondrial dysfunction, immune dysregulation, chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, angiogenic dysfunction, imbalance of the oxidation-antioxidant system, apoptosis, autophagy and ferroptosis , , ; however, whether FDX-1 affects airway remodeling through the above signaling pathways is not fully understood.
Our research demonstrated that cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial cells participated in airway remodeling in chronic asthma by EMT induction through regulation by FDX-1. However, reports suggest that phenotypic changes in airway smooth muscle cells (ASMCs) (from contractile to synthetic and/or proliferative phenotypes) are also involved in airway remodeling ; whether cuproptosis affects the phenotypic transition of ASMCs and then mediates airway remodeling also requires further exploration.

## Conclusions
Cuproptosis of bronchial epithelial cells is involved in the development of airway remodeling in chronic asthma by EMT induction through FDX-1 regulation and may be a therapeutic target in chronic asthma
