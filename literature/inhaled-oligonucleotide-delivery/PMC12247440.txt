## Abstract

## Background
Cervical cancer is one of the most common malignancies in women, and its progression is closely associated with hypoxia and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) polarization in the tumor microenvironment (TME). The dynamic interaction between cancer cells and TAMs in the hypoxic tumor microenvironment is the key to promoting tumor progression. However, the mechanisms underlying this interaction remain unclear.

## Methods
Macrophages were exposed to hypoxia, and next-generation sequencing was performed to analyze differentially expressed genes. TCGA data were used to identify the up-regulated genes that were independently associated with patient survival. Multiplex immunofluorescence was employed to identify semaphorin 6B (SEMA6B)-positive TAMs in human cervical cancers, and the correlation between their infiltration and clinicopathological characteristics were analyzed. TCGA, GEO, and TIMER databases were used to assess correlations between SEMA6B expression and patient survival and immune cell infiltration patterns. The SEMA6B expression in macrophages was knocked down by using siRNAs. Cell proliferation and mobility were evaluated in vitro, and flow cytometry, PCR and Western Blotting were conducted to determine the polarization of macrophages.

## Results
After exposure to hypoxia, 236 genes were up-regulated in macrophages. Among them, SEMA6B exhibited a significant association with poor survival. In addition, abundant CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs were associated with poor prognosis in cervical cancer patients. Database analysis revealed that SEMA6B expression was positively correlated with the infiltration of M2 macrophages and Tregs and negatively correlated with the infiltration of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells. In vitro, knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibited macrophage M2 polarization and the migration ability of macrophages. Furthermore, after coculture of macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown and cervical cancer cells, the proliferation, migration and invasion of SiHa and HeLa cells was significantly reduced in vitro.

## Conclusions
Knocking down SEMA6B may attenuate the progression of cervical cancer through regulating macrophage M2 polarization. The mechanism of action of SEMA6B on the progression of cervical cancer in vivo still needs further research. Targeting SEMA6B may be a potential immunotherapy approach for treating cervical cancer.

## Introduction
Cervical cancer is a common malignant tumor of the female reproductive system and is the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths in women [ ]. According to global cancer statistics, in 2020, there were approximately 600,000 new cases of cervical cancer each year, and 340,000 women died from cervical cancer [ ]. Although surgery and chemotherapy are currently the main treatment and prevention measures for cervical cancer, patients with advanced and recurrent metastatic cervical cancer still have poor prognosis [ ]. The rise of immunotherapy provides hope for improving the treatment and survival of cervical cancer patients. However, due to the complexity of immune regulatory mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment (TME) and the heterogeneity of malignant tumors, tumor cells evade immune effects through various pathways [ ]. Therefore, finding a new immunotherapy target for cervical cancer is crucial for improving the prognosis of cervical cancer.
Hypoxia, the main characteristic of the tumor microenvironment, can not only enhance the invasion and metastasis ability of tumors but also promote tumor cells to enter a “dormant state” to avoid immune surveillance, leading to immunotherapy failure [ ]. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) intricately regulate antitumor immune responses in the tumor immune microenvironment through interactions with different immune cell subsets. TAMs can activate immune checkpoints, downregulate antigen presentation, and secrete regulatory factors to coordinate CD8 + T-cell responses [ ]. In addition, Lymph node and tumor-associated PD-L1(+) macrophages antagonize dendritic cell vaccines by suppressing CD8(+) T cells [ ]. TAMs that secrete TGF-β1 (Transforming Growth Factor-beta 1) and CSF-1 (Macrophage Colony-Stimulating Factor) can promote the expansion of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), while TAMs also recruit immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs) and impairing natural killer T (NKT) cell function, fostering an immunosuppressive milieu [ – ]. However, the impact of hypoxia on macrophages is currently unclear, and understanding the mechanism by which hypoxia affects macrophages is crucial for identifying new immunotherapy targets for clinical cancer treatment.
We analyzed the differential gene expression of macrophages induced from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) of cervical cancer patients under hypoxic and normoxic conditions by mRNA sequencing and revealed a significant increase in SEMA6B expression. Recent research has confirmed that high SEMA6B expression is associated with poor prognosis and a tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients [ ]. Herein, we confirmed that high SEMA6B is associated with poor prognosis in cervical cancer patients and provided evidence that SEMA6B induces M2-polarized immune infiltration of macrophages to promote cervical cancer progression in vitro through the use of clinical data and experimental models combined with bioinformatics methods, indicating that SEMA6B could be a new immunotherapy target in cervical cancer.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture and transfection
HeLa cells (cervical adenocarcinoma, CADC), SiHa cells (cervical squamous cell carcinoma, CSCC), and the human monocyte line THP-1 were purchased from the American Type Culture Library (ATCC, Manassas, Virginia, USA) and cultured according to its guidelines. The THP-1 monocytes were differentiated to macrophages with 10 ng/ml phorbol-12-myristate-13-acetate (PMA) for 24 h. All cell lines were identified within 2 years via short tandem repeat analysis. The cells were placed under low oxygen tension (1% O2, 5% CO2, and 94% N2). After the cells were placed in a normoxic incubator (21% O2, 5% CO2, and 74% N2), Human-SEMA6B small interfering RNA (H-siRNA) was purchased from Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Mouse-SEMA6B small interfering RNA (M-SEMA6B-siRNA) was purchased from Shanghai GenePharma Co.,Ltd., and transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. All the siRNA target sequences are listed in Table .

## Human blood and tissue samples
The human body research plan was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Wuhan, China) (UHCT-IEC-SOP-016-03-01), and informed consent was obtained from all patients.
This study conducted tissue microarray, immunofluorescence, and survival analyses on paraffin-embedded surgical specimens from 58 cervical cancer patients admitted from September 2020 to May 2021 who underwent surgery in our hospital before initiating antitumor therapy and who did not receive radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or antiangiogenic treatment. All patients were pathologically diagnosed with primary cervical cancer, with no other malignant diseases or symptoms related to immune or hematopoietic dysfunction. The pathological characteristics of these cervical cancer patients are summarized in Table  . Patients with a follow-up period exceeding 6 months were included in the survival analysis. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to death from any cause, with living patients censored at the last follow-up. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from surgery to the first documented tumor recurrence/progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first, with event-free patients censored at the last follow-up.
 

## Murine bone marrow derived macrophage isolation and culture
Six-week-old wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 female mice were purchased from Shulaibao (Wuhan) Biotechnology Co., Ltd for DSS-induced colitis experiments and marrow derived macrophages (BMDM) isolation. To prepare bone BMDM, C57BL/6 mice were euthanized via carbon dioxide (CO2) inhalation. Marrow was flushed out from femurs and tibiae of hind legs using complete RPMI medium. After red blood cells were lysed, 106 cells/well (duplicate wells per sample) were seeded onto cell culture plates in complete RPMI medium with 20 ng/ml M-CSF and cultured for 6 days to prepare for subsequent experiments.

## Ficoll density gradient centrifugation for isolating peripheral blood mononuclear cells and inducing macrophages
The human body research plan was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Wuhan, China), and informed consent was obtained from all patients. We collected peripheral blood from cervical cancer patients and separated PBMCs using Ficoll Paque (TBD Science, Tianjin, China) density centrifugation. The collected PBMCs were placed in an incubator overnight cultivation; the cells were washed with RPMI 1640 (50 ng/mL M-CSF) once the next day to remove mixed lymphocytes; and a normal oxygen incubator (21% O2, 5% CO2, and 74% N2) was used. After 7 days of cultivation, monocytes develop into macrophages (Supplementary Fig. 1A). After 24 h of hypoxia and normoxia culture, the collected macrophages were collected for hematocrit determination and mRNA sequencing.

## mRNA sequencing (RNA-seq)
The collected TAMs treated with hypoxia or normoxia as described in Sect. 2.4 above were sent to Servicebio (Wuhan, China) for RNA-seq analysis via an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) were identified with a fold change (FC) ≥ 1.5 and adj-P < 0.05 as the cutoff criteria. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses were further conducted to interpret the biological significance of the DEGs.

## Database analysis
From The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (available at https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), RNA-Seq spectra and clinical information from cervical cancer patients were downloaded to determine their correlation with clinical characteristics and the prognosis of patients with SEMA6B and cervical cancer. It is a web-based platform for systematic analysis of immune infiltration in different cancer types in the TCGA. In this study, the expression of SEMA6B in TAMs and its correlation with tumor purity, M2 macrophages, Tregs infiltration, CD8 + T cells, and CD4 + T cells in cervical cancer were analyzed by using the TIMER database.

## Tissue Immunofluorescence
Immunofluorescence assays were performed on CD206 and SEMA6B cells using tissue microarrays. Tissue microarrays (TMA) were constructed using formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue cores from 58 patient samples, with 1 cores per patient. Each TMA block included 6 cores of normal tissue as internal controls. Briefly, after dewaxing, the slices were placed in antigen repair buffer under microwave irradiation at 15 °C for 90 min. Then, they were washed and sealed at 10 °C for 26 min and incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-CD206 (# ab313398; 1:1000; Abcam) and anti-semaphorin 6B (SEMA6B) (sc-390928; 1:200 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology) antibodies. The slides were incubated with Alexa Fluor 488- and 594-conjugated secondary antibiotics (A11034, A21125; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Immunostating was visualized under a slice scanner (Pannoramic MIDI: 3Dhistech, Hungary) on a scanning slide and evaluated by Image-Pro Plus Software (Media Cybernetics, Silver Springs, MD). The fluorescence intensities of SEMA6B, CD206, and DAPI were measured using a Zeiss scanning microscope. Using the CD206&SEMA6B/ CD206 ratio, a value < 0.3 was defined as a low content of CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs, and a value ≥ 0.3 was defined as a high content of CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs.

## Cellular Immunofluorescence
THP-1 cells were inoculated at a density of 2 × 104 cells /well into a 24-well plate, induced to differentiate into macrophages by adding PMA for 24 h, and incubated under hypoxic or normoxic conditions for 24 h at 37 °C. Then, the plate was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min. After washing twice with PBS and 1% BSA, the antigen was blocked at room temperature for 1 h and then incubated overnight with primary antibody (SEMA6B, sc-390928; 1:50 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology) at 4°C. Next, the plate was incubated with a secondary antibody (goat anti-rabbit IgG Cy3; 1:500 dilution, Abcam) at room temperature for 1 h and then incubated with DAPI for 15 min to stain the nucleus. Images were obtained using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus), and the average fluorescence intensity was quantified using Image J.

## Coculture determination
A Transwell system (6 Transwell boards, 0.4 µM aperture, Corning Life Sciences, USA) was used for indirect cocultivation. After macrophages (2.5 × 104 cells) were inoculated into the insert, cervical cancer cells (2.5 × 104 cells) were cultured in the basal chamber. The cocultures were incubated for 48 to 72 h, after which the cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

## Cell cycle assay
Cell cycle was analyzed using a cell cycle testing kit (Multisciences, Hangzhou, China). The detailed procedures refer to kit manufacturer’s instruction (Multisciences, Hangzhou, China). The cells were detected by flow cytometry according to the standard procedure. Generally, 2–30,000 cells were counted and the results were analyzed by FlowJo 10.9.0 software.

## RNA extraction and quantitative real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (qRT‒PCR)
Total cell RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (TaKaRa), and reverse transcription was performed (HiScript® III RT SuperMix Vazyme). The primers were synthesized by Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and their sequences are shown in Table . qRT‒PCR was performed using qPCR SYBR Green premix (Vazyme) on a Step-One Plus real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR system (Thermo Fisher). Three biological replicates were conducted.

## Western blotting
Whole-cell protein extraction and Western blotting were performed according to previous methods [ , ]. The antibodies used for protein blotting are listed in Table . The grayscale values of the protein bands were checked by ImageJ (ImageJ software, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland). Each experiment was conducted at least three times.

## Flow cytometry
After transfection with siRNA, macrophages differentiated from THP-1 monocytes cultured with cervical cancer cells for 48 h, single-cell suspensions were obtained and incubated with stained with BV421-conjugated-HLA-DR and APC-conjugated-CD163 antibodies and BMDMs stained with APC-conjugated CD206 and PE-Cy7-conjugated-F4/80 antibodies according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequently, the cells were analyzed by flow cytometry (FACSVerse 8, BD), and data analysis was performed using the FlowJo software (Version 7.6.1, Treestar, USA).

## 5-Ethynyl-20-deoxyuridine (EdU)
EdU determination was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions for the EdU experimental kit (RiboBio). Briefly, cells were plated in 96-well plates at 1 × 104 per well and incubated with 50 µM EdU for 2 h. Subsequently, the wells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde followed by Apollo staining and Hoechst33342 staining. The ratio of the number of EdU-positive nuclei and Hoechst-stained nuclei represents the cell proliferation rate. The experiment was performed in three biological replicates.

## Wound healing and transwell measurements
For the wound healing assay, cells were inoculated into a 6-well plate at 1 × 105 cells per well. After transfection with siRNA, the cells were cultured for 48 h. When the cells formed a converging monolayer, a 200 µL pipette suction head was used to scrape them. An inverted optical microscope (Olympus, Japan) was used to visualize the wound area at 0 and 24 h. ImageJ (version 1.51) software was used to measure the proportion of wound healing areas and represent the migration characteristics of cancer cells. For transwell measurements, 200 µL of serum-free medium containing 5 × Inoculate cells onto the upper surface of the chamber coated without (migration assay) or with (invasion assay) matrix glue, and 600 µL of complete culture medium was added to the bottom chamber. After incubation at 37 °C for 24 h, the cells were fixed through 8x using 4% paraformaldehyde. Then, the cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet. The number of cells in five random fields of view for each insert was calculated to evaluate the migration and invasion characteristics of the cancer cells at 200× magnification. Each experiment was conducted three times.

## Statistical analysis
All the statistical analyses were conducted using the R environment (version 3.6.3) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2). OS was analyzed by Kaplan-Meier (KM) estimation using the ‘survival’ and ‘survminer’ R packages, with log-rank tests for significance assessment. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to identify survival-associated genes. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to determine the relationships between survival-related genes. The associations between SEMA6B expression and clinicopathological features were evaluated using the Mann‒Whitney U test. Significant differences between different groups were evaluated through Student’s t tests. A P value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

## Results

## Hypoxia induced increased expression of SEMA6B in macrophages
We collected peripheral blood from cervical cancer patients, separated PBMCs and induced to macrophages (Fig. A). After 24 h of hypoxia (1% oxygen) and normoxia (21% oxygen) culture, the RNA sequencing results showed that 236 DEGs were upregulated and 385 DEGs were downregulated (|Log2FC|>1.5, adj-P < 0.05, Fig.  A, S1B). The 236 upregulated DEGs were subjected to GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. These genes were mainly enriched in positive regulation of response to external stimulus and cellular response to chemokine (Fig.  B, S1D, S1E). Thirty-one genes whose expression was significantly upregulated were identified (Fig.  C) and associated with OS through the TCGA database (Fig.  D). To evaluate the correlation between the 31 OS-related genes, Pearson correlation analysis was conducted, and 31 identified key genes exhibited a coexpression pattern (Fig. C). Semaphorins play important roles in regulating various immune system responses. Tian et al. revealed that SEMA3A drives alternative macrophage activation in the resolution of periodontitis via PI3K/AKT/mTOR Signaling [ ]. SEMA3G exacerbates joint inflammation through the accumulation and proliferation of macrophages in the synovium [ ]. To validate the RNA sequencing findings, cellular immunofluorescence was performed using a SEMA6B-specific antibody. The results showed a significant increase in red fluorescence intensity in macrophages cultured under hypoxic conditions compared to those under normoxic conditions (Fig.  E), confirming that hypoxia induced the upregulation of SEMA6B in macrophages.
 

## Validation of the association between CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs and the progression of cervical cancer
In order to study the expression pattern of SEMA6B in cervical cancer tissues and its clinical implications, we also performed the tissue immunofluorescence assay to examine the SEMA6B expression in human cervical cancer tissue array containing 58 patient samples. CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs were mainly located in the cervical cancer tissue matrix (Fig.  A). We confirmed that the lower the percentage of CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs was, the better the OS (HR = 0.10, 95% CI 0.01–0.74, P = 0.024, Fig.  B) and PFS (HR = 0.10, 95% CI 0.01–0.76, P = 0.026, Fig.  C) were for cervical cancer patients. We also found that abundant CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs were associated with late clinical stage (P = 0.030), lymph node metastasis (P = 0.015), poor differentiation (P = 0.001), and lymphovascular space invasion (P = 0.048) in cervical cancer tissues (Table  ; Fig.  D-G).
 

## Databases confirm that SEMA6B is associated with poor prognosis and immune infiltration in cervical cancer patients
According to the analysis of the TCGA dataset, the OS, PFS and disease-specific survival (DSS) of cervical cancer patients with high expression of SEMA6B were significantly reduced (P = 0.008, 0.038 and 0.025 respectively) (Fig.  A). Multivariate analysis revealed that high SEMA6B expression was closely related to lymph node metastasis and late pathological T stage in cervical cancer patients (P = 0.002 and < 0.003, respectively) (Fig.  B). The above results all indicate that SEMA6B is associated with poor prognosis in cervical cancer. To further determine the correlation between SEMA6B and immune infiltration, we analyzed the relationships between SEMA6B and different immune cell marker genes using the TIMER database. After correlation adjustment through purity, the results showed that SEMA6B mainly expressed in Neutrophils, Mast cell, Eosinophils, Effective Memory T Cell(Tem) and macrophages (Fig.  C and D) and was positively correlated with the infiltration of M2 macrophages and Tregs and negatively correlated with the infiltration of CD8 + T cells and CD4 + T cells (Fig.  E). In addition, qPCR results showed that knocking down SEMA6B downregulates PD-L1 and interleukin 10 (IL-10) while upregulates IFN–γin macrophages (Fig.  F). These results indicate that SEMA6B plays a crucial role in promoting the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.
 

## Knocking down SEMA6B inhibits macrophage M2 polarization and migration by inhibiting AKT phosphorylation
Hypoxia can clearly promote M2 polarization of macrophages [ ]. In the TIMER database, we found a positive correlation between SEMA6B expression and M2 macrophage infiltration. Therefore, we suspect whether SEMA6B can promote M2 polarization or increase migration of macrophages. We verified that knocking down SEMA6B resulted in a decrease in the proportion of CD163 + macrophages differentiated from THP-1 monocytes (Fig.  A) and CD206 + macrophages in BMDM (Fig.  B), indicating that knocking down SEMA6B can inhibit macrophages M2 polarization. PCR revealed that knocking down SEMA6B resulted in a decreasing expression of M2 markers (ARG-1 and CD206) in macrophages (Fig.  C). The migration ability of macrophages was evaluated by transwell assays, and as expected, SEMA6B-knockdown macrophages exhibited reduced migration ability in macrophages (Fig.  D and E). Previous studies have confirmed that AKT phosphorylation are associated with macrophage polarization [ ]. We further determined that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibited AKT phosphorylation (Fig.  F). Chemokines serve as the main cytokines involved in communication between TAMs and cervical cancer cells. Previous studies have confirmed that CXCL2 and CXCL8 were downstream genes of AKT in macrophages [ , ]. We further determined that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages could reduce the expression of CXCL2 and CXCL8 in macrophages (Fig.  G). These results suggested that SEMA6B could regulate macrophage M2 polarization.
 

## Knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibits the proliferation of cervical cancer cells
After coculture of macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown and cervical cancer cells, the proliferation of SiHa and HeLa cells was significantly reduced according to EdU proliferation assay (Fig.  A and B). Flow cytometry analysis showed that the number of SiHa and HeLa cells in the G0/G1 phase was significantly increased while S phase was significantly decreased (Fig.  C and D). Taken together, these results demonstrated that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibits the proliferation of cervical cancer cells.
 

## Knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibits the migration and invasion of cervical cancer cells
After coculture of macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown and cervical cancer cells, the invasion and migration abilities of SiHa and HeLa cells were significantly reduced according to Transwell assays (Fig.  A and C). The migration ability of SiHa and HeLa cells was significantly reduced according to the scratch assay (Fig.  B and D). Western blot analysis revealed that E-cadherin expression increased, while N-cadherin and vimentin expression decreased in SiHa and HeLa cells after coculture with macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown (Fig.  E). These findings suggest that macrophage-expressed SEMA6B may contribute to cervical cancer progression by enhancing tumor cell motility and EMT in vitro.
 

## Discussion
Although immunotherapy for cervical cancer has achieved significant clinical success, its clinical application is greatly limited due to its low response rate, low applicability and severe adverse reactions [ ]. The development of novel immune biomarkers and targets for the diagnosis and immunotherapy of cervical cancer is highly important. This study provides the first evidence that hypoxia enhances SEMA6B expression in macrophages induced from PBMCs of cervical cancer patients and knocking down SEMA6B attenuates the progression of cervical cancer through regulating macrophage M2 polarization in vitro.
Hypoxia plays an important role in the recurrence and metastasis of malignant tumors. Multiple studies have confirmed that hypoxia stimulates cancer cells to undergo mitotic arrest, allowing them to escape immune monitoring and form an immunosuppressive microenvironment, leading to recurrence, chemotherapy resistance, and distant metastasis [ , ]. Multiple studies have confirmed that a large number of tumor-related macrophages aggregate in the hypoxic zone of tumor tissue and play important roles in the occurrence, growth, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and lymphangiogenesis of tumors [ ]. TAMs are attractive targets for tumor immunotherapy, but the lack of specific biomarkers hinders their research and clinical application. Understanding the mechanism by which hypoxia affects TAMs and identifying specific biomarkers are highly important for the diagnosis, recurrence, and metastasis of cervical cancer. From a structural point of view, these semaphorin proteins contain a highly conserved N-terminal semaphorin domain of approximately 500 amino acids, a plexin–semaphorin–integrin domain and distinct protein domains that further define semaphorins, including immunoglobulin-like, thrombospondin, and basic C-terminal domains [ ]. In this study, we found a significant increase in SEMA6B expression in macrophages induced from PBMCs of cervical cancer patients under hypoxia conditions. Previous studies have confirmed that SEMA4B is a direct target of hypoxia inducible Factor 1 (HIF-1) α, a hypoxia responsive element (HRE) that can recognize the SEMA4B gene [ ]. However, SEMA3A is expressed at low levels in hypoxia-treated cardiomyocytes due to hypoxia-induced inflammatory factors TNF-α and IL-1β, which are related to IL-6 secretion, decreased cell viability, myocardial cell apoptosis, ROS release, decreased ATP production, and a decreased GSH/GSSG ratio [ ]. Therefore, different SEMA proteins may play different roles in hypoxic microenvironments.
According to the analysis of the TCGA dataset, the prognosis of cervical cancer patients with high expression of SEMA6B is poor. High expression of SEMA6B was closely related to lymph node metastasis and late pathological T stage in cervical cancer patients. Another noteworthy finding of this study is that SEMA6B is positively correlated with the infiltration of M2 macrophages and Tregs and negatively correlated with CD8 + T cells and CD4 + T cells, indicating that SEMA6B plays a crucial role in immune escape by promoting the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. These results are consistent with those of previous studies by Li et al., who confirmed a moderate to strong positive correlation between SEMA6B expression levels and infiltration levels in macrophages, MDSCs, NK cells, Tregs and Th6 cells in colorectal cancer and a significant positive correlation between infiltration levels of CD4 + T cells, neutrophils, and dendritic cells and SEMA6B expression in colorectal cancer [ ]. To further explore the effect of SEMA6B on T cells in macrophages, we detected the effect of SEMA6B on PD-L1, IL-10 and IFN-γ by PCR and the result showed that knocking out SEMA6B could significantly downregulate PD-L1, IL-10, Simultaneously upregulating IFN–γ. Previous studies have shown that IFN - γ can increase the motility of antigen-specific CD8 + T cells towards antigen expressing (target) cells and enhance the killing ability of target cells. When IFN-γ+/+ and IFN-γ-/-CD8 T cells were incubated with target cells, the authors observed a significant increase in the effectiveness of IFN-γ competent cells. Adding anti IFN-γ antibodies to co culture systems can significantly reduce target cell killing [ ]. Macrophages regulate their phagocytic and antigen presentation functions through the PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint, promoting tumor cell evasion and clearance. At the same time, the interaction between PD-1/PD-L1 promotes CD8 + T cell exclusion and suppresses tumor immunity [ ]. TAMs, which accounts for 50% of the tumor mass, causes the functional exhaustion of T lymphocytes through the secretion of interleukin-10 (IL-10), forming a tumor immune suppressive microenvironment and leading to tumor progression [ ].
We found that the expression of SEMA6B in macrophages significantly increased after hypoxia treatment and further revealed that abundant CD206 + SEMA6B + TAMs were associated with poor prognosis, late clinical stage, lymph node metastasis, poor differentiation, and lymphovascular space invasion in cervical cancer tissues. The proliferation, invasion and migration ability of SiHa and HeLa cells significantly decreased after coculture with macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown. In summary, our research suggested that high SEMA6B expression predicts poor OS and may serve as a potential prognostic immune marker for cervical cancer patients.
Cheng et al.‘s study confirmed that hypothalamic exosomes were transmitted to surrounding cells, which resulted in a significant increase in lncHAR1B level, thereby relieving the dysfunction of endothelial cells and promoting the switch from M1 to M2 macrophages under high glucose conditions [ ]. Zhang et al.‘s study showed that low oxygen pressure promotes the generation of M2 macrophages, which further promotes cancer progression and temozolomide (TMZ) resistance in GBM by secreting vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). Specifically, clinical data indicate that M2 macrophages are significantly enriched in GBM tissue compared to normal tissue adjacent to cancer. The following in vitro experiments have validated that low oxygen pressure promotes M2 polarized macrophages by upregulating hypoxia inducible factor-1 α (HIF-1α) [ ]. Previous studies have confirmed that the SEMA family mainly acts on the integrins β1, NRP and plexin C1 receptors to regulate various physiological and pathological processes [ – ]. Kang et al. revealed the important roles of the mTOR-SEMA6D-PPARγ signaling pathway in controlling macrophage polarization [ ]. Another study suggested that the association of SEMA4D with M2 macrophages may help stimulate tumor angiogenesis in epithelial ovarian cancer [ ]. SEMA3A can bind to the NP1-1 receptor of TAMs, leading to downstream PI3K/Akt phosphorylation, continuous recruitment of monocytes and M2 polarization, thereby promoting cancer cell resistance to androgen deprivation therapy [ ]. A recent study confirmed that hypoxia therapy can polarize macrophages toward the M2 phenotype and promote the development of keloids, mainly by activating the PTEN-PI3K/AKT pathway [ ]. This research result is consistent with our findings.
Previous studies have confirmed that recombinant SEMA3A can induce AKT by binding to a complex of the T-cell surface receptors NRP-1 and PlexinA1- κ to inhibit B signal transduction [ ]. AKIP1 regulates NF-κB via the CXCL1, CXCL8 and AKT pathways and promotes GBM activity, migration, and chemical radiation resistance [ ]. Previous studies have confirmed that malaria parasite infection in female BWF1 lupus mice regulates the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis and its downstream signals PI3K/AKT, NF-κB and ERK to weaken B-cell autoreactivity [ ]. Atelectasis causes an alveolar hypoxia-induced inflammatory response, mainly through the secretion of NF-κB by pulmonary epithelial cells, which is dependent on increased CXCL-1 secretion [ ]. Our study discovered that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages can inhibit macrophage M2 polarization and inhibit macrophage migration by inhibiting AKT phosphorylation in vitro.
Previous studies have confirmed that SEMA3D promotes liver cancer cell proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, and lymphangiogenesis through PI3k/AKT [ ]. SEMA6D can reduce the number of cardiac muscle cells in mouse models by downregulating the levels of cyclin D1 and cyclin D2 [ ]. Knocking down SEMA7A/β1 integrin can inhibit endothelial cell apoptosis and EMT occurrence [ ]. Knocking down SEMA7A/β1 integrin can inhibit endothelial cell apoptosis and EMT occurrence. In this study, we found that after coculture of macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown and cervical cancer cells, the proliferation of SiHa and HeLa cells was significantly reduced and flow cytometry analysis showed that the number of SiHa and HeLa cells in the G0/G1 phase was significantly increased while S phase was significantly decreased in vitro. Taken together, these results demonstrated that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages inhibits the proliferation of cervical cancer cells. However, further research is needed to understand how SEMA6B in macrophages regulates the proliferation cycle of cervical cancer cells.
EMT is the process by which cancer cells acquire an invasive phenotype leading to metastasis. During the EMT process, transformed epithelial cells acquire mesenchymal features and are transformed into metastatic cells. One hallmark of metastatic cells is the loss of expression of epithelial markers (such as E-cadherin) and induction of mesenchymal markers (including N-cadherin, vimentin, and Zeb1), E-cadherin plays a potential role in inhibiting invasion, and its regulation may lead to inhibition of tumor progression [ ]. SEMA6D can upregulate ZEB1 SNAI2, ZEB2 and TWIST promote EMT in breast cancer [ ]. Previous studies have shown that SEMA4A can regulate the secretion of IL-10 in stromal cells and, in combination with IL-10, promote prostate cancer invasion by inducing epithelial mesenchymal transition [ ]. SEMA3D and PlxnD1 have been shown to promote the metastasis of various types of cancer and regulate the EMT of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma [ ]. Recent studies have confirmed that overexpression of SEMA6B significantly increases the levels of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, in RAW264.7 cells IL-10, IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-2, IL-12, Chemokine CCL2 and adhesion molecule ICAM-1. On the contrary, knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages, as well as primary SEMA6B knockout macrophages, resulted in significant downregulation of these cytokines [ ]. We revealed that after coculture of macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown and cervical cancer cells, the invasion and migration abilities of SiHa and HeLa cells were significantly reduced. Moreover, E-cadherin expression increased, while N-cadherin and vimentin expression decreased in SiHa and HeLa cells after coculture with macrophages with SEMA6B knockdown. We further determined that knocking down SEMA6B can reduce the expression of CXCL2 and CXCL8 in macrophages. CXCL2 and CXCL8 promote the occurrence of EMT during the progression of tumors. Previous studies have confirmed that M2 macrophages further activate the TGF-β/Smad2/3 pathway through CXCL2 to promote EMT in colorectal cancer [ ] CXCL8 is a key chemokine secreted by TAMs. CXCL8 can enhance the migration, invasion and epithelial mesenchymal transformation (EMT) of human breast cancer breast cancer [ ]. We speculate that knocking down SEMA6B in macrophages to inhibit cervical cancer EMT may be closely related to these cytokines.
In summary, this study revealed that knocking down SEMA6B may attenuate the progression of cervical cancer through regulating macrophage M2 polarization. The mechanism of action of SEMA6B on the progression of cervical cancer in vivo still needs further research. Targeting SEMA6B may be a potential immunotherapy approach for treating cervical cancer. However, the mechanism of SEMA6B in the hypoxic tumor immune microenvironment of cervical cancer and its potential target for targeting TAMs still need further exploration. In addition, these findings require additional preclinical models, clinical patient cohorts, and clinical trials for further validation.

## Limitations of the study
There are several limitations of the present study that we should consider when we interpret the results. Firstly, this study mainly utilized in vitro experiments and human tissue specimens, further in vivo experiments are needed to confirm the effect of SEMA6B on the progression of cervical cancer. Secondly, the limited number of clinical samples included may bias the results, and further clinical translation requires the inclusion of more clinical samples to further confirm our conclusions.
