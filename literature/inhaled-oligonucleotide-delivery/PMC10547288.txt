## Abstract

## Background
The renin‐angiotensin system plays a crucial role in the development of heart failure, and Ang II (angiotensin II) acts as the critical effector of the renin‐angiotensin system in regulating cardiac fibrosis. However, the mechanisms of cardiac fibrosis are complex and still not fully understood. IGF1R (insulin‐like growth factor 1 receptor) has multiple functions in maintaining cardiovascular homeostasis, and low‐dose IGF1 treatment is effective in relieving Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis. Here, we aimed to investigate the molecular mechanism of IGF1R in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis.

## Methods and Results
Using primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells and fibroblasts, in vitro experiments were performed. Using C57BL/6J mice and clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/CRISPR‐associated 9 (Cas9)‐mediated IGF1R heterozygous knockout (Igf1r
+/−) mice, cardiac fibrosis mouse models were induced by Ang II for 2 weeks. The expression of IGF1R was examined by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, immunohistochemistry, and Western blot. Mice heart histologic changes were evaluated using Masson and picro sirius red staining. Fibrotic markers and signal molecules indicating the function of the Akt (protein kinase B)/ERK (extracellular signal‐regulated kinase)/nuclear factor‐κB pathway were detected using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction and Western blot. RNA sequencing was used to explore IGF1R‐mediated target genes in the hearts of mice, and the association of IGF1R and G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5 was identified by coimmunoprecipitation. More important, blocking IGF1R signaling significantly suppressed endothelial‐mesenchymal transition in primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells and mice in response to transforming growth factor‐β1 or Ang II, respectively. Deficiency or inhibition of IGF1R signaling remarkably attenuated Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts and mice. We further observed that the patients with heart failure exhibited higher blood levels of IGF1 and IGF1R than healthy individuals. Moreover, Ang II treatment significantly increased cardiac IGF1R in wild type mice but led to a slight downregulation in Igf1r

+/−
 mice. Interestingly, IGF1R deficiency significantly alleviated cardiac fibrosis in Ang II–treated mice. Mechanistically, the phosphorylation level of Akt and ERK was upregulated in Ang II–treated mice, whereas blocking IGF1R signaling in mice inhibited these changes of Akt and ERK phosphorylation. Concurrently, phosphorylated p65 of nuclear factor‐κB exhibited similar alterations in the corresponding group of mice. Intriguingly, IGF1R directly interacted with G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5, and this association decreased ≈50% in Igf1r

+/−
 mice. In addition, Grk5 deletion downregulated expression of the Akt/ERK/nuclear factor‐κB signaling pathway in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts.

## Conclusions
IGF1R signaling deficiency alleviates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, at least partially through inhibiting endothelial‐mesenchymal transition via the Akt/ERK/nuclear factor‐κB pathway. Interestingly, G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5 associates with IGF1R signaling directly, and it concurrently acts as an IGF1R downstream effector. This study suggests the promising potential of IGF1R as a therapeutic target for cardiac fibrosis.

Keywords: angiotensin II, cardiac fibrosis, G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5, insulin‐like growth factor 1 receptor, protein kinase B/extracellular signal‐regulated kinase/nuclear factor‐κB pathway

## Research Perspective

## What Is New?

Blocking IGF1R (insulin‐like growth factor 1 receptor) signaling significantly suppressed endothelial‐mesenchymal transition in primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells and mice in response to transforming growth factor‐β1 or angiotensin II, whereas deficiency or inhibition of IGF1R signaling significantly attenuated angiotensin II–induced cardiac fibrosis in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts and mice.
The patients with heart failure exhibited higher blood levels of IGF1 and IGF1R than healthy individuals, whereas angiotensin II–treated IGF1R‐deficient mice displayed a lower level of cardiac IGF1R than wild type mice.
IGF1R signaling deficiency alleviates angiotensin II–induced cardiac fibrosis, at least partially through inhibiting endothelial‐mesenchymal transition via the protein kinase B/extracellular signal‐regulated kinase/nuclear factor‐κB pathway. Interestingly, G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5 associates with IGF1R signaling directly and simultaneously acts as an IGF1R downstream effector.

## What Question Should Be Addressed Next?

Targeting IGF1R or its downstream effector G protein–coupled receptor kinase 5, may be a promising treatment strategy for cardiac fibrosis.

Cardiac fibrosis is a hallmark of cardiovascular disease and contributes to the progression of heart failure (HF), whereas the renin‐angiotensin system plays a key role in the progression of HF, and angiotensin II (Ang II) is crucial in regulating cardiac fibrosis. Moreover, cardiac fibrosis is characterized by the collagen deposition in the extracellular matrix, particularly collagen I (Col I) and collagen III (Col III), which leads to the loss of organ architecture and compliance, induction of pathologic signaling, and eventual HF. Recently, endothelial‐mesenchymal transition (EndMT) is reported to be 1 of the mechanisms of organ fibrosis, which can be triggered during cardiac fibrosis. This leads to the activation of cardiac fibroblasts and then differentiation into α‐smooth muscle actin (α‐SMA)–expressing cardiac fibroblasts. , Further study reveals that EndMT is characterized by the downregulation of endothelial markers, such as endothelial cell adhesion molecule (CD31) and vascular endothelial (VE)–cadherin, and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers, such as α‐SMA and fibronectin. Increasingly, studies have shown that Ang II has a direct effect on cardiac fibroblast function. , , , In addition, Ang II has a strong indirect effect on collagen production in cardiac fibroblasts, contributing to cardiac fibroblast differentiation. Hence, Ang II is speculated to play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis. However, therapeutic drugs that specifically target the Ang II–mediated heart fibrosis are currently unavailable. Thus, the exploration of this aspect of treatment would be helpful to prevent the progression of HF.
It is reported that insulin‐like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF1R) plays a critical role in the maintenance of cardiovascular homeostasis. , , Moreover, the activation of IGF1R could promote physiological cardiac growth and improve contractile function, whereas the deficiency of cardiac IGF1R resulted in a reduced hypertrophic response to exercise training or myocardial pathologic features associated with delayed aging. Furthermore, the continuous myocardial expression of IGF1 in transgenic mice led to a decrease of systolic performance and an increase of interstitial fibrosis. The overexpression of cardiac IGF1R signaling could alleviate diastolic dysfunction and cardiac fibrosis in diabetes, and the IGF1R/protein kinase B (Akt) signaling pathway could be a therapeutic target for treating diabetic cardiomyopathy. In addition, IGF1R activates the phosphoinositide 3‐kinase–Akt signaling pathway, thereby promoting myocardium survival, hypertrophy, and aging. , , Collectively, these data demonstrate that cardiac IGF1R could be a promising target of the heart in the treatment of various cardiovascular diseases. However, the molecular mechanism of IGF1R in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis is still not fully defined and requires further exploration.
The mechanisms of fibrosis are complex, and various pathways mediate in the progression of fibrosis, such as TGF‐β(transforming growth factor β)/SMAD(suppressor of mother against decapentaplegic)/SLUG(snail family transcriptional repressor 2) signalling, Notch signalling, and the Wnt(wingless‐related integration site)/β‐catenin signaling pathway. , , , , , These pathways further activate EndMT and promote organ fibrosis. Intriguingly, endothelial fibroblast growth factor receptor 1 and SIRT3 (sirtuin 3) signaling could induce a protective effect against the activation of EndMT and organ fibrosis; , thus, fibroblast growth factor receptor 1 is considered as a key regulator of fibrosis. Nevertheless, it remains undetermined if IGF1R is a regulator of fibrosis, and the understanding of Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis is still limited. Although angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors and Ang II receptor blockers are efficient in treating fibrosis of the heart in human and animal models of HF, they are yet to be approved for the treatments of cardiac fibrosis. In addition, N‐acetyl‐seryl‐aspartyl‐lysyl‐proline, an endogenous peptide, which has a potential antifibrotic effect, is a therapeutic candidate in inhibiting cardiac fibrosis and kidney fibrosis. Hence, the treatment options for cardiac fibrosis are hampered by its unclear mechanism and undefined molecular targets.
In this study, we aim to clarify the hypothesis that (1) IGF1R deficiency alleviates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts and mice, and that (2) Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis can be attenuated by targeting IGF1R through the Akt/ERK (extracellular signal‐regulated kinase)/NF (nuclear factor)‐κB pathway.

## Methods
The data in this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

## Isolation of Primary Neonatal Mice Fibroblasts and Small Interfering RNA Transfection
Primary cardiac fibroblasts from neonatal mice within 48 hours were obtained as previously reported. , , To knock down Igf1r or Grk5, transfections with small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting Igf1r or Grk5 were performed in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX following the manufacturer's instructions (Genepharma, Shanghai, China). The cells were cultured into 6‐well plates for 24 hours until they reached 60% to 80% confluence. The culture medium was then replaced with 1.5 mL Opti‐MEM (Gibco) and 500 μL siRNA‐Lipofectamine complex solution to obtain the final concentration (100 nmol/L) of siRNA. After 6 hours of incubation, the medium was replaced with 10% fetal bovine serum–containing culture medium and cultured for another 24 hours before harvesting.

## Primary Mouse Cardiac Microvascular Endothelial Cells and Treatment
Primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells were purchased from Procell Life Science and Technology (Wuhan, China) and treated with TGF‐β1 (No. CK33; Novoprotein, China) and IGF1R blocker OSI‐906 (HY‐10191S; MCE) for 24 hours.

## Ethical Statement
Animal experiments were conducted per the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, published by the Shandong Provincial People's Government, and the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (National Institutes of Health No. 8523, revised 1978). The experimental procedures were approved by the Weifang Medical University Animal Care and Use Committee (No. 2020SDL163). Mice were kept at the Centre of Experimental Animals of Weifang Medical University (Weifang, China) in a 12‐hour light‐dark cycle in a temperature‐controlled room. Using isoflurane inhalation (3%), the effect of anesthesia was evaluated in terms of the reflexes, respiratory rate, and heart rate of the mice. Mice were euthanized with carbon dioxide after heart excision.
Human blood sample collection was approved by the Weifang Medical University Human Ethics Committee (No. 2022YX040). All participants provided written consent before their inclusion in the study. This study was performed in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (1996, and revised 2013).

## Generation of IGF1R Heterozygous Knockout (Igf1r

+/−
) Mice
IGF1R heterozygous knockout mouse model (C57BL/6J; Igf1r

+/−
) was obtained from Cyagen Biosciences (Guangzhou, China). Igf1r

+/−
 mice were constructed using CRISPR/Cas9‐mediated gene editing method. Briefly, 21 exons were identified with the ATG start codon in exon 1 and the TGA stop codon in exon 21, and the target sites were exon 3 to exon 20 and exon 1 to exon 21. Two pairs of genomic RNAs to vectors were generated and identified using RNA sequencing. Cas9‐mediated mRNA and generated genomic RNAs were injected together into fertilized eggs. The genotype of the pups was identified using polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The zygote was injected with the genomic RNA to mouse Igf1r and Cas9 mRNA to produce targeted knockout offspring. After PCR identification and sequencing, F2 founders were placed into the reproductive system of wild type (WT) mice for breeding, and the resulting F3 mice were identified. The genotyping for heterozygous IGF1R knockout (Igf1r

+/−
) mice was performed using PCR with specific primers: mouse Igf1r‐forward: GCCTCCTCTCTTCATCACTTTGTATGC; Igf1r‐reverse: AACGGCCTTCTCTAATGGCTCCC. DNA samples were amplified using PCR products with different DNA sizes for Igf1r

+/−
 (560/778 bp). To avoid the effect of sex on experiments, 8‐ to 10‐week‐old male WT and Igf1r

+/−
 mice were used in this study.

## Animal Experiments
OSI‐906 (linsitinib) is a potent selective inhibitor of the insulin receptor and IGF1R. , To investigate the role of IGF1R in cardiac fibrosis, we used 8‐week‐old male C57BL/6J mice subjected to the subcutaneous injection of Ang II (2 mg/kg per day) with or without vehicle PBS and with OSI‐906‐treatment (50 mg/kg per day) for 2 weeks. Mice (n=30) were randomly divided into 3 groups: (1) vehicle mice (PBS control); (2) Ang II mice (cardiac fibrosis mouse model); and (3) Ang II mice with OSI‐906 treatment.
To further explore the underlying mechanism of IGF1R in cardiac fibrosis, we constructed an IGF1R heterozygous knockout mouse model by inactivating Igf1r gene in mutant mice (Igf1r
+/−). Cardiac fibrosis mouse model was constructed by the subcutaneous injection of Ang II in Igf1r

+/−
 mice at the dose of 2 mg/kg per day with or without vehicle PBS for 2 weeks. The 8‐week‐old male WT mice (n=49) and Igf1r

+/−
 mice (n=46) were randomly assigned into 4 groups: (1) vehicle WT mice, littermates were age matched and negative for Igf1r deficiency; (2) Igf1r
+/− mice; (3) Ang II–treated WT mice; and (4) Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice.

## Human Blood Samples and ELISA

A total of 14 pairs of human blood samples were collected from patients with HF and healthy individuals in 2022, at the First Affiliated Hospital, Weifang Medical University. All patients with HF were diagnosed by the experienced clinical cardiologists. Demographic information (sex and age) and echocardiographic parameters are listed in Table  . The blood concentrations of IGF1 and IGF1R in patients with HF were measured using ELISA following the manufacturer's instructions (Western Bio‐Tech Co, Shanghai, China).
 

## Histologic Analysis and Immunohistochemistry
Mice histologic changes of the heart were evaluated using Masson and picro sirius red staining. Briefly, heart ventricles were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (pH=7.4) for 24 hours. After paraffin embedding, heart sections (5 μm thick) were stained with Masson and picro sirius red for collagen deposition analysis. For immunohistochemistry, the heart sections were heated using the pressure cooker method for antigen retrieval, incubated with anti‐IGF1R (CST; No. 3069) followed by incubation with goat anti‐rabbit horseradish peroxidase reagent, and stained using a 3,3'‐diaminobenzidine (DAB) detection kit.

## Quantitative Reverse Transcription PCR
The quantitative reverse transcription PCR experiments were performed according to the previous report. Total RNA was obtained by TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) from left ventricular tissues of mice and cardiac cells. For the quantification of mRNA levels for the activated gene markers, the ReverTraAce qPCR‐RT Kit (Toyobo, Japan) and SYBR Green Real‐time PCR Master Mix (Toyobo) were used to convert RNA into complementary DNA, and quantitative PCR was performed. The quantitative reverse transcription PCR was performed and analyzed by Light Cycle 480 II (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). β‐Actin was used as a negative control. The primers used in this study are presented in Table  .
 

## Coimmunoprecipitation and Western Blot
The proteins in heart ventricles and cells were extracted by radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer. Lysates were stirred gently in ice chamber for 40 minutes. The supernatants were collected after centrifugation at 13 400g at 4 °C for 20 minutes. The concentrations of protein samples were determined by a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay kit (Thermo Scientific) and incubated at 95 °C with a loading buffer for 10 minutes for denaturation. Proteins (molecular weight, >10 kD) were electrophoretic on 10% Tris‐glycine‐SDS‐PAGE, then transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. The membranes were blocked for 1 hour at room temperature with nonfat milk (5%) and Tris‐buffered saline/Tween‐20 (pH=7.4). Protein bands were determined by the Chemiluminescence Fluor Chem FC3 system (Protein Simple). For coimmunoprecipitation, heart ventricles were extracted and examined as in the previous study. Furthermore, the following antibodies were used: anti‐IGF1R (CST; No. 3069), anti–phosphorylated Akt (CST; No. 4060), anti‐Akt (CST; No. 4691), anti–NF (nuclear factor)‐κB (CST; No. 8242), anti–phosphorylated NF‐κB (CST; No. 3033), anti‐ERK (CST; No. 4695T), anti–phosphorylated ERK (CST; No. 4370T), anti–G‐protein–coupled receptor kinase 5 (GRK5) (Santa; sc‐518 005), or anti‐GAPDH (ZSGB; TA‐08). The concentration of the above antibodies was 1:1000, except for the anti‐GRK5 (1:500).

## Statistical Analysis
On the basis of the assessment of the normal distribution of the data, all experiments were performed at least in triplicate. Data are presented as mean±SD, and analyzed by Student's t test for unpaired groups. For multiple groups, 1‐ or 2‐way ANOVA, followed by a post‐Tukey test, was performed. P<0.05 was regarded as statistically significant, and P<0.05 values are shown in the figures.

## Results

## Deficiency or Inhibition of IGF1R Signaling Suppressed EndMT and Ang II–Induced Cardiac Fibrosis
It is reported that Ang II induces EndMT and promotes cardiac fibrosis. To determine the effects of IGF1R signaling on Ang II–induced EndMT in vivo, we evaluated the expressions of EndMT markers (CD31, VE‐cadherin, claudin‐5, α‐SMA, collagen 1a, and fibronectin) in the hearts of mice using quantitative reverse transcription PCR assay. As shown in Figure  and , the expressions of endothelial markers (CD31, VE‐cadherin, and claudin‐5) were significantly decreased in WT mice treated with Ang II, whereas Igf1r
+/− mice showed no reduction in these mRNA levels, and the expressions of mesenchymal markers (ɑ‐SMA, collagen 1a, and fibronectin) were increased in Ang II–treated WT mice but not Igf1r
+/−mice. Recently, TGF‐β1 is considered the major inducer of EndMT. Furthermore, to identify the role of IGF1R signaling on EndMT in vitro, primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells were induced with TGF‐β1 (40 mg/L) and treated with IGF1R blocker OSI‐906 (3 μmol/L). As shown in Figure  and , TGF‐β1 induced the downregulation of mRNA encoding CD31, VE‐cadherin, and claudin‐5, and the upregulation of ɑ‐SMA, collagen 1a, and fibronectin, in primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells compared with vehicle‐control group, whereas OSI‐906 treatment remarkably reversed these alterations in the model group. Collectively, our results demonstrate that blocking IGF1R signaling at least partly suppresses cardiac EndMT in primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells and mice in response to TGF‐β1 or Ang II, respectively.
Fibroblasts play a critical role in the collagen deposition in the extracellular matrix, and α‐SMA is a mesenchymal marker indicating the early stage of EndMT. In this study, to investigate the role of IGF1R signaling in cardiac fibrosis, we used primary cardiac fibroblasts from neonatal mice within 48 hours, which were transfected by siRNA with Igf1r knockdown and were treated with and without Ang II (Figure  ). Interestingly, Ang II led to a significant increase of Col I, Col III, and periostin at the mRNA level in primary cardiac fibroblasts compared with vehicle‐control group, whereas Ang II–treated fibroblasts with Igf1r knockdown reduced these fibrotic responses compared with Ang II–treated group (Figure  ). Moreover, Ang II resulted in a significant increase of α‐SMA both at the mRNA and protein levels in primary cardiac fibroblasts, but caused a remarkable decrease of α‐SMA in Ang II–treated deficient mice compared with Ang II–treated WT mice (Figure  and ). We further used OSI‐906 treatment to blunt IGF1R signaling in the hearts of mice with or without Ang II induction for 2 weeks (Figure  ). Interestingly, Ang II led to a higher level of cardiac IGF1R in mice, whereas OSI‐906 treatment reduced the expression of IGF1R in Ang II–treated mice (Figure  and ). Moreover, Masson staining was used to investigate the role of IGF1R signaling in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, and the results showed that OSI‐906 treatment could significantly inhibit perivascular fibrosis and interstitial fibrosis in the Ang II–treated mice compared with the control mice (Figure  and ). Similarly, Ang II resulted in a significant increase of Col I, Col III, periostin, and α‐SMA at the mRNA level in mice, but OSI‐906 treatment reduced these fibrotic responses compared with Ang II–treated mice (Figure  ). In addition, Ang II elevated the expression of α‐SMA at the protein level, but OSI‐906 treatment reversed this alteration in Ang II–treated mice (Figure  and ). Collectively, our findings suggest that inhibition of IGF1R signaling can attenuate Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis induced by Ang II.
 

## Increased Expression of IGF1R in Patients With HF and Ang II–Treated Mice
To investigate the role of IGF1R in HF, we detected the blood concentrations of IGF1 and IGF1R in patients with HF by ELISA assay. As shown in Figure  and , patients with HF had a higher blood level of both IGF1 and IGF1R than that of healthy individuals. To further investigate the role of IGF1R in cardiac fibrosis, Igf1r
+/− mice and the age‐matched WT littermates were used to construct the cardiac fibrosis mouse model induced by Ang II for 2 weeks (Figure  ). Interestingly, Ang II led to a significant increase of IGF1R at the mRNA level in WT mice, whereas Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice had ≈60% lower level of IGF1R than that of Ang II–treated WT mice (Figure  ). Moreover, the immunoreactivity of IGF1R was significantly decreased in Igf1r
+/− mice compared with WT mice, whereas Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice showed a lower level of IGF1R than that of Ang II–treated mice (Figure  and ). Consistently, Ang II induced a higher level of IGF1R at the protein expression in Ang II–treated mice, whereas Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice showed the lower level of IGF1R compared with Ang II–treated WT mice (Figure  and ). These data confirm that ablation of IGF1R, and IGF1R is involved in cardiovascular conditions.
 

## 
IGF1R Deficiency Alleviated Cardiac Fibrosis Induced by Ang II in Mice
To clarify whether IGF1R signaling mediates in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, Igf1r
+/− mouse model was treated Ang II for 2 weeks through subcutaneous injection. The results demonstrated that Ang II led to a severe diffused interstitial fibrosis and perivascular fibrosis of the heart in WT mice, whereas there was a remarkable decrease of fibrosis in the hearts of Igf1r
+/− mice (Figure  and ). Consistently, picro sirius red staining of left ventricles in Ang II–treated WT mice showed a severity of fibrosis compared with control group but led to a significant decrease compared with Ang II–treated WT mice (Figure  and ). However, the feature of heart fibrosis induced by Ang II in mice of this study was diffused but not radiated from perivascular origin. Similarly, the results showed increased expressions of Col I, Col III, and periostin at the mRNA level in Ang II–treated WT mice than in vehicle‐treated WT mice, but Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice exhibited a decrease in these fibrotic responses compared with Ang II–treated WT mice (Figure  ). Notably, Ang II induced a significant increase of α‐SMA at the protein level in the left ventricles of WT hearts compared with control group but led to a slight downregulation of α‐SMA in Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice (Figure  and ). Collectively, these findings suggest that IGF1R deficiency is effective in alleviating Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in mice.
 

## Blocking IGF1R Signaling Downregulated the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB Pathway
To investigate the molecular mechanism of IGF1R in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, we evaluated the phosphorylation levels of Akt, ERK, and NF‐κB pathways with or without OSI‐906 treatment in Ang II–treated mice (Figure  through ). Interestingly, OSI‐906 dampened Ang II–induced phosphorylation of Akt and ERK, suggesting that IGF1R directly or indirectly suppressed the activity of the Akt/ERK pathway. In addition, Ang II phosphorylated NF‐κB and IκB in Ang II–treated mice compared with control mice. These data suggest inhibition of IGF1R by blocker OSI‐906 downregulates the expressions of the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis.
 Furthermore, we determined if the expressions of Akt, ERK, and NF‐κB were IGF1R dependent in Igf1r
+/− mouse model. Consistently, Ang II significantly increased the phosphorylation level of Akt in the left ventricles of WT mice, but not of Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice (Figure  and ). As illustrated in Figure  and , the activation of downstream effector ERK and its targets in Ang II–induced WT mice was significantly reduced in the hearts of Ang II–induced Igf1r
+/− mice. Moreover, the level of ERK phosphorylation was lower in Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− hearts. These data confirm the reduced level of ERK signal transduction, which is downstream of Akt kinase, in the left ventricle of Igf1r
+/− mice. In addition, another effector of Akt activation, the transcription factor NF‐κB, was detected (Figure  and ). The results showed a significant decrease of p‐65 of NF‐κB in Igf1r
+/− mice but an obvious increase of p‐65 in WT mice. A similar pattern change was observed for IκB protein expression in the corresponding group of mice. Collectively, our findings indicate that IGF1R deficiency downregulated the activation of the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis.

## 
IGF1R Directly Interacted With GRK5 in Mice
To further determine the mechanism of IGF1R in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, we used RNA‐sequencing assay to identify target genes in Igf1r
+/− mice and WT mice. The gene selections with fold changes >1.5 and P<0.05 highlighted 178 and 103 genes that were upregulated and downregulated, respectively, in Igf1r
+/− mice compared with WT mice (Figure  and ). Functional clustering analysis further showed that the GPCR (G protein‐coupled receptor) signals were highly affected (Figure  ). Interestingly, the expressions of GRK5 at both the mRNA and protein levels were higher in Ang II–treated WT mice than in Ang II–treated Igf1r
+/− mice (Figure  through ), indicating the involvement of GRK5 in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis after Ang II stimulation. Coimmunoprecipitation determined the association between IGF1R and GRK5, revealing that IGF1R directly interacted with GRK5 in mice, whereas this interaction was inhibited by ≈50% in Igf1r
+/− mice compared with that of WT mice (Figure  through ). These data suggest that IGF1R directly interacts with GRK5 in mice, and the deficiency of IGF1R partially suppresses this association.
 

## 
Grk5 Knockdown Downregulated the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB Pathway
GRK5 participates in the establishment and development of cardiac fibrosis. , To investigate if IGF1R signaling‐mediated Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis was GRK5 dependent, we performed Grk5 knockdown experiments using siRNA in primary cardiac fibroblasts (Figure  and ). As shown in Figure  and , Ang II induced a significant increase of IGF1R in Ang II–treated primary cardiac fibroblasts but a significant decrease in siRNA targeting Grk5 transfected control group. However, Ang II induced a high phosphorylation level of Akt, ERK, and NF‐κB, which was inhibited by GRK5 knockdown (Figure  through ). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that GRK5 has an inhibitory effect on IGF1R and acts as the downstream effector of the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway.
 

## Discussion
This study elucidates a novel mechanism for IGF1R in potentiating cardiac fibrosis using in vitro and in vivo cardiac fibrosis models (Figures  , , , , , and Figure  ). Interestingly, we discovered the potential signal transduction pathway mediated by IGF1R downstream effectors using IGF1R‐deficient mice. Our findings demonstrate that blocking IGF1R signaling suppresses EndMT in primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells and mice in response to Ang II or TGF‐β1, respectively. Furthermore, deficiency or inhibition of IGF1R signaling significantly attenuates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts and mice. Mechanistically, IGF1R signaling deficiency alleviates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, at least partly through inhibiting EndMT via the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway, and GRK5 acts as an IGF1R downstream effector. A schematic illustrating our main findings is shown in Figure  . As well known, IGF1 and its receptor (IGF1R) belong to the family of insulin‐like growth factors, whereas IGF1 activates intracellular signaling pathways by binding to IGF1R. Moreover, IGF1R plays a key role in regulating cardiac homeostasis, and IGF1 therapy reduces cardiac fibrosis in acute myocardial infarction. Particularly, low‐dose IGF1 treatment suppresses Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis. However, these studies have not detected blood levels of IGF1 and IGF1R in human patients. Our findings provide a complementary in this regard and highlight IGF1R as a target for treating Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in mice.
 Recently, EndMT has emerged as a possible source of tissue myofibroblasts to promote cardiac fibrosis, , and phenotypic changes ensue that are consistent with the switch to a mesenchymal phenotype, including excessive collagen production, enhanced contractile ability, and increased invasive ability into the matrix and other substrates. In this study, we found that blocking IGF1R signaling increased the expressions of endothelial markers (CD31, VE‐cadherin, and claudin‐5) and decreased the expressions of mesenchymal markers (ɑ‐SMA, collagen 1a, and fibronectin) in Ang II–induced mice and TGF‐β1–treated primary mouse cardiac microvascular endothelial cells (Figure  ), indicating that inhibition of IGF1R signaling at least partly suppresses cardiac EndMT both in vitro and in vivo. Concurrently, resident fibroblasts might be responsive to Ang II and increase the productions of collagen depositions accordingly. Consistently, we further observed that deficiency or inhibition of IGF1R signaling attenuated Ang II–induced collagen depositions, such as Col I and Col III, in both primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts and mice (Figure  ). Therefore, our findings reveal that IGF1R signaling may be involved in the regulation of EndMT and further mediate Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis.
We observed that patients with HF had a higher blood level of both IGF1 and IGF1R than healthy individuals. Consistently, IGF1R knockout mouse model further demonstrated that Ang II induced a higher level of cardiac IGF1R, whereas IGF1R deficiency decreased this alteration (Figure  ). Previous study showed that failed human hearts in end‐stage patients with HF exhibited increased IGF1 and IGF1R signaling activity, whereas the overexpression of IGF1R in the cardiomyocytes of mice led to physiological hypertrophy and superior cardiac function in the early stages but resulted in accelerated cardiac aging and HF later in life. Therefore, our study further reveals that IGF1R is involved in cardiovascular conditions and can be a promising therapeutic target for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis and even HF.
Myocardial interstitial fibrosis is a common characteristic in nonischemic cardiovascular disease, and cardiovascular death is associated with the severity of interstitial fibrosis in patients with HF. Previous studies have demonstrated that inhibition of IGF1R attenuates adverse cardiac remodeling in aged mice, whereas IGF1R monoclonal antibody treatment improves myocardium dysfunction in aged female mice. , In this study, our results of Masson and picro sirius red staining exhibit that Ang II induces a severity of myocardial interstitial fibrosis in mice, whereas the deficiency of IGF1R significantly attenuates these alterations (Figure  ). A previous study reported that the depositions of Col I and Col III increased in the progression of cirrhosis from mild to moderate and even to the advanced stage. Similarly, our study also showed that Ang II induced a significant increase in the collagen depositions of Col I and Col III, indicating moderate or even advanced cardiac fibrosis in mice, whereas the deficiency of IGF1R reduced the fibrosis at this stage. Moreover, Ang II–treated IGF1R heterozygous knockout mice did not exhibit these cardiac fibrotic changes. Therefore, our findings demonstrate that deficiency of IGF1R effectively alleviates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in mice.
Our findings further reveal that Ang II upregulates the Akt/ERK pathway and NF‐κB transcriptional activity in mice, whereas blocking IGF1R signaling downregulates the phosphorylation level of Akt and ERK pathways and NF‐κB activity induced by Ang II (Figure  ). Previous study has shown that low‐dose IGF1 treatment attenuates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis via the Akt pathway and rho‐associated coiled‐coil containing kinases–mediated α‐SMA. Moreover, elevated Ang II activates the NF‐κB signaling, which promotes cardiac fibrosis and contributes to the progression of HF. Herein, our findings reveal that blocking IGF1R signaling is beneficial for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis, at least partially via the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway.
GRK5, a major isoform of GPCR signaling, is involved in cardiac fibrosis and HF. , GRK5 is negatively related to heart health in HF. To determine whether the GPCR signal is affected in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis in Igf1r
+/− mice, we used RNA‐sequencing assay to identify the targets of IGF1R mediated in the heart (Figure  ). Notably, GPCRs and related ligand‐binding proteins are upregulated or downregulated, respectively. Furthermore, IGF1R deficiency decreased the association of IGF1R and GRK5 ≈50% in mice. Thus, this study demonstrates a novel mechanism of IGF1R signaling in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis.
GRK5 is a critical mediator of IGF1R regulation in the heart and acts as a therapeutic target for HF. Our findings identify, for the first time, to the best of our knowledge, that the regulation of IGF1R after Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis is GRK5 dependent (Figure  ). Intriguingly, Grk5 knockdown downregulated the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway in primary cardiac fibroblasts, thereby indicating that the IGF1R downstream effectors in Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis were GRK5 dependent. However, this study has limitations. The roles of IGF1R signaling are controversial in female mice compared with male mice; therefore, this study used male mice rather than female mice as study subjects. Hence, further studies clarifying the sex‐dependent nature of IGF1R signaling are required.
In conclusion, our findings reveal that IGF1R signaling deficiency alleviates Ang II–induced cardiac fibrosis, at least partly through suppressing EndMT via the Akt/ERK/NF‐κB pathway. Interestingly, IGF1R signaling associates with GRK5 directly, which acts as an IGF1R downstream effector. Therefore, IGF1R‐mediated signaling may be the promising potential therapeutic target for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. This study provides a strategy for attenuating cardiac fibrosis by targeting IGF1R signaling.
