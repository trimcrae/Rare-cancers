## Introduction
Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a common and serious microvascular complication of diabetes . According to a report from the International Diabetes Federation in 2021, there were approximately 537 million diabetic patients worldwide, a number expected to increase to 783 million by 2045, with approximately 30–40% of developing DKD . The onset of DKD is slow, but once a patient develops clinical proteinuria, renal pathological damage becomes irreversible. Moreover, DKD can progress rapidly to end-stage renal disease, ultimately requiring renal replacement therapy . Currently, the main treatment for DKD involving controlling blood sugar, blood lipids, and blood pressure. Although several new hypoglycemic drugs, such as sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, and endothelin receptor antagonists, have shown some renoprotective effects in recent years, people with diabetes remain at high risk of developing kidney disease .
Most complications in people with diabetes are related to hyperglycemia. Although blood sugar levels can be controlled through diet, exercise, and medications such as insulin, many patients still suffer from diabetes complications after their blood sugar is under control, suggesting the presence of “metabolic memory” caused by previous hyperglycemia in the target cells . Epigenetic modifications, including DNA modifications, histone modifications, RNA modifications and noncoding RNAs, are believed to mediate the generation of this “metabolic memory”. Methylation of m6A (N6-methyladenosine) is an epigenetic modification in RNA that affects mainly mRNA stability, movement of the nucleus and translation initiation. m6A refers to the process of introducing a methyl group at the N6 position of adenosine . As the most common internal modification of polyadenylation in higher eukaryotes, abnormal expression of enzymes involved in m6A modification has also been implicated in several human diseases. Research has shown a correlation between m6A/C-modified nucleosides found in the serum of DKD patients and clinical biochemical indicators . In addition, Yang et al. reported reduced m6A content in patients with type 2 diabetes . Moreover, the mRNA expression levels of methyltransferases 3 (METTL3), methyltransferase 14 (METTL14) and WT1-associated protein (WTAP), as well as the demethylases fat mass and obesity associated (FTO), and ALKB homolog 5 (ALKBH5), which are involved in the m6A biological modification process, were increased – . These findings indicate that m6A modification may serve as a key regulatory mechanism in the development of DKD.
Epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT) is the process by which epithelial cells transition to mesenchymal cells, and it is an important pathological feature of DKD. Normally, renal tubular epithelial cells are tightly connected through different cell adhesion mechanisms. E-cadherin is a specific adhesion molecule that plays a key role in maintaining the integrity of renal tubular epithelial cells, and its downregulation disrupt this integrity . When EMT occurs, the expression of E-cadherin, an epithelial cell-specific gene (E-cadherin), is inhibited, while vimentin, a phenotypic marker of interstitial cells, is activated. The actin skeleton recombines with stress fibers, and epithelial cells lose their cobblestone morphology and become elongated spindles with α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression . Lumbrokinase (LK), an extract of Lumbricus rubellus consisting of a group of bioactive proteolytic enzymes, was identified in recent decades. Previous studies have shown that LK has antirenal fibrotic effects, possibly related to its inhibition of TGF-β1, α-SMA and Col-I expression in renal tissue . Recent research has shown that snail is expressed in response to key TGF-β1 factors that induce EMT . METTL3 was confirmed to regulate cancer cell EMT through the transcription of snail in tumor research . Therefore, m6A methylation may promote the occurrence of EMT.
In this study, we revealed the m6A RNA levels and expression patterns of m6A-modifying enzymes in db/db mouse renal tissue and human renal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2) cultured in high glucose. The m6A eraser METTL3 was expressed at low levels in db/db mouse renal tissue and in high glucose-stimulated HK-2 cells. overexpression of METTL3 reversed the high glucose-induced activation of the TGF-β1/Smad pathway and EMT through snail in vitro. However, LK was able to restore the expression of the m6A-modifying enzyme METTL3 in vivo and in vitro. LK suppressed the occurrence of EMT and alleviated renal interstitial fibrosis by downregulating snail.

## Results

## Effects of LK on metabolic and biochemical parameters in diabetic mice
In this study, db/db mice were used to establish a type 2 diabetes model. The results revealed that the levels of FBG, TG, TC, BUN, Scr, and mALB were significantly increased in the DM group (P < 0.01) (Fig.  A–F), indicating the successful construction of a hyperglycemia-induced DKD mouse model. However, LK administration attenuated the levels of blood lipids (TC, TG) and renal injury parameters (BUN, SCr, and mALB) compared with those in the DM group (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B–F). Notably, LK did not effect on the blood glucose concentration (P > 0.05) (Fig.  A). 

## Effects of LK on renal histopathology
In addition to the renal injury parameters, we further evaluated the histopathological changes in the mouse kidneys. As shown in Fig.  A, renal tissue structure was intact in the Con group, and no abnormal pathological changes were observed. In contrast, mice in the DM group exhibited glomerular hypertrophy, basement membrane thickening (blue arrow), mesangial cell and matrix hyperplasia (green arrow), narrowing of the capsule cavity (black arrow), renal tubular epithelial cell swelling, vacuolar degeneration (red arrow), interstitial inflammatory cell infiltration, and fibrosis (yellow arrow). Following LK intervention, pathological changes in the kidneys were alleviated, inflammatory infiltration was reduced, and mesangial cell and matrix proliferation were also reduced. As shown in Fig.  B, semiquantitative analysis revealed that collagen deposition was lower in the renal tissues of the Con group than in those of the control group. In contrast, collagen deposition was significantly greater in the renal tissues of the DM group (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B). After intervention with LK, collagen disposition was significantly reduced (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B). 

## Effects of LK on EMT, renal interstitial fibrosis and snail in diabetic mice
EMT is characterized by the loss of epithelial markers (i.e., E-cadherin) and the overexpression of mesenchymal markers (i.e., vimentin and α-SMA) . Western blot to analysis of renal lysates (Fig.  A) revealed that expression of E-cadherin was suppressed, whereas the expression of vimentin and α-SMA was increased in the DM group (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B). However, these effects were moderately improved after intervention with LK (P < 0.01, P < 0.05) (Fig.  B). Moreover, as a key component of the ECM, the expression of fibronectin (FN), which is closely related to renal fibrosis, showed decreased expression after treatment with LK (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B). Consistent with these results, immunofluorescence staining (Fig.  C,D) demonstrated recovery of E-cadherin expression and reduced α-SMA deposition in renal tissues after LK intervention. Furthermore, Snail is the key EMT transcription factor. As shown in Fig.  A,B, Snail expression was significantly greater in the DM group than in the Con group (P < 0.01), but LK treatment significantly inhibited this effect (P < 0.01). 

## Effects of LK on the TGF-β/smad pathway in diabetic mice
TGF-β1 is the most effective fibrotic growth factor and promotes EMT by directly modulating ECM protein expression in diabetic kidneys . Western blot analysis revealed marked TGF-β1 induction in the DM group (P < 0.01). However, only a small amount of protein was observed after treatment with LK (P < 0.01) (Fig.  A,B). Since Smad signaling plays a central role in TGF-β-mediated fibrosis, we further analyzed the expression and subsequent activation of Smad proteins. Western blot analysis revealed considerably high levels of p-Smad2/3 in the renal tissues of the DM group (P < 0.01). However, LK administration relatively reversed these effects (P < 0.01) (Fig.  A,B). 

## Effects of LK on m6A RNA methylation and m6A modification enzyme expression
To understand the underlying roles of m6A methylation in DKD renal tissues, we first detected m6A RNA methylation levels using a colorimetric assay. The results indicated that the RNA m6A content was lower than that in the Con group (P < 0.01). However, LK administration significantly reversed the increase in the m6A methylation level (P < 0.05) (Fig.  A). We subsequently investigated the expression of m6A regulators. Five major enzymes involved in m6A modification, namely, METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, FTO, and ALKBH5, were assessed via qPCR. Compared with the Con group mice, the expression of METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP was significantly reduced (P < 0.01), and the expression of ALKBH5 was increased (P < 0.01), while FTO mRNA levels were not significantly different in DKD renal tissues (P > 0.05) (Fig.  B). However, LK administration significantly reversed the expression changes of METTL3 and ALKBH5 (P < 0.01), but LK had no significant effect on METTL14, WTAP, or FTO (P > 0.05) (Fig.  B). In addition, we examined the protein expression of these five genes via western blotting. The results revealed that the protein expression of METTL3 and METTL14 was decreased (P < 0.01) and that the expression of WTAP and ALKBH5 was increased in the DM group (P < 0.01), while the expression of FTO was not significantly different in DKD renal tissues (P > 0.05) (Fig.  C,D). However, LK administration significantly reversed the changes in METTL3 and ALKBH5 expression (P < 0.01), but did not significantly affect METTL14, WTAP, or FTO expression (P > 0.05) (Fig.  C,D). 

## Colocalization of METTL3 and snail in DKD mice and patients
Research has shown that in METTL3-knockdown cells, Snail is a key factor responding to the regulation of EMT in cancer cells by TGF-β1 . In this study, we investigated the association between METTL3 and Snail in the renal tissues of DKD patients and mice via immunofluorescence. Colocalization studies of METTL3 and Snail in DM group mice revealed partial recovery of colocalization after LK treatment (Fig.  A). These findings were further verified validated in DKD patients, showing that the m6A eraser METTL3 is associated with the key EMT transcription factor Snail (Fig.  B). The co-immunoprecipitation results subsequently confirmed the interaction between METTL3 and Snail molecules (Supplementary Fig.  ). Additionally, immunofluorescence staining of E-cadherin and α-SMA was performed in control and DKD patients. Compared with the control group, E-cadherin expression was lower (P < 0.05), while the α-SMA expression was higher in the DKD patients (P < 0.01), confirming the occurrence of EMT in DKD patients (Fig.  C–H). 

## Snail participates in m6A-regulated EMT in high-glucose-stimulated HK-2 cells
The CCK8 results revealed that when the LK concentration was less than 150 U/ml, there was no significant effect on HK cell viability. However, at concentration higher than 300 U/ml, HK cell viability tended to decrease (P < 0.01) (Fig.  A). Additionally, the expression of METTL3 was inhibited and recovered after LK administration in diabetic mice. In this study, we examined the function of LK in regulating METTL3 and EMT-related proteins in high glucose–stimulated HK-2 cells. We explored whether the antirenal fibrotic effect of LK is related to the repair of METTL3. Previous studies have shown that TGF-β plays a key role in the development of renal fibrosis . We found that high glucose-treated HK-2 cells presented lower levels of RNA m6A. However, LK administration significantly restored the m6A methylation level (P < 0.01) (Fig.  B). Moreover, lower levels of METTL3 and E-cadherin and higher levels of TGF-β1 and α-SMA were detected in high glucose–stimulated HK-2 cells (P < 0.01); however, these effects were reversed after LK treatment (P < 0.01) (Fig.  C,E). Immunofluorescence colocalization studies of METTL3 and Snail in high glucose–stimulated HK-2 cells revealed that METTL3 partially recovered colocalization with Snail after LK treatment (Fig.  D). These results are consistent with the in vivo findings. To investigate the underlying mechanisms of m6A-regulated EMT through changes in the expression of METTL3, endogenous METTL3 was silenced, and the cells were transfected with a plasmid expressing METTL3. We measured EMT biomarkers and the EMT key transcription factor Snail. Following siMETTL3 transfection, METTL3 expression decreased (P < 0.01) (Fig.  F,H), whereas transfection with the METTL3 expression vector increased METTL3 levels (P < 0.01) (Fig. G,I). We found that knockdown of METTL3 suppressed the expression of E-cadherin and increased the levels of α-SMA, TGF-β1 and Snail, whereas overexpression of METTL3 had the opposite effect (P < 0.01) (Fig. F–I). However, LK restored METTL3 and E-cadherin expression and inhibited the levels of α-SMA, TGF-β1, and Snail (P < 0.01) (Fig.  F,H). Therefore, if the METTL3 gene was knocked out, the anti-EMT effect of LK was weakened, and increased Snail expression. 

## Discussion
DKD is a significant complication of diabetes, and its pathogenesis is complex. Hyperglycemia, alterations in renal hemodynamics, oxidative stress, and inflammation can lead to glomerular hyperfiltration, glomerular hypertrophy, thickening of the basement membrane, and accumulation of the extracellular matrix . Clinically, proteinuria is the main clinical manifestation of DKD. BUN and Scr are also the most widely used indicators of renal function, and an increase in these parameters is considered a hallmark of renal injury in DKD. In our study, db/db mice, which are the most widely used model of type 2 diabetes, were used as animal models. At 6 weeks of age, these mice exhibited notable obesity, elevated fasting blood glucose levels, increased water consumption and urine volume, and DKD occur at 8–12 weeks . This study revealed that at the end of the experiment, the mALB of db/db mice was significantly greater than that of db/m mice; renal function was impaired (BUN, Scr), and changes in renal pathology were observed, which indicated that the DKD model of db/db mice was successfully established. Although LK is known as a fibrinolytic enzyme, it has not been extensively studied in the context of DKD. However, accumulating evidence suggests that LK possesses various beneficial properties, including antioxidative, anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, antimicrobial, and anticancer effects. In this study, LK treatment significantly reduced mALB, improved renal function, and renal pathological damage and renal interstitial fibrosis. However, LK did not affect blood glucose levels in diabetic mice. In addition, abnormal lipid levels contribute to the development of DKD by increasing renal lipotoxicity . After treatment with LK, the blood lipid levels of diabetic mice were significantly reduced.
Previous studies have shown that glomerular injury is an early lesion of DKD, with tubulointerstitial injury is secondary to glomerular injury. However, recent research has highlights that tubulointerstitial fibrosis is a crucial pathological basis for the progression of DKD to ESRD and may be a better predictor of disease progression than glomerular damage , . The extracellular matrix, such as FN and collagen I, accumulates excessively in the glomerular mesangium and tubulointerstitium, occupying normal tissue space and ultimately leading to a decreased glomerular filtration rate, proteinuria, hypertension, and renal dysfunction . Under pathological conditions, activated myofibroblasts are the main effector cells that produce the extracellular matrix . The EMT of renal tubular epithelial cells is an important source of myofibroblasts. Iwano et al. reported that up to 36% of myofibroblasts originate from the local EMT of renal tubular epithelial cell during renal fibrosis . Thus, EMT of renal tubular epithelial cells is an important mechanism of renal tubulointerstitial fibrosis . EMT involves tubular epithelial cells losing their epithelial phenotype (e.g., E-cadherin), damage to the tubular basement membrane, and the migration of some epithelial cells into the renal interstitium, where they acquire a mesenchymal phenotype (e.g., α-SMA) and transform into myofibroblasts . Many studies have shown that the RMT process is regulated by multiple signaling pathways and factors, which can cooperate to initiate and complete the entire EMT response – . TGF-β1 is the most potent driver of EMT . The TGF-β1 pathway interacts with the Wnt pathway and can activate the Wnt/β-catenin pathway to mediate EMT of renal tubular epithelial cells . Our study demonstrated that the expression levels of TGF-β1, p-smad2, and p-Smad3 were significantly increased in the renal tissues of diabetic mice but decreased substantially after intervention with LK. Moreover, LK administration upregulated the protein expression of E-cadherin and inhibited the protein expression of vimentin, α-SMA, and FN in the renal tissue of diabetic mice. In summary, we report for the first time that LK may inhibit the activation of the TGF-β/Smad pathway and improve the EMT of renal tubular epithelial cells.
M6A methylation is considered a reversible dynamic modification in many species and plays an important role in various human diseases, including cancer progression, diabetes, neurodevelopmental disorders, and ischemic heart disease . Previous studies have shown that m6A modification can respond to various physiological stimuli by influencing mRNA transcription, splicing, localization, translation, and stability and posttranscriptional regulation at the RNA level , . There is also evidence suggesting a close relationship between m6A modification and renal injury, which reveals an important biological role of m6A in the regulation of renal injury . A recent study revealed that m6A-methylated RNA was also significantly increased in a renal ischemia‒reperfusion injury mouse model . Furthermore, m6A-methylated RNA levels were significantly higher in kidney biopsies from patients with acute kidney injury (AKI) than in those from non-AKI patients. Moreover, m6A levels are positively correlated with the serum creatinine level in these patients . However, research on the role of m6A modification in DKD is still limited. In this study, we found that a change in the m6A modification level was associated with renal interstitial fibrosis and that the m6A modification level of total RNA was significantly decreased in the renal tissues of diabetic mice and HK-2 cells stimulated with high glucose, which differed from the finding in the AKI and ischemia‒reperfusion renal injury models. However, LK treatment significantly inhibited this effect. With the development of m6A detection technology and the emergence of other new technologies, various of m6A modification-related regulatory enzymes have been identified, which is helpful for understanding their potential biological functions. M6A modification is regulated by the methyltransferases METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP; demethylases such as ALKBH5 and FTO; and reader proteins (including YTHDF1, YTHDF2, and YTHDF3) – . We found that the protein and RNA levels of METTL3 and METTL14 were decreased and that the ALKBH5 level was increased. However, the expression of FTO was not significantly different between renal tissues and high glucose–stimulated HK-2 cells. The trend of METTL3 and ALKBH5 expression was reversed after LK treatment. In addition, LK administration had no significant effect on METTL14, WTAP, or FTO.
The m6A methyltransferase METTL3 was upregulated in LK-treated db/db mice, and the results of the in vitro experiments were consistent with those of the in vivo experiments. Thus, we believe that METTL3 is a crucial regulatory gene involved in how LK protects against renal fibrosis. Snails (encoded by SNAI1) belongs to the family of transcriptional inhibitors, which can bind to the E-box structure in the promoter region of E-cadherin, inhibit its transcription and expression, reduce adhesion between cells, and induce the occurrence of EMT . Tumor studies have confirmed that Snail is the key transcription inhibitor of E-cadherin expression in EMT. Snail recruits chromatin modifiers, such as lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1) and Suv39H1 histone methyltransferases, and triggers both transient and long-lasting chromatin changes in the CDH1 promoter, which endows tumor cells with cancer stem cell-like traits and promotes drug resistance, tumor recurrence, and metastasis , . In METTL3-knockdown cells, Snail is a key factor that responds to the regulation of EMT by TGF-β1 in cancer cells . In this study, we found that METTL3 knockdown suppressed E-cadherin expression and increased the levels of α-SMA and Snail, whereas overexpression of METTL3 had the opposite effects. Furthermore, LK restored the METTL3 expression. If METTL3 was knocked down, the anti-EMT effect of LK was weakened, and Snail expression increased.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, we provide convincing evidence in vitro and in vivo that m6A is involved in high glucose–induced renal interstitial fibrosis. METTL3 is a crucial regulator of m6A modification and protection of LK against renal fibrosis. LK ameliorated renal fibrosis by restoring METTL3 to regulate EMT. These findings may provide new insights into the role of m6A modification in DKD and contribute to the development of new drugs for alleviating renal fibrosis. However, our research has several limitations. For example, we focused only on the m6A-modified protein METTL3. However, other modified proteins were not explored in this study. Further investigations are needed to enhance the precision and significance of these experiments.

## Materials and methods

## Animals
SPF grade male db/m and db/db mice aged 11–12 weeks were purchased from Changzhou Cavens Experimental Animal Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China, license number: SCXK (Su) 2021-0013). Among them, there were 8 db/m mice and 16 db/db mice. All mice were fed under the conditions of 12 h light/dark cycle, temperature (24 ± 1)°C, humidity 50%-70%, and could freely eat and drink water. This study was performed in accordance with the China Animal Welfare Legislation and approved by the Ethics Committee of Hebei University of Chinese Medicine (number DWLL202203117), China. The study was also conducted according to the ARRIVE guidelines (PLoS Bio 8(6), e1000412, 2010).

## Patients
Kidney tissue specimens were obtained from biopsy-proven diabetic nephropathy patients (n = 3), while the normal kidney specimens were taken from patients with renal tumors, characterized by minimal histological changes and distant renal tissue from the tumor (n = 3). The institutional review board of Hebei University of Chinese Medicine approved this study (number YXLL202203013), China. The experiment were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, all patients volunteered for this survey and signed informed consent.

## Main chemicals and reagents
A urine microalbumin kit (mALB, batch number H127-1–2) were provided by Nanjing JianCheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). The anti-α-SMA (GB111364), HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (GB23303), RNA extract (G3013), Servicebio® RT First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (G3330), 2 × SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (High ROX) (G3321), protease inhibitor and phosphorylase inhibitor (G2007) were purchased from Wuhan Saiwei Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China). The anti-METTL3 (ab195352), anti-METTL14 (ab252562), anti-WTAP (ab155655), anti-ALKBH5 (ab195377), anti-FTO (ab94482), anti-TGF-β1 (ab215715), anti-smad2 (ab33875), anti-smad3 (ab40854), anti-p-smad2 (ab188334), anti-p-smad3 (ab52903), anti-FN (ab2413), anti-Snail (ab180714), goat anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 488) (ab150077), were obtained from from Abcam, UK. The anti-E-cadherin (bs-1519P), anti-Vimentin (bs-23063R) were purchased from Bioss Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). The anti-β-actin antibody (AF0003), ECL hypersensitive chemiluminescence kit (P0018S), RIPA protein lysate (P0013B), PAS staining kit (C0142M), HE staining kit (C0105S), BCA protein concentration determination kit (P0009) were purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China).

## Experimental drug
The LK in this study was provided by Beijing Baiao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd (approval certificate: H11021129). The powder was dissolved in distilled water. Then, the combined solution was filtered with a 0.22 µm microporous filter membrane, and the sample was used to treated cells.

## Grouping, administration and sample collection
The animals were fed adaptively for 1 week, and 8 db/m mice were taken as the control group (Con group), and the db/db mice were randomly divided into DM group and LK group, with 8 mice in each group. The LK group mice were given LK (234KU/kg daily by gavage), the mice were gavaged with an equal volume of drinking water in the Con and DM groups. After 12 weeks of gavage, the mice were put in metabolic cages to collect urine. Then the mice were fasted for 12 h. After inhalation anesthesia (3% isoflurane), the blood was collected from the femoral artery, and sacrificed by cervical dislocation. The abdominal cavity was quickly opened, the kidney was cut off and the renal capsule was stripped. The kidney was divided into two parts. One part was fixed in 4% neutral formaldehyde for histopathological and immunohistochemical detection, and the other part was frozen in a refrigerator at − 80 ℃ for related protein expression detection.

## Cell culture, treatment, and transfection
Human renal tubular HK-2 cells (Procell, China) grow in MEM (PM150410) containing 10% FBS (fetal bovine serum) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Beyotime, China). The HK-2 cells was incubated at 37 °C in 5% CO2. The HK-2 cells were treated with or without high glucose (30.0 mmol/L) for 24 h in the absence and presence of LK. Next, according to the methods described by the manufacturer, the HK-2 were transfected with the synthesized METTL3 siRNA oligonucleotides and scrambled oligonucleotides at a final concentration of 50 nmol/L using Lipofectamine 3000. To generate METTL3 overexpression HK-2 cells, The cells were transfected with pcDNA/METTL3 or vector control through the liposome-mediated transfection. Through liposome-mediated transfection, METTL3-overexpressing HK-2 cells were generated by transfecting cells with pcDNA/METTL3 or vector control. The transfected HK-2 cells were stimulated with or without high glucose (30.0mmol/L) after 24 h of transfection in the absence and presence of LK. Finally, the expression of METTL3 was checked by western blot analysis.

## Cell counting Kit-8 assay
The cytotoxicity of LK was determined by CCK-8 assay. Briefly, the HK-2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured until they adhered completely. Then, cells were treated with LK at different concentrations (100 U/ml, 150 U/ml, 300 U/ml, 500 U/ml) for 24 h at 37 °C, the LK was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide and filtered through a needle filter. Then, 10 µl of CCK-8 solution (MCE, HY-K0301) was added onto the cells and incubated for another 4 h. The optical density (OD) was measured at 450 nm, and experiments were performed parallelly in triplicate.

## Biochemical parameters
Mice urine samples were centrifuged at 3000 r/min in 4℃ for 15 min, and the supernatant was taken. According to the instruction of urine microalbumin kit, the concentration of mALB was detected in each group of mice and the urinary mALB was expressed as milligrams per 24 h. The levels of urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Scr), cholesterol (TC), and triglyceride (TG) were determined by a Hitachi automatic biochemical analyzer (7600–020, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan).

## Histological examination
The kidney tissue of mice was fixed in 4% neutral formaldehyde for 24 h. After paraffin embedding, the sections were sliced, dehydrated in gradients, cleared with xylene, and stained with hematoxylin–eosin (HE), periodic acid-schiff (PAS) and Masson, respectively. The procedure of section staining was carried out according to the instructions. The pathological changes of renal tissue were observed under a microscope (Olympus Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) in a blind manner. The amount of collagen deposition in the pathological sections of kidney tissue was semi-quantitatively analyzed by Image-Pro Plus analysis software (Media Cybernetics, USA).

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) assay
Take 100 mg renal tissue and HK-2, then fully homogenize it on ice, add TRIzol (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) to extract total RNA, and analyze its concentration with Nanodrop 2000C spectrophotometer (thermo Scientific, Wilmington, USA). Next, according to the manufacturer’s instructions, the RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA by a Servicebio®RT First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). qRT-PCR was operated with the 2 × SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (High ROX) (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Gene-specific primers (Table ) were designed and synthesized by Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). The reaction conditions are as follows: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing at 58 °C for 15 s, extension at 72 °C for 10 s, and repeated 40 cycles. The results were calculated by the 2−ΔΔ Ct method. 

## Western blot assay
This assay was conducted based on the methods described previously . The primary antibodies used are as follows: METTL3 (1:800), METTL14 (1:600), WTAP (1:800), ALKBH5 (1:800), FTO (1:800), TGF-β1 (1:1000), smad2 (1:1000), smad3 (1:1000), p-smad2 (1:1000), p-smad3 (1:1000), Snail (1:800), E-cadherin (1:600), Vimentin (1:800), FN (1:800), α-SMA (1:800), β-actin(1:1000). The protein bands were visualized by the ECL kit and captured by the ImageQuant LAS4000 imaging system (GE, USA). The β-actin antibody was used as a loading control, and relative density of each band was performed and quantified by the ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA).

## Co-immunoprecipitation assay
The proteins extracted from the renal tissues of mice were rotationally incubated with 15 μl of protein A/G magnetic beads (MedChenExpress, Shanghai, China) overnight at 4 °C. Next, the compound was mixed with METTL3 antibody at 4 °C for 12 h, rotationally. The test tube was placed on a magnetic rack to separate the magnetic beads. After cleaning, the samples were boiled for 5 min at 100 °C. METTL3 and Snail proteins were detected by using WB.

## Immunofluorescence
For immunofluorescence analysis, Take paraffin slices and bake them at 60 ℃ for 4h, place them at room temperature for 10min, and then gradually dewax them to water. After washing, tissue sections were incubated with primary antibodies against E-cadherin (1:100), α-SMA (1:100) METTL3 (1:100) at 4 °C overnight. Then, the secondary antibody was added dropwise in the dark and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. For immunofluorescence double staining, The Snail primary antibodies (1:100) with double staining were added dropwise and placed in the refrigerator at 4 °C overnight. The tissue sections were washed with PBS, dropped the secondary antibody and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. DAPI was used as a counterstaining. Finally, the sections were observed with an Olympus IX71 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan). The mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was quantified using an image analysis system (Image-Pro Plus 6.0).

## Measurement of m6A modification
UPLC-MS/MS analysis of m6A was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Data analysis
All data were analyzed by SPSS software (version 26.0, IBM, Chicago, IL, USA) and expressed as means ± standard deviation (SD). The one-way ANOVA was used for comparison between groups, and GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software (GraphPad Software Inc.,V5.02, San Diego, CA, USA) was used to make histograms. A value of P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant for all analyses.
