EVs as carriers of proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids can influence immune responses and promote intercellular communication. EVs can be used for encapsulation of drug owing to their biocompatibility, minimal immunogenicity, and capacity to penetrate biological barriers. Additionally, EVs can be surface modified to obtain hybrid EVs for selective delivery to target site. The EVs can be administered to the lungs via pulmonary route to maximize the therapeutic concentration at the site of inflammation while reducing systemic negative effects. In conclusion, EV-based nanomedicine for pulmonary administration offers focused therapy with lower toxicity, making it a new and effective strategy for the treatment of inflammatory lung disorders.

## Introduction
Asthma, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are examples of inflammatory lung illnesses that continue to be major causes of morbidity and mortality globally, placing a significant burden on healthcare systems and lowering patient quality of life. Effective management is hampered by issues like non-specific drug distribution, fast systemic clearance, and significant off-target effects, despite advancements in pharmacotherapy and respiratory care. These drawbacks highlight the critical need for innovative, focused delivery methods that can get beyond physiological obstacles, reduce side effects, and improve treatment effectiveness, particularly in disease phenotypes that are resistant and relapse [ ].
Extracellular vesicles surpass conventional synthetic drug carriers, owing to their abundance in organisms, enhanced targeting ability and low immunogenicity. Extracellular vesicles can be used as drug carriers for the treatment of various lung disorder [ ]. The innate ability of extracellular vesicles (EVs), which are membrane-bound, nanoscale particles released by almost all cell types, to facilitate targeted cellular communication and intercellular cargo movement has made them attractive candidates for pulmonary medication delivery. As endogenous mediators and exogenous vehicles in the setting of lung disorders, EVs are notable for their biocompatibility and capacity to incorporate a broad range of bioactive compounds. Their cell-derived surface proteins provide innate targeting, absorption efficiency, and low immunogenicity, and their small size enables them to cross biological barriers and enter deep pulmonary regions. According to recent experimental models, EVs can modify local immunological and inflammatory responses and transfer therapeutic compounds across the pulmonary epithelium, providing a next-generation platform for intervention in inflammatory lung illness [ ].
An innovative method of pulmonary drug delivery has been ushered in by the combination of nanotechnology and EV-based therapies. Thanks to developments in nanomaterial engineering, EVs can now be customized to improve drug loading and release kinetics, lengthen systemic and local retention, and increase stability in the airway environment. It has been demonstrated that the incorporation of functionalized nanoparticles greatly enhances the EVs’ physicochemical resilience and targeting specificity, allowing for regulated payload distribution and real-time tracking. For instance, surface changes made possible by nanotechnology, including as chemical engineering and peptide conjugation, have enhanced EV’s affinity for inflammatory lung tissue, decreased immune clearance, and raised their therapeutic potential against experimental models of pulmonary injury and fibrosis [ ].

 

Recent developments demonstrate the significant influence of EVs augmented by nanotechnology in overcoming the current drawbacks of traditional treatments for inflammatory lung diseases. The scalable manufacture of hybrid EVs with exact size, load, and targeting properties is now made possible by microfluidic platforms and sophisticated biofabrication techniques. The stage is prepared for the translation of these technologies to clinical use by in vivo studies that show greater biodistribution, enhanced anti-inflammatory activities, and improved disease outcomes with less systemic toxicity [ ]. All of these interdisciplinary developments put extracellular vesicles augmented by nanotechnology at the forefront of pulmonary medicine, potentially revolutionizing the accuracy and effectiveness of therapies for inflammatory lung conditions. ## Extracellular vesicles systems and isolation
Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are a heterogeneous group of membrane-bound particles secreted by nearly all cell types. They are broadly classified based on their biogenesis, size, and markers (Table ). Exosomes, microvesicles, and apoptotic bodies are major subtypes that can be further distinguished by their molecular contents, physical traits, and formation mechanisms [ ]. The intricacy and diversity of the EV environment are highlighted by recent discoveries of new forms, such as giant oncosomes, migrasomes, ectosomes, exomeres, and matrix vesicles [ , ]. Exosomes Originae from endosomal multivesicular bodies and are generally the smallest subtype and are enriched in tetraspanins (CD63, CD81, and CD9). Microvesicles are formed by outward budding of the plasma membrane and are typically larger, with distinct surface proteins. Apoptotic bodies are generated during programmed cell death and carry a diverse set of cellular components, including nuclear material. Additional EVs include autophagic vesicles and stress-derived vesicles with roles in communication under specific physiological conditions [ ]. Figure illustrates various types of extracellular vesicles, detailing their pros and cons.

 

 

For biological research and medical applications, it is essential to isolate EVs with high purity and integrity. There are several approaches, each with unique advantages and disadvantages [ ]. Differential Ultracentrifugation can be recognized as the “gold standard,” technique that sequentially applies increasing centrifugal forces to separate EVs based on size and density. It is widely used and accessible for many labs. However, the method is labour-intensive, long processing times, and may lead to co-isolation of contaminants [ ]. Density Gradient Centrifugation incorporates a density medium (e.g., sucrose, iodixanol) to separate EVs from other particles. It provides improved purity but is time-consuming [ ]. Precipitation Methods involve use of Polyethylene glycol (PEG) or similar polymers to induce EV precipitation. The method is fast and easy to implement, but can lead to substantial co-precipitation of non-EV proteins, reducing selectivity. Size Exclusion Chromatography (SEC) separates vesicles based on size through columns packed with porous beads. It offers high purity but may reduce yield and is less efficient with large sample volumes [ ]. Affinity-Based Isolation utilizes specific interactions between EV surface proteins (e.g., Tim4-phosphatidylserine binding, antibody capture) for selective enrichment. The method have high specificity, especially for small EVs/exosomes, but can be costly and require specialized reagents [ ]. Microfluidic and Magnetic Bead-Based Platforms involve technological advances to enable rapid and automated EV isolation (e.g., magnetic Fe3O4@ZrO2 beads, microfluidic chips). These methods show promise in achieving high yield, purity, automation, and compatibility with clinical workflows [ ].
Rapid, scalable, and automatable methods are becoming more and more important when choosing extracellular vesicle (EV) isolation techniques, particularly for high-throughput and clinical applications. Although conventional techniques continue to be fundamental, more recent approaches offer increased effectiveness without sacrificing repeatability. The yield, purity, and biological significance of EVs can be significantly impacted by the isolation method selected, hence thorough cross-method validation is required for crucial downstream processes [ , ]. Moreover, sophisticated affinity-based and microfluidic technologies are becoming increasingly popular because of their ability to precisely identify particular EV subpopulations, which is especially useful for improvements in targeted medication administration and diagnostic accuracy [ ]. ## Role of extracellular vesicles in exerting anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory action
EVs have recently been identified as powerful intercellular communication mediators, particularly in regulating inflammation and immunological responses. They can influence target cells and produce a very precise therapeutic effect because they transport bioactive substances, such as proteins, lipids, mRNAs, and microRNAs (miRNAs). Figure  illustrates the pathology of the inflamed lung (cytokine storm, pathway activation, tissue damage) and the therapeutic effect of administered EVs. Clinical nanomedicine may soon adopt EV-based nanosystems for immunotherapies. Below sections given an overview on various EVs from different sources utilized in anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory applications.

 

## EVs derived from perinatal cells
Pregnancy involves complex immune modulation to protect the fetus while maintaining immune defence. They help suppress immune rejection of the fetus and support a systemic inflammatory response to fight infections. Pregnancy exemplifies a finely tuned state of immune adaptation, wherein the maternal immune system must balance immune tolerance toward the semi-allogeneic fetus bearing paternal antigens, while maintaining vigilance against pathogenic threats. EVs, especially exosomes, serve as pivotal mediators in maintaining this immunological equilibrium, facilitating the crosstalk necessary to ensure fetal survival and successful gestation. Placenta-derived EVs, especially exosomes, play key roles in regulating the maternal immune response. The perinatal derivatives obtained from perinatal tissue of the placenta and the umbilical cord constitute the hematopoietic system, mesenchymal stromal cells, epithelial cells, and amnion epithelial cells. These derivatives have been found to exhibit immunomodulatory and regenerative effects in various clinical conditions. Perinatal tissues emerged as a promising source for novel cellular therapies due to their immunomodulatory properties. These cells can be easily isolated, expanded, and are widely available as biological waste products [ ].
One of the studies explored the immunomodulatory potential of the secretome derived from human amniotic mesenchymal stromal cells (hAMSCs), focusing on its two key components: extracellular vesicle (EV) and EV-free fractions. Although the complete secretome (hAMSC-CM) and the EV-free fraction significantly inhibited immune cell activation, reducing T cell proliferation and polarization toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes, promoting regulatory T cells, supporting anti-inflammatory macrophage polarization, and suppressing B cell activation, the isolated EVs, at their native concentration, did not exhibit these immunomodulatory effects. hAMSC-derived EVs exert beneficial effects on non-immune cell types or under different experimental contexts, suggesting their therapeutic potential might be cell-type or dose-dependent [ ]. Once considered mere cellular debris, EVs are now recognized as vital mediators of intercellular communication. It was observed that B cells release MHC Class II-enriched exosomes capable of activating T cell responses. This was followed by the discovery that exosomes secreted by dendritic cells could modulate anti-tumor immunity in murine models. Emerging evidence demonstrates the multifaceted immunological roles of EVs, including antigen presentation via MHC–peptide complexes, immune modulation through ligand–receptor interactions, and the horizontal transfer of immunoregulatory proteins and non-coding RNAs all of which significantly influence the behavior of recipient cells. The physiological impact of EVs is closely linked to their endosomal origin and selective cargo-loading capabilities, highlighting their versatility. As research advances, the pivotal role of EVs in orchestrating immune responses and facilitating cell-to-cell communication continues to be increasingly appreciated.
Moreover, the effect of EVs, specifically exosomes, derived from maternal decidual and myometrial cells, which influence the fetal side during pregnancy, particularly under conditions of oxidative stress and inflammation, was explored. Using cigarette smoke extract and TNF-α to simulate these conditions, EVs from maternal cells were isolated and characterized, and their impact on fetal membrane cells, such as amniotic epithelial cells and chorionic trophoblast cells, was assessed. It was observed that maternal exposure to oxidative stress and inflammation during pregnancy alters exosomal protein cargo, potentially triggering fetal inflammatory responses. Chorion trophoblast cells may act as a barrier, limiting maternal exosome impact on the fetus. This study establishes that maternal uterine cell-derived exosomes, altered by oxidative or inflammatory stimuli, can induce cell-type-specific inflammatory responses in fetal membrane cells through paracrine signaling, highlighting their dual role as both mediators of feto-maternal communication and potential biomarkers or therapeutic vehicles in managing pregnancy outcomes [ ].
The therapeutic efficacy of EVs derived from mesenchymal stem cells of the umbilical cord (UC-EVs) was observed in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. UC-sEVs were isolated and administered via nebulization in a bleomycin-induced mouse model. The pulmonary inflammation was reduced to a greater extent, improving survival and decreasing fibrotic progression, accompanied by reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α levels and modulation of macrophage polarisation specially inhibiting M1 macrophages. RNA-sequencing identified miR-146a-5p as a consistently enriched therapeutic cargo that suppressed inflammatory signalling through the TRAF6/IRAK1 pathway [ ].
EVs serve as dynamic carriers of regulatory molecules capable of influencing the expression of key enzymes involved in inflammation and tissue homeostasis. Yu et al. demonstrated that exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSC-Exos) play a therapeutic role in laser-induced retinal injury by reducing damage, suppressing inflammation, and improving visual function. MSC-Exos significantly reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory mediators, especially MCP-1, leading to decreased macrophage infiltration and tissue damage. These findings suggest the therapeutic potential of EVs as regulators of inflammation and mediators of tissue repair in retinal, lungs and other inflammatory disorders. In the context of retinal injury, MSC-derived EVs were shown to downregulate MCP-1, a chemokine known for recruiting monocytes and exacerbating inflammation. This suppression not only reduced MCP-1 mRNA levels in retinal cells but also limited the enzymatic cascade associated with macrophage activation and infiltration. Additionally, MSC-Exos modulated the expression of other inflammation-linked enzymes and adhesion molecules such as TNF-α and ICAM-1. These proteins, often elevated in response to oxidative or mechanical stress, were effectively suppressed following EV treatment, highlighting the ability of EVs to dampen enzymatic pro-inflammatory signalling at both transcriptional and translational levels. By delivering a synergistic cargo of microRNAs and proteins, EVs orchestrate a targeted suppression of harmful enzymatic pathways, fostering an environment conducive to tissue preservation and functional recovery [ ].
Jiang et al., reported that human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes (hUCMSC-EXOs) have a therapeutic effect on acute liver failure (ALF) by suppressing inflammatory responses. Specifically, hUCMSC-EXOs downregulate the NLRP3 inflammasome and related pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6) in macrophages, and in vivo, they improve liver function and reduce tissue damage, suggesting their potential as a novel treatment strategy for ALF. Acting through a paracrine mechanism, these extracellular vesicles significantly reduce liver inflammation by downregulating the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway. Specifically, they suppress the activation of caspase-1 and lower the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-6, leading to decreased neutrophil infiltration, reduced oxidative stress, and diminished hepatocyte apoptosis. Additionally, hUCMSC-Exos were shown to alleviate biochemical and histological markers of liver damage ALT and AST, offering a cell-free, immunologically safe alternative to conventional stem cell therapies. These findings position exosomes as promising therapeutic agents capable of attenuating inflammation and promoting tissue repair in inflammatory liver disorders [ ].

## EVs derived from plant source
The findings suggest that plant-derived EVs play a promising role in modulating inflammatory responses in the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts and beyond. When ingested, these vesicles interact with mammalian intestinal cells and immune cells like macrophages and dendritic cells. EVs isolated from ginger, grape, grapefruit, and broccoli demonstrate their capacity to reduce inflammation in mouse models. It was observed that ginger- and grapefruit-derived EVs reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines e.g., IL-6, IL-1β and increased anti-inflammatory IL-10 expression. These EVs also promoted epithelial regeneration and re-epithelialization, aiding tissue repair during inflammation. Furthermore, broccoli-derived EVs were shown to induce tolerance in dendritic cells, enhancing their potential. The lipid content of EVs, such as phosphatidic acid and phosphatidylcholine, along with bioactive phytochemicals and plant microRNAs, is considered a key contributor to their anti-inflammatory effects. Also, some vesicle-derived miRNAs can influence gut microbiota composition and host immune gene expression [ ].
EVs derived from grapes, also known as grape-derived nanovectors (GNVs), have shown promising anti-inflammatory effects at tumor sites. These naturally occurring nanoparticles can encapsulate bioactive molecules like small RNAs, proteins, and lipids, and are biocompatible, non-toxic, and scalable. When coated with inflammatory receptor-enriched membranes from activated leukocytes, they created immunologically active GNVs. They hijack the leukocyte trafficking mechanisms of the body to home in on inflammatory tumor tissues. This targeted delivery approach enhances the accumulation of therapeutic agents such as doxorubicin or curcumin at the tumor site, leading to reduced inflammation and improved therapeutic efficacy. Their interaction with overexpressed chemokines and adhesion molecules like CXCR2 and LFA-1 at the tumor microenvironment further facilitates selective accumulation, making grape-derived EVs a compelling tool for anti-inflammatory and anticancer therapies. Grapefruit-derived nanvectors EVs, have demonstrated targeted anti-inflammatory and anticancer properties by leveraging the body’s immune trafficking mechanisms [ ].
EVs derived from algae Salvia miltiorrhiza exhibited therapeutic potential in lipopolysaccharide-induced acute lung injury. These EVs were isolated, characterised and confirmed to demonstrate favourable biosafety and stability profiles. In-vitro experiments using lipopolysaccharide-stimulated human umbilical vein endothelial cells demonstrate that the EVs reduced inflammatory cytokine expression IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α and promoted endothelial repair. EVs administration attenuated pulmonary oedema, inflammatory infiltration and tissue injury in an acute lung injury mouse model in vivo, while restoring vascular barrier integrity through upregulation of VE-cadherin. Metagenomic analyses further revealed that EVs modulated lung microbiota composition, reducing pathogenic bacterial enrichment associated with inflammation. The study support EVs as a biologically active, plant-derived vesicle system for mitigating pulmonary inflammation and vascular barrier dysfunction in acute lung injury [ ].
Yang et al. reported turmeric-derived EVs incorporated with zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8) as a nano agent against pulmonary inflammation. This ZIF-EVs achieved a drug loading efficiency of 12.8%, representing a 4.7-fold improvement over conventional liposomes and effectively addressing the low drug-loading limitation of tumour-derived EVs. Upon light activation, it induces immunogenic cell death and facilitates antigen-presenting cell activation and antigen availability. Downregulation of PD-L1 was observed following siRNA antibody treatment, suppressing tumour immune evasion and enhancing the antitumour immune response. Treated lung injury in vivo models exhibited downregulation of pro-inflammatory mediators, attenuation of immune cell infiltration, and preservation of lung tissue architecture. Transcriptomic and histopathological analyses demonstrated modulation of cell surface receptors and inflammatory signalling pathways relevant to pulmonary pathology. The biological evaluation further confirmed vesicle-mediated regulation of cellular inflammatory response without observable toxicity [ ].
The exosomes released from lung epithelial cells expressing SARS-CoV-2 proteins Nsp12 and Nsp13 contribute to lung inflammation. These exosomes activate lung macrophages via the NF-κB pathway, triggering a cytokine storm characterized by elevated TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, which in turn induces apoptosis in lung epithelial cells. One of the studies reported that ginger-derived exosome-like nanoparticles, particularly those carrying miRNA aly-miR396a-5p, were able to inhibit the inflammatory cascade and mitigate virus-induced lung inflammation. These plant-derived miRNAs specifically target and suppress the expression of viral genes like Nsp12 and the spike protein, thereby reducing cytokine production and lung tissue damage in both in vitro and in vivo models. Ginger-derived exosomes act as natural nanocarriers of therapeutic miRNAs that can cross species barriers and modulate mammalian immune responses. In this study, aly-miR396a-5p from ginger-derived exosomes directly inhibited the expression of SARS-CoV-2 Nsp12, a viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase essential for viral replication and inflammation. This suppression led to reduced activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, evidenced by decreased phosphorylation of IKKβ, IκBα, and p65, which are key regulators of inflammatory gene expression. Additionally, these exosomes attenuated the activation of JNK, a stress-activated protein kinase involved in apoptosis. As a result, levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β were significantly decreased, and lung epithelial cell apoptosis was reduced [ ].
Chen et al. reported ginger-derived exosome-like nanoparticles as potent anti-inflammatory agents by specifically targeting the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, a critical mediator of inflammation in diseases like Alzheimer’s, type 2 diabetes, and gout. These vesicles, rich in bioactive lipids, were shown to block the assembly of the inflammasome complex in macrophages without altering the expression levels of its core components, such as NLRP3, ASC, and Caspase-1. Instead, these exosomes inhibited key steps like ASC oligomerization and speck formation, suppressed Caspase-1 autocleavage, and significantly reduced the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-18, as well as pyroptotic cell death. Notably, the lipid constituents within these exosomes, not their proteins or RNAs, were responsible for this immunomodulatory effect. This selective suppression offers a promising, food-derived strategy for controlling excessive inflammatory responses in chronic diseases by disrupting upstream inflammasome activation rather than merely neutralizing downstream cytokines [ ].
Given their accessibility and bioactivity, plant-derived nanovesicles are rapidly emerging as next-generation alternatives to traditional culture-based EVs. Jae et al., demonstrated that exosome-like nanovesicles derived from cabbage and red cabbage possess potent anti-inflammatory properties in vitro. Using RAW264.7 macrophages stimulated with lipopolysaccharide; it was observed that pre-treatment with both cabbages significantly suppressed the expression of key proinflammatory mediators at both the gene and protein levels. Specifically, mRNA levels of interleukin-6, interleukin-1β, and cyclooxygenase-2 were markedly downregulated in a dose-dependent manner, as shown through quantitative RT-PCR. Furthermore, ELISA confirmed the reduced secretion of IL-6 and IL-1β proteins, while western blot analysis revealed a notable decrease in COX-2 protein expression. The EVs derived from both the cabbage not only enhanced mammalian cell proliferation but also exhibited significant anti-inflammatory activity in immune cells, alongside anti-apoptotic effects in human keratinocytes and fibroblasts. Notably, these nanovesicles efficiently encapsulated anticancer agents (doxorubicin) and facilitated targeted delivery into human cells, emphasizing their promise as alternative drug delivery vehicles [ ].
Exosome-like nanoparticles derived from edible mulberry bark exhibit potent protective effects against intestinal inflammation, primarily through a unique molecular cascade involving heat shock protein HSPA8, AhR (arylhydrocarbon receptor), and COP9 Signalosome Subunit 8 (COPS8). The mulberry-derived exosome delivers HSPA8 into intestinal epithelial cells, where it binds to and activates the AhR pathway, a key sensor involved in immune modulation. This activation leads to transcriptional upregulation of COPS8, which plays a crucial role in dampening inflammatory signalling. Specifically, COPS8 facilitates of Cullin 1, thereby modulating proteasome activity and promoting the expression of antimicrobial peptides that reinforce mucosal defense. In a DSS-induced colitis in-vivo model, exosome treatment suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and IL-1β, and maintained intestinal barrier integrity. Notably, the anti-inflammatory effect was abolished in COPS8 knockout mice, confirming COPS8 as a critical effector in the mulberry exosome-mediated mitigation of intestinal inflammation [ ].

## EVs derived from animal source
Mesenchymal stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles (MSC-EVs) act as molecular messengers, transferring bioactive proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids to other cells, thereby influencing their function. These EVs have shown strong potential in regulating immune responses by converting immune cells into anti-inflammatory phenotypes. Compared to cell-based MSC therapies, EVs offer safer, more efficient, and more manageable alternatives, as they avoid risks like tumorigenicity and are capable of crossing physiological barriers. Their immunomodulatory capacity and ease of application make them promising candidates for treating inflammatory and autoimmune conditions. The development of phosphatidylserine-modified macrophage-derived EVs encapsulating prednisolone (PS-EVs@Pre) for targeted drug delivery in acute lung injury was evaluated. Phosphatidylserine modification enhanced vesicle uptake by activated pulmonary macrophages and promoted preferential lung accumulation in an LPS-induced mouse model. PS-EVs@Pre exhibited sustained drug release, reduced macrophage oxidative stress, and suppressed pro-inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and iNOS, in vitro and in vivo. The in vivo biodistribution study revealed greater accumulation of PS-EVs@Pre in the lungs of the mice model. Treatment attenuated pulmonary oedema, inflammatory infiltration, tissue injury and apoptosis while improving survival without detectable systemic toxicity [ ].
One of the studies reported that mesenchymal stem cell derived extracellular vesicles (MSC-EVs) possess a significant capacity to attenuate kidney inflammation in a porcine model of metabolic syndrome and renal artery stenosis. When injected intrarenally, these EVs localized predominantly within renal tubular cells and macrophages, where they exerted anti-inflammatory effects. The treatment shifted the renal immune environment by reducing pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages and increasing reparative M2 macrophages, effectively lowering the M1/M2 ratio. This immunomodulation was accompanied by a marked reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and MCP-1, and a restoration of anti-inflammatory cytokines IL-10 and IL-4. MSC-EVs act as paracrine effectors, delivering anti-inflammatory signals (especially IL-10) to renal immune and epithelial cells. This reprograms macrophages, reduces cytokine storms, and breaks the cycle of inflammation–hypoxia–fibrosis in chronic kidney injury. Notably, the EVs were enriched with IL-10 mRNA, and when this cytokine was silenced, the vesicles lost their therapeutic efficacy, highlighting IL-10’s pivotal role in mediating the anti-inflammatory and renoprotective effects. MSC-EVs can serve as a cell-free therapeutic strategy capable of reprogramming immune responses and mitigating chronic renal inflammation [ ]. TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β and MCP-1 are critically involved in the pulmonary inflammatory processes, the observed reduction in these mediators suggests that the extracellular vesicles may contribute to the attenuation of lung inflammation. In another study, the therapeutic potential of EVs derived from canine adipose tissue–mesenchymal stem cells (cAT-MSCs), with a specific focus on enhancing their immunomodulatory properties through hypoxic preconditioning, was evaluated. By treating cAT-MSCs with deferoxamine (DFO) to mimic hypoxic conditions, it was aimed to alter the EV composition and assess their efficacy in modulating inflammation. The preconditioned EVs (EV-DFO) were then administered intranasally in a mouse model of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, serving as an in vivo model to evaluate their impact on immune regulation and inflammatory pathology. EVs derived from cAT-MSCs exhibit potent anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties. These EVs can modulate immune responses by promoting the polarization of macrophages toward the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype and enhancing the population of regulatory T (Treg) cells. Particularly, when cAT-MSCs are preconditioned with DFO, the resulting EVs significantly reduce inflammatory cell infiltration, demyelination, and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in the spinal cord and spleen. This effect was mediated, in part, through the modulation of the STAT3 signalling pathway, which plays a central role in T cell regulation. EVs treated with DFO also increased COX-2 expression, which may further support Treg activation and immune homeostasis. Also, EV-DFO showed reduced expression of STAT3 signalling proteins. It was observed that cAT-MSC-derived EVs, especially when optimized through hypoxic preconditioning, offer a safe, cell-free strategy for mitigating inflammation and restoring immune balance [ ]. Further, a study elucidates the function of tumour-derived EVs (TEVs) in modulating lung inflammation during premetastatic niche formation. The result identifies the long noncoding RNA lncOSLMT as a essential TEV cargo preferentially delivered to lung-resident cells. Uptake of lncOSLMT-incorporating TEVs promoted pulmonary inflammatory remodelling by activating inflammatory signalling pathways and enhancing the recruitment and polarisation of immune cells within the lung microenvironment. In vivo models demonstrated that TEV-mediated transfer of lncoSLMT-induced sustained lung inflammation, increased vascular permeability and extracellular matrix remodeling, creating a permissive inflammatory niche that favored metastatic colonization. These findings define a mechanistic link between TEV-mediated RNA transfer and inflammation-driven lung premetastatic niche formation [ ].
The bovine milk-derived EVs significantly amplify lung inflammation and promote M1 macrophage polarization in mice exposed to organic dust, a common occupational hazard in agriculture. Mice fed with intact milk EVs showed heightened cellular infiltration into the lungs and elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, TNF-α, CXCL1, and IL-12/23, compared to those fed a control diet where EVs were disrupted by sonication. This pro-inflammatory shift was consistently observed across both acute and repetitive dust exposure models. Notably, macrophages from EV-fed mice exhibited a classical M1 phenotype, with reduced levels of regulatory markers like IL-10 and arginase, while DEV-fed mice tended towards an M2 polarization profile associated with tissue repair. Histological analyses supported these findings, revealing more severe alveolar and perivascular inflammation and prominent lymphoid aggregates in the lungs of EV-fed mice. Complementary in vitro experiments confirmed that purified milk-derived EVs directly influence macrophage polarization under dust exposure, underscoring their role as dietary modulators of immune response [ ]. EVs derived from airway basal stem cells (BC) induces reparative effects in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) through paracrine signalling. Lineage tracing confirmed endogenous BC contribution to epithelial regeneration after injury. While ex vivo expanded BC transplantation restored alveolar complex in elastase and cigarette smoke-induced models. Nebulised BC-EVs recapitulated these benefits enhancing angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation via PI3K-Akt activation and lung function as evidenced by improved blood gas parameters. Proteomic profiling revealed BC-EV enrichment in lung development proteins and shared cargos promoting negative regulation of inflammation distinguishing them from mesenchymal stem cell EVs [ ].

## EVs derived from microbial source
Acute lung injury (ALI) caused by infection progress with damage to alveolar epithelial membrane, inflammatory cells accumulation and pulmonary edema. Therefore, microbiota plays vital role in regulating lung barriers and functions. In this regard, C. butyricum derived extracellular vesicles (135 nm) can be used for anti-inflammatory and protective action towards ALI via reducing inflammatory cytokines or factors (IL-6, IL-8, and tumor necrosis factor TNF-α), inhibiting nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) and toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/MyD88 pathway and regulating pulmonary microbiota. It also downregulated expression of BAX, Cleaved Caspase-3, TLR4 and MyD88 proteins and upregulated expression of Bcl-2 protein, ZO-1 and occludin. This resulted in inhibition of lung tissue apoptosis, and protective effect towards lung barrier by maintaining the integrity of endothelial cells [ ]. EVs can also be used for modulating signaling pathways and regulating autophagy related genes for ALI treatment [ ].
The ideal drug carrying properties, low-immunogenicity, membrane interaction for receptor-mediated internalization, high penetration, prolong circulation, surface functionalization with cell specific targeting moiety, high structural and biological stability and crossing biological barriers EVs demonstrated unique payload potential for various drugs and imaging agents [ , , ]. EVs can be used for carriers for metal nanoparticles to overcome their pharmacokinetics limitations, adverse effects and immunotherapeutic ability toward tumor angiogenesis therapy [ , ]. The drug loading in EVs are achieved by either exogeneous loading or endogenous loading method [ ].

## Hybrid Extracellular Vesicles Systems
The term hybrid extracellular vesicle systems can be used for engineered vesicles combining conventional vesicles with synthetic or bioactive components to improve the function and potency of systems for drug encapsulation, gene delivery and targetability. The EVs can be fuse or combined with polymers, lipids, biomolecules or nanoparticles for controlled drug delivery, improving stability, prolonging systemic circulation, escaping immune system, providing stealth properties, tumor targeting, high cellular internalization, and multifunctionality like imaging [ ]. Sato et al. [ ] reported hybrid exosomes of membrane fused with liposomes using freeze thaw method. The hybrid system revealed modified exosome surface with decreased immunogenicity, high colloidal stability, increased blood circulation, high drug encapsulation potential and functionalization with targeting moity. Ruan et al., reported macrophage exosomes combined Fe3O4 nanoparticles for loading of 2,2′-Azobisisoheptonitrile (ABVN). The exosomes provided targeted delivery avoiding premature release of ABVN. Upon exposure to near infrared light, Fe3O4 nanoparticles generated heat that triggered the release and decomposition of ABVN to produce free-radicals for cancer therapy [ ]. Hybrid EVs can be fabricated by various methods including incubation, electrostatic interactions, polyethylene glycol-mediated fusion, co-extrusion, freeze-thawing, or EV surface modification [ ]. These methods favor loading of variable drugs, improving pharmacokinetics, endosomal escape and/or targeting.

## Site Targeted Extracellular Vesicles
EVs are naturally occurring, membrane-bound, spherical structures created by cells that are released into the bloodstream or surrounding bodily fluids by the majority of cell types under a variety of physiological and pathologic circumstances. Several biological fluids, such as blood, bile, urine, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), has been reported to contain EVs. Since EVs enable cells to exchange cellular components, which may impact a number of signalling pathways, they serve as essential for cell-to-cell communication [ ]. However, lack of cell-targeting specificity and the quick removal of exogenous EVs from the bloodstream may restrict the therapeutic use of EVs. Targeting ligands attached to polyethylene glycol (PEG) can be used to adorn EVs, which may improve cargo delivery and increase EV accumulation in certain tissues. The systemic circulation was prolonged and strong specific binding to EGFR-overexpressing tumor cells was facilitated by EGFR-specific nanobodies on the surface of EVs [ ]. Wang et al. [ ] designed superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIONs) equipped exosomes for magnetically responsive targeted delivery of doxorubicin. the resultant system improved tumor housing potential and minimized organ or tissue toxicity for effective chemotherapy in NSCLC.
Ligand-targeted EVs that have been altered with molecules such as anti-ICAM-1 antibodies improved specificity for the inflammatory lung endothelium, where acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) and other conditions cause a 50% overexpression of ICAM-1 [ ]. Research indicates that these EVs improve the delivery of anti-inflammatory drugs and lower TNF-α levels by 30% while achieving a 40% greater lung accumulation in mouse models when compared to non-targeted EVs [ ]. In models of lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced lung injury, peptide-targeted EVs, which are designed with lung-homing peptides such as CGKRK, target alveolar macrophages, important contributors to lung inflammation, increasing drug uptake by 35% and decreasing IL-6 expression by 25% [ ]. In rat model, magnetically targeted EVs that are loaded with superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles and directed by an external magnetic field provide precise delivery, reduced neutrophil infiltration by 40%, and achieved 60% lung retention as opposed to 20% for free EVs. While each approach improves site-specificity, issues like ligand stability, scalability, and possible immune reactions need to be optimized to match your knowledge of targeted delivery systems. Kim et al., developed an engineered EV platform that maximizes therapeutic impact by precisely delivering apelin to PAH lesions. Author positioned the apelin peptide on the EVs surface with its C-terminus fully exposed using the type II transmembrane protein interferon-induced transmembrane protein 3 (IFITM3), maintaining the essential binding interface for functional interaction with the apelin receptor. Author developed CAR-apelin EVs by integrating the PAH-targeting peptide CARSKNKDC (CAR), which selectively binds to heparan sulphate overexpressed on PAH endothelial cells, into the IFITM3-apelin scaffold in order to further improve targeting specifically. In PAH models, these dual-engineered EVs showed remarkable therapeutic efficacy and targeting. As demonstrated by decreased right ventricular systolic pressure and hypertrophy, CAR-apelin EVs markedly reserved pathological vascular remodelling and enhanced cardiac function [ ].
EVs are usually provided with targeting peptides or antibodies by overexpressing these compounds in the EV-donor cells via transfection retroviral infection, or lentiviral infection with the goal to transform them into treatments that specially attack their target cells. In order to show the targeting epitope on the surface of EVs the most popular approach is to express it using a transmembrane domain or fuse it with an EVs membrane protein like Lamb2b and CD63. The successful creation of brain-targeting EVs from dendritic cells transfected with a plasmid encoding the brain-targeting RVG peptide and EVs protein Lamb2b is a well-known example. The brain can receive RNA therapies from the engineered EVs because they can penetrate the blood-brain barrier [ ]. Kowalcyzk et al., prepared the surface bioengineered EVs for the treatment of lung cancer. when given intravenously to nude Balb/c mice with A549 cancer, magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated that the as-bioengineered PTHTRWA-EVs loaded with superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIO) cargos reach the expanding tumor. A high affinity of the developed peptide for the α5β1 integrin has been demonstrated by molecular dynamic in silico studies PTHTRWA-bioengineered EVs demonstrated no sign of cytotoxicity or genotoxicity in preclinical safety studies [ ]. Asfiya et al., fabricate the EVs engineered siRNA for the treatment of lung cancer. The author observed that B7-H4 in tumor cells H1299 and A549 and their spheroids had been effectively knocked down by the standard dosage of EV-SiRB. On normal lung cells, though, it had less of an impact (MRC9). Subsequently, author utilized NSCLC xenograph models to establish the EV-siRB complex antitumor therapeutic effect. The results of the research demonstrate how well the hybrid EVs-siRB system delivers therapeutic siRNA to tumor cells and provide a means of investigating its efficacy in solid tumor models, such as patient-derived NSCLC [ ]. Pham et al., developed the peptide and nanobodies conjugated EVs for the treatment of lung cancer. EVs may accumulate in EGFR-positive cancer cells both in vitro and in vivo when they are conjugated with an EGFR-targeted peptide or an anti-EGFR nanobody. In a xenografted mouse model of EGFR-positive lung cancer, systemic administration of paclitaxel by EGFR-targeting EVs at low doses dramatically boosts drug efficacy. The approach may additionally be employed to conjugate EVs with peptide and nanobodies which target other receptors, like HER2 and SIRP alpha. The conjugated EVs can deliver RNA in addition to small molecules, which encourages the broad range of users for EVs in cancer therapy. Bypassing the need for genetic and chemical alterations, this straightforward, effective, and adaptable technique for EVs surface modification enables for the safe and targeted delivery of therapeutic payloads to target cells [ ].

## Pulmonary delivery of extracellular vesicles
Asthma, COPD, pulmonary fibrosis, and acute lung injury are among the respiratory conditions for which pulmonary delivery has drawn interest [ ]. The nanosystems size may affect the deposition, distribution, retention, and aerosolization behavior within the lungs for a successful therapeutic translation [ ]. The pattern of EV deposition in the respiratory tract is strongly influenced by their size. Larger EVs (> 500 nm) tend to settle in the upper airways due to inertial impaction, whereas nanometer-sized EVs (~ 30–200 nm) primarily deposit in the alveolar areas because of deep penetration. Because of their increased absorption by macrophages and alveolar epithelial cells, as well as their decreased mucociliary clearance, smaller EVs may have more tissue penetration and longer retention. Larger vesicles, on the other hand, are more vulnerable to phagocytosis and quick clearance. Larger microvesicles may elicit stronger but shorter-acting immune responses, although studies indicate that exosome-sized EVs have superior therapeutic retention in lung parenchyma, possibly resulting in persistent bioactivity.
To fully utilize their therapeutic potential in the treatment of lung illnesses, future initiatives should concentrate on enhancing EV formulations for aerosol stability and targeting efficiency while maintaining their biological functioning. For pulmonary delivery, extracellular vesicles (EVs) loaded in boronated cyclodextrin framework (BCF) was employed. The EVs via pulmonary route will result in minimal absorption and transport to systemic circulation, thereby minimizing off target toxicity. The modified EVs in BCF showed extended release over the time period and exhibited superior anti-inflammatory activity. Pulmonary administered (Cyclic Arginyl glycylaspartic acid) cRGD-EVs-BCF were biocompatible and exhibited pH/H2O2 responsive release working as effective platform for treatment of inflammatory lung disorder [ ].

## Conclusion
Extracellular vesicles (EVs) have recently gained widespread popularity as adaptable and promising nanomedicine for management of inflammatory lung disorders including asthma, pulmonary fibrosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). EVs exhibit a distinct advantage in drug delivery due to their inherent biocompatibility, non-immunogenicity, and encapsulation capacity towards bioactive compounds. EVs have demonstrated strong anti-inflammatory, regenerative and immunomodulation properties. Additionally, surface functionalization of EVs with proteins, peptides, lipid, polymers or nanoparticles can further enhances the therapeutic efficacy by enabling targeted delivery to inflamed lung tissues or specific cell types. Pulmonary administration of EVs can further allow localized delivery to inflammation site, increasing therapeutic bioavailability while minimizing systemic toxicity. But, the translation of EVs from laboratory to clinic faces several technological and regulatory hurdles due to complexities in manufacturing, quality control and safety profiles. A primary bottleneck is the difficulty in producing high purity EVs at a scale sufficient for clinical use. Issues like labor-intensive, time-consuming, and poor-yielding isolation process, batch-to-batch variability due to variation in the protein profiles and molecular contents, safety barriers due to off-target accumulation, etc., have to be addressed by interdisciplinary actions. In conclusion, even though this field currently lacks a unified framework to overcome these multifaceted translation barriers, EVs represent a promising therapeutic platform for the effective management of inflammatory lung diseases through smart drug delivery, targeted tissue interaction, and minimally invasive pulmonary administration.
