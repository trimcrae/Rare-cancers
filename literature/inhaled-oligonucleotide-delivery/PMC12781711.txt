## Abstract

## Objective
To investigate the role of lncRNA GAS5 in COPD and its underlying mechanism.

## Methods
COPD mouse model was conducted as an in vivo inflammatory response model. CSE was used to stimulate human airway epithelial cells (16HBE) and human normal lung epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) to establish an in vitro inflammatory response model. Recombinant adenoviruses containing GAS5 or GAS5 shRNA were used to overexpression or knockdown of GAS5. The expression of p120/NF-κB was detected by RT-PCR and Western Blot. The distribution of NF-κB p65 protein in cells was detected by western blot and immunofluorescence staining. The expression levels of IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α in downstream NF-κB were detected by RT-PCR and ELISA.

## Result
The expression of lncRNA GAS5 was downregulated in COPD in vivo and in vitro. We showed that overexpression of GAS5 alleviates inflammation and NF-κB activation in COPD in vivo and in vitro. Moreover, we demonstrated that GAS5 regulate NF-κB activation by bind with p120. Knocking down of p120 inhibits the protective effect of GAS5 on inflammation.

## Conclusion
LncRNA GAS5 can bind with p120, enhance p120 stability and inhibit the NF-κB pathway and production of inflammatory factors downstream of NF-κB, thus playing a protective role in the airway.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), is a group of chronic airway obstructive diseases. It progresses slowly and is characterized by irreversible airflow restriction, which will eventually lead to chronic respiratory failure [ ]. Currently, COPD is the third leading cause of death in the world, globally, COPD has attracted much attention because it reduces the quality of life of patients, increases the hospitalization rate and medical burden of patients, and leads to an increased risk of premature death of patients [ , ]. At the same time, during the epidemic of the global public health disease COVID-19, patients are more likely to be infected with the novel coronavirus, and infection is associated with the severity and mortality of COPD [ ]. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanism of COPD is very important for improving the treatment and prognosis of COPD.
The breakdown of the airway epithelial barrier and persistent inflammatory response are closely related to the occurrence and development of COPD [ ]. The airway epithelial barrier is mainly composed of continuous airway epithelial cells, and its integrity depends on the tight junctions and adhesion junctions between cells, among which the intercellular adhesion is the key to maintaining the integrity of the polar alignment and homeostasis of the epithelial structure [ ]. p120 catenin (p120) is an adhesion molecule in the adhesion junction complex, an important component of adhesion junctions in epithelial cells and other polarized cells, and plays a key role in the integrity of the airway epithelial barrier [ ].
A large number of studies have confirmed that NF-κB signaling pathway play a significant role in COPD pathology [ , ]. NF-κB is activated and activates the expression of a variety of pro-inflammatory genes in COPD, including IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α and other inflammatory factors, leading to the amplification of inflammatory response, which is particularly obvious in the acute exacerbation of COPD [ , ]. NF-κB activation creates a self-sustaining inflammatory milieu that directly leads to the bronchial inflammation, tissue damage, and mucus hypersecretion that define chronic COPD [ ]. NF-κB-driven oxidative stress inactivates Histone Deacetylase 2 (HDAC2), which disrupts the glucocorticoid receptor’s ability to suppress inflammation, thereby creating a state of steroid resistance that is paradoxically perpetuated by NF-κB itself [ ]. In COPD exacerbations, pathogens potently activate NF-κB, which acts as the central amplifier of a destructive inflammatory storm, unleashing a flood of mediators that create a vicious cycle of inflammation and accelerated decline, all atop a steroid-resistant baseline that makes control profoundly difficult. Given its role in the trilogy of core pathologies—chronic inflammation, steroid resistance, and exacerbations—NF-κB represents a rational and highly relevant therapeutic target in COPD [ ].
Studies have shown p120 catenin acts as an endogenous anti-inflammatory factor and regulates inflammation by regulating the activation of NF-κB [ ]. P120 catenin overexpression has been shown to inhibit RhoA in several instances [ ]. More specifically, higher cytosolic levels of delta-catenin inhibit RhoA activity [ ]. According to the previous results of our laboratory, we found that in airway inflammation models such as LPS-stimulated mechanical injury and CSE stimulation, the expression level of p120 mRNA and protein in human airway epithelial cells 16HBE was down-regulated, and NF-κB signaling can be activated through RhoA/ROCK pathway to play an important role in airway inflammation [ ]. Our previous results showed that p120-mediated NF-κB signaling activation in airway epithelial inflammation is partially RhoA/ROCK dependent [ ].
LncRNAs are a class of RNA molecules longer than 200 nucleotides that control gene expression, chromatin modification, mRNA stability, miRNA sponge, mRNA degradation and protein translation. LncRNAs have emerged as important regulators of inflammation-related gene expression and signaling pathways [ ]. A large number of studies have shown that LncRNAs affect inflammatory responses by controlling the NF-κB pathway and play a key role in lung diseases such as COPD [ ]. Decreased lncRNA GAS5 expression serves as a prognostic biomarker in COPD, being inversely correlated with patient survival and the risk of severe exacerbations. GAS5 modulates the activity of key signaling pathways in COPD, such as NF-κB and JAK-STAT, by binding to the glucocorticoid receptor and sequestering miRNAs like miR-21 [ ]. Li et al. constructed both in vivo and in vitro models of lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced acute lung injury (ALI), demonstrating that lncRNA GAS5 expression is significantly reduced in ALI-affected lung tissues. Their findings further revealed that GAS5 attenuates inflammatory responses by targeting the miR-429/DUSP1 signaling axis [ ].
In this study, we showed that overexpression of GAS5 alleviates inflammation and NF-κB activation in COPD in vivo and in vitro. Moreover, we demonstrated that GAS5 regulate NF-κB activation by bind with p120. We investigated the role of lncRNA GAS5 in COPD and its potential regulatory mechanism, and it may serve as a new biomarkers or therapeutic targets for the diagnosis and treatment of COPD.

## Materials and methods

## Human specimen collection
Paraffin specimens of COPD lung tissue from patients with clinical COPD complicated with lung cancer after surgery were obtained in the Department of Pathology of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology. And paraffin specimens of normal lung tissues resected by lung cancer surgery were used as controls. All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Use Subcommittee of Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

## Animals and CS exposure
Wild-type C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). All testing methods were performed in accordance with the guidelines and regulations approved by the Animal Use Subcommittee of the Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology. To establish the COPD mouse model, mice inhaled LPS (2 mg/kg in 50 µl saline) at day 1 and day 14. The mice in the smoking group were placed in the passive smoking animal poisoning system, and exposed to cigarette smoke (10 cigarettes each hour, 1 h each time, twice a day) for 8 weeks, excluding the days of LPS injection, After the poisoning, the mice were put back into the clean room for feeding normally. The mice in the normal control group were fed under the same conditions exposed to normal air without smoking. Cigarette used in this study is Hongjinlong brand, produced by Hubei Tobacco Industry CO., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Each cigarette yields 9 mg tar, 0.7 mg nicotine and 12 mg CO.

## Cigarette Smoke Extract (CSE) Preparation
The cell medium is placed in a sterilized drainage bag and drained with a syringe. After that, the cigarette is ignited to make it burn fully, and the cigarette smoke is extracted by slowly drawing with a syringe. At the same time, the cigarette smoke is fully dissolved into the culture medium by shaking well. Smoke three tubes of higher concentration smoke per cigarette, and draw a total of three cigarettes. Sterilization with a 0.2 μm filter, that is, the final concentration storage solution, diluted with different volumes of culture solution according to the required concentration, and then used.

## Cell culture and airway inflammation model construction
Human airway epithelial cells (16 HBE cells) were given by Dr D.Gruenert (California); Human normal lung epithelial cells (BEAS-2B cells) were purchased from Fenghui Bio, preserved in liquid nitrogen. When the state of 16HBE cells and BEAS-2B cells was good and reached 80%-90% confluence, the CSE storage solution was diluted with the complete cell culture medium according to the experimental purpose, and the required concentration of CSE was prepared to stimulate the above two cells.

## Histological assessments
The lung tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Then 5 μm-thick lung sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The representative images were obtained using an optical microscope (ECLIPSE 80i, Nikon, Japan). The morphometric analyses was performed using ImageJ software.

## Masson’s collagen staining
The lung tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Then tissues were sliced into 5-µm sections, and Masson staining was performed using Masson’s Trichrome Staining Kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The representative images were obtained using an optical microscope (ECLIPSE 80i, Nikon, Japan). The morphometric analyses was performed using ImageJ software.

## Immunofluorescence
When the cells grow to 50%~60% confluence, after 15% CSE stimulation for 6 h, 4% paraformaldehyde was added to them and fixed at room temperature, then treated with membrane permeable reagent 0.3% Triton X-100 at room temperature, and incubated with primary antibody against NF-κB-p65 (Cell Signaling Technology, San Jose, CA, USA) at 4 °C overnight. The nuclei were re-stained with DAPI. The representative images were obtained using an Eclipse FV500 confocal laser scanning microscopy system (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

## In Situ Hybridization (ISH)
GAS5 expression in human and mouse COPD lung tissues was detected by digoxigenin labeled antisense oligonucleotide probes. ISH was performed using the ISH kit (Boster Bio-Engineering Company, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Immunohistochemistry
P120 immunohistological staining was performed by streptavidin-perosidase (SP) method as described previously [ ].

## Quantitative real-time PCR
When the cells grow to 80%~90% confluence, after 15% CSE stimulation for 6 h, Quantitative real-time PCR were performed as described previously [ ].

## Enzyme-Linked Immune Sorbent Assay (ELISA)
When the cells grow to 80%~90% confluence, after 15% CSE stimulation for 9 h, the supernatant of cell lysate was collected and a commercial kit from MultiSciences Biotech was used to detect IL-6, IL-8 and TNFα by ELISA, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The concentrations of IL-6 IL-8 and TNFα were measured by using an internal standard curve.

## Western blot
When the cells grow to 80%~90% confluence, after 15% CSE stimulation for 6 h, Western blot analysis were performed as described previously [ ].

## Recombinant adenovirus and adeno-associated virus production
16 HBE cells were infected in vitro with recombinant adenoviruses containing GAS5, GAS5 shRNA, p120 shRNA, green fluorescent protein (GFP, as a negative control), or scrambled shRNA (as a non-specific control), respectively. GAS5 was overexpressed and knocked down in vivo using recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 6 (rAAV6; Obio Technology). The expression of GAS5 was manipulated in vivo via tail vein injection of rAAV6 which contains control and GAS5 with a lung-specific promoter (SP-A promoter) or scrambled shRNA and GAS5 shRNA. Mice were injected with 50 µl virus containing 2 × 1011 VG of the AAV6 vector genomes via intratracheal infusion. All adenoviruses and AAV were supplied by Obio Technology (Shanghai, China).

## RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) analysis
RIP assay was performed using EZ-Magna RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The enriched RNAs were detected by qPCR.

## Ubiquitination assay
The expression constructs with wild type UB-HA, K48R-UB-HA (lysine 48 mutated to arginine), K63R-UB-HA (lysine 63 mutated to arginine) and K48O-UB-HA(all lysines except lysine 48 mutated to arginines) were used for in vitro ubiquitination analysis. p120 ubiquitination was analyzed by western blot with antibodies specific for ubiquitin.

## Statistical analysis
Each experiment in this study was independent and was repeated more than 3 times. The data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. One-way analysis of variance was used to compare the mean among multiple groups. p < 0.05 was considered as significant difference.

## Results

## Downregulation of lncRNA GAS5 expression in COPD
Many literatures have reported that lncRNA plays an important regulatory role in various inflammatory diseases and tumors, including regulating the occurrence and development of COPD. We first examined the expression profile of long non-coding RNA GAS5 in COPD pathogenesis. Comparative analysis revealed a significant downregulation of GAS5 in lung tissues from COPD patients compared to non-COPD controls (Fig.  A). This finding was further corroborated by in situ hybridization, which demonstrated markedly reduced GAS5 signals in COPD-affected lung regions (Fig.  B). In addition, the same result was detected in the COPD mouse model, GAS5 expression was also decreased in lung tissue of COPD model mice compared to control mice (Fig.  C and D). Consistent with established protocols for modeling cigarette smoke-induced airway inflammation, we exposed 16HBE cells to escalating concentrations of cigarette smoke extract (CSE, 0–15%) and observed a dose-dependent suppression of GAS5 expression (Fig.  E). This inverse correlation between CSE concentration and GAS5 levels was further validated in BEAS-2B human bronchial epithelial cells (Fig.  F).
 

## Overexpression of GAS5 alleviates inflammation and NF-κB activation in COPD in vivo
To investigate the functional role of lncRNA GAS5 in COPD pathogenesis, C57BL/6J mice were administered either AAV-GAS5 or AAV-Control via tail vein injection. Two weeks post-viral delivery, a COPD model was established through chronic cigarette smoke exposure combined with LPS challenge, as outlined in the experimental timeline (Fig.  A). 
 We detected the expression of AAV GAS5 in vivo, and the immunofluorescence results showed that AAV GAS5 could express GAS5 in lung tissue, mainly in SP-A positive cells, that is, alveolar type II epithelial cells. In addition, some GAS5 was also expressed in SP-A negative cells (Fig. S1A). RT-qPCR results also showed that AAV GAS5 could achieve GAS5 overexpression (Fig. S1B). 
Histopathological analysis of lung tissues revealed that AAV overexpressing of GAS5 significantly attenuated emphysematous alveolar destruction and reduced inflammatory cell infiltration compared to the control group, as evidenced by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining (Fig.  B). Masson stain showed that AAV overexpressing of GAS5 reduced the degree of fibrosis than control group (Fig.  C). Immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated that AAV-mediated GAS5 overexpression significantly attenuated the infiltration of CD11b⁺ and F4/80⁺ inflammatory cells compared to the control group (Fig.  D). RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that AAV-mediated GAS5 overexpression significantly downregulated mRNA levels of inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α) and fibrotic markers (Col I, Col III, and CTGF) compared to the AAV-Control group (Fig.  E). Consistent with this anti-inflammatory phenotype, Western blot analysis revealed a concomitant reduction in phosphorylated NF-κB p65 (p-p65) protein expression in GAS5-treated mice (Fig.  F).

## GAS5 regulate inflammatory cytokine expression and NF-κB activation in CSE induced in vitro model
To explore the role of lncRNA GAS5 in vitro, we established a cigarette smoke extract (CSE)-stimulated inflammation model using 16HBE and BEAS-2B cells. RT-qPCR analysis revealed that CSE stimulation led to an increase in the mRNA expression of inflammatory cytokines. Compared with the control group, adenovirus-mediated overexpression of GAS5 (Ad GAS5) decreased the mRNA expression of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α (Fig.  A). ELISA results also indicated that Ad GAS5 reduced the secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α compared to the control group (Fig.  B). Conversely, RT-qPCR and ELISA demonstrated that Ad GAS5 shRNA increased the mRNA expression and secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α (Fig.  C-D). Western blot analysis showed that Ad GAS5 decreased the expression of p-p65 compared to the control group, while Ad GAS5 shRNA increased the expression of p-p65 (Fig.  E). Additionally, we performed immunofluorescence to examine the distribution of p65 in 16HBE cells from different groups. The results showed that CSE promoted the translocation of p65 into the nucleus, and overexpression of lncRNA GAS5 inhibited this translocation (Fig.  F).
 

## GAS5 regulate NF-κB activation by bind with p120
Many studies have shown that LncRNA can form induced silencing complex (RISC) with Ago2 to affect downstream target gene expression [ ]. To investigate the mechanism of lncRNA GAS5 on NF-κB activation, we knockdown Ago2 by shRNA, and found that the Ago2 expression was downregulated by Ago2 shRNA, but knocking down of ago2 has no effect on p-p65 and p65 (Fig. S2A). Suggest there were other mechanisms. Then we performed RNA pulldown and LC-MS/MC use biotinylated GAS5 (Fig. A). The results showed that p120 could bind with GAS5 (Fig. S2B). Furthermore, RIP showed GAS5 could bind with p120 (Fig. B). The expression of p120 was downregulated in COPD mice model and CSE induced inflammation model (Fig. S2CD, Fig. CD). Ad GAS5 increased the expression of p120 and downregulate the expression of Rock1 and p-p65 compared with the control group (Fig. C). Our previous research has shown that p120-mediated NF-κB signaling activation in airway epithelial inflammation is partially RhoA/Rock1 dependent. The opposite result was obtained when lncRNA GAS5 was knocked down by shRNA (Fig. D). In COPD mice model, AAV GAS5 also increased the expression of p120 and downregulate the expression of Rock1 and p-p65 compared with the control group (Fig. E).
 

## GAS5 inhibited the inflammatory response by enhancing p120 stability
It was found that overexpression of GAS5 had no effect on the mRNA level of p120 (Fig.  A). As to whether GAS5 affects the degradation of p120 protein, we applied proteasome inhibitor MG132 and lysosome inhibitor CQ respectively on the basis of knocking down GAS5, and found that MG132 could reduce the inhibitory effect of knocking down GAS5 on p120 protein (Fig.  B). It is suggested that GAS5 may affect the stability of p120 through proteasome pathway. Protein half-life experiments confirmed that knocking down GAS5 could shorten the half-life of p120 protein (Fig.  C). In addition, we found that knocking down GAS5 could increase the ubiquitination level of p120 (Fig.  D), and by mutating different lysine sites of UB, our results indicated that the degradation of p120 was mediated by GAS5 knockdown through K48-linked ubiquitination (Fig.  E).
 

## Knocking down of p120 inhibits the protective effect of GAS5 on inflammation
To investigate the role of p120 on lncRNA GAS5 in vitro, we use 16HBE and BEAS-2B cells to conduct CSE stimulated inflammation model. RT-PCR showed that knocking down of p120 by shRNA increased the mRNA expression of inflammatory cytokines, and reversed the protective effect of GAS5 on the mRNA expression of inflammatory cytokines IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α (Fig.  A). ELISA also showed that the reduced secretion of inflammatory cytokines IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α by Ad GAS5 were also reversed by Ad p120 shRNA (Fig.  B). Furthermore, immunofluorescence results showed that Ad p120 shRNA increased the translocation of p65 into the nucleus, and reversed the downregulation effect of GAS5 (Fig.  C). Western blot showed that Ad p120 shRNA increased the expression of ROCK1 and p-p65, which reversed the downregulation effect of GAS5. Ad p120 downregulated the expression of ROCK1 and p-p65, which eliminated the upregulation effect of Ad GAS5 sh. (Fig.  D).
 

## Discussion
The pathogenesis of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is not fully understood at present, but more and more evidence shows that COPD is a complex and diverse respiratory inflammatory reaction involving multiple cells and molecules, and its pathogenic mechanism is based on the innate and adaptive immune response of inhaling toxic and harmful gases and particles [ ]. Airway epithelium is the first line of defense in contact with the external environment, and is vulnerable to inhalation pathogens and environmental pollutants. Cigarette smoke (CS) is a major risk factor for COPD, which can disrupt airway epithelial barrier function, increase oxidative stress, and promote the activation of pro-inflammatory pathways in the airway epithelium [ ].
At present, the expression and role of lncRNA GAS5 in COPD is not fully understood. In this study, we found that the expression level of lncRNA GAS5 was high in 16HBE, while the expression of lncRNA GAS5 was down-regulated after the cells were treated with CSE, which aroused our interest, and subsequent studies were conducted. In this study, it was found that CSE treated 16HBE cells and BEAS-2B cells could activate NF-κB signaling pathway, and knocking down lncRNA GAS5 further enhanced the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway, mainly manifested by up-regulated expression of phosphorylated NF-κB p65 and increased nuclear incorporation. At the same time, the expressions of downstream inflammatory factors IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α were up-regulated. In contrast, lncRNA GAS5 overexpression can inhibit the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway. Yang et al. showed that GAS5 alleviated the expression of cytokines in periodontal ligament stem cells by inhibiting activation of the TNF-α-mediated NF-κB pathway [ ]. Su et al. showed that LncRNA GAS5 exerts an inhibitory effect on HSC activation by suppressing NF-κB signaling through regulation of the miR-433–3p/TLR10 axis [ ]. Which were consistent with our results.
Recently Mo et al. have indicated that lncRNA GAS5 promotes pyroptosis in COPD by functioning as a ceRNA to regulate the miR‑223‑3p/NLRP3 axis [ ]. Previous studies have also confirmed that miR-223 expression is up-regulated in the LPS-stimulated airway inflammation model and can mediate the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway through targeted regulation of p120, ultimately promoting the occurrence and development of airway inflammation. In this study, we again confirmed that p120 expression was down-regulated after CSE stimulation. However, lncRNA GAS5 overexpression could promote the expression of p120. Our previously study showed that miR-223 could directly target p120 catenin [ ]. We speculate that whether lncRNA GAS5 may regulate the p120 expression by functioning as a ceRNA to regulate the miR‑223‑3p. It was found that after overexpression of GAS5, the mRNA expression of p120 showed an upward trend, but there was no statistical difference. On the other hand, we found that knocking down GAS5 could increase the ubiquitination level of p120 and shorten the half-life of p120 protein. What’s more, by mutating different lysine sites of UB molecules, we found that knocking down GAS5 could promote the ubiquitination modification of p120 K48.
Finally, we also investigated whether the expression level of p120 can affect the regulation of lncRNA GAS5 on NF-κB signaling pathway. To this end, we overexpressed lncRNA GAS5 in 16HBE cells while knocking down p120 expression, and it was found that p120 knockdown could alleviate the inhibition of lncRNA GAS5 overexpression on NF-κB signal.
One limitation of our results is that in vivo AAV transfection of GAS5 was not cell type specific, their cytoprotective effect could be lung tissue independent. Future studies using cell type-specific approaches are needed to clarify the contributions of GAS5 and p120 in different lung cell populations.
In conclusion, our study suggests that lncRNA GAS5 may be a promising therapeutic target for airway inflammation.
