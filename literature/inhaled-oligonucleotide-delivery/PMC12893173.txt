## ABSTRACT

## Background
Sepsis-induced myocardial injury (SIMI) contributes significantly to morbidity and mortality in sepsis, but its molecular mechanisms are not fully understood. Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), an endogenous signaling molecule, regulates inflammation, oxidative stress, and cell death in cardiovascular diseases, with protein sulfhydration as a key mechanism.

## Methods
We used in vitro and in vivo sepsis models we investigated the protective to examine the effects of H₂S donors (GYY4137 and Allicin) on SIMI. We focused on ferroptosis and the HIF1α/BNIP3 axis, and applied transcriptomic, proteomic, and molecular biology approaches.

## Results
Sepsis suppressed the CSE/H₂S pathway, increasing ferroptosis and myocardial injury. Exogenous H₂S attenuated cardiac dysfunction, inflammation, and cell death. Mechanistically, H₂S promoted HSPA8 sulfhydration at Cys574, enhancing HIF1α degradation and inhibiting BNIP3, thereby reducing oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and myocardial damage. Allicin, a natural H₂S donor, induced endogenous H₂S production, restored HSPA8 sulfhydration, and provided cardioprotection without toxicity.

## Conclusion
This study reveals a novel H₂S–HSPA8–HIF1α–BNIP3 axis in regulating ferroptosis and myocardial injury during sepsis. Protein sulfhydration mediates the cardioprotective effects of H₂S, and Allicin emerges as a promising therapeutic agent for septic cardiomyopathy.

KEYWORDS: Sepsis-induced myocardial injury, hydrogen sulfide, protein sulfhydration, HSPA8, HIF1α, BNIP3, ferroptosis, Allicin

## Introduction
Sepsis remains a major clinical challenge worldwide, with a persistently high incidence and mortality rate despite advances in medical care [ , ]. According to recent epidemiological studies, millions of individuals are affected by sepsis each year, and the global mortality rate can reach as high as 25% to 30%, especially among critically ill patients [ ]. Among the various complications of sepsis, sepsis-induced myocardial injury (SIMI) has attracted increasing attention as a significant contributor to adverse outcomes. It is estimated that approximately 40% to 50% of septic patients develop some degree of cardiac dysfunction during the disease, and this myocardial impairment is closely associated with increased morbidity and mortality [ , ]. The presence of myocardial injury in sepsis not only exacerbates hemodynamic instability but also significantly reduces the chances of survival, making it a key determinant of prognosis in septic patients. Despite its clinical importance, the underlying molecular mechanisms of SIMI remain incompletely understood. Therefore, in-depth investigation into the pathogenesis of SIMI is essential for identifying novel therapeutic targets and improving the management and prognosis of patients with sepsis.
Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) is an important endogenous gaseous signaling molecule that plays critical roles in various physiological and pathological processes. In mammals, H₂S is primarily enzymatically generated by cystathionine-γ-lyase (CSE), cystathionine-β-synthase (CBS), and 3-mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase (3-MST). In cardiac tissue, CSE serves as the predominant enzyme responsible for H₂S production, playing a crucial role in maintaining cardiovascular homeostasis [ ]. Increasing evidence has shown that H₂S is involved in the regulation of inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis, thereby exerting protective effects in multiple organ injuries during sepsis [ ]. Importantly, H₂S has also been demonstrated to play a significant role in alleviating SIMI, making it a promising target for the treatment of septic cardiomyopathy. For example, Zhou et al. found that the slow-releasing H₂S donor GYY4137 ameliorates sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy (SIC) primarily by inhibiting the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway and reducing oxidative stress [ ], while Wang et al. showed that sulfide-modified nanoscale zero-valent iron, capable of releasing H₂S, significantly improves cardiac function and survival in septic mice through modulation of AMPK signaling and inflammatory pathways [ ]. Notably, protein sulfhydration represents a critical pathway for the direct action of H₂S on target proteins, and is increasingly recognized for its significance in cardiovascular protection [ , ]. However, despite these findings, the precise molecular mechanisms by which H₂S confers cardioprotection in SIMI remain incompletely elucidated.
Ferroptosis is a newly recognized form of regulated cell death that is mechanistically distinct from apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy. It is characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxides to lethal levels, leading to oxidative damage of cellular membranes and subsequent cell death [ ]. The execution of ferroptosis is tightly regulated by several key molecular pathways. Central to its process is the imbalance between the production and detoxification of lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS). Specifically, the depletion of glutathione (GSH) and the inactivation of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), a critical antioxidant enzyme, result in the unchecked accumulation of lipid peroxides. Iron overload further catalyzes the Fenton reaction, amplifying ROS generation and exacerbating lipid peroxidation [ , ]. Ferroptosis has been increasingly implicated in the pathogenesis of SIMI. Recent studies have demonstrated that targeting ferroptosis can effectively ameliorate cardiac dysfunction in sepsis models. For instance, Lu et al. reported that TaoHe ChengQi decoction protects against sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction by inhibiting ferroptosis via activation of the Nrf2 pathway [ ]. Similarly, Liu et al. showed that melanin nanoparticles alleviate SIMI by suppressing both ferroptosis and inflammation [ ]. Additionally, Jiang et al. found that a ceria nanozyme coordinated with curcumin reduces cardiac injury in sepsis by inhibiting ferroptosis and inflammatory responses [ ]. These findings underscore the pivotal role of ferroptosis in SIMI. Notably, H₂S has been shown to protect against various types of cardiac injury by inhibiting ferroptosis. For example, Zhang et al. demonstrated that H₂S protects cardiomyocytes from doxorubicin-induced ferroptosis through activation of the SLC7A11/GSH/GPx4 pathway mediated by Keap1 S-sulfhydration and Nrf2 activation [ ]. Similarly, Wang et al. reported that exogenous H₂S alleviates diabetic cardiomyopathy by suppressing ferroptosis via the Nrf2/GPx4/GSH pathway [ ]. In addition, Yang et al. found that the H₂S donor AP39 inhibits ferroptosis by suppressing mitochondrial autophagy through the PINK1/parkin pathway, thereby improving myocardial fibrosis after myocardial infarction [ ]. These findings collectively indicate that H₂S-mediated inhibition of ferroptosis plays a crucial role in the protection against multiple forms of cardiac injury. However, whether ferroptosis is involved in the cardioprotective effects of H₂S in SIMI remains largely unreported.
Allicin, a reactive sulfur species (RSS) derived from garlic, possesses diverse biological activities, including notable antibacterial, antifungal, and cardiovascular protective effects [ ]. Its redox activity, primarily mediated through interactions with thiol groups in glutathione and proteins, underlies its pharmacological properties. Recent studies have shown that Allicin can confer protective effects in various disease models, such as acute lung injury, ischemic brain injury, and spinal cord injury [ ]. Importantly, Allicin has been recognized as a potential H₂S donor [ ], suggesting its possible role in modulating redox signaling and cellular defense mechanisms. These properties make Allicin a promising candidate for investigating the therapeutic effects of H₂S in disease contexts.
In summary, this study elucidates a novel protective mechanism by which H₂S alleviates SIMI. We demonstrate that H₂S promotes the sulfhydration of HSPA8, leading to the degradation of HIF1α and subsequent inhibition of BNIP3-mediated mitophagy. This cascade effectively reduces oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and cardiac damage in sepsis models. Importantly, we identify Allicin as a safe and potent inducer of endogenous H₂S, providing a promising therapeutic approach for septic cardiomyopathy.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Animal experiments
Eight-week-old male C57/BL6 mice (22-25 g) were purchased from the laboratory animals of Shanghai Institute of Family Planning (Shanghai, China) and placed in an SPF-class housing of laboratory in a temperature-controlled environment (23-25°C) to eat and drink freely. All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of the School of Life Sciences, Shanghai University (Approval No. YS 2023-055). Conditional cardiac muscle-specific CSE-knockout (CSE-cKO) mice were generated by crossbreeding Myh6-Cre mice with CSE (flox/flox) mice, which were generously provided by the laboratories of Guan You-fei and Ni Xin respectively. Mice were subjected to cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) procedures to induce a SIMI model. The sepsis model was established by anesthetizing mice with sodium pentobarbital at a dose of 30 mg/kg via intraperitoneal injection. Subsequently, a surgical procedure was performed, which included making a 2–3 cm midline abdominal incision below the xiphoid process. The distal segment of the cecum was then ligated, and two punctures were made in the intestinal wall using a 27-gauge needle. To further promote sepsis induction, a small amount of intestinal content was carefully extruded. After these procedures were completed, the cecum was repositioned into the abdominal cavity, and both the peritoneal and skin layers were tightly sutured using 4/0 surgical thread. In the sham-operated control group, the intestines were exposed surgically but not subjected to ligation or puncture [ ]. GYY4137 was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 50 mg/kg 1 h before the CLP procedure. After 24 h, the mice were subjected to echocardiographic assessment, then euthanized using CO2 inhalation, followed by the collection of cardiac tissues and plasma for subsequent experimental analyses.

2.2.

## Echocardiography
The mice were anesthetized via inhalation of 2.5% (v/v) isoflurane. Before echocardiographic assessment, a B-mode image was acquired initially to ensure accurate positioning of the ultrasound transducer. Transthoracic M-mode echocardiography was then carried out using an Esaote MyLab Touch system (Esaote Biomedica, Genova, Italy). Under the parasternal long-axis view, left ventricular diastolic diameter (Dd) and systolic diameter (Ds) were measured. Further parameters assessed included left ventricular end-diastolic and end-systolic internal diameters (LVEDD and LVESD). Fractional shortening (FS) and ejection fraction (EF) were calculated using the Teichholz formula [ , ]. These parameters were calculated as follows: LVEDV (mm) = 7 × (Dd3/(2.4 + Dd); LVESV (mm) = 7×Ds3/(2.4 + Ds; EF (%) = (LVEDV-LVESV) / LVEDV ×100% and FS (%) = (Dd-Ds)/Dd × 100%. All measurements represented the mean values of the signals from three consecutive cardiac cycles.

2.3.

## Cell culture and treatments
AC16 cells (obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences) were cultured in DMEM/F-12 medium (Gibco, Cat#11320033) supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS (Gibco, Cat#10099141C) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% (v/v) CO₂. Cells at passage six were seeded into twelve-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁵ cells/well. On the following day, cells were treated with various reagents as indicated. In preliminary experiments, we found that increasing the concentration of GYY4137 to 0.8 mM resulted in a marked protective effect, while further elevation to 1 mM did not provide additional benefit. Considering the potential toxicity risks associated with higher concentrations of H₂S donors, 0.8 mM was selected as the optimal dose for subsequent experiments. After treatment, both cells and cell culture supernatants were collected for further assays.

2.4.

## Western blot analysis
Mouse heart tissues and AC16 cells were lysed using a cold RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B) for protein extraction. The resulting lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4°C. The supernatants were carefully transferred to pre-chilled tubes, and the total protein concentration was determined using a BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime, P0012S). Approximately 15 μg of protein was separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto 0.45-μm PVDF membranes (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5% (w/v) skim milk in TBST for 2 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies against GPX4 (1:1000, Proteintech, 67763-1-Ig), CSE (1:1000, Santa Cruz, sc-374249), HIF1α (1:1000, Proteintech, 20960-1-AP), BNIP3 (1:1000, Proteintech, 68091-1-Ig), HSPA8 (1:1000, Proteintech, 66074-1-Ig) and β-actin (1:5000, Sigma, A5441), all diluted at a ratio of 1:1000, overnight at 4°C. After washing, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescence substrate (Vazyme, E411-04), and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (version 1.8.0.112).

2.5.

## Quantitative real-time PCR
Total RNA was extracted from heart tissues and cells using the Animal Tissue/Cell Total RNA Extraction Kit (50-100 mg tissue or 1-5 × 10⁶ cells per reaction, Servicebio, Cat#G3013). The isolated RNA was then reverse transcribed into cDNA using HyperScript III RT SuperMix for QPCR with gDNA Remover (Vazyme, Cat#R323-01). Real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) analysis was subsequently performed using the SYBR Green I-based fluorescence detection method on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The primer sets used were as follows: HIF1α (NM_010431):
Forward 5′-ACCTTCATCGGAAACTCCAAAG-3′, Reverse 5′-TTCTGTTTGTTGAAGGGAGCAA-3′; BNIP3 (NM_011793): Forward: 5'-GCTCCAAGTCCGAGAATCAGA-3’ Reverse: 5'-TCAGTTCTCGCTTGGAGCAG-3’; CSE (NM_145953): Forward: 5'-GGAAGGAGTTGGATGTGCTG-3’ Reverse: 5'-CAGCATCTTGGGCTTGAGAT-3’. The PCR cycling conditions consisted of an annealing temperature range of 58–62 °C and a total of 40 amplification cycles. The expression levels of the target mRNAs were normalized to the housekeeping gene β-actin to ensure accurate quantification.

2.6.

## TdT-mediated dUTP-biotin nick end-labeling (TUNEL) assay
Paraffin-embedded heart samples were prepared and evaluated using a TUNEL assay kit (Boster, Wuhan, China, Cat#EK1124) to detect the presence of apoptotic cells. Apoptotic cells were defined as those exhibiting brown or brown-yellow staining. TUNEL-positive signals were examined under an Olympus microscope, and five high-power fields were randomly selected for analysis from each tissue section.

2.7.

## Immunofluorescence (IF) staining
For immunofluorescence staining, 5 μm paraffin sections were rehydrated and microwaved in citric acid buffer to retrieve antigens. After blocking with an immunofluorescence blocking solution (Beyotime, China, P0102) for 30 min, the sections were incubated with primary antibodies against GPX4 (1:1000, Proteintech, 67763-1-Ig), HIF1α (1:1000, Proteintech, 20960-1-AP) and CSE (1:1000, Santa Cruz, sc-374249) at a dilution of 1:400 at 4°C overnight. Subsequently, the sections were washed three times with PBS for 5 min each, and then incubated with an Alexa Fluor-594 conjugated donkey anti-mouse (1:800, Abcam, ab150080), Alexa Fluor-488 (1:800, Abcam, ab150105) conjugated donkey anti-rabbit or Alexa Fluor-488 (1:800, Abcam, ab150073) conjugated donkey anti-rabbit secondary antibody solution in the dark at room temperature for 1 h. After washing with PBS for 3 times, nuclei were counterstained with 4’6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (Sigma-Aldrich, D9542). The fluorescence signals were observed with fluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Japan).

2.8.

## Transmission electron microscopy
Fresh heart tissues were fixed in a solution comprising 2.5% (v/v) glutaraldehyde (Servicebio, G1102) and 0.1 M sodium cacodylate buffer ((Servicebio, G1106; pH 7.4) for a duration of 72 h at 4 °C. Subsequently, the samples underwent post-fixation with osmium tetroxide, dehydration via a graded ethanol series, and embedding in epoxy resin. Ultrathin sections were then cut and counterstained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Morphological analysis was carried out using a Hitachi H7650 transmission electron microscope.

2.9.

## Hematoxylin–eosin (HE) staining
Mouse heart tissues were fixed in 4% (v/v) paraformaldehyde solution, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 5 μm-thick slices. The tissue sections were subsequently stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) kit. Histological alterations were assessed under a light microscope at ×200 magnifications.

2.10.

## ELISA detection
The levels of LDH, cTnl and CK-MB in the serum of septic mice as well as the levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TNFα, LDH, cTnl, CK-MB and MDA in the cell culture supernatant were measured using commercial ELISA kits (TW-reagent, Shanghai, China).

2.11.

## Construction of HIF1α knockdown plasmid and HIF1α overexpression and knockdown C57 mice
The pGPU6/short hairpin RNA (shRNA) construction for HIF1α and negative control (NC) were designed and purchased from Shanghai GenePharma Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). To establish shRNA cell lines, 1 × 105 AC16 cells were transfected with 1 μg pGPU6/shRNA constructs by Lipofectamine® 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) for 24 h and selected in complete medium containing 100 μg/ml neomycin for 14 days. The colonies stably expressing shRNAs were expanded and used for subsequent experiments. HIF-1α was overexpressed or knocked down in neonatal mouse ventricular cardiomyocytes infected with (multiplicity of infection [MOI] = 10). To deliver the gene in vivo, 5 × 1011 adenovirus particles containing HIF-1α or a control vector were injected directly into the anterior wall of the left ventricle with a syringe. After the operation, the mice were fed for a period of time, and the heart samples were collected to detect the expression level of HIF1α gene through Western Blot, qpcr and other experiments to verify the effect of knockdown and overexpression. The efficiency of gene knockdown and overexpression was assessed by qPCR, and the results are shown in Supplementary Figure 1.

2.12.

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
Co-immunoprecipitation assays were performed to investigate the interaction between HIF1α and HSPA8 in AC16 cardiomyocytes. Briefly, AC16 cells were lysed in ice-cold RIPA buffer (Beyotime, P0013B) containing protease inhibitors. The lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4°C, and the supernatants were collected. For each reaction, 500 μg of total protein was incubated overnight at 4°C with 2 μg of anti-HIF1α antibody (1:1000, Proteintech, 20960-1-AP) or anti-HSPA8 antibody (1:1000, Proteintech, 66074-1-Ig), along with 30 μl of Protein A/G agarose beads (Beyotime, P2055). As a negative control, normal IgG was used under the same conditions. The beads were washed five times with cold PBS, and the bound proteins were eluted by boiling in SDS sample buffer for 5 min. The immunoprecipitated samples were subjected to SDS-PAGE and analyzed by Western blotting with antibodies against HIF1α and HSPA8. The interaction was visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (Vazyme, E411-04), and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

2.13.

## Determination of the proteins with S-sulfhydration
The biotin-switch assay was performed to detect the S-sulfhydration of proteins in AC16 cells and mouse heart tissues. Briefly, samples were lysed in ice-cold T-PER lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) containing protease inhibitors. The lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C, and the supernatants were collected. Free thiol groups in the proteins were blocked by incubating the lysates with 100 mM iodoacetamide (IAM) at room temperature for 30 min in the dark. Excess IAM was removed by acetone precipitation. The proteins were then resuspended and incubated with 1 mM biotin-HPDP (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 1 h at room temperature to label the S-sulfhydrated cysteine residues. For specificity control, parallel samples were treated with 10 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) to reduce persulfide bonds prior to biotin labeling. The biotinylated proteins were purified using streptavidin-agarose beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and eluted with SDS sample buffer containing 100 mM DTT. The eluted proteins were subjected to SDS-PAGE and analyzed by Western blotting using HSPA8 antibodie. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

2.14.

## HSPA8 mutation analysis
Cysteine 574 was identified as a sulfhydrylation site in HSPA8 based on our mass spectrometry analysis, which provided direct experimental evidence of post-translational modification at this residue. To further validate the specificity and functional relevance of sulfhydrylation at C574, we selected cysteine 603 as a control site for mutation. C603 is another cysteine residue in HSPA8, chosen randomly without prior evidence of sulfhydrylation, to serve as a comparative control. By comparing the effects of C574S and C603S mutations, we aimed to demonstrate that the regulatory effects observed are specific to the C574 site rather than a general property of cysteine residues in HSPA8. Site-directed mutagenesis was performed to generate mouse Hspa8 cysteine mutants (C574S and C603S) for functional analysis. The coding sequence of mouse Hspa8 was cloned into the pcDNA3.1(+) expression vector. Mutations were introduced using the QuikChange Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit (Agilent Technologies) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, with specific primers designed to substitute cysteine (C) to serine (S) at positions 574 and 603. The mutated plasmids were verified by Sanger sequencing. AC16 cells were transfected with wild-type or mutant mouse Hspa8 plasmids using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After 48 h, cells were harvested for subsequent assays, including biotin-switch assay and Western blotting, to detect the expression and S-sulfhydration levels of Hspa8. The efficiency of mutagenesis and protein expression was confirmed by immunoblotting.

2.15.

## Murine sepsis score
The murine sepsis score (MSS) is a clinical scoring system used to assess the severity of sepsis in mice. The method evaluates seven parameters: spontaneous activity, response to touch, response to auditory stimuli, posture, respiratory rate, respiratory quality, and appearance (piloerection). Each parameter is scored from 0 (normal) to 4 (severely abnormal), and the total score reflects the overall clinical status. Mice are euthanized if the total MSS exceeds 21 or if the score for respiratory rate or quality increases by more than 3 points. The MSS enables reliable and standardized monitoring of disease progression and therapeutic outcomes in experimental sepsis models. The scoring method was performed as described by Shrum et al [ ].

2.16.

## Gene modification in AC16 cells
AC16 human cardiomyocytes were transfected with CSE shRNA, BNIP3 shRNA, HIF1α shRNA, HIF1α overexpression plasmid, or GPX4 siRNA using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by qPCR analysis 48 h after transfection. The efficiency of gene knockdown and overexpression was assessed by qPCR, and the results are shown in Supplementary Figure 1.

2.17.

## BDPY 581/591 C11 staining in cells
AC16 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and subjected to experimental treatments as indicated. After removing the culture medium, cells were washed once with PBS and incubated with 1 ml of freshly prepared BDPY 581/591 C11(S0043M, Beyotime Biotechnology, China) working solution (2 μM in PBS) at 37°C for 10–30 min in the dark. Following incubation, the staining solution was removed, and cells were washed twice with PBS. Finally, cells were covered with 2 ml PBS and immediately observed under a fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan). For quantification, the ratio of red (reduced form, Ex/Em: 581/591 nm) to green (oxidized form, Ex/Em: 488/510 nm) fluorescence was analyzed to assess lipid peroxidation levels.

2.18.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 20 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Data are presented as mean ± SEM for approximately normally distributed data, or as median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test for each dataset. Homogeneity of variances was evaluated using Levene’s test. For comparisons between two independent groups, an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used when data met assumptions of normality and homoscedasticity; otherwise, the Mann–Whitney U test was applied. For comparisons among more than two independent groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test was used when assumptions were met; otherwise, the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn’s multiple-comparisons test was applied. For experiments involving two factors, two-way ANOVA followed by Sidak’s multiple-comparisons test was used when assumptions were met; if normality assumptions were violated, data were analyzed using a suitable non-parametric alternative / transformation approach, specify. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Normality testing outcomes and the corresponding statistical tests used for each dataset are reported in the Results.

## Result
3.1.

## LPS impairs the CSE/H₂S pathway and exogenous H₂S donor GYY4137 mitigates inflammatory and myocardial injury in cardiomyocytes
To investigate the effects of LPS on the CSE/H₂S pathway in cardiomyocytes, we first evaluated CSE expression and endogenous H₂S production. As shown in (A and C), both CSE protein and mRNA levels were significantly decreased following LPS treatment compared to controls. Immunofluorescence staining further confirmed a substantial reduction in CSE expression in the LPS group ( B). Consistently, endogenous H₂S levels were markedly reduced upon LPS exposure ( D), indicating that LPS suppresses the CSE/H₂S pathway in cardiomyocytes. Next, we evaluated the protective effects of the exogenous H₂S donor GYY4137 against LPS-induced cellular injury. Cell viability assays demonstrated that LPS treatment led to a pronounced decline in cardiomyocyte viability, while administration of GYY4137 restored cell viability in a dose-dependent manner ( E), suggesting that exogenous H₂S supplementation mitigates LPS-induced cytotoxicity. As shown in E, both 0.8 and 1 mM GYY4137 exhibited similar protective effects on cell viability. Given that higher concentrations may be associated with an increased risk of cytotoxicity, we chose 0.8 mM as the working concentration of subsequent experiments to balance efficacy and safety. To further clarify the role of the CSE/H₂S pathway in the inflammatory response, CSE expression was silenced using shRNA, which resulted in a significant reduction in CSE mRNA levels ( F). LPS stimulation caused a marked increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β ( G), IL-6 ( H), IL-8 ( I), and TNF-α ( J). Notably, CSE knockdown further aggravated the LPS-induced elevation of these cytokines, whereas GYY4137 supplementation effectively suppressed their expression. Furthermore, markers of myocardial injury were evaluated, including cell viability ( K), LDH ( L), CK-MB ( M), and cTn-l release ( N). LPS exposure led to a significant reduction in cell viability and an increase in LDH, CK-MB, and cTn-l levels. CSE knockdown further exacerbated these changes, while GYY4137 treatment effectively reversed the LPS-induced myocardial injury, as evidenced by improved cell viability and decreased LDH, CK-MB, and cTn-l levels. Collectively, these results demonstrate that LPS impairs the CSE/H₂S pathway, leading to enhanced inflammatory responses and myocardial injury in cardiomyocytes. Supplementation with the H₂S donor GYY4137 provides significant protection against LPS-induced inflammatory damage and cardiac injury, highlighting the therapeutic potential of targeting the CSE/H₂S axis.

 
3.2.

## CSE/H₂S pathway deficiency exacerbates SIMI in Vivo and GYY4137 provides cardioprotection
Building on our previous in vitro findings, we next investigated the role of the CSE/H₂S pathway in SIMI using a murine CLP model. Immunohistochemical staining and quantitative analysis revealed that CSE protein expression in cardiac tissue was significantly decreased in the CLP group compared to controls ( A), indicating that sepsis markedly suppresses cardiac CSE expression in vivo. Consistently, serum H₂S levels were significantly reduced in CLP mice ( B), confirming impairment of endogenous H₂S production during sepsis. To assess myocardial cell death, TUNEL staining was performed ( C). The number of TUNEL-positive cells was markedly increased in the CLP group and further aggravated in the CLP + CSE-cKO group, indicating exacerbated cardiomyocyte death following CSE knockout. Notably, administration of the H₂S donor GYY4137 significantly reduced TUNEL-positive cell counts, suggesting a protective effect against sepsis-induced cardiomyocyte death. Sepsis severity, as indicated by septic scores, was significantly elevated in the CLP group compared to CSEf/f controls, and was further aggravated in the CLP + CSE-cKO group, demonstrating that CSE knockout exacerbates sepsis severity. Importantly, GYY4137 treatment markedly reduced septic scores in these mice ( D). Markers of myocardial injury, including LDH ( E), CK-MB ( F), and cTn-l ( G), were all significantly elevated in CLP mice, and these increases were further potentiated by CSE knockout. Importantly, GYY4137 administration markedly reduced levels of these cardiac injury markers. Hematoxylin and eosin staining ( H) revealed that white arrows indicated the compactness of cardiomyocyte arrangement in different groups. Cardiomyocytes in the CSEf/f group were tightly and regularly organized, while those in the CLP + CSEf/f group were loosely and disorderly arranged with markedly widened intercellular spaces. In contrast, the compactness of cardiomyocyte arrangement was partially improved in the CLP + CSE-cKO group and the CLP + CSEf/f + GYY4137 group relative to the CLP + CSEf/f group, although it did not fully return to the normal level. FigureEchocardiographic assessment ( I) revealed that CLP-induced sepsis led to significant impairment of cardiac function, as evidenced by reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (EF, J) and fractional shortening (FS, K), along with increased LVESD ( L) and LVEDD ( M). These functional deficits were most severe in the CLP + CSE-cKO group, while GYY4137 administration partially restored cardiac contractility and chamber dimensions. Transmission electron microscopy ( N) showed substantial mitochondrial damage in the CLP group, characterized by swelling and cristae disruption, which was further aggravated by CSE knockout. Treatment with GYY4137 ameliorated these ultrastructural abnormalities. Collectively, these in vivo results demonstrate that sepsis suppresses the CSE/H₂S pathway, leading to aggravated myocardial injury, increased cardiomyocyte death, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired cardiac function. CSE deficiency further exacerbates these pathological changes, while exogenous H₂S supplementation via GYY4137 confers significant cardioprotection.

 
3.3.

## Ferroptosis is a key contributor to septic cardiomyopathy and is attenuated by the CSE/H₂S pathway
To further elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the protective effects of the CSE/H₂S pathway in septic cardiomyopathy, we investigated the involvement of various forms of programed cell death, with a particular focus on ferroptosis. In vitro, cardiomyocytes were treated with LPS and various cell death inhibitors, including ferroptosis inhibitor (Fer-1), necroptosis inhibitor (Nec-1), apoptosis inhibitor (z-VAD-fmk), and pyroptosis inhibitor (Disulfiram) ( A). Cell viability assays showed that all four inhibitors significantly improved cell survival compared to LPS alone, indicating that multiple cell death pathways contribute to LPS-induced cardiomyocyte injury. Given that Fer-1 exhibited the most pronounced protective effect, we subsequently focused our investigation on the role of ferroptosis in septic cardiomyopathy. Western blot analysis revealed that LPS stimulation markedly decreased GPX4 protein levels, which was further exacerbated by CSE knockdown. Treatment with the H₂S donor GYY4137 significantly restored GPX4 expression ( B). Consistently, Fe²+ content ( C), MDA levels ( D) and Lipid ROS levels ( E), both hallmarks of ferroptosis, were increased upon LPS exposure and further elevated following CSE knockdown, while GYY4137 administration significantly reduced both indices. In vivo, immunofluorescence staining of heart tissues demonstrated a substantial reduction in GPX4 expression in the CLP group compared to CSEf/f controls, with the lowest levels observed in the CLP + CSE-cKO group. GYY4137 treatment notably restored GPX4 expression ( F). Quantitative analysis of GPX4 fluorescence intensity ( G) and Western blot results ( H) confirmed these findings, showing that CSE deficiency aggravated the CLP-induced decrease in GPX4, while exogenous H₂S supplementation reversed this effect. Furthermore, Fe²+ concentration ( I) and MDA levels ( J) were significantly elevated in the CLP group and further increased in the CLP + CSE-cKO group, indicating aggravated ferroptosis in the absence of CSE. GYY4137 administration effectively reduced both Fe²+ and MDA levels. Collectively, these data demonstrate that sepsis induced by CLP promotes myocardial ferroptosis, which is exacerbated by CSE deficiency. Exogenous H₂S supplementation with GYY4137 alleviates these pathological changes by inhibiting ferroptosis, underscoring the critical protective role of the CSE/H₂S pathway in septic cardiomyopathy.

 
3.4.

## H₂S ameliorates LPS-induced myocardial injury and inflammation primarily by inhibiting ferroptosis
To further elucidate whether the protective effect of H₂S against septic cardiomyopathy is mediated by the inhibition of ferroptosis, we performed in vitro experiments using both a ferroptosis inhibitor (Fer-1) and a ferroptosis inducer (Erastin). As shown in A–D, LPS stimulation significantly decreased cell viability ( A) and increased the release of myocardial injury markers, including LDH ( B), CK-MB ( C), and cTn-l ( D), indicating pronounced cardiomyocyte damage. Treatment with either the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 or the H₂S donor GYY4137 markedly improved cell viability and reduced the levels of these injury markers compared to the LPS group. Notably, administration of the ferroptosis inducer Erastin abolished the protective effects of GYY4137, resulting in decreased cell viability and elevated LDH, CK-MB, and cTn-l levels, further aggravating myocardial injury. In addition, LPS stimulation led to a significant upregulation of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β ( E), IL-6 ( F), IL-8 ( G), and TNFα ( H). Both Fer-1 and GYY4137 treatments effectively suppressed the secretion of these cytokines, indicating attenuation of the inflammatory response. However, Erastin reversed the anti-inflammatory effects of GYY4137, resulting in increased cytokine levels. Meanwhile, we also used GPX4 siRNA to treat cells. As shown in I–P, knockdown of GPX4 abolished the protective effects of the H₂S donor GYY4137 against LPS-induced injury. Specifically, in the presence of GPX4 siRNA, GYY4137 no longer improved cell viability ( I), nor did it reduce the release of myocardial injury markers, including LDH ( J), CK-MB ( K), and cTn-I ( L), compared to the LPS group. Furthermore, GPX4 knockdown reversed the anti-inflammatory effects of GYY4137, as evidenced by increased levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β ( M), IL-6 ( N), IL-8 ( O), and TNFα ( P). These results demonstrate that the protective effect of H₂S against LPS-induced myocardial injury and inflammation relies on the inhibition of ferroptosis. Both pharmacological modulation and genetic knockdown of GPX4 confirm that suppression of ferroptosis is essential for the cardioprotective actions of H₂S.

## Inhibition of ferroptosis by Fer-1 alleviates myocardial injury and improves cardiac function in septic mice
To further explore the role of ferroptosis in septic cardiomyopathy in vivo, we utilized a CLP-induced sepsis mouse model and assessed the effects of the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer-1 ( ). As shown in A, the septic score was significantly elevated in the CLP group compared to the control group, indicating severe systemic illness. Treatment with Fer-1 markedly reduced the septic score, suggesting that inhibition of ferroptosis alleviates the severity of sepsis. Myocardial injury markers, including LDH ( B), cTn-l ( C), and CK-MB ( D), were all significantly increased in the CLP group, reflecting substantial cardiac damage. Administration of Fer-1 significantly decreased the levels of these markers, indicating effective protection against septic myocardial injury. H&E staining ( E) demonstrated cardiomyocyte morphological differences across groups, with white arrows highlighting variations in cellular compactness. In the Control group, cardiomyocytes exhibited a highly ordered, tightly packed arrangement. In stark contrast, the CLP group displayed disorganized, loosely aligned cardiomyocytes with significantly expanded intercellular gaps and evident inflammatory cell infiltration. Treatment with Fer-1 in the CLP + Fer-1 group partially restored the compact and regular arrangement of cardiomyocytes, accompanied by diminished inflammatory cell infiltration. Echocardiographic assessment ( F) demonstrated impaired cardiac function in CLP mice, as evidenced by abnormal ventricular wall motion. Fer-1 treatment partially restored cardiac contractility. Quantitative analysis ( G–J) showed that CLP-induced sepsis led to a significant reduction in ejection fraction (EF) and fractional shortening (FS), as well as increased left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) and end-systolic diameter (LVESD). Fer-1 administration significantly improved these cardiac function parameters. Transmission electron microscopy ( K) further revealed severe mitochondrial damage and myofibrillar disarray in the CLP group. In contrast, Fer-1 treatment preserved mitochondrial structure and maintained myofibril integrity. Collectively, these results demonstrate that inhibition of ferroptosis by Fer-1 significantly reduces myocardial injury, improves cardiac function, and mitigates ultrastructural damage in septic mice. These findings highlight the critical role of ferroptosis in septic cardiomyopathy and suggest that targeting ferroptosis may represent a promising therapeutic strategy.

 
3.6.

## H₂S alleviates sepsis-induced myocardial ferroptosis via inhibition of HIF1α signaling
To further elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which H₂S alleviates sepsis-induced myocardial ferroptosis. Previous studies have reported that HIF1α plays a critical role in septic cardiac injury [ , ]. Therefore, we hypothesize that HIF1α may be a key molecular target through which H2S exerts its protective effects against septic myocardial injury. Immunofluorescence staining and Western blot revealed that HIF1α expression in myocardial tissue was significantly upregulated in the CLP group compared to the sham group, while treatment with the H₂S donor GYY4137 markedly reduced HIF1α expression ( A, B). To further clarify the pivotal role of HIF1α in H₂S-mediated inhibition of ferroptosis and improvement of septic myocardial injury, we performed loss- and gain-of-function studies by knocking down HIF1α with shRNA and overexpressing HIF1α. Meanwhile, GPX4 protein expression was significantly decreased in the myocardium after CLP but was restored by either GYY4137 treatment or HIF1α knockdown. Notably, the protective effect of GYY4137 on GPX4 expression was abolished when HIF1α was overexpressed ( C). Similarly, both Fe²+ and MDA levels were significantly increased in the CLP group, and were reduced by GYY4137 or HIF1α knockdown, whereas HIF1α overexpression eliminated the inhibitory effects of GYY4137 on Fe²+ and MDA levels ( D, E). In addition, HIF1α knockdown reversed the reduction of GPX4 and the elevation of Fe²+ and MDA levels observed after CLP in both wild-type and CSE-cKO mice ( F–I). Collectively, these results demonstrate that both exogenous and endogenous H₂S alleviate sepsis-induced myocardial ferroptosis primarily through inhibition of the HIF1α signaling pathway. Furthermore, knockdown and overexpression studies confirm that HIF1α is central to the regulation of ferroptosis and that the protective effect of H₂S against myocardial injury during sepsis is dependent on HIF1α inhibition.

## HIF1α is a critical mediator of H₂S-induced protection against ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes
Building on the in vivo findings presented in , we next investigated these mechanisms at the cellular level. As shown in , LPS treatment markedly increased HIF1α protein expression in cardiomyocytes, while administration of the H₂S donor GYY4137 significantly inhibited this upregulation. Knockdown of HIF1α significantly restored GPX4 protein expression and reduced Fe²+ and MDA levels following LPS stimulation, indicating an alleviation of ferroptosis and lipid peroxidation. Importantly, overexpression of HIF1α abolished the inhibitory effect of GYY4137 on ferroptosis, resulting in decreased GPX4 levels and increased Fe²+ and MDA accumulation even in the presence of GYY4137. Additionally, in the CSE knockdown model, endogenous H₂S deficiency further aggravated LPS-induced ferroptosis, as evidenced by lower GPX4 expression and higher Fe²+ and MDA levels. HIF1α knockdown mitigated these detrimental changes, further supporting that HIF1α is a critical mediator in regulating ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes under septic conditions.

Both HIF1α knockdown and administration of the H₂S donor GYY4137 markedly reduced cell death, whereas HIF1α overexpression abolished the protective effect of GYY4137. ## Cellular evidence confirms H₂S inhibits HIF1α to prevent LPS-induced myocardial injury and inflammation
Consistent with the in vivo findings ( ), the cellular results shown in further confirm the protective effects of H₂S against LPS-induced myocardial injury. LPS significantly decreased cell viability ( A) and increased the release of LDH ( B), CK-MB ( C), and cTn-l ( D), indicating pronounced cardiomyocyte injury. Moreover, LPS markedly elevated the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β ( E), IL-6 ( F), IL-8 ( G), and TNF-α ( H). ## BNIP3 acts downstream of HIF1α to mediate ferroptosis and myocardial injury in septic cardiomyocytes
Recent studies have demonstrated that BNIP3 is a direct downstream target of HIF1α, and that HIF1α can regulate ferroptosis in septic cardiomyocytes through BNIP3-mediated pathways [ ]. To further elucidate this mechanism, we systematically investigated the role of BNIP3 in mediating HIF1α-induced ferroptosis in septic cardiomyocytes. As shown in A, both BNIP3 mRNA and protein levels were significantly elevated in the CLP group compared to controls, indicating robust activation of BNIP3 during sepsis. Treatment with the H₂S donor GYY4137 markedly suppressed BNIP3 expression at both the protein and mRNA levels. Similar findings were observed in vitro ( B), where LPS stimulation led to a pronounced increase in BNIP3 expression, which was effectively attenuated by GYY4137. To clarify the regulatory relationship between HIF1α and BNIP3, we analyzed BNIP3 expression in cardiomyocytes with HIF1α knockdown or overexpression. In both CLP-induced ( C) and LPS-induced ( D) models, BNIP3 mRNA levels were substantially increased following injury, but were significantly decreased upon HIF1α knockdown or GYY4137 treatment. In contrast, HIF1α overexpression reversed the inhibitory effect of GYY4137 and induced a marked upregulation of BNIP3, confirming that BNIP3 is a critical downstream effector of HIF1α in this context. To further investigate the effect of HIF1α activation, we employed FZ, a pharmacological agonist of HIF1α, in subsequent experiments. Cell viability was markedly reduced after LPS stimulation and further decreased upon HIF1α activation by FZ. Knockdown of BNIP3 significantly improved cell viability, suggesting a protective effect against ferroptosis ( E). Consistently, Fe²+ ( F) and MDA ( G) levels were elevated after LPS and HIF1α activation, while BNIP3 knockdown reversed these increases. Moreover, LDH ( H), CK-MB ( I), and cTn-l ( J), indicators of myocardial injury, were all significantly increased in the LPS group and further elevated with HIF1α activation by FZ, whereas BNIP3 knockdown mitigated these elevations. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that HIF1α promotes ferroptosis and myocardial injury in septic cardiomyocytes primarily through upregulation of BNIP3. Inhibition of BNIP3 effectively attenuates HIF1α-induced ferroptotic cell death and myocardial injury, underscoring BNIP3 as a critical downstream effector and a potential therapeutic target in septic cardiomyopathy.

## HSPA8 sulfhydration at Cys574 is essential for H₂S-mediated inhibition of HIF1α and ferroptosis
As shown in A, the mRNA level of HIF1α was significantly increased in the CLP group compared to control, but was not affected by GYY4137 treatment, indicating that GYY4137 does not regulate HIF1α at the transcriptional level. Therefore, we further analyzed the intersection between sulfhydrated proteins and HIF1α-interacting proteins in cardiomyocytes to identify potential targets through which H₂S may directly regulate HIF1α protein levels via sulfhydration modification. B presents a Venn diagram illustrating the strategy for identifying sulfhydrated proteins in cardiomyocytes. By intersecting the set of proteins interacting with HIF1α and the set of sulfhydrated proteins, HSPA8 was identified as a candidate target. Mass spectrometry analysis ( C) further confirmed the sulfhydration of HSPA8 and suggested that Cys574 is the potential site for this modification. To validate the specificity of HSPA8 sulfhydration, biotin-switch assays were performed. As shown in D, DTT treatment abolished the sulfhydration signal of HSPA8, confirming the specificity of this modification. In vivo, HSPA8 sulfhydration was significantly decreased in the CLP group compared to control but was restored by GYY4137 administration ( E). Similar results were observed in vitro, where LPS stimulation reduced HSPA8 sulfhydration, and GYY4137 treatment rescued this effect ( F). Mutation analysis showed that the C574S mutant of HSPA8 abolished the sulfhydration signal, whereas wild-type HSPA8 and the C603S mutant retained sulfhydration, indicating that Cys574 is the critical sulfhydration site, consistent with the mass spectrometry results. (Note: The original figure had C574S and C603S labels reversed.) Co-immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrated that HIF1α interacts with HSPA8 in cardiomyocytes ( G–H). This interaction was diminished following LPS stimulation but enhanced by GYY4137 treatment. Importantly, the interaction was significantly reduced in cells expressing the HSPA8 C574S mutant, suggesting that sulfhydration at Cys574 is required for optimal HIF1α-HSPA8 binding. Western blot analysis showed that HIF1α protein levels were elevated after LPS stimulation and reduced by GYY4137 treatment in cells expressing wild-type HSPA8, but not in those expressing HSPA8 C574S ( I). Similarly, the mRNA level of BNIP3, a downstream effector of HIF1α, was decreased by GYY4137 in wild-type HSPA8-expressing cells, but not in HSPA8 C574S-expressing cells ( J). GPX4, a key regulator of ferroptosis, was downregulated after LPS stimulation, and its expression was rescued by GYY4137 in wild-type HSPA8 cells, but not in HSPA8 C574S mutants ( K). Functionally, cell viability was significantly improved by GYY4137 in wild-type HSPA8 cells, but this protective effect was abolished in HSPA8 C574S mutants ( L). Markers of myocardial injury, including LDH ( M), CK-MB ( N), and cTn-l ( O), were elevated after LPS stimulation, and reduced by GYY4137 in wild-type HSPA8 cells, but not in C574S mutants. Similarly, levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β ( P), IL-6 ( Q), IL-8 ( R), and TNF-α ( S) were decreased by GYY4137 in wild-type HSPA8 cells, but not in cells expressing the C574S mutant. Collectively, these results demonstrate that HSPA8 is a key sulfhydration target of H₂S in cardiomyocytes. Sulfhydration of HSPA8 at Cys574 is essential for its interaction with HIF1α, and for the inhibitory effects of H₂S on HIF1α signaling, ferroptosis, and inflammatory injury during sepsis-induced myocardial damage.

## Allicin restores H₂S levels and alleviates LPS-induced myocardial injury by suppressing ferroptosis
Allicin, a compound recognized as an endogenous donor of H₂S, has been reported to exert protective effects in various forms of cardiac injury. However, its role in SIMI has not yet been documented. In this study, the chemical structure of Allicin is presented ( A). LPS stimulation significantly decreased H₂S content in cardiomyocytes, whereas Allicin treatment restored H₂S levels ( B). Western blot analysis and quantification revealed that LPS markedly reduced the expression of the ferroptosis-related protein GPX4, and this reduction was effectively reversed by higher concentrations of Allicin ( C). Consistent with these findings, immunofluorescence staining demonstrated an elevation of GPX4 expression following Allicin administration in LPS-treated cells ( D). Intracellular Fe²+ levels were increased after LPS exposure but decreased upon Allicin treatment, as depicted in E. Moreover, LPS induced higher levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), a marker of lipid peroxidation, which were attenuated by Allicin ( F). Cell viability was suppressed by LPS but restored after Allicin administration, as demonstrated in G. Myocardial injury markers, including LDH ( H), CK-MB ( I), and cTn-l ( J), were significantly elevated in the LPS group and reduced following Allicin treatment. Collectively, these results indicate that Allicin alleviates LPS-induced myocardial injury in cardiomyocytes by restoring H₂S levels, suppressing ferroptosis, and reducing cellular and tissue damage.

 

4.

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrate that H₂S protects against septic cardiomyopathy primarily through the sulfhydration of HSPA8 at Cys574, which promotes the degradation of HIF1α. This process leads to a reduction in BNIP3-mediated ferroptosis and myocardial injury during sepsis. Furthermore, we identify Allicin as a safe and effective endogenous H₂S donor that can robustly induce H₂S production without toxicity. Allicin administration restores H₂S levels, facilitates HSPA8 sulfhydration, and suppresses the pathological cascade involving HIF1α and BNIP3, resulting in improved cardiac outcomes in models of sepsis. Collectively, these findings elucidate a novel protective mechanism of H₂S in septic cardiomyopathy and highlight Allicin as a promising therapeutic agent for the induction of endogenous H₂S and the prevention of sepsis-related cardiac injury.
Recent studies have increasingly highlighted the therapeutic potential of H₂S in ameliorating SIMI [ , ]. Multiple investigations have demonstrated that exogenous H₂S supplementation can attenuate cardiac dysfunction, reduce inflammation, and inhibit various forms of programed cell death in both animal and cellular models of sepsis. For example, Li et al. reported that H₂S administration significantly improved cardiac function in infant rats with sepsis by inhibiting the expression of cold-inducible RNA-binding protein (CIRP) [ ], while Wang et al. introduced sulfide-modified nanoscale zero-valent iron as a novel H₂S-releasing material that effectively mitigated septic myocardial injury [ ]. Other studies have shown that H₂S can modulate macrophage phenotype via mitochondrial pathways [ ], further supporting its multifaceted protective role in sepsis-related organ dysfunction. Notably, these findings are consistent with our results, which demonstrate that both endogenous and exogenous H₂S confer robust cardioprotection in sepsis by suppressing inflammation and ferroptosis.
Among the various H₂S donors, GYY4137 is widely used due to its slow and sustained release profile, which more closely mimics physiological H₂S generation and avoids the cytotoxicity associated with rapid-releasing donors like NaHS [ ]. Our study and others have confirmed that GYY4137 administration effectively restores H₂S levels, reduces inflammatory cytokine production, and preserves cardiac function in septic models [ , ]. However, despite these advantages, GYY4137 still faces challenges such as limited tissue specificity, suboptimal bioavailability, and the potential for off-target effects. Recent advances, such as the development of nano-based H₂S donors [ ], offer promising strategies to overcome these limitations by enabling targeted and controlled H₂S delivery to the myocardium. In our study, we demonstrated that Allicin, a natural extract derived from garlic, significantly ameliorates SIMI by inducing endogenous H₂S production and subsequently reducing oxidative stress, inflammation, and ferroptosis in the heart. Unlike traditional exogenous H₂S donors such as GYY4137 and NaHS, which directly supply H₂S to the system, Allicin stimulates the body's own H₂S-generating pathways, offering a more physiological and regulated approach to H₂S supplementation. Additionally, Allicin exhibits excellent safety, low toxicity, and high biocompatibility [ ], making it highly suitable for clinical translation. Its ability to induce sustained endogenous H₂S production, together with a favorable pharmacokinetic profile and minimal off-target effects, positions Allicin as a promising therapeutic agent for SIMI. These advantages highlight the potential of Allicin to overcome the limitations of traditional H₂S donors and accelerate the clinical application of H₂S-based therapies.
HIF1α is increasingly recognized as a central regulator in the pathogenesis of sepsis-related organ dysfunction [ , ]. Acting as a master transcription factor in response to hypoxia and inflammation, HIF1α orchestrates a wide range of cellular processes, including metabolic reprograming, inflammatory gene expression, and cell fate decisions. In the context of sepsis, recent studies have demonstrated that excessive or sustained activation of HIF1α can drive organ injury through multiple mechanisms. For example, HIF1α aggravates acute kidney injury by promoting ferroptosis and disturbing redox homeostasis [ ], while in the liver and lungs, HIF1α activation in macrophages amplifies pro-inflammatory responses and tissue damage [ ]. Furthermore, the integration of HIF1α with metabolic and inflammatory pathways in immune cells has been shown to exacerbate systemic inflammation and organ dysfunction during sepsis [ ]. Collectively, these findings highlight the dual role of HIF1α in both adaptive and maladaptive responses during sepsis, making it a critical node in the regulation of organ injury.
However, research specifically addressing the role of HIF1α in SIMI remains very limited. To date, only two studies have explored this area in depth. One study by Li et al. used metabolomics and network pharmacology to identify HIF1α as a potential therapeutic target of metformin in septic myocardial injury, revealing that metformin modulates HIF1α expression and related signaling pathways to improve cardiac outcomes [ ]. Another study by Yu et al. demonstrated that the caloric restriction mimetic phlorizin protects against sepsis-induced myocardial dysfunction by activating autophagy through the HIF1α/Bnip3 axis [ ]. Both studies suggest that HIF1α is involved in the pathogenesis of septic cardiomyopathy, but the precise mechanisms, especially in relation to cell death pathways such as ferroptosis, remain unclear. Our study addresses a critical knowledge gap by elucidating a novel H₂S–HIF1α–ferroptosis axis in septic cardiomyopathy. We demonstrate that exogenous H₂S administration significantly alleviates myocardial ferroptosis and improves cardiac function in sepsis models, predominantly through suppression of HIF1α. Mechanistically, we show that HIF1α knockdown mimics, while HIF1α overexpression abolishes, the protective effects of H₂S on ferroptosis and redox homeostasis in cardiomyocytes, confirming HIF1α as a decisive molecular switch in this process. These findings establish a direct mechanistic link between gasotransmitter signaling and regulated cell death and provide a new conceptual framework for targeting the H₂S–HIF1α–ferroptosis pathway in the treatment of sepsis-induced cardiac injury.
Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that H₂S can modulate HIF1α expression and activity across various pathological settings. For instance, previous studies have reported that H₂S inhibits hypoxia-induced HIF1α activation in a von Hippel-Lindau- and mitochondria-dependent manner [ ], attenuates cigarette smoke-induced HIF1α upregulation in alveolar epithelial cells [ ], and regulates HIF1α-mediated angiogenesis and tumor progression in multiple disease models [ , ]. Despite these observations, the precise molecular mechanisms by which H₂S modulates HIF1α protein stability have remained largely undefined, with most studies focusing on descriptive or correlative findings rather than direct mechanistic insights. In this context, our study makes a significant conceptual advance by uncovering the critical role of HSPA8 sulfhydration (HSPA8-SSH) in mediating H₂S-induced HIF1α degradation. We demonstrate that H₂S promotes the sulfhydration of HSPA8, a key molecular chaperone, thereby enhancing the degradation of HIF1α in septic cardiomyocytes. Disruption of HSPA8 sulfhydration abrogates this effect, resulting in impaired HIF1α turnover and increased susceptibility to ferroptosis. This mechanistic discovery not only clarifies the direct molecular link between H₂S signaling and HIF1α protein homeostasis but also establishes HSPA8-SSH as a pivotal regulatory node connecting gasotransmitter biology to the control of regulated cell death. Thus, our work fills a critical gap left by previous studies, providing the first definitive evidence for the post-translational modification pathway through which H₂S orchestrates HIF1α degradation and confers myocardial protection in sepsis.

5.

## Limitation
Despite the comprehensive investigation of the CSE/H₂S pathway and its role in septic cardiomyopathy, several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. First, while GYY4137 and Allicin were used as exogenous H₂S donors, their pharmacokinetics, optimal dosing, and long-term safety profiles in vivo remain to be thoroughly evaluated before clinical application. Second, the study primarily focused on the HIF1α/BNIP3/ferroptosis axis; however, other signaling pathways and cell types involved in sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction were not extensively explored. Third, although the role of HSPA8 sulfhydration was demonstrated, the downstream molecular events and potential off-target effects of H₂S modification require further clarification. Fourth, although our study demonstrates that HSPA8-Cys574 sulfhydration is associated with decreased HIF-1α protein levels, the precise molecular mechanism underlying this regulation remains to be elucidated. One plausible hypothesis is that sulfhydration of HSPA8 may facilitate the ubiquitination and proteasome-dependent degradation of HIF-1α, possibly by enhancing the interaction between HIF-1α and ubiquitin ligases or by modulating chaperone-mediated substrate recognition and transport. Future studies employing immunoprecipitation, ubiquitination assays, and functional analyses of HSPA8 mutants will be necessary to clarify these mechanistic details. Such investigations will not only deepen our understanding of HSPA8-mediated post-translational regulation of HIF-1α but also provide new insights into the broader role of protein sulfhydration in cellular stress responses. Fifth, this study was designed as a parallel validation to our previously published work on the kidney [ ], in which we utilized a similar experimental framework to investigate the organ-specific effects and molecular mechanisms of GYY4137 in sepsis-induced injury. While the core detection indicators and animal model were consistent between the two studies, the present work focuses on the heart, aiming to determine whether the regulatory patterns observed in the kidney are also applicable to cardiac tissue. By examining HSPA8 sulfhydration and related ferroptosis markers in the heart, we sought to extend our findings and evaluate the organ-specific relevance of this pathway in sepsis. Due to experimental limitations, direct cross-organ comparative data were not included; however, our results provide important parallel evidence supporting the broader physiological significance of HSPA8 sulfhydration in sepsis. We acknowledge the similarities in study design and have cited the previous kidney study to ensure scientific transparency. Future work will focus on systematic cross-organ comparisons to further clarify the specificity and generalizability of the mechanisms identified.

6.

## Conclusion
our findings reveal that disruption of the CSE/H₂S pathway plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of SIMI by promoting HIF1α-driven ferroptosis and inflammation. Importantly, we identify Allicin – a natural H₂S donor – as an effective agent that restores endogenous H₂S levels, suppresses ferroptosis, and alleviates myocardial injury in both in vitro and in vivo models of sepsis. Mechanistically, Allicin and exogenous H₂S donors such as GYY4137 exert their protective effects via sulfhydration of HSPA8 at Cys574, thereby facilitating HIF1α degradation and inhibiting downstream BNIP3-mediated ferroptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction. These results not only expand our understanding of the redox regulation underlying septic cardiomyopathy but also highlight the translational potential of targeting the CSE/H₂S–HSPA8–HIF1α axis. Allicin, as a clinically accessible and naturally derived compound, offers a promising therapeutic strategy for the prevention and treatment of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction, warranting further investigation in clinical settings.
