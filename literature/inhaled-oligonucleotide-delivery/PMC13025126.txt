## Abstract
There have been rapid advances in nucleoside-modified messenger RNA–based vaccines, particularly during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, demonstrating a rapid and cost-effective approach for addressing infectious diseases. A major challenge remains in developing a delivery system that protects mRNA from degradation and facilitates its passage through tissue and cellular barriers. Current four-component lipid nanoparticles enable efficient endosomal escape and are a pivotal technology for the delivery of mRNA vaccines. However, challenges such as stability, availability, durability, and adverse effects persist. We have developed a self-assembling one-component ionizable amphiphilic Janus dendrimers (IAJD) delivery system with naturally occurring amino heads, capable of efficiently delivering mRNA to the spleen and lymph nodes. This single-component system simplifies synthesis, reduces development complexity, and enables rapid global distribution of mRNA vaccines during pandemics. As a proof of concept, one-component IAJD97, formulated with mRNA encoding norovirus mRNA capsid protein, demonstrates the potential of IAJDs as efficient delivery platform for mRNA vaccines, advancing their effectiveness and expanding applications to improve public health outcomes.

PEG-free, one-component Janus dendrimers deliver mRNA with high stability and strong immunity, enabling scalable vaccines.

## INTRODUCTION
Since the onset of the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) pandemic in 2019, the development of nucleoside-modified mRNA vaccine has seen remarkable progress, driven by major research institutions and the pharmaceutical industry ( – ). A critical aspect of this advancement has been the efficient delivery of nucleic acids into cells, which is essential for the success of nucleoside-modified mRNA vaccines and therapeutics ( , ). To maximize efficacy and minimize vaccine-associated risks, finding a suitable RNA delivery system is vital.
Now, the most widely used delivery system for mRNA vaccines is the four-component lipid nanoparticle (LNP) ( – ). This system comprises ionizable lipids, cholesterol, phospholipids, and lipid-conjugated polyethylene glycol (PEG) and is typically assembled using microfluidics or T-tube technology ( , ). However, four-component LNPs face major challenges, including low stability, susceptibility to mRNA degradation, and reliance on multiple lipid components including a large proportion of “structural” lipids. These factors limit their global clinical application ( – ).
To address these limitations, we recently introduced a one-component multifunctional sequence-defined ionizable amphiphilic Janus dendrimer (IAJD) ( – ). These IAJDs can coassemble with mRNA into dendrimersome nanoparticles through a simple one-step mixing process, offering a streamlined and effective delivery system for mRNA vaccines and therapeutics ( , , ).
In this study, we evaluated the stability, efficacy, and ability of norovirus capsid viral protein 1 (VP1) mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine formulations to induce immune responses. Our findings demonstrate that this innovative approach simplifies the conventional four-component LNP design while addressing its key weaknesses. Compared with the complex synthesis of four-component LNPs, the production of one-component IAJDs requires fewer and simpler reaction steps ( , ).
This streamlined process introduces modifications that could eliminate PEG-related adverse side effects associated with four-component LNPs ( – ). For example, it removes cholesterol, which was previously needed for specific fluidic and mechanical properties, it eliminates the segregation of neutral ionizable lipids in the LNP core, known to reduce activity, and it discards PEG-conjugated lipids, which were used to enhance in vitro stability ( , , ). As a result, this approach not only is more cost-effective and straightforward but also enhances scalability, making one-component IAJDs better suited to respond to epidemiological crises compared with four-component LNPs.
Beyond ease of assembly, one-component IAJDs exhibit efficient and durable targeted mRNA delivery, reduced toxicity, and increased stability at 4°C. These advantages position IAJDs as a promising platform for a wide range of vaccines and nanotherapeutics. This study highlights a faster, simpler, and more cost-effective method for vaccine development using one-component IAJDs, paving the way for future innovations in the field.

## RESULTS

## IAJD97-mediated Luc mRNA targeted delivery to lymphoid organs
We screened more than 300 IAJDs from our in-house rationally designed libraries and tested their ability to deliver firefly luciferase (Luc) mRNA in vivo ( – ). For these initial screening experiments, we used nucleoside-modified mRNA encoding Luc. Luc mRNA was selected for the screening because of its short half-life of functional protein activity, such that protein activity is a measure of ongoing translation.
Our preliminary data showed that IAJDs, depending on their chemical structure, can selectively deliver Luc mRNA to the lungs, liver, spleen, and lymph nodes (LNs) ( – , ). IAJD97 was selected for further characterization because of several factors: Its chemical structure includes two nitrogen atoms and hydrophilic head domains with a hydroxyethyl group on top, which we believe plays a key role in efficient delivery in vivo. In addition, IAJD97 was easy to synthesize and demonstrated targeting specificity for the spleen and LNs ( – ).
To evaluate splenic delivery, the Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation was administered at a dose of 5 μg of mRNA per mouse. Four hours postinjection, luminescence from the whole body, LNs, and major organs were quantified using an in vivo imaging system (IVIS). Representative images ( ) and quantified luminescence data ( ) demonstrate that luciferin protein expression in the spleen was strong, with several magnitudes higher compared with other organs. Specifically, total protein expression in the spleen was 590-fold greater than in the heart, 18-fold greater than in the lungs, and 9-fold greater than that in the liver (table S2). The Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation was also analyzed for particle diameter (nanometers), polydispersity index (PDI), encapsulation efficiency (%), pKa (where Ka is the acid dissociation constant), and zeta potential. demonstrated key properties for effective delivery, including a uniform particle size with a low PDI, high encapsulation efficiency, and an optimal pKa, confirming the formulation’s stability and efficient mRNA encapsulation. Cryo–transmission electron microscopy (cryo-TEM) images of the Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulations ( ) reveal a multilamellar, onion-like structure, consistent with previously published data ( ).
 

## Luciferin protein expression persists for at least 96 hours following mRNA-IAJD97 intravenous injection
To evaluate the kinetics of protein production over time, mice were intravenously injected with Luc mRNA-IAJD97 at a dose of 5 μg per mouse. Live mice were imaged at various time points (4, 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours) postinjection, and the luciferase protein intensity of whole body, draining LNs, and spleen was quantified using the IVIS system ( ). Whole-body luciferase activity decreased by 24 hours postinjection but remained at 45% of the initial signal. At 48, 72, and 96 hours postinjection, the signal declined more sharply to 4, 1.0, and 0.5%, respectively ( and table S3). During the first 48 hours, the whole-body luciferase signal overlapped with the spleen but diverged over time. In contrast, luciferase kinetics in the LNs more closely mirrored the decay pattern observed in the whole body. Protein expression in the spleen decreased to 0.1% at 72 hours and further declined to 0.05% at 96 hours, respectively. However, the signal in the spleen remained detectable by IVIS 96 hours postinjection.
 

## Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation remains stable at +4°C for at least 26 weeks
The stability of the Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation was investigated over time at +4°C. Luc mRNA was coassembled with IAJD97 and left in acetate buffer (pH 4.81; ) or diluted with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (pH 6.02; ) and then injected into mice at a dose of 10 or 5 μg per mouse, respectively, either 1 hour after formulation preparation (fresh) or after storing the formulations at +4°C for 2, 4, 8, 20, 26, and/or 40 weeks. Whole-body and spleen luciferase intensity for acetate stored nanoparticles remained stable until 26 weeks ( ). After 40 weeks of storage at +4°C, the signal for both the whole body and spleen dropped sharply. However, this observation was based on a single sample (n = 1), and statistical analysis could not be performed. Similarly, for the PBS-stored formulation, luciferase activity remained relatively stable around 108 p/s but began to decline after 20 weeks of storage at +4°C.
 To evaluate RNA integrity after 7 months of storage at +4°C, CT/GII.4 and Luc mRNA were extracted from IAJD97 formulations and analyzed by capillary gel electrophoresis using an Agilent Bioanalyzer. As shown in fig. S2, both mRNAs encapsulated in IAJD97 retained high integrity, as indicated by clear major peaks and DV200 values of 93.2 and 94.7% for CT/GII.4 and Luc mRNA, respectively. The DV200 metric reflects the percentage of RNA fragments greater than 200 nucleotides and is a robust indicator of sample quality, with higher values corresponding to more intact RNA suitable for downstream applications. In contrast, CT/GII.4 mRNA formulated with a conventional four-component LNP (SM102) and stored under identical conditions showed marked degradation, characterized by the absence of a clear peak and a lower DV200 value of 28%, indicating a higher degree of fragmentation and a reduced proportion of usable RNA. These data demonstrate that the one-component IAJD97 formulation effectively preserves RNA integrity, luciferase activity, particle size, and PDI for at least 26 weeks at 4°C (tables S4 and S5 and and fig. S2). This level of stability compares favorably with the tier 2 storage framework proposed by Hare et al. ( ), which allows long-term storage at ≤−15°C with short-term stability of up to 90 days at +2° to 8°C.

## Lack of toxicity with Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation delivery
To assess the potential toxicity of one-component IAJD97, mice were intravenously injected with 10 and 30 μg per mouse of Luc mRNA-IAJD97. Histological sections of organs were evaluated 24 hours postinjection. No sign of inflammation or pathological structural alterations were observed in the heart, lungs, liver, spleen, or kidneys at any of the tested doses when compared with naïve mice ( ). However, in the spleen at the high dose of 30 μg, mild to moderate, multifocal periarteriolar lymphoid sheath lymphocyte depletion with increased apoptosis was observed. At the dose of 10 μg, the splenic finding was mild and consistent with low-grade, likely reversible immune modulation without evidence of systemic toxicity. Liver function was also evaluated by measuring serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT), known markers of liver function ( ). There was no measurable change in ALT or AST observed at the 30-μg dose compared with naïve ( ).
 Thus, systemic pathological and toxicological markers are not significantly elevated in the 10- and 30-μg doses of mRNA-IAJD97. Established four-component LNPs are typically dosed below 1 to 3 μg for vaccinations in mice, indicating that 10 μg would likely be an excess ( ).

## Norwalk/GI.1 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine elicits potent and sustained humoral immune responses
To analyze the immunogenicity of mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine, mice were immunized on days 0 and 28 by intravenous ( ) or intramuscular (i.m.) ( ) injection with increasing doses (1, 3, 5, and 10 μg) of mRNA encoding NW/GI.1. Neutralizing antibody (nAb) titers were consistently high across all doses tested, indicating that the minimal effective dose is below 1 μg of mRNA per mouse. For comparison with a conventional four-component LNP, mice were vaccinated with NW/GI.1 mRNA formulated with SM102. nAb titers against Norwalk/GI.1 were comparable between mice that received the NW/GI.1 mRNA encapsulated in IAJD97 or the clinically approved SM102 LNP ( ). To investigate the durability of the nAb response, serum from mice vaccinated with NW/GI.1 mRNA-IAJD97 was collected on day 150 postprime and analyzed for nAb titers. Following i.m. injection, nAb titers remained durable and persisted at a high level until at least day 150 postimmunization, with a 50% inhibitory dilution (ID50) of ~3000 at a dose of 5 μg of mRNA ( ). Additional characteristics of the NW/GI.1 mRNA-IAJD97 formulation, including particle diameter (nanometers), PDI, encapsulation efficiency (%), pKa, and zeta potential were also determined and shown in table S1.
 

## Norovirus VP1 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine elicits strong cellular immune responses
Due to the availability of a peptide pool specific to GII.4 but not GI.1 norovirus, the T cell assay analyzed the GII.4 variant. To investigate the capacity of mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine to induce a strong cellular response, mice were immunized with 5 μg of CapeTown2012/GII.4 mRNA coassembled with IAJD97 at days 0 and 28 ( ). Two weeks postboost, splenocytes were collected, and CD4 and CD8 T cell responses were evaluated for intracellular cytokine staining following stimulation with a GII.4 VP1 synthetic peptide pool as previously described ( ). GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccines elicited antigen-specific, polyfunctional CD4 and CD8 T cells expressing cytokines associated with type 1 immune responses: interferon-γ (IFN-γ), interleukin-2 (IL-2), and tumor necrosis factor–α (TNF-α) ( ). There were significantly higher levels compared with the control of triple-positive IFN-γ+IL-2+TNF-α+ CD4 T cells, highlighting the multifunctional T cell population elicited ( ). These data are consistent with what has been observed for the four-component LNP ( ), where IFN-γ emerges as the dominant cytokine in CD8 T cells, emphasizing its crucial role in the immune response alongside TNF-α ( ). The coexpression of IFN-γ and TNF-α by CD8 T cells further highlighted the potential significance of multifunctional T cells in mediating both protection and potency of the immune response ( ).
 To evaluate the capacity of GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccines to induce strong antigen-specific T follicular helper cell (TFH cell) and germinal center (GC) B cell responses, mice were i.m. immunized with a single dose of 10 μg per mouse. Draining LNs were examined 7 days postprime, and total numbers of TFH cells and GC B cells were enumerated by flow cytometry as CD4+B220−CD44hiCD62L−Bcl6+CXCR5+ and CD4/CD8−CD19+FAS+GL7+, respectively ( – ). A single immunization with norovirus-GII.4 mRNA-IAJD elicited robust increases in TFH cells and GC B cells ( ). The similar induction of high levels of type 1–biased TFH cells and GC B cells by LNPs and IAJDs suggests that the same adjuvant activity is present, which was expected as we identified that the ionizable lipid is responsible for the TFH cells inducing adjuvant activity ( ).

## DISCUSSION
Nucleoside-modified mRNA-based vaccines represent an innovative technology for combating infectious diseases, as demonstrated during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. While the four-component LNP system was pivotal in the success of COVID-19 vaccines developed by BioNTech/Pfizer and Moderna, it has limitations including low stability, a high proportion of structural lipids, and complex manufacturing requirements. These challenges hinder scalability and efficiency ( ).
In this study, we present a one-component IAJD platform for vaccine delivery. This platform uniquely self-assembles, offering a simplified and innovative approach to mRNA delivery. Unlike conventional four-component LNPs, the IAJD platform eliminates the need for cholesterol and PEG-conjugated lipids, improves stability at 4°C, reduces adverse effects, and achieves highly efficient targeted mRNA delivery to the spleen and LNs. These organs are critical for immune cell activation, particularly B and T cells ( ). By delivering mRNA to lymphoid organs, the IAJD platform enhances immunogenicity and promotes robust adaptive immune responses ( ).
To gain further insight into this enhanced immunological performance, it is important to explore the mechanistic basis of IAJD97’s unique biodistribution profile, specifically its preferential accumulation in lymphoid tissues. This distinct targeting behavior not only differentiates IAJD97 from conventional LNPs but also provides a foundation for developing next-generation delivery systems with improved immune organ selectivity. Previously, we reported a library of 31 simplified single-single (sSS) IAJDs, in which the ionizable amine group functions as the hydrophilic domain of the Janus dendrimers ( ). More than half of these sSS IAJDs, including IAJD97, demonstrated specific in vivo mRNA delivery to the spleen and LNs. It is possible that the final particle acquires a net negative charge after forming a corona with host proteins. Consistent with this, physicochemical properties beyond chemical structure, such as surface hydration and protein corona composition, likely play key roles in determining biodistribution ( , ).
Conventional mRNA-LNPs often accumulate in the liver due to its high blood flow and fenestrated endothelial cells ( ). Hepatocyte uptake is further facilitated by interactions between apolipoprotein E and low-density lipoprotein receptors. Although the liver’s tolerogenic immune environment can be ideal for reducing inflammation in chronic therapeutic interventions, it is less effective for vaccines as robust adaptive immune responses typically require activation of antigen-presenting cells in lymphoid tissues ( ). By contrast, targeting the spleen and LNs ensures strong immune activation by delivering mRNA directly to dendritic cells and macrophages. This enhances cytokine production and stimulates B and T cells, resulting in potent and long-lasting immunity, as demonstrated in mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine formulations.
Noroviruses are important human pathogens that cause an estimated 200,000 deaths per year, mostly in young children in resource limited nations across the globe ( – ). Virus tropism to mucosal tissues, high rates of evolution in response to herd immunity, and the lack of approved countermeasures dictate a critical need for improved vaccines ( , – ). The norovirus NW/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine elicited nAb titers comparable to those induced by the conventional four-component LNP SM102. There was no significant difference in nAb titers at day 56 between the two vaccines, indicating that the one-component IAJD97 platform elicits humoral responses equivalent to those induced by the benchmark SM102 LNP. The NW/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine also induces robust T cell activation ( ) comparable to the GII.4 mRNA-LNP platform ( ), as measured by CD4 and CD8 T cell cytokine production. Both mRNA-IAJD97 ( ) and mRNA-LNP ( ) vaccines stimulate TFH cell and GC B cell responses, which are critical indicators of vaccine efficacy. Both platforms also induce strong histo-blood group antigen (HBGA) blockade titers, a surrogate nAb titer, with mRNA-IAJD97 ( ) eliciting responses comparable to published LNP data ( ). Strong T helper 1 cell responses characterized by IFN-γ and IL-2 are elicited after natural norovirus infection as well ( ).
Temperature stability is crucial for vaccine quality as environmental factors profoundly affect efficacy. For the approved COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, the Moderna vaccine remains stable at −20°C for 6 months, and the Pfizer-BioNTech vaccine is stable at −80° to −60°C for the same duration. However, when stored at 2° to 8°C, their shelf lives drop to 30 and 5 days, respectively ( ). These limitations have restricted vaccine accessibility in underresourced regions and led to substantial waste of prepared vaccines ( ). By contrast, the mRNA-IAJD97 vaccine demonstrated exceptional and consistent thermostability. It retained luciferase activity above 108 luminescence flux after 26 weeks of storage at 4°C ( ). Even after 40 weeks, substantial luciferase activity persisted in both the whole body and spleen.
We conducted a side-by-side comparison of RNA integrity between our IAJD97 formulation and a conventional four-component LNP (SM102). The results showed that IAJD97 effectively preserves long RNA fragments after 7 months of storage at 4°C, indicating strong stability under refrigerated conditions. In contrast, mRNA formulated with SM102 and stored under identical conditions showed marked degradation, characterized by the absence of a clear peak and a lower DV200 value of 28.1%, indicating a higher degree of fragmentation and a reduced proportion of usable RNA (fig. S2). These findings underscore the superior long-term stability of mRNA in the IAJD97 formulation and support its potential as a robust delivery platform for cold-chain compatible vaccine development.
Recent clinical studies have reported that components of mRNA-LNP vaccines, including intact mRNA and ionizable lipids, can circulate in the bloodstream for up to 2 weeks postvaccination in some individuals ( ). This prolonged circulation has raised concerns about biodistribution, clearance, and the potential unintended immune responses such as anti-PEG antibody induction ( , ). Additionally, expression of spike protein has been detected in multiple tissues following COVID-19 mRNA vaccination in isolated publications ( ), although this was not observed in many other studies ( , ). Although we did not assess late time-point circulation or antigen expression in specific organs in the present study, we did not observe signs of inflammation or histopathological toxicity in major tissues, including the liver, lungs, and heart. Nonetheless, we recognize the importance of evaluating whether expression of VP1 in these organs may lead to localized immune activation or adverse effects.
Our IAJD platform is PEG-free and structurally distinct from conventional four-component LNP systems. These features may offer improved biodistribution and pharmacokinetic profiles. Future studies will include comprehensive analysis of long-term biodistribution, clearance, antigen expression in major tissues, and associated immune responses to ensure a complete safety and efficacy profile of IAJD-formulated mRNA vaccines.
There is growing interest in Janus dendrimers as delivery vehicles for mRNA and DNA vaccines, offering a promising alternative to conventional delivery methods ( ). Although the present study focuses on mRNA delivery, the IAJD platform is highly adaptable and can accommodate a range of nucleic acid cargos, including small interfering RNA (siRNA), circular RNA (circRNA), and plasmid DNA (pDNA). Because siRNAs are shorter and more rigid than mRNAs, careful optimization of the nitrogen-to-phosphate (N/P) ratio or lipid-to-RNA mass ratio is essential to ensure efficient formulation and intracellular delivery. In our preliminary experiments, IAJD97 successfully coassembled with siRNA and produced nanoparticles with similar size and PDI to those prepared with mRNA, demonstrating its flexibility for smaller cargos. Previous studies have also shown that Janus-type particles can efficiently deliver anticancer siRNAs, resulting in marked tumor suppression ( – ). For pDNA, which is larger and structurally more complex than mRNA, additional optimization of formulation parameters, such as charge ratio, mixing rate, and buffer conditions, will likely be required for effective condensation and delivery. Supporting this potential, successful pDNA delivery using polyamidoamine dendrimers has been demonstrated ( ). It has also been shown that dendrimer architecture and nucleic acid structure influence multivalent interactions, enabling adaptive and selective delivery ( ).
The one-component IAJD97 system offers adjuvant activity comparable to the established four-component LNP platform, as evidenced by similar levels of T cell and TFH cell activation and by nAb titers measured using a surrogate HBGA–virus-like particle (VLP) blocking assay. However, its simplified formulation offers substantial advantages. The IAJD97 platform is cost-effective and easier to manufacture, requiring fewer steps and eliminating the need for complex microfluidic equipment. This simplicity enhances accessibility and makes it suitable for widespread use in low- and middle-income countries. Furthermore, its stability at 4°C reduces storage and distribution challenges, particularly in regions with limited cold-chain infrastructure. These attributes position IAJD97 as a practical, scalable, and globally equitable vaccine delivery system.
In summary, our data demonstrate that the one-component IAJD97, coassembled with the norovirus VP1 mRNA, achieves efficient and durable targeted mRNA delivery, exhibits no detectable toxicity, remains stable for at least 26 weeks at 4°C, and induces robust humoral and cellular immune responses. Although norovirus mRNA was used here as proof of concept, the IAJD platform’s modular design should allow straightforward adaptation for a wide range of immunogens and nucleic acid, including siRNA, circRNA, and pDNA. These findings highlight the potential of one-component IAJDs as an efficient and adaptable platform for mRNA vaccine and therapeutics development.
The one-component IAJD platform offers a simplified, PEG-free alternative to conventional four-component LNPs. Its ability to self-assemble without cholesterol or PEG, together with efficient mRNA delivery to lymphoid tissues, supports potent adaptive immune responses. In murine models, these responses are comparable to those induced by the benchmark SM102 LNP vaccine, and the platform provides enhanced thermostability at 4°C and superior RNA integrity. Its streamlined formulation process, reduced toxicity, and cold-chain compatibility with standard, low-complexity manufacturing make this system particularly attractive for large-scale production and deployment in low- and middle-income settings. Together, these features position IAJD as a next-generation, modular, and scalable delivery platform for vaccines and other RNA-based therapeutics.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Norovirus VP1 mRNA production and characterization
mRNA encoding firefly luciferase (Luc) or VP1 capsid proteins of norovirus (Norwalk1968/GI.1 or CapeTown2012/GII.4) were synthesized through in vitro transcription using T7 RNA polymerase (MEGAscript, Ambion, catalog no. AMB13345) using linearized plasmids encoding codon-optimized constructs for norovirus. Codon-optimized sequences for Norwalk1968/GI.1 (GenBank, AGM33223.1) and CapeTown2012/GII.4 (GenBank, ALX87357.1), a strain of the GII.4 Sydney variant, were synthesized and cloned into plasmids for mRNA production as described ( , ). To optimize expression, nucleoside-modified mRNAs were transcribed to include poly(A) tails of 101 nucleotides. Modified nucleosides were incorporated by substituting uridine 5′-triphosphate with 1-methylpseudouridine-5′-triphosphate (TriLink, catalog no. N-1081). During transcription, the trinucleotide cap1 analog CleanCap (TriLink, catalog no. N-7413) was used for cotranscriptional capping as previously described ( ). The resulting m1Ψ-containing mRNAs were purified by cellulose purification as previously decries ( ), analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis, and stored frozen at −20°C. Nucleoside-modified mRNAs were assessed for double-stranded RNA, endotoxin, and dendritic cell–induced IFN-α levels as previously reported ( , ).

## Coassembly of mRNA with IAJD97
IAJD97 was synthesized as previously described ( , ). It should be noted that the first step to synthesize IAJD97, the alkylation of methyl gallate and 2-ethylhexyl bromide, was performed at 80°C for 12 hours. The final product IAJD97 was washed with 2% sodium bicarbonate solution to ensure that it was deprotonated and in a neutral state before coassembly with mRNA ( ). The purity and structural identity of final products and intermediates were determined using multiple techniques, including thin-layer chromatography, carbon 13 nuclear magnetic imaging (13C NMR), hydrogen nuclear magnetic imaging (1H NMR), and electrospray ionization time-of-flight (ESI-TOF) mass spectrometry (fig. S1, A and B). NMR spectra for both 1H and 13C were obtained using a Bruker Avance Neo spectrometer (400 MHz) at 298 K, with deuterated chloroform (CDCl3) serving as the solvent. NMR chemical shifts were referenced to the residual protic solvent signals of CDCl3 [1H, δ 7.26 parts per million (ppm); 13C, δ 77.16 ppm]. ESI-TOF mass spectrometry was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific Exactive instrument. For analysis, IAJD97 was dissolved in dichloromethane at the concentration of 3 mg/ml. The report includes the ionization method (positive-mode ESI), the chemical formula, the ion species and charge state, and the mass-to-charge ratio with both calculated and observed values.
Coassembly of IAJD97 and mRNA was performed using a previously published method with some modifications ( , ). Briefly, mRNA encoding luciferase, norovirus GI.1, or GII.4 capsid proteins were prepared at a concentration of 4 mg/ml in UltraPure deoxyribonuclease/ribonuclease-free polymerase chain reaction–certified water (Teknova, no. W3440). For the coassembly with IAJD97, 50 μg of mRNA was mixed with 15 mM acetate buffer at pH 4.0. This mixture was rapidly combined with IAJD97 dissolved in ethanol at an N/P ratio of 40, followed by 5 s of vortexing. The resulting formulation was subsequently diluted in PBS at pH 7.4 to achieve the desired mRNA amounts for administration.
Cryo-TEM was used to examine the morphology of mRNA-IAJD97 nanoparticles. Sample preparation involved depositing 20 μg of the solution (5 μl) onto a 300-mesh copper TEM grid, followed by rapid freezing in liquid ethane using a Vitrobot Mark IV system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The frozen samples were kept at liquid nitrogen temperature, and imaging was conducted at −170°C with an acceleration voltage of 200 kV using a Talos F200C G2 microscope.
The average hydrodynamic diameter (nanometers), PDI, and zeta potential of the mRNA-IAJD97 resulting formulation were measured using dynamic light scattering with a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern Instruments Ltd., Malvern, UK) before injection. The efficiency of mRNA encapsulation, defined as the ratio of encapsulated mRNA to free mRNA, was assessed using the Quant-iT RiboGreen RNA Assay Kit (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer’s protocol. To quantify free mRNA (nonencapsulated), the mRNA-IAJD formulations were diluted in tris-EDTA (TE) buffer and treated with RiboGreen reagent. For total mRNA quantification, the formulations were disrupted using 2% Triton X-100 in TE buffer before RiboGreen reagent treatment. Fluorescence changes were measured with a Qubit 4 Fluorometer (Invitrogen) at an excitation wavelength of 485 nm and an emission wavelength of 528 nm. The encapsulation efficiency was calculated using the formula: (total mRNA − free mRNA)/total mRNA × 100% (table S1).
The surface pKa of the resulting formulation was measured by a TNS [2-(p-toluidinyl) naphthalene-6-sulfonic acid] assay ( , ). Briefly, a buffer solution containing 150 mM sodium chloride, 20 mM sodium phosphate, 25 mM ammonium citrate, and 20 mM ammonium acetate was adjusted to reach pH values ranging from 4 to 9. In a black 96-well plate, 90 μl of each buffer solution, 5 μl of the mRNA-IAJD97 formulation, and 5 μl of TNS fluorescent probe were added to each well. The fluorescence intensity of each well was measured with an excitation wavelength of 320 nm and an emission wavelength of 450 nm using Synergy H1 microplate reader (BioTek). The half-maximal point of the fluorescence versus pH plot was determined as the surface pKa (table S1).

## Animal studies
BALB/c mice, 6- to 8-week-old male and female, were purchased from Charles River Laboratories (Wilmington, MA). For all in vivo studies, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (3 to 4% in oxygen for induction and 1 to 2% in oxygen for maintenance) and administrated retro-orbitally (intravenously) or intramuscularly (i.m.) with indicated doses of mRNA in 100 or 50 μl, respectfully. Norwalk1968/GI.1 (NW/GI.1) or CapeTown2012/GII.4 (CT/GII.4) mRNA-IAJD97 vaccines were diluted to the indicated mRNA amounts in PBS. For comparison with the conventional four-component encoding, NW/GI.1 was formulated with SM102 as previously described ( ). All vaccines were stored on ice after dilution and before administration. Vaccines were administered under isoflurane anesthesia by i.m. injection (50 μl) into the upper hind of both legs or by intravenous injection (100 μl), using 1, 3, 5, or 10 μg of NW/GI.1 or CT/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 vaccines and 3 μg of NW/GI.1-SM102 vaccine. Sera was collected at 4 weeks postprime (day 28) and postboost (day 56) via retro-orbital bleeding and stored at −80°C before thawing for analysis. Sera samples were analyzed against Norwalk/GI.1 VLPs for the ability to block the interaction of VLP with binding ligand in a surrogate neutralization assay as previously described ( , ).
Mice were housed under standard conditions with food and water provided ad libitum. After reaching end points, mice were euthanized by carbon dioxide inhalation, with death confirmed by the absence of vital signs and corneal reflex. All in vivo experiments were conducted in accordance with the University of Pennsylvania Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee–approved protocols (803941) adhering to the US National Institutes of Health’s Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Mice were housed and cared for in Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care International–accredited facilities.

## Determine mRNA-IAJD97 formulation stability
The stability of Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation was assessed over time at 4°C. Freshly prepared Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulations were stored under two different conditions: in acetate buffer (resulting pH 4.81 ± 0.08) or after dilution with PBS (resulting pH 6.02 ± 0.04). Mice were injected with a dose of 10 or 5 μg per mouse, respectively, either 1 hour after formulation (fresh) or after storing the formulations at +4°C for 1, 2, 4, 8, 20, 26, and 40 weeks. IVIS images of mice’s whole body, draining LNs, and major organs were analyzed 4 hours postinjection after indicated storage time. The particle diameter (nanometers) and PDI of the Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulations were determined before each injection.
To evaluate RNA integrity over time, Luc or CT/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 formulations were prepared as described in the Co-assembly of mRNA with IAJD97 section of the Materials and Methods. For comparison with a conventional 4-component LNP, CT/GII.4 mRNA was encapsulated with SM102 as previously described ( ). All formulations were stored at +4°C for 7 months. After storage, formulations were disrupted with chloroform, and the extracted mRNA was precipitated using isopropanol and salt, followed by centrifugation. The resulting pellets were washed with ethanol and resuspended in nuclease-free water. mRNA concentrations were quantified, and equal amounts were analyzed for integrity by capillary gel electrophoresis using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer at the Penn Genomics and Sequencing Core.

## Bioluminescence characterization for Luc mRNA-IAJD97 formulation delivery
Female or male 6- to 8-week-old BALB/c mice were administered 5 or 10 μg of Luc mRNA-IAJD97 in a 100-μl volume via retro-orbital intravenous injection. Four hours postinjection, mice were injected intraperitoneally with d-luciferin (Regis Technologies) at a dose of 150 mg/kg of body weight. The mice were anesthetized in a ventilated anesthesia chamber with 3% isoflurane (Piramal Healthcare Limited) in oxygen and imaged 10 min post–d-luciferin injection using an IVIS (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA). For organ imaging, mice were euthanized, organs were collected immediately, and bioluminescence imaging was performed. Bioluminescence was quantified as photon flux (photons per second) in each region of interest using nonsaturated acquisitions and background subtraction in Living Image software (PerkinElmer), as previously described ( – ). Photon flux is measured continuously and is independent of the produced images, which may appear to be saturated.

## Evaluation of the toxicity of mRNA-IAJD formulation
Luc mRNA was coassembled with IAJD97 as described above. The formulation was then concentrated using a Vivaspin ultrafiltration spin column MWCO 10,000 (Cytiva, no. 28-9322-47) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. Mice were randomly assigned to control or treated. To assess the potential toxicity of the formulation, mice were anesthetized with 3% isoflurane and received a single retro-orbital intravenous injection of 10 or 30 μg of Luc mRNA-IAJD97, naïve mice were used as a control. The following criteria were used to assess the toxicity of the formulated Luc mRNA-IAJD97: mouse behavior, serum cytokine analysis, liver enzyme activities, and histological scores of main organs. Mice were monitored for any signs of distress and were euthanized 24 hours postinjections. Serum samples and organs were collected for further analysis.
The left lung lobe was inflated with 3% paraformaldehyde and then heart, liver, spleen, and kidney tissues were fixed in 3% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Four-micrometer sections were cut, slide mounted, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to observe histological changes. A board-certified pathologist evaluated the histological sections of the organs for signs of inflammation and deviations from normal histology. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays for AST (Abcam ab263882) and ALT (Abcam ab282882) were performed in serum in a 1:80 dilution according to the manufacturer’s guidelines.

## Surrogate antibody neutralization assay
Norovirus VLPs of GI.1 (AFS33555.1) were produced by insertion of the capsid gene into Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus replicons as described ( , ). For antibody blockade of ligand binding assays, VLPs (0.25 μg/ml) were incubated with two- or three-fold serial dilutions of sera before being transferred to plates coated with porcine gastric mucin (10 μg/ml) (Sigma Aldrich, catalog no. H4784). Bound VLPs were detected using rabbit anti-VLP sera (custom sera, Cocalico) followed by anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G–horseradish peroxidase (Cytiva, catalog no. NA934) and visualized with TMB ultra (Thermo Fisher Scientific, catalog no. 34029). The percentage of VLP binding with serum pretreatment was compared with binding without serum pretreatment, and the ID50 titers were calculated using dose-response sigmoidal curve fitting of normalized data in GraphPad Prism v9.5.1. Samples that did not block ≥50% of control binding at the lowest dilution tested ( ) were assigned a titer of 20 for statistical comparisons ( , ).

## Polyfunctional T cell analysis
The induction of antigen-specific T cells was evaluated through intracellular cytokine staining following peptide stimulation. BALB/c mice (male and female, aged 6 to 8 weeks) were immunized via intravenous injection with 5 μg of CapeTown2012/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 per mouse on days 0 and 28. Control mice received an equal volume of sterile PBS. Splenocytes were harvested 14 days postboost, counted with a Vi-Cell automated cell counter, and plated at one million splenocytes per well in 100 μl of RPMI 1640 medium. Peptide stimulation was performed by adding 80 μl of a CT/GII.4 VP1 synthetic peptide pool (150 peptides, 15 amino acids each with 11 amino acid overlaps, 80 to 90% purity, synthesized by GenScript) to the medium to achieve a final concentration of 1.5 μg/ml, with CD28 (1 μg/ml) added as a costimulatory signal. The cells were incubated for 1 hour at 37°C. To block cytokine secretion, 20 μl of brefeldin A (final concentration, 0.2 μM; BD Biosciences, catalog no. 51-2301 ZK) and monensin (final concentration, 0.14 μM; BD Biosciences, catalog no. 51-2092KZ) were added 1 hour after stimulation, and cells were incubated for an additional 5 hours at 37°C. Splenocytes were then washed twice with fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) buffer (PBS with 2% fetal bovine serum) and stained with Aqua Live/Dead dye (Invitrogen, catalog no. L34966) for 10 min in the dark at room temperature. After blocking with anti-CD16/CD32 (TruStain FcX, BioLegend, catalog no. 101320) for 15 min at 4°C, surface markers CD4 and CD8 were stained in FACS buffer for 30 min in the dark at 4°C. Subsequently, cells were washed with FACS buffer and permeabilized using the Cytofix/Cytoperm kit (BD Biosciences, catalog no. 51-2090KZ) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Intracellular cytokine staining for induction of IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α was performed at 4°C for 30 min. For flow cytometry analysis, splenocytes were fixed in 1% paraformaldehyde in PBS and stored at 4°C until analysis. Samples were analyzed using a modified Cytek Aurora spectral analyzer, collecting 500,000 events per sample. Data analysis was performed using FlowJo software (Tree Star Inc., Ashland, OR, USA). Overlapping peptide pools were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), which also served as a negative control to evaluate cytokine production in CD4 and CD8 T cells in the absence of peptide stimulation. Gating was based on DMSO-treated cells and applied to peptide-treated cells for each animal, ensuring reliable assessment of vaccine-specific T cell activation and functionality. Positive controls included samples stimulated with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (10 ng/ml; MilliporeSigma, catalog no. P1585-1 M6) and ionomycin (250 ng/ml; STEMCELL Technologies, catalog no. 73722). Splenocytes were stained as per the antibody panel in table S6. The gating strategy was performed as published ( ).

## Flow cytometric phenotyping of TFH cell and B cell responses
BALB/c male and female mice, aged 6 to 8 weeks, were immunized by i.m. injection with 10 μg of CT/GII.4 mRNA-IAJD97 per mouse on day 0 (prime). Control mice received an equivalent volume of sterile PBS. For the immunophenotyping of murine TFH cells and GC B cells, bilateral popliteal and inguinal LNs were collected from immunized mice 7 days postprime. LNs were processed through 40-μm cell strainers in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium to obtain single-cell suspensions. One million cells were resuspended in 100 μl of FACS buffer for analysis. All cells were stained for viability using eBiosciences Fixable Viability Dye eFluor780 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, catalog no. 65-0865-14) for 10 min in the dark at room temperature, followed by Fc blocking with TruStain FcX (BioLegend, catalog no. 101320) for 15 min at 4°C. After washing with FACS buffer, cells were stained for 1 hour using the antibody panel listed in table S7. Staining cells were then washed twice, fixed with 200 μl of 1% paraformaldehyde, and analyzed using a modified LSR II flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). Flow cytometry data were processed using FlowJo software (Tree Star Inc., Ashland, OR, USA). The gating strategy was as previously published ( ).

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were completed using GraphPad Prism version 9 or 10. Results are reported as means ± SEM. Data were tested for normal distribution using a Shapiro-Wilk test. If normally distributed, statistical significance for multiple group comparisons was determined using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s or Šídák’s post hoc test and is further indicated in the figure legends. If not normally distributed, statistical significance for multiple comparisons was determined using a Kruskal-Wallis H test with Dunn’s multiple comparisons data. For parametric single comparisons, statistical significance was determined using an unpaired t test compared with control groups as indicated in the figure legends. If data were not equal variance, as determined by an F test, a Welch’s correction was used. If single comparisons were nonparametric, statistical significance was determined using a Mann-Whitney U test. All P values of <0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical tests were conducted using a 5% significance level. n = 33 animals per group and is further indicated in the figure legends.
