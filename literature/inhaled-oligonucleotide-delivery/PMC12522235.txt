## Abstract

## Background
Numerous studies have highlighted the beneficial effects of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) in various inflammatory disorders. However, the regulatory role of MSCs in inflammatory atherosclerosis and the molecular mechanisms underlying their anti-inflammatory properties have largely remained elusive.

## Methods
Differential ultracentrifugation was performed to isolate extracellular vesicles (EVs) released by menstrual blood-derived mesenchymal stem cells (MenSCs). An ApoE knockout atherosclerotic animal model was employed to investigate the regulatory effect of MenSC-EVs on inflammatory atherosclerosis. miRNA microarray screening analyses were conducted to identify potential effectors in MenSC-EVs that play a key role in the suppression of atherosclerosis mediated by the EVs.

## Results
We demonstrated the remarkable potential of MenSC-EVs in alleviating atherosclerosis through the NF-κB signaling pathway. miR-574-5p serves as a crucial effector molecule transported by MenSC-EVs, suppressing endothelial inflammation and promoting nitric oxide production. This regulation contributes to the attenuation of atherosclerosis by regulating the abundance of c-Rel. The miR-574-5p/c-Rel axis shows significant clinical relevance to atherosclerosis.

## Conclusions
This study reveals that the engineering of EVs derived from MenSCs holds significant promise as a strategic clinical approach for addressing inflammatory atherosclerosis.

## Highlights
• MenSC-derived EVs possess an intrinsic ability to suppress inflammatory atherosclerosis.

• miR-574 knockout clearly promotes atherosclerotic plaque formation.
• The MenSC-EVs lacking miR-574-5p exhibit a diminished therapeutic effect on inflammatory atherosclerosis.
• miR-574-5p negatively regulates NF-κB signaling through the posttranscriptional degradation of c-Rel.

## Background
Atherosclerosis is a chronic inflammatory disorder characterized by the activation of endothelial cells and smooth muscle cells, the recruitment of monocytes to the endothelium, and the accumulation of lipid material in the subendothelial intima. The initial formation of advanced atherosclerotic plaques is attributed to the retention of apolipoprotein B (apoB)-containing lipoproteins (LPs) in the intima [ – ]. These LPs can undergo modifications that resemble those of pathogens, thereby triggering low-grade endothelial inflammation and dysfunction [ ]. This early inflammation of endothelial cells is considered one of the most significant risk factors for the development of advanced atherosclerosis [ , ]. In response to inflammation, endothelial cells secrete a variety of vascular adhesion molecules, chemokines, and cytokines that play crucial roles in recruiting macrophages to the subendothelial intima, contributing to the development of advanced atherosclerotic lesions [ , ].
Numerous investigations have reported that multiple inflammatory signaling pathways are involved in early endothelium inflammation, among which NF-κB is the major inflammatory signaling pathway. The NF-κB family comprises five closely related proteins, namely p50 (NF-κB1), p52 (NF-κB2), p65 (RelA), RelB, and c-Rel (Rel). These proteins share a conserved N-terminal Rel homology domain (RHD) consisting of approximately 300 amino acids [ – ]. In cells, NF-κB proteins exist as dimers, either homo or heterodimers, that are capable of binding to DNA. The RHD directly interacts with DNA, whereas distinct protein domains facilitate positive or negative modulation of target gene transcription through the recruitment of coactivators or corepressors, respectively. It is well established that the aberrant activation of NF-κB signaling induces the secretion of proinflammatory factors, including adhesion molecules such as VCAM-1 and ICAM-1, as well as inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and IL-6 [ – ]. This process contributes to early endothelial inflammation and the development of atherosclerotic lesions. In contrast to these atherogenic factors, multiple atheroprotective regulators, including endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS)—a downstream regulator of NF-κB signaling—negatively regulate atherogenesis by promoting vascular smooth muscle relaxation and inhibiting platelet aggregation and adhesion through the production of endothelial nitric oxide (NO) [ , ].

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are adult stem cells characterized by their pluripotent nature, allowing them to differentiate into a wide range of cell types [ – ]. These MSCs can be derived from various tissues in the human body and can be expanded and self-renew in vitro. Regardless of their tissue source, MSCs share several common characteristics, including adhesion growth under standard tissue culture conditions. They express specific cell surface markers, such as CD73, CD90, and CD105, but lack the expression of markers such as CD34, CD45, and CD79 [ – ]. In vitro studies have demonstrated the ability of MSCs to differentiate into adipose, osteoblastic, and cartilage cells. Moreover, research has shown that MSCs can mediate the transfer of specific microRNAs (miRNAs), mRNAs, lipids, proteins, and even organelles through the extracellular vesicle (EV)-dependent pathway [ – ]. This transfer enables signal transmission and communication between different cells, ultimately influencing the biological function of recipient cells by modulating their microenvironment and internal signaling pathways. MSCs have been reported to have critical therapeutic effects on multiple inflammatory disorders through their anti-inflammatory properties via the EV pathway [ , ]. In this study, we identified MenSC-EVs as a potential therapeutic approach for treating inflammatory atherosclerosis. Through small RNA screening analysis, we discovered that miR-574-5p, a previously identified regulator closely associated with the modulation of inflammatory signaling cascades—particularly the NF-κB pathway—exhibited high expression levels in MenSC-EVs. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that miR-574-5p carried by MenSC-derived EVs is likely to suppress inflammatory atherosclerosis by regulating the NF-κB pathway. Moreover, MenSCs offer several advantages over other sources of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), as they can be collected periodically, non-invasively, and without causing any trauma [ , ]. In our earlier research, we discovered that 30–50 ml of menstrual blood can be collected during a menstrual cycle, and a 5 ml sample can produce 100 million passage 3 MSCs, which is adequate for clinical applications [ , ]. Therefore, this groundbreaking research presents a promising therapeutic strategy for addressing inflammatory atherosclerosis.

## Methods

## Cell culture
Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were cultured in complete ECM obtained from ScienCell (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Human monocyte THP-1 cells were cultured in RPMI medium (HyClone) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal calf serum (Gibco) and 100 units ml−1 streptomycin and penicillin (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. Both the HUVEC and THP-1 cell lines were purchased from ScienCell Research Laboratories (ScienCell, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Human embryonic kidney 293 T cells (HEK293T) were grown in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. MEFs were isolated from embryos following previously reported methods [ ].

## Antibodies and chemical reagents
Anti-CD9 (#BS9833M) and anti-CD81 (#BS91318) antibodies were purchased from Bioworld Technology, Inc. Anti-c-Rel (#82,000, for IF) antibody was from Cell Signaling Technology. Anti-CD63 (#25,682–1-AP), anti-calnexin (#10,427–2-AP), anti-eNOS (#27,120–1-AP), anti-c-Rel (#27,693–1-AP, for WB) and Anti-VCAM-1 (#11,444–1-AP) antibodies were purchased from Proteintech Group, Inc. For chemical reagents, Lipofectamine 3000 (#L3000015) was purchased from Invitrogen, GW4869 (#HY-19363) was purchased from MedChemExpress (Shanghai, China), TNFα (#H8916) was obtained from Sigma, and CellTracker™ Green CMFDA (#C2925) was purchased from Invitrogen. The NO-specific fluorescent dye, DAF-FM-DA (3-amino, 4-aminomethyl-2′, 7′-difluorescein, diacetate) was obtained from Beyotime Biotechnology (#S0020S).

## RNA interference
The siRNAs targeting human c-Rel (sic-Rel #1: siG000005966A-1–5; sic-Rel #2: siG000005966C-1–5) were purchased from Ribobio (Guangzhou, China). The siRNAs targeting mouse c-Rel (#siB170209024724-1–5) were also from Ribobio. In this study, we employed a combination of two distinct shRNAs for the AAV-mediated knockdown of c-Rel. The specific sequences used were: 5′-GAATGAGCCTGGACTATCA-3′ and 5′-GATGCATTTGATAGATCAA-3′. For the AAV-mediated knockdown of c-Rel, these target sequences were inserted into an AAV-based core plasmid, creating two separate shRNAs. The core plasmids, along with the pAAV-RC8 and pHelper plasmids, were co-transfected into HEK293A cells to facilitate AAV production. Subsequently, the AAV was purified using an AAVpro Purification Kit (6666, Takara) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Transfection of miR-574-5p inhibitors or mimics and the preparation of miR-574-5p-overexpressing EVs
HUVECs were transfected with in vitro synthesized oligonucleotides designed to bind to the complementary sequence of miR-574-5p, thereby inhibiting its expression in HUVECs. The miR-574-5p inhibitor (#miR20004795-1–5) and the miR-574-5p mimic (#miR10004795-1–5) were both purchased from Ribobio (Guangzhou, China). To prepare miR-574-5p-overexpressing MenSC-EVs, a pLV-U6-based core vector expressing miR-574-5p was cotransfected with the psPAX2 packaging and pMD2.G envelope plasmids into HEK293T cells to produce lentiviral particles, following a previously described procedure [ ]. The resulting lentiviruses were then used to infect MenSCs, and EVs were isolated at least 48 h post-infection.

## Animal study
The ApoE−/− mice were from Cyagen (#C001067). The miR-574± mice were generated using the CRISPR/Cas9 gene-editing system. The gRNA sequences for the miR-574± mice are as follows: gRNA1: CGAGGGCCCTGCGTGGGTGCGGG; gRNA2: GAGGGTGCAGACCGGGCGTGCGG; gRNA3: TCACACGCCCGCACCCACGCAGG; gRNA4: CACCCACACGCCCGCACGCCCGG. To generate sufficient ApoE−/−&miR-574−/− double homozygous mice, we crossed male ApoE−/−&miR-574−/− mice with female ApoE−/−&miR-574± mice, which were produced through in vitro fertilization using sperm and egg cells from heterozygous ApoE±&miR-574± mice. This cross generates ApoE−/−&miR-574−/− male mice, which were used for atherosclerosis model studies. Genotyping was verified using PCR analysis (Fig. S5), with the following primer sequences: For ApoE−/− mice: Forward, 5′-GCCTAGCCGAGGGAGAGCCG-3′; Reverse, 5′-GCCGCCCCGACTGCATCT-3′. For miR-574−/− mice: Forward, 5′-CCCCAGTGCTTCGTAATGGAAG-3′; Reverse, 5′-TGATGGATCAGGATGGAGGTCAAG-3′. For the induction of the atherosclerosis model, 8-week-old male mice were fed a high-fat diet (HFD) containing 1.25% cholesterol and 21% crude fat for 16 weeks. Throughout the high-fat feeding period, EVs released by 1 × 106 MenSCs were dissolved in 60 µl of PBS and administered to the mice via tail-vein injection once every 2 weeks. For AAV-mediated c-Rel depletion, we administered a combination of AAVs encoding two shRNAs that specifically target c-Rel to 8-week-old male mice via tail vein injection at a concentration of 1 × 1012 viral genome/ml (vg/ml). All the mice were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions with a 12-h light/12-h dark cycle at 21–24 °C and 40–60% humidity. At the conclusion of the animal studies, all the mice were euthanized using carbon dioxide inhalation. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines and regulations established by the Ethics Committee of Bengbu Medical University. Mice from different groups were randomly assigned to eliminate bias related to the order of MenSC-EV or virus injections. Experimental data were collected and analyzed in a blinded manner, ensuring that both the operator and designer remained unaware of each other’s identities.

## Oil red O staining analysis
The entire aorta was isolated and subsequently subjected to Oil Red O staining for en-face analysis. Photographs of the isolated aorta were taken via a digital camera. In addition, the aortic roots were frozen in optimal cutting temperature (OCT) compound and sectioned at a 7 µm thickness for subsequent Oil Red O staining to measure the lipid content.

## Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR)
Reverse transcription of 1 µg of total RNA extracted from specific cells was performed via a commercial kit (#G486, Applied Biological Materials). For miRNA reverse transcription, a stem loop-based method was employed to synthesize first-strand cDNA, and U6 snRNA was used as a normalizer and quantifier. The quantitative PCR assay was conducted using EvaGreen qPCR Master Mix following the provided instructions. Relative gene expression was determined via the 2−ΔΔCT method. For the quantification of proinflammatory factors, GAPDH was utilized as a normalization control. The sequences of primers used for qRT-PCR in this study are shown in Additional file 1: Tables 1 and 2.

## Preparation of MenSCs
MenSCs were obtained from Healthy young women aged 25 to 35, provided by the Zhongyuan Stem Cell Research Institute in the Xinxiang High Tech Zone. Informed consent was obtained from all participants involved in the study. The isolation of MenSCs from menstrual blood followed established protocols [ , ]. In brief, menstrual blood was collected using menstrual cups and subsequently diluted with an equal volume of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 1% puromycin and a small amount of Heparin sodium to prevent clotting. The diluted samples were then separated using a standard Ficoll procedure. The deciduous endometrium and karyocytes were re-suspended in DMEM complete medium and cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2. After 24 h of incubation, unattached cells were discarded, and fresh medium was added to the culture. For the experiments, MenSCs from passages three to five were utilized. The phenotype of MenSCs was evaluated using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) with a stem cell surface marker detection kit (562,245, BD Biosciences, USA) via flow cytometry analysis. The results indicated that MenSCs were positive for CD90, CD73, and CD105, while negative for CD45, CD34, CD11b, CD19, and HLA-DR (Additional file 1: Fig. S1).

## Isolation of MenSC-EVs
For the isolation of MenSC-derived extracellular vesicles (MenSC-EVs), the MenSCs were cultured until they reached 80% confluency, at which point the culture medium was collected. This medium was then processed through differential ultracentrifugation using an XPN-100 (Beckman Coulter, USA), following previously established protocols [ ]. The isolated MenSC-EVs were characterized using transmission electron microscopy and nanoparticle flow cytometry. Positive surface markers of the EVs, including CD63, CD9, and CD81, along with the negative marker Calnexin, were identified through Western blot analysis. The isolated EVs were subsequently dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for further application in an animal study. Each mouse received a tail-vein injection of the EVs, with 60 µl of PBS used to dissolve the EVs derived from 1 × 106 MenSCs.

## Monocyte adhesion assay
HUVEC monolayers infected with the miR-574-5p mimic were cultured in a 6-well plate. The cells were subsequently treated with TNFα (20 ng/ml, 2 h) before being incubated with THP-1 monocytes. The THP-1 cells were prelabeled with CellTracker Green CMFDA (8 µM, 0.5 h) at 37 °C. Each well of HUVECs was then incubated with 0.2 ml of CMFDA-labeled THP-1 cells dissolved in a complete medium (107 cells ml−1). After 2 h of incubation at 37 °C, the cells were subjected to three washes with a complete medium. Finally, the adherent monocytes were imaged via fluorescence microscopy.

## Nitric oxide (NO) assay
HUVECs cultured in a 6-well plate were transfected with either a miR-574-5p mimic or c-Rel shRNAs when they reached 80% confluence. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, the cells were incubated with a NO-specific fluorescent dye, DAF-FM-DA (3-amino, 4-aminomethyl-2′, 7′-difluorescein, diacetate), at 37 °C for 40 min, then washed with PBS. After three washes, the cells were imaged using a fluorescence microscope.

## Unbiased mRNA sequencing and miRNA microarray chip screening
The mRNA sequencing experiments were conducted by Annoroad (Beijing, China). In brief, total RNA was extracted via TRIzol reagent and subjected to Library construction following standard Illumina protocols. The Libraries were sequenced via the paired-end RNA-seq approach on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencing platform. For data analysis, the raw reads were aligned to the reference genome via HTSeq-count and subsequently processed via Cufflinks. Cufflinks utilizes normalized RNA-seq fragment counts to assess the relative abundances of transcripts. DESeq2 is designed for differential gene expression analysis, based on the negative binomial distribution for count values. Unlike DESeq, DESeq2 estimates sample depth and gene-specific parameters, using linear regression for dispersion to account for shared expression deviations among similarly expressed genes. It estimates the expression level of each gene in each sample using linear regression, calculates p-values with the Wald test, and corrects them using the BH method. Genes with q ≤ 0.05 and |log2_ratio|≥ 1 are identified as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The raw data have been deposited in the Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database with the accession numbers SRR25209372, SRR25209373, SRR25209374, SRR25209375, SRR25209376, and SRR25209377.
The miRNA microarray chip screening experiments were conducted by OE Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Briefly, total RNA was extracted from MenSC-EVs derived from two different donors and quantified. Sample labeling, microarray hybridization, and washing were performed according to the manufacturer’s standard protocol. The raw data have been deposited in the NCBI GEO database under accession number GSE223537.

## Luciferase assay
To investigate whether c-Rel is a direct target gene of miR-574-5p, a construct containing the c-Rel 3′-UTR with either the normal miR-574-5p-binding site or a mutant binding site was inserted into the psiCHECK-2 reporter vector. These constructed reporter vectors were cotransfected with either the miR-574-5p mimic or a mock negative control into HEK293T cells. The cells were then incubated for 48 h. After incubation, the cells were harvested and lysed. The luciferase activity was measured via a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (#RG028, Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Cellular uptake of EV-transported miR-574-5p
First, MenSCs were transfected with miR-574-5p mimics. After 24 h of transfection, MenSCs overexpressing the miR-574-5p mimic were pretreated with GW4869 (10 µM) for 24 h, while the control group received PBS as a pretreatment. Following GW4869 treatment, MenSCs were subsequently seeded in the upper chamber of a 0.4 µm transwell plate and then cocultured with HUVECs seeded in the lower chamber.

## In vitro incubation of MenSC-EVs with HUVECs
One microgram of MenSC-EVs was incubated with 1 × 105 HUVECs for 10 h. Prior to harvesting, the HUVECs were exposed to TNFα (20 ng/ml, 2 h) or a vehicle control (PBS). The cells were subsequently subjected to qPCR and Western blotting analyses.

## luorescent in situ hybridization (FISH)
Briefly, in this study, the cells were first rinsed with PBS and then fixed with 4% formaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature. The fixed cells were then permeabilized with buffer containing 0.5% Triton X-100 and 5 mM vanadyl ribonucleoside complex (#R3380, Sigma) on ice for 10 min. After being washed with 2 × SSC, the cells were subjected to hybridization using an antisense probe against miR-574-5p, which had the sequence 5′-ACACACTCACACAC ACACACTCA-3′ and was conjugated with Cy3. The detailed procedures for hybridization were conducted as previously described [ ].

## Patient samples
Two patients with coronary artery atherosclerosis were recruited to analyze the correlation between the miR-574-5p/c-Rel axis and atherogenesis. A normal great saphenous vein served as a negative control. Informed written consent was obtained from all participants involved in the study. Detailed information regarding the patients is provided in Additional file 1: Table 3.

## Statistical analysis
In this study, statistical analyses were performed via GraphPad Prism software. For comparisons between two groups, a two-tailed Student’s t-test was used. For comparisons involving three or more groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was utilized. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data presented in the study are based on at least three independent experiments and are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD).

## Results

## The extracellular vesicles released by MenSCs suppress inflammatory atherosclerosis
An expanding body of prior research has revealed the anti-inflammatory attributes of menstrual blood-derived mesenchymal stem cells (MenSCs) in diverse pathological conditions facilitated by intercellular communication through the extracellular vesicle (EV) pathway [ , – ]. In this study, we aimed to investigate the potential of MenSC-released EVs (MenSC-EVs) in improving inflammatory atherosclerosis. Initially, stem cells were isolated from the menstrual blood of women, and the cell surface markers of the MSCs were characterized via flow cytometry analysis. As shown in Additional file 1: Fig.S1, more than 95% of the cells were positive for CD90, CD73, and CD105, with fewer than 1% showing positivity for CD45, CD34, CD11b, CD19, and HLA-DR. These results indicate the distinctive properties of MSCs. We subsequently obtained MenSC-EVs through ultracentrifugation and confirmed their characteristics via western blotting, scanning electron microscopy, and nanoparticle tracking analysis (Additional file 1:Fig.S2A‒C). To assess the uptake of MenSC-EVs, we conducted an EV uptake assay and observed the transfer of green fluorescent protein (GFP)-tagged CD63, a specific marker protein for EVs, into human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) after 48 h of incubation. The fluorescence microscope analysis clearly demonstrated the internalization of MenSC-secreted EVs by endothelial cells (Fig.  A). To investigate the ability of MenSC-EVs to inhibit endothelial inflammation, we performed a monocyte adhesion assay and observed a notable reduction in TNFα-induced THP-1 cell adhesion to HUVECs following intervention with MenSC-EVs (P = 0.0012 for the TNF vs. TNF + EVs group) (Fig.  B). Furthermore, quantitative PCR and western blot analyses revealed a significant decrease in the expression of proinflammatory factors in HUVECs following EV treatment (Fig.  C and D). To validate the inhibitory role of MenSC-EVs in advanced atherosclerosis, we systematically administered EVs every 2 weeks to 8-week-old ApoE knockout (KO) mice. These mice are widely recognized models for studying atherosclerosis. Additionally, the mice were simultaneously fed a high-fat diet for 16 weeks before being sacrificed (Fig.  E). Notably, the administration of EVs significantly suppressed atherosclerotic formation (P = 0.0051 for the PBS vs. EVs group in Fig.  F; P = 0.0028 for the PBS vs. EVs group in Fig.  G) (Fig.  F and G). Collectively, these findings provide solid evidence supporting the inhibitory effects of MenSC-secreted EVs on inflammatory atherosclerosis.
 

## MiR-574-5p carried by MenSC-EVs negatively regulates inflammatory atherosclerosis
Numerous studies have demonstrated that miRNAs can be delivered to target cells through EV-mediated pathways, where they play crucial roles in various biochemical functions. On the basis of this understanding, we hypothesized that the inhibitory effects of MenSC-EVs on atherosclerosis might be mediated by specific miRNAs. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a comprehensive small RNA microarray screening analysis. Notably, miR-574-5p, a previously identified regulator closely associated with the modulation of inflammatory signaling cascades [ ], exhibited one of the highest expression levels in MenSC-EVs and demonstrated significant species conservation (Fig.  A and B). Furthermore, we observed a notable reduction in the level of miR-574-5p in the aortic root (a region known for its susceptibility to atherosclerosis) of ApoE−/− mice after the introduction of a high-fat diet, as confirmed by a fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assay (Fig.  C). These collective results strongly suggest that miR-574-5p may play a pivotal role in the regulation of inflammatory atherosclerosis.
 To investigate the potential role of miR-574-5p in the regulation of endothelial inflammation, we initially suppressed miR-574-5p expression in HUVECs via synthetic oligonucleotides in an in vitro setting. The cells were subsequently exposed to THP-1 monocytes after treatment with either TNFα or a vehicle control (PBS). As anticipated, the monocyte adhesion assay revealed a significant increase in monocyte-HUVEC adhesion upon miR-574-5p inhibition (P = 0.0019 for the Ctrl + TNF vs. Inh + TNF group) (Fig.  A). This observation was further supported by quantitative PCR analysis, which revealed a marked increase in both the RNA and protein levels of adhesion molecules, such as VCAM-1 and ICAM-1, as well as the chemokine MCP-1 (Fig.  B and C). To gain a more comprehensive understanding of the regulatory role of miR-574-5p in advanced atherosclerosis in vivo, we utilized the CRISPR/Cas9 gene-editing technique to establish miR-574 KO mice. These mice were subsequently crossed with ApoE−/− mice to generate double-KO mice. Importantly, our analysis via Oil Red O staining revealed that the absence of miR-574 significantly augmented the formation of atherosclerotic plaques (Fig.  D and E). Consistent with this observation, the protein levels of VCAM1 were markedly increased in the aortic roots of mice following miR-574 KO, as demonstrated by an immunofluorescence assay (Fig.  F). To further confirm the inhibitory effect of miR-574-5p on the inflammatory response, we isolated mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) from both ApoE−/− mice and double-KO mice. We subsequently assessed the changes in the expression of proinflammatory factors. Quantitative PCR analysis demonstrated that the disruption of miR-574 resulted in a marked increase in both the RNA and protein expression levels of proinflammatory factors in MEFs (Fig.  G and ). In summary, the cumulative data presented here provide compelling evidence to support the crucial role of miR-574-5p as a negative regulator in the context of inflammatory atherosclerosis.
 

## miR-574-5p is required for MenSC-EV-mediated inhibition of inflammatory atherosclerosis
Given the pivotal role of miR-574-5p in the regulation of inflammatory atherosclerosis, we sought to investigate whether the inhibition of endothelial inflammation and advanced atherosclerosis by MenSC-EVs is dependent on miR-574-5p. To address this hypothesis, we initially conducted a coculture experiment involving MenSCs and HUVECs to determine whether the miR-574-5p carried by MenSCs could be transferred to HUVECs via the EV pathway. Notably, a significantly greater amount of miR-574-5p was observed in HUVECs in the lower chamber of the transwell plate when cocultured with MenSCs transfected with miR-574-5p mimics than in those cocultured with MenSCs expressing a mock control. This transfer of miR-574-5p was nullified upon treatment with GW4869, a specific inhibitor of EV production (Fig.  A and B), indicating that the transmission of miR-574-5p relies heavily on EVs. We subsequently explored whether EVs carrying a relatively high level of miR-574-5p exert a relatively strong inhibitory effect on inflammatory atherosclerosis. Coincubation of MenSC-EVs with HUVECs revealed that miR-574-5p-overexpressing EVs had a more pronounced inhibitory effect on monocyte adhesion to HUVECs than did EVs expressing the empty vector control (Fig.  C). This observation was consistent with the lower levels of proinflammatory factors observed in HUVECs incubated with MenSC-EVs overexpressing miR-574-5p (Fig.  D and E). Furthermore, we administered these different types of EVs to ApoE KO mice to assess the impact of MenSC-EVs with or without miR-574-5p overexpression on atherosclerosis. Analysis of Oil Red O staining clearly revealed that EVs overexpressing miR-574-5p had a more potent inhibitory effect on atherosclerotic plaque formation (P = 0.0132 for the EVs vs. EVs-miR-574-5p group in Fig.  F; P = 0.0098 for EVs vs. EVs-miR-574-5p group in Fig.  G) (Fig.  F and G). Consistent with these findings, the expression of VCAM1 in the aortic root of mice treated with miR-574-5p-overexpressing EVs was lower than that in the aortic root of mice treated with the mock control, as demonstrated by an immunofluorescence assay (Fig.  H). The collective findings presented herein highlight the pivotal role of miR-574-5p as a key regulator in the suppression of inflammatory atherosclerosis mediated by MenSC-EVs.
 To confirm the crucial role of miR-574-5p in the inhibition of endothelial inflammation and atherosclerosis mediated by EVs, we generated miR-574-5p-deficient MenSCs through lentivirus expression of a sponge that specifically depletes miR-574-5p. We subsequently isolated EVs for administration to ApoE−/− mice. Our findings revealed that compared with control EVs, miR-574-5p-null EVs were unable to suppress atherosclerotic plaques as effectively (P = 0.0314 for the EVs vs. EVs-miR-574-5p group in Fig.  A; P = 0.0227 for EVs vs. 574-5p-def-EVs group in Fig.  B) (Fig.  A and B). Immunofluorescence assays consistently demonstrated that MenSC-EVs lacking miR-574-5p suppressed VCAM-1 production in the aortic root (Fig.  C). In addition, our in vitro coincubation experiment of EVs with HUVECs revealed a notable decrease in the inhibition of endothelial inflammation when miR-574-5p was depleted, as evidenced by the monocyte adhesion assay (P = 0.0055 for the EVs vs. 574-5p-def-EVs group) (Fig.  D). Consistent with these findings, MenSC-EVs lacking miR-574-5p showed a diminished ability to suppress the levels of proinflammatory factors in HUVECs compared with EVs derived from MenSCs infected with an empty vector control (Fig.  E and F). On the basis of these observations, we conclude that miR-574-5p is indispensable for MenSC-EV-mediated inhibition of inflammatory atherosclerosis.
 

## miR-574-5p negatively regulates NF-κB pathway activation through posttranscriptional degradation of c-Rel
The functional significance of miR-574-5p in modulating inflammatory atherosclerosis prompted us to investigate its potential molecular mechanism. To achieve this goal, we conducted an experiment in which we overexpressed miR-574-5p or used a mock control in HUVECs to establish a transcriptomic expression profile. Our findings revealed that 75 genes were upregulated, whereas 140 genes were downregulated (Fig.  A). Notably, upon miR-574-5p overexpression, the RNA levels of a specific set of NF-κB target genes significantly decreased (Fig.  B). Furthermore, the results of the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis indicated that the NF-κB pathway was enriched following miR-574-5p overexpression (Fig.  C). To further validate the inhibitory effect of miR-574-5p on the expression of NF-κB downstream targets, a quantitative PCR assay was performed, and the results confirmed our observations (Fig.  D). We additionally conducted a luciferase reporter assay to evaluate the effect of miR-574-5p mimic overexpression on NF-κB transcriptional activity. As anticipated, our results indicated that the enforced overexpression of miR-574-5p significantly reduced luciferase activity driven by the promoters of both ICAM1 and CCL2 (Fig.  E), which are representative NF-κB target genes. Collectively, these observations suggest that miR-574-5p negatively regulates the transcriptional activity of NF-κB.
 To clarify the detailed molecular mechanism by which miR-574-5p regulates NF-κB signaling activation, we used a predictor called “TargetScan” to identify a potential target of miR-574-5p, which was found to be the human c-Rel gene (Fig.  A). To validate our hypothesis, we initially generated a luciferase reporter vector containing the segment of the c-Rel 3′ UTR that is targeted by the miR-574-5p seed sequence. We subsequently conducted luciferase assays using HEK293T cells. Notably, we observed a significant reduction in luciferase activity upon overexpression of miR-574-5p (Fig.  B). In contrast, no significant changes were observed in the luciferase activity of a miR-574-5p-resistant reporter vector, generated by nucleotide mutations in this segment, when subjected to miR-574-5p overexpression (Fig.  B).
 Similarly, increased miR-574-5p mimic expression resulted in a noticeable decrease in c-Rel RNA levels (Fig.  C). Through sequence analysis, we also successfully identified the identical seed region targeted by miR-574-5p within the 3′UTR of the mouse c-Rel gene (Fig.  D). This finding corroborates our previous observations with the human c-Rel seed region luciferase reporter. Notably, the robust expression of miR-574-5p also significantly suppressed luciferase activity in the reporter carrying the wild-type (WT) mouse c-Rel seed region but did not affect the mutant form of the c-Rel seed region (Fig.  E). Importantly, these luciferase assay results are consistent with our quantitative PCR data, which unequivocally demonstrate that the KO of miR-574 profoundly upregulates c-Rel RNA and protein expression levels in MEFs (Fig.  F and G). To further elucidate the inhibitory impact of miR-574-5p on NF-κB signaling via c-Rel, we performed c-Rel siRNA knockdown in miR-574−/− MEFs and analyzed alterations in the expression of NF-κB downstream target genes. As expected, the suppression of c-Rel successfully attenuated the elevated expression levels of NF-κB transcriptional targets induced by miR-574 KO (Fig.  H and ).

## c-Rel feedback regulate the expression of miR-574-5p
Previous studies have shown that miR-574 can be regulated by NF-κB [ , ]. We therefore hypothesized that c-Rel, which is post-transcriptionally modulated by miR-574-5p, could target the promoter of miR-574 and, in turn, provide feedback regulation of its expression. To test this hypothesis, we first performed a chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay to determine whether miR-574 is a transcriptional target of c-Rel. Our results indicated that c-Rel can bind to the promoter region of miR-574 (Additional file 1: Fig. S3A). Quantitative PCR analyses demonstrated that the knockdown of c-Rel significantly reduced the RNA levels of miR-574-5p (Additional file 1: Fig. S3B). To further validate the transcriptional regulation of miR-574 by c-Rel, we constructed a luciferase reporter driven by the miR-574 promoter (− 2000 bp to + 96 bp) and co-transfected it with siRNAs targeting c-Rel into HEK293T cells to assess the impact of c-Rel depletion on luciferase activity. Our data showed that the disruption of c-Rel markedly suppressed the luciferase activity driven by the miR-574 promoter (Additional file 1: Fig. S3C). Together, these observations suggest a negative feedback regulatory mechanism between miR-574-5p and c-Rel expression.

## The miR-574-5p secreted by MenSC-EVs increases the production of nitric oxide (NO) by downregulating the expression of c-Rel
As a widely recognized atheroprotective regulator, endothelial nitric oxide (NO) produced by endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) promotes vascular smooth muscle relaxation and inhibits platelet aggregation and adhesion [ , ]. Moreover, previous studies have demonstrated that the NF-κB cascade plays a crucial role in regulating the expression levels of eNOS [ , ]. Based on this, we hypothesized that the miR-574-5p secreted by MenSC-EVs may modulate the production of NO catalyzed by eNOS. To test this hypothesis, we first assessed the effect of MenSC-EVs with and without miR-574-5p depletion on the levels of eNOS. Our results indicated that MenSC-EVs significantly enhanced the expression of eNOS (Fig.  A). However, the depletion of miR-574-5p in MenSC-EVs attenuated the elevated levels of eNOS (Fig.  A), suggesting that miR-574-5p plays a role in modulating eNOS expression. Consistent with this, the overexpression of a miR-574-5p mimic significantly elevated both the RNA and protein levels of eNOS (Fig.  B). This increase was also observed following c-Rel knockdown in HUVECs (Fig.  C). Furthermore, we investigated the impact of c-Rel disruption on the decreased eNOS levels resulting from miR-574 knockout. Our results indicated that c-Rel knockdown restored the reduced protein levels of eNOS associated with miR-574 knockout in MEFs (Fig.  D). We subsequently measured the effects of miR-574-5p and c-Rel on NO production using a NO-specific fluorescent dye (3-amino, 4-aminomethyl-2′, 7′-difluorescein, diacetate, DAF-FM-DA). In line with the observed eNOS expression pattern, both the overexpression of the miR-574-5p mimic and the knockdown of c-Rel significantly enhanced NO production (P = 0.0314 for the EVs vs. EVs-miR-574-5p group in Fig.  E; P = 0.0227 for EVs vs. 574-5p-def-EVs group in Fig.  F) (Fig.  E and F). These findings collectively indicate that the miR-574-5p secreted by MenSCs promotes eNOS-mediated NO production by downregulating c-Rel.
 

## c-Rel is essential for miR-574-5p-mediated atherosclerosis, and their expression levels are correlated with clinical atherosclerosis
To determine whether c-Rel is necessary for the enhanced atherosclerotic plaques resulting from miR-574 knockout, we utilized adeno-associated virus (AAV) infection to deplete c-Rel. We introduced an AAV carrying c-Rel shRNA into the aortas of miR-574 and ApoE double knockout mice via tail-vein injection to assess the impact of c-Rel disruption on atherosclerotic plaque formation. As anticipated, the reduction of c-Rel significantly inhibited the advanced atherosclerosis caused by miR-574 knockout (P = 0.0017 for the AAV-shNC miR-574−/−&ApoE−/− vs. AAV-shc-Rel miR-574−/−&ApoE−/− group in Fig.  A; P = 0.0011 for the AAV-shNC miR-574−/−&ApoE−/− vs. AAV-shc-Rel miR-574−/−&ApoE−/− group in Fig.  B) (Fig.  A and B). Additionally, immunofluorescence assays revealed a decrease in VCAM-1 levels and an increase in eNOS levels in the aortic root following the disruption of c-Rel (Additional file 1: Fig. S4A–C). In summary, these findings provide strong evidence that the enhanced atherosclerotic plaques associated with miR-574 knockout are heavily dependent on c-Rel.
 We subsequently evaluated the clinical relevance of miR-574-5p and c-Rel in two patients with coronary artery atherosclerosis, using a normal great saphenous vein as a negative control. Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) coupled with immunostaining analyses indicated that, compared to the normal control, the two patients with atherosclerotic lesions exhibited a marked decrease in the levels of miR-574-5p, accompanied by a significant increase in the levels of both c-Rel and VCAM-1 (Fig.  C). This suggests a potential relevance of the miR-574-5p/c-Rel axis in the progression of clinical atherosclerosis.

## Discussion
Numerous studies have consistently demonstrated that inflammation plays a pivotal role in the initiation and progression of atherosclerotic plaque formation, making it a critical risk factor. Consequently, anti-inflammatory therapy has emerged as a significant approach in the clinical treatment of atherosclerosis. In this groundbreaking study, we identified MenSC-secreted EVs as highly promising candidates for potential clinical application in the treatment of atherosclerosis. Additionally, this study provides compelling evidence supporting the vital role of EVs as crucial mediators in the intercellular communication of MSCs, allowing them to carry out various regulatory functions. These findings highlight the importance of EVs as key messengers utilized by MSCs to facilitate efficient signal transmission.
Despite previous studies exploring the beneficial effects of MenSCs in various inflammatory disorders [ , – ], the molecular mechanisms underlying these effects need to be better understood. To address this gap in knowledge, we conducted a comprehensive small RNA screening approach aimed at identifying the functional miRNAs involved in MenSC-EV-mediated suppression of inflammatory atherosclerosis. Our findings were surprising, as we identified miR-574-5p as a crucial regulator of this biological process. We observed that the KO of miR-574-5p significantly enhanced the inflammatory response and increased the formation of atherosclerotic plaques. Notably, when MenSC-EVs lacking miR-574-5p were used, their suppressive effect on inflammatory atherosclerosis was markedly reduced.
Furthermore, we discovered that MenSC-EVs carrying more miR-574-5p exhibited a stronger inhibitory effect on endothelial inflammation and advanced atherosclerosis. These results highlight the essential role of miR-574-5p in the therapeutic potential of MenSC-EVs in the context of inflammatory atherosclerosis. miR-574-5p, a highly conserved miRNA across multiple mammalian species, plays a significant role in various pathological processes, such as insulin resistance, tumorigenesis, and cardiac remodeling [ – ]. However, limited evidence exists regarding the association between miR-574-5p and the inflammatory response. This study aims to provide substantial evidence elucidating the critical role of miR-574-5p as a repressor in the progression of endothelial inflammation. Our findings demonstrate that both genetic disruption of miR-574 and in vitro inhibition of miR-574-5p via synthetic oligonucleotides lead to a noticeable increase in the expression of proinflammatory factors, thereby significantly contributing to the endothelial inflammatory response. Although a previous study reported that miR-574-5p could target human c-Rel for post-transcriptional degradation [ ], it remains unclear whether c-Rel is functionally required for miR-574-5p-mediated inflammatory atherosclerosis. Our luciferase analysis provided compelling evidence demonstrating that miR-574-5p targets both human and mouse c-Rel, which was further substantiated by the significant increase in c-Rel RNA levels following miR-574 knockout. Additionally, our study revealed that the knockdown of c-Rel effectively blocked the upregulation of NF-κB-regulated pro-inflammatory factors induced by miR-574-5p knockout. As is well established, NF-κB negatively regulates the activity of eNOS [ , ], a widely accepted atheroprotective regulator. We therefore investigated whether miR-574-5p secreted by MenSC-EVs is involved in the regulation of endothelial NO production. Our results demonstrated that both the incubation of endothelial cells with MenSC-EVs and the overexpression of miR-574-5p significantly increased the levels of eNOS and NO. Functionally, studies using an atherosclerosis mouse model indicated that AAV-mediated disruption of c-Rel significantly reduced atherosclerotic plaque formation associated with miR-574 knockout, while also decreasing levels of adhesion molecules and enhancing eNOS production. Notably, both miR-574-5p and c-Rel were found to have significant clinical relevance in advanced atherosclerosis, with miR-574-5p levels decreased and c-Rel levels increased. While previous studies have reported elevated miR-574-5p levels in plasma samples from patients with coronary artery disease [ , ], these plasma levels may reflect a more complex pathological disorder. In contrast, our study specifically examines the levels of miR-574-5p in the inner layer of blood vessels, a region widely recognized as a key contributor to atherosclerosis due to endothelial dysfunction. These findings highlight the critical role of the miR-574-5p/c-Rel/NF-κB axis in the progression of inflammatory atherosclerosis.
Although the MenSC-EVs demonstrate beneficial effects on improving inflammatory atherosclerosis in this study, we acknowledge several limitations. One limitation of this study is that the ultracentrifugation-based isolation method may disrupt their structure due to the applied centrifugal force. This disruption could adversely affect their characteristics in treating inflammatory atherosclerosis. Additionally, ultracentrifugation is prone to introducing co-precipitation impurities, which may interfere with the therapeutic effect. Therefore, further optimization of the extraction strategy for MenSC-EVs will be a key focus of future research. Moreover, the small clinical sample size poses another limitation, as it stems from the challenges associated with collecting blood vessels from patients with atherosclerosis in clinical practice. Thus, the clinical relevance of miR-574-5p, c-Rel, and other members of the NF-κB subunit family in inflammatory atherosclerosis, as well as their roles in the observed effects, warrant further in-depth investigation in future studies.

## Conclusions
This study provides compelling evidence for the inhibitory effects of MenSC-secreted EVs on inflammatory atherosclerosis. Using a small RNA screening approach, we identified miR-574-5p as a key mediator in the MenSC-EV-induced suppression of inflammatory atherosclerosis. Mechanistically, our investigations suggest that miR-574-5p directly targets NF-κB/c-Rel for transcriptional degradation. MenSC-EV-derived miR-574-5p significantly restrains the NF-κB/c-Rel-mediated inflammatory response while promoting eNOS-mediated NO production through the downregulation of c-Rel. This dual action effectively suppresses endothelial inflammation and advanced atherosclerosis. Notably, our clinical studies reveal a significant correlation between the levels of miR-574-5p and c-Rel and the progression of clinical atherosclerosis, suggesting a potential cell-based therapeutic strategy for treating inflammatory atherosclerosis. In summary, our study offers new perspectives on the efficacy of MenSC-EVs in attenuating inflammatory atherosclerosis in mice through the inhibition of the miR-574-5p-mediated c-Rel/NF-κB pathway (Fig.  ). Based on these findings, we anticipate that future research will further investigate the therapeutic potential of stem cells in clinical settings.
