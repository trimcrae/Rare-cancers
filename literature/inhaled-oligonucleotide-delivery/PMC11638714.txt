## Abstract
Understanding the molecular interactions within the neuromuscular apparatus in the
stomach is crucial for understanding their role in maintaining interstitial cells, such as
the interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC), smooth muscle, and enteric neurons, as well as
their contribution to gastric motility in both healthy and diseased states. Disruptions of
these systems can lead to various gastric motor disorders and diseases, making it
essential to explore their functions in detail. We herein present a protocol for gene
knockdown using small interfering RNA (siRNAs) in organotypic culture. This ex
vivo approach allows the precise manipulation of the gene expression in a
tissue environment that closely mimics in vivo conditions, providing
valuable insights into the gene function and its effects on gastric physiology. The
protocol includes detailed steps for tissue preparation to ensure the preservation of the
gastric muscles and the associated neuromuscular apparatus. We then describe the process
of siRNA-mediated gene knockdown, offering tips for optimizing transfection efficiency and
gene silencing. Additionally, we outline methods for analyzing the effectiveness of
knockdown, including both quantitative and qualitative methods for the evaluation of the
target gene expression. This protocol is adaptable to various research needs, allowing
researchers to focus on specific genes of interest within the neuromuscular system of the
stomach. By applying this approach, investigators can deepen their understanding of the
molecular mechanisms underlying gastric motility and contribute to the development of new
therapeutic strategies for treating gastric motor disorders and diseases.
Keywords: small interfering RNA, stomach, knockdown

## Introduction
The gastric muscle layers contain multiple components, such as smooth muscle cells, enteric
neurons, immune cells, interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC), and platelet-derived growth factor
receptor alpha (PDGFRA)-positive cells, which were previously referred to as
“fibroblast-like” cells ( , , ). When used in combination with
gene knockdown techniques, gastric muscles provide valuable insights into the complex
relationships between specific molecules, signaling pathways, and cellular functions that
are difficult to study directly in vivo.
Organotypic cultures of gastric muscles are ex vivo (outside the organism)
models that maintain the structural and functional characteristics of the original tissue
( , ). In
these cultures, sections of gastric muscle tissue, including smooth muscle layers, enteric
neurons, and ICC, are kept alive and functional in a controlled laboratory environment. This
allows researchers to study the physiology, cellular interactions, and responses of gastric
muscle tissue under conditions that closely mimic the in vivo (within the
living organism) environment without the complexities of the entire organism ( ).
In our previous study, we investigated the physiological roles of ICC-related genes,
particularly KIT, ETV1, and PDGFRA, in gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs), the
oncogenic counterparts of ICC, and diabetes-related gastric motor dysfunctions ( , , , , ). By knocking down or pharmacologically inhibiting these
genes in organotypic cultures, we explored their pathophysiological effects ( , ). This article
provides a detailed outline of our gene knockdown protocol, which is highly versatile and
applicable to any gene, thus enabling the analysis of various cellular phenotypes.

## Preparation of silicon-based dishes
Timing: 24 h
Note: Prepare the dishes at least one day prior to use. One container of Sylgard 184
should make approximately 100 dishes of 35 mm in diameter.
a. Pour a small container of the Silicon Elastomer Curing Agent into the Silicon
Elastomer Base. Mix thoroughly using a stirrer for at least 10 min.
b. Pour the mixture into 35-mm dishes.
c. Place the 35-mm dishes in a cell culture hood and allow them to cure overnight,
ensuring that Sylgard 184 solidifies completely.
d. Sterilize the dishes by spraying with 100% ethanol in a cell culture hood. After
ethanol has dried in a cell culture hood, store the dishes at room temperature.

## Preparation of small interfering RNAs (siRNAs)
Timing: 1 h
a. Choose predesigned siRNAs.
Select pre-designed siRNAs from commercial sources such as Horizon Discovery, Thermo
Fischer Scientific, and Qiagen.
b. Dissolve powdered siRNA.
Dissolve the powdered siRNA in Nuclease-Free Water to prepare a 50 µM stock solution and
store it at −20°C.
c. Dilute stock solution.
Dilute the stock solution to a final concentration of 5 µM with sterile RNase-free
water.
Notes: Before adding RNase-free water to the siRNA stock, spin down the tube for at least
1 min to ensure that all the powder settles at the bottom. Avoid immediate pipetting after
adding water to the siRNA powder, as it can easily adhere to the pipette tips. Instead,
gently add RNase-free water and leave the tube for 10 min with slight agitation on ice or
at 4°C to ensure uniform hydration. Once hydrated, vortex thoroughly to ensure complete
dissolution of the siRNA.

## Step-by-Step Method Details

## Peeling gastric mucosa to obtain muscle layers
Timing: 2 h
a. C57BL/6J mice (age: 10–12 days) were anesthetized with carbon dioxide (CO2)
inhalation for at least 3 min and euthanized by decapitation.
b. Use forceps to carefully remove the mucosa to obtain muscle layers in silicon-based
dishes containing ice-cold Krebs solution.
Note: Change the Krebs solution every 5 min to ensure better tissue viability.
c. Place peeled gastric muscles in M199 medium containing 1% antibiotic-antimycotic for
at least 1 h at 37°C.
d. Pin out tissue.
Note: Ensure the tissue is pinned securely during this process. Avoiding tissue
stretching, which may cause tearing.
e. Wash tissues at least 7 times with 2 ml of culture media. The culture medium consists
of M199 medium supplemented with 1% gentamycin and 1% l-glutamine. After washing, place
the tissues in an incubator set to 37°C.
Note: The organotypic culture of gastric muscle is self-sustaining, with ICC surviving
for over 50 days ( , ). The M199 medium has a simple composition, allowing for more precise
manipulation of specific nutrient effects and pharmacological interventions ( , ). If an
alternative medium is required for specific experimental conditions, culture parameters
should be adjusted appropriately.

## siRNA transfection
Timing: 1 h
1. Transfection of siRNA (Day 1)
This protocol outlines the steps for siRNA transfection using Lipofectamine Dharmafect 4.
Please refer to the manufacturer’s instructions for additional details. This protocol is
specifically designed for the 5-dish (35 mm) format.
a. Add 20 µL of Dharmafect reagent to 980 µL of M199 medium in a 10 mL Falcon tube. In a
separate container, add 50 µL of siRNA solution into 950 µL of M199 medium. Wait for 5 min
at room temperature.
b. Combine the solutions from step a, gently vortex and incubate at room temperature for
20 min.
c. Combine the mixture with 8 ml complete medium. The final concentration of the siRNA of
interest or the corresponding scrambled sequence (Scr) is 25 nM. Discard the existing
media from the culture dishes and add 2 mL of the combined solution to the 35-mm
dishes.
Note: Adjust the volumes accordingly when using different well formats based on the
bottom area of the well. When transfecting multiple wells or using different siRNAs
simultaneously, prepare appropriate volumes.
d. After 24 h, change the medium. Repeat the medium change after 48 h. At 72 h, harvest
the tissues and verify the efficacy of siRNA knockdown using western blotting.

## Expected outcomes
Following siRNA treatment, the transfected gastric muscle tissues are suitable for
downstream analyses. These include immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, quantitative
polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), and immunoblotting, as shown in . As an example of this protocol, shown in , siRNA-mediated knockdown of succinate dehydrogenase complex subunit
B (Sdhb) using constructs J-042339-09 (#9) and J-042339-12
(#12) resulted in a significant reduction in the expression of SDHB protein relative to
the scrambled sequences (Scr) in organotypic gastric muscle cultures ( ).
 

## Limitations
There are several limitations to consider when using gastric muscles obtained through
this protocol. These muscles may exhibit variations in their gene expression and
morphology relative to in vivo conditions. Therefore, the outcomes should
be cautiously interpreted.
While this protocol is optimized for juvenile mice (age: 10–12 days), there may be
instances, such as in aging research, where gastric muscles from aged or progeric mice are
required ( ). It is important to note that siRNA
transfection in the gastric muscle of adult mice can be challenging because of the lower
transfection efficacy, which is often attributed to the increased thickness of the muscle
tissue. While siRNA-mediated knockdown is generally effective in these systems, it may
sometimes result in lower knockdown efficacy relative to that observed in cell lines or
primary cultures. Therefore, it is advisable to verify the knockdown efficiency throughout
the experiment.
Despite these limitations, this ex vivo culture system remains a
valuable tool for studying the molecular mechanisms that are difficult to investigate
in vivo.

## Troubleshooting
Problem 1
Low knockdown efficiency.
Potential solution
Ensure that no antibiotic-antimycotic agent, other than gentamycin, is present in the
culture media.
Confirm that the gastric muscles remain intact throughout the experiment.
Try alternative siRNA sequences.
Problem 2
Contamination in culture dishes
Potential solution
Thoroughly wash peeled muscles before placing them into the media.
Increase the frequency of washing with culture media to reduce the risk of
contamination.
