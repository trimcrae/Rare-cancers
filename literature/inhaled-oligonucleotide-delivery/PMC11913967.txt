## Abstract
Plasticizers, particularly phthalates, are widely used to enhance the properties of plastics, yet their harmful effects on human health, especially reproductive health, have raised concerns. This has led governments to promote the use of non-phthalate substitutes. Preterm premature rupture of fetal membranes (PPROM), affecting 3–4% of pregnancies and contributing to 40–50% of preterm births worldwide, has been associated with traditional phthalate exposure. However, no clear link has yet been established between alternative plasticizers and preterm births. Among these substitutes, DINCH (used as a phthalate replacement) and its metabolite, MINCH, appear to be promising options, although their health impacts remain largely unexplored. This study aims to assess the potential effects of DINCH and MINCH on the physiology of fetal membranes, focusing on the nuclear receptor PPARγ, which plays a critical role in pregnancy maintenance. An amniotic epithelial cell model was used to evaluate DINCH and MINCH influence on cytotoxicity, cell viability, and PPARγ activity, including its anti-inflammatory properties. Both exhibited no cytotoxic effects, did not alter PPARγ expression, and did not affect its anti-inflammatory properties. These findings suggest that DINCH and MINCH could serve as safe alternatives to phthalates, potentially reducing the risk of weakening and premature rupture of fetal membranes.
Keywords: Non-phthalate plasticizer, Preterm pre-labor rupture of membranes (PPROM), PPARγ, Fetal membranes

## Introduction
Plasticizers are commonly used in a wide range of products (food packaging, medical devices, self-care products) to provide durability, flexibility, and longevity to polymers (Rahman and Brazel ). The best-known additives in the plastic industry are phthalate esters, constituting 92% of the market (Tan et al. ). This extensive use has led to the ubiquitous presence of phthalate esters and the contamination of the environment, thereby negatively affecting wildlife and humans, because these esters easily leach into the environment (Erythropel et al. ). Consequently, humans are exposed to phthalates through ingestion, inhalation, and dermal absorption (Giovanoulis et al. ); these compounds have been detected in the form of metabolites in urine, blood, and amniotic fluid. Several studies have demonstrated various toxic effects of phthalate esters on human health, including cardiotoxicity (Valvi et al. ), neurotoxicity (Holahan and Smith ), as well as endocrine and reproductive toxicity (Radke et al. ). With regard to reproductive toxicity, epidemiological studies suggest that phthalate exposure during pregnancy is associated with increased risks of pregnancy loss, preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, and preterm birth (Cantonwine et al. ; Ferguson et al. ; Boss et al. ; Warner et al. ).
Fetal membranes maintain pregnancy and promote parturition at term by, for example, triggering sterile inflammation as a physiological phenomenon (Challis et al. ). In pathological cases, preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM) refers to the rupture of fetal membranes before 37 weeks of gestation, thereby affecting approximately 3%–4% of pregnancies and contributing to 40%–50% of preterm births (Xu et al. ). Moreover, it remains the leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide (Galaz et al. ). The main causes of PPROM include amniotic fluid infection (bacterial colonization), mechanical amniotic stretch, increased oxidative stress, and early inflammation of the amnio-chorionic membranes (Challis et al. ; Joyce et al. ; Stout et al. ). Early inflammation of the amnio-chorionic membranes leads to the weakening of fetal membranes, which result from exacerbated production of hormones, pro-inflammatory cytokines, and proteolytic enzymes (Gomez et al. ); this could be due to the presence of phthalate pollutants in amniotic fluid that come into direct contact with the amniotic epithelium (Silva et al. ; Jensen et al. ).
To function as pejorative actors, it is now well known that phthalates could negatively act by deregulating the action of nuclear receptors (for example, the androgen, estrogen, and thyroid receptors). Apart from these, the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are ligand-activated transcription factors that activate the transcription of target genes as heterodimers with retinoid X receptors (RXR). Their binding to specific response elements (PPRE) located in the 5’ end region of target genes permits the control of their expression (Palmer et al. ). All three PPAR isoforms are expressed in the placenta and fetal membranes (Fournier et al. ; Antoine et al. ). PPARγ has already been shown to have anti-inflammatory actions (Lappas et al. ) and may indirectly modulate the activation of matrix metalloproteinases that degrade the extracellular matrix (Chuang et al. ). In fetal membranes, PPARγ expression is reduced as term approaches (Dunn-Albanese et al. ), thereby suggesting that the lowering of its action at the end of pregnancy could possibly be one of the direct causes of a sterile inflammation that would lead to the weakening of physiological fetal membranes. As already demonstrated by (Shoaito et al. ), in the placenta, MEHP, the active metabolite of DEHP (a widely used phthalate) can negatively impact PPARγ signaling pathways. Furthermore, such a substance is also demonstrated to reduce PPARγ anti-inflammatory properties in an amniotic cell line (Antoine et al. ), which could be considered a piece of the puzzle in understanding the weakening and potential rupture of preterm fetal membranes.
Based on multiple reported toxicities, several phthalates have been regulated and their use in various products has been banned worldwide (Smith ; CPSIA, ). Growing demand for safer and more eco-friendly plasticizers has driven the industry to explore and develop non-phthalate options. Numerous alternative plasticizers are now available on the market such as di(isononyl)cyclohexane-1,2-dicarboxylate (DINCH). However, limited information is available on the potential health impacts of DINCH, particularly on reproduction, although it is considered to have more favorable toxicological properties. Currently, DINCH metabolites are detectable in human urine (Völkel et al. ), and have been found in over 95% of samples from French pregnant women (Philippat et al. ); moreover, these metabolites have also been found in amniotic fluid (personal unpublished data).
Like MEHP, the DINCH metabolite—1,2-cyclohexanedicarboxylic acid mono-4-methyloctyl ester (MINCH)—is already demonstrated to interfere with nuclear receptors such as estrogen (ERα and ERβ), androgen, and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARα and PPARγ) (Engel et al. ). Furthermore, with regard to the importance of PPARγ in the integrity of fetal membranes, it is important to note that structural similarities between endogenous PPARγ ligands and these plasticizers may explain the results of in vitro studies, which have demonstrated that MINCH (> 50 µM) activates PPARγ and could modify the expression of several of its target genes (Campioli et al. ; Engel et al. ; Schaffert et al. ). Thus, as has been done for phthalates, the aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of the new non-phthalate plasticizer DINCH and its active metabolite MINCH on the AV3 amniocyte cell line by focusing on their impact on the PPARγ nuclear receptor pathway. Specifically, we aim to determine whether the use of DINCH and MINCH could lead to the deregulation of PPARγ’s anti-inflammatory properties, potentially triggering an inflammatory cascade and the onset of PPROM or whether it will be harmless for such cellular phenomena.

## Materials and methods

## In vitro culture
The AV3 amniotic epithelial cell line (CCL-21, obtained from ATCC) was cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2, 95% humidified air) for 24 h or 48 h in DMEM-F12-GlutaMAX™ (21,041,025, Gibco™, Fisher Scientific, Illkirch-Graffenstaden, France). The medium was supplemented with 10% charcoal-stripped fetal calf serum (S181F-500, BioWest, MC2, Aubière, France), antibiotics (10.000 UI/ml ampicillin, 10 mg/ml streptomycin, and 0.025 mg/ml amphotericin B) (CABPSA00-0U, Eurobio Scientific, Fisher Scientific, Illkirch-Graffenstaden, France).

## Treatments with alternative plasticizers
DINCH (CAS no. 166412–78-8, technical grade purity, TRC, Toronto, ON, Canada) and MINCH (product no. C987305, 95% purity, TRC, Toronto, ON, Canada) were dissolved in dimethylsulfoxyde (DMSO) (D2650-5, Sigma-Aldrich, Darmstadt, Germany). The PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone (AGO) (ab120762) and the PPARγ antagonist GW9662 (ANT) (ab141125) were acquired from Abcam (Paris, France) and dissolved in DMSO. The AV3 cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 3 × 105 cells/well. The following day, cells were pretreated with 0.2% DMSO (control)—for DINCH at concentrations of 0.1, 1, or 10 µM, MINCH at concentrations of 2.5, 10, or 100 µM, and with the PPARγ antagonist GW9662 at 1 µM. Three hours later, rosiglitazone was added at 1 µM.

## Cytotoxicity and cell viability assay
Cytotoxicity was assessed through the spectrophotometric determination of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity in the culture medium at 24- and 48-h post-treatment. LDH activity was quantified at 340 nm using an automated analyzer (Atellica®Solution, Siemens Healthineers, Paris, France) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. To test the cell viability, cells were seeded at a density of 104 cells/well in 96-well plates. The impact of plasticizers on cell viability was evaluated using the Cell-counting Kit-8 (WST-8/CCK8) assay (ab228554, Abcam, Paris, France) at 24- and 48-h post-treatment in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. The amount of formazan produced is directly proportional to the number of living cells and is measured by absorbance at 460 nm after 4 h incubation (37 °C, 5% CO2, 95% humidified air).

## Real time PCR
RNA extraction was performed with NucleoSpin® RNA (740,955.250, Macherey–Nagel, Düren, Germany) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The quantity of RNA was spectrophometrically determined at 260 nm (DeNovix® DS-11FX, Denovix Inc., USA). Retrotranscription was performed using 1 µg of total RNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (4,374,967, Fisher Scientific, France) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Subsequently, quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using LightCycler® 480 SYBR® Green I Master (04887352001, Roche, Mannheim, Germany) and specific oligonucleotides (Table  ). The following program was applied on the LightCycler® 480 Instrument II: 5 min at 95 °C, followed by 45 amplification cycles for 10 s at 95 °C, 10 s at the annealing temperature specific to each primer pair (61 °C), and 15 s at 72 °C. Thereafter, transcript quantification was conducted utilizing standard curves to determine the quantity of amplified transcripts. The results obtained were standardized to the geometric mean of the human housekeeping genes RPLP0 and RPS17, following the recommendations of the MIQE guidelines (Bustin et al. ).

 

## Protein immunoblotting
Extraction of total proteins for cells was conducted using RIPA buffer. Subsequently, samples were separated (35 μg of total protein per well) on 4%–15% Mini-PROTEAN TGX Stain Free Gels (4,568,086, BIO-RAD, Marnes-la-Coquette, France). Proteins were then transferred on to nitrocellulose membranes, and non-specific binding sites were blocked for 45 min with Tris-buffered saline (TBS) containing 5% nonfat dry milk (blocking solution). Membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a 1/750e dilution of PPARy antibody (ab209350, Abcam, Paris, France). After rinsing three times with TBS containing 0.1% Tween 20, membranes were incubated for 1.5 h at room temperature, with a 1/10.000e dilution of horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody in a hybridization solution (BI 2407, Abliance, Compiègne, France). Following three wash cycles in TBS-0.1% Tween 20, peroxidase activity on the membranes was detected using enhanced chemiluminescence (Clarity Max™ Western ECL Substrate, BIO-RAD) and integrated on the ChemiDoc™ MP imaging system (BIO-RAD). Further, the relative intensities of protein bands were analyzed using Image Lab 6.1 software (BIO-RAD). Total protein normalization (Bio-Rad), a method allowing normalization using the total protein loaded, was used to analyze the results of the Western blot analysis.

## PPARγ luciferase assay
The methodology applied for this experiment follows the approach described in our previous study (Antoine et al. ).

## The measurement of cytokine concentrations
The AV3 cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 3 × 105 cells/well. The following day, cells were pretreated: 0.2% DMSO (control); DINCH at concentrations of 0.1, 1, or 10 µM; MINCH at concentrations of 2.5, 10, or 100 µM or with the GW9662 at 1 µM overnight. The next day, the culture medium was refreshed and cells were pretreated similarly to how it was done previously: 0.2% DMSO (control); DINCH at concentrations of 0.1, 1, and 10 µM/ml; and MINCH at concentrations of 2.5, 10, or 100 µM/ml. Three hours later, AGO at 1 µM/ml and cytomix (mix of TNF (15 ng/ml) and IL1-β (15 ng/ml)) were added for 24- and 48-h (the treatment medium is supposed to be renewed every 24 h). The mRNA levels of inflammatory markers IL6 and IL8 were quantified using qPCR as previously described. The quantification of IL6 and IL8 protein release in the culture media following 24- or 48-h of treatment was performed through automated multiplex immunoassays using ELLA™ (Bio-techne, San Jose, CA, USA). The total protein concentration was determined utilizing a Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit (23,227, Fisher Scientific). Cytokine concentrations underwent normalization relative to the total protein concentration.

## Data analysis
Data were presented as median ± 95% confidence interval (CI) and the number of performed independent experiments is indicated in each figure legend. Due to deviations from the normal distribution, nonparametric tests were employed. Initially, a one-way ANOVA Kruskal–Wallis test was conducted for comprehensive comparisons among groups. Subsequently, multiple comparisons with Dunn’s correction were performed for comparisons among more than two groups. Statistical analysis was executed using PRISM software 5.02 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Significance levels were set at p < 0.05 (*) and p < 0.01 (**).

## Results

## Effect of DINCH and MINCH on AV3 cytotoxicity and viability
To study the effects of DINCH and its metabolite MINCH on cell viability and cytotoxicity, epithelial amniotic AV3 cells were exposed to different concentrations of DINCH (μM) (0.1, 1, or 10 μM/ml) and MINCH (2.5, 10, or 100 μM/ml) for 24 h or 48 h. The results reveal no significant difference between control (DMSO 0.2% in cell media), DINCH, or MINCH regarding cytotoxicity in terms of dose and/or time of treatment (Fig.   left and right panels, respectively). In addition, similar results were observed after treatments with rosiglitazone (AGO) alone, or rosiglitazone combined with DINCH/MINCH (Fig.  ). Furthermore, our results also demonstrate that DINCH has no effect on AV3 cell viability, regardless of the time and/or dose of treatment (Fig.  , left and right panels). Similar results were observed after treatments with rosiglitazone alone (as expected) or a combination of both. The results for MINCH are identical to those for DINCH, with the exception of the highest concentration in combination with rosiglitazone, which is a significant decrease in cell viability after 24 h or 48 h of treatment.
 

## Effects of DINCH and MINCH on PPARγ expression
To evaluate a possible direct or indirect interference of DINCH and MINCH on PPARγ cellular action, we first investigate the potential action of these molecules on PPARγ mRNA and protein expression. Here, DINCH (0.1, 1, or 10 μM/ml), MINCH (2.5, 10, or 100 μM/ml), or rosiglitazone at an exposure of 1 µM (for 24 h or 48 h) have no effect on PPARγ transcripts (Fig.  ) or protein levels in AV3 cells (Fig.  A and B). The same results were observed when combinations of the treatment were utilized (rosiglitazone with DINCH or MINCH).
 

## Impact of DINCH and MINCH on PPARγ transcriptional activity in AV3 cells
We investigated the effect of DINCH and MINCH on PPARγ transcriptional activity using a luciferase gene reporter assay. First, to validate this test, we treated AV3 cells by rosiglitazone and/or GW9662 at 1 μM for 24 h (Fig.  , left and right panels). As expected, rosiglitazone (AGO) induced an increase in the transcriptional activity of PPARγ (two-fold change) (p = 0.0015), and this activity was significantly inhibited by GW9662 (a PPARγ antagonist -ANT) (p = 0.0017) after 24 h of treatment. In contrast, DINCH treatments alone (Fig.  , left panel), regardless of the dose used, had no effect on luciferase expression. Then, we wanted to know whether DINCH could affect the action of this receptor when activated by its agonist. The combined treatment of DINCH and rosiglitazone were found not to suppress the transcriptional activity induced by the PPARγ agonist. In the same sense, MINCH treatments alone (Fig.  , right panel), regardless of the dose used, had no effect on luciferase expression at lower concentrations but tended to increase slightly at the highest concentration used (100 μM/ml). Further, combined treatments with MINCH and rosiglitazone were found not to not suppress the transcriptional activity induced by the agonist and did not show a cumulative effect with rosiglitazone.
 

## The influence of DINCH and MINCH on the anti-inflammatory activity of PPARγ
The PPARγ signalling cascade has an anti-inflammatory effect by reducing the expression and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Vázquez-Carrera and Wahli ). To test a possible influence of DINCH and MINCH on the anti-inflammatory effect of this receptor, AV3 cells were incubated with cytomix to induce an inflammatory status and with different treatment combinations (including agonist alone (AGO), DINCH or MINCH alone, or a combination of both). The mRNAs (Fig.  ) and protein expressions (Fig.  ) of well-known inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 and IL-8) were analyzed using RT-qPCR and Ella™ assay after 24 h or 48 h of treatment and compared to the AGO + cytomix condition. It was found that DINCH and MINCH alone have no impact on the inflammatory status of AV3 cells. In addition, no inhibitory, additive, or synergistic effects of alternative plasticizers were found on IL6 and IL8 expression compared to rosiglitazone alone.
 

## Discussion
Plastics are ubiquitous in daily life and their impact on health is being well documented and closely monitored by governmental agencies. Plastic additives such as phthalates (i.e., di(2-ethylhexyl)phthalate (DEHP)) have been extensively studied, thereby leading to the prohibition of certain products used (selfcare products, food packaging) by sensitive populations (children, intensive care patients, pregnant women) due to, for example, their reprotoxic effects (Meeker et al. ; Howdeshell et al. ). Numerous studies have reported an increased risk of preterm birth or shorter gestational duration associated with pregnant women’s exposure to phthalates (Meeker et al. ; Ferguson et al. ; Boss et al. ; Gao et al. ; Santos et al. ). Consequently, manufacturers have sought to replace them with safer products such as long-chain phthalates or alternative plasticizers (Albert et al. ; LCSP, ). Since its introduction as a phthalate substitute (Schütze et al. ), DINCH has been widely used (Koch et al. ), and data obtained from an animal model has suggested that it has no exhibited toxicological effects (Bui et al. ). Nevertheless, the biological effects of this new plasticizer remains poorly understood in humans due to its novelty and partial epidemiological data. Indeed, studies in this regard are limited and most of our knowledge comes directly from manufacturers’ tests (Bhat et al. ; Langsch et al. ). DINCH itself has no significant effect, but a few of its metabolites—such as MINCH—are capable of acting on human nuclear receptors (Engel et al. ). Of course, since its introduction in various commonly used products, and as for phthalates, the presence of DINCH and its metabolites in various human fluids has been detected in an increasing quantity (Philippat et al. ); however, no studies thus far report this presence in the amniotic fluid of pregnant women (even despite the unpublished personal data that indicates its presence). However, studies have assessed the association between maternal exposure to DINCH during pregnancy and preterm birth (Rodríguez-Carmona et al. ; Yland et al. ). Nevertheless, as for other pollutants, their presence is beyond doubt and could also be a cause of pregnancy complications, such as PPROM, as a consequence of the weakening of the fetal membranes. As a starting point regarding the possible effects of new plasticizers, with regard to the integrity of fetal membranes, our study precisely examined the effects of an acute exposure of amniotic cells to both DINCH and MINCH in terms of cellular viability and their influence on the transcripts, proteins, and anti-inflammatory activities of the nuclear receptor PPARγ.

## Impact of DINCH and MINCH on fetal membranes
Our findings indicate that DINCH and MINCH do not exhibit cytotoxic effects on amniotic epithelial cells (AV3), as evidenced by cell viability and LDH release assays. Equivalent results have already been obtained from other human cell lines, such as HepG2 and HEK293 (Engel et al. ; Vasconcelos et al. ). The only significant result was concerned with the use of the PPARγ agonist in combination with MINCH at the highest concentration in viability experiments. This result could not be considered of primary importance due to the fact that this amount of the substance will never be encountered in human fluid. Nevertheless, we test this concentration to be comparable with studies testing MEHP in the reproductive environment (Shoaito et al. ; Antoine et al. ). This lack of cytotoxicity is promising, thereby suggesting that these plasticizers could be safe alternatives to phthalates in terms of basic cellular health. However, cytological safety does not guarantee the absence of adverse effects on cellular functions in the fetal membrane, such as signaling cascades.

## Dysregulation of the PPARγ signaling cascade by DINCH and MINCH or a neutral effect?
MINCH, the metabolite of DINCH, has already demonstrated its ability to activate PPARγ in HEK293 human cells for the highest concentration tested (100 µM) (Engel et al. ). In our study context, and given the importance of PPARγ in controlling inflammation to prevent PPROM, our results indicate that DINCH and MINCH do not deregulate the cellular action of this nuclear receptor pathway in amniotic cells nor its anti-inflammatory properties, which is in contrast to the phthalate MEHP (Antoine et al. ). Again, only the highest concentration tested for MINCH (100 µM) showed a tendency toward activation, which is in keeping with the results obtained transcriptionally.

## Perspectives and future research
Following our study, DINCH and MINCH appear to be potentially safer plasticizers than traditional phthalates, revealing no evident cytotoxic effects on amniotic epithelial cells and no reduction in the anti-inflammatory properties of PPARγ in fetal membranes. While our findings are encouraging, it is crucial to indicate that further investigations must be conducted. First, in vitro studies, although informative, cannot fully replicate the complexity of in vivo biological interactions. Nevertheless, in such case, animal models are not useful for studying the rupture of fetal membranes. This is why amniotic epithelial cells should not be considered in isolation. Thus, the action of alternative plasticizers must be considered in terms of the cellular crosstalk among layers of fetal membranes. For this, the use of the recently developed “organ on chip” technology could be an innovative solution (Menon and Richardson ). Furthermore, as indicated by most of the studies published on human pollutants, MINCH should not be considered as a unique pollutant affecting fetal membranes. This is why future studies must consider the use of “pollutant cocktails” and precisely determine the concentration of phthalates (already done) and commonly used alternative plasticizers (acetyl tributyl citrate (ATBC), tricapryl trimellitate (TCTM), trioctyl trimellitate (TOTM)) in amniotic fluid. Additionally, quantifying these cocktails in individual women will enable epidemiological studies that could help establish correlations between exposure to these plasticizers and PPROM rates in human populations, as was done for other risk factors (Waters and Mercer ). Such epidemiological studies will also provide insights into the long-term impact of chronic exposure to pollutants compared to acute exposure in classical in vitro studies.
In conclusion, for all pollutants tested, all targeted organs, and all related human pathologies, such scientific advances appear to be of primary importance to guide regulatory and industrial decisions regarding common human-use manufacturer products. This is particularly important for pregnant women, who must be protected throughout pregnancy without living in an unrealistic sterile environment. Preventing exposure to harmful substances is now recognized as critical, given that pregnancy complications affect not only the mother but also the long-term health and well-being of the child.
