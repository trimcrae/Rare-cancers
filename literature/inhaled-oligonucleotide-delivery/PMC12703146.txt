## Abstract
Acute lung injury (ALI) represents a critical clinical challenge characterized by uncontrolled pulmonary inflammation and disrupted tissue homeostasis, often leading to severe respiratory dysfunction. Current pharmacological interventions and vaccines have demonstrated suboptimal clinical outcomes in modulating disease progression, highlighting the urgent need for innovative therapeutic strategies. A key pathophysiological feature of ALI involves dysregulation of redox homeostasis and excessive pulmonary inflammation. Based on the demonstrated capacity of PBX1 to mitigate reactive oxygen species (ROS)-induced cellular damage, we hypothesized its therapeutic potential for ALI management. Overexpression of PBX1 in A549 cells can alleviate the increase in tumor necrosis factor α, interleukin (IL)-1β, and IL-6 levels caused by lipopolysaccharide (LPS). To circumvent the limitations associated with viral transduction while enabling efficient macromolecular delivery, we engineered a novel TAT-PBX1 fusion protein. In vivo, TAT-PBX1 effectively attenuated LPS-induced ROS accumulation and inflammatory cytokines, while preserving mitochondrial morphology. It restored ATP levels and the NAD+/NADH ratio, upregulated PGC-1α/TFAM expression, and suppressed cGAS-STING pathway activation. Mechanistically, TAT-PBX1 targets AMPKγ2 to activate the AMPK-TFAM signaling pathway, exerting anti-inflammatory and lung-protective effects. These findings identify TAT-PBX1 as a promising therapeutic candidate for mitigating pathological responses in ALI.
Keywords: PBX1, LPS, acute lung injury, AMPK, cGAS-STING

It targets AMPKγ2 to activate AMPK-TFAM signaling, alleviating LPS-induced mouse acute lung injury and delaying pulmonary fibrosis.

## Introduction
Acute lung injury (ALI) is a severe and potentially fatal illness characterized by an imbalance in pulmonary inflammation and redox homeostasis, alveolar epithelium damage, and blood-air barrier disruption. , During the global pandemic caused by SARS-CoV-2, the coronavirus was often accompanied by acute respiratory distress syndrome, a more severe form of ALI, in critically ill patients. , The pathophysiological processes of ALI include oxidative stress disorders and inflammation, which can alter pulmonary vascular permeability, leading to pulmonary edema and ultimately impairing gas exchange in the lungs. When an exaggerated inflammatory response occurs, immune cells such as neutrophils are recruited in lung tissue, triggering the release of proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α). These cytokines exacerbate the inflammatory response and the progression of ALI. Moreover, activated immune cells in the lungs produce large quantities of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Excess ROS can cause DNA damage, leading to apoptosis. Therefore, anti-inflammatory effects and reduction in ROS accumulation are prerequisite strategies to prevent ALI.
Mitochondria play an essential role in regulating cellular energy metabolism and innate immunity and are also key players in the inflammatory response process. Various cellular stresses, including lipopolysaccharides (LPS), can disrupt mitochondrial homeostasis, usually leading to a decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) and the accumulation of ROS, and the entering of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytoplasm through mitochondrial permeability transition pores. mtDNA is the main component that constitutes the extracellular trap of neutrophils and is involved in the formation of extracellular traps when neutrophils respond to bacteria, viruses, or other pathogens. Moreover, mtDNA triggers an inflammatory response through a cyclic guanosine monophosphate-adenosine monophosphate (GMP-AMP) synthase (cGAS) stimulator of interferon genes (STING) axis. The AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) signaling pathway is one of the main pathways regulating mitochondrial biogenesis, and the activation of AMPK is crucial for maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis. 
Pre-B cell leukemia homeobox transcription factor 1 (PBX1) is a member of the triple amino acid loop extension homeobox gene family. PBX1 plays an important role in the regulation of stem cell self-renewal, multipotency, and highly proliferative potential, and it is also involved in the maintenance of tissue homeostasis through the regulation of cellular oxidative stress and apoptosis. Our previous study revealed that PBX1 can mitigate oxidative damage by reducing ROS accumulation and increasing ATP and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide-positive (NAD+) levels. Therefore, we speculated that PBX1 may play a role in the protection of ALI from exotic toxics; moreover, the underlying mechanism needs to be studied further.
Although PBX1 plays an important role in the maintenance of tissue homeostasis, its clinical application faces great challenges due to its inability to penetrate the cell membrane. TAT is a short peptide with transmembrane capability extracted from an HIV-1 transcriptional activator. Therefore, TAT has been widely used for delivering macromolecular drugs. The TAT fusion protein not only has strong delivery ability but also ensures its uniform distribution in cells or organs through nonspecific cellular uptake. Additionally, TAT fusion technology has lower toxicity and immunogenicity than other delivery strategies, thus maximizing its protective effect. 
In our study, we showed that TAT-PBX1 administration significantly ameliorated LPS-induced mitochondrial dysfunction and subsequent cGAS-STING hyperactivation in ALI models, with genetic and pharmacological evidence indicating that the effect of TAT-PBX1 is mediated by targeting AMPKγ2 and activating the AMPK pathway.

## Results

## PBX1 expression was downregulated in serum of clinical patients and in vivo and in vitro models of ALI
The level of PBX1 in the peripheral blood of pneumonia patients was significantly lower than that in the peripheral blood of healthy people ( A). To further determine whether PBX1 is associated with pneumonia, patients were divided into three groups based on the CURB-65 score: mild patients (0–1 score), moderate patients (2 score), and severe patients (≥3 score). The score indicators of CURB-65 are shown in B. As shown in C, peripheral blood PBX1 levels were significantly lower in patients with severe disease than in those with mild disease, although this difference was not significant between mild-moderate and moderate-severe disease, indicating that greater severity of the disease was associated with lower peripheral blood PBX1 levels.
 Next, LPS-induced in vitro was used to further investigate the relationship between PBX1 and ALI. LPS concentrations ranging from 0.625 to 10 μg/mL did not affect the viability of A549 cells within 24 h. Moreover, 20 and 40 μg/mL LPS decreased cell viability to a certain extent, whereas 80 μg/mL LPS significantly decreased cell viability ( D). After A549 cells were stimulated with LPS (40 μg/mL) for 24 h, the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in the cell supernatant increased significantly ( E). The results of ELISA and western blot analyses both showed that LPS led to a decrease in the expression level of PBX1 ( F and 1G).
To evaluate the effect of LPS-mediated ALI on PBX1 expression in vivo, each mouse was administered LPS (10 mg/kg) via tracheal instillation to establish an ALI model. The results of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining ( H) indicated that LPS intervention successfully induced ALI in mice. The immunohistochemistry (IHC) and western blotting results ( I and 1J) further demonstrated that the expression level of PBX1 in the lung tissues of LPS-intervened mice decreased significantly.
Overall, these findings indicate that the expression level of PBX1 is downregulated in both in vivo and in vitro ALI models as well as in ALI patients, suggesting the key role of PBX1 in the pathobiological development of ALI.

## Overexpression of PBX1 alleviates LPS-induced cellular inflammation
Next, to investigate whether PBX1 affects inflammation, A549 cell lines overexpressing PBX1 were generated ( K). As shown in L, compared with control, LPS stimulation significantly increased the levels of inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6), whereas PBX1 overexpression significantly decreased the levels of these cytokines. The above results identify PBX1 as a potential bio-drug in combating LPS-mediated inflammation.

## Characterization and safety evaluation of TAT-PBX1
To achieve macromolecular drug delivery, we constructed the fusion protein TAT-PBX1, with the preparation procedure illustrated in A. The staining results of Coomassie brilliant blue show that a band was detected between 50 and 70 kDa in the third channel (eluent solution), which was more prominent in the fourth channel (concentrate solution) ( B). This protein was identified as TAT-PBX1 by the 6× His antibody ( C). Dynamic light scattering measurements revealed that the mean hydrodynamic diameter of TAT-PBX1 was 280 nm ( D). We found that TAT-PBX1 had good stability at 4°C and 37°C by monitoring the changes in protein concentration ( E and 2F). Additionally, TAT-PBX1 at a concentration of below 160 μg/mL did not show any detected toxicity in the viability of A549 cells ( G) and induced hemolysis in peripheral blood ( H), indicating its excellent biocompatibility.
 To evaluate the acute toxicity of TAT-PBX1, mice were given different doses (5, 10, and 20 mg/kg) of TAT-PBX1 by intraperitoneal injection. Seven days later, the mice were sacrificed for routine blood and biochemical tests and H&E staining. No significant differences in routine blood tests and bifunctional assay (liver and kidney) were found between control and TAT-PBX1-treated mice ( I). Additionally, compared with control animals, histological examination showed no pathological changes in the organs of brain, heart, lung, liver, spleen, kidney, and intestine in mice injected with TAT-PBX1 ( A). These results indicated that TAT-PBX1 has an excellent biosafety profile.

## TAT-PBX1 alleviates LPS-induced ALI in vitro and in vivo
To evaluate the ability to penetrate the cell membrane and enter the tissue, TAT-PBX1 was labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) and Cy5.5, respectively. As shown in A, in vitro experiments, green fluorescence intensity within A549 cells increased over time, confirming the transmembrane capability of TAT-PBX1. In vivo biodistribution studies demonstrated that no detected PBX1 was found in brain, heart, lung, liver, spleen, kidney by molecular image assay. In contrast, TAT-PBX1 distributed in almost all the organs mentioned above, mostly accumulating in lung, kidney, and liver. The distribution of TAT-PBX1 in the lung reached a peak 3 h post-intraperitoneal administration and persisting for 72 h ( B).
 To verify the in vitro anti-inflammatory activity of TAT-PBX1, A549 cells were intervened with 80 μg/mL TAT-PBX1 for 3 h, and then A549 cells were stimulated with 40 μg/mL LPS for 24 h. TAT-PBX1 treatment significantly decreased the LPS-induced expression of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α ( C). Due to the damage to cell mitochondria caused by LPS, a large amount of ROS was produced while the MMP was disrupted, eventually leading to cell death. TAT-PBX1 effectively reduced the accumulation of ROS induced by LPS ( D). In addition, TAT-PBX1 can restore MMP destroyed by LPS ( E). Flow cytometry analysis showed that TAT-PBX1 inhibited LPS-induced apoptosis ( F).
The process of the animal experiment is shown in A. During the experiment, we observed the survival status of the mice ( ). The mice in the control group have normal activity and shiny fur, while the mice in the LPS group have limited activity and dull fur. Compared with the mice in the LPS group, the mice treated with TAT-PBX1 are more active and have shiny fur. Compared with the control group, the weight of the mice in the LPS group decreased before sacrifice, the mean linear intercept and the lung index increased, and the wet weight/dry weight ratio of the lung tissue increased. In addition, the protein concentration in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) increased significantly, and the number of total cells, macrophages, and neutrophils increased. All these results indicated significant pulmonary edema and alveolar-capillary barrier dysfunction in the mice treated with LPS, and these conditions can be counteracted by TAT-PBX1 administration ( B–4H).
 Next, H&E staining was performed to evaluate lung tissue injury ( I). The lung alveolar structure of the mice in the control group and the TAT-PBX1 group did not show any abnormal histological changes, with no inflammatory cell infiltration and no lung injury observed. The alveolar structure of the LPS group was disorganized, and the lung injury score was significantly greater than that of the control group. In addition, the average lining interval was significantly thicker ( J), the average alveolar number was significantly lower than that of the control group ( K), and inflammatory cell infiltration and other pathological changes were prominent. TAT-PBX1 intervention restored LPS-induced lung injury.
To evaluate the in vivo anti-inflammatory activity of PBX1, the levels of inflammatory factors in serum and BALF were detected by ELISA. Compared with the control group ( L), the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in the serum and BALF were significantly higher in the LPS group, indicating that LPS caused lung tissue and systemic inflammatory responses in the mice. After TAT-PBX1 treatment, the levels of these cytokines decreased significantly.
Flow cytometry analysis showed that TAT-PBX1 alleviated LPS-induced ROS accumulation in mouse lungs ( A). Finally, the apoptosis of mouse lung cells was analyzed by TUNEL staining. As shown in B, compared with the control group, the number of TUNEL+ cells in the LPS group was significantly higher, whereas the number of TUNEL+ cells in the LPS+TAT-PBX1 group was significantly lower than that in the LPS group. In conclusion, TAT-PBX1 alleviated LPS-induced ALI in mice.
 

## TAT-PBX1 retards ALI-to-PF transition
ALI often eventually leads to pulmonary fibrosis (PF). To determine whether TAT-PBX1 affects the transition from ALI to PF, a bleomycin (BLM)-induced PF mouse model was generated. As expected, TAT-PBX1 significantly improved the survival rate of BLM mice ( C). H&E staining and Masson staining revealed severe lung tissue damage and extensive alveolar interstitial fibrosis in mice treated with BLM, whereas TAT-PBX1 significantly alleviated PF, including collagen deposition and interstitial fibrosis ( D and 5E). IHC analysis revealed that the expression levels of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) increased in the lung tissue of mice treated with BLM, which was also significantly inhibited by TAT-PBX1 ( F). Furthermore, in another 35-day animal test, TAT-PBX1 demonstrated a significant capacity for attenuating PF in BLM-treated mice ( A). These findings suggested that TAT-PBX1 can prevent the progression from ALI to PF.

## TAT-PBX1 alleviated ALI by restoring mitochondrial function
Mitochondrial dysfunction is a common feature of LPS-induced ALI. The morphology of the mitochondria and osmiophilic lamellar bodies in mouse type II alveolar cells was observed by transmission electron microscopy ( A). Compared with the control group, the mitochondria (red arrow) in the LPS group were swollen, with the absence of the mitochondrial ridge and increase in vacuolated osmiophilic lamellar bodies (green arrow). In the LPS+TAT-PBX1 group, the mitochondrial morphology returned to normal, and the osmiophilic lamellar bodies recovered their lamellar structure in a parallel arrangement. We also detected the levels of ATP and NAD+/NADH in the lung tissue samples of fresh mice ( B–6E). Compared with the control group, the ATP content and NAD+/NADH ratio in the LPS group were significantly lower, whereas the ATP content in the LPS+TAT-PBX1 group was significantly greater, and the NAD+/NADH ratio returned to nearly normal. These results indicated that TAT-PBX1 can alleviate LPS-induced mitochondrial dysfunction.
 

## TAT-PBX1 alleviated ALI by inhibiting the cGAS-STING pathway
To explore the mechanism by which TAT-PBX1 alleviates ALI, the PBX1 level in mice was detected. It was found that TAT-PBX1 administration significantly increased the level of PBX1 in the lung tissue of mice ( F). As the cGAS-STING pathway is a crucial signaling pathway in inflammatory response in the body, we analyzed the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway in mice under different treatment conditions. Western blotting assays revealed that LPS significantly increased cGAS and STING expression and phosphorylation of their downstream factors TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3), whereas TAT-PBX1 treatment successfully inhibited the activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, as shown by downregulation of the expression of cGAS and STING and phosphorylated TBK1 and IRF3 ( G).

## TAT-PBX1 alleviated ALI by activating the AMPK-PGC1α-TFAM signaling pathway
The mtDNA released by damaged mitochondria is an important factor in the activation of cGAS-STING, while the AMPK-transcription factor A, mitochondrial (TFAM) signaling pathway is involved in regulating mitochondrial biogenesis and restoring mitochondrial function. The results of the western blotting assays revealed that TAT-PBX1 reversed the inhibition of LPS on the AMPK-TFAM pathway ( H).
To prove that TAT-PBX1 has a positive effect on ALI by activating AMPK, dorsomorphin (10 mg/kg), an AMPK inhibitor, was administered to mice to inhibit AMPK activation. H&E ( A) and TUNEL staining ( B) showed that in mice treated with LPS, TAT-PBX1 administration significantly reduced lung injury area, whereas in mice treated with LPS and dorsomorphin, TAT-PBX1 administration did not show any protective ability to reduce lung injury area. Western blot assay showed that the AMPK pathway was inhibited in mice treated with both LPS and dorsomorphin as shown by the decreased phosphorylated (p)-AMPK/AMPK ratio, and the inhibitory effect of TAT-PBX1 on cGAS-STING was counteracted by dorsomorphin ( C and 7D). These results indicated that the protective effect of TAT-PBX1 against ALI was partially dependent on the activation of the AMPK signaling pathway.
 

## TAT-PBX1 targeted AMPKγ2 in protection of LPS-induced ALI
Protein interaction prediction and analysis were performed to determine the mechanism by which TAT-PBX1 activates AMPK. The γ-3 subunit of AMPK exists only in muscle tissue; therefore, we did not consider the role of the γ-3 subunit. Computational models revealed that the individual subunits of AMPK (α1-2, β1-2, and γ1-2) can interact with PBX1 or TAT-PBX1 ( A). Then, the predicted surface interface areas and thermodynamic properties of these complexes were comprehensively analyzed. Details of the surface interface area and binding free energy ( ) of each complex are shown in . For all models of the interactions between the proteins above, negative values indicated that these complexes can form stable interactions. Among them, the AMPKγ2 subunit showed the strongest binding effect with TAT-PBX1. All amino acid binding sites of the TAT-PBX1 and AMPKγ2 complexes were visualized using PyMOL ( A). Subsequently, we performed co-immunoprecipitation (coIP) experiments to verify the interaction of TAT-PBX1 and AMPKγ2 ( B). Additionally, IHC and western blot assays revealed that TAT-PBX1 upregulated the AMPKγ2 expression in the lung tissue of mice treated with or without LPS ( C and 8D). The above results indicated that TAT-PBX1 targeted AMPKγ2 and upregulated its expression.
 Given that AMPKγ2 is the target protein of TAT-PBX1 for exerting its function, small interfering RNA (siRNA) was used to inhibit the expression of AMPKγ2. Both siRNAs exhibited significant knockdown efficacy against AMPKγ2, and siAMPKγ2-1 was chosen for use in subsequent experiments ( B). The protective effects of TAT-PBX1 against LPS-induced ROS accumulation, MMP disruption, and apoptosis in A549 cells were reversed by AMPKγ2 knockdown ( E–8G). These findings indicated that TAT-PBX1 mitigated ALI by targeting AMPKγ2.

## Discussion
In this study, we investigated the correlation between PBX1 and pulmonary inflammation through the use of clinical, cell, and animal samples and verified this correlation via the overexpression of PBX1. To circumvent the limitations of viral transduction and achieve the efficient delivery of large-molecule drugs, we generated recombinant fusion protein TAT-PBX1. Our research found that TAT-PBX1 activates the AMPK-TFAM pathway, improves mitochondrial function, inhibits the cGAS-STING pathway, and has a protective effect on LPS-induced ALI.
The main characteristics of ALI are inflammation and excessive immune responses. PBX1 plays a crucial role in regulating the development and differentiation of various immune cells, such as T cells, B cells, and natural killer cells. PBX1 can inhibit cell aging and apoptosis by reducing ROS accumulation and increasing ATP and NAD+ levels in cells. PBX1 is expressed at low levels in patients with pneumonia, but its exact role in inflammation is unclear. Clinical data have suggested that plasma PBX1 concentrations are lower in patients with pneumonia than in healthy individuals. Lower PBX1 levels are associated with higher CURB-65 scores, indicating that PBX1 may be involved in the development process of pneumonia. Additionally, PBX1 expression decreased in both the in vitro and in vivo LPS-induced models, suggesting that PBX1 is a potential biomarker of lung inflammation. To deeply study the effect of PBX1 on ALI, we established an ALI model through LPS administration and generated the fusion protein TAT-PBX1 as an intervention drug. TAT-PBX1 could penetrate the cell membrane and distribute to lung tissues after intraperitoneal injection. TAT-PBX1 exhibited anti-inflammatory effects both in vitro and in vivo, and was able to delay the progression of PF in mice.
Regarding the mechanism by which TAT-PBX1 alleviates ALI, we first considered the cGAS-STING signaling pathway, which is closely related to the production of inflammatory factors. To elaborate, cGAS catalyzes the synthesis of GTP and ATP from cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP) after it recognizes double-stranded DNA in the cytoplasm. Then, cGAMP binds and activates STING, promoting its translocation to the Golgi apparatus. STING can subsequently activate downstream TBK1 and phosphorylate IRF3. The cytoplasmic IRF3 dimer enters the nucleus after phosphorylation and leads to the production of downstream interferons and TNF, which are involved in various pathological processes, especially inflammation. As a double-stranded DNA sensor, cGAS recognizes the mtDNA released into the cytoplasm after mitochondria are destroyed and activates downstream factors. Studies have shown that mitochondria are destroyed in the alveolar epithelial cells of LPS-induced ALI mice and that mtDNA is released into the cytoplasm. In this study, TAT-PBX1 restored the damaged mitochondrial morphology and function in the lung tissue of ALI mice. The expressions of cGAS and STING in LPS-induced ALI mice were upregulated, and the phosphorylation levels of IRF3 and TBK1 increased. However, TAT-PBX1 intervention reduced the expressions of cGAS and STING, and the phosphorylation levels of IRF3 and TBK1 decreased. Furthermore, TAT-PBX1 successfully reduced the increase in inflammatory factors induced by LPS. These findings indicate that TAT-PBX1 can restore mitochondrial homeostasis, inhibit the cGAS-STING pathway, and alleviate inflammation.
AMPK is an important factor involved in mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism. It clears dysfunctional mitochondria by driving mitochondrial autophagy, stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis to create new mitochondria, and promotes mitochondrial fusion to produce more efficient mitochondria. AMPK activation promotes mitochondrial biogenesis through SIRT1-dependent deacetylation of PGC-1α or regulation of PGC-1α and its downstream factor TFAM expression, thus restoring impaired mitochondrial function. Although TAT-PBX1 repairs damaged mitochondria, the effect of TAT-PBX1 on the AMPK signaling pathway remains unclear. In this study, we found that the expression of p-AMPK, PGC-1α, and TFAM was downregulated in LPS-induced ALI mice and that TAT-PBX1 treatment increased the expression of p-AMPK, PGC-1α, and TFAM. After the administration of the AMPK inhibitor dorsomorphin, the protective effect of TAT-PBX1 on ALI mice was reversed. Furthermore, dorsomorphin reversed the activation of the AMPK-TFAM pathway by TAT-PBX1 and the inhibition of the cGAS-STING pathway. These results indicate that the protective effect of TAT-PBX1 on ALI depends to a certain extent on the activation of the AMPK-TFAM signaling pathway.
AMPK is a heterotrimeric protein complex consisting of α, β, and γ subunits, each of which has multiple isoforms (α1, α2, β1, β2, γ1, γ2, and γ3). , The binding of AMP to the γ subunit results in a conformational change in the β subunit and promotes the phosphorylation of AMPK kinases at Thr172 in the α subunit, including LKB1 and calmodulin-dependent kinases, resulting in AMPK activation. Although AMP binds to the γ subunit to activate AMPK, other events that can regulate AMPK activity, besides the AMP/ATP ratio, have been investigated. For example, different γ subunits mediate differential effects, and overexpression of AMPKγ2 can enhance endogenous AMPK signal transduction, allowing longer AMPK signal transduction by improving protection of the AMPKα domain and increasing sensitivity to induced ADP along with AMP. We predicted the protein structure of PBX1, TAT-PBX1, and the six subunits of AMPK using AlphaFold3 and analyzed the protein interactions between them. An analysis of the thermodynamic properties of the compounds revealed that PBX1 and TAT-PBX1 were highly likely to interact with AMPKγ2. The experiment verified the interaction between TAT-PBX1 and AMPKγ2. IHC and western blot analyses revealed that TAT-PBX1 upregulated the expression of AMPKγ2. The protective effect mediated by TAT-PBX1-dependent on AMPKγ2 was demonstrated by pharmacological inhibition of AMPK activation or knockdown of AMPKγ2.
This study had several limitations. First, although AlphaFold3 is a relatively advanced tool for predicting structure and interactions, the tool has limitations in capturing protein dynamics, instantaneous interactions, and post-translational modifications. In future studies, the protein structure needs to be verified via X-ray diffraction, nuclear magnetic resonance, cryo-electron microscopy, and other techniques. Second, the mechanism underlying the role of TAT-PBX1 in pneumonia needs to be studied further. Structurally, the difference between TAT-PBX1 and PBX1 is the His-HA-TAT sequence, which is very small and has a negligible effect on the structure of the PBX1 protein. As the His-HA-TAT tag occupies the N terminus of the PBX1 protein sequence, the C-terminal sequence may play an important role in the anti-inflammatory function of PBX1. This speculation needs to be confirmed further by sequence truncation in subsequent studies. By constructing truncated forms of PBX1 through genetic engineering and comparing them with the full-length gene, the roles of the C-terminal sequence or N-terminal sequence can be verified. Given that ALI is a common complication of acute respiratory infectious diseases and TAT-PBX1 displayed significant protection of mice from LPS-induced ALI in our experiment, we speculate that TAT-PBX1 may have the potential to protect mice from respiratory virus-induced ALI. However, further animal tests using respiratory virus instead of LPS as toxicant to verify the protective effects of TAT-PBX1 are needed.

## Human samples
The seroprevalence study was approved by the ethics committee of the School of Public Health, Jilin University (No. 2024-09-03). All methods were performed according to the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all the patients and/or their legal guardian. The samples were analyzed anonymously. This study involved 34 patients who were diagnosed with pneumonia and 20 healthy controls at the First Bethune Hospital of Jilin University from March to June 2024. Serum was obtained from all these patients.

## Construction of PBX1-overexpressing cell line
The human PBX1 coding region was cloned into the pLVX-IRES-mCherry lentiviral vector (Youbio, Changsha, China). We used 293T cells to produce viruses. Lipofectamine 2000 was used as the transfection reagent. Viral particles were harvested 24 and 48 h after transfection. A549 cells were transduced with lentiviral particles encoding PBX1. Each 0.5 mL of lentiviral viruses were added to 9.5 mL complete medium for transduction. After 24 h, the supernatant was replaced with complete medium. After 72 h, the transduction efficiency was evaluated by western bolt.

## Generation of fusion protein TAT-PBX1
The PBX1 gene sequence (NM_002585.4) and His peptide sequence (HHHHHH) were inserted into the expression vector pTAT-HA to construct pTAT-HA-PBX1. The pTAT-HA-PBX1 plasmid from DH5α was extracted and transformed into Escherichia coli Rosetta. Rosetta was cultured overnight in Luria-Bertani medium at 37°C. Positive colonies were selected and continued to be cultured in liquid culture medium. After TAT-PBX1 expression, the bacterial pellet was collected and subjected to sonication (200 W, 0.5-s intervals, 15 min, twice) using a sonicator (Duoning, Shanghai, China). The lysate was purified using an Ni-NTA gravity affinity chromatography system. Imidazoles of different concentrations (30–200 mM) were used to remove impurities and wash off target proteins. The imidazole was removed by dialysis against PBS buffer (pH 7.3). The pHA-PBX1 plasmid was provided by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). The preparation method of the PBX1 recombinant protein is the same as that of TAT-PBX1.
SDS-PAGE was used to analyze the original sample mixture, wash mixture, eluent mixture, and concentrated mixture in the purification process of the TAT-PBX1 fusion protein. The hydrodynamic diameter was measured via DLS using a Nano ZS (Malvern, Worcestershire, UK).

## Safety evaluations of TAT-PBX1
A549 cells were incubated with different concentrations of TAT-PBX1 for 24 h, and the cytotoxicity was detected by Cell Counting Kit 8. Fresh blood from mice was used for hemolysis experiments to investigate the safety of TAT-PBX1 in the blood. C57BL/6J mice were intraperitoneally injected with TAT-PBX1 (5, 10, or 20 mg/kg) or normal saline once per day for 7 days. After sacrificing the mice, blood samples were taken for hematological and liver and kidney function tests, and histological examinations were conducted on the main organs.

## Transmembrane ability of TAT-PBX1
The cells were incubated with FITC-labeled TAT-PBX1, and the distribution of TAT-PBX1 in A549 cells at different times was observed under fluorescence microscopy. For the in vivo experiments, Cy5.5-labeled TAT-PBX1 was intraperitoneally injected into C57BL/6 mice. The distribution of TAT-PBX1 in the main organs was monitored using the AniView DXA multimodal live imaging system for small animals (BLT, Guangzhou, China).

## Animals
All animal procedures in this study were in compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85-23, revised in 1996), and were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the School of Public Health, Jilin University (No. SY2024-12-007). C57BL/6J male mice (6–8 weeks old) were obtained from Qianhe Biotech (Changchun, China). All the mice were kept in a specific-pathogen-free environment (temperature, 20°C–25°C; humidity, 50% ± 5%). ALI was induced in mice by intratracheal instillation of LPS (10 mg/kg) for 24 h. TAT-PBX1 (10 mg/kg) was intraperitoneally injected prophylactically 3 h before LPS treatment. Dorsomorphin (25 mg/kg) was administered 1 h before TAT-PBX1 treatment. PF was induced in mice by intratracheal instillation of BLM (5 mg/kg). TAT-PBX1 (10 mg/kg) was intraperitoneally injected prophylactically 3 h before BLM treatment and then injected once every 2 days for 21 days or 35 days. The blood of the mice was collected under deep anesthesia, the mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation, and BALF was collected. The left lobe of each mouse was used for pathological analysis. The upper lobe of the right lung was dried at 80°C to a constant weight. The lung wet-dry ratio (W/D) was recorded to assess pulmonary edema.

## Protein concentration and cell count in BALF
To obtain BALF samples, the murine lungs were lavaged three times with 0.5 mL ice-cold PBS (pH 7.4) each time. The BALF samples were subsequently centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 10 min to pellet the cells. The protein concentration of the BALF supernatant was detected via the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method. Total cells, macrophages, and neutrophils in the BALF were stained with Wright-Giemsa stain and counted in a double-blinded manner via a hemocytometer using an Olympus (Tokyo, Japan) microscope.

## H&E staining
Lung tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h and embedded in paraffin after serial dehydration. Sections of 5-μm-thick paraffin-embedded tissues were deparaffinized and stained with H&E. Pulmonary edema, alveolar congestion, infiltration of inflammatory cells, and atelectasis were evaluated. The specific scoring criteria for lung injury were as follows: 0 points, no damage; 1 point, less than 25% damage; 2 points, 25%–50% damage; 3 points, 50%–75% damage; and 4 points, 75%–100% damage. The mean alveolar number and the mean lining interval were calculated.

## IHC staining
A standard IHC protocol was used for lung tissue. Briefly, the lung samples were blocked with 5% BSA and incubated overnight with primary antibodies against AMPKγ2, PBX1, and α-SMA at 4°C. The next day, the sections were washed three times with PBS and incubated with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody for 60 min at room temperature. The samples were cleaned with PBS and observed under fluorescence microscope.

## Detection of lung apoptosis
Apoptosis in lung tissues was examined via a TUNEL kit. The sections were dewaxed with xylene and exposed to gradient ethanol. The samples were treated with Proteinase K and then immunostained at 37°C for 20 min. Afterward, the samples were incubated with TUNEL assay solution at 37°C in the dark for 60 min. The sections were counterstained with DAPI and sealed with anti-fluorescence quenching sealing solution. Fluorescence images were taken under a fluorescence microscope.

## Cell culture and treatment
A549 cells were purchased from the Cell Resource Center of Shanghai Institutes for Biological Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China) and maintained in DMEM (high glucose) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. A549 cells were treated with TAT-PBX1 (80 μg/mL) for 3 h. Then, A549 cells were treated with LPS (40 μg/mL) for 24 h. The cells and the supernatant were collected for detection.

## Detection of ROS and the MMP
A549 cells or lung tissue cell suspension were incubated with 10 mmol/L DCFH-DA at 37°C for 30 min. Fluorescence of A549 cells was detected under a fluorescence microscope, and ROS in lung tissue was detected by flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ).
To measure the MMP ΔΨm, the A549 cells were incubated with 10 μg/L JC-1 at 37°C for 30 min. Then, fluorescence was observed by a fluorescence microscope.

## Detection of ATP and NAD+/NADH
The levels of ATP and NAD+/NADH in lung tissue were quantified using commercially available ATP and NAD+/NADH detection kits. All procedures were carried out in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Apoptosis assay
Cell apoptosis was assessed via an annexin V-APC/7AAD kit. After digestion with trypsin, A549 cells were collected and centrifuged for 5 min at 1,000 × g. The sediment was exposed to binding buffer. The cell suspension (100 μL) was incubated with 5 μL annexin V-APC for 10 min in the dark and then with 10 μL 7AAD for 10 min in the dark. The sample was added to 200 μL PBS and then subjected to flow cytometry assay. The data were analyzed via FlowJo software.

## Western blotting
Lung tissues or A549 cells were homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer, supplemented with a protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail. Equal amounts of protein per sample were separated by SDS-PAGE (10%) and electrotransferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane. Membranes were incubated with the primary antibody overnight at 4°C with continuous shaking, followed by a 1-h incubation with the secondary antibody at room temperature. All the bands were developed and visualized with an enhanced chemiluminescence western blotting kit.

## Determination of cytokines by ELISA
ELISAs were used to detect TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in the serum, BALF, and cell supernatants, respectively, according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Statistical analysis
The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). The t test was employed to compare the means of two samples, while the one-way and two-way analysis of variance were utilized to compare the means of multiple samples. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA). A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
