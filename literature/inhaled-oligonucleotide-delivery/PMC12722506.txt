Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) circulating levels are associated with hypertension in humans. This study aims to investigate the roles of SGLT2i and FGF21 in improving hypertension and their potential mechanisms. A mouse model of Ang II-induced hypertension was established. Wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 mice and FGF21 knockout (FGF21−/−) mice were sequentially treated with Angiotensin II (Ang II) and dapagliflozin. Blood pressure was monitored. Cardiac structure was assessed using echocardiography. Serum FGF21 levels were measured, and the expression of fibroblast growth factor receptor 1 (FGFR1) in the thoracic aorta was quantified. Vascular pathology and oxidative stress responses were evaluated. Human aortic smooth muscle cells (HASMCs) were treated with Ang II or SGLT2i, and FGF21 was knocked down in HASMCs to explore its mechanism of action. SGLT2i increased the expression of FGF21 and FGFR. SGLT2i improved Ang II-induced systolic blood pressure elevation, myocardial hypertrophy, vascular wall thickening, fibrosis, and oxidative stress in WT mice. These protective effects were reduced in FGF21−/− mice. Knockdown of FGF21 in HASMCs abolished the SGLT2i-induced upregulation of antioxidant markers and the downregulation of TGF-β and fibrosis-related proteins. SGLT2i-mediated blood pressure-lowering and vascular protective effects are primarily achieved through the activation of the FGF21/FGFR1.

## Introduction
Hypertension induces pathological changes in endothelial and smooth muscle cells, accompanied by increased deposition of the vascular wall matrix, thereby predisposing individuals to thrombosis, stenosis, or aneurysm formation [ – ]. Hypertension remains one of the most prevalent and modifiable risk factors for both cardiovascular disease (CVD) and chronic kidney disease (CKD) [ ]. Despite the availability of multiple classes of antihypertensive agents, the global burden of poorly controlled blood pressure continues to rise. Recent updates in hypertension management guidelines, including the 2023 European Society of Hypertension (ESH) [ ] and 2024 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) [ ] recommendations emphasize the need for innovative strategies that offer both blood pressure control and organ protection. Nevertheless, unsatisfactory blood pressure control remains prevalent, and significant gaps persist in understanding the etiology of hypertension as well as the most effective treatment strategies. This highlights the need for further exploration of the underlying mechanisms involved in its management.
Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) [ ] is primarily located in the proximal tubules of renal nephrons. Notably, a small amount of its expression has also been detected in vascular smooth muscle cells [ ]. SGLT2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) [ ], including dapagliflozin (Dapa), empagliflozin, canagliflozin, and ertugliflozin, have been extensively studied. SGLT2i were initially developed as antidiabetic agents but have since demonstrated compelling cardiovascular and renal benefits, including reductions in hospitalization for heart failure, delay in CKD progression, and modest blood pressure-lowering effects [ , – ]. These benefits are thought to arise from pleiotropic mechanisms beyond glycemic control, including reduced sympathetic nervous system (SNS) activity [ ], potential interactions with the nucleus tractus solitarius in the brain [ ], natriuresis [ ], improved endothelial function [ ], suppression of inflammation [ ], and weight loss [ ]. Nevertheless, further exploration is required to elucidate additional antihypertensive and cardiovascular protective mechanisms of SGLT2i. This study hypothesizes that SGLT2i can simultaneously exert antihypertensive and vascular protective effects and aims to explore their underlying mechanisms.
Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is primarily synthesized and secreted by the liver, skeletal muscle, heart, and vasculature. As an endocrine fibroblast growth factor (FGF), FGF21 signals through fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFRs), predominantly FGFR1, along with co-receptors klotho and heparan sulfate cofactors [ ]. FGF21 plays a crucial role in ameliorating metabolic diseases [ , ] including type 2 diabetes, obesity, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, metabolic syndrome, and mitochondrial disorders. Recent studies have highlighted its significant role in cardiac protection [ ], as a metabolic hormone with anti-inflammatory and antioxidative properties, FGF21 improves endothelial function, mitigates arterial stiffness, and reduces oxidative stress [ ], all of which are critical in managing hypertension and preventing target organ damage. The ability of FGF21 to interconnect metabolic, vascular, and inflammatory pathways highlights its importance as a novel therapeutic target in the broader context of SGLT2i benefits.
Current studies on the correlation between SGLT2i and FGF21 primarily focus on the expression levels and cardioprotective effects of FGF21. Earlier research has suggested that SGLT2i can increase serum FGF21 levels by activating the catabolic pathway [ ]. Moreover, recent studies have indicated that SGLT2i can reduce inflammation, oxidative stress and autophagy by activating FGF21, thereby exerting a protective role in the heart [ ]. These findings collectively suggest that SGLT2i may play a crucial role in cardiovascular diseases through FGF21.
It remains unclear whether FGF21 is involved in the process by which SGLT2i improves hypertension. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the impact of SGLT2i treatment on Ang II-induced hypertension both in vitro and in vivo. We hypothesize that the vascular effects of SGLT2i treatment may be attenuated in FGF21 knockout (FGF21−/−) mice and in human aortic smooth muscle cells (HASMC) deficient in FGF21. This study seeks to explore whether SGLT2i improves hypertension through FGF21 and its potential role in vascular protection, based on our observational findings, aiming to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms involved.

## Material and Methods

## Animals and Treatment
Male FGF21−/− mice with C57BL/6 background and Wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 mice were generously granted by Gempharmatech (Jiangsu, China). All mice were six weeks old and weighed between 20 and 25 g on average. The mice were housed in controlled environments with constant humidity and temperature, following a 12-h light–dark cycle, and were provided unrestricted access to food and water.
Hypertension and vascular injury models were induced by infusing Ang II (A920, Solarbio, Beijing, China). WT C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to the following groups (n = 4 per group): (1) treated with saline; (2) treated with Ang II at a dose of 416 ng/kg/minute [ ]; (3) treated with dapagliflozin (Dapa) (S31536, Solarbio, Beijing, China) at a dose of 10 mg/kg/day [ ]; (4) treated with Ang II followed by Dapa. FGF21−/− mice were also randomly assigned to the following groups (n = 4 per group): (1) treated with saline; (2) treated with Ang II at the dose of 416 ng/kg/minute; (3) treated with Dapa at a dose of 10 mg/kg/day; (4) treated with Ang II followed by Dapa. Ang II or saline was administered via subcutaneously implanted osmotic micro-pumps (Alzet, Model1007D, DURECT, Cupertino, USA). From day 15 onward, Dapa or saline was administered via oral gavage once daily for 28 consecutive days.

## Cell Culture and Treatment
HASMCs obtained from Qincheng, Shanghai, China, were cultured in 12-well plates for 24 h in DMEM (Biological Industries, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Invitrogen, USA) and penicillin/streptomycin (Invitrogen, USA) until they reached 50% confluency. The cells were maintained in a CO2 incubator at 37 °C under a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 95% air.
For gene silencing experiments, small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting human FGF21 (sense: GAGGACGGAUACAAUGUUUTT and antisense: AAACAUUGUAUCCGUCCUCTT) and non-targeting control siRNA were purchased from Tsingke (Nanjing, China). FGF21 siRNA (si FGF21) was transfected into HASMCs at a final concentration of 150 nM, while the corresponding negative control siRNA (si NC) was transfected at 150 nM using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) in serum-free medium for 6 h. After transfection, the cells were incubated in complete DMEM medium for an additional 24 h. The HASMCs transfected with FGF21 siRNA or the negative control siRNA were subsequently used to investigate the molecular mechanism by which SGLT2i alleviate vascular remodeling. For the in vitro cell studies, Ang II (10–7 M, A920, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was applied for 24 h to induce the expression of fibrosis-related proteins in the cells. Subsequently, Dapa at a concentration of 10 μM was used to treat the cells for another 24 h to evaluate the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on oxidative stress and fibrosis.

## Measurement of Blood Pressure
Blood pressure was measured using the internationally recognized noninvasive tail-cuff method (BP 2000, Visitech Systems, USA), as previously described [ ]. Mice were trained to acclimatize to the instrument for at least 7 days before the establishment of hypertensive models. To minimize fluctuations in systolic blood pressure (SBP) due to the diurnal cycle, measurements were taken between 14:00 and 18:00. Blood pressure was recorded when pulse waveforms stabilized to sinusoidal patterns. Blood pressure data were measured and recorded every 5 days.

## Echocardiographic Measurements of Mice
Echocardiographic measurements were performed on mice using the VINNO 6 echocardiography system (VINNO Technology, Suzhou, China). Following inhalation anesthesia with isoflurane, M-mode echocardiography was used to obtain cardiac images. The recorded data were analyzed to determine various parameters, including interventricular septal thickness (IVST), left ventricular posterior wall thickness in diastole (LVPWD), and left ventricular internal diameter in diastole (LVIDD).

## Hematoxylin–Eosin (HE) and Masson’s Trichrome Staining
Thoracic aortas were fixed in 10% buffered formalin (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and processed into paraffin-embedded sections. These sections were stained using HE and Masson’s trichrome staining methods. For HE staining, tissue sections were dewaxed using standard protocols and sequentially stained with hematoxylin, differentiated with hydrochloric acid alcohol solution, blued with lithium carbonate, and counterstained with eosin solution. After staining, sections were dehydrated through graded alcohols, cleared with xylene, mounted with neutral balsam, and coverslipped. For Masson's trichrome staining, the tissue sections were stained using Weigert's iron hematoxylin, followed by differentiation in hydrochloric acid alcohol solution. Subsequently, sections were stained in Masson's bluing solution, followed by staining with Lichunfuchsin, phosphomolybdic acid, and aniline blue sequentially. Finally, slides were coverslipped. Images were acquired using a digital camera attached to an optical microscope (Olympus, Japan) and analyzed with ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, NIH, USA).

## DHE Staining
Cryosections of the thoracic aorta were incubated with 10 μM dihydroethidium (DHE; Beyotime, China) at 37 °C for 20–30 min in the dark and subsequently washed with PBS. DHE oxidized by reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the cells was detected at 555 nm. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

## ELISA
The ELISA kit (RX203011M, Ruixinbio, China) was equilibrated to room temperature for 1 h. Blank control wells, standard wells, and sample wells were prepared accordingly. For the blank control wells, no solution was added. Sample diluent and the standard product were added to the sample wells and standard wells, respectively. The biological antigen was then added and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min. The plate was washed with washing solution, followed by the addition of enzyme-labeled avidin and further incubation for 30 min. After the plate was washed again, the chromogenic substrate was added and incubated for 15 min. The reaction was terminated using the termination solution. The optical density (OD) value of each well was measured at 450 nm to establish the standard curve.

## Immunofluorescence
Cryosections were washed with PBS and pre-incubated with 5% BSA for 15 min. The samples were then incubated at 4 °C overnight with primary antibodies diluted 1:300. The primary antibodies used were: anti-FGFR1 (1:300, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China, AF6156), anti-phospho-FGFR1 (1:300, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China, AF3157), and anti-SGLT2 (1:300, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 24654–1–AP). Subsequently, the samples were incubated with Alexa Fluor 555-conjugated Donkey anti-Rabbit secondary antibody (1:250, Invitrogen, USA, A-31572) and stained with 4,6-diamino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (C1005, Beyotime, China) for 5 min. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (DM6B, Leica, China) and quantified with ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, NIH, USA) by two investigators blinded to treatment groups.

## Western Blotting
Proteins were extracted from HASMCs, denatured, subjected to SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were incubated overnight with primary antibodies diluted 1:1000. The primary antibodies used were: anti-FGF21 (DF8947, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), anti-TGF-β (BF8012, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), anti-GAPDH (AF7021, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), anti-Nrf2 (ab62352, Abcam, USA), anti-pSMAD2/3 (AP0548, ABclonal, Wuhan, China), anti-SOD1 (A0274, ABclonal, Wuhan, China), anti-COL1 A1 (R26615, ZENBIO, Chengdu, China), and anti-Tubulin (10,094–1, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). Following incubation, the membranes were washed, incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and stained using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (BMU102, Abbkine, Wuhan, China). Signals were detected using a luminescent analyzer (JS-1070P, Shanghai, China) and analyzed with ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, NIH, USA).

## Polymerase Chain Reaction
Mouse gene identification using polymerase chain reaction (PCR): Approximately 2 mm of mouse tail tissue was collected and incubated in a sodium hydroxide solution. The sample was then placed in a metal bath set to 99 °C, heated for 30 min, and subsequently prepared for PCR amplification. The resulting PCR product was subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis. The amplification product for the knockout FGF21 homozygote was approximately 600 bp, while the product for the wild type was approximately 430 bp.

## Statistical Analysis
Experimental data are expressed as means ± SEM. Statistical charts were generated using GraphPad Prism 8 software. Statistical significance of the experimental results was assessed using two-way ANOVA or a t-test. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant."n"represents the number of experiments.

## Results

## SGLT2i Induce Increased Expression and Activation of FGF21/FGFR1
An Ang II-induced hypertension mouse model was established (Fig.  a). Serum FGF21 levels in the mice and the expression of FGFR1 in the thoracic aortic rings were measured. The results indicated that serum FGF21 levels in mice treated with Ang II followed by Dapa were significantly higher than that in mice treated with Ang II alone (Fig.  b). Consistent with the elevation of serum FGF21, the expression of FGFR1 on the vascular wall of the thoracic aorta showed a similar upward trend (Fig.  c-d). This indicates that integrated FGF21 on the vascular wall was markedly elevated in mice treated with Ang II followed by Dapa, compared to those treated with Ang II alone. 
To investigate the relationship between SGLT2i and the expression and activation of FGF21, experiments were conducted using cultured HASMCs. Dapa, a widely used SGLT2i, was selected for the experiment. HASMCs were randomly divided into the following groups: negative control (NC), Ang II (10–7 M), Ang II (10–7 M) + Dapa (10 μM), and Dapa (10 μM). Initially, changes in FGF21 and FGFR1 expression levels in these cells were assessed following Ang II stimulation and subsequent treatment with Dapa. Ang II was found to upregulate the expression of FGF21 and FGFR1. Moreover, treatment with Dapa further increased the expression of FGF21 and FGFR1 beyond that induced by Ang II alone (Fig.  e-g). These results suggest that treatment with SGLT2i leads to increased expression and activation of FGF21/FGFR1.

## SGLT2i Improves Hypertension and Hypertensive Cardiac Remodeling Through the Action of FGF21
To further investigate the antihypertensive effects of SGLT2i and the potential influence of FGF21, we utilized WT and FGF21−/− mice to establish an Ang II-induced hypertension model (Fig.  a). No significant differences in body weight were observed between treatment groups (P > 0.05; Supplementary Tables and ). Blood pressure monitoring revealed that in the WT groups, SBP sharply increased during the first five days following Ang II infusion, and maintained a high level thereafter. SGLT2i treatment in the WT groups significantly reduced the Ang II-induced SBP elevation (Fig.  b). Moreover, in FGF21−/− mice, the increase in SBP following Ang II treatment was consistent with the response observed in WT mice. Notably, no significant difference in SBP was observed between FGF21−/− mice treated with Ang II followed by Dapa and those treated with Ang II alone (Fig.  c). Meanwhile, diastolic blood pressure (DBP) (Supplementary Fig. ) and mean arterial pressure (MAP) (Supplementary Fig. ) also showed similar trends. Taken together, our data suggests that SGLT2i may exert an antihypertensive role through FGF21. 
Hypertension increases cardiac afterload, resulting in left ventricular hypertrophy and thickening of the interventricular septum. Using mouse echocardiography (Fig.  d), we observed that compared to controls, Ang II-induced WT mice showed increased IVST and LVPWD, with decreased LVIDD, improvements observed following Dapa treatment (Fig.  e). At the same time, in FGF21−/− mice, Ang II similarly increased IVST and LVPWD, with decreased LVIDD, notably, these metrics did not improve with Dapa treatment (Fig.  f). This suggests Dapa improves hypertension-induced cardiac remodeling, wherein FGF21 plays a pivotal role. These findings highlight the essential role of FGF21 in the improvement of Ang II-induced hypertension and cardiac remodeling by SGLT2i.

## SGLT2i Improves Ang II-Induced Vascular Wall Thickening and Fibrosis in Mice Through FGF21
We evaluated morphological changes in the thoracic aorta. HE staining was employed to assess the thickness of the aortic wall in mice (Fig.  a). In the WT group, Ang II treatment led to vascular wall thickening, which was significantly improved by Dapa treatment (Fig.  b). However, in the FGF21−/− group, Ang II treatment also induced vascular wall thickening, but Dapa lost its ability to improve Ang II-induced vascular wall thickening due to FGF21 knockout (Fig.  c). Simultaneously, Masson's trichrome staining was employed to assess the extent of fibrosis in the thoracic aorta of mice (Fig.  d). The results demonstrated that Ang II treatment increased fibrosis in both WT and FGF21−/− mice, importantly, Dapa treatment improved Ang II-induced vascular fibrosis in the WT group but not in the FGF21−/− group (Fig.  e-f). These findings suggest that the effects of SGLT2i on improving hypertension-related vascular wall thickening and fibrosis are mediated by FGF21. 

## SGLT2i Improves Ang II-Induced Oxidative Stress by Upregulating FGF21-Nrf2-SOD1 Signaling Pathway
Oxidative stress, resulting from elevated ROS production, is a well-established pathological mechanism driving vascular smooth muscle cell fibrosis and proliferation [ ]. In our study, we observed that in the WT group, ROS deposition was significantly elevated in mice treated with Ang II alone. In contrast, ROS deposition was substantially reduced in mice administered Ang II followed by Dapa, compared to the group treated with Ang II alone. However, in FGF21−/− mice, no significant difference in ROS deposition was observed between those treated with Ang II followed by Dapa and those treated with Ang II alone (Fig.  a-c). These findings highlight the crucial role of FGF21 in attenuating Ang II-induced oxidative stress in the context of SGLT2i treatment. We then investigated the antioxidant mechanisms underlying the effects of SGLT2i via FGF21. Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2), a well-known transcription factor associated with antioxidant genes, mitigates atherosclerotic inflammation and oxidative stress by upregulating antioxidant proteins such as superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1), playing a crucial role in cardiovascular improvement. We hypothesized that SGLT2i may enhance Nrf2 and SOD1 expression through FGF21 upregulation. To test this hypothesis, we evaluated the impact of SGLT2i on the expression levels of Nrf2 and SOD1 in HASMCs treated with Ang II. Our results demonstrate that treatment with Dapa following Ang II stimulation significantly increased Nrf2 and SOD1 expression in si NC cells, compared to cells stimulated with Ang II alone. In contrast, no significant upregulation of Nrf2 and SOD1 was observed in si FGF21 cells following Dapa treatment (Fig.  d-f), indicating the suppression of antioxidative stress effects and emphasizing the indispensable role of FGF21 in mediating the antioxidative effects of SGLT2i in HASMCs. Based on these results, we propose that SGLT2i mitigates vascular oxidative stress via the FGF21/FGFR1-Nrf2-SOD1 signaling pathway. 

## SGLT2i Upregulates FGF21 to Inhibit TGF-β Expression and Further Suppresses p-SMAD2/3-COL1 A1 to Alleviate Vascular Fibrosis
TGF-β is recognized as a critical regulator of cardiovascular fibrosis due to its influence on cell growth, apoptosis, differentiation, extracellular matrix production, fibroblast viability, inhibition of metalloproteinase production, and promotion of collagen degradation [ , ]. To further investigate the cellular mechanisms by which SGLT2i improves Ang II-induced vascular fibrosis, we conducted an experiment to assess changes in the expression levels of FGF21, TGF-β, and fibrotic markers, including p-SMAD2/3 and COL1 A1, in HASMCs treated with SGLT2i following Ang II stimulation. We first explored the expression of FGF21 and TGF-β in HASMCs treated with Ang II using dose gradients (10–8 M, 10–7 M, 10–6 M, 10–5 M, 10–4 M) followed by an examination of the effect of Dapa treatment on Ang II-induced HASMCs. The data indicated a significant increase in the expression of both FGF21 and TGF-β in HASMCs when the concentration of Ang II reached 10–7 M (Fig.  a-b). Additionally, the expression of FGF21 and TGF-β in HASMCs treated with Ang II (10–7 M) was assessed over temporal gradients (0 h, 12 h, 24 h, 36 h, 48 h), suggesting that Ang II promotes the expression of FGF21 and TGF-β in a time-dependent manner (Fig.  c-d). Based on these findings, to determine the optimal experimental conditions for studying the effect of SGLT2i on FGF21 and TGF-β expression in Ang II-treated HASMCs, we cultured HASMCs with Dapa (0, 1, 5, 10, 50 μM) for 24 h in the presence of Ang II (10–7 M, 24 h). The results demonstrated that Dapa promoted the expression of FGF21 while concurrently reducing TGF-β levels in a dose-dependent manner (Fig.  e-g). In this study, we observed that Ang II promoted the expression of TGF-β in HASMCs in a time- and concentration-dependent manner, thereby promoting vascular fibrosis. Moreover, SGLT2i promoted FGF21 expression while inhibiting TGF-β expression in a concentration-dependent manner. To explore the correlation between the increase in FGF21 expression and the decrease in TGF-β expression caused by SGLT2i, normal HASMCs were randomly divided into negative control (NC), Ang II (10–7 M), Ang II (10–7 M) + Dapa (10 μM), and Dapa (10 μM) groups. Consistent with previous findings, it was observed that Ang II could promote the level of TGF-β. Comparatively, the group treated with Ang II (10–7 M) + Dapa (10 μM) exhibited decreased TGF-β expression (Fig.  h-i). 
The classical TGF-β/SMAD pathway is well-known, where the activated TGF-β receptor phosphorylates downstream SMAD proteins, resulting in the activation of p-SMAD, which then exerts transcriptional regulation [ ]. Numerous studies have established that the TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway plays a crucial role in cardiovascular fibrosis [ , ]. Previous studies have confirmed that TGF-β plays a critical role in upregulating COL1 A1 via the TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway. Our previous results have revealed that FGF21 deficiency blocked the role of SGLT2i in alleviating vascular injury and fibrosis in mice induced by Ang II. To comprehensively explore the changes of fibrosis-related proteins in HASMCs after SGLT2i treatment and to further understand the specific signaling pathways involved in the anti-fibrotic effect of SGLT2i, we then examined the changes in COL1 A1, TGF-β, and p-SMAD2/3 induced by SGLT2i in both Ang II-treated si NC and si FGF21 HASMCs. The results revealed that Ang II treatment significantly increased the expression of TGF-β, p-SMAD2/3, and COL1 A1 in HASMCs. Dapa treatment in the siNC control group reduced the expression of these proteins. However, in the si FGF21 group, Dapa treatment did not significantly alter the expression of these proteins. Specifically, the ability of Dapa to decrease the expression of TGF-β, p-SMAD2/3, and COL1 A1 was lost in HASMCs with FGF21 knockdown (Fig.  j-m). These findings suggest that Dapa reduces the expression of TGF-β and fibrosis-related proteins through FGF21.

## Discussion
In this study, we observed that SGLT2i resulted in a significant increase in serum FGF21 levels, while demonstrating both antihypertensive and vascular protective effects. The findings suggest that FGF21 plays a pivotal role in mediating the vascular protective effects of SGLT2i. Furthermore, the activation of the FGF21/FGFR1-Nrf2-SOD1 and TGF-β signaling pathways appears to contribute to the Dapa-mediated amelioration of vascular injury. These results broaden our understanding of the cardioprotective effects of SGLT2i, particularly in the context of hypertension and vascular injury.
Numerous trials have demonstrated the protective effects of SGLT2i on the heart beyond glycemic control, including in heart failure, fibrosis, atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction, and remodeling [ – ]. Dapa has been shown in clinical trials to reduce cardiovascular adverse outcomes in heart failure patients, regardless of their diabetic status. Consequently, the cardiovascular protective effects of SGLT2i in patients without type 2 diabetes mellitus have garnered widespread attention [ ]. Current clinical studies have revealed the beneficial effects of SGLT2i in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and cardiomyopathy [ ]. Researchers have extensively explored the cardioprotective mechanisms of SGLT2i, proposing potential pathways involving reduction of volume overload [ ], metabolic reprogramming [ ], modulation of central nervous system cardiovascular regulatory regions [ ], anti-inflammatory effects, attenuation of oxidative stress, inhibition of apoptosis [ ], and improvement of fibrosis [ ]. Our present study provides evidence that SGLT2i can markedly ameliorate Ang II-induced hypertension, vascular wall thickening, fibrosis, and oxidative stress. Subsequent investigations into the underlying mechanisms revealed that Dapa increased serum FGF21 levels, and inhibition of FGF21 countered the aforementioned effects. These results suggest that the protective effects of SGLT2i on blood vessels may be mediated by FGF21.
FGF21 is a recently acknowledged endocrine metabolic hormone, belonging to the fibroblast growth factor family, and is activated through binding to FGFR1 [ , ]. Current research on FGF21 primarily centers on its association with liver stress [ ], dysglycolipid metabolism [ ], and cardiovascular diseases. Protective effects of FGF21 against atherosclerosis have been demonstrated in numerous preclinical trials, positioning FGF21 levels as a biomarker for primary prevention of coronary artery disease (CAD) and subclinical atherosclerosis [ ]. In animal and cell models, FGF21 improves heart failure by modulating cardiac remodeling and combating pathological cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Furthermore, research has highlighted FGF21's influence on vascular smooth muscle cells, specifically, it improves pulmonary artery remodeling via the FGF21/miR-130/PPARγ axis, albeit within the context of hypoxia-induced effects on smooth muscle cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis [ ]. Studies have indicated that FGF21 inhibits the onset and progression of atherosclerosis by reducing endothelial cell damage and apoptosis [ ]. It is conceivable that FGF21 may mitigate cardiovascular disease by regulating glycolipid metabolism and potentially exerting direct effects on the heart and vasculature. Supporting this proposition, Pan et al. discovered that FGF21 acts on adipocytes and kidney cells to promote angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), an enzyme that converts Ang II to Angiotensin I, thus offering protection against Ang II-induced hypertension and vascular damage[ ]. These findings suggest the promising potential of FGF21 as a novel target for addressing hypertension, although the antihypertensive mechanism of FGF21 warrants further exploration. We posit that FGF21 may directly impact the vasculature by acting on vascular smooth muscle receptors, and our results indicate that SGLT2i and FGF21 collectively improve hypertension and alleviate vascular damage.
Ang II-induced hypertension involves multiple mechanisms, including vasoconstriction, renal hemodynamic changes, and vascular injury. Concurrently, vascular injury can also contribute to the persistence of hypertension. Vascular injury due to hypertension is a central element in the pathogenesis of multi-target organ damage and is a key factor in various cardiovascular diseases. It represents a crucial point for early intervention and treatment strategies, with vascular smooth muscle cells playing a pivotal role [ ]. Our findings show that after Ang II infusion in mice, SBP rose and remained elevated. This suggests that, in addition to the vasoconstriction induced by Ang II, vascular injury may occur concurrently, contributing to the sustained high blood pressure. This condition improved with Dapa treatment. Additionally, we observed that Ang II infusion increased the expression of FGF21 and FGFR in mice, which is related to the body's protective feedback mechanism [ ]. Dapa treatment further increased the expression of FGF21 and FGFR. Moreover, in cultured HASMCs, significant differences in FGFR expression were observed after Dapa treatment. This outcome may suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors regulate vascular smooth muscle function primarily through the FGF21-FGFR1 pathway.
Recently, the focus of research on the cardiovascular protective effects of SGLT2 inhibition has shifted toward investigating the link between SGLT2 inhibition and inflammation and ROS [ ]. However, the precise molecular mechanism and targets involved remain unclear. Our study has confirmed that SGLT2i upregulate the expression of both FGF21 and FGFR1 in both the vascular of mice models and in HASMCs. This suggests that SGLT2i may exert their effects by enhancing FGF21 activation rather than solely altering the quantity of FGF21 in the internal environment.
It is widely recognized that excessive oxidative stress, characterized by elevated ROS in the arteries, can lead to vascular injury [ , ]. Nrf2 is a transcription factor associated with antioxidant stress genes, capable of upregulating the transcription of various genes related to oxidative stress [ ]. Additionally, relevant studies have demonstrated the close relationship between FGF21 and SOD. Research by Yu et al. has indicated that FGF21 protects the mouse liver from oxidative stress and apoptosis through the activation of Nrf2 and the PI3 K/Akt pathways [ ]. Several studies have confirmed the causal role of ROS in profibrotic progression [ ]. Additionally, SOD has been demonstrated to participate in reducing the expression of TGF-β, which is considered a key regulator of cardiac fibrosis [ ]. In our research, we assessed the levels of Nrf2, SOD1, COL1 AI, TGF-β, and pSMAD2/3 in HASMCs treated with Ang II followed by Dapa. We found that Dapa significantly upregulated FGF21 and its downstream Nrf2 and SOD1 molecules, while decreasing the expression of TGF-β, pSMAD2/3, and COL1 A1. Therefore, SGLT2i may improve vascular damage through the FGF21-Nrf2 signaling pathway.
While our experiments have revealed novel insights into the mechanisms of SGLT2i in treating hypertension and elucidated how SGLT2i improve vascular injury through FGF21, the relationship between vascular injury and homeostasis is intricate. Factors such as altered vascular tone, vascular calcification, and smooth muscle cell phenotype transformation still require further exploration. Our study indicates that both FGFR and SGLT2 are expressed in vascular smooth muscle cells, but their specific localization and interaction within cells remain unclear. The interaction network among FGF21, FGFR, and SGLT2 warrants further investigation in the future. FGF21 is known to be produced not only in the liver but also in heart tissue, especially under conditions of cardiac stress such as heart failure and hypertrophy [ , ]. Although our study primarily focused on vascular tissue and circulating FGF21 levels, we did not investigate organ-specific FGF21 expression in the heart or liver. Future studies should incorporate tissue-specific assessments to determine whether SGLT2i induces local FGF21 production, and how this might contribute to organ crosstalk during hypertensive stress. Such findings could offer new insights into the systemic and tissue-specific mechanisms underlying SGLT2i-mediated protection. One more limitation of this study is that only male mice were used in order to minimize hormonal variability, particularly the influence of estrogen on vascular function and oxidative stress, which could confound the mechanistic interpretations. Future studies should validate these findings in female models to fully understand the sex-specific responses to SGLT2i. Moreover, clinical studies are warranted to validate the translational potential of these findings in the context of cardiovascular disease management.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, our data indicate that SGLT2i improve Ang II-induced hypertension and exert vascular protective effects through the FGF21/FGFR pathway. In this process, FGF21 plays an indispensable role, while antioxidant stress and TGF-β are implicated. This study offers novel insights into the antihypertensive and vascular protective mechanisms of SGLT2i.
