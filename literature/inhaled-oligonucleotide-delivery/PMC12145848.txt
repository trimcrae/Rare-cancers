## Summary
This study aims to explore whether vascular endothelial cells (VECs) derived from young rats enhance neurological recovery following ischemic stroke in aged rats. Middle cerebral artery occlusion models were established, and the rats received lateral ventricle injections of VECs isolated from the cerebral cortex of either young or aged rats. Young VECs treatment facilitated neurorestoration in aged rats on days 3, 7, and 21 following cerebral ischemia. Aged subjects treated with young VECs exhibited reduced neuronal apoptosis, smaller infarct volumes, increased microvessel density, and elevated nerve growth factor expression relative to those receiving aged VECs. Protein levels did not differ between age groups on days 1, 3, or 7 post-stroke (p > 0.05), while DNA binding activity of hypoxia-inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF-1α) was significantly greater in young than in aged rats (p < 0.05). Young-derived VECs promote neurological recovery after cerebral ischemic stroke in aged rats through HIF-1α.

## Introduction
Cerebral strokes, encompassing both ischemic and hemorrhagic types, represent the leading cause of disability among humans. Ischemic strokes account for 71–80.5% of all stroke cases, predominantly affecting older adults. In this demographic, the older stroke patients exhibit poorer outcomes compared to their younger counterparts, however, the underlying mechanisms contributing to delayed functional recovery in elderly individuals post-stroke remain poorly understood. Currently, there is no effective treatment available that can enhance neurological function in elderly stroke patients. Hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α) serves as a primary regulator of oxygen homeostasis following cerebral ischemia by promoting vascular endothelial cells (VECs) renewal and mediating angiogenesis. The role of HIF-1α in improving neuronal function after brain injury remains contentious. Barteczek et al. reported a significant reduction in infarct size and edema in HIF-1α-deficient rats compared to wild-type controls during the early stages of cerebral ischemic stroke. , Several studies have highlighted the detrimental inflammatory response associated with HIF-1α activation, which exacerbates apoptosis and compromises blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity following ischemic events. , , This suggests that HIF-1α may exacerbate neuronal damage during the acute phase of cerebral ischemic stroke. Nevertheless, other investigations have indicated that HIF-1α plays a beneficial role in facilitating neuronal recovery both in vivo and in vitro. , , Furthermore, HIF-1α is implicated in adaptive responses to ischemic stress and cellular regeneration, thereby exhibiting neuroprotective properties. , 
HIF-1 is composed of α and β subunits. Due to the overexpression of HIF-1β in cells, the transcriptional activity of HIF-1 is predominantly regulated by the HIF-1α protein. Under normal conditions, the HIF-1α subunit undergoes hydroxylation by prolyl hydroxylase (PHD), followed by ubiquitination through an E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, ultimately leading to its degradation via the 26S proteasome. In contrast, under hypoxic conditions, PHD becomes inactive, preventing hydroxylation of HIF-1α. Consequently, HIF-1α accumulates in the cytoplasm and forms a heterodimer with the HIF-1β subunit; this complex translocates into the nucleus where it binds to hypoxia response elements on target gene promoters to modulate cellular responses to oxygen and glucose deprivation. , Furthermore, HIF-1α regulates various biological processes involved in erythropoiesis, cellular metabolism, angiogenesis, neurogenesis, and cell proliferation.
The neurovascular unit (NVU) serves as the structural foundation for establishing functional connections between brain cells and the microvasculature, comprising primarily endothelial cells, astrocytes, neurons, and microglial cells. Following ischemic stroke, HIF-1α facilitates functional diversity within the NVU by modulating the expression of downstream target genes. Endothelial cells constitute a major component of the BBB, which is considered an essential interface between the central nervous system and blood vessels; its stability is crucial for maintaining BBB integrity. HIF-1α enhances angiogenesis and neovascularization in ischemic injured tissues through upregulation of VEGF, a key factor associated with endothelial cell proliferation and migration. Additionally, VEGF acts as an important regulator of neuroprotection and neurogenesis following cerebral ischemia. Its neuroprotective effects are predominantly mediated through promoting angiogenesis and neurogenesis after 12 h post-cerebral ischemia. Ischemic events lead to damage in nerve cells and disruption of the microvascular system, resulting in loss of neural function; however, neovascularization can supply essential nutrients necessary for nerve cell regeneration processes. Consequently, angiogenesis emerges as a pivotal element in reconstructing neural function after cerebral ischemia. Hypoxia-mediated signaling pathways linked to HIF-1α have been demonstrated to exert protective effects on neuronal health following cerebral ischemia. PHD serves as a critical mediator in the degradation process of HIF-1α; thus, inhibition of PHD activity or genetic knockout can prevent HIF-1α degradation while stimulating cellular protective responses to tissue hypoxia. , Previous studies have indicated that HIF-1α mitigates cerebral infarction severity in rats subjected to ischemic injury by upregulating sodium-calcium exchanger-1 (NCX1). 
Currently, the inadequate recovery of neurological function in elderly individuals represents a significant global challenge. Nevertheless, the role and underlying mechanisms of HIF-1α in facilitating neurological repair following cerebral ischemia in this demographic remain poorly understood. Previous investigations by our research team demonstrated that inhibition of HIF-1 transcriptional activity adversely affected neurological recovery after ischemic injury in aged rats. In the present study, we administered brain VECs derived from young rats to aged cerebral stroke models to evaluate their therapeutic efficacy and elucidate the associated mechanisms.

## Results

## The expression level and DNA binding activity of HIF-1α in aged rats
The recovery of neurological function following ischemic stroke was significantly impaired in aged rats compared to their younger counterparts, with young rats exhibiting superior neurological performance on the 21st day post-MCAO ( A and 1B). Although there were no significant differences in mRNA and protein expression levels of HIF-1α in the ischemic cortex between young and aged groups at various time points after ischemia ( A–2C), the DNA binding activity of HIF-1α was markedly diminished in aged rats relative to that observed in young rats ( D). 

## Young VECs treatment promotes neurologic function recovery of aged rats
VECs were isolated from the microvessels of the rat cerebral cortex and characterized by their expression of von Willebrand factor ( A). Following hypoxia treatment, although there was no significant difference in HIF-1α protein levels between young and aged VECs, young VECs exhibited a markedly stronger HIF-1α DNA binding activity compared to their aged counterparts (n = 5 per group, p < 0.05, B–3D). The expression of HIF-1α protein was significantly inhibited by HIF-1α siRNA in young VECs ( C and 3D). Young VECs significantly enhanced neurological recovery in aged rats at various time points post-ischemic stroke when compared to aged VECs, with this therapeutic effect being abolished following silencing of the HIF-1α gene via siRNA ( A). TTC staining demonstrated that the infarct volume in the MCAO + Young VEC group was significantly reduced relative to that in the MCAO + Aged VEC group (16.96 ± 9.59% vs. 34.30 ± 8.25%, n = 10 per group, p < 0.05, B and 4C). Furthermore, the infarct volume in the MCAO + Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA group was significantly increased compared to that observed in the MCAO + Young VEC group (35.80 ± 13.94% vs.16.96 ± 9.59%, n = 10 per group, p < 0.05; B and 4C). TUNEL staining indicated that treatment with young VECs substantially suppressed apoptosis within ischemic regions of aged rats as opposed to treatment with aged VECs (n = 10 per group, p < 0.05), while apoptosis rates increased following silencing of the HIF-1α gene using siRNA ( ). The microvessel density within the MCAO + Young VEC cohort was notably higher than that found within the MCAO + Aged VEC cohort (328 0.80 ± 111 0.46/mm2 vs. 0.171 0.80 ± 87 0.13/mm2, n = 10 per group, p < 0 0.05, A), whereas microvessel density decreased in the Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA group comparing with the Young VEC group. In addition, the protein expression level of NGF was significantly elevated in MCA O + Young VEC group compared to MCAO + Aged VEC group 24 h after MCAO (n = 10 per group, p < 0.05, F ig. 6B), and NGF protein expression was inhibited in the MCAO + Young VEC with HIF -1α si RNA group comparing with MCAO + Young VEC treatment (n = 10 per group, p < 0.05, B). 

## Discussion
Stroke remains a significant health challenge, particularly among the elderly population. There is an ongoing deficiency in effective strategies aimed at enhancing rehabilitation outcomes for older adults. In comparison to younger individuals, the elderly exhibit poorer recovery results. Thus, elucidating the mechanisms underlying neurological function repair is of paramount importance.
VECs are the primary cell type that constitutes the NVU, and the expression of HIF-1α in VECs is crucial for regulating neural function. In this study, we present for the first time the use of brain-derived VECs from young rats to treat aged rats suffering from cerebral ischemic injury through ventricular transplantation, resulting in a significant enhancement of neurological recovery in the aged subjects. Notably, when HIF-1α is inhibited by siRNA in VECs, their therapeutic efficacy disappears. We assessed microvessel density, brain infarction volume, neuronal apoptosis, as well as levels of HIF-1α and NGF to elucidate the mechanisms underlying improvements in neurological function.
In this report, the administration of young VECs into the lateral ventricle significantly enhanced neurological recovery, increased microvascular density, and reduced both brain infarction volume and the rate of neuronal apoptosis in the peri-infarct region of aged rats. Previous studies have established that VECs not only serve as critical components of the BBB, providing barrier protection and nutrient transport, but also play a pivotal role in guiding neurogenesis. Following cerebral ischemic injury, VEC proliferation was observed around the infarct area within 12–24 h. , Endothelial cells release soluble factors that promote self-renewal among neural stem cells while enhancing their neurogenic potential. Shen et al. reported that co-culture with VECs stimulates contact with neuroepithelial cells and activates Notch signaling pathways along with Hes1 expression to promote self-renewal. , In our study, we observed a marked reduction in apoptotic rates among aged rats treated with young VECs on day 3 post-MCAO. Furthermore, neurological function recovery was significantly superior in the young VEC group compared to both vehicle controls and aged VEC groups at days 3, 7, and 21 following MCAO. Thus, it is reasonable to conclude that young VECs mitigate neuronal apoptosis while preserving neuronal functionality.
Angiogenesis serves as a highly effective mechanism for enhancing the oxygenation of hypoxic tissues within the body. Following cerebral ischemia, VEGF signaling pathways are promptly activated, leading to the binding of VEGF to its receptors on VECs, thereby initiating angiogenesis and commencing vessel formation. Neurorestorative processes encompass both neurogenesis and angiogenesis. Research has demonstrated that vascular regeneration is a critical component in functional recovery and tissue reconstruction following ischemic stroke. Vessel formation occurs through two primary processes: vasculogenesis and angiogenesis, both of which contribute to neurorepair mechanisms including neurogenesis and synaptogenesis. , , Neural elements and blood vessels are intricately linked through the NVU network, facilitating mutual interactions; furthermore, NVU directly participates in angiogenic responses and neovascularization within ischemically injured tissues. In this study, we observed a significant increase in microvessel density surrounding the infarction zone in aged rats treated with young VECs by day 7 post-MCAO. This finding suggests that young VECs effectively promote cerebrovascular neovascularization in aged rats after ischemic stroke, thereby providing an early nutritional foundation for neural function repair.
The findings of this study indicate that young VECs enhance neurorestoration capabilities and induce morphological changes in the infarction zone of aged rats. However, this beneficial therapeutic effect was abolished when HIF-1α expression in young VECs was inhibited by siRNA. Consequently, the molecular mechanism underlying this phenomenon is evidently linked to HIF-1α. As a primary response factor in tissue adaptation to ischemia and hypoxia, HIF-1α is expressed across various cell types, including neurons, astrocytes, endothelial cells, and microglial cells. , , The HIF-1α secreted by young VECs can more effectively regulate the expression of downstream genes such as VEGF, glucose transporter 1, and glycolytic enzyme genes. VEGF stands out as a key mediator of neurogenesis while NGF also plays a role in angiogenesis as previously reported. In response to hypoxic conditions, HIF-1 binds to hypoxia-responsive elements within the promoter region of the VEGF gene to upregulate its transcription and expression. VEGF exhibits high specificity and participates in multiple stages of angiogenesis including both angiogenic processes and nerve regeneration. By promoting angiogenesis, VEGF enhances blood supply perfusion to neural tissues and directs the migration of neural stem cells, thereby facilitating neuronal growth and providing neuroprotection. During this process, NGF—known for regulating proliferation, growth, migration, apoptosis, and metabolism of nerve cells—is activated. Consequently, there is a reduction in neuronal apoptosis rates alongside improvements in neurological function. Thus, the observed increase in vessel density coupled with reduced neuronal apoptosis following administration of young VECs can be elucidated through these mechanisms.
The reduction in infarct volume observed 48 h post-ischemic stroke, along with the increase in neuronal cells noted 72 h after cerebral ischemia, was associated with the induction of HIF-1α. Furthermore, HIF-1α appears to safeguard neurological function by modulating inflammatory and glial responses up to 12 days following ischemic stroke in rat models. The mRNA and protein levels of HIF-1α peaked at 24 h post-ischemia before gradually declining. The findings in this study align with previous research. The biological activity of HIF-1α is primarily contingent upon its accumulation and transcriptional efficacy, which refers to its capacity to initiate downstream gene expression. , The DNA binding affinity of HIF-1α for target genes underpins this transcriptional activity. In our study, there were no significant differences in the protein expression levels of HIF-1α within the ischemic cortex between young and aged groups at 24 h, as well as at 3 days and 7 days post-brain ischemia; however, the DNA binding activity of HIF-1α was markedly diminished in aged rats compared to their younger counterparts. This finding suggests a substantial attenuation in the transcriptional activity of HIF-1α among aged rats, potentially contributing to their impaired recovery of neurological function. Nonetheless, further investigation is warranted to elucidate how alterations in DNA binding activity of HIF-1α influence neurological recovery. This study aims to provide a foundation for therapeutic strategies targeting cerebral ischemic stroke in elderly populations. In summary, young rat VECs can significantly enhance neurological recovery in aged rats following cerebral ischemic stroke, mediated by HIF-1α. The mRNA and protein expression levels of HIF-1α showed no significant differences between young and aged rats post-brain ischemia. Notably, HIF-1α secreted by young VECs exhibited superior DNA binding activity compared to that released by the aged counterparts.

## Limitations of the study
This study demonstrates that the administration of young rat VECs to aged rats after a stroke markedly improves their neural repair capacity, highlighting the leading role of HIF-1α in this process. Furthermore, our findings reveal that HIF-1α secreted from young VECs exhibits enhanced DNA binding activity relative to that produced by aged VECs. However, due to current technological limitations, the precise contribution of DNA binding activity to the enhancement of neural function in aged rats remains inadequately defined and represents a promising direction for future research. This study utilized male SD rats as experimental models, which could not fully simulate the clinical features of cerebral infarction observed in both male and female populations. Fortunately, this experiment was to investigate the biological mechanism of neural function recovery, which is not affected by gender, so the findings retain their significance.

## Experimental model and study participant details

## Animals
Male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (2-months-old) and aged male SD rats (18–22-months-old) weighing 280–320 g were placed in a temperature and humidity-controlled room at 23°C and 65% humidity with a 12-h light/dark cycle and free access to food and water. Rats were anesthetized by isoflurane inhalation using an animal anesthesia apparatus (R540, RWD Life Science, China). The body temperature of the rats was maintained at 37 ± 0.5°C with a heating pad. Rats were euthanized by inhaling pure CO2. All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Southwest Medical University (approval number KY2019035)and performed following the guidelines of the National Institutes of Health of the United States.

## Middle cerebral artery occlusion model and neurological assessment
A middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) model was established as previously described. , Briefly, a 0.32-mm diameter suture (Beijing Sunbio Biotech Co. Ltd., China) was inserted into the right internal carotid artery to block the middle cerebral artery. The suture was removed 1 h later to allow reperfusion. The sham group underwent the same procedure without insertion of sutures. Systolic blood pressure, blood pH value, partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2), partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2), and plasma glucose concentration were measured before and after MCAO. The modified neurology severity score (MNSS) and a corner test were used to assess neurological function as previously described. , In MNSS, the neurological function was graded on a scale of 0–18 points, and a higher score indicates greater impairment. In corner test, the mice were placed between the boards at an angle of 30°. When the rat enters the depth of the angle, both sides of the vibrissae are stimulated simultaneously. The rats lifted himself forward and then turned away from the angle and climbed out. The non-ischemic rats turned left or right about equally often, but the ischemic rats preferentially turned to the undamaged unilateral (right) side. Each rat was tested 10 times, and the percentage of right turns was considered the degree of neurological impairment. Turning movements that were not part of a rearing movement were not scored. A higher right turn percentage indicates a more severe lesion in the brain.

## Isolation and culture of VECs
VECs were isolated from the frontal brain cortices of young and aged rats. Briefly, the cerebral cortex was digested with collagenase II. Following centrifugation in DMEM containing 20% bovine serum albumin, the pellet was incubated with a collagenase-dispase mixture (Roche) and DNase in DMEM for 45 min at 37°C. VECs were separated on a 33% continuous Percoll gradient (GE Healthcare) and grown in DMEM of human microvascular endothelial cells (Procell Life Science & Technology) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, heparin, and endothelial cell growth supplements. VECs were characterized by Von Willebrand factor expression. , After incubation with 5% bovine serum albumin/1x PBS for 2 h at 37°C, the cells were incubated with primary antibody (anti-Von Willebrand factor, 1:200, Abcam) at 4°C overnight. After a brief wash with PBS, the cells were then incubated with secondary antibody (Donkey Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L, 1:1000,Abcam) for 1 h at 37°C. After being washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with 1:300 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) stain (Invitrogen) for 5 min at room temperature. The results were imaged using a fluorescence microscope. To induce hypoxia, VECs were incubated in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 0.2% O2 for 6 h at 37°C.

## Method details

## HIF-1α siRNA
VECs were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 2 x 105 cells per well. Prior to transfection, the VECs were subjected to a 1-h period of serum starvation in Opti-MEM I Reduced Serum Medium. Following this 24-h post-transfection incubation period, the cells were then prepared for further experimentation. The siRNA targeting rat HIF-1α and mismatched control (Guangzhou, China) were designed and synthesized from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). The siRNA sequences for HIF-1α were as follows: sense, 5′-GGAAACGAGUGAAAGGAUATT-3′; anti-sense, 5′-UAUCCUUUCACUCGUUUCCAA-3′. The optimal transfection efficiency was determined from our preliminary study testing the range from 10 to 100 nM, and we selected the condition that the siRNA caused an optimal silencing effect with minimal cytotoxicity. After rat VECs were transfected with 25 nM annealed siRNA targeting rat HIF-1α or negative mismatched control siRNA in serum-free and antibiotics-free media, a Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, California, U.S.A) was used as the transfection reagent according to the manufacturer’s instruction.

## Transplantation of VECs
Aged rats were divided into five groups: MCAO, MCAO + vehicle, MCAO + aged VEC, MCAO + young VEC, and MCAO + Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA. Following anesthesia, rats were mounted on a stereotaxic frame. The 5 μL of phosphate buffered saline was transplanted into right lateral ventricular of the rats in the MCAO + vehicle group. The 5 μL aged VECs (1 × 105 cells/mL), 5 μL young VEC (1 × 105 cells/mL), 5 μL Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA(1 × 105 cells/mL) was transplanted respectively into right lateral ventricular of the rats in the MCAO + aged VEC, MCAO + young VEC, and MCAO + Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA groups using a 10 μL Hamilton syringe. The bregma coordinates were 1.6 mm posterior, 1.0 mm lateral, and 3.3 mm depth. , , The injection rate was 2 μL/min. Then, MCAO operation was performed.

## Measurement of infarct volume
On the 3rd day after MCAO, Infarct volume was measured using 2,3,5-triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC, ACMEC, China) staining. In brief, the frontal brain was stored at −80°C for 15 min and then successively cut into six 2-mm-thick slices. The slices were stained with TTC (2%) for 30 min in the dark and fixed with 10% formalin for 24 h. Images were acquired. The size of the TTC-stained area in each slice (S1 to S6) was measured using Photoshop 17.0. The volume was calculated as (S1 + S2 + S3 + S4 + S5+ S6) × 2 mm3. Infarct volume (%) was calculated as (V1 − V2)/V1 × 100%. , V1 = volume of the normal hemisphere; V2 = volume of the normal cerebral tissue in the ischemic hemisphere.

## Microvessel density
Aged rats were divided into five groups: MCAO, MCAO + vehicle, MCAO + aged VEC, MCAO + young VEC, and MCAO + Young VEC with HIF-1α siRNA. On the 7th day after MCAO procedure, immunohistochemistry staining with anti-CD31 antibody (ab281583, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) was used to measure the microvessel density of the brain. The section was observed at a magnification of 200×. Two fields were randomly selected in the infarct zone. Images with 1024 pixels × 1024 pixels, 0.62 μm/pixel, were acquired using image-pro plus 6.0. The CD31-positive hollow tubular microvessels were counted.

## ELISA
DNA binding activity of HIF-1α was measured using a HIF-1α transcription factor assay kit (#10006910, Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. , Briefly, each well of the ELISA plate was pre-coated with a specific double-stranded DNA containing the HIF-1α response element (5′-ACGTG-3′). After adding the samples, the plate was incubated overnight at 4°C, followed by overnight incubation with an anti-HIF-1α antibody (#10009269, Cayman Chemical). Afterward, the secondary antibody was added into each well and incubated for 1 h at room temperature. After the washing process, the developing solution was added and incubated at 37°C for 30 min. Then, the stop solution was added to each well, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured within 5 min of adding the stop solution. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, the absorbance is converted to a percentage to represent the binding activity of HIF-1α.

## Protein expression of HIF-1α and nerve growth factor (NGF)
The nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions were prepared using a nuclear extraction kit (#10009277, Cayman Chemicals). The protein expression of HIF-1α around the ischemic penumbra area was determined by Western blot analysis at the different time points, and NGF protein expression was detected at the 24 h after cerebral ischemia. Equal amounts of proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. Then, the membrane was incubated with primary antibody against HIF-1α (1:1,000; Signalway Antibody, USA), NGF (1:1000; Santa Cruz, USA), or β-actin (1:1,000; Santa Cruz, USA) at 4°C. The membrane was incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 30 min. Blot images were analyzed using Vilber lourmat fusion FX (France).

## Real-time PCR
Total RNA was isolated from ischemic brain tissue using a Qiagen RNeasy mini kit at the different time points and reversely transcribed to cDNA using a high-capacity cDNA reverse transcription kit (#4368814; Applied Biosystems). The primer sequences for HIF-1α were as follows: sense, CGGCGAGAACGAGAAGAAAAATA; antisense, ATGTCAAGATCACCAGCACCTA. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using a real-time PCR master mix (#QPK-201; Toyobo, China) on a Stepone real-time PCR machine (Life Technologies, USA).

## Nerve cells apoptosis was detected by TUNEL staining
TUNEL staining was carried out to examine apoptosis in the brain section (6-μm-thick) using an in situ cell death detection kit (Roche Applied Science, Germany) following the manufacturer’s instructions at 24 h after MCAO. Images were acquired using a confocal microscope at a magnification of 200× and analyzed using image-pro plus 6.0 (Media Cybernetics, USA). Six infarct areas were randomly selected from each section to calculate the percentage of TUNEL-positive cells.

## Quantification and statistical analysis
Data were presented as the means ± standard deviation (SD). ANOVA or Student’s t-test was used to compare numerical variables between groups. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 18.0, and a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.
