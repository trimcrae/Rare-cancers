## Abstract

## Purpose
Airway remodeling is a critical feature of asthma. Secreted protein acidic and rich in cysteine (SPARC), which plays a cardinal role in regulating cell-matrix interactions, has been implicated in various fibrotic diseases. However, the effect of SPARC in asthma remains unknown.

## Methods
We studied the expression of SPARC in human bronchial epithelial cells and serum of asthmatics as well as in the lung tissues of chronic asthma mice. The role of SPARC was examined by using a Lentivirus-mediated SPARC knockdown method in the ovalbumin (OVA)-induced asthma mice. The biological processes regulated by SPARC were identified using RNA sequencing. The function of SPARC in the remodeling process induced by transforming growth factor β1 (TGFβ1) was conducted by using SPARC small interfering RNA (siRNA) or recombinant human SPARC protein in 16HBE cells.

## Results
We observed that SPARC was up-regulated in human bronchial epithelia of asthmatics and the asthmatic mice. The levels of serum SPARC in asthmatics were also elevated and negatively correlated with the forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) to forced vital capacity ratio (FVC) (r = −0.485, P < 0.01) and FEV1 (%predicted) (r = −0.425, P = 0.001). In the chronic asthmatic mice, Lentivirus-mediated SPARC knockdown significantly decreased airway remodeling and airway hyper-responsiveness. According to gene set enrichment analysis, negatively enriched pathways found in the OVA + short hairpin-SPARC group included ECM organization and collagen formation. In the lung function studies, knockdown of SPARC by siRNA reduced the expression of remodeling-associated biomarkers, cell migration, and contraction by blocking the TGFβ1/Smad2 pathway. Addition of human recombinant SPARC protein promoted the TGFβ1-induced remodeling process, cell migration, and contraction in 16HBE cells via the TGFβ1/Smad2 pathway.

## Conclusions
Our studies provided evidence for the involvement of SPARC in the airway remodeling of asthma via the TGFβ1/Smad2 pathway.

## INTRODUCTION
Bronchial asthma is an airway disease characterized by airway inflammation, airway hyper-responsiveness (AHR), and airway remodeling, with significant contributions from both genetic and environmental factors. , Due to improvements in clinical management and treatment, including inhaled corticosteroids, bronchodilators, and biologics, many asthmatics are in good control of their symptoms. However, a significant subpopulation of patients with asthma are refractory to these existing therapies and even experience repeatedly deterioration in lung function. , , Therefore, it is valuable to develop novel therapies for prevention and treatment.
Asthma-related structural alterations in the airways, commonly known as airway remodeling, are the pathogenic hallmark of chronic asthma. The structural changes associated with airway remodeling, which can affect both small and large airways, are characterized by goblet cell metaplasia, subepithelial collagen deposition, airway angiogenesis, and smooth muscle hypertrophy/hyperplasia. Airway remodeling has been implicated in irreversible airflow limitation with consequent poor symptom control and lack of response to treatment. Although prior research has indicated that persistent airway inflammation contributes to airway remodeling in animal models, subsequent studies have shown that airway remodeling occurs even when airway inflammation is absent. It is evident that the process of airway remodeling is extremely complicated, and affects the pathological process and the therapeutic response of asthmatics. Therefore, it is necessary to focus future therapeutic strategies on changes in airway structure if we want to achieve breakthrough progress in the treatment of asthma.
Secreted protein acidic and rich in cysteine (SPARC), also known as BM-40 or osteonectin, is a matricellular protein, but do not play a structural role. SPARC regulates the interactions of cells and extracellular matrix (ECM), and has a pivotal impact on wound healing, angiogenesis, immune response. and fibrosis. , , In the lung, SPARC has been studied to be linked to cancer pathogenesis, pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary vascular remodeling and chronic airway diseases. , , , Veith et al.
 revealed that SPARC was one of consistently downregulated genes by whole genome DNA microarray analysis in a reverse-remodeling model of pulmonary hypertension, and SPARC knockdown in adult mice by adeno-associated virus dramatically improved hemodynamic and pulmonary vascular remodeling. Chang et al.
 found SPARC down-regulation attenuated alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) fibroblasts. Likewise, exogenous SPARC enhanced the expression of collagen I, collagen IV, and fibronectin by dermal fibroblasts from systemic sclerosis patients. Some studies have proven that the expression of SPARC can be induced in many cell types by transforming growth factor β1 (TGFβ1), , which is an important cytokine involved in airway remodeling. Conversely, SPARC has also been reported to regulate TGFβ expression and activity. Our previous work showed that follistatin-like protein 1 (FSTL1), a member of the SPARC family, was strongly correlated to asthma. Knocking out the FSTL1 could alleviate and even reverse the airway remodeling in asthma. However, the role of SPARC in asthmatic airway remodeling is not well understood. In the current research, our team aimed to investigate the biological role of SPARC in asthma, especially in airway remodeling.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Bioinformatic analysis
The Human Tissue Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) was used to identify the expression of SPARC in organs and tissues. GSE18965 and GSE40374 datasets obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) were applied to explore the expression of SPARC in airway epithelial brushing cells (healthy subjects and asthmatics) or M-BE cells (with or without TGFβ1 stimulation). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis was proceeded using GSEA software (v4.3.2), and the gene sets were downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database (Mouse MSigDB v2022.1. Mm). A P value of < 0.05, |normalized enrichment score (NES)| > 1, and false positive rate (FDR) < 0.25 were considered significant. GeneMANIA (http://genemania.org/) and STRING v11.5 (https://string-db.org/) were used to predict proteins related to SPARC.

## Patients and specimen sampling
Lung tissues were obtained by endobronchial biopsies. Normal bronchial epithelial samples were obtained from subjects who were suspected with intra-airway masses but no abnormal pathological findings. Bronchial epithelium tissues were obtained from asthmatics at the time of bronchoscopy. Moreover, serum specimens were obtained from patients in the outpatient clinic. We recruited 22 healthy subjects and 35 asthmatics from The First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Human Studies of our institution (ethics review number: 2021-S923), and each participant signed an informed consent. All the asthmatics were required to meet the diagnostic criteria of the Global Initiative for Asthma 2021. Peripheral blood eosinophil counts were evaluated, and serum total immunoglobulin E (IgE) levels were measured by ImmunoCAP (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). In addition, 4 mL of blood samples were collected from all participants for serum extraction. The tubes were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes. Then, the supernatant serum was collected and immediately stored in −80°C.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The cell culture supernatants were collected in 1.5-mL Eppendorf tubes and centrifuged at 3,500 rpm for 15 minutes. The supernatant was collected for the next experiment. The levels of SPARC in serum and cell supernatant were measured by commercial ELISA kits (EK1210; Boster, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Lung function test
Lung function tests were performed using a computer spirometer, and the FEV1 (%predicted) and the forced expiratory volume in one second to forced vital capacity ratio (FEV1/FVC) ratio were measured using spirometry.

## Animal experiments
Female C57BL mice (6–8 weeks) were purchased from PengYue Laboratory Animal Breeding Co., Ltd. (Jinan, China). We performed SPARC knockdown by Lentivirus (LV)-mediated delivery of SPARC-short hairpin (sh) RNA. Mice were randomly assigned to 4 groups (6 mice in each group): the control, ovalbumin (OVA) + LV2-negative control (NC), LV2-NC, and OVA + sh-SPARC groups. The mice were stimulated with intraperitoneal injection of 10 μg of OVA emulsified in 0.8 mg of aluminum hydroxide (AL[OH]3) in 0.2 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) on days 0, 7, and 14, and were then exposed to 1.5% OVA aerosol every 2 days from day 21. LVs were respectively administered to groups 2, 3, and 4 by vein injection on days 21, 33, and 45. Airway resistance of mice was measured within 24 hours after the last challenge, and the assessment methods were performed as previously described by our research team. All mice were euthanized 24 hours after the last exposure.

## Cell culture and treatment
The human bronchial epithelial cell line (16HBE) was purchased from FuHeng Biology (Shanghai, China) and incubated in Keratinocyte Medium (ScienCell, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 1% Keratinocyte Growth Supplement (ScienCell, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Cells were cultured in the humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C. The 16HBE cells were stimulated by 10 ng/mL TGFβ1 (Abbkine Scientific Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China) at various times (0, 24, 48, and 72 hours) to selected the appropriate stimulating time point.

## Cellular transfection and recombinant protein stimulation
SPARC small interfering RNA (siRNA) was used to knock down or silence the expression of SPARC. Shanghai GenePharma Biological synthesized the siRNA. The siRNAs sequences targeted SPARC were as follows:
siRNA 1:5’-GACUUCGAGAAGAACUAUATTUAUAGUUCUUCUCGAAGUCTT-3’
siRNA 2:5’-GCCACUUCUUUGCCACAAATTUUUGUGGCAAAGAAGUGGCTT-3’
siRNA 3:5’-GGAUAUCGACAAGGAUCUUTTAAGAUCCUUGUCGAUAUCCTT-3’
NC siRNA or each siRNA was mixed with Lipofectamine EndoFectinTM-Max (GeneCopoeia, Rockville, MD, USA) in Opti-MEM (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA). 16HBE cells were treated with the mixtures of Lipofectamine-siRNA when the cells were 60%–70% confluent. After 12 hours, the medium was removed, and then the cells were stimulated with respective recombinant proteins. Four groups were formed for the cell experiment: 1. si-NC group; 2. si-NC + TGFβ1 group; 3. si-SPARC group; 4. si-SPARC + TGFβ1 group. In addition, cells were divided into 4 groups to investigate the effects of human recombinant SPARC protein (PrimeGene, Shanghai, China): the control, TGFβ1, recombinant human SPARC, and TGFβ1 + recombinant human SPARC groups.

## Quantitative real-time-polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted from 16HBE cells using the RNA Fast 200 RNA Extraction kit (Fastagen Biotech, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The Merinton SMA4000 UV-VIS Spectrophotometer was used to measure the purity and concentration of RNA. RT-qPCR was performed with ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). β-actin was used as control. Primers sequences were available in .

## Western blotting analysis
The proteins of cells or lung tissues were lysed in radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer lysis buffer with phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (Servicebio, Beijing, China). Protein concentrations were quantified using BCA protein quantification kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Protein samples were segregated using sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, then transferred to 0.45-μm polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked in tris-buffered saline and 0.1% Tween 20 with 5% bovine serum albumin at room temperature for 2 hours. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight. The primary antibodies were used: anti-SPARC (1:200; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc., Santa Cruz, CA, USA), anti-GAPDH (1:5,000; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), anti-E-cadherin (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), anti-N-cadherin (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology), anti-fibronectin (1:1,000; HUABIO, Hangzhou, China), anti-p-Smad2 (1:1,000; HUABIO), anti-Smad2 (1:1,000; HUABIO), anti-collagen I (1:1,000; Abcam), anti-collagen III (1:1,000; Abcam), anti-α-SMA (1:1,000; HUABIO). The second day, the bands were incubated with goat anti-mouse or goat anti-rabbit IgG (secondary antibodies) for 1 hour at room temperature. Then the bands were visualized to chemiluminescence by using enhanced chemiluminescent reagent.

## Migration assay
Migration assay was performed using 8-μm Transwell chambers (Corning, NY, USA). 16HBE cells were treated with 0.25% Trypsin-EDTA and re-suspended in Keratinocyte Medium. 150 μL of cell suspension containing 1 × 106 cells were added on the upper chamber of Transwell plates. Then, 600 μL of Keratinocyte Medium supplemented with 1% Keratinocyte Growth Supplement were added to the wells under the chambers as a chemoattractant. The cells that migrated to the other side of membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde after 24 hours and the nonmigrated cells were mechanically removed with cotton swabs. Cells adherent to the membrane were stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 15 minutes. The number of cells that penetrated the membrane were examined under light microscopy at 100× magnification.

## Collagen gels contraction assay
Collagen solution containing 250 μL of Type I rat collagen (5 mg/mL; Corning) and 10 μL of 1 nM NaOH was mixed with 750 μL of cells for a final concentration of 1.25 mg/mL collagen. The mixture of cells/collagen was plated onto 24-well plates and kept still at room temperature for 30 minutes. Once the gel was formed, the gel was separated from the wall of the well with a needle. Then, 500 μL of Keratinocyte Medium supplemented with 1% Keratinocyte Growth Supplement were added on the top of the gels. After incubated at 37°C for 24 hours, gels were photographed and the contractility was calculated as percentages of the total original gel area.

## Lung histopathology and Immunohistochemical assay
The lung tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde 48 hours. After dehydration, tissues were embedded in paraffin, and 5-μm-thick paraffin sections were cut as slides for pathological staining like hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Periodic Acid Schiff (PAS), and Masson’s trichrome.
Immunohistochemical staining was performed using standard procedures. After deparaffinization and hydration using xylene and sequentially decreasing concentrations of ethanol, the sections were repaired in antigen repair solution and treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) to remove endogenous peroxides. Then, the sections were blocked with goat serum for 1 hour. The samples were incubated with primary antibodies (anti-SPARC [1:200; HUABIO], anti-E-cadherin [1:200; Cell Signaling Technology], anti-N-cadherin [1:200; Cell Signaling Technology], anti-fibronectin [1:200; HUABIO], anti-collagen I [1:200; BOSTER], anti-collagen III [1:200; Abcam], anti-α-SMA [1:200; HUABIO]) overnight at 4°C, followed by HRP-labelled secondary antibody at 37°C for 30 minutes. Color development was performed with 3,3-diaminobenzidine solution, and the reaction was stopped with PBS.

## Immunofluorescence staining
After deparaffinization and hydration using xylene and sequentially decreasing concentrations of ethanol, the sections were repaired in antigen repair solution and treated with 3% H2O2 to remove endogenous peroxides. Then, the sections were blocked with goat serum for 1 hour. Primary antibodies specific to the protein of interest were incubated on the slides overnight at 4°C. The next day, appropriate secondary antibodies conjugated with fluorescent dyes were incubated on the slides for 1 hour at room temperature. 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole dihydrochloride was used to stain the nuclei.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) and SPSS software (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Normal distributed data was presented as the means ± standard deviation and non-normal distributed data was present as medians with interquartile ranges. Student’s t-test (normal distributed parameters) and Mann-Whitney test (non-normal distributed parameters) were used for significant differences between 2 groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett’s Test and Tukey’s post hoc test were used for significant differences for multiple groups. The χ2 test was used for categorical measures. Correlation analysis was used Spearman analysis. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## RESULTS

## SPARC is highly expressed in airway and lung tissues
In this research, we aimed to investigate the relationship between SPARC and airway remodeling in chronic asthma. First, we searched SPARC expressions in all major tissues and organs by using the Human Protein Atlas database and discovered that SPARC was highly expressed in the lung tissues ( ). Additionally, we studied various organs and tissues of mice by western blot analysis and confirmed the high levels of SPARC in the airway and lung tissues ( ).
 

## SPARC levels are significantly increased in asthmatics and chronic asthmatic mice
SPARC levels in chronic asthma have not been characterized so far. To this end, we collected the airway epithelium biopsy sections from asthmatics and constructed OVA-induced chronic asthma mouse model. Immunohistochemical staining of SPARC showed that a significant expression of SPARC in the airway epithelium cells of asthmatics ( ). Similarly, OVA-challenged chronic asthmatic mice had significantly more SPARC proteins expression than control mice ( ). SPARC staining was prominently distributed in the airway epithelium, and alveolar epithelium was also present. Western blot analysis indicated that the levels of SPARC were increased significantly in the lung tissues of chronic asthmatic mice compared to the control group ( ). After that, we verified the above findings in the brushing specimens of asthmatics in the GEO database. Then we locked the GSE18965 dataset, including 7 healthy subjects and 9 atopic asthmatics. As shown in , the expression of SPARC in the airway epithelium was considerably higher in the asthma group in comparison to the control group.
 In addition, we detected the levels of SPARC in the serum of healthy controls and asthmatic subjects. The characteristics of participants are shown in . According to the ELISA results, the serum levels of SPARC in asthmatics were significantly higher than in healthy controls ( ). Moreover, the serum levels of SPARC were negatively correlated to FEV1 (%predicted) (r = −0.425, P = 0.001) ( ) and FEV1/FVC (r = −0.485, P < 0.01) ( ). Then, we divided asthmatics into 2 groups according to whether eosinophils in peripheral blood exceeded 300 cells/μL. No significant difference in the SPARC levels between the high and low peripheral blood eosinophil groups ( ). Moreover, asthmatics were divided into the high and low total IgE groups according to if the level of total IgE exceeded 100 IU/mL. Serum SPARC levels had no significant difference between the high and low total IgE groups ( ).

## SPARC is needed for airway remodeling in the OVA-induced chronic asthma mouse model
Considering the increased expression of SPARC in asthmatic mice, we verified the effects of SPARC by lentiviral-packaged SPARC knockdown approach in chronic asthmatic mice. The chronic asthma mouse model was constructed following the flowchart in . The lentiviral was injected to C57BL/6 mice through the tail vein for silence SPARC. IHC and immunofluorescence staining confirmed that SPARC was markedly inhibited in the OVA-challenged chronic asthmatic model ( ). The analysis of lung function in mice showed that asthmatic mice exhibited evident exacerbated AHR, while the administration of SPARC shRNA could alleviate airway resistance in the OVA-induced asthmatic mice ( ). We carried out various staining of lung tissue sections. PAS staining showed that goblet cell proliferation and mucus production increased in OVA-induced asthmatic mice compared with control mice, and this phenomenon was significantly improved after knocking down SPARC ( ). Similarly, Masson trichrome staining demonstrated that remarkable fibrotic lesions around the airway were exhibited in the mice exposed to OVA. However, these effects were significantly attenuated after tail vein injection of lentivirus ( ).
 In addition, we performed H&E staining to explore the role of SPARC in airway inflammation. Unfortunately, the LV-mediated SPARC knockdown group and the OVA-challenged asthmatic mice group showed similar inflammatory cell infiltration ( ). indicating that knockdown of SPARC could relieve mucus hypersecretion, AHR and airway remodeling in OVA-induced asthmatic mice, which were independent of airway inflammation.

## Biological processes mediated by SPARC in chronic asthma mice
In order to explore the transcriptome changes caused by SPARC knockdown, we extracted RNA from the lung tissues of OVA + LV2-NC and OVA + sh-SPARC groups, and performed RNA sequencing. Differential gene expression analysis identified numerous genes significantly between the OVA + sh-SPARC and OVA + LV2-NC groups from the transcriptional profiles. As shown in the volcano plot, 618 up-regulated and 673 down-regulated genes were revealed ( ). Through the GSEA using the reactome gene sets, negatively enriched pathways found in the OVA + sh-SPARC group include ECM organization, collagen formation ( ). Simultaneously, we found that the expression of some proteins related to airway remodeling in asthma, such as COL1A1 and COL3A1, was significantly down-regulated in SPARC knockdown group ( ). These transcriptional profiling results suggested that SPARC may play an important role in airway remodeling by affecting ECM.
 After that, we examined the expression of ECM proteins associated with airway remodeling in asthmatic mice. Western blot demonstrated that the levels of remodeling markers (fibronectin, collagens I and III) of asthmatic mice were significantly higher than that of control mice, while these indicators were significantly reduced after knockdown of SPARC ( ). Immunohistochemical staining also has the same trend ( ).
Considering that epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is an important pathological mechanism of airway remodeling in asthma, we also explored the effects of SPARC on EMT. The western blot results exhibited that OVA-sensitized asthmatic mice displayed a considerable elevation of N-cadherin and α-SMA, as well as a decrease of E-cadherin, and these alterations could be partially reversed in the OVA + sh-SPARC group ( ). Likewise, Immunohistochemistry showed similar trends ( ).

## SPARC is up-regulated in bronchial epithelial cells with TGFβ1
TGFβ1 has been identified as a main mediator of airway modeling in asthma and was a well described inducer of ECM deposition and EMT. In order to investigate whether SPARC was involved in TGFβ1- induced remodeling and EMT models, we conducted bioinformatics analyses by exploiting data from the GEO database. We selected a microarray dataset, GSE40374, and found up-regulated genes in the human bronchial epithelial cell line with/without recombinant TGFβ1 treatment. As shown in the volcano plot, SPARC expression is significantly up-regulated between the TGFβ1-stimulated group and the control group ( ). Besides, protein-protein interaction network showed functional networks between the SPARC protein and other proteins such as COL1A1, COL3A1 and FN1 ( ). We further verified these observations in 16HBE cells in vitro. 16HBE cells were treated with TGFβ1 for variable times to imitate the process of EMT and remodeling. The results from ELISA showed that the SPARC levels in the cell culture supernatant after TGFβ1 stimulation were significantly higher compared to the control group ( ). The results from the RT-qPCR and western blot showed that a decreased expression of E-cadherin accompanied by up-regulation of N-cadherin, fibronectin, and SPARC ( ). Combined with the variation in those indicators, we chose 48-hour time point for subsequent experiments.
 

## Silencing SPARC reverses TGFβ1-induced EMT and remodeling by TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling pathway in 16HBE cells
To determine the effect of SPARC in the process of EMT and remodeling in 16HBE cells, we silenced the expression of SPARC by transfecting 16HBE cells with siRNA. A remarkably reduced level of SPARC protein and mRNA was observed for the siRNA #1, #2 and #3 by Western blot and PT-qPCR ( ). Statistical analysis suggested that siRNA #1 displayed the highest transfection efficiency. As shown in , after SPARC siRNA1 transfection and TGFβ1 stimulation in 16HBE cells, the E-cadherin protein levels were up-regulated in the si-SPARC + TGFβ1 group compared to the si-NC + TGFβ1 group, while the protein levels of N-cadherin, fibronectin, and p-Smad2 were lower in the 2 above groups. Additionally, the migration assay showed that the cell migration ability was enhanced after TGFβ1 stimulation, while this effect was weakened after knockdown of SPARC ( ). Similarly, the cell contraction test also showed the same trend ( ). Taken together, SPARC appeared to be related to EMT and remodeling induced by TGFβ1.
 

## Exogenous SPARC enhances TGFβ1-induced EMT and remodeling by TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling in airway epithelial cells
We had verified that inferring the expression of SPARC mitigated the process of EMT and remodeling triggered by TGFβ1 in airway epithelial cells, we next added exogenous recombinant human SPARC protein to 16HBE cells with or without TGFβ1 stimulation to further explore the role of SPARC in EMT and remodeling in airway epithelial cells. As illustrated in , the TGFβ1-triggered EMT and remodeling processes were significantly aggravated upon exogenous SPARC treatment for the levels of E-cadherin were decreased and N-cadherin, Fibronectin and p-Smad2 were increased compared to TGFβ1 exposure alone. Furthermore, exogenous SPARC promoted TGFβ1-induced cell migration ( ) and cell contraction ( ). These findings collectively highlight that SPARC is critical for the early stage of the remodeling process in a TGFβ1-dependent manner.
 

## DISCUSSION
As a hallmark pathological process of asthma, airway remodeling is involved in an irreversible loss in lung function. In this study, we demonstrated that SPARC was significantly expressed in the airway epithelial cells and serum of patients with asthma and the lung tissues of the OVA-induced chronic asthma murine model. In vitro, TGFβ1 induced the mesenchymal phenotype and fibrosis in 16HBE cells; meanwhile, SPARC significantly increased. Furthermore, knocking down SPARC reversed the expression of TGFβ1-induced EMT markers and remodeling indicators in 16HBE cells. By contrary, using recombinant human SPARC proteins could promote the effects induced by TGFβ1, including EMT markers and remodeling-related proteins expression. The results of in vivo experiments further validated the above results. In vivo, intravenous silencing of SPARC prevented OVA-challenged asthmatic airway remodeling. Our research first indicated that suppression of SPARC may ameliorate OVA-induced asthmatic airway remodeling via the TGFβ1/Smad2 pathway, and SPARC could be an effective target for finding therapeutic strategies against airway remodeling.
SPARC is a matricellular protein that contributes to ECM remodeling, but generally is not incorporated into insoluble ECM. Elevated expression of SPARC has been observed in a variety of tissues, including heart, lung, liver, eye and kidney. The present results showed that SPARC was reliably detected in serum and statistically increased in the serum of asthmatic patients. Meanwhile, correlation analysis indicated that there was a strong correlation between serum SPARC levels and lung function indices. These results implied that the SPARC was related to fixed airflow limitation. To this end, we constructed a chronic asthma mice model, and a significant elevation of SPARC had been detected in the lung tissues of the mice model of asthma. Hence, we carried out in vitro and in vivo experiments to validate the prediction that SPARC may be involved in airway remodeling of chronic asthma.
TGFβ1, a cytokine elevated in the airway of asthmatics, plays a “master switch” role in airway remodeling in asthma. In patients with asthma, it contributes to the remodeling of airway, epithelium alterations, subepithelial fibrosis, remodeling of the smooth muscles lining the airway, and microvascular abnormalities. , Some previous reports demonstrated that SPARC activity overlaps with TGFβ1 in a few of cell types, such as human dermal fibroblasts and human lung fibroblasts. , Tiago Carvalheiro, et.al reported that exogenous SPARC was a vital pro-fibrogenesis factor promoting the pathology driving systemic sclerosis via TGFβ pathway. The activation of collagen I, collagen IV, and fibronectin induced by exogenous SPARC was abrogated via inhibiting TGFβ signaling. Shibata and Ishiyama found that SPARC promoted the secretion of H2O2 in human fetal lung fibroblast by TGFβ and played an important role in epithelial damage in the IPF lung. In melanoma cells, SPARC triggered EMT-related features, and overexpression of SPARC led to down-regulation of E-cadherin, which may correlate with induction of Snail. Considering airway epithelial had been regard as an “initiator” in airway remodeling of asthma and TGFβ1 produced from epithelial cells was assumed as an important contributor to the pathology of asthma, we detected the role of SPARC in human airway epithelial cells. Our data from in vitro indicated that SPARC was significantly upregulated in human airway epithelial cells induced by TGFβ1. In the absence of SPARC, the EMT process induced by TGFβ1 was partially reversed and ECM deposition was attenuated though the TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling. Besides, exogenous SPARC significantly enhanced the TGFβ1 role in inducing EMT process though the TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling. Moreover, TGFβ1 could promote the secretion of SPARC in 16HBE cells. These findings suggested that SPARC may mediate the EMT process, and ECM production and deposition induced by TGFβ1 though the TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling. Our results demonstrated the SPARC’s cooperative function in the remodeling process induced by TGFβ1 and showed that SPARC and TGFβ1 interact in a feedback loop that contributed to remodeling. However, SPARC may act as a tumor suppressor for some cancer types, such as ovarian and neuroblastomas. Thus, it can be seen that the regulatory effect of SPARC is related to the immune status of the organism.
We carried out SPARC knockdown in mice using LV-mediated SPARC-shRNA delivery in order to evaluate the in vivo applicability of our findings. This method demonstrated that SPARC knockdown attenuated airway remodeling in asthma in vivo. We showed that OVA-challenged shRNA-SPARC mice had less airway fibrosis, goblet cell hyperplasia and airway hyperresponsiveness. However, no significant change was observed in airway inflammation, suggesting that SPARC knockdown was primarily alleviated asthmatic airway remodeling. In the existing literature, SPARC has a bi-directional effect on airway inflammation. , , The role of SPARC in regulating inflammation may depend on the source of SPARC-expressing cells. And the function of SPARC in airway inflammation in asthma will be explored in future studies. These results in airway remodeling were consistent with the findings from our transcriptional profiling experiments. Through the GSEA from the data of RNA-seq, we found that negatively enriched pathways included ECM organization and collagen formation, pointing that SPARC silencing experiment in vivo may attenuate the development of airway remodeling through participating in ECM remodeling. Recent studies have already described the function of SPARC in ECM deposition. SPARC consists of 3 modular domains and the C-terminal of SPARC can bind with collagens and cell surfaces. Bradshaw et al.
 found that the SPARC Null TAC mice had decreased cardiac diastolic stiffness and the absence of SPARC diminished the changes in ECM fibrillar collagen induced by pressure-overload. Collectively, these findings suggested that SPARC may provide a novel therapeutic target for airway remodeling of asthma. In conclusion, our study is the first to demonstrate the involvement of SPARC in airway remodeling of asthma and that SPARC may regulate ECM deposition and EMT through the TGFβ1/Smad2 signaling pathway. The findings provide a possible drug target for the treatment of asthma.
