## Graphical abstract
Cigarette smoke decreases DNA methylation level of GADD45B promoter in bronchial epithelial cell, thereby promotes GADD45B expression. Subsequently, GADD45B upregulation can directly promote release of inflammatory cytokines through phosphorylation of p38, and can also promote cell senescence by interacting with FOS, which furtherly aggravates inflammation via phosphorylating p38.
 Keywords: GADD45B, Inflammation, Cellular senescence, DNA methylation, Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

## Highlights
•GADD45B expression was increased in the bronchial epithelium of COPD patients and reversely associated with lung function.
•In vivo and in vitro experiments furtherly demonstrated that GADD45B facilitated inflammatory response and cellular senescence in bronchial epithelial cells.
•Mechanically, GADD45B-facilitated inflammation was directly mediated by p38 phosphorylation, while GADD45B interacted with FOS to promote cellular senescence.
•Cigarette smoke-induced GADD45B upregulation in COPD was partially mediated by DNA hypomethylation.
•GADD45B will serve as a promising intervention target for COPD.

## Abstract

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a heterogeneous disease typically characterized by chronic airway inflammation, with emerging evidence highlighting the driving role of cellular senescence-related lung aging. Accelerated lung aging and inflammation mutually reinforce each other, creating a detrimental cycle that contributes to disease progression. Growth arrest and DNA damage-inducible (GADD45) family has been reported to involve in multiple biological processes, including inflammation and senescence. However, the role of GADD45 family in COPD remains elusive.

## Objectives
To investigate the role and mechanism of GADD45 family in COPD pathogenesis.

## Methods
Expressions of GADD45 family were evaluated by bioinformatic analysis combined with detections in clinical specimens. The effects of GADD45B on inflammation and senescence were investigated via constructing cell model with siRNA transfection or overexpression lentivirus infection and animal model with Gadd45b knockout. Targeted bisulfite sequencing was performed to probe the influence of DNA methylation in GADD45B expression in COPD.

## Results
GADD45B expression was significantly increased in COPD patients and strongly associated with lung function, whereas other family members presented no changes. GADD45B upregulation was confirmed in mice exposed by cigarette smoke (CS) and HBE cells treated by CS extract as well. Moreover, experiments involving bidirectional modulation of GADD45B expression in HBE cells further substantiated its positive regulatory role in inflammatory response and cellular senescence. Mechanically, GADD45B-facilitated inflammation was directly mediated by p38 phosphorylation, while GADD45B interacted with FOS to promote cellular senescence in a p38 phosphorylation-independent manner. Furthermore, Gadd45b deficiency remarkably alleviated inflammation and senescence of lungs in CS-exposed mice, as well as improved emphysema and lung function. Eventually, in vivo and vitro experiments demonstrated that GADD45B overexpression was partially mediated by CS-induced DNA hypomethylation.

## Conclusion
Our findings have shed light on the impact of GADD45B in the pathogenesis of COPD, thereby offering a promising target for intervention in clinical settings.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a heterogeneous pulmonary condition characterized by chronic respiratory symptoms and irreversible airflow limitation, which has emerged as the third leading cause of mortality worldwide and poses a major threat to human health , . Cigarette smoke (CS) has long been considered as the primary risk factor resulting in COPD pathogenesis, where various pathophysiological abnormalities exert pivotal pathogenic role including inflammatory response and oxidative stress injury, as well as imbalance between protease and antiprotease . However, these researches cannot fully elucidate the exact mechanism underlying COPD up to now.
Bronchial epithelium, as the first line of defense against numerous inhaled detrimental agents, plays a fundamental role in COPD onset . Exposure to CS can affect phenotypic changes in bronchial epithelial cells and initiate typical chronic inflammation injury, partially mediated by epigenetic regulation like DNA methylation , . Recently, accumulating evidence indicates that CS can induce cellular senescence in the bronchial epithelium, as supported by the upregulation of senescent markers p16 and p21. This process contributes to accelerated lung aging and subsequently promotes the progression of COPD , . Senescent cells remain metabolic activity and potentially exhibit phenotypic shift to secretory profile, featured as increased production of inflammatory factors and interleukins . This change in senescent cells, termed as senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), not only reshapes its microenvironment but also affects surrounding cells , . Although cellular senescence and SASP have been shown to be associated with chronic inflammation of COPD, the molecular mechanism therein remains under investigation .
The members of growth arrest and DNA damage-inducible (GADD45) family display a fundamental role in the integration of cellular response to diversified environmental stressors in mammals , and participate in regulation of multiple biological processes, such as cellular survival and death , cellular senescence and cycle arrest , as well as inflammatory response . Previous studies have indicated that these proteins display a critical function in various diseases and are promised to become intervening target, including hematopoietic malignancy , obesity , neuromuscular disease , and lung injury . Nonetheless, the expressions and roles of GADD45 family proteins in COPD have not been explored yet.
In the current study, we aimed to determine whether and which of GADD45 family members are differentially expressed in COPD and elucidate their role in promoting bronchial epithelial inflammation and senescence. Our preliminary findings from lung tissue specimens of human suggested that increased expression of GADD45B may play a pathogenic role in COPD development. To validate this hypothesis, cell and mouse models were constructed to confirm that CS upregulated the expression of GADD45B in bronchial epithelial cells through DNA hypomethylation, subsequently promoting inflammation and cellular senescence. These findings may facilitate a better understanding for the pathogenesis of COPD and provide a viable target for its prevention and control.

## Materials and methods

## Bioinformatic analysis
COPD-related gene expression microarray dataset (GSE38974) was obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds), including 9 healthy controls and 23 COPD subjects . Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened out with a log foldchange (|logFC|) of >0.94 and a P-value of <0.05. The threshold of 0.94 was determined using the formula: mean(abs(logFC)) + 2*sd(abs(logFC). Pearson’s correlation analysis was carried out to estimate the associations of GADD45 family genes with inflammation-related or aging-related genes. Bioinformatic analysis was performed using R studio (version 4.1.2).
Human tissue sample single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) of individuals with and without COPD was reanalyzed with the data previously published by Adams et al. (available in GEO database as GSE136831). One COPD sample without cigarette-smoking history was excluded. Control samples from individuals <40 years of age were excluded to ensure that the control and COPD samples were age-matched. After the quality control of data for analysis, expression values were normalized to 10,000 transcripts per cell and log-transformed using a pseudocount of 1. Clustering and differential analysis was performed with the Seurat package (v.4.4.0) in R, and marker genes for each cell type were identified by Seurat FindAllMarkers. The expression distribution of GADD45B was mapped in Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), and expression levels between COPD and control cells were compared using Wilcoxon rank sum test.

## Clinical subjects
All participants and lung tissue specimens were prospectively enrolled and collected into this study from Tongji Hospital, Wuhan, China, between 2021 and 2023. All subjects were categorized into three groups according to the smoking history and lung function: non-smokers, smokers and COPD. COPD was diagnosed based on the criteria of Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) 2021. Age- and gender-matched non-smokers and smokers without COPD were recruited as controls. Participants were excluded when they had other chronic respiratory diseases (such as interstitial lung disease and asthma), active tuberculosis, or recently treated with inhaled or oral steroids.

## Pulmonary function test
Forced vital capacity (FVC) and forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) were obtained from the flow-volume curve using an appropriately calibrated spirometer (Jaeger, Wurzburg, Germany) before and 20 min after β-agonist (salbutamol) inhalation. Three technically acceptable measurements were taken for each patient, and the highest value was selected and expressed as a percentage of the reference value. According to prediction equations recommended by the ATS/ERS Task Force 2005, the predicted FEV1 was calculated using the formula: 4.30 × height in meters-0.029 × age-2.49.

## Mouse model
Gadd45b knockout (Gadd45b KO) mice and wild-type (WT) littermates were procured from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). All mice were housed in a specific pathogen-free facility of Tongji hospital under 12-hour light/dark cycle at about 23℃ with clean water and food.
All mice (8–12 weeks old) were exposed to room air or CS in a chamber for approximately 3 h daily (12 cigarettes/45 min/time, 4 times a day) and 5 days a week for 6 months with Marlboro Red cigarettes. During CS modelling, the following parameters were required: the concentration of CO was kept at 600–1200 ppm, O2 concentration >18 % and the concentration of total particulate matter at 80–120 mg/m3. The mice were randomly classified into four groups with 5–6 mice per group, including Air + WT, CS + WT, Air + Gadd45b KO and CS + Gadd45b KO. In addition, experimental mice, when the exposure to room air or CS reached up to 4 months, were intraperitoneally administrated with 5-Azacytidine (5-Aza, an inhibitor of DNA methylation, 2.5 mg/kg) or equivalent-volume PBS every other day for the remaining 2 months. The groups covered PBS + Air, PBS + CS, 5-Aza + Air and 5-Aza + CS, with 4 mice per group. After mouse model was completed, all mice were anesthetized intraperitoneally with 1 % sodium pentobarbital for 100 mg/kg. The lung function of mice, including forced expiratory volume at 0.05 s (FEV0.05s) and forced vital capacity (FVC), was measured using the FlexiVent system (SCIREQ, Canada). The supernatant of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected for detections of inflammatory cytokines, and the total cells were counted and classified for identifying different cell types with Liu’s staining (Baso, Zhuhai, China). The left lungs were subjected to 4 % paraformaldehyde for fixation and then paraffin-embedded, while the right lungs were preserved in a freezer of −80℃ for subsequent experiments.

All experiments involving animals were conducted according to the ethical policies and procedures approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Tongji Hospital (TJH-202105013).

## Reagents and antibodies
Cigarette smoke extract (CSE) was prepared as stated previously . SB202190, T-5224 and 5-Aza were procured from MedChem Express (Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA). Lipofectamine 3000 was procured from Invitrogen (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Magnetic beads were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). DNA extraction kit was obtained from TIANGEN Biotech (Beijing, China). SA-β-gal staining kit was procured from Solarbio (Beijing, China). ELISA kits of human IL-6, IL-8 and mouse IL-6, KC, IL-1β were obtained from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA). ELISA kit of human IL-1β was purchased from Dakewe Bio-engineering (Shenzhen, China). Antibodies against GADD45B was procured from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA) or Affinity Biosciences (Cincinnati, OH, USA). Antibodies against β-Actin, P16, P21, FOS, IgG and GAPDH were acquired from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). Antibodies against Flag, p-p38 and p38 were procured from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA).

## Cell culture and treatment
Human bronchial epithelial cell HBE4-E6/E7 (ATCC, CRL-2078) was cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin/streptomycin in a humidified incubator at 37 °C and 5 % CO2. HBE cells were treated with CSE at different concentrations and durations. In overexpression experiments, HBE cells were infected with lentiviral vector (Negative Control or GADD45B-overexpressing) from the OBiO Biotechnology (Shanghai, China), or transfected with cDNA vector (Negative Control or FOS expression vector) from WZ Biosciences (Shandong, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In knockdown experiments, HBE cells were transfected with small interfering RNA (siRNA) (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) targeting GADD45B (5′-GAAUUAUAGAGACAAUCUATT-3′), P21 (5′-GUGGACAGCGAGCAGCUGAUU-3′), P16 (5′-CGCACCGAAUAGUUACGG UTT-3′) or negative control using lipofectamine 3000 following the manufacturer’s instructions. The efficiency of overexpression or knockdown was determined using real-time qPCR or Western blot. In pharmacological experiment, HBE cells were treated with 10 μM SB202190 or 20 μM T-5224 for 24 h for exploring pathway mechanisms. HBE cells were pretreated with 5 μM 5-Aza for 1 h before CSE stimulation for investigating if GADD45B was regulated by DNA methylation.

## Histological and immunostaining analysis
Mouse lung sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE). The degree of peribronchial inflammation and mean linear intercept (MLI) was estimated based on at least 5 random photomicroscopic images of HE-stained sections per mouse. The scoring of peribronchial inflammation was conducted according to previously described method . MLI value was calculated to assess the extent of emphysema referring to previous study . Briefly, cross-reference lines were formed right in the middle of each microscopic field and the total length (L) and number of alveolar septa (NS) were measured: MLI = L/NS. As previously reported , collagen deposition around airways was evaluated by Masson staining of mouse lung sections and semi-quantitated using ImageJ. For immunohistochemical experiments, paraffin-embedded sections of human or mouse lungs were stained with anti-GADD45B antibody (1:500) or anti-FOS (1:200) complying with the manufacturer’s instructions. The semi-quantitative analysis for immunohistochemical images against GADD45B were performed independently by two experienced researchers, and subjected to the method as previously reported . Briefly, the semi-quantitative results were calculated based on the score of staining intensity (Negative, 0; Light yellow, 1; Brown, 2; and Dark brown, 3) multiplied by the score of staining scope (the ratio of positive cells to counted cells around airways, ratio ≤ 25 %, 1; ratio of 26 %–50 %, 2; ratio of 51 %–75 %, 3; and ratio ≥ 75 %, 4). For immunofluorescent experiments, the human lung sections were subjected to the incubation with anti-GADD45B (1:50, #DF-2375, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), anti-P16 (1:100, #10833-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) or anti-P21 (1:100, #10355-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), while the mouse lung sections were to the incubation with anti-P16 (1:200, #10833-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), anti-P21 (1:200, #10355-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) or anti-α-SMA antibody (1:100, #19245, Cell Signaling Technology, USA). And then the fluorescent secondary antibodies of corresponding species were utilized for staining (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Image acquisition was completed using a Nikon Spot image system (United States). The semi-quantitative analysis was conducted using ImageJ.

## Real-time qPCR
Total RNA was isolated from lung tissues or HBE cells using Trizol (Takara, Japan), and reversely transcribed with the cDNA RT-PCR Kit (Takara, Japan). Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was then performed using TB GreenR Premix Ex TaqTM II (Takara, Japan) on BioRad CFX384 (Bio-Rad, CA, United States). Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method with β-actin as endogenous control. The detailed information for primers was displayed in .

## Western blot
Proteins were extracted from lung tissues or cells using RIPA lysis buffer containing phosphatase inhibitors, separated by 10 % or 12 % SDS-PAGE, transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes, incubated with primary antibodies overnight, and detected using chemiluminescent procedure (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) following the instructions of manufacturer. The intensity of individual band was quantified by ImageJ.

## ELISA
The levels of IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β in HBE cell supernatants or the concentrations of IL-6, KC and IL-1β in mouse BALF were determined respectively with ELISA kits following the manufacturer’s instructions.

## SA-β-gal assay
HBE cells or frozen sections of mouse lung were stained with SA-β-gal staining kit following the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, HBE cells or frozen lung sections were washed with PBS and fixed in β-galactosidase fixation solution for 15  min, stained in SA-β-gal staining solution overnight at 37 °C, and were captured for images with a Nikon Spot image system (United States). The percentage of staining-positive cells was determined from the average value of at least 3 random fields.

## RNA sequence and data analysis
After HBE cells were infected with lentivirus (GADD45B-overexpression or negative control), total RNA was immediately isolated and extracted. RNA-sequence was performed at Wuhan Benagen Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China). As described previously, DEGs were determined based on the parameters of |logFC| > 0.71 and P-value <0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis for the DEGs was performed using clusterProfiler version 3.8. Furthermore, all DEGs were uploaded to online STRING website for identifying genes potentially interacted with GADD45B (https://cn.string-db.org/). The Venn diagram was plotted online (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/).

## Immunoprecipitation assay (IP)
Whole-cell lysate from HBE cells with lentivirus infection was used to perform immunoprecipitation following the instructions of manufacturer. Firstly, the lysates were incubated with anti-Flag or anti-IgG with rotation at 4 °C overnight to form immunocomplexes. Magnetic beads were then used for immunoprecipitation with the complexes at 4 °C for 2 h. Finally, the bound protein was eluted from the magnetic beads and further detected by western blot.

## Targeted bisulfite sequencing (TBS)
Gene-specific DNA methylation was evaluated by a next generation sequencing-based bisulfite sequencing PCR (BSP) according to the previous method . The BSP primers of human GADD45B or mouse Gadd45b were designed using the online MethPrimer software and listed in . Barcoded libraries from all samples were sequenced on the Illumina platform at Acegen Technology Co., Ltd (Shenzhen, China). Methylation level was defined as the read counts that supported methylated C at a particular site divided by the total read counts that covered the site.

## Statistical analysis
Data were expressed as mean ± SD unless otherwise stated. Statistical difference was assessed using Student’s t-test for two groups or one-way ANOVA for multiple comparisons. The correlations were analyzed by Pearson correlation. All statistical analysis and graphic generation were completed by GraphPad Prism 8 Software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, United States). A two-sided P-value of <0.05 was deemed as statistically significant.

## Results

## GADD45B was upregulated in bronchial epithelium of COPD patients and model mice
In this study, we first conducted a bioinformatic analysis for a human dataset of COPD (GSE38974) to investigate the expression alterations of GADD45 family members in COPD. A total of 868 DEGs were identified with 422 upregulations and 446 downregulations ( a, ). Thereinto, GADD45B was markedly increased in COPD patients compared to healthy controls, and GADD45A upregulation was indistinctive despite of significant difference, whereas GADD45G presented no differential expression ( b). The correlation analyses indicated a positive association between GADD45A and GADD45B with the expression of inflammatory or senescent genes, such as IL-6, IL-8, IL-1β, and CDKN1A ( c, ), suggesting their potential involvement in the pathogenesis of COPD. In addition, we performed a secondary analysis for publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing on COPD lung tissues, and found the upregulation of GADD45B in multiple cell populations including ciliated cells, the most common cells of airway epithelium ( ).
 To furtherly validate the expressions and roles of GADD45 family members in COPD, we collected 62 subjects in clinic, including 20 non-smokers, 20 smokers without COPD, and 22 COPD patients. The detailed clinical information of all participants was shown in . In general, these three groups of subjects were matched for age, gender and body mass index (BMI). The smoking index of smoker and COPD were prominently higher than non-smoker. FEV1% predicted and FEV1/FVC of COPD patients were lower than smokers and non-smokers as expected. Subsequently, qPCR results demonstrated that GADD45B expressions were successively elevated in non-smoker, smoker and COPD, while GADD45A and GADD45G showed no marked changes ( d–f). Meanwhile, the mRNA levels of GADD45B in COPD patients were reversely correlated with FEV1% predicted ( g), which suggested the potential role of GADD45B in the decline of lung function and development of COPD. In addition, western blot assay and immunohistochemical staining confirmed the same increased expression of GADD45B in protein level as mRNA level, and the expression alterations primarily occurred in bronchial epithelium ( h, i). As indicated by the results of immunofluorescent co-staining in , cells with GADD45B upregulation showed an increased expression in senescent markers (P16 and P21) likewise in the airways of COPD patients, which furtherly confirmed the possible connection between GADD45B and epithelial cell senescence.
Consistent with detections in human subjects, Gadd45b expressions were significantly enhanced in CS-exposed mice than Air-exposed mice both at mRNA level and protein level ( j, k). Immunohistochemical staining also indicated that Gadd45b upregulation was principally located in bronchial epithelium ( l). In view of the predominant expression of GADD45B in bronchial epithelium, CSE-treated HBE cells were utilized to investigate the regulatory role of GADD45B thereinto. By detecting the GADD45B levels in HBE cells treated by varying concentrations and durations of CSE, we observed a significant upregulation of GADD45B particularly following treatment with 5 % CSE for 24 h ( m, n). Taken together, these results indicated that bronchial epithelium in COPD exhibited an upregulation of GADD45B.

## GADD45B facilitated inflammation and cellular senescence in CSE-induced HBE cells
To specify the function of GADD45B in bronchial epithelium, we first performed an RNA-sequencing analysis for HBE cells infected with lentivirus of GADD45B overexpression or GFP. 296 upregulated DEGs and 114 downregulated DEGs were screened out ( a, ). Similar to results of bioinformatic analysis, these DEGs were enriched in biological processes associated with inflammation and cellular senescence, including inflammatory response, cytokine-mediated signaling pathway, cytokine activity, regulation of cell cycle and aging ( b, ). Subsequently, we constructed stable-infected HBE cells of GADD45B overexpression via lentivirus and transient-transfected HBE cells of GADD45B knockdown through siRNA respectively, to validate the effect of GADD45B in inflammation and senescence of HBE cells.
 In experiments of GADD45B overexpression, we primarily ensured the GADD45B overexpression in HBE cells ( c and a). ELISA assay revealed that overexpressing GADD45B promoted the release of inflammatory cytokines containing IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β from HBE cells with or without CSE stimulation ( d). The results of qPCR also supported the positive regulation of GADD45B in inflammation at mRNA level ( b). Moreover, we discovered that upregulating GADD45B significantly enhanced P16 and P21 expressions, two critical senescence-related molecules, in HBE cells regardless of CSE treatment ( e–g). Consistently, SA-β-gal staining showed that HBE cells with GADD45B overexpression had higher percentage of positive cells, especially after CSE addition ( h–i).
For knockdown experiments, the reduced expression of GADD45B was determined in HBE cells ( j and c). And then, ELISA and qPCR examinations indicated that suppressing GADD45B could obviously alleviate CSE-induced elevations in expressions and secretions of IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β ( k and d). As expected, decreased GADD45B expression mitigated P16 and P21 expressions in CSE-treated HBE cells ( l–n), as well as the percentage of positive cells stained with β-galactosidase ( o, p). Collectively, these findings clarified that GADD45B could promote inflammation and cellular senescence in bronchial epithelial cells.

## P38 phosphorylation mediated GADD45B-induced inflammation not cellular senescence in HBE cells
P38 MAPK signaling pathway was reported to closely associate with GADD45 family proteins , however, whether p38 mediated GADD45B-related biological processes in bronchial epithelial cells remains unknown. Firstly, we found the positive regulation of GADD45B in p38 phosphorylation, given that overexpressing GADD45B increased p-p38/p38 while reducing GADD45B expression decreased p-p38/p38 in HBE cells ( a–b). Afterwards, administration with inhibitor of p38 phosphorylation could markedly alleviate inflammatory cytokine release in HBE cells with GADD45B overexpression covering IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β ( c). Interestingly, this inhibitor didn’t affect the expressions of P16 and P21( d), which suggested that GADD45B promoted cellular senescence in HBE cells independent of p38 phosphorylation. Furthermore, we were surprised to discover that knocking down either P16 or P21 in HBE cells could distinctly reduce the phosphorylated level of p38 with or without CSE stimulation, as well as the production of inflammatory factors ( ). Therefore, these data demonstrated that GADD45B could directly promote inflammation through p38 phosphorylation or indirectly regulate it via cellular senescence-induced p38 phosphorylation in HBE cells.
 

## GADD45B interacted with FOS to promote cellular senescence in HBE cells
To clarify the mechanism underlying GADD45B-induced cellular senescence, further analysis of RNA-sequence data was carried out. Through protein–protein interaction analysis and screening of core gene using Venn diagram, we identified FOS potentially interacted with GADD45B to promote cellular senescence ( e, f). IP assay was performed to prove the interaction between GADD45B and FOS in HBE cells ( g). Bidirectional intervention experiments against GADD45B expression unraveled that GADD45B positively regulated FOS expression in CSE-exposed HBE cells ( h, i). To determine whether FOS mediated GADD45B-induced cellular senescence, we then conducted the corresponding recovery experiments targeting FOS expression. We confirmed the most significant inhibitory role for FOS expression in HBE cells with administration of 20 μM T-5224 (a specific FOS inhibitor) ( j). Western blot analysis indicated that suppressing FOS expression effectively mitigated GADD45B-induced senescence in HBE cells, characterized by the recovery of P16 and P21 ( k). SA-β-gal staining supported the recovery effect of FOS inhibitor on cellular senescence likewise ( l, m). In addition, experiments with FOS overexpression confirmed that FOS elevation was most significant in HBE cells transfected with 2 μg/mL FOS-overexpressing cDNA vector and could furtherly aggravate GADD45B-induced cellular senescence, as supported by increased expressions in P16 and P21 and SA-β-gal positive-staining cells ( ). Accordingly, it can be concluded that GADD45B facilitated cellular senescence via interacting with FOS and promoting its expression in HBE cells.

## Gadd45b deficiency improved lung function, lung destruction and inflammation, and bronchial epithelial senescence in CS-exposed mice
To explore the in vivo role of GADD45B in COPD, we established six-month passive smoking models of WT or Gadd45b KO mice. By analyzing the genomic DNA of mouse tail, the genotypes were confirmed ( a). Upon model completed, Gadd45b expressions between groups were determined respectively at mRNA and protein level ( b, c). Lung function results showed that CS-exposed WT mice had lower FEV0.05s and FEV0.05s/FVC compared to Air-exposed WT mice, while these two parameters in CS-exposed Gadd45b KO mice were markedly higher than CS-exposed WT mice ( d). Additionally, histopathological sections indicated that the extent of lung destruction was more severe and the MLI was larger in CS-exposed WT mice in comparison to Air-exposed WT mice. Conversely, these indices showed improvement in CS-exposed Gadd45b KO mice ( e, f). Consequently, the results of in vivo experiments indicated that depletion of Gadd45b could significantly ameliorate lung function and emphysema induced by CS exposure.
 Moreover, we evaluated the effect of Gadd45b in airway and lung inflammation by BALF cell count, ELISA, inflammation score of HE sections and qPCR examinations. Firstly, the total number of BALF cells, macrophages, neutrophils and lymphocytes in CS + Gadd45b KO group were distinctly decreased compared to those in CS + WT group ( g). Meanwhile, ELISA results of IL-6, KC and IL-1β levels in BALF supernatants kept consistent with above changes ( h). Subsequently, HE staining demonstrated that the infiltration of peribronchial inflammatory cells and inflammation score in CS-exposed Gadd45b KO mice were significantly inferior to those in CS-exposed WT mice ( i). RNA from lung tissues between groups was detected for lung inflammation. Results demonstrated that the expressions of IL-6, KC and IL-1β were remarkably lower in CS + Gadd45b KO group than CS + WT group ( j). Furthermore, we also investigated the role of Gadd45b in airway remodeling, another typical feature of COPD. As shown in , Gadd45b depletion had no impact on airway remodeling in CS-exposed mice, as indicated by a-SMA expressions in mouse airways and Masson assay. Therefore, Gadd45b knockout in mouse could alleviate CS-induced airway and lung inflammation.
Finally, we further investigated the influence of Gadd45b in senescence of bronchial epithelium in vivo. As expected, the expressions of p16 and p21 were obviously reduced in CS + Gadd45b KO group relative to CS + WT group ( k). Immunofluorescence staining ulteriorly indicated that the alterations in p16 and p21 between groups mainly occurred in bronchial epithelium ( l–n). SA-β-gal staining of frozen lung sections displayed the same changes as above experiments ( o). Additionally, the expression of coregulatory protein Fos presented a marked decrease in CS + Gadd45b KO mice compared with CS + WT mice ( ). Overall, these data in vivo manifested that Gadd45b deficiency could effectually improve lung function, lung destruction and inflammation, and bronchial epithelial senescence in CS-exposed mice.

## DNA hypomethylation enhanced GADD45B expression in bronchial epithelium cells
Given that DNA methylation displayed an important role in COPD pathogenesis , we hypothesized that GADD45B upregulation in bronchial epithelium of COPD might be influenced by DNA methylation. We subsequently conducted experiments in vitro and in vivo to validate this hypothesis. Primarily, we selected 12 lung tissues for TBS detection, including 6 non-smokers and 6 COPD patients. We tested the methylated levels of GADD45B promoter around transcriptional start site ( a). A total of 55 loci were detected with altered methylation, and the global methylation level was distinctly reduced in COPD than controls ( b, ). Among these, there were 5 loci that displayed a distinct reduction in methylation levels ( c). Then, we treated HBE cells with 5-Aza, a demethylation drug, and found that 5-Aza overtly promoted GADD45B expression in HBE cells regardless of CSE both at mRNA and protein level ( d, e).
 To further investigate the effect of CS on GADD45B promoter methylation and expression, we performed TBS detection in mouse lungs, mirroring our human subjects. The global Gadd45b promoter methylation rate was clearly lower in CS-exposed mice, with 2 loci of significant decrease ( f, g, and ). Additionally, through the administration of 5-Aza during the later stage of CS modeling in mice, we observed a progressive increase in both RNA and protein expressions of Gadd45b in mouse lungs across four groups, with the highest expression observed in the 5-Aza + CS group ( h, i). Immunohistochemical staining showed the Gadd45b expression changes mainly located in bronchial epithelium ( j). Collectively, these findings suggested that cigarette smoke decreased DNA methylated levels of GADD45B promoter, thereby enhanced GADD45B expression in bronchial epithelial cells.

## Discussion
In the present study, we elucidated the function of GADD45B in COPD pathogenesis. Specifically, we have proven that CS decreased DNA methylation level of GADD45B promoter and thereby augmented GADD45B expression in bronchial epithelial cells both of human and mouse. Subsequently, we verified that GADD45B deficiency alleviated CS-induced inflammation and cellular senescence in vivo and in vitro. Mechanically, GADD45B directly promoted release of inflammatory cytokines via p38 phosphorylation, or interacted with FOS to facilitate cellular senescence, which further aggravated inflammation via p38 phosphorylation ( ). These findings clarified the essential role of GADD45B in COPD and provided a viable insight for developing preventive or therapeutic strategy of COPD by targeting GADD45B.
 Chronic inflammation is one of the most classic and crucial pathological changes in COPD, as featured by the excess secretion of numerous inflammatory cytokines from airway and related cells . Bronchial epithelial cells, as the major component forming the lining of airway wall, are in direct contact with inhaled environmental factors such as cigarette smoke . Upon exposure to the harmful insults, these cells will be activated and generate numerous proinflammatory mediators including IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β, perpetuating the inflammatory response and contributing to disease progression , , . Previous studies have reported that these cytokines were upregulated in COPD and closely associated with airway obstruction and pulmonary dysfunction , , . Our study demonstrated that GADD45B was overexpressed in COPD and GADD45B deletion suppressed production of IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β in vivo and vitro. Meanwhile, animal experiments indicated Gadd45b deficiency also alleviated CS-induced peribronchial inflammation and lung dysfunction in mice, which could be probably attributed to Gadd45b-regulated generations of inflammatory cytokines. Preceding literatures have shown that GADD45B increased the levels of IL-6 and IL-1β in the rodent amygdala , while promoted the production of IL-8 and IL-1β in grass carp , which supported our results to some extent. Moreover, we substantiated that GADD45B-induced inflammation was mediated by p38 phosphorylation. In previous researches, p38 MAPK was regarded as the most involved in airway and lung inflammation underlying COPD , and GADD45B was reported to related tightly to p38 signaling pathway, facilitating the disease progression . Therefore, these evidences also suggested the reliability of our findings from the side.
As population aging gradually becoming the social concern, COPD is extensively acknowledged as an age-related disease as manifested with accelerated lung aging . Aging lung can elevate the vulnerability to various environmental exposures and then contribute to COPD progression by facilitating the loss of lung elasticity, remodeling airway structure, declining lung function, reducing pulmonary repair and regeneration , , . Cellular senescence, as the primary hallmark of aging, plays an indispensable role in COPD pathogenesis likewise and is now regarded as an crucial driving mechanism . Senescent cells, including epithelial and endothelial cells, characterized by the upregulations in senescent markers of p16 and p21, accumulate in the lungs of COPD patients , , and consistently exhibit other characteristics of aging, such as telomere attrition, mitochondrial dysfunction, and defective autophagy . Excess senescent cells will persistently damage lung structure and function of COPD patients. Nevertheless, the exact mechanism underlying cellular senescence in COPD remains obscure. In the current study, we revealed that GADD45B, an important regulator in cell cycle, played an integral role in enhancing CSE-induced senescence of bronchial epithelial cells. In vivo experiments further validated that Gadd45b deficiency could mitigate lung aging and cellular senescence in bronchial epithelium caused by CS, which partially accounted for improvements in pulmonary function and emphysema in Gadd45b KO mice. In accordance with our findings, previous studies indicated the regulatory role of GADD45B in stress-induced senescence and tissue aging as well, such as in articular cartilage and skin aging , . These hinted that GADD45B might be a feasible intervention target for alleviating CS-induced senescence in bronchial epithelium and lung tissue.
During COPD development, despite replicative arrest, senescent cells are metabolically active and may affect other cells via secretion of various cytokines or mediators, namely SASP . The senescence-associated increase in proinflammatory factors (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α), growth factors (TGF-β, VEGF), and chemokines (CXCL1, CXCL8) is particularly detrimental for cells in the lung niche, which aggravates inflammatory response in COPD , , . The same phenomenon has been confirmed by our study, and results showed that suppressing either p16 or p21 in HBE cells markedly restrained the production of IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β, suggesting that cellular senescence may be contributory to the chronic inflammation of COPD. In return, inflammation can augment cellular senescence, echoed by previous evidence that CXCL8 binds to CXCR2 and subsequently induces DNA lesion and cellular senescence . Collectively, considering the pivotal role of GADD45B both in inflammation and cellular senescence, therapy targeting GADD45B is promised to be the breakthrough for vicious circle between inflammation and senescence during disease process of COPD.
In recent years, epigenetic mechanisms have emerged as the key mediators of environmental and genetic interactions, leading to the progression of lung disease . Extensive evidences demonstrate that smoking leads to abnormal DNA methylation patterns, which are tightly relevant to the occurrence and development of COPD . Specifically, DNA methylation changes at certain gene loci in COPD patients play a crucial role in gene regulation containing inflammation-related genes, thereby affecting the intensity and persistence of inflammatory response , . In our research, we substantiated that CS lessened the methylated level of GADD45B promoter to promote its expression, which clarified the source of GADD45B upregulation in COPD and benefited the elucidation of COPD pathogenesis.
There remain a few limitations in our study. Firstly, we demonstrated that GADD45B interacted with FOS to promote cellular senescence in HBE cells, but the specific underlying mechanisms were unknown. And animal experiments with the gene-edited mouse targeting FOS should be investigated further to verify the in vitro results. Secondly, more researches are warranted regarding the regulation of GADD45B in other biological processes participating in COPD pathogenesis, like epithelial-mesenchymal transformation and hypersecretion of airway mucus. Furthermore, both the analysis of scRNASeq data and immunohistochemical results of the lung sections indicated the upregulation in GADD45B expression in alveolar epithelium of COPD, the potential role of GADD45B in alveolar epithelium therefore merits an in-depth investigation. Lastly, future research is needed to explore the effects of GADD45B on the functions of other structure cells or immune cells around airway in COPD.
In conclusion, the current study demonstrated that GADD45B upregulation in COPD was mediated by CS-induced DNA hypomethylation, and promoted the inflammation and cellular senescence in bronchial epithelial cells. These findings are of great value to the elucidation for the pathogenesis of COPD and provide a promising therapeutic approach targeting GADD45B for COPD in clinical settings.
