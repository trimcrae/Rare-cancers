## Abstract
Motor function recovery after complete spinal cord injury remained as a challenge in medical field, while one of the key approaches is promoting the local microenvironments. In this research, we performed a conjugated therapy by transplantation of neural stem cell (NSC) scaffolds and umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell derived exosomes (ucMSC-exos) for the treatment of complete transactional spinal cord injury (SCI). We first demonstrated the anti-inflammatory effects of ucMSC-exos in vitro and found that ucMSC-exos could regulate microglia polarization from M1 to M2, an anti-inflammatory phenotype. Besides, ucMSC-exos also promoted NSC proliferation and neural differentiation during in vitro culturing. On the other hand, core-shell hydrogel microfibers were used as transplantation scaffolds for both small and large SCI defects. The core-shell microfibers could carry large amounts of NSCs in the core portion and the shell portion is highly permeable for nutrient and metabolite transportation. In in vivo experiments, we found that conjugated transplantation of ucMSC-exos and NSC microfibers could decreased inflammatory cytokines at lesion sites, gave rise to more neurons and promoted angiogenesis, thus comprehensively improved the local microenvironment while compared with transplantation of NSC scaffolds only. These beneficial results were in accordance with those in vitro experiments and further led to better locomotor function recovery. In summary, this research has demonstrated that that conjugated transplantation of ucMSC-exos and NSC microfibers could make a potential tool for complete SCI repair.
Keywords: Complete spinal cord injury, Coaxial printing, Neural stem cells, Exosomes

## Introduction
Spinal cord injury (SCI) could cause ischemia, inflammation and necrotic neuron death in acute phase, leading to the formation of cystic cavitations and glial scars, which dramatically hinder the reconstruction of neural pathways [ ]. Cell-laden scaffold transplantation has been demonstrated to be effectual in SCI treatment since the scaffolds own capabilities of bridging the lesion gap, replenishing neural cells, suppressing glial scar formation and guiding axon regrowth [ ]. In recent years, hydrogel scaffolds have been intensively researched due to their superiorities of adaptable cell accommodation, controllable cell arrangement and capable of growth factor incorporation, which collectively promote the locomotor function recovery after SCI [ , , , ]. Furthermore, with the aid of 3D bioprinting, cell-laden hydrogels could be fabricated into pre-designed biomimetic structures in a precise and efficient manner [ ]. As a typical 3D-bioprinted biomimetic hydrogel scaffolds, fiber-like hydrogel structures have been successfully used in complete SCI repair with fine microenvironment for cell survival and differentiation [ , ]. However, in current stage, the locomotor function recovery after complete SCI is still unsatisfactory due to the incomplete regeneration of motor pathways [ ]. Therefore, it is an urgent issue to raise new approaches for complete SCI treatment.
Neural stem cells (NSCs) are commonly used for SCI repair mainly due to their neuroprotection, immunomodulation and neuroregeneration effects mainly induced by paracrine signaling [ , ]. In a recent research, it has been demonstrated that brain-derived NSCs could lead to emergence of mature nerve fibers in transactional SCI defects while spinal-derived NSCs could directly give rise to mature neurons [ ]. Moreover, pre-differentiated NSCs with dorsal and ventral characteristics could give rise to more mature neurons and these graft-derived neurons further built synapse connections with host neurons [ ]. These results indicated that spinal cord-derived NSCs with spinal cord characteristics could work more effectively to replenish neurons in SCI treatment. To this end, transplantation of spinal cord organoids was also performed and it was verified that implanted organoids built effectual connections with host neurons [ ]. Therefore, at current stage, it is important to combine abundant NSC accommodation and appropriate NSC microenvironment during scaffold design and fabrication. In our previous research, a core-shell microfiber containing enormous amount of NSCs was fabricated for transactional SCI transplantation [ ]. The advantages of core-shell microfiber include: 1) abundant NSC accommodation with adequate cell-cell interaction; 2) facilitating NSC spheroid formation with high percentage of neuronal differentiation; 3) applicable to larger SCI defects and capable of guiding axon regrowth by the fiber-like structures.
Meanwhile, exosome-mediated therapies have also been applied in central nervous system repair since the exosomes could transport across the brain-blood barrier (BBB) and bring lower tumorigenicity compared with cell transplantation [ ]. It has been demonstrated that stem cell-derived exosomes owned the capabilities of inflammation attenuation, cell-apoptosis reduction and angiogenesis promotion in contusive SCI treatment [ , ]. Generally, cell-derived exosomes always contain information biomarcomolecules from their sources. By single-cell sequencing, Sun et al. have identified exosomes from a specific bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell subpopulation owned significant capability to facilitate axon growth both in vitro and in vivo [ ]. Moreover, exosomes could also serve as nanocarriers delivering siRNA to target SCI region, thereby regulating neurogenerative factor expression [ ]. In addition to intravenous injection, it is also feasible to combine exosomes with transplantable scaffolds for regulation of cell recruitment or axon regrowth at lesion sites [ , , , ]. These evidences above suggest that exosome-mediated therapy could be a potential approach for complete SCI treatment. Therefore, we anticipate that co-transplantation of exosomes and NSC scaffolds to SCI would meliorate local microenvironment for better NSC accommodation and further facilitate NSC differentiation to neurons.
Herein, we performed a conjugated therapy combining NSC microfiber scaffolds and umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell derived exosomes (ucMSC-exos) for complete transactional SCI repair ( ). We first verified that ucMSC-exos could shift microglial M1/M2 polarization in vitro and nourish the anti-inflammation phenotype M2 microglia. Meanwhile, we evaluated the neurotrophic effect of ucMSC-exos by co-culturing with NSCs in both petri dishes and hydrogels. Next, the NSC-laden core-shell microfibers were fabricated with our pre-established method ensuring high NSC viability [ ]. in vivo transplantation was performed in rodent models with small or large complete transactional SCI defects for different time length. Levels of inflammation, neuroregeneration and locomotor function were evaluated and compared with control group (microfiber transplantation only). We expected that the utilization of ucMSC-exos and NSC microfibers would promote anti-inflammation and neuroregeneration after complete transactional SCI and finally lead to better locomotor function recovery.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Cell isolation and cell culture
Primary neural stem cells (NSCs) were isolated from the cortex of fetal ICR mice as previously reported [ ]. Pregnant mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation at day 13 or 14 to obtain the fetuses. The obtained fetal cortex tissue was immersed in 5 mL DPBS with 2.5 mL 0.25 % Trypsin-EDTA and 100 μL DNase (Solarbio) at 37 °C for 10 min. The dissociated cell suspension was further processed with 40 μm filters and centrifuged at 1000r/min to finally get the NSC pellets. NSC pellets were resuspended and maintained in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) with 2 % B27, 1 % N2, 1 % Glutamine, 20 ng/mL bFGF, 20 ng/mL EGF and 1 % Penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C, 5 % CO2. NSCs were passaged every 3 days. Furthermore, the BV-2 microglia (Pricella, CL-0493) for in vitro co-culturing with exosomes were maintained in DMEM with 10 % serum and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C.

2.2.

## Exosome acquisition and identification
ucMSC-exos were purchased from Huamei BioTech Co. Ltd (www.hmcell.com, ZA0010). The average size and zeta potential of exosomes were analyzed on nanoparticle tracking analyzer (Zetaview) with laser scattering microscopy. In detail, ucMSC-exos were suspended in PBS solution (pH = 7.0) at concentration of 3.9 × 107/mL. Zeta potential was measured under 28 °C.

2.3.

## OHA synthesis and hydrogel preparation
The oxidized hyaluronic acid (OHA) was synthesized by oxidation of hyaluronic acid (HA) with sodium periodate as previously reported [ ]. The reaction product was dialysed and lyophilized to obtain the final solid OHA. Carboxymethyl chitosan (CMC) and OHA were separately dissolved in PBS at 2 %. Rheologic characterization of OHA-CMC blend was reported in our previous report [ ]. For core-shell microfiber fabrication, CMC and OHA solutions were mixed in equal volume to form the hydrogel precursor. Digested NSCs were gently resuspended in the hydrogel precursor at 5 × 107/mL with 0.1 Matrigel addition to generate the final bioink for the core portion.

2.4.

## in vitro M1/M2 polarization of BV-2 under the existence of exosomes
Exosomes were labelled with red fluorescence PKH-26 (Umibio, UR52302) following the manufacturer's instructions and co-cultured with BV-2 microglia for no less than 6 h. The culture medium was eliminated and the BV-2 were labelled with DAPI. Exosome uptake of BV-2 was recorded under a fluorescence microscope. 0.5 μg/mL LPS (Sigma, L4391) and 40 ng/mL IL-4 (Novoprotein, CK15) were used to polarize BV-2 to M1 and M2 phenotype, respectively. 1 × 108/mL ucMSC-exos were added as a separate group during each polarization process.
Flow cytometry was performed to quantify the proportion of M1 or M2 phenotype after BV-2 polarization. Each separate group of BV-2 were fixed and permeabilized with off-the-rack reagents (Invitrogen, 00–8222 and 00–8333) according to the manufacturer's instructions. CD86 antibodies (Invitrogen, 469419) and CD206 antibodies (Invitrogen, 2538350) were used to label the M1 and M2 phenotype of BV-2 after treatment, respectively. BV-2 microglia were incubated with antibodies for 30 min before cell sorting on a flow cytometer.

2.5.

## in vitro apoptosis of M2 phenotype BV-2 under TNF-α treatment
The M2 BV-2 microglia was further treated with TNF-α for creation of inflammatory environment and the neuroprotective effect of ucMSC-exos was detected. Briefly, the M2 phenotype BV-2 were collected and incubated with TNF-α (PeproTech, 315-01A) for 24 h 1 × 108/mL ucMSC-exos were added to culture medium as a separate group. Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit (Invitrogen, 88-8102-74) was used to detect cell apoptosis after TNF-α treatment with flow cytometry according to the manufacturer's instructions.

2.6.

## in vitro apoptosis of primary NSCs under TNF-α treatment
Primary NSCs were divided into 4 separate groups: NSCs only (blank), NSCs with ucMSC-exos, NSCs in hydrogel blend, NSCs with ucMSC-exos in hydrogel blend. The hydrogel blend consisted of 1 % OHA-CMC and Matrigel as described in 2.3. Harvested NSCs were gently resuspended in hydrogel precursor and extruded as NSC-laden bulk hydrogel by manually pipetting. Each group was incubated for 24 h with exposure to 50 ng/mL TNF-α. A One-Step TUNEL Apoptosis Assay Kit (Beyotime, C1089) was used to detect NSC apoptosis after treatment.

2.7.

## Western blot
Western blot (WB) assay was performed to quantify the protein expression level in every relevant section. All WB assays in this research were performed as follows. Cells were grinded and lysed with cell lysis buffer (Life-iLab, AP01L013) on ice. The supernatant was collected after centrifuging at 10000 r/min for 20 min. Total protein concentration was quantified with BCA Protein Quantification Kit (Vazyme) following the manufacturer's instructions. The proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes by electrophoresis and blocked with 5 % skim milk for 1 h. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. Primary antibodies used in this experiment include Tuj-1 (Proteintech, 66375), GFAP (Proteintech, 16825), Olig2 (Proteintech, 13999), Sox2 (Proteintech, 11064), Bax (Proteintech, 50599), Bcl2 (Proteintech, 12789), Arg1 (Invitrogen, AB2792410) and iNOS (Invitrogen, AB2572890). On the next day, membranes were washed with TBST and incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies (Invitrogen, 31460, 31430, 31470) for 1 h. Immune bands were visualized with BeyoECL Star Kit (Beyotime, P0018AM) on imaging instrument.

2.8.

## Fabrication of core-shell NSC microfibers
Core-shell NSC microfibers were fabricated as previously reported [ ]. Briefly, 5 × 107 NSCs were harvested and gently resuspended in the OHA-CMC-Matrigel hydrogel precursor described in 2.3 as the bioink for core portion. 3 % sodium alginate solution was used as bioink for the shell portion. The core and shell bioinks were loaded in two separate 10 mL syringes, which were fixed on the channels of a microsyringe pump and connected to the core and shell inlets of a coaxial nozzle. The outlet of the nozzle was immersed in 3 % calcium chloride bath. Core-shell NSC microfibers were obtained by gently extruded the bioinks into the bath. Calceim-AM/PI Kit (Solarbio, CA1630) was used to estimate the viability of primary NSCs right after microfiber fabrication. The NSC microfibers were maintained in NSC culture medium described in 2.1.

2.9.

## Animal model
Experimental methods were performed in accordance with the approved guidelines and regulations by the Medical Ethical Committee and the Inspection of the Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital (No.2021179). SD rats were used for building complete spinal cord transection models. The rats were anesthetized by isoflurane inhalation and immobilized on the operating table. T9-T11 laminectomy was performed after vertebrae exposure. A section of spinal cord with 3 mm–10mm length was completely removed for the creation of defects. During the acute SCI stage, NSC microfibers with ucMSC-exos, NSC microfibers or nothing were implanted as different groups, respectively. In addition, for transplantation in larger defects of 10 mm, electrospinning PCL membranes were rolled over around the microfibers as conduits for immobilization, as reported in our previous work [ ]. The musculature and skin was carefully sutured and the animals were kept in clean cages. Bladder evacuation was performed in the initial 2 weeks.

2.10.

## Locomotor function assessment
On week 1, 2, 3 and 4 post surgery, the locomotor function recovery of SCI animal was assessed by open-field test. Each animal was placed in an open field for 3 min to record the behavior. The locomotion was scored by 2 observers according to Basso-Beattie-Bresnahan (BBB) scale. On week 4 post surgery, the open-field behavior was also recorded for 30 min and analyzed by VisuTrack software to calculate the total distance and average speed of each animal. Furthermore, swimming test was performed to evaluate the whole body movement on week 4.

2.11.

## Electromyography
Electromyography of experimental animals was gathered with EMG 100C amplifier on week 4. The positive and negative electrodes were inserted in two ends of hindlimb gastrocnemius. The grounding electrode was inserted subcutaneous in the root of tails. Electromyography was recorded with proper parameters set on AcqKnowledge 5.0.

2.12.

## Histological analysis
The spinal cord samples were isolated by heart perfusion. Briefly, the animals were administrated with overdose anesthesia and perfused with PBS through the left ventricle. After that, spinal cord tissues were fixed by perfusion of 4 % paraformaldehyde. The isolated samples were kept in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 24 h at 4 °C and in 30 % sucrose solution for 3 days before slicing. Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining, Nissl staining and Silver staining were performed accordingly to visualize the cells, neurons and neurites. Briefly, the paraffin sections were dewaxed with xylene solution and a series of ethanol solutions. HE staining was performed as followed: the sections were stained with hematoxylin solution, treated with acid alcohol solution, stained with eosin solution, dehydrated, transparentized and mounted. For Nissl staining, the sections were stained with 1 % Toluidine blue solution at 56 °C for 20 min, treated with acid alcohol solution, dehydrated, transparentized and mounted. For Silver staining, the sections were successively immersed into glycine silver solution C, B, AⅠ and AⅡ, washed, dehydrated and mounted.

2.13.

## Immunostaining
In in vivo experiments, immunostaining was performed to further visualize the cell phenotypes at the lesion site of each spinal cord sample. Briefly, the sections were dewaxed, dehydrated, treated with 0.5 % Triton and blocking solution successively. Sections were then stained with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. On the next day, the sections were washed with DPBS and stained with secondary antibodies for 2 h. The primary antibodies used in this experiment are same as that used in Western Blot. Antifade mounting medium containing DAPI was added on the surface before microscopic observation.

2.14.

## Statistical analysis
The data in this research was presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Bonferroni post-hoc test was used to compare results across multiple groups. Student's t-test was used to compare results between two separate groups. Statistical significance was considered as ∗ p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01 and p < 0.001.

3.

## Results and disscussion
3.1.

## in vitro evaluation of regulation effects of ucMSC-exos on shifting microglia polarization
ucMSC-exos have been demonstrated to be beneficial in alleviating neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer and Parkinson diseases, where a dominant mechanism is the modulation on microglia [ , ]. Herein, the neuroprotection effect of ucMSC-exos was evaluated first. A typical cup-shaped morphology of ucMSC-exos is shown in A. Meanwhile, the diameters of ucMSC-exos were detected with nanoparticle tracking analysis which had a peak value of 109.2 nm ( B). Zeta potential of exosomes depicts their colloidal stability in mediums [ ]. The zeta potential of ucMSC-exos in this experiment was −37.00 ± 1.07 mV as measured ( B), which is consistent with previously reported [ ].
 Next, we examined whether the implementation of ucMSC-exos could shift the microglial polarization from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype [ ]. For this purpose, LPS stimulation was performed to shift BV-2 from M0 to M1 phenotype [ ]. ucMSC-exos were added additionally to induce the M1 to M2 shift (LPS + exo group) ( C). After treating with 1 μg/mL and 2 μg/mL LPS, the BV-2 was found to exhibit similar viability of >95 % as measured with CCK-8 assay and flow cytometry ( ). Considering the similar efficiency to induce M1 phenotype at the same time, we selected 1 μg/mL LPS for the treatment to minimize the drug dosage ( ). It has been reported that exosomes could be transported into cells and located at perinuclear regions [ ]. Here we presented a gross view of PKH-26-labelled ucMSC-exos and DAPI-labelled BV-2 nucleus showing that a portion of ucMSC-exos aggregated close to cells and some ucMSC-exos had entered the cells as reported ( D). After LPS and ucMSC-exo treatment, the expression of Arg1, a M2 microglia marker, was measured to be significantly higher in LPS + exo group than that in LPS and blank group ( E). On the other hand, there is no significance in the expression of M1 microglia marker iNOS among the three groups ( E). Furthermore, as measured with flow cytometry, the LPS + exo group has shown 87.5 % CD206+ (M2 microglia marker) proportion within the defined range, higher than that of LPS (62.5 %) and blank (45.3 %) group. Meanwhile, the LPS + exo group has shown lower CD86+ (M1 microglia marker) proportion (68.8 %) compared with LPS group (98.9 %), but slightly higher compared with blank group (53.3 %) where no LPS was added ( F). These results collectively indicated that ucMSC-exo addition successfully facilitated the microglia shifting from M1 to M2 phenotype during LPS stimulation, as previously reported [ ]. Immunostaining results further verified this conclusion where the CD206 expression was significantly higher in LPS + exo group compared with LPS and blank group. Meanwhile, the iNOS expression measured by immunostaining was also similar to WB results. There was only slight significance between the fluorescence intensities of LPS + exo and LPS group ( G). Additionally, the co-culturing of M2 microglia and shsy5y cells, which are commonly used as in vitro neuronal models, was performed since it had been reported M2 microglia could regulate the proliferation and differentiation of neural cells [ ]. Here we found that shsy5y cells co-cultured with M2 microglia exhibited slightly higher Ki67+ percentage and NeuN fluorescence intensity with no statistical significance ( ). Therefore, these results were presented as supporting evidence to suggest the neurotrophic and neuroprotection effects of ucMSC-exos on neural cells.

3.2.

## in vitro evaluation of neuroprotection and neurotrophic effects of ucMSC-exos on primary NSCs
In 3D bioprinted hydrogel scaffolds, cells are commonly encapsulated in permeable hydrogel networks which create a 3D microenvironment different from that in petri dishes [ ]. Herein, we evaluated the neurotrophic and neuroprotection effects of ucMSC-exos on NSCs both in petri dishes (2D) and hydrogel bulks (3D) ( A). The hydrogel blend contained OHA and CMC which could form the network through dynamic crosslinking. As previously reported, the OHA-CMC blend exhibited optimal modulus (∼100Pa) for neuronal growth and permeability for matter transportation [ ]. A lyophilized sample showed the porous structure under SEM ( B). Moreover, 0.1 Matrigel was added in the final hydrogel blend for better NSC proliferation and differentiation [ ]. A CCK-8 assay was performed to evaluate the proliferation-promoting effect of ucMSC-exos in petri dishes and the results have shown that ucMSC-exo addition have helped maintaining the NSC activity at a significantly higher level ( C).
 It has already been demonstrated that exosomes could protect neurons from apoptosis in neurodegenerative disease [ ]. In this study, primary NSCs were in the presence of TNF-α in 2D and 3D while ucMSC-exos were added for neuroprotection. As a result, NSCs in petri dishes exhibited 66.72 ± 17.07 % and 70.57 ± 14.79 % apoptosis rate after treated with TNF-α, as stained with Tunel and Bax, respectively. However, the NSC apoptosis rate with ucMSC-exo addition significantly decreased to below 15 % in both 2D and 3D environment, indicating its neuroprotection effect ( D). Moreover, it is interesting to find that NSCs encapsulated in hydrogel with no ucMSC-exo addition also exhibited low apoptosis rate, which could be partially explained by the anti-apoptosis effect of HA [ ]. On the other hand, the hydrogel network might result in prolonged TNF-α infiltration time and thus reduced damage to encapsulated NSCs.
Since it has been reported that exosomes contain certain miRNAs that could regulate neuronal differentiation of stem cells [ ], we further researched whether ucMSC-exos could regulate NSC differentiation in 2D or 3D environment as well. Primary NSCs were cultured in differentiation medium with or without ucMSC-exos. As evaluated by Western Blot, the expression of neuronal marker Tuj-1 was significantly higher in NSC + Exo group than NSC group with average relative gray value intensity 1.09 ± 0.11 vs 0.08 ± 0.04. As for astrocyte marker GFAP, there was no difference between the two groups. However, the oligodendrocyte marker Olig2 was measured to be significantly higher in NSC group than NSC + Exo group ( E). This result suggested that ucMSC-exos might contain certain factors that suppress the oligodendrocyte differentiation of NSCs. While further evaluated by immunostaining, few Tuj-1+ cells were detected in 2D group but Tuj-1+ cells with neurites were distinctly shown in 2D + Exo group. Meanwhile, there were distinct Tuj-1+ cells in both 3D and 3D + exo groups ( E). We speculated that the hydrogel modulus was also a key mechanical cue to regulate neuronal differentiation of 3D NSCs apart from ucMSC-exos according to a previous research [ ]. Besides, the immunostaining results also indicated that GFAP + cells existed in all groups ( F). Additionally, ucMSC-exos were added during the culturing of fibroblasts and it was found that collagen Ⅳ expression was increased ( ). Since collagen Ⅳ composed part of basement membranes related to hemorrhage in nervous system [ ], this increase of collagen Ⅳ might contribute to angiogenesis and hemostasis in surrounding tissues during acute SCI treatment.
The in vitro results above collectively verified that ucMSC-exos could promote proliferation and protect primary NSCs against apoptosis in both 2D and 3D environments. Meanwhile, these ucMSC-exos contained certain components that facilitate neuronal differentiation of NSCs and further sequencing is needed to sort them out. In a word, ucMSC-exos could be beneficial in further in vivo SCI repair by both immune regulation and cell nourishment while implanted with microfiber scaffolds.

3.3.

## in vivo transplantation of NSC microfibers with ucMSC-exos in small transactional SCI defects
3.3.1.

## Fabrication of coaxial core-shell NSC microfibers
As previously described, the core-shell NSC microfibers were fabricated by a coaxial extrusion manner [ ]. These microfibers could be extruded with meter length and layered core-shell structures. In the core portion, large amount of cells and extracellular matrices which facilitate cell migration and cell growth were combined for rapid tissue maturation while the shell portion served as a protecting part against external stimulus ( A) [ ]. Herein, NSCs aggregation occurred along the center axis of the core portion during microfiber culturing ( B). Interestingly, there was brightened region on the surface of NSC aggregation which was similar to the epithelium layer of embryoids during neural organoid formation [ ], suggesting that NSC microfibers might be a promising manner for organoid modeling. As examined by live-dead staining, most primary NSCs remained alive after fabrication (Calcein-AM positive, C), which is in accordance with our previous report [ ]. Long-term survival of encapsulated NSCs is important for spinal cord repair. As reported, the alginate shell is highly permeable to enable nutrient and metabolite transportation [ ]. In our previous research, we had examined the NSC metabolic activity in core-shell microfibers after printing [ ]. The results showed that during 9-day culturing, NSCs kept proliferating for the initial 3 days and maintained high level metabolic activity afterwards, which indicated that NSCs could survive in the microfibers. NSC viability in vivo was also estimated with Tunel staining and the results indicated that a majority of cells remained survival at lesion site where microfibers were transplanted at day 28 ( ). Furthermore, the microfibers could be weaved to form more complex structures ( D). Here we presented a sample to weave NSC- and oligodendrocyte-laden microfibers together as an entire heterogeneous cellular structure ( E). Generally, core-shell microfibers are a kind of easy-to-get microcarrier with multiple applications and the native fiber-like structure made it a potential regenerative scaffold for complete SCI repair [ ].
 
3.3.2.

## Evaluation of cellular regeneration and motor function recovery after in vivo transplantation
NSC scaffold therapies in SCI repair have been intensively researched since the emergence of tissue engineering, aiming at filling lesion cavity and modifying microenvironment to regulate stem cell fate and achieve motor function recovery [ , , ]. On the other hand, ucMSC-exos have also been demonstrated as a potential therapy in SCI due to their superiorities of alleviating inflammatory and promoting neurite outgrowth [ ]. Since exosomes could transport through BBB, intravenous injection or intranasal administration were commonly used to deliver exosomes to lesion sites [ ]. We have already demonstrated the neuroprotection and neurotrophic effects of ucMSC-exos on primary NSCs in in vitro experiments. Therefore, a conjugated therapy combining NSC microfiber scaffolds and in situ ucMSC-exo transplantation was performed to detect whether their interaction could make a greater contribution to in vivo SCI repairment.
In this experiment, NSC microfibers with no exosome addition were set as control group ( F). 3–5 mm spinal cord tissue were completely removed before transplantation ( G). After 4 weeks, the spinal cords were entirely extracted with implants. It could be observed that the lesion cavities were filled in microfiber + exo group and control group while there were still large cavities in blank group ( H). The histological sections further presented detailed views on the extracted spinal tissues, where the tissue from microfiber + exo group formed a relatively complete structure while the tissue from control group still contained cavities in the lesion epicenter ( I). Moreover, there was limited newly sprouted tissue in blank group, indicating the limited self-regeneration capabilities of rodent spinal cords ( I).
The cellular components at lesion sites from each group were labelled by immunostaining. As for neural cells which were needed for neural regeneration, microfiber + exo therapy gave rise to more Tuj-1 positive cells at lesion epicenter compared with control group, indicating there were more early-stage neurons [ ], which was in accordance with the in vitro results. Meanwhile, tissues from both groups (microfiber + exo and control) contained hardly any GFAP positive cells which might be due to the inhibition of multiple cell types by transplanted scaffolds [ ]. However, in blank group, there was nearly no cellular components labelled by fluorescence, despite little tissue that connected both distal ends ( A). Nestin is commonly regarded as a marker for NSCs. However, there were other researches revealing that Nestin was expressed in multiple cell types with self-renewal capability [ , ]. There was no significant difference in Nestin expression between the two groups ( B), which might be due to that both groups contained NSCs. Olig2 is a marker of oligodendrocytes that forming myelin sheathes. Interestingly, microfiber + exo transplantation gave rise to more Olig2 positive cells ( B). Since it has been reported that different oligodendrocyte subpopulations presented different susceptibilities to the injury during chronic phase of SCI [ ], we inferred that there was a certain oligodendrocyte subpopulation existing in the lesion site benefiting from the improved microenvironment. Finally, we compared the expression of myelin sheath marker MBP and mature neuron marker MAP2 in both groups. Although the expression of these two markers was slightly higher in microfiber + exo group, these was no statistical significance since 4 weeks was not enough for myelin sheath formation ( C and D).
 Next, the inflammation-related cells and cytokines were examined. IBA1 is a marker for microglia. Here, IBA1 expression was slightly higher in microfiber + exo group compared with control group. However, the expression of endothelial cell marker CD31 in microfiber + exo group was significantly higher ( A), indicating the angiogenesis-promoting effects of ucMSC-exos which have also been demonstrated in multiple types of exosomes [ , , ]. Moreover, three inflammation-related cytokines, IL-6, TNF-α and IL-10, were examined at lesion sites. It has been reported that the decrease of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α and increase of anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 synergistically facilitate microglia polarization from M1 to M2 [ ]. In this experiment, we found that IL-6 and TNF-α expression in microfiber + exo group was lower than control group and the expression of IL-10 was higher in microfiber + exo group with eminent fluorescent area ( B–D). There was statistical significance between the two groups in each separate statistical analysis ( E). Similar to in vitro results, ucMSC-exo transplantation also showed capability to ameliorate inflammation after SCI in vivo.
 At last, the locomotor function recovery level was evaluated. Generally, there were several animals from microfiber + exo group presented normal hindlimb posture with touching ground movements. On the contrary, in blank group, their hindlimbs of remained insensible ( A). As further evaluated by BBB score, several animals from microfiber + exo group presented coordinated body limb movements at week 4 where the average score is significantly higher ( B). Swimming test was also performed on week 4 which could evaluate the recovery level of forelimb dependency and body position apart from hindlimb movement [ ]. The average score of microfiber + exo group was significantly higher than the other two groups ( C). Furthermore, animal behaviors in open field were tracked for a 20 min period. Animals from microfiber + exo group showed more frequent movements with significantly higher total distance and average speed of 63068.87 ± 30371.03 mm and 52.56 ± 25.31 mm/s, respectively ( D and E). Collectively, the results above indicated better motor function recovery with microfiber + exo treatment. Meanwhile, the electromyograms of animal hindlimbs were recorded. Compared with control group, microfiber + exo group presented denser recruitment phase ( F). The frequency of abnormal fibrillation potential was also fewer in a 200 ms period ( G). These results indicated better recovery of nerve conduction in hindlimbs of microfiber + exo group animals. Additionally, as labelled by toluidine blue, more neurons existed in lesion epicenter of microfiber + exo group ( H). Neuron cell bodies and sprouted neurites across lesion area were also labelled by glycine silver ( I).
 

3.4.

## in vivo transplantation of NSC microfibers with ucMSC-exos in large transactional SCI defects
The regeneration of large spinal cord complete transactional defect is still a hard problem waiting to be solved since it is difficult for sprouted axons to grow across the lesion site and connect with distal end. Herein, we made 10 mm complete transactional SCI defects in rats and attempted to perform treatment with microfibers and ucMSC-exos ( A). We have examined their motor function recovery for the initial 4 weeks and the BBB score of microfiber + exo group was higher than control group, but limited motor recovery was achieved ( B). After 8 weeks, as shown by the histological sections, tissue fusion occurred in microfiber + exo group presenting relatively complete spinal cord tissues. In control group, although the lesion cavity was fulfilled with implants, the tissue at lesion epicenter seemed not to fuse with distal ends well ( C). Immunostaining results further indicated that there were more CD31 and Tuj-1 positive cells in microfiber + exo group ( D and E). However, there was no statistical significance ( F). These results indicated that the transplantation of microfibers and ucMSC-exos might be beneficial in regeneration of large transactional defects with prolonged time period.
 In summary, we presented a conjugated strategy for complete transactional SCI repair with NSC microfibers and ucMSC-exos. The microfibers are feasible to be rearranged into aligned bundles which is regarded as an efficient structure for axon regrowth [ , ]. More importantly, these microfibers facilitate NSC migration and aggregation to evolve into functional organoid-like structures [ ]. Meanwhile, ucMSC-exos were combined in transplantation to improve local microenvironment and facilitate NSC differentiation. We believed that the addition of ucMSC-exos is an effective supplement to microfiber scaffolds for gathering neurons. For a more in-depth exploration, prolonged observation time is needed after transplantation in further study.

4.

## Conclusion
In this experiment, we performed in vitro evaluation on the anti-inflammation and regeneration-promoting effects of ucMSC-exos. We demonstrated that ucMSC-exos could promote microglia polarization from pro-inflammatory type M1 to anti-inflammatory type M2. Meanwhile, ucMSC-exos facilitated proliferation and differentiation of primary NSCs in both petri dishes and 3D hydrogel bulks. Furthermore, ucMSC-exos were transplanted in small and large complete transactional spinal cord defects with core-shell NSC microfibers. It was found that this conjugated therapy promoted cavity fulfilling, gave rise to more neurons and anti-inflammatory immune cells at lesion sites, as well as facilitated angiogenesis. These effects collected promoted the lesion microenvironment and further resulted in better motor function recovery compared with no-exosome scaffolds. In summary, this research has proposed a conjugate therapy with ucMSC-exos and NSC microfibers, which might be a potential way to achieve better motor function recovery after complete transactional SCI repair.
