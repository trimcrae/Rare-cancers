Department of Cardiovascular Medicine, Tohoku University Graduate School of Medicine, 1-1 Seiryo-machi, Aoba-ku, Sendai, 980-8574, Japan Currently, management of PAH primarily involves the use of pulmonary vasodilators that target the endothelin, nitric oxide (NO)-soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC)-cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) signalling, or prostacyclin pathways. Although monotherapy and combination regimens have substantially improved clinical outcomes, mortality rates remain high, with certain patients exhibiting limited responses to vasodilator therapies. 
Although the European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society (ESC/ERS) guideline provides a framework for vasodilator selection, practical implementation often relies on an empirical approach, resulting in inadequate clinical outcomes; ∼30–40% of patients with PAH continue experiencing mean pulmonary artery pressures >45 mmHg. The lack of adequate evidence to guide the precise application of vasodilators, along with a poor understanding of their molecular features, contributes to unresolved issues in patient management.
The C1q/tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-related proteins (CTRPs) are a family of secreted proteins produced by various organs that share functional similarities with adiponectin. Dysregulation of CTRPs has been linked to metabolic abnormalities such as obesity, Type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Among the CTRPs, CTRP7 is a well-established plasma biomarker and an indicator of insulin resistance. However, despite its high expression in the lungs, the role of CTRP7 in the pathogenesis of PAH remains unknown. 
Therefore, in the current study, we aimed to elucidate the mechanisms underlying responsiveness to pulmonary vasodilators by conducting multiple analyses utilizing pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) and plasma samples obtained from patients diagnosed with PAH. The lung tissues and plasma samples were harvested and preserved from control subjects and patients with PAH who had undergone catheterization, lung surgery, or lung implantation, to explore the molecular mechanism of pulmonary arteries in a case–control study (see , ). By combining data from both human and basic studies, we present evidence demonstrating that CTRP7 exerts a negative regulatory effect on prostacyclin analogue receptor (PTGIR) expression via interleukin (IL)-6 induced inflammation and Rab5a and, consequently, impairs the response to selexipag in cultured PASMCs and hypoxic mice.

2.

## Methods
2.1

## Clinical data
We conducted a retrospective investigation involving 114 patients with precapillary pulmonary hypertension (PH) who had undergone right heart catheterization between January 2015 and December 2023. The diagnosis was established through a combination of echocardiography, computed tomography, spirometry, ventilation/perfusion lung scans, and right heart catheter examination, adhering to the guidelines established by the European Society of Cardiology and the European Respiratory Society in 2015 and 2022, respectively. , Diagnostic criteria included a resting mean pulmonary artery pressure of ≥20 mmHg, as measured via right cardiac catheterization, and a pulmonary artery wedge pressure of ≤15 mmHg. Exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with substantial valvular heart disease (defined as moderate-to-severe stenosis or moderate-to-severe regurgitation) and those who had undergone heart or lung transplants. Patients who had undergone catheterization but did not exhibit PH were categorized as the non-PH control group (n = 70). From both the PAH and non-PH control groups, plasma samples were collected from the pulmonary artery using a Swan-Ganz catheter.
All protocols were approved by the Institutional Review Board of Tohoku University, Sendai, Japan, and complied with the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Before this study, we obtained written informed consent from the controls and patients with PAH for conducting a case–control study or previous studies. For secondly research use of plasma samples and lung tissues, the Medical Ethics Review Committee has approved to wave written informed consent by using opt-out method (Approval numbers, Plasma samples; 2021-1-208, Lung tissues; 2021-1-1076).
The plasma samples were collected from them (Groups 1, 3, 4 PH classified by the World Symposium on PH proceedings) at catheterization at the Tohoku University Hospital for case–control studies, targeting the protein analyses of patients with PAH (see , ). The current study excluded samples from patients with Group 2 PH. The data of patients were collected on the date of each preservation (see , ), not including their prognosis. Reported data follow the STROVE initiative.

2.2

## Human lung samples
Lung tissues were procured from patients with PAH at the time of lung transplant and from non-PH patients undergoing thoracic surgery for lung cancer, with the tissue samples collected at a location distant from the tumour margins, as previously documented. PAH-PASMCs were isolated from pulmonary arteries with an outer diameter of <1.5 mm, following established procedures. The PASMCs were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 and 95% air. PASMCs at passage 4–7, when they reached 70–80% confluence, were employed in the experiments.

2.3

## Animal experiments
All animal experiments were conducted in compliance with the protocols approved by the Tohoku University Animal Care and Use Committee (approval no. 2021-ido-073), which conformed to Directive 2010/63/EU and the ARRIVE guidelines. Hypoxia-induced PH models were employed to evaluate the development of PH in mice. Eight-week-old male wild-C57BL/6 type mice fed a normal chow diet (Kumagai-shouten, Sendai, Japan) were subjected to either hypoxia (10% O2) or normoxia for 4 weeks, as described previously. In brief, mice exposed to hypoxia were housed in an acrylic chamber supplied with a non-recirculating gas mixture of 10% O2 and 90% N2, utilizing an adsorption-type oxygen concentrator for exhaust air (Teijin, Tokyo, Japan). Concurrently, normoxic mice were housed in room air (21% O2) under a 12 h light-dark cycle. Two weeks after exposure to hypoxia or normoxia, mice were daily administered either 1 mg/kg selexipag or a vehicle containing 1% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). Four weeks after exposure to hypoxia or normoxia, mice were anaesthetized using 1.0% isoflurane. To evaluate the development of PH, the following measurements were performed: right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP), right ventricular hypertrophy, and pulmonary vascular remodelling. For right heart catheterization, a 1.2-Fr pressure catheter (SciSense Inc., ON, Canada) was inserted into the right jugular vein and advanced into the right ventricle to measure the RVSP. All data were recorded and analysed using the Power Lab data acquisition system (AD Instruments, Bella Vista, Australia) and were averaged over 10 sequential beats. For establishing the monocrotaline-induced PH-model, 7-week-old male SD rats (Kumagai-shouten, Sendai, Japan) were subcutaneously injected with 60 mg/kg monocrotaline (Sigma-Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) as described previously. Two weeks post-monocrotaline injection, rats were daily administered either 10 mg/kg selexipag or a vehicle containing 1% DMSO. In the Sugen/hypoxia model, mice and rats (male Sprague-Dawley, aged 7–10 weeks) were subcutaneously injected with the vascular endothelial growth factor-receptor inhibitor SU5416 (Sigma-Aldrich) at a dosage of 20 mg/kg body weight under isoflurane anaesthesia. Subsequently, rats were exposed to hypoxia (10% O2) for 3 weeks following established procedures. After the completion of experiments, all mice and rats were deeply anaesthetized through isoflurane inhalation (>4.5%) and subsequently euthanized via cervical dislocation.

2.4

## Statistical analysis
Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. A two-tailed Student’s t-test was employed to compare means between two groups with equal variances. Alternatively, the Mann–Whitney U test was utilized for comparisons between two groups with unequal variances. To evaluate the mean responses associated with the two main effects of distinct groups, a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted, followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test for multiple comparisons. The relationship between plasma CTRP7 levels and IL-6 was analysed using Pearson's rank correlation coefficient. Statistical significance was assessed using JMP 12 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) or GraphPad Prism 7.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). All the reported P-values are two-tailed, with a P-value of <0.05 considered statistically significant. All experiments were performed and repeated in a blinded manner by different researchers. Furthermore, results from in vitro and in vivo experiments are representative of at least three independent experiments.

3.

## Results
3.1

## Up-regulation of CTRP7 in the PASMCs of patients with PAH
Current pulmonary vasodilators target sGC, endothelin, and prostacyclin receptors, which primarily act in PASMCs; therefore, we focused on PASMCs to explore responsiveness to treatment and performed two RNA sequencing analyses. The first RNA sequencing was performed on PASMCs obtained from patients with and without PH, identifying variations in 3017 genes, among which PTGIR expression was relatively low (Figure A). For further analysis, we selected PASMCs exhibiting reduced and increased PTGIR expression, considering their cAMP production (Figure B). Using these cells, the second RNA sequencing identified significant differences in the mRNA expression levels of 704 genes (Figure B).
 Additionally, in the search for potential PAH biomarkers, we narrowed down the candidates to those with a reported biomarker status in existing clinical research (Figure C). Tissue and plasma samples from patients with and without PH were collected, as described in Section 2. Baseline characteristics of the control and PAH groups, including age, sex, use of steroids, and comorbidities, were comparable (see , ).
Both RNA sequencing analyses revealed a significant increase in CTRP7 mRNA expression in the PASMCs from patients with PAH (log2 fold change 5.37), particularly in cells with reduced PTGIR expression (log2 fold change 2.18), compared with that in the respective controls (Figure A and B). Plasma analysis revealed that CTRP7 levels were higher in patients with PAH compared to those with non-PH [non-PH, n = 70 vs. PAH, n = 114; 11.8 (0.58–61.57) vs. 19.68 (9.8–90.5), P = 0.0087] and other PH groups [Group 3 PH, n = 34, 15.17 (0.38–145.1), P = 0.020 vs. PAH; Group 4 PH, n = 38, 12.43 (1.45–149.8), P = 0.110 vs. PAH] (Figure C and , ), surpassing candidates such as phospholipase A2 Group IIA or tenascin C (see , ).
To confirm the expression site of CTRP7 in the vasculature, the expression data of ACTA2 (representing smooth muscle) and CTRP7 genes were extracted from the Tabula Muris project, under which various murine tissues were investigated using single-cell RNA sequencing experiments (Figure D). According to the single-cell analysis of mouse lungs, CTRP7 was expressed in stromal cells rather than in endothelial cells or lymphocytes (Figure D). The cluster expressing ACTA2 indicates the presence of smooth muscle cells, which overlapped with the area of CTRP7 expression, indicating that CTRP7 was also expressed in these smooth muscle cells. Consistent with the results of mRNA analyses, CTRP7 protein levels were notably elevated in PASMCs derived from patients with PAH (Figure E) as well as in the lung homogenates harvested from animal models of PH, including both the hypoxic mouse model and Sugen/hypoxia rat model (Figure F and G), compared with those in the respective controls. CTRP7 expression remained unaltered in the adipose tissue, liver, and left and right ventricles of hypoxic mice when compared with that in normoxic mice (see , ).

3.2

## CTRP7 down-regulates prostaglandin I2 receptor in the PASMCs of patients with PAH and in a rodent animal model
Next, we aimed to elucidate the role of CTRP7. Accordingly, we selectively down-regulated CTRP7 in PASMCs using specific siRNA. Down-regulated CTRP7 did not alter the levels of cell proliferation factors such as cyclin D1 (CCND1) and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (see , ). To explore the role of CTRP7 in the vasoreactivity of pulmonary arteries, we evaluated key targeting factors in the three major mechanisms of pulmonary vasodilators—the prostaglandin (PG)I2, NO-sGC-cGMP axis, and endothelin pathways. In PASMCs, CTRP7 down-regulation substantially enhanced the expression of PTGIR, although the expression of sGC β subunit and endothelin receptor type A was not significantly altered (Figure A). Similarly, PAH PASMCs exhibited higher CTRP7 expression and lower PTGIR expression than the non-PH PASMCs (Figure E), which were reversed by CTRP7 down-regulation (Figure B). Furthermore, the siRNA-mediated down-regulation of CTRP7 in PAECs did not alter the expression of 6-keto-PGF1a, one of the major metabolites of prostacyclin (see , ). These findings indicated that CTRP7 was up-regulated in PAH PASMCs and regulated PTGIR expression. Immunofluorescence analyses revealed that patients with PAH exhibited markedly reduced PTGIR expression in the pulmonary arteries when compared with patients without PH (Figure C). Likewise, PTGIR expression was lower in the pulmonary arteries and lung homogenates of hypoxic mice and Sugen/hypoxia rats than in those of the corresponding controls (Figure D–F).
 
3.3

## IL-6 transcriptionally regulates CTRP7 expression in PASMCs
Considering the well-established role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of PAH, we postulated that inflammation may play a crucial role in the elevation of CTRP7 expression observed in patients with PAH. Comprehensive plasma analyses revealed that levels of IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, and TNF-α were relatively increased in the plasma of both patients with non-connective tissue disease-associated (CTD) PH and those with CTD-PAH (Figure A and , ). Notably, patients with CTD-PAH exhibited relatively higher plasma levels of IL-6 and CTRP7 than those with non-CTD-PH (Figure A). Among these cytokines, IL-6 levels significantly correlated with CTRP7 levels (r = 0.519; P = 0.0001) (Figure B and , ).
 To elucidate the mechanistic role of IL-6 in the regulation of CTRP7 expression, we co-cultured PASMCs with recombinant IL-6 (20 ng/mL) for 24 h. IL-6 stimulation significantly increased CTRP7 mRNA expression (Figure C). A chromatin immunoprecipitation assay was performed to examine the transcriptional regulation of CTRP7 mediated by IL-6. We predicted that the promoter regions were located within the first 1000 bp of the CTRP7 transcriptional starting site, as described previously (see , ). Upon treatment with IL-6, the PASMCs isolated from both non-PH individuals and patients with PAH exhibited increased levels of histone 3 lysine 27 (H3K27) acetylation and histone 3 lysine 4 (H3K4) methylation in the CTRP7 promoter region (Figure D).
Western blotting revealed that IL-6 stimulation significantly enhanced CTRP7 expression and reduced PTGIR expression (Figure A). Furthermore, the IL-6-induced decrease in PTGIR expression was attenuated by the siRNA-mediated down-regulation of CTRP7 (Figure A). In mouse models of PH, CTRP7 levels were substantially increased in both the plasma and lung tissues at the onset of hypoxic exposure or following monocrotaline stimulation, along with an increase in IL-6 and a decrease in PTGIR expression in the pulmonary arteries (Figure B–D). Single-cell analysis using available Gene Expression Omnibus data was conducted to examine the expression and correlation between IL-6 and CTRP7 gene expression in vivo. Consistent with previous findings, both CTRP7 and IL-6 levels were significantly elevated in stromal cells of PAs from hypoxic mice compared to normoxic mice at the single-cell level (Figure E and , and ). Additionally, the mRNA expression levels of IL-6 and CTRP7 were strongly correlated, with this correlation being more pronounced under hypoxic conditions (Figure E).
 In addition to PASMCs, IL-6 expression was significantly elevated in fibroblasts. A ligand–receptor analysis using CellChat revealed the interaction of IL-6 signalling from fibroblasts to PASMCs (see , ). Based on the data from recombinant IL-6 and CTRP7 stimulation, paracrine effects from fibroblasts or other cells likely play a role in the CTRP7/PTGIR mechanism in PASMCs.
During the chronic phase of hypoxic conditions, these reactions in IL-6 and CTRP7 relatively decreased but were sustained at a high level. A similar pattern was observed in the monocrotaline-induced PH-model rats (see , and ). Collectively, these findings indicated that CTRP7 regulates PTGIR expression via IL-6.

3.4

## Inflammation induces the internalization of prostacyclin analogue receptor with Rab5a in PASMCs
Rab5a is known to modulate the vesicle-mediated transport of plasma membrane proteins into the endosomal compartment. We observed an increase in Rab5a expression in PAH PASMCs, which was significantly attenuated by the siRNA-mediated down-regulation of CTRP7 (Figure A and B). Additionally, the expression of active form of Rab5a, Rab5-GTP, was significantly increased in PAH PASMCs compared with that in the non-PH PASMCs. Rab5-GTP expression was further elevated following stimulation with recombinant CTRP7 (see , ). Consistent with these findings, co-incubation with recombinant CTRP7 significantly reduced PTGIR expression in non-PH PASMCs (Figure C), indicating the role of CTRP7 in regulating Rab5a expression.
 Immunofluorescence analyses revealed that both PAH and non-PH PASMCs incubated with IL-6 exhibited elevated Rab5a expression when compared with the untreated non-PH PASMCs (Figure D). Importantly, distinct PTGIR staining was observed around the cell surface of non-PH PASMCs. Conversely, images of PAH or non-PH PASMCs incubated with IL-6 suggested intracellular accumulation of PTGIR and Rab5a (yellow arrow), which was attenuated by the siRNA-mediated down-regulation of CTRP7 (Figure D). To quantitatively assess the cell surface expression of PTGIR, an indicator of the internalization rate, we analysed cultured PASMCs using flow cytometry, revealing that down-regulation of CTRP7 increased the cell surface expression of PTGIR in PAH PASMCs, indicating a reduction in internalization (Figure E).
Following internalization, PTGIR is directed to the lysosomal degradation process. In line with this, co-incubation with chloroquine (100 μM), a lysosome inhibitor, counteracted the reduction in PTGIR expression induced by co-incubation with recombinant CTRP7 (Figure C). Furthermore, immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated that the co-localization of PTGIR and LC3B (white arrow), a marker of lysosomal degradation, was significantly increased upon stimulation with recombinant CTRP7, suggesting that up-regulation of CTRP7 enhanced the lysosomal degradation of PTGIR (Figure F).

3.5

## CTRP7 regulates the responsiveness to MRE-269, an active form of selexipag
Selexipag, an oral prostacyclin receptor agonist and a potent vasodilator, has been approved for the treatment of patients with PAH. Selexipag is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and metabolized to its active form, corresponding to MRE-269, which was used in our basic experiments. We assessed the responsiveness to MRE-269 by measuring phospho-regulatory subunit 2 of protein kinase A (PKAR2) phosphorylation, as described previously. Treatment with MRE-269 increased the phosphorylation levels of PKAR2 in both non-PH and PAH PASMCs, with a significantly higher response observed in non-PH than in PAH PASMCs (see , ). Importantly, down-regulation of CTRP7 exacerbated the MRE-269-induced PKAR2 phosphorylation in both PAH and non-PH PASMCs (see , ).
To further assess the effect of selexipag, we measured intracellular cAMP accumulation in cultured PASMCs incubated with MRE-269. Consistent with the results for PKAR2 phosphorylation, the MRE-269-induced intracellular cAMP accumulation was lower in PAH PASMCs than in non-PH PASMCs, irrespective of the concentration (100 or 1000 nM) (Figure G). Furthermore, CTRP7 down-regulation significantly enhanced cAMP accumulation in both PAH and non-PH PASMCs (Figure G). These findings indicate that CTRP7 plays a role in regulating the responsiveness to selexipag via the internalization and degradation of PTGIR ( ).

3.6

## CTRP7 down-regulates the response to PG I2 analogues in PH rodent models
To investigate whether the attenuation of CTRP7 could restore PTGIR expression and improve responsiveness to selexipag in vivo, we designed a recombinant adeno-associated virus (AAV) carrying EGFP under the cytomegalovirus promoter (AAV6-shCTRP7) to down-regulate CTRP7 in the pulmonary arteries (see , ), as described previously. Hypoxic mice and monocrotaline rats were administered AAV6-shCTRP7 via intratracheal instillation 4 weeks prior to harvesting the lung tissues, followed by analysis of the lung-specific down-regulation of CTRP7 (Figure A and , and ). This approach resulted in reduced CTRP7 expression in both the lung homogenates and plasma samples collected from mice and rats (Figure B and , ), without notable alterations detected in the skeletal muscle or haematopoietic cells ( , ).
 Immunofluorescence images revealed that PTGIR expression was reduced in the pulmonary artery smooth muscle layer of hypoxic mice rats compared with that in normoxic mice or rats and that AAV-6-shCTRP7 transduction increased PTGIR expression (Figure C and , ). Furthermore, Rab5a expression was notably elevated in the pulmonary artery smooth muscle layer of hypoxic mice and monocrotaline rats and was attenuated by AAV6-shCTRP7 transduction (Figure C). Regarding the endothelial function, AAV6-induced CTRP7 down-regulation did not alter the production of 6-keto-PGF1a, a key prostacyclin metabolite (see , ). Elastica Masson (EM) staining revealed that AAV6-shCTRP7 transduction did not alter pulmonary arterial remodelling in hypoxic mice and monocrotaline rats (see , and ).
Considering the haemodynamics of mice and rats, AAV6-shCTRP7 transduction did not alter PH, heart rate, or blood pressure (Figure D and , and ). This was evidenced by the increased RVSP, total pulmonary artery resistance [RVSP/cardiac output (CO)] (Figure D and online, ), RVH (weight ratio of the right and left ventricle (RV/LV) + septum) (Figure E and , ), LV and RV dilation or function (right ventricular diastolic dimension), tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion, right ventricular fractional area change, and pulmonary acceleration time per ejection time (PAT/ET) ( online, and ), and reduced exercise capacity (Figure F and , ).
To evaluate the role of CTRP7 in responsiveness to selexipag, hypoxic mice, and monocrotaline rats were administered AAV6-shCTRP7 at 3-week intervals, followed by treatment with selexipag (1 mg/kg/day in mice and 10 mg/kg/day in rats) via gavage (Figure A and , ). In both animal models, selexipag improved RVSP without significantly altering CTRP7 expression following AAV transduction, whereas the CO, total pulmonary resistance (TPR), RV hypertrophy, and exercise capacity were comparable (Figure D–F). Transduction of AAV6-shCTRP7 significantly enhanced the selexipag-induced improvement in RVSP in hypoxic mice and monocrotaline rats (Figure D and , ). Likewise, hypoxic mice treated with AAV6-shCTRP7 and selexipag exhibited additional improvements in the CO, TPR, RV hypertrophy, and exercise capacity (Figure D–F and , , , and ). As shown in Figure G, pre-treatment with CTRP7 resulted in a significant right-shift in the MRE-269-induced relaxation curve of second-order pulmonary arteries isolated from Sprague Dawley rats. These findings are consistent with those showing improved haemodynamic parameters in response to treatment with selexipag in the hypoxia-induced PH mice treated with AAV6-shCTRP7 (Figure D). These results suggest that CTRP7 plays a role in diminishing the responsiveness to selexipag without markedly impacting the progression of pulmonary arterial remodelling.
A meta-analysis of clinical studies has reported a mean pulmonary vascular resistance improvement of 139.62 dyne-s-cm−5 in patients with PAH following selexipag treatment. In the current cohort, patients were divided into poor and better response groups using a pulmonary resistance cutoff of 140 dyne-s-cm−5. The group with poor response to all PGI2 analogues had higher plasma CTRP7 levels than the group with better response [poor response (n = 43) vs. better response (n = 34): 36.16 (7.99–72.12) vs. 5.16 (1.46–41.38) ng/mL; P = 0.0139] (see , ).
Sub-analyses revealed a trend of reduced CTRP7 levels in the better response group for both selexipag [poor response (n = 25) vs. better response (n = 24): 25.97 (7.36–52.36) vs. 4.56 (1.11–35.71) ng/mL, P = 0.053] and other PGI2 analogues [poor response (n = 18) vs. better response (n = 10): 43.68 (28.01–81.27) vs. 8.35 (4.19–41.38) ng/mL; P = 0.073] (see , ). Furthermore, when the cohort was divided into two groups equally based on the median CTRP7 level (19.68 ng/mL), the patients exhibiting higher CTPR7 level showed poor response to the treatment with PGI2 analogues [lower CTRP7 (n = 38) vs. higher CTRP7 levels (n = 40); −209.0 ± 188.4 vs. −115 ± 167.6 dyne-s-cm−5, P = 0.004], selexipag [lower CTRP7 (n = 28) vs. higher CTRP7 levels (n = 21); −195.8 ± 149.3 vs. −136.4 ± 181.0 dyne-s-cm−5, P = 0.060], and other types of PGI2 analogues [lower CTRP7 (n = 10) vs. higher CTRP7 levels (n = 19); −245.9 ± 265.7 vs. −91.3 ± 147.8 dyne-s-cm−5, P = 0.045], in comparison to the corresponding controls (see , ). These findings suggest that plasma CTRP7 could be a potential biomarker for responsiveness to PGI2 analogues.

3.7

## Improvement in selexipag responsiveness via inhibition of IL-6 signalling in a PH mouse model
To determine whether IL-6 blockade can enhance PTGIR expression and improve the responsiveness to selexipag in vivo (Figure A), hypoxic mice were administered an IL-6 receptor-neutralizing antibody (IL-6-R Ab; #AF 1830; R&D Systems) at 1.5 mg/kg/week in accordance with the online supplemental method. The inhibition of IL-6 signalling reduced CTRP7 expression in both the lung homogenates and plasma obtained from hypoxic mice (Figure B and C). Inhibition of IL-6 signalling markedly increased PTGIR expression in the pulmonary artery smooth muscle layer of hypoxic mice, as evidenced by immunofluorescent analysis (Figure D). EM staining revealed that IL-6 inhibition did not alter pulmonary arterial remodelling in hypoxic mice (see , ). Treatment with IL-6 antibody alone did not impact the RVSP, TPR (Figure E), RV hypertrophy (Figure F), heart rate, blood pressure (see , ), and exercise intolerance (Figure G). These findings suggested that treatment with a low-dose IL-6 antibody (1.5 mg/kg/week) restored PTGIR expression in the pulmonary arteries without improving pulmonary artery remodelling and hypertension.
 Importantly, treatment with an IL-6 antibody significantly enhanced the selexipag-mediated improvement in RVSP in hypoxic mice (Figure E). Likewise, hypoxic mice treated with an IL-6 antibody and selexipag exhibited additional improvements in CO, TPR, RV hypertrophy, and exercise capacity (Figure E–G and , and ). These results indicate that the IL-6-CTRP7-PTGIR pathway plays a role in the responsiveness to selexipag without substantially impacting the progression of pulmonary arterial remodelling.

4.

## Discussion
Upon combining data from both human and basic analysis, the key findings of this study were as follows: (i) multiple analyses using lung and plasma samples revealed CTRP7 as one of the candidates regulating pulmonary artery responsiveness; (ii) the IL-6-CTRP7-PTGIR axis emerged as the central mechanism connecting inflammation to the pulmonary responsiveness; and (iii) CTRP7 expression in the pulmonary arteries correlated with its plasma expression.
4.1

## CTRP7: a biomarker and modulator of pulmonary vasodilator responsiveness in PH
The primary objective of this study was to identify potential biomarkers capable of predicting patient responsiveness to pulmonary vasodilators. To date, basic research utilizing lung tissue samples has failed to contribute substantially to the development of new treatment strategies in clinical practice, which could be attributed to the lack of assessment modalities for target factor expression in the pulmonary arteries of patients. Therefore, to bridge this gap between research findings and clinical implications, our study leveraged not only lung tissue but also plasma samples obtained from patients with PAH and rodent PH models.
Herein, our approach involved a step-by-step process encompassing RNA sequencing, single-cell analysis of pulmonary artery tissue/cells, and assessment of plasma levels. As the targets of current pulmonary vasodilators are mainly expressed in PASMCs, we employed cultured PASMCs to perform two different RNA sequence analyses. The first RNA sequencing analysis revealed that PAH PASMCs expressed lower levels of PTGIR than the controls, while sGC, endothelial receptor, or related proteins were expressed in comparable levels. Considering the lower expression of PTGIR in PAH PASMCs, the second RNA sequencing analysis explored the features of those cells. In the third multiple analysis, the candidates were narrowed down to those reported in existing clinical research. Using this comprehensive approach, CTRP7 emerged as a prominent biomarker. Notably, elevated CTRP7 expression was detected in the PASMCs and plasma from representative PH animal models, including hypoxic mice and monocrotaline or Sugen/hypoxia rats (Figure F and G).
CTRP7, a member of the CTRP cytokine family primarily associated with glucose metabolism and inflammation, is predominantly expressed in the lungs. However, its role in pulmonary vascular function remains elusive. To explore the mechanistic role of CTRP7 in pulmonary artery responsiveness to pulmonary vasodilators, we conducted experiments that involved the siRNA-mediated down-regulation or recombinant stimulation of CTRP7. Our findings revealed that CTRP7 could markedly down-regulate PTGIR, the prostacyclin receptor (Figures A and C). Furthermore, both in vitro and in vivo experiments, along with the myography of isolated pulmonary arteries, demonstrated that CTRP7 could attenuate responsiveness to selexipag or its active form (MRE-269) (Figures G and G).
These findings underscored the elevated expression of CTRP7 in the pulmonary arteries of patients with PAH or rodent PH models as a pivotal factor associated with vasodilator responsiveness.

4.2

## IL-6-CTRP7 axis in pulmonary vasodilator responsiveness in PAH
Inflammation plays a crucial role in PAH pathogenesis, with mediators such as monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 serving as key players. Employing ChIP/single-cell analyses and comprehensive inflammatory assessments, our study provides compelling evidence that IL-6 drives CTRP7 expression in PASMCs via transcriptional regulation (Figures D and E and , ). The IL-6-CTRP7 axis modulated PTGIR expression, influencing the response to selexipag (Figure ). Thus, CTRP7 emerged as a central mediator linking inflammation to pulmonary vasodilator responsiveness.
From a genetic perspective, no CTRP7 (C1QTNF7) abnormalities associated with increased expression in patients with PAH have been reported. This was confirmed through a search using the public resource ClinVar, which analyses relationships between human genetic variations and phenotypes. This observation aligns with our concept that inflammatory conditions may predominantly influence CTRP7 expression in the pulmonary arteries.
In clinical practice, IL-6 receptor-neutralizing antibody therapies have demonstrated efficacy in various diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis and scleroderma. However, in patients with PAH, therapeutic IL-6 blockade (e.g. tocilizumab) did not improve pulmonary resistance. Further analysis of the same study data revealed that IL-6 inhibition showed favourable effects (reduced pulmonary resistance) in CTD-PAH, which has an inflammatory component. 
A detailed review of studies utilizing IL-6 blockade reveals that pulmonary vasodilators were not administered during the follow-up period, and the effect of IL-6 blockade on vasodilator responsiveness was not assessed. The present study demonstrates that an IL-6 receptor antibody enhanced selexipag responsiveness without improving PH (Figure ). Using a hypoxic mouse model with pronounced pulmonary artery inflammation, our findings underscore the importance of addressing and targeting inflammatory conditions as part of PAH treatment strategies in clinical practice. Further clinical research may determine whether IL-6 blockade provides additional benefits for patients with PAH receiving PGI2 analogues.

4.3

## Clinical implication: plasma assessment of CTRP7 for monitoring pulmonary vasoreactivity
The findings of our study suggest that plasma levels of CTRP7 could serve as a valuable biomarker for monitoring different phases of PH. As mentioned earlier, patients with PAH and certain rodent PH models had elevated CTRP7 expression in both the lung tissue and plasma (Figures C and B). Notably, the AAV-mediated down-regulation of CTRP7 in mouse and rat lungs resulted in reduced plasma CTRP7 levels (Figure B), indicating a mechanistic link between CTRP7 expression in lung tissue and the plasma. Furthermore, in our in vivo experiments, CTRP7 levels were markedly elevated in both the plasma and lung tissues during the early stages of hypoxic exposure or after monocrotaline stimulation, closely correlating with elevated IL-6 levels (Figure B and C and , ). This temporal pattern may resemble the onset of PAH or the active phase of connective tissue diseases in clinical scenarios. Although these responses diminished subsequently, CTRP7 levels remained elevated during the chronic phase of hypoxic conditions. These findings highlight the potential utility of assessing plasma CTRP7 levels, either independently or in conjunction with other inflammatory markers, for predicting pulmonary arterial expression and responsiveness to selexipag in clinical practice.
However, challenges persist in accurately predicting protein expression in the pulmonary arteries using plasma samples owing to the intricate nature of protein secretion in the vasculature and other organs as well as the heterogeneous pathogenesis of PAH. Given that lung or pulmonary artery biopsies, categorized as Class III in the ESC/ERS guidelines, pose potentially lethal risks to patients with PAH, investigating target factors using tissue and plasma samples becomes imperative. Moreover, additional markers or tests, such as IL-6 and other inflammatory markers, could enhance the precision and effectiveness of estimating CTRP7 expression in the pulmonary arteries based on plasma levels (Figure A and B).

## Translational perspective
PAH poses a severe threat to life; despite notable progress in pulmonary vasodilator therapies, some individuals exhibit limited responses, mainly owing to the lack of suitable biomarkers for appropriate selection. This study utilized RNA sequencing of PASMCs and conducted multiple plasma analyses in patients with PAH, identifying CTRP7 as a significant biomarker. Mechanistically, IL-6 increased the transcriptional levels of CTRP7, leading to PTGIR internalization and a diminished response to selexipag in the pulmonary arteries. These findings suggest that CTRP7 could be biomarker associating with responsiveness to prostacyclin analogues, providing valuable insights into the development of innovative PAH treatment strategies.

5.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. AAV transduction was not specifically targeted to the smooth muscle layer in the pulmonary arteries. In in vivo experiments, we evaluated the CTRP7-mediated pulmonary artery responsiveness only during the chronic phase of hypoxia in mice and not during the acute and other phases. In addition to inflammation, a low-dose IL-6R neutral antibody may exert an unexpected effect on pulmonary artery function. The ChIP of PASMCs demonstrated that IL-6 participated in the methylation of the promoter regions of CTRP7; however, the detailed transcriptional mechanisms remain unknown. Further studies are needed to determine whether the large variation in plasma CTRP7 levels is associated with the heterogeneity of patient conditions in both non-PAH and PAH groups. The validation cohort analyses of clinical data were not performed. Further collection of data of patients, including those with diverse racial backgrounds, and improvements in measurement methods are necessary to elucidate CTRP7 plasma levels, given the observed variability.

6.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, among the candidates with increased expression based on the RNA sequencing of PASMCs, the expression of CTRP7 was elevated in patients with PAH compared with that in the non-PH controls. CTRP7 regulated PTGIR internalization via IL-6-induced inflammation and Rab5a, influencing responsiveness to selexipag. Accordingly, CTRP7 emerged as a compelling biomarker associating with responsiveness to prostacyclin analogues, potentially advancing the development of innovative PAH treatment strategies.
