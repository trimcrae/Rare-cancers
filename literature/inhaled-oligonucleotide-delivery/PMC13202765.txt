## Abstract

## Background
Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is characterized by progressive nucleus pulposus cell loss and extracellular matrix degradation, in which persistent oxidative stress plays a critical pathogenic role. Transplantation of nucleus pulposus–derived stem cells (NPSCs) is a promising therapeutic strategy, yet the hostile oxidative microenvironment severely compromises cell survival. Although cellular quiescence has been suggested to enhance stress tolerance, its regulatory mechanisms and relevance in NPSCs remain largely unexplored.

## Methods
Oxidative stress was evaluated in human degenerated disc tissues, a rat needle-puncture degeneration model, and tert-butyl hydroperoxide–treated NPSCs in vitro. Proliferating and quiescent NPSCs were compared for reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, apoptosis, viability, and transcriptomic profiles. Pathway enrichment analyses were performed to identify critical signaling mechanisms. Recombinant transforming growth factor-beta 3 (rhTGF-β3) was used to activate the pathway, while small interfering RNA targeting the transforming growth factor-beta receptor type 2 (Tgfbr2) and the pharmacological inhibitor SB431542 were applied for pathway suppression. Functional assays, organ culture, and in vivo transplantation were conducted to assess cell survival and regenerative effects.

## Results
Elevated oxidative stress was consistently observed across clinical, animal, and cellular models of disc degeneration. Quiescent NPSCs demonstrated enhanced resistance to oxidative injury, with reduced ROS accumulation, decreased apoptosis, and improved survival. Transcriptomic analyses revealed suppression of metabolic and P53-mediated apoptotic pathways, alongside marked activation of TGF-β/SMAD signaling. Activation of this pathway induced quiescence, reduced ROS levels, inhibited mitochondrial apoptotic signaling, and protected NPSCs from oxidative injury, whereas pathway inhibition abolished these protective effects. In both organ culture and in vivo transplantation models, quiescent and TGF-β–activated NPSCs exhibited superior survival and significantly improved disc structural preservation compared with proliferating or pathway-blocked cells.

## Conclusion
Activation of the TGF-β/SMAD pathway induces NPSC quiescence and enhances oxidative stress tolerance by suppressing P53-dependent mitochondrial apoptosis. Pharmacological induction of quiescence represents a potential strategy to improve stem cell–based therapies for intervertebral disc degeneration.

## Background
Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is a major contributor to chronic low back pain, which represents one of the leading causes of disability worldwide [ , ]. Characterized by progressive depletion of nucleus pulposus (NP) cells and extracellular matrix (ECM) deterioration, IVDD ultimately results in disc collapse, instability, and nerve compression [ , ]. Increasing evidence indicates that oxidative stress is a critical driver in this degenerative cascade. Excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) disrupts mitochondrial homeostasis, induces DNA damage, activates stress-responsive signaling pathways, and promotes apoptotic death of NP cells, thereby accelerating matrix degradation and structural failure of the disc microenvironment [ , ]. Notably, both clinical specimens and animal models of IVDD consistently exhibit sustained elevation of ROS levels, highlighting oxidative stress as a hallmark pathological feature of disc degeneration rather than a mere secondary phenomenon.
Based on these mechanistic insights, various antioxidant strategies have been explored for the treatment of IVDD, including small-molecule ROS scavengers, mitochondrial protectants, and biomaterial-based redox buffering approaches [ – ]. Although such interventions mitigate oxidative stress to some extent, their therapeutic effects remain limited and transient, particularly in advanced degeneration stages where NP cellularity is already markedly reduced. Consequently, cell-based regenerative therapy has emerged as a promising strategy aiming to replenish NP cell populations and restore matrix homeostasis [ , ].
Multiple preclinical studies demonstrated short-term improvements following transplantation of mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) or nucleus pulposus-derived stem/progenitor cells (NPSCs) into degenerative discs [ , ]. However, clinical translation remains hindered by one major bottleneck - the extremely low survival rate of transplanted cells within the hostile degenerative disc microenvironment characterized by hypoxia, nutrient deprivation, acidity, and sustained oxidative stress [ , ]. Rapid apoptosis of implanted cells significantly compromises regenerative efficacy and results in unsatisfactory long-term outcomes [ , ].
Emerging evidence from stem cell biology suggests that the intrinsic cellular state critically determines stem cell survival under unfavorable conditions. Quiescent stem cells, defined as residing in a reversible, non-proliferative G0 state [ , ], display a unique metabolic program characterized by diminished glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation activities, resulting in reduced endogenous ROS generation and enhanced stress resistance [ , ]. Indeed, seminal studies in hematopoietic stem cells demonstrated that elevated ROS levels promote stem cell exhaustion and apoptosis, whereas maintenance of the quiescent state protects long-term stem cell function [ – ]. Similar principles were subsequently extended to neural and mesenchymal stem cells, in which quiescent populations showed stronger tolerance to environmental stressors and superior persistence following transplantation [ – ]. Consistent with these concepts, our prior studies established a serum starvation-based approach to induce reversible quiescence in NPSCs and showed that quiescence preconditioning enhances adaptive metabolism, in vivo survival, and disc-protective efficacy; however, the upstream regulatory mechanisms governing this state in NPSCs remain insufficiently defined [ , ].
Importantly, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) signaling has been identified as a key molecular regulator of stem cell quiescence across multiple tissues [ – ]. Through SMAD2/3 activation, TGF-β signaling enforces cell-cycle arrest and sustains the quiescent phenotype in adult neural stem cells and hematopoietic stem cells [ – ]. In parallel, recent studies demonstrated that TGF-β/SMAD signaling confers resistance against oxidative stress and suppresses mitochondrial apoptotic pathways in various stem cell populations [ – ], indicating a dual role in maintaining quiescence and mediating cytoprotection.
Despite these advances, the mechanistic involvement of TGF-β signaling in regulating the oxidative stress response and survival of NPSCs within the degenerative disc microenvironment has not been elucidated. It remains unknown whether pharmacological activation of TGF-β signaling can induce a protective quiescent state in NPSCs, suppress ROS-triggered apoptotic cascades - particularly the P53-mediated mitochondrial pathway - and ultimately improve cell survival and regenerative outcomes in IVDD. Addressing these questions is essential for developing an effective strategy to optimize stem cell-based disc regeneration therapies.
In the present study, we demonstrate that NPSCs in the degenerative disc are exposed to a persistently oxidative microenvironment that severely compromises cell viability. We reveal that quiescent NPSCs exhibit remarkable resistance to oxidative stress through metabolic suppression and inhibition of P53-dependent apoptosis. Integrated transcriptomic and functional analyses identify activation of the TGF-β3/TGFBR2/SMAD2 signaling axis as a key regulatory mechanism underlying maintenance of the quiescent phenotype and redox tolerance. Furthermore, pharmacological induction of quiescence by recombinant TGF-β3 markedly enhances NPSC survival against oxidative stress both in vitro and in organ culture, and significantly promotes long-term disc regeneration in an in vivo IVDD model. Collectively, our findings establish TGF-β-mediated induction of NPSC quiescence as a previously unrecognized but effective strategy to overcome oxidative microenvironmental stress and improve the therapeutic efficacy of stem cell-based disc regeneration.

## Methods

## Patients and sample collection
Patients with lumbar disc herniation who underwent percutaneous transforaminal endoscopic discectomy at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University were consecutively enrolled in this study. A total of 16 patients were included. Preoperative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed for all participants, and the degree of intervertebral disc degeneration was graded according to the Pfirrmann classification system (Grades I–V) (Supplementary Table 1). NP tissues were obtained intraoperatively through the endoscopic procedure. Immediately after harvest, tissue specimens were rinsed twice with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove blood contaminants. The annulus fibrosus tissue was carefully dissected away under sterile conditions, and isolated NP samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for subsequent analyses.
All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. The study protocol was approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (Approval No. I-BREC2023-6), and all procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Isolation and culture of rat NPSCs
Rat NPSCs were isolated as previously described in our published protocol [ , ]. Briefly, 8-week-old Sprague–Dawley rats were anesthetized by isoflurane inhalation, and euthanized by cervical dislocation in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines. Nucleus pulposus tissues were harvested from the caudal intervertebral discs of 8-week-old Sprague–Dawley rats under aseptic conditions. Tissues were enzymatically digested with 0.2% type II collagenase for 2 h at 37 °C. The digested suspension was centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min, and the cell pellet was resuspended in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/F12 (DMEM/F12; Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, USA). Cells were cultured at 37℃ in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2, and the medium was replaced every 3 days. After the appearance of sufficient cell colonies, adherent cells were detached with trypsin–EDTA and serially passaged. Cells at passage 3 were used for subsequent experiments. Cells within 12–48 h after passaging, corresponding to the logarithmic growth phase, were defined as proliferating NPSCs (P-NPSCs). Quiescent NPSCs (Q-NPSCs) were generated by serum starvation for 48 h following initial attachment [ , ].
All animal procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Nanchang University (Approval No. NCULAE-20221031077), and were conducted in compliance with institutional guidelines for laboratory animal care and use. This study was reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

## Cell viability assays
Cell morphology was observed by crystal violet staining. Briefly, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 15 min at room temperature, followed by gentle washing with PBS prior to microscopic observation.
Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Solarbio, China). To establish an oxidative stress model in vitro, tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP; Aladdin, China) was applied at different concentrations (0, 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 µM) for 12 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader to evaluate cell viability. Based on the dose–response results, 100 µM TBHP was selected for subsequent experiments.

## Intracellular ROS measurement
Intracellular ROS levels were measured using a commercially available ROS detection kit (Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, NPSCs were treated with TBHP and subsequently incubated with the fluorescent probe provided in the kit. After staining, intracellular ROS levels were observed using a fluorescence microscope. Quantitative analysis was further performed using flow cytometry, and the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was calculated to reflect ROS production. Flow cytometry data were analyzed using FlowJo software.

## Live/dead double staining
Live/dead cell staining was performed using Hoechst 33,342 and propidium iodide (PI). Briefly, cultured NPSCs were washed twice with PBS and incubated with Hoechst 33,342 (5 µg/mL) and PI (2 µg/mL) in serum-free DMEM/F12 for 10 min at 37 °C in the dark. After incubation the cells were washed once with PBS and immediately imaged under a fluorescence microscope. Quantification of cell survival was based on nuclear staining: Hoechst-positive nuclei represent total cells and PI-positive nuclei represent dead cells with compromised membrane integrity. Cell counts were obtained either manually or by automated image analysis using ImageJ software. The cell survival rate was calculated as: Cell survival rate (%) = [(Number of Hoechst⁺/ PI⁻ cells) ÷ (Total number of Hoechst⁺ cells)] × 100.

## Apoptosis analysis
Cell apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry using an Annexin V/PI apoptosis detection kit (UElandy, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, NPSCs were collected after treatment, washed twice with cold PBS, and resuspended in 1× binding buffer at a concentration of approximately 1 × 10^6 cells/mL. Subsequently, 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL PI were added to each 100 µL cell suspension and incubated for 10–15 min at room temperature in the dark. After adding 400 µL binding buffer, samples were analyzed by flow cytometry within 1 h.
Data were analyzed using FlowJo software. Cell populations were classified as viable (Annexin V⁻/PI⁻), early apoptotic (Annexin V⁺/PI⁻), late apoptotic or necrotic (Annexin V⁺/PI⁺), and necrotic (Annexin V⁻/PI⁺). The total apoptosis rate was calculated as the sum of early and late apoptotic populations.

## RNA sequencing
RNA sequencing was performed by Kangce Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China) using total RNA extracted from TBHP-treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs, with three biological replicates per group (n = 3). Raw reads were filtered using fastp (v0.23.2) to remove adaptor sequences and low-quality reads. Clean reads were deduplicated based on unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) to reduce PCR amplification bias and sequencing errors. An average of 44–61 million clean reads per sample was obtained.
High-quality reads were aligned to the rat reference genome (Rnor_6.0) using STAR (v2.7.6a), achieving overall mapping rates of 97.42% − 98.10%, with 90.99% − 92.64% uniquely mapped reads. Gene-level read counts were generated using featureCounts (Subread v1.5.1), and differential expression analysis was conducted using edgeR (v3.40.2). Genes with a fold change ≥ 2 and a p value < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed.

## Functional enrichment analysis
Functional enrichment analyses of DEGs were performed using KOBAS software (v2.1.1), including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using GSEA software with curated GO and KEGG gene set collections obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB).

## Gene and protein expression analysis
Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using SYBR Green on an ABI 7900 HT system (Applied Biosystems, USA), with GAPDH as the internal control. All primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 2. Protein samples were prepared using RIPA lysis buffer (Servicebio, China), and concentrations were determined by BCA assay. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech, China), and visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL; UElandy, China). β-Actin was used as the internal reference for quantification. Antibody information is shown in Supplementary Table 3.

## Cell transfection
NPSCs were transfected with siRNA targeting Tgfbr2 (siTgfbr2; GenePharma, China) using GP-transfect-Mate (GenePharma, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 60–70% confluence and incubated overnight. siRNA and GP-transfect-Mate were diluted in serum-free DMEM, mixed gently, and added to the cells. After 6 h of incubation, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR and Western blot 48 h post-transfection.

## TGF-β signaling activation and inhibition
For pathway activation, NPSCs were treated with recombinant TGF-β3 (rhTGF-β3) at 0, 2, 5, 10, 50, or 100 ng/mL for 48 h, a duration selected to achieve stable SMAD2 phosphorylation and establish a quiescence-associated phenotype. For pharmacological inhibition, SB431542 (5 µM) was applied 1 h before rhTGF-β3 stimulation or other indicated treatments and was maintained in the culture medium throughout the subsequent exposure and readout period. For genetic inhibition, NPSCs were transfected with siRNA targeting Tgfbr2 (siTgfbr2) as described above, and downstream analyses were performed 48 h post-transfection. To evaluate ligand-induced signaling under receptor knockdown, rhTGF-β3 was added at 48 h post-transfection and cells were incubated for an additional 48 h before analysis.

## Histological staining
Paraffin-embedded sections of human NP tissues and rat intervertebral disc specimens were subjected to HE, SO, and TB staining using standard protocols to assess tissue morphology and extracellular matrix composition. For ROS detection, sections were stained with dihydroethidium (DHE; Servicebio, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and imaged by fluorescence microscopy. DHE fluorescence intensity was quantified as MFI using ImageJ software.

## Immunofluorescence
Immunofluorescence (IF) staining was performed on cultured cells grown on coverslips or on tissue sections. Briefly, samples were fixed, permeabilized, and blocked, followed by incubation with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. After washing, samples were incubated with fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech, China) at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescent images were captured using a fluorescence microscope or confocal microscope. Quantitative analysis of fluorescence intensity was performed using ImageJ software.

## Cell retention study in whole-disc organ culture
Intervertebral disc organ cultures were established from caudal motion segments isolated from normal rats. Discs were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 15% FBS and antibiotics, with the osmolarity adjusted to physiological nucleus pulposus levels (~ 380–420 mOsm/kg) using sterile NaCl and verified with an osmometer. A low-dose TBHP (10 µM) was continuously added to the culture medium to induce chronic oxidative stress while preserving tissue viability, and the medium was refreshed every 2–3 days.
The day after TBHP addition, discs were implanted with GFP-labeled P-NPSCs, rhTGF-β3–pretreated P-NPSCs, Q-NPSCs, or siTgfbr2-treated Q-NPSCs (1 × 104 cells in 2 µL PBS per disc). The implantation day was defined as Day 0. Fluorescence imaging was performed on Day 0, 3, and 7 using an animal imaging system (AniView600, BLT, China) to assess cell retention and survival. Fluorescence intensity within the disc region was quantified using the manufacturer’s software.

## Evaluation of therapeutic efficacy in a rat IVDD model
A rat caudal IVDD model was established by needle puncture as previously described [ ]. Briefly, adult Sprague-Dawley rats (body weight ~ 200 g) were anesthetized by inhalation of isoflurane (induction: 4–5%; maintenance: 2%), and the coccygeal intervertebral discs were exposed under aseptic conditions. Degeneration was induced by percutaneous puncture of the central region of the target caudal discs using a sterile 21-gauge needle. The needle was inserted vertically to a depth of approximately 5 mm and rotated 360° back and forth for 30 s before withdrawal. All puncture and injection procedures were performed by the same team of experienced investigators.
Two weeks after puncture, animals were randomly allocated to the following treatment groups using a computer-generated randomization sequence: (1) PBS group, (2)P-NPSC group, (3) rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSC group, (4) Q-NPSC group, and (5) siTgfbr2–treated Q-NPSC group, with a sham-operated group serving as a non-degenerative control (n = 6 animals per group). For cell-based interventions, a total of 1 × 10⁴ cells suspended in 2 µL sterile PBS were injected into the nucleus pulposus of each target disc using a microsyringe. Injections were performed twice at a one-week interval. Animals in the PBS group received equal volumes of PBS following the same schedule.
At 8 weeks after the initial puncture, which corresponds to 6 weeks after the final injection, radiographic imaging was performed to evaluate disc morphology, and the disc height index (DHI) was calculated as the primary quantitative outcome [ ]. Subsequently, animals were euthanized by cervical dislocation following deep isoflurane anesthesia. Caudal intervertebral disc tissues were harvested for histological analysis, including HE, SO, and TB staining, to assess tissue architecture and extracellular matrix restoration. Histological degeneration was scored according to the histological grading system for disc degeneration (Supplementary Table 4) [ ]. Radiographic and histological evaluations were independently performed by three investigators in a blinded manner, and the animal was considered the experimental unit for all analyses.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0). All quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments (n ≥ 3). Comparisons between two groups were conducted using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t test. Comparisons among three or more groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## IVDD is accompanied by persistent oxidative stress
With the progression of IVDD, MRI images gradually show decreased signal intensity and loss of normal structural stratification, which can be classified into Pfirrmann grades I–V. In this study, human NP tissues of different degeneration grades were collected from patients undergoing percutaneous transforaminal endoscopic discectomy (Fig.  A, Supplementary Table 1). Gross examination revealed that mildly degenerated NP tissues (grade II) exhibited abundant water content with a translucent gelatinous appearance and good elasticity. HE staining demonstrated continuous and orderly ECM organization with high cellularity. In contrast, severely degenerated NP tissues (grade V) appeared yellowish and fibrotic with loss of elasticity, and HE staining showed disrupted tissue architecture with marked cellular depletion (Fig.  B). DHE staining revealed significantly elevated ROS levels in severely degenerated NP tissues (Fig.  C), indicating that excessive oxidative stress may serve as a key driver of cellular loss and disc degeneration.
 To further validate this observation in vivo, a rat tail disc puncture model of IVDD was established (Fig.  D). Four weeks after puncture, HE, SO, and TB staining revealed marked disc collapse, pronounced loss of nucleus pulposus tissue and proteoglycan content, extensive fibrotic replacement, and severe disruption of the annulus fibrosus compared with the control group (Supplementary Fig. 1). Gross inspection of the punctured discs revealed marked loss of NP tissue accompanied by fibrotic replacement and neovascular formation (Fig.  E). DHE staining further demonstrated pronounced ROS accumulation within the NP region and at the NP–annulus fibrosus interface, exhibiting a distribution pattern comparable to that observed in severely degenerated human discs (Fig.  E, F). Moreover, NPSCs were successfully isolated and cultured from rat NP tissues (Fig.  G). In vitro exposure to TBHP significantly increased intracellular ROS levels, concomitantly reducing NPSC viability and promoting cell death (Fig.  H, I).
Collectively, these results demonstrate that IVDD is characterized by a persistent high-ROS microenvironment across human tissues, animal models, and in vitro NPSC systems, and that oxidative stress directly compromises NPSC survival, thereby accelerating cellular loss and matrix degeneration.

## Quiescent NPSCs exhibit enhanced resistance to oxidative stress
Our previous work demonstrated that induction of NPSC quiescence (Q-NPSC) markedly improves cell survival following transplantation into degenerated discs [ ]; however, their oxidative stress tolerance and underlying mechanisms remained unclear. Therefore, proliferating NPSCs (P-NPSC) and Q-NPSCs were subjected to TBHP-induced oxidative stress (Fig. A).
 CCK-8 assays showed that Q-NPSCs maintained significantly higher viability than P-NPSCs following TBHP exposure (Fig.  B). Intracellular ROS measurements revealed that Q-NPSCs effectively suppressed ROS accumulation induced by TBHP (Fig.  C, D). Morphologically, P-NPSCs exhibited characteristic apoptotic features, including nuclear condensation and blurred cellular outlines, whereas Q-NPSCs preserved their compact morphology and typical quiescent phenotype (Fig.  E). Live/dead staining further demonstrated markedly increased cell death in P-NPSCs, while Q-NPSCs showed significantly enhanced survival (Fig.  F, G). Consistently, Annexin V/PI flow cytometry confirmed that both early and late apoptotic populations were substantially higher in P-NPSCs compared with Q-NPSCs (Fig.  H, I). These data indicate that quiescence confers robust oxidative stress resistance to NPSCs by limiting ROS accumulation, suppressing apoptosis, and maintaining cellular viability.

## Transcriptomic profiling reveals suppression of metabolic activity and the P53 apoptotic pathway in quiescent NPSCs
To elucidate the molecular basis of oxidative stress resistance in Q-NPSCs, we performed RNA sequencing on TBHP-treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs. Using a cutoff of |fold change| > 2 and p < 0.05, a total of 3,765 differentially expressed genes were identified, including 2,347 upregulated and 1,418 downregulated genes in Q-NPSCs (Fig.  A, B and Supplementary Table 5).
 GO and KEGG pathway analyses demonstrated that metabolic and proliferation-associated processes, including glycolytic process, ATP metabolic process, regulation of cell cycle, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and carbon metabolism, were significantly downregulated in Q-NPSCs, consistent with their low-metabolic, low-proliferative quiescent phenotype (Fig.  C, D). Importantly, pathways associated with cell death and stress responses, such as apoptotic mitochondrial changes, response to hydrogen peroxide, and regulation of apoptotic process, were also markedly suppressed. Further KEGG enrichment and GSEA confirmed significant negative enrichment of the P53 signaling pathway in Q-NPSCs (Fig.  D, E).
qRT-PCR validation revealed significantly reduced expression of Ki67 and the energy metabolism regulator Atp5if1, together with marked upregulation of the quiescence marker P27 in Q-NPSCs. Meanwhile, transcripts of the pro-apoptotic genes P53, P21, and Bax, as well as the oxidative stress–responsive gene Sod2, were significantly downregulated, whereas the anti-apoptotic gene Bcl2 was markedly elevated (Fig.  F). Western blot analysis further confirmed consistent protein-level changes in P53, P21, P27, cytochrome c (CYC), BAX, and BCL-2 expression across groups (Fig.  G-I).
Together, these data show that quiescence protects NPSCs at the molecular level by suppressing metabolic activity, arresting the cell cycle, and inhibiting P53-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis signaling.

## Activation of the TGF-β/SMAD signaling pathway sustains NPSC quiescence
Transcriptomic enrichment analysis revealed significant positive regulation of the TGF-β signaling pathway in Q-NPSCs (Fig.  A, B). Consistently, qRT-PCR showed increased expression of Tgfb3, Tgfbr2, Smad2, and Bmp4 in Q-NPSCs relative to P-NPSCs (Fig.  C). Western blot confirmed robust upregulation of TGF-β3, TGFBR2, and p-SMAD2 proteins in Q-NPSCs following TBHP exposure (Fig.  D, E). Immunofluorescence staining further demonstrated enhanced expression and nuclear translocation of TGFBR2 and p-SMAD2 in Q-NPSCs (Fig.  F-G). These findings indicate that TGF-β/SMAD signaling remains persistently activated in quiescent NPSCs and is maintained under oxidative stress conditions.
 

## Blockade of TGF-β signaling reverses quiescent phenotypes in NPSCs
To determine the functional requirement of the TGF-β3/TGFBR2 axis in regulating NPSC quiescence, P-NPSCs were exposed to rhTGF-β3 to activate TGF-β signaling, while pathway inhibition was achieved through genetic silencing of Tgfbr2 using siRNA or pharmacological blockade with the TGF-β receptor kinase inhibitor SB431542 (Fig.  A). CCK-8 analysis demonstrated that 48 h of rhTGF-β3 (10 ng/mL) treatment did not induce overt cytotoxicity in P-NPSCs, supporting its suitability for pathway activation experiments (Fig.  B). The efficiency of Tgfbr2 knockdown was subsequently validated by qRT-PCR and Western blot following siRNA transfection, confirming robust suppression of receptor expression (Fig.  C, D).
 EdU incorporation assays showed that rhTGF-β3 markedly suppressed DNA synthesis, whereas both siTgfbr2 and SB431542 significantly restored proliferative capacity (Fig.  E, F). Immunofluorescence analysis further revealed strong induction of p-SMAD2 in response to rhTGF-β3, consistent with robust pathway activation. Conversely, blockade of TGFBR2 effectively abolished p-SMAD2 activation (Fig.  G, H). Cell-cycle analysis demonstrated that rhTGF-β3 induced a pronounced G0/G1 arrest (> 80%), accompanied by a sharp reduction in S and G2/M phases. Inhibition of TGFBR2 via siTgfbr2 or SB431542 significantly rescued cell-cycle progression, reducing G0/G1 accumulation and restoring entry into S and G2/M phases (Fig.  I, J). Collectively, these findings identify the TGF-β3/TGFBR2/p-SMAD2 signaling axis as a central regulator required for the induction and maintenance of NPSC quiescence.

## Activation of TGF-β signaling suppresses ROS accumulation and apoptosis
Activation of TGF-β signaling by rhTGF-β3 markedly reduced TBHP-induced ROS accumulation in NPSCs, whereas inhibition of the pathway - either through siTgfbr2 or the receptor kinase inhibitor SB431542 - significantly reversed this antioxidant effect (Fig.  A, B). Annexin V/PI flow cytometry revealed a marked suppression of apoptosis by rhTGF-β3 treatment, while inhibition of the pathway significantly increased apoptotic rates (Fig.  C, D). Immunofluorescence staining further revealed that rhTGF-β3 reduced the expression of P53 and CYC, whereas both siTgfbr2 and SB431542 restored their induction under oxidative stress (Fig.  E, F). Collectively, these findings indicate that activation of the TGF-β/SMAD pathway enhances NPSC resistance to oxidative stress by limiting ROS accumulation and suppressing P53-mediated mitochondrial apoptotic signaling.
 

## Quiescent NPSCs exhibit enhanced survival in an ex vivo organ culture model
To compare the antioxidant protective capacity of pharmacologically induced quiescence (rhTGF-β3) with serum starvation-induced natural quiescence (Q-NPSC), all groups were subjected to TBHP-induced oxidative stress (Fig.  A). In crystal violet staining, both rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs retained intact morphology and adhesive integrity after TBHP exposure, whereas Q-NPSCs transfected with siTgfbr2 exhibited pronounced morphological collapse and ghost-like cellular outlines (Fig.  B). Consistently, live/dead staining (Hoechst 33342/PI) revealed that both rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs displayed markedly higher survival, while untreated P-NPSCs and siTgfbr2-transfected Q-NPSCs showed extensive cell death (Fig.  B, C).
 To further assess cell survival in a disc-like microenvironment, we established an ex vivo intervertebral disc organ culture model under chronic low-dose TBHP exposure (10 µM) (Fig.  D). Histological evaluation of disc explants after 7 days confirmed that overall disc architecture remained largely preserved, while Safranin O/Fast Green staining indicated a moderate reduction of proteoglycan-rich matrix within the nucleus pulposus, supporting a sustained oxidative-stress milieu rather than catastrophic tissue destruction (Supplementary Fig. 2). NPSCs used for transplantation were GFP-labeled (Supplementary Fig. 3), enabling longitudinal tracking after injection into the nucleus pulposus region (Fig.  D). Over 0, 3, and 7 days, GFP signal intensity decreased rapidly in P-NPSCs and siTgfbr2-transfected Q-NPSCs, whereas both rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs maintained significantly higher fluorescence, indicating superior survival under oxidative stress (Fig.  E). Collectively, these findings indicate that rhTGF-β3–induced quiescence provides antioxidant protection comparable to natural Q-NPSC quiescence, while disruption of TGFBR2 compromises the ability of Q-NPSCs to withstand oxidative stress.

## TGF-β–induced quiescent NPSCs promote long-term disc regeneration in vivo
In a rat IVDD model, disc degeneration was first established by needle puncture, and NPSC transplantation was initiated 2 weeks after modeling. P-NPSCs, rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs, Q-NPSCs, or siTgfbr2-treated Q-NPSCs were injected into degenerated discs once per week for two consecutive weeks, and regenerative outcomes were evaluated at 8 weeks after modeling (corresponding to 6 weeks after the first cell transplantation) according to the experimental scheme (Fig.  A). X-ray imaging at this time point demonstrated that discs receiving rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs or Q-NPSCs exhibited marked preservation of disc height compared with those treated with untreated P-NPSCs or siTgfbr2-transfected Q-NPSCs (Fig.  B). Quantitative analysis further confirmed a significantly higher DHI in the rhTGF-β3 and Q-NPSC groups, whereas blockade of Tgfbr2 largely abolished this protective effect (Fig.  C). Consistently, histological evaluation using HE, SO, and TB staining revealed that rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs effectively preserved disc architecture, enhanced extracellular matrix reconstruction, and increased proteoglycan and glycosaminoglycan content within the nucleus pulposus. In contrast, discs receiving untreated P-NPSCs and siTgfbr2-treated Q-NPSCs showed pronounced structural disruption and reduced matrix deposition (Fig.  D). Quantitative histological scoring further supported these observations, showing significantly lower degeneration scores in the rhTGF-β3–treated P-NPSC and Q-NPSC groups compared with the PBS and untreated P-NPSC groups, whereas knockdown of Tgfbr2 in Q-NPSCs partially abolished this protective effect (Supplementary Fig. 4). These findings indicate that induction of a TGF-β–associated quiescent state in NPSCs confers sustained regenerative benefits in vivo during intervertebral disc repair.
 

## Discussion
Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is now recognized as a complex, multifactorial process in which oxidative stress plays a pivotal pathogenic role. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that excessive ROS promotes NP cell senescence, apoptosis and ECM degradation, thereby forming a vicious cycle that accelerates disc degeneration [ – , ]. Consistent with these reports, our analyses of human degenerated NP tissue, puncture-induced rat IVDD models and TBHP-treated NPSC cultures collectively confirmed that sustained high ROS levels constitute a common pathological feature of the degenerative disc microenvironment (Fig. ). Both mature NP cells and NP-derived stem/progenitor cells exhibit pronounced vulnerability to oxidative damage, resulting in progressive cell loss and compromised regenerative potential.
Stem cell-based therapy has emerged as a promising approach for IVDD regeneration, yet its efficacy remains limited by poor survival of transplanted cells in the harsh microenvironment of degenerated discs. Several studies have documented that most MSCs or NP progenitor cells fail to survive long-term following intradiscal injection due to hypoxia, nutrient deprivation and oxidative stress [ , ]. In our previous work, we observed that quiescent NPSCs (Q-NPSCs) survive significantly better in vivo than proliferating NPSCs (P-NPSCs) [ , ]. Notably, the quiescent program represents a reversible, non-proliferative G0 state with preserved reactivation potential, rather than an irreversible growth arrest or senescence-like phenotype that would compromise regenerative competence. Nevertheless, the biological basis underlying this survival advantage remained unclear. The present study provides direct mechanistic evidence demonstrating that quiescence fundamentally enhances the oxidative-stress tolerance of NPSCs.
Our in vitro results showed that Q-NPSCs exhibit markedly higher viability, lower ROS accumulation and reduced apoptosis under TBHP challenge compared with P-NPSCs (Fig. ). These findings align with reports from other adult stem-cell systems [ , ]. Quiescent hematopoietic stem cells and neural stem cells are well known to maintain low intracellular ROS levels and to possess enhanced resistance to genotoxic stress compared with activated cells [ , ]. Low metabolic activity and restrained mitochondrial respiration serve as central mechanisms limiting ROS production in dormant stem cells [ , ]. Consistent with this paradigm, our transcriptomic analysis revealed significant suppression of glycolysis, ATP metabolism and carbon metabolic pathways in Q-NPSCs, reinforcing the concept that reduced metabolic flux underlies ROS limitation in the quiescent state (Figs. and ). Importantly, serum starvation-induced quiescence in our system represents a reversible G0 state, as NPSCs readily re-enter the cell cycle upon serum re-stimulation. Although we did not directly examine whether reactivation under oxidative challenge abolishes the protective phenotype, the coordinated suppression of metabolic pathways and P53-associated apoptotic signaling observed in Q-NPSCs, together with the ability of TGF-β/SMAD activation to induce a comparable quiescence-associated stress-resistant program, strongly supports the notion that enhanced oxidative tolerance is intrinsically linked to the quiescent state rather than being a nonspecific consequence of nutrient deprivation.
Beyond metabolic reprogramming, regulation of apoptosis is critical for stem-cell preservation under stress. Our GO, KEGG and GSEA analyses identified significant downregulation of apoptosis-related processes, particularly apoptotic mitochondrial changes and response to hydrogen peroxide, in Q-NPSCs (Fig. ). Importantly, the canonical P53 signaling pathway emerged as one of the most significantly suppressed pathways. P53 serves as a master regulator of DNA damage responses; while basal P53 activity contributes to maintenance of stem-cell quiescence and genomic stability, excessive activation triggers mitochondrial apoptosis via Bax/Bak, cytochrome-c release and caspase activation [ , ]. Our qPCR and immunoblot validations confirmed decreased expression of P53, P21 and Bax, together with increased expression of the anti-apoptotic factor Bcl-2 in Q-NPSCs. These results suggest that quiescence protects NPSCs by attenuating the P53-mediated mitochondrial apoptotic cascade, thereby enabling cell survival under ROS-rich stress conditions.
A major novel contribution of this study is the identification of TGF-β3/TGFBR2/SMAD2 signaling as the upstream regulatory axis governing NPSC quiescence and stress resistance (Fig. ). Transcriptomic enrichment clearly demonstrated persistent activation of the TGF-β pathway in Q-NPSCs. Among its components, Tgfb3 and Tgfbr2 were selectively elevated, accompanied by increased SMAD2 phosphorylation. These molecular findings are consistent with prior studies showing that TGF-β signaling maintains quiescence in multiple adult stem-cell niches. In neural stem cells, TGF-β/SMAD signaling restrains cell-cycle progression and preserves dormancy [ , , ]. Similarly, in hematopoietic progenitors, TGF-β enforces G0 arrest and limits proliferative exhaustion [ , , ]. However, the role of TGF-β signaling in NPSCs had remained largely unexplored.
Functionally, our gain- and loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that exogenous rhTGF-β3 administration is sufficient to drive P-NPSCs into quiescence, as evidenced by reduced EdU incorporation, accumulation in the G0/G1 phase, increased P27 expression, and enhanced p-SMAD2 activation. Conversely, disruption of the pathway via Tgfbr2 knockdown or pharmacologic inhibition with SB431542 reversed these effects, restoring cell-cycle progression and reducing p-SMAD2 levels (Fig.  ). These observations establish TGF-β3/TGFBR2/SMAD2 as both necessary and sufficient for maintaining the quiescent phenotype of NPSCs.
Beyond controlling cell-cycle entry, activation of TGF-β signaling also exerted robust cytoprotective effects against oxidative stress. rhTGF-β3 significantly suppressed TBHP-induced ROS accumulation and inhibited apoptosis, whereas blockade of TGFBR2 abolished these benefits (Fig. A-D). Immunofluorescence further demonstrated that TGF-β activation downregulated mitochondrial apoptotic mediators, including P53 and cytochrome-c, consistent with inhibition of intrinsic apoptosis (Fig. E, F). Notably, crosstalk between TGF-β/SMAD signaling and the p53 network is well documented but highly context dependent; SMAD2/3 often cooperate with p53 at selected target promoters rather than directly repressing Tp53 transcription [ , ]. Thus, our data do not establish direct SMAD2/3 binding to the Tp53 promoter, and the observed decrease in P53-associated mitochondrial apoptotic signaling may instead reflect an indirect, functional attenuation of p53 pathway activity secondary to reduced oxidative burden and/or modulation of intermediate regulators (e.g., p21/CDKN1A). Future studies using SMAD2/3 ChIP-based assays and promoter-reporter analyses will be needed to determine whether this regulation is direct and to identify potential intermediates.
Importantly, the protective capacity conferred by pharmacologic quiescence induction closely mirrored that achieved through serum-starvation–induced “natural” quiescence (Fig.  ). In both static cultures and disc organ explants, rhTGF-β3–conditioned P-NPSCs and Q-NPSCs exhibited similarly elevated survival rates under oxidative challenge, while siTgfbr2-transfected Q-NPSCs rapidly lost this advantage. These results indicate that TGF-β–dependent quiescence is the core determinant of stress resistance.
 In vivo transplantation experiments further validated these conclusions. rhTGF-β3–preconditioned NPSCs and Q-NPSCs effectively preserved disc height and promoted ECM restoration in degenerated rat discs, whereas unconditioned P-NPSCs and siTgfbr2-modified cells exhibited minimal regenerative benefit. Although previous cell therapy studies reported modest structural improvement using unmodulated MSCs or NP progenitor cells [ , , , ], our approach demonstrates that preconditioning NPSCs into a protective quiescent state significantly enhances their long-term therapeutic efficacy.
Several limitations warrant discussion. First, all mechanistic experiments were performed using rat NPSCs; validation in human NP progenitors is essential for clinical translation. Second, our study primarily focused on canonical SMAD2-dependent TGF-β signaling. The potential contributions of non-canonical TGF-β pathways, including MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling, were not explored and remain to be clarified in future studies [ – ]. Third, the fate and mode of action of transplanted stem cells after intradiscal delivery remain incompletely understood. Increasing evidence suggests that the therapeutic benefits of stem cell transplantation are largely mediated by transient cell survival and paracrine effects rather than long-term engraftment or direct differentiation [ – ]. In this context, stem cell–derived exosomes have emerged as key effectors that modulate oxidative stress, inflammation, and extracellular matrix homeostasis within the degenerative microenvironment. Notably, accumulating studies indicate that quiescent stem cells exhibit a distinct and potentially superior exosomal profile, characterized by enhanced cytoprotective, anti-oxidative, and pro-regenerative cargo, which may underlie their improved therapeutic efficacy [ – ]. Whether quiescent NPSCs exert their reparative effects predominantly through exosome-mediated mechanisms warrants further investigation and represents an important direction for future studies.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, this work establishes a conceptual framework in which TGF-β3-driven quiescence coordinates metabolic suppression and inhibition of P53-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis to protect NPSCs from oxidative injury. These mechanisms afford transplanted cells improved survival and regenerative performance within the hostile microenvironment of degenerated discs (Fig.  ). Our findings not only advance understanding of stem-cell quiescence regulation in the disc niche but also propose a translational strategy - pharmacologic induction of a reversible, protective quiescent state - to enhance the success of cell-based IVDD therapies.
