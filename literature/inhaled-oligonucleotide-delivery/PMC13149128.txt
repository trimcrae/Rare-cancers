## INTRODUCTION
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a lung disease associated with inflammation and age. It is characterized by changes in lung function and lung structure caused by long-term inhalation of cigarette smoke (CS) and other toxic particles. The main clinical manifestations are persistent inflammation of airway and lung tissue, emphysema, and airway limitation ( ; ). COPD is not only a common disease that endangers public health, but also the most frequent reason for death from chronic respiratory diseases. It is also a leading cause of mortality worldwide, so COPD is a serious public health problem. The molecular mechanism of the pathogenesis and progression of COPD is very complex and has not been fully elucidated. Clinicians mainly use corticosteroids, β2 receptor agonists and other drugs to help patients relieve symptoms in the short term, but they cannot block the malignant progression of the disease ( ). Thus, revealing the mechanisms of disease progression and developing or finding new drugs with the help of reliable therapeutic sites holds significant clinical importance.
After CS enters the bronchus, it can induce reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in airway epithelial cells, cause inflammation and oxidative stress, change airway epithelial barrier function, lead to epithelial cell dysfunction, and promote airway epithelial cell senescence ( ). Senescent cells exhibits permanent cell cycle arrest, impaired cell function and intercellular communication, and damage to other adjacent cells ( ). Studies have shown that in the pathogenesis of COPD, the occurrence of senescence associated secretory phenotype (SASP) in bronchial epithelial cells is associated with excessive inflammatory response activation, and activated cells produce ROS ( ; ). In addition, mitochondrial dysfunction is a crucial factor for cell senescence ( ), which is characterized by the accumulation of mitochondrial DNA mutations, changes in mitochondrial quality control, decreased efficiency of biogenesis and clearance, and destruction of mitochondrial dynamics. Mitochondrial dysfunction and mitochondrial dynamics imbalance are also one of the pathogenesis of COPD ( ). Due to the continuous stimulation of CS, the mitochondrial quality control mechanism is often disordered, resulting in mitochondrial dysfunction ( ). Promoting the expression of mitochondrial fusion protein can reduce oxidative stress and lung cell senescence, thereby improving COPD ( ). The above results suggest that the positive feedback loop of inflammation, oxidative stress, senescence, and mitochondrial dysfunction may contribute to COPD pathogenesis.
Chinese medicine provides unique benefits in COPD therapy by virtue of its small side effects, high safety, and the characteristics of ‘multi-target, multi-level and multi-channel’ overall regulation. As an important medicine of the lung meridian, ephedra has the effects of sweating, relieving the exterior, dispersing the lung, relieving asthma, diuresis and detumescence, and is widely used in lung disease therapy. Mahuang Decoction can reduce airway inflammation and improve asthma in rats ( ). Houpu Mahuang Decoction has an inhibitory effect on asthma exacerbation caused by CS ( ). Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine (PSE) were the highest content in the chemical components of Ephedra sinica Stapf. A variety of effective components of ephedra have the effect of relieving cough and asthma, relieving bronchial smooth muscle, and is important for the treatment of lung diseases. Ephedrine can inhibit endoplasmic reticulum stress to improve COPD ( ). PSE has significant anti-inflammatory effects. The combination of PSE and emodin can regulate macrophage polarization and improve lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced pulmonary damage ( ). Nevertheless, the effect of PSE on COPD has not yet been elucidated, and whether its mechanism involves senescence and mitochondrial damage requires further explored.
Therefore, the effects of PSE on lung function and pathological damage of lung tissue were studied by constructing COPD cells and rat models based on senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction, and the possible mechanism of reducing lung tissue damage was discussed. It is hoped to offer novel clues for COPD clinical treatment and provide reference for the rational application and development of PSE.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Preparation of cigarette smoke extract (CSE)
Hongmei brand cigarettes (tar 10 mg/cig) were purchased from Hongta Tobacco Co., Ltd. (Yunnan, China). Preparation of extract containing 3 cigarette smoke. A 20 mL DMEM absorption solution was added to each of the two large Bauer tubes in series. One end of the tube was connected to a cigarette with the filter removed, and the other end was connected to a 50 mL syringe. After the cigarette is ignited, each cigarette is smoked 15 times, the smoke is extracted at a constant speed until the cigarette is burnt out, and the glass bottle is gently shaken to fully dissolve the smoke. The collected liquid was moved into a centrifuge tube, adjusted to pH 7.4 after filtration, and filtered through a 0.22 μm filter, that is, 100% CSE stock solution, stored at 4°C, and set aside. The 20% CSE solution required for the experiment was diluted with serum-free medium, and the prepared CSE must be used in the experiment within 30 min ( ).

## Cell culture and drug administration
Human normal lung bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) were purchased from SUNNCELL (SNL-203, Wuhan, China). BEAS-2B cells were routinely cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum in a 5% CO2 cell incubator at 37°C. When the cells grew to more than 80% confluence, they were passaged. The BEAS-2B cells were collected, cell suspension concentration was adjusted, and the cell count was performed using a blood cell counting plate. When the cell growth reached more than 80% in the incubator, the medium was discarded, and 0, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 400 μM PSE (E2625, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) was added to intervene for 24 h, or treated with 0, 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, and 160 μM PI3K pathway agonist 740Y-P (HY-P0175, MedChemExpress, USA) for 1 h. Then, 10 μL of CCK-8 working solution (96992, Sigma-Aldrich) was added to each well and incubated in dark for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured by a microplate reader (Synergy H1, BioTek, USA) to evaluate cell viability and screen for appropriate intervention doses of PSE and 740Y-P.
Subsequently, the CSE injury model was established and treated, and 3 mL of 20% CSE medium was added to each well plate cell for 24 h, and a control group was established. At the same time, 5, 10, 25, 50, and 100 μM PSE (E2625, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) were added for treatment for 24 h, respectively. The 25, 50, and 100 μM PSE treatment groups were recorded as PSE low, medium and high dose (CSE+L-PSE, CSE+M-PSE and CSE+H-PSE ) groups. In addition, cells in the CSE+PSE+740Y-P group were pretreated with 20 μM 740Y-P for 1 h, and then treated with 20% CSE and 50 μM PSE for 24 h.

## Flow cytometry experiments
After the intervention of BEAS-2B cells, the cells were digested with trypsin without EDTA, centrifuged and washed, and the supernatant was discarded. Cells were resuspended with 100 μL 1× binding buffer. Subsequently, 5 μL Annexin V-FITC dye (APOAF, Sigma-Aldrich) was added in turn and incubated in dark at room temperature for 15 min. Then 5 μL PI dye was added and incubated in dark for 5 min. After incubation, 400μL of binding buffer was added to the flow tube, and apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry (CytoFLEX, Beckman Coulter, USA).

## Establishment and administration of COPD rat model
Sixty-three 6-week-old SPF healthy male Wistar rats, weighing (170±30) g, were purchased from SiPeiFu (Beijing, China). Rats were raised in a barrier-level animal room at 25°C and relative humidity of 60%-70%. They were free to drink and eat, with normal circadian rhythm, and free to drink and eat. This experiment strictly abides by the ‘3R’ principle of experimental animals, and all animal experiment procedures are approved by Hebei Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine ethics committee.
The rats were randomly divided into control group, COPD group, COPD+PSE low, medium and high dose group (COPD+L-PSE, COPD+M-PSE and COPD+H-PSE), COPD+PSE treatment+PI3K/AKT agonist 740Y-P (COPD+PSE+740Y-P) group and COPD+PSE treatment+PI3K/AKT inhibitor LY294002 (COPD+PSE+LY294002) group, n=9 rats. The rat COPD disease model was established by the combination of LPS intratracheal instillation and smoking ( ). On the 1st and 14th day, the rats were anesthetized with 0.9% pentobarbital sodium (5 mL/kg), and LPS (200 μg/kg, 0.2 mL) solution was intratracheally instilled. Then the rats were kept upright and gently shaken for 20 s, so that LPS could diffuse evenly in the lungs. Passive smoking was performed on the 2nd-28th day of modeling (except the 1st and 14th days). The rats were placed in a self-made smoking box (85 cm×40 cm×45 cm) for smoking. Four cigarettes were lit each time, smoked for 30 min, and smoked twice a day, that is, eight cigarettes a day. The Control group did not smoke. Rats in the PSE administration groups were intragastrically administered with low, medium and high doses (10, 20, and 40 mg/(kg/d)). Rats in the 740Y-P and LY294002 groups were intraperitoneally injected with 10 mg/kg 740Y-P (Y860417, MACKLIN, Shanghai, China) and 15 mg/kg LY294002 (L832989, MACKLIN) 1 h before intragastric administration of PSE every day. Rats in the Control and COPD groups were intragastrically administered with the same amount of distilled water once a day for 28 days.
After the last administration, the rats were fasted for 24 h. The pulmonary function indexes of tidal volume (TV), peak expiratory flow (PEF), minute ventilation (MV) and expiratory flow at 50% of tidal volume (EF50) were detected by the animal lung function analysis system (FinePointe WBP, DSI Buxco, USA). After the lung function test was completed, record the body weight. The rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 0.9% pentobarbital sodium. The abdomen was cut open along the median line, and the abdominal aorta was isolated and exposed. The blood was taken in a sterile vacuum blood collection tube with separation gel and coagulant, and stored in a refrigerator at 4°C. After the blood was taken, it was centrifuged (centrifugal radius 10.5 cm, Centrifuge 5418 R, Eppendorf, Germany), and the separated and labeled serum was stored in a –80°C medical low-temperature refrigerator. After the blood was taken, the thoracic cavity was quickly opened, and the lung tissue was bluntly separated along the main bronchus. The complete left lung tissue was dried and weighed using a high-precision electronic balance. The weight at this time was the wet weight of the lung. The lung tissue was dried to a constant weight in an oven at 60°C, and the weight at this time was the dry weight of the lung. The wet/dry (W/D) ratio of lung tissue was calculated based on the measured data of lung wet weight and lung dry weight. At the same time, the heart, liver, spleen and kidney tissues of the mice were collected. The heart, liver, spleen, kidney and the upper lobe of the right lung were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and the remaining lung tissue was quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen with a cryopreservation tube, and then transferred to a –80°C low temperature refrigerator for subsequent experiments.

## Pathological staining
Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining: The upper lobe of the right lung was fixed for 24 h, dehydrated and transparent, embedded in paraffin and sliced (5 μm). After gradient ethanol dewaxing to water, hematoxylin staining of the nucleus, hydrochloric acid ethanol differentiation and water washing, blueing, eosin staining of the cytoplasm, neutral gum sealing, and finally placed under a microscopic camera to observe the pathological structure of the lung tissue. Mean alveolar number (MAN, mm2), mean linear intercept (MLI, μm) and mean small airway wall thickness (μm) were measured using ImageJ software.
Alcian blue-periodic acid-Schiff (AB-PAS) staining: Paraffin sections of lung tissue were dewaxed with xylene, hydrated with gradient ethanol, stained with 1% Alcian blue (pH 2.5) for 20 min, then treated with 0.5% periodate oxidation solution at room temperature for 10 min, and then incubated with Schiff’s reagent at room temperature for 20 min. The nucleus was re-stained with hematoxylin for 2 min, rinsed with running water, and finally sealed with neutral gum. The sections were placed under a microscope camera to observe the central and peripheral airways. The acidic mucus material was blue, and the neutral and mixed mucus material was red or purple. The degree of AB-PAS positive staining was semi-quantitatively determined using a five-level grading system: grade 0, no AB-PAS staining; grade 1, the proportion of AB-PAS staining in airway epithelial cells was 25% or lower; grade 2, the proportion of AB-PAS staining in airway epithelial cells was 26% to 50%; grade 3, the proportion of AB-PAS staining in airway epithelial cells was 51% to 75%; grade 4, the proportion of AB-PAS staining in airway epithelial cells was more than 75%.
Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining: BEAS-2B cells were stained using the senescence-associated SA-β-gal staining kit (C0602, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the experimental manual. First, BEAS-2B cells were cleaned with PBS buffer for 3 times, 5 min each time. BEAS-2B cells were fixed with 1 mL SA-β-gal staining fixative for 15 min, and SA-β-gal staining working solution (X-Gal solution, SA-β-gal staining solution A, B, C ratio of 5:1:1:93) was configured and added to the hole, 2 mL per hole. It was sealed with cling film and incubated at 37°C for 24 h. After staining, the samples were washed with 70% ethanol and added with PBS buffer. The images were observed and photographed under an inverted microscope (CHL2FM3, Olympus, Japan). Five groups of visual fields were randomly selected, and the positively stained cells was counted. Under the inverted microscope, the cells with obvious blue granular deposition in the cytoplasm were judged as senescent positive cells, and the cells without coloration or only weak background color were judged as negative. At the same time, the fresh rat lung tissue was frozen sectioned and fixed with SA-β-gal staining fixative for 30 min according to the kit instructions. The volume was appropriate to fully cover the tissue and rinsed. SA-β-gal staining working solution was added, incubated overnight at 37°C, and the staining solution was discarded. After observing and photographing under a microscope, the proportion of senescent cells was calculated.

## Lung permeability index (LPI)
The lung tissue was exposed by thoracotomy, and the right main bronchus was ligated by hemostatic forceps. 4 mL of sterile medium was slowly injected into the left lung, and the liquid was withdrawn. The bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was repeatedly lavaged for 3 times, and about 10 mL of BALF was recovered. The collected BALF and blood were centrifuged at 4°C, 1 500 r/min for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. The total protein of BALF and serum was detected by BCA protein quantification kit (G2026, Servicevio, Wuhan, China). LPI was calculated, LPI=BALF total protein/serum total protein.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) detection
Tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6 and IL-10 ELISA kits (MM-0180R2, MM-0047R2, MM-0190R2, MM-0195R2, MEIMIAN, Jiangsu, China) were used for detection. The lung tissues of rats in each group were homogenized with normal saline, and the supernatant was collected after centrifugation (4°C, 10 000 r/min, 10 min). Microplate reader (EPOCH2, BioTek, USA) was used to detect the OD value at 450 nm and calculate the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-10.

## Kit detection
0.1 g of lung tissue was added with 1 mL of extract, homogenized in ice bath, centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected. Myeloperoxidase (MPO), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), superoxide dismutase (SOD) and malondialdehyde (MDA) kits (BC5715, BC1315, BC5165, BC6410, Solarbio, Beijing, China) were used to analyze MPO, T-AOC, SOD and MDA levels in lung tissue and serum according to the supplier’s protocol. The experimental operation was carried out following kit instructions, and the OD values were detected by microplate reader.
Approximately 200 mg of lung tissue cryopreserved at –80°C was taken, 2 mL of lysate was added, homogenized on ice, centrifuged, and the supernatant was taken. BEAS-2B cells were added with 200 μL lysate and centrifuged (12 000 r/min, 5 min) to remove the supernatant. Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content was determined according to ATP assay kit (S0026, Beyotime). 100 μL ATP detection working solution was added to 20 μL supernatant, and the OD value was measured by microplate reader. The ATP content in lung tissues was calculated following the standard curve, and the protein concentration of the sample was determined by BCA kit (P0010, Beyotime).

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
The paraffin sections of lung tissue were dewaxed and hydrated, incubated in 3% H2O2 for 10 min, and blocked with 5% BSA for 10 min. Matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2, ab86607, 1:200, Abcam, UK), MMP9 (ab76003, 1:1000, Abcam) and cytochrome C oxidase (DF8920, 1:100, Affinity, Jiangsu, China) were added dropwise, incubated at 37°C for 1 h. A proper amount of HRP-labeled IgG secondary antibody (ab7090,1:1000, Abcam) solution was added, incubated for 1 h, colored with DAB solution, counterstained with hematoxylin, and sealed with neutral gum. Nuclear and cytoplasmic staining was brownish yellow or brown as positive, and non-overlapping fields were randomly selected. The stained sections were evaluated under a microscope and photographed.

## DCFH-DA fluorescent probe
The rat lung tissue was frozen in liquid nitrogen and frozen sections were made. The frozen sections of the cut tissue were placed for 20 min. The cells were stained according to the instructions of DCFH-DA fluorescence kit and incubated with 4,6-diamidino2-phenyl-indole dihydrochloride (DAPI) in dark for 2 min. Observe and take photos immediately under a fluorescence microscope (vhx-5000, Leica, Germany). Image J software was used to analyze the DCFH-DA fluorescence staining slice images of lung tissue in each group. Red fluorescence was ROS staining.

## Immunofluorescence (IF)
BEAS-2B cells in each group were taken for cell climbing, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, infiltrated with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min, and blocked with 5% goat serum for 1 h. Paraffin sections of rat lung tissue were taken, dewaxed with xylene, hydrated with gradient ethanol, and boiled in sodium citrate buffer for 20 min to repair antigen. 5% goat serum was added and incubated for 30 min. The excess blocking solution was discarded, and P21 (AF6290, 1:500, Affinity, Jiangsu, China), phosphorylated histones H2AX (γH2AX, ab81299, 1:250, Abcam) and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1, ab307799, 1:500, Abcam) were added as primary antibodies and incubated overnight at 4°C. The next day, PBS buffer was washed again, and Alexa Fluor 488 labeled secondary antibody (ab150077, 1:200, Abcam) was added to incubate for 2 h. DAPI solution was used to stain the nucleus for 15 min, and the anti-quenching tablet was sealed. The staining was observed under a fluorescence microscope, the image was taken.

## Western blot
Radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysate (G2002, Servicebio) was used to extract the total protein of lung tissue and BEAS-2B cells in each group, and the protein concentration was determined by BCA method (P0009, Beyotime). Protein samples were denatured by boiling, separated by 10% SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked overnight with TBST containing 5% skim milk. The primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4°C. The next day, the IgG secondary antibody (ab97080,1:5000, Abcam) was incubated. After full washing, the membrane was developed by ECL and imaged by gel imager (UVP ChemiStudio815, Analytik Jena, Germany). The gray value of the band was analyzed by Image J software.
Primary antibodies: ZO-1 (ab307799, Abcam), Occludin (OCC, ab224526, Abcam), P21 (AF6290, Affinity), P16 (ab51243, Abcam), γH2AX (ab81299, Abcam), optic atrophy 1 (Opa1, ab119685, Abcam), mitofusin 1 (Mfn1, ab221661, ab191853, Abcam), dynamin-related protein 1 (Drp1, ab184248, Abcam), mitochondrial fission 1 (FIS1, ab286127, Abcam), B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2, ab32124, Abcam), Bcl-2 associated X protein (Bax, ab182733, Abcam), caspase-3 (ab184787, Abcam), Cleaved caspase-3 (ab32042, Abcam), phosphoinositide 3-kinase p85 (PI3K p85, p85, AF6242, Affinity), p-PI3K p85 (p-p85, AF3242, Affinity), protein kinase B (AKT, ab233755, Abcam), p-AKT (ab278565, Abcam), mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR, ab32028, Abcam), p-mTOR (AF3308, Affinity) and GAPDH (ab181603, 1:10 000, Abcam), dilution ratio was 1:1 000.

## TUNEL staining
Paraffin sections of lung tissue were routinely dewaxed to water, and antigen retrieval was performed using sodium citrate. Proteinase K without DNase was incubated for 20 min, and Triton was incubated for 8 min. At the same time, BEAS-2B cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and then permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 5 min. The TUNEL reaction solution (G1504, Servicebio) was added to the lung tissue sections and cells. The TUNEL reaction solution was incubated for 1 h, and reacted with DAB for 10 min. After washing, the sections were observed under a fluorescence microscope. The green fluorescence was positive for apoptotic cells and the blue was the nucleus.

## Transmission electron microscope (TEM)
BEAS-2B cells in each group were scraped with a cell scraper, centrifuged, and the supernatant was discarded. The precooled 2.5% glutaraldehyde was slowly added along the tube wall to fix the cells and stored at 4°C overnight. Immobilize with 1% OsO4 for 1.5 h at 4°C, then wash with PBS for 3 times. The cell samples were dehydrated by gradient concentration of ethanol, and finally treated with anhydrous acetone for 20 min. The sample was treated in a mixture of acetone and epoxy resin Ep812 (GP2001, Servicebio) for 4 h, followed by overnight in epoxy resin Ep812. The samples were cut into 80 nm-thick sections using an ultra-thin slicing machine, and stained with uranium acetate and lead citrate for 5 min. Finally, the ultrastructure of mitochondria was observed using a transmission electron microscope (JEM1400, Jeol, Japan).
Part of the lung tissue was cut into 1 mm3 pieces and immersed in a sufficient amount of electron microscope fixative (G1102, Servicebio) for 4 h in the dark. The acetone was dehydrated step by step, and the epoxy resin Ep812 was embedded. Ultramicrotome was used to cut into 80 nm thick sections, which were stained with uranium acetate and lead citrate respectively. Finally, transmission electron microscopy was used for image acquisition and analysis to observe the ultrastructure of mitochondria in lung tissue.

## JC-1 fluorescent probe evaluated mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) level
BEAS-2B cells were incubated with 2 mL JC-1 working solution (C2003S, Beyotime) for 30 min. Frozen sections were prepared from 3 mm3 lung tissue, and JC-1 working droplets were added to the frozen sections and incubated in the dark for 30 min. Then the images were observed and collected under a fluorescence microscope. The red and green fluorescence intensities were analyzed by Image J software.

## MitoSOX Red detected ROS levels
After BEAS-2B cells were treated, the culture medium was discarded, and the sterile PBS was gently shaken and washed three times, and the PBS was sucked. The prepared MitoSox Red (5 μmol/L) solution was added and incubated for 30 min. PBS was washed twice to remove unbound probes. Frozen rat liver tissue was cut into 10 μm thick frozen sections. The sections were incubated with MitoSOX red fluorescent probe at 37°C in dark for 20 min. The stained sections were stained with DAPI staining solution at room temperature for 10 min in dark. Fluorescence microscope was used to observe the image and collect the image. Image J software was used to analyze the fluorescence intensity and calculate the ROS level.

## Molecular docking
The 3D structure of PSE was downloaded from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) database, the MOL2 structure was obtained from TCMSP, and the PDB format file of PI3K protein was downloaded from RCSB PDB (https://www.rcsb.org/) database. Subsequently, Pymol 2.6.0 software was used to ‘remove water molecules’ and ‘remove hydrogen atoms’ of the target protein. Import the above files into AutoDockTools-1.5.7, and process and save them as pdbqt format files. The docking result files and docking energy were obtained by running Autogrid and Autodock. The lower the binding energy between the ligand and the receptor protein, the more stable the molecular binding and the higher the possibility of interaction. Pymol 2.6.0 software was used to visualize the results.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 27.0. Normality and homogeneity of variance were assessed for all data sets. One-way ANOVA was utilized, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for group comparisons. Data for each group were presented as mean ± standard deviation, with p<0.05 deemed statistically significant.

## RESULTS

## PSE ameliorated cigarette smoke-induced bronchial epithelial cell senescence of COPD
Airway epithelium serves as first defensive barrier of the respiratory system and the first tissue that directly interacts with smoke. CS induced epithelial cell dysfunction and promoted airway epithelial cell senescence, which may be the main factor and early event of COPD ( ). Firstly, the cell safety of PSE and its protective effect on CSE injury were evaluated by CCK-8 assay. The results showed that PSE had no significant effect on the viability of normal BEAS-2B cells in the concentration range of 0-100 μM. When the concentration was increased to 200 μM and 400 μM, the cell viability decreased significantly ( ). Subsequently, CSE induction significantly reduced the viability of BEAS-2B cells, while 25, 50, and 100 μM PSE could restore cell viability in a dose-dependent manner ( ), so 25, 50, and 100 μM PSE were selected as L-PSE, M-PSE and H-PSE for subsequent experiments. Secondly, in this study, BEAS-2B cells are treated using CSE and PSE to observe the bronchial epithelial cell senescence response. After CSE induction, a large blue positive area appeared in SA-β-gal staining of BEAS-2B cells, suggesting a rise in the senescent cell number. PSE treatment reduced the number of senescent cells ( , ). Cell senescence is usually caused by irreversible cell cycle arrest caused by a replicative positive feedback mechanism, which is characterized by potential apoptosis and the emergence of SASP ( ). CSE significantly increased the fluorescence intensity of P21 (a cell cycle-inhibiting protein) and γH2AX (a DNA damage marker), and significantly decreased the fluorescence intensity of ZO-1, indicating that CSE induced BEAS-2B cell cycle arrest and caused airway epithelial damage. After PSE treatment, the fluorescence intensity of P21 and γH2AX decreased significantly, and the fluorescence intensity of ZO-1 increased significantly ( - ). ZO-1 is a core protein that maintains the integrity of the airway epithelial barrier, and its decreased expression and disordered localization are signs of epithelial barrier dysfunction. PSE could restore the expression and correct localization of ZO-1, suggesting that it had the effect of protecting and repairing the airway epithelial barrier. Western blot also confirmed this result. PSE markedly increased ZO-1 and OCC proteins and significantly inhibited P21, P16 and γH2AX proteins ( - ). TUNEL results showed that the apoptosis rate elevated markedly after CSE induction and decreased significantly after PSE treatment ( , ). Flow cytometry experiments also confirmed that CSE treatment significantly increased the total apoptosis rate of cells, while PSE significantly inhibited apoptosis ( , ). Western blot analysis of apoptosis-related proteins showed that CSE treatment decreased the expression of Bcl-2, increased the expression of Bax and Cleaved caspase-3, and PSE treatment restored these protein expression levels in a dose-dependent manner ( , ). The above experiments showed that PSE inhibited the bronchial epithelial cell senescence process induced by CSE, including improving cell cycle arrest and inhibiting apoptosis.

## PSE inhibited cigarette smoke-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in COPD cells
Mitochondrial homeostasis can be disrupted by tobacco smoke, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction. TEM showed that the mitochondrial structure and morphology of normal BEAS-2B cells were relatively regular, the inner ridge structure was clear, and the cell membrane was basically intact. After CSE induction, the mitochondria swelled, the volume increased, the internal cristae blurred, fractured, or even disappeared, showing vacuolar changes, and the cell membrane ruptured seriously. PSE alleviated the above mitochondrial damage ( ). JC-1 fluorescent probe detected the change of MMP level. After CSE induction, red fluorescence weakened, green fluorescence enhanced, and MMP level decreased. After the addition of PSE, the MMP level was increased ( , ), suggesting that PSE improved mitochondrial dysfunction. And we also detected a considerable reduction in ATP level after CSE induction and a considerable rise after PSE treatment ( ). MitoSOX Red results showed that ROS levels increased significantly after CSE induction and decreased significantly after PSE treatment ( , ). Mitochondrial fusion is mediated by mitochondrial outer membrane protein Mfn1 and inner membrane protein Opa1, which is essential for maintaining mitochondrial network structure, functional complementation and content exchange. Mitochondrial fission is driven by the recruitment of cytoplasmic protein Drp1 to mitochondrial outer membrane receptors (such as FIS1) and assembly into a ring structure, which is essential for removing damaged mitochondria and regulating mitochondrial quality. Finally, CSE induction significantly reduced Opa1 and Mfn1 protein expressions, and increased Drp1 and FIS1 protein expressions. PSE treatment enhanced mitochondrial fusion and suppressed excessive fission of mitochondria ( - ). In conclusion, CSE induction could lead to mitochondrial function damage in COPD cells, while PSE could improve this damage and inhibit mitochondrial dysfunction.

## PSE inhibited PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway to improve cigarette smoke-induced bronchial epithelial cell senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction of COPD
In order to explore the molecular mechanism of PSE improving cigarette-induced bronchial epithelial cell senescence of COPD, we first predicted and confirmed that PSE could bind to PI3K protein stably by molecular docking, and its binding energy was –6.4 kcal/mol ( ). In general, the binding energy ≤ -5.0 kcal/mol indicates that the material component has a good binding to the docking target protein. Western blot results showed that CSE could significantly increase the expression levels of p-p85, p-AKT and p-mTOR proteins and activate the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, while PSE could inhibit the phosphorylation level of this pathway in a dose-dependent manner ( , ). In order to verify the role of this pathway, we first determined the concentration of PI3K specific activator 740Y-P. CCK-8 assay showed that 0-20 μM 740Y-P treatment had no significant effect on the viability of BEAS-2B cells ( ). 5, 10, and 20 μM 740Y-P could significantly increase the level of p-p85 protein, and the promotion effect of 20 μM 740Y-P was the most significant ( , ). Subsequently, CSE-induced BEAS-2B cells were treated with 20 μM 740Y-P and 50 μM PSE. Compared with PSE alone, 740Y-P significantly increased the expression levels of p-p85, p-AKT and p-mTOR proteins ( , ), increased the number of senescent cells ( , ) and apoptosis rate ( , ), promoted the expression of Bax and Cleaved caspase-3 proteins, and reduced the expression of Bcl-2 protein ( , ). This established the key role of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in mediating the anti-aging and anti-apoptotic effects of PSE. Further mitochondrial function tests showed that 740Y-P co-treatment again reduced MMP levels ( , ) and ATP production ( ), increased ROS levels ( , ), reduced Opa1 and Mfn1 protein levels, and increased Drp1 and FIS1 protein levels ( , ), confirming that the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway was a key target pathway for PSE to exert its efficacy. The above results indicated that PSE improved bronchial epithelial cell senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction of COPD by targeting inhibition of excessive activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway.

## PSE improved lung function decline and pathological damage in COPD rats
Long-term CS exposure is the main risk element to COPD. A rat model of COPD was constructed by systemic CS exposure combined with LPS tracheal instillation, and PSE was used for treatment to view lung tissue change in rats. The body weight in COPD rats was significantly reduced ( ), lung function was also significantly decreased, and TV, MV, PEF and EF50 were significantly reduced ( - ). PSE increased body weight and improved lung function in rats ( - ). The lung tissue structure of normal rats was basically intact, and there was no congestion and edema around the mucosa and airway wall, only scattered individual inflammatory cells. The alveolar space of COPD rats was enlarged, the septum ruptured and fused into pulmonary bullae, the airway lumen was narrowed, many secretions and inflammatory cell infiltration were observed, which was consistent with the pathological changes of COPD pulmonary tissues, and MAN was significantly reduced, MLI and mean small airway wall thickness were significantly increased. The alveolar space of each PSE group was enlarged and ruptured, with minimal inflammatory cell infiltration visible. The pathological changes under the microscope were less than those in the model group, MAN increased significantly, MLI and mean small airway wall thickness decreased significantly ( - ). Lung W/D ratio and LPI were used as indicators to evaluate lung permeability. After cigarette smoking and LPS interference, the W/D ratio and LPI of rat lung raised significantly, indicating that pulmonary edema and permeability increased. PSE inhibited pulmonary edema and increased permeability in COPD rats ( , ). AB-PAS staining showed that a large number of AB-PAS positive substances were accumulated in the airway of COPD rats, and goblet cell hyperplasia was obvious, indicating that there were significant pathological changes of mucus hypersecretion. PSE treatment dose-dependently reduced the area and intensity of airway AB-PAS positive area, and airway mucus blockage was significantly alleviated ( , ). In summary, PSE significantly inhibited the decline of lung function and alleviated pathological change in COPD rats. In order to evaluate the safety of PSE administration, we performed histological examinations on the heart, liver, spleen and kidney of rats. The results of HE staining showed that the morphological structure of the main organs of the rats in each dose of PSE treatment group was intact, and no obvious pathological changes were observed, such as neat arrangement of myocardial fibers, clear structure of hepatocyte cords, normal morphology of glomeruli and renal tubules, and clear boundary between white pulp and red pulp of spleen. This was not significantly different from the COPD group or the Control group ( ). This indicated that PSE did not produce significant major organ toxicity in rats during the dose and cycle of this experiment, and had good drug safety.

## PSE ameliorated chronic airway inflammation and oxidative response in cigarette smoke and LPS-induced COPD rats
Senescence of lung cells leads to the release of SASP, causing the production of inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, MMP2 and MMP9 ( ). IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α contents of COPD rats were significantly raised, and IL-10 level was significantly decreased, suggesting that COPD rats had inflammatory response. PSE significantly inhibited pro-inflammatory factor contents, enhanced anti-inflammatory factor contents ( - ), and inhibited inflammatory injury. The generation of hypochlorous acid from hydrogen peroxide and chloride ions, catalyzed by neutrophil-derived MPO, serves as a key indicator of enhanced oxidative stress and leads to ROS release ( ). As a marker of neutrophil activation, MPO level was markedly increased in COPD rats and markedly decreased after PSE treatment ( ), suggesting that PSE inhibited oxidative stress and inflammation. MMP2 and MMP9 expressions were markedly enhanced within COPD rats and significantly decreased after PSE treatment ( - ). At the same time, we detected indicators related to oxidative stress. DCFH-DA fluorescent probe detected that ROS levels were also significantly reduced after PSE treatment ( , ). T-AOC and SOD contents in COPD rat serum were markedly decreased, and MDA content was significantly elevated. PSE increased T-AOC and SOD contents, and decreased MDA level ( - ). These experiments suggested that PSE could inhibit oxidative damage in COPD rat pulmonary tissues.

## PSE inhibited lung tissue senescence induced by cigarette smoke and LPS in COPD rats
The effects of PSE on lung tissue senescence was explored again in rats, which was consistent with the results of cell experiments. Large blue senescent cells appeared in COPD rat lungs, and PSE reduced the number of senescent cells ( , ). IF and Western blot data showed that ZO-1 and OCC protein levels in COPD rat lung tissue were markedly decreased, and P21, P16 and γH2AX expressions were markedly increased. PSE significantly reversed the above protein levels ( - ). Finally, a large number of apoptotic cells appeared in the lungs of COPD rats, and PSE inhibited apoptosis ( , ). In conclusion, PSE inhibited lung tissue senescence of COPD rats.

## PSE inhibited mitochondrial dysfunction induced by cigarette smoke and LPS in COPD rats
Loss of mitochondrial fusion protein function leads to mitochondrial fragmentation and increased ROS production, which in turn activates the P53-P21 pathway and induces cell cycle arrest and SASP ( ; ). Studies have found that up-regulation of Mfn2 and Opa1 can reduce COPD lung cell senescence and mitochondrial phagocytosis ( ), so this study next explored the effect of PSE on pulmonary mitochondrial dysfunction in rats. TEM showed that the mitochondria in normal rat lung tissue were abundant, the morphological structure was clear, the inner and outer membranes were intact, the number of internal ridges was large and neatly arranged, and the typical double membrane structure was observed, indicating that the mitochondrial function was normal. In COPD rats, the boundary of mitochondrial inner and outer membranes was blurred, mitochondria were swollen, the internal ridge structure was defective and reduced, and vacuolar degeneration was observed in many places. After PSE therapy, the mitochondrial structure was improved, the inner and outer membranes were relatively complete, and the internal mitochondrial ridge was obvious ( ). MMP ( , ) and ATP ( ) contents in lungs of COPD rats were significantly reduced, and ROS ( , ) level was markedly increased. PSE increased MMP and ATP levels and decreased ROS levels ( - ). Cytochrome C oxidase can catalyze the last step of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, and it serves as a reliable marker for mitochondrial respiratory function and structural stability ( ; ). IHC results showed that cytochrome C oxidase levels were significantly reduced in COPD rats and significantly increased after PSE treatment ( , ). Finally, PSE markedly enhanced Opa1 and Mfn1 proteins, and declined Drp1 and FIS1 proteins ( - ). In summary, PSE significantly improved mitochondrial damage induced by cigarette smoke and LPS, regulated mitochondrial fission, and thus inhibited mitochondrial dysfunction in COPD rats.

## PSE inhibited lung tissue senescence and inflammation in COPD rats via suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
Blocking the PIK3C3/AKT/mTOR pathway and improving chronic inflammation can reduce COPD development ( ). By suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling, β-elemene can reduce CS-triggered inflammation and oxidation reactions in human bronchial epithelial cells ( ). Therefore, this research explored the roles of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in the improvement of COPD by PSE. p-p85, p-AKT and p-mTOR proteins in COPD rat lung tissues were significantly increased, suggesting that PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis was activated. PSE inhibited PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling ( , ). After that, COPD rats were intraperitoneally injected with 10 mg/kg PI3K/AKT agonist 740Y-P and 15 mg/kg inhibitor LY294002 every day, and 40 mg/kg PSE was given by gavage 1 h later for 28 days. The results showed that compared with PSE treatment, 740Y-P markedly elevated p-p85, p-AKT and p-mTOR proteins, and LY294002 markedly declined these proteins ( , ). This indicated that PSE suppressed PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling. Furthermore, 740Y-P significantly increased senescent cell number ( , ) and IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α contents ( - ), markedly enhanced P21, P16, and γH2AX protein expression, and reduced ZO-1 and OCC proteins ( - ), indicating that activating PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling promoted lung tissue senescence and inflammation in COPD rats. LY294002 had the opposite effect. In summary, PSE improved lung tissue senescence and inflammation through suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis, thereby improving COPD.

## PSE inhibited cigarette smoke and LPS-induced mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidation reaction in COPD rats through PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
In addition, compared with PSE treatment, 740Y-P significantly increased ROS levels ( , ), reduced MMP ( , ) and ATP ( ) levels, and 740Y-P also significantly reduced Opa1 and Mfn1 proteins, increased Drp1 and FIS1 proteins ( , ), indicating that activating PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway promoted mitochondrial excessive fission in COPD rats, triggering mitochondrial dynamics imbalance, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction; LY294002 enhanced the inhibitory effect of PSE on mitochondrial dysfunction. In addition, LY294002 increased T-AOC and SOD contents and declined MDA content ( - ), indicating that suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling alleviated oxidative stress in COPD rats induced by cigarette smoke and LPS. In summary, PSE improved pulmonary mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress via suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis, thereby improving COPD.

## DISCUSSION
This study reported for the first time that PSE can effectively alleviate CSE-induced bronchial epithelial cell senescence, mitochondrial dysfunction and cell damage, and verified its improvement effect on lung function and pathology in COPD animal models. The key innovation is that we have revealed for the first time a new mechanism by which PSE rescues mitochondrial function and blocks the aging process by inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. This study not only provides new ideas for the new use of old drugs, but also provides a new target perspective for the pathogenesis and treatment intervention of COPD.
By inducing inflammation and oxidation reactions in airway epithelial cells, CS promotes cell injury and shedding, weakens or loses their functions, leading to the occurrence of COPD ( ). Because COPD seriously threatens the life and health of patients, and there is no specific therapeutic drug, seeking new therapeutic drugs has become the focus of current research. CS is the most common inducer of COPD animal models ( ). LPS is a kind of endotoxin, which can stimulate monocytes and endothelial cells to release inflammatory mediator when it enters the lungs, thus inducing inflammation in the lungs and airways of animals. The establishment of COPD animal model by intratracheal instillation of LPS combined with smoking method can successfully simulate the pathological changes of COPD, such as chronic lung inflammation, airway resistance, lung tissue injury, emphysema, etc., and can greatly shorten the modeling time. Therefore, in this experiment, the COPD rat model was successfully established by smoking combined with LPS tracheal instillation. The body weight, TV, MV, PEF and EF50 were significantly reduced, and lung W/D ratio and LPI were significantly increased. A large number of secretions and inflammatory cell infiltration were observed in lung tissues, MAN was markedly lessened, MLI and mean small airway wall thickness were significantly increased, confirming the destruction of alveolar septum and the expansion of alveolar cavity. PSE had a certain improvement effect on lung function decline, pulmonary edema and histopathological damage induced by CS+LPS in rats, and significantly increases MAN and reduces MLI. MLI and MAN are classical morphological indicators for evaluating emphysema. This finding indicated that the protective effect of PSE could directly act on the lung parenchyma and inhibit the destruction of alveolar structure, thus interfering with the progression of COPD. Moreover, AB-PAS staining showed that PSE effectively reduced mucus hypersecretion in the airway of COPD rats, which might be a comprehensive effect achieved by inhibiting inflammation and restoring epithelial barrier function, suggesting that it also had an improvement effect on chronic bronchitis components. HE staining analysis of main organs further confirmed that PSE did not show obvious systemic toxicity such as liver, kidney and heart at the dose of this experiment, which provided a preliminary basis for its safety.
Studies have shown that CS recruits inflammatory cells in the airways, releases inflammatory factors (TNF-α and IL-6), and promotes ROS production in the mitochondria of airway epithelial cells, causing oxidative damage to mitochondria and promoting COPD development ( ; ). Among them, TNF-α is one of the earliest factors to initiate the inflammatory response. It can promote inflammatory factor release by recruiting neutrophils, lymphocytes and macrophages, and aggravate the inflammatory response. It can also promote airway epithelial goblet cell proliferation and excessive release of mucin, aggravate airway obstruction and aggravate COPD. IL-6 is important for inducing and maintaining respiratory inflammation. It is a risk factor for repeated onset of COPD patients and can be used to predict the frequency of exacerbation of COPD ( ). Inhibiting inflammatory factor expressions can exert a protective effect in the occurrence and development of COPD ( ). In addition, inhibition of TNF-α, IL-6 contents and ROS production, increased SOD levels, can inhibit CS-induced inflammation, oxidative stress, reduce apoptosis, inhibit airway remodeling and COPD ( ). The increase of inflammatory cells and inflammatory mediators, the increase of lipid oxidation level and the decrease of antioxidant level are common in COPD ( ; ). This study also confirms this. IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, MPO and ROS contents in COPD rats were markedly increased, and IL-10, T-AOC and SOD contents were markedly decreased. PSE reduced pro-inflammatory factors and ROS production, increased T-AOC and SOD contents, indicating that PSE could improve COPD by inhibiting inflammation and oxidative stress.
Senescence is characterized by loss of cell replication, cell cycle arrest, up-regulation of SASP factors ( ). Among them, overexpression of cell cycle arrest proteins (P21 and P16) and SA-β-gal positive staining are classic markers for identifying senescent cells. Cellular senescence leads to increased SA-β-gal activities ( ); in addition, γH2AX positive staining is also considered to be one of the markers of senescent cells ( ). These cellular senescence characteristics can not only identify senescent hepatocytes, but also provide targets for intervention of anti-aging drugs. Lu et al. confirmed that miR-377-3p can increase SA-β-gal positive cells and up-regulate P21 and P53 proteins, thereby inducing cell senescence and exacerbating COPD ( ). The data of this research found that in cigarette smoke and LPS-induced COPD rat pulmonary tissues and BEAS-2B cells, there were characteristic senescence phenotypes, γ-H2AX positive staining, and P21 and P16 protein expression levels were up-regulated. In this study, we found that cigarette smoke and LPS could induce senescence of bronchial epithelial cells and lung tissues, showing cell cycle arrest and increased expression of senescence-associated proteins. PSE could reverse the above phenomenon, indicating that PSE could delay bronchial epithelial cell and lung tissue senescence.
The airway epithelial barrier can resist the invasion of harmful substances such as air allergens, pollutants and pathogens. It can also initiate an immune response in time to remove harmful substances and maintain the homeostasis of the airway microenvironment. The destruction of the airway epithelial barrier is an important factor that causes airway pathological changes and promotes the occurrence and development of COPD ( ). Airway epithelial cells constitute a complete barrier through OCC, ZO-1 and other tight junction proteins and adhesion junction proteins, which is the first natural barrier for the respiratory system to contact with the outside world. CSE can reduce ZO-1 and OCC proteins and destroy COPD mice epithelial barrier ( ). Enhanced level of barrier function-related protein ZO-1 among airway epithelium can improve CS-induced airway epithelial barrier dysfunction and achieve the purpose of treating COPD ( ). PSE could increase the levels of OCC and ZO-1 proteins, suggesting that PSE could reduce the damage of airway epithelial cells and lung cells by protecting epithelial barrier function.
Mitochondria are the energy factory of cells, the main source of ROS and the core site of ATP production ( ). When mitochondrial function is impaired, it will lead to excessive accumulation of ROS and reduced ATP synthesis. And ROS accumulation can cause oxidative stress damage and further lead to cell senescence. Mitochondria regulate the number, size, morphology, and location in the cytoplasm through continuous fission and fusion ( ). Drp1, which mediates mitochondrial fission, is recruited to the mitochondrial membrane through MFF receptors, driving membrane contraction and inducing membrane breakage. Mitochondrial inner membrane and outer membrane fusion were mediated by Opa1 and Mfn1 proteins, respectively ( ). Mitochondrial fusion is thought to be associated with more efficient ATP production. Song et al. showed that Bufei Yishen Formula III improved mitochondrial dysfunction in COPD by restoring MMP ( ). Wan et al. found that silencing KLF6 significantly inhibited CSE-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in BEAS-2B cells ( ). In rat pulmonary tissues and BEAS-2B cells induced by cigarette smoke and LPS, the inner and outer membrane boundaries of mitochondria were blurred, mitochondria were swollen, the internal ridge structure was defective and reduced, and many vacuoles were present. The levels of MMP, ATP and cytochrome C oxidase were significantly reduced, Opa1 and Mfn1 proteins were also significantly declined, and Drp1 and FIS1 protein expressions were significantly increased, suggesting mitochondrial dynamics disorder and mitochondrial dysfunction. PSE significantly improved mitochondrial damage induced by cigarette smoke and LPS, regulated mitochondrial fission, thereby inhibiting mitochondrial dysfunction and improving COPD.
PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is vital for modulating mitochondrial homeostasis ( ; ). This signaling pathway may ensure mitochondrial function, maintain cell energy supply, and promote cell growth and proliferation by coordinating control mechanisms such as mitophagy and mitochondrial biosynthesis. And this signaling pathway is also closely related to cell senescence ( ; ). Under physiological conditions, PI3K maintains a low activity state, and activates PI3K when cells are stimulated by external cytokines. After PI3K activation, mTORC2 can phosphorylate AKT, so that AKT is transferred from the cytoplasm to the cell membrane to become p-AKT, and p-AKT transmits the signal to mTOR. Li et al. found that PSP improved skeletal muscle aging and mitochondrial dysfunction by regulating this pathway ( ). Inactivation of this pathway activates mitophagy and regulates mitochondrial redox homeostasis, thereby counteracting cell senescence ( ). Previous work suggests that stressors such as CS and LPS can continuously stimulate lung tissue cells, leading to activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting mitophagy and exacerbating mitochondrial dysfunction, while inducing SASP release, further amplifying lung inflammation, oxidative stress and tissue damage, and promoting COPD progression ( ; ; ; ). The results of this study are also consistent with previous studies. PSE could significantly inhibit the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in lung tissue of COPD rats, thereby improving mitochondrial dysfunction, inhibiting lung tissue senescence, and reducing lung inflammation and oxidative stress injury ( ). More importantly, after treatment with PI3K specific activator 740Y-P, the protective effect of PSE on improving mitochondrial function and inhibiting bronchial epithelial cell and lung tissue senescence was significantly reversed, confirming that PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway was one of the core targets for PSE to exert anti-COPD effects.
In addition, the progression of COPD is the result of coordinated regulation of multiple pathways. In addition to the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, NF-κB, Nrf2/HO-1, MAPK and other pathways also play a key role in its pathological process ( ; ; ; ). The NF-κB pathway is the core pathway mediating chronic inflammation in the lungs, which can be activated by CS, LPS and other stimuli and promote the release of inflammatory factors. The Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is an important anti-oxidative stress pathway in the body. Impaired function will lead to decreased antioxidant capacity in lung tissue and aggravate oxidative stress injury. Combined with the results of this study that PSE can significantly reduce lung inflammation and oxidative stress in COPD rats, it is speculated that PSE may play an anti-COPD role through ‘multi-pathway synergistic regulation’. On the one hand, PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is the core to regulate mitochondrial homeostasis and cell senescence. On the other hand, it may enhance the antioxidant capacity of lung tissue by activating Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, inhibit the excessive activation of inflammatory pathways such as NF-κB and MAPK, reduce the release of inflammatory factors, and finally break the pathological cycle of COPD from multiple dimensions. Subsequent studies will further verify the regulatory effect of PSE on these pathways and clarify its complete molecular mechanism network for anti-COPD.
In summary, PSE reduced the secretion of related inflammatory factors and oxidative stress indicators via regulating PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, alleviated tissue senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction, and thus effectively improved COPD lung function. This study confirmed the therapeutic effect of PSE on COPD and explored its mechanism, which is helpful for the application of botanical medicine in clinical COPD therapy and the improvement of its treatment technology. However, this study has certain limitations. There is no reliable standard for CSE.At present, the related research on CSE is from the laboratory made by cigarettes. Although the self-made extract is used for quality control and quantitative labeling in this study, its effect is also affected by cigarette brand and tar content. The quality control and dose labeling of CSE greatly affect the stability of the data and the reliability of the experimental conclusions, which is also part of the reason for the inconsistency of the results in the published articles. It is hoped that a representative and reliable CSE standard can be developed to provide a guarantee for the establishment of cell models.
