## Abstract
Skeletal muscle fiber-type composition is key determinant of systemic metabolism and health. However, how fiber-type-specific gene expression patterns are established and maintained to specify myofiber identity remains unclear. Here we show that BRD4 is a crucial regulator for muscle fiber identity and metabolism. In humans, BRD4 expression correlates with muscle contractile properties, and is notably altered in individuals with obesity. In mice, HFD feeding elevates BRD4 protein levels, preceding body weight changes, while muscle-specific Brd4 deletion induces a slow-oxidative fiber shift, enhances energy expenditure, and protects against diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance. Mechanistically, BRD4 cooperates with MEF2 and CHD4 to regulate fast-twitch myofiber gene expression. CHD4 deletion mirrors the metabolic benefits seen with BRD4 loss. Importantly, BRD4 inhibition with JQ1 replicates these effects in mice and human myotubes. These findings establish BRD4 axis as a critical determinant of myofiber identity and metabolism, offering a potential therapeutic strategy for metabolic disorders.

## Introduction
Skeletal muscle comprises ~40% of total human body mass and plays a crucial role in regulating systemic metabolic homeostasis. It consists of a heterogeneous mix of myofibers with distinct contractile and metabolic characteristics, broadly classified into slow-twitch (type I) and fast-twitch (type II) fibers. Type I fibers are mitochondria-rich, rely primarily on oxidative metabolism for ATP production, and utilize sarcolipin-mediated calcium cycling, making them highly fatigue-resistant and essential for sustained energy expenditure – . In contrast, fast-twitch type II fibers generally contain fewer mitochondria, have lower oxidative capacity, and are more prone to fatigue; they are further divided into type IIa, IIx, or IIb subtypes in rodents based on their myosin heavy chain (MHC) isoforms – . Notably, skeletal muscle fibers demonstrate remarkable plasticity, enabling dynamic structural and metabolic adaptations in response to physiological stimuli like exercise or pathological conditions. Endurance or aerobic exercise drives a beneficial shift from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers, enhancing mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and overall energy expenditure, which collectively improve muscle endurance and systemic metabolic health , , . Conversely, in obesity, diabetes, and physical inactivity, skeletal muscle undergoes an unfavorable shift away from slow-oxidative fibers, along with diminished oxidative capacity, impaired glucose disposal, and insulin resistance , , .
Skeletal muscle fiber identity is governed by the precise regulation of contractile and metabolic gene expression programs that define fiber type specification and metabolic function. Each muscle fiber type, slow-twitch and fast-twitch, possesses distinct gene expression profiles tailored to their metabolic demands and functional roles. However, how such fiber-type-specific patterns of gene expression are established and maintained remains unclear. Previous studies have identified various transcription factors, including PPARs, ERRs, MEF2, SOX6, TBX15, SIXs, and MAFs, along with co-regulators like PGC-1s, NCoR1, and HDACs, in the regulation of muscle metabolic and contractile gene expression – . Enhancer elements play a pivotal role in orchestrating cell-type-specific gene expression, marked by histone modifications such as H3K4me1 and H3K27ac, which are mediated by methyltransferases (MLL3/MLL4) and acetyltransferases (CBP/p300) , – . These histone modifications serve as docking sites for epigenetic reader proteins with bromodomains, enabling the translation of enhancer activity into gene transcription – . However, whether and how these enhancer-associated readers coordinate muscle fiber-type-specific gene expression patterns to regulate myofiber identity and metabolic function is still unclear.
The Bromodomain and Extra-Terminal (BET) family of proteins, including BRD2, BRD3, BRD4, and the testis-specific BRDT, function as epigenetic readers that regulate gene expression by recognizing acetylated lysine residues on histones and transcriptional regulators through their two conserved bromodomains , . Among these, BRD4 is the most extensively studied and plays a crucial role in transcriptional activation by binding to acetylated histones at active enhancers and promoters, facilitating the release of paused RNA polymerase II and promoting transcription , – . BRD4 has been implicated in various diseases, including cancer, neurological disorders, and inflammatory diseases, making it a promising therapeutic target , , . Importantly, BRD4 can be pharmacologically targeted by small-molecule inhibitors like JQ1, which binds to its bromodomains, displacing BRD4 from chromatin and disrupting enhancer and promoter activity , , . This inhibition suppresses enhancer and promoter transcription, offering potential treatment strategies for many diseases – .
In this study, we explored the role of enhancer readers in skeletal muscle fiber-type specification and systemic metabolic regulation, addressing a critical gap in understanding how enhancer-driven mechanisms orchestrate muscle contractile and metabolic identity. BRD4 expression was enriched in fast twitch glycolytic myofibers and negatively correlated with both the proportion of type I oxidative fibers and the expression of slow‑fiber gene programs. Notably, BRD4 was upregulated in skeletal muscle following high‑fat‑diet (HFD) feeding, suggesting that it acts as a nutrient‑responsive enhancer reader. These observations, together with BRD4’s established role in transcriptional activation, led us to hypothesize that BRD4 governs muscle fiber‑type determination and metabolic homeostasis. We found that muscle-specific deletion of BRD4 induced a robust switch from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers, leading to increased energy expenditure, reduced adiposity, and improved glucose homeostasis in mice subjected to HFD. Mechanistically, BRD4 localizes to enhancers of fast-twitch myofiber genes, cooperating with MEF2 and CHD4 to recruit RNA polymerase II and drive gene expression. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of BRD4 with JQ1 mimicked the genetic deletion effects, promoting slow-oxidative muscle transformation and protecting against obesity and insulin resistance. These results establish the BRD4 axis as a critical regulatory mechanism in muscle fiber identity switch and systemic metabolism that can be exploited for pharmaceutical intervention.

## Results

## BRD4 expression is associated with muscle contractile and metabolic properties in humans
To identify new regulators of muscle fiber type remodeling that influence systemic metabolism, we analyzed gene expression in quadriceps femoris muscle samples from 11 human participants enrolled in the ESSENTIAL trial (ChiCTR2100053359). Individuals with obesity exhibiting lower type I fiber abundance (Fig.  , b). This analysis identified BRD4, a key enhancer-associated epigenetic reader, as a candidate regulator. BRD4 expression positively correlated with BMI and negatively with the proportion of type I muscle fibers (Fig.  ). Moreover, BRD4 expression inversely correlated with the expression of genes encoding slow-twitch muscle fiber proteins (TNNC1, TNNI1, TNNT1, MYL2 and MYOZ2) (Fig.  ). Supporting these findings, RT-qPCR analysis in mice revealed that Brd4 mRNA expression was significantly higher in fast-twitch, glycolytic white vastus lateralis (WV) muscle compared to the slow-twitch, oxidative soleus muscle (Fig.  ). We then tested whether nutrient excess modulates BRD4 protein levels in the muscles. This was indeed the case, as muscle BRD4 protein levels increased markedly after 1 week high-fat diet (HFD) feeding (Fig.  ). However, Brd4 mRNA levels remained unchanged under the same conditions (Supplementary Fig.  ), indicating that the increase in BRD4 protein is likely regulated through post-transcriptional mechanisms. Together, these data from human and mouse studies implicate BRD4 as a regulator of muscle fiber type composition and metabolic function.
 

## Muscle-specific deletion of BRD4 protects against high-fat diet-induced obesity and improves systemic glucose homeostasis in mice
To directly assess the physiological role of BRD4 in skeletal muscle, we generated a muscle-specific Brd4 knockout (BRD4 mKO) mouse model. Using Brd4f/f mice with exon 5 flanked by loxP sites, we crossed them with mice expressing Cre recombinase under the control of the human skeletal actin (HSA) promoter, resulting in muscle-specific deletion of BRD4 in both fast- and slow-twitch muscle fibers (Supplementary Fig.  ). BRD4 mKO mice were born at expected Mendelian ratios and displayed no gross abnormalities compared to wild-type (WT) littermates (Supplementary Fig.  ). RT-qPCR analysis confirmed a marked reduction in Brd4 transcript levels in both fast and slow skeletal muscles, with no changes in other tissues such as the heart, liver, or adipose tissue (Supplementary Fig.  ). Transcript levels of Brd2 and Brd3, the other members of the BET family, were not affected by BRD4 deletion in skeletal muscle (Supplementary Fig.  ). Immunoblot analysis further validated efficient postnatal deletion of BRD4 protein in skeletal muscle (Fig.  ). Under chow diet (CD), BRD4 mKO mice had lower body weight and altered body composition, with a lower percentage of fat mass (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). Histological analysis of gastrocnemius (GC) muscle revealed a shift toward smaller muscle fiber cross-sectional areas in BRD4 mKO mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, the expression of multiple muscle atrophy-related genes was either unchanged or decreased in BRD4 mKO muscles, suggesting that the reduced fiber size was not due to muscle atrophy (Supplementary Fig.  ).
To evaluate the metabolic impact of BRD4 deletion, BRD4 mKO and WT mice were fed a HFD (60% kcal from fat) for 16 weeks. We found that BRD4 mKO mice gained significantly less weight during HFD feeding (Fig.  ) with an overall reduction of ~34% in net weight gain (Fig.  ) and significantly lower fat mass (Supplementary Fig.  ). To account for the pronounced difference in dietary obesity, we performed indirect calorimetric analysis in HFD-fed mice using the Comprehensive Lab Animal Monitoring System (CLAMS). Despite no significant differences in locomotor activity (Supplementary Fig.  ), BRD4 mKO mice exhibited increased oxygen consumption (VO₂) (Fig.  ), elevated carbon dioxide production (VCO₂) (Supplementary Fig.  ), and higher overall energy expenditure (Fig.  ) compared to WT mice. These findings indicate that skeletal muscle-specific BRD4 deletion enhances systemic energy expenditure, contributing to protection against diet-induced obesity.
We next evaluated glucose homeostasis in BRD4 mKO mice. Fasting blood glucose and insulin levels, as well as the homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), were significantly reduced in BRD4 mKO mice compared to WT controls, indicating enhanced insulin sensitivity in the context of HFD feeding (Fig.  ). Glucose tolerance tests (GTT) demonstrated that BRD4 mKO mice were more glucose tolerant than WT littermates (Fig.  ). Similarly, insulin tolerance tests (ITT) confirmed improved insulin responsiveness in BRD4 mKO mice following HFD feeding (Fig.  ). Additionally, BRD4 mKO mice exhibited smaller liver size (Supplementary Fig.  ) and reduced liver weights (Supplementary Fig.  ) compared to WT controls. Histological analyses using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Oil Red O staining revealed significantly decreased hepatic lipid accumulation in BRD4 mKO mice (Fig.  , r). This was further confirmed by biochemical analysis showing lower liver triglyceride (TG) content (Fig.  ). Histological analysis also showed smaller adipocytes in both brown adipose tissue (BAT) and white adipose tissue (WAT) of BRD4 mKO mice compared with WT controls (Supplementary Fig.  ). We also sought to determine whether major thermogenic pathways are affected in adipose tissue from BRD4 mKO mice. We found that the expression of the genes involved in both UCP1-dependent and -independent thermogenesis were not different in BAT or inguinal WAT from WT control and BRD4 mKO mice (Supplementary Fig.  ), suggesting that adipose thermogenesis is likely not upregulated in BRD4 mKO mice. Collectively, these results strongly suggest that BRD4 acts in the skeletal muscle to regulate systemic energy consumption and metabolism.

## Loss of BRD4 promotes fast- to slow-twitch oxidative muscle transformation
To define the molecular basis of the reduced adiposity and improved glucose homeostasis in BRD4 mKO mice, we performed global RNA-seq analysis on GC muscle samples from BRD4 mKO mice and WT littermate controls fed either a CD or a HFD. BRD4 deletion resulted in the differential expression of 1,137 genes on CD and 2,196 genes on HFD (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ), indicating broad transcriptional reprogramming in response to BRD4 loss. Gene Ontology (GO) pathway enrichment analyses revealed significant enrichment of muscle contraction-related pathways in BRD4-regulated genes under both dietary conditions (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, several chemokines and cytokines involved in neutrophil recruitment were upregulated in BRD4-deficient muscle (Supplementary Table  ). These findings suggest that BRD4 plays a critical role in regulating genes associated with muscle fiber composition and contractile function. Comparative transcriptomic analysis identified extensive fiber-type switching and novel myofiber-specific gene expression in BRD4 mKO muscles. Specifically, genes associated with slow-twitch oxidative fibers were significantly upregulated, while fast-twitch glycolytic fiber genes were downregulated (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). Although our RNA-seq analysis revealed an increase in regeneration or denervation marker gene mRNA levels in BRD4 mKO muscle, histological analysis by immunofluorescence or H&E staining showed preserved muscle integrity in BRD4 mKO muscle, with no evidence of degeneration, fibrosis, or increased centralized nuclei compared to WT controls (Supplementary Fig.  ). RT-qPCR analysis further confirmed increased expression of the slow-twitch type I myosin isoform Myh7 (MHC1) and slow-twitch troponin genes (Tnni1, Tnnc1, Tnnt1) in BRD4 mKO muscles under both CD and HFD conditions (Fig.  ). In contrast, expression of the fast-twitch type IIb myosin isoform Myh4 (MHC2b) and fast-twitch troponin genes (Tnni2, Tnnc2, Tnnt3) was significantly reduced (Fig.  ). Similar expression patterns were observed in the soleus and WV muscles of BRD4 mKO mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). Consistent with these transcriptional changes, immunofluorescence staining for MHC1 revealed a substantial increase in type I oxidative fibers in both GC and soleus muscles of BRD4 mKO mice compared to WT controls, regardless of diet (Fig.  , f and Supplementary Fig.  ).
 Type I oxidative fibers rely on coordinated expression of genes involved in slow-contractile function, calcium regulation, and oxidative metabolism to produce a unified effect on muscle function. In line with this, BRD4 mKO muscles showed marked alterations in the expression of calcium-handling genes (Supplementary Fig.  ). Specifically, genes associated with slow-fiber calcium handling (Sln, Atp2a2, Nucb2) were significantly upregulated, while fast-fiber-enriched calcium regulatory genes (Calm3, Cacng6, Atp2b3) were downregulated in BRD4 mKO muscles (Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, the dramatic upregulation of Sln is particularly significant, as sarcolipin (SLN) is a key regulator of whole-body energy expenditure , . Western blot analysis confirmed robust increases in SLN protein expression in BRD4 mKO muscles under both CD and HFD conditions (Fig.  , h). To further assess myofiber metabolic remodeling, we examined mitochondrial oxidative capacity via histochemical staining for succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), a marker of oxidative metabolism. SDH activity was significantly elevated in GC muscles of BRD4 mKO mice (Fig.  , j), while the activity of glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GPDH), a glycolytic enzyme abundant in fast-twitch fibers, was reduced (Fig.  , j). Additionally, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) isoenzyme analysis indicated that the amounts of isoenzymes containing LDH-B, which is enriched in oxidative myofibers, were higher in BRD4 mKO muscles compared to WT controls (Supplementary Fig.  ). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that BRD4 deletion in skeletal muscle triggers a comprehensive molecular, metabolic, and contractile shift from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers.
Taken together, these results demonstrate that loss of BRD4 promotes an overt conversion of fast-twitch glycolytic muscles to a slow-twitch oxidative phenotype. This myofiber contractile and metabolic identity switch contributes to protection against diet-induced obesity and improved systemic glucose homeostasis.

## Acute ablation of BRD4 in mature muscles transforms toward slow-oxidative myofibers
To further investigate BRD4’s role in muscle fiber identity, we generated an additional muscle-specific Brd4 knockout mouse model (Brd4f/fMCK-Cre) by crossing Brd4f/f mice with MCK-Cre mice (Supplementary Fig.  ), resulting in significantly reduced Brd4 mRNA levels in skeletal muscle (Supplementary Fig.  ). This deletion increased the expression of slow-twitch myosin and troponin genes while reducing fast-twitch myosin and troponin gene expression compared to wild-type controls (Supplementary Fig.  ). Sln mRNA expression was also significantly elevated in Brd4f/fMCK-Cre muscle (Supplementary Fig.  ). Immunofluorescence staining and SDH activity assays confirmed a marked increase in slow-oxidative type I fibers in the soleus and GC muscles of Brd4f/fMCK-Cre mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). The consistent fiber-type switching phenotypes in both the Brd4f/fMCK-Cre and the previously described BRD4 mKO models strongly demonstrate that BRD4 is essential for establishing skeletal muscle contractile and metabolic identity.
To assess whether BRD4 is required for maintaining myofiber identity in mature muscle, we acutely deleted BRD4 in skeletal muscle using an adeno-associated virus (AAV) approach. Four-week-old Brd4f/f mice were intramuscularly injected with either AAV2/9-GFP (control) or AAV2/9-Cre to induce Brd4 deletion, and muscle tissues were analyzed eight weeks post-injection (Fig.  ). Compared to control AAV2/9-GFP injections, AAV2/9-Cre-mediated deletion of BRD4 significantly reduced Brd4 mRNA levels in the GC muscle (Fig.  ). This acute BRD4 ablation led to upregulation of slow-twitch myosin and troponin genes and downregulation of fast-twitch myosin and troponin genes (Fig.  ). Consistent with these gene expression changes, MHC immunofluorescence staining and SDH activity assays revealed a significant increase in slow-oxidative type I fibers in the AAV2/9-Cre-injected muscles compared to the control group (Fig.  ). These findings demonstrate that acute loss of BRD4 in mature skeletal muscle is sufficient to induce a fiber-type switch toward a slow-oxidative phenotype.
 To further validate these findings, we generated a tamoxifen-inducible, muscle-specific Brd4 knockout mouse model (Brd4f/f; HSA-MCM, hereafter referred to as BRD4 i-mKO) by crossing Brd4f/f mice with mice expressing a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase under the control of the HSA promoter (Fig.  ). This model allowed for temporal control of BRD4 deletion in mature skeletal muscle. Eight-week-old BRD4 i-mKO mice were treated with tamoxifen for five consecutive days, resulting in efficient depletion of Brd4 transcripts in GC muscle (Fig.  ). Similar to the constitutive BRD4 mKO models, BRD4 i-mKO mice exhibited elevated expression of slow-twitch myosin and troponin genes, increased Sln mRNA levels, and reduced expression of fast-twitch isoforms compared to tamoxifen-treated Cre-negative control mice (Fig.  ). Immunofluorescence analysis further confirmed a significant increase in slow type I fibers in both the GC and soleus muscles of BRD4 i-mKO mice (Fig.  ). Collectively, these results demonstrate that BRD4 is essential not only for the establishment but also for the maintenance of muscle fiber identity. Both acute and inducible deletion of BRD4 in mature skeletal muscle consistently promotes a shift from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers.

## BRD4 colocalizes with MEF2 on enhancers to regulate muscle fiber-type specific gene expression
To elucidate the molecular mechanism by which BRD4 regulates muscle fiber-type switching, we examined the possibility that BRD4 regulates factors known to drive slow-oxidative myofiber gene expression. Notably, the expression of Ppargc1a, Ppargc1b, Ppard, Esrrg, Esrra, and Esrrb genes was either unchanged or decreased in BRD4 mKO muscles (Supplementary Fig.  ). We next integrated our transcriptomic data with a published BRD4 ChIP-seq dataset to identify direct BRD4 targets in skeletal muscle (Fig.  ). This analysis revealed that ~78% of BRD4-regulated genes were directly bound by BRD4, as determined by the nearest gene to BRD4-binding peaks (Fig.  ). GO analysis of these 891 direct targets showed significant enrichment for fiber-type-related biological processes, mirroring the transcriptional changes observed in BRD4 mKO mice (Fig.  ). This strong correlation between BRD4’s cistromic landscape and transcriptomic alterations underscores BRD4’s direct role in regulating muscle fiber-type-specific gene programs. De novo motif analysis of BRD4-bound regions in skeletal muscle, performed using HOMER, identified the sequence 5′-GCTATTTTTAGC-3′ as the most significantly enriched motif (P = 1e-51) (Fig.  ). This motif closely matches the well-established consensus binding site for the myocyte enhancer factor 2 (MEF2) family of transcription factors , suggesting that MEF2 likely collaborates with BRD4 to regulate fiber-type-specific gene expression. To validate this, we examined a published MEF2 ChIP-seq data alongside BRD4 ChIP-seq data. Heatmap visualization revealed substantial co-occupancy of BRD4 and MEF2 at shared genomic binding sites (Fig.  ). Moreover, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) demonstrated that genes co-bound by BRD4 and MEF2 were significantly downregulated in BRD4 mKO muscle compared to WT controls (Fig.  ), supporting the idea that BRD4 cooperates with MEF2 to regulate muscle fiber-type-specific gene expression.
 Given BRD4’s established role as an enhancer reader that bridges active enhancer marks with RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) to drive transcription , , , , we next investigated how BRD4 deletion impacts enhancer and promoter activity at muscle fiber-type-specific genes. Western blot analysis of global histone modifications revealed no significant changes in overall histone acetylation or methylation levels upon BRD4 loss (Fig.  , g). Genome-wide ChIP-seq analysis of H3K27ac, a marker of active enhancers, showed that H3K27ac enrichment at BRD4-bound regions remained largely unchanged in BRD4 mKO muscle compared to WT controls (Fig.  ). However, Pol II ChIP-Seq analysis revealed a significant reduction in Pol II occupancy at BRD4-bound enhancers in BRD4-deficient muscles (Fig.  ), suggesting reduced gene expression despite intact enhancer acetylation. Notably, BRD4 and MEF2 co-localized at enhancers of many fast-twitch muscle fiber genes (Myh4, Tnnt3, Tnni2, Tnnc2, and Acta1), which also showed H3K27ac and Pol II signals in WT muscles (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). However, BRD4 deletion markedly reduced Pol II recruitment at these loci, while Pol II expression levels were not decreased (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). This disruption in Pol II occupancy corresponded with significantly decreased mRNA expression of fast-twitch muscle fiber genes in BRD4 mKO muscles (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). Interestingly, BRD4 binding was largely absent at enhancers of slow-twitch muscle fiber genes (Supplementary Fig.  ), suggesting a selective role for BRD4 in activating fast-twitch fiber-specific transcriptional programs. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that BRD4 colocalizes with MEF2 at enhancer regions to regulate the expression of fast-twitch glycolytic muscle fiber genes. Loss of BRD4 disrupts Pol II recruitment at fast-fiber-specific enhancers, results in the transcriptional downregulation of fast-twitch genes and leads to a fiber-type switch toward a slow-oxidative phenotype.

## BRD4 regulates muscle fiber type switching in cooperation with CHD4
Initial co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) experiments assessing the direct interaction between BRD4 and MEF2 in HEK293T cells yielded negative results (Fig.  ), suggesting that BRD4 and MEF2 may not interact directly. We hypothesized that an intermediary protein might facilitate the functional interaction between BRD4 and MEF2. To identify potential bridging factors, we employed an unbiased proximity-dependent biotinylation approach using TurboID biotin ligase to label BRD4- and MEF2-associated proteins (Fig.  ). Comparative analysis of the BRD4- and MEF2-TurboID proximity interactomes revealed 10 shared interacting proteins, prominently including CHD4, a core component of the NuRD chromatin-remodeling complex (Fig.  ). The identification of CHD4 is particularly notable due to its established interaction with BRD4 and its known role in muscle gene regulation , . To determine whether CHD4 mediates the cooperation between BRD4 and MEF2, we performed additional Co-IP experiments in HEK293 cells co-transfected with combinations of MEF2, BRD4, and CHD4 expression constructs. BRD4 was not co-immunoprecipitated with MEF2 when expressed alone, however, co-expression of CHD4 enabled BRD4–MEF2 interaction (Fig. ), indicating that CHD4 is capable of promoting BRD4–MEF2 association. We further performed Co-IP using an anti-CHD4 antibody on GC muscle lysates. Anti-CHD4 antibody pulled down both BRD4 and MEF2 (Fig.  ), suggesting that CHD4 may mediate the interaction between BRD4 and MEF2 to regulate muscle-specific gene expression. We next performed genome-wide ChIP-seq to map CHD4 occupancy in WT mouse skeletal muscle. Co-localization analysis revealed substantial overlap between BRD4, MEF2, and CHD4 binding at enhancers of key fast-twitch muscle fiber genes, including Myh4 and Tnni2 (Fig.  , g). This co-occupancy suggests a cooperative regulatory mechanism involving BRD4 and CHD4 in regulating fast-fiber gene programs.
 To assess the functional role of CHD4 in muscle fiber-type regulation, we conducted an in vivo CHD4 loss-of-function experiment. C57BL/6 J mice were intramuscularly injected with either control AAV2/9-shRNA (shCtrl) or AAV2/9-shRNA targeting Chd4 (shChd4) (Fig.  ). Chd4 knockdown was confirmed by significantly reduced Chd4 mRNA levels in the GC muscle of shChd4-injected mice compared to controls (Fig.  ). Importantly, CHD4 depletion led to a pronounced upregulation of slow-twitch troponin genes (Tnnc1, Tnni1, Tnnt1) and a concomitant downregulation of fast-twitch troponin genes (Myh4, Tnni2, Tnnc2) in skeletal muscle (Fig.  ). Given that HDAC1 is a key component of the NuRD complex alongside CHD4, we next assessed whether it plays a similar role. AAV2/9-mediated Hdac1 knockdown in GC muscle led to an increase in slow-twitch troponin genes (Myh7, Tnni1, Tnnt1) and a decrease in fast-twitch isoforms (Myh4, Tnni2, Tnnc2) (Supplementary Fig.  ). These findings suggest that HDAC1, like CHD4, contributes to maintaining fast-twitch fiber gene expression. This shift in gene expression mirrors the phenotype observed in BRD4-deficient muscles, indicating that NuRD component CHD4 is essential for maintaining fast-twitch muscle fiber identity and repressing slow-twitch gene programs.

## Loss of muscle CHD4 promotes slow-oxidative muscle transformation, reduces adiposity and improves glucose homeostasis
To further elucidate the physiological role of CHD4 in skeletal muscle, we generated a skeletal muscle-specific Chd4 knockout (CHD4 mKO) mouse line by crossing Chd4f/f mice with HSA-Cre mice (Fig.  ). In this model, exons 3–10 of the Chd4 gene were flanked by two loxP sites, allowing for muscle-specific deletion. As expected, Chd4 mRNA levels were significantly reduced in the GC muscle of CHD4 mKO mice compared to WT littermates (Fig.  ). Consistent with the cooperative role of CHD4 and BRD4 in muscle gene regulation, CHD4 mKO muscles exhibited increased expression of slow-twitch muscle fiber genes and suppressed expression of fast-twitch muscle fiber genes relative to WT controls (Fig.  ). Furthermore, CHD4 deficiency significantly reduced BRD4 occupancy at MEF2-bound regions of fast-twitch muscle genes (Supplementary Fig.  ), suggesting that CHD4 is required for efficient BRD4 chromatin occupancy at these regulatory loci. To comprehensively assess muscle fiber-type-specific gene expression changes, RNA-seq analysis was performed on GC muscles from CHD4 mKO and WT mice. This analysis revealed 2,143 upregulated and 1,885 downregulated genes regulated by CHD4 in skeletal muscle (Supplementary Fig.  ). GO analysis of these differentially expressed genes showed significant enrichment in muscle fiber-type-related pathways (Supplementary Fig.  ). Heatmap analysis further highlighted widespread changes in muscle contraction-related genes distinguishing fast and slow muscle fibers in CHD4 mKO mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). GSEA confirmed that deletion of CHD4 significantly recapitulated the expression profile of BRD4-regulated genes, further supporting functional convergence between CHD4 and BRD4 in muscle gene regulation (Fig.  , e). Additionally, immunostaining for slow type I fibers and oxidative mitochondrial enzyme SDH activity revealed a pronounced increase in slow-oxidative myofibers in CHD4 mKO muscle compared to WT controls (Fig.  ). Western blot analysis also demonstrated a significant upregulation of SLN in CHD4 mKO muscles (Fig.  ). These findings collectively demonstrate that muscle-specific deletion of CHD4 drives a robust transition from fast-twitch glycolytic to slow-twitch oxidative muscle fibers, mirroring the phenotype observed in BRD4 mKO mice. The expression of fast-glycolytic muscle genes is also regulated by large MAFs and FNIP1 , – , we found that the expression of Fnip1 and large MAFs was also reduced in BRD4-deficient muscle compared to WT controls (Supplementary Fig.  ). These results suggest that BRD4 may also function upstream of these regulators to sustain fast-fiber gene expression and repress the slow-oxidative muscle fiber program.
 To investigate the systemic metabolic impact of CHD4 deletion in skeletal muscle, we subjected WT and CHD4 mKO mice to a HFD challenge. Similar to BRD4 mKO mice, CHD4 mKO mice displayed significantly lower body weights than WT controls when fed a CD (Fig.  ). Notably, CHD4 mKO mice were remarkably protected from HFD-induced obesity over a 3-month feeding period, exhibiting significantly less weight gain and fat mass accumulation compared to WT littermates (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). In addition to resistance to obesity, CHD4 mKO mice demonstrated substantial improvements in systemic glucose metabolism. After 12 weeks of HFD feeding, CHD4 mKO mice exhibited significantly reduced fasting glucose and insulin levels, along with a lower HOMA-IR, compared to HFD-fed WT controls (Fig.  ). GTT and ITT further confirmed that CHD4 mKO mice had improved glucose tolerance and enhanced insulin sensitivity relative to WT mice under HFD conditions (Fig.  , q). Consistent with these systemic improvements, CHD4 mKO mice had significantly reduced liver mass compared to WT controls following HFD feeding (Supplementary Fig.  ). Histological analysis using H&E and Oil Red O staining revealed markedly reduced hepatic lipid accumulation in CHD4 mKO mice (Supplementary Fig.  ), which was corroborated by biochemical measurements showing decreased hepatic TG levels (Supplementary Fig.  ). Collectively, these results demonstrate that skeletal muscle-specific deletion of CHD4 promotes a fast-to-slow muscle fiber-type switch, reduces adiposity, and improves systemic glucose homeostasis. The metabolic phenotype observed in CHD4 mKO mice closely mirrors that of BRD4 mKO mice.

## Pharmacologic inhibition of BRD4 by JQ1 activates slow-oxidative muscle transformation and improves systemic metabolism
The BRD4 regulatory axis identified in this study presents a promising therapeutic target for enhancing slow-oxidative muscle fibers, which are critical for mitigating the adverse effects of caloric excess. JQ1, a well-characterized pan-BET inhibitor with the highest binding affinity for BRD4 among 41 human bromodomain (BD)-containing proteins . This raises the intriguing possibility that pharmacologic inhibition of BRD4 by JQ1 could stimulate slow-oxidative muscle fiber transformation and offer therapeutic benefits for obesity-related metabolic disorders. To explore this, we assessed the pharmacological impact of JQ1 in the context of diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance. C57BL/6 mice were fed a HFD for five months and received daily intraperitoneal injections of JQ1 (50 mg/kg/day) or vehicle control during this period (Fig.  ). Consistent with prior reports of JQ1’s metabolic benefits , , JQ1 administration conferred robust protection against HFD-induced obesity (Fig.  ). Notably, JQ1 treatment did not significantly alter the mRNA expression levels of Brd2, Brd3, or Brd4 in skeletal muscle (Supplementary Fig.  ). JQ1-treated mice exhibited significantly reduced weight gain compared to vehicle-treated controls (Fig.  ). Analysis of body composition revealed that the reduction in fat mass was more pronounced than the reduction in lean mass, resulting in a significantly lower fat mass-to-body weight ratio in JQ1-treated mice (Fig.  ). Tissue weight analysis further supported this observation: the percentage of WAT and liver weights were significantly reduced, whereas the percentage of GC and TA muscle weights were higher in JQ1-treated mice compared to controls (Fig.  ). Immunofluorescence staining of GC and soleus muscle sections revealed a marked increase in slow-twitch type I fibers (MHC1-positive) in JQ1-treated mice, closely resembling the phenotype observed in BRD4 mKO mice (Fig.  , f). To gain deeper insight into JQ1’s molecular effects, we conducted RNA-seq analysis on GC muscle from JQ1-treated mice. The transcriptomic analysis revealed significant enrichment in pathways associated with slow muscle contraction and oxidative metabolism, closely mirroring the gene expression profile of BRD4 mKO mice (Fig.  , h). These findings demonstrate that both genetic and pharmacological inhibition of BRD4 can induce a muscle fiber-type switch from fast glycolytic to slow oxidative fibers. In alignment, JQ1-treated mice exhibited significantly improved glucose homeostasis, as evidenced by enhanced glucose and insulin tolerance compared to vehicle-treated controls (Fig.  , j). Histological examination of liver sections using H&E and Oil Red O staining revealed markedly reduced lipid droplet accumulation in JQ1-treated mice, accompanied by significantly lower hepatic TG levels (Supplementary Fig.  ). Together, these data demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of the BRD4 pathway by JQ1 protects from diet-induced obesity and improves metabolic parameters, concomitant with activation of slow-oxidative muscle fiber program in mice.
 To evaluate whether JQ1 can similarly activate slow-type I muscle programs in human skeletal muscle cells, we utilized LHCN-M2 myotubes, a well-established model for studying human muscle metabolism and disease . Treatment of LHCN-M2 myotubes with JQ1 for four days significantly increased the expression of slow-twitch muscle genes MYH7, TNNI1, and TNNC1, as confirmed by RT-qPCR analysis (Fig.  ). Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining for MHC isoforms demonstrated a notable increase in slow-type I fibers in JQ1-treated myotubes compared to controls (Fig.  ). These findings support the conclusion that pharmacological inhibition of BRD4 by JQ1 can effectively induce slow muscle fiber programs in both murine and human skeletal muscle cells.

## Discussion
Skeletal muscle, the largest metabolic organ in the human body, plays a crucial role in whole-body energy homeostasis, which is largely dictated by the structural and metabolic properties of specialized myofibers. However, the mechanisms governing the regulation of muscle fiber identity and function remain unclear. In this study, we identify BRD4 as a key enhancer reader that regulates myofiber identity and systemic metabolism (Fig.  ). Our findings reveal that BRD4 expression correlates with muscle contractile and metabolic properties in humans. Using both constitutive and inducible muscle-specific Brd4 knockout models in mice, we demonstrate that BRD4 is essential for establishing and maintaining muscle fiber type-specific gene expression programs that define myofiber identity. Loss of BRD4 resulted in a pronounced shift toward slow-oxidative type I fibers, leading to increased oxidative capacity, enhanced systemic energy expenditure, and protection against HFD-induced obesity with improved glucose homeostasis. Mechanistically, BRD4 localizes to enhancers of fast-twitch myofiber genes, where it cooperates with MEF2 and CHD4 to facilitate RNA polymerase II recruitment and transcription, thereby sustaining fast-fiber identity. Furthermore, CHD4 deletion similarly induced a myofiber identity switch and metabolic benefits, mirroring BRD4 loss. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of BRD4 with JQ1 recapitulated these effects, promoting slow-oxidative muscle transformation and metabolic improvements. These findings identify BRD4 and CHD4 as critical molecular determinants of myofiber identity and metabolism that can be exploited pharmaceutically to ameliorate obesity and metabolic disorders.
 Enhancer activation is key to cell-type-specific gene expression, and the translation of enhancer activity into transcriptional signals is a vital step in gene activation. While considerable attention has been given to transcription factors such as PPARs, ERRs, MEF2, SOX6, SIXs, TBX15, BCL6, and MAFs, as well as co-regulators like PGC-1s, NCoR1, BAF60C and HDACs in regulating muscle gene expression and metabolism – , , , , our understanding of enhancer regulatory mechanisms remains in their infancy. In this study, we identify BRD4 as a crucial enhancer reader that directs the contractile and metabolic identity of skeletal muscle fibers. Notably, we demonstrate that BRD4 is essential not only for the establishment but also for the maintenance of muscle fiber identity in adult muscle. Muscle-specific BRD4 ablation leads to a profound shift in fiber composition and systemic metabolic improvements, underscoring its critical role in muscle physiology. While previous studies have demonstrated beneficial effects of pan-BET inhibition in dystrophic skeletal muscles, such as the cancer cachexia and dystrophic DMD/mdx muscles , these works relied on non-selective BET blockade, making it impossible to resolve the individual roles of distinct BET family members. In contrast, our study employs multiple muscle-specific genetic deletion approaches to precisely define BRD4’s critical physiological and metabolic functions in adult skeletal muscle under both basal and dietary stress conditions. Moreover, we demonstrate that BRD4 inactivation enhances oxidative fiber-type metabolism and protects against diet-induced obesity, a set of phenotypes not observed or explored in the previous studies , . Therefore, our work identifies a previously unrecognized BRD4–MEF2–CHD4 axis governing myofiber identity and systemic metabolism under physiological and dietary stress conditions, thereby providing a framework for BRD4 function in skeletal muscle beyond BET inhibition paradigms.
The well-characterized enhancer writers MLL3/MLL4 and CBP/P300 deposit H3K4me1 and H3K27ac marks, respectively, to regulate gene activation – . As a key reader of histone acetylation, BRD4 recognizes H3K27ac and facilitates the recruitment of the transcriptional machinery , , , . However, our findings reveal an unexpected functional divergence between BRD4 and MLL4 in muscle fiber regulation—while our previous study demonstrated that MLL4 confers slow-twitch oxidative fiber identity , BRD4 is indispensable for maintaining fast-twitch glycolytic fiber programs, and its deletion induces a shift toward type I oxidative fibers. Notably, BRD4 has been shown to facilitate transcription by forming phase-separated condensates at super-enhancer regions, where it promotes high local concentrations of transcriptional machinery and Pol II to drive robust gene expression . This phase-separation property allows BRD4 to function not only as a chromatin-bound enhancer reader but also as a key organizer of transcriptional condensates, particularly at genes with high transcriptional demand . These findings suggest that enhancer readers like BRD4 may exert independent control over distinct gene programs or collaborate with different enhancer writers to fine-tune muscle fiber-type patterning. Collectively, our study provides new insights into the intricate coordination between enhancer writers and readers in shaping muscle contractile and metabolic identity.
Our findings strongly indicate that BRD4 and CHD4 play a muscle-specific role in regulating whole-body metabolism by modulating muscle fiber type composition. The significant shift toward slow-oxidative fibers observed in Brd4 and Chd4 knockout (mKO) mice is linked to marked reductions in adiposity and improved glucose homeostasis. Our results suggest that the systemic metabolic improvements observed in BRD4 mKO mice are not attributable to adipose thermogenic activation. These metabolic benefits align with the well-established role of slow-oxidative fibers in enhancing systemic energy expenditure and insulin sensitivity , , – . Notably, the robust upregulation of slow type I fiber-enriched sarcolipin (SLN), a key regulator of muscle-based energy expenditure through calcium cycling, may contribute to the observed metabolic improvements in BRD4 and CHD4 mKO mice, providing protection against HFD-induced obesity. Our data suggest that the activation of energy-consuming futile cycles, driven by increased SLN expression, plays a pivotal role in the enhanced glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity seen in these models. Supporting this concept, previous studies have demonstrated that transgene-mediated SLN overexpression in skeletal muscle effectively protects against diet-induced obesity and glucose tolerance by promoting energy expenditure .
The remarkable plasticity of skeletal muscle is largely attributed to the diversity of muscle fiber types and their ability to undergo adaptive transformation. However, a longstanding challenge in the field has been determining whether signaling pathways that drive fiber-type specification during development also regulate fiber-type switching in adult muscle . Our findings establish BRD4 as a critical regulator of myofiber identity maintenance in adult skeletal muscle. We demonstrate that both acute and inducible deletion of BRD4 in mature muscle consistently trigger a shift from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers, underscoring its essential role in sustaining fiber-type specificity. This transformation is accompanied by coordinated alterations in gene expression, increased oxidative enzyme activity, and reduced glycolytic metabolism, reinforcing the notion that BRD4 is indispensable not only for fiber-type specification during development but also for maintaining metabolic and contractile properties throughout adulthood. Notably, the fiber-type shift following acute BRD4 deletion appears less pronounced than in the constitutive knockout models. This likely reflects differences in the timing, duration, and cellular context of BRD4 deletion. Specifically, chronic deletion via HSA-Cre or MCK-Cre induces BRD4 loss early in muscle development, allowing prolonged remodeling of transcriptional programs during the dynamic period of fiber-type specification and maturation. In contrast, tamoxifen-induced HSA-MCM or AAV-Cre approaches trigger recombination in fully differentiated adult myofibers, where the transcriptional landscape is more stable and fiber identity is largely established, thus limiting the plasticity for BRD4-dependent remodeling. Moreover, recombination efficiency may be lower in acute systems compared to the sustained deletion in constitutive knockout models, further contributing to the observed difference. Nevertheless, these insights provide compelling evidence for the ongoing role of enhancer regulation in adult muscle plasticity.
Our study reveals a previously unrecognized cooperation between BRD4 and the chromatin remodeler CHD4 in regulating fast-twitch muscle gene expression. We demonstrate that the CHD4/NuRD complex, traditionally known for its role in transcriptional repression in stem cell biology and differentiation – , plays a critical and essential function in maintaining skeletal muscle fiber identity. Our data demonstrate that both CHD4 and HDAC1, key components of the NuRD complex, are critical for preserving fast-fiber gene expression. CHD4 may directly interact with BRD4 to form a regulatory complex with MEF2, co-occupying enhancer regions of fast-twitch glycolytic muscle genes to sustain their expression while repressing slow-fiber gene programs. The loss of CHD4 disrupts this regulatory network, leading to a shift from fast-twitch glycolytic fibers to slow-twitch oxidative fibers, accompanied by increased energy expenditure, resistance to diet-induced obesity, and improved glucose homeostasis. The striking metabolic improvements observed in CHD4 knockout mice mirror those of BRD4-deficient mice, underscoring their cooperative role in muscle fiber-type determination and systemic metabolic regulation. These findings highlight a crucial epigenetic mechanism by which BRD4 and CHD4 collaboratively orchestrate muscle fiber-type switching. Emerging evidence indicates that transient inflammation in muscle can serve adaptive functions, promoting mitochondrial biogenesis, angiogenesis, and metabolic remodeling . It is possible that BRD4 loss elicits an adaptive inflammatory signature that supports beneficial metabolic reprogramming. Indeed, cytokines SPP1 is a crucial factor for angiogenic remodeling in skeletal muscle , the upregulation of SPP1 in BRD4-deficient muscle may facilitate slow-oxidative muscle vasculature. Our data suggest that certain genes traditionally associated with developmental or denervation contexts (e.g., Myh3, Chrng) may also be transiently or partially engaged during fiber-type remodeling, particularly during developmental or epigenetically driven reprogramming, rather than indicating active damage. It is possible that BRD4 constrains specific transcriptional programs during muscle maturation, and that its deletion unmasks gene expression patterns that are permissive for oxidative fiber identity rather than pathological remodeling.
Our global BRD4-directed transcriptional analysis reveals that the BRD4-regulated muscle fiber-type gene program operates in close cooperation with MEF2. This finding is particularly intriguing given MEF2’s well-established role in muscle development and fiber-type remodeling throughout adulthood , , – . The MEF2 transcription factor family, comprising MEF2A, MEF2B, MEF2C, and MEF2D, exhibits distinct tissue distributions, with MEF2A, C, and D highly enriched in skeletal muscle and expressed across all myofiber types , whereas MEF2B is mainly expressed in lymphoid tissues. As a nodal regulator, MEF2 coordinates multiple downstream transcriptional pathways that govern myofiber identity, and previous studies have shown that MEF2 binding sites are among the most statistically enriched within active regulatory regions of skeletal muscle, underscoring its pivotal role in myogenesis and fiber-type specification , . Additionally, analysis using MEF2-lacZ transcriptional reporter mouse demonstrates that calcineurin-dependent MEF2 activation occurs in both fast and slow skeletal muscle fibers with a greater inducibility in fast fibers indicating that MEF2 also exhibits functional transcriptional activity in fast myofibers. Moreover, genetic studies have shown that muscle-specific deletion of Mef2c or Mef2d leads to a marked reduction in slow fiber gene expression and a decrease in type I fibers , , whereas global Mef2a deletion has minimal impact on fiber composition . Conversely, triple knockout of Mef2a/c/d in myoblasts severely impairs differentiation and reduces fast fiber markers (Myh1,Myh4) as well as the pan-muscle gene Ckm . Beyond the gene expression changes observed in the knockout models, functional DNA-binding and luciferase reporter assays demonstrate MEF2C directly binds to the promoter regions and activates the transcription of Myomesin1 (Myom1) and Myomesin2 (Myom2) with Myom1 expressed in both slow- and fast-fiber-enriched muscles and Myom2 preferentially expressed in fast-fiber-enriched muscles , highlighting MEF2’s broad regulatory influence across both slow- and fast-twitch gene programs. Consistent with this, our data demonstrate that MEF2 occupies both slow- and fast-fiber gene loci, whereas BRD4 and CHD4 binding is more restricted to fast-fiber-associated gene loci. These findings support a model in which MEF2 functions as a global transcriptional scaffold that integrates upstream cues, while BRD4, in cooperation with CHD4, acts as a specialized enhancer-associated co-regulator directing MEF2 activity toward fast-fiber gene expression and the maintenance of glycolytic muscle identity. However, the precise mechanism by which the BRD4-CHD4 complex is tethered to MEF2 binding sites to activate fast-twitch muscle gene expression remains to be fully elucidated. It is plausible that the loss of BRD4 or CHD4 disrupts this complex, thereby redirecting MEF2’s regulatory activity toward slow-twitch fiber gene programs, leading to a fiber-type switch characterized by the upregulation of slow-fiber-specific genes such as slow myosin and troponin genes as well as Sln, where MEF2 binding is detected at their enhancers. Since the expression of fast-glycolytic muscle genes is also regulated by transcriptional regulators such as large MAFs and SIXs , , , it remains to be tested whether BRD4/CHD4 may also contribute to the regulation of MAFs and SIXs in muscle. Additionally, it will be of interest to determine whether BRD4/CHD4 plays an opposing role as an indirect transcriptional repressor of slow myofiber-specific gene expression. At any rate, these findings highlight the BRD4-CHD4-MEF2 axis as a key molecular mechanism that fine-tunes the balance between slow-oxidative and fast-glycolytic muscle fiber identities, providing new insights into the molecular basis of muscle plasticity.
Our findings highlight the potential of BRD4 inhibition as a promising strategy to promote myofiber identity switching toward a slow-oxidative phenotype in both adult mice and human myotubes. With obesity on the rise globally, there is a critical need for therapies that selectively reduce fat mass while preserving muscle mass. While GLP-1 receptor agonizts are effective for weight loss, their associated muscle loss underscores the need for alternative approaches. Our study demonstrates that pharmacological inhibition of BRD4 with JQ1 significantly reduces fat mass by 85% while preserving muscle mass, closely mirroring the effects seen in BRD4-deficient mice. Notably, JQ1-treated mice tolerated high doses without major systemic side effects, supporting its therapeutic potential. However, it is important to acknowledge that JQ1 exerts systemic effects beyond skeletal muscle, including its previously reported role in inhibiting adipogenesis , , . While pharmacological inhibition with the pan-BET inhibitor JQ1 phenocopies BRD4 deletion, these effects may involve broader BET inhibition. JQ1 lacks isoform selectivity and may affect BRD2, BRD3, and BRD4 simultaneously. Given that BRD3 may play distinct or even antagonistic roles compared to BRD4 , BRD3’s isoform-specific function is intriguing and warrants future study using BRD3 specific genetic models. Nevertheless, future strategies that modulate BRD4 activity may hold potential for treating metabolic disorders by promoting oxidative muscle remodeling and enhancing energy expenditure.

## Methods

## Mouse models
All animal experiments were performed in strict compliance with institutional guidelines for the humane treatment of animals and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) at the Model Animal Research Center (MARC) of Nanjing University (approval number. GZJ08). Wild-type male C57BL/6 J mice were obtained from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd (Jiangsu, China). Brd4f/f mice were generated by the Cam-Su Genomic Research Center using targeted embryonic stem (ES) cells. Chd4f/f mice were created by the transgenic mouse facility at the MARC of Nanjing University. All genetically modified mice were backcrossed to the C57BL/6 J background for at least six generations before being used in experiments. To achieve skeletal muscle-specific deletion of BRD4, Brd4f/f mice were crossed with transgenic mice expressing Cre recombinase under the control of either the human skeletal actin (HSA) promoter (Jackson Laboratory; stock no. 006139) or the muscle creatine kinase (MCK) promoter (Jackson Laboratory; stock no. 006475). For tamoxifen-inducible, muscle-specific deletion of BRD4 (BRD4 i-mKO), Brd4f/f mice were crossed with mice expressing a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase under the control of the HSA promoter (Jackson Laboratory; stock no.025750) , 8-week-old male mice were treated with tamoxifen (75 mg/kg mouse body weight/day; Sigma, T5648) for five consecutive days via intraperitoneal injections. Similarly, Chd4f/f mice were crossed with HSA-Cre transgenic mice to generate skeletal muscle-specific Chd4 knockout (CHD4 mKO) mice. Notably, both male and female mice were used in the study, muscle fiber type transformation compared with littermate controls, were similar in female compared with male for BRD4 mKO lines. The mice were euthanized with CO2 or isoflurane inhalation, followed by cervical dislocation at the end of the experiment. Mice aged 2–8 months of the indicated genotypes were used for all experiments. The mouse numbers for each experiment are provided in the legend of each Figure and Supplementary Figs. Mice were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility at Nanjing University. They were maintained in plastic cages under controlled conditions (temperature: 21  ±  2 °C; relative humidity: 55  ±  10%) with a 12-h light/12-h dark cycle. All mice had ad libitum access to food and water. For dietary studies, 4-week-old male BRD4 mKO or CHD4 mKO and their respective WT littermate controls were fed either standard rodent chow (XieTong Biology, 1010082) or a high-fat diet (HFD; 60% calories from fat, Research Diets no. D12492), as indicated in the experimental design. JQ1 was purchased from Jilin Chinese Academy of Sciences-Yanshen Technology Co., Ltd. (YSZC372). JQ1 was dissolved in 10% 2-hydroxypropyl-β-Cyclodextrin for intraperitoneal injection. For preventing obesity, 4-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were fed a HFD for 22 weeks and were intraperitoneally injected with JQ1 (50 mg/kg mouse body weight/day) during the HFD feeding. Mice were randomly assigned to experimental groups to minimize bias. Littermate controls were used in all experiments to ensure genetic consistency. All procedures and experimental designs were approved by the IACUC and conducted in accordance with institutional and national ethical standards.
Human studies. This study involving human participants was reviewed and approved by Clinical Trial Ethics Committee of Jinling Hospital affiliated to Nanjing University (Clinical Trial Registration Number: 2023DZKY-015-01). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment, in compliance with institutional and ethical guidelines. Quadriceps femoris muscle samples were collected from 11 human participants (9 male, 2 female) enrolled in the ESSENTIAL trial (Chinese Clinical Trial Registry Number: ChiCTR2100053359). Participants did not receive any compensation for their participation. Sex was not considered as a biological variable in the study design due to the limited sample size. Patients were divided into two groups: non-obese (BMI < 28, 51.5 ± 4.69 years old) and individuals with obesity (BMI > 28, 41 ± 6.81 years old). All muscle biopsies were collected at baseline, prior to the initiation of any trial intervention. Muscle biopsies were obtained using a sterile biopsy gun under aseptic conditions. Muscle tissues were cleaned and immediately embedded in OCT compound for morphological analysis or the tissue samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at –80 °C until RNA extraction was performed for downstream analysis.

## Cell culture
All cells were grown at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 humid atmosphere, and all growth mediums were supplemented with 1000 units/ml penicillin and 100 mg/ml streptomycin. HEK293T Cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection and maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% FBS. Transient transfections in HEK293T cells were conducted using polyethylenimine (PEI) Transfection Reagent (Polysciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were harvested for downstream applications 24–48 h post-transfection. LHCN-M2 human skeletal muscle cells (T0756, abm) were obtained from Applied Biological Materials Inc. (BC, Canada) and cultured in DMEM/M199 medium (4:1) supplemented with 1000 units/ml penicillin, 100 mg/ml streptomycin, 15% (v/v) FBS, 20 mM HEPES, 30 ng/mL zinc sulfate, 1.4 µg/mL vitamin B12, 55 ng/mL dexamethasone, 2.5 ng/mL recombinant human hepatocyte growth factor, and 10 ng/mL basic FGF. For myocyte differentiation, LHCN-M2 cells were cultured with 2% horse-serum/DMEM differentiation medium adding 50 nM JQ1 or DMSO used as control, cells were induced to differentiation for 3 or 5 days.
Histological analyses. Mouse skeletal muscle tissues were rapidly harvested and frozen in isopentane pre-cooled in liquid nitrogen to preserve tissue integrity. Notably, the GC and soleus of 4-month-old C57BL/6 J male mice of the indicated genotypes under HFD condition, or 8-week-old male and female C57BL/6 J mice of the indicated genotypes under CD condition were dissected intact and processed into 10-μm serial sections. For immunofluorescence (IF) staining , muscle sections were stained with specific antibodies to identify muscle fiber types: anti-MHC1 (slow-twitch fibers; BA-D5, catalog AB 2235587; Developmental Studies Hybridoma Bank), anti-MHC2b (fast-twitch fibers; BF-F3, catalog AB 2266724; Developmental Studies Hybridoma Bank), anti-MYH3 (F1.652, catalog AB 528358; Developmental Studies Hybridoma Bank) or anti-Laminin (catalog L9393; MilliporeSigma). For succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) and glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GPDH) histochemical staining , the frozen sections were thawed to room temperature and then incubated in a staining solution consisted of 100 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.6), 48 mM disodium succinate for SDH and 9.3 mM a-glycerol phosphate for GPDH, 1 mM l-methoxyphenazine methosulfate (MPMS), 1.5 mM nitro blue tetrazolium (NBT), 5 mM EDTA, and 1 mM sodium azide for 10 min at 37 °C in the dark. Standard H&E staining was performed on adipose tissue and muscle sections using a commercial H&E staining kit (MilliporeSigma) to assess tissue morphology. Muscle sections were stained with FITC-conjugated WGA (MilliporeSigma, L4895) to visualize the sarcolemma and extracellular matrix. The cross-sectional area (CSA) of individual myofibers was measured using ImageJ software (NIH), providing quantitative analysis of muscle fiber size. Liver tissues were embedded in Tissue-Tek OCT compound (Leica) and frozen at -80 °C. Cryosections (10 μm thick) were prepared using a cryostat. Sections were stained with 0.5% Oil red O to assess lipid accumulation and counterstained with H&E for structural evaluation.

## Metabolic measurements in vivo
Metabolic assessments were performed using the Comprehensive Lab Animal Monitoring System (CLAMS; Columbus Instruments). 4-month-old male WT and BRD4 mKO mice (n = 12-14 mice per group) under HFD condition were individually housed in specialized metabolic cages under controlled environmental conditions, maintained on a 12-h light/12-h dark cycle with free access to standard chow and water. To minimize stress and ensure accurate measurements, mice were acclimated to the metabolic cages for 24 h prior to data collection, following the manufacturer’s guidelines. During the recording period, the following metabolic parameters were continuously and simultaneously monitored: energy expenditure (kcal/h), physical activity (beam breaks), oxygen consumption (VO2) (mL/kg/h) and carbon dioxide production (VCO2) (mL/kg/h).

## Blood and tissue chemistry
Following a 12-week high-fat diet (HFD) regimen, 4-month-old male BRD4 mKO or CHD4 mKO mice and their respective WT littermate controls (n = 5–13 mice per group) were subjected to an overnight fast for 16 h, starting at 5:00 p.m. Blood samples were collected the next morning for the analysis of glucose and insulin levels. Plasma insulin concentrations were quantified using a commercially available insulin ELISA kit (Millipore), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Liver tissues (50 mg) were carefully excised, homogenized, and centrifuged to collect the supernatant. Hepatic triglyceride levels were measured using the Free Glycerol Reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, F6428). Glycerol standards (Sigma-Aldrich, G7793) were used to generate a standard curve for accurate quantification.

## Glucose and insulin tolerance testing
Glucose tolerance tests (GTT) and insulin tolerance tests (ITT) were conducted to assess glucose metabolism and insulin sensitivity in mice. 4-month-old male BRD4 mKO or CHD4 mKO mice and their respective WT littermate controls (n = 4–16 mice per group) upon CD and HFD conditions were enrolled. For GTT, mice were fasted overnight for 16 h. Mice received an intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of D-glucose (1.5 g/kg body weight). For ITT, mice were fasted for 4 h prior to testing. Mice were administered an i.p. injection of human regular insulin (Sigma-Aldrich) at a dose of 0.75 U/kg body weight. Blood glucose levels were measured from tail vein blood at 0, 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min after glucose or insulin injection using a OneTouch UltraMini glucose meter (OneTouch). The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to quantify glucose and insulin responses. AUC was determined as the difference between baseline glucose levels and the changes induced by glucose or insulin challenge.

RNA-seq studies. Transcriptomic analyses were conducted using RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) . For each sample, 1 μg of total RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara Bio). Random hexamer primers were used for reverse transcription, according to the manufacturer’s instructions, ensuring the elimination of genomic DNA contamination. Real-time quantitative PCR was carried out using the ABI Prism Step-One System (Applied Biosystems) in combination with SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara Bio). Specific oligonucleotide primers targeting genes of interest were designed and are listed in the Supplemental Table  . All primers are ordered from Tsingke Biotechnology Co.,Ltd. Gene expression levels were normalized to the housekeeping gene 36b4 to account for variations in RNA input and reverse transcription efficiency. Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCt method and expressed as arbitrary units relative to 36b4 expression.
ChIP, ChIP-qPCR and ChIP-seq. Mouse gastrocnemius muscles from 2-month-old male BRD4 mKO or CHD4 mKO and their respective WT littermate controls were harvested, minced, and cross-linked in 2.5% formaldehyde for 15 min followed by quenching with 1/20 vol of 2.5 M glycine solution for 5 min and two washes with 1× Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) wash buffer. Nuclear extracts were prepared by a Dounce homogenizer in ice-cold hypotonic buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Chromatin fragmentation was performed by sonication in lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-HCL at pH 8.0, 1% SDS, 10 mM EDTA) using a Bioruptor (Diagenode) to obtain DNA fragments of appropriate size. An aliquot of fragmented chromatin was pre-cleared with Protein G agarose beads to reduce nonspecific binding. Immunoprecipitation was carried out using the following specific antibodies: Anti-Pol II (17-620; Millipore), Anti-H3K27ac (ab4729; Abcam), Anti-CHD4 (ab70469; Abcam), Anti-BRD4 (13440; Cell Signaling Technology) were used. Cross-links were reversed, and immunoprecipitated DNA was purified using the standard phenol–chloroform extraction method. Immunoprecipitated DNA was analyzed by quantitative PCR using the Roche LightCycler 96 Instrument with Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix from Vazyme Bio. The enrichment of genomic regions was calculated relative to input DNA using the standard curve method. SYBR Green-based quantification of BRD4-ChIP normalized to IgG controls. Specific oligonucleotide primers for target regions are listed in Supplementary Table  . Purified DNA samples were processed for ChIP-seq library preparation following the standard protocol provided by Illumina. High-throughput sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform by Beijing Novogene Bioinformatics Technology Co. to generate genome-wide binding profiles.
ChIP-seq data processing. ChIP-seq data were processed by aligning the sequencing reads to the mm10 genome and subsequent peak calling. Paired-end 150-nucleotide sequencing reads were aligned to the mouse reference genome (UCSC mm10) using Bowtie 2 (version 2.2.5). Only reads that uniquely mapped to the genome with no more than one mismatch were retained for further analysis. These high-confidence alignments were considered valid reads for peak calling. Peaks representing enriched protein-DNA binding regions were identified against matching input control using the peak-calling algorithm MACS2 (version 2.1.1) with the parameter settings --keep-dup = 1, -B, --SPMR and a default q-value of 0.05 to generate signal pileup tracks in bedGraph format on a per-million-reads basis. Identified peaks were annotated by associating genomic regions with the nearest transcription start site (TSS) using the UCSC Genome Browser annotations. Genome-wide ChIP-seq binding profiles were visualized using the Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV) (version 2.3.70), allowing for detailed inspection of enriched binding regions across the genome. The mice muscle ChIP-seq data have been deposited in the NGDC Genome Sequence Archive and are accessible through GSA Series accession number: CRA022300 and CRA022314.
Histone extraction. Histone proteins were extracted from 2-month-old male WT and BRD4 mKO mice (n = 4-6 mice per group) skeletal muscle tissues using a standardized acid extraction protocol. Skeletal muscle tissues were thoroughly washed with ice-cold PBS to remove blood and debris. Tissues were then resuspended in Triton extraction buffer (TEB, PBS containing 0.5% Triton X-100 (vol/vol), 2 mM phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride and 0.02% (wt/vol) NaN3). The homogenized samples were centrifuged to pellet the nuclei. The nuclear pellet was resuspended in 200 μL of 0.2 N HCl and incubated at 4 °C overnight to solubilize histone proteins.

## Immunoprecipitation
Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) experiments were conducted in HEK293 cells co-transfected with combinations of MEF2, BRD4, and CHD4 expression constructs. 2-month-old WT male C57BL/6 J skeletal muscle tissues and HEK293T cells were harvested and lysed in ice-cold lysis buffer (50 mM Tris (pH 7.5), 150 mM NaCl, 2 mM EDTA, 1.5% NP-40, 1× cOmplete (Roche) and 1 mM phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride). Lysates were incubated on ice for 30 min and centrifuged to remove cellular debris. The clarified supernatants were collected for immunoprecipitation. Lysates were incubated with 1 μg of the following antibodies: anti-HA, anti-CHD4 or IgG. Antibody–antigen complexes were captured using Protein G-conjugated agarose beads and incubated with gentle rotation at 4 °C for 4 h or overnight. Beads were washed multiple times with lysis buffer to eliminate non-specific binding. Bound proteins were eluted by boiling in SDS loading buffer. The immunoprecipitated proteins were resolved by SDS-PAGE, transferred to membranes, and analyzed by Western blotting using specific antibodies to detect protein interactions.
TurboID sample preparation for mass spectrometry analysis. TurboID proximity labeling was performed in HEK293T cells to identify proteins interacting with BRD4 and MEF2C, followed by mass spectrometry analysis. HEK293T cells were infected with BRD4-Turbo or MEF2C-Turbo lentiviral particles for 24 h. Infected cells were subjected to selection with puromycin (1 μg/mL) for 3 days to enrich for successfully transduced cells. Cells were incubated with 500 μM biotin for 30 min to enable biotinylation of proximal proteins. Biotin labeling was terminated by transferring the cells to ice and gently washing twice with ice-cold Dulbecco’s PBS (DPBS). Cells were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer and incubated for 30 min at 4 °C with gentle agitation. Lysates were clarified by centrifugation at 13,760 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected for enrichment of biotinylated proteins. 200 μL of Pierce NeutrAvidin Agarose Beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #29200) were washed twice with RIPA lysis buffer. The washed beads were incubated with the clarified lysates overnight at 4 °C with rotation to capture biotinylated proteins. Beads were washed twice with 1 mL PBS and twice with 1 mL RIPA lysis buffer to remove nonspecific proteins. Biotinylated proteins were eluted by boiling the beads at 95 °C for 15 min in 30 μL 3× protein loading buffer supplemented with 20 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) and 2 mM biotin. Eluted proteins were clarified by centrifugation at 13,760 × g for 10 min, and the supernatant was transferred to fresh tubes. Proteins were resolved by SDS-PAGE and visualized using mass spectrometry-compatible Coomassie Brilliant Blue staining. Gel slices containing protein bands were excised and washed three times with 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate:acetonitrile (1:1). Gel pieces were dried using a SpeedVac and digested with trypsin at a 1:50 trypsin-to-protein mass ratio overnight at 37 °C. Peptides were extracted from the gel by vortexing three times in 10% formic acid:acetonitrile (1:1) for 15 min and dried via SpeedVac. Dried peptides were resuspended in 15 μL of 3% acetonitrile and 2% formic acid. Peptide samples were analyzed by nanoLC-MS/MS for protein identification. Candidate interacting proteins identified through TurboID-based proteomics were further validated in skeletal muscle models using biochemical interaction assays and functional analyses.

## Statistical and reproducibility
All mouse and cell studies were analyzed by 2-tailed Student’s t test when two groups were compared. One-way ANOVA coupled to a Fisher’s least significant difference (LSD) post-hoc test or two-way ANOVA coupled to Bonferroni’s post-hoc test were used when more than 2 groups were compared. No statistical methods were used to predetermine sample sizes, and sample sizes (ranging from n = 4 to n = 16) are explicitly stated in the figure legends. No data were excluded from the analyses. The experiments were not randomized. The Investigators were not blinded to allocation during experiments and outcome assessment. All data points were used in statistical analyses. Data represent the mean ± SEM, with a statistically significant difference defined as a value of P < 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 software. Correlations were assessed using Pearson’s correlation test (two-sided). Significant differences were defined as P < 0.05. The values of VO2, VCO2 and energy expenditure are adjusted by ANCOVA with one-sided F-test for body mass.
