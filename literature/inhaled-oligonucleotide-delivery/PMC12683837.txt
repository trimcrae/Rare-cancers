## Abstract
Atrial fibrillation (AF), the most common form of cardiac arrhythmia, is closely associated with atrial fibrosis and electrophysiological remodeling, both of which contribute to its persistence and recurrence. Current treatment strategies remain limited in effectiveness and safety, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic approaches. Nanocarrier-mediated delivery systems represent a promising approach, enabling targeted delivery of drugs and genes to modulate fibrotic pathways and restore electrical homeostasis. The review examines the molecular mechanisms driving AF-related fibrosis, including dysregulation of the TGF-β/Smad and Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascades, as well as the influence of neuro-cardiac interactions, lipid metabolism abnormalities, and oxidative stress. We systematically evaluate nanocarriers, such as lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), polymeric systems (PLGA/PEG), and metal-based nanomaterials (AuNPs, AgNPs), to enhance cardiac targeting, prolong circulation, and mitigate off-target effects. Notably, CRISPR-Cas9-loaded nanoparticles and siRNA/mRNA delivery systems demonstrate efficacy in suppressing extracellular matrix deposition and restoring connexin 43-mediated electrical coupling. In addition, stem cell-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) integrated with nanocarriers provide cell-free therapeutic options for myocardial repair. Key challenges, including biocompatibility, delivery precision, and long-term biosafety, are critically discussed, alongside emerging research directions involving artificial intelligence-guided nanodrug design and hybrid systems combining bioelectronic interfaces with nanoscale platforms. The integration of nanobiotechnology with molecular cardiology offers a path toward precision nanomedicine, enabling mechanism-based interventions that may transform the prevention and treatment of AF and facilitate the translation of preclinical advances into clinical application.

## Introduction
Recent progress in nanomedicine has led to increasing attention on the application of nanocarriers for managing atrial fibrillation (AF)-related fibrosis [ ]. Nanoparticle-based systems have created new opportunities for antifibrotic therapy by improving the cardiac specificity of drug delivery and minimizing systemic adverse effects [ ]. Moreover, the development of stimuli-responsive nanomaterials, including pH-sensitive and enzyme-activated release platforms, has shown promise in enhancing the precision and controllability of AF treatment [ ].

## Global burden and challenges of AF
AF is one of the most common cardiac arrhythmias, affecting millions of patients worldwide, with both incidence and prevalence continuing to rise. The Global Burden of Disease Study estimated approximately 37 million people had AF in 2020, which is projected to reach 60 million by 2050. The incidence of AF increased significantly with age, and the lifetime risk exceeds 20% among individuals older than 65 years [ ]. AF reduces quality of life and substantially increases the risk of stroke, heart failure, and mortality [ , ]. The risk of ischemic stroke in patients with AF is approximately five times higher than in those without AF [ ]. The coexistence of AF and heart failure is also frequent, with AF tripling the risk of developing heart failure [ ]. Moreover, AF has been linked to neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease, where cerebral hypoperfusion and microembolism associated with AF may accelerate cognitive decline [ , ].
Currently, the main treatment strategies for AF include pharmacological therapy, catheter ablation, and surgical interventions. Anticoagulant therapy (e.g., warfarin and novel oral anticoagulants) significantly reduces the risk of AF-related stroke but increases the likelihood of bleeding [ , ]. Rhythm control strategies using antiarrhythmic drugs, including amiodarone and sotalol, are effective in selected patients. However, long-term use often leads to serious adverse reactions such as thyroid dysfunction, pulmonary toxicity, and proarrhythmic effects [ ]. Catheter ablation has emerged as a rapidly advancing therapeutic technique that eliminates arrhythmogenic foci within the pulmonary veins and other trigger sites [ ]. Despite these advances, AF frequently recurs after ablation, with recurrence rates reaching 50% in patients with atrial fibrosis [ ]. Treatment of AF remains challenging, particularly in achieving durable rhythm control and preventing disease recurrence.
In recent years, growing attention has been directed toward emerging therapeutic strategies such as gene therapy, stem cell therapy, and nanomedicine to enhance the efficacy of AF treatment [ ]. Among these, nanomedicine has shown remarkable potential due to its distinctive advantages in precision and delivery efficiency. Nanocarriers have been employed to transport therapeutic agents or genes to targeted cardiac cells, thereby enhancing treatment efficacy while reducing systemic toxicity. Recent work introduced CRISPR/Cas9-based lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) that specifically modulate the KCNJ2 channel in atrial cardiomyocytes, effectively reversing electrical remodeling [ ]. Research on AF has gradually shifted toward the molecular level and personalized therapy, aiming to develop more effective and safer treatment strategies.

## The central role of atrial fibrosis in the onset and progression of AF
The atrium and ventricle exhibit marked differences in both structural and electrophysiological properties, rendering the atrium more susceptible to the initiation and maintenance of AF. The atrial myocardium has a thinner wall, a less-developed transverse tubule (T-tubule) network within atrial myocytes, and a higher density of autonomic nerve terminals, factors that collectively reduce electrical stability [ , ]. Atrial fibrosis represents a major pathophysiological mechanism contributing to AF onset and progression. It is defined by excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) components within the atrial interstitium, resulting in both structural disorganization and impaired myocardial function [ ]. The process involves abnormal proliferation of fibroblasts and excessive deposition of collagen types I and III, leading to decreased atrial compliance and disrupted electrical conductivity. These alterations generate regional conduction delays and heterogeneous propagation of electrical impulses, favoring the formation of micro-reentrant circuits that sustain AF [ , ]. Furthermore, atrial fibrosis reduces the expression of gap junction proteins such as connexin 43 (Cx43), which further exacerbates electrical remodeling and contributes to the persistence of arrhythmic activity [ , ].
Numerous studies have demonstrated that the autonomic nervous system plays a critical role in regulating atrial electrophysiology, promoting structural remodeling, and initiating arrhythmias [ , ]. Disruption of autonomic balance, characterized by excessive sympathetic activation, induces intracellular calcium overload in cardiomyocytes, enhances spontaneous ectopic activity, and accelerates atrial fibrosis and electrical remodeling. In contrast, vagal nerve remodeling shortens the atrial effective refractory period, increasing the likelihood of local atrial reentry [ ]. The atria contain a dense network of autonomic ganglia and nerve fibers, particularly the ganglionated plexi located at the junction between the atria and pulmonary veins, which serve as major trigger sites for AF. Persistent autonomic imbalance and pathological remodeling of these neural circuits disturb the atrial electrophysiological environment and intensify structural alterations through inflammatory activation and ECM remodeling, thereby establishing both anatomical and electrical substrates for AF [ ]. Enhanced sympathetic activity and vagal remodeling can alter the atrial effective refractory period and conduction velocity, thereby facilitating a pathological substrate prone to arrhythmogenesis [ ].
Major contributors to atrial fibrosis include chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, mechanical stress, and metabolic dysregulation. Chronic inflammation plays a pivotal role in both the initiation and progression of AF. Multiple studies have reported elevated serum concentrations of interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) in patients with AF, which activate cardiac fibroblasts and promote excessive ECM deposition [ ]. Oxidative stress further accelerates fibrosis by generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) and activating the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway, leading to cellular injury and enhanced collagen synthesis [ ]. Mechanical stress arising from increased atrial pressure, as seen in hypertension or valvular disease, stimulates fibroblast proliferation and ECM accumulation through mechanotransduction pathways such as RhoA/ROCK signaling [ ]. In addition, metabolic disorders, including diabetes and obesity, increase circulating glucose and fatty acid levels, promoting fibrosis through non-enzymatic glycation and chronic inflammatory activation (Fig. A) [ , ].
 Current antifibrotic therapeutic strategies remain limited. The clinical efficacy of conventional antifibrotic drugs such as pirfenidone and nintedanib in AF management has not been fully established [ , ]. Therefore, exploring novel antifibrotic strategies has become a key direction in AF research, among which nanomedicine offers a promising therapeutic approach.

## The potential of nanomedicine in AF and antifibrotic therapy
Recent advances in nanomedicine have demonstrated considerable potential in the treatment of cardiovascular diseases, particularly in antifibrotic therapy and electrophysiological remodeling. Nanocarrier-based delivery systems such as liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, and inorganic nanomaterials enable precise drug targeting, improve therapeutic efficacy, and minimize adverse reactions [ ]. Nanocarrier-based drug delivery strategies mainly focused on targeting fibroblasts to reduce excessive ECM deposition, modulating inflammatory cytokines to inhibit fibrotic progression, and correcting conduction abnormalities to restore normal atrial rhythm [ ].
The advantages of nanocarriers lie in their strong targeting capability and favorable biocompatibility. Surface modification with cardiac-specific peptides or antibodies allows selective recognition of diseased tissues, thereby enhancing drug accumulation at pathological sites and improving treatment outcomes [ ]. In addition, nanomaterials such as gold nanoparticles (AuNPs), silver nanoparticles (AgNPs), and iron oxide nanoparticles have been explored for electrophysiological modulation by facilitating local release of antiarrhythmic agents or directly influencing the membrane potential of cardiomyocytes to prevent AF recurrence (Fig. B) [ , ].

## Mechanisms of atrial fibrosis and electrophysiological remodeling
Recent studies using single-cell RNA sequencing of atrial tissue from patients with AF have identified several fibroblast subpopulations with distinct functional profiles. Certain subtypes promote excessive ECM accumulation through activation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Targeted inhibition of this pathway using small-interfering RNA (siRNA) or small-molecule inhibitors has emerged as a promising therapeutic approach. For example, suppression of transforming growth factor-β–activated kinase 1 (TAK1) or modulation of connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) expression via miR-133a markedly reduced the synthesis of fibrosis-related proteins [ ]. Oxidative stress contributes to calcium-handling abnormalities and electrical conduction heterogeneity by activating the ROS/CaMKII pathway. Inhibition of NADPH oxidase 2 (NOX2) or treatment with mitochondrial ROS scavengers such as mitoTEMPO improves atrial electrical remodeling [ ]. Notably, epigenetic regulation, including histone deacetylase inhibition by valproic acid, suppresses fibrotic progression by reversing DNA methylation modifications, offering a novel direction for multi-target combination therapy [ ].

## Mechanisms and molecular basis of atrial fibrosis

## Activation and proliferation of fibroblasts
Activation and abnormal proliferation of fibroblasts constitute a fundamental mechanism underlying atrial fibrosis [ ]. Under normal physiological conditions, cardiac fibroblasts remain quiescent and become activated only in response to myocardial injury or mechanical stress. Under chronic pathological stimuli such as persistent inflammation or metabolic imbalance, fibroblasts acquire an activated phenotype characterized by enhanced proliferative capacity and increased expression of α-smooth muscle actin, leading to differentiation into myofibroblasts [ ]. These myofibroblasts exhibit strong secretory activity, producing large quantities of ECM proteins, including collagen types I and III, fibronectin, and proteoglycans, which progressively increase tissue stiffness and rigidity within the atrial stroma [ ].
Multiple cytokines and growth factors contribute to fibroblast activation, among which TGF-β is a key regulator. TGF-β promotes myofibroblast differentiation and excessive ECM accumulation through both Smad-dependent and Smad-independent signaling pathways [ ]. Angiotensin II and endothelin-1 amplify fibroblast proliferation and ECM synthesis via activation of G protein-coupled receptor signaling [ , ]. Evidence from atrial tissue of patients with AF indicates a significant increase in fibroblast density and proliferative activity, which correlates closely with conduction system abnormalities and electrical remodeling [ ].
Lipid metabolism disorders play an important role in the development of atrial fibrosis. Obesity and high-fat diets increase plasma free fatty acid levels, which activate signaling pathways such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α (PPAR-α), nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB), and TGF-β. These pathways promote fibroblast activation and excessive synthesis of ECM proteins [ ]. Elevated free fatty acids cause lipid droplet accumulation in cardiomyocytes and impair mitochondrial function, leading to excessive production of ROS. The resulting oxidative stress and inflammation further drive atrial remodeling [ ]. Epicardial adipose tissue also contributes to fibrosis by secreting proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, which act on adjacent atrial tissue through paracrine mechanisms [ ]. Recent evidence indicates that lipid stress increases AF susceptibility by inhibiting the Akt signaling pathway [ ]. These findings suggest that lipid metabolic disturbances serve not only as risk factors for AF but also as key drivers of its structural and electrophysiological remodeling.
Mechanical stress also plays a critical role in fibroblast activation. Chronic atrial overload resulting from hypertension or valvular disease enhances fibroblast mechanosensitivity through integrin- and focal adhesion kinase–mediated signaling, enabling a rapid response to sustained mechanical stimulation [ – ]. This process promotes excessive ECM accumulation and accelerates fibrosis progression (Fig. ).
 

## Abnormal deposition and degradation of the ECM
The ECM, primarily composed of collagen, elastin, fibronectin, and glycosaminoglycans, is essential for maintaining the structural integrity and mechanical stability of cardiac tissue [ ]. During the development of atrial fibrosis, the ECM undergoes marked compositional changes, characterized by excessive deposition of collagen types I and III. Such accumulation leads to atrial stiffening, reduced compliance, and impaired contractility [ ]. Excessive ECM accumulation not only alters myocardial architecture but also disrupts electrical conduction, thereby increasing susceptibility to AF [ ].
An imbalance between matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and their endogenous inhibitors, tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs), plays a central role in ECM dysregulation. Under physiological conditions, MMPs degrade ECM components to preserve matrix homeostasis [ ]. Chronic inflammation associated with AF suppresses MMP activity and elevates TIMP expression, resulting in impaired ECM degradation and progressive matrix accumulation [ ]. Clinical studies have shown that the MMP-9/TIMP-1 ratio is significantly reduced in atrial tissue of patients with AF, suggesting a critical mechanism underlying abnormal ECM metabolism [ ].
In addition, inflammatory responses and oxidative stress aggravate the ECM metabolic imbalance. Pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α upregulate TGF-β signaling, promoting ECM synthesis [ – ]. Simultaneously, ROS induce oxidative modifications of ECM proteins, increasing their resistance to enzymatic degradation and amplifying matrix accumulation (Fig. ) [ , ]. Therefore, restoring the balance between ECM synthesis and degradation represents a promising therapeutic approach for the prevention and reversal of atrial fibrosis.
 

## Inflammation-mediated signaling pathways
Inflammation plays a central role in the development of atrial fibrosis associated with AF. Histological analyses have revealed increased infiltration of inflammatory cells and sustained elevation of inflammatory mediators in atrial tissue from patients with AF [ ]. Among these, the NF-κB signaling pathway serves as a major regulatory mechanism in inflammation-induced fibrosis. Upon inflammatory stimulation, NF-κB becomes activated and translocates into the nucleus, where it promotes the transcription of cytokines such as IL-6, TNF-α, and TGF-β. These mediators further activate fibroblasts, enhance ECM synthesis, and contribute to progressive fibrotic remodeling [ , ].
TGF-β acts as a key proinflammatory and profibrotic factor that promotes ECM production and suppresses matrix degradation through both Smad-dependent and Smad-independent pathways [ , ]. IL-6 also plays a pivotal role by activating the JAK/STAT signaling pathway, which induces fibroblast proliferation and differentiation [ ]. Elevated IL-6 concentrations have been positively correlated with AF recurrence, indicating that IL-6 may represent an important therapeutic target for fibrosis prevention [ ].
Anti-inflammatory strategies have demonstrated therapeutic potential in mitigating AF-related fibrosis. Experimental evidence shows that inhibition of the NF-κB, IL-6, and TGF-β pathways reduces ECM accumulation and improves atrial conduction function. Pharmacological interventions targeting inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin antagonists, are currently under investigation and may offer novel approaches for clinical management of AF (Fig. ) [ , ].
 Recent studies have also explored nanocarrier-based delivery systems to modulate inflammation and metabolism in fibrotic processes. Delivery of lipid metabolism regulators such as miR-103a-3p and miR-27a using engineered nanocarriers effectively attenuated fibrosis mediated by metabolic signaling, suggesting a promising mechanistic approach for future AF therapy [ – ].

## Mechanisms of electrophysiological remodeling
Atrial fibrosis not only alters the structural composition of atrial tissue but also significantly affects its electrophysiological properties, thereby promoting the initiation and maintenance of AF. Electrophysiological remodeling refers to changes in the electrical activity of atrial cardiomyocytes, including shortened action potential duration, reduced effective refractory period, and increased spatial heterogeneity of repolarization and conduction. These alterations collectively lower the excitation threshold and stabilize AF persistence [ ].
Fibrosis disrupts the normal architecture of atrial tissue, leading to the formation of localized conduction block areas that impair the synchrony of electrical propagation. Such structural discontinuities provide a substrate for reentrant excitation. Reduced intercellular coupling among cardiomyocytes increases conduction heterogeneity, making excitation waves more susceptible to delay or block, thereby promoting arrhythmogenesis [ ].
Oxidative stress plays a crucial role in electrophysiological remodeling. ROS activate the Ca²⁺/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) signaling pathway, resulting in abnormal calcium handling within cardiomyocytes. This dysfunction induces sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ leakage and delayed afterdepolarizations, which increase the likelihood of arrhythmia formation [ ]. ROS also oxidize sodium channels (Nav1.5) and potassium channels (IKur and IK1), altering action potential duration and repolarization kinetics, which further destabilize electrical activity.
Inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α contribute to electrophysiological remodeling by modulating ion channel expression through the JAK/STAT and NF-κB pathways. These cytokines reduce potassium channel density and impair electrical synchrony. Elevated IL-6 levels are associated with higher AF recurrence risk, as IL-6 amplifies atrial inflammation and promotes the parallel progression of structural and electrical remodeling [ ]. Lipid metabolism disturbances also influence atrial electrophysiological remodeling by affecting ion channel regulation and calcium homeostasis [ ].
Interventions targeting oxidative stress and inflammation have demonstrated potential in alleviating electrophysiological remodeling. Inhibitors of NOX2 and mitochondrial ROS scavengers such as mitoTEMPO effectively improve atrial electrical stability and calcium handling in experimental models, supporting the therapeutic potential of antioxidant strategies in AF prevention [ ].
Structural and electrophysiological remodeling are closely interconnected. Structural fibrosis provides the anatomical substrate for electrical disturbances, while electrophysiological alterations reinforce reentrant activity, together forming a self-perpetuating cycle that drives AF progression (Fig.  ).
 

## Application of nanocarriers in antifibrotic therapy for AF

## Nanodrug delivery systems

## LNPs
LNPs have emerged as an important nanoplatform for antifibrotic therapy in AF due to their high biocompatibility, stability, and efficient drug delivery capability. By encapsulating small interfering RNA (siRNA), microRNA (miRNA), or small-molecule compounds, LNPs effectively suppress fibroblast activation and ECM deposition [ ]. LNPs are typically composed of phospholipids, cholesterol, and cationic lipids, providing advantages such as ligand-mediated targeting, in vivo biodegradability, and reduced systemic toxicity [ ]. Furthermore, through modification with cardiac-targeting ligands such as cyclic arginine-glycine-aspartic acid (cRGD), LNPs significantly enhance drug delivery efficiency to fibrotic myocardial tissue [ ], demonstrating favorable therapeutic efficacy and clinical application potential.
Experimental studies have shown that siRNA-loaded LNPs significantly downregulate the expression of TGF-β and CTGF, leading to the inhibition of fibroblast activation [ ]. Moreover, LNP-mediated delivery of miR-29 and miR-133 effectively reduces collagen types I and III expression, thereby alleviating ECM accumulation and improving myocardial remodeling [ ].
LNPs have also been employed to deliver small-molecule antifibrotic agents such as pirfenidone, which effectively suppresses cardiac fibroblast activation by inhibiting the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway [ , ]. Modification of LNPs with cardiac-targeting ligands further enhances drug uptake by fibroblasts, improving therapeutic efficacy. Studies demonstrated that cRGD-modified LNPs exhibit strong affinity for fibrotic myocardial regions with high αvβ3 integrin expression, resulting in a 3.5-fold increase in drug delivery efficiency [ ]. The cRGD peptide serves as a widely used ligand for cardiac fibroblast targeting, specifically binding to the αvβ3 integrin receptor and enhancing nanoparticle accumulation in diseased tissue [ ]. Research has demonstrated that cRGD-modified LNPs can significantly improve miRNA delivery efficiency to the atrial tissue of AF patients, reducing ECM deposition.
Recent advances have expanded the application of LNPs toward delivering lipid metabolism–related regulatory molecules to modulate atrial fibrosis [ , ]. LNP-based miRNA delivery systems have been developed to regulate lipid metabolism and atrial remodeling [ ]. By selectively silencing the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 pathway, miRNA-loaded LNPs effectively reduce fibroblast activation and collagen I expression, thereby mitigating electrophysiological remodeling and AF susceptibility [ ]. These findings indicate that lipid-based nanocarriers function as efficient vehicles for antifibrotic drugs and as delivery systems for lipid metabolism–modulating miRNAs, providing new opportunities for mechanistic intervention and personalized therapy in AF.

## Polymeric nanocarriers
Polymeric nanocarriers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) and polyethylene glycol (PEG)-modified nanoparticles have been widely used in the biomedical field due to their biodegradability [ ], low immunogenicity, and high drug-loading capacity [ , ]. PLGA nanoparticles, characterized by controllable drug release kinetics and extended circulation time, have been used to deliver antifibrotic drugs including pirfenidone, nintedanib, and indomethacin (10.1088/0957-4484/23/50/5051010.3389/fmolb.2021.677547). PEG modification prolongs systemic retention and reduces macrophage-mediated clearance, thereby enhancing drug accumulation within fibrotic cardiac tissue.
PLGA nanocarriers have also been developed for gene delivery, particularly for transporting siRNA or mRNA to suppress ECM-related gene expression. For instance, PLGA-encapsulated TGF-β1 siRNA effectively downregulated fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis in atrial tissue [ , ]. Moreover, PEG-modified PLGA nanoparticles enhanced miRNA delivery to atrial tissue to regulate signaling pathways involved in cardiac fibrosis 10.1039/d3na00149k; 10.1186/s12951-024-02403-w). Recent studies have demonstrated that PLGA nanoparticles loaded with miRNA-133 significantly improved atrial fibrosis and restored electrophysiological homeostasis in AF mouse models [ ]. The T7 peptide, which binds to transferrin receptors, facilitates nanocarrier penetration across vascular barriers. T7-modified PLGA nanoparticles markedly improved pirfenidone bioavailability and alleviated atrial fibrosis in AF models [ ].
In addition, PLGA carriers have been employed for antioxidant delivery; encapsulated N-acetylcysteine effectively reduced ROS production, suppressed inflammation, and limited fibrotic progression [ , ]. The combination of controlled release, biocompatibility, and multifunctional design establishes PLGA-based systems as a promising platform for antifibrotic therapy in AF [ ].

## Inorganic nanomaterials
Inorganic nanomaterials, such as AuNPs, AgNPs, and silica nanoparticles (SiNPs), have attracted attention in biomedical applications due to their excellent stability and biocompatibility. AuNPs possess strong photothermal conversion properties, which can be utilized for selective ablation of pathological tissue and enhancement of drug delivery [ ]. Functionalization of AuNPs with specific ligands, such as TGF-β receptor antagonists, allows selective inhibition of fibroblast activation and reduction of fibrotic progression [ ].
AgNPs exhibit both anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, suppressing the release of cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α and slowing fibrosis development [ – ]. In addition, AgNPs regulate fibroblast apoptosis and limit ECM deposition, improving atrial structural remodeling in AF [ , ]. Studies have shown that AgNPs loaded with miRNA-21 inhibitors effectively reduced the activation of atrial fibroblasts and improved cardiac electrophysiological stability [ ].
SiNPs also show strong potential due to their excellent modifiability and biocompatibility. Covalent attachment of siRNA to SiNPs enables targeted silencing of ECM-related genes, thereby attenuating fibrotic progression [ ]. Furthermore, SiNP-based systems have been applied to deliver antiarrhythmic agents, effectively improving electrophysiological abnormalities associated with AF [ , ].
In summary, inorganic nanomaterials, due to their multifunctionality, hold significant potential for application in antifibrotic therapy related to AF.

## Targeted delivery strategies

## Nanocarrier-Mediated mRNA and protein delivery strategies
mRNA and protein-based therapeutics show strong potential for precise AF treatment by restoring electrophysiological stability, suppressing atrial fibrosis, and modulating neural signaling. In recent years, nanodrug delivery systems have provided efficient platforms for macromolecule transport due to their ability to protect therapeutic agents from degradation, enable tissue-specific targeting, and achieve controlled release.
For mRNA delivery, LNPs have emerged as the predominant platform due to their excellent biocompatibility and efficient intracellular release. Chen et al. successfully delivered KCNJ2 mRNA, which encodes the Kir2.1 potassium channel, to atrial tissue using LNPs. The treatment restored Kir2.1 expression, improved electrophysiological function in AF animal models, and significantly reduced AF incidence, thereby validating mRNA therapy in AF [ ].
For protein delivery, botulinum toxin (BoNT) has been explored for its ability to inhibit acetylcholine release from presynaptic terminals, thereby prolonging the atrial effective refractory period and attenuating vagus nerve–mediated autonomic remodeling [ , ]. Although BoNT provides therapeutic benefits, its delayed onset and short duration limit long-term efficacy. To overcome these limitations, chitosan nanoparticles have been used to improve BoNT delivery and pharmacokinetics [ ]. Chitosan, a biodegradable and cationic polymer, enhances BoNT solubility at physiological pH and exhibits strong membrane affinity, facilitating targeted transport and sustained release. Animal studies demonstrated that chitosan-based carriers extend BoNT’s neuroblocking duration, enhance autonomic regulation, and reduce AF inducibility [ ].
Smad7 has been identified as a key inhibitory regulator of the TGF-β1/Smad pathway, and its expression is markedly downregulated in AF-induced models, resulting in excessive atrial fibrosis [ ]. The delivery of Smad7 protein or mRNA to atrial tissue using nanocarriers represents a rational therapeutic strategy to suppress fibrotic remodeling. However, no studies have yet demonstrated this approach experimentally, and further investigations are needed to confirm its feasibility and efficacy.
To enhance nanocarrier localization in atrial tissue and autonomic ganglia, researchers have functionalized nanoparticle surfaces with myocardium-targeting peptides such as CSTSMLKAC. Studies reported that CSTSMLKAC-modified inhalable calcium phosphate (CaP) nanoparticles accumulated selectively in fibrotic myocardial regions and autonomic ganglia, thereby improving cardiac function [ ]. For example, nanoparticles modified with the cRGD peptide can target the αvβ3 integrin receptor, enhancing the targeted delivery of antifibrotic agents [ ].
In summary, combining mRNA and protein therapeutics with nanodelivery systems offers new perspectives for mechanism-based precision interventions in AF (Fig.  ). Current applications have demonstrated promising outcomes in restoring electrophysiological balance, reducing fibrosis, and regulating autonomic function. Nonetheless, strategies such as Smad7 delivery remain at the conceptual stage and require further validation. Future multifunctional nanoplatforms incorporating targeting peptides may achieve efficient, controlled, and site-specific drug delivery to atrial lesions, advancing the clinical translation of precision AF therapy.
 

## Nanocarrier-Mediated delivery of other advanced therapeutics
Beyond mRNA and protein therapeutics, other classes of biomolecules and advanced modalities have attracted growing attention for AF treatment. Nanocarriers provide a versatile platform to deliver these agents with improved stability, targeted distribution, and controlled release.
CRISPR–Cas9 gene editing offers a promising strategy to correct pathogenic mutations or regulate disease-related genes such as GJA1, which encodes connexin 43, and CACNA1C, which encodes CaV1.2 [ ]. Lipid nanoparticles, gold nanoparticles, and calcium phosphate nanocarriers have been used to encapsulate Cas9 mRNA or ribonucleoprotein complexes, achieving targeted genome modification in cardiac tissue and improving electrophysiological stability in preclinical models. However, efficient atrial-specific delivery and minimizing off-target genome editing remain critical challenges.
Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) provide another means of regulating gene expression by binding to RNA transcripts to promote degradation or alter splicing patterns [ ]. Nanocarriers, particularly PEGylated liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles, can protect ASOs from nuclease degradation and facilitate their uptake by atrial cells. For example, ASO delivery targeting pro-fibrotic mediators such as CTGF or TGF-β receptors has shown potential in reducing atrial fibrosis.
Programmable DNA/RNA nanostructures possess inherent modularity and the capacity for multivalent presentation, enabling the co-assembly of siRNAs, aptamers, and small-molecule drugs on a single carrier. Moreover, designs based on DNA origami allow the incorporation of cardiac-targeting ligands such as cRGD peptides, thereby enhancing myocardial tissue accumulation [ ]. Building upon this foundation, multi-drug co-delivery strategies can enable the synchronous administration of siRNAs and antiarrhythmic small molecules, thereby achieving synergistic modulation of structural remodeling and electrophysiological abnormalities in AF. When combined with stimuli-responsive and sequential-release mechanisms, such systems can further optimize drug timing and enhance therapeutic efficacy [ ].
In summary, integrating these advanced therapeutics with nanocarrier delivery systems could expand the range of mechanism-driven interventions for AF, offering opportunities for multi-target, personalized treatment strategies.

## Nanocarrier-mediated electrophysiological remodeling strategies
CRISPR–Cas9 technology combined with nanocarrier delivery systems has been employed to repair GJA1 gene defects that encode the gap junction protein connexin 43 (Cx43). Such targeted correction enhances Cx43 expression in atrial cells and improves electrical coupling between cardiomyocytes. Preliminary animal studies demonstrated that this approach strengthened atrial conduction and significantly reduced the recurrence of AF.

## Targeted regulation of cardiac ion channels

## Nanocarrier delivery of rapid delayed rectifier potassium current/Slow delayed rectifier potassium current (IKr/IKs) modulators
Potassium ion channels (IKr and IKs) play a central role in maintaining cardiac action potential repolarization. Abnormalities in these channels contribute to the initiation and persistence of AF [ , ]. Both IKr and IKs regulate repolarization timing and ensure electrical stability in atrial and ventricular myocardium [ ]. Dysfunction of IKr/IKs channels in AF leads to delayed repolarization and increases arrhythmogenic risk [ , ]. Conventional potassium channel modulators often show extensive systemic distribution, which results in adverse effects such as QT interval prolongation and increased proarrhythmic risk [ , ].
In recent years, nanocarriers have shown great potential for the targeted delivery of IKr/IKs modulators. LNPs encapsulating IKr agonists enhanced drug accumulation in cardiac tissue and minimized systemic toxicity [ , ]. Moreover, PLGA nanoparticles loaded with IKs activators improved channel function and enhanced atrial repolarization properties [ ]. Lipid metabolism–related factors, such as miR-103a-3p, also influence electrophysiological stability indirectly by modulating the Akt and TGF-β pathways. Conjugating nanocarriers with atrial-targeting ligands further enables tissue-selective delivery and enhances therapeutic efficiency [ , ].
Moreover, mRNA delivery systems have emerged as a promising strategy for modulating IKr/IKs channel expression [ ]. Nanoparticles carrying ERG1 mRNA, which encodes the IKr channel, effectively restored normal electrical conduction in cardiomyocytes [ ]. This approach provides a novel framework for gene-based electrophysiological modulation and establishs a foundation for precision therapies targeting cardiac ion channel remodeling in AF (Fig. A).
 

## Targeted delivery of CaV1.2 channel modulators
CaV1.2 serves a central role in cardiac excitation–contraction coupling, and its dysfunction contributes to arrhythmias and myocardial impairment. In patients with AF, abnormal CaV1.2 expression leads to intracellular calcium overload, activating calcium-dependent signaling cascades and promoting atrial fibrosis. Targeted modulation of CaV1.2 activity has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy [ ].
Nanodelivery provides a feasible pathway for multi-level modulation of CaV1.2 [ , ]. Formulating verapamil as solid lipid nanoparticles can improve cardiac exposure and produce cardioprotective effects in animal models, suggesting the application potential of “L-type calcium-channel blocker and nanocarrier” [ ]. In cardiomyocytes, interventions targeting CACNA1C can downregulate CaV1.2 and reduce L-type currents; meanwhile. Non-viral LNPs have successfully achieved nucleic acid delivery to myocardial and cardiac tissues both ex vivo and in vivo, thereby establishing a solid foundation for the application of siRNA or ASOs in modulating CaV1.2 expression in vivo [ , ]. Recent advances in selective organ targeting (SORT)-LNPs further demonstrated organ-specific delivery and gene editing of mRNA or CRISPR cargos in mice, providing a technical basis for coupling gene-editing tools to CaV1.2 regulation [ ].
CRISPR–Cas9 technology has also been applied to modulate CaV1.2 expression. Studies reported that Cas9-loaded nanoparticles effectively silenced CaV1.2 overexpression, restored calcium homeostasis, and improved atrial electrophysiological properties (Fig.  B). The success of this strategy highlights a major step forward in nanomedicine for precise ion channel regulation in AF therapy.

Its dysfunction leads to Ca²⁺ overload and prolonged action potentials, which exacerbate arrhythmogenic activity [ ].
KCNJ2 encodes the inward rectifier potassium channel Kir2.1, which is essential for maintaining resting membrane potential and terminal repolarization. MicroRNA-1 (miR-1) targets both KCNJ2 and GJA1, the gene encoding connexin 43 (Cx43), to co-regulate atrial action potentials and electrical coupling. LNPs carrying miR-1 have been shown to increase Cx43 expression, improve electrical conduction, and markedly reduce AF incidence [ ].
Cx43, the principal gap junction protein in the atria, is significantly downregulated in AF, leading to impaired electrical synchronization and the formation of micro-reentrant circuits. Restoring Cx43 expression has become a major therapeutic goal for stabilizing atrial conduction. Nanocarrier-mediated delivery of miR-1, miR-133, or siRNA has been used to regulate Cx43 levels effectively. In preclinical studies, PLGA nanoparticles loaded with Cx43 siRNA enhanced intercellular electrical connectivity in atrial tissue [ ]. Similar outcomes have been observed with LNPs encapsulating miR-1, which upregulated Cx43 expression and restored conduction stability [ ]. The successful application of these nanocarriers underscores their potential for correcting AF-related electrophysiological abnormalities (Fig. C).
Nanocarrier-loaded miRNA-1, miRNA-133, and siRNA have been applied to regulate Cx43 expression effectively. Studies demonstrated that lipid nanoparticles carrying miRNA-1 increased Cx43 levels, enhanced atrial electrical coupling, and significantly reduced the incidence of AF [ ]. Similarly, siRNA-mediated modulation of Cx43 expression has been validated in animal models, where PLGA nanoparticles loaded with Cx43 siRNA markedly improved intercellular electrical connectivity in atrial tissue [ ].
Nanocarrier-mediated electrophysiological remodeling enables precise regulation of atrial action potential repolarization, calcium balance, and gap junction communication by targeting key molecules such as cardiac ion channels (IKr, IKs, and CaV1.2) and Cx43. Compared with conventional pharmacological approaches, nanodelivery systems offer improved cardiac tissue targeting, higher bioavailability, and lower systemic toxicity, while supporting diverse therapeutic modalities including small molecules, nucleic acids, mRNA, and CRISPR–Cas9 gene editing. Animal studies have confirmed that these strategies enhance atrial electrophysiological stability, suppress electrical remodeling, and reduce AF recurrence, highlighting nanomedicine as a promising precision therapy platform for AF (Table  ).
 

## Promotion of myocardial regeneration and repair

## Exosome-Based nanocarrier repair strategies
Stem cell–derived exosomes, which contain bioactive molecules such as miRNAs, proteins, and metabolites, have emerged as important mediators of myocardial regeneration [ ]. Incorporating these exosomes into nanocarrier systems enhances their accumulation in cardiac tissue and strengthens their reparative capacity. Recent studies demonstrated that nanoparticles carrying mesenchymal stem cell (MSC)–derived exosomes promoted regeneration of damaged cardiomyocytes and attenuated atrial fibrosis [ ]. Furthermore, exosome-based nanocarriers loaded with miRNAs have been used to repair injured atrial tissue. For example, nanoparticles encapsulating miR-21 enhanced cardiomyocyte proliferation and suppressed fibrotic signaling activation, highlighting a promising cell-free approach for myocardial repair while reducing immune rejection risk associated with stem cell transplantation [ , ].
Lin et al. reported that rapidly paced atrial cardiomyocytes release miR-210-3p via exosomes, which target and inhibit GPD1L in fibroblasts and activate the PI3K/AKT pathway, thereby stimulating fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis. Inhibition of miR-210-3p reversed these fibrotic changes and significantly decreased AF incidence and fibrosis severity in an angiotensin II–induced model [ ].
Exosomes derived from cardiomyocytes, cardiac fibroblasts, and stem cells are enriched with antifibrotic miRNAs such as miR-378, miR-29a/b, and miR-455, which exert synergistic effects on fibrosis suppression through multiple molecular pathways. miR-29a/b directly inhibits collagen (COL1A1, COL3A1) and fibronectin synthesis, thereby reducing ECM accumulation. miR-378 downregulates MAPK pathway–related molecules, limiting fibroblast activation and migration. miR-455 interferes with the TGF-β/Smad pathway to suppress fibrotic signal transduction. In addition, these exosomal miRNAs can reduce MMP9 expression and preserve ECM homeostasis, collectively mitigating cardiac fibrosis through multi-target coordination. The combined antifibrotic and regenerative actions of these exosome-based nanocarriers demonstrate strong therapeutic potential for AF-associated myocardial injury.

## Synergistic nanomaterial delivery strategies to promote myocardial regeneration
Recent advances have focused on nanomaterial-based strategies for myocardial regeneration, emphasizing the precise delivery of bioactive proteins and the synergistic application of multifunctional nanomaterials to enhance cardiac repair. Ahmadi et al. developed chitosan–hyaluronic acid nanoparticles to deliver basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), which significantly promoted cardiomyocyte proliferation and capillary formation in a mouse model of myocardial infarction [ ].
Amiodarone, a commonly used antiarrhythmic agent, generates active metabolites with prolonged half-lives that can accumulate in the body, leading to potential toxicity in non-cardiac organs such as the lungs, liver, and thyroid [ ]. Encapsulation of amiodarone within PLGA nanoparticles allows controlled release, enhances cardiac bioavailability, and markedly reduces systemic toxicity, offering long-term safety for chronic administration [ ]. This approach minimizes off-target effects while maintaining stable rhythm control and supporting myocardial recovery.
Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) is a standard therapy for arrhythmias. However, postoperative myocardial inflammation can trigger AF recurrence. Thermosensitive hydrogels composed of poly(N-isopropylacrylamide-co-acrylamide) copolymer nanoparticles have been engineered to release anti-inflammatory agents (e.g., budesonide) in response to the localized heat produced during RFA. This approach effectively suppresses inflammation, reduces myocardial injury, and facilitates local tissue repair [ ]. Moreover, nanoparticles that respond gradually to environmental stimuli can provide sustained drug release, ensuring prolonged therapeutic support during the myocardial regeneration period.
In summary, the multifunctionality and engineerability of nanomaterials provide diverse strategies for myocardial regeneration therapy. These systems enhance protein delivery efficiency and enable spatially and temporally controlled release through environment-responsive mechanisms. Future studies should explore combined delivery of proteins and RNAs, as well as synergistic integration with exosome-based therapies, to achieve comprehensive and durable myocardial regeneration.

## Advances and challenges of nanocarriers in the treatment of AF

## Progress in preclinical research

## Summary of recent major research findings
In recent years, nanocarrier-based strategies for AF therapy have achieved substantial progress. Various nanomaterials (e.g., LNPs and PLGA), and inorganic nanomaterials (e.g., AuNPs and AgNPs), have been employed to deliver antifibrotic and antiarrhythmic agents [ , ]. Most nanoparticles ranged from 50 to 150 nm in diameter, were surface-modified with PEG to enhance circulatory stability, and were administered intravenously to target cardiac tissue. Several preclinical studies demonstrated effective therapeutic strategies. For example, CaP nanoparticles functionalized with a cardiac-targeting peptide (CTP) and carrying a TGF-β inhibitor reduced atrial ECM deposition and lowered AF incidence [ , ]. AuNP-mediated delivery of a CRISPR-Cas9 system targeting Cx43 improved electrical coupling [ , , ], providing multiple promising therapeutic avenues (Table ).
 Further studies demonstrated that miRNA-loaded nanoparticles downregulated ECM-related genes, attenuated atrial fibrosis, and enhanced electrophysiological performance [ , ]. CRISPR–Cas9 gene-editing technology combined with nanocarrier delivery systems has also been applied to repair AF-related mutations, offering a potential platform for precision therapy [ – ]. However, current applications remain confined to preclinical animal studies.
The development of nanodrug delivery systems for AF has increasingly emphasized personalized precision therapy. Nanoparticles modified with cardiac-targeting ligands such as T7 peptides markedly enhanced myocardial drug accumulation while reducing systemic side effects. Previous studies confirmed that peptide-modified nanodelivery systems improved local cardiac deposition and enhanced treatment specificity [ , ]. Calcium phosphate nanoparticles modified with CTP and delivered by inhalation demonstrated excellent biocompatibility and tissue selectivity in cardiomyopathy models, suggesting a novel noninvasive approach for AF management [ , ]. Moreover, nanocarriers targeting ion channels such as CaV1.2 and IKr/IKs effectively improved atrial electrophysiology and decreased AF recurrence [ ], highlighting the multifaceted therapeutic potential of these systems.
Substantial advances have also been achieved in EV–based nanocarrier research. Nanoparticles incorporating MSC–derived EVs effectively delivered reparative miRNAs such as miR-21, promoting cardiomyocyte regeneration and suppressing fibrosis. MSC-derived EVs offered superior biocompatibility and immune evasion compared with synthetic nanomaterials. For instance, MSC EVs embedded within injectable hydrogels modulated CCR2⁺ macrophage niches, enhancing cardiac conduction and angiogenesis [ – ]. In contrast, cardiac tissue–derived EVs, including those from epicardial adipose tissue, carried pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic mediators that promoted atrial remodeling through activation of TGF-β and IL-6 signaling pathways. Targeted regulation of these EVs and their secretion may mitigate AF-related fibrosis [ , ].
Recent findings have also linked abnormal lipid metabolism to AF pathogenesis, identifying related miRNAs such as miR-103a-3p and miR-27a as novel therapeutic targets. Experimental studies demonstrated that LNP-mediated delivery of these miRNAs effectively corrected lipid metabolism–induced fibrosis and electrophysiological dysfunction [ , ]. These findings broaden the mechanistic framework of nanotherapy for AF and provide new perspectives for the clinical translation of personalized metabolic interventions.

## Animal experimental models
Animal models have played a pivotal role in evaluating the efficacy and safety of nanocarrier-based therapies for AF [ ]. Canine and porcine models are most commonly used to reproduce the pathophysiological characteristics of AF and assess the therapeutic potential of emerging nanomedicine strategies. Owing to its close resemblance to human cardiac electrophysiology, the canine AF model is widely utilized to examine the antiarrhythmic effects of nanodrug formulations [ ]. In a canine rapid-pacing AF model, Lin et al. demonstrated that exosome-mediated miR-21-5p signaling promoted atrial fibrosis through regulation of the TIMP3/TGF-β1 pathway. Inhibition of exosome secretion with GW4869 alleviated electrophysiological abnormalities, underscoring the importance of canine models in elucidating the mechanisms of structural remodeling in AF [ ]. The porcine AF model, characterized by cardiac dimensions and anatomy comparable to those of humans, serves as an optimal system for studying AF-associated fibrosis and electrophysiological remodeling [ ]. Gene therapy targeting connexin 43 (Cx43) restored atrial electrical coupling and prevented persistent AF in porcine studies [ ], demonstrating cross-species translational potential of gene-based interventions that modulate electrical conduction. These findings support the integration of antifibrotic agents or Cx43-pathway modulators with nanoparticle delivery systems to enhance therapeutic outcomes (Fig. ).
 

## Existing challenges

## Biosafety and immunogenicity
Despite the promising therapeutic potential of nanocarriers for AF, biosafety remains a major obstacle to clinical translation. Studies have shown that inorganic materials such as SiNPs and AuNPs may induce cytotoxicity due to long-term accumulation in vivo, and activate Toll-like receptor signaling pathways, resulting in chronic inflammation [ , ]. In addition, some nanoparticles can elicit immune responses and inflammatory reactions that may limit their clinical applicability [ ].
PEG modification has proven effective in reducing immune recognition by forming a hydration layer that minimizes opsonin adsorption and prolongs circulation time. For instance, PEGylation extended the half-life of liposomal nanoparticles from 4 to 24 h [ ]. Biodegradable materials such as PLGA and CaP nanoparticles demonstrated superior metabolic properties, as their degradation byproducts (lactic acid and glycolic acid) were metabolized via the tricarboxylic acid cycle, thus reducing the risk of tissue accumulation [ – ]. For example, PLGA nanoparticles encapsulating budesonide reduced serum edema by 30% in a porcine AF model without disturbing cortisol rhythmicity [ ].
Nevertheless, long-term toxicological validation remains necessary, particularly through extended animal studies lasting 6–12 months. Recent evidence suggests that the acidic microenvironment generated during PLGA degradation can induce autophagy dysregulation in cardiomyocytes, a process that may be mitigated through surface functionalization with materials such as chitosan to improve biocompatibility [ – ]. Furthermore, biomimetic nanocarriers, such as cell membrane-coated nanoparticles, mimicked natural cell membrane components, evading immune clearance. AF-associated myocardial fibrosis models achieved a 40% increase in targeting efficiency [ ].

## Challenges in vivo targeting efficiency
The in vivo targeting efficiency of nanocarriers remained a major challenge. Although targeting ligands such as cRGD and T7 peptides enhanced nanoparticle accumulation in cardiac tissue [ , , ], a portion of nanodrugs was still non-specifically, reducing therapeutic efficacy. In addition, hemodynamic forces and tissue barriers may hinder targeted delivery, leading to insufficient drug accumulation at the lesion site.
To address this issue, researchers have explored magnetically guided delivery strategies based on magnetic nanoparticles (e.g., Fe3O4 nanoparticles), enabling the directional distribution of nanocarriers through external magnetic fields. Moreover, stimuli-responsive nanocarriers—such as pH-triggered release systems—have been developed to improve site-specific drug release in cardiac tissue (Fig.  ).
 

## Future directions

## Artificial intelligence (AI)-Driven nanodrug design
AI and machine learning are accelerating innovation in nanodrug design. AI can predict nanoparticle biocompatibility, optimize physicochemical parameters of drug carriers [ ], and improve the efficiency of targeted delivery [ ]. Deep learning models have been applied to simulate in vivo distribution patterns of nanodrugs, enabling optimization of particle design and pharmacokinetic behavior [ ]. Kwon et al. used machine learning to develop a lipid molecular structure–performance model and designed ionizable lipid nanoparticles that achieved high mRNA delivery efficiency, resulting in targeted mRNA expression in cardiac tissue in murine models [ ]. Similarly, Chen et al. integrated AI algorithms with microphysiological chip technology to conduct high-throughput screening of nanomaterial–cell interactions in myocardial models, enabling predictive analysis of myocardial regeneration potential, which was later confirmed in vivo [ ].
AI has also been applied to develop personalized therapeutic strategies for nanomedicine. Through large-scale data analysis, AI can identify molecular and clinical profiles of AF patients and recommend individualized nanotherapy regimens [ ]. This technological progress is expected to promote precision therapy in the field of AF nanomedicine.

## Integration of nanocarriers with bioelectronic medicine
Recent advances in nanotechnology combined with bioelectronic platforms have created new possibilities for the diagnosis and treatment of arrhythmias such as AF. The integration of nanocarriers with bioelectronic medicine represents a promising direction for next-generation AF therapy. One representative innovation is the nanoelectronic cardiac patch, which incorporates flexible conductive nanomaterials with miniature electronic circuits. The system enables real-time recording of myocardial electrical activity and provides mild electrical stimulation when necessary to stabilize cardiac rhythm [ ]. Animal studies have confirmed its ability to improve myocardial electrical conduction and reduce the recurrence of AF [ ].
Implantable biosensors have been developed for continuous monitoring of AF. Constructed from nanoscale conductive polymers, MXene materials, or metallic nanowires, these devices enable real-time detection of arrhythmias, electrophysiological fluctuations, and changes in inflammatory or metabolic biomarkers. Certain biosensors can function synergistically with nanocarrier-based drug delivery systems, allowing signal-triggered therapeutic feedback to maintain cardiac stability [ , ].
Magnetoelectric nanoparticles have recently gained attention for their potential in modulating the neuro–cardiac axis. Under an external magnetic field, these nanoparticles can remotely stimulate autonomic neural circuits, thereby influencing cardiac conduction, adjusting the atrial effective refractory period, and reducing the likelihood of AF initiation [ ]. The noninvasive nature of this remote modulation approach offers a promising pathway for achieving closed-loop, patient-specific therapy.
In summary, the integration of bioelectronics and nanoplatforms is advancing AF treatment from single-drug interventions toward an integrated “sensing–response–modulation” system. With continuous developments in materials science, sensor technology, and nanodelivery systems, this approach holds great promise for achieving smarter, more efficient, and personalized AF management pathways in the future.

## Outlook and conclusion

## Outlook

## Nanomedicine holds great promise for AF treatment but requires interdisciplinary collaboration to advance clinical translation
Nanomedicine has shown considerable potential in the treatment of AF, particularly in antifibrotic therapy, electrophysiological remodeling, and myocardial regeneration. Abnormal lipid metabolism, a major contributor to AF, has gained increasing attention, with growing interest in nanocarrier-mediated delivery of lipid metabolism regulators such as miR-103a-3p and miR-27a. These molecules not only regulate fibroblast activation but also influence oxidative stress and electrical remodeling, marking a transition from purely antifibrotic therapy toward integrated strategies combining metabolic modulation and electrophysiological stabilization. Through the efficient delivery capabilities of nanocarriers, researchers have achieved targeted delivery of small molecules, RNA interference agents such as siRNA and miRNA, and gene-editing tools including CRISPR-Cas9 to correct AF-related pathophysiological abnormalities. Integration of stem cell-derived EVs with nanocarriers has also introduced a cell-free approach for myocardial repair and fibrosis inhibition. These developments expand the therapeutic landscape for AF, improve cardiac targeting, reduce systemic toxicity, and enhance drug accumulation within atrial tissue.
However, despite notable progress in preclinical and animal studies, translating nanomedicine into clinical practice remains a major challenge. Long-term biosafety and immunogenicity must be thoroughly evaluated. Inorganic nanomaterials such as silver and silica nanoparticles can accumulate in tissues and impair organ function, while polymeric carriers like PLGA may provoke inflammatory responses. The biodistribution and metabolic pathways of nanocarriers also remain insufficiently understood, necessitating further in vivo pharmacokinetic investigations to refine delivery efficiency. Moreover, the fabrication of nanomaterials requires coordination among multiple disciplines, including chemistry, materials science, biomedicine, and engineering. Large-scale production and quality control continue to pose significant barriers to clinical application. Therefore, successful clinical translation of nanomedicine for AF treatment depends on close interdisciplinary collaboration among pharmaceutical scientists, biomedical engineers, and clinicians to establish safe, effective, and standardized therapeutic platforms.

## Future directions include AI-Driven High-Throughput nanodrug screening and the development of personalized therapies to enhance the precision and safety of AF treatment
Rapid advances in AI have significantly expanded its role in nanomedicine, particularly in nanodrug design, high-throughput screening, and personalized therapy development. AI has been applied to predict nanoparticle biocompatibility and stability, optimize physicochemical properties, and simulate in vivo pharmacokinetics through computational modeling. Machine learning algorithms have analyzed large nanodrug datasets to refine surface modifications and targeting strategies, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects. AI has also enabled high-throughput screening and classification of nanocarriers based on their biological behavior, accelerating the discovery of next-generation nanotherapeutics.
Precision medicine represents a key direction for future nanomedicine development. Multiple factors, including genetic variation, inflammatory activity, metabolic imbalance, and electrophysiological properties, influence the onset and progression of AF. Integrating multi-omics data (e.g., genomics, proteomics, metabolomics) with AI-based analytical models enables the design of individualized therapeutic strategies for more precise intervention. Based on patient-specific genetic profiles, AI can identify optimal miRNA delivery schemes to selectively regulate genes associated with fibrosis or ion channel dysfunction. Furthermore, AI can continuously monitor physiological parameters in patients and adjust nanodrug dosage and delivery frequency in real time to enhance treatment accuracy and overall efficacy.
Beyond AI and personalized therapy, future research may focus on integrating nanocarriers with bioelectronic medicine, including nanoelectronic cardiac patches and intelligent wearable devices, to achieve more accurate monitoring and intervention for AF. Implantable nanosensors could continuously track cardiac electrical activity and biochemical fluctuations, initiating controlled drug release when early AF signals are detected. Such integration enhances diagnostic sensitivity and enables precise, on-demand therapy, thereby improving overall treatment efficacy (Fig.  ).
 In summary, combining nanomedicine with AI, high-throughput screening, and personalized therapeutic strategies is expected to advance AF management toward precision medicine. Future studies should aim to optimize in vivo delivery efficiency, ensure long-term biosafety, and establish intelligent diagnostic and therapeutic platforms to improve clinical outcomes and quality of life in patients with AF.

## Summary
AF is the most common persistent cardiac arrhythmia worldwide, characterized by a high recurrence rate and a strong association with adverse outcomes such as stroke and heart failure. Substantial evidence indicates that atrial fibrosis and electrophysiological remodeling form the dual pathological basis for the initiation and persistence of AF. Current treatment strategies remain limited in their capacity to reverse structural abnormalities and conduction disturbances, particularly in patients with advanced fibrosis or impaired electrical coupling. Therefore, developing therapeutic approaches that can simultaneously address both structural and functional abnormalities is essential for improving long-term outcomes in AF.
Nanomedicine offers new avenues for AF intervention. Various nanocarriers, including LNPs, PLGA, inorganic nanomaterials, and exosomes, enable efficient delivery of antifibrotic agents, miRNA/siRNA, mRNA, and CRISPR-Cas9 systems. These platforms can precisely modulate key signaling pathways such as TGF-β/Smad, Wnt/β-catenin, and NOX2/CaMKII, while restoring essential proteins including Cx43, CaV1.2, and KCNJ2, thereby achieving coordinated regulation from structural remodeling to electrophysiological repair. Ligand modifications such as cRGD, T7, and CSTSMLKAC further enhance cardiac tissue targeting and improve therapeutic efficacy, supporting the development of personalized treatment strategies.
Despite encouraging preclinical outcomes, the clinical translation of nanotherapeutics remains limited. Major challenges include insufficient assessment of long-term biosafety and immunogenicity, low delivery efficiency and tissue accumulation in vivo, and the absence of standardized evaluation criteria across nanoplatforms. Complex pathological conditions, including chronic inflammation and lipid metabolic disorders, may further alter nanomedicine biodistribution and pharmacodynamics, introducing additional uncertainty in therapeutic outcomes. Manufacturing control and the establishment of clinical-grade quality standards also represent key obstacles that must be overcome to ensure safe and effective clinical application.
Bridging the gap between basic research and clinical application requires focused effort in three areas. First, future work should advance the design of intelligent, stimuli-responsive, and biomimetic nanocarriers to enhance in vivo stability and targeting specificity. Second, integrating artificial intelligence with high-throughput screening can optimize nanodrug design and enable personalized treatment strategies, promoting the realization of precision therapy. Third, interdisciplinary collaboration must be strengthened to verify long-term efficacy and safety in large animal models and to establish reproducible, regulatory-compliant manufacturing standards. As these technologies and frameworks mature, nanomedicine is expected to deliver transformative therapeutic options for AF, shifting treatment from symptomatic control toward fundamental mechanistic reversal.
