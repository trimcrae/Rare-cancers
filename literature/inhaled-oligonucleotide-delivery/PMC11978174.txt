## Background
Melanoma (SKCM) is an extremely aggressive form of cancer, characterized by high mortality rates, frequent metastasis, and limited treatment options. Our study aims to identify key target genes and enhance the diagnostic accuracy of melanoma prognosis by employing multi-omics analysis and machine learning techniques, ultimately leading to the development of novel therapeutic strategies.

## Methods
We obtained and processed transcriptomic data, including RNA expression profiles, methylation microarray data, gene mutation data, and clinical information, from the TCGA dataset using multi-omics analysis and machine learning techniques. We comprehensively evaluated the molecular subtypes of melanoma, the characteristics of the tumor microenvironment (TME), and their effects on patient outcomes. By analyzing the TCGA-SKCM and GEO cohorts, we identified three melanoma subtypes with distinct prognostic features. Additionally, we developed a machine learning-driven signature (MLDS) based on marker genes for different molecular subtypes to significantly improve the prognostic prediction accuracy for melanoma patients. We also extensively examined differences in clinical features, immune cell infiltration, mutational landscapes, and drug treatment effects between high- and low-scoring subgroups. The predictive reliability of MLDS was further explored by knocking down the key signature gene AGPAT2 in melanoma cells using small interfering RNA.

## Results
The MLDS demonstrated high C-index values in both the training and validation cohorts, indicating its potential for clinical decision-making. The study also found that MLDS scores were associated with reduced immune cell infiltration and lower expression levels of immune checkpoints. Patients in the low MLDS group may be more responsive to chemotherapeutic agents and more likely to benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Single-cell sequencing analysis revealed that the MAPK signaling pathway between AGPAT2 + melanoma cells and fibroblasts/myeloid cells promotes tumor survival in the TME. Finally, the oncogenic role of AGPAT2 in melanoma cell lines was successfully confirmed through cell function assays and subcutaneous tumor formation assays in nude mice.

## Conclusion
This study not only uncovers the diversity and complexity of melanoma molecular subtypes but also underscores the crucial role of the TME in melanoma progression. It provides new insights and tools for personalized treatment and prognostic assessment of SKCM.

## Introduction
Melanoma, originating from melanocytes in the skin’s outer layer, is an aggressive form of cancer. It is known for its rapid spread and resistance to therapy, leading to a high fatality rate [ ]. While the five-year survival rate for early-stage melanoma is nearly 100%, this figure significantly decreases to just 20% for cases with distant metastasis [ ]. Surgery is the mainstay of treatment for early-stage melanoma; however, many patients are not detected until advanced disease, at which point the prognosis deteriorates significantly. Currently, targeted therapies are only recommended for patients with BRAF-mutant melanoma, and there is a lack of sufficient therapeutic evidence for other types of mutations [ ]. Therefore, in-depth study of the mechanisms of melanoma development, identification of key biomarkers, and exploration of key target genes to improve the diagnostic value of melanoma is essential for the development of new therapeutic strategies and improvement of patient prognosis.
With the rapid development of therapeutic technologies, the medical community has continued to explore and experiment with innovative approaches in the field of melanoma treatment. Although immunotherapy, a breakthrough in melanoma treatment, has shown significant efficacy in some patients, treatment strategies for advanced melanoma, especially in patients who have progressed after first-line treatment, still face many challenges [ ]. The failure of many patients to derive the expected clinical benefit from immunotherapy may be related to the remarkable heterogeneity and complex molecular subtypes of melanoma. The heterogeneity of melanoma, which results in significant differences in disease progression and treatment response in patients, arises from the genetic and phenotypic diversity of tumor cells and their interactions with the tumor microenvironment (TME). Cells in the TME, such as immune, stromal, and vascular cells, interact with tumor cells and collectively determine the likelihood of disease progression, therapeutic efficacy, and therapeutic resistance [ ]. However, previous studies have mainly focused on tumor cells, neglecting the critical role of TME in melanoma development. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is a powerful tool for elucidating the complex biological mechanisms and gene expression patterns within melanoma cells. By examining the variability in gene expression across individual cells, this technique captures the randomness and dynamism inherent in the transcriptional profiles of key genes. With its high-resolution capability, scRNA-seq uncovers the full spectrum of cellular heterogeneity, offering detailed insights into the molecular landscape of skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) and advancing our comprehensive knowledge of the disease [ ].
In this study, we comprehensively assessed the molecular subtypes of melanoma, the characteristics of the tumor microenvironment, and their impact on patient prognosis using multi-omics analysis and machine learning techniques. By analyzing the TCGA-SKCM database and the GEO cohort, we identified three melanoma molecular subtypes with significantly different prognostic features. This finding not only reveals the diversity and complexity of melanoma molecular subtypes but also provides new targets for personalized treatment. Additionally, we constructed a machine learning-driven prognostic signature (MLDS) that significantly improved the accuracy of prognostic prediction for melanoma patients. Compared to traditional prognostic models, MLDS integrates multiple machine learning algorithms to more accurately identify high-risk patients, offering robust support for clinical decision-making. Our prognostic signature further identified a new prognostic gene, AGPAT2. Through single-cell RNA sequencing, we meticulously characterized the oncogenic role of AGPAT2 and its associated signaling pathways in the TME of melanoma. This finding offers a new perspective for the study of melanoma molecular mechanisms. We confirmed the importance of AGPAT2 for melanoma proliferation, migration, and poor prognosis by knocking down AGPAT2 with small interfering RNA in both in vitro and in vivo experiments. These results not only validate the prognostic value of AGPAT2 but also provide potential therapeutic targets for future treatment strategies. Moreover, this study highlights the critical role of the TME in disease progression. By analyzing TME characteristics, we provide new insights and tools for personalized treatment and prognostic assessment of melanoma. In summary, our study has made significant progress in the research of melanoma molecular subtypes and prognostic biomarkers, and it offers a new direction for future clinical applications and the development of treatment strategies.

## Materials and methods

## Sources of raw data
The flowchart of this study is shown in Fig.  . We retrieved multi-omics data for the TCGA-SKCM project from the UCSC Xena platform (https://xenabrowser.net/), including mRNA expression profiles, long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) expression profiles, miRNA expression profiles, methylation microarray data, gene mutation data, and associated clinical information. To ensure data consistency and comparability, all expression data were first converted into TPM (Transcripts Per Million) format to eliminate the effects of sequencing depth and gene length, followed by log2 transformation to reduce data skewness and meet the assumptions required for subsequent statistical analyses. Additionally, we downloaded three independent external validation datasets (GSE19234, GSE59455, and GSE65904) from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to verify the reliability and generalizability of our findings [ – ]. To eliminate systematic biases that may arise from different platforms and experimental batches, we used the ComBat function provided by the “sva” package in R for batch effect correction and further enhanced data consistency using the removeBatchEffect function from the “limma” package [ ]. During data preprocessing, we also conducted rigorous quality control, including filtering out low-expressed genes (e.g., TPM < 1) and checking sample grouping consistency through principal component analysis (PCA) and hierarchical clustering. Finally, all standardized and corrected data were saved as normalized matrices for subsequent analyses, including differential expression analysis, survival analysis, and functional enrichment analysis.

 

## Single-cell sequencing data download and processing
Single-cell sequencing data from three cases of melanoma were retrieved from the dataset GSE215120, as referenced in the study [ ]. The Read10X_h5 function was employed to process the h5 file format data, as detailed in another study [ ]. Utilizing the “Seurat” R package, Seurat objects were created and subjected to a stringent filtration process. This involved selecting cells that expressed a gene count between 200 and 7000, had less than 20% mitochondrial genes, and contained less than 10% hemoglobin genes, following the guidelines outlined in the literature [ ]. To mitigate the impact of batch effects among the three samples, the “harmony” R package was implemented. The single-cell sequencing data underwent a series of standardization processes, including normalization via the “NormalizeData” function. This was complemented by scaling, batch effect correction, and dimensionality reduction, focusing on highly variable genes. Once the cells were categorized into distinct clusters, the cell types were identified and annotated based on the specific gene expression profiles characteristic of each subgroup. The developmental trajectory analysis was then conducted using the Monocle2 algorithm, which was applied to the inferred tumor cells. The analysis utilized default parameters following the downsampling and unit sorting of the data, as per the methodology described in the relevant publication [ ]. Furthermore, the “Cellcall” R package was employed to deduce the intercellular communication networks and internal regulatory signals. This package compiles KEGG pathway-based ligand-receptor-transcription factor (L-R-TF) axis datasets. It facilitates the inference of cell-to-cell communication by integrating prior knowledge of L-R-TF interactions with the expression levels of ligands/receptors and the activity of downstream transcription factors, as reported in the study [ ].

## Multi-omics consensus cluster analysis
In our research, we utilized the “getElites” function from the “MOVICS” R package to identify key genes. For continuous data types, including mRNA, lncRNA, miRNA, and methylation profiles, we applied the “mad” method to filter and select the top 1500 most variable genes. We then switched to the “Cox” method to integrate clinical data, focusing on genes with significant prognostic value at a p-value cutoff of less than 0.05. For mutation data, treated as a binary variable, we first used the “oncoPrint” function from the maftools package to identify the top 5000 genes with the highest mutation rates. We refined this list by prioritizing the top 1% of genes with the highest mutation frequency using the “freq” method. After consolidating the findings from these five dimensions, we moved on to determine the optimal number of clusters for our study using the “getClustNum” function from “MOVICS.” This function uses the Clustering Predictive Index (CPI), gap statistics, and profile coefficients to estimate the number of subgroups, leading us to classify the samples into three distinct subtypes. We then conducted cluster analysis with the “getMOIC” function, applying 10 clustering algorithms: CIMLR, ConsensusClustering, SNF, iClusterBayes, PINSPlus, moCluster, NEMO, IntNMF, COCA, and LRA. The “methodslist” parameter in the “getMOIC” function was set with these algorithms, following the default parameters of the MOVICS package, to produce clustering results for each. Subsequently, we used the “getConsensusMOIC” function to merge the clustering outcomes from the different algorithms, employing consensus clustering to strengthen the robustness of the clustering by integrating the results into a single classification. To delve deeper into the regulatory mechanisms, we constructed transcriptional regulatory networks, or regulons, using the “RTN” R package. We also assessed the distribution of immune checkpoints across the identified subtypes, estimating the immune and stromal scores of tumor tissues with the “estimate” R package. For subtype stability evaluation, we began by validating the clustering results against subtype-specific biomarkers in a validation cohort. We further compared the consensus clustering with the NTP and PAM classifiers for consistency, following the methodologies outlined in the referenced study [ ]. This comprehensive approach ensures a thorough evaluation of the subtypes and their stability, contributing to a deeper understanding of the underlying biology and potential therapeutic implications.

## Integrating machine learning algorithms to build predictive models
We conducted a comprehensive evaluation of the prognostic significance of 300 mRNAs related to molecular subtyping in both the TCGA and GEO cohorts. This assessment was performed through univariate Cox regression analyses, providing a foundational understanding of the association between each mRNA and patient outcomes. Building on these results, we ventured into the realm of machine learning to develop predictive models. We employed 101 combinations of 10 distinct machine-learning algorithms. Each algorithm underwent a rigorous 10-fold cross-validation process, with the TCGA cohort used for training and the GSE19234, GSE22153, and GSE65904 cohorts used for validation. The algorithms we utilized included a mix of established and advanced methods, such as StepCox, Lasso, Ridge, partial least squares Cox regression (plsRcox), Cox Boost, Random Survival Forest (RSF), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Elastic Net (Enet), Supervised Principal Component Analysis (SuperPC), and Survival Support Vector Machine (Survival-SVM).
Our machine learning approach is similar to the combination of 10 machine learning algorithms used in the study by Liu et al. [ , ]. We used the same algorithms: StepCox, Lasso, Ridge, plsRcox, CoxBoost, RSF, GBM, Enet, SuperPC, and Survival-SVM. Both studies employed 101 algorithm combinations and used the concordance index (C-index) as the metric for evaluating model performance. The primary difference lies in the cross-validation method: we used 10-fold cross-validation, while Liu et al. used leave-one-out cross-validation. Our main objective was to identify the most valuable prognostic and immunotherapeutic features, as indicated by the highest C-index. The C-index served as the benchmark for assessing the predictive accuracy of our models. After a meticulous comparison of the C-index across all models tested in the validation set, the model that achieved the highest rank was identified as the optimal machine learning-driven signature (MLDS).

## Construction of the nomogram
Participants were categorized into high and low MLDS score groups based on the median MLDS score. To evaluate the prognostic disparity between these groups, we deployed the “survival” and “survminer” R packages. Furthermore, the “timeROC” R package was employed to determine the AUC values. A column chart was constructed to estimate the one-, three-, and five-year overall survival (OS) probabilities, with risk score, age, and clinical stage serving as independent prognostic indicators. The precision of the column-line graph was appraised through the generation of ROC curves. We conducted consistency index analysis and decision curve analysis (DCA) to evaluate the incremental value of the column-line diagram in conjunction with clinical characteristics. Lastly, mutation data from the TCGA cohort were extracted and normalized into a binary matrix. We calculated the tumor mutation burden (TMB) and mutation frequency for the MLDS scoring group to further elucidate the genetic landscape associated with MLDS.

## Immune cell infiltration landscape and tumor microenvironment score
Based on gene expression data from the Cancer Genome Atlas, we used the R package “estimate” to infer the presence and number of mesenchymal stromal and immune cells in tumor tissues and to measure tumor purity [ ]. To delve deeper into the immune cell composition of the SKCM tumor microenvironment (TME), we accessed the TIMER 2.0 database, a comprehensive resource providing multiple methods of immune cell analysis and evaluation. Single-sample genomic enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was then applied to measure the relative prevalence of various immune cell populations in the samples and their associated functions. This approach provides us with a nuanced understanding of the immune environment within the TME and helps to explore the interactions between tumor cells and their surrounding immune cells in greater detail.

## Immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and enrichment analysis
Xu et al. have developed an online resource that compiles a repository of genes implicated in cancer and immunological processes, which serves as a valuable reference in our research [ ]. Additionally, Mariathasan’s research has identified a set of genes that are responsive to anti-PD-L1 therapies. To explore the potential link between these genetic profiles and the risk scores, we quantified the expression of these gene sets using the Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) method and depicted the results with the aid of the “ggcor” R package [ ]. The Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA) web tool provides comprehensive immunogenomic analysis results (https://tcia.at/home). The Immune Profile Score (IPS) is a tumor immunogenicity score ranging from 0 to 10 that is used to predict effective responses to ICIs [ ]. In addition, we determined the semi-inhibitory concentration (IC50) values for a range of chemotherapeutic agents commonly used in the treatment of melanoma using the “pRRophetic” R software package. This analysis helps to understand the sensitivity of melanoma cells to various treatments [ ]. To gain insight into the biological pathways and functions associated with the different MLDS scoring groups, we performed a single genome enrichment analysis (GSEA). With this approach, we were able to identify changes in gene ontology (GO) and KEGG signaling pathway activation to gain more insight into the molecular mechanisms behind MLDS scoring.

## Functional experiments of melanoma cells
Cell culture and siRNA transfection methods for A875 and A375 cell lines have been described in our previous study [ ]. The sequence of siRNA used for AGPAT2 is shown: 5’ CCUGUGUGUCAUCGUCUCCAA. After successful transfection of siRNA, we verified by Cell counting kit-8, colony formation, Wound-healing assay, and transwell assay that the function of AGPAT2 in melanoma such as proliferation migration was used in a previous bioinformatics study [ ].

## Subcutaneous tumor xenografts and Immunofluorescence in nude mice
Healthy four-week-old male nude mice were purchased from Zhejiang Life River Laboratory and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions. In this study, all animal care and handling procedures were in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the First Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University. Nude mice were euthanised at the end of the experiment. The choice of euthanasia method was based on ensuring that the animals were rapidly rendered unconscious in a painless and fearless state and minimising pain and anxiety during the death process. For mice, we used an inhalational anaesthetic euthanasia method. Specifically, mice were anaesthetised by inhalation of isoflurane or sevoflurane prior to euthanasia to ensure that they rapidly lost consciousness. Subsequently, euthanasia is carried out by intravenous injection of an overdose of sodium pentobarbital (100 mg/kg), a drug that rapidly causes cardiac and respiratory arrest and ultimately loss of brain function. This method is considered a humane and effective euthanasia because it ensures that the animal dies painlessly and unconsciously. During euthanasia, the animal is placed in a quiet, darkened environment to minimise external stimuli. Euthanasia is performed by professionally trained technicians to ensure the accuracy and humanity of the operation. The A375 control and Si-AGPAT2 cell lines were cultured to the logarithmic growth phase, rinsed twice with PBS, and cells were collected. The cell concentration was adjusted to 1 × 107 cells/ml. 100 µl of cell suspension was injected subcutaneously into the right abdomen of each 6-week-old female nude mouse (BALB/c-nu), three per group. The length and width of the tumours were measured with calipers every three days and the body weights were recorded. Tumour volume was calculated as Volume (mm³) = 0.5 × length (mm) × width (mm)². On the 21st day after injection, mice were executed, and tumors were excised and weighed. Immunofluorescence (IF) staining analysis for HMB45 (affinity, Jiangsu, China), and Ki-67 (affinity, Jiangsu, China) was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Images were acquired and positive expression levels of the above markers were measured using ImageJ software.

## Statistical analysis
Our statistical analyses were conducted using R software, version 4.2.1. To evaluate the prognostic significance and compare survival curves among different patient subgroups within our datasets, we employed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis coupled with the log-rank test. When assessing the association between continuous variables across two distinct groups, we relied on the nonparametric Wilcoxon rank sum test and applied the Bonferroni correction to control the false-positive rate. Additionally, we examined the strength and direction of relationships between variables through Spearman’s correlation analysis. In our analyses, a threshold of P < 0.05 was set as the benchmark for statistical significance.

## Results

## Multi-omics to identify SKCM prognostically relevant molecular subtypes
In this study, TCGA-SKCM was used for molecular subtyping by multi-omics, and the GSE19234, GSE59455, and GSE65904 cohorts were used as validation cohorts for subtyping. After effective preprocessing for all data quality, the principal component analysis showed the three GEO cohorts after the removal of batch effects (Fig.  A). We then independently identified three molecular subtypes of melanoma patients, cancer subtypes (CSs), from 10 multi-omics integrated clustering algorithms, and determined the classification of the subtypes by a combination of cluster prediction indices, gap statistical analysis, and silhouette score characteristics (Fig.  B, C ). Subsequently, we further integrated the clustering results with the molecular expression patterns of transcriptomics, epigenetics, and somatic mutations to explore the clustering process and molecular characteristics of the three subtypes (Fig.  D-F). Kaplan-Meier curves showed that subtype 2 (CS2) exhibited the worst overall survival (OS) among all the subtypes evaluated, while CS1 showed the best prognostic characteristics (Fig.  G).

 

## Classification of molecular subtypes of the integrative consensus and characterization of subtype immunity
Continuing our exploration, we delved into the molecular characteristics and biological functions that distinguish these melanoma subtypes. Utilizing the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm, we quantified the enrichment of various molecular features across the samples. Notably, we observed significant enrichment of pathways such as epithelial-mesenchymal transition and hypoxia in the CS2 subtype, which is associated with the worst prognosis. This finding suggests that patients within the CS2 group might be less responsive to immunotherapies (Fig.  A). To probe into the transcriptional variances, we scrutinized the potential regulatory elements and transcription factors implicated in cancer chromatin remodeling. We discovered that regulators like EGFR and TP63 were markedly activated in the CS2 subtype, whereas KAT5 and SIRT2 were specifically enriched in the CS3 subtype. These regulators are pivotal in the precise modulation of gene expression, influencing cell fate and presenting themselves as potential targets for cancer therapeutics. Our findings underscore the dynamic interplay between gene regulation and epigenetic alterations in the progression of SKCM (Fig.  B). Acknowledging the pivotal role of tumor immunity in tumorigenesis and disease progression, we employed the CIBERSORT algorithm to quantify the infiltration levels of immune cells across different molecular subtypes. We also examined the expression of co-suppressive immune checkpoints and evaluated the immunity score, stroma score, and methylation score of the samples. Our results indicated a significant upregulation of immune checkpoint expression in the CS1 subtype, hinting at the activation of pathways involved in immune regulation within this group (Fig.  C). Building on the genes differentially expressed between subtypes, we identified and selected genes that were uniquely upregulated in each subtype to serve as classifiers. These were then validated in an external GEO cohort, reinforcing the robustness of our subtype classification (Fig.  D). Furthermore, we assessed the concordance between the subtypes identified, the NTP algorithm, and the PAM algorithm, providing a comprehensive view of the subtypes’ consistency and reliability (Fig.  E-G).

 

## Construction of machine learning-driven signature (MLDS)
Using the “MOVICS” toolkit, we identified a total of 300 mRNAs that were specifically upregulated in three cancer subtypes (CSs). These mRNAs are closely related to the molecular classification of melanoma patients and were used for further analysis in our study (Supplementary Table). The TCGA-SKCM cohort, comprising 469 subjects, was designated as our training dataset. Subsequently, the GSE19234, GSE59455, and GSE65904 datasets were utilized as our validation cohorts for the machine learning algorithms. In the initial phase, we employed univariate Cox regression to select 33 prognostic genes from both the TCGA and GEO datasets, with a stringent P-value threshold of 0.05. These genes were then integrated as the foundation for our machine learning-driven approach, culminating in the development of the machine learning-driven signature (MLDS). A meticulous 10-fold cross-validation process was conducted on 101 machine learning-integrated prognostic models within the training dataset, with each model’s C-index determined across all datasets. It is imperative to emphasize that a model’s efficacy is evaluated based on its consistent performance across both the training and validation datasets. Based on the highest C-index from the TCGA training set, we selected the optimal model, a blend of Random Survival Forest (RSF) and Partial Least Squares Regression with Cox Model (plsRcox). With the highest average C-index (0.66) across the three validation sets, it was chosen for its superior predictive accuracy (Fig.  A). This model was selected for its superior predictive accuracy. Ultimately, a subset of 24 genes pivotal to molecular classification was identified to construct the MLDS. We then calculated MLDS scores for each participant across all cohorts. It was observed that individuals with higher MLDS scores exhibited a trend towards poorer overall survival (OS) in the TCGA-SKCM, GSE19234, GSE59455, and GSE65904 cohorts (Fig.  B-E). Notably, the area under the ROC curve (AUC) substantiated the high efficacy of our MLDS in predicting OS for melanoma patients within the TCGA and GEO cohorts (Fig.  F-I), underscoring its potential clinical utility in prognostic assessment.

 

## Nomogram construction and validation of clinical traits
In the TCGA cohort, based on the results of univariate Cox and multivariate Cox analyses, MLDS could be an independent prognostic indicator for patients compared to the clinical indicators of age, grade, and stage (Fig.  A, B). To extend the clinical usability of the constructed machine learning-driven signature to help clinicians make better clinical decisions, based on the correlation between the above clinicopathologic features and risk scores, we created a nomogram for predicting the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of SKCM patients (Fig.  C). From the model results, age, stage, and MLDS had a greater impact on predicting OS, indicating that a signature based on 24 genes related to typing could better predict the prognosis of SKCM patients. The calibration curve can also show that the nomogram can make an accurate prediction (Fig.  D). In addition to this, the area under the curve of the 3-year nomogram score is much higher than that of other clinicopathologic signatures (Fig.  E). The 3-year DCA curve (Fig.  F) and the C-index values (Fig.  G) both indicate that our constructed nomograms have the highest net benefit and that the impact of the machine-learning-driven signatures is more influential than the traditional models in clinical decision-making. We show the associations of different MLDS subgroups and clinical features, as well as the expression of all modeled genes, while MLDS is mainly associated with the age of the patients (Fig.  H, I).

 

## MLDS is closely related to immune function
In our quest to understand the immune landscape as indicated by the MLDS score, we delved into the correlation between the MLDS score and the presence of immune cells and immune checkpoint expression. Utilizing seven distinct methodologies, we computed the immune infiltration scores within the TCGA dataset (Fig.  A). To deepen our comprehension of the link between MLDS scores and immune functions, we employed the ssGSEA technique to quantify the various subpopulations of immune cells. The ‘estimate’ tool was leveraged to determine the proportions of stromal and immune cells across risk groups, thereby providing an estimation of tumor purity. Given the profound implications of the dysregulated expression and functionality of immune checkpoint molecules (ICs) on the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy, we scrutinized the expression profiles of these checkpoints across different MLDS subgroups. Our findings indicated that the subgroups with lower MLDS scores exhibited elevated immune cell infiltration scores, and also displayed heightened expression of ICs (Fig.  B). Figure  C illustrates the disparities in tumor mutational burden (TMB) and the frequency of mutant genes between the groups with high and low MLDS scores. Further correlation analysis revealed that the majority of the genes integral to our model were positively correlated with the genes encoding immune checkpoints. In contrast, the MLDS scores exhibited a negative correlation with the expression levels of these immune checkpoint molecules (Fig.  D). This suggests a complex interplay between the MLDS score, immune cell infiltration, and the expression of immune checkpoints, which could have a significant bearing on the responsiveness of melanoma patients to immunotherapeutic interventions.

 

## MLDS links to clinical care and cancer pathways
Immune checkpoint inhibitors are therapeutic agents that bolster the immune system’s capacity to identify and combat cancer cells, demonstrating remarkable potential in treating certain malignancies. To further elucidate the influence of the MLDS on immunotherapy efficacy and ICI responsiveness, we conducted a detailed analysis of the relationship between MLDS scores and characteristics indicative of ICI response. Our findings revealed a significant positive correlation between the MLDS score and the proteasome, while a significant negative correlation was observed with other immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) response features. Moreover, the MLDS score exhibited a substantial negative correlation with pivotal phases of the tumor immune cycle, including the presentation of cancer antigens (step 2), initiation and activation (step 3), T-cell recruitment (step 4), and the destruction of cancer cells (step 7) (Fig.  A). Individuals who would benefit from immunotherapy can be identified using the Immune Profile Score (IPS). Violin plots show the correlation between IPS values and MLDS subgroups, with higher IPS values indicating enhanced responses to PD-1 and CTLA-4 inhibitors. Whether PD-1 or CTLA-4 inhibitors were used alone or in combination with these two immunotherapies, the low-scoring subgroups showed a better response to immunotherapy (Fig.  B). The IC50 value, representing the drug concentration that inhibits 50% of cell growth, is a critical parameter in pharmacology. We discovered a significant positive correlation between the IC50 values of cisplatin and temozolomide with MLDS scores, implying that patients with lower MLDS scores might exhibit greater sensitivity to these chemotherapeutic agents (Fig.  C). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) indicated an enrichment of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway in the group with low MLDS scores (Fig.  D). We further investigated the prognostic significance of MLDS model genes across various cancer types using univariate Cox analysis. Of particular interest, AGPAT2 was the only risk gene identified for melanoma, and thus we selected AGPAT2 for subsequent experimental analysis (Fig.  E). Lastly, we examined the connection between MLDS model genes and the HALLMARK pathways. Oxidative phosphorylation, integral to tumorigenesis, progression, metastasis, and immune evasion, showed a positive association with AGPAT2 in many tumor types, with a particularly notable link in melanoma (Fig.  F). These findings underscore the multifaceted role of MLDS in the complex interplay between melanoma biology, treatment response, and immune interactions.

 

## AGPAT2 expression and signaling communication in single-cell sequencing
In the initial phase, we conducted a dimensionality reduction and clustering analysis on the single-cell sequencing data from three limbal melanoma cases, sourced from the GSE215120 dataset. This process involved categorizing the clusters based on the distinctive markers they expressed, which facilitated the identification of various cell types, including melanoma cells, T/NK cells, proliferating cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, myeloid cells, and smooth muscle cells, as depicted in Fig.  A and B. Notably, the expression of AGPAT2 was predominantly observed in melanoma and T/NK cells (Fig.  C). Further analysis segregated melanoma cells into two groups: those expressing AGPAT2 (AGPAT2+) and those not expressing it (AGPAT2-), as shown in Fig.  D. Utilizing the “monocle” R package, we delineated the developmental trajectories and pseudotime distributions of epithelial cells within the tumor tissues, revealing five distinct cell states during melanoma progression. Intriguingly, AGPAT2 + melanoma cells were predominantly found in the intermediate stages of cell development (Fig.  E). The “cellcall” R package, a powerful tool for deducing intercellular communication networks and internal regulatory signals by amalgamating both intracellular and extracellular signals, was employed in our study. The circular graph in Fig.  F illustrates the intensity of ligand-receptor signaling among different cell types, with AGPAT2 + epithelial cells demonstrating enhanced communication with fibroblasts. Additionally, our analysis of signaling pathway activities revealed a robust MAPK signaling pathway interaction specifically between AGPAT2 + epithelial cells and fibroblasts (Fig.  G).

 

## The expression and function of AGPAT2 was verified by cellular experiments
We evaluated the role of AGPAT2 in human melanoma by A375, and A875 cell lines. Knockdown of AGPAT2 inhibited the clone-forming ability of melanoma cells (Fig.  A-C). CCK-8 assay experiments showed that the proliferative ability of both A375 and A875 cell lines was significantly decreased after the knockdown of AGPAT2 (Fig.  D, E). Transwell assay showed that compared with the control group, the knockdown of AGPAT2 significantly reduced the A375 and A875 cell lines in the number of migrating and invading cancer cells (Fig.  F, G, H). In wound healing assays, AGPAT2 knockdown significantly reduced the wound healing rate in A375 and A875 cells (Fig.  I, J, K). Overall the proliferation, migration, and invasion ability of melanoma cell lines were significantly inhibited after AGPAT2 knockdown.

 

## Silencing AGPAT2 inhibits subcutaneous melanoma growth in vivo
We subcutaneously injected the A375 human melanoma cell line transfected with either the control group or the Si-AGPAT2 group into nude mice and dynamically observed the body weight and tumor growth of the mice. The results showed that the tumor weight and volume of mice in the Si-AGPAT2 group were significantly reduced compared with the control group, while there was no significant difference in body weight between the two groups (Fig.  A-D). Immunofluorescence analysis of mouse tissues showed that the expression of Ki-67-positive cells in the tumor tissues of mice in the Si-AGPAT2 group was significantly reduced, suggesting that Si-AGPAT2 treatment was effective in inhibiting tumor proliferation (Fig.  E, F).

 

## Discussion
The regulation of gene activity is a complex process involving numerous genetic and epigenetic mechanisms such as DNA methylation, gene mutations, and chemical modifications of histones. In-depth analysis of multi-omics data from patients enables us to understand more comprehensively the regulatory networks of specific diseases. However, most current studies focus on a single histological level, and the choice of histological clustering methods is often influenced by researcher preference, as these methods are widely used, their respective limitations are magnified [ , ]. In this study, a comprehensive analysis revealed that melanoma patients can be classified into three molecular subtypes with significant prognostic differences. In particular, the second subtype (CS2) showed the poorest overall survival, a phenomenon that may be related to the significant activation of its internal epithelial-mesenchymal transition and hypoxia signaling pathways, which play a key role in the aggressive growth of melanomas and resistance to treatment. In addition, the low expression of immune checkpoint molecules in CS2 may be a potential reason for the poor response to immunotherapy in this subtype of patients.
Machine learning algorithms, particularly those employed in the creation of predictive models, are renowned for their ability to handle vast and intricate datasets. One of their key strengths lies in their capacity to identify non-linear connections and intricate data patterns, which might be elusive to conventional statistical techniques. This capability allows them to uncover subtle insights and relationships that could be overlooked by more traditional approaches. As a result, they are increasingly being utilized in various fields, including healthcare, finance, and technology, to enhance decision-making and forecast outcomes more accurately [ ]. Through machine learning, we can extract deep features from multi-omics data that may be closely related to melanoma prognosis [ ]. Feature selection is a crucial step when constructing prognostic models. Correct feature selection not only improves the predictive accuracy of the model but also reduces the complexity of the model and avoids overfitting. To ensure the generalization ability of the model, we adopt a cross-validation approach to evaluate the performance of the model. This approach reduces the model’s dependence on a specific dataset and improves the applicability of the model across different patient populations [ ]. In this study, we carefully selected features associated with melanoma prognosis through univariate COX regression analysis and integration of multi-omics data, which were subsequently used to train machine learning models. Our study shows that MLDS exhibits high C-index values in both the training and validation cohorts, which indicates its high accuracy in predicting the prognosis of melanoma patients. C-index value is an important measure of the discriminative power of a predictive model, and the closer the value is to 1, the better the predictive power of the model. Therefore, the high C-index value of the MLDS provides strong evidence for its use as a prognostic prediction tool. The MLDS not only performs well as a stand-alone prognostic indicator but also provides a more accurate prognostic prediction when used in conjunction with traditional clinical characteristics, such as a patient’s age and clinical stage. This combined approach compensates for the possible limitations of a single indicator and provides more comprehensive information for clinical decision-making through a multifactorial approach.
The correlation between MLDS scores and the expression levels of immune checkpoint molecules further emphasizes the importance of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in melanoma prognosis. Immune checkpoints, such as PD-1/PD-L1, are key molecules that regulate the immune response. Their expression levels can influence the activity of T cells and the immune escape capabilities of tumors. The degree of immune cell infiltration can reflect the immunogenicity of the tumor and its potential responsiveness to immunotherapy. High immune cell infiltration is usually associated with a better prognosis, as it may imply that the immune system is actively combating the tumor [ ]. In the tumor microenvironment of melanoma and other cancers, infiltration of immune cells such as T cells, B cells, and natural killer (NK) cells is strongly associated with patient prognosis. The immune infiltration observed in the low MLDS subgroup is therefore likely to represent an immunosuppressive microenvironment [ , ]. Thus, the close relationship between MLDS and immune function provides a new perspective on immunotherapy for melanoma. In particular, we found a significant correlation between MLDS scores and the response profiles to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Patients with low MLDS scores are likely to exhibit greater immune cell activation and expression of immune checkpoint molecules, suggesting a better response to immunotherapy. Furthermore, TCIA analysis revealed that patients with low MLDS scores are more likely to benefit from combined immunotherapy blocking PD-1 and CTLA-4. These findings underscore the importance of considering tumor molecular subtypes and associated immune characteristics when developing personalized immunotherapy regimens. In this study, the analysis of chemotherapy revealed a link between melanoma molecular subtypes and sensitivity to chemotherapeutic drugs. Specifically, we observed a positive correlation between MLDS scores and the IC50 values of certain chemotherapeutic agents, indicating that melanoma patients with low MLDS scores may exhibit higher sensitivity to chemotherapeutic drugs such as cisplatin and temozolomide. Additionally, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) found enrichment of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway in patients with low MLDS scores, which may be related to the resistance of tumor cells to chemotherapeutic agents [ ]. These results suggest that the MLDS score is not only useful for predicting the prognosis of melanoma patients but may also serve as a biomarker for evaluating patients’ responsiveness to specific chemotherapeutic agents, providing valuable information for the development of personalized chemotherapy regimens.
Our constructed MLDS and further bioinformatics research preliminarily demonstrate the critical role of AGPAT2 in the molecular typing and prognostic progression of melanoma, and single-cell sequencing analysis reveals the unique biological role played by AGPAT2-expressing melanoma cells in the TME. AGPAT2 is involved in the synthesis of phosphatidylinositol (PA) and triacylglycerol (TAG), which are important molecules that make up cell membranes and energy storage [ ]. Cancer cells avoid lipotoxicity triggered by excess free fatty acids and provide cells with energy reserves by increasing the uptake and synthesis of fatty acids and converting them to triglycerides for storage in lipid droplets. Enhanced enzymatic activity of the TAG synthesis and lipid droplet formation pathways in tumor cells is a metabolic strategy to adapt to their rapid proliferative demands [ ]. Numerous investigations have documented higher levels of AGPAT2 in cancerous cells and human malignancies, correlating with heightened proliferation and movement of these cells, as well as augmented tumor expansion and aggressiveness, culminating in unfavorable outcomes. Furthermore, the suppression of AGPAT2 has been observed to curtail the growth of cancer cells and improve their response to chemotherapy treatments [ – ]. A recent examination has unveiled that AGPAT2 is selectively and precisely activated by HIF-1 in cancerous cells, bolstering their survival and resistance to chemotherapy under low-oxygen environments [ ]. The profound impact of AGPAT2 expression on cancer cell proliferation suggests its potential as a therapeutic target, leading to the investigation and development of AGPAT2 inhibitors. Preliminary findings from numerous studies indicate that small-molecule AGPAT2 inhibitors can induce apoptosis and successfully reduce cancer cell proliferation, tumor xenograft growth, and resistance to conventional chemotherapy [ , ]. However, so far the mechanistic studies related to AGAPT2 and melanoma remain a gap, and understanding the expression pattern of AGPAT2 in different cell types helps us to identify the metabolic properties and potential therapeutic targets of melanoma cells.
Our preliminary research indicates that signal communication between AGPAT2 + tumor cells and other cell types may impact the microenvironment and cell-to-cell interactions in melanoma. The MAPK signaling pathway is a cascade of protein kinases that transmit signals within the cell, regulating various biological processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, survival, and death. Our observations suggest that the expression of AGPAT2 may affect myeloid cells and fibroblasts in the tumor microenvironment, and the interactions of these cells with tumor cells may influence the activity of tumor cells through the MAPK signaling pathway [ ]. In addition, AGPAT2 is a key oncogene in MLDS, positively correlated with the oxidative phosphorylation pathway, especially in melanoma. Given the metabolic plasticity of melanoma cells, simultaneously blocking oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis may be an effective therapeutic approach. For example, using mTORC1 inhibitors to block oxidative phosphorylation and MAPK pathway inhibitors to block glycolysis can restore the sensitivity of melanoma resistant to BRAF and other MAPK pathway component inhibitors [ ].
Finally, through a series of in vivo and ex vivo experiments, we verified the expression and function of AGPAT2 in melanoma. Knocking down AGPAT2 inhibited the clonogenic ability of melanoma cells, significantly reduced the proliferative capacity of the A375 and A875 cell lines, and decreased the number of migrating and invasive cancer cells in these cell lines. In vivo, silencing AGPAT2 suppressed the growth of subcutaneous melanoma in nude mice, and the tumor weight and volume were significantly reduced in the AGPAT2-silenced group compared to the control group, while there was no significant difference in body weight between the two groups of mice. These findings suggest that AGPAT2 may play a significant promotional role in the development of melanoma and could be a potential therapeutic target. By inhibiting the activity of AGPAT2, the proliferation, migration, and invasion of melanoma cells can be reduced, which may improve the therapeutic outcomes for patients with melanoma.
In this study, we utilized a variety of machine learning algorithms, including Random Survival Forest (RSF) and Partial Least Squares Regression of Cox models (plsRcox), to determine the optimal model combination through 10-fold cross-validation. Each algorithm has its strengths and limitations; thus, selecting the appropriate algorithm and fine-tuning it is crucial for enhancing model performance. Although the MLDS demonstrated good predictive performance in this research, its applicability and stability across a broader patient population require further validation. Future studies should consider validating the predictive efficacy of MLDS in patient populations of diverse ethnicities and geographic regions, and continuously refine the model based on new data to improve its generalizability and the scope of its clinical applications [ , ]. Given the diversity and complexity of biomarkers, future research might explore the integration of MLDS with other biomarkers, such as protein and metabolite markers, to further enhance the precision and reliability of prognostic predictions. This multi-biomarker strategy could potentially reveal more profound insights into disease progression and treatment responses. It should be noted that our focus is on the continuous prediction of survival probability or risk, rather than simple binary classification, and thus its applicability in the clinical setting is limited or even non-existent. In addition, we plan to conduct analyses of positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) at specific thresholds in our future work to further explore the clinical utility of our model.
