## Abstract

## Aims
Vascular calcification is strongly linked to the development of major adverse cardiovascular events, but effective treatments are lacking. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are an emerging category of oral hypoglycemic drugs that have displayed marked effects on metabolic and cardiovascular diseases, including recently reported vascular medial calcification. However, the roles and underlying mechanisms of SGLT2 inhibitors in vascular calcification have not been fully elucidated. Thus, we aimed to further determine whether SGLT2 inhibitors protect against vascular calcification and to investigate the mechanisms involved.

## Methods and results
A computed tomography angiography investigation of coronary arteries from 1554 patients with type 2 diabetes revealed that SGLT2 inhibitor use was correlated with a lower Agatston calcification score. In the vitamin D3 overdose, 5/6 nephrectomy chronic kidney disease-induced medial calcification and Western diet-induced atherosclerotic intimal calcification models, dapagliflozin (DAPA) substantially alleviated vascular calcification in the aorta. Furthermore, we showed that DAPA reduced vascular calcification via Runx2-dependent osteogenic transdifferentiation in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). Transcriptome profiling revealed that thioredoxin domain containing 5 (TXNDC5) was involved in the attenuation of vascular calcification by DAPA. Rescue experiments showed that DAPA-induced TXNDC5 downregulation in VSMCs blocked the protective effect on vascular calcification. Furthermore, TXNDC5 downregulation disrupted protein folding-dependent Runx2 stability and promoted subsequent proteasomal degradation. Moreover, DAPA downregulated TXNDC5 expression via amelioration of oxidative stress and ATF6-dependent endoplasmic reticulum stress. Consistently, the class effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification were validated with empagliflozin in intimal and medial calcification models.

## Conclusions
SGLT2 inhibitors ameliorate vascular calcification through blocking endoplasmic reticulum stress-dependent TXNDC5 upregulation and promoting subsequent Runx2 proteasomal degradation, suggesting that SGLT2 inhibitors are potentially beneficial for vascular calcification treatment and prevention.

Keywords: Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, Vascular calcification, Thioredoxin domain containing 5, Runx2, Endoplasmic reticulum stress

## Introduction
Vascular calcification portends a poor clinical outcome and is considered a distinct predictor of major adverse cardiovascular events, particularly in individuals with diabetes, chronic renal failure, aging and atherosclerosis [ ]. Although considerable progress in research on vascular calcification has been achieved, the complex nature of these processes has impeded the translation of the obtained results into effective vascular calcification treatments. Thus, studies aiming to further explore the potential mechanisms of vascular calcification and identify novel therapies are very important.
Vascular calcification is similar to the process of physiological bone formation, and is characterized by the transdifferentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) into an osteoblast-like phenotype [ , ]. VSMCs exposed to a pro-calcification environment lose the expression of contractile markers such as smooth muscle protein 22α (SM22α) and alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and exhibit increased expression of osteogenic markers, including osteocalcin, osteopontin and alkaline phosphatase [ ]. Runt-related transcription factor-2 (Runx2) plays a crucial role in this regulation [ ].
Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors such as dapagliflozin (DAPA), empagliflozin (EMPA) and canagliflozin consistently decrease the risk of all-cause mortality and rehospitalization in heart failure patients, regardless of the diabetes status [ , , ]. However, the cardioprotective effects of SGLT2 inhibitors involve noncanonical activities. It is intriguing that whether SGLT2 inhibitors can influence vascular calcification. Very recently, canagliflozin and EMPA have been reported to ameliorate medial vascular calcification through the inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome [ , ]. Despite these innovative work, whether DAPA or the SGLT2 inhibitor class effects exist in either intimal or medial calcification and which molecular mechanisms underlie these effects remain unclear.
Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress occurs when the import of unfolded proteins into the ER exceeds the ability of ER to fold them, triggering the unfolded protein response (UPR). In response to osteogenic stimuli, calcium phosphate crystal accumulation induces ER stress. Reciprocally, ER stress enhances VSMC osteogenic differentiation and promotes the development of vascular calcification. Moreover, ER stress is reportedly related to the effectiveness of SGLT2 inhibitors in endothelial cells [ ]. Nevertheless, researchers have not clearly determined whether SGLT2 inhibitors impact ER stress during VSMC osteogenic transdifferentiation.
Thioredoxin domain-containing 5 (TXNDC5), an ER protein, is involved in the ER stress response signaling. TXNDC5 catalyzes and rearranges disulfide bonds in the ER via its thioredoxin domains [ ]. TXNDC5 functions by facilitating correct protein folding to maintain protein stability and avoid UPR-induced degradation, ultimately promoting protein production. As a result, abnormal TXNDC5 expression can lead to a variety of illnesses, including organ fibrosis and atherosclerosis [ , , , ]. However, the role of TXNDC5 in vascular calcification remains unclear.
With these gaps in our understanding of the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on calcification in mind, we conducted the present study. We confirmed that SGLT2 inhibitors significantly suppressed vascular calcification in vitro and in vivo. Our mechanistic studies showed that SGLT2 inhibitors disrupted TXNDC5-dependent Ruxn2 folding and accelerated Ruxn2 degradation. Thus, our study revealed a novel antivascular calcification effect of SGLT2 inhibitors and showed that it occurs via the attenuation of ATF6-TXNDC5-Runx2-dependent osteogenic reprogramming.

## Methods
A thorough Materials and Methods section is available in the Supplementary Material Online, Materials.
2.1.

## Study population
Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus who underwent coronary computed tomography angiography (CTA) at Xinqiao Hospital of Army Medical University between Jan. 2018 and Oct. 2022 were enrolled retrospectively. The Agatston score of the coronary arteries was estimated after coronary CTA, as indicated by clinicians. The patients were separated into two groups according to whether they were using SGLT2 inhibitors. The baseline characteristics are shown in . The study involving human participants followed the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the ethical committee of the Xinqiao Hospital, Army Medical University (Third Military Medical University, No#2023-009-01). The ethical committee waived the need for written consents because of the retrospective design of this study.

2.2.

## Vascular calcification animal models
A cholecalciferol (vitamin D3) overload-induced calcification model was generated in 8-week-old C57BL/6J male mice. The mice were continuously injected subcutaneously with 600,000 IU/kg body weight of vitamin D3 or saline for 3 days and then sampled after another 3 days [ , ]. DAPA was administered by oral gavage at 1.5 mg/kg/day (equal to 10 mg/day for humans).
A two-step procedure was performed in the 5/6 nephrectomy chronic kidney disease (CKD) model, Briefly, two thirds of the right renal cortex was electrocoagulated, and the left total nephrectomy was performed two weeks later [ ]. A similar procedure was performed on the sham-operated control mice except that no kidney tissue was removed. After one week of recovery, the sham-operated mice were placed on a standard laboratory diet supplemented with normal phosphate (0.5 %), whereas the CKD mice were fed with a high-phosphate (1.5 %) diet supplemented with DAPA (1.5 mg/day/kg, equivalent to 10 mg/day for humans) or empagliflozin (3 mg/day/kg, equal 20 mg/day for humans) for twelve weeks.
In the atherosclerotic calcification model, ApoE−/− mice were fed with a Western diet (Cat# XT108C, Xietong, Jiangsu, China) containing dapagliflozin (1.5 mg/day/kg) or empagliflozin (3 mg/day/kg) as reported for 24 weeks [ ]. Mice were euthanized by isoflurane inhalation (1.5 %, Cat# 792,632, Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) and subsequent cervical dislocation.
The Institutional Animal Care Committee of Xinqiao Hospital, Army Medical University, approved all animal experiments (No. AMUWEC20211565), which were performed in accordance with the requirements of Directive 2010/63/EU.

2.3.

## RNA-sequencing analysis
Total RNA was extracted from three biological replicates of rat VSMCs treated as indicated. RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis was performed at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Differential gene expression among the samples was established using DESeq2 (P adjust< 0.05, |fold change| > 1.5). Functional enrichment of GO (Gene Ontology) terms and KEEG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathways was then performed.

2.4.

## Silencing TXNDC5 in vivo with an adeno-associated virus (AAV)
Adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV) for smooth muscle cell-specific transfection with a mouse TXNDC5 siRNA (AAV-siTXNDC5) or negative control (AAV-siNC) was generated by Genechem Biotech (Shanghai, China). Briefly, a mouse TXNDC5 siRNA or negative control was cloned and inserted into a double-stranded AAV9 backbone carrying a constitutive-SM22α promoter and GFP construct (Genechem Biotech, Shanghai, China). AAV9 stains were produced by cotransfecting the three plasmids into 293T cells and were subsequently purified via column purification. AAV-siTXNDC5 titers (5.2 × 1012 v. g./mL) or negative control virus was injected into the tail veins of 6-week-old C57BL/6J male mice three weeks before subcutaneous vitamin D3 overloading as described above.

2.5.

## Statistical analysis
Propensity-score matching was performed using a previously described method [ ]. In brief, a propensity score was calculated for each patient through multivariable Logistic regression analysis. Age, gender, and coronary artery disease were variables included. The SGLT2 inhibitor usage group was matched to the non-SGLT2 inhibitor usage group in a 1:2 ratio using the Nearest Neighbor Matching algorithm and a caliper of 0.05. A good matching between treatment groups was regarded if a cutoff value < 0.10.
Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism version 9.3.1 (GraphPad Software, MA, USA) and SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and PS Matching in SPSS (version 3.04, SourceForge, San Diego, USA). Data throughout the article are reported as the means ± SEMs or median with interquartiles. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to determine the normality of the data. Differences in means between two groups were evaluated using an unpaired 2-tailed Student's t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test as appropriate. More than two groups were compared using one-way ANOVA or the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test. P-values <0.05 values were considered statistically significant.

3.

## Results
3.1.

## SGLT2 inhibitor use is associated with decreased coronary artery calcification scores in diabetic patients
To investigate the association of SGLT2 inhibitor prescriptions with coronary artery calcification, the Agatston scores obtained via coronary computed tomography angiography were calculated for 1554 type 2 diabetic patients with or without SGLT2 inhibitor prescriptions in a retrospective cohort. The baseline characteristics of both groups are displayed in . We found that type 2 diabetic patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors had significantly lower overall Agatston scores than patients who did not use SGLT2 inhibitors ( A). For the major coronary branches, the Agatston scores were notably lower in the SGLT2 inhibitor use group than in the control group for the left main trunk, left anterior descending artery, left circumflex artery, and right coronary artery, respectively ( B–E). Furthermore, a subgroup analysis showed that DAPA use and EMPA use were correlated with decreased total calcification scores for the coronary arteries ( F). Moreover, after propensity score matching of baseline characteristics ( ), the total Agatston score in the SGLT2 inhibitor group remained significantly lower than that in the non-SGLT2 inhibitor usage group ( G). Representative computed tomography angiography images of coronary calcification are presented in H. These clinical data suggested a strong negative correlation between SGLT2 inhibitor use and vascular calcification.
 
3.2.

## Dapagliflozin attenuates the intimal and medial vascular calcification in vivo
It was of interest to investigate whether SGLT2 inhibitors might attenuate vascular calcification progression in vivo. The effect of SGLT2 inhibitors was investigated initially in an animal model of acute and severe calcification caused by vitamin D3 overdose injection ( A), with calcification largely occurring in the aortic tunica medium. We found that treatment of mice subjected to vitamin D3 overdose with DAPA attenuated aortic calcium deposition, as evidenced by Alizarin Red staining, von Kossa staining, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and calcium content assays ( B–D). Likewise, 5/6 nephrectomy combined with a high-phosphate diet was used to induce CKD and vascular calcification in mice. The CKD group fed a high-Pi diet had significantly higher levels of serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, and phosphate levels ( ). Aortas in the DAPA group exhibited greatly decreased calcium deposition, as shown in E–G. These findings suggested that DAPA reduces medial vascular calcification.
 Given the great potential of SGLT2 inhibitors, we investigated our research on whether SGLT2 inhibitors reduced intimal calcification by utilizing an atherosclerotic mouse model. ApoE−/− mice fed with a Western diet for 24 weeks were employed to assess the effects of DAPA on atherosclerotic calcification ( H). Consistent with previous reports [ , ], total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol levels were significantly lower in the dapagliflozin group than in the control group ( ). Likewise, Oil Red O staining of the entire aortic root showed that total atherosclerotic lesion areas were decreased in the DAPA group after 24 weeks of Western diet feeding ( I and J). DAPA also significantly decreased the calcium level and ALP activity in the aortas of ApoE−/− mice ( K-L). Furthermore, compared with control ApoE−/− mice, ApoE−/− mice treated with DAPA had reduced calcification formation of the aortic root, as revealed by Alizarin red and von Kossa staining ( M). Taken together, our findings indicate that DAPA prevents both intimal and medial vascular calcification.

3.3.

## Dapagliflozin attenuates vascular calcification in vitro
Because VSMCs are the major contributors to both medial and intimal calcification [ , , ], primary rat VSMCs were cultured in osteogenic medium (OM) containing 2.8 mmol/L phosphate (Pi) in vitro to further explore the effect of DAPA on vascular calcification. We first detected the expression of SGLT2 in VSMCs. The SLGT2 was expressed in the VSMCs and upregulated when treated with OM (Supplementary
 ). Interestingly, DAPA significantly attenuated VSMC calcification, as shown by Alizarin red staining and von Kossa staining ( A–C). Furthermore, we determined whether these effects of DAPA were dependent on the drug concentration. As shown in ( A–E), VSMC calcification was inversely associated with the concentration of DAPA.
 To explore the translational significance of these observations, we tested the effects of DAPA in human VSMCs treated with OM. Similar inhibitory effects of DAPA on calcification were observed in human VSMCs ( F–J). To confirm this finding, calcification of rat aortic rings was successfully induced in OM for 14 days, and this calcification was similarly alleviated by DAPA, as shown by von Kossa staining and Alizarin red staining ( K). Taken together, these data suggested an inhibitory effect of DAPA on vascular calcification.

3.4.

## Dapagliflozin modulates Runx2-dependent osteogenic reprogramming of VSMCs
Vascular calcification is characterized by VSMC transdifferentiation into an osteogenic phenotype and requires the import of phosphate by sodium-dependent phosphate cotransporters (PIT1 and PIT2), accompanied by the upregulation of Runx2 and osteogenic gene reprogramming. We first measured the expression of PIT1 and PIT2 and found that DAPA had no effect on their expression (Supplementary
 ). Next, we examined whether DAPA modulates VSMC osteogenic phenotype switching. Immunoblotting and qRT‒PCR analysis revealed that a high concentration of Pi upregulated the expression of Runx2, collagen I alpha 1 (COL1A1), osteocalcin (OCN) and osteopontin (OPN) in rat VSMCs, and these effects were reversed by DAPA in a dose-dependent manner ( A–C). In contrast, the expression of SM22α, a contractile marker, increased with DAPA supplementation ( A and B). Furthermore, Runx2, COL1A1, OCN and OPN expression levels were also upregulated in human VSMCs cultured in OM, while SM22α expression was downregulated ( D and E). Immunofluorescence staining analysis revealed that in human VSMCs, Runx2, COL1A1, and OCN were downregulated while SM22α was upregulated in the presence of DAPA ( F and G).
 To further confirm these findings in vivo, we administered DAPA in CKD and atherosclerotic calcification mouse models and found that vascular calcification was alleviated, as evidenced by the downregulation of Runx2, COL1A1, and OPN at the mRNA and protein levels ( H-L). These data indicate that DAPA inhibits vascular calcification via osteogenic reprogramming.

3.5.

## RNA-seq links TXNDC5 to vascular calcification
RNA-seq was performed on VSMCs treated with DAPA or EMPA to determine the impact of SGLT2 inhibitors at the molecular level. The transcriptomic profiles were clearly separated via unsupervised hierarchical clustering ( ) and principal component analyses ( ). The Venn diagram shows the 149 overlapping differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the groups subjected to pretreatment and posttreatment with OM and the unique mRNAs between the groups subjected to pretreatment and posttreatment with either DAPA or EMPA ( A and ). GO enrichment and KEGG pathway analysis of the 149 DEGs revealed that the SGLT2 inhibitor-regulated genes were enriched in protein folding, sorting and degradation, phosphate metabolic processes, and protein synthesis responses to stimuli ( B and ).
 Strikingly, TXNDC5, an ER stress marker, was among the upregulated genes induced by a high-Pi stimulus but was consistently downregulated by DAPA and EMPA, as shown by the hierarchical clustering of the top candidates among the 149 DEGs ( C). Furthermore, the differential expression of TXNDC5 detected using RNA-Seq was verified. The qRT‒PCR and immunoblotting showed that Pi successfully triggered the upregulation of TXNDC5, and DAPA treatment reversed this effect ( D–F). Immunofluorescence staining showed that the expression of TXNDC5 was also notably increased in VSMCs cultured in OM, and this increase was accompanied by the upregulation of Runx2 ( G).

3.6.

## TXNDC5 knockdown abrogates vascular calcification
Because the expression of TXNDC5 was upregulated in VSMCs cultured in OM, we hypothesized that the inhibition of TXNDC5 might have a beneficial impact on vascular calcification. VSMCs were cultivated in OM with or without TXNDC5 siRNA. TXNDC5 knockdown markedly reduced VSMC calcification as shown by Alizarin red staining, von Kossa staining ( A–C), calcium content assays and ALP activity measurements ( ). In addition, the expression of Runx2 was decreased at 10–14 days of culture in OM after treatment with TXNDC5-siRNA, as shown by qRT‒PCR and immunoblotting ( D–F). Immunofluorescence staining revealed that TXNDC5 knockdown inhibited Runx2 expression in VSMCs ( G).
 We next explored the influence of TXNDC5 knockdown on vascular calcification in a vitamin D3 overdose-induced calcification mouse model. AAV9 carrying the TXNDC5 or negative control siRNA was injected via the mouse tail vein. Three weeks later, the mice were given a three-day vitamin D3 overdose injection and the aortas were harvested one week later. TXNDC5 knockdown was successfully induced at three weeks after transfection, as validated by qPCR, immunofluorescence staining, and immunoblotting ( ). Strikingly, upon vitamin D3 induction, TXNDC5 knockdown markedly alleviated vascular calcification compared to the control groups, as indicated by Alizarin red and von Kossa staining ( H), total calcium content assays and ALP activity analysis ( I and J). In addition, TXNDC5 knockdown in mice with vitamin D3-induced calcification decreased the protein and mRNA levels of Runx2 and COL1A1, while SM22α was upregulated ( K-M). These results suggested that TXNDC5 deficiency alleviates vascular calcification.

3.7.

## Dapagliflozin suppresses vascular calcification via TXNDC5
Next, we explored whether DAPA inhibited vascular calcification via TXNDC5. TXNDC5 was overexpressed in human VSMCs via transfection with a lentivirus carrying TXNDC5, and TXNDC5 was successfully overexpressed in human VSMCs ( A–D). Compared to the control treatment, TXNDC5 overexpression significantly restored the high Pi-induced vascular calcification that was attenuated by DAPA, as shown in E–I. Immunofluorescence staining revealed that overexpression of TXNDC5 reversed the downregulation of Runx2 expression induced by DAPA ( J), with similar trends in the expression of Runx2 and COL1A1 detected by immunoblotting and qRT‒PCR ( K-M). Ex vivo, the overexpression of TXNDC5 in cultured rat aortic rings mitigated the reduction in osteogenic marker expression ( ). These observations demonstrated that TXNDC5 downregulation mediates the anticalcification effects of DAPA.
 
3.8.

## Dapagliflozin-mediated downregulation of TXNDC5 accelerates osteogenic protein degradation in VSMCs
Because TXNDC5 downregulation contributes to the reduction in vascular calcification mediated by DAPA, we next investigated how TXNDC5 mediated the anticalcification effects of DAPA. TXNDC5, an ER-resident protein, assists in folding proteins in the ER. TXNDC5 deficiency causes the degradation of misfolded proteins by the proteasome. We predicted that TXNDC5 promotes the folding of Runx2 and osteogenic proteins in response to high Pi treatment, resulting in vascular calcification. TXNDC5 depletion may result in osteogenic protein misfolding and subsequent proteasome-mediated degradation. Consistent with this hypothesis, when de novo synthesis of protein was inhibited by cycloheximide, TXNDC5 knockdown markedly increased the protein degradation of Runx2 and downstream COL1A1 and OPN proteins compared to the scrambled siRNA group ( A–D). In contrast, overexpression of TXNDC5 via a lentivirus stabilized the Runx2, COL1A1, and OPN proteins and delayed their degradation compared to those in empty vector-transduced VSMCs ( E–H). Moreover, Runx2 protein degradation induced by TXNDC5 knockdown in VSMCs was partially restored in the presence of MG132, a selective proteasome inhibitor ( I and J). Furthermore, coimmunoprecipitation experiments demonstrated that TXNDC5 interacts with Runx2 in VSMCs ( K). TXNDC5 knockdown increased Runx2 ubiquitination in VSMCs ( L). These findings show that TXNDC5 maintains the stability of the osteogenic proteins Runx2, COL1A1 and OPN.
 
3.9.

## Dapagliflozin blocks high Pi-induced TXNDC5 upregulation via an ATF6-dependent ER stress pathway
Previously, ER stress has been linked to vascular calcification. Because TXNDC5 was upregulated via the ER stress pathway, we hypothesized that SGLT2 inhibitors might block ER stress pathway-dependent TXNDC5 expression upon osteogenic stimulation in VSMCs. As expected, Pi treatment notably increased the activation of three ER stress signaling pathways, as indicated by the upregulation of BIP, ATF6, ATF4, and XBP1u ( A and B). DAPA significantly suppressed the increase in the protein levels of these ER stress markers induced by high Pi ( A and B), and this effect was accompanied by the downregulation of TXNDC5 in VSMCs. These findings suggest that DAPA blocked the ER stress pathway signaling induced by high Pi.
 Next, we determined whether the ER stress pathway regulates TXNDC5 transcription. The general ER stress inhibitor 4-phenylbutyrate (4-PBA, 2 mM) was employed to attenuate the ER stress induced by high Pi. 4-PBA markedly decreased the expression of genes related to ER stress signaling, including TXNDC5, BIP, XBP1u, ATF4 and ATF6 expression, and similarly downregulated the expression of the osteogenic differentiation markers Runx2 and COL1A1 ( C and D). Contrarily, the supplementation of thapsigargin, a well-recognized ER activator, diminished the inhibitory effects of DAPA on calcification in the OM ( E–G). Similarly, thapsigargin triggered the BIP/AFT6 signaling activation and blunted DAPA-mediated downregulation of Runx2 and CLO1A1 expression at the protein level ( H and I). Consistent with reports that ATF6 transcriptionally regulates TXNDC5 [ , ], we observed that ATF6 depletion via siRNA significantly suppressed TXNDC5 expression ( J and K). Taken together, these results indicate that SGLT2 inhibitors mitigate the high Pi-induced ER stress pathway and downregulate TXNDC5 expression via ATF6-mediated transcriptional regulation.

3.10.

## Oxidative stress alleviation mediates the blockade of high Pi-induced TXNDC5 upregulation and vascular calcification by dapagliflozin
Oxidative stress plays a pivotal role in vascular calcification. We further determined whether reactive oxygen species (ROS) participate the process of vascular calcification blunted by DAPA. We first observed that the total and mitochondrial ROS levels were elevated in response to OM treatment, as measured with DCFH-DA or MitoSox. DAPA suppressed the ROS burst induced by OM in VSMCs ( A–E). Furthermore, the expression of NADPH oxidase family was detected and we found that the ROS was generated from Nox4 rather than from Nox2 upon osteogenic stimulation ( F and G). Meanwhile, the abrogation of OM-induced ROS production with 2 mM N-acetyl cysteine (NAC) resulted in the suppression of VSMC osteogenic markers expression as detected with immunofluorescence staining and immunoblotting ( H–J). In contrast, supplementation with hydrogen peroxide aggravated the vascular calcification, as indicated by elevated levels of osteogenic markers ( K-L). Finally, the upregulation of TXNDC5 induced by high Pi was blocked when ROS were neutralized with NAC ( H–J). Supplementation with hydrogen peroxide compensated the DAPA-mediated reduction in TXNDC5 expression in VSMCs cultured with OM ( K-L). Conclusively, DAPA abrogates high Pi-induced TXNDC5 upregulation and vascular calcification through oxidative stress.
 
3.11.

## SGLT2 inhibitors exert class effects during vascular calcification
Clinical trials have shown that DAPA, EMPA, and canagliflozin (CANA) have internally and externally comparable class impacts on cardiorenal outcomes [ ], We hypothesized that EMPA may have a similar impact on vascular calcification as DAPA. In vitro, EMPA dramatically reduced VSMC calcification induced by OM ( A-C). It also decreased the expression of osteogenic markers and had the opposite effect on the contractile marker SM22α ( D-E). Strikingly, in both the atherosclerotic intimal calcification mouse model and the CKD induced medial calcification mouse model, EMPA treatment markedly reduced aortic calcification, calcium deposition and ALP activity in the aorta ( F–I). Taken together, our findings suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors have a protective effect on vascular calcification.
 

4.

## Discussion
Given the pleiotropic effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on cardiovascular diseases, the current study investigated the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification. We discovered that the use of an SGLT2 inhibitor is associated with less vascular calcification of the coronary arteries in diabetic patients, and that DAPA and EMPA significantly improve medial and intimal vascular calcification both in vivo and in vitro by reducing the progression of VSMC osteogenic reprogramming. The inhibition of calcification by DAPA and EMPA was shown to be Runx2 dependent. Mechanistically, the expression of TXNDC5 was found to be elevated under pro-calcification conditions, and the modulation of TXNDC5 expression significantly interfered with vascular calcification. Furthermore, DAPA was shown to protect against vascular calcification by inhibiting ATF6-dependent ER stress and TXNDC5 expression, which exacerbates Runx2 instability and proteasomal degradation, resulting in Runx2 downregulation and inhibition of vascular calcification.
This study added to our understanding of SGLT2 inhibitors’ effects on vascular calcification. Until recently SGLT2 inhibitors have not been reported to be associated with vascular calcification. Canagliflozin protects against vascular calcification in a vitamin D3 plus nicotine medial calcification model and in rats with CKD [ , ]. Autophagy and the NLRP3 inflammasome were suggested to mediate the observed anti-calcification effects [ , ]. In addition, EMPA modulates the redox state and NLRP3 inflammasome to attenuate chronic kidney disease and diabetic medial vascular calcification in mice [ , ]. These studies initially revealed the protective roles of SGLT2 inhibitors in vascular calcification, however, the class effects of SGLT2 inhibitors remain inconclusive. In the present study, we investigated the class effects of SGLT2 inhibitors with DAPA and EMPA and revealed that both SGLT2 inhibitors ameliorate intimal and medial vascular calcification.
Since vascular calcification is governed by the osteogenic transcription factor-mediated gene reprogramming, we further revealed that SGLT2 inhibitors modulate the expression of the key transcription factor Runx2 during VSMC osteogenic transdifferentiation and subsequent vascular calcification [ ]. DAPA or EMPA notably inhibited the expression of Runx2 and osteogenic markers and blunted VSMC osteogenic transdifferentiation. Consistently, Lu CW et al. later reported that EMPA inhibited Runx2 expression and suppressed Runx2 by activating the AMPK pathway [ ]. Canagliflozin was reported to attenuate Runx2 by regulating the redox state and autophagy regulation [ ]. Thus, we inferred that the class effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification might be related to the regulation of Runx2. The transcription of Runx2 was downregulated at the late stage of VSMC calcification treated with SGLT2 inhibitors. Furthermore, increasing evidence has shown that the regulation of Runx2 via posttranslational modifications plays a key role in osteoblast development [ ], thus modulating the Runx2 translocation to nucleus to activate gene transcription. Collectively, these data highlighted that SGLT2 inhibitors, at least partially, regulate the modulator Runx2 at the posttranslational level during vascular calcification.
As a method to elucidate the underlying mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitors in the context of vascular calcification, transcriptome profiling was performed in this study to detect ER stress signaling pathways, including the expression of TXNDC5, which is a factor involved in vascular calcification. Recent studies have suggested that ER stress contributes to the pathogenesis of vascular calcification. The ER stress inducers tunicamycin and thapsigargin aggravate vascular calcification. ER stress is also a key factor in vascular mineralization induced by stimuli, including saturated fatty acids, oxidized lipids, C5a and TNFα. Among the three ER stress, the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4, IRE 1α-XBP1s and BIP-ATF6 pathways are reportedly activated, thereby promoting VSMC osteogenic transdifferentiation [ , , ]. In line with previous reports, we observed that the levels of the ER stress markers were notably increased in VSMCs upon OM stimulation. Genetic or chemical targeting of ATF4 and CHOP attenuates VSMC mineralization and transdifferentiation in response to osteogenic inductive factors. In the IRE1α-XBP1 arm of the ER stress response, the deletion of XBP1 aggravates CKD-induced vascular calcification [ ]. As shown in our study, the ER stress inhibitor 4-PBA mitigated vascular calcification. Given the transcriptional regulation of TXNDC5, BIP-ATF6 signaling was investigated and we observed that SGLT2 inhibitors alleviated ER stress by downregulating ATF6 and BIP. Moreover, SGLT2 inhibitor treatment reduces ER stress in degenerative diseases and metabolic disorders such as diabetes [ ], and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease [ ]. Whether the PERK and IRE1α pathways contribute to the regulation of TXNDC5 and mediate the effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification remain to be determined in future studies.
Another important finding is that we established the key role of TXNDC5 in vascular calcification. The pivotal roles of TXNDC5 in multiple diseases have been uncovered. Specifically, endothelial TXNDC5, a potential mechanosensitive molecule, can be induced by disturbed flow, and in vivo targeting of endothelial TXNDC5 protects against atherosclerosis [ ]. However, the effects of TXNDC5 on vascular calcification have not been reported. To our knowledge, this study is the first to reveal the role of TXNDC5 during vascular calcification. We demonstrated that TXNDC5 is upregulated during vascular calcification both in vitro and in vivo. Deletion of TXNDC5 in vivo abrogates vascular mineralization in a vitamin D3 overdose animal model. Further elucidation of the role of TXNDC5 in vascular calcification by exploiting an animal model with selective TXNDC5 deletion in VSMCs might be valuable.
Furthermore, we delineated an unreported paradigm in which TXNDC5 mediates Runx2 deactivation in the presence of SGLT2 inhibitors. TXNDC5, which harbors a CGHC motif with PDI activity in the TRX domain, functionally facilitates proper protein folding and inhibits UPR-related apoptosis [ , ]. Accordingly, TXNDC5 upregulation facilitates the folding of ECM proteins, such as collagen and fibronectin, during cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Similarly, TXNDC5 directly binds to and stabilizes the TGFβR1 protein posttranslationally during fibrotic diseases [ , ]. Consistent with this observation, we observed that TXNDC5 stabilizes the Runx2 protein and decreases its ubiquitin‒proteasome-mediated degradation. Furthermore, a detailed investigation of the domain via which TXNDC5 interacts with Runx2 should be performed in future studies. Interestingly, TXNDC5 was reported to increase the ubiquitination and proteasome-mediated degradation of heat shock factor 1 during atherosclerosis [ ]. However, further research is needed to determine the precise mechanisms by which TXNDC5 regulates proteostasis through the ubiquitin-proteasome system during vascular calcification.
Clinical evidence has shown that SGLT2 inhibitors protect the heart and kidneys of patients with or without type 2 diabetes and preserve their function or delay loss of function [ ]. In cardiovascular settings, SGLT2 inhibitors markedly decrease the incidence of myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and are widely recommended by the European and American Diabetes and Cardiology Society guidelines [ , , ]. However, the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification have not been comprehensively investigated until now. Together with previous reports, the three representative SGLT2 inhibitors confirmed the existence of class effects on vascular calcification.
Several limitations should be taken into consideration when interpreting this study. First, the beneficial effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on vascular calcification are off-target effects, and the intrinsic targets of SGLT2 inhibitors in VSMCs remains unclear. New systematic approaches for discovering associations among SGLT2 inhibitors and proteins might be needed. Second, although we illustrated that the effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on ER stress signaling under OM stimulation was related to decreased oxidative stress, the in-depth mechanism by which SGLT2 inhibitors regulate oxidative stress and ER stress awaits further exploration. Third, the intimal calcification differs from medial calcification, especially the endothelial cells exert more profound role than VMSCs during intimal calcification. We focused more on the mechanisms in the VSMCs and future studies are warranted to depict the mechanism in endothelial cells. Finally, many clinical variables including the course of diabetes, blood sugar control, and the occurrence of complications are all related to vascular calcification. In this retrospective cohort, although propensity score matching and multiple regression were applied to minimize the bias, the clinical observation of the effects of SGLT2 inhibitor on vascular calcification remains exploratory. Evidence from well-designed prospective cohorts or randomized controlled trials is needed to translate these findings into clinical practice.
In conclusion, SGLT2 inhibitors ameliorate vascular calcification via ATF6-TXNDC5-Runx2-mediated VSMC osteogenic reprogramming. SGLT2 inhibitors have great potential as tools for the prevention and treatment of patients with vascular calcification.
