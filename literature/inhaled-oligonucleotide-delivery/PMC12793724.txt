## Highlights
•The expression of Oxysterol Binding Protein (OSBP)-related protein 5 (ORP5) was upregulated during the progression of pathological cardiac hypertrophy.
•Knockdown of ORP5 had no effect on basal cardiac function but prevented hypertrophic cardiac remodeling.
•ORP5 exerts a pro-hypertrophic action by transporting mTORC1 to the lysosomal membrane for activation, thereby triggering the downstream protein synthesis pathway.
•ORP5 has the potential to serve as a diagnostic or therapeutic target for pathological cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure.

## Abstract

## Introduction
Oxysterol binding protein (OSBP)-related protein 5 (ORP5) mainly functions as a lipid transfer protein at membrane contact sites (MCS). ORP5 facilitates cell proliferation through the activation of mTORC1 signaling. While the pro-hypertrophic effects of mTORC1 are well-documented, the specific role of ORP5 in the context of pathological cardiac hypertrophy remains inadequately understood.

## Methods
To investigate the role of ORP5 in pathological cardiac hypertrophy, AAV9-treated mice and neonatal rat ventricular myocytes (NRVMs) were utilized. Cardiac function, morphology, and mTORC1 signaling alterations induced by pro-hypertrophic stimuli were assessed in both myocardium and NRVMs. Additionally, a range of molecular techniques were employed to elucidate the regulatory mechanisms of ORP5 on mTORC1 in hypertrophied hearts.

## Results
Increased expression of ORP5 was observed in the hearts of patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), in mice subjected to transverse aortic constriction (TAC), and in NRVMs treated with angiotensin II (AngII). We found that ORP5 binds to mTOR in cardiomyocytes. Upon exposure to TAC surgery, ORP5-deficient hearts exhibited enhanced cardiac function, reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and diminished collagen deposition than wild type. Conversely, overexpression of ORP5 significantly aggravated hypertrophic responses in both hearts and NRVMs. Notably, the promotion of cardiac hypertrophy induced by ORP5 overexpression was reversed by rapamycin, an inhibitor of mTORC1. Mechanistically, our study elucidated that the ORD domain of ORP5 interacts with mTORC1, facilitating its translocation to the outer membrane of the lysosome for subsequent activation. This activation triggers the downstream signaling pathways involving S6K1 and 4E-BP1, which initiate protein synthesis, thereby promoting pathological cardiac hypertrophy.

## Conclusions
Our findings provide the inaugural evidence that ORP5 facilitates pathological ventricular hypertrophy through the translocation of mTORC1 to the lysosome for subsequent activation. Consequently, ORP5 has the potential to serve as a diagnostic biomarker or therapeutic target for pathological cardiac hypertrophy in the future.

## Introduction
Over the past several decades, cardiovascular diseases have emerged as a significant public health concern and a primary cause of morbidity and mortality . Pathological cardiac hypertrophy, often accompanied by severe fibrosis, represents a common cardiac response to various cardiovascular conditions such as hypertension and valve disease. This hypertrophic response is not merely a compensatory mechanism to diverse stimuli but also constitutes an independent risk factor for a range of cardiac pathologies, including ischemic heart disease, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death . Several factors, including AngII, endothelin 1, catecholamines and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), have been identified as contributors to myocardial hypertrophy . However, the precise molecular mechanisms driving pathologic cardiac hypertrophy remain inadequately elucidated.
Cardiomyocyte growth involves increased protein synthesis or reduced degradation. Mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR), a serine/threonine-protein kinase, operates in two complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, to regulate growth factor signaling . mTORC1 activity rises during the development of pathological hypertrophy due to biochemical, mechanical, and metabolic signals. mTORC1 enhances ribosomal protein production by activating protein S6 kinaseβ1 (S6K1) and inhibiting eIF4E-binding protein 1 (4EBP1), enabling cap-dependent translation by eIF4E . While this boost in protein synthesis helps the heart adapt to acute pressure overload, prolonged mTOR activation harms protein quality control . This signaling pathway is crucial in mediating the pro-hypertrophic effects of mTORC1. Evidence indicates that pharmacological inhibition of mTORC1 via rapamycin attenuates myocardial hypertrophy and enhances cardiac function . The protective mechanism can be initiated either before or after the establishment of the cardiac pressure overload model, including during the decompensation period , . This observation suggests that mTORC1 may serve as a central molecule in the regulation of cardiac hypertrophy. For mTORC1 activation, a specific condition must be met: mTORC1 must translocate to lysosomes, where it encounters Ras homolog enriched in brain (Rheb), a small GTPase that activates mTORC1 . Importantly, the activation of mTORC1 by Rheb occurs at the outer membrane of the lysosome , . However, the precise mechanism by which mTORC1 is recruited to the lysosome remains unclear.
Oxysterol binding protein (OSBP) and OSBP-related proteins (ORPs) constitute a class of lipid transfer proteins that play a crucial role in regulating the lipid composition of cellular membranes, lipid metabolism, and sterol transport by mediating the non-vesicular transfer of lipids between various organelles . ORP5, a member of the OSBP family, is predominantly localized at the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane . ORP5 is implicated in the transport of phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI4P) and phosphatidylserine (PS) . Recent findings indicate that ORP5 facilitates cell proliferation through the activation of mTORC1 signaling in the HeLa cell line . Analogously, in hepatic tissue, sterol transport between lysosomes and ER is contingent upon the regulation of mTORC1 by ORP5 . These observations collectively suggest a potential association between ORP5 and mTORC1.
In the current investigation, elevated ORP5 expression was observed in NRVMs treated with AngII, in the hearts of mice subjected to transverse aortic constriction (TAC), and in the myocardium of patients with HCM. These findings suggest a potential role for ORP5 in pathological cardiac hypertrophy. To further explore this hypothesis, we conducted loss- and gain-of-function experiments utilizing genetically modified mice and NRVMs. Aberrant expression of ORP5 was associated with pronounced cardiac hypertrophy and subsequent heart failure, whereas ORP5 deficiency mitigated the hypertrophic response to pathogenic stimuli. Mechanistically, our findings indicate that ORP5 serves as a critical effector in the translocation of mTORC1 to the lysosomal surface, thereby facilitating hypertrophic remodeling through the activation of downstream S6K1/S6 and 4EBP1 protein synthesis pathways.

## Materials and methods

## Experimental Animals
Male C57BL/6 mice (8–10 weeks old, 24–26 g) were procured from SiPeiFu Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The mice were housed in a controlled environment with a temperature of 23 ± 2°C and relative humidity of 50 % ± 5 %, under a 12-hour light/dark cycle and specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions. All animal experiments were conducted by the guidelines of the Animal Care Committee at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, and adhered to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH publication no. 85–23, revised 1996).

## Establishment of the TAC model
Mice of comparable backgrounds and ages underwent transverse aortic constriction (TAC) and sham procedures. General anesthesia was induced via inhalation of 2.5–3 % isoflurane and maintained with 2 % isoflurane. Following a left thoracotomy and mechanical ventilation, a 27-gauge needle (0.4 mm in diameter) was positioned on the aortic arch, located between the left common carotid artery and the brachiocephalic trunk. The transverse aorta and needle were ligated using a 6–0 nylon suture, after which the needle was removed to induce aortic stenosis. The sham surgery was performed using the identical technique, except the ligature.

## In vivo experimental design
Adeno-Associated Virus 9 (AAV9)-OE-ORP5, AAV9-shORP5, AAV9-ΔORD, and AAV9-GFP control viruses were created by OObio (Shanghai, China). Animal experimentation details are graphically illustrated in the text. The mTORC1 inhibitor Rapamycin (MCE, #HY-102190) was obtained from MCE (New Jersey, USA) and dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide. The solvent for rapamycin consisted of 0.2 % sodium carboxymethylcellulose and 0.25 % polysorbate-80 in water, which also served as the vehicle for animal experiments. Two weeks post-TAC surgery, mice received intraperitoneal injections of rapamycin (2 mg/kg/day) or a vehicle thrice weekly for two weeks. Following this treatment, transthoracic echocardiography was conducted.

## Echocardiography
Mice were anesthetized with 2 % isoflurane, and transthoracic echocardiography was conducted using a Visual Sonics Vevo2100 with a 35 MHz transducer. M–mode images were captured from the parasternal left ventricular long axis, recording cardiac parameters such as fraction shortening (FS), ejection fraction (EF), cardiac output (CO), and wall thickness.

## Histochemical analysis
Heart tissues were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. WGA and Masson’s Trichrome staining were conducted following the manufacturer’s instructions (ServiceBio, Wuhan). Slides were scanned using Olympus Slideview VS200, and cardiac fibrosis and cardiomyocyte size were analyzed with ImageJ.

## Human samples
Human heart samples for the study were obtained from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy patients at Zhongshan Hospital's Cardiology Department. The research use of these samples was approved by Fudan University's Ethical Review Board (approval numbers 2019–023 and 2020–009).

## Neonatal rat ventricular myocytes (NRVMs) Isolation
NRVMs were isolated from 1 to 2-day-old Sprague-Dawley rats using trypsin and collagenase type II digestion, followed by Percoll centrifugation as previously described . The cardiomyocytes were then cultured in DMEM/F12 (Gibco) with 10 % FBS (Gibco) and 1 % penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco).

## Neonatal mouse ventricular myocytes (NMVMs) Isolation
NMCMs were isolated from one to two days old mice. Briefly, hearts were cut into small pieces, washed, and digested by ADS (NaCl 6.8 g/L, HEPES 4.76 g/L, Na2HPO4 0.138 g/L, Glucose 0.6 g/L, KCl 0.4 g/L, MgSO4 0.102 g/L, pH 7.35–7.45) containing 0.6 mg/mL trypsin and 0.4 mg/mL collagenase type II at 37 °C with shaking. Then, cardiomyocytes were purified by Percoll density gradient centrifugation. In the end, supernatants containing cardiomyocytes were plated on gelatin-coated culture dishes and incubated in DMEM supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % P/S.

## HL-1 cell culture
HL-1 murine cardiomyocytes (Sigma-Aldrich, #SCC065) were cultured in DMEM with 10 % fetal bovine serum. Post-transfection with the previously described Lentivirus, cells were maintained in DMEM containing 5 μg/mL puromycin dihydrochloride.

## Lentivirus transfection
The pLVX-shORP5 and pLVX-ORP5 plasmids encoding the rat and mouse ORP5 gene were obtained from OObio (Shanghai, China). These were co-transfected with psPAX2 and pMD2.G plasmids into HEK 293 T cells using LipoSmart 3000Plus Transfection Reagent (Genetic Biotech, Shanghai) to produce shORP5 and OE-ORP5 lentiviruses. Culture media were collected and filtered 48 h and 72 h post-transfection using a 0.45 μm filter (Millipore, NY, USA). The filtered supernatants were added to NRVMs and polybrene (5 μg/ml, Beyotime Nantong).

## Plasmid transfection
Flag-tagged ORP5, ΔORD, ΔPH, and ΔTM plasmids were obtained from OObio (Shanghai) and transfected using LipoSmart 3000Plus (Genetic Biotech, Shanghai) as per the manufacturer's instructions.

## In vitro experiment Design
To study the role of ORP5 in promoting cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, NRVMs were divided into four groups: PBS, AngII, Lenti-shORP5 + PBS, and Lenti-shORP5 + AngII. After 24 h of lentivirus transfection, cells were cultured in serum-free DMEM/F12 for another 24 h. Cardiomyocyte hypertrophy was induced with 1 mM angiotensin II (AngII) for 24 h. Rapamycin (100 nM, MCE #HY-10219) was used to inhibit mTORC1, and Lonafarnib (2 μM, MCE #HY-15136) was used to inhibit Rheb, as previously described . NRVMs were collected after all treatments.

## Immunofluorescence Microscopy
After washing with PBS three times, cultured cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 20 min. They were then treated with 0.2 % Triton X-100 in PBS for 20 min and blocked with 10 % goat serum in PBS for 2 h. Cells were incubated with primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. Following three PBS washes, NRVMs were incubated with secondary antibodies (1:200, Jackson, #111–095-003, #111–165-003, #705–626-147) at 37 °C in the dark for 1 h. Finally, DAPI (1:10000, Beyotime, #C1002) was added to stain the nuclei and incubated for 10 min. Images were taken with a Leica SP8 confocal microscope. Cardiomyocyte size was measured using ImageJ software, with at least 20 cells counted per group.

## Small interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection
siRNA was transfected into NRVMs using LipoRNAi™ Transfection Reagent (Beyotime, #C0535) as per the protocol. mTOR-targeting siRNA was obtained from Dharmacon (#J-003008–11 and J-003008–12).

## Western Blot analysis
Heart tissues and cultured cardiomyocytes were lysed using RIPA buffer with a protease inhibitor cocktail. Total protein was extracted by centrifugation at 12,000g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cell membranes. Protein concentration was measured with a BCA assay. Samples containing 10 µg of protein were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membrane was blocked with Fast Blocking buffer (Epizyme, Shanghai, #PS108) for 30 min at room temperature, then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C (see for details). After applying HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, protein band intensity was visualized using ImageQuant LAS 4000 mini (GE, USA) and analyzed with ImageJ software.

## Lysosome Isolation
We isolated lysosomes from cultured NRVMs using the MinuteTM Lysosome Isolation Kit (Invent, #LY-034), then lysed them with RIPA for Western Blot analysis. Lamp-2 and GAPDH served as markers for lysosomal and plasma proteins, respectively.

## Co-immunoprecipitation Assays
Proteins from cultured cardiomyocytes were extracted using IP buffer and incubated with Anti-Flag magnetic beads (Selleck, #B26101) overnight at 4 °C. The immunocomplex was collected, washed six times with cold PBST, and then analyzed by immunoblot with specific primary antibodies.

## Quantitative Real‐Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT‐PCR)
Total RNA was isolated from heart tissues or cultured cardiomyocytes using the RNA isolator Total RNA Extraction Reagent (Vazyme, #R401-01). Genomic DNA was removed, and cDNA was synthesized with HiScript III Select RT SuperMix (Vazyme, #R233-01). mRNA expression was quantified using the QuantStudio5 (ThermoFisher) and ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, #Q331-02), with β-actin as the housekeeping gene. Fold change was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primers are listed in .

## Molecular docking
Protein structures of ORP5 (PDB ID: 1HD7) and MTOR (PDB ID: 1ORF) were sourced from the RCSB database. GRAMM docking was conducted with ORP5 (UniProt ID: Q9H0X9) as the receptor and mTOR (PDB ID: 8ERA) as the ligand. The top-ranked docked conformation was visualized and analyzed using PyMol, and interaction forces were examined with Ligplot 2.2.4.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± SD from a minimum of three independent experiments. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0. An independent sample t-test compared two groups, while one-way ANOVA or two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni’s post hoc test compared multiple groups. A p-value < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

## Results

## ORP5 expression was upregulated in pathological cardiac hypertrophy
To determine if ORP5 expression changes in human heart failure, we analyzed 106 HCM samples and 76 heart failure samples from GEO data sets (GSE36961 and GSE57338). We found significantly increased OSBPL5 expression in the ventricles of these patients ( A). Additionally, myocardial biopsy samples from HCM patients showed elevated protein and mRNA expression of ORP5 ( B). We treated isolated NRVMs with AngII or PE over time to induce hypertrophy, observing a progressive increase in ORP5 expression compared to the PBS group ( C, A). ORP5 mRNA levels also rose with AngII or PE treatment, indicated by higher Nppa expression ( D, B). We also found elevated ORP5 expression in AngII-treated NMVMs. ( A, B). Fluorescence images showed ORP5 primarily in the cytoplasm of NRVMs, with increased expression due to AngII ( E). To identify the source of elevated ORP5 in hypertrophied myocardium, we stained ORP5 and α-actinin (cardiomyocyte marker), CD31(endothelial cell marker) and α-SMA (fibroblast marker) in mouse cardiac tissues post-sham or TAC surgery. The results indicated ORP5 expression was elevated in heart sections from mice after TAC surgery and ORP5 is primarily found in cardiomyocytes, with some presence in fibroblasts and minimal expression in endothelial cells ( F, A, B).
 

## The knockdown of ORP5 mitigated pathological cardiac hypertrophy in vitro and in vivo
Given the high levels of ORP5 in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy myocardium and NRVMs, we explored the impact of ORP5 downregulation on cardiac hypertrophy. We created AAV9-shORP5 under the control of the cTnT promoter and produced cardiac-specific ORP5 knockdown mice through in situ heart injection ( A, B). These mice were healthy and similar to AAV9-Veh mice in cardiac structure and function at baseline. Subsequently, we subjected the mice to TAC and sham surgeries ( A). We employed cardiac ultrasound to validate the efficacy of TAC surgical modeling ( A, B). Western Blot analysis confirmed that AAV9-shORP5 effectively knocked down ORP5 expression in the mouse heart ( B). Four weeks post-surgery, ORP5 deficiency ameliorated pressure-overload–induced cardiac hypertrophy, as demonstrated by a decreased heart weight to body weight ratio (HW/BW) ( C), reduced gross heart size ( D), and decreased cross-sectional area of cardiomyocytes ( E). Furthermore, compared to the control group, ORP5 knockdown mitigated the TAC-induced decline in fractional shortening and ejection fraction ( C). Mice in the AAV9-sh-ORP5 with TAC group showed increased cardiac output and stroke volume, with smaller end-systolic and end-diastolic left ventricle dimensions, indicating better cardiac morphology and function than the control ( A-F, ). In ORP5-deficient mice, mRNA levels of hypertrophic markers (Nppa, Nppb, and Myh7; H) were significantly lower than in AAV9-Veh mice after TAC surgery. Masson trichrome staining showed that AAV9-shORP5 mice had less left ventricular fibrosis than the sham group 4 weeks post-TAC ( F, G). Also, after TAC surgery, AAV-shORP5 mice had lower fibrosis markers (col1a and col3a) ( G).
 To verify ORP5′s regulatory effects on isolated cardiomyocytes under pro-hypertrophic conditions, we conducted in vitro experiments. Using Lenti-shORP5, we created NRVMs with reduced ORP5 levels ( I, C). These cells were treated with AngII to induce hypertrophy, with a PBS-treated group as a control. After 24 h, cell surface area was measured via α-actinin immunostaining. No significant differences were observed in the morphology and size of NRVMs in the PBS group. ORP5 deficiency reduced AngII-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and lowered hypertrophic marker mRNA levels in NRVMs ( J, K). We obtain similar results in AngII-processed NMVMs ( D, G). Lenti-shORP5 played a similar role in PE-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy ( C, D).

## ORP5 interacts with mTOR and regulates the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway in pathological cardiac hypertrophy
Previous studies show that ORP5 boosts cancer cell growth through the mTORC1 pathway. We observed increased ORP5 expression in a cardiac hypertrophy model and, given the role of the mTORC1 pathway in cardiac hypertrophy, we suggest ORP5 may influence cardiac hypertrophy by regulating this pathway. We conducted molecular docking and found that ORP5 binds to mTOR with a binding affinity of −13.9 kcal/mol, indicating strong potential activity ( A, A-B). After transfecting NRVMs with Flag-ORP5 plasmid and purifying the protein using Flag magnetic beads, Western blot confirmed ORP5′s interaction with mTOR in cardiomyocytes ( B). Persistent activation of the mTORC1 pathway in pathological cardiac hypertrophy is detrimental. However, we found that ORP5 knockdown inhibited the mTORC1 pathway in AngII-treated NRVMs. This led to reduced activation of mTORC1 downstream protein synthesis pathways (S6K1/S6, 4EBP1) in AngII-treated sh-ORP5 cardiomyocytes ( C, E). Similarly, mTORC1 activation was not significant in ORP5 knockdown mice after TAC surgery compared to controls ( D, F). What’s more, the knockdown of ORP5 did not alter mTOR expression in vitro and in vivo ( A, B). To fully illustrate the effect of ORP5 knockdown on the mTORC1 downstream pathway, we examined AngII-treated NMVMs and obtained similar results to NRVMs ( C, E, F). mTORC1 also promotes cell growth by suppressing autophagy. An important early step in autophagy is the activation of unc-51-like kinase 1 (ULK1), a kinase that drives autophagosome formation . Under nutrient-replete conditions, mTORC1 phosphorylates ULK1, thereby preventing its activation by AMPK . So, we further tested the effect of knocking down ORP5 on the level of ULK1 phosphorylation. In contrast, it was less significantly altered than the former two (S6K1 and 4EBP1) ( A). In addition, p62, a widely studied autophagy substrate, was also not significantly altered ( A). We concluded that autophagy is inhibited in pathological cardiac hypertrophy and is regulated by a variety of upstream molecules, which may be more dominant than the activation of mTORC1 by ORP5. As ORP 5 is a lipid transfer protein, we investigate whether the lipid transfer function of ORP 5 is required to promote cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, we mutated key residues in ORP 5 conserved for binding PS (L389 D), PI (4)P (H478 A/H479 A), and cholesterol (H538 A/K540 A) . We found that compared with NRVMs transfected with ORP5, NRVMs transfected with the indicated ORP5 mutant did not show significant alterations in cell area as well as cardiac hypertrophy markers after AngII induction. It suggests that ORP5 promotes cardiomyocyte hypertrophy independent of the function of transported lipids ( A, B).
 

## Pathological cardiac hypertrophy was exaggerated by ORP5 overexpression and reversed by rapamycin
To explore ORP5′s role in cardiac hypertrophy, we conducted in vitro tests on ORP5 overexpression NRVMs. ORP5 overexpression in NRVMs activated the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway in response to AngII, aligning with the hypertrophic phenotype ( A, B). mTORC1 plays a crucial role in ORP5-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, as rapamycin, an mTOR inhibitor, blocked ORP5′s effects on cell size and hypertrophic markers ( C-E). These findings suggest that ORP5 promotes cardiomyocyte hypertrophy by regulating the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway.
 In vivo experiments couldn't fully capture ORP5′s regulation of the pathological cardiac hypertrophy, so we conducted in vitro experiments ( F). Under normal conditions, AAV9-OE-ORP5 mice were healthy with no significant differences in cardiac function and morphology compared to AAV9-Veh mice. However, ORP5 overexpression significantly increased TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy, as shown by cardiac function ( G), heart size ( H), and cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area ( I). Echocardiography parameters for TAC-treated AAV9-Veh and AAV9-OE-ORP5 mice are detailed in A-F and . ORP5 overexpression led to increased heart fibrosis post-TAC ( J, K, G) and elevated mRNA levels of hypertrophy markers compared to controls ( N). Consistent with in vivo results, ORP5 overexpression did not alter mTOR levels in TAC-operated mice ( B). However, the mTORC1 downstream pathway was largely overactivated by ORP5 overexpression ( L, M). We administered rapamycin intraperitoneally to ORP5-OE mice post-TAC surgery, following previous protocols as shown in A . The AAV-OE-ORP5 mice treated with rapamycin exhibited improved cardiac function ( G), reduced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy ( I) and fibrosis ( K, G), and lower hypertrophic markers ( N) compared to the AAV-OE-ORP5 + DMSO + TAC group. These findings indicate that ORP5 promotes hypertrophic cardiac remodeling via the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway.

## ORP5 enhances mTORC1-mediated cardiac hypertrophy by promoting its translocation to the lysosome
In the following study, we reduced mTORC1 expression by siRNA, yielding similar results as rapamycin ( A-D). mTORC1 activation requires targeting to the lysosomal surface . ORP 5, which interacts with lysosomal membrane protein NPC1 involved in cholesterol trafficking , suggests a potential link between ORP 5 and lysosomes. We hypothesized that ORP5 overexpression could facilitate mTORC1 translocation to lysosomes, enhancing its downstream signaling. To test this, we extracted lysosomes from NRVMs transfected with Lenti-Veh and Lenti-OE-ORP5 and used WB to detect mTOR and ORP5, with GAPDH and Lamp-2 as markers. Results showed a significant increase in lysosomal mTOR in ORP5-overexpressing NRVMs after AngII treatment ( E). We also observed that mTOR co-localizes with Lamp-2 in HL-1 cells overexpressing ORP5 using immunofluorescence. In Lenti-Veh transfected cells, mTOR partly localized to Lamp-2-positive lysosomes, and this co-localization increased after AngII treatment. In Lenti-OE-ORP5 transfected cells, mTOR binding to lysosomes was significantly enhanced post-AngII treatment compared to Lenti-Veh cells ( F). Once mTORC1 was translocated to the lysosomal membrane, it was activated by Rheb. To investigate if ORP5 enhances mTORC1 activation on lysosomes, we used the Rheb inhibitor lonafarnib. Lonafanib inhibited the overactivation of the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway due to ORP5 overexpression ( G, I). Immunofluorescence of NRVMs confirmed the reverse effect ( H, J). These findings indicate that ORP5 promotes cardiac hypertrophy by transporting mTORC1 to the lysosomal membrane for activation.
 

## The ORD domain of ORP5 is indispensable for ORP5-mediated mTORC1-dependent cardiac hypertrophy
Next, we aimed to identify the ORP 5 domain necessary for mTOR interaction. Molecular docking indicated key amino acids mainly in the ORD domain ( ). To confirm this, we created Flag-tagged ORP 5 mutants missing the PH (Flag-ORP 5 Δ PH), ORD (Flag-ORP 5 Δ ORD), or TM (Flag-ORP 5 Δ TM) domains ( A). NRVMs were transfected with Flag-Vector, Flag-ORP5, or deletion mutants. Anti-Flag immunoprecipitation revealed that Flag-ORP5 pulled down the most mTOR. Deleting ORD significantly reduced ORP5′s interaction with mTOR, indicating ORD's necessity for this interaction. Deleting PH and TM also partially affected the ORP5-mTOR interaction ( B). We investigated whether deleting ORD influenced ORP5′s regulation of the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway. In ORP5 knockdown NRVMs, cells with ΔTM or ΔPH plasmids showed better mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway recovery post-AngII treatment than the ΔORD group ( C, D), supported by immunofluorescence results ( E). Similarly, we transfected these plasmids to wild-type NRVMs with similar results to sh-ORP5 NRVMs ( A-C).
 The interactions among various myocardial cells can influence in vitro experiment outcomes. To address this, AAV9-ΔORD was injected into AAV9-shORP5 mice hearts. Mice with AAV9-ORP5 served as a positive control. Post-TAC, AAV9-shORP5 mice with AAV9-ΔORD showed no significant cardiac function difference compared to the AAV9-shORP5 group, while AAV9-ORP5 mice exhibited cardiac function decline ( G, A-F, ). Supplementing with AAV9-ΔORD did not increase pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy compared to full-length ORP5, as shown by less heart weight, size, and cardiomyocyte area ( H, I). AAV9-ΔORD mice also had less interstitial and perivascular fibrosis than sh-ORP5 mice ( J, K, G). Additionally, mRNA levels of cardiac hypertrophy in AAV9-ΔORD hearts were similar to AAV9-shORP5 hearts and lower than in the ORP5 group ( N). Moreover, supplementing AAV9-ΔORD failed to reverse mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway inhibition caused by ORP5 knockdown, indicating that ORP5 without the ORD domain cannot activate mTORC1 on lysosomes ( L, M). These findings suggest that ORP5 interacts with mTORC1, and the ORD domain is essential for this interaction ( ).
 

## Discussion
ORP5 functions as a sterol sensor and lipid transfer protein (LTP), playing a critical role in the regulation of lipid homeostasis, insulin signaling transduction, cellular proliferation, and migration in vivo . Prior research has shown that ORP5 facilitates lysosomal degradation and enhances the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process, thereby accelerating renal cell tumor progression through the promotion of C-Cbl and c-Met ubiquitination . Furthermore, ORP5 has been implicated in the promotion of migration and invasion in cervical cancer by mitigating endoplasmic reticulum stress . However, there are no studies on the role of ORP5 in the cardiovascular system.
In this study, we observed an upregulation of ORP5 expression in hypertrophied cardiomyocytes and hearts. To elucidate the precise role of ORP5 in the development of pathological cardiac hypertrophy, we generated murine models and NRVMs with modified ORP5 expression levels. Our results suggest persistent aberrant activation of the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway during the development of pathological cardiac hypertrophy, which is detrimental to cardiac hypertrophy. ORP5 overexpression exacerbates hypertrophic remodeling induced by pathological hypertrophy stimuli by translocating mTORC1 to the lysosomal membrane for its activation and promoting its downstream protein synthesis pathways, whereas ORP5 knockdown significantly mitigates this effect. Our study illustrates for the first time the critical role of ORP5 in pathological cardiac hypertrophy and subsequent HF progression.
ORP5, a member of the oxysterol-binding protein (OSBP)-related protein (ORP) family, possesses a C-terminal transmembrane domain that facilitates its anchorage to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane . Its principal function involves the sensing, binding, and translocation of lipids at MCS between the ER and various organelles, including the plasma membrane , mitochondria , lysosomes , and lipid droplets . Research has demonstrated that ORP5 facilitates the transport of PS at the endoplasmic reticulum-plasma MCS through a phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI (4)P) countercurrent exchange mechanism. Additionally, phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PI (4,5) P2) can function as a co-exchanger for ORP5 . Since membrane PS can bind to specific residues within the regulatory structural domain of Akt, an upstream effector of mTORC1, this interaction is essential for Akt activation and subsequent downstream signaling . Although the lipid transport function of ORP5 found in this paper does not seem to be related to cardiac hypertrophy, the effect of its transport function on molecules upstream of mTORC1 remains a direction for future research. Besides the PI3K/Akt pathway, the ORP5-mTORC1 pathway may interact with other pathways. ORP5 activates mTORC1, inhibiting FoxO and diminishing its antioxidant and pro-apoptotic roles, thereby worsening cardiac hypertrophy . Conversely, the AMPK, Hippo, and p38/MAPK pathways counteract mTORC1 pathway in cardiac hypertrophy progression .
At the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-lysosome MCS, ORP5 interacts with the cholesterol transporter NPC1, which is anchored in the lysosomal membrane and facilitates the transfer of cholesterol from the limiting membrane to the ER . This interaction suggests a potential functional link between ORP5 and lysosomal activity. ORP5, in ER-mitochondria MCS, interacts with outer mitochondrial membrane protein PTPIP51 to regulate mitochondrial morphology and respiratory function, possibly through PS enrichment . The interaction of ORP5 with different organelles in the MCS is also an important direction to study its promotion of cardiac hypertrophy.
The PH domain and TM domain are situated at the N-terminal and C-terminal regions of ORP5, respectively, and are essential for directing ORP5 to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane and non-ER organelle membranes . For instance, OSBP, another member of the ORP family, utilizes its PH domain to connect the Golgi apparatus to the ER membrane . The ORD domain, characterized by the signature motif EQVSHHPP, adopts a barrel-like structure with hydrophobic pockets and lids that safeguard transported lipids in the cytoplasmic aqueous environment . This ORD domain is crucial for the lipid-transporting function of ORP5 . In this study, we discovered that ORP5 interacts with mTORC1 via its ORD domain. It is plausible that ORP5 facilitates the transport of mTORC1 to the lysosomal membrane in a manner analogous to its transport of PS, although the specific mTOR residues involved in this interaction require further elucidation. Co-IP results indicate that the PH and TM domains also influence the interaction between ORP5 and mTORC1, implying that ORP5′s capacity to translocate mTORC1 diminishes once it dissociates from the membrane.
The mTORC1 signaling pathway is crucial for cardiovascular development and adaptation, but chronic activation under stress leads to harmful effects like pathological hypertrophy and energy stress . Partial inhibition of mTORC1, via genetic or pharmacological methods, has proven beneficial in pro-hypertrophic conditions . Knocking down ORP5 partially inhibits mTORC1 activation, slowing pathological cardiac hypertrophy, and making ORP5-mTORC1 a promising therapeutic target. Amino acids activate RAG GTPase, enabling it to bind Raptor and recruit mTORC1 to the lysosome, where Rheb is found . The exact recruitment mechanism is unclear. Our study shows that overexpressing ORP5 in cardiomyocytes moves mTORC1 to the lysosome for activation, suggesting ORP5 may guide mTORC1 in response to cardiac hypertrophy, offering a new way to regulate the mTORC1 pathway.
mTORC1 facilitates protein synthesis by phosphorylating S6K1, enhancing translation initiation , and 4EBP1, which normally inhibits translation by binding eIF-4E. Phosphorylation of 4EBP1 by mTORC1 prevents this inhibition, allowing eIF-4E to form the eIF-4F complex and initiate protein synthesis . This process is also essential for protein synthesis in cardiomyocytes. S6K1 and 4EBP1 are crucial in mTORC1-induced cardiac hypertrophy, especially when triggered by cardiac hypertrophy . In the present study, we found that ORP5 promotes mTORC1 activation to activate mainly protein synthesis pathways (S6K1 and 4EBP1), with relatively little effect on autophagy-related pathways (ULK1).
This study found that reducing ORP5 levels partially inhibited mTORC1 activation in response to pro-hypertrophic stimuli. ORP5 can activate mTORC1 by recruiting it to the lysosome, explaining why ORP5 overexpression enhances the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway. Given the facilitating role of ORP5 for the overactivation of the mTORC1 protein synthesis pathway in cardiac hypertrophy, drugs specific for the ORD domain of ORP5 transporting mTORC1 could be developed to block its transport of mTORC1. In addition, ORP5 is also expressed in plasma but cannot be detected due to current technological limitations; in the future, with technological advances, ORP5 is expected to become a diagnostic marker for DCM.

## Conclusion
Our data indicates that pro-hypertrophic stimuli elevate ORP5 expression. While ORP5 knockdown didn't impact normal cardiac function, it inhibited hypertrophic remodeling. Conversely, ORP5 overexpression worsened cardiac hypertrophy by facilitating mTORC1 activation at the lysosome membrane. These findings clarify the role of ORP5 in mTORC1 signaling and offer potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets for pathological cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure.
