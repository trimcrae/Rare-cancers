## Summary
Neuronal cell cultures are highly sensitive to their microenvironment, particularly the choice of coating substrate. Here, we present a protocol to isolate extracellular matrix (ECM) from mouse brain tissue, providing a native coating material that closely mimics in vivo conditions. We detail decellularization steps, guidelines for measuring ECM quality, and instructions for co-culturing neuronal cells on the resulting substrate. By promoting improved neuronal survival, growth, and differentiation, this protocol has broad implications for in vitro neurobiological research and downstream applications.
Subject area: Cell culture, Neuroscience, Tissue engineering

## Before you begin
Neuronal cells cultured in vitro often face substantial challenges to maintain viability and proper function. Most commercial coating options are either single-component solutions or are derived from complex tumor-based materials, potentially lacking the native complexity of in vivo extracellular matrices. By contrast, extracellular matrix (ECM) prepared from whole brain tissue can more closely mimic the natural neuronal microenvironment, thereby supporting better neuronal survival, growth, and differentiation. This protocol describes how to decellularize mouse brain tissue to produce a brain-specific ECM substrate for neuronal cell culture.
This protocol describes the generation of decellularized brain ECM (dBEM), adapted from methods originally developed for brain, heart, and liver tissues. , , The approach can be used to produce coatings that support various neuronal cell types. While this procedure focuses on brain-derived ECM, similar techniques may be applied to other soft tissues. Readers interested in exploring additional neuronal cell lines can use this protocol for guidance on compatible cell models.

## Mouse brain dissociation

Timing: 30 min

1.Anesthesia:a.Fill the vaporizer with isoflurane, set the oxygen flow rate as 1–2 L/min, and ensure the scavenging system is active.
b.Place the mouse in an induction chamber with 3–5% isoflurane until it loses its righting reflex, then reduce the concentration of isoflurane to 1–2% for maintenance.

Note: Since the injection of 5% hydrated trichloroacetaldehyde solution for anesthesia is indeed restricted by many Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees (IACUC), we used commonly approved alternative such as isoflurane inhalation.

2.Surgery:a.Place the mouse in a supine position, and then make a midline abdominal incision to expose the chest cavity.
b.Carefully incise through the ribcage and excise the pericardium completely to fully reveal the heart.

3.Perfusion:a.Stabilize the heart with forceps and incise the right atrial appendage.
b.Attach a 20 mL syringe to an integrated intravenous needle of the disposable infuser and fill it with ice-cold PBS.
c.Insert the needle into the left ventricle near the apex at an angle roughly parallel to the horizontal axis of the heart.
d.Rapidly inject approximately 20 mL of PBS. When the liver transitions from a deep red to nearly white color and clear fluid flows from the right atrium, perfusion is considered complete.

Note: You will feel a distinct “give” when the needle penetrates the ventricular wall. Avoid excessive force or a steep insertion angle, which may perforate the interventricular septum or pass through both ventricles, potentially damaging the heart.

4.Brain isolation:a.Decapitate the mouse and place the head on a chilled workstation. Expose the brain by carefully cutting the cranial vault.
b.Use ophthalmic scissors to make a horizontal incision near the eyes, and then cut upward from the cervical spine.
c.Gently remove the brain with forceps and place it into a 5 mL Eppendorf tube, followed by rapid freezing in liquid nitrogen ( A).

CRITICAL: Work swiftly to minimize tissue damage during brain removal. Ensure that neither the scissors nor the forceps press too deeply into the brain, to avoid tissue destruction.

Note: The brain samples can be immediately stored in −80°C for future use.

 

## Solution preparation (two ingredients)

Timing: 1 h

5.1 M Sucrose solution:a.Weigh 17.115 g of sucrose powder, dissolve it in 50 mL of ddH2O, and mix thoroughly.
b.Filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Store at 4°C for several months.

Note: We prepare 15% (w/v) sucrose solution and 30% (w/v) sucrose solution in a similar way.

6.3% (w/v) SDS solution:a.Weigh 1.5 g of SDS powder, dissolve it in 50 mL of ddH2O, and mix thoroughly.
b.Filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Store at 20°C–25°C for several months.

CRITICAL: SDS (sodium dodecyl sulfate) is a detergent that can irritate the skin, eyes, and respiratory tract. Here we use a dust-free SDS product to minimize the risk of inhalation or skin exposure, thereby reducing potential hazards to health.

7.4% ethanol solution:a.Add 2 mL of anhydrous ethanol to 48 mL of ddH2O, and mix thoroughly.
b.Prepare this solution fresh.

Note: We prepare 70% ethanol solution in a similar way.

8.0.2% (w/v) gelatin solution:a.Weigh 0.1 g of gelatin powder, dissolve it in 50 mL of DPBS, and autoclave at 121°C for 30 min.
b.Gently shake and allow it to cool to 20°C–25°C. Store at 4°C for several months.

9.1% glutaraldehyde solution:a.Add 1 mL of glutaraldehyde (50% in water) to 49 mL of DPBS, and mix thoroughly.
b.Filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Prepare this solution fresh.

CRITICAL: Glutaraldehyde is a toxic, corrosive fixative that can irritate the skin, eyes, and respiratory tract. Always handle it with appropriate PPE (e.g., gloves, lab coat, goggles) in a well-ventilated area or fume hood, and promptly clean any spills with absorbent material. Dispose of waste in accordance with local chemical regulations.

10.1 M glycine solution:a.Weigh 3.75 g of glycine powder, dissolve it in 50 mL of DPBS, and mix thoroughly.
b.Filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Store at 4°C for several months.

11.PBST buffer:a.Add 50 μL of Triton X-100 to 50 mL of DPBS, and mix thoroughly.
b.Filter the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Prepare this solution fresh.

## Step-by-step method details

## Decellularization of brain tissue

Timing: 2 days

1.Tissue preparation:a.Weigh the excised mouse brain. Make one horizontal and one vertical cut, dividing the brain into four equal pieces.
b.Place one piece (control group) in a 50 mL centrifuge tube and store it at −80°C. Transfer the remaining three tissue pieces to another 50 mL centrifuge tube for the following experiments.

Note: Perform all cutting on a chilled workstation to minimize protein degradation caused by temperature fluctuations.

2.Overnight soaking: Add approximately 50 mL of double-distilled water (ddH2O) to the 50 mL tube containing the experimental brain tissue pieces and incubate at 4°C for approximately 12–16 h ( B).

CRITICAL: Soaking for an extended period helps loosen the brain’s structure, enhancing tissue exposure to decellularization reagents and improving overall efficiency.

3.Initial rinse:a.Discard the ddH2O gently using a plastic Pasteur pipette.
b.Add approximately 50 mL of fresh ddH2O, then place the tube on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 1 h at 4°C.
c.Aspirate and discard the water by a plastic Pasteur pipette.

Note: Use a plastic Pasteur pipette to gently add or remove water from the tube during wash steps, minimizing disturbance to the tissue pieces. All subsequent steps follow this same approach for solution decanting and addition.

4.Trypsin treatment:a.Pre-warm the trypsin-EDTA (0.05%) solution at 37°C for 10 min.
b.Add 5 mL of the trypsin-EDTA (0.05%) solution to the tube containing the brain tissue pieces, then place the tube on a rotary shaker at 60 rpm for 90 min at 37°C.
c.Carefully remove the trypsin solution and wash the tissue pieces three times with ddH2O.

Note: The trypsin enzyme specifically cleaves peptide bonds on the C-terminal of the amino acids lysine and arginine, facilitating to disrupt cell-to-cell and cell-to-matrix adhesion for brain tissue dissociation.

5.Triton X-100 treatment:a.Add 5 mL of 3% Triton X-100 solution to the tissue-containing tube.
b.Incubate on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 120 min at 4°C.
c.Gently discard the solution and wash the tissue pieces three times with ddH2O.

CRITICAL: Ammonium hydroxide (NH4OH) solution for preparing 3% Triton X-100 solution is hazardous because it is a corrosive base that can cause chemical burns to the skin and eyes, and its vapors can irritate the respiratory tract. Always handle it in a well-ventilated area with appropriate personal protective equipment (e.g., gloves, goggles, lab coat).

Note: This non-ionic surfactant Triton X-100 disrupts lipid–lipid and lipid–protein interactions, thus aiding in cell lysis while preserving extracellular matrix integrity.

6.DNase I treatment:a.Dilute a 5 mg/mL DNase I stock to 10 μg/mL in DPBS.
b.Add 5 mL of 10 μg/mL DNase I to the tissue, then place the tube on a shaker at 120 rpm for 30 min at 37°C.
c.Carefully remove the solution and wash the tissue three times with ddH2O.

CRITICAL: DNase I digests single- or double-stranded DNA into smaller nucleotides or oligonucleotides, further eliminating residual DNA.

7.Sucrose treatment:a.Add 5 mL of 1 M sucrose solution to the tube and incubate on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 30 min at 4°C.
b.Carefully discard the solution and wash the tissue three times with ddH2O.

8.Osmotic shock:a.Add 5 mL of ddH2O, place on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 15 min at 4°C.
b.Aspirate the water and wash three times with fresh ddH2O.

Note: Rapidly adding ddH2O after 1 M sucrose incubation leverages osmotic pressure to lyse any remaining cells more effectively.

9.SDS treatment:a.Add 5 mL of 3% (w/v) SDS solution and place on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 60 min at 20°C–25°C.
b.Carefully remove the SDS solution and wash the tissue three times with ddH2O ( C).

Note: As an anionic surfactant, SDS efficiently solubilizes cellular components, aiding in decellularization.

10.Ethanol treatment:a.Add 5 mL of 4% ethanol solution and incubate on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 120 min at 4°C.
b.Discard the ethanol solution and wash the tissue three times with ddH2O.

Note: Ethanol dehydrates cells by replacing intracellular water, damaging cell integrity and dissolving lipids.

11.DPBS wash: Add 5 mL of DPBS, then place on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 15 min at 4°C. Aspirate and discard.
12.ddH2O wash: Add 5 mL of ddH2O, place on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, then discard.
13.Antibiotic treatment: Add 5 mL of DPBS containing 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin, incubate on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm for 60 min at 4°C, and carefully discard.

Note: Penicillin and streptomycin effectively target both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, preventing contamination of the dBEM.

14.Homogenization and lyophilization:a.Transfer the tissue to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and homogenize it with a plastic pestle.
b.Immediately snap-freeze the homogenate in liquid nitrogen. Place the sample tube in a −80°C freezer for 12 h.
c.Lyophilize the homogenate using a freeze dryer to obtain the dBEM product ( D).i.Once the freeze-dryer reaches −40°C and a vacuum of ≤0.120 mBar, load samples by turning the valve to “VAC” and waiting for the vacuum to recover.
ii.After freeze-drying for 6–8 h, turn the valve to “VENT,” and remove the samples.

Note: Before homogenization, cut one piece of dBEM weighing about 40 mg for H&E staining assay. Typically, we obtain a net weight of approximately 80 mg for a control brain piece and 100 mg for three pieces after decellularization. After lyophilization, 40 mg of control brain tissue yields 5 mg of dry powder, while 60 mg of decellularized ECM produces 6 mg of dry powder.

## Detection of cell removal efficiency in decellularized brain ECM

Timing: 2 days

15.Sample preparation:a.Cut one piece of unprocessed mouse brain tissue weighing about 20 mg to repeat step 14 for prepare the control brain powder.
b.Measure approximately 1 mg each of the dBEM powder and non-decellularized (control) mouse brain tissue powder separately for subsequent DNA purification.

Note: Excess sample volume may reduce both DNA yield and purity. Control the amount of tissue powder carefully.

16.DNA purification:a.Incubate the samples with the digestion buffer from DNA isolation mini kit in a 55°C water bath until the tissue is fully digested, inverting the tubes periodically to enhance the digestion process.CRITICAL: Complete digestion typically occurs within around 2 h and is indicated by the absence of visible precipitate.

b.Perform the following DNA isolation steps according to merchant’s instructions of the kit.
c.Do two rounds of elution with 30 μL of Elution Buffer (preheated to 70°C) to maximize the DNA obtaining efficiency.

17.DNA quantification: Measure DNA concentration using a NanoDrop 2000 (or equivalent) spectrophotometer ( A).
18.Frozen section preparation:a.Excise a small piece (weighing approximately 40 mg) from either dBEM or control (non-decellularized) mouse brain tissue piece before lyophilization. Place each sample into separate 5 mL centrifuge tubes.
b.Add 4 mL of fixative solution to each tube so that the tissue is fully submerged. Fix 12–16 h at 4°C.
c.Discard the fixative solution and replace it with 15% (w/v) sucrose solution. Incubate until the tissue sinks to the bottom for around 12 h at 4°C.
d.Replace the 15% sucrose solution with 30% (w/v) sucrose solution and continue incubating until the tissue once again settles for around 12 h at 4°C.
e.Carefully remove the tissue and place it into a small plastic mold. Add sufficient O.C.T. compound to fully immerse the tissue.
f.Transfer the mold to a −40°C freezer for approximately 2 h, allowing the tissue to freeze into a block.
g.Using a cryostat at −20°C, cut tissue into 8 μm-thick sections. Place the sections into labeled slide boxes and store at −40°C until further use.

19.H&E staining:a.Fill a 50 mL centrifuge tube with ddH2O. Place the dBEM sections and corresponding control sections into the tube. Gently shake on a rocking shaker for 10 min to remove residual O.C.T. compound.
b.Perform H&E staining kit according to merchant’s instructions.
c.Histological imaging examination. Observe the stained sections under an Easyscan 6 (or equivalent) slide scanner. Capture images as needed for documentation ( B).

 

## Mouse brain ECM dissolution and reconstitution

Timing: 30 min to 1 h

20.Sample preparation:a.Weigh out 3 mg of dBEM lyophilized powder in a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. Add 300 μL of ice-cold DPBS containing a protease inhibitor cocktail.
b.Sonicate for 30 s on/off, for a total of 3 cycles.
c.Centrifuge at 13,800 g for 5 min at 4°C to collect the supernatant.

CRITICAL: If any dBEM solid powder remains undissolved or the solution remains turbid after 3 cycles, increase the number of sonication cycles until the sample appears sufficiently dissolved.

Note: We use a probe-free bioruptor for sonication in this protocol; however, a regular sonicator (e.g. SFX 150 by BRANSON) can also be used instead.

21.Mouse dBEM filtering:a.Add 3 μL of penicillin-streptomycin (100×) solution to 300 μL of sonicated mouse dBEM solution (with a final 1% concentration).
b.Filter-sterilize the solution through a 0.22 μm filter. Aliquot 30 μL of the dBEM solution per tube and store at −80°C for future use.

## Assay of mouse brain ECM protein content

Timing: 4–6 h

22.BCA protein quantitation assay:a.Use the mouse dBEM solution to measure total protein amount according to the BCA protein quantification kit’s standard protocol.
b.Calculate the protein concentration of the mouse dBEM solution ( A).

23.SDS-PAGE electrophoresis:a.Prepare a 10% SDS-PAGE gel according to the standard protocol (refer to a Bio-Rad protocol: https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6201.pdf). Load the dBEM samples (0.4 mg/lane) and run the gel at 15 mA until the dye front reaches the bottom of the gel.
b.Stain the gel with a Coomassie Brilliant Blue staining kit and record the protein banding pattern using a Chemiscope 6200 gel imaging system ( B).

Note: Gel electrophoresis helps assess potential ECM protein degradation. And both BCA protein quantification data and the SDS-PAGE gel image showed that our protocol enables to generate dBEM that preserves most of the extracellular matrix components.

 

## ECM coating preparation

Timing:20–24h

24.Glass coverslip retreatment:a.Lay glass coverslips flat, spaced apart (without overlapping), in a 10 cm glass dish. Add 6 mL of acetone and incubate at 20°C–25°C for 30 min.
b.Aspirate the acetone. Add 8 mL of anhydrous ethanol and place on a rocking shaker at 20 rpm for 30 min. Discard the ethanol.
c.Add 8 mL of the 70% ethanol to the glass dish and place on a rocking shaker at 30 rpm for 5 min. Repeat the 70% ethanol wash one more time.
d.Rinse slides five times with 10 mL ddH2O, each for 5 min.

25.Autoclaving:a.Wrap the glass dish in aluminum foil and autoclave at 121°C for 30 min.
b.Heat-dry the coverslips and store them in a sterile area until use.

26.UV sterilization:a.Place the glass dish containing the coverslips in a biosafety cabinet and expose to UV light for 1 h.
b.Using forceps, carefully transfer the coverslips to a 24-well cell culture plate and rinse each well three times with DPBS.

Note: Maintain the orientation of each coverslip in the 24-well plate as it was in the glass dish.

27.Gelatin base layer coating:a.Pre-warm 0.2% (w/v) gelatin solution to 37°C.
b.Pipet 500 μL of this solution to each well and incubate at 37°C for 1 h.
c.Aspirate the gelatin solution and rinse gently with 1 mL DPBS.

28.Crosslinking:a.Gently pipet 500 μL of 1% glutaraldehyde solution to each well.
b.Incubate for 30 min at 20°C–25°C.
c.Aspirate the solution and rinse gently with 1 mL DPBS.

29.Quenching:a.Pipet 500 μL of 1 M glycine to each well and incubate for 20 min at 20°C–25°C to quench residual glutaraldehyde.
b.Aspirate the solution and rinse gently with 1 mL DPBS, added against the wall of the well.

Note: Gelatin-coated coverslips can be used immediately or stored overnight at 4°C in culture medium containing 1% penicillin-streptomycin.

30.dBEM top layer coating:a.Dilute the dBEM solution 1:100 in DPBS. Add 500 μL of this dilution to the gelatin-coating coverslip in each well and incubate in CO2 incubator for cell culture for 12–16 h at 37°C.
b.Aspirate and rinse gently with 1 mL DPBS.

Note: We have included steps 27–29 to generate a 0.2% gelatin base layer on the coverslip. This initial layer, combined with crosslinking and quenching processes, enhances the adhesion of the dBEM solution to form top layer, thereby improving the stability and uniformity of the final coating. If not used immediately, add 500 μL of DPBS containing 1% penicillin-streptomycin to each well and store the coated coverslip at 4°C for up to one week.

## Neuronal cell co-culture

Timing: 7–8 days

31.Matrigel coating preparation:a.Place pretreated coverslips into a 24-well plate and rinse three times with DPBS.
b.Dilute Matrigel matrix in ice-cold DMEM/F12 medium at a ratio of 1:50, pipet up and down thoroughly, and add 500 μL of the mixture to each well. Incubate in CO2 incubator for 1 h at 37°C.

Note: Perform this step 1 h before cell seeding so that coverslips are ready when cells are harvested.

CRITICAL: Matrigel matrix is temperature-sensitive and can prematurely solidify if allowed to warm above 4°C. To avoid this, keep Matrigel on ice or in a cold environment throughout preparation and handling. Additionally, pre-chill pipette tips, tubes, and any contact surfaces to maintain the low temperature and prevent early polymerization.

32.SH-SY5Y cell seeding:a.Suspend the SH-SY5Y cells in its culture medium and seed them onto the pre-coated coverslips in the 24-well plate. The recommended seeding density is approximately 3× 104 cells in 500 μL of medium per well.
b.Immediately transfer the plate to a cell incubator (5% CO2, 37°C), and briefly swirl in a “cross pattern” to distribute cells evenly and prevent clumping.

33.Complete medium change:a.Every 2 days, remove the medium and replace it with an equal volume of fresh, pre-warmed culture medium.
b.Observe the cell growth and harvest cells on Day 6 (or as needed for downstream assays).

34.Immunofluorescence staining:a.Fix, permeabilize and block the cells on coverslip following a standard protocol (refer to a Abcam protocol: https://docs.abcam.com/pdf/protocols/immunocytochemistry.pdf).
b.Dilute the the anti-rabbit Tuj1 primary antibody to 1:400 in 500 μL of PBST buffer. Incubate the coverslip with this solution for 12 h at 4°C.
c.Rinse the coverslip three times with PBST buffer, allowing 5–10 min for each wash.
d.Incubate the coverslip with anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody (1: 600 for dilution) in 500 μL of PBST buffer for 2 h at 20°C–25°C, protected from light.
e.Rinse the coverslip three times with PBST buffer, allowing 5–10 min for each wash.
f.Stain nuclei with a 1:1000 dilution of DAPI solution (1 mg/mL) for 5 min at 20°C–25°C in the dark.
g.Rinse the coverslip three times with DPBS buffer, allowing 5–10 min for each wash.
h.Add 10 μL of Fluoromount-G mounting medium onto a microscope glass slide and carefully invert the coverslip over it. Gently press to remove any trapped air bubbles.
i.Use EVOS M5000 microscope (or equivalent) to observe and capture fluorescent images of the cells ( A and 4B).

 

## Expected outcomes
By using this protocol, the decellularized brain extracellular matrix (dBEM) is expected to exhibit high purity, containing no more than 1% residual DNA, while retaining the major extracellular components and structural integrity characteristic of native brain tissue. Quick histological and biochemical analyses (e.g., H&E staining, DNA quantification) should confirm the thorough removal of cellular constituents alongside the preservation of key ECM proteins.
Furthermore, the dBEM can be efficiently dissolved via sonication and reconstituted for use as a tissue-specific coating. Neuronal cells cultured on this reconstituted dBEM substrate are anticipated to display enhanced viability and more physiologically relevant morphology compared with those grown on traditional commercial coatings. This outcome underscores the potential of brain-specific ECM to provide a more supportive and biomimetic niche for neuronal cell maintenance, differentiation, and experimental applications.

## Limitations
While this protocol outlines an effective approach for isolating and preparing decellularized brain extracellular matrix (dBEM) from mouse brain, several limitations should be recognized. First, the protocol relies on specific sonication equipment (e.g., a Bioruptor), which is not universally available in all laboratories. Alternative sonication systems or methods may be substituted, although they could vary in efficiency and require optimization. Second, the mouse brain–derived ECM itself may exhibit batch-to-batch variability. Factors such as donor characteristics, dissection technique, and handling conditions can all impact ECM composition and downstream experimental results. This variability highlights the importance of careful sourcing and consistent protocols when expanding or replicating studies. Lastly, complete removal of cellular components (i.e., cell debris and DNA) can fluctuate across isolation experiments, underscoring the necessity for routine quality control checks. Conducting DNA quantification and histological assessments after decellularization will help ensure that the resulting ECM is sufficiently pure and reproducible, thereby minimizing confounding factors in cell culture applications.

## Troubleshooting

## Problem 1
Low DNA removal efficiency in extracted mouse dBEM (Decellularization of brain tissue step 2 and step 6).

## Potential solution
Soak the dissected mouse brain tissue in water for approximately 12 h prior to decellularization. The step of extended water pre-treatment helps loosen the tissue structure, facilitating more efficient removal of cellular components. If residual DNA remains high, extend the DNase I treatment duration to ensure more thorough degradation of nucleic acids.

## Problem 2
Excessive protein loss from the extracted ECM (Decellularization of brain tissue step 5 and step 10).

## Potential solution
Firstly, minimize freeze-thaw cycles of the dBEM sample. Repeated freezing and thawing of tissue can damage ECM ultrastructure by forming ice crystals, which accelerate protein degradation. Secondly, use a higher concentration or longer wash time with Triton X-100 solution to eliminate residual ionic detergents that might denature proteins. Ensure subsequent rinses are sufficient to remove all detergent. In addition, reduce the duration of ethanol treatment to limit its impact on the ultrastructure, thereby preserving more native ECM proteins.

## Problem 3
Low solubility of reconstituted dBEM (Mouse brain dissolution and reconstitution step 20b).

## Potential solution
Increase sonication cycles. Add more sonication on/off intervals (or longer sonication time) to fully dissolve the dBEM. Check for visible precipitates and continue sonication until the solution appears clear.

## Problem 4
Bacterial contamination during cell co-culture (Mouse brain dissolution and reconstitution step 21).

## Potential solution
Because earlier stages of dBEM preparation typically occur in standard laboratory settings, the reconstituted dBEM solution should be filtered in a biosafety cabinet using a 0.22 μm sterile filter. And antibiotic supplementation such as 1% penicillin-streptomycin should be included in the dBEM solution to suppress bacterial growth. Adhere to aseptic technique throughout dBEM storage and subsequent cell culture to prevent contamination.
