However, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of heat stroke (HS)-induced myocardial dysfunction remain largely elusive. In this study, we constructed a cardiomyocyte-specific peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) knockout mouse model subjected to HS to investigate the key role of PPARγ. RNA sequencing analysis was performed to identify downstream targets of PPARγ. Rosiglitazone, a PPARγ agonist, was examined for its therapeutic potential against HS-induced myocardial injury. Our results showed that HS significantly downregulated the expression of PPARγ. Cardiomyocyte-specific knockout of PPARγ exacerbated myocardial injury in mice subjected to HS. RNA-seq analysis revealed that differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in lipid metabolism-related pathways, particularly ABC transporters. Further experiments demonstrated that ABCC5 serves as a pivotal downstream factor mediating the cardioprotective effects of PPARγ overexpression against HS. HS also led to the accumulation and deposition of lipids in the myocardium and serum over an extended period, which was partly attributed to the downregulation of the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway. Importantly, we demonstrated that treatment with either rosiglitazone (a PPARγ agonist) or atorvastatin (a lipid-lowering drug) holds promising therapeutic potential for ameliorating HS-induced myocardial dysfunction. These findings indicate that PPARγ protects against HS-induced myocardial pathological manifestations through ABCC5-dependent regulation of lipid metabolism.

## Introduction
Climate change is posing an escalating threat to public health. Recent assessments indicate that the global average temperature has already risen by approximately 1.1 °C to 1.2 °C above pre-industrial levels, leading to an increased frequency of extreme heat events [ ]. As global warming persists, record-breaking heatwaves have led to a rise in heat-related fatalities, including those caused by Heat Stroke (HS) [ ]. Classic HS is a life-threatening condition resulting from an imbalance in body temperature regulation due to exposure to high environmental temperatures and/or intense physical exertion. It is clinically characterized by an elevated core body temperature exceeding 40 °C and central nervous system dysfunction. Without timely and appropriate intervention, the mortality rate can be as high as 50% or more. Additionally, patients with HS often develop acute liver and kidney failure, arrhythmia, cerebral edema, shock, and even multiple organ failure. Survivors may experience lasting neurological and cardiovascular complications and face an ongoing risk of mortality. Previous studies have revealed notable parallels between the pathophysiological pathways of HS and sepsis [ ]. Furthermore, myocardial injury has been identified as a critical factor in the adverse prognosis of HS patients [ ]. Nevertheless, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying HS-induced myocardial dysfunction remain largely unclear.
Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), comprising PPARα, PPARβ/δ, and PPARγ, are ligand-activated transcription factors that orchestrate a wide range of vital cellular processes. Among them, PPARγ has attracted substantial scientific interest due to its pivotal roles in regulating inflammation, lipid metabolism, and glucose homeostasis [ , ]. While PPARα is a well-known regulator of fatty acid oxidation in the heart, PPARγ has also been shown to play a protective role in cardiac tissues under stress, potentially by promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and modulating ectopic fat deposition [ ]. Importantly, PPARγ is considered a potential therapeutic target for various inflammatory diseases, including sepsis [ ]. Specifically, its activation has been found to counteract LPS-induced mitochondrial loss and to restore cardiac fatty acid oxidation, thereby maintaining cardiac function in septic models [ ]. However, to the best of our knowledge, the therapeutic potential of PPARγ in HS and HS-induced myocardial dysfunction remains unexplored.
In this study, we investigated the potential role and underlying mechanism by which PPARγ ameliorates cardiac pathological features through modulation of ABCC5-dependent lipid metabolism. Additionally, we examined the efficacy of the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone as a potential therapeutic intervention for HS. Together, our findings support a convincing cardioprotective role of targeting the PPARγ/ABCC5 signaling axis in HS, highlighting its promising therapeutic potential.

## Materials and methods
2.1

## Mice
Male BALB/c mice aged 8–10 weeks (body weight 22-25 g) were obtained from the Animal Center of Air Force Military Medical University (Xi'an, Shaanxi, China). All experiments were conducted in a randomized manner, with investigators blinded to group assignment through the use of numerical codes for mice and samples. Cardiomyocyte-specific PPARγ knockout mice were generated by Cyagen Biosciences Inc. (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China) on a C57BL/6J background. Animals were euthanized by cervical dislocation following isoflurane anesthesia (induced with 4.0% isoflurane inhalation). All animal procedures were reviewed and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Northwest University (Xi'an, Shaanxi, China; Approval No. NWU-AWC-20230130 M) and conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health.

2.2

## Cell culture
HL-1 cells were cultured in Claycomb medium (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), 0.1 mM norepinephrine (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and 2 mM l-glutamine (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) as previously described [ ]. The cultures were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Cells were seeded in 60-mm dishes 24 h prior to experimentation.

2.3

## siRNA transfection
Specific siRNAs targeting PPARγ and ABCC5, as well as negative control siRNAs, were designed and synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The siRNA target sequences used for PPARγ knockdown in HL-1 cells were: PPARγ-1: GACAGTGACTTGGCTATAT; PPARγ-2: CCATCCGATTGAAGCTTAT; PPARγ-3: CGCATTCCTTTGACATCAA. The siRNA target sequences used for ABCC5 knockdown in HL-1 cells were: ABCC5-1: GGAAGCGCCUCAAGUCUAA; ABCC5-2: GACCGUGUCCAGAAGAUGA. Transfection was performed following the manufacturer's instructions using Lipofectamine 2000 Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). Cells were harvested 48 h after transfection for subsequent analysis.

2.4

## Construction of PPARγ overexpression in HL-1 cell lines
For recombinant lentivirus construction, PPARγ cDNA was amplified by PCR and inserted into the plenti-SFFV-COgfp-Flag-P2A-BSD-WPRE vector purchased from Genecarer Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Xi'an, Shaanxi, China). The vector plenti-SFFV-COgfp-Flag-P2A-PURO-WPRE was used as a parallel control. HL-1 cells were seeded in 24-well plates at 50–60% confluency. Recombinant lentivirus was produced by co-transfecting the constructed plasmid along with pSPAX2 and pMD2G packaging plasmids into HL-1 cells using Lipofectamine 2000. The virus-containing supernatant was collected and used to infect fresh HL-1 cells. Selection was performed with 5 μg/mL puromycin (Servicebio, Wuhan, Hubei, China).

2.5

## Construction of HS model
Mice were randomly assigned to the Sham and HS groups. Sham group mice were maintained at room temperature (25 ± 2 °C), while HS group mice were placed in an artificial climate incubator (Changzhou Enpei Instrument Manufacturing Co., Ltd., Changzhou, Jiangsu, China). The incubator was set to 39.5 °C with 60% relative humidity. After 2.5 h of heat exposure, mice were removed for subsequent behavioral scoring, rectal temperature measurement, and echocardiographic analysis at specified time points.
For the in vitro model, HL-1 cells were passaged upon reaching approximately 80% confluency. To establish the heat stress model, cells were cultured in a 5% CO2 incubator at 42.5 °C for 9 h (unless otherwise specified) before proceeding to further experiments.

2.6

## Behavioral scoring and rectal temperature measurement
Following model establishment, murine behavioral scoring was performed independently by two investigators. The evaluation was based on a previously described scoring system [ ], which included general appearance, fur condition, eye state, level of consciousness, response to stimuli, activity, and respiratory status.
Subsequently, rectal temperature was measured using a murine digital thermometer. Mice were gently restrained, and the temperature probe was lubricated and carefully inserted into the rectum. The reading was recorded once the instrument displayed a stable temperature.

2.7

## Echocardiography analysis
Cardiac function was assessed using a dedicated small-animal echocardiography system (Visual Sonics Vevo 3100, Toronto, Canada). Mice were anesthetized with 1–2% isoflurane and secured on the imaging platform to fully expose the cardiac region. M-mode images were acquired, and cardiac function parameters—including stroke volume (SV) and cardiac output (CO)—were measured at the target heart rate. All subsequent analyses were performed by an investigator blinded to treatment group and genotype.

2.8

## Histopathology analysis
Cardiac tissues from mice were excised, fixed in formalin, and embedded in paraffin. Sections of 4-5 μm thickness were obtained from paraffin-embedded blocks and mounted on slides. For hematoxylin and eosin staining (HE staining), sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through a graded ethanol series, and stained according to standard protocols.
Picrosirius red staining (PSR) and Masson's trichrome staining were performed to assess myocardial fibrosis; slides were incubated with the respective dye solutions as recommended. Oil Red O staining was used to visualize lipid deposits. Briefly, frozen sections were hydrated, incubated in Oil Red O working solution for 8–10 min in the dark, differentiated in 70% ethanol, rinsed in distilled water, and counterstained with hematoxylin.
For immunofluorescence, tissues and cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and blocked with 5% normal goat serum in PBS for 1 h. Sections and cells were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against PPARγ (Proteintech, 66936-1-1g) and ABCC5 (Bioss, bs-1437R), followed by incubation with appropriate secondary antibodies for 1 h. Images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope (Invitrogen EVOS M5000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and fluorescence intensity was quantified with ImageJ Pro Plus software.

2.9

## Blood routine and blood biochemical measurement
Mice were anesthetized, and whole blood (at least 10 μL) was collected by left orbital enucleation using sterile surgical forceps. Levels of white blood cells (WBC), lymphocytes (LYM), intermediate cells (MID), granulocytes (GRA), red blood cells (RBC), and platelets (PLT) were analyzed using an automatic hematology analyzer (KT6200VET, Shenzhen, Guangdong, China). From the remaining blood, approximately 150 μL was separated for serum preparation. The blood sample was kept at 4 °C for 2 h and then centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 10 min. The supernatant serum (∼150 μL) was collected for measurement of creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) using an automatic biochemical analyzer.

2.10

## Determination of free fatty acid (FFA)and triglyceride (TG) content
The contents of FFAs and TGs in heart tissues and HL-1 cells were determined using commercial assay kits (Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The absorbance was measured using a microplate reader at the specified wavelengths. All data were normalized to the total protein concentration of each sample.

2.11

## Statistical analysis
All data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Shapiro-Wilk test was applied to assess normality of the data distribution. For comparisons among more than two groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used, followed by Bonferroni post-hoc testing when data were normally distributed. Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test when normality assumptions were met. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.
More methods were shown in the Supplementary Materials.

## Results
3.1

## HS downregulates PPARγ expression in Mice and Cardiomyocytes
To examine changes in PPARγ expression in myocardial tissue and cardiomyocytes following HS, we first established murine and cellular models of heat injury. Mice were exposed to controlled hyperthermia at >39.5 °C for 2.5 h in an artificial climate chamber to induce heat injury. Successful model induction was confirmed by increased behavioral scores ( A), elevated rectal temperature (>40 °C, B), and significant impairment of cardiac function ( C). Subsequent analysis showed that PPARγ expression in cardiac tissue was decreased in heat-injured mice compared with sham-operated controls ( D–G). Further examination of PPARγ isoforms revealed that PPARγ1 mRNA levels were significantly downregulated at 2.5 h after heat injury and remained reduced at 3 weeks post-injury. In contrast, PPARγ2 mRNA levels did not change significantly initially but were markedly downregulated at 3 weeks ( ). 
Consistent with these in vivo observations, in vitro experiments demonstrated that exposure to high temperature resulted in a time-dependent decrease in cell viability, accompanied by evident cellular shrinkage and accumulation of cellular debris ( H–J). Immunofluorescence staining and quantitative PCR (qPCR) further confirmed that both PPARγ protein and mRNA expression were downregulated in HL-1 cardiomyocytes following HS ( K–M). In particular, mRNA levels of PPARγ1 and PPARγ2 were significantly reduced in HL-1 cells after 3, 6, and 9 h of heat exposure ( ).
Given the current lack of clinical transcriptomic databases specifically for heat-stressed patients, and considering the overlapping pathogenic mechanisms between HS and sepsis—such as systemic inflammation and multi-organ dysfunction [ , ]—we analyzed a publicly available sepsis-related GEO dataset (GSE79962). Bioinformatic analysis revealed significantly reduced PPARγ expression in myocardial tissues from patients with septic cardiomyopathy ( N). These findings, combined with the pathophysiological similarities between the two conditions, suggest that downregulation of PPARγ may likewise be a key factor in the pathogenesis of HS-induced myocardial dysfunction.

3.2

## Cardiomyocyte-specific PPARγ knockout exacerbates HS-induced myocardial injury
To further investigate the role of PPARγ in HS, cardiomyocyte-specific PPARγ knockout mice were generated ( ). Successful knockout was confirmed via Western blot, quantitative RT‑qPCR, and Southern blot analysis ( A–B and ). Heat-stressed PPARγ-cKO mice displayed higher behavioral scores ( C) and slightly elevated rectal temperatures compared to WT ( D). Moreover, myocardial injury markers such as LDH and AST were significantly upregulated in PPARγ-cKO mice following HS ( E–F). Echocardiography revealed aggravated cardiac dysfunction in these mice, as indicated by reduced CO and SV ( G–I). Consistent with these findings, histological assessment using HE and PSR demonstrated more severe structural disruption and increased myocardial fibrosis in heat-stressed PPARγ-cKO mice ( J). 
Based on the above findings, we further investigated the impact of PPARγ deficiency on HS injury in cardiomyocytes. PPARγ-specific siRNA did not affect baseline cell viability but significantly reduced PPARγ mRNA expression, with the most pronounced knockdown achieved by PPARγ siRNA 2 ( ), which was therefore selected for subsequent experiments. As anticipated, PPARγ knockdown exacerbated the detrimental effects of HS on cell viability ( A–C). Furthermore, PPARγ siRNA treatment increased apoptosis, promoted ROS accumulation, and aggravated mitochondrial membrane potential disruption following heat injury ( D–K). Additionally, PPARγ knockdown not only reduced PPARγ levels but also elevated the expression of the inflammatory mediator NLRP3 ( L–M). Collectively, these results indicate that PPARγ plays a critical protective role against HS-induced myocardial injury, and its depletion significantly aggravates cellular damage. 

3.3

## PPARγ overexpression attenuates HS-induced myocardial Injury
Subsequently, we examined whether PPARγ overexpression could alleviate HS-induced cardiac injury. HL-1 cardiomyocytes were engineered to overexpress PPARγ, and successful overexpression was confirmed by quantitative RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis ( A–B). In contrast to control cells, PPARγ-overexpressing cells showed significant protection against HS injury: they exhibited less reduction in cell viability, decreased apoptosis levels, attenuated ROS accumulation, and better preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential following heat exposure ( C–M). Taken together, these results demonstrate that PPARγ overexpression confers substantial protection against HS-induced myocardial damage. 

3.4

## ABCC5 functions as a critical downstream effector in PPARγ-Mediated cardioprotection
To systematically elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the cardioprotective effects of PPARγ overexpression, we performed whole-genome RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) in HL-1 cardiomyocytes to identify transcriptomic changes under HS conditions following PPARγ OE. Differential gene expression (DGE) analysis revealed a markedly altered transcriptional profile, with 2058 genes upregulated and 1399 downregulated ( A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis showed that these differentially expressed genes were predominantly enriched in lipid-related metabolic processes ( B). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis further indicated significant enrichment of the ABC transporter pathway, which is closely associated with lipid metabolism, in the PPARγ OE + HS group ( C). Given the critical role of metabolic dysregulation in the progression of HS [ ], these results suggest a potential interplay among PPARγ signaling, ABC transporters, and lipid homeostasis. Consequently, we focused our subsequent investigation on the relationship between ABC transporter-mediated lipid metabolism and PPARγ expression. 
Hierarchical clustering analysis of ABC transporter family members revealed distinct expression patterns in the PPARγ-OE + HS group. Specifically, ABCB1A, ABCG4, ABCA1, ABCC5, ABCA8A, ABCA9, ABCC6, TAP2, ABCA6, and ABCB4 were significantly upregulated, while ABCD2, ABCB7, ABCC10, ABCA2, and ABCA3 were downregulated ( D). To further verify these findings, we established HL-1 cells stably overexpressing PPARγ. FFA and TG are crucial components of cellular energy metabolism; however, excessive accumulation of these lipids is associated with metabolic dysregulation and potential lipotoxic injury [ ]. Quantitative analysis demonstrated that HS disrupted cellular lipid homeostasis, as shown by a marked increase in FFA and TG levels ( E). Importantly, PPARγ overexpression effectively attenuated this lipid accumulation. These results indicate that PPARγ helps maintain lipid homeostasis during HS, likely contributing to its cardioprotective effects by mitigating abnormal lipid deposition.
Under HS conditions, PPARγ overexpression increased the mRNA levels of PPARγ, ABCC5, ABCB1A, ABCC6, TAP2, ABCA6, ABCB4, ABCC10, and ABCA2, while decreasing the levels of ABCG4, ABCA1, ABCA8A, ABCA9, ABCD2, ABCB7, and ABCA3. Notably, some discrepancies were observed between qPCR results and RNA-seq data for ABCA8A, ABCC10, ABCA1, and ABCG4 ( F and G).ABCC5, a member of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter superfamily, functions as a molecular machine that couples ATP binding and hydrolysis to the translocation of diversas substrates across cellular membranes [ ]. In the cardiovascular system, ABCC5 is highly expressed and plays a crucial role in the efflux of cGMP and lipid-related compounds to maintain cellular homeostasis [ , ]. Previous studies have reported that cardiac ABCC5 expression is significantly impaired under inflammatory stress, such as during septic shock [ ]. Given that HS shares overlapping pathogenic mechanisms with sepsis, we investigated the regulatory relationship between ABCC5 and the transcription factor PPARγ in the context of HS-induced injury.
To investigate the regulatory relationship between PPARγ and ABCC5, we examined their co-localization at the cellular level. The results showed that after HS, ABCC5 expression decreased in parallel with the downregulation of PPARγ ( ). Western blot analysis further confirmed that protein levels of PPARγ, ABCC5, ABCC1, and leptin were significantly reduced following HS injury, supporting the notion that PPARγ may function, at least in part, through modulation of ABCC5 ( ).
Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining showed a marked reduction in ABCC5 expression at multiple time points following heat injury, including immediately after injury and at 1, 2, 3, and 4 weeks post-injury ( A). Immunohistochemistry yielded consistent results ( B). Based on previous literature indicating a link between lipid accumulation and HS injury, we also examined the expression of the lipid accumulation marker leptin. RT-qPCR analysis revealed that leptin mRNA levels were downregulated in a time-dependent manner after heat exposure ( C). 
Building on these findings, we employed a PPARγ-knockout (PPARγ-KO) mouse model to further examine the regulatory effect of PPARγ deficiency on ABCC5 expression under HS conditions. Compared with wild-type (WT) mice, PPARγ-KO mice showed significantly reduced ABCC5 protein expression in cardiac tissue after HS injury ( D). Notably, during the three-week recovery period following heat injury, immunofluorescence co-localization of PPARγ and ABCC5 in cardiac tissue was markedly lower in PPARγ-KO mice than in WT controls ( E–F). This suggests that PPARγ deficiency not only reduces basal ABCC5 expression but also impairs its dynamic recovery during post-injury repair. Together with the upregulation of ABCC5 observed in PPARγ-OE models, these results further support the hypothesis that ABCC5 acts as a key downstream effector of the PPARγ pathway and likely participates in PPARγ-mediated cardiac adaptation to HS.

3.5

## The PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway alleviates lipid accumulation in HS-injured mice, and ABCC5 knockdown reverses PPARγ-Mediated cardioprotection
To further clarify the regulatory relationship between ABCC5 and the transcription factor PPARγ, we first examined the expression of ABCC5 and leptin in HL-1 cells subjected to time-graded heat shock ( ). Both ABCC5 and leptin were significantly downregulated in a time-dependent manner,a phenomenon also observed in other inflammatory cardiac conditions [ ].Western blot analysis similarly showed decreased levels of other lipid-accumulation markers ( ). In PPARγ-OE cells, ABCC5 protein expression was elevated under both basal and heat-shock conditions ( ), further supporting a regulatory link between PPARγ and ABCC5.
To complement the gain-of-function data, we performed loss-of-function studies using PPARγ siRNA. Under HS conditions, PPARγ knockdown consistently suppressed ABCC5 expression ( ), confirming the regulatory role of PPARγ on ABCC5. To identify functional PPARγ response elements in the ABCC5 promoter, we predicted and designed mutations targeting three distinct candidate sites and performed luciferase reporter assays in HL-1 cells. The results showed that PPARγ significantly enhanced luciferase activity driven by the wild-type ABCC5 promoter. In contrast, mutations at sites Mut2 and Mut3, especially Mut3, markedly attenuated reporter activity ( A). CUT&Tag analysis further corroborated PPARγ binding to the ABCC5 promoter, showing that PPARγ OE significantly increased promoter occupancy compared with control. While mutations at Mut1 and Mut2 partially reduced this enrichment, the Mut3 mutation completely abolished it, which aligns with the reporter assay results ( B). 
To functionally confirm that ABCC5 acts downstream of PPARγ, ABCC5-specific siRNAs were designed and synthesized for use in HL-1 cells. Knockdown efficiency was verified by qPCR, with ABCC5 siRNA-2 selected for subsequent experiments ( C). Transfection of ABCC5 siRNA-2 into PPARγ-OE cells reversed the cytoprotection conferred by PPARγ under HS conditions, as indicated by reduced cell viability, increased apoptosis, elevated ROS levels, loss of mitochondrial membrane potential ( D–L), and downregulated expression of PPARγ, ABCC5, ABCC1 and leptin ( M − N). Together, these results establish ABCC5 as a key transcriptional target of PPARγ in the HS response and demonstrate its essential role in mediating the cardioprotective effects of PPARγ activation.
Subsequently, we sought to determine whether the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway improves lipid metabolism and contributes to myocardial protection in HS. We evaluated long-term outcomes in mice at 1, 2, 3, and 4 weeks after a single HS episode. Behavioral scores were significantly elevated throughout the 4-week observation period ( ). In contrast, routine blood parameters and blood biochemistry showed little long-term change ( ), and echocardiographic assessment revealed no persistent impairment in cardiac function ( ). However, histological analysis using HE staining indicated sustained structural damage in the heart at all time points after HS ( A), accompanied by pronounced cardiac fibrosis ( B–C). These persistent histological alterations may reflect chronic adaptive or compensatory responses triggered by HS injury. 
Notably, Oil Red O staining revealed substantial lipid accumulation and deposition in the myocardium, with the most pronounced effect observed 3 weeks after HS ( D). Interestingly, we also examined hepatic fat accumulation following HS and found that the most significant lipid deposition in the liver occurred between 2 and 3 weeks post-exposure ( ). Therefore, we focused on the 3-week time point to assess longer-term effects of heat injury. At this time point, lipid metabolism indices showed an unfavorable pattern, characterized by decreased HDL-C and increased LDL-C levels in serum ( E). These results suggest that HS promotes prolonged lipid accumulation and deposition in the myocardium, which is partly linked to downregulation of the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway [ ].

3.6

## The PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone confers protection against HS-induced myocardial Dysfunction
To explore the potential therapeutic or preventive effects of PPARγ activation in HS-induced myocardial injury, we evaluated the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone in both in vivo and in vitro HS models. In heat-injured HL-1 cells, rosiglitazone treatment significantly improved cell viability, reduced apoptosis, attenuated oxidative stress, and preserved mitochondrial membrane potential ( A–K). Moreover, rosiglitazone modulated the expression of key lipid-transport molecules, upregulating ABCC5, ABCC1, ABCG1, ABCA1, and leptin ( L–N). Immunofluorescence analysis further showed that rosiglitazone largely reversed HS-induced alterations in the lipid-accumulation markers SR-B1 and PPARα ( ). 
At the in vivo level, mice were pretreated with rosiglitazone via intraperitoneal injection every other day for six days at doses of 2.5, 5.0, or 10 mg/kg, with an additional dose administered before HS induction ( A). In the acute model, rosiglitazone treatment suppressed the HS-induced rise in serum LDH and CK-MB levels ( B and C) and attenuated the decline in cardiac functional parameters such as CO and SV ( D–F). Histological assessment further showed that rosiglitazone improved myocardial architecture and reduced the degree of myocardial fibrosis ( G–I). More importantly, at three weeks after heat exposure, rosiglitazone significantly improved dyslipidemia, lowering serum LDL-C and raising HDL-C levels ( J and K). Immunohistochemical analysis indicated that rosiglitazone effectively reversed HS-induced alterations in SR-B1, PPARα, and PGC-1α expression ( ), which was consistent with immunofluorescence results ( ). Collectively, these findings reinforce the pivotal role of PPARγ in the organismal response to HS and point to the potential clinical translation of PPARγ agonists for treating HS-related myocardial injury. 
To further substantiate these conclusions, PPARγ knockdown was performed using siRNA. The results showed that silencing PPARγ abolished the protective effects of rosiglitazone in the heat-injured cellular model, which was reflected in reduced cell viability, increased apoptosis, elevated LDH release, heightened oxidative stress, and disrupted mitochondrial membrane potential ( A–J). Furthermore, rosiglitazone-induced regulation of lipid-metabolism-related gene expression was reversed upon PPARγ knockdown ( K and L), confirming that the therapeutic benefits of rosiglitazone are dependent on PPARγ ( ). 

3.7

## The lipid-lowering drug atorvastatin alleviates HS injury in vivo and in Vitro
Given the central role of lipid metabolism in the pathophysiology of HS, we investigated whether atorvastatin (ATOR) could mitigate HS-induced injury in both cellular and animal models. ATOR, a 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase inhibitor, is a cornerstone in the clinical management of hyperlipidemia and coronary artery disease [ ]. Beyond its lipid-lowering efficacy, recent high-level clinical evidence has demonstrated the potent cardioprotective effects of ATOR, such as its ability to significantly reduce the incidence of anthracycline-associated cardiac dysfunction [ ]. These findings suggest that ATOR may possess broader therapeutic potential in mitigating acute myocardial injury beyond its traditional role in metabolic regulation. In vitro, we first evaluated the cytotoxicity of ATOR in HL-1 cardiomyocytes. Cells were exposed to increasing concentrations of ATOR (1, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, and 40 μM) for 3 h followed by HS induction. Concentrations up to 10 μM did not significantly affect cell viability or morphology, whereas 20 μM markedly reduced viability ( ). Therefore, concentrations of 1–10 μM were selected for subsequent experiments. Under HS conditions, ATOR treatment significantly improved cell viability, suppressed the accumulation of ROS and LDH ( ), and effectively restored mitochondrial membrane potential ( ). Notably, ATOR also reversed the HS-induced downregulation of PPARγ and ABCC5 expression ( ).
In the in vivo arm of the study, mice received ATOR via oral gavage at doses of 5, 10, or 20 mg/kg daily for 7 days prior to HS induction. ATOR provided significant protection against HS-induced injury. Although ATOR treatment did not affect body weight ( ), it improved the regulation of rectal temperature and ameliorated alterations in hematological parameters ( ). Furthermore, ATOR partially restored both systolic and diastolic cardiac function ( ). Importantly, immunohistochemical analysis showed that ATOR enhanced the fluorescence intensity of PPARγ and ABCC5 in myocardial tissue ( ).
To explore the underlying mechanism, we performed PPARγ knockdown using siRNA. Silencing PPARγ abolished the protective effects of ATOR in the heat-injured cellular model, as demonstrated by decreased cell viability, increased apoptosis, elevated oxidative stress, and loss of mitochondrial membrane potential stability ( ). Furthermore, ATOR -induced modulation of lipid-metabolism-related gene expression was reversed upon PPARγ knockdown ( ), confirming that the pharmacological actions of ATOR are PPARγ-dependent.

## Discussion
HS represents one of the most severe consequences of extreme heat exposure, contributing significantly to heat-related mortality. While the clinical features of HS—including hyperthermia, systemic inflammation, and multi-organ dysfunction—are well recognized, the molecular mechanisms driving myocardial injury remain incompletely understood. The present study identifies PPARγ as a key transcription factor that protects the heart against HS-induced injury through the regulation of lipid metabolism, primarily mediated via its downstream target ABCC5.
4.1

## PPARγ is downregulated following HS and its loss exacerbates cardiac injury
We observed a significant downregulation of PPARγ expression in both in vivo and in vitro models of HS, with the cardiac-enriched isoform PPARγ1 showing particularly pronounced and persistent suppression. To directly assess the functional role of PPARγ, we employed a cardiomyocyte-specific knockout mouse model. PPARγ-deficient mice exhibited aggravated cardiac dysfunction, worsened histopathological damage, and increased markers of inflammation, oxidative stress and apoptosis following HS. Hyperthermia-induced oxidative stress and systemic inflammation are known drivers of cellular apoptosis during HS [ ]. These data establish PPARγ as an endogenous cardioprotective factor whose expression is compromised during HS and whose absence renders the heart more vulnerable to thermal injury.

4.2

## RNA-seq implicates lipid metabolism and ABC transporters in PPARγ-mediated protection
Transcriptomic analysis of hearts from PPARγ-overexpressing and wild-type mice subjected to HS revealed that differentially expressed genes were predominantly enriched in pathways related to lipid metabolism. This aligns with comparative transcriptome analyses showing that heat exposure significantly reshapes lipid droplet dynamics and metabolic profiles in various tissues [ , ].Notably, ABC transporters—a superfamily of membrane proteins involved in the transport of various substrates including lipids—emerged as a major category. Among these, ABCC5 was identified as a candidate downstream effector of PPARγ. ABCC5 has been previously implicated in metabolic diseases and cardiac stress responses, highlighting its potential as a critical regulatory node [ ].Subsequent validation experiments confirmed that ABCC5 expression correlates with PPARγ activity and is essential for the protective effects of PPARγ overexpression in heat-stressed cardiomyocytes.

4.3

## ABCC5 is a direct transcriptional target of PPARγ and mediates its cardioprotective effects
Using luciferase reporter assays and CUT&Tag analysis, we demonstrated that PPARγ directly binds to the promoter region of ABCC5 and drives its transcription. Functionally, ABCC5 knockdown abolished the protective effects of PPARγ overexpression on cell viability, apoptosis, oxidative stress and mitochondrial membrane potential in heat-injured HL-1 cells. These findings position ABCC5 as a critical downstream mediator through which PPARγ modulates lipid homeostasis and mitigates HS-induced cardiomyocyte injury.

4.4

## HS leads to prolonged lipid dysregulation linked to suppression of the PPARγ/ABCC5 axis
An important observation was that while short-term heat exposure did not cause overt lipid accumulation, prolonged HS (e.g., 2 weeks) resulted in significant lipid deposition in the myocardium and serum, even after normalization of core temperature and routine biochemical parameters. This suggests that metabolic dysregulation outlasts the acute hyperthermic phase. Recent studies have highlighted the sex-specific and time-dependent nature of lipid metabolic adaptation to heat exposure, often involving complex shifts in fatty acid oxidation and transport [ ]. The downregulation of the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway appears to contribute to this persistent lipid disturbance, highlighting the role of this axis in maintaining long-term metabolic balance under thermal stress.

4.5

## Therapeutic targeting of the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway
Given the central role of PPARγ downregulation in HS-induced cardiomyopathy, we evaluated the therapeutic potential of pharmacological PPARγ activation. Rosiglitazone, a thiazolidinedione agonist of PPARγ, significantly attenuated myocardial injury, improved cardiac function, and reduced lipid accumulation in HS models. Contrary to concerns that PPARγ agonists might exacerbate lipid deposition, rosiglitazone restored lipid homeostasis in the context of HS, likely by compensating for the pathological suppression of PPARγ and reactivating downstream regulators of lipid uptake and oxidation (e.g., CD36, CPT1β, PGC-1α).The activation of AMPK-PGC1α-PPAR signaling pathways has been shown to be essential for maintaining mitochondrial function and lipid balance in response to various stressors [ , ].
We also examined the lipid-lowering drug atorvastatin, which is widely used in cardiovascular disease. Although its canonical target HMG-CoA reductase is primarily hepatic, atorvastatin exhibited clear cardioprotective effects in our HS models. Interestingly, these benefits were associated with upregulation of both PPARγ and ABCC5, suggesting that atorvastatin may exert pleiotropic effects that converge on and reinforce the PPARγ/ABCC5 axis. atorvastatin exhibited clear cardioprotective effects in our HS models. This supports the growing consensus that statins possess pleiotropic anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties that can mitigate multi-organ dysfunction in acute heat injury [ ]. The protection afforded by atorvastatin was abolished by PPARγ knockdown, further supporting the functional importance of this pathway in its mechanism of action.

4.6

## Clinical implications and future directions
Rapid cooling remains the cornerstone of HS management, but adjunctive therapies that address the underlying metabolic and inflammatory disturbances are urgently needed. Our findings position PPARγ agonists and statins as promising candidates for mitigating myocardial injury in HS. Notably, recent high-level clinical evidence has reinforced the cardioprotective potential of atorvastatin (ATOR) in acute settings, such as its ability to prevent anthracycline-associated cardiac dysfunction [ ] and its long-term efficacy in managing coronary artery disease [ ]. Our study provides a mechanistic basis for these pleiotropic effects in the context of thermal stress, suggesting that ATOR exerts its protective role via the PPARγ/ABCC5 axis.
Moreover, given the pathophysiological overlap between HS and sepsis—both characterized by systemic inflammation and metabolic dysfunction—the PPARγ/ABCC5 pathway may also hold relevance for septic cardiomyopathy. Recent studies have underscored that lipid metabolic dysregulation in HS can trigger specialized forms of regulated cell death, such as ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis, which significantly contributes to both rhabdomyolysis and myocardial damage [ ]. Given that PPARγ is a master regulator of lipid metabolism and can influence ferroptosis sensitivity, further exploration into the crosstalk between the PPARγ/ABCC5 axis and ferroptotic signaling may provide additional therapeutic targets for comprehensive organ protection in HS.
It is important to acknowledge limitations. Our study focused on classic (non-exertional) HS; whether similar mechanisms operate in exertional HS remains to be determined. Furthermore, while we identified ABCC5 as a key effector, the PPARγ/ABCC5 axis is likely part of a broader regulatory network involving other lipid transporters and metabolic genes [ , ]. Future studies should explore the crosstalk between this pathway and other stress-responsive signals, as well as the potential role of PPARγ in other organs affected by HS.

5

## Conclusion
In summary, this study elucidates a previously unrecognized cardioprotective mechanism in which PPARγ, through direct transcriptional regulation of ABCC5, preserves lipid homeostasis and mitigates myocardial injury during HS. The downregulation of this axis contributes to both acute cardiac dysfunction and long-term metabolic disturbances. Pharmacological activation of PPARγ with rosiglitazone or reinforcement of the pathway with atorvastatin confers significant protection, highlighting a translatable therapeutic strategy for HS-induced cardiomyopathy. As extreme heat events become more frequent, targeting the PPARγ/ABCC5 signaling pathway may offer a promising approach to reducing the cardiovascular burden of heat-related illness.
