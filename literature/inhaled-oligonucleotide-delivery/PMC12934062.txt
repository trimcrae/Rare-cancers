ZhangMin – ]. The Global Initiative for Asthma recommends stratifying severe asthma by type 2 (T2) inflammation into T2-high and T2-low. T2-high inflammation is driven by T helper 2 (Th2) cells, group 2 innate lymphoid cells, and cytokines, including interleukin (IL)−4, IL-5, and IL-13, and is characterised by elevated blood/sputum eosinophils and/or fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO). In China, most severe asthma cases are T2-high, with eosinophilic phenotype accounting for up to 78.6% [ , ]. In T2-high asthma, airway inflammation can be further classified into eosinophil-neutrophil co-elevated (EOS+NEU+) and eosinophil-only elevated (EOS+NEU−) types. Combined elevated sputum eosinophils and neutrophils in asthma suggests lower lung function and greater healthcare utilization [ ]. However, differences in airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) between these two inflammatory types remain unclear [ , ].
AHR is a hallmark of asthma and plays a central role in its onset, progression, and exacerbations [ , ]. AHR may persist even in clinical remission [ ], making it a crucial therapeutic target. Our previous studies showed that combining large or small airway function indices with FeNO predicts AHR [ – ], suggesting a joint contribution of type 2 inflammation and airway dysfunction. While most patients with T2-high asthma respond to standard anti-eosinophilic therapies (e.g., inhaled corticosteroids, anti-IL-5, IL-4/IL-13 biologics) [ – ], quite a few failed to respond well. Patients with mixed granulocytic inflammation (elevated eosinophils and neutrophils) often show lower lung function (forced expiratory volume in 1 s [FEV1], forced vital capacity [FVC], FEV1/FVC ratio), greater corticosteroid resistance, and higher exacerbation risk. Moreover, their AHR tends to remain uncontrolled despite T2-targeted treatment [ , ]. This EOS+NEU+ endotype may thus represent a distinct subgroup that requires tailored therapies and more precise biomarkers to identify those at higher risk of progression.
NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) plays a pivotal role in asthma pathophysiology [ – ]. Upon activation, NLRP3 assembles with apoptosis-associated speck-like protein (ASC) and pro-caspase-1 to form the inflammasome, leading to caspase-1 activation and cleavage of pro-IL-1β into its mature form, which contributes to airway inflammation and AHR. Elevated NLRP3 and IL-1β levels in induced sputum correlate positively with neutrophilic inflammation, disease severity, and corticosteroid resistance [ ]. In an allergen-induced eosinophilic asthma model, e.g., house dust mite and ovalbumin (OVA), NLRP3 activation promotes inflammasome assembly and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β [ – ]. IL-1β drives neutrophil proliferation and differentiation, amplifying airway inflammation and AHR [ , ]. Inhibiting NLRP3 with agents such as MCC950 has been shown to attenuate airway inflammation and AHR [ ]. Thus, targeting NLRP3 may offer therapeutic benefits, though its precise role in regulating IL-1β release and mediating inflammation across asthma phenotypes remains unclear. Recent study have confirmed that GSDMD, the main executor of cell pyroptosis, is involved in the pathogenesis of asthma [ ]. The expression of GSDMD in the airway epithelial tissues of asthma patients significantly increases. However, the relationship between GSDMD and airway hyperresponsiveness in EOS+NEU+ asthma and its upstream regulatory mechanism remain unclear.
Accordingly, to clarify the role of NLRP3 signalling in AHR associated with different phenotypes, especially EOS+NEU− asthma, we conducted a prospective clinical study alongside mechanistic experiments. Patients with asthma were classified into non-eosinophilic (non-EOS), EOS+NEU−, and EOS+NEU+, based on complete blood counts (CBCs), induced sputum cytology and FeNO. Induced sputum inflammatory cytokines, particularly those related to NLRP3 signalling, alongside the predictors of AHR (e.g., spirometry variables reflecting large and small airway function, FeNO) were analysed. Furthermore, via in vivo airway responsiveness assessments and in vitro bronchial ring airway smooth muscle (ASM) tension assays with a multiwire myograph system, in vivo and in vitro models were used to assess the effects of inhibiting NLRP3 (via MCC950 or knockout), Gasdermin D (GSDMD) (via disulfiram or gene silencing), and IL-1β signalling (via IL-1R knockout) on AHR.

## Methods

## Part I clinical characteristics related to airway hyperresponsiveness and expression of NLRP3 pathway cytokines in induced sputum across different inflammatory phenotypes

## Study design, patient enrolment and data collection
This prospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai General Hospital (approval No. 2020 [ ]) and conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (1964, as revised). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.
Patients aged 18–75 years with a known history of asthma were enrolled in the Pulmonary Outpatient Clinic of Shanghai General Hospital between November 2021 and December 2022. Healthy controls were recruited. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplementary.
All participants underwent spirometry, a bronchodilation test, CBCs, FeNO measurement, and induced sputum analysis (Supplementary). In order to determine the activation of NLRP3/IL-1β signal, we tested the level of NLRP3 and IL-1β as well as Th1, Th2, Th17-related cytokines in the sputum supernatant via Elisa.

## Classification of asthma inflammation phenotypes based on eosinophils and neutrophils
Eosinophilic (EOS+) inflammation was defined by meeting at least one of the following criteria: sputum eosinophils ≥ 2%, peripheral blood eosinophil count ≥ 150 cells/µL or FeNO ≥ 20 parts per billion (ppb) [ ]. Patients not meeting any of these criteria were classified as non-EOS.
In the absence of a universally established cutoff for neutrophilic airway inflammation, the median sputum neutrophil percentage among all participants was used as the threshold value. The EOS+ group was further stratified into two subgroups: EOS+NEU+ (with concomitant neutrophilic inflammation) and EOS+NEU− (without neutrophilic inflammation).
Intergroup comparisons were performed between controls and patients with asthma, as well as among asthma subgroups, focusing on potential AHR predictors: demographic characteristics, peripheral blood eosinophil count, FeNO concentration, sputum cell differentials, spirometry indices, and inflammatory factors related to NLRP3 signalling.

## Part II role of NLRP3 on asthma epithelial pyroptosis

## Mice
Five- to six-week-old specific pathogen-free C57BL/6 wild-type (WT) mice NLRP3 knockout (NLRP3−/−) mice and IL-1R knockout (IL-1R−/−) mice were used. All animal procedures were approved by the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Shanghai General Hospital and conducted in accordance with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Efforts were made to minimise animal suffering and reduce the number of animals used in this study. WT mice were randomly assigned to the control, OVA, ozone, or OVA+ ozone group, as described in our previous studies, 24 h after the final OVA challenge with/without ozone exposure, mice were anesthetized and then were performed for spirometry and airway responsiveness measurement, following previously published protocols [ , ].
For GSDMD inhibition, disulfiram (50 mg/kg in corn oil; HY-B0240, MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) or vehicle was administered via intraperitoneal injection before each OVA challenge, following established protocols [ , ].

## Cell culture
16-HBE cell line were cultured in RPIM 1640 medium (Gibco, USA) with 10% foetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. To mimic EOS+NEU+ inflammation, HBEs were stimulated with IL-13 and IL-17 A (100 ng/mL each, R&D System). For inhibition studies, 10 µM MCC950 (MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China), a selective NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor, was added before cytokine stimulation.

## Bronchial ring Preparation and myograph analysis
Mouse bronchial rings (approximately 2 mm) were dissected and incubated in a four-channel organ bath system (Danish Myo Technology, Aarhus, Denmark) containing normal saline solution (0.9% NaCl) at 37 °C, gassed with 95% O2 and 5% CO2. Rings were mounted on an isometric myograph, with tension recorded using LabChart software (AD Instruments Ltd., Hastings, UK). Optimal resting tension was defined as the minimal passive stretch required to achieve maximal active force generation, beyond which additional stretch produced no further increase in contractile force [ ].
Acetylcholine concentration-response curves were generated and fitted using nonlinear regression. Bronchial reactivity was assessed by calculating pEC50, the negative logarithm of the concentration of acetylcholine causing 50% maximal contraction. Additional methodological details are available in the Supplementary.

## Statistical analyses
Baseline characteristics were summarised descriptively. Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed data are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data are expressed as median (interquartile range, IQR).
Two-group comparisons used the Mann–Whitney U test (continuous) and Fisher’s exact test (categorical). For comparisons across > 2 groups, a one-way ANOVA or chi-square test was used.
Spearman’s correlation coefficients were calculated and visualised using OriginPro 2022 (64-bit) SR1 (version 9.9.0.225, OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, Mass). All other statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 5.01. Statistical significance was defined with two-sided p-values < 0.05.

## Results

## Part I. EOS+NEU+ group is characterised by enhanced airway Inflammation, severe airflow Limitation, and activation of NLRP3 signalling

## Baseline characteristics
We enrolled 56 participants, including eight controls and 48 patients with asthma. 87.5% of the enrolled patients were classified as GINA Step 3, while only 12.5% were Step 4. Notably, all Step 4 patients were exclusively in the eosinophilic asthma group, and among different inflammatory phenotypes, the mixed inflammation group (EOS+NEU+) had the highest proportion of Step 4 patients, reaching 22.22%. Among the patients with asthma, 38 (79.17%) exhibited eosinophilic inflammation and were further stratified using the median sputum neutrophil percentage (32.62%) into EOS+NEU− (n = 20) and EOS+NEU+ (n = 18) subgroups (Table  ).

 

Compared with controls, patients with asthma had a higher sputum eosinophil count and a trend toward elevated FeNO. The non-EOS group had no significant differences in both counts and percentages of peripheral eosinophils or FeNO levels compared to controls. In contrast to the non-EOS group, both EOS+ subgroups demonstrated significantly elevated peripheral eosinophil parameters (absolute counts and percentages) and FeNO levels (all p < 0.01). No significant differences in these eosinophilic markers were observed between EOS+NEU− and EOS+NEU+ subgroups, suggesting that concomitant neutrophilic inflammation does not influence eosinophil-associated biomarkers. The proportion of participants with elevated FeNO (> 20 ppb) was comparable between the EOS+NEU+ (72.22%) and EOS+NEU− (65%) subgroups (p = 0.73).
Sputum neutrophil parameters were similar between the overall asthma and control groups and between the non-EOS and EOS+ asthma subgroups. However, the EOS+NEU+ subgroup showed markedly elevated neutrophilic inflammation, with both the highest neutrophil percentage (40.16% vs. 16.6% in EOS+NEU−, 33.47% in non-EOS and 30.12% in controls; all p < 0.001) and absolute sputum neutrophil counts (248.6 × 104/L vs. 45.76 × 104/L in EOS+NEU−, 87.80 × 104/L in non-EOS and 105.5 × 104/L in controls; all p < 0.001).
The EOS+NEU+ group showed elevated neutrophils and a higher sputum eosinophil count and percentage than those in the EOS+NEU− group (78.62 × 104/L vs. 15.67 × 104/L; 13.40% vs. 5.71%; p < 0.001), suggesting a distinct “dual eosinophil-neutrophil” pattern and potential synergistic inflammatory effect.
All asthma subgroups demonstrated reduced large and small airway function compared with controls, with greater impairment in EOS+ subgroups. The EOS+NEU+ group showed the lowest FEV1/FVC ratio (79.02%), which was significantly lower than that in the EOS+NEU− group (82.53%; p = 0.018). Maximal expiratory flow at 50% of vital capacity (MEF50) was reduced in the EOS+NEU+ group (84.7%), while both maximal expiratory flow at 25% (MEF25) and maximal mid-expiratory flow (MMEF) showed trends toward lower values (61.45% and 75.65% predicted, respectively) compared with those in the EOS+NEU− group. Small airway dysfunction (SAD), defined as abnormalities in ≥ 2 of MEF50, MEF25, or MMEF (< 70% predicted) [ ], was more prevalent in the EOS+NEU+ group (61.1% vs. 35.0%).

## Differences in NLRP3 Pathway-Associated inflammatory mediators across asthma inflammatory phenotypes
Compared with controls, participants with asthma showed elevated NLRP3 levels in induced sputum supernatants (2.77 ± 4.14 ng/mL vs. 23.83 ± 49.48 ng/mL). NLRP3 expression increased progressively from non-EOS (8.78 ± 14.12 ng/mL) to EOS+NEU− (34.67 ± 49.96 ng/mL) and further in EOS+NEU+ (40.27 ± 88.52 ng/mL) (Table  ). IL-18 was elevated in patients with asthma and higher in the EOS+NEU+ group than that in the EOS+NEU− group. IL-1β trended upward but showed no significant difference between the subgroups.

 

No significant differences in Th1, T2, or Th17 cytokines were observed across asthma subgroups or between participants with asthma and controls (Table  ).

## Associations between sputum NLRP3 markers and clinical Characteristics, sputum inflammatory Biomarkers, and lung function variables
NLRP3 levels were not associated with age, body mass index, or sex (median: males 18.69 ng/mL vs. females 15.17 ng/mL; p = 0.389).
In induced sputum, NLRP3 correlated positively with IL-1β (r = 0.42), IL-18 (r = 0.40), IL-6 (r = 0.41) and IL-8 (r = 0.45; all p < 0.01), but showed no correlation with Th1 (IFN-γ), Th2 (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13) or Th17 (IL-17 A) cytokines (Fig.  a).

 

Sputum NLRP3 levels correlated positively with counts and percentages of peripheral/sputum eosinophils and FeNO (r ≥ 0.37). IL-1β correlated positively with FeNO (r = 0.26) and sputum neutrophils (rcounts = 0.28, rpercentage = 0.31; p < 0.05), suggesting its involvement in both eosinophil-neutrophil inflammation. IL-18 correlated with FeNO (r = 0.35) but not with neutrophils, suggesting functional divergence. Given the stronger dual association of IL-1β, it may better reflect NLRP3-mediated dual inflammation.
Both NLRP3 (r = −0.34) and IL-1β (r = −0.3) correlated negatively with baseline FEV1/FVC (p < 0.05, Fig.  c). NLRP3 correlated negatively with small airway indices, including MEF50 (r = −0.40), MEF25 (r = −0.30) and MMEF (r = −0.37; all p < 0.05). IL-1β demonstrated weaker but significant correlations (r = −0.34, −0.27, and − 0.28 for MEF50, MEF25, and MMEF, respectively).

## Part II: NLRP3/GSDMD/IL-1β signalling activation contributes to AHR in EOS+NEU+ inflammation asthmatic mice

## EOS+NEU+ asthmatic mice exhibit AHR with elevated epithelial NLRP3 expression
We established asthma models using OVA with or without ozone exposure (Fig.  a). HE staining confirmed OVA sensitisation/challenge predominantly induced eosinophilic inflammation (EOS+NEU−) (Fig.  b, c), while OVA plus ozone-induced mixed eosinophil-neutrophil inflammation (EOS+NEU+) (Fig.  b, d).

 

Compared to controls, EOS+NEU− mice exhibited elevated IL-4, IL-13, and IL-17 A in BALF. Ozone alone had minimal cytokine effects, whereas EOS+NEU+ mice showed further cytokine upregulation. Histopathological analysis revealed moderate mucus hypersecretion and subepithelial collagen deposition in EOS+NEU− mice. These pathological features were markedly worse in EOS+NEU+ mice, which exhibited severe mucin accumulation and peribronchial fibrosis (Fig.  e-j).
Although ozone alone did not significantly increase airway resistance, both EOS+ groups showed AHR (Fig.  k, l). In addition, EOS+NEU− mice had reduced large airway index (FEV25/FVC) and small airway indices (FEF50 and MMEF). EOS+NEU+ mice displayed severe large airway dysfunction (LAD) and SAD, surpassing ozone-only or EOS+NEU− groups (Fig.  m-p).
NLRP3 expression at both mRNA and protein levels was upregulated in lung tissues of EOS+NEU− mice, with further elevation observed in EOS+NEU+ mice (Fig.  q, r). IL-1β levels in BALF increased significantly (Fig.  j), consistent with patient data. Immunofluorescence localisation revealed predominant NLRP3 expression in airway epithelial cells and, to a lesser extent, in alveolar macrophages (Fig.  s). Epithelial NLRP3 expression was markedly increased in both EOS+NEU− and EOS+NEU+ mice.
These findings indicate that OVA sensitisation/challenge combined with ozone exposure induces AHR accompanied by mixed EOS+NEU+ inflammation, concurrent LAD, and SAD, along with upregulated NLRP3 expression in airway epithelial cells.

## GSDMD-mediated epithelial pyroptosis contributes to EOS+NEU+ asthma
To investigate the role of NLRP3 signalling in airway epithelial cells under EOS+NEU+ inflammation, HBEs were stimulated with IL-13 and IL-17 A. After 24 h of co-stimulation with cytokine combinations, NLRP3 and cleaved caspase-1 were activated (Fig.  a), accompanied by more secretion of IL-1β and elevated LDH release into cell culture supernatants compared with single stimulation(Fig.  b, c). Scanning electron microscopy revealed membrane pore formation (Fig.  d), suggesting pyroptosis. This was further supported by significant upregulation of the pyroptosis-executing GSDMD-NT, while full-length GSDMD (GSDMD-FL) protein levels remained unchanged.

 

In vivo, EOS+NEU+ mice exhibited significant upregulation of GSDMD-NT and cleaved caspase-1 proteins, as well as increased mRNA expression of GSDMD, caspase-1, and IL-1β in lung tissue (Fig.  e, g-i). Transmission electron microscopy further revealed the disappearance of junctional proteins between airway epithelial cells, indicating compromised barrier integrity (Fig.  f).
Collectively, these findings demonstrate that NLRP3 inflammasome activation and GSDMD-mediated airway epithelial pyroptosis, coupled with IL-1β secretion, contribute significantly to the pathogenesis of mixed EOS+NEU+ asthma.

## Inhibition of NLRP3 reduces GSDMD-mediated epithelial pyroptosis and ameliorates AHR
To determine whether NLRP3 regulates GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis and contributes to AHR, NLRP3−/− mice were used. Genotyping was performed via PCR (S-Figure ). Following OVA/ozone exposure, NLRP3⁻/⁻ mice showed no significant improvement in large airway function (FEV25) or small airway function (FEF50, MMEF) compared to WT mice (S-Figure ). However, they showed reduced airway resistance and increased logPC100, indicating attenuated AHR (Fig.  a, b). Peribronchial eosinophil/neutrophil infiltration and mucus production were decreased (Fig.  c-g). Compared to WT, OVA/ozone-exposed NLRP3−/− mice presented lower IL-4 and IL-17 A levels in both lung tissue samples and BALF (Fig.  i, j), reduced caspase-1 and IL-1β expression (Fig.  k, l), and suppressed epithelial GSDMD-NT expression revealed via immunofluorescence (Fig.  h).

 

In vitro, MCC950 pretreatment reduced IL-13/IL-17 A-induced GSDMD-NT and cleaved caspase-1 in HBEs, with diminished GSDMD-NT fluorescence (Fig.  m, n). Additionally, supernatant IL-1β and LDH levels—key pyroptosis-related markers—were significantly decreased (S-Figure ).
These findings demonstrate that NLRP3 inflammasome activation promotes GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis and contributes to AHR. Pharmacological inhibition of NLRP3 effectively alleviates both airway epithelial cell death and AHR, suggesting a causal role in asthma pathogenesis.

## GSDMD Inhibition reduces IL-1β release and improves AHR
Disulfiram, a GSDMD inhibitor that blocks membrane pore formation, was administered at a previously validated dose (50 mg/kg) [ ] in EOS+NEU+ mice. Treatment significantly improved large airway function (FEV25/FVC) and restored small airway indices (FEF50, MMEF, and FEF75) (Fig. a-d). Airway resistance was reduced, as shown by increased logPC100 values (Fig. e, f), indicating alleviated AHR. Peripheral eosinophil/neutrophil infiltration and mucus hypersecretion were markedly decreased (Fig. g-i). Disulfiram further lowered IL-4, IL-5, and IL-1β levels in BALF (Fig. j-l).

 

In vitro, GSDMD small interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection of HBEs for 24 h followed by IL-13/IL-17 A stimulation significantly reduced IL-1β release (Fig.  m, n). These in vivo and in vitro findings demonstrated that GSDMD inhibition reduces IL-1β release, ameliorating AHR in EOS+NEU+ asthma.

## IL-1R Blockade mitigates bronchial contraction and AHR
To assess the role of IL-1β in AHR, IL-1R−/− mice were subjected to OVA sensitisation/challenge plus ozone exposure. Compared to WT mice, IL-1R−/− mice showed significantly improved large airway (FEV25/FVC) and small airway (FEF50, MMEF and FEF75) indices (Fig.  a-d), reduced peribronchial eosinophil/neutrophil infiltration, lower BALF IL-4, IL-5 and IL-1β levels; and alleviated mucus hypersecretion (Fig.  h-m). Concurrently, airway resistance decreased, with elevated logPC100 values indicating improved AHR (Fig.  e, f).

 

In vitro bronchial ring myography demonstrated reduced contraction in IL-1R−/− mice compared to WT after OVA/ozone exposure (Fig.  g). These findings indicate that IL-1β directly contributes to the development of AHR, as evidenced by the significant improvement in lung function following IL-1R blockade.

## Discussion
This study integrated a prospective cohort with in vitro and in vivo models to investigate the role of the NLRP3/GSDMD/IL-1β signalling in AHR in EOS+NEU+ asthma. Compared to patients with EOS+NEU− inflammation, the patient with the EOS+NEU+ phenotype showed more severe eosinophil-neutrophil inflammation and greater impairment of large/small airway function, contributing to worse AHR. Induced sputum NLRP3 expression was significantly elevated in patients with asthma, increasing progressively across non-EOS, EOS+NEU−, and EOS+NEU+ groups, with similar trends for downstream IL-1β,while T2 related cytokines failed to differentiate in EOS+NEU−, and EOS+NEU+ group. Sputum NLRP3 levels correlated positively with blood/sputum eosinophils and FeNO; IL-1β correlated with FeNO and sputum neutrophils, indicating dual-inflammatory involvement. Both NLRP3 and IL-1β correlated negatively with lung function, though IL-1β showed weaker correlations. Mechanistic studies confirmed the involvement of NLRP3/IL-1β pathway in AHR. In EOS+NEU+ mice and HBEs in dual-inflammation condition stimulated by IL-13 and IL-17 A, we investigated molecular mechanism by which NLRP3 regulates IL-1β, we performed the experiment by inhibiting NLRP3 to regulate GSDMD, demonstrated that inhibing NLRP3 suppresses GSDMD-NT activation, with less LDH releasing and hole-fromation in epithelial cell membrane. Inhibiting GSDMD could improved epithelial pyroptosis, reduced IL-1β and abolish the decrease of lung function and inflammation to improve AHR. Further, IL-1R knockout mice exhibited decreased airway smooth muscle tone and alleviated AHR. These findings indicate that NLRP3-driven pyroptosis exacerbates dual granulocytic inflammation and AHR. Targeting this pathway may offer a promising strategy for personalised treatment of EOS+NEU+ asthma.
First, in our prospective study, EOS+NEU+ inflammation was defined by the median sputum neutrophil percentage (32.62%) in all participants, mostly patients with mild-to-moderate asthma. By contrast, the Severe Asthma Research Program (SARP) by the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, which focused on severe asthma, defined EOS+NEU+ as sputum eosinophils ≥2% and neutrophils ≥40% [ ]. Given our smaller cohort, a lower neutrophil cutoff was deemed appropriate. Using this cutoff, 47.37% (18/38) of patients with eosinophilic asthma were classified as EOS+NEU+, showing neutrophilic inflammation and significantly higher sputum eosinophils, peripheral eosinophils, as well as FeNO levels than those of the EOS+NEU− group, suggesting synergistic amplification of eosinophil-neutrophil inflammation. Patients with mixed EOS+NEU+ inflammation may develop a more severe stage, requiring higher-step therapeutic strategies.
In the SARP study, EOS+NEU+ inflammation was associated with lower lung function, steroid resistance, increased exacerbation risk, and greater healthcare utilization [ , , ]. Similarly, we observed more severe airway obstruction and a higher prevalence of SAD in patients with EOS+NEU+ phenotype. Unlike SARP, which only focused on large airway impairment, our study revealed greater reductions in small airway indices (MEF50 and MMEF) in EOS+NEU+ versus EOS+NEU−, indicating the presence of neutrophils further aggravate the damage to lung function. Previous research also showed that neutrophils infiltration in airways is associated with increased Th1/IFN-γ and Th17/IL-17 response and it exerts additional adverse influences, including severe airway inflammation, mucus hypersecretion mediated by neutrophil elastase, increased airway smooth muscle responsiveness, and a rapid decline in lung function [ ]. Both SAD and airway inflammation, as measured by FeNO, were strongly predictive of AHR, as shown in our previous studies [ ], suggesting that eosinophil-neutrophil co-inflammation aggravates airway dysfunction and contributes to more severe AHR, potentially accelerating asthma progression.
NLRP3 was expressed in patients with both EOS+NEU− and EOS+NEU+ asthma, independent of BMI or age, but was significantly higher in the EOS+NEU+ group. Previous studies reported heightened sensitivity of peripheral blood mononuclear cells to NLRP3 inflammasome activation in severe asthma—with increased IL-1β release—correlating with airway eosinophil and neutrophil levels [ ]. This suggests that NLRP3 activation is enhanced in mixed granulocytic inflammation and may serve as a shared therapeutic target across inflammatory subtypes. In our study, sputum NLRP3 in the EOS+NEU+ group correlated positively with sputum eosinophils, neutrophils and FeNO, and correlated negatively with FEV1/FVC, MEF50, and MMEF. Additionally, the downstream effector IL-1β correlated positively with FeNO and sputum neutrophils, indicating involvement in eosinophil-neutrophil inflammation. The parallel increase in NLRP3 and IL-1β alongside worsening LAD and SAD supports a role for NLRP3 in promoting inflammation and airway obstruction via IL-1β, contributing to AHR. Other cytokines related to Th1, Th2 and Th17 signalling failed to distinguish the EOS+NEU− and EOS+NEU+ inflammation. These findings provide clinical evidence linking NLRP3 and IL-1β signalling to EOS+NEU+ asthma pathogenesis which may be promising biomakers to predict AHR of EOS+NEU+ patients.
To validate this hypothesis, we established an EOS+NEU+ asthma mouse model using OVA + ozone exposure and assessed airway inflammation and AHR. Mice exhibited worsened LAD and SAD, increased peribronchial eosinophil-neutrophil infiltration, heightened AHR, and further upregulation of NLRP3 mRNA and protein compared to the EOS+NEU− model—consistent with clinical observations. In EOS+NEU+ group, the expression of cleaved Caspase-1 in lung tissue was higher, and GSDMD-NT expression also increasing more. As well, we found a higher level of IL-1β in EOS+NEU+ asthma mouse model.
IL-13 and IL-17 A stimulation in HBEs mimicking EOS+NEU+ inflammation induced marked NLRP3 expression and more obvious membrane pore formation with higher level of IL-1β and LDH releasing compared with single IL-13 or IL-17 A, indicating epithelial pyroptosis. Pyroptosis, a form of programmed cell death and a key source of IL-1β release, depends on membrane pore formation mediated by the gasdermin family, particularly GSDMD [ – ]. Evidence of pyroptosis was supported using electron microscopy, which revealed epithelial pore formation, disrupted junctions, and swollen mitochondria. GSDMD-NT expression was increased. These findings suggest that under EOS+NEU+ inflammation, NLRP3 promotes epithelial pyroptosis and IL-1β release, contributing to AHR.
Inhibition of NLRP3 alleviates allergen-induced eosinophilic inflammation—typically EOS+NEU− phenotype—and AHR in asthma models [ , ], but its role in EOS+NEU+ inflammatory remains unclear. Using NLRP3-knockout mice and in vitro inhibition with MCC950, we demonstrated that NLRP3 deficiency in the EOS+NEU+ model reduced AHR and epithelial GSDMD-NT expression. MCC950 lowered IL-1β and LDH levels in cell supernatants. While previous studies have reported NLRP3-driven epithelial pyroptosis in eosinophilic asthma models (e.g. house dust mites or toluene diisocyanate-induced) [ , ], our findings extend this evidence to EOS+NEU+ asthma, offering both clinical and mechanistic insight.
While NLRP3 knockout alleviated AHR, it failed to restore lung function in asthmatic mice. In contrast, direct inhibition of GSDMD alleviated AHR, LAD, and SAD, suggesting that GSDMD-mediated epithelial pyroptosis may occur via NLRP3-independent pathways. For example, heat shock protein 90α upregulation in epithelia was previously reported to activate the cGAS-STING pathway, promoting GSDMD-driven pyroptosis [ ]. Thus, directly targeting GSDMD or IL-1β may be more effective for controlling AHR in EOS+NEU+ asthma than inhibiting NLRP3 alone.
Using bronchial provocation, we confirmed that IL-1R knockout alleviated AHR in EOS+NEU+ mice. Additionally, a multiwire myograph system was used to measure bronchial ring ASM tension in vitro, and we demonstrated for the first time that IL-1R knockout significantly reduced bronchial smooth muscle contractility in vitro. Mechanistically, IL-1β binding to IL-1R triggers Toll/IL-1 receptor domain signalling [ , ], activating eosinophils to release major basic protein, which disrupts M2 receptors regulation and promotes acetylcholine release and muscle contraction [ ]. IL-1β facilitates Th17 differentiation and neutrophil recruitment [ , ]. These findings highlight IL-1β/IL-1R as a promising target for AHR treatment in EOS+NEU+ asthma.
This study has certain limitations: (1) although macrophages express NLRP3, their role in our model requires further validation; (2) ozone and OVA exposure may induce pyroptosis and IL-1β release via alternative pathways contributing to AHR. Future studies incorporating single-cell sequencing and conditional knockout models are warranted to elucidate the cell-specific functions of NLRP3.

## Conclusion
Both clinical and animal studies confirmed that the EOS+NEU+ phenotype is associated with enhanced AHR. In patients with EOS+NEU+ asthma, elevated levels of NLRP3 and IL-1β in induced sputum were negatively correlated with lung function, suggesting their potential as biomarkers for this inflammatory subtype and AHR severity. Animal experiments further demonstrated that NLRP3 contributes to AHR by promoting GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis and IL-1β release, which enhances eosinophil activation and neutrophil recruitment, exacerbating airway obstruction. Targeting this pathway may offer a novel therapeutic strategy for asthma, particularly for patients with EOS+NEU+ inflammation.
