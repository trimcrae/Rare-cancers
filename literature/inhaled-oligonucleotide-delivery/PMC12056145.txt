## Abstract
Surgical aortic valve replacement (AVR) is the standard of care for severe aortic valve disease; however, post-operative complications continue to pose challenges. Inflammation caused by increased release of cell-free mitochondrial DNA (cf-mtDNA) during surgery may be a contributing factor. The aim of this pilot study was to investigate the relationship between cf-mtDNA release and the risk of inflammatory-mediated post-operative complications in adult AVR patients. Plasma was collected from patients undergoing an AVR with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) at baseline, intra-operatively and post ICU admission at 6, 12 and 18 h. Cf-mtDNA was quantified and inflammatory biomarker expression in leukocytes and plasma was assessed. Cf-mtDNA levels increased 16-fold at the end of CPB with a corresponding increase in average fragment size. Cf-mtDNA levels may be associated with post-operative bleeding, infection, hepatic failure and hospital length of stay. Gene expression and plasma analysis revealed altered levels of markers associated with inflammation and innate immune responses. Several markers were associated with post-operative complications and outcomes. Our results indicate that cf-mtDNA levels, including average fragment sizes, are increased following initiation of CPB in AVR patients, and may be associated with post-operative complications and outcomes. However, larger studies are needed to validate these findings.

## Introduction
Surgical aortic valve replacement (AVR) is a life-saving treatment for aortic valve disease; the most common cardiac valve disorder in the developed world . However, approximately 50% of patients will develop a heightened inflammatory state in the post-operative period and 11% will develop persistent global inflammation, known as systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) , . SIRS pathophysiology results in activation of innate and adaptive inflammatory mediators, disruption to coagulation and fibrinolysis and impaired endothelial function . It is associated with a multitude of downstream complications including post-operative atrial fibrillation (POAF), acute kidney injury (AKI) and multiple organ dysfunction (MODS) . Onset is primarily attributed to the release of intracellular molecules known as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) in response to surgical trauma, ischemia-reperfusion injury and alterations in physiological conditions caused by the cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) circuit .
Early recognition and appropriate management of SIRS is crucial for improving patient outcomes and a biomarker that can predict its onset is invaluable. Emerging evidence indicates that a pro-inflammatory molecule, cell-free mitochondrial DNA (cf-mtDNA) could be a promising candidate due to its recognized involvement in innate immune modulation. Cf-mtDNA is released following apoptosis/necrosis or active cellular secretion and accumulates due to overwhelmed or impaired clearance mechanisms . This activates several key pathways including Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR-9), Nod-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) and cyclic GMP–AMP synthase stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS-STING) , . Activation results in increased levels of several proinflammatory cytokines including tumor necrosis factor-α (TNFα), interleukin (IL) 1β, IL6, IL18 and regulated upon activation normal T-cell expressed and secreted factor (RANTES) . Furthermore, in vivo studies have demonstrated that the injection of mtDNA increases the release of pro-inflammatory matrix-metalloproteinase-8 (MMP8) and promotes neutrophil activation , . Additionally, elevated levels of cf-mtDNA have been documented in cardiac surgery involving CPB and high levels in peripheral blood may be predictive of developing new-onset POAF and AKI after cardiac surgery .
We hypothesized that cf-mtDNA released into the systemic circulation during AVR may act as a pro-inflammatory mediator, promoting a heightened inflammatory state and increasing the risk of post-operative complications. The objective was to investigate whether cf-mtDNA could be used as a prognostic marker of inflammatory-mediated post-operative complications in AVR patients.

## Results

## Patient characteristics and operative data
Baseline characteristics and operative details are summarized in Table  . The enrolled patients (n = 24) had a mean age of 64 years, a mean body mass index of 28.8, and 58% were males. Of the study cohort, 21% of patients had diabetes mellitus, 63% had hypertension and 46% had hyperlipidaemia. Mean CPB time was 69 (± 21) minutes with a mean operative time of 157 (± 32) minutes.
 

## Association of cf-mtDNA with post-operative complications and outcomes
Analysis of average fragment size revealed significant changes in total cfDNA size with cardioplegia delivery (mean baseline 243 ± 16 to 359 ± 20 bp) with a corresponding decrease in % cfDNA (85 ± 4 to 61 ± 7%; p < 0.0001 both) (Fig.  a). There was a significant and sustained increase in cf-mtDNA levels (Fig.  b) over time (p < 0.001) peaking at the end of CPB (mean baseline 472 ± 68 to 7722 ± 3896 copies/µL). Univariable mixed effect modelling was used to assess the correlation between circulating cf-mtDNA levels and post-operative complications and outcomes (Table  ). There was a trend for an association between cf-mtDNA levels with bleeding (p = 0.057), infection (p = 0.067), hepatic failure (p = 0.057) and hospital length of stay (p = 0.084).
 

## Altered expression of leukocyte markers in AVR patients and association with post-operative complications and outcomes
White blood cell numbers (primarily granulocytes) increased over time, peaking 6 h post ICU admission (mean baseline 3.4 ± 1.2 to 12.3 ± 3.1; p < 0.0001 vs. baseline). Several genes associated with inflammation and innate immune responses in leukocytes were differentially expressed including increased levels of IL1β and NFκB (both p < 0.001) over time, increased IL10 (p < 0.01) and NLRP3 (p = 0.002) post-surgery and decreased expression of TNFα, IL8, CCL5/RANTES, TLR9 and CD40L (all p < 0.001) as well as IL18 (p = 0.046) and PF4 (p = 0.035) (Fig.  ). There were no significant changes in the levels of P-Selectin or HMGB1, while IL6 levels were below the limit of detection. Several inflammatory markers were associated with post-operative complications and outcomes including renal failure (CCL5/RANTES, TNFα, TLR9, IL8, MPO, PF4), hepatic failure (TNFα), atrial fibrillation (NLRP3), infection (IL18, TNFα, TLR9, IL8), bleeding (HMGB1), ICU (P-Selectin, PF4) and hospital length of stay (TNFα, NLRP3, IL1β, HMGB1) (Table  ).
 

## Increased release of cytokines, chemokines and platelet activation in AVR patients and association with post-operative complications and outcomes
Inflammatory responses in plasma were characterized by significantly altered levels from baseline over time. Levels of the cytokines TNFα, IL6, IL8, IL1β, CD40L, IL18, chemokine (IL8) and platelet activation marker P-Selectin (p < 0.001 for all) were significantly increased post-surgery while levels of the anti-inflammatory IL10 (p < 0.001) increased at the end of CPB (Fig.  ). Several inflammatory markers were associated with post-operative complications including renal failure (IL1β, P-Selectin, CD40L, IL18), hepatic failure (TNFα, IL18), atrial fibrillation (IL6, IL8, IL10, IL1β, CD40L, IL18), infection (TNFα, IL8, IL1β, IL18), bleeding (TNFα, IL8, P-Selectin, CD40L, IL18), ICU (TNFα, IL10, IL1β) and hospital length of stay (TNFα, IL10, IL1β, P-Selectin, CD40, IL18) (Table  ).
 

## Increased expression and release of neutrophil activation markers in AVR patients and association with post-operative complications and outcomes
Gene expression data from leukocytes showed increased levels of neutrophil activation markers NE, MPO and MMP8 during surgery with a subsequent decrease in expression (all p < 0.001) (Fig.  a). Neutrophil markers were associated with atrial fibrillation (NE, p = 0.005; MPO p = 0.009), renal failure (MPO, p = 0.021) and infection (MPO, p = 0.007). This was associated with a corresponding increase in circulating levels of NE and MPO (p < 0.001 for both) from cardioplegia delivery to 18 h post ICU admission (Fig.  b). NE plasma levels were also associated with post-operative bleeding (p = 0.004).
 

## Discussion
The inflammatory response during surgery with CPB is a key determinant of post-operative complications. Although AVR involving CPB is a common surgical procedure, accurately predicting which patients will experience post-surgical complications remains difficult. Post-operative organ dysfunction can occur in 30–50% of patients, with atrial fibrillation and AKI frequently reported , . Current pre-operative models which use clinical risk factors, (cardiac/demographic) and pre-existing comorbidities to risk-stratify patients, tend to underestimate mortality . Emerging evidence suggests that cf-mtDNA may provide an early means of identifying patients with an elevated risk of developing complications , , . Increased cf-mtDNA levels have been reported in patients undergoing cardiac surgery and have been associated with atrial fibrillation and mortality . Our study demonstrates that cf-mtDNA might represent a feasible early biomarker to assess risk of post-operative complications in patients undergoing AVR surgery.
We reveal that initiation of CPB in AVR patients increases cf-DNA fragment size and results in a sustained increase in cf-mtDNA levels over time. This increase appears to be primarily driven by direct insult to cells, including shear stress associated with CPB and surgical trauma rather than ischemia reperfusion injury as the association between aortic cross clamp time and cf-mtDNA levels was not statistically significant. Furthermore, changes correspond with a trend toward increased risk of bleeding, infection, hepatic failure and hospital length of stay. Consistent with the literature, AVR was associated with significant changes in the expression and release of several inflammatory markers. To our knowledge this is the first study to provide evidence that cfDNA fragment size is increased following initiation of CPB in SAVR patients (Fig.  a), a finding which presents novel insight into the dynamic nature of cfDNA release and the processes governing both fragmentation and clearance . Moreover, it illustrates that the shear stress associated with mechanical circulatory devices, can promote a shift towards release of larger fragments, like di-and poly-nucleosomes. A phenomenon that may correspond with changes in the contribution from different release mechanisms, disruption to pathways including apoptosis or reflect recent evidence that fragmentation is a stepwise process, both intra- and extracellularly .
Our finding that cf-mtDNA levels increase following CPB is consistent with several previous studies , . Once released, cf-mtDNA is recognized by TLR9, cGAS-STING and the NLRP3 inflammasome, resulting in immune cell activation and upregulation of gene expression , . Despite previous reports that activation of TLR9 and NLRP3 leads to upregulation of gene expression , , our study did not show any correlation with cf-mtDNA levels. Failure to activate these pathways could be attributed to the mechanism of release, as previous studies have suggested that oxidative damage may be a critical determinant of the immunostimulatory effects of cf-mtDNA . Alternatively, as many comparative studies have been conducted in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting, it is possible that the lower level of invasiveness in combination with shorter bypass times attenuated the response. This may have prevented cf-mtDNA levels from reaching a sufficient level of saturation to evoke a strong immune response in our patient cohort. Furthermore, as clearance is regulated by the reticuloendothelial system, renal filtration, and nuclease degradation, excluding patients with pre-existing organ dysfunction may have resulted in more controlled release within our cohort . This factor is often overlooked in studies assessing the role of circulating nucleic acids in disease. Lastly, despite attempts to mitigate the effects of haemodilution using normalization to haematocrit, differences in circulating blood volume and the timing of administration of fluids between patients may contribute to additional variation in the immune and clearance response.
The release of cytokines in patients undergoing CPB is a well-known phenomenon , . Consistent with the literature our study showed increases in both pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines over time, although a slight decrease is visible following delivery of cardioplegia despite correction for haemodilution. This observation may be attributed to attenuation of the inflammatory process by procedural factors including the anaesthetic drug regimen, hypothermic CPB and cardioprotective effects associated with cardioplegia delivery . Levels of anti-inflammatory IL10 are also reduced, supporting a temporary decrease in inflammation, but rises again at the end of CPB, to counteract the ensuing inflammatory response. Our analysis revealed that increasing levels of the inflammatory markers IL1β, IL6, IL8, IL10, IL18, TNFα, P-Selectin, CD40L play a role in the progression of post-operative complications including POAF, bleeding, infection, renal and hepatic failure. Of the six patients that experienced post-operative bleeding, five required administration of blood bank products, and one underwent re-operative exploration but did not require administration of blood bank products. Our findings reflect previous studies linking inflammatory cytokines to AKI and hepatic failure post cardiac surgery. Our finding that increased levels of IL6 and TNFα are associated with POAF is supported by Wu et al., (2013) however, other studies have failed to show an association . Differences may arise from variations in the study population, selection criteria, observation period, or patient management including the use of a prophylactic medications which reduce POAF incidence. POAF typically occurs on post-operative day (POD) two, with 70% of cases occurring by day four, therefore shorter observation periods may underestimate incidence . One third of cases (n = 2) were observed on POD two in our cohort, with the remainder (n = 4) witnessed on POD three. Half of all individuals were administered prophylactic beta-blockers, which have potential anti-inflammatory effects . Despite this, a clear association between increased IL6 and TNFα persisted.
Activation of neutrophils during surgery involving CPB is widely reported and plays a pivotal role in adverse outcomes . Furthermore, interest in the role of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) in development of post-operative complications following CPB has grown. NETs are composed of a nuclear DNA and mtDNA backbone and are a major source of cfDNA. Previous studies indicate that NETs and higher post-operative neutrophil/lymphocyte ratios are associated with POAF post cardiac surgery . The neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio is also an independent predictor of post-operative acute kidney injury . Consistent with neutrophil activation, we witnessed an elevation in granulocyte numbers combined with increased expression and circulating levels of neutrophil elastase (NE) and MPO. Moreover, neutrophil markers were associated with POAF, renal failure and infection. An association with cfDNA, suggests that NETs were not the primary source of release. It is possible that in our patient cohort neutrophils released less cfDNA or were trapped in the microvasculature preventing release of cfDNA into circulation.
As a single-centre observational study with a small sample size our study has several limitations. First, small sample size limited the ability to interpret sex-specific differences in cf-mtDNA levels. Second, although our decision to assess changes relative to a baseline measurement taken at admission, rather than comparison with a heathy control cohort is widely used, it does not negate the potential bias of confounders that are not eliminated by the study selection criteria. Third, the limited number of post-operative complications and outcomes may affect the generalizability of the data. Therefore, the results should be considered preliminary. Lastly, our study employed strict exclusion criteria focusing on a low-risk cohort. Including higher-risk patients could improve stratification and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the entire AVR population. Ultimately larger prospective studies are required to validate these findings.
In conclusion, we have demonstrated that average cfDNA fragment sizes and cf-mtDNA levels increase during AVR surgery and may be associated with post-operative complications and outcomes including bleeding, infection, hepatic failure and hospital length of stay. Development of a biomarker panel may improve sensitivity, specificity and diagnostic value in this patient cohort. Further investigation is needed to validate these findings.

## Methods

## Patient selection
This prospective observational single-centre study included adult patients (> 18 years) undergoing AVR with CPB (n = 24). The research protocol was approved by the Metro North Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC/20/TPCH/60634) and ratified by the University of Queensland (2020/HE000689-60634). All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to commencement of study procedures. Patients who had re-operative surgery, concomitant procedures, pre-existing kidney or liver dysfunction, active infection, anti-platelet (except aspirin) therapy, moderate or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, recent treatment with immunosuppressants, acute coronary syndrome or an abnormal ventricle (left ventricular ejection fraction < 50%) were excluded from the study.

## Isolated surgical aortic valve replacement procedure
Induction of anaesthesia was performed using a combination of midazolam, fentanyl, propofol and non-depolarizing muscle relaxant (rocuronium, vecuronium or cisatracurium). Anaesthesia was maintained with inhaled gases (sevoflurane or isoflurane) along with low dose propofol infusion prior to CPB and bi-spectral index titrated propofol infusion thereafter (on-CPB, and in the ICU). In all cases SAVR was carried out via full median sternotomy. CPB management included patient cooling on CPB to 32 °C for the SAVR, standard cardioplegia (Baxter cardioplegia AHK5534 ‘Monash Medical Centre Solution’ with procaine) dosing of 1000 mL upon initial cross-clamping of the aorta, and repeat dosing every 20–25 min thereafter with 500 mL of cardioplegia. Both antegrade and retrograde cardioplegia was utilized for all cases – initial dosing 500 mL antegrade and 500 mL retrograde cardioplegia with the retrograde route used for all recurrent dosing.

## Post-operative care
Prior to ICU arrival all patients received low dose propofol infusion either with or without the addition of dexmedetomidine dependant on individual requirements. Following arrival, patients received a nurse-controlled analgesia regimen of oxycodone with 1 mg boluses of opioid available at 5 min intervals, titrated to respiratory rate, haemodynamic parameters and patient pain scores. Following extubation, patients received either oral oxycodone or sublingual buprenorphine dependent on individual needs.

## Blood collection and processing
Whole blood samples were collected into 10 mL EDTA tubes (BD-Plymouth, UK) and processed at the following time points: before surgery (after insertion of an intra-arterial catheter prior to commencement of surgery), after sternotomy, post first cardioplegia delivery, at the end of CPB (just prior to weaning) and 6, 12 and 18 h post ICU admission (± 2 h). Samples were immediately centrifuged at 3000 x g, 15 min, 4 °C before being fractionated. The upper 3 mL of plasma was transferred into nuclease-free 1.5mL microcentrifuge tubes and centrifuged at 16,000 x g, 10 min, 4 °C. The top 80% was pooled into a sterile 10 mL tube, briefly vortexed and stored at – 80 °C until cfDNA extraction. Platelet poor plasma was obtained following additional centrifugation at 3000 x g, 15 min, 4 °C and stored at – 80 °C for inflammatory, neutrophil and platelet marker analysis. Buffy coat was resuspended in RBC lysis buffer (Abcam, VIC, Australia) and left at room temperature for 10 min. Following incubation, the solution was centrifuged at 500 x g for 10 min and the supernatant was discarded. The resultant pellet was washed in sterile phosphate buffered saline (PBS), centrifuged at 500 x g for 5 min and resuspended in TRIzol Reagent for gene expression analysis (ThermoFisher Scientific, VIC, Australia).

## Cell-free DNA extraction
CfDNA was isolated from 2 mL of plasma using the QIAamp Circulating Nucleic Acid kit (Qiagen, VIC, Australia) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Total cfDNA was quantified with a 1X dsDNA High Sensitivity assay (ThermoFisher Scientific, MA, USA) on a Qubit 4 Fluorometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, MA, USA). Fragment size and percentage cfDNA was assessed using a cell-free DNA ScreenTape Assay (Agilent, CA, USA) on a 4150 TapeStation (Agilent, CA, USA).

## Absolute quantification of Cf-mtDNA
Custom PrimeTime qPCR probe assays (Integrated DNA Technologies, USA) targeting the mitochondrial genes cytochrome B (MT-CYB) and Cytochrome C oxidase II (MT-CO2) were designed using Primer3Plus. Sequences are provided in Supplementary Table . Absolute quantification was performed in accordance with MIQE guidelines using serial dilution of a custom gBlock gene fragment (Integrated DNA Technologies, USA) (Supplementary Table ) and PrimeTime Gene Expression Master Mix (Integrated DNA Technologies, USA) on a ViiATM7 Real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, MA, USA).

## RNA extraction and gene expression analysis
RNA was isolated using phenol-chloroform extraction and contaminating DNA was removed using a TURBO DNA-free™ kit (ThermoFisher Scientific, VIC, Australia). Clean-up was performed using 3 M pH 5.2 sodium acetate (Invitrogen, MA, USA) followed by ethanol precipitation. RNA was quantified using a NanoDrop One (ThermoFisher Scientific, VIC, Australia) and RNA integrity number was determined on a 4150 TapeStation (Agilent, CA, USA). The expression of 18 genes of interest (Supplementary Table ) and the six reference genes was assessed on an nCounter® SPRINT profiler (NanoString Technologies, WA, USA). Assays were constructed using nCounter® Elements™ 24-plex TagSets and custom capture and reporter oligonucleotide probes designed by NanoString Technologies (Seattle, USA) and manufactured by Integrated DNA Technologies (IA, USA). Reporter and capture probe sequences are provided in Supplementary Table . Normalization and differential expression analysis were performed with nSolver™ software (version 4.0). Raw counts were normalized to the top three housekeeping genes: PGK1, LDHA, and GUSB, as determined by geNorm.

## Cytokine quantification
Samples were assayed in duplicate using custom Luminex Magpix-based assays (R&D Systems, MN, USA), in accordance with manufacturer’s instructions. Analytes measured included TNFα, IL6, IL8, IL10, IL1β, CD40L, IL18, P-Selectin and myeloperoxidase (MPO). Concentrations were calculated using a 5-parameter logistic standard curve, corrected for background readings. In addition, the levels of neutrophil elastase (NE) were measured using a standard commercial (R&D Systems) enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Statistical analysis
Data were analysed using STATA™ version 17.0. Normally distributed data were summarized as mean and standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR) (non-normal distribution). Categorical data were summarized as proportion (percentage). Repeated measures ANOVA was used to detect any significant change in mean plasma or gene marker concentration over time. As the data formed a longitudinal time-series (panel dataset), the dataset was analysed using the patient ID as the panel variable and the intervention times as the temporal variable. Multivariable model building began using a fully saturated model with sequential deletion of non-significant predictors until a final model was reached. Each model iteration was checked for validity using a likelihood ratio test. Regression results were tabulated as beta (β), the regression coefficient slope, with its 95% confidence interval (CI) and associated p-value. When comparing individual complications and ICU or hospital length of stay as the outcome variable against cf-mtDNA copies per microlitre either univariate logistic or linear regression was used respectively. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for binary data and the Pearson chi-squared test for continuous and categorical variables. All analyses were corrected for haemodilution by including each outcome variable as co-variates in the multivariable regression or ANOVA model. The level of significance set at α < 0.05 throughout.
An a priori power analysis was not conducted for this pilot study. A two-year recruitment period was selected as an end point.
