Despite recent advances in understanding its molecular mechanisms, effective therapies remain limited. Circular RNAs have emerged as key regulators of gene expression, yet their role in pulmonary fibrosis is poorly understood. Here, we investigated circSmad4 and its therapeutic potential. Our results demonstrate that circSmad4 expression was markedly upregulated in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis, suggesting a role in fibrotic progression. Silencing circSmad4 by siRNA significantly alleviated lung fibrosis, reducing lung weight, collagen deposition, and inflammatory cytokine expression. Consistently, pharmacological inhibition of FGFR2 with FGFR2-IN-1 also suppressed the pro-fibrotic effects of TGF-β1, mimicking si-circSmad4. These findings suggest that circSmad4 functions as a central regulator of pulmonary fibrosis by modulating fibroblast activation, ECM deposition, and inflammation. In conclusion, circSmad4 represents a novel driver of pulmonary fibrosis, and targeting circSmad4 may offer a promising therapeutic strategy.

## INTRODUCTION
Pulmonary fibrosis is a chronic and progressive form of interstitial lung disease characterized by an unknown etiology and poor prognosis. Notably, the mortality rate of pulmonary fibrosis surpasses that of several types of cancer [ ]. The pathological hallmark of pulmonary fibrosis is an aberrant wound healing response following alveolar epithelial injury, leading to excessive fibroblast activation and subsequent accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) [ , ]. This process involves epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, macrophage activation, and fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition, ultimately contributing to clinical manifestations such as persistent cough, exertional dyspnea, hypoxemia, and respiratory failure [ , ]. Although genetic factors, aging, environmental factors, and autoimmune diseases have been identified as potential risk factors, the precise mechanisms underlying the initiation and progression of fibrosis remain largely elusive. The currently Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved antifibrotic drugs, pirfenidone and nintedanib, exhibit only limited efficacy in slowing disease progression [ , , ]. As lung transplantation remains the only curative option, the urgent need for developing effective therapeutic strategies and identifying novel therapeutic targets is evident.
Recent studies have highlighted the role of RNA-based regulatory mechanisms, including microRNAs (miRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs, in fibrotic processes [ , ]. Circular RNAs (circRNA) represents a novel class of endogenous non-coding RNA (ncRNA) characterized by a covalently closed-loop structure, lacking both 5′-3′ polarity and a polyadenylated tail, which confers remarkable stability [ ]. With advancements in high-throughput sequencing technology, circRNAs have been identified as ubiquitously expressed across various tissues and cell types. Their biogenesis is regulated by cis-elements and trans-factors, including Alu repeats, reverse complementary sequences, and RNA-binding proteins. CircRNAs serve as critical gene regulators through multiple mechanisms, including acting as miRNA sponges, templates for mRNA translation, direct interaction with RNA polymerase II to enhance transcription of host genes, and scaffolding for protein interactions [ ]. These functional attributes implicate circRNAs in various pathological conditions, including atherosclerosis, neurological disorders, fibrosis, and cancer, positioning them as potential biomarkers for clinical diagnosis and prognosis.
Recent studies have demonstrated dysregulated circRNA expression in pulmonary fibrosis, suggesting its potential involvement in disease development and progression. CircRNAs regulate fibroblast proliferation and activation by binding to and inhibiting miRNAs, thereby contributing to the progression of pulmonary fibrosis. Chen et al. [ ] reported that circFBXW4 modulates hepatic fibrosis by directly targeting miR-18b-3p and thereby regulating the expression of FBXW7. Zhu et al. [ ] demonstrated that the overexpression of circNFIB significantly reduced the proliferation of primary mouse cardiac fibroblasts by sponging miR-433, while Tang et al. [ ] reported that circRNA_000203 promoted the activation of mouse cardiac fibroblasts by targeting miR-26b-5p. The effects of circRNAs on pulmonary fibrosis vary depending on the specific circRNA, and there are also species-specific differences in responsiveness. In addition, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying circRNA formation and function in pulmonary fibrosis remain largely elusive.
Our previous study identified circSmad4 as a novel circRNA that regulates cardiac fibrosis. The expression of circSmad4 was upregulated upon transverse aortic constriction (TAC) and TGF-β1 treatment, whereas silencing circSmad4 alleviated cardiac fibrosis and remodeling. Mechanistically, circSmad4 acted as a sponge for miR-671-5p, thereby modulating the pro-fibrotic function of Fgfr2. In addition, circSmad4 was highly expressed in the heart, lung, and brain, with notable changes observed in the lung. These findings suggest that circSmad4 may also play a role in pulmonary fibrosis. This study aims to elucidate the role of circSmad4 not only in cardiac fibrosis but also in pulmonary fibrosis, and further propose it as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis and pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.

## METHODS

## Bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis model
All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chonnam National University Medical School Research Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (CNU IACUC-H-2024-3) and performed according to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health. Eight- to ten-week-old male C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Damul Science.
For bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis studies, mice were anesthetized and subjected to a single intratracheal injection of 3 U/kg bleomycin sulfate (#B2434, Sigma). Control mice received an equivalent volume of saline as a sham treatment. Mice were sacrificed two weeks after bleomycin treatment, and lung tissues were harvested for further analysis.

## circSmad4 siRNA delivery
For in vivo siRNA delivery, siRNA targeting circSmad4 was administered to C57BL/6J mice using in vivo-jetPEI (#101000040, Polyplus) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The experimental design is described in the results. Briefly, siRNA and in vivo-jetPEI reagent were diluted in 5% glucose solution and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The resulting siRNA complex was then intravenously injected via the tail vein.

## RNA extraction, quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), and conventional PCR
Total RNA was extracted using TRI Reagent (#TR118, Molecular Research Center) following the manufacturer’s protocol. To eliminate residual DNA, samples were treated with DNase I (#2270A, Takara). For cDNA synthesis, 1 μg of total RNA (20 μl reaction volume) was reverse transcribed using RevertAid reverse transcriptase (#EP0442, Thermo Scientific) and random hexamers (#SO142, Thermo Scientific). For miRNA analysis, cDNA synthesis was performed using the TaqMan Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (#A28007, Applied Biosystems). qRT-PCR was performed using the QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Kit (#204143, Qiagen) with specific primers and a Rotor-Gene Q real-time PCR cycler (Qiagen). The primers were purchased from Bioneer, and their sequences are listed in .
To validate circRNA expression, conventional PCR was conducted using the nTaq (Mg2+ Plus) Kit (#P050A, Enzynomics) with specifically designed primers for circRNA amplification. Divergent primers were designed across the back-splicing junction to ensure the selective amplification of circRNA transcripts. For circRNA quantification, agarose gel electrophoresis images were analyzed using ImageJ software, measuring band intensity to assess transcript levels.

## Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluids and histological examination
After euthanizing the mice, the left main bronchus was ligated. BAL was then performed on the right lung, followed by tissue collection for protein analysis or histological examination. BAL fluid was collected by instilling 1 ml of phosphate-buffered saline into the trachea via a cannula and withdrawing it. This process was repeated four times. The collected BAL fluid was centrifuged at 500 × g for 10 min, and the supernatant was stored at –80°C for further analysis. Additionally, the right lung of each mouse was excised and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The tissues were then dehydrated and embedded in paraffin. For histological analysis, paraffin blocks were sectioned into 5-μm-thick slices.

## Picrosirius Red staining
For pulmonary histopathology, mouse lungs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated, and embedded in paraffin. Paraffin blocks were sectioned at 5 μm, and the sections were stained with Picrosirius Red to detect collagen deposition.

## Sircol collagen assay
The total soluble collagen content was measured using the Sircol Collagen Assay Kit (#S1000, Biocolor Assays) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 100 μl of lung tissue lysate or cell culture supernatant was mixed with 1 ml of Sircol dye reagent and a reference collagen standard and incubated for 30 min. The mixture was then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min to precipitate the collagen-dye complex. The resulting pellet was dissolved in an alkali reagent, and the absorbance was measured at 555 nm using a Spectramax Abs Plus (Molecular Devices). The total soluble collagen content was quantified using a standard collagen calibration curve provided by the manufacturer.

## ELISA for IL-6, TNF-α, TGF-β1 in BAL fluid
BAL fluid was measured using ELISA kits (Wuhan Boster Biological Technology., Ltd.) according the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Cell culture, transfection and reagents
NIH3T3 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (LM001-05, Welgene) with 10% fetal bovine serum (S001-07, Welgene) and antibiotics (15240062, ThermoFisher Scientific). Cells were maintained in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C. For circRNA knockdown, NIH3T3 cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 105 cells. When confluent, NIH3T3 cells were treated with siRNA, using Lipofectamine RNAiMax (#13778075, Thermo), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. FGFR2-IN-1 (HY-145230; MedChemExpress) was used as a selective FGFR2 inhibitor and dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide to the desired concentrations.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed with PASW Statistics 26 (IBM SPSS) and GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software). Data are presented as means ± standard error of the mean (SEM). To compare two independent groups, we used a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test or the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U-test after checking for a normal distribution. To compare more than two groups, we used one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with post-hoc tests according to the levels of independent variables. When the interaction between independent variables was significant, stratification was performed for pairwise comparison. The assumption of equal variance was confirmed using Levene’s test. For post-hoc tests, we performed Tukey’s HSD (honestly significant difference) for multiple comparisons in equal variance, whereas the Dunnett’s T3 test was used for unequal variance. Statistical significance was considered when the p-value was < 0.05. Asterisks in the figures denote statistical significance as follows: ns, not significant; *p ≤ 0.05; **p ≤ 0.01; ***p ≤ 0.001; ****p ≤ 0.0001.

## RESULTS

## circSmad4 expression in TAC-induced pulmonary fibrosis
Heart failure is a fatal condition that leads to impaired cardiac function, and TAC is widely used to induce cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure in murine models. Chronic TAC has also been reported to cause severe pulmonary fibrosis, inflammation, and alveolar remodeling [ , ]. Heart failure leads to a significant increase in left atrial pressure, which subsequently elevates pulmonary capillary pressure, resulting in excessive fluid filtration into the lungs, blood congestion, and ultimately pulmonary edema [ ]. Furthermore, increased lung weight has been observed in TAC-induced heart failure models, suggesting that TAC may contribute to the development of severe pulmonary disease. In our previous study, we found that mice with TAC-induced heart failure model exhibited an increase in lung weight due to blood flow congestion caused by reduced cardiac output [ ]. Accordingly, TAC induced marked pulmonary fibrosis, as evidenced by increased Picrosirius Red staining and higher Ashcroft scores, whereas this fibrotic response was attenuated in mice treated with si-circSmad4 ( ). We confirmed the expression of circSmad4 in the lungs and found that its expression was increased in lungs with TAC-induced pulmonary fibrosis ( ). To verify that the circSmad4 expressed in the lung is identical to the cardiac circSmad4 characterized in our previous study, we performed sanger sequencing. An identical back-splice junction was observed for circSmad4 in both heart and lung, supporting that they represent the same isoform ( ). In addition, tail vein injection of si-circSmad4 effectively knocked down circSmad4 expression in the lung. These results suggest that circSmad4 plays an active role in the regulation of lung fibroblast activation.

## circSmad4 expression in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis
To distinguish the effects of si-circSmad4 in primary pulmonary fibrosis from those in secondary pulmonary fibrosis associated with heart failure, we aimed to assess its therapeutic potential in a primary lung disease model. To this end, bleomycin treated animals have been documented to exhibit histopathological characteristics, including endobronchial bud loss, collagen deposition, and alveolar space reduction, closely resembling those observed in pulmonary fibrosis patients [ ]. This evidence supports the utility of the bleomycin model in reliably recapitulating key pathological features of human disease, reinforcing its widespread application in pulmonary fibrosis research [ ].
To evaluate the in vivo therapeutic effects of circSmad4, we established a pulmonary fibrosis mouse model using a single intratracheal administration of bleomycin ( ). The survival rate of mice was monitored for 14 days following bleomycin administration. As shown in , we noted a significant reduction in survival in mice with bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis, whereas administration of si-circSmad4 with bleomycin effectively delayed and reduced mortality in mice with pulmonary fibrosis.
At 14 days after induction of pulmonary fibrosis, lung tissue analysis showed a significant increase in lung weight in the siNC + bleomycin group, which was significantly attenuated by si-circSmad4 treatment ( ). We evaluated in vivo lung delivery of si-circSmad4 by measuring endogenous circSmad4 levels. Intravenous si-circSmad4 significantly reduced circSmad4 expression in vehicle controls (first vs. second bar) and in bleomycin-treated mice (third vs. fourth bar) ( ). Bleomycin treatment induced pulmonary fibrosis, as shown by the increased Picrosirius Red staining area in the lower-left panel relative to the upper-left control ( ). This increase was diminished in si-circSmad4-treated lungs (lower-right panel). The antifibrotic effect of si-circSmad4 is quantified by Ashcroft scores in the bar graph on the right of .
Bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis was further confirmed by increased mRNA levels of fibrosis-associated genes (third vs. first bars in ). The elevations in Acta2, Col1a1, Col3a1, and Ctgf were significantly attenuated by co-administration of si-circSmad4 (fourth vs. third bars in ). Fibroblast activation is associated with the release of collagen into the culture media (media collagen) or the ECM (matrix collagen). Therefore, we quantified collagen levels in lung tissue and BAL fluid using a collagen assay kit. Bleomycin markedly increased soluble collagen levels in both lung and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid compared with controls, whereas co-treatment with si-circSmad4 significantly attenuated these increases ( ). These findings suggest that circSmad4 plays a crucial role in the progression of pulmonary fibrosis.

## si-circSmad4 inhibited the IL-6, TNF-α, and TGF-β production
Inflammation plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis, and previous studies have reported that TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-17 act as pro-inflammatory cytokines/chemokines associated with lung injury [ ]. Furthermore, the accumulation of ECM proteins is closely linked to pulmonary fibrosis. In the lung tissues of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients, a substantial number of myofibroblasts have been observed, and myofibroblasts are considered key effector cells responsible for matrix deposition and structural remodeling [ , ]. Therefore, we measured the expression of inflammatory cytokines, including, IL-6, TNF-α, and TGF-β, in BAL fluid using ELISA analysis. The results demonstrated that bleomycin treatment significantly increased the expression of IL-6, TNF-α, and TGF-β compared to the Sham group. However, si-circSmad4 treatment effectively suppressed the upregulation of these inflammatory cytokines and ECM-related factors ( ). These findings suggest that si-circSmad4 may exert anti-inflammatory effects and inhibit ECM protein accumulation in pulmonary fibrosis.

## circSmad4 induces Fgfr2 expression via targeting miR-671-5p
In our previous study, we demonstrated that circSmad4 promotes cardiac fibrosis by suppressing miR-671-5p and subsequently upregulating Fgfr2 expression [ ]. To investigate whether this signaling pathway also regulates pulmonary fibrosis, we examined the expression levels of miR-671-5p and Fgfr2 in the lungs of bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis mouse models. The results showed that si-circSmad4 treatment significantly restored the reduced expression of miR-671-5p observed in the bleomycin-treated group ( ). Additionally, the increased expression of Fgfr2 in the bleomycin-treated group was markedly reduced upon circSmad4 inhibition ( ). These findings suggest that circSmad4 exacerbates pulmonary fibrosis by suppressing miR-671-5p and consequently enhancing Fgfr2 expression in the pulmonary fibrosis model.

## Knock-down of circSmad4 inhibits TGF-β1 induced NIH3T3 cell activation
Fibrosis is recognized as a major cause of morbidity and mortality in the lung and other organs [ ]. In particular, pulmonary fibrosis is a chronic, fatal, and irreversible disease, characterized by fibroblast proliferation and excessive ECM accumulation in the lung [ ]. Additionally, TGF-β1 plays a crucial role in pulmonary fibrosis progression by mediating pulmonary fibroblast activation [ ]. Therefore, to determine whether circSmad4 directly affects mouse embryonic lung fibroblasts, we analyzed its effect on fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation in NIH3T3 cells. First, we examined whether circSmad4 expression was upregulated in response to TGF-β1 ( ). The results showed that TGF-β1 treatment significantly increased circSmad4 expression. Next, si-circSmad4 treatment resulted in a significant reduction in the expression of fibrosis-related genes, including Acta2, Col1a1, Col3a1, and Ctgf ( ). We quantified both media collagen and matrix collagen using an assay kit. TGF-β1 increased media collagen and matrix collagen. Co-treatment with si-circSmad4 significantly attenuated the TGF-β1–induced increase in media collagen and also reduced matrix collagen ( ). These findings indicate that circSmad4 actively participates in the regulation of pulmonary fibroblast activation.

## circSmad4 induces Fgfr2 expression via targeting miR-671-5p
In NIH3T3 cells, TGF-β1 treatment significantly reduced the expression of miR-671-5p ( ). Next, we examined whether miR-671-5p regulates fibroblast differentiation. Transfection of miR-671-5p mimic significantly reduced the TGF-β1-induced expression of Acta2, Col1a1, Col3a1, and Ctgf ( ). Furthermore, TGF-β1 treatment significantly increased media collagen release, whereas this increase was suppressed upon miR-671-5p mimic transfection ( ). These findings suggest that circSMAD4 functions as a pro-fibrotic mediator, at least in part by antagonizing the anti-fibrotic effects of miR-671-5p.
Next, we investigated whether the expression of Fgfr2 mRNA, a target gene of miR-671-5p identified in our previous experimental models, is upregulated in response to fibrosis-inducing stress and whether this upregulation is modulated by si-circSmad4. In NIH3T3 cells, TGF-β1 treatment significantly increased Fgfr2 expression ( ). TGF-β1 significantly upregulated Fgfr2 mRNA, whereas the miR-671-5p mimic blunted the TGF-β1-induced increase ( ). Consistently, si-circSmad4 attenuated the TGF-β1-induced increase in Fgfr2 expression ( ). We confirmed that pharmacological inhibition of FGFR2 recapitulated the effects observed with si-circSmad4 treatment. Treatment with an Fgfr2 inhibitor FGFR2-IN-1 did not appreciably affect basal Fgfr2 mRNA levels; however, it suppressed the TGF-β1-induced upregulation of Fgfr2 ( ). Treatment with the FGFR2-IN-1 significantly attenuated the TGF-β1-induced upregulation of Acta2, Col1a1, and Fibronectin-1 (Fn1), whereas Ctgf expression showed a decreasing trend but did not reach statistical significance ( ).

## DISCUSSION
This study identifies circSmad4 as a key regulator of pulmonary fibrosis by promoting fibroblast activation, ECM deposition, and inflammation through the miR-671-5p/Fgfr2 axis. Bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis models showed upregulated circSmad4 expression, and its knockdown significantly reduced lung fibrosis, collagen deposition, and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, confirming its role as a pro-fibrotic mediator. Mechanistically, circSmad4 suppresses miR-671-5p, leading to Fgfr2 upregulation, a driver of fibroblast proliferation and differentiation. si-circSmad4 treatment also inhibited TGF-β1-induced fibroblast activation and collagen secretion, reinforcing its role in ECM homeostasis. Despite its therapeutic potential, further studies are needed to explore circSmad4’s central regulatory network and its clinical applicability. These results suggest that circSmad4 is a central regulator of fibrotic remodeling by promoting fibroblast activation, ECM accumulation, and inflammation.
Pulmonary fibrosis is a rapidly progressing interstitial lung disease with a high mortality rate, yet effective curative treatments remain unavailable [ , ]. The excessive accumulation of ECM due to hyperproliferative and activated fibroblasts is considered a central pathological mechanism [ ]. Currently, the FDA-approved antifibrotic agents, nintedanib and pirfenidone, have demonstrated limited efficacy in slowing disease progression and do not achieve disease remission [ , , , ]. As a result, lung transplantation remains the only curative option. Thus, identifying novel therapeutic targets and elucidating the underlying molecular mechanisms are crucial steps toward developing effective treatment strategies for this refractory disease.
Whole-transcriptome RNA sequencing has revealed the existence of ncRNAs, and increasing evidence suggests that dysregulation of ncRNAs is closely associated with various human diseases [ ]. CircRNAs, generated through back-splicing, form a highly stable structure and function as key gene regulators through multiple mechanisms, including acting as miRNA or protein sponges, decoys, translation templates, and protein scaffolds. Due to these unique properties, circRNAs possess greater therapeutic potential than other RNA molecules [ ].
In recent years, ncRNAs have been increasingly recognized for their crucial role in the pathogenesis and progression of pulmonary fibrosis [ , ]. Notably, Li et al. [ ] identified circTADA2A as an anti-fibrotic factor, whereas Zhang et al. [ ] demonstrated that circHIPK3 regulates myofibroblast proliferation via fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition. These findings highlight the growing interest in circRNAs as potential modulators of pulmonary fibrosis. Previous studies have reported circSmad4 expression not only in cardiac fibroblasts but also in the lungs and brain, suggesting its broader physiological relevance [ ]. Based on these findings, we extended to circSmad4 research into fibrotic diseases. In this study, we systematically analyzed the expression pattern of circSmad4 in fibrotic mouse lung tissues and NIH3T3 cells, aiming to elucidate its functional role in pulmonary fibrosis. However, this study is limited to mouse models and murine-derived cell experiments, necessitating further validation of circSmad4-targeting therapeutic approaches in primary human lung fibroblasts isolated from lung tissues of pulmonary fibrosis patients, as well as in patient-derived lung tissue samples. Additionally, while si-circSmad4 demonstrates a significant inhibitory effect on pulmonary fibrosis in the short term, its long-term administration requires further investigation to assess potential adverse effects and safety concerns.
Accumulating evidence, including findings from this study, suggests that the circSmad4-miR-671-5p-Fgfr2 signaling pathway is implicated in the fibrotic process, indicating the potential of circSmad4-targeting therapies in the regulation of multi-organ fibrosis, thereby highlighting its translational relevance. Additionally, this study proposes a comprehensive mechanism encompassing not only ECM deposition accumulation inhibition but also inflammation modulation, broadening the therapeutic scope of circSmad4 in fibrosis management.
Additionally, in this study, siRNA was administered via intravenous injection. However, for diseases such as pulmonary fibrosis, it has been reported that delivering miRNAs or drugs encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles via aerosolization using a nebulizer connected to a nose-only inhalation exposure system can reduce systemic side effects and enhance therapeutic efficacy [ ]. Therefore, delivering si-circSmad4 via aerosolization in this study is expected to be feasible. If successful, this approach may also facilitate the development of si-circSmad4 as a therapeutic agent.
In conclusion, the current study elucidated that circSmad4 could alleviate pulmonary fibrosis by inhibiting fibroblast activation via the miR-671-5p/Fgfr2 signaling pathway. These findings implicate circSmad4 as a promising therapeutic target for the development of novel treatment strategies for pulmonary fibrosis, a disease with currently limited therapeutic options.
