## Abstract

## Background
Ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI) limits graft function and long-term outcomes after kidney transplantation. Proximal tubular (PT) cells are highly mitochondria-rich and metabolically active, making them vulnerable to ischemic and oxidative stress. However, the molecular mechanisms linking mitochondrial dysfunction to graft function remain incompletely understood.

## Methods
We integrated single-nucleus RNA sequencing, bioinformatics analyses, and experimental validation to identify mitochondria-associated genes in PT cells related to graft injury and vulnerability in the transplant setting. Mitochondria-related differentially expressed genes (Mito-DEGs) were used to construct machine learning models for delayed graft function (DGF) risk stratification. The candidate gene HAO2 was further evaluated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in kidney transplant biopsies, a murine IRI model, and H₂O₂-treated HK-2 cells. Human biopsy samples were obtained at or prior to reperfusion and may not fully capture the extent of post-transplant IRI. Both overexpression and siRNA-mediated knockdown were performed to assess its function. Cell viability, apoptosis, and mitochondrial function were assessed using standard assays.

## Results
PT cells from acute kidney injury samples exhibited mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic impairment. Donor kidney clustering suggested heterogeneity in DGF risk associated with mitochondrial bioenergetic capacity. An eight-gene Mito-DEG signature demonstrated moderate performance in stratifying DGF risk. IHC analysis demonstrated that HAO2 expression was reduced in DGF recipient biopsies. Consistently, HAO2 was downregulated in murine IRI kidneys and injured HK-2 cells. Functionally, HAO2 overexpression alleviated oxidative stress, apoptosis, and mitochondrial dysfunction, whereas HAO2 knockdown exerted opposite effects and further aggravated H₂O₂-induced injury. Regulatory analysis identified 14 miRNAs and four TFs potentially controlling HAO2, while downstream pathways linked HAO2 to amino acid and fatty acid metabolism, extracellular matrix organization, and immune responses.

## Conclusions
These findings suggest that HAO2 may serve as a functional indicator of mitochondrial metabolic capacity associated with graft vulnerability rather than being specific to IRI alone. Given the observational nature of the clinical data, further studies are required to determine whether HAO2 provides added value beyond established histopathological assessment and to clarify its potential role in guiding mitochondrial-targeted conditioning strategies, such as oxygenated machine perfusion, aimed at preserving graft function during transplantation.

## Background
Kidney transplantation (KT) is the preferred treatment for patients with end-stage renal disease, significantly improving both survival and quality of life [ ]. However, ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI), an unavoidable consequence of organ procurement and transplantation, remains a major factor limiting graft function and long-term outcomes [ ]. IRI contributes to delayed graft function (DGF) and acute rejection by inducing tubular epithelial cell injury, inflammation, and oxidative stress [ ]. Clinically, DGF is associated with prolonged hospitalization, higher immunosuppressive burden, and inferior graft survival [ , ]. Despite advancements in preservation solutions, surgical techniques, and perioperative management, the complex interplay of cellular stress responses, inflammatory signaling, and metabolic dysregulation during IRI remains incompletely characterized, highlighting the importance of identifying novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets to enhance graft resilience and patient outcomes.
Mitochondria are central regulators of cellular energy metabolism and redox homeostasis, and their dysfunction has been increasingly recognized as a key mediator of renal IRI [ ]. During ischemia, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and ATP depletion lead to tubular epithelial cell injury, while reperfusion exacerbates oxidative stress through excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, further damaging mitochondrial structure and function. Recent studies highlight that mitochondrial bioenergetic capacity and integrity are closely linked to renal tubular survival, suggesting that targeting mitochondria-related pathways may represent a promising strategy to mitigate IRI-induced renal injury [ , ]. Therapeutic strategies aimed at preserving mitochondrial bioenergetics, enhancing antioxidant defenses, or modulating mitochondrial quality control pathways have shown promise in preclinical models, underscoring the potential of mitochondria-targeted interventions to mitigate renal IRI and improve transplant outcomes [ – ]. Furthermore, the identification of mitochondria-related biomarkers may provide predictive insights into graft function, enabling more precise perioperative risk stratification and personalized interventions [ ].
In the present study, we systematically investigated mitochondria-related genes in proximal tubular (PT) cells in the context of renal injury and transplantation using single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq). We identified hydroxyacid oxidase 2 (HAO2) as a mitochondrial function-related gene whose expression was markedly downregulated in both human acute kidney injury (AKI) samples and murine IRI models. To evaluate its biological role, we performed both gain- and loss-of-function experiments in HK-2 cells, investigating the impact of HAO2 on oxidative stress, apoptosis, and mitochondrial homeostasis. Additionally, we explored the regulatory networks and downstream pathways associated with HAO2. Given that human biopsy samples were obtained at or prior to reperfusion, our analyses primarily reflect early injury states and may not fully capture the extent of post-transplant IRI. This study seeks to delineate mitochondria-related alterations in PT cells associated with graft vulnerability and functional recovery rather than IRI alone.

## Methods

## Dataset acquisition
A total of four datasets were included in this study (Table ). The GSE210622 dataset is a snRNA-seq dataset generated using the 10x Genomics platform, comprising kidney tissue samples from six controls and eight patients with AKI [ ]. This dataset was used to identify genes in PT cells associated with mitochondrial function in the context of renal injury. The GSE43974 and GSE54888 datasets contain microarray profiles of kidney tissues subjected to IRI prior to transplantation [ ], whereas GSE21374 includes data related to long-term graft survival following kidney transplantation [ ]. All datasets were downloaded from the GEO database, and mitochondrial-related genes were obtained from the MitoCarta3.0 database [ ].

## SnRNA-Seq data processing and analysis
SnRNA-seq from GSE210622 were processed using Seurat in R. For each sample, an object was created, and low-quality cells were removed based on gene counts (> 500 genes per cell), UMI counts (> 500), and mitochondrial gene percentage (< 5%). Cells were also filtered to retain only genes expressed in at least three cells. Each cell was annotated with its sample origin, and cell barcodes were renamed to avoid overlap between samples. After quality control, a total of 14 samples were retained for further analysis. Doublets were subsequently identified and removed using DoubletFinder. For multi-sample integration, data were normalized, highly variable genes were identified, and integration was performed using Seurat’s reciprocal principal component analysis method to correct batch effects. Dimensionality reduction and graph-based clustering were conducted to identify distinct cell populations. Cell-type annotation was performed using canonical marker genes and previously published datasets [ , ]. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in PT cells were identified using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with significance defined as |log₂ fold change (FC)| > 0.25 and adjusted P < 0.05.

## Molecular subgroup identification and gene set enrichment analysis
In the GSE43974 dataset, DEGs identified from PT cells in the snRNA-seq data were used for consensus clustering. Consensus clustering was performed to classify samples into molecular subgroups, and the optimal number of clusters was determined based on the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curve. Post-transplant DGF incidence among different clusters was compared using the chi-square test.Subsequently, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted to explore functional differences between clusters. Adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

## Construction and validation of predictive models
Mitochondria-related DEGs (Mito-DEGs) identified in PT cells were mapped to the GSE43974 dataset, and genes showing differential expression between the control and DGF groups were selected for further analysis. Feature selection was performed using both LASSO regression and random forest algorithms, and the intersecting genes from the two methods were retained as candidate variables. The GSE43974 dataset was randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%). Ten machine learning algorithms were used to construct predictive models, including logistic regression, random forest, support vector machine (SVM) with radial kernel, k-nearest neighbors, naive Bayes, decision tree, gradient boosting machine, extreme gradient boosting, neural network, and linear discriminant analysis. The model with the highest predictive performance in the testing set was selected as the optimal model. Finally, the GSE54888 dataset served as an external validation cohort to evaluate the model’s predictive accuracy.

## Association of model genes with graft survival and renal function
To further investigate the association between the genes included in the model and graft survival, survival analysis was conducted using the GSE21374 dataset. Recipients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups according to the median expression level of each gene. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were plotted, and the log-rank test was applied to evaluate differences between groups. Spearman correlation analysis was then performed using the NephroSeq database to assess the relationship between gene expression levels and renal function parameters. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Prediction of HAO2 regulatory network and pathway analysis
MiRNA and transcription factors (TF) play critical roles in regulating gene expression. To identify potential upstream regulators of HAO2, miRNAs targeting HAO2 were predicted using the multiMiR R package [ ], and upstream transcription factors were predicted based on the hTFtarget database [ ]. Potential downstream pathways of HAO2 were further explored. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and differential expression analysis were performed on the GSE21374 dataset to identify pathways closely associated with graft loss, using |log₂ FC| > 0.25 as the threshold for significance. Subsequently, Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to assess the relationships between HAO2 expression and these pathways. Adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

## Mouse I/R model with histology, biochemistry, and transmission electron microscopy
Male C57BL/6 mice (8–10 weeks old) were obtained from the Xi’an Jiaotong University Experimental Animal Center (Xi’an, China). All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Xi’an Jiaotong University and performed according to institutional guidelines (XJTUAE2025-3259). Renal IRI was induced under anesthesia using an inhalation anesthesia machine. After a midline abdominal incision, the left ureter and renal vessels were permanently ligated, and the left kidney was removed. The right kidney was exposed, and ischemia was induced by clamping the renal pedicle with a microvascular clamp for 30 min, while body temperature was maintained at 37 °C. Sham-operated mice underwent left nephrectomy without right renal pedicle clamping. Twenty-four hours after I/R, blood was collected via the inferior vena cava for serum creatinine (Scr) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) measurements. Kidney tissues were divided into four portions: one fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, periodic acid-schiff (PAS) stained, and blindly scored for histological injury (1: <10%, 2: 10–25%, 3: 25–50%, 4: 50–75%, 5: >75%) in five randomly selected fields [ ]; two portions snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for protein and RNA extraction; and one portion fixed for ultrastructural analysis using transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Hitachi, Japan).

## Cell culture and treatment
HK-2 cells were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences Cell Bank. Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco), penicillin (100 U/mL, Gibco), and streptomycin (100 µg/mL, Gibco) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. When cells reached 70–80% confluence, they were detached using 0.25% trypsin and resuspended in complete medium to obtain a single-cell suspension. All cultures were confirmed to be free of bacterial, fungal, and mycoplasma contamination. To simulate in vitro injury, cells were treated with 500 µM H₂O₂ for 24 h.

## HAO2 overexpression in HK-2 cells
For HAO2 overexpression, HK-2 cells were seeded in 6-well culture plates and cultured to achieve approximately 70% confluence prior to transfection. Cells were transfected with either a HAO2 expression plasmid (pENTER-HAO2, purchased from WZ Biosciences Inc., Jinan, China) or an empty pENTER vector control using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). Briefly, for each well, 2.5 µg of plasmid DNA and 5 µL of P3000™ Enhancer Reagent were first diluted in 125 µL of Opti-MEM medium (Gibco). In a separate tube, 3.75 µL of Lipofectamine 3000 Reagent was diluted in another 125 µL of Opti-MEM. The two solutions were then combined, incubated for 15 min at room temperature, and the resulting mixture was added dropwise to the cells. Transfection efficiency was subsequently verified by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and western blot 48 h post-transfection.

## HAO2 knockdown in HK-2 cells
For HAO2 knockdown, HK-2 cells were seeded in 6-well culture plates and cultured to reach approximately 60–70% confluence prior to transfection. Cells were transfected with either HAO2-specific small interfering RNA (si-HAO2) or negative control siRNA (si-NC) (synthesized by GenePharma, Shanghai, China) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). The sequences of siRNAs targeting HAO2 are listed in Supplementary Table . Briefly, for each well, 50 pmol of siRNA was diluted in 125 µL of Opti-MEM medium (Gibco). In a separate tube, 3.75 µL of Lipofectamine 3000 Reagent was diluted in another 125 µL of Opti-MEM. The two solutions were gently mixed and incubated for 15 min at room temperature to allow complex formation, and the resulting mixture was then added dropwise to the cells. The medium was replaced with fresh complete medium after 6 h of incubation. Knockdown efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR and western blot analysis 48 h post-transfection.

## Cell viability assays
Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay (Beyotime, China). Briefly, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well of a 96-well plate containing the test cells, followed by incubation for 45 min. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, CA). Cell viability was expressed relative to untreated control cells, which were considered 100% viable.

## Flow cytometric analysis of apoptosis in HK-2 cells
Cell apoptosis was assessed using an Annexin V–APC and 7-AAD apoptosis detection kit (Yeasen Biotechnology, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were collected, washed with cold PBS, and incubated with Annexin V–APC and 7-AAD for five minutes at room temperature in the dark. Apoptotic cells were immediately analyzed by flow cytometry, and the percentages of apoptotic and dead cells were quantified using FlowJo software (version 10; Treestar, OR, USA).

## Measurement of cellular and tissue ATP levels
ATP levels in HK-2 cells and kidney tissues were measured using a commercial ATP assay kit (Beyotime, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells or homogenized kidney tissue were lysed, and supernatants were collected after centrifugation. ATP content was determined via a luciferase-based luminescence reaction using a microplate reader and normalized to total protein concentration measured by bicinchoninic acid assay (BCA, Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

## RNA extraction and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction
Total RNA was extracted from HK-2 cells or kidney tissues using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA). cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Accurate Biotechnology, China). RT-qPCR was performed using 2×SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Accurate Biotechnology, China) and a Bio-Rad CFX96 RT-PCR System. Gene expression levels were normalized to β-actin and calculated using the 2^–ΔΔCt method. Detailed information regarding the primer sequences is listed in Supplementary Table .

## Western blot analysis
HK-2 cells or kidney tissues were lysed in RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA assay. Ten micrograms of total protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature. The membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, including rabbit anti–B-cell lymphoma-2 (Bcl-2, 1:1000, Abclonal); rabbit anti–Bcl2-associated X protein (Bax, 1:1000, Abclonal); rabbit anti–cleaved Caspase3 (1:1000, Abclonal); rabbit anti-hydroxyacid oxidase 2 (HAO2, 1:1000, Cusabio), and rabbit anti–β-actin (1:5000, Abclonal). After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence system and quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, USA), with β-actin used as the loading control for normalization.

## Flow cytometric analysis of JC-1 and MitoSOX
Mitochondrial membrane potential and mitochondrial ROS in HK-2 cells were assessed using JC-1 and MitoSOX Red (Yeasen Biotechnology, China), respectively, according to the manufacturers’ instructions. For JC-1 staining, cells were incubated with JC-1 working solution for 20 min at 37 °C in the dark, washed with PBS, and analyzed by flow cytometry. For MitoSOX staining, cells were incubated with 500 nM MitoSOX for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark, washed, and immediately analyzed by NovoCyte Flow Cytometer Systems (Agilent). Data were processed and quantified using FlowJo software (version 10; Treestar, OR, USA).

## Immunohistochemical analysis of HAO2 in human donor kidney biopsies
To evaluate the association between HAO2 expression and baseline graft condition, a total of 20 kidney transplant recipients receiving grafts from deceased donors (all brain-death donors, DBD) were enrolled in this study, including 10 patients with DGF and 10 with stable initial graft function. All grafts were preserved using hypothermic machine perfusion (HMP). Cold ischemia time (CIT) was recorded for all cases. DGF was defined as the requirement for dialysis within the first week after KT [ ]. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (Approval No: XJTU1AF2023LSK-450) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Donor kidney biopsy specimens were obtained at the end of HMP and prior to reperfusion (zero-hour biopsies), and therefore reflect pre-reperfusion baseline injury rather than the full extent of IRI. Tissue cores were procured using a 16-gauge Tru-Cut needle, followed by formalin fixation, paraffin embedding, and standard processing, which included staining with hematoxylin and eosin, PAS, Masson’s trichrome, and Jones methenamine silver. Specimen adequacy was confirmed if ≥ 10 glomeruli and ≥ 1 artery were present, consistent with Banff recommendations [ ]. The baseline histopathological alterations were evaluated by three experienced renal pathologists, who were blinded to all clinical information, using the Remuzzi scoring system. This system grades four chronic lesions—glomerulosclerosis, tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and vascular intimal thickening—each on a 0–3 scale, which are then summed to generate the total Remuzzi score [ ]. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) for HAO2 was performed on 4-µm formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded donor kidney sections. Following deparaffinization, rehydration, and heat-induced epitope retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0), endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide and nonspecific binding was reduced using normal goat serum. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary anti-HAO2 antibody (1:250, Cusabio), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibody detection and visualization with DAB, with hematoxylin counterstaining. IHC staining was quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software (NIH, USA). For each section, at least five randomly selected, non-overlapping cortical fields were captured under identical magnification and exposure settings. The positive staining area of HAO2 was determined using a color deconvolution plugin followed by threshold segmentation. The percentage of HAO2-positive area relative to the total tissue area (area fraction) was calculated and used for statistical analysis. All quantifications were performed in a blinded manner.

## Statistical analysis
Bioinformatics analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.0), and the packages used in this study, along with their respective versions, are listed in Supplementary Table . For in vitro and in vivo experiments, quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. Comparisons between two groups were performed using the unpaired Student’s t-test, while comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way ANOVA followed by post hoc Tukey’s test. For clinical data, continuous variables of donors and recipients are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Spearman’s rank correlation was used to assess the relationship between HAO2 expression and the Remuzzi score. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Single-cell transcriptomic profiling and validation of renal injury–associated mitochondrial alterations
After quality control and doublet removal (Figure A), a total of 122,802 cells were retained for downstream analysis. Following multi-sample integration, cell clusters were evenly distributed across samples and groups in the UMAP visualization (Fig.  A, Figure B-D). In total, 15 distinct cell subtypes were identified, including thick ascending limb (TAL), PT, collecting duct principal cell (CD-PC), endothelial cells (EC), thin limb, distal convoluted tubule (DCT), collecting duct intercalated cells type A, fibroblast, connecting tubule, vascular smooth muscle cell, podocytes, macrophage, parietal epithelial cell, T cell, and collecting duct intercalated cells type B (Fig.  B, Figure E). Among them, TAL, PT, and CD-PC were the three most abundant populations, whereas PT, DCT, and EC exhibited the highest numbers of DEGs (Fig.  C-D). Given these findings, and considering that PT cells are well recognized in previous studies as the most injury-prone and prognostically critical population in AKI, we focused our subsequent analyses on PT cells to further elucidate their molecular characteristics and functional alterations. Based on GSEA analysis (Fig.  E-F), multiple energy metabolism–related pathways were found to be markedly downregulated in PT cells from AKI patients, including modified amino acid transport, organic acid catabolic process, beta-alanine metabolism, glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, and glycerolipid metabolism. In contrast, numerous immune response–related gene sets were significantly upregulated, such as innate immune response, humoral immune response, and antigen processing and presentation.
 Since mitochondrial function is tightly linked to cellular energy metabolism, we next established a mouse model of IRI to further investigate mitochondrial morphology and bioenergetic alterations in vivo. Following IRI, Scr and BUN levels were markedly elevated (Fig.  G-H). Additionally, NGAL expression in renal tissues was markedly increased after IRI (Fig.  I). PAS staining demonstrated typical pathological features of acute tubular injury, including tubular cast formation, brush border loss, and epithelial necrosis (Fig.  J-K). TEM revealed pronounced mitochondrial morphological abnormalities in renal tubular epithelial cells, characterized by swelling, disrupted cristae structure, and reduced mitochondrial density (Fig.  L). Consistently, ATP quantification assays confirmed a significant decrease in renal ATP content after IRI, indicating severe mitochondrial dysfunction (Fig.  M). Collectively, these results suggest that mitochondrial injury and impaired energy metabolism are prominent features associated with renal injury.

## Consensus clustering and gene set enrichment analysis
Based on the DEGs identified in PT cells, we performed consensus clustering of kidney transplant recipients using the donor kidney microarray dataset GSE43974. The CDF curve indicated that the clustering was most stable when the number of clusters (k) was set to two (Fig.  A), as evidenced by a clear and well-separated consensus matrix at this value (Fig.  B). Notably, the incidence of DGF differed significantly between the two clusters. Recipients in Cluster 2 exhibited a markedly higher risk of developing DGF compared to those in Cluster 1, suggesting distinct biological characteristics between the groups (Fig.  C). To further explore the molecular basis underlying these differences, we conducted GSEA between the two clusters (Fig.  D-G). The results revealed that Cluster 1, characterized by better graft functional recovery, showed significant upregulation of pathways related to aerobic respiration, ATP synthesis coupled electron transport, and oxidative phosphorylation. In contrast, Cluster 2 displayed marked enrichment of immune- and inflammation-related processes, including the mitogen-activated protein kinase, NOD-like receptor, and toll-like receptor signaling pathways.
 

## Identification of mitochondrial-related DEGs in PT cells and construction of model for DGF
Our previous analyses revealed significant mitochondrial dysfunction under renal injury conditions. To further explore this relationship, Mito-DEGs were identified by intersecting PT cell DEGs with the Mitocarta database, resulting in 106 candidate genes. Among these, 29 genes were differentially expressed in the GSE43974 dataset and were subjected to feature selection (Fig.  A). Using two complementary approaches—LASSO-logistic regression and random forest analysis—we identified 9 and 13 candidate genes, respectively (Fig.  B-C). To enhance robustness and minimize algorithm-specific bias, we took the intersection of the two methods, yielding eight overlapping genes (Fig.  D). The GSE43974 dataset was randomly divided into a training (70%) and testing (30%) cohort. Ten machine learning algorithms were applied to construct predictive models in the training set, and their performance was assessed by the area under the curve (AUC) in the testing set. Among them, the SVM model achieved the best performance, with an AUC of 0.70 (Fig.  E). External validation using the independent dataset GSE54888 confirmed the robust predictive capability of the SVM model, yielding a consistent AUC of 0.70 (Fig.  F).
 

## Survival analysis and validation of hub gene expression
Early identification of molecular markers that can predict long-term graft outcomes after kidney transplantation is of great clinical importance. Based on the GSE21374 dataset, we performed survival analyses for the eight genes included in our predictive model to evaluate their association with long-term graft survival. Three genes—acyl-coA dehydrogenase long chain (ACADL), radical s-adenosyl methionine domain containing 1 (RSAD1), and HAO2—were significantly correlated with graft survival, and their expression patterns were consistent with the direction observed in the predictive model (Fig.  ). To further assess their clinical relevance, we analyzed the correlation between gene expression levels and Scr using the Nephroseq database (Fig.  A-C). Among the three genes, HAO2 showed the strongest negative correlation with Scr (r=-0.77), suggesting its potential role in maintaining renal function. Therefore, HAO2 was selected for subsequent experimental validation. In the in vivo IRI mouse model, both mRNA and protein levels of HAO2 were markedly decreased in renal tissues following injury (Fig.  D-F). Consistently, in the in vitro H₂O₂-induced HK-2 cell injury model, HAO2 expression was also significantly downregulated at both the transcript and protein levels (Fig.  G-I). SnRNA-seq data further demonstrated that HAO2 expression was predominantly localized to PT cells and was significantly reduced in PT cells from patients with AKI, whereas its expression remained low in other renal cell subpopulations (Fig.  J). To assess the clinical utility of HAO2 as a marker of donor kidney quality, we further performed IHC staining of HAO2 on human donor kidney tissues (Fig.  K). To evaluate the clinical relevance of HAO2, we analyzed a cohort of 20 kidney transplant recipients receiving grafts from DBD donors, all of which were preserved via HMP (Table ). The cohort was stratified into DGF and non-DGF groups. Most donor and recipient variables, including donor age, Scr, and cold ischemic time, were comparable between the two groups. Consistent with its association with renal injury, quantitative IHC analysis of zero-hour biopsies revealed that the HAO2-positive area was markedly reduced in the DGF group compared with the non-DGF group (8.21% vs. 25.80%, P < 0.001). Although the DGF group exhibited significantly higher Remuzzi scores (Fig.  L, r= -0.48, P = 0.012), indicating poorer baseline quality, the reduction in HAO2 expression was also significant. Furthermore, Spearman’s correlation analysis confirmed a significant inverse correlation between HAO2 expression and Remuzzi score, suggesting that reduced HAO2 levels closely parallel pre-existing chronic histopathological damage.
 

## Overexpression of HAO2 alleviates H₂O₂-induced injury and apoptosis in HK-2 cells
To further elucidate the functional role of HAO2 in PT cells, we established an HAO2 overexpression model in HK-2 cells. RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses confirmed that HAO2 expression was significantly upregulated at both mRNA and protein levels following transfection (Fig.  A-C). Notably, under basal conditions, no significant differences in cell viability or apoptosis were observed between the HAO2 overexpression group and the EV control, as assessed by CCK-8 assay and flow cytometric analysis of apoptosis, respectively (Figure ). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that the mRNA level of the renal injury marker NGAL was significantly reduced in the HAO2-overexpressing group compared with the H₂O₂-treated control (Fig.  D). Cell viability was markedly reduced after H₂O₂ treatment, indicating successful induction of oxidative injury. Notably, HAO2 overexpression effectively reversed the H₂O₂-induced decrease in cell viability, suggesting a cytoprotective effect (Fig.  E). Flow cytometric analysis using Annexin V/7-AAD staining demonstrated that HAO2 overexpression markedly decreased the proportion of apoptotic cells (Fig.  F-G). Similarly, Western blotting showed reduced levels of cleaved caspase-3 and Bax, accompanied by increased expression of Bcl-2 (Fig.  H-K). Overall, these results suggest that in vitro overexpression of HAO2 protects HK-2 cells against H₂O₂-induced apoptosis and tubular injury.
 

## Overexpression of HAO2 alleviates H₂O₂-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in HK-2 cells
To investigate the effects of HAO2 on mitochondrial integrity, we first assessed mitochondrial membrane potential using JC-1 staining (Figure S3 A-B). H₂O₂ treatment led to a significant increase in the proportion of JC-1 monomers, indicating a loss of mitochondrial membrane potential and suggesting substantial mitochondrial depolarization. Notably, HAO2 overexpression effectively reversed this H₂O₂-induced increase in monomers, restoring the membrane potential toward normal levels. In addition to membrane potential, we evaluated mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) production using MitoSOX-based flow cytometry (Figure S3 C-D). Consistent with the JC-1 results, H₂O₂ treatment markedly elevated mitochondrial ROS levels, whereas HAO2 overexpression significantly reduced ROS accumulation, indicating a protective effect against oxidative stress. Furthermore, cellular ATP content, an important indicator of mitochondrial bioenergetic function, was significantly decreased following H₂O₂ exposure. Overexpression of HAO2 restored ATP levels, further confirming its role in preserving mitochondrial function under oxidative stress conditions (Figure S3E). Together, these results consistently demonstrate that HAO2 overexpression confers robust protection against H₂O₂-induced mitochondrial dysfunction in HK-2 cells.

## Knockdown of HAO2 aggravates H₂O₂-induced apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction in HK-2 cells
To further corroborate the functional role of HAO2 in PT cells, we established an HAO2 knockdown model in HK-2 cells using siRNA (Fig.  ). RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses confirmed that HAO2 expression was significantly reduced at both the mRNA and protein levels following transfection (Fig.  A–C). Knockdown of HAO2 significantly promoted apoptosis and impaired cell viability in HK-2 cells compared with the si-NC group. Notably, HAO2 silencing further potentiated H₂O₂-induced cellular injury, as indicated by increased apoptotic rates and a more pronounced reduction in cell viability in the si-HAO2 + H₂O₂ group relative to the si-NC + H₂O₂ group (Figure S4F). This was accompanied by an elevated Bax/Bcl-2 ratio (Fig.  D-E), increased cleaved caspase-3 expression (Fig.  F-G), and a higher proportion of apoptotic cells (Fig.  H-I).
 Knockdown of HAO2 further impaired mitochondrial function in HK-2 cells (Figure S4). JC-1 staining revealed a significant increase in the proportion of JC-1 monomers in the si-HAO2 group compared with the si-NC group, indicating a loss of mitochondrial membrane potential. Consistently, MitoSOX staining showed that mitochondrial ROS levels were markedly elevated following HAO2 silencing. In addition, intracellular ATP levels were significantly reduced in the si-HAO2 group, suggesting compromised mitochondrial energy metabolism. Furthermore, under H₂O₂ stimulation, these mitochondrial abnormalities were further aggravated in the si-HAO2 + H₂O₂ group compared with the si-NC + H₂O₂ group, as evidenced by a more pronounced increase in JC-1 monomers (Figure S4A-B) and mitochondrial ROS levels (Figure S4C-D), along with a further decline in ATP production (Figure S4E). Overall, these findings indicate that HAO2 knockdown aggravates apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction, thereby increasing the susceptibility of HK-2 cells to H₂O₂-induced injury.

## Upstream regulators of HAO2 and associated pathway predictions
To explore the upstream regulatory mechanisms of HAO2, we first predicted potential miRNAs targeting HAO2, identifying a total of 14 candidate miRNAs (Fig.  A). In parallel, TFs potentially regulating HAO2 were predicted based on the hTFtarget database, revealing four candidates: SUMO2, CTCF, SPI1, and EP300 (Fig.  A).
 To further investigate the pathways associated with graft survival, we conducted GSVA in the GSE21374 dataset (Figure S5). The analysis revealed that, in renal tissues from recipients who experienced graft loss, several metabolic processes were significantly downregulated, including valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation, glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, and fatty acid β-oxidation via acyl-CoA dehydrogenase. In addition, gene sets related to immune and cell death processes, such as positive regulation of pyroptosis, regulation of apoptotic cell clearance, and graft-versus-host disease, were significantly upregulated.
Subsequently, we analyzed the correlation between HAO2 expression and the gene sets associated with graft survival (Fig.  B-C). HAO2 expression was positively correlated with numerous metabolic pathways, including glucuronate catabolic process to xylulose 5-phosphate, aldehyde catabolic process, pyruvate-family amino acid metabolic process, glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, and fatty acid metabolism. Moreover, HAO2 expression was negatively associated with pathways related to extracellular matrix and immune responses, such as ECM-receptor interaction, graft-versus-host disease, allograft rejection, and positive regulation of pinocytosis.

## Discussion
IRI is widely recognized as an important determinant of graft function and long-term outcomes after KT [ ]. PT cells are particularly vulnerable to the oxidative and metabolic disturbances induced by ischemia-reperfusion [ ]. In this study, we integrated snRNA-seq with clinical validation to identify HAO2 as a mitochondrial function–related gene. Our findings demonstrated that an eight-gene signature including HAO2 is significantly associated with DGF and long-term survival. While this clinical link is associative rather than causative, our gain- and loss-of-function experiments in HK-2 cells confirmed that HAO2 is a functional participant in maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis. Specifically, HAO2 overexpression protected against ATP depletion and apoptosis without altering baseline viability, whereas its knockdown sensitized cells to injury. To minimize clinical confounders, our cohort was standardized to include only DBD kidneys preserved via HMP, with comparable cold ischemia time between groups. Under these controlled conditions, these results suggest that HAO2 may serve as a functionally relevant metabolic marker that links baseline graft quality to cellular resilience, providing a potential candidate for advanced conditioning strategies such as oxygenated machine perfusion to improve graft outcomes.
In this study, we utilized machine learning–based feature selection to construct a predictive model consisting of eight Mito-DEGs, which demonstrated a consistent association with the occurrence of DGF across independent datasets. It is essential to emphasize that this model serves as a statistical tool to identify signature patterns associated with DGF, rather than providing evidence of direct causality. Among these candidates, ACADL, RSAD1, and HAO2 were identified as core components. ACADL and RSAD1 are known mediators of fatty acid β-oxidation and metabolic stress, respectively, and their inclusion suggests a collective metabolic perturbation during renal injury. However, their specific functional roles in the pathogenesis of DGF remain to be fully elucidated. Among the three candidates, HAO2 exhibited the strongest correlation with renal functional recovery, prompting our subsequent mechanistic investigations. HAO2 is a peroxisomal enzyme that catalyzes the oxidation of hydroxy fatty acids to keto acids and hydrogen peroxide [ ]. Zuo et al. revealed that HAO2 expression is markedly downregulated in proximal tubules in murine chronic kidney disease models, and its overexpression improves mitochondrial membrane potential and enhances fatty acid oxidation-dependent ATP production [ ]. In renal malignancy, HAO2-mediated regulation of lipid catabolism has been shown to constrain the proliferative phenotype of clear cell renal cell carcinoma [ ]. Consistently, we observed significant reductions in HAO2 mRNA and protein levels in both in vivo and in vitro IRI models. Moreover, snRNA-seq profiling of AKI patients indicated that HAO2 is predominantly expressed in PT cells, in agreement with experimental findings [ ]. These results suggest that HAO2 may serve as an indicator of mitochondrial dysfunction and may be involved in downstream apoptotic processes.
To elucidate the mechanisms responsible for HAO2 downregulation and its biological consequences, we investigated both upstream regulatory elements and downstream functional pathways. A total of 14 candidate miRNAs and four TFs were predicted to target HAO2, many of which have been implicated in mitochondrial regulation and AKI pathophysiology. For instance, miR-103a-3p and miR-15a-5p promote apoptosis in sepsis-induced AKI, while their inhibition restores mitochondrial homeostasis and reduces tubular cell injury, suggesting that dysregulation of these miRNAs may contribute to HAO2 downregulation in ischemic contexts [ , ]. Among transcription factors, SPI1 drives fibroblast activation and tubulointerstitial injury after IRI [ ], while EP300 preserves mitochondrial function by attenuating JAK/STAT-mediated endothelial damage [ ]. These observations raise the possibility that such regulators may be involved in modulating HAO2 expression; however, this requires further experimental validation. Downstream analyses further revealed that HAO2 expression is closely associated with both metabolic and immune-related pathways relevant to renal graft function. Correlation analysis indicated that HAO2 expression was positively associated with amino acid and fatty acid metabolism, including pyruvate-family amino acid metabolism and glycine/serine/threonine metabolism, consistent with the role of mitochondrial metabolism in supporting tubular cell survival under stress. In contrast, HAO2 expression was negatively associated with extracellular matrix and immune-related pathways, such as ECM–receptor interaction and allograft rejection, suggesting a link between reduced HAO2 and enhanced inflammatory and fibrotic responses. These findings suggest that HAO2 reflects mitochondrial metabolic capacity and links metabolic and immune pathways in renal tubular cells. Reduced HAO2 may increase susceptibility to stress; however, these associations do not establish causality and require further validation.
Mitochondria play a central role in cellular energy metabolism by regulating ATP production through mitochondrial dynamics and oxidative phosphorylation. Therefore, peri-transplant strategies aimed at preserving or restoring mitochondrial function may represent a rational approach to improve graft quality and post-transplant outcomes. Compared with conventional static cold storage, emerging preservation approaches such as oxygenated hypothermic machine perfusion and normothermic machine perfusion have been shown to better preserve mitochondrial integrity and bioenergetic capacity [ ]. Oxygenated perfusion can partially restore mitochondrial respiration, limit the accumulation of ischemic metabolites, and attenuate the burst of reactive oxygen species upon reperfusion [ ]. Similarly, normothermic machine perfusion maintains near-physiological metabolic activity, enabling continuous ATP generation, improved substrate utilization, and real-time assessment of organ viability [ ]. Collectively, these strategies have been associated with reduced oxidative stress, improved mitochondrial ultrastructure, and enhanced post-transplant functional recovery. In addition to preservation strategies, mitochondria-targeted therapies have shown preclinical efficacy, including MitoQ, which has been shown to alleviate ischemic injury and improve graft function [ ]. From a diagnostic perspective, assessment of mitochondrial function may provide additional value for evaluating donor kidney quality beyond conventional histopathology [ , ]. In our study, reduced HAO2 expression in pre-implantation biopsies was associated with higher Remuzzi scores and increased risk of DGF, and showed a significant inverse correlation with baseline structural injury. These findings suggest that mitochondrial-related markers such as HAO2 may reflect the metabolic competence of the graft and complement existing scoring systems by capturing functional vulnerability not fully represented by structural assessment alone. However, given its association with histopathological injury, HAO2 should be considered a supportive rather than independent predictor, and its clinical utility requires further validation. From a translational perspective, modulation of HAO2 expression during the peri-transplant period may represent a potential strategy to improve graft quality. Given that current machine perfusion platforms provide a controllable ex vivo environment, they may offer a feasible window for targeted metabolic intervention. For instance, pharmacological agents, gene delivery systems, or small molecules that enhance HAO2 expression or activity could be administered during perfusion to restore mitochondrial homeostasis prior to implantation. In parallel, optimization of perfusion conditions, including oxygen tension, substrate composition, and redox balance, may further support HAO2-associated metabolic pathways. Nevertheless, whether HAO2 modulation can directly translate into improved graft function remains to be established, and future studies integrating mechanistic validation with clinically relevant perfusion models are warranted to define its therapeutic potential.
Despite the strengths of our study, several limitations should be acknowledged. The clinical cohort was relatively small and derived from a single center, and the predictive model showed only moderate performance, requiring validation in larger, independent cohorts. In addition, the associations between the 8-gene signature, HAO2 expression, and DGF are observational and do not establish causality. Although functional experiments support a role for HAO2 in mitochondrial homeostasis, in vivo validation is still needed. Importantly, donor kidney biopsies were obtained prior to reperfusion, which may underestimate the extent of post-reperfusion injury and limit the assessment of dynamic mitochondrial alterations. Notably, HAO2 expression was inversely correlated with Remuzzi score, suggesting that it may reflect baseline structural injury and may partially reflect underlying tissue damage rather than acting as an independent determinant of graft outcomes. Furthermore, transcriptomic analyses may not fully reflect protein-level activity or enzymatic function, which warrants further investigation. Further studies are required to clarify its mechanistic role and clinical utility.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, our study underscores the importance of PT cell mitochondrial function in determining graft outcomes after KT. We identify HAO2 as a functionally relevant metabolic marker associated with the link between baseline structural injury and cellular susceptibility to stress. While the 8-gene signature remains associative, the consistent experimental evidence from both overexpression and knockdown models supports a role for HAO2 in maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis. Modulation of HAO2 expression through advanced organ conditioning strategies may represent a potential avenue for preserving donor kidney quality and improving post-transplant outcomes.
