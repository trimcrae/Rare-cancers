## Abstract

## Background
Vascular inflammation triggers endothelial dysfunction, a pivotal factor in the pathogenesis of diabetic vascular complications. Complement component 1q (C1q), a crucial component of the immune system, is implicated in inflammation and immune responses. However, its role in modulating endothelial function in diabetes remains poorly understood. In this study, we aimed to investigate how C1q influences endothelial inflammation in diabetes and the underlying signaling mechanisms involved.

## Methods
Gene expression in the aortas of streptozotocin‐induced mice or primary endothelial cells was detected using real‐time quantitative polymerase chain reaction, western blot, and immunofluorescence staining. Molecular docking identified dl‐citrulline as a target for C1qa. Dihydroethidium staining was used to assess vascular reactive oxygen species generation, while Evans blue staining was used to evaluate vascular permeability.

## Results
Here, we demonstrated that the upregulation of C1q in the aortic endothelial cells significantly reduces the expression of Nr4a1 (orphan nuclear receptor 4a1) and activates the nuclear factor κB signaling pathway, leading to vascular damage. Increasing Nr4a1 expression can mitigate C1q‐induced vascular injury. Additionally, the small molecule dl‐citrulline downregulates C1q expression and alleviates endothelial inflammation in diabetes. Overexpression of Hoxa3 (homeobox a3), an upstream negative regulator of C1q transcription, protects against diabetic vascular injury.

## Conclusions
Together, these findings highlight that C1q downregulation ameliorates endothelial inflammation in diabetes, providing mechanistic insights for developing therapeutic strategies against diabetic vascular complications.

## Research Perspective

## What Is New?

C1q (complement component 1q) downregulates orphan nuclear receptor 4a1 and activates the nuclear factor κB pathway, thereby triggering inflammation and exacerbating endothelial damage.

dl‐citrulline mitigates endothelial dysfunction in diabetes by downregulating C1q expression.

## What Question Should Be Addressed Next?

Further studies are required to clarify the critical molecular interactions between C1q and diabetic endothelial dysfunction and to explore the clinical applicability of potential interventions targeting this axis.

Vascular complications are a major cause of morbidity and death in diabetes, and vascular inflammation–induced endothelial dysfunction is a pivotal pathological process underlying these complications. , Endothelial cells (ECs), forming the inner lining of blood vessels, play a critical role in maintaining vascular homeostasis; however, in the diabetic milieu, persistent hyperglycemia, oxidative stress, and metabolic dysregulation drive a proinflammatory state that disrupts endothelial function. , 

C1q (complement component 1q), the recognition subunit of the classical complement pathway, has emerged as a key modulator of inflammation and immune responses in various diseases. , Beyond its canonical role in complement activation, C1q also acts as an inflammatory mediator, interacting with multiple cell surface receptors to initiate downstream signaling cascades. , , ECs are potential targets of C1q, which is involved in various pathophysiological processes in these cells. , While accumulating evidence has linked dysregulated C1q expression to inflammatory conditions such as atherosclerosis and neurodegeneration, , , , its specific contribution to endothelial dysfunction in diabetes remains incompletely understood. Given the centrality of ECs in vascular health and the established role of C1q in inflammation, elucidating how C1q impacts diabetic endothelium could reveal novel therapeutic targets.
In this study, we sought to dissect the mechanisms by which C1q influences endothelial inflammation in diabetes. Our findings demonstrate that C1q upregulation in diabetic aortic ECs triggers a signaling cascade involving Nr4a1 (orphan nuclear receptor 4a1) downregulation and NF‐κB (nuclear factor κB) activation, culminating in vascular damage. Additionally, we identify dl‐citrulline as a small molecule capable of suppressing C1q expression and alleviating endothelial inflammation, and uncover Hoxa3 (homeobox a3) as an upstream regulator with therapeutic potential. Collectively, these results establish C1q as a critical driver of diabetic endothelial dysfunction and propose targeted modulation of the C1q axis as a promising strategy to mitigate vascular complications.

## METHODS
The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

## Animals
All animal experiments were carried out in accordance with protocols approved by the Animal Care Committee of Jiangnan University (JN No. 20220315c0200930[093]). The animals were housed under controlled environmental conditions (24±1 °C, 12‐hour light/dark cycles, free access to dry feed and water). Specifically, mice were housed in conditions that were free from pathogens, with a maximum of 5 animals per cage. To eliminate any potential influence of sex‐related variables on the cellular composition of the cardiovascular system, this study exclusively used male mice and assigned them randomly to different treatment groups. Eight‐week‐old male C57BL/6J mice were used for the experiments. For the diabetes model, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with 50 mg/kg per day of streptozotocin (No. 60256ES76; Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) for 5 consecutive days to induce high blood sugar levels. Five days after the injection, the mice whose blood glucose levels exceeded 16.8 mmol/L were selected for subsequent experiments. The control group was injected with 0.1 mol/L citric acid–sodium citrate buffer. For the drug treatment approach, the diabetic mice were randomly assigned (n=5 mice per group) to receive either dl‐citrulline (250 mg/kg) or vehicle via gavage for a duration of 4 weeks. Adeno‐associated virus (AAV) carrying C1qa, Hoxa3, and empty vector with the mouse endothelial specific Tie2 (AAV‐Tie2‐C1qa, AAV‐Tie2‐Hoxa3 and AAV‐Tie2 vector) were injected into mice via the tail vein (n=5 mice per group).

## Isolation of Single Cells
Single cells from the mouse aorta were prepared as we described previously. Briefly, the mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation, and the aorta isolated from each mouse was finely cut and exposed to type IA collagenase (2 mg/mL, No. C9891, Sigma, St. Louis, MO) in PBS for 18 minutes at 37 °C. The suspension was passed through a 40‐μm filter after being dispersed with an 18G needle syringe and centrifuged at 180g for 5 minutes. Next, ACK lysis buffer (No. R7757, Sigma) was added to the cell pellet for 3 minutes at room temperature. Cell viability was assessed via trypan blue staining.

## Real‐Time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction

Total RNA from cells was extracted via TRIzol reagent (No. 15596–026, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) per the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, cDNA was prepared from 1 μg of total RNA via Hifair III First‐Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (No. 11141ES60; Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). Polymerase chain reaction was then performed using SYBR‐Green Master mix (No. 10137ES08; Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) in a LightCycler 480 II real‐time polymerase chain reaction machine (Roche, Indianapolis, IN). The sequences of the primers are shown in Table  . Quantification was performed with the comparative Ct method (2−ΔΔCt) by normalization to β‐actin levels.

## Western Blot
The protein concentrations were measured via a bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit (No. P0012; Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Equal amounts of protein were then separated by SDS‐PAGE and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. Primary antibodies against C1qa (No. ab155052; Abcam, Cambridge, MA), β‐tubulin (No. ab6160; Abcam), Nr4a1 (No. 12235; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL), NF‐κB p65 (No. 3033T; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA), IκBα (No. 4812T; Cell Signaling Technology) and GAPDH (No. 2118T; Cell Signaling Technology), along with corresponding secondary antibodies, were used for detection. Band quantification was performed via ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD).

## Immunofluorescence
Aortic artery segments were removed from the mice and flushed multiple times with PBS to remove any residual blood. The arteries were then fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 1 hour at room temperature, washed in PBS, and longitudinally dissected to expose the endothelium en face. The samples were subsequently permeabilized and blocked in PBS containing 1% Triton X‐100 and 5% bicinchoninic acid at room temperature for 1 hour. The samples were subsequently incubated with the primary antibodies anti‐C1qa (No. ab155052; Abcam), anti‐CD31 (No. C1002; BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ), anti–interleukin‐6 (No. ab290735; Abcam), anti–MCP‐1 (monocyte chemoattractant protein 1; No. ab308522; Abcam), anti‐TNF‐α (tumor necrosis factor‐α; No. ab205587; Abcam), anti–interleukin‐1β (No. ab254360; Abcam), anti‐Nr4a1 (No. 12235; Proteintech), and anti‐Hoxa3 (No. sc‐374 237, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Santa Cruz, CA) overnight at 4 °C. Afterward, the samples were incubated with the appropriate secondary fluorescence‐labeled antibody (Alexa Fluor 568 or Alexa Fluor 647) for 2 hours at room temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were captured via confocal laser‐scanning microscopy (LSM 880; Zeiss Microscopy, Jena, Germany).

## Rhodamine Staining
Primary mouse aortic ECs were seeded onto confocal dishes and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 1 hour at room temperature, followed by three 5‐minute washes with PBS. The cells were then permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X‐100 at room temperature for 10 minutes and blocked with 5% bicinchoninic acid for 1 hour. The cells were then incubated with rhodamine dye solution (0.2 μmol/L in 1% PBS) for 30 minutes, followed by DAPI staining (1:500 in PBS) for 10 minutes in the dark. After three 5‐minute washes with PBS, images were captured via an LSM880 inverted confocal microscope (LSM Tech, Wellsville, PA).

## Luciferase Reporter Assays
To construct luciferase reporter gene vectors containing the C1qa promoters, the full‐length promoters of C1qa were cloned into the pGL3‐basic vector. These constructs were then cotransfected into primary cultured aortic ECs with either the Hoxa3 overexpression vector or a control vector. After 48 hours of transfection, relative luciferase activity was measured using a dual luciferase assay kit (No. 11402ES60; Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), following the manufacturer's protocol.

## Dihydroethidium Staining
To evaluate oxidative stress levels, freshly isolated aortic tissue was incubated with 2 μM dihydroethidium (No. S0063; Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) for 30 minutes at 37 °C in the dark. Following incubation, the tissue was washed 3 times with PBS, and fluorescence was then measured via an LSM880 inverted confocal microscope.

## Analysis of Reactive Oxygen Species Generation
5,6‐Chloromethyl‐2′,7′‐dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (No. C6827; Invitrogen) is a widely used indicator of oxidative stress. Primary mouse aortic endothelial cells were seeded onto confocal plates and incubated with 5,6‐chloromethyl‐2′,7′‐dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (5 μM) at 37 °C for 30 minutes. Fluorescence images were captured via an LSM880 inverted confocal microscope. The argon laser was excited at 488 nm, and the emitted fluorescence was measured between 510 and 530 nm. The relative fluorescence intensity was calculated by selecting 3 random areas per sample, measuring the fluorescence (after subtracting the background), and averaging the values.

## Evans Blue Permeability Assay
The mice were anesthetized with 4% chloral hydrate and secured on a plate. Evans blue dye (5 mg/kg, 1%; Sigma‐Aldrich) was injected into the inferior vena cava to stain the aorta. After 30 minutes, the aorta was removed and photographed. To measure aortic permeability, free Evans blue was removed via PBS perfusion. The aorta was weighed, cut into pieces, and incubated with formamide at 55 °C overnight to extract Evans blue. The concentration of Evans blue in the extract was measured at 620 nm, and the amount per gram of tissue was calculated.

## Statistical Analysis
The results are expressed as the means±SEMs. Statistically significant data from the in vitro and in vivo assays were assessed via an unpaired 2‐tailed Student's t test or 2‐way ANOVA. P values of <0.05 were considered significant.

## RESULTS

## C1q Upregulation Enhances NF‐κB Activation
Our previous studies have confirmed that C1q exerts a proinflammatory effect on vascular ECs of diabetic mice. As shown in Figure  , after primary aortic ECs were incubated with C1qa, the levels of inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules including TNF‐α, interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1β, MCP‐1, ICAM‐1 (intercellular adhesion molecule‐1), and VCAM‐1 (vascular cell adhesion protein 1) were significantly higher than those in the control group, and showed a C1qa concentration‐dependent increase (Figure  ). Meanwhile, the intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) level was also significantly upregulated (Figure  ). NF‐κB is a key transcription factor that regulates various stages of the inflammatory response, and its activation has been well characterized as inducing endothelial inflammation and oxidative stress under inflammatory conditions. , To investigate whether C1q regulates the production of cytokines by activating the NF‐κB pathway, we used a luciferase assay and found that C1q strongly activated NF‐κB reporter cell activity in primary ECs (Figure  ). Similarly, immunoblotting and immunofluorescence staining revealed that C1q could induce the nuclear translocation of the NF‐κB subunit p65 (Figure  and Figure  ). IκBα is a negative regulator of NF‐κB, and we further found that C1q downregulated IκBα levels (Figure  ). Conversely, knocking down C1qa led to reduced nuclear translocation of p65 (Figure  and Figure  ) and increased IκBα expression (Figure  ). These findings suggest that C1q effectively activates the NF‐κB pathway.
 

## C1q Promotes NF‐κB Activation Through the Downregulation of Nr4a1
To investigate the molecular mechanism by which C1q activates NF‐κB, we performed an RNA sequencing analysis on primary aortic endothelial cells before and after incubation with C1qa (10 μg/mL). Compared with those in the control group, genes related to Nr4a1 were significantly downregulated after C1qa incubation (Figure  ). Nr4a1 is a member of the orphan nuclear receptor NR4A family, and it has been reported to potentially play a role in regulating hyperglycemia‐induced endothelial dysfunction. To confirm the RNA sequencing results further, we incubated ECs with C1qa (10 μg/mL) for 30 minutes. Consistent with the RNA sequencing results, quantitative polymerase chain reaction and western blot analysis revealed that the expression of Nr4a1 was significantly downregulated compared with that in the control group (Figure  ; Figure  ). Conversely, after C1qa was silenced in ECs (Figure  ), the expression of Nr4a1 was significantly upregulated (Figure  ; Figure  ).
 Previous studies have shown that Nr4a1 plays a crucial role in regulating the activation of NF‐κB in endothelial cells. , On the basis of this, we put forward the hypothesis that C1q may affect the activation of NF‐κB through its interaction with Nr4a1 in endothelial cells. Subsequently, we overexpressed Nr4a1 in primary aortic endothelial cells (Figure  ) and then incubated them with C1qa. The results revealed that Nr4a1 reduced C1q‐induced NF‐κB reporter activity (Figure  ), weakened p65 nuclear translocation (Figure  and Figure  ), and increased IκBα expression (Figure  ), which is consistent with previous research results that overexpression of Nr4a1 in endothelial cells increases the expression of IκBα and inhibits NF‐κB activation. These results indicate that C1q can promote NF‐κB activation by downregulating Nr4a1 in endothelial cells.

## Nr4a1 Upregulation Improves C1q‐Induced Endothelial Inflammation
To evaluate whether Nr4a1 affects C1q‐induced endothelial inflammation, we overexpressed Nr4a1 in ECs and then incubated them with C1qa (10 μg/mL). Compared with those in the control group, the levels of inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules, including TNF‐α, interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1β, MCP‐1, ICAM‐1, and VCAM‐1, were significantly lower (Figure  ). Additionally, overexpression of Nr4a1 also reduced C1q‐induced ROS production (Figure  ) and significantly diminished the rearrangement of the endothelial cytoskeleton (Figure  ). These results suggest that Nr4a1 can improve C1q‐induced inflammation and oxidative stress.
 Considering that C1q can inhibit the expression of Nr4a1 in the aortic ECs, we attempted to increase Nr4a1 expression in vivo. Cytosporone‐B is the first selective agonist discovered to increase Nr4a1 transcriptional activity. We treated mice overexpressing C1qa with cytosporone‐B (5 mg/kg, 5 days of intraperitoneal injection) and found that the expression of Nr4a1 was significantly increased (Figure  ; Figure  ). Notably, compared with that in the control group, ROS production in the aortic EC layer of the aortic segment of diabetic mice was reduced (Figure  ). Furthermore, we observed that cytosporone‐B led to a significant decrease in key inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules, including TNF‐α, IL‐6, IL‐1β, MCP‐1, ICAM‐1, and VCAM‐1, in the aortic ECs of diabetic mice (Figure  ). Immunohistochemical analysis also revealed the downregulation of interleukin‐1β, TNF‐α, and MCP‐1 expression (Figure  and Figure  ; Figure  ). Next, we evaluated the vascular permeability of the aorta in diabetic mice via Evans blue staining. Compared with the control, cytosporone‐B caused a significant reduction in Evans blue dye leakage and permeability (Figure  ). These results indicate that cytosporone‐B can improve C1q‐induced aortic lesions by increasing the expression of Nr4a1.

## 
dl‐Citrulline Treatment‐Mediated C1q Downregulation Improves Aortic Endothelial Inflammation in Diabetic Mice
Given that C1q can effectively regulate vascular function, we analyzed the 3‐dimensional structure of C1qa via molecular docking. The predictions suggested that dl‐citrulline could bind to C1qa (Figure  and Figure  ). Furthermore, luciferase reporter gene assays demonstrated that dl‐citrulline could reduce the transcriptional activity of C1qa (Figure  ). Therefore, when we treated primary aortic ECs isolated from diabetic mice with dl‐citrulline (300 μM), we observed a decrease in C1qa protein expression (Figure  ). In addition, there was a reduction in ROS production in endothelial cells (Figure  ), a decrease in p65 nuclear translocation (Figure  ), and an increase in IκBα expression (Figure  ).
 Subsequently, we established diabetic mouse models (Figure  ) and administered dl‐citrulline (250 mg/kg) to these diabetic mice via gavage for 4 weeks. It was observed that the expression of C1qa in aortic ECs was significantly decreased (Figure  ; Figure  ) and the expression of Nr4a1 was increased (Figure  ). Additionally, dl‐citrulline reduced ROS production in aortic ECs (Figure  ), decreased the expression of inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules such as TNF‐α, interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1β, MCP‐1, ICAM‐1, and VCAM‐1 (Figure  ), decreased vascular segment permeability (Figure  ), reduced p65 nuclear translocation (Figure  ), and increased IκBα levels (Figure  ). Overall, these data suggest that dl‐citrulline–mediated C1q reduction is a viable therapeutic strategy to prevent diabetic aortic endothelial inflammation.

## 
Hoxa3 Downregulates C1q and Exerts a Protective Effect on Diabetic Mouse Aortic Endothelial Inflammation
To identify potential upstream regulators of C1q, we consulted the PROMO database, , which predicted Hoxa3, Tgif, and Usf2 as candidates. Considering the upregulation of C1q in diabetic aortic ECs and its role, we first examined the expression of these genes in diabetic mouse aortic ECs. Quantitative polymerase chain reaction experiments revealed that Hoxa3 was significantly downregulated and that Usf2 was significantly upregulated in diabetic mice (Figure  ). Next, we overexpressed Hoxa3, Tgif, and Usf2 in primary aortic ECs and found that Hoxa3 inhibited the expression of C1qa, whereas the other 2 genes did not significantly change, indicating a reverse regulatory relationship between Hoxa3 and C1q (Figure  ). Furthermore, based on the activity of the C1qa promoter, luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that Hoxa3 could reduce the transcriptional activity of C1qa (Figure  ). These results suggest that Hoxa3 is a negative regulator of C1q.
 
Hoxa3 is a transcription factor that promotes diabetic wound healing , ; however, its function in diabetic aortic endothelial dysfunction is unclear. Here, we introduced endothelial‐specific AAVs overexpressing Hoxa3 and injected them into diabetic mice via the tail vein alongside control viruses. Three weeks later, we observed a significant increase in Hoxa3 expression in the aortic ECs of diabetic mice (Figure  ; Figure  ), along with a reduction in C1q expression (Figure  ; Figure  ) and an increase in Nr4a1 expression (Figure  ). Additionally, mice overexpressing Hoxa3 presented reduced aortic endothelial ROS production (Figure  ), decreased expression of the inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules TNF‐α, interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1β, MCP‐1, ICAM‐1, and VCAM‐1 (Figure  through Figure  ; Figure  ), and reduced vascular segment permeability (Figure  ). Overall, these findings reveal that Hoxa3 negatively regulates C1q in diabetic aortic ECs. Hoxa3 overexpression effectively downregulates C1q and activates Nr4a1, protecting diabetic aortic ECs.

## DISCUSSION
The upregulation of C1q in patients with diabetes may be associated with the promotion of inflammation and immune activation , , ; however, its specific role in vascular ECs remains unclear. In our previous research, we demonstrated that C1q is upregulated in the aortic ECs of diabetic mice and participates in the regulation of vascular endothelial dysfunction ; this is consistent with previous findings that activation of the complement system is closely linked to the development and progression of diabetic complications, , , although the underlying mechanism has not been fully elucidated. In this study, we first revealed that C1q upregulation in the aortas of diabetic mice exacerbates endothelial dysfunction by downregulating the expression of Nr4a1, a key transcription factor involved in endothelial protection. This dysregulation activates the NF‐κB signaling pathway, leading to inflammation and further endothelial damage. Using molecular docking, we identified dl‐citrulline as a compound that can inhibit C1q and activate Nr4a1, improving vascular endothelial dysfunction. Hoxa3, a negative regulator of C1q, was found to have a protective effect on aortic endothelial function in diabetic mice when overexpressed. These results underscore the importance of C1q in the pathophysiology of diabetic vascular complications, as it connects the immune system and endothelial dysfunction.
Endothelial dysfunction is a central feature in the pathogenesis of diabetic vascular complications, contributing to both microvascular and macrovascular damage. , One of the emerging players in this process is the complement system, particularly the complement protein C1q, which has been implicated in modulating inflammation and endothelial dysfunction. , Studies have found that C1q can target ECs and induce the expression of adhesion molecules, as well as interleukin‐8 and interleukin‐6 in these cells. Recent studies have further emphasized that C1q not only plays a pivotal role in the immune response but also directly affects endothelial function in diabetic conditions. , Our findings suggest that C1q upregulation in the aortas of diabetic mice exacerbates endothelial dysfunction by downregulating the expression of Nr4a1, a key transcription factor involved in endothelial protection. , , 

Multiple studies have confirmed that Nr4a1 functions as a protective mediator: its deficiency exacerbates aortic atherosclerosis , and pulmonary vascular injury. , However, in cerebral ischemia models, Nr4a1 has been found to promote EC apoptosis ; in contrast, studies by Wu et al revealed that Nle4‐D‐Phe7‐α–melanocyte‐stimulating hormone, by binding to the melanocortin‐1 receptor, can improve neuroinflammation and blood–brain barrier disruption after intracerebral hemorrhage in mice via the cAMP response element–binding protein/Nr4a1/NF‐κB pathway. In addition, Nr4a1 is also associated with cardiac remodeling, cardiac fibrosis, and subsequent diastolic dysfunction ; while studies by Wang et al have shown that Nr4a1 can alleviate renal fibrosis by inhibiting vascular endothelial growth factor–induced angiogenesis. These conflicting findings suggest that Nr4a1's function may depend on the local microenvironment (such as cell type–specific signals or stages of inflammation). In our study, we focused on aortic ECs in the context of chronic diabetic inflammation, where the protective role of Nr4a1 is consistent with previous studies; namely, Nr4a1 can improve endothelial dysfunction in diabetes. , 

NF‐κB is a key regulator of the inflammatory response. Chronic activation of NF‐κB in ECs leads to increased expression of proinflammatory cytokines, adhesion molecules, and other mediators that disrupt vascular integrity. Nr4a1 can dynamically regulate NF‐κB activity, either by inhibiting or enhancing the expression of target genes, thereby altering inflammatory outcomes. , In ECs, Nr4a1 can inhibit NF‐κB activation and proinflammatory cytokine production by increasing IκBα expression. Similarly, in macrophages, Nr4a1 can suppress lipopolysaccharide‐induced NF‐κB activation by inhibiting the binding of p65 to DNA. , Furthermore, the association between complement system and NF‐κB activation has been reported in various diseases. , Wang et al demonstrated that astrocytes and microglia form a positive feedback loop through C3/C3a receptor/NF‐κB signal crosstalk, which irreversibly induces the formation of A1 astrocytes. Additionally, Song et al confirmed that activation of the NF‐κB pathway may be a key intermediate in C1q‐mediated macrophage polarization in lung adenocarcinoma. In the present study, we further found that C1q activates NF‐κB transcriptional activity by reducing Nr4a1, leading to increased levels of inflammatory factors and adhesion molecules in ECs, which provides a novel mechanistic explanation for the crosstalk between C1q and NF‐κB. These findings suggest that targeting the C1q‐Nr4a1 signaling axis may be a therapeutic strategy to alleviate endothelial dysfunction and prevent the progression of diabetic vascular complications.

Hoxa3 is a transcription factor that promotes diabetic wound healing and has been shown to enhance angiogenesis, reepithelialization, and macrophage polarization during healing. , , However, the potential protective role of Hoxa3 in vascular endothelial dysfunction and its impact on the regulation of C1q have not been clearly elucidated. Here, we further demonstrated that Hoxa3 is a negative regulator of C1q, and overexpression of Hoxa3 can protect against endothelial dysfunction in diabetic mice by inhibiting the upregulation of C1q and its downstream inflammatory effects. This indicates that Hoxa3 is a potential therapeutic tool for diabetic endothelial dysfunction and also lays a theoretical foundation for the development of precision therapeutic strategies targeting Hoxa3. However, the precise molecular mechanisms by which Hoxa3 regulates C1q and its broader impact on endothelial function remain to be fully understood.
Furthermore, the identification of dl‐citrulline as a potential inhibitor of C1q provides a promising avenue for therapeutic intervention. Citrulline is known to have vasodilatory effects and has been shown to improve endothelial function. , We found that dl‐citrulline can inhibit C1q activity and restore Nr4a1 expression, suggesting a new perspective on its potential application in the treatment of diabetic endothelial dysfunction. However, further research is needed to confirm its clinical efficacy and explore its broader therapeutic potential in diabetic vascular diseases.
In addition, diabetes can affect multiple vascular beds, such as coronary arteries and retinal microvessels. A substantial body of research has indicated that the C1q and Nr4a1 pathways may play conserved roles in other diabetes‐associated vascular systems. , , Nevertheless, different vascular beds exhibit variations in their structural and functional requirements. Thus, in‐depth investigation into the role of the C1q/Nr4a1 signaling axis within these blood vessels is of great significance for future research. Further, sex differences have been observed in diabetes‐related cardiovascular diseases, which are the main causes of morbidity and death in patients with diabetes. Studies have confirmed that estrogen helps enhance innate immunity, inhibit inflammatory responses, and improve vascular function. , Studies by Venegas‐Pino et al have also found that the absence of estrogen promotes the development of hyperglycemia and atherosclerosis. In the present study, we used only male mice as research subjects. In future studies, we will further explore sex‐specific dynamic changes to ensure that the therapeutic potential of the C1q/Nr4a1 pathway is applicable to both sexes.
In conclusion, our study reveals the important molecular interactions between C1q and diabetic endothelial dysfunction, providing valuable insights into the role of C1q in diabetic endothelial dysfunction and highlighting potential therapeutic strategies, including the use of dl‐citrulline and the modulation of Hoxa3 expression. Targeting the C1q/Nr4a1 pathway may offer a novel approach to improving vascular health in patients with diabetes and preventing the progression of diabetes‐related cardiovascular complications. Further research is needed to validate these findings and explore the clinical applicability of these potential interventions.
