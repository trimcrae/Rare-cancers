## Highlights
•TCF7L1 is upregulated in a mouse model of AAAs.
•TCF7L1 overexpression increases the formation of AAAs induced by Ang II. Knockdown TCF7L1 significantly reduces the formation of AAAs induced by Ang II.
•TCF7L1 overexpression promotes VSMC phenotypic transformation, while knocking down prevents VSMC phenotypic transformation in vitro.
•TCF7L1 overexpression attenuates the transcriptional activity of SRF, which is known to critically regulate VSMC phenotypic stability.

## Summary
Phenotypic switching of vascular smooth muscle cells is a central process in abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) pathology. We found that knockdown TCF7L1 (transcription factor 7-like 1), a member of the TCF/LEF (T cell factor/lymphoid enhancer factor) family of transcription factors, inhibits vascular smooth muscle cell differentiation. This study hints at potential interventions to maintain a normal, differentiated smooth muscle cell state, thereby eliminating the pathogenesis of AAA. In addition, our study provides insights into the potential use of TCF7L1 as a biomarker for AAA.

The reported prevalence of AAAs ranges from 2% to 12%, and approximately 8% of men over the 65 years of age have AAAs. Patients with AAAs are usually asymptomatic until the aneurysm ruptures, causing serious illness and high mortality. , , , Effective therapeutic drugs for patients with AAAs are lacking, and open or endovascular surgery is the only treatment option. , Thus, understanding the development of AAAs is required to identify new therapeutic targets.
AAAs result from elastin loss, smooth muscle cell apoptosis, and compensatory collagen deposition. , Further, phenotype switching of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) has an important impact in the occurrence and development of AAAs. , , , , VSMCs are the most prevalent cell type in arterial vessels and are essential for maintaining the blood vessel structure and homeostasis. , , , , Under pathophysiological conditions, contractile smooth muscle cells (SMCs) switch to a secretory type that facilitates their migration to the intima and proliferation, contributing to neointimal lesions. , , Phenotypic switching of VSMCs is a distinguishing feature of various vascular remodeling diseases such as atherosclerosis and neointimal hyperplasia after vascular injury. Accumulating evidence suggests that the phenotypic switching of VSMCs directly participates in the AAA pathogenesis.
Transcription factor 7-like 1 (TCF7L1), also known as TCF3 (high-mobility group box transcription factor), is a member of the LEF/TCF (lymphoid enhancer factor/T cell factor) family of transcription factors that interact with β-catenin and act as DNA-binding proteins. , , TCF7L1 regulates Wnt-β-catenin and c-Myc (cellular Myc) signaling by directly binding to multiple genes and interacting with proteins, thereby playing a role in tumor cell differentiation, proliferation, and malignancy. The highly conserved Wnt signaling pathway regulates critical processes during the embryonic development, including cell fate, and has been implicated in several pathologies. , , , , Recently, Wnt signaling has also been suggested to regulate VSMC differentiation , ; however, the role of TCF7L1 in AAA development is poorly understood. In this study, we aimed to investigate the potential role of TCF7L1 in angiotensin II (Ang II)–induced AAAs in a mouse model and to understand the underlying mechanisms.

## Methods

## Experimental animals
Male ApoE-/- mice (8 weeks of age) used in this study were purchased from GemPharmatech. All mice were raised in a pathogen-free environment with the temperature maintained at 22 °C and 60% to 65% humidity. The experiment started after 1 week of acclimatization. All mice had free access to normal mouse food. All experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command and were conducted in accordance with existing guidelines on the care and use of laboratory animals. All animal care and experimental protocols complied with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

## Ang II–induced murine AAA model
Eight-week-old ApoE-/- mice were infused with saline (0.9% sodium chloride) or Ang II (1,000 ng/kg/min) via an osmotic pump (Alzet model 1004; AlzaCorp) for 28 days. Ang II (A1042) was purchased from APExBIO Technology. Briefly, mice were anesthetized with inhaled isoflurane and the minipumps were implanted into the subcutaneous space of the mice in the back of the neck. Twenty-eight days later, aortic diameters were measured using ultrasound (Visual Sonics). The aortic tissues were harvested for RNA, protein, and morphological or histological analyses. AAA incidence was defined by an increase of external aorta diameter by 50% or greater as compared with aortas from saline-infused mice.

## Adeno-associated virus–mediated TCF7L1 interference and overexpression in vivo
To explore the role of TCF7L1 during the occurrence and development of AAAs, adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) AAV-cmv-TCF7L1 and AAV-sh-TCF7L1 and the negative control were injected into ApoE-/- mice through the tail vein (2 ×1011 vector genomes). The target sequence of mouse TCF7L1 small interfering RNA (siRNA) was as follows: 5′-GAGAAGAACAGGCCAAATA-3′. Serotype 2/8 adeno-associated virus (AAV2/8) carrying this sequence (AAV2/8-sh-TCF7L1) or the scramble control (AAV-sh-RNA) was generated by OBIO Technology Company. Full-length mouse TCF7L1 complementary DNA (cDNA) (NM_001079822.2) was cloned into the pAAV-CMV-EGFP-P2A-3xFLAG-WPRE plasmid. AAV2/8 carrying the plasmid (AAV2/8-cmv-TCF7L1) or the scramble control (AAV-cmv-GFP) was also generated by the OBIO Technology Company.
Twenty-one days after AAV transfection, ApoE-/- mice were randomly grouped and treated with Ang II (1,000 ng/kg/min) or saline using an osmotic pump for 4 weeks. The survival rate and the incidence of AAAs were calculated. After 4 weeks, aortic diameters were measured using ultrasound. The aortic tissues were harvested for RNA, protein, and morphological or histological analyses.

## Ultrasonic imaging
The aortic diameters were examined using a small animal ultrasound system Vevo 2100 apparatus (Visual Sonics) equipped with a 30-MHz probe to check the incidence of AAAs and to measure 3 representative parameters, including the diameter of the aorta, superior renal artery, and inferior renal artery. Each mouse was anesthetized with 2% isoflurane throughout the ultrasonography process. The artery diameters were assessed by a blinded researcher.

## Morphometric analysis
Murine aortas were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. After fixation, tissues were embedded, cut, and stained. Briefly, hematoxylin and eosin staining were used for morphological evaluation, Masson's trichrome staining (Solarbio) was used to evaluate collagen deposition, and Verhoeff Van Gieson Elastic (Sigma-Aldrich) was used to evaluate elastin. The extent of collagen content was expressed as the collagen volume fraction (CVF). The CVF was calculated as the ratio of collagen-specific staining to the total tissue area. CVF was measured using Image-Pro Plus software 7.0 (Meyer Instruments): CVF (%) = collagen area / tissue total area × 100%. Elastin degradation was scored as follows: 1) no degradation and a well-organized lamina; 2) mild degradation with some interruptions or breaks in the lamina; 3) moderate degradation with multiple interruptions or breaks in the lamina; and 4) severe fragmentation or loss or aortic rupture.

## Immunohistochemistry
Paraffin sections of mouse aorta were dehydrated and blocked with 5% goat serum (X0907) in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Endogenous peroxidase was used to block nonspecific binding sites. The sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with mouse anti-α smooth muscle actin (SMA) (1:100; Abcam), rabbit anti-SM22α antibody (1:100; Abcam), or rabbit anti-TCF7L1 (1:100; Abcam) using an immunohistochemistry kit (MXB Biotechnologies). The sections were photographed using a microscope (Zeiss). All specimens were counterstained with DAB and hematoxylin, dehydrated, and mounted on slides. Two independent, blinded researchers randomly evaluated areas on at least 3 slides.

## VSMC isolation and culture
Mouse primary VSMCs were isolated from freshly dissected aortas. Briefly, the tunica media was separated by peeling off the tunica intima from aortic tissue in PBS. The tunica media was cut into about 2-mm pieces and digested with 0.15% type II collagenase (Sigma-Aldrich). VSMCs were then seeded into 6-well plates and cultured in fresh Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium containing 20% fetal bovine serum (Biochrome AG).

## Immunofluorescence staining
We used mouse primary VSMCs between the third and sixth passages for immunofluorescence assays. The cells were cultured in serum-free medium and treated with Ang II (1 μM) for 24 hours. Germ-free coverslips were placed in 6-well plates. Then each well was seeded with 105 VSMCs and cultured for 24 hours. After culturing, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes at room temperature. The cells were washed 3 times in PBS and incubated with PBS containing 0.1% Triton X-100 for 15 minutes at room temperature. The cells were incubated with mouse anti-αSMA (1:100; Abcam), rabbit anti-SM22α (1:100; Abcam), and rabbit anti-TCF7L1 (1:100; CST) for overnight. The cells were washed with PBS and incubated with goat anti-rabbit or goat anti-mouse secondary antibody for 2 hours at 37 °C in the dark. The cells were then incubated with DAPI for 5 minutes. Then, the coverslips were taken out and the cells were mounted using ProLong Gold antifade reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Finally, the cells were imaged with a fluorescence microscope (Zeiss).

## siRNA Transfection
For establishing the cells with TCF7L1 and serum response factor (SRF) knockdown, siRNA against mouse TCF7L1 (siTCF7L1), SRF (siSRF), and scramble siRNA were synthesized by RIBOBIO. The target sequence of TCF7L1 was GAGAAGAACAGGCCAAATA and the target sequence of SRF was CAGTTCAGCTTCACCAGAT. VSMCs were transfected with siRNA (20 nM) using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

## Plasmid construction
For establishing the cells with TCF7L1 or SRF overexpression, VSMCs were transfected with mouse TCF7L1 overexpression plasmid (pcDNA3.1 TCF7L1), SRF overexpression plasmid (pcDNA3.1 SRF), or control vector (pcDNA3.1FLAG) for 24 hours, which were designed and constructed by OBIO Technology Company. Transfections were performed with Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

## Cell migration assays
Cell migration was measured using wound healing and Transwell assays. For wound healing assays, 90% confluent VSMCs were serum-starved overnight. Next, the VSMC layer was scratched with a sterile 10-μL pipette tip. The cells were washed with PBS and treated with Ang II for 24 hours. The cells were imaged every 24 hours using a light microscope (Olympus). Each experiment was carried out for 3 times. The relative gap distance was calculated as follows: relative gap distance = (width at 24 hours or 0 hours) / (width at 0 hours in the control group). For Transwell assays, 2.5 × 104 cells were seeded into the upper chamber of a Transwell chamber and cultured in serum-free medium. The lower chamber contained 10% Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium. After 24 hours, the migrated cells were fixed with methanol and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (Solarbio) for 20 minutes at room temperature. The number of migrated cells was quantified by counting the number of cells from 10 random fields at ×40 magnification.

## Western blotting
Mouse aortic tissues and VSMCs were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer. The protein was separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies: mouse anti-αSMA (1:1,000; Abcam), rabbit anti-SM22α (1:1,000; Abcam), rabbit anti-matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-2 (1:1,000; Abcam), rabbit anti-TCF7L1 (1:1,000; CST), or rabbit anti-SRF (1:1,000; CST). β-actin was as the internal reference (1:1,000; CST). Then, the membranes were incubated with mouse or rabbit secondary antibodies at the ratio of 1:1,000 for 1 hour at room temperature for 2 hours at room temperature.

## Real-time polymerase chain reaction
Total RNA from aortic tissues and VSMCs was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The isolated RNA was used to produce cDNA using a PrimeScript RT with gDNA Eraser kit (Takara). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed in triplicate using SYBR1 Premix Ex Taq II (Takara). The primer sequences were as follows: TCF7L1, 5′-ACGAGCTGATCCCCTTCCA-3′ (forward) and 5′-CAGGGACGACTTGACCTCAT-3′ (reverse); αSMA, 5′-GTCCCAGACATC AGGGAGTAA-3′ (forward) and 5′-TCGGATACTTCAGCGTCAGGA-3′ (reverse); SM22α, 5′-CAACAAGGGTCCATCCTACGG-3′ (forward) and 5′-ATCTGGGCGGCC TACATCA-3′ (reverse); SRF, 5′-CGAGATGGAGATCGGTATGG-3′ (forward) and 5′-GGGTCTTCTTACCCGGCTTG-3′ (reverse); and GAPDH, 5′-AGGTCGGTGTG AACGGATTTG-3′ (forward) and 5′-GGGGTCGTTGATGGCAACA-3′ (reverse). The average cycle threshold was used to quantify relative gene expression. Relative gene expression levels were calculated by 2-ΔΔCt method.

## JASPAR database analysis
The JASPAR database is a collection of transcription factor binding sites identified using published eukaryotic experiments. Putative TCF7L1 binding sites in the SRF were identified by searching the JASPAR database.

## Luciferase assays
The 293T cells were seeded in 96-well plates for 24 hours and were transfected with pGL3-SRF-luciferase reporter (GENEWIZ), luciferase control reporter vector, and pcDNA3.1 TCF7L1 plasmids in the presence of pcDNA3.1 FLAG vector. Transfection was performed with Lipofectamine 2000. At 24 hours after transfection, luciferase activity was measured using a dual luciferase analysis system kit (Promega).

## Statistical analysis
All values are expressed as mean ± SEM. Data were analyzed using Prism 7 software (GraphPad Software). Normality tests were performed using Shapiro-Wilk statistics. Differences between 2 groups were compared using unpaired Student’s t-test. Differences among 3 or more groups were compared using 1-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple pairwise comparisons. The Kaplan-Meier survival curve was used to analyze the survival percentages of saline- or Ang II–infused mice. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## TCF7L1 expression is up-regulated in the aortic tissues of Ang II–induced AAAs in vivo
To elucidate the potential role of TCF7L1 in the development of AAAs, we investigated TCF7L1 expression in a mouse model of AAAs generated by infusing ApoE-/- mice with Ang II. Control group mice were infused with saline for 28 days. Compared with control mice, AAAs developed 28 days after Ang II infusion in ApoE-/- mice, indicating that AAA model was successfully established ( ).
Real-time PCR and Western blotting showed that both TCF7L1 messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein levels were significantly higher in aortic tissues of mice with Ang II–induced AAAs than those of the control mice. Furthermore, SM22α and αSMA, which are VSMC markers, were significantly decreased at the mRNA and protein levels. The protein level of MMP-2 was increased ( ). Immunohistochemical staining confirmed that the expression of TCF7L1 was significantly higher, while those of SM22α and αSMA were significantly lower in mice with AAAs than that of the control mice ( ). These findings suggest that TCF7L1 might be involved in AAA formation. 
To further elucidate the potential role of TCF7L1 in aneurysm development, we examined TCF7L1 expression in human AAA tissues and compared it with the expression in the adjacent aortic tissues (control tissues) using immunohistochemical staining and Western blotting. We found that TCF7L1 was also upregulated in human AAA tissues compared with control tissues ( ).

## TCF7L1 expression is up-regulated during Ang II–induced phenotypic switching in vitro
To investigate the role of TCF7L1 in VSMC phenotype switching, VSMCs were treated with Ang II for 24 hours. VSMCs of the control group were treated with PBS for 24 hours. We found that both mRNA and protein levels of TCF7L1 were significantly higher in VSMCs treated with Ang II than those in the control VSMCs. Matrix remodeling throughout the vasculature is associated with systemic enhancement of MMP-2 expression in VSMCs. The expressions of αSMA and SM22α were down-regulated, while MMP-2 was up-regulated ( ). Similar results were observed using immunofluorescence staining ( ).

## TCF7L1 deficiency alleviates Ang II–induced AAA formation
To further identify whether TCF7L1 plays a causative role in AAA formation, we selected an AAV carrying TCF7L1 siRNA to perform loss-of-function studies. Group details are shown in . Compared with the saline group, the AAV-sh-TCF7L1 group had a significantly enhanced aortic green fluorescent protein signal, indicating a high efficiency of viral transfection ( ). Moreover, TCF7L1 mRNA and protein levels were significantly decreased in aortic tissues of the AAV-sh-TCF7L1 group compared with the AAV-sh-RNA group ( ). Twenty-one days after transfection, mice were treated with Ang II (1,000 ng/kg/min) or saline for 4 weeks.
The incidence of AAAs in the Ang II–induced AAV-sh-RNA group was 56% but the incidence of AAAs decreased by 20% in ApoE-/- mice treated with Ang II and AAV-sh-TCF7L1 ( ). However, there was no significant difference in the survival rate between the AAV-sh-RNA-AngⅡ and AAV-sh-TCF7L1-AngⅡ groups ( ). Ultrasound images showed that the maximum abdominal aortic diameter was decreased by TCF7L1 knockdown ( ). Correspondingly, there was less elastin degradation in the aortic tissues of the AAV-sh-TCF7L1-Ang II group than that in the AAV-GFP-Ang II group. Similarly, lower collagen disruption was observed in the abdominal aortic tissues of the AAV-sh-TCF7L1-Ang II group. Immunohistochemical staining showed that αSMA and SM22α were significantly up-regulated in the AAV-sh-TCF7L1-Ang II group compared with the AAV-GFP-Ang II group ( ). Upregulated αSMA and SM22α expression and down-regulated MMP-2 expression were detected in the AAV-sh-TCF7L1 group compared with the AAV-sh-RNA group, as assessed by Western blotting ( ). These results indicate that TCF7L1 knockdown inhibited AAA formation and was related to VSMCs phenotypic switching. 

## TCF7L1 overexpression amplifies Ang II–induced AAA formation in ApoE-/- mice
We used an AAV carrying a TCF7L1 overexpression plasmid (AAV-cmv-TCF7L1) to perform gain-of-function studies in Ang II–perfused ApoE-/- mice. Twenty-one days after AAV injection, the aortic green fluorescent protein signal in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1 group was significantly enhanced compared with the saline group ( ). Moreover, the overexpression plasmid group showed significantly increased TCF7L1 mRNA and protein expression compared with the saline group ( ). Beginning 21 days after transfection, mice were treated with Ang II (1,000 ng/kg/min) or saline for 4 weeks. The incidence of AAAs in the Ang II–induced AAV-GFP group was 60%. However, the incidence of AAAs increased by 20% in ApoE-/- mice treated with Ang II and AAV-cmv-TCF7L1 ( ). However, there was no significant difference in the survival rate between the AAV-cmv-GFP-Ang Ⅱ and AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang Ⅱ groups ( ). Ultrasound images showed that the maximum abdominal aortic diameter was increased by TCF7L1 overexpression ( ). Correspondingly, elastin degradation in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang II group was severer than that in the AAV-GFP-Ang II group. Increased collagen disruption was observed in the abdominal aortas in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang II group. Immunohistochemical staining showed that αSMA and SM22α expression was lower in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang II group than that of the AAV-GFP-Ang II group ( ). Western blotting results showed that αSMA and SM22α proteins were remarkably down-regulated in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang II group compared with the AAV-GFP-Ang II group, while MMP-2 protein was significantly higher in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1-Ang II group compared with the AAV-GFP-Ang II group ( ). These results revealed that TCF7L1 plays a central role in Ang II–induced AAAs. 

## TCF7L1 knockdown inhibits VSMC phenotypic switching and migration
Previous studies have found that Ang II induced VSMC phenotypic switching. Western blotting and real-time PCR showed that TCF7L1 knockdown decreased MMP-2 protein levels and increased the expression of SM22α and αSMA ( ). Transwell assays demonstrated that knockdown of TCF7L1 suppressed the effect of Ang II on VSMC migration ( ). Similar results were obtained in the cell scratch assay ( ). 

## TCF7L1 overexpression promotes VSMC phenotypic switching and migration
Western blotting and real-time PCR showed that Ang II treatment and TCF7L1 overexpression markedly decreased the expression of αSMA and SM22α, while they increased the MMP-2 protein expression ( ). Consistently, Transwell assays demonstrated that TCF7L1 amplified the effect of Ang II on VSMC migration ( ). Similar results were observed in the cell scratch assay ( ).

## TCF7L1 modulates Ang II–induced VSMC phenotype switching by suppressing SRF transcription
To further clarify the mechanism by which TCF7L1 induces VSMCs dedifferentiation, we examined its effects on the expression of SRF, which is the principal moderator of VSMC-specific genes and is critical for maintaining VSMC homeostasis. After 28 days of Ang II infusion, TCF7L1 protein expression was notably higher in the AAV-cmv-TCF7L1 group, whereas SRF protein expression was significantly lower than that in the AAV-cmv-GFP group ( ). In contrast, TCF7L1 protein levels were significantly lower in the AAV-sh-TCF7L1 group, but SRF protein levels were significantly higher than those in the AAV-sh-RNA group ( ). Similar results were obtained in vitro, with reduced SRF mRNA and protein levels upon TCF7L1 overexpression ( ), while knockdown of TCF7L1 increased SRF mRNA and protein expression ( ). 
As TCF7L1 repressed SRF mRNA levels, we next determined whether TCF7L1 directly binds to the SRF promoter region. We scanned the TCF7L1 binding consensus sequences in SRF promoters by searching the publicly available JASPAR database, an online database for profiling transcription factor binding sequences. Interestingly, we found 2 putative TCF7L1 binding motifs in mouse ( ). To determine whether these 2 putative TCF7L1 binding sequences regulate the transcriptional activity of SRF, we mutated the 2 putative TCF7L1 sites individually and in combination. By transfecting WT or mutated SRF-luciferase reporter plasmids into 293T cells, we observed that TCF7L1 overexpression decreased the SRF transcriptional activity. This suppression was reversed by mutating the first putative TCF7L1 binding site, but mutation of the other putative binding sites did not ( ). Taken together, these results indicate that TCF7L1 modulates Ang II–induced VSMC phenotype switching by attenuating the transcriptional activity of SRF.

## TCF7L1-mediated VSMC phenotype switching is prevented by SRF intervention
To determine whether TCF7L1 regulates VSMC phenotype switching by attenuating the transcriptional activity of SRF, siRNA of SRF was transfected into VSMCs with a TCF7L1 knockdown. The mRNA and protein levels of αSMA, SM22α, and MMP-2 were reversed by SRF knockdown ( ). Transwell assays demonstrated that the effect of TCF7L1 knockdown on VSMC migration was inhibited by SRF ( ). Similar results were observed in the cell scratch assay ( ). Next, we constructed SRF plasmids to perform rescue experiments. During TCF7L1 overexpression, an SRF plasmid was transiently transfected into the VSMCs. Western blotting and real-time PCR showed that the mRNA and protein expression of αSMA and SM22α were increased and MMP-2 was decreased by overexpressing SRF ( ). Transwell assays demonstrated that the effect of TCF7L1 overexpression on VSMC migration was attenuated by SRF overexpression ( ). These results were confirmed by a cell scratch assay ( ). These results indicate that TCF7L1 could regulate VSMCs phenotype switching by attenuating the transcriptional activity of SRF. 

## Discussion
Our results showed that TCF7L1 was upregulated in a mouse model of AAAs. In vivo experiments demonstrated that TCF7L1 overexpression increased the maximal diameter of the abdominal aorta in Ang II–induced AAAs. Knockdown of TCF7L1 significantly reduced Ang II–induced AAA formation in ApoE-/- mice, the diameter of the maximal aorta, and elastin degradation scores. In vitro, SM22α and αSMA mRNA and protein levels were significantly decreased by Ang II treatment, which were rescued by knocking down TCF7L1. Our results reveal that TCF7L1 plays a key role in AAA development.
Studies have shown that VSMC phenotype switching plays an important role in the formation of AAAs. , , , , , VSMCs have high plasticity and can switch from a contractile phenotype to a synthetic phenotype under various stimuli such as inflammation. In healthy blood vessels, VSMCs express several contractile proteins with a low replication rate, including αSMA and SM22α. In response to vascular injury, VSMCs change from a contractile phenotype to a synthetic phenotype, as indicated by decreased αSMA and SM22α expression. 
Compared with the control group treated with PBS, VSMCs stimulated by Ang II showed increased cellular migration. This increase in cellular migration seems to be related to increased extracellular matrix degradation by MMP-2, , proteolytic enzymes that regulate the composition of the extracellular matrix, and dysregulated MMP constitutes an important mechanism in the occurrence and development of AAAs. , In this study, we confirmed that MMP-2 expression is increased by Ang II infusion. TCF7L1 overexpression aggravated Ang II–induced increase in MMP-2 expression in vivo. These findings were also consistent with the results obtained in vitro.
VSMC migration to the intima is an important early stage of stenosis in AAAs. In fact, VSMC migration can explain the existence of undeveloped atherosclerotic lesions in aneurysm tissue. Moreover, inhibiting VSMC migration may attenuate AAA development. Our research showed that TCF7L1 knockdown inhibited VSMC migration induced by Ang II, while TCF7L1 overexpression increased Ang II–induced VSMC migration. These results indicated that TCF7L1 may be considered a target for the treatment of AAAs.
Transcription factors of the TCF and LEF families, including LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1, and TCF7L2, are important mediators of canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling during cardiac development. They are all able to bind with high affinity to specific DNA sequences called Wnt-responsive elements present in numerous promoters and other transcriptional regulatory regions, such as enhancers. , , , Previous studies in the lungs highlighted Wnts as potentially important regulators of VSMC differentiation. , , TCF7L1 is a key regulatory protein that establishes transcriptional programs for cell development and proliferation. In human coronary artery disease–relevant tissues, TCF21 expression is associated with decreased coronary artery disease risk, establishing a protective role for both TCF21 and SMC phenotypic modulation in this disease. 
The MADS box transcription factor SRF plays a key role in phenotypic switching of VSMCs. Theoretically, SRF can bind up to 1,216 permutations of 10-base pair cis elements, which are called the CArG-box. , , SRF binds to the CarG-box element in the promoter region of target genes involved in VSMC differentiation. , , , Our results showed that SRF transcription was repressed when TCF7L1 was overexpressed. However, the opposite results were observed when TCF7L1 was knocked down. SRF promoter contains 2 putative TCF7L1 binding sites. We showed that TCF7L1 represses promoter activation by interacting with binding site 1. However, whether TCF7L1 directly interacts with the putative binding site must be further verified by chromatin immunoprecipitation assays.

## Study Limitations
There are several limitations in this study. First, to better understand the role of TCF7L1 in the formation and development of AAAs, the upstream mechanisms of increased TCF7L1 expression is need to be investigated in the future. Second, to prove that TCF7L1 regulates VSMCs phenotype switching through SRF, we only carried out rescue experiments in vitro. More in vivo experiments are needed to prove this conclusion.

## Conclusions
We revealed that TCF7L1 plays a causative role in AAA formation, which is mediated by VSMC phenotypic switching. These findings provided new insights into the key role of TCF7L1 in AAA pathogenesis. Finally, our findings suggest that TCF7L1 may be an effective therapeutic target for preventing AAA progression.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: Phenotypic switching of VSMCs is a central process in AAA pathology. We found that knockdown TCF7L1 inhibited the phenotypic switching of VSMCs. This study provides insights into the potential use of TCF7L1 as a biomarker for AAAs.
TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: The present study is the first study to show that TCF7L1 could be a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target for AAAs. Additional research is needed to investigate the use of molecules that inhibit TCF7L1 activation that may be potentially useful for the treatment of human aortic aneurysms.
