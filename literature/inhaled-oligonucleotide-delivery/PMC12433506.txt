## Abstract
Goose astrovirus (GAstV) is an important pathogen causing joint and visceral gout in goslings. Its high incidence and mortality had caused enormous economic losses to the goose farming industry. To achieve the simultaneous and rapid identification and detection of Goose astrovirus genotype 1 (GAstV-1) and genotype 2 (GAstV-2), the present study aimed to design and synthesize specific recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) primers and exo probes targeting the ORF1b gene of GAstV. Utilizing a portable fluorescence detector (Genie III), the RPA primers and exo probes were optimized for concentration, and the reaction temperature was adjusted to establish a dual real-time reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-qRPA) assay for the simultaneous identification and detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2. The specificity and sensitivity of the assay were further evaluated. The results demonstrated that the established dual RT-qRPA assay could complete detection within 20 minutes at 42 °C, the specific amplification curves for GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were produced in the assay, with no amplifications for other tested common goose pathogens. Utilizing the in vitro transcribed RNA as templates, the detection limits for GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were determined to be 109 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 87.93-169.73 copies/reaction) and 86 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 65.11-179.02 copies/reaction), respectively. The developed dual RT-qRPA assay was further evaluated on 104 liver and cloacal swab samples from goslings, The GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were detected in 19 (18.27 %, 19/104) and 69 (66.35 %, 69/104) samples, respectively, in which 16 samples (15.38 %, 16/104) carried both GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 nucleic acids. These results were consistent with those obtained using the dual RT-qPCR assay, showing a 100 % diagnostic concordance. Further analysis indicated a good linear relationship between the TT values from RT-qRPA and the CT values from RT-qPCR, with R2 values of 0.965 and 0.948 for GAstV-1 and GAstV-2, respectively. The developed dual RT-qRPA assay in this study performed well on the clinical samples, thereby providing robust technical support for the rapid field differential detection of GAstV infections in goose flocks.
Keywords: GAstV-1, GAstV-2, ORF1b gene, Dual real-time RT-RPA, Rapid field differential detection

## Introduction
Goose astrovirus (GAstV) belongs to the genus Avastrovirus in the Astroviridae family and is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus, named for its characteristic star-shaped protrusions on the surface ( ). The viral genome is approximately 7.2 kb in length, consisting of a 5′-untranslated region (5′-UTR), three open reading frames (ORF1a, ORF1b, and ORF2), a 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR), and a polyadenylated (Poly A) tail. Among these, ORF1a and ORF1b encode non-structural proteins, such as RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp), serine protease motifs (Pro), and nuclear localization signals (NLS), which play critical roles in viral replication and transcription. ORF2 encodes the structural protein (capsid protein, Cap), which is involved in processes including viral adsorption, invasion, and host-specific immune responses ( ). Based on genetic homology, GAstV can be classified into Goose astrovirus genotype 1 (GAstV-1) and Goose astrovirus genotype 2 (GAstV-2), with a whole-genome similarity of 57.9 % between the two genotypes ( ).
GAstV is a significant pathogen causing articular and visceral gout in goslings, mainly infecting goslings aged 4 to 16 days. ( ). Goslings' gout in affected goslings is characterized by typical urate deposits in the viscera and joint cavities, leading to symptoms such as diarrhea and paralysis, with infection and mortality rates as high as 80 % and 50 %, respectively ( ; ; ). Since its outbreak in Shandong Province, China, in 2016, gout in gooslings has become a major epidemic affecting the healthy development of China's goose industry, causing huge economic losses ( ). GAstV-1 was the first genotype reported, represented by the FLX strain ( ) and endemic in the provinces of Jiangsu ( ) and Jiangxi ( ). Subsequently, an epidemic characterized by gout as the primary symptom was reported in the goose-farming provinces of Shandong, Sichuan, and Heilongjiang, from which strain SD01 ( ) was isolated and identified as GAstV-2. Currently, GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 are widely spread in China, but GAstV-2 has become the predominant genotype in the epidemic ( ) and co-infection with GAstV-1 is common ( ; ). Notably, the presence of GAstV has also been reported in goose embryos, Cherry Valley chicks and ducks ( , ; ), suggesting the risk of vertical and cross-species transmission of the virus, which poses difficulties in the prevention and control of GAstV.
Due to the similarity of clinical symptoms caused by GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 infections in geese and the presence of co-infection, it is difficult to achieve accurate identification of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 based on clinical manifestations alone. Traditional virus isolation methods ( ) are time-consuming and laborious, and cannot meet the needs of rapid clinical diagnosis. Various molecular biology assays, such as RT-PCR and RT-qPCR ( ; ; ; ; ), have been widely used for GAstV detection, but their reliance on high-precision, costly instrumentation, specialized technicians, and good laboratory environments is difficult to be met in the grass-roots laboratories or on-site assays, where equipment is in short supply. In recent years, nucleic acid isothermal amplification technology has effectively compensated for the lack of PCR technology in the detection of GAstV. The reverse transcription loop-mediated isothermal amplification (RT-LAMP) assay ( ) can amplify target fragments at a constant temperature of 60 °C. However, this assay has a long amplification time (60 min), requires a high termination temperature (80 °C), and uses six primers in one reaction, which increases the difficulty of primer design. Recombinase Polymerase Amplification (RPA) assay ( ) relies mainly on recombinases that bind single-stranded nucleic acids (oligonucleotide primers), single-stranded DNA-binding proteins (SSB), and strand-replacing DNA polymerases ( ), which are free from expensive thermal cycling experimental setups and are capable of amplifying the target fragments within 10-20 min at a constant temperature of 37-43 °C. Currently, RPA technology has been widely used for on-site rapid detection of a variety of animal diseases ( ; , ), showing great potential for immediate detection in grassroots laboratories or in the field.
In this study, the specific RPA primers and exo probes were designed to target the conserved ORF1b genes of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2, and a dual real-time reverse transcription recombinase amplification (RT-qRPA) assay was developed for the simultaneous and rapid detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2. The assay could provide technical support for the rapid on-site differential detection of GAstV and facilitate the rapid diagnosis of their infection.

## Materials and methods

## Virus and clinical samples
GAstV-1 (HeB-SJZ strain), GAstV-2 (HeB-BD1 strain) and Fowl adenovirus serotype 4 (FAdV-4) were isolated and preserved in the Preventive Veterinary Laboratory of Hebei Agricultural University, China. The viral genomic nucleic acids of Avian influenza virus subtype H9 (AIV-H9), Novel duck reovirus (NDRV), Muscovy duck parvovirus (MDPV), and Goose parvovirus (GPV) were all stored in the Technical Center of Shijiazhuang Customs, China.
One hundred and four liver and cloacal swab samples from 12 to 27 days old goslings were collected from different regions in Hebei Province, China, between August 2024 and May 2025, including 58 liver samples and 46 cloacal swab samples. For the 104 samples, 68 were collected from goslings exhibiting clinical symptoms of gout (57 liver samples and 11 cloacal swab samples) and 36 were collected from those without clinical symptoms of gout (1 liver sample and 35 cloacal swab samples).

## Viral and sample DNA/RNA extraction
For liver samples, 50 mg was clipped into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, 1 mL of PBS buffer and 4 sterile grinding steel beads were added, and the sample was placed in a tissue grinder and shaken at 30 Hz for 5 min, and then mixed thoroughly. The tissue grinding solution was centrifuged at 4 °C and 10000 g for 5 min, and the supernatant was aspirated and set aside. For cloacal swab samples, 1 mL of PBS buffer was added, vortexed thoroughly, and 300 μL of the sample was centrifuged at 4 °C and 10,000 g for 5 min, and the supernatant was aspirated and set aside. For both samples, GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 RNA were extracted using a viral RNA extraction kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China). Nucleic acids were stored at −80 °C for subsequent analysis.

## Primers and probe design
Based on the GAstV-1 (OM264914.1, PP763758.1, MW353015.1, OL762471.1, OR211592.1) and GAstV-2 (OL536330.1, PP966939.1, PQ552759.1, MT934439.1) genomic sequences published on GenBank, OL762473.1, OP621341.1, MW413813.1) ORF1b genome sequences were analyzed by gene sequence comparison using Megalign software, and Primer5.0 software was used to design the basic RPA primers for ORF1b gene.
Candidate RPA primers were screened for the best performers according to the TwistAmpTM Amplification Guide (TwistDx Ltd., Cambridge, UK) using the ZC BioScienceTM Basic Kit (ZC BioScience, Hangzhou, China). The basic RPA products were purified by a common DNA product purification kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China) and then analyzed by 2 % gel electrophoresis. The best primer pairs with bright bands and no non-specific amplification primer combinations were selected, and the exo probes were designed according to the best primer pairs. The primer and probe sequences were listed in and were synthesized by GeneRay Biological Engineering (Shanghai, China).
 

## Construct of in vitro transcribed RNA
The extracted RNA of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 was reverse-transcribed into cDNA and then amplified using RT-PCR primers ( ) with the cDNA as a template. The amplified products were purified and recovered, then ligated into the pGEM@-T Easy vector (Promega, Madison, US) and transformed into Escherichia coli competent cells DH5α (Tiangen, Beijing, China) for amplification. Recombinant plasmids were extracted using the TIANprep mini Plasmid Kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China). The recombinant plasmids were linearized and purified using Nde I enzyme (Promega, Madison, US), and the purified products were transcribed in vitro using the T7 RiboMAXTM Express Large Scale RNA Production System kit (Promega, Madison, US), yielding the in vitro transcribed RNA of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 ( ). Finally, the concentrations of the in vitro transcribed RNA of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were measured using a NanoDrop 2000c (Thermo, Waltham, US). The number of RNA copies was calculated using the following formula ( ): (6.02 × 1023) × [X (ng/μL)] × 10−9/(RNA length × 340) = Y (copies/μL).
In this study, the copy numbers of in vitro transcribed RNA of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were 3.2 × 1010 copies/μL and 2.8 × 1010 copies/μL, respectively.

## Optimization of the dual RT-qRPA assay
A volumetric 1:1 mixture of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in vitro transcribed RNA at a concentration of 1.0 × 105 copies/reaction was used as a template for the optimization of the dual RT-qRPA assay using the ZC BioScienceTM fluorescent-type kit (ZC BioScience, Hangzhou, China). GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 primers and exo probe final concentrations were optimized in the range of 0.2-0.6 μmol/L and 0.08-0.24 μmol/L, respectively. The reaction system consisted of 25 μL of A Buffer, 2.5 μL of B Buffer (magnesium acetate 280 mmol/L), 2 μL of RNA template, and was made up to 50 μL with ddH2O. According to the kit instructions, prepare a mixture of A Buffer, primer, exo probe and ddH2O according to the reaction quantities and mix them evenly, dispense them into 0.2 mL assay unit tubes containing the reaction dry powder. Then add the template, add B Buffer to the cap of the assay unit tube sequentially, cap the tube, mix well by turning up and down 5-6 times, centrifuge instantly, and collect the liquid to the bottom of the tube. Subsequently, it was immediately put into the Genie III isothermal amplification fluorescence detector (OptiGene Limited, West Sussex, UK), and the reaction was kept at a constant temperature for 20 min, and Channel1 (ROX) and Channel2 (FAM) were selected at the same time, and fluorescence signals were collected every 30 s. The detection results were expressed as threshold time (hh: mm: ss).
After determining the optimal primer probe concentration, optimization of the reaction temperature was carried out. The reaction temperature was set in the range of 39-43 °C, and a negative control was also set up. The early peak time, high fluorescence intensity value and smooth amplification curve were taken as the optimal results and determined as the best reaction temperature.

## Analytical specificity and sensitivity analysis of the dual RT-qRPA assay
The analytical specificity of the dual RT-qRPA assay was evaluated using nucleic acids of GAstV-1, GAstV-2, AIV-H9, FAdV-4, NDRV, MDPV and GPV, with ddH2O serving as a blank control. The specificity test was repeated three times.
Subsequently, the in vitro transcribed RNA standards of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 were each subjected to a 10-fold gradient dilution to give a concentration range of 1.0 × 106-1.0 × 10° copies/reaction, and 1 μL of each was taken to prepare mixed templates for RT-qRPA sensitivity assay, and ddH2O was used as a blank control. Then, two 2-fold dilutions of the mixed template with a concentration of 1.0 × 102 copies/reaction were made. Each concentration gradient was repeated 8 times respectively.
A Probit (predicted proportion positive) analysis (SPSS v22.0, Armonk, US) with data of the positive samples from each of the 8 replications was performed to establish the limit of detection at 95 % confidence interval.

## Evaluation of the dual RT-qRPA assay on the clinical samples
The extracted RNA from 104 goosling liver and cloacal swab samples was used as templates for simultaneous differential detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in clinical samples using the established dual RT-qRPA method. The diagnostic agreement with the established dual RT-qPCR method ( ) was calculated. Furthermore, the correlation between the TT values from RT-qRPA and the CT values from RT-qPCR was analyzed by linear regression (GraphPad Prism 8.0.2, San Diego, US) in order to evaluate the value of the established assay in practical application.

## Results

## Results of optimal primer probe screening
Six pairs of GAstV-1 RPA primers and three pairs of GAstV-2 RPA primers were designed for the ORF1b genes of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2, respectively, and amplified by basal RT-RPA, and the products were purified and verified by 2 % agarose gel electrophoresis. The results showed that 6 pairs of GAstV-1 primers and 3 pairs of GAstV-2 primers amplified the target bands ( ), and the RT-qRPA-F2/RT-qRPA-R2 of GAstV-1 and RT-qRPA-F3/RT-qRPA-R3 of GAstV-2 had bright bands without non-specific amplification, and the above two pairs of primers were selected as the optimal primers, and the probes were designed.
 

## Optimization results of the Dual RT-qRPA assay
The dual RT-qRPA assay system and conditions were optimized using a mixture of 1.0 × 105 copies/reaction of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 RNA standards as a template. The optimal concentrations of primers and probe for GAstV-1 were determined to be 0.4 μmol/L for both forward and reverse primers, and 0.24 μmol/L for the probe, while those for GAstV-2 were set at 0.4 μmol/L for both primers and 0.12 μmol/L for the probe. The optimal reaction system as follows: 25 μL of A Buffer, 2 μL each of GAstV-1 RT-qRPA-F2/RT-qRPA-R2 (10 μmol/L), 1.2 μL of GAstV-1 RT-qRPA-P1 (10 μmol/L), 2 μL each of GAstV-2 RT-qRPA-F3/RT-qRPA-R3 (10 μmol/L), 0.6 μL of GAstV-2 RT-qRPA-P2 (10 μmol/L), 2 μL of template, 2.5 μL of B Buffer, and ddH₂O to a final volume of 50 μL. Under these conditions, the amplification curves of GAstV-1 (detected in Channel 1 with ROX) and GAstV-2 (detected in Channel 2 with FAM) exhibited smooth profiles, early fluorescence signal emergence, and high peak intensities when performed at 42 °C.

## Analysis of the analytical specificity
The specificity of the developed dual RT-qRPA assay was validated using GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 as target viruses. The results indicated that Channel 1 specifically amplified GAstV-1, while Channel 2 exclusively detected GAstV-2. No cross-amplification was observed in either channel when testing common avian pathogens, including AIV-H9, FAdV-4, NDRV, MDPV, and GPV ( ). These results were reproducible across three independent replicates, confirming the high specificity of the dual RT-qRPA assay for simultaneous discrimination of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2.
 

## Analysis of the analytical sensitivity
The established dual RT-qRPA assay was analyzed for sensitivity using a 10-fold gradient dilution of the RNA standard mix as a template. The results showed that GAstV-1 was detected 8/8 at concentrations between 1.0 × 106-1.0 × 103 copies/reaction, 7/8 at 1.0 × 102 copies/reaction, 2/8 at 5.0 × 101 copies/reaction, and 2.5 × 101 copies/reaction, 1.0 × 101 copies/reaction and 1.0 × 10° copies/reaction were not detected ( A). GAstV-2 was detected at concentrations ranging from 1.0 × 106-1.0 × 102 copies/reaction, and was detected in 3/8 of the cases at a concentration of 5.0 × 101 copies/reaction, and was not detected in 2/8 of the cases at a concentration of 2.5 × 101 copies/reaction, 1/8 was detected, and neither was detected at concentrations of 1.0 × 101 copies/reaction and 1.0 × 10° copies/reaction ( B). Probit analysis showed that the detection limits of the assay for both pathogens were 109 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 87.93-169.73 copies/reaction) ( C), 86 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 65.11-179.02 copies/reaction) ( D).
 

## Detection results of the clinical samples
The established dual RT-qRPA assay was applied for simultaneous differential detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in 104 liver and cloacal swab samples collected from gooslings. The results showed that the detection rate of GAstV-1 was 18.27 % (19/104), GAstV-2 was 66.35 % (69/104), and the mixed infection rate was 15.38 % (16/104). Comparison with the results of the dual RT-qPCR assay showed a 100 % concordance rate ( ). The results of dual RT-qRPA detection (TT value) showed a good linear relationship with the results of dual RT-qPCR detection (CT value), with R2 of 0.965 and 0.948, respectively ( ).
 

## Discussion
Rapid differential detection of GAstV is one of the key factors in the prevention and control of GAstV infection. In this study, a dual RT-qRPA assay was successfully established, which realized the simultaneous rapid differential detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in the same reaction tube. The assay results could be obtained within 4-16 min, which significantly shortened the assay time compared with the dual RT-qPCR assay established in our laboratory ( ). The dual RT-qRPA assay established in this study was rapid, specific, sensitive and easy to perform, and could be effectively applied to the simultaneous identification and detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in goose liver and cloacal swab samples, providing a new technological tool for immediate detection in the field.
In this study, specific RT-RPA primers and exo probes were designed for the conserved region of the ORF1b gene of GAstV, and a dual RT-qRPA assay was established, which can rapidly detect and differentiate GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 simultaneously. The dual RT-qRPA method established in this study was comparable to the established LAMP and RPA methods ( ; ) in terms of detection limit (1 × 102 copies/reaction), but the reaction time was much faster, while the established LAMP and RPA methods only achieved a single detection of GAstV-2, and could not realize the simultaneous identification of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2. Comparison of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 sequences included in GenBank revealed that the ORF1b gene is more conserved ( ), and most of the GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 epidemic strains have greater than 98% homology in the ORF1b gene; however, the primers and probes designed in this study have a mismatch of 1-6 bases with some of the reference strains. It has been shown that RPA is resistant to base mutations in primers and probes and can tolerate 6-9 base mismatches without affecting amplification ( ). This suggests that the primer and probe combination is highly resistant to mutation. GAstV is an RNA virus, so the in vitro transcribed RNA standards are better suited for the optimization of new assays and can also better mimic the detection of the virus in real samples. In this study, the establishment and optimization of a dual RT-qRPA assay were carried out using GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 ORF1b in vitro transcribed RNA standards as templates. For the dual RT-qRPA assay established in this study, the concentration of the primer probe is a key factor affecting the amplification efficiency and needs to be fully optimized. The primer and probe concentrations in this study were optimized in the ranges of 0.2-0.6 μmol/L and 0.08-0.24 μmol/L, respectively, and the reaction temperatures were optimized in the range of 39-43 °C, and the combinations of early peak time and high fluorescence intensity values were finally selected as the best reaction conditions. After this series of optimization, the optimal primer probe concentrations were determined to be 0.4 μmol/L and 0.24 μmol/L for GAstV-1 and 0.4 μmol/L and 0.12 μmol/L for GAstV-2, respectively, and the optimal reaction temperature of the system was 42 °C. The established assays showed a detection limit of 109 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 87.93-169.73 copies/reaction) for GAstV-1, 86 copies/reaction (95 % CI: 65.11-179.02 copies/reaction), which was comparable to the detection limit of the dual RT-qPCR assay ( ), showing high sensitivity. In addition, the assay showed good specificity, detecting only GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 and not the other common avian pathogens mentioned in this study. Good detection performance was demonstrated in 104 goosling liver and cloacal swab samples. The detection rate of GAstV-1 was 18.27 % (19/104); the detection rate of GAstV-2 was 66.35 % (69/104), and it was also found that 15.38 % (16/104) of the samples presented mixed infection of both genotypes. Validated by the dual RT-qPCR method ( ), the diagnostic compliance rate of the two methods was 100 %, and the detection performance was comparable, but the RT-qRPA method could obtain the results faster. In this study, GAstV-2 dominated the infection, a finding consistent with previous studies ( ; ).
As an isothermal nucleic acid amplification technology that can make up for the shortcomings of the current PCR technology, RPA technology has been widely used in the field of rapid on-site detection of animal diseases due to its unique advantages. Compared with PCR technology, RPA could run isothermally at relatively low temperatures ( ). RPA reagents can be stored not only for a long period at room temperature, but also at a high temperature of 45 °C for about 20 days, which reduces the difficulty of storage and transportation ( ). In addition, RPA tolerates inhibitory compounds better than PCR ( ). Combined with a compact and portable detection device such as Genie III, it is capable of realizing immediate on-site detection of pathogens within 20 min. The above characteristics make RPA more suitable than PCR for use in outbreak sites or inadequately equipped laboratories, as well as resource-limited environments or for on-site rapid diagnosis of various pathogens. However, the current RPA technology is mostly applied to single-pathogen detection, and with the complexity of detection needs, there is a need to realize the simultaneous detection of multiple pathogens. Based on the portable fluorescence detector Genie III, this study realized the simultaneous detection and differentiation of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in the same reaction system, which can be used as a means of rapid detection in the field of epidemic. However, it should be noted that this study is still using conventional assays to extract nucleic acids, which takes a long time. Subsequently, more rapid nucleic acid extraction reagents will be selected to achieve the real sense of immediate detection at the scene of the epidemic.

## Conclusion
In this study, we developed a dual RT-qRPA assay that enables the simultaneous differential detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2 in goose livers and cloacal swabs within 20 min. At present, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of the application of RPA for the simultaneous differential detection of GAstV-1 and GAstV-2. The established RT-qRPA assay is specific, sensitive, easy to perform, and has a short reaction time, which can be used as a routine method for GAstV detection in the laboratory. Due to the portability of the fluorescence analyzer and the availability of rapid results, it is also suitable for rapid identification and detection of goose astrovirus at the scene of an outbreak, especially in resource-limited situations.
