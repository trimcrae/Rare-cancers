## Background
Myocardial fibrosis is a pivotal pathological component of the failing heart where the sympatho‐β‐adrenergic receptor (β‐AR) signaling is augmented. Intermediate‐conductance Ca2+‐activated K+ channel (KCa3.1) is expressed in fibroblasts and mediates fibrosis, but the regulation of KCa3.1 expression by β‐AR activation remains unclear. Used for treatment of skin diseases, dimethyl fumarate (DMF) is recently found to suppress transcription cofactor yes‐associated protein (YAP). Here, we examined whether DMF ameliorates myocardial fibrosis induced by β‐AR activation through inhibiting YAP‐mediated KCa3.1 expression in fibroblasts.

## Methods
We used 2 mouse models of established cardiac fibrosis induced by administration of isoproterenol (30 mg/kg per d SC) for 1 week with animals studied 4 weeks afterwards, or transgenic overexpression of β2‐AR (β2‐TG) exhibiting age‐related worsening of cardiac fibrosis. In both models, DMF was administered (50 mg/kg per d IP) for 4 weeks. The mechanism of DMF in regulating KCa3.1 expression was studied in cultured adult mouse cardiac fibroblasts exposed to isoproterenol.

## Results
Mice subjected to repeated isoproterenol injections developed myocardial inflammation and fibrosis with irreversible cardiac dysfunction. These phenotypes seen in both models were reversed by DMF treatment in vivo. Mechanistically, in both in vivo models and in cultured fibroblasts, we observed declined YAP phosphorylation and enhanced YAP nuclear localization induced by isoproterenol, changes accompanied by upregulation of KCa3.1 expression at mRNA and protein levels. These changes were largely abolished by DMF treatment.

## Conclusions
DMF reverses the established myocardial fibrosis following β‐AR activation by inhibiting YAP nuclear localization and resultant KCa3.1 expression in fibroblasts.

## Subject Categories
TreatmentPharmacology Next, we examined histological and molecular characteristics in LV tissues harvested after 1‐week isoproterenol stimulation. LV fibrosis in the isoproterenol group was evident by a marked increment of collagen deposition measured by either Masson's trichrome staining or hydroxyproline content (Figure  and ). In LV tissues of isoproterenol‐treated versus control mice, quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction and immunoblotting revealed enhanced expression of a number of inflammatory and fibrotic genes, including Kcnn4 (potassium calcium‐activated channel subfamily N member 4), Il‐6 (interleukin‐6), Cd68 (cluster of differentiation 68), Lgals3 (galectin‐3), Postn (periostin), Col1a1 (collagen type I α1 chain), Col3a1 (collagen type III α1 chain), Mmp2 (matrix‐metalloproteinase 2) and Mmp9 (Figure  ), and elevated protein levels of KCa3.1, interleukin‐6, CD68, galectin‐3, and periostin (Figure  and ), albeit levels of the α‐smooth muscle actin (α‐SMA) gene (Acta2) and protein were not significantly altered (Figure  through ). By immunohistochemistry, hearts of isoproterenol‐treated mice showed upregulated expression of CD45 and CD68 in the cardiac interstitial and perivascular regions (Figure  and ). These results suggested that 1‐week isoproterenol administration evoked myocardial inflammation and activation of fibroblasts.
 We also investigated whether KCa3.1 upregulation induced by isoproterenol mediated fibrotic signaling in cardiac fibroblasts in vitro. Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction showed that isoproterenol (10−6 M, 48 hours) increased expression of fibrotic genes, including Acta2, Fn1 (fibronectin 1), Ccn2 (connective tissue growth factor), Postn, Lgals3, Col1a1, and Col3a1 (Figure  ). Importantly, the effect of isoproterenol on this panel of genes was abolished by the addition of TRAM‐34 (10−6 M), a specific blocker of KCa3.1 (Figure  ). These results indicate that KCa3.1 is a causal mediator in isoproterenol‐induced fibrotic signaling.

## 
DMF Improved Isoproterenol‐Induced Myocardial Dysfunction
We then explored whether DMF was able to reverse the sustained cardiac dysfunction in the isoproterenol model. As shown in Figure  , mice were initially treated with isoproterenol for 1 week (30 mg/kg per d SC), followed by the administration of vehicle or DMF (50 mg/kg per d IP) for a period of 4 weeks, and then studied at the end. The mice in the isoproterenol group exhibited significantly increased wet weights of the heart and lungs normalized by tibia length (Figure  and ). Importantly, all these changes in isoproterenol‐treated mice were largely abolished by DMF therapy (Figure  and ). The effect of DMF on myocardial hypertrophy was examined by measurement of the CSA of LVs in the 3 groups, with results showing that DMF treatment reversed the increased CSA seen in the isoproterenol group (Figure  ). This finding was inconsistent with the quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction data, revealing that alterations in the expression of hypertrophy‐related genes, Nppa (natriuretic peptide A), Nppb, Myh7 (myosin heavy chain 7), Myh6, and Atp2a2 (ATPase sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ transporter 2), seen in the isoproterenol group, were basically restored to the control level by DMF treatment (Figure  ). These changes indicate that DMF prevents isoproterenol‐induced cardiac hypertrophy (Figure  ). In addition, the terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling staining revealed significant cell apoptosis (including cardiomyocytes) in isoproterenol‐treated mouse hearts, which was largely abolished by treatment with DMF (Figure  ). Importantly, by echocardiography isoproterenol‐induced LV hypertrophy and dysfunction were evident, judged by increment in the LV mass, LV end‐systolic dimensions, and LV end‐diastolic dimensions with a decline in fractional shortening. All these changes by echocardiography were largely reversed by DMF treatment (Figure  and ).
 

## 
DMF Alleviated Isoproterenol‐Induced Myocardial Inflammation and Fibrosis
We further examined the effects of DMF on isoproterenol‐induced cardiac inflammation and fibrosis in mice studied 4 weeks after the isoproterenol challenge. In LV tissues from isoproterenol‐treated mice, fibrosis, measured by quantitative histology and hydroxyproline content, were significantly increased (Figure  and ). Relative to the control mice, the isoproterenol group also exhibited higher levels of selected inflammatory and fibrotic markers at mRNA (Kcnn4, Il‐6, Cd68, Postn, Acta2, Col1a1, Col3a1, and Mmp2) and protein levels (KCa3.1, interleukin‐6, CD68, periostin, and α‐SMA) (Figure  through ), although galectin‐3 expression in LV tissues was not altered in this stage. Once again, treatment with DMF largely abolished myocardial fibrosis together with lowered expression of KCa3.1 and other fibrotic and inflammatory factors (Figure  through ). The higher levels of CD45 and CD68 determined by immunohistochemistry in the isoproterenol group were also significantly reduced by DMF treatment (Figure  and ).
 

## 
DMF Inhibited Nuclear Localization of YAP in the Myocardium or Cardiac Fibroblasts
To ascertain the underlying mechanism responsible for the antifibrotic and anti‐inflammatory effects of DMF, we determined expression of Ser127‐phospho‐YAP (p‐YAPS127) and total YAP by western blotting in protein prepared from LV tissues. Relative to the control group, the isoproterenol group showed a significant decrease in both p‐YAPS127 expression and the ratio of p‐YAPS127/total YAP, and the isoproterenol‐induced change in p‐YAPS127 was reversed by DMF treatment, leading to a rebound in the p‐YAPS127/YAP ratio (Figure  ). The Ser127 phosphorylation of YAP would be expected to influence its nuclear entry. We further determined YAP content in both cytoplasmic and nuclear protein fractions prepared from LV tissues. Compared with the control group, there was a reduction in the cytoplasmic YAP (cYAP; by 44%) together with a 1.75‐fold increase in nuclear YAP (nYAP), causing a higher nYAP/cYAP ratio in the isoproterenol group. DMF treatment reduced the nYAP/cYAP ratio back to the control level (Figure  ). These results imply that isoproterenol treatment promotes YAP nuclear localization, where YAP binds to and activates transcription factors, activating the expression of inflammatory and fibrotic genes. DMF treatment reduces YAP nuclear abundance and inhibits its activity of transcriptional regulation with the amelioration of cardiac inflammation and fibrosis.
 We further explored the effect of DMF on YAP phosphorylation and nuclear localization in cardiac fibroblasts prepared from adult mice. Isoproterenol‐treated (10−6 M, 48 hours) cells exhibited a decrease in protein expression of p‐YAPS127, yielding a lower p‐YAPS127/YAP ratio, suggestive of YAP activation. Addition of DMF to isoproterenol‐treated fibroblasts showed a clear increase in the p‐YAPS127/YAP ratio (Figure  ). Similarly, diverse changes in cYAP (reduced by 48%) and nYAP (increased by 1.4‐fold) led to a higher nYAP/cYAP ratio (by 2.7‐fold) in isoproterenol‐treated fibroblasts. Treatment with DMF reduced the nuclear fraction of YAP, resulting in a decrease in the nYAP/cYAP ratio (Figure  ). All these findings in vitro were in keeping with our in vivo results from the isoproterenol model. Images from an immunofluorescence assay in fibroblasts confirmed that isoproterenol promoted YAP nuclear localization, with a 19% increment of YAP‐positive nuclei. This effect of isoproterenol on translocation of YAP from cytoplasm to nucleus in fibroblasts was inhibited by DMF treatment (Figure  ). The transcription factor TEAD1 is the well‐defined YAP partner. Further coimmunoprecipitation of nuclear protein showed the increased physical interaction of YAP and TEAD1 in isoproterenol‐treated fibroblasts, indicative of an enhanced YAP‐TEAD1 transcriptional activity (Figure  ). Importantly, DMF treatment effectively abolished isoproterenol‐induced increase of nuclear YAP‐TEAD1 interaction in fibroblasts (Figure  ).

## 
DMF Reversed Isoproterenol‐Induced KCa3.1 Expression in Cardiac Fibroblasts via Inhibiting YAP

Given that KCa3.1 expressed in fibroblasts plays a crucial role in myocardial fibrosis, , , we then investigated whether the observed KCa3.1 upregulation by isoproterenol in LV tissues was mediated through YAP signaling in fibroblasts. Incubation with isoproterenol (10−6 M) for 48 hours increased the protein level of KCa3.1 by ≈158%, and the concomitant addition of DMF (10−5 M) abolished the effect of isoproterenol on KCa3.1 expression (Figure  ). Consistent with the effect of isoproterenol, the specific YAP activator PY‐60 (10−5 M, 48 hours) significantly increased expression of KCa3.1 at both mRNA and protein levels (Figure  ). Conversely, verteporfin (10−6 M, 48 hours), an inhibitor of nuclear YAP‐TEAD1 interaction, inhibited isoproterenol‐induced increase of KCa3.1 at mRNA and protein levels (Figure  ). Il‐6 is regarded as a YAP target gene expressed predominantly in fibroblasts. We observed in fibroblasts concomitant changes in the expression of Il‐6 akin to that of KCa3.1 by treatment with isoproterenol, DMF plus isoproterenol (Figure  ), PY‐60, or verteporfin plus isoproterenol (Figure  ). Importantly, isoproterenol (10−6 M, 48 hours) augmented the expression of KCa3.1 and interleukin‐6 at mRNA and protein levels, and this effect of isoproterenol was abolished by Yap gene silencing using small interfering RNA (Figure  through ). Furthermore, protein expression of galectin‐3 as another YAP target in fibroblasts was also upregulated by isoproterenol, and this change was prevented by DMF treatment (Figure  ). Using a chromatin immunoprecipitation—quantitative polymerase chain reaction assay, we further found that isoproterenol stimulation increased, while DMF reduced the abundance of TEAD1 binding to the Kcnn4 promoter region (Figure  ). Thus, isoproterenol upregulates the expression of KCa3.1 by enhancing transcriptional activity of YAP‐TEAD1, while DMF suppresses the expression of KCa3.1 by inhibiting YAP‐TEAD1 activity in cardiac fibroblasts.
 

## Effect of DMF on LATS1 Phosphorylation and Prediction of Potential DMF Binding to LATS1

Expression of total LATS1 and its phosphorylation at Ser909 were then determined in cardiac fibroblasts by immunoblotting. Consistent with changes in p‐YAPSer127 seen in isoproterenol‐treated cells, isoproterenol treatment tended to lower the level of LATS1 phosphorylation at Ser909 without change in total LATS1, leading to a clear decrease of LATS1 phosphorylation at Ser909/LATS1 ratio (Figure  ), and once again, DMF treatment restored the ratio of LATS1 phosphorylation at Ser909/LATS1 (Figure  ). Thus, the cardioprotective effect of DMF depends on its disinhibition of isoproterenol‐mediated LATS1 kinase inactivation in fibroblasts.
 Using molecular docking analyses, the binding affinity of DMF with LATS1 was evaluated, and 3 potential binding sites were predicted (Figure  and ). On the basis of the docking scores, van der Waals interaction energies, and hydrogen bonding interactions for each site, site 2 appeared to be the most favorable DMF‐LATS1 binding characteristics, albeit this binding waits to be confirmed directly.

## 
DMF Reversed Isoproterenol‐Induced Reduction in Nrf2 Protein Expression in the Myocardium and Fibroblasts
To test whether Nrf2 protein expression was altered by isoproterenol stimulation, western blotting was performed in protein extracted from LV tissues of mice subjected to isoproterenol or isoproterenol plus DMF. Nrf2 protein expression in the myocardium of the isoproterenol group was found to be 57% lower, which was completely reversed after 4‐week treatment with DMF (Figure  ). Similarly, in cardiac fibroblasts, isoproterenol stimulation (10−6 M, 48 hours) repressed Nrf2 protein expression (by 37%), which is partially prevented by DMF treatment (10−5 M, 48 hours) (Figure  ). These results indicate that Nrf2 expression is altered in the setting of β‐AR stimulation, which is reversed by DMF treatment.

## 
DMF Ameliorated Myocardial Fibrosis and Hypertrophy in the β2‐TG Mice
The effect of DMF on myocardial fibrosis was further examined in the β2‐TG strain of mice, an independent cardiomyopathy model with severe and progressively worsening cardiac fibrosis owing to persistent transgenic activation of β2‐AR. β2‐TG mice had increased overall heart mass, CSA, and lung wet weight, implying myocardial hypertrophy and pulmonary congestion. In this model, the 4‐week treatment with DMF (50 mg/kg per d IP) significantly inhibited cardiac hypertrophy (measured as cardiac weight and CSA) and pulmonary congestion (estimated by lung weights) (Figure  through ). As described previously, upregulation of hypertrophic, inflammatory, and fibrotic factors measured at mRNA (Nppa, Nppb, Myh6, Myh7, Atp2a2, Kcnn4, Il‐6, Cd68, Lgals3, Postn, Acta2, Col1a1, and Col3a1) and protein levels (KCa3.1, galectin‐3, and α‐SMA), as well as fibrosis by histological and biochemical assays, were detected in LV tissues from β2‐TG mice versus nontransgenic controls (Figure  through ). Treatment of β2‐TG mice with DMF largely reversed these changes as indicated above (Figure  through ).
 

## DISCUSSION
In the present study testing the therapeutic efficacy of DMF, we used 2 in vivo models with irreversible or progressively worsening cardiac fibrosis due to β‐AR activation. Cultured fibroblasts from adult mouse hearts were used for mechanistic study focusing on β‐AR activation induced changes in the activity of LATS1 and YAP in relation to KCa3.1 expression. Two novel findings have been made. First, DMF treatment effectively reversed myocardial fibrosis, inflammation, and cardiac dysfunction induced by β‐AR activation via pharmacological or genetic means. Second, we observed in the isoproterenol model in vivo and in cultured cardiac fibroblasts in vitro, the upregulation of KCa3.1 expression together with reduction in YAPSer127 phosphorylation and enhanced YAP nuclear localization. These changes were abolished by treatment with DMF. Thus, our findings reveal that DMF ameliorates myocardial fibrosis induced by β‐AR activation, at least in part, via inhibiting the nuclear localization of YAP and subsequent KCa3.1 expression in fibroblasts.
Our central goal in this study was to test whether DMF treatment was able to reverse established cardiac fibrosis induced by β‐AR stimulation. Experimentally, isoproterenol is commonly used to reproduce cardiomyopathy models in therapeutic testing. Using the protocol of repeated isoproterenol injections for 1 week followed by isoproterenol washout for 4 weeks, we obtained the model with enhanced inflammatory signaling at the early phase of isoproterenol exposure; persistent profibrotic signaling throughout; and irreversible fibrosis, hypertrophy, and LV dysfunction when studied at the chronic phase. Our second in vivo fibrosis model is induced by cardiac‐restricted β2‐AR activation (β2‐TG) that served as an independent model of cardiomyopathy with age‐dependent worsening of myocardial hypertrophy, cardiomyocyte loss, inflammation, and fibrosis. Importantly, we demonstrated upregulated expression of KCa3.1 in both models in vivo. These characteristics of β‐AR activation‐induced cardiac remodeling/fibrosis allow us to test the efficacy of DMF with the aim of reversing established fibrosis.
Using both in vivo models, we revealed that the 4‐week treatment with DMF largely reversed myocardial fibrosis, inflammation, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and apoptosis, together with improved LV function in the isoproterenol model. The therapeutic efficacy of DMF in the current study was in line with the evidence from a study in rats that prophylactic administration of DMF (25 mg/kg per 12 hours IP) for 28 days inhibited isoproterenol (5 mg/kg SC at the last 7 days) induced cardiac hypertrophy through interfering with the myeloid differentiation primary response 88–dependent toll‐like receptor signaling pathway. Another earlier study in diabetic cardiomyopathy showed that DMF attenuated myocardium oxidative stress and inflammation as well as remodeling and dysfunction via Nrf2 upregulation. Importantly, we demonstrated here, either in vivo or in cultured cardiac fibroblasts, that DMF treatment inhibited upregulation of KCa3.1 at mRNA and protein levels in the setting of β‐AR activation. Preclinical studies, including ours, have underscored the profibrotic nature of KCa3.1 in cardiac fibrosis, , , and the antifibrotic property of the specific KCa3.1 blocker TRAM‐34 in animal models with cardiac fibrosis induced by abdominal aortic constriction, angiotensin II infusion, MI, or the β2‐TG. Elevation of intracellular Ca2+ level is crucial for fibroblast activation and proliferation. KCa3.1 is known to be associated with transient receptor potential channels in cardiac fibroblasts, and activation of KCa3.1 in fibroblasts induces hyperpolarization, which increases Ca2+ entry through transient receptor potential channels with elevated intracellular Ca2+. , Thus, our findings in the present study of the potent antifibrotic efficacy of DMF and suppressed KCa3.1 expression further support a causal link between KCa3.1 and cardiac fibrosis and provide new evidence for protection by DMF against myocardial fibrosis through downregulating KCa3.1 expression.
Whereas the profibrotic nature of upregulated expression of KCa3.1 is well established, the transcriptional regulation of KCa3.1 is largely unknown. Recent studies have provided strong evidence for a pivotal role of the transcriptional co‐activator YAP in mediating fibrotic signaling. , YAP is activated in cardiac fibroblasts in response to nonreperfused MI or angiotensin II stimulation, and genetic inhibition of fibroblast YAP attenuates cardiac fibrosis and dysfunction in settings of MI or chronic neuroendocrine stimulation. Given the established link among β‐AR activation, KCa3.1 expression, and galectin‐3 (a YAP target gene) leading to cardiac fibrosis, , we further addressed the role of YAP in regulating KCa3.1 expression in the context of the antifibrotic effect of DMF. We elected to study heart tissues of isoproterenol‐treated mice at the chronic phase when β‐AR stimulation already subsided whilst the fibrotic signaling remained at its peak judged by quantitative histology and upregulation of profibrotic molecules including KCa3.1. We found in isoproterenol‐treated mouse hearts and fibroblasts, a reduced abundance of p‐YAPSer127 without change in total YAP, together with increased YAP nuclear localization and KCa3.1 upregulation. These findings on isoproterenol‐induced changes in cardiac YAP differed from previous reports on mice subjected to isoproterenol challenge for 1 or 7 days, when elevated p‐YAPSer127 was evident denoting a suppressed transcriptional activity of YAP. , Such differences are likely due to the highly dynamic response by cardiomyocytes that is predominant at the acute phase but diminished following isoproterenol withdrawal, whereas YAP signaling in fibroblasts sustains throughout the chronic phase, when a set of YAP‐TEAD1 targets like Il‐6, Ccn2, and Lgals3 are upregulated in fibroblasts. Interestingly, upregulated expression of this panel of profibrotic genes by β‐AR stimulation was suppressed by DMF. We previously showed synergistic interactions of these profibrotic factors in promoting the fibrotic signaling, and that inhibition of KCa3.1 suppresses expression of some of these factors like galectin‐3, connective tissue growth factor, and transforming growth factor‐β in the myocardium of β2‐TG mice. Here, we revealed that DMF treatment reversed isoproterenol‐evoked upregulation of KCa3.1 as well as interleukin‐6 and galectin‐3, and that blockade of KCa3.1 in fibroblasts prevented isoproterenol‐induced expression of galectin‐3, connective tissue growth factor, and other fibrotic genes. These findings underscore a central role of KCa3.1 in the fibrotic signaling and the significance of antifibrotic efficacy of DMF, as shown in the current study. Importantly, in the presence of isoproterenol, DMF treatment effectively restored p‐YAPSer127 with reduced YAP nuclear localization in LV tissues in vivo or in cultured fibroblasts in vitro and reduced YAP‐TEAD1 interaction as well as the abundance of TEAD1 in the Kcnn4 promoter region in vitro. We also found that the change of the p‐LATS1/LATS1 ratio in isoproterenol‐treated fibroblasts is in accordance with the characteristics of Gi‐protein–coupled receptor that inhibits LATS1/2‐YAP phosphorylation and increases YAP activity. Notably, treatment with DMF in isoproterenol‐exposed fibroblasts rescued the decrease in the p‐LATS1/LATS1 ratio. Molecular docking analyses further indicated that site 2 in LATS1 structure is potentially the priority target for DMF biological activity. Thus, our findings reveal for the first time that Kcnn4 is a YAP–TEAD1 target gene, and that DMF ameliorates β‐AR activation induced cardiac fibrosis through inhibiting the nuclear YAP localization and KCa3.1 expression in fibroblasts.
Inflammatory and fibrotic signaling synergistically contribute to fibrogenesis. Here, we also observed the anti‐inflammatory effect of DMF therapy, evidenced by decreased expression of interleukin‐6, CD68, and CD45 in the heart with enhanced β‐AR signaling. Indeed, previous studies have shown DMF's anti‐inflammatory action through inhibiting the nuclear factor‐κB signaling pathway in dendritic cells or heart tissues of isoproterenol‐treated rats. An earlier study demonstrates that YAP/tafazzin (TAZ) promote proinflammatory macrophage phenotype by directly regulating Il‐6 promoter activity or through the p38‐dependent mitogen‐activated protein kinase pathway. Deletion of YAP/TAZ gene attenuates early inflammatory response and promotes timely polarization of proinflammatory macrophages to reparative macrophages, mitigating adverse remodeling and cardiac dysfunction after MI. On the basis of the inhibition by DMF of YAP activation as well as interleukin‐6, CD68, and CD45 expression in our in vivo models, the anti‐inflammatory action of DMF therapy should contribute to its overall antifibrotic property.
We observed in both isoproterenol and β2‐TG models significant cardiac hypertrophy phenotype that was reversed by DMF therapy. The pathological nature of hypertrophy is indicated by the activation of the fetal gene profile and coexistence of myocardial fibrosis. The mechanism involved in the antihypertrophic efficacy of DMF is not explored further in the current study focusing on fibrosis. The pleotropic actions of DMF underlie its antifibrotic, anti‐inflammatory, and anti‐hypertrophic efficacy, as demonstrated by our in vivo experimentations on both isoproterenol and β2‐TG models. The molecular mechanism is likely attributable to its inhibition of YAP activity in different cardiac cells. In addition to profibrotic action of YAP in fibroblasts and proinflammatory action in immune cells, there is evidence for YAP as a prohypertrophic factor in models with significant cardiac hypertrophy (eg, transverse aortic constriction). , We previously showed that acute β‐AR activation results in Hippo pathway activation/YAP inactivation in cardiomyocytes. In our model used in the current study (ie, 7‐day isoproterenol treatment and then studied 4 weeks afterward), the β‐AR mediated activation of Hippo pathway might have subsided, and prohypertrophic signaling, including enhanced YAP activity in cardiomyocytes, is likely activated, leading to cardiac hypertrophy. It is reported that in mouse models with enhanced cardiac β‐AR activity there exist complex intercellular interactions by which cardiomyocyte hypertrophy/dysfunction and activation of fibroblasts influence each other via a panel of molecules, such as galectin‐3, interleukin‐6, and interleukin‐18, , , , as well as metabolites including lactate, ceramides, and α‐ketoglutarate. , , We recently revealed in mouse hearts following β‐AR activation, elevated α‐ketoglutarate mediates upregulation of KCa3.1 in fibroblasts and fibrosis. α‐Ketoglutarate also promotes isoproterenol‐induced cardiac hypertrophy by activating mammalian target of rapamycin signaling. It would be appealing to explore whether DMF and its metabolite fumarate confer an antihypertrophic effect in vivo. In addition, in the rat model of isoproterenol‐induced cardiac hypertrophy, Ahmed et al showed the antihypertrophic effect of DMF that involves interference of the myeloid differentiation primary response 88–dependent toll‐like receptor signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting nuclear factor‐κB activation and its nuclear translocation. Therefore, the anti‐inflammatory effect of DMF observed in our in vivo models likely contributes to its antihypertrophic efficacy. Thus, we propose that DMF attenuates myocardial hypertrophy directly by inhibiting hypertrophic signaling (including YAP) and indirectly through inhibiting the prohypertrophic signaling derived from activated fibroblasts or immune cells.
There is also evidence that expression of Nrf2 in the mouse heart is critical for redox homeostasis and that upregulation of Nrf2 counteracts isoproterenol‐induced oxidative stress and pathological remodeling. Ashrafian et al showed in mice subjected to MI or in cardiomyocyte‐like HL‐1 cells, that treatment with DMF upregulated expression of Nrf2 protein and its target genes. DMF ameliorates doxorubicin‐induced cardiac injury in rats by regulating oxidative stress and apoptosis through the Nrf2 pathway. Our terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling staining in heart sections revealed that treatment with DMF abolished isoproterenol‐induced apoptosis. Given emerging evidence that DMF attenuates organ fibrosis by upregulating Nrf2 signaling, , , we examined changes in Nrf2 protein expression in LV tissues and cultured fibroblasts. We observed that Nrf2 protein expression was suppressed by isoproterenol and that DMF restored Nrf2 expression in the presence of isoproterenol. Interestingly, changes in Nrf2 expression were less pronounced in fibroblasts versus that in LV tissues, indicating that cardiomyocytes (and other cell types) are a major cell type for Nrf2 expression under our experimental conditions. Thus, the Nrf2 pathway likely contributes to the overall cardioprotective property of DMF in our experiment setting.
A few limitations of this study deserve discussion. First, we did not perform experiments to confirm the direct binding of DMF to LATS1 and therefore our finding by the molecular docking analysis remains inconclusive. Second, we did not analyze possible changes in LV diastolic function in the isoproterenol model at the time of echo experimentation.
In summary, our results demonstrated that DMF acts as a protectant against myocardial fibrosis induced by β‐AR activation, action that is at least in part by virtue of its downregulation of KCa3.1 through inhibition of YAP–TEAD1 interaction with reduced TEAD1 binding to the Kcnn4 promoter. These findings provide firm evidence for DMF as a promising drug to amend cardiac fibrosis in the clinic. The current clinical use of DMF with a good safety record would facilitate translational investigation.
