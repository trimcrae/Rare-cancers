## Summary
Pulmonary fibrosis is driven by persistent fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation, yet upstream regulators that initiate and sustain these programs remain incompletely defined. Here, we tested whether the ribosomal S6 kinase 1-serum response factor (RSK1-SRF) axis contributes to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and represents a tractable therapeutic target. Mendelian randomization supported a causal association between RSK1 and IPF risk, which aligned with increased RSK1 activity in human IPF lungs and bleomycin-injured mice. In lung fibroblasts, pharmacologic RSK1 inhibition blunted TGF-β-induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition and ECM production. Mechanistically, RSK1 associated with SRF and promoted SRF phosphorylation and activation, thereby enhancing SRF-dependent profibrotic transcription. In vivo, perturbation of the RSK1-SRF axis attenuated fibroblast activation, ECM deposition, and histologic fibrosis. Collectively, these findings indicate RSK1 as a druggable upstream driver of SRF-dependent profibrotic programs and motivate further evaluation of RSK1-directed strategies for IPF.

## Introduction
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and fatal interstitial lung disease characterized by aberrant fibroblast proliferation, stem cell differentiation, and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, resulting in irreversible distortion of pulmonary architecture and loss of respiratory function. , The prognosis for IPF remains poor, with an estimated mean survival of 3–5 years following diagnosis. The reported incidence and prevalence of IPF have increased over recent decades, with substantial heterogeneity across regions and studies, potentially influenced by population aging as well as differences in case ascertainment, diagnostic definitions, and occupational/environmental exposures. , Pathologically, IPF is characterized by persistent fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition (FMT) and excessive ECM deposition, which disrupt lung structure and impair gas exchange. Current evidence suggests that fibrogenesis is primarily driven by expansion and state transitions of resident stromal populations, while injured epithelial, endothelial, and immune cells can adopt overlapping profibrotic transcriptional programs that amplify stromal activation. , , Fibroblasts are central to fibrogenesis, transitioning into myofibroblasts under profibrotic stimuli and promoting ECM accumulation. , , Despite ongoing research, current treatment options are limited; FDA-approved anti-fibrotic agents, such as pirfenidone and nintedanib, only attenuate disease progression without improving survival or quality of life, while immunosuppressants and corticosteroids are constrained by low efficacy and adverse effects. Therefore, elucidating the regulatory mechanisms and molecular pathways underlying IPF progression is imperative to facilitate the development of more effective therapeutic strategies.
The 90 kDa ribosomal S6 kinases (RSKs) are a family of serine/threonine kinases that regulate a broad spectrum of cellular activities, including cell growth, motility, and survival. Accumulating evidence suggests that the four RSK isoforms (RSK1–4) play pivotal roles in vascular pathology; for instance, recent studies have implicated RSKs in promoting endothelial cell dysfunction and atherosclerosis in diabetes models. Mechanistically, RSKs are activated by the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) pathway and participate in diverse signal transduction cascades that orchestrate cell proliferation, apoptosis, and transformation. Our previous work demonstrated that reactive oxygen species (ROS) can promote epithelial β-catenin accumulation and nuclear translocation by oxidizing specific cysteine residues on nucleoredoxin, thereby inducing epithelial injury via the lymphoid enhancer-binding factor 1 (LEF-1) pathway and further activating RSKs. Furthermore, we have shown that fibroblast RSKs induce epithelial apoptosis through oxidative stress-mediated β-catenin/FOXO1 signaling. Collectively, these findings underscore the central role of RSKs in mediating fibroblast-epithelial crosstalk and highlight the interplay between oxidative stress and key signaling pathways in the pathogenesis of kidney fibrosis. However, the role of RSKs in IPF progression remains largely unexplored. Notably, kaempferol, a non-selective pharmacological inhibitor of RSKs, has been reported to attenuate advanced liver fibrosis by suppressing hepatic collagen deposition and the expression of profibrotic genes In addition, FMK, a fluoromethylketone inhibitor of RSKs, has been shown to mitigate advanced pulmonary fibrosis. Despite these advances, the precise effect of RSK on fibroblast activation, excessive ECM deposition, and its potential involvement in the pathogenesis of IPF remain to be elucidated.
Here, we employed Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to establish RSK1 as a causal risk factor for IPF and utilized integrated bioinformatic approaches to identify key fibroblast activation hub genes, including serum response factor (SRF), within the fibrotic lung transcriptome. Experimental validation revealed that the RSK1-SRF signaling axis drives FMT and ECM deposition and that the pharmacological inhibition of this pathway robustly attenuates pulmonary fibrosis in vivo. These findings highlight the RSK1-SRF axis as a pivotal molecular module in IPF pathogenesis and a promising therapeutic target.

## Results

## RSK1 is a causal risk factor for IPF and is upregulated in fibrotic lungs
Recent evidence suggests that RSKs are involved in various fibrotic pathologies; however, their contribution to IPF remains largely uncharacterized. To clarify the potential causal relationship between RSK1 and IPF, we first performed MR analysis. The absence of significant heterogeneity among single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)-effect estimates (Cochran’s Q = 10.95, p = 1 > 0.05) and a non-significant MR-Egger intercept (α = 0.0001, p = 0.28 > 0.05) excluded substantial pleiotropy and confounding ( A). Across all five MR approaches within the TwoSampleMR package, a significant positive causal effect of RSK1 on IPF risk was observed, with consistent results in sensitivity analyses ( B–1D). These genetic findings support RSK1 as a causal risk factor for the development of IPF. To further substantiate the pathological relevance of RSK1 in IPF, transcriptomic analysis of the GSE70866 dataset revealed significantly increased RPS6KA1 mRNA expression in lung tissue from IPF patients relative to healthy controls ( E). 
To experimentally validate these observations in vivo, we established a bleomycin (BLM)-induced murine model of pulmonary fibrosis. Sequential histological analyses at 7 and 14 days post-BLM administration, using H&E, Masson’s trichrome, and Sirius Red staining, corroborated by hydroxyproline (HYP) quantification, demonstrated progressive aggravation of fibrotic lesions and increasing collagen accumulation over time ( A–S1D). Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) revealed time-dependent upregulation of canonical fibrogenic genes (FN1, COL2A1, TNF, and ACTA2) in BLM-treated lungs ( E). Concordantly, western blot analysis showed increased protein levels of fibronectin (FN), collagen II, vimentin, and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) at both 7 and 14 days post-injury ( F and S1G). Importantly, western blotting revealed a robust elevation of both total and phosphorylated RSK1 (p-RSK1) in BLM-treated lungs, with p-RSK1 exhibiting a pronounced increase on day 14 compared with day 7 ( F and 1G). Quantitative analysis confirmed that RSK1 and p-RSK1 levels in the BLM group were significantly higher than those in saline-treated controls at both time points ( H and 1I). In line with the murine data, immunohistochemical analysis of human lung specimens indicated that RSK1 and p-RSK1 expressions were markedly upregulated in IPF tissues relative to normal lungs ( J and 1K).
To elucidate the cellular localization of RSK1 activation in fibrotic lungs, double immunofluorescence staining was performed for p-RSK1 and lineage-specific markers, including F4/80 (macrophages), E-cadherin (epithelial cells), and FN (fibroblasts). Notably, p-RSK1 was predominantly localized within FN-positive fibroblasts in the fibrotic regions of BLM-challenged lungs ( L). Collectively, these results establish RSK1 as a causal and mechanistically relevant factor in IPF, with its activation notably enriched in fibroblasts, underscoring its central role in fibroblast-mediated fibrogenesis.

## Inhibition of RSK1 attenuates TGF-β-induced fibroblast activation and ECM production
Building upon the identification of RSK1 as a causal risk factor for IPF and its upregulation in fibrotic lung tissues, we next investigated whether RSK1 is functionally required for TGF-β-driven fibroblast activation, focusing on FMT and ECM production. Consistent with a profibrotic induction, TGF-β stimulation robustly increased RSK1 activation in human fetal lung fibroblast (HFL-1) cells, as indicated by elevated phosphorylated RSK1 (p-RSK1) accompanied by marked upregulation of fibrotic markers including FN, collagen II, and α-SMA ( A). In parallel, qPCR showed that TGF-β treatment (10 ng/mL, 48 h) significantly increased RSK1 mRNA levels ( B). 
To directly establish loss-of-function evidence for RSK1, we silenced RSK1, using siRNA in TGF-β-stimulated HFL-1 cells. Western blot analysis confirmed that TGF-β-induced RSK1 phosphorylation was substantially attenuated upon RSK1 knockdown ( C and 2D). Notably, genetic inhibition of RSK1 markedly suppressed the TGF-β-driven induction of FN, collagen II, and α-SMA, as demonstrated by western blotting and quantitative analysis ( E and 2F). Immunofluorescence staining further corroborated these findings, showing that siRSK1 effectively blunted the TGF-β-induced increases in p-RSK1 and fibrotic marker signals in HFL-1 cells ( G–2J and A–S2D). Importantly, we confirmed these key observations in normal adult human lung fibroblasts (IMR-90): siRSK1 significantly attenuated TGF-β-induced RSK1 phosphorylation ( A and S3B) and reduced the upregulation of fibrotic markers (FN, collagen II, and α-SMA) ( E and S3F), supporting that the profibrotic requirement of RSK1 observed in HFL-1 cells is preserved in IMR-90 cells.
We next asked whether pharmacological blockade yields consistent anti-fibrotic effects. Co-treatment with the RSK inhibitor BI-D1870 not only attenuated TGF-β-induced RSK1 phosphorylation, as shown by western blotting and quantitative analysis ( K and 2L), but also effectively suppressed the TGF-β-driven upregulation of FN, collagen II, and α-SMA ( M and 2N), supporting the loss-of-function results obtained by genetic silencing. Consistently, immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated that BI-D1870 effectively blunted the TGF-β-induced increase in p-RSK1 signal in HFL-1 cells ( O and 2Q). Moreover, BI-D1870 markedly reduced TGF-β-induced fibrotic marker signals, including FN ( P and 2R), collagen II ( E and S2G), and α-SMA ( F and S2H), further validating the anti-fibrotic effects of pharmacological RSK inhibition at the cellular level.
To further substantiate the specificity of RSK1 loss-of-function, re-expression of RSK1 in siRSK1-treated HFL-1 cells restored TGF-β-induced profibrotic marker upregulation, supporting an on-target requirement of RSK1 in this response ( A–S4C). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that inhibition of RSK1—either by siRNA-mediated knockdown or pharmacological blockade—suppresses TGF-β-induced FMT and ECM production, underscoring a critical role for RSK1 in mediating profibrotic signaling in pulmonary fibroblasts.

## Bioinformatic identification of hub genes associated with fibroblast activation in IPF lung fibroblasts
To further delineate the molecular networks underlying RSK1-driven fibrogenic activation, we conducted an integrated bioinformatic analysis of transcriptomic datasets from IPF lung fibroblasts. Specifically, we merged and normalized three publicly available datasets (GSE1724, GSE40839, GSE44723), each comprising fibroblast samples from IPF patients, to reduce batch effects and enhance data comparability. Principal-component analysis (PCA) confirmed improved data distribution and distinct clustering between the experimental groups following normalization ( A–3C). Differential expression analysis using limma (FDR-adjusted p value < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.5) identified 77 significantly dysregulated genes in IPF-derived fibroblasts, as illustrated by the volcano plot and heatmap ( D and 3E). 
Given that network- and pathway-level regulators can display moderate but coordinated expression shifts, we adopted an inclusive fold-change cutoff to retain functionally relevant transcripts for downstream enrichment and network reconstruction. To evaluate whether our key findings depend on the fold-change threshold, we repeated the differential expression analysis by using a more stringent criterion (adjusted p value <0.05 and |log2FC| > 1) and re-assessed downstream enrichment. Under the stricter cutoff, the total number of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) decreased, whereas the enrichment patterns of core signaling pathways remained consistent with the original analysis (with adjusted p value < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.5) ( A–S5D). These results support the robustness of our pathway-level inferences and provide a rationale for using |log2FC| > 0.5 to capture broader regulatory networks in this context.
To further refine the pool of fibrosis-related genes, we cross-referenced these DEGs with pulmonary fibrosis-associated targets retrieved from OMIM, DisGeNET, and GeneCards, yielding a reference set of 2,019 unique genes after duplicate removal. Intersecting the 77 DEGs with this pulmonary fibrosis-related gene set identified 22 core genes implicated in pulmonary fibrosis ( F). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis using the STRING database revealed that these 22 genes formed a highly interconnected network (21 nodes and 46 edges; G). Hub gene analysis with CytoHubba (using Betweenness, Stress, and BottleNeck algorithms) consistently highlighted 11 hub genes: VCAM1, IL1R1, CSF1, NFKBIA, IL7, SMAD3, CASP1, TRIM21, CDH2, PTHLH, and SRF ( H). Functional enrichment analyses indicated that these hub genes are primarily involved in cell number homeostasis and cytokine receptor binding (Gene Ontology [GO]), with significant enrichment in the TNF signaling pathway and Th17 cell differentiation (KEGG). Disease Ontology analysis further linked these genes to viral infectious diseases and various pulmonary diseases ( I–3K).
To identify robust and clinically relevant biomarkers, we applied LASSO regression, ranking the hub genes by the absolute value of their coefficients. CSF1, CDH2, and SRF emerged as the top candidates (coefficients: 8.57, 5.87, and 2.36, respectively; L and 3M). Collectively, these analyses provide a comprehensive bioinformatic characterization of the fibrogenic gene landscape in IPF lung fibroblasts, and CSF1, CDH2, and SRF emerged as the top candidates associated with fibroblast activation in the context of IPF.

## MR supports a causal role of the RSK1-SRF signaling axis in IPF
Based on our prior transcriptomic comparison of lung fibroblasts from IPF patients and healthy controls, using public datasets, SRF emerged as one of the top three candidates associated with fibroblast activation. Notably, previous studies have implicated SRF as a key downstream effector in RSK1-mediated signaling. Here, MR analysis supported a significant causal effect of genetically proxied RSK1 elevation on increased SRF expression. Cochran’s Q test indicated no significant heterogeneity among SNP-effect estimates (Q = 67.05, p = 0.053; A), while the MR-Egger intercept showed no evidence of directional pleiotropy (α = 0.007, p = 0.451). All five MR methods consistently demonstrated a positive causal relationship ( B), with four yielding significant effects (p < 0.05) and the inverse variance weighted (IVW) estimator showing the strongest effect (β = 0.023, p = 6.58 × 10−11; A). Sensitivity analyses—including leave-one-SNP-out assessment ( C) and MR-PRESSO outlier exclusion ( D)—confirmed the robustness of these findings, establishing that elevated RSK1 levels causally enhance SRF expression. 
To assess the causal effect of SRF on IPF risk, MR analysis demonstrated that genetically proxied SRF elevation significantly increases susceptibility to IPF, with consistent effects observed across all five MR approaches ( F). Instrumental variable assumptions were validated by the absence of heterogeneity (Cochran’s Q = 10.95, p = 1; E) and no evidence of directional pleiotropy (MR-Egger intercept α = 0.0001, p = 0.28; E). Sensitivity analyses further supported these results ( G and 4H). Independent reanalysis of the GSE40839 dataset confirmed elevated SRF expression in IPF lung tissues compared with controls ( I and 4J), collectively establishing SRF as a causal risk factor for IPF development.
To further explore SRF activation during fibrogenesis, we examined the expression of phosphorylated SRF (p-SRF) in the BLM model. Phosphorylated SRF was significantly upregulated on day 7 and further increased by day 14 ( K and 4L), with levels being markedly higher in the BLM group than in controls at both time points ( M and 4N). Consistently, lung sections from IPF patients exhibited elevated p-SRF compared with normal tissues ( O). Collectively, these findings indicate that RSK1 may promote fibroblast activation and fibrotic progression by enhancing SRF phosphorylation, as evidenced by MR analyses linking RSK1 to SRF and SRF to IPF, together with increased p-SRF levels observed in both BLM-induced and human IPF lung tissues.

## Inhibition of SRF prevents TGF-β-induced fibroblast activation and ECM deposition
Building on our MR analysis that identified the RSK1-SRF axis as a causal driver in IPF pathogenesis, we next investigated the cellular localization and functional significance of p-SRF in fibrotic lung tissue. Double immunofluorescence staining for p-SRF and lineage-specific markers revealed predominant co-localization of p-SRF with FN-positive fibroblasts, rather than F4/80-positive macrophages or E-cadherin-positive epithelial cells, in BLM-induced fibrotic mouse lungs ( A). These data indicate that p-SRF is not only overexpressed but also likely plays a critical role in fibroblast-mediated fibrogenesis. 
To directly assess the functional relevance of SRF activation, HFL-1 cells were stimulated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β to induce activation and were simultaneously treated with 10 μM CCG-1423, a selective SRF inhibitor, for 72 h. Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated that TGF-β significantly increased both total and phosphorylated SRF levels, whereas CCG-1423 effectively abrogated this induction ( B–5E). Notably, TGF-β-induced upregulation of p-SRF was substantially suppressed upon SRF inhibition.
To provide genetic evidence for SRF dependency, we silenced SRF using siRNA in TGF-β-stimulated HFL-1 cells. SRF knockdown reduced total SRF and p-SRF and markedly suppressed fibroblast activation markers including FN, collagen II, and α-SMA ( A–S6C). We then performed a 0–120 min time course (30-min intervals) under vehicle, TGF-β, or TGF-β + CCG-1423. TGF-β rapidly increased p-SRF. With CCG-1423, p-SRF remained unchanged at 30 min, increased at 60 min, and plateaued from 60 to 120 min ( D). Total SRF was unchanged by TGF-β alone within 120 min but was markedly reduced by CCG-1423 co-treatment ( D).
Concomitantly, CCG-1423 markedly inhibited TGF-β-induced ECM protein production in HFL-1 cells. Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses confirmed that the suppression of SRF pathway activity reduced the expression of key fibrotic markers, including fibronectin, collagen II, and α-SMA ( F–5M), highlighting its inhibitory effect on fibroblast activation and fibrogenic ECM remodeling. Together, these findings demonstrate that p-SRF predominantly localizes to activated fibroblasts in fibrotic lung tissue and that pharmacological suppression of SRF pathway output, supported by complementary genetic evidence, attenuates TGF-β-induced FMT and ECM synthesis.

## RSK1 directly regulates SRF phosphorylation during TGF-β-induced fibroblast activation
To elucidate the regulatory mechanisms of TGF-β-induced FMT, double immunofluorescence staining was performed, which showed that TGF-β stimulation markedly increased nuclear co-localization of p-RSK1 and p-SRF in fibroblasts. Consistently, in HFL-1 cells treated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β for 72 h, p-RSK1 and p-SRF co-localized within the nuclei of cells, suggesting that the nuclear accumulation of p-RSK1 is associated with SRF phosphorylation and activation during FMT ( A and 6B). 
To establish a genetic requirement for RSK1 in SRF activation, we silenced RSK1 using siRNA. In both HFL-1 and IMR-90 cells, siRSK1 significantly reduced TGF-β-induced p-SRF and total SRF levels, as demonstrated by western blot; in HFL-1 cells, this effect was further confirmed by immunofluorescence ( C–6F, C, and S3D). Re-expression of RSK1 in the siRSK1 background (TGF-β + siRSK1 + OERSK1) restored the p-SRF signal, while total SRF remained slightly lower than with TGF-β alone. Consistently, RSK1 overexpression further increased both p-SRF and total SRF levels under TGF-β stimulation ( A and S4D). Together, these genetic loss- and gain-of-function data support RSK1 as an upstream regulator in controlling SRF phosphorylation and abundance during fibroblast activation. In parallel, pharmacological inhibition of RSK1 with BI-D1870 significantly reduced the TGF-β-induced p-SRF signal, as demonstrated by western blot and immunofluorescence analyses ( A–S7D), providing complementary support for the requirement of RSK1 activity in SRF activation.
We next examined whether RSK1 modulates SRF through physical interaction. Co-immunoprecipitation (coIP) assays in HFL-1 cells confirmed that RSK1 physically interacts with SRF, with p-RSK1 and p-SRF detectable in the immunocomplex under basal conditions ( G–6J). Upon TGF-β stimulation, these interactions were enhanced; importantly, siRSK1 treatment attenuated the stimulus-enhanced RSK1-SRF interaction ( K and 6L), and BI-D1870 similarly weakened the interaction detected by coIP ( E and S7F), indicating that RSK1 activity is required to sustain robust RSK1-SRF complex formation during TGF-β-induced activation.
To determine whether RSK1 regulates SRF activation directly and independent of de novo protein synthesis, cycloheximide (CHX, 20 μg/mL) was used to block translation. Co-treatment with TGF-β and CHX elevated SRF and p-SRF levels, and this induction was abolished by siRSK1 and BI-D1870 ( M–6R and G–S7I), indicating that maintenance of SRF abundance and phosphorylation under translation blockade requires RSK1 activity. Moreover, RSK1 overexpression combined with CHX treatment enhanced SRF phosphorylation, which was suppressed by BI-D1870 ( S–6X), further supporting a direct regulatory role of RSK1 in SRF activation.
Given that SRF phosphorylation represents a key regulatory step during fibroblast activation, we assessed whether mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)-activated protein kinase 2 (MK2) could account for the TGF-β-induced SRF phosphorylation observed in our fibrosis models. Although MK2 has been implicated in SRF phosphorylation in other settings, gene expression analysis from the IPF dataset GSE70866, together with mRNA and protein assays in TGF-β-treated HFL-1 cells and BLM-induced fibrotic lungs, revealed no significant alterations in MK2 expression or phosphorylation ( A–S8C). These results suggest that MK2 is unlikely to be the primary mediator of TGF-β-induced SRF phosphorylation in our system. Instead, our data support RSK1-dependent regulation of SRF phosphorylation and activation during TGF-β-driven fibroblast activation. Collectively, these findings identify a mechanism by which RSK1 directly interacts with and regulates SRF activation to drive TGF-β-induced FMT and ECM production, highlighting the RSK1-SRF axis as a candidate therapeutic vulnerability in pulmonary fibrosis.

## Superior anti-fibrotic efficacy of targeting the RSK1-SRF axis in pulmonary fibrosis
Our findings identify the RSK1-SRF axis as a central and immediate signaling module in the transcriptional reprogramming that drives fibroblast activation during fibrogenesis. To evaluate the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis in vivo, we first assessed the effects of SRF pathway suppression. Administration of CCG-1423 (0.15 mg/kg, daily for 7 days) in the BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis mouse model reduced SRF pathway activation in lung tissue, as reflected by decreased SRF abundance and a concomitant reduction in p-SRF signal by IHC and western blot analyses ( A–7D). Expression of fibroblast-associated markers—including FN, collagen II, vimentin, and α-SMA—was markedly decreased in CCG-1423-treated mice ( E and 7F), indicating the suppression of fibroblast activation and ECM deposition. 
We next examined the effect of pharmacological RSK1 inhibition. Treatment with BI-D1870 (0.5 mg/kg, daily for 7 days) significantly decreased p-RSK1 levels in lung tissues, accompanied by reduced expression of FN, collagen II, vimentin, and α-SMA ( G–7L), supporting the role of RSK1-dependent SRF activation in fibrogenesis. Notably, BI-D1870 treatment also led to a pronounced reduction in both p-SRF and total SRF levels ( M–7P), consistent with upstream regulatory control of SRF activation by RSK1 in vivo. Histological assessments—including H&E, Masson’s trichrome, and Sirius Red staining—revealed substantial attenuation of collagen deposition and fibrotic lesions in mice treated with BI-D1870 or CCG-1423, with BI-D1870 demonstrating a more pronounced therapeutic effect ( Q–7S). Consistent with these histopathological improvements, biochemical quantification of total lung collagen by HYP assay showed that BLM-induced collagen accumulation was significantly reduced in both the BI-D1870- and CCG-1423-treated groups ( A), providing global, quantified evidence that overall fibrosis burden was diminished.
Together, these results indicate that disruption of the RSK1-SRF axis effectively attenuates key profibrotic outputs in vivo. Both RSK1 inhibition and suppression of SRF pathway activity reduced FMT-associated markers, ECM deposition, and collagen accumulation in the BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis model. Importantly, the greater efficacy of RSK1 inhibition underscores its potential as an upstream therapeutic target, positioning the RSK1-SRF axis as a critical intervention point for IPF treatment.

## Discussion
IPF is characterized by alveolar epithelial damage, excessive ECM deposition, enhanced FMT, and increased myofibroblast proliferation. In recent years, fibroblast activation has been defined as FMT, a pathological process now recognized as a central driver of fibrosis. Although considerable progress has been made in identifying potential targets and therapeutic strategies for FMT, the matrix-related mechanisms underlying this process remain incompletely understood, and more effective, specific interventions are urgently needed. This study identifies RSK1 as a causal risk factor for IPF, with significantly elevated expression in fibrotic lung tissues ( ). Pharmacological inhibition of RSK1 effectively suppressed TGF-β-induced fibroblast activation and ECM production. Integrated bioinformatic analyses revealed SRF as a key downstream effector linked to fibroblast activation in IPF. MR analysis further supported a causal role for the RSK1-SRF signaling axis in IPF pathogenesis. In vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that inhibition of either RSK1 or SRF attenuates fibroblast activation, ECM deposition, and fibrosis progression. Mechanistically, our data support RSK1 as an upstream regulator of SRF phosphorylation and activation during FMT. Together, these findings identify the RSK1-SRF axis as a key signaling pathway in pulmonary fibrogenesis. 
RSK1 is a core effector in the Ras-MAPK signaling cascade and functions downstream of ERK1/2. Upon activation, ERK1/2 phosphorylates and activates RSK1, which, in turn, regulates multiple downstream effectors. RSK1 has been shown to promote cell proliferation by modulating cyclin-dependent kinases and increasing cyclin protein levels. , Beyond cell cycle control, RSK1 enhances invasion and metastasis in several malignancies, including those of the breast, colon, kidneys, stomach, and prostate. Moreover, emerging evidence implicates RSK1 in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis across various disease contexts. Recent studies have highlighted the role of enhanced RSK activation in the pathogenesis of fibrotic diseases, including liver and lung fibrosis. , Our previous findings suggest that RSK1 promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and aberrant ECM deposition, contributing to organ fibrosis, such as in the kidney. However, the specific role of RSK1 in IPF remains incompletely understood. Limited evidence indicates that RSK inhibition suppresses TGF-β1-induced Smad3 nuclear translocation and Smad-binding element-dependent transcriptional activity, without affecting Smad3 phosphorylation. Additionally, RSK1 has been implicated in lung injury and fibrosis through phosphorylation of C/EBP-β. Our study identified RSK1 as a causal risk factor for IPF through MR analysis, which revealed a strong genetic association between elevated RSK1 levels and increased IPF susceptibility. Experimental validation showed significant upregulation of RSK1 in fibrotic lung tissues and its functional involvement in promoting FMT, a key pathological feature of IPF. Integrated bioinformatic analysis of multiple transcriptomic datasets further identified SRF as a central downstream effector, highlighting the critical role of the RSK1-SRF axis in mediating fibroblast activation.
SRF, a key transcription factor of the MADS-box family, contains a conserved MADS domain essential for signal transduction during tissue development and remodeling. SRF binds to the serum response element (SRE; CArG box) in target gene promoters, regulating genes involved in proliferation, migration, and differentiation. Myocardin and myocardin-related transcription factors (MRTFs), critical SRF cofactors, translocate from the cytoplasm to the nucleus upon activation and cooperate with SRF to induce the transcription of genes related to cytoskeletal organization and myogenic differentiation. , , Consistent with our findings, recent studies have reported elevated SRF expression in IPF lung tissues, particularly in fibrotic regions and fibroblastic foci, highlighting its role in fibroblast activation and fibrogenesis. , SRF activation is primarily driven by upstream signals such as serum, GPCRs, and cytoskeletal dynamics, with profibrotic mediators including TGF-β further enhancing this process through converging pathways. Notably, TGF-β markedly upregulates SRF expression and activity during myofibroblast differentiation, and SRF inhibition disrupts α-SMA expression. Beyond its canonical functions, SRF is also implicated in non-canonical signaling and cellular homeostasis and plays a pivotal role in fibrotic expansion, as evidenced by its enrichment in fibrotic foci and interaction with profibrotic molecules in IPF and other fibrotic lung diseases. , In this study, MR analysis revealed a significant causal association between elevated SRF expression and increased susceptibility to IPF, identifying SRF as a critical genetic risk factor for disease pathogenesis. Additionally, our data demonstrate a strong causal link between RSK1 and SRF, supporting SRF as a direct downstream effector of RSK1 involved in TGF-β-mediated FMT and fibrotic progression. These findings are consistent with and extend previous reports implicating SRF as a central transcription factor governing fibroblast activation and ECM remodeling in fibrotic disorders.
Studies on the therapeutic potential of RSK1 inhibition in fibrotic diseases, particularly IPF, remain limited. Although preclinical data from other organ fibrosis models suggest a profibrotic role for RSK1, evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of RSK1-targeted therapies in lung fibrosis is scarce, warranting further investigation. In contrast, extensive research has explored the pharmacological inhibition of SRF or disruption of its interaction with key cofactors. Agents such as noscapine and FGF2 have also exhibited anti-fibrotic effects, partly by modulating SRF-associated pathways. Notably, small-molecule inhibitors of SRF signaling, including CCG-1423 and CCG-203971, have shown potent anti-fibrotic effects in both in vitro and in vivo models. , Although SRF has been implicated as a key transcription factor in fibrotic diseases, the upstream regulatory mechanisms governing SRF phosphorylation and pathway activation during fibroblast activation remain insufficiently characterized. Elucidating the upstream regulatory mechanisms governing SRF activity is, therefore, essential to address this knowledge gap. Our study proposes a mechanism in which RSK1 functions as an upstream regulator of SRF during TGF-β-induced FMT in IPF. We demonstrated that RSK1 physically associates with SRF and promotes SRF phosphorylation and accumulation during TGF-β-induced fibroblast activation. Under CHX treatment, disruption of RSK1 activity (genetically or pharmacologically) abolished the TGF-β-induced increase in p-SRF and total SRF, supporting that RSK1 regulates SRF activation through a proximal mechanism that does not require de novo protein synthesis. RSK1-dependent SRF activation amplifies SRF-driven profibrotic transcription, including upregulation of key markers such as ACTA2 and COL1A1. Notably, RSK1 inhibition not only reduces SRF phosphorylation but also decreases total SRF protein levels, suggesting multi-level regulatory control. Compared with the direct SRF pathway blockade, RSK1 inhibition may offer a more selective and potentially safer strategy to suppress aberrant SRF activation in fibrotic microenvironments, thereby limiting off-target effects. The superior anti-fibrotic efficacy observed with RSK1 inhibition in vivo further supports its therapeutic potential.
In summary, although RSK1 and SRF have long been implicated in fibrotic diseases, their functional coupling in driving fibroblast activation and IPF progression has remained insufficiently defined. A key finding of this study is the integration of MR with multi-omics and experimental validation to support a causal contribution of RSK1—and the broader RSK1-SRF signaling axis—to IPF susceptibility and fibrogenic remodeling. Mechanistically, we provide evidence that activated RSK1 physically associates with SRF and governs SRF phosphorylation and accumulation during TGF-β-driven fibroblast activation. Notably, our data further support that RSK1 regulates SRF through a proximal mechanism that can operate independently of de novo protein synthesis, enabling rapid and context-dependent modulation of fibrotic gene programs. Therapeutically, targeting this axis may offer advantages: compared with direct blockade of broadly acting transcription factors such as SRF, inhibition of the upstream kinase RSK1 may provide a more tractable and potentially safer strategy to suppress aberrant SRF activation and the FMT-associated transcriptional program. In our experimental models, RSK1 inhibition was associated with stronger anti-fibrotic effects than SRF pathway suppression, supporting RSK1 as an upstream therapeutic target. Collectively, these findings position the RSK1-SRF axis as a promising molecular target for developing more effective and selective therapies for IPF and other fibrotic disorders.

## Limitations of the study
Despite the comprehensive experimental design and robust findings, several avenues for future refinement and validation remain. Incorporating additional primary fibroblast lines derived from IPF patients could help better reflect the cellular heterogeneity observed in the human disease context. The use of genetically engineered mouse models, such as those with fibroblast-specific RSK1 or SRF knockout, would provide more definitive evidence for the causal role of the RSK1-SRF axis in fibrogenesis and enhance the mechanistic depth of in vivo analyses.

## Method details

## Antibodies and reagents
Primary antibodies and key reagents are listed in the . Secondary HRP-conjugated antibodies, Alexa Fluor 488– and Alexa Fluor 594–conjugated secondary antibodies, fetal bovine serum (FBS), and common supplements were obtained from Fisher Scientific unless otherwise indicated. CCG-1423 (MedChemExpress, HY-13991) and BI-D1870 (MedChemExpress, HY-10510) were used as specified below.

## Cell culture and transfection
HFL-1 cells were cultured in Ham’s F-12K medium supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. IMR-90 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. All cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Cells were passaged by standard trypsinization and used for experiments in the logarithmic growth phase within a controlled passage range.
siRNAs targeting RSK1 and SRF, as well as non-specific control siRNAs, were synthesized by GenScript (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) and are listed in . Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were harvested and resuspended in serum-free medium at a concentration of 2.86 × 106 cells/mL. For each electroporation, 100 μL of the cell suspension (2.86 × 105 cells) was mixed with siRNA at a final concentration of 60 nM and transferred into a 96-well electroporation plate. Electroporation was performed using an X-Porator H1 electroporator (EBXP-H1; Etta Biotech Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China).
A plasmid encoding human RSK1 isoform 1 was synthesized by GenScript (Nanjing, China) and introduced into HFL-1 cells by electroporation. For plasmid electroporation, 2.86 × 105 cells were mixed with 1.2 μg plasmid DNA per reaction. For both siRNA and plasmid electroporation, the following conditions were used: 260 V, 100 μs pulse width, 6 pulses, with a 100 ms interval between pulses. Post-electroporation, cells were immediately recovered in complete medium and cultured prior to downstream assays.

## Preparation of small-molecule inhibitors
BI-D1870 (MedChemExpress, Cat# HY-10510) stock: 5 mg BI-D1870 was dissolved in 1.2774 mL DMSO to obtain a 10 mM stock solution. For cell experiments, BI-D1870 was used at 10 μM. For animal experiments, BI-D1870 was administered at 0.5 mg/kg (adjusted to body weight).
CCG-1423 (MedChemExpress, Cat# HY-13991) stock: 10 mg CCG-1423 was dissolved in 1.0995 mL DMSO to obtain a 20 mM stock solution. For cell experiments, CCG-1423 was used at 10 μM. For animal experiments, CCG-1423 was administered at 0.15 mg/kg (adjusted to body weight).
For in vivo studies, DMSO stocks were diluted in the injection vehicle immediately prior to use. Control animals received matched vehicle preparations administered via the same route and at the same injection volume as the corresponding drug-treated groups.

## Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was isolated from HFL-1 cells or lung tissues using RNA-easy isolation reagent (Vazyme) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, followed by reverse transcription using the Easyscript first-strand cDNA synthesis supermix kit (Vazyme). qPCR was performed on a ViiA 7 system (Applied Biosystems) using ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme). Samples were analyzed in technical triplicates. Relative expression was calculated using GAPDH as an internal control. Primer sequences are provided in the .

## Western blotting
Proteins were extracted from cells or lung tissues using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails. Equal amounts of protein were separated on 8%–10% SDS–PAGE gels and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBS (25 mM Tris, 0.15 M NaCl, pH 7.3) for 1 h at room temperature and washed with TBS containing 0.05% Tween 20 (TBST). Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies ( ) overnight at 4°C, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Signals were visualized using an ECL chemiluminescence system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ and normalized to GAPDH or β-actin.
For cell-based immunoblot experiments, biological replicates (n) were defined as independent cell culture batches of the same cell line that were cultured and treated separately to generate independently prepared lysates; unless otherwise indicated, all immunoblot experiments were independently repeated at least three times (n ≥ 3). Densitometric quantification was performed for each biological replicate, and these independent values were used for statistical analysis. Uncropped/full immunoblot images have been provided as source data and deposited in Mendeley Data (https://doi.org/10.17632/Yp2jvj4696.1).

## Immunofluorescence staining
Cells or lung tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and blocked with 5% BSA at 4°C for 30 min. Primary antibodies were diluted 1:100 in PBS containing 1% BSA and 0.3% Triton X-100 and incubated with samples for 1 h, followed by Alexa Fluor–conjugated secondary antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1 μg/mL). Imaging was performed on a Nikon Eclipse microscope. Fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ. For each sample, at least three randomly selected fields per section were analyzed using identical acquisition settings. Data were obtained from three independent experiments and reported as mean ± SD.

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
Co-IP was performed using the Pierce Co-IP kit (Thermo Scientific) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Antibodies were immobilized on AminoLink Plus Coupling Resin for 2 h, washed, and incubated with lung tissue lysates overnight. Kit-provided negative controls were processed in parallel to assess nonspecific binding. Immunocomplexes were washed five times and analyzed by SDS-PAGE followed by immunoblotting with antibodies listed in the .

## Induction and pharmacological treatment of pulmonary fibrosis in mice
Pulmonary fibrosis was induced by a single intratracheal instillation of bleomycin hydrochloride (MedChemExpress, Cat# HY-17565A) at a dose of 5 mg/kg body weight (mass of bleomycin hydrochloride per kg), dissolved in 50 μL sterile normal saline. Control animals received 50 μL saline alone.
For pharmacological studies, mice received daily intraperitoneal injections of CCG-1423 (0.15 mg/kg) or matched vehicle starting on day 7 post-bleomycin for 7 consecutive days, and were sacrificed on day 15 for tissue collection. To evaluate the role of RSK1, mice were treated with BI-D1870 (0.5 mg/kg, once daily starting on day 7 post-bleomycin) and sacrificed on day 15 for downstream analyses. Stock solutions were prepared in DMSO and diluted in the injection vehicle immediately prior to use. Vehicle control mice received the same injection vehicle at the same injection volume as the drug-treated groups.

## Tissue preparation, histology, and immunohistochemistry
Lung tissues were paraffin-embedded, sectioned at 5 μm, and deparaffinized. Sections were processed for H&E staining or subjected to antigen retrieval and immunohistochemistry using the indicated primary and species-specific secondary antibodies. Primary antibodies included phospho-SRF (Ser103), phospho-RSK1 (Thr359/Ser363), and total RSK1 ( ), each used at 1:100.

## Masson’s trichrome staining and Sirius Red staining
Paraffin-embedded lung sections (5 μm) were stained with Masson’s trichrome and Sirius Red to assess collagen deposition. For Sirius Red staining, sections were stained using the Sirius Red Staining Kit (Solarbio; G1472) per manufacturer instructions, including incubation in saturated aqueous picric acid containing 0.1% Sirius Red for 30 min. Collagen deposition was quantified using ImageJ by calculating collagen-positive area relative to total tissue area, normalized to the control group.

## Hydroxyproline measurement
Hydroxyproline (HYP) content in mouse lung tissues was quantified using a commercial hydroxyproline assay kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China; BC0250) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, ∼0.2 g of lung tissue was homogenized in 6 mol/L HCl and hydrolyzed in a boiling water bath until fully digested. After cooling, the hydrolysate was neutralized to pH 6–8 with 10 mol/L NaOH, brought to a defined final volume with distilled water, and clarified by centrifugation (16,000 × g, 4°C, 20 min). The supernatant was used for colorimetric detection. Absorbance was measured at 560 nm, and hydroxyproline concentrations were calculated from a standard curve generated with the kit-provided hydroxyproline standard. Final HYP content was normalized to tissue wet weight (μg/g). All measurements were performed in triplicate.

## Mendelian randomization analyses
Mendelian randomization (MR) was used to assess potential causal relationships between expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL)-instrumented exposures and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This study adhered to the three core MR assumptions: (1) Relevance assumption—the genetic variant(s) must be robustly associated with the exposure of interest; (2) Independence assumption—the genetic variant(s) must be independent of confounders that jointly influence the exposure and the outcome; and (3) Exclusion restriction assumption—the genetic variant(s) must influence the outcome only through the exposure, and not via alternative biological pathways. Summary statistics were obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS database. We used eQTL summary datasets for the RSK1 signaling pathway (31,684 samples; 16,468 SNPs; selection threshold p < 5 × 10−6) and SRF regulatory elements (30,935 samples; 17,622 SNPs), together with an IPF GWAS dataset comprising 1,369 cases and 435,866 controls.
Instrument selection and harmonization were performed as follows: independent instruments were obtained via LD clumping (100 kb window, r2 < 0.01); effect alleles were harmonized between exposure and outcome datasets, and ambiguous palindromic SNPs were excluded when unresolvable by allele frequency information; instrument strength was evaluated using SNP-level F-statistics (F = (β/SE) ), and variants with F-statistics ≤10 were removed to minimize weak-instrument bias. Heterogeneity across instruments was assessed using Cochran’s Q statistic (p < 0.05 indicating significant heterogeneity). Potential horizontal pleiotropy was evaluated using the MR-Egger intercept test, with a non-significant intercept (p > 0.05) indicating no evidence of directional pleiotropy. Causal effects were primarily estimated using the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method, with complementary analyses using MR-Egger, weighted median, simple mode, and weighted mode implemented in the TwoSampleMR R package. Robustness was evaluated based on consistency in the direction and magnitude of effect estimates across methods in conjunction with pleiotropy/heterogeneity diagnostics. Finally, leave-one-out analyses were conducted to assess whether the overall MR estimate was disproportionately driven by any single SNP. All instrumental variables and their SNP-level statistics, including F-statistics and SNP–exposure/outcome effect estimates (β, SE, p-values), are summarized in .

## Acquisition of disease-related targets and transcriptomic datasets
Fibroblast transcriptomic datasets were downloaded from NCBI GEO and included three microarray series: GSE1724 (platform GPL96, Affymetrix Human Genome U133A; 6 cases/6 controls), GSE40839 (platform GPL14550, Agilent microarray; 3 cases/10 controls), and GSE44723 (platform GPL6480, Agilent microarray; 10 cases/4 controls) ( ). Expression data were imported as log2-scale gene expression matrices as provided by GEO; when necessary, probe identifiers were mapped to gene symbols and summarized at the gene level. After gene-level summarization, batch effects across datasets were mitigated using surrogate variable analysis (SVA) followed by ComBat harmonization, and principal component analysis (PCA) was used to assess inter-batch clustering before and after correction.
Potential pulmonary fibrosis–related targets were collected from OMIM, DisGeNET, and GeneCards using “pulmonary fibrosis” as a keyword. Retrieved lists were merged, duplicates removed, and false positives were excluded based on database annotations and supporting literature evidence.

## Differential expression, enrichment, network, and feature selection analyses
Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package. Genes meeting both FDR-adjusted p-value <0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5 were considered significant. Differential expression results were visualized using ggplot2. Functional enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler for Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway analyses, and WebGestalt for Disease Ontology (DO), with FDR <0.05 as the significance threshold. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks were constructed using STRING with a confidence score ≥0.4 and visualized in Cytoscape. Hub genes were prioritized using CytoHubba based on Betweenness, Stress, and BottleNeck centrality metrics; genes consistently ranked among the top candidates across algorithms were retained as putative hubs. Finally, LASSO logistic regression (glmnet v4.1.8) with 10-fold cross-validation was used to select key features that discriminate IPF fibroblasts from controls based on the candidate DEG set.

## Quantification and statistical analysis
Unless otherwise stated, results are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical analyses were performed using Student’s t test for two-group comparisons and one-way ANOVA for comparisons involving three or more groups. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Details regarding the number of biological replicates (n), definitions of technical replicates, and any specific post hoc tests are provided in the corresponding figure legends and/or Results text.
