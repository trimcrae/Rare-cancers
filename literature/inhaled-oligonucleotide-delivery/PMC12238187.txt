## Abstract

## Purpose
Ovarian cancer (OC) is the most lethal gynecological malignancy, with widespread metastasis and ascites being the leading causes of patient mortality. However, the mechanisms driving OC metastasis have not been sufficiently studied. This study aimed to investigate the mechanisms and key molecules promoting OC metastasis.

## Methods
Public databases (StemChecker, GeneCards, GEO, and TCGA) were screened to identify metastasis-associated genes. Immunohistochemical staining and western blotting were employed to evaluate THEMIS2 expression and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) marker profiles across experimental groups. RNA sequencing coupled with pathway enrichment analysis revealed THEMIS2-regulated signaling pathways, while immunoprecipitation-mass spectrometry was utilized to identify THEMIS2 interaction partners. GST pull-down assays for active Rap1 quantified Rap1-GTP levels under varying THEMIS2 expression conditions. Wound healing and transwell invasion assays respectively assessed migratory and invasive capacities of OC cells following THEMIS2 expression perturbations in vitro. Abdominal cavity implantation metastasis model was established to evaluate OC cell colonization and invasive potential in vivo.

## Results
THEMIS2 expression is significantly elevated in OC tissues compared to normal ovarian tissues, and its high expression correlates with poor prognosis and malignant features. Experimental manipulation of THEMIS2 levels revealed that knockdown impended the migratory and invasive capacities of OC cells both in vitro and in vivo, while its overexpression exacerbated metastasis. THEMIS2 is involved in EMT and cytoskeleton rearrangement. RNA-seq analysis revealed that THEMIS2 positively correlates with Rap1 signaling pathway. Inhibition of Rap1 activity reversed the metastasis-promoting effects induced by THEMIS2 overexpression both in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, we uncovered that THEMIS2 functions as a molecular scaffold that recruits TBK1 (TANK Binding Kinase 1) to DOCK4 (Dedicator of Cytokinesis 4), facilitating site-specific phosphorylation at serine 1787 (S1787). This post-translational modification enables DOCK4 to engage with CRKII, subsequently triggering Rap1 signaling activation. These findings suggest that THEMIS2 promotes the metastatic potential of OC cells via DOCK4-mediated activation of Rap1 signaling.

## Conclusion
THEMIS2 may serve as a predictive biomarker for OC prognosis, and targeting the Rap1 signaling pathway with specific inhibitors represents a promising therapeutic strategy for OC treatment.

## Introduction
Ovarian cancer (OC) maintains its status as the 8th most prevalent malignancy in female populations globally. Global epidemiological data from 2020 documented 324, 398 new cases (age-standardized motility rate [ASR]: 6.6 per 100, 000 women) culminating in 206, 839 fatalities (ASR: 4.0 per 100 000 women) [ ]. Emerging projections estimate 19, 710 new diagnoses and 13, 270 disease-related deaths within the United States by 2023 [ ]. Over the coming decades, both the age-standardized incidence (projected to rise from 4.77 in 2019 to 8.95 in 2049) and the mortality rate (from 2.88 in 2019 to 4.03 in 2049) are predicted to increase substantially. Metastasis, the most lethal manifestation of cancer, is the primary cause of treatment failure and the high mortality rates patients with OC. Ovarian malignant cells detach from the primary tumor site and disseminate into the abdominal fluid, where they aggregate into spheroids that exhibit enhanced survival mechanisms while in suspension [ ]. Over 70% OC cases are diagnosed with extensive pelvic and abdominal spread and metastasis, leading to suboptimal surgical outcomes in more than 50% of advanced cases [ ]. Tumor metastasis often leads to relapse following chemotherapy-induced regression, and effective targeted therapies to address tumor aggressiveness remain lacking in clinical practice. Consequently, delineating metastasis-associated molecular circuitry and deciphering the underlying molecular mechanisms are crucial for developing improved treatment strategies for OC.
Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a fundamental biological process orchestrating cellular plasticity during embryogenesis and tumorigenesis, is characterized by the loss of stable epithelial cell-cell junction, apical-basal polarity, and interactions with the basement membrane. This phenotypic reprogramming confers mesenchymal traits including front-rear polarization, dynamic focal adhesion remodeling, and cytoskeletal realignment, collectively endowing cells with migratory competence and tissue-invasive potential [ ]. In OC, peritoneal malignant ascites creates a highly dynamic tumor microenvironment, playing a pivotal role in tumor recurrence [ ]. Tumor cells shed from the primary lesion travel as spheroids through ascitic fluid into the peritoneal cavity, where EMT drives anoikis resistance and facilitates their implantation at secondary sites via the transcoelomic metastasis pathway [ ]. This process is initiated by the downregulation of epithelial proteins, such as E-cadherin, alongside the upregulation of mesenchymal proteins, including N-cadherin and vimentin.
The cytoskeleton is a fibrous network of protein filaments, including microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments, and microtubules. It not only maintains cell morphology but also plays a vital role in various cellular processes such as migration, division, signal transduction, and vesicular transport. During metastasis, cancer cell movement and changes in cell shape require extensive cytoskeletal remodeling. Ample evidence indicates that the cytoskeleton is deregulated in cancer, and several cytoskeletal-interacting proteins significantly influence the migratory and metastatic phenotype of cancer cells [ ].
Thymocyte expressed molecule involved in selection 2 (THEMIS2), also known as ICB-1 or C1orf38, is a member of the THEMIS family initially identified as a gene with elevated expression in endometrial tumor cells that differentiated in vitro on a reconstituted basement membrane [ ]. Similar to Themis1, Themis2 contains two conserved cysteine-based domains, a proline rich region, and a nuclear localization signal [ ]. In murine macrophages, THEMIS2 expression is induced during development. Upon LPS stimulation, THEMIS2 undergoes inducible tyrosine phosphorylation and interacts with Lyn kinase, the Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor Vav, and the adaptor protein Grb2, enhancing the activation of the MAP kinases ERK and p38 [ ]. In T cells, THEMIS2 can restore T cell development and is recruited with Grb2 signaling complexes to LAT following TCR engagement [ ]. In natural killer (NK) cells, THEMIS2 regulates memory function and formation, and suppresses antitumor activity by inhibiting activating NK receptor signaling [ , ]. Regarding tumorigenesis, THEMIS2 transcription is estrogen-responsive, with its knockdown enhancing estrogen sensitivity in breast and ovarian cancer cells [ ]. Elevated THEMIS2 protein levels have been observed in clear cell carcinoma of the ovary and endometrial adenocarcinoma [ ]. The invasion and migratory abilities of OC cells were significantly impaired following THEMIS2 knockdown in vitro [ ]; however, the molecular basis for the function of THEMIS2 in OC remain unclear. A recent study by Huang et al. found significantly increased THEMIS2 expression in breast cancer stem cell lines, identifying it as a novel regulator of cancer stemness and chemoresistance. THEMIS2 disrupts the interaction between PTP1B and p-MET, thereby enhancing MET signaling in cancer cells [ ].
Rap1 (Ras-Related Protein Rap-1 A), a member of the Ras family of small GTPases, transitions between GDP- and GTP-bound states, with its interactions dictated by nucleotide bound. This allows Rap1 to serve as a molecular switch, linking diverse extracellular signals to various cellular responses across different cell types. The GDP-to-GTP transition is facilitated by guanine nucleotide-exchange factors (GEFs), which promote nucleotide release and enable more abundant GTP to rebind. Emerging research highlights the critical role of Rap1 dysregulation in malignancies development. Enhanced Rap1 activation dramatically augments migrative and invasive capabilities of prostate cancer cell both in vivo and in vitro [ ]. In breast epithelial cells, Rap1 regulates tissue polarity, lumen formation, and invasive potential [ ]. Notably, pharmacological inhibition of Rap1 with GGTI-298 has been shown to impair the migration of MCF7 cancer cells in a scratch-wound assays. Rap1 activation is contingent upon GDP-GTP exchange, catalyzed by specific GEFs. Among the Dock family GEFs, characterized by the conserved Dock homology region-2 (DHR-2) domain that interacts with Rho GTPase to drive GDP-GTP exchange, DOCK4 stands out. DOCK4 facilitates Rap1 and Rac1 activation, playing roles in dendritic spine formation [ ], maintains adherens junction necessary for lung vascular barrier integrity [ ], and promoting migration in MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells [ ].
In this study, we reported that elevated expression of THEMIS2 is significantly correlated with poor clinical outcomes in OC patients. Silencing THEMIS2 impaired EMT and disrupted the cytoskeleton, thereby suppressing the migration and invasion of OC cells. Conversely, overexpression of THEMIS2 potently promoted metastatic properties of OC both in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, we discovered a novel mechanism by which THEMIS2 facilitates OC metastasis through Rap1 signaling activation. This process involves THEMIS2-mediated recruitment of TBK1 to DOCK4, leading to site-specific phosphorylation at serine 1787 (S1787) and subsequent stabilization of the interaction between DOCK4 and CRKII. Collectively, our findings unveiled a pivotal role of THEMIS2 in OC metastasis, offering potential diagnostic and therapeutic implications for OC management.

## Materials and methods

## Data mining
A stemness-related gene set was constructed using common genes retrieved from 26 stemness gene sets downloaded from StemChecker (http://stemchecker.sysbiolab.eu/), which is based on updated collection of published stemness signatures defined by RNAi screens, gene expression profiles and so on. The GeneCard database (https://www.genecards.org/) was utilized to obtain invasion-related genes with gifts greater than 35. THEMIS2 expression patterns and its prognostic value of THEMIS2 in OC were analyzed using three GEO datasets (GSE6388, GSE26193 and GSE222982) and TCGA. The primary data for GEO datasets were downloaded from https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo. Information regarding the diagnostic significance of THEMIS2 in OC was obtained from publicly available datasets, including the Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) and Kaplan-Meier Plotter (http://kmplot.com/analysis/). Additionally, primary data from Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium datasets were downloaded from https://proteomics.cancer.gov/programs/cptac. PhosphositePlus (https://www.phosphosite.org/homeAction.action) database and 和Group-based Prediction System (GPS) 6.0 algorithm (https://gps.biocuckoo.cn/) were employed for in silico prediction of protein phosphorylation sites.

## Clinical specimens
10 ovarian tissues from patients with uterine leiomyoma or ovarian cyst (benign control) and 39 ovarian cancer tissues were collected at Shanghai Cancer Center, Fudan University, from 2015 to 2024 during surgery. All patients in this study had pathologically confirmed diagnoses, and their pathological stages were determined based on the AJCC and TNM classification systems. The proposal for this study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

## In vivo assay
All animal experiments followed the National Institute of Health guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC-IACUC-2024345). Athymic female nude mice (BALB/c nu/nu, aged 5 weeks) were purchased from Beijing Life River Laboratory Animal Technology and maintained in a pathogen-free facility. Each group included five mice. Approximately OC cells (5 × 106) suspended in 150 ul of PBS were injected into the peritoneal space of the mice. D-luciferin (150 mg/kg, Beyotime, ST196) was injected intraperitoneally to monitor tumor growth in an Xenogen-IVIS Imaging System. Body weight of mice was measured on a regular basis. Three weeks after injection, animals were euthanized by CO2 inhalation anesthesia, and metastases in the peritoneal cavity were assessed.

## Cell lines and cell cultivation
Human OC cell lines (Hey and OVCAR8) were gained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Hey cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotics (Gibco, 15240062), while OVCAR8 cells were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotics. All cultures were incubated at 37 °C, 5% CO2, and saturated humidity. All the cell lines were tested free of mycoplasma contamination.

## Immunoprecipitation (IP)
Briefly, cultured cells were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013C) supplemented with 1 × protease/phosphatase Inhibitors and the supernatant was collected. Equal amounts of total proteins were used for IP. Magnetic beads (Thermo Scientific, 88803) were crosslinked with an anti-Flag antibody (Proteintech, 66008-4), DOCK4 (Proteintech, 21861-1-AP), CRKII (Abcepta, AP92457), or species-matched control IgG according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Lysates were centrifugated, and supernatant was incubated with antibody-conjugated magnetic beads for overnight at 4 °C with gently rotation. After washing with PBS for several times, IP products were dissolved in 2 × SDS Laemmli buffer without β-mercaptoethanol and β-mercaptoethanol was supplemented before western blot.

## Mass spectrometry
After electrophoresis, SDS-PAGE gels were stained with Coomassie blue solution for 15 min at room temperature. The protein bands of interest were equilibrated twice in ultrapure water twice and then soaked in 100 µl Destain buffer (25mM NH4HCO3 and 50% acetonitrile in water) for 5 min or longer until the gel chips lose color. The supernatant was replaced sequentially with 50% acetonitrile in water and 100% acetonitrile for 30 min each to dehydrate the gel. Subsequently, the gels were immersed in 50ul reduction buffer (10mM DTT/25mM NH4HCO3) and incubated at 55℃ for 1 h, followed by soaking in 50ul alkylation buffer (50mM IAA/25mM NH4HCO3) for 30 min. The gels were then dehydrated twice for 30 min each using gel recover buffer (25mM NH4HCO3). After drying, the gels were rehydrated with 0.02 µg/µL trypsin in 25mM NH4HCO3 and incubated overnight at 37 °C. Prior to spectrometric analysis, peptides were desalted. Finally, Spectrometric analysis was conducted on an UltiMate 3000 RSLC nano (ThermoFisher Scientific)and a Q-Exactive plus (ThermoFisher Scientific).

## GST pull-down assay for Rap1 activation
To evaluate the contribution of THEMIS2 to Rap1 activity in OC cells, cell lysate from cells in the exponential growth phase was prepared. The Rap1 Activation Assay Kit (NewEast Biosciences, #81401) was used to measure the levels of active GTP loading of Rap1. In accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, cells were lysed in ice-cold 1X Assay/Lysis Buffer and were centrifuged for 15 min at 14,000 ×g and 4℃. The supernatant was transferred into a new reaction tube containing 1 µL anti-Rap1-GTP antibody. 20 µL of resuspended protein A/G Agarose bead slurry was added to above tube. Immunoprecipitation was performed rotating at 4℃ for 1 h. afterward, beads were washed 3 times with 0.5 mL of 1X Assay/Lysis Buffer, centrifuging and aspirating each time. Following the washing steps, 2X reducing SDS- PAGE sample buffer was added to the beads, and the samples were boiled at 95 °C for 5 min, and analyzed by western blot. Equal amounts of total cell protein were used to normalize RAP activity across cell lines.

## Statistical analysis
Data were presented as mean ± SD or as boxplots unless indicated otherwise, and all statistics were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software and Excel. One-way ANOVA, two-way ANOVA, or two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test were used as appropriate for the dataset. Comparison of Kaplan-Meier survival curves was used to illustrate the overall survival in patients with OC, and significance was determined by the log-rank Mantel-Cox test. Specific statistical test used for each figure was described in the corresponding figure legend. All experiments with cell lines were done in at least triplicates unless otherwise specified. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## THEMIS2 is upregulated in ovarian cancer tissues and correlates with poor prognosis
The stemness of cancer stem cells represents a critical factor in tumor metastasis. In order to assemble OC metastasis jigsaw puzzle, we collected 4317 stemness-related genes (SRGs) from StemChecker database and 9980 invasion-related genes (IRGs, Gifts > 35) from GeneCard database. Through comparative analysis of these gene sets, we identified 2535 candidate genes that are potentially involved in both stemness and invasion processes. To investigate the clinical relevance of candidate genes, we analyzed 3 independent OC GEO datasets (GSE6388, GSE26193, GSE222982). Bioinformatic screening identified 4 prognostic genes (NFKBIB, ZNF70, EPB41 and THEMIS2) demonstrating significant associations with poor prognosis (Hazard. Ratio > 1, p < 0.05; Fig.  A). Comparative expression analysis revealed marked upregulation of EPB41 and THEMIS2 in metastatic lesions versus primary tumors (Fig.  B). External validation through the Human Protein Atlas database confirmed THEMIS2 overexpression as an independent prognostic indicator (p = 0.031), while EPB41 failed to reach statistical significance (p = 0.067; Fig.  C). Multi-cohort validation across 8 independent datasets (GSE19829, GSE30161, GSE3149, GSE9891, GSE15622, GSE27651, TCGA Ovarian JHU, PNNL Proteome) consistently demonstrated that elevated THEMIS2 expression correlated with adverse clinical outcomes (Fig.  D and E). Immunohistochemical analysis of clinical specimens revealed significant THEMIS2 overexpression in OC tissues compared to normal ovarian epithelium (Fig.  F and G). Semi-quantitative scoring incorporating staining intensity and distribution patterns showed differential expression profiles: low THEMIS2 levels predominated in non-metastatic cases without without ascites and lymphatic metastasis, whereas high expression correlated with ascites and lymphatic metastasis (Fig.  G). Subsequent clinicopathological correlation analysis further established THEMIS2 as a significant predictor of malignant ascites development (Table  ).
 

## THEMIS2 promotes the migration and invasion of OC cells both in vitro and in vivo
To investigate the functional implications of THEMIS2 in OC cells motility, we conducted comprehensive in vitro and in vivo analyses. Initial trans-well invasion assays and wound closure experiments were performed to investigate whether THEMIS2 expression levels significantly influence cellular movement patterns. Through lentiviral transduction with PLKO.1-shTHEMIS2 particles in Hey and OVCAR8 cells, we successfully established stable THEMIS2-knockdown cell models. Conversely, ectopic expression was achieved by transfecting 293T cells with our engineered pCDH-THEMIS2 overexpression plasmid (Fig.  A and B). Quantitative assessment revealed a positive correlation between THEMIS2 expression levels and metastatic potential, with overexpression enhancing while knockdown suppressing both migratory capacity and invasive properties (Fig.  C, D, E and F; Supplementary Fig. 1). For in vivo validation, we generated an abdominal cavity implantation metastasis model through intraperitoneal inoculation of luciferase-expressing Hey cells. Bioluminescence tracking demonstrated significant attenuation of metastatic progression in THEMIS2-deficient groups, evidenced by diminished photon emission intensity over time (Fig.  G and H). As shown in Fig.  I, THEMIS2 knockdown impeded sporadic intraperitoneal metastatic colonization to various organs. The number of metastatic nodules in liver, intestine and diaphragm was smaller in the THEMIS2 silencing group compared to the control group (Fig.  J).
 

## THEMIS2 deficiency restricts EMT and induces F-actin degradation but THEMIS2 overexpression reverses these phenotypes in OC cells
To delineate the pathobiological significance of THEMIS2 in OC, we performed GSEA on transcriptomic profiles from TCGA and GSE9891 datasets. GSEA results demonstrated significant enrichment of EMT and cytoskeleton gene sets in high-THEMIS2 expressers (Fig.  A). EMT progression in OC metastasis is characterized by pathognomonic molecular shifts involving cadherin switching-specificall, transcriptional suppression of E-cadherin coupled with induced expression of N-cadherin and vimentin. Parallel to these changes, zinc-dependent endopeptidases of the matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) family, particularly MMP2/MMP9, mediate extracellular matrix (ECM) proteolysis. This enzymatic activity facilitates basement membrane disintegration and subsequent epithelial cell detachment. Our experimental validation revealed THEMIS2-dependent regulation of EMT effectors: THEMIS2 knockdown upregulated E-cadherin expression while downregulating vimentin, MMP2 and MMP compared to controls. Conversely, THEMIS2 overexpression induced reciprocal expression patterns (Fig.  B; Supplementary Fig. 2A). Immunohistochemical analyses of xenograft tissues confirmed conserved regulation of these EMT biomarkers in vivo (Supplementary Fig. 2B).
 The mechanical forces driving tumor cell metastasis originated from dynamic cytoskeletal remodeling processes, which are tightly coordinated by cytoskeletal-associated proteins interacting with diverse signaling cascades. As a principal structural element of the cytoskeletal framework, actin constitutes the predominant protein component in eukaryotic cellular systems. The dynamic equilibrium between monomeric G-actin (globular actin) and polymeric F-actin (filamentous actin) establishes distinct structural domains including cortical networks, stress fibers, and protrusive apparatuses. Notably, actin remodeling dynamics participate in the complete metastatic cascade, with F-actin exhibiting mechanosensitive properties that orchestrate cellular adhesion, directional migration, and metastatic dissemination. To investigate THEMIS2’s regulatory role in cytoskeletal organization, we performed phalloidin-based fluorescence imaging using AlexaFluor488 conjugates. In control cells, the F-actin was clear and expressed in the cytoplasm with regular arrangement. However, following THEMIS2 deletion, became unclear, inordinate and fragmented, suggesting that THEMIS2 loss facilitated F-actin collapse (Fig.  C). Consistent with these morphological alterations, THEMIS2 knockdown significantly attenuated membrane protrusion formation (lamellipodia/filopodia), effects that were conversely augmented by THEMIS2 overexpression (arrows in Fig.  C). Compared to the control, THEMIS2 knockdown reduced the F-actin/G-actin ratio (Fig.  D), indicating that THEMIS2 favors F-actin assembly. Given the critical role of depolymerization regulators, we focused on the actin depolymerizing factor (ADF)/cofilin family. Emerging evidence implicates dysregulation of cofilin pathway components in tumor invasiveness, with enhanced cofilin activity being functionally linked to invasive protrusion formation [ ]. Notably, our results revealed elevated cofilin activity in THEMIS2-overexpressing OC cells (Fig.  E), providing mechanistic insight into their augmented migratory capacity.

## THEMIS2-induced activation of Rap1 enhances the migration and invasion of OC cells
To elucidate the molecular basis of THEMIS2-driven OC metastasis, comparative transcriptomic profiling between THEMIS2-silenced OC cells and controls was performed (Fig.  A), followed by KEGG pathway annotation. Bioinformatic interrogation revealed predominant enrichment in pathway in cancer, the Hippo signaling pathway and the Rap1 signaling pathway (Fig.  B). Notably, small GTPase superfamily members-established modulators of actin cytoskeletal dynamics and EMT-emerged as key molecular determinants [ , ]. Functional validation through pulldown assays demonstrated marked attenuation of Rap1 activation status (Rap1-GTP levels) in THEMIS2-silenced OC cells, with no concomitant changes in total Rap1 expression (Fig.  C), implicating THEMIS2 in Rap1 regulation. Pharmacological perturbation using the Rap1-specific inhibitor GGTI298 recapitulated the metastatic phenotype, partially rescuing the pro-migratory and invasive capacities of THEMIS2-overexpressing OC cells (Fig.  D, E and F). Intriguingly, GGTI298-induced hyperphosphorylation of cofilin (elevated p-cofilin), mirroring the cytoskeletal destabilization phenotype observed in THEMIS2-silenced cells (Fig.  G and H).
 

## THEMIS2 promotes Rap1 signaling by reinforcing the interaction of DOCK4 with CRKII
To further determine the mechanism by which THEMIS2 governs Rap1 signaling in OC cells, we utilized immunoprecipitation coupled with mass spectrometry (IP-MS) to scrutinize the THEMIS2 interactome (Fig.  A). following computational subtraction of nonspecific IgG-associated proteins, 712 proteins that are putative THEMIS2 interacting proteins were identified (Supplementary Table 1), including multiple GEFs such as DOCK4, ARHGEF17, ARHGEF2, and ARHGEF1. Of these, only DOCK4-a documented Rap1 activator [ , ] was prioritized for mechanistic validation (Fig.  B). IF staining substantiated the colocalization of THEMIS2 and DOCK4 within OC cells (Fig.  C), with reciprocal co-IP assays establishing biochemical evidence for their endogenous interaction (Fig.  D), providing evidence that DOCK4 may serve as a downstream target of THEMIS2 in OC cells. However, ectopic THEMIS2 expression did not noticeably alter the expression or subcellular localization of DOCK4 (Supplementary Fig. 3). Notably, Vijay Yajnik and colleagues demonstrated through GST pull-down assays that DOCK4 interacts with the N-terminal SH3 domain of the adaptor protein CRKII, subsequently activating small G protein Rap1 by promoting GDP-to-GTP exchange [ ]. Given the dominative roles of CRKII in Rap1 signaling pathway, we speculated that THEMIS2 silencing impairs DOCK4-CRKII complex integrity in OC cells. Endogenous immunoprecipitation assays substantiated this premise, demonstrating THEMIS2 knockdown significantly attenuated DOCK4-CRKII physical association (Fig.  E). Functional interrogation via DOCK4 siRNA-mediated silencing in THEMIS2-overexpressing OC cells revealed Rap1 activation dependency on DOCK4 (Fig.  F and G), concomitant with abrogation of THEMIS2-driven migratory and invasive phenotypes (Fig.  H and I; Supplementary Fig. 4). Collectively, these data establish THEMIS2 as a critical molecular regulator orchestrating DOCK4-CRKII complex stability, thereby perturbing downstream Rap1 signaling pathway.
 

## THEMIS2 facilitates DOCK4-CRKII complex formation through TBK1-mediated binding to DOCK and subsequent phosphorylation at Ser1787
To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying THEMIS2-mediated regulation of DOCK4-CRKII complex formation, we systematically investigated its impact on DOCK4 phosphorylation status. Endogenous DOCK4 was immunoprecipitated from Hey and OVCAR8 cells with THEMIS2 knockdown or overexpression, followed by immunoblotting using a phospho-serine/threonine-specific antibody. The results show that THEMIS2 knockdown significantly reduced DOCK4 phosphorylation levels, whereas THEMIS2 overexpression markedly enhanced this modification (Fig.  A). To identify kinases associated with THEMIS2 that potentially mediate DOCK4 phosphorylation, we screened putative THEMIS2-interacting proteins and identified five candidate kinases: TBK1 (Fig.  B), LMTK2, OXSR1, PLK1, and RIOK2. Subsequent co-immunoprecipitation assays confirmed a specific physical interaction between THEMIS2 and TBK1 in OC cells (Fig.  C). To further validate the interaction between DOCK4 and TBK1, we examined their binding affinity under conditions of THEMIS2 modulation. Co-immunoprecipitation analysis revealed that THEMIS2 overexpression significantly enhanced the DOCK4-TBK1 interaction, whereas THEMIS2 knockdown markedly reduced their binding affinity compared to control (Fig.  D). These findings suggest that THEMIS2 functions as a molecular scaffold facilitating the physical interaction between TBK1 and DOCK4. Moreover, pharmacological inhibition of TBK1 using the specific inhibitor GSK8612 effectively attenuated both DOCK4 phosphorylation (Fig.  E) and its subsequent association with CRKII (Fig.  F). Bioinformatic analysis using PhosphoSitePlus database and the Group-based Prediction System (GPS) 6.0 algorithm identified serine 1787 of DOCK4 as a potential phosphorylation site mediated by TBK1 (Fig.  G, H). Strikingly, GSK8612 significantly inhibits serine/threonine phosphorylation of DOCK4WT but shows no observable inhibition of DOCK4S1787A serine/threonine phosphorylation in OC cells, suggesting TBK1 is essential for DOCK4 phosphorylation at S1787(Fig.  I). Functional characterization of phosphor-mutants revealed that the phosphor-defective S1787A mutant abrogated Rap1 signaling activation and CRKII binding capacity, whereas the phospho-mimetic S1787E mutant exhibited enhanced biological activity compared to wild-type DOCK4 (Fig.  J-K).
 

## Rap1 Inhibition diminishes the facilitative impact of THEMIS2 on OC metastasis in vivo
To substantiate THEMIS2’s pro-metastatic function in vivo, isogenic hey cells (vector control vs. THEMIS2-overexpressing) were intraperitoneally engrafted into nude mice (Fig.  A). Total luminescence flux derived from bioluminescence imaging was used to evaluate orthotopic tumor growth. Mice with injected THEMIS2-overexpressing cells showed a significant increase in tumor colonization, and, whereas pharmacological GGTI298 significantly attenuated THEMIS2-driven metastasis compared to vehicle controls (Fig.  B, C). These findings collectively establish Rap1 targeting as a viable therapeutic strategy to counteract THEMIS2-mediated OC progression. Meanwhile, body weight of mice was measured every week, with no significant difference observed between the four groups (Fig.  D). As indicated in Fig.  E, the volume of metastatic implants along the small bowel surface and liver was larger in mice injected with THEMIS2-overexpressing cells. Counting the number of mice with intestinal, liver and diaphragmatic metastases (Fig.  F), we found that THEMIS2 plays a key role in the homing of metastatic tumors to organs. Symptom relief was also observed in mice following GGTI298 treatment. Metastatic tissues in the intestine, liver and diaphragm of THEMIS2-OE mice were identified by hematoxylin and eosin staining (Fig.  G). IHC analysis unveiled THEMIS2-induced EMT reprogramming, characterized by downregulation of E-cadherin and upregulation of vimentin, MMP2, and MMP9 (Fig.  H). Notably, GGTI298 administration partially restored epithelial differentiation markers, suggesting therapeutic reversibility of THEMIS2-associated mesenchymal transition (Fig.  H). Collectively, this evidence positions Rap1 inhibition as a promising modality for OC metastasis interception.
 

## Discussion
Metastasis development involves a series of complex biological processes, including the detachment of tumor cells from their primary site, acquisition of migratory properties, invasion through the basement membrane and surrounding tissues, and eventual entry into the vasculature [ ]. In this study, we identify THEMIS2 as a critical intrinsic regulator of metastatic capacity in OC, the most lethal gynecological malignancy characterized by widespread metastatic dissemination. Our findings show that genetic ablation of THEMIS2 dramatically impairs the invasiveness and migratory potential of OC cell lines in vitro, while also markedly inhibiting abdominal cavity implantation and metastasis in vivo. Mechanistically, THEMIS2 operates within a sophisticated signaling network to modulate EMT as well as intracellular actin dynamics, thereby enhancing tumor cell metastatic capacity. Specifically, THEMIS2 sustains Rap1 activity in metastatic tumor cells by stabilizing the DOCK4-CRKII complex, which facilitates the GDP-GTP exchange of Rap1. This is achieved through THEMIS2-mediated recruitment of TBK1 to DOCK4, inducing phosphorylation at serine 1787 (S1787) and enhancing DOCK4-CRKII binding (Fig.  ). Consequently, THEMIS2 emerges as a pivotal positive regulator of DOCK4-mediated Rap1 activation, which not only promoting EMT but also suppresses cofilin phosphorylation. The reduction in cofilin phosphorylation prevents actin depolymerization and stress fiber disassembly, thereby maintaining the cytoskeletal integrity necessary for tumor cell motility and metastatic progression in OC.
 THEMIS2 has been demonstrated as an oncogenic marker across multiple malignancies. Initially characterized in the human endometrial tumor cell line HEC1B, its expression was shown to be progressively upregulated in response to basement membrane exposure [ ]. Subsequent investigations revealed that a 3.5-fold increase in THEMIS2 levels in endometrial cancer tissue compared to normal endometrium, with transcript abundance positively correlating with HER2 expression [ ]. In clear cell carcinoma, immunohistochemical analyses further demonstrated pronounced THEMIS2 overexpression relative to normal ovarian tissues, suggesting its potential pathogenic role in this aggressive subtype [ ]. Despite these associations, the mechanistic underpinnings of THEMIS2 in tumorigenesis remain poorly elucidated. Current literature on THEMIS2’s functional contributions to cancer is sparse. For instance, in breast cancer models, THEMIS2 was reported to stabilize MET receptor activation by competitively inhibiting PTP1B-mediated dephosphorylation of p-MET, thereby promoting cancer stemness and chemoresistance [ ]. In contrast to these prior observations, our study delineates a previously unrecognized mechanism: THEMIS2 orchestrates metastatic progression in OC by recruiting TBK1 to phosphorylate DOCK4 at serine 1787. This posttranslational modification facilitates CRKII binding and subsequent Rap1 activation, which collectively drive EMT and F-actin polymerization in metastatic OC cells. Regarding the structural basis of THEMIS2 recruiting TBK1 to DOCK4, THEMIS1 (paralog of THEMIS2) emerges. THEMIS2 and THEMIS1 demonstrate dual conservation in tertiary architecture and compensatory functional profiles. Functional reconstitution studies in Themis1(-/-) murine systems confirmed their indistinguishable functional equivalence in restoring thymocyte differentiation and TCR signaling cascades; Biochemical profiling revealed both isoforms exhibit analogous tyrosine phosphorylation dynamics and co-localize within LAT-associated Grb2 signalosomes post-TCR stimulation [ ].Structural characterization further showed THEMIS1 engages in constitutive binding with the scaffolding protein Grb2 through polyproline-mediated docking to its C-terminal SH3 structural module [ ]. Notably, the docking protein DOCK4 harbors an N-terminal SH3 domain, a structurally conserved region critical for intermolecular recognition [ ]. Based on these structural insights, we propose a mechanistic paradigm wherein THEMIS2 orchestrates DOCK4 engagement through its polyproline motif, whereas the CABIT domains execute TBK1 recruitment via coordinated interface utilization.
The activity of Rap1 GTPase is dynamically regulated by Rap GEFs and GTPase activating proteins (GAPs), which govern its cycling between active and inactive states. Aberrant Rap1 signaling driven by its pleiotropic roles in diverse cellular processes, has been implicated in multiple pathological conditions. In OC, elevated Rap1-GTP levels are observed in tumor tissues [ ], where Rap1 activation promotes metastasis through EMT induction via ERK/p38 and Notch signaling [ ]. Beyond gynecological malignancies, Rap1 enhances NF-κB activity and sustains BCL-2 expression to confer cisplatin resistance in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), while depletion of Rap1-GTP sensitizes NSCLC cells to chemotherapy [ ]. Additionally, Rap1 coordinates cytoskeletal remodeling through the cofilin pathway to regulate MTOC polarization, in B cells and Jurkat cells [ , ]. Notably, RhoA signaling antagonizes this process by phosphorylating cofilin into its inactive form [ ], whereas RhoA inhibition reduces p-cofilin levels and restores cytoskeletal plasticity [ ]. Rap1 could suppresse the RhoA-Rho-associated kinase (ROCK)-LIM-kinase (LIMK)-cofilin axis to facilitate cell migration in hepatic stellate cells [ ]. Inhibition of Rap1 signaling disrupted tumor cell adhesion dynamics and impaired their ability to traverse endothelial cell monolayers [ ]. Consistent with these findings, our unpublished data reveal that THEMIS2 potentiates Rap1 signaling, concomitant with elevated FAK phosphorylation and enhanced cell adhesion. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of Rap1 using GGTI298 attenuated THEMIS2-driven migratory and invasive capacities of OC cells in vitro and suppressed metastatic dissemination in vivo. Our preliminary unpublished data that Rap1 inhibitor GGTI-298 exhibited more potent anti-migratory and anti-invasive effects in THEMIS2-high OC cells compared to THEMIS2-low cells, suggesting the potential therapeutic value of targeting the THEMIS2/Rap1 axis in THEMIS2-overexpressing tumors. However, prolonged Rap1 blockade may trigger compensatory pathway activation-a limitation that necessitates further investigation. To address this, future studies will employ transcriptomic profiling to identify adaptive molecular mechanisms and evaluate combinatorial therapies targeting both Rap1 and its bypass pathways for synergistic anti-metastatic efficacy.
Rap1 activation is governed by GEFs, with well-characterized members including C3G [ ], Epac [ , ], RasGRPs [ ], PDZ-GEFs [ ], and DOCK4 [ ]. Adaptor proteins such as Crk (CRKII), CrkL, and Grb2 are known to modulate Rap1 signaling. For instance, co-expressing these adaptors with C3G in Cos1 cells significantly elevates the GTP/GDP binding ratio of Rap1 [ ]. Studies demonstrate that disrupting the CRKII-DOCK4 interaction via the DOCK4 mutation impairs Rap1 activation [ ], consistent with our observation that THEMIS2 knockdown compromises TBK1 recruitment to DOCK4, thereby preventing its phosphorylation and subsequent CRKII binding, ultimately abrogating Rap1 activation. Rap1 is exceptionally versatile and crucially important as a signal. Further investigation into Rap1 signaling mechanisms is recommended.
