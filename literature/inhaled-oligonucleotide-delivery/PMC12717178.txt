## Abstract
Asthma is a complex disease characterized by chronic airway inflammation, and obesity can exacerbate its pathological process and reduce the efficacy of conventional treatment. In this study, we constructed an obese asthma mouse model by combining a high-fat diet with ovalbumin (OVA) to investigate the effects and mechanisms of metformin on airway inflammation in obese asthma mice. ELISA was used to detect serum IL-1β, IL-18 and leptin levels, HE staining was used to assess the pathological changes in lung tissue, and Western blot and QRT-PCR were used to analyze the expression of proteins and mRNAs related to the AMPK/NLRP3 signaling pathway. The results demonstrated that the obesity-associated asthma group exhibited significantly higher airway inflammation scores and proportions of leukocyte subtypes (particularly eosinophils) in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) compared to the normal asthma group (P < 0.05). Additionally, pulmonary expression of p-AMPK was downregulated, while levels of inflammatory mediators including NLRP3, Caspase-1, as well as IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin, were markedly elevated.Following metformin intervention, the aforementioned inflammatory parameters were significantly ameliorated: p-AMPK expression was upregulated, NLRP3 inflammasome activation was suppressed, and levels of IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin were reduced (P < 0.05). In conclusion, mechanistic investigations indicate that metformin treatment leads to increased AMPK phosphorylation, reduced expression of NLRP3/Caspase-1, and decreased release of downstream inflammatory cytokines. These findings suggest a potential regulatory relationship between metformin intervention and the activation of AMPK coupled with inhibition of the NLRP3/Caspase-1 pathway. Collectively, the results support the promising role of metformin in alleviating obesity-related asthma via modulation of the AMPK/NLRP3 signaling axis.

## Introduction
Asthma and obesity represent major global public health challenges, with epidemiological data indicating rising prevalence rates worldwide , . The 2014 Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) guidelines for the first time classified obesity-associated asthma as a distinct phenotype , characterized by heightened airway hyperresponsiveness, poor response to corticosteroid therapy, and unfavorable prognosis. Obesity not only significantly increases asthma risk but also exacerbates symptoms, increases exacerbation frequency, and substantially elevates disease severity and treatment difficulty. Consequently, discovering novel therapeutic approaches for obesity-associated asthma is urgently needed.
Obesity-related asthma is fundamentally characterized by the interplay between metabolic dysregulation and chronic inflammation. The AMPK/NLRP3 signaling axis has been identified as a critical molecular bridge linking these two core pathological mechanisms. AMPK, a central hub in cellular energy metabolism, is structurally composed of one catalytic subunit α and two regulatory subunits, β and γ. Its activation depends on cellular energy status; an increased AMP/ATP ratio indirectly activates AMPK via upstream kinases LKB1 or CaMKKβ, or AMP directly binds the γ subunit causing allosteric activation . Research links AMPK dysregulation to various human diseases. In type 2 diabetes, AMPK activation promotes glucose uptake in skeletal muscle, exerting beneficial effects . In cancer, activated AMPK induces cell cycle arrest, impairs proliferation, and inhibits growth . In atherosclerosis, AMPK activation, by upregulating the expression of cholesterol efflux transporters in foam cells and promoting cholesterol efflux, prevents cholesterol accumulation, attenuates inflammatory cytokine release from macrophages, and corrects vascular dysfunction . In pulmonary diseases, AMPK activation suppresses or attenuates oxidative stress and inflammation, exerting anti-inflammatory effects crucial for halting disease progression.
However, in obese individuals, AMPK activity is often suppressed due to mitochondrial dysfunction and elevated leptin levels, which not only directly impairs lipid metabolic homeostasis but also relieves the inhibition of downstream inflammatory signaling pathways, thereby exacerbating inflammatory responses . In contrast, As a core multiprotein complex mediating inflammatory reactions, the NLRP3 inflammasome primarily regulates Caspase-1 activation and the maturation and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-18, and is hyperactivated in both obesity and asthma . In recent years, numerous studies have revealed a direct regulatory relationship between AMPK and NLRP3 , . Although targeting the AMPK/NLRP3 pathway has been shown to effectively alleviate airway inflammation in the classic ovalbumin (OVA)-induced asthma model , its specific role and regulatory mechanisms in obesity-related asthma remain unclear.
Metformin, primarily an oral anti-diabetic drug for type 2 diabetes, has garnered substantial evidence in recent years for its efficacy in asthma via multiple pathways . Firstly, it inhibits airway inflammation and reduces inflammatory cell infiltration . Secondly, it may ameliorate asthma symptoms by modulating immune responses . As a classical activator of AMPK, the anti-inflammatory effects of metformin in asthma have already been validated . Therefore, we hypothesize that metformin may activate AMPK, thereby regulating NLRP3 inflammasome activity, and ultimately ameliorate airway inflammation and pathological damage in obesity-associated asthma.
Based on the above, this study employed metformin intervention in an obesity-associated asthmatic mouse model. By examining pulmonary inflammation status, signaling pathway protein alterations, and inflammatory cytokine changes across experimental groups, we explored the role of the AMPK/NLRP3 signaling pathway in obesity-associated asthma, providing a scientific basis for identifying novel therapeutic targets.

## Materials and methods

## Materials
The following antibodies were used in this study: AMPK (A00994-6; diluted at 1:1000), NLRP3 (BA3677; diluted at 1:1000), and Caspase-1 (BA2220; diluted at 1:1000), as well as goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP conjugated secondary antibody (BA1054; diluted at 1:5000), all of which were purchased from Boster Biological Technology (China). The antibody against p-AMPK (AF3423; diluted at 1:1000) was obtained from Affinity Biosciences. GAPDH (db106; diluted at 1:5000) was acquired from DEGE (China).

## Laboratory animals
Thirty SPF-grade healthy c57BL/6 mice, weighing 18–20 g, 4 weeks old, were purchased from Spivey (Beijing) Biotechnology Co. Ltd, License No.: SCXK (Beijing) 2019–0010, with the room temperature maintained at 20–25 °C and the indoor humidity at 40%-70%, and were fed with either a high-fat (45% fat-feeding) or a normal chow (15% fat-feeding) diet Feeding. Euthanasia was carried out by intravenous injection of a dose of 150 mg/kg sodium pentobarbital after isoflurane anesthesia. Death was confirmed when vital signs ceased for more than 5 min. The animal experiments in this study were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Henan University of Chinese Medicine (Approval No. DWLL202203013). All procedures strictly adhered to relevant Chinese national regulations on laboratory animal welfare and ethics, and complied with the ARRIVE guidelines. Every effort was undertaken to minimize animal suffering and reduce the number of animals utilized.

## Grouping of animals
Thirty C57BL/6J mice were acclimatized and fed for one week before they were divided into five groups, namely, control group, obese group, asthma group, obese asthma group, and intervention group, with six mice , in each group, according to the randomized numeric table method.

## Obesity-related asthmatic models
The control group and the asthma group were fed with normal chow for 12 weeks, and the obese group, the obese asthma group and the intervention group were fed with high-fat chow for 12 weeks, and the average body weight exceeded 20% of the normal group, which was regarded as the successful modeling of the obesity model; the asthma model was modeled by using OVA, Then, they were sensitized by intraperitoneal injection of 0.2 ml (20 µg OVA with 0.2 ml AL(OH)3) on days 0, 7, and 14, and challenged with 1% OVA inhalation for 30 min from day 21, 3 times per week for 8 weeks. The remaining mice in the normal and obese groups were sensitized and challenged with 0.9% saline; 200 mg/kg of metformin was given by gavage to the intervention group half an hour before nebulization for 6 weeks.

## Collection of samples
Within 24 h of the last nebulization, blood was collected quickly from anesthetized mice by removing the eyeballs and placed in 1.5mL EP tubes, which were allowed to stand at room temperature for 4 h, then centrifuged in a centrifuge at 4000r/min for 10 min to obtain the supernatant, and stored in a refrigerator at -80℃. Mice were anesthetized with 5% isoflurane via face mask inhalation. Upon confirmation of loss of corneal reflex, orbital blood sampling was performed. Following blood collection, euthanasia was administered through intravenous injection of pentobarbital sodium solution (150 mg/kg). Cessation of heartbeat was verified, and death was confirmed by cervical dislocation. After blood collection, the lungs were rinsed 3 times with 1mL PBS, and BALF was collected to ensure the recovery rate was more than 80%, then the collected alveolar lavage fluid was put into a 1.5mL EP tube, and the alveolar lavage fluid was collected for measurement. The right lung was separated, and the right lung tissue was soaked in formalin-filled EP tubes for 48 h, then paraffin-embedded, and then sliced into thin slices with a thickness of 3–5 μm. The left lung tissue was placed in another EP tube for subsequent testing.

## Alveolar lavage fluid leukocyte counts and classification counts
The collected alveolar lavage fluid was centrifuged at 1500r/min for 10 min at 4℃, the cell precipitate after centrifugation was collected and resuspended with PBS, 10ul of the resuspension was taken for leukocyte counting, and then the rest was taken for smear, left to dry and then stained with Rachel Giemsa staining, and then after drying, cell counting was carried out, and the percentages of eosinophils and neutrophils were counted respectively.

## Lung histopathology
The left lung tissue was fixed in formalin, embedded in regular paraffin, and sliced at a thickness of 3–5 μm. HE staining was to observe the inflammatory cell infiltration in the airway, and two persons who did not know the experimental grouping scored the scores as follows: ①0: no inflammatory cell infiltration; ②1: little inflammatory cell infiltration; ③2: more inflammatory cell infiltration; ④3: occasional inflammatory cell infiltration in clusters; ⑤4: large number of clusters in clusters: A large number of aggregated clusters of inflammatory cell infiltration.

## Western blot
Protein extraction and Western blot analysis were performed according to methods outlined in previous studies . Total protein was extracted from lung tissues using RIPA lysis buffer (Jingcai, China, JC-PL001) containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using the BCA assay (Beyotime, China, P0012). Subsequently, proteins were separated by SDS–PAGE (Jingcai, China, TC-PE022/R) and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight, followed by incubation with secondary antibodies at room temperature. After washing, the membranes were visualized using a fluorescence scanning system. Image acquisition was performed with appropriate parameter settings, and optimal signal intensity was achieved by adjusting brightness, contrast, and coloration strength prior to final output.

## ELISA
The ELISA was performed according to methods described in previous studies . Mouse serum samples were collected, and assays were carried out following the instructions of the respective ELISA kits (Jianglai Bio, China: Leptin, JL11317; IL-1β, JL20253; IL-18, JL18442). Briefly, 100 µL of standard or serum sample was added to each well, with blank wells receiving 100 µL of universal diluent. After sequential incubation with biotinylated antibody working solution and enzyme conjugate working solution, substrate solution was added and incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 15 min. The reaction was terminated by adding stop solution. Absorbance (OD value) was measured at 450 nm. A standard curve was plotted using standard concentrations as the horizontal axis and OD values as the vertical axis, and the concentrations of leptin, IL-1β, and IL-18 in the samples were calculated.

## QRT-PCR
QRT-PCR was conducted as outlined in previous reports . Total RNA was extracted from mouse lung tissues using Trizol reagent, followed by purification with a rapid RNA extraction kit (Thermo, 15596026) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Servicebio, G3330-100). A mixture containing 1 µL of cDNA, 0.8 µL of specific primers, and 10 µL of Green qPCR Master Mix was thoroughly combined, and qPCR was performed using a fluorescence quantitative PCR system (TELSTAR BIO-II-A). GAPDH was used as the internal reference to calculate the relative expression levels of the target genes AMPK, NLRP3, and Caspase-1. The primer sequences are listed in (Table ).
 

## Statistical analysis
The software SPSS25.0 was used for statistical analysis of the data, which were expressed as mean ± standard deviation ( ± s), and if the data conformed to the normal distribution, one-way ANOVA was used to compare the differences between the groups, and the LSD method was used for a chi-square, while Dunnett’s method was used for a non-chi-square, and if it did not conform to the normal distribution, the rank-sum test was used, with P < 0.05 as statistically significant.

## Results

## Changes in body weight of mice
The body weights of the mice fed through high-fat chow were significantly higher than that of the normal chow-fed (P < 0.05). Compared with the control group and the asthma group, the difference in body weight between the obese group, the obese asthma group and the intervention group was not statistically significant (P > 0.05) (Fig.  A). The weight of mice fed with high-fat chow exceeded 20% of those fed with normal chow, indicating successful obesity modeling.
 

## General conditions of mice
After nebulization, the mice in the asthma group and obese asthma group showed symptoms such as scratching their noses, irritability and sneezing, and the mice in the obese asthma group subsequently showed symptoms such as urinary incontinence and nodding respiration; the above manifestations in the metformin intervention group were lighter than those in the asthma group and obese asthma group; and the mice in the control group and the obese group did not show the above symptoms.

## Alveolar lavage fluid leukocyte count and classification counts
The total number of leukocytes and percentage of neutrophils in mice in the obese group were elevated compared with mice in the control group, P < 0.05; the total number of leukocytes, percentage of eosinophils, and percentage of neutrophils in mice in the asthma group and obese asthma group were elevated compared with mice in the control group, P < 0.05; the total number of leukocytes, percentage of eosinophils, and percentage of neutrophils in mice in the obese asthma group were significantly higher compared with mice in the obese and asthma groups, P < 0.05; total leukocyte count, percentage of eosinophils, and percentage of neutrophils were significantly lower in mice in the intervention group than in the obese asthma group, P < 0.05. (Table  ).
 

## Histopathologic changes in the lungs of mice
Control mice did not see obvious inflammatory cell infiltration, no thickening of bronchial wall and bronchial smooth muscle, and no collagen fiber deposition; mice in the asthma group showed obvious inflammatory cell infiltration, thickening of bronchial wall and smooth muscle, and collagen fiber deposition; the above manifestations in the obesity group were slight compared with those in the asthma group; the above changes in the obesity-asthma group were significantly aggravated compared with those in the asthma group and obesity group; and the above changes in the metformin group were significantly attenuated compared with those in the obesity-asthma group. The above changes in the metformin group were significantly less severe than those in the obese asthma group. (Fig.  B–D).

## Expression of AMPK, p-AMPK, NLRRP3 and Caepase-1 in mouse lung tissue
The lung tissue p-AMPK expression of mice in the obese group, asthma group, and obese asthma group were all lower than that of the control group, and the expression of NLRP3 and Caspase-1 were all higher than that of the control group, P < 0.05; in the obese asthma group, the expression of p-AMPK was lower than that of the obese and asthma groups, and the expression of NLRP3 and Caspase-1 was higher than that of the obese and asthma groups, P < 0.05; in the intervention group, the mice lung tissue p-AMPK expression was significantly higher than that in the obese asthma group, and NLRP3 and Caspase-1 expression was significantly lower than that in the obese asthma group, P < 0.05. (Fig.  A,B).
 

## Serum IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin levels in mice
The serum levels of IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin in mice in the obese group, asthma group, and obese asthma group were all elevated compared with those in the control group, P < 0.05; the levels of IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin in mice in the obese asthma group were significantly higher than those in the obese group and asthma group, P < 0.05; and the levels of IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin in mice in the intervention group were significantly lower than those in the obese asthma group, P < 0.05 (Fig. A).
 

## AMPK, NLRP3, Caspase-1 mRNA levels in mouse lung tissue
The mRNA expression of AMPK in the lung tissues of mice in the obese, asthma, and obese asthma groups was lower than that in the control group, while the mRNA expression of NLRP3 and Caspase-1 was higher than that in the control group, P < 0.05. In the obese asthma group, the expression of AMPK mRNA was lower than that in both the obese and asthma groups, and the expressions of NLRP3 and Caspase-1 mRNA were higher than those in both the obese and asthma groups, P < 0.05. In the intervention group, the mRNA expression of AMPK was significantly higher than that in the obese asthma group, whereas the mRNA expressions of NLRP3 and Caspase-1 were significantly lower than that in the obese asthma group, P < 0.05 (Fig. B).

## Discussion
This study successfully established a murine model of obesity-associated asthma by combining high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity with ovalbumin (OVA)-induced asthma. We investigated the effects of the AMPK activator metformin and explored the role and mechanism of the AMPK/NLRP3 signaling pathway. Our findings demonstrate that metformin intervention activates the AMPK signaling pathway, inhibits the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and its downstream effector Caspase-1, consequently reduces levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-18, and leptin, and ameliorates airway inflammation in obesity-associated asthmatic mice.
Obesity represents a state of low-grade chronic inflammation associated with alterations in gut microbiota, cellular metabolism, lipid handling, immune function, insulin resistance, and adipokine production by adipose tissue . Two primary hypotheses link obesity and asthma: one involves mechanical effects such as diaphragmatic displacement due to fat deposition and reduced chest wall compliance , while the other emphasizes the role of adipose tissue-derived immunomodulatory and inflammatory adipokines, such as leptin and adiponectin . Leptin, a key pro-inflammatory adipokine, exacerbates airway pathology through multiple immunoregulatory mechanisms. Studies indicate leptin promotes CD4 + T cell differentiation towards Th1/Th17 phenotypes via the JAK2-STAT3 pathway, upregulates pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, and simultaneously suppresses Th2 cytokines and regulatory T cell (Treg) function. This results in increased Th1 cell infiltration and decreased Treg proportion in the lung tissue of obese asthmatic mice, aggravating airway inflammation . Wang et al. reported that leptin significantly upregulates JAK2-STAT3 phosphorylation in obese asthmatic mice, inducing M1 macrophage polarization and promoting the expression of M1 markers (iNOS, IL-6, TNF-α), while suppressing M2 markers (Arg-1). This polarization shift showed a significant positive correlation with airway wall collagen deposition scores, directly linking it to airway remodeling and suggesting its involvement in structural airway alterations . Marek et al. demonstrated that leptin facilitates NLRP3 inflammasome assembly by upregulating TXNIP, enhancing Caspase-1 activation and IL-1β maturation/release. This process was effectively blocked by TXNIP-specific inhibitors or siRNA, resulting in significantly reduced NLRP3 assembly and markedly decreased IL-1βsecretion . Furthermore, leptin induces reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation by activating oxidative stress pathways, establishing a “leptin–oxidative stress–inflammasome” positive feedback loop . Thus, leptin acts as a core driver of metabolic-immune imbalance in obesity-associated asthma by regulating multiple pathological mechanisms, including T-cell polarization, macrophage activation, inflammasome triggering, and oxidative stress. Targeting leptin represents a promising therapeutic strategy for developing novel interventions for this asthma phenotype.

AMPK, a pivotal hub integrating energy metabolism and inflammation regulation, plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of obesity-associated asthma. Suppression of its activity exacerbates disease progression. Xia et al. revealed that reduced AMPK phosphorylation in obesity stems from an imbalance in the AMP/ATP ratio caused by HFD-induced mitochondrial dysfunction, directly inhibiting AMPK activation . Research has confirmed that in the obese state, elevated leptin levels coincide with decreased receptor sensitivity . This leads to sustained JAK2 phosphorylation and consequent STAT3 activation. Phosphorylated STAT3 directly binds to the promoter region of the LKB1 gene, suppressing its transcriptional expression. As LKB1 is the upstream kinase of AMPK, its impaired activity directly blocks phosphorylation of the AMPKαsubunit at Thr172, resulting in reduced AMPK activity . Conversely, AMPK activation significantly improves obesity-associated asthma. Zhu et al., in studies using an obese asthmatic mouse model, demonstrated that AMPK activation attenuates airway inflammation by inhibiting the NF-κB pathway. This inhibition reduces the expression of pro-inflammatory factors (such as TNF-α and iNOS) and the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, thereby suppressing inflammatory cell recruitment and apoptosis resistance. Furthermore, AMPK activation alleviates the oxidative microenvironment in obese asthmatic mice. It achieves this by inhibiting iNOS-mediated nitric oxide (NO) production, reducing levels of the oxidative stressmarker 8-hydroxy-2’-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), and enhancing total antioxidant capacity, thus mitigating oxidative damage to the airway and vascular endothelium . Lei et al. demonstrated that AMPK activation, via the JAK2/STAT3 pathway, blocks transcription of M1 macrophage polarization-related genes while promoting M2 macrophage marker expression, reducing the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β). Ultimately, these effects reduce airway inflammatory cell infiltration, goblet cell hyperplasia, and collagen fiber deposition, leading to improved airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) and attenuated airway remodeling in obese asthmatic mice . Furthermore, AMPK activation promotes Nrf2 nuclear translocation and upregulates HO-1 expression, concurrently inhibits the NF-κB pathway by reducing p-IκBα and p65 nuclear translocation, decreases pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β), enhances antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD, GSH, CAT), and lowers MDA and ROS levels, thereby ameliorating airway inflammation and remodeling in asthmatic mice . Collectively, these mechanisms establish AMPK as a central nexus connecting energy metabolism and inflammation, highlighting its importance as a therapeutic target for obesity-associated asthma.
Concurrent inhibition of AMPK signaling and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome drive the inflammatory cascade in obesity-associated asthma. Excessive NLRP3 inflammasome activation leads to caspase-1 cleavage/activation and the maturation/release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-189. Substantial evidence indicates that NLRP3 inflammasome activation can be inhibited by targeting key upstream or downstream components. Zhang et al. demonstrated that GLCCI1 inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome activation by suppressing the PI3K pathway. This inhibition reduces the protein expression of NLRP3, ASC, and Caspase-1, along with the secretion of IL-1β and IL-18, thereby ameliorating airway inflammatory cell infiltration, goblet cell hyperplasia, and collagen deposition in asthmatic mice. Consequently, airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) and airway resistance are improved . Liu et al. discovered MUC1 binds to TLR4, impeding its interaction with MyD88, thereby inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation, downregulating NLRP3, Caspase-1, IL-1β protein expression, and alleviating airway inflammation in asthmatic mice . In our experiment, IL-1β and IL-18 levels were significantly higher in the obese asthma group compared to controls and positively correlated with airway inflammation scores. These results further confirm that the AMPK-NLRP3 pathway serves as a core hub linking dysregulation of energy metabolism to activation of the innate immune response.
Metformin, a classical AMPK activator, exerts therapeutic effects in obesity-associated asthma through multiple mechanisms. Relevant studies suggest metformin inhibits mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I, disrupting electron transport, leading to AMP and ADP accumulation and ATP depletion, thereby indirectly activating AMPK . In addition, metformin activates AMPK, resulting in mTOR phosphorylation and inactivation. This suppresses S-phase kinase-associated protein 2 (Skp2) expression, preventing Skp2-mediated degradation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1B (p27). Consequently, p27 inhibits airway smooth muscle cell proliferation, thereby suppressing airway remodeling and preventing irreversible airflow obstruction . Our study confirmed that metformin intervention significantly increased p-AMPK protein expression while decreasing NLRP3 and Caspase-1 protein levels, and downregulated serum leptin, IL-1β, and IL-18 levels in obese asthmatic mice. Histopathology revealed metformin treatment significantly reduced bronchial wall thickness, collagen deposition area, and total leukocyte count along with neutrophil/eosinophil proportions in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). This demonstrates that metformin attenuates airway inflammation and remodeling, underscoring the crucial role of AMPK activation by metformin in mitigating obesity-associated asthma.
Although this study establishes the central role of the AMPK-NLRP3 pathway in obesity-associated asthma, limitations exist. Given species differences between mice and humans, further investigation using patient samples and data is warranted. This will elucidate whether metformin confers similar protective effects in humans and inform better therapeutic strategies for obese asthmatic patients. In this study, OVA was used to induce asthma, a model widely employed in allergic asthma research due to its high reproducibility , . However, OVA is not the only agent used for asthma induction; other stimuli such as house dust mite (HDM) extract and cockroach allergens can also elicit asthmatic responses , . The HDM-induced asthma model better recapitulates the chronic features and heterogeneity of human asthma, including corticosteroid resistance. Obesity was induced in this study using a high-fat diet (HFD), which represents a standard model for studying obesity-related diseases . It should be noted, however, that Western diets are typically characterized by high sugar content, and excessive sugar intake can directly contribute to adipose tissue accumulation and metabolic inflammation . Compared to high-fat diets, high-sugar diets promote obesity through distinct mechanisms and may elicit different inflammatory responses in the airways. Therefore, future studies could employ high-sugar diet-induced obesity models to examine whether the modulatory effects of metformin on the AMPK/NLRP3 signaling pathway and airway inflammation remain consistent. Furthermore, this study demonstrates that metformin inhibits the upstream NLRP3/Caspase-1 pathway in the lungs and concurrently reduces serum IL-1β and IL-18 levels, but lacks direct measurement of these cytokines in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid or lung homogenates. Future research directly quantifying active IL-1β and IL-18 levels in the pulmonary microenvironment will provide more definitive evidence for the local anti-inflammatory effects of metformin. Finally, although the sample size in this study was sufficient to detect significant effects, future investigations employing larger cohorts (e.g., n = 8 to 10 per group) would provide even greater statistical power and reinforce the generalizability of these findings.
Airway inflammation in obesity-associated asthma results from the interplay of metabolic dysregulation, aberrant signaling pathways, and cytokine imbalance. Within this context, suppressed AMPK signaling and concomitant NLRP3 inflammasome activation represent a central pathogenic hub. Metformin, by activating AMPK and suppressing the NLRP3/Caspase-1 pathway and downstream pro-inflammatory cytokine release, significantly ameliorates airway inflammation and pathological remodeling in obesity-associated asthma. This study not only delineates the pathogenic mechanisms underlying obesity-associated asthma in greater detail but also provides crucial experimental evidence supporting the development of AMPK-targeted therapeutics for this condition.
