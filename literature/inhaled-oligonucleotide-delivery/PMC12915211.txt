## Summary
This study examines the pivotal role of M2-type macrophages in chronic pancreatitis (CP) and the existing challenges in targeted intervention. A peptide-drug conjugate (PDC) was developed for this investigation by linking the S100A9 inhibitor Tasquinimod to a peptide that selectively targets M2 macrophages. In experimental models, this conjugate demonstrated a marked capacity to alleviate pancreatic injury, inflammation, and fibrotic progression. Compared to the free drug, it showed enhanced targeting, greater efficacy, and a reduced toxicity profile without causing significant damage to vital organs. Mechanistic analysis indicated that its effects are mediated through the activation of the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor α (PPARα) signaling pathway, leading to suppressed phosphorylation of the NF-κB p65 subunit and c-Jun, which in turn inhibits M2 macrophage polarization. These results uncover a functional mechanism and provide a foundation for developing targeted immunomodulatory therapies against CP.

## Introduction
Chronic pancreatitis (CP) is a progressive fibroinflammatory disorder that often starts with repeated pancreatitis attacks, eventually resulting in widespread fibrous tissue replacement, pancreatic secretory deficiency, and long-term pain. , Globally, CP demonstrates a modest incidence rate alongside a substantially higher prevalence, with both measures continuing to rise annually on a population-wide basis. Owing to the unclear specific pathogenesis of CP, the clinical treatment of CP relies on surgery or symptomatic treatment as the primary method, which fails to restore pancreatic function or halts the process of pancreatic fibrosis. A central feature of CP progression is the dynamic behavior of macrophages, which are crucial for tissue repair and inflammation. During the progression of CP, macrophages undergo a switch from a proinflammatory phenotype (classically activated M1-like) in the early stage to a profibrotic and anti-inflammatory phenotype (alternatively activated M2-like) in the late stage, leading to an increase in the M2/M1 ratio with increasing severity of pancreatic fibrosis. The interaction between M2-like macrophages and pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs) involves the secretion of cytokines such as transforming growth factor β (TGF-β), leading to an inflammatory microenvironment that aggravates pancreatic fibrosis. Therapeutic strategies targeting the inflammatory microenvironment have become a cutting-edge area of research in a variety of diseases. Therefore, while therapeutic strategies targeting this microenvironment are a cutting-edge research area, a key biological challenge remains: how to specifically target and modulate the function of M2-like macrophages in CP. 
The protein S100A9 is an important inflammatory mediator in pancreatic diseases, primarily expressed in myeloid cells such as monocyte-macrophages. Its inhibition can significantly reduce M2-like macrophages infiltration in CP, positioning the S100A9 pathway as a potential node for intervention. Tasquinimod (Tasq) is an orally administered compound that acts as a targeted inhibitor of S100A9 by binding to it and preventing its interaction with receptors such as toll-like receptor (TLR)4 and the receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE). However, its systemic use has been limited by off-target adverse events, and its specific effects and mechanisms in CP are unexplored. Nevertheless, these limitations present an opportunity to repurpose Tasquinimod as a targeted tool for probing S100A9 biology specifically within M2-like macrophages in CP.
To address the challenge of cell-specific targeting, we turned to peptide-drug conjugate (PDC) technology. PDC offers advantages such as smaller molecular size and better tissue penetration. They function by using a peptide for targeted delivery, facilitating cellular entry and subsequent drug release. However, the strategy of using PDC to target M2-like macrophages in inflammatory diseases such as CP is still developing. , 
Building on this, we hypothesized that using an M2-targeting peptide (M2pep) to deliver Tasquinimod specifically to M2-like macrophages would create a superior investigative tool. This approach allows for more effective delineation of the role of the M2/M1 ratio within this critical cell population and its downstream effects on pancreatic fibrosis. Accordingly, we designed and synthesized an M2-like macrophage-targeting PDC, M2pep-Tasq, by coupling Tasq with M2pep. This study aimed to utilize this conjugate not merely as a therapeutic candidate but as a mechanism-exploring tool to evaluate the feasibility of targeted M2 macrophage intervention and to elucidate the associated downstream signaling pathways in models of CP.

## Results

## Design and synthesis of M2-targeting peptide-tasquinimod
Molecular docking was employed to predict the binding site of Tasq on S100A9 ( A). Analysis revealed that the quinoline ring constitutes the primary active group involved in this interaction. Within the predicted binding site, Tasq exhibits suitable steric complementarity with S100A9. Otherwise, π-π stacking and hydrogen bonding occurred between Tasq and S100A9. The naphthyl ring in Tasq formed π-H stacking with the carbon atom of Lys86. The oxygen atom on the carboxide in Tasq, regarded as a hydrogen bond acceptor, formed a hydrogen bond with the nitrogen atom of Lys113. van der Waals force interactions were formed between Tasq and the surrounding residues. Collectively, these forces contribute significantly to the overall binding energy between Tasq and the S100A9 protein.
 To enhance binding activity, structural modifications were introduced to Tasq via linker incorporation, aiming to improve its capacity to associate with targeted peptides ( B). In the synthesis of Tasq, 4-(4-hydroxy-5-methoxy-N, 1-dimethyl-2-oxo-1, 2-dihydroquinolin-e−3-carboxamido) benzoic acid combines with 1-hydroxypyrrolidine-2, 5-dione, activating its carboxyl group to increase the activity of Tasq for subsequent reactions. As shown in C, a PDC (M2pep-Tasq) was finally synthesized after a series of complex biosynthetic steps, which couples Tasq with the polypeptide YEQDPWGVKWWY, which targets M2 macrophages through the flexible connector GGGSKKK.
In addition, the SPR experimental result was analyzed using the “Biacore Insight Evaluation Software.” The fitting method selected the affinity 1:1 binding model for analysis. The binding curve and fitted curve of the M2pep-Tasq with the S100A9 protein are shown in D. The calculated affinity between the PDC drug and the S100A9 protein was determined to be 2.01 M.

## Comparative assessment of long-term toxicity profiles
Tissues from the primary organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, and gut) of CP model mice were collected and examined for pathological changes. Compared with Ctrl mice, no significant damage was observed in organs other than the pancreas in the CP group. However, following continuous administration of Tasq for four weeks, renal tissue exhibited glomerular contraction and structural alterations, indicating potential nephrotoxicity associated with prolonged exposure. Additionally, an increase in splenic immune cell numbers was noted in the treated group, suggesting immunomodulatory activity. Notably, M2pep-Tasq attenuated these organ toxicities ( A). Serum biomarkers related to hepatic and renal function were analyzed ( B and 2C). Although no significant differences in aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), or blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels were detected in mouse serum among the different treatment groups, the highest content of creatinine (CRE) was detected in the serum of CP mice treated with Tasq. This finding is consistent with the pathological results showing that long-term use of Tasq may cause damage to the kidney rather than liver damage. Compared with Tasq, long-term use of M2pep-Tasq reduced serum CRE levels in mice, suggesting that using M2pep-Tasq instead of Tasq could reduce the renal toxicity associated with long-term use of Tasq. Systemic inflammatory response was further evaluated via serum C-reactive protein (CRP) levels. The serum CRP concentrations rose in CP model mice relative to controls; Tasq treatment did not significantly alter this elevation, whereas M2pep-Tasq administration notably lowered CRP levels ( D).
 

## Differential binding to macrophage subtypes
To assess targeting specificity across macrophage subtypes, following the polarization of bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) into M1-like macrophages ( A and S1B), subsequent analysis by flow cytometry and laser confocal microscopy indicated that M2-type BMDMs exhibited significantly stronger binding signals for M2pep-Tasq compared to M1-type cells ( A and 3B). Further validation using human macrophages yielded consistent results, with M2-polarized RAW264.7 cells demonstrating a specific binding response toward M2pep-Tasq ( C and 3D).
 

## Comparative cellular uptake and tissue distribution
In an in vitro model of IL-4-induced M2-like polarization of macrophages, the fluorescence intensity of FITC-M2pep-Tasq within the cells was greater than that of FITC-Tasq after administration under the same conditions ( A). This finding was consistent with flow cytometry data, which showed a greater signal from FITC-M2pep-Tasq in M2-like macrophages, indicating enhanced cellular association relative to the unmodified counterpart ( B). Further quantitative analysis of fluorescence co-localization was performed using ImageJ. The Pearson correlation coefficient between FITC and Phalloidin signals across all cells was calculated. A weak positive correlation (r = 0.166) was found for the FITC-Tasq group. In contrast, a strong positive correlation (r = 0.937) was measured for the FITC-M2pep-Tasq group. Subsequent in vivo validation in CP mice was conducted following tail vein injection of equivalent doses. Live imaging revealed a stronger fluorescence signal from FITC-M2pep-Tasq at matched time points, suggesting improved tissue penetration ( C). Ex vivo analysis of organ fluorescence at 1-h post-administration showed distinct distribution patterns. FITC-Tasq exhibited the greatest mean fluorescence intensity in the intestine, with the pancreas coming next. Conversely, for FITC-M2pep-Tasq, the pancreas exhibited the highest intensity, followed by the small intestine. Both groups showed relatively low fluorescence in other examined organs ( D and 4E).
 

## Assessment of therapeutic effects on pancreatic injury and fibrosis
HE and Masson’s trichrome staining revealed that CP-induced oedema, bleeding, cystic cell necrosis, inflammatory cell infiltration, and fibrosis in pancreatic tissue were restored by Tasq treatment, as were significant decreases in the tissue pathological score and fibrosis-positive areas. The findings indicate that Tasq has a therapeutic impact on CP mice. Compared with those in Tasq-treated mice, pancreatic injury and fibrosis were further reduced in CP mice treated with M2pep-Tasq, indicating that M2pep-Tasq had a greater therapeutic effect on CP than Tasq ( A and 5B). These results demonstrated that Tasq had a certain ameliorative effect on pancreatic injury and tissue fibrosis induced by CP, whereas M2pep-Tasq exhibited a superior therapeutic effect on damage and fibrosis in the pancreas caused by CP.
 

## Modulation of M2-like macrophage polarization and inflammatory profiles
To assess the effect on macrophage polarization, pancreatic tissue sections were subjected to immunofluorescence co-staining for F4/80 and CD206. Analysis revealed a reduction in the number of F4/80+CD206+ macrophages following treatment with M2pep-Tasq ( A). Flow cytometric analysis of isolated pancreatic cells indicated that CP promoted a shift toward an M2-like phenotype, as reflected by a marked increase in the fraction of CD68+CD206+ macrophages. Relative to the CP group, the administration of either Tasq or M2pep-Tasq significantly decreased this proportion, with a more pronounced inhibitory effect observed with the conjugated form ( B). Further analysis of serum inflammatory mediators showed that both treatment forms significantly suppressed levels of Interleukin (IL)-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). However, only M2pep-Tasq significantly inhibited the expression of IL-2 and IL-3 compared to the CP group ( C and 6D).
 

## In vitro modulation of M2-like macrophage polarization
IL-4 stimulation polarized bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) from C57BL/6 male mice into an M2-like state, as verified by an expanded F4/80+CD206+ population and elevated mRNA levels of hallmark markers (Arg-1, Cd206, and Fizz1). ( A and 7B). Upon treatment with either Tasq or M2pep-Tasq, the mRNA levels of these markers were downregulated, indicating that both compounds attenuated M2-type polarization in primary macrophages ( C). A similar inhibitory effect was observed in RAW264.7 cells. F4-80/CD206 double staining showed a decrease in the number of double-positive macrophages after treatment ( D and 7E). Consistent with this, mRNA expression of Arg-1 and Cd206 was reduced in RAW264.7 cells following the administration of either compound, further supporting their inhibitory role in M2-like polarization ( F).
 

## Mechanistic investigation of M2 macrophage polarization inhibition via the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor α signaling pathway
RNA-Seq analysis was used to explore the mechanism of M2pep-Tasq’s inhibition of M2 macrophages polarization by comparing and screening differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the M2pep-Tasq and Tasq groups. As shown in A, there were 1525 DEGs between M2pep-Tasq and Tasq, with 980 genes upregulated and 545 genes downregulated. A Venn diagram of the DEGs between different groups is shown in . KEGG pathway analysis of these DEGs indicated that the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway was a prominently enriched pathway, suggesting its potential role in mediating the observed effects ( B). Consistent with this, heatmap visualization and qPCR validation confirmed that the expression of key PPAR pathway-related genes, including Plin5, Acox1, Acox2, Pparα, Apg7, and Pck2, was significantly higher following treatment with M2pep-Tasq compared to Tasq ( C and 8D).
 To further assess the functional impact on this pathway, protein analysis was conducted. In RAW264.7 cells, Western blotting revealed that treatment with M2pep - Tasq led to a marked downregulation of CD206 protein levels relative to Tasq treatment ( A). Additionally, an analysis of relevant protein levels in the PPAR signaling pathway of mouse pancreatic tissue revealed that, compared to Tasq, M2pep-Tasq appreciably upregulated PPARα protein expression and considerably diminished the phosphorylation levels of P65, a key protein in the NF-κB signaling pathway, and c-Jun, a critical protein involved in AP-1 transcription ( B). A dependency on PPARα activity for these effects was tested using the inhibitor MK-886. When PPARα activity was inhibited, the significant changes in P65 and c-Jun phosphorylation induced by M2pep-Tasq were abolished ( C). This result indicates that the modulation of downstream P65 and c-Jun phosphorylation is closely associated with the activation of the PPARα signaling pathway.
 

## Discussion
This research shows that a peptide-directed strategy can effectively deliver a therapeutic agent to M2-like macrophages, resulting in marked attenuation of pancreatic damage and fibrosis in CP. This effect is mechanistically linked to the activation of the PPARα signaling pathway. This finding provides a direct mechanistic link between targeted cellular intervention, a specific molecular pathway, and the inhibition of a key fibrotic process, offering a promising strategic direction for a condition lacking effective disease-modifying therapies.
The significant involvement of M2-like macrophages in fibrosis is widely acknowledged, mainly due to their role in collagen deposition. Our findings extend this understanding by identifying PPARα activation within this cellular context as a critical inhibitory node. The observed M2pep-Tasq-mediated upregulation of PPARα, coupled with the consequent suppression of phosphorylated p65 (NF-κB) and c-Jun (AP-1), delineates a clear signaling cascade. Since the activity of these transcription factors is known to associate with the M2 polarization state, their suppression offers a direct explanation for the decrease in M2 macrophage prevalence and the concomitant reduction in fibrosis. , , This positions PPARα not merely as a bystander in inflammation but as a potent regulator of the pro-fibrotic macrophage phenotype. The convergence of the PPARα, NF-κB, and AP-1 pathways through this mechanism highlights a potentially powerful integrative point for therapeutic intervention.
A crucial aspect of this study lies in the methodological approach represented by the PDC itself. This strategy was adopted to address two interrelated issues. On one hand, inhibiting S100A9 holds clear therapeutic promise for CP. On the other hand, further development and potential approval of the S100A9 inhibitor Tasq have been hindered by nephrotoxicity associated with its long-term administration. , , Drug repurposing presents a strategic approach to overcoming the high expenses and lengthy timelines of drug development. , Targeted delivery systems, such as PDC, RDC (nuclide conjugate drug), SMDC (small molecule conjugate drug), AOC (antibody oligonucleotide conjugate), ADeC (antibody degradation conjugate drug), , , address this issue precisely by enhancing specificity. The primary function of the PDC constructed in this study is to serve as a research tool, enabling the specific delivery of the payload to M2-like macrophages. This specificity has been confirmed. In vitro by enhanced cellular binding, and in vivo by an altered biodistribution pattern that favors pancreatic tissue over non-target organs such as the kidneys, the primary site of toxicity associated with the parent compound. This targeted approach validated the central hypothesis that M2 macrophages are a critical driver of pathology and that selectively modulating their function is therapeutically beneficial. Secondly, the PDC platform demonstrated tangible therapeutic refinement, as the conjugated form mitigated the renal toxicity observed with the parent compound while simultaneously enhancing anti-fibrotic efficacy in the target tissue. This underscores the value of PDC not just as drug delivery vehicles, but as enabling technologies for probing cell-specific functions and refining pharmacological profiles.
The translational promise of targeted therapy is evident in the evolution of conjugate medicines. Following the success of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), design strategies that link cell-targeting peptides to therapeutic payloads via cleavable linkers-leveraging advantages such as synthetic tractability and pharmacokinetic optimization—have propelled modalities such as PDC to the forefront. , , The enhanced tissue penetration and preferential pancreatic accumulation observed for M2pep-Tasq illustrate how contemporary drug design can directly steer both biodistribution and site-specific efficacy. While the intestinal accumulation of Tasq may reflect non-specific distribution or a secondary site of action in pancreatitis, M2pep-Tasq’s ability to redirect this profile toward the pancreas validates the effectiveness of active targeting. The ability to rationally alter a drug’s metabolism and distribution is central to advancing compounds that show mechanistic promise yet possess a narrow therapeutic window.
This study demonstrates that the targeted modulation of M2-like macrophages, mediated through the activation of the PPARα signaling pathway, effectively alleviates pancreatic injury and fibrosis in CP. To this end, a peptide-drug conjugate was developed, which successfully localized the therapeutic action to the lesion site while markedly reducing the renal toxicity associated with the prolonged administration of the parent compound. The coupling of enhanced efficacy with reduced off-target toxicity underscores the value of a targeted delivery strategy in improving the therapeutic index. From a broader perspective, this work illustrates how drug repositioning, when integrated with targeted delivery technologies, can overcome existing therapeutic limitations and renew the potential of compounds whose development had been stalled. Although these preclinical mechanistic findings offer fresh insight into immune-related fibrosis, further translation of this strategy will require systematic evaluation of its pharmacokinetic profile and long-term safety in more advanced models.

## Limitations of the study
There are several limitations in this study that should be taken into account. First, the M2-targeting peptide was selected based on earlier mouse studies, and its binding affinity and specificity for human M2-like macrophages require further validation. Moving forward, techniques such as phage display could be employed to identify human-specific targeting motifs or peptides with improved affinity. Second, the findings presented here do not fully address the central hurdles to clinical translation. On the one hand, all conclusions are derived from a preclinical mouse model, and the relevance of its pathological features to human disease remains to be confirmed. On the other hand, systematic assessment of the long-term safety profile of the conjugate is still lacking.
In addition, it should be noted that the measured peptide-target interaction exhibits relatively weak binding affinity and a high dissociation rate. The relatively weak binding affinity of peptide-based drugs for their protein targets, often reflected in larger dissociation constants (KD), stems from several interrelated factors. Primarily, peptides possess a smaller binding interface compared to full-length proteins, which limits the number of non-covalent interactions—such as hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic contacts – that can be simultaneously formed. Furthermore, they typically lack the extensive and cooperative network of interactions characteristic of protein-protein interfaces. Additionally, the inherent structural flexibility of many peptides frequently incurs a significant conformational entropy penalty upon binding, further destabilizing the complex. These factors collectively contribute to the generally lower binding affinity observed in peptide-protein interactions. Such low affinity coupled with rapid off-kinetics indicates that the peptide in its current form would face considerable challenges within conventional drug development pipelines. This outcome underscores a common hurdle in peptide-based drug discovery: initial candidates often require substantial optimization to achieve pharmacologically relevant binding strength. Consequently, future efforts must prioritize affinity maturation strategies, which could include: (i) systematic amino acid substitutions to enhance contacts with key binding site residues , , ; (ii) introduction of conformational constraints (e.g., cyclization) to reduce the entropic penalty of binding and improve proteolytic stability , ; and (iii) incorporation of non-canonical amino acids or side-chain modifications to strengthen intermolecular interactions. ,

## Experimental model and study participant details

## Animals
C57BL/6 male mice were randomized into control (Ctrl), CP, Tasq, and M2pep-Tasq treatment groups. In accordance with previously reported methods, a CP mouse model was established through intraperitoneal injections of caerulein (50 μg/kg) on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, with one-hour intervals between injections, five times a day, for 8 weeks. The mice in the Tasq and M2pep-Tasq treatment groups were injected with Tasq (0.154 μmol/kg) or M2pep-Tasq (0.154 μmol/kg) for 4 weeks on the second day after the 4th week of caerulein injection. The mice received intraperitoneal injections of equal amounts of 0.9% NaCl over the same period as the control ( ). The administered dose was obtained based on the human-mouse conversion company. In clinical trials, Tasq is administered at a clinical dose of 0.5 mg. Using a standard weight of 70 kg, the human equivalent dose is 0.00714 mg/kg. Using the human-to-mouse allometric scaling factor of 9.1, we derived a mouse dose of 0.065 mg/kg. Owing to the molecular weight discrepancy between M2pep-Tasq and Tasq, we subsequently transformed the dose into molar units, thereby determining a final mouse dose of 0.154 μmol/kg. Following the final drug injection, the experimental mice were put under anesthesia, and their serum along with sections of the pancreas were collected and preserved at -80°C. The remaining organs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for examination.
With approval from the Biomedical Ethics Committee of Dalian Medical University (Approval Number: AEE22107). All animal experiments were performed in line with the international 3R principles of Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement. C57BL/6 male mice (Beijing Huafukang Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility and kept in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment with regulated temperature (25±2°C) and humidity (65%-70%). The mice were kept under a 12-hour light/dark cycle. At the conclusion of the experiments, they were euthanized using an overdose of inhaled isoflurane.

## Cell culture
The RAW264.7 mouse macrophage cell line, sourced from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC), was cultured in DMEM with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. In the experimental treatments, cells were initially exposed to lipopolysaccharide (LPS; 1 μg/mL) for 24 hours or IL-4 (20 ng/mL) for 48 hours. Subsequently, the cells were co-incubated with either Tasq (10 mM) or M2pep-Tasq (10 mM) for an additional 24-hour period. To delve deeper into the interaction between PPARα and M2pep-Tasq, this study included MK-886+Tasq and MK-886+M2pep-Tasq groups in the cell treatment process. As a PPARα inhibitor, MK-886 was used in the following manner: RAW264.7 cells were stimulated with IL-4 (20 ng/mL) for 48 h. Subsequently, MK-886 (0.5 μM) was co-incubated with Tasq (10 mM) or M2pep-Tasq (10 mM) for an additional 24 h.

## Extraction of mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs)
C57BL/6 male mice were put under anesthesia and then euthanized. Bone marrow was extracted from the bilateral femurs and tibias using PBS. The bone marrow cells were filtered through a 70-μm mesh. The resulting suspension was then centrifuged at 1000 rpm. Finally, the resulting supernatant was carefully discarded. The collected bone marrow cells were resuspended in the prepared bone marrow macrophage induction medium [10% FBS+1% penicillin/streptomycin+20 ng/mL granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF)]. After 5 days of culture, BMDMs were identified by immunofluorescence and flow cytometry. To induce macrophages polarization, BMDMs were stimulated with LPS (1 μg/mL, 24 h) for M1 phenotype induction, while an M2 phenotype was promoted by treatment with IL-4 (20 ng/mL, 48 h).

## Method details

## Molecular docking
Using MOE software (version 2018.01011), the molecular docking study was performed. The three-dimensional structure of the S100A9 (PDB: 6ZDY) protein was obtained from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (www.rcsb.org). Concurrently, the two-dimensional structure of the Tasq compound was retrieved from PubChem and subsequently energy-minimized within MOE to generate its 3D conformation (pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). An “induced fit” protocol was employed for the docking simulations, permitting movement of the receptor pocket side chains in response to the ligand, albeit with positional constraints. Initially, the generated poses were ranked with the London dG score. The top 30 poses were then refined using a force field approach and finally assessed with the GBVI/WSA dG scoring function. The visualization and analysis of ligand-receptor interactions were performed with PyMOL. (www.pymol.org).

## Modification and synthesis of M2pep-Tasq
To enhance the bioactivity of Tasq and optimize its ability to bind to the linker, which in turn binds the target peptides to form PDC, we first treated Tasq by the previous methods to increase its affinity to the linker. On this basis, we effectively combined modified Tasq with a flexible linker (GGGSKKK) and a targeting peptide (YEQDPWGVKWWY) and further verified its structure and purity using High Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) and mass spectrometry (MS) analysis ( and ).

## Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) detection
The affinity between M2pep-Tasq and its target protein S100A9 was determined using Biacore 8K. The specific procedure was as follows: First, the surface of the CM5 chip was activated by injecting 400 mM EDC and 100 mM NHS at a flow rate of 10 μL/min for 600 s. Next, the diluted ligand protein S100A9 was injected into the experimental channel (Fc2) of the CM5 chip at a flow rate of 10 μL/min, with a fixation amount of approximately 6000 RU. Finally, the chip was blocked with 1 M ethanolamine at a flow rate of 10 μL/min for 600 s. The reference channel (Fc1) was only activated and blocked without ligand coupling. Meanwhile, the diluted M2pep-Tasq was sequentially injected into the Fc2 and Fc1 channels at a flow rate of 30 μL/min, with a binding time of 60 s and a dissociation time of 60 s. The temperature of both the chip compartment and sample compartment was set to 25°C to determine the affinity between the analyte and the ligand.

## Determination of multiple parameters
Commercial reagent kits from Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute in Nanjing were used to measure the serum levels of CRP, CRE, BUN, ALT and AST.

## Haematoxylin & eosin (HE) staining
Tissue samples fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde were processed through washing, dehydration, paraffin infiltration, embedding, and sectioning. Subsequently, sections underwent deparaffinization and rehydration through xylene and a graded series of ethanol to distilled water. Haematoxylin and eosin were used to stain the nuclei and cytoplasm, respectively, and images were captured using light microscopy.(Olympus DP73; Tokyo, Japan).

## Masson staining
Masson-stained sections were prepared according to the instructions of the Masson's trichrome staining reagent kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). The degree of fibrosis in the mouse pancreatic tissue was observed under a light microscope (Olympus DP73; Tokyo, Japan).

## Live animal imaging
To further validate the targeting of M2pep-Tasq in vivo using a small-animal imager, we synthesized FITC-Tasq and FITC-M2pep-Tasq with FITC attached ( and ). CP mice induced by intraperitoneal injections of caerulein were divided into FITC-Tasq and FITC-M2pepTasq groups, and then 0.154 μmol/kg FITC-Tasq or FITC-M2pep-Tasq was injected into the tail vein of the mice. The mice were observed in the small-animal imaging apparatus after 15 min, 30 min and 60 min of administration. The mouse heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, pancreas, and small intestine were collected and observed under a small-animal imaging device PE lumina III (PerkinElmer, MA, USA) 1 h after administration.

## Quantification of cytokines
Multiple inflammatory factors in mouse serum were detected using Quantibody® Mouse Inflammation Array 1 (RayBiotech, Atlanta, USA) following the instructions provided in the product manual. The signals were visualized using a laser scanner equipped with a Cy3 wavelength (Molecular Devices Axon GenePix® 4300A&4400A; Silicon Valley, USA). The protein arrays corresponding to the inflammatory factor microarrays are shown in .

## Flow cytometry
Macrophages or isolated pancreatic single cells were treated with a fixation and permeabilization solution at 4°C for 30 minutes, followed by incubation with CD68 and CD206 antibodies (1:200) at 4°C in the dark for another 30 minutes. Afterward, the cells were spun down, reconstituted, and moved to a flow cytometry tube for examination (BD lsrfortessa™, New Jersey, USA).

## Immunofluorescence staining
Following blocking with 5% BSA at 37°C for one hour, pancreatic tissue sections or macrophages were exposed to primary antibodies against F4/80 and CD206 (diluted 1:200) overnight at 4°C. Subsequent to DAPI staining, which was conducted at room temperature in the dark for 5 minutes, samples were imaged using a fluorescence microscope. (Keyence BZ-X810; Osaka, Japan).

## Laser confocal imaging
Macrophages were prestimulated with IL-4 (20 ng/mL) for 48 h and treated with FITC-Tasq (10 mM) or FITC-M2pep-Tasq (10 mM) for 24 h, respectively. The cells were fixed with fixation and permeabilization solution for 30 min, incubated with phalloidin-Alexa Fluor 594 (1:1000) for 2 h, and stained with DAPI. Finally, the images were captured under a confocal microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

## RNA-Seq
Total RNA was isolated from mouse pancreatic tissues (CP, Tasq, and M2pep-Tasq groups) using TRIzol Reagent (Accurate Biology, China), following the manufacturer's protocol. To eliminate genomic DNA contamination, the RNA preparations were subsequently digested with DNase I (Accurate Biology). Finally, sequencing libraries were constructed and analyzed on an Illumina platform by Majorbio (Shanghai, China). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected by DESeq2 (1.12.4), and the filtering thresholds were set to a q value of <0.05 and a multiplicity of differences |log2FC|>1.5.

## Real-time quantitative PCR detection system (qPCR)
RNA was purified from murine pancreatic tissue or treated cells with the RNAex Pro RNA reagent (Solarbio, China). Following extraction, cDNA synthesis was carried out using an All-in-One First-Strand Synthesis MasterMix (with dsDNase; Yugong, China). Gene expression quantification was then performed by qPCR with Taq SYBR Green Premix (Universal) (Tli RNaseH Plus; Yugong, China) on either an ABI7500 (Applied Biosystems, USA) or a Bioer 9600 FQD-96A (Bioer, China) system. β-Actin served as the endogenous control, and all primer sequences are provided in .

## Western blotting
Protein was isolated from mouse pancreatic tissue, and its concentration was quantified with a BCA kit (Keygen, China). After separation via SDS-PAGE (Solarbio) and transfer to a PVDF membrane (Millipore), the membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 2 hours. It was then incubated overnight at 4°C with a primary antibody targeting PPARα (1:1000; Sanying, China), NF-κB p65 (1:1000; CST, USA), p-NF-κB p65 (1:1000; CST, USA), C-JNU (1:1000; CST, USA), p-C-IUN (1:1,000; CST, USA), CD206 (1:1000; ABclonal, China) and β-actin (1:20000; ABclonal, China). Following this, the membrane was probed with the appropriate secondary antibody for one hour at room temperature. Protein bands were then detected and documented using a Tanon 4200 imaging system (Shanghai, China).

## Pearson correlation coefficient
For each cell or region of interest, the fluorescence intensity values of FITC (green) and Phalloidin (red) were quantified. ImageJ software was used to measure the fluorescence intensity of each channel. The correlation between FITC and Phalloidin fluorescence intensities was assessed using the Pearson correlation coefficient. A Pearson correlation coefficient r close to +1 indicates a strong positive correlation between FITC and Phalloidin fluorescence intensities, while a value close to -1 implies a strong negative correlation. If r is near 0, there is no significant linear correlation between their fluorescence intensities.

## Quantification and statistical analysis
The data are shown as mean ± SEM and were analyzed with GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad, CA, USA). Differences among several groups were evaluated using one-way ANOVA, while differences between two groups were assessed using unpaired Student's t test. Statistical significance was denoted by P values of <0.05 or <0.01. The treatment of outliers is based on the recommendations of the ARRIVE guidelines.
