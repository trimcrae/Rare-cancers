While long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are emerging as critical regulators of CKD, their roles in RIF pathogenesis are poorly understood. Here, we identify the fibrosis-associated lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 as a key modulator of RIF through integrated analyses of unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) mice and TGF-β-stimulated human renal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2), combined with RNA sequencing, RNA pull-down, ubiquitination profiling, and autophagic flux assays. P4HA2-AS1 was markedly upregulated in fibrotic kidneys, and its suppression attenuated fibrotic phenotypes in vivo and in vitro while restoring autophagic flux. Mechanistically, P4HA2-AS1 directly binds the E3 ubiquitin ligase TRIM32, impeding its proteasomal degradation. This stabilization enhances TRIM32-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination of ULK1, a master autophagy initiator, leading to aberrant autophagic activation and fibrotic progression. Our study uncovers a previously unrecognized P4HA2-AS1/TRIM32/ULK1 axis that couples dysregulated autophagy to RIF, proposing lncRNA-protein interaction targeting as a therapeutic strategy against renal fibrosis.

## Introduction
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a major global health burden, with an estimated worldwide prevalence of approximately 850 million individuals in 2022 . Strikingly, 16% of the global population aged over 65 years is affected, while China reports an 11% incidence rate among middle-aged and elderly populations, positioning CKD as a critical public health challenge . Renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF), characterized by excessive extracellular matrix deposition and progressive loss of renal function, serves as the central pathological driver of CKD progression to end-stage renal disease. Current therapeutic strategies remain largely palliative, with dialysis and transplantation constituting the only life-sustaining interventions – options frequently constrained by prohibitive costs and limited availability . These clinical imperative underscores the urgent need to elucidate CKD pathogenesis and identify potential therapeutic targets.
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), defined as transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides with minimal protein-coding potential, have emerged as pivotal regulators of cellular processes and disease pathogenesis . These versatile molecules exert multi-level control of gene expression through dynamic interactions with DNA, RNA, and proteins. Emerging evidence implicates lncRNA dysregulation in diverse pathologies, including cancer, where they serve as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets , cardiovascular disorders , neurological diseases , and fibrotic conditions . Notably, genome-wide RNA microarray analyses consistently reveal differential lncRNA expression profiles in experimental CKD models compared to controls, suggesting their functional involvement in disease progression , . Mechanistic studies have identified specific lncRNAs orchestrating renal fibrosis through distinct molecular pathways. For instance, Erbb4-IR modulates transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)/Smad signaling via Smad7 regulation , while TUG1 governs hyperuricemia-induced renal fibrosis in rodent models . Similarly, SOX2OT regulates mesangial cell proliferation and fibrotic processes in diabetic nephropathy through Akt/mTOR-mediated autophagy .
The long non-coding RNA P4HA2 antisense RNA 1 (P4HA2-AS1, Gene ID: 100861518) is identified as an RNA gene located on chr5:132184538-132185039. Preliminary expression data indicate that P4HA2-AS1 is widely expressed across critical organs, including the heart, brain, and kidney. A comprehensive review of standard biological databases confirms a critical and complete absence of functional annotation for the P4HA2-AS1 transcript. Specifically, there are no existing data concerning its expression profile, secondary structure, stability, or direct mechanism of action. The complete lack of prior functional and mechanistic research unequivocally validates the scientific necessity of this investigation.
Autophagy, the lysosomal degradation pathway essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis through clearance of misfolded proteins, damaged organelles, and intracellular pathogens under stress conditions , exhibits context-dependent roles in disease pathogenesis. While baseline autophagic activity sustains physiological functions, its pathological hyperactivation may drive disease progression – exemplified by studies demonstrating that persistent tubular autophagy post-AKI promotes maladaptive repair and renal fibrosis via FGF2 secretion, with autophagy deficiency suppressing both FGF2 production and fibrotic outcomes . Paradoxically, caloric restriction exacerbates renal dysfunction and fibrosis in ischemia-reperfusion injury models through mTORC1 inhibition-mediated autophagy enhancement . Furthermore, PLK1 critically maintains lysosomal function and autophagic flux through phosphorylation of ATP6V1A, and its inhibition attenuates obstructive nephropathy by suppressing pathological hyperactivation of autophagy . These findings collectively underscore the therapeutic potential of modulating autophagic flux to mitigate renal fibrosis. Emerging evidence implicates lncRNAs in autophagy-mediated disease regulation. For example, EIF3J-DT enhances chemoresistance in gastric cancer by stabilizing ATG14 mRNA through competitive binding with MIR188-3p, thereby activating autophagy . Conversely, CAIF suppresses cardiac autophagy and alleviates myocardial infarction by inhibiting p53-mediated troponin transcription . Despite these advances, the role of P4HA2-AS1 in RIF remains unexplored. Tripartite motif-containing 32 (TRIM32), a member of the TRIM protein family, functions as an E3 ubiquitin ligase through its N-terminal RING domain, which mediates canonical catalytic activity to ubiquitinate substrate proteins . Under metabolic stress, ATM-CHK2 pathway-mediated phosphorylation of TRIM32 at Ser55 triggers K63-linked ubiquitination of ATG7 at Lys45, activating the ULK1/BECN1 complex to initiate autophagosome formation . In this study, we identify P4HA2-AS1 as a fibrosis-promoting lncRNA upregulated in unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced fibrotic kidneys and TGF-β-treated human kidney tubular epithelial cells (HK-2) cells. Genetic inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 attenuated renal pathology, collagen deposition, and functional decline in murine models. Mechanistically, P4HA2-AS1 stabilizes TRIM32 by impeding its proteasomal degradation, thereby enhancing TRIM32-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination of ULK1. This aberrant activation of autophagy drives fibrotic progression, positioning P4HA2-AS1-TRIM32 axis as a potential therapeutic target for CKD.

## Results

## P4HA2-AS1 is upregulated in renal fibrosis models
To investigate the role of lncRNAs in renal fibrosis, we performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis of renal tissue from UUO mice (Fig.  ). The top 10 most significantly upregulated and downregulated genes were individually analyzed, revealing that lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 was among the five most differentially expressed genes (Fig.  ). RT-qPCR validation of the top 10 upregulated genes in the UUO model confirmed significant increases in the expression of LOC105379385, SERPINE1, and P4HA2-AS1 (Fig.  ). Further validation in the renal ischemia-reperfusion and streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic nephropathy mouse models confirmed the differential expression of LOC105379385, SERPINE1, and P4HA2-AS1 RNA. These results demonstrate that lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 is stably upregulated in UUO, renal ischemia-reperfusion injury, and STZ-induced diabetic nephropathy models (Fig.  ), and its expression correlates with renal function in UUO mice (Fig.  ). RNA-FISH combined with immunofluorescence staining revealed that UUO and STZ treated induce upregulation of P4HA2-AS1 expression in the cytoplasm of Lotus Tetragonolobus Lectin (LTL)-positive proximal tubular cells (Fig.  ). 
In vitro, we treated HK-2 with TGF-β and observed its impact on the top 10 upregulated genes in the UUO model. Significant differential expression was observed for LOC105379385, SERPINE1, and P4HA2-AS1 (Supplementary Fig.  ). Notably, P4HA2-AS1 was consistently upregulated in response to TGF-β, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), and high glucose (HG) treatments (Supplementary Fig.  ), with its expression showing both time- and dose-dependent increases upon TGF-β induction (Supplementary Fig.  ). Subcellular fractionation PCR revealed that P4HA2-AS1 primarily localized to the cytoplasm, with a size of 836 bp (Fig.  ). These findings suggest that lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 may play a role in the development of renal fibrosis induced by UUO.

## Inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 ameliorates renal fibrosis in in vivo and in vitro models
To assess the role of P4HA2-AS1 in UUO-induced renal fibrosis, we delivered Adeno-Associated Virus 9 (AAV9)-P4HA2-AS1 via renal cortical microinjection in UUO mice (Fig.  and Supplementary Fig.  ). The widespread expression of eGFP fluorescence in renal tubular epithelial cells indicated successful AAV9 infection. RT-qPCR analysis confirmed a significant reduction in P4HA2-AS1 RNA levels in the P4HA2-AS1i and UUO + P4HA2-AS1i groups, indicating effective suppression of P4HA2-AS1 expression (Fig.  ). Histological analysis of HE, Masson’s trichrome, and Sirius Red staining revealed renal tubular dilation, interstitial inflammatory cell infiltration, and increased collagen deposition around the interstitium and vasculature following UUO intervention for one week (Fig.  ). Additionally, fibrotic markers such as fibronectin, COL-I, and α-SMA were significantly upregulated (Fig.  ). However, inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 expression improved these fibrotic changes and renal tubular damage induced by UUO (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). 
In vitro, we transfected HK-2 cells with lentivirus carrying shRNA targeting P4HA2-AS1 (shRNA1) to generate a stable cell line with suppressed P4HA2-AS1 expression (Supplementary Fig.  ). After 20 ng/ml TGF-β treatment, we observed significant increases in the expression of fibrotic markers including fibronectin, COL-I, and α-SMA. Notably, inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 reduced the TGF-β-induced upregulation of these fibrotic proteins in HK-2 cells (Supplementary Fig.  ). We next examined whether P4HA2-AS1 modulates the expression and secretion of pro-fibrotic factors, CTGF and FGF2, which are critical for fibroblast activation. qRT-PCR and ELISA results showed that TGF-β-induced expression of both CTGF and FGF2 was significantly reduced upon lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 inhibition in HK-2 cells (Supplementary Fig.  ). These results suggest that inhibiting lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 alleviates the development of renal fibrosis both in vivo and in vitro, although the precise mechanisms warrant further investigation.

## P4HA2-AS1 inhibition attenuates fibrosis by modulating autophagic flux
To further explore the mechanism through which P4HA2-AS1 inhibition alleviates renal fibrosis, RNA-seq was performed on HK-2 cells treated with TGF-β and TGF-β + shRNA1 (Fig.  ). GO and GSEA revealed significant enrichment of autophagy-related biological processes (Fig.  ). A combined analysis of differentially expressed genes and autophagy-related genes identified 28 genes associated with autophagy that exhibited significant differential expression (Fig.  ). Additionally, we observed that TGF-β treatment enhanced autophagic flux in HK-2 cells (Fig.  ). Analysis of LC3-II levels in the presence or absence of Baf.A1, a lysosomal inhibitor, indicated that P4HA2-AS1 inhibition did not affect autophagosome formation or basal autophagic flux under normal conditions (Fig.  ), suggesting that P4HA2-AS1 is not critical for maintaining basal autophagy. However, following TGF-β treatment, inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 attenuated the TGF-β-induced enhancement of autophagic flux (Fig.  ). Using eGFP/mCherry-LC3B adenovirus, we also observed that inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 reduced the TGF-β-induced autophagic flux (Fig.  ). Furthermore, suppression of P4HA2-AS1 partially alleviated the accumulation of LC3BII and degradation of P62 induced by TGF-β in HK-2 cells (Supplementary Fig.  ). 
In vivo, inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 reversed the accumulation of LC3BII and P62 degradation in renal tissue from UUO mice (Fig.  , Supplementary Fig.  ). Transmission electron microscopy revealed that P4HA2-AS1 inhibition improved the excessive accumulation of autophagosomes induced by UUO in renal tissue (Fig.  ). To further validate the role of autophagy in UUO, we used ULK1 knockout mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). ULK1 knockout alleviated the excessive autophagy activation, fibrin deposition, and tubular damage induced by UUO (Supplementary Fig.  ). These results suggest that inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 improves renal fibrosis in both in vivo and in vitro models by modulating autophagy levels.

## P4HA2-AS1 stabilizes TRIM32 by blocking proteasomal degradation
To explore the specific mechanism by which P4HA2-AS1 regulates autophagy, we performed RNA pull-down assays in HK-2 cells using biotin-labeled P4HA2-AS1 to identify interacting proteins, followed by mass spectrometry analysis. A total of 33 proteins that significantly interacted with P4HA2-AS1 were identified, with TRIM32 being one of the key interactors (Fig.  ). RNA pull-down and RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays confirmed the interaction between P4HA2-AS1 and TRIM32 (Fig.  ). RNA-FISH and TRIM32 immunofluorescence analysis revealed that TGF-β treatment enhanced the interaction between P4HA2-AS1 and TRIM32 in the cytoplasm of HK-2 cells (Fig.  ). Numerous studies have shown that lncRNAs can regulate protein stability and proteasomal degradation following binding to proteins. To investigate the effect of P4HA2-AS1 binding to TRIM32 and whether proteasomal degradation contributes to TRIM32 turnover, we pretreated HK-2 cells with Baf.A1 or MG132 before TGF-β treatment. The results showed that TGF-β induced TRIM32 expression, and MG132 further inhibited TRIM32 degradation (Fig.  ). Similarly, after inhibiting protein synthesis with cycloheximide (CHX), we found that suppression of P4HA2-AS1 accelerated the proteasomal degradation of TRIM32 (Fig.  ). These results indicate that TRIM32 undergoes proteasomal degradation and that P4HA2-AS1 binding to TRIM32 inhibits its proteasomal degradation. 

## P4HA2-AS1 exerts antifibrotic effects partially via TRIM32 suppression
To further investigate the role of TRIM32 in regulating renal fibrosis in the UUO-induced in vivo model, kidney tubule-specific TRIM32 knockout mice derived from KSP-cre mice and TRIM32-flox mice were utilized (Supplementary Fig.  ). As shown in Supplementary Fig.  , Mice with the genotype TRIM32fl/fl, Ksp-Cre were selected as the knockout group, while littermate TRIM32fl/fl mice served as the control group. Western blot analysis of kidney lysates validated the deletion efficiency, revealing a significant reduction in TRIM32 protein levels in TRIM32fl/fl, Ksp-Cre mice (Supplementary Fig.  ). HE, Masson’s trichrome, and Sirius Red staining revealed that, similar to the inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 expression, TRIM32 cKO significantly reduced renal tubular dilation, interstitial inflammatory cell infiltration, and collagen deposition around the interstitium and vasculature, compared to wild-type UUO mice (Fig.  ). Immunofluorescence double staining further showed reduced fluorescence of fibrotic markers, such as fibronectin and COL-I, in TRIM32 cKO mice (Fig.  ). In an in vitro TGF-β-treated HK-2 model, we observed the expression of TRIM32, and siTRIM32 effectively suppressed its expression (Supplementary Fig.  ). In HK-2 cells, inhibition of TRIM32 significantly alleviated TGF-β-induced expression and deposition of fibrotic proteins (Supplementary Fig.  ) and attenuated the activation of autophagic flux induced by TGF-β (Supplementary Fig  ). These in vitro and in vivo data suggest that kidney tubular-specific TRIM32 knockout alleviates renal fibrosis progression. 
To confirm that P4HA2-AS1 regulates fibrosis through its downstream target, TRIM32, we conducted rescue experiments by overexpressing TRIM32 in cells with stable P4HA2-AS1 inhibition. Overexpression of TRIM32 partially reversed the effects of P4HA2-AS1 suppression on fibrotic protein deposition and autophagic flux (Fig.  ). These results indicate that the anti-fibrotic effect of P4HA2-AS1 inhibition is partially mediated through the degradation of TRIM32.

## TRIM32 drives ULK1 activity via P4HA2-AS1-Dependent K63-linked polyubiquitin
TRIM32 has been reported to regulate autophagy by modulating the activity of ULK1 and BECLIN1 , . Given the upregulation of autophagy in our renal fibrosis model, we aimed to explore the role of TRIM32 in regulating TGF-β-induced autophagy in HK-2 cells. Immunoprecipitation assays in 293 T cells transfected with TRIM32, ULK1, and BECLIN1 vectors revealed a specific interaction between TRIM32 and ULK1 (Fig.  ), with no significant binding to BECLIN1 under the same conditions. Furthermore, in TGF-β-treated HK-2 cells, endogenous immunoprecipitation confirmed that TGF-β promotes the interaction between TRIM32 and ULK1 (Fig.  ). We next explored whether P4HA2-AS1 plays a role in this interaction. Immunoprecipitation analysis showed that downregulation of P4HA2-AS1 disrupted the interaction between TRIM32 and ULK1 (Fig.  ), indicating that P4HA2-AS1 is required for the formation of the TRIM32-ULK1 complex. Given that TRIM32 is an E3 ubiquitin ligase, we used antibodies recognizing K48- and K63-linked polyubiquitin chains to assess whether TRIM32 modulates ULK1 ubiquitination. In 293 T cells transfected with a TRIM32 overexpression plasmid, we found that TRIM32 induced K63-linked polyubiquitination of ULK1, while K48-linked polyubiquitination remained unaffected (Fig.  ). Consistent with its role in the TRIM32-ULK1 complex, P4HA2-AS1 downregulation significantly reduced TRIM32-mediated ULK1 ubiquitination (Fig.  ). Since K63-linked ubiquitination can enhance ULK1 kinase activity , we further explored whether TRIM32 promotes ULK1-mediated autophagic function. Phosphorylation analysis of two well-known ULK1 substrates, VPS34 and BECLIN1 , revealed that TRIM32 enhances ULK1 kinase activity (Fig.  ). These data collectively suggest that TRIM32 interacts with ULK1 in a P4HA2-AS1-dependent manner and promotes its activity through K63-linked polyubiquitination, thereby inducing autophagy. 
To further confirm the role of K63 polyubiquitination in activating ULK1, we analyzed the functional relationship between TRIM32 and ULK1 during TGF-β-induced autophagy. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments in HK-2 cells revealed that TGF-β treatment promotes the interaction between ULK1 and TRIM32 (Supplementary Fig.  ) and increases K63-linked polyubiquitination (Supplementary Fig.  ). We then investigated whether P4HA2-AS1 is required for the binding of K63-linked polyubiquitin to ULK1 under TGF-β stimulation. Inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 in HK-2 cells reduced the amount of K63-linked polyubiquitination associated with ULK1 after TGF-β treatment (Supplementary Fig.  ). Moreover, P4HA2-AS1 downregulation led to reduced ULK1 activity, as evidenced by decreased phosphorylation of its substrates, BECLIN1 and VPS34 (Supplementary Fig.  ). NEDD4L, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, has been shown to target ULK1 for proteasomal degradation . Silencing NEDD4L in HK-2 cells resulted in increased ULK1 stability and higher autophagic activity upon TGF-β treatment (Fig.  ). Together, these findings indicate that downregulation of P4HA2-AS1 inhibits K63-linked polyubiquitination of ULK1, thereby alleviating TGF-β-induced autophagic overactivation.

## ULK1 is essential for P4HA2-AS1-mediated fibrotic progression
To further validate the role of P4HA2-AS1 in regulating renal fibrosis through ULK1 in vivo, we overexpressed P4HA2-AS1 in ULK1 knockout mice using AAV9 vectors. As shown in Fig.  , compared to wild-type UUO mice, ULK1-/- + UUO mice exhibited reduced renal tubular dilation and fibrotic deposition. Fibrotic markers, including collagen, were also significantly reduced (Fig.  ). In contrast, overexpression of P4HA2-AS1 in wild-type mice exacerbated UUO-induced tubular damage and fibrosis progression. However, overexpression of P4HA2-AS1 in ULK1-/- mice did not further aggravate UUO-induced tubular injury or fibrosis progression. These results further confirm that P4HA2-AS1 regulates renal fibrosis progression by modulating ULK1 activity in vivo. 

## Discussion
This study focuses on the role and mechanism of lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 in obstructive nephropathy-induced renal fibrosis. Renal fibrosis, as a progressive pathological condition, represents the end stage of nearly all CKD and progressive renal diseases, encompassing glomerulosclerosis and interstitial fibrosis. The pathogenesis of renal fibrosis remains unclear, and a deeper understanding of its development and strategies to delay its progression holds significant clinical implications for treating CKD.
After renal injury, the initial deposition of fibrotic matrix may assist in tissue repair, and the fibrotic matrix can be resorbed during the repair process following mild damage. However, in chronic injuries such as CKD, the damage is ongoing, and fibrotic matrix deposition continues, eventually disrupting organ structure and function, leading to renal failure . In obstructive nephropathy, conditions such as ureteral obstruction due to stones, tumors, or strictures result in urine retention and elevated renal pelvic pressure, directly compressing renal tubules, inducing tubular epithelial cell necrosis and apoptosis, and releasing inflammatory mediators. This triggers a chronic inflammatory microenvironment. Additionally, under the influence of TGF-β, damaged tubular epithelial cells undergo partial epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and secrete extracellular matrix components, ultimately leading to interstitial fibrosis . Multiple cell types in the kidney contribute to the transition from obstructive nephropathy to CKD, with proximal renal tubules being considered the initiating factor and playing a central role . This rationale underpins our focus on renal tubular epithelial cells in vitro. However, the specific cytokines produced by renal tubular cells and the mechanisms underlying their production remain incompletely understood.
Non-coding RNAs are classified into small nucleolar RNAs, microRNAs, circular RNAs, and lncRNAs . LncRNAs are involved in various physiological and pathological processes, including the regulation of cellular homeostasis, development, and epigenetic modifications, and have been extensively studied for their roles in regulating the progression of numerous diseases , . In the present study, we utilized the UUO-induced ureteral obstruction model and identified the upregulation of lncRNA P4HA2-AS1 through RNA-seq analysis. Further validation in diabetic nephropathy and renal ischemia-reperfusion mouse models, as well as in vitro TGF-β, high glucose, and H2O2 treatments in HK-2 cells, confirmed the increased expression of P4HA2-AS1. Notably, P4HA2-AS1 was predominantly localized in the cytoplasm of HK-2 cells, suggesting its potential role as a key factor in renal interstitial fibrosis. Our results show that the inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 expression both in vivo and in vitro alleviates extracellular matrix deposition and fibrosis progression. RNA-seq, GO, and GSEA enrichment analyses further revealed that P4HA2-AS1 regulates autophagic flux. To explore the specific mechanism through which P4HA2-AS1 modulates autophagy and participates in the progression of renal fibrosis, we performed RNA pull-down assays to identify interacting proteins, followed by mass spectrometry analysis. We identified TRIM32 as an interacting partner of P4HA2-AS1, which regulates TRIM32 protein stability. Nevertheless, further studies are warranted to fully elucidate the precise mechanism of P4HA2-AS1 modulates TRIM32 degradation and the specific structural domains responsible for the interaction between P4HA2-AS1 and TRIM32. Meanwhile, it is essential to determine the upstream regulatory mechanisms of P4HA2-AS1.
TRIM32 contains a RING domain with E3 ligase activity, which ubiquitinates specific substrates to exert its biological function . Ubiquitination is a post-translational modification that covalently attaches ubiquitin peptides to target proteins, either as single molecules or chains. Ubiquitin can form chains linked through various lysine residues, such as K48 and K63, generating different signals that lead to proteasomal degradation or activation of specific pathways . Previous studies have shown that TRIM32 regulates K63 polyubiquitination of GPX4, ATG7, and ULK1, thereby promoting autophagic flux – . In this study, we ruled out the interaction of TRIM32 with BECLIN1 in HK-2 cells and confirmed that TRIM32 activates autophagy by regulating K63-linked polyubiquitin of ULK1. Furthermore, we found that P4HA2-AS1 is essential for the K63 polyubiquitination of ULK1 induced by TRIM32 during TGF-β-induced autophagy in HK-2 cells. Additionally, renal tubular-specific TRIM32 knockout mice further validated the regulatory role of TRIM32 in UUO-induced renal fibrosis. Overexpression of P4HA2-AS1 in ULK1 knockout mice confirmed that P4HA2-AS1 regulates autophagy and participates in renal fibrosis progression through ULK1. Based on these results, we demonstrate that the P4HA2-AS1/TRIM32/ULK1 pathway plays a crucial role in renal fibrosis and could serve as a potential therapeutic target. The observation that TRIM32 overexpression only partially rescued the phenotype induced by P4HA2-AS1 modulation suggests that P4HA2-AS1 likely regulates renal fibrosis through additional parallel mechanisms independent of the TRIM32 pathway. Considering the extensive research establishing the involvement of lncRNAs in ceRNA networks within the context of renal fibrosis , , the cytoplasmic localization of P4HA2-AS1 in HK-2 cells further hints at a simultaneous ceRNA function that could modulate other pro-fibrotic factors. This multifaceted role underscores the complexity and multilayered function of P4HA2-AS1. Therefore, further investigation into these alternative regulatory mechanisms controlled by P4HA2-AS1 is warranted to fully elucidate its contribution to fibrosis.
The ULK1 complex, which includes ULK1, ATG13, FIP200, and ATG101, directly activates the VPS34 complex (composed of VPS34, BECLIN-1, VPS15, and ATG14L) and mediates ATG9 transport, leading to the formation of autophagosomes . The ULK1 complex undergoes a variety of post-translational modifications to convert various stress signals into autophagy signals. Phosphorylation of ULK1 by mTORC1 inhibits its catalytic activity, suppressing autophagy initiation . AMPK can deactivate mTORC1 by phosphorylating its key protein RAPTOR and also directly phosphorylate ULK1 on several serine residues to activate it . mTOR can phosphorylate the VPS34 complex binding partner AMBRA1, inhibiting its interaction with ULK1. Upon mTOR inhibition and autophagy induction, AMBRA1 recruits the E3 ligase TRAF6 to ULK1, resulting in K63-linked polyubiquitination, self-association, and subsequent stabilization of ULK1, enhancing its activity and promoting autophagy . ULK1 also undergoes other ubiquitin modifications, and the E3 ligase NEDD4L has been reported to regulate ULK1 degradation during prolonged starvation . In our study, we found that the E3 ligase TRIM32 mediates K63-linked polyubiquitination of ULK1, a process dependent on the continuous presence of P4HA2-AS1. Inhibition of P4HA2-AS1 reduces TRIM32 degradation and impairs K63-linked polyubiquitination of ULK1, while silencing NEDD4L prevents ULK1 ubiquitination and degradation, sustaining autophagic activation. Our results further highlight the crucial role of the P4HA2-AS1/TRIM32/ULK1 pathway in regulating autophagy during renal fibrosis.
The role of autophagy in renal fibrosis was first suggested by Ding et al. using a UUO model . However, whether autophagy promotes or inhibits fibrosis remains controversial – . In 2016, Livingston et al. demonstrated that Atg5-specific knockout in renal tubular cells improved renal recovery after UUO, reducing interstitial fibrosis . Similarly, Yang et al. found that inhibiting PLK1-mediated autophagy alleviated interstitial fibrosis progression in UUO, suggesting that tubular autophagy may promote fibrosis in the adverse repair process of the kidney . In contrast, Wang et al. showed that lycopene inhibited the AKT signaling pathway to activate autophagy and ameliorated TGF-β-induced renal fibrosis , indicating that autophagy may inhibit fibrosis progression in aristolochic acid-induced renal dysfunction. In our study, both TRIM32 and ULK1 knockout mice exhibited reduced autophagic flux and alleviated UUO-induced renal fibrosis. Initially, autophagy plays an anti-fibrotic role by clearing damaged organelles, reducing inflammation, and preventing EMT. However, sustained autophagic activation during maladaptive repair due to prolonged obstruction becomes detrimental and promotes fibrosis. The conflicting role of autophagy in renal fibrosis may be attributed to cell-type specificity, disease stage dynamics, autophagic flux threshold effects, signaling pathway interactions, and experimental model differences. Future studies should employ single-cell sequencing, spatial-temporal gene editing, and dynamic imaging techniques to elucidate the role of autophagy in specific cell types and stages, providing insights for targeted autophagy therapies.
This study has several limitations. First, due to limited access to human renal samples, we primarily used animal and cell-based in vitro models to assess the differential expression of P4HA2-AS1. Although a correlation between P4HA2-AS1 and renal function was observed in mice, its clinical relevance requires validation with human samples. Second, it remains unclear whether TRIM32 regulates the ubiquitination of other autophagy-related proteins besides BECLIN1 and ULK1. Third, while we have demonstrated the interaction between P4HA2-AS1 and TRIM32 and its regulation of TRIM32 stability, the exact mechanisms by which P4HA2-AS1 modulates TRIM32 degradation need further investigation. Moreover, while HK-2 cells play a pivotal role in UUO-induced renal fibrosis, future studies should explore whether the P4HA2-AS1/TRIM32/ULK1 pathway also regulates autophagy in other renal cell types, such as interstitial macrophages and myofibroblasts.
This study focuses on the role of long non-coding RNA P4HA2-AS1 in obstructive nephropathy-induced renal fibrosis. Through in vivo and in vitro experiments, we found that P4HA2-AS1 expression is elevated in renal fibrosis models, and its inhibition alleviates extracellular matrix deposition and fibrosis progression. Mechanistically, P4HA2-AS1 interacts with TRIM32, enhancing its stability, and induces K63 polyubiquitination of ULK1, thereby activating autophagic flux, which promotes fibrotic protein deposition and renal fibrosis. Overall, this study establishes the critical role of the P4HA2-AS1/TRIM32/ULK1 pathway in renal fibrosis and offers new directions for future research and drug target development.

## Materials and methods

## Animals
Wild-type C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old) were obtained from the Hubei Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention. ULK1-knockout (ULK1-KO) mice (C57BL/6JCya-Ulk1em1/Cya, Strain S-KO-20861) were purchased from Cyagen (Cyagen Biosciences Inc., China). TRIM32-floxed mice (NCBI reference sequence: NM_001161782.1; MGI:1917057; Gene ID: 69807; Ensembl: ENSMUST00000107366) and kidney-specific Ksp-Cre mice (Ksp-Cre, YDS0122) were generated via CRISPR/Cas9 technology and acquired from Shulaibao Biotechnology (Wuhan, China). TRIM32 conditional knockout (TRIM32-cKO) mice were generated by crossing TRIM32-floxed (TRIM32Fl/Fl) mice with Ksp-Cre mice, enabling Cre recombinase-mediated deletion of TRIM32 in renal tubules. To generate TRIM32-floxed mice, Cas9 mRNA and sgRNA were synthesized by in vitro transcription. A homologous recombination donor vector was constructed using fusion cloning, containing a 4.0-kb 5′ homology arm, a 0.7-kb floxed region, and a 4.0-kb 3′ homology arm. C57BL/6 J zygotes were microinjected with Cas9 mRNA, sgRNA, and the donor vector. Founder mice were genotyped by PCR amplification of tail DNA followed by Sanger sequencing. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (Approval No.: 20230302). Mice were housed in specific pathogen-free facilities at the Animal Experiment Center of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University under a 12-hour light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water.

## Adeno-Associated Virus (AAV) administration
To achieve renal-specific knockdown or overexpression of P4HA2-AS1, AAV9 vectors were utilized for targeted delivery. Recombinant AAV9 particles encoding either a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) against murine P4HA2-AS1 (AAV9-shP4HA2-AS1; shRNA target sequence: 5′-GGUAAAGCCACAAUAGAUATT-3′) or a CMV promoter-driven P4HA2-AS1 overexpression construct with a C-terminal 3×Flag tag and ZsGreen reporter (AAV9-CMV-P4HA2-AS1-ZsGreen) were procured from HanBio (Shanghai, China). Control vectors (AAV9-ZsGreen) expressing only the ZsGreen reporter were used for normalization. Male mice were anesthetized via inhalation of 2% isoflurane and positioned in lateral recumbency. AAV9 suspensions (1.5 × 10¹² vector genomes [vg]/mL in sterile PBS) were bilaterally microinjected into the renal cortex using a glass micropipette (10 μL per site, 5 injection sites per kidney). Postoperative analgesia and monitoring were performed for 72 hours. Kidney tissues were harvested 4 weeks post-injection for validation of P4HA2-AS1 modulation via qRT-PCR or immunofluorescence.

## Animal models
We generated a randomization sequence using a Random Number Table Method prior to the initiation of the experiment. For the Unilateral Ureteral Obstruction (UUO) Model, mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane, and a left flank incision was made to expose the left kidney and ureter. The ureter was double-ligated near the renal pelvis using non-absorbable silk sutures (5-0). Postoperative care included thermal support and antibiotic prophylaxis (enrofloxacin, 5 mg/kg/day). Kidneys were harvested 7 days post-surgery, with the contralateral kidney serving as an internal control. Fibrosis severity was assessed via renal hypertrophy, hydronephrosis grading, and histopathology (HE, Masson’s trichrome staining, and Siris red staining). Diabetes was induced by intraperitoneal injection of STZ (50 mg/kg/day in citrate buffer, pH 4.5) for 5 consecutive days. Mice with fasting blood glucose levels persistently ≥16.7 mmol/L at day 7 post-injection, accompanied by polyuria/polydipsia, were considered diabetic. Glycemic stability was maintained via insulin glargine (0.5–1.0 IU/day, subcutaneously) to prevent hypoglycemia-related mortality. Renal tissues and blood samples were collected 14 weeks post-induction for biochemical and histological analyses. For the ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) model, under 2% isoflurane anesthesia, a midline laparotomy was performed to expose bilateral renal pedicles. Both renal arteries were occluded with non-traumatic vascular clamps for 30 minutes, followed by clamp removal to restore perfusion. Core temperature was maintained at 37–38 °C using a feedback-controlled heating pad. Kidneys were harvested 7 days post-surgery. Prior to initiating molecular biology experiments, we performed rigorous pathological examinations of the kidney tissue. Specifically, any animal in which the modeling procedure failed to induce observable kidney damage or fibrosis was excluded from subsequent analyses.

## Cell culture
The human renal proximal tubular epithelial cell line HK-2 and human embryonic kidney 293 T cell line were obtained from Procell Life Technology Co., Ltd. (HK-2: CL-0109, HEK-293T: CL-0005, Wuhan, China). 293 T cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco, 12491015) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, A5256701), 2 mM L-glutamine, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (YEASEN Biotechnology, 60162ES76). HK-2 cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco, 12634010) containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. All cells were incubated at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ and 95% humidity, with medium refreshed every 48 hours. Subculture was performed at ~80% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, 25300054). For in vitro injury modeling, HK-2 cells were synchronized in serum-free medium for 12 hours, followed by treatment with 10 ng/mL recombinant human TGF-β1 (R&D Systems, 7754-BH), 30 mM D-glucose (Sigma, G8270), or 200 μM H₂O₂ (Nanjing Reagent, C0404510123) for 24 hours in complete medium. Post-treatment samples were harvested for downstream analyses.

## Lentiviral transduction and plasmid transfection
To stably knockdown P4HA2-AS1 in HK-2 cells, lentiviral particles encoding validated shRNAs targeting human P4HA2-AS1 (Supplementary Fig.  , shRNA1: 5′-GCAUGCUAUUUACCACUAUTT-3′; shRNA2: 5′-GCUGCUAAAGCAUUCAUUATT-3′) were generated from HanBio (China, Shanghai). Cells were transduced at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10 in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene (Beyotime, C0351). After 24 hours, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium, and eGFP expression was confirmed by fluorescence microscopy at 48–72 hours post-transduction. Stable clones were selected using 2 μg/mL puromycin (MedChemExpress, HY-K1057) for 7 days, with knockdown efficiency verified by qRT-PCR. For overexpression, the full-length sequences of P4HA2-AS1 and TRIM32 were PCR-amplified using primers flanked by EcoRI/NotI restriction sites and cloned into the pcDNA3.1(+) vector (Thermo Fisher, V79020). Plasmid integrity was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. HK-2 cells were transfected with 2 μg plasmid DNA using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, L3000015) per manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were harvested 48 hours post-transfection for qRT-PCR analysis. For autophagic flux analysis, HK-2 cells were infected with adenoviral particles encoding eGFP-mCherry-LC3B (HanBio) at an MOI of 10. After 24 hours, cells were treated as indicated and imaged using confocal microscopy (Olympus, Japan). Autophagosomes (yellow puncta, merged eGFP + /mCherry + ) and autolysosomes (red puncta, mCherry+/eGFP−) were quantified using ImageJ (≥20 cells/group). Data were normalized to total LC3B puncta/cell.

## RNAi
HK-2 cells were transfected with 50 nM ON-TARGETplus siRNA targeting TRIM32 (5′-ATAACTCCCTCAAGGTATATA-3′) or NEDD4L (5′-CCUCUGUAAUGAGGAUCAUUUTT-3′) (Dharmacon) using Lipofectamine 3000. siRNA-lipid complexes were prepared in Opti-MEM (Gibco, 31985070) and incubated with cells for 6 hours before replacing with complete medium. Knockdown efficiency was assessed at 48 hours by qRT-PCR and Western blot.

## RNA sequencing
Total RNA was isolated from renal tissues or HK-2 cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen™,15596026CN) and subjected to quality control (RIN ≥ 8.0, Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer). Ribosomal RNA was depleted via the NEBNext rRNA Depletion Kit (Human/Mouse/Rat) (NEB), and mRNA-enriched libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB). Double-stranded cDNA was synthesized, end-repaired, adapter-ligated, and PCR-amplified (12 cycles). Library quality and concentration were assessed using a Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher) and Agilent High Sensitivity DNA Kit. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (150 bp paired-end reads). Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 (v1.34.0), applying a significance threshold of adjusted p < 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and |log₂(fold change)| ≥1. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was performed using clusterProfiler (v4.2.2) with Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases.

## Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)
Total RNA was isolated from tissues or cells using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, 15596026CN) followed by DNase I treatment (RNase-free, Thermo Fisher). RNA concentration and purity were measured using a NanoDrop one (Thermo Fisher, AZY2332326). First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng total RNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, 6215A, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. qRT-PCR was performed in technical triplicate using the SYBR Premix Ex Taq II Kit (Takara, RR820A, Japan) on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Reaction conditions included an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 60 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 45 s. Primer sequences (Supplementary Table  ) were designed using NCBI Primer-BLAST, with amplicons spanning exon-exon junctions to avoid genomic DNA amplification. Gene expression levels were normalized to β-actin (2-ΔΔCt method) and quantified via standard curve analysis.

## RIP
Cells were crosslinked with 0.37% formaldehyde for 10 minutes at room temperature, followed by quenching with 125 mM glycine for 5 minutes. After PBS washes, cells were lysed in RIP buffer (150 mM NaCl, 10 mM HEPES [pH 7.4], 3 mM MgCl₂, 10% glycerol, 1% NP-40, 2 mM DTT, 1 mM PMSF) supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail (HY-K0010, MedChemExpress) and SUPERase-In RNase inhibitor (1000 U/mL, Thermo Fisher, AM2696). Lysates were sonicated (3 × 10 s pulses, 30% amplitude) and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4 °C. For immunoprecipitation, 500 μg of pre-cleared lysate was incubated overnight at 4 °C with 2 μg of anti-TRIM32 antibody or IgG as an isotype negative control. Antibody complexes were captured using Protein A/G Magnetic Beads (MedChemExpress, HY-K0202) for 2 hours at 4 °C. Beads were washed five times with RIP buffer, and RNA was extracted using TRIzol with glycogen (20 μg/mL) as a carrier. The immunoprecipitated RNA concentration and purity were determined by NanoDrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Co-precipitated RNA was reverse-transcribed (PrimeScript RT kit, Takara, RR037A, Japan) and analyzed by qRT-PCR. For quality control, the qRT-PCR products were routinely subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis to confirm the identity and expected size of the amplified cDNA.

## RNA-FISH
To localize P4HA2-AS1 lncRNA, cells and kidney sections were processed using the Ribo™ lncRNA FISH Kit (RiboBio, C10910, Guangzhou, China). For cellular analysis, HK-2 cells were fixed with 4% PFA for 15 mins at room temperature, permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 mins, and pre-hybridized in pre-warmed hybridization buffer at 37 °C for 30 mins. CY3- or FITC-labeled P4HA2-AS1-specific probes (5 μM, RiboBio) were hybridized overnight at 37 °C in a humidified chamber. For kidney tissue sections, deparaffinized and rehydrated samples were fixed with 4% PFA for 20 minutes. Antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 95 °C for 15 minutes. Sections were hybridized with 20 μM CY3-conjugated probes overnight at 55 °C. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1 μg/mL, Beyotime, C1005). Imaging was performed on confocal microscope. Signal quantification was conducted using ImageJ, with background subtraction and thresholding applied.
Nuclear-Cytoplasmic RNA Fractionation
Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNAs were fractionated using the Nuclear and Cytoplasmic RNA Extraction Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. β-actin (cytoplasmic control) and U6 snRNA (nuclear control) were analyzed using SYBR Green assays.

## Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
FLAG-tagged BECLIN1, TRIM32, and VPS34 constructs were generated by cloning respective cDNAs into pcDNA3.1-FLAG vectors, while MYC-ULK1 and HA-TRIM32 were subcloned into pcDNA3.1-MYC and pcDNA3.1-HA vectors, respectively (all plasmids constructed by OriGene). For ectopic interaction assays, 293 T cells were co-transfected with the indicated plasmids using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). Cells were lysed 48 hours post-transfection in non-denaturing buffer (10 mM Tris pH 8.0, 140 mM NaCl, 1% Triton X-100, 0.1% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, 1 mM EDTA, 1 mM PMSF). Lysates were immunoprecipitated with anti-FLAG, anti-MYC, or anti-HA antibodies bound to Protein A/G magnetic beads (MedChemExpress, HY-K0202). Precipitates were washed four times with lysis buffer, eluted in SDS sample buffer, and analyzed by Western blot using antibodies listed in Supplementary Table  . IgG isotype controls confirmed specificity. For endogenous interaction studies, HK-2 cells were treated as indicated, lysed, and subjected to immunoprecipitation with anti-TRIM32 or anti-ULK1 antibodies. Protein complexes were detected by immunoblotting with validated antibodies.

## RNA pull-down and mass spectrometry
Biotinylated P4HA2-AS1 RNA probes were synthesized via in vitro transcription using T7 RNA polymerase (Roche, 12352204) and biotin-16-UTP (Roche, 11388908910), followed by DNase I digestion (RNase-free, YEASEN, 10325ES80) and purification with the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, 74104). RNA integrity was verified by Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (RIN > 8.0). For RNA-protein interaction assays, 3 μg of biotinylated RNA was denatured at 95 °C for 2 minutes and gradually renatured in folding buffer (10 mM Tris pH 7.4, 100 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl₂). Renatured RNA was incubated with pre-cooled HK-2 cytoplasmic lysates (1 mg total protein, supplemented with protease/RNase inhibitors) in binding buffer (20 mM HEPES pH 7.5, 150 mM KCl, 0.5% NP-40) for 2 hours at 4 °C. Antisense RNA and no-RNA controls were included. Streptavidin magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher, 65605D) were added for 1 hour at 4 °C, followed by stepwise washing with high-salt (500 mM KCl) and low-salt (150 mM KCl) buffers. Bound proteins were eluted in 2× SDS buffer and analyzed by nanoLC-Orbitrap Fusion Lumos mass spectrometry (Thermo Fisher). Raw data were processed using MaxQuant (v2.1.0) against the UniProt human database, with significant interactors identified via SAINT (v3.6.0; FDR < 1%). Candidate proteins were validated by Western blot.

## Histopathological analysis
Renal tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. For histopathological evaluation, sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E, Solarbio, G1120), Sirius red (Solarbio, G1472), or Masson’s trichrome (Solarbio, G1340) and imaged at ×400 magnification. Tubulointerstitial injury was graded on a 0–4 scale based on tubular dilation, necrosis, and corticomedullary damage extent: 0 (none), 1 (<25%), 2 (25–50%), 3 (50–75%), or 4 (>75%). Fibrosis area (%) was quantified using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software by calculating the ratio of Sirius red- or Masson’s trichrome-positive pixels to total cortical area. For immunohistochemistry (IHC), deparaffinized sections underwent heat-mediated antigen retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0, 95 °C, 20 minutes), 3% H2O2 endogenous peroxidase blocking, and 5% goat serum blocking. Rabbit anti-E-cadherin antibodies were applied overnight at 4 °C, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (goat anti-rabbit IgG, Abcam ab6721, 1:1000) and DAB chromogen development. Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin, and sections were mounted with neutral resin.

## Immunofluorescence
Paraffin-embedded kidney sections and treated HK-2 cells were fixed, permeabilized, and blocked with 5% BSA. Samples were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by Alexa Fluor 488/555/647-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:2000, Cell Signaling Technology) for 1 hour at 25 °C. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1 μg/mL, Beyotime, C1005) for 10 minutes. Images were acquired using a laser-scanning confocal microscope (Olympus FV3000, Japan) and analyzed with ImageJ.

## Western Blot
Tissues or cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (Servicebio, G2033) supplemented with phosphatase inhibitors A/B (Servicebio, G2007), protease inhibitor cocktail (HY-K0010, MedChemExpress), and 1 mM PMSF (Servicebio, G2008) for 30 minutes. Lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 minutes at 4 °C, and supernatants were quantified via BCA assay (Beyotime, P0012). Proteins (20–30 μg) were denatured in 5× SDS sample buffer at 95 °C for 10 minutes, resolved on 12% SDS-PAGE gels, and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBST for 1 hour at room temperature, incubated with primary antibodies (Supplementary Table  ) overnight at 4 °C, washed three times with TBST, and probed with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit or anti-mouse IgG secondary antibodies (CST, 7074 and 70761:5000) for 1 hour at room temperature. Signals were detected using ECL substrate (Bio-Rad) and quantified by ImageJ (NIH), with target protein expression normalized to β-actin/GAPDH.

## Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)
Renal tissue samples were rapidly dissected into 1 mm³ fragments and immersion-fixed in pre-chilled 2.5% glutaraldehyde/4% paraformaldehyde (0.1 M phosphate buffer, pH 7.4) at 4 °C for 24 hours. After three washes in 0.1 M phosphate buffer, tissues were post-fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide for 2 hours, dehydrated through a graded ethanol series and acetone, and embedded in epoxy resin. Ultrathin sections (70 nm) were double-stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate for 15 minutes each. Autophagosomes were identified by double-membrane structures and luminal contents under a Hitachi HT7800 transmission electron microscope. Autophagosome density (autophagosomes/μm²) was quantified across 10 randomly selected non-overlapping fields per sample.

## Serum Creatinine and Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)
Serum creatinine (Elabscience, E-BC-K188-M) and BUN (Elabscience, E-BC-K183-M) levels were quantified using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

## Statistics and reproducibility
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Normality was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Homogeneity of variances was assessed using Levene’s test. For data that met the assumptions of normality and homoscedasticity. One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used to assess differences among three or more groups. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using unpaired two-tailed t-tests. If the assumption of normality was not met, appropriate non-parametric tests were employed. Continuous data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables are presented as counts. All experiments were performed with three technical replicates and were independently reproduced at least three biological replicates. All tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.
