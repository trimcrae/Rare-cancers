Here, we investigated the clinical relationship between serum HSP90α (an isoform of HSP90) levels and LVH in patients with hypertension or aortic stenosis (AS) and explored underlying mechanisms in pressure overload mouse model. We built a pressure overload mouse model via transverse aortic constriction (TAC). Compared to controls, elevated serum HSP90α levels were observed in patients with hypertension or AS, and the levels positively correlated with LVH. Similarly, HSP90α levels increased in heart tissues from patients with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), and in mice post-TAC. TAC induced the enhanced cardiac expression and secretion of HSP90α from cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts. Knockdown of HSP90α or blockade of extracellular HSP90α (eHSP90α) attenuated cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction by inhibition of β-catenin/TCF7 signaling under pressure overload. Further analysis revealed that eHSP90α interacted with EC1-EC2 region of N-cadherin to activate β-catenin, enhancing the transcription of hypertrophic genes by TCF7, resulting in cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction under pressure overload. These insights suggest the therapeutic potential of targeting HSP90α-initiated signaling pathway against cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure under pressure overload.

## Introduction
Myocardial hypertrophy, particularly left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), is prevalent in approximately 7%–19 % of the general adult population [ ] and rises to over 35 % in hypertensive patients [ ] or 67 % in those with asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (AS) [ ]. Chronic cardiac overload caused by sustained hypertension or AS initiates myocardial hypertrophy, or the thickening of the ventricular walls, which is followed by adverse cardiac remodeling or disrupted myocardial structure. The progressive cardiac hypertrophy significantly escalates the risk of heart failure or cardiac death [ ]. Thus, preventing the development of myocardial hypertrophy has become a potential strategy for the treatment of chronic heart failure.
Heat shock protein 90 (HSP90), one of the most abundant chaperone proteins, plays a vital role in the maturation and activation of numerous signaling proteins, underpinning various cellular functions in the eukaryotic cell. Cytosol HSP90 comprises two isoforms: HSP90α and HSP90β [ ]. HSP90α, an ATP-dependent molecular chaperone, participates in the folding, activation, and stabilization of many oncogenic proteins [ ]. Upon tissue injury, HSP90α is translocated to cell surface and released into extracellular space which is collectively referred as “extracellular HSP90α” (eHSP90α) [ ]. Elevated circulating levels of HSP90α have been implicated in several cancers, closely linked with tumorigenesis and progression [ , ]. In contrast, HSP90β is considered a constitutive isoform [ ]. In cardiovascular systems, HSP90 is associated with progression of vascular damage in diabetes [ ] and carotid atherosclerotic plaque formation in ApoE mice [ ]. Inhibition of HSP90 has shown to mitigate pathological cardiac remodeling induced by pressure overload or angiotensin II (Ang II) stimulation [ , , ]. However, the specific roles of HSP90 isoforms in cardiac remodeling remain unclear. Notably, an increase in serum HSP90α levels has been observed in obese patients with newly diagnosed arterial hypertension [ ]. Whether HSP90α or extracellular HSP90α plays a critical role in maladaptive cardiac hypertrophy or LVH under pressure overload remains largely unknown.
In the present study, we found the elevated serum HSP90α levels positively correlated with LVH in patients with hypertension or aortic stenosis. We identified that increased cardiac secreted-HSP90α were from cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts in response to pressure overload, plays a pivotal role in pathological cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction. These effects are mediated by activation of LRP6-N-cadherin/β-catenin/TCF7 signaling axis. Our findings suggest that HSP90α-initiated signaling could be a potential therapeutic target to combat myocardial hypertrophy and heart failure during pressure overload.

## Materials and methods
2.1

## Analysis of serum HSP90α
The serum Hsp90α were detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (Hsp90α: Cat# CSB-E13462h, Wuhan, China) follow the steps specified in the kit manual.

2.2

## Animal experiments
All animal procedures were approved by the Animal Care Committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, and in comply with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85-23, 1996, revised 2011; www.nap.edu/catalog/5140.html). Anesthesia was induced in mice with 2.5 % isoflurane in oxygen until deep anesthesia, defined by lack of response to toe/tail pinch, and maintained with a ventilator at 1.5 % isoflurane in oxygen (flow rate, 1–2 L/min). Transverse aortic constriction (TAC) was performed to establish the pressure overload mouse model. The 8–10 weeks C57BL/6 mice were fixed in supine position on the operating table, the aortic arch was separated and tied with a 6-0 silk thread and a 27-gauge needle, then the needle was immediately removed. From 2 weeks to 4 weeks after sham or TAC operation, 17-DMAG in corn oil or corn oil via intraperitoneal injections every 2 days [ ]. 1G6-D7 or IgG was infused into mice by minipump for two weeks after TAC or sham operation (See Supplementary methods). At the designated time-points, mice were euthanized followed by deep anesthesia with a combination of isoflurane inhalation and intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (200 mg/kg, ip, Sigma-Aldrich). The hearts and blood were collected by bilateral thoracotomy for further analysis.

2.3

## Mechanical stretch
We stretched cells as a previous study [ ]. The autoclaved silicone sheet (20 × 40 mm) was coated with rat tail collagen dissolved in 0.1 % acetic acid. The cells were cultured on the collagen-coated silicone sheet for 48 h, followed by serum-free for 24 h. These cells were then stretched by fixing the silicone sheets in the stretching frame, and the sheet was stretched to 20∗50 mm (that is, stretch to 120 %). The control groups were cultured on the silicone sheet without mechanical stretch.

2.16

## Human study
We examined serum HSP90α levels in sampled cases and controls from the assembled cohort of eligible individuals in the Zhongshan Hospital of Fudan University (Shanghai, China), hypertension cases (HBP) and the control from October 2020 to April 2021, severe aortic stenosis patients and their control from July 2021 to January 2022 (described in Study I and Study II). HSP90α levels were detected in heart tissues form hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) and control donors (details in Study III). All the human studies were approved by the ethics committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University (Approval No: B2019-088R for Study I, B2021-886R for Study II, B2021-418R for Study III). Before being included in the study, all patients/family members had written informed consent. Our studies strictly comply with the principles in the Declaration of Helsinki.
Study I, HSP90α in Healthy Controls and Patients with Hypertension. 89 controls and 188 hypertensive patients participated in the study. The cases were all individuals diagnosed with hypertension. The diagnosis of hypertension is based on the 2018 European Society of Hypertension (ESH)/European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Hypertension Diagnostic Standards) [ ]. Shortly, systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥90mmg or taking antihypertensive medication. We divided hypertension patients into two groups, the well-controlled group and the poor-controlled group. The patients’ blood pressure in the well-controlled group is reduced to systolic blood pressure <140 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure<90mmg under optimal treatment according to the current guidelines. Poor-controlled refers to persistent hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥90mmg) despite optimal therapy according to the current guidelines, without end-organ damage or vascular complications. Controls were individuals who were free of hypertension at the time of enrollment. To reduce differences due to potential confounders when comparing the cases and controls, we used propensity score matching (PSM) to balance across confounding variables (age, sex, BMI, heart rate, smoking, and medical history). Participants were men or women aged at least 18 years. Standard exclusion criteria for both of patients and controls were with kidney dysfunction, liver dysfunction, acute infection within 1-month, any of severe diseases (cancer, acute myocardial infarction, acute heart failure, and acute cerebrovascular accident within 2-week, etc.).
Study II, HSP90α in Controls and Patients with Severe Aortic Stenosis. Cases were all individuals diagnosed with severe aortic stenosis (AS, n = 72) and controls (n = 76) were individuals who were free of AS at the time of enrollment. Similar to Study I, we used PSM to balance across confounding variables (age, sex, history, laboratory and physical exam). Finally, 68 patients with AS and 68 normal controls were enrolled. According to the guidelines [ , ], severe aortic stenosis (AS) is defined by an aortic valve area (AVA) < 1.0 cm2 and/or a mean transaortic pressure gradient (MPG) > 40 mmHg and/or a peak aortic jet velocity (Vmax) > 4 m/s. Normal controls were defined as individuals with normal results of echocardiography, without stenosis of coronary artery confirmed by coronary CT or angiography. Participants were men or women aged at least 18 years. Standard exclusion criteria refer to Study I.
Study III, we obtained six heart tissues from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) patients with severe outflow track obstruction undergoing surgical myectomy/septal reduction therapy in Zhongshan Hospital of Fudan University. We obtained six cardiac tissues as the control group from non-failing donors which were not suitable for transplant. Cardiac HSP90α levels were examined by Western blot.

2.17

## Statistical analysis
For human study, the number of continuous cases met inclusion criteria during the study period determined the sample size. Normally distributed variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for human study. We analyzed these data including subgroup data about well-controlled or poor-controlled hypertension patients with the unpaired t-test for comparison between two groups. Non-normally distributed variables are expressed as median [interquartile range, IQR] and were analyzed by the Kruskal-Wallis test for comparison between two groups. We used percentages to represent categorical variables and the chi-square test to analyze these data. We use propensity score matching (PSM) analysis to adjust the potential baseline confounding variables. Matching covariates included gender, age, smoking, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and atrial fibrillation. PSM was performed with a tolerance of 0.05. The results of PSM were consistent with traditional regressions. For the relationship between serum HSP90α and echocardiographic parameters, we performed natural log transformation for HSP90α to yield normalization, and Pearson's correlation were used to assess the strength and direction of association between log (HSP90α) and per echocardiographic variables. We restricted analyses to patients with complete data on key variables required for a particular analysis. Patients with missing data (<5 %) were typical of the entire sample.
The results were expressed as mean ± standard error (SEM) for cell and animal studies. For multiple group analyses, we use one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni multiple comparison or two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc multiple comparisons test. We use an unpaired Student's t-test (unpaired, two-sided) to analyze the data for two groups. In each animal experiment, we randomly assigned C57BL/6 mice of the same age to different experimental groups. When P-value is less than 0.05, we think it is statistically significant.
In human studies, we used GraphPad Prism 8.3 and IBM SPSS 27.0 software analyses the data. We use GraphPad Prism 8.3 software to analyze cell and animal studies data.

## Results
3.1

## Elevated serum HSP90α levels positively correlate with myocardial hypertrophy in patients with hypertension or aortic stenosis (AS)
To examine the clinical relevance of serum HSP90α levels to cardiac hypertrophy induced by pressure overload, we first examined the serum HSP90α levels in patients with hypertension (HBP) and control group ( A). The basic characteristics of both groups are presented in . Systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP and DBP) in the hypertension group were higher than those in control group (SBP: 139.31 ± 20.62 vs 121.65 ± 11.40, p < 0.001; DBP: 80.84 ± 11.23 vs 76.07 ± 8.87, p < 0.001). Serum HSP90α levels (ng/mL) were elevated in patients with hypertension compared to those in the control group (11.47 ± 7.81 vs 5.56 ± 3.00, p < 0.001 ). To adjust the imbalance in age and BMI between the control and hypertension groups, we conducted propensity score matching (PSM) analysis to balance confounding variables (age, sex, BMI, heart rate, smoking, and medical history). After PSM, the study included 74 matched pairs from each group; differences between groups were effectively balanced, as shown in . Post-PSM analysis showed that serum HSP90α levels (ng/mL) remained higher in HBP patients than in the control group after PSM (8.48 ± 8.89 vs 5.22 ± 4.14, p = 0.005, B). Additionally, patients were categorized into four groups—normal, elevated, Stage 1, and Stage 2 according to the American College of Cardiology (ACC) and American Heart Association (AHA) released the diagnosis criteria for hypertension in 2017 [ ]. Normal: SBP<120 mmHg and DBP<80 mmHg; elevated:120 mmHg ≤ SBP<130 mmHg and DBP<80 mmHg; stage 1: 130 mmHg ≤ SBP<140 mmHg or 80 mmHg ≤ DBP<90 mmHg; stage 2: SBP≥140 mmHg or DBP≥90 mmHg. The results showed that serum HSP90α levels were progressively increasing across normal, elevated, Stage 1 and Stage 2 group C). 
Considering all the patients with HBP received anti-hypertensive treatment, we divided them into the well-controlled group and the poor-controlled group according to SBP and DBP. Well-controlled HBP: SBP <140 mmHg and DBP<90 mmHg; poorly controlled HBP group or SBP≥140 mmHg or DBP≥90 mmHg. Gender distribution was not balanced between these groups. PSM was used to adjust for confounding variables. There were no imbalanced confounding variables after PSM analysis ( ). Both before and after PSM, the HSP90α level was higher in the poor-controlled group than in the well-controlled group (before PSM, 14.26 ± 8.36 vs 8.54 ± 5.94, P < 0.001; after PSM, 12.10 ± 9.86 vs 8.97 ± 4.54; P = 0.014; ). 
To further explore whether serum HSP90α levels were increased by direct cardiac pressure overload, we compared serum HSP90α levels between control subjects (n = 76) and patients with serious aortic stenosis (AS) (n = 72). Basic characteristics of the two groups of patients are shown in . Serum HSP90α levels (ng/mL) were significantly higher in patients with AS than that in the control group (18.14 ± 6.32 vs 7.43 ± 4.25 vs P < 0.001; ). To adjust the imbalance in BMI between the control and AS groups, we performed PSM analysis to balance confounding variables ( ). Following PSM to adjust for BMI, serum HSP90α levels remained elevated in the AS group compared to controls (18.43 ± 6.27 vs 7.46 ± 4.41 P < 0.001; F). 
We then analyzed the relationship between serum HSP90α levels and echocardiographic parameters from patients of HBP or AS. HSP90α levels significantly correlated with the interventricular septal thickness (IVS) and left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPW) in HBP or AS patients ( E and G, and , ). However, no correlation was found with other echocardiographic parameters. 
These findings provide robust clinical evidence that the elevated serum HSP90α levels are associated with cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload.

3.2

## Pressure overload induces the increased expression and secretion of HSP90α from cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts
To explore the relationship between HSP90α level and cardiac hypertrophy, we analyzed the RNA-seq data (GSE120895) from dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) patients and the control left ventricular samples. The analysis revealed that mRNAs of HSP90α were significantly elevated in DCM group compared to control group ( ). We then detected the protein levels of HSP90α in heart tissue from patients of HCM and control subjects. Hypertrophy markers atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) were higher in HCM groups than those in control hearts. HSP90α expression was significantly enhanced in HCM hearts compared to control group ( A). Experimental pressure overload mouse model was constructed by TAC. Cardiac expression profiles of HSP90α were investigated at different time-points (3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks) after TAC. HSP90α expression progressively increased from 3 days to 4 weeks following TAC, compared to sham group ( B). In parallel, hypertrophic markers, ANP and BNP, or ERK activation were elevated from 3 days and remained high until 4 weeks after TAC compared to sham group ( B). These findings suggest that the increased HSP90α may play a role in cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload. 
To investigate the cellular source of cardiac HSP90α production under pressure overload, In vitro, we performed mechanical stretch (MS) to imitate pressure overload in vivo to detect HSP90α expression in cultured neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs), cardiac fibroblasts (NRCFs) and cardiac microvascular endothelial cells (CMECs) under MS. In NRCMs, MS induced the increased expressions of ANP, BNP, p-ERK from 3 h to 48 h compared to control group, indicating MS promotes cardiomyocyte hypertrophy ( ). HSP90α levels progressively increased in cell lysates or the conditional medium from 3 h to 48 h after MS ( C). In NRCFs, we observed a similar expression profile of HSP90α after MS ( D). However, HSP90α levels showed no change in CMECs under MS ( ). Furthermore, we isolated cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts from TAC 2-week mice (TAC-2W) and sham mice to detect HSP90α levels; HSP90α was increased in cardiomyocytes or cardiac fibroblasts from TAC-2W groups, compared to sham group ( E).
These findings suggest that the increased expression and secretion of HSP90α from cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts may be critical for cardiac hypertrophy induced by pressure overload.

3.3

## Knockdown or inhibition of HSP90α attenuates cardiac hypertrophy and improves heart failure under pressure overload
To observe the direct role of HSP90α on cardiac hypertrophy, we treated NRCMs with varying concentrations of exogenous rHSP90α (0–50 ng/ml). Exogenous rHSP90α enhanced p-ERK, ANP and BNP protein level, as well as ANP and BNP mRNA levels in a concentration-dependent manner in cultured NRCMs ( ). rHSP90α significantly increases the cell size of NRCMs in vitro ( ). Subsequently, we knocked HSP90α down in NRCMs by transfection of si-HSP90α to assess cardiac hypertrophy markers after MS. Si-HSP90α dramatically reduced HSP90α expression and secretion, and significantly attenuated the hypertrophic responses stimulated with MS ( A, S3D-E). 
To assess the roles of the elevated HSP90α in cardiac hypertrophy during pressure overload, we decreased cardiac HSP90α levels by injection of sh-HSP90α-AAV9 in mice two weeks prior to sham or TAC operation ( B). The reduced expression of HSP90α was shown in heart tissue or serum from sham or TAC mice pre-injected with sh-HSP90α-AAV9 ( ). In cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts isolated from TAC mice, sh-HSP90α-AAV9 injection significantly suppressed HSP90α levels compared to sh–NC–AAV9 group ( ). Echocardiographic data indicated the lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) or fraction shortening (FS) at four weeks after TAC than those in sham group, these effects were significantly mitigated by sh-HSP90α-AAV9 injection in TAC mice ( C). TAC induced cardiac hypertrophy evidenced by the increased heart size, heart weight/body weight ratio (HW/BW), heart weight/tibia length ratio (HW/TL), cross section area (CSA), were substantially reduced by sh-HSP90α-AAV9 injection ( D and E). Consistently, knockdown of HSP90α attenuated the increased expression of hypertrophic markers (ANP, BNP and p-ERK) induced by TAC ( F). Additionally, sh-HSP90α-AAV9 injection ameliorated fibrosis mediated by TAC ( ). Increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production has been implicated in the process of cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis [ , ]. We also observed that sh-HSP90α significantly suppressed the increase in ROS level induced by TAC ( ). There is no obvious difference in these parameters between sh-HSP90α-AAV9 and sh–NC–AAV9 treatment in sham group. These data indicated that knockdown of HSP90α greatly attenuated cardiac remodeling and dysfunction induced by pressure overload.
To explore whether inhibition of HSP90α could mitigate cardiac hypertrophy and prevent maladaptive process in TAC mice, we treated mice with 17-DMAG, an HSP90 inhibitor, from 2 weeks to 4 weeks post-TAC ( ). The inhibitor greatly improved cardiac dysfunction ( ), attenuated cardiac hypertrophy, and inhibited the increased hypertrophic markers mediated by TAC ( ). Similar results were observed in stretched cardiomyocytes ( ).
These findings underscore the critical role of elevated cardiac HSP90α levels in driving cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload.

3.4

## Blockage of extracellular HSP90α suppresses cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload
To explore whether cardiac secreted or extracellular HSP90α (eHSP90α) promotes cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload, we utilized a specific monoclonal anti-HSP90α antibody, 1G6-D7, which only blocks the effects of eHSP90α [ ]. In vitro, 1G6-D7 significantly suppressed the hypertrophic responses evidenced by the decreased expression of ANP, BNP and p-ERK, while it didn't affect the elevation of HSP90α in conditional medium or cell lysates of NRCMs in response to MS ( A-B). 
We further validated these effects in vivo. 1G6-D7 was administered to mice by minipump for two weeks after TAC or sham operation ( C). As expected, TAC induced decreased LVEF and FS at four weeks indicating cardiac dysfunction. 1G6-D7 treatment greatly relieved these effects ( D). Similarly, 1G6-D7 treatment significantly attenuated cardiac hypertrophy indicated by the reduced heart size, HW/BW, HW/TL, LVPW or CSA in TAC hearts ( E and F). 1G6-D7 treatment also inhibited the increased expression of ANP, BNP and p-ERK, while the treatment didn't affect the elevation of HSP90α in serum or heart tissue from TAC mice ( G and H). We also observed 1G6-D7 infusion attenuated cardiac fibrosis at 4 weeks after TAC (data not shown). Similarly, 1G6-D7 infusion greatly reduced ROS level at 4 weeks after TAC ( ).
These findings suggest that inhibiting eHSP90 improves cardiac function and remodeling after TAC. In the present study, we focused on elucidating the potential mechanisms by which HSP90α promotes cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload.

3.5

## Secreted HSP90α activates β-catenin/TCF7 to promote cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload
To elucidate the potential mechanisms, we analyzed pathways involved in HSP90 (GSE12287) or cardiac hypertrophy (GSE32453 in HCM) via GEO database. We identified an overlap with the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway ( A). Activation of β-catenin is known to contribute to cardiac hypertrophy induced by pressure overload [ , ]. Our recent study showed TAC facilitated the activation of β-catenin in heart tissue [ ]. As anticipated, MS or rHSP90α directly induced the activation of β-catenin in NRCMs ( ). Inhibition of HSP90α or 1G6-D7 antibody treatment markedly inhibited β-catenin activation in TAC hearts or stretched-NRCMs ( , 5B-E). Mechanically, the β-catenin inhibitor, tegatrabetan, significantly suppressed cardiac hypertrophy and improved cardiac function infused with rHSP90α ( F and G). Similarly, tegatrabetan inhibited the hypertrophic response in cultured NRCMs induced by rHSP90α or MS ( I– ). β-catenin agonist, SKL2001, abolished si-HSP90α-mediated-inhibition of hypertrophic responses in stretched-NRCMs ( J). Neither tegatrabetan nor SKL2001 affected the levels of HSP90α in conditional medium or cell lysates of cultured NRCMs under MS ( ). These data demonstrated that eHSP90α promotes cardiac hypertrophy by activation of β-catenin under pressure overload. 
Active β-catenin enters nucleus and binds to T-cell factor (TCF) and lymphoid enhancer factor (LEF) family transcriptional factors (LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1, TCF7L2, LEF1), promoting the transcription of Wnt/β-catenin target genes [ , ]. Knockdown of HSP90α significantly reduced nuclear β-catenin levels in heart tissue from TAC mice ( ). Similarly, the mRNAs of ANP and BNP were suppressed by knockdown of HSP90α in TAC mice ( A). β-catenin inhibitor, tegatrabetan, attenuated the increased the mRNA levels of hypertrophic genes ANP and BNP in HSP90α-treated NRCMs ( B), while β-catenin agonist, SKL2001, enhanced their transcription ( C). To explore the critical transcriptional factors from TCF/LEF family in mediating the expression of hypertrophic genes in HSP90α treated-NRCMs, we analyzed Jaspar database and identified that TCF7 (also named TCF1) as a critical transcription factor interacting with promoter regions of ANP and BNP ( ). ChIP-qPCR confirmed that rHSP90α promoted the binding of TCF7 and ANP or BNP respectively ( D and E), while β-catenin inhibitor attenuated the interaction significantly ( F). In vivo, TAC induced the increased expression of TCF7, which was inhibited by 1G6-D7 treatment ( G). In cultured NRCMs, rHSP90α enhanced TCF7 mRNA and protein level, and si-TCF7 greatly attenuated the increased expression of ANP and BNP without affecting ERK activation mediated by rHSP90α ( H and I). An ERK specific inhibitor, SCH772984, partly abolished the increase in TCF7 expression mediated by HSP90α in NRCMs ( J), suggesting eHSP90α increased the expression of TCF7 via ERK signaling. 
These findings demonstrate that cardiac-secreted HSP90α promotes cardiac hypertrophy by activating the β-catenin/TCF7 signaling axis under pressure overload.

3.6

## Secreted HSP90α interacts with N-cadherin to activate β-catenin signaling in cardiomyocytes under pressure overload
To identify whether membrane receptors mediate eHSP90α-induced the activation of β-catenin/TCF7 in cardiomyocytes, we performed Nano-HPLC-MS/MS analysis of membrane proteins pull down from HCM hearts with human HSP90α antibody, with an IgG antibody serving as a negative control ( A). The data revealed that 79 membrane proteins are associated with HSP90α( ). Among these membrane proteins, we focused on N-cadherin (cadherin 2, CDH2) ( B), which regulates β-catenin activation [ , , ] and interacts with β-catenin according to STRING Database analysis ( C). 
To gain insight into the direct interaction of HSP90α and N-cadherin in activation of β-catenin mediated by HSP90α. We transfected N-cadherin plasmid into Cos7 cells and treated with rHSP90α, observed that HSP90α interacted with N-cadherin ( D). We assessed the transcriptional activity of T-cell factor (TCF)/lymphoid enhancer factor (LEF) using a TOPflash luciferase reporter assay, which indicated higher transcriptional activity in the N-cadherin group compared to the control group following rHSP90α treatment ( E). Proximity ligation assay (PLA) revealed that there is a strong direct interaction between HSP90α and N-cadherin in HSP90α treated-cardiomyocytes ( F). IP analysis indicated that the association of HSP90α with N-cadherin was stronger, while the interaction of N-cadherin with β-catenin was weaker, the binding of β-catenin to TCF7 was more pronounced in rHSP90α-treated- or stretched-NRCMs than those in control group ( ), and a similar profile in TAC hearts and HCM hearts, compared to their respective control groups ( G, ). In TAC hearts, PLA analysis confirmed these effects, and more co-localization between HSP90α and N-cadherin or the less co-localization between N-cadherin and β-catenin, were observed in TAC hearts than that in sham hearts ( H–J). 1G6-D7 treatment greatly inhibited these effects in TAC hearts or stretched NRCMs ( ). These findings suggest that eHSP90α binds to N-cadherin to induce β-catenin activation in cardiomyocytes under pressure overload.
These findings suggests that eHSP90α induces the increased expression of N-cadherin and acts on N-cadherin to promote the release of β-catenin from N-cadherin, which then binds to TCF7 in the nucleus, subsequently enhancing the transcription of hypertrophic genes under pressure overload.

3.7

## N-cadherin is critical for cardiac hypertrophy mediated by secreted HSP90α
To verify the effects of N-cadherin in eHSP90α-induced β-catenin activation and cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, we transfected si-N-cadherin into NRCMs treated with or without HSP90α, respectively. Si–N-cadherin effectively reduced the expression of N-cadherin in NRCMs, and inhibited the increase in active β-catenin, TCF7, p-ERK, ANP and BNP levels in HSP90α-treated-cardiomyocytes ( A). Similarly, si-N-cadherin attenuated the increased cell area in HSP90α-treated-cardiomyocytes ( B). 
To further determine the roles of N-cadherin in the HSP90α mediated-myocardial hypertrophy in vivo, we administered shRNA-N-cadherin-AAV9 (sh-N-cadherin) to C57BL/6 mice two weeks before a chronic infusion of rHSP90α or PBS via a minipump ( C). ShRNA-scramble-AAV9 (sh-scramble) injection was as control. Sh-N-cadherin injection greatly improved cardiac function indicated by the increased EF and FS in mice infused with rHSP90α ( D). Similarly, shRNA-N-cadherin treatment significantly attenuated cardiac hypertrophy indicated by the reduced heart size, HW/BW, HW/TL, and CSA compared with sh-scramble group infused by rHSP90α ( E and F). Additionally, shRNA-N-cadherin treatment inhibited the increased expression of hypertrophic markers or signal, ANP, BNP, and p-ERK, as well as the level of active-β-catenin and TCF7 but didn't affect the elevation of HSP90α ( ). These data suggested the increased eHSP90α interacts with N-cadherin to activate β-catenin and hypertrophic responses under pressure overload.
To further understand the molecular domains of N-cadherin responsible for interaction with eHSP90α to activate β-catenin signaling, we constructed and transfected truncated mutants of N-cadherin including N-cadherin del160-724(ΔEC1-EC5, extracellular domain of N-cadherin) and N-cadherin del160-382 (ΔEC1-EC2) as well as full-length N-cadherin for TOPFlash assays in Cos7 cells, respectively ( A). Western blot analysis confirmed the expressions of truncated mutants of N-cadherin in Cos7 cells ( B). Exogenous HSP90α induced a sharply increased β-catenin activation in full-length N-cadherin transfection group, and these effects were almost abolished in N-cadherin del160-724 or N-cadherin del160-382 transfection group ( C). There is no obvious difference in the activation of β-catenin between N-cadherin del160-724 and N-cadherin del160-382 transfection group. This data indicated that secreted HSP90α binds with the 160–382 domain of N-cadherin to activate β-catenin and cardiac hypertrophy. 

4

## Discussion
We first demonstrated the elevated serum HSP90α levels in patients with HBP or AS, compared with control group, and the levels positively correlate with cardiac hypertrophy in these patients. In mice, pressure overload promotes the expression and secretion of HSP90α from cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts, and the increased cardiac secreted-HSP90α exaggerates myocardial hypertrophy by N-cadherin/β-catenin/TCF7 axis, resulting in heart failure under pressure overload. A schematic illustration of our findings is in D. The present study has identified a novel molecular mechanism linking cardiac secreted-HSP90α with pathological cardiac remodeling under pressure overload.
HSP90α is an essential intracellular molecular chaperone protein involved in various cellular processes [ ]. Accumulating evidence revealed that HSP90α localizes extracellularly, where it facilitates tumor migration and invasion, lymphatic metastasis, wound healing or pulmonary fibrosis [ , , , ]. Katarzyna E reported that a significant increase in serum HSP90α level in overweight or obese patients with newly diagnosed arterial hypertension [ ], but few studies explored the critical roles of circulation HSP90α in cardiac remodeling induced by chronic pressure overload such as hypertension or AS. Our data showed serum HSP90α levels are increased in patients with hypertension or AS compared with their control group, we also observed that serum HSP90α elevates with the increased in blood pressure in patients with HBP and AS. It indicated that the elevation of serum HSP90α levels positively correlate with the degree of pressure overload. In animal experiments, serum or cardiac HSP90α levels increase in time-dependent manner in chronic pressure overload mouse model, and in vitro, the expression and secretion of HSP90α from cardiomyocytes or cardiac fibroblasts are enhanced in response to mechanical load. We observed exogenous HSP90α directly promotes cardiomyocytes hypertrophy, knockdown of HSP90α or blockage of extracellular HSP90α (eHSP90) attenuated cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis under pressure overload. Experimental evidences showed that HSP90 promotes cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis after a myocardial infarction [ ], under pressure overload [ ] or in hypertensive rats [ ]. In the present study, treatment of a HSP90 inhibitor greatly prevented the development of maladaptive cardiac hypertrophy treatment at 4 weeks after TAC. Several studies demonstrated that HSP90 promotes cardiac fibrosis by calcineurin-NFAT and c-Raf-Erk pathways or TGFβ/SMAD3 signaling [ , ]. S-nitrosylation of HSP90 in nucleus mediates cardiac hypertrophy through GSK3β signaling in mice [ ]. But the detailed mechanisms about secreted HSP90α induced-cardiac hypertrophy are largely unknown.
The activation of β-catenin has been involved in cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload [ ]. In small-cell lung cancer cells, secreted HSP90α activates β-catenin signaling by AKT/GSK3β to attenuate the efficacy of anticancer drugs [ ]. In canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling, Wnt ligands bind to LRP5/6 and frizzled receptors (FZDs), prompt the phosphorylation of LRP6 at Ser1490 and then blocks GSK3β kinase activity, thus abrogating GSK3β-mediated β-catenin phosphorylation and proteasomal degradation, resulting in its cytoplasmic accumulation and subsequent nuclear translocation. Although Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway has been implicated in myocardial remodeling [ ], our data showed that HSP90α directly activated β-catenin to induce cardiac hypertrophy under pressure overload. Nuclear β-catenin associates with TCF/Lef family (DNA binding transcription factors) to activate Wnt-β-catenin-target genes and then execute various cell function [ , ]. We performed JASPAR database analysis, to find TCF7 (also named TCF1, one of TCF/Lef family members) was the most likely transcription factor for hypertrophic genes ANP and BNP, and then verified secreted HSP90α promoted interaction of TCF7 and the promoter regions of ANP and BNP by activation of β-catenin to induce cardiac hypertrophy.
In the present study, we found-by Nano-HPLC-MS/MS analysis then verified secreted HSP90α binds N-cadherin in cardiomyocytes membrane to promote cardiac hypertrophy by activation of β-catenin/TCF7 under pressure overload. N-cadherin, one of cadherins, is a single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein that is critical for cell-cell adhesion. Most of β-catenin is bound through its N-terminal domains to cytoplasmic tail of cadherins, linking them to the actin cytoskeleton, and therefore securing the formation of cell–cell adhesion complexes [ ], which is required to maintain structural integrity of intercalated disc between the cardiomyocytes [ ]. Previous studies reported by other investigators have also consistently shown N-cadherin enhances β-catenin-mediated MMP-10/MMP-13/HGF expression and production from mesenchymal stromal cells to protect heart against ischemic cardiac injury [ ]. Downregulation or inhibition of N-cadherin reduces the activation of β-catenin signaling in neuronal differentiation and cell survival [ ]. Urokinase-type plasminogen activator (uPA), triggers the detachment of β-catenin from the intracellular domain of N-cadherin, resulting in the nuclear translocation of β-catenin, which contributes to astrocytic wound healing [ ]. The present study revealed that the extracellular region (160-382aa) of N-cadherin is critical for binding to secreted HSP90α in cardiomyocytes. These results jointly suggest secreted HSP90α binds to N-cadherin, promotes the release of β-catenin from N-cadherin, and causes nuclear translocation of β-catenin and the enhanced interaction of β-catenin and TCF7, contributing to cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure under pressure overload.
In addition, we couldn't exclude other molecular mechanisms involved in cardiac hypertrophy mediated by HSP90α under pressure overload. Mechanical stress initiates ROS production, and redox signaling dysfunction has been involved in hypertrophy response or other pathological process [ , ]. Ikwegbue PC et al. reported that the generation of ROS triggers the release of HSPs including HSP90α, in human inflammatory diseases, and cancer [ ]. It is possible that pressure overload enhances the expression and secretion of HSP90α by ROS signaling. HSP90 has been involved in the release of ROS by degradation of GPX4 in non-small-cell lung cancer ferroptosis [ ]. Our data indicated that knockdown or blockage of HSP90α significantly attenuated ROS levels under pressure overload. But downregulation of N-cadherin didn't affect the increased ROS levels under pressure overload (data not shown). Therefore, we speculated that pressure overload promotes the expression and secretion of HSP90α by ROS signaling, and HSP90α enhances the production of ROS, which promotes cardiac hypertrophy independent of N-cadherin signaling.
Together with the above, our findings strongly suggested that HSP90α/N-cadherin axis could be the potential target for cardiac remodeling modulation for pressure overload or other cardiovascular diseases.

5

## Conclusions
Our findings demonstrated the elevated serum HSP90α levels positively correlate with cardiac hypertrophy in patients under hypertension or AS. The chronic pressure overload triggers cardiac myocytes or fibroblasts to secrete HSP90α, which in turn stimulates cardiomyocytes hypertrophy by activation of β-catenin/TCF7 signaling through N-cadherin-mediated signaling. Intervention of secreted HSP90α or N-cadherin signaling may be a new therapeutic strategy for cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure induced by pressure overload.
