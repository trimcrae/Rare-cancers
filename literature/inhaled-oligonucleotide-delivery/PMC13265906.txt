## Summary
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is characterized by alveolar injury and pathological fibroblast expansion. Our study investigated the aberrant intercellular communication underlying this process. By analyzing single-cell sequencing from GEO database and experimentally validating the results in vivo and in vitro, we demonstrated that the abnormally regulated alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells secrete midkine (MDK) to activate the low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1) receptor in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, which inhibits fibroblast ferroptosis. Mechanistically, LRP1 signaling upregulates the deubiquitinase OTUB1, which binds to and stabilizes the ferroptosis inhibitor SLC7A11. This molecular pathway was verified in an in vitro cell co-culture model and in vivo mouse models subjected to adenovirus-mediated overexpression and knockdown. Collectively, these results elucidate a novel pathway of disease progression in IPF that could potentially be targeted for the diagnosis or intervention.

## Introduction
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic fibrosing interstitial disease of unknown cause, characterized by an irreversible decrease in alveolar cells and a progressive increase in fibroblasts. During the pathological process of IPF, alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells fail to differentiate into AT1 cells, resulting in decreased alveolar numbers, and structural abnormalities. , The uncontrollable accumulation of fibroblasts and collagen impacts the function of AT2 and AT1 cells, eventually leading to severe lung function damage, respiratory failure, and death. , Previous studies have identified a unique population of collagen triple helix repeat-containing 1+ (CTHRC1+) fibroblasts, primarily found in fibrotic lungs, that play an indispensable role in IPF development. , , Single-cell analysis has confirmed that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts are more migratory than other collagen-producing fibroblast subtypes. , , Furthermore, abnormal AT2 cells typically accumulate near fibroblast foci in IPF, indicating a potential interaction between fibroblasts and epithelial cells. , However, the underlying mechanism of the interaction between CTHRC1+ fibroblasts and AT2 cells remains unknown.
Ferroptosis is a novel type of programmed cell death characterized by lipid peroxidase-dependent accumulation. Recently, direct induction or secondary regulation by targeting ferroptosis-associated proteins has emerged as a practical therapeutic approach for modulating various disease processes, including lung injury and cell death. Previous studies have demonstrated that miR-125b-5p alleviates pulmonary microvascular endothelial cell ferroptosis via the KEAP1-NRF2-GPX4 pathway in sepsis lung injury. STAT6, which competitively binds to CREB-binding protein, inhibits p53 acetylation and transcriptionally restores SLC7A11 expression, has been demonstrated to negatively regulate ferroptosis and attenuate crystalline silica- and LPS-induced acute lung injury. Thus, a better understanding of the cellular and molecular mechanisms of ferroptosis in IPF progression is urgently needed.
The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) modulates the function of key factors in ferroptosis, mainly by regulating the ubiquitination level of crucial proteins, thus influencing their stability. Previous studies have shown that otubain1 (OTUB1), a member of the OTU domain family of deubiquitinating enzymes, improves osteogenic differentiation and mineralization in bone homeostasis by stabilizing FGFR2. However, the function of deubiquitinating enzymes and their effect on ferroptosis in IPF remains unknown.
In this study, by reanalyzing single-cell sequence datasets, we demonstrated that IPF leads to abnormal AT2 cell proliferation and elevates MDK secretion, which activates the LRP1 receptor in fibroblasts in humans and mice. OTUB1 is upregulated by the stimulation of LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts. Deubiquitylation modification of OTUB1 subsequently stabilizes the expression level of SLC7A11, resulting in a decrease in fibrotic ferroptosis and worsening progression of lung fibrosis.

## Results

## Dysfunctional AT2 cells and abnormally increased CTHRC1+ fibroblasts are detected in advanced idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
To explore the potential mechanism of IPF, lung tissues of end-stage IPF patients and non-neoplastic adjacent tissues of lung cancer were extracted. Simultaneously, a mouse pulmonary fibrosis model was established by dripping bleomycin (BLM) into the airway. In both the human IPF and mouse BLM groups, H&E staining showed that the alveolar structure was severely destroyed and the lung interstitium was thickened ( A). Furthermore, Masson staining demonstrated abundant deposition of collagen ( B; A), and the lung hydroxyproline levels and Ashcroft score were increased in the IPF and BLM groups compared with normal lung tissues from humans or mice ( C–1E). , Interestingly, AT1 cells (PDPN+) in the IPF and BLM groups were significantly downregulated around fibroblast areas with abnormal collagen deposition. However, AT2 cells (SFTPC+) and aberrant epithelial cells (KRT17+) were clustered around fibroblast foci (CTHRC1+) ( F and 1G; B). A gradual decrease in AT1 cells was observed from day 7 to day 21 in mice, while AT2 cells decreased at day 7 but were slightly restored at days 14 and 21 around the fibroblast foci, indicating that the differentiation from AT2 cells into AT1 cells was inhibited. CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, defined as collagen producing fibroblasts, also were increased in mouse lung tissues ( C). As shown in D, CTHRC1 protein levels were significantly elevated in IPF lung tissues compared to healthy controls. In mouse BLM model, the protein expression levels of CTHRC1 increase as the duration of the model progresses. IHC staining verified the upregulation of CTHRC1+ fibroblasts and downregulation of AT1 cells in the disease group for both humans and mice ( E). Overall, these results indicate that the dysfunction of AT2 cells and abnormally increased fibroblasts are associated with IPF status. 

## CTHRC1+ fibroblasts maintain lower ferroptosis levels by suppressing oxidative stress
To evaluate the mechanisms of CTHRC1+ fibroblasts in IPF, we downloaded and reanalyzed the results of single-cell sequencing (GEO: GSE135893 and GSE227136) through the GEO public database ( A). , Cell types were identified according to cell markers ( B and 2C; A), and the proportions of each type of cell in pulmonary fibrosis were analyzed by disease status ( D). Next, we extracted the fibroblasts and identified the CTHRC1+ sub-fibroblast cell type as previously described. The results showed that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts were significantly enriched in IPF lung tissues ( E and 2F). GSEA analysis of the differentially expressed genes in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts further demonstrated that GO terms for lipid peroxidation were highly enriched, revealing increased accumulation and decreased metabolism of lipid peroxidation in the IPF group compared to the control group ( G). We extracted primary lung fibroblasts from IPF lung tissues and non-neoplastic adjacent tissues of lung cancer to validate the lipid peroxidation level in fibroblasts. Moreover, the ferroptosis inducer erastin was added to IPF-group fibroblasts to verify whether the differences in fibroblasts between the two groups were due to ferroptosis. Flow cytometry and fluorescence confocal microscopy using the BIODIPY C11 probe verified that lipid peroxidation levels were lower in fibroblasts of the IPF group ( H and 2I; B). Additionally, the MDA level was reduced while the GSH/GSSG ratio was increased in the IPF group, which suggests that fibroblasts of the IPF group were subjected to a lower level of oxidative stress and ferroptosis ( C). Consistently, in fibroblasts of the IPF group, the ferroptosis suppressor genes SCL7A11, FTH1, and GPX4 were elevated, and the ferroptosis-promoting gene ACSL4 was downregulated ( J; D). The addition of erastin to IPF fibroblasts reversed the accumulation of lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis levels. Consequently, we hypothesize that the excessive proliferation of fibroblasts in IPF is strongly correlated with their ferroptosis levels ( H–2J; B–S2D). To investigate the involvement of ferroptosis in pulmonary fibrosis under classical pro-fibrotic conditions, normal human primary lung fibroblasts were pretreated with bleomycin (BLM) for 48 h, followed by exposure to the ferroptosis inducer erastin with or without the specific ferroptosis inhibitor liproxstatin-1 (Lip-1). As shown in E and S2F, pretreatment of normal human lung fibroblasts with BLM increased SLC7A11 and GPX4 protein levels and reduced basal lipid peroxidation. After erastin challenge, BLM-pretreated fibroblasts displayed attenuated lipid peroxidation compared with control cells, and this protection was reversed by the ferroptosis inhibitor Lip-1. These findings confirm that BLM-induced pro-fibrotic activation confers ferroptosis resistance. To further evaluate the function of CTHRC1 in ferroptosis, we transfected fibroblasts with CTHRC1 overexpression plasmid using lentiviral vectors ( A). In the CTHRC1-OE group, the ferroptosis suppressor genes were elevated and the ferroptosis-promoting gene was downregulated ( B). Additionally, the lipid peroxidation level was lower ( C), the MDA level was reduced, and the GSH/GSSG ratio was increased in the CTHRC1-OE group as compared to the control group ( D). As a control, the ferroptosis level in the CTHRC1-OE group was significantly maintained by the ferroptosis inducer erastin ( A–S3D). To validate the causal role of CTHRC1 in ferroptosis resistance, we performed siRNA-mediated knockdown of CTHRC1 in human IPF fibroblasts. Efficient knockdown of CTHRC1 protein was confirmed by qRT-PCR and western blotting ( E and S3F). CTHRC1-deficient fibroblasts exhibited significantly reduced SLC7A11 and GPX4 protein levels ( F), along with elevated lipid peroxidation at baseline ( G), decreased GSH/GSSG ratio, and increased MDA levels ( H). Importantly, the increased lipid peroxidation induced by CTHRC1 knockdown was completely reversed by the ferroptosis inhibitor Lip-1 ( G), confirming ferroptosis specificity. These results provide causal evidence that CTHRC1 expression protects fibroblasts from ferroptosis. Collectively, these results suggest that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts maintain lower ferroptosis levels by suppressing oxidative stress. 

## AT2 cells secrete MDK to activate LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts and inhibit fibrotic ferroptosis
To further investigate the relationship between AT2 cells and CTHRC1+ fibroblasts in IPF, we analyzed their interactions and mapped differentially expressed genes in both cell populations in two GEO datasets ( A; A and S4B). The results demonstrate that the MDK-LRP1 receptor-ligand pathway is activated in IPF. To further investigate the interaction, we performed PCR and western blotting to verify the expression levels of MDK and LRP1 in primary AT2 and fibroblasts isolated from IPF lung tissues and non-neoplastic adjacent lung cancer tissues. The results showed that the expression levels of MDK in AT2 cells and LRP1 in fibroblasts were significantly elevated in the IPF group ( B; C). Given the central role of MDK in the AT2-fibroblast crosstalk, we explored whether circulating MDK levels could serve as a translational biomarker for IPF. Plasma samples were collected from IPF patients (n = 20) and healthy controls (n = 20). As shown in D, ELISA analysis revealed significantly elevated plasma MDK levels in IPF patients compared with healthy controls. Moreover, plasma MDK levels exhibited a significant negative correlation with diffusion lung capacity for CO (DLCO) % predicted (R2 = 0,7985, p < 0.001, E), suggesting that higher MDK concentrations are associated with more severe pulmonary function impairment. To further validate this observation, we measured plasma MDK in a mouse model of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis at different time points. Plasma MDK levels increased progressively from week 1 to week 3 post-BLM, paralleling the upregulation of MDK expression in lung tissue ( F). Importantly, a strong positive correlation was observed between plasma MDK levels and Ashcroft fibrosis scores in BLM-treated mice (R2 = 0.7379, p < 0.001, G), indicating that plasma MDK reflects the severity of fibrotic remodeling. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that circulating MDK is elevated in IPF and correlates with disease severity, supporting its potential utility as a non-invasive biomarker for IPF. To obtain additional insight into the roles of MDK and LRP1 in IPF, we isolated AT2 cells from the normal group and co-cultured them with CTHRC1+ fibroblasts ( C). Western blotting showed that upon either knockdown of MDK in AT2 cells or knockdown of LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, the ferroptosis suppressor genes were downregulated and the ferroptosis promotor gene was elevated in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, whereas the opposite result was observed after MDK overexpression in AT2 cells or LRP1 overexpression in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts ( D; A). Furthermore, knockdown of MDK in AT2 cells or LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts resulted in increased accumulation of lipid peroxidation ( E and 3F; B), increased MDA levels, and a decrease in the GSH/GSSG ratio in fibroblasts ( C). To further validate that the interaction between MDK and LRP1 affects ferroptosis in fibroblasts, we added the ferroptosis inhibitor liproxstatin-1 (Lip-1) to observe whether a rescue effect occurred. The AT2 and fibroblasts co-culture model was employed after knocking down MDK in AT2 cells or knocking down LRP1 in fibroblasts. Compared with observations in the knockdown groups, Lip-1 addition in fibroblasts significantly restored ferroptosis suppressor protein levels and reduced lipid peroxidation levels. These findings indicate that Lip-1 can rescue the increased ferroptosis levels caused by knocking out either MDK or LRP1, confirming that the MDK-LRP1 interaction primarily governs cellular fate by influencing ferroptosis in fibroblasts ( G–3I; D–S5F). Notably, co-IP assays indicated a direct binding relationship between LRP1 and MDK ( J). Laser confocal microscopy further confirmed that LRP1 and MDK exhibit partial subcellular co-localization in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, providing evidence that MDK may directly bind to LRP1 ( K). These results suggest that AT2 cells secrete MDK and activate LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, providing a mechanism for inhibiting fibrotic ferroptosis in IPF. 

## LRP1 knockdown in fibroblasts reverses MDK-induced pulmonary fibrosis and ferroptosis in vivo
To verify the in vivo role of MDK-LRP1, we prepared cell-type-specific adeno-associated viruses and administered them to BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis mice by tracheal dripping to overexpress MDK in AT2 cells or knock down LRP1 in fibroblasts. First, after infecting mice with AAV9 vectors expressing mCHERRY-SP-CP targeting AT2 cells and GFP-mCOL1A2 targeting fibroblasts, we stained for the AT2 cell marker SFTPC and the fibroblast marker CTHRC1. The Pearson correlation coefficient of mCHERRY-SP-CP and SFTPC was 0.75, and that of GFP-mCOL1A2 and CTHRC1 was 0.72, confirming the specificity of the AAV viral constructs ( A). To further validate the cellular specificity of MDK and LRP1, we employed mIHC techniques to label MDK, LRP1, AT2 cells, and CTHRC1 cells within tissue. SFTPC exhibited a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.86 with MDK, while LRP1 demonstrated a coefficient of 0.91 with CTHRC1. This finding confirms the cell-specific localization of MDK and LRP1 within tissues ( B). H&E staining, Masson’s staining, hydroxyproline levels and Ashcroft scores demonstrated that overexpression of MDK significantly escalated the fibrosis; however, simultaneous LRP1 knockdown reversed the progress of fibrosis, supporting the in vivo interaction of MDK-LRP1 ( A–4D; C). We further demonstrated that in vivo LRP1 knockdown resulted in a significant decrease in the ferroptosis suppressor proteins and an increase in the ferroptosis promoter protein, regardless of MDK overexpression in AT2 cells ( E; D). Additionally, overexpression of MDK resulted in decreased lipid peroxidation ( F and 4G; E), decreased MDA levels, and an increased GSH/GSSG ratio in fibroblasts ( H), and these changes were suppressed by LRP1 knockdown. These rescue experiments demonstrate that MDK-secreted AT2 cells and the LRP1 receptor in fibroblasts functionally interact in vivo, thereby regulating the ferroptosis levels in a BLM mouse model. 

## LRP1 upregulates OTUB1 to reduce ferroptosis in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts
To further investigate the molecular mechanisms of LRP1-dependent ferroptosis, we stimulated CTHRC1+ fibroblasts using different concentrations of MDK protein. The activation level of LRP1 peaked at 15 μg/mL MDK ( A). To assess whether MDK functionally activates LRP1, we examined ligand-induced internalization and downstream signaling. Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that MDK stimulation (100 ng/mL, 12 h) induced significant co-localization of LRP1 with the early endosome marker EEA1 (Pearson’s R value = 0.71), indicating receptor internalization ( A). Moreover, MDK rapidly induced phosphorylation of AKT and p38 MAPK, which was abolished by the LRP1 antagonist RAP ( B). These results confirm that MDK activates LRP1, triggering both receptor endocytosis and downstream signaling cascades. Consequently, we performed RNA-seq of 15 μg/mL MDK-stimulated versus PBS-control stimulated CTHRC1+ fibroblasts. In the RNA-seq results, the volcano plot and heatmap analyses revealed differentially expressed genes between the two groups ( B and 5C). Among the differentially expressed genes identified via RNA-seq analysis, we observed a significant increase in the expression levels of genes such as PPARG, SREBF1, SREBF2, and NR2F2 within the MDK-stimulation group. Previous studies have demonstrated that these genes may be responsible for the elevated LRP1 expression levels observed following MDK stimulation ( D). Furthermore, GO analysis suggested that ubiquitylation modification and lipid oxidation pathways are differentially activated upon MDK-stimulated and LRP1-mediated ferroptosis in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts ( E). By validating the expression levels of the top 5 differentially expressed genes in both cells and lung tissues, we verified that OTUB1 was stably upregulated upon MDK-mediated activation of LRP1 ( C). Consistently, knockdown of LRP1 in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts led to a significant reduction in the expression of OTUB1, as evaluated by western blotting and immunofluorescence assay ( F and 5G). Additionally, the expression of the ferroptosis suppressor gene SLC7A11 decreased significantly after OTUB1 silencing, which suggests that OTUB1 may regulate ferroptosis through regulation of SLC7A11 ( H; D–S7E). Flow cytometry and fluorescence confocal microscopy verified that the lipid peroxidation levels of CTHRC1+ fibroblasts were significantly elevated after OTUB1 knockdown ( J; F). Furthermore, MDA and GSH assays demonstrated that the MDA level increased and the GSH/GSSG ratio declined upon OTUB1 knockdown ( I). To further identify whether transcription factors mediating LRP1-induced OTUB1 upregulation, we analyzed the OTUB1 promoter region (−1500 to +100 bp relative to the transcription start site) using the UCSC Genome Browser and JASPAR database. This analysis identified high-confidence (relative score >0.9) putative binding sites for NR2F2 and SREBF2 ( G). To test whether these transcription factors directly regulate OTUB1 transcription, we generated wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) OTUB1 promoter luciferase reporter constructs, in which the predicted NR2F2 or SREBF2 binding sites were mutated. Dual-luciferase assays were performed in 293T cells co-transfected with the reporter constructs and either NR2F2 or SREBF2 overexpression plasmids. As shown in G and S7I, overexpression of NR2F2 or SREBF2 significantly increased OTUB1 promoter activity compared with the vector control. Mutation of the NR2F2 binding site ( H) or the SREBF2 binding site ( I) completely abolished the transactivation effect induced by the respective transcription factor, confirming specific and direct binding. Collectively, these results identify NR2F2 and SREBF2 as direct transcriptional activators of OTUB1 downstream of LRP1 signaling and may contribute to the OTUB1 upregulation. Collectively, these results suggest that MDK activation of LRP1 regulates ferroptosis in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts by upregulating OTUB1 expression. 

## OTUB1 binds SLC7A11 and stabilizes its expression level
To explore the regulatory relationship between OTUB1 and SLC7A11, we evaluated their potential binding interaction by molecular docking ( A). Co-IP experiments validated the direct binding relationship between OTUB1 and SCL7A11 both in human primary lung fibroblasts and 293T cell line ( B). Co-transfection of Myc-tagged SLC7A11 and Flag-tagged OTUB1 into 293T cells further verified that OTUB1 binds stably to SLC7A11 in vitro ( C). Additionally, laser confocal imaging showed that OTUB1 and SLC7A11 share the same subcellular localization ( D and 6E) shows the structures of the OTUB1 and SLC7A11 proteins and their fragments. To further characterize the binding relationship between OTUB1 and SLC7A11, we prepared a series of truncated Myc-SLC7A11 and Flag-OTUB1 constructs, which we transfected into 293T cells. CoIP assays demonstrated that fragments containing amino acids 292–501 within the C terminus (M3) of SLC7A11 and amino acids 86–195 (M2) of OTUB1 could bind with each other ( E and 6F). To determine whether OTUB1 regulates the expression level of SLC7A11, we detected the mRNA and protein levels of SLC7A11 after shRNA-mediated knockdown. OTUB1 knockdown did not affect the SLC7A11 mRNA expression level ( A). However, silencing of OTUB1 destabilized the SLC7A11 protein level, and this was reversed by the addition of the proteasome inhibitor MG132, which suggests that shOTUB1 stimulates proteasome pathway-mediated degradation of SLC7A11 ( G). Conversely, overexpression of Flag-OTUB1 WT increased the levels of SLC7A11, whereas a catalytically inactive OTUB1 mutant (Flag-OTUB1 CA) did not have this ability ( H). To test the effect of OTUB1 on the stability of endogenous SLC7A11, we measured the protein levels of SLC7A11 in the presence of the protein synthesis inhibitor cycloheximide (CHX). Compared with the control, the half-life of endogenous SLC7A11 was shorter after silencing of OTUB1 ( I), while Flag-OTUB1, but not Flag-OTUB1-CA, had the converse effect ( J). The above results suggest that OTUB1 regulates the expression level of SLC7A11 through proteasome pathway-mediated ubiquitination. 

## OTUB1 deubiquitinates SLC7A11 and directly cleaves the Lys 48-polyubiquitin chain
To explore whether OTUB1 modifies the ubiquitination level of SLC7A11, we evaluated ubiquitination in OTUB1 knockdown fibroblasts, which demonstrated that OTUB1 knockdown dramatically increases the SLC7A11 ubiquitination level ( A). For additional verification, we co-expressed Myc-SLC7A11 and HA-ubiquitin with either Flag-OTUB1 WT or Flag-OTUB1 CA in fibroblasts. Flag-OTUB1 WT suppressed the SLC7A11 ubiquitination levels; however, Flag-OTUB1 CA caused little or no deubiquitylation effect ( B). These results were verified in 293T cells using GST-OTUB1(WT) or GST-OTUB1 (CA) and His-SLC7A11 ( C). Next, to explore which polyubiquitin chain in SLC7A11 is cleaved by OTUB1, we transfected HA-UB plasmid with the most common polyubiquitin chains into 293 T cells. The results suggest that OTUB1 cleaves the Lys48 polyubiquitin chain of SLC7A11 ( D). Consistently, mutation of lysine residue K48 (48R) blocked the deubiquitylation effect of OTUB1 ( E). In vitro deubiquitylation assays with HA-UB (Lys48) further confirmed that OTUB1 removes the Lys48-polyubiquitin chains from SLC7A11 ( F). Next, we expressed a Lys48-resistant (Lys48-Res) form of ubiquitin in OTUB1 knockdown fibroblasts and found that it significantly attenuated the ability of shOTUB1 to promote SLC7A11 downregulation ( G). These results suggest that OTUB1 regulates the ubiquitination and stability level of SLC7A11 by regulating its Lys48 polyubiquitin chain. 

## SLC7A11 knockdown in fibroblasts partially reverses pulmonary fibrosis in vivo
Finally, to verify the role of SLC7A11 in mediating the effects of OTUB1 in vivo, we prepared fibroblast-specific adeno-associated viruses for OTUB1 overexpression or SLC7A11 knockdown, which we applied to the tracheas of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis mice. H&E, Masson’s staining, hydroxyproline levels, and Ashcroft scores demonstrated that overexpression of OTUB1 significantly increased the fibrosis, whereas SLC7A11 knockdown attenuated the OTUB1-mediated progression of fibrosis ( A–8D; A). Western blot assays confirmed that overexpression of OTUB1 in vivo resulted in a significant increase in SLC7A11 ( E; B). Furthermore, flow cytometry and fluorescence confocal results showed that lipid peroxidation levels were lower in fibroblasts from the OTUB1 overexpression group ( F and 8G; C). OTUB1 overexpression also resulted in a reduction in MDA levels and an increase in GSH/GSSG ratio in fibroblasts ( H), suggesting that the interaction between OTUB1 and SLC7A11 in fibroblasts may regulate ferroptosis in vivo. Collectively, these results provide evidence that SLC7A11 knockdown significantly inhibits fibrotic ferroptosis and exacerbates IPF development in vivo. 

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrated that the number of AT2 cells decreases in the early stages of IPF, followed by a slight rebound surrounding the fibroblast foci. AT1 cells, on the other hand, are persistently reduced, which indicates a severe disruption of alveolar structure and abnormal differentiation from AT2 to AT1 cells. We also observed a significant increase in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts. By performing cell-to-cell interaction and co-culture experiments, we demonstrated that AT2 cells secrete MDK to activate the LRP1 receptor in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts. We identified a significant reduction in lipid peroxidation accumulation and ferroptosis in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts. Furthermore, by transfecting human primary lung fibroblasts with CTHRC1+ lentiviral vectors, we overexpressed MDK to stimulate CTHRC1+ human primary fibroblasts in vitro and then subjected the cells to RNA-seq. The results of our study showed that MDK increases the expression level of OTUB1. Upon OTUB1 knockdown in vitro, there was a remarkable improvement in the level of ubiquitination of SLCA11, decreased expression of SLC7A11, and increased levels of fibrotic ferroptosis. In vivo rescue experiments demonstrated that the knockdown of LRP1 and SLC7A11 notably reversed pulmonary fibrosis disease progression in mice. These results reveal a mechanism that underlies the progression of IPF.
CTHCR1 is enriched in injured tissues and promotes cell migration. CTHRC1+ fibroblasts are a specific and unique population of cells in IPF. In a previous study, CTHRC1+ fibroblasts were found to express the highest levels of collagens and other known components of the pathologic extracellular matrix (ECM). RNA velocity and pseudotime trajectory analysis further indicated that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts are differentiated from alveolar fibroblasts. In our study, we found that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts were gradually upregulated and were surrounded by AT2 cells in pulmonary fibrosis, both in humans and mice. Overexpression of CTHRC1 in human primary lung fibroblasts significantly reduced the levels of lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis. Therefore, we suggest that AT2 cells closely interact with CTHRC1+ fibroblasts and that their reciprocal relationship may be a key factor contributing to the progression of IPF.
Cell-to-cell interaction analysis further demonstrated that the MDK-LRP1 interaction pair plays an important role in IPF. Emerging evidence suggests that MDK, a member of the heparin-binding growth factor family, is induced under hypoxic conditions and binds to various receptors with high affinity. LRP1 is a large, ubiquitously expressed endocytic/signaling receptor that controls lipid metabolism and tissue remodeling in multiple organs. Previous studies have indicated that among the four ligand-binding domains of LRP1, the N-terminal half of the second domain has the strongest affinity for MDK. Global gene expression profiling has revealed that LRP1-depleted human lung fibroblasts lose cytoskeleton reorganization, cell-ECM contact, and ECM production capabilities. In this study, the human lung tissues analyzed were obtained from patients with end-stage IPF, as early-stage diagnostic biopsies were not available in our tissue bank. We therefore cannot directly evaluate MDK or LRP1 expression at the protein level in early human fibrotic lesions. However, as an alternative approach, we assessed circulating MDK levels in a cohort of IPF patients (n = 20) and healthy controls (n = 20). We found that plasma MDK levels were significantly elevated in IPF patients and, importantly, showed a strong negative correlation with diffusion lung capacity for CO (DLCO) % predicted (R2 = 0,7985, p < 0.001, E), a key indicator of disease severity that declines progressively as fibrosis advances. This correlation suggests that MDK dysregulation is not merely a late-stage phenomenon but likely occurs early enough to impact lung function. While circulating levels do not directly reflect local tissue expression at early disease stages, the robust association between plasma MDK and functional impairment supports the translational relevance of MDK as a biomarker that reflects ongoing disease activity, potentially from relatively early phases. Future prospective studies with longitudinal sampling of both plasma and, when clinically indicated, early-stage biopsy tissues will be required to definitively characterize the temporal dynamics of MDK-LRP1 axis activation in IPF pathogenesis.
Our findings revealed that LRP1 gene and protein expression levels in fibroblasts increase with increasing MDK concentrations. MDK-triggered LRP1-mediated endocytosis internalizes the MDK-LRP1 complex into the cell, subsequently activating downstream signaling pathways. Multiple studies have demonstrated that the MDK complex can stimulate increased transcription factor expression levels. These transcription factors may bind to the LRP1 promoter region, forming a positive feedback loop that further activates LRP1 expression. , , , Kazuma Sakamoto et al. employed an ER trapper (containing an ER retention signal and an LRP1 fragment), demonstrating that MDK-LRP1 aggregation triggers an unfolded protein response, indirectly upregulating LRP1 synthesis to compensate for functional loss. Furthermore, in colorectal cancer, the expression levels of LRP1’s molecular chaperone RAP correlate positively with MDK expression. This effect may occur through competitive binding to LRP1, resulting in MDK release and, subsequently, enhanced LRP1 recycling and expression. In bladder and esophageal squamous cell carcinomas, the MDK-LRP1 axis further upregulates LRP1 expression by recruiting immunosuppressive cells (e.g., M2 macrophages) and promoting the anchorage-independent growth of cancer cells. , In mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs) and bone repair, MDK activates AKT and HO-1 pathways via LRP1 to maintain stem cell survival and promote LRP1 expression. , By analyzing RNA-seq data from MDK-stimulated versus control groups, we also identified increased expression of transcription factors regulating LRP1 levels, including PPARG, SREBF1, SREBF2, and NR2F2. This finding provides further evidence that MDK promotes LRP1 expression changes via transcriptional regulation. Moreover, our findings demonstrate that LRP1 activation in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts by AT2 cell-secreted MDK increases fibroblast viability and decreases their ferroptosis levels, contributing to IPF progression.
Although MDK is also expressed in airway epithelial cells such as basal cells in IPF lungs, several considerations support the relevance of AT2-derived MDK for alveolar fibroblast regulation. AT2 cells reside in close anatomical proximity to fibroblasts, providing a spatial advantage for paracrine signaling. Moreover, AT2 cells remain numerically abundant in IPF and show high MDK expression per cell. Functionally, our AAV-based experiments ( ) demonstrate that selective overexpression of MDK in AT2 cells alone is sufficient to exacerbate pulmonary fibrosis and induce fibroblast ferroptosis resistance, establishing functional sufficiency. We acknowledge that other epithelial sources may contribute synergistically, and future studies using cell-type-specific MDK knockout models will be required to dissect their relative contributions. Nonetheless, our findings identify AT2-derived MDK as a key driver of fibroblast survival and fibrosis progression in IPF. Beyond fibroblasts, LRP1 is widely expressed in macrophages, airway epithelial cells, and endothelial cells. In macrophages, MDK-LRP1 signaling could promote M2-like persistence by protecting them from ferroptosis, indirectly supporting fibroblast activation. In airway epithelial cells, MDK-mediated ferroptosis protection might preserve barrier integrity but could also promote aberrant proliferation or EMT. In endothelial cells, LRP1 activation may protect against ferroptosis-induced microvascular dysfunction, potentially counteracting fibrosis. The net effect of the MDK-LRP1 axis on IPF progression likely integrates these cell-type-specific actions in a context-dependent manner. Future studies using cell-type-specific LRP1 knockout models are needed to dissect these distinct roles.
Numerous studies investigating OTUB1 have demonstrated its deubiquitylation ability in many biological processes. , , OTUB1 expression is elevated in various human cancers, and it directly binds to multiple proteins, removing ubiquitination modifications and stabilizing expression. , To our knowledge, the present study is the first to demonstrate that OTUB1/SLC7A11 affects fibroblast ferroptosis in IPF, leading to disease progression and deterioration.
Notably, there are no specific promoters for lung fibroblasts in vivo, and consequently, in this study, we instead opted for the broad-spectrum fibroblast promoter mCOL1A2 and stimulated fibroblasts in the lungs using transnasal adeno-associated virus. Furthermore, the knockdown of LRP1 in fibroblasts in vivo may concurrently increase overall MDK protein levels, and we speculate that there may be a possible reverse regulatory loop between MDK and LRP1. However, this needs to be investigated in further studies.
Our study investigated the cell interaction and cellular mechanism of IPF progression. IPF leads to abnormal proliferation of AT2 cells that secrete MDK, which activates the fibroblast LRP1 receptor and increases OTUB1 in fibroblasts. Deubiquitylation modification of OTUB1, which stabilizes SLC7A11 expression, decreases the fibroblast ferroptosis level, ultimately leading to the deterioration of IPF.

## Limitations of the study
While our study delineates a novel MDK-LRP1 axis in suppressing fibroblast ferroptosis and promoting pulmonary fibrosis, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, due to the lack of a truly specific promoter for lung fibroblasts in vivo, we utilized the mCOL1A2 promoter for AAV-mediated gene manipulation. Although this promoter effectively targeted collagen-producing cells, its activity might not be exclusive to the pathogenic CTHRC1+ fibroblast subpopulation, potentially influencing the precision of our in vivo observations. Second, our primary in vivo model was the bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis model in mice, which recapitulates key features of human IPF but is an acute injury model that may not fully capture the chronic, progressive nature of the human disease. The translational relevance of our findings requires further validation in other models, such as genetic or aged mouse models of fibrosis. Third, the potential bidirectional regulatory loop between MDK and LRP1, hinted at by our in vivo knockdown experiments, was not fully dissected. The mechanisms by which LRP1 depletion might affect overall MDK levels and signaling in the fibrotic niche remain an open question for future investigation. Finally, although we validated key findings using human primary cells and single-cell sequencing data from IPF patients, the sample size for some functional validations was limited, and future studies with larger cohorts will be important to confirm the clinical robustness of this pathway. Despite these limitations, the consistent evidence from integrated bioinformatics, cellular assays, and animal models strongly supports our central conclusion that targeting this axis could be a viable therapeutic strategy for IPF.

## Experimental model and study participant details

## Human lung tissues
Approval for the study on human lung tissues was granted by the Ethics Committee of Wuxi People’s Hospital Affiliated with Nanjing Medical University (reference number KY22060). The IPF lung tissues were obtained post-lung transplantation surgery and were classified according to American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society guidelines by each subject’s primary pulmonologist. Healthy lung tissue samples were obtained from non-neoplastic adjacent tissues of lung cancer patients. Before collecting samples, patients or their family members provided written informed consent.

## Animal model of pulmonary fibrosis
The Animal Ethics Committee of Nanjing Medical University (Reference number NJMU-EA-2019-420) approved all animal protocols used in this study. C57B/L6 mice (aged 6–10 weeks, male) were sourced from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Company in China. The mice were anesthetized by isoflurane inhalation for approximately 3 min. After that, they were transnasally administered with either bleomycin (BLM) (0.75 mg/kg in 100 μL PBS, MedChemExpress) to induce pulmonary fibrosis or PBS as a control (Gibico). The mice were euthanized 21 days after BLM administration.

## Method details

## H&E and masson’s staining
Lung tissues were preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde and fixed at 4°C for 24 h. The tissues were then subjected to gradient dehydration using standard protocols and embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, the tissues were sliced into 3-μm thick sections. H&E staining was performed according to a standard protocol. Briefly, after dehydration and the removal of excess material, the nuclei were stained with hematoxylin for 7 minutes, and the cytoplasm was stained with eosin for 7 minutes. Masson’s staining was performed according to the Masson’s trichrome staining Kit protocol (Solarbio, G1340). This technique stains collagen blue. All quantitative evaluation was performed using ImageJ software. The severity of pulmonary fibrosis was semiquantitatively assessed according to the Ashcroft scale. , 

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Lung tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight. After embedding in paraffin, 3 μm-thick sections were sliced for IHC staining. Paraffin sections of lung tissue were treated with citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at ∼115°C after routine dewaxing and hydration for antigen retrieval. The sections were then blocked with goat serum for 1 h at room temperature. Next, the sections were incubated with HRP-labelled goat anti-mouse/rabbit IgG for 1 h at room temperature, followed by streptavidin/HRP for 20 min at room temperature. DAB was used to stain the nuclei. 

## Immunofluorescence staining
Cells or lung tissue sections were treated with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, followed by 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min for membrane permeabilization. To prevent nonspecific antibody binding, the slides were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h. The primary antibody was then applied overnight at 4°C. The sections were then incubated with secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488 and Alexa Fluor 647 (abcam) for 1 h at room temperature. Finally, the nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were captured under the identical exposure times and conditions.

## Hydroxyproline levels
Lung hydroxyproline levels were examined according to the kit manufacturer’s instructions (abcam, ab222941). Briefly, lung tissue samples were hydrolyzed by adding 10 N NaOH for 1 h at 120°C. After cooling on ice and neutralization with 10 N HCl, the samples were centrifuged and supernatants were collected. The samples and standards were added to the wells and evaporated to dryness with heating at 65°C. Subsequently, the oxidizing reagent mixture was added to dissolve the crystalline residue for 20 min at room temperature. Developer was added, and the samples were incubated at 37°C for 5 minutes. Then, DMAB concentrate was added, and the samples were incubated at 65°C for 45 min and detected using a microplate reader.

## MDK ELISA assay
Plasma levels of midkine (MDK) in IPF patients and healthy controls were measured using the Human Midkine SimpleStep ELISA Kit (ab193761, Abcam), while murine plasma MDK was quantified using the Mouse Midkine SimpleStep ELISA Kit (ab279416, Abcam), both according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Briefly, diluted plasma samples (human: 1:2; mouse: 1:8) were added to pre-coated plates together with the antibody cocktail, incubated for 1 h at room temperature with shaking, washed, and developed with TMB substrate. Absorbance was read at 450 nm, and MDK concentrations were calculated from a four-parameter logistic (4-PL) standard curve run in duplicate. Correlation analyses between plasma MDK levels and DLCO% predicted (human) were performed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient and Ashcroft scores (mouse) were performed using Spearman’s correlation coefficient.

## Multiplexed immunohistochemistry (mIHC)
Paraffin sections were deparaffinized sequentially in xylene, anhydrous ethanol, 85% alcohol, 75% alcohol and distilled water. The sections were then incubated in EDTA antigen repair buffer. After natural cooling, the slides were washed three times in PBS, placed in 3% hydrogen peroxide solution to block endogenous peroxidase, washed in PBS, and blocked with BSA. Primary antibody was added, and the samples were incubated at 4°C overnight and rinsed with PBS. Then the samples were incubated with secondary antibody, rinsed with PBS, and incubated in freshly prepared fluorescent chromatographic solution. After that, the antibody was eluted, and the blocking, primary antibody, secondary antibody, and color development steps were repeated until the staining of PDPN, SFTPC, KRT17 and CTHRC1 was completed. Finally, the nuclei were stained with DAPI, and the slides were sealed. Images were captured and analyzed using 3D HISTECH acquisition.

## Single-cell seq analysis
Data were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (Database: GSE135893 and GSE227136) and then imported into R (Version 4.1.0) using the Seurat package. To exclude low-quality cells, the following criteria were used: >40,000 UMI/cell, <500 genes/cell, >5,000 genes/cell and >20% mitochondrial genes. Doublets were identified and removed by the DoubletFinder R package. To reduce the dimensionality and avoid the batch effect, RunPCA and RunUMAP functions were used. Afterward, the cell clusters were identified using a default resolution of 0.5 and the FindClusters function implemented in Seurat. The clusters were annotated as different cell types based on the average gene expression of canonical markers. To functionally describe control and IPF fibroblasts, pathway analysis was performed using specific gene sets from the MSigDB database. The pathway activity of individual cells was estimated using the gene set variation analysis (GSVA) package with standard settings. Activity scores were contrasted for each fibroblast subgroup with the Limma package. A cell-to-cell communication analysis was conducted to investigate potential interactions across different cell clusters. Analysis was performed using the CellPhoneDB package in Python (version 2.1.7). To determine the average receptor and ligand expression levels within interacting clusters, we first randomly permuted the cluster labels of all cells 1000 times. We then enriched receptor-ligand interactions between two cell types based on the expression of a receptor by one cell type and the expression of the corresponding ligand by another cell type. Next, we identified the most relevant cell type-specific interactions between ligands and receptors and those expressed in less than 10% of the corresponding subcluster of cells. Finally, by statistical interactions of ligands and receptors in each cell cluster, we summarized the inferred intercellular communication network of ligand-receptor pair.

## RNA-seq
Paired-end reads were obtained from the Illumina HiSeq 4000 sequencer, and quality control was performed using the Q30 standard. After trimming the 3′ adaptor, low-quality reads were removed using cut adapt software (v1.9.3). The resulting high-quality clean reads were aligned to the human reference genome (UCSC hg19) using Hisat2 software (v2.0.4). Then, guided by the Ensemble gtf gene annotation file, Cuffdiff software (v2.2.1, part of Cufflinks) was used to obtain gene level FPKM expression profiles of mRNAs. Fold change and p-value were calculated based on the FPKM, and GO and Pathway enrichment analyses were performed based on the resulting differentially expressed mRNAs. The RNA-seq data has been uploaded to the GEO database (Database: GSE262882).

## Isolation and culture of human primary lung fibroblasts
Human primary lung fibroblast cells were isolated from lung transplantation recipients with IPF or nonfibrotic lungs. The cells were separated and cultured as described in previous studies. Briefly, the lung tissues were cut into small pieces and then immersed in a tissue mild enzyme digestion kit (DHGT-5004, RWD). Then, the tissue mixture was dissociated using a gentleMACS Octo Dissociator (DSC-400, RWD). After removing the remaining tissue lumps, the cells were collected through 70 μm cell filters and then maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic. High-purity fibroblast cells were obtained after three or more passages. , 

## Isolation of human AT2 cells
Human lung tissues were cut into small pieces and then dissociated using a gentleMACS Octo Dissociator (DSC-400, RWD). The single cell suspension was passed through a series of cell strainers, starting with a 100 μm strainer, then a 70 μm strainer and finally a 40 mm strainer. A single cell suspension was sorted using the single-cell-select-mode on BD FACS Aria II and III instruments to select EPCAM+HTII 280+CD45−CD31− cells. 

## Adeno-associated virus infection
To generate MDK-overexpression AT2 cells and OTUB1-overexpression fibroblasts in vivo, pAAV-SP-Cp-MDK-SV40 PolyA (SPC-MDK OE) and mCOL1A2-OTUB1-SV40 PolyA (mCOL1A2-OTUB1 OE) were used, with SP-Cp-MIR155 (MCS)-SV40 PolyA (SPC-CP MDK NC) and mCOL1A2-mir155 (MCS)-SV40 PolyA (mCOL1A2-OTUB1 NC) as a control vector. To decrease the exogenous expression of LRP1 and SLC7A11 in fibroblast cells in vivo, AAV9-mCOL1A2 vector was used for the expression of short hairpin RNAs (mCOL1A2-LRP1 KD, mCOL1A2-SLC7A11 KD) or a control hairpin (mCOL1A2-NC). Adeno-associated viruses with a final volume of 80 μL and a final titer of 1 × 1012 μg/mL were intranasally delivered into BLM-treated mice. 

## Plasmids and adenoviral infection
Adenovirus shRNA-control (Ad-shCtrl), adenovirus shRNA-LRP1, shRNA-MDK, and shRNA-OTUB1 (shLRP1, shMDK, or shOTUB1) were used to silence expression in cells. The full-length sequences of human CTHRC1, MDK, and LRP1 were cloned into pcDNA3.1 vectors for overexpression in AT2 and fibroblast cells. Similarly, full-length sequences of human OTUB1, SLC7A11, and ubiquitin were subcloned into Flag-, Myc-, HA-, GST-, and His-tagged pcDNA3.1 vectors using the EcoRI and NotI sites. The shRNA sequences used are provided in Additional file 1.

## Protein docking
The structures of SLC7A11 (Protein DataBank code 7CCS) and OTBU1 (Protein DataBank code 4DDL) were used for docking simulations. Unbiased rigid body docking (exhaustive search of all possible binding sites and binding poses) was performed using three servers: HDOCK, LZerD, and ZDOCK. The molecular docking results were selected according to the rank-sum score, where the smaller the score is, the better the docking will be.

## Real-time quantitative PCR
qRT-PCR was performed as previously described. The expression levels of the target genes were normalized to β-actin. The primers were synthesized by Tsingke Biotech.

## Western blotting
Lung tissue and cultured cells were collected at specific time intervals. Protein extraction and Western blotting were performed using published methods. The expression levels of the target proteins were normalized to β-actin, and the antibodies used are listed in .

## Measurement of lipid ROS
The cells were incubated with 5 μM BODIPY 581/591 C11 fluorescence probe (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 30 min in a humidified incubator (at 37°C, 5% CO2). Subsequently, the cells were subjected to confocal microscopy (Carl Zeiss LSM880) or trypsinization suspension followed by flow cytometry (BD LSRFortessa Cell Analyzer) to analyze lipid peroxidation levels.

## Measurement of GSH and MDA
Cell and tissue lysates were obtained using RIPA Lysis Buffer (Beyotime). The levels of reduced glutathione were measured using the Reduced GSH Detection Kit (Nanjing Jincheng Bioengineering Institution). MDA was assessed using the Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 

## Immunoprecipitation
The cells were collected, and an appropriate volume of cell immunoprecipitation (IP) lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with protease inhibitors was added. The cells were then lysed on ice or at 4°C for 30 min, and the supernatant was extracted after centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 30 min. Twenty microliters of lysate was collected for Western blotting, and the indicated antibodies and protein A/G-beads were added to the remaining lysate. The mixture was then slowly shaken and incubated at 4°C overnight. After the IP reaction, the protein A/G-beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were centrifuged at 4°C at 3000 × g for 5 min. The supernatant was carefully removed, and the protein–A/G bead complexes were washed with lysis buffer four times. Finally, the bound protein was eluted by boiling, and Western blotting was performed. 

## GST Pull-down assays
GST and GST-tagged fusion proteins were purified using GST resin (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. To isolate HIS-SLC7A11 proteins, 293T cells transfected with HIS-SLC7A11 were lysed in buffer and then immunoprecipitated with anti-Flag antibody-coupled beads. The bound proteins were eluted with buffer containing 2% NP-40 and FLAG peptide. GST and GST-OTUB1 were incubated with the purified HIS-SLC7A11 overnight at 4°C in buffer to assess interactions. After washing three times, the bound proteins were eluted for 2 h at 4°C in elution buffer (20 mmol/L reduced glutathione in 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) and then subjected to Western blotting. 

## Quantification and statistical analysis
The results are presented as means and standard deviations. Prior to analysis, all datasets were tested for normal distribution using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. For normally distributed data, Student’s t test or analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized for determining group differences. For non-normally distributed data, non-parametric counterparts, such as Mann-Whitney U-test or Kruskal-Wallis ANOVA, were used. P-values less than 0.05 were considered as statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using PRISMA software.
