## Highlights
•Cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression combined with chimeric SARS-CoV-2 infection in K18-hACE2 mice establishes a BSL-2–compatible model achieving 100% penetrance with macrophage-predominant infiltration and cardiomyocyte necrosis that recapitulates human COVID-19 myocarditis; IDOL-mediated LDLR degradation completely abrogates the phenotype.
•A novel high-affinity LDLR-OPN interaction (mapped to CR2-CR5 domains) drives monocyte recruitment. OPN-knockout macrophages fail to migrate toward LDLR-overexpressing cardiomyocytes, confirming the functional indispensability of this axis.
•Among inflammatory cell death pathways, only GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis is selectively activated in LDLR-driven myocarditis, and dimethyl fumarate treatment reduces cardiac inflammatory foci.
•Human myocarditis autopsy hearts show up-regulated LDLR and ICAM-1, providing translational validation of the LDLR–OPN axis as a clinically relevant driver of inflammatory myocardial injury.

## Summary
COVID-19–associated myocarditis is marked by macrophage-rich inflammation and adverse cardiac outcomes, yet its mechanisms remain unclear. We developed a reproducible BSL-2 mouse model by combining cardiac-specific human low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) overexpression (AAV9-cTnT-hLDLR) with chimeric SARS-CoV-2 infection in keratin 18 human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 transgenic mice, achieving 100% penetrance of macrophage-predominant myocarditis with cardiomyocyte necrosis and gasdermin D–mediated pyroptosis. We identified a previously unrecognized high-affinity interaction between LDLR (CR2-CR5 domains) and osteopontin that drives monocyte recruitment. Induced degrader of LDLR–mediated LDLR degradation completely abrogated myocarditis, while dimethyl fumarate significantly reduced pyroptosis and inflammatory burden. Importantly, human myocarditis autopsy hearts exhibited >10-fold up-regulation of LDLR and intercellular adhesion molecule-1, mirroring the murine findings. These data establish the LDLR-osteopontin axis as a mechanistic and targetable driver of COVID-19 myocarditis and provide a translational platform for therapeutic development in viral myocarditis.

Myocarditis associated with COVID-19 infection, long COVID syndrome, and mRNA COVID-19 vaccination has emerged as a critical clinical challenge, with a >15-fold increased risk of myocarditis in COVID-19 patients compared with uninfected individuals, demonstrated in a comprehensive analysis of over 900 U.S. hospitals. This heightened risk is accompanied by long-term cardiovascular sequelae, including heart failure, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death, underscoring the urgency of understanding the underlying mechanisms. , Unlike traditional viral myocarditis, which often involves direct myocardial infection or autoimmune responses, COVID-19–associated myocarditis is characterized by robust inflammatory cell recruitment, predominantly macrophages, driven by systemic and local immune dysregulation. , Although existing mouse models of viral myocarditis rely on direct cardiac infection or autoimmune responses, they fail to capture the inflammatory cell recruitment that characterizes COVID-19 myocarditis, limiting their translational relevance.
The low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR), traditionally recognized for its role in cholesterol homeostasis was recently implicated in cardiac inflammation and heart failure. , Elevated cardiac LDLR expression in heart failure patients is associated with enhanced inflammatory signaling, suggesting a potential role in mediating immune cell infiltration during COVID-19 myocarditis. , Furthermore, lipid dysregulation and LDLR interaction with the Na+/H+ exchanger have been linked to poor outcomes in COVID-19, potentially exacerbating myocardial injury through inflammatory pathways. In parallel, pyroptosis, a programmed, proinflammatory cell death pathway mediated by gasdermin D (GSDMD), has emerged as a key contributor to COVID-19–related cardiac damage, with inflammasome activation driving cytokine storm and tissue injury. , Whether LDLR, inflammatory cell recruitment, and GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis operate through a unified mechanism in COVID-19 myocarditis has not been established.
Three converging lines of evidence implicate osteopontin (OPN), a multifunctional secreted glycoprotein, as a critical molecular bridge in this process. First, in experimental viral myocarditis, OPN is predominantly expressed by infiltrating macrophages and monocytes and is dramatically up-regulated during acute inflammatory stages. OPN-deficient mice show significantly reduced inflammatory infiltration and cardiac fibrosis, establishing its causal role in disease progression. Second, multiple independent clinical studies have demonstrated that plasma OPN levels are significantly elevated in COVID-19 patients compared with healthy controls, with OPN concentrations directly correlating with disease severity, need for mechanical ventilation, and mortality. Importantly, elevated OPN measured within 48 hours of hospital admission independently predicts adverse outcomes in COVID-19 patients, serving as a stronger predictor than traditional inflammatory markers. , , , Third, mechanistically, OPN mediates macrophage chemotaxis via α4 and α9 integrins and directly induces expression of key chemokines (MCP-1 and MIP-1β) that amplify monocyte recruitment. , Despite this evidence, the potential interaction between LDLR and OPN, and its contribution to myocardial inflammation, has not been explored.
Given the established role of the LDLR in lipid metabolism and our previous findings linking it to cardiac inflammation, we hypothesized that LDLR mediates inflammatory cell recruitment in COVID-19 myocarditis via interactions with OPN and activation of GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis. To test this, we developed a highly reproducible model utilizing cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression in keratin 18 human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (K18-hACE2) transgenic mice infected with a chimeric SARS-CoV-2 (chSARS-CoV-2) virus that recapitulates the macrophage-driven inflammatory cell recruitment observed in human COVID-19 myocarditis. This model not only addresses the limitations of existing models but unveils a previously unrecognized LDLR-OPN axis and GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis as pivotal mechanisms driving COVID-19-associated myocardial injury, offering novel insights into therapeutic targets for this urgent unmet clinical need.

## Methods

## Experimental design and animal model
K18-hACE2 transgenic mice that express human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (hACE2) under the keratin 18 promoter were purchased from Jackson Labs. Male and female mice were housed under pathogen-free conditions in temperature- and humidity-controlled rooms with a 12-hour light–dark cycle. Mice were provided ad libitum with water and food. The 2-month-old mice received intravenous injections of adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) vectors (1 × 1012 viral particles): AAV9-cTnT-human (h)LDLR for cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression, AAV9-cTnT-IDOL for LDLR degradation, or AAV9-cTnT-Luciferase as control. Four weeks post-AAV delivery, mice were infected intratracheally with 107 IU or 2 × 106 plaque-forming units per mouse replication-competent chimeric VSV-eGFP-SARS-CoV-2-spike recombinant virus (chSARS-CoV-2) in a BSL-2 facility. The following groups were evaluated: 1) control (No chSARS-CoV-2); 2) AAV-cTnT-LDLR; 3) AAV-cTnT-Luciferase+chSARS-CoV-2; 4) AAV-cTnT-LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2; and 5) AAV-cTnT-hIDOL+chSARS-CoV-2 (n = 4-9 per group). After 5 days, tissues were harvested and hearts analyzed by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and WB to validate the expression of delivered genes. For therapeutic intervention, mice received daily intraperitoneal injections of dimethyl fumarate (DMF) (30 mg/kg) for 5 days post chSARS-CoV-2 infection to inhibit GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis (n = 5). Control groups received vehicle (dimethyl sulfoxide) (n = 5). All of the procedures were performed in mice anaesthetized with isoflurane at 3% to 5% in an induction chamber and between 0.5% and 2% for maintenance, depending on the procedure and duration. In accordance with the protocol, animals were monitored daily for clinical signs of disease. Weight loss was monitored daily for 5 dpi. Mice losing >30% of starting body weights were immediately killed. Mice which appeared moribund or unlikely to recover were also sacrificed. At the endpoint, mice were sacrificed by isoflurane inhalation (3%-5%) followed by cervical dislocation, thoracotomy, exsanguination, and perfusion with diethyl pyrocarbonate phosphate-buffered saline . Hearts were collected for histology and molecular assays.

## Chimeric virus generation and proliferation
VSV-eGFP-SARS-CoV-2 chimeric virus, , was generously donated and expanded according to protocols from Drs. Whelan and Diamond. This is a replication-competent vesicular stomatitis virus expressing eGFP in the first position of the genome, and a modified version of the SARS-CoV-2 spike in place of the native VSV glycoprotein. According to previous in vivo studies, , , , pseudo- or chimeric viruses have been used in place of authentic viruses, supporting the suitability of this chimeric SARS-CoV-2 model for in vivo applications. Protocols for chSARS-CoV-2 have been optimized for BSL-2/BSL-2+ safety, with multiplicity of infections and time points based on preliminary data showing robust inflammatory responses. 

## Histopathology and immunostaining
Heart tissues were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and immunostaining. Sections from hearts were incubated for 45 minutes at 70 °C; dewaxed with xylene washes; and hydrated by graded ethanol washes of 100% (twice for 3 minutes), 95%, 80%, and 70% followed by water immersion (twice for 4 minutes). Next, slides were steamed for 45 minutes with citrate Antigen Retrieval Buffer (ab93678). Sections were rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline, permeabilized with 0.2% triton x-100 (Sigma-Aldrich) for 30 minutes, blocking with 10% donkey serum (45 minutes) and incubated overnight with primary antibodies: anti-HA (Thermo A190138A), anti-CD68 (MCA1957GA), anti-CD3 (thermo 11-0032-82), anti–intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1) (sc-390483), anti-phosphorylated Akt (cell signaling 4060), and anti-OPN (ab8448). On the next day, slides were incubated for 1 hour with HRP-conjugated or Alexa Fluor secondary antibodies. Nuclei were stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole before mounting with ProLong Gold Antifade (Invitrogen P36934) and coverslips. Controls without primary antibody were used for all staining procedures and produced close to zero signal. Myocarditis was established by histological analysis according to the Dallas criteria: immunological and immunohistochemical criteria (>14 leukocytes/mm2 including up to 4 monocytes/mm2 with the presence of CD3 positive T-lymphocytes >7 cells/mm2) plus cardiomyocyte degeneration or necrosis. , H&E staining was analyzed blindly by a clinical pathologist (AGS). For imaging and quantification, slides were scanned at 20× magnification using an Olympus VS120–L100 Virtual Slide Microscope (Tokyo, Japan) or imaged with a Zeiss Microscope available at the UM Imaging Core. Five images from randomly selected tissue sections were used to quantify markers using Image J where myocardium regions were selected for quantification (biomarker expression/tissue area × 100).

## 3-dimensional reconstruction
High-resolution, quantitative 3-dimensional reconstruction of whole cardiac tissues were generated from serial histological sections by applying advanced image processing and deep learning–assisted analysis. The methodology allowed for detailed analysis of immune infiltration, necrosis, and tissue remodeling, facilitated by the University of Miami Pegasus Supercomputer core. Initially, sliced tissues were aligned to a unified coordinate framework beginning with coarse alignment through rigid-body registration, where radon transforms and cross-correlation were applied to determine initial rotational and translational corrections. Subsequently, an elastic registration phase refined the alignment, correcting local deformations using grid-based interpolation of the rigid alignments. The workflow then advanced to pixel-wise classification, where a Matlab-based DeepLabv3+ model with a ResNet50 backbone segmented the tissue into 8 distinct categories: foci, endothelium, myocardium, background, atria, valves, inflammatory regions, and adipose tissue, using high-resolution images and annotations tailored to myocarditis to improve detection of pathological features.
The University of Miami Pegasus Supercomputer was used to perform nonlinear image registration on high-resolution histological tissue images (0.5 μm/pixel, ×20 magnification). Images were down sampled to 5 μm/pixel using Matlab with nearest neighbor interpolation. The center image of each sample served as the reference for global and elastic registration. Global registration was achieved by iteratively calculating registration angle and translation through cross-correlation maximization of Gaussian-filtered, grayscale images (80 μm/pixel). Radon transforms determined the registration angle, followed by translation computation. Elastic registration involved rigid registration of 1.5-mm image tiles, interpolated to 8 μm/pixel resolution, and smoothed with a Gaussian filter (σ = 2 pixels) to generate a nonlinear transformation.
Afterward, a custom QuPath-based code processed the AI-analyzed images to enable manual adjustments of the inflammatory infiltrate and necrotic foci, allowing for refined pathological assessments and validation of automated segmentations. This hybrid approach, combining automated AI segmentation with expert manual oversight, significantly increases the accuracy of the reconstructions by identifying and correcting potential algorithmic errors such as false positives in inflammatory detection caused by artifactual staining or underestimation of necrotic areas caused by variable tissue contrast, thereby reducing overall misclassification rates and ensuring more robust, error-minimized data for downstream analysis.

## Protein and molecular analyses
Protein concentration from heart tissue lysates was measured using a Bradford assay. Samples were prepared and separated using a 4% to 12% Novex mini 15-well gradient gel (Bolt System, Life Technologies) and probed with the anti-ICAM-1 (sc-390483), anti-HA (Thermo A190138A), anti-GSDMD (Cell Signaling 39754), anti-KEAP1 (Cell Signaling 8047), anti-xCT/SLC7A11 (Cell Signaling 12691), anti-GPX4 (Cell Signaling 52455), anti-FTH1 (Cell Signaling 4393), anti–mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) (Cell Signaling 37705), anti–phospho-MLKL (Cell Signaling 37333), and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (sc-365062) antibodies. Signals were detected by means of chemiluminescence (Femto, Thomas Scientific) on photographic films. Digitized images were analyzed using Image J (National Institutes of Health [NIH]). Protein band densitometry was normalized to that of glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase, and the averaged results were plotted as normalized densitometry units (n.d.u.).

## Quantitative RT-PCR
RNA was extracted from heart tissues by Mirvana Paris kit and gene transcripts quantified by qPCR using Taqman assays (Applied Biosystems) for hLDLR, and IDOL. For each group, fluorescence was plotted against the number of cycles on a logarithmic scale and data normalized to the endogenous 18S control. The normalized cycle thresholds (delta Cts) were converted to fold change relative to the control group.

## In vitro macrophage migration
Monocyte migration or recruitment (cell function) were quantified using 24-transwell chambers (6.5-mm insert; 3.0-μm membrane). The lower phase was seeded with H9c2 cardiomyocytes ± hLDLR, and the upper well were seeded with wildtype or OPN-knockout (OPN-KO) mouse peritoneal macrophages (4 × 104 cells), then infected with chSARS-CoV-2 (multiplicity of infection = 0.5). After 24 hours, the chamber was fixed and stained with crystal violet 1%. Migrated cells were counted in both phases under an inverted light microscope. Macrophage migration was assessed post-chSARS-CoV-2 infection.

## Kinetic binding assay
We used a ForteBio Octet21 that monitors biomolecular interactions (protein to protein binding, quantitation, affinity, and kinetics) through Bio-Layer Interferometry based on fiberoptic biosensors. The kinetics and receptor: ligand binding activity between LDLR domain(s) and OPN was characterized via immobilization of the LDLR-Fc peptide(s) on Octet biosensors and real time measurement of OPN analyte in solution. The system was also used to study competition of LDLR peptides on the binding of OPN to LDLR.

## Proximity ligation assay
Protein-protein interactions were assessed using the proximity ligation assay (PLA). The Duolink In Situ Detection Reagents kit (DUO92106, Sigma-Aldrich) was used according to the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, samples were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against HA-tag (goat polyclonal, ThermoFisher, A190138A, 1:100) and OPN (rabbit polyclonal, Abcam, ab8448, 1:200). Following washes, samples were incubated with species-specific secondary antibodies conjugated to oligonucleotide probes (PLA probes PLUS and MINUS) for 1 hour at 37 °C. Ligation solution containing complementary oligonucleotides and ligase was then added; when the 2 PLA probes are in close proximity (<40 nm), the oligonucleotides hybridize and are ligated to form a closed circular DNA template. Rolling circle amplification was subsequently performed by adding amplification solution containing polymerase, nucleotides, and fluorescently labeled oligonucleotides. The oligonucleotide arm of 1 PLA probe serves as a primer for rolling circle amplification, generating a concatemeric product extending from the circularized template. Fluorescently labeled oligonucleotides hybridize to the amplified product, producing discrete fluorescent puncta visible by confocal microscopy (Leica Stellaris 5). Each fluorescent spot represents a single protein-protein interaction event. As a positive control, the known CD44:OPN interaction was assessed under identical conditions using antibodies against CD44 (rabbit polyclonal, Abcam, ab157107, 1:200) and OPN (goat polyclonal, R&D Systems, AF808, 1:100).

## Statistical analysis
Data are reported as mean ± SEM. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. For comparisons among 3 or more groups, normally distributed data were analyzed by 1-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey's post hoc test, whereas non-normally distributed data were analyzed by the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn's post hoc test. For comparisons of 2 groups, the unpaired 2-tailed Student's t-test was applied to normally distributed data, and the Mann-Whitney U test was applied to non-normally distributed data. Concentration-response curves and EC50 values for LDLR–OPN and LDLR–PCSK9 binding were calculated by nonlinear regression using a 4-parameter logistic model. Kinetic binding constants (kon, koff, KD) from bio-layer interferometry were derived by global curve fitting of association and dissociation data. Analyses were performed using Graph Pad Prism, version 9.0.0 (GraphPad Software). A 2-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Cardiac-specific overexpression of hLDLR causes myocarditis in K18-hACE2tg mice infected with chSARS-CoV-2
To investigate the role of the LDLR in COVID-19–associated myocarditis, we utilized K18-hACE2 transgenic mice, that express hACE2 and are susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 infection. We administered intravenous injections of AAV9 vectors (1 × 1012 viral particles) to achieve cardiac-specific overexpression of hLDLR (AAV9-cTnT-hLDLR), LDLR degradation via IDOL (AAV9-cTnT-IDOL), or luciferase as a control (AAV9-cTnT-Luciferase). Four weeks post-AAV delivery, mice were infected intratracheally with ∼107 IU of chSARS-CoV-2, and hearts were analyzed 5 days postinfection ( ). Western blot confirmed robust cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression in the AAV9-cTnT-hLDLR group ( ). Histopathological analysis using Dallas criteria revealed that only the LDLR-overexpressing group infected with chSARS-CoV-2 developed myocarditis, with 100% penetrance (9/9 mice), characterized by immune cell infiltration, cardiomyocyte necrosis, and degeneration ( ). Compared with the control group, the LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group evidenced significantly higher inflammatory area ratio relative to total heart tissue (3.6% ± 1.1% vs 0.0% ± 0.0%; P < 0.001) and foci number (5.4 ± 1.1 foci/section vs 0.0 ± 0.0 foci/section; P < 0.001) ( ). The LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group developed major immune foci distribution in the left ventricle and right atria evidenced in a 3-dimensional reconstruction (0.85% ± 0.26% vs 0.0% ± 0.0%) ( ). Compared with controls, the LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group exhibited significantly greater infiltration of CD3+ T cells (49.9 ± 9.9 cells/mm2 vs 3.2 ± 0.9 cells/mm2; P < 0.001) and CD68+ macrophages (97.1 ± 13.5 cells/mm2 vs 3.3 ± 0.8 cells/mm2; P < 0.001), with a macrophage-to-lymphocyte ratio of 2.57 ± 0.5 vs 1.69 ± 0.9 (P < 0.05) ( ). Expression of inflammatory markers, including ICAM-1 (9.3% ± 1.8% vs 0.3% ± 0.07%; P < 0.001), phosphorylated Akt (15.5% ± 2.6% vs 1.6% ± 0.4%; P < 0.001), and OPN (16.0% ± 3.1% vs 0.5% ± 0.1%; P < 0.001), were markedly elevated in the LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group compared with controls ( ). Notably, IDOL-mediated LDLR degradation in IDOL+chSARS-CoV-2 hearts blocked the increased levels of pAkt, OPN, and ICAM-1 and abrogated myocarditis ( ). The results establish cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression as both necessary and sufficient to drive myocarditis in response to chSARS-CoV-2 infection, recapitulating the inflammatory hallmarks of human COVID-19 myocarditis. The predominance of CD68+ macrophages over CD3+ T cells aligns with clinical observations of macrophage-driven inflammation in COVID-19 myocarditis. Up-regulation of ICAM-1, a known mediator of leukocyte adhesion in acute myocarditis, and its suppression by IDOL-mediated LDLR degradation suggests that LDLR modulates inflammatory cell recruitment via adhesion molecule pathways. Heart tissue sections showed LDLR-positive cardiomyocytes surrounded by OPN-expressing inflammatory cells, indicating immune activation associated with COVID-19–induced myocarditis. High-magnification images confirmed inflammatory infiltration and cardiomyocyte degradation consistent with ongoing myocardial injury ( ). These findings highlight LDLR as a critical regulator of the inflammatory cascade in this model. 

## Pyroptosis mediates myocardial injury in LDLR-driven myocarditis
To elucidate the cell death mechanisms underlying LDLR-mediated myocarditis, we analyzed markers of pyroptosis, ferroptosis, and necroptosis in heart tissues by Western blot. The LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group exhibited significant up-regulation of GSDMD, a key effector of pyroptosis, compared with luciferase and LDLR controls (28,796 ± 7,755 n.d.u. vs 1,173 ± 473 vs 599.7 ± 0.0 n.d.u.; P < 0.05, respectively) ( and ). In contrast, no consistent changes were observed in ferroptosis (KEAP1, xCT/SLC7A11, GPX4, FTH1) or necroptosis markers (MLKL, phospho-MLKL) ( ). To confirm the role of pyroptosis, we treated mice with the GSDMD inhibitor DMF (30 mg/kg intraperitoneally daily for 5 days) or vehicle. DMF treatment significantly reduced inflammatory foci (1.2 ± 0.7 foci/section vs 3.8 ± 0.7 foci/section; P < 0.02) and myocardial injury in the LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 group ( ). The selective activation of GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis in this model underscores its role as the dominant cell death pathway in LDLR-driven COVID-19 myocarditis. The absence of ferroptosis or necroptosis suggests specificity in the inflammatory response, consistent with reports linking inflammasome-driven pyroptosis to COVID-19 cardiac injury. The protective effect of DMF highlights GSDMD inhibition as a promising therapeutic strategy, potentially mitigating the cytokine storm and myocardial damage observed in clinical settings. 

## LDLR directly binds OPN to facilitate inflammatory cell recruitment
We hypothesized that the role of LDLR in myocarditis involves novel ligand interactions driving immune cell recruitment. Using concentration-dependent binding assays, we identified a previously unrecognized interaction between LDLR and OPN (EC50 = 0.22 μmol/L), with binding affinity comparable to the positive control LDLR-PCSK9 interaction (EC50 = 0.4 μmol/L) ( and ). The LDLR ectodomain, comprising 7 cysteine-rich repeats (CR1-CR7), was mapped, revealing that OPN binds specifically to LDLR domains CR2 to CR5 (EC50 = 0.46 μmol/L) but not CR2 alone, indicating structural requirements ( , and ). To determine kinetic binding of OPN to LDLR, we performed an Octet binding assay wherein LDL-R-Fc was loaded onto antihuman-IgG-Fc biosensors and tested for association/dissociation to OPN-His. Binding constants and global fits were calculated for association and dissociation of each analyte to LDL-R-Fc ( ). In support of ligation in situ, LDLR+chSARS-CoV-2 mouse hearts revealed OPN-LDLR interaction in infiltrating immune cells and LDLR cardiomyocytes. This interaction was validated using a PLA, incorporating the established CD44–OPN complex as positive control ( ). Immunofluorescence imaging further demonstrated LDLR-OPN colocalization at sites of LDLR-expressing cardiomyocytes and OPN-expressing immune cells, as well as within α-smooth muscle actin-positive vascular smooth muscle cells and activated fibroblasts ( ), confirming this interaction occurs across multiple intracardiac cell types. To assess functional significance, we used an in vitro transwell assay with H9c2 cardiomyocytes overexpressing LDLR cocultured with wildtype or OPN-knockout (OPN-KO) mouse peritoneal macrophages post-chSARS-CoV-2 infection. Compared with GFP control plus chSARS-CoV-2, LDLR overexpression significantly enhanced migration of wildtype macrophages but not OPN-KO macrophages in both migration phases (Transwell—upper phase 14.1 ± 1.6 macrophages/mm2 vs 7.5 ± 0.3 macrophages/mm2; P < 0.01; and plate—lower phase 24.4 ± 1.0 macrophages/mm2 vs 8.8 ± 0.2 macrophages/mm2; P < 0.001), confirming that the LDLR-OPN interaction is essential for monocyte recruitment ( ). The discovery of LDLR as a novel receptor for OPN represents a paradigm shift in our understanding of LDLR roles beyond lipid metabolism. The specific binding to CR2-CR5 domains suggests a structurally defined interaction that facilitates macrophage recruitment, a hallmark of COVID-19 myocarditis. The abrogation of macrophage migration in OPN-KO conditions further validates the functional relevance of this interaction, positioning the LDLR-OPN axis as a critical driver of inflammatory cell infiltration. 

## Elevated expression of LDLR and ICAM-1 in hearts of myocarditis patients
To evaluate the translational relevance of our findings, we analyzed cardiac tissues from autopsies of myocarditis (n = 3) and nonmyocarditis (n = 4) patients. Immunohistochemistry and Western blot revealed significantly higher LDLR (16.5% ± 1.8% vs 2.2% ± 0.9%; P < 0.001) and ICAM-1 (11.8% ± 2.3% vs 0.3% ± 0.1%; P = 0.002) expression in myocarditis cases compared with controls ( ). These findings align with our mouse model data and prior reports of elevated cardiac LDLR in heart failure and COVID-19 patients, , suggesting LDLR and ICAM-1 as biomarkers of myocarditis susceptibility. Elevated LDLR and ICAM-1 expression in human myocarditis samples is consistent with a mechanistic role of LDLR in inflammatory cell recruitment observed in our mouse model. The consistency with prior studies linking LDLR to heart failure and COVID-19 outcomes support its potential as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target. The small sample size limits generalization, but these data provide a critical translational bridge, warranting further investigation in larger cohorts ( ). 

## Discussion
In this study, we establish a translationally relevant model of COVID-19 myocarditis that overcomes key limitations of existing models. By combining cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression with chSARS-CoV-2 infection in hACE2-expressing mice, we reproduced the cardinal pathological features observed in patients, including macrophage-predominant infiltration, ICAM-1 up-regulation, and extensive cardiomyocyte necrosis, without relying on supraphysiologic viral inoculation or autoimmune priming. This model addresses gaps in prior approaches that fail to capture the extent of inflammatory cell recruitment characteristic of human COVID-19 myocarditis, and provides a translationally relevant platform for mechanistic and therapeutic discovery. , 
Our discovery that LDLR overexpression is necessary and sufficient to trigger myocarditis following SARS-CoV-2 spike infection constitutes a breakthrough in our understanding of myocarditis etiology following SARS-CoV-2 infections and a novel example of refunctioning a host protein in response to viral infection. LDLR family also bind Spike, potentially constituting a viral hijack; indeed, the LDLR recruitment of monocytes could also be considered a host hijack because it initially assists viral spread that ultimately backfires into cytokine storm and excessive cell death. Abrogation of the entire myocarditis phenotype by IDOL-mediated depletion of LDLR provides an essential confirmation and positions LDLR as a proximal regulator of the myocarditis inflammatory cascade. The almost 10-fold increased expression of LDLR in human myocarditis autopsies supports clinical relevance of our findings. We previously reported similarly increased levels of cardiac LDLR associated with heart failure patients relative to nonfailing controls. Consistent with this, emerging evidence further links dyslipidemia and LDLR signaling to exacerbated COVID-19 outcomes, including endothelial dysfunction and atherogenic inflammation potentially amplifying cardiac vulnerability in patients with preexisting metabolic conditions. , Evidence has shown that SARS-CoV-2 directly infects coronary vessels, inducing plaque inflammation that could intersect with LDLR-mediated pathways to promote acute cardiovascular complications. 
We further demonstrate that monocyte recruitment relies on the binding of LDLR with OPN through CR2-CR5 domains with nanomolar binding affinity. The interaction is indispensable as shown by the failure of postinfection OPN knockout macrophages to migrate toward LDLR-overexpressing cardiomyocytes, revealing an unexpected immunomodulatory role for LDLR, potentially linking dyslipidemia and proinflammatory signaling in cardiovascular injury. Because of the known association of OPN with COVID-19 severity, this axis may be particularly relevant to patients with preexisting cardiometabolic disease. High circulating OPN levels have been correlated with severe COVID-19 and multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children, underscoring its role in amplifying inflammation and organ damage. Furthermore, OPN elevation in inflammatory lung disease, including SARS-CoV-2 cases, has been documented, suggesting broader implications for monocyte-driven pathologies. Although our cardiac-specific overexpression model demonstrates LDLR-OPN binding primarily on cardiomyocytes, LDLR is also expressed in fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and vascular smooth muscle cells. We have previously demonstrated LDLR-OPN colocalization in renal tissue, and we now observe similar colocalization in cardiac tissue at both cardiomyocyte-immune cell interfaces and within smooth muscle actin-positive smooth muscle cells and myofibroblasts ( ). The relative contribution of LDLR-OPN interactions in each cell type to inflammatory cell recruitment and myocardial injury remains an important area for investigation that may reveal further cell type–specific mechanisms of immune modulation.
Among the major inflammatory cell death pathways, only GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis was activated in LDLR-driven myocarditis. Pharmacologic inhibition with DMF significantly reduced myocardial inflammation and injury, suggesting a targeted therapeutic opportunity. This is also consistent with reports implicating inflammasome activation and pyroptosis in COVID-19 cardiac injury. Recent evidence emphasizes pyroptosis as a key driver of SARS-CoV-2–induced cytokine storm and tissue injury with GSDMD pores facilitating interleukin-1β release in infected cells. , Pyroptosis has been linked specifically to cardiac complications in COVID-19, reinforcing its relevance beyond general inflammation. 

## Translational implications
The findings suggest multiple intervention points: 1) LDLR modulation, pharmacologic degradation, or functional blockade of LDLR may prevent immune cell recruitment and myocardial injury; 2) OPN–LDLR interaction blockade, targeting this high-affinity binding, could selectively limit monocyte-driven inflammation without broadly suppressing immunity; and 3) pyroptosis inhibition agents such as DMF or novel GSDMD inhibitors may mitigate acute cardiac damage. The identification of LDLR and ICAM-1 up-regulation in human myocarditis underscores the potential of these molecules as biomarkers for risk stratification or early diagnosis, consistent with reports of persistent myocardial injury in COVID-19 survivors. Moreover, studies have shown elevated risks of myocarditis postvaccination or infection, highlighting the need for targeted therapies in susceptible populations. 

## Study limitations and future directions
Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, although cardiac-specific LDLR overexpression via AAV9 effectively demonstrated the receptor’s causal role in driving COVID-19 myocarditis in our model, this genetic manipulation does not fully recapitulate the physiological, multifactorial regulation of LDLR in human disease, which likely involves inflammatory cues, dyslipidemia, and possible direct or indirect viral effects. Furthermore, this approach confines LDLR–OPN interactions predominantly to cardiomyocytes, whereas in native disease, LDLR is expressed across multiple cardiac cell types, including fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and vascular smooth muscle cells. Cell type–specific LDLR manipulation and single-cell spatial transcriptomics will be required to delineate the relative contributions of each compartment to inflammatory recruitment and myocardial injury.
Second, the human myocarditis specimens examined were heterogeneous in etiology, including lymphocytic and Chagas-associated cases rather than exclusively COVID-19 myocarditis, and the cohort size limits statistical power for translational correlations. However, the >10-fold elevation of LDLR in myocarditis autopsy hearts, consistent with prior reports in heart failure, and the fact that GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis has been implicated across diverse myocarditis forms, including viral lymphocytic and immune checkpoint inhibitor–induced disease, , , support the broader relevance of the LDLR–OPN–pyroptosis axis beyond COVID-19. Validation in larger, etiology-stratified human cohorts is an important next step.
Third, the use of a chimeric spike virus (chSARS-CoV-2), enables safe BSL-2 studies, but does not capture the full repertoire of SARS-CoV-2 proteins that may modulate cardiac injury. Emerging evidence links prolonged elevation of free spike protein in circulation to post-mRNA vaccine myocarditis, and whether elevated spike antigen amplifies LDLR–OPN–mediated monocyte recruitment through a shared mechanism remains to be determined. Future studies in authentic SARS-CoV-2 infection models and mRNA vaccine platforms will be needed to test this directly and to broaden the model's applicability to vaccine-associated myocarditis. 
Finally, although our in vitro assays with purified proteins convincingly demonstrate direct LDLR-OPN binding and sufficient OPN-dependent macrophage chemotaxis, cell-surface heparan sulfate proteoglycans may modulate or enhance this interaction in vivo. Precise structural epitopes mediating binding remain to be resolved by crystallography or cryo-EM. Additional priorities include testing LDLR-targeted therapies and GSDMD inhibitors in established myocarditis models, assessing the in vivo modifiability of the LDLR–OPN interaction with small molecules or biologics, and evaluating whether circulating OPN and LDLR levels serve as actionable biomarkers in prospective clinical cohorts. Together, these directions will refine the mechanistic framework established here and accelerate translation of the LDLR–OPN axis toward therapeutic application.

## Conclusions
We identify LDLR as a mechanistic and targetable driver of COVID-19 myocarditis through a novel interaction with OPN and activation of GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis. These findings reframe LDLR from a metabolic receptor to an immune amplifier, providing new therapeutic opportunities for COVID-19–associated and potentially other forms of viral myocarditis.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: COVID-19–associated myocarditis is characterized by macrophage-predominant inflammation and adverse long-term cardiac outcomes; yet, its molecular drivers have remained poorly defined. This study identifies cardiac LDLR as a central mediator of myocardial inflammation following SARS-CoV-2 spike protein exposure, operating through a previously unrecognized direct interaction with OPN that drives monocyte recruitment and gasdermin D–mediated pyroptosis. Clinicians should recognize that dyslipidemia and elevated cardiac LDLR expression, observed at >10-fold higher levels in human myocarditis autopsy specimens, may confer heightened susceptibility to inflammatory myocardial injury in the setting of COVID-19 infection, long COVID syndrome, or mRNA vaccination. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for the clinical observation that patients with preexisting cardiometabolic disease face disproportionate risk of COVID-19 cardiac complications and highlight LDLR and ICAM-1 as candidate biomarkers for early identification of at-risk individuals.
TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: The LDLR–OPN axis identified in this study represents a novel, targetable pathway with multiple intervention points for viral or COVID-19 myocarditis. Future studies should assess whether pharmacologic LDLR modulation, through agents such as PCSK9 inhibitors, which up-regulate LDLR in the context of systemic lipid lowering, or targeted IDOL-mediated degradation strategies, differentially affects myocardial inflammatory susceptibility in the setting of viral infection. The therapeutic benefit of gasdermin D inhibition demonstrated here with dimethyl fumarate supports advancement of GSDMD-targeted agents into preclinical myocarditis models and, ultimately, early-phase clinical trials in patients with confirmed viral myocarditis. Larger human cohort studies examining cardiac LDLR and ICAM-1 expression alongside circulating OPN levels are needed to validate their utility as diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers and to define the patient populations most likely to benefit from LDLR–OPN pathway–directed therapy.
