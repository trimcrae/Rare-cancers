## Highlights
•CCR9 and CCL25 levels were increased in myocardial tissues from ADR-injured mice or ADR-injured HL-1 cardiomyocytes.
•CCR9/CCL25-mediated signaling machinery mitigated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through multiple pathological mechanism.
•AMPK serves as a cardinal molecule mediating CCR9/CCL25 signaling.
•Lithospermic acid alleviated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through regulation of CCR9/CCL25-AMPK signaling.

## Introduction
Adriamycin (ADR)-induced cardiomyopathy is a common problem in many cancer survivors. Recently, specific chemokine receptors have garnered interest as therapeutic targets in cardiovascular diseases.

## Objectives
This study aim to report the role of C–C chemokine receptor 9 (CCR9)/C–C chemokine ligand 25 (CCL25) and its therapeutic potential in ADR-induced cardiomyopathy.

## Methods
Functional gene knockout and overexpression mouse models were utilized to investigate the role of CCR9 against ADR-induced cardiomyopathy. Transcriptome sequencing was also performed to identify the downstream molecular mechanisms of CCR9.

## Results
This study revealed that CCR9 and CCL25 levels were increased in mice and HL-1 cells injured by ADR, consistent with the results of patients with heart failure. Both in vivo and in vitro, CCR9 overexpression overtly aggravated cardiac dysfunction, accompanied by decreased AMPK activity and increased mitochondrial dysfunction, fibrosis, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. However, the cardiac harmful effects of ADR were reserved by CCR9 knockdown, as well as CCR9 overexpression aggravated cardiotoxicity were reserved by AMPK agonist GSK621. By constructing different domain-missing CCR9 mutants, we suspected that the △4 region of CCR9 is important for AMPK activity. Furthermore, transcriptome sequencing  further illustrated the mechanism of CCR9 overexpression aggravated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, which was associated with CYP1A1. Finally, lithospermic acid (LA) was screened and alleviated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through regulation of CCR9/CCL25-AMPK signaling, bolstering CCR9-targeted potential clinical application.

## Conclusion
These findings present a promising target and drug for treating chemotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity.

## Introduction
Adriamycin (ADR) is a first-line chemotherapeutic drug widely used in the treatment of a variety of malignant tumors . However, its clinical use is hindered by dose-dependent cardiotoxicity and an increased risk of heart failure, which is a leading cause of death in cancer patients . Previous studies have shown ADR-induced cardiotoxicity is attributed to complex mechanisms, including oxidative stress, inflammatory response, mitochondrial function, myocardial fibrosis, and apoptosis , . Inflammation is an important pathological basis of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity , , it can induce the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-18, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), activate inflammatory and immune responses, thereby aggravating myocardial cell apoptosis and death , .
Dexrazoxane remains the cardiac protective drug approved by FDA for the prevention of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity , but it is associated with a reduced tumor response rate to ADR and a possible risk of secondary malignancy , greatly limiting its use. In addition, many studies have been focused on the protective effects of drugs and natural products (Melatonin, Metformin, and Psoralidin) against ADR-induced cardiotoxicity , . However, at present, the clinical efficacy and safety of cardioprotective agents used in patients receiving anthracycline-based chemotherapy have not met expectations. Therefore, a better understanding of the mechanism of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity can offer directions to develop new treatments and drug.
Chemokines and their receptors play a prominent role in overseeing and regulating the cell communication, immunity, and inflammatory responses. Chemokines can be divided into four groups based on the position and number of N-terminal cysteine residues: CXC, CX3C, (X)C, and CC group. Among the CC group, CCL25 can promote the activation, proliferation and chemotaxis of immune cells to the inflammatory response site by interacting with its specific receptor CCR9 (a kind of G protein-coupled receptors) . Previous studies on CCR9 have mainly focused on cancer and inflammatory diseases , , . Regarding the cardiovascular system, Huang and his team confirmed that CCR9 knockout can improve ventricular structural and electrical remodeling in mice post-myocardial infarction by reducing infarction area, inhibiting inflammation and fibrosis , . Moreover, CCR9 deficiency attenuated pressure overload-induced hypertrophy, whereas CCR9 overexpression strikingly enhanced cardiac hypertrophy . However, there are currently no studies reporting the role of CCR9/CCL25 in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity. Therefore, inhibition of CCR9/CCL25 pathway may not only effectively suppress the growth and metastasis of various tumors, but also may effectively reduce ADR-induced cardiotoxicity (if any), so as to may achieve the dual effect of attenuating toxicity and enhancing efficacy, which is of great research value and a potential therapeutic strategy for cancer patients.
Lithospermic acid (LA), a phenolic acid compound isolated from the root and rhizoma of Salvia miltiorrhiza, is known to possess pharmacological activities, including reducing inflammatory response , scavenging free radicals , activating autophagy and inhibiting apoptosis . Due to its multiple pharmacological benefits, LA has shown promise in the treatment of various diseases , , . In cardiovascular studies, LA plays a crucial role in myocardial infarction and coronary heart disease . However, whether LA plays an important role in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through the CCR9/CCL25 pathway remains unclear.
This study aim to investigate the regulatory role of CCR9/CCL25 and AMPK in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, and to verify whether CYP1A1 is the key target of CCR9 involved in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity by RNA-seq and experiment. Finally, this study screened a natural compound targeting CCR9 (LA) from a natural compound library and assessed the efficacy of the screened natural product in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity. Taken together, the results of this study highlight a novel and translationally important CCR9-based therapeutic treatment for the cardiomyopathy induced by anti-cancer drugs.

## Methods

## Patients
Human myocardial tissues (left atrial appendage) were obtained from the patients with heart failure undergoing heart transplant or surgery and from non-heart failure patients undergoing heart transplantation or surgery for other reasons. Obtained heart samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen or fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde prior to the experiments. All patients written informed consent. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (Number: 2022-KY-0982–001) and was approved by the ethical guidelines of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki.

## Mice
The male Balb/c mice (25–30 g, 8–10 weeks) were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Center of Air Force Medical University (Xi’an, Shaanxi Province). All experimental protocols were conducted in compliance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals issued by the US National Institutes of Health and were approved by the Animal Care Ethics Committee of Northwestern University (Approval number: NWU-AWC-20190902M). All mice were housed in a controlled environment at a temperature of 26°C with a 12 h light/dark cycle, and were provided with ad libitum access to food and water.

## Adriamycin (ADR)-induced cardiotoxicity model
For in vitro experiment, cells were administrated with ADR (2 μM) for 12 h . Subsequently, the cells were harvested for further analysis. For in vivo experiment, mice were anesthetized by 3% inhaled isoflurane before the operation. Myocardial injury was then induced by administering a single dose of ADR at 10 mg/kg to the mice via intraperitoneal injection , . The mice were used for biochemical measurements and functional measurements on 3 or 5 d of ADR administration . Euthanasia was performed through cervical dislocation. Simply, the cervical vertebrae of mice are rapidly twisted by hand or with a tool, resulting in spinal cord injury, which causes the mouse to immediately lose consciousness and stop breathing. The mice were euthanized on the 5th day of ADR administration for the follow-up experiment, unless otherwise specified.

## Lithospermic acid (LA) and melatonin (MEL) treatment
LA (2.5 and 5 mg/kg) and MEL (30 mg/kg) were initially dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), respectively. For the pretreatment experiment, LA or MEL was administered (i.p.) 1 times every 2 d (a total of 3 times), and once at 0.5 h prior to ADR injury. Subsequently, mice were injured by ADR on the 6th day and executed on the 11th day.

## Statistical analyses
Statistical analysis was performed with GraphPad Prism version 9.0. Significant differences were evaluated using the Student’s t test and one-way, depending on experimental design. Following significant ANOVAs, multiple comparisons were performed using a Tukey’s HSD post hoc test. According to the Shapiro-Wilk normality test, the groups meet the normal distribution criteria. All data are displayed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). P<0.05 was considered as significant differences.
See Supplemental Material for Details. Detailed materials and methods used in this study are provided in the Supplement Material. The data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

## Results

## CCR9 and CCL25 levels were increased in ADR injured mice and HL-1 cardiomyocytes
To investigate the role of CCR9 in ADR-induced cardiomyopathy, an in vivo ADR-induced cardiotoxicity model was constructed and functional tests were performed on 3 and 5 d of ADR administration ( A). ADR injury increased markers of heart injury, including creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), lactic dehydrogenase (LDH), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) at both 3 and 5 d ( B). Echocardiographic analysis revealed ADR significantly decreased ejection fraction (EF), fraction shortening (FS), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), and left ventricular end diastolic volume (LVEDV), along with increased left ventricular anterior wall thickness of systole period (LVPWs) and left ventricular anterior wall thickness of diastole period (LVPWd, C). Cardiac structure and myocardial fibrosis are considered important indicators of myocardial injury. Histological staining with HE, Masson, and Sirius Red showed significantly myocardial damage and collagen deposition in ADR-injured myocardium compared to Sham group ( D-F). Subsequently, the levels of CCR9 and CCL25 mRNA and protein in the myocardium were evaluated. The results showed that ADR injury up-regulated the expression of CCR9 and CCL25 mRNA and protein ( G-H). These findings confirm that the cardiotoxicity model induced by ADR was successful establishment, with the 5 d time point was selected in vivo studies. 
Then, HL-1 cardiomyocytes were treated with ADR for different time periods (3 h, 6 h, and 12 h) in vitro. Of note, ADR injury decreased cell viability ( I) as well as increased the level of CCR9 and CCL25 mRNA and protein ( J-K) with the most noticeable effects at 12 h. Therefore, 12 h was chosen as the appropriate time point for subsequent experiments. To further confirm these findings, heart tissue-samples were collected from patients undergoing surgical treatment for heart failure or other conditions for immunostaining and Western blot ( ). As shown in A-B, the levels of CCR9 and CCL25 were increased in myocardium of patients with heart failure (red fluorescence), consistent with the Western blot results. These results suggest that CCR9 and CCL25 may play a pivotal role of in the development of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity.

## CCR9 overexpression aggravated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity
Firstly, CCR9 overexpression (CCR9-OE) mice were generated by injecting adeno-associated virus (AAV) into the tail vein and confirmed by using Western blot and qRT-PCR ( C-D). Then, the cardiac structure, function, myocardial fibrosis, and molecular mechanism alterations were examined in ADR-injured mice with CCR9-OE. As shown in A-B, CCR9-OE aggravated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, as demonstrated by decreased EF, FS, and SV, increased LVPWd and LVPWs, as well as increased myocardial injury markers LDH and AST levels. Morphological analysis and TUNEL detection revealed that, compared to AAV-Ctrl+ADR group, CCR9-OE induced more pronounced abnormalities of myocardial structure (evidenced by widened interstitial spaces, disordered and swollen muscle fibers and increased inflammatory cell infiltration, C) and increased the frequency of TUNEL-positive cells in ADR-injured mice ( D). To monitor the effects of CCR9 on fibrosis, Masson staining and Sirius red staining were performed. As shown in E-F, obvious collagen fiber deposition in the ADR-injured myocardium was observed, CCR9-OE aggravated the degree of myocardial fibrosis. Immunohistochemistry, qRT-PCR, and Western blot results showed that CCR9 and CCL25 levels were significantly elevated in the myocardium of AAV-CCR9-OE+ADR mice ( E and G-H). Subsequently, the mechanisms of the ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, such as inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6), oxidative stress (Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1), apoptosis (Caspase 3 p17, Bcl2, and Bax), and mitochondrial function (NRF1 and TFAM) were assay. Compared to the AAV-Ctrl+ADR group, CCR9-OE significantly increased TNF-α mRNA level, and slight increased IL-1β and IL-6 mRNA levels, but the differences were not statistically significant ( G), upregulated Caspase 3 p17 protein expression, downregulated Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1 protein expression ( H). 
To verify the mechanisms in vitro, CCR9-OE cardiomyocytes were constructed ( F-G). CCR9-OE prominently decreased cell viability ( A), increased apoptosis ( B) and intracellular ROS ( C) in ADR-injured HL-1 cells. Molecularly, immunofluorescence staining result showed that CCR9-OE significantly increased the expression of CCR9 ( D). Moreover, CCR9-OE also led to an up-regulation of CCR9, CCL25, and Caspase 3 p17, as well as a down-regulation of Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, Nrf2, and HO-1 in ADR-injured cells ( E). Together, these in vivo and in vitro findings suggest that CCR9-OE aggravated the pathology of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, including fibrosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis.

## CCR9 knockdown attenuated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity
Given the evident exacerbation of ADR-induced cardiotoxicity observed in CCR9-OE mice or HL-1 cells, this study sought to examine whether CCR9 knockdown (CCR9-KD) would produce opposite results. Likewise, CCR9-KD mice were constructed via AAV (AAV-CCR9-KD, A-B), and followed by ADR administration. Echocardiography revealed that CCR9-KD improved cardiac systolic and diastolic functions, as depicted by increased EF, FS, SV, CO, LVESV, and LVEDV, and decreased LVPWd and LVPWs in ADR-injured mice ( A). Consistently, overtly improved myocardial damage ( B), decreased apoptosis ( C), and alleviated myocardial fibrosis ( D-E) were also observed in AAV-CCR9-KD+ADR mice, suggesting that CCR9-KD improved cardiac function, structure and fibrosis in ADR-injured mice. Moreover, the mRNA levels of CCR9 and CCL25 together with inflammatory factors TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 were significantly decreased in the CCR9-KD+ADR group ( F). CCR9-KD also significantly down-regulated protein levels of CCR9 and CCL25 in ADR-injured mice ( C). Furthermore, compared to the AAV-Ctrl+ADR group, CCR9-KD increased Nrf2, NQO1, HO-1, NRF1, and TFAM protein expression, decreased Caspase 3 p17, CCR9, and CCL25 protein expression in ADR-injured mice ( G). 
To determine whether CCR9-KD in vitro yielded protective effects similar to those observed in vivo experiments. CCR9 siRNA targeting HL-1 cells were synthesized to generate CCR9-deficiency cells (si-CCR9, D-E). The results demonstrated si-CCR9 visibly increased cell viability ( A), reduced apoptosis ( B) and intracellular ROS accumulation ( C) in ADR-injured HL-1 cells. In addition, si-CCR9 not only down-regulated levels of CCR9 ( D-E) but also affected multiple ADR injury-related pathways in HL-1 cells. This was supported by evidence of inhibited apoptosis (increases in Bcl2/Bax and decreases in Caspase 3 p17, E), oxidative stress (increase in Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1, E) as well as enhanced mitochondrial function (increase in NRF1 and TFAM, E). These data indicate that CCR9 inhibition distinctly protected against ADR-induced cardiotoxicity both in vivo and in vitro.

## AMPK participated in the role of CCR9/CCL25 pathways in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity
AMPK acts as an energy sensor and imparts both acute and chronic adaptations to stress conditions , which has been demonstrated to play a protective role against ADR-induced cardiotoxicity . In ADR-injured mice and HL-1 cells, the levels of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC were down-regulated ( A-B). While CCR9-KD significantly increased the levels of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC in ADR-injured mice ( C), consistent with the results obtained from ADR-injured cells with si-CCR9 ( D). Conversely, in CCR9-OE myocardium and HL-1 cells, the levels of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC were down-regulated ( E-F). These results imply that CCR9 plays a crucial role in regulating AMPK activity. 

## Effects of CCR9 overexpression were abolished by AMPK agonist (GSK621)
To further clarify the regulatory relationship between AMPK and CCR9, GSK621 (an agonist of AMPK) was used. HL-1 cells were treated with the 10, 20, and 50 μM GSK621 to assess toxicity. GSK621 treatment did not result in any significant changes in cell viability ( A), indicating the safety of GSK621. Moreover, 50 μM GSK621 effectively activated AMPK/ACC pathway at 6 h ( B-C), which were then administrated to HL-1 cells with CCR9-OE for next experiment. Compared with the CCR9-OE+ADR group, GSK621 treatment resulted in increased cell viability ( A), decreased cell apoptosis ( B) and intracellular ROS accumulation ( C). At a molecular level, GSK621 not only activated the AMPK/ACC pathway but also inhibited the CCR9/CCL25 pathway ( D). Additionally, the up-regulated levels of Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1 were also observed in the CCR9-OE+G+ADR group (G represents GSK621, D). Combined with the above results suggest that GSK621 reversed the harmful effects of CCR9-OE against cardiomyocytes injury, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis. 
Besides, according to the structure of CCR9, this study further constructed truncations missing in different regions of CCR9, including CCR9-△1 (1–74), CCR9-△2 (1–109), CCR9-△3 (1–150), CCR9-△4 (1–185), CCR9-△5 (1–243), CCR9-△6 (1–283), CCR9-△7 (1–321) and CCR9-Full Length (FL) ( E). These truncations were transfected into HL-1 cells, and the changes in CCL25 and AMPK-related molecules were analyzed. As depicted in F, transfection with CCR9-FL resulted in the inhibition of the p-AMPK/AMPK level compared to Ctrl group. However, CCR9-△4 missing inhibited CCL25 level while increased p-AMPK level. Therefore, we speculate that the △4 region of CCR9 may play a crucial role in AMPK activity. Above, these data denoted that AMPK might be a negative regulator downstream of CCR9 in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity.

## RNA-seq further elucidated the mechanism of CCR9-OE aggravated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity
RNA-seq was employed to fully understand the pathological regulatory mechanisms of CCR9-OE aggravated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity. A total of 480 up-regulated and 438 down-regulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between Ctrl+ADR and CCR9-OE+ADR group ( A-B). Functional enrichment analysis (KEGG and GO) revealed that these DEGs were principally enriched in drug metabolism-cytochrome P450 or metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450 signaling pathways ( A and C). Cytochrome P450 family is important in mitochondria function and metabolism of drugs including ADR . A heatmap showed that, compared with the Ctrl+ADR group, CCR9-OE overtly increased cytochrome P450-related genes including CBR1, GSTT1, CBR3, GSTP2, GSTK1, CYP1A1, AIDH1A3, and HSD11B1 in ADR-injured cells ( B). Among them, CCR9-OE also led to a decrease in AMPK (PRKAA1, is the catalytic alpha-subunit of 5′-AMPK) expression, which was consisted with our results ( B). Subsequently, qRT-PCR verified the RNA-seq results ( C). Analysis of the protein–protein interaction (PPI) network indicated that CYP1A1 may be a center target in CCR9-OE-aggravated cardiotoxicity induced by ADR ( D). 
As shown in E, the protein levels of CYP1A1 were significantly elevated in the CCR9-OE+ADR group. However, administration of GSK621 led to a decrease of CYP1A1 level in CCR9-OE+ADR group ( F). Then, CYP1A1 siRNA was applied in HL-1 cells to verify the role of CYP1A1 in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity. As shown in G-I, compared to si-Ctrl+ADR, the higher cell viability together with the reduced numbers of apoptotic cell and the lower ROS accumulation was observed in the si-CYP1A1+ADR group. In addition, molecular experiments showed that CYP1A1 siRNA decreased CYP1A1, Caspase 3 p17 and increased p-AMPK/AMPK, p-ACC/ACC, Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, while had no effects on CCR9, CCL25, Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1 in ADR-injured cells ( J). Similarly, this study analyzed the protein levels of CYP1A1 after CCR9 truncation, and found that CCR9-△4 deletion significantly reduced CYP1A1 levels ( K). These findings suggest that inhibition of CYP1A1 alleviated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity, which was related to AMPK/ACC pathway, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis.

## Lithospermic acid, screened from the natural compound library, protected against ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through inhibiting CCR9
Above, CCR9/CCL25 pathway activation exerted negative roles in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity. Hence, targeting inhibition of CCR9 may be an effective therapeutic strategy. This study proceeded to screen for a CCR9 pharmacological inhibitor from natural compound library containing over 2000 kinds (TargetMol). Ultimately, the TOP10 drugs (lithospermic acid B, sennoside A, sennoside B, 1,2,3,4,6-O-pentagalloylglucose, 9'''-MethyllithosperMate B, lithospermic acid, plantainoside D, chlorrrogenic acid, CYNARIN, and lsochlorogrnic acid C) were screened according to the virtual screening and literature review. Then, HL-1 cardiomyocytes were treated with these drugs at the concentration of 10 μM. Dual-luciferase reporter assay and qRT-PCR showed that LA effectively suppressed both the transcription activity and mRNA level of CCR9 ( A-B). The safety of LA in HL-1 cells was then evaluated, with different concentrations (2.5, 5, 7.5, and 10 μM) showing no noticeable changes in cell viability ( C). LA administration also significantly decreased the protein levels of CCR9 and CCL25 while increased the protein levels of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC ( D). These results suggest that LA is an effective and safe modulator of CCR9.

## Lithospermic acid mitigated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through CCR9/CCL25-AMPK pathway in HL-1 cells
Subsequently, HL-1 cells were treated with 2.5, 5, 7.5, and 10 μM of LA for 3 h prior to ADR injury ( A). As shown in B-D, compared to ADR group, LA treatment overtly increased cell viability, decreased apoptosis and intracellular ROS. Specifically, 2.5 μM of LA showed the most significant protective effect and was chosen for further molecular mechanism study. Moreover, LA treatment not only inhibited the CCR9/CCL25 pathway but also increased the activity of AMPK and ACC, accompanied by the inhibition of apoptosis (increased Bcl-2/Bax and decreased Caspase 3 p17), the improvement of mitochondrial function (increased NRF1 and TFAM) and oxidative stress (increased Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1) in ADR-injured cells ( E). Furthermore, the beneficial effects of LA on ADR-induced cardiotoxicity were reversed by CCR9-OE, as evidenced by decreased cell viability ( F), increased apoptosis ( G) and intracellular ROS accumulation ( H). The up-regulated levels of CCR9, CCL25 and Caspase 3 p17, and down-regulated levels of p-AMPK/AMPK, p-ACC/ACC, Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, Nrf2, and NQO1 were also observed in the CCR9-OE+LA+ADR group ( I). Together, LA improves mitochondrial function, inhibits apoptosis, and oxidative stress in ADR-injured HL-1 cells, which principally depends on inhibition of CCR9 signaling. 

## Lithospermic acid mitigated ADR-induced cardiotoxicity through CCR9/CCL25-AMPK pathway in mice
Finally, this study aim to determine whether LA possessed a significant protective effect in ADR-injured mice. The mice were treated with 2.5 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg of LA, then administrated with ADR injury ( A). A marked cardiac dysfunction (including a decrease in EF, FS, CO, SV, LVEDV, LVESV, and an increase in LVPWd, LVPWs, B) and myocardial injury markers (an increase in LDH and AST, C) was reversed by 2.5 mg/kg LA treatment, but partially reversed by 5 mg/kg LA. In addition, compared with ADR group, LA treatment decreased the frequencies of TUNEL-positive cells ( D) and improved structure disorder ( E) in ADR-injured mice. Molecular mechanisms denoted LA treatment also reversed ADR-induced up-regulation of CCR9 and CCL25 levels ( ). Meanwhile, LA treatment also downregulated CYP1A1, CCR9, CCL25, Caspase 3 p17 levels, and upregulated p-AMPK/AMPK, p-ACC/ACC, Bcl2/Bax, NRF1, TFAM, and NQO1 levels ( F). 
Moreover, the role of the optimal concentration of 2.5 mg/kg LA to compare with MEL ( A), a well-known cardioprotective agent, in ADR-induced cardiotoxicity was investigated , , . As shown in B, MEL improved ADR-induced myocardial dysfunction, as evidenced by increased LVEDV, LVESV, SV, and CO. Similarly, LA brought several myocardial function closer to normal values of the MEL protection group. Furthermore, as before, LA or MEL treatment also improved structure disorder ( C) and decreased collagen deposition, interstitial fibrosis, and structural disorder ( D-E) in ADR-injured mice. To evaluate the effect of ADR on cardiomyocyte mitochondrial function, transmission electron microscopy was performed. Our data revealed pronounced myocardial damage, including disrupted mitochondria, distortion of sarcomeres and myofilaments following ADR challenge, whereas LA or MEL treatment reversed these changes ( F). Molecular mechanisms denoted that LA could reduce the levels of CCR9, and increase the levels of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC ( G). These results strongly suggested that LA exerts protective effect against ADR-induced myocardial injury through the CCR9 related signaling pathway.

## Discussion
Since cardiotoxic events of ADR are so prominent, extensive studies have been carried out to search for the protective strategies . Our research results revealed a novel role of CCR9/CCL25 in ADR-induced cardiomyopathy. Herein, we highlighted that: 1) CCR9 and CCL25 levels were increased in ADR-injured myocardium and cardiomyocytes; 2) CCR9-KD or siRNA mitigated ADR cardiotoxicity through increasing AMPK activity, whereas CCR9-OE yielded the opposite results; 3) AMPK agonist GSK621 could abolish the cardiac harmful effects induced by CCR9-OE; 4) RNA-seq further illustrated that the CCR9-OE-aggravated cardiotoxicity was related to metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450 signaling pathways, of which CYP1A1 was the key target; 5) LA was screened from the natural compound library and exerted anti-ADR cardiotoxicity through regulation of CCR9/CCL25-AMPK signaling.
Chemokine is a structure-related protein with relatively small molecular weight, composed of at least 28 members, and they exerted the physiological and pathological effects through 10 known chemokine receptors . These receptors are expressed on many inflammatory cells like neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages. Among these chemokines, CCR9 is not exclusively expressed on immune cells to regulate cells communication and may be involved in the pathogenesis of many diseases such as cardiovascular diseases and cancers . CCR9 exacerbates pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction . However, CCR9 knockout ameliorates ventricular electrical remodeling and improves the prognosis of myocardial infarction . Notably, CCR9 has been found to be highly expressed in a wide range of cancers , . In this study, we speculated that CCR9 might participate in the regulation of ADR myocardial injury. In vivo experiments showed that CCR9-OE-aggravated ADR cardiotoxicity, while CCR9-KD attenuated ADR cardiotoxicity, consistent with the in vitro results.
In addition to CCR9-mediated inflammation, accumulated evidence indicates that a wide array of pathophysiological and molecular events contributes to the development of ADR cardiotoxicity, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis , . The overproduction of inflammatory cytokines may cause severe pathological changes including oxidative stress. Meanwhile, oxidative stress also lead to the disorder of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation system and eventually apoptosis. Interference with different pathological processes is a key aspect among the molecular and cellular determinants of ADR cardiotoxicity. Fortunately, this study has shown that CCR9 could be a converging point in the complex regulatory network of ADR cardiotoxicity. To be specific, CCR9 inhibition overtly improved inflammation, inhibited oxidative stress (increase in Nrf2, NQO1, and HO-1) and apoptosis (increase in Bcl2/Bax and decrease in Caspase 3 p17), and enhanced mitochondrial function (increase in NRF1 and TFAM). On the other hand, CCR9 overexpression yielded opposite outcomes, suggesting CCR9-elicited response and mechanism in ADR cardiotoxicity. However, we did not evaluate whether CCR9 inhibition could alleviate myocardial injury while simultaneously suppress the effects of tumor growth during chemotherapy. Nonetheless, based on the results from our study and the current considerable understanding for the role of CCR9 in various cancer, it is reasonable to believe that the potential “synergy and attenuation” effects of CCR9 offer promising benefits for anti-cancer drug therapy.
Maintaining energy balance is crucial for the physiological homeostasis of cardiomyocytes. AMPK is a key energy sensor and regulates cellular metabolism to maintain energy homeostasis and restore energy balance at the cellular and physiological levels during metabolic stress . As a major regulator of energy metabolism, AMPK is mainly expressed in the liver, kidney, and heart . The first evidence of the important role of AMPK inactivation in ADR cardiotoxicity comes from a study by Tokarska-Schlattner et al. in 2005 . Short exposure of isolated perfused rat hearts to low doses of ADR (2 μM), representative of plasma levels in ADR-treated patients, led to reduced AMPK protein levels and p-AMPK as well as reduced downstream targets p-ACC . This occurred before the onset of cardiac dysfunction and strongly suggests that perturbed cardiac energy homeostasis is involved in the development of ADR cardiotoxicity . Subsequently, a number of studies have also confirmed that ADR inhibits AMPK protein expression and phosphorylation in the rat heart, contributing to the development of ADR myocardial injury , , . However, there is no research confirming a direct link between AMPK and CCR9. Therefore, in this study we focus on the CCR9 effects on AMPK/ACC in ADR-induced myocardial injury. Molecular regulatory mechanisms have revealed that AMPK serves as a critical down-regulator of CCR9-mediated signaling and effect. CCR9-OE significantly inhibited AMPK activation, while CCR9-KD increased the AMPK activity. RNA-seq analyze results have showed that CCR9-OE decrease the expression of AMPK (PRKAA1) compared with Ctrl+ADR group. Particularly, AMPK agonist GSK621 abolished CCR9-OE-aggravated myocardial injury. These data indicated that CCR9 accelerated ADR-induced cardiac dysfunction by inhibiting AMPK activation. Through constructing different domain-missing CCR9 mutants, we suspected that the △4 region of CCR9 is important for AMPK activity.
CYP1A1 is one of the main cytochromes P450 that participates in the metabolism of a vast number of xenobiotics, including ADR , . In physiological conditions, the production of CYP1A1 is inhibited, while it is induced by its substrates (such as drugs or endogenous substances). CYP1A1 participates in the metabolic clearance of anticancer drugs, making CYP1A1 enzymes inhibition a promising strategy for chemoprevention and alleviating drug-induced toxicity , . Studies also hinted that CYP1A1-mediated reactive metabolism pathways are complicated in gefitinib (a cancer targeted drug)-induced cardiotoxicity . In our study, combining RNA-seq, PPI, and in vitro experiments, we identified CYP1A1 as a critical candidate target of CCR9-OE-elicited harmful effects. CCR9-OE increased the CYP1A1 levels, while CYP1A1 siRNA displayed enhanced cell survival. Interestingly, CYP1A1 siRNA led to an increase in mitochondrial function-related proteins and a decrease in apoptosis-related protein without influencing the CCR9/CCL25 as well as oxidative stress pathways. This was attributed to the upstream of CCR9/CCL25 and the metabolism personality of CYP1A1.
AMPK and CYP1A1 mainly involves the relationship between them in response to cellular metabolic regulation and metabolism of exogenous compounds in interaction , . Research has showed that CYP1A1 knockdown significantly increased the phosphorylation of AMPK, and compound C (inhibition of AMPK) treatment partially abolished the pro-apoptotic effect induced by CYP1A1 knockdown, suggesting that the effect of CYP1A1 is partially mediated through AMPK signaling . Consistent with CYP1A1 knockdown, pharmacologic reduction of CYP1A1 levels by the phytopolyphenol carnosol also correlated with impaired proliferation and induced AMPK phosphorylation . Our study demonstrated that CYP1A1 siRNA can significantly increase the level of p-AMPK/AMPK and p-ACC/ACC. Meanwhile, activating AMPK reversed the increase in CYP1A1 caused by CCR9-OE. Taken together, these findings suggest that the effect of CYP1A1 is mediated in part through AMPK signaling.
G protein-coupled receptors have been successfully used as targets for the most commonly prescribed drugs , . Chemokine receptors, in particular, are considered highly promising drug targets for inflammation and immunity-related diseases due to it’s the low off-target effect and compensation response , . For instance, cenicriviroc (CCR2 and CCR5 antagonist) and maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist) have progressed to Phase 2 clinical trials for treating nonalcoholic steatohepatitis and immunodeficiency virus, respectively , . Currently, vercirnon (also known as CCX282-B) remains the only reported CCR9 antagonist. Unfortunately, a randomized clinical trial of vercirnon is unsuccessful due to a lack of beneficial efficacy and an increase in adverse events . Natural compounds serve as valuable resources for pinpointing lead compounds that can eventually be developed into safe and effective therapies. We screened optimal CCR9 pharmacological inhibitor from natural compounds library (over 2000 kinds) through molecular docking. Ultimately, LA displayed the best performance and was selected as the lead compound. LA, a polycyclic phenolic carboxylic acid isolated from Salvia miltiorrhiza, has also shown positive effects on the cardiovascular system, especially in atherosclerosis and myocardial ischemia–reperfusion injury , , . Our study highlights the cardioprotective properties of LA against ADR cardiotoxicity, mediated by the CCR9-CCL25/AMPK pathway. These results implicate a valuable translational treatment in cardioprotective strategies for the cardiotoxicity induced by anti-cancer drug.
This study is not without limitations. Firstly, we only focused on the mechanism of LA on CCR9/CCL25-AMPK signal, failing to elucidate other complex mechanisms and metabolic pathways in vivo. Another open question is that the potential benefit of altering the balance of CCR9 and CCL25 signaling in vivo and assessing the therapeutic window of CCR9 inhibition, which needs to be delineated in the next study. Last but not least, due to the clinical difficulty in obtaining chemotherapy-induced heart failure specimens, we collected heart tissue-samples from patients with heart failure due to other reasons, not chemotherapeutic agents, to support our in vivo and in vitro data. Since the same results and principles apply to other diseases, targeting CCR9 may be useful for heart failure in a broad range of clinical contexts.

## Conclusion
In summary, the findings of our work revealed a critical role of CCR9/CCL25 signaling in ADR-induced cardiomyopathy through fibrosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, apoptosis, for which AMPK serves as a cardinal molecule mediating CCR9/CCL25 signaling. Our results further noted a role for LA, a safe and effective CCR9 pharmacological antagonist, can act as a potential candidate for the clinical treatment of the cardiotoxicity induced by anti-cancer drug ( ).
