Exosomes (Exo) contain RNA, peptides, or proteins and communicate between cells through a bio-genesis pathway. In the present study, Gastric cancer (GC) cell exosomes containing galangin were obtained. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) in exosomes containing galangin (galangin-Exo) were screened by RNA-seq. GC cells were treated with galangin-Exo and the changes of cell proliferation were analyzed. Besides, the mouse model of GC was established to verify the effect of galangin-Exo in vivo. The results showed that galangin-Exo inhibited the growth of AGS and BGC823 cells. Additionally, RNA-seq data showed that miR-10b-5p was differentially expressed in exosomes. The reduced expression of miR-10b-5p which increased the expression of P53 inhibited the growth and induced apoptosis of GC cells. The in vivo results confirmed that exosomes can inhibit tumor growth by delivering galangin. In conclusion, our results suggest that galangin-Exo have anti-cancer effect on GC and can be used as a potential therapeutic strategy.

## Introduction
Gastric cancer (GC) is a significant disease—the third leading cause of common cancer deaths globally. At present, the main methods of treating GC remain surgery or endoscopic resection . Chemotherapy is carried out before, during, and after surgery to prolong the survival of patients. Despite the progress in pathogenesis and clinical research in recent years, the overall prognosis is still unsatisfactory . The poor early diagnosis and treatment of GC bring great inconvenience to patients, so a significant challenge is the development of effective treatment methods, such as natural compound.
Galangin is a flavonoid compound isolated from the rhizome of galangal. It is also found in honey and propolis foods and in plants such as male flowers . Galangin has antibacterial, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor effects. In recent years, many studies have shown that galangin has anticancer effects on various cancers, such as gastric, lung, liver, breast, and esophageal cancer. Studies have shown that galangin induces apoptosis of GC cells by regulating ubiquitin carboxy-terminal hydrolase isoenzyme L1 and glutathione S-transferase P . Other studies have shown that galangin inhibits GC growth by promoting STAT3-mediated reactive oxygen species production . These results suggest that galangin has a therapeutic effect on GC. Galangin also regulates miRNA, such as miR-10b-5p. MiRNA is a class of non-coding, single-stranded RNA molecules with a length of about 22 nucleotides, which are involved in gene expression regulation. MiR-10b-5p is up-regulated in GC cells. Research has shown that circ-0021977/miR-10b-5p/P21 and P53 regulatory axes inhibit the proliferation, migration, and invasion of colorectal cancer cells . Additional research has demonstrated that miR-10b induced by an acid microenvironment can promote the proliferation and metastasis of cancer cells . These results indicate that galangin can regulate miRNAs, such as miR-10b, that effect cell fate in GC.
Galangin has a therapeutic effect on tumors, however, there are only a few reports showing how to deliver it to tumor sites. Exosomes are vesicle-like bodies with a double membrane structure secreted by cells , . Exosomes can carry drugs, proteins, lipids, and RNAs, as well as mediate cell-to-cell communication between various cell types in the body with superior biocompatibility . Exosomes can transfer biomolecules to target cells and are therefore involved in cancer, inflammation and other pathological conditions – . Studies have shown that melanoma cells preferentially ingest their own exosomes compared with exosomes from other cells, indicating that exosomes have good specificity to delivery cargos . Thus, exosomes have the potential to be developed as a natural drug delivery tool in GC. Studies have shown that HGF siRNA carried in exosomes can be transported into GC cells, inhibiting their proliferation and migration . Another study applied exosomes to deliver miR-193a-3p, which significantly promoted cancer apoptosis in vitro and in vivo, and enabled real-time visualization and evaluation of gene therapy efficiency in human GC cells and mouse xenograft tumor models . Therefore, we applied exosomes encapsulation of galangin for delivery to produce better targeted treatment of GC.
In this study, the galangin-Exo were obtained to treat GC. Moreover, the specificity of exosomes secreted was confirmed by GC cells, and further analyzed the effects of galangin-Exo on the growth, migration, invasion, and apoptosis. In addition, miR-10b-5p which target P53 was screened by RNA-seq. Our findings demonstrate that galangin-Exo can reduce the level of miR-10b-5p, thereby promoting the apoptosis of AGS and BGC823 cells and inhibiting cell proliferation.

## Material and methods

## Ethics declarations
The animal study protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jilin University (SY202109003). All experiments were conducted in accordance with GB/T 35892-2018 Laboratory Animal—Guideline for ethical review of animal welfare and in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

## Cell culture and galangin treatment
Human GC cell lines AGS and BGC823 were cultured in Ham’s F-12 medium and RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) at 37℃ and 5% CO2. Human gastric epithelial cells GES1 cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) at 37℃ and 5% CO2 were used as control. GES1, AGS and BGC823 lines are from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, https://www.atcc.org/). GES1 and GC cells were treated with 50 μM galangin (Yuanye Bio-Technology, China) for 24–48 h to achieve 80–90% fusion, and were prepared for subsequent experiments.

## Preparation of exosomes and drug encapsulation
The exosomes derived from GC cells were isolated by ultracentrifugation. Firstly, GC cells was cultured for 48 h in the special medium for exosome extraction (Beyotime, China). Cell supernatant was then collected. Cell fragments were removed by centrifugation at 300 g at 4 °C for 30 min, 2,000 g for 30 min, and 12,000 g for 45 min. After filtration by a 0.22 μm membrane, centrifugation was performed at 4 °C, 110,000 g, 70 min. After the supernatant was removed, the precipitate was suspended in PBS (Meilunbio, China) and centrifuged at 110,000 g for 70 min. The ultracentrifugation was performed using an ultracentrifuge (Thermo Scientific, USA) with a rotor radius of 165.9 mm (AH-629). Finally, exosomes were re-suspended in PBS and stored at − 80 °C. BCA protein analysis kit (Tiangen, China) was used to quantify the protein content of exosomes, thus indicating the concentration of exosomes. Galangin was added to exosomes by co-incubation. As mentioned before, galangin is dissolved in DMSO. Exosomes was incubated with galangin solution at room temperature for 2 h. Finally, exosomes loaded with galangin were purified by ultracentrifugation . The exosomes were lysed by heating to release galangin and diluted. After detection by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), the loading of galangin in every 10 μg exosomes was calculated to be 6 μg.

## Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
Exosomes containing galangin were immobilized with 2% PFA for 5 min. Then, the exosomes suspension was loaded into the grid and incubated for 10 min. The EM mesh surface was stained with 1% uranyl acetate solution for 2 min. The excess UA solution was then removed from the mesh with filter paper and dried at room temperature. The morphology of exosomes was observed by transmission electron microscope (Hitachi, HT-7800, Japan).

## Nanoparticle tracking analysis
The particle size and concentration of exosomes were measured by NTA using NanoFCM instrument (Flow NanoAnalyzer, China). The exosomes obtained by ultrafast centrifugation were diluted with PBS, and then samples were sent for detection. Exosomes sample loading can be carried out after passing the instrument performance test with the standard product, and the sample is gradient diluted to avoid clogging the injection needle.

## High-sensitivity flow cytometry (HSFCM)
Dilute 40 μL exosomes to 120 μL, add 20 μL fluorescently labeled antibodies (IgG, CD9, CD63, CD81) to 30 μL diluted exosomes solution, and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min away from light. The precooled PBS was added 1 mL and centrifuged at 110,000g at 4 °C for 70 min. After the supernatant was removed, 1 mL of pre-cooled PBS was added and centrifuged again at 110,000g at 4 °C for 70 min. The supernatant was removed and resuspended with 50 μL of precooled PBS. The results of the protein index detected by the NanoFCM instrument could be obtained.

## In vitro tracking of exosomes by PKH-26 staining
To track in vitro uptake of exosomes, the PKH-26 kit (Umibio, China) was used to label exosomes according to the manufacturer’s procedures. The exosomes were incubated with 500 μL diluted C solution and 4 μL PKH-26 dye solution in a dark environment at room temperature for 5 min. Then 500 μL 1% bovine serum albumin was added to stop the staining. After 70 min of super centrifugation at 100,000g, 200 μL PBS was used for suspension to obtain labeled exosomes. To determine whether exosomes could be internalized by cells, cells were cultured to 70–80% fusion in a six-well plate. Then PKH-26-labeled exosomes (22.5 μg/mL) were added and incubated with the cells for 0, 12, and 24 h. Direct observation was conducted with laser scanning confocal microscopy (LSCM, Olympus, Japan).

## RNA-seq analysis
GC cells were treated with 50 μM galangin for 48 h, and then galangin-Exo were isolated by ultracentrifugation. Then DNBSEQ platform (Beijing Genomics Institute, BGI, China) was used to detect small RNA in exosomes. The Bowtie2 software was used to compare the sequencing readings with the reference sequence. DEGseq and DESeq programs were used for gene expression analysis and differential gene expression analysis (Fold Change > 0.5, FDR < 0.001). ClusterProfiler was used for enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes, including the Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway – . The data were analyzed on the Dr. Tom network platform of BGI. The data were submitted to the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (GSE288835).

## Knockdown and overexpression of miR-10b-5p
Mimics and inhibitors of miR-10b-5p were obtained from GenePharma (China). GC cells were transfected with mimics-miR-10b-5p and inhibitors-miR-10b-5p for 24–48 h, and control cells were transfected with non-specific mimics and inhibitors (Nc-mimics and Nc-inhibitor).

## Real-time PCR
Total RNA was isolated from GES1 and GC cells, and tumor samples using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). The cDNA were synthesized according to instructions using the FastKing RT kit (Tiangen, China). Gene expression was analyzed by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR).The qPCR reaction conditions were as follows: heating at 95 °C for 10 min, denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s after 35 cycles. After 15 s annealing at 60 °C and 20 s extension at 72 °C, GAPDH and U6 were used as an internal reference to detect genes and miRNAs. The sequence of qPCR primers (Comatebio, China) are listed in the Table . 

## Cell counting kit-8 assay
Cell viability was detected with CCK8 cell counting kit (Bimake, USA). The cells were sub-cultured into 96-well plates at a density of 4 × 103 cells/well. After different treatments, add 100 μL culture solution and 10 μL CCK8 solution to each well. Incubate in 37 °C for 30 min–2 h. Cell viability was measured at 450 nm absorbance (OD) with a microplate reader.

## Cell apoptosis analysis and TUNEL staining
Apoptosis was detected after GC cells were treated with galangin, miR-10b-5p mimics, miR-10b-5p inhibitors and galangin-Exo. Annexin V-FITC/PI reagent (Beyotime, China) was then added to the cells for 30 min reaction and fluorescent cells were detected by flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, USA). After the cells were treated with PBS, galangin and galangin-Exo, the cells were laid on the glass plate in the 24-well plate. After 24 h fixation, TUNEL staining was performed according to the instruction of TUNEL kit (Beyotime, China). After staining, the tablets were sealed and observed by fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Japan).

## Cell migration and invasion analysis
GC cells were treated with galangin, miR-10b-5p mimics or miR-10b-5p inhibitors. GC cell migration was analyzed by wound healing experiment. After cell scratches in serum-free medium, cells were cultured. After 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h, cell mobility was expressed by scratch area. In the cell invasion assay, Matrigel (20 μl, BD Biosciences, United States) and serum-free medium were diluted at a ratio of 1 : 8 and added to the Transwell chamber. After Matrigel coagulation, cells treated with galangin, miR-10b-5p mimics or miR-10b-5p inhibitor for 24 h were added into cells. The 0.5 mL medium containing 10% FBS was filled in the small room and cultured for 24 h, then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Solarbio, China). Staining cells were detected by ImageJ software.

## Western blot analysis
Total protein was extracted from treated GC cells and tumor samples using protein extraction buffer (Beyotime, China). The proteins were then quantified using the BCA protein analysis kit. The proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulphate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. After electrophoresis, the protein was transferred to PVDF membrane. Then the membrane and primary antibody of P53, BCL2 and BAX (Abcam, USA) were incubated overnight at 4 °C. Different secondary antibodies (goat anti-rabbit) (Boster, China) were added and incubated at 37 °C for 1.5 h. Finally, the strips are analyzed using ECL Super Signal (Pierce, USA).

## Colony formation assay
The 1000 treated cells were sub-cultured in a 6-cm dish. The solution was changed every 3—5 d and the culture was finished two weeks later. Fixed with 95% ethanol and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. The dishes were dried and photographed, and the cell colonies were counted.

## Animals and animal care
Female nude mice aged 6–8 weeks were used for in vivo experiments. The mice were purchased from Jilin University Laboratory Animal Center and housed in groups. Mice were inoculated with 3 × 105 GC cells under the left rib and were injected after 7 d of feeding. The mice were divided into a PBS treatment group, the galangin (20 mg/kg) treatment group, and the exosomes-containing galangin (400 μg/mouse and 500 μg/mouse) treatment group. galangin-Exo were injected in situ into the tumor site in nude mice. Injected once every 2 d for a total of 7 injections. To observe the therapeutic effect of galangin-Exo on tumor. All experiments were performed when the tumor volume was less than 1.5 mm3. Experimental mice were raised under specific pathogen free (SPF) conditions, and all animal experiments were conducted after ethical review.

## Hematoxylin and eosin staining (HE)
The tumor tissue was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, and cut into 5 mm slices. The sections were stained with HE and the infiltration of cancer cells was observed under a microscope.

## In vivo tracking of galangin-exo by BLI analysis
After tumor bearing, nude mice were injected with PKH-26 labeled galangin-Exo. The same amount of PBS was used as control. At 0, 12, 24, 36, and 48 h after injection, the mice anesthetized by inhalation anesthesia (isoflurane) were imaged using the bioluminescent imaging system (λex = 530 nm, λem = 600 nm, Biolight Biotechnology, China). The study was reviewed and approved by the institutional ethics committee prior to its conduct.

## Statistical analysis
The unpaired student T test was used in this study. SPSS 16.0 software was used for statistical analysis. All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Anticancer effect of galangin
Many studies have indicated that galangin is an antitumor extract of traditional Chinese medicine. Therefore, we validated the therapeutic effect of galangin on GC. First, we treated AGS and BGC823 cells with different concentrations of galangin. Cell proliferation assay results showed that galangin treatment at 50 μM effectively inhibited cell growth (Fig.  A, Supplementary Fig.  A). Therefore, according to the CCK8 results as well as previous literature , the cells were treated with galangin at a concentration of 50 μM. AGS and BGC823 cells were treated with 50 μM of galangin to detect the effects of galangin on apoptosis, migration and invasion. Plate cloning experiments showed that galangin significantly inhibited the clonogenesis of GC cells (Fig.  B,C, Supplementary Fig.  B,C). In addition, compared with the control group, galangin significantly induced apoptosis (Fig.  D,E, Supplementary Fig.  D,E). Moreover, galangin inhibited the invasion of AGS and BGC823 cells (Fig.  F-G, Supplementary Fig.  F-G). Furtherly, cell scratch assay results showed that galangin inhibited GC cell migration (F g.  H,I, Supplementary Fig.  H,I). These results all prove that galangin has anticancer effects, can promote cell apoptosis, and inhibit cell migration and invasion. 

## Extraction and identification of exosomes
In the present study, the exosomes were used which have good biocompatibility to coat galangin. Exosomes can specifically deliver galangin and be internalized by the GC cells. Exosomes were isolated from BGC823 cells and BGC823 cells cultured with galangin. Furthermore, exosomes containing galangin were observed under transmission electron microscopy (Fig.  A), showing typical spherical bilayer structures. The particle size analysis shows that the particle size of exosomes is distributed between 50 and 150 nm (Fig.  B,C). In addition, nanoparticle tracking analysis results showed that exosome surface markers CD9, CD63, and CD81 were detected by HSFCM (Fig.  D). After high concentration of galangin was incubated with exosomes, the content of galangin in exosomes was determined by HPLC (Supplementary Fig.  A,B). 

## Exosomes containing galangin induced cell apoptosis
To detect the specific uptake of exosomes by GC cells, HEK293T, SCC9 cells, and GC cells were treated with PKH-26-labeled exosomes. The results showed that GC cells prefer to ingest exosomes isolated from the supernatant of BGC823 cells (Fig.  A,B). After the synthesis of galangin-Exo, the regulation of galangin-Exo on the apoptosis of GC cells were analyzed. Moreover, the therapeutic effects of different exosomes concentrations on GC cells were explored. TUNEL staining results showed that compare to control group, galangin-Exo group could significantly induce AGS and BGC823 cell apoptosis (Fig.  C,D, Supplementary Fig.  A,B). To confirm this result, the apoptosis of expressed genes and proteins were detected. The qPCR and western blot results showed that galangin-Exo treatment could up-regulate P53 gene expression in GC cells (Fig.  E,F, Supplementary Fig.  C,D, Supplementary Fig.  A). These results suggested that galangin-Exo can induce apoptosis in GC cells by up-regulate P53 gene expression. 

## Differentially expressed genes were screened by RNA-Seq
In order to further clarify the miRNA regulating GC cell apoptosis gene, we used RNA-seq. A total of 13 differentially expressed miRNAs were obtained by RNA-seq screening (Fig.  A). These genes included nine up-regulated miRNAs (miR-122-5p, miR-26a-5p, miR-490-5p, miR-1246, miR-2110, miR-3529-3p, miR-451a, miR-92a-3p, miR-92b-3p) and four down-regulated miRNAs (miR-10b-5p, miR-619-5p, miR-247-5p, miR-302-5p). The heat map of differential gene expression pattern and KEGG pathway showed, these miRNAs were related to cell growth and apoptosis (Fig.  B,C). To confirm this data, the qPCR were performed to analysis these miRNAs expression pattern in GES1 and BGC823 cells. The qPCR was performed for all up-regulated expressed miRNAs to screen the related genes (Fig.  D,E). Besides, the expression of all down-regulated miRNAs in GES1, BGC823 cells, and BGC823 cells treated with galangin were detected (Fig.  F,G). The qPCR results showed that the expression level of miR-10b-5p was increased in GC cells but decreased in galangin treated GC cells (Fig.  D–G). These results demonstrated that miR-10b-5p had an effect on GC cells treated with galangin. 

## MiR-10b-5p mediated cell apoptosis through the P53 protein
To further investigate whether miR-10b-5p plays an important role in galangin-Exo-mediated apoptosis of GC cells, the mimics and inhibitor of miR-10b-5p were used. The results showed that reduced expression of miR-10b-5p could induced apoptosis and inhibited invasion in AGS and BGC823 cells compared with the Nc-inhibitor group (Fig.  A–D, Supplementary Fig.  A–D). In addition, CCK8 showed that the inhibitor-miR-10b-5p significantly inhibited cell growth (Fig.  E, Supplementary Fig.  E). These results suggest that miR-10b-5p down-regulation inhibits the growth of AGS and BGC823 cells and induces apoptosis. 
The bioinformatics analysis showed that miR-10b-5p had a binding site with apoptosis-related P53 gene (Fig.  H). To verify this result, qPCR and western blot were used to analyze P53 expression levels. Western blot and qPCR results showed that inhibitor-miR-10b-5p increased the expression of the P53 protein, while mimics-miR-10b-5p decreased the expression of P53 protein (Fig.  F,G, Supplementary Fig.  F,G, Supplementary Fig.  B). These results suggest that miR-10b-5p mediates the apoptosis of GC cells by regulating the expression of the P53 protein.

## Exosomes containing galangin inhibited tumor growth in vivo
To optimize the application of galangin-Exo in vivo, galangin-Exo was prepared for administration in tumor-bearing mice. In order to verify the anti-tumor effect of exosomes in vivo, we used small animal BLI for analysis. The fluorescence intensity results showed that the galangin-Exo still remained in the tumor site during 48 h (Fig.  A). 
GC mice were treated with PBS, galangin, 400 μg/mouse galangin-Exo, and 500 μg/mouse galangin-Exo. Injections are given every 2 d for a total of seven treatments. The tumor was removed after treatment, and the results showed that the tumor volume of 500 μg/ mouse galangin-Exo group was significantly reduced (Fig.  B–D). The qPCR and western blot results suggests that reduced expression of miR-10b-5p and increased expression of P53 after galangin-Exo treatment in tumor tissue (Fig.  E–G). The tumors and organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney) of these groups of mice were stained with HE. In addition, HE staining results showed that compared with other groups, the galangin-Exo group was more effective in inhibiting tumors (Fig.  H). In addition, no significant changes or pathological changes were observed in the main organs of mice in the galangin-Exo group, which further confirmed the biocompatibility of galangin-Exo (F g.  I). These results suggest that galangin-Exo, especially exosomes at 500 µg concentration, has anti-tumor effects in vivo.

## Discussion
Gastric cancer (GC) is a kind of malignant tumor, and the factors causing GC are very complicated. At present, various treatments for GC have been reported . However, these methods are difficult to achieve targeted therapy for GC, resulting in large side effects and poor therapeutic effects, further limiting their clinical application. Galangin is a kind of flavonoids extracted from Chinese traditional medicine or food material, which has strong anti-tumor activity in a variety of cancers. Studies have shown that galangin induces human colon cancer cell death through mitochondrial dysfunction and caspase-dependent pathways . In addition, it has been shown that galangal inhibits hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation by a reverse Warburg effect . Galangin reduces the expression of PD-L1 by synergistically inhibiting STAT3 and Myc, and inhibits the proliferation, migration and invasion of tumor cells by inhibiting PD-L1 . Besides, other studies have shown that galangin promotes apoptosis by upregulating the pro-apoptotic gene BAX in triple-negative breast cancer . These results indicate that galangin may be a potential anticancer drug. Additionally, galangin has been shown to modulate miRNA expression, and conversely, miRNA expression can influence the anti-cancer efficacy of galangin. Specifically, investigations have revealed that galangin inhibits the growth of cholangiocarcinoma, which is related to the increase of miR-455-5p and the inhibition of PPP1R12A expression. Intriguingly, overexpression of miR-455-5p can eliminate the anticancer effect mediated by galangin . At present, there are few studies on the therapeutic effect and delivery method of galangin on GC. Our results showed that galangin can effectively inhibit the migration, invasion and proliferation of AGS and BGC823 cells and promote cell apoptosis.
Indeed, the efficiency of traditional drug delivery methods is still limited by the inability to reach target tissues or contact intracellular targets . Considering the targeted delivery of galangin, exosomes was selected as the delivery vector in this study. Exosomes seems to be inherently cellular targeting and can overcome natural barriers such as the blood–brain barrier, with good safety , . It has been reported that exosomes from cancer cells can be specifically taken up by the same type of tumor tissue or cell. Consider that if tumor-derived exosomes is injected into the body, the exosomes will eventually be taken up specifically by the original tumor tissue . Therefore, we extracted exosomes from BGC823 cell culture medium. Our results suggest that GC cells can specifically uptake exosomes which isolated from GC cells. The targeting specificity of exosomes can enhance the therapeutic effect of galangin in vivo and in vitro.
Through high-throughput sequencing of galangin-Exo, the differentially expressed gene miR-10b-5p were screened. Moreover, RNA-seq results showed that galangin-Exo was closely related to cell proliferation and apoptosis. Studies have shown that miR-10b metastasis can promote breast cancer cell invasion . Our results showed that the expression of miR-10b-5p in galangin-Exo was down-regulated, which induced apoptosis of AGS and BGC823 cells and inhibited cell growth and invasion. Recent studies have shown that miR-10b-5p silencing can up-regulate the expression of PTEN in GC cells and inhibit the PI3K/Akt/mTORC1 signaling pathway, thus significantly reducing the colony formation and cell viability of GC cells . Furthermore, our results prove that miR-10b-5p promote the apoptosis of GC cells by up-regulating the expression of P53. P53 is an important protein affecting cell apoptosis. Thus, miR-10b-5p/P53 axis can affect cell growth and apoptosis in GC cells.
The findings have unequivocally demonstrated that galangin-Exo exhibit a remarkable anti-cancer efficacy. However, the current techniques and efficiency pertaining to the encapsulation of galangin within exosomes are in need of further advancement and refinement. Moreover, although previous studies have delved into the anti-cancer properties of galangin, a more in-depth exploration of its underlying molecular mechanisms is still required. Comprehensively understanding the mechanisms through which galangin exerts its anti-cancer effects, optimizing its utilization in cancer patient treatment, and guaranteeing its safety and efficacy in clinical settings pose a crucial challenge.

## Conclusion
In the present study, we have achieved the successful synthesis of galangin-Exo. Both in vivo and in vitro experimental results strongly demonstrated that galangin-Exo possess the remarkable ability to induce apoptosis in gastric cancer (GC) cells and effectively inhibit tumor growth. Once the galangin encapsulated within the exosomes enters the cells, it triggers a series of molecular events. Specifically, it leads to an increase in the expression of P53 by down-regulating miR-10b-5p. This upregulation of P53 subsequently promotes the apoptosis of AGS and BGC823 cells, while simultaneously suppressing their proliferation and invasion capabilities. Our findings indicated that galangin-Exo exhibit a significant anti-GC effect and are selectively internalized by GC cells. Given these promising results, galangin-Exo, as a potential drug delivery system, hold promise for further in-depth investigation in the field of GC research.
