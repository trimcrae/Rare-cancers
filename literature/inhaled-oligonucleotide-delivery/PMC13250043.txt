## Abstract
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive lung disorder linked to oxidative stress, mitochondrial damage, and impaired mitophagy. Tanreqing (TRQ) inhalation solution is widely used for respiratory diseases but its mechanism against COPD remains poorly defined. Here we investigated the protective effects of TRQ and its underlying pathway in a COPD rat model and cigarette smoke extract (CSE)-stimulated A549 cells. Our results showed that TRQ treatment alleviated body weight loss, lung pathological injury, alveolar destruction, and airway remodeling in rats. TRQ reduced ROS and MDA levels, restored SOD activity, and improved mitochondrial function including ATP production, mitochondrial membrane potential, and mitochondrial permeability transition pore homeostasis. Moreover, TRQ enhanced autophagy and mitophagy by upregulating Bnip3, Nix, Pink1, and Parkin expression. In vitro, knockdown of Pink1 abolished TRQ-mediated mitophagy activation, confirming that the Pink1/Parkin pathway was essential for TRQ action. In conclusion, TRQ inhalation solution ameliorates COPD by suppressing oxidative stress and restoring mitochondrial homeostasis via activating Pink1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy. This study identifies TRQ as a promising candidate for COPD treatment.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), also known as slow-onset lung, is a common, preventable, and treatable heterogeneous disease that is characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation. It is usually caused by abnormal airways and/or exposure to toxic particles or gases and is influenced by host factors such as abnormal lung development. Complications can increase disability and mortality from COPD . Inflammation and oxidative stress are important factors in the initiation and development of COPD. Mitochondria, a processing plant of cell energy metabolism, are the main targets of inflammation and oxidative stress. Changes in energy metabolism and mitochondrial structural and functional changes play a critical role in cellular senescence and survival , . Both in vivo and in vitro models of COPD have found that mitochondrial membrane swelling, membrane potential, adenosine triphosphate (ATP), reactive oxygen species (ROS) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) change significantly within the lung tissues , .
In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the syndrome of “phlegm-heat obstructing the lungs” refers to a pathological state in which exogenous heat evil combines with phlegm turbidity, leading to stagnation of pulmonary Qi circulation-a TCM concept describing the normal flow and dispersing function of lung Qi that is disrupted in this condition. This syndrome is characterized by the lung disrupted by external heat pathogens (e.g., wind-heat invasion) or internal stagnation of damp-phlegm, leading to acute inflammation, excessive sticky sputum, and airway obstruction , . Acute exacerbations of COPD are attributed to the invasion of exogenous pathogenic factors, such as wind-heat or phlegm-heat, which disrupt the lung’s defensive function, triggering inflammation and excessive mucus secretion , . Modern studies align with this framework, demonstrating that bacterial or viral infections in COPD exacerbations elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines and activate mucin-producing goblet cells via many pathways. This pathological process mirrors the TCM concept of “phlegm-heat congesting the lungs”, characterized by heat-induced phlegm stagnation, airway obstruction, and labored breathing – .
Tanreqing (TRQ), a classic TCM formula designed for “clearing heat, resolving toxicity, transforming phlegm, and relieving asthma”, directly targets these mechanisms. It’s composed of the effective components of honeysuckle, forsythia, scutellaria baicalensis, etc., which synergistically suppress inflammatory mediators and mucin overproduction, consistent with its clinical use in alleviating acute COPD exacerbations. Emerging evidence supports TRQ’s action against phlegm-heat syndrome. Preclinical studies show that TRQ reduces airway neutrophilia and IL-8 levels in cigarette smoke-exposed , while clinical trials report improved forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) and reduced hospitalization rates in COPD patients with phlegm-heat symptoms – .
In this study, the active components in TRQ were identified and analyzed. Then the efficacy and mechanism in COPD model were explored via proteomics analysis to explore how TRQ impact COPD-induced mitochondrial damage and cellular senescence through PTEN-induced putative kinase 1 (Pink1) and Parkin RBR E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase (PRKN) in vitro and in vivo (Fig.  ). The research can enrich understanding of clinical application of TRQ in COPD and provide sufficient scientific basis for TCM theory.
 

## Materials and methods

## Experimental animals, cells, instruments, and reagents
Animals used were purchased from Beijing Weitong Lihua Experimental Animal Technology Co., Ltd. with a certificate number of 110,011,201,108,432,853. The experimental animals were kept in the animal room of the Institute of Traditional Chinese Medicine at the Chinese Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences. This animal experiment strictly adhered to the principles of animal welfare and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Institute of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences (ethical approval No.: 2019A130).
Human alveolar type II epithelial cell A549 cells were purchased from the National Experimental Cell Resource Sharing Platform, batch number 20,181,102. A549 cells were cultured in 1640 complete medium (10% fetal bovine serum + 1% penicillin-streptomycin) and placed in a 5% CO2 cell incubator.
The instruments used in this experiment mainly included the nose and mouth exposure inhalation tower, the Melton Inhalogic Nies Nose-only inhalation exposure system (China), whole body plethysmography (WBP, DSI), a microplate reader (Molecular Devices, SpectraMax i3x), a high-speed refrigerated centrifuge (Sigma, 3-18KS) and a smoke generator (Suzhou Alos Environmental Generator Co., Ltd., SPG-C).
Additional materials included Hongyun cigarettes (Hebei Tobacco Industry Co., Ltd., bar code: 6901028250542), Pentobarbital Sodium (MERCK, P11011), bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS) dry powder (Sigma, 120M4030V), dexamethasone sodium phosphate injection (Guizhou Tiandi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 19110802 A), TRQ (inhalation solution, 6.4 mg/ml baicalin‌, Shanghai Kaibao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 1906303X), Mitophagy Detection Kit (Dojindo Laboratories, MD01), MitoTracker Red CMX Ros (Invitrogen, M46752).
The antibodies used in this experiment were shown in Table  .
 

## Identification and analysis of active components of TRQ
Six Male Wistar rats (220g–260g, 8g–10g weeks old) were randomly divided into two groups, Control group and Treatment group. The Control group did not receive any treatment. The Treatment group was inhaled TRQ for 20 min. After one hour, intraperitoneal injection of 48 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium for animal anesthesia, blood was collected from the abdominal aorta and the animals were sacrificed with pentobarbital sodium (3 times the anesthetic dose). Then the serum was prepared for identification and analysis of active components of TRQ. Chromatographic conditions: the chromatographic column is ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18 (100 mm × 2.1 mm i.d., 1.7 μm; Waters, Milford, USA); Mobile phase A is 2% acetonitrile water (containing 0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B is acetonitrile (containing 0.1% methyl acetate). The sample volume is 3 µL, and the column temperature is 40 ℃. Mass spectrometry conditions: the sample was ionized by electrospray ionization, and the mass spectrometry signals were collected by positive and negative ion scanning modes respectively.

## Animal experiments

## Animal grouping and model creation
Seventy-two male Wistar rats (220g–260g, 8g–10g weeks old) were randomly divided into six groups according to body weight: control group (Ctrl), model group (Model), positive control group (Pos), high-dose group (High), middle dose group (Middle), and low-dose group (Low). All groups except for controls were induced into a mode of COPD. Model animals were smoked by smoke generator combined with atomization tower inhalation, inhaling 4 mg/mL LPS for 20 min on the 1st and 14th days of the experiment. They were also exposed to continuous passive smoking for 90 days, every morning for 7 days/week for approximately 1.5 h per session . After the completion of this exposure the experimental drug was administered in the afternoon. The positive control group was intraperitoneally injected with dexamethasone solution (4 mg/kg). The high, medium, and low-dose groups were atomized and inhaled TRQ for 20, 10, and 5 min, respectively. The dose conversion was 0.12, 0.06, and 0.03 mL/kg , .

## Rat survival status, weight change, lung function testing, and sample retention
The mental activity, eating, drinking water, and coat color of the rats in each group were recorded throughout the experiment, with body weights measured every week. After the completion of the last drug administration, the animals were anesthetized with 48 mg/kg pentobarbital sodium via intraperitoneal injection and their lung function was measured using a whole body scanning system. Then the animals were sacrificed with pentobarbital sodium (3 times the anesthetic dose). Thelungs and thymus were weighed, and the organ index was calculated. Their lungs were preserved in the manner required by their different detection requirements.

## Cytokine detection
Some of the rat lung tissues were homogenized and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were used to detect interleukin 1β (IL-1β) and interleukin 6 (IL-6) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Additional cytokines that were measured included nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), C-reactive protein (CRP), interferon-γ (IFN-γ), and procalcitonin (PCT).

## Histopathologic examination
A portion of the lung tissue was fixed, embedded, sliced, dewaxed, and stained with hematoxylin-eosin. After sealing, images were collected and analyzed with a microscope. Ten fields of vision from different groups were randomly selected and evaluated using image analysis software (ImageJ, V1.8.0.112, National Institutes of Health NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). Mean linear intercept (MLI) was measured to reflect alveolar enlargement . Airway wall fibrosis was assessed with Sirius red staining (PSR) of thick collagen bundles. Wall thickness was evaluated as a function of airway wall area/basement membrane length. Six airways were randomly selected from each mouse for evaluation by blind researchers using Image J .

## Proteomics
The lung tissues of three animals in the control, model, and TRQ medium-dose groups were randomly selected for Tandem mass tags (TMT) quantitative proteomics bioinformatics analysis. The specific analysis process includes protein extraction, peptide digestion, TMT labeling, chromatographic classification, LC-MS/MS data acquisition, and database retrieval. The above process is completed by APTBIO (Shanghai, China).

## Detection of oxidative stress indicators
The control group was analyzed using a ROS determination kit (chemical fluorescence method), malondialdehyde (MDA) determination kit (TBA method), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD) determination kit (WST-1 method), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) detection kit (ABTS method) microplate method at the Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute. The indices of oxidative stress in the lung tissues of the model group and the TRQ medium-dose group are shown in the instructions.

## Detection of mitochondrial damage
The ATP content test kit of the Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, the cytochrome c oxidase activity assay kit for tissue samples, mitochondrial membrane potential detection kit (JC-1 method), and mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) detection kit for Beyotime were used to detect mitochondrial damage. ATP, cytochrome (Cyt), and mPTP were measured using lung tissue homogenate while JC-1 was detected using fluorescence microscopy after single cell dissociation of fresh lung tissue and smear staining.

## Cell experiments

## Small interfering RNA and transfection
Small interfering RNA (siRNA) against Pink1 (Applied Biosystems Life Technologies, s35166, s35167) and negative control siRNAs (NC siRNA) (Applied Biosystems Life Technologies, AM4635, AM4641) were purchased from Life Technologies. Using the Neon® Transfection System (Invitrogen Life Technologies, MPK5000), A549 cells were transfected using the corresponding optimized transfection kit (Invitrogen Life Technologies, MPK10096) .

## Preparation of smoke-concentrated extract
Smoke-concentrated extract was prepared using the negative pressure filtration principle. Basic 1640 medium was placed in a flask, and the smoke from 10 cigarettes was passed through and partially dissolved in 50 mL basic 1640 medium using negative pressure filtration. The resulting cigarette smoke extract (CSE) solution was prepared with a concentration of 100%. After filtration and sterilization, it was stored at −20 °C for later use .

## Cell experiment design
After exploring and confirming the concentration of the CSE and the effective dose of TRQ in vitro in previous experiments, the following groups were set according to different experimental conditions : NC siRNA+, CSE-/TRQ-/Pink1 siRNA-, CSE+/TRQ-/Pink1 siRNA- (1% CSE 24 H), , CSE+/TRQ+/Pink1 siRNA-(1% CSE 24 H + 0.1% TRQ 24 H), CSE-/TRQ-/Pink1 siRNA+ (Pink1 siRNA 24 H༉and CSE+/TRQ-/Pink1 siRNA+༈Pink1 siRNA 24 H + 1% CSE 24 H.

## High-content screening analysis
Following a 48-hour incubation with 0.1% TRQ, the culture medium was aspirated and replaced with a 200 µl PBS cocktail containing 100 nM Mtphagy Dye, 1 mM Lyso Dye and 1 µg/ml Hoechst 33,342, and 1 µg/ml Hoechst 33,342 or 75 µM MitoTracker Red CMX Ros staining, and 1 µg/ml Hoechst 33,342. Subsequently, the plates were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Image capture was performed using a Keyence BZ-X800LE confocal system.

## Western blotting
The lung tissues of three animals in the control, model, and medium TRQ dose groups were randomly selected, and cells were collected. protein preparation and western blotting were performed according to described in previous experimental methods . The expression levels of microtubule-associated protei1 light chain 3B (LC3B), Beclin1, Ubiquitin-binding protein p62, Binps3, NIP3-like protein X (Nix), Pink1, and Parkin proteins were compared between groups.

## Immunofluorescence staining
The randomly selected control group, model group, and TRQ medium-dose lung tissue were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and paraffin sectioned for immunofluorescence staining . Cells in different groups were treated under different conditions, 100% fixed, 1% BSA (Sigma-Aldrich, A2153) blocked for 60 min, labeled with a primary antibody, secondary antibody, and DAPI then sealed and observed using laser confocal microscopy (TCS SP8 STED 3X, Leica, Germany). For quantitative analysis, the mean fluorescence intensity of target proteins was measured in at least 12 random fields per sample using ImageJ software. To evaluate the co-localization of target proteins with mitochondria, the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) was calculated using the Coloc 2 plugin in ImageJ. PCC values range from 0 to 1, where a higher value indicates a stronger degree of co-localization between the target protein and the mitochondrial marker TOMM20/TOMM20. At least 12 fields per group were analyzed to ensure statistical reliability.

## RNA extraction and PCR
The SYBR green method was used to extract RNA from tissues and cells (TIANGEN® RNA Easy Fast Tissue/Cell Kit) for reverse transcription (TRAN EasyScript® First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix) and real-time PCR (TRAN TransStart® Top Green qPCR SuperMix). The primer sequences were shown in Table  .
 

## Statistical methods
SPSS 20.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Data were expressed as means ± s, and multiple sets of data were compared using one-way ANOVA. When the variance was homogeneous, pairwise comparisons were performed using the least significant difference method. When the variance was not homogeneous, Tamhane’s T2 method (T test method) was used for testing. Significance was defined as P < 0.05.

## Results

## Identification and analysis of active components of TRQ
A total of 46 compounds were identified as effective components of TRQ, which were classified into Flavonoids, Steroids and derivatives, Terpenoids, Alcohols, Quinonoids, Lignans, Carbohydrates, Coumarins and derivatives, Organic acids and derivatives, and others (Fig.  ). Among them, the most abundant components are mainly Cholic acid, Baicalin, Hyodeoxycholic acid, Wogonin, Wogonin 7-O-glucuronide, Tauroursodeoxycholic acid, Kaempferol, Aloeemodin, etc. Flavonoids were the most abundant substances in blood, mainly including Baicalin, Wogonin, Wogonin 7-O-glucuronide, Kaempferol, Baicalein, Calycosin, etc.
 

## TRQ can significantly improve the symptoms of COPD rats
TRQ treatment significantly improved the trend toward slow weight gain in the model group. Body weight was monitored for 12 weeks. The Ctrl group gained the most weight, while the Model and Pos groups showed significant growth inhibition. TRQ treatment dose-dependently improved weight gain, with the high-dose group exhibiting the strongest recovery (Fig.  A). At the end of the experiment, the TRQ middle and high-dose groups had significantly increased organ coefficients of their lungs and lymph nodes compared with the model group (Fig.  B). HE results showed that the main histopathologic changes in the model group were general inflammation in the lungs and there was significantly less damage to the lungs and trachea in the positive control group and the medium and high TRQ dose groups (Fig.  C). MLI analysis showed that the alveolar space significantly decreased in the positive control group and the medium and high TRQ dose groups (Fig.  D). Picro sirius red (PSR) staining showed that the collagen fibers of the trachea, bronchus, and bronchioles in the model group were significantly thickened, but less so in the positive control group and the medium and high-dose TRQ groups (Fig.  E). The positive group and the medium and high TRQ dose groups had significantly reduced wall thickness(Fig.  F). Lung function testing showed that respiratory frequency (frequency, f), minute volume (MVB), peak inspiratory flow (PIFb), peak expiratory flow (PEFb), mid-expiratory flow rate (EF50), TB, TP, and Rinx were significantly reduced compared with the model group the middle and high TRQ dose groups (Tables  and ).
 ELISA was used to measure cytokine levels in lung tissue of rats with COPD treated with TRQ. IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB, TNF-α, CRP, IFN-γ, and PCT all were significantly increased in the model group compared with the control group, but decreased to a certain extent in the positive control group and the medium and high TRQ dose groups (Table  ).
 The above results confirmed that the COPD model was successfully prepared based on body weight, lung coefficient, thymus coefficient, HE staining (MLI), PSR staining (wall thickness), lung function, and inflammation measurements. Medium and high doses of TRQ improved the symptoms of COPD in these animals. The middle dose of TRQ was defined as the effective dose.

## Proteomic analysis and the detection of TRQ in the treatment of COPD
In order to further explore the mechanism of TRQ in the treatment of COPD, we selected lung specimens from rats administered a middle dose of TRQ and compared them to lung specimens from the control and model groups using TMT quantitative proteomics bioinformatics analysis. Compared with the control group, 7 proteins were down-regulated and 21 proteins were up-regulated in the model group. Compared with the model group, 18 proteins were down-regulated and 43 proteins were up-regulated in the TRQ group (Fig.  A). Fourteen extracellular proteins, 19 cytoplasmic proteins, 20 nuclear proteins, 6 mitochondrial proteins, 4 lysosomal proteins, and 13 plasma membrane proteins were different in the TRQ group compared with the model group (Fig.  B–C). Compared with the model group, the domains of the differential proteins in the TRQ group mainly included assembly, mitochondrial proton-transport ATP synth complex (assembly, mitochondrial proton transport ATP synthesis complex), ATP: guanido phosphotransferase, C-terminal catalytic domain. ATP: guanidophosphotransferase, N-terminal domain (Fig.  D). Compared with the model group, the cellular components of the differential proteins in TRQ-treated animals mainly included the mitochondrial membrane part, mitochondrial envelope, mitochondrial membrane, and mitochondrial part (Fig.  E). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) results showed that important signaling pathways such as chemical carcinogenesis-ROS and lysosome were significantly different (Fig.  F).
 These findings suggest that the therapeutic effects of TRQ on COPD may be closely related to its anti-oxidative stress and mitophagy properties.

## TRQ can significantly inhibit oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage caused by COPD both in vivo and in vitro
TRQ can significantly reduce the expression of ROS (Fig.  A) and MDA (Fig.  B), inhibit oxidative stress, increase SOD (Fig.  C) and T-AOC (Fig.  D), and promote anti-oxidative stress in lung tissues of COPD model rats. The expression levels of ATP and mPTP in the lung tissues of COPD model rats decreased, while that of cytochrome C (Cytc) increased, reflecting a certain degree of damage to the mitochondria (Fig.  E–G). Further detection of single-cell dissociation, smear, membrane potential and ROS in mitochondrial of A549 cell line showed that mitochondrial membrane potential was reversed (Fig.  H–I). After the administration of TRQ, the expression levels of ATP and mPTP in the lung tissues improved, the release of Cytc was reduced, and the reversed mitochondrial membrane potential and the expression of ROS in the mitochondria was also reduced with the treatment of TRQ in lung cells in vitro. These findings suggest that TRQ can reverse the oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage caused by COPD both in vivo and in vitro.
 

## TRQ promotes autophagy in COPD rats
Western blot analysis revealed that the ratio of LC3B-II/LC3-I, as well as the protein levels of Beclin1 and phosphorylated p62 (p-p62), were markedly elevated in the model group compared with the control group, while total p62 expression was decreased (Fig.  A). TRQ administration further upregulated the LC3B-II/LC3-I ratio and Beclin1 expression, and restored p-p62 levels, indicating enhanced autophagic flux (Fig.  A). Consistent with the Western blot results, immunofluorescence staining and quantitative analysis confirmed that LC3B-II (Fig.  B, C) and Beclin1 (Fig.  D, E) fluorescence intensities were significantly increased in the model group, and were further elevated following TRQ treatment. These findings demonstrate that TRQ effectively promotes autophagy in COPD, which may contribute to its protective effects against lung injury.
 

## TRQ promotes mitophagy in COPD rats
Immunofluorescence co-staining for mitophagy markers (LC3, Beclin1, p62) and the mitochondrial marker TOMM20 revealed that the fluorescence intensity of mito-autophagy and lysosomal autophagy, as well as the Pearson correlation coefficient of mitochondrial co-localization with these autophagic proteins, were markedly increased in the model group compared to the control group, and were further elevated following TRQ administration (Fig.  A–D). Consistent with these observations, Western blot analysis demonstrated that the protein expression levels of Bnip3 and Nix were significantly upregulated in the model group, and TRQ treatment further augmented their expression (Fig.  E). Similarly, qRT-PCR analysis confirmed that the mRNA levels of Bnip3 and Nix were increased in the model group and further enhanced by TRQ (Fig.  F). Immunofluorescence co-staining of Bnip3/Nix with TOMM20 further validated these findings: the fluorescence intensity of Bnip3 and Nix, along with their co-localization with mitochondria, were significantly increased in the model group, and TRQ treatment promoted this mitochondrial co-localization (Fig.  G–H).
 Collectively, these results indicate that TRQ effectively enhances mitophagy in COPD, likely through upregulating Bnip3/Nix expression and promoting their mitochondrial translocation.

## TRQ activates the Pink1/Parkin pathway to promote mitophagy in COPD rats
Western blot analysis revealed that the protein expression of Pink1 was significantly upregulated in the model group compared with the control group, while Parkin expression was markedly decreased (Fig.  A). TRQ administration partially reversed these changes: it reduced the elevated Pink1 expression and restored the downregulated Parkin expression (Fig.  A). Consistent with the Western blot results, qRT-PCR analysis confirmed that the mRNA level of Pink1 was increased, while Parkin was decreased in the model group, and TRQ treatment significantly restored their expression (Fig.  B).
 Immunofluorescence co-staining further validated these findings: For Pink1 (Fig.  C), the fluorescence intensity of Pink1 and its co-localization with the mitochondrial marker TOMM20 were significantly increased in the model group, and TRQ treatment reduced this mitochondrial co-localization. For Parkin (Fig.  D), the fluorescence intensity of Parkin and its mitochondrial co-localization were decreased in the model group, and TRQ treatment promoted the translocation of Parkin to mitochondria, as evidenced by increased co-localization with TOMM20.
Collectively, these results demonstrate that TRQ regulates the Pink1/Parkin pathway to restore mitophagy in COPD, likely by promoting Parkin mitochondrial translocation and modulating Pink1 expression.

## Pink1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy contributes to the protective effects of TRQ against CSE-induced cellular injury
Western blot analysis revealed that CSE exposure significantly upregulated the protein expression of Nix, Pink1, and Parkin compared with the control group, and TRQ treatment further enhanced their expression (Fig.  A). Knockdown of Pink1 via siRNA markedly abolished the TRQ-induced upregulation of Nix, Pink1, and Parkin, confirming the dependency of TRQ’s effects on the Pink1/Parkin pathway (Fig.  A).
 Immunofluorescence co-staining and quantitative analysis validated these findings: For Nix (Fig.  B), CSE increased Nix fluorescence intensity and its mitochondrial co-localization with TOMM20, which was further enhanced by TRQ. Pink1 siRNA significantly reduced Nix expression and its mitochondrial co-localization, even in the presence of TRQ. For Pink1 (Fig.  C), TRQ promoted Pink1 expression and mitochondrial translocation in CSE-treated cells, while Pink1 siRNA effectively suppressed these effects. For Parkin (Fig.  D), TRQ enhanced Parkin expression and its mitochondrial co-localization, and Pink1 knockdown abrogated this enhancement.
Collectively, these results demonstrate that TRQ promotes mitophagy in CSE-injured A549 cells via activating the Pink1/Parkin pathway, which contributes to its protective effects against cellular damage.

## Discussion
TRQ has unique advantages in the treatment of COPD and the relief of its symptoms. Clinically, there have been reports on the use of TRQ in the treatment of COPD. Preliminary clinical trials have shown that TRQ can eliminate phlegm and relieve coughing and asthma . The treatment of acute COPD exacerbations with TRQ compared with intravenous medication is simple. The drug effect is direct and fast, with minimal digestive tract stimulation. The airway can be wet and slippery and the mucosa can be protected. This is more conducive to sputum discharge , . TRQ atomization inhalation can also reduce its systemic dosage, minimizing the incidence of adverse reactions such as rash, high fever, laryngeal edema, chest tightness, and shortness of breath .
In the early stages of researching the atomization of TRQ, we used a PARI TurboBoy N red nozzle sprayer to measure the particle size and transmission percentage of the TRQ inhalation solution. It was found that the two changed little with time. The average Dv (50) of the aerosol was 2.512 μm, and its transmittance was 74.867%. At the same time, baicalin (BAI), and oroxyloside (ORO) in TRQ were selected as its target components, and the HPLC-MS/MS analysis conditions of BAI and ORO were optimized. BAI and ORO reached Cmax at 5,10 and 30 min and eliminated slowly. The Cmax (69.817, 130.466, 244.107 ng/ml) and ORO (3.512, 5.318, 10.527 ng/ml) of BAI increased with dose . BAI and ORO were rapidly distributed to the lung, respiratory mucus, and trachea after inhalation. As the inhalation process progressed, drug concentration gradually increased. At the end of inhalation (20 min), drug concentration decreased slowly. BAI and ORO in the lungs, BALF and trachea were maintained at high concentrations for 4 h .
In this study, medium and high doses of TRQ significantly improved the body weight of COPD model rats, relieved lung edema, reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, reduced airway resistance of model animals, improved lung compliance, and improved the symptoms of COPD. HE and Sirius red staining analysis showed that the alveolar space (MLI) and collagen fibers (wall thickness) of the animals in the middle and high-dose TRQ groups were significantly reduced. The expression of cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB, TNF-α, CRP, IFN-γ, and PCT were also reduced in these groups to a certain extent. Based on the above results, medium dose (atomized inhalation for 10 min, converted to 0.06 by dose) is the effective dose of TRQ in the treatment of COPD.
Smoking is the most important cause of COPD. In 2015, Ahmad etc. pointed out that smoking can induce impaired mitophagy, leading to cellular senescence and the incidence and development of COPD . Smoking can increase the production of ROS in the mitochondria of lung airway structural cells, which initiates a chain reaction of oxidative damage that leads to increased cellular senescence , . In order to further explore the mechanism of TRQ in the treatment of COPD, we selected lung specimens from the TRQ medium dose, control group, and model group for TMT quantitative proteomics bioinformatics analysis. Through a series of processes such as qualitative and quantitative analysis of target proteins, subcellular structure prediction, domain prediction, differential protein function enrichment, and KEGG data analysis, TRQ differed from the model group concerning six mitochondrial differential proteins, and the domains of these proteins are mainly concentrated in assembly, the mitochondrial proton-transport ATP synth complex (assembly, mitochondrial proton transport ATP synthesis complex), ATP: guanido phosphotransferase. C-terminal catalytic domain, ATP: guanidophosphotransferase, N-terminal domain. The cellular component of the differentially expressed proteins mainly included the mitochondrial membrane part, mitochondrial envelope, mitochondrial membrane, and the mitochondrial part. We also found that chemical carcinogenesis-ROS and Lysosome signaling pathways were altered.
In order to confirm the above results, we explored the role of TRQ in the treatment of COPD in vivo and in vitro for oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage. TRQ significantly inhibited oxidative stress caused by COPD. By further detecting the levels of ATP, mPTP, Cytc, and mitochondrial membrane potential, we found that TRQ could reverse the mitochondrial damage caused by COPD. Zhang etc. found that CSE can increase the expression of the mitophagy proteins Bnip3 and Nix in airway epithelial cells, suggesting that mitophagy is enhanced . Reports by Kyung and others confirmed that mitophagy plays a very important role in the pathogenesis of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease . Mitophagy is a balanced physiologic state under normal circumstances. Once this balance is destroyed, it will cause cell damage and lead to cellular death.
In our study, we found that TRQ can improve the expression of LC3-II, Beclin1 protein, and mRNA in a COPD model, enhancing autophagy. By evaluating the co-localization of autophagy-related proteins and mitochondria and the expression of mitophagy-related proteins Bnip3 and Nix protein and mRNA, we found that a certain degree of mitophagy occurs in the lung tissue of COPD model rats. TRQ further promoted this mitophagy COPD rats.
In the early stages of this study, we explored and verified the time and concentration of the CSE-induced A549 cell model and the effects of TRQ on this model (data not shown). We found that the LD50 of CSE-induced A549 cells was 1%. We therefore used 1% CSE over 24 h to induce A549 cells to simulate an in vitro model of COPD. After induction, 0.2%, 0.1%, and 0.05% TRQ were administered over 24 h. Cellular activity was detected using CCK-8, and the oxidative stress indexes such as ROS, MDA, SOD, and T-AOC were detected using ELISA. It was found that 0.1% was the effective dose of TRQ in the CSE-induced A549 cell model (data not shown).
Pink1/Parkin has been shown to play a critical role in the dynamic balance of mitophagy. The mitochondrial outer membrane protein Pink1 is a protein kinase. In normal mitochondria, the full-length Pink1 protein on the mitochondrial outer membrane is maintained at a very low level via mitochondrial membrane potential-dependent degradation , , . Only when the mitochondrial membrane potential collapses will the full-length Pink1 protein be stable on the mitochondrial outer membrane, permitting its increased expression. Pink1 locates the recruited Parkin (an E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase) on the damaged mitochondria, which enhances the E3 ubiquitin ligase activity of Parkin and ubiquitinates the mitochondrial matrix protein , . Parkin-mediated ubiquitination leads to the accumulation of p62 and the aggregation of damaged mitochondria, ultimately completing the degradation of damaged mitochondria , . This process is closely related to cellular senescence – . Pink1 and Parkin increased and decreased to varying degrees in our COPD model. TRQ significantly reversed the expression of Pink1 and Parkin. This shows that Pink1 and Parkin are involved in the mechanism of action of TRQ in the treatment of COPD.
COPD is characterized by chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Both the Pink1/Parkin pathway and the Bnip3/Nix pathway were involved in its pathological processes. The Pink1/Parkin pathway may reduce the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and cell apoptosis by eliminating damaged mitochondria induced by smoking or inflammation . However, functional defects in this pathway can lead to insufficient mitochondrial autophagy, exacerbating damage to alveolar epithelial cells and inflammatory responses. On the other hand, Bnip3/Nix may promote mitochondrial autophagy in hypoxic microenvironments, such as in emphysema areas, to adapt to low-oxygen metabolism. However, overactivation of this pathway can accelerate the apoptosis of pulmonary vascular endothelial cells, worsening pulmonary hypertension. Additionally, the imbalance in the expression of Pink1/Parkin and Bnip3/Nix may influence the polarization of inflammatory cells , such as macrophages, by regulating mitochondrial quality, promoting the M1 inflammatory phenotype in COPD. The relationship between TRQ and these pathways in COPD requires further experimental verification and clarification. In future studies, we will use Pink1 knockout mouse models to further clarify and verify the role of the Pink1/Parkin pathway in the treatment of COPD with TRQ.
In summary, TRQ inhalation solution regulates Pink1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy and effectively alleviates lung injury in COPD, thereby improving lung function, cytokine profiles, alveolar structure, and airway wall morphology. These findings support the considerable application potential and clinical value of TRQ for COPD treatment (Fig.  ). Future studies will further explore whether TRQ acts through additional metabolic pathways and verify its consistent effects across tracheal and lung tissue compartments.
