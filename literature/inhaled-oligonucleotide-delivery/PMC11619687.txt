Convincing evidence suggests that an imbalance in mitochondrial homeostasis resulting from repeated injury to alveolar epithelial type 2 cells (AEC2) underlies IPF. Non-receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase 21 (PTPN21) performs various functions in cancer; however, its role in IPF has not been studied. This study aimed to investigate the role of PTPN21 in lung fibrosis. The experimental results showed that loss of PTPN21 exacerbated lung fibrosis by increasing cell numbers in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, lung hydroxyproline content, and extracellular matrix protein expression of fibronectin and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) in bleomycin-challenged mouse lungs. In A549 cells (AEC2), knockdown of PTPN21 suppressed focal adhesion and migration, reduced mitochondrial fission and increased fusion, increased the level of mitochondrial superoxide, decreased mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP levels. Simultaneously, knockdown of PTPN21 impaired autophagy, and increased intracellular reactive oxygen species levels. Treatment of fibroblasts (MRC-5) and primary human lung fibroblasts (PHLF)) with the supernatant from PTPN21-knockdown A549 cells increased the expression of fibronectin, collagen 1 and α-SMA. Conversely, overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells produced opposite effects. However, treatment of MRC-5 and PHLF with the supernatant from PTPN21-overexpressing A549 cells only slightly reduced the expression of fibronectin, collagen 1 in MRC-5 cells, but did not change the expression of α-SMA. In summary, this study revealed that the loss of PTPN21 in epithelial cells disrupted mitochondrial metabolic homeostasis, leading to epithelial cell inactivation and increased the deposition of extracellular matrix proteins in fibroblasts, thereby exacerbating experimental pulmonary fibrosis.

## Background
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive lung disease characterized high mortality and unclear pathogenesis [ ]. Multiple risk factors including age, alterations in genetics and epigenetics, and environmental factors, contribute to the susceptibility to IPF [ ]. Convincing evidences suggests that an imbalance in mitochondrial homeostasis, resulting from repeated injury of the alveolar epithelial type 2 cells (AEC2) were the underlying pathogenesis of IPF [ ]. Following injury to AEC2, various pro-fibrotic growth factors, including TGF-β1, are secreted and act on fibroblasts, promoting their differentiation into myofibroblasts [ ]. This is a primary source of excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition in the lung tissue of IPF patients [ ]. Currently, there is no cure for this devastating disease, and available treatments are limited to slowing disease progression. Without lung transplantation, the median survival of IPF is approximately 3 to 5 years, with motility rates steadily increasing among men and individuals over 65 years old [ ]. Therefore, the most urgent task is to further study its pathogenesis and identify effective treatment methods.
Non-receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase 21 (PTPN21), also known as protein tyrosine phosphatase D1, is a member of the protein tyrosine phosphatase family. It is a protein composed of 1174 amino acids, containing an N-terminal four-point-one ezrin, radixin, moesin (FERM) domain and a C-terminal phosphatase domain (PTP) [ ]. Previous studies have shown that PTPN21 may have weak PTP catalytic activity for certain substrates. For example, PTPN21 dephosphorylates pY530 of Src in human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells [ ]. In the bone marrow microenvironment, PTPN21 regulates cell mechanical properties by dephosphorylating Septin1 (Tyr246), and the loss of PTPN21 reduces the stiffness of hematopoietic stem cells and enhances their deformability and motility [ ]. Additionally, Lu Chen et al. found that disrupting the FERM-PTP interaction keeps PTPN21 in an open state, promoting extracellular signal-regulated kinase signaling and thereby enhancing cell migration [ ]. In recent years, PTPN21 has been identified as a tumor-associated protein involved in various cancers, including gliomas and cervical cancer. In gliomas, its expression is upregulated, promoting cancer cell growth and differentiation. Silencing PTPN21 has significant anti-cancer effects by inhibiting proliferation, blocking the cell cycle, suppressing metastasis, and enhancing chemotherapy sensitivity through the inhibition of the EGFR/PI3K/AKT pathway [ ]. In cervical cancer associated with human papillomavirus (HPV), elevated expression of PTPN21 is considered oncogenic [ ]. Downregulation of the PTPN21 gene can inhibit the migration and invasion of HPV E7 HaCaT keratinocytes [ ]. However, the specific role of PTPN21 in IPF was not been studied.
Due to the high density of mitochondria in AEC2, many researchers have focused on the mitochondrial function of AEC2. Abundant mitochondrial abnormalities have been observed in AEC2 derived from patients with IPF and in mice treated with bleomycin (BLM) [ , ]. Impaired mitochondrial function in AEC2 manifests as reduced efficiency of mitochondrial autophagy and stress-induced inhibition of mitochondrial biogenesis [ ]. Damaged mitochondria release mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitochondrial DNA into the microenvironment [ ]. Consequently, local fibroblasts exposed to harmful conditions undergo transformation into myofibroblasts, leading to ECM production and the progression of fibrosis [ ]. In addition to the FERM domain and PTP domain, PTPN21 also contains SH2 and SH3 binding domains, which may serve as molecular platforms for various signal transduction molecules [ ]. However, whether PTPN21 regulates mitochondrial functions in AEC2 cells during pulmonary fibrosis remains unclear to date.
In this study, by intratracheal instillation of BLM into AAV-shPTPN21-challenged mice, we found that knockdown of PTPN21 aggravated BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis. Furthermore, by overexpressing and knocking down PTPN21 in A549 cells, we found that PTPN21 not only regulated focal adhesion and migration, but also affected mitochondrial function, and mitochondrial dynamics, Thus, these results provided novel insights for revealing the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.

## Materials and methods

## Human lung tissue sampling
Lung tissue samples for immunohistochemical staining and for isolating primary lung fibroblasts were obtained from the Henan Provincial Chest Hospital. The IPF samples (n = 3) were surgical remnants of biopsies from patients with IPF undergoing pulmonary transplant. Control lung tissue samples (n = 3) were normal histology tissue obtained from normal disease-free margin of lung cancer resection specimens.

## Mouse model of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis
Fourteen wild-type C57BL/6 N (8-week-old, male) mice were purchased from Vital River Laboratories in Beijing (China). The mice were anesthetized by inhaling 40% isoflurane (diluted in 1, 2-Propanediol, Aladdin, Shanghai, China)), then given 50 µL normal saline (n = 7) or BLM (n = 7) (1.5 U/kg) by intratracheal injected. On day 21, the mice were sacrificed and lung tissue samples were taken.

## AAV-mediated in vivo PTPN21 knock down
Mice were intratracheal instilled with 50 µL of pAAV-CMV-PTPN21 shRNA (Sequence of target, CGGAATAGAATGGGATTATAT, virus titer:1.0 × 1012 vg/m L) or pAAV-CMV-NC). One week later, the mice were challenged of BLM (1.5 U/kg) dissolving in saline or equal volume of saline. Each group consisted of 10 mice. The body weight of the mice was measured every other day during the experiment. After challenged of BLM for 14 days, the mice were anesthetized, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BAL) was taken and counted with a cell counter. Subsequently, the lung tissue samples were collected. Total right lung hydroxyproline content was tested by the Hydroxyproline Assay Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), and the experimental procedures followed the instructions. Data were calculated and presented as µg hydroxyproline/g right lung.

## A549 cells culture and treatment
Human A549 cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (CCL-185) and propagated in a complete medium of Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium nutrient mixture Ham F-12 (DME/F-12) (Procell, Wuhan, China) with 10% (v/v) FBS, 100 IU penicillin, and 100 µ g/mL streptomycin under standard culture conditions.
For the H2O2 or BLM treatment on A549 cells, the cells were seeded in 6-well plates, when the cell density reached approximately 50% confluence, the cells were treated with H2O2 (50 µM) or BLM (20 mU) for indicated duration.

## Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT‑PCR)
Total RNA from A549 cells and mouse lungs was extracted using RNeasy kits (Qiagen) as previously described [ ]. cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Promega Corporation, Wisconsin, USA), and the concentration and purity of the cDNA were analyzed using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer. Subsequently, according to the manufacturer’s instructions, qRT-PCR was performed using an SYBR green kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) under a Light Cycler 96 fluorescent quantitative PCR system (Roche). ACTB was used as an internal reference, and the data were calculated using the 2−∆∆Ct method. The forward primer (FP) and reverse primer (RP) pairs for PTPN21 used in this study are listed in Table  . The ACTB primers used are listed in the previous study [ ], the Fibronectin 1 (Fn1), Collagen1a1 (Col1a1) and Acta2 primers of mouse used are listed in the previous study [ ].

 

## Plasmid transfection
For the plasmid transfection, when the A549 cell reached approximately 80% confluence, the overexpression PTPN21 plasmid of pcDNA3.1 + PTPN21 or the empty pcDNA3.1, and knock down plasmid of pcDNA3.1 + U6 + shPTPN21 (Sequence of target, CAACGAATGATGAAAGGTGTA) or empty pcDNA3.1+U6 plasmid were transfected to A549 cells by Lipofectamine3000 (ThermoFisher, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The amount of plasmid added in 6-well plate, 24-well plate, and 96-well plate was 2.5 µg/ well, 0.5 µg/ well, and 0.1 µg/ well, respectively.

## MRC-5 cells culture and treatment
MRC-5 cells were purchased from ATCC and propagated in a complete medium of MEM (Procell, Wuhan, China) with 10% (v/v) FBS, 100 IU penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin under standard culture conditions.
After 48 h of PTPN21 overexpression or knockdown in A549 cells, the cell culture media (CM) was collected and centrifuged at 800 g for 5 min at room temperature to remove cell debris. MRC-5 cells were digested and seeded at 5 × 104 cells per well in a 6-well plate with 2 mL of MEM complete medium. After 12 h, 1 mL of the original medium was discarded and replaced with 1 mL of the cell CM from A549 cells with PTPN21 overexpression or knockdown. The cells were then cultured for an additional 48 h.

## Isolation of primary human lung fibroblasts and treatment
For detailed procedures, please refer to our previous publication [ ]. Briefly, human lung tissue was dissected into small cubes and then digested in Hank’s balanced salt solution (Hyclone) containing collagenase (1 mg/mL; C0130; Sigma) at 37 °C for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 500 g for 5 min. The tissue was then washed and resuspended in high-glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (Hyclone) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/L streptomycin (Solarbio). The tissue was incubated in a cell culture incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 30 min to allow the tissue fragments to settle. The medium was gently replaced to remove non-adherent cells and debris, and the culture was continued for 5 days to obtain primary human lung fibroblasts (PHLF). The subsequent treatment of PHLFs was the same as that for MRC-5 cells.

## Reactive oxygen species assay
The ROS Assay kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was used to assay ROS levels in the A549 cells. The experimental procedures were according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, every well of 96-well plates was added 100 µL of DME/F-12 diluted DHE red fluorescent dye (5µM), and the cells were incubated in the dark at 37 ℃ for 30 min, then the staining solutions were removed and the cells were gently rinsed with warm HBSS with Calcium and Magnesium buffer for 3 times. Within 2 h of staining, the DHE red signaling were excited at 530 nm and read at 570 nm with a fluorescent multiplate reader or photographed under a fluorescence microscope.
To ensure that changes in ROS levels were not attributable to alterations in mitochondrial content, A549 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and subjected to identical treatments. Total DNA was extracted from the cells using a cell/tissue DNA extraction kit (Yeasen), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and nuclear DNA were then amplified using the Hieff Canace PCR Master Mix (Yeasen) and the Human mtDNA Monitoring Primer Set (TaKaRa). The relative quantification of mtDNA copy number was determined according to the instructions provided. Finally, ROS fluorescence intensity was normalized by the relative mtDNA levels in the cells.

## Mitochondrial superoxide assay
The MitoSOX™ Red (mitochondrial superoxide fluorescent probe) (Thermo Fisher, Shanghai, China) was used to detected mitochondrial superoxide. The experimental steps were followed by the manufacturer’s instructions. In short, after transfected the plasmids for 48 h, A549 cells in 96-plates were stained with 5 µM MitoSOX™ Red and incubated cells for 30 min at 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 in dark, then cells were washed gently 3 times with warm HBSS with Calcium and Magnesium buffer. The plates were excited at 518 nm and read at 570 nm using a fluorescent multiplate reader or photographed under a fluorescence microscope within 2 h of staining. Finally, the MitoSOX levels was normalized by the relative mtDNA levels in the cells.normalized to the relative mtDNA levels in the cells.

## Mitochondrial membrane potential assay
The mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) of the A549 cell in 96-well plates was measured by MMP assay kit with JC-1 (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). the cells were first washed with PBS, then 100 µL of cell culture medium was added to each well, and then equal volume of JC-1 staining working solution was added and thoroughly mixed. After incubating at 37 ℃ for 20 min, the supernatant was aspirated and washed with JC-1 staining buffer. The green and red fluorescence intensities of the cells were excited at 485 nm and 530 nm and read separately on a multifunctional enzyme plate. Next, calculate the red/green fluorescence ratio to evaluate changes of MMP.

## Mitotracker staining
Mitotracker staining was used to visualize mitochondrial morphology in A549 cells. Sterile coverslips were placed in a 24-well plate, and A549 cells were seeded onto the coverslips. The cells were treated with either overexpression or knockdown of PTPN21. After 48 h, Mitotracker dye (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was diluted in DF12 medium to a final concentration of 20 nM to prepare the working solution. A volume of 300 µL of this solution was added to each well to ensure complete coverage of the cells. The cells were incubated at 37 °C for 30 min, then washed three times with PBS to remove any excess dye. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 5 min, followed by three additional PBS washes. The nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were captured using a confocal microscope. Two random fields were imaged per group, and the number of fragmented mitochondria in each field was counted.

## Detection of autophagic flow
The A549 cells were seeded on the coverslips in 24-well plates (3 × 104 cells/well). GFP-RFP-LC3 plasmid and pcDNA3.1-PTPN21 or control plasmid, and GFP-RFP-LC3 plasmid and pcDNA3.1-U6-shPTPN21 or the control plasmid were simultaneously transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, the cells were treated with chloroquine (50 µM) for 5 h, then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with DAPI working solution for 10 min. Images were obtained using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

## ATP level detection
ATP levels were measured using the ATP assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The treated A549 cells were lysed on ice, then centrifugated at 12 000 ×g for 5 min, the supernatant (20 µl) is added to 100 µl of ATP assay working solution and mixed. The absorbance was measured with a multifunctional microplate reader. Finally, the obtained readings were normalized by the relative mtDNA levels in the cells.

## Cell adhesion assay
After the plasmid was transfected for 48 h, the A549 cells were digested by 0.25% trypsin, and about 5 × 104 cells were added to one 24-well plates which precoated with human fibronectin (Med Chem Express, Shanghai, China) and three multiple wells were set in each group. The culture plates were incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 h, then non-adherent cells were removed by gentle washing three times with PBS, and the remaining cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 5 min. After washing, images were taken under a phase contrast microscope on 100 × field.

## Wound healing assay
After the plasmid was transfected for 48 h in 6-well plates and grown to 100% confluence, the cell monolayers were wound vertically with a steriled 200 µL pipette tip, and then gently washed with PBS. The culture medium was replaced with DME/F-12 containing 2% FBS. At 0 h, 12 h and 24 h, pictures were taken respectively under an inverted microscope to check the condition of wound closure.

## Immunohistochemical (IHC)
For IHC staining, mice or IPF paraffin-embedded tissue sections were successively deparaffinized in xylene, gradient ethanol, and subsequently added to PBS, then antigens of the tissue sections were repaired with sufficient sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 95 ℃ for 10 min and naturally lowered the temperature to room temperature. Next, the sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 and blocked with 5% goat serum at room temperature for 30 min, then incubated with the corresponding specific primary antibodies (PTPN21 (1: 100)) at 4 ℃ overnight. After washing with PBS, lung sections were incubated with biotin-labeled secondary antibodies (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) at 37 ℃ for 30 min. Then, a DAB working solution was used to develop the lung sections, followed by staining the nucleus with hematoxylin, the slices were sealed using neutral gum. The stained lung sections were photographed with a light microscopy. Lung tissue specimens from IPF patients were obtained from Henan Provincial Chest Hospital. This study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial Chest Hospital (No. 2020-03-06).

## Immunofluorescence (IF)
For the cell, the cells on the slides were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution at room temperature for 10 min firstly, then neutralized with 2% glycine for 5 min, and softly washed with PBS. Before incubation with the second antibody, the procedures of IF of the lung tissue and the cells were the same as IHC. Lung tissue sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies, including rabbit anti-PTPN21 (1:100, Abcam), mouse anti-Surfactant Protein C (SP-C) (1:100, US Biological), or mouse anti-EMR1(also named F4/80) (1:100, US Biological). After incubation, the sections were washed with PBS, the secondary antibodies of Alexa Fluor® 594/488-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG, and Alexa Fluor® 594/488-conjugated anti-mouse IgG (Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China) were added and incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 h, then the nucleus was stained with a DAPI working solution. Cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a 1:100 dilution of the primary antibody α-SMA. The following day, a 1:400 dilution of phalloidin was added to the secondary antibodies, Alexa Fluor® 488-conjugated anti-mouse IgG, and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h, then the nucleus was stained with a DAPI working solution. Image acquisition used a Zeiss microscope or a confocal laser scanning microscope (Leica, WETZLAR, Germany).

## Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining
H&E staining were carried out as described previously. Briefly, mouse paraffin-embedded lung tissue sections were routinely deparaffinized to distilled water. Then H&E staining were performed using the staining kit from Beyotime Biotechnology, following the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Western blotting
Briefly, the total proteins of the mice lung tissues, A549 cells and MRC-5 cells were extracted, and the concentration of the proteins was determined as previously described. Then total proteins (30 µg) were run by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis gel and then transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membrane, followed by blocking the membrane with 5% fat-free milk for 45 min, the membranes were incubated with specific primary antibody (Table  ) (overnight at 4 ℃), then the corresponding horseradish-peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG or goat anti-rabbit IgG were added and incubated for about 1.5 h at room temperature. The immunoreactive bands were rinsed with a chemiluminescence reagent kit purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology, and images were taken by the image station (Gene Company Limited, Shanghai, China).

 

## Statistical analyses
All data were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used for normal distribution. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparison between groups with non-normal distribution, and the unpaired t test was used for comparison between groups with normal distribution. The data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with statistical significance considered at p < 0.05.

## Results

## The expression of PTPN21 was decreased in fibrotic lungs of IPF and BLM-induced mouse lungs
To investigate the overall expression of PTPN21 in IPF lung tissue, we analyzed publicly available gene expression data from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) microarray dataset GSE32537 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE32537) and RNA sequencing dataset GSE124685 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE124685). We found that PTPN21 is significantly downregulated in IPF lung tissue (Fig.  A and B). To further determine the localization of PTPN21 expression in the lung, we performed double staining of IF for PTPN21 and the alveolar epithelial cell-specific marker SP-C in lung tissues from both human donors and control mice. Our results clearly showed co-localization of PTPN21 with SP-C-positive alveolar epithelial cells (Supplementary Fig.  A and B). Furthermore, to determine whether PTPN21 is expressed in macrophages, we conducted additional immunofluorescence double staining for PTPN21 and EGF-like module containing mucin-like hormone receptor (EMR1, (F4/80)), a well-established marker for macrophages and monocytes [ ]. Our analysis revealed that PTPN21 co-localized with only a minimal number of EMR1-positive cells in both human and murine lung tissues (Supplementary Fig.  C and D). These findings indicate that PTPN21 is predominantly expressed in alveolar epithelial cells, rather than in alveolar macrophages, within lung tissues.

 

In our previous study, we assessed PTPN21 mRNA expression levels using Single-Cell RNA-seq in lung tissues from both control and bleomycin-treated mice [ ]. The results demonstrated that, compared to controls, PTPN21 expression was significantly downregulated at the mRNA level in bleomycin-induced fibrotic lung tissues, as shown in Supplementary Fig.  E.
To investigate the specific role of PTPN21 in pulmonary fibrosis, we next tested the expression of PTPN21 in fibrotic lungs. IHC of IPF lung tissue showed that PTPN21 was decreased than the doner group (Fig.  C). Meanwhile, PTPN21 was also decreased in BLM-induced mouse lungs, the protein and mRNA expression of PTPN21 in the fibrotic lung tissues of BLM-induced mice were significantly decreased comparing with than the control mice (Fig.  J, K, L and M). When A549 cells were treated with H2O2 (Fig.  D, E and F) and BLM (Fig.  G, H and I), the protein and mRNA expression of PTPN21 were both significantly reduced. These results indicate that PTPN21 expression is reduced in fibrotic lung tissue, particularly in alveolar epithelial cells.

## Knock-down PTPN21 aggravated BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice
To investigate the specific roles of PTPN21 knockdown on pulmonary fibrosis, the AAV- PTPN21 shRNA mice were challenged by intratracheal instillation of BLM or not (Fig.  A). PTPN21 shRNA significantly reduced the protein and mRNA expression of PTPN21 in lung tissues on day 14 (Fig.  H, I and L). After BLM instillation, the knockdown of PTPN21 increased the mortality of mice (Fig.  C), and it also reduced the body weight of mice in the BLM-induced group. H&E staining showed that PTPN21 knockdown significantly increased the fibrotic area of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis (Fig.  D). The Ashcroft score revealed a significant increase in BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis mice, while PTPN21 shRNA significantly increased the score in BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis mice (Fig.  E), the hydroxyproline content in the right lung (Fig.  F) and total cell number of BAL (Fig.  G). WB results also found that PTPN21 knockdown increased the expression of ECM protein (Fig.  H, J and K) including fibronectin and α-SMA in lung tissues of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis. In addition, the qRT-PCR results also found that PTPN21 knockdown increased the mRNA of Fn1, Col1a1 and Acta2 (Fig.  M, N and O) in lung tissues of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis. In conclusion, PTPN21 knockdown aggravated BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice.

 

## PTPN21 promoted focal adhesion and migration of alveolar epithelial cells
To determine the roles of PTPN21 on alveolar epithelial cell, we transfected PTPN21 overexpression plasmid pcDNA3.1 + PTPN21 and PTPN21 knockdown plasmid pcDNA3.1 + U6 + shPTPN21 into A549 cells in vitro, and observed focal adhesion and migration of alveolar epithelial cells. The results found that overexpression of PTPN21 significantly increased the cell focal adhesion (Fig.  A, B) and migration (Fig.  C, D), and WB showed that overexpression of PTPN21 obviously promoted the expression of focal adhesion related protein including focal adhesion kinase (FAK), paxillin and vinculin (Fig.  E, F). On the contrary, knockdown of PTPN21 significantly reduced the cell adhesion (Fig.  G, H) and migration (Fig.  I, J), and WB showed that knockdown of PTPN21 decreased the expression of FAK, paxillin and vinculin (Fig.  K, L). In conclusion, PTPN21 promoted focal adhesion and migration of alveolar epithelial cells.

 

## PTPN21 altered mitochondrial dynamics and regulated mitochondrial function in alveolar epithelial cells
To determine whether PTPN21 regulated mitochondrial dynamics in alveolar epithelial cells, A549 cells overexpressing PTPN21 were labeled with mitotracker, revealing a significant increase in fragmented mitochondria (Fig.  A and B). WB analysis detected protein levels of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator-1 alpha (PGC-1α), which showed a significant increase. The expression of mitochondrial fusion-related proteins mitofusin 1 (MFN1), mitofusin 2 (MFN2), and optic atrophy 1 (OPA1) was significantly reduced (Fig.  C and D). Additionally, the protein expression of the mitochondrial fission-related protein Drp1 (S616) was significantly increased, while the levels of Drp1, Drp1(S637), and mitochondrial adaptor fission 1 (Fis1) remained unchanged (Fig.  E and F). In contrast, in A549 cells with PTPN21 knockdown, mitotracker labeling showed a significant decrease in fragmented mitochondria (Fig.  G and H). The protein expression of PGC-1α was reduced, while the levels of MFN1, MFN2, and OPA1 were significantly increased (Fig.  I and J). The protein expression of Drp1 (S616) was significantly decreased, with no changes in the levels of Drp1 (S637), Drp1, and Fis1 (Fig.  K and L). In summary, PTPN21 overexpression promoted mitochondrial biogenesis and fission while inhibiting mitochondrial fusion, whereas PTPN21 knockdown inhibited mitochondrial biogenesis and fission and promoted mitochondrial fusion.

 

To investigate whether PTPN21 also affected mitochondrial function, we examined several commonly used indicators for assessing mitochondrial function and normalized the results based on the relative mtDNA levels. The results showed that overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells reduced the levels of mitochondrial superoxide (Fig.  A and B), increased MMP (Fig.  C), and enhanced intracellular ATP content (Fig.  D). Conversely, knockdown of PTPN21 in A549 cells increased the levels of mitochondrial superoxide (Fig.  E and F), decreased the MMP (Fig.  G), and reduced intracellular ATP content (Fig.  H). In conclusion, PTPN21 regulated mitochondrial dynamics and altered mitochondrial function in alveolar epithelial cells.

 

## PTPN21 regulated autophagy in alveolar epithelial cells
To further investigate the effect of PTPN21 on autophagy in alveolar epithelial cells, A549 cells were treated with the autophagy inhibitor CQ following 24 h of plasmid transfection. The results indicated that CQ significantly promoted the expression of LC3II(Fig.  A). After overexpression PTPN21 in A549 cells, WB results showed a significant increase in the relative expression level of LC3II/LC3I protein (Fig.  A and B). In CQ-treated cells, overexpression of PTPN21 promoted the quenching of green fluorescence and significantly reduced the co-occurrence of red and green fluorescence compared to the vector group (Fig.  C and D), indicating that PTPN21 overexpression promoted autophagy. Simultaneously, compared to vector cells, overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells decreased the content of ROS (Fig.  E and F). Conversely, after knockdown PTPN21 in A549 cells, WB results showed a significant decrease in the relative expression level of LC3II/LC3I protein (Fig.  G and H). In CQ-treated cells, knockdown of PTPN21 inhibited the quenching of green fluorescence and increased the co-occurrence of red and green fluorescence compared to the NC group (Fig.  I and J), indicating that PTPN21 knockdown inhibited autophagy. Additionally, knockdown of PTPN21 in A549 cells increased the content of ROS (Fig.  K and L). Overall, increased expression of PTPN21 promoted autophagy in epithelial cells, while the loss of PTPN21 inhibited autophagic function in these cells.

 

## Altering PTPN21 expression in alveolar epithelial cells affected the ECM protein expression in fibroblasts
To evaluate the impact of PTPN21 modifications in alveolar epithelial cells on fibroblast, MRC-5 cells and PHLF cells were exposed to cell culture media obtained from A549 cells either overexpressing or with knockdown of PTPN21 (Fig.  A and H). The findings demonstrated that media from PTPN21-overexpressing cells slightly suppressed the expression of fibronectin, collagen 1, and did not change the expression of α-SMA in MRC-5 cells (Fig.  B and C) and PHLF cells (Fig.  I and J), whereas media from PTPN21 knockdown cells enhanced the expression of fibronectin, collagen 1 and α-SMA in MRC-5 cells (Fig.  E and F) and PHLF cells (Fig.  L and M). Additionally, immunofluorescence staining for α-SMA and phalloidin (which labels F-actin) in MRC-5 (Fig.  D) and PHLF cells (Fig.  K) showed that treatment with media from PTPN21-overexpressing cells did not significantly change the immunofluorescence intensity of α-SMA and F-actin. However, treatment with media from PTPN21 knockdown cells led to a significant increase in the immunofluorescence intensity of both α-SMA and F-actin in MRC-5 (Fig.  G) and PHLF cells (Fig.  N). In summary, increased expression of PTPN21 in A549 cells only reduced the levels of fibronectin and collagen in fibroblasts without altering α-SMA. In contrast, loss of PTPN21 in A549 cells significantly enhanced the expression of fibronectin, collagen, and α-SMA in fibroblasts.

 

## Discussion
IPF is the most common interstitial lung disease with unknown etiology. The disturbance of mitochondrial function of AEC2 caused by repeated injury is an important cause of IPF [ ]. Although correcting and improving mitochondrial disorders was an important way to explore new therapeutic targets for IPF [ , ], the specific molecular mechanisms regulating its occurrence remain unclear. This study demonstrated that reduced expression of PTPN21 in AEC2 (A549 cells) can lead to the cell inactivation by disrupting mitochondrial dynamics and impairing autophagy. At the same time, increased the deposition of extracellular matrix-related proteins in fibroblasts, thereby exacerbating pulmonary fibrosis (Fig.  ). This may be an important pathogenesis of the continuous exacerbation of pulmonary fibrosis. Conversely, overexpression of PTPN21 in AEC2 (A549 cells) only slightly reduced fibronectin and collagen 1 expression, but did not change α-SMA in fibroblasts. Since α-SMA is a marker of fibroblast differentiation, we concluded that PTPN21 overexpression in epithelial cells did not effectively reverse the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts.

 

Lung biopsy of IPF lung patients showed evidence of insufficient adhesion to extracellular matrix of some alveolar epithelial cells in early fibrotic areas, which may contribute to the progression nature of IPF [ ]. FAK together with vinculin and paxillin were involved in the formation of focal adherent plaques. It was essential for the turnover of focal adhesion and cell motility. The loss of FAK, vinculin and paxillin reduced the number of focal contacts [ ]. This may be one factor that contribute to gap formation in the cell layer, thereby interfering with the defensive barrier function of the airway epithelium in vivo. Notably, among FAK, vinculin and paxillin, FAK was the most focused protein. In IPF patients, the activated FAK promoted the survival, migration, fibrotic matrix production in lung fibroblasts thus playing a pro-fibrotic role. Therefore, inhibition of FAK activity has been proposed as a therapeutic target for pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. But in alveolar epithelial cells, the activated FAK bound to caspase-8 and prevented the activation of the caspase cascades, and the activated FAK in AEC was required to limit lung injury and AEC apoptosis in a model of pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Thus, the coordination of FAK activation in fibroblasts and alveolar epithelial cells may be responsible for attenuating lung injury and fibrosis. Previous study had demonstrated that PTPN21 interacted with FAK and regulated its autophosphorylation and FAK signaling in HEK293T cells [ ]. Therefore, in this study, we examined whether PTPN21 regulated the adhesion and migration ability, as well as the expression of related proteins in A549 cells. The results found that overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells promoted the migration and the focal adhesion between cells and extracellular matrix (fibronectin), and increased the expression of FAK, vinculin and paxillin. While knockdown of PTPN21 inhibited focal adhesion and migration in A549 cells, which may be a significant factor contributing to the persistent aggravation of fibrosis following the onset of pulmonary fibrosis.
PGC-1α is a transcriptional coactivator involved in mitochondrial metabolism and biogenesis, regulating mitochondrial biogenesis and remodeling [ ]. Studies have shown that PGC-1α expression was reduced in IPF and BLM-induced lung fibrosis in murine lung AECs [ ]. PGC-1α gene knockout exacerbated BLM-induced lung fibrosis in mice [ ]. Increasing PGC-1α expression promoted mitophagy and mitochondrial biogenesis, thereby alleviating BLM-induced lung fibrosis in mice [ ]. This study found that overexpression of PTPN21 in alveolar epithelial cells (A549) promoted PGC-1α expression, while knockdown of PTPN21 inhibited PGC-1α expression. Mitochondria are highly dynamic organelles, and the balance between fusion and fission is crucial for maintaining the number, size, and function of mitochondria [ ]. To further evaluate the effect of PTPN21 on mitochondrial dynamics, this study used mitotracker to label mitochondria and found that overexpression of PTPN21 increased fragmented mitochondria in alveolar epithelial cells, while inhibition of PTPN21 reduced fragmented mitochondria. Changes in mitochondrial morphology suggested that PTPN21 may be involved in regulating mitochondrial dynamics. As we all know, fission-associated proteins such as Drp1, Drp1 (S616), and Fis1 play key roles in regulating mitochondrial fission to ensure an adequate number of mitochondria for cell division and growth [ ]. On the other hand, mitochondrial fusion is regulated in both the outer and inner mitochondrial membranes. MFN1 and MFN2 are GTPases located in the mitochondrial outer membrane and mediate outer membrane fusion by forming homodimers or heterodimers. OPA1 is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane and mediates inner membrane fusion [ ]. Here, we found that overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells reduced the protein levels of mitochondrial fusion regulators MFN1, MFN2, and OPA1, while increasing the protein level of the fission regulator Drp1 (S616). However, the protein levels of Drp1, Drp1 (S637), and Fis1 remained unchanged. In A549 cells with PTPN21 knockdown, the protein levels of MFN1, MFN2, and OPA1 increased, accompanied by a decrease in the protein level of Drp1 (S616), while the levels of Drp1, Drp1 (S637), and Fis1 remained unchanged. These results indicated that overexpression of PTPN21 inhibited mitochondrial fusion and promoted mitochondrial fission in alveolar epithelial cells, which may play a key role in promoting mitochondrial turnover. Conversely, knockdown of PTPN21 promoted mitochondrial fusion and inhibited mitochondrial fission in alveolar epithelial cells, preventing mitochondrial self-renewal and leading to the gradual accumulation of damaged mitochondria.
Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell, providing most of the intracellular ATP. During oxidative respiration, the asymmetric distribution of protons and other ions across the inner mitochondrial membrane forms the MMP. A normal MMP is a prerequisite for mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and ATP production. Mitochondrial superoxide is primarily produced by complexes I and III of the electron transport chain [ ]. Enhanced activity of complexes I and III reduces mitochondrial superoxide production and increases ATP synthesis, whereas the decreased activity of complexes I and III leads to reduced ATP synthesis. The dynamic balance of mitochondrial morphology, achieved through continuous movement, fission, and fusion, helps maintain mitochondrial function. Given that changes in PTPN21 expression affect mitochondrial fission and fusion, this study further examined whether mitochondrial function was altered. The results showed that in alveolar epithelial cells (A549 cells), overexpression of PTPN21 reduced mitochondrial superoxide levels, accompanied by increased MMP and ATP levels. Conversely, knockdown of PTPN21 in alveolar epithelial cells (A549 cells) increased mitochondrial superoxide levels, accompanied by decreased MMP and ATP levels. This indicates that changes in PTPN21 expression in alveolar epithelial cells not only affect mitochondrial dynamics but also regulate mitochondrial function.
Autophagosomes selectively engulf dysfunctional or excess mitochondria and degrade them in lysosomes. Autophagy is a well-established quality control mechanism that is closely related to mitochondrial dynamics and function [ ]. Given that this experiment found that PTPN21 regulated mitochondrial dynamics and function, we hypothesize that changes in PTPN21 expression may affect autophagy in alveolar epithelial cells. To investigate the role of PTPN21 in autophagy, the expression of the autophagy marker microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (MAP1LC3/LC3) was examined. During autophagy induction, LC3B-I attached to phosphatidylethanolamine on the autophagosome surface, forming LC3B-II. The level of LC3B-II reflected the number of autophagic vesicles/vacuoles. An increase in the LC3B II/LC3B I ratio suggested the initiation of autophagy [ ]. In this experiment, overexpression of PTPN21 in A549 cells increased the LC3B II/LC3B I ratio, promoting autophagy and reducing intracellular ROS. Conversely, knockdown of PTPN21 reducing the LC3B II/LC3B I ratio, impairing autophagy and increasing intracellular ROS. This impaired autophagy led to the accumulation of damaged mitochondria, which inhibited mitochondrial fission and increased mitochondrial fusion, affecting oxidative phosphorylation and reducing ATP production.
Activated and differentiated myofibroblasts are a primary source of tissue remodeling in IPF. A key mechanism contributing to this feature is the abnormal or damaged pulmonary epithelial cells and the aberrant crosstalk between these cells and pulmonary fibroblasts. This epithelial-fibroblast interaction simulates the specific exchange of nutrients, spatial arrangements, and cell type-specific communications between epithelial and fibroblast cells during the progression of pulmonary fibrosis. This interaction is a significant cause of excessive tissue repair in the lungs during the progression of IPF. Studies using in vitro co-culture and organoid models to assess abnormal epithelial-fibroblast interactions are instrumental in revealing the pathogenesis of IPF [ , ]. This study found that changes in PTPN21 expression regulate multiple functions of alveolar epithelial cells. To assess the impact of altered PTPN21 expression in alveolar epithelial cells on fibroblasts, MRC-5 cells and PHLF were cultured with conditioned media from A549 cells either overexpressing or knocking down PTPN21. The results revealed that conditioned media from PTPN21-overexpressing cells only slightly inhibited the expression of fibronectin and collagen 1, while did not alter the expression of α-SMA in fibroblasts. Conversely, conditioned media from PTPN21-knockdown cells promoted the expression of fibronectin, collagen 1 and α-SMA in fibroblasts. We suggest that damage to alveolar epithelial cells leads to reduced expression of PTPN21, which in turn inhibits mitochondrial fission and promotes mitochondrial fusion, disrupting normal mitochondrial dynamics. This imbalance results in excessive production of mitochondrial superoxide, a decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential, and reduced ATP levels. Furthermore, the loss of PTPN21 impairs autophagy, leading to the accumulation of damaged mitochondria and other organelles, which in turn raises intracellular ROS levels. These effects contribute to the inactivation of epithelial cells, which subsequently stimulates fibroblast differentiation into myofibroblasts and increases ECM deposition, thereby exacerbating pulmonary fibrosis, and this finding were also confirmed in animal experiments. Conversely, PTPN21 overexpression in epithelial cells improved mitochondrial function and promoted autophagy. Although these effects effectively reduced fibronectin and collagen in fibroblasts, they did not change α-SMA expression. In addition, in recent years, PTPN21 has been identified as a tumor-associated protein involved in various cancers, attempts to prevent or treat pulmonary fibrosis by overexpression of PTPN21 may increase the risk of cancer.
Although A549 cells are an epithelial model, it is important to note that, due to their high proliferation and tumorous nature, they might not accurately reflect the observed effects. we are currently validating our findings using additional epithelial cell lines to ensure the robustness of our conclusions.
In conclusion, this study revealed that the loss of PTPN21 in alveolar epithelial cells (A549 cells) exacerbated BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice. The loss of PTPN21 in alveolar epithelial cells disrupted mitochondrial metabolic homeostasis and impaired autophagy, leading to epithelial cell inactivation and promoting the transformation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts. Therefore, this study provided a new insight for understanding the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.
