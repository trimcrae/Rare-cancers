## Introduction
Triggered by stimuli including bacteria, viruses, and sepsis, acute lung injury (ALI) is a clinical syndrome characterized by lung inflammation, and its most severe manifestation is acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) ( , ). In 2016, a study of patients in 459 ICUs from 50 countries reported that 10% of ICU patients and 23% of mechanically ventilated patients fulfilled the criteria for ARDS. Although the survey was conducted during the winter viral season and included ARDS that resolved rapidly, the hospital mortality of 35%−45% closely resembled that described by the large datasets used to validate the Berlin definition. Even patients whose ARDS resolved rapidly had a mortality rate of 31% ( , ). Currently, there are no effective pharmaceutical treatments to impede the malignant development of pathological states for ALI caused by the emergence of new pathogens or the outbreak of severe viruses. Patients will first initiate an indiscriminate fixed immune response to prevent the malignant development of ARDS. Therefore, early warning diagnosis and advanced intervention for ALI patients can contribute to reducing mortality and improving outcomes. It is necessary to comprehend the innate immune response to ALI to provide new and better therapeutic choices and increase the understanding of pathogenesis.
An intricate immune system of inherent and adaptive immune cells is present in the lungs. As a gas exchange organ, it encounters a wide array of environmental components, such as particulate toxins, chemicals, allergens, infectious bacteria, and lately airborne pathogens ( , ). When exposed to potentially harmful stimuli, innate immune responses serve as the first line of defense. Pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) or damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) trigger the formation of inflammasomes, which are massive, multiprotein cytosolic complexes with a micrometer scale. They contract in response to certain stimuli, release proinflammatory cytokines and cause pyroptosis. Excessive immune responses mediated by inflammasomes can aggravate tissue damage ( , ). The most widely activated inflammasome sensor, NOD-, LRR-, and pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3), which belongs to the NLR protein family, responds to various stressors in the cell that results in the release of reactive oxygen species (ROS), cytosolic K+ efflux, or Ca2+ cytosolic inflow ( ). Substantial advances have demonstrated the vital roles played by NLRP3 inflammasomes in the pathogenesis of ALI.
Natural products are a significant impetus for developing new medications for thousands of years. Due to their multi-target, multi-link, and multi-pathway characteristics, they have offered substantial benefits in treating complicated disorders, including ALI ( , ). Furthermore, the intervention effects of TCM and natural products on ALI induced by different triggers are tightly correlated with the modulation of NLRP3 inflammasome, implying that their viewpoint may be a cutting-edge ALI treatment method ( ). This review highlights our rapidly growing understanding of NLRP3 inflammasomes in the lung, then focuses on the TCM and natural products, providing a reference for the mechanisms and drugs research of ALI. The review also presents the challenges that lie ahead in better utilizing this understanding for ALI.

## Pathogenesis of NLRP3 in ALI
2.1

## Process of NLRP3 activation
NLRP3 inflammasome is a multiprotein complex composed of the effector protein Caspase-1, NLRP3, the adaptor protein-an apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain (CARD) (ASC), and the mitotic kinase never in mitosis gene A (NIMA)-related kinase 7 (NEK7) ( ). For its activation, a two-step process needs to be initiated and activated. The priming signals are mediated by toll-like receptors (TLRs), the interleukin-1 receptor (IL-1R), and the tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR), and the activation step is provided by wide-ranging stimuli ( ). The schematic representation is shown in . The activation signal of NLRP3 in ALI caused by various pathogens is also mediated by different stimuli to trigger the assembly of NLRP3 inflammasomes. The different mechanisms that mediate the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome depend on the properties of these stimuli. For example, RNA virus is the primary pathogen that invades the lungs. It recognizes the virus’s structural components and endogenous danger signals through pattern recognition receptors of various immune cells and epithelial cells. In mouse neutrophils and human neutrophils, Streptococcus pneumoniae can activate the assembly of NLRP3 inflammasome, activate Caspase-1, and participate in the maturation and secretion of interleukin-1β (IL-1β) ( , ). 
The excessive inflammatory immune response of the host to various causes of infections is the primary pathogenesis of ALI/ARDS. NLRP3 inflammasomes mainly exist in innate immune cells attacking pathogens, triggering inflammatory processes in response to foreign pathogens or endogenous danger signals ( ). Besides, NLRP3 inflammasome performs a crucial part in the onset of ALI by activating Caspase-1 and secreting proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-18 and IL-1β ( ), and the effect of NLRP3 inflammasome in stable ALI has been extensively investigated in vivo or in vitro. In clinical tests, the upregulation of IL-18 levels in ARDS patients is associated with poor prognosis over the long term in ALI/ARDS ( , ). Intervention with either antibody (Ab) neutralization of IL-1b or IL-1R antagonist alleviated ALI in various animal models ( , ). In addition to evidence for NLRP3 inflammasome activation products (such as IL-1β, IL-18, etc.) in accelerating ALI, there is also the significance of the NLRP3 inflammasome in driving ALI. Besides, in experimental ALI models, NLRP3-knockout mice alleviated lung damage following exposure to lipopolysaccharide (LPS) compared with wildtype (WT) mice ( ). After encountering intestinal ischemia/reperfusion (I/R), excessive inflammation and ROS production were markedly alleviated by NLRP3 deficiency ( ). This ongoing progress suggests that NLRP3 inflammasome is the crucial participant in the initiation and development of ALI.
NLRP3 inflammasome is also regulated by different programmed cell death cellular processes, such as pyroptosis, apoptosis, and autophagy. The electron transport chain and other mitochondrial components are damaged directly by overproducing inflammatory mediators, which changes mitochondrial respiration and makes it dysfunctional. Subsequently, defective mitochondria remain inside the cell when the mitophagy mechanism is not functionized correctly, which leads to increased ROS production, the release of cytochrome-c and mitochondrial DNA into the cytosol, and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome ( , ). Enhanced autophagy would inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome activation at the late stage of ALI, which further delayed ALI ( ). Besides, in a hyperinflammatory state, the executioners Caspase-3 and Caspase-7 are activated and catalyzed by the proapoptotic proteins B-cell lymphoma-2-associated X (BAX) and B-cell lymphoma-2-antagonist/killer (BAK), which also function upstream of the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and the release of IL-1β to eventually initiate apoptosis ( ). Furthermore, when encountering damaging signals, NLRP3 inflammasome begins to assemble in the cytoplasm and promote cytokine release, pyroptosis, and severe inflammatory cascade. These biological events all involve the NLRP3 inflammasome to further activate the hyperinflammatory response in ALI. Thus, NLRP3 inflammasome serves as a central hub point in the fundamental research of ALI.
In addition to canonical inflammasomes that are not negligible in ALI, non-canonical NLRP3 inflammasomes also play an essential role, which is primarily initiated by LPS. In mice, Caspase-11 directly recognizes LPS, in human, this recognition function is mediated by Caspase-4 and Caspase-5, both of which ultimately trigger the activation of Caspase-11. By inducing K+ efflux, activated Caspase-11 aids in the IL-1 and IL-18 maturation processes. Activated Caspase-11 contributes to IL-1β production and IL-18 maturation by causing K+ efflux. Additionally, activated Caspase-11 cleaves gasdermin D (GSDMD) to cause the development of membrane pores formation and pyroptosis ( ).

2.2

## NLRP3 inflammasome activation leads to endothelial dysfunction
The lung vascular endothelium is an active organ that has a central role in the development of ALI by synthesizing and releasing of several inflammatory mediators. IL-1β, IL-18 and high mobility group box-1 (HMGB1) are the main products after NLRP3 inflammasome activation. These inflammatory mediators enhance the expression of adhesion molecules (such as ICAM1, VCAM1, etc.) and chemokines (such as IL-8, IL-6, TNF-α, etc.) in the endothelium via binding to their cell surface receptors, respectively, and activating the nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) pathway. Endothelial NF-κB activation initiates transcriptional activation of chemokines and adhesion molecules, which can increase vascular endothelial permeability by altering cell contractility and disrupting intercellular connections ( ). Studies found that the intervention of MCC950 (NLRP3 inhibitor) improved sepsis-related endothelial dysfunction ( , ). In the I/R-induced ALI model, I/R stimuli induced the release of intestinal lipid mediators, which enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome activation in lung vascular endothelial cells. This activation of NLRP3 inflammasome drove the production of IL-1β, which in turn increased lung vascular permeability and exacerbated the inflammatory response ( ).
HMGB1 is closely related to NLRP3 inflammasome activation and endothelial barrier dysfunction. Reports showed that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome and subsequent release of HMGB1 are a late mediator of sepsis, which uniquely participates in the evolution of sepsis-induced ALI. HMGB1-mediated a reflection of a loss of barrier integrity, which produced dose-dependent increases in paracellular gap formation in concert with the loss of peripheral organized actin fibers, dissociation of cell–cell junctional cadherins, and the development of central stress fibers, a phenotypic change associated with increased contractile activity and increased endothelial cell permeability ( ). HMGB1 contributes to vascular hyperpermeability during ventilator-induced lung injury, decreasing VE cadherin expression and increasing endothelial permeability, leading to mitochondrial oxidative damage in primary cultured mouse lung vascular endothelial cells. Resveratrol protects against lung endothelial barrier dysfunction onigiated by high tidal volume (HTV), and the mechanism involves inhibiting of mechanical stretch-induced HMGB1 release, HMGB1-induced mitochondrial oxidative damage, and a nuclear erythrocyte 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)-dependent mechanism ( ). LPS through toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) activates NLRP3 inflammasome in mouse lung vascular endothelial cells and subsequently induces Caspase-1 activation. However, hemorrhagic shock-induced release of HMGB1, which acts through the receptor for advanced glycation end products to initiate endothelial cell endocytosis of HMGB1. Subsequently, it triggers a cascade of molecular events, including cathepsin B release from ruptured lysosomes followed by pyroptosome formation and Caspase-1 activation. Lung vascular endothelial cell pyroptosis significantly exaggerates lung inflammation and injury ( ).

2.3

## NLRP3 inflammasome activation leads to epithelial dysfunction
Previous reports demonstrated that inflammasome occurs in macrophages but few in epithelial cells. Epithelial cells are the first barrier to encounter stimuli from the microenvironment and play essential roles in sensing and initiating inflammation.
For the permeability of the airway, Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection induced disruption of airway barrier function and tissue damage, but necrostatin-1 (Nec-1) treatment in vivo maintained the airway permeability. An initial inflammatory reaction in the airway epithelium for subsequent inflammatory cascade resulting in tissue damage provides a novel strategy for ALI clinical treatment ( , ). IL-17A/p38 signaling pathway induces alveolar epithelial cell pyroptosis and hyperpermeability in sepsis-induced acute lung injury by activating NLRP3 inflammasome ( , , ). Prolonged NLRP3 inflammasome activation caused injury and aberrant lung epithelial regeneration. Silica-induced lung epithelial injury and fibrosis are vital pathogenesis of silicosis. Silica-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation disrupted the epithelial architecture, impaired mucociliary clearance, induced cellular hyperplasia and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition in 2D air–liquid interface culture. It inhibited organoid development in 3D organoid system. MCC950, the NLRP3 inhibitor, ameliorated these silica-induced structural and functional abnormalities. Further studies indicated that the NF-κB, Shh-Gli, and Wnt/β-catenin pathways were involved in NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated abnormal differentiation and dysfunction of the airway epithelium ( ). The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome was detected in alveolar epithelial type II cell (AEC II) of ARDS patients and LPS-induced ALI mice. In vitro, inhibition or silencing CaMK4 in AEC II significantly inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation, reducing IL-1β production and ameliorating lung injury in LPS-induced ALI mice ( , ). Ji et al. found that the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome was raised in alveolar epithelial cells in the lung tissue of both IPF patients and pulmonary fibrosis mouse models. Further research revealed that after the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, epithelial cells could induce the myofibroblast differentiation of lung-resident mesenchymal stem cells. Inhibiting the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in epithelial cells promoted the expression of dickkopf-1 (DKK1), a secreted Wnt antagonist. DKK1 suppresses the profibrogenic differentiation of lung-resident mesenchymal stem cells and bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis ( ). The inflammasome component NLRP3 regulates epithelial attachment and detachment in different ways. Kostadinova et al. found that NLRP3 increased alveolar epithelial cells’ attachment to fibronectin-containing matrices, such as the basal membrane. NLRP3 protects the alveolar wall by preventing the detachment of epithelial cells. The lung barrier stabilizing effects of NLRP3 were independent of inflammasomes, IL-1β, and IL-18 during the acute stage of injury ( ).

## Therapeutic strategies to target NLRP3 inflammasome in ALI by TCM and natural products
Numerous TCMs and their derived products have been approved for the treatment of ALI, and among those have also shown convincing efficacy in targeting NLRP3 inflammasome for anti-ALI. Furthermore, due to the unique “Preventive Treatment of Disease” theory, TCM also made many contributions to impede the ravages of pneumonia. To date, here we have reviewed and summarized recent paper about how TCM and its active constituents are regulated by NLRP3 inflammasome and implicated in ALI.
3.1

## TCM formulas for therapy of ALI
The process by which multi-pathway TCM acts on the body is complicated, some prescriptions exert their effects directly on NLRP3 inflammasome, while others exert their effects indirectly upstream or downstream of NLRP3 inflammasome. For instance, Huanglian Jiedu Decoction exerts an anti-ALI mechanism by inhibiting the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome and its downstream events via activation of sphingolipid pathway in addition to remodeling the host’s metabolic balance ( ). Besides, as a recombination of ancient Chinese classical prescriptions, Qingwenzhike (QWZK) had a protective impact on ALI in part because it inhibited the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome components, as well as the diminution of the content of downstream products of NLRP3 inflammasome. In other words, a vital mechanism by which QWZK protects against LPS-induced ALI involves the inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation ( , , ). Xiang et al. have covered that Xiao-Xu-Ming Decoction regulates the ROS/NLRP3-mediated NLRP3 inflammasome activation in an LPS-induced ALI model ( ). Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction (XFZYD), a modified formula of Taohong Siwu Decoction and Sini Powder, which demonstrated effectiveness in alleviating the development of ALI after cardiopulmonary bypass, mitigating NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR)-induced ALI rats as well as inactivating I-κBα/NF-κB signaling pathway ( , , ). The additional research reaffirms the crucial function of the NLRP3 inflammasome in ALI and sheds light on the mechanism by which TCM controls the development of ALI. Xuanfei Baidu Formula corrected impaired mitochondrial dynamics and Casp11-dependent NLRP3 inflammasome abnormal activation, thus compromising mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and NF-κB pathways against hyperinflammatory response ( ). Shenhuangdan Decoction improved cecum ligation and puncture (CLP)-induced lung injury by blocking the GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis signaling pathway in septic mice, including inhibiting the protein expression levels of NLRP3, GSDMD, GSDMD-N, cleaved Caspase-1, Caspase-1 and ASC in lung tissues ( ). Modified Guo-Min Decoction could prevent PM2.5-induced lung injury by reducing inflammation and pulmonary fibrosis, probably by inhibition of the phosphatidylin-ositol-3-kinase (PI3K)-protein kinase B (AKT) and MAPK signaling pathways, and inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome ( , ). Xuanbai Chengqi Decoction can effectively reduce excessive lung inflammation in ALI, partially by inhibiting of NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in the lungs and macrophages. These effects were consistent with the NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950. Furthermore, overexpression of NLRP3 reversed the anti-inflammatory effect of Xuanbai Chengqi Decoction ( ). Qinhuo Shanggan oral solution, a traditional Chinese herbal formula, is clinically used as an effective medication for various lung diseases, including ALI. Subsequent research found that Qinhuo Shanggan oral solution alleviated ALI, through its anti-inflammation and immunoregulation, primarily by attenuating TLR4/NF-κB signaling cascade and inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation ( ). Shiwei Qingwen Decoction significantly alleviates symptoms of lung injury, which is associated with TLR4/NF-κB inflammation pathway, including reduced expression of TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway-associated proteins (TLR4, Myd88, p-IκB, and p-p65) and NLRP3 inflammasome (NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, and cleaved-IL-1β) in vivo and in vitro ( , ). Chaihu Qingwen granules significantly ameliorated lung tissue pathological injury by decreasing the expression of TLR4, p-IκBα and NF-κB proteins, increasing the level of IκBα, regulating the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway, and inhibiting the activation of NLRP3 ( ).
Above all, this study found that some formulas were not used initially to treat ALI, but now have shown unique therapeutic potential in treating ALI. This reflects the TCM principle of homotherapy for heteropathy, implying that the cost of expanding their indications to them is more convenient than creating novel agents. Since the pathogenesis of ALI is complex and may involve multiple factors and links, in order to obtain more effective treatment, further preclinical research and more experimental support are required ( ). 

3.2

## Traditional herbal medicine (THM) extracts and mechanisms of NLRP3 modulation in ALI
For the therapy of ALI, in addition to the complex prescriptions listed above, studies have shown that individual herbs and extracts can also repress ALI by regulating the NLRP3 inflammasome. However, it is rarely applied alone in clinical practice ( , ). Ejiao ameliorates LPS-elicited pulmonary inflammation via inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS), which is mediated by the downregulation of NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, etc. ( ). Impatiens textori Miq., a traditional medicinal herb whose whole plant shows unique value against inflammatory disorders. Additionally, its methanolic extract could weaken NLRP3 expression, resulting in attenuated NLRP3-ASC complex formation and ASC oligomerization, which could further lead to pro-IL-1β remaining at the insoluble part. Further in vivo work on the ALI mouse model supports the exact protection against LPS-induced ALI by reducing cell recruitment at the inflammation site through the regulation of NLRP3 inflammasome ( ). A THM generally employed to handle multifarious respiratory diseases, P. ternata alleviated LPS-induced ALI, accompanied by the inhibition of IL-1β contents and reduced of the NLRP3 inflammasome in lung tissues and the cellular level. These biological events were closely linked with endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and binding immunoglobulin protein (Bip)/activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4)/CCAAT-enhancer-binding protein homologous protein (CHOP) axis ( ). Laurus nobilis L. is a commonly-used vegetable and folk medicine due to its extensive pharmacological activities. Moreover, L. nobilis leaf extract repressed NLRP3 inflammasome activation, including Caspase-1 activity, IL-1β secretion, and ASC complex formation. As the essential ingredient of L. nobilis extract, 1,8‐cineole consistently constricted NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and this constituent in the extract might be responsible for delivering such remarkable NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitory effects ( ). In Mongolia, China, and Eurasia, the common plant Thalictrum minus L. (TML) is used as a folk remedy to treat lung inflammation and fungus infections. TML slowed down the development of ALI by multi-signal cascades, such as p38-NLRP3/Caspase-1, COX-2, and AMPK-Nrf2/Kelch-like epichlorohydrin-associated protein (KEAP) axis ( ). Another study also provided a basis for TML, which is a new complementary traditional medicine used to treat ALI, and TML reduced inflammation in ALI by decreasing MAPKs-NLRP3/Caspase-1, COX2, and apoptotic-related signaling pathways ( ). The ethanolic extract of S. baicalensis pith-decayed root suppressed the NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway by preventing macrophage activation, decreasing the release of IL-1β, reducing the release of ROS, and controlling the progression of inflammation ( ). Polygala tenuifolia Willd. extract could alleviate LPS-induced histopathological lung damage, reduce the lung wet/dry ratio and decrease the level of inflammatory factors by affecting TLR4/NF-κB and NLRP3 signaling pathways ( ). 

3.3

## Natural products of TCM modulate ALI through NLRP3 inflammasome
Natural products have always inspired drug development, thus excavating these compounds may provide novel strategies for resisting ALI development. Here we discuss the available pre-clinical and clinical progress, underlying mechanisms, as well as the implications of natural products through NLRP3 inflammasome for the therapy of ALI. These bioactive extracts, as revealed in extensive research, function as a protective mechanism against ALI by suppressing aberrant activation pathways (AKT/mTOR, NF-κB, etc.) and controlling other connected mediators like Nrf2 and HMGB1 ( ). 
3.3.1

## Flavonoids and their derivatives
To date, abundant studies have revealed a desirable role of natural flavonoids in scavenging free-radical oxidants and avoiding diseases, mainly resulting from oxidative stress and inflammation, such as ALI.
Procyanidin B2 (PCB2), a B-type proanthocyanidin, is a natural dietary phytochemical with various physiological functions. Jiang et al. reported that in LPS-induced human AECs and LFs, PCB2 restrained TLR4 expression and p-NF-κB, reduced the expressions of NLRP3, cleaved Caspase-1, and ASC, which may display the suppressive capacity against TLR4 and NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathways ( ). Dihydromyricetin (DHM) is a natural product with rich pharmacological activities, which was isolated from the leaves and tender stem of Ampelopsis grossedentata (Hand.-Mazz.) W. T. Wang species ( ), which exerts protective functions in CLP-stimulated ALI mice by constricting inflammatory reactions. This anti-inflammatory capacity was based on the suppression of NLRP3 inflammasome activation (NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, IL-1β, IL-18) and pyroptosis (GSDMD p30, pro-GSDMD) signaling pathways ( ). Chrysin (CHR) plays an activist role in alleviating inflammation and exhibiting anti-ALI effects. Furthermore, the anti-inflammatory response of CHR is associated with the elevation of anti-oxidant ability, the remission of pneumonedema, and the suppression of phosphorylated inositol-requiring kinase 1α (p-IRE1α), thioredoxin-interacting protein (TXNIP), and NLRP3 ( ). Vitexin, as an active component of many medicinal plants, enhanced the activity of Nrf2 and HO-1. Besides, ROS and IL-1β productions were decreased in vitexin-treated cells. However, the siNrf2 in cells reversed the effect of vitexin. ROS and multiple inflammatory factors were associated with the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling platform; evidence also showed that vitexin inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome ( ). Isoliquiritigenin (ISL), as the regulator of AMPK/Nrf2/ARE and NLRP3/NF-κB pathways, was reflected by LPS-challenged pathological changes and the restriction of inflammatory injury and oxidative damage. The inhibitory effects of histopathological alterations and ROS generation were decreased in Nrf2-deficient mice, while ISL’s inhibition of the NF-κB and NLRP3 pathways had both Nrf2-independent and Nrf2-dependent effects ( ), that is, NF-κB inhibition might not be adequate to dampen ALI. In addition to NF-κB, additional priming signals may also regulate NLRP3. As an effective lung protectant, puerarin alleviated ALI symptoms by downregulating histone deacetylase 1 (HDAC1) expression, while the ectopic expression of HDAC1 reversed these actions. Furthermore, HDAC1 overexpression also downregulated protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) expression, implying that PP2A participated in the functions of HDAC1 on ALI. PP2A showed inhibitory effects on NLRP3 inflammasome. Meanwhile, puerarin hindered the progression of ALI by silencing HDAC1 or overexpressing PP2A ( ). Calycosin (CAL) is an extract from the root of Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bunge. The study confirmed that CAL intervention showed a positive role in treating ALI in septic rats, characterized by relieved lung pathological changes, reduced pulmonary edema, repressed respiratory dysfunction, resisted release of inflammatory cytokines and oxidative stress, as well as inactivating high-mobility group box (HMGB)/myeloid differentiation primary response gene 88 (MyD88)/NF-κB and NLRP3 signaling pathways ( ). Besides, diosmetin improved ALI mice, which partially contributed to restricting inflammatory reactions and inhibiting oxidative injury. Also, evidence suggested that two important mechanisms by which diosmetin cures ALI include scavenging ROS via Nrf2 activation and limiting inflammation via monitoring NLRP3 ( ). Xanthohumol protected ALI mice against oxidative stress and inflammation damage, which were partially dependent upon activation of the Nrf2 pathway via activation of AMPK/GSK3β, thereby suppressing LPS-activated Txnip/NLRP3 inflammasome and NF-κB signaling pathway ( ). Isoorientin (ISO) is the main active ingredient of T. minus Inflammatory reaction and oxidative stress of mice after the LPS challenge can be moderated by ISO via regulating the Keap1/Nrf2-heme-oxygenase-1 (HO1), NLRP3 inflammasome signal, and apoptosis pathways, thereby protecting the lung from damage ( ). Also, the protective effects of Orientin against LPS-induced ALI may derive from its regulatory effects of anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidant bioactivities that are mediated by suppressing NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome and upregulating Nrf2 signaling pathways ( ). Morin acts to expel acute lung inflammation by affecting the activity of the NLRP3 inflammasome in ALI mice, contributing to the clearance of bacterial infections ( ). Nervilifordin F (NF) stemmed from Nervilia fordii (Hance) Schltr., which could improve intestinal IR-induced ALI in rats. NF activates FKBP25 and inhibits the mTOR pathway, simultaneously decreasing the levels of inflammatory factors with TLR4/p65/NLRP3/Caspase-1. Additionally, when combined with p70S6K, TLR4, mTOR, and NLRP3, NF was further validated the regulatory effect on ALI by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome-related genes and mTOR pathway ( ). Silibinin purified from Silybum marianum (L.) Gaertn., and silibinin intervention ameliorated pulmonary pathology injury in ALI mice. It significantly inhibited LPS-induced inflammatory factor release, and the expression of NF-κB (p-p65, p-IκBα) and NLRP3 (NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1) were down-regulated ( ). Hong et al. aimed to reveal that pre-administration with hydnocarpin D attenuated LPS-induced super-inflammatory response in vivo, including blocking TLR4, the phosphorylation of NF-κB and p-ERK pathways. In vitro, hydnocarpin D inhibited LPS-induced oxidative stress and inflammation, which strongly rely on the activations of the Nrf2 pathway, thereby suppressing the LPS-activated NLRP3 inflammasome and MAPK/NF-κB signaling pathways ( ).

3.3.2

## Terpenes and their derivatives
Artesunate preconditioning partially alleviated renal ischemia–reperfusion-mediated ALI by reducing the activation of ROS-triggered NLRP3 inflammasome, while N-acetylcysteine (NAC), also known as ROS scavenger pretreatment, hindered RIR-induced activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome ( ). Andrographolide-lipoic acid ameliorated pulmonary damage by inhibiting the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, as shown by the inhibition of NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, IL-1β, and GSDMD, as well as restoring tight junction (TJ) protein expression, such as zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), claudin-1, occludin, etc. ( ). Loganin exerted a protective response against CLP-elicited ALI that may be linked with the regulation of macrophage polarization, the inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and the blockage of ERK and NF-κB pathways ( ). Oridonin increased Nrf2 and its downstream target expression, which was intervened by the activation of MAPK and Akt. Moreover, oridonin downregulated the pro-inflammatory pathways NLRP3 inflammasome and NF-κB pathways, while these two pathways were confirmed to be Nrf2-independent by applying a Nrf2 inhibitor. Additionally, the expression level of LPS-induced NLRP3 inflammasome and NF-κB pathways was more noticeable in Nrf2-deficient mice but could still be reversed by oridonin ( ). Anemoside B4 restored mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), inhibited the production of NLRP3 inflammasome-related proteins like NLRP3, Caspase-1, and IL-1β, and disrupted TLR4 dimerization. Altogether, by inhibition of TLR4 dimerization and NLRP3 inflammasome activation, anemoside B4 could prevent the emergence of ALI ( ). Glycyrrhetinic acid exacted from licorice, which was proven to weaken ALI development in mice by inactivating NLRP3 inflammasome via the ROS-PI3K/AKT pathway ( ). Glycyrrhizin administration alleviated inflammation by reducing multiple cytokines and immune cell infiltration. Additionally, glycyrrhizin inhibits NF-κB and p38/ERK pathways. When researchers applied different inhibitors to treat infected A549 cells, they found that the p38 inhibitor was the most effective inhibitor for reducing pro-inflammatory factors overproduction, suggesting that the p38 signaling pathway might be the major pathway for ALI ( ). Hederasaponin C, targeted inhibition of PIP2, an epigenetic regulator exerts a positive effect on ALI. Hederasaponin C suppressed cytokines storms through the NF-κB pathway, NLRP3 inflammasome, and intracellular Ca2+-related signaling pathways, all of which were affected by PIP2 ( ). Asiatic acid exhibits a protective effect on spinal cord injury-induced ALI by alleviating the inflammatory response and inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation and oxidative stress with the regulation of the Nrf2 anti-oxidant defensive pathway ( , ). Saikosaponin exerts an anti-inflammatory effect by inhibiting NF-κB and NLRP3 signaling pathways and protecting against LPS-induced ALI ( ). The activation of TLR4/MyD88 by LPS stimulation causes the activation of NF-κB via p-p65 and p-IκB, and platycodin D downregulates TLR4 and MyD88 expression and inhibits the levels of p-p65 and p-IκB ( ).

3.3.3

## Anthraquinones
Aloin extracted from the leaf exudates of aloe vera, Lei et al. found that aloin inhibited the activation of NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome in the ALI model. Moreover, aloin could increase the level of silent information regulator 1 (SIRT1), and EX-527 (the inhibitor of SIRT1) reversed the preventive effects of aloin ( ). Emodin is a natural product of rhubarb that is a THM and has a long history of medicinal use in China. Cold-inducible RNA-binding protein (CIRP) is a pro-inflammatory mediator, contributing to the pathophysiology of ALI in rats. Emodin regulates the CIRP-activated NLRP3/IL-1β pathway to retard the CIRP-induced C-X-C chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1) activity in vitro, thereby weakening the symptoms of ALI rats ( ). Another study also displayed that emodin prevented lung and pancreatic injury in the pancreatic‐induced ALI rat model. Inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome and IL‐1β seems to be an essential factor in protecting neutrophil recruitment in the lungs. And the findings supported the notion of NLRP3 inflammasome‐induced neutrophil recruitment, which may be a potential target for preventing severe cases of acute pancreatitis (SAP)‐induced ALI ( , ). Additionally, emodin prevented the biological process of pyroptosis, which included that pyroptosis-related downstream molecules such as NLRP3, GSDMD, Caspase-1, and ASC that were largely hindered by emodin. Emodin decreased the expression of Caspase-1, ASC, IL-1β, and IL-18 after siRNA was used to knock down NLRP3 in vitro ( ).

3.3.4

## Phenolic compounds
With the advancement of phytochemistry and pharmacology research, many natural polyphenolic compounds have been shown to exert anti-inflammatory defense and anti-oxidant defense systems, including honokiol, resveratrol, forsythoside I, and syringaresinol, which are present in edible plant medicinal plants and are used in TCM. Honokiol reversed the mRNA and protein levels of HO-1, Nrf2, and NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis protein, thereby reducing oxidative stress and ameliorating ALI ( ). Besides, as a botanical component medicated by SIRT1, resveratrol downregulated LPS-induced NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1 activity, and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in lung tissue. Moreover, resveratrol administration suppressed the p65 nuclear translocation, NF-κB activity, ROS production and inhibited the interaction of TXNIP-NLRP3 ( , ). Forsythoside I can suppress TXNIP expression and inactivate NLRP3 inflammasome in vivo and in vitro. As an upstream target of NLRP3, forsythoside I attenuates lung excessive inflammation by decreasing TXNIP to block NLRP3 inflammasomes ( ). Syringaresinol reduces mortality and exerts protective effects in CLP-challenged ALI. The mechanism of these positive effects could be that syringaresinol inactivated the NLRP3 inflammasome by manipulating TLR4/NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway ( ).

3.3.5

## Other type compounds
Various activators, including ROS, may cause the NLRP3 inflammasome to become active. Curcumin could regulate ROS-related cascade reactions and ameliorate ALI symptoms. Moreover, curcumin has a protective effect on ALI. It may lessen inflammation by preventing the NLRP3 inflammasome’s pyroptosis-dependent activation through the stimulation of SIRT1 ( ). The paraquat-induced ALI model was characterized by the overgeneration of ROS, which may further contribute to activating the NLRP3 inflammasome. However, in treatment with curcumin, the ROS production and apoptosis were significantly attenuated, as well as downregulating the expression levels of TXNIP, NLRP3, IL-1β and IL‐18, and Caspase‐1. Notch1 pathways rather than ERK1/2 phosphorylation were restored by curcumin ( ). Moreover, amygdalin, physalin B, and atractylodin reduce lung inflammation by regulating NF-κB. Amygdalin is frequently found in Rosaceae plants ( ). Two noteworthy signaling pathways, NF-κB and NLRP3, are involved in the protective effects of amygdalin against ALI ( ). Moreover, atractylodin reduced LPS-induced ALI by suppressing cytokines overgeneration, which was partially explained by the inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome and TLR4-related NF-κB and MAPK axis activation ( ). Ligustrazine mitigated ALI by blocking the TLR4/TRAF6/NF-κB/NLRP3/Caspase-1 and TLR4/Caspase-8/Caspase-3 axis, thus abrogating macrophages polarization, reducing apoptosis and pyroptosis ( , ). Additionally, anti-ALI effects of physalin B are closely linked with NF-κB and NLRP3 pathways, which were inactivated through activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway due to physalin B intervention. In contrast, administration of PI3K inhibitors increased activation of these pathways ( ). In the Lycium barbarum polysaccharide (LBP) reduced hyperoxia-induced ALI murine model, it triggered inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome was abolished when the SIRT1 was knocked down by siRNA in vitro ( ). The suppression of NLRP3 inflammasome induction by LBP is partially mediated through the SIRT1 in hyperoxia-induced ALI in mice and hyperoxia-stimulated mouse lung microvascular endothelial cells. By preventing the NLRP3-NEK7 interaction, NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, and subsequent activation, 4-benzene-indol derivative (1, 2-diol) did not depend on K+ efflux while inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation ( ).
These publications demonstrate the quickly developing understanding of lung innate immunity and the difficulties that lie ahead in fully utilizing this understanding to develop novel therapies for common and uncommon ALI. Altogether, by reviewing and summarizing TCM prescriptions, THM, and its extracts, accruing evidence found that the biological processes of most medications involve anti-oxidative stress and anti-inflammatory functions. ## Prospective and challenge
ARDS is a complicated syndrome of ALI that results in noncardiogenic pulmonary edema from a variety of sources that is heterogeneous in its clinical presentation and associated with a 40% fatality rate. Its clinical presentation is variable. Several therapies have been demonstrated in animal experiments to be beneficial in reducing harm, but no “magic bullet” has been widely acknowledged yet. Natural products are an essential source of drug discovery, and many of them have been reported to treat ALI effectively. Curcumin, due to its physiological activity and safety, has been recognized and promoted in several countries, and both Europe and the United States have included curcumin in Pharmacopoeia. Oridonin, a covalent NLRP3 inhibitor, has exhibited promising therapeutic and preventive effects on NLRP3 inflammasome-associated disease models such as peritonitis and arthrolithiasis ( ). Developing entirely new agents takes much longer than expanding their indications. Most natural products also have excellent security and large treatment windows. Moreover, natural products may contain additional qualities, including anti-inflammatory, anti-bacterial, and anti-fungal capabilities. These functions could further help with ALI treatments.
Previously, attempts to inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome have focused on blocking cytokines generated by its activation, with mixed results from this work. For example, Anakinra, a recombinant human IL-1RA that has been slightly altered, showed initially encouraging outcomes in small cohort studies. However, the limited clinical application and further safety exploration hindered its development ( ). Most attempts to inhibit the NLRP3 inflammasome have focused on compounds that directly bind to and inhibit NLRP3. Additionally, in vitro and in vivo studies suggest that there are many other strategies to interfere with the function of NLRP3 inflammasome, which requires adjusting the cellular mechanism of NLRP3 at the level of post-translational modifications and protein–protein interaction, as well as further observations of strategies that trigger events upstream of inflammasome activation. For example, 1,2-diol disrupts NLRP3-NEK7 interaction and NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and the subsequent activation, thus mitigating ALI progression. In clinical trials, to stop the NLRP3 inflammasome activating due to the NEK7-NLRP3 interaction, oridonin forms a covalent connection with cysteine 279 of nucleotide-binding and oligomerization (NACHT). Although some NLRP3 blockers (such as MCC950, NT-0796, etc.) are entering early clinical trials, the inadequate understanding of the molecular mechanisms of the inhibitors and the best clinical indications for NLRP3 inflammasome blockade remains to be determined. Due to the benefits of low side effects and high effectiveness specificity of TCM, they will be widely used for the safe and effective treatment of ALI and other associated lung disorders.
Traditionally, for NLRP3 inflammasome activation induced by different pathogen infection stimuli, TCM with heat clearing and detoxifying and strengthening body resistance and eliminating evil properties are often used, which can both inhibit inflammation deterioration and inflammasome activation. For ALI caused by trauma or shock, drugs for activating blood circulation and removing blood stasis or drugs for diuresis and detumescence are often used. According to the pathological characteristics of different etiologies, different TCMs are comprehensively used to strengthen the body and eliminate pathogens, adjust the balance of yin and yang, and improve the pathological state of the lung. There are some limitations in basic research on TCM attenuating ALI development through regulating NLRP3 inflammasome, such as the fact that the experimental achievement described above is only valid when the model animals only suffer from ALI, and indeed many clinical patients also suffer from other diseases such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes and so on. Therefore, to investigate the therapeutic value of TCM as a whole on complicated disorders, a model of ALI must be created in which various diseases coexist. Furthermore, can these TCMs and natural products work together to kill two birds with one stone? Additionally, NLRP3 inflammasome is an essential component of the homeostatic regulation of innate immunity. Since animal studies cannot fully respond to the internal environment of the human body, it is necessary to understand better how NLRP3 inflammasome is modulated in the innate immunity of ALI patients. Particularly important, several signaling pathways are “druggable” and inhibitors are already in clinical development for treating diseases like tumors and cardiac dysfunction. Besides, when is the right moment to intervene is a scientific issue regarding to NLRP3 inflammasome inhibiting therapies and anti-inflammatory medications. The establishment of efficient immunity may be hampered by premature intervention, subsequent therapies may not control the rapid amplification of inflammation. Besides, in the context of NLRP3-mediated damage during ALI, the interaction between immunological, parenchymal, and vascular cells and their related cells is also an expanding topic of investigation.
Overall, our knowledge of the molecular relationships between NLRP3 and ALI has spurred rapid advances since NLRP3 is an essential target for the treatment of ALI. However, there are still a lot of unresolved concerns with significant therapeutic implications. Many innovative medications are created for the therapy of ALI, but they are only meant to be used in preclinical applications, and there is still a long way to go before they can be used from the bench to the bedside.
