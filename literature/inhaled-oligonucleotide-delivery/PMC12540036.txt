## Abstract
Acrolein is a significant high priority hazardous air pollutant. This research explore the relationship of acrolein-induced ferroptosis with the pathogenesis of lung injury by combining cellular, transcriptome, and animal-level studies. The results in vivo and in vitro models indicated that a total of 18,868 differenced lncRNAs and 15,639 differenced mRNAs were obtained after acrolein exposure. After screening, 636 lncRNAs and 214 mRNAs were obviously different of the acrolein injured group with the control group. Differential genes were mainly enriched in the iron ion binding, tissue homeostasis, etc. Among them, lncRNA MIR155HG significantly responded to the toxic effects of acrolein, the knockdown of lncRNA MIR155HG could inhibit miR-155-5p expression, promote CD200R1 expression, and improve cell viability. Furthermore, it effectively inhibited acrolein-induced the activation of ferroptosis pathway in BEAS-2B cells and lung tissue, indicating that lncRNA MIR155HG might be a potential new target for the inhibition of acrolein toxicity. LncRNA-MIR155HG/miR-155-5p/CD200R1 axis played an significant role in the lung injury, and induced ferroptosis by regulating the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway. This study is the first to reveal that acrolein exposure significantly affected lncRNA and mRNA expression in BEAS-2B cells, and found that lncRNA MIR155HG effectively regulated acrolein-induced ferroptosis pathway, providing novel insights for the treatment of lung injury.
Keywords: Acrolein, Lung injury, Transcriptome sequencing, LncRNA MIR155HG, Lipid peroxidation, Ferroptosis

## Introduction
Acrolein is a highly toxic α-β unsaturated aldehyde and widely found in the environment, food, and water [ ]. It enters the human body mainly through skin contact, food intake, and especially inhalation. The sources of acrolein in the environment mainly include vehicle exhaust, tobacco burning, kitchen fumes, commercial poisons, and fire [ , ]. As one of the most harmful toxins in cigarette smoke, acrolein primarily enters the human body through tobacco combustion [ ]. The people are exposed to very high concentrations of acrolein through smoking, After smoking, acrolein level in saliva within 5–38 μM,whereas in the respiratory tract lining fluid reach 80 μM [ ]. The inhalation of acrolein is a significant risk for lung injury, and high expression has been detected in pulmonary fluid and plasma of patients with lung injury [ , , ]. Nevertheless, the precise molecular pathways underlying acrolein-mediated lung injury have yet to be fully elucidated. Considering that acrolein is widely present, highly toxic and has a high exposure concentration in the human body, elucidating the toxicological mechanisms involved remains a critical research priority.
Transcriptomics is a global approach to the study of gene transcription, which is widely used in environmental toxicology [ ]. Through transcriptomics technology, it is possible to determine the influence of different chemical substances on gene expression in organisms, identify the differentially expressed genes caused by chemicals, and analyze their functions, to reveal the influence and mechanism of chemical substances on the molecular level in organisms. The protein-encoding genes in the human genome only account for 2 % of the total length of the human genome, and the rest of the sequences are non-coding sequences, which also have important functions [ , ]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are crucial regulators of diverse biological processes [ ]. Emerging evidence increasingly implicates lncRNAs in the pathogenesis of various respiratory disorders, providing new biomarkers and potential targets. Multiple lncRNA, including NEAT1, TUG1, MALAT1, CASC2, and HAGLROS, played a regulatory role by regulating downstream miRNA and mRNA, and participate in the process of pyrocytosis, apoptosis, autophagy, and inflammation in acute lung injury [ ]. Acrolein has been reported to regulate apoptosis and accelerate atherosclerotic progression through lncRNA NEAT1 expression regulation in vascular smooth muscle cells [ ]. However, the regulatory function of acrolein on other lncRNA have not been reported, and many lncRNA and their biological functions related to lung injury remain unclear. Although research have shown that a variety of lncRNA effects a important role in the pathological process of lung injury, there is a lack of more comprehensive and unbiased approach to simultaneously study lncRNA with functional roles in the acrolein-damaged BEAS-2B cell models.
Ferroptosis represents an iron-regulated form of programmed cellular death that also involves the regulation of multiple oxidative and antioxidant systems [ , ]. Free iron can promote LPO, which is the main executor of ferroptosis, relying primarily on glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and glutathione (GSH) to remove it [ , ]. As demonstrated by Shou et al. [ ], GPX4 suppresses ferroptosis by eliminating lipid hydroperoxides [ ], its expression and activity are regulated by glutathione (GSH) levels, which in turn are determined by cysteine bioavailability ( ,[ ]). SLC7A11 mediates the uptake of cysteine, which affects the production of GSH [ ]. Therefore, the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis serves as the central mechanism regulating ferroptosis [ ]. Importantly, Nrf2 mediates the transcriptional activation of numerous factors in this pathway [ ]. Nrf2 can inhibit the antioxidant defense system and trigger ferroptosis by blocking the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis [ ]. However, whether acrolein can induce ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells remains unclear, and the specific mechanism of ferroptosis and development of lung injury needs to be further investigated.
In our previous study, acrolein was found to be cytotoxic to BEAS-2B cells, inducing cellular oxidative stress and DNA damage [ ]. Based on the previous results, this study investigated acrolein-induced ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells and lung tissue, and analyzed the effects of acrolein on the overall lncRNA expression in cells and the role of target lncRNA MIR155HG in ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells and lung tissue. Firstly, whether acrolein exposure induced BEAS-2B cells ferroptosis was investigated. Then, transcriptome sequencing was performed to analysis the effects of acrolein exposure on the overall lncRNA and mRNA level of BEAS-2B cells, and the regulatory effects of targeted lncRNA MIR155HG on ferroptosis pathway. Finally, the male rat lung injury model induced by acrolein and the effect of target lncRNA MIR155HG knocking down on ferroptosis pathway were examined at the animal level. This study is the first to report the effects of acrolein on overall lncRNA and mRNA expression in BEAS-2B cells, provides a new perspective on the toxicity of lung injury induced by acrolein and its prevention and treatment, and provides the experimental basis for the subsequent genomics and proteomics studies related to lung injury.

## Materials and methods
2.1.

## Materials and methods
Acrolein, CAS No. 107-02-8, purity > 98 %, was obtained from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Malondialdehyde (MDA), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and GSH assay kits were obtained from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institution (Nanjing, China), and GPX4 ELISA kits were purchased from Dingguo changsheng Biotechnology CO., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Hematoxylin-eosin dye solution kit was purchased from Beijing Solarbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Masson dye solution kit and Prussian Blue dye solution kit were purchased from Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Control small interfering RNA (siRNA), MIR155HG siRNA, and Primers of genes were synthesized by Shanghai Sangon (Shanghai, China).

2.2.

## Transcriptome sequencing analysis
The grouping of the cells was based on the previous experiment, and the control group and 80 μM acrolein-injured BEAS-2B cell group were selected for lncRNA transcriptome sequencing [ ]. Total RNA extraction from BEAS-2B cells and subsequently determined RNA concentration and purity [ ]. After the cDNA was obtained by reverse transcription, the cDNA library was constructed and the transcriptome was sequenced by Illumina Hiseq 2000 sequencer. This test was conducted by Shanghai Personalbio technology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China).

2.3.

## Transcriptome data analysis
Illumina HiSeq platform was selected to obtain the original data, and the filtered high-quality sequence was compared to the reference genome [ ]. HISAT2 v2.1.0 was used to compare the data with the reference genome and calculate the gene expression. On this basis, the expression difference analysis of the samples was further conducted, and the data were screened according to the threshold of P < 0.05 and |log2 Fold Change| > 1, with negative log2FC values indicating downregulation and positive values representing upregulation [ , ]. The cluster analysis and enrichment analysis of the differential genes were performed.

2.4.

## Cell viability analysis
The BEAS-2B cells used in this study were passage 3–6, and cultured on 96-well plates with different dose of Ferrostatin-1, Erastin, and acrolein, following 24 h incubation, cells were treated with 20 μL of 5 mg/mL MTT solution for 4 h. Following incubation, crystallization were solubilized with 150 μL dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) per well, and absorbance value was detected at 490 nm [ ].

2.5.

## Cell morphology observation
Cell morphology and number can reflect cell growth state. BEAS-2B cells were grown in 6-well culture plates, Ferrostatin-1, Erastin, and acrolein, treated for 24 h, and then observed and took photos under a microscope [ ].

2.6.

## Measurement of ROS level
BEAS-2B cells was inoculated on plate cells in 12-well, the cells including the control group, acrolein injury group, 80 μM acrolein co-incubated with Ferrostatin-1 group, and Erastin group, the cells were incubated with 5 μM Ferrostatin-1 for 1 h prior to treatment. Next, 80 μM acrolein and 5 μM Erastin were added for 24 h. Following treatment, the DCFH-DA probe was added for 45 min under light-protected conditions [ , ]. Phosphate buffered saline (PBS) was added for leaching, and fixed solution was added for 30 min. Next, observed under a microscope.

2.7.

## GSH content, GPX4 activity, MDA content, LDH release, and iron content
BEAS-2B cells were divided into control group, acrolein injury group (20, 40, and 80 μM), 80 μM acrolein co-incubated with Ferrostatin-1 group and Erastin group, after treated with acrolein, GSH content, GPX4 activity, MDA content, Iron content in cells, and LDH release in cell supernatant were detected according to the kit instructions [ , , , , ].

2.8.

## Cell transfection
Lipofectamine 2000 was transfected following the manufacturer's guidelines. The BEAS-2B cells group were divided into a small interfering RNA (siRNA) group (si-MIR155HG) and a nonsensical negative control group (si-NC) [ ]. si-MIR155HG Sense: CCAACUGACUCCUACAUAUTT and Antisense: AUAUGUAGGAGUCAGUUGGTT. After the treatment, the interference efficiency was assessed via quantitative Real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR).

2.9.

## Animal modeling
The male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (6–8 weeks) were maintained in a room with controlled illumination (12 h light/dark cycle), temperature (22 ± 2 °C), and humidity (55 ± 5 %) and were given free access to regular rat diet and tap water. This work was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of China Medical University (CMUKT2022289). The rats were randomly divided into control group, 2, 4, and 8 ppm acrolein injury group, 4 ppm acrolein + interfering adenovirus si-MIR155HG group (si-MIR155HG group), 4 ppm acrolein + negative control NC group (si-NC group), with 6 animals in each group. Aerosolized acrylein was inhaled 6 h/day, 5 days/week for 4 consecutive weeks. For si-MIR155HG group and si-NC group, nasal drops of si-MIR155HG adenovirus (1 × 1011 PFU mL−1) or si-NC negative control were given 2 days before aerosolized inhalation, and repeated drops were given 2 weeks later (HANBIO, Shanghai) [ ].

2.10.

## Measurement of lung tissue sections
After animal modeling, rat lung were separated and cut into 4 μm slices for HE, MASSON, and Prussian blue staining [ , , ]. Staining was conducted following the kit instructions. After staining, the images were collected and analyzed under the microscope.

2.11.

## GSH content, MDA content, and ROS level in lung tissue
Took lung tissue samples, added homogenization solution, homogenized thoroughly. Discarded the precipitate and took the supernatant for the determination of GSH content, MDA content, and ROS level. Conducted the test according to the instructions of the kit.

2.12.

## Detection of lung wet-to-dry (W/D) weight ratio
After the modeling was completed, the left lung was immediately weighed to obtain wet weight, dried in an oven at 65 °C for 48 h, and reweighed for dry weight determination. The W/D ratio was then computed to assess pulmonary edema [ , ].

2.13.

## qPCR detection
RNA was isolated from both cells and lung tissue using a RNA extraction kit. Then, RNA templates were converted to cDNA by reverse transcription ( ), and neck ring primers were reverse-transcribed using miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit was used to follow the instructions. The expression were quantified using the comparative 2−ΔΔCT [ , ].
 
2.14.

## Western blot
After treatment with acrolein, cells were harvested for protein extraction and the concentration were examined using a Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) kit [ ]. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE, with equal amounts of total protein loaded per lane for electrophoresis. After electrophoresis, it was transferred onto Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF) membrane for immunoblotting. After membrane conversion, 5 % skimmed milk powder was used to seal PVDF membrane for 1h, and then incubated the first antibody overnight. Next, incubated the second antibody for 1 h. Took out PVDF film and drop it into Enhanced Chemiluminescence (ECL) luminous solution for exposure [ , ].

2.15.

## Data analysis
The experimental data were all represented as the mean ± Standard Deviation of at least three replicates. GraphPad Prism 9.0 was used for mapping, statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27 software. ∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01 indicated that the results have significant differences (Control group VS Acrolein injury group). #P < 0.05, ##P < 0.01 indicated that the results have significant differences (Acrolein injury group VS si-MIR155HG group).

## Results and discussion
3.1.

## Acrolein triggered ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells
Whether acrolein could induce cell ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells and its specific mechanism remain unclear. Therefore, this study combined ferroptosis inhibitors and inducers to analysis the induction of ferroptosis by acrolein in BEAS-2B cells [ ]. It could be seen from A, the ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin-1 (0.0625–15 μM) exhibited no significant cytotoxicity in BEAS-2B cells. B revealsed that the classical ferroptosis inducer Erastin significantly reduced BEAS-2B cell viability, indicating that Erastin induced the ferroptosis of BEAS-2B cells. Based on the preliminary experimental results and combined with the published literatures, 5 μM ferrostatin-1 [ ] and 5 μM Erastin [ ] were selected for the subsequent experiments.
 Based on our previous research, acrolein was shown to significantly reduce cell viability. Next, the effect of ferrostatin-1 on acrolein-induced decreased viability of BEAS-2B cells was examined. Ferrostatin-1 was chosed to analysis the presence of ferroptosis in cells treated with acrolein [ , ]. As shown in C, cell viability in the acrolein injury group was obviously decreased compared with that in the control group, and was similar to that in Erastin treatment. After Ferrostatin-1 co-incubation with acrolein, compared to acrolein treatment alone, cell viability was significantly restored (P < 0.01), suggesting that acrolein triggered ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells. Furthermore, the change of iron concentration is also one of the important signs of ferroptosis, and also plays a key role in COPD [ , ]. As demonstrated in D, acrolein induced a dose-dependent increase in iron concentration in BEAS-2B cells, and Erastin treatment also induced a significant (P < 0.01) increase in iron concentration. After Ferrostatin-1 co-incubation, iron concentration was obviously decreased compared with acrolein group alone (P < 0.01), indicating that acrolein could induce ferrotosis in BEAS-2B cells.
Cell morphology is an important indicator of cell growth, and the changes in cell morphology after acrolein exposure were shown in E and F, the cells in the acrolein treatment group were wrinkled and rounded, which was similar to that in the Erastin treatment group, and the cell morphology was improved after the Ferrostatin-1 treatment compared with the acrolein injury group, and the decrease of the adhesion cell rate induced by acrolein was effectively inhibited. Consistent with acrolein's effects, in ferroptosis induced by PM2.5 exposure, the viability of BEAS-2B cells was also significantly reduced., and the cells were wrinkled and rounded, which was similar to the toxic effect of acrolein [ ].

3.2.

## Acrolein-induced ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells
To analyze the mechanism of the toxic effect of acrolein, the ROS level, GSH content, GPX4 activity, MDA content, and LDH release were determined in BEAS-2B cells [ , ]. ROS might induce oxidative stress, which depleted GSH and GPX4 levels, thereby regulating the sensitivity of cells to ferroptosis [ ]. GPX4 is the earliest and most important core regulatory enzyme in the field of ferroptosis research [ ]. The down-regulation or functional imbalance of GPX4 and the increase of lipid peroxides are important typical indicators of ferroptosis [ , ]. The degree of lipid peroxidation was detected by MDA content, which together with the release of LDH reflected the degree of cell damage. In this study, acrolein-induced intracellular ROS levels ( G and H), MDA content ( C), and the release of LDH ( D) in supernatant to be obviously increased compared with control group (P < 0.01), GSH content ( A) and GPX4 activity ( B) were significantly reduced (P < 0.01), and Erastin showed similar effects. After co-incubation with Ferrostatin-1, the ROS level, MDA content, and LDH release was obviously decreased (P < 0.01) while GSH content and GPX4 activity were obviously increased compared with acrolein injury group alone. These data showed that acrolein induced ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells.
 Researches have shown that in MIN6 cells [ ], human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cells [ ], and corneal stromal fibroblasts [ ], acrolein exposure also caused an obvious reduction in intracellular GSH content, which was in agreement with our results. The reduction of GSH content also means that the antioxidant capacity of cells was destroyed, and affected GPX4 expression or activity. It was reported that after BEAS-2B cells were exposed to PM2.5, the GSH content decreased and the MDA level increased [ ]. Not only that, acrolein exposure resulted in a 34.7 % ± 2.65 % decrease in GSH level, a 0.44 ± 0.06 fold decrease in GPX4 expression, a 6.94 ± 0.32 fold increase in cell ROS level, and a 4.06 ± 0.33 fold increase in lipid peroxide level in hCSF cells compared with the control group [ ]. These data showed that important indicators in the ferroptosis pathway were significantly regulated by acrolein exposure, which further enhanced the correlation between ferroptosis and acrolein, and better supported the conclusion of this study that acrolein could induce ferroptosis in BEAS-2B cells.

3.3.

## Mechanism of ferroptosis induced by acrolein
Cells have evolved a variety of ferroptosis monitoring mechanisms to defend against ferroptosis [ ]. To analysis the mechanism of ferroptosis caused by acrolein, the level of Nrf2/GSH/GPX4 pathway protein, a key defense mechanism for ferroptosis in cells, was detected. In E–I, after treatment with acrolein and Erastin alone, the protein expressions of Nrf2 into the nucleus, SLC7A11, GPX4, and HO-1 were obviously decreased (P < 0.01), while the protein expressions in the Ferrostatin-1 and acrolein co-incubated group were significantly increased, indicating that acrolein exposure reduced the anti-oxidant capacity of BEAS-2B cells and induced ferroptosis. Studies have found that cigarette smoke and exposure to particulate matter could inhibit the Nrf2 pathway, resulted in a obviously reduction of GPX4 activity and GSH levels, accompanied by elevated lipid peroxidation products, leading to increased airway inflammation. Existing evidence indicated that cigarette smoke triggered ferroptosis, with lung tissues from smokers exhibiting significantly reduced Nrf2 protein levels compared to non-smokers, suggesting that smoking could reduce the level of Nrf2. and analysis revealed significantly lower Nrf2 protein expression in COPD patients compared to smokers without COPD (P < 0.01), indicating its potential pathogenic significance in COPD [ ]. After exposure to glyphosate, L02 cells also showed a decreased level of GPX4 activity, SLC7A11, and Nrf2 proteins [ ], which was consistent with our results, indicating that acrolein exposure inhibited the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway and induced ferroptosis in cells.

3.4.

## RNA-sequencing analysis
3.4.1.

## Differential expression of lncRNA and mRNA
In respiratory diseases, lncRNAs are associated with COPD, interstitial pneumonia, lung cancer, pulmonary hypertension, and acute lung injury [ ]. Acrolein could induce cell ferroptosis. To further explore the role of lncRNA in acrolein-induced cell ferroptosis, and to better analysis the effect of acrolein toxicity on the transcriptome of cells, transcriptome sequencing of lncRNA in the control group and the acrolein-injured group was conducted. The transcriptome sequencing results of acrolein injury group and control group samples were compared, 18868 differential lncRNAs and 15639 differential mRNAs were obtained. The majority of lncRNA and mRNA with moderate expression accounted for a small part, while those with low expression and high expression accounted for a small part ( , and M). Among them, there were 636 lncRNAs and 214 mRNAs with significant differences, including 355 up-regulated and 281 down-regulated lncRNAs, 107 up-regulated and 107 down-regulated mRNAs ( A–F, J, and N). According to the control group and 80 μM acrolein group of visible lncRNA and mRNA genomic circles ( G and K), it could be found that lncRNA and mRNA were evenly distributed on chromosomes, and there was no obvious preference for location [ ]. These data suggested that acrolein exposure induced the significant differences of the lncRNA and mRNA level, suggesting that these lncRNA and mRNA might play an significant role in the cytotoxicity of BEAS-2B induced by acrolein. Consistent with these observations, Wang et al. performed lncRNA transcriptome analysis in β-dione antibiotic-exposed zebrafish, revealing DKA-induced dysregulation of both lncRNAs and their putative target genes, which might modulate zebrafish immune responses [ ].
 
3.4.2.

## Differential lncRNA and mRNA GO enrichment analysis
The gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis results of differentially expressed lncRNA target genes were annotated based on GO categories, including molecular function (MF), biological process (BP), and cell component (CC), as shown in O and P, the enrichment pathways of different lncRNA target genes mainly included regulation of oligodendrocyte apoptotic process, regulation of mitochondrial membrane potential, DNA topological change and other processes. GO analysis results of differential mRNA showed that differential genes were mainly involved in iron ion binding, gated channel activity, tissue homeostasis, etc. A comprehensive analysis of the enrichment results of differential genes of lncRNA and mRNA showed that acrolein treatment might affect tissue homeostasis, iron ion, MAPK signaling pathway, apoptosis pathway, etc. In our previous studies, we found that treatment with acrolein could induce the reduction of mitochondrial membrane potential, induce mitochondrial pathway apoptosis, and activate DNA damage response [ , ]. The cell level was closely related to the results obtained by sequencing, which could be mutually validated and complemented. At the cellular level in this study, acrolein exposure has been shown to induce ferroptosis. To verify the accuracy of the sequencing results, it was subsequently verified and combined with the animal level detection results to further explore the toxic effect of acrolein on lung injury.

3.5.

## Verification and analysis of differentially expressed genes
Based on the data obtained by transcriptome sequencing, qPCR experiments were used to verify the sequencing accuracy. As shown in A and B, three lncRNA (FTV1, MIR155HG, MALAT1) and three mRNA (TMEM74, EFNA2, CD200R1) were randomly selected for expression verification at the cellular level. After 80 μM acrolein treated, the lncRNA expressions of FTV1, MIR155HG, and MALAT1 were significantly increased, mRNA expressions of TMEM74 and EFNA2 were significantly increased, CD200R1 was obviously reduced, corroborating the transcriptomic data and supporting the reliability of the sequencing analysis. LncRNA MIR155HG was significantly highly expressed in sequencing results and cell level detection. To explore the association between lncRNA MIR155HG and acrolein cytotoxicity, the interaction between lncRNA MIR155HG and acrolein toxic-related proteins was predicted by the RPISeq database. As shown in C, at the overall level, the RF and SVM scores of lncRNA MIR155HG interacting with proteins related to ferroptosis pathway were both greater than 0.5. These results indicated that lncRNA MIR155HG might interact with proteins related to ferroptosis at the overall level [ ]. Based on this, to elucidate the regulatory effect of lncRNA MIR155HG, the influence of knockdown lncRNA MIR155HG in acrolein toxicity was further explored.
 
3.6.

## LncRNA MIR155HG/miR-155-5p/CD200R1 regulatory axis
The lncRNA/miRNA/mRNA regulatory axis as key regulators in diverse pathological processes. For instance, the regulatory axis of lncRNA NEAT1/miR-944/TRIM37 and lncRNA TUG1/miR-34b-5p/GAB1 were involved in inflammatory pathways in lung injury [ ]. In brain ischemia/reperfusion models, lncRNA PVT1 regulated ferroptosis through miR-214-mediated expression of TFR1 and TP53, and there might be a positive feedback loop [ ]. High levels of lncRNA MIR155HG may affect the pathogenesis of lung diseases by aggravating inflammatory responses. LncRNA/miRNA serve as critical regulators in the regulation of gene expression, especially lncRNA, as the host gene, regulates miRNA expression. As shown in D, lncRNA MIR155HG was reported to be located on chromosome 21 and consists of 3 exons, with miR-155-5p located in the exon 3 region of its host gene lncRNA MIR155HG and is regulated by it [ ]. Next, downstream target genes of miR-155-5p were screened. miRNA regulates gene expression mainly by interacting with the 3′UTR of target mRNA. By predicting the downstream target protein of miR-155-5p, the intersection of miRNA and acrolein was screened by differential mRNA sequencing results after exposure, as shown in E, there were 6 intersections, indicating that there were potential binding sites between miR-155-5p and CD200R1, SLITRK6, FGF9, MAK, IGDCC4 and the 3′ UTR region of HLA-DPA1. Among them, CD200R1 is closely related to inflammation and can inhibit the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and participates in the regulation of NF-κB signaling pathway. Notably, transcription of lncRNA MIR155HG was regulated by various transcription factors, such as NF-κB [ ]. These results supported that acrolein might play an key regulatory role in cytotoxicity through the regulatory axis of lncRNA MIR155HG/miR-155-5p/CD200R1/NF-κB. Our previous results have also shown that acrolein exposure induced the activation of NF-κB pathway in BEAS-2B cells [ ]. However, its ability to regulate acrolein-induced ferroptosis remains unclear and will be investigated in the following studies.

3.7.

## LncRNA MIR155HG knockdown alleviated the cytotoxic effect of acrolein
3.7.1.

## Regulate the gene expression of miR-155-5p/CD200R1 and improve cell viability
To determine whether lncRNA MIR155HG modulates the miR-155-5p/CD200R1 axis, the level of lncRNA MIR155HG was knocked down to detect whether there were corresponding changes in miR-155-5p and CD200R1. Quantitative analysis revealed that si-MIR155HG group significantly reduced both lncRNA MIR155HG and miR-155-5p expression levels compared to acrolein treatment alone, while concurrently elevating CD200R1 expression ( A–C). The results indicated that si-MIR155HG group could effectively reduce the expression of lncRNA MIR155HG, and lncRNA MIR155HG could affect the expression of miR-155-5p and CD200R1. Moreover, compared with acrolein group alone, cell viability was significantly increased after MIR155HG knockout, suggesting that MIR155HG might be negatively correlated with cell viability ( D). In addition, the effects of MIR155HG knockdown on cell ROS levels, GSH levels, MDA contant, and Iron concentration were also measured. The data showed that compared with the acrolein treatment group, MIR155HG knockdown significantly induced a decrease in ROS levels, MDA content and Iron concentration, and a significant increase in GSH levels ( ). Based on this, the expression of key proteins of ferroptosis in si-MIR155HG group were subsequently detected to further clarify its specific role in the mechanism of acrolein toxicity.
 
3.7.2.

## Effect of lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown on ferroptosis pathway
As demonstrated in E–I, the protein expressions of Nrf2, HO-1, SLC7A11, and GPX4 in si-MIR155HG group were obviously increased compared with those in the acrolein injury group (P < 0.01), and those in si-NC group were similar to those in the acrolein injury group. The results showed that lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown inhibited acrolein-induced ferroptosis. Furthermore, 5-lipoxygenase (ALOX5) plays an important role in cell death and inflammatory responses, multiple reports have shown that ALOX5 can promote ferroptosis in cells [ , ]. In this study, acrolein significantly increased ALOX5 protein level. After lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown, its increase was effectively inhibited ( J–L). In addition, multiple lncRNAs have identified as critical modulators of ferroptosis, and previous studies found that lncRNA RNA H19 was significantly down-regulated by RNA sequencing in curcumin-treated lung cancer cells compared to untreated controls. Overexpression of lncRNA H19 eliminated the anti-cancer effect of curcumin, while low expression of lncRNA H19 promoted curcumin-induced ferroptosis [ ]. Similarly, studies have also showed that lncRNA MEG3 expression was significantly up-regulated in lipopolysaccharide-induced acute lung injury in mice, and lncRNA MEG3 down-regulation inhibits the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway by up-regulating miR-93 level, thus inhibiting the pathological process of lung injury. It was suggested that lncRNA MEG3 might be a feasible target for the treatment of acute lung injury [ ]. Based on the data obtained at the cellular level, the toxicity of acrolein will be tested at the animal level.

3.8.

## Knocking down lncRNA MIR155HG inhibited acrolein-induced lung injury in rats
Firstly, whether lncRNA MIR155HG effectively knockdown was detected, and whether its knockdown had a regulatory effect on the level of miR-155-5p and CD200R1 was analyzed. A–C confirmed MIR155HG knockdown efficacy, which also led to the down-regulation of miR-155-5p and the up-regulation of CD200R1. The concordance between in vitro and in vivo data confirmed lncRNA MIR155HG modulated miR-155-5p and CD200R1 expression, indicating that lncRNA MIR155HG might play an significant regulatory role in the toxicity of acrolein. Based on the effective knockdown of lncRNA MIR155HG, the regulatory effect of the knockdown on lung injury in rats was tested.
 The weight growth rate of rats induced by acrolein exposure decreased. Compared with the 4 ppm group alone, the weight growth rate reduction of the si-MIR155HG group was alleviated, but the difference was not significant, and the weight of the rats in the si-NC group was close to that of the injured group ( D). In addition, organ index is a commonly used index in toxicology, and the weight of the injured organ may change, which will affect organ index. As shown in E, acrolein exposure induced an increase in lung index, and after lncRNA MIR155HG was knocked down, the lung index of rats was significantly decreased, indicating that the low expression of lncRNA MIR155HG could improve the lung injury induced by acrolein. When acrolein exposure concentration was 4 ppm, the W/D ratio of rats was obviously increased, indicating that it induced pulmonary edema in rats. The W/D ratio of si-MIR155HG group was obviously lower than that of acrolein injury group, indicating that the low expression of lncRNA MIR155HG could alleviate acrolein-induced pulmonary edema ( F).
Lung morphological changes are illustrated in G. In the control group, the lung surface was smooth, lung injury was induced by acrolein exposure, and the abnormalities in lung morphology were very obvious, including nodules, hard mass and surface roughness. Compared with the 4 ppm injury group alone, nodules and hard mass of lung tissue were reduced after lncRNA MIR155HG knock down. Lung morphology of the si-NC group also showed abnormal lung morphology accompanied by hard mass, similar to that of the injury group, indicating that lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown effectively alleviated acrolein-induced lung injury. Furthermore, the effects of 2, 4, and 8 ppm acrolein on lung morphology in rats were also shown in . Similarity, after exposure to LPS, it was also found that the lung tissue W/D ratio of mice was significantly higher than that of the control group, and the lung W/D ratio of mice after knocking down lncRNA MEG3 was reduced [ ]. Furthermore, acrolein exposure also induced the activation of p38MAPK, DNA damage response, and apoptosis signaling pathway in rats ( , and 5).

3.9.

## Knockdown MIR155HG alleviated lung injury induced by acrolein
3.9.1.

## Histologic observation
To determine whether lncRNA MIR155HG modulates acrolein-induced lung structural damage,HE and Masson staining were used to evaluate the histopathology of lung tissue. As shown in A, acrolein induced structural damage of lung tissue in rats, accompanied by alveolar rupture, alveolar cavity expansion, and alveolar wall thickening. Compared with the 4 ppm acrolein group alone, the structural destruction of lung tissue was improved in the si-MIR155HG group, and the expansion of alveolar cavity and the destruction of alveolar wall were alleviated. In the LPS-induced model of acute lung injury, severe damage to lung structure, and thickening of the alveolar wall of lung tissue were also observed. However, after lncRNA MEG3 knockdown, lung damage was significantly improved, which was similar to the lungs of control mice [ ]. Masson staining is one of the classical methods for collagen color development in tissues. Collagen fibers showed blue color, while no obvious blue deposition was observed in the control group. Relative to the control group, blue collagen fiber deposition increased and showed diffuse distribution in the 4 ppm acrolein exposure group, indicating that exposure induced pulmonary tissue fibrosis. Compared with acrolein injury group, si-MIR155HG group showed a small amount of blue collagen fiber deposition, and the degree of lung fibrosis was significantly reduced ( B). The results of HE staining and Masson staining of rat lung tissue after 2,4, and 8 ppm acrolein exposure were also presented in . Previous studies have reported that knocking down lncRNA XIST alleviatesd renal fibrosis by up-regulating miR-19b and inhibiting SOX6 [ ].
 
3.9.2.

## Knockdown of lncRNA MIR155HG inhibited acrolein-induced ferroptosis
The iron deposition is one of the indicators of ferroptosis, to assess lung tissue iron accumulation following acrolein exposure, prussian blue staining was performed, as demonstrated in C, iron accumulation in acrolein-treated lung tissues obviously increased, while the iron content of si-NC group was close to that of acrolein exposure group. After knocking down lncRNA MIR155HG, the tissue iron content decreased significantly ( D). Not only that, the effects of MIR155HG knockdown on ROS levels, MDA contant, and GSH levels in lung tissue were also detected. Acrolein triggered serious oxidative stress, evidenced by ROS accumulation, lipid peroxidation, and GSH depletion. However, this trend was improved in the MIR155HG knockdown group. Relative to the acrolein injury group, MIR155HG knockdown significantly induced a decrease in ROS levels and MDA contents, and a significant increase in GSH levels ( ). Furthermore, as shown in A–D, acrolein exposure induced a decrease in the expression of Nrf2 into the nucleus in lung tissue, and the protein expressions of HO-1, GPX4, and SLC7A11 were significantly decreased (P < 0.01), which was consistent with the results of BEAS-2B cells, indicating that acrolein exposure reduced the antioxidant capacity of rat lung tissue and induced ferroptosis. However, the protein expressions of Nuclear Nrf2, HO-1, SLC7A11, and GPX4 were significantly increased after lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown (P < 0.01) ( E–H), and the effects were similar between the si-NC group and the acrolein injury group. In addition, evidence implicated lncRNA MIR155HG as a key regulator in cervical cancer, compared with neighboring cervical tissues, lncRNA MIR155HG levels are obviously up-regulated in cervical cancer tissues, and its knocking down inhibits the progression of cervical cancer by binding SRSF1 [ ]. LncRNA have been reported to have a variety of regulatory effects, zhou et al. [ ] observed high lncRNA NEAT1 level in both mice with acute lung injury and LPS-induced A549 cells, and down-regulating lncRNA NEAT1 could alleviate cytotoxicity by inhibiting HMGB1/RAGE/NF-κB pathway. In addition, lncRNA NEAT1 up-regulated TLR4 level by regulating miR-98-5p, and aggravated the LPS-induced inflammatory response in A549 cells [ ]. Knocking down lncRNA MEG3 inhibited inflammation in mice with LPS-induced acute lung injury [ ]. These data demonstrate indicated that the knockdown of lncRNA MIR155HG effectively improved acrolein-induced ferroptosis.
 

3.10.

## Acrolein induced inflammatory responses in BEAS-2B cells and lung tissue
Chemical-induced toxicity frequently arises from dysregulated inflammatory responses, which exacerbate cellular stress and organ injury To elucidate the regulatory function of lncRNA MIR155HG in acrolein-induced inflammation in BEAS-2B cells ( A–E) and rat lung tissue ( F–J), this study also detected the effects of acrolein exposure on the NF-κB signaling pathway in BEAS-2B cells and rat lung tissue, the results are shown in . After acrolein injury, the nuclear translocation NF-κB p65 level significantly increased (P < 0.01), indicating that the NF-κB pathway was activated and promoting the protein level of inflammatory factors IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-18 (P < 0.01).
 However, after lncRNA MIR155HG knockdown, the protein expression ratios of p-IκBα/IκBα and Nuclear p65/Total p65 decreased significantly (P < 0.01), while the expressions of IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-18 decreased obviously (P < 0.01) ( ). It indicated that lncRNA MIR155HG could effectively inhibit the activation of the NF-κB inflammatory pathway induced by acrolein in BEAS-2B cells and lung tissues, reduce the protein level of related inflammatory factors.
 

4.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, acrolein exposure promotes lung injury by inducing ferroptosis. At the cellular and animal levels, the lncRNA-MIR155HG/miR-155-5p/CD200R1 axis was a key pathogenic pathway in the lung injury caused by acrolein, and induced ferroptosis by regulating the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway. After knockdown of lncRNA MIR155HG, it effectively regulated the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 pathway and inhibited the ferroptosis pathway. These results revealed the toxic mechanisms of acrolein, suggesting that lncRNA MIR155HG might be a potential therapeutic target and might provide a new treatment strategy for lung injury.

5.

## Limitation of the study
This investigation primarily examined acrolein-induced lung injury in cell and male rat model, the toxic effect in the actual environment is more complex, and the sensitivity of different age groups, different genders and susceptible groups to the toxic effect of acrolein is still unclear. The biological toxicity of acrolein will be analyzed from multiple dimensions in the future, so as to provide data reference for the health effects of human exposure in the actual polluted environment.
