## Abstract
Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is a severe neuromuscular disease with emerging therapeutic complexity. This review aims to systematically map the global pipeline of investigational treatments for SMA. Using ClinicalTrials.gov and complementary international registries, we identified 21 planned or ongoing interventional trials from 2020 to 2025 targeting novel agents, alternative dosing and delivery routes. Inclusion criteria focused on unapproved therapies or new uses of existing drugs, with rigorous data extraction across demographics, modalities and trial phases. Results reveal a shift towards dual‐pronged strategies: refining SMN‐targeted interventions and expanding SMN‐independent approaches, such as myostatin inhibitors, neuromuscular modulators and ion‐channel blockers. Early‐phase outcomes suggest promising motor function improvements and acceptable safety profiles, though long‐term efficacy remains under investigation. This evolving landscape underscores the importance of registry‐based analyses in tracking translational innovation, informing stakeholders and guiding future research priorities.
Keywords: clinical trials, gene therapy, investigational therapies, SMN‐independent treatments, spinal muscular atrophy

## INTRODUCTION
Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is a rare, inherited neuromuscular disease caused by mutations in the survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene, with an estimated incidence of 1 in 10 000 live births and a prevalence of 1–2 per 100 000 individuals. , The most common form, known as 5q‐linked SMA, results from homozygous deletions or mutations in SMN1, located on chromosome 5q13. A nearly identical paralogue, SMN2, partially compensates for the loss of SMN1 by producing small amounts of functional SMN protein. However, due to a critical single‐nucleotide difference, the majority of SMN2 transcripts undergo alternative splicing that excludes one of the exons, leading to the expression of a truncated, unstable protein and an overall deficiency of functional SMN.
The SMN protein plays an essential role in the assembly of small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs), a key step in the pre‐mRNA splicing. , Besides, SMN contributes to many post‐transcriptional processes that are critical for neuronal maintenance and functions, such as axonal mRNA transport, cytoskeletal dynamics, mitochondrial functions and protein homeostasis. , , , Thus, despite the ubiquitous expression of SMN, its deficiency primarily affects the lower motor neurons of the spinal cord, leading to progressive, symmetrical muscle weakness and atrophy. 

SMA is traditionally categorized into five clinical types (0–4), ranging from severe prenatal‐onset disease with early mortality (Type 0) to adult‐onset mild proximal weakness and near‐normal lifespan (Type 4). , This phenotypic spectrum reflects underlying differences in SMN protein level, largely dictated by the copy number of the SMN2 gene and other genetic and molecular modifiers. Symptom severity and disease trajectory are characterized by the age of onset, the highest motor milestone attained and the rate of neuromuscular decline. , Updated nosological frameworks now reflect the era of early, often presymptomatic detection and classify individuals based on current motor function and anticipated disease course. These include the presymptomatic SMA nosology and treatment‐oriented frameworks informing disease‐modifying therapies (DMT)‐related decision‐making. The SMA community continues to refine disease classification to keep pace with this evolving therapeutic landscape.
The therapeutic landscape of SMA has been transformed by the approval of several DMTs. , , , These include nusinersen, an antisense oligonucleotide that modifies SMN2 pre‐mRNA splicing to increase production of full‐length SMN protein, onasemnogene abeparvovec, an adeno‐associated virus (AAV9) vector that delivers SMN1 gene to motor neurons, and risdiplam, an oral small‐molecule splicing modifier that promotes exon 7 inclusion in SMN2 transcripts, thereby increasing systemic levels of functional SMN protein. These agents have markedly improved survival, motor function and quality of life, especially in infants and young children when initiated presymptomatically. , , , , , , , , , However, despite their efficacy, current therapies primarily target SMN restoration and do not fully address downstream pathogenic processes, such as glial dysfunction, impaired neuromuscular junction integrity and systemic metabolic abnormalities. , , 

As SMA evolves into a chronically managed disorder, there is a growing imperative to explore next‐generation therapeutic strategies that go beyond SMN‐dependent mechanisms. Second‐generation antisense oligonucleotides and gene therapies are being refined to enhance the production of functional SMN protein. Meanwhile, SMN‐independent approaches are explored. For example, antibodies against myostatin, a negative regulator of muscle growth, are used to promote muscle hypertrophy and improving motor output. β₂‐adrenergic agonists and small‐molecule androgen‐receptor (AR) modulators stimulate anabolic signalling to enhance muscle mass and endurance. To enhance neuromuscular transmission, ion channel modulators are being investigated for their ability to regulate motor neuron excitability and synaptic efficacy. Concurrently, efforts are underway to optimize the delivery, dosing and administration routes of existing therapies.
Recently, several of these approaches have progressed from preclinical basic research into early‐phase clinical trials. This momentum reflects not only a deepening understanding of their underlying molecular mechanisms but also the urgent need to expand treatment options for patients across the SMA spectrum. The objective of this scoping review is to systematically map and critically evaluate the current landscape of investigational treatments for SMA, with particular emphasis on unapproved agents, novel dosing regimens and alternative delivery routes. By surveying ongoing and forthcoming clinical trials, this review aims to capture the latest wave of therapeutic innovation and to identify emerging opportunities that may shape the future treatment landscape of this complex and multifaceted disease.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
We conducted a search of ClinicalTrials.gov, the most extensive international registry of randomized controlled trials (RCTs), to identify investigational therapies for SMA. To the best of our knowledge, ClinicalTrials.gov lists over 300 000 studies globally, in contrast to the European Union Clinical Trials Register, which includes approximately 34 000 trials. Given the global nature of multicentre RCTs evaluating novel therapeutics, particularly those seeking regulatory approval in the United States, Europe and beyond, ClinicalTrials.gov was deemed the most appropriate primary data source for this analysis.
On July 9, 2025, two independent reviewers (A.B. and E.J.) systematically searched ClinicalTrials.gov using the keyword ‘spinal muscular atrophy’. The following inclusion criteria were applied: (1) Study phase: early Phase 1, Phase 1, Phase 2, or Phase 3; (2) Study design: interventional clinical trials; (3) Recruitment status: not yet recruiting, recruiting, active but not recruiting or enrolling by invitation; (4) Study initiation date: on or after January 1, 2020, and up to July 9, 2025.
Only studies investigating novel therapeutic strategies were included. These included: new molecular entities not yet approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the treatment of SMA; new dosing regimens (e.g., higher than the approved dose); or novel routes of administration or drug delivery systems.
Sponsors typically conduct interim analyses during active clinical trials, often influencing the decision to proceed with or terminate development programmes. A five‐year capture window was therefore selected to allow sufficient time for the emergence and identification of meaningful investigational treatments that may progress towards regulatory review.
To ensure completeness, a manual secondary review of the European Union Drug Regulating Authorities Clinical Trials Database (EudraCT and EU Clinical Trials portal) and the World Health Organization (WHO) International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP) was conducted independently by two independent reviewers (I.S., A.B.). We additionally cross‐checked ClinicalTrials.gov entries against the corresponding records in the EU Clinical Trials Databases when available, to ensure consistency and accuracy of trial description. Any additional eligible studies identified from these sources were included in the final analysis.
All relevant data were extracted into a predefined Microsoft Excel spreadsheet developed for this study (Table  ). Data fields extracted included: NCT number, study title, study URL, phase, geographic location, investigational substance/interventions, dosing regimen, route of administration and delivery system, condition/SMA subtype, eligibility criteria, primary and secondary outcomes, study start and completion dates, anticipated or actual number of enrolled participants, recruitment status, sponsor and collaborators.

## New molecular entities not yet approved by the US FDA or EMA
3.2.1.

## Monoclonal antibodies

NCT05115110, the MANATEE study (last updated on June 22, 2025; Hoffmann‐La Roche; started June 2022; estimated completion February 2029), is an ongoing international, multicentre Phase II/III study designed to evaluate the pharmacokinetics, safety and efficacy of the investigational humanized anti‐myostatin antibody RO7204239 that targets an epitope in the predomain region of the inactive precursor of myostatin, in combination with risdiplam in individuals with SMA, evaluating the clinical value of combining a muscle‐directed therapy with standard SMN2 splicing modulation. The trial plans to enrol approximately 259 participants across up to 35 sites in the United States, Australia, Belgium, Canada, Croatia, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Spain and the United Kingdom. Eligible participants include ambulant and non‐ambulant patients aged 2–25 years with genetically confirmed 5q‐linked SMA who may be treatment‐naïve or previously exposed to nusinersen or onasemnogene abeparvovec, provided that sufficient washout and recovery period criteria are met. Specific subcohorts are defined as follows: Part 1 Cohorts A and B who are ambulant participants and Cohort D who are non‐ambulant participants, who are all 5–10 years old, and part 1 cohort C who are ambulant participants who are 2–4 years old. Part 2 cohort are all ambulant participants who are 2–25 years old. Cohort D comprises of non‐ambulant individuals who meet certain functional criteria using MSM32 and RULM. Exclusion criteria include recent use of anti‐myostatin agents, severe scoliosis (>40° curvature), clinically significant unstable diseases, significant ECG or echocardiographic abnormalities, recent hospitalizations or surgery or prior participation in certain investigational studies.
The study comprises two sequential parts: Part 1, a dose‐finding randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled phase wherein participants first receive daily oral risdiplam during an 8‐week run‐in period, and then randomized 2:1 to RO7204239 or placebo via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks over 6 months. The primary endpoints in Part 1 include safety and tolerability, particularly the proportion of participants experiencing AEs or SAEs up to 4.5 years, and detailed pharmacokinetic measures of RO7204239 (i.e., serum Cmax, AUC, Ctrough and time to peak concentration) and risdiplam (i.e., plasma levels and metabolite concentrations). Immunogenicity is assessed through the incidence of anti‐drug antibodies (ADAs) in the blood. Exploratory pharmacodynamic biomarkers include changes in total, free latent and mature serum myostatin levels and quantitative MRI‐ or DXA‐based assessments of skeletal muscle contractile area in thigh and calf muscles. Whereas Part 2 is the pivotal, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled trial among ambulant patients aged 2–25 years, which administers an a priori dose of RO7204239 with risdiplam vs. placebo with risdiplam every 4 weeks for 72 weeks (~18 months). Primary and secondary efficacy outcomes include motor function assessment, such as change from baseline in the Revised Hammersmith Scale (RHS) total score at Week 72, MFM Domain 1 + 2 and total scores, timed performance measures (e.g., 10‐m walk/run test and floor‐rise time) and lean mass quantified by full‐body DXA. Other assessments include PK/PD and immunogenicity monitoring. Lastly, both parts transition to the 2‐year open‐label extension study to evaluate long‐term safety and durability.

NCT05337553, the RESILIENT study (last updated on June 18, 2025; Biohaven Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; started July 2022; estimated completion on January 2026), is an ongoing international, multi‐centre Phase III randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of taldefgrobep alfa (BHV‐2000 or BMS‐986089), a bioengineered adnectin fusion protein that targets the active/free form of myostatin, among participants with SMA. The trial aims to enrol approximately 269 ambulant or non‐ambulant individuals with genetically confirmed 5q‐autosomal recessive SMA, across 53 sites in the United States, Belgium, Czechia, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom. RESILIENT is among the first pivotal, placebo‐controlled trials to test systemic anti‐myostatin therapy as an adjunct to standard‐of‐care SMN‐directed DMTs in a broad SMA population. Eligible participants must be receiving and are expected to remain on an existing SMA DMT throughout the trial, specifically nusinersen, risdiplam or following prior treatment with onasemnogene abeparvovec, and must weigh at least 15 kg. Key exclusions include prior exposure to anti‐myostatin agents, clinically significant respiratory insufficiency requiring daytime ventilatory support, spinal fusion surgery, except for MAGEC rod non‐surgical adjustments within the past 6 months, and the presence of an implanted CSF shunt or CNS catheter.
During the double‐blind phase, randomization is conducted in a 1:1:1 allocation across three arms: taldefgrobep alfa 35 mg weekly, taldefgrobep alfa 50 mg weekly or matched placebo administered weekly; all participants are maintained on stable, background SMA DMT. This is followed by an open‐label extension phase, wherein participants are receiving taldefgrobep alfa at the same weekly dosing. The primary efficacy endpoint is the change from baseline in the Motor Function Measure‐32 (MFM‐32) total score, comparing taldefgrobep alfa to placebo at Week 48. Secondary endpoints include changes in RHS, lean body mass, DXA‐measured bone mineral density, Tanner staging and quality‐of‐life measures. Safety and tolerability are evaluated through the incidence of new or worsening laboratory abnormalities and AEs and SAEs, particularly those leading to study drug discontinuation during the double‐blind phase. Exploratory measures include plasma Ctrough of taldefgrobep alfa and participant‐reported assessments of injection acceptability.

NCT05626855, the ONYX study (last updated May 5, 2025; Scholar Rock, Inc.; started April 2023; estimated primary completion May 2029), is an ongoing international, multicentre open‐label extension trial designed to evaluate the long‐term safety, tolerability, efficacy, pharmacokinetics and immunogenicity of the investigational anti‐myostatin monoclonal antibody apitegromab, a fully human monoclonal antibody against pro‐ and latent‐myostatin (also known as SRK‐015), among patients with SMA who previously participated in earlier apitegromab trials. The study tests multi‐year safety and efficacy in participants who have already completed earlier apitegromab trials, offering rare longitudinal data on the durability of myostatin inhibition in SMA. It plans to enrol approximately 238 participants across 49 sites in the United States, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom. Eligible participants must have completed either the Phase II TOPAZ (SRK‐015‐002) study or the Phase III SAPPHIRE (SRK‐015‐003) trial, defined by completion of their respective end‐of‐study visits or active participation when the feeder trial ends. These are patients with SMA Type 2 or 3, irrespective of ambulatory status, provided they have a life expectancy >2 years, and can tolerate IV infusions through peripheral or existing long‐term access devices. For females of childbearing age, they should have a negative pregnancy test and should agree to use at least 1 high‐effective contraception throughout the clinical trial and 20 weeks after receiving the last dose of apitegromab. Key exclusion criteria include permanent discontinuation of apitegromab in the feeder trial or currently enrolled in any trials apart from TOPAZ or SAPPHIRE, unstable nutritional status or feeding tube dependence, chronic daytime non‐invasive ventilation (>16 h daily), prior severe hypersensitivity to SMN‐targeted therapies or apitegromab itself and clinically significant comorbidities. All participants will receive apitegromab at 20 mg/kg administered via intravenous infusion every four weeks throughout the extension period of 104 weeks.
Primary measures include the incidence and severity of AEs and SAEs. Secondary efficacy endpoints include longitudinal changes in validated motor function measures, including the HFMSE and RULM and WHO motor development milestones at prespecified time points for non‐ambulatory patients, and RHS, 6‐Minute Walk Test (6MWT), and 30‐second Sit‐to‐Stand for ambulatory patients. Immunogenicity was measured using the incidence of antidrug antibodies against apitegromab up to 6 years. Other measures include PK/PD using apitegromab concentrations and total latent myostatins concentrations, respectively, in blood samples at prespecified time points for up to 6 years, and patient‐ and caregiver‐reported disability and fatigability measured using Paediatric Evaluation of Disability Inventory Computer Adaptive Test, Patient‐reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) Fatigue Questionnaire and Assessment of Caregiver Experience with Neuromuscular Disease (ACEND).

NCT07047144, or the OPAL study (last updated on July 2, 2025; Scholar Rock, Inc.; planned study start September 2025; completion by March 2029), is a planned, multicentre Phase II clinical trial designed to evaluate the PK, PD, safety and preliminary efficacy of apitegromab in combination with standard SMN‐targeted therapies (i.e., onasemnogene abeparvovec, nusinersen or risdiplam) among infants and toddlers with 5q‐linked SMA who are younger than 2 years at enrollment. The study aims to enrol around 52 participants, all of whom must have a genetically confirmed SMA with at >1 copy of SMN2, delayed motor milestones for age or a CHOP‐INTEND score < 55, and must either be receiving or have previously received an approved SMN‐targeted therapy. The participants would have had a gestational age ≥ 35 weeks, birth weight ≥ 2.0 kg and bodyweight above the 1st percentile for age. Exclusion criteria include unstable nutritional status, major orthopaedic interventions (e.g., scoliosis surgery) planned during the study period and physical limitations precluding standardized motor function assessments. Participants will receive apitegromab intravenously every 4 weeks, randomized to either high‐ or low‐dose groups, alongside background SMN2‐directed therapy, i.e., nusinersen intrathecally or risdiplam orally. The study design focuses on characterizing the PK profile and PD biomarkers of apitegromab in this population. Additional measures include motor function, including standardized motor milestone scales adapted for infants, and comprehensive safety monitoring, such as treatment‐emergent AEs, laboratory abnormalities and immunogenicity. Among myostatin‐targeting programmes, this Phase II study is distinctive in restricting enrolment to infants and toddlers already receiving SMN‐directed therapy, thereby examining whether very early adjunctive myostatin inhibition can further enhance outcomes in the newborn‐screening era. This emphasis on treatment during the earliest developmental window aligns with emerging evidence that gestational age may influence treatment response, potentially because a shorter period of untreated motor‐neuron degeneration in utero preserves a larger pool of salvageable motor units at the time therapy is initiated.

## Gene therapies delivered through adeno‐associated virus vector

ChiCTR2200066399 (last updated on December 5, 2022; GeneCradle Inc.; started November 28, 2022; estimated completion November 28, 2028) is an ongoing, multi‐centre, open‐label Phase I/IIa dose‐escalation study in China, evaluating the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of GC101, a self‐complementary adeno‐associated virus (AAV) vector delivering the SMN1 gene, in infants with genetically confirmed SMA type 1. The study plans to enrol 15 participants, each with biallelic SMN1 pathogenic variants, two copies of SMN2, and symptom onset before six months of age. Participants must meet strict inclusion criteria, including negative or low anti‐AAV9 antibody titres, no prior exposure to gene therapy, and absence of severe organ dysfunction, uncontrolled epilepsy or significant scoliosis. The trial consists of two sequential phases. Phase I is safety and tolerability stage, assessing the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs after a single intrathecal administration. They also capture tolerability biomarkers, including AAV transgene load, immunogenicity and vector shedding. Phase IIa is the exploratory efficacy phase, which evaluates motor milestone achievement, e.g., sitting without assistance for ≥10 s, and changes in CHOP‐INTEND scores at 18 months. Other measure at 18 months includes growth indicators and event‐free survival measures.

NCT05824169 (last updated July 3, 2025; GeneCradle Inc.; started in February 2023; estimated completion in December 2026) is another open‐label, multicentre Phase I/II trial in China, designed to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of GC101 among infants with genetically confirmed SMA type 1. The trial plans to enrol 18 symptomatic participants aged six months or younger at the time of vector infusion, diagnosed with SMA through confirmation of biallelic SMN1 deletion or point mutations and two copies of SMN2. Exclusion criteria include significant organ dysfunction, high baseline anti‐AAV9 antibody titres (≥1:50), prior gene therapy, severe scoliosis and uncontrolled epilepsy. GC101 is administered intrathecally as a single dose among patients enrolled sequentially into two dose groups, either 2.4 × 1014 vg or 4.8 × 1014 vg, accompanied by prophylactic corticosteroids to mitigate immune response. Participants will be followed for up to 24 months to monitor both short‐ and medium‐term outcomes. The study is evaluating safety by the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs, and efficacy, particularly the proportion of participants who are able to sit unassisted for >10 s at 18 months of age. Secondary endpoints include changes from baseline in CHOP‐INTEND scores and achievement of motor milestones based on the WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study and survival without permanent ventilation.

NCT05901987 (last updated on July 3, 2025; GeneCradle Inc.; started August 1, 2023; completion by December 2028) is an ongoing, multicentre Phase I/II clinical trial conducted in China to evaluate the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of GC101 among paediatric patients with SMA type 2. The study is enrolling approximately 33 participants aged >6 months and <5 years, with genetically‐confirmed biallelic SMN1 mutations and with two copies of SMN2, who are able to sit independently but cannot stand or walk unaided. Eligible participants must not have previously received other SMA gene therapies or recent SMN2‐splicing modifiers, i.e., nusinersen <4 months or risdiplam <15 days, and not must have anti‐AAV9 neutralizing antibodies titre ≥1:200. Exclusion criteria include significant comorbidities, significant elevation of AST/ALT or contraindications to lumbar puncture. There are three parallel dosing cohorts: a low‐dose group receiving a single intrathecal infusion of 1.2 × 1014 vg; a medium‐dose group with 2.4 × 1014 vg; and a high‐dose group with 4.8 × 1014 vg. The primary endpoints assess safety through the incidence and nature of treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs. The study also assesses early efficacy signals, including the proportion of children able to stand unassisted for >3 s and changes in HFMSE at 12 months follow‐up. Secondary and exploratory outcomes include the proportion achieving walking five independent steps, improvement of ≥3 points on HFMSE and changes in independent ventilatory support time.

NCT06421831 (last updated on July 3, 2025; GeneCradle Inc.; started May 10, 2024; completion by December 2028) is an ongoing, Phase I/II clinical trial in multiple centres in China, evaluating the safety and preliminary efficacy of GC101 among patients with SMA type 3. The study is enrolling approximately 21 participants aged ≥2 years, each with genetically confirmed bi‐allelic SMN1 deletions and baseline HFMSE scores between 10 and 54. Participants should have not received nusinersen within 120 days or risdiplam within 15 days prior to dosing, free from significant comorbidities, and have AAV9 neutralizing antibody titres <1:200. Patients are excluded if they have high ventilatory support requirements (≥16 h/day), need for enteral feeding, severe bodily contractures and any clinically significant systemic disease or prior gene‐therapy exposure. All participants receive a single intrathecal injection of GC101 at 1.2 × 1014 vector genomes (vg). The primary outcome is the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs. Key efficacy outcomes include changes in HFMSE and RULM scores and the proportion of participants achieving a increase in HFMSE score by ≥3‐point at Month 12.

NCT06971094 (last updated on June 5, 2025; GeneCradle Inc.; started May 27, 2025; completion by December 31, 2026) is an ongoing, multi‐centre Phase III clinical trial in China evaluating the safety and efficacy of GC101 among patients with SMA type 2 who previously received long‐term treatment with nusinersen. The trail aims to enrol around 50 participants who meet the following inclusion criteria: genetically confirmed 5q‐SMA, ability to sit independently but inability to walk, a baseline HFMSE score ≥ 10 and continuous nusinersen therapy for >1 year prior without recent risdiplam use <2 months before enrolment and no planned use <12 months after. Exclusion criteria include abnormal liver function, contraindications to glucocorticoid use and lumbar puncture, a serum anti‐AAV9 neutralizing antibody titres >1:50, severe comorbidities (e.g., epilepsy, infections, CSF circulation disorders) and with recent vaccination or gene therapy exposure. Following a parallel assignment design without masking, participants are randomly assigned 1:1 to either a single intrathecal injection of GC101 (1.2 × 1014 vg per person) followed by discontinuation of nusinersen or to continue standard‐of‐care treatment with nusinersen alone. The primary outcome measure is the change from baseline in HFMSE score. Key secondary outcomes include the proportion of participants achieving an HFMSE increase of ≥ 3 points, changes in RULM and SMA Independence Scale (SMAIS) scores, and improvements in WHO‐MGRS motor milestones. Safety is monitored through the incidence AEs, SAEs and adverse events of special interest (AESIs).

ChiCTR2500095667 (last updated on January 10, 2025; GeneCradle Inc.; started on January 18, 2025; completion by December 2030) is an ongoing, single‐centre Phase I/II clinical study evaluating the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of GCB‐001, another gene‐replacement therapy delivered by a self‐complementary AAV9 vector that carries a functional SMN gene cassette, among patients with SMA type 2 in China. They plan to enrol six participants, each receiving a single‐dose intrathecal injection of GCB‐001.
Eligible children must have genetically confirmed SMA type 2, with symptom onset between >6 and <18 months of age. This is characterized by the ability to maintain sitting without hand support for more than 3 s but never able to walk ≥4 steps unassisted, according to HFMSE criteria.
Exclusion criteria included conditions that may have undue risk with gene replacement therapy; previous participation in AAV gene therapy or other SMA drug trials; recent treatment with nordenafil sodium <4 months), risperidone <15 days or β2‐agonists <30 days, except inhaled salbutamol; allergies to glucocorticoids or excipients; dependence on NIV ≥ 12 h/day; anti‐AAV9 neutralizing antibody titre >1:200; significant lab abnormalities in terms of ALT/AST, total bilirubin, creatinine, urine protein/haematuria and thrombocytopnia; weight below −2 standard deviations for age and sex or dependence on enteral feeding; O2 saturation <95% while awake; contraindications to lumbar puncture or intrathecal dosing; active infection, including HIV, hepatitis B or C or syphilis; and vaccination <2 weeks of dosing.
The participants are enrolled in two dosing cohorts, a low‐dose group (n = 3), receiving intrathecal administration of low‐dose GCB‐001 and a high‐dose group (n = 3), who receive a higher single intrathecal dose of GCB‐001. Dose escalation is sequential upon safety review. The primary endpoint focuses on the incidence, severity and nature of treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs. Secondary objectives include exploring the preliminary efficacy of the therapy, measured by clinically relevant motor milestones and standardized functional motor assessments. Exploratory endpoints include AAV viral load, immunogenicity through anti‐AAV neutralizing antibodies and viral shedding profiles.

NCT05747261, the BLUEBELL study (last updated on February 2, 2024; Biocad; started February 2, 2023; completion by August 2030) is an ongoing Phase I/II, multicentre, open‐label, dose‐escalation trial evaluating the safety and preliminary efficacy of ANB‐004, a standard AAV9 vector carrying a codon‐optimized SMN1 gene cassette, among infants with SMA. Conducted across two sites in the Russian Federation and Belarus, the trial is enrolling approximately 40 participants. Participants included are infants <240 days old with genetically confirmed 5q‐SMA and either two copies of SMN2, among presymptomatic or symptomatic with onset ≤180 days, or three copies of SMN2, among those with SMA type 1 symptoms onset ≤180 days. Participants are excluded if they had prior treatment with gene therapy or SMN2‐targeted drugs (e.g., nusinersen, risdiplam), ventilatory support for ≥16 h/day, significant hepatic or renal dysfunction, elevated anti‐AAV9 antibody titre at >1:50, serious illness, or participation in other investigational trials. Participants receive an intravenous infusion of escalating doses of ANB‐004, i.e., dose 1, dose 2 and dose 3, each administered over 60 min. The primary outcomes focus on safety, particularly the proportion of participants experiencing AEs, SAEs and grade ≥3 AEs graded by CTCAE 5.0 or DAIDS. Secondary outcomes focus on motor development, particularly changes in HINE scores and mortality. Other outcomes include immunogenicity, e.g., anti‐AAV9 and anti‐SMN1 antibody and T‐cell responses, and distribution of the AAV9 vector in body fluids.

NCT06288230 (last updated October 10, 2024; Lantu Biopharma; started October 2024; completion by October 2027) is an open‐label, single‐centre Phase I/II clinical study evaluating the safety and preliminary efficacy of vesemnogene lantuparvovec, another self‐complementary AAV serotype 9 vector carrying a codon‐optimized human SMN1 gene, among patients with genetically confirmed SMA. Conducted in China, the trial plans to enrol approximately six participants. Eligible patients must have bi‐allelic SMN1 pathogenic variants, regardless of SMA type or age, although exclusions include active viral infection, including HIV, hepatitis B or C, clinically significant laboratory abnormalities, or invasive ventilatory support, previous participation in SMA investigational studies, those with signs of aspiration on swallowing test or those unwilling to use alternative feeding routes. Each participant received a single intravenous infusion of vesemnogene lantuparvovec. The primary endpoint is the incidence and nature of treatment‐emergent AE and SAEs. Key secondary outcomes include changes in daily hours on ventilatory support and motor function scores, particularly HFMSE and the proportion of ambulant participants who retain walking ability over 24 months.

NCT05614531 (last updated September 19, 2024; Hoffmann‐La Roche; started February 2023; estimated completion August 2025) is an ongoing Phase I/II, open‐label, multicentre trial designed to evaluate the safety, PK and preliminary tolerability of EXG001‐307, a self‐complementary AAV9 vector carrying a codon‐optimized SMN1 gene cassette under the neuron‐specific hSyn promoter with CMV enhancer,
 
in infants with SMA type 1 to contribute data on very early treatment in pre‐ or minimally symptomatic infants. Conducted exclusively across two sites in China, the study plans to enrol 12 participants, with an initial cohort of two newborns. Eligible participants are male or female <180 days post‐natal at first dose, born at ≥35 weeks' gestation, and identified to have genetically confirmed 5q‐autosomal recessive either through prenatal genetic testing or newborn screening. Infants should have history and signs consistent with SMA type 1, including hypotonia, joint hypermobility, poor head control, delayed motor function and round shoulder posture. Exclusion criteria include oxygen saturation < 96% at screening without ventilatory support or need for invasive ventilation, tracheotomy or ≥ 16 h/day of non‐invasive ventilation; weight below the 3rd percentile based on WHO standards; active viral infections (e.g., HIV, COVID‐19, hepatitis B/C, TORCH, EBV, syphilis); recent serious illness or infection; significant cardiac disease; hypersensitivity to prednisolone or glucocorticoids; recent immunosuppressive or immunomodulatory therapy; anti‐AAV9 antibody titre > 1:50 (with retest allowed); prior DMT or other SMA clinical trial participation; planned major surgery; and other conditions deemed unsuitable by investigators.
EXG001‐307 is administered as a single intravenous infusion in escalating dose cohorts, with all participants receiving oral glucocorticoid prophylaxis to mitigate immune responses to the vector. There is no placebo or comparator, consistent with the early‐phase, exploratory design. Primary PK endpoints include plasma concentration profiles, AUC and steady‐state concentration (Css). Safety and tolerability are assessed through the proportion of participants experiencing treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs, including those leading to treatment.

NCT06888661 (last updated on March 21, 2025; Hangzhou Jiayin Biotech Ltd; started March 10, 2025; completion by December 2026) is an ongoing, early Phase I clinical study designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability and preliminary efficacy of EXG001‐307 among infants and young children with genetically confirmed SMA. Conducted across multiple centres in China, the trial plans to enrol four participants who will each receive a single intrathecal injection of EXG001‐307. Eligible patients include two distinct subgroups: those with SMA type 1 aged ≤ 180 days carrying two copies of SMN2, and those with SMA type 2 aged > 180 days to ≤ 2 years carrying ≤ 3 copies of SMN2. All must have confirmed biallelic SMN1 mutations and clinical signs consistent with SMA. Extensive exclusion criteria address prior SMA‐specific treatments, invasive ventilation, major comorbidities, severe scoliosis, significant infections, abnormal imaging findings and contraindications to lumbar puncture or corticosteroid use. The primary endpoint is the incidence and nature of treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs. Secondary endpoints include changes in motor function, assessed by CHOP‐INTEND and HINE‐2.The rapid expansion of the SMN gene‐replacement landscape reflects accelerating innovation by multiple international developers. These ongoing studies also arise from a combination of dose‐finding uncertainty, population stratification, jurisdiction‐specific regulatory requirements and evolving clinical questions. First, heterogeneity in SMA phenotype and treatment timing necessitates stratified development pathways. OAV101 trials are explicitly partitioned by clinical stage and SMN2 copy number: NCT03461289 targets symptomatic SMA type 1 infants, NCT04174157 focuses on presymptomatic infants with two or three SMN2 copies, while NCT05335876 serves as a long‐term follow‐up cohort for previously treated participants. A similar logic is visible in the GC101 trials, where studies in early symptomatic SMA type 1 (ChiCTR2200066399, NCT05824169, NCT05901987) , , are complemented by a later‐onset cohort already receiving nusinersen (NCT06421831 ). Second, differences in delivery route and dosage are being investigated in separate trials. NCT03381729 evaluates intrathecal OAV101 in SMA type 2, while NCT05335876 follows participants treated with either IV or IT formulations. Analogously, within the Chinese programme, ChiCTR2500095667 represents an intrathecal AAV‐SMN1 construct studied in parallel with the intravenous GC101 trials (ChiCTR2200066399, NCT05824169, NCT05901987, NCT06421831, NCT06971094 ). Also, the OAV101 programme separated intravenous dose exploration and intrathecal dosing into distinct early‐phase studies (NCT03461289 and NCT03381729 ), while the GC101 programme includes a dedicated Phase I/II dose‐escalation trial (NCT05901987 ) alongside other studies that use fixed mid‐ or high‐dose tiers (e.g., ChiCTR2200066399, NCT05824169 ).
Regulatory frameworks and study governance can also contribute to the fragmentation of development programmes across multiple studies. Large multicentre industry‐sponsored trials (e.g., STR1VE‐EU and SPR1NT ) typically follow a conventional Phase I/II‐to‐III sequence, whereas other settings may adopt parallel sponsor‐initiated and investigator‐initiated designs with different ethics pathways and operational constraints (as in the GC101/GCB‐001 portfolio). Together, these examples show that the multiplicity of SMN gene‐replacement trials arises from dose‐finding needs, population‐specific questions, route‐of‐delivery experimentation and jurisdictional requirements. Although this has produced a degree of fragmentation across early‐phase studies, these parallel efforts are likely to yield complementary and clinically valuable insights once results mature, helping to refine dose optimization, timing of intervention and the selection of appropriate delivery routes.

## Antisense oligonucleotide

NCT05575011 (last updated July 9, 2025; Biogen; started October 2022; estimated completion in November 2031) is an international, multicentre Phase I/II trial studying the safety, tolerability and PK of BIIB115 (salanersen), an investigational, chemically optimized antisense oligonucleotide designed to enhance the splicing of SMN2 in patients with genetically confirmed 5q‐linked SMA. The trial aims to enrol approximately 62 participants at sites across North America, Europe and Asia, stratified into sequential study parts. This early‐phase trial is uniquely positioned to evaluate intrathecal BIIB115 in patients who have previously received onasemnogene abeparvovec, directly addressing how antisense therapy can be sequenced after gene therapy in SMA.
The trial comprises of Parts A and B, followed by a long‐term extension of Part B (Part B LTE), with distinct eligibility criteria for each part. Part A only include investigator‐judged healthy males aged 18–55 years with a body mass index of 18–30 kg/m2. Exclusions include any condition that increases the risk for lumbar puncture injuries, any significant comorbid systemic disease, serious or chronic infections <90 days before screening, or recent participation in any other trial. Whereas Part B participants must be aged 6 months to 12 years, weigh at least 7 kg, and have genetically confirmed 5q‐SMA with at least one copy of SMN2, who have had sufficient washout period after receiving intravenous onasemnogene abeparvovec, and is assessed by the investigator to have a potential for clinical improvement. Exclusions include unresolved AE from prior gene therapy, ongoing steroids at screening, prior SMN2‐splicing modifiers after gene therapy or nusinersen <12 months, permanent ventilation, history of serious liver injury, thrombotic microangiopathy, hydrocephalus or shunts or other factors increasing lumbar puncture risk. While Part B LTE allows participation of Part B participants, those who have new or worsening medical conditions, clinically significant laboratory or ECG abnormalities, or treatment with approved SMN2‐splicing modifiers during the LTE period are absolute exclusions.
In Part A, healthy male adults are randomized to receive either a single intrathecal bolus dose of BIIB115 or placebo (dose escalation, four cohorts) blinded, with follow‐up over approximately 13 months. In Part B, paediatric patients with SMA receive two open‐label intrathecal bolus doses of BIIB115, dose of which are guided by Part A, and with follow‐up period of around 24 months. Those completing part B may participate in Part B LTE wherein they would receive five additional BIIB115 doses over about five years. The primary endpoint across all study parts is the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs. Key secondary endpoints include PK measures of BIIB115, including CSF and serum concentrations, elimination half‐life (t½), Cmax, Tmax, AUC0‐last and AUCinf, assessed over short‐term and longer‐term periods. The LTE phase continues long‐term monitoring of exposure and safety.

## Ion Channel blockers

NCT05794139, The SYNAPSE‐SMA study (last reported on March 13, 2025; NMD Pharma A/S; started September 21, 2023; completion by January 2026), is an ongoing Phase II, multicentre, randomized, placebo‐controlled crossover trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of NMD670, an inhibitor of chloride channel ClC‐1 in skeletal muscle which reduces membrane hyperpolarization and thus increases muscle fibre activation in response to motor neuron signals, among ambulatory adults with genetically confirmed SMA type 3. This study is the first randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled, two‐way crossover trial of a first‐in‐class ClC‐1 inhibitor (NMD670) and reflects an emerging interest in therapies that augment motor‐neuron‐to‐muscle signalling. , Support for this therapeutic concept comes from earlier clinical trials such as NCT02941328 and NCT03781479, which demonstrated that Pyridostigmine and Amifampridine, by increasing acetylcholine availability at the neuromuscular junction, can improve either HFMSE scores or self‐reported fatigability in SMA patients. Together, these findings provide a mechanistic and clinical rationale for exploring agents like NMD670 as adjuncts to SMN‐directed therapies, particularly for symptoms such as exertional fatigue that remain insufficiently addressed by current standards of care.
Conducted across more than 40 sites in Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain and the United States, the SYNAPSE‐SMA study is enrolling approximately 54 participants. Participants included are male or female ≥18 years old with a SMA type 3, genetically confirmed 3–5 copies of the SMN2 gene, able to walk ≥50 m unaided on the 6‐MWT and BMI of 19–35 kg/m2. Participants are excluded if they have prior surgery or fixed deformity (e.g., scoliosis, contractures), other neuromuscular disorders or significant diseases, significant laboratory or clinical safety findings, prior treatment with an investigational drug <30 days or <5 half‐lives, whichever is longer, or a history of poor compliance with SMA therapy. Participants are randomized in a two‐way crossover to receive oral NMD670 or a matching placebo. The primary outcome is the change in total distance walked in the 6‐MWT. Secondary outcomes include change in muscle strength, 6MWT fatigue index, RHS scores, electrophysiological measures, e.g., jitter and blocking, and safety assessments including treatment‐emergent AEs, SAEs, abnormalities in ECG and vital signs, and ophthalmological assessments, and suicidality.

## New dosing regimens or delivery routes of already FDA/EMA‐approved SMA therapies
3.3.1.

## Risdiplam

NCT05808764, the PUPFISH Study (last updated on June 22, 2025; Hoffmann‐La Roche; started April 26, 2024; completion by October 30, 2025), is an ongoing Phase II, open‐label, multicentre trial designed to characterize the PK and safety of risdiplam among newborn infants with genetically confirmed 5q‐autosomal recessive SMA. The trial aims to study the plasma exposure and steady‐state concentrations of risdiplam if initiated among newborns. This study is distinctive in that it focuses specifically on pharmacokinetics and safety of risdiplam in very young infants, generating infant‐specific exposure data rather than simply extrapolating from older paediatric cohorts. Conducted across approximately seven sites in Belgium, Canada, Germany, Italy, Norway, Poland and the United States, the study plans to enrol around 10 participants. Participants are included if they are male or female newborn infants aged < 20 days at first dose, born at gestational age ≥ 37 weeks, identified either via newborn screening or prenatal testing, clinically stable and receiving adequate nutrition and hydration. Exclusion criteria include clinical symptoms of SMA type 0, significant ECG or cardiovascular abnormalities, inadequate venous or capillary access, use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers <4 weeks or multidrug and toxin extrusion substrates <2 weeks, and prior treatment with nusinersen or onasemnogene abeparvovec. Participants receive oral risdiplam, starting at 0.15 mg/kg orally once a day for 28 days. The primary outcomes include PK parameters, specifically AUC, Css and free fraction of risdiplam. Safety endpoints include proportion of participants with AEs, SAEs and treatment discontinuation from AEs.

3.3.2.

## Nusinersen

NCT04729907 (last updated on February 3, 2025; Biogen; started on April 19, 2021; completion by July 31, 2026) is an ongoing Phase III, global, open‐label extension study evaluating the long‐term safety and tolerability of higher doses of nusinersen (BIIB058) in participants with SMA who previously completed the ONWARD trial (NCT04089566). Conducted across 39 sites in countries including the United States, Europe, Asia, Latin America and the Middle East, the trial is enrolling approximately 115 participants who received nusinersen at the Day 302 visit in the ONWARD study. The trial excludes those who were treated with another investigational therapy after completing the earlier trial or those who began another SMA treatment at Day 302. The study population only includes participants who rolled over from ONWARD. Using a treatment‐focused, non‐randomized, open‐labelled design, participants continue intrathecal nusinersen according to their prior dosing history from the ONWARD trial. One group receives their previous maintenance dose of 28 mg every 4 months. Another group, previously on maintenance dose of 12 mg, receives 50 mg dose on Day 1 and then 28 mg maintenance dose every 4 months until Day 1921. The primary outcomes focus on safety, particularly the incidence of AEs and SAEs, changes in growth parameters, laboratory values, including coagulation measures and urine protein, ECG findings (i.e., QTcF prolongation), vital signs. Neurological developmental milestones are assessed by age group.

NCT05067790, the ASCEND study (last updated on May 6, 2025; Biogen; started January 21, 2022; completion by June 14, 2027), is an open‐label, multicentre Phase III trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of high‐dose nusinersen (BIIB058) among adolescents and adults with later‐onset SMA previously treated with risdiplam. Conducted in the United States, Belgium, Germany, Italy, Poland and Spain, the study is enrolling 45 non‐ambulatory participants aged 15–50 years, each with genetically confirmed 5q SMA and onset of symptom >6 months of age. All included participants must have previously received oral risdiplam; those who are nusinersen naive are required to have been on risdiplam for at least 6 months before enrolment, whereas those who were previously treated with nusinersen must have discontinued nusinersen for >16 months and received risdiplam for >12 months perior to enrolment. Included participants should be >20 kg, have the ability to complete functional assessments, with RULM total score of 5–30 and item A score of ≥3, unable to walk 15 ft unaided, and willing to discontinue risdiplam and initiate high‐dose nusinersen. Exclusion criteria include significant illness <1‐month, untreated active infections, CNS shunts or catheters, permanent tracheostomy or ventilation, need for daytime NIV, previous bacterial meningitis, encephalitis or hydrocephalus, serious comorbidities, pregnancy or breastfeeding, recent participation in investigational treatments <30 days or <5 half‐lives), and any prior or current gene therapy for SMA.
All participants receive two loading doses of 50 mg intrathecally about two weeks apart and then maintenance doses of 28 mg every 4 months. An optional LTE phase allows continued maintenance dosing of 28 mg for up to two additional years. The primary outcome is the change in RULM total score. Secondary outcomes include the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs and SAEs, changes in clinical laboratory parameters, ECG findings and vital signs, including O2 saturation.
Higher doses of nusinersen are being investigated in these trials based on the hypothesis, stated in the ONWARD and ASCEND trial protocols, that increased intrathecal exposure may offer additional clinical benefit in individuals whose response to the approved 12 mg maintenance dose or to prior risdiplam therapy has plateaued.

NCT06555419, the PIERRE‐PK study (last updated on July 17, 2025; Biogen; started January 16, 2025; completion by May 15, 2026) is an ongoing Phase I, multicentre, open‐label PK study designed to evaluate how nusinersen is processed in the body when administered via the ThecaFlex DRx™ implantable intrathecal delivery system compared with standard lumbar puncture, as part of the first pivotal Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) programme of this device. Conducted across 10 sites in France, Italy, Spain, Poland, Germany and the United States, is enrolling approximately 58 participants who must also be enrolled in the parent PIERRE study. Eligible participants include those aged ≥3 years with SMA already receiving nusinersen at the standard 12 mg maintenance dose every 4 months and are scheduled for implantation of the ThecaFlex DRx™ system, primarily indicated by challenging lumbar puncture from anatomical or respiratory limitations. Exclusion criteria include those nusinersen‐naive, those who have already undergone divide implantation, and those receiving other investigational SMA therapies apart from apitegromab and taldefgrobep alfa. Nusinersen is delivered at the same maintenance dose of 12 mg intrathecally, comparing PK parameters after administration via standard lumbar puncture and after delivery through the ThecaFlex DRx™ device. The primary outcomes include Cmax and AUC0–24 h following both delivery methods.

## Onasemnogene abeparvovec

NCT05335876, the SPECTRUM study (last updated on December 10, 2024; Novartis Pharmaceuticals; started December 19, 2022; completion by June 26, 2030), is an ongoing Phase III, multicentre, long‐term follow‐up study evaluating the safety and durability of developmental and motor outcomes among patients SMA previously treated with onasemnogene abeparvovec (OAV101) in earlier clinical trials. Conducted across approximately 29 sites spanning the United States, Australia, Belgium, Canada, China, Denmark, France, Italy, Japan, Malaysia, the Netherlands, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Spain, Taiwan, Thailand, the United Kingdom and Vietnam, the trial is enrolling around 175 participants who received either intravenous or intrathecal OAV101 as part of parent studies COAV101A12306, COAV101B12301 or COAV101B12302. Participants are included if they have completed an earlier OAV101 trial; there is no specified exclusion criteria. There is no new dosing intervention, rather it is a prospective collection of safety and efficacy date over extended follow‐up period. The primary outcomes include the incidence of treatment‐emergent AEs, SAEs and AESIs. Secondary outcomes include motor‐function measures (HFMSE, RULM) alongside a structured WHO‐based Developmental Milestone Checklist assessing six binary gross‐motor milestones. This dedicated developmental assessment is a novel feature among SMA follow‐up studies and, together with motor scales, allows SPECTRUM to evaluate the long‐term durability of OAV101‐related gains with greater developmental precision than earlier trials.

## DISCUSSION
This scoping review systematically identifies 21 clinical trials investigating novel therapeutic strategies for SMA. By mapping novel molecular entities and trials of re‐engineered delivery or dosing of approved agents, our review provides a comprehensive snapshot of the current global SMA investigational pipeline across diverse age groups, phenotypes and mechanistic targets. Our analysis reveals a trend towards increasingly diversified therapeutic strategies, extending beyond SMN2 splicing correction and SMN1 gene replacement towards novel mechanistic interventions. Among the therapies under ongoing clinical trials, antibodies or antibody‐like molecules that target myostatin (e.g., apitegromab, taldefgrobep alfa) and neuromuscular modulators (e.g., clenbuterol, mexiletine, NMD670) reflect a growing focus on muscle function restoration, independent of upstream SMN level. Meanwhile, next‐generation gene therapies (e.g., GC101, vesemnogene lantuparvovec, GCB‐001) and oligonucleotides (e.g., BIIB115) highlight the ongoing refinement of SMN‐targeted strategies. These clinical trials reflect a dual‐pronged therapeutic approach in the SMA treatment landscape, one focused on enhancing SMN expression and the other on improving muscle performance (Figure  ).
 This review offers a comprehensive database of the SMA therapeutic pipeline, summarizing investigational trials identified through public registries. Whereas many studies , , , , , focus primarily on the post‐approval experience with currently available SMA treatments, our work examines ongoing investigational therapies and considers how emerging mechanisms and trial designs may guide future clinical decision‐making. In doing so, we extend earlier reports, such as, , by providing a comprehensive, internationally sourced, trial‐level synthesis of the current investigational pipeline in SMA. Furthermore, we broaden the scope of the recent US‐centred clinical‐trial landscape survey by incorporating non‐US trials and by providing detailed scientific context, including mechanistic rationale, delivery strategies and comparative features of the investigational products. This combined approach offers a strategically oriented, globally inclusive overview of the next generation of SMA therapeutics.
The findings of this scoping review align with and extend the current understanding of investigational therapies for SMA as documented in preclinical studies, early‐phase clinical trials and conference abstracts. Many of the therapeutic directions now represented in active clinical development, including novel molecular entities, alternative dosing regimens and innovative routes of administration, mirror the research priorities previously identified. These emerging approaches have demonstrated encouraging results in preclinical models, such as increased SMN protein expression, improved neuromuscular function and enhanced motor neuron survival, underscoring the translational potential of the evolving therapeutic landscape. Early‐phase trials have further supported the safety and pharmacokinetic profiles of these interventions, providing a rationale for progression to larger efficacy studies. However, a notable gap persists between the breadth of ongoing investigations registered in clinical trial databases and the limited number of peer‐reviewed publications available to date. This discrepancy highlights the critical role of clinical trial registries in offering timely and comprehensive insight into the evolving therapeutic landscape, capturing studies that may not yet have reached publication or that report preliminary findings. Such registries thus provide a unique vantage point for observing which therapeutic strategies, among the vast and diverse body of preclinical research, are being prioritized for clinical testing. In doing so, they offer a snapshot of how pharmacological knowledge is translated into human studies and which mechanistic hypotheses are gaining early clinical traction. Such analyses thus serve as invaluable tools for researchers, clinicians and patients seeking to understand the full scope of innovation in SMA treatment and to identify emerging opportunities for participation and collaboration. 

This review uses ClinicalTrials.gov as the primary source of data because it offers the most comprehensive, detailed and consistently updated repository of global interventional trials relevant to SMA. A comparative analysis of major clinical trial registries underscores the predominance of ClinicalTrials.gov in capturing the majority of relevant RCTs. ClinicalTrials.gov distinguishes itself by offering broader coverage, more comprehensive and granular data entries and more frequent updates relative to other registries such as the EudraCT/EU Clinical Trials and the WHO ICTRP. The registry's robust infrastructure and mandatory registration policies for trials conducted under U.S. regulatory oversight contribute to its extensive and up‐to‐date repository. In contrast, EudraCT and WHO ICTRP, while valuable in their respective contexts, often provide less detailed trial information, experience delays in data updates and may not capture the full spectrum of ongoing international research activities. In particular, EudraCT does not include non‐interventional trials such as observational studies, nor clinical trials involving surgical procedures, medical devices or psychotherapeutic interventions. It also lacks public access to national regulator authorization documents and ethics committee opinions. Its coverage is limited to trials with sites in the EU/EEA, except for agreed Paediatric Investigational Plans (PIPs). Additionally, it does not support trial participant recruitment, and its content is only available in English, restricting access for non‐English speakers. These constraints highlight the need for ongoing enhancements to the register to ensure it meets the diverse informational needs of stakeholders in clinical research.
Despite the clear advantages of ClinicalTrials.gov, reliance on a single registry risks overlooking studies conducted outside the U.S. or those not subject to U.S. regulatory requirements. As such, triangulating data across multiple registries remains essential to ensure a comprehensive and accurate mapping of the global clinical trial landscape. This approach is particularly important for identifying studies registered exclusively in regional or national databases, thereby minimizing the risk of publication and registration bias. Moreover, cross‐referencing information from multiple sources enhances the reliability of trial identification, facilitates the detection of discrepancies or omissions and supports more informed decision‐making for researchers, clinicians and policymakers. The continued harmonization and integration of registry platforms will be critical for advancing transparency, data completeness and global accessibility in clinical research.
The implications of our findings are multifaceted. Mapping the investigational pipeline provides clinicians and policymakers with foresight regarding forthcoming treatment options, while also highlighting underrepresented subgroups, such as specific SMA subtypes or age cohorts, that may benefit from targeted research efforts. The diversity of therapeutic approaches, including non‐SMN‐targeting agents, combination regimens and immune modulators, reflects a growing recognition of the complexity of SMA pathophysiology and the need for personalized therapeutic strategies.
Our review also highlights the practical value of clinical trial registries for patients and clinicians, particularly in the context of Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products, whose high costs may limit access outside of clinical trials. The availability of user‐friendly search tools, such as the EU Clinical Trials Register (https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search) and the recently launched clinical trial map (https://euclinicaltrials.eu/search-for-clinical-trials/trial-map/?lang=en), facilitates identification of active studies and trial locations, with publicly accessible contact information for investigators. Despite these advances, there remains a general lack of awareness regarding search strategies and the availability of such resources, underscoring the need for better education and outreach. An important objective of this review is to enhance awareness and provide practical guidance on how clinicians, patients and families can effectively search for active clinical trials in SMA using publicly available databases. In the EU, databases such as the EU Clinical Trials Register offer user‐friendly search functionalities that allow users to identify ongoing trials and their specific locations. Searches can be conducted using key words, including the protected code or generic name of the investigational medicinal product (IMP) ‐for example, ‘RO7204239’ or ‘Onasemnogene Abeparvovec‐xioi’—as well as broader terms such as ‘spinal muscular atrophy’. Importantly, these platforms provide detailed information about each trial, including the locations of participating sites and direct contact details for investigators, such as names, emails and phone numbers, thereby facilitating communication and potential trial enrolment. A recent advancement is the launch of the EU clinical trial map in May 2025, which further streamlines the search process by enabling users to filter trials by medical condition, country, region and recruitment status. This tool is particularly valuable for patients and families, as it presents complex information in an accessible format and supports informed decision‐making about trial participation. Despite these resources, there remains a general lack of familiarity among both clinicians and patients regarding effective search strategies and the availability of such comprehensive data. By highlighting these tips and resources, this review aims to empower stakeholders to navigate clinical trial databases more efficiently, thereby improving access to investigational therapies and supporting participation in ongoing research. , , 

The strengths of this study include its comprehensive, multi‐database search strategy, systematic inclusion criteria and focus on registry‐based evidence. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. Clinical trial registries are subject to underreporting, delayed updates and incomplete data entries, which may result in the omission of relevant studies. Lack of access to unpublished data, may further limit the scope of our analysis. Additionally, while early‐phase trials are included, it is recognized that only a minority of Phase 1 studies ultimately progress to regulatory approval, particularly in the context of orphan drug development and gene therapies. The unique challenges of trial design in rare diseases, such as the frequent use of single‐arm, nonrandomized or open‐label studies, also warrant consideration when interpreting the evidence base. Furthermore, because we did a scoping review limited to peer‐reviewed publications and registry data within the defined search time window, we did not systematically perused press releases or conferences. Hence, some included trials may since have been completed, but these updates were beyond the scope of our review. It is important to note that the focus of this review was limited to pharmacological interventions. Other types of interventions, such as optimized rehabilitation programmes, respiratory support techniques, neuromodulation, and digital health applications, are also under investigation in SMA but were not included in our analysis.
In summary, this scoping review provides a timely and comprehensive synthesis of the current therapeutic landscape for spinal muscular atrophy, underscoring the breadth of innovation alongside the inherent complexities of navigating a rapidly evolving field. The dynamic nature of SMA drug development, encompassing diverse modalities from gene therapy to small molecules, reflects significant progress yet also presents challenges in maintaining up‐to‐date, accessible and high‐quality data repositories. To optimize clinical and research utility, ongoing initiatives aimed at improving registry transparency, ensuring data completeness and enhancing user accessibility are imperative. Such efforts will be critical to facilitating robust evidence‐based decision‐making, accelerating translational research and ultimately promoting equitable access to emerging treatments for all individuals affected by SMA.
