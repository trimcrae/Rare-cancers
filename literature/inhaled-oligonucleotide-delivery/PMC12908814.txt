## Abstract

## Background:
The nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain [NOD-], leucine-rich repeats [LRR-], and Pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome plays an essential role in hypertension-related atrial fibrillation (AF). p300 is involved in cardiovascular inflammation. In this study, we aimed to investigate the role of p300 in NLRP3 inflammasome activation and its subsequent impact on the Ikur current in angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced HL-1 cells and Ang II-infused mice.

## Methods:
Expression levels of p300, Kv1.5, and NLRP3 in left atrial appendage (LAA) tissues from AF and participants with sinus rhythm (SR) were detected by Western blot. A hypertension mouse model was established in p300 knockout (p300-KO) mice via Ang II infusion, and AF incidence was assessed by electrocardiogram (ECG) after rapid atrial pacing. In vitro, the expression level of p300 in HL-1 cells was modulated by adenoviral overexpression, curcumin (an inhibitor of p300) treatment, and small interfering RNA (siRNA) knockdown. NLRP3 inflammasome activation was evaluated by Western blot and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and electrophysiological properties of HL-1 cells were analyzed using whole-cell patch-clamp recordings. Co-immunoprecipitation assays were performed to investigate the interaction between p300 and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB).

## Results:
The expression levels of p300, Kv1.5, and NLRP3 were found to be significantly higher in the LAA tissue of AF patients compared to SR patients. p300-KO decreased AF incidence in Ang II-infused mice by impairing NLRP3 inflammasome activation. p300-OE facilitated NLRP3 inflammasome activation, which subsequently increased the Ikur density and shortened the action potential duration of HL-1 cells. Both curcumin and p300-siRNA treatments reversed Ang II-induced atrial electrical remodeling and NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Moreover, co-immunoprecipitation showed that p300 interacts with NF-κB to promote NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

## Conclusions:
p300 participates in hypertension-induced AF susceptibility by interacting with NF-κB to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, which subsequently upregulates the transmembrane current of Ikur in atrial cardiomyocytes.

Keywords: Atrial fibrillation, Hypertension, p300, NLRP3 inflammasome, Ikur, Nuclear factor kappa B

## Introduction
Atrial fibrillation (AF) represents one of the most prevalent sustained cardiac arrhythmias, with studies indicating a rise in incidence correlated with advanced age.[ – ] Hypertension constitutes the predominant cardiovascular pathology predisposing individuals to AF, being present in approximately 80% of AF group and independently responsible for 14% of AF cases as a solitary risk factor.[ – ] While hypertension evidently exhibits a significant association with AF predisposition, the precise pathophysiological mechanisms underlying hypertension-mediated induction of AF currently remain incompletely elucidated.
The pathogenic mechanisms underlying AF include structural and electrical remodeling of the atrium. Fibrosis is a marker for structural remodeling. Electrical remodeling involves functional changes in atrial cardiomyocytes and atrial arrhythmias. Markers of atrial electrical remodeling include shortening of the effective refractory period (ERP), loss of frequency adaptation, and prolongation of atrial conductivity, which are due to the changes in transmembrane ion channels. Accumulation of intracellular calcium and decreased inward L-type Ca2+ current (ICa,L) in phase 2 of the action potential (AP) are major phenomena of AF-related electrical remodeling. Although fewer studies have examined the ultra-rapid delayed rectifier K+ current (Ikur), it is known to be solely atrium-selective.[ ] Ikur, carried by the Kv1.5-α subunit and Kvβ1.2/Kvβ1.3 accessory subunits, is thought to function in phases 1–3 of the AP of atrial cardiomyocytes, but not in ventricular cardiomyocytes.[ , ] Hence, Kv1.5 is regarded as a promising molecular target in the treatment of AF without concomitant ventricular adverse effects.[ ]
The transcriptional co-activator p300 plays an important epigenetic role in heart development.[ ] p300 has been shown to be involved in atrial fibrosis induced by hypertension.[ ] However, its role in cardiac electrical remodeling is unclear. Inflammation is associated with AF, and the nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain (NOD), leucine-rich repeats (LRR), and Pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome regulates innate immunity and is involved in AF pathogenesis. Increased AF susceptibility was correlated with NLRP3-mediated upregulation of Kv1.5 expression in a high-fat diet-induced obesity mouse model.[ ] Thus, targeting the NLRP3 inflammasome may benefit AF treatment. A recent study showed that p300 may play an important role in NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Curcumin, a p300 inhibitor, inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation and reduced inflammation in mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages, resulting in reduced interleukin 1 beta (IL-1β) secretion and inhibition of the inflammatory response.[ ] However, it remains unclear whether p300 activates the NLRP3 inflammasome in atrial cardiomyocytes, leading to AF development.
Plasma angiotensin II (Ang II) is increased in patients suffering from hypertension.[ ] Ang II stimulates intravascular NLRP3 inflammasome activation, leading to endothelial dysfunction and vascular remodeling, while the NLRP3 inhibitor, MCC950, alleviates these pathological changes.[ ] However, whether Ang II causes activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome within atrial cardiomyocytes during the development of AF and the specific mechanisms of Ang II-induced NLRP3 activation require further elucidation.
This study aimed to examine the role of p300 in the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in Ang II-induced hypertension and determine whether this process increases atrial susceptibility to AF.

## Methods

## Patients
This study was reviewed and approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital, Guangdong Academy of Medical Sciences (No. GDREC2019071H; Guangzhou, Guangdong, China) and performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all patients. Atrial tissues were collected from participants during the necessary surgical procedures for structural heart diseases. Patients who presented with sepsis, pneumonia, or other infectious diseases were excluded from this study. Patients’ heart rhythms were verified by electrocardiograms (ECGs) before surgery. LAA samples were frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for further experiments. Whole blood samples from patients were collected for testing the levels of cytokines using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). All detailed clinical data on patients are summarized in Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C336.

## Animals
All animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee on Experimental Animals of South China University of Technology (Ethic code: 2019063). The investigation involving the use of animals conformed to the guidelines from Directive 2010/63/EU of the European Parliament on the protection of animals used for scientific purposes and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The p300 flox/flox mice were used to mate with CAG-cre mice to generate p300 knockout (p300-KO) mice on a C57BL/6J background, as described previously.[ ] The mice were raised in specific pathogen-free conditions in the animal facility of South China University of Technology, with a 12-h light/dark cycle. The mice were randomly assigned to the following four groups: the sham-operated control group, control plus Ang II group, p300-KO group, and p300-KO plus Ang II group. Ang II (1000 ng·kg−1·min−1 in saline) was subcutaneously infused into the mice via osmotic mini-pumps (Alzet 2006, Alzet Osmotic Pumps, Cupertino, CA, USA) continually for 6 weeks. Mice were sacrificed by inhalation of 2–3% isoflurane and cardiac exsanguination. Blood samples were collected for subsequent analyses.

## Electrophysiological analysis
Mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (30−40 mg/kg) injected intraperitoneally. The mice were monitored and kept at body temperature (37−38°C) with a heating pad (RWD Life Science, San Diego, CA, USA). The fur around the neck region was removed using depilatory cream. Limbs were taped down to ensure that the mice remained in the supine position during procedures. ECGs were recorded and monitored continuously with the iWorx Data acquisition and analysis system (iWorx Systems, Dover, NH, USA).
After isolation of the right jugular vein, a 1.9 F octapolar catheter (iWorx Systems) was introduced into the right atrium for subsequent electrophysiological analysis. The catheter was placed at the site where the amplitudes of the intra-atrial electrograms were higher than those of the intra-ventricular electrograms, and constant atrial captures were attained.
AF was induced with 6 s of superfast atrial burst pacing at a cycle length of 20 ms with a 2 ms pulse width. Stimulation was carried out repeatedly 10 times. AF was defined as rapid and fragmented atrial electrograms (fibrillatory baseline in the ECG) with irregular R-R intervals lasting at least 1 s on the ECG.

## Cell culture and treatment
The HL-1 cell line was a present from Dr. William Claycomb (Louisiana State University Health Science Center, USA). HL-1 cells were cultured in Claycomb medium (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Sigma-Aldrich), 2 mmol/L l-glutamine (Gibco, Gaithersburg, MD, USA), 100 μmol/L noradrenaline (Sigma-Aldrich), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (Gibco) in flasks that had been pre-coated with fibronectin and gelatin (Sigma-Aldrich) for 4−6 h. Cells were incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere (5% CO2, 95% air). The culture medium was changed the following day and every 2 days thereafter.
Primary neonatal mouse atrial cardiomyocytes were isolated from neonatal C57BL/6J mice. A thoracotomy was performed on 1 to 3-day-old neonatal C57BL/6J mice under aseptic conditions to obtain the hearts. Following digestion with collagenase (0.04%) and trypsin (0.08%), 10% serum-containing medium was added to form a cell suspension. According to the different adhesion times of fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes, differential adhesion was performed for 1.5 h to remove fibroblasts and finally obtain cardiomyocytes for subsequent treatment.
To mimic a pathological microenvironment, HL-1 cells were treated with Ang II (0.1 μmol/L, 1.0 μmol/L, and 10.0 μmol/L) for 24 h after the cell density reached 50%. HL-1 cells were pretreated with curcumin (5 μmol/L, 10 μmol/L, and 15 μmol/L), valsartan (10 μmol/L and 40 μmol/L), or MCC950 (0.1 μmol/L, 1.0 μmol/L, and 10.0 μmol/L) for 6 h before Ang II (10 μmol/L) stimulation for 24 h. Cells were collected for western blotting, immunofluorescence staining, and whole-cell patch-clamp recording.

## Immunofluorescence staining
Immunofluorescence staining was carried out as previously described.[ ] Primary antibodies against p300 (1:100; Cell Signaling Technology (CST), Danvers, MA, USA, #70088) and Kv1.5 (1:200; Alomone Labs, Jerusalem, Israel, #APC-004) were used, along with the corresponding secondary antibodies obtained from CST (1:500; #4412). Stained cells were observed using a laser scanning confocal microscope (SP5-FCS, Leica, Germany).

## Small interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection and adenoviral infection
In the RNA interfere (RNAi) assay, HL-1 cells and primary mouse atrial cardiomyocytes were transfected with scramble RNA and siRNA targeting mouse p300 (5′-CCTGTGCATCACTTGTTAT-3′, 5′-GGTGCTTAGTATCCTTCAT-3′, 5′-GACAAGTCTTGGCATAGTA-3′) using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Santa Clara, CA, USA) at a final concentration of 50 nmol/L for 24 h. Then, cells were treated with Ang II for 24 h. Western blot and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analyses were used to confirm the efficiency of silencing. Mouse p300-siRNA and control siRNAs were purchased from RiboBio, Guangzhou, China.
To overexpress p300, recombinant adenoviruses were constructed by Genechem Company (Shanghai, China). The adenoviral vector carrying the mouse p300 gene (Ad-p300) was delivered to HL-1 cells as described by the manufacturer’s protocol. HL-1 cells were infected with adenoviruses in serum-free Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium for 24 h. Western blot analysis was used to verify the overexpression of p300.

## qRT-PCR
Total RNA was isolated from HL-1 cardiomyocytes using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1000 ng RNA using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Accurate Biotechnology, Guangzhou, China). qRT-PCR was performed on a QTOWER3G system (Analytik Jena AG, Jena, Germany) using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Accurate Biotechnology). qRT-PCR reactions contained 1 μL cDNA in a total volume of 10 μL. Gene expression was normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH). The primers used are listed in Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C336.

## Whole-cell patch-clamp recording
HL-1 cells were digested and resuspended in Claycomb medium, then transferred to small dishes. A whole-cell patch-clamp was used to measure the IKur and membrane capacitance (Cm) once the cells adhered to the bottom of the dishes. The AP was recorded using the current-clamp mode. The internal and external solutions were prepared, as described in a previous study.[ ]
Patch pipettes were pulled from borosilicate glass capillaries and filled with internal solution, with tip resistances within 2−5 MΩ. Tip potentials were compensated when pipettes were immersed into the external solution. After gigaseal formation, a slight negative pressure was used to establish the whole-cell configuration. Current signals were recorded and amplified using a MultiClamp 700B amplifier and Digidata 1440A system (Axon Instruments, Molecular Dynamics, San Jose, CA, USA). Series resistance and capacitance compensation were both accomplished before recording. Leak currents were subtracted after data acquisition. The current density was obtained by dividing the IKur amplitude by the Cm. The action potential duration at 50% (APD50), 70% (APD70), and 90% (APD90) repolarization were measured. Data acquisition and analysis were performed using pCLAMP 10.5 software (Axon Instruments, Union City, NJ, USA).

## Western blot analysis
Western blot analysis was used to detect protein expression levels in human LAA tissue, mouse LAA tissue, and HL-1 cells, as previously described.[ ] The following primary antibodies were used: NLRP3 (1:1000; CST#15101), Kv1.5 (1:1000; Alomone Labs; Alomone#APC-004), p300 (1:1000; CST#70088), interleukin 18 (IL-18) (1:1000; Signalway Antibody, Greenbelt, MD, USA; SAB#32166), IL-1β (1:1000; SAB#54413), p65 (1:1000; CST#6956S), acetyl-p65 (1:1000; CST#3045S), and GAPDH (1:5000; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA).

## ELISA
Serum was isolated from whole blood samples collected from mice and humans for quantifing the concentrations of cytokines, specifically IL-1β, IL-18, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α). The assays were conducted strictly following the manufacturer’s instructions. The ELISA kits employed in this study were obtained from Huamei Biological Engineering Co. Ltd (Wuhan, China).

## Masson staining
Left atrial tissues were excised from C57BL/6 mice and subsequently fixed in formalin before being embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded atrial specimens were sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm. These sections were then subjected to deparaffinization, rehydration, and washing procedures. Following these preparatory steps, the sections were stained with Masson’s trichrome. This staining protocol was employed to enable histological assessment under microscopic scrutiny.

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
Co-IP and immunoprecipitation (IP) were performed, as described previously.[ ] The precleared lysates of HL-1 cells were incubated with equal amounts of p300 and immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies overnight at 4°C. The samples were then immunoprecipitated with protein A- and protein G-agarose beads at 4°C for 1 h with shaking. The immunocomplexes were collected and subjected to western blot analysis or IP.

## Statistical methods
Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) or standard deviation (SD) when appropriate. Differences between groups were analyzed using a two-tailed Student’s t-test, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), Fisher’s exact test, or chi-squared test, where appropriate. The differences between multiple groups in mice were analyzed using ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s post hoc tests to determine specific group differences. A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Graphics were generated with GraphPad Prism 6.01 (GraphPad Prism Software, California, San Diego, USA).

## Results

## p300, NLRP3, and inflammatory factor levels in patients with AF or SR
To determine whether p300 and NLRP3 were involved in the development of AF, we examined the expression levels of p300 and NLRP3 in the LAA tissue of the AF group and SR group. As shown in Figure A, p300 and NLRP3 protein expression levels were significantly higher in the LAA tissue of the AF group compared to the SR group. Furthermore, ELISA revealed that IL-18, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 levels were significantly higher in the serum of AF group than in the SR group [Figure B].
 

## Effects of valsartan on p300, Kv1.5, and NLRP3 protein expression levels in Ang II-treated HL-1 cells
To examine the relationship between p300 and NLRP3 in hypertension, HL-1 cells were treated with different concentrations of Ang II to induce hypertension. We found that p300, NLRP3, Kv1.5, IL-1β, and IL-18 protein expression levels increased under Ang II conditions compared to control cells, and all reached a statistically significant difference at 10.0 μmol/L [Figure A]. Treatment with valsartan, an antagonist of the angiotensin II type-1 receptor (AT1R), alleviated the Ang II-induced increase in p300, NLRP3, Kv1.5 [Figure B].
 

## Effects of curcumin on NLRP3 inflammasome activation and the electrophysiological properties of Ikur channels in Ang II-treated HL-1 cells
To determine whether p300 regulates NLRP3 inflammasome activation during Ang II stimulation, HL-1 cells were treated with varying concentrations of the p300 inhibitor, curcumin. Western blot analysis revealed that curcumin significantly reversed the effects of Ang II on the expression levels of p300, NLRP3, Kv1.5, IL-1β, and IL-18 [Figure A].
 Consistent with the expression of Kv1.5 protein, treatment with 10 μmol/L Ang II increased the peak amplitude of IKur compared to the control (10.04 ± 0.47 pA/pF [the ratio of peak current amplitude to the cell membrane capacitance] vs. 4.73 ± 0.57 pA/pF, P <0.01, Figure B). The Ang II-induced upregulation in the IKur peak amplitude was alleviated by treatment with 15 μmol/L curcumin (10.04 ± 0.47 pA/pF vs. 5.90 ± 0.35 pA/pF, P <0.01). The APD50, APD70, and APD90 were significantly shorter after 10 μmol/L Ang II stimulation compared to the control (12.14 ± 1.31 ms vs. 20.22 ± 2.94 ms, P <0.05; 22.56 ± 2.14 ms vs. 42.64 ± 3.98 ms, P <0.01; 36.89 ± 2.97 ms vs. 67.69 ± 4.71 ms, P <0.01 for APD50, APD70, and APD90, respectively, Figure C). Furthermore, the Ang II-stimulated APD levels fully recovered following the addition of 15 μmol/L curcumin (12.14 ± 1.31 ms vs. 21.67 ± 2.66 ms, P <0.01; 22.56 ± 2.14 ms vs. 41.33 ± 3.85 ms, P <0.01; 36.89 ± 2.97 ms vs. 69.4 ± 5.68 ms, P <0.01 for APD50, APD70, and APD90, respectively, Figure C). These results indicate that Ang II-induced changes in NLRP3 activation and the electrophysiological properties of IKur are likely mediated by p300.

## Silencing p300 abrogates Ang II-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation/expression and the electrophysiological properties of Ikur
Because our findings suggested that Ang II activated the NLRP3 inflammasome and increased the IKur peak amplitude, while the p300 inhibitor curcumin reversed these effects, we next sought to determine the relationship between p300 and the NLRP3 inflammasome by transfecting Ang II-stimulated HL-1 cells with p300-siRNA. We first performed qRT-PCR and western blot to confirm the efficiency of siRNA. The p300-siRNA was found to decrease the gene expression levels of p300 compared to the negative control (NC) siRNA group (P <0.05, Supplementary Figure 1A, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C336). The knockdown of p300 by siRNA led to a significant decrease in p300 protein expression levels (P <0.05, [Figure A]). In addition, significant decreases in NLRP3, Kv1.5, IL-1β, and IL-18 expression levels were observed in p300-siRNA-treated cells compared to the NC siRNA group [Figure A, B]. Immunofluorescence staining [Figure C] revealed that Kv1.5 fluorescence intensity (on the cellular membrane) was higher in the Ang II group than in the control group, while p300 siRNA abrogated this effect. Consistent with the Kv1.5 protein expression data, a significant reduction in IKur density was also observed (10.56 ± 0.63 pA/pF vs. 5.40 ± 0.43 pA/pF, P <0.01, Figure D). Furthermore, prolonged APD50, APD70, and APD90 were observed in the p300-siRNA group compared to the NC siRNA group (20.35 ± 1.35 ms vs. 11.71 ± 0.80 ms, P <0.01; 38.61 ± 2.32 ms vs. 23.65 ± 1.17 ms, P <0.01; 57.17 ± 2.20 ms vs. 40.18 ± 2.07 ms, P <0.01 for APD50, APD70, and APD90, respectively, Figure E).
 In addition, to further validate our conclusions, primary mouse atrial cardiomyocytes were treated during Ang II stimulation with or without p300 siRNA. Compared to the control group, the expression levels of p300, NLRP3, Kv1.5, IL-1β, and IL-18 were increased in primary mouse atrial cardiomyocytes during Ang II stimulation (0.70 ± 0.09 vs. 1.19 ± 0.17, P <0.05; 0.78 ± 0.07 vs. 1.33 ± 0.15, P <0.05; 0.80 ± 0.11 vs. 1.32 ± 0.15, P <0.05; 0.32 ± 0.09 vs. 1.08 ± 0.13, P <0.01; 0.85 ± 0.01 vs. 1.22 ± 0.08, P = 0.1, respectively; Figure F, G), while this trend was alleviated by p300 siRNA treatment.
Thus, our findings further suggest that Ang II-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation and Kv1.5 upregulation in HL-1 cells and primary neonatal mouse atrial cardiomyocytes are mediated by p300.

## Overexpression of p300 upregulates Kv1.5 expression and increases Ikur density
We next examined the effects of p300 overexpression by transducing HL-1 cells with a p300-overexpressing adenovirus. Western blot analysis confirmed that p300 was successfully overexpressed (0.32 ± 0.11 vs. 0.84 ± 0.03, P <0.05, Figure A). In addition, we found that overexpression of p300 led to a significant increase in the expression levels of NLRP3, Kv1.5 (0.41 ± 0.09 vs. 1.94 ± 0.37, P <0.01, Figure A), IL-1β, and IL-18. The IKur density was also significantly higher in the Ad-p300 group than in the NC group (10.21 ± 0.49 pA/pF vs. 5.24 ± 0.30 pA/pF, P <0.01, Figure B). Finally, shorter APDs (APD50, APD70, and APD90) were observed in the Ad-p300 group compared to the NC group (11.41 ± 1.19 ms vs. 25.12 ± 2.43 ms , P <0.01; 22.54 ± 1.35 ms vs. 43.94 ± 2.79 ms, P <0.01; 40.18 ± 1.04 ms vs. 61.80 ± 2.73 ms , P <0.01 for APD50, APD70, and APD90, respectively, Figure C).
 

## Effects of the NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950 on Ang II-treated HL-1 cells
Next, we sought to determine whether p300 induced changes in Kv1.5 protein expression and IKur through activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome by treating Ang II-stimulated HL-1 cells with varying concentrations of MCC950. Western blot analysis indicated that MCC950 had no significant effect on p300 and NLRP3 expression levels, but it was found to abrogate the Ang II-induced upregulation of Kv1.5 [Figure A, B]. In addition, Ang II-induced APD shortening and increased IKur were ameliorated by inhibiting NLRP3 with MCC950 [Figure C, D]. These results suggest that Ang II-induced electrical remodeling could be alleviated by inhibiting the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome.
 

## p300 promotes NLRP3 transcription by binding to NF-κB
Although our results have shown that Ang II can promote the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome through p300, the specific mechanism of p300-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation remains unclear. NF-κB is an activator of the NLRP3 inflammasome and is critical for NLRP3 expression. p300 is a co-activator located in the nucleus that can interact with transcription factors to regulate the expression of downstream genes. We hypothesized that p300 could promote the transcription of NLRP3 by binding to NF-κB in the nucleus following Ang II stimulation. We used Co-IP assays to test our hypothesis.
Previous study have demonstrated that p300 induces acetylation of NF-κB p65, which is required for its full transcriptional activity.[ ] Our results showed that Ang II promoted p65 acetylation (Ac-p65) but had no significant effect on the protein expression of p65 [Figure E, F]. Cell lysates (Input) were used for IP of p300, and co-precipitated NF-κB p65 was detected by western blotting. Furthermore, we found that p300 co-immunoprecipitated with NF-κB p65 in HL-1 cells [Figure G]. Moreover, increased binding between NF-κB p65 and p300 was observed in Ang II-treated HL-1 cells [Figure G]. Immunofluorescence staining revealed that the p300 fluorescence intensity in the nuclei was higher in Ang II-treated cells than in control cells [Figure H]. Increased interactions between NF-κB and p300 were observed in the Ang II group, while p300 siRNA abrogated this effect. Finally, our findings demonstrated that Ang II treatment of HL-1 cells resulted in increased NLRP3 mRNA expression levels. Notably, this effect was abrogated by the additional application of p300 siRNA [Figure I]. Together, our findings suggest that Ang II leads to increased p300 expression in the nucleus and promotes NLRP3 transcription by acetylating NF-κB p65.

## Ang II-induced AF susceptibility and inflammation are attenuated in p300-deficient mice
Finally, to confirm our in vitro data, we established a p300-KO mouse model and infused wild-type (WT) with saline or Ang II using osmotic mini-pumps. The general characteristics and electrophysiological data are shown in Supplementary Table 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C336. We found that Ang II-infused mice had significantly higher systolic, diastolic, and mean blood pressures than control mice. Additionally, Ang II-treated mice exhibited significantly prolonged P-wave durations (PWDs). Rapid atrial pacing revealed that the incidence of AF was significantly higher in Ang II-treated WT mice compared to control WT mice (79% vs. 23%, respectively, Figure A). Masson staining showed increased deposition of collagen fibers in Ang II-treated control mice compared to WT mice. p300-KO attenuated interstitial collagen accumulation in the myocardial interstitium; however, the specific mechanism remains unknown and requires further study. Worse cardiac function was observed in Ang II-treated control mice compared to p300-KO mice [Figure B]. The echocardiographic data are summarized in Supplementary Table 4, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C336. Western blot analysis demonstrated significantly higher p300, NLRP3, and Kv1.5 protein expression levels in the LAA tissue of Ang II-treated mice compared to WT mice [Figure C]. Moreover, significantly higher levels of the inflammatory factors IL-18, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α were observed in the serum of Ang II-treated mice [Figure D]. The Ang II-related incidence of AF was evidently attenuated in Ang II-treated p300-KO mice compared to Ang II-treated WT mice (29% vs. 79%, respectively, Figure A). Western blot analysis revealed that p300-KO significantly reversed the upregulated protein expression of NLRP3 and Kv1.5 in mouse LAA tissue induced by Ang II [Figure C]. Similarly, IL-18, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α levels were also decreased in the serum of p300-KO mice [Figure D]. Together, these results indicate that p300-KO reversed the Ang II-induced increase in Kv1.5 expression in atrial cardiomyocytes and inflammatory factors in the serum, thereby improving the electrical remodeling of the atrium. Furthermore, p300-KO effectively alleviated the inflammatory response caused by Ang II through inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome, ultimately reducing susceptibility to AF.
 

## Discussion
The findings of our study confirmed that Ang II activated the NLRP3 inflammasome in atrial cardiomyocytes through p300, thereby promoting Kv1.5 protein expression, increasing IKur, and shortening APD. These alterations ultimately result in cellular electrical remodeling, thereby increasing the susceptibility to AF [Figure ]. p300 promoted the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome by interacting with the NF-κB subunit p65 in the nucleus of Ang II-treated HL-1 cells. Electrical remodeling of atrial cardiomyocytes induced by Ang II was effectively reversed by interfering with the interaction between p300 and NLRP3. Furthermore, in vivo, p300 gene knockout in mice effectively ameliorates inflammation induced by Ang II and reduces the susceptibility to AF.
 Multiple risk factors for AF have been previously reported, including hypertension and inflammation.[ , ] The NLRP3 inflammasome is an upstream factor of the inflammatory cascade and is involved in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases, including hypertension and preeclampsia.[ , ] In this study, we constructed a mouse model of hypertension by continuously infusing mice with Ang II via the osmotic pump. Rapid atrial pacing demonstrated that AF was induced in a significantly higher number of Ang II-induced hypertensive mice compared to WT mice (79% vs. 23%). Our ELISA data revealed that mice with increased susceptibility to AF had higher levels of inflammatory factors such as IL-18, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α.
The NLRP3 inflammasome has been an area of intense research in the cardiovascular field in recent years. Recent studies have found that both cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts express the NLRP3 inflammasome.[ , ] Production of IL-1β and IL-18 following NLRP3 inflammasome activation has been shown to be positively correlated with the prognosis of AF.[ ] In this study, LAA tissue from AF and SR groups was collected and subjected to western blot analysis. We found that NLRP3 expression in the LAA tissue of AF group was significantly higher than that of SR group. Additionally, we demonstrated that NLRP3 protein expression levels were significantly higher in the LAA tissue of Ang II-induced hypertensive mice compared to WT mice. These findings suggest that the NLRP3 inflammasome may be involved in the occurrence and development of AF. Therefore, the NLRP3 inflammasome could be a potential target for AF treatment. Thus, we next sought to elucidate the mechanisms underlying NLRP3 activation and NLRP3-induced AF.
The transcriptional coactivator p300 is an epigenetic regulatory enzyme that possesses acetyltransferase activity. Recently, the p300 inhibitor C646 was shown to alleviate dextran sodium sulfate-induced colitis by inhibiting the activation of NLRP3.[ ] Previous studies have also demonstrated that curcumin, another inhibitor of p300, alleviated a variety of inflammatory diseases, including lupus, colitis, and septic shock, by inhibiting the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome.[ , ] These findings suggest that p300 may be involved in regulating NLRP3 activation. In this study, we found that both curcumin and p300-siRNA reversed Ang II-induced upregulation of NLRP3, IL-1β, and IL-18 expression in vitro. Moreover, overexpression of p300 using adenoviral vectors was found to increase NLRP3, IL-1β, and IL-18 expression levels. Subsequently, we constructed p300-KO mice to further verify our in vitro results in vivo. We found that p300-KO ameliorated the inflammatory response in hypertensive mice. Thus, taken together, our findings suggest that p300 robustly promotes the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in atrial cardiomyocytes in response to Ang II treatment.
The mechanisms by which NLRP3 increased susceptibility to AF were also examined in the current study. Previous studies have shown that NLRP3 affects the membrane ion channel activities of cardiomyocytes. Cardiomyocyte-specific activation of NLRP3 was found to promote abnormal release of Ca2+ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in the NLRP3 cardiomyocyte-specific knock-in mouse model.[ ] A recent study reported that lipopolysaccharide and glucose-induced activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome promoted age-related AF.[ ] The protein Kv1.5, which mediates the cardiac IKur, is regarded as a safe and efficacious therapeutic target for AF due to its atrial-specific expression.[ ] Through either overexpression (Ad-p300) or knockdown (curcumin and p300-specific siRNA) of p300 in HL-1 cells, we demonstrated that both Kv1.5 expression levels and the density of IKur were parallel to those of p300 in vitro. Our data indicated that NLRP3 acts downstream of p300, as NLRP3 expression was inhibited by curcumin and p300-siRNA in response to Ang II and increased following p300 overexpression with Ad-p300. In addition, Ang II-induced APD shortening and increased IKur were ameliorated by the NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950. Our study demonstrated that MCC950 does not affect NLRP3 expression levels, which is consistent with its known mechanism of action. MCC950 specifically inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome activation by blocking NLRP3-induced ASC oligomerization, without interfering with TLR signaling or the priming phase of NLRP3 activation.[ ] Recent research has suggested that NLRP3 may function as a transcription factor coactivator. Upon activation, NLRP3 has been shown to enhance the mRNA expression of several key ion channel subunits, including RyR2, Kv1.5, GIRK1, and GIRK4, which are implicated in the arrhythmogenesis of AF. Conversely, cardiomyocyte-specific knockdown of Nlrp3 reverses the overexpression of these subunits.[ ] These findings indicate that NLRP3 may play an alternative role in modulating gene transcription or RNA stability, beyond its established function in inflammasome formation. However, the precise mechanisms by which the inflammasome complex influences ion channel subunit expression remain unclear and warrant further investigation. Consistent with our in vitro findings, we observed increased Kv1.5 expression in Ang II-induced hypertensive mice, which was alleviated by p300 knockdown. Collectively, our results suggest that Ang II induces an increase in IKur in atrial myocytes via the p300/NLRP3 axis.
Given that these findings suggested Ang II induces electrical remodeling of atrial cardiomyocytes through the p300/NLRP3 axis, we next examined the specific mechanism by which p300 mediates NLRP3 activation. NF-κB is a recognized activator of the NLRP3 inflammasome and is critical for NLRP3 expression.[ , ] Macrophages must first be exposed to priming stimuli, such as ligands for toll-like receptors (TLRs), NLRs (e.g., NOD1 and NOD2), or cytokine receptors, which activate the transcription factor NF-κB and subsequently upregulate the expression of NLRP3.[ ] p300 is a transcriptional co-activator in the nucleus that interacts with transcription factors to regulate the expression of downstream genes. In this study, Co-IP assays confirmed that p300 co-immunoprecipitated with NF-κB p65 under physiological conditions, and this binding effect was amplified when HL-1 cells were stimulated by Ang II. Thus, our findings suggest that p300 may promote the transcription of NLRP3 by interacting with and acetylating NF-κB p65.
There were several limitations in this study. First, our investigation focused solely on one type of potassium channel (IKur), and the correlation between the p300/NLRP3 axis and other ion channels was not explored. Therefore, the observed shortening of APD might not be solely attributable to the upregulation of IKur. Second, while the use of a murine model with rapid pacing-induced AF provides valuable insights, it may not fully reflect the complex pathophysiology of AF in humans. Finally, the use of CAG-Cre mice resulted in global p300-KO rather than cardiac-specific deletion. Consequently, potential influences of p300 deficiency in other organs on cardiac function cannot be excluded.
In conclusion, this study demonstrated that p300 may promote the transcription and activation of NLRP3 by interacting with and acetylating NF-κB p65. Activation of NLRP3 induced electrical remodeling of atrial cardiomyocytes by increasing IKur. Thus, the p300/NLRP3 signaling axis may serve as a potential therapeutic target for AF.
