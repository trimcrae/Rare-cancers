## Abstract
In the domain of respiratory illnesses, asthma remains a critical obstacle. The heterogeneous nature of this chronic inflammatory disease poses challenges during its treatment. Glucocorticoid-based combination drug therapy now dominates clinical treatments for asthma; however, glucocorticoid resistance, numerous adverse effects, the incidence of inadequate drug delivery, and other factors need the development of more effective therapies. In recent years, there has been extensive research on nanotechnology in medicine. It has been shown in studies that these drug delivery systems can greatly enhance targeting and bioavailability and decrease the toxicity of medication. Nanoparticle drug delivery systems offer improved therapeutic efficacy compared to conventional administration techniques. Nanotechnology enables advancements in precision medicine, offering benefits for heterogeneous conditions such as asthma. This review will examine the critical factors of asthma to consider when formulating medications, as well as the role of nanomaterials and their mechanisms of action in pulmonary medicine for asthma treatment.
Keywords: Asthma, Nanotechnology, Nanomedicine, Drug delivery, Nanocarrier

## Introduction
Asthma, a chronic inflammatory condition, afflicts a substantial portion of the global population, causing a range of debilitating symptoms like persistent coughing, chest tightness, breathlessness, and wheezing. These manifestations, primarily driven by airway inflammation, lead to mucus production, bronchial remodeling, and bronchial hyperresponsiveness (BHR). Notably, bronchial hyperresponsiveness is a hallmark of asthma, characterized by the heightened reactivity of smooth muscle cells to nonspecific stimuli. Asthma is classified in several ways. Although asthma often has its onset during childhood, referred to as early-onset asthma, some individuals develop the condition later in life, known as late-onset asthma. These two forms of asthma exhibit substantial disparities in several aspects. Late-onset asthma, for instance, tends to be more severe and is less frequently associated with allergic triggers compared to early-onset asthma [ ].
The diverse clinical presentations of asthma extend beyond the age of onset and encompass a range of factors, including comorbidities, pulmonary function test abnormalities, and the reversibility of airflow obstruction. This heterogeneity underscores the complex nature of asthma, leading to variations in the rate of disease exacerbation, the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions, and the likelihood of achieving complete recovery [ ]. Consequently, patients with asthma often face considerable challenges throughout their treatment [ ]. Mitigating the risk of disease exacerbations, managing symptoms, and addressing the underlying inflammation have remained the central objectives of asthma therapy [ , ]. Presently, glucocorticoid-based combination therapies dominate the landscape of clinical asthma management. However, this approach is not without limitations, including glucocorticoid resistance, the occurrence of numerous adverse effects, suboptimal drug delivery, and other complicating factors that collectively necessitate the pursuit of innovative therapeutic avenues [ ].
In the realm of medicine, the emerging nanotechnology domain has garnered substantial attention. This interdisciplinary approach emphasizes the design and usage of nanoscale materials and devices for various medical purposes, including drug delivery. A body of research has shown that nanotechnology can offer significant enhancements in the targeting, toxicity, and bioavailability of pharmaceutical agents, thereby revolutionizing the therapeutic domain for respiratory disorders. Nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems hold particular promise in this context, as they have demonstrated the potential to greatly enhance the effectiveness of medications in comparison with conventional delivery techniques [ ]. Among the different kinds of nanoparticles, polymeric nanoparticles have acquired prominence as the preferred drug delivery platform due to their remarkable versatility, tunability, and a host of other advantageous attributes [ ].
This review paper will critically examine the compelling arguments in favor of nanoparticles as an innovative and cutting-edge method for managing asthma. This research aims to contribute to ongoing efforts to develop more effective and patient-centric strategies for managing this heterogeneous and often debilitating condition by exploring the potential of nanotechnology to address the complex challenges posed by asthma.

## Factors for asthma
In the past few years, research discoveries on several novel and known asthma triggers, including allergens, air impurities, interaction with microbes, paracetamol use, and obesity, have been updated (Fig.  ). The relative risk was higher in all age categories for outdoor air pollutants such as carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, ozone, and nitrogen dioxide, as well as particulate matter smaller than 10 mm in diameter. A majority of allergens and air pollutants activate protease-activated receptors, or toll-like receptors (TLRs), which in turn cause epithelial cells to produce cytokines and chemokines (PARs). The result is the stimulation of dendritic cells (DCs), which travel to the draining nodes and boost Th2 cell growth, as well as trigger the innate immune cells that are drawn to the air passages and proceed to create a variety of cytokines that aggravate airway inflammation [ ].
 Recent developments in the use of microbial products have been numerous. Interestingly, future outcomes involving asthma may be predicted by the nasopharyngeal microbiome. Using 16S mRNA profiling, a study investigated the autumnal nasal microbiota in kids with asthma. The risk of aggravation was elevated for nasal Moraxella species (Table  ). Streptococcus-dominated microbiotas were associated with higher chances of respiratory infection, whereas microbiotas dominated by Staphylococcus or Corynebacterium were correlated with fewer RV diseases and fewer aggravations. In comparison to a separate cohort of type 2 (T2)-low asthmatics characterized by raised levels of IL-6, IL-7, IL-8, and MIP-3a, the mucus microbiome exhibited a lower bacterial variety. In endobronchial samples from individuals with T2-high inflammation (eosinophil number greater than 300/mL) in contrast to T2-low inflammation, fungal diversity was reduced, and Trichoderma species were more abundant (Table  ). Those with atopic asthma compared to those without, and asthma patients compared to healthy controls, had higher concentrations of Penicillium species [ ].

 According to earlier investigations, there was less endobronchial microbial abundance in the Type 2-high group as compared to the Type 2-low category. According to the 'Childhood Origins of Asthma' study, having a Staphylococcus-rich microbiome during the initial 6 month period of birth increases the likelihood of developing chronic asthma by age three and recurrent wheezing. Each 10% increase in antibiotic prescriptions resulted in a 24% increase in the incidence of asthma. The gut microbiota at 1 year of age may link exposure to antibiotics and asthma at 5 years, as increased gut microbiota diversity was linked to a 32% lower chance of asthma at 5 years old [ ]. Also linked to asthma are soy isoflavones, polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), and acetaminophen. Boys born to mothers who have asthma are more likely to develop n-6/n-3 PUFA than girls. A unique connection among child sex, maternal asthma, and n-6/n-3 PUFA was also found by the authors, and it showed that sons born to asthmatic mothers who had a greater PUFA ratio had a greater chance of developing asthma. The understanding of the significance of obesity or being overweight for asthma risk has advanced. ## Asthma phenotypes and endotypes
Asthma exhibits heterogeneity, demonstrated by notable variations among patients in terms of when it begins, the concerned risk factors, and the extent of criticality, multimorbidities, and treatment responsiveness. Historically, there have only been two basic types of asthma recognized: allergic and nonallergic asthma, however, this has proven to be oversimplified. Allergy-based asthma typically develops in childhood and is linked to the responses of T helper 2 (Th2) cells. Type 2 cytokines, namely interleukins (IL-13, IL-5, IL-4, and IL-9), are generated by specific Th2 cells upon allergen recognition. These cytokines cause the accretion of several eosinophils in the bronchial passage, increased mucus synthesis, and the production of immunoglobulin E by allergen-specific B cells. IgE levels can be identified either in the blood serum or through a positive allergy reaction test [ ].
In contrast, non-allergic asthma emerges comparatively later in life, is more extensive among obese individuals and females, and can present considerable treatment challenges [ ]. Th2 and non-Th2 late-onset asthma were the two categories used to group late-onset asthma phenotypes. The non-Th2 variant is frequently linked to smoking, age, and obesity. The Th2-associated variant can be accompanied by elevated eosinophil counts in the airways, aspirin sensitivity, and frequent and persistent rhinosinusitis, along with the presence of nasal polyps (CRSwNP).
Clinicians now understand that dividing asthma into just two clinical types was oversimplifying the condition. In recent years, the categorization of asthma profiles has been modified to encompass asthma endotypes, including type 2-high or ultrahigh (predominantly caused by eosinophils) (Fig.  ) and type 2-low (non-eosinophilic, occasionally affected by neutrophils, and metabolism) (Fig.  ). Endotypes are characterized by fundamental pathophysiological systems, which may directly affect how each one responds to standard treatments. Th2-linked cytokines like IL-13, IL-5, and IL-4 control the T2-high endotype, with ultrahigh asthma suggesting a more intense version of the condition. It is more complicated, and there are currently no known biomarkers for the type 2-low endotype. Thus, all asthmatics who do not have type 2-high sensitivity are believed to have type 2-low asthma [ ].
 

## Current asthma treatments
The following two major lung barriers are typically overcome by therapeutic medications when treating lung disorders:
Mechanical barrier: lung disease (from an infection, for example) can produce bronchoconstriction or excessive mucus secretion, which narrows the airway. The lung mucosa cilia can also clear debris that has been lodged in the airway. These are a few of the lungs' mechanical barrier symptoms [ ].
Immunological and chemical barriers: Alveolar macrophages, proteolytic enzymes, and surfactants make up the majority of the immunological and chemical barriers found in the lungs. They all have an impact on how the lungs function, and overcoming their negative side effects helps enhance the effectiveness of medications used for lung therapy [ ]. The medication is deposited in the lung after it enters. Drug deposition is a highly intricate process that is affected by some variables, like the drug's particle size, location, and rate of deposition [ ].
Therapeutic drugs for lung ailments usually gain access to the body via intravenous injection, oral ingestion, or inhalation to deliver their therapeutic benefits.
Respiratory illnesses are treated with oral medication therapy. This approach can encourage patients to take medications as prescribed due to the large size of the intestinal epithelium's surface and ease of delivery [ , ]. The phase of breaking through the mucosal wall, which is still a difficult task when administered by inhalation, can be avoided with intravenous administration for the treatment of respiratory disorders [ , ].
It has been widely suggested to use inhalation treatment. This approach may be the most efficient way to treat infectious lung disorders since it can prevent an unfavorable impact on healthy cells, and achieve local administration. The biological barriers of the lungs can also be bypassed by medication delivery mechanisms (for example, nanoparticle transport mechanisms), which can also boost the bioavailability of anti-infective medications. Peptides, proteins, RNA, DNA, and other beneficial biological macromolecules can all be inhaled with the use of this delivery mechanism. Moreover, pulmonary distribution can greatly boost the bioavailability of therapeutic macromolecules in comparison to other drug delivery methods. Although excessive and frequent dosages of intravenous and oral antibiotics can be avoided with nebulized inhalation, drugs can rapidly attain an efficacious concentration at the affected site, thereby lowering the likelihood of systemic adverse effects [ , ]. Antihistamines and allergy medications are crucial in the management of asthma. By reducing histamine signaling, certain histamine receptor blockers reduce lung inflammation [ ].

## Drawbacks of current treatment methods
The enteral route of administration has some shortcomings for oral administration, though. For instance, the epithelium’s barrier effect can prevent hydrophilic medications from being absorbed, and it also makes it challenging for medication to properly act on the location. Also, the nonselective distribution of drug carriers and the breakdown of pharmaceuticals during gastrointestinal transport may have hazardous effects [ ].
The intravenous mode of delivery is intrusive and causes the patient inconvenience, increased medical expenses, and therapeutic side effects (like central venous catheter issues) when receiving extended care. The challenges presented by the body's partial clearance mechanisms (such as the mononuclear phagocytes found in the spleen and liver or the initial chemical alterations that occur in the liver) affect the elimination or metabolic activity of effective pharmaceutical substances and contribute to the indiscriminate distribution of drugs. These are additional difficulties that both the intravenous and enteral routes must overcome [ ]. While inhalation therapy has many benefits, it also has some drawbacks. For instance, the antibiotic will have a short half-life and a lower bacteriostatic concentration because alveolar macrophages will clear it quickly. Loss of active ingredients and/or particle deformation that may accompany inhalation-based administration could make it challenging to provide the formulation's active ingredient precisely [ ]. Importantly, some antihistamines have legal restrictions in several nations due to their potential for adverse effects, including sleepiness and new cardiac arrhythmias [ ]. Despite all these drawbacks, drug manufacturers are modifying traditional medications by employing various forms of nanoparticles [ ].

## Reasons to shift to nanotechnology
Nanotechnology is a growing discipline of research, centers on the study, manufacturing, and handling of materials on the nanometer scale (109 m). Nanotechnology, as a pivotal factor in the advancement of medicine, has spread across sectors such as early illness diagnosis, treatment, and prevention, as well as bioengineering research, showing promising prospects [ ].
Nanomaterials are nanometer-sized materials with new features such as quantum effects and a significant ratio of surface area to volume. The altered physical–chemical characteristics significantly impacted the nature of the interaction when these nanosized particles came into contact with biological systems. Utilizing pulmonary drug delivery represents an appealing strategy. Some research has indicated the benefits of approaches focused on administering medication to the lungs directly in the context of lung diseases. Additionally, these approaches have the potential to enhance the effectiveness of treatment [ ].
As polymeric, inorganic, and lipid-based nanoparticles are designed using more accurate methods, they can be optimized for more personalized medication administration, ushering in the era of precision medicine (Fig.  ). Size influences several biological processes, including immune detection and transit through biological barriers. In this respect, nanotechnology's diverse uses, such as the distribution of medication and nucleic acid-based treatments to specific disease locations, make it the most promising technology of the era [ ]. By employing methods such as cell-specific targeting and molecular transport to particular organelles, nanotechnology holds promise for mitigating the constraints linked to conventional drug delivery. These challenges encompass broad aspects, including biodistribution, as well as more intricate hurdles like intracellular trafficking [ ]. In addition to their obvious benefits in terms of pharmacokinetics, blood circulation time, and medication toxicities, nanomaterials also enable targeted drug delivery, slow drug release, and increase drug solubility. NPs can be employed to transport therapeutic molecules like medications and antibodies [ ]. Given the limited research on novel nanotherapies for asthma, particularly in addressing asthma attacks related to aberrant gene expressions, the effectual amalgamation of nano-gene carriers with specific genes presents a promising frontier in asthma treatment.
 

## Nanoparticles for asthma diagnosis
Many different fields are using nano-based biosensors for early illness diagnosis and detection. Based on nanosensors, an electronic nose was created. It can non-invasively find particular volatile organic chemicals in exhaled gas. The type of asthma can also be identified using electronic nose technology. There are four inflammatory phenotypes of asthma, and identifying one is crucial for developing a customized treatment plan. For high-precision, non-invasive tests to differentiate between individuals with various asthma phenotypes, including those characterized by eosinophils, neutrophils, and polynuclear granulocytes, electronic noses have been employed. The method's effectiveness was tested in a clinical experiment, and the findings demonstrated that the electronic nasal device's respiratory chart could discern between the various inflammatory asthma phenotypes [ ].

## Nanodrugs

## Steroid NPs
Nanoparticle carriers for steroid drugs in asthma treatment have been shown to significantly reduce toxicity. Moreover, studies have shown that NPs containing steroids are more effective at treating any airway wall inflammation in mouse models of asthma than free steroids. Their localized delivery minimizes the side effects associated with prolonged use of steroid drugs, like osteoporosis [ ]. PEGylated PAMAM [Poly(amidoamine)] dendrimer was used to carry beclomethasone dipropionate to the lungs, increasing drug accumulation there, enhancing bioavailability, and lowering the dosage and adverse effects. The research further demonstrated that dexamethasone may be successfully delivered to the lung using a nanocarrier, reducing allergic pulmonary inflammation, decreasing eosinophil formation, and decreasing inflammatory cytokine release [ , ].
Nanoparticle delivery also improves the solubility and stability of poorly water-soluble drugs like Budesonide. Combination drugs can enhance drug efficacy; polymeric nanoparticles have been utilized to co-deliver multiple drugs (eg, budesonide and theophylline), resulting in improved therapeutic outcomes in asthma models [ , ].

## Nucleic acid-based drugs
Certain patients face steroid-unresponsive asthma. The usual course of treatment doesn’t work effectively on them. Alternate drugs such as nucleic acid-based drugs become useful in such cases. Recently, several researchers created powders with nano-embedded microparticles (NEM). The findings demonstrate that the best inhalation therapy options presently available are the spray-dried NEM compositions with nucleic acids. According to a study, inhaling dry powder nanoparticles can enhance the medication's lung permeability and delivery effectiveness [ ].
Gene silencing is used to downregulate GATA3 genes in activated TH2 cells by siRNA-based polyplex (tf-MeI-PEI) to control asthma [ ].
Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) are single-stranded DNA or RNA molecules that regulate the activity of mRNA by binding to them. This binding can upregulate or downregulate their gene expression through various mechanisms. Certain modifications to these or attachments to the vectors help in the delivery of the nucleotides/ nucleic acids [ ]. Phase 2a clinical trials for SB010, a novel class of ASO therapeutics that targets and cleaves GATA3 mRNA specifically, have begun. It was shown that cells implicated in allergic inflammation overexpressed GATA3. The trial's findings demonstrated that inhaled SB010 considerably reduces both the early- and late-phase asthmatic reactions brought on by allergens [ ].

## Salbutamol NPs
Salbutamol is a bronchodilator frequently prescribed to asthmatics (Table  ). Studies have revealed that the conversion of salbutamol into NPs increased the overall amount of salbutamol deposited in the lungs by more than twice. Liposomal nanoparticles containing salbutamol can extend the duration of drug retention at the location of action, resulting in a more effective treatment outcome compared to unencapsulated medication, according to a study [ ].

 

## Others
According to studies, the main target of novel asthma medications can be T-helper type 2 (Th2) cytokines. In the group receiving the nano-drug, Th2 cytokine production was decreased, according to a histological study of lung slices [ ]. In comparison to glucocorticoids, andrographolide (AG) exhibits anti-asthmatic properties with lower toxicity and reduced occurrence of adverse impacts; however, it has a lower bioavailability and a shorter plasma half-life. Few scientists have used AG in NPs (AGNP) for the treatment of asthma to overcome these flaws. The bioavailability of the AG nano-formulation was dramatically increased, and the release of inflammatory factors was also noticeably reduced following oral or pulmonary treatment in a mouse asthma model. The study's findings also indicate that pulmonary administration has a more effective therapeutic outcome than oral administration [ ].
Broad research opportunities exist for the technique known as nano immunotherapy, which targets certain asthma-inducing antigens. For people with dust-mite allergic asthma, researchers have created a nano vaccine that has been shown to have good therapeutic results. The outcomes also demonstrate that the NP vaccine's particle size has a significant impact on avoiding allergies brought on by house dust mites [ ]. Gene therapy has recently taken a prominent part in the treatment of several ailments and has emerged as one of the hot issues in medicine. Asthma treatment using nanotechnology and gene therapy may be successful. According to studies, ovalbumin (OVA)-induced allergic asthma in mice can be greatly reduced by the administration of chitosan-IFN-pDNA NPs (CIN) [ ].

## Polymeric carriers
The most varied and often utilized type of biomaterials are polymers, which are made of monomeric units bonded together covalently. Given their superior pharmacokinetics, lengthy circulation durations, and desirable biocompatibility and biodegradability, they have garnered attention as drug delivery carriers [ ]. Polymers used to treat asthma include poly (D, L-lactide-co-glycolide) (PLGA), chitosan (CS), poly (N-(2-hydroxypropyl) methacrylamide) (PHPMA), and Poly(amidoamine) (PAMAM) [ ].

## CS (chitosan)
Chitosan (CS) is a naturally derived linear polysaccharide that boasts numerous amino groups. It is derived through the deacetylation of chitin, which is sourced from the exoskeleton of crustaceans and is a prevalent biopolymer in the natural world [ ]. CS is frequently used in the creation of innovative nanotherapeutic drugs for asthma because of its suitability with airway epithelial cells, excellent mucoadhesion, penetration-improvement capabilities, and antibacterial activities [ ]. Through positive and negative electrosorption, the positive charge of CS modifies characteristics and increases adherence to mucus. Chitosan loaded with proteins can penetrate the ciliary layer of the nasal mucosa when it opens the tight junctions between airway epithelial cells [ ].
They are preferred over others because they provide an array of features, including high permeability, biodegradability, good compatibility, good performance in allergen-specific immunotherapy, protection of nucleic acid components from nucleases, increased antibacterial actions, regulated medication release, improved cellular uptake, and improved nanoparticle stability [ ].
Inhalation of nanoparticles containing recombinant IL-17RC protein encapsulated in chitosan (CS-RC) led to a reduction in airway inflammation in asthma characterized by the Th2-low endotype, sometimes known as "severe asthma," predominantly through neutrophil infiltration and the IL-17 pathway [ ]. Chitosan-based hydrogels are much sought after for drug delivery applications [ ].

## PLGA (poly D, L-lactic-co-glycolic acid)
It is commonly used to encapsulate hydrophilic and hydrophobic pharmaceuticals in drug delivery systems [ ]. The primary constituents responsible for the breakdown of PLGA are glycolic and lactic acids, which undergo swift metabolism and elimination by the system. Consequently, they are widely favored in the creation of new nanoparticles for lung drug delivery [ ]. It aids in the constant delivery and dose reduction of hydrophobic hormone medicines (for example, budesonide). PLGA found extensive use not only in the delivery of hormones but for delivering herbal extracts as well.
It provides good biocompatibility, biodegradability, protein and nucleic acid permeability, and fewer adverse effects on other organs [ ].
Unmethylated CpG-oligonucleotides (CpG-ODNs) treatment successfully induces Th1 immunity and reduces existing Th2 responses, consequently modifying the immune responses to alleviate allergic symptoms associated with asthma [ ]. It demonstrates promising methods for overcoming mucous and alveolar macrophage absorption deep in the lungs.

## PAMAM (poly(amidoamine))
PAMAM dendrimers are nano-sized units with branch-like structures composed of macromolecules with precisely engineered arrangements, regulated through iterative synthesis. PAMAM dendrimers have NH2 terminal components, facilitating their customization to specific structures, and rendering them highly versatile dendrimers, especially in drug delivery and biological contexts. [ ]. To reduce potential toxicity, the positively charged groups on their surface are often modified into neutral or negatively charged components. The surface functionalities of amine-terminated PAMAM dendrimers can interact with nucleic acids, forming nano-sized complexes that can improve siRNA targeting to the bronchial epithelium cell cytoplasm [ ].
PAMAM dendrimers offer several key advantages in medical applications. They are safe for use on respiratory tissues, enhancing drug absorption while enabling the delivery of nucleic acids and proteins. Furthermore, they exhibit excellent water solubility, making them highly versatile in various therapeutic contexts [ ].

## PHPMA (poly(n-(2-hydroxypropyl) methacrylamide)
PHPMA is a polymer known for its non-immunogenic properties, structural stability, and high hydrophilicity, making it suitable for metabolic processes within the human body. Within these mechanisms, the PHPMA backbone undergoes modification with biodegradable oligopeptide side chains, with targeting antibodies and medications attached to the ends, distributed sporadically along the backbone. Such constructs are tailored as delivery systems for proteins or drugs, aiding in the precise targeting and distribution of therapeutic agents [ ].
The advantages of this approach are significant, including prolonged retention duration within the system, safer administration, enhanced treatment effect, and improved metabolism of substances, all contributing to its appeal in medical applications [ ].

## Lipid-based carriers
Adsorbing or encapsulating medicines in lipid membranes results in the formation of solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs). Composed of biodegradable and safe lipid constituents, such as biological or artificial solid lipids like lecithin and triacylglycerols. These nanoparticles are known for their versatility in transporting various therapeutic agents, encompassing small medication molecules, big macromolecules, vaccine antigens, and genetic material [ ]. Nanoparticles can also be readily aerosolized into droplets or enclosed within aerodynamically suitable particles, facilitating the proper deposition of an active chemical deep within the lungs [ ].
These nanoparticles offer a range of advantages, including robust physical stability, resilience against environmentally sensitive and unstable drugs, demonstrated in vivo efficacy with strong cytocompatibility, effective penetration of lipid bilayers, and outstanding physical stability, making them a valuable choice for various applications [ ].

## Extracellular vesicle-associated carriers
Extracellular vesicles, which are naturally produced nanomaterials released by cells, have been harnessed for the treatment of asthma due to their role in facilitating intercellular communication within the immune system. These vesicles, including microvesicles and nanoscale exosomes, have the potential to convey proteins and nucleic acids as part of asthma therapy. Moreover, certain extracellular vesicles exhibit the capability to distinguish between various allergens in patients and possess therapeutic properties for asthma. Exosomes, having properties like low immunogenicity and an excellent safety profile, can navigate through the pores of the mucus layer, enabling directly sending therapeutic agents to airway epithelial cells, such as adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ADSCs) with minimal immunogenicity [ ].
The quantity of oxygen in the blood is critical for the growth of mesenchymal stem cells and the production of extracellular vesicles. Hypoxia therapy shows promise in a variety of disease models [ ].
These nanoparticles come with significant advantages, including excellent cytocompatibility and a non-toxic nature, making them a safe and promising option for various biomedical applications [ ].

## Metal nanoparticles
Metal nanoparticles have garnered attention in biomedical research because of their advantageous properties, including their ability to reduce excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) in asthmatic airways [ ].
Silver nanoparticles: In the progression of asthma, silver nanoparticles led to a reduction in the excessive secretion of mucin 5AC (Muc5ac) and significantly suppressed the activity of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) and phosphorylated Akt strain levels within the air passages. Its method of action could be to reduce inflammation by blocking the PI3K signaling pathway [ ]. Silver nanoparticles contributed significantly to the development of airway scaffolds. Their administration has been seen to reduce antigen-induced airway inflammation and hyperresponsiveness and reduce IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, and NF-κB levels in lungs in Murine models [ ]. In addition to being employed as catalysts in chemistry, metal nanoparticles have revolutionized the medical industry. Due to their benefits, metal nanoparticles have been investigated in the field of biomedicine. For example, they have been shown to significantly inhibit phosphoinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) and phosphorylated Ak strain transforming (Akt) levels in the airways, as well as reduce excess oversecreted mucin 5AC (Muc5ac) during the development of asthma. Its mode of action may involve blocking the PI3K signaling pathway to have an anti-inflammatory effect. Furthermore, a different study showed that the NF-κB signaling pathway may be involved in the anti-inflammatory action of silver nanoparticles. In addition, silver nanoparticles have been shown to considerably reduce the amount of highly produced ROS in asthmatic mice's lungs and to lessen hyperresponsiveness and airway inflammation brought on by antigens [ ]. Furthermore, a different study showed that the NF-κB signaling pathway may be involved in the anti-inflammatory action of silver nanoparticles. In addition, silver nanoparticles have been shown to considerably reduce the amount of highly produced ROS in asthmatic mice's lungs and to lessen hyperresponsiveness and airway inflammation brought on by antigens [ ]. Silver nanoparticles' exceptional antioxidant capability holds enormous potential for asthma treatment. Furthermore, silver nanoparticles had a remarkable role in the development of airway scaffolds. The airway scaffolds that Silver built Cisplatin and antiproliferative nanoparticles enhanced the surface functionality of conventional scaffolds [ ]. They discovered that the particles could control several pathways, such as coagulation, chemokine-mediated inflammation, and T lymphocyte (T-cell) activation. An effective technique for screening silver nanoparticle adjuvants is bronchoalveolar lavage (BALF) proteomic characterization of differential proteins in healthy and asthmatic people [ ].
Gold nanoparticles: They are less toxic. Some research found that gold nanoparticles could increase leukocyte infiltration while decreasing mucus and cytokine production in mice. The mode of action could be connected to reducing oxidative stress in the lungs [ ]. Furthermore, gold nanoparticles have garnered interest recently because of their decreased toxicity as compared to silver nanoparticles [ , ]. Gold nanoparticles have the potential to modify pulmonary function in mice, enhance leukocyte infiltration, and lower cytokine production and mucus secretion levels. The mechanism of action may be associated with the reduction of pulmonary oxidative stress levels [ ]. While gold nanoparticles' anti-inflammatory properties might help treat asthma, it's crucial to think about the possibility of systemic absorption leading to adverse effects. Omlor et al. synthesized polyethylene glycol gold nanoparticles to enhance mucus penetration and intrapulmonary targeting while circumventing the unfavorable effects of systemic absorption [ ].
Superparamagnetic iron oxide (SPIO): Researchers are interested in ultra-small-sized SPIO nanoparticles because of their reduced inherent toxicity, rapid coupling to target entities, surface functionalization, and ease of detection [ ].
Wu et al. demonstrated that DiR-SPION conjugated to anti-suppression of tumorigenicity 2 (ST2) blocking antibodies (anti-ST2 NPs) significantly reduced airway inflammation by lowering the levels of IL-33 and IL-13 as well as the percentage of CD4 + T cells, and effectively suppressed the development of asthma by blocking the function of group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2s) [ ].
Hollow mesoporous silica nanoparticles (HMSNs): Because of their vast surface area, customizable pore size, and well-defined surface features, HMS nanoparticles with outstanding biocompatibility and minimal cytotoxicity are attractive inorganic nanocarriers [ ]. A vaccine for allergic asthma was developed using HMSNs by filling them with the major house dust mite (HDM) Der f2 (Der f2-HMSNs). They showed a controlled release and gave good results in the mice model [ ].
Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs): They feature a large surface area, a transparent structure, an adjustable pore structure, notable porosity, and a normal form, among other characteristics [ ].
Cerium oxide nanoparticles: A variety of nanoparticles, referred to as "nanoenzymes," have the potential to exhibit enzymatic functions. These nanoenzymes can help mitigate the overstimulation of signaling pathways such as NF-B, making them a promising candidate for anti-inflammatory delivery systems in prospective asthma treatments [ ].

## Targeted airway epithelial nanoparticles for asthma treatment
Extracellular vesicles, or EVs, are membrane-bound nanostructures released into the extracellular environment. Under both normal and pathological circumstances, they are widely discharged from cells and display a variety of sizes, contents, and surface marks. These EVs are abundant in human serum, yet it might be difficult to separate them from non-EV lipid particles and serum proteins. These vesicles influence several physiological and pathological processes, including those that occur in the tumor microenvironment (TME), by transporting different cellular constituents such as proteins, mRNAs, miRNAs, DNA, and lipids across distances. EVs are potential possibilities for therapeutic agents, medication delivery methods, and disease biomarkers due to their crucial functions in cellular communication [ ]. EVs and their synthetic analogs are examples of NPs that have been produced for medicine administration in respiratory illnesses. When it comes to asthma, where airway epithelium dysfunction—including dysregulated epithelial cell differentiation, impaired barrier function, and impaired immune response—may offer opportunities to repair or reverse these dysfunctions with therapeutic interventions. Reduced immunogenicity and innate intrinsic cell-targeting capabilities are two advantages that EVs offer for airway epithelial targeting. Muco-inert polymers can be applied to synthetic NPs to help them get past biological barriers like mucus and the immune cells' phagocytic reaction. To improve targeting specificity to epithelial cells, additional targeting ligands could be introduced [ ].
The airway epithelium, which is considered to be an essential regulator of the host response to environmental stimuli such as allergens, viruses, and pollutants that contribute to the pathophysiology of asthma, can be the focus of inhaled nanoparticle-based therapy. The production of diverse mediators by the airway epithelium is a crucial aspect of innate immunity and the inflammatory response. Asthma can lead to the deregulation of this response. Desmosomes, hemidesmosomes, and adherens junctions are a few examples of cell–cell and cell-extracellular matrix interactions that keep the airway epithelium structurally intact under normal circumstances [ ]. The morphological abnormalities of the airway epithelium in asthma are associated with hypertrophy airway smooth muscle (ASM), mucus production, and a weakened barrier. These abnormalities are underpinned by the dysregulated formation and function of epithelial cells [ ]. There is a lot of promise for nanotechnology to transform human disease detection and therapy. EVs are naturally occurring, nanoscale (40–1,000 nm) membrane particles that transport a variety of biomolecules, such as proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids, to facilitate cell-to-cell communication [ ]. Depending on their pathophysiological condition and cellular origin, EVs can have a very diverse composition [ ]. Their capacity for cargo transfer, elevated stability, extended half-life in circulation, and advantageous safety profile render them appealing options for the identification and management of multiple ailments, including respiratory disorders.
Moreover, EVs have improved biocompatibility and inherent cell-targeting properties due to their endogenous origin [ ]. According to Kamerkar et al. [ ] extended drug release is possible via EVs generated by mesenchymal cells that resemble primary fibroblasts and evade immunological clearance by monocytes and macrophages. Furthermore, a lot of studies indicate that simple EVs may be used therapeutically to treat lung conditions including asthma []. Despite the large body of research on NP systems for respiratory illnesses, relatively little of it has looked at the effectiveness of epithelial-targeted NPs in the treatment of asthma. In vitro and in vivo gene transfection of the airway epithelium was recently achieved by Tagalakis et al. [ ] using a targeted nanocomplex vector approach for intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1). These lipid-based NPs can selectively internalize into airway epithelial cells, which could lead to successful transfection and the restoration of gene expression. Nebulisation dispersion was also effectively achieved with these epithelial-targeted NPs. These results provide evidence in support of the potential application of NPs that target the airway epithelium in therapeutic asthma therapy.

## Challenges in the NP delivery system
Drug delivery and imaging applications of nano-delivery devices are common. Yet, they frequently struggle with the problem of research failure due to various factors. Mass production is challenging, repeatability is weak, and this novel medicine may cost more than more conventional ones.
Lung delivery methods also confront many difficulties. The efficacy of nanoparticles (NPs) in systemic drug delivery can be impacted by various elements, such as the size of the particles; NP clearance by mucosal cilia; the absorption of nanoparticles (NPs) by alveolar macrophages; NP targeting capability; adhesion interaction and steric hindrance between NPs and drug delivery pathways; and the breakdown of nano drugs by proteases generated in the body [ ].
In addition to size, nanoparticle shape, and aspect ratio are important factors that influence their cytotoxic effects in vivo. Because of the enhanced bioavailability and decreased clearance of the nanoparticles, it is thought that a larger aspect ratio of the particles corresponds with increased cytotoxicity. Such particles can cause cancer formation and, macrophage cell death during phagocytosis [ ]. One of the challenges in delivering drugs to the lungs via nanoparticles is understanding how the particles interact with biological systems and their eventual fate. Fast particle clearance limits the prolonged release of the drug, and particle translocation may lead nanoparticles to unintended regions of the body [ ].
Engineered Nanomaterials affect several cellular and molecular targets, such as serum IgE, AHR (airway hyper-responsiveness), BALF (broncho-alveolar lavage fluid) cell count (neutrophils, eosinophils, etc.), and pro-inflammatory cytokines. These interactions can exacerbate the inflammation [ ]. When nanoparticles (NPs) are deposited in the respiratory zone, their various physicochemical properties can lead to the adsorption of surfactant components, such as surfactant lipids and proteins, onto their surface. This interaction may then influence their clearance, translocation, and other related processes [ ].
It can be challenging to produce certain nanoparticles on a large scale for therapeutic applications due to their initial complex, expensive, and unreliable preparation procedure. Additionally, pulmonary medication delivery is significantly impacted by the composition, size, shape, and zeta potential of the nanoparticles. These ascertained if the asthmatic patients' thicker mucus layer could be penetrated by the nanoparticles. Additionally, as drug release from encapsulated nanoparticles might impact the stability of nanoparticles, more attention needs to be paid to drug release events such as burst release and leakage from nanoparticles. Finally, but just as importantly, there should be concern over the buildup of nanoparticles in the lungs. It is best to avoid using non-metabolizable nanoparticles in lung tissue while designing nanoparticles [ , ].

## Tolerogenic nanoparticles in the treatment of asthma
It is an alluring idea to cure autoimmune or allergic illnesses by inducing antigen-specific immunological tolerance. The allergen-specific immunological tolerance when promoted with a transdermal microneedle patch might prevent allergic asthma. To create sustained-release microneedles, the biodegradable tolerogenic nanoparticles (tNPs) were synthesized and loaded with the immunomodulator rapamycin and the model allergen ovalbumin (OVA). The tNPs were then filled into the tips of the microneedles by centrifugation. The cargos were effectively injected into the skin by the microneedles during intradermal immunization, and they were continuously released for more than ninety-six hours. Crucially, the microneedles stimulated regulatory T cells (Treg) specific to allergens, lowered pro-inflammatory cytokine and antibody levels while raising anti-inflammatory cytokines, and ultimately helped to restore immunological homeostasis. The sustained-release microneedles considerably reduced the infiltration of eosinophils, lowered the deposition of mucus and collagen, and significantly alleviated asthma symptoms, according to the lung tissue analysis. Our findings revealed a new therapeutic approach for the management of autoimmune and allergy diseases: the sustained-release microneedle-based transdermal delivery method can effectively and more consistently generate antigen-specific immunological tolerance [ ].

## Future aspects
Nanoparticles as gene vectors are an upcoming concept to be used in Asthma treatment. Asthma is recognized as an inflammatory pulmonary disorder involving various cells and mediators, which can serve as potential molecular targets for silencing or overexpression within the inflammatory cascade using gene vectors [ ]. We could work on modeling the nanoparticles to modify their surfaces and working mechanisms. First and foremost, to enable large-scale manufacture, low-toxicity carriers must be used for the synthesis of nanoparticles, and the production process must be made simpler. Drugs can be released under particular conditions by engineering nanoparticles with tailored response capabilities, such as pH sensitivity and inflammation sensitivity, to govern drug release. To target release in inflamed lungs, pH-sensitive nanoparticles, for example, can be created [ ]. To increase the retention duration in the lungs and allow them to pass through the mucus layer, nanoparticles can be made smaller, more electrically neutral, contain compounds that are specifically targeted, have a high encapsulation efficiency, and be less poisonous. PEG can be used to modify the surface of nanoparticles to facilitate their simple transit through mucous membranes [ ].
The focus of recent developments in the management of asthma has mostly been on innovative treatments such as biologics, biomarkers, and precision medicine. Because of the variety and complex pathophysiology of asthma, which calls for detailed knowledge of asthma biomarkers, phenotypes, endotypes, genotypes, and geographical patterns, precision medicine is especially well-suited to address these issues. The future of treating asthma appears to be quite promising for patients with targeted medications and personalized therapeutic approaches. By enabling regulated, targeted medicine release that is tailored to each patient's immunology and severity, nanotechnology can help with this [ ].
In the authors’ opinion, nanotechnology offers significant potential in the treatment and management of asthma. Bronchodilators and corticosteroids, the potential treatments of asthma have systemic side effects and may not achieve optimal targeting of the affected areas in the lungs whereas drug delivery, is enhanced with the applications of nanotechnology-based approaches along with the treatment efficiency is significantly improved and side effects are reduced. Since nanotechnology offers focused, effective, and less invasive solutions, it represents a frontier in the treatment of asthma. As further research is conducted, nanomedicine may be able to significantly improve asthma patients' quality of life by offering more individualized, less adverse effects, and more effective treatments. Nanoparticles containing drugs can be engineered to only release their contents in reaction to specific triggers, including inflammation or a shift in the pH levels in the lungs. Side effects from asthma treatments, particularly prolonged usage of steroids, might include immune system suppression, weight gain, and elevated blood pressure. By offering more localized treatment and lowering the requirement for large doses of systemic medications, nanotechnology can help lessen these negative effects. Nanocarriers limit the drug's ability to move to other parts of the body, hence reducing systemic adverse effects, by delivering the medication directly to the inflammatory areas of the lungs. Inflammation is a major factor in airway constriction in asthma. Treatment results can be enhanced by using nanoparticles to deliver anti-inflammatory medications straight to the inflammatory tissue. Additionally, several nanoparticles are designed to target inflammatory cells like eosinophils, which are increased during asthma episodes. By teaching the immune system to become less reactive to allergens, researchers are investigating the use of nano vaccines to prevent asthma. For those who have allergic asthma or are at risk of developing the condition, this method may be a prophylactic measure. Future studies demand the use of nanotechnology sensors in smart inhalers that can track drug distribution and give real-time information about how well a treatment is working. These gadgets can notify patients if they are missing dosages or if their medicine is not getting to the right place [ ].

## Conclusion
Asthma signifies a condition of the bronchial airways that results in chronic inflammation, affecting millions of people worldwide. The disease is heterogeneous and can be caused by different aspects such as microbial products, environmental triggers, or genetics. A stepwise approach to Asthma management involves treatment escalation and de-escalation based on symptoms, lung functions, and exacerbation risk. Pharmacological interventions like controlled medications reduce inflammation and prevent symptoms and quick-relief medications provide immediate relief during exacerbations. The application of nanotechnology in asthma treatment has the potential to improve drug delivery, targeting, and bioavailability for better therapeutic outcomes in asthmatic patients. Nanoparticles (NPs) have demonstrated significant promise in the treatment and detection of asthma. They could be used to transport drugs like salbutamol and beclomethasone dipropionate, leading to improved drug delivery efficiency, enhanced lung permeability, and reduced side effects. Nanotechnology shows us a future in precision medicine. Nanocarrier safety and efficacy have been supported by pre-clinical research. Yet, to conduct more reasonable trials, a deeper understanding of their mechanisms of action and the chemical structures of nanocarriers is essential. There are still challenges to be addressed in the delivery of NPs, such as particle size, clearance by mucosal cilia, and degradation by proteases produced in vivo. Despite these challenges, NPs offer a hopeful avenue for personalized asthma treatment.
