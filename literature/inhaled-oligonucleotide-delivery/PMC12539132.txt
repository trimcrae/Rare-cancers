## Abstract

## Objective
Sarcopenia is a progressive skeletal muscle disease that is prevalent among the elderly. We aim to explore the role of the C-type lectin domain containing 4, member D (CLEC4D) in sarcopenia.

## Methods
Hub genes were identified from protein-protein interaction networks based on differentially expressed genes from GSE145480 and GSE139204 datasets. Sarcopenia in mice was induced by Dexamethasone. Pathological estimation was conducted using hematoxylin-eosin and Masson staining. Myoblasts were treated with D-galactose (D-gal) to induce senescence, which was assessed by senescence-associated β-galactosidase staining. The viability and apoptosis were evaluated using the Cell Counting Kit-8 assay and flow cytometry. Protein and gene expression were examined by western blot and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays were performed to measure inflammatory factors.

## Results
We identified 8 hub genes associated with sarcopenia, among which CCL5, CLEC4D, LGALS3, ESR2, FCGR4, and CLEC4N were upregulated in sarcopenia. CLEC4D knockdown enhanced myogenic differentiation, suppressed muscular fibrosis and atrophy, and alleviated sarcopenia in mice via reducing inflammatory cytokine levels. CLEC4D knockdown attenuated D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction by enhancing differentiation and downregulating the expression of inflammatory cytokines and muscle atrophy markers. The Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathway was activated in D-gal-induced myoblasts. CLEC4D knockdown inhibited the JAK/STAT pathway in D-gal-induced myoblasts. Notably, an activator of the JAK/STAT pathway reversed the inhibitory effect of CLEC4D knockdown on D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction and inflammation.

## Conclusion
Targeting CLEC4D represents a promising therapeutic strategy for sarcopenia, as its knockdown suppresses disease progression by inhibiting the JAK/STAT signaling pathway.

## Introduction
Sarcopenia, primarily a geriatric condition marked by progressive skeletal muscle mass loss and functional decline, significantly elevates risks of disability, falls, injury from falls, hospitalizations, dependency, and mortality among the elderly [ , ]. Low levels of physical activity, which may be one of the key causes leading to a reduction in muscle mass, along with other factors such as insufficient caloric intake, progressive fibrosis, abnormal muscle metabolism, the presence of a chronic inflammatory state, oxidative stress responses, and degenerative changes at the neuromuscular junctions, all contribute to the onset of sarcopenia [ – ]. The management strategies for sarcopenia primarily focus on non-pharmacological methods such as resistance exercise and nutrition [ ]. Currently, there are no Food and Drug Administration-approved drugs for the treatment of sarcopenia [ ]. Therefore, identifying potential biomarkers for sarcopenia is crucial for developing novel therapeutic strategies.
C-type lectin domain containing 4, member D (CLEC4D), also referred to as CD303 or BDCA-2, is a member of the Dectin-2 subfamily within the C-type lectin receptor family [ ]. CLEC4D has been observed to be expressed in various myeloid cells, including neutrophils, monocytes, and dendritic cells [ – ]. Consequently, its role in defending against bacterial pathogens has been extensively investigated [ , ]. Existing research indicates that CLEC4D is involved in the progression of various cardiovascular diseases, including cardiac remodeling, acute myocardial infarction, aortic dissection, acute myocardial ischemia, and the advancement of several types of cancer [ – ]. At present, it is well-documented that sarcopenia correlates with an accelerated progression of cardiovascular diseases, elevated mortality rates, heightened fall risks, and diminished quality of life, particularly among the elderly population [ ]. However, the involvement of CLEC4D in the pathogenesis and progression of sarcopenia remains to be elucidated and warrants further investigation.
The Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathway is a ubiquitously expressed intracellular signaling cascade that plays a crucial role in numerous key biological processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and immune regulation [ ]. Also, the sustained activation of the JAK/STAT signaling pathway is closely associated with many immune and inflammatory diseases [ ]. With aging, the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines increases, and these inflammatory sources can influence the process of sarcopenia through direct or indirect pathways [ ]. Targeting inflammatory sources could influence muscle function, which could be considered a therapeutic target for sarcopenia [ ]. Current research indicates that the JAK/STAT pathway plays a significant role in skeletal muscle atrophy [ – ]. Hence, investigating the specific mechanisms of the JAK/STAT pathway in sarcopenia is crucial.
In this study, we identified CLEC4D as a significant regulatory factor in sarcopenia through comprehensive bioinformatics analysis. We investigated the effects of CLEC4D on sarcopenic mice and D-galactose (D-gal)-induced myoblasts. Additionally, we further explored the potential regulatory pathways of CLEC4D in sarcopenia and examined its specific mechanisms of action at the cellular level. We aimed to explore the potential benefits of CLEC4D on sarcopenia by observing the impact of CLEC4D knockdown on the JAK2/STAT pathway.

## Materials and methods

## Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) determination
In this study, we analyzed gene expression profiles downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database of the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) in the United States (https://www.ncbi.nih.gov/geo/). We retrieved datasets related to “sarcopenia” from the database and identified two high-throughput sequencing datasets that met our requirements: GSE145480 and GSE139204. To ensure consistency in differential gene screening criteria across the GSE139204 and GSE145480 datasets, a standardized protocol was applied using GEO2R (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r). Raw data from both datasets underwent identical preprocessing steps. DEGs were identified based on an adjusted p-value (adj.p) < 0.05 calculated via Benjamini-Hochberg correction and an absolute log fold change (|logFC|) ≥ 1. These criteria were uniformly enforced for “Sarcopenia vs. Control” comparisons in both datasets. The identified DEGs were visualized using heat maps and volcano plots. Additionally, Venn diagrams were used to display the common upregulated and downregulated DEGs between the two datasets after filtering. Genes associated with “sarcopenia” were also retrieved from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) using the keyword “sarcopenia”. Co-expressed genes for candidate genes were screened using the Coxpresdb database (https://coxpresdb.jp/), and the intersection of both datasets was selected for further analysis.

## Functional enrichment analysis
To elucidate the functional roles of the common DEGs identified across the datasets, we utilized the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis tools provided by the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) website (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/summary.jsp). The DEGs were uploaded to the DAVID platform, and a comprehensive GO/KEGG analysis was performed to categorize the genes based on biological processes (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular components (CC), as well as their involvement in specific KEGG pathways. The resulting GO-KEGG data were then exported for further visualization and interpretation. Using the “enrichment plot” package in R software, we generated dot plots and bar graphs to illustrate the functional enrichment analysis. Specifically, the most significantly enriched terms with the smallest p-values were selected for presentation, highlighting the most prominent GO terms and pathways associated with the common DEGs.

## Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks and hub genes
To investigate the potential interactions between proteins encoded by DEGs that may play crucial roles in the mechanism of sarcopenia, we utilized the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING) online database (https://www.string-db.org/). The DEGs were input into the STRING platform. We set a confidence interaction score of 0.400 as the significance criterion to ensure robust interaction predictions. Following the prediction of PPI networks, we exported the resultant data and visualized the PPI network using Cytoscape software (www.cytoscape.org/). Within Cytoscape, we employed the CytoHubba plugin to identify hub genes within the network. Using the degree algorithm provided by CytoHubba, we selected the top eight genes with the highest scores as hub genes, which were considered to be central nodes within the PPI network and potentially critical in the development of sarcopenia.

## Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve
To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of the selected genes, we performed ROC curve analysis using gene expression profile data through an interactive analysis platform provided by GEPIA (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/). The ROC curve was generated to illustrate the performance of the selected genes in distinguishing between sarcopenia and control samples. Additionally, the Area Under the Curve (AUC) was calculated to quantitatively assess the diagnostic accuracy of the selected genes.

## Animals and treatment
To investigate the role of CLEC4D knockdown in sarcopenia mice, male C57BL/6J mice (12 weeks old, weighing 25–27 g; SPF Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were divided into four groups: Control, SP (sarcopenia model), SP + lentiviral vector of short hairpin RNA (LV-sh) NC (sarcopenia model + lentiviral negative control), and SP + LV-shCLEC4D (sarcopenia model + CLEC4D knockdown lentiviral vector) groups (N = 6 per group). Following a 7-day environmental acclimatization period, mice in SP, SP + LV-shNC, and SP + LV-shCLEC4D groups received daily subcutaneous injections of dexamethasone (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight for 10 consecutive days to induce a skeletal muscle atrophy model, as adapted from established protocols [ ]. For the exploration of the effect of CLEC4D knockdown on sarcopenic mice, CLEC4D knockdown lentiviral vectors (GeneChem, Shanghai, China) and corresponding lentiviral negative controls (GeneChem) were constructed and injected into the bilateral gastrocnemius muscles of mice in the SP + LV-shCLEC4D and SP + LV-shNC groups, respectively. The hindlimbs of the mice were shaved before injection. The grip strength time of the mice was recorded using the grip strength test. Subsequently, all mice were anesthetized through inhalation of a 1-1.5% isoflurane-oxygen mixture for 4–5 min and then euthanized by cervical dislocation. The gastrocnemius muscle tissues were extracted for further analysis.
This study was conducted in strict compliance with international and national guidelines for animal welfare. The protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of our institution. All procedures involving animals were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) guidelines and the ethical standards set forth by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

## Grip strength assessment
To evaluate muscle endurance in sarcopenic mice, a modified grid suspension test was conducted. A custom-built suspension apparatus consisting of a horizontal metal grid (1 cm × 1 cm mesh) elevated 30 cm above a cushioned surface was used. Mice were placed on the grid, and latency to fall was recorded as the primary outcome. Each mouse underwent three trials with a 2-minute rest interval, and the longest duration was analyzed to minimize variability. Prior to testing, mice were habituated to the apparatus for 3 days (5 min/day).

## Histological staining
The tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution, then embedded in paraffin and cut into sections (4 μm). The sections were dehydrated through a series of immersions in xylene I and II, absolute ethanol I and II, and graded ethanol solutions (95%, 80%, and 70%).
For the Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) staining, the sections were stained with hematoxylin, differentiated in 1% hydrochloric acid ethanol, and processed in 50%, 70%, and 80% ethanol. The sections were then immersed in eosin solution for counterstaining and further processed with 95% ethanol, absolute ethanol I and II, as well as xylene I and II, before being sealed with neutral balsam and observed under a microscope to capture images.
For Masson staining, the paraffin sections were dewaxed to water, stained with hematoxylin, followed by Masson’s ponceau acid fuchsin solution for 5 min, and then stained with aniline blue solution for 5 min. Finally, after clearing in 95% ethanol, absolute ethanol, and xylene, the sections were mounted with neutral balsam and observed under a microscope.

## Cell culture and treatment
Mouse myoblasts, C2C12 cells (iCell Bioscience Inc., Shanghai, China), were cultured in a proliferation medium consisting of Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, under an atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 95% air. Cells were divided into the following groups: the Control group (cells without any treatment), D-gal group (cells treated with D-gal), D-gal + Lv-sh NC group (cells treated with D-gal and transfected with negative control lentiviral plasmid), D-gal + Lv-sh CLEC4D group (cells treated with D-gal and transfected with CLEC4D knockdown lentiviral plasmid), and D-gal + Lv-sh CLEC4D + RO8191 (cells treated with D-gal, transfected with CLEC4D knockdown lentiviral plasmid, and incubated with JAK/STAT pathway agonist) group. To establish the D-gal-induced C2C12 cell model, cells were incubated with D-gal (20 g/L) for 48 h. For the CLEC4D knockdown group, C2C12 cells were transfected with a lentiviral vector encoding CLEC4D-targeting short hairpin RNA (LV-shCLEC4D) or a corresponding negative control vector (LV-shNC) using Lipo3000™ kits (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). For the induction of differentiation, the culture medium was then switched to a differentiation medium composed of DMEM supplemented with 2% horse serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, which was refreshed at least every two days. Cells were induced to differentiate for 8 days before proceeding with further studies [ ]. Additionally, the JAK/STAT pathway was activated by treating cells with the JAK/STAT activator RO8191 (2 µM; MedChemExpress, New Jersey, USA) for 24 h.

## Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining
SA-β-gal activity was detected using a commercial senescence detection kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s specifications. Briefly, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, rinsed twice with phosphate-buffered saline, and incubated overnight at 37 °C with freshly prepared SA-β-gal staining solution. Following incubation, cells were washed three times with PBS. Senescent cells were identified by the presence of cytoplasmic blue precipitate under brightfield microscopy.

## Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
Cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. For the experiment, cells were trypsinized, counted using a hemocytometer, and seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 103 cells per well in 100 µL of complete growth medium to allow for 80% confluence overnight. After reaching approximately 80% confluence, the growth medium was replaced with a maintenance medium consisting of 2% fetal bovine serum and 98% DMEM. Following a 24-hour incubation, the cells were then gently washed twice with Dulbecco’s Phosphate-Buffered Saline (DPBS). Subsequently, 90 µL of fresh Maintenance medium was added to each well, followed by the addition of 10 µL of CCK-8 solution (final concentration: 10%). The plates were returned to the incubator for an additional 2 h. The absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## Apoptosis detection
To assess the extent of cell apoptosis, we utilized a fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) analysis with an Annexin V-FITC and Propidium Iodide (PI) dual staining kit (Biosharp, Hefei, China), adhering to the manufacturer’s instructions. The process involved collecting cells and suspending them in a binding buffer that included FITC-tagged Annexin V and PI. Following a 15-minute incubation period, additional binding buffer was added to dilute the cell suspension. The samples were then subjected to FACS analysis to ascertain the proportion of apoptotic cells by interpreting the fluorescence emissions from FITC and PI.

## Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
RNA was extracted from cells and tissues using the TRIZOL reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The concentration of RNA and optical density (OD) values at 260 nm and 280 nm were measured using an Ultraviolet and Visible Spectrophotometer. Samples with an OD260/OD280 ratio between 1.9 and 2.0 were considered to have high purity. Reverse transcription to generate cDNA was performed using the Hiscript II QRT Supermix for qPCR kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) under specific conditions: 5 min at 25 °C; 30 min at 42 °C; and 5 s at 85 °C. RT-qPCR was conducted on an ABI7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) with the following thermal profile: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 30 s. Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) served as the internal reference gene. The Ct values obtained were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Each experiment was repeated three times. The primer sequences are detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

## Enzyme-Linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and IL-8 in the collected serum and cell supernatant samples were determined using commercially available ELISA kits specific for each cytokine (Esebio, Shanghai, China). The assays were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 96-well microtiter plates pre-coated with monoclonal antibodies specific for TNF-α, IL-1β, or IL-8 were used. Standard curves were generated using serial dilutions of recombinant cytokines provided with the kits. For the assay, 100 µL of each standard or sample was added to the respective wells in duplicate. After incubation at room temperature for 2 h, the wells were washed three times with wash buffer to remove unbound substances. Then, 100 µL of biotinylated detection antibody was added to each well, followed by another incubation for 2 h at room temperature. After washing, 100 µL of streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase (HRP) conjugate was added to each well and incubated for 20 min at room temperature. The unbound enzyme was removed by washing, and 100 µL of substrate solution was added to each well to develop color. The reaction was stopped with the stop solution after 20 min, and the absorbance (OD values) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## Western blot
Total protein was extracted using a RIPA Lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis. After electrophoresis, the separated proteins were transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. The PVDF membrane was then blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk in Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 (TBST) for 2 h on a shaking platform. Following blocking, the PVDF membrane was washed with TBST and incubated overnight at 4 °C on a shaking platform with the following primary antibodies: Myogenic Differentiation Factor 1 (MyoD1; ab64159; Abcam, Cambridge, UK; 1:2000), Myogenin (ab1835; Abcam; 1:2000), Myosin Heavy Chain (MyHC; ab207926; Abcam; 1:2000), Muscle RING Finger Protein 1 (MuRF1; ab313852; Abcam; 1:1000), Myostatin-regulated F-box protein (MAFbx; ab168372; Abcam; 1:1000), CLEC4D (ab219800; Abcam; 1:1000), JAK2 (ab108596; Abcam; 1:2000), p-JAK2 (ab195055; Abcam; 1:2000), STAT3 (ab68153; Abcam; 1:2000), and p-STAT3 (ab183031; Abcam; 1:2000). After primary antibody incubation, the membrane was washed three times with TBST for 10 min each. It was then incubated with the HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody (ab288151; 1:5000; Abcam) or anti-mouse secondary antibody (ab205719; 1:5000; Abcam) for 1 h on a shaking platform. Following secondary antibody incubation, the membrane was washed three times with TBST for 10 min each. Chemiluminescent detection was performed, followed by exposure, development, and fixation. Data analysis was conducted to quantify the protein expression levels.

## Statistical analysis
All data were processed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 statistical software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA), and the data were represented as mean values ± standard deviation. Comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) or two-way ANOVA methods. Post-ANOVA pairwise comparisons were performed using Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. Comparisons between the two groups were made using t-tests, with a P value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

## Results

## DEGs identification
In this study, two gene expression profiling datasets, GSE139204 and GSE145480, were analyzed to investigate DEGs in skeletal muscle samples from mice of different ages. The dataset GSE139204 comprised six gastrocnemius muscle samples from 30-month-old mice (sarcopenia group) and six samples from 10-month-old mice (Control group). Using DESeq2 for differential analysis, a total of 191 DEGs were identified, including 142 upregulated and 49 downregulated genes. In the GSE145480 dataset, nine gastrocnemius muscle samples from 28-month-old mice (sarcopenia group) and eight samples from 8-month-old mice (Control group) were analyzed using the same criteria. This analysis yielded 1082 DEGs, with 610 being upregulated and 472 downregulated. A cluster analysis of the DEGs from both datasets was performed, resulting in a volcano plot, which illustrated the distribution of the DEGs based on their statistical significance and fold change (Fig.  A). Additionally, heatmaps were generated to visualize the expression patterns of the top 15 upregulated and downregulated DEGs in both GSE139204 and GSE145480 (Supplementary Tables 2–3; Fig.  B). A Venn diagram was used to highlight the overlap between the two datasets, revealing that there were 143 co-expressed DEGs in common between GSE145480 and GSE139204. Of these, a total of 40 genes were commonly downregulated, while 103 genes were commonly upregulated (Fig.  C).
 

## Functional enrichment analysis of common DEGs and hub gene screening
GO and KEGG pathway analyses were conducted on the 143 co-expressed DEGs. The GO enrichment analysis was categorized into three main classes: MF, BP, and CC. For each category, the top 10 most significantly enriched GO terms with the lowest p-values were selected. The results were presented in a bar graph, indicating that the DEGs were significantly enriched in BP terms such as neutrophil chemotaxis, neutrophil migration, and granulocyte chemotaxis; in CC terms like extracellular space and plasma membrane; and MF terms including gated channel activity, ion channel activity, and transmitter-gated ion channel activity (Fig.  A). The KEGG pathway analysis revealed the top 10 most significantly enriched pathways based on the smallest p-values. These pathways were illustrated in a bubble chart, highlighting key metabolic and signaling pathways affected by the aging process in skeletal muscle (Fig.  B).
 To further explore the PPI network among these DEGs, the STRING tool was employed to construct a PPI network (Figure ). Visualization of this network using Cytoscape software allowed for the identification of hub genes through degree topology analysis using the CytoHubba plugin. The eight highest-scoring genes identified included IL-1β, Chemokine (C-C motif) ligand 5 (CCL5), CLEC4D, Galectin-3 (LGALS3), Estrogen receptor 2 (ESR2), Fc fragment of IgG, receptor IV (FCGR4), C-type lectin domain containing 4, family N (CLEC4N), and Chitinase-3-like protein 1 (CHIL3) (Fig.  C). The ROC curves for these hub genes were generated using the original expression data from both GSE139204 and GSE145480 datasets using the R language. All hub genes exhibited high predictive value with an AUC indicative of their potential as biomarkers (Fig.  D).

## Construction of a mouse model of sarcopenia to validate hub gene expression
The construction of a mouse model of sarcopenia was successfully achieved, and grip strength tests were employed to evaluate muscle capability in mice. Results indicated that the grip time of mice in the SP group was significantly lower than that of the Control group (Fig.  A). To assess inflammatory responses, ELISA was conducted to measure serum levels of inflammatory cytokines. It was found that the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-8 were significantly elevated in the SP group compared to the Control group (Fig.  B). Histological evaluations using HE and Masson staining were performed on gastrocnemius muscle tissues, revealing that the sarcopenia model group exhibited looser muscle cell structures, increased fibrous tissue, and evident interstitial fibrosis, compared to the Control group (Fig.  C). These results collectively confirmed the validity of the model. Subsequent analysis of hub gene expression in the gastrocnemius muscle tissues showed that the mRNA expression levels of CCL5, CLEC4D, LGALS3, ESR2, FCGR4, and CLEC4N were upregulated in the SP group, consistent with bioinformatics analyses (Fig.  D).
 

## Knockdown of CLEC4D promotes myogenic differentiation and ameliorates sarcopenia in mice
Among the identified hub genes, CLEC4D was prioritized based on its potential role in modulating inflammation and fibrosis, which were processes implicated in the pathogenesis of sarcopenia, and its central position within the PPI network, indicating a likely key regulatory role in muscle dysfunction. To further investigate the regulatory role of CLEC4D in sarcopenia, a CLEC4D knockdown mouse model was established. Grip strength tests were conducted to evaluate muscle capability in mice. The results indicated that the grip time for the SP + LV-shCLEC4D group was increased compared to the SP + LV-shNC group, suggesting that the knockdown of CLEC4D enhanced muscle strength in the SP group (Fig.  A). ELISA was performed to measure serum levels of inflammatory cytokines. It was found that knockdown of CLEC4D significantly inhibited the elevated levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-8 in the SP model mice in the SP + LV-shCLEC4D group, compared to the SP + LV-shNC group (Fig.  B). HE and Masson staining showed that the SP model group and the SP + LV-shNC group exhibited looser muscle cell structures and increased fibrous tissue with evident interstitial fibrosis, whereas the SP + LV-shCLEC4D group showed restored muscle cell structure and reduced fibrosis (Fig.  C). RT-qPCR and western blot analysis were performed on gastrocnemius muscle tissues to detect the expression of CLEC4D and myogenic markers. The RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that the mRNA expression level of CLEC4D was upregulated in the SP group and significantly downregulated in the SP + LV-sh CLEC4D group, confirming the effectiveness of lentivirus knockdown (Fig.  D). Additionally, compared to the Control group, the mRNA expression levels of myogenic markers MyHC, MyoD, and Myogenin were significantly downregulated in the SP group but were reversed after the knockdown of CLEC4D (Fig.  E). The western blot results further confirmed that the expression of CLEC4D protein was upregulated in the SP group and significantly downregulated in the SP + LV-shCLEC4D group. Moreover, compared to the Control group, the expression levels of myogenic markers MyHC, MyoD, and Myogenin were significantly downregulated in the SP group. Conversely, the expression levels of MyHC, MyoD, and Myogenin proteins in the SP + LV-sh CLEC4D group were reversed after the knockdown of CLEC4D (Fig.  F). To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying CLEC4D-mediated regulation of muscle atrophy-related genes, we performed RT-qPCR and western blot analyses to assess the expression of muscle atrophy markers, MuRF1 and MAFbx. Compared to the Control group, the mRNA levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx were significantly elevated in the SP group and SP + LV-shNC group. In contrast, knockdown of CLEC4D via lentiviral shRNA (SP + LV-shCLEC4D group) markedly suppressed this induction, as evidenced by a substantial reduction in MuRF1 and MAFbx mRNA expression relative to the SP + LV-shNC group (Fig.  G). Consistent with the transcriptional changes, western blot analysis revealed parallel alterations in MuRF1 and MAFbx protein abundance, further validating the regulatory role of CLEC4D in modulating proteolytic pathways during muscle atrophy (Fig.  H).
 

## Silencing CLEC4D attenuates D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction and inflammation, thus promoting myogenic differentiation
We investigated the effect of silencing CLEC4D on myoblast differentiation at the cellular level. The western blot results showed that D-gal induction upregulated CLEC4D expression relative to the Control group. Compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, CLEC4D expression was downregulated in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group, confirming the effectiveness of the transfection (Fig.  A). The RT-qPCR results showed that D-gal treatment caused an increase in CLEC4D mRNA expression relative to the Control group. Compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the mRNA expression of CLEC4D was downregulated in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group (Fig.  B). CCK-8 assays were conducted to measure cell viability, revealing reduced proliferation activity of myoblasts following D-gal induction, compared to the Control group, which was partially restored after CLEC4D knockdown (Fig.  C). SA-β-gal staining revealed a significant increase in the proportion of SA-β-gal-positive cells in the D-gal group compared to the Control group, indicative of enhanced cellular senescence induced by D-gal treatment. Notably, the D-gal + LV-shNC group exhibited comparable levels of SA-β-gal positivity to the D-gal group, suggesting that non-specific lentiviral vector delivery did not alter the senescence phenotype. In contrast, the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group demonstrated a marked reduction in SA-β-gal-positive cells relative to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  D). Flow cytometry analysis was used to assess cell apoptosis, indicating that D-gal induced an increase in myoblast apoptosis levels relative to the Control group. After CLEC4D knockdown, the apoptosis was inhibited in D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  E). RT-qPCR analysis determined that the mRNA expression levels of myogenic markers, MyHC, MyoD, and Myogenin were reduced in the D-gal group, compared to the Control group. CLEC4D knockdown reversed the reduction in mRNA expression levels of these myogenic differentiation markers induced by D-gal in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  F). Moreover, the RT-qPCR results illustrated that D-gal treatment caused an increase in the mRNA expression levels of muscle atrophy markers (MuRF1 and MAFbx) relative to the Control group. Compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the mRNA expression levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx were downregulated in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group (Fig.  G). Western blot was performed to detect the expression of proteins, showing that the expression levels of MyHC, MyoD, and Myogenin proteins were reduced in the D-gal group, compared to the Control group. CLEC4D knockdown reversed the reduction in protein expression levels of these myogenic differentiation markers induced by D-gal in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  H). The western blot results showed that D-gal induction elevated the expression levels of muscle atrophy markers (MuRF1 and MAFbx) relative to the Control group. Compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the expression levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx were downregulated in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group (Fig.  I). ELISA was utilized to measure inflammatory cytokine levels, demonstrating that the levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-8 were elevated in the D-gal group, compared to the Control group. Moreover, CLEC4D knockdown inhibited the elevation of inflammatory cytokine levels induced by D-gal in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  J).
 

## Screening of potential downstream pathways of CLEC4D in sarcopenia
To screen the downstream pathways of CLEC4D, we utilized the Coxpresdb database to identify co-expressed genes of CLEC4D (top 2000 genes) and the GeneCards database to filter sarcopenia-related genes (430 genes). Venn diagram analysis revealed 36 common genes (Fig.  A). KEGG enrichment analysis of these common genes indicated significant enrichment in the JAK/STAT pathway (Fig.  B). We then investigated the effect of CLEC4D knockdown on the JAK/STAT pathway by performing western blot analysis to detect protein levels of key components in this pathway. The results demonstrated that D-gal treatment caused an increase in the levels of P-JAK2/JAK2 and P-STAT3/STAT3 in the D-gal group. Moreover, CLEC4D knockdown inhibited the levels of P-JAK2/JAK2 and P-STAT3/STAT3 in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group (Fig.  C).
 

## Silencing CLEC4D attenuates D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction and inflammation via inhibiting the JAK/STAT pathway
To explore the specific mechanisms of action of the JAK/STAT pathway, we used the JAK/STAT pathway activator RO8191 for feedback experiments. CCK-8 assays showed that compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the knockdown of CLEC4D promoted the proliferation activity of myoblasts in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group. The cell viability significantly decreased after RO8191 treatment compared to the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group (Fig.  A). Flow cytometry analysis indicated that compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the apoptosis level of myoblasts in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group was significantly reduced, and this effect was reversed following RO8191 treatment (Fig.  B). SA-β-gal staining revealed that CLEC4D knockdown significantly attenuated D-gal-induced senescence in myoblasts. Specifically, the proportion of SA-β-gal-positive cells in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group was markedly reduced compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group. Notably, pharmacological intervention with RO8191 reversed the suppressive effect of CLEC4D knockdown on SA-β-gal positivity in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D + RO8191 group (Fig.  C). The RT-qPCR results demonstrated that knockdown of CLEC4D increased the mRNA expression levels of myogenic differentiation markers in D-gal-induced myoblasts relative to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, whereas this effect on the mRNA expression of myogenic differentiation markers was reversed after RO8191 treatment (Fig.  D). Moreover, the knockdown of CLEC4D downregulated the mRNA expression levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx in D-gal-induced myoblasts relative to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, whereas this effect of CLEC4D knockdown on the mRNA expression levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx was reversed after RO8191 treatment (Fig.  E). Additionally, western blot demonstrated that knockdown of CLEC4D enhanced the protein expression levels of myogenic differentiation markers in D-gal-induced myoblasts relative to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, whereas this promoting effect on the expression of myogenic differentiation markers was reversed after RO8191 treatment (Fig.  F). The expression levels of MuRF1 and MAFbx proteins in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group were decreased, compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group. Conversely, the treatment of RO8191 reversed the effect of CLEC4D knockdown on D-gal-induced myoblasts relative to the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group (Fig.  G). ELISA revealed that compared to the D-gal + LV-shNC group, the inflammatory cytokine levels in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D group were significantly reduced, and RO8191 treatment reversed the inhibitory effect of CLEC4D knockdown on the inflammatory cytokine levels induced by D-gal in the D-gal + LV-shCLEC4D + RO8191 group (Fig.  H).
 

## Discussion
Early and accurate identification of sarcopenia, along with a comprehensive understanding of its underlying mechanisms and therapeutic targets, is crucial for effectively improving patient quality of life [ ]. Herein, we identified 8 hub genes related to sarcopenia with high predictive value. We observed that CCL5, CLEC4D, LGALS3, ESR2, FCGR4, and CLEC4N were upregulated in the gastrocnemius muscle tissue of sarcopenic mice. Among these, we selected CLEC4D as the gene of interest. By employing a lentiviral transfection model to knock down the expression of CLEC4D in mice, we found that CLEC4D knockdown promoted myogenic differentiation and inhibited muscle atrophy, alleviating sarcopenia in mice. In vitro, CLEC4D knockdown mitigated D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction, promoting their differentiation and suppressing the levels of inflammatory factors and muscle atrophy markers. Furthermore, we identified the JAK/STAT pathway as a key signaling cascade regulated by CLEC4D in the context of sarcopenia, which was activated in D-gal-induced myoblasts. Knockdown of CLEC4D inhibited the JAK/STAT pathway in D-gal-induced myoblasts. Notably, an activator of the JAK/STAT pathway reversed the effects of CLEC4D knockdown on D-gal-induced C2C12 cells.
In the present study, CCL5, CLEC4D, LGALS3, ESR2, FCGR4, and CLEC4N were upregulated in sarcopenia mice, which served as hub genes with significant diagnostic and prognostic value for sarcopenia. Real-time PCR gene array analysis is used to monitor the activity of pathways related to sarcopenia and osteoporosis, revealing that CCL5 is a key regulatory factor in the pathology of muscle atrophy diseases such as sarcopenia [ ]. Using an unbiased Affymetrix gene chip array for screening, CLEC4D is identified as a novel target of Homeodomain-Interacting Protein Kinase 2, which is involved in cardiomyocyte myogenic differentiation and regulates pathological cardiac remodeling [ ]. LGALS3 is a traditional biomarker in predicting the development of sarcopenia and frailty in patients with heart failure [ ]. Muscle stem cell-specific ESR2 knockout controls muscle regeneration and muscle stem cell aging through autophagic clearance [ ]. Single-cell RNA sequencing results indicate that FCGR4 is increased in C-C chemokine receptor type 2+ monocyte-derived macrophages from patients with myocarditis, suggesting it is a potential therapeutic target [ ]. CLEC4N (Dectin-2) knockout can regulate myofibroblast differentiation, modulate Th1 differentiation, and improve wound healing post-myocardial infarction [ ]. Taken together, CCL5, CLEC4D, LGALS3, ESR2, FCGR4, and CLEC4N play crucial roles in sarcopenia and myogenic differentiation, representing potential biomarkers of sarcopenia.
Chronic inflammatory systemic diseases, osteoarthritis, and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies can lead to muscle loss [ ]. CLEC4D has been demonstrated to play a significant role in the resolution of inflammation [ ]. We found that CLEC4D knockdown alleviated sarcopenia in mice, while also mitigating D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction in vitro by suppressing inflammatory factors. The role of CLEC4D in myogenic differentiation remains unclear. However, studies have shown that inhibiting CLEC4D prevents pathological cardiac remodeling by suppressing myocardial fibrosis [ ]. Not only that, CLEC4D recognizes the umbilical cord factors of Mycobacterium tuberculosis and can also sense the molecular patterns released by dead cells and drive inflammation [ ]. Additionally, studies have shown that CLEC4D is a Treg-associated central gene, primarily involved in the immune response and metabolism of Tregs in systemic sclerosis with interstitial lung disease, mediating vascular lesions, pulmonary arterial hypertension, and fibrosis [ ]. All in all, CLEC4D plays a significant role in fibrosis and inflammation, and CLEC4D knockdown can alleviate sarcopenia by inhibiting fibrosis and muscle atrophy, promoting myogenic differentiation, and reducing inflammation.
The JAK/STAT pathway, as one of the classic inflammatory pathways, is related to the pathogenesis of inflammation and autoimmune diseases [ ]. We found that CLEC4D knockdown inhibited the JAK/STAT pathway in D-gal-induced myoblasts, which was the key regulatory mechanism of CLEC4D in sarcopenia. Studies have demonstrated that the JAK/STAT pathway directly modulates muscle cell differentiation by transmitting IL-6-mediated signals to enhance satellite cell proliferation and activate myogenic transcription programs, thereby orchestrating skeletal muscle regeneration and hypertrophic growth [ ]. Furthermore, the JAK/STAT pathway, particularly the JAK2/STAT3 axis, drives cardiac fibrosis by directly inducing profibrotic transformation of cardiac fibroblasts (e.g., proliferation and collagen I/III synthesis) via mechanical stress, and IL-6 family cytokines, with genetic ablation of STAT3 exacerbating remodeling and pharmacological JAK2/STAT3 inhibition attenuating fibrotic responses [ ]. In experimental arthritis, JAK inhibitors increase muscle mass by suppressing IL-6/JAK/STAT activation and promoting the expression of muscle differentiation markers [ ]. Additionally, studies have found that exercise can improve muscle strength, endurance, and fiber size, and can also regulate the JAK-STAT pathway in sarcopenic mice [ ]. Aging leads to significant sarcopenia symptoms in SAMP8 mice, and endurance training effectively improves muscle function, mass, and strength, with the JAK2/STAT3 pathway being a potential mechanism for this improvement [ ]. We found that JAK2/STAT pathway agonists reversed the protective effects of CLEC4D knockdown on D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction, as well as its inhibitory effects on inflammation. In summary, CLEC4D knockdown alleviates sarcopenia by inhibiting the JAK2/STAT pathway.
While our study identifies CLEC4D as a potential therapeutic target for sarcopenia, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the dexamethasone-induced sarcopenia model, while pharmacologically validated, represents an acute, single-factor insult that differs fundamentally from the chronic, multifactorial nature of human age-related sarcopenia. Results should therefore be extrapolated to clinical aging contexts with caution. Future validation in genetic aging models or human aged tissues is essential. Second, while our lentiviral knockdown targeted muscle tissue, we cannot fully exclude potential effects on infiltrating immune cells expressing CLEC4D during inflammation. Cell-type-specific models are needed to dissect the distinct contributions of myocytic versus immune CLEC4D. Finally, although rescue experiments with the JAK/STAT agonist RO8191 support CLEC4D’s upstream regulation of this pathway, complementary approaches using STAT3 siRNA or JAK inhibitors would strengthen mechanistic validation in future studies.
In conclusion, the knockdown of CLEC4D demonstrates effective therapeutic potential in D-gal-induced myoblast dysfunction and sarcopenia in mice. Mechanistically, the knockdown of CLEC4D exerts therapeutic effects on inflammation and dysfunction of myocytes by suppressing the JAK2/STAT pathway in the context of sarcopenia. This finding underscores the potential of CLEC4D and the JAK2/STAT pathway as promising treatments for conditions involving sarcopenia.
