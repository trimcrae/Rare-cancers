## Abstract

## Background
Prolonged exposure to airborne particulate matter (PM) is associated with emphysema and COPD; however, the precise underlying mechanism remains unclear. In a previous high-throughput screen, we identified inter-α-trypsin inhibitor heavy chain 4 (ITIH4) as a biomarker of long-term PM exposure. We hypothesised that ITIH4 is implicated in PM-related emphysema.

## Methods
We investigated the association between ITIH4 expression and ambient PM exposure through a clinical cohort analysis (220 patients with COPD and 61 healthy participants) and in vitro studies.

## Results
The COPD cohort studies revealed significant correlations between emphysema severity, ambient PM exposure and serum ITIH4 levels. In primary small airway epithelial cells from COPD patients with low basal levels of ITIH4, exposure to PM and oxidative stress led to increased apoptosis. However, ITIH4 overexpression significantly inhibited oxidative-stress-induced apoptosis in normal and COPD airway epithelial cells. Acute exposure to hydrogen peroxide resulted in the rapid degradation of ITIH4 protein with no effect on transcriptional level, although ITIH4 gene expression is downregulated in the lung tissue of patients with COPD. A human apoptosis antibody array revealed that ITIH4 overexpression attenuated hydrogen-peroxide-induced apoptotic signalling. Furthermore, extracellular ITIH4 protein confers cytoprotective functions in cells exposed to PM or oxidative stress. Mechanistically, ITIH4 attenuated oxidative-stress-induced c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) activation and β-catenin decrease. A deficiency of ITIH4 exacerbated the effects of oxidative stress.

## Conclusions
We identified a novel pathogenetic mechanism involving ITIH4, where chronic exposure to air pollution induces or promotes emphysema.

## Introduction
COPD is a leading cause of death and disability globally. Its primary aetiology is chronic inhalation of harmful particles, notably from sources such as tobacco smoke and pollutants, combined with genetic, developmental and social factors [ ]. Although persistent airflow limitation and respiratory symptoms are common clinical features, COPD is a heterogeneous and complex disease. Air pollution, recognised as a crucial modulator of COPD morbidity and mortality, is an early determinant of COPD [ ]. In contrast to the extensively studied effect of cigarette smoking, the role of air pollution in COPD pathogenesis remains unclear.
Long-term exposure to ambient air pollution, particularly fine particulate matter (particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter <2.5 µm (PM2.5)), is associated with reduced lung function and increased risk of emphysema and COPD [ , ]. The deposition of PM2.5 in the alveoli is associated with the severity of emphysema in COPD patients, especially in the right middle and upper lobes of the lungs [ ]. PM2.5 exposure may aggravate oxidative stress-induced lung inflammation and impairment, as well as apoptosis of alveolar and bronchial epithelial cells [ , ]. Oxidative stress, triggered by exposure to cigarette smoke and ambient particulate matter or reactive oxygen species (ROS) produced by inflammatory and structural cells within the lungs, can contribute to tissue destruction and emphysema development [ – ]. This process causes excessive loss of alveolar and bronchial epithelial cells. Although antioxidants are known to be effective in cell and animal studies, their clinical utility for COPD treatment is restricted, partly due to concerns regarding their toxicity [ ].
The recently discovered protein inter-α-trypsin inhibitor heavy chain 4 (ITIH4) is a serine protease inhibitor, and understanding of its biological role remains limited [ ]. ITIH4 acts as a protease inhibitor, inhibiting mannan-binding lectin-associated serine protease (MASP)-1, MASP-2 and plasma kallikrein, which are key proteases involved in endovascular host defence [ ]. Notably, elevated post-treatment ITIH4 levels are associated with clinical remission in patients with inflammatory bowel disease [ ]. Similarly, ITIH4 was downregulated and inversely related to C-reactive protein in patients with rheumatoid arthritis [ ]. Furthermore, ITIH4 might play a role in tissue repair in the mammary gland [ ] and in liver development and generation [ ]. Through unbiased proteomics studies, we previously demonstrated that serum ITIH4 levels in COPD patients were negatively correlated with exposure to particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter <10 µm (PM10)), suggesting its potential as a novel biomarker for particulate air pollution [ ]. This inverse association was also observed in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) specimens from healthy individuals [ ]. Additionally, pulmonary ITIH4 expression was reduced in rats exposed to systemic air pollution, which may be related to alveolar epithelial cell senescence and apoptosis [ ]. To understand the role of ITIH4 in COPD and its clinical significance, we studied a cohort including COPD and healthy participants. ITIH4 levels in serum and lung tissue were associated with exposure to particulate matter and clinical factors. Furthermore, ITIH4 participates in c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK)-dependent and JNK-independent anti-apoptosis and repair mechanisms.

## Materials and methods
For details of the materials and methods, please refer to the supplementary material.

## Study participants
All COPD participants aged 40–80 years with respiratory symptoms underwent high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) at Shuang Ho Hospital, Taipei Medical University (New Taipei City, Taiwan) between March 2015 and March 2023. A total of 281 participants (COPD n=220, healthy n=61) were enrolled, and serum samples were obtained from 170 COPD and 61 healthy participants. The study protocol was approved by the joint institutional review board of Taipei Medical University.

## Cell culture
The BEAS-2B and MLE-12 cell lines obtained from the American Type Culture Collection were cultured in Gibco DMEM at 37°C and 5% carbon dioxide, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Human small airway epithelial cells (HSAEpCs) from healthy (aged 53–70 years) and COPD (aged 59–67 years) donors (C-12642, PromoCell, Heidelberg, Germany) were cultured as submerged undifferentiated monolayers in PromoCell cell growth medium and passaged fewer than five times to prevent cell senescence.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics V25.0 and GraphPad Prism V9.0. The results are expressed as mean±sd. Two-tailed unpaired t-tests and Mann–Whitney U-tests were used to analyse the normal and non-normal distribution data between the two groups, respectively. One-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc tests were used for comparisons among three or more groups. Correlations between variables were expressed using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. p<0.05 indicated statistical significance.

## Results

## Reduced ITIH4 expression is correlated with exposure to particulate matter and emphysema severity in COPD patients
The baseline characteristics of our study cohort are presented in supplementary table S1. We estimated PM exposure in patients over the past 10 years using inverse distance-weighted interpolation, utilising data from the three air pollution measurement stations closest to the participants' residences [ ]. In patients with COPD, serum ITIH4 levels were inversely correlated with PM10 and PM2.5 exposure ( ). Furthermore, serum ITIH4 levels in COPD patients were decreased and correlated with disease stage, with the lowest expression in Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease stage IV patients compared with nonsmokers or smokers ( ). ITIH4 levels were correlated with forced expiratory volume in 1 s in COPD (correlation coefficient (rs)=0.387) and all (rs=0.652) participants in a Spearman's rank correlation analysis ( ). Total lung emphysema, quantified by HRCT and expressed as the percentage of low attenuation area (LAA%), was correlated with exposure to PM10 or PM2.5 in COPD patients ( ). Exposure to smoking, defined as pack-years, was also positively associated with emphysema ( ). However, in contrast to PM exposure, smoking exposure was not associated with serum ITIH4 levels. Representative HRCT images depicting total lung emphysema and corresponding ITIH4 expression in patients are presented in . Moreover, ITIH4 levels were significantly and negatively correlated with total LAA% (rs=−0.425). In addition, we observed positive and negative associations of PM exposure over the past 1–10 years with emphysema and lung function, respectively (supplementary table S2). This association was more pronounced with PM2.5 exposure, particularly in the upper lobes.
 To further estimate local concentrations including land use confounders, we assessed particulate matter exposure at the individual level using hybrid kriging/land use regression (LUR) [ ]. The investigation involved assessing the impact of past-year particulate matter exposure; total lung emphysema was associated with PM2.5 exposure (supplementary table S3). Similarly, PM2.5 exposure estimated by the kriging/LUR model was inversely associated with serum ITIH4 levels (supplementary figure S1).
We further investigated whether ITIH4 levels were reduced exclusively in serum. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis revealed that ITIH4 levels were also reduced in the airway or alveolar epithelium of COPD patients compared with controls ( ). To further elucidate the effects of ambient particulate matter or cigarette smoke exposure on ITIH4, we examined ITIH4 expression in lung sections from animals chronically exposed to traffic-based PM2.5 [ ] and cigarette smoke [ ] (supplementary figure S2). Notably, ITIH4 levels were reduced in the airway or alveolar epithelium of particulate-matter- or cigarette-smoke-exposed animals; however, a greater decrease in ITIH4 was observed in the particulate-matter-exposed group. Furthermore, serum ITIH4 concentration in COPD patients was negatively correlated with particulate matter exposure, but not smoking. These data suggest that exposure to ambient particulate matter is associated with reduced ITIH4 levels and emphysema in COPD.

## Oxidative-stress- and nonoxidative-mediated reduction in ITIH4 expression
Oxidative damage plays an imperative role in COPD progression. ITIH4 production was reduced in normal primary HSAEpCs and bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) upon exposure to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), but not to lipopolysaccharide or interleukin (IL)-1β ( ). Notably, the baseline expression of ITIH4 was lower in primary COPD HSAEpCs compared with normal cells. The reduction of ITIH4 induced by H2O2 or IL-1β was more pronounced in COPD HSAEpCs, and this effect that could be eliminated by N-acetyl-l-cysteine (NAC) pre-treatment. However, it had no effect on the low basal ITIH4 levels in COPD HSAEpCs. Collectively, oxidative stress leads to decreased ITIH4 expression in airway epithelial cells, and this effect is enhanced in cells from COPD patients.
 To further assess the effect of particulate matter on ITIH4 expression, ITIH4 was found to be decreased in normal and COPD cells exposed to standard particulate matter from diesel exhaust particles (DEP) and urban dust, but not carbon black particles ( ). Moreover, the particulate-matter-induced reduction in ITIH4 in both normal and COPD cells could be reversed by NAC, indicating an oxidative-mediated effect. To understand how ITIH4 levels are regulated, we measured ITIH4 mRNA expression. Although ITIH4 mRNA levels were reduced in COPD lung tissue ( ) and COPD HSAEpCs, no significant changes in ITIH4 expression were observed in response to irritants ( , e). Next, we evaluated the stability of the ITIH4 protein by inhibiting protein biosynthesis with cycloheximide. Notably, H2O2 led to a rapid depletion of ITIH4 protein when cells were pre-treated with cycloheximide ( ). Together, these results suggest that COPD status is associated with reduced endogenous ITIH4 gene expression, independent of oxidative stress. Conversely, acute oxidative stress leads to decreased ITIH4 expression through protein depletion, which is more pronounced in COPD.

## Low ITIH4 expression is associated with PM2.5- and oxidative-stress-mediated lung epithelial apoptosis
Analysis of cell type-specific ITIH4 expression in lung tissue using single-cell transcriptome datasets revealed that ITIH4 is expressed in all epithelial cells and more strongly in macrophages (supplementary figure S3). Notably, alveolar macrophages may contribute to lung tissue destruction in patients with emphysema. However, PM2.5 exposure had only a minor effect on ITIH4 protein levels in murine alveolar macrophages (supplementary figure S4). Thus, this supports the notion that lung epithelial cells are significant pulmonary sources of ITIH4 impacted by chronic exposure to particulate matter.
Notably, cell viability was significantly reduced after treatment with H2O2 or particulate matter, accompanied by a decrease in ITIH4 protein (supplementary figure S5). Apoptosis of lung tissue cells plays an imperative role in emphysema [ ]. IHC staining analysis revealed significantly elevated levels of cleaved caspase-3 in bronchial and alveolar epithelial cells in COPD patients ( ). ROS indicate particulate-matter-induced oxidative stress and are known to cause apoptosis of alveolar epithelial cells [ , ]. PM2.5 exposure increased ROS generation in BEAS-2B cells in a dose-dependent manner, whereas NAC treatment significantly alleviated this effect ( ). PM2.5 also induced ROS in normal HSAEpCs, with this effect being enhanced in COPD cells. Notably, NAC treatment reversed the reduction in secreted ITIH4 proteins in BEAS-2B cells exposed to PM2.5 as well as normal and COPD HSAEpCs ( ). Functionally, knockdown of endogenous ITIH4 increased basal and PM2.5-induced apoptosis in BEAS-2B cells and normal HSAEpCs ( ). Conversely, overexpression of ITIH4 robustly attenuated PM2.5-induced apoptosis in BEAS-2B cells and COPD HSAEpCs. Similar effects were observed when manipulating ITIH4 in HSAEpCs exposed to H2O2 ( ). Collectively, ITIH4 exhibits a protective function against particulate-matter- and oxidative-stress-induced apoptosis in lung epithelial cells. Reduced ITIH4 expression may reflect an increased stress response in COPD patients.
 To provide additional evidence for the antiapoptotic role of ITIH4, we employed a human apoptosis antibody array. ITIH4 overexpression noticeably inhibited apoptosis-related proteins in H2O2-treated cells ( ). In addition, we verified that ITIH4 overexpression significantly reduced H2O2-mediated caspase-3 activation ( ). Notably, apoptosis-related proteins mainly involved in the extrinsic pathway [ ], such as BAD, TRAIL-R1/2, Fas, TNF-R1/2, HSP60 and p21CDKN1A, are regulated by ITIH4. These data indicate that ITIH4 attenuates apoptotic signalling in lung epithelial cells.
 Since ITIH4 is a secreted protein, we explored whether extracellular ITIH4 signalling mediated prevention of cell death. We then examined the cytoprotective function of full-length recombinant ITIH4 protein (rITIH4) produced by mammalian cells under particulate-matter exposure or oxidative stress conditions. Notably, rITIH4 attenuated the decrease in cell viability of PM2.5- and H2O2-treated BEAS-2B or MLE-12 type II alveolar epithelial cells (AECII) ( and supplementary figure S6). Consistent with the functions exhibited by ITIH4-overexpressing cells, rITIH4 prevents apoptosis mediated by exposure to particulate matter or oxidative stress ( ). Notably, administration of rITIH4 or overexpression of ITIH4 significantly reduced ROS production in PM2.5- or H2O2-treated cells ( ). To obtain direct antioxidant evidence, we further tested the free-radical-scavenging ability of rITIH4. Interestingly, rITIH4 exhibits scavenging of H2O2-activated free radicals (supplementary figure S7). rITIH4 also displayed cytoprotective effects in air–liquid interface cultures of differentiated human bronchial epithelial cells exposed to PM2.5 (data not shown). These findings suggest that extracellular ITIH4 can also alleviate particulate-matter- or oxidative-stress-mediated cell death.
To confirm the functional importance of extracellular ITIH4 protein, brefeldin A (BFA) was used to block protein transport and vesicle formation [ ]. Notably, cell viability was rescued in cells treated with PM2.5 or/and BFA in combination with rITIH4 protein ( ). However, ITIH4 overexpression failed to attenuate PM2.5-mediated cell death while blocking cellular ITIH4 secretion by BFA treatment. We further measured whether PM2.5 or H2O2 mediated apoptotic DNA fragmentation using a terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick-end labelling (TUNEL) assay. The proportion of TUNEL-positive cells increased in BEAS-2B ( ) or MLE-12 cells (supplementary figure S6c) exposed to PM2.5 and H2O2, whereas administration of rITIH4 effectively attenuated this effect. Collectively, extracellular ITIH4 protein is able to alleviate particulate-matter- or oxidative-stress-mediated cell death.

## ITIH4 deficiency promotes cellular destruction through JNK-dependent and JNK-independent mechanisms
Reduced WNT/β-catenin signalling may lead to lung tissue destruction and impaired repair in emphysema [ ]. JNK is not only a vital player in apoptosis [ ], but is also known to phosphorylate β-catenin and regulate cellular junctions [ ]. In BEAS-2B cells, H2O2-induced decrease in ITIH4 was associated with reduced β-catenin and JNK activation ( ). Similar effects were observed in BEAS-2B cells treated with DEPs, urban dust and PM2.5 ( , c), as well as in DEP- and PM2.5-treated HSAEpCs ( ), but with varying magnitudes. We then investigated whether ITIH4 regulated the levels of β-catenin and JNK activation. Overexpression of ITIH4 attenuated β-catenin reduction and inhibited JNK activation stimulated by H2O2 ( ). Conversely, ITIH4 knockdown enhanced the reduction of β-catenin and the induction of JNK phosphorylation in response to H2O2 ( and 6d).
 The JNK inhibitor SP600125 robustly, but not completely, inhibited H2O2-induced apoptosis in BEAS-2B cells. However, ITIH4 knockdown enhanced H2O2-induced apoptosis, and this effect was counteracted by SP600125 ( ). Consistent results were observed by immunoblot analysis of cleaved caspase-3 ( ). However, neither p38 nor extracellular signal-regulated kinase inhibitors affected H2O2-induced apoptosis. Intriguingly, both NAC and the pan caspase inhibitor Z-VAD completely prevented H2O2-induced apoptosis ( , c). The oxidative-stress-mediated effects of H2O2 were confirmed by NAC pre-treatment experiments ( ). These findings suggest that JNK is the primary pro-apoptotic signal in response to H2O2 and that ITIH4 alleviates apoptosis by inhibiting JNK activation. However, JNK inhibition did not appear to affect the levels of β-catenin in the presence of H2O2 in si-control or si-ITIH4-transfected cells, suggesting the involvement of a JNK-independent pathway ( ). Collectively, ITIH4-deficient lung epithelial cells are more susceptible to oxidative stress, leading to apoptosis and impaired repair through JNK-dependent and JNK-independent mechanisms.
 

## Discussion
Our findings indicate the clinical and functional roles of ITIH4 in COPD, especially in response to exposure to particulate matter. Notably, low expression of ITIH4 in serum and lung tissues due to particulate matter exposure is correlated with epithelial cell apoptosis, increased severity of emphysema, and diminished lung function. Mechanically, functional ITIH4 attenuates particulate-matter-induced apoptosis and reduces impaired β-catenin signalling. Impairment of this signalling may exacerbate particulate-matter-induced lung tissue injury, leading to emphysema development. An important characteristic of COPD is the widespread narrowing and disappearance of small airways prior to the onset of emphysematous destruction [ ]. Additionally, similar tissue remodelling and destruction mechanisms exist in bronchioles and alveolar tissues [ ]. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed decreased ITIH4 expression in both bronchiolar and alveolar epithelial cells in COPD. Notably, HSAEpCs isolated from distal bronchioles, including epithelial cells of bronchioles and alveoli, exhibited ITIH4 downregulation in response to particulate matter exposure. Consistent results were observed in AECII cells, MLE-12 and A549 [ ], and bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B).
The imbalance between proteases and antiproteases is also an important pathophysiological mechanism of COPD [ ]. Some COPD patients are associated with genetic factors, specifically serum α1-antitrypsin (A1AT) deficiency. A1AT is a serine protease inhibitor and plays a crucial role in protecting the lungs from neutrophil elastase, a serine protease implicated in the severity of COPD. Based on the observation of increased CD8+ T-cells that produce interferon (IFN)-γ in COPD, an inducible IFN-γ overexpression mouse model showed that IFN-γ induced pulmonary emphysema and caused alterations in pulmonary protease/antiprotease balance [ ]. A1AT may block MASP-2 activity and the lectin complement pathway in COPD [ ] and effectively inhibit neutrophil elastase, matrix metalloproteinases and caspase-3 [ ]. Notably, ITIH4 is also a recognised inhibitor of MASP-2 in purified systems and in serum [ ]. Our study revealed that ITIH4 inhibits caspase-3 to alleviate lung epithelial cell impairment caused by oxidative stress. It remains to be determined whether the effect of ITIH4 in COPD is through inhibition of proteases, including MASP-2 or neutrophil elastase, independently or synergised with A1AT or other protease inhibitors.
We have previously demonstrated reduced ITIH4 expression in the lungs of rats exposed to traffic particulate matter, potentially associated with alveolar epithelial cell apoptosis [ ]. This study further demonstrated the association of ITIH4 regulation in the lung and serum of COPD patients with particulate matter exposure, lung function, and emphysema severity. Notably, serum ITIH4 levels in COPD patients were inversely associated with particulate matter exposure, but not with smoking. Indeed, this association may be due to particulate matter exposure being more accurate in quantifying smoking exposure than patient-provided information [ ]. ITIH4 levels are reduced in airway and alveolar epithelial cells in COPD patients, which may be related to PM2.5 exposure, and this change is also reflected in serum levels. Evidently, ITIH4 deficiency renders pulmonary epithelial cells more vulnerable to environmental stressors, thereby inducing or promoting emphysema. Significantly, the cytoprotective effect of rITIH4 was observed in cells treated with particulate matter or oxidative stress. Thus, ITIH4 may serve a particulate-matter-associated biomarker in COPD and provide a basis for new drug development.
The health effects of air pollution can be acute or chronic [ ]. We observed rapid degradation of ITIH4 protein in cells after acute particulate matter exposure. However, low basal ITIH4 gene expression in COPD patients may be related to chronic particulate matter exposure. Furthermore, lung tissue transcriptome analysis revealed that ITIH4 was also significantly expressed in T-cells and smooth muscle cells (supplementary figure S3). We previously found that PM2.5 exposure was associated with decreased ITIH4 levels in BAL fluid of healthy nonsmokers [ ], suggesting an effect of PM2.5 on lung source of ITIH4. ITIH4, produced by epithelial and other cells, may function extracellularly to shield against particulate matter-mediated lung injury, whereas chronic exposure to particulate matter impairs this protective function. However, PM2.5 can penetrate the lungs, enter the bloodstream and affect organs beyond the respiratory system, including the liver [ ]. Thus, the primary source of ITIH4 in the serum remains to be determined because it was also a liver-produced plasma protein [ ].
Low ITIH4 levels are associated with inflammatory diseases [ , ]. Citrullination of ITIH4 protein may render it inactive and exacerbate arthritis [ ]. Moreover, we previously found that ITIH4 was negatively correlated with IL-6 levels in BAL fluid of particulate-matter-exposed rats [ ]. ITIH4 may inhibit serine proteases involved in intravascular host defence after cleavage [ ]. Furthermore, phosphoglycerate kinase 1 binds to ITIH4 and prevents its cleavage [ ], thereby inhibiting pro-inflammatory responses. However, the role of ITIH4 regulation in inflammation in COPD patients remains unclear. Notably, ITIH4 appears to have ROS-scavenging activity in vitro; however, the situation in vivo may be more complicated by modulating ITIH4 expression or its activity.
Mechanistically, ITIH4 may alleviate downstream apoptosis by directly or indirectly inhibiting H2O2-induced JNK activation. Furthermore, ITIH4 alleviated H2O2-induced β-catenin reduction, independent of JNK. Given that attempts to prevent JNK or mitogen-activated protein kinase-mediated diseases using small-molecule inhibitors have generally failed, application of ITIH4 may provide an alternative therapeutic strategy. ITIH4 can also suppress inflammation and, more interestingly, may play an anti-ageing role in particulate-matter responses, thus having a broader range of clinical functions than currently [ ]. This observation is supported by our results ( ) showing that ITIH4 inhibits the ageing signalling protein p21CDKN1A.
Taken together, our findings demonstrate that ITIH4 deficiency plays a pathological role in inducing or promoting emphysema in COPD patients exposed to particulate matter, and this novel mechanism may provide a new approach to treat particulate-matter-related emphysema.
