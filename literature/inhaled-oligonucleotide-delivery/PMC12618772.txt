## Abstract
Heart failure (HF) remains a critical global health challenge, with accumulating evidence implicating iron metabolism dysregulation through ferritinophagy activation in disease progression. Our study focused on sterol O-acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1), an established regulator of hypoxia-induced cardiomyocyte dysfunction, investigating its role in HF pathogenesis through ferritinophagy and ferroptosis regulation. Here, mouse HF models were generated via transverse aortic constriction (TAC), while primary cardiomyocyte injury was induced by isoproterenol (ISO) exposure. We demonstrated that SOAT1 knockdown alleviated pressure overload-induced left ventricular hypertrophy and enhanced systolic function in vivo, while attenuating ISO-induced cardiomyocyte death in vitro. Mechanistically, SOAT1 deficiency suppressed ferritinophagy-mediated ferroptosis by blocking autophagy-dependent ferritin degradation, consequently reducing intracellular iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation in both cardiac tissues and isolated cardiomyocytes. Molecular analyses revealed that SOAT1 enhanced NCOA4 protein stability through acetylation modification, while the RNA acetyltransferase NAT10 maintained SOAT1 expression via N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C)-dependent mRNA modification, enhancing transcript stability and translation. Crucially, NCOA4 overexpression reversed the cardioprotective effects of SOAT1 knockdown, and NAT10 silencing attenuated ISO-induced ferritinophagy and ferroptosis. Collectively, our findings establish that ac4C-modified SOAT1 exacerbates HF by acetylating NCOA4 and promoting NCOA4-dependent ferroautophagy and ferroptosis.

## Introduction
Heart failure (HF) is the final stage of most cardiovascular diseases and is characterized by diminished cardiac function and an inability to pump blood efficiently to meet the demands of the body [ ]. HF is a global problem accompanied by high morbidity and mortality. Despite the continuous development of therapeutic strategies, the 5-year survival rate of patients with HF is still low (about 40%) [ ]. Therefore, new therapeutic strategies are necessary to cope with this growing public health problem.
Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of non-apoptotic cell death characterized by the lethal accumulation of lipid peroxides in cellular membranes [ ]. Ferroptosis is regulated by multiple metabolic pathways, including the system Xc⁻/glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) axis (anti-ferroptotic) and iron metabolism/oxidative stress axis (pro-ferroptotic). The light-chain subunit SLC7A11 of system Xc⁻ facilitates the 1:1 antiport of cystine and glutamate across the plasma membrane, providing cysteine for glutathione (GSH) biosynthesis. As a pivotal member of the GPX family, GPX4 utilizes reduced GSH as a cofactor to detoxify lipid peroxides by converting them into harmless alcohols, thereby blocking lipid peroxidation and suppressing ferroptosis [ ]. Ferritin, composed of ferritin light chain (FTL) and ferritin heavy chain 1 (FTH1), plays a crucial role in maintaining iron homeostasis by sequestering excess iron to prevent Fenton reaction-mediated oxidative damage in cells [ ]. Selective autophagy of ferritin is termed ferritinophagy. When ferritin is chelated inside the autophagosome, it will be degraded and leads to the rapid release of large amounts of free iron, thereby enhancing ferroptosis [ ]. Nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4) has been identified as a cargo receptor for ferritin autophagic degradation and is essential for selective autophagy of ferritin [ ]. Recent evidence suggests that NCOA4-driven ferroptosis is involved in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases, including heart failure [ , ].
Sterol-O acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1, also known as ACAT1) is an integral membrane protein located in the region of the endoplasmic reticulum and is universally expressed in almost all cells. The SOAT1 protein converts excess cholesterol to inert cholesteryl esters and stores them in lipid droplets [ , ]. Studies indicated that down-regulation of SOAT1 suppressed the malignant progression of several tumors by regulating cholesterol metabolism in tumor cells [ , ]. Moreover, SOAT1 was involved in the progression of cardiovascular diseases, including coronary heart disease and atherosclerosis [ , ]. One of our previously published studies demonstrated that piRNA-6426 inhibited SOAT1 expression by promoting DNMT3B-mediated methylation of the SOAT1 promoter, thereby attenuating hypoxia-induced cardiomyocyte dysfunction and heart failure in rats [ ]. Additionally, a close link between cholesterol metabolism and ferroptosis sensitivity has been reported [ , ]. We explored whether SOAT1 was involved in the progression of pressure overload-induced heart failure by regulating ferroptosis.
N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) is a highly conserved RNA modification [ ]. N-acetyltransferase 10 (NAT10), a member of the general control of non-repressor 5 (GCN5)-associated N-acetyltransferase (GNAT) family, was the first acetyltransferase identified to regulate the ac4C modification of mRNA [ ]. NAT10 catalyzes acetylation at the wobbling position in the coding sequence to promote mRNA stability and translational efficiency [ ]. Emerging evidence suggests that NAT10-mediated modification of ac4C may be associated with the onset and progression of many diseases, such as cancer [ ], inflammatory diseases [ ], and osteoporosis [ ]. A previous study indicated that piRNA and NAT10-mediated ac4C acetylation mechanisms were involved in the regulation of apoptosis in cardiomyocytes [ ]. However, the role of NAT10-mediated ac4C acetylation in the progression of pressure overload-induced heart failure still needs to be deeply explored.
In this study, we demonstrated that SOAT1 knockdown impeded pressure overload-induced cardiac dysfunction by inhibiting cardiomyocyte ferritinophagy activation and ferroptosis. Mechanistically, ac4C modified SOAT1 activated ferritinophagy and ferroptosis via acetylation of NCOA4. Our study suggested that inhibition of SOAT1 might be a therapeutic strategy for HF.

## Materials and methods

## Animal experiments
All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Shaanxi Provincial People’s Hospital, and followed the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the National Institutes of Health. 8−12-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (China). All animals were housed under SPF conditions (12 h light/dark cycle at 25 ℃ and 60% humidity) with free access to water and food. The animals were operated after 7 days of acclimatization.
Transverse aortic constriction (TAC) was performed in mice using a 26-gauge needle as previously described [ ]. Mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 0.3% sodium pentobarbital (0.25 mL/10 g). The left anterolateral thorax was incised at the second intercostal space of the mouse, a 26-gauge needle was placed parallel to the transverse aorta, and a 6 − 0 silk suture was placed between the abdominal aorta and the left carotid artery. The needle was quickly ligated and withdrawn. No ligature was placed around the aorta in the sham group of mice. On day 1 post-TAC surgery, mice were randomly assigned to receive tail vein injections (100 µL, 1.5 × 1011 vg/mL) of AAV9-sh-SOAT1 or its negative control AAV9-sh-NC, and AAV9-NCOA4 or its empty vector control AAV9-Vector. Mice in the sham group were injected with 100 µL of saline via tail vein. Echocardiography was performed 3 weeks later, and tissue and blood samples were collected for further examination. The mouse Troponin T (Tn-T) ELISA Assay Kit (CB10368-Mu, COIBO BIO, Wuhan, Chian) was applied to measure the serum TnT levels following the manufacturer’s protocols.

## Echocardiography
Mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane inhalation, and transthoracic echocardiography was conducted by using a VEvo 2100 image system (VisualSonics, Toronto, Ontario, Canada) with a center frequency of 30 MHz. Left ventricular (LV) functional parameters were analyzed by using VEvo 2100 software.

## Histological analysis
Left ventricular samples were embedded in OCT compound (Thermo Fisher) and then immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. The samples were cut into 5-µm-thick sections and processed using two fixation approaches: for conventional histology, sections were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (15 min) and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H;E; 60524ES60; Yeasen, Shanghai, China); for specialized staining, parallel sections were fixed in acetone (−20 °C, 10 min) prior to Masson’s trichrome staining (G1006; Servicebio, Wuhan, China) for collagen visualization and wheat germ agglutinin labeling (Alexa Fluor™ 488 conjugate; W11261; Invitrogen) for cellular membrane profiling, with all procedures performed according to manufacturers’ instructions.

## Isolation and culture of primary neonatal mouse cardiomyocytes
Cardiomyocytes were isolated from the hearts of euthanized 1–3-day-old C57BL/6 mice (males and females) as previously described [ ]. Mice were wiped with 75% ethanol for superficial disinfection, and then the hearts were removed and immediately transferred to cold DPBS (Gibco). Heart tissues were cut into 0.5–1 mm pieces and placed in a buffer containing 0.8% NaCl, 0.05% trypsin, 0.2% Hepes, 0.1% D-(+)-Glucose, 0.035% NaHCO3, and 0.03% KCl for digestion. Subsequent purification was performed using Percoll (Sigma-Aldrich) gradient centrifugation. Harvested cardiomyocytes were cultured in DMEM/F-12 (Gibco) medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Cardiomyocytes were stimulated with isoprenaline (ISO, 10 µM, Sigma-Aldrich) for 48 h to induce cardiomyocyte hypertrophy.

## Cell transfection
The full lengths of SOAT1, NAT10 and NCOA4 were inserted into the pcDNA3.1 vector. The siRNAs against SOAT1 and NAT10 and the shRNAs against SOAT1 were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The cTnT promoter-driven AAV9-cTnT-sh-SOAT1, AAV9-cTnT-sh-NC or AAV9-cTnT-NCOA4 vectors were purchased from Genechem Co. (Shanghai, China). Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher) was applied to conduct cell transfection. The sequences used in this study were as follows: sh-NC-5’-CCG GCA ACA AGA TGA AGA GCA CCA ACT C-3’, sh-SOAT1-5’- GCT TGG TTC CAT TGC AAT TCA-3’, si-NC-5’-UUC UCC GAA CGU GUC ACG UTT-3’, si-SOAT1-5’-TAA TGG TCG AAT TGA CAT AA-3’, si-NAT10-5’- GCA UGG ACC UCU CUG AAU ATT-3’.

## Immunofluorescent staining
Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series (100%, 95%, 80%, and 70%). Antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer, followed by blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h at room temperature. Cells grown on glass coverslips were fixed with paraformaldehyde (4%) for 1 h, permeabilized in Triton X-100 (0.3%) for 1 min, and blocked in 5% BSA for 1 h. Next, tissue and cell samples were stained with the following primary antibodies: anti-LC3B (ab48394, 1:400, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), anti-FTH1 (ab308115, 1:200, Abcam), and anti-lysosomal-associated membrane protein 2a (LAMP2a; ab125068, 1:400, Abcam,), followed by incubation with the corresponding secondary antibodies. Images were obtained using a fluorescence microscope (Nikon TE-2000, Tokyo, Japan).

## Cell viability assay
Cell Counting Kit-8 kit (CCK-8, Dojindo, Tokyo, Japan) was applied for cell viability assay. Briefly, cells were inoculated in 96-well plates at a density of 1000 cells per well, and after 48 h of incubation, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well. After 2 h of incubation, the absorbance value at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader.

## Cell death assay
The treated cells were collected and fixed with cold 70% ethanol for 1 h at 4 ℃. After washing with PBS,
cells were dual-stained by first incubating with 2 µM Calcein-AM (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at 37 °C for 20 min to mark metabolically active live cells (green fluorescence), then treated with 5 µg/mL PI (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 10 min to label membrane-compromised dead cells (red fluorescence), with all steps performed protected from light to prevent dye degradation. Fluorescence images were acquired using a Nikon TE-2000 inverted microscope (Nikon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).

## Lipid peroxidation assay
The treated cells were incubated with 5 µM BODIPY 581/591 C11 (Invitrogen, CA, USA) for 0.5 h and then washed with PBS. Lipid peroxidation levels were assessed using immunofluorescence.

## Detection of ferrous ion content
A Ferrous Ion Content Assay Kit (BC5415; Solarbio, Beijing, China) was employed to measure the cardiac tissue homogenates and intracellular ferrous iron levels following the manufacturer’s instructions. The absorbance at 593 nm was measured by using a microplate reader.

## Malondialdehyde (MDA) and glutathione (GSH) measurements
GSH/GSSG and MDA were measured in cardiac tissue homogenates and myocardial cell lysates using the GSH and GSSG Assay Kit (S0053; Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and the Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (S0131S; Beyotime), respectively, in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

## LDH release assay
Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels in cell culture supernatants were determined using an LDH release assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Jiangsu, China). According to the requirements of the kits, the LDH assay buffer (48 µL) were mixed with the substrate mixture (2 µL), and then the mixed liquid were added to the samples or standards, followed by incubation at 37 ℃ for 1 h. The absorbance values were read at 440 nm using a microplate reader (Thermo fisher scientific).

## Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
Cells were sequentially fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde and 2% osmium tetroxide, followed by dehydration with ethanol and propylene oxide. The samples were embedded in epoxy resin, cut into ultrathin sections, and then stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Images were observed using a transmission electron microscopy (HITACHI, Tokyo, Japan).

## Dual luciferase reporter gene experiment
Wild-type and mutant (C was replaced by G in the CAC ac4C motif sequence) SOAT1 3’UTR sequences were inserted into the pmirGLO vector. HEK293T cells were inoculated in 24-well plates, and were transfected with both the two luciferase vectors and sh-NC/sh-NAT10. 24 h later, cells were obtained and the luciferase activity was detected.

## Co-immunoprecipitation assay
Cells were lysed with ice-cold IP lysis buffer on ice for 30 min, and the supernatant was collected by centrifugation at 4 °C. Anti-SOAT1 (ab307597; 1:100, Abcam), anti-NCOA4 (#66849; 1:50, Cell Signaling Technology (CST), Boston, MA, USA), anti-Flag (#14793; 1:50, CST), and IgG (#2729; 1:50, CST) were used for immunoprecipitation, and then the immune complexes were captured using protein A/G plus agarose (P2055; Beyotime). Finally, the agarose beads were mixed with SDS-PAGE loading buffer and boiled for 10 min. The precipitated proteins were analyzed using Western blotting.

## Ac4C RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay
An RNA immunoprecipitation kit (GK-5044; Genelily Biotech, Guangzhou, China) was applied to conduct the ac4C-RIP assay. Cells were lysed using RIP lysis buffer and the collected lysate was incubated with protein A/G beads coupled with anti-ac4C (ab252215; 1:50, Abcam) or IgG (#2729; 1:50, CST) for 6 h at 4 °C. The levels of SOAT1 mRNA in the immune complexes were analyzed using RT-qPCR.

## Western blot analysis
Total protein was extracted with RIPA lysis buffer (ab156034; Abcam) and determined with a BCA Assay Kit (23225; Thermo Fisher). Proteins samples were separated through SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Germany). Next, the membranes were blocked with 5% skimmed milk powder for 1 h, followed by incubation with the following primary antibodies: anti-SOAT1 (ab307597; 1:1000, Abcam), anti-NAT10 (ab194297; 1:2000, Abcam), anti-FTH1 (#3998; 1:1000, CST), anti-GPX4 (ab252833; 1:1500, Abcam), anti-β-tubulin (#5346; 1:1000, CST), anti-Ack (M30069M; 1:1000; Abmart, Shanghai, China), and anti-NCOA4 (#66849; 1:1000, CST) at 4 °C overnight. Then, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h. An ECL kit (Beyotime) was used for detecting the protein bands, and the ImageJ software was used to analyze the density of the bands.

## RT-qPCR
Total RNA was extracted with RNAiso Plus kit (No.9108; TaKara, Dalian, China). cDNA was obtained from 1 µg of total RNA with PrimeScript Reverse Transcription Master Mix (RR036A; Takara). RT-qPCR was conducted on an Applied Biosystems 7500 (Foster City, California, USA) using the SYBR Green Premix Ex Taq (AK8806; Takara). Relative gene expression was normalized to GAPDH and calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. The following primers were used in this study: GAPDH: forward: 5’ -GTC GTG GAG TCT ACT GGC GTC TTC A-3’ and reverse: 5’ -TCG TGG TTC ACA CCC ATC ACA AAC A-3’; Nppa: forward 5’-TCG TCT TGG CCT TTT GGC T-3’ and reverse 5’-TCC AGG TGG TCT AGC AGG TTC T-3’; Nppb: forward 5’-AAG TCC TAG CCA GTC TCC AGA-3’ and reverse 5’-GAG CTG TCT CTG GGC CAT TTC-3’; Col3a1: forward 5’-CCC GGG TGC TCC TGG ACA GA-3’ and reverse 5’-CAC CCT GAG GAC CAG GCG GA-3’; Col1a2: forward 5’-ACG CGG ACT CTG TTG CTG CT-3’ and reverse 5’-GCG GGA CCC CTT TGT CCA CG-3’; SOAT1: forward 5’-AGC CCA GAA AAA TTT CAT GGA CAC ATA CAG-3’ and reverse 5’-CCC TTG TTC TGG AGG TGC TCT CAG ATC TTT-3’.

## Statistical analysis
The data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD), and analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prime 9.5. Comparisons between groups were made by unpaired Student’s t-test and One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

## Results

## Knockdown of SOAT1 attenuated pressure overload-induced heart failure
To elucidate the role of SOAT1 in pressure overload-induced cardiac remodeling, we employed transverse aortic constriction (TAC) in mice coupled with sh-SOAT1 knockdown via tail vein injection., We first assessed the temporal expression pattern of SOAT1 and found that its protein levels progressively increased at 1, 3, and 7 days post-TAC, peaking by day 7 and remaining elevated at day 28 (Fig.  A, B). Subsequent echocardiographic assessment was performed to evaluate cardiac structure and function. Compared with sham controls, TAC mice exhibited significant cardiac dysfunction, as evidenced by increased left ventricular end-systolic (LVIDs) and end-diastolic (LVIDd) diameters, elevated left ventricular (LV) mass, and a higher LV weight-to-tibial length ratio, along with reduced fractional shortening (FS) (Fig.  C-G). Importantly, SOAT1 knockdown attenuated these pathological alterations, mitigating both cardiac hypertrophy and functional impairment (Fig.  C-G). To further characterize the morphological changes in cardiomyocytes, we conducted H&E and WGA staining. The results demonstrated that SOAT1 deficiency prevented TAC-induced cardiomyocyte enlargement, as reflected by a reduced cross-sectional area (Fig.  H, I). We then examined the impact of SOAT1 on cardiac fibrosis. Histological analysis revealed that SOAT1 silencing significantly suppressed the expansion of fibrotic areas (Fig.  H, J). Consistently, SOAT1 knockdown downregulated the expression of fibrosis-related genes (Col1a2 and Col3a1) (Fig.  K, L). Finally, to assess the overall progression of cardiac remodeling, we measured the mRNA levels of established biomarkers (Nppa and Nppb). These markers were markedly elevated in TAC mice compared to sham controls, whereas SOAT1 inhibition effectively normalized their expression (Fig.  M, N).
 

## Knockdown of SOAT1 suppressed pressure overload-induced ferroautophagy activation and ferroptosis
Building upon previous evidence linking ferritinophagy activation to heart failure pathogenesis, we systematically investigated the regulation of ferritinophagy under pressure overload and its modulation by SOAT1. Western blot analysis first established the dynamic changes in key ferritinophagy markers, indicating that NCOA4 levels were progressively increased while FTH1 levels were decreased at 1, 3, and 7 days post-operation compared to sham controls (Fig.  A-C). Although both markers showed partial recovery by day 28, NCOA4 remained elevated (Fig.  A, B) and FTH1 remained suppressed (Fig.  A, C) relative to sham levels. To directly visualize ferritinophagic flux, we performed immunofluorescence co-localization studies, which revealed enhanced co-localization of LC3B-FTH1 and LAMP2a (lysosomal-associated membrane protein 2a)-FTH1 in TAC hearts, while SOAT1 deficiency significantly attenuated these interactions (Fig.  D, E), providing direct evidence that SOAT1 silencing inhibited pressure overload-induced ferritinophagy. Subsequent biochemical analyses elucidated the functional changes of ferritinophagy activation, showing that pressure overload induced iron overload (elevated Fe2+ levels) and lipid peroxidation (increased MDA levels), and impaired antioxidant defenses (reduced GSH/GSSG ratio and GPX4 protein levels) (Fig.  F-J). All hallmarks of ferroptosis that were markedly ameliorated by SOAT1 knockdown (Fig.  F-J). Additionally, measurement of serum troponin T (TnT) confirmed that SOAT1 knockdown attenuated pressure overload-induced cardiomyocyte death (Fig.  K).
 

## Knockdown of SOAT1 inhibited isoproterenol induced ferroautophagy and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes
Base on established evidence of catecholamine involvement in heart failure pathogenesis, we investigated SOAT1’s role in isoprenaline (ISO)-induced cardiomyocyte injury through a series of mechanistically interlinked experiments. To establish the involvement of SOAT1 in ISO-induced ferritinophagy, we performed Western blot analysis. The results showed that ISO treatment significantly upregulated both SOAT1 and NCOA4 while downregulating FTH1, and that SOAT1 knockdown reversed these effects (Fig.  A-D). To directly visualize ferritinophagic flux, we conducted immunofluorescence studies, which indicated that ISO stimulation enhanced the co-localization of LC3B-FTH1 and LAMP2-FTH1, while SOAT1 silencing significantly reduced this co-localization (Fig.  E, F), providing evidence that SOAT1 mediated ISO-induced ferritinophagy activation in cardiomyocytes. Subsequent biochemical assays revealed that SOAT1 silencing protected against ISO-induced iron dyshomeostasis (reduced Fe²⁺ accumulation, Fig.  G), oxidative stress (decreased lipid ROS and MDA levels, Fig.  H-J), and membrane integrity damage (attenuated LDH release, Fig.  K). We further confirmed the role of SOAT1 in ferroptosis prevention by showing it maintained GPX4 expression under ISO stress (Fig.  L, M). Functional assessments demonstrated that SOAT1 preserved cardiomyocyte viability and reduced cell death under ISO stimulation (Fig.  N-P). Additionally, transmission electron microscopy provided ultrastructural evidence that SOAT1 deficiency mitigated ISO-induced mitochondrial damage by reducing mitochondrial crumpling and mitochondrial cristae (Fig.  Q).
 

## SOAT1 promoted the acetylation level and protein stability of NCOA4
Next, we investigated whether SOAT1 regulates NCOA4 expression and stability through post-translational modification. Co-immunoprecipitation assays confirmed a physical interaction between SOAT1 and NCOA4 in cardiomyocytes (Fig.  A, B), suggesting their potential functional association. Given SOAT1’s known acetyltransferase activity, we then examined whether it mediates NCOA4 acetylation. The data revealed that SOAT1 overexpression significantly enhanced while SOAT1 silencing reduced NCOA4 acetylation levels (Fig.  C, D). Importantly, neither SOAT1 overexpression nor knockdown affected NCOA4 mRNA levels (Fig.  E). Up-regulation of SOAT1 markedly increased the protein level of NCOA4, whereas silencing of SOAT1 exerted an opposite effect (Fig.  F, G), indicating post-translational regulation. To evaluate the role of SOAT1 silencing in NCOA4 protein degradation, we employed cycloheximide (CHX) to inhibit protein synthesis. Our results demonstrated that SOAT1 knockdown significantly accelerated NCOA4 protein degradation in the presence of CHX (Fig.  H).
 

## Knockdown of SOAT1 suppressed ISO-induced ferroautophagy and ferroptosis by down-regulating NCOA4
To determine whether SOAT1 regulates ferroautophagy and ferroptosis through NCOA4-dependent mechanisms, we performed comprehensive rescue experiments in ISO-stimulated cardiomyocytes. Western blot analysis demonstrated that SOAT1 knockdown significantly reduced NCOA4 while increasing FTH1 protein levels, and these effects were reversed by NCOA4 overexpression, establishing a direct functional relationship between SOAT1 and NCOA4 in ferritin regulation (Fig.  A-C). Immunofluorescence studies further confirmed that NCOA4 overexpression neutralized SOAT1 silencing-induced reduction in LC3B-FTH1 and LAMP2-FTH1 co-localization (Fig.  D, E), providing visual evidence that SOAT1 modulated ferritinophagic flux through NCOA4. Subsequent biochemical analyses revealed that NCOA4 upregulation counteracted the protective effects of SOAT1 knockdown by restoring intracellular Fe²⁺ levels (Fig.  F), increasing lipid ROS and MDA levels (Fig.  G-I), and elevating LDH release (Fig.  J). Consistently, NCOA4 overexpression abolished SOAT1 deficiency-mediated GPX4 preservation (Fig.  K, L). Functional assays (CCK-8 and PI staining) showed that NCOA4 upregulation effectively abolished SOAT1 knockdown-induced improvements in cell viability and survival (Fig.  M-O). Additionally, transmission electron microscopy provided ultrastructural evidence that SOAT1 silencing alleviated ISO-induced mitochondrial crumpling and mitochondrial cristae reduction, whereas NCOA4 overexpression aggravated mitochondrial injury (Fig.  P).
 

## NAT10 mediated the ac4C modification of SOAT1 mRNA
To elucidate the upstream regulatory mechanisms controlling SOAT1 expression, we investigated the potential role of N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) RNA modification. We employed PACES (Prediction of N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) modification sites in mRNA), a computational tool for predicting ac4C modification sites in mRNAs, and identified the presence of ac4C modifications on SOAT1 transcripts (Fig. A), suggesting post-transcriptional regulation potential. To experimentally validate this prediction, we performed ac4C RNA immunoprecipitation followed by qPCR (RIP-qPCR), demonstrating that NAT10 (the known writer enzyme of ac4C modifications) directly regulated SOAT1 mRNA acetylation levels, as evidenced by decreased ac4C modification upon NAT10 silencing and increased modification with NAT10 overexpression in cardiomyocytes (Fig. B). Since ac4C modifications are known to enhance mRNA stability and translation [ ], we next examined SOAT1 mRNA stability following NAT10 knockdown, observing significantly reduced stability (Fig. C). Finally, we confirmed the functional consequences of NAT10-mediated ac4C modification by showing that NAT10 overexpression increased both SOAT1 mRNA and protein levels, while NAT10 knockdown produced the opposite effect (Fig. D-F).
 

## Knockdown of NAT10 suppressed isoproterenol induced ferroautophagy and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes
To investigate whether NAT10 mediates ISO-induced cardiomyocyte injury through the SOAT1-ferritinophagy axis, we performed comprehensive functional analyses. Western blotting assays demonstrated that ISO treatment significantly upregulated SOAT1 and NCOA4 while downregulating FTH1 protein levels, and these effects were markedly attenuated by NAT10 knockdown (Fig.  A-D), establishing NAT10 as an upstream regulator of the ferritinophagy pathway. Immunofluorescence analysis further confirmed that NAT10 silencing suppressed ISO-induced enhancement of LC3B-FTH1 and LAMP2-FTH1 co-localization (Fig.  E, F), linking NAT10 to autophagic ferritin degradation. Subsequent biochemical assays revealed that NAT10 deficiency protected against ISO-induced iron overload (reduced Fe²⁺ accumulation, Fig.  G), oxidative stress (decreased lipid ROS and MDA levels, Fig.  H-J), and membrane damage (attenuated LDH release, Fig.  K). Importantly, NAT10 knockdown increased GPX4 expression in ISO-stimulated cardiomyocytes (Fig.  L, M). Functional validation through CCK-8 and PI staining demonstrated that NAT10 silencing significantly improved cardiomyocyte viability and reduced cell death under ISO stress (Fig.  N-P).
 

## Knockdown of SOAT1 alleviated pressure overload-induced heart failure through downregulating NCOA4 expression
To elucidate the functional interplay between NCOA4 and SOAT1 in pressure overload-induced heart failure, we performed in vivo analyses. Echocardiographic assessment revealed that SOAT1 deficiency significantly improved cardiac function in TAC mice, as evidenced by reduced ventricular dimensions (LVIDd, LVIDs), decreased LV mass and LV weight-to-tibia length ratio, and improved FS, while NCOA4 overexpression abolished these protective effects (Fig.  A-E). Molecular analysis demonstrated that SOAT1 knockdown reduced NCOA4 and increased FTH1 protein levels in TAC hearts, and these effects were reversed by NCOA4 overexpression, confirming their regulatory relationship in ferritinophagy regulation (Fig.  F-H). Histological examination showed that NCOA4 upregulation blocked SOAT1 deficiency-induced reduction in cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area (Fig.  I, J), attenuation of cardiac fibrosis (Fig.  I, K), and downregulation of fibrotic markers (Col1a2 and Col3a1, Fig.  L, M). Furthermore, NCOA4 overexpression reversed SOAT1 knockdown-mediated suppression of cardiac stress markers (Nppa and Nppb, Fig.  N, O).
 

## Discussion
Several studies have demonstrated the involvement of sterol O-acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1) in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases. Notably, decreased SOAT1 methylation levels were observed in peripheral blood leukocytes from patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) compared to healthy controls, with lower methylation status correlating with an elevated CHD risk [ ]. In ApoE−/− mice, SOAT1 knockout ameliorated high-fat diet-induced atherosclerotic lesions by reducing collagen deposition, oxidative stress, and inflammatory factor expression within plaques [ ]. Our previous research further revealed that piRNA-6426 attenuated coronary artery occlusion-induced heart failure in rats by suppressing SOAT1 expression through enhanced DNMT3B-mediated methylation of the SOAT1 promoter [ ]. In the current study, we observed upregulated SOAT1 protein levels in cardiac tissues of mice subjected to 3 weeks of pressure overload. Genetic knockdown of SOAT1 significantly alleviated pressure overload-induced left ventricular hypertrophy and systolic dysfunction, suggesting SOAT1 inhibition may represent a novel strategy for preventing pressure overload cardiomyopathy in clinical conditions like hypertension and aortic stenosis. Emerging evidence suggests a close relationship between cholesterol metabolism and ferroptosis [ , ]. As a key regulator of cholesteryl ester formation, SOAT1 has been shown to modulate iron homeostasis in glioma cells by regulating SLC40A1 expression, thereby suppressing ferroptosis [ ]. Our findings demonstrated that SOAT1 deficiency conferred robust cardioprotection by attenuating both pressure overload-induced ferritinophagy and ferroptosis in myocardial tissues in vivo, and isoproterenol-triggered ferritinophagy and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes in vitro. This protective effect is mediated through inhibition of autophagy-dependent ferritin degradation, consequently reducing intracellular iron accumulation and lipid peroxidation. These results position SOAT1 as a promising target for developing cardioprotective therapies against ferroptosis-driven myocardial damage, particularly in conditions where iron overload contributes to disease progression.
Emerging as a distinct regulated cell death pathway, ferroptosis is characterized by iron dysregulation and lipid peroxidation driven by ROS accumulation, playing increasingly recognized roles in cardiovascular pathophysiology [ , – ]. Central to this process is iron homeostasis, where ferritin normally sequesters excess iron [ ], but NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy disrupts this balance by degrading ferritin and releasing free iron to promote ferroptosis [ , ]. Notably, recent work has demonstrated that the inhibition of NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy confers cardioprotection against hemodynamic stress [ ], highlighting this pathway’s therapeutic potential. We observed significant increases in FTH1 and autophagosome/lysosome co-localization, iron overload, and lipid peroxidation in pressure-overloaded hearts, conclusively demonstrating ferritinophagy-ferroptosis activation in cardiac remodeling. This ferroptosis activation was similarly observed in ISO-stimulated cardiomyocytes through autophagy-dependent ferritin degradation. Crucially, we identified SOAT1 as a novel regulator of this pathway, with its knockdown protecting against ferritinophagy and ferroptosis in both models. Given the critical role of NCOA4 in the induction of ferroautophagy, we hypothesized that SOAT1 may act by regulating NCOA4 expression levels. Building on known roles of SOAT1 in protein acetylation (e.g., SOAT1 acetylates GNPAT at K128 and promoting lipid metabolism [ ]), we found that SOAT1 physically interacts with NCOA4 to enhance NCOA4 acetylation and stabilize NCOA4 protein, thereby promoting ferritinophagy. The functional significance of this interaction was confirmed when NCOA4 overexpression reversed the protective effects of SOAT1 knockdown both in vitro and in pressure-overloaded hearts. These findings position the SOAT1-NCOA4 axis as a promising therapeutic target, particularly for iron-overload associated cardiac conditions, where modulating NCOA4 acetylation could potentially prevent pathological iron release while maintaining essential iron homeostasis.
Emerging evidence highlights the critical role of NAT10-mediated ac4C mRNA acetylation in cardiovascular pathogenesis through multiple molecular mechanisms. Previous studies have demonstrated that this post-transcriptional modification contributes to disease progression by regulating key cellular processes. PiRNA HAAPIR was found to modulate cardiomyocyte apoptosis by targeting NAT10-mediated ac4C acetylation of transcription factor EC (Tfec) mRNA [ ]. Elevated NAT10 expression promoted cardiomyocyte ferroptosis during myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury through ac4C modification of Mybbp1a mRNA, leading to p53 activation and SLC7A11 suppression [ ]. Furthermore, NAT10 was shown to drive myocardial hypertrophy and fibrosis by enhancing the stability and translational efficiency of CD47 and ROCK2 mRNAs via ac4C modification [ ]. Building upon these findings, our current study revealed an additional mechanism whereby NAT10 regulates cardiac pathophysiology, and we found that NAT10 mediated ac4C modification of SOAT1 mRNA, thereby enhancing its stability and translation efficiency in cardiomyocytes. This observation aligns with prior reports showing NAT10 knockdown reduces SOAT1 mRNA acetylation and stability in cancer cells [ ]. Importantly, we demonstrated that NAT10 silencing effectively attenuated ISO-induced ferritinophagy and ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes, suggesting that targeting the NAT10-SOAT1 axis may represent a novel therapeutic strategy for cardiac diseases characterized by iron dysregulation and lipid peroxidation.
In conclusions, our study revealed that SOAT1 knockdown protected against cardiac dysfunction in pressure-overloaded mice and ISO-induced cardiomyocyte death. Mechanistically, NAT10 stabilized SOAT1 mRNA via ac4C modification, while SOAT1 enhanced NCOA4 acetylation to promote ferritinophagy and ferroptosis. Our data indicated that targeting SOAT1 and ferritinophagy might be an effective alternative therapeutic strategy for HF.
