## Abstract
The tumor microenvironment plays a critical role in the progression and metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), characterized by its immunosuppressive nature. Identifying the mechanisms that contribute to the remodeling of this environment is essential for developing therapeutic strategies. Heat shock factor 2-binding protein (HSF2BP) is implicated in tumor proliferation and immune evasion, but the mechanisms by which HSF2BP exerts these effects remain poorly understood. This study investigates the role of HSF2BP in modulating Basonuclin 1 (BNC1) expression and subsequent immune responses in LUAD. Matched tumor and adjacent normal tissue samples from 30 LUAD patients were collected between January 2023 and June 2024. Using lentiviral transduction, HSF2BP and BNC1 were overexpressed or knocked down in LUAD cell lines. Gene and protein expression were quantified by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and Western blotting. We analyzed the immunological profile of LUAD tumors and assessed the interaction between HSF2BP and the BNC1/Transforming Growth Factor Beta (TGF-β)/SMAD3 signaling pathway through in vitro and in vivo experiments. Immune profiling included flow cytometry for Natural Killer (NK) cells and ELISA for cytokine analysis (IL-2, IL-4, IL-10, INF-γ, and TNF-α). In vivo studies involved subcutaneous tumorigenesis in BALB/c nude mice. Immunohistochemistry and co-immunoprecipitation were used to validate protein interactions and to assess remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment. HSF2BP expression was markedly higher in LUAD tissues in matched adjacent normal tissues, whereas BNC1 expression was significantly reduced. Overexpression of HSF2BP in LUAD cells (H1299) enhanced proliferation, reduced the proportion of NK cells, decreased levels of IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α, and increased levels of IL-4 and IL-10. Conversely, knockdown of HSF2BP in A549 cells reduced proliferation and restored the proportion of NK cells and levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines. In vivo studies using HSF2BP-overexpressing mice confirmed these findings, demonstrating increased tumor volumes and altered cytokine profiles. Molecular assays revealed that HSF2BP binds directly to BNC1, with the C-terminal hydrophobic domain being essential for this interaction, thereby modulations in TGF-β and SMAD3 signaling pathways. HSF2BP significantly promotes LUAD progression by modulating the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis and reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment. Targeting the HSF2BP-BNC1 interaction can provide novel therapeutic strategies for enhancing immune responses against LUAD.

## Introduction
Lung cancer is a prevalent type of primary carcinoma targeting the lungs . Most types originate from the mucous epithelium of the bronchi and represent the most common cancer globally. High morbidity and mortality remain global challenges in lung cancer . Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the most common type of lung cancer, is increasing worldwide and affecting progressively younger patients . The development of LUAD mainly results from the synergistic effects of genetic factors, environmental exposures, and genomic alterations . Currently, therapeutic modalities for lung cancer include surgical intervention, chemotherapy, radiation, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. Nevertheless, existing platinum-based chemotherapy regimens have not demonstrated significant survival benefits in advanced LUAD, underscoring the need to identify novel molecular targets.
Tumor microenvironment (TME), categorized into immune and non-immune components, plays a significant role in carcinogenesis . The immunological condition, particularly cellular immunity mediated by NK cells and T cells, heavily influences , heavily influences tumor progression. NK cells not only regulate immune function by producing lymphokines and using other mechanisms, but they also possess the ability to possess innate, non-specific cytotoxicity against tumor cells, which contributes to anti-tumor immunity and restricts tumor dissemination , .
Heat shock proteins (HSPs) are small proteins rapidly synthesized when cells are exposed to temperatures slightly above physiological levels when cells are exposed to temperatures slightly above physiological levels . Heat shock transcription factor 2 (HSF2) can regulate the transcriptional expression of HSP25, HSP40, HSP110, and particularly the major heat shock protein HSP70 , . Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), The Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx), and Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) databases revealed that HSF2 is aberrantly expressed in multiple tumor types . HSF2 expression is strongly correlated with several immunological markers in liver cancer tissues. Knockdown of HSF2 markedly reduces proliferation, migration, invasion, and colony formation of liver cancer cells . The present study provides evidence that HSF2 has a substantial influence on the remodeling and progression of the tumor immune microenvironment. The HSF2 binding protein (HSF2BP) gene encodes two 334-amino-acid polypeptides and may interact with HSF2 to augment its transcriptional activity . Aberrant expression of HSF2BP has been detected in various tumor types, including breast and ovarian cancers, such as breast cancer and ovarian cancer , . The abnormally expressed HSF2BP can increase the genomic instability by inhibiting homologous recombination during interstrand DNA cross-link repair, and then enhance the malignant proliferation of cells .
Basonuclin (BNC1) plays a function in regulating gene transcription and is involved in controlling cell development and differentiation . Previous studies have found that HSF2BP binds to BNC1 to regulate its transcriptional activity . BNC1 is a negative regulatory factor of the transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β)/SMAD3 signaling pathway . It is crucial in the processes of development and growth and has a significant association with the initiation and progression of malignancies . The TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling system affects many aspects of life activities . At the cellular level, it can regulate cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, adhesion, and stroma generation. In human tumors, it is one of the most commonly altered signaling pathways. During the advanced phase of tumor development, TGF-β/SMAD3, acting as a facilitator of tumor growth, enhances the growth of tumors by driving immunosuppression and extracellular matrix production . In this study, we aimed to elucidate the function of HSF2BP in LUAD and its potential interactions with BNC1 and the TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis. By investigating both in vitro and in vivo models, we sought to determine how HSF2BP may influence tumor cell proliferation and immune modulation. More specifically, we hypothesized that HSF2BP could remodel the immune microenvironment through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling pathway, thereby promoting tumor progression and immune evasion.
To address these hypotheses, we collected clinical samples of LUAD tissues and adjacent normal tissues from patients and employed various molecular and cell biology techniques. Additionally, we assessed the biological impacts of HSF2BP overexpression and knockdown in cell lines and a nude mouse subcutaneous tumor model. Our comprehensive approach aimed to provide a deeper understanding of the molecular interplay between HSF2BP and BNC1, and how these interactions drive oncogenesis and immune suppression. This work opens new avenues for targeted therapies designed to disrupt the HSF2BP–BNC1 axis, potentially halting tumor growth and enhancing antitumor immunity in LUAD patients.

## Materials and methods

## Clinical sample collection
Between January 2023 and June 2024, matched tumor and adjacent normal tissue samples from 30 LUAD patients were collected at the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University. All diagnoses were confirmed by histopathological examination. No patient had received any form of radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or other biological treatment before surgery. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (No. D-2023026). All patients/participants provided written informed consent. All methods are carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

## Cell culture and cell transduction
Following transduction with lentiviruses containing vector, HSF2BP-overexpressing (HSF2BP-OE), BNC1-overexpressing (BNC1-OE), sh-NC, HSF2BP knockdown (sh-HSF2BP), and BNC1 knockdown (sh-BNC1), the media was removed 12–20 h post-transduction. Cells were obtained from Wuhan Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. and cultured at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Cells were passaged upon reaching approximately 80% confluency. Following cell attachment (24 h after seeding), 10 µL of puromycin was added to each well. After approximately 2 weeks of puromycin selection, stable cell lines were successfully established. The stable cell lines were passaged for subsequent cellular and animal experimental use. The experimental groups were: empty vector control (vector), HSF2BP-OE, BNC1-OE, HSF2BP-OE + BNC1-OE, sh-NC, sh-HSF2BP, sh-BNC1, sh-HSF2BP + sh-BNC1. Human peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC, Procell, CP-H158) were cultured in complete media (Procell, CM-H158). Human PBMCs were utilized for ​​in vitro co-culture experiments​​ and to establish ​​humanized PBMC mouse models​​ via tail vein injection in nude mice at a dosage of 10 × 106 cells per mouse.

## Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8)
Cells were seeded into 96-well plates, with multiple replicate wells per group. At 24, 48, 72, and 96 h post-seeding, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well. After a 1 h incubation at 37 ℃, absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader.

## Real-time quantitative polymerasechain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted from each group of cells or tissues using TRIzol reagent. The PCR amplification process began with the denaturation of the reaction mixture at a temperature of 94 ℃ for a duration of 3 min. This was followed by 30 cycles, each consisting of denaturation at 94 ℃ for 30 s, annealing at 58 ℃ for 30 s, and extension at 72 ℃ for 1 min. A final extension was performed at 72 ℃ for 10 min. With GAPDH as the internal reference, the primer sequences of each gene are as follows: HSF2BP: forward: 5’-GAAAGAGGACCTGGAACGG-3’; reverse: 5’-CCACCACACGAAAGGGATAA-3’; GAPDH: forward: 5’-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTCA-3’; reverse: 5’-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3’.

## Western blotting (WB)
Cellular proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer, and protein concentrations were determined with a BCA protein assay kit. 30 µg of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and then transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBST at room temperature for 1 h, then, then incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with the following primary antibodies (Abcam; all diluted 1:1 000 in 5% BSA/TBST): HSF2BP, BNC1, TGF-β, SMAD3, and GAPDH. After three washes with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using ECL reagents, and images were captured on a multifunctional imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

## Percentage of NK cells
NK cells were quantified as follows: 0.5 mL of culture medium was collected for total cell counting, and the suspension was adjusted to 1 × 106 cells per mL. Then, 100 µL of t this suspension was transferred to flow cytometry tube and stained with CD3-APC and CD56-PE monoclonal antibodies (dilutions as per manufacturer). Tubes were incubated at room temperature in the dark for 15 min, washed twice with PBS and resuspended in 100 µL of PBS. Flow cytometry was performed to determine the percentage of NK cells. Total and NK cell counts were calculated based on the 0.5 mL sampling volume and the measured NK cell percentage.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
Cconcentrations of IL-2, IL-4, IL-10, INF-γ, and TNF-α were measured using a double-antibody sandwich ELISA kit (Beyotime, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, standards or samples (culture supernatant or serum) were added to Microplate wells and incubated at 37℃ for 90 min. After washing three times, biotinylated detection antibodies were added and incubated at 37℃ for 60 min. Wells were washed again, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated streptavidin at 37℃ for 30 min. After five washes, substrate solution was added and incubated at 37 ℃ for 15 min. The reaction was stopped with stop solution, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm. Cytokine concentrations were derived from standard curves.

## Subcutaneous tumorigenesis in nude mice
40 3–4 weeks-old BALB/c nude mice were weighed and randomly assigned to experimental groups. Then, collect the tumor cells that have been previously prepared and are in the logarithmic growth phase. Homogenize and equally distribute the cells to about 2 × 106 cells/ml. Simultaneously, cool the cell solution on ice to decrease the cellular metabolism, then inject it subcutaneously into the mice using an inoculation amount of 100 µL. Next, use iodophor cotton balls to disinfect the underside of the mouse, starting from the neck and going down to the groin and the posterior axillary line. Repeat this process twice. The skin of the neck and back of the naked mouse is pinched using the left thumb and index finger, while the tail of the mouse is held by the little finger. Tumor cells are then inoculated subcutaneously in the posterior region of the axilla of the front leg. Once all the cells have been implanted, diligently monitor the condition of the mice and promptly identify any instances of mouse mortality. Approximately one week after inoculation, subcutaneous lumps become visible. Utilize a vernier caliper to measure and document the dimensions of the tumor, including its longest and shortest sections, every three days, twice a week. Additionally, record the body weight of the mice throughout these measurements. The formula for calculating the volume of a tumor is 1/2 multiplied by the product of the long axis (A) and the square of the short axis (B). Lentivirus infection was used to overexpress HSF2BP or knockdown HSF2BP in the H1299 cell line respectively, and then injected subcutaneously into mice through tumorigenesis injection, and the tumor images of mice.

## Statement for animal ethics
The animal study was reviewed and approval by the Animal Experimentation Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (No. 2023BC017). All surgical interventions are performed under anesthesia (inhalation of isoflurane). Euthanasia was performed by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium at a dosage of 150 mg/kg. all animal protocols adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines (https://arriveguidelines.org) and AVMA Euthanasia Guidelines (https://www.avma.org). All methods are carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

## Immunohistochemistry
The expression of HSF2BP, BNC1, TGF-β, and SMAD3 in tumor tissue was detected. Paraffin sections were dewaxed and hydrated, and endogenous catalase was removed using 3% H2O2. Primary antibody (HSF2BP, BNC1, TGF-β, and SMAD3) monoclonal antibody (1:50) was applied and incubated overnight at 4℃, while PBS served as the blank control. The sections were stained with DAB, washed with distilled water, counterstained with hematoxylin, and rinsed again with distilled water. Subsequently, the sections were rapidly dehydrated using 75%, 85%, 95% ethanol, and anhydrous ethanol, followed by clarification with xylene. Finally, the samples were mounted on a resin mount. Under a light microscope, positive staining was observed as a granular deposition of brown-yellow in the designated areas.

## Co-immunoprecipitation
In order to create the plasmids for HSF2BP, the coding sequence of it (which represents amino acids 1-338) was amplified using the forward primer 5’-GCTGAGGCGGCGCTATG-3’ and the reverse primer 5’-AACATTACAGTCCAGGGGC-3’. The amplified sequence was then inserted into the pFLAG-CMV-4 (Sigma) and pEGFP-N1 vectors (Clontech) through subcloning. Multiple deletion segments of HSF2BP containing the FLAG epitope were created using a similar method. This resulted in the production of Flag-HSF2BPΔ1 (1-260), Flag-HSF2BPΔ2 (1-126), Flag-HSF2BPΔ3 (1–77), Flag-HSF2BPΔ4 (78–260), and Flag-HSF2BPΔ5 (78–338). After a period of forty-eight h, the cells were collected using NP-40 lysis buffer. Afterwards, the cells’ active agent was mixed with anti-FLAG M2 agarose resin (Sigma) or anti-GFP antibody (Santa Cruz) and left to spin overnight at a temperature of 4 ℃. Next, the resin underwent five washes using a 1% NP-40 lysis buffer. Finally, the isolated protein complexes were examined by WB analysis using the appropriate antibodies.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed utilizing GraphPad Prism (version 10) and SPSS (version 21.0). Data were presented as the mean ± SD. For normally distributed datasets, comparisons between two groups were made using the Student’s t-test. Conversely, the Mann-Whitney test was applied to non-normally distributed data. Multiple group comparisons involved one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett’s post hoc test. A P value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. In animal experiments, each group had a sample size of at least 4, while cell experiments were conducted in triplicate or more. Significance levels were indicated as follows: P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and **P < 0.001.

## Results
Expression Levels of HSF2BP, BNC1, TGF-β, and SMAD3 in Patients with LUAD.
Clinical characteristics of 30 LUAD patients were provided in Table  . Matched tumor and adjacent normal tissue samples from 30 LUAD patients were analyzed. RT-qPCR results demonstrated that HSF2BP mRNA was significantly higher in tumor tissues than in adjacent normal tissues (Fig.  A). WB analysis further showed marked upregulation HSF2BP, TGF-β, and SMAD3 proteins, and a concomitant downregulation of BNC1, in tumor tissues versus adjacent normal tissues (Fig.  B-E and Supplementary Fig. 1).
 

## HSF2BP promotes the proliferation of LUAD cells through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis
We assessed the mRNA expression of HSF2BP in various cell lines including the human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B, small cell lung cancer (SCLC): H524, H209, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC): A549 (adenocarcinoma), H1299 (large cell carcinoma) using RT-qPCR. Our findings indicated that the expression level of HSF2BP was comparatively elevated in the A549 and H1299 cells (Fig.  A). Subsequent WB showed a significant increase in the protein expression levels of BNC1, TGF-β, and SMAD3 in cancer cells compared to BEAS-2B (Fig.  B-F). To investigate the biological function of HSF2BP in lung cancer cells, we increased HSF2BP expression in H1299 cells and decreased its expression in A549 cells, verified by WB (Fig.  G-H). CCK-8 assays demonstrated a considerable increase in cell proliferation for HSF2BP-overexpressing (HSF2BP-OE) cells compared to vector, and a lower proliferation rate in HSF2BP knockdown (sh-HSF2BP) cells compared to sh-NC (Fig.  I-J). Additionally, through rescue experiment, WB analysis revealed no change in HSF2BP protein levels in BNC1-overexpressing (BNC1-OE) cells compared to vector, but a significant increase in BNC1 and a decrease in TGF-β and SMAD3 protein levels. In HSF2BP-OE + BNC1-OE cells, HSF2BP protein expression remained unchanged compared to HSF2BP-OE cells, while BNC1 significantly increased and TGF-β and SMAD3 decreased. In A549 cells, sh-BNC1 did not change HSF2BP protein levels, but significantly reduced BNC1 and increased TGF-β and SMAD3. In sh-HSF2BP + sh-BNC1 cells, HSF2BP protein expression remained unchanged compared to sh-HSF2BP cells, but BNC1 expression was reduced, and TGF-β and SMAD3 increased (Fig.  K-O). CCK-8 assays showed lower cell proliferation in BNC1-OE compared to vector, and in HSF2BP-OE + BNC1-OE compared to HSF2BP-OE (Fig.  P). Proliferation in sh-BNC1 was higher than sh-NC, and in sh-HSF2BP + sh-BNC1 was higher than sh-HSF2BP alone, confirming our findings (Fig.  Q).
 

## HSF2BP remodels the immune microenvironment of LUAD through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis
In H1299 cells, the proportion of NK cells significantly reduced in the HSF2BP-OE compared to vector, but increased in BNC1-OE and HSF2BP-OE + BNC1-OE (Fig.  A). In A549 cells, NK cells increased significantly in sh-HSF2BP compared to sh-NC, while sh-BNC1 reduced NK cells. In sh-HSF2BP + sh-BNC1, NK cells reduced compared to sh-HSF2BP (Fig.  B). Typically, IL-4 and IL-10 have a suppressive function in the immune response against tumors . These cytokines have a tendency to facilitate the control and equilibrium of the immune system, and sometimes diminish the severity of the immunological response, thereby aiding cancer cells in evading the immune system’s onslaught . TNF-α can directly induce tumor cell apoptosis, IFN-γ can enhance antigen presentation and activate T cells, while IL-2 mainly acts on enhancing the proliferation and activity of them – . ELISA findings in H1299 cells showed reduced levels of IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α, and increased IL-4 and IL-10 in HSF2BP-OE compared to vector. In BNC1-OE compared to vector, IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α increased and IL-4 and IL-10 decreased. In HSF2BP-OE + BNC1-OE compared to HSF2BP-OE, IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α increased and IL-4 and IL-10 decreased (Supplementary Fig. 2A-E). ELISA results in A549 cells demonstrated increased IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α in sh-HSF2BP compared to sh-NC, while IL-4 and IL-10 were decreased. In sh-BNC1 compared to sh-HSF2BP, IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α were reduced and IL-4 and IL-10 increased. Similar results were observed for sh-HSF2BP + sh-BNC1 compared to sh-HSF2BP (Supplementary Fig. 2F-J). These findings suggest that HSF2BP modifies the immunological microenvironment in lung cancer via the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling pathway.
 

## In vivo validation of HSF2BP’s role in remodeling the immune microenvironment of LUAD through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signalling axis
To validate cellular findings in vivo, we analyzed tumor volume and weight in mice. The tumor volume and weight significantly increased in the HSF2BP-OE group compared to vector and reduced in sh-HSF2BP-treated mice compared to sh-NC (Fig.  A-D). Immunohistochemical and WB analyses revealed that HSF2BP, TGF-β, and SMAD3 protein levels were higher in HSF2BP-OE compared to vector, while BNC1 levels were reduced. Conversely, in sh-HSF2BP, levels of HSF2BP, TGF-β, and SMAD3 decreased, and BNC1 increased (Fig.  E-I, Supplementary Fig. 3A-E). ELISA analyses showed that sh-HSF2BP treatment significantly decreased IL‐10 and increased TNF-α levels compared with sh‐NC, whereas HSF2BP-OE led to elevated IL‐10 and IL‐4 but reduced TNF-α relative to vector controls (Fig.  J-N). Moreover, sh-HSF2BP treatment significantly decreased IL-4 while increasing both IFN-γ and IL-2 levels compared with sh-NC, whereas HSF2BP-OE further reduced IFN-γ and IL-2 relative to vector (Fig.  O-S). NK cell proportions were significantly reduced in HSF2BP-OE compared to vector and increased sh-HSF2BP compared to sh-NC (Supplementary Fig. 4). These results support our cellular-level findings.
 

## HSF2BP binds to BNC1 to reshape the immune microenvironment of LUAD
To understand the molecular mechanism of the impact of HSF2BP on the immune microenvironment, we reviewed pertinent literature suggesting that HSF2BP can interact with BNC1. RT-qPCR analysis confirmed the efficiency of BNC1 manipulations, in H1299 cells transduced with a BNC1 overexpression construct, BNC1 mRNA was elevated, whereas in A549 cells infected with sh-BNC1, BNC1 transcript levels were reduced relative to sh-NC (Supplementary Fig. 5). COIP assays confirmed this interaction in H1299 and A549 cell lines (Fig.  A). Subsequently, we created a deletion mutant of HSF2BP (Fig.  B). HSF2BP protein tagged with a Flag tag or its deletion mutant was introduced into the H1299 and A549 cell lines, respectively, and then co-cultured with NK cells. The findings indicated that the C-terminal hydrophobic region of HSF2BP is essential for its interaction with BNC1 (Fig.  C-D). WB analysis showed that in truncated HSF2BP (HSF2BPΔ2), HSF2BP, TGF-β, and SMAD3 levels were reduced, while BNC1 increased compared to full-length HSF2BP (Fig.  E-M). Cell proliferation assays revealed that HSF2BPΔ2 significantly reduced proliferation compared to full length HSF2BP (Fig.  N-O). In HSF2BPΔ2, ELISA results showed increased IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α, and decreased IL-4 and IL-10 (Supplementary Fig. 6A-J), along with a higher proportion of NK cells (Supplementary Fig. 6K-L). These findings suggest that the C-terminal hydrophobic domain of HSF2BP interacts with BNC1, leading to the alteration of lung cancer’s immune microenvironment.
 

## Discussion
At present, comprehensive treatment—primarily radiotherapy and chemotherapy—is mostly used to control tumor progression and reduce recurrence . However, patient responses to these therapies are heterogeneous and concurrent chemoradiation often causes severe adverse reactions, resulting in variable prognoses. Tumor development and immune microenvironment remodeling are intricate regulated by numerous molecular factors. Research has shown that a multitude of genes and proteins have a role in controlling the immunological microenvironment of tumors . Identifying prognostic markers of the tumor immune microenvironment in LUAD is therefore crucial.
HSF2BP binds directly to HSF2 and was first identified in 1998 by yeast two-hybrid screening of a human testicular cDNA library . Despite its early discovery, HSF2BP’s functions remain poorly understood. Previous research has shown that the HSF2BP protein can interact with both BRCA2 and BNC1 proteins, and it fulfills distinct functions in various cellular processes. Nevertheless, the precise biological function of HSF2BP in lung cancer remains uncertain. Our study elucidates the role of HSF2BP in LUAD and its influence on tumor proliferation and immune microenvironment modulation through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis. Using a combination of in vitro and in vivo methodologies, we demonstrated that HSF2BP significantly augments LUAD cell proliferation while concurrently reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment. Our initial findings revealed significant overexpression of HSF2BP in LUAD tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues, aligning with previous studies linking HSF2BP with cancer progression. Conversely, the expression of BNC1 was notably reduced in cancer tissues. This inverse relationship between HSF2BP and BNC1 suggests a regulatory interplay, possibly facilitating tumor growth and immune evasion.
Cellular signaling pathways include those regulating cell growth, apoptosis, cancer cell invasion and metastasis, and interactions within the tumor microenvironment . BNC1 protein is a transcription factor that specifically binds to zinc finger proteins and is found on chromosome 15. The first discovery of BNC1 occurred in human epidermal keratinocytes that were cultivated in a laboratory setting. Subsequent research have further shown the strong association between BNC1 and many types of cancer . For instance, TGF-β has a regulatory role in breast cancer by inhibiting epithelial differentiation. BNC1 functions as a suppressor of the TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling pathway. The TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling pathway is crucial in the processes of development and growth, and it is intimately associated with the initiation and progression of malignancies . TGF-β is a family of growth factors with diverse biological functions. In mammals, three isoforms—TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3—bind to TGF-β receptors, thereby phosphorylating receptor-regulated SMAD family. This signaling cascade regulates key tumor-related processes including cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and extracellular matrix production . The overexpression of HSF2BP correlated with increased lung cancer cell proliferation, as evidenced by CCK-8 assays. Notably, HSF2BP knockdown significantly suppressed cell proliferation, affirming its oncogenic role. Further molecular analysis indicated that overexpressed HSF2BP resulted in decreased BNC1 and increased TGF-β and SMAD3 levels, suggesting that HSF2BP drives tumor progression through the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 axis. BNC1 overexpression mitigated these effects, confirming BNC1’s potential as a tumor suppressor.
We explored the impact of HSF2BP on the immune microenvironment, revealing reduced NK cell proportions and altered cytokine profiles in HSF2BP-overexpressing cells—further supporting its role in immune evasion. The observed decrease in IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α, combined with increased IL-4 and IL-10, aligns with an immunosuppressive phenotype conducive to tumor growth. Despite these promising results, several limitations should be considered. Our study included a limited number of lung adenocarcinoma samples (n = 30), which may affect the generalizability of the findings. In future work, we intend to extend our mechanistic understanding of HSF2BP-driven immune remodeling by directly perturbing the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD-3 axis. Specifically, we will employ both pharmacological inhibitors (e.g., SB-431542 for TGF-β receptor blockade and selective SMAD3 antagonists) and siRNA-mediated knockdown of BNC1, TGF-β, and SMAD-3 in our established xenograft models. These studies will be complemented by time-course and dose-response WB to quantify pathway activation, as well as in vivo NK-cell cytotoxicity assays to link signaling perturbations with functional immune outcomes. Moreover, future studies must validate these findings in larger patient cohorts. Although our mouse models supported the underlying hypotheses, they cannot fully recapitulate the complexity of human tumors. Incorporating additional preclinical systems—such as patient-derived xenografts and genetically engineered mouse models—could yield deeper mechanistic insights. Moreover, because this work focused primarily on NK cells and a narrow cytokine panel, expanding analyses to include other tumor-associated immune populations (e.g., T cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells) via single-cell RNA sequencing or comprehensive immunophenotyping would further clarify HSF2BP’s role in tumor–immune crosstalk. Finally, the therapeutic potential of disrupting HSF2BP interaction with BNC1, remains untested; future research should evaluate the effects of HSF2BP inhibition on tumor growth and immune modulation in clinical settings.
In summary, HSF2BP exhibits elevated expression in LUAD. This effect is likely mediated by the interaction of HSF2BP’s C-terminal domain with BNC1, resulting in suppressed BNC1 expression. Consequently, the protein expression level of TGF-β/SMAD3 is enhanced, leading to the proliferation of LUAD. Additionally, this process inhibits the proportion of NK cells, thereby influencing the regulation of the immune microenvironment (Fig.  ). Our comprehensive analysis underscores HSF2BP’s pivotal role in lung adenocarcinoma progression via the BNC1/TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling axis. The modulation of the immune microenvironment by HSF2BP suggests potential therapeutic strategies targeting this pathway to enhance anti-tumor immunity. Future research should investigate the clinical applicability of HSF2BP and BNC1 as biomarkers and therapeutic targets, with a focus on elucidating the complete signaling networks involved.
