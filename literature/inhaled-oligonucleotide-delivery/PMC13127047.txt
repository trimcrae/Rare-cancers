## Abstract

## Background
Cardiac fibrosis is a pivotal pathological process driving adverse cardiac remodeling and a defining feature of end-stage heart disease. Nicotine, a principal constituent of tobacco products, is now recognized as an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease. However, its direct effects on cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) biology and the molecular mechanisms underlying nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis remain incompletely understood.

## Methods
Primary CFs and a rat model of nicotine exposure were used to access the pro-fibrotic effects of nicotine. Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) and cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) were employed to identify the cellular targets of nicotine. Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP), RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) were used to quantify m⁶A modification and microRNA biogenesis. miR-125b-5p overexpression or inhibition, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A2/B1 (HNRNPA2B1, abbreviated as A2B1 in all figures) silencing, and pharmacological inhibition with cinacalcet HCl were performed both in vitro and in vivo to evaluate the impact of this signaling axis on nicotine-induced fibrotic phenotypes. Collagen deposition, CFs proliferation, and activated transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)/Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) signaling were assessed by histology, immunoblotting, and immunofluorescence.

## Results
Nicotine promoted CFs proliferation and migration, myofibroblasts (MFs) transformation, and collagen accumulation. HNRNPA2B1 was identified as a potential binding target of nicotine. Mechanistically, nicotine up-regulated methyltransferase-like 14 (METTL14), thereby increasing m⁶A methylation of pri-miR-125b. HNRNPA2B1 recognized methylated pri-miR-125b, facilitated its interaction with DiGeorge Syndrome Critical Region 8 (DGCR8), and thereby accelerated miR-125b-5p maturation. Elevated miR-125b-5p suppressed p53 and activated the TGF-β1/MAPK axis, driving cardiac fibrosis. Knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 or treatment with cinacalcet HCl markedly reduced the levels of miR-125b-5p and ameliorated nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis in vitro and in vivo.

## Conclusions
Nicotine induces excessive maturation of miR-125b-5p through an m⁶A-dependent, HNRNPA2B1-mediated mechanism, thereby promoting cardiac fibrosis. Targeting this signaling pathway—either genetically or pharmacologically with cinacalcet HCl—effectively attenuates fibrotic remodeling, providing a novel mechanistic rationale and potential therapeutic strategy management of cardiac fibrosis.

## Introduction
Smoking significantly impacts the cardiovascular system, leading to a substantial burden of morbidity and mortality [ ]. Nicotine, a prominent active component of tobacco, is widely recognized as a major contributor to cardiovascular disease, especially cardiac fibrosis [ ]. This exaggerated fibrotic response can lead to increased ventricular wall stiffness, impaired systolic and diastolic function, ventricular dilation, reduced compliance, dangerous arrhythmia, and ultimately heart failure [ , ]. The health risks and addictive properties of smoking have prompted heightened emphasis on associated health and safety concerns. Although the fibrotic effects of smoking have long been recognized, the mechanisms underlying its contribution to cardiac fibrosis remain poorly understood.
Recent studies indicate that the progression of cardiac fibrosis is influenced by genetic alterations, highlighting the significant role of epigenomics and epigenetic modifications [ ]. Thus, exploration of novel epigenetic regulatory targets hold promise for developing therapeutic strategies against cardiac fibrosis. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is one of the most prevalent internal modifications of eukaryotic messenger RNAs (mRNAs) [ ]. The m6A modification is a dynamic and reversible process regulated by methyltransferases (“writers”), and demethylases (“erasers”), and recognized by binding proteins (“readers”) [ ]. Approximately 25% in the hearts of healthy mice and humans contain m6A RNA methylation. During the progression to heart failure in both mice and humans, changes in m6A RNA methylation exceed those in gene expression, with altered methylation primarily associated with metabolic and regulatory pathways. The demethylase FTO accelerates heart failure progression, resulting in reduced ejection fraction and increased cardiac dilatation [ ]. METTL3 has been identified as a key driver of cardiac fibrosis, regulating pro-fibrotic gene expression through m6A modification. This process drives CFs activation and excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition [ ]. Additionally, the upregulation of WTAP has been shown to promote the proliferation and migration of CFs, thereby contributing to diabetic cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Current antifibrotic therapies remain in the developmental phase. While RNA modification networks have revealed novel therapeutic opportunities, the identification of viable therapeutic targets remains challenging.
HNRNPA2B1 is a member of the heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein (hnRNP) family and participates in multiple processes, including RNA transcription, stabilization, splicing and translation [ ]. HNRNPA2B1 has been implicated in the primary processing of microRNAs (miRNAs). miRNAs are initially transcribed as pri-miRNAs, which are subsequently cleaved into pre-miRNAs by the nuclear microprocessor complex containing RNASEN (Drosha) and DGCR8. Pre-miRNAs are subsequently processed into mature miRNAs, typically 22–25 nucleotides in length [ ]. miRNAs play a role in the development of cardiovascular diseases. It has been reported that miR-125b contributes to cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting anti-fibrotic factors [ ]. Here, we investigated whether HNRNPA2B1 promotes nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis by enhancing the biogenesis of mature miR-125b-5p.
Our research group has previously focused on screening clinically approved drugs for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. Cinacalcet HCl, a calcimimetic agent, has been approved for the treatment of secondary hyperparathyroidism in patients with chronic kidney disease and hypercalcemia in patients with parathyroid carcinoma [ ]. Studies have reported that cinacalcet HCl significantly reduces the incidence of cardiovascular events [ ]. We previously demonstrated that it inhibits the proliferation of CFs. However, it remains unclear whether cinacalcet HCl can alleviate nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. If so, the specific mechanisms by which it exerts this effect (if applicable) and the underlying molecular pathways warrant further investigation.
Here, we report that nicotine induces a profibrotic effect of miR-125b-5p in CFs through an m⁶A-dependent mechanism. Specifically, nicotine stimulation increases m⁶A modification on pri-miR-125b by upregulating METTL14. The RNA-binding protein HNRNPA2B1, upregulated by nicotine, preferentially recognizes m⁶A methylated pri-miR-125b, thereby facilitating its interaction with the Microprocessor component DGCR8 and accelerating the maturation of miR-125b-5p. Excessive miR-125b-5p directly targets p53 mRNA, markedly suppresses p53 protein expression, and concurrently activates the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, thereby promoting cardiac fibrosis. Cinacalcet HCl suppresses the expression of METTL14 and HNRNPA2B1, reduces the abundance of mature miR-125b-5p, upregulates p53, inhibits TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway and inhibits the progression of cardiac fibrosis by nicotine-induced. This study provides novel therapeutic targets for nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis and provides evidence supporting the repurposing of cinacalcet HCl for this indication.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
Primary antibodies were listed in Table  . Cell Counting Kit-8 assay (CCK-8, GK3607-500T, DINGGUOCHANG-SHENG, BIOTECHNOLOGY, Beijing, China) and Cell-Light 5-ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EdU) Apollo567 In Vitro Kits (100T, C10310-1; Ruibo, Guangzhou, China) were used in this study. Cinacalcet HCl (S1260) and Pirfenidone (TGF-β1 inhibitor) were purchased from Selleck (Shanghai, China). SP600125 (JNK inhibitor), SB203580 (p38MAPK inhibitor), and PD98059 (ERK inhibitor) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai, China). The rat N6-methyladenosine (m6A) ELISA kit (KYY-71155R1) was purchased from Shandong Keyun Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China).
 

## Animals and ethics
Five-week-old male Wistar rats (weighing 150 g) were housed under specific-pathogen-free (SPF) conditions at 20–25 °C and 50–60% relative humidity on a 12-h light/12-h dark (12 L/12 D) photoperiod. The rats were provided with sterile drinking water ad libitum and standard chow. All rats were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Jinan Central Hospital (Approval No. JNCHIACUC2021-66).

## Adeno-associated virus (AAV) and construction and infection
The adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector was generated by cloning short hairpin RNA (shRNA) fragments into the adeno-associated virus vector (Genechem Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China). The viral titer of AAV9-HNRNPA2B1-RNAi was approximately 2.625 × 10¹² particles/mL, and that of the control vector (CON305) was approximately 3.435 × 10¹² particles/mL. For adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) experiments, 6-week-old rats received a dose of 1 × 1011 vector genomes (vg) of the cardiotropic AAV9 expressing full-length of HNRNPA2B1 or negative control (NC) under the control of the Tcf21 promoter via tail vein injection 2 weeks prior to the first nicotine injection.
The experimental groups were as follows: (1) Control: 8-week-old rats received daily intraperitoneal injections of 200 µL normal saline daily. (2) Nicotine: 8-week-old rats received daily intraperitoneal injections of nicotine (1 mg/kg/day, dissolved in 200 µL normal saline). (3) Nicotine + AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1: Rats were injected with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 via tail vein at 6 weeks of age, and 2 weeks later received daily intraperitoneal injections of nicotine (1 mg/kg/day). (4) Nicotine + cinacalcet HCl: At 8 weeks of age, rats received daily intraperitoneal injections of nicotine (1 mg/kg/day) and oral gavage of cinacalcet HCl (5 mg/kg/day). Treatments were continued for 6 consecutive.

## Cell culture
Two- to three-day-old Wistar rats were anesthetized by inhalation of 2% isoflurane for 3 min. The ventricles were then minced and washed twice with Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS). Cardiac tissues were digested in HBSS containing 200 U type Ⅱ collagenase and 0.4% horse serum. The resulting cell suspension was transferred to a culture flask and cultured in a 37 °C incubator with 95% O2 and 5% CO2 for 1.5 h. Non-adherent cardiomyocytes were removed, adherent CFs were collected for subsequent experiments.

## Cell proliferation assay
For the CCK-8 assay, CFs were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured for 24 h prior to treatment. Each treatment group consisted of six replicate wells. Cells were then incubated with CCK-8 reagent (10% v/v in culture medium) for 4 h, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Cell proliferation was also assessed using the Cell-Light EdU Apollo 567 In Vitro Kit.

## Quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)
Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed as previously described [ ].Primer sequences are listed in Table . Relative mRNA expression levels were quantified using the 2^⁻ΔΔCT method and normalized to β-actin.
 

## Western blot
Protein lysates were prepared using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with 1% phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF), 1% protease inhibitor, and 1% phosphatase inhibitor. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA protein assay kit. Protein samples (8 µg) were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). Proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. Membranes were probed with primary antibodies overnight, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Signals were detected using ECL reagent and normalized to β-actin.

## Immunofluorescence
Culture was aspirated, and cells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, washed with PBS, and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 3 min. Cells were blocked with 10% donkey serum for 40 min and incubated with primary antibodies against α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) (1:200) at 4 °C overnight. After three washes with PBS, cells were incubated with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies for 40 min at room temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope.

## Cardiac echocardiography
Rats were anesthetized by inhalation of 2% isoflurane for 15 min. B-mode, M-mode, and Doppler echocardiography were performed using a Vevo3100LT imaging system (FUJIFILM Visual Sonics, Toronto, Ontario, Canada) to assess cardiac dimensions and function. The following left ventricle parameters were observed: left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), fractional shortening (LVFS), anterior wall thickness in systolic (LVAWs) and diastolic (LVAWd), and left ventricular internal diameter in systole (LVEDs), and diastolic (LVEDd). The ratio of early to late mitral inflow velocities (E/A ratio) was calculated from the apical four-chamber view to assess left ventricular diastolic function. Image analysis was performed using Vevo LAB software (version 3.2.6; FUJIFILM Visual Sonics; Toronto, Ontario, Canada).

## Cardiovascular magnetic resonance scans
Rats were scanned using a 9.4 T small animal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) system (BioSpec 94/20USR; Bruker, USA). Each rat was placed in a closed glass cylinder and anesthetized by inhalation of 3% isoflurane for 3 min. Rats were immediately transferred to scanning bed, where anesthesia was maintained with 2% isoflurane for 45 min. The chest was centered over the coil, electrocardiogram (ECG) electrodes were attached into the forelimbs and right hindlimb, and a respiratory sensor was placed on the abdomen. MRI scanning was initiated after respiratory and ECG signals stabilized. A three-plane localizer scan of the chest was performed, followed by acquisition of cardiac four-chamber, two-chamber, and short-axis images. Global cardiac function was assessed by analysis cine images using CVI42 software (Circle Cardiovascular Imaging, Calgary, Alberta, Canada).

## Sirius red staining
Cardiac fibrosis was assessed by Sirius Red Staining (G1472; Solarbio, Beijing, China). Sections were visualized under a light microscope. The Collagen deposition (red-stained areas) was quantified using Image J software (version 1.46r; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland, USA).

## Cell transfection of siRNA
Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs; 50 nM) were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Grand Island, NY, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Cellular thermal shift assay
The binding of nicotine to potential protein targets was validated using CETSA [ ]. Cells were treated with either 500 nM nicotine or DMSO control for 6 h. Cells were then harvested and resuspended in PBS containing protease inhibitors. Aliquots were distributed into PCR tubes for thermal denaturation at graded temperatures for 3 min, followed by immediate cooling in an ice bath for 3 min. This heating-cooling cycle was repeated three times. After centrifugation at 20,000 × g, supernatants were collected and boiled for 10 min in SDS-PAGE loading buffer. Protein stability was assessed by Western blot analysis. In a separate experiment, CFs were treated with varying concentrations of nicotine (1 nM-1000 nM) at a fixed temperature. Subsequent steps were performed as described above.

## Drug affinity responsive target stability assay
The DARTS assay was performed in CFs according to a previously published protocol [ ]. Cell lysate was prepared by M-PER™ Mammalian Protein Extraction Reagent (78501; Thermo Fisher), briefly centrifuged, and incubated with nicotine or DMSO for 1 h on a rotator. Protease (P5147, Sigma-Aldrich) was added to the mixture and incubated for 15 min at room temperature. Digestion was terminated by incubation with protease inhibitor cocktail for 5 min on ice. Samples were boiled in SDS loading buffer subjected to SDS-PAGE and Western blot analysis.

## RNA immunoprecipitation assay
RNA immunoprecipitation assay (RIP) was performed using the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore). Antibodies against HNRNPA2B1, DGCR8, and Drosha were used. For each immunoprecipitation, total RNA (input) and isotype-matched IgG were included as controls. Co-immunoprecipitated RNAs were analyzed by qRT-PCR. To assess m⁶A modification levels in pri-miR-125b, total RNA was extracted and subjected to m⁶A-specific RIP. Immunoprecipitation was performed using an anti-m⁶A antibody pre-conjugated to magnetic Dynabeads in RIP buffer (Magna RIP Kit, Millipore). Following proteinase K (10 mg/mL) digestion, RNAs were extracted and analyzed by qRT-PCR using primers specific for pri-miR-125b. Results were normalized to input.

## Wound healing assay
CFs were seeded in six-well plates. Cells were treated with nicotine, miR-125b-5p mimic, cinacalcet HCl and knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 and METTL14. After adherence, a scratch was generated using a 200-µL pipette tip and cells incubated at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2. Medium containing 1% FBS was added, and cells were cultured for 24 h. Images were acquired at 0 and 24 h to assess cell migration.

## Statistical analysis
Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups were performed using unpaired Student’s t-tests, and comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni’s post hoc test. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Images were processed using Adobe Photoshop CS6 (Adobe Systems, San Jose, CA, USA). All experiments were independently repeated at least three times. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

## Results

## Nicotine promotes the proliferation and activation of CFs in vivo and in vitro
Prolonged nicotine administration (6 weeks) significantly increased the proliferation and activation of CFs in rat hearts compared with vehicle controls. In vivo, nicotine exposure promoted CFs proliferation and activation (Fig.  A). This was accompanied by enhanced CFs activation, as evidenced by an increased number of α-SMA-positive MFs (Fig.  B). Nicotine treatment upregulated the mRNA expression of connective tissue growth factor (CTGF), α-SMA, fibronectin (Fn), collagen type I (Col1), and collagen type III (Col3), indicating enhanced collagen synthesis (Fig.  C). In vitro, nicotine stimulation enhanced CFs proliferation, as demonstrated by increased the number of PCNA-positive of CFs, enhanced EdU incorporation, and elevated CCK-8 absorbance (Fig.  D-F). Nicotine also increased α-SMA immunofluorescence intensity (Fig.  G). Consistently, nicotine treatment upregulated mRNA levels of CTGF, α-SMA, Fn, Col1, and Col3 (Fig.  H). At the protein level, nicotine significantly upregulated expression of PCNA, α-SMA, Col1, and Col3 (Fig.  I). In addition, wound healing assays confirmed that nicotine stimulation promoted CFs migration (Figure A). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that nicotine promotes CFs proliferation, migration and activation, thereby facilitating cardiac fibrosis.
 

## Nicotine induces cardiac fibrosis through METTL14-mediated m6A methylation, but its underlying binding target is HNRNPA2B1, not METTL14
Previous studies have demonstrated that nicotine regulates the m⁶A modification of key proteins, thereby promoting colorectal cancer and hypertension [ , ]. In the present study, we hypothesized that nicotine promotes cardiac fibrosis by regulating m⁶A modification. Our results demonstrated that nicotine stimulation increased the m⁶A level in CFs, peaking at 6 h and subsequently declining (Fig. A). We next investigated the involvement of methyltransferases and demethylases are implicated in nicotine-induced m⁶A modification. Nicotine stimulation selectively upregulated expression of the methyltransferases METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP, whereas demethylases (FTO and ALKBH5) remained unchanged. Notably, METTL14 was most prominently upregulated (Fig. B). To determine the functional role of METTL14, we silenced METTL14 using siRNAs (Fig. C). METTL14 knockdown abrogated the nicotine-induced increases in m⁶A levels (Fig. D), indicating that METTL14 is essential for the nicotine-mediated m⁶A elevation. Additionally, METTL14 knockdown reduced protein levels of METTL14, PCNA, α-SMA, Col1, and Col3 (Figure A-F). Moreover, METTL14 knockdown attenuated nicotine-induced upregulation of METTL14, α-SMA, Col1, Col3, CTGF, and Fn mRNA expression (Figure G-L). METTL14 knockdown significantly inhibited nicotine-induced CFs migration (Figure M). We employed the DARTS assay to investigate whether nicotine binds directly to METTL14 and to elucidate the mechanisms of nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. Schematic diagram of a nicotine-binding target protein (Fig. E). In contrast, nicotine did not bind to METTL14 (Fig. F). Using DARTS coupled with SDS-PAGE, Coomassie blue staining, and mass spectrometry, we identified HNRNPA2B1 (an m⁶A reader protein) as a novel nicotine-binding protein, may serve as a potential binding target of nicotine. The interaction between nicotine and HNRNPA2B1 was confirmed by Western blot analysis of DARTS samples (Fig. G). CETSA further demonstrated that nicotine stabilized HNRNPA2B1 in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. H-I). These findings demonstrate that nicotine can potentially bind to HNRNPA2B1. Nicotine stimulation increased HNRNPA2B1 protein expression (Fig. J). Additionally, nicotine exposure also upregulated HNRNPA2B1 mRNA levels (Fig. K). These results suggest that HNRNPA2B1 is a key mediator of nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis.
 

## Nicotine promotes the proliferation and activation of CFs by regulating miR-125b-5p expression through HNRNPA2B1
We next investigated the functional role of HNRNPA2B1 in nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. HNRNPA2B1 knockdown efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig. A). HNRNPA2B1 knockdown attenuated nicotine-induced CFs proliferation and activation, as assessed by CCK-8 assays, PCNA immunofluorescence, and EdU incorporation (Fig. B-E). HNRNPA2B1 knockdown also reduced α-SMA immunofluorescence intensity, indicating alleviated CFs to MFs (Fig. F). At both protein and mRNA levels, HNRNPA2B1 knockdown attenuated nicotine-induced CFs proliferation, activation, and fibrotic marker expression (Fig. G-R). HNRNPA2B1 knockdown attenuated nicotine-induced CFs migration (Fig. S). miRNAs function as post-transcriptional regulators of CFs proliferation and differentiation during cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Given that HNRNPA2B1 recognizes m⁶A-modified pri-miRNAs and promotes their maturation, we integrated published data to identify miRNAs coregulated by HNRNPA2B1 and associated with smoking [ , ]. miR-99a-5p and miR-125b-5p were identified as candidate miRNAs and validated by qRT-PCR (Fig. A). qRT-PCR results showed that miR-125b-5p expression was significantly regulated by nicotine and HNRNPA2B1 (Fig. B). The miR-125b-5p mimic exacerbated nicotine-induced CFs proliferation (Fig. C-F). Additionally, it enhanced α-SMA immunofluorescence intensity and exacerbated CFs-to-MFs transition (Fig. G). At both protein and mRNA levels, miR-125b-5p mimic exacerbated nicotine-induced CFs proliferation and activation (Fig. H-J). The miR-125b-5p mimic enhanced nicotine-induced CFs migration (Fig. K). Our findings demonstrate that nicotine stimulation upregulates the expression of HNRNPA2B1, which in turn enhances miR-125b-5p expression, ultimately leading to the proliferation, activation and migration of CFs.
 

## HNRNPA2B1 recognizes the m6A modification of pri-miR-125b mediated by METTL14, thereby promoting the maturation of miR-125b-5p
We next investigated the mechanism by which HNRNPA2B1 promotes miR-125b-5p maturation. As an m⁶A reader protein, HNRNPA2B1 is known to recognize miRNA precursors and facilitate their processing. Nicotine stimulation decreased pri-miR-125b levels while increasing pre-miR-125b levels. However, HNRNPA2B1 knockdown reversed these effects and abrogated the mature miR-125b-5p production (Fig. A-C). Previous studies have confirmed that HNRNPA2B1 interacts with DGCR8 and Drosha, core components of the Microprocessor complex, to facilitate pri-microRNAs processing [ ]. Here, we hypothesized that HNRNPA2B1 facilitates the processing of pri-miR-125b processing through a similar mechanism, thereby promoting the mature miR-125b-5p production. RIP assays confirmed the interaction between HNRNPA2B1 and pri-miR-125b in CFs (Fig. D). Co-IP assays showed a persisting interaction between HNRNPA2B1 and DGCR8 despite ribonuclease treatment, suggesting that this is a protein–protein association (Fig. E). Furthermore, HNRNPA2B1 knockdown significantly attenuated the interaction between endogenous DGCR8 and pri-miR-125b (Fig. F), indicating that HNRNPA2B1 facilitates the recruitment of DGCR8 to pri-miR-125b. As established above, nicotine stimulation significantly upregulated METTL14 expression. We speculate that METTL14 serves as the catalytic component responsible for the elevated m⁶A modification levels observed in pri-miR-125b. In METTL14-deficient CFs, we observed an accumulation of pri-miR-125b, whereas the levels of pre-miR-125b and mature miR-125b-5p were concomitantly reduced (Fig. G-I). Subsequent MeRIP assays coupled with qRT-PCR analysis, revealed a significant reduction in the m⁶A modification levels of pri-miR-125b were significantly reduced within METTL14-knockdown CFs (Fig. J). We found that METTL14 knockdown significantly impaired the binding affinity of endogenous DGCR8 and HNRNPA2B1 for pri-miR-125b (Fig. K-L). Conversely, overexpress of METTL14 significantly increased the levels of pre-miR-125b and mature miR-125b-5p, while reducing the abundance of pri-miR-125b (Fig. M-P). HNRNPA2B1 recognizes METTL14-mediated m⁶A modification on pri-miR-125b, which promotes the processing of pri-miR-125b into its mature form, which ultimately drives nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. Collectively, these findings indicate that METTL14-mediated m6A methylation facilitates the recruitment of HNRNPA2B1 and DGCR8 to pri-miR-125b, thereby enhancing the biogenesis of miR-125b-5p.
 

## miR-125b-5p targets p53 and activates the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, thereby promoting nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis
The TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway plays a crucial role in cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Previous studies have shown that miR-125b-5p exacerbates collagen production and promotes photoaging by regulating TGF-β1 [ ]. In the present study, Western blot analysis revealed that nicotine stimulation activated TGF-β1 and increased the protein levels of phosphorylated JNK, ERK, and p38MAPK (Fig. A-B). The miR-125b-5p inhibitor blunted nicotine-induced activation of TGF-β1 and reduced the levels of phosphorylated JNK, ERK, and p38MAPK (Fig. C-G). Treatment with signaling pathway inhibitors, including pirfenidone (a TGF-β1 inhibitor), SP600125 (a JNK inhibitor), PD98059 (an ERK inhibitor), and SB203580 (a p38MAPK inhibitor) —effectively attenuated nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis (Figure ). Thus, activation of the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway may represents a potential mechanism through which nicotine drives cardiac fibrosis via the miR-125b-5p axis. The p53 protein is a well-established downstream target of miR-125b-5p and serves as a major negative regulator of fibrogenesis. Extensive research has demonstrated that p53 modulates the activation of CFs and the production of ECM, highlighting its essential role in the progression of cardiac fibrosis [ ]. miR-125b acts as a pro-fibrotic factor that promotes angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced CFs proliferation and cardiac fibrosis through repressing p53 activity [ ]. In the present study, the miR-125b-5p inhibitor significantly increased p53 expression at both the protein and mRNA levels (Fig. H-I). These results suggest that the METTL14/HNRNPA2B1/miR-125b-5p/p53 axis, along with TGF-β1/MAPK signaling, plays a pivotal role in nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis.
 

## Cinacalcet HCl relieves nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting METTL14 and HNRNPA2B1
Having previously identified cinacalcet HCl as a potent inhibitor of CFs proliferation during a drug screening, we hypothesized that it might exert protective effects against cardiac fibrosis. Subsequently, we investigated whether cinacalcet HCl could alleviate nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis and explored the underlying molecular mechanisms. We found that cinacalcet HCl (10 µM) effectively inhibited nicotine-induced CFs proliferation, as evidenced by CCK-8, EdU, and PCNA immunofluorescence assays (Fig.  A-C). Furthermore, cinacalcet HCl treatment suppressed CFs activation, as indicated by a significant reduction in α-SMA immunofluorescence intensity (Fig.  D). Western blot analysis further confirmed that cinacalcet HCl downregulated the protein expression of PCNA, α-SMA, Col1, and Col3 (Fig.  E–I). Similarly, qRT-PCR results demonstrated that cinacalcet HCl attenuated the nicotine-induced upregulation of these cardiac fibrotic markers at the transcriptional level (Fig.  J–N). Wound healing assays demonstrated that cinacalcet HCl attenuated nicotine-induced CFs migration (Fig.  O). Additionally, cinacalcet HCl treatment effectively suppressed the nicotine-induced activation of the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, as well as the expression of HNRNPA2B1, METTL14, and p53 (Figure A-H). Cinacalcet HCl attenuated the level of nicotine-induced m⁶A modification (Figure I). MeRIP-coupled qRT-PCR analysis revealed that cinacalcet HCl significantly lowered the m⁶A modification levels of pri-miR-125b relative to nicotine treatment alone (Figure J). Cinacalcet HCl significantly suppressed the mRNA expression of HNRNPA2B1 and METTL14 (Figure K-L). Treatment with cinacalcet HCl led to the accumulation of pri-miR-125b, while simultaneously reducing the levels of pre-miR-125b and mature miR-125b-5p (Figure M-O). Furthermore, cinacalcet HCl treatment significantly effectively rescued the nicotine-induced inhibition of p53 expression (Figure P). In this study, we demonstrate for the first time that cinacalcet HCl inhibits the proliferation and activation of CFs, thereby alleviating nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. Specifically, cinacalcet HCl downregulates METTL14 and HNRNPA2B1 expression, reducing the m⁶A methylation of pri-miR-125b and impairing its processing into miR-125b-5p. This cascade ultimately restores p53 expression and suppresses the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, thereby mitigating nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis.
 

## In vivo, the knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 and treatment with cinacalcet HCl both attenuate nicotine-induced cardiac dysfunction and cardiac fibrosis
In vivo, HNRNPA2B1 was silenced using AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1, and the knockdown efficiency was confirmed via qRT-PCR as shown in Figure A. Representative echocardiographic images from each experimental group are shown in Figure B. LVAWs and LVAWd were increased in the nicotine group but these changes were reversed by AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl treatment (Figure C-D). LVEDd and LVEDs were decreased in the nicotine group, while treatment with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl partially rescued this effect (Figure E-F). Color Doppler ultrasound was used to assess changes in the E/A ratio. The E/A ratio was significantly reduced in the nicotine group compared to the control group. However, treatment with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl partially restored the E/A ratio (Figure G). Interestingly, following six weeks of nicotine administration, no significant alterations were observed in EF or FS (Figure H-I). Global longitudinal strain (GLS) is a well-established marker of subclinical myocardial dysfunction and correlates with the severity of cardiac fibrosis and left ventricular filling pressure [ , ]. Our results showed that the magnitude of GLS was significantly reduced in the nicotine group; however, this reduction was reversed by treatment with either AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl (Figure J). Similarly, the trend observed for global radial strain (GRS) was consistent with that of GLS (Figure K). Sirius Red staining revealed extensive collagen deposition within the myocardial interstitial and perivascular regions in the nicotine group. This fibrotic deposition was significantly attenuated following treatment with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl (Fig. A-C). Western blot analysis revealed that nicotine stimulation significantly upregulated the protein levels of PCNA, α-SMA, Col1, and Col3. Conversely, these effects were effectively reversed by treatment with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl (Fig. D-H). Immunofluorescence staining further confirmed a substantial increase in the population of α-SMA-positive cells in nicotine-treated hearts (Fig. I-J). Our in vivo findings further elucidate the pivotal role of HNRNPA2B1 in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis. Furthermore, our study demonstrates that cinacalcet HCl, an FDA-approved drug for hyperparathyroidism, exhibits significant efficacy in mitigating cardiac fibrosis. These findings highlight a novel therapeutic strategy for cardiac fibrosis.
 

## In vivo, the knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl treatment attenuate nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting p53 and TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathways
We further elucidated the molecular mechanisms underlying nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis and validated the antifibrotic effects of cinacalcet HCl in vivo. The qRT-PCR analysis showed that treatment with AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 or cinacalcet HCl reversed the nicotine-induced upregulation of cardiac fibrotic markers (Figure A-E). The expression levels of HNRNPA2B1, METTL14, p53, and miR-125b-5p were significantly elevated in the nicotine group; however, these increases were attenuated in both the cinacalcet HCl- and AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1-treated groups (Figure F-I). In vivo, cinacalcet HCl significantly reduced the nicotine-induced m⁶A level, whereas AAV9-shHNRNPA2B1 failed to reverse this effect (Figure J). In vivo, nicotine stimulation upregulated the expression of HNRNPA2B1 and METTL14, downregulated p53 levels and activated the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway. Conversely, both HNRNPA2B1 knockdown and cinacalcet HCl treatment effectively suppressed these signaling molecules (Figure K-R). These findings suggest that HNRNPA2B1 represents a potential therapeutic target for the treatment of nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. Furthermore, our study demonstrates that cinacalcet HCl, a known treatment for hyperparathyroidism, exhibits potent antifibrotic properties, offering a promising strategy for treating cardiac fibrosis.

## Discussion
In the present study, we identified HNRNPA2B1 as a novel molecular target of nicotine in CFs using DARTS and CETSA assays, suggesting that HNRNPA2B1 represents a promising therapeutic target for cardiac fibrosis. We demonstrated that HNRNPA2B1 deficiency effectively attenuates nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis and the associated cardiac dysfunction. Our study provides several novel insights: First, nicotine promotes the proliferation and activation of CFs by increasing m6A levels mediated by METTL14 levels. Second, nicotine potentially binds to HNRNPA2B1, rather than METTL14. Subsequently, HNRNPA2B1 recognizes METTL14-mediated m6A modification on pri-miR-125b, thereby promoting the biogenesis of miR-125b-5p. Third, nicotine drives cardiac fibrosis via the METTL14/HNRNPA2B1/miR-125b-5p/p53 axis and the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway. Fourth, we report for the first time that cinacalcet HCl —an FDA-approved drug for hyperparathyroidism—mitigates nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting the expression of METTL14 and HNRNPA2B1 expression. We have discovered the antifibrotic effects of cinacalcet HCl and elucidated its mechanisms of action. This will provide important evidence for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis.
Our previous study also demonstrated that 6 weeks of nicotine exposure induced autophagy blockade, which in turn led to cardiac collagen deposition and increased expression of Col1 and Col3 in both normal rats and spontaneously hypertensive rats [ , ]. In the present study, we successfully recapitulated the nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis phenotype. Notably, nicotine delivered via electronic cigarettes exhibits high bioavailability, thereby increasing the risk of cardiovascular diseases [ ]. Compared with traditional cardiovascular risk factors, e-cigarette use is also independently associated with an elevated risk of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) [ ]. Growing evidence suggests that smoking cessation remains one of the most effective and feasible strategies for preventing cardiovascular disease. Nicotine, the primary component of tobacco, drives epigenetic reprogramming, including DNA methylation, histone post-translational modifications, and miRNA dysregulation. These epigenetic alterations, in turn, orchestrate aberrant gene expression that accelerates ECM deposition and myofibroblast activation [ – ]. Thus, these epigenetic alterations represent a previously underrecognized but critical mechanistic axis linking nicotine exposure to the progression of cardiac fibrosis.
N6-methyladenosine (m⁶A) is a common, abundant, and evolutionarily conserved internal modification in eukaryotic RNAs, and it undergoes a dynamic and reversible biological process [ ]. Recent studies have demonstrated that m⁶A plays an indispensable role in cardiac fibrosis by regulating mitochondrial reactive oxygen species production [ ]. Additionally, it has been reported that deficiencies in METTL3 and METTL14 significantly attenuate Ang II-induced cardiac inflammation and fibrosis by reducing m⁶A modification on MyD88 and TGF-β1 mRNAs [ ]. Separately, YTHDF1 recognizes m⁶A-modified Piezo2 mRNA and regulates CFs autophagy and cardiac fibrosis via an m⁶A-dependent mechanism [ ]. Here, we found that nicotine stimulation significantly elevated global the m⁶A levels in CFs. The m6A methylation landscape is dynamically regulated by demethylases (erasers), such as FTO and ALKBH5, and a methyltransferase complex (writers) comprising METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP. Our results demonstrated that nicotine exposure significantly upregulates METTL14 mRNA expression, suggesting that METTL14 is a pivotal regulator of nicotine-induced m⁶A modification. METTL14 is a core component of the m⁶A methyltransferase complex and is implicated in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases. For example, METTL14 knockdown has been shown to inhibit arterial stiffness, arterial remodeling, and endothelial senescence [ ]. Furthermore, METTL14-mediated m⁶A methylation of pri-miR-5099 has been reported to trigger cardiomyocyte pyroptosis following myocardial infarction [ ]. In the present study, METTL14 knockdown significantly abrogated the nicotine-induced increase in m6A levels and suppressed CFs proliferation and activation, thereby attenuating cardiac fibrosis. These findings are consistent with previous reports showing that inhibiting METTL14 expression alleviates doxorubicin-induced cardiac injury and fibrosis [ ]. Taken together, these data underscore the potential of METTL14 as a therapeutic target for preventing the progression of cardiac fibrosis.
However, neither DARTS nor CETSA experiments provided evidence that nicotine potentially binds to METTL14. Mass spectrometry analysis, followed by experimental validation unexpectedly revealed that nicotine potentially binds to HNRNPA2B1—a heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein (hnRNP) that functions as both an RNA-binding protein and an m⁶A reader. Nicotine stimulation significantly increased protein expression levels of HNRNPA2B1. HNRNPA2B1 is a key member of the A/B subfamily of hnRNPs. It has been clearly demonstrated HNRNPA2B1-mediated maturation of miRNAs promotes the activation of MFs [ ]. Previous studies have suggested that HNRNPA2B1 may mediate m⁶A function via an “m⁶A switch” mechanism, rather than acting as a direct reader of m⁶A modifications. Specifically, m⁶A has been shown to enhance the accessibility of HNRNPA2B1 to pri-miRNAs, rather than binding directly to HNRNPA2B1, thereby facilitating pri-miRNA maturation [ ]. HNRNPA2B1 associates with a subset of pri-miRNA transcripts via binding to m⁶A modifications, and promotes pri-miRNA processing by recruiting the microprocessor complex, which consists of Drosha and DGCR8 [ ]. Among the miRNAs both regulated by HNRNPA2B1 and influenced by smoking, miR-125b-5p is notably modulated by this protein. Specifically, HNRNPA2B1 interacts with DGCR8 to facilitate pri-miR-125b processing, leading to increased expression of mature miR-125b-5p. Knockdown of HNRNPA2B1 reduces the binding of endogenous DGCR8 to pri-miR-125b. Moreover, this interaction persists even after ribonuclease treatment, suggesting that the association between HNRNPA2B1 and DGCR8 is mediated by protein-protein interactions. Consistent with these findings, silencing HNRNPA2B1 significantly decreases the expression levels of miR-125b-5p, indicating that HNRNPA2B1 plays a crucial role in the m⁶A-mediated processing of miR-125b-5p.
We also found that METTL14 is a key methyltransferase in regulating the m⁶A modification of pri-miR-125b. Knockdown of METTL14 leads to a reduction in the m⁶A modification of pri-miR-125b ([m⁶A]pri-miR-125b), which subsequently inhibits the production of mature miR-125b-5p and alleviates cardiac fibrosis. Conversely, overexpression of METTL14 significantly increases the expression levels of mature miR-125b-5p. These results strongly suggest that the upregulation of miR-125b-5p levels induced by nicotine is mediated by a critical epigenetic mechanism, thereby supporting miR-125b-5p as a potential risk biomarker for cardiac fibrosis. However, the role of miR-125b-5p in cardiac fibrosis remains controversial. For instance, some studies have shown that miR-125b-5p is involved in the critical process of fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition [ ]. Conversely, other reports have highlighted an anti-fibrotic role of miR-125b-5p [ ]. This discrepancy may stem from differences in the pathological contexts across the respective studies. Our findings demonstrate that HNRNPA2B1 recognize the m⁶A modification on pri-miR-125b that is dependent on METTL14, thereby promoting the processing of pri-miR-125b and subsequently enhancing the production of miR-125b-5p. Collectively, METTL14 emerges as a pivotal factor in nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. However, the precise mechanisms underlying nicotine’s regulation of METTL14 expression remain to be fully elucidated. This area will be the primary focus of our subsequent investigations.
Multiple studies have indicated that p53 is a key downstream target of miR-125b-5p [ , , ]. p53 plays a crucial yet complex role in regulating organ fibrosis. It has been reported that cardiac fibroblast-specific p53 conditional knockout mice subjected to left ventricle pressure overload exhibited excessive CFs proliferation, and ECM deposition, suggesting that p53-dependent CFs proliferation is involved in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis [ ]. In recent years, p53 has emerged as a promising therapeutic target for cardiac fibrosis. Our results demonstrate that nicotine stimulation promotes the proliferation and activation of CFs by upregulating miR-125b-5p expression and downregulating the expression of its downstream target gene p53, an important antifibrotic factor. Here, we have determine that nicotine-induced suppression of p53 expression represents an important mechanism underlying cardiac fibrosis.
TGF-β1 is widely recognized as the most potent pro-fibrotic cytokine identified to date. It acts as the master driver of CFs activation and pathological cardiac fibrosis in diseased hearts [ ]. It is well established that intracellular MAPK signaling cascades play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis [ ]. Notably, a potential complementary binding relationship has been reported between miR-125b-5p and TGF-β1 mRNA, which in turn upregulates collagen production and promotes cardiac fibrosis [ ]. In the present study, we find that miR-125b-5p activates the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, a mechanism that represents another critical contributor to nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis.
Cinacalcet HCl is a calcimimetic agent that has been approved for the treatment of secondary hyperparathyroidism in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and for the management of hypercalcemia in patients with parathyroid carcinoma [ ]. In patients undergoing hemodialysis, cinacalcet HCl has been shown to slow the progression of vascular and cardiac valve calcification [ ]. Previous studies have shown that 4-week treatment with cinacalcet HCl at 5 mg/kg/day attenuates cardiac remodeling and dysfunction in a diabetic cardiomyopathy model without significantly altering blood glucose, serum calcium, or serum phosphorus levels [ ]. This dosing regimen is clinically relevant and falls within the established safe range [ ]. Additionally, cinacalcet HCl at 5 mg/kg/day does not induce parathyroid cell proliferation or weight changes in sham-operated rats, nor does it affect serum phosphorus levels [ ]. In the present study, cinacalcet HCl at 5 mg/kg/day—a safe and effective dose—ameliorates nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. Our results demonstrate for the first time that cinacalcet HCl inhibits the proliferation and activation of CFs, as well as collagen synthesis. Specifically, this drug reduces the expression of METTL14 and HNRNPA2B1, reduces the global m⁶A methylation level, inhibits the maturation of miR-125b-5p, activates p53, and suppresses the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway. The drug significantly inhibits nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. This provides a critical foundation for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. Our study reveals that nicotine induces aberrant m⁶A modification of pri-miR-125b, upregulates the expression of miR-125b-5p, and thereby promotes cardiac fibrosis. Therefore, targeting m⁶A levels represents a promising therapeutic strategy for nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis. This study elucidates the epigenetic mechanisms underlying nicotine-induced cardiac fibrosis and provides critical insights into smoking-related cardiovascular diseases.
However, our study also has certain limitations. This study was conducted exclusively in male rats, which presents certain limitations given the established sex differences in nicotine metabolism and cardiovascular disease pathophysiology [ ]. Females metabolize nicotine differently from males, potentially resulting in altered nicotine exposure levels and tissue distribution [ ]. Future studies should validate these findings in female animals to comprehensively characterize sex-based disparities in nicotine-related cardiovascular toxicity. And, owing to objective constraints, we were unable to employ cigarette smoke exposure to model human smoking behavior. In habitual smokers, blood nicotine concentrations typically range from 10 to 40 ng/mL, with cardiac and brain tissue levels reaching 2–3 times higher values [ ]. To model chronic nicotine exposure comparable to that in regular smokers, previous studies have commonly employed doses of 1–4 mg/kg/day. Administration at 1 mg/kg/day achieves steady-state plasma concentrations of approximately 25 ng/mL, closely matching levels observed in habitual smokers [ ]. In the present study, we established a cardiac fibrosis model using daily intraperitoneal injections of nicotine at 1 mg/kg, consistent with previously validated dosing protocols [ , ].
The results of the present study indicate that nicotine promotes the maturation of miR-125b-5p by enabling HNRNPA2B1 to recognize METTL14-mediated m⁶A modifications on pri-miR-125b. This process further mediates the downregulation of p53 expression and the activation of the TGF-β1/MAPK signaling pathway, ultimately inducing cardiac fibrosis. In contrast, cinacalcet HCl treatment inhibits the expression of HNRNPA2B1 and METTL14, reduces the levels of mature miR-125b-5p, and thereby suppresses cardiac fibrosis. Notably, this study is the first to delineate the potential mechanisms underlying the interactions between nicotine stimulation and the key molecules HNRNPA2B1, METTL14, miR-125b-5p, p53, and TGF-β1, providing valuable insights into therapeutic strategies for cardiac fibrosis.
