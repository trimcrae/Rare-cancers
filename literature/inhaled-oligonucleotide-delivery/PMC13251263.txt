## Abstract

## Abstract
Sepsis-induced acute lung injury (ALI) is characterized by severe oxidative stress and senescence of alveolar type II epithelial cells (AECⅡ), yet effective therapies capable of reversing these pathological processes remain lacking. We developed a multifunctional ICAM1-targeted MnO2@ZIF8 nanoplatform encapsulating Tanshinone IIA (TSA) that integrates selective targeting, redox regulation, and senescence reprogramming. The nanoplatform preferentially accumulated in inflamed lung regions, efficiently scavenged reactive oxygen species (ROS), activated the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathway, and suppressed the IL-33/ST2 inflammatory axis. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed that treatment reshapes AECⅡ differentiation trajectories, suppresses senescence-related transcriptional signatures, and enhances alveolar regenerative potential by restoring stem-like phenotypes. Proteomic and metabolomic analyses further confirmed improved mitochondrial metabolism, SASP modulation, and inflammatory resolution. Functionally, Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA restored alveolar structure, boosted oxygenation, and markedly increased survival in CLP-induced septic mice. Collectively, these findings present a nanobiotechnological strategy that rejuvenates senescent epithelial cells and provides a mechanistically guided therapeutic approach for the treatment of sepsis-associated pulmonary failure.

## Introduction
 Sepsis-induced acute lung injury (ALI) is a frequent and life-threatening complication in critical care medicine. Characterized by rapid progression and complex pathophysiology, it often develops into acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) [ – ]. Despite advances in supportive care, effective targeted therapies that substantially improve clinical outcomes remain limited. Current studies primarily attribute the pathogenesis of ALI to cytokine storms, oxidative stress, immune dysregulation, and disruption of the alveolar epithelial barrier. However, chronic pathological changes such as cellular dysfunction and impaired tissue regeneration are frequently overlooked, particularly the essential role of alveolar epithelial type II cells (AECⅡ) in maintaining alveolar homeostasis and promoting lung repair [ – ]. Recently, increasing attention has been directed toward AECⅡ senescence, which not only compromises epithelial repair capacity but also aggravates tissue injury through the release of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) factors, thereby establishing a persistent pro-inflammatory microenvironment [ – ]. Nevertheless, the mechanistic contribution of AECⅡ senescence to the progression of sepsis-induced ALI remains poorly defined, and effective therapeutic strategies targeting this process are still lacking, substantially limiting the clinical translation of relevant basic research.
Multiple inflammatory and stress-related signaling pathways contribute to the pathogenesis of sepsis-induced ALI. Among these, interleukin-33 (IL-33), a recently identified alarmin, forms a signaling axis with its receptor suppression of tumorigenicity 2 (ST2), which plays a critical role in tissue injury, immune activation, and fibrotic remodeling [ – ]. IL-33 is consistently upregulated in several models of acute inflammation, and its immunomodulatory functions are closely linked to oxidative stress. However, whether IL-33 directly contributes to AECⅡ senescence remains unclear, largely due to the lack of spatial and single-cell level evidence [ – ]. Nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) is a central transcription factor that governs cellular antioxidant responses by regulating downstream targets such as superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2), heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), thereby maintaining redox homeostasis [ , ]. Increasing evidence indicates that Nrf2 also plays an essential role in cellular senescence. Reduced Nrf2 activity promotes oxidative stress accumulation, disrupts antioxidant defense systems, and accelerates cellular functional decline, highlighting its potential importance in the development of AECⅡ senescence [ – ].
Based on these considerations, a major challenge in sepsis-induced ALI therapy is to achieve precise recognition, targeted delivery, and coordinated multi-pathway intervention in AECⅡ to reverse cellular senescence and restore alveolar function. In recent years, nanomedicine has shown considerable promise in inflammatory disease treatment because of its favorable tissue permeability, drug-loading capacity, and responsiveness to pathological microenvironments. Among these strategies, nanozymes have emerged as versatile therapeutic platforms with catalytic antioxidant activity and disease-responsive properties [ , ]. Manganese dioxide (MnO2), owing to its superoxide dismutase (SOD)- and peroxidase (POD)-like activities, enables sustained clearance of reactive oxygen species (ROS), while the porous structure of zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF8) allows for dual pH- and ROS-responsive drug release, thus forming an intelligent delivery system [ – ]. Surface modification with Anti-ICAM1 antibodies further enhances selective accumulation in inflamed pulmonary tissues, thereby improving targeting efficiency [ , ]. Tanshinone IIA (TSA), a natural histone deacetylase inhibitor, exhibits anti-inflammatory and epigenetic modulatory properties and has shown therapeutic potential in various aging-related models [ – ]. The integration of these components provides a promising strategy for constructing an intelligent nanoplatform capable of targeted recognition, ROS scavenging, epigenetic modulation, and signaling regulation to counteract AECⅡ senescence.
Therefore, this study develops an ICAM-1 antibody-modified MnO2@ZIF8 nanozyme loaded with TSA (Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA) to alleviate pulmonary injury in sepsis-induced ALI by targeting AECⅡ senescence. A comprehensive evaluation system combining in vivo and in vitro approaches is established, including a cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) mouse model, AECⅡ-derived organoids, and primary cell models. These biological platforms are integrated with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), proteomics, and metabolomics to systematically investigate how the nano-platform regulates AECⅡ targeting efficiency, ROS homeostasis, senescence trajectory remodeling, and pulmonary functional recovery. Mechanistically, the study explores the crosstalk between IL-33/ST2 and Nrf2 signaling pathways during AECⅡ senescence and regeneration. The proposed strategy integrates a targeting antibody, intelligent nanozyme, and natural compound to enhance therapeutic specificity and pharmacological stability, providing a potential translational framework for lung-targeted therapy in sepsis-induced ALI.

## Materials and methods

## Synthesis of Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA Nanoplatform
Key reagents and materials used in this study are listed in Tables  . MnO2 nanoparticles were synthesized using a hydrothermal reduction method. Equal volumes of 0.1 M potassium permanganate (KMnO4) and 0.05 M ascorbic acid (VC) were mixed and incubated in a 60 °C water bath for 6 h. The resulting dark-brown precipitate was collected by centrifugation (12,000 rpm, 10 min), washed three times with deionized water, and lyophilized to obtain MnO2 nanozyme particles. For core–shell assembly, MnO2 nanoparticles were encapsulated with zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF8). Briefly, MnO2 particles were dispersed in a solution containing 160 mM 2-methylimidazole and 40 mM zinc nitrate and reacted under mild ultrasonication and magnetic stirring for 30 min at room temperature to form MnO2@ZIF8 nanoparticles. Tanshinone IIA (TSA) loading was achieved by incubating MnO2@ZIF8 nanoparticles with 10 µM TSA (ethanol solution) under gentle agitation for 24 h, allowing diffusion and adsorption within the porous ZIF8 shell. For antibody functionalization, 1 mg of MnO2@ZIF8@TSA was dispersed in MES buffer, followed by activation with 0.2 M EDC and 0.1 M NHS for 1 h to activate surface carboxyl groups. ICAM-1 antibodies (50 µg/mL) were subsequently added and reacted for 2 h at room temperature under gentle shaking. After centrifugation to remove unbound antibodies, the final targeted nanoplatform, Anti-ICAM1-MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA, was obtained.
 

## The physicochemical properties of the nanoplatform were characterized using multiple analytical techniques
The physicochemical properties of the nanoplatform were characterized using multiple analytical techniques. Hydrodynamic particle size distribution and zeta potential were measured at 25 °C using dynamic light scattering (DLS; Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS). Each sample was measured in triplicate, and mean values were reported. Nanoparticle morphology and structural integrity were examined using transmission electron microscopy (TEM; JEOL 2100) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM; HITACHI S-4800). High-resolution images were acquired to evaluate particle morphology, shell thickness, and the core–shell interface between MnO2 and ZIF8. The enzyme-mimetic activity of MnO2 nanozymes was evaluated using POD-like and SOD-like assays. POD-like activity was determined using the TMB/H2O2 colorimetric system, with absorbance measured at 450 nm. SOD-like activity was assessed using the nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) reduction assay, with absorbance recorded at 560 nm. Stimuli-responsive drug release behavior was evaluated under different microenvironmental conditions. Nanoparticles were incubated in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at pH 5.0, 6.5, and 7.4, as well as in PBS containing 1 mM H2O2. At predetermined time points (0, 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h), supernatants were collected and TSA concentrations were quantified using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC; Agilent 1260). Cumulative drug release curves were generated to assess pH- and ROS-responsive release behavior. All experiments were performed in the dark. Drug release data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) (n = 3) and plotted using GraphPad Prism 9.

## Isolation of AECⅡ cells and establishment of the senescence model
Lung tissues were harvested from anesthetized C57BL/6 mice and digested at 37 °C for 45 min in a solution containing 1 mg/mL collagenase I and 0.5 U/mL elastase. After filtration, single-cell suspensions were obtained and centrifuged, followed by washing with PBS. AECⅡ was positively selected using CD326 (EpCAM) magnetic beads (Miltenyi Biotec). Cells were seeded into Matrigel-precoated 6-well plates and cultured in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. After 24 h of incubation, experimental treatments were initiated. An inflammation-induced cellular senescence model was established by stimulating cells with 1 µg/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 h. Upon completion of stimulation, cells were randomly assigned to five groups for intervention: Control, LPS, LPS + MnO2@ZIF8, LPS + MnO2@ZIF8@TSA, and LPS + Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA. All groups included three independent replicates. Nanoparticle treatments were administered at a final TSA concentration of 10 µM for 24 h [ , ].

## Western blot (WB) analysis
Cells were lysed using pre-chilled RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Lysates were incubated on ice for 30 min with intermittent vortexing and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatants were collected, and protein concentrations were determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit. Equal amounts of protein (20–40 µg) were separated by SDS–PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk or 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) prepared in TBS-T buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Tween-20) for 1 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After washing with TBS-T, the membranes were incubated with the appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents and detected with a chemiluminescence imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software and normalized to β-actin. Detailed antibody information is provided in Table .
 

## RT-qPCR
Total RNA from AECⅡ cells was extracted using TRIzol™ Reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 15596026). RNA concentration and purity were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Samples with an A260/A280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.0 were used for subsequent experiments. For reverse transcription, 1 µg of total RNA from each sample was processed using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Cat. No. RR047A) to remove genomic DNA contamination and synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Takara, Cat. No. RR820A) on an ABI QuantStudio 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. A melt curve analysis was conducted after amplification to verify product specificity [ ]. The target genes analyzed included TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, p16, p21, p53, Nrf2, HO-1, and SOD2, with β-actin serving as the internal reference gene. All primer sequences are listed in Table  . Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method and normalized to the control group. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.
 

## Assessment of oxidative stress and senescence phenotypes
Intracellular ROS levels were detected using the fluorescent probe DCFH-DA (10 µM), while mitochondrial ROS was measured using MitoSOX Red (5 µM). Cells were incubated with the probes for 30 min in the dark and washed three times with PBS. Fluorescence images were acquired using a Leica SP8 confocal microscope and quantified with ImageJ software. Cellular senescence was evaluated using senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) staining. After staining, cells were incubated at 37 °C in a non-CO2 incubator for 16 h. Blue-stained cells were considered SA-β-Gal positive, and the senescence rate was calculated as the percentage of positive cells. Senescence-related markers at the transcriptional and protein levels, including p16Ink4a, p21Cip1, and p53, were analyzed by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and WB, respectively. Total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method, followed by reverse transcription and amplification using a SYBR Green-based qPCR system with β-actin as an internal control. For WB, total protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer, quantified using the BCA assay, and subjected to SDS-PAGE followed by membrane transfer.

## Analysis of signaling pathway activation and nanoparticle uptake
The activation of the IL-33/ST2 signaling pathway and its downstream effectors (p38 MAPK, NF-κB, and Nrf2) was assessed using both immunofluorescence and WB. For immunostaining, cells were fixed and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100, followed by blocking in blocking buffer and overnight incubation with primary antibodies (IL-33, ST2, phosphorylated NF-κB [p-NF-κB], Nrf2, etc.). After washing, cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies and counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescence images were obtained using confocal microscopy. Nanoparticle uptake was evaluated using Cy5-labeled Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoparticles. Mitochondria were simultaneously labeled using MitoTracker Green (100 nM). Confocal imaging was performed to visualize red (nanoparticles) and green (mitochondria) fluorescence signals. Co-localization analysis was conducted using the Coloc2 plugin in ImageJ to calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient, enabling quantitative assessment of nanoparticle targeting within cells.

## Organoid culture
AECⅡ and lung fibroblasts were mixed at a ratio of 1:20 and suspended in 50% Matrigel. A 25 µL droplet of the cell-Matrigel mixture was placed in the center of each well of a 24-well plate and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min to allow gelation. Organoid culture medium (DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10 ng/mL EGF, 10 ng/mL FGF10, 1% N2, 1% B27, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin) was then added. The medium was refreshed every two days. After 7 days of continuous culture, organoids with characteristic vesicle-like cavities were formed [ – ]. To establish an inflammatory model, 1 µg/mL LPS was added to the culture medium for 24 h. Control and treatment groups were subsequently established. The treatment groups received MnO2@ZIF8, MnO2@ZIF8@TSA, or Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoparticles, all at a final TSA concentration of 10 µM.

## Morphological and functional activity analysis
Organoid morphology was observed using an inverted microscope. Cell viability was evaluated by Calcein-AM/propidium iodide (PI) dual staining. Organoids were incubated with staining solution containing 2 µM Calcein-AM and 4.5 µM PI for 30 min and imaged using confocal laser scanning microscopy. Cell viability was determined by calculating the ratio of green (live cells) to red (dead cells) fluorescence signals. Oxidative stress was assessed by incubating organoids with dihydroethidium (DHE, 10 µM) for 30 min. DNA oxidative damage was further evaluated by immunofluorescent staining of 8-hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG). Inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 and TNF-α, were quantified in organoid culture supernatants using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), with concentrations calculated from standard curves. Fluorescence intensity and area were quantified using ImageJ software, and ELISA results were analyzed by standard curve fitting.

## Spatial localization analysis
Organoids were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned for spatial expression analysis. RNAscope in situ hybridization was performed to detect the mRNA expression of IL-33, ST2, p16, and Nrf2 using multiplex fluorescent probes. Immunofluorescence co-staining with structural markers, including surfactant protein C (SP-C) and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), was conducted to identify alveolar epithelial structures. Fluorescence images were acquired using a laser confocal microscope. Spatial expression patterns and co-localization of target signals in different cellular compartments were analyzed using the Coloc plugin in ImageJ.

## Transcriptomic analysis of alveolar organoids
In the senescence-induced organoid model, the intervention group was co-incubated with the ICAM1-functionalized nanosystem for 48 h. Subsequently, organoid samples were harvested, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (#15596026, Invitrogen, USA). RNA integrity was assessed with the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA). Library preparation was performed using the Illumina TruSeq RNA Sample Preparation Kit (#RS-122–2001, Illumina, USA), followed by high-throughput sequencing on the NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were analyzed with DESeq2 (v1.30.1), applying thresholds of |log2 fold change (FC)| > 1 and adjusted p < 0.05. Volcano plots and heatmaps were generated using R software (v4.1.2). Functional enrichment analysis for Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways was conducted with the clusterProfiler package (v4.0.5). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed against the HALLMARK gene sets in the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, v2023.1) using GSEA software (v4.3.2, Broad Institute, USA).

## Senescence/SASP signature analysis
To systematically evaluate senescence-related transcriptomic programs, gene set variation analysis (GSVA) was performed on the normalized bulk RNA-seq expression matrix. Senescence and SASP-related gene signatures were curated in a species-consistent manner using murine gene symbols, covering core cellular senescence regulators, SASP components, DNA damage-associated senescence, PI3K-AKT-mTOR-related aging signaling, and extracellular matrix remodeling. For each sample, GSVA scores were computed for each signature and compared across experimental groups. Heatmaps and per-signature score distributions were generated to visualize signature activities and their changes upon ICAM1-nanoplatform intervention.

## Single-Cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data processing and analysis
Lung tissues from mice in the CLP model group and the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA treatment group (n = 1 per group) were subjected to single-cell isolation and transcriptome sequencing. Immediately after euthanasia, lung tissues were perfused, minced, and enzymatically digested at 37 °C for 40 min using collagenase I (#SCR103, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and DNase I (#DN25, Sigma-Aldrich, USA). The digested suspension was filtered, and red blood cells were lysed to obtain a single-cell suspension. Dead cells were removed using the Dead Cell Removal Kit (#130-090-101, Miltenyi Biotec, Germany). After cell counting and viability assessment, cells were loaded onto the Chromium platform (10x Genomics, USA) for library construction using the Chromium Single Cell 3′ Reagent Kits v3.1, followed by sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform.
Raw sequencing data were processed using Cell Ranger (v6.1.2, 10x Genomics) with alignment to the mouse reference genome (mm10) to generate gene expression matrices. Downstream analysis was conducted using the Seurat package (v4.3.0). Low-quality cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or mitochondrial gene content exceeding 20% were excluded. Batch effects were corrected using the Harmony algorithm (v0.1.1), followed by principal component analysis (PCA) and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) for dimensionality reduction and visualization. Cell clustering was performed using the FindClusters function with a resolution of 0.6, and clusters were annotated based on canonical marker genes. Marker expression patterns were visualized using FeaturePlot and DotPlot, and cell-type proportions were calculated using the prop.table function.
To further characterize alveolar epithelial populations, epithelial cells were re-clustered to identify AECⅡ subpopulations. Pseudotime trajectories were reconstructed using Monocle 2 (v2.24.0) with dimensionality reduction by the DDRTree algorithm. Gene expression dynamics along pseudotime were visualized using the plot_genes_in_pseudotime function. Pathway activity related to senescence and inflammation was evaluated using AUCell (v1.16.0) and the AddModuleScore function. Gene sets included senescence- and inflammation-associated markers (Il6, Cxcl1, Cdkn2a, and Mmp3), as well as IL-33 and NF-κB signaling pathways. Statistical analyses were performed in R, and significance was determined using a two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test (p < 0.05).

## Animal model establishment and grouping
C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old, SPF grade) were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center. Male mice were used because previous studies have shown that they exhibit stronger inflammatory responses and greater susceptibility in sepsis and lung injury models than females, providing a more stable platform for evaluating therapeutic interventions and minimizing variability associated with the estrous cycle [ , ]. A sepsis-induced ALI model was established using cecal CLP. Mice were fasted for 12 h prior to surgery. Under 1.5% isoflurane anesthesia, a midline laparotomy was performed to expose the cecum. The cecum was ligated at one-third of the distance from the distal end and punctured twice with a 22G needle. A small amount of fecal content was gently extruded, and the cecum was returned to the abdominal cavity before closing the incision. Sham-operated mice underwent identical surgical procedures without ligation or puncture. All animals received subcutaneous injection of 1 mL sterile saline for postoperative fluid resuscitation. Mice were randomly assigned to the following groups (n = 15 per group): Sham, CLP, CLP + MnO2@ZIF8, CLP + MnO2@ZIF8@TSA, CLP + Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA, CLP + Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA + IL-33 neutralizing antibody (0 h), CLP + Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA + IL-33 neutralizing antibody (6 h), CLP + Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA + IL-33 neutralizing antibody (12 h), and CLP + metformin. Nanoparticle formulations were administered via tail-vein injection within 6 h after CLP surgery (TSA equivalent dose: 10 mg/kg), once daily for three consecutive days. The IL-33 neutralizing antibody was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 5 mg/kg at 0 h, 6 h, or 12 h after CLP induction. Metformin (MedChemExpress; HY-B0627) was dissolved in sterile saline and administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 25 mg/kg within 1 h after LPS injection (MET group) [ ].

## In vivo imaging and drug distribution
To evaluate the pulmonary accumulation of the nanoparticles, DiR-labeled MnO2@ZIF8@TSA was administered, and in vivo fluorescence imaging was performed using an IVIS Spectrum imaging system (PerkinElmer). Fluorescence images were acquired at designated time points after administration. At the experimental endpoint, lung tissues were harvested, embedded, and sectioned for frozen tissue preparation. Immunofluorescence staining followed by confocal microscopy was conducted to assess the spatial distribution of the nanoplatform within lung tissues. In parallel, the concentration of TSA in lung tissues was quantitatively analyzed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS; Sciex QTRAP 6500). Pharmacokinetic profiles were subsequently generated based on the measured tissue concentrations.

## Pulmonary function, tissue injury assessment, and mechanistic validation
Pulmonary function was evaluated using the Buxco system. Under intravenous anesthesia, mice were intubated and connected to a mechanical ventilation circuit to record Raw and lung compliance (Crs). For histological assessment, the left lung was processed for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining to assess structural damage, Masson’s trichrome staining to assess fibrosis, and TUNEL staining to detect apoptosis. Pulmonary edema was quantified by measuring the lung wet-to-dry (W/D) weight ratio. Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence staining were used to detect the expression of Nrf2, p16, IL-33, ST2, and SASP-related factors (e.g., IL-6, MMP3) in lung tissues. Images were acquired under identical exposure conditions, and staining intensity or the percentage of positive area was quantified using ImageJ software. Total RNA and protein were extracted from lung tissues for qPCR and WB analysis to evaluate the expression of key signaling molecules, including Nrf2, Keap1, p38, p53, NF-κB, SOD2, and GPX4, to investigate the molecular mechanisms associated with oxidative stress and cellular senescence.

## Metabolite extraction and LC-MS/MS analysis
At the experimental endpoint, lung tissues were rapidly excised and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. Approximately 50 mg of tissue was homogenized in 1 mL of pre-chilled methanol/acetonitrile (1:1, v/v). The homogenate was vortexed and incubated at −20 °C for 30 min to precipitate proteins. After centrifugation at 15,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected, vacuum-dried, and reconstituted in water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid for metabolomic analysis. Metabolomic profiling was performed using an ultra-HPLC-tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS) system (Thermo Fisher Q Exactive HF). Data were acquired in both positive and negative ion modes within a mass range of m/z 70–1050. Raw data were processed using XCMS (v3.2) for peak detection, alignment, and normalization to generate a quantitative metabolite matrix. Multivariate analyses, including PCA and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), were conducted using MetaboAnalyst 5.0. Differential metabolites were identified based on a variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1 and adjusted p < 0.05 (Benjamini–Hochberg correction). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the MetaboAnalyst platform. Metabolic pathway maps, heatmaps, and boxplots were generated using R software (v4.2.1).

## Tandem mass tag (TMT)-labeled proteomic analysis
To elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which the ICAM1-targeted nanoplatform intervenes in sepsis-induced ALI, lung tissue samples from the CLP model group and the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA treatment group (n = 6 per group) were subjected to TMT-labeled quantitative proteomic analysis. Lung tissues were ground in liquid nitrogen and lysed in buffer containing 8 M urea and 100 mM triethylammonium bicarbonate (TEAB, pH 8.0) supplemented with protease inhibitors. Samples were sonicated and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min to collect the supernatant. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay kit (#23225, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). For protein digestion, 100 µg of protein was reduced with dithiothreitol (DTT, 5 mM, 37 °C, 30 min) and alkylated with iodoacetamide (IAA, 10 mM, room temperature, 15 min in the dark). The samples were diluted to < 2 M urea and digested overnight with Trypsin Gold (Promega, USA) at an enzyme-to-substrate ratio of 1:50. The digested peptides were desalted using C18 solid-phase extraction cartridges (#8B-S100-UBJ, Agilent Technologies, USA) and labeled with the TMTsixplex™ isobaric labeling kit (#90110, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The labeled samples were mixed and fractionated by high-pH reverse-phase liquid chromatography. Final samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS using an Orbitrap Exploris™ 480 mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) coupled with an EASY-nLC 1200 system in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode at a resolution of 120,000. Raw MS data were searched against the UniProt Mouse database using Proteome Discoverer software (v2.4), with trypsin as the proteolytic enzyme, allowing up to two missed cleavages. Precursor ion mass tolerance was set at 10 ppm, fragment ion tolerance at 0.02 Da, and the false discovery rate (FDR) was controlled below 1%. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were identified using a threshold of p < 0.05 and |log2 FC| > 1. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks were constructed using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/), and hub proteins with high connectivity were screened using Cytoscape software (v3.9.1) with the cytoHubba plugin. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using the R package clusterProfiler (v4.0.5), with significance defined as p.adjust < 0.05. The classification and visualization of enrichment results were conducted using ggplot2 (v3.4.0).

## Blood analysis
Mice were euthanized 28 days after administration of anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA. The PBS-injected group served as the control and was sacrificed at the same time point (n = 5 per group). Blood samples (~1 mL per mouse) were collected via the retro-orbital venous plexus. Serum biochemical parameters were analyzed using commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute), including alkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBIL), urea, and creatinine (CRE). Hematological parameters were measured using a veterinary hematology analyzer, including red blood cells (RBC), white blood cells (WBC), platelets (PLT), mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC), hemoglobin (HGB), and hematocrit (HCT) [ ].

## Histological analysis
Major organs, including the heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney, were harvested at the end of the 28-day experimental period. Tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. The sections were stained with H&E and examined under a digital microscope (Olympus, Japan) [ ].

## Statistical analysis
All experimental data are presented as mean ± SD. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test, while comparisons among three or more groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s or Bonferroni post hoc tests. For non-normally distributed data, the Mann-Whitney U test was applied. Survival curves were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Single-cell and multi-omics datasets were analyzed using tools such as Seurat, MAST, and Scanpy. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## The Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform was successfully constructed and exhibits physicochemical stability features
A multifunctional nanoplatform with targeted delivery and oxidative stress–responsive properties was constructed by sequentially synthesizing MnO2 nanozyme cores, encapsulating them within a ZIF8 shell, loading the hydrophobic drug TSA, and conjugating anti-ICAM1 antibodies via EDC/NHS chemistry. The final construct was designated Anti-ICAM1-MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA (Fig.  A).
 High-resolution TEM further demonstrated well-defined lattice fringes within the ZIF8 shell, with an interplanar spacing of 0.31 nm corresponding to the characteristic crystal planes of ZIF8 (Fig.  B). SEM demonstrated uniformly spherical particles with smooth surfaces and minimal aggregation (Fig.  C). Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) line-scanning confirmed spatial elemental separation, with Mn predominantly localized in the core and Zn/N enriched in the shell region, indicating successful encapsulation of MnO2 by the ZIF8 framework (Fig.  C). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns displayed characteristic diffraction peaks of both MnO2 and ZIF8, confirming preservation of their crystalline structures. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) further detected the characteristic absorption peaks of TSA together with Zn–N and C = N vibrations, verifying successful incorporation of both the drug and the framework.
DLS analysis showed that antibody conjugation increased the hydrodynamic diameter from 112.8 ± 7.5 nm (MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA) to 123.6 ± 8.4 nm (anti-ICAM1–MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA). The zeta potential simultaneously increased from +6.1 ± 1.2 mV to +12.3 ± 1.7 mV, indicating the formation of a positively charged surface layer that may enhance electrostatic interactions with negatively charged cell membranes (Fig.  D–E). The polydispersity index (PDI) remained below 0.15, suggesting a narrow size distribution and good colloidal stability.
Immunogold TEM demonstrated the localization of anti-ICAM-1 antibodies on the nanoparticle surface (Fig.  F). WB analysis confirmed antibody conjugation, showing a clear heavy-chain band, with a loading capacity of 26.4 ± 3.2 µg/mg and a conjugation efficiency of 52.8% (Fig.  G). XPS analysis revealed increased nitrogen content and enhanced C–N and N–C = O signals after modification, consistent with successful EDC/NHS-mediated coupling. UV–Vis spectroscopy further showed an intensified protein absorption peak at 280 nm. In addition, the surface contact angle decreased from 75.2° to 56.7°, indicating increased hydrophilicity and improved dispersion in physiological environments.
To evaluate the drug-loading capacity and stimulus-responsive release behavior of the anti-ICAM1–MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform, a schematic illustration of drug encapsulation and responsive release was constructed (Figure A). HPLC showed that nanoparticles loaded 37.2 ± 2.9 µg TSA per mg with an encapsulation efficiency of 74.6% ± 3.1% (Figure B). Drug release studies under different conditions revealed minimal TSA release at neutral pH (7.4), whereas acidic environments (pH 6.5 and 5.0) and oxidative stress (1 mM H2O2) significantly accelerated release (Figure C-D). Kinetic modeling indicated that release under acidic and oxidative conditions followed the Higuchi diffusion model (R2 = 0.945 and 0.928), while release at neutral pH approximated a zero-order profile (Figure E). TEM imaging further showed structural stability under neutral conditions but partial shell degradation in acidic environments (Figure F), confirming the environmental responsiveness of ZIF8.
Confocal fluorescence imaging demonstrated markedly enhanced uptake of Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA in inflammation-stimulated HUVECs, AECⅡ cells, and alveolar organoids compared with non-targeted nanoparticles (Fig.  H–J). Pre-blocking with free anti-ICAM1 antibodies significantly reduced nanoparticle internalization, indicating ICAM-1-dependent targeting.
Further intracellular imaging in AECⅡ cells using Cy5-labeled nanoparticles and the mitochondrial probe MitoTracker Green revealed pronounced colocalization with the mitochondrial network. Z-stack three-dimensional reconstruction showed linear or ring-like nanoparticle distributions surrounding the nucleus. The Pearson correlation coefficient reached 0.75, whereas antibody pre-blocking reduced the coefficient to 0.25, confirming the specificity of mitochondrial-proximal targeting (Fig.  K-L).

## The nanoplatform retains POD-Like and SOD-Like activities and effectively scavenges ROS
To comprehensively evaluate the antioxidative performance of the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform, in vitro enzymatic assays were conducted to assess nanozyme functionality. POD-like activity was assessed using a TMB-H2O2 colorimetric system. The nanoparticles rapidly catalyzed the oxidation of TMB, producing a characteristic blue-colored product. Correspondingly, absorbance at 450 nm increased markedly within 10 min and was significantly higher than that of the blank control group (Figure A). Michaelis-Menten kinetics further confirmed the enzymatic behavior of the reaction, with a calculated Kₘ value of 0.31 mM and Vmax of 5.68 × 10− 8 mol/min, indicating high substrate affinity and catalytic efficiency (Figure B). SOD-like activity was evaluated using the NBT reduction assay. The MnO2-based nanoplatform significantly inhibited superoxide anion generation, as evidenced by a marked reduction in absorbance at 560 nm (Figure C). Notably, the ZIF8 shell did not compromise the catalytic function of the MnO2 core and maintained stable enzymatic activity across a physiological pH range (5.0–7.4.0.4) and temperature ranges (25–37 °C), demonstrating that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform preserves the intrinsic enzyme-mimetic properties of MnO2 and exhibits strong ROS-scavenging capability.

## The nano-system effectively scavenges cellular and mitochondrial ROS and Alleviates oxidative stress in AECⅡ Cells
To elucidate the mechanism by which the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nano-system mitigates oxidative stress in AECⅡ, a 3D schematic diagram was constructed to illustrate its pathway for targeted cellular entry, coordinated drug release, and ROS scavenging (Fig.  A). The nanoplatform targets AECⅡ through ICAM-1 binding, while the MnO2 core exhibits SOD- and POD-like activities to eliminate ROS. Meanwhile, the ZIF8 shell disassembles under acidic and ROS-rich conditions, enabling TSA release to further enhance antioxidant capacity and alleviate mitochondrial oxidative damage.
 To quantify intracellular and mitochondrial ROS levels, DCFH-DA and MitoSOX fluorescent probes were employed (Fig.  B). LPS stimulation significantly increased intracellular ROS levels in AECⅡ cells. Treatment with anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA markedly reduced green fluorescence intensity, indicating effective ROS scavenging. Notably, the antioxidant effect was superior to that observed in the MnO2@ZIF8 and MnO2@ZIF8@TSA groups (Fig.  C).
MitoSOX staining further demonstrated significant mitochondrial ROS accumulation following LPS stimulation. Treatment with the anti-ICAM1-modified nanoplatform markedly reduced mitochondrial ROS levels (Fig.  D-E). Kinetic analysis of ROS clearance revealed that the ICAM1-modified group exerted a rapid antioxidant effect within 2 h, and maintained a low-ROS state for up to 24 h (Fig.  F). Mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) was assessed using the JC-1 fluorescent probe. LPS exposure significantly decreased the red/green fluorescence ratio, indicating loss of membrane potential. In contrast, the anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA group markedly restored red JC-1 aggregate fluorescence, showing superior efficacy compared to the non-modified nanoplatform (Fig.  G-H).
In summary, the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform not only exhibits rapid and efficient antioxidant activity but also alleviates both total cellular and mitochondrial ROS at multiple levels, thereby effectively restoring mitochondrial functional status and demonstrating substantial therapeutic potential against oxidative stress.

## Nano-system effectively suppresses LPS-induced senescence phenotypes and senescence-associated factors in AECⅡ cells
To investigate the anti-senescence effects of the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nano-system under oxidative stress conditions, a schematic illustration was generated to depict its proposed mechanism, including targeted delivery, ROS scavenging, and suppression of senescence-associated signaling pathways (Fig.  A).
 Cellular senescence was subsequently evaluated using SA-β-Gal staining. LPS stimulation markedly induced senescence in AECⅡ cells, as evidenced by a significant increase in the proportion of blue-positive cells. Treatment with MnO₂@ZIF8 and MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA reduced the percentage of senescent cells, whereas anti-ICAM1–MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA further significantly decreased SA-β-Gal positivity, indicating superior anti-senescence efficacy (Fig.  B-C).
At the molecular level, WB analysis revealed that LPS stimulation significantly upregulated the expression of senescence-associated proteins p16Ink4a, p21Cip1, and p53 compared with the control group. Treatment with the Anti-ICAM1-modified nano-system markedly suppressed their expression (Fig.  D). Quantitative PCR analysis further confirmed consistent trends at the transcriptional level in the Anti-ICAM1 group (Fig.  E). Dual immunofluorescence co-staining also supported these findings: in the LPS group, the co-expression of the senescence marker p16 and the AECⅡ-specific marker SP-C was markedly elevated, while Anti-ICAM1 nano-system intervention significantly reduced this co-localization (Fig.  F-G), indicating targeted modulation of senescence pathways in AECⅡ cells.
Furthermore, LPS stimulation significantly increased the mRNA expression of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) cytokines, including IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α. Treatment with the Anti-ICAM1-modified nanoplatform markedly reduced the expression of these inflammatory mediators (Fig.  H).
Collectively, these results demonstrate that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform effectively suppresses LPS-induced senescence in AECⅡ cells by inhibiting the p16/p21/p53 signaling axis and attenuating SASP-associated inflammatory responses.

## Anti-ICAM1 nano-platform activates Nrf2 and inhibits the IL-33/ST2-NF-κB inflammatory axis signaling pathway
To further elucidate the anti-inflammatory and antioxidant mechanisms of the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nano-system in AECⅡ cells, a schematic diagram was constructed illustrating the coordinated regulation of the IL-33/ST2–NF-κB inflammatory axis and the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway (Fig.  A). Upon ICAM1-mediated endocytosis into AECⅡ cells, the nano-system releases TSA and MnO2. Within the ROS-enriched microenvironment, MnO2 decomposes to generate O2, alleviating local hypoxia and promoting Nrf2 nuclear translocation. Concurrently, TSA enhances Nrf2 transcriptional activity by inhibiting HDACs and suppresses NF-κB pathway activation. This dual mechanism results in the downregulation of IL-33, ST2, p-NF-κB, and phosphorylated p38 (p-p38), thereby limiting inflammatory signal amplification.
 Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that LPS stimulation markedly increased IL-33 and ST2 expression in AECⅡ cells, presenting as strong clustered signals indicative of inflammatory activation (Fig.  B). WB analysis confirmed significant upregulation of IL-33 and ST2 proteins compared with the control group. In addition, phosphorylation levels of downstream signaling molecules p-NF-κB (p65) and p-p38 were markedly elevated (Fig.  C). Treatment with Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA significantly reduced the expression of IL-33, ST2, p-NF-κB, and p-p38, indicating effective suppression of the IL-33/ST2-mediated inflammatory signaling cascade.
Regarding oxidative stress regulation, immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated that Nrf2 remained predominantly localized in the cytoplasm following LPS treatment, suggesting impaired antioxidant signaling. In contrast, Anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform treatment induced pronounced Nrf2 nuclear translocation (Fig.  D). Nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation followed by WB further confirmed a marked increase in nuclear Nrf2 expression. Concurrently, downstream antioxidant enzymes SOD2 and GPX4 were significantly upregulated (Fig.  E). qPCR analysis corroborated these findings, showing that mRNA levels of Nrf2, SOD2, and HO-1 were elevated in the Anti-ICAM1 group compared to the LPS group (Fig.  F).
Collectively, these results indicate that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform exerts dual regulatory effects by activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway while simultaneously suppressing the IL-33/ST2–NF-κB inflammatory signaling axis. This coordinated modulation effectively attenuates oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in AECⅡ cells.

## The nanoplatform significantly alleviates oxidative stress and preserves structural integrity in organoid models
A 3D alveolar organoid model was established by co-culturing AECⅡ cells, followed by LPS stimulation to induce senescence (Fig.  A). After 7 days of culture, well-defined spherical organoids with central luminal structures were observed. Immunofluorescence staining for DAPI and SP-C confirmed that the organoids were predominantly composed of AECⅡ cells (Fig.  B). Upon LPS challenge, organoids exhibited pronounced shrinkage and collapse, with disrupted luminal architecture and significant reductions in both surface area and diameter. In contrast, treatment with the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform effectively preserved organoid morphology compared to the LPS group, and maintenance of luminal integrity (Fig.  C).
 Calcein-AM/PI staining further demonstrated the cytoprotective effects of the nanoplatform. LPS exposure markedly reduced the proportion of viable (green) cells, whereas Anti-ICAM1-modified nanoplatform treatment significantly restored organoid viability (Fig.  D).
DHE staining revealed a substantial increase in nuclear ROS accumulation in LPS-treated organoids, as indicated by elevated red fluorescence. Treatment with Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA significantly reduced ROS levels, approaching those observed in the untreated control group (Fig.  E). Moreover, 8-OHdG immunofluorescence staining demonstrated that LPS exposure significantly elevated DNA oxidative damage, with an increased proportion of 8-OHdG-positive cells. In the Anti-ICAM1 treatment group, the proportion of 8-OHdG-positive cells was reduced relative to the LPS group (Fig.  F). Combined with organoid morphological scoring and quantitative image analysis (Fig.  G), these findings demonstrate that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform effectively reduces oxidative stress, preserves structural integrity, and enhances functional viability in the three-dimensional alveolar organoid model.

## The nanoplatform significantly ameliorates CLP-induced ALI and improves pulmonary function
In a CLP-induced mouse model of sepsis-induced ALI, animals exhibited marked respiratory distress and impaired pulmonary function. The mechanistic illustration (Fig. A) summarizes how the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform mitigates alveolar damage, suppresses inflammation, alleviates oxidative stress, and preserves lung function via multiple synergistic mechanisms.
Pulmonary function testing demonstrated that mice in the CLP group exhibited a significant increase in airway resistance (Raw) and a marked reduction in lung compliance (Crs) compared with the Sham group, indicating severe pulmonary dysfunction. Treatment with anti-ICAM1–MnO2@ZIF8@TSA or metformin significantly decreased Raw and increased Crs, showing superior therapeutic efficacy compared with the MnO2@ZIF8@TSA and MnO2@ZIF8 groups (Fig. B).
Micro-CT imaging further confirmed structural improvement. The CLP group displayed extensive pulmonary consolidation and collapse of ventilatory structures. In contrast, the anti-ICAM1-treated and metformin-treated groups showed restored lung translucency, clearer airway architecture, and lung volumes approaching those of the Sham group, with significantly greater improvement than the MnO2@ZIF8@TSA and MnO2@ZIF8 groups (Fig. C). Measurement of the lung wet-to-dry (W/D) weight ratio revealed a marked increase in the CLP group, indicating severe pulmonary edema. This ratio was significantly reduced in both the anti-ICAM1 and metformin groups (Fig. D).
Histological evaluation by H&E staining showed disrupted alveolar architecture, extensive neutrophil infiltration, and pronounced interstitial edema in the CLP group. Treatment with anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA or metformin markedly reduced the histopathological score and improved lung structural integrity, outperforming the MnO2@ZIF8@TSA and MnO2@ZIF8 groups (Fig. E). Masson’s trichrome staining demonstrated substantial interstitial collagen deposition in the CLP group. While MnO2@ZIF8@TSA and MnO₂@ZIF8 treatments produced modest improvement, collagen accumulation was significantly reduced in the anti-ICAM1 and metformin groups (Fig. F). TUNEL staining further revealed a pronounced increase in apoptotic cells in the CLP group, which was significantly attenuated following anti-ICAM1 or metformin treatment (Fig. G).
In summary, the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform demonstrated significant protective effects on lung function in the CLP model by suppressing inflammation and oxidative damage, improving tissue architecture and cellular viability, thereby providing strong support for its translational potential in clinical intervention for ARDS.

## The nanoplatform enhances TSA delivery efficiency in lung tissue and achieves inflammation-targeted distribution
To evaluate the lung-targeting delivery capacity of the nanoplatform, IVIS was used to track DiR-labeled nanoparticles. As shown in Fig. A, the anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA group exhibited strong fluorescence signals in the bilateral lung regions as early as 1 h after intravenous injection, which were markedly higher than those observed in the non-conjugated MnO2@ZIF8@TSA group. Fluorescence intensity peaked at 6 h and remained detectable at 24 h, indicating sustained pulmonary retention of the targeted nanoplatform. Quantitative analysis further confirmed significantly higher pulmonary fluorescence intensity in the Anti-ICAM1 group compared with the non-targeted group (Fig. B).
To further assess the spatial distribution of nanoparticles within lung tissue, confocal immunofluorescence imaging was performed on lung sections. In the anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA-treated group, fluorescence signals were predominantly enriched around SP-C+ alveolar type II epithelial cells, displaying a continuous and uniform pericellular distribution pattern (Fig.  C). In contrast, the non-targeted MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA group showed weaker and more dispersed fluorescence signals. Quantitative analysis confirmed significantly enhanced fluorescence intensity within SP-C⁺ regions in the Anti-ICAM1-modified group, indicating efficient targeting and retention of the nanoplatform within inflammation-associated AECⅡ cells.
To precisely quantify TSA distribution across different tissues, LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on lung, blood, and liver samples. As shown in Fig.  D, TSA concentrations in lung tissue were significantly higher in the Anti-ICAM1 group than in the non-targeted MnO2@ZIF8@TSA group, whereas drug levels in blood and liver were markedly lower, suggesting enhanced pulmonary targeting with reduced systemic exposure.
Pharmacokinetic analysis further demonstrated that the pulmonary half-life of TSA was significantly prolonged in the Anti-ICAM1 group (9.2 ± 1.1 h) compared with the non-targeted group (5.6 ± 0.9 h; p < 0.01) (Fig.  E), indicating sustained local drug release.
Collectively (Fig.  F), these findings demonstrate that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform achieves efficient lung-targeted delivery, prolonged pulmonary retention, and improved TSA biodistribution, supporting its therapeutic potential for inflammatory lung injury and aging-related pulmonary disorders.

To further elucidate the mechanisms by which the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform regulates senescence signaling in lung tissue, a comprehensive evaluation was performed using immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence co-staining, WB, and qPCR (Fig. A).
In the CLP model, p16, p21, and p53 were markedly upregulated in the alveolar epithelium, showing strong and diffuse positive staining by immunohistochemistry (Fig.  B). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform remodels the CLP-induced pulmonary senescence microenvironment by suppressing the p16/p21/p53 senescence axis and the IL-33/ST2–NF-κB inflammatory pathway while activating Nrf2-mediated antioxidant responses.

## ICAM1 nanoplatform remodels pulmonary metabolic networks to improve energy homeostasis and oxidative stress
To systematically evaluate the regulatory effects of the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform on the metabolic profile of sepsis-induced lung injury, we performed untargeted metabolomic analysis of lung tissues from CLP model mice and treatment group mice (n = 6) (Fig. A). PCA revealed clear separation between the two groups along the first (PC1) and second (PC2) principal components, indicating substantial alterations in the global metabolic profile following treatment (Fig. B). Multivariate analysis further identified 47 differential metabolites, including 24 upregulated and 23 downregulated metabolites in the treatment group (Fig. C).
 KEGG pathway enrichment analysis indicated that these differential metabolites were mainly concentrated in pathways related to energy metabolism and antioxidative processes, including the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, arginine biosynthesis, glutamine metabolism, pyruvate metabolism, and taurine and hypotaurine metabolism (Fig. D). Pathway impact analysis (bubble plot) further confirmed the TCA cycle, glutamate metabolism, and arginine metabolism as key enriched pathways (Fig. E). Pathway mapping of the TCA cycle revealed significant alterations in several central intermediates, including citrate, fumarate, and malate (Fig. F).
Finally, visualization of representative differential metabolites, including malate, succinate, α-ketoglutarate, cysteine, and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺), revealed a marked recovery trend in antioxidant and energy metabolism indicators in the treatment group (Fig. G).
Restoration of malate and succinate, two key intermediates of the TCA cycle, suggests recovery of mitochondrial metabolic activity and improved TCA cycle flux. Such metabolic normalization is generally associated with enhanced oxidative phosphorylation and improved cellular energy status, consistent with the concept that mitochondrial dysfunction represents a hallmark of cellular senescence [ ]. In addition, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a central coenzyme in cellular redox reactions, is frequently depleted in senescent cells. Restoration of NAD⁺ levels may activate NAD+-dependent deacetylases such as SIRT1, thereby promoting mitochondrial homeostasis and suppressing senescence-associated transcriptional programs, ultimately contributing to improved cellular function and tissue repair [ , ].

To investigate the regulatory effects of the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform in sepsis-induced ALI, we performed scRNA-seq on lung tissues from CLP model mice and nanoplatform-treated mice, generating ~17,000 high-quality single-cell transcriptomes (Fig.  A). Cell cycle scoring indicated balanced distribution of cell cycle states in both groups without significant bias (Fig.  B). After batch effect correction using the Harmony algorithm, samples were well integrated in low-dimensional space (Fig.  C).
 Clustering analysis identified 18 cellular subclusters, which were annotated into nine major cell types based on canonical marker gene expression: endothelial cells, T cells, macrophages, granulocytes, NK cells, epithelial cells, B cells, fibroblasts, and undefined populations (Figs.  D-E). DotPlot and FeaturePlot further validated the specific expression patterns of key markers (e.g., Cd68, Cd3d, S100a8, Epcam), confirming accurate and reliable cell annotation (Figs.  F-G).
Cell composition analysis revealed increased proportions of inflammatory cells, including macrophages and granulocytes, in the CLP group. In contrast, nanoplatform treatment increased the proportions of endothelial and epithelial cells while reducing macrophages, granulocytes, and fibroblasts (Fig.  H). Notably, the increase in epithelial cells accompanied by a decrease in fibroblasts suggests enhanced epithelial repair and suppression of fibrotic cell expansion.
In multiple inflammatory subclusters, key inflammatory factors (e.g., Il1b, Cxcl1, S100a8) were broadly downregulated in the intervention group (Fig.  I), further supporting the ability of the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform to alleviate immune activation and inflammatory injury in lung tissue.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis using Monocle 2 revealed a continuous developmental trajectory from early to late cellular states (Fig.  C). Mapping Seurat-defined clusters onto the trajectory demonstrated branch-specific state transitions under disease conditions (Fig.  D). Trajectory-associated gene dynamics showed that the senescence-related gene Cdkn2a (p16) increased at intermediate-to-late states (Fig.  E), while the alveolar function-related gene Sftpb exhibited a biphasic pattern, initially increasing and subsequently declining along the trajectory (Fig.  F), suggesting dynamic regulation during alveolar epithelial maintenance.
 To further evaluate functional states of AECⅡ cells, module scores were calculated for senescence- and inflammation-related gene sets. Compared with the CLP group, nanoplatform-treated AECⅡ cells showed significant reductions in key senescence and inflammatory modules, including Il6, Cxcl1, Cdkn2a, and Mmp3 (Figs.  G-J). Moreover, activity scores of the IL-33 and NF-κB signaling pathways were markedly reduced in the treatment group (Figs.  K-L), indicating that the ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform effectively alleviated senescence of AECⅡ cells by reducing proinflammatory factor release and signaling activation.

## Anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform significantly improves pulmonary function and structural integrity in a sepsis-induced ALI model
To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform in vivo, a sepsis-induced ALI model was established via CLP. Mice received intravenous tail injections of the nanoplatform 6 h post-surgery, followed by daily treatments for three consecutive days (n = 15/group). Pulmonary function assessment revealed that CLP-induced mice exhibited a significant decrease in Crs and an increase in Raw. Notably, Anti-ICAM1 treatment significantly improved Crs and reduced Raw, approaching levels observed in the Sham group (Fig.  A).
 Histopathological evaluation using H&E staining demonstrated severe alveolar structural disruption, interstitial edema, and inflammatory cell infiltration in CLP mice, with an Ashcroft injury score of 4.1 ± 0.6. Anti-ICAM1 treatment substantially restored alveolar architecture and reduced the injury score (Fig.  B). TUNEL staining revealed extensive apoptosis of alveolar epithelial cells in the CLP group, which was markedly reduced following Anti-ICAM1 treatment (Fig.  C).
Three-dimensional pulmonary microCT imaging demonstrated extensive parenchymal collapse and ventilation deficits in the CLP group, whereas Anti-ICAM1 treatment markedly improved lung volume and ventilation architecture (Fig.  D). The oxygenation index (PaO2/FiO2) significantly increased from 162 ± 24 mmHg in the CLP group to 276 ± 21 mmHg in the Anti-ICAM1 group (p < 0.001), while the lung W/D weight ratio decreased from 6.7 ± 0.5 to 5.0 ± 0.3, indicating a substantial alleviation of pulmonary edema (Fig.  E).
Near-infrared fluorescence imaging and Raman spectroscopy further confirmed the targeted delivery capability of the nanoplatform. Fluorescence intensity of Anti-ICAM1-modified nanoparticles in the lungs of CLP mice was 2.4-fold higher than that of the non-targeted group (Fig.  F). Raman spectral analysis also revealed a pronounced TSA characteristic peak in lung tissues, indicating efficient drug accumulation within inflamed pulmonary regions.

## Anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform significantly improves survival rate and prolongs lifespan in septic mice
To comprehensively evaluate the long-term therapeutic efficacy of the Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform in sepsis-induced ALI, a 28-day survival study was conducted using the CLP mouse model (n = 15 per group). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that the CLP group exhibited a mortality rate of 60% within 48 h after surgery, with only 20% survival remaining by day 7 (Fig.  A). In contrast, treatment with anti-ICAM1–MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA significantly prolonged survival time, increasing the 7-day survival rate to 73.3% and maintaining a 60.0% survival rate at day 28, comparable to the effect of metformin.
 By comparison, the MnO₂@ZIF8 and MnO₂@ZIF8@TSA groups showed only modest improvement, without reaching statistical significance. Notably, combination therapy with an IL-33 neutralizing antibody further improved the 28-day survival rate in the anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform group. Administration of the IL-33 antibody at 6 h after CLP induction yielded the most pronounced survival benefit (Fig.  B), suggesting that the IL-33 signaling axis plays a pivotal role in the pathophysiology and therapeutic response of sepsis-induced ALI.
To investigate the underlying mechanisms contributing to improved long-term survival, pulmonary function, inflammatory status, oxidative stress, and cellular senescence markers were comprehensively analyzed. Pearson correlation analysis (Fig.  C) revealed a positive correlation between 28-day survival rate and lung compliance (Crs), and a negative correlation with SA-β-Gal positivity. Furthermore, both the anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform group and the metformin-treated group exhibited significantly reduced expression of TNF-α and IL-6 (Fig.  D), along with increased expression of SOD2 and HO-1 (Fig.  E). The enhanced Raman signal of TSA further indicated synergistic effects of drug enrichment and downstream pathway activation (Fig.  F).
To evaluate the long-term in vivo biosafety of the nanoplatform, serum biochemical analysis, hematological examination, and histological assessment of major organs were performed. Serum biochemical analysis demonstrated that mice injected with anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA showed comparable levels of ALP, ALT, AST, TBIL, BUN, and CREA to those of the Sham control group at 28 days post-administration, with no statistically significant differences observed (p > 0.05, Figure S3). Hematological analysis further revealed that parameters including RBC, WBC, PLT, MCV, MCHC, HGB, and HCT remained within normal physiological ranges at all examined time points, with no significant differences compared to the control group (Figure S4). In addition, H&E staining of major organs, including the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, and stomach, showed no evident inflammatory infiltration, cellular necrosis, or structural abnormalities, indicating the absence of detectable systemic toxicity within the 28-day observation period (Figure S5). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the nanoplatform exhibits favorable biocompatibility and does not induce apparent systemic toxicity upon long-term in vivo administration.

## Discussion
Sepsis-induced ALI represents a clinically prevalent and highly fatal complication in critical care medicine. Targeting the core pathogenic mechanisms—senescence of AECⅡ and oxidative stress dysregulation—we developed a multifunctional nanoplatform (Anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA) with active targeting, pathological responsiveness, and synergistic therapeutic capabilities. The nanoplatform demonstrated selective accumulation in inflamed pulmonary tissue, efficiently scavenged both intracellular and mitochondrial ROS, activated the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathway, and suppressed IL-33/ST2 axis-driven senescence signaling. Multilevel validation across CLP mouse models, alveolar organoid systems, and primary AECⅡ cultures revealed significant improvements in pulmonary compliance, oxygenation indices, and overall survival following treatment. Structurally, the nanoplatform restored alveolar architecture and preserved the stem-like phenotype of AECⅡ cells. Mechanistically, the intervention suppressed expansion of the p16+ senescent AECⅡ population and promoted cell-state transitions toward earlier epithelial lineages with enhanced regenerative potential. These findings suggest the existence of a coordinated anti-senescence regulatory framework integrating oxidative stress control, transcriptional regulation, and epithelial differentiation dynamics. Compared with conventional inflammation-focused supportive therapies, this study proposes a novel therapeutic paradigm centered on “antioxidant intervention–anti-senescence regulation–multi-omics remodeling.” By directly targeting AECⅡ senescence, the proposed nanoplatform provides a mechanistically grounded and translationally promising strategy for the treatment of ALI and other severe pulmonary injuries (Fig.  ).
 Previous studies have established that dysfunction of AECⅡ cells plays a critical role in the progression of ALI. Oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired regenerative capacity have been identified as major contributors to alveolar barrier disruption and respiratory failure [ , ]. While concepts such as “inflammaging” and the SASP are widely discussed in senescence research, direct experimental evidence confirming whether AECⅡ undergoes a programmatic and reversible form of senescence remains lacking [ ]. The IL-33/ST2 signaling axis, a canonical alarmin pathway, has been implicated in amplifying inflammatory responses across multiple pulmonary disease models. However, its potential role in regulating senescence signaling specifically within AECⅡ cells has not yet been clearly defined [ ]. Although the Nrf2 pathway is well established as a central regulator of antioxidant defense, and experimental evidence has demonstrated the induction of downstream enzymes such as HO-1 in ALI models, the functional association between Nrf2 signaling and the expression of SOD2 and GPX4 remains incompletely defined. Specifically, it is still unclear whether Nrf2 directly regulates the transcription of SOD2 and GPX4 or whether their coordinated expression reflects indirect or context-dependent mechanisms. Furthermore, the role of Nrf2 in maintaining alveolar epithelial stemness and in reshaping senescence-associated transcriptional programs warrants further in-depth investigation [ ].
During multilevel mechanistic investigations, several unexpected yet informative findings emerged. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed a co-expression relationship between IL-33 and p16 in AECⅡ cells, suggesting that IL-33 may promote AECⅡ senescence through induction of p16 expression. Notably, treatment with the Anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform reversed inflammation- and senescence-associated transcriptional signatures, including IL-33, Ccl2, and Mmp3. Further clustering and pseudotime analyses of AECⅡ subpopulations showed that cells from the treatment group were predominantly distributed in early trajectory states, indicating that the intervention not only suppressed senescence activation but also promoted re-differentiation toward regenerative epithelial lineage. Consistently, in the 3D AECⅡ-derived organoid model, LPS stimulation induced luminal collapse, ROS accumulation, and increased SA-β-Gal activity, recapitulating a senescence-like microenvironment. Anti-ICAM1 nanoplatform treatment restored organoid structural integrity and reinstated the expression of progenitor markers SOX9 and ABCA3, indicating preservation of alveolar progenitor identity and re-establishment of a regenerative niche. Collectively, these findings enrich the mechanistic axis of “anti-senescence - reprogramming - functional restoration” and provide compelling biological evidence supporting the reversibility of alveolar injury and senescence.
From the perspective of metabolic reprogramming, the metabolomic analysis in this study provides mechanistic insight into the energetic and biosynthetic basis underlying the reversal of AECⅡ senescence. As key intermediates of the TCA cycle, the restoration of malate and succinate levels suggests improved mitochondrial metabolic function and recovery of TCA cycle flux. Such metabolic normalization is generally associated with enhanced oxidative phosphorylation and improved cellular bioenergetic status, consistent with the notion that mitochondrial dysfunction represents a hallmark of cellular senescence [ ]. Moreover, NAD+ serves as an essential cofactor in redox reactions, and its depletion is a common feature of senescent cells. Restoration of NAD+ levels can activate NAD+-dependent deacetylases, such as SIRT1, thereby promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis. In addition, NAD⁺ repletion suppresses senescence-associated transcriptional programs, ultimately improving cellular function and supporting tissue regeneration [ , ].
The design of this targeted nanoplatform offers a novel therapeutic strategy for inflammatory-senescence-coupled diseases such as sepsis-induced ALI. ICAM-1 is highly expressed on inflamed endothelial cells and activated AECⅡ, providing a molecular basis for Anti-ICAM1-mediated targeting. Our findings demonstrate that the nano-system achieves lesion-specific accumulation via antibody recognition. IVIS imaging, Raman spectroscopy, and LC-MS/MS quantification consistently show that pulmonary enrichment efficiency is 2–3 times higher than that of the non-targeted counterpart, while off-target accumulation in the liver and kidneys is significantly reduced, thereby improving both biosafety and therapeutic efficacy. Although in vivo quantitative flow cytometric analysis of nanoparticle uptake was not performed due to technical limitations, such as low recovery efficiency of AECⅡ cells during in situ lung tissue isolation and instability of fluorescence signals, multiple lines of evidence support the specificity and functional advantage of ICAM-1 antibody-mediated targeting. Specifically, in vivo IVIS quantification using the IgG-conjugated control group, together with in vitro competitive blocking and flow cytometry data, collectively demonstrate that ICAM-1 antibody modification confers distinct targeting specificity and enhanced delivery efficiency in vivo. From a clinical perspective, the system exhibits physicochemical properties compatible with pulmonary delivery routes such as aerosol inhalation, offering the potential for targeted inhalation therapy that minimizes systemic toxicity. The combination of this platform with IL-33 neutralizing antibodies also demonstrates promise for multi-target synergistic intervention, providing a basis for future drug combination optimization strategies. Together, these results expand the clinical applicability of nano-systems in pulmonary diseases and offer a realistic translational path for the precision treatment of respiratory disorders associated with cellular aging.
Current clinical management of sepsis-associated acute lung injury (ALI/ARDS) remains largely supportive, including mechanical ventilation, fluid management, and conventional anti-inflammatory agents such as glucocorticoids. However, these approaches often fail to substantially improve prognosis and are associated with systemic adverse effects, including immunosuppression and an increased risk of multi-organ dysfunction. Moreover, traditional small-molecule anti-inflammatory or antioxidant drugs are limited in clinical efficacy due to widespread systemic distribution and insufficient tissue specificity [ ]. Nanomedicine-based drug delivery systems, such as liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles, have demonstrated improved tissue targeting and therapeutic potential in preclinical ALI models. Compared with these approaches, the anti-ICAM1-MnO2@ZIF8@TSA nanoplatform developed in the present study achieves active targeting of inflamed pulmonary regions while incorporating microenvironment-responsive release under acidic and oxidative conditions. Moreover, the system facilitates coordinated modulation of multiple pathological pathways, including ROS scavenging, inhibition of the IL-33/ST2 signaling axis, and activation of Nrf2-dependent antioxidant responses. These integrated functional advantages significantly enhanced therapeutic efficacy across multi-level experimental models and reduced the risk of off-target organ accumulation. Collectively, these comparisons highlight the innovation of the present platform in precise lesion targeting, responsive functionality, and pathological process intervention, offering a promising strategy to overcome the limitations of conventional therapeutic approaches.
Despite the comprehensive mechanistic insights and functional validations presented in this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the murine CLP model and AECⅡ-derived organoids exhibit high experimental reproducibility, inherent pathological differences from human lung tissue may limit the translational relevance of the findings, underscoring the need for further clinical validation. Second, the relatively limited sample size may reduce the statistical power for detecting differences in certain cellular subpopulations; therefore, future studies with larger cohorts are required to confirm the robustness of the observed effects. Third, although single-cell analyses provided a detailed regulatory landscape, direct evidence for transcriptional regulatory binding at key signaling loci remains lacking. Additional approaches, such as ChIP-seq, will be necessary to validate these regulatory interactions. Moreover, differences between the murine CLP model and human sepsis-associated ALI exist in terms of AECⅡ biology, immune responses, and cytokine kinetics [ ]. These variations may influence therapeutic responsiveness and disease progression. Consequently, future translational studies will require optimization of dosage regimens and administration strategies, including aerosolized inhalation delivery, to enhance therapeutic efficacy and clinical applicability [ ].
Building upon the current findings, future research should delve into several critical areas. First, although single-cell transcriptomic analysis and immunocolocalization studies in this work revealed a strong association between IL-33 and ST2 expression in AECⅡ cells, and in vivo IL-33 neutralization experiments supported their functional relevance in sepsis-associated lung injury, it should be emphasized that the existing evidence is largely correlative. At present, these data are insufficient to establish whether IL-33 directly regulates the transcription of ST2 or senescence-associated genes such as p16 and p21 in AECⅡ cells. Future studies will therefore incorporate gain- and loss-of-function approaches to clarify the direct regulatory role of IL-33 signaling. For example, CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) or siRNA-mediated knockdown of IL-33, as well as exogenous administration of recombinant IL-33 protein followed by assessment of p16, p21, and ST2 expression changes, will be employed. Such experiments will enable systematic dissection of the causal role of IL-33 signaling in determining AECⅡ senescence fate and stemness maintenance, thereby refining the proposed inflammation-senescence coupling model presented in this study. Second, further optimization of the Anti-ICAM1 nano-system is needed in terms of dosing regimen, therapeutic time window, and delivery route—such as aerosol inhalation—to better align with clinical application requirements. In terms of microenvironment modeling, more complex AECⅡ-immune-fibroblast co-culture organoid systems should be developed to more accurately recapitulate the injured alveolar niche. Finally, the integration of AI-assisted drug screening and RNA delivery tools could further expand the functional modules and intervention spectrum of the nano-system, paving the way toward personalized therapies for senescence-related pulmonary diseases.
Overall, this study proposes and validates a tri-modal synergistic anti-senescence strategy centered on the axis of “ROS reprogramming (Nrf2 activation)-senescence transcriptional derepression (p16/SASP modulation).” By integrating a nanozyme-based ROS scavenger, a natural product therapeutic, and a targeting antibody into a unified nanoplatform, we establish a coordinated therapeutic framework for functional restoration of AECⅡ cells in sepsis-associated ALI. This work provides a novel conceptual and interventional paradigm for addressing inflammation-senescence coupling in acute lung injury.
