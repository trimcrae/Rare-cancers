## Abstract

## Background
Mandibular bone marrow stem cells (BMSCs) from patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) have poor osteogenic differentiation capacity. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms by which circular RNAs (circRNAs) play specific roles in T2DM will reveal new diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

## Methods
BMSCs with different circ_0054633 expression levels were generated. Furthermore, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity, alizarin red staining (ARS), and transplantation of HA/tricalcium phosphate into BMSCs were performed to detect the osteogenic effects of different levels of circ_0054633 expression in BMSCs in vivo and in vitro.

## Results
In this study, we identified 80 differentially expressed circRNAs in jawbone-derived BMSCs from patients with T2DM. Notably, significant downregulation of circ_0054633 promoted the osteogenic differentiation of these cells in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, circ_0054633 acts as a miRNA sponge; specifically, it actively regulates the expression of RUNX2 by sponging miR-590-3p and thus promoting the osteogenic differentiation of the BMSCs. In addition, we found that circ_0054633 was a direct transcriptional target of RUNX2. RUNX2 overexpression activated the circ_0054633 promoter and promoted the generation of nuclear circ_0054633, whereas RUNX2 knockdown abrogated the osteogenic role of circ_0054633 and formed a circ_005463/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 positive feedback loop.

## Conclusions
Our results suggest that the circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 positive feedback loop promotes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and is expected to be a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for bone regeneration in T2DM.

## Introduction
Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) tend to have impaired calcium and phosphorus metabolism, which affects bone tissue remodelling and disrupts bone tissue regeneration and repair, making diabetes mellitus a risk factor for oral implants [ – ]. Unfortunately, owing to the complexity of the pathogenesis of diabetes, the mechanisms by which diabetes interferes with implant bone bonding are still unclear. 
The osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) is an important factor in implant osseointegration [ , ]. BMSCs are adult stem cells derived from bone marrow that are multipotent and exhibit self-renewal ability [ , ]. After implantation into a patient’s jawbone, alveolar bone BMSCs begin to accumulate at the implant–bone interface, where they continuously proliferate and differentiate into osteoblasts and bone cells under the action of various growth factors. During the implant healing period, the implant gradually achieves stable integration with the jawbone through this process [ , ]. Therefore, the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs is not only a key step in the formation of new bone but also an important link in the formation of implant bone integration after implantation in alveolar bone [ , ]. However, T2DM largely inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, and there is a lack of effective therapies to promote the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in patients with T2DM. Therefore, a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms through which T2DM inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs is essential for the development of novel and effective therapies for the treatment of this disease [ , ].
Circular RNAs [ ](circRNAs) are endogenous noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) without a 5′ end cap or a 3′ end poly(A) tail that form a closed loop structure via covalent bonds. Among ncRNAs, circRNAs have a unique loop structure and were once considered “byproducts” of transcription [ – ]. With the rapid progress of high-throughput sequencing technologies and bioinformatics, an expanding array of circRNAs has been discovered. These molecules are characterized by their stability, wide presence, evolutionary conservation, and tissue-specific expression. They are increasingly recognized for their critical roles in the regulation of gene expression. CircRNAs can be used as biomarkers for diseases and have broad application prospects in clinical diagnosis and treatment [ – ]. These molecules play crucial roles in the development of human physiological diseases, including tumours [ ], immunity [ ], metabolism [ ], infection [ ], and genetic diseases [ ]. CircRNAs have emerged as a prominent area of research because of their ability to sponge microribonucleic acids (miRNAs) and bind and interact with proteins, with the potential to transform novel proteins with functional characteristics similar to those of mRNAs [ , ]. In 2017, Yu et al. were the first to report the link between circRNAs and stem cell differentiation, demonstrating that downstream circRNAs regulate this process via the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism [ ]. More recent studies have identified numerous circRNAs that are differentially expressed during MSC osteogenesis. Some of these circRNAs sponge miRNAs, targeting and regulating functional protein expression to influence the osteogenic differentiation of stem cells [ , ]. In bone tissue, circRNAs are essential for a wide range of cellular processes, such as osteogenic differentiation, osteoclastogenesis and bone remodelling. These circRNAs regulate and modulate key signalling pathways engaged in the maintenance of bone homeostasis through complex multifaceted mechanisms [ ]. Interestingly, circRNAs regulate bone metabolism in a bidirectional manner, with circRNAs promoting bone formation and maintenance as well as osteoclastogenesis and bone resorption [ , ]. An analysis revealed distinctive circRNA expression patterns in the bone tissue of T2DM patients, suggesting their potential as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers. The identification of circRNAs, miRNAs and their target protein regulatory mechanisms may lead to the exploitation of novel therapeutic strategies for the treatment of T2DM-associated disorders of bone metabolism.
In this study, we identified a novel circRNA (hsa_circ_0054633) through RNA sequencing that is downregulated in the jawbone-derived BMSCs of T2DM patients. Next, we investigated the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs modulated by hsa_circ_0054633, the ceRNA mechanism, and the positive feedback regulatory function of target genes. This study explores the circRNA functions and regulatory mechanisms involved in the bone metabolic repair of T2DM, provides new insights for clinical trials of bone defects in T2DM patients, and identifies promising therapeutic targets.

## Materials and methods

## Subject registration and ethical statement
This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Stomatological Hospital, Capital Medical University (approval number: CMUSH-IRB-KJ-YJ-2023-07), and patients provided informed consent. All participants were recruited by a surgeon from the dental implant centre. The T2DM patients were diagnosed with type 2 diabetes by endocrinologists and had HbA1c levels less than 8%. Five patients meeting the blood glucose control criteria and preparing for implantation surgery were included. For comparison, a 1:1 matched non-T2DM group was used, ensuring alignment in terms of age, sex, health status, diabetes type, and implant placement to control for confounding factors. Patients with contraindications for implant surgery, such as cardiovascular or kidney disease or uncontrolled periodontitis, were excluded. The study followed the STROBE guidelines for methodological rigour. (Table S1).

## Cell culture
The primary culture method used in this study for the human jawbone-derived BMSCs has been previously described [ ]. Stem cells undergo multidirectional differentiation into bone, fat, and cartilage, and flow cytometric marker identification revealed the negative expression of CD34 and CD45 but the positive expression of CD44 and CD105 (Figure S1). The HEK293T cells were obtained from SIBS, CAS, China.

## BMSC osteogenic induction and analysis
BMSCs were seeded at 5 × 10^5 cells per well in 6-well plates and cultured in an osteogenic medium for 14 days using a StemPro osteogenic differentiation kit (Invitrogen, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Osteogenic differentiation was initiated at 70–80% confluence. On day 10, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity was assessed using an ALP assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China), with the results standardized by protein concentration. The OD was measured at 520 nm. After 14 days, the cells were fixed in 70% ethanol and stained with 2% Alizarin Red S (ARS; Sigma‒Aldrich, USA) for 5 min. To quantify mineralization, isopropanol was added to dissolve the red derivative from the calcium nodules, and the OD was measured at 550 nm using a spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

## Cell transfection
MiR-590-3p inhibitors, inhibitor controls, miR-590-3p mimetics, mimetic controls, and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting circ_0054633 and RUNX2, along with unrelated negative control siRNAs, were obtained from Shanghai Jima Gene Biotechnology Co., Ltd. To create stable cell lines, circ_0054633-specific shRNA or a control vector (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) was introduced into the BMSCs. The cells were selected with 1 μg/ml puromycin for 3–4 weeks, and selection was maintained for 48 h. For transfection, the BMSCs in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into 12-well plates at 2.5 × 105 cells per well. Transfection was performed using the GP reagent (GenePharma, China) in MSCM without serum or antibiotics, following the manufacturer’s guidelines. After 6 h, the cells were cultured in a serum-containing medium for at least 48 h before further experiments. Transfection efficiency was evaluated by qRT‒PCR.

## Western blot analysis
The cells were harvested, and the proteins were extracted using lysis buffer supplemented with inhibitors. The lysate was centrifuged, and the protein concentration was measured by the Pierce BCA assay (Thermo Fisher). The extracted proteins were isolated electrophoretically on 10% SDS‒PAGE gels, followed by immunoblotting on polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Bioscience, USA). The primary antibodies used were anti-Runx2 (1:1000), anti-ALP (1:1000), anti-OCN (1:1000), and anti-β-actin (1:10,000) (ABclonal, Abcam). The secondary antibodies used were HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:1000) and goat anti-mouse IgG (1:1000) (all antibodies were purchased from ABclonal).

## RT‒PCR
Total RNA was extracted from the cell or tissue samples using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). For reverse transcription, 1 μg of total RNA was converted to cDNA with HiScript Q-RT SuperMix (TaKaRa Biotech). β-Actin was used as an internal control for normalization. A qPCR kit (Vazyme Biotech) was used to perform quantitative PCR (qPCR) on a QuantStudio 6-Flex system (Applied Biosystems, USA). The results were expressed as the fold change using the comparative threshold cycle (Ct) method. The primer sequences are shown in Table S3.

## RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and circRNA analysis
Total RNA was extracted from the jawbone BMSCs of five groups of T2DM patients and non-T2DM patients with the use of TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA-seq libraries were constructed and sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq2500 platform (Illumina, USA) according to standard protocols.

## Luciferase report analysis
To assess promoter activity, the circ_0054633 promoter was cloned and inserted into the GV238 vector and cotransfected with RUNX2 siRNA into HEK293T cells. Human embryonic kidney (HEK)-293 T cells were purchased from Otwo Biotech (Guangdong, China) [ ]. For the 3′-UTR luciferase reporter assay, luciferase reporters were generated by cloning either wild-type (wt) or mutant (mt) BC200 3′-UTR sequences containing the miR-590-3p binding site. These cells were cotransfected with the miR-590-3p mimic or control vector together with either GV272-circ_0054633 or GV272-circ_0054633-mt (miR-590-3p). Similarly, HEK293T cells were cotransfected with GV272-RUNX2 or GV272-RUNX2-mt (miR-590-3p) and miR-590-3p mimic or control vectors. After 48 h, luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA), and firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

## Microscopic thermography (MST)
For the MST, the Monolith NT.11 system5 (NanoTemper Technologies GmbH, Germany) and standard capillaries were used. A NanoTemper MO-L011 Monolith™ kit was used to label the recombinant RUNX2 protein with the fluorescent dye Red NHS according to the manufacturer’s instructions. An Impen GmbH nanophotometer™ (Munich, Germany) was used to measure the absorbance at 260 and 546 nm, and a fluorescence/copolymer ratio of 0.5 was determined. The labelled RUNX2 protein (200 nM) was incubated with the test compounds at concentrations ranging from 1 to 62.5 µM. Analyses were conducted at 25 °C in a binding buffer containing 0.05% Tween 20. The fluorescence signals were normalized and fitted using Hill’s equation with MO Affinity Analysis v2.1.3 software (NanoTemper).

## DNA pulldown assay
For the DNA pulldown assay, 50–75 µg of the cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions were incubated at room temperature for 1–2 h, and 4 µg of the degraded 5′-biotinylated sample were added to 200 µl of binding buffer C. The PNPT1 DNA pulldown assay was also performed in PBS as a binding buffer. After electrophoresis, silver staining was performed, and photos were taken to record the gel running images for analysis. The sequences of the primers used were as follows: PNPT1 F: 5′ TGGGGAAACTGAGGCTCA 3′, PNPT1- R:5′ GCAGGCTGTGCCCTGATT 3′ (1824 bp).

## RNA pulldown assay
DNA fragments containing the circ_0054633 sequence or a negative control sequence were amplified by PCR using T7 RNA polymerase (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). The resulting plasmid DNA was linearized with the XhoI restriction enzyme. For the biotin-labelled RNA synthesis, reverse transcription was performed using a mixture of biotin-labelled RNA (Roche) and T7 RNA polymerase (TaKaRa Biomedical Technology). The product was treated with DNase I (Roche) to remove any residual DNA while avoiding RNase contamination and then purified using an RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, MD, USA). In addition, the product was treated with a RUNX2 antibody (A2851; 1:1000, ABclonal), and the cell lysate was assessed via biotinylated circ_0054633 and random probe samples.

## Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)
For the RNA EMSAs, biotinylated circ_0054633 RNA probes were obtained through in vitro transcription via a biotinylated RNA labelling mixture (Roche). A LightShift Chemiluminescence RNA EMSA Kit (Thermo Scientific) was used for displacement determination according to the manufacturer’s instructions. This experiment was repeated three times.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)
Crosslinking was performed with a 1% formaldehyde solution for 10 min and terminated with glycine. A magnetic bead ChIP kit (Thermo Fisher, USA) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The DNA was then enriched via RT‒qPCR and separated by agarose gel electrophoresis.

## LC‒MS mass spectrometry
Mass spectrometry was performed according to previous methods [ ]. This experiment was repeated three times.

## Animals
The work has been determined to be in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0. The animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Beijing Stomatological Hospital (KQYY-202211-007) and followed established guidelines. Forty-eight 8-week-old male BALB/C nude mice (SPF Biotechnology Co., China) were used. All the mice were caged under a 12 h light/dark cycle at 22 ± 2 °C and 55–60% humidity with free access to a standard rodent diet and sterile water. The experimental animals used in this study were in strict compliance with the 3Rs principle, and the experimental procedures were performed according to the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NIH Publications No. 8023, revised 1978). After anaesthesia from inhalation of isoflurane, the experimental mice were euthanized via cervical dislocation (Yuyan Biotechnology Co., China).

## Grouping and treatment of animals
The work has been determined to be in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0. The mice were divided, using a simple randomization method, into 6 treatment groups (n = 8) as follows: the Nc group, Sh-circ group, Circ group, Nc-miR group, miR-inhibitor group, and miR-mimics group. The BMSCs were isolated, suspended at 4 × 10^6 cells/ml, incubated with 40 mg of hydroxyapatite bone powder at 37 °C for 1 h, and centrifuged. The processed and control cells were subcutaneously implanted into 8-week-old male BALB/C nude mice (nu/nu) at two symmetrical sites. The mice were housed (6 per cage) under controlled conditions with access to sterile food and water. Eight weeks after implantation, the mice were euthanized with isoflurane (Yuyan Biotechnology Co., China), and bone specimens were collected. The samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The samples were decalcified in 10% ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA; pH 7.4) for 8 weeks and then embedded in paraffin. The Sects. (5 µm thick) were sliced and stained with Masson’s trichrome and haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stains.

## Statistical analyses
Differences between the control and experimental groups were assessed using Student’s t test, with analyses performed using SPSS 22.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0. Data from three independent experiments are shown as the means ± SDs, and a p value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Pearson’s correlation test was used for correlation analysis, with a p value < 0.05 deemed statistically significant.

## Results

## Expression and identification of circ_0054633 in the BMSCs of T2DM patients
To identify differentially expressed circRNAs in jawbone-derived BMSCs from T2DM patients, high-throughput RNA sequencing was performed on BMSCs isolated from five groups of T2DM patients and their matched non-T2DM counterparts. A total of 80 circRNAs were found to be differentially expressed according to a fold change threshold of ≥ 2 and a P value of < 0.05. Overall, 53 circRNAs were upregulated and 27 were downregulated in the T2DM BMSCs (Fig.  A) (Table S2). Furthermore, using T2DM and circRNA as search terms in PubMed, we screened and analysed differentially expressed circRNAs related to T2DM for interactions in high-throughput data. Among the downregulated circRNAs, hsa_circ_0054633 attracted our attention (Fig.  B). The P value for hsa_circ_0054633 was 0.013274291, and the Q value was 0.957575402. Further validation by qRT‒PCR revealed that hsa_circ_0054633 expression levels were significantly reduced in the 15 pairs of BMSCs in the T2DM group (Fig.  C). Hsa_circ_0054633 consists of four exons (exon 20, exon 21, exon 22, and exon 23). The post-splicing junction sites of exon 20 and exon 23 were validated by Sanger sequencing in the BMSCs (Fig.  D). We amplified cDNA and gDNA from cells to determine whether there was a transfer or genomic rearrangement. Circ_0054633 was detected only in cDNA and not in gDNA (Fig.  E). To test the stability of circ_0054633, we treated RNA extracted from the BMSCs with RNase R. Surprisingly, circ_0054633 was resistant to digestion with RNase R, but its linear form was significantly reduced (Fig.  F, ). Overall, these results confirmed that circ_0054633 is a highly stabilized circRNA that may be relevant to the function of BMSCs from T2DM patients.
 

## Circ_0054633 promotes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in vitro and in vivo
We confirmed the impact of circ_0054633 on the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. Human BMSCs derived from the jawbone were transfected with a circ_0054633-knockdown lentivirus (Fig.  A), and the knockdown efficiency was validated (Fig.  B). Osteogenic differentiation of the BMSCs was induced using an osteogenic induction medium, and we observed that the knockdown of circ_0054633 led to a decrease in ALP staining (Fig.  C), ALP activity (Fig.  D), ARS staining (Fig.  E) and calcium levels (Fig.  F) in the BMSCs. Compared with the vector control, circ_0054633 knockdown led to a reduction in the expression of the osteogenic markers ALP (Fig.  G) and OCN at days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 21 during osteogenic induction. Immunoblotting further revealed that circ_0054633 knockdown decreased the protein levels of the osteogenic differentiation markers ALP and OCN (F g.  I). The heterotypic osteogenesis of BMSCs in nude mice revealed that circ_0054633 knockdown reduced the area of osteoid tissue, which is indicated by black dashed lines (Fig.  J). A circ_0054633 overexpression lentivirus was used to transfect human jawbone-derived BMSCs (Fig.  K), and the knockdown efficacy was verified (Fig.  L). The overexpression of circ_0054633 promoted ALP staining (Fig.  M) and increased the viability (Fig.  N) of the BMSCs, along with ARS staining (Fig.  O) and calcium levels (Fig.  P). The overexpression of circ_0054633 resulted in increased expression of ALP (Fig.  Q) and OCN (Fig.  R). In addition, the overexpression of circ_0054633 increased the protein levels of ALP and OCN (Fig.  S). The overexpression of circ_0054633 significantly increased the ectopic osteogenic area of the BMSCs, the osteoid tissue is marked with black dashed lines (Fig.  T).
 

## Circ_0054633 is an effective miR-590-3p sponge
Many circRNAs have biological effects by sponging miRNAs and then regulating the expression of downstream genes [ ]. Given the high stability of circ_0054633, we speculate that it regulates the biological behaviour of BMSCs through a miRNA sponge mechanism. Therefore, we predicted the potential targets of circ_0054633 via miRNA target prediction databases (CircInteractome, circBank) and PubMed. By combining the results from the three prediction databases, we identified three candidate miRNAs for further validation: hsa-miR-215-5p, hsa-miR-590-3p, and hsa-miR-370-3p (Fig.  A). Compared with hsa-miR-215-5p and hsa-miR-370-3p, miR-590-3p was significantly upregulated after the expression of circ_0054633 was blocked (Fig.  B) and significantly decreased when circ_0054633 was overexpressed (Fig.  C).
 In 15 pairs of T2DM-BMSCs, the expression of miR-590-3p and circ_0054633 was simultaneously upregulated (Fig.  D, ). Pearson correlation analysis revealed a negative correlation between miR-590-3p and circ_0054633 (Fig.  F). To verify the bioinformatics predictions, luciferase reporter plasmids for circ_0054633-WT and circ_0054633-MUT were constructed (Fig.  G). In 293 T cells, the miR-590-3p mimic decreased the luciferase activity of circ_0054633-WT, whereas the inhibitor increased it. However, the luciferase activity of circ_0054633-MUT remained unchanged after transfection with either the mimic or the inhibitor (Fig.  H). To further explore the potential direct interaction between circ_0054633 and miR-590-3p, we performed RNA pulldown assays using biotinylated circ_0054633 probes or oligoprobes and found that, compared with the control, the circ_0054633 probe specifically pulled down miR-590-3p (F g.  I). In addition, the MST results confirmed that the target RNA hsa-miR-590-3p-3′CY5 can bind to hsa_circ_0054633-WT with a Kd (M) = 1.942E-06 (Fig.  J) but cannot bind to hsa_circ_0054633-MT (Fig.  K). Overall, these findings suggest that hsa_circ_0054633 acts as a sponge for miR-590-3p in BMSCs.

## MiR-590-3p inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in vitro and in vivo
To verify the effect of miR-590-3p on BMSC osteogenic differentiation, a miR-590-3p knockdown lentivirus was used to transfect BMSCs of human jawbone origin (Fig.  A), and the knockdown effect was verified (Fig.  B). Osteogenic induction medium was used to induce the osteogenic differentiation of the BMSCs, and the results revealed that miR-590-3p knockdown increased ALP staining (Fig.  C) and activity (Fig.  D), as well as ARS staining (Fig.  E) and calcium levels (Fig.  F), in the BMSCs. The real-time RT‒PCR results revealed that, compared with the vector treatment, miR-590-3p knockdown increased the expression of the osteogenic markers ALP and OCN at days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 21 of osteogenic induction (Fig.  G). Immunoblotting further confirmed that miR-590-3p knockdown increased the protein levels of ALP and OCN (F g.  I). Heterotopic osteogenesis analysis in nude mice revealed that miR-590-3p knockdown expanded the area of bone-like tissue (Fig.  J). Human jawbone-derived BMSCs were transfected with a miR-590-3p overexpression lentivirus (Fig.  K), and the knockdown efficacy was validated (Fig.  L). The overexpression of miR-590-3p inhibited ALP staining (Fig.  M), activity (Fig.  N), ARS staining (Fig.  O), and calcium levels (Fig.  P) and reduced the expression of ALP (Fig.  Q) and OCN (Fig.  R). Additionally, miR-590-3p overexpression decreased the protein levels of ALP and OCN (Fig.  S). It also significantly inhibited the ectopic osteogenic area of the BMSCs, with osteoid tissue marked by black dashed lines (Fig.  T).
 

## RUNX2 is a direct target of miR-590-3p in BMSCs
To explore the underlying mechanism by which miR-590-3p inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, we conducted bioinformatic analyses via TargetScan, RNAlnter, and miRDB. Interestingly, among the 2663 candidate target genes, we identified RUNX2, which is closely related to osteogenic differentiation, and predicted its binding sites (Fig.  A, ). Our data demonstrated that the inhibition of miR-590-3p resulted in the upregulation of RUNX2 expression in the cells (Fig.  C-E), whereas miR-590-3p overexpression led to the significant downregulation of RUNX2 at both the mRNA and protein levels (Fig.  F-H). To confirm that RUNX2 is a direct target of miR-590-3p in BMSCs, we constructed luciferase reporter plasmids containing RUNX2 3′ UTR-WT and RUNX2 3′ UTR-MUT (F g.  I). A luciferase assay revealed that miR-590-3p markedly inhibited the luciferase activity of 293 T cells transfected with RUNX2 3′ UTR-WT but had no effect on cells transfected with RUNX2 3′ UTR-MUT; in contrast, miR-590-3p knockdown had the opposite effect (Fig.  J). These findings suggest that miR-590-3p inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs by directly targeting RUNX2.
 

## Circ_0054633 promotes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs through the miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis
Further analysis confirmed that circ_0054633 enhances the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs via the miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis and that circ_0054633 negatively regulates miR-590-3p. We observed that knockdown of circ_0054633 resulted in a reduction in RUNX2 levels at both the mRNA and protein levels (Fig.  A, , ), whereas its overexpression significantly elevated RUNX2 expression (Fig.  D, , ). Moreover, the decrease in RUNX2 levels caused by circ_0054633 knockdown was reversed by the miR-590-3p inhibitor (Fig.  G). Conversely, overexpression of RUNX2 fully counteracted the inhibitory effect of circ_0054633 shRNA on osteogenic differentiation, as demonstrated by ALP staining and activity (Fig.  H, ) as well as ARS staining and quantification (Fig.  J, ). The promotion of osteogenic differentiation in the BMSCs by circ_0054633 overexpression was abolished through RUNX2 knockdown, as shown by ALP staining and viability (Fig.  L and ) and ARS staining and quantification (Fig.  N and ). Therefore, our findings indicate that circ_0054633 promotes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs through the miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis.
 

## RUNX2 transcriptional regulation of circ_0054633 expression levels in BMSCs
To elucidate the potential mechanism of abnormal overexpression of circ_0054633 in T2DM, we used the public UCSC genome browser (http://genome.ucsc.edu/index.html) and ChIP-seq data to predict the potential transcription factor sequences of circ_0054633 (Table S4) (https://www.omicshare.com), and local alignment of BLAST sequences was performed on Runx2 and the predicted transcription factors. Interestingly, there was potential binding between the transcription factor RUNX2 and the upstream regulatory region of the circ_0054633 gene. According to the prediction, the transcription factor RUNX2 has a binding site (ATTTGTTTAATTGTATTATAATCAAGATATTTATTAAAAAGTATATACATCT) on the circ_0054633 promoter, which we analysed via bioinformatics (JASPAR, ifti.org) (Fig.  A) (Supplementary Document 1). To investigate the interaction between RUNX2 and circ_0054633, we first synthesized a circ_0054633 probe through DNA pulldown analysis. After labelling, the PCR products were subjected to 1% agarose gel electrophoresis analysis. After the probe was successfully labelled, the molecular weight increased, and the electrophoresis band lagged (Fig.  B). The different bands were detected via SDS‒PAGE (Fig.  C). The original mass spectrometry file was processed and converted with MM File Conversion software to obtain an MGF-formatted file, which was then used in MASCOT (http://www.matrixscience.com). Analysis of the LC–MS data retrieved from the UniProt database revealed that RUNX2 (Table S5) was indeed present in the pulldown product. Table S5 shows the filtered protein list; the protein cover of RUNX2 was 1.8, that of prot-pi was 8.15, and that of emPAI was 0.05. To verify the binding of the two molecules, we performed MST, and the results confirmed that hsa_circ_0054633 can bind to RUNX2-WT with a Kd (M) = 1.648E−06 (Fig.  D) but cannot bind to RUNX2-MT (Fig.  E). To further determine the transcriptional activity of RUNX2 on the circ_0054633 gene promoter, we knocked down RUNX2, which significantly reduced the activity of the circ_0054633 promoter (Fig.  F). ChIP analysis revealed that RUNX2 was significantly enriched at binding site 1 of the circ_0054633 promoter (Fig.  G). In addition, we verified that RUNX2 can affect circ_0054633 in BMSCs and that the knockdown of Runx2 significantly reduces the expression of circ_0054633 (Fig.  H). In addition, we further clarified the binding of the Runx2 and circ_0054633 promoters through gene synthesis, a cell-free expression system, and EMSA experiments. T1 binds to the protein, competes with T1 (cold), and does not bind to the protein (F g.  I). More importantly, we conducted rescue experiments to determine whether RUNX2 promotes osteogenesis through circ_0054633 in cells. We found that RUNX2 inhibition reduced osteogenic differentiation, whereas circ_0054633 overexpression significantly reversed this effect (Fig.  J-M). In contrast, the overexpression of RUNX2 increased osteogenic differentiation, whereas the inhibition of circ_0054633 significantly reversed this effect (Fig.  N-Q). Overall, these results indicate the role of the circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 positive feedback loop in promoting the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs.
 Our study revealed that T2DM downregulates the expression of circ_0054633, which can promote the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and act as a miRNA sponge. After its release from the nucleus, miR-590-3p inhibits the function of miR-590-3p, preventing the binding of miR-590-3p to RUNX2 and thereby upregulating the expression of RUNX2 and promoting the differentiation of BMSCs into osteoblasts. RUNX2 overexpression activated the circ_0054633 promoter at exons 20–23, promoting the generation of nuclear circ_0054633 and forming a positive feedback loop. In summary, the circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 positive feedback loop plays an important role in the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in T2DM (Fig.  ).
 

## Discussion
In this study, we demonstrate for the first time that a novel circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis is actively involved in the progression of bone regeneration in BMSCs. Through high-throughput screening and validation, under enhanced screening conditions, we identified 80 differentially expressed circRNAs. Unlike previous studies, we not only restricted the fold change to ≥ 2 and a P value < 0.05 but also used the PubMed database to assess candidate circRNAs. In previous studies, we reported that Hsa_circ_0054633 was downregulated in the serum of T2DM patients, was negatively correlated with C-peptide, and was positively correlated with IL-17 and TNF-α. These findings suggest that hsa_circ_0054633 may be a potential early indicator of the response to insulin therapy, which attenuates inflammation in T2DM patients [ ]. This finding is consistent with the expression levels found in the BMSCs of T2DM patients, further highlighting the potential of circ_0054633 as a research target in T2DM-related diseases. Further circRNA analysis revealed that circ_0054633 is a circRNA with a stable structure. In terms of function, we found that circ_0054633 expression levels were positively correlated with the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. circ_0054633 promoted the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in vitro and in vivo, suggesting that circ_0054633 plays a significant role in promoting the function of BMSCs. In addition, owing to the stability and high abundance of circRNAs in the cytoplasm, circ_0054633 is expected to serve as an effective therapeutic target and a promising biomarker for predicting the success rate of implantation in T2DM patients.
Since Jeck WR et al. revealed that circRNAs can serve as miRNA sponges [ ], numerous studies have elucidated the miRNA sponge mechanism through which circRNAs influence stem cell function and tissue regeneration [ – ]. These studies have provided a foundational understanding of how circRNAs interact with miRNAs to modulate gene expression and cellular processes. Our findings revealed that miR-590-3p is upregulated in the BMSCs of T2DM patients and is negatively correlated with the expression of circ_0054633. Given that circ_0054633 contains miRNA-responsive elements, we hypothesize that this circRNA may exert its biological effects via a competing endogenous RNA mechanism. Our findings revealed that miR-590-3p is upregulated in the BMSCs of T2DM patients and is negatively correlated with the expression of circ_0054633. miR-590-3p is a common circulating miRNA in humans. In terms of function, the overexpression of miR-590-3p can increase the efficacy of hiPSC CMs in myocardial repair [ ], and the target lysozyme promotes the phenotypic transition of vascular smooth muscle cells [ ] and the proliferation and metastasis of hepatocellular carcinoma cells [ ]. Interestingly, we found that miR-590-3p has effects that are functionally opposite to those of circ_0054633 in regulating the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. Specifically, miR-590-3p was shown to inhibit both the in vivo and in vitro osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. This inhibitory effect may stem from the ability of miR-590-3p to directly bind to AIMP3/p18 transcripts, subsequently reducing AIMP3/p18 expression levels. The downregulation of AIMP3/p18 has been implicated in the induction of cellular ageing and the inhibition of osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs [ ]. These findings further highlight the importance of miR-590-3p in osteogenesis and suggest that its regulation is critical for maintaining BMSC functionality, especially under pathological conditions such as T2DM. To investigate the downstream mechanisms through which miR-590-3p operates, we employed bioinformatics analysis, qRT‒PCR, Western blotting, and dual luciferase assays. These experiments confirmed that the 3′UTR of RUNX2 mRNA contains a “seed” region that directly interacts with miR-590-3p. RUNX2 is a pivotal transcription factor that regulates the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs. During the early stages of osteoblast differentiation, RUNX2 drives the expression of essential bone matrix genes, laying the groundwork for subsequent mineralization and bone formation. The activity of RUNX2 is finely tuned by various upstream signalling molecules and pathways, which collectively influence osteogenesis, bone repair, regeneration, and remodelling processes [ – ]. Moreover, we discovered that miR-590-3p directly targets RUNX2 mRNA, suppressing its transcription and, consequently, its protein expression. Our results indicate that circ_0054633 plays a role in the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs through the circ_005463/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis. This intricate molecular interplay not only underscores the importance of circ_0054633 in maintaining the osteogenic capacity of BMSCs but also highlights its potential as a therapeutic target for addressing impaired bone formation and regeneration, particularly in metabolic disorders such as T2DM. Further exploration of this axis could pave the way for novel strategies to enhance bone health and repair.
RUNX2, a fundamental transcription factor, is essential for the development and mineralization of both bone and teeth. The critical role of RUNX2 in regulating osteogenesis is further emphasized by its involvement in a positive feedback loop that has been shown to influence bone metabolism. This feedback loop serves to create an osteogenic microenvironment within cells, driving the process of bone metabolic reprogramming and ensuring that cells actively participate in osteogenic differentiation and bone remodelling [ – ]. In this study, we applied a range of experimental techniques, including bioinformatics analysis, MST, ChIP, and EMSA, to investigate the relationship between RUNX2 and circ_0054633. Our results confirmed that RUNX2 directly binds to the circ_0054633 promoter, thus promoting the transcriptional activation of circ_0054633. These findings provide compelling evidence for the regulatory connection between RUNX2 and circ_0054633. Further rescue experiments corroborated our findings, showing that the inhibition of RUNX2 led to a significant reduction in osteogenic differentiation. In contrast, the overexpression of circ_0054633 significantly rescued this phenotype, restoring osteogenic differentiation. Conversely, when RUNX2 was overexpressed, osteogenic differentiation increased, and the inhibition of circ_0054633 reversed this effect. These findings support the existence of a positive feedback loop between circ_0054633 and RUNX2 that regulates the osteogenic process. This feedback circuit is not unique to our study; similar mechanisms have been described in cancer research. For example, Wang Zehao and colleagues identified a feedback loop involving circROBO1/KLF5/FUS, which plays a pivotal role in breast cancer liver metastasis by promoting carcinogenesis. This circuit inhibits the selective autophagy of afadin through the suppression of BECN1 transcription [ ]. In addition, RUNX2 has been shown to activate various signalling molecules, such as the phosphorylation-induced activation of the MMP13 promoter, which ultimately promotes osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs [ ]. Taken together, our findings suggest that the circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis plays an important role in regulating osteogenic differentiation, particularly in the context of T2DM.
Taken together, our findings suggest that the circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis plays an important role in regulating osteogenic differentiation, particularly in the context of T2DM. On the basis of these observations, we propose a novel mechanism for regulating the function of BMSCs. We found that T2DM results in reduced expression of circ_0054633 in BMSCs, which impairs the osteogenic differentiation process. The persistence of circ_0054633 in cells allows it to act as a miRNA sponge, sequestering miR-590-3p and thereby preventing its binding to its target mRNAs. This leads to an increase in the levels of free miR-590-3p within the cytoplasm, which subsequently downregulates RUNX2 through miRNA-mediated silencing, impairing osteogenic differentiation. Additionally, we demonstrated that RUNX2 can upregulate circ_0054633 expression via a positive feedback loop. Our data suggest that this regulatory mechanism is dependent on RNA-binding interactions between RUNX2 and circ_0054633. However, the precise molecular mechanism through which RUNX2 facilitates the circularization of circ_0054633 remains to be fully elucidated, and we plan to investigate this further in our future research.
In this study, we found that circ_0054633/miR-590-3p/RUNX2 axis was actively involved in osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs in jaw bone-derived BMSCs of patients with type 2 diabetes and non type 2 diabetes. In the BMSCs of patients with type 2 diabetes, we found that the expression of hsa_circ_0054633 was reduced, and the expression of miR-590-3p was increased compared to that in controls. The expression pattern of these two factors may be an important reason for the decrease in the osteogenic ability of BMSCs in type 2 diabetes patients. Mechanistically, these two factors interact to affect the osteogenic differentiation function of BMSCs in non type 2 diabetes patients. In the study of the pathogenesis of T2DM, researchers screened key circRNAs such as hsa_circ_0060614 through high-throughput RNA sequencing technology. These RNAs can regulate the expression of MT2A gene by adsorbing hsa-mir-4668-3p, providing a new perspective for molecular mechanism research on obesity-related T2DM [ ]. Unfortunately, there is still a lack of reports on the molecular mechanism by which circRNAs act as ceRNAs to mediate T2DM, leading to abnormal osteogenesis and differentiation of BMSCs. As the current study is preliminary, we still need to build a pathological model of BMSCs in type 2 diabetes patients to explore its correlation with abnormal bone metabolism in T2DM.

## Conclusion
Given the critical role of circ_0054633 in osteogenic differentiation, we believe that circ_0054633 has significant potential not only as a biomarker for monitoring and correcting abnormal osteogenic differentiation in BMSCs but also as a promising therapeutic target for improving the osseointegration of implants. The modulation of circ_0054633 expression could provide a novel approach for enhancing bone regeneration and repair, especially in conditions such as T2DM, where impaired osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs is a prominent issue. We are eager to explore this avenue further in our ongoing and future studies, with the hope that it may ultimately lead to therapeutic strategies for improving bone health and enhancing the outcomes of bone-related treatments and implant surgeries.
