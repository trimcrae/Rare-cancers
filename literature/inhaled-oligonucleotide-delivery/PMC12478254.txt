## Background & Aims
Elevated F-box/SPRY domain protein 1 (FBXO45) expression is associated with human hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). While TP53 mutations represent a common genetic alteration in HCC, the potential regulatory interplay between TP53 and FBXO45 in modulating HCC progression remains unclear.

## Methods
We analyzed the relationship between TP53 and FBXO45 in patients with HCC. A series of assays were also performed to explore the effect of FBXO45 on invasion and metastasis in TP53-mutated HCC cell lines and mouse models. These experimental findings were further validated in clinical samples.

## Results
FBXO45 expression was upregulated in 78.3% (83/106) of patients with HCC with TP53 mutation, marking an aggressive subtype. Overexpression of FBXO45 significantly enhanced HCC cell migration and invasion capacity (p <0.001), whereas FBXO45 silencing suppressed these effects (p <0.01). Mechanistically, FBXO45 promoted Trk-fused gene (TFG) Lys103 ubiquitination and then increased its stability. This post-translational modification facilitates the binding of transcription factor activating transcription factor 2 (ATF2), leading to subsequent upregulation of nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) p65 expression and ultimately promoting the migratory and invasive properties of TP53-mutant HCC cells. In vivo validation using an orthotopic xenograft model showed that FBXO45 overexpression significantly promoted HCC lung metastasis incidence (7/9 mice with metastases), whereas TFG knockdown abrogated this metastatic potential. Clinically, patients exhibiting high co-expression of FBXO45 and TFG defined an aggressive HCC subtype with significantly worse prognosis (p = 0.0015).

## Conclusions
Our results provide new mechanistic insight into the role of FBXO45 in driving HCC invasion and metastasis. Therefore, targeting the TP53–FBXO45–TFG–ATF2–NF-κB axis represents a promising therapeutic approach for treating aggressive HCC with TP53 mutations.

## Impact and implications
This study highlights that FBXO45 is highly expressed in patients with HCC with TP53 mutations. FBXO45 promotes HCC lung metastasis by activating the TFG–ATF2–NF-κB–epithelial–mesenchymal transition signaling axis. In addition, we found that targeting the TP53–FBXO45–TFG–ATF2–NF-κB axis could be a novel approach for the treatment of metastatic HCC.

## Introduction
Primary liver cancer is the sixth most common malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounting for >80% of all liver cancer cases. Although various treatments have led to great progress over the past few decades, the prognosis of patients with HCC, especially those with TP53 mutations, remains poor because of the high recurrence and metastasis rates in these patients. Therefore, it is particularly important to explore the mechanisms of HCC metastasis to help develop new treatment strategies.
Ubiquitination is a common post-translational modification of proteins and comprises a three-enzyme cascade reaction mediated by ubiquitin-activating enzyme (E1), ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme (E2), and ubiquitin ligase (E3). Ubiquitination results in monoubiquitin or polyubiquitin chain modification of substrate proteins and regulates the stability and activity of substrates, thus participating in a variety of physiological cellular processes, including protein localization, cell survival and apoptosis, cellular differentiation, and DNA damage. , As core members of the ubiquitination system, E3 ubiquitin ligases are decisive for the specific recognition of substrates and, therefore, have attracted increased attention as potential therapeutic targets for cancer. , Moreover, increasing numbers of studies have confirmed that dysregulated E3 ubiquitin ligases have a key role in the development and progression of HCC. For example, abnormal upregulation of the E3 ubiquitin ligases seven in absentia homolog (SIAH-1), Hakai, and FBXW10 promotes the occurrence of HCC, and synoviolin (SYVN1) mediates HCC invasion and metastasis. , , , The E3 ubiquitin ligase F-box/SPRY domain protein 1 (FBXO45) is abnormally highly expressed in HCC tissues and promotes hepatocarcinogenesis. Its expression is an independent risk factor for HCC and is positively correlated with poor patient prognosis. However, whether FBXO45 is associated with HCC invasion and metastasis remains unclear.
Trk-fused gene (TFG) protein is a three-part protein comprising an N-terminal Phox and Bem1p homology (PB1) domain, a coiled-coil domain, and a proline- and glutamine-rich region, and is located at the exit site of the endoplasmic reticulum in the cytoplasm. TFG is involved in coat protein complex II (COPII)-mediated vesicular transport and, although its main function is to regulate the integrity of the endoplasmic reticulum–Golgi interface, it has other roles in protein secretion, protein stability, autophagy, and the antiviral innate immune response. , , Previous studies showed that TFG acts as an oncogene and is involved in tumorigenesis and progression in prostate cancer, lymphoma, and thyroid cancer. , , , By contrast, TFG is also reported as a tumor suppressor in radiation-induced T cell lymphomas and melanoma. However, to our knowledge, whether the involvement of TFG in tumorigenesis and progression depends on its ubiquitination remains unknown.
In this study, we demonstrated that FBXO45 promotes the invasion and metastasis of TP53-mutated HCC and promotes lung metastasis of HCC in mice. Mechanistically, mutant TP53 results in high expression of FBXO45. Subsequently, FBXO45 specifically binds to TFG, promotes TFG polyubiquitination, and maintains its stability; TFG then activates the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathway to mediate HCC invasion and metastasis via the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway. Together, our findings could provide a new therapeutic strategy for patients with aggressive HCC with high FBXO45 expression.

## Materials and methods

## Patients and tissue specimens
HCC tissues were obtained from the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery, Southwest Hospital of Chongqing, China. The samples were confirmed histologically, fixed in paraformaldehyde solution, and embedded in paraffin for additional histological analysis. This study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Southwest Hospital (KY2020127). Informed consent was obtained from all patients, and detailed clinical and pathological data were obtained for each patient.

## Cell culture studies
The human HCC cell line HCCLM3 was obtained from BeiNa Culture Collection (Beijing, China). The Huh7 cell line was obtained from Cell Bank of Type Culture Collection of Fudan University (Shanghai, China). HEK-293T, HepG2, and SK-Hep-1 were obtained from ATCC. Mycoplasma contamination was not detected in any of the cell lines, which were then cultured at 37 °C, 5% CO2 using DMEM or MEM containing 10% FBS (ThermoFisher Scientific, NY, USA).

## Animal studies and orthotopic xenograft model in nude mice
BALB/c nude mice (4–5-weeks old, males, n = 9 per group) were obtained from GemPharmatech Co. Ltd (Chengdu, China) and were fed under specific pathogen-free conditions. Nude mice were divided into four groups and anesthetized with isoflurane by inhalation. The skin was then incised along the middle of the abdomen to expose the left lobe of the liver. Nude mice were injected with HCCLM3 cells expressing stable transitions (2 × 106 cells/50 μl containing 25 μl of Matrigel) and the abdomen was then fully closed. After 4 weeks, the mice were euthanized, their livers and lungs were excised and preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde. Sections of the lungs were taken for H&E and immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining to observe the tumor lesions. The incidence of lung metastasis, and tumor size, weight, and volume were determined in each group. The whole process was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Army Medical University and complied with all relevant ethical regulations (AMUWEC20224092).

## In vivo ubiquitination assay
To examine the ubiquitination of TFG by FBXO45, HEK293T cells were transfected with His-Ub and Flag-FBXO45 with HA-TFG or its mutant HA-TFG (K103A). After 72 h of transfection, the cells were treated with 10 μM MG132 for 4–6 h before harvest for an in vivo ubiquitination assay. This assay was performed as described previously using Ni-bead pull-down assays. In brief, the cells were lysed in buffer A (6 mol/L guanidinium-HCl, 0.1 mol/L Na2HPO4/NaH2PO4, 10 mmol/L Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 5 mmol/L imidazole, and 10 mmol/L β-mercaptoethanol) and exposed to 50 μl Ni-NTA beads (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA) overnight. The beads were washed with buffer A plus 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol, buffer B (8 mM urea, 0.1 M Na2HPO4/NaH2PO4, 10 mM Tris/HCl (pH 8.0), and 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol), buffer C (8 mM urea, 0.1 M Na2HPO4/NaH2PO4, 10 mM Tris/HCl (pH 6.3), 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol, and 0.2% Triton X-100), and buffer C plus 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol and 0.1% Triton X-100. His6-tagged ubiquitinated proteins were then eluted with buffer D (200 mM imidazole, 0.15 M Tris-HCl (pH 6.7), 30% glycerol, 0.72 M β-mercaptoethanol, and 5% SDS). The elution was analyzed using Western blot.

## Statistical analysis
All data are presented as means ± SEMs. Comparisons between two groups were performed with a two-tailed unpaired t test. When more than two groups were compared, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s post hoc test was used when the data passed the Shapiro‒Wilk normality test. Statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA). Clinicopathological correlations were analyzed using the Chi-square test and Pearson’s Chi-square test. Overall survival (OS) was analyzed via the Kaplan‒Meier method with the log-rank test. Differences were significant at p <0.05.

## Results

## TP53 loss causes high FBXO45 expression in HCC
FBXO45 is highly expressed in HCC tissues and promotes HCC development, but the underlying mechanism remains unknown. Here, we found that FBXO45 was highly expressed in TP53-mutated HCC samples via the cBioPortal database ( A). The expression level of FBXO45 was positively correlated with mutated TP53 status and, of the patients with TP53 mutations, 78.3% (83/106) exhibited elevated expression of FBXO45 ( B). In addition, high FBXO45 expression was positively associated with poor OS in patients with TP53-mutated HCC (log-rank test, p = 0.0094) but not in those with TP53 wild-type (WT) HCC (log-rank test, p = 0.0739) ( C). In addition, the relationship between FBXO45 expression and the clinicopathological characteristics in 106 patients with TP53-mutated HCC was evaluated. A high FBXO45 level was positively associated with tumor–node–metastasis (TNM) stage ( and ). Moreover, FBXO45 mRNA and protein were more highly expressed in TP53-mutated HCC cell lines (Huh7 and HCCLM3) than in WT HCC cell lines (HepG2 and SK-Hep-1) ( ). 
Next, we used IHC staining to measure TP53 and FBXO45 expression in 141 pairs of HCC tissues. The results revealed a negative correlation between TP53 and FBXO45 in HCC tissues ( D and E; ). In agreement with this finding, overexpression of WT TP53 significantly downregulated FBXO45 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels ( F and G).
Previous research showed that most tumor-derived TP53 mutations occur in the region of the TP53 DNA-binding domain. Thus, we constructed a mutant of the TP53 plasmids with deletion of the DNA-binding domain (HA-TP53ΔDNA-binding) and overexpressed it in WT HCC cells. The results suggested that these TP53 mutants significantly upregulated FBXO45 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels in SK-Hep-1 and HepG2 cells, and promoted HCC cell invasion and migration ( ).
We previously reported that TP53 insufficiency increased mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling, which promoted HCC tumorigenesis and lung metastasis. Therefore, to determine whether mTOR is involved in the FBXO45-mediated metastasis of TP53-mutated HCC, we measured FBXO45 mRNA and protein expression after treatment with the mTOR inhibitor rapamycin. Inhibition of mTOR by rapamycin significantly reduced FBXO45 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels ( H and I). Taken together, these results suggest that TP53 mutations cause high FBXO45 expression through mTOR signaling in HCC.

## FBXO45 promotes cell migration and invasion via the EMT pathway in TP53-mutated HCC cells
Metastasis of HCC is the main cause of poor patient prognosis, but whether FBXO45 overexpression is an independent risk factor for metastatic HCC is unclear. To investigate the association between FBXO45 and HCC metastasis, we analyzed clinical specimens from 60 patients with HCC with TP53 mutations and found that FBXO45 was positively correlated with HCC metastasis, histological grade, recurrence, and TNM stage ( A; ). To further clarify the effect of FBXO45 on HCC metastasis, we performed Transwell assays to examine the effects of FBXO45 overexpression or silencing on the migration and invasion of TP53-mutated HCC cells (HCCLM3 and Huh7). FBXO45 overexpression significantly promoted the migration and invasion of TP53-mutated HCC cells ( B and D), whereas FBXO45 knockdown significantly inhibited cell migration and invasion ( C and E). Consistent with these findings, expression of metastasis-related genes, including those encoding intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM1), urokinase-type plasminogen activator (uPA), C–C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), and matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP9), was markedly increased in FBXO45-overexpressing HCC cells compared with control cells ( F). By contrast, FBXO45 knockdown in TP53-mutant HCC cells reduced the expression of these metastasis-related genes ( G). 
EMT, which mainly involves the transient dedifferentiation of epithelial cells into cells with mesenchymal phenotypes, is a major trigger for cancer metastasis and invasion. To determine whether EMT is involved in FBXO45-mediated metastasis, the EMT-associated proteins N-cadherin and Snail were analyzed by Western blotting. FBXO45 significantly increased the levels of both proteins ( H and I). Taken together, these results suggest that FBXO45 promotes cell migration and invasion through the EMT pathway in TP53-mutated HCC cells.

## FBXO45 promotes TFG K103 polyubiquitination and stability
FBXO45, an atypical E3 ubiquitin ligase, binds to substrates to perform various functions. We used proteomics, ubiquitomics, and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) mass spectrometry to screen FBXO45 substrates. In all, 713 proteins were upregulated, and 615 proteins were upregulated at ubiquitin sites in HCC cells with high expression of FBXO45. In addition, an anti-FBXO45 antibody pulled down 46 proteins as candidate proteins. We generated Venn diagrams from these data and identified two overlapping proteins: TFG and ribosomal protein S27A (RPS27A). FBXO45 overexpression in HCC cells did not significantly affect the mRNA levels of TFG and RPS27A ( ). Both proteins exhibited high levels of ubiquitination and changes in expression ( A), suggesting them to be substrates of FBXO45. However, only TFG has been reported to be associated with tumor development. , , , According to The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data from the HCC cohort, TFG and RPS27A were highly expressed in HCC tumors and were positively associated with poor OS ( ); by contrast, compared with RPS27A knockdown, TFG knockdown dramatically inhibited HCC cell migration ( ), which suggests that TFG, but not RPS27A, has a crucial role in FBXO45-driven HCC cell migration. 
To confirm the interaction between FBXO45 and TFG, we examined the binding of these two proteins via a pull-down assay. The results revealed that endogenous and exogenous TFG was precipitated by FBXO45 but not by a FBXO45 domain deletion mutant (FBXO45ΔF). Reciprocally, exogenous FBXO45 was pulled down from the endogenous TFG precipitates ( B and C; ). TFG in HCC also exhibited increased polyubiquitination at the K103 site ( A). Next, we further examined the TFG protein sequence and found that this site is evolutionarily conserved among species ( D). To further validate whether FBXO45 promotes TFG polyubiquitination, HEK293T cells were subsequently transfected with FBXO45 plasmids containing TFG or its K103A mutant. In vivo ubiquitination experiments demonstrated that FBXO45 promoted TFG polyubiquitination, whereas the K103A mutation significantly blocked this effect ( E). E3 ubiquitin ligases typically catalyze K63-linked polyubiquitination, which causes target protein accumulation and activation, whereas K48-linked polyubiquitination induces protein degradation. We observed that mutation of K63 to arginine (K63R) in ubiquitin dramatically blocked FBXO45-mediated TFG polyubiquitination compared with K48R or WT ubiquitin ( ), indicating that FBXO45 catalyzes the K63-linked polyubiquitination of TFG.
A ubiquitination assay was performed to investigate whether TFG ubiquitylation was dependent on SKP1 and PAM activity. Knockdown of either SKP1 or PAM significantly blocked FBXO45-mediated TFG ubiquitination ( ). To further clarify the effect of TFG ubiquitination on protein stability, a time-course analysis following cycloheximide (CHX) blockade of protein synthesis indicated that overexpression of FBXO45 extended the half-life of TFG in HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells ( F; ). Therefore, these results suggest that FBXO45 promotes the stability of TFG via polyubiquitination at the K103 site.
To confirm that TFG K103 ubiquitination affects HCC cell migration, we transfected HA-TFG or its mutant HA-TFG (K103A) into Huh7 and HCCLM3 cells. TFG significantly promoted the migration of TP53-mutated HCC cells, whereas its K103A mutant form did not ( G and H). Taken together, these findings indicate that FBXO45 mediates HCC cell migration and invasion by promoting TFG K103 ubiquitination and increasing its stability.

## FBXO45 promotes HCC metastasis and EMT via TFG
In lymphoma and thyroid carcinoma, TFG usually leads to tumorigenesis directly through the formation of fusion genes. , In this study, to determine the effects of TFG on cell migration, invasion, and the EMT pathway, HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells were transfected with TFG plasmids or TFG small interfering (si)RNAs. The results revealed that TFG overexpression significantly increased HCC cell migration and invasion ( A and B). In addition, TFG knockdown reduced the expression of N-cadherin, vimentin, and Snail, and upregulated the expression of E-cadherin ( C). Next, to confirm whether FBXO45-mediated cell migration and invasion and EMT pathways are dependent on TFG, we transfected FBXO45 plasmids containing TFG siRNA into HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells. TFG knockdown did not have any effect on cell proliferation but significantly blocked FBXO45-mediated migration and invasion of HCC cells within the same time periods, indicating that TFG had a more crucial role in promoting the migration and invasion of HCC cells ( D and E; ). In agreement with these findings, TFG knockdown significantly blocked FBXO45-induced increases in the expression of N-cadherin and Snail ( F and G). In summary, FBXO45 was shown to induce HCC EMT and metastasis via TFG. 

## TFG regulates the ATF2/NF-κB pathway to promote HCC metastasis
Previous studies reported that TFG promotes tumorigenesis via fusion with oncogenes, , while other studies showed that NF-κB can promote HCC metastasis through the EMT pathway. , , Therefore, we sought to determine whether TFG regulates the NF-κB pathway. TFG knockdown significantly blocked NF-κB p65 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels ( A and B). Given that TFG is primarily oncogenic as a fusion protein, the regulatory mechanism of TFG on NF-κB p65 might be indirect. It was previously reported that NF-κB p65 expression was positively regulated by the transcription factor activating transcription factor 2 (ATF2) in arterial inflammation, which is highly expressed in HCC and promotes HCC invasion and metastasis. , , , To further test whether TFG interacts with ATF2 and then regulates NF-κB p65 expression, we performed Co-IP and found that exogenous TFG could pull down endogenous ATF2. Moreover, knockdown of ATF2 significantly reduced the expression of NF-κB p65 in HCC. Thus, these results suggest that TFG binds ATF2 and then activates NF-κB p65 expression ( ). 
To investigate whether TFG-mediated HCC cell migration and invasion depend on the NF-κB pathway, HCCLM3 and Huh7 cells were subsequently transfected with TFG plasmids and siRNAs targeting NF-κB p65. NF-κB p65 silencing not only significantly attenuated the TFG-mediated migration of HCC cells, but also downregulated the TFG-mediated increase in the expression of N-cadherin and Snail ( C–E). These findings suggest that TFG activates the NF-κB pathway and promotes the metastasis of HCC cells via EMT.

## Knockdown of TFG blocks FBXO45-driven HCC tumorigenesis and lung metastasis in mice
We demonstrated that FBXO45 promotes the migration and invasion of TP53-mutated HCC cells through the ubiquitination and increased stability of TFG. To further confirm the role of FBXO45 and TFG in HCC lung metastasis, we established an orthotopic xenograft model in nude mice. Four groups of lentivirus-transfected HCCLM3 cell lines (vector, FBXO45, shTFG, and FBXO45+shTFG) were injected into the livers of the mice in situ ( A). After 4 weeks, all mice were euthanized, and the tumors were imaged and detected by Western blotting ( B; ). The results revealed that FBXO45 promoted HCC tumorigenesis and that TFG knockdown significantly attenuated this effect, as indicated by the tumor volume, tumor weight and largest tumor size ( C–E). In addition, H&E and IHC analyses of lung tissues revealed that TFG knockdown impaired the FBXO45-mediated HCC pulmonary metastatic phenotype, as demonstrated by the incidence of pulmonary metastases ( F and G). Taken together, these findings indicate that TFG knockdown dramatically blocks the FBXO45-driven metastasis of HCC to the lungs. 

## Positive association between FBXO45 and TFG in patients with TP53-mutated HCC, leading to poor prognosis
A human TP53-mutated HCC cohort (60 paired HCC tissues and adjacent normal tissues) was used to evaluate the expression level of TFG. IHC staining revealed that TFG was highly expressed in HCC tissues ( A). Furthermore, a high level of TFG was positively associated with poor OS in patients with TP53-mutated HCC (log-rank test, p = 0.0006) ( B). Subsequently, the relationships between the level of TFG and the clinicopathological characteristics of the patients were evaluated. A high TFG expression level was positively associated with tumor size, metastasis, and advanced TNM stage ( C–E; ). Next, IHC staining was performed on TP53-mutated HCC and adjacent normal tissues, and the correlation between FBXO45 and TFG was analyzed using the Chi-square test and Spearman correlation. FBXO45 was positively correlated with TFG expression in the 60 paired TP53-mutated HCC tissues (R = 0.5229, p <0.0001), 45% (27/60) of which presented high expression of both proteins ( F and G; ). 
To further determine the importance of FBXO45 and TFG in metastasis, we evaluated the expression of FBXO45 and TFG in primary tumors of patients with HCC with and without metastasis. The results suggest that FBXO45 and TFG are more highly expressed in patients with metastasis ( ). Kaplan‒Meier survival curve analysis revealed that, compared with patients with low expression of these proteins, patients with TP53-mutated HCC with high expression of FBXO45 and TFG had shorter OS times ( H). Consistent with these findings, high expression of FBXO45 and TFG was positively associated with large tumor size, advanced TNM stage, tumor metastasis, and recurrence ( ), which indicates that the FBXO45–TFG axis is positively correlated with the progression of TP53-mutated HCC. These findings further reveal that high FBXO45 expression promotes TFG stability, leading to metastasis in patients with TP53-mutated HCC ( ). 

## Discussion
Most patients with HCC have a poor prognosis because of tumor metastasis, but the precise mechanisms underlying this metastasis are not well understood. Previous studies showed that aberrant expression of E3 ubiquitin ligases is closely associated with HCC and can mediate the development of HCC through ubiquitination, leading to the activation of some proto-oncogenes or the degradation of oncogenes. , In this study, we found that: (1) FBXO45 is highly expressed in patients with HCC with TP53 mutations and is positively correlated with metastasis in patients with HCC; (2) FBXO45 can significantly promote the invasion and migration of HCC through the EMT pathway; (3) FBXO45 binds to TFG and then promotes its ubiquitination to increase the stability of TFG, thereby activating the NF-κB pathway to promote HCC metastasis; (4) in vivo experiments confirmed that TFG knockdown significantly inhibits FBXO45-driven HCC lung metastasis in mice; (5) IHC staining analysis revealed that FBXO45 expression is significantly correlated with that of TFG in patients with TP53-mutated HCC and that high FBXO45 and TFG co-expression indicates a poor prognosis in patients with HCC. Thus, targeting the TP53–FBXO45–TFG–NF-κB axis might represent a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of HCC metastasis in patients with high FBXO45 expression.
FBXO45, an atypical E3 ubiquitin ligase, is incompatible with the scaffold protein Cullin1 because of a point mutation in the F-box functional domain, but its F-box motif specifically binds to SKP1. Members of the PAM–Highwire–RPM-1 (PHR) protein family are recruited to replace Cullin1 and continue to function as E3 ubiquitin ligases. Recent studies reported that FBXO45 interacts with the tumor suppressor prostate apoptosis response protein 4 (Par-4) in the cytoplasm through VASA-like sequences and that FBXO45 mediates the ubiquitination and proteolysis of par-4, which leads to reduced apoptosis and increased cancer cell survival. In glioma, FBXO45 activates mTORC1 through PI3K/AKT signaling to promote the growth and motility of glioma cells. In addition, other studies revealed that, in esophageal and pancreatic cancers, FBXO45 also negatively regulates the tumor suppressors gametogenetin binding protein 2 (GGNBP2) and ubiquitin-specific peptidase 49 (USP49) through ubiquitination, thereby promoting tumor growth and metastasis. , Our previous studies reported for the first time that FBXO45 is abnormally highly expressed in HCC and that FBXO45 ubiquitination activates insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 1 (IGF2BP1), which promotes HCC proliferation and growth via the IGF2BP1‒PLK1 axis. However, it is unclear whether high FBXO45 expression is associated with metastasis in patients with HCC. In this study, our data suggested that high FBXO45 expression was positively correlated with HCC metastasis and that FBXO45 promoted the migration and invasion of HCC cells.
TFG is located at the exit site of the endoplasmic reticulum in the cytoplasm and is involved in a variety of biological functions. In thyroid cancer, TFG fuses with the 3' end of NTRK1 to produce the TRK-T3 fusion gene, which can cause cancer. In this study, we performed ubiquitomics analyses and identified TFG as a new substrate for FBXO45 in HCC cells, and reported the role of TFG in HCC for the first time. We verified via ubiquitination experiments that FBXO45 exerts its biological function via ubiquitination of TFG at the K103 site. Our results suggest that ubiquitination of TFG at K103 is positively correlated with its ability to promote cell invasion and migration. The NF-κB family contains five members: p65 (RelA), RelB, c-Rel, NF-κB1, and NF-κB2. Unlike the other family members, NF-κB1 and NF-κB2 are synthesized as proforms (p105 and p100) and are proteolytically processed to become p50 and p52, respectively. NF-κB is a key factor in HCC invasion and metastasis. Activated NF-κB can promote HCC invasion and metastasis by regulating EMT. , TFG can activate NF-κB signaling by binding to NF-κB essential modulator (NEMO) and TRAF family member-associated NF-κB activator (TANK) in HeLa cells. TFG can increase the activity of NF-κB and the androgen receptor, which promotes the occurrence of prostate cancer and is closely related to recurrence of this cancer after surgery. 
ATF2, a member of the ATF/CREB family of basic leucine zipper proteins, is ubiquitously expressed and has key roles in gene transcription involved in cell apoptosis, proliferation, development, survival, and skeletal remodeling. , Previous studies reported that ATF2 acts as a transcription factor of NF-κB p65 and is also involved in the progression of HCC. , , In this study, we demonstrated that TFG interacted with ATF2 and upregulated NF-κB p65 expression, thereby upregulating proteins associated with the EMT pathway and subsequently promoting the invasion and migration of HCC cells via EMT.
Overall, our study demonstrated that FBXO45 is abnormally overexpressed in patients with HCC with TP53 mutations. A high level of FBXO45 recognizes and binds TFG and promotes ubiquitination at the lysine 103 residue of TFG, which promotes the stability of TFG and maintains high levels of the TFG protein. In turn, this activates the NF-κB signaling pathway and mediates HCC metastasis via EMT. These results emphasize that targeting the TP53–FBXO45–TFG–ATF2–NF-κB axis may be a novel approach for the treatment of metastatic HCC.
