## ABSTRACT
During the maternal-to-zygotic transition (MZT), the programmed decay of maternal mRNAs is critical for successful embryonic development. Although autophagy is known to participate in early embryonic development, its specific role in maternal mRNA clearance remains unclear. MAP1LC3B/LC3B, a key autophagy-related protein, has recently been identified as an RNA-binding protein; however, whether it contributes to maternal mRNA degradation has not been established. Through integrative analyses combining RIP-seq, RNA-seq, and CUT&Tag in early embryos, we identified LC3B as a maternal mRNA-binding protein essential for mRNA degradation. LC3B-mediated mRNA decay exhibited faster kinetics than the classical BTG4-CCR4-NOT pathway. Knockdown of LC3B or inhibition of autophagy significantly delayed maternal mRNA clearance, resulting in impaired zygotic genome activation (ZGA) and developmental arrest. Further analysis revealed the maternal Suv39h2 as a key LC3B-target gene, whose abnormal persistence correlates with developmental failure. Our findings revealed an autophagy-dependent mRNA clearance pathway mediated by LC3B, providing novel mechanistic insights into maternal mRNA decay and developmental regulation during mammalian MZT.

## Introduction
The maternal-to-zygotic transition (MZT) is a fundamental biological process during early embryogenesis that coordinates two interdependent events: maternal mRNA decay (MD) and zygotic genome activation (ZGA) [ ]. Maternal transcripts undergo two distinct phases of degradation, with the first wave occurring during oocyte maturation [ ] and the second initiated following fertilization [ ]. Current evidence classifies MD into two temporally distinct categories: M-decay, occuring before ZGA, and Z-decay occuring after ZGA [ ]. Post-fertilization MD is essential for successful ZGA and subsequent developmental progression [ ].
Maternal mRNA degradation involves a sequential three-stage process: decapping, deadenylation and exonucleolytic decay [ , ], each mediated by specialized RNA-binding proteins. BTG4, a critical adaptor protein, facilitates mRNAs deadenylation by recruiting the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex in early embryos [ ]. Genetic ablation of BTG4 disrupts poly(A) tail shortening, leading to maternal transcript persistence and developmental arrest at the 4- to 8-cell stage [ , ]. Parallel degradation mechanisms exist for short-tailed maternal mRNAs, mediated by the YAP1-TEAD4-TUT4-TUT7 axis [ , ]. Mechanistically, YAP1-TEAD4 transcriptionally upregulates TUT4 and TUT7 in zygotes, enabling 3’-terminal uridylation and subsequent decay of target mRNAs [ ]. Collectively, these findings highlight RNA-binding proteins as key regulators of MD and highlight the necessity to explore additional factors involved in this process.
Autophagy, a lysosome-dependent quality control system, maintains cellular homeostasis by selective elimination of damaged organelles and protein aggregates [ , ]. Emerging evidence implicates autophagy in mammalian early embryonic development [ ], with atg5−/− embryos arresting at the 4- to 8-cell stage [ ], a phenotype also observed in atg7- and becn1-null models [ , ]. Intriguingly, recent cancer cell studies have revealed LC3B, a canonical autophagy protein involves in autophagosome biogenesis [ ], as a dual-function RNA-binding protein. Athough LC3B is initially reported to mediate mRNA degradation prior to autolysosome formation [ ], subsequent studies indicate that LC3B can stabilize mRNAs through direct transcript interactions [ ]. These contrasting observations prompted us to investigate the potential role of LC3B in embryonic MD.
Through RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (RIP-seq) in early embryos, we demonstrated that LC3B preferentially binds maternal Z-decay mRNAs, accelerating their degradation and thereby modulating ZGA efficiency. Notably, LC3B-mediated decay operates through an autophagy-dependent pathway, distinct from BTG4-mediated deadenylation or TUT4 and TUT7 mediated uridylation.

## Results

## LC3B functions as an RNA-binding protein in early embryos
During early embryonic development, specific maternal proteins mediate the MD enabling proper ZGA [ , ]. We analyzed 573 proteins associated with RNA degradation, among which 399 were maternal genes [ ], including Btg4, Yap1, and several members of the Cnot family ( ). Transcript analysis of these 399 genes in late 2-cell (L2C) embryos revealed high expression of Map1lc3b/Lc3b, a gene recently identified as mediator of RNAs degradation in HEK 293T cells ( ). This finding prompted us to investigate whether LC3B mediates MD in early embryos. The expression of Lc3b was abundant in germinal vesicle (GV) and metaphase II (MII) oocytes, and further increased during ZGA (Fig. S1A). Immunofluorescence (IF) analysis revealed dynamic numbers of LC3B dots across embryonic stages, peaking at the L2C stage ( ).

 To characterize LC3B-targeted mRNAs, we performed RIP-seq on pronuclear stage 5 (PN5), early 2-cell (E2C), and L2C embryos ( ). Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated stage-specific dynamics in LC3B-target genes ( ). Specifically, a total of 5,650 peaks covering 1,818 mRNAs were identified in PN5 embryos, 61,783 peaks covering 5,201 mRNAs in E2C embryos, and 73,663 peaks covering 5,863 mRNAs in L2C embryos ( and Figure S1B). LC3B-targeted genes exhibited dynamic changes between adjacent stages, with most targets gained from PN5 to L2C and a small fraction lost from E2C to L2C ( ). Analysis of LC3B-bound mRNAs revealed significant enrichment in untranslated regions (3’UTR and 5’UTR), transcription termination sites (TTS), exons, and promoters ( and Figure S1C). Motif enrichment analysis further showed that LC3B predominantly bound to AU-rich sequences, and specifically associated with AAAUCA and UUAAAA motifs at the E2C and L2C stages, respectively (Fig. S1D). Interestingly, LC3B preferentially bound maternal mRNAs over ZGA or mid-preimplantation genes activation (MGA) mRNAs, despite high LC3B expression during ZGA ( ). Maternal genes such as Flna, Akr1b1, and Anxa7 were consistently targeted by LC3B across PN5, E2C, and L2C stages ( ). Collectively, these findings indicated that LC3B may act as an RNA-binding protein that regulate the MD in early embryos.

## LC3B mediates maternal Z-decay
We identified 2,533 MD genes highly expressed at the GV and MII stages that gradually decayed after fertilization, and 1,115 ZGA genes highly expressed at the L2C stage ( ) [ ]. LC3B primarily targeted MD genes, binding 28.6% of MD genes and 1.8% of ZGA genes at the PN5 stage, 68.4% of MD genes and 6.1% of ZGA genes at the E2C stage, and 50.8% of MD genes and 34.5% of ZGA genes at the L2C stage ( ). LC3B-targeted MD genes exhibited higher expression than non-targeted MD genes (Fig. S1E), suggesting that LC3B preferentially binds highly expressed maternal mRNAs. Further analysis revealed that LC3B primarily facilitated Z-decay rather than M-decay ( ). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis indicated that LC3B-targeted Z-decay genes were associated with cell division, while non-targeted genes were primarily linked to mitochondrial function ( ). We compared LC3B-mediated MD with that mediated by BTG4, a well-known maternal MD factor. LC3B and BTG4 co-regulated 413 maternal mRNAs (16.3% of total maternal genes), while 876 mRNAs (34.54%) were specifically regulated by LC3B and 484 (19.06%) by BTG4 ( and Figure S1F). LC3B-specific targeted genes were enriched in cell division associated processes, whereas BTG4-specific targeted genes were associated with mitochondrial respiration ( ). Co-regulated genes exhibited the fastest decay rates, followed by LC3B-specific and BTG4-specific genes ( ). TUT4-TUT7 are other critical RNA-binding proteins in early embryos. LC3B and TUT4-TUT7 co-regulated 787 maternal mRNAs (31.06% of total maternal genes), while 502 mRNAs (19.78%) were specifically regulated by LC3B and 713 (28.11%) by TUT4-TUT7 (Fig. S1G and H). Co-regulated genes exhibited the fastest decay rates, followed by TUT4-TUT7-specific and LC3B-specific genes (Fig. S1I). Similarily, LC3B-specific targeted genes were enriched in chromosome segregation associated process, whereas TUT4-TUT7-specific targeted genes were associated with aerobic respiration (Fig. S1J).

 To validate these findings, we knocked down and overexpressed LC3B in embryos ( ). The efficiency of knockdown (LC3B KD) and overexpression (LC3B OE) were confirmed (Fig. S2A-E). RNA-seq analysis (Fig. S2F and G) revealed that LC3B knockdown upregulated Z-decay genes and downregulated ZGA genes, while overexpression had the opposite effects ( ). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) confirmed these findings ( ). In addition, LC3B KD did not affect the expression of the key CCR4-NOT pathway proteins BTG4, CNOT1, and CNOT7 (Fig. S2H-J). These results demonstrated that LC3B mediates maternal Z-decay, thereby influencing ZGA and embryonic development, likely in cooperation with BTG4 and TUT4-TUT7 mediated pathway.

## Autophagy influences maternal Z-decay
LC3B, a key autophagy-related protein, plays a role in the expansion and closure of autophagosome membranes [ ]. We hypothesized that LC3B mediates maternal mRNAs degradation through the autophagy pathway. To test this, we treated embryos with the autophagy inhibitor CQ and the activator Rapa. Both treatments significantly altered the number of LC3B dots (Fig. S3A and B). CQ treatment caused embryonic arrest at various stages ( ), whereas Rapa had no adverse effects on embryo development (Fig. S3C). Neither treatment induced elevation of reactive oxygen specie (ROS) and apoptosis (Fig. S3D-G). Bafilomycin A1 (BafA1), another autophagy inhibitor, also blocked embryonic development (Fig. S3H).

 RNA-seq analysis of CQ- and Rapa-treated embryos (Fig. S4A and B) revealed 846 downregulated and 946 upregulated genes after CQ treatment, 561 downregulated and 402 upregulated genes after Rapa treatment. CQ treatment repressed the degradation of Z-decay genes and thereafter impaired ZGA, whereas Rapa treatment enhanced the process of Z-decay and consequently promoted ZGA ( ). GSEA further confirmed that Z-decay genes were enriched in CQ-upregulated and Rapa-downregulated genes, while ZGA genes were enriched in CQ-downregulated and Rapa-upregulated genes ( ). Similar to CQ, BafA1 treatment resulted in the failure of Z-decay genes degradation (Fig. S4C-E). Moreover, knockdown of the key autophagy gene Atg5 also led to a significant upregulation of Z-decay genes (Fig. S4G-K). These results indicated that autophagy plays a pivotal role in the regulation of MD, and consequently affected ZGA in early embryos. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) revealed that CQ treatment inhibited chromosome segregation, while Rapa treatment promoted nuclear division and suppressed RNA methylation ( ). ZGA and maternal mRNAs showed opposite trends after autophagy inhibition or activation ( ). In addition, the expression of the key CCR4-NOT pathway proteins BTG4, CNOT1 and CNOT7 were not affected by CQ or Rapa (Fig. S4L-Q). Collectively, these findings demonstrated that autophagy facilitates maternal Z-decay and therafter supports ZGA in early embryos.

## LC3B mediates maternal Suv39h2 decay via autophagy
To explore how LC3B regulates MD, we identified 595 Z-decay genes consistently targeted by LC3B at PN5, E2C, and L2C stages. These included known maternal genes such as Zp2, Nobox, Padi6, Mrps2, Ccdc69, and Suv39h2 ( ). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these genes are involved in histone modification, critical for ZGA ( ). Suv39h2 is a methyltransferase for H3K9me3, LC3B peaks were significantly enriched at Suv39h2 mRNAs in L2C embryos ( ), and RIP-qPCR confirmed their interaction ( ). RNA affinity-isolation experiments in embryonic stem cells (ESCs) further validated the binding of LC3B to Suv39h2 ( ). Because autophagy delivers cargo to lysosomes for degradation, we performed fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) combined with immunofluorescence to examine whether LC3B transports maternal mRNAs to lysosomes. We observed colocalization of maternal Suv39h2 with lysosomal markers, which decreased after CQ or BafA1 treatment ( ). These results suggested that LC3B mediates the degradation of maternal Suv39h2 through autophagy.

 

## Impaired Suv39h2 degradation prevents H3K9me3 reduction and disrupts embryonic development
Suv39h2 is highly expressed in oocytes and gradually declines in early embryos (Fig. S5A and B). Similarly, SUV39H2 protein levels decreased during early embryonic development (Fig. S5C). CQ treatment increased both Suv39h2 mRNA and protein levels in L2C embryos ( ), and similar effects were observed following BafA1 treatment and LC3B KD (Fig. S5D-G). Overexpression of Suv39h2 in zygotes (Suv39h2 OE) (Fig. S5H and I) caused developmental arrest and compromised blastocyst quality ( and Figure S5J). RNA-seq analysis revealed 172 downregulated and 245 upregulated genes in Suv39h2 OE embryos (Fig. S5K and L). GSEA showed significant enrichment of ZGA-associated genes among downregulated genes ( ). H3K9me3 modification was dynamically regulated during early embryonic development (Fig. S5M), and incomplete demethylation of H3K9me3 severely blocks ZGA and early embryo development [ ]. Overexpression of Suv39h2, autophagy inhibition, or LC3B KD increased global H3K9me3 levels ( and Figure S5N-P). Cut&Tag analysis revealed enhanced H3K9me3 peaks coverage and signal density after CQ treatment, such as Ormdl3, Clu, Gulo and Adm2. ( ; Figure S6A). Genomic coverage analysis identified 36,672 gained and 26,059 lost H3K9me3 peaks after CQ treatment (Fig. S6B). These peaks were enriched in Long Terminal Repeat (LTR) and Long Interspersed Nuclear Element (LINE) regions, particularly MERVL-int, which is essential for ZGA ( ; Figure S6C) [ ].

 Treatment with chaetocin, a specific inhibitor of SUV39H2 [ ], rescued the embryonic arrest and H3K9me3 increase caused by autophagy inhibition ( ). Transcriptomic analysis revealed 308 downregulated and 319 upregulated genes in the CQ+chaetocin group compared to CQ group (Fig. S6D and E). Notably, GSEA demonstrated restoration of ZGA genes expression ( ). Similarly, Suv39h2 knockdown achieved a rescue effect comparable to that of chaetocin (Fig. S6F-L). These findings suggested that autophagy inhibition leads to Suv39h2 accumulation, increased H3K9me3 modification on MERVL, and subsequent ZGA failure and embryonic development arrest.

## Discussion
Maternal mRNAs decay is a critical regulatory process mediated by RNA-binding proteins during the MZT [ ]; however, its mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Through RIP-seq analyses in early embryos, we identified LC3B as an RNA-binding protein targeting maternal mRNAs and primarily facilitating maternal Z-decay. Upon autophagy induction, LC3B directly interacts with maternal transcripts, recruiting them into autophagosomes for lysosomal degradation. Additionally, we demonstrated that autophagy inhibition disrupts LC3B-mediated clearance of maternal Suv39h2, leading to increased H3K9me3 modification at MERVL retrotransposons, impaired ZGA and embryonic developmental arrest ( ).

 Our findings extended the current understanding of MD regulatory networks. While CCR4-NOT-mediated degradation remains the principal pathway for maternal mRNAs clearance [ , ], we identified LC3B-dependent autophagic degradation as a complementary mechanism with distinct target specificity. Comparison between LC3B- and BTG4-targeted transcripts identified three distinct functional categories: 1) BTG4-exclusive targets genes enriched in mitochondrial respiratory chain components; 2) LC3B-specific targets predominantly encoding meiotic regulators and chromosomal segregation machinery; and 3) co-regulated transcripts associated with iron-sulfur cluster assembly. These differential function and degradation rates among maternal mRNAs subgroups underscore distinct mechanistic strategies between the two degradation pathways. Transcripts co-targeted by LC3B and BTG4 showed the fastest decay rates, suggesting a synergistic action between autophagic and CCR4-NOT pathway that enhances the efficiency of maternal mRNA degradation. Similarly, we also identified a substantial number of maternal mRNAs co-degraded by LC3B and TUT4-TUT7, which likewise showed the most rapid degradation. Our results further demonstrated that LC3B knockdown or autophagy inhibition did not affect the expression of key proteins in the CCR4-NOT and TUT4-TUT7 pathways, indicating that autophagy dysfunction does not impair traditional degradation mechanisms. Moreover, when autophagic activity is reduced or canonical pathways are impaired, these two distinct mechanisms may compensate for each other, particularly in the degradation of co-targeted maternal mRNAs.
LC3B-mediated degradation accounted for a larger proportion of maternal mRNAs and proceeded at a faster rate compared to BTG4-mediated degradation. Previous studies have shown that Btg4 and Cnot7, key components of the CCR4-NOT pathway, are maternal genes whose expression declines after fertilization [ ]. In contrast, Lc3b mRNA expression gradually increases in the early embryos. In addition, expression ranking of RNA-binding proteins at the 2-cell stage also indicated that Lc3b expression exceeded that of Btg4, Cnot1 and Cnot7. This differential expression might contributed to the greater extent and accelerated rate of LC3B-mediated maternal mRNAs degradation. Temporal coordination was evidenced by LC3B activity peaking during ZGA initiation at the 2-cell stage, coinciding with embryonic demands for timely maternal factors elimination. This phased activation pattern suggested that autophagy-mediated degradation may serve as a rapid response system complementing the traditional degradation pathway.
The epigenetic implications of LC3B-mediated MD further confirms its developmental importance. By mediating Suv39h2 mRNA degradation, this pathway effectively removed a key methyltransferase responsible for H3K9me3 modification at MERVL retrotransposons [ , ]. Given that MERVL elements simultaneously function as both retrotransposons and zygotic genome activation hubs, their chromatin state modulation is critical for embryonic development [ , ]. Autophagy inhibition experiments confirmed the functional interplay between MD and chromatin remodeling: accumulated Suv39h2 mRNA enhanced repressive H3K9me3 marks at MERVL, directly impairing ZGA efficiency. This mechanistic coupling ensured coordinated execution of maternal transcript elimination and zygotic transcriptional reprogramming during the MZT.
Overall, our study identified LC3B as a novel RNA-binding protein mediated maternal mRNAs clearance through autophagy, thereby facilitating ZGA and early embryonic development. This discovery extended the mechanistic repertoire of MD beyond conventional degradation pathways. Autophagic activity in early embryos declines with advancing maternal age [ , ], and enhancing it can improve developmental competence in aged embryos [ , ]. Our study elucidated the underlying mechanism through autophagy-mediated MD, offering a potential strategy to enhance assisted reproductive technology (ART) outcomes in women with advanced age.

## Materials and methods

## Mice
Female and male ICR mice aged 6–8 weeks were selected for this study. The mouse experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Chongqing Maternal and Child Health Hospital (approval number: 2,023,040), and the relevant guidelines of animal ethics and animal welfare were strictly observed.

## Embryo collection
Female mice were injected with 10 IU of pregnant horse serum gonadotropin (PMSG; Sansheng Biological, S181111), 10 IU of human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG; Sansheng Biological, S180801) was injected 48 h later and they were 1:1 crossed with male mice. 17 h later, the female mice were sacrificed and the fertilized zygotes from the ampulla of the fallopian tube were collected for culture in vitro. PN5, early 2 cell (E2C), middle 2 cell (M2C), late 2 cell (L2C), 4 cell, and 8-cell stage embryos were collected after HCG: 24 h, 34 h, 41 h, 48 h, 58 h, 69 h.

## Medical treatment
In this study, CQ (MedChemExpress, HY-17589A) was used as an autophagy inhibitor, rapamycin (MedChemExpress, HY-10219) as an autophagy activator, and chaetocin (MedChemExpress, HY-N2019) as an inhibitor of SUV39H2. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (was used for dissolution, and the negative control group embryos were cultured with 1:1000 of DMSO. The fertilized zygotes were treated with chemicals 24 h after HCG.

## In vitro transcription of Trim21 mRNA
To prepare mRNAs for microinjection, pGEMHE-mCherry-mTrim21 (Addgene, 105,522) vectors were linearized and purified with phenol – chloroform, followed by ethanol precipitation. Linearized DNAs were transcribed and added to poly (A) tails (200–250 bp) in vitro using the HiScribe T7 ARCA mRNA Kit (with tailing; NEB, E2060S), recovered by lithium chloride precipitation, and resuspended in nuclease-free water. Trim21 mRNA was frozen and stored at −80°C.

## siRNA and trim away for gene knockdown
All siRNAs used in this study were purchased from GenePharma. 1 µg/µL Trim21 mRNA, 0.5 µg/µL antibodies, and 10 µM siRNA were mixed and centrifuged at 16,099 g for 10 min at 4°C, and stored at 4°C until use. Then the mixtures were injected into zygotes through microinjection. Microinjection was performed using a FemtoJet 4i microinjector (Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany) and E LIPSE Ti micromanipulators (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). For injection, a glass capillary Femtotip (Eppendorf) was loaded with 2 μL of mixtures using a Microloader (Eppendorf), and the solution was injected into the cytoplasm in a drop of M2 medium (Merck, M7167). The injection volume was approximately 2–5 pL. The injection conditions consisted of an injection pressure of 250 hPa, compensation pressure of 60 hPa, and injection time of 0.7 s. Immediately after microinjection, embryos were cultured in KSOM medium at 37°C in 5% CO2.

## Immunofluorescence staining
After removing the zona pellucida with 0.3% HCl, the mouse embryos were fixed at room temperature in 4% paraformaldehyde (Invitrogen, FB002) for 30 min, and then permeated in 1% Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich, 93,443) for 10 min. After washing in 0.01% Triton X-100 solution 3 times, the embryos were blocked in a 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA; Beyotime, P0216) PBS (Hyclone, SH30256.01) solution at room temperature for 1 h. LC3B (Cell Signaling Technology, 83,506; 1:500), SQSTM1/p62 (abcam, ab109012; 1:200), H3K9me3 (abcam, ab8898; 1:200), and SUV39H2 (abcam, ab190870; 1:500) primary antibodies were incubated at 4°C overnight. The next day, the embryos were washed three times in the washing solution, and the secondary antibodies (Invitrogen, A10040; 1:2000) were incubated at room temperature for 1 h, and then the nucleus was stained with Hoechst 33,342 (Beyotime, C1022; 1:500) for 10 min. After washing the embryos in the washing solution, embryos were sealed with Antifade Mounting Medium (Beyotime, P0126). Embryo imaging was performed using an inverted confocal microscope (Leica, TCS SP8) and analyzed using LAS X software.

## qPCR
Total RNA was extracted using the Arcturus PicoPure RNA Isolation Kit (Ambion, 12,204–01) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, RR036) to generate cDNA. We performed qPCR using the TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, RR820) and the CFX96 Real-Time System. The reaction parameters were as follows: 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 two-step cycles of 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 34 s. Hprt was used as a reference gene. Ct values were calculated using the CFX96 system software, and the target sequences were normalized to the reference sequence using the 2−∆∆Ct method. The primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

## RNA-seq library construction
RNA-seq libraries were prepared as previously described. Briefly, 5 embryos were used per reaction, and replicates were performed for each group. All embryos and cells were washed three times with 0.5% BSA buffer to avoid possible contamination. Then, we used Phusion Hot Start II High- Fidelity PCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher, F–565S) to obtain cDNA. The NEB Next Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit (New England Biolabs, E7805S) according to the manufacturer’s instructions was used to generate the library. The quality of the cDNA product was tested using a Qubit®3.0 fluorometer (ThermoFisher Scientific) and a 2100 bioanalyzer (Agilent, Santa Clara) to ensure that its length was approximately 1–2 kb. The samples were sequenced by Giinga.

## RNA-seq data processing
For RNA-seq data, Fastp was used to remove raw sequence reads that contained adapter contamination and poor-quality reads. We used HISAT2 (version 2.1.0) [ ] to map the clean reads to the mouse reference genome (mm10). FeatureCounts (version 2.0.1) [ ] was used to calculate the read count of genes based on the GRCm38.101 annotation file which was downloaded from the Ensemble database. Differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq2 (version 1.30.0) [ ] package in R project, and genes with significantly higher changes (absolute value of log2 (fold changes) > 1 and P-value < 0.05) were considered as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis was carried out by using clusterProfile [ ] with default parameters.GESA analysis were conducted by GSEA software.

## Library preparation for RIP-seq
Two duplicate samples were separately prepared for PN5, E2C and L2C embryos, and 1000 embryos were collected for each sample. Merck’s RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation kit (Merck, 17–701) was used following the manufacturer’s instructions. And a ChIP level LC3B primary antibody (NOVUS, NB100-2220SS) was used. Finally, the RNA samples were sent to BGI for library construction and sequencing.

## RIP-Seq data analysis
Raw reads were cleaned by SOAPnuke [ ] with “-n 0.001 -l 20 -q 0.4 –adaMR 0.25 –polyX 50 –minReadLen 100” parameters. Clean reads were then aligned to the mm10 genome with TopHat (v2.1.1). Peak calling of LC3B Rip seq was performed using the piranha [ ] software with specific option settings (-z 20 -a 0.9) and input sample were used as covariance. Only overlapping peak from two biological replicates were used for further analyses. Peaks annotation and bedtools intersect tools were used to get the overlap of peaks with mm10 annotation files. Enrichment of Rip peaks in different genomic region was calculated using observed/expected value. The observed value was calculated using the length of the Rip peaks that covers the related genomic regions versus the length of the total Rip peaks, and the expected value was calculated using the length of the total related genomic regions versus the length of the mouse genome.

## Library preparation for Cut&tag
Hyperactive InSitu ChIP Library Prep Kit for Illumina kit (Vazyme Biotech, TD901) was used for Cut&Tag. According to the manufacturer described, embryos were incubated with Dig-wash buffer containing 0.5 μg H3K9me3 primary antibody and 50 μL antibody buffer at 4°C overnight. After washing with Dig-wash buffer twice, 100 μL Dig-wash buffer containing 1 μg secondary antibody was added and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. After being washed with Dig-wash buffer for 2 times, 50 μL Dig-300 buffer containing 1 μL pA/G-Tnp was added and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Embryos were washed with Dig-300 buffer twice and put in 50 μL Dig-300 buffer containing 10 μL 5×TTBL (50 μL for each sample), then incubated at 37°C for 60 min. Each sample was added with 1.75 μL 0.5 M ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA; Solarbio, IE9040), 0.5 μL 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS; Solarbio, IR9180) and 0.5 μL 20 mg/mL protease K (Vazyme, 7E020314) and incubated at 50°C for 1 h. DNA was extracted using DNA extraction buffer (G-CLONE, EX0128) and PCR was used for library amplification. VAHTS DNA Clean Beads (Vazyme Biotech, N411) were used to purify the amplified products according to the specification. The quality of the library was tested using a Qubit®3.0 fluorometer and a 2100 bioanalyzer. The samples were sequenced by Giinga.

## Cut&tag data analysis
Fastp was used to remove raw sequence reads that contained adapter and poor-quality. Clean reads were then mapping to mm10 gemome use bwa mem tools. Picardtools MarkDuplicates were used to remove the PCR duplication reads. Samples from different replication were merge by samtools merge. MACS2 was used to call peaks with the following parameter “-f BAMPE -g mm – n – nomodel – broad.” Heatmaps or lineplot of Cut&Tag data were ploted by DeepTools [ ].

## Gene group categorization
Maternal genes were defined as genes with FPKM values in the GV or MII stage > 5. ZGA genes were defined as genes with FPKM values in the GV and MII stages < 1, FPKM values in the zygote and 2C stages > 1, and fold change (2C/GV) values > 4. ZGA genes were classified by the following formulas: Minor ZGA genes: FPKM values in GV and MII stages < 1 and L1C or L2C > 1; 4C/L2C > 2; others were defined as Major ZGA genes. MGA genes were defined as genes with FPKM values in the GV, MII, L1C, L2C stage < 1 and 4C > 1 and foldchange(4C/GV) > 4. Maternal decay genes were defined as genes with FPKM values in the GV or MII stage > 5 and fold change values (GV/2C) > 4. The MD transcripts, M-decay, Z-decay were classified according to published article [ ].

## FISH-IF
The Suv39h2, Rn18s rRNA and negative control probes were designed by Gemma Gene. An RNA FISH SA-Biotin amplification system kit (RNA FISH SA-Biotin; Gemma Gene, F12402/50) was used for experiments. Briefly, the glass coverslips were coated with 0.01% polylysine glue (Beyotime, ST508), and then the embryos with removed pellucida were fixed on glass coverslips with 4% polyformaldehyde. The probe was pretreated before use: the probe dry powder was dissolved in enzyme-free water according to the instructions, and the probe was fluorescently labeled with cy5 (included in the kit). The probe was diluted to the working concentration and the embryos were incubated overnight in a constant temperature shaking bed at 37°C. The next day, the probe mixture was inhaled and cleaned with PBS, then closed with 0.1% BSA-PBS for 1 h, and then incubated with LAMP1 primary antibody (abcam, ab208943; 1:200) overnight. On the second day, secondary antibodies were incubated and nucleated with DAPI (included in the kit). After incubation of fluorescent probe, the whole experiment was kept away from light.

## Statistical analyses
All statistical analyses were performed using Graphpad 7.0. Data are expressed as means ±standard error of the mean (SEM). Differences between means were evaluated using the two-tailed Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank sum test. Asterisks indicate significant differences as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001, p < 0.0001.
