## Abstract
Dysregulated inflammatory reactions can result in detrimental effects to the body, thereby causing various diseases. Traditional treatments relying on anti-inflammatory drugs or nanoformulations often undermine the body's physiological immune functions or potentially exhibit biotoxicity. Extracellular vesicles, which contain a diverse array of anti-inflammatory substances and possess nanomedicine transport properties, are emerging as highly promising candidates for next-generation drug delivery systems and active biological agents. Plant-derived vesicle-like nanoparticles (PDVLNs) are nanostructured particles isolated from plants. Given their wide availability and low immunogenicity, PDVLNs are considered to hold great potential in the treatment of inflammatory diseases (IDs). In this review, we introduce the principle, design consideration and treatment mechanism associated with PDVLNs in treating various IDs. Specifically, the natural ingredients carried by PDVLNs not only help eliminate danger signals such as reactive oxygen species and reactive nitrogen species, but also hinder the initiation of inflammatory responses through various mechanisms. Moreover, engineered PDVLNs nanotechnology has been successfully employed in the treatment of IDs. Finally, the review discusses the current opportunities and challenges in this field and provides insights for the future clinical applications of PDVLNs in treating IDs.
Keywords: Plant-derived vesicle-like nanoparticles, Inflammatory diseases, Nano-drug delivery, Design principle, Therapy strategy

## Introduction
The four primary symptoms of inflammation, namely redness, swelling, heat and pain, were described by Cornelius Celsus, a Roman writer, early in the first century. Since then, researchers have continually investigated the pathological mechanisms underlying inflammatory diseases (IDs) as well as effective therapeutic strategies to address these conditions. As scientists understanding of the pathological mechanism deepens, inflammation is considered as a defensive physiological response of biological tissues triggered by invading pathogens or endogenous signals, such as those released from damaged cells. These physiological responses trigger the corresponding anti-inflammatory mechanisms to achieve a homeostasis in the body, while at the same time removing harmful substances and restoring normal life activities [ ]. However, uncontrolled inflammation frequently disrupts the balance between the pro- and anti-inflammatory systems, resulting in the excessive release of various inflammatory mediators including nitric oxide species, reactive oxygen species, chemokines, and cytokines. Dysregulation of inflammatory processes can affect multiple bodily systems and is strongly linked to a wide range of diseases. Therefore, the development and design of effective anti-inflammatory drugs represent a critical strategy for the precise regulation of excessive inflammation in various IDs. While certain anti-inflammatory drugs, including non-steroidal agents, glucocorticoids, and biological therapies, have shown considerable efficacy in clinical practice for reducing inflammation [ , ], they may also lead to a variety of side effects. Potential complications include an increased risk of infection, osteoporosis, elevated blood sugar levels, liver damage, peptic ulcers, glaucoma, skin bruising, confusion, interstitial nephritis and other health issues. Nanomedicines possess distinctive biological properties that arise from their nanostructures, providing several advantages, including improved drug solubility, stability, and selectivity [ , ]. As a result, they significantly reduce the side effects commonly associated with anti-inflammatory drug therapies. Among the various nanoplatforms available for clinical translation, vesicle-based nanomedicine emerges as the most widely employed. However, synthetic nanovesicle delivery systems continue to face challenges in the treatment of infectious diseases, primarily due to their limited physical stability and inadequate ability to penetrate biological barriers such as tissue and cell membranes.
Extracellular vesicles (EVs) possess a structure analogous to that of synthetic nanovesicles, such as liposomes, and are primarily composed of phospholipids [ ]. These EVs are secreted by a wide variety of cell types, including immune cells, cancer cells, stem cells, blood cells, and so on. EVs can be classified into three subgroups based on their size and biological functions: exosomes (40–160 nm), microvesicles (100–1,000 nm), and apoptotic bodies (100–5,000 nm) [ ]. These vesicles have the capacity to transport a wide range of signaling molecules originating from their parent cells, including nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, and metabolites. They play a vital role in cellular communication, signaling, and intercellular interactions. Their primary functions include facilitating cell communication and signaling, regulating immune responses, promoting tissue repair and regeneration, enabling drug delivery, and contributing to disease treatment. More importantly, due to their inherent characteristics, EVs possess a remarkable ability to navigate biological barriers and achieve homologous targeting during in vivo transport. Consequently, anti-inflammatory strategies utilizing EVs have been demonstrated to be effective across various IDs [ ]. Despite the promising applications of EVs, current research mainly focuses on mammalian cell-derived EVs. However, several significant challenges impede the clinical application of these EVs, including: (1) low yield, (2) a time-consuming and labor-intensive production process and (3) difficulties in obtaining high-quality and uniform EVs [ ].
Plant-derived drugs exhibit multifunctional and multi-target anti-inflammatory properties. Among these, plant-derived vesicle-like nanoparticles (PDVLNs) stand out as particularly promising drug candidates due to their unique nanostructure and inherent anti-inflammatory effects [ ]. The identification of carrot-derived PDVLNs by Halperin in 1967 initiated the successful isolation of PDVLNs from various plant sources. Since then, these nanoparticles, derived from a diverse array of plants, have been employed in the modulation and intervention of human diseases, as illustrated in A [ , , ]. In recent years, PDVLNs have been produced in substantial quantities using conventional methods, such as plant crushing and differential centrifugation, which are similar to those used for the preparation of EVs ( B). These nanoparticles not only encapsulate a variety of bioactive molecules, including proteins, RNA, and other pharmacologically active compounds, but they also exhibit distinct morphological and compositional features ( C). PDVLNs share several advantages with animal-derived EVs, including the ability to penetrate biological barriers, low immune specificity, and prolonged blood circulation time. Moreover, due to the distinctive characteristic of plant-based therapies in treating diseases, PDVLNs exhibit incomparable advantages of animal-derived EVs: (1) PDVLNs are abundant in phospholipids that are typically absent in mammalian-derived EVs, including phosphatidic acid (PA) and phosphatidylcholine (PC); These qualitative lipid features contribute to the intrinsic regulatory activities of mammalian cells. (2) PDVLNs originate from a variety of sources, enabling researchers to select PDVLNs that are most suitable for specific diseases; (3) PDVLNs primarily derived from edible plants or traditional Chinese medicine are non-immunogenic and free from pathogens such as rabies, avian influenza, and anthrax, which can cause human diseases. Furthermore, PDVLNs have demonstrated minimal cytotoxicity to healthy tissues and exhibit good biocompatibility; (4) In contrast to mammalian cell culture, which is considerably more expensive, plants can be sourced from a diverse range of beneficial renewable sources through large-scale manufacturing processes, offering advantages in terms of broad availability and high cost-effectiveness; (5) PDVLNs are environmentally friendly due to their natural and sustainable plant origin. Therefore, PDVLNs hold significant therapeutic potential in the field of IDs ( D).

## Physical characterization and composition analysis of PDVLNs
The rapid development of PDVLNs nanotechnology has led to increased demands for precise and diverse characterization. Characterizing the structure and composition of PDVLNs serves two primary purposes: verifies the successful isolation of PDVLNs, and establishes the relationship between their structure, composition and functions. Accurate characterization holds significant value as a reference for future clinical applications. The characterization process primarily involves evaluating their potential, particle size, surface morphology and composition.
2.1.

## Physical characterization of PDVLNs
2.1.1.

## Potential and particle size
Most PDVLNs exhibit negative charges on their surfaces, although their potentials can vary significantly, ranging from −1 to −50 mV. This variation may be attributed to the differing components of PDVLNs from various sources. The literature reports several methods for measuring the size of PDVLNs, including dynamic light scattering (DLS), tunable resistive pulse sensing (TRPS) and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA). DLS utilizes light scattering fluctuations to calculate the hydrodynamic diameter of PDVLNs [ ]. However, due to polydispersity and potentially weak scattering contrast, DLS is unable to accurately determine the true state of PDVLNs. In contrast, NTA captures the motion trajectories of individual PDVLNs and employs mathematical calculations to assess both the size distribution and quantity of PDVLNs. As a result, NTA has emerged as one of the most widely adopted techniques for characterizing PDVLNs [ ]. TRPS, an emerging technology, determines the size, potential and concentration of particles by monitoring the changes in resistance pulses generated as particles traverse a pore. PDVLNs typically exhibit particle sizes ranging from 20 to 1,000 nm, a range that is closely linked to their compositional type and the separation process employed.

2.1.2.

## Morphology
Morphological characterizations are essential in the study of PDVLNs, as they provide researchers with direct information. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) are commonly used techniques for imaging PDVLNs. When examined under an electron microscope, PDVLNs typically display a spherical, elliptical or cup-shaped morphology, which is regarded as the classic morphology [ ]. However, the size of PDVLNs observed through electron microscopy is smaller than that calculated by DLS or NTA [ , ]. This discrepancy may be attributed to the extensive sample preparation procedures necessary for SEM and TEM, which include fixation, dehydration and staining. These processes can lead to dehydration and deformation of PDVLNs. In contrast, cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) enables the analysis of PDVLNs at −100 °C, thereby eliminating the need for fixation and staining, which ensures the preservation of their true morphology. In addition to electron microscopy-based methods, atomic force microscopy (AFM) can provide three-dimensional surface information, as well as physical parameters such as viscoelasticity and membrane stiffness. However, the utilization of AFM for characterizing the morphology of PDVLNs is less common than that of electron microscopy, which may be attributed to the more complex testing procedures and stringent sample requirements associated with AFM.

2.2.

## Composition analysis
PDVLNs primarily consist of lipids, proteins, nucleic acid and metabolites. The composition of PDVLNs varies significantly depending on their plant source ( ) [ ]. Additionally, there are notable distinctions between the composition of PDVLNs and mammalian-derived EVs. Consequently, to enhance the biomedical applications of PDVLNs, it is essential to isolate and identify the active components present in the various types of PDVLNs.
 2.2.1.

## Lipid
The lipid bilayer structure of PDVLNs resembles that of animal-derived EVs, playing a crucial role in its stability. However, the lipid composition of PDVLNs differs markedly from that of these vesicles. Substantial evidence suggests that the specific lipids present in PDVLNs possess unique therapeutic functions not found in animal-derived EVs. Notably, PDVLNs is characterized by elevated levels of PA, PC, phosphatidylglycerol (PG) and phosphatidylinositol (PI). Additionally, components such as galactosediglycerol (DGDG) and monogalactosediacylglycerol (MGDG) are also present [ ]. The composition and concentration of these lipids significantly influence the drug loading and delivery capabilities of PDVLN, as well as its biodistribution and transport functions within the body.
Analysis of tea-derived PDVLNs using triple quadrupole mass spectrometry (TQMS) revealed that lipid composition primarily consisted of PA (32.7%−45.7% of total lipids), PG (19.3%−22.7%), PC (13.6%−19.2%) and PI (5.9%−6.2%) [ ]. Mass spectrometry analysis further demonstrated that the PA content in the lipids of PDVLNs derived from turmeric, grapefruit and garlic was 34.4%, 3.5% and 5.5%, respectively [ ]. Another study reported that ginger-derived PDVLNs contained PA (41.9%), PC (6.5%), DGDG (27.4%), PI (3.5%), MGDG (18.9%), PE (0.7%) and PG (0.2%) [ ]. Lipidomic analysis of ginseng-derived PDVLNs revealed the presence of DGMG (59.4%), PE (16.8%) and ceramide (13.8%) [ ]. Furthermore, tandem mass spectrometry analysis of oat-derived PDVLNs identified PC (30%) and DGDG (29.8%) as the major lipid components [ ]. Li's group employed lipidomic analysis to determine that Portulaca oleracea L-derived PDVLNs primarily consisted of DGDG (24.47%), triglyceride (17.09%) and PC (9.28%) [ ]. Similarly, Sheng's group used LC-MS in conjunction with the lipid maps structure database (LMSD) to identify the lipid components of PDVLNs derived from Robinia pseudoacacia L. at both the class and subclass levels. Their findings indicated that Robinia pseudoacacia L-derived PDVLNs were enriched in fatty acyls (51.72%), glycerophospholipids (17.73%), glycerolipids (7.88%), sphingolipids (7.39%), sterol lipids (6.90%), prenol lipids (5.91%) and polyketides (2.46%) [ ].

2.2.2.

## Protein
Proteins play a crucial role in the formation, regulation, and information transmission of PDVLNs, as well as in their docking with donor cells [ , ]. From an evolutionary biology standpoint, plants are often perceived as less advanced than animals, which may contribute to a deficiency in the complex proteins required for energy metabolism and inter-tissue communication [ ]. As a result, the protein structure of PDVLNs is relatively simple and less abundant compared to EVs derived from animals. The proteins present in PDVLNs can be primarily classified into three categories: transmembrane proteins, intracellular proteins, and peripheral membrane proteins.
Common techniques employed for protein analysis include colorimetric methods, LC-MS, sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), western blotting, and flow cytometry [ ]. Flow cytometry analysis of garlic-derived PDVLN revealed that it contains CD9, CD63 and HSP70 components [ ]. Xiao et al. [ ] employed LC-MS and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis to identify various protein components in PDVLNs derived from edible camellia. Notable proteins identified include actin, Acyl-CoA-binding protein, aquaporin, beta-actin and caffeine synthase. LC-MS/MS analysis of PDVLNs derived from Dendropanax morbifera revealed the presence of peroxidase, peroxidase 4, peroxidase P7-like, glucan endo-1,3-beta-glucosidase and other protein components [ ]. Passion fruit-derived PDVLNs were found to contain protein components such as CD9, CD81, CD47 and CD63 [ ]. Carvajal et al. [ ] employed SDS-PAGE and LC-MS analysis to identify a range of protein components in broccoli-derived PDVLNs, including aquaporins, plasma membrane-type Ca ATPase, nitrate transporter and Ca-transporting ATPase 2. Moreover, Zhang et al. [ ] identified various protein components in lycium ruthenicum murray-derived PDVLNs through SDS-PAGE and LC-MS analysis, including enolase and 11S globulin-like proteins. Similarly, Vestuto et al. [ ] reported a diverse range of protein components in salvia hairy root-derived PDVLNs using proteomic profiling analysis, which included ATP synthase subunits, nucleic acid binding proteins, ribosomal constituents, and cytoskeletal elements.

2.2.3.

## miRNA
One of the most intriguing findings regarding PDVLNs is their capacity to facilitate the transfer of various miRNA species between cells. These miRNAs are generally small RNA molecules (sRNAs) that are <30 nucleotides in length and exhibit significantly less diversity compared to animal-derived EVs. Growing evidence indicates that miRNAs within PDVLNs possess a strong binding affinity for animal miRNAs, potentially influencing the regulation of host cell growth, metabolism, and disease processes [ , ].
To elucidate the potential role of miRNAs carried by PDVLNs in the treatment of IDs, Juan et al. employed bioinformatics to investigate the relationship between miRNAs and their human target genes. The results of target gene prediction suggest that specific miRNAs within PDVLNs may facilitate interspecies cell communication and theoretically regulate mammalian genes [ ]. The nucleotide sequence of PDVLNs derived from ginger was analyzed using the Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST), which identified two miRNAs: aly-miR396a-5p and rlcv-miR-rL1–28–3p [ ]. Subsequent RNA sequencing analysis revealed the presence of mdo-miRNA7267–3p, gma-miR396e and ath-miR167a in ginger-derived PDVLNs [ ]. Fratantonio's group utilized the High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit and conducted qRT-PCR analysis, demonstrating that blueberry-derived PDVLNs contain miR-156e, miR-162 and miR-319d [ ]. Duan's group utilized the RNA sequencing analysis demonstrate that konjac-derived PDVLNs contain miR408, miR166e, miR156d and miR169a [ ].

2.2.4.

## Metabolite
Plants are recognized as abundant sources of a wide array of metabolites with significant pharmacological properties. Besides these substances mentioned above, PDVLNs also encompass various secondary metabolites that vary according to the plant species from which they are extracted [ ]. This variation in composition represents the most notable distinction between PDVLNs and mammalian-derived EVs. These secondary metabolites include terpenes, phenols and alkaloids. Although plant metabolites are primarily thought to function as defense mechanisms against insects and herbivores, as well as growth factors, their potential as natural resources for disease treatment has been extensively explored. For instance, PDVLNs derived from broccoli have been found to contain sulforaphane [ ]. Ginger-derived PDVLNs were isolated through differential centrifugation and sucrose gradient purification. The resulting sample was subsequently analyzed using thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on a 60 Å silica gel plate, which confirmed the presence of shogaol [ ]. Further investigation discovered that ginger-derived PDVLNs also contained 6-gingerol and 6-balsam phenol. Stanly et al. [ ] isolated metabolites from grapefruit-derived PDVLNs using centrifugation and phase separation techniques, identifying myo-inositol and quinic acid as the metabolites through gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC–MS) analysis. Valentino et al. [ ] isolated PDVLNs from cactus fruits, and the metabolites analyzed by LC-MS included kaempferol-3-O-pentose (89.4%), chlorogenic acid (78.4%), arbutin (78.9%), syringic acid (72.5%) and apigenin (69.1%). Furthermore, Barlian's team demonstrated through GC–MS analysis that solanum nigrum-derived PDVLNs contains neral, a monoterpene compound with known anti-inflammatory properties [ ]. Additionally, Kim et al. reported that, based on Q-TOF-MS analysis, the metabolites isolated from ramie-derived PDVLNs include ethyl caffeate, methyl-l-enolate, oleamide, erucamide and stearamide [ ].

## Role of PDVLNs in IDs
3.1.

## Pathogenesis of IDs
Inflammation is not merely a simple disease; rather, it constitutes a fundamental immune response that is essential for maintaining balance and survival within the body. Furthermore, it is intricately linked to a variety of human diseases. Inflammation is typically triggered by pathogens or damaged cells, which results in the release of inflammatory signals. These signals stimulate resident immune cells to produce reactive oxygen species, chemokines, cytokines and other signaling molecules. Eventually, circulating immune cells are recruited to the site of inflammation to repair and protect the host from invasion. Currently, extensive research has been conducted to explore and validate the intricate pathological mechanisms underlying inflammation. This review aims to categorize the pathogenesis of inflammation into three distinct stages, as shown in .
 (1) The induction of an inflammatory response can be attributed to various factors, including tissue damage, pathogen infection, alien infestation and impairment of regulatory functions. Notably, tissue damage is recognized as a significant trigger for inflammation, as it creates opportunities for pathogen invasion [ ]. Upon detecting tissue damage, the body promptly activates pattern recognition receptors, including damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), to initiate the inflammatory response [ ]. Furthermore, exposure to environmental toxins or viruses can lead to alien infestations, resulting in temporary or permanent loss of function in respiratory or digestive tissues. This can trigger stress-induced anti-inflammatory and physiological behavioral defense mechanisms that facilitate adaptation to xenobiotics [ ]. Impaired regulatory function, known as physiological inflammation, is typically caused by external factors like cold, hunger or panic, as well as internal diseases such as obesity, aging or cancer [ , ].
(2) Sensors and mediators: Various inducers from different IDs activate signaling receptors, primarily in white blood cells and cytokines, leading to the release of specific inflammatory signals. Neutrophils, the first group of circulating white blood cells to infiltrate infected tissue, are essential for pathogen clearance and the resolution of inflammation during the early stages of infection [ ]. In addition, other types of white blood cells, including macrophages, eosinophils and lymphocytes, are progressively recruited to the site of inflammation. Inflammatory sensor cells detect the presence of inflammation through various mediators such as cytokines, chemokines, inflammation-related serum proteins (including complement and C-reactive protein), and cell receptors that respond to defense signals, such as Toll-like receptors, which are released by infected tissues. These cells then interact with the surface proteins of inflammatory host cells, triggering various mechanisms to inhibit the spread of inflammatory infection [ ].
(3) Effector action: Inflammatory signals primarily target effector cells responsible for the eradication of inflammatory triggers, as well as organs or tissues that may not be directly engaged in pathogen elimination. These signals promote the activation, recruitment, and differentiation of effector cells. In contrast, effector cells typically modulate inflammation via negative feedback loops, which include the correction of systemic variables such as pathogen clearance. Furthermore, surrounding tissues and organs are generally unaffected by dysfunction in the targeted inflammatory tissue and receive inflammatory signals in a regulated manner. In other words, inflammatory signals consistently contribute to maintaining tissue homeostasis across different tissues [ ].

3.2.

## Types of IDs and their impact on disease
Inflammation is a sustained immune response that encompasses the body's inherent mechanisms for repairing damaged tissues and safeguarding host cells from injury [ ]. At this stage, the disease progresses into chronic inflammation, which poses the risk of immune tolerance and life-threatening complications. The long-term interplay between the inflammatory response and host immune imbalance frequently results in organ degeneration, malignant hyperplasia, or a systemic imbalance in immune-inflammatory responses, thereby facilitating the onset of various diseases [ ]. In general terms, an ID is characterized by an abnormal and exaggerated immune response that leads to pathological processes, either locally or systemically, in tissues and organs ( ) [ ].
 3.2.1.

## Acute inflammation and its related diseases
Acute inflammation can be induced through various mechanisms, including skin or tissue puncture, oral ingestion, and inhalation [ ]. Exogenous mechanical injuries, such as skin or tissue punctures, can lead to direct damage to the skin or result in ischemia-reperfusion injury of the affected tissues. Besides, chemicals or pathogens ingested orally or inhaled from the environment can directly trigger respiratory infections and lead to significant systemic damage. During this process, the body's pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) rely on the DAMPs and PAMPs pathway to recognize pathogens and initiate the activation of receptors and inflammatory mediators [ ]. Within the infected tissue, resident innate immune cells, including neutrophils, dendritic cells, and macrophages, function as inflammatory receptors. Upon receiving DAMPs or PAMPs released by pathogens, these cells can activate capillary venous endothelial cells, which subsequently up-regulate the release of white blood cells [ ]. This cascade process involves the signaling of various integrins on circulating leukocytes, ultimately leading to their exosmosis. Additionally, activated dendritic cells and macrophages function as antigen-presenting cells, effectively phagocytizing foreign bodies and releasing a variety of inflammatory mediators, such as inflammatory cytokines or chemokines. These inflammatory mediators play a crucial role in recruiting more immune cells to eliminate excessive inflammation and restore balance [ ].
Although non-pathogenic induction signals differ from those of invading pathogens, they engage receptors and inflammatory signals that are similar to those involved in pathogenic inflammatory processes. Even in the absence of pathogens, resident immune cells and endothelial cells within the inflammatory environment utilize comparable anti-inflammatory strategies. They release chemokines and inflammatory factors to recruit neutrophils, thereby initiating different anti-inflammatory processes. These processes aim to inhibit and balance the inflammatory response in the body [ ]. During the anti-inflammatory process, remaining neutrophils and necrotic cells are typically rapidly engulfed by monocytes. This feedback loop stimulates neutrophils to produce excessive neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) and ROS which further damage surrounding host cells and cause acute tissue or organ damage [ ]. This imbalance subsequently results in a series of clinical manifestations characterized by inflammation, as seen in conditions such as acute coronavirus infection (COVID-19) and excessive organ damage, exemplified by acute alcoholic liver injury [ ]. Among them, COVID-19 is a typical acute ID, characterized by rapid infection, sudden onset, and strong pathogenicity.

3.2.2.

## Chronic inflammation and its related diseases
In instances where long-term invading pathogens or insults persist, host cells and neutrophils continue to release inflammatory cytokines, a phenomenon commonly referred to as a cytokine storm. The acute inflammatory response can evolve into a chronic (pathological) phase if it is excessive or unable to effectively suppress inflammatory stimuli. This chronic phase is marked by a delayed onset of inflammation. While chronic inflammation is predominantly linked to the cells of the adaptive immune system, the innate immune system also contributes to this process. In chronic IDs, recurrent episodes of acute inflammation lead to cumulative tissue damage [ ].
While both chronic and acute inflammation share certain common mechanisms, their pathogenesis exhibits significant differences. Chronic inflammation is not triggered by a singular stimulus; instead, it arises from a combination of factors, including cellular stress or damage resulting from prolonged acute inflammation, as well as the body's physiological changes associated with conditions such as disease, obesity, and aging. In contrast to acute inflammation, which is triggered by both DAMPs and PAMPs, chronic inflammation is primarily elicited by DAMPs. During the effector response of chronic inflammation, immune cells continuously initiate the innate immune response, albeit to a lesser extent. Furthermore, fibroblasts, a specialized type of effector cell, play a crucial role in chronic inflammation. Activated fibroblasts secrete chemokines and recruit leukocytes to sites of inflammation [ , ]. They also proliferate at sites of injury and inflammation, producing collagen that induces fibrosis [ ]. Autoimmune diseases arise when the body gradually loses its resistance to adaptive immune responses. In particular, prolonged oxidative stress can lead to inflammatory tissue regeneration [ , ].
Chronic inflammation serves as both a cause and a marker for numerous diseases, playing a particularly significant role in cancer. It is closely related to tumor development, the transformation of malignant phenotypes and the effectiveness of various cancer chemotherapy drugs [ ]. Specifically, chronic inflammation Specifically, chronic inflammation creates a conducive environment for tumor development and metastasis through the induction of immune suppression. The carcinogenic microenvironment this induces can be traced to chronic tissue damage and ongoing repair processes designed to respond to the release of different cytokines and growth factors. Concurrently, the tumor microenvironment is altered to establish a tumor-permissive state, which facilitates tumor promotion by delaying the anti-tumor immune response and providing direct signals to both tumor and epithelial cells , .

3.3.

## PDVLNs themselves acting as therapeutic agents for inflammation treatment
Understanding the pathogenesis of inflammation is essential for the development of effective anti-inflammatory strategies. In recent years, PDVLNs sourced from various origins have been selected according to the specific characteristics associated with each stage of inflammation. These PDVLNs offer the advantages of high efficacy and minimal side effects, positioning them as a promising approach for the treatment of IDs. In this review, we categorize existing PDVLNs into three distinct categories based on their anti-inflammatory mechanisms ( ).
 3.3.1.

## Scavenging strategies
3.3.1.1.

## ROS scavenger
Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are signaling molecules generated by organisms during cellular metabolism or in response to environmental stimuli. During inflammation, cells associated with host defense frequently produce elevated levels of ROS. These increased concentrations of ROS can disrupt cellular function by non-specifically targeting DNA, proteins, and lipids [ ]. To prevent inflammation-related diseases, it is crucial to regulate ROS levels through antioxidant strategies, which can help maintain intracellular redox homeostasis [ , ]. Grapefruit-derived PDVLNs have shown promising effects on wound healing. The results indicate that these PDVLNs can reduce ROS production and exhibit potential as a biotechnological agent for wound healing [ ]. As illustrated in , Xiao's team devised an innovative strategy to selectively induce apoptosis in breast tumors through the application of PDVLNs derived from tea flowers. These PDVLNs demonstrated substantial cytotoxic effects on cancer cells, which can be attributed to the enhancement of ROS amplification. This study offers valuable insights into the environmentally friendly production of natural PDVLN nanoplatforms for the inhibition of breast cancer [ ]. Furthermore, PDVLNs derived from blueberries have demonstrated the ability to counteract TNF-α-induced ROS generation and to regulate the expression of genes involved in the inflammatory response, cytokine release, and oxidative stress. Consequently, PDVLNs may be regarded as potential therapeutic carriers for bioactive compounds [ ]. Research on PDVLNs derived from fresh Panax notoginseng root have shown that these PDVLNs are capable of reducing inflammatory factor levels and alleviating cerebral ischemic damage by modulating the phenotype of microglia [ ]. Peng's team successfully isolated PDVLNs derived from B. javanica, demonstrating their potential utility in cancer treatment through the regulation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway and the promotion of ROS/caspase-mediated apoptosis [ ]. In a separate study, Martínez Fajardo et al. [ ] isolated PDVLNs from Arbutus unedo, revealing that these PDVLNs contain carotenoids known for their anti-inflammatory properties and ability to clear ROS. This finding suggested a potential role for PDVLNs derived from Arbutus unedo in contributing to anti-inflammatory activity.
 In inflammatory lesions, the levels of key antioxidant enzymes, including catalase (CAT), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase, are relatively low, resulting in diminished clearance capabilities for ROS [ , ]. Currently, antioxidant enzymes and naturally occurring small molecules, such as vitamins C and E, are commonly used as enzyme mimics to eliminate excessive ROS, thereby reducing cellular oxidative damage and the progression of IDs. However, these natural antioxidants present several limitations, including their susceptibility to harsh conditions (e.g., acidic pH or redox environments), instability in antioxidant capacity, suboptimal pharmacokinetics, nonspecific tissue accumulation, and potential adverse effects at elevated doses. Merlin's team discovered that 6-gingerol, present in ginger-derived PDVLNs, exhibited significant antioxidant activity by effectively clearing ROS. Additionally, it reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β, while simultaneously increasing the levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines IL-10 and IL-22 in colitis models. These findings suggested that 6-gingerol may be beneficial for the prevention and treatment of colitis [ ]. One study indicated that PDVLNs derived from aloe vera peels can enhance antioxidant activity by increasing the activities of Cat and SOD. These findings suggested that aloe vera peels-derived PDVLNs hold significant promise as antioxidants for the treatment of diseases associated with oxidative stress [ ]. Yu's team utilized PDVLNs derived from fresh Rehmanniae Radix, resulting in a reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α), ROS and NO Furthermore, these findings indicated that PDVLNs can alleviate LPS-induced lung inflammation, positioning them as novel and effective therapeutic agents for the prevention of lung inflammation [ ]. Notably, PDVLNs derived from Dendropanax morbifera (DM) demonstrated therapeutic effects on breast cancer. DM-derived PDVLNs exhibit peroxidase-like activity that inhibits tumor formation and prevents cancer by eliminating excess ROS. Therefore, the use of naturally occurring PDVLNs presents a novel approach for integrated cancer therapy in conjunction with chemotherapy [ ]. Similarly, Liu's team investigated the antioxidant and anti-apoptotic properties of blueberry-derived PDVLNs in the treatment of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). Blueberry-derived PDVLNs significantly increased the activity of SOD) and GSH, while also enhancing the expression of Bcl-2 and HO-1 proteins. These findings suggest that blueberry-derived PDVLNs may serve as a novel antioxidant for the treatment of NAFLD [ ].

3.3.1.2.

## Immunogen scavenger activates and secretes inflammatory cytokines
In inflamed tissues, dying cells release high concentrations of immunogens, including cell-free DNA (cfDNA), which can activate TLRs on the surface of neutrophils and macrophages. This activation promotes the secretion of inflammatory cytokines [ ]. Additionally, antibodies targeting cfDNA can form immune complexes with cfDNA, thereby promoting the proliferation of B cells and the secretion of additional inflammatory factors [ ]. Consequently, the elimination of concentrated immunogens emerges as a critical strategy for suppressing both innate and adaptive immunity [ ]. As shown in a and b, Wang's team successfully isolated PDVLNs from the fresh roots of Panax notoginseng. Their findings indicate that PDVLNs reduce the levels of inflammatory factors and mitigate cerebral ischemic injury by modifying the phenotype of microglia [ ]. Ginseng-derived PDVLNs have been shown to enhance wound healing. The results indicate that PDVLNs sourced from ginseng can modulate the immune response by promoting the polarization of M2 macrophages, which subsequently facilitates wound healing [ ]. Similarly, Barlian's team discovered the anti-inflammatory potential of goldenberry-derived PDVLNs in the context of macrophage polarization [ ]. Furthermore, Li's team successfully isolated and identified portulaca oleracea-derived PDVLNs for the treatment of ulcerative colitis. Research indicated that the oral administration of these PDVLNs effectively suppressed the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12 and IL-1β [ ]. Catharanthus roseus-derived PDVLNs were utilized to enhance the polarization and phagocytic activity of macrophages, as well as to stimulate lymphocyte proliferation. The findings indicated that PDVLNs represent a nanometer-scale biomaterial characterized by exceptional stability and biocompatibility, making them suitable for use as immunoadjuvants in post-chemotherapy therapy [ ]. Li's team utilized miRNA (PU-MIR2916-P3) in garly-derived PDVLNs to inhibit the production of serum pro-inflammatory cytokines, specifically TNF-α and IL-6, in mice with colitis [ ]. Meanwhile, Barlian's team demonstrated that papaya-derived PDVLNs significantly reduced NO production in cells, down-regulated the mRNA expression of pro-inflammatory cytokine genes (IL-1B and IL-6), and up-regulated the mRNA expression of anti-inflammatory cytokine genes (IL-10). These findings indicate the potential of papaya-derived PDVLNs as an effective anti-inflammatory agent [ ].
 Nucleic acid-binding polymers have demonstrated promise as cfDNA scavengers in the context of anti-inflammatory therapies. For instance, polycationic polymers have been found to mitigate the activation of all nucleic acid-sensitive TLRs [ ]. However, the clinical applications of nucleic acid-binding polymers are constrained by poor biodistribution and r rapid metabolism. On the other hand, acid-conjugated nanoparticles and nanofibers exhibit higher scavenging capacity and better biodistribution [ , , , , , ]. For instance, cationic micelles composed of poly(lactic-glycolic acid) and poly(2-(diethylamine)ethyl methacrylate) have been explored for their anti-inflammatory properties in animal models of arthritis. Administration of these micelles via intravenous injection has demonstrated a reduction in bone and cartilage damage, as well as a decrease in swelling of the ankle joint and surrounding tissues. Notably, these micelles exhibited a significantly enhanced therapeutic effect compared to the control group [ ]. As illustrated in C, garlic-derived PDVLNs inhibit the expression of TLR4, resulting in a decreased secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines. These PDVLNs also offer protection to the colon against DSS-induced damage by targeting the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway. This research presents a novel perspective for the development of value-added garlic ophthalmic products aimed at preventing inflammatory bowel disease [ ].

3.3.2.

## Blockage strategies
3.3.2.1.

## Regulating leukocyte-endothelial cell interactions
The recruitment of activated leukocytes to sites of inflammation constitutes an innate immune response that involves their adhesion to endothelial cells. This process is facilitated by calcium-related transmembrane glycoproteins known as selectins. Specifically, P-selectin is expressed in platelets and endothelial cells, whereas l-selectin is expressed by leukocytes [ ]. The effective blockade of selectins presents a potential therapeutic strategy to inhibit leukocyte recruitment and mitigate inflammation. Zhang's team successfully isolated PDVLNs from ginger for the treatment of periodontitis. The results indicated that ginger-derived PDVLNs significantly reduced the recruitment of macrophages, leukocytes, and CD3 cells to the oral tissue microenvironment. This finding demonstrates the potential of PDVLNs as a therapeutic agent for the prevention and treatment of chronic periodontitis [ ]. Broccoli-derived PDVLNs enhance AMPK signaling and may promote the induction of AMPK-activated anti-inflammatory factors in dendritic cells. This research establishes a foundation for the development of edible nanoparticle profiles and facilitates their classification based on their immune regulatory roles [ ]. The lectins derived from garlic-based PDVLNs interact with CD98 on the cell membrane, facilitating cell internalization and enhancing anti-inflammatory effects. This study contributes to the understanding of interspecies communication and offers a novel perspective on the benefits of dietary garlic [ ]. Pocsfalvi et al. found that grapefruit-derived PDVLNs reduced the expression of the cell adhesion molecule ICAM-1, potentially inhibiting the excessive adhesion of melanoma cells. These findings indicate that vesicles derived from grapefruits may diminish the aggressiveness of melanoma, presenting significant potential for the control of the metastatic capabilities in cancer treatment [ ].
Heparin demonstrates anti-inflammatory properties by inhibiting P-selectin and l-selectin. However, its application as an anti-inflammatory agent is constrained by the potential for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia and the risk of uncontrolled bleeding. Recent studies have indicated that non-anticoagulant heparinoid molecules effectively reduce inflammation by acting as selectin blockers [ ]. Wang's team demonstrated that garlic-derived PDVLNs enhance the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy. Their research revealed that these PDVLNs significantly improve the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade therapy in solid tumors by effectively activating intestinal interferon-γ (IFNγ)-producing γδ T cells and promoting the expansion of endogenous γδ T cells in vivo [ ].

3.3.2.2.

## Blockage of toll-like receptors
Blocking TLRs on the surfaces of neutrophils and macrophages can significantly diminish inflammatory responses [ ]. Studies have reported that ginseng-derived PDVLNs can activate the TLR4 signaling pathway and promote macrophage polarization. As an immunomodulator, it participates in the mammalian immune response and may represent a new class of nanodrugs in cancer immunotherapy [ ]. Zhang's team utilized ginger-derived PDVLNs to protect against alcohol-induced liver damage. The results indicated that shogaol within the ginger-derived PDVLNs plays a role in the induction of Nrf2 in a TLR4-dependent manner. This finding opens new avenues for investigating ginger-derived PDVLNs as a potential means of protecting against the development of liver-related diseases [ ]. Aditionally, Yepes-Molina et al. used sulforaphane (SFN) from broccoli derived PDVLNs to regulate the secretion of inflammatory factors by inhibiting TLR4-mediated cell activation. The results indicated a reduction in interleukins that are critical for the development of inflammation, including TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6 [ ].

3.3.3.

## Regulation of metal ions
3.3.3.1.

## Iron ion
Iron (Fe) is essential for numerous physiological processes within the body [ ]. However, imbalances in Fe levels can impair the function of various cellular proteins, such as transferrin (TF), divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) and ferroportin (Fpn). Additionally, these imbalances can disrupt several signaling pathways linked to oxidative damage [ , ]. One such pathway is ferroptosis, a form of programmed cell death that depends on Fe ions and is characterized by phospholipid peroxidation. This process plays a crucial role in regulating various cellular redox homeostasis pathways. Consequently, researchers have explored the development of Fe chelators that target multiple sites to inhibit lipid peroxidation and the subsequent inflammatory cascade. Wang's research group demonstrated that the traditional Chinese medicine extract Biochanin A can directly reduce intracellular Fe concentration. This effect is achieved through the inhibition of TfR1 and the promotion of Fpn. Additionally, Biochanin A possesses the capability to scavenge free radicals and prevent lipid peroxidation by modulating the Nrf2/system Xc-/GPX4 signaling pathway. This study elucidates the role of Biochanin A in regulating iron ions in osteoarthritis, thereby paving the way for its potential therapeutic applications. Sheng's team demonstrated that PDVLNs derived from Robinia pseudoacacia L. significantly ameliorated hypoxia-induced mucosal injury in the gastric and small intestine of mice. This effect was achieved through the downregulation of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) and HIF-2α expression, as well as the inhibition of hypoxia-mediated ferroptosis. These findings suggest a promising therapeutic approach for addressing hypoxia-induced gastric and small intestinal mucosal ferroptosis [ ].

3.3.3.2.

## Calcium ion
Calcium (Ca) is an essential metal ion in the human body, playing a critical role in the regulation of various cellular processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, metabolism, and apoptosis [ ]. However, imbalances in intracellular Ca levels can trigger a cascade of reactions that lead to energy deficiency and excessive production of ROS, ultimately resulting in cell death. For example, during hypoxia, the Na-Ca exchanger on the cell membrane is reversely activated, causing a significant influx of Ca into the cell [ ]. Simultaneously, hypoxia induces the production of a substantial amount of ROS, which inflicts damage on both the cell membrane and the endoplasmic reticulum membrane, thereby promoting the influx of Ca into the cytoplasm [ ]. The excessive accumulation of Ca in mitochondria results in the overopening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore and the subsequent collapse of the mitochondrial membrane potential. This disruption inhibits ATP synthesis, enhances the production of ROS, and facilitates the release of cytochrome C. These events ultimately initiate the caspase cascade, leading to apoptosis. Cho's team utilized yam-derived PDVLNs to stimulate osteoblast formation and prevent osteoporosis in mice. The results indicated that these yam-derived PDVLNs promote cell proliferation and mineralization by promoting calcium deposition in osteoblasts [ ]. Similarly, Li's team employed card-derived PDVLNs to promote differentiation and mineralization of primary human bone mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSC) in vitro, as well as in ovariectomy (OVX)-induced osteoporosis rats [ ].

3.4.

## PDVLNs serving as a drug delivery system for therapeutic purposes
Natural PDVLNs not only demonstrate therapeutic potential for IDs, but they also function as crosstalk messengers and natural active nanocarriers in the context of nanoparticles. These nanocarriers are capable of encapsulating functional therapeutic molecules, such as small molecule drugs or nucleic acids, and delivering them to inflammatory cells [ , , ]. Consequently, PDVLNs serve as excellent drug nanocarriers for the treatment of IDs. The use of PDVLNs as drug nanocarriers offers several notable advantages, particularly in terms of biocompatibility and reduced immunogenicity when compared to other synthetic alternatives, such as liposomes, micelles, and inorganic nanoparticles [ ]. A significant illustration of this is a study conducted by Zhang's group, which demonstrated that grapefruit-derived PDVLNs are highly effective nanocarriers for therapeutic drug delivery [ ]. Additionally, PDVLNs possess considerable potential as candidates for innovative oral delivery systems, owing to their resilience in the highly acidic gastric environment and their stability against active proteolytic enzymes in the intestine [ ].
3.4.1.

## Delivering small molecule drugs
Currently, PDVLNs are employed for drug loading and delivery using two primary methods: passive loading, which involves incubation, and active loading, which utilizes techniques such as ultrasound or electroporation [ ]. Passive loading technologies rely on the diffusion interactions between drug molecules and the lipid bilayer of PDVLNs. Wang et al. developed an oral delivery system by incubating the anti-inflammatory drug methotrexate (Methotrexate) with grapefruit-derived PDVLNs. This study demonstrated that MTX exhibited significantly reduced toxicity while effectively maintaining its therapeutic function, resulting in precise uptake by intestinal macrophages. These findings conceptually support the notion that grapefruit-derived PDVLNs serve as intestinal immunomodulators that help maintain intestinal macrophage homeostasis. Moreover, this approach can be further optimized for the oral delivery of small molecule drugs intended to mitigate inflammatory responses in human diseases [ ].
In addition to passive loading, active loading technologies, such as ultrasound and electroporation, can transiently modify the membrane structure of PDVLNs, thereby enhancing the successful integration of drugs. Evidence suggests that this approach increases the drug loading capacity by a factor of 11 compared to passive loading techniques [ ]. Zeng et al. [ ] proposed an optimized method for isolating PDVLNs from aloe vera, as illustrated in . The PDVLNs demonstrate appropriate size, excellent stability, and high safety in both in vitro and in vivo contexts. These attributes render them a viable option for loading indocyanine green (ICG) for melanoma treatment, thereby laying the groundwork for the development of cost-effective and safe ICG drug delivery systems. Furthermore, the PDVLNs exhibited promising transdermal properties, indicating their potential for non-invasive transdermal drug delivery and skin cancer treatment. Chen et al. [ ] utilized ultrasonic technology to encapsulate the anticancer drug 5-FU within bitter gourd-derived PDVLNs. This active loading strategy not only enhanced the anticancer efficacy of 5-FU but also reduced its resistance in oral squamous cell carcinoma. Additionally, the therapeutic BMEVs not only contained the 5-FU drug but also demonstrated a significant downregulation of NLRP3, an inflammasome sensor protein. This downregulation may play a role in the mechanism by which drug resistance is diminished, ultimately promoting a synergistic and effective treatment. Keck's group investigated the transdermal effects of cucumber-derived PDVLNs for the delivery of lipophilic active ingredients. The researchers compared the transdermal effectiveness of PDVLNs produced through high-pressure homogenization (HPH) with those obtained via the traditional ultra-high-speed centrifugation method. The results demonstrated that PDVLNs derived from HPH exhibited twice the transdermal efficacy for lipophilic active ingredients and effectively penetrated the dermal layer. Thus, HPH emerges as a promising technology for the production of PDVLNs and the enhancement of drug delivery [ ]. In a separate study, Sharma et al. [ ] utilized a rapid freeze-thaw method to incorporate ginkgo and berberine into avocado-derived PDVLNs. Their findings indicated that these PDVLNs were rapidly internalized by peritoneal macrophages in a time- and dose-dependent manner. This study suggests that natural PDVLNs could serve as effective nanocarriers for poorly soluble nutraceuticals, potentially aiding in the prevention of inflammation and the formation of macrophage foam cells. Saura's team employed ultrasound technology to incorporate heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) into PDVLNs derived from grapefruit juice. Their findings indicated that these grapefruit-derived PDVLNs exhibited the greatest capacity for loading the exogenous protein HSP70, as well as the highest potential to function as drug delivery vehicles specifically targeting glioma cells. This underscores the promising role of PDVLNs as functional ingredients in therapeutic applications [ ]. Wang et al. [ ] also utilized ultrasound to isolate isoliquiritigenin (ISL) from PDVLNs derived from Lycium barbarum. Their research demonstrated that PDVLNs derived from Lycium barbarum enhanced the solubilization of ISL and improved its anti-inflammatory and neuronal differentiation properties.
 
3.4.2.

## Delivering nucleic acid drugs
The development of effective nanocarriers for gene delivery continues to pose a significant challenge in the field of gene therapy. Currently utilized viral and non-viral carriers present risks associated with immunogenicity and toxicity [ ]. In recent years, PDVLNs-based gene nanocarriers have emerged as a promising solution for targeted delivery and therapeutic effects, owing to their stability under various environmental conditions [ , ]. One extensively studied application of PDVLNs-based gene nanocarriers is the delivery of therapeutic RNA for a range of diseases. For example, research conducted by Zhang's team involved the cultivation of exogenous miRNA (miR17) within grapefruit-derived PDVLNs to enhance its ability to penetrate the BBB. Both in vitro and in vivo studies have confirmed the efficient delivery of miR17 into brain tumors via intranasal administration of grapefruit-derived PDVLNs. Furthermore, it was demonstrated that miR17 can inhibit the growth of brain tumors by down-regulating the expression of histocompatibility complex class I (MHC class I) in natural killer cells [ ]. These findings suggest that PDVLNs-based miRNA nanocarriers hold potential as oral therapeutic agents. Umezu et al. investigated the efficacy of PDVLNs derived from acerola in encapsulating miRNA to suppress target genes associated with this miRNA. Their findings revealed that one day post-oral administration of these PDVLNs, the inhibition of target genes in the small intestine and liver of mice peaked. This suggests that acerola-derived PDVLNs have the potential to serve as oral therapeutic agents for the treatment of digestive diseases [ ]. This discovery has advanced the field of oral delivery of siRNA. In a separate study, ginger-derived PDVLNs were co-incubated with siRNA targeting CD98, demonstrating transfection efficiency comparable to that of liposomes in both colon-26 and RAW 264.7 cell lines. By orally administering siRNA-CD98 encapsulated in ginger-derived PDVLNs, the researchers successfully downregulated CD98 expression in the colon [ ]. Similarly, Li et al. [ ] showed effective silencing of survivin gene expression in subcutaneous tumors through the intravenous delivery of siRNA using ginger-derived PDVLNs. Additionally, Yu's group employed green tea-derived PDVLNs as delivery vehicles to load an antagomir targeting heart-apoptosis-associated piRNA via electroporation. This study illustrates effective regulation of vascular remodeling, thereby reducing the occurrence and progression of Alzheimer's disease [ ].

4.

## The therapeutic effect of engineered PDVLNs in IDs
Although natural PDVLNs exhibit therapeutic potential for IDs, fully realizing their benefits remains challenging due to their low content of bioactive molecules and the lack of precise targeting to inflammatory sites. Consequently, there is an urgent need to design PDVLNs that facilitate targeted treatment of IDs. Active targeting is essential for achieving this precision. In recent years, researchers have concentrated on developing "customized'' targeting strategies to identify various types of inflammatory cells associated with different IDs. These strategies primarily involve membrane engineering and artificial bionics, as illustrated in . This section critically examines recent research on engineered PDVLNs, emphasizing their advantages and limitations.
 4.1.

## Membrane engineering
With the exception of a few PDVLNs that exhibit limited homing capabilities to the gut, most native PDVLNs demonstrate suboptimal targeting ability. Unfortunately, native PDVLNs may not adequately address these treatment requirements. Consequently, the advent of synthetic biology technology has significantly improved the targeting of inflammation and enhanced the therapeutic efficacy of PDVLNs. By engineering PDVLNs with specific functions tailored to the various biological molecules associated with the pathogenesis of different IDs, their functionality can be more precisely directed, and their applications can be more distinctly defined.
4.1.1.

## Physical modification
4.1.1.1.

## Physical mixture
The incorporation of PDVLNs into biocompatible 3D scaffolds through a straightforward and rapid physical mixing technique has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy in the fields of tissue engineering and regenerative medicine. This method offers significant potential for enhancing the retention time and facilitating the sustained release of PDVLNs at the target site of disease. For example, the scaffold functionalized with medicinal plant ginseng-derived PDVLNs was used in the field of nerve regenerative medicine, as shown in A and B Utilizing light-induced hydrogels, a photosensitive gelatin methacryloyl prepolymer (GelMA) was synthesized for in-situ gel formation, leveraging its biocompatibility and adaptability to accommodate damage defects. GelMA enabled sustained infiltration and neural differentiation of PDVLNs within the nerve injury site. Notably, the miRNAs derived from PDVLNs were capable of being transferred to mammalian stem cells, which subsequently upregulated the phosphatidylinositol 3 Kinase (PI3K) signaling pathway, ultimately enhancing the expression of neurotrophic factors [ ]. Another study investigated the incorporation of sophora japonica-derived PDVLNs into a polydopamine modified hyaluronic acid hydrogel (pDA-Gel) for the treatment of spinal cord injury. With the sustained release of PDVLNs at the injury site, this strategy can rapidly improve impaired motor function and relieve urinary dysfunction by regulating spinal cord inflammation and oxidative damage [ ]. Cheng's group developed a multifunctional fusion vesicle for the treatment of autoimmune skin diseases. As shown in C and D, they achieved this by combining grapefruit-derived PDVLNs, which were loaded with the immunosuppressant (CX5461), with CCR6+ nanovesicles derived from the membranes of engineered gingiva-derived mesenchymal stem cells (GMSCs). This fusion vesicle not only preserved the biological activity of PDVLNs and GMSCs, but also possesses the ability to target inflammatory sites. Experimental results demonstrated that this multifunctional fusion vesicle reduced the secretion of inflammatory factors, suppressed the activation of Th17 cells, promoted the infiltration of Treg cells, reshaped the immune microenvironment, and serves as a versatile nanocarrier platform for targeted immunotherapy of autoimmune diseases [ ]. Qiao's research team discovered that dandelion-derived PDVLNs were incorporated into GelMA hydrogel for the treatment of wounds infected with Staphylococcus aureus exotoxin. The findings revealed that the released PDVLNs specifically neutralize Staphylococcus aureus exotoxin, thereby protecting host cells from damage. Additionally, the three-dimensional structure of the hydrogel promotes cell proliferation and migration, and when combined with PDVLNs, it significantly accelerates the healing of infected wounds [ ].
 Stent-based delivery systems have the potential to achieve targeted mass delivery of PDVLNs, providing a new approach in the fields of regenerative medicine and tissue engineering. Nevertheless, further investigation is required into the existing physical mixtures of PDVLNs to address the limitations inherent in natural PDVLNs. This research is essential for a thorough enhancement of the pathological manifestations linked to various diseases.

4.1.1.2.

## Membrane hybridization
Membrane hybridization represents an effective approach for engineering PDVLNs, enabling the safe and efficient utilization of their biological functions. This technique allows for precise targeting and detection of other phospholipid nanomicelles. For instance, the hybridization of grapefruit-derived PDVLNs with membranes enriched with inflammation-related activated leukocyte receptors positively influences cancer treatment ( ). This membrane hybridization technology not only preserves the benefits of grapefruit-derived PDVLNs in the efficient delivery of multiple therapeutic agents but also enhances the ability of the leukocyte receptor-enriched membrane to target inflammatory tumor tissues [ ]. Thylakoid (TK), a unique type of PDVLNs, is extracted from plant chloroplasts and contains native CAT and photosystem II, a protein complex responsible for oxidizing water during photophosphorylation [ , ]. Building on the success of the biofilm hybridization strategy, TK has been combined with the membranes of mammalian cells, such as natural killer cells and macrophage cells [ , , ]. This hybridization approach leverages TK's plant-based functions, such as ROS scavenging, alongside the dual targeting capabilities of animal nanovesicles. Consequently, this strategy has demonstrated potential in various IDs [ , ]. Recently, Chen's group developed a unique hybridization of TK by integrating it with tumor-associated macrophage membranes, which were modified with hollow mesoporous Prussian blue for the purpose of tumor immunotherapy. This multifunctional hybrid membrane not only facilitates the targeted homing of TAMs to the tumor site but also mitigates hypoxia at the tumor site by leveraging the properties of TK. The researchers demonstrated that this multifunctional hybrid membrane significantly enhanced tumor accumulation and alleviated the hypoxic microenvironment of the composite system [ ]. This innovation holds great potential for regulating the tumor microenvironment and improving the effectiveness of cancer immunotherapy. In addition, Chen's research group developed a novel type of ROS-responsive multifunctional fusion extracellular nanovesicles, utilizing grapefruit-derived PDVLNs in conjunction with mesenchymal stem cell membrane-derived nanovesicles. The researchers demonstrated that this multifunctional hybrid membrane effectively mitigates ischemia-reperfusion injury, presenting a promising therapeutic option for minimizing early transplant complications and immune rejection. With further optimization and refinement, this innovative platform is anticipated to offer tailored treatments for a range of diseases beyond transplant rejection [ ].
 Considering the distinctive inherent advantages of PDVLNs and their ability to specifically target the membrane of mammalian cells, there are numerous opportunities to explore their potential applications in different IDs. Furthermore, it is important to examine how these PDVLNs can effectively navigate the significant shielding effects posed by the larger body size of human patients.

4.1.1.3.

## Membrane camouflage
The binding of PDVLNs to nanoparticles primarily occurs through the formation of nanoparticles that are camouflaged by PDVLNs. In this process, the PDVLNs membrane envelops the nanoparticles. To facilitate this camouflage, PDVLNs are initially treated with a hypotonic solution, which serves to remove biomolecules from the chamber. Subsequently, the membrane was extracted from PDVLNs and mixed with nanoparticles through sonication or repeated extrusion. These methods combine the targeting capability and stimuli-triggered drug release functions of nanoparticles with the therapeutic properties of biomolecules derived from PDVLNs [ ]. In a notable study by Wang et al., [ ] mesoporous silica was integrated with PDVLNs for applications related to colitis. As shown in , ginger-derived PDVLNs were used to deliver anti-inflammatory macromolecular drugs specifically targeting colonic cells. Biodistribution data indicated that ginger-derived PDVLNs effectively and safely transported anti-inflammatory drugs across intestinal epithelial cells. Additionally, mesoporous silicon facilitated the sustained release of the drugs, leading to anti-inflammatory activity by blocking the activation of inflammasome NLRP3. This innovative approach using mesoporous silica-labeled PDVLNs offers a promising avenue for the oral treatment of inflammatory bowel disease. Recently, researchers have successfully transplanted TK into mammalian cells by employing membrane camouflage techniques to coat the membranes of these cells, thereby unlocking the cross-species energy transfer mechanism. Tang and co-works systematically verified that chondrocyte-encapsulated TK not only effectively evades immune clearance but is also selectively taken up by degenerative chondrocytes. Through non-invasive in vitro light therapy, the levels of ATP and NADPH in degenerative chondrocytes can be precisely enhanced, thereby maintaining their endurance. As a result, the anabolic processes of chondrocytes have been restructured, facilitating the treatment of degenerative osteoarthritis and other related diseases [ ]. Weng's team discovered that ginger-derived PDVLNs, coated with ZIF-8 nanoparticles, demonstrated effective targeting of colon and macrophage cells, along with significant resistance to acidic degradation in the stomach. Furthermore, it exhibited a high loading capacity for TNF-α siRNA and facilitated the escape of siRNA from cellular lysosomes. These findings suggested that fusion nanoparticles possess favorable biosafety profiles for long-term treatment and hold promise for the management of ulcerative colitis [ ].
 

4.1.2.

## Chemical modification
In the field of chemical modification methods, two conventional approaches are employed to engineer targeted drug delivery systems, referred to as PDVLNs. The first approach involves covalent modification of active functional small molecules or multifunctional nanomaterials. Another strategy entails non-covalent interactions facilitated by hydrophobic compounds or receptor ligands.
4.1.2.1.

## Covalent modification
PDVLNs exhibit a diverse array of proteins on their surface, which facilitates covalent modification reactions by providing numerous active sites, such as amino groups. Zhang et al. developed a PDVLNs delivery platform that targets cancer cells by combining folic acid (FA) with grapefruit-derived PDVLNs. The study demonstrated that the covalent coupling of the surface carboxyl group of FA covalently coupled to the surface amino group of grapefruit-derived PDVLNs significantly enhanced the specific targeting of these nanoparticles to cancer cells with high folate receptor expression [ ]. In another study by Fan's team, as shown in A and B, the amino group of lemon-derived PDVLNs was efficiently reacted with the carboxyl group of functional heparin-cRGD to overcome multidrug resistance in cancer [ ].
 Beyond the modification of PDVLNs with targeting agents, artificial nanomaterials have been used in the field of bio-engineering to create PDVLNs for drug delivery in disease therapy. Wang's group constructed a biomimetic DOX nanodrug delivery system with the grapefruit-derived PDVLNs. The system involved attaching DOX-loaded heparin nanoparticles to the PDVLNs for the treatment of glioma. In contrast to the conventional method of adsorbing DOX onto the surface of PDVLNs, the patch strategy employed a three-dimensional grafting approach, achieving a notable 4-fold enhancement in loading efficiency. This bionic drug delivery system effectively traversed both the BBB and the blood-tumor barrier through receptor-mediated endocytosis and membrane fusion. This integrated approach not only greatly enhances the drug loading capacity of PDVLNs but also expands their potential applications [ ]. Another recent report by the Xu's group, ginger-derived PDVLNs were conjugated with carboxy-functionalized Pd-Pt through abundant amino groups, facilitating a synergistic anti-infective treatment. As illustrated in C and D, the introduction of ginger-derived PDVLNs significantly increased the circulation time of Pd-Pt nanoparticles in the bloodstream, thereby inhibiting immune clearance and facilitating their accumulation at the infection site. Notably, the PDVLNs-Pd-Pt nanoparticles exhibited the ability to penetrate the interior of bacteria in a lipid-dependent manner, indicating a strong interaction with bacterial cells and enhancing their bactericidal efficacy [ ].

4.1.2.2.

## Non-covalent modification
Another crucial method of chemical modification involves the non-covalent binding of hydrophobic molecules or receptor ligands to the bioactive membranes of PDVLNs. PDVLNs that express the FA receptor demonstrate potential as drug delivery vehicles, owing to their accessibility, high biocompatibility, and capacity to selectively target diseased cells. For instance, Li et al. designed an arrow-shaped FA targeting ligand with hydrophobic properties, enabling it to spontaneously integrate into the hydrophobic chains of phospholipids within PDVLNs. The application of these arrow-shaped FA ligands allows for directional control over the loading and surface display functions on the PDVLN membrane, thereby enhancing the specificity of PDVLNs for their target cells [ ]. Fan's group reported the development of a novel drug called EVs engineered structural droplet drugs (ESDDs). As illustrated in , the ESDDs were created by emulsifying cRGD-modified, fruit-derived PDVLNs with the chemotherapy drugs DOX and squalene. The ESDDs demonstrated a remarkable capacity to penetrate both the blood-brain barrier and the blood-brain tumor barrier, resulting in a significant enhancement of anticancer treatment efficacy against glioblastoma [ ]. Peng's group reported that pueraria lobata-derived PDVLNs were engineered with a ternary ligand (DSPE-PEG-RVG), which could spontaneously integrate into the hydrophobic chains of the phospholipids in PDVLNs. The results of the research demonstrated that the use of DSPE-PEG-RVG significantly promoted the survival of dopaminergic neurons, resulting in reduced cellular degeneration, increased density of Nissl substance, and enhanced expression of tyrosine hydroxylase. Additionally, there was a notable alleviation of both motor and non-motor symptoms [ ].
 The integration of PDVLNs with functional molecules or nanoparticles offers distinct advantages and has been widely employed to deliver therapeutic agents. This approach enhances cell targeting, minimizes systemic side effects, and facilitates synergy among various therapeutic strategies. Therefore, ongoing research focused on the chemical modification of PDVLNs for drug delivery applications is poised to significantly influence the utilization of PDVLNs in therapeutic treatments.

4.2.

## Artificial bionic PDVLNs
Because of their unique bio-endogenous properties, PDVLNs have been identified as ideal candidates for drug delivery in numerous preclinical studies of IDs. However, many clinical trials involving PDVLNs have faced challenges, including low yield and the presence of impurities. More importantly, the structure and composition of PDVLNs are multiplex and uncertain, influenced by factors such as the type of parent plant, growing conditions, and production methods. Consequently, a pressing requirement exists for a robust and scalable method to create artificial bionic nanoparticles based on PDVLNs, enabling the complete realization of the clinical capabilities of therapies utilizing PDVLNs. The therapeutic roles of PDVLNs significantly depend on lipids [ ]. Some researchers have successfully isolated specific phospholipids responsible for these functions and have reconstituted them to form artificial nano-lipids representing an innovative engineering technique. Recently, Huang-Ge Zhang's group extracted phospholipids from grapefruit-derived PDVLNs to reconstruct nanocarriers for glioma treatment. Following modifying the targeting agents FA and loading the therapeutic drug miR17, the artificial bionic PDVLNs demonstrated the capability to efficiently deliver miR17 across the BBB, resulting in effective glioma inhibition [ ]. In a related study, a nanocarrier composed of ginger-derived PDVLNs was developed as a delivery platform for the therapeutic drug Dox aimed at treating colon cancer. As illustrated in , nanoparticles extracted from ginger were reassembled into ginger-derived PDVLNs. Characterization results indicated that ginger-derived PDVLNs were effectively internalized by colon cancer cells [ ]. Another study showed that the oral administration of recombinant nano-lipids from ginger-derived PDVLNs, which contain functional small interfering RNA (divalent metal transporter 1, Dmt1), effectively reduces iron overload in hereditary hemochromatosis [ ]. These findings represent a significant shift in the current paradigm of siRNA delivery, moving from synthetic nanoparticles to the utilization of naturally derived nanocarriers sourced from food plants. Xiao's group synthesized a fluorinated pluronic (FP127) and employed it to functionalize the surface of mulberry biomass-derived nanoparticles (MLNs) loaded with resveratrol nanocrystals (RNs). This process resulted in the production of EVs with uniform size and morphology, referred to as FP127@RN-MLNs. These FP127@RN-MLNs demonstrated effective internalization by colon epithelial cells and macrophages. Furthermore, these findings indicate that FP127@RN-MLNs can effectively restore the damaged epithelial barrier, mitigate oxidative stress, promote the polarization of macrophages towards the M2 phenotype, and attenuate inflammatory responses. In vivo studies have further substantiated the substantial therapeutic benefits conferred by FP127@RN-MLNs, demonstrating their capability to significantly reduce both colonic and systemic inflammation, enhance the integrity of colonic tight junctions, and restore balance within the intestinal microbiota [ ]. Chen's team extracted phospholipids from turmeric-derived PDVLNs to reconstitute nanocarriers aimed at obesity intervention. These PDVLNs are characterized by highly aggregated arrangements of curcumin and other co-encapsulated bioactive compounds. These compounds demonstrate dose-dependent lipid-lowering effects in mature 3T3-L1 cells by promoting lipolysis, inhibiting lipogenesis, inducing adipocyte browning, and triggering apoptosis through various internalization pathways [ ].
 In summary, the ``destruction-recombination'' technology is utilized for drug loading and the manipulation of plant lipids to achieve specific functionalities, thereby enabling process tunability and functional customization of PDVLNs. However, it is important to note that the disruption of the PDVLNs' membrane may lead to membrane asymmetry and the potential loss of functional components, such as miRNAs and soluble proteins ( ).
 

5.

## Clinical trials of PDVLNs
In recent decades, numerous studies have greatly enhanced the scientific community's understanding of the functional advantages of PDVLNs, offering valuable insights into their potential biomedical applications for the treatment of IDs. The University of Louisville in the United States has registered several clinical trials focused on the clinical management of PDVLNs, owing to their remarkable anti-inflammatory effects, as illustrated in . For example, a recently completed trial (NCT01668849) investigated the efficacy of grape-derived PDVLNs in preventing chemotherapy-associated oral mucositis in patients suffering from head and neck cancer. This trial also examined the impact of grape-derived PDVLNs on cytokine production, immune responses to tumor exosome antigens, and various metabolic and molecular markers in the patients. A separate investigation carried out by Bohler and colleagues focused on examining the effectiveness of PDVLNs derived from ginger or aloe vera in alleviating insulin resistance and chronic inflammation among individuals diagnosed with polycystic ovary syndrome. However, these PDVLNs have not yet been approved for clinical trials (NCT03493984). PDVLNs have demonstrated considerable potential as nanocarriers for the delivery of hydrophobic drugs. Miller et al. [109] utilized PDVLNs to transport curcumin for the treatment of colon cancer, and Phase I clinical trials are currently in progress to assess the delivery efficacy of PDVLNs in both normal and colon cancer tissues (NCT01294072). In a follow-up study, Zhang et al. sought to explore the efficacy of ginger-derived PDVLNs as natural anti-inflammatory agents in the context of treating irritable bowel disease, a condition that can significantly impair quality of life. The findings from this clinical trial, identified by the registration number NCT04879810, indicate that ginger-derived PDVLNs show considerable potential as therapeutic nanoparticles.
 While PDVLNs offer the benefits of being animal-free and are grounded in traditional Chinese medicine theory, it is crucial to emphasize that PDVLNs derived from edible plants are expected to be safe and may advance through the later stages of clinical trials more swiftly than conventional investigational new drugs. Nonetheless, there is a pressing need to scale up the isolation, purification, and production processes of PDVLNs. Additionally, the production process of PDVLNs in clinical trials must comply with GMP standards. illustrates the expected process for translating PDVLNs research from the laboratory to the clinic. Currently, the complete realization of the process from culture, isolation, and characterization to clinical trials and production of PDVLNs has not been achieved. A significant challenge in the field is the absence of standardized protocols for the extraction and isolation of PDVLNs from diverse plant sources. To facilitate their clinical application, it is essential to resolve the current issues associated with GMP production and to undertake research focused on GMP-grade PDVLNs. PDVLNs are exosome-like vesicles derived from various sources, including leaves, rice buds, flower seeds, roots, and juice. However, there is a paucity of information regarding the characterization of PDVLNs, especially concerning specific markers. The predominant techniques employed for their characterization, such as TEM, NTA, and SDS-PAGE, closely resemble those utilized for animal-derived EVs. Thus, the development of GMP-grade PDVLNs, particularly regarding purification and characterization, can benefit from advancements made in the field of animal-derived EVs. In light of the current absence of clinical trials, it is clear that additional clinical studies are necessary to further the application of PDVLNs in future biotherapeutic treatments.
 
6.

## Conclusion and perspectives
IDs are a prevalent class of diseases affecting the general population. The majority of drugs currently used to treat these diseases are synthetic, which often lead to serious side effects and consequently multiple complications when used for prolonged durations. As a result, there is an urgent need to develop novel anti-inflammatory drugs with ideal efficacy and minimal side effects. From ancient times to the present day, plants have been the foundation of many traditional medicine systems worldwide, with countries like China, India, and Brazil having a long history of utilizing plants as medicines for treating IDs. Plant-derived drugs offer several advantages, including easy accessibility, satisfying safety profile, and being mixtures of multiple pharmacologically active ingredients. These active ingredients exhibit structural diversity, target multiple pathways simultaneously, display strong bioactivity, and induce low levels of toxicity and side effects [ ]. Moreover, EVs, due to their biogenic nature and nanoscale effects, have the ability to transfer active biomolecules to target cells in distant organs or neighboring cells, playing a significant role in intercellular communication and amplifying the biological effects of the carried biomolecules [ ]. The distinct properties of EVs position them as a highly promising option for next-generation drug delivery systems. These PDVLNs not only serve the purpose of transporting therapeutic agents but also comprise a diverse array of bioactive compounds, including lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, and metabolites. This rich content enables EVs to play an active role in modulating biological processes associated with IDs [ ]. PDVLNs can be produced through environmentally friendly procedures from a wide range of sources on a large scale at a low cost, possess good biocompatibility, and can be engineered to enhance their therapeutic effects. Furthermore, engineering technology can enrich the therapeutic function of PDVLNs. All these properties highlight the promising prospects of PDVLNs being utilized for the development of novel anti-inflammatory drugs. The accumulation of research on PDVLNs has greatly expanded our understanding of the bio-generation process of natural PDVLNs and has also promoted the development of PDVLNs-based therapeutic nanomedicine. Compared with artificially-synthesized nanoparticles, PDVLNs possess the following advantages: 1) The endogenous structure of these nanoparticles plays a significant role in enhancing their biocompatibility and ensuring high levels of bioavailability; 2) The nanoscale size of these particles is a crucial factor that contributes to their ability to penetrate various biological barriers and to achieve high permeability specifically at sites of inflammation; 3) The presence of transmembrane and membrane ankyrin proteins on the surface of these nanoparticles gives them a unique advantage in targeting specific cell types. Furthermore, PDVLNs not only overcome ethical concerns associated with animal EVs but can also be produced on a large scale, making them highly potential for clinical applications. Currently, there is a growing research interest in utilizing PDVLNs as a drug delivery nanoplatform in the management of IDs. Therefore, we herein provide a comprehensive review of related investigations, discussing the biological significance of each enriched component of PDVLNs and summarizing their engineering strategies in the context of IDs treatment. Although advancements in nanotechnology have greatly benefited the development of PDVLNs-based nanomaterials, studies on the utilization of PDVLNs in biomedical fields are still in their infancy. Several significant challenges need to be addressed before the clinical translation of PDVLNs can come to a success:
(1) There is an urgent need to upgrade the PDVLNs production method to fulfill their large-scale application in clinical settings. Current PDVLNs production methods involve ultracentrifugation, ultrafiltration, density gradient centrifugation, and size exclusion chromatography, which are time-consuming, low-yield, and lack uniformity and standardization, hindering the large-scale production of clinical-grade PDVLNs. In light of the diverse range of plant species, their distinct life stages, and the varying regions in which they are found, it is crucial to undertake a thorough investigation into the establishment of a reliable method for assessing the relationship between plant raw materials and PDVLNs.
(2) PDVLNs possess a substantial quantity of endogenous cargo, such as nucleic acids and proteins, which may constrain the capacity for drug loading. To improve the encapsulation efficiency of drugs within PDVLNs, two approaches can be explored: eliminating their inherent contents and increasing their volume. However, the process of removing endogenous content carries the risk of undermining the membrane integrity of PDVLNs and disrupting their structure. Moreover, it remains unclear whether, and to what extent, increasing the volume of PDVLNs compromises their nanoscale effect. All these issues warrant careful consideration when improving the encapsulation efficiency of PDVLNs.
(3) Most of the therapeutic effects of PDVLNs depend on the biological functions and natural biodistribution of the original plants. In recent years, molecular biology techniques have been used to engineer PDVLNs into bio-nano platforms for targeted drug delivery in the treatment of IDs. One key aspect of this approach is the integration of tailored artificial nanomaterials into hybrid carrier formulations with diverse functions, controllable structures, and high drug-loading capacities. By leveraging this technology, it is possible to mitigate the immunogenicity and cytotoxicity associated with artificial nanomaterials, while also overcoming biological barriers and evading the mononuclear phagocyte system. However, it is important to note that research on engineered PDVLNs is still in its early stages. To further enhance the biological functions and expand the application scope of PDVLNs, researchers can selectively draw insights from a variety of technologies employed in engineered animal-derived EVs.
At present, PDVLNs are at a critical crossroad in their clinical application for treating IDs. At this time point, it is imperative to integrate research on PDVLNs plant biology and IDs cell biology to establish a strong theoretical foundation for the clinical translation of PDVLNs. At the same time, the development of scalable and consistent PDVLNs purification and characterization strategies, as well as the establishment of a comprehensive regulatory system for their production and application, will also facilitate their successful clinical translation.
