## Abstract

## Background
Pulmonary fibrosis is a serious interstitial lung disease with no viable treatment except for lung transplantation. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R), commonly regarded as an antidiabetic target, exerts antifibrotic effects on various types of organ fibrosis. However, whether GLP-1R modulates the development and progression of pulmonary fibrosis remains unclear. In this study, we investigated the antifibrotic effect of GLP-1R using in vitro and in vivo models of pulmonary fibrosis.

## Methods
A silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis mouse model was established to evaluate the protective effects of activating GLP-1R with liraglutide in vivo. Primary cultured lung fibroblasts treated with TGF-β1 combined with IL-1β (TGF-β1 + IL-1β) were used to explore the specific effects of liraglutide, MCC950, and 3PO on fibroblast activation in vitro. Cell metabolism assay was performed to determine the glycolytic rate and mitochondrial respiration. RNA sequencing was utilized to analyse the underlying molecular mechanisms by which liraglutide affects fibroblast activation. ChIP‒qPCR was used to evaluate histone lactylation at the promoters of profibrotic genes in TGF-β1 + IL-1β- or exogenous lactate-stimulated lung fibroblasts.

## Results
Activating GLP-1R with liraglutide attenuated pulmonary inflammation and fibrosis in mice exposed to silica. Pharmacological inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome suppressed PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and vice versa, resulting in decreased lactate production in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated lung fibroblasts. Activating GLP-1R inhibited TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast activation by disrupting the interaction between the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and subsequently prevented lactate-mediated histone lactylation to reduce pro-fibrotic gene expression. In addition, activating GLP-1R protected mitochondria against the TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced increase in oxidative phosphorylation in fibroblasts. In exogenous lactate-treated lung fibroblasts, activating GLP-1R not only repressed NLRP3 inflammasome activation but also alleviated p300-mediated histone lactylation. Finally, GLP-1R activation blocked silica-treated macrophage-conditioned media-induced lung fibroblast activation.

## Conclusions
The antifibrotic effects of GLP-1R activation on pulmonary fibrosis could be attributed to the inhibition of the interaction between NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, and histone lactylation in lung fibroblasts. Thus, GLP-1R is a specific therapeutic target for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis.

Keywords: Pulmonary fibrosis, GLP-1R, NLRP3 inflammasome, Glycolysis, Histone lactylation

## Background
Pulmonary fibrosis is a progressive, often lethal disease characterized by limited therapeutic options and eventually irreversible lung tissue damage. More than 200 causes of pulmonary fibrosis have been identified including environmental and occupational exposure to toxins, genetic mutations, autoimmune diseases, infections, drugs and radiation [ , ]. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is one of the most common and devastating forms of IPF with a poor life expectancy [ ]. Although the pathogenesis is complex and multifactorial, the phenotypes and pathological features of pulmonary fibrosis are basically similar. Generally, alveolar macrophage inflammation and epithelial cell damage are the triggering events of pulmonary fibrosis, followed by fibroblast proliferation and myofibroblast activation in response to cytokines, which in turn results in excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition [ , ]. Transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1), which is primarily derived from alveolar macrophages, has been identified as a dominant mediator driving fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation and inducing the expression of fibrosis-related genes [ ]. Chronic or uncontrolled inflammation is a prominent feature of pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. The nucleotide-binding and oligomerization domain, leucine-rich repeat, and pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome is a multiprotein complex comprising NLRP3, apoptosis speck-like protein (ASC), and pro-caspase-1. The formation of the inflammasome leads to the activation of caspase-1, which has been confirmed to initiate sustained inflammation by maturating and releasing interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and IL-18 [ ]. Activated caspase-1 also cleaves gasdermin D (GSDMD) to liberate the N-terminal domain (GSDMD-NT), which drives pyroptosis [ ]. IL-1β either stimulates fibroblast ECM production directly or augments TGF-β1 signaling, thereafter accelerating fibrosis progression [ ]. NLRP3 inflammasome activation not only facilitates inflammatory factor production but also triggers macrophage pyroptosis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and fibroblast activation and differentiation during pulmonary fibrogenesis [ ]. These findings suggest that the NLRP3 inflammasome is a pivotal therapeutic target for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis. However, considering its important effects on innate immunity, long-term inhibition of the inflammasome comes with a risk of infection [ ], which limits the clinical application of NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors.
During the past decade, metabolic reprogramming, particularly aberrant aerobic glycolysis in fibroblasts and macrophages, has been confirmed to play a significant role in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Lung tissues from IPF patients showed the upregulation of glycolysis-related gene expression and high level of the glycolytic end-product lactate [ ]. The mechanism of glycolysis-driven organ fibrosis is multifactorial. First, lactate overproduction by glycolysis promotes myofibroblast proliferation and differentiation as well as TGF-β activation in a pH-dependent manner [ ]. Second, glycolysis provides essential biosynthetic intermediates for the production of nucleotides, lipids, and amino acids, which are essential for myofibroblast proliferation and ECM synthesis [ ]. Third, glycolytic reprogramming modulates epigenetics (such as histone acetylation and DNA methylation) by providing substrates and cofactors for epigenetic modifiers and chromatin remodelers [ ]. A recent discovery highlights the role of histone lactylation, a new epigenetic modification driven by the glycolytic product lactate [ ], which is upregulated at the promoters of the profibrotic genes and contributes to bleomycin (BLM)-induced lung fibrosis [ ]. Therefore, targeting pivotal regulators of cellular metabolism may pave the way for innovative therapeutic strategies for pulmonary fibrosis.
Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R) is a G protein-coupled receptor with a broad expression profile across various tissues, including the pancreas, heart, kidneys, blood vessels, liver, central nervous system and lungs [ ]. In addition to its primary role in facilitating insulin release from pancreatic β-cells, GLP-1R has been reported to exhibit anti-inflammatory effects by suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome activity on various diseases, such as ischaemic stroke [ ], coronary artery disease [ ], and asthma [ ]. In addition, GLP-1R is recognized as a crucial regulator of cellular glucose metabolism. In mouse astrocytes, GLP-1R knockout led to diminished mitochondrial function and promoted aerobic glycolysis [ ]. In cardiomyocytes, GLP-1R activation has no significant effect on myocardial glucose metabolism under nonischemic conditions [ ], but there is an increased expression of oxidative phosphorylation protein and a simultaneous downregulation of glycolysis pathways during the early phases of myocardial infarction [ ]. Evidently, the mechanisms by which GLP-1R regulates cellular metabolism are complicated. In addition to these roles, GLP-1R activation has been demonstrated to exert antifibrotic effects on the fibrosis of solid organs, including the heart, liver and kidneys [ ]. Recently, the GLP-1R agonist liraglutide was shown to ameliorate BLM-induced experimental lung fibrosis [ ]. However, the underlying molecular mechanism involved remains obscure.
Over the years, various preclinical mouse models of pulmonary fibrosis induced by drugs or toxic factors (such as BLM, silica, paraquat, and fluorescein isothiocyanate) have been developed [ ]. Interestingly, silica particles serve a dual purpose: acting as a substrate for a robust murine pulmonary fibrosis model characterized by sustained fibrotic lesions and functioning as a potent activator of the NLRP3 inflammasome [ ]. Therefore, in this study, the effects of GLP-1R on pulmonary fibrosis were investigated by using the GLP-1R agonist liraglutide in a mouse model subjected to intratracheal silica particle instillation. Considering that fibroblasts are direct executors of organ fibrosis, the underlying mechanisms of GLP-1R in pulmonary fibrosis were explored further in primary cultured lung fibroblasts treated with a combination of TGF-β1 and IL-1β.

## Methods

## Animal studies
All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Nanjing Medical University (IACUC-2306028) (Nanjing, China). Male C57BL/6 mice aged 6–8 weeks were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions at Nanjing Medical University Experimental Animal Center (Nanjing, China). A total of 40 mice were randomly divided into four groups, with ten mice in each group: a control group, a silica group, a silica plus liraglutide group, and a control plus liraglutide group. Mice in the silica groups underwent intratracheal instillation of a single dose of silica particles (100 mg/kg, size distribution: purity 99%, particle diameter 0.5–10 μm, average diameter 1.7 μm, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) in 50 µl of sterile saline. Mice in the liraglutide group were subcutaneously injected with liraglutide (Lir, 150 µg/kg, Novo Nordisk Inc., Bagsvaerd, Denmark) daily. All mice were euthanized at 56 days after treatment, tracheal cannulated and lavaged three times with 0.5 ml of sterile cold phosphate-bufered saline (PBS) to collect the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). Then, the lungs were perfused with PBS through the right ventricle until all blood was completely removed, after which the lung tissues were isolated. The isolated lung tissues and BALF were stored at -80 °C for further analysis.

## Histological and immunohistochemical analysis
Left lung tissues were fixed in 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde for 24 h at room temperature, embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm thickness. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson’s trichrome staining were applied to these sections to evaluate inflammation and fibrosis. Immunohistochemical staining of α-SMA (1:2000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 14395-1-AP) and collagen I (1:1000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 14695-1-AP) was performed following a standardized procedure, and the tissues were then analyzed under a Nikon Eclipse Ci microscope.

## Immunofluorescence
Left lung tissues were fixed in 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde for 24 h at room temperature, embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm thickness. For immunofluorescence staining, the lung sections were incubated with primary antibodies against α-SMA (1:50, Servicebio, Wuhan, China, GB13044) and ASC (1:50, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA, 67824s) overnight at 4 °C. After washing with PBS, secondary antibodies (goat anti-mouse-Alexa 488 for α-SMA and goat anti-rabbit-Alexa 555 for ASC) were incubated for 1 h at room temperature. The nuclei were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 10 min. Fluorescence images were collected by fluorescence microscopy (Carl Zeiss, Jena, Germany).
Fibroblasts were seeded on chamber slides and transfected with NC siRNA or NLRP3 siRNA. Then, fibroblasts were treated with lactate (20 mM). After 24 h incubation, fibroblasts were fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, blocked with QuickBlock™ Blocking Buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and incubated with ASC antibody (1:400, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA, 67824s) at 4℃ overnight. After washing with PBS, fibroblasts were labeled with goat anti-rabbit-Alexa 555 (Invitrogen) at room temperature for 1 h. The nuclei were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 10 min. Fluorescence images were collected by fluorescence microscopy (Carl Zeiss, Jena, Germany).

## Primary mouse lung fibroblast isolation, culture and treatment
Primary mouse lung fibroblasts were isolated from 6- to 8-week-old C57BL/6 mouse lungs. Briefly, lungs were perfused with PBS, minced into small pieces and digested with collagenase A (2 mg/ml, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) and 0.25% Trypsin-EDTA (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) at 37 °C for 30 min. After centrifuged and washed with PBS three times, the cell pellet was resuspended and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) containing 10% FBS and cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Fibroblasts from passages three to five were used in the experiments. The cells were exposed to a range of treatments: TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml, R&D Systems Inc., Minneapolis, USA), IL-1β (10 ng/ml, R&D Systems Inc., Minneapolis, USA), MCC950 (10 µM, Selleck, Houston, TX, USA), 3PO (10 or 30 µM, Selleck, Houston, TX, USA), liraglutide (10, 100 or 1000 nM, Selleck, Houston, TX, USA), and sodium L-lactate (20 mM, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

## siRNA transfection
For the knockdown of NLRP3 expression in fibroblasts, three complementary oligodeoxynucleotides of NLRP3 siRNA (siNLRP3) and a non-specific control siRNA (siNC) were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The siRNAs sequences were as follows: NC, forward, 5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3’, reverse, 5’-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3’; NLRP3 siRNA #1, forward, 5’-CCGCAUCUCCAUUUGUAAATT-3’, reverse, 5’-UUUACAAAUGGAGAUGCGGTT-3’; NLRP3 siRNA #2, forward, 5’-GCGAGAGAUUCUACAGCUUTT-3’, reverse, 5’-AAGCUGUAGAAUCUCUCGCTT-3’; NLRP3 siRNA #3, forward, 5’-GGUCUCUUCUCAAGUCUAATT-3’, reverse, 5’-UUAGACUUGAGAAGAGACCTT-3’; Fibroblasts were transfected with the siRNAs using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 48 h transfection, total protein was extracted and the inhibitory efficiency of siRNAs against NLRP3 was detected by western blotting.

## Preparation of silica-induced macrophage-conditioned media
The MH-S mouse alveolar macrophage cell line was purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China) and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 µg/ml streptomycin at 37 °C and 5% CO2. To obtain silica-induced macrophage conditioned media (MCM), MH-S cells were exposed to silica-bearing medium (50 µg/cm2) for 24 h. Then, the medium was collected, filtered through a 0.22 μm pore size syringe filter, and stored at -20 °C for subsequent experiments.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The concentrations of IL-1β (CUSABIO, Wuhan, China), IL-18 (CUSABIO, Wuhan, China) and TGF-β1 (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) in BALF or culture medium were measured by commercially available ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Histone extraction
Total histones were extracted from lung tissues or fibroblasts using the EpiQuik Total Histone Extraction Kit (Epigentek, Farmingdale, NY, USA) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. In brief, lung tissues or fibroblasts were homogenized with 1× Pre-Lysis Buffer and centrifuged at 1000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. The tissue or cell pellet was resuspended in Lysis Buffer and incubated on ice for 30 min. Post-centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was transferred to a fresh tube and immediately supplemented with balance-DTT buffer. The extracted histones were preserved at -80 °C until western blotting analysis.

## Western blotting
Total protein from lung tissues or fibroblasts was extracted with RIPA lysis buffer containing cOmplete Protease Inhibitor (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Equal amounts of protein or histones were separated via electrophoresis in 8-12% sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gels and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA). After blocking, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: PFKFB3 (1:2000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 13763-1-AP), NLRP3 (1:500, Novus, Littleton, CO, USA, NBP2-12446), ASC (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA, 67824s), Caspase-1 p20 (1:1000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 22915-1-AP), collagen I (1:2000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 14695-1-AP), α-SMA (1:4000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 14395-1-AP), β-actin (1:5000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, 20536-1-AP), Pan-Kla (1:1000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1401RM), H4K5la (1:1000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1407RM), H4K12la (1:1000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1411RM), H3K18la (1:1000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1406RM), H4 (1:2000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1004), H3 (1:2000, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1001), and p300 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA, 67621). Then, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:20000, Jackson ImmunoResearch, West Grove, PA) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using WesternBright Quantum (Advansta, Menlo Park, California, USA) and quantified by a ChemiDoc™ XRS + system with Image Lab software (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

## Cell viability assay
Fibroblast viability was assessed by a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) assay. Fibroblasts were seeded in a 96-well plate and exposed to the indicated reagents for 24 h. Subsequently, 10 µl of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the plate was incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a spectrophotometer (BioTek, Vermont, USA).

## EdU proliferation assay
A 5-ethynyl-20-deoxyuridine (EdU) incorporation assay kit (Ribobio, Guangzhou, China) was used to assess fibroblast proliferation. Following treatment, the fibroblasts were incubated with EdU (final concentration, 50 µM) for 2 h at 37 °C. The cells were then fixed, permeabilized, and stained. EdU-positive cells were quantified relative to total Hoechst 33,342-stained cells using fluorescence microscopy (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

## Transwell assay
Treated fibroblasts were placed in the upper chambers of a 24-well transwell plate with 8 μm pores (Corning, NY, USA). Then, complete medium containing 20% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After 24 h at 37 °C, the migrating cells were fixed, stained, and quantified under an optical microscope.

## Cell contraction assay
Fibroblast contractility was assessed using a Cell Contraction Assay Kit (Cell Biolabs, San Diego, CA, USA). Briefly, fibroblasts were harvested and resuspended in DMEM at a concentration of 1 × 106 cells/ml. The cell suspension was combined with Collagen Gel Working Solution at a 1:4 ratio. Then, 500 µl/well of the cell-collagen mixture was dispensed in a 24-well plate, and incubated for 1 h at 37 °C until gelled. DMEM (1 ml/well) containing 10% FBS and the specified reagents was added on top of each collagen gel. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C, the collagen gels were gently released from the sides of the wells with an 18 G needle. Finally, images of the collagen gels were captured with a digital camera, and the contraction ratio (relative to the initial collagen gel size) was quantified using ImageJ software.

## Wound-healing assay
A wound-healing assay was employed to study fibroblast migration. Fibroblasts were seeded into a 6-well plate and transfected with NC siRNA or NLRP3 siRNA. The monolayer was scratched with a sterile 200 µL pipette tip, and cells were then treated with the TGF-β1 (10 ng/ml) + IL-1β (10 ng/ml) for 24 h at 37 °C. Microscopic images were captured, and the wound healing area was analyzed using ImageJ software.

## Hydroxyproline assay
Collagen contents in lung tissues were quantified using a Hydroxyproline content assay kit (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The absorbance was read at 550 nm using a spectrophotometer (BioTek, Vermont, USA). The results are expressed as µg/mg in lung tissues.

## Lactate assay
Following fibroblast treatment, the culture medium was collected for extracellular lactate measurement. The fibroblasts were harvested, homogenized with sterile cold PBS and centrifuged, after which the supernatants were collected for intracellular lactate measurement. For lung tissue lactate measurement, equal amounts of lung tissue were homogenized with sterile cold PBS and centrifuged, and cell-free supernatants were collected. Lactate levels in culture medium, BALF, fibroblasts and lung tissue homogenates were measured using a Lactate Assay Kit (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The absorbance was read at 530 nm, and the concentrations were normalized to the cell number or the protein concentration.

## Seahorse assay
A total of 2 × 104 fibroblasts were seeded in a Seahorse assay microplate and treated with the indicated reagents for 24 h. Then, the fibroblasts were washed twice with Seahorse DMEM (SM media) supplemented with 1 mM pyruvate, 2 mM glutamine and 10 mM glucose (pH = 7.4) and equilibrated in a non-CO2 incubator for 1 h. The glycolytic rate was determined using an XF Glycolytic Rate Assay Kit (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) with final concentrations of 0.5 µM rotenone/antimycin A (Rot/AA) and 50 mM 2-deoxy glucose (2-DG) sequentially injected into the SM media. An oxygen consumption rate (OCR) assay was performed using the Seahorse Cell Mito Stress Test Kit (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) with a final concentration of 1.5 µM oligomycin, 1.0 µM FCCP and 0.5 µM Rot/AA sequentially injected into the SM media. The glycolytic rate or OCR was monitored and recorded on a Seahorse XFe96 Analyzer. Data analysis was conducted using Seahorse Wave Desktop Software. GlycoPER and OCR were used as surrogates for the glycolytic rate and mitochondrial respiration, respectively.

## RNA isolation and qRT‒PCR analysis
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Takara Bio, Tokyo, Japan) and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT‒PCR) was performed using Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The relative expression of each target gene was normalized to that of β-actin. The primer sequences used were as follows: β-actin, 5’-GATTACTGCTCTGGCTCCTAGC-3’ and 5’-GACTCATCGTACTCCTGCTTGC-3’; α-SMA, 5’-GCTGGTGATGATGCTCCCA-3’ and 5’-GCCCATTCCAACCATTACTCC-3’; collagen I, 5’-CCAAGAAGACATCCCTGAAGTCA-3’ and 5’-TGCACGTCATCGCACACA-3’; and fibronectin (Fn), 5’-GTGTAGCACAACTTCCAATTACGAA-3’ and 5’-GGAATTTCCGCCTCGAGTCT-3’.

## RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis
Total RNA from fibroblasts was extracted using TRIzol (Takara Bio, Tokyo, Japan). The quantity and quality of the RNA were evaluated using a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Then RNA libraries were constructed using the TruSeq™ Stranded mRNA LT Sample Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The sequencing of total RNA from fibroblasts treated with the indicated reagents was performed by OE Biotech (Shanghai, China). Differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis was calculated by DESeq2 R package. A P value < 0.05 and fold change (FC) > 1.5 or FC < 0.67 were set as the thresholds for significant DEGs.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)‒qPCR
Fibroblasts were seeded in a 10 cm dish and treated with the indicated reagents for 24 h. Then, the cells were cross-linked with 37% formaldehyde, quenched with 125 mM glycine, lysed, sonicated and sequentially immunoprecipitated with anti-H3K18la (1:100, PTM Biolabs, Hangzhou, China, PTM-1406RM) following the protocol described by the Pierce™ Magnetic ChIP Kit (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA). The precipitated DNA fragments were purified and analyzed by qPCR with the Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) to detect the genomic DNA sequences in the proximal promoter regions of Acta2 and Col1a1. The following primers were used for ChIP‒qPCR: Acta2, 5’-TCCAGCCTTCCCTAACAAGA-3’ and 5’-CACAAGAGCAAATGGCTGAA-3’; and Col1a1, 5’-TGCTGTTCTTGGGGGACTAC-3’ and 5’-GCCATAGAGGGGTGTTCTCA-3’.

## Statistical analysis
GraphPad Prism 8.0 was used for the statistical analyses. All data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of at least three independent experiments. Statistical differences between two groups were evaluated by unpaired Student’s t tests. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni post hoc analysis was used for comparisons among multiple groups. For all the statistical tests, P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Activating GLP-1R attenuates silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice
>To investigate the effects of GLP-1R activation on silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis, mice were intratracheally instilled with a single dose of silica, followed by daily treatment with the GLP-1R agonist liraglutide for 56 days. H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining showed that silica-induced pulmonary inflammation and fibrotic changes were significantly reduced by liraglutide (Fig.  a-c), which was consistent with the quantification of hydroxyproline contents in lung tissues (Fig.  d). Immunohistochemical staining and western blotting for α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and collagen I in lung tissues were used to further evaluate lung fibrosis. Liraglutide also significantly attenuated silica-induced lung fibrosis, as shown by a decrease in the abundance of α-SMA and a reduction in collagen I deposition (Fig.  e, f).
 The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome was assessed due to its critical role in the development of silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Liraglutide treatment significantly inhibited silica-induced upregulation of NLRP3, ASC and cleaved caspase-1 p20 in lung tissues (Fig.  g). Moreover, the production of IL-1β, IL-18 and TGF-β1 in BALF were suppressed by liraglutide (Fig.  h). To further confirm the cell type in which NLRP3 inflammasome was activated, ASC expression in lung tissues was investigated by immunofluorescence staining. A co-localization of ASC and α-SMA (a widely used marker of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts) was found, which indicated the NLRP3 inflammasome activation occurred in lung fibroblasts during silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis (Fig.  i).
The 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2, 6-biphosphatase 3 (PFKFB3), a crucial enzyme of glycolysis, has been reported to be associated with pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. In our study, PFKFB3 was found to be overexpressed in fibrotic lungs 56 days after silica instillation (Fig.  g). Consistently, the concentration of the glycolytic end-product lactate in both the BALF (Fig.  j) and lung tissues (Fig. 1k) of silica model mice was significantly greater than that in control mice. However, the silica-induced upregulation of PFKFB3 and the overproduction of lactate were inhibited by liraglutide (Fig.  g, j, k). Lactate is the critical substrate for histone lactylation, a novel epigenetic modification associated with inflammation [ ] and fibrotic diseases [ , ]. Our results showed that silica instillation resulted in significantly increased levels of pan-histone lysine lactylation (Pan-Kla), lactylation on histone H3 lysine 18 (H3K18la) and H4K12la in the lungs, which were suppressed by liraglutide (Fig.  l). These results suggested that GLP-1R activation reduced PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, lactate production and histone lactylation in silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis.

## TGF-β1 + IL-1β promotes NLRP3 inflammasome activation, glycolysis and histone lactylation in lung fibroblasts
Since fibroblasts play a central role in pulmonary fibrosis, primary mouse lung fibroblasts were isolated and cultured for further experiments. TGF-β1 combined with IL-1β (TGF-β1 + IL-1β) was applied to induce fibroblast activation in vitro. As shown in Fig.  a and b, TGF-β1 + IL-1β challenge enhanced cell viability and promoted the expression of α-SMA and collagen I in fibroblasts in a time-dependent manner, validating the pro-fibrotic effects of TGF-β1 + IL-1β on fibroblasts. Next, whether TGF-β1 + IL-1β challenge activated the NLRP3 inflammasome or PFKFB3-driven glycolysis in fibroblasts was examined. Western blotting showed that TGF-β1 + IL-1β treatment time-dependently upregulated the expression of NLRP3, ASC and active caspase-1 p20 in fibroblasts, while GSDMD-NT did not change significantly (Fig.  c). In addition, upregulation of PFKFB3 (Fig.  d) and an increase in extracellular lactate were detected (Fig.  e), indicating an enhancement of PFKFB3-driven glycolysis in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-activated fibroblasts. Moreover, the levels of Pan-Kla, H3K18la and H4K12la were markedly increased in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-treated fibroblasts, while H4K5la remained unchanged (Fig.  f).
 

## Inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome alleviates TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated glycolysis and fibroblast activation
Previous studies have demonstrated that NLRP3 inflammasome regulates glycolysis in macrophages [ ]. Here, we investigated the interaction between the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis in fibroblasts under the stress of TGF-β1 + IL-1β stimulation. MCC950, a selective NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitor, successfully suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation, as indicated by the downregulation of caspase-1 p20 (Fig. 3a). Additionally, MCC950 decreased the TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced upregulation of PFKFB3 and the expression of the fibrotic markers α-SMA and collagen I (Fig.  a). Coinciding with PFKFB3, TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced increase in either intracellular or extracellular lactate was also partially reversed by MCC950 (Fig.  b, c). The glycolytic rate was assessed by GlycoPER using a Seahorse XFe96 Analyzer. Our data showed that TGF-β1 + IL-1β increased basal and maximal GlycoPER, indicating the enhancement of glycolysis during fibroblast activation, and these responses were inhibited by MCC950 (Fig.  d). Moreover, MCC950 attenuated TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast proliferation (Fig.  e), migration (Fig.  f) and contraction (Fig.  g).
 To further confirm the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in fibroblast activation, three different siRNAs were designed to knock down NLRP3 expression in fibroblasts. As shown in Fig.  h, compared with siNC group, siNLRP3 #1 exerted the highest inhibitory effect on the expression of NLRP3 in fibroblasts, which was thereby chosen for subsequent experiments. Western blotting showed that NLRP3 knockdown significantly decreased the protein levels of caspase-1 p20 and the activated fibroblasts marker collagen I in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblasts (Fig.  i). A wound-healing assay revealed that siNLRP3 transfection markedly declined TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast migration (Fig.  j). In addition, NLRP3 knockdown also significantly suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated PFKFB3 overexpression in fibroblasts (Fig.  k). Thus, these results suggested that NLRP3 inflammasome contributed to TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated glycolysis and fibroblast activation.

## Inhibiting PFKFB3 attenuates TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated NLRP3 inflammasome activation and fibroblast activation
The effects of PFKFB3-driven glycolysis on NLRP3 inflammasome activation in fibroblasts under the stress of TGF-β1 + IL-1β were investigated by using 3PO, a selective PFKFB3 inhibitor. Both 10 and 30 µM 3PO suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and α-SMA and collagen I expression, with significant inhibitory effects at 30 µM (Fig.  a). Although 30 µM 3PO alone had no effect on basal lactate production or glycolysis, it significantly inhibited TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced production of lactate as well as the basal and maximal GlycoPER (Fig.  b-d). Moreover, 3PO also significantly attenuated TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast proliferation (Fig.  e), migration (Fig.  f) and contraction (Fig.  g). These results indicated that PFKFB3-driven glycolysis involves TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation in fibroblasts.
 

## Activating GLP-1R inhibits TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated NLRP3 inflammasome activation in fibroblasts
The above data suggested that NLRP3 inflammasome interacted and coordinated with glycolysis to drive TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast activation. Next, the effects of GLP-1R on TGF-β1 + IL-1β-challenged fibroblasts were investigated by using liraglutide. Liraglutide alone had no effect on the viability of fibroblasts at concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 10 µM (Fig.  a). However, liraglutide (10 nM to 1000 nM) suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced increase in cell viability in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig.  b), as well as the overexpression of α-SMA and collagen I in fibroblasts (Fig.  c). Moreover, liraglutide (1000 nM) significantly suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast proliferation (Fig.  d), migration (Fig.  e) and contraction (Fig.  f), demonstrating that GLP-1R negatively regulates TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast activation. In addition, liraglutide decreased the expression of NLRP3, ASC and caspase-1 p20 in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblasts (Fig.  g).
 To further uncover the effects of liraglutide on TGF-β1 + IL-1β-challenged fibroblasts, the gene expression profile of fibroblasts was investigated by RNA sequencing. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed substantial upregulation shifts in NOD-like receptor and ECM organization pathways in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblasts, which were suppressed by liraglutide treatment (Fig.  h, i). Differential gene expression (DGE) analysis (TGF-β1 + IL-1β vs. Control, TGF-β1 + IL-1β + Lir vs. TGF-β1 + IL-1β) of these two pathways was presented as a heatmap in Fig.  j. Notably, the anti-inflammatory effects of GLP-1R activation were not limited to the NLRP3 inflammasome but also affected other critical inflammatory signals, such as nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB), mitogen-activated protein kinases and chemokines (Fig.  j). These results indicated that GLP-1R activation exerted a wide range of anti-inflammatory effects, at least partially by inhibiting the NLRP3 inflammasome in fibroblasts.

## Activating GLP-1R inhibits PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and histone lactylation in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblasts
Our results demonstrated that TGF-β1 + IL-1β augmented PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, lactate production and histone lactylation in fibroblasts in vitro (Fig.  d-f). Activating GLP-1R with liraglutide inhibited TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced upregulation of PFKFB3 in fibroblasts in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig.  a). In addition to identifying the upregulation of PFKFB3, GSEA revealed the upregulation of a series of genes involved in glycolysis/gluconeogenesis in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-treated fibroblasts, and these genes were inhibited by liraglutide (Fig.  b). DEG analysis revealed that these genes included Ldha, Hk2, Pkm, etc. (Fig.  c). Coincidently, TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced increases in the intracellular and extracellular lactate levels (Fig.  d, e), and the basal and maximal GlycoPER (Fig.  f) were also significantly decreased upon liraglutide treatment.
 In activated fibroblasts, enhanced glycolysis under aerobic conditions has been reported to interfere with mitochondrial respiration (oxidative phosphorylation) [ ]. Therefore, the effects of liraglutide on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation were determined by oxygen consumption rate (OCR) analysis. TGF-β1 + IL-1β significantly boosted mitochondrial respiration, including basal respiration, maximal respiration and ATP-linked respiration in fibroblasts (Fig.  g). However, this effect was partially reversed by liraglutide (Fig.  g), indicating that GLP-1R activation effectively maintained mitochondrial homeostasis under inflammatory stress.
The effects of activating GLP-1R on TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced histone lactylation in fibroblasts were determined by western blotting. Liraglutide blocked TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated histone lactylation, i.e., Pan-Kla, H3K18la and H4K12la in fibroblasts (Fig.  h). It has been reported histone lactylation, especially H3K18la, can promote gene transcription including numerous pro-fibrotic genes [ , , ]. Therefore, ChIP‒qPCR was applied to analyze the enrichment of H3K18la at the promoter regions of Acta2 and Col1a1 in fibroblasts. Our data demonstrated that TGF-β1 + IL-1β stimulation resulted in H3K18la enrichment at the promoters of Acta2 and Col1a1 (Fig. 6i), subsequently leading to α-SMA and collagen I mRNA transcription (Fig.  j). However, these effects of TGF-β1 + IL-1β were inhibited by liraglutide (Fig.  i, j). Together, these data suggested that activating GLP-1R alleviated glycolysis, at least partially by the suppression of PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, and attenuated lactate-mediated histone lactylation, which in turn suppressed the transcription of profibrotic genes in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-challenged fibroblasts.

## Activating GLP-1R suppresses exogenous lactate-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation and histone lactylation in fibroblasts
Extracellular acidosis has been reported to directly trigger or augment NLRP3 inflammasome activation [ , ], and our results showed that the release of lactate was highly enhanced in activated fibroblasts. Therefore, whether lactate can promote fibroblast activation via NLRP3 inflammasome was investigated by exogenous lactate treatment and NLRP3 knockdown. Western blotting revealed that lactate (20 mM) significantly upregulated the expression of collagen I, ASC and caspase-1 p20, which was suppressed by NLRP3 knockdown (Fig.  a). NLRP3 knockdown also reduced lactate-induced IL-1β production (Fig.  b). These results demonstrated that lactate promoted fibroblast activation by activating NLRP3 inflammasome.
 We next investigated the effects of liraglutide on lactate-induced fibroblast activation. Exogenous lactate treatment caused a surge in the intracellular lactate level in fibroblasts, which was not affected by the treatment of liraglutide (Fig.  c). Neither exogenous lactate nor liraglutide affected the expression of PFKFB3 (Fig.  d). However, liraglutide effectively inhibited lactate-induced upregulation of collagen I, NLRP3, ASC and caspase-1 p20 (Fig.  d, e). A notable cellular feature of NLRP3 inflammasome activation is the formation of ASC specks, which reflects the assembly of NLRP3 inflammasome. Immunofluorescence staining showed an increased ASC speck formation upon lactate treatment in fibroblasts, which was reduced by liraglutide (Fig.  f), Moreover, the overproduction of IL-1β and IL-18 upon lactate treatment were decreased by liraglutide (Fig.  g). These results suggested that GLP-1R activation inhibited fibroblast activation by suppressing lactate-induced NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and activation.
Exogenous lactate treatment also stimulated histone lactylation (H3K18la and H4K12la), which was inhibited by liraglutide (Fig.  h). The acetyltransferase p300 is recognized as a “writer” of histone lactylation [ ]. Western blotting revealed that exogenous lactate significantly upregulated the expression of p300, while this effect of lactate was attenuated in the presence of liraglutide (Fig.  h). Additionally, ChIP‒qPCR demonstrated that liraglutide reduced H3K18la enrichment at the Acta2 and Col1a1 promoters induced by exogenous lactate (Fig.  i) and thereby decreased exogenous lactate-induced mRNA transcription of α-SMA and collagen I (Fig.  j). Taken together, activating GLP-1R not only protected fibroblasts against lactate-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation, but also inhibited histone lactylation by reducing the expression of p300 thereafter suppressing the transcription of profibrotic genes.

## Activating GLP-1R antagonizes the profibrotic effects of conditioned media from silica-stimulated macrophages
Macrophage-fibroblast interactions play a critical role in fibroblast activation and fibrosis development and progression [ ]. In this study, the effects of silica-stimulated macrophages on NLRP3 inflammasome activation, PFKFB3-mediated glycolysis, and histone lactylation in fibroblasts were investigated by using macrophage-conditioned media (MCM). MH-S cells were stimulated with different concentrations of silica particles. CCK-8 assay indicated that macrophage viability significantly decreased at silica concentrations greater than 50 µg/cm2 (Fig.  a). Exposure to silica particles (50 µg/cm2) markedly increased the levels of TGF-β1, IL-1β, and extracellular lactate in the MCM (Fig.  b, c). When cultured with silica-stimulated MCM, fibroblasts exhibited pronounced overexpression of NLRP3, caspase-1 p20, PFKFB3 and collagen I (Fig.  d). However, these effects of silica-stimulated MCM on lung fibroblasts were suppressed by liraglutide (Fig.  d). Similarly, silica-stimulated MCM-induced intracellular lactate overload (Fig.  e) and histone lactylation (H3K18la and H4K12la) (Fig.  f) in fibroblasts were effectively suppressed by liraglutide. Moreover, qRT‒PCR showed that the silica-stimulated MCM-induced transcription of the fibrotic genes α-SMA, collagen I and Fn was also inhibited by liraglutide (Fig.  g). Therefore, activating GLP-1R in fibroblasts could antagonize macrophage-derived signal-triggered fibroblast activation by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation, PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and histone lactylation.
 

## Discussion
Silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis is a typical devastating pulmonary fibrotic disease worldwide [ ]. Since no effective therapy to delay or reverse silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis is available, there is an urgent need to elucidate its pathogenesis and explore effective therapeutic targets for this disease. In the present study, we confirmed that activating GLP-1R with liraglutide attenuated silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis in vivo and that TGF-β1 + IL-1β induced lung fibroblast activation in vitro. Using pharmacological approaches, we investigated the interaction between the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, which involves TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblast activation. GLP-1R activation exerted antifibrotic effects by blocking the interaction between the NLPR3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, leading to the inhibition of lactate production and subsequent histone lactylation, and thereby decreasing profibrotic gene expression in fibroblasts under inflammatory stress.
Inhalation of crystalline silica is a widely accepted rodent model used for understanding the mechanism of pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Within the past decade, the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages in the development of silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis has been well recognized [ ]. Emerging evidence indicates that NLRP3 inflammasome is involved in the TGF-β1-induced activation of lung fibroblasts [ , ]. Moreover, it is well known that IL-1β activates NLRP3 inflammasome via IL-1R-mediated NF-κB activation [ ]. Herein, we showed that TGF-β1 + IL-1β markedly induced fibroblast proliferation and differentiation by activating NLRP3 inflammasome. Notably, NLRP3 inflammasome activation often results in proinflammatory programmed cell death called pyroptosis, which mainly occurs in immune cell subsets, such as macrophages, monocytes and neutrophils. GSDMD-NT is a major effector of NLRP3/caspase-1/GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis [ ]. A recent study demonstrated that NLRP3/caspase-1/GSDMD-mediated pyroptosis in fibroblasts is relatively limited compared with that in macrophages, which express high levels of NLRP3, caspase-1 p20 and GSDMD-NT under LPS plus ATP stress, a strong NLRP3 inflammasome activator and pyroptotic trigger. Moreover, prolonged exposure (more than 20 h) to LPS plus ATP did not induce significant cell death in fibroblasts [ ]. Consistently, the present study revealed that TGF-β1 + IL-1β significantly activated NLRP3 inflammasome and induced fibroblast activation, but did not affect the protein level of GSDMD-NT. Moreover, suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome with MCC950 markedly inhibited TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced fibroblast activation. These findings indicated that TGF-β1 + IL-1β was sufficient to induce fibroblast activation by activating NLRP3 inflammasome, but insufficient to cause NLRP3/caspase-1/GSDMD-mediated fibroblast pyroptosis, while the underlying mechanism remains to be further explored. Previous studies have shown that NLRP3- or ASC-deficient mice are resistant to silica-induced pulmonary inflammation, collagen deposition and fibrosis progression [ ]. Recently, pharmacological inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome was reported to alleviate PM2.5- or silica-induced pulmonary inflammation and fibrosis in mice [ , ]. This preclinical evidence suggests that the NLRP3 inflammasome could be a promising therapeutic target for pulmonary fibrosis. However, it should be noted that NLRP3 deficiency causes undesirable infection in mice [ ], and MCC950 was not recommended in a phase II clinical trial for rheumatoid arthritis due to its liver toxicity [ ].
In addition to NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated chronic inflammation, metabolic reprogramming, particularly aberrant aerobic glycolysis, has been implicated in the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis. Increasing evidence has shown that several glycolytic enzymes are key players in regulating the activity of lung fibroblasts/myofibroblasts in vitro or accelerating the development and progression of fibrosis in vivo [ , – ]. PFKFB3 is a critical glycolytic enzyme that converts fructose-6-phosphate (F-6-P) into fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (F-2, 6-BP), which in turn allosterically activates 6-phosphofructo-1-kinase (PFK1). PFK1 is one of the three rate-limiting enzymes for glycolysis and enhances glycolysis by increasing fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (F-1, 6-BP) biosynthesis and glucose uptake [ ]. Thus, the upregulation of PFKFB3 is associated with enhanced glycolysis. The overexpression of PFKFB3 has been found not only in cultured lung fibroblasts stimulated by inflammatory cytokines [ , , ], but also in myofibroblasts from IPF patients [ ] and in lung tissues from different animal models of pulmonary fibrosis [ , ]. Moreover, inhibition of PFKFB3 by 3PO has been shown to exert anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects on experimental lung fibrosis in mice [ ]. Consistently, our data showed that TGF-β1 + IL-1β significantly enhanced lactate production in fibroblasts by upregulating PFKFB3 expression, which could be inhibited by 3PO. Therefore, suppressing PFKFB3-driven excessive glycolysis could be a promising therapeutic strategy for pulmonary fibrosis. However, no glycolysis-targeting drugs are currently available for clinical antifibrotic therapies [ ]. Moreover, despite the encouraging preclinical performance of 3PO, its clinical evaluation has been delayed due to several challenges, including poor solubility, low selectivity, and the necessity for high dosages to achieve therapeutic potency [ ].
In addition to its intrinsic role in catalysing glycolysis, PFKFB3 was recently reported to be involved in NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated inflammation, oxidative stress, and pyroptosis during sepsis [ ]. In our current study, inhibiting PFKFB3 with 3PO not only repressed glycolytic lactate production but also potently inhibited the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-activated lung fibroblasts. Previous studies confirmed that lactate was required for fibroblast activation in a pH-dependent manner [ ]. Moreover, lactate-induced intracellular acidification is essential for NLRP3 inflammasome activation [ , ]. In this study, exogenous lactate markedly increased intracellular lactate level, and activated NLRP3 inflammasome in fibroblasts. Notably, liraglutide significantly inhibited lactate-induced NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and activation. Thus, our data suggested that PFKFB3-driven glycolysis could facilitate NLRP3 inflammasome activation by supplying excessive production of lactate in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-activated fibroblasts. The NLRP3 inflammasome has also been reported to modulate glycolysis by increasing PFKFB3 in an IL-1β-dependent manner [ ]. In fibroblasts, inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome with MCC950 downregulated TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced PFKFB3 expression and lactate production. In addition, NLRP3 knockdown also decreased PFKFB3 expression, indicating that NLRP3 inflammasome activation contributed to PFKFB3-driven glycolysis. Together, these data indicated an agonistic interaction between NLRP3 inflammasome activation and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, which accelerated fibroblast activation under the stress of profibrotic cytokines. Therefore, breaking down the interaction between NLRP3 inflammasome and glycolysis could be a new strategy for preventing fibroblast activation and decelerating fibrosis development.
GLP-1 analogs and GLP-1R agonists, such as liraglutide or semaglutide, have gained prominence in the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity with an established safety profile and tolerability [ ]. Beyond glycemic control, activating GLP-1R exerts anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects on diverse fibrotic diseases, such as renal fibrosis, cardiac fibrosis, liver fibrosis and BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis [ , ]. Notably, activating GLP-1R by liraglutide has been reported to exert anti-inflammatory effects by targeting NLRP3 inflammasome. For instance, a recent study illustrated that liraglutide effectively alleviated asthmatic airway inflammation via the suppression of NLRP3 inflammasome activation and IL-1β secretion in high-fat diet-induced obese asthmatic mice [ ]. Similarly, liraglutide has shown promise in attenuating lipopolysaccharide-induced acute lung injury by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activity [ ]. Our current study showed that the activation of GLP-1R by liraglutide attenuated silica-induced lung fibrosis in mice and suppressed TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced activation of cultured lung fibroblasts. Both in vivo and in vitro studies demonstrated that liraglutide treatment inhibited the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in the lungs of pulmonary fibrosis model mice and in profibrotic cytokine-challenged fibroblasts. Given the importance of NLRP3 inflammasome in the pathogenesis of fibrotic disease, the antifibrotic effects of GLP-1R could partially be attributed to its inhibitory potential against NLRP3 inflammasome activation.
Increasing evidence suggests that GLP-1R plays a critical role in maintaining cell metabolism homeostasis by improving mitochondrial function [ , ]. In pancreatic β-cells [ ] and ischaemic coronary vascular cells [ ], activating GLP-1R promoted mitochondrial respiration, while in astrocytes, GLP-1R activation suppressed mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation stimulated by β-amyloid peptides [ ]. In our study, liraglutide prevented the TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced augmentation of mitochondrial respiration in fibroblasts, indicating that activating GLP-1R could maintain mitochondrial homeostasis under inflammatory stress. The effect of GLP-1R activation on glycolysis is also multifaceted. Activating GLP-1R has been reported to enhance glycolysis in insulin-resistant neurons [ ], ischemic rat hearts [ ], and natural killer cells from obese individuals [ ]; However, activating GLP-1R has also been reported to suppress glycolysis in the early stage of myocardial infarction [ ], T cells [ ] and human neuroblastoma cells [ ]. Notably, GLP-1R deletion in primary mouse astrocytes results in subtle mitochondrial dysfunction accompanied by a compensatory enhancement of glucose transporter-1-mediated aerobic glycolysis [ ]. In the present study, liraglutide effectively inhibited PFKFB3-driven glycolysis in TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblasts and silica-induced fibrotic lungs, as well as the enhancement of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in fibroblasts, indicating that activating GLP-1R was sufficient to maintain metabolic homeostasis during pulmonary fibrosis.
Based on the above findings, both the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis are involved in silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis and TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated fibroblast activation. Pharmacological inhibition of the NLRP3 inflammasome suppressed PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and vice versa, resulting in decreased lactate production, which indicated an interaction between the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis in activated lung fibroblasts. We speculate that this intricate interaction potentially serves as a novel mechanism of fibrosis development, but further explorations using genetic approaches are needed to validate this hypothesis. Moreover, GLP-1R activation achieved therapeutic effects through the dual inhibition of the interaction between NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, and ultimately alleviated pulmonary inflammation and glycolytic lactate production. Notably, metabolic reprogramming has been implicated in the pathological features of fibroblast/myofibroblast activation. TGF-β-induced increases in glycolysis and mitochondrial respiration promote fibroblast activation and matrix production [ ]. In the present study, enhancement of GlycoPER and OCR demonstrated that TGF-β1 + IL-1β-stimulated glycolysis provided pyruvate not only for the generation of lactate in the cytosol, but also for the augmentation of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. Activating GLP-1R significantly inhibited the TGF-β1 + IL-1β-induced increase in GlycoPER and OCR, indicating that GLP-1R may be an important target for maintaining metabolic homeostasis in activated fibroblasts during pulmonary fibrosis.
In addition to serving as a metabolite and signaling molecule, lactate has been verified as a substrate for histone lactylation, a novel post-translational modification regulated by glycolytic reprogramming [ ]. Recent studies have demonstrated that histone lactylation plays a critical role in fibrosis progression by promoting histone lactylation at the promoters of profibrotic genes. Increased histone lactylation has been reported in both IPF lungs and BLM-induced fibrotic lungs [ ]. Moreover, conditioned media from activated fibroblasts and exogenous lactate have been shown to trigger the expression of profibrotic genes in macrophages by inducing histone lactylation [ ]. Similarly, hypoxia-induced lactate production and histone lactylation in human preeclamptic placentas have been shown to lead to an increase in H3K18la at the Fn gene promoter, thereby exacerbating placental fibrosis [ ]. A recent report showed that HK-2-mediated H3K18la enrichment at the promoter of the Acta2 gene in TGF-β1-induced hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) was a prerequisite for HSC activation and liver fibrosis [ ]. In the present study, increased levels of Pan-Kla, H3K18la and H4K12la were detected not only in silica-induced fibrotic lungs, but also in TGF-β1 + IL-1β- or exogenous lactate-stimulated fibroblasts; notably, these levels substantially decreased after treatment with liraglutide. Additionally, we showed that enrichment of H3K18la at the promoters of Acta2 and Col1a1 contributed to fibroblast activation by facilitating fibrosis-related gene expression. These results indicated that histone lactylation was an important driving force for fibrosis both in vivo and in vitro. Moreover, activating GLP-1R effectively prevented histone lactylation in response to profibrotic stimuli. As discussed above, this function of GLP-1R could be partially attributed to its role in regulating glucose metabolism to inhibit lactate overproduction. Notably, the GLP-1R agonist liraglutide suppressed exogenous lactate-induced upregulation of the acetyltransferase p300, which has been identified as a “writer” of histone lactylation by transferring the lactyl group from lactyl-CoA to specific histone lysine residues [ ]. Thus, activating GLP-1R could also reduce histone lactylation by inhibiting the expression of p300. However, the mechanism by which GLP-1R regulates the expression of p300 needs to be further investigated.
The inhaled silica particles can be directly phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages (AMs), and thereby triggers NLRP3 inflammasome activation and IL-1β production, which results in a cascade release of proinflammatory and profibrotic cytokines, i.e., IL-6, tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α) and TGF-β1. These cytokines lead to the progression of inflammation and cell-cell communication between macrophages and fibroblasts, resulting in fibroblast activation, which is a hallmark of pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. Therefore, lung fibroblasts treated with profibrotic factors constitute a well-established in vitro cell model that is widely utilized to investigate the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis. Consistently, our study demonstrated that exogenous TGF-β1 + IL-1β induced fibroblast activation, and this effect could be inhibited by liraglutide. Previous studies also shown that conditioned media from macrophages exposed to silica particles promoted fibroblast activation [ , ]. Recently, it was reported that macrophage-derived TNF-α enhanced PFKFB3-driven glycolysis and lactate production in fibroblasts [ ]. In our study, silica particles increased IL-1β and TGF-β1 secretion, as well as lactate production in cultured macrophages. Conditioned media from silica-treated macrophages (MCM) was sufficient to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, and lactate-mediated histone lactylation in cultured fibroblasts. However, these effects of MCM were inhibited by liraglutide. Therefore, these results demonstrated an inflammation-metabolism interactions between macrophages and fibroblasts in silica-induced fibrosis, while this interaction could be disrupted by activating GLP-1R.
Although our study identified GLP-1R as a key therapeutic target in lung fibroblasts during silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis, the antifibrotic effect of GLP-1R activation may not be solely attributable to targeting fibroblasts for the prevention of silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis. In addition to fibroblasts, many other lung cells, such as immune cells and epithelial cells, participate in the pathology of pulmonary fibrosis via multiple mechanisms. A more expansive investigation will be required to define the antifibrotic role of GLP-1R in other microenvironmental cell types in this setting of pulmonary fibrosis.

## Conclusion
In summary, we revealed that activating GLP-1R inhibited fibroblast activation in vitro and ameliorated silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis in vivo. GLP-1R activation exerted antifibrotic effects by disrupting the interaction between the NLRP3 inflammasome and PFKFB3-driven glycolysis, and subsequently inhibiting lactate-mediated histone lactylation to reduce the expression of profibrotic genes in activated lung fibroblasts (Fig. ). Together, our findings indicate that GLP-1R is a promising therapeutic target for pulmonary fibrosis.
