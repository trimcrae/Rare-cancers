## Objective
The aim of this study was to investigate the mechanism by which quercetin (Que) affects apoptosis and autophagy in pediatric acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) cells by inhibiting the activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway through the regulation of the miR-224-3p/PTEN axis.

## Methods
Blood samples were collected from AML children and healthy volunteers. miR-224-3p and PTEN expression levels were measured. AML cells were pre-treated with Que. MiR-224-3p and PTEN expression levels in AML cells were altered via plasmid transfection. After intervention, PI3K/AKT phosphorylation, AML cell proliferation and apoptosis, concentrations of interleukin-1 β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) in AML cell culture supernatant, apoptosis-related genes Bax and Bcl-2, and autophagy markers LC3-I and LC3-II were tested. The targeting relationship between miR-224-3p and PTEN was identified.

## Results
MiR-224-3p expression was elevated in AML children, while PTEN was decreased. Que was available to accelerate AML cell apoptosis and restrain its autophagy. Que inhibited miR-224-3p expression and promoted PTEN expression. Upregulating miR-224-3p or downregulating PTEN weakened the effect of Que on AML cell apoptosis and autophagy. MiR-224-3p negatively modulated PTEN expression. Up-regulation of PTEN reversed the effects of up-regulation of miR-224-3p on apoptosis and autophagy in AML cells. In addition, Que inhibited PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation, while up-regulation of miR-224-3p or down-regulation of PTEN could attenuate the inhibitory effect of Que on PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Moreover, up-regulation of PTEN reversed the effect of up-regulation of miR-224-3p on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.

## Conclusion
Que affects apoptosis and autophagy in pediatric AML cells by inhibiting PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation through regulation of miR-224-3p/PTEN axis.

## Introduction
Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) is an aggressive haematological malignancy characterised by reduced proliferation and arrested differentiation of myeloid progenitor cells in the bone marrow [ ]. The incidence of AML is elevated with age, taking up 20% of all leukemias in childhood [ ]. In the occurrence of AML, immature myeloblasts recur multiple times as a result of genetic and epigenetic changes that lead to abnormal proliferation [ ]. Conventional chemotherapy has a lot of side effects and is prone to drug resistance in addition to a painful treatment process [ ]. Consequently, it is an urgent to develop novel therapy strategies for AML.
Quercetin (Que), a biological flavonoid extracted from plants, has anticancer activities in multiple tumors [ ]. As reported, Que is available to constrain cancer cell growth [ ]. Que stimulates AML cell apoptosis via suppressing vascular endothelial growth factor/AKT pathway [ ]. MicroRNA (miRNA), a group of short non-coding single stranded RNAs, negatively modulates mRN) via targeting the 3’-untranslated region of mRNA [ ]. miRNAs perform as oncogenes or tumor suppressors to mediate the malignant progression of solid and hematologic tumors [ ]. For instance, miR-485-5p constrains AML cell proliferation via targeting interferon regulatory factor 2 [ ]. MiR-224-3p, a newly discovered miRNA, exerts a crucial role in some human cancers [ ]. For instance, miR-224-3p restrains cervical cancer cell autophagy via targeting FIP200 [ ]. Nevertheless, the role of miR-224-3p in AML remains unknown.
In this study, novel latent molecular mechanisms of Que in AML was explored. The molecular mechanism by which Que regulates AML cell apoptosis and autophagy via miR-224-3p to target phosphatase and tensin homologue (PTEN) was uncovered, proposing a novel therapeutic strategy for AML.

## Materials and methods

## Clinical sample collection
Forty diagnosed pediatric patients with AML were selected from The Affiliated Taizhou People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (No.202006TZ25). There were 21 male and 19 female patients, all of whom were under 18 years old. All AML patients were diagnosed using Wright Giemsa staining smears and morphological evaluation of immunophenotypes by flow cytometry. The exclusion criteria were as follows: Heart, liver and kidney dysfunction, infection, autoimmune diseases, and other malignancies. According to the French-American-British (FAB) classification system [ ], there were no case with AML M1, 15 cases with AML M2, 1 case with AML M3, 10 cases with AML M4, 7 cases with AML M5, 2 cases with AML M6, and 5 cases with AML M7, respectively. Patients received 10 days of induction chemotherapy, and the dose of chemotherapy was adjusted according to the bone marrow response on day 7. In addition, 40 healthy children of similar age were selected as the control group in this study, who had no clinical symptoms of cancer, liver, joint, metabolic or endocrine diseases, and whose bone marrow morphology was detected by cytology and histology. Peripheral blood (5 mL) from each subject was extracted. Once after the blood collection, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated by Ficoll-Hypaque (Lymphodex, inno-Train) density gradient centrifugation at 400 g for 20 min. The research proposal was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Taizhou People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. Informed consent was obtained from all patients (or guardians) before sampling.

## Cell culture and treatment
Human AML cell lines HL-60 and KG-1a were purchased (The Institute of Hematology, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences, Beijing, China). AML cells were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 (Invitrogen, NY, USA) replenished with 10% inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Invitrogen), penicillin (100 U/mL)/streptomycin (100 µg) and 2 mM L-glutamine. For Que treatment, cells were seeded at 5 × 105 cells/mL one day before the experiment and incubated with different concentrations of Que (25, 50 or 75 µmol/L). Control cells were cultured with 0.1% Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO).

## Cell transfection
miR-224-3p inhibitor, inhibitor negative control (NC), miR-224-3p mimic, and mimic NC were purchased (GenePharma, Shanghai, China). pcDNA 3.1-PTEN, pcDNA 3.1-NC, siRNA targeting PTEN (si-PTEN), and siRNA-NC (si-NC) were obtained (Ribobio, Guangzhou, China). HL-60 cells (5 × 105 cells/well) were cultured in 6-well plates and transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen). The transfection efficacy was evaluated by RT-qPCR.

## Cell viability assay
Cell viability was monitored by CCK-8 assay. In brief, cells (5 × 103 per well) were incubated in 96-well plates for 72 h. Subsequently, CCK-8 solution (Dojindo, Japan) was added to each well, followed by 4-h incubation. Absorbance at 490 nm was measured on a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

## Cell colony formation assay
The cells were cultured in 24-well plates in RPMI-1640 medium containing 1.6% methyl cellulose (Sigma, USA) for 7–10 days. After staining with 0.1% crystal violet (Solarbio, Beijing, China), the number of colonies (containing ≥ 40 cells) was counted and the efficiency of colony formation was assessed [ ].

## Flow cytometry
Flow cytometry analysis is based on the translocation of phosphatidylserine from the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane to the cell surface in early apoptotic cells [ ]. Briefly, cells were resuspended in a binding buffer. Next, annexin V-FITC solution and PI solution (BD Bioscience, USA) were added and the solution was incubated at room temperature for 15 min in the dark, followed by assay on FACScan (Becton Dickinson, NJ, USA). The percentage of apoptosis was computed using Cell-Quest software (Becton Dickinson). Cells in the left upper quadrant Q1 are necrotic cells (AnnexinV-FITC−/PI+). Cells in the upper right quadrant Q2 are late apoptotic cells (AnnexinV-FITC+/PI+). Cells in the lower right quadrant Q3 are early apoptotic cells (AnnexinV-FITC+/PI−). Cells in the lower left quadrant Q4 are living cells (AnnexinV−FITC-/PI−). The apoptosis rate is the sum of Q2 and Q3 quadrant percentages.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The concentrations of interleukin-1 β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) in HL-60 cell culture supernatant were measured were measured using ELISA kits (Solarbio, Beijing, China).

## Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Extraction of miRNA and mRNA was conducted using mirVana™ miRNA Isolation Kit (Ambion) and RNEasy Kit (Qiagen), respectively. The quantity of the extracted RNA was confirmed by Nanodrop ND-1000 (Optical Density (OD) 260/280 nm ratio > 1.8). SuperScript III first-strand Synthesis SuperMix (Life Technologies) or TaqMan MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems) was used to prepare cDNA, and measurement was implemented via SYBR Mastermix (Applied Biosystems) or TaqMan Universal (Applied Biosystems). U6 and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) were loading controls. Primer sequences were presented in Table  .

 

## Western blot
Extraction of total protein from cells was performed with radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer. After quantification via bicinchoninic acid, the proteins was separated via sulfate polyacrylamide gel electropheresis and electroblotted onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes at 250 mA. After treatment with 5% skim milk, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies including PTEN, p-phosphoinositide-3-kinase (PI3K), p-AKT, LC3-I, LC3-II, and GAPDH (1: 1000; Cell Signaling Technology). The membrane was then incubated with horseradish peroxidase coupled secondary antibodies (1: 10000). Ultimately, the membrane was treated with enhanced chemiluminescence reagent and developed.

## The luciferase activity assay
The 3-UTR fragments of PTEN containing the binding site of miR-224-3p was predicted by starBase and inserted into the pMIR-Report vector (Ambion Inc., Austin, TX, USA) to construct PTEN-WT. The binding site was mutated to construct the luciferase reporter PTEN-MUT. When HL-60 cells (5 × 104) were grown to 80% confluence in 24-well plates, co-transfection of miR-224-3p mimic or mimic NC (100 nM) was conducted with reporter plasmid (100 ng) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). After transfection of 48 h, luciferase activity was determined using the luciferase kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Cells transfected with 100 ng pMIR-control vector were used to detect the transfection efficiency.

## Xenograft tumor
BALB/c nude mice (6-week-old, male) were purchased from Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology (Beijing, China). HL-60 cells (5 × 106 cells) were subcutaneously injected into nude mice. One week after tumor implantation, mice were intraperitoneally injected with Que (10 mg/kg/d) or 0.1% DMSO (control group) for one week continuously. Five weeks after tumor implantation, all mice were euthanized by inhalation of 5% isoflurane (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), after which the tumors were weighed and collected for subsequent experiments. The length and width of the subcutaneous tumor was measured by the digital caliper (Guanglu, Guilin, China), and the volume of subcutaneous tumor was calculated: volume = (length × width × width)/2. Animal experiments were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for Care and Use of Laboratory Animal and were approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Taizhou People’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (No.202103TZ08).

## Statistical analysis
All data were analyzed via SPSS 21.0 statistical software (SPSS, Inc, Chicago, IL, USA). Measurement data were shown in the form of mean ± standard deviation (SD). The normality distribution and the homogeneity of variance were evaluated using the Shapiro-Walk test and the Levene’s test, respectively. The P > 0.05 indicated that the assumption of normality of data and homogeneity of variance was consistent, and further parameter testing could be performed. Two-group comparison of measurement data subject to normal distribution was performed via t-test, and comparison among multiple groups was implemented via one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Tukey’s post-hoc test. The correlation between miR-224-3p and PTEN in clinical samples was analyzed by Pearson correlation. All experiments were done in triplicate. P < 0.05 was accepted as indicative of significant differences.

## Results

## Que increases AML cell apoptosis and constrains AML cell autophagy
To explore Que’s action in AML, HL-60 cells were treated with different concentrations of Que (25, 50 or 75 µmol/L). As determined, Que treatment reduced HL-60 cell viability and proliferation and enhanced apoptosis. Moreover, Que reduced IL-1β and TNF-α levels dose-dependently (Fig.  A-D). Meanwhile, Que distinctly elevated pro-apoptotic protein Bax and declined anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 in HL-60 cells dose-dependently (Fig.  E). Que reduced the ratio of LC3-II/LC3-I dose-dependently (Fig.  F). In addition, similar results were found in the AML cell line KG-1a (Supplementary Fig.  A-F).

 

## Que inhibits mir-224-3p expression and promotes PTEN expression
To assess the effects of miR-224-3p and PTEN on AML disease progression, miR-224-3p and PTEN in PBMCs from children with AML and healthy volunteers were examined, and the results showed that miR-224-3p was significantly elevated in the PBMCs of children with AML (Fig.  A) while PTEN was decreased (Fig.  B). Furthermore, to explore whether que regulates miR-224-3p and PTEN, miR-224-3p and PTEN were examined in HL-60 cells after treatment with different concentrations of Que, and the results showed that Que inhibited miR-224-3p and enhanced PTEN (Fig.  C-E).

 

## Upregulating mir-224-3p weakens the effect of que on AML cell apoptosis and autophagy
To further investigate the effect of miR-224-3p on AML, inhibitor NC, miR-224-3p inhibitor, mimic NC and miR-224-3p mimic were transfected into HL-60 cells treated with Que (75 µmol/L). The transfection results were verified (Fig.  A). Elevating miR-224-3p reversed the effect of Que on HL-60 cell viability, proliferation, apoptosis, levels of IL-1β and TNF-α, Bax and Bcl-2 mRNA expression, and LC3-II/LC3-I ratio (Fig, 3B-G). Inhibiting miR-224-3p enhanced the effect of Que on AML cells (Fig.  B-G).

 

## Downregulating PTEN weakens the effect of que on AML cell apoptosis and autophagy
To further explore the role of PTEN on AML, pcDNA-NC/PTEN and si-NC/PTEN were transfected into HL-60 cells treated with Que (75 µmol/L) and the transfection was verified (Fig.  A, B). The results showed that silencing PTEN reversed the effect of Que on AML cells (Fig.  C-H).

 

## MiR-224-3p negatively modulates PTEN
In AML patients, miR-224-3p and PTEN mRNA expression were negatively correlated (Fig.  A). After silencing miR-224-3p, PTEN expression was elevated (Fig.  B-C). Therefore, we speculated a potential targeting relationship between miR-224-3p and PTEN. Starbase predicted the binding sites between miR-224-3p and PTEN (Fig.  D). Subsequently, to further verify their targeting, luciferase acitivity assay was implemented, elucidating that the co-transfection of PTEN-WT and miR-224-3p mimic reduced HL-60 cell luciferase activity (Fig.  E). In short, miR-224-3p negatively targeted PTEN.

 

## Upregulating PTEN reverses the effect of upregulating mir-224-3p on AML cell apoptosis and autophagy
To test whether Que mediates AML cell apoptosis and autophagy via miR-224-3p/PTEN axis, rescue experiments were performed. The successful transfection of miR-224-3p mimic + pcDNA-NC/PTEN was verified (Fig.  A-B). Experimental results indicated that elevating PTEN inhibited cell development and the production of inflammatory factors (Fig.  C-G). The accelerating effect of miR-224-3p upregulation on cellular autophagy was reversed upon PTEN elevation (Fig.  H).

 

## Que inhibits PI3K/AKT signalling pathway activation by regulating miR-224-3p/PTEN axis
Next, we examined the phosphorylation level of PI3K/AKT in the PTEN downstream signaling pathway, and Western blot results showed (Fig.  A-C) that Que inhibited the phosphorylation level of PI3K/AKT in a dose-dependent manner. Up-regulation of miR-224-3p or down-regulation of PTEN could attenuate the inhibitory effect of Que on the phosphorylation level of PI3K/AKT. Elevated PTEN impaired the effect of miR-224-3p upregulation on PI3K/AKT phosphorylation.

 

## Que inhibits tumor growth in vivo
Finally, we explored the effect of Que on tumor growth in vivo. Que significantly inhibited xenograft tumour volume and weight (Fig.  A-C).

 

## Discussion
AML is an extremely common malignancy with a high mortality rate [ ]. Conventional treatments for AML have cytotoxic and systemic side effects, so there is still a need to find novel therapies that target tumor cells without affecting normal cells [ ]. Natural plant active ingredients are widely used in cancer treatment due to their low cost, low side effects and effectiveness. Que, a polyphenol extracted from plants for the past few years, has anti-cancer activities in multiple solid tumors [ ]. For instance, Que constrains melanoma cell migration and invasion via repressing HGF/c-Met signal [ ]. Que stimulates AML cell apoptosis via targeting pro-apoptotic genes’ epigenetic regulators [ ]. Que stimulates intracellular oxidative stress via activating ERK-mediated apoptosis pathway, thereby inducing AML cell apoptosis [ ]. However, there are generally few studies on the effects of Que on AML, and its latent mechanisms remain largely uncertain. In this study, we found that Que inhibited PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation by regulating the miR-224-3p/PTEN axis, which promotes apoptosis and inhibits cellular autophagy in AML cells.
Que stimulated AML cell apoptosis and restrained autophagy based on the following observations: (1) AML cell growth was suppressed dose-dependently after Que treatment; (2) After Que introduction, pro-apoptotic protein Bax in AML cells was elevated, while anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 was reduced; (3) Que distinctively reduced the ratio of autophagy marker LC3-II/LC3-I. Furthermore, we found that Que significantly inhibited xenograft tumour growth. These results demonstrate the anti-cancer effects of Que in AML. In vivo studies reported that low doses of 50–500 mg/kg per day of Que do not cause relevant adverse effects or carcinogenic effects in animals [ ]. In humans, a phase I clinical study recommended a dose of 1400 mg/m2, i.e., 2.5 g for subjects weighing 70 kg, administered via intravenous infusions for 3 weeks at weekly intervals. High doses exceeding 50 mg/kg (i.e., 3.5 g/70 kg) produced renal toxicity without signs of nephritis or obstructive uropathy [ ]. Other studies found no adverse effects associated with oral administration of Que in a single dose of up to 4 g or after one month with 500 mg twice daily [ ]. In 1999, the International Agency for Research on Cancer concluded that Que is not classified as carcinogenic to humans. In the United States and Europe, supplements of Que are commercially available, and the beneficial effects of Que supplements were reported from clinical trials [ ]. Therefore, despite the lack of clinical studies evaluating the efficacy of Que in AML patients, we continue to believe that Que has potential as a therapeutic agent for AML.
Accumulating evidence suggests that aberrant expression of miRNA is involved in various cancers, including hematologic malignancies [ – ]. It has been shown that miR-224-3p expression is up-regulated in a variety of cancers [ , ]. In this study, miR-224-3p expression was upregulated in AML patients. Que inhibited miR-224-3p expression in AML cells. Down-regulation of miR-224-3p enhanced the promotion of apoptosis and inhibition of autophagy in AML cells by Que, and up-regulation of miR-224-3p attenuated the promotion of apoptosis and inhibition of autophagy in AML cells by Que. In addition, Que promoted PTEN expression in AML cells. Up-regulation of PTEN could enhance the promotion of apoptosis and inhibition of autophagy in AML cells by Que, and down-regulation of PTEN could attenuate the promotion of apoptosis and inhibition of autophagy in AML cells by Que. PTEN, also known as phosphatase and tensin homologue, has been testified to be the critical regulator of PI3K signaling and is a recognized tumor suppressor gene as well [ ]. PTEN is abnormally expressed in multiple cancers [ ]. This study discovered PTEN was silenced in AML, which was consistent with the results of a previous study [ ] and negatively associated with miR-224-3p. Besides, PTEN was elevated after silencing miR-224-3p. There was a targeting relationship between miR-224-3p and PTEN. The targeting relationship between miR-224-3p and PTEN was verified by starbase database and dual luciferase reporter gene assay experiments. Additionally, we also found that up-regulation of PTEN reversed the effects of up-regulation of miR-224-3p on apoptosis and autophagy in AML cells.
The PI3K/AKT pathway participates in diversified biological processes, and its activation is associated with multiple tumor pathogenesis and progression [ ]. For instance, Long non-coding RNA AK023391 boosts gastric cancer invasion via activating the PI3K/Akt pathway [ ]. MiR‑93 knockdown inhibits AML cell growth via inactivating the PI3K/AKT pathway by upregulating DAB2 [ ]. Mutated activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway boosts cell proliferation, DNA repair, survival and chemotherapy resistance in AML [ ]. As reported, targeted repression of co-mutation and therapy-stimulated AKT activation are available to effectively strengthen the sensitivity of AML to chemotherapy [ ]. PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitors have also been testified to stimulate apoptosis and constrain leukemia growth in preclinical models of AML [ ]. In this research, We found that Que inhibited PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activation, whereas up-regulation of miR-224-3p or down-regulation of PTEN attenuated the inhibitory effect of Que on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Moreover, up-regulation of PTEN could reverse the effect of up-regulation of miR-224-3p on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.
However, the present study has limitations. We did not further explore the effects of PI3K/AKT signaling pathway activators or inhibitors on apoptosis and autophagy in AML cells, and we hope that in the future, we can further validate the anticancer effects of the Que/miR-224-3p/PTEN axis in AML by modulating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.

## Conclusion
This study reveals for the first time the molecular mechanism by which Que inhibits the activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway through the regulation of the miR-224-3p/PTEN axis, thereby promoting apoptosis and inhibiting autophagy in AML cells.
