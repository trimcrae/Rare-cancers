## Background
Telomere shortening and RNA pseudo-uridylation are common features of tumors. NOP10 is a member of the H/ACA snoRNP family, essential for maintaining telomerase activity and RNA pseudouridylation. NOP10 has been indicated to be substantially expressed in tumors such as breast and lung cancers and is associated with poor prognosis. Currently, no investigation exists on NOP10 in pancreatic cancer (PC). This is the first investigation to elucidate the impact on tumorigenesis and prognostic value of NOP10 in pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD).

## Method
NOP10 expression and its survival prognostic significance were analyzed via clinical PAAD data from the TCGA database and NOP10 expression in other tumors from the GEPIA database. Furthermore, the NOP10 expression and survival prognosis in clinical samples were validated by qRT-PCR. In-vitro experiments were carried out to elucidate the impact of NOP10 on the biological function of PC cells.

## Results
It was revealed that NOP10 expression was increased in PC tissues than in the normal pancreatic tissues. High NOP10 expression was markedly linked with poorer prognosis. NOP10 may be involved in focal adhesion, channel activity, cAMP signaling pathway, the interaction of neuroactive ligand-receptor, and cell adhesion molecules cams. NOP10 was associated with the tumour immune microenvironment and drug sensitivity. Down-regulation of NOP10 expression suppressed PC cells’ ability to proliferate, migrate, and invade.

## Conclusions
This investigation elucidated the prognostic and predictive importance of NOP10 in PAAD and revealed that NOP10 is associated with poor prognostic features, survival prognosis and TIME. Knockdown of NOP10 inhibits the progression of PAAD.

## Introduction
Pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) is an aggressive digestive system tumor with substantial morbidity and mortality, increasing the human health crisis [ , ]. Due to the asymptomatic onset of PAAD and the difficulty of early diagnosis, most patients have already developed local infiltration or distant metastasis at diagnosis, thereby losing their best chance at surgery [ , ]. In addition, PAAD is insensitive to radiotherapy and prone to chemotherapy resistance [ ]. Clinical drug therapy, including folinic acid, 5-fluo-rouracil, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin (FOLFIRINOX) and gemcitabine hydrochloride with nab-paclitaxel (GA), increased the 5 years survival rate for PAAD by only 9% (from 2 to 11%) [ ]. Additionally, targeted therapy and immunotherapy have failed to achieve satisfactory results. Therefore, it is essential to actively elucidate the molecular mechanisms associated with PAAD progression for its early diagnosis and therapy.
Nop10 is a rare 7 kDa protein, essentially associated with the assembly and function of H/ACA small nucleolar ribonucleoproteins (snoRNPs) [ – ]. Each H/ACA snoRNP comprises a unique snoRNA and 4 markedly conserved core proteins, Dyskerin (DKC1), GAR1, NHP2, and NOP10 [ – ]. H/ACA snoRNPs stabilize and aggregate telomerase RNA, which maintains normal telomerase activity [ , ]. Telomerase stabilizes the telomere length; once telomeres reach a critical length, they stimulate cell cycle arrest by initiating cellular senescence or apoptotic pathways [ , ]. Telomerase enables cells to replicate immortally by maintaining telomere length without reaching a critical length [ , ]. The immortality of cell replication is one of the characteristics of cancer [ ]. Furthermore, H/ACA snoRNP catalyzes the uridine isomerization to pseudo-uridine, the most frequent RNA modification [ – ]. Knockdown of DKC1 reduced pseudouridylation of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) by > 50% [ ]. Aberrant pseudo-uridylation because of NOP10 and DKC1 mutations causes nephrotic syndrome with hearing impairment, cataracts, and enterocolitis [ ]. Moreover, increased NOP10 expression was linked with poor breast and lung cancers prognosis [ , ]. Altogether, enhanced H/ACA snoRNPs function and expression may promote cancer pathogenesis.
However, NOP10 expression and its prognostic value in PAAD remains undetermined. This investigation elucidates the impact of NOP10 on tumorigenesis and its prognostic value in PAAD for the first time. Bioinformatics data mining and analysis were performed on the clinical significance of NOP10 from public databases. The above analysis results were validated in a clinical sample that was followed up. In-vitro experiments explored the influence of NOP10 on the biological function of PAAD. This investigation suggested that high NOP10 expression might indicate poor PAAD prognosis, and its deficiency suppresses the PAAD cell’s ability to proliferate, migrate, and invade.

## Materials and methods

## Bioinformatics analysis
For bioinformatics assessment, the data were acquired from Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/), and Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/) databases. The TCGA and GTEx datasets were downloaded using UCSC Xena (http://xena.ucsc.edu/).

## Expression of NOP10 in pan-carcinoma and PAAD
The NOP10 mRNA expression in the PAAD and normal tissues were assessed in TCGA and GTEx datasets. Furthermore, its expression in paired and para-cancer tissue was also elucidated using GSE28735 and GSE62452 in GEO. NOP10 mRNA expression in other tumors was also assessed using the GEPIA data.

## Prognostic survival analysis
For PAAD survival analysis, the data were downloaded from TCGA. Based on the median NOP10 expression values, the patients were categorized into high- and low-expression groups. The Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves were acquired by the R package “survminer” and “Survival.” Lastly, to predict the model’s survival analysis accuracy, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve models at 1, 3, and 5 years were established.

## Diagnostic value analysis
TCGA-PAAD and GTEx normal pancreas tissue data were downloaded from UCSC XENA, ROC assay was carried out utilizing the “pROC” package, and the results were visualized with “ggplot2”.

## Gene enrichment analysis
The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with substantial correlation with NOP10 in the TCGA-PAAD data were screened, and then GO, and KEGG enrichment analyses were carried out.

## Tumor immune microenvironment and drug sensitivity analysis
The data of immune and stromal cell scores were obtained from UCSC and calculated using the R package “ESTIMATE” and “limma.” The Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) systematically and algorithmically assesses immune infiltration in cancer samples from TCGA datasets. Therefore, the TIMER database was utilized for exploring the association between NOP10 and 6 different immune cell infiltration levels, including neutrophils, B, CD4 + T, macrophages, CD8 + T, and dendritic cells. The drug sensitivity processed data was obtained from the CellMiner dataset (https://discover.nci.nih.gov/cellminer/).

## Clinical samples
Sixty-six PAAD and corresponding non-tumor tissues from July 2014 and December 2018 were acquired from the Affiliated Huai’an Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University. This investigation was authorized by the ethical board of the hospital, and participants were first informed about the research, and then their consent was acquired.

## Cell culture
Four human pancreatic cancer (PC) (PANC-1, BxPC-3, CFPAC-1, and MiaPaCa-2) and 1 human pancreatic ductal epithelial (HPNE) cell line were provided by the cell bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China. All the cells were propagated at 37 °C in DMEM augmented with 10% serum and penicillin/streptomycin (100 U/mL, respectively) in a 5% CO2 incubator.

## RNA extraction and RT-qPCR
Whole cellular and tissue RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Vazyme, China), per the kits’ instructions, and subsequently quantified via spectrophotometer. Reverse transcription was performed using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR kit instructions. For qRT-PCR, the LightCycle®96 (ROCHE, Switzerland) was utilized with SYBR Green (Vazyme, China). The human β-actin and 18 S rRNA expression were utilized as a control to assess the original cellular and tissue mRNA concentration, respectively. The NOP10 expression was elucidated via the 2−ΔΔCT protocol.

## siRNA interference
RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) synthesized the NOP10 siRNA sequences and a scrambled siRNA was set as a negative control. For transfection, Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) was used per the manufacturer’s guide. Total RNA was acquired 48 h post-transfection. Table enlists the NOP10 primers and siRNA sequences utilized.

## Functional experiments

## Cell count kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
The CCK-8 kit was utilized for the elucidation of cell proliferation. Briefly, cells (2 × 103/well) were propagated in 96-well plates for 5 days, then CCK-8 reagent (10 µL) and complete medium (100 µL) were mixed and inoculated in each well in the dark for 2 h at 37 °C. Lastly, the absorbance was assessed at 450 nm using Multiskan™ FC Microplate Photometer (ThermoFisher Scientific).

## Ethynyl-20-deoxyuridine (EdU) assay
Cell proliferation rate was analyzed via the BeyoClick™ EdU Cell Proliferation Kit with DAB (Beyotime) per the kits’ instruction. Briefly, cells were inoculated in 96-well plates for 24 h, followed by fixation, washing, permeabilization, reaction, and color development. Cells treated with EdU were imaged and quantified using an Olympus FSX100 microscope (Olympus. Japan).

## Wound healing assay
Cells (8 × 105/well) were inoculated in 6-well plates, and with 100% confluency, the cells were scratched using the tip of a 200ul pipette, washed with PBS, and then continued to be propagated in the basal media. At 0 h and 24 h, each wound was imaged after PBS rinsing to remove the debris. The wound healing rate was calculated using Image J software. The percentage of remaining wound area was calculated using the following formula: % remaining wound area = 24 H wound area/0H wound area×100%. A higher value for the remaining wound area indicates a poorer migration ability.

## Transwell invasion and migration assay
Around 2 × 104 cells were inoculated in the basal media (200 µL) and propagated in the upper Transwell membrane layer. Complete medium (600 µL) was inoculated in a 24-well plate for 24 h at 37 °C in 5% CO2; subsequently, the cells in the upper layer of the membrane were gently washed using a cotton swab, and those in the lower membrane layer were dyed for 30 min using 0.1% crystal violet to elucidate cell migration. For invasion analysis, Matrigel (BD Bioscience Pharmingen) was spread on the upper layer per the manufacturer’s protocol; the remaining protocol was the same as above.

## Immunohistochemical staining
The tissue sections were subjected to a process of deparaffinisation, utilising a solution of xylene, and subsequently dehydrated through a graded alcohol series. Subsequently, the slices were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide and pressure-cooked in order to facilitate antigen retrieval. Subsequently, the slices were incubated with anti-NOP10 primary antibodies from Abcam (Cambridge, UK) at 4 °C overnight, followed by an incubation with the appropriate secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature. Subsequently, the sections were treated with 3’-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride and counterstained with haematoxylin.
The following criteria were employed in order to determine the results of positive NOP10 expression: The semi-quantitative determination of NOP10 expression was conducted according to the proportion of tumour cells exhibiting colour and the intensity of staining. The results were evaluated by means of a double-blind reading and scored in accordance with the degree of chromogenicity, whereby 0 points were assigned to negative results, 1 point to weak staining, 2 points to moderate staining, and 3 points to strong staining. The proportion of positive cells was quantified according to a scoring system. A score of 0 was assigned to samples with less than 10% positive cells, 1 to samples with 10–25% positive cells, 2 to samples with 25–50% positive cells, 3 to samples with 50–75% positive cells, and 4 to samples with greater than 75% positive cells. Degree of positivity score = degree of colour development score ×positive cell rate score. Score ≤ 3 is negative (-), 3 < score ≤ 6 is weakly positive (+), score > 6 is strongly positive (++).

## Western blot analysis
Proteins were extracted from PDAC cells using RIPA buffer, which was supplemented with PMSF, and then mixed with SDS sampling buffer. The resulting solution was then boiled for a period of five minutes. Subsequently, the proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane via electrophoresis. The bands were excised according to the molecular size of the protein, non-specific sites on the membrane were blocked with skimmed milk, and the membranes were incubated separately with the appropriate antibodies overnight at 4 ℃. Subsequently, the membranes were washed three times with TBST buffer. Following this, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding HRP-labelled secondary antibodies for two hours at room temperature, after which they were washed again three times with TBST. Finally, the.
membranes were exposed and visualized with Chemiluminescence HRP Substrate. β-actin was used as an internal reference.

## Animal studies
Five-week-old male nude mice (BALB/c), purchased from GemPharmatech Co.Ltd, were separated into two groups. The animal experiment was approved by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments of Nanjing Medical University (IACUC-1903002). Five mice per group were used to construct subcutaneous tumor formation models. Nude mice were anesthetized by inhalation with 2% isoflurane and the skin was disinfected with 75% alcohol. Stable PANC-1 cell line transfected with NOP10 KD virus and corresponding NC virus were constructed. Each mouse was subcutaneously injected with 1 × 106 cells in 100 µL into the inguinal area. When the tumor became visible, the tumor size was measured from eighth day and sized every 6 days for 32 days, and the formula (width2×length)×0.52 was used to calculate the tumor volume. At the end of the experiment, the nude mice were executed by cervical dislocation under anaesthesia.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical measurements were carried out with the help of GraphPad Prism (version 9.0), SPSS (version 25.0), and R (4.2.1) software. The means ± SD represents the quantitative data. The paired t-test was carried out to compare NOP10 expression in PAAD and matched noncancerous tissues. Two tests determined the relationships between NOP10 expression and clinicopathological characteristics. KM with log-rank tests were performed to plot the survival curves for assessing statistical significance. All the tests were two-tailed, and *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, or ****p < 0.0001 depicted a statistically substantial difference.

## Results

## Expression level of NOP10 and association with prognosis in PAAD
This investigation was the first to identify via bioinformatics analysis that NOP10 mRNA levels were highly expressed in PAAD relative to normal pancreatic tissue in TCGA-PAAD combined with GTEx-pancreas data (Fig.  A). Consistent with paired- and para-cancer tissues in GEO datasets GSE28735 and GSE62452 (Fig.  B). Additionally, it was revealed that in the GEPIA dataset, NOP10 was substantially expressed in GBM, DLBC, OV, CESC, and TGCT, whereas it was reduced in LAML (Fig.  D). Similarly, Similarly, it was found from the HPA database that NOP10 showed the same results at the protein level in pancreatic cancer (Fig.  E and F). To further verify the expression of NOP10 at the protein level in pancreatic cancer, immunohistochemical staining was performed, which demonstrated that the protein expression of NOP10 in pancreatic cancer tissues was significantly higher than that observed in normal pancreatic tissues (Fig.  G,  H, I). The patients were divided into two cohorts based on the median NOP10 expression values. A time-dependent ROC curve model was constructed for 1, 3, and 5 years to predict the model’s survival analysis accuracy. This indicated that NOP10 could effectively predict the survival times of PAAD patients (Fig.  J and K) and has high accuracy as a variable to predict normal and tumour outcomes (Fig.  L). The log-rank test was conducted, which revealed statistically significant intergroup differences in terms of overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (DSS) prognosis. The cohort exhibiting high expression levels indicated a less favourable prognosis (Fig.  M and N). Furthermore, an analysis of the clinical characterisation data revealed a correlation between NOP10 expression and PC sites (Table  ). The data indicated that patients with tumours located in the tail of the pancreas exhibited higher levels of NOP10 expression.

 

To further validate the NOP10 expression in PC and its prognostic value, its expression in 66 PC patients enrolled at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University was evaluated. It was revealed that NOP10 was markedly expressed in PC tissues (Fig.  C), and the high NOP10 expression group corresponded to a poorer prognosis (Fig.  O). Furthermore, the link between NOP10 expression and clinical features of PC was analyzed, but there was no significant correlation (Table ).

 

## GO and KEGG enrichment pathways of NOP10-related DEGs in PAAD
The DEGs with marked links with NOP10 were screened and plotted as a heatmap according to NOP10’s high and low expression (Fig.  A). The GO analysis revealed that the DEGs were substantially enriched in the biological processes of chemical synaptic transmission modulation, synapse organization, trans-synaptic signaling regulation, membrane potential regulation, signal release, and multicellular organismal signaling (Fig.  B). Cellular component alterations were markedly enriched in the neuronal cell body, presynapse, ion and cation channel complex, transmembrane transporter complex, and synaptic membrane (Fig.  B). The molecular function indicated that DEGs were primarily enriched in activities including those of passive transmembrane transporter, ion channel, gated channel, and cation channel (Fig.  B). According to the KEGG pathway assessment, the DEGs enriched the circadian entrainment, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), calcium signaling pathway, cAMP signaling pathway, and focal adhesion (Fig.  C-D).

 

## Correlation with Tumor immune microenvironment and drug sensitivity
Tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) affects tumor growth, metastasis, and response to therapy. Therefore, the association of NOP10 expression with TIME was assessed, and a positive link was observed between NOP10 and stromal and comprehensive scores of PAAD (Fig.  E). According to the TIMER analysis, in PAAD, NOP10 significantly linked with infiltrating levels of neutrophils (r = 0.182, p = 1.72e-02), Purity (r=-0.158, p = 3.83e-02), and dendritic cells (r = 0.184, p = 1.61e-02) (Fig.  F). We explore the relationship between NOP10 expression and drug sensitivity. Utilizing the CellMiner database, we accessed sensitivity data for FDA-approved drugs and those currently in clinical trials. Our analysis demonstrates a distinct correlation between NOP10 expressions and drug sensitivity, as depicted in Fig.  G. NOP10 exhibits a positive correlation with the sensitivity of Asparaginase, Hydroxyurea, and Dexrazoxane, but a negative correlation with Rapamycin.

## Downregulation of NOP10 suppresses pancreatic cancer cells’ ability to proliferate, migrate, and invade in vitro and in vivo
The above findings indicate that alterations in NOP10 expression can result in changes to a range of biological processes and signalling pathway molecules. Furthermore, they suggest that this can affect the TIME and drug sensitivity, thus influencing tumour progression. To gain further insight into the precise manner by which NOP10 influences pancreatic cancer progression, it is first necessary to elucidate the impact of NOP10 on pancreatic cancer cell function. To this end, a series of cell function experiments were conducted. The loss-of-function experiments were carried out to investigate the effect of NOP10 on the proliferation and invasive ability of PC cells. First, we evaluated the expression of NOP10 in PANC-1, MiaPaCa-2, CFPAC-1, BxPC-3, and HPNE. The expression of NOP10 was markedly elevated in PC cells relative to HPNE cells at both the mRNA and protein levels (Fig.  A and D). Firstly, three siRNAs were designed to specifically reduce the expression of NOP10 in MiaPaCa-2 and PANC-1. Subsequently, the two siRNAs with the most effective interference efficiency were selected based on the results of a qRT-PCR assay (Fig.  B-C). Once more, the effectiveness of the interference is confirmed by WB (Fig.  E). Furthermore, we investigated the expression of NOP10 in pancreatic cancer tissues and normal pancreatic tissues using Western blot. The results demonstrated that NOP10 expression was significantly elevated in pancreatic cancer tissues in comparison to normal pancreatic tissues (Fig.  F). EdU and CCK-8 analyses indicated that NOP10 knockdown suppressed the proliferative ability of MiaPaCa-2 and PANC-1 (Fig.  G-J). To further evaluate the inhibitory effect of NOP10 on PDAC in vivo, NOP10 KD and KD NC stably transfected PANC-1 cells were injected subcutaneously in nude mice. The results showed that NOP10 knockdown significantly inhibited tumour growth (Fig.  K). Tumour volume and weight were significantly reduced in the NOP10 knockdown group compared to the control group (Fig.  L-M).

 

Considering that PC is more vulnerable to metastasis, the impact of NOP10 on the invading and migratory abilities of PC cells was explored. Wound healing experiments demonstrated that cell migration ability was significantly attenuated after the down-regulation of NOP10 in MiaPaCa-2 and PANC-1 cells (Fig.  A). Furthermore, NOP10 knockdown alleviated MiaPaCa-2 and PANC-1 cells’ ability to migrate and invade in Transwell assays (Fig.  B). Taken together, these results suggest that NOP10 promotes the PAAD cells’ ability to proliferate, migrate, and invade in-vitro.

 

## Discussion
Telomere shortening and RNA pseudouridylation are common features of tumors, and NOP10, a H/ACA snoRNP family member, essentially maintains telomerase activity and RNA pseudouridylation [ – ]. Balogh E. et al. reported that a defect in pseudouridylation caused by a loss-of-function mutation in NOP10 could result in nephrotic syndrome with hearing loss and cataracts [ ]. Monteagudo et al. found that NOP10 significantly facilitates telomerase-modulated telomere lengthening in PCC and PGL and that NOP10 overexpression is a novel prognostic index for PPGL [ ]. IHC by Khloud A. Elsharawy et al. showed that NOP10 is markedly expressed in breast cancer; its high expression is a prognostic bio-index for poor breast cancer prognosis and can help predict chemotherapy resistance [ ]. Similarly, Chunhong Cui et al. reported that enhanced NOP10 mRNA or protein levels were linked with substandard NSCLC patients’ prognosis [ ]. However, Patricia Carolina Dos Santos et al. showed a significantly alleviated NOP10 expression in CLL [ ].
This investigation assessed NOP10 expression levels in PAAD patients and elucidated its effects on PC cells’ ability to proliferate, migrate, and invade. It is noteworthy that, per our knowledge, this is the first analysis of NOP10 in PC.
Firstly, the gene expression data of PC patients and related clinical data were acquired from TCGA and GTEx databases, and it was found that NOP10 expression was increased in PC tissues than in the normal pancreatic tissues via bioinformatics analysis. Furthermore, this was also validated by paired PC and paracancerous tissues analyses of the GEO dataset. Subsequently, the association of NOP10 expression with clinical PC prognosis was analyzed, which revealed that increased NOP10 expression was markedly linked with poorer prognosis and that NOP10 had high precision as a variable predicting tumor and normal outcome. Moreover, it was indicated that the NOP10 expression was linked with the tumor’s location, validated by RT-qPCR of 66 PC and paracancerous tissues. RT-qPCR revealed that the NOP10 level was significantly elevated in PC tissues, and its high expression corresponded to a poor prognosis, consistent with previous findings. However, no significant correlation between NOP10 and clinical characteristics was observed in the data acquired from this investigation, probably due to insufficient sample size. Altogether, it is hypothesized that NOP10 serves as a diagnostic and prognostic PC index.
The TME of PAAD consists of the cancer cell nest and stroma. The stroma contains various components, primarily the stromal matrix and various cells. The immune system is a complex network of cells and molecules that work together to protect the body from infection and disease. In PAAD, immune cells represent a significant portion of the cellular component, comprising nearly 50% of the total. These cells include neutrophils, CD3 + T cells, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, Treg cells, NK cells, and others. Our research has revealed a significant correlation between NOP10 expression levels and the infiltration of neutrophils, purity, and dendritic cells. These findings may provide a new direction for immunotherapy of tumours. In other words, it would be beneficial to monitor immune checkpoints in patients with high NOP10 expression in order to inform clinical decisions regarding immunotherapy.
Despite the absence of a notable correlation between NOP10 and the chemotherapeutic agents folinic acid, 5-fluorouracil, irinotecan, oxaliplatin, gemcitabine, and paclitaxel, which are frequently employed in pancreatic cancer chemotherapy, our findings revealed a significant correlation between NOP10 and several other compounds (asparaginase, hydroxyurea, dexrazoxane, and rapamycin). Hinze L et al. found that asparaginase had little efficacy in APC or β-catenin mutant CRC, but was profoundly toxic in the setting of R-spondin fusions. Pharmacologic GSK3αinhibition was sufficient for asparaginase sensitization in APC or β-catenin mutant CRC, but not in normal intestinal progenitors [ ]. Hydroxyurea targets proliferating cells and suppresses DNA replication as an inhibitor of ribonucleoside diphosphate reductase [ ]. Dexrazoxane is the sole pharmaceutical agent that has received approval from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the management of doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity [ ]. Rapamycin is a selective inhibitor of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR), and its efficacy has been demonstrated in the treatment of various malignancies, including advanced renal cancer (RCC), progressive pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours (PNET), advanced neuroendocrine carcinomas (NEC), and advanced or recurrent endometrial cancers [ ]. From these, we postulated that these compounds might affect the expression of NOP10, thereby exerting a potential influence on the progression of pancreatic cancer. Further research is required to elucidate the mechanism of action of these compounds.
Furthermore, to understand the specific molecular mechanisms associating NOP10 with PC progression, bioinformatics analysis was carried out, which revealed that NOP10 might be involved in channel activity, neuroactive ligand-receptor communication, cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), cAMP signaling pathway, and focal adhesion. It is possible that these changes may affect cell proliferation, invasion and migration. To further investigate this issue, we conducted pertinent cell function experiments to ascertain whether NOP10 affects the proliferation, invasion, and migration of pancreatic cancer cells. The results of our study indicate that the knockdown of NOP10 significantly inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of MiaPaCa-2 and PANC-1 cells. These findings are consistent with the established functions of NOP10 in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This indicates that NOP10 plays a significant role in tumour pathogenesis. Nevertheless, further investigation is required to elucidate the precise molecular mechanisms of action.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, this research reveals the prognostic and predictive importance of NOP10 in PAAD. NOP10 is associated with poor prognostic features and survival prognosis. Knockdown of NOP10 suppresses PAAD progression. Therefore, NOP10 is suggested to be implemented as a new index for diagnosing PAAD patients to enhance their prognosis for early detection, thereby improving the treatment strategies.
