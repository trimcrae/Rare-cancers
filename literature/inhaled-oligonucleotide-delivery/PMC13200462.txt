## Abstract
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is characterized by persistent oxidative stress and inflammation, for which current antioxidant therapies lack efficiency and targeting. Here, we developed macrophage membrane-biomimetic bimetallic manganese-platinum nanozymes (MM-BiMP NZs) for targeted pulmonary delivery. The synthesized MM-BiMP NZs retained potent scavenging activity against multiple reactive oxygen species (ROS), including hydroxyl radicals, singlet oxygen, and superoxide anions. Using an acute lipopolysaccharide-induced lung injury model, we determined 2.5 mg/kg as a safe and effective dose for intratracheal administration. In a cigarette smoke-induced COPD mouse model, treatment with MM-BiMP NZs significantly attenuated pulmonary oxidative stress, reduced inflammatory cytokine levels, improved lung function, and ameliorated emphysema and airway remodeling. The vitro experiments demonstrated that MM-BiMP NZs were efficiently internalized, mitigated cigarette smoke extract-induced oxidative damage, and suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokine release both in the bronchial epithelial cells and alveolar macrophages. Mechanistically, transcriptomic and biochemical analyses revealed that the therapeutic effects of MM-BiMP NZs were mediated through the inhibition of the ROS-PI3K-AKT signaling pathway. This study presents a novel biomimetic nanoplatform that effectively targets the pulmonary microenvironment, combats oxidative stress, and alleviates experimental COPD, offering a promising strategy for this debilitating disease.

## Introduction
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent respiratory disorder characterized by persistent airflow limitation, primarily driven by chronic airway inflammation and parenchymal lung destruction [ , ]. Globally, COPD remains a major public health burden, representing the third leading cause of death worldwide. Cigarette smoking (CS) is the most significant risk factor, responsible for the majority of all cases [ , ]. Although current treatments—such as bronchodilators and corticosteroids—can provide symptomatic relief and reduce the frequency of acute exacerbations, they fail to effectively halt disease progression or reverse pulmonary function decline [ ]. The limited improvement in clinical outcomes underscores the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies with disease-modifying potential.
The pathogenesis of COPD is multifactorial, involving chronic inflammation, protease-antiprotease imbalance, and oxidative stress [ ]. Among these, oxidative stress plays a central role in disease initiation and progression. Cigarette smoke contains high concentrations of free radicals and oxidants that directly induce excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in airway epithelial cells and alveolar macrophages [ ]. This oxidative burden results in lipid peroxidation, protein modification, and DNA damage, which in turn activate inflammatory signaling pathways such as NF-κB, promoting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-8 and TNF-α, thereby amplifying airway inflammation [ , ]. Furthermore, ROS contribute to disease pathology by inducing apoptosis, disrupting epithelial integrity, and enhancing mucus hypersecretion, collectively accelerating airway remodeling and emphysematous changes [ , ]. Thus, targeted antioxidant interventions have emerged as a promising approach to modulate COPD pathogenesis.
Although conventional antioxidants such as N-acetylcysteine (NAC) exhibit some ROS-scavenging capacity, their clinical efficacy is constrained by short duration of action, limited potency, poor cellular selectivity, and inadequate accumulation at disease sites [ ]. Recent advances in nanomedicine and biomaterials offer new opportunities to enhance the targeting and sustained activity of antioxidant therapies. Manganese-platinum (Mn-Pt) nanoalloys have attracted attention due to their enzyme-mimetic catalytic activity, which enables efficient clearance of multiple ROS species and confers remarkable anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties [ ]. However, their translational potential is limited by inherent shortcomings such as low biocompatibility and insufficient target specificity.
Biomimetic membrane coating technology has shown great promise in improving the targeted delivery of nanotherapeutics [ ]. Macrophage membranes could express specific chemokine receptors like CCR2 that enable innate homing to inflammatory sites, making them ideal materials for constructing targeted nanocarriers [ , ]. By coating Mn-Pt nanoparticles with macrophage membranes, similar to the previous publications [ , ], the resulting biomimetic system can leverage active tropism towards inflamed lung regions in COPD, particularly enhancing drug accumulation around activated macrophages and epithelial cells. This strategy offers the potential to increase local drug concentration and therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.
In the current study, we hypothesize that appropriate intratracheal injection of macrophage membrane-biomimetic bimetallic manganese-platinum nanozymes (MM-BiMP NZs) can achieve targeted delivery to inflammatory lung microenvironments, effectively scavenge ROS, alleviate oxidative stress, and subsequently mitigate inflammatory responses and structural lung damage. This biomimetic nanotherapeutic approach may represent a novel and promising antioxidant strategy for the treatment of COPD.

## Methods

## Characterization
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was recorded using a FEI Tecnai G2 spirit microscope with an accelerating voltage at 100 kV. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurements and zeta potential were performed on a Brookhaven NanoBrook 90Plus PALS. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained with X-ray powder diffraction on Rigaku Ultima IV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) data were obtained on Thermo Scientific K-Alpha+. The electron spin resonance (ESR) spectrometer was measured by a Bruker A300-10/12. The UV-vis absorption spectra were measured using a Shimadzu UV-2600i UV-vis spectrophotometer.

## Synthesis of BiMP NZs and MM-BiMP NZs
As shown in Scheme A, 98 mg of Pt(acac)₂ (0.25 mmol, Adamas, Shanghai, China) and 323 mg of 1,2-hexadecanediol (1.25 mmol, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) were dissolved in 10 mL of benzyl ether (Acros, USA). The solution was heated to 100 °C under a nitrogen atmosphere and maintained for 15 min to ensure complete dissolution of the solids. Then, 195 mg of Mn₂(CO)₁₀ (0.5 mmol, Sigma-Aldrich, USA), 675 µL of oleylamine (Acros, USA), and 650 µL of oleic acid (Alfa Aesar, USA) in 8 mL of benzyl ether (Acros, USA) were injected into the reaction flask, and the reaction mixture was heated to reflux at 297 °C for 1 h. After cooling to room temperature, the product was collected and washed with ethanol, and the product was stored in 8 mL tetrahydrofuran (Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China). We incorporated the hydrophilic group DSPE-PEG-NH₂ (J&K Scientific, Beijing, China) onto the nanozyme surface to enhance its water solubility and biocompatibility. Disperse Mn-Pt (200 µL) with 2 mg DSPE-PEG-NH₂ in 240 µL chloroform and mix thoroughly by sonication. Subsequently, the chloroform was completely removed under vacuum. Finally, Mn-Pt was redispersed in deionized water and subjected to ultrasonic treatment, thereby obtaining bimetallic Mn-Pt nanozymes (BiMP NZs) with excellent dispersibility. 
 Macrophage membranes were extracted from the Raw264.7 cell line according to previous reports [ ]. Subsequently mix BiMP NZs and macrophage membrane at a volume ratio of 10:1.2. Vortex the mixture for 2 min, followed by a 10-minute incubation, and repeat three times. Finally, the mixture was sonicated for 2 min to obtain biomimetic macrophage membrane-coated bimetallic Mn-Pt nanozymes (MM-BiMP NZs).

## Detection of extracellular 1O2 scavenging
ESR spectra were recorded on a Bruker A300-10/12 spectrometer. Briefly, BiMP NZs /MM-BiMP NZs (final concentration 25 µg/mL) and TEMP were added to H2O to a final volume of 2 mL. H2O2 (7.5 mM, 200 µL) and sodium hypochlorite (2.5 mM, 200 µL) were incubated for 5 min to generate singlet oxygen (¹O₂). Then 1.6 mL NZs and TEMP solution were added and incubated for 5 min prior to measurement.

## Detection of extracellular ·OH scavenging
FeSO₄ (1.8 mM, 350 µL, Adamas, Shanghai, China) and H₂O₂ (5 mM, 350 µL) were mixed and stirred at room temperature for 10 min. To this mixture, BiMP NZs or MM-BiMP NZs were added to reach final concentrations of 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 40, or 50 µg/mL, and the mixture was stirred for another 5 min. Methylene blue (MB, 100 µg/mL, Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co. Ltd) was then added, and stirring was continued for 30 min. After the reaction, UV-vis spectrophotometry was employed to record the absorbance of MB at 665 nm. The hydroxyl radicals (·OH) elimination rate was determined using the formula: Elimination rate (%) = [(A0 - A) / A0] × 100%, where A0 represents the control absorbance and A denotes the absorbance of each test sample.

## Detection of extracellular O₂·⁻ scavenging
120 µL Methionine (130 mM, Adamas, Shanghai, China), 120 µL riboflavin (200 µM, Adamas, Shanghai, China), 120 µL NBT (750 µM, Adamas, Shanghai, China), and BiMP NZs /MM-BiMP NZs with final concentrations of 0, 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20 µg/mL were added to PBS solution (0.01 M, pH 7.4) and fixed the final volume to 1.2 mL, respectively. Subsequently, the mixed solutions were irradiated with a 365 nm UV lamp for 15 min. The absorbance at 560 nm was measured immediately by a microplate reader. The elimination rate of superoxide anions (O₂·⁻) was calculated as follows: elimination rate (%) = [(A0 - A)/A0] × 100%, where A is the absorbance of each sample and A0 is the absorbance of the control.

## Animals and ethical statement
Male C57BL/6 mice (10–12 weeks old) were supplied by GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Chengdu, China) and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions at Jinfeng Laboratory. Animals were provided with sterile water and standard rodent chow ad libitum. All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jinfeng Laboratory (Approval No. IACUC-JFLAB-2025-017).

## Animal modeling and treatment
Acute lung injury (ALI) model was induced by a single intranasal administration of lipopolysaccharide (LPS; Sigma-Aldrich, USA) at a dose of 3 mg/kg (in 50 µL of sterile PBS). Mice were randomly assigned to five groups (n = 3 per group): Control, LPS, LPS + 0.1 mg/kg MM-BiMP NZs, LPS + 0.5 mg/kg MM-BiMP NZs and LPS + 2.5 mg/kg MM-BiMP NZs. MM-BiMP NZs were administered intratracheally 0.5 h post-LPS challenge. All mice were euthanized 24 h after LPS instillation for sample collection.
Cigarette (Chaotianmen, Chongqing, China) smoke-induced COPD model was established by exposing mice to mainstream CS in a whole-body exposure chamber for 12 weeks (2 one-hour sessions per day, 5 days per week; 12 cigarettes per session). The chamber environment was maintained with the following parameters, including carbon dioxide concentration < 0.2%, oxygen concentration > 18%, and particulate matter concentration between 300 and 500 mg/m3. Mice were randomly divided into four experimental groups (n = 5 per group): Air, CS, CS+ BiMP NZs and CS + MM-BiMP NZs. During the final four weeks of smoke exposure (weeks 9–12), mice in the treatment groups received intratracheal administration of BiMP NZs or MM-BiMP NZs (2.5 mg/kg) twice weekly. Upon completion of the 12-week CS exposure, mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of 1% pentobarbital sodium. Pulmonary function was assessed using the lung function instrument (Tawang, Shanghai, China). Key parameters, including forced expiratory volume in 0.05 s (FEV0.05s) and forced vital capacity (FVC), were recorded. Blood samples were then collected for subsequent biochemical analysis. Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed by instilling and withdrawing sterile ice-cold PBS three times, and the collected bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was centrifuged. The cell pellet was resuspended for total and differential cell counts using Diff-Quick stain (Solarbio, Beijing, China), while the supernatant was stored at -80 °C for cytokine quantification. The left lung was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h at 4 °C, followed by paraffin embedding for histological sectioning and staining. The right lungs were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for further molecular analyses.

## Cell culture and treatment
The human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B (Hycyte, Suzhou, China) was cultured in DMEM medium, and the murine macrophage cell line Raw264.7 (Procell, Wuhan, China) was maintained in RPMI-1640 medium. Both media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. For experimental treatments, cells were exposed to various concentrations of cigarette smoke extract (CSE; prepared using the methods described previously [ ]), bare BiMP NZs, or MM-BiMP NZs. To investigate the involvement of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, cells were pretreated with the specific PI3K inhibitor LY294002 (MedChem Express, USA) at 20 µM for 1 hour prior to subsequent stimulations. For knockdown experiments, BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 were respectively transfected with small interfering RNA (siRNA; Tsingke Biotech, Beijing, China) targeting PI3K (human: 5’- GUACGAGAUGCGUCUACUAdTdT-3’; mouse: 5’-GUACCGAGAGAUCGACAAAdTdT-3’) or negative controls using Lipo3000 following the manufacturer’s instructions.

## Histological analysis
Lung tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for pathological assessment. For each H&E-stained section, five random fields were imaged using an Olympus imaging system (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Peribronchial inflammation, reflecting airway inflammation, was evaluated using a semi-quantitative scoring system adapted from established methods [ ]. The scoring criteria were as follows: 0 (no pathology), 1 (minimal cellular infiltration), 2 (inflammatory cell layer ≤ 1 cell depth), 3 (inflammatory cell layer 2–4 cells deep), and 4 (inflammatory cell layer > 4 cells deep). Emphysema was assessed by measuring the mean linear intercept (MLI). In brief, a crosshair grid was overlaid on each field, and the total length of the grid lines (L) and the number of alveolar septal intersections (NS) were counted. The MLI was calculated as L/NS. To evaluate collagen deposition, lung sections were stained with Sirius Red and Masson’s trichrome. The area of peribronchial collagen deposition was quantified using ImageJ.

## Living fluorescence imaging
To evaluate the pulmonary biodistribution and retention of the nanozymes, CS-exposed mice were intratracheally administered with DiD fluorescent dye-labeled BiMP NZs or MM-BiMP NZs. At predetermined time points (1, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h) post-administration, mice were anesthetized and separated lung tissues were immediately subjected to whole-body fluorescence imaging using an in vivo Chemi Dog Ultra automatic multifunctional imaging system (Tanon, Shanghai, China).

## Arterial blood gas analysis
Arterial blood was collected via cardiac puncture immediately following euthanasia. Blood samples were placed in pre-heparinized syringes and analyzed within 15 min using a portable I-STAT blood analyzer (Abbott, Singapore). Key parameters, including pH, partial pressure of oxygen (PO₂), and partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PCO₂), were recorded for each sample.

## Assessment of oxidative stress
For flow cytometric analysis of lung ROS, single-cell suspensions were prepared from fresh lung tissues. Intrapulmonary cells, as well as BEAS-2B and Raw264.7, were incubated with the ROS-sensitive fluorescent probe DCFH-DA (10 µM) at 37 °C for 1 h in the dark. After washing, the fluorescence intensity of Dichlorofluorescein was immediately analyzed using a flow cytometer (BD Fortessa, USA), with data expressed as mean fluorescence intensity (MFI).
For biochemical assays of oxidative/antioxidant markers, homogenized lung tissues and cells were subjected to cold PBS. The levels of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and malondialdehyde (MDA), as indicators of oxidative damage, were measured respectively using H₂O₂ assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and malondialdehyde colorimetric assay kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturers’ instructions. Total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD) activity was determined by T-SOD activity assay kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China). The ratio of reduced glutathione to oxidized glutathione (GSH/GSSG) was quantified using T-GSH/GSSG colorimetric assay kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) to evaluate the redox state. All absorbance readings were obtained using a microplate reader (Shanpu, Shanghai, China).

## Toxicity and biosafety assessment
To evaluate systemic toxicity, serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), urea (UREA), creatinine (CREA), creatine kinase (CK), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) were quantified using standard chemistry analyzers at Servicebio technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China). Tissue sections from mouse liver, heart, kidney and spleen were H&E-stained to assess potential organ damage. The cytotoxicity of CSE, BiMP NZs, and MM-BiMP NZs on BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 cells was determined using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; MedChemExpress, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

## Cellular uptake assay
BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 cells were incubated with DID-labeled BiMP NZs or MM-BiMP NZs in medium for 4 h at 37 °C. After washing to remove uninternalized particles, cellular uptake was assessed qualitatively by confocal microscopy following fixation and DAPI nuclear staining, and quantitatively by flow cytometry to determine the mean DID fluorescence intensity.

## Real-time qPCR
Total RNA was extracted from lung tissues and cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Dalian, China), reversely transcribed to cDNA, and subjected to RT-qPCR on a Real-Time PCR Detection System (Thermo, Shanghai, China). Gene expression levels were normalized to GAPDH and quantified using the 2−△△Ct method. The sequences of all primers used are listed in Table S1.

## ELISA
IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α levels in mouse BALF and Raw264.7 supernatants, and IL-6, IL-8, and IL-1β levels in BEAS-2B supernatants, were measured using ELISA kits (Boster, Wuhan, China) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

## Analysis of macrophage polarization
Raw264.7 macrophages treated with BiMP NZs or MM-BiMP NZs were then harvested, and incubated with F4/80 and CD86 antibodies. After washing, cells were resuspended in staining buffer and analyzed immediately using a flow cytometer (BD Fortessa, USA). Data were analyzed with FlowJo software, and the percentage of F4/80 + CD86+ cells was calculated to represent the M1-polarized macrophage population.

## RNA sequencing analysis
Total RNA was extracted from lung tissues of CS-exposed mice treated with or without MM-BiMP NZs (n = 3 per group) using TRIzol reagent. RNA sequencing was performed at Masterbio Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were determined as per the parameters of Fold Change > 1.5 and P-value < 0.05. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler (R v4.3.1).

## Western blot analysis
Total protein was extracted from lung tissues or cultured cells using ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with a protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail. The protein concentration was determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Germany). After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies against the following targets: CD11b (1:1000; Abclonal, Wuhan, China), CD18 (1:1000; Abclonal, Wuhan, China), p-PI3K (1:1000; Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), PI3K (1:5000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), p-AKT (1:1000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), AKT (1:5000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), and GAPDH (1:5000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China). Following three washes with TBST, the membranes were incubated with appropriate horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China) and imaged with a chemiluminescence imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

## Statistical analysis
Data are expressed as mean ± standard error of mean. Statistical comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). GraphPad Prism software (version 8.0, California, USA) was used for all statistical analyses and data visualization. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Scheme was created with biogdp.com.

## Results

## Synthesis and characterization of MM-BiMP NZs
The successful fabrication of macrophage membrane-biomimetic bimetallic Mn-Pt nanozymes was confirmed through comprehensive physicochemical characterization. TEM images revealed the uniform spherical morphology of the bare BiMP NZs and the formation of a distinct core-shell structure upon macrophage membrane coating (Fig.  A). This was corroborated by DLS analysis, which showed an increase in the average hydrodynamic diameter from 21.67 nm (BiMP NZs) to 112.68 nm (MM-BiMP NZs), consistent with successful encapsulation (Fig.  B). XRD confirmed the crystalline structure of the BiMP NZs, matching the standard pattern for Mn₀.₃₇Pt₀.₆₃ (JCPDS No. 03-065-5033) (Fig.  C). XPS analysis indicated the coexistence of Mn²⁺/Mn⁴⁺ and Pt⁰/Pt²⁺ oxidation states within the nanozymes (Fig.  D, E). To further quantify the overall elements content, inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) was conducted, and the results showed a Mn/Pt molar ratio of approximately 1:4.71, which is consistent with the feeding ratio during synthesis. The surface charge, measured by zeta potential, shifted from − 17.9 mV for BiMP NZs to approximately − 30.4 mV for MM-BiMP NZs, closely resembling that of native macrophage membranes (MM, -30.1 mV), confirming effective surface modification (Fig.  F). Furthermore, SDS-PAGE and western blot analyses confirmed the successful retention of macrophage membrane proteins on the MM-BiMP NZs, as evidenced by characteristic protein bands and the specific presence of membrane markers CD11b and CD18 (Fig.  G, H). To further verify the spatial location of the nanozyme relative to the macrophage membrane, we performed HRTEM-EDS elemental mapping. As shown in Fig.  I, Mn and Pt signals, together with P, S, and N, are uniformly distributed within the nanoparticle, indicates that the membrane coating forms a thin and conformal layer around the nanozyme surface.
 

## ROS-scavenging capacity of BiMP NZs and MM-BiMP NZs
We next evaluated the antioxidant enzyme-mimetic activity of the synthesized nanozymes against key ROS, including ¹O₂, ·OH, and O₂·⁻ (Fig.  A). Using ESR spectroscopy, both BiMP NZs and MM-BiMP NZs effectively quenched ¹O₂ signals, with the latter showing a slight reduction in activity, likely due to the membrane coating partially limiting surface accessibility (Fig.  B). The ·OH scavenging capability, assessed via a methylene blue decolorization assay, was concentration-dependent for both nanozyme formulations (Fig.  C, D). Quantitative analysis confirmed their high ·OH elimination efficiency (Fig.  E). Similarly, both BiMP NZs and MM-BiMP NZs demonstrated potent, dose-dependent O₂·⁻ scavenging activity (Fig.  F-H). Collectively, these results confirm that the core BiMP NZs possess robust multi-ROS elimination capacity, which is largely preserved after macrophage membrane coating.
 

## Preliminary efficacy and safety of MM-BiMP NZs in an acute lung injury model
To initially evaluate the therapeutic potential and biosafety profile of MM-BiMP NZs, we employed an acute lung injury model induced by LPS (Fig.  A). Histopathological examination of lung tissues by H&E staining demonstrated that intratracheal administration of MM-BiMP NZs significantly attenuated LPS-induced inflammatory infiltration and alveolar damage in a dose-dependent manner, with 2.5 mg/kg showing the most pronounced protective effect (Fig.  B). Consistently, MM-BiMP NZs treatment at 2.5 mg/kg markedly reduced pulmonary H2O2 levels (Fig.  C) and suppressed the mRNA expression of intrapulmonary pro-inflammatory cytokines determined by qRT-PCR, including IL-6, IL-1β and TNF-α (Fig.  D). Furthermore, H&E staining of major organs like liver, heart, kidney and spleen across groups revealed no obvious pathological alterations, indicating a favorable preliminary safety profile at the administered doses (Fig.  E).
 

## MM-BiMP NZs ameliorated pathological hallmarks in experimental COPD
We next investigated the therapeutic efficacy of MM-BiMP NZs administrated at 2.5 mg/kg intratracheally in CS-exposed chronic COPD mouse model (Fig.  A). First, fluorescence imaging experiments against lung tissues in COPD-model mice at different time post-administration confirmed superior lung retention of MM-BiMP NZs compared to uncoated BiMP NZs and PBS group, with signals persisting for up to 72 h (Fig.  B), which supported the rationality of administration period. Treatment with MM-BiMP NZs, but not bare BiMP NZs, significantly improved lung dysfunction caused by chronic CS exposure, as evidenced by increased FEV0.5s and FEV0.5s/FVC ratio via the detection data from small animal lung function instrument (Fig.  C). Meanwhile, MM-BiMP NZs could correct CS-induced arterial blood gas abnormalities to some extent, including elevated PH value and PO2, as well as the declined PCO2 examined by a blood analyzer (Fig.  D). Histological analysis by H&E staining revealed that relative to mice in CS group, those in CS + MM-BiMP NZs group presented effectively attenuated pathogenic injury encompassing emphysema and peribronchial inflammation, reflected by a decreased mean linear intercept, and reduced peribronchial inflammation scores (Fig.  E). Additionally, Masson’s trichrome and Sirius Red staining showed that MM-BiMP NZs treatment markedly inhibited CS-induced peribronchial collagen deposition, indicating the mitigation effect of MM-BiMP NZs on airway remodeling (Fig.  F).
 The long-term biosafety of MM-BiMP NZs was systematically assessed in the COPD-model mice. H&E staining of heart, liver, spleen and kidney from mice in various groups showed no evident histopathological damage (Fig. S1A). Furthermore, serum biochemical analysis indicated that key markers of hepatic (ALT, AST), renal (UREA, CREA), and cardiac (CK, LDH) function remained no remarkable abnormalities (Fig. S1B), which suggested the favorable in vivo biocompatibility and safety of MM-BiMP NZs.

## MM-BiMP NZs alleviated the intrapulmonary oxidative stress and inflammation in COPD-model mice
Mechanistically, MM-BiMP NZs improved the pathogenic hallmarks of COPD-model mice potently by mitigating CS-induced oxidative stress and subsequent inflammation in the lungs. As depicted in Fig.  A and B, flow cytometry and biochemical assays showed a significant reduction in total ROS, H2O2, and lipid peroxidation product MDA, alongside increased antioxidant capacity (T-SOD activity, GSH/GSSG ratio) in the MM-BiMP NZs-treated group compared to CS group. This reduction in oxidative burden was closely associated with a profound anti-inflammatory effect. As expected, MM-BiMP NZs treatment markedly downregulated the pulmonary mRNA expression of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α determined by qRT-PCR (Fig.  C), and reduced total inflammatory cell infiltration in BALF detected by differential cell counts, particularly neutrophils and macrophages (Fig.  D). Moreover, ELISA assay of BALF supernatant indicated that MM-BiMP NZs could decrease the protein levels of these inflammatory cytokines in BALF (Fig.  E). Collectively, these results conclude that appropriate intratracheal administration of MM-BiMP NZs could alleviate the oxidative and inflammatory injury within the lungs, thereby improving lung dysfunction, emphysema and peribronchial pathologic changes in experimental COPD.
 

## MM-BiMP NZs mitigated cellular oxidative stress and inflammation in vitro COPD model
Given the pivotal roles of bronchial epithelial cells and alveolar macrophages in mediating oxidative stress and perpetuating inflammation in COPD [ ], we next evaluated the biocompatibility and bioactivity of our nanozymes in relevant cell models. First, we conducted cytotoxicity detections via CCK-8 assays in BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 cells with different concentrations of CSE to determine the modelling conditions, and results showed that 3% CSE is appropriate to BEAS-2B while 5% CSE to Raw264.7 (Fig. S2A, D). Moreover, CCK-8 assays for BiMP NZs and MM-BiMP NZs showed that concentrations under 0.5 µg/mL are non-toxic for BEAS-2B and concentrations under 10 µg/mL for Raw264.7 (Fig. S2B, C, E, F). Subsequently, we examined the cellular uptake capacity of nanozymes within these two cells. Confocal scanning imaging and flow cytometry analyses in BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 cells demonstrated favorable internalization of DID-labeled MM-BiMP NZs and BiMP NZs (Fig. S3). While MM-BiMP NZs treatment presented obviously worse internalization ability relative to that with BiMP NZs, which may be ascribed to that the macrophage membrane coating led to the escape of engulfment to some extent.
We then investigated the therapeutic effects against CSE-induced cell injury. In BEAS-2B cells, biochemical assays demonstrated that treatment with MM-BiMP NZs effectively mitigated CSE-induced oxidative damage, significantly reducing levels of cellular MDA and 8-OHdG while restoring T-SOD activity (Fig.  A). Flow cytometry for ROS content confirmed that a pronounced reduction in intracellular ROS was observed in MM-BiMP NZs-treated BEAS-2B (Fig.  B). In addition, both the secretion and mRNA expression of key pro-inflammatory cytokines detected by ELISA and qRT-PCR in CSE-stimulated epithelial cell models, like IL-6, IL-8 and IL-1β, were markedly suppressed by MM-BiMP NZs (Fig.  C, D). Parallel experiments in Raw264.7 macrophages yielded consistent results. MM-BiMP NZs significantly reduced intracellular MDA and 8-OHdG levels while increased T-SOD activity in CSE-exposed Raw264.7 cells, supported by the results of biochemical examinations (Fig.  E). Similarly, flow cytometry for ROS content showed that MM-BiMP NZs markedly ameliorated CSE-induced ROS increase within Raw264.7 cells. (Fig.  F). MM-BiMP NZs treatment potently inhibited CSE-driven M1 pro-inflammatory polarization determined by a flow cytometer (Fig.  G). Furthermore, it significantly attenuated the release and gene expression of inflammatory mediators examined using ELISA and qRT-PCR, including IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α (Fig.  H, I). To sum up, these in vitro data demonstrate that MM-BiMP NZs are safe, are efficiently internalized by function cells, and exert potent antioxidative and anti-inflammatory effects at the cellular level.
 

## PI3K/AKT signaling mediated the anti-inflammatory effects of MM-BiMP NZs in CS-induced injury models
To elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms of MM-BiMP NZs, we performed transcriptomic analysis on lung tissues from CS-exposed mice with or without MM-BiMP NZs treatment. Compared to the untreated CS group, MM-BiMP NZs treatment resulted in significant alterations in gene expression profiles, with 562 upregulated genes and 619 downregulated genes (Fig.  A). GO analysis revealed that the differentially expressed genes were primarily involved in processes related to inflammatory response and oxidative stress (Fig.  B). Notably, KEGG pathway analysis identified the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway as one of the most significantly enriched pathways (Fig.  C), suggesting its potential role in mediating the therapeutic effects. We then validated this finding in vivo and vitro. Western blot analysis confirmed that either MM-BiMP NZs or BiMP NZs significantly inhibited the CS-induced phosphorylation of PI3K and AKT in mouse lung tissues (Fig.  D). Consistent results of suppressed activation of PI3K/AKT signaling were observed in both CSE-stimulated BEAS-2B cells and Raw264.7 macrophages, evidenced by western blot analysis (Fig.  E, F).
 To functionally establish the necessity of this pathway, we employed the specific PI3K/AKT inhibitor LY294002 and genetic intervention of siRNA. We firstly determined via western blot that 20 µM LY294002 could significantly repress the phosphorylated levels of PI3K and AKT both in CSE-treated BEAS-2B and Raw264.7 cells (Fig.  G, H). Subsequently, ELISA result indicated that inhibition of PI3K/AKT signaling by LY294002 synergized with MM-BiMP NZs to further reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in CSE-stimulated BEAS-2B cells (Fig.  I). Similarly, in Raw264.7 macrophages, co-treatment with LY294002 and MM-BiMP NZs led to a more pronounced inhibition of M1 polarization determined by flow cytometry (Fig.  J) and a greater suppression of inflammatory mediator release at the protein level determined by ELISA (Fig.  K). In addition, knockdown of PI3K could similarly inhibit the phosphorylated levels of PI3K and AKT in both cells as supported by western blot results (Fig. S4A, B). Consistent with the inhibitor experiments, knockdown of PI3K not only furtherly suppressed the release of inflammatory cytokines in CSE-stimulated BEAS-2B cells with MM-BiMP NZs treatment detected by ELISA (Fig. S4C), but also collaboratively alleviated M1 polarization and inflammation in CSE-insulted Raw264.7 macrophages respectively determined by flow cytometry and ELISA assay (Fig. S4D, E). Accordingly, these results indicate that MM-BiMP NZs ameliorate CS-induced injury, at least in part, through the modulation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.

## Discussion
This study employed airway nebulization to deliver macrophage membrane-coated manganese-platinum bimetallic nanozymes and demonstrated a significant efficacy in mitigating lung injury induced by chronic cigarette smoke exposure. This therapeutic effect was substantiated by multiple endpoints, including improved pulmonary function, attenuated airway inflammation, and reduced emphysema severity. Mechanistic investigations revealed that MM-BiMP NZs exert their protective effects by suppressing oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in bronchial epithelial cells and macrophages. Notably, the ROS-PI3K-AKT signaling axis was identified as the key regulatory pathway mediating these cellular effects, which ultimately ameliorated the pathological progression of airway inflammation and emphysema (Scheme B). This study not only presents a novel nanozyme delivery system with promising translational potential for COPD treatment, but also offers a new therapeutic approach and experimental foundation for modulating oxidative stress and inflammation in chronic lung diseases.
COPD remains a leading global health burden. While its pathogenesis is complex and not fully elucidated, the central pathophysiological role of persistent airway inflammation and progressive parenchymal destruction is well established [ , ]. In recent years, cell membrane-based biomimetic nano-delivery systems have gained considerable attention in biomedicine owing to their excellent biocompatibility, low immunogenicity, and intrinsic targeting capabilities, with notable successes particularly in oncology [ , ]. However, the application of such systems to diffuse inflammatory conditions like COPD is still in its infancy. To address this gap, we innovatively designed macrophage membrane-biomimetic nanoparticles (MM-BiMP NZs) for targeted COPD intervention. This design capitalizes on the innate biological functions of macrophage membranes through several key mechanisms. First, retained surface receptors like CCR2 and ICAM-1 delivered the inflammatory targeting capacity, facilitating active homing to inflamed sites by binding to overexpressed ligands [ , ]. Moreover, “Self”-marker proteins on the membrane prolonged the retention time by minimizing immune clearance [ ]. Third, the natural membrane structure mitigated potential immunogenicity and toxicity associated with synthetic nanomaterials to enhance biocompatibility [ , ]. Capitalizing on the chronic inflammatory core of COPD, we encapsulated manganese‑platinum bimetallic nanozymes—possessing antioxidant activity—within these macrophage membranes. This strategy enables targeted pulmonary delivery and precise accumulation within the inflammatory microenvironment, thereby effectively attenuating airway inflammatory responses.
This study confirms that within the inflammatory microenvironment of COPD, MM-BiMP NZs effectively scavenge intrapulmonary ROS, thereby significantly mitigating oxidative stress-induced lung injury and demonstrating pronounced lung-protective effects. These findings are consistent with existing reports that manganese- and platinum-based nanozymes can synergistically eliminate multiple ROS types (e.g., superoxide anion and hydrogen peroxide) under inflammatory conditions by mimicking endogenous antioxidant enzymes like SOD and CAT, thus alleviating oxidative damage [ , ]. From a mechanistic perspective, Mn active centers can undergo reversible valence transitions (Mn2+/Mn3+/Mn4+), enabling redox reactions analogous to Mn-SOD and CAT [ , ]. Specifically, Mn3+ reacts with O2•⁻ to generate Mn2+ and O2, followed by Mn2+-mediated conversion of another O2•⁻ to H2O2 ( , ). The generated H2O2 can be further decomposed into H2O and O2 through CAT-like pathways ( ). This dynamic valence transition provides Mn centers with sustained redox capacity under oxidative conditions. In contrast, Pt nanozymes primarily operate through surface-mediated catalytic processes. Surface-exposed Pt atoms facilitate the adsorption and activation of O2•⁻ and H2O2, promoting the dismutation of O2•⁻ and the decomposition of H2O2 ( ), thereby limiting radical accumulation under near-physiological conditions [ – ]. Therefore, when Mn and Pt are integrated into a bimetallic nanozyme, their complementary catalytic features may function cooperatively. Mn valence transitions enable superoxide dismutation and peroxide decomposition, while Pt sites facilitate surface-mediated catalytic turnover of peroxide species. Their integration facilitates a reinforced SOD/CAT-like redox cascade, thereby improving electron-transfer dynamics and overall antioxidant performance, thus enhancing ROS-scavenging performance. In future studies, we will further investigate these interactions to elucidate the cooperative mechanism in more detail. Notably, however, in the context of tumor therapy, structurally analogous manganese‑platinum nanomaterials have been reported to perform a pro-oxidative function within the tumor microenvironment. By catalyzing the generation of high levels of ROS, they can induce tumor cell apoptosis and suppress metastasis [ ]. This apparent functional duality, namely antioxidant in inflammatory settings versus pro-oxidant in tumors, likely arises from a complex interplay of factors. These encompass fundamental differences in disease pathophysiology (chronic inflammation versus malignancy), microenvironmental specificities (pH, enzymatic profiles, redox states), variations in material dosage and exposure kinetics, as well as distinct nanomaterial-biointerface interactions with specific cellular or molecular targets. This phenomenon underscores a critical principle that the biological effects of a given nanomaterial can be profoundly divergent, even opposite, across different pathological contexts, highlighting a strong “microenvironment-dependent” functionality. Consequently, future research must delineate the precise molecular switches and regulatory networks that dictate this context-dependent shift in mechanism. Elucidating these determinants is essential for the rational design and safe, precise application of nanozymes in biomedicine.
The nebulized inhalation strategy adopted in this study presents a promising approach to address the limited efficacy of current antioxidant therapies in COPD. By delivering MM-BiMP NZs directly to the respiratory tract and alveoli as an aerosol, this route enables targeted drug accumulation and prolonged retention at the disease site. Compared to systemic administration like oral or intravenous, localized pulmonary delivery offers dual advantages. It achieves higher effective drug concentrations at pulmonary lesions, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy, while simultaneously minimizing systemic exposure in off-target organs. This not only reduces potential systemic toxicity but also broadens the therapeutic safety window [ – ]. Significantly, this strategy aligns with the emerging paradigm in inflammatory lung disease treatment, that is the development of targeted intrapulmonary delivery systems. Previous studies indicate that such systems can effectively modulate the airway microenvironment by elevating local drug concentrations, leading to reduced mucus hypersecretion, inhibition of key pro-inflammatory mediators, and improved mucociliary clearance, thereby alleviating airway obstruction and injury [ , ]. Within this context, our findings advance the field by demonstrating that nebulized MM-BiMP NZs not only effectively scavenge ROS and alleviate oxidative stress but, crucially, exert synergistic therapeutic effects in vivo by promoting alveolar epithelial repair and reducing inflammatory infiltration. Future optimization of the nanomedicine’s physicochemical properties, potentially in combination with other agents, could pave the way for a more efficient and precise comprehensive treatment paradigm for COPD.
This work provides the first demonstration that MM-BiMP NZs effectively attenuate pathological inflammation in both bronchial epithelial cells and macrophages through targeted modulation of the ROS–PI3K–AKT signaling axis. This intervention significantly ameliorates core COPD pathologies in model systems, including airway inflammation and alveolar structural disruption. Hyperactivation of PI3K/AKT signaling has been recognized as a major driver of COPD pathogenesis and airway inflammation, linking oxidative stress to structural damage of the airways and alveoli [ ]. In particular, excessive ROS can promote PI3K activation and AKT phosphorylation, thereby amplifying pro-inflammatory cascades and airway remodeling in chronic lung diseases [ ]. Consistent with these findings, our data show that COPD induction is associated with increased levels of p-PI3K and p-AKT in both bronchial epithelial cells and macrophages, indicating activation of this pathway. Given that PI3K-AKT signaling regulates key downstream targets involved in cytokine production, cell survival, fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition, and tissue remodeling in chronic airway diseases [ , ], the observed inhibition of pathway phosphorylation provides a plausible explanation for the reduced airway inflammation and preservation of alveolar architecture in our model. These findings are consistent with previous studies showing that pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of PI3K/AKT signaling can attenuate airway inflammation, mucus overproduction, emphysema-like changes, and vascular or fibrotic remodeling in COPD and COPD-related lung injury models [ , ]. Previous work has reported that nanozymes or metal-based nanomaterials with ROS-scavenging activity can exert significant anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective effects in inflammatory diseases of the lung and other organs, although the upstream signaling mechanisms were not fully defined [ , ]. By directly linking MM-BiMP NZ-mediated ROS scavenging to the downregulation of PI3K-AKT pathway phosphorylation in airway epithelial cells and macrophages, our work refines this mechanistic understanding and highlights PI3K-AKT as a central node for the immunomodulatory actions of nanozymes in chronic airway inflammation. This mechanistic insight not only advances the understanding of nanozyme applications in respiratory diseases but also furnishes key experimental evidence and a theoretical foundation for developing future targeted therapies or nano-intervention strategies centered on this pathway.
Compared with earlier macrophage membrane-camouflaged nanoparticles that were mainly developed for cancer and acute lung injury, MM-BiMP NZs extend this biomimetic strategy to chronic airway inflammation and, specifically, to COPD, a disease area where cell membrane-based delivery remains largely unexplored [ , ]. By integrating macrophage membrane-mediated homing with aerosol inhalation, MM-BiMP NZs achieve direct deposition in the respiratory tract and improved accumulation and retention in inflamed lung tissue, which may overcome some of the barriers and off-target exposure associated with systemically administered membrane-coated or inorganic nanoparticles [ ]. In addition, most prior nanozymes or ROS-scavenging biomaterials in pulmonary and other inflammatory diseases emphasized broad antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects without clearly linking their activity to upstream signaling pathways in COPD [ ]. In contrast, MM-BiMP NZs combine Mn-Pt bimetallic catalytic centers, which support cooperative SOD/CAT-like ROS clearance, with targeted suppression of the PI3K-AKT axis in airway epithelial cells and macrophages, a pathway recognized as a major driver of COPD-related oxidative stress, inflammation, and tissue remodeling [ ]. These combined features position MM-BiMP NZs as a next-generation nanozyme platform that unifies biomimetic targeting, optimized pulmonary delivery, and pathway-specific immunomodulation for chronic inflammatory lung disease.
Nevertheless, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, while we have preliminarily delineated the role of the ROS–PI3K–AKT axis in mediating the effects of MM-BiMP NZs, potential crosstalk involving other intermediary molecules or feedback loops within this pathway warrants further elucidation. Second, translating these findings from bench to bedside faces considerable challenges, including the evaluation of long-term biosafety, in vivo metabolic fate, and scalable manufacturing processes of MM-BiMP NZs, all of which necessitate comprehensive future assessment. Furthermore, while the in vivo therapeutic advantages of MM-BiMP NZs were clearly demonstrated, the in vitro cellular uptake of the coated formulation was moderate compared to uncoated nanoparticles. This observation likely reflects the designed immune-evasion function of the macrophage membrane coating, which may transiently reduce non-specific phagocytosis. Further optimization of the coating density or membrane composition could potentially improve cellular internalization without compromising immune evasion. In addition, although therapeutic efficacy was demonstrated, the direct impact of repeated dosing on macrophage phagocytic function and host defense was not assessed, and employing pathogen challenge assays are warranted in the future. Besides, comprehensive long-term biodistribution and metal accumulation studies using quantitative methods such as ICP-MS are necessary for translational evaluation and will be addressed in subsequent work. Finally, the current work focused on bronchial epithelial cells and macrophages, but the impact of MM-BiMP NZs on other critical cell types involved in COPD pathogenesis (e.g., airway smooth muscle cells, neutrophils) remains unexplored. Consequently, the overall therapeutic efficacy and cell-type-specific mechanisms require further validation in more physiologically relevant models, such as advanced co-culture systems or in vivo models of greater complexity.
In summary, our study successfully developed a macrophage membrane-coated bimetallic nanozyme for targeted COPD therapy. Administered via intratracheal nebulization, MM-BiMP NZs effectively accumulated in inflamed lungs, scavenged multiple ROS, and suppressed oxidative stress and inflammation predominantly through the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, thereby alleviating key pathologies in experimental COPD. This study provides a promising biomimetic nanotherapeutic strategy with translational potential for chronic inflammatory lung diseases.
