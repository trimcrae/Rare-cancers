HA2, the neck of the HA protein, and M2e, the extracellular N-terminal structural domain of the M2 protein, are conserved and effective protective antigens.
In this study, the HA2 sequences were fused with three M2e copies (H9N2, H1N1 and H5N1) to the norovirus VP1 protein via the SpyTag-SpyCatcher platform to form self-assembled nanoparticles and display antigenic proteins on its surface, yielding pYL262. Finally, a synthesized 20-repeat CpG adjuvant gene fragment was inserted into pYL327, resulting in the plasmid pYL331. All the constructed plasmids were then transformed into the sifA gene-deficient Salmonella vector χYL56 for oral immunization.
The results showed that sifA-deficient Salmonella could efficiently increase antigen-specific mucosal sIgA antibody titers, especially in alveolar lavage samples, whereas the presence of the phage-54 nanobody could dramatically increase intracellular IFN-γ mRNA levels, indicating its ability to enhance the Th1-type immune response. Finally, the presence of the CpG adjuvant clearly increased T-cell proliferation and promoted DC activation, with elevated splenic TLR21 levels observed. Strikingly, after oral immunization with χYL56 (pYL331), chickens were protected against challenge with the G57 genotype H9N2 virus, which presented similar or even better levels of virus shedding and body weight gain compared with the commercial inactivated vaccine, providing a new option for controlling H9N2 virus infection in the future.

## Introduction
Avian influenza viruses (AIVs) are members of the genus Influenza A virus of the family Orthomyxoviridae. In recent decades, various subtypes of avian influenza viruses, such as H5N1, H7N9, and H9N2, have become prevalent.
The presence of H9N2 viruses in developing countries has been found to be proportionately very high in prevalence surveys ( ; ). This may be due to its low pathogenicity, which allows for recurrent infections in long-lived laying hens and breeders and consequent transmission between farms and family farms, and the ability of H9N2 avian influenza to exacerbate infections by weakening the immune response of the host through immunosuppression and coinfection ( ; ). In particular, the G57 genotype of H9N2 AIV has recently been shown to be the most dominant serotype across China ( ).
Vaccination is the main strategy used to control the continuous spread of avian influenza viruses in poultry ( ); the influenza virus antigenic proteins NA, HA, M2, and NP are often used for vaccine development; and the use of conserved antigens to develop a universal influenza vaccine is necessary because of the susceptibility of influenza viruses to antigenic drift. HA proteins are able to mediate the binding of viruses to the cell's salivary acid receptor and accomplish invasion under acidic conditions ( ; ), and they are considered to be the most important antigenic determinant clusters. However, among these genes, HA1 is genetically susceptible to antigenic drift, whereas the HA2 gene is more conserved than the HA1 gene, which can induce cross-immune responses between different subtypes of viruses ( ). However, M2e, which is derived from the extracellular N-terminal domain of the M2 protein, is also characterized by a high degree of conservatism, and the first nine amino acids of its sequence MSLLTEVET are more than 99 % conserved among all influenza A viruses ( ; ). As a result, many studies have used M2e as a target for vaccine design, but the immunogenicity of M2e alone is relatively poor; until now, the level of M2e-specific antibodies in influenza virus-infected mice has remained unsatisfactory ( ; ). Therefore, the combination of M2e antigens with different vectors, adjuvants, or other antigenic proteins to improve vaccine immunogenicity is usually employed ( ; ; ). In particular, the combination of HA2 and M2e effectively induces an immune response in animals and provides protection against influenza virus challenge ( ; ; ).
To increase the immunogenicity of vaccine candidates, the combination of antigenic proteins with self-assembled nanoparticles can be considered to promote the recognition of antigenic proteins by the host immune system, resulting in rapid activation of the host immune response. In the last decade, nanoscale materials such as virus-like particles (VLPs)( ), liposomes ( ), dendritic polylysine ( ), cationic nanoparticles ( ), poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles ( ) and self-assembled proteins ( ) have received attention as potential delivery vehicles for H9N2 vaccine antigens. Among these, VLPs have attracted increasing attention because of their high degree of immunity and safe properties. For example, VLPs designed on the basis of the prominent region of the norovirus capsid protein VP1 are capable of assembling into nanoparticles, which could serve as an efficient vaccine delivery platform ( ). With high immunogenicity and stability, VP1 can be produced stably and efficiently in E. coli, where each P structural domain contains three cyclic structural domains that can display exogenous antigens to increase their immunogenicity ( ). In particular, norovirus VP1 nanoparticles efficiently display M2e or HA2 of influenza virus via SpyTag/SpyCatcher conjugation ( ). The SpyTag-SpyCatcher system, which was designed on the basis of the CnaB2 structural domain of FbaB, the virulence factor of Streptococcus pyogenes, is highly stable and is commonly used to assemble proteins into nanoparticles ( ) or bacterial outer-membrane vesicles ( ), yielding better presentation of antigens and stronger immune responses ( ; ; ). Most importantly, SpyCatcher003 enables efficient VLP conjugation and extreme thermal resilience, resulting in strongly induced neutralizing antibodies ( ). Notably, in vitro purification and injection or intranasal immunization are still needed in most studies.
Dendritic cells (DCs) are the most powerful antigen-presenting cells, and they play multiple roles in immunity against influenza A virus (IAV) infection. In recent years, many studies have focused on targeting DCs to improve the presentation efficiency of vaccine candidates and increase the level of the immune response ( ; ; ). The commonly used targeting strategy is to target antigens with DCs that target antibodies or ligands to dendritic cells in situ ( ; ). Above all, Hai Xu et al. reported that DCs targeting T7 phage delivery of H5N1 HA2 protein can stimulate high levels of antibody and cytokine expression, in which the phage-54 nanobody was found to adsorb rapidly to the DCs of chickens, ducks, and geese ( ; ). Nanobodies have the advantages of high antigenic specificity, small size, and ease of labeling and have been used in tumor research ( ; ), which may suggest their excellent potential for antigen presentation.
Toll-like receptors play an indispensable and important role in the activation of the immune system in animals, and mammalian TLR9 or TLR21 in chickens ( ) can be activated by pathogen-derived nonmethylated cytidine-phosphate-guanosine DNA (CpG DNA) or artificial synthetic nonmethylated CpG-containing oligodeoxynucleotides (CpG ODN), which directly or indirectly initiate an immune response through downstream signaling, thus resisting the invasion of pathogens ( ). It has been reported that the CpG gene is able to act as a DC stimulator used as an adjuvant for H9N2 avian influenza ( ) and is capable of facilitating vaccine candidate antigenic protein-induced cytotoxic T-cell responses and protective effects ( ; ).
Salmonella is considered a promising vaccine delivery vector, in particular, Roy Curtiss III et al. developed several regulated delayed attenuation, expression and lysis Salmonella vectors for the induction of a better immune response ( ). Notably, Salmonella can be phagocytosed by lysosomes and form Salmonella-containing vacuole (SCV) structures after its entrance into host cells, and the formation of SCVs is not beneficial for the delivered antigens carried within Salmonella. The deletion of the sifA gene is a determinant of SCV formation. SifA induces mobilization of the late endosomal membrane region via PLEKHM1 (M1, which contains pleckstrin homology and RUN structural domains), Rab7, and HOPS (homotypic fusion and protein sorting) complexes to generate SCVs ( ). It has been shown that sifA gene-deficient Salmonella are able to escape SCV and become less pathogenic ( ) to enhance the host immune response against infectious bronchitis virus ( ), influenza virus ( ) and Clostridium perfringens ( ).
In this study, we first constructed a sifA gene deletion Salmonella vector based on our previous regulated delayed lysis Salmonella χ11802, named χYL56. The HA2 of the G57 genotype H9N2 subtype avian influenza virus was subsequently fused with 3M2e of different subtypes (H9N2, H5N1 and H1N1) and displayed on the surface of a self-assembled nanoparticle based on the norovirus VP1 protein and SpyTag-SpyCatcher platform. In addition, chicken DCs targeting phage-54 nanobodies and CpG adjuvants were also included to increase the humoral immune response. The purpose of this study was to explore a novel approach for using Salmonella as an oral immunization vector to deliver self-assembled nanoparticles for H9N2 AIV in vivo, avoiding in vitro purification and injection immunization, which could dramatically benefit its application in poultry production.

## Materials and methods

## Bacterial strains and culture conditions
The bacterial strains and plasmids used in this study are listed in . Escherichia coli (E. coli) χ6212 was used for molecular cloning of host cells, and Salmonella strains χ11802 or χYL56 were used to immunize chickens. Bacterial culture was performed at 37°C in Luria–Bertani (LB) media supplemented with 50 μg/mL diaminoheptanedioic acid (DAP) (Sigma, USA), 0.1 % arabinose (Sigma, USA), and 0.2 % mannose (Sigma, USA), if necessary. The gelatin-containing buffer (BSG) contained 8.5 g/L NaCl, 0.3 g/L KH2PO4, 0.6 g/L Na2HPO4, and 0.1 g/L gelatin ( ). 

## Construction of Salmonella with sifA gene deletion
The primers used in this study are shown in . Using the χ11802 genome as a template, the upstream and downstream DNA fragments of the sifA gene were amplified via the PCR method, and the above two fragments were fused via the fusion PCR method. The DNA fragment resulting from the deletion of the sifA gene was integrated into the genome of Salmonella χ11802, and the sifA gene within the genome of the bacterium was replaced. By homologous recombination of the suicide plasmid, the resulting Salmonella with the deletion of the sifA gene was named χYL56 ( A). 

## Detection of the lysosomal escape ability of Salmonella via transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
DF-1 cells were cultured in 6-well plates until the cell density reached approximately 5 × 105 per well. The cells were then incubated with χYL56 or χ11802 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10 for 24 h. Then, the cells were collected and fixed at 4°C with 2.5 % glutaraldehyde fixative overnight. The cells were observed via TEM (services provided by Harbin Veterinary Research Institute, China).

## Plasmid construction
The primers and plasmids used in this study are shown in , . The empty vector pYA3681 containing the Ptrc prokaryotic expression promoter was used as a starting vector. The VP1 protein of norovirus fused with the Spytag003 sequence ( ) was first inserted under the control of the Ptrc promoter; then, the gene fragment encoding the HA2 protein of the G57 subtype of H9N2 AIV was fused with three copies of the M2e protein (each from H9N2, H5N1 and H1N1 influenza viruses), and the SpyCatcher sequence was inserted under the control of an additional SD sequence, yielding pYL262 ( A). To construct a chicken DC-targeting nanoparticle plasmid, pYL287 was used as a template, and the primers phage-54-F/phage-54-R were used to amplify the phage-54 nanobody sequence and inserted into the pYL262 plasmid digested with ApaⅠ by seamless cloning, yielding pYL327 ( A), which fused the phage-54 nanobody to the N-terminus terminus of the 3M2e-HA2 protein. To further construct a CpG adjuvant plasmid, pYL328 was used as a template and amplified with the primers CpG-F/CpG-R, and the resulting fragment was inserted into pYL327 digested with XbaⅠ by seamless cloning, yielding pYL331 ( A). 

## Synthesis of recombinant proteins
Salmonella carrying the plasmid constructed above was cultured in LB medium containing 0.2 % Ara until the OD600 reached 0.6 in a thermostatic shaker at 37°C. IPTG (1 mM) was added to the bacterial broth, which was used to induce protein expression, the mixture was incubated for 20 h at 37°C, and the bacteria were collected. Lysate containing DNase I (50 μg/mL), lysozyme (0.5 mg/mL), and protease inhibitor was mixed with the bacteria and then shaken at 37°C for 1 h. The supernatant of the lysate was collected and filtered through a 0.45-μm filter membrane, 15 % sucrose solution was added underneath the collected protein mixture, and the protein particles were collected via ultracentrifugation. Protein precipitates were dissolved in PBS at 4°C overnight, and reducing and nonreducing Western blots were detected with antibodies against the HA protein of H9N2 and the His-tag and M2 proteins of H9N2, respectively. Particle formation within the protein mixture was then observed via TEM (services provided by Harbin Veterinary Research Institute, China).

## Chicken immunization and challenge study
A schematic diagram of the experiment conducted on birds in this study is shown in A. In this study, 134 2-day-old female Hyland Brown chickens were purchased from Changchun Agricultural Research Institute (Changchun, China) and randomly divided into 8 groups: the blank control group (BSG), inactivated vaccine control group, and groups of Salmonella χ11802 harboring the empty vector pYA3681 (S3681) or plasmid pYL262 (S262). In addition, Salmonella χYL56 strains harboring the pYL262 (ΔS262), pYL327 (ΔS327) and pYL331 (ΔS331) plasmids were also included, with 18 birds in each group. Another normal group (n = 8) was also designed in which the birds without immunization or challenge were monitored during the challenge process for comparation. When the maternal antibody titer of the birds dropped below 24, the chickens were immunized by oral administration at a dose of 1 × 109 CFU per bird, which was recorded as 0 days postimmunization (dpi). At 14 dpi, the birds were then boosted with the same dose. For the commercial inactivated vaccine (YEBIO BIOENGINEERING, China), intramuscular injection was performed according to the instructions with 100 μL/bird at 0 dpi, this group was named the vaccine group. 
At 14 days after the second immunization (14 dp2i), the birds were challenged with the G57 genotype H9N2 virus (A/Chicken/Jilin/DH109/2012, kindly provided by Professor Yanlong Cong from Jilin University) via nasal inhalation at a dose of 107 EID50, and the body weight changes were recorded for 14 days continuously. Oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs were then collected from the birds, and these samples were inoculated into 10-day-old chicken embryos to detect virus shedding on Days 3, 5, 7, and 9 post-viral challenge (dpc). The samples were repeatedly freeze-thawed three times before being inoculated into 10-day-old chicken embryos, which were incubated at 37°C for 3 days, after which the allantoic fluid of the embryos was collected. The obtained allantoic fluid was then tested for viral positivity via a hemagglutination assay.
Three chickens in each group were sacrificed at 5 dpc, and the trachea and lungs were collected and subjected to RT‒qPCR to determine the virus titers via the use of pYL283 as a standard plasmid and chH9N2-M1F/R as primers. Furthermore, the mRNA levels of the inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β in the lungs were also measured via RT‒qPCR with the primers IL-1β-F/R and IL-6-F/R, and GAPDH was used as an internal reference gene. Histopathological changes by paraffin sectioning and HE staining were also performed.

## Sample collection and analysis
At 10 days post-second immunization (10 dp2i), four birds from each group were sacrificed, and the serum, tracheal washing, alveolar washing, and intestinal washing samples were collected and subjected to enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) to determine antibody levels. Another three birds were sacrificed, and the spleen cells of the birds were collected and analyzed for the expression of IFN-γ and IL-4 via RT‒qPCR and ELISA, as described below. In addition, the proliferative capacity of the cells stimulated by specific antigen-related proteins was also determined via the Cell Counting Kit (CCK8) method and CFSE assay. The maturation of chicken spleen DCs was also evaluated via RT‒qPCR to determine the surface expression of CD83, CD86, CCL5 and CCR7. RT‒qPCR was also performed to evaluate the expression level of TLR21 in spleen lymphocytes.

## Detection of specific antibody levels via ELISA
ELISAs were performed as described previously ( ) with minor modifications. In detail, the ELISA plates were coated with either mixed M2e peptides (H1N1, H9N2, H5N1, synthesized by GenScript, Nanjing, China) or purified HA2 protein at a concentration of 1 μg/mL. Two hundred microlitres of serum sample (at the beginning dilution of 1:50) or tracheal, alveolar, or intestinal washing samples (at the beginning dilution of 1:5) were subsequently added to the first well, and two dilutions were performed. After incubation at 37°C for 1 h, the plates were washed three times with 0.15 % PBST, and a biotin-labeled chicken IgY antibody or IgA antibody was then added at a dilution of 1:5000 or 1:1000 as the primary antibody, followed by streptavidin-HRP at a dilution of 1:3000 as the secondary antibody. Finally, the absorbance values of the wells were detected at 405 nm using ABTS as the substrate.

## Splenic lymphocyte isolation and evaluation of the cellular immune response
Splenic lymphocytes were isolated and collected via a chicken splenic lymphocyte isolation kit (Solarbio, China), cell proliferation levels were detected via the CCK8 method ( ), and cell differentiation value-added levels were detected via CFSE analysis, as described previously ( ). Specifically, splenic lymphocytes were stained with antibodies against CD4, CD8 and CFSE after 72 h of coincubation with proteins, the cell fluorescence information was analyzed via flow cytometry to select CD4+ and CD8+ cell populations, and the proliferation of the two types of lymphocytes was analyzed by analyzing the precession of the CFSE signal between them. The splenic cellular immune response was also determined via an ELISpot assay according to the manufacturer's protocol (Mabtech, Sweden).

## Detection of IFN-γ and IL-4 in splenocytes by ELISA and RT‒qPCR
The isolated splenocytes mentioned above were added to 6-well cell culture plates at a concentration of 3 × 106 cells/mL and cultured for 72 h in a 37°C cell culture incubator, where the medium contained 10 μg/mL M2e or HA2 protein. The cell culture supernatants were then collected, and cytokine production was determined via commercial ELISA kits (Kete, Jiangsu, China). The remaining cells were also collected, and whole-cell RNA was extracted via the RNA Extraction Kit (TransGen Biotech, China). Then, 1 μg of RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA via the BeyoRT™ First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Then, 2 μg of cDNA was used to detect the mRNA copy number of IFN-γ and IL-4 via an ABI7500 Real-Time PCR system, in which GAPDH was used as an internal reference gene.

## Determination of spleen DC maturation and TLR21 levels by RT‒qPCR
To determine the maturation of chicken spleen DCs, the relative expression levels of CCL5, CCR7, CD83 and CD86 were selected as targets and evaluated via RT‒qPCR with the primers CCR7-F/R, CD83-F/R and CD86-F/R. In addition, the expression level of TLR21 (a chicken homolog of TLR9 in mammals ligated with CpG)( ) in spleen lymphocytes was also measured via RT‒qPCR with the primers TLR21-F/R to determine the effects of the CpG adjuvant. For both analyses, the primers GAPDH-F/R were used to measure the level of transcription of the internal reference gene GAPDH as a standard.

## Statistical analysis
All the data are displayed as the means ± standard deviations (SDs) and were analyzed via Prism 8.3 (GraphPad). One-way ANOVA tests or t tests were performed for statistical analysis (P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a significant difference).

## Results

## Construction and identification of Salmonella strains with sifA gene deletion
In this study, regulated delayed lysis Salmonella χ11802 was used as the original strain to delete the sifA gene via the suicide plasmid-homologous recombination method, as shown in A, yielding χYL56. To determine whether the deletion of the sifA gene had any side effects on the growth of Salmonella, both strains were inoculated in 200 mL of liquid LB medium supplemented with arabinose and DAP, the OD600 value of the bacterial culture was measured once every two hours for a total of 12 h, and the OD600 value of the bacterial culture was measured again at 24 h after inoculation. The bacterial growth curve based on the values is shown in B, which indicated that the deletion of the sifA gene did not affect the growth of Salmonella. To visualize the position of the mutant Salmonella χYL56 with respect to the cellular lysosome and the difference from χ11802, cell sections were observed via transmission electron microscopy (TEM), as shown in C, which revealed that the deletion of the sifA gene increased the lysosomal escape ability of the bacteria, resulting in the presence of bacteria in the cytoplasm.

## Synthesis of recombinant proteins and evaluation of VLP production
In this study, the prokaryotic expression vector pYL3681 with the Ptrc promoter was used as the parental plasmid, and three different constructs, pYL262, pYL327 and pYL331, were included for comparison ( A). All three plasmids contained a SpyTag003-fused norovirus VP1 protein as a framework for the production of a self-assembled VLP backbone. An additional SD sequence was subsequently inserted after the operon of VP1 to initiate the transcription of the next gene. The 3M2e-HA2 and 3M2e-HA2-phage54 nanobody operons were then inserted after the SD sequence, yielding pYL262 and pYL327, respectively. Subsequently, a 20-copy synthesized CpG sequence was inserted into pYL327 to yield pYL331. Notably, both the 3M2e-HA2 and 3M2e-HA2-phage54 fragments were fused with a SpyCather003 sequence, which allows its specific binding with the norovirus VLP via SpyTag003.
Reduced and nonreduced Western blotting experiments were then performed to determine the expression and assembly properties of the three proteins, VP1, 3M2e-HA2, and phage-54, using His-Tag, M2e specific, and Myc-Tag as primary antibodies, respectively. As shown in B, the presence of the VP1-His protein was confirmed in all three plasmids, with a protein band at approximately 111 kDa. However, the synthesis of 3M2e-HA2 (68 kDa) in pYL262 was confirmed when the M2e-specific antibody was used, whereas the presence of the nanobody phage-54 increased the protein size to approximately 100 kDa in both the pYL327 and pYL331 constructs. Finally, the nanobody phage-54 was also confirmed by its fused Myc tag, which showed a specific Myc band of approximately 100 kDa in both the pYL327 and pYL331 plasmids but not in the pYL262 plasmid. An additional nonreducing Western blot assay was also performed to determine the presence of VLP nanoparticles ( C). Most likely, the most evident results were observed when the M2e-specific antibody was used as the primary antibody, which indicated that the presence of a large protein band greater than 880 kDa could be observed in all three constructs, indicating the successful combination of the antigen and the VP1 backbone. Notably, when His- or Myc-tagged antibodies were used, the results somewhat differed from previous observations, probably due to the hiding of the His and Myc tags during the formation of large nanoparticles. To further confirm the presence of the VP1-based VLPs, TEM was also conducted, and the results revealed that a granular structure of approximately 50 nm was present in all three constructs ( D). Notably, many large nanoparticles of different sizes could still be observed in all three samples, indicating that some unexpected nanoparticles could be formed during VLP production.

## Delivery of DCs targeted with nanoparticles plus a CpG adjuvant by sifA-deficient Salmonella significantly enhances humoral and mucosal immune responses
The immunization procedure for the birds was carried out as shown in A. Ten days after the second immunization, the birds’ serum, alveolar lavage, tracheal lavage and intestinal lavage were collected, and the specific antibody levels in these samples were detected via ELISA. Compared with the nontargeting Groups S262 and ΔS262, the two DC-targeting groups ΔS327 and ΔS331 exhibited significantly increased M2e-specific antibody levels (P < 0.05) ( B, left), indicating that the DC-targeting strategy was critical for the generation of M2e-specific antibodies. However, the HA-specific antibody levels in the serum of all the sifA-deficient Salmonella groups were significantly greater than those in both the BSG control and empty vector control groups (P < 0.05) ( B, right), even though the HA-specific antibody levels were not as high as those in the commercial vaccine group. With respect to IgA antibody production in alveolar lavage fluid, all three sifA-deficient Salmonella groups elicited significantly increased production of IgA compared with the negative control; in this case, the DC-targeting design did not perform better than the nontargeting strains ( C), indicating that sifA-deficient Salmonella could efficiently elicit sIgA antibody production in the lungs. Similar trends were also observed in the production of sIgA in tracheal lavage ( D) and intestinal lavage ( E) samples, even though the antibody titers were not as obvious as those observed in serum or alveolar lavage, indicating that sifA-depleted Salmonella could also perform better than the original strain at these positions. In general, compared with the parent strain, sifA-deficient Salmonella could elicit greater production of antigen-specific serum IgG and mucosal IgA antibodies, indicating that lysosomal escape ability is a critical factor for vaccine design. Moreover, the DC-targeting strategy as well as the presence of a CpG adjuvant could further improve the generation of antibodies, even though the situation is not always the same.

## The combination of a DC-targeting strategy with a CpG adjuvant significantly enhanced cell proliferation
The results of the splenocyte proliferation analysis via the CCK8 assay revealed that, compared with the BSG control group, the HA2 protein ( A), M2e peptide ( B) and ConA ( C) stimulation groups significantly increased cell proliferation. Notably, no dramatic difference could be observed between the parent S262- and sifA-deficient ΔS262 groups in this case. However, the presence of the phage-54 nanobody as well as the CpG adjuvant could further increase cell proliferation compared with the other agents. Similar trends were also observed when CSFE was used as a label to determine splenic CD4+ ( D) and CD8+ ( E) cell proliferation. In particular, compared with the other groups, the presence of the CpG adjuvant significantly stimulated the proliferation of CD4+ T cells (P<0.001) ( D) and CD8+ T cells (P<0.05) ( E), indicating the critical role of CpG in enhancing the cellular immune response. 

## Increased production of IFN-γ and IL-4 in spleen lymphocytes due to the presence of a DC-targeting design and a CpG adjuvant
In this study, the mRNA transcript levels of two cytokines, IFN-γ and IL-4, were determined in splenic lymphocytes stimulated with antigen proteins via RT‒qPCR. Compared with those in the other groups, the presence of the phage-54 nanobody in the ΔS327 group significantly increased the mRNA levels of IFN-γ when it was stimulated with either M2e or HA2 ( A), whereas the additional presence of the CpG adjuvant in the ΔS331 group decreased the levels. However, the mRNA levels of IL-4 in the DC-targeting ΔS327 group did not appear to be affected, whereas significantly increased levels were observed in the nontargeting ΔS262 group and the CpG adjuvant group, especially when the cells were stimulated with the M2e peptide ( B). All the results mentioned above indicated that the DC-targeting strategy could efficiently increase the mRNA levels of IFN-γ but not those of IL-4. However, the presence of a CpG adjuvant appeared to be more efficient in inducing IL-4 than IFN-γ. The cell culture supernatants were also collected and subjected to ELISA to determine the production of IFN-γ and IL-4. In general, similar trends were observed for both IFN-γ ( C) and IL-4 ( D). Notably, the sifA mutation appeared to significantly increase the mRNA levels of IL-4 but not those of IFN-γ ( A and B), which is consistent with previous observations indicating the possible role of lysosomal escape in stimulating an efficient antibody response ( C). 
Moreover, an ELISpot assay was also conducted to determine antigen-specific IFN-γ production in spleen lymphocytes stimulated with mixed HA2 and M2e proteins. Compared with the BSG control, empty vector control (S3681) or commercial vaccine control, all the Salmonella-containing antigen groups exhibited a significantly increased production of IFN-γ, although large variation in the individual groups was observed ( E). In this case, the combination of a DC-targeting design and a CpG adjuvant appeared to be the most efficient approach to stimulate IFN-γ production.

## The presence of the CpG adjuvant significantly stimulated the differentiation and maturation of chicken DCs and TLR21 molecule activation in spleen cells
The surface differentiation and maturation markers of chicken DCs in spleen lymphocytes after stimulation with HA2 and the M2e peptide were evaluated via RT‒qPCR. Compared with those in the other groups, the sifA-deficient Salmonella-delivered DC-targeting constructs without (ΔS327) or with the presence of a CpG adjuvant (ΔS331) increased the mRNA levels of CD83 ( A), CD86 ( B) and CCR7 ( C), including the inactivated vaccine group, which was more evident in the ΔS331 group. These findings indicate that the combination of sifA deletion, DC targeting and the CpG adjuvant may have a beneficial effect on DC maturation. 
To further determine the possible underlying mechanisms of the beneficial effects of the CpG sequence, spleen lymphocytes were subjected to RT‒qPCR to evaluate TLR21 mRNA levels. The results demonstrated that the 20 copies of CpG in pYL331 significantly increased the TLR21 levels compared with those in all the other groups (P<0.01) ( D).

## Enhanced protection against H9N2 challenge by the combination of sifA deletion, DC targeting and CpG adjuvant design
Chickens were challenged with the G57 subtype H9N2 influenza virus at a dose of 107EID50 at 14 dp2i, and virus shedding from oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs was determined by hemagglutination assay measurements at 3, 5, 7 and 9 dpc ( ). Compared with the parent Salmonella strain, the presence of the sifA mutation did not significantly decrease virus shedding in either oropharyngeal or cloacal swabs when 3M2e-HA2-containing VP1 nanoparticles were delivered (ΔS262 vs. S262). Compared with the nontargeting nanobody, the phage-54 nanobody decreased virus shedding in both samples (ΔS327 vs. ΔS262). Finally, the presence of the 20-copy CpG sequence efficiently decreased virus shedding in both samples (ΔS331 vs. ΔS327), which was equal to or even better than that of the commercial inactivated vaccine considering the shedding of virus from oropharyngeal swabs at 3 dpc. 
The protective effect against H9N2 viral challenge was also determined by morphological observations of the lungs at 5 dpc ( A), HE staining of the lungs ( B) and trachea ( C), assessment of the viral titers of the lungs ( D) and trachea ( E), generation of the inflammatory cytokines IL-6 ( F) and IL-1β ( G), and assessment of body weight gain within 14 days after viral challenge ( H). 
The results of the morphological observations ( A) revealed more severe hemorrhages in both the BSG and S3681 empty vector groups, with reduced elasticity and white exudates under pressure, whereas the lung samples from all the other groups appeared to be healthier than those from these two groups, except for one case of marginal hemorrhage in the S262 and vaccine groups.
HE staining of the lungs ( B) revealed enlarged alveolar septa and massive inflammatory cell infiltration accompanied by parenchymal lesions in the BSG and S3681 groups and milder plasma exudates and inflammatory cells in the inactivated vaccine group. However, all four Salmonella-immunized groups exhibited attenuated viral challenge. HE staining of tracheal sections ( C) revealed that the tracheal villi were severely detached and that the mucosal and submucosal layers were severely damaged in the two control groups. All four Salmonella-immunized groups dramatically decreased pathological damage, even though the damage was not as severe as that caused by the inactivated vaccine.
Similar results were also observed for the virus titers of the lung ( D) and tracheal ( E) samples via RT‒qPCR, in which all four Salmonella-immunized groups presented significantly lower virus titers in both organs compared with the BSG control (P < 0.0001), which was lower than that of the inactivated vaccine group, even though no statistically significant difference was detected. The generation of inflammatory cytokines was also evaluated via RT‒qPCR. Similarly, in all four Salmonella-immunized groups, the inactivated vaccine significantly decreased the production of both IL-6 ( F) and IL-1β ( G) in the lungs compared with BSG and the empty vector. Finally, the body weight gains were also recorded, and the results revealed that all chickens were affected by H9N2 virus challenge compared with normal chickens ( H), but the chickens in the ΔS331 group performed better than those in the other groups during the 14 days of observation, even better than those in the inactivated vaccine group. At the end of the study, body weight gain was significantly greater in the S262, ΔS262, and ΔS327 groups than in the BSG control group (P < 0.05).

## Discussion
Currently, traditional inactivated and live attenuated vaccines are still the most dominant vaccines for AIV infection and focus mainly on the generation of humoral immunity, as assessed by serum antibodies, whereas the induction of cellular immunity is still not satisfactory. However, bacterial vectors such as Salmonella may be able to enhance the host cellular immune response. For example, Yong Zhi et al. reported that Salmonella could be used to deliver foot-and-mouth disease VLPs, resulting in significantly increased IgG and IgA antibody levels and elevated Th1, Th2, and Th17 cell activation in mice ( ). Since Salmonella is a pathogenic bacterium, modification of Salmonella is inevitable due to its wide utilization, and the present study was based on the regulated delayed lysis of Salmonella developed by Roy Curtiss III ( ), which could be destroyed by itself after oral immunization due to the absence of arabinose in vivo. Then, the synthesized antigen can be released into host cells or the tissue microenvironment where the initiation of the immune response occurs. Salmonella can be phagocytosed by lysosomes after entering host cells, which severely affects the induced immune response due to the low pH of the SCV. A sifA gene deletion was subsequently introduced into our Salmonella strain to help it escape from the SCV. In this study, the synthesized 3M2e-HA2 protein, with or without the phage-54 nanobody, could bind to the norovirus VP1 framework via SpyTag003-SpyCatcher003 to generate VLPs with surface-decorated antigens in Salmonella. After escape from the SCV, Salmonella can be lysed by itself and release the produced VLPs into the host cytoplasm to stimulate the host immune response. As expected, SifA gene deletion significantly increased sIgA-specific antibody levels in alveolar lavage chickens (ΔS262 vs. S262) ( C). Moreover, the intracellular IL-4 mRNA levels were also significantly increased upon antigen stimulation, especially for M2e peptide stimulation ( B), which could be another clue associated with its ability to enhance the antibody response, as IL-4 is usually considered to be biased toward the Th2-type immune response. However, the presence of sifA deletion had little effect on cellular proliferation ( ).
As antigen-presenting cells, DCs are capable of rapidly recognizing and responding to antigens, and DC-targeting delivery strategies can make full use of the properties of these cells to activate the host immune response. Therefore, DC-targeting strategies in vaccine design have focused mainly on mammals. Recently, the chicken DC-targeting strategy has also received much attention, and DC peptide ( ), C-type lectin-like domain-containing proteins ( ), monoclonal antibodies ( ) and nonobody ( )-targeting approaches have been reported. In our previous study, we used a phage-74 nanobody to target H9N2 AIV virus in chicken DCs, therefore, another reported phage-54 nanobody was selected for this study. The results showed that the presence of the phage-54 nanobody efficiently elevated the M2e-specific serum IgY antibody (ΔS327 vs. ΔS262) ( B) and tracheal lavage sIgA antibody ( D) but not the HA2 antibody, probably because the M2e peptides were located outside of HA2 on the surface of the VLPs. In addition, the results of cell proliferation upon stimulation with M2e, HA2 or ConA ( A–C) demonstrated that the DC-targeting design could increase proliferation, although the difference was not statistically significant. Most likely, the most interesting result of the DC-targeting approach was that the presence of the phage-54 nanobody could significantly increase the intracellular IFN-γ mRNA levels ( A), as well as the IFN-γ levels in the cell supernatants ( C), upon stimulation. This indicates its ability to enhance the Th1-type immune response, which is consistent with a previous report that the DC-targeting strategy was able to significantly induce a Th1-type immune response ( ).
CpGs have often been used as adjuvants in vaccine studies because of their ability to directly stimulate the activation and maturation of antigen-presenting cells and upregulate a variety of cytokines ( ). In this study, a 20-copy CpG fragment was synthesized and inserted into pYL327 to compare its immune enhancement effects. Unexpectedly, the presence of the CpG adjuvant did not appear to affect the humoral immune response or mucosal immune response in terms of the antibody titers ( B–E). However, the CpG fragment significantly increased the proliferation of splenic T cells, especially CD4+ T cells ( D, E), as determined by flow cytometry. Another interesting finding was that the presence of a CpG fragment appeared to dramatically affect chicken DC activation. CD83 is an early dendritic cell activation marker that plays an important role in lymphocyte homeostasis and antibody production during influenza A virus infection ( ). The levels of CD83 ( A) and the differentiation marker CD86 ( B) significantly increased in the presence of CpG, indicating that the CpG sequence could dramatically affect the activation of chicken DCs. To elucidate the underlying mechanism, TLR21 elevation was also determined by RT‒qPCR, and the results demonstrated that the presence of CpG could significantly stimulate the mRNA levels of TLR21 in splenic lymphocytes ( D).
In this study, we first used a norovirus VP1-based self-assembled VLP platform to display the 3M2e-HA2 antigen protein on its surface via SpyTag-SpyCatcher ligation. Delivered by a regulated delayed lysis Salmonella vector, Salmonella can be lysed after oral immunization and release its synthesized nanoparticles into the host environment, avoiding in vitro purification and dependence on an injection immunization approach, which should be suitable for poultry production in the field. To further improve our vaccine efficiency, a sifA gene deletion, which would allow more efficient escape from the SCV, a DC-targeting strategy mediated by the phage-54 nanobody, and a 20-copy CpG adjuvant sequence were combined to determine the protective effect of our final construct. Strikingly, the combination of sifA-deficient Salmonella χYL56 with the pYL331 plasmid provided protection against H9N2 challenge as efficiently as the commercial inactivated vaccine did. In terms of some results, including virus shedding and body weight gain, our oral immunization appeared to be even better than the inactivated vaccine, indicating that it would be feasible as a new option for controlling H9N2 virus infection in the future. Interestingly, it has been noticed that the virus shedding in oropharyngeal swabs were always higher than cloacal swabs during our experiments and some others ( ; ). Despite the higher levels of antigen-specific antibodies in respiratory tissues, influenza viruses more predominantly infested the upper respiratory tract compared to infections in the digestive tract, which is probably one of the reasons for this result. The other possible reason is the antigen we selected was M2e and HA2, inclusion of some other neutralization antigens such as HA1 ( ) should possibly increase the efficiency of our design.
