## Abstract
This paper explains the transmission of SARS-CoV and influences of several environmental factors in the transmission process. The article highlighted several methods of collection, sampling and monitoring/estimation as well as surveillance tool for detecting SARS-CoV in wastewater streams. In this context, WBE (Wastewater based epidemiology) is found to be the most effective surveillance tool. Several methods of genomic sequencing are discussed in the paper, which are applied in WBE, like qPCR-based wastewater testing, metagenomics-based analysis, next generation sequencing etc. Additionally, several types of biosensors (colorimetric biosensor, mobile phone-based biosensors, and nanomaterials-based biosensors) showed promising results in sensing SARS-CoV in wastewater. Further, this review paper outlined the gaps in assessing the factors responsible for transmission and challenges in detection and monitoring along with the remediation and disinfection methods of this virus in wastewater. Various methods of disinfection of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater are discussed (primary, secondary, and tertiary phases) and it is found that a suite of disinfection methods can be used for complete disinfection/removal of the virus. Application of ultraviolet light, ozone and chlorine-based disinfectants are also discussed in the context of treatment methods. This study calls for continuous efforts to gather more information about the virus through continuous monitoring and analyses and to address the existing gaps and identification of the most effective tool/ strategy to prevent SARS-CoV-2 transmission. Wastewater surveillance can be very useful in effective surveillance of future pandemics and epidemics caused by viruses, especially after development of new technologies in detecting and disinfecting viral pathogens more effectively.
Keywords: SARS-CoV, Wastewater, Surveillance, Biosensors, Genomic sequencing, Disinfection

## Introduction
Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) is an ongoing global pandemic caused grave respiratory disorder by coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The first case of SARS-CoV-2 infection was discovered in December 2019 ( ). The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The coronavirus is characterized as a single-stranded, positive-sense RNA virus belonging to the Betacoronavirus family (subfamily: Coronavirinae, family: Coronaviridae, order: Nidovirales) ( ; ). The four genera of the subfamily are called Alpha-, Beta-, Gamma-, and Delta-coronavirus. However, the respiratory infection in humans is usually caused by Alpha- and Beta-coronavirus ( ). Phylogenetic analysis of SARS-CoV-2 revealed that the virus has more than 85% genomic resemblance with two SARS-like CoV strains, derived from bat species (bat-SL-CoVZC45 and bat-SL-CoVZXC21) ( ). It is also found that SARS-CoV-2 is genetically connected to SARS-CoV and the Middle-East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV) with considerable genetic resemblance of 79% and 50%, respectively ( ). The pandemic has severely affected the public health system, environment, and the global economy, while comprehensive knowledge of the virus is still limited and several perspectives of sustainable remedies of the virus are yet to be explored ( ; ). In humans, respiratory illness is common, while the symptoms of SARS-CoV can vary from a simple cold to respiratory infections such as fever, dry cough, myalgia, dyspnoea, etc. with less common symptoms such as nasal congestion, headache, runny nose, sore throat, diarrhoea, etc ( ) ( ). As viral shedding can happen before an individual becomes symptomatic, however, the pre-symptomatic and asymptomatic individuals are major sources of rapid worldwide spread of virus ( ).
The communication pathways of SARS-CoV are either through direct contact with the infected person or the respiratory droplets, and air routes transmission ( ; ; ; ). Recently, it was stated that the SARS-CoV can be present in wastewater treatment system's sludge, municipal sewage or wastewater, and hospital's wastewater, commercial cruise ships, aircraft, secondary treated water, river, etc ( ; , ; ; ; ; ; ; , ). SARS-CoV-2 has likely discharged into sewer systems via faeces, saliva, swabs, and/or sputum of infected persons. The surveillance of SARS-CoV-2 ribonucleic acid (RNA) measurements in wastewater has been utilised to examine COVID-19 epidemiology ( ; ).
The paper discusses different routes of entry of SARS-CoV-2 in environment and highlighted wastewater as a potent source of SARS-CoV-2. The objective of this review paper is to systematically highlight all the possible sources, occurrence, movement of SARS-CoV in wastewater streams and its possible disinfection methods. This comprehensive yet detailed review covers wider perspectives of environmental epidemiology and risk assessment in the context of SARS-CoV-2 transmission, encompassing from source to treatment. The study also addresses the existing research gaps and identification of the most effective tool/ strategy to prevent SARS-CoV-2 transmission.

## Environmental factors responsible for the fate and transmission of SARS-CoV
2.1.

## Transmission of SARS-CoV via air
The transmission routes of SARS-CoV in the air is influenced by many environmental factors such as temperature and humidity, solar radiation, microbial resistance, and biological and physical stress on the distribution of novel coronavirus in the air ( ; ; ). The transport of coronavirus in water also increases the spread of SARS-CoV through aerosol formation ( ) i.e., the communication routes of SARS-CoV through airborne dust ( ; ). Longer duration of inhalation can lead to the movement of virus deeper in tracheobronchial regions, which could increase the chance of infection ( ; ). Small size respiratory droplets (< 5 µm) can remain suspended in the air for a longer duration and could travel to longer distance resulting in maximum chance of infections as compared to large size respiratory droplets (> 5 µm), which persist in the air for shorter duration ( ; ). Numerous researchers have found that aerosols with PM 2.5 can transmit to the respiratory tracts more easily and can help in spreading the infection ( ; ; ), as evident in case of SARS-CoV outbreak in Hong Kong and MERS outbreak in South Korea ( ).
In the rainforest region, higher humidity is the most favourable climatic factor for the spread of these viruses ( ). But in arid region, such as in Iran, humidity has a reverse relationship with the disease in the infection rate ( ). The risk of spread of SARS-CoV is more in high relative humidity zone and less in the diurnal temperature range as highlighted by . The persistence of novel coronavirus in the aerosols stays for about three hours ( ). However, had pointed out that the novel coronavirus may become inactive at high temperatures, high or low pH, and high intensity sunlight conditions. However, found that the novel coronavirus became inactive despite being high humidity and high-temperature environment conditions. A recent study conducted on the infection of COVID-19 during January and February 2020 for 122 cities in China found that the rate of infection does not decrease with the increase in the temperature ( ). However, there is still gap in the routes of airborne transmission of novel coronavirus.

2.2.

## Existence and diffusion of SARS-CoV in water
The entry points of SARS-CoV in water system are untreated wastewater treatment plants, untreated sewage, water system without proper sanitation facilities, etc ( ; ; ). The novel coronavirus can be survived in the wastewater and surface water for many days ( ). Leaks in the pipeline or low disinfectant levels can contaminate the drinking water supply, as a results aerosols generated by this contaminants have the potential to infect humans however, the water environmental conditions such as temperature and water matrix can affect the persistence of virus ( ). SARS-CoV-2 can persist more than one day in wastewater, the survival of viruses enhances with decrease in temperature ( ). A recent study also reported that SARS-CoV-2 could remain viable in aerosols for up to 16 hours, which indicates that the aerosols generated in wastewater treatment facilities could be contagious and could affect the proximate population ( ). Aerosol viruses can be carried out through the wind during wastewater treatment ( ). SARS-CoV-2 must be active before it reaches the water bodies in order for water to be considered a virus communication route. Therefore, the aquatic environment can be deliberated a prospective route of communication of SARS-CoV-2. ( ) evaluated the occurrence of SARS-COV-2 in the different locations of Quito river in Ecuador on 5th June, 2020.

2.3.

## Transmission of SARS-CoV-2 via soil and biota
SARS-CoV-2 transmission through soil is similar to that of airborne transmission routes, where the contaminated soil is re-suspended and may get inhaled by humans and animals ( ; ). Water runoff is another route of transmission of SARS-CoV-2 to the soil, where untreated wastewater is used for irrigation purposes or it may directly diffuse into soil near the water flow ( ).
Soil physical properties such as soil types, pH, temperature are various factors that can affect the transmission of coronaviruses ( ; ). However, due to a lack of data on the persistence of SARS-CoV-2 in the soil, the actual spread of pathogens in soil has not been established yet.
Biota plays a major role in the spread of the novel SARS-CoV-2. Several viruses can survive on plants irrigated using sewage water from few days up to several weeks depending on certain other conditions, like the nature of the vegetables, initial level of contamination, and the temperature associated with sunlight ( ; ). The crops irrigated by virus contaminated water can retain considerable number of viruses on their surface ( ). So, it is necessary to grow vegetables free from viruses at the time of their harvest by leaving them for few days after the last irrigation schedule.

## Transmission routes of SARS-CoV-2 in different types of wastewaters
There are numerous routes for spread of SARS-CoV-2 in the wastewater system, as outlines in

The wastewater plumbing system is the considered pathway for the spread of SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 in the communities buildings and they can also spread through aerosols or microscopic water droplets ( ; ). At the same time, the SARS-CoV-2 virus has been discovered in blood, sputum, respiratory secretions, and faeces from symptomatic patients ( ; ; ).
 3.1.

## Bio-medical waste
Solid and hazardous waste generated and released from households, community centres, hospitals, treatment centres, and industrial units (e.g. pharmaceutical and chemical industries, packing industries) have been augmented considerably after the outburst of COVID-19 ( ). Bio-medical waste (BMW) generated from those hospitals, clinics, laboratories etc. contain various pathogenic agents. The materials such as cotton, ribbon, bandage, etc. used during healthcare activities and the discarded materials like syringes, operated organ, body parts etc. have considerable potential of increasing the risk of contamination and infection. The virus contamination causes health risk both directly and indirectly ( ). Particularly in developing countries, the risk for the transformation of COVID-19 is more as the people neglect to follow the protocol for waste treatment. WHO reported that solid wastes generated from urban and rural sectors contain identical microorganisms and that 2% of blood-stained waste verified positive for hepatitis viruses, poliovirus, and echovirus recognized in the defiled diapers of domestic wastes ( ). People are enforced to wear a face mask made up of cloth, surgical or self-made, etc. as a compulsory policy imposed by government authorities in public places against the extent of SARS-CoV-2 ( ; ; ).

## Stool and sewage

## Collection of samples and estimation of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater
Wastewater is one of the main entry points for faecal and urine excretions of human population. Most of the wastewater effluent is released into surface water after treatment at the wastewater plants. Hence, it is quite evident that the water contaminants and pathogenic microorganisms released from human bodies can recycle back to humans again if the wastewater treatment processes lack efficiency ( ).
The RT-qPCR and ELISA (enzyme linked immune-sorbent assay) are generally being practiced in clinical tests for SARS-CoV-2 detection using sputum, asopharyngeal swab, or blood ( ; ; ). Several investigations on SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV testified the occurrence of viral RNA in the stool samples of both symptomatic and asymptomatic person ( ; ). Both viable SARS-CoV-2 and viral RNA are shack in human excreta (e.g., saliva, sputum or faeces) and eventually reach the wastewater streams ( ; ; ; ; ). In several countries, the novel coronavirus was detected in wastewater ( ).
Some of the recent studies analysed the stool samples collected from both symptomatic and asymptomatic patients, which showed high prevalence of viral shedding (30–90% of infected individuals) and associated high individual viral shed rates (up to 7.5 to10 g of faeces) ( ; ). Evidence of high viral loads in stool indicates the potential use of wastewater to monitor population infection rates. These data could then be applied to wastewater derived from population assessments, and can act as an initial warning mechanism to analyse and forecast the outbreaks of viruses at the community level ( ).
Wastewater surveillance methods include monitoring chemical or microbiological targets to gain insight into the behaviours or characteristics of a community. For SARS-CoV-2, these surveillance methods can detect the initial occurrence or repetition of COVID-19 in a local community ( ). Some researchers suggested that wastewater sludge can be considered as a dependable source for sampling and observing SARS-CoV-2 levels. As the enclosed virus can be attached over the organic matter of the sludge, the sludge can be a more focused cause. Virus strength data can be employed to screen the infection trend, identify the hot-spot, and inform the decision makers regarding renewing efforts and guiding the resources ( )
SARS-CoV-2 could persist for more than a day in wastewater, with an increasing survival time as the temperature reductions ( ). Another study reported that SARS-CoV-2 could stay viable in aerosols for almost up to 16 hours, which indicates that the aerosols generated in wastewater treatment facilities could be infectious and can affect the proximate population ( ). Researchers previously showed that SARS-CoV-2 stays in faecal samples for three days and in urine samples for about seventeen days ( ). The existence of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater streams for considerable periods can pose severe threats to wastewater plant workers and the adjoining communities.

## Wastewater monitoring and surveillance in COVID- 19
5.1.

## Wastewater based epidemiology
Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE) is an effective surveillance mechanism to detect the presence of pathogens in entire communities and provide a strong indication of the extent of transmission ( ; ). Continuous and thorough monitoring of wastewater can help in early detection of virus mutations/variants and subsequently can help in taking necessary precautions ( ).
WBE can be an effective way of tracking COVID-19 hotspots and dynamics of the disease spread in the community within the proximity of wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) ( ). Although WBE cannot be considered as a substitute for clinical-testing and diagnosis, it can still work effectively as an initial warning tool. It can indicate the presence of infected individuals in communities or within a specific WWTP catchment ( ).
Limited data is available across few countries for wastewater monitoring in case of COVID-19, hence it is not an easy assignment to apply WBE for a diverse global population. However, research works showed a significant correlation between WBE and clinical data of COVID-19, indicating that sewage surveillance could be an useful mechanism to determine the circulation of viruses in the population ( ).
To study WBE for evaluating the spread of COVID-19, three sampling methods have predominantly been used by the researchers, (i) grab sampling, (ii) composite sampling and (iii) passive sampling ( ; ). All these sample types are analysed to monitor viral RNA levels and the infection cases versus SARS-CoV-2 RNA concentrations detected ( ; ). Comparing methods and disease surveillance using composite, grab, and passive sampling in detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA revealed that, in some cases, passive sampling can generate better qualitative results compared to grab samples ( ).
Recently, to monitor SARS-Cov-2, a drain-based sampling method is applied in the urban areas of Bangladesh, where only a drain system is available instead of a centralized sewage system ( ). Parallelly, a hotspot-based sampling method is applied to collect wastewater samples for the rural areas, where the drain system is unavailable. Both the methods showed promising results and can be useful to monitor the transmission of other contagious diseases in locations where onsite sanitation systems contain both drain and non-drain components ( ).
For appropriate documentation of viral strains in wastewater, samples are generally concentrated before analysis. The methods available for SARS-CoV-2 concentration are of two main types: physico-chemical or only physical methods. The physico-chemical methods integrate the use of chemical reagents (e.g. combined use of polyethylene glycol with sodium chloride, dextran or alum). The combination of chemical reagents is being used for liquid-liquid fractionation (aqueous two-phase technique) for the extraction, separation, purification and enrichment of viruses. The physical based methods for viral concentration follow an amalgamation of centrifugation and filtration/ultra-centrifugation and re-suspension methods ( ).

5.2.

## Application of genomic sequencing in WBE for COVID- 19
5.2.1.

## qPCR based wastewater testing
The qPCR-based wastewater analysis has been applied by several countries for COVID-19 tracking by viral load monitoring in wastewater ( ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ). Though, qPCR techniques can generate data of viral load, it is incapable so far for maximizing the information that could be gathered from wastewater. Next generation sequencing techniques can be effective to provide the entire genomic sequence of SARS-CoV-2 found in wastewater samples. This can be helpful to identify the dominant viral strain in wastewater circulation, including those mutations which could affect the diagnostic or therapeutic measures. The new mutations arising in the viruses can influence the efficacy of the vaccines, hence continuous monitoring of new strains is essential ( ; ; ).
Several qRT-PCR assays suitable for monitoring of wastewater have been developed and applied for SARS-CoV-2 detection ( ; ; ). However, there are substantial difference between the performances of different assays. Differences in viral detection rates were found upon the application of different primer/probes for quantification. There are also disadvantages of using the digital reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction method, which has a high cost, long turnaround time, and no remote settings. These disadvantages reduce the widespread utilization of digital PCR procedures in environmental research and routine monitoring of wastewater systems ( ).

5.2.2.

## Viromics and sequencing
Metagenomics based analysis of the viruses in wastewater has been extensively used to monitor the prevalence of different pathogens and could be applied as an effective initial warning tool for the detection of novel viral pathogen outbreaks ( ; ). A high-throughput sequencing approach was utilized in recent researches as an alternative to qRT-PCR to explore the diversity of different viruses, like, enterovirus D, hepatitis A, and hepatitis E v and mastadeno-virus in wastewater ( ). Potential use of viral genomics can explore the future directions in SARS-CoV-2 detection and management.
Next generation sequencing can be useful to monitor more than one pathogen at a time (e.g. RNA sequencing which sequences all of the RNA present in one sample). Metagenomic WBE can be an effective method for detection and identification of emerging pathogens like SARS-CoV-2. Non-invasive sampling techniques can be applied to monitor the pathogens circulating in the communities ( ; ).

5.3.

## Use of biosensors in COVID-19 diagnosis
Biosensors are potential devices for prompt diagnosis of diverse infectious diseases and for studying their transmission. Biosensors can quickly diagnose COVID-19 by the process of SARS-CoV-2 RNA detection ( ). Numerous types of COVID-19 detecting biosensors have been established in recent times for point-of-care (POC) diagnosis of infectious diseases, including colorimetric, paper-based, and nanomaterial-based biosensors ( ; ; ).
Colorimetric biosensors can be effective in diagnosis of COVID-19 by the naked eye, where Au-NPs were capped with thiol-modified antisense oligonucleotides that are specific for N-gene of COVID-19 and this leads to agglomeration occurrence, which subsequently makes alterations in its surface plasmon resonance (SPR), and the used Au-NPs could be detected within 10 min ( ). Paper-based biosensors are small and portable devices, which can detect SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater onsite and are also helpful in tracking the carriers of the virus in the community. Continuous data can be generated by using paper-based biosensors, which can be effective in developing early warning sensing system and subsequent isolation of the virus carriers ( ).
Mobile technologies combined with internet can be helpful in developing diagnostic biosensors, which can provide a new method for the analysis, tracking, monitoring, and control of infectious diseases like COVID-19 ( ). Besides, microfluidics detecting tool for efficient drug screening and transfer can also show a promising result in therapy of SARS-CoV-2 while minimising the health risks ( ).
Nanomaterials-based biosensors such as magnetized nanomaterial (using gold, iron, zinc etc.) and carbon nanotubes-based biosensors had been experimented and applied for detection of COVID-19 in wastewater streams ( ). A field-based transistor biosensor (made of grapheme-sheets plated transistors) showed the potential to detect SARS-CoV-2 spike protein ( ). Optical biosensors can also perform efficiently by developing high-resolution images of viral strains during pandemic time ( ).
Other available techniques for SARS-CoV-2 detection include nucleic acid detection and augmentation (e.g. PCR, RT-PCR, qPCR, etc.); isothermal augmentation methods (e.g. NASBA, LAMP, HAD, RCA, NEAR, SDA, TMA, etc.); immunoassays (e.g. fluorescent antibody staining, ELISA, etc.); DNA sequencing (e.g. sanger sequencers, next-generation sequencers, DNA microarrays, etc.); mass spectrometric methods (e.g. MALDI-TOF); direct visualization of viruses (e.g. using electron microscopy), microelectronics and microfluidics-based techniques (e.g. lab-on-a-chip [LOC], point of care [POC] testing, and surface plasmon resonance [SPR] technique) ( ).
With the development of digital technologies, including artificial intelligence, data science, big data, bioinformatics, there are better opportunities in monitoring and prediction of COVID-19 and related crisis. Data techniques like advanced statistical analysis, machine learning, deep learning and predictive models and algorithms can be useful in predictive analysis and decision-making tools in the COVID-19 and related pandemic. WBE also involve modelling and computational analysis of data to analyse the status of active cases, both in local and global levels ( ). In spite of rapid development of this research domain, there are immense scope for further innovation and implementation of novel techniques related to data science.

## Remediation and disinfection of SARS-CoV-2 present in the wastewater
The SARS-CoV-2 exist in the excreta of the infected patient, Thus, the influent wastewater reaching the centralized WWTPs via the sewerage system may contain SARS-CoV-2 RNA with a substantial viral-load. 
summarized the different treatment methods for the remediation of SARS-CoV-2 in the wastewater.
 Chlorine and chlorine dioxide can be used to analyse the disinfection of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater . 10 mg/L of chlorine can inactivate SARS-CoV entirely in 10 min; however, Escherichia Coli and bacteriophage f2 were inactivated at the rate of 14.29 % and 18.32 %, respectively. In contrast, 40 mg/L of chlorine dioxide can be 100% effective for SARS-CoV-2 within 5 min ( ).
6.1.

## Primary treatment phase
In WWTP, physical treatments are used for the removal of fixed and volatile suspended solids from contaminated wastewater. The physical processes in the treatment include removal of volatile and fixed solids suspended in sewage water by application of physical barriers. Virus particles can be adsorbed onto the large suspended solids in sewage, which is followed by gravitational sedimentation method and eventual settling. This process is considered one of the considerable and preliminary mechanisms in the treatment phase for removal of the virus ( ). Various authors have shown the removal of the enteric virus by the process of sedimentation ( ; ; ( ). showed the occurrence of the SARS-CoV-2 RNA in 25% of the effluent samples using a primary settler. However, recent researches showed that the gravitational sedimentation process involved in the primary treatment phase is inadequate to remove SARS-CoV-2 virus properly from the sewage water ( ).

6.2.

## Secondary treatment phase
The process of secondary treatment in WWTP includes diverse biological methods, namely activated sludge process (ASP), membrane bioreactor (MBR), moving bed biofilm reactor (MBBR), sequencing batch reactor (SBR), treatment ponds, etc., which are generally employed for the removal of organic matter and suspended solids from WWTPs. ASP technology is comparatively more utilizable as it can remove about 90% of enteroviruses ( ). showed that there is a substantial decrease in the viral load to a level of < 2.5  ×  104 copies/L, after using ASP method for SARS-CoV-2 removal in secondary treatment of wastewater. In the ASP, the viruses are adsorbed onto the organic biomass and are removed by settling method in the secondary clarifier ( ). Moreover, extracellular enzymatic activities by hydrolases and proteases in the biological treatment processes can be effective in inactivation of SARS-CoV-2; similar results can be seen in case of other virus ( ; ; ). Several environmental factors such as pH, temperature, presence of sunlight, and solids can considerably affect the sustenance and survival of SARS-CoV-2 ( ; ).

6.3.

## Tertiary treatment phase
Various types of water contaminants such as organics, turbidity, nitrogen, phosphorus, metals, and pathogens loads can be removed by tertiary treatment methods. The process involves in the tertiary treatments are coagulation, filtration, ultraviolet (UV) treatment, chlorination, ozonation, performic acid, nanomaterials, etc ( ). Some nanomaterials such as titanium dioxide, zero-valent iron, and carbon nanotubes can be favourable for the inactivation and elimination of viruses in the WWTPs ( ; ). Free residual chlorine above 0.5 mg/L concentration and contact time of 30 min. (pH < 8) and 2.19 mg/L of chlorine dioxide (ClO2) is recommended in a recent study for complete inactivation of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater. Free chlorine is capable of inactivating SARS-CoV as compared to chlorine dioxide ( ).

6.3.1.

## Ultraviolet (UV) light
The viral genome is targeted by the UV radiation at 100-280 nm (UV-C) triggering pyrimidine dimers and breakage in nucleic acids further affecting the viral replication ( ). But the mechanism behind the inactivation of viruses in the wastewater by applying UV-C has been briefly described by ( ). Recently, studied the deactivation of SARS-CoV-2 on environmental surfaces and personal protective equipment using a pulsed xenon UV light. The study executed high efficiency in the disinfection rate for hard surfaces at 1, 2, and 5 min with 3.53 log10, >4.54 log10, and >4.12 log10 viral load reduction, respectively, and a viral load reduction of >4.79 log10 in 5 min for N-95 respirators.

6.3.2.

## Ozone
Ozone has high oxidation capacity and have strong antimicrobial effects on diverse types of viruses, bacteria and protozoans ( ). Additionally, upon interaction with viruses, ozone molecules can generate oxidative stress, and subsequent detriment effects on viruses. Ozone can be a powerful oxidant against SARS-CoV-2, and can damage the proteins and lipids present in the viral membranes (spikes and envelopes) ( ). Various studies recommended that ozone is likely to be highly effective in SARS-CoV-2 virus inactivation in contaminated water ( ). Some of the studies demonstrated that ozone at low CT was effective in rapid inactivation of various virus types in wastewater or potable water with reductions of 4-log10 ( ). Further research is needed on disinfection properties of ozone to determine the efficacy of commercial devices and the minimum ozone CT values (residual concentrations and contact time) to meet the required log10 virus reduction.

6.3.3.

## Chlorine-based disinfectants
Chlorine-based disinfectants are exclusively applied for the cleaning of surfaces to prevent the spread of SARS-CoV-2. The mechanism for the use of bleach is based on the denaturation of some proteins, such as tryptophan and tyrosine, and cysteine residues, and when chlorine dioxide reacts with them the virus inactivation results very rapidly ( ).
Experiments showed that in low-level wastewater, SARS-CoV was inactivated using chlorine with 0.5 mg/L as free chlorine residual. However, high risks are associated with storage of liquid chlorine and other concerns raised with its usages such as the use of chlorinator and corrosion-resistance materials (copper pipes and hard PVC pipes) ( ). Chlorine dioxide can efficiently be used as a disinfectant in acidic condition because of its high oxidization capacity and more efficiency than chlorine and ozone towards inactivation of few viruses. However, in some studies, it was less effective than chlorine in case of SARS-CoV disinfection ( ).
Hypochlorite is more effective in disinfection of virus as compared to chlorine dioxide against SARS-CoV ( ). Though the sequential wastewater treatment processes can remove or inactivate coronaviruses, several factors affecting their survival in wastewater may also contribute considerably.

## Limitations of COVID-19 detection and monitoring methods in wastewater
There are several challenges and limitations in the available methods of detection and monitoring of COVID-19, like:
1Lack of reproducibility of the concentration methods in wastewater samples,
2Lack of optimization and standardization of wastewater volume for virus extraction,
3Difficulties in identifying the best surrogate with stability, zero toxicity, unaffected by wastewater matrix,
4PCR inhibition during detection process in wastewater,
5Challenges in specifying the information on the virus viability in the wastewater samples ( ).
Novel methods of sample concentration and assays would be helpful to evaluate SARS-CoV-2 infection frequency rates, leading to community-level epidemic mitigation and risk assessment.

8.

## Conclusions
COVID-19 pandemic shows the significance of water management, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and human health protection ( ). Effective surveillance and tracking of the virus in wastewater are essential for developing early warning system (EWS) and public health monitoring in the context of COVID-19. Wastewater surveillance systems for COVID-19 have been designed or implemented more than 50 countries in the last three years, showing considerable success across diverse climatic, economic, development, and infrastructural conditions ( ). Though wastewater surveillance systems have their limitations, it is also evident that the method can generate utilizable epidemiological data of infectious disease, including COVID-19 ( ; ).
Isolation and quarantine measures are being followed globally to reduce the extent of coronavirus infection. However, considering the perspectives of viral transmission through wastewater streams, it is equally important to monitor and disinfect wastewater for reducing the spread of the virus. The recognition of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater and sewage-systems, especially in municipal wastewater treatment plants can facilitate the process of COVID-19 diagnosis at large scale, well before clinical diagnostic testing can reveal the condition. Continuous monitoring and surveillance of COVID-19 in sewage and wastewater along with monitoring the environmental factors responsible for the survival of SARS-CoV-2 is necessary in this context ( ). Developing onsite WBE systems for detection of SARS-CoV-2 genetic materials in wastewater is also the need of the hour ( ). Environmental monitoring along with development of appropriate disinfection methods may effectively control the spread of COVID-19 global pandemic. Additionally, the emerging techniques like m-Health can be helpful in precise surveillance and accurate modelling of SARS-CoV-2 occurrence and transmission in wastewater streams.
Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE) can provide a cheap and effective early warning system for future COVID-19 and related viral outbreaks. The process can be a viable means of effective surveillance in low-income countries, where several viral outbreaks happened previously. In addition, most of these countries do not have proper sanitation facilities. Therefore, WBE can also be applied to monitor surface water and occurrence of potential viral pathogens. A recent research work showed the importance of WBE as an important method in environmental surveillance Monkeypox virus, the outbreak of which has been reported in 74 countries since May 2022 ( ). This work further proves the relevance of WBE in combating future pandemics and epidemics.
Though high-performance genetic sequencing has been done by collecting samples from symptomatic patients, monitoring genetic alterations of the SARS-CoV-2 genome in the wastewater samples need further extensive research. New methods need to be developed for identification of strains and removal of virus from wastewater to minimize faecal–oral transmission risk and to maximize reusing and recycling of wastewater during the future epidemic and pandemic situations. Development of novel technology in detecting and disinfecting viral pathogens can help the scientific community and healthcare centres to deal with future pandemics more effectively.
