## Background
Previous studies have identified the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) Uc001kfo as significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissues, particularly in advanced stages, compared to adjacent non-cancerous tissues. This study aims to further explore the molecular mechanisms by which Uc001kfo promotes HCC metastasis, focusing on its regulation of α-SMA expression.

## Methods
The study investigate the effects of Uc001kfo on proliferation, migration, and invasion in HCC cells using in vitro assays. Additionally, we also explored the molecular mechanism by which Uc001kfo indirectly regulates α-SMA gene transcription through its targeting of Sp1. Finally, we conducted preliminary validation in mice model to assess the potential for Uc001kfo-targeted silencing to inhibit HCC cell invasion and metastasis.

## Results
The results of the study demonstrate that the Uc001kfo/Sp1/α-SMA pathway plays a role in regulating HCC metastasis. Uc001kfo inhibits the degradation of Sp1 protein, thereby promoting Sp1 binding to the α-SMA promoter and enhancing its transcription. Consequently, silencing Uc001kfo can indirectly suppress α-SMA expression, effectively inhibiting HCC cell proliferation, invasion, and migration in vitro, as well as liver metastasis in mice through the spleen.

## Conclusion
Uc001kfo plays a critical role in promoting HCC metastasis, making it a potential a promising therapeutic target for inhibiting tumor progression and metastasis in HCC.

## Introduction
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the third leading cause of cancer mortality in both of men and women worldwide due to its metastatic properties [ ]. So far, numerous studies have demonstrated that invasive pseudopodia, formed by actin bundles or networks, are essential for cancer cell movement and migration from the primary tumor. The polymerization and depolymerization of actin facilitate pseudopodia extension. Therefore, actin-regulated cell motility plays a critical role in tumor invasion and metastasis [ – ]. It has been confirmed that smooth muscle actin alpha(α-SMA) promotes the invasion and metastasis of HCC, and silencing α-SMA significantly inhibits the invasion and metastasis of various tumors, including HCC [ – ]. Our previous study also confirmed that α-SMA is highly expressed in the HCC tissues with portal vein metastasis or poor differentiated [ ]. Uc001kfo, locates on human chromosome 10, has been implicated in the regulation of tumor progression through various mechanisms a diverse range of mechanisms. For instance, it functions acts as a tumor suppressor in developments of breast and colorectal cancer, but promotes the proliferation of ovarian cancer cells [ , ]. Our previous studies have shown that Uc001kfo was highly expressed in HCC tissues and closely correlated with HCC progression. More importantly, our data also showed that Uc001kfo and α-SMA protein were significantly elevated in HCC tissues with distant metastasis, and their expression maintained a consistent positive correlation across HCC tissues of different grades as well as their adjacent tissues [ ]. This suggests that Uc001kfo and α-SMA may jointly regulate HCC metastasis. However, the precise mechanism underlying this relationship warrants further investigation.

## Materials and methods

## Cell culture
Human HCC cells SUN-449, Hep3B, Huh-7, HCCLM3 and Li-7 and SV40 transformation of liver epithelial cells THLE-3 cells were all purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). All these cells were maintained in Dulbecco minimum essential medium (DMEM, 12430054, Invitrogen, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, 10099141C, Invitrogen) and were incubated in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 ℃. These adherent cells were passaged 1:3 every 2-3 days after reaching 80% confluence.

## Lentivirus mediated Uc001kfo silencing and α-SMA overexpression in HCC cells
The recombinant lentiviruses Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo, Lv-NC, Lv-α-SMA and Lv-Sp1 were generated. In addition to addressing gene delivery efficiency in HCC cells, these recombinant viruses were used to introduce siRNA-Uc001kfo (5′-GUGAUCUGGUGUUACAGGG-3′), a non-targeting siRNA (5′-GAAGCCAGAUCCAGCUUCC-3′). Meanwhile, recombinant viruses Lv-Uc001kfo, Lv-α-SMA, and Lv-Sp1 were also prepared to efficiently express Uc001kfo, α-SMA, and Sp1 in HCC cells using a widely applicable CMV promoter. HCC cells SUN-449 and HCCLM3 in logarithmic phase were seeded in 6-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well. One day later, lentiviruses (Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo or Lv-α-SMA) were added into the cell culture medium at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10. The efficiency of virus infection was evaluated by detecting the expression of the green fluorescent protein (GFP) 72 h after infection. Total RNA was then isolated from the cells and subjected to Quantitative Real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) to detect Uc001kfo and α-SMA mRNA levels which were used to evaluate the efficiency of gene intervention. Additionally, the interaction between Uc001kfo and α-SMA will be used to analyze their upstream and downstream relationship.

## Cell invasion and migration assays
SUN-449 and HCCLM3 cells, post 72 h infection with lentiviruses were used for invasion and migration assays. Cell invasion experiments were performed using the Cell Invasion Assay kit (ab235887. Abcam, Cambridge, UK) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, culture medium (500 μl) supplemented with 10% FBS was used as a chemoattractant and about 1 × 105 cells were inoculated in the upper chamber using serum-free DMEM medium. The cells were cultured under normal conditions for 48 h. Then the membrane was cut off used to evaluate the number of cells passing through the membrane by 4′,6-diamidino2-phenylindole (DAPI) staining and counted in five random fields using a light microscope. Cell migration was assessed using a wound healing assay. Approximately 5 × 105 cells were grown onto 6-well plates in 2 ml complete medium and incubated overnight. A 200 μL pipette tip was used to generate wounds by were scraping the cell monolayer, creating wounds. The monolayers were washed twice with 2 ml Dulbecco's Phosphate Buffered Saline (dPBS, pH = 7.4) to remove dislodged cells. The plates were incubated in DMEM with 2%FBS for 24 and 72 h before examining the wounds under a phase contrast light microscope (CKX41, Olympus, Japan). The distance was measured using ImageJ2x.

## Dual-luciferase assay
We obtained the human α-SMA gene promoter sequence from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database and predicted the Sp1 transcription factor binding site (TFBS) using the online software “TRANSFAC”. Human genomic DNA was used to amplify the α-SMA promoter (937 bp), which was then used to construct pGL3-pro-(TFBS-wt)-α-SMA, an expression system composed of the α-SMA promoter and the luciferase gene. This system allows for the evaluation of promoter binding to the target transcription factor based on changes in luciferase activity following transfection into mammalian cells. Furthermore, the TFBS in pGL3-pro-(TFBS-wt)-α-SMA was mutated from 5’-GGGCGG-3’ to 5′-GCGGGG-3′ to construct pGL3-pro-(TFBS-mt)-α-SMA which was used as a control. The 293 cells were seeded into 6-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well and cultured in DMEM with 10%FBS at 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 for 24 h and then the plasmid transfection was performed using Lipofectamine2000(11668–027, Invitrogen). Luciferase activity was measured 48 h post-transfection using the dual luciferase reporter assay system (E1980, Promega, MI, USA) to evaluate the interaction between Sp1 and the α-SMA promoter. Sp1 expression in the cells was upregulated by adding recombinant lentivirus Lv-Sp1 at an MOI of 5, 24 h before transfection.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)
HCCLM3 cells transfected with the Sp1 expression vector containing HA tag in logarithmic phase were harvested and subjected to Chip-PCR referring to the method described method of Zhang et al. [ ] by using a CHIP assay kit (ab500. Abcam). Briefly, after purifying the intracellular Sp1 protein by immunoprecipitation, its bound DNA was collected and used for PCR amplification with the paired primers which were designed specifically for the sequences containing TFBS on the α-SMA promoter. The primers for PCR were: 5′-TGACCCTCGCCCCACCCCATC-3′ and 5′-TCTCGAGCCATATTTGGGTG-3′, producing a hypothetical 219 bases product. Five microliters of the PCR product were loaded onto a 2% agarose gel for electrophoresis, and the presence of the hypothetical product was used to determine whether there is an actual binding between Sp1 and the α-SMA promoter. Three micrograms of the primary antibody of HA tag (ab9110. Abcam) were used for Sp1 immunoprecipitation.

## RNA binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay
Sp1 and α-SMA proteins in HCCLM3 cells precipitated by immunoprecipitation after 48 h of transfected with HA labeled expression vector of pcDNA-HA-Sp1 and N-6*His labeled expression vector pcDNA-His-α-SMA. The target sequence within Uc001kfo was then amplified by using RNA from eluents of immunoprecipitated proteins as the template employing the One Step RT-PCR Kit (210212, Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). The specific RIP protocol follows Khalil's research [ ]. Two micrograms of primary antibodies of HA and His (ab9110/213204, Abcam) were used for immunoprecipitation. The PCR primer sequences designed to match Uc001kfo are as follows: 5′-TTGGCTAGGAATGATTTGG-3′(forward) and 5′-CTGTCAGTCAAGATGAAAAAGAATGG-3′(reverse). Five micrograms of PCR product were subjected to 2% agarose gel electrophoresis.

## Protein synthesis and degradation assays
Seventy-two hours after Lv-Uc001kfo infection, SUN-449 cells re-suspended in DMEM medium with 10% FBS were reseeded into 6-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well and cultured overnight. The medium was then replaced with serum-free DMEM medium containing 50 μM proteasome inhibitor MG-132 (ab141003, Abcam) or 100 μg/ml cycloheximide (CHX, ab120093, Abcam), and the cells were incubated for another 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 h. At these time points, cells were collected and total cellular protein was extracted for detecting Sp1 protein expression via western blot. Sp1 protein synthesis and degradation rate curves were subsequently developed.

## Liver metastasis model
Thirty-two female 6-weeks-old nude mice were purchased from Zhengzhou University, and housed at the Zhengzhou University animal experiment center where the implantation experiment was performed. All the protocols were previously approved by the Zhengzhou University Experiment Animal Ethics Committee (No.20190910). The nude mice were anesthetized by inhalation of ether, and then a small upper abdominal incision was made to expose the mouse spleens, followed by injection of 1 × 106 HCCLM3 cells with and without infection of Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo. Seven minutes later, the portion of the spleen containing the tumor cell injection site was resected. The hepatic metastatic burden was assessed on day 20 after tumor cell inoculation. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stain was performed in livers and photographed to evaluate metastatic burden.

## RNA isolation and RT-qPCR
Total RNA was isolated with Trizol Reagent (155960-18, Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instruction and reversely transcribed into cDNA using M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase and the Random9 primer (2641Q. Takara, Dalian, China). RT-qPCR was carried out using the Fast SYBR Green Master Mix (4385610. Thermofisher, CA, USA) and Applied Biosystems 7500 (Thermofisher). PCR reaction parameters were as follows: 95 °C for 10 s, 60 °C for 20 s, 72 °C for 20 s, 40 cycles. The differences between groups of Uc001kfo and α-SMA mRNA were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method with U6 and β-actin as the internal reference. The following specific PCR primers were used: Uc001kfo, 5′-AACGAGTCAGAGCTTTGGC-3′ (forward) and 5′-AACAGGAATACGATGAAGCC-3′(reverse); α-SMA, 5′-CTATCAGGGGGCACCACTATGTA-3′ (forward) and 5′-GCTCCGGAGGGGCAATGA-3′ (reverse); β-actin, 5′-CCTGTACGCCAACACAGTGC-3′(forward) and 5′-ATACTCCTGCTTGCTGATCC-3′ (reverse); U6, 5′-GTGCTCGCTTCGGCAGCACAT-3’ (forward) and 5’- TACCTTGCGAAGTGCTTAAAC-3′(reverse).

## Western blot
The total protein was extracted from the cells using the M-PER mammalian protein extraction reagent or from tissues using the T-PER tissue protein extraction reagent (78503/78510, Pierce, IL, USA). Equal amounts of protein (20 μg per lane) estimated by the bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit (A55865, Pierce) was loaded onto (11%) SDS-PAGE gels and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. The blots were probed with a monoclonal antibody against human α-SMA (ab5831, 1:200), Vimentin (ab20346, 1:400), E-cadherin (ab231303. 1:350) and β-actin (ab8226, 1:1200) (Abcam), followed by the secondary HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit antibody (ab28815, 1:3000) (Abcam). After washing, the bands were detected by chemiluminescence and imaged with X-ray films. β-actin was used as an endogenous reference for normalization.

## Statistical analysis
Data were obtained from at least 3 biological individual experiments and expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). The statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS 18.0 software (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). A one-way ANOVA followed by a post hoc test was used for multiple group comparisons, and Student’s t-test (two-tailed) was used for comparisons of two groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## The expression levels of Uc001kfo and α-SMA in HCC cells were significantly increased than those in normal hepatocytes
The RT-qPCR results showed that the expression levels of Uc001kfo and α-SMA mRNA were significantly increased in Hep3B and Luh-7 compared to the THLE-3 (all p < 0.01, vs. THLE-3 cells), and they were even higher in SUN-449 and HCCLM3 cells (all p < 0.01, vs. THLE-3 cells) (Fig.  A). Western blot results confirmed that the expression trend of α-SMA protein was consistent with its mRNA levels in these cells (Fig.  B). We selected SUN-449 and HCCLM3 cells for subsequent experiments due to their relatively low and high α-SMA expression. 

## Uc001kfo is an upstream transcriptional activator of α-SMA
RT-qPCR data showed that the expression levels of Uc001kfo were significantly decreased in Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo infected cells (p < 0.01, vs. control group), there was no significant difference between the Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo + Lv-α-SMA group and the Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group (p > 0.5). The RT-qPCR data also showed that Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo infection significantly decreased the α-SMA mRNA levels in HCC cells (p < 0.01, vs control group), while the Lv-α-SMA infection reversed the effect of Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo on α-SMA mRNA levels (p < 0.01, vs. Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group). Lv-NC had no significant effect on the expression of Uc001kfo and α-SMA mRNA in these HCC cells (p > 0.5, vs. control group) (Fig.  C, ). Importantly, the above results showed a high degree of consistency in these two HCC cell lines, SUN-449 and HCCLM3. These data suggest that Uc001kfo silencing reduces Uc001kfo levels in HCC cells, independent of exogenous α-SMA. Furthermore, Uc001kfo silencing led to a decrease in α-SMA mRNA levels, which was reversed by the addition of exogenous α-SMA, indicating that Uc001kfo regulates α-SMA transcription from an upstream position in HCC cells.

## Uc001kfo silencing can significantly inhibit the invasion and migration in HCC cells, which can be reversed by the exogenous α-SMA
Cell invasion assay results demonstrated that Uc001kfo silencing inhibited the invasion of SUN-449 cells (p < 0.01, vs. control group). This inhibitory effect of Uc001kfo silencing was reversed by the exogenous α-SMA (p < 0.01, vs. Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group) (Fig.  A, ). The wound healing assay indicated that the migration abilities of SUN-449 cells in the different groups followed trends consistent with their invasion capabilities (Fig.  C, ). The experiments conducted in another HCC cell line, HCCLM3, yielded identical results (data not shown). These experimental results collectively suggest that Uc001kfo silencing inhibits the invasion and migration of HCC cells by suppressing α-SMA expression, supporting our previous hypothesis that Uc001kfo is a positive upstream regulatory factor of α-SMA. 

## Uc001kfo silencing can affect the expression of metastasis-regulating proteins in HCC cells by suppressing α-SMA
Western blotting results showed that Uc001kfo silencing significantly suppressed the expression of proteins α-SMA and Vimentin, but significantly up-regulated the expression of E-cadherin in SUN-449 cells (all p < 0.01, vs. control group). However, overexpression of exogenous α-SMA reversed the effect of Uc001kfo silencing on these metastasis-regulating proteins (all p < 0.01, vs. Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group) in SUN-449 and HCCLM3) (Fig.  E). At the same time, experiments carried out in another HCC cell line, HCCLM3, produced consistent results (data not shown).

## The nuclear transcription factor Sp1 mediates the transcriptional regulation of Uc001kfo on α-SMA
Bioinformatics prediction showed that there was a TFBS (5’-GGGCGG-3’) of Sp1 in the promoter of human α-SMA. Luciferase assay data showed that luciferase activity in 293 cells was significantly enhanced in the Lv-Sp1 + pGL3-pro-(TFBS-wt)-α-SMA group (p < 0.01, vs. pGL3-pro-(TFBS-wt)-α-SMA group), but remained unchanged in the Lv-Sp1 + pGL3-pro-(TFBS-mt)-α-SMA group (p > 0.05, vs. pGL3-pro-(TFBS-wt)-α-SMA group) (Fig.  A). The ChIP experiment results revealed that DNA fragments from the human α-SMA gene promoter were successfully identified in the eluate of the Sp1 protein in HCCLM3 cells (Fig.  B). Furthermore, our RIP experiment data confirms that α-SMA protein is not directly bound to Uc001kfo (data not shown), while Sp1 can directly bind to Uc001kfo in HCCLM3 cells (Fig.  C). The aforementioned results collectively suggest that Uc001kfo indirectly enhances α-SMA gene transcription by increasing the activity of Sp1 protein. However, what is the direct interaction pattern between Uc001kfo and Sp1? To investigate this, we examined the potential effects of Uc001kfo on the synthesis and degradation of Sp1 protein. Experimental findings revealed that exogenous expression of Uc001kfo did not influence the synthesis of intracellular Sp1 protein but significantly inhibited its degradation in SUN-449 cells (Fig.  D). This finding supports the hypothesis that Uc001kfo promotes α-SMA transcription by upregulating Sp1 protein in HCC cells. 

## Uc001kfo silencing effectively inhibited the splenic-to- liver metastasis of HCC cells in mice
Twenty days after splenic injection of HCCLM3 cells, we evaluated the effect of Uc001kfo silencing on liver metastasis by measuring liver weight and performing HE staining. Liver weight was significantly higher in the model and Lv-NC groups compared to the Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group (p < 0.05, vs. Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group) (Fig.  E). HE staining confirmed the reduced metastatic burden in the livers of mice in the Lv-shRNA-Uc001kfo group compared to the model and Lv-NC groups (Fig.  F, ).

## Discussion
Significant progress has been made in liver cancer prevention and treatment, including approved anti-angiogenic therapies and new potential targets for HCC angiogenesis. However, further study on angiogenesis mechanisms is needed to develop more effective treatments [ ]. Experimental studies on the immunoglobulin superfamily (IgSF) member EWI2 have shown that it suppresses various solid malignant tumors, including brain, lung, skin, and prostate cancers, in animal models. Additionally, it inhibits tumor cell movement and growth in vitro. EWI2 and its related family members have emerged as significant regulators of malignant diseases, with promising potential as anti-cancer therapeutics and targets for cancer therapy, influencing processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, metastasis, metabolism, senescence, and drug resistance [ ]. These findings highlight the importance of identifying genetic therapy targets for precision treatment in liver cancer. Current evidence suggests that lncRNA play a crucial role in regulating key cancer hallmarks, including proliferation, apoptosis, metastasis, metabolism, senescence and drug resistance [ ]. To date, lncRNAs have been demonstrated to play diverse roles in the progression of HCC [ , ]. Panzitt et al. [ ] found that lncRNA-HULC, highly upregulated in HCC, had a significant predictive effect on tumor growth and metastasis of HCC. The mechanisms study revealed that HULC abolishes the binding of miR-107 on the Atg12 3'-UTR and promotes autophagy and metastasis of HCC by acting as an endogenous sponge. MALAT1 has been proved to be closely associated with high risk of metastasis and poor prognosis of HCC patients [ ]. HOTAIR, served as an auxiliary inhibitor of transcription silencers, suppressed the transcription of tumor metastasis suppressors to increase the risk of metastasis. Studies have shown that high expression levels of HOTAIR are typically indicative of high metastasis rates and low survival rates in HCC patients [ , ]. Uc001kfo, as a novel lncRNA validated in our previous study. Our study firstly showed that Uc001kfo was significantly increased in HCC tissues than that in cirrhosis liver tissues, and the clinical follow-up data showed HCC patients with higher levels of Uc001kfo had a shorter survival time compared to those with lower Uc001kfo [ ]. Therefore, we believed that Uc001kfo should play a crucial role in the progression of HCC.
Transcription information of UCSC database displayed that Uc001kfo located on human chromosome10, and located between the base sites 90,689,711 to 90,682,420. As the transcripts, the size of Uc001kfo was 2043 bp. Using a genome browser and RNAplex, α-SAM was predicted as the target protein of UC001kfo. Our previous study also showed that Uc001kfo and α-SMA maintained a positive correlation in expression levels across different grades of HCC tissues and their adjacent non-cancerous tissues. More importantly, both Uc001kfo and α-SMA expression levels increased with the malignant progression of HCC, particularly in relation to invasion and distant metastasis characteristics. Additionally, our functional experiments confirmed that silencing Uc001kfo effectively inhibited the proliferation and invasion of HCC cells by suppressing α-SMA expression [ ]. However, the mechanism by which Uc001kfo promotes α-SMA expression remains to be further studied.
Previous literatures showed that α-SMA was one of six conservative actin isotypes and was a major component of cytoskeleton and the main member of contraction mechanism, which is responsible for cell movements and the maintenance of cell shape. Other studies also showed that cancer cells with high α-SMA expression have characteristics of mesenchymal cells, and α-SMA expression was a marker of EMT in cancer cells and closely related to patient prognosis [ , ]. Our previous study demonstrated that α-SMA was highly expressed in HCC tissues than that in the adjacent tissues. The HCC cells with strong α-SMA staining were fibroblast-like in shape, indicating that α-SMA enhanced the invasion and proliferation of HCC cells, which is in agreement with previous studies [ ]. In the present study, we first verified the correlation between Uc001kfo and α-SMA in HCC cells and normal hepatic epithelial cells and found that Uc001kfo and α-SMA were significantly increases in HCC cells than that in normal hepatic epithelial cells. Moreover, there is a synchronous change of α-SMA between groups at the transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels (mRNA and protein), suggesting that the impact of Uc001kfo on α-SMA originates from a mechanism of action at the transcriptional level. In order to clarify the relationship between Uc001kfo and α-SMA, we first verified the upstream–downstream relationship between the two. The results showed that in Uc001kfo silenced HCC cells, the contents of Uc001kfo and α-SMA mRNA decreased significantly, and α-SMA overexpression did not affect the effect of Uc001kfo silencing on itself, but reversed the effects of Uc001kfo silencing on α-SMA expression in HCC cells. Therefore, we believed that Uc001kfo should be an upstream regulatory factor of α-SMA and there should be a nuclear transcription factor which mediates the transcriptional regulation of α-SMA by Uc001kfo in HCC cells.We investigated the possible transcription factors which can bind to the α-SMA promoter using JASPAR software, and searched the database and literature for transcription factors associated with HCC invasion and metastasis at the same time. After a series of progressive screening, the transcription factor Sp1 was identified. Luciferase and ChIP experiments confirmed that Sp1 could up-regulate the transcriptional activity of α-SMA by binding to its promoter on TFBS(5′-GGGCGG-3′). More importantly, the RIP data confirmed that Uc001kfo could not directly bind to protein α-SMA but Sp1, suggesting that Uc001kfo regulates α-SMA indirectly and supporting our hypothesis that Sp1 mediates their interaction. So far, we have enough reasons to believe that a new regulatory pathways Uc001kfo/Sp1/α-SMA exists in the invasion and metastasis of HCC. Sp1 is a sequence-specific DNA-binding protein that regulates the transcription of cellular and viral genes containing GC-rich promoters [ ]. It is widely present in the nucleus of almost all tissues and is involved in the regulation of a variety of physiological and pathological processes. Sp1 plays an important role in the development of many tumors by regulating a variety of genes which are important in the growth, differentiation and metastasis of tumor cells [ , ]. Although Sp1 can regulate the expression of thousands of genes there are no reports on Sp1 involving in transcriptional regulation of α-SMA so far. Although our RIP experiment confirmed the direct binding of Uc001kfo to Sp1, further experimental evidence is needed to substantiate their direct interaction. Subsequent protein half-life experiment showed that exogenous Uc001kfo could significantly prolong the half-life of Sp1 protein in SUN-449 cells by inhibiting its degradation. We then predicted the ubiquitination site of Sp1 through the bioinformatics software Gromacs, the results showed that the ubiquitination site of Sp1 might be located in the amino acid (LRWHTG) sequence at 647–652 sections of Sp1 which is the binding site of E3 to ubiquitin ligase. Even so, whether the inhibition of Sp1 degradation by Uc001kfo depends on its suppression of Sp1 protein ubiquitination requires extensive further research.
Our study suggests that the Uc001kfo/Sp1/α-SMA axis plays a key role in regulating HCC progression. In rescue experiments, exogenous α-SMA reversed the reduced invasion and migration of HCC cells caused by Uc001kfo silencing, and counteracted changes in EMT markers like Vimentin and E-cadherin. This indicates that Uc001kfo, as an upstream regulator in this pathway, could be a potential therapeutic target for HCC (Fig.  ). Preliminary evaluation using a spleen-to-liver metastasis mouse model showed that Uc001kfo silencing inhibited HCC metastasis. Although this is the first confirmation of the Uc001kfo/Sp1/α-SMA pathway’s involvement in HCC progression, several questions remain. We need to explore the causes of Uc001kfo’s abnormal expression, and its demographic and genetic characteristics. To address this, we have started building a large HCC sample bank and patient database. Early findings suggest that promoter methylation may contribute to the abnormal expression of Uc001kfo in HCC tissues. 
In conclusion, we have identified the Uc001kfo/Sp1/α-SMA pathway as a key regulator in the progression of HCC and explored the interactions among its components (Fig.  ). These findings provide valuable theoretical support for considering Uc001kfo as a potential new therapeutic target for HCC.
