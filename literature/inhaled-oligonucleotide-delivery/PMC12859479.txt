However, persistent inflammation drives a pro-fibrotic shift, leading to cardiac remodeling and heart failure, rendering single-target therapies largely ineffective in addressing the full pathological course. To address this challenge, we developed a temporally modulated nanodelivery system. This platform integrates the natural immunomodulator zymosan A (ZymA) with siRNA-21 (SiR21) through intelligent assembly, enabling dual immunoregulatory and gene-silencing functions (ZymA@SiR21). The system is associated with the binding of mannose groups on the ZymA surface to mannose receptors to achieve macrophage-specific targeting. This nanoplatform demonstrates time-dependent therapeutic precision: (1) During acute MI, ZymA induces macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype, enhancing phagocytic clearance of necrotic debris; (2) In the chronic-phase, released SiR21 sustains macrophage reprogramming to reparative M2 phenotypes and inhibits fibroblast hyperactivation. This “clearance-to-repair” strategy achieved superior outcomes in animal models and no systemic immune hyperactivation. By orchestrating macrophage dynamics, the system accelerates the restoration of inflammation-repair balance post-MI, offering a paradigm-shifting approach for stage-specific cardiac therapy.

## Introduction
Myocardial infarction (MI) is one of the most common types of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) [ , ]. Over the past decade, despite optimized treatment regimens, the MI remains the main cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [ , ]. In recent years, accumulating evidence has shown that occlusion of the coronary artery leads to widespread cardiomyocyte necrosis, which in turn triggers a robust inflammatory response aimed at clearing necrotic cellular debris [ , , ]. It is well established that cardiac remodeling is associated with poor prognosis in patients after MI [ ]. The extent of cardiac remodeling following MI is determined by the balance between necrotic debris clearance and the regulation of scar formation [ , ]. Post infarct healing and optimal scar formation play crucial roles in the strengthening of the infarct tissue and in preventing adverse remodeling and cardiac rupture [ , , ]. The inflammatory response mediated by various immune cells as well as inflammatory factors play vital roles in the process of myocardial necrosis and repair after MI [ , , ]. The spectrum of inflammatory dysregulation—ranging from an excessive response to its improper resolution is a critical determinant of post-MI outcomes. It directly influences the efficacy of myocardial repair, thereby modulating the risk of adverse ventricular remodeling and the subsequent development of heart failure [ , ]. Accumulating evidence has shown that cardiac repair post-MI requires an orchestrated inflammatory reaction [ , , ]. In the early phase of inflammation response dominated by immune cells. After MI, the intensive pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines are released to outbreak inflammatory process to digest damaged cells and extracellular matrix (ECM) tissue [ ]. Next, the inflammatory phase gradually switches to reparative phase including inflammation resolution, neovascularization and scar formation [ , ].
As the primary responders of the body's immune system, macrophages are indispensable for both cardiac homeostasis and myocardial repair [ , , , , ]. Macrophages are generally divided into classically activated M1 and alternatively activated M2 macrophages [ , ]. The early post-MI period is marked by the dominance of pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages, which mediate extracellular matrix breakdown and phagocytosis of cellular debris. This phenotypic switch is associated with the transition to the reparative phase (days 4–7), during which anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages become prominent, acting to dampen inflammation, stimulate angiogenesis, and support collagen deposition [ , ]. Accumulating evidence suggests that the prolonged and excessive M1 macrophages may exacerbate the inflammatory response and disturb the reparative stage, thus modulation of macrophage polarization status is critical for myocardial repair [ ]. Recently, earlier phenotype shift of M1 to M2 macrophages has demonstrated significant improvements in MI wound modeling and cardiac function [ , , , ]. Although early inflammatory activation is a necessary event for the transition to the later reparative program, proper and timely repression and resolution of acute inflammation are determinants of the quality of cardiac repair [ , ]. Given this context, promoting an orchestrated shift from the M1 to the M2 macrophage phenotype—specifically by enhancing earlier and more robust M2 macrophage infiltration represents a promising therapeutic strategy for mitigating myocardial injury post-MI. It is well established that the innate immune system detects pathogens via pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs) [ , ] or C-type lectin receptors (CLRs) [ ], which are expressed on innate immune cells and recognize pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) like fungal cell wall polysaccharides. Upon detecting these signals, the innate immune response induces immune cells to alter their gene expression profiles and secrete cytokines, thereby orchestrating adaptive immunity [ , ].
Zymosan A (ZymA) is a non-cellular, potent innate immune response activator with immunomodulatory functions, primarily consisting of a β-1,3-glucan core linked to chitin and β-1,6-glucans [ , , ]. Upon engaging PRRs on innate immune cells such as dendritic cells and macrophages, ZymA induces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ. Recent evidence indicates that this pro-inflammatory activity, however, underpins its beneficial effects in cardiac repair post I/R injury. ZymA facilitates the recovery of cardiac pumping function by mounting a sharp, acute immune response and recruiting macrophages, thereby confirming the essential role of timely inflammation and macrophages in promoting tissue repair [ , ]. Also, ZymA improved cardiac healing and ameliorated doxorubicin-induced ventricular remodeling and dysfunction by activating macrophages [ , , ]. By the way, ZymA can also be used as a nanocarrier to achieve drug delivery [ , , , ]. Translating these preclinical findings into clinical practice faces two principal barriers: the reliance on invasive local injection for efficacy studies and the systemic toxicity of unmodified ZymA due to immune overactivation. For ZymA to realize its potential as a myocardial protective agent, development of a nano-formulation is essential, offering a pathway to overcome these limitations through several key advantages: (1) a reduced particle size distribution facilitates greater exposure of targeting motifs, enhancing macrophage specificity; (2) improved macrophage uptake efficiency allows for a potential reduction in therapeutic dose; (3) mitigated systemic toxicity as a result of lower dosage requirements; and (4) an increased surface-area-to-volume ratio enables its utility as a versatile drug delivery platform.
Beyond cellular and molecular regulators, emerging evidence underscores the critical roles of miRNAs in the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of myocardial infarction [ , , ]. Among these, miR-21 is notably abundant in the cardiac microenvironment, where it is recognized for its potent profibrotic activity [ , ]. Reducing miR-21 expression specifically in fibroblasts has been demonstrated to attenuate pathological fibrosis progression [ , ]. Recent studies further demonstrate that miR-21 exacerbates myocardial fibrosis and adverse remodeling by driving macrophage polarization toward a pro-inflammatory (M1) phenotype and modulating paracrine signals, which in turn activates the differentiation of cardiac fibroblasts into myofibroblasts [ , ]. This insight implicates macrophage-specific inhibition of miR-21 as a viable strategy to suppress pathological fibrosis and support functional recovery post-MI. Accordingly, small interfering RNA targeting miR-21 (siR21) emerges as a promising novel therapeutic paradigm for mitigating myocardial infarction injury [ ]. However, the systemic delivery of naked SiR21 faces major challenges stemming from the inherent physicochemical limitations of modified RNA (modRNA). Moreover, direct myocardial administration typically requires invasive procedures, including catheter-based intramyocardial injection or open-chest surgery. Importantly, these delivery methods share a critical drawback with cell injection therapies: rapid clearance via venous drainage, which severely limits local retention and efficacy [ ]. Collectively, it is imperative to develop a novel delivery system for miR-RNA therapeutics that fulfills three critical requirements: (1) providing adequate protection for the miR-RNA therapeutic agents, (2) enabling systemic administration, and (3) achieving effective accumulation in the targeted pathological regions. 
Based on the aforementioned background, this study focuses on exploring novel cardioprotective strategies following MI through the immunomodulation of macrophage behavior. A key challenge in this field lies in the coordinated regulation of the acute inflammatory response and the long-term tissue repair process, aiming to prevent secondary damage from excessive inflammation while promoting the restoration of homeostasis. The design of ZymA@SiR21 specifically addresses this dual requirement, aiming to achieve spatiotemporal synergy between inflammation initiation and repair regulation. This system leverages the inherent macrophage-targeting property of ZymA to achieve efficient delivery to cardiac inflammatory sites. It regulates macrophage function through a dual “spatiotemporal coordination” mechanism: ZymA rapidly activates an inflammatory response to initiate pathogen clearance, while the co-delivered SiR21 exerts a synergistic therapeutic effect—both appropriately moderating potential excessive inflammation induced by ZymA and continuously downregulating miR-21 levels to suppress macrophage-mediated excessive fibrosis during the repair phase, thereby achieving long-term cardioprotection. The mechanism of action can be divided into two complementary phases: short-term and long-term ( ). In the short-term phase, the ZymA component within the nano-system swiftly triggers a controlled acute immune response. This process does not merely exacerbate inflammation; rather, it actively and orderly recruits and activates immune cells such as macrophages to accelerate the clearance of necrotic cells and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) in the infarcted area, achieving efficient “immune debridement”. This phase removes pathological obstacles for subsequent repair and promotes the transition of the microenvironment from a pro-inflammatory state to reparative stage. In the long-term phase, the carrier-loaded SiR21 exerts sustained and prolonged regulatory effects. Unlike traditional fast-acting drugs, the SiR21 gradually modulates the expression of target genes, continuously influencing the phenotype and function of key cells such as macrophages—for instance, promoting their polarization toward a reparative phenotype and coordinating collagen deposition and angiogenesis in the repair process. Although this gene-level regulation acts relatively slowly, its effects are enduring. It seamlessly aligns temporally with the acute response mediated by ZymA, jointly ensuring a smooth transition from the resolution of acute inflammation to robust tissue repair. ZymA@SiR21 achieves complementary and synergistic effects through its two components along the temporal dimension: the acute “immune debridement” dominated by ZymA creates favorable conditions for tissue repair, while the long-term “gene regulation” mediated by SiR21 consolidates and sustains the repair outcomes. Together, they optimize the entire injury-repair process following myocardial infarction, ultimately aiming to achieve long-term improvement in cardiac function and prognosis.

## Materials & methods
2.1

## Reagents and materials
All chemical reagents and biological materials were obtained from commercial suppliers as follows: N,N-Dimethylacrylamide, ZymA, ICG, and FITC were sourced from Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Molecular biology reagents including SiR21, Tris-acetate-EDTA (TAE) buffer, Gel Red nucleic acid stain, and agarose gel were purchased from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Cell assay kits (BCA protein assay kit, Calcein/PI cell viability kit, CCK-8 assay kit) and DNA loading buffer were obtained from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). RAW264.7 murine macrophage cell line and CD206 mouse monoclonal antibody (1:500 dilution) were acquired from Servicebio (Wuhan, China). Fetal bovine serum (FBS) was procured from Gibco (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). iNOS rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:500 dilution) was purchased from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). H9c2 rat cardiomyocyte cell line was obtained from Pricella (Wuhan, China). PCR-related reagents (PrimeScript RT reagent kit and FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master Mix) were sourced from Vazyme Biotech (Nanjing, China).

2.2

## Preparation of ZymA@SiR21
Positively charged quaternized poly (dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate) (PDMA) synthesis was performed as previously reported in the literature [ , ]. The details are as follows: an appropriate amount of chain transfer agent and purified, was synthesized using N,N-Dimethylacrylamide, which were dissolved in anhydrous dioxane. The mixture was placed in liquid nitrogen for decompression and dissolution. This process was repeated thrice, and the reaction system was sealed and polymerized at 80 °C for 24 h. The ratio of initiator to chain transfer agent was 0.2: 1, and the total amount of various monomers and the weight ratio of chain transfer agent to dioxane was 0.5. After completion, the reaction mixture was put into liquid nitrogen for termination. The mixture was precipitated with excess methyl tert butyl ether to obtain the residue. After vacuum drying, the polymer was obtained and stored at −20 °C. The product was analyzed via 1H NMR, and the degree of polymerization was calculated as PDMA50 with a molecular weight of approximately 5.0 kDa ( ).
2.2.1

## Preparation of quaternized PDMA
The quaternization reaction of PDMA was carried out by dissolving PDMA in an organic solvent, followed by the addition of an excess amount of bromoethane [ , ]. The reaction was allowed to proceed overnight at room temperature. After the reaction, the product was purified through precipitation to obtain quaternized PDMA.

2.2.2

## Treatment of ZymA
ZymA was dissolved in pure water at a concentration of 5 mg/mL. The solution was subjected to ultrasonic treatment using an ultrasonic homogenizer (FS-1200N, 20 kHz, 1500 W, Shanghai Sonxi Ultrasonic Instruments Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) in pulse mode (4 s on, 2 s off) at 30 % power and maintained at 4 °C. The size distribution and zeta potential of ZymA dispersions were measured after different durations of ultrasonic treatment to determine the optimal conditions for obtaining ZymA dispersions with suitable particle size and surface properties.

2.2.3

## Fabrication of ZymA@SiR21
The obtained ZymA dispersion (1 mg/mL) was mixed with a 0.1 mg/mL aqueous solution of quaternized PDMA at different volume ratios under rapid stirring. After the system stabilized, the size distribution and surface charge of the mixed system were measured to evaluate the formation of the ZymA NPs. This process ensures the preparation of ZymA NPs with tailored physicochemical properties for further applications. For FITC labeling, an FITC ethanol solution was first mixed with the ZymA dispersion, and the organic solvent was removed. Subsequently, RNA and the PDMA solution were added sequentially following the same procedure described above. After mixing and standing, centrifugation was performed to remove unbound free FITC, yielding ZymA NPs-FITC or ZymA@SiR21-FITC. The ICG labeling process was consistent with the RNA loading steps, and after preparation, centrifugation was carried out to remove free ICG, thereby obtaining the ZymA NPs-ICG or ZymA@SiR21-ICG.

2.2.4

## Optimization of RNA loading in ZymA@SiR21
To evaluate RNA loading efficiency, we prepared ZymA@SiR21 complexes at varying ratios. The complexes were mixed with DNA loading buffer and electrophoresed on a 1 % agarose gel (containing Gel Red) in 1 × TAE buffer at 150 V for 20 min. Gel images were acquired using a Bio-Rad Chemi Doc imaging system.

2.3

## Cell culture
The mouse mononuclear macrophage RAW264.7 cells and the cardiomyocytes H9c2 rat cells were cultured in DMEM with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin/streptomycin with 5 % CO2 at 37 °C.

2.4

## Cellular uptake
In vitro uptake experiments were performed using mononuclear macrophage RAW264.7 cell lines and cardiomyocytes H9C2 cell lines. All cells were seeded in confocal dishes at 5 × 104 cells and incubated with ZymA NPs-FITC or ZymA@SiR21-FITC. For the mannose inhibition experiment, 100 μg/mL of mannose was pre-incubated with macrophages overnight, followed by incubation with ZymA@SiR21-FITC for different durations to observe cellular uptake. For the LPS stimulation experiment, 1 μg/mL of LPS was pre-incubated with macrophages overnight, followed by incubation with ZymA@SiR21-FITC for different durations to observe cellular uptake. A laser confocal microscope (OLYMPUS, FV3000) was used to visualize the labeled cells (the excitation wavelength was 405 nm and the emission wavelength was 460 ± 35 nm for Hoechst 33,342; the excitation wavelength was 488 nm and the emission wavelength was 515 ± 10 nm for FITC).

2.5

## Cytotoxicity and safety evaluation of ZymA@SiR21
It initially evaluated the therapeutic potential of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 for MI injury through cytotoxicity assessments conducted in both RAW264.7 murine macrophage and H9c2 rat cardiomyocyte cell lines. For in vitro cytotoxicity evaluation, a CCK-8 assay was performed on both RAW264.7 and H9c2 cell lines. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well and cultured overnight for adherence. Following incubation, the culture medium was replaced with either fresh medium or medium containing ZymA@SiR21 at varying concentrations (25, 50, 100, 200, 400 μg/mL) for 24 h. Subsequently, 100 μL of medium containing 10 % CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for 1 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a Bio-Rad microplate reader (USA). Cell viability was calculated as: (ODexperimental/ODcontrol) × 100 %, with data presented as mean ± SD from triplicate experiments.

2.6

## Cell viability assessment
Cell viability was evaluated using the Calcein-AM/PI Cell Viability/Cytotoxicity Assay Kit. Briefly, RAW264.7 and H9c2 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well and allowed to adhere. Following experimental treatments, cells were incubated with a working solution containing both calcein-AM and propidium iodide (PI) for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. Fluorescence images were acquired from randomly selected fields using a Leica DMi8 fluorescence microscope (Leica Microsystems, Germany) equipped with appropriate filter sets.

2.7

## Hemolysis test
For hemocompatibility evaluation, The blood (1 mL) was collected from mice and immediately mixed with ice-cold PBS (2 mL, pH 7.4). After centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min, the serum was carefully removed and the pelleted red blood cells (RBCs) were washed with PBS. Finally, a 2 % (v/v) RBC suspension was prepared in PBS for subsequent experiments. Then, the positive group, negative group, and experimental group were conducted following previously reported methods [ ]. The deionized water and red blood cells were mixed in a 1:1 ratio (v/v) in the positive group, while PBS was mixed with red blood cell suspension in a 1:1 vol ratio in the negative group. Then, the nanodrug solution was mixed with different concentrations of the red blood cell suspension in a 1:1 ratio (v/v), and the final concentration of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 was 25, 50, 100, 200, 400 μg/mL. Each group consisted of three parallel experiments, conducted in duplicate. The mixture of each group was kept at room temperature for 3 h and centrifuged. The absorbance of the supernatant was measured by a UV–vis spectrometer at 570 nm to calculate the hemolysis rate using the following formula: Hemolysis rate (%) = (ODsample-ODnegative)/(ODpositive-ODnegative) × 100 %.

2.8

## Immunofluorescence staining
The RAW264.7 macrophages were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells/well and fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 30 min at room temperature. After washed with PBS three times, the cells were permeabilized with 0.1 % Triton X-100 for 15 min. The plate was incubated overnight with primary antibody iNOS (1:500) Rabbit pAb and CD206 (1:500) mouse mAb at 4 °C, and then incubated with immunofluorescent secondary antibody anti-rabbit IgG-(H + L) (1:500) for 2 h at room temperature and away from light. The plate was washed thrice with PBS for 10 min, and then incubated with immunofluorescent secondary antibody anti-mouse IgG-(H + L) (1:500) for 2 h at and washed thrice again. Then the samples incubated with DAPI, and incubated for 15 min in the darkness, washed thrice again with PBS for 10 min each. The cells were then observed using a fluorescence inverted microscope.

2.9

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) analysis
Total RNA from the RAW264.7 cells in different groups was extracted from cells using RNAiso Plus and reverse transcription was performed using a PrimeScript RT reagent Kit to produce cDNA. One microgram cDNA was subjected to qPCR using FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master Mix. Reactions were performed in triplicate using Applied Biosystems 7500 machine (primers were listed in ). β-actin was used to normalize the expression level. The total reaction system is 10 μL. Reaction conditions: 95 °C for 30 s, 95 °C for 3 s, 55 °C for 30 s, 72 °C for 30 s, 95 °C for 60 s, 55 °C for 30 s, and 95 °C for 30 s. 42 amplification cycles were performed. The PCR was confirmed by the product melting curve, and the Ct values of the target gene and β-actin of the sample were obtained based on the PCR curve. The relative expression level for target genes were performed with a value of 2-ΔΔCt.

2.10

## Mouse model of myocardial infarction
A total of SPF-grade C57BL/6J male mice (8 weeks old, weighs about 22 g) were purchased from Beijing SiPeiFu Company. All animals were maintained on a 12 h light-dark cycle with free access to normal chow and water. The animals care and procedure were aligned with the Care and Use Committee of Shanxi Medical University. All experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Shanxi Medical University. All animal studies were conducted in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines.
All mice were randomly divided into four groups, namely sham operation group, MI model group, ZymA NPs group and ZymA@SiR21 group, respectively. The mice were anesthetized with isoflurane gas (inhalation of 2 % L/min isoflurane) and their normal breathing was maintained via a ventilator. A horizontal incision of approximately 5 mm was made on the left side of the sternum, between the fourth ribs. The subcutaneous tissue was dissected, and the pectoral muscles and intercostal muscles were carefully separated to expose the intercostal space. The intercostal space was opened, and the heart was gently compressed with the left hand to make it protrude. Using 7-0 suture, the left atrium was ligated approximately 5 mm below the left auricle, resulting in whitening of the myocardium. The chest was then closed rapidly, and the skin was sutured using 3-0 suture material. Each animal was numbered postoperatively using a systematic labeling for identification. Different nanomedicines were injected via the tail vein at 30 min after model preparation. Blood was taken from the orbit of the mice three days after the administration for short term inflammatory factors, and then the mice were continued to be fed. After 4 weeks, an ultrahigh frequency photoacoustic ultrasound imaging system (Vevo LAZR-X, Fuji Visual Sonics) was used to monitor the left ventricular function of mice via cardiac echocardiography imaging. Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane, and 2D Mmode guided tracking was used to provide short-axis maps at the level of papillary muscles. The left ventricular ejection fraction (EF), left ventricular rational shortening (FS), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVVol; d), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVVol; s), and left ventricular mass (corrected) were measured.

2.11

## Histological evaluation
All mice were randomly divided into two groups, the short-acting group and the long-acting group. Mice in the short-acting group were humanely sacrificed on the third day after administration. After injecting the Nanomedicines at 28 days, mice in the long-acting group were humanely sacrificed. The hearts, lungs, livers, and kidneys from each group were extracted, washed with PBS, and fixed in 4 % formaldehyde at 4 °C for 48 h. Subsequently, these organs underwent dehydration using an ethanol gradient, were cleared with xylene, and ultimately embedded in paraffin. Following tissue sectioning, staining was performed, and an examination was conducted under a fluorescence microscope. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining were used for observation of pathological changes. Masson staining was used to observe the degree of myocardial fibrosis in the transverse section of the heart. The percentage of fibrosis area to the total LV area was then calculated. The blood serum was separated to detect the content of TGF-β1, IL-10, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α levels. Remaining steps were followed to the manufacturer's instruction, and the absorbance at 450 nm was recorded by using a microplate reader.

2.12

## Fluorescence imaging ex vivo
ZymA NPs-ICG or ZymA@SiR21-ICG were intravenously injected into the Sham and MI model for targeted imaging of heart. These mice were monitored using the IVIS system after 1, 2, 4, 8, 24 h, postinjection to obtain fluorescence images. For fluorescence imaging with ICG, the excitation wavelength was 780 nm and the emission wavelength was 800–820 nm.

2.13

## Statistical analysis
Treatment across groups was randomly generated and subjects identified with random identifiers. Investigators were blinded to treatment during both data acquisition and analysis. Unless otherwise specified, all analyses are represented as mean±standard error of the mean (SEM). Comparisons across groups utilized one-way analysis of variance, while individual comparisons between groups were performed using Tukey's honestly significant difference test. All statistical analyses were performed using Prism 5.0 statistical software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA). P values of <0.05 were considered significant.

## Results
3.1

## Preparation and characterization of ZymA@SiR21 delivery system
To safely employ ZymA as an immune-stimulatory agent for in vivo injection, optimization is required. Given the size distribution and surface charge of its aqueous dispersion, homogenization was first performed via ultrasonication. This approach is expected to enhance negative charge exposure while narrowing the size distribution. Immunogenicity analysis of natural-product-based drug carriers revealed that immunogenicity was significantly attenuated with smaller particle sizes, while enhanced immunogenic responses were observed in coarse dispersions and larger particles [ ]. Moreover, the reduced size distribution allows for increased nucleic acid drug loading capacity while minimizing dosage, thereby achieving controlled immune stimulation. Based on the above considerations, the size distribution of its aqueous dispersion was optimized through ultrasonic treatment ( ).
Before ultrasonic treatment,the size distribution of ZymA was approximately 1.8 μm ( ), with a weakly negative surface charge and a zeta potential of −4.7 ± 0.4 mV. After 1 h of ultrasonic treatment, the size distribution decreased to approximately 400 nm ( ), with a zeta potential of −8.3 ± 2.6 mV. Following 8 h of ultrasonic treatment, the size distribution was further reduced to approximately 50 nm ( ), with a zeta potential of −12 ± 0.5 mV. This indicates that ultrasonic treatment disrupted the molecular chains of ZymA, increasing its specific surface area, which is beneficial for the loading of therapeutic components. As the ultrasonic treatment duration increased, the particle size gradually decreased, and the surface potential gradually increased ( ), exposing more negative charges. ZymA treated for 8 h was deemed most suitable for subsequent studies. To investigate whether the 8 h ultrasonication leads to structural degradation of ZymA, FTIR analysis was performed on ZymA before and after the treatment. As shown in , the FTIR spectra of the ultrasonicated ZymA sample exhibited highly consistent characteristic absorption peaks compared to the untreated sample. No significant changes were observed in the key functional groups or the characteristic peaks associated with the typical polysaccharide backbone. We speculate that possible reasons may include: ultrasonication primarily improved the dispersion state of the zymosan suspension, resulting in smaller particle sizes; or even if molecular chain scission occurred, the newly exposed chemical groups were identical to those originally present on the material surface.
The optimized ZymA will be used to construct nanocomplexes for macrophage targeting and modulation, as well as nucleic acid drug delivery.To achieve this, a cationic polymer was introduced to prepare nanocomplexes via electrostatic interactions with both ZymA and RNA. First, the ratio of ZymA to cationic polymer was optimized to form nanoparticles with desirable dispersion properties and surface characteristics. To optimize the preparation ratio of the nanocomplexes, size distribution and zeta potential of nanocomplexes formed after complexation of ZymA and PDMA were measured using dynamic light scattering (DLS). As the volume ratio of ZymA to PDMA gradually increased from 1:1 to 10:1, the size distribution of nanocomplexes rapidly decreased from approximately 600 nm–78 nm, while maintaining favorable polydispersity ( a and b, a). This indicates that ZymA and PDMA provide a reliable foundation for controlling the size distribution of nanocomplex. On the other hand, as the volume ratio of ZymA to PDMA increased, the zeta potential of nanocomplexes decreased from +19.4 ± 0.4 mV to −0.59 ± 0.05 mV ( ), suggesting that at this ratio, PDMA neutralized the negative charge of ZymA. These findings reveal that PDMA, as a positively charged carrier, can flexibly regulate the size distribution and surface properties of the nanocomplexes, thereby providing favorable conditions for subsequent loading of negatively charged nucleic acid. The size and morphology observed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were consistent with the DLS results ( d–f). 
For subsequent studies, the ZymA:PDMA formulation was fixed at a volume ratio of 10:1. The RNA loading capacity of the resulting nanoparticles was then evaluated. After RNA loading, DLS results showed that the system maintained a favorable size distribution of approximately 80 nm ( g and h), with a zeta potential of approximately −15 mV ( ). Agarose gel electrophoresis analysis of ZymA NPs mixed with RNA at different ratios revealed complete RNA loading at a complex-to-RNA ratio of 200:1 ( i). These results confirm the potential of ZymA NPs as an RNA delivery carrier. To evaluate the protective effect of ZymA@SiR21 on siRNA, experiments were performed in cell culture medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum to simulate the complex in vivo environment. Under these conditions, the stability of siRNA within the complex was assessed. The results demonstrated that naked siRNA was rapidly degraded in serum, with its stability difficult to maintain beyond 1 h. In contrast, siRNA within the ZymA@SiR21 complex was significantly protected and remained stable even after 4 h of incubation ( ). These findings indicate that the ZymA carrier can effectively bind and protect siRNA against degradation by nucleases in serum, providing in vitro experimental support for the integrity of siRNA during systemic circulation. Finally, to investigate the cellular uptake behavior, in vivo distribution, and cardiac targeting properties of the nanoparticles, fluorescent labeling was performed using FITC (for cellular uptake studies, ) and ICG (for in vivo imaging, ). The results indicated that the size distribution of the fluorescently labeled nanoparticles was largely consistent with that of the unlabeled group. The above results indicate that by optimizing the ratio of ZymA to PDMA, it can serve as an effective carrier for RNA or dye molecules, demonstrating excellent loading capacity and beneficial physicochemical properties. This lays a solid foundation for further investigation into its biological effects.

3.2

## Selective cellular uptake and cytotoxicity of ZymA@SiR21
To elucidate the targeting and uptake properties of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21, their selective cellular uptake was evaluated in RAW264.7 macrophages and H9c2 cardiomyocytes. The results revealed that both nanoparticles exhibited time-dependent, highly efficient uptake and internalization in RAW264.7 macrophages, with intracellular accumulation increasing continuously over time ( a and b). In contrast, only weak fluorescence signals were detected in H9c2 cardiomyocytes even after 8 h of incubation ( ). Further quantitative analysis showed no significant difference in the uptake efficiency of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 by macrophages ( ), indicating that the nano-system possesses a clear selective targeting ability toward macrophages, while being minimally taken up by cardiomyocytes. To investigate the underlying targeting mechanism, a mannose inhibition assay was performed to examine the specific recognition of ZymA@SiR21 by RAW264.7 macrophages ( ). The results demonstrated that pretreatment with mannose significantly inhibited the uptake of nanoparticles by macrophages, Infering that the targeting effect of ZymA to macrophages be mediated through mannose receptors on the cell surface. These findings strongly support the hypothesis that ZymA@SiR21 achieves specific macrophage targeting via recognition by mannose receptors. To more rigorously validate the specific recognition mechanism between ZymA and macrophages, the uptake of ZymA@SiR21 in LPS-pre-stimulated macrophages was further examined. The results indicated that after LPS pre-stimulation, the uptake efficiency of ZymA by macrophages was also significantly enhanced ( ). This finding can be interpreted from two perspectives: first, the uptake pathway of ZymA differs from that of LPS, which is likely attributed to differences in their surface glycosylation patterns—ZymA is rich in mannose residues, whereas LPS contains almost none of such structures; second, after LPS pre-stimulation, the macrophages polarize into the M1 state and in this stage macrophages exhibit enhanced phagocytic capacity, thereby leading to increased uptake of ZymA@SiR21. These results were further confirmed by flow cytometry ( ), which showed that ZymA-based nanoparticles can specifically target macrophages, and that loading SiR21 does not interfere with their recognition and uptake by macrophages. This provides a solid foundation for further in-depth investigation into the biological effects of this macrophage-targeting nano-system. These results indicated that ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 could be well uptaken by RAW264.7 macrophages after recognition by PRRs [ , ] of membrane. 
This study employs ZymA as an immunomodulator and RNA carrier to achieve myocardial protection through targeted regulation of macrophage function. However, given the potential off-target effects of nanomaterials and the limited regenerative capacity of terminally differentiated cardiomyocytes, it is necessary to evaluate the potential toxicity of this nano-formulation on cardiomyocytes to ensure the safety of the therapeutic strategy. Thereafter, to evaluate the biosafety profile of ZymA-based nanocarriers, it assessed the cytotoxicity of both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 in RAW264.7 macrophages and H9c2 cardiomyocytes using CCK-8 assay and live/dead cell staining in vitro. The cytotoxicity was evaluated where cells were either left untreated (control), or treated with different concentrations of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21. The results demonstrated that both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 exhibited excellent biosafety and even promoted proliferation of RAW264.7 macrophages at concentrations up to 400 μg/mL. Notably, the pro-proliferative effect peaked at 100 μg/mL for ZymA NPs, followed by a concentration-dependent decrease ( c). The H9c2 cardiomyocytes showed that in the concentration range of 200 μg/mL (either ZymA NPs or ZymA@SiR21) promoting cell proliferation. Same to the RAW264.7 macrophages, in the concentration of 100 μg/ml, the promotion effect was the highest, and then began to decline as the concentration increased. when the concentration exceeds 400 μg/mL, the cells proliferation rate begins to be inhibited ( ). Interestingly, while ZymA@SiR21 exhibited significantly weaker proliferation-promoting effects compared to ZymA NPs at equivalent concentrations in RAW264.7 macrophages, this trend was reversed in H9c2 cardiomyocytes. These findings collectively demonstrate the safety profile of ZymA-based nanocarriers.
Consistent with this observation, live/dead cell staining confirmed the non-toxic nature of both ZymA@SiR21 and ZymA NPs in RAW264.7 macrophages ( d–g) and H9c2 cardiomyocytes ( ). Taken together, these results establish that ZymA@SiR21 possesses excellent macrophage uptake efficiency while maintaining robust biosafety in vitro settings.

3.3

## Biological effects of ZymA@SiR21 on immune cells
Previous studies have demonstrated that the ZymA NPs at 100 μg/mL concentration exhibited minimal cytotoxicity while inducing maximal proliferation in RAW264.7 macrophages. Consequently, this optimal concentration was selected for subsequent experimental evaluations. It set up two experimental groups, cells were incubated with ZymA NPs or ZymA@SiR21 for 48 h, while an equal amount of PBS was added to the control group. As shown in a, compared with the Control group, the expression of iNOS (an M1 macrophage marker) was upregulated in both the ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 groups, and the iNOS level in the ZymA@SiR21 group was lower than that in the ZymA NPs group. The expression of CD206 (an M2 macrophage marker) was upregulated main in ZymA@SiR21 group,and the upregulation was not obvious in the ZymA NPs group. But the expression of CD206 was higher in the ZymA@SiR21 group compared to the ZymA NPs group. These findings were further validated by quantitative analysis of M1 and M2 related gene expression using qPCR ( b and c). These results initially indicated that ZymA NPs can significantly induce M1 polarization in macrophages, while SiR21 can prevent excessive inflammation caused by ZymA NPs by promoting the transformation of macrophages to the M2 phenotype. 
In order to gain insight into the mechanism by which ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 promotes polarization of macrophages, transcriptional changes were quantitatively estimated using RNA-seq. Biological replicates in Control group, ZymA NPs group and ZymA@SiR21 group were collected, and the homogeneity of the samples was examined by the Pearson correlation between the samples ( ). The CoexVenn diagram shows the number of uniquely expressed genes in each group, while the overlapping area shows the 10,549 genes that are co-expressed in d, indicating a significant gene expression overlap in these three samples. Clustering analysis can be performed on the differentially expressed gene sets. As shown in the heatmap in e, each groups were thoroughly examined using a principal component analysis and heatmap, and genes with similar expression patterns were grouped together. RNA-seq showed that 2503 genes were up and 2120 genes were down regulated in the ZymA@SiR21 groups compared to those in the Control groups, suggesting the possible involvement of these genes in the process of polarization of macrophages inducted by ZymA@SiR21, as shown in the volcano plot ( f). Further pathway enrichment analysis of differential gene expressions (DEGs) with KEGG analysis suggested that the ZymA@SiR21 groups were associated with inflammatory pathways signaling pathways. Such as MAPK signaling pathway, JAK-STAT signaling pathway and TNF signaling pathway ( g). All of these signaling pathways are closely related to macrophage polarization and inflammation. A comparative analysis between the experimental group and the control group revealed that there were a total of 1250 differentially expressed genes between the two groups ( ). The volcano plot of ZymA NPs groups compared to Control groups showed that 2359 genes were up and 1990 genes were down regulated ( ). The volcano plot of ZymA NPs groups compared to ZymA@SiR21 groups showed that 1167 genes were up and 1129 genes were down regulated ( ). Further pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs with KEGG analysis were showed in . Take togetagher, the comprehensive KEGG analysis revealed that the MAPK signaling pathway might play a significant role in the regulation of macrophage polarization by ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21. MAPK signaling pathway is a major mediator of macrophages and is thought to be one of the key signal transducers in macrophage polarization. Therefore, we focused on MAPK signaling in ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 treated macrophage cells and found that the levels of p-p38 MAPK and p-ERK were up-regulated in the ZymA NPs compared to the control groups, and the levels of p-p38 MAPK and p-ERK were down-regulated in the ZymA@SiR21 compared to the ZymA NPs groups ( h and i). All these results indicated that ZymA NPs might mediate the inflammatory response of macrophages by activating the MAPK pathway, and SiR21 can regulates the polarization of macrophages by modulating the activity of this pathway.

3.4

## ZymA@SiR21 induced acute immune response after MI in short term
Based on the in vitro results, we performed in vivo short-acting experiments in mice to further verify the role of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 in macrophage polarization. Before the in vivo experiment, the safety of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 was evaluated by hemolysis test. The results demonstrated that both have good biological safety in vivo and can be used for intravenous injection ( ). To evaluate the bio-distribution of nanoparticles, the ICG labeled ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 were prepared. ZymA NPs-ICG and ZymA@SiR21-ICG were administered to healthy mice and myocardial infarction model mice, respectively. The cardiac targeting characteristics as well as the in vivo biodistribution and metabolic dynamics of both formulations were systematically investigated using fluorescence imaging. The results revealed that in MI model mice, both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 began to accumulate significantly in the infarcted area starting from 1 h post-intravenous injection, with the enrichment continuing until it peaked at 8 h, indicating the highest level of nanoparticle retention in the infarcted myocardium at this time point ( a and b). In contrast, no obvious cardiac accumulation was observed for either nanoparticle in healthy mice. Furthermore, the liver was confirmed as the primary metabolic organ. The clearance of nanoparticles from the body was relatively slow, and they were not completely metabolized even after 24 h ( ). Further analysis demonstrated that ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 exhibited highly consistent cardiac targeting capability and in vivo distribution patterns, indicating that the loading of SiR21 did not alter the biological distribution behavior or targeting properties of the nanoparticles. Importantly, to determine any potential tissue damage, ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 were administered to mice via tail vein injection at a concentration of 1 mg/mL (a final dose of 200 μL), and the main organs of mice in each group were sliced and stained with H&E. The results showed that the liver, lung, kidney, and spleen of each group had no signs of tissue damage or disorder compared with normal physiological structure and cell morphology ( c). These results indicate excellent biological safety and targeting properties of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21. The results can be attributed to the negative surface charge of both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21, coupled with their optimized particle size distribution which ensures biosafety. 
To evaluate the short-term immunomodulatory effects of ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 in a mouse model of MI, cytokine levels in the blood of mice from each experimental group were measured 3 days after tail vein injection. During the acute phase, both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 were able to rapidly induce macrophage activity and accelerate the acute inflammatory response. After three days of treatment, compared with the Sham group, the levels of key pro-inflammatory cytokines in all of the MI, ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 groups were elevated, especially with statistically significant increases observed for IL-6 and IL-1β. although the levels of inflammatory factors in ZymA NPs group was higher than those in MI group and in ZymA@SiR21 group was lower than those in ZymA NPs group, there were no significant difference in pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α ( d–f). Meanwhile, there were no significant differences were detected in anti-inflammatory cytokines among the experimental groups ( g and h). This indicates that in the acute phase, ZymA plays an inductive role by inducing and activating inflammatory response to promote the clearance of apoptotic cells and.
DAMPs, thereby creating favorable conditions for subsequent repair without causing excessive inflammation in the body. The SiR21 plays an chronic role to prevents excessive inflammation. Taken together, all these results confirmed that systemic application of ZymA could effectively induced an acute immune response after MI in the short term.

3.5

## Long-term therapeutic efficacy of ZymA@SiR21 in mice myocardial injury
Based on the above results, the effect of ZymA@SiR21 was further validated for myocardial protection in mice with MI. To further verify the cardiac protection of ZymA@SiR21 were administered by tail intravenous injection. After establishment of the MI mouse model, therapeutic efficacy was assessed over 4 weeks. Other experimental groups included the sham group, and groups receiving injections of PBS, ZymA NPs. After treatment, the cardiac function of mice in each group was monitored by MI-mode echocardiography ( a). Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane, and 2D Mmode guided tracking was used to provide short-axis maps at the level of papillary muscles. The left ventricular ejection fraction (EF), left ventricular rational shortening (FS), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVVol; d), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVVol; s), and left ventricular mass (corrected) were measured. The echocardiographic examination showed that MI diminished cardiac function, which was significantly improved by ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 treatment. The effects of different treatment groups on left ventricular ejection fraction (EF, b), left ventricular rational shortening (FS, c), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVVol; d, d), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVVol; s, e) and left ventricular mass (LV Mass, f) were calculated. The EF and FS in MI mice were lower than those in the sham group, while EF and FS values in ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 treated mice were higher. The LVVol and LVVol in MI were bigger than those in the sham groups, while LVVol and LVVol values in ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 treated mice were smaller than MI group. 
Cardiac remodeling was assessed by H&E staining of mouse hearts, while infarct area and fibrotic remodeling were quantified using Masson's trichrome staining. Masson staining results demonstrated a significant protective effect on cardiac morphology in the treatment groups compared to the MI group. Specifically, the ZymA@SiR21 treatment group exhibited the most pronounced cardioprotective effect, showing the smallest scar area and the least degree of fibrosis ( g). These results collectively indicate that both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 effectively prevented myocardial cell loss, with ZymA@SiR21 demonstrating superior efficacy in significantly reducing fibrosis.
ZymA@SiR21 exerted satisfactory cardiac protection, as demonstrated by the results discussed above. Then we performed immunofluorescence staining of the four groups of heart tissues, and the results showed that the expression of iNOS was significantly reduced, whereas the expression of CD206 was significantly increased in ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 groups compared with MI group ( a and b). The results suggest that ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 long-acting protects against heart failure in MI mice through M2 macrophage polarization. In the chronic phase, as shown in c–g, both ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 down-regulated the levels of inflammatory cytokines compared with the MI model group, with statistically significant differences observed between the two groups for IL-6 and TNF-α. Concurrently, anti-inflammatory cytokines were up-regulated in the treatment groups, and IL-10 also showed a significant difference between the ZymA NPs and ZymA@SiR21 groups. These results suggest that SiR21 begins to play a dominant role at this stage, shifting the therapeutic effect toward inflammation suppression and repair promotion. Building upon the previously observed immunomodulatory effects, this study further investigated the therapeutic efficacy of ZymA@SiR21 on cardiomyocyte apoptosis and angiogenesis. TUNEL staining results demonstrated that ZymA@SiR21 treatment significantly reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis in the infarcted heart. Furthermore, immunohistochemical analysis for VEGF and α-SMA indicated that prolonged administration of ZymA@SiR21 effectively promoted angiogenesis in the border zone of the infarction ( ). These results reveal that the therapeutic benefits of ZymA@SiR21 are mediated not only through its immunomodulatory properties but also via the inhibition of apoptosis and the enhancement of vascular regeneration. To sum up, all these results substantiated that systemic administration of ZymA NPs can effectively reduce cardiac remodeling after myocardial injury and promote the recovery of cardiac function by stimulating the heart to quickly enter the inflammatory phase and then rapidly transition to the repair phase. Meanwhile, SiR21 can prevent excessive inflammation by regulating the polarization of cardiac macrophages. 

4

## Conclusions
In this study, we developed a chronologically regulated macrophage polarization nanocomplex, ZymA@SiR21, to improve cardiac repair following myocardial infarction. The nanocomplex consists of ZymA nanoparticles and SiR21, which work synergistically to coordinate the immune microenvironment at different pathological stages through targeted delivery, thereby promoting the recovery of myocardial structure and function. In vitro experiments demonstrated that ZymA nanoparticles were efficiently taken up by macrophages, inducing an acute inflammatory response, while SiR21 effectively suppressed excessive inflammation. Transcriptomic analysis indicated that both components regulated macrophage phenotype switching via the MAPK signaling pathway. In animal studies, tail vein injection of ZymA@SiR21 elicited a inflammatory response during the acute phase (within 3 days), facilitating the clearance of necrotic tissue. As SiR21 gradually taked effect, macrophages polarized toward the M2 phenotype, driving the anti-inflammatory and repair processes. Long-term observations revealed that ZymA@SiR21 synergistically reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis, suppressed fibrosis, and promoted angiogenesis, significantly reducing infarct size and improving cardiac function. Immunohistochemical analysis further confirmed that the treatment enhanced vascular generation in the infarct border zone and inhibited cell death, laying the foundation for cardiac remodeling. Importantly, ZymA@SiR21 exhibited dynamic differences in cytokine regulation between the early and late stages of treatment: it primarily promoted inflammation in the early phase while significantly enhancing repair-related signals in the later phase, demonstrating its precise temporal therapeutic advantage. This platform combines high biocompatibility, simple preparation, and low toxicity, offering a promising therapeutic strategy for myocardial infarction and other tissue injuries through chronologically regulated macrophage polarization.
