Dysregulated and excessive production of cytokines and chemokines, known as cytokine storm, is frequently seen in patients with severe COVID-19 disease and it can provoke a severe systematic inflammation in the patients. In this review, we highlight the biochemical properties of IRAKs and their role in regulating inflammatory molecular signaling pathways and discuss the potential targeting of IRAKs to suppress the SARS-CoV-2-induced cytokine storm in COVID-19 patients.

## Introduction
Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), an enveloped positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus (ssRNA) [ ]. COVID-19 was rapidly developed in a global pandemic and resulted in a major public health complications. The particularly high infectious capacity of the virus, along with the significant morbidity and mortality for patients has caused a great deal of concerns around the world. The clinical spectrum of SARS-CoV-2 infection ranges from mild symptoms to critical illness, and approximately 15–30% of patients hospitalized with COVID-19 disease experienced acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) [ ]. Immunocompromised persons, including patients with cancer and solid-organ transplant recipients were at higher risk of becoming severely sick and death [ ]. For instances, large retrospective cohort studies in North America, Europe, and China indicate high case fatality rates and hospitalization of immunocompromised cancer patients with COVID-19 [ – ].
Many vaccines against COVID-19 have been developed since the SARS-CoV-2 was discovered. Although the vaccines considerably reduced the number of people who get COVID-19 disease and serious illness [ ], it seems that they are not as effective at completely preventing the disease, and it is still controversial whether vaccine administration may impact the prevalence of long-COVID infections [ ]. Also, novel SARS-CoV-2 variants such as delta and omicron have been identified, mainly in areas of high virus prevalence, and these viruses could evade host immune responses [ ]. Moreover, “vaccine breakthrough infections” may occur in people who are fully vaccinated and still get COVID-19 infection if they are exposed to the SARS-CoV-2 virus [ , ]. Collectively, although the present vaccines have been shown to provide protection against the severe illness of COVID-19, challenges include the emergence of new genetically different SARS-CoV-2 variants, durability of the humoral immunity and safety of vaccines make us in need to look for new efficient and safe therapy to decrease the severe symptoms in patients with COVID-19, especially in people who are at the highest risk of becoming seriously ill. We think that studying the molecular pathways of SARS-CoV-2 infected cells as well as the characterization of signal transduction pathways induced by SARS-CoV-2 infection is a fundamental way to provide key insights into significant molecular targets to develop more efficient anti-SARS-CoV-2 therapeutics.
The SARS-CoV-2 entry process into host cells is mediated by at least two main mechanisms which involve two key proteins located on the surface of the cells, which are the angiotensin converting enzyme II (ACE2) and the transmembrane protease serine 2 (TMPRSS2) [ ]. The first mechanism is mediated by receptor mediated endocytosis which occurred by engagement of the SARS-CoV-2 spike (S) glycoprotein with the ACE2 receptor, whereas the second is induced by the TMPRSS2 protease that cleaves the (S) glycoprotein of SARS-CoV-2 to generate unlocked fusion-catalyzing form of the virus and facilitates its entry to host cells via direct fusion of the viral and cellular plasma membrane leading to the release of the viral ssRNA into the cytoplasm which will generate new viruses [ ]. Once inside the host cell, the nascent SARS-CoV-2 regulates multiple signaling pathways of the cell leading to an overall increase in proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines production and a decrease in the innate antiviral defense [ ]. In fact, the major cause of death in COVID-19 patients is probably due to dysregulation of the innate immune system and development of the cytokine storm that triggers severe systematic inflammation [ , ].
Interleukin-1 receptor/Toll-like receptors (IL-1R/TLRs) family plays a fundamental role in the immune response and cytokines production. Upon recognition of their cognate ligands, they trigger complex signaling pathways that result in increased expression of multiple proinflammatory genes [ ]. The interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinases (IRAKs) are key downstream mediators of signal transduction pathways initiated by the IL-1R/TLRs. In fact, recent studies have demonstrated the IRAKs-mediated signaling as a key component of the regulatory pathways which have direct and determining roles in inflammation [ ]. Also, direct inhibition of IRAKs, particularly IRAK1 and IRAK4, has been shown to be effective in suppression of various types of inflammatory diseases [ , ].
In this review, we shed some light on the biochemical properties of IRAKs, immunopathogenesis of SARS-CoV-2 infection, IRAKs-mediated molecular mechanisms that regulate SARS-CoV-2-induced cytokine storm and discuss the potential targeting of IRAKs by chemical molecules to suppress the SARS-CoV-2-induced severe inflammation in COVID-19 patients.

## SARS-CoV-2 infection induced inflammatory cytokines hyperproduction
The pathophysiology of SARS-CoV-2 infection is not completely understood, however, it has all been linked to a disrupted response of the immune system particularly hyperproduction of cytokine and chemokine leading to a cytokine storm [ , ]. Cytokine storm is a life-threatening systemic inflammatory condition that involves elevated levels of circulating cytokines and immune-cells hyperactivation, which may cause multi-organ dysfunction that can lead to death if inadequately treated [ ]. There are a wide range of clinical and laboratory abnormalities that can be observed in patients suffering from a cytokine storm, such as acute systemic inflammatory symptoms, secondary organ dysfunction (often pulmonary, renal or hepatic) and a prominent elevation in serum inflammatory cytokine levels, including interferon-γ (INF-γ), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), soluble interleukin-2 receptor alpha (sIL-2rα), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), CXCL9 and CXCL10 chemokines [ , ].
Cytokines are small secreted proteins with low molecular weight (~ 6–70 kDa), released by many cell types (macrophages, lymphocytes, natural killer cells, and mast cells), and these molecules may act on the cells that secreted them (autocrine action), on neighboring cells (paracrine action), or on distant cells (endocrine action) [ ]. Cytokines are key regulators of a large number of biological processes including intercellular communication, innate and adaptive immune responses, growth and responsiveness of immune cells [ ]. Each cytokine binds to a specific receptor on the surface of target cells to initiate a cell signaling cascade that control cell functions, and this may include the negative or positive regulation of multiple of genes and their transcription factors [ ]. It is important to mention that the uncontrolled or increased production of cytokines such as during a cytokine storm could lead to a life-threatening systemic inflammatory syndrome if inadequately treated. For example, it is hypothesized that the “cytokine storm syndrome” is responsible for the poor prognosis of critical COVID19 patients [ , , ].
Upon inhalation, SARS-CoV-2 could infect multiple cell types including alveolar epithelial cells and macrophages by engagement of its spike (S) protein with the two key proteins located on the surface of host cells which are: the ACE2 and the TMPRSS2 [ ]. SARS-CoV-2 entry into target host cells initiates intracellular signals that modulate their activation, including the production of proinflammatory mediators such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) and a variety of inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β, IL-6 and TNFα [ , ], which lead to recruitment and infiltration of other immune cells such as monocytes, macrophages, T-lymphocytes and NK cells from blood circulation into inter-alveolar septa and alveolar space. The accumulation of immune cells contribute in increasing the production of proinflammatory cytokines, including INF-γ, IL-6, IL-1β, sIL-2rα, TNF-α, CXCL9 and CXCL10 [ ] (Fig.  ). This excessive release of inflammatory mediators (cytokine storm) may cause permanent damage to capillary endothelial cells and alveoli epithelial cells, fibrosis, hyaline membrane formation, intracapillary thrombosis, multiple organ injuries and eventually death [ ]. 

## IRAKs are key players in the IL-1R/TLRs inflammatory signaling pathway
IL-1Rs family comprises ten signaling receptors that mediate inflammatory responses, ultimately helping to orchestrate both innate and adaptive immunity [ ]. Once active IL-1 cytokines are secreted, they can bind to their cognate cell surface receptors and initiate signaling to induce potent inflammatory responses. Within the IL-1R family, the mechanism of signal initiation is highly conserved, and all members share a common structure of an intracellular Toll/Interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain [ ]. TLRs are a family of receptors localized on the cell surface or in the endosome of several immune-relevant cell types, including macrophages, T and B cells, dendritic cells and are involved in pathogen recognition and host defense [ ]. The TLR family can recognize a wide variety of bacterial, viral, protozoan and fungal components, generally referred to as pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) [ ]. Notably, the TLRs signaling pathway is analogous to IL-1R signaling as they both depend on homotypic interactions between TIR domain-containing proteins for signal transduction that resulted in activation of downstream pathways.
Upon recognition of their cognate ligands, IL-1R/TLRs proteins, homo- or hetero dimerize, could engage with one or more proteins through recruitment of different combinations of Toll/Interleukin-1 receptor (TIR)-domain-containing adaptor proteins to their TIR domain to initiate a downstream signaling cascade [ ]. These protein adaptors such as MyD88, Mal/TIRAP, TRIF, and TRAM connect the IL-1R/TLRs receptors with the intracellular molecules that convert their signals into a biological response and play a critical function in innate immunity [ ]. Among them, the MyD88 is the most widely utilized adaptor protein involved in IL-1R/TLRs signaling cascade [ ].
IRAKs are key regulatory kinases of molecular pathways initiated by the IL-1R/TLRs signaling network in cells. These kinases play a central role in regulating innate immunity and inflammatory responses by promoting the expression of various inflammatory molecules in immune cells [ ]. IRAK family consists of four members IRAK1, IRAK2, IRAK3 (IRAKM) and IRAK4. The IRAK4 has been shown to play a key role in mediating IL1R/TLRs intracellular signaling and is rapidly recruited to the receptor-signaling complex upon IL1R/TLR activation [ ]. Once activated, IRAK4 interacts with IRAK2 and MyD88 to form a large signaling complex, called the Myddosome [ ] (Fig.  ). The assembly of this complex brings the kinase domains of IRAKs into proximity for phosphorylation and activation which leads to the IRAK4-mediated recruitment and phosphorylation of IRAK1 [ ]. Subsequently, the phosphorylated IRAK1 could be released from the receptor complex and binds to the TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) E3 ubiquitin ligase that transduces signals to activate the inhibitor of nuclear factor κB (IκB) kinases (IKKs) and the mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) pathways which cause the induction of key regulatory transcription factors, including NF-κB and activator protein 1 (AP-1), and ultimately result in the expression of a broad array of proinflammatory cytokines (Fig.  ) [ , ]. The AP-1 is an important target of the MAPKs pathway [ ]. AP-1 is a heterodimer transcription factor that regulates the expression of key inflammatory-associated genes in response to a variety of stimuli, including cytokines, and it consists of members belonging to the basic leucine-zipper (bZIP) proteins, mainly of the Jun and the Fos families [ , ]. In mammalian cells, NF-κB family comprises five members of DNA-binding subunits which are: p65 (RelA), RelB, c-Rel, p105/p50 (NF-κB1) and p100/p52 (NF-κB2) [ ]. These transcription factors mediate signaling from the cell surface to regulate key genes involved in inflammation, cell division and immunity [ ]. Unlike other IRAK family members, IRAK3 negatively regulates the IL1R/TLRs signaling pathway. In fact, IRAK3 has been proposed to interfere with TRAF6 activation by IRAK1 resulting in inhibition of downstream proteins including NF-κB and AP-1 [ , , ]. 
In summary, IRAKs play a key role in innate immunity and inflammatory response in cells through the regulation of expression of the two main transcription factors NF-κB and AP-1 which control critical immune processes including immune cells maturation and cytokines production.

## Structure and biological function of IRAKs
All IRAKs proteins share similar domain organization, consisting of a conserved N-terminal domain (death domain) essential for dimerization and interaction with the MyD88, a proline/serine/threonine-rich (ProST) domain, and a kinase and/or pseudokinase domain [ ] (Fig.  A). However, only IRAK1 and IRAK4 were found to exhibit kinase activity [ , ]. In addition, only IRAK1, IRAK2, and IRAK3 contain a C-terminus domain, which is required for binding and activation of the downstream signaling molecule TRAF6 [ ]. 

## IRAK1
IRAK1, the first member of the IRAKs family to be discovered, is an important player in the signaling complex of the IL1R/TLRs pathway, and it was first identified experimentally through co-immunoprecipitation and in vitro kinase assays [ ]. The IRAK1 gene is composed of 14 exons and is located on the X chromosome [ ]. The human IRAK1 protein consists of an N-terminal death domain (residues 1–106), a ProST domain (residues 107–212), a central kinase domain (residues 213–521) and a C-terminus domain (residues 522–712) (Fig.  A) [ ]. Sequence alignment of the kinase active site revealed the presence of the critical motifs required for catalytic activity, including a glycine-rich G-loop sequence (GEGGFG) which resembles the conserved amino acid consensus motif (GxGxxG) [ ], the catalytic loop His-Gly-Asp (HGD) motif [ ] and the Asp-Phe-Gly (DFG) motif [ ].
Eukaryotic protein kinases have a common structural fold, consisting of an N-lobe and a C-lobe with the ATP-binding pocket and key catalytic residues located between the two lobes [ ]. The N-lobe is primarily composed of β-sheets and is involved in anchoring and orienting the ATP. The C-lobe is mainly α-helical and usually is responsible for substrate binding and mediating phosphotransfer mechanisms [ ]. The crystal structure of human IRAK1 kinase domain has recently been resolved [ ]. Obviously, the 3D structure exhibits the canonical N and C lobes architecture of the protein kinase (Fig.  B). Additionally, typical parts of protein kinases can be observed including the β-strand–turn–β-strand structure comprises a glycine-rich loop which points toward the catalytic cleft and functions to bind and position ATP for phosphotransfer, a DFG-containing activation loop that forms part of the ATP-binding site and coordinates magnesium binding [ ] and HGD-containing catalytic loop (Fig.  B).
In fact, IL1R/TLRs signaling engages IRAK1 and IRAK1 phosphorylation, to drive downstream events including NF-kB signaling in inflammatory responses [ ]. Upon IL1R/TLR ligand binding, MyD88 is rapidly recruited to the receptor through its toll-interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) homology domain. IRAK2 and IRAK4 interact with MyD88 via their death-domain leading to the formation of MyD88/IRAK2/IRAK4 (Myddosome complex), which facilitates the recruitment, phosphorylation and subsequent activation of IRAK1 [ ]. It has been shown that the phosphorylation and activation of IRAK1 is a multistep process that primarily requires a key threonine residue at position 209 (Thr209). Indeed, a mutation of this residue resulted in a kinase-inactive IRAK1 [ ]. The Thr209 phosphorylation step causes a conformational change of the kinase domain which leads to further phosphorylation steps to occur at residues within its activation loop (such as Thr387) and ProST region [ ]. Upon phosphorylation, IRAK1 will be dissociated from the upstream Myddosome complex and released in the cytoplasm to bind downstream effectors such as the TRAF6 E3 ubiquitin ligase. The activated TRAF6 complex in turn drives other downstream events, including the NF-κB/MAPK activation [ ].
Multiple lines of evidence have suggested that IRAK1 is a critical mediator of innate immunity, and inhibition of IRAK1 kinase could suppress inflammation. For example, upregulated IRAK1 expression levels has been reported in inflammatory-related diseases such as in adipose tissues from obese diabetic individuals and in CD4+ T cells isolated from systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) patients [ , ]. Furthermore, compared to wild type mice, IRAK1-deficeint mice showed a decrease in cytokines production, delayed inflammatory response, low incidence of organ damage following microbial sepsis infection [ , ]. Also, Nanda et al. [ ], have reported that splenomegaly, autoimmunity, and liver and kidney inflammation were reduced in a knock-in ABIN1 [D485N] mouse expressing catalytically inactive mutants of IRAK1. Moreover, inhibition of IRAK1 phosphorylation by pacritinib was demonstrated to reduce liver fibrosis in STAM mice [ ]. Overall, this may indicate that the lack of IRAK1-mediated signaling could downregulate the early phase of cytokine cascade production during inflammation.

## IRAK2
IRAK2 is another component of the IL1R/TLRs signaling cascade. The human IRAK2 gene consists of 13 exons and is located on the chromosome 3. The IRAK2 protein comprises a death domain (residues 1–94), a ProST domain (residues 95–192), a central pseudokinase domain (residues 193–507) and a C-terminus domain (residues 508–625) (Fig.  A) [ ]. Although the crystal structure of IRAK2 has not been solved yet, sequence alignment of pseudokinase domain showed missing critical motifs required for catalytic activity, including the conserved residues: aspartic acid of the HGD- motif and glycine of the DFG- motif [ ]. However, it is controversial whether IRAK2 has intrinsic kinase activity [ , ].
IRAK2 has been shown to be a critical component of the multimeric MyD88/IRAK2/IRAK4 signaling complex that is formed through death-domain interactions downstream of IL1R/TLRs activation [ ]. Additionally, IRAK2 may play a role in mediating post-transcriptional regulation of inflammatory transcripts. Yin et al. [ ], has demonstrated that kinase-inactive IRAK2 macrophages could reduce cytokines mRNA stability and translation in response to lipopolysaccharide agonist compared to kinase-active IRAK2 macrophages. Although the precise mechanism of IRAK2-regulation of the posttranscriptional process of inflammatory cytokines is still unclear, a recent study has indicated that IRAK2 could phosphorylate and inhibit the serine/arginine splicing factor 1 (SRSF1) mRNA binding protein, leading to the assembly of nuclear export machinery to export the inflammatory mRNAs to the cytoplasm for translation [ ].
Interestingly, modulation of IRAK2 experimentally has been reported to interfere with inflammatory pathway. For example, inhibition of IRAK2 via IRAK2 small interfering RNA (siRNA), in two mouse models of atopic dermatitis and psoriasis inflammatory diseases, has been shown to relieve the epidermal thickness and inflammation in both mouse models [ ]. In addition, the IRAK2-genetic variant (coding D431E) has been reported to increase NF-κB activity in D431E-IRAK2 expressing cells and resulted in upregulation of proinflammatory cytokines production, upon influenza virus infection, compared with IRAK2- wild type [ ].

## IRAK3 (IRAK-M)
IRAK3 protein (also known as IRAK-M) is crucially involved in signaling initiated by IL1R/TLRs activation. Unlike other IRAK family members that are widely expressed in a variety of cell types, IRAK3 is mainly found in monocytes, macrophages and dendritic cells [ ]. The human IRAK3 gene consists of 12 exons and is located on the chromosome 12. The protein contains a death domain (residues 1–106) that interacts with other IRAK family members, a small ProST domain (residues 107–145), a pseudokinase domain (residues 146–454) and a C-terminus domain for interaction with TRAF6 (Fig.  A). Like IRAK2, IRAK3 is thought to be catalytically inactive pseudokinase, as it lacks the essential catalytic motifs. In fact, the canonical HRD-motif in IRAK3 is entirely replaced with ‘CGS’, DFG-motif is altered to ‘DFA’ and GxGxxG-motif is changed into ‘GxGxxF’ [ ]. The crystal structure of the human IRAK3 pseudokinase has recently been identified [ ]. The N-lobe is mainly consisting of anti-parallel β-sheets formed by (β1-5) strands, atypical glycine-loop, a DFA-containing activation loop and a HGD-containing catalytic loop (Fig.  B). The C-lobe is made primarily of five α-helices and contains a loop with the degenerate CGS-motif. It is also important to indicate that IRAK3 contains a cryptic guanylate cyclase (GC) center, embedded within the pseudokinase domain, which produces cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) that has been connected with its suppressive inflammatory function [ ].
In contrast to other IRAK family members, IRAK3 is thought to act as a negative regulator of IL1R/TLRs downstream cascade through interfering with the TRAF6 activation by IRAK1 resulting in a deactivation of NF-κB and AP-1 proteins and overall decrease in proinflammatory cytokines production [ , , ]. Indeed, IRAK3−/− mice were shown to be more susceptible to bacterial infection and the isolated mouse IRAK3−/− macrophages exhibited elevated levels of proinflammatory cytokines production upon TLR ligand stimulation [ ]. Also, IRAK3 in wild-type mice was described to prevent the damaging neutrophil-dependent lung injury during influenza infection of the respiratory tract compared with IRAK3−/− mice [ ]. Paradoxically, some studies have demonstrated IRAK3 to promote the expression of inflammatory mediators [ , ]. For example, in dendritic cell lines, the immune axis IL-33/PIN1/IRAK3 was shown to induce the production of cytokines (IL-6, IL-5 and IL-13) after extracellular stimulation of cells with IL-33 [ ]. Indicating that IRAK3 activation may exacerbate inflammation. However, a very recent meta-analyses of a total of 49 in vitro and in vivo mice studies, following two-challenge intervention, have confirmed the inhibitory effect of IRAK3 on cytokines production, particularly TNF-α and IL-6 cytokines [ ]. Overall, there is several of evidence supports the negative regulatory function of IRAK3 in inflammation. However, it is still possible that the anti-inflammatory activity of IRAK3 is dependent on factors such as cell type and mode of stimulation.

## IRAK4
IRAK4 is the most recent IRAK family member to be discovered. The human IRAK4 gene consists of 15 exons and is located on the chromosome 12. The 460 amino acid enzyme consists of a death domain (residues 1–104) that interacts with Myd88 to mediate the assembly of the Myddosome, a small ProST domain (residues 105–186) and a kinase domain (residues 187–460) (Fig.  A). Similar to IRAK1, sequence alignment of IRAK4- kinase domain revealed the presence of the key motifs: glycine-rich G-loop sequence (GEGGFG), catalytic loop (HRD) and the (DFG) [ ]. The 3D crystal structure of human IRAK4 kinase domain adopts the canonical architecture of protein kinases [ ], with a small N-terminal lobe consists mainly of five stranded β-sheets and one C-helix, and a large C-terminal lobe which is predominantly made of α-helix (Fig.  B). Furthermore, IRAK-4 has a unique tyrosine gatekeeper residue (Tyr262) at the ATP-binding site which it thought to interact with the conserved glutamine residue from helix αC which helps in stabilization of the αC in the active conformation [ ].
Inhibition of IRAK4 has been shown to block cytokines production and reduce inflammation in several studies [ – ]. For example, knocking down IRAK4 in microglial mouse cells significantly decreased IRAK4 expression, inhibited the downstream production of proinflammatory cytokines and resulted in a significant reduction in intracerebral hemorrhage-induced brain inflammation in mice [ ]. In addition, introducing the IRAK4 kinase dead in knock-in mice was shown to cause resistance to the induction and progression of Th17-cells driven experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis [ ]. It is worth mentioning that Th17-cells are considered as key pathogenic T cells involved in multiple inflammatory and autoimmune diseases [ ]. Also, lupus-prone mice harboring the kinase-inactive IRAK4 showed reduced glomerulonephritis, splenomegaly, serum anti-nuclear antibodies and lower TNF-α expression in macrophages compared with lupus-prone mice with functional IRAK4 [ ].

## IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 could be the major pathway that regulates SARS-CoV-2-induced cytokine storm
Growing studies have indicated the crucial role of IRAKs-mediated activation of transcription factors NF-κB and AP-1 in regulating the innate immune system as they participate in signaling networks of the innate axis of the immune response, particularly the Toll-like receptors (TLRs) pathways. These signaling pathways are important for the regulation of inflammation, antiviral response and the subsequent activation of the adaptive immune responses [ ]. TLRs play a vital role in activating immune responses against various infectious agents and are generally divided into those that are localized at the cell surface and are activated by lipid or protein agonists, and those that signal intracellularly in response to non-self-nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) from endosomal compartments [ ]. The engagement between the activating ligand and the TLRs stimulate homo- or hetero-dimerization of the receptor leading to interactions with adaptor proteins such as the Myddosome protein which then promotes the assembly of the higher-order complex Myd88 and subsequent activation of IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway [ ]. Interestingly, very recent reports have indicated that SARS-CoV-2 could modulate IRAKs signaling pathway. For example, direct inhibition of IRAK4 has been shown to prevent SARS-CoV-2-mediated acute respiratory distress syndrome in mouse models [ ]. Also, IRAK4 dysregulation by small interfering RNA has been reported to obstruct the SARS-CoV-2-induced cytotoxic effect in vitro [ ]. Moreover, inhibition of TLR8-induced phosphorylation mechanism of IRAK1 by pacritinib drug has been demonstrated to inhibit SARS-CoV-2-mediated proinflammatory cytokines production in human cells [ ].
A key question arising from this is how SARS-CoV-2 is able to modulate the inflammatory response through the IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway? Here, we conclude three possible scenarios. First, the engagement between SARS-CoV-2 and ACE2 receptors on the cell surface could initiate intracellular molecular signals that suppress IRAK3-mediated negative regulatory function leading to hyperactivation of IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway and subsequent cytokines production (Fig.  A). Remarkably, a very recent study have shown that binding of SARS-CoV-2 spike protein to the ACE2 receptors on macrophages caused a decrease in IRAK3 levels and an increase in gene expression of proinflammatory cytokines [ ]. 
Second, after SARS-CoV-2 entry into host cells via the ACE2-mediated endocytosis, the released viral ssRNA, within the endosomal compartment, could interact with TLR7 and/or TLR8 leading to recruitment and binding of the Myd88 proteins and subsequent activation of the IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway (Fig.  B). It is well established in the literature that TLR7 and TLR8 could sense the viral single-stranded RNA [ – ]. Indeed, TLR7 has been indicated to be activated during SARS-CoV-2 infection in in vitro lung models [ ]. Also, it has been very recently shown that neutrophils pretreated with the TLR8-specific inhibitor (CU-CPT9a) and incubated with SARS-CoV-2-ssRNA resulted in a reduction in proinflammatory cytokines production [ ]. Indicating the key role of TLR8- dependent pathway in mediating SARS-CoV-2 infection. Thus the potential use of TLR7 and/or TLR8 antagonists for anti-SARS-CoV-2 vaccines and immunological interventions to clear the viral infections provides a promising strategy in the search for new effective anti-SARS-CoV-2 treatments. It is worth mentioning that multiple potential small chemical antagonists for TLR7 or TLR8 have been published in the recent few years that look efficient based on preliminary pre-clinical studies, however, it still needs to be proven clinically [ – ].
SARS-CoV-2-encoded proteins interfering with TLR4/IRAKs-cellular signaling pathways is the third possible scenario. The SARS-CoV-2 genome encodes 29 proteins, including 4 structural, 16 nonstructural and 9 accessory proteins [ ]. In fact, few studies have shown potential interactions between TLR4 and SARS-CoV-2- spike protein (S1) [ , ]. Interestingly, Olajide et al. [ ] have demonstrated that stimulation of human peripheral blood mononuclear cells with a recombinant SARS-CoV-2- spike protein (S1) in vitro resulted in activation of NF-κB and MAPK pathways and induced cytokines release. Thus, in the context of SARS-CoV-2 viral infection, it is possible that high levels of circulating viral proteins could continually engage TLR4 on the cell surface, activating MyD88-IRAK dependent signaling (Fig.  C). Also, we think that some of these SARS-CoV-2-encoded proteins once released or shed inside host cells could lead to a series of signaling events resulting in the activation of IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway and cytokine production (Fig.  C). SARS-CoV-2-encoded proteins may interfere with IRAKs-molecular pathways via induction of MyD88-IRAKs complex formation or by suppression of IRAK3 which ultimately results in activation of IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1 pathway and an overall increase in cytokine production. However, these proposed models remain to be fully addressed. It is worth noting that Gordon et al. [ ], have recently reported a protein interaction map for SARS-CoV-2 proteins in host cells. Although no direct interactions between the viral proteins and IRAKs proteins were detected, that does not exclude the presence of indirect interactions. Moreover, in the aforementioned study the HEK-293T/17 kidney cells were used in the analysis of SARS-CoV-2-host protein interactions, and this is a limitation in the study. Because different cell types could have different interaction networks. Thus, we still think that intracellular interactions between SARS-CoV-2-encoded proteins and IRAK enzymes in alveolar macrophages is possible and could lead to activation of the IRAKs/NF-κB/AP1pathway (Fig.  C), but that will need further investigations.

## Targeting IRAKs represents an attractive strategy to counteract SARS-CoV-2- associated severe inflammation
IRAKs-mediated signaling pathways play an important role in inflammation. Indeed, it has been shown that direct inhibition of IRAKs, particularly (IRAK1 and IRAK4), could lead to suppression of various types of inflammation both in vitro and in vivo systems. For example, dual pharmacological inhibition of IRAK1 and IRAK4 has been shown to suppress endothelial inflammation and reduced atherosclerotic lesions formation in ApoE−/− mice models [ ]. Also, selective inhibition of IRAK4 by small chemicals was demonstrated to relieve arthritis and block gout formation in mice [ ]. Therefore, IRAK1 and IRAK4 have been the focus of multiple drug development platforms and recently advanced to clinical studies for various types of disorders particularly inflammatory diseases [ , ]. Since COVID-19 is an inflammatory disease associated with induction of cytokine release, inhibition of IRAK proteins can reduce the high release of cytokines and hence reduce the respiratory distress syndrome [ ]. Some of IRAK inhibitors are still in preclinical stages, others are already in clinical trials for the treatment of different diseases, including cancers and rheumatoid arthritis [ ].
Most synthetic inhibitors of IRAKs’ activity have suppression activity against IRAK1 and/or IRAK4 proteins with close potencies [ ]. There are selective IRAK1 inhibitors, such as Jh-X-119-01 that has been shown to be effective against B-cell lymphoma [ ]. Selective IRAK4 inhibitors such as Pf-06650833 and emavusertib (CA-4948) have been developed for rheumatoid arthritis and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, respectively [ , ]. Still, there is no inhibitor or degrader of IRAK2 or IRAK3 isoforms used for the therapeutic application [ ]. The followings summarize the available IRAKs inhibitors with their major clinical uses (Table ). 
Pacritinib is an anti-bone marrow cancer drug used clinically in the treatment of myelofibrosis. It has a multikinase inhibition activity against Janus kinase 2 (JAK2), fms-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3), and IRAK1 [ ]. It was reported that overexpression of IRAK1 is one of the resistance mechanisms of hematologic malignancies against anticancer drugs [ ]. Accordingly, it was suggested that pacritinib can be used in hematologic malignancies to reduce cancer resistance due to pacritinib’s activity against IRAK1 [ ]. Smith et al. [ ], showed that emavusertib could reduce bone marrow blasts among acute myeloid leukemia (AML) patients in a clinical trial. Some of those AML patients who achieved a complete response had mutations in the U2 Small Nuclear RNA Auxiliary Factor 1 gene which results in the induction of IRAK4 isoform. Accordingly, it is suggested that emavusertib is an inhibitor of IRAK4 or it can reduce its activity by other mechanisms [ ]. KIC-0101 is a dual IRAK4/PIM1 inhibitor that has been reported to suppress proinflammatory cytokine induction in vitro and ameliorated cartilage damage and inflammation in vivo rheumatoid arthritis mouse models [ ].
The selectivity of IRAK inhibitors is one of the major concerns when prescribing IRAKs inhibitors clinically. For example, IRAK family proteins and transforming growth factor-β activated (TAK1) have high sequence identity within the ATP-binding pocket, limiting the development of highly selective IRAK1/4 or TAK1 inhibitors. Scarneo et al. [ ] could develop a selective IRAK1 and IRAK4 inhibitor “takinib” which has a high inhibition activity against IRAK 1 and IRAK4 with minimal activity against TAK1 protein. However, takinib is still in the preclinical trial stages and its safety profile is still clinically unknown [ ].
Recently, molecules of two functional ligands commonly referred to as proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTAC) were developed to hijack endogenous ubiquitin E3 ligase (by one ligand) to ubiquitinate, thus degrading, targeted (by the other ligand) protein (review [ ]). First attempts to develop PROTAC were utilizing IRAK4 selective inhibitor Pf-06650833 as the IRAK4 ligands while evaluating the effectiveness of three available E3 ligands to degrade IRAK4 in vitro using PBMC cells. However, despite the confirmation of IRAK4 degradation, compound 9 (the potential PROTAC for IRAK4) treatment displays an insignificant reduced level of cytokine in phenotypic assay compared to Pf-06650833 only treatment [ ]. In 2020, however, another PROTAC compound using IMiD (Immunomodulatory drug; an E3 ligand) targeting the non-kinase part of IRAK4 was reported. The preliminary report indicates that the compound is able to promote IRAK4 degradation selectively in diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL) cells and perform better (lower IC50) compared to IRAK4 kinase inhibitors only treatment [ ]. PROTAC against IRAK1 were patented in 2021 indicating > 90% effective degradation in vitro. The patent document, however, does not specify which E3 ligase used to perform the in vitro assay [ ]. Important to note, that one type of E3 ligands, IMiD, have been shown to promote degradation of TLR associated transcription factors, Ikaros and Aiolos [ ]. Therefore, PROTAC against IRAKs using IMiD on the E3 ligands side, would probably act as synergistically [ ].
It is worth mentioning that some natural metabolites can inhibit IRAKs. One of the natural metabolites with anti-inflammatory activity is caffeic acid. It was found that caffeic acid suppressed the nuclear translocation of activator protein 1 family proteins and the related signaling cascade including IRAK1 and IRAK4 proteins [ ]. Also, thymoquinone, a bioactive component of black seed (Nigella sativa), was able to inhibit IRAK1 activity using in vitro methods [ ]. In addition, 1,4-naphthoquinone compounds were reported to inhibit IRAK1 activity and suppress proinflammatory cytokines release in vitro models [ ].
However, most of the available IRAK inhibitors lack selectivity toward IRAKs which may result in different unwanted effects [ ]. Accordingly, lacking selectivity and multiple unwanted effects can affect the decision of prescribing the available IRAK inhibitors in the treatment of COVID-19 disease. In addition, it is still unclear whether monotherapy is efficient or whether there is a need for a combination of different IRAK inhibitors in the treatment of COVID-19. The future trend is to design different effective and selective IRAK1 and/or IRAK4 inhibitors with minimal unwanted side effects.

## Conclusions
Most of COVID-19 patients develop mild to moderate symptoms of common viral infections such as cough, fever and fatigue. However, in severe cases, COVID-19-associated cytokine storm could lead to acute respiratory distress syndrome, hepatic injury, kidney injury and acute cardiac injury which may lead to reversible or irreversible end-organ dysfunction and even death [ ].
The current treatment for patients with COVID-19-associated cytokine storm is mainly based on the administration of traditional ant-inflammatory (corticosteroids, colchicines and chloroquine) and anti-viral (remdesivir) drugs [ , ]. Despite the numerous published data on the use of these drugs, particularly the steroids, in COVID-19 disease [ , , ], there is no clear evidence of the effectiveness of these treatments in comparison to the risks and adverse events including cardiovascular diseases, secondary infections and delayed SARS-CoV-2 viral clearance. It is worth mentioning that recent reports have shown the development of many cytokine-targeted biological compounds including recombinant cytokines and monoclonal antibodies, which specifically target a single inflammatory cytokine or its receptor, and has been described to exert a considerable efficacy in COVID-19 patients [ , – ]. However, knowing which cytokine or cytokine receptor to target in severe COVID-19 remains a puzzle. In addition, there are many challenges of using these biologics including the high cost, stability, safety and manufacturing.
IRAKs are crucial mediators of signal transduction pathways initiated by IL-1R/TLRs in cells that have been shown to regulate key components of the regulatory pathways which have determining role in inflammation and cytokines production. Also, direct inhibition of IRAKs, particularly IRAK1, IRAK2 and IRAK4, has been shown to be effective in suppression of cytokines production in various types of inflammation [ , , ]. Whereas the IRAK3 has been suggested to exert an inhibitory role within the IL-1R/TLRs pathway [ ]. Multiple promising chemical molecules that directly target IRAKs have been proposed in recent years (Table ). However, there are some challenges that should to be considered carefully before clinical use of IRAK-targeted drugs such as selectivity, safety and bioavailability. Also, one of the main questions regarding these IRAK-targeted drugs is whether they will show efficacy as monotherapies, and, if not, what an effective combination approach would be used.
In summary, cytokine storm is a major cause of mortality in COVID-19. Therapeutic interventions to manage cytokine storm could provide a novel avenue to decrease COVID-19 associated morbidity and mortality. Here we present IRAKs as potential targets for therapeutic strategy against COVID-19-associated cytokine storm. However, further in vitro and in vivo studies will be important to better understand the pharmacological relevance of targeting IRAKs in COVID-19 to ameliorate cytokine storm.
