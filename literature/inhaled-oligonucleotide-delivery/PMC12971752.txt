## Background
A disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 1 (ADAMTS1) is involved in the occurrence and development of myocardial fibrosis. Here, we sought to explore the specific regulatory mechanism of ADAMTS1 in cardiac fibrosis post-myocardial infarction (CFPMI).

## Methods
Blood samples from patients with myocardial fibrosis were collected. A CFPMI mouse model and in vitro models involving human or mouse cardiac fibroblasts treated with TGF-β1 or Ang II were constructed. ChIP was used to confirm that SMAD2 binds to ADAMTS1, and Co-IP was used to verify the interaction between ADAMTS1 and HDAC6. Cellular models with SMAD2 knockdown, ADAMTS1 regulation, and HDAC6 inhibitor treatment were used to study their roles in fibrosis. Finally, AAV-shRNA-HDAC6 and ADAMTS1 inhibitor effects were verified in vivo.

## Results
ADAMTS1 levels were higher in myocardial fibrosis patients’ serum. Increased ADAMTS1 and p-SMAD2 were found in fibrotic mouse hearts and human cardiac fibroblasts stimulated with fibrotic factors. ChIP validated the binding of SMAD2 to ADAMTS1. Mechanistically, SMAD2 regulated ADAMTS1 expression during TGF-β1-induced fibrosis in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts. Overexpression of ADAMTS1 enhanced the production of collagen fiber proteins in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts induced by TGF-β1. Moreover, HDAC6 expression was elevated in CFPMI mouse hearts and ADAMTS1 inhibited HDAC6 to regulate fibrosis. ADAMTS1 interacted with HDAC6 during fibrosis. In vivo, shRNA-HDAC6 and ADAMTS1 inhibitor treatment alleviated myocardial fibrosis and improved cardiac function after CFPMI.

## Conclusions
Targeting ADAMTS1/HDAC6 alleviated TGF-β1/SMAD2-associated cardiac fibrosis in CFPMI. This study may provide a novel theoretical basis for the treatment of myocardial fibrosis.

## Introduction
Myocardial infarction (MI) is among the most dangerous cardiovascular events. Pathological changes caused by MI include changes in the extracellular matrix of the left ventricle, which could result in cardiac stiffness and compromise systolic and diastolic performance. Signals emanating from necrotic tissue trigger an immune cascade, thereby inducing a widespread inflammatory reaction, which is subsequently followed by reparative fibrosis in the infarcted region (Zaidi et al. ). Cardiac fibrosis serves as a prevalent pathological characteristic of postinjury remodeling, correlates with adverse clinical outcomes, and there is no effective treatment (Qian et al. ). Excessive cardiac fibrosis constitutes the pathological underpinnings of heart failure subsequent to MI (Chen et al. ). Additionally, myocardial fibroblasts differentiation into myofibroblasts and collagen deposition in extracellular matrix are the hallmarks of myocardial fibrosis, and this process is regulated in a time-dependent manner by multiple signaling pathways and multiple cell types (Yin et al. ). However, there are currently no treatments to limit cardiac fibrosis development and subsequent heart failure (Bouvet et al. ). Therefore, further investigations of the mechanism of cardiac fibrosis post-MI (CFPMI) are highly important.
A disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs (ADAMTS) proteins include 19 proteases which modulate the structure and functional properties of extracellular proteins in extracellular matrix and blood (Santamaria and Groot ). ADAMTS1 plays an essential role in organogenesis and angiogenesis (Lambert et al. ). Studies have shown that ADAMTS1 regulates extracellular matrix turnover by degrading extracellular matrix components and that its overactivation leads to a variety of pathological states, including fibrosis (Toba et al. ). In acute and chronic myocarditis, ADAMTS1 expression increases proportionally with collagen accumulation, which may play a vital role in the occurrence and development of myocardial fibrosis via the regulation of collagen metabolism (Shen et al. ). Therefore, in this study, we focused on the role of ADAMTS1 in CFPMI, specifically its involvement in Transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)-induced cardiac fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix (ECM) production.
Members of the TGF-β superfamily are key regulators of cell differentiation, phenotype, and function, and are involved in numerous diseases pathogenesis (Hanna and Frangogiannis ). TGF-β1 plays a vital role in cardiac development, myocardial hypertrophy, ventricular remodeling, and the early-phase response to MI (Purnomo et al. ). Studies have shown that TGF-β1 is an effective stimulator of heart fibroblasts that produce collagen (Khan and Sheppard ). Repeated stimulation that triggers TGF-β1 expression can cause sustained damage, which is associated with increased angiotensin II (Ang II) activity in cardiac tissue (Lim and Zhu ). By inhibiting myocardial fibroblast activation, degrading the extracellular matrix, downregulating TGF-β1 expression, and regulating the TGF-β1/suppressor of mother against decapentaplegic (SMAD) signaling pathway, it can ameliorate myocardial fibrosis (Ren et al. ). Furthermore, in the aging myocardium, ADAMTS1 blocks antibodies to inhibit SPARC-induced Collagen I secretion, as well as the cellular expression of TGF-β1 and phosphorylated SMAD2 (Toba et al. ). Bioinformatics prediction analysis revealed potential binding sites of the transcription factor SMAD2 with the ADAMTS1 promoter region. However, the mechanism of ADAMTS1 and TGF-β1/SMAD2 in CFPMI remains to be explored.
Histone deacetylases (HDACs) constitute conserved enzymes that modulate gene transcription via catalyzing the deacetylation of acetyl groups from histone and nonhistone lysine residues (Bahl and Seto ; Curcio et al. ). Research has revealed the involvement of histone deacetylase 6 (HDAC6) and SP1 in the HDAC inhibition-induced expression of angiostatic ADAMTS1 (Chou and Chen ). Weng et al. reported that TGF-β1/SMAD3 inhibits CFPMI by inhibiting HDAC3-mediated regulation of PDCD5 (Weng et al. ). Therefore, we hypothesized that ADAMTS1 may modulate the fibrotic response through HDAC6 during cardiac fibrosis. Thus, we hypothesize that TGF-β1/SMAD2 may regulate ADAMTS1 to mediate CFPMI by inhibiting HDAC6 ubiquitination, but the specific mechanism needs to be further explored. Our study provides a new theoretical basis for the treatment of myocardial fibrosis.

## Materials and methods

## Collection of clinical samples
Blood samples were collected from clinical patients with myocardial fibrosis at Changsha Central Affiliated Hospital. Altogether, 30 blood samples from such patients, collected between January 2024 and June 2024, were included. The baseline characteristics of the cardiac fibrosis patients are shown in Table . All subjects received secondary prevention drug therapy for coronary heart disease. Representative cardiac MR images of short-axis view sections through the left ventricular lateral and inferior endocardium of cardiac fibrosis patients are shown in Figure . Blood samples from 30 age-matched disease-free individuals were used as healthy controls. All the samples were grouped into the Normal group or the CFPMI group. Myocardial fibrosis was diagnosed on the basis of history, physical examination, cardiac function and delayed gadolinium enhancement on cardiac magnetic resonance imaging. For subsequent experiments, serum samples were collected and cryopreserved at −80 °C.

## Animals
SPF-grade C57BL/6 male mice (8–12 weeks) were procured from Slac Jingda Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. All mice were housed in a controlled facility with a 12-h light/dark cycle at 23 ± 3 °C. To avoid the influence of estrogen fluctuations in female mice, male mice were selected to ensure the consistency of the results of this study. After the animals were in place, formal experimental procedures were initiated following one week of adaptive feeding.
Group 1: Mice were randomized into a Sham group and a CFPMI group, with 6 mice/group. When designing the experiment, we referred to the sample sizes of previous similar studies and determined that each group required at least six mice based on statistical requirements (such as power analysis) (Hayasaka et al. ; Li et al. ). All operations and analyses were performed in a blinded manner. The CFPMI mouse model was constructed with reference to previous studies (Weng et al. ). The mice were anesthetized with 3% isoflurane for a short time and then inhaled 1.5% isoflurane to maintain anesthesia. Each mouse’s left chest region was shaved and disinfected, and the mice were fixed in a supine position. Following endotracheal intubation, the mice were connected to a small-animal dedicated ventilator. The opening was 1 cm ± at the most obvious location of the heartbeat in the left chest. Subcutaneous fascia and muscles were dissected to fully expose the 3rd and 4th ribs. The intercostal muscles were cut across three or four intercostal spaces, the pericardium was incised to expose the heart, and the opening was secured with a spinner. The left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD) was quickly ligated using a No. 8 sterile band suture needle. The needle was inserted at the lower edge of the left atrial appendage, and the needle was removed from the pulmonary artery (insertion depth 0.5–1 mm, width 2 mm). Chest gas was removed (to prevent pneumothorax), and the wound was quickly closed and disinfected. The mice were kept warm until they awoke. The same operation was performed on Sham mice without LAD ligature.
Group 2: Mice were randomized into the Sham, CFPMI, and CFPMI + sh-ADAMTS1 groups. Mice in the CFPMI + sh-ADAMTS1 group were injected with 100 μL of shRNA-ADAMTS1 lentivirus slowly through the tail vein before surgery, and the lentivirus titer was 1 × 108 TU/mL. In contrast, mice in Sham and CFPMI groups were injected with adeno-associated virus (AAV) shRNA at the same dose as the control through the tail vein.
Group 3: Mice were randomized into the Sham, CFPMI, CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1, CFPMI + sh-HDAC6, and CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 groups. After CFPMI model establishment, mice in CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 group were administered 10 mg/kg anti-ADAMTS1 monoclonal antibody (ab134430, Abcam) via intraperitoneal injection once a week for 4 weeks (Wang et al. ). The ADAMTS1 antibody (ab134430, Abcam) used in this study is a specific monoclonal antibody targeting the ADAMTS1 protein. It specifically recognizes the extracellular domain of ADAMTS1, enabling specific targeting and blocking of its pathological functions. ADAMTS1 is primarily localized to the ECM and cell membrane, with its main biological effects exerted through extracellular interactions that regulate cellular behaviors (Li et al. ; Kong et al. ). Although the ADAMTS1 antibody cannot penetrate cells, it effectively inhibits the extracellular actions of ADAMTS1, thereby attenuating its regulatory effects on the ECM and cellular behaviors. The mice in the CFPMI + sh-HDAC6 group and the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group were injected with 100 μL of shRNA-HDAC6 lentivirus slowly through the tail vein before surgery, and the lentivirus titer was 1 × 108 TU/mL. AAV shRNA lentivirus were utilized for the knockdown of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6, owing to their high transfection efficiency, low immunogenicity, suitability for long-term expression, and ability to efficiently integrate into the host genome, thereby ensuring stable transgene expression. Mice in Sham and CFPMI groups were administered an equivalent dose of control AAV shRNA via tail vein. All the mice were subsequently housed in the animal facility and carefully fed for one week before being used for modeling. The experiment lasted for 4 weeks, and the mice were euthanized. Heart tissues were promptly excised and immersed in 4% paraformaldehyde for fixation. The hearts were cut into multiple cross-sectional slices for subsequent histological analysis. Due to the 28-day fibrosis evaluation period (including assessments of cardiac function, hematoxylin‒eosin (HE) staining and picrosirius red staining, protein expression level at 28 days’ post-infarction, etc.), we excluded animals that died within 28 days after myocardial infarction. The sequences of the mouse AAV-sh-HDAC6 (HG-AAVSH010413, HonorGene) were as follows: GGCCAAGATTCTTCTACTAGA, AAV-sh-NC: TGCACAACACCCAGGTGGTGA.

## Cell culture and treatment
Mouse cardiac fibroblasts (AW-YCM032, Abiowell) were cultured in fibroblast medium (AW-MC023, Abiowell), which consisted of fibroblast basal medium with 1% fibroblast additive, 10% fetal bovine serum, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Human cardiac fibroblasts (CP-H078, Procell) were cultured in human cardiac fibroblast cell complete medium (CM-H078, Procell), which contained all necessary components for the growth of human cardiac fibroblasts. This medium requires no additional supplements and can be directly used for in vitro culture. Human cardiac fibroblasts were incubated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 (HY-P78168, MCE) or 0.1 μM Ang II (HY-13948, MCE) for 12, 24, and 48 h to establish in vitro models, and mouse cardiac fibroblasts were treated with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 (50698-M08H-B, Sino Biological) for 12, 24, and 48 h to establish in vitro models (Weng et al. ), which was divided into the following groups: the Control, TGF-β1/Ang II (12 h), TGF-β1/Ang II (24 h), and TGF-β1/Ang II (48 h) groups. Regarding the exposure of cells to Ang II or TGF-βunder serum-containing conditions, our experiment included a normal control (Control) group for the model. By setting up this Control group, we could eliminate potential influences of Ang II or TGF-β that might be present in the culture medium. After the optimal time point (48 h) was determined, the samples were further divided into the Control, TGF-β1 and Ang II groups. Next, we used 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 or 0.1 μM Ang II to induce human or mouse cardiac fibroblasts for 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min. We also used 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 to stimulate human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts for 12, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h. Furthermore, human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts were exposed to 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 for 48 h, and assigned to Control and TGF-β1 groups. Subsequently, we interfered with SMAD2 expression, with the groups designated as si-NC and si-SMAD2-1/2. After the best si-SMAD2 was selected, the cells were further divided into the si-NC, TGF-β1, si-SMAD2, and TGF-β1 + si-SMAD2 groups. Furthermore, we overexpressed ADAMTS1 and divided the cells into the oe-NC, TGF-β1, oe-ADAMTS1, and TGF-β1 + oe-ADAMTS1 groups. Additionally, we interfered with ADAMTS1 in the following groups: si-NC, si-ADAMTS1-1, and si-ADAMTS1-2. After the si-ADAMTS1 group was selected, the cells were further divided into the si-NC, TGF-β1, si-ADAMTS1, and TGF-β1 + si-ADAMTS1 groups. Moreover, 5 μM HDAC6 inhibitor ACY1215 was treated for 48 h (Ruan et al. ), assigned to si-NC, si-ADAMTS1, si-NC + ACY1215, and si-ADAMTS1 + ACY1215 groups. Additionally, changes in level of ubiquitinated HDAC6 protein in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts transfected with oe-ADAMTS1/si-ADAMTS1 or oe-NC/si-NC in the presence of 10 μM MG132 were observed. We utilized siRNA technology to knock down ADAMTS1 (si-ADAMTS1) and set up a control group (NC). In human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts treated with si-ADAMTS1 and NC, proteasome inhibitor MG132 (10 μM) or DMSO (solvent control) were added respectively and incubated for 24 h. The groups were as follows: NC, MG132, si-ADAMTS1, and si-ADAMTS1 + MG132.
For the knockdown experiments of SMAD2 and ADAMTS1, we used siRNA fragments provided by HonorGene. The specific procedures were as follows: siRNAs were transfected into target cells via Lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Invitrogen). The knockdown efficiency of SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 was verified 48 h after transfection. In addition, for the overexpression experiment of ADAMTS1, we used the overexpression plasmids provided by HonorGene. The specific procedures were as follows: oe-ADAMTS1 was transfected into the target cells via Lipofectamine 2000, and the overexpression efficiency of ADAMTS1 was verified 48 h after transfection. The sequences of the human interference fragment si-SMAD2 (HG-SH005901, HonorGene) were as follows: si-SMAD2-1: GACCCCCCGGATCTGACGGG; si-SMAD2-2: GAACTTCCGCCTCTGGATGAC. The sequences of the mouse interference fragment si-SMAD2 (HG-SM010754, HonorGene) were as follows: si-SMAD2-1: GCGCGCCCGCTCGGCCGGCG; si-SMAD2-2: GCGTCACAGCCCTCGCTCAC. The human overexpression fragment oe-ADAMTS1 (HG-HO006988) and the mouse overexpression plasmid oe-ADAMTS1 (HG-MC105434) were obtained from HonorGene. The sequences of the human interference fragment si-ADAMTS1 (HG-SH006988, HonorGene) were as follows: si-ADAMTS1-1: GCCACTCTGCCTGCGCC TGCCTC; si-ADAMTS1-2: GCGCCCCTCCGAGGAGGACGAG. The sequences of the mouse interference fragment si-ADAMTS1 (HG-Mi050834, HonorGene) were as follows: si-ADAMTS1-1: GCACACATGCTGTTGCTGCTCCTC; si-ADAMTS1-2: GA CCCCACGCCTCAGGACGCGG.

## Conventional echocardiography and doppler imaging
Based on a previous study (Schiattarella et al. ), transthoracic echocardiography was conducted with a VisualSonics Vevo 2100 system coupled with an MS400 transducer. Diastolic function parameters were acquired from apical four-chamber views of anesthetized mice via pulsed-wave and tissue Doppler imaging at mitral valve level. Representative left ventricular M-mode echocardiograms were recorded, and ejection fraction (EF, reflecting the pumping efficiency of the heart with each contraction) and fractional shortening (FS, reflecting the degree of ventricle shortening during contraction) were calculated.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
ADAMTS1 levels in clinical serum samples were measured using an ADAMTS1 ELISA kit (CSB-E15060h, CUSABIO) following manufacturer’s protocol.

## HE and picrosirius red staining
HE staining was utilized to evaluate cardiac morphological alterations. Sections were heated at 60 °C for 12 h, followed by immersion in xylene three times for 20 min each. Subsequently, they were transferred to gradient ethanol (from high to low concentration) for hydration, and then soaked in distilled water. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin, differentiated, and blued in PBS. Afterward, sections were stained with eosin, followed by dehydration in gradient ethanol (95% to 100%, increasing concentration) for 5 min per gradient. Finally, sections were transferred to xylene for transparency, mounted with neutral balsam, and observed under a light microscope.
Fibrosis in mouse heart tissue was assessed by picrosirius red staining. We primarily analyzed the fibrotic conditions of the infarcted and non-infarcted areas. Using picrosirius red staining, we assessed collagen deposition in these two areas. Specifically, we analyzed the degree of fibrosis at the edge of infarct area and in the non-infarcted area to comprehensively evaluate the changes in fibrosis. Sections were heated at 60 °C followed by immersion in xylene. Subsequently, sections were subjected to gradient ethanol (from high to low concentration) for hydration and then rinsed thoroughly with distilled water. Thereafter, sections were stained with picrosirius red staining solution for 5–10 min. Finally, sections were air-dried thoroughly, cleared with xylene, and mounted with neutral balsam for microscopic observation. We used the ImageJ software to quantitatively analyze the picrosirius red-stained sections to assess the area of the fibrotic region. Each group should have at least three independent samples. The specific steps are as follows: Use a microscope to take pictures of Sirius red-stained sections, ensuring that the magnification and exposure time of all images are consistent. Set a threshold based on the depth of staining to separate the fibrotic area (the red-stained area) from the background. Use the “Measure” function of ImageJ to calculate the area of the fibrotic region and express it as a percentage (fibrotic region area/total tissue area).

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
ADAMTS1, p-SMAD2, and HDAC6 levels in mouse heart tissues were evaluated using IHC. Sections were heated at 60 °C for 12 h, followed by immersion in xylene. Subsequently, sections were sequentially immersed in 100%, 95%, 85%, and 75% ethanol for 5 min per concentration for hydration. Thereafter, antigen retrieval was conducted via heat treatment. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 1% periodic acid. Primary antibodies against ADAMTS1 (12,749–1-AP, 1:200, Proteintech), p-SMAD2 (Ab280888, 1:200, Abcam), and HDAC6 (bs-2811R, 1:200, Bioss) were diluted appropriately and incubated with sections at 4 °C overnight. Subsequently, 50–100 μL of HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. Thereafter, DAB chromogen working solution (50–100 μL per section) was added for color development, and the reaction process was monitored under a microscope to control the staining intensity. Sections were then counterstained with hematoxylin and blued in PBS. Next, sections were dehydrated via a graded ethanol series (60%, 75%, 85%, 95%, 100%) for 5 min per concentration. Finally, sections were cleared in xylene twice for 10 min each, mounted with neutral balsam, and observed under a light microscope.

## Western blot
Total protein was isolated from cells and mouse heart tissues, and ADAMTS1, Collagen I, fibronectin (FN), SMAD2, p-SMAD2, α-SMA, HDAC6 and TGF-β1 levels were determined via Western blot. Briefly, total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer (AWB0136, Abiowell). Equal amounts of protein (20 μg per lane) were separated by SDS‒PAGE and electrotransferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. Subsequently, membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk. The blocked membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following primary antibodies: ADAMTS1 (ab236497, 1:2000, Abcam), Collagen I (ab270993, 1:1000, Abcam), FN (AWA00143, 1:1000, Abiowell), SMAD2 (12,570–1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech), p-SMAD2 (ab280888, 1:1000, Abcam), α-SMA (ab5694, 1 μg/ml, Abcam), HDAC6 (128,341-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech), TGF-β1 (AWA00083, 1:1000, Abiowell), and GAPDH (10,494–1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech) at 4 °C overnight. The membrane was then incubated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse/rabbit IgG (SA00001-1/SA00001-2, 1:5000/1:6000, Proteintech) for 1.5 h. Protein bands were visualized by incubating membranes with enhanced ECL reagent (AWB0005, Abiowell) for 1 min, followed by imaging with a chemiluminescence imaging system. The original images are shown in Figure .

## Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT‒PCR)
ADAMTS1, Collagen I, FN, SMAD2 and HDAC6 levels were determined using qRT-PCR. Briefly, total RNA was extracted from samples with TRIzol total RNA extraction kit (15,596,026, Thermo). The concentration and purity of the extracted RNA were determined with NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized from mRNA by reverse transcription kit (CW2569, CWBIO). qRT-PCR was conducted on an ABI 7900 Real-Time PCR System using Ultra SYBR Master Mix (CW2601, CWBIO) to quantify gene expression levels. GAPDH acted as internal reference, and target gene levels were calculated using 2−△△Ct method. Primer sequences are shown below: H-ADAMTS1-F: CTAAAGCATTACCTTCTCACGTT, H-ADAMTS1-R: CGGCCCCTTCTGTTCATCG; H-Collagen I-F: GCAAGAACCCCGCCCGCACC, H-Collagen I-R:GCTCTCGCCGAACCAGACATGCC; H-FN-F: ATTCACCTACAATGGCAGGACGTT, H-FN-R: GCACCAAAGATGTCCGTCCTGT; H-SMAD2-F: TGCATCACAGCCCTCACTCAC, H-SMAD2-R: CTTGTCATTTCTACCGTGGCAT; H-HDAC6-F: CTCAACAGAAACACCGCATCCG, H-HDAC6-R: CCAGAACCTACCCTGCTCGT; H-GAPDH-F: ACAGCCTCAAGATCATCAGC, H-GAPDH-R: GGTCATGAGTCCTTCCACGAT; M-SMAD2-F: CTACACCCACTCCATTCC, M-SMAD2-R: GCAGGTTCCGAGTAAGTAA; M-HDAC6-F: GTCATCCAAGTCCATCGCAAA, M-HDAC6-R: CTATGGTCTTCCTCACGCTTT; M-ADAMTS1-F: AAGAATCCGCAGCTTTAGTCC,M-ADAMTS1-R: CCCCACTCTTCAATCACCCAC; M-GAPDH-F: GCGACTTCAACAGCAACTCCC, M-GAPDH-R: CACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCCGTA.

## Immunofluorescence (IF)
Vimentin (VIM), HDAC6, ADAMTS1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I, and FN levels in cultured cells were determined via IF staining. Briefly, cells grown on glass slides were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature. Fixed cells were then permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS to facilitate antibody penetration. Subsequently, non-specific binding sites were blocked with 5% BSA. Primary antibodies against VIM (10,366–1-AP, 1:200, Proteintech), HDAC6 (12,834–1-AP, 1:50, Proteintech), ADAMTS1 (12,749–1-AP, 1:50, Proteintech), FN (AWA10327, 1:50, Abiowell), Collagen I (ab138492, 1:200, Abcam), and p-SMAD2 (ab280888, 1:50, Abcam) were incubated at 4 °C overnight. Goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) (AWS0003a, Abiowell) or goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (AWS0006a/AWS0005a, Abiowell) (50–100 μL) was incubated for 90 min at 37 °C. Furthermore, cell nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 10 min. Finally, slides were mounted with anti-fade buffered glycerol, sealed, and either stored at 4 °C in the dark or immediately imaged under a fluorescence microscope.

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)
Binding of SMAD2 to ADAMTS1 was verified by ChIP. Cells were fixed in 1% formaldehyde (10 min, room temperature) for protein-DNA crosslinking. Subsequently, chromatin was isolated, and genomic DNA was sheared randomly into 200–800 bp fragments via ultrasonication and immunoprecipitated with targeted protein-specific antibodies against SMAD2. For the purposes of purifying and eluting ChIP DNA, one hundred microlitres of H2O was applied. A total of 2.5 μL of ChIP-DNA was subjected to qRT‒PCR. SMAD2 enrichment in the ADAMTS1 gene promoter was assessed with different primers: H-ADAMTS1-F: CTTCCGCCCCTAGATTGACGAG, H-ADAMTS1-R: CTCCTCCCCTTTGGCCCTT; M-ADAMTS1-F: CTGTTTCGGCGTCTTACACCC, M-ADAMTS1-R: CCGCCCCTTTATAGCCACA.

## Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
Co-IP was used to assess whether ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 interact and whether ADAMTS1 and SMAD2 interact in cells. ADAMTS1 or HDAC6 antibodies were used as bait proteins, and ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 expression was assessed by Western blot. First, cells were collected for protein extraction. Subsequently, equal amounts of protein extracts were immunoprecipitated in RIPA buffer (AWB0136, Abiowell) containing ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 antibodies at 4 °C overnight. Following this, protein A/G agarose beads were introduced into immunoprecipitation mixture for 2 h. Subsequently, the mixture underwent three washing cycles. The immunoprecipitated sample was then resuspended in SDS‒PAGE buffer, subjected to boiling, resolved by SDS‒PAGE, and then blotted onto a nitrocellulose membrane. Membrane was initially probed with primary antibodies against ADAMTS1 (ab236497, 1:2000, Abcam) and HDAC6 (128,341-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech), followed by incubation with matching secondary antibodies. ECL solution was applied to the nitrocellulose membrane for 1 min, and a gel imaging system was used for imaging.

## Statistical analysis
All statistical evaluations were carried out via GraphPad Prism 8.0. Quantitative data were denoted as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Kolmogorov‒Smirnov test combined with exploratory descriptive statistics was applied to assess the normality of data distribution and the homogeneity of variance. Subsequent analyses verified that the dataset followed a normal distribution pattern and displayed homogeneous variance. Parametric tests were applied for data analysis. When comparing two independent groups, we used unpaired Student’s t-tests; for comparisons involving multiple groups, one-way ANOVA was followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. To assess group differences across different time points, two-way ANOVA was performed with Bonferroni correction as the post hoc test. The analysis results were reported using three key metrics: degrees of freedom, sum of squares, and mean sum of squares. Statistical significance was indicated by P < 0.05.

## Results

## ADAMTS1 levels were increased in the serum of clinical patients with myocardial fibrosis, in the cardiac tissues of mice after CFPMI and in cardiac fibroblasts stimulated with fibrotic factors
First, we collected clinical serum samples to assess ADAMTS1 levels. In comparison to Normal group, ADAMTS1 levels were elevated in CFPMI group (Fig.  A). Next, we constructed a mouse model of CFPMI. Figure  B presents the Echocardiography results and showed that in comparison to Sham group, EF and FS in CFPMI group were notably inhibited. Furthermore, CFPMI group exhibited a notably lower survival rate relative to Sham group (Fig.  C). HE staining further confirmed the hypertrophy of myocardial cells and the disorder of tissue structure in CFPMI group. The picrosirius red staining results further confirmed the increase of collagen deposition in CFPMI group. In comparison to Sham group, Collagen Volume Fraction in CFPMI group increased markedly (Fig.  D). Additionally, in contrast to sham group, cardiac ADAMTS1 levels were markedly elevated in CFPMI group (Fig.  E and 1 F). The IF staining clearly showed the expression of VIM in both mouse and human cardiac fibroblasts, confirming their identity and purity (Figure ). Next, we constructed in vitro models via induction with 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 or 0.1 μM Ang II for 12, 24, and 48 h to treat human cardiac fibroblasts. In contrast to Control group, ADAMTS1, Collagen I, and FN levels in 12 h, 24 h and 48 h groups gradually increased with increasing time points (Fig.  G and 1H). Furthermore, following treatment with TGF-β1 and Ang II, ADAMTS1, Collagen I, and FN mRNA levels changed notably at 12 h and 48 h relative to Control group (Fig.  I). Therefore, 48 h was selected as the time point for subsequent experiments. IF results also revealed increased levels of ADAMTS1 in the TGF-β1 and Ang II groups in contrast to Control group (Fig.  J). Additionally, we evaluated ADAMTS1, Collagen I, and FN mRNA levels in human cardiac fibroblasts treated with TGF-β1 and Ang II at multiple time points. As shown in Fig.  K, these target genes exhibited marked changes in mRNA expression at distinct time points; specifically, their expression was progressively upregulated in a time-dependent manner with prolonged TGF-β1 and Ang II exposure. Our results revealed that ADAMTS1 levels were increased in the serum of clinical patients with myocardial fibrosis, in the cardiac tissues of mice after CFPMI and in cardiac fibroblasts stimulated with fibrotic factors. 

## SMAD2 regulated ADAMTS1 expression in TGF-β1-stimulated human and murine cardiac fibroblasts
To further elucidate the functional effect of ADAMTS1 in TGF-β1-induced cell fibrosis, we analyzed ADAMTS1 expression after 15–120 min of TGF-β1 exposure. TGF-β1 (10 ng/mL) was employed to induce human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts for 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min. In comparison to Control group, TGF-β1 increased p-SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 expression with increasing treatment time in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts. In contrast, SMAD2 expression exhibited no statistically significant differences across all treatment groups (Fig.  A and 2B). To further verify the expression pattern of ADAMTS1, we detected the mRNA levels of ADAMTS1, Collagen I, and FN at hourly time points (12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h). Consistent with the protein levels, their mRNA levels increased rapidly after fibrotic stimulation, peaking mainly at 48 h and remaining elevated thereafter. This demonstrated high consistency between transcriptional regulation and protein expression of these molecules, with their expression significantly increasing primarily in the late stage of fibrosis (Fig.  C). Furthermore, we used IF double staining to detect the expressions of p-SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 at 48 h. In Fig.  D, we present the staining results of p-SMAD2; the 48-h time point was selected to comprehensively evaluate the dynamic changes of p-SMAD2 after TGF-β1 stimulation. Although we had observed an increase in ADAMTS1 protein level at 15 min, indicating that the downstream response of the TGF-β1 signaling pathway is extremely rapid, the activation and translocation of p-SMAD2 to the nucleus may require a longer time to complete its transcriptional regulatory function. Therefore, the 48-h time point was chosen to capture the stable accumulation and sustained activation of p-SMAD2 in the nucleus, which is more relevant to the transcriptional regulation of ADAMTS1. Our results demonstrated that p-SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 levels were elevated in TGF-β1 group at 48 h in contrast to Control group (Fig.  D). In addition, we examined p-SMAD2 expression in mouse heart tissue. In contrast to Sham group, cardiac p-SMAD2 levels were markedly elevated in CFPMI group (Fig.  E). We predicted SMAD2 binding sites on ADAMTS1 promoter in JASPAR (https://jaspar.elixir.no/), and ChIP further showed that the SMAD2 protein could pull down ADAMTS1 mRNA, with SMAD2 being significantly enriched at the ADAMTS1 promoter locus (Fig.  F). This indicated that SMAD2 may regulate the transcription of ADAMTS1 by directly binding to its promoter region. However, Co-IP verification revealed that SMAD2 antibody could not pull down the ADAMTS1 protein, and there was no obvious interaction between SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 (Figure ). This suggested that there may be no direct physical interaction between SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 proteins in the cellular environment. Then, we interfered with SMAD2 expression. In contrast to si-NC group, SMAD2 levels were inhibited in the si-SMAD2-1 and si-SMAD2-2 groups. Notably, si-SMAD2-2 group exhibited the most pronounced reduction in SMAD2 expression and was therefore selected for subsequent study (Fig.  G). In comparison to si-NC group, ADAMTS1, Collagen I, and FN levels were facilitated in TGF-β1 group and inhibited in si-SMAD2 group. After interfering with SMAD2 via TGF-β1 treatment for 48 h in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts, ADAMTS1, Collagen I and FN expression was decreased (Fig.  H). Our results suggested that SMAD2 regulated ADAMTS1 expression in TGF-β1-stimulated human and murine cardiac fibroblasts. 

## Overexpression of ADAMTS1 enhanced the production of collagen fiber proteins in TGF-β1-stimulated human and murine cardiac fibroblasts
Next, we overexpressed ADAMTS1. In comparison to oe-NC group, ADAMTS1, Collagen I, FN and α-SMA protein levels were elevated in TGF-β1 and oe-ADAMTS1 groups. These proteins levels were further elevated after ADAMTS1 overexpression in TGF-β1-treated human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts for 48 h (Fig.  A). Consistent with the protein expression data, IF staining results also revealed increased levels of Collagen I and FN in TGF-β1 and oe-ADAMTS1 groups in contrast to oe-NC group, with further elevation upon ADAMTS1 overexpression in the TGF-β1-treated human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts (Fig.  B-3D). In addition, ADAMTS1 knockdown was conducted to verify its functional role. In comparison to si-NC group, ADAMTS1 expression in si-ADAMTS1-1 and si-ADAMTS1-2 groups was decreased. Among them, si-ADAMTS1-1 group displayed the most significant reduction in ADAMTS1 levels and was thus chosen for subsequent functional assays (Fig.  E). In comparison to si-NC group, ADAMTS1, Collagen I, FN and α-SMA levels were facilitated in TGF-β1 group and repressed in si-ADAMTS1 group. After interfering with ADAMTS1 via TGF-β1 treatment of human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts, these proteins levels decreased (Fig.  F and 3G). The IF results revealed elevated levels of Collagen I and FN in TGF-β1 group but repressed levels of Collagen I and FN in si-ADAMTS1 group in contrast to si-NC group. In comparison to TGF-β1 group, Collagen I and FN levels were lower in TGF-β1 + si-ADAMTS1 group (Fig. H and I). Our results showed that overexpression of ADAMTS1 enhanced the production of collagen fiber proteins in TGF-β1-stimulated human and murine cardiac fibroblasts. 

## Knockdown of ADAMTS1 alleviated TGF-β1-induced fibrosis by downregulating HDAC6 protein expression
A previous study showed that restraining HDAC6 activity notably represses TGF-β1-induced fibroblast differentiation (Qin et al. ) and that HDAC6 and SP1 are involved in the HDAC inhibition-induced expression of angiostatic ADAMTS1 (Chou and Chen ). Therefore, we hypothesized that ADAMTS1 may modulate the fibrotic response through HDAC6 during cardiac fibrosis. First, we validated HDAC6 expression in mouse heart tissue. In comparison to Sham group, HDAC6 expression in the hearts of the mice in CFPMI group was markedly elevated (Fig.  A). Additionally, interference with ADAMTS1 decreased ADAMTS1 mRNA expression and ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 protein expression, whereas it exerted no statistically significant effect on HDAC6 mRNA expression (Fig.  B and 4 C). Moreover, HDAC6 expression was greater in the TGF-β1 and oe-ADAMTS1 groups than in the oe-NC group. After ADAMTS1 was overexpressed, HDAC6 expression further increased (Fig.  D). HDAC6 levels were elevated in TGF-β1 group and repressed in si-ADAMTS1 group in contrast to si-NC group. After interfering with ADAMTS1 via TGF-β1 treatment in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts, HDAC6 expression was reduced (Fig.  E). Next, we treated human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts with the HDAC6 inhibitor ACY1215 at a concentration of 5 μM after interference with ADAMTS1. In comparison to si-NC group, TGF-β1, Collagen I, FN and ADAMTS1 levels in si-NC and si-NC + ACY1215 groups were lower. TGF-β1, Collagen I, FN and ADAMTS1 expression was further decreased after treatment of human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts with ACY1215 via interference with ADAMTS1 (Fig.  F). Furthermore, we conducted an experiment to treat human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts with HDAC6 inhibitor ACY1215 after overexpressing ADAMTS1. ADAMTS1, HDAC6, TGF-β1, Collagen I and FN levels were assessed via Western blot. As shown by the experimental data, in contrast to oe-NC group, ADAMTS1, HDAC6, TGF-β1, Collagen I, and FN levels were notably upregulated in oe-ADAMTS1 group. Conversely, following additional treatment with the HDAC6 inhibitor ACY1215, these proteins were markedly downregulated (Fig.  G and 4H). Our results suggested that the knockdown of ADAMTS1 alleviated TGF-β1-induced fibrosis by downregulating HDAC6 protein expression. Additionally, ADAMTS1 mediated the expression of the HDAC6 protein without affecting the transcriptional process of HDAC6. 

## ADAMTS1 interacted with HDAC6 during fibrosis
Next, Co-IP further confirmed that ADAMTS1 interacted with HDAC6 (Fig.  A and 5B). IF further demonstrated the positive co-localization of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 under TGF-β1 stimulation. In Fig.  C, we present the co-localization of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 in TGF-β1-treated human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts. Notably, marked co-localization of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 was observed in TGF-β1 group. In contrast to Control group, ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 levels in TGF-β1 group were notably elevated. Additionally, in the presence of 10 μM MG132, the level of HDAC6 protein ubiquitination in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts transfected with oe-ADAMTS1 or si-ADAMTS1 was decreased or increased in contrast to their respective control groups (Fig.  D). In addition, we added MG132 (10 μM) to si-ADAMTS1-treated human and mouse myocardial fibroblasts for treatment. Western blot revealed in contrast to cells treated with NC, cells treated with si-ADAMTS1 had significantly reduced levels of ADAMTS1 protein ubiquitination, indicating that ADAMTS1 significantly impacted the stability of HDAC6 protein. The addition of MG132 to si-ADAMTS1 treated cells led to a notable elevate in HDAC6 protein ubiquitination levels, suggesting that MG132 primarily restored HDAC6 protein ubiquitination levels by inhibiting the proteasome pathway (Fig.  E). Furthermore, to explore the effects of TGF-β1 and SMAD2 on HDAC6 ubiquitination, we conducted the following group experiments in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts: si-NC, TGF-β1, si-SMAD2, and TGF-β1 + si-SMAD2. In contrast to si-NC group, HDAC6 protein ubiquitination levels were markedly facilitated in TGF-β1 group. The addition of si-SMAD2 treatment significantly reduced the ubiquitination level of HDAC6 (Fig.  F). This indicated that TGF-β1 could significantly increase the ubiquitination level of HDAC6 and decrease its total protein level, while the knockdown of SMAD2 could partially counteract the effects of TGF-β1 on HDAC6 ubiquitination and degradation, indicating that SMAD2 may mediate regulating HDAC6 within the TGF-β1 signaling pathway. Our results suggested that ADAMTS1 interacted with HDAC6 during fibrosis. 

## AAV-shRNA-ADAMTS1 treatment alleviated myocardial fibrosis and improved cardiac function after CFPMI
To verify the role of ADAMTS1 in vivo through HDAC6, we utilized sh-ADAMTS1 in the CFPMI mouse model. The experimental groups included: Sham group, CFPMI group, and CFPMI + sh-ADAMTS1 group. Figure  A presents the echocardiography results from different treatment groups. In contrast to Sham group, EF and FS were observably reduced in the CFPMI group, and treatment with sh-ADAMTS1 partially restored these parameters. Furthermore, CFPMI group exhibited a notably lower survival rate relative to Sham group, and treatment with sh-ADAMTS1 increased the survival rate (Fig.  B). HE staining results further confirmed cardiac hypertrophy and disorganized tissue structure in the CFPMI group, which were ameliorated by sh-ADAMTS1 treatment. Picrosirius red staining results further confirmed increased collagen deposition in the CFPMI group, which was mitigated by sh-ADAMTS1 treatment. In comparison to Sham group, Collagen Volume Fraction was markedly elevated in CFPMI group. Treatment with sh-ADAMTS1 notably repressed Collagen Volume Fraction (Fig.  C). These findings indicated sh-ADAMTS1 treatment exerted a prominent protective effect against cardiac hypertrophy, improving cardiac function and reducing cardiac cell hypertrophy and collagen deposition. Moreover, HDAC6 mRNA levels were notably elevated in CFPMI group in contrast to Sham group, and sh-ADAMTS1 treatment reduced HDAC6 mRNA expression (Fig.  D). In comparison to Sham group, TGF-β1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I, and FN levels were upregulated in cardiac tissues of CFPMI group. Administration of sh-ADAMTS1 in CFPMI mice markedly downregulated TGF-β1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I, and FN levels, whereas SMAD2 expression levels exhibited no statistically significant differences across all experimental groups (Figs.  E and 6 F). Collectively, these results demonstrated that ADAMTS1 acted through HDAC6 to mediate cardiac fibrosis in vivo. 

## AAV-shRNA-HDAC6 transfection combined with ADAMTS1 inhibitor treatment alleviated myocardial fibrosis and improved cardiac function after CFPMI
Furthermore, we investigated the mechanism of action of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 at the animal level. Figure  A presents the echocardiography results from different treatment groups. The results indicated CFPMI group had markedly decreased cardiac function parameters (EF and FS) relative to the Sham group, which were partially restored by anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 treatments, with the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group showing the most pronounced recovery. Additionally, CFPMI group exhibited a notably lower survival rate relative to Sham group, and treatments with anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 increased survival rates, particularly the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group, which exhibited the highest survival rate (Fig.  B). HE staining results further confirmed cardiac hypertrophy and disorganized tissue structure in the CFPMI group, which were ameliorated by anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 treatments, especially the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group showing more evident tissue protection effects. Picrosirius red staining results further confirmed increased collagen deposition in the CFPMI group, which was mitigated by anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 treatments, with the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group showing the lowest collagen deposition. In comparison to Sham group, CFPMI group showed a markedly increased Collagen Volume Fraction, indicating increased myocardial fibrosis. Treatments with anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 significantly reduced the Collagen Volume Fraction, especially the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group, which showed the lowest Collagen Volume Fraction, indicating the strongest inhibitory effect on myocardial fibrosis (Fig.  C and 7D). These results suggested that anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 treatments have protective effects against cardiac hypertrophy, improving cardiac function, reducing cardiac cell hypertrophy, and collagen deposition, with the CFPMI + anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group showing the best therapeutic effect. In addition, HDAC6 expression was elevated in CFPMI group compared with sham group. After AAV-sh-HDAC6 alone or anti-ADAMTS1 and AAV-sh-HDAC6 were used on the basis of CFPMI, HDAC6 expression was decreased, while anti-ADAMTS1 alone on the basis of CFPMI showed no significant change in HDAC6 expression (Fig.  E). Furthermore, in comparison to Sham group, TGF-β1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I, FN and ADAMTS1 expression in heart tissue of mice in CFPMI group was increased. TGF-β1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I and FN expression was decreased after the use of anti-ADAMTS1 or AAV-sh-HDAC6 alone on the basis of CFPMI. However, ADAMTS1 expression was decreased after the use of anti-ADAMTS1 alone on the basis of CFPMI. After the simultaneous use of anti-ADAMTS1 and AAV-sh-HDAC6 in combination with CFPMI, TGF-β1, p-SMAD2, Collagen I and FN expression was further reduced, but ADAMTS1 expression did not change significantly. Whereas SMAD2 expression levels exhibited no statistically significant differences across all experimental groups (Fig.  F and 7G). Our results suggested that AAV-sh-HDAC6 combined with ADAMTS1 inhibitor treatment alleviated myocardial fibrosis and improved cardiac function after CFPMI. In conclusion, combining the above results, we concluded that TGF-β1/SMAD2 regulated ADAMTS1 by mediating CFPMI through HDAC6 ubiquitination (Fig.  ). 

## Discussion
Adverse remodeling after MI is characterized by a phenotypic change in myocardial fibroblasts into myofibroblasts, pathological excessive deposition of a fibrotic extracellular matrix dominated by fibronectin and collagen (Ruocco et al. ), and cardiac dysfunction, which can eventually lead to severe heart diseases and conditions, such as heart failure (Li et al. ). Therefore, understanding the key mechanism (fibrosis) will facilitate further drug development and patient treatment (Han et al. ). In the current study, we systematically explored the underlying mechanisms of CFPMI using complementary in vitro and in vivo experimental models. We found that targeting ADAMTS1/HDAC6 alleviated TGF-β1/SMAD2-associated cardiac fibrosis in CFPMI.
Developing cardiomyocytes respond to signals from the endocardial layer to form a trabecular network characteristic of the ventricles of the vertebrate heart. During late embryogenesis, ADAMTS1 begins to be expressed in the endocardium to degrade cardiac glia and prevent excessive trabeculae (Stankunas et al. ). ADAMTS1 is a secreted metalloproteinase that primarily participates in ECM remodeling through its enzymatic activity (Minns et al. ). It can degrade various ECM components, such as chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans and collagen, thereby modulating the interactions between cells and the ECM (Toba et al. ; Parlak Ak et al. ). This enzymatic activity plays a crucial role in myocardial fibrosis: by degrading ECM components, ADAMTS1 can alter the extracellular microenvironment and subsequently regulate cellular behaviors (Li et al. ; Kong et al. ). ADAMTS1 can be used as a new marker for the postmortem differential diagnosis of MI (Nakamura ). At 3 h after MI, the myocardial endothelium and myocardium (mainly in the infarction area) expressed strong ADAMTS1 mRNA signals (Pehlivan et al. ). Hatipoglu et al. reported that ADAMTS1 mRNA levels increased rapidly under TNF-α stimulation, but the induction of ADAMTS1 mRNA peaked at 6 h after stimulation, and ADAMTS1 mRNA levels decreased (Hatipoglu et al. ). Through verification, we found that ADAMTS1 levels were elevated in serum of clinical patients with myocardial fibrosis, in cardiac tissues of mice after CFPMI, and in fibrotic factor-stimulated cardiac fibroblasts. In this study, although we did not supplement Ang II within the 48-h processing time, we put forward a reasonable conjecture to explain the observed incremental effect. We hypothesize that Ang II induces the upregulation of ADAMTS1 expression, and ADAMTS1 secretion may further induce myocardial cell fibrosis. This conjecture is supported by our experimental results: we observed ADAMTS1, Collagen I and FN levels gradually elevated over time. These results demonstrated increased ADAMTS1 may be a key factor for the continuous progression of myocardial fibrosis after Ang II treatment. Therefore, we further explored the mechanism of ADAMTS1 in CFPMI.
SMAD2 is a core transcription factor in TGF-β signaling pathway and exerts a critical regulatory role in cardiac fibrosis (Chia et al. ). TGF-β promotes extracellular matrix gene expression and fibrous tissue deposition by activating SMAD2/3 complex (Khalil et al. ). During cardiac fibrosis, the activation of SMAD2/3 is considered a crucial factor in regulating the expression of fibrotic genes (Zhang et al. ). Studies have shown that inhibiting macrophage‒fibroblast crosstalk and retraining the activation of IL-18‒TGF-β1‒p-SMAD2/3 pathway in myocardial fibroblasts during the acute phase after MI could improve cardiac function and reduce myocardial fibrosis (Zhao et al. ). Hui et al. reported that CTRP9 reduced Collagen I and III, α-SMA and TGF-β1 levels 7 days after MI. Left atrial fibrosis may be inhibited by inhibiting the toll-like receptor 4/NF-κB and SMAD2/3 pathways (Hui et al. ). In addition, carbonic anhydrase 3 promotes fibroblast activation through the Smad7-TGF-β/SMAD2/3 signaling pathway and plays a protective role in cardiac wound repair after MI (Gorski et al. ). These studies suggest that the TGF-β1/SMAD2 pathway plays a vital role in myocardial fibrosis. In pulmonary fibrosis, inactivating TGF-β1/ADAMTS1 signaling leads to increased degradation of Collagen I and III within lung tissues, which may be an important mechanism by which emodin protects against pulmonary fibrosis in rats (Liu et al. ). However, the roles of SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 in myocardial fibrosis remain unclear. TGF-β promotes cardiac fibrosis by activating SMAD2/3, and ADAMTS1, a downstream target of TGF-β, may further enhance this process by regulating extracellular matrix synthesis and remodeling (Li et al. ). In this study, we further verified that SMAD2 regulated TGF-β1 to induce ADAMTS1 expression in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts during fibrosis. In addition, overexpression of ADAMTS1 enhanced the production of collagen fiber proteins in human and mouse cardiac fibroblasts induced by TGF-β1. Our findings provide a novel understanding of the interaction between SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 in cardiac fibrosis. SMAD2, a downstream effector of TGF-β1 signaling, mediates the transcriptional regulation of ADAMTS1. In turn, ADAMTS1 promotes the production of collagen fibrin, a key component of the fibrotic extracellular matrix (Chang et al. ). This regulatory axis (TGF-β1/SMAD2/ADAMTS1) is crucial for the fibrotic response in cardiac fibroblasts. Thus, therapeutic strategies developed to modulate SMAD2 and ADAMTS1 expression may mitigate CFPMI and improve cardiac function. SMAD3’s role in cardiac fibrosis has been extensively investigated, and its activation is closely associated with the activation of cardiac fibroblasts, collagen synthesis, and deposition (Dobaczewski et al. ; Zhang et al. ). Although both SMAD2 and SMAD3 serve critical roles in TGF-β1 signaling pathway (Yao et al. ), studies have shown that SMAD2 may have unique functions in certain cell types (Nitschkowski et al. ; Bronneberg et al. ). Furthermore, preliminary experimental data suggest that the activation of SMAD2 in cardiac fibroblasts is closely correlated with regulating ADAMTS1 expression. Thus, we chose SMAD2 as the primary object of study to uncover its specific mechanisms in cardiac fibrosis. However, we acknowledge that focusing solely on SMAD2 without in-depth investigation of SMAD3’s role is a limitation. Future research needs to further explore whether SMAD3 could also bind to the ADAMTS1 promoter and whether SMAD2 inhibition affects SMAD3 activation. This will help to more fully understand the mechanisms of the TGF-β1 signaling pathway in cardiac fibrosis.
In recent years, many HDAC inhibitors have shown promising clinical value in treating heart disease (Jin et al. ). HDAC activity is closely linked to cardiac fibrosis progression (Tao et al. ). A previous study revealed that dynamic regulation of the gut microbiota could improve tissue repair after MI in rats through butyric acid-mediated HDAC inhibition (Song et al. ). HDAC6 is a member of class IIb HDAC family, and its unique structure is a vital basis for specific interactions: it contains two tandem catalytic domains (CD1 and CD2) and a zinc finger ubiquitin-binding domain (ZnF-UBP), whereas HDAC1, 2, and 3 are class I HDACs, containing only a single catalytic domain and are primarily located in the nucleus (Curcio et al. ). As a secreted metalloenzyme, ADAMTS1 mainly functions in the cytoplasm and extracellular space, naturally providing conditions for binding with HDAC6, which is mainly located in the cytoplasm, whereas there is a spatial distribution difference with HDAC1, 2, and 3, which are primarily nuclear (Ustinova et al. ). Studies have shown that the ZnF-UBP domain of HDAC6 is the key site for its specific binding to target proteins, a domain unique to HDAC6 and not present in HDAC1, 2, and 3 (Balmik et al. ). Moreover, the catalytic domains of HDAC6 (especially CD1) significantly differ from HDAC1, 2, and 3 in terms of active site contour and amino acid composition, leading to distinct substrate binding specificity (Osko and Christianson ). For instance, selective inhibitors for HDAC6 could specifically bind to its catalytic domain without inhibitory activity against HDAC1, 2, and 3, further demonstrating the uniqueness of HDAC6 at the molecular binding level (Campiani et al. ). Additionally, previous research has confirmed HDAC6 knockdown notably repressed TGF-β1-induced fibroblast differentiation (Qin et al. ), and HDAC6 could bind to the ADAMTS1 promoter (Chou and Chen ). Therefore, we hypothesize that ADAMTS1 may regulate fibrotic responses through HDAC6 during cardiac fibrosis. Targeting HDAC6 emerges as a promising therapeutic strategy for the management of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction in preclinical mouse models (Ranjbarvaziri et al. ). In addition, overexpression of HDAC6 in cardiomyocytes can lead to decreased hardness of myofibrils (Lin et al. ). Zhou et al. revealed that SAHA (a potent HDAC inhibitor) treatment could downregulate the expression of VEGF, bFGF, TGF-β1 and EGF while increasing the expression of TSP-1, TSP-2 and ADAMTS1 (Zhou et al. ). Additionally, previous studies have confirmed HDAC6 acts as a critical transcriptional regulator governing ADAMTS1 gene expression (Chou and Chen ), suggesting that the two molecules are involved in the same regulatory network. Our research findings demonstrate that ADAMTS1 could modulate HDAC6 protein levels through its enzymatic activity. Specifically, ADAMTS1 directly interacted with HDAC6 and inhibited its ubiquitination, thereby stabilizing the HDAC6 protein. This interaction plays a crucial role in myocardial fibrosis: by stabilizing HDAC6, ADAMTS1 could further regulate ECM remodeling and cellular behaviors.
In this study, the rationale for investigating TGF-β1 expression after silencing ADAMTS1 and inhibiting HDAC6 was to elucidate how the crosstalk between ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 modulates the expression and secretion of TGF-β1 protein. Notably, prior studies have demonstrated that highly selective HDAC6 inhibitor CAY10603 significantly attenuates cigarette smoke extract-induced TGF-β1 release in human bronchial epithelial cells (Zhang et al. ). Another selective HDAC6 inhibitor ACY1215 has been shown to inhibit renal fibrosis primarily by suppressing TGF-β1 expression and SMAD3 phosphorylation induced by unilateral ureteral obstruction, while simultaneously increasing Smad7 expression (Chen et al. ). These studies suggested that HDAC6 may mediate TGF-β1 expression and contribute to the fibrotic process. At the molecular level, HDAC6 knockout (HDAC6−/−) has been shown to significantly reduce TGF-β1, p-Smad-2/3, and collagen I and III protein levels in ischemic cardiac tissues (Fang et al. ). In our study, consistent with previous findings, TGF-β1 expression was decreased in cells and animal tissues after silencing ADAMTS1 and inhibiting HDAC6. In vivo studies demonstrated that AAV-shRNA-HDAC6 transfection combined with ADAMTS1 inhibitor treatment alleviated myocardial fibrosis and improved cardiac function after CFPMI. Our study preliminarily confirmed the mechanism of ADAMTS1 and HDAC6 in CFPMI. However, due to limitations in experimental funding, we did not explore the potential mechanisms by which HDAC6 may regulate TGF-β1/SMAD2 signaling. In the future, we will explore the mechanism in depth.
Ubiquitination modification is the core mechanism for regulating HDAC6 function and also an important pathway by which ADAMTS1 modulates its target proteins. Previous studies have confirmed that the stability and activity of HDAC6 depend on ubiquitination: K48-linked ubiquitination mediates its proteasomal degradation, while K63-linked ubiquitination promotes its binding to target proteins (Waltho et al. ; Piscopo et al. ). Our study data demonstrated that silencing or overexpressing ADAMTS1 significantly altered HDAC6 ubiquitination levels, suggesting that ubiquitination is a key regulatory node in the interaction between these two molecules. We therefore hypothesize that ADAMTS1 directly interacts with HDAC6 to inhibit its K48-linked polyubiquitination, thereby stabilizing the HDAC6 protein. Additionally, ADAMTS1 degrades ECM components via its enzymatic activity, and the released signaling molecules could affect intracellular signaling pathways, which in turn regulate the ubiquitination status of HDAC6. However, we cannot confirm this mechanism at present, which represents a limitation of our study. To clarify the specific ubiquitination pathway in future research, we will construct a K63 ubiquitination site mutant of HDAC6. We will verify whether ADAMTS1 affects the ubiquitination level of this mutant via Co-IP) and detect the mutant’s impact on HDAC6 activity and fibrotic gene expression, aiming to directly confirm the role of K63-linked ubiquitination.
In addition, in the in vivo experiment, we applied anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 to the animals. In a study focusing on brain injury secondary to cardiac arrest (CA) and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), a rat model was established via CA/CPR induction, with adenovirus-encapsidated sh-HDAC6 administered to rats via tail vein injection (Chen et al. ). In our study, the mice in sh-HDAC6 group and anti-ADAMTS1 + sh-HDAC6 group were injected with 100 μL of shRNA-HDAC6 lentivirus slowly through the tail vein before surgery, and the lentivirus titer was 1 × 108 TU/mL. Research has revealed that anti-ADAMTS1 antibody relieved muscle dysfunction and fibrosis in dystrophic mice (Wang et al. ). In breast cancer, ADAMTS1-targeting antibody treatment inhibits tumor growth without eliciting adverse effects (Hirano et al. ). In our study, after model establishment, mice assigned to anti-ADAMTS1 group received an intraperitoneal injection of 10 mg/kg anti-ADAMTS1 monoclonal antibody once weekly for 4 consecutive weeks (Wang et al. ). These studies indicate that the two intervention methods of anti-ADAMTS1 and sh-HDAC6 for animals are feasible and have also been verified in other studies.
The core focus of this study is on the biological functions and downstream regulatory mechanisms of the interaction between ADAMTS1 and HDAC6. During the experimental design phase, we prioritized verifying the direct binding relationship between the two and the role of this interaction in target gene expression and cellular phenotype regulation, rather than expanding the experimental scope to comparative validation with other members of the HDAC family. Therefore, the current study can only confirm that there is an interaction between ADAMTS1 and HDAC6, and it cannot completely rule out the possibility of weak interactions between ADAMTS1 and other class I HDAC members such as HDAC1/2/3. This also leads to a lack of sufficient experimental support for the inference that “the ADAMTS1-HDAC6 interaction is specific”, and there is a certain degree of uncertainty in the conclusion. Subsequent studies will systematically compare the binding abilities of ADAMTS1 with different subclasses of the HDAC family members (including class I HDAC1/2/3 and class II HDAC4/5, etc.) through Co-IP, Pull-down experiments, to clarify the specificity of the interaction; combined with molecular docking simulations and site-directed mutagenesis experiments, to analyze the key structural domains of ADAMTS1 binding to HDAC6, explaining the structural basis of their specific binding at the molecular level; further functional rescue experiments will verify that only the complementation of HDAC6, not other HDAC family members, can reverse the cellular phenotype changes caused by ADAMTS1 knockdown, thereby substantiating the specificity of this interaction at the functional level. However, due to the current lack of efficient and specific small molecule activators for HDAC6, research interests and tool development in this field have long been focused on inhibitors. Therefore, we did not use an HDAC activator for treatment here. This is also a limitation of our study. Our conclusions are primarily derived from the perspective of activity inhibition. In the future, the emergence of new activation tools is expected to provide a more comprehensive interpretation of HDAC6 functions.
In summary, in this study, we evaluated the mechanism of action of ADAMTS1 in CFPMI and its relation to TGF-β1/SMAD2 and HDAC6. Our results suggest a new way to alleviate CFPMI. In addition, a better understanding of the role of ADAMTS1, TGF-β1/SMAD2 and HDAC6 in CFPMI may offer a theoretical basis for developing innovative CFPMI therapies.
