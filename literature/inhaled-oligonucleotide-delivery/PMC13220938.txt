## Background:
In pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), ferroptosis could influence vascular remodeling by affecting the pulmonary artery endothelial cells (PAECs). However, the underlying pathogenic mechanisms of ferroptosis in PAH and its impact on the pulmonary vascular cellular landscape remain poorly understood.

## Methods:
In this study, we integrated single-cell transcriptomic sequencing, proteomics, and summary-data-based Mendelian randomization to investigate the regulatory mechanisms of ferroptosis in PAH. Our findings were validated using monocrotaline-induced PAH rat models and hypoxia-induced PAEC models.

## Results:
Single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed an increased propensity for ferroptosis in PAECs, while general capillary endothelial cells showed enhanced proliferative tendencies. Integrated analysis of single-cell transcriptomics, proteomics, and summary-data-based Mendelian randomization suggested that SMAD7 may serve as a negative regulator of ferroptosis in PAECs. Additionally, experiments conducted both in vitro and in vivo showed that reducing SMAD7 expression enhanced ferroptosis in PAECs, whereas SMAD7 overexpression alleviated ferroptosis and pulmonary vascular remodeling, potentially by blocking the transforming growth factor-beta-1-SMAD2/3 signaling pathway.

## Conclusions:
This study provides preliminary evidence that SMAD7 may negatively regulate ferroptosis via the transforming growth factor-beta-1-SMAD2/3 pathway in PAECs and thereby contribute to pulmonary vascular remodeling in PAH. These findings highlight SMAD7 as a potential therapeutic target for PAH.

## INTRODUCTION
Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a serious vascular disorder characterized by the gradual remodeling of the pulmonary arteries, leading to right heart failure and premature death ( , ). Despite advances in current therapies, the 5-year survival rate is roughly 73.5% for patients who do not undergo lung transplantation ( ). The treatments available are mostly vasodilators focusing on the nitric oxide, endothelin, and prostacyclin pathways. These therapies offer symptom relief but do not stop the disease from advancing. The absence of curative options highlights the complex and multifactorial etiology of PAH, stemming from the interaction of genetic, external environmental, and hemodynamic factors ( ). Consequently, understanding the genetic foundation of PAH is crucial for creating targeted treatments and enhancing clinical results.
New evidence points to ferroptosis, an iron-dependent regulated cell death distinguished by heightened lipid peroxidation, as a potential contributor to PAH development ( , ). The disturbance of iron balance, which is a key aspect of ferroptosis, is connected to a range of diseases, such as cancer, cardiovascular issues, and age-related diseases ( , ). In PAH, pulmonary arterial endothelial cells (ECs) exhibit marked mitochondrial dysfunction and altered iron metabolism, which may generate a cellular microenvironment that is highly susceptible to ferroptotic injury ( ). In line with this hypothesis, both pharmacological inhibition and genetic suppression of ferroptosis have been found to lessen disease severity in experimental PAH models ( ). Nevertheless, the precise role of ferroptosis in PAH remains unresolved. It is uncertain whether ferroptosis is a direct driver of pulmonary vascular remodeling or a secondary reaction to metabolic stress and endothelial injury. Moreover, the identification of causal ferroptosis-related genes in PAH is challenged by reverse causality and residual confounding inherent to observational studies. Therefore, a systematic and rigorous approach is required to clarify causal relationships and to delineate the regulatory network governing ferroptosis in PAH.
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has recently been commonly applied to explore cellular variations in PAH, enabling the identification of distinct endothelial, smooth muscle, and immune cell subpopulations, as well as dynamic transcriptional changes at single-cell resolution ( , ). However, scRNA-seq captures only the transcriptome and may not fully reflect functional protein-level regulation, particularly for pathways governed by post-transcriptional control, protein turnover, and enzymatic activity. To better link transcriptional states to molecular phenotypes, proteomics provides a complementary layer by directly quantifying protein abundance and pathway activity in diseased tissues. Mendelian randomization (MR) provides a robust method for determining causality by reducing reverse causation, employing genetic variants, including single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as instrumental tools. However, conventional MR analysis is limited in its ability to elucidate disease-specific molecular mechanisms ( ).
The SMAD protein family serves as intracellular effectors downstream of transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) signaling. Among them, SMAD7 is a classic inhibitory regulator of TGF-β signaling, functioning by preventing the phosphorylation of the SMAD2/3 complex and the subsequent nuclear translocation, thereby antagonizing its transcriptional activity. This action effectively suppresses TGF-β signaling and plays a crucial role in maintaining endothelial cell function and modulating fibrosis in various diseases ( ). Moreover, abnormal activation of ferroptosis has been shown to exacerbate cellular damage ( , ), thereby promoting the progression of myocardial fibrosis. However, the involvement of SMAD7 in ferroptosis in PAH remains unclear.
This study involved the integration of single-cell transcriptomic sequencing, proteomics, and summary-data-based MR (SMR) to investigate ferroptosis-related cellular subpopulations and their potential regulatory genes in PAH. In addition, the functional role of Smad7 in regulating ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs was validated using both in vivo and in vitro models, with the aim of discovering new therapeutic targets for PAH.

## METHODS

## Data sources
scRNA-seq data (GSE244781), comprising lung samples from three PAH patients and 12 healthy controls, were downloaded from GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/, accessed on October 13, 2025) for analysis. Since the data in the GEO database are publicly accessible, no further ethical approval was necessary for this study.
Lung tissue samples for proteomics sequencing were collected during lung transplantations from three PAH patients and three donors (healthy controls) at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital. The proteomics data collected in this study have been submitted to the China National Center for Bioinformation OMIX repository (OMIX014026) at https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/omix/preview/urRtufjL.
Genes associated with ferroptosis were sourced from FerrDb V2 (564 genes; https://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/current/). Information on blood methylation quantitative trait locus (mQTLs) was gathered from a meta-analysis involving two European groups: the Brisbane Systems Genetics Study with 614 individuals and the Lothian Birth Cohorts with 1,366 individuals ( ). Data on blood expression quantitative trait locus (eQTLs) were obtained from the eQTLGen consortium, which includes 31,684 participants, predominantly of European ancestry ( ). The Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) data for PAH were derived from a meta-analysis using inverse-variance weighting of two separate cohorts with European ancestry, including GCST007228 with 2,085 cases and 9,659 controls, and the UK Biobank dataset (code X415.21) with 446 cases and 402,375 controls (Supplementary Table S1, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C801).

## Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis
The GSE244781 dataset was acquired from GEO and analyzed in R (v4.5.1). Downstream processing was conducted using Seurat (v5.0.2) ( , ). Cells expressing <500 or >5,000 genes, genes were less than 3 cells, or cells with ≥10% mitochondrial reads were excluded as low quality. Potential doublets were identified using DoubletFinder (v2.0.4) with default settings ( ). Data normalization was performed using SCTransform, and the Harmony package (version 1.2.0) was used to correct batch effects ( ). Marker genes were identified by FindAllMarkers, and cell subpopulations were annotated based on established canonical markers.
Genes were selected for gene set enrichment analysis if their adjusted P value was under 0.05 and were present in at least 10% of cells. Eligible genes were ranked by fold change and analyzed using clusterProfiler (v4.10.1) with gene ontology (GO) gene sets ( ).
The AddModuleScore function was used to assess endothelial cell subpopulations and the differences in their activities and functions between control and PAH groups. Scores were calculated for pro-inflammatory, cell death, proliferation, ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and necroptosis. Gene sets were curated from relevant literature. The inflammation score comprised 419 genes from interleukin and the tumor necrosis factor superfamily pathways. The cell death score was based on the GO:0008219 gene set, and the proliferation score was based on the GO:0001935. Necroptosis, pyroptosis, and ferroptosis were assessed using the GO:0070266, GO:0141201, and GO:0160020 gene sets, respectively.

## Pseudotime analysis
Cell developmental trajectories were inferred with the help of the Monocle package version 2.30.1 ( ). Initially, the top 2,000 genes associated with cellular differentiation were identified using the differentialGeneTest function and designated as ordering genes. The ReduceDimension function was employed for dimensionality reduction and clustering, and trajectories were inferred using OrderCells with default settings. Gene expression dynamics along pseudotime were visualized using the plot_genes_in_pseudotime function.

## Cell communication analysis
Cellchat (v2.2.0) was employed to infer biologically important ligand-receptor interactions from scRNA-seq data. Following the creation of the CellChat object, analysis was conducted according to the official workflow, using the CellChatDB.human database. A weighted directed network was constructed by calculating the number of ligands and the strength of intercellular interactions, incorporating key ligands derived from interacting cell populations. Next, the number of intercellular communications, communication strength, and signaling pathway activities were visualized for cell populations in both control and PAH groups using CellChat ( ).

## Proteomics sequencing
Data-independent acquisition (DIA) was used to perform proteomic profiling on lung tissues from both PAH patients and healthy donors. Frozen specimens were ground in liquid nitrogen, lysed in 8 M urea with protease inhibitors, sonicated, and centrifuged to remove debris. The bicinchoninic acid assay was used to measure protein concentrations. Protein aliquots (100 µg) were reduced with dithiothreitol, alkylated with iodoacetamide, diluted with ammonium bicarbonate, and digested overnight with trypsin, followed by C18 desalting. Peptides (200 ng) were separated on a Vanquish Neo system and examined using an Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer operating in DIA mode. Using DIA-NN (v1.8.1), the raw files underwent a library-free search against the human UniProt proteome, maintaining a 1% false discovery rate. Proteins were considered differentially expressed if they had a fold change of at least 1.5 or no more than 0.67, with a P value less than 0.05. Z-score normalization was applied to the differential proteins. And volcano plots and clustering heatmaps were generated to visualize protein expression differences among samples.

## PAH animal model established
Male Sprague-Dawley rats (150 g) were purchased from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. and randomly assigned to receive a single intraperitoneal injection of monocrotaline (MCT; 60 mg/kg; HR10512, Heruibio, Fuzhou, China) mixed in vehicle (10% dimethyl sulfoxide) or the same volume of vehicle without any additives (control), as previously described ( ). Rats were maintained under the standard environment (a cycle of 12 hours light/12 hours dark, 22–25°C), and food and water were available without limits. At the study endpoint, rats were euthanized, and the main pulmonary arteries and lung tissues were collected for later analyses.
Inhalation of adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) was carried out as outlined in earlier methods ( ). Following anesthesia, the rat limbs were fixed, and a 3 mm midline incision was made in the neck. The surrounding tissues were bluntly dissected to expose the tracheal rings. A total of 50 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solution containing 3 × 1011 VG AAV9-control or AAV9-Smad7 was slowly injected through 3–4 tracheal rings. Rats were then mechanically ventilated for 10 minutes before the skin incision was sutured. The rats in the AAV control group received a 50 µL PBS injection into the trachea, while rats in the MCT and normal groups received 50 µL PBS via the trachea. One week later, rats in the MCT group, AAV9-control+MCT group, and AAV9-Smad7+MCT group were given an intraperitoneal injection of MCT at a dosage of 60 mg/kg to establish a PAH model. Rats in the normal group were given the same volume of PBS via an intraperitoneal injection. All rats were housed under the identical conditions for 30 days after injection. The design and construction of AAV9 were performed using standard methods with the assistance of GenePharma (Shanghai, China).
One week after MCT administration, rats in the MCT group received daily intraperitoneal PBS, rats in the MCT+vehicle group received daily intraperitoneal vehicle (2% dimethyl sulfoxide, 50% polyethylene glycol, 5% Tween 80, and 43% distilled water), and rats in the MCT+Fer-1 group received daily intraperitoneal injections of the ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1 (1 mg/kg; MedChemExpress, HY-100579). Because the phenotype was more robust in males, female rats were not included.

## Assessment of pressure in the right ventricle and its mass index
Rats received an intraperitoneal injection of heparin, followed by anesthesia with sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneally). A PE-50 catheter linked to a pressure transducer was used to measure right ventricular pressure (Techman, Chengdu, China). Pressure signals were recorded using an RM6240 multichannel physiological recorder system (Techman, China). Following right ventricular pressure measurement, physiological saline was carefully used to flush the pulmonary circulation through the right jugular vein, and the heart and lungs were swiftly taken out. The right ventricle (RV) was separated from the interventricular septum (VS), and then the weights of RV and the left ventricle plus VS (LV+VS) were recorded separately. The right ventricular mass index (RVMI) was determined by dividing the RV by the sum of LV and VS weights.

## Assessment of pulmonary vascular remodeling
A 4% paraformaldehyde solution was used to perfusion-fix the lung tissues, which were embedded in paraffin and sliced into 5 μm thick sections. To examine the morphology of pulmonary vessels, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was conducted. Morphometric analysis was conducted by a blinded investigator, who measured the thickness of the medial layer in small pulmonary arterioles (50–100 µm in external diameter). Following morphological assessment, the main pulmonary artery was carefully dissected under a surgical microscope for subsequent cellular analyses.

## Extraction and cultivation of primary pulmonary arterial ECs
The main pulmonary arteries were cut open along their length, flattened, and divided into 1 mm3 sections for explant culture. The culture flasks were inverted for an hour to help the tissue adhere. Tissue pieces were placed in dishes with 3–5 mL of complete pulmonary artery endothelial cells (PAECs) medium and incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2. After 4–6 hours, tissues were fully immersed in the endothelial cell culture medium (ECM, 1001, Sciencel) and cultured statically for 4–5 days. Every 3 days, the culture medium was changed. Once the cells had moved from the explants and achieved 75% - 90% confluence, the tissue fragments were taken out. When cultures reached approximately 90% confluence, cells were detached for passaging using 0.25% trypsin (25200056, Gibco, Waltham, Massachusetts). These ECs were used between passages 3 and 6.
For hypoxia treatment with serum deprivation, after replacing the medium with serum-free medium, the culture dishes were placed in a humidified sealed chamber, where a controlled hypoxic gas mixture (5% CO2 and 1% O2) was introduced and maintained. Cells in the hypoxic group were placed in a 37°C incubator for 48 hours, whereas those in the normoxic group were maintained in fresh medium with 5% CO2 and 21% O2 ( ). Following the instructions, rat recombinant TGF-β1 (TGF-β1, HY-P7117, MedChemExpress) was prepared over 48 hours. In the experiments, TGF-β1 was used at a concentration of 10 ng/mL, with the medium refreshed every 24 hours ( ).

## Cellular transfection of PAECs
Primary rat PAECs were used for transfection experiments to investigate the intrinsic regulatory role of Smad7. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, transfections were carried out using EndoFectin Max transfection reagent (Genecopoeia, Guangzhou, China; EF013-S). Smad7 small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) were purchased from GenePharma (Guangzhou, China). The siRNA sequences were as follows: 5′-GUGAAUCUUACGGGAAGAUTT-3′ and 5′-AUCUUCCCGUAAGAUUCACTT-3′. Briefly, a sterile, nuclease-free microcentrifuge tube was filled with 42.5 μL of buffer, then 75 nmol of siRNA and 15 μL of transfection reagent were added. The mixture was gently pipetted to ensure thorough mixing and then added directly to the cell culture medium. After transfection, cells were incubated for 48 hours before analysis of protein expression. For Smad7 overexpression, plasmids were obtained from GenePharma, with sequence information provided in Supplementary Table S2, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C802. The plasmids were mixed with the transfection reagent and added to the culture medium, and cells were harvested 48 hours after transfection for subsequent analyses ( ).

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction
Lung tissues were used to extract total RNA with TRIpure Reagent (Accurate Biology, China, AG21023). Employing the Hifair II First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit from YEASEN (11121ES60), 500 ng of total RNA was converted into cDNA. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction was conducted on a T100 Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, Hercules, California) using primers for Smad7, and the relative expression levels were determined using the 2^-ΔΔCt method with β-actin as the reference gene. Primers were synthesized by SunYa (the forward primer for β-actin: CGCGAGTACAACCTTCTTGC; the reverse primer for β-actin: CCTTCTGACCCATACCCACC; the forward primer for Smad7: AAGTGAGGAGCAAGATCGGC; the reverse primer for Smad7: CAGTGTGGCGGACTTGATGA).

## Immunofluorescence staining
Rat lung samples that were fixed in formalin and embedded in paraffin were deparaffinized and exposed to a 20-minute heat-based method for antigen retrieval using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid buffer at pH 8.0. To block endogenous peroxidases in tissues, a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution was applied for 25 minutes at room temperature, followed by a 30-minute block with 3% bovine serum albumin. Primary antibodies were used to incubate the sections overnight at 4°C, followed by a 1-hour incubation with secondary antibodies at 25°C. Images were acquired by Pannoramic Scanner software and calculations, and analysis were conducted using Slide Viewer (version 2.4) and ImageJ (v2.14.0). Antibodies included anti-Cd31 antibody (1:200, ab222783, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), anti-α-SMA antibody (1:200, GB111364, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), anti-Smad7 antibody (1:200, ab216428, Abcam), anti-4-HNE antibody (1:200, ab48506, Abcam), Anti-Gpx4 antibody (1:200, ab125066, Abcam), anti-pSmad3 (1:200, ab109532, Abcam).
For PAECs, cells were treated with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 minutes to fix them, treated with 0.1% Triton X-100 (HRD0141, Heruibio) to permeabilize, followed by a 30-minute block in phosphate-buffered saline with Tween-20 containing bovine serum albumin and glycine. Cells were left with primary antibodies for 8 hours at 4°C, then incubated with secondary antibodies in the dark for 1 hour at 25°C. Cells underwent treatment with 0.5 μg/mL Hoechst 33342 and were washed before imaging using LAS X software. Antibodies included anti-Cd31 antibody (1:200, ab222783, Abcam), anti-Smad7 antibody (1:200, ab216428, Abcam), anti-pSmad3 (1:200, ab109532, Abcam).

## Electron microscopy using transmission techniques
The lung tissues were collected right after the rats were anesthetized and then sliced into small 1 mm3 pieces. The samples were preserved using 2% glutaraldehyde at 4°C and subsequently washed in a 0.1 mol/L phosphate buffer with a pH of 7.4, exposed to 1% osmium tetroxide, and stained with 1% aqueous uranyl acetate. After being dehydrated using a sequence of ethanol solutions, the samples were encased in Epon 812, and ultrathin slices were mounted on copper grids. The sample sections were processed with uranyl acetate and alkaline lead citrate, then examined using an HT7800 transmission electron microscope (Hitachi, Japan).

## Western blot analysis
Radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) with a protease inhibitor cocktail was used to extract protein lysates from rat lung homogenates or PAECs. Protein levels were determined using a bicinchoninic acid assay. Equivalent protein quantities were resolved on 10% - 15% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis gels and then moved to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. Following blocking, the membranes were incubated at 4°C overnight with primary antibodies targeting Gpx4 (1:2000, ab125066, Abcam), Slc7a11 (1:2000, ab307601, Abcam), Smad7 (1:3000, ab216428, Abcam), Gapdh (1:10000, ABclonal, A19056), and β-actin (1:10,000, bs-4968R, Bioss). The membranes were then incubated with HRP-linked secondary antibodies (1:10,000, bs-0295G, Bioss) for 1 hour at 25°C. Signals were identified through enhanced chemiluminescence and quantified with Quantity One (Bio-Rad) and ImageJ. Band intensities were normalized to β-actin or Gapdh.

## SMR, Heterogeneity in Dependent Instruments (HEIDI) test, and colocalization analysis
The SMR method was used to assess possible causal links between ferroptosis-related molecular characteristics, such as gene expression and DNA methylation (exposures), and PAH (outcome). This approach combines summary data from GWAS and QTL research, employing SNPs as instrumental variables to determine causality. The analysis was performed using SMR software (version 1.3.1) ( ).
To distinguish genuine causal links from horizontal pleiotropy or confounding caused by linkage disequilibrium (LD), in which a single SNP independently influences both the molecular trait and PAH, the HEIDI test is used. Associations showing a HEIDI P value less than 0.01 were deemed likely due to pleiotropy or LD and were excluded from further analysis.
For the SMR analysis, the top associated cis-quantitative trait locus (cis-QTL) for each gene within a ±1 Mb window was selected, requiring a genome-wide significant association with the molecular trait (P < 5.0 × 10−8) ( , ). Two complementary SMR statistics were calculated: a standard SMR P value (P-SMR) based on the single strongest cis-QTL, and a multi-SNP SMR P value (P-SMR-multi), which incorporates multiple independent cis-QTLs within the region to provide a more robust assessment of causality. To ensure high confidence in the results, stringent filtering criteria were applied, and only associations meeting all three conditions were retained: both P-SMR and P-SMR-multi were below 0.05, whereas P-HEIDI was above 0.01. These high-confidence signals were subsequently subjected to colocalization analysis. Plots for the SMR analysis were generated in R using the CMplot package. The locus plot and effect plot algorithms were applied as previously described ( ).
To further strengthen causal inference, colocalization analysis was used to evaluate the posterior probability that a molecular QTL and PAH have a shared single common causal variant within a given genomic region. This approach aids in distinguishing true causal relationships from coincidental associations arising from LD. The analysis was conducted using the coloc package in R, evaluating five different hypotheses: H0 indicates no link with either trait; H1 suggests a link only with trait 1; H2 implies a link only with trait 2; H3 shows a link with both traits, but driven by different causal variants. A posterior probability for H4 greater than 0.5 (PPH4 > 0.5) was regarded as strong evidence supporting colocalization and a shared genetic etiology ( ). Genomic regions were defined as ±1 Mb zones with the lead QTL SNP at the center.

## Statistical analysis
The outcomes of all in vitro and in vivo experiments are shown as the mean ± SD. Statistical comparisons between two groups were conducted using an unpaired, two-tailed Student’s t test. To compare three or more groups, a one-way analysis of variance was applied, followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. All statistical analyses were carried out using GraphPad Prism 10.0.2, with a P value less than 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

## RESULTS

## Altered cellular composition suggests a relative increase in ECs in PAH patients
Ferroptosis has been implicated in the progression of PAH. However, its precise cellular localization and mechanistic underpinnings remain controversial. To further investigate the pathogenic role of ferroptosis-related genes in PAH, we analyzed scRNA-seq data from lung tissues of 12 healthy controls and 3 PAH patients. After quality control (Supplementary Figure S1A, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C803), doublets were removed, and potential batch effects were corrected using the Harmony algorithm (Supplementary Figure S1B, C, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C803), yielding expression matrices for 30,439 cells in the controls and 10,769 cells in the PAH patients. Unsupervised clustering identified 31 clusters (Supplementary Figure S1D, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C803). Established markers annotated 10 cell types: ECs (PECAM1, VWF, CDH5), epithelial cells (KRT19, DMKN), lymphatic ECs (PROX1, LYE1), smooth muscle cells (ACTA2, MYH11, TAGLN), fibroblasts (DCN, COL1A1), T and natural killer (T/NK) cells (CD3D, CD3E, NKG7), B cells (CD79A, CD79B), plasma cells (IGHG1, IGHG2), monocytes/macrophages (Mo/Mø) (CD68, CD163), and mast cells (TPSAB1, CPA3) (Fig. , A and C and Supplementary Figure S1E, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C803) ( , ). Comparison of cell composition between groups revealed a trend toward an increased proportion of ECs in PAH patients (Fig. B), suggesting that ECs may play an important role in PAH pathobiology. Given that ferroptosis has been characterized as an inflammatory mode of regulated cell death, we next calculated pro-inflammatory scores across all cell types. Pro-inflammatory scores were highest in ECs and fibroblasts (Fig. D). However, when comparing PAH patients with controls, a significant increase in the inflammatory score was observed only in ECs (Fig. E), whereas fibroblasts showed no significant intergroup difference (Fig. F). Cell death scores indicated the greatest propensity for cell death in ECs, with relatively elevated scores also observed in fibroblasts and monocytes/macrophages. Similarly, only ECs exhibited a significant increase in cell death score in PAH compared with controls (Fig. , H–J).
 

## Potential involvement of endothelial cell subpopulations in the development and progression of PAH
Endothelial dysfunction is a central feature in PAH progression and contributes substantially to its pathophysiology ( , ). To further explore the role of endothelial subpopulations in PAH, unsupervised clustering was performed, and four EC subpopulations were identified: aerocyte capillary ECs (aCAP), general capillary ECs (gCAP), arterial ECs (arterial EC), and venous ECs (venous EC) (Fig. A and Supplementary Figure S2A, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C804). aCAPs exhibited high expression of EDNRB and HPGD, gCAP expressed GPIHBP1 and APLNR, arterial ECs exhibited high expression of GJA5 and DKK2, Venous ECs expressed ACKR1 and NR2F2 (Fig B, Supplementary Figure S2B, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C804). In PAH patients, the proportions of gCAP and Venous ECs were increased, whereas those of aCAPs and arterial ECs were decreased compared with healthy controls (Fig. C). Gene set enrichment analysis using GO biological process gene sets indicated that aCAPs were enriched for antigen processing and presentation functions, gCAPs were enriched for endothelial cell proliferation-related processes, arterial ECs were enriched for inflammatory response pathways, and Venous ECs were enriched for fatty acid and nitric oxide biosynthetic processes (Fig. D). As ferroptosis has been described as an inflammatory mode of regulated cell death, addmodule analyses were applied to evaluate pro-inflammatory signatures across EC subpopulations. Inflammatory scores were higher in arterial ECs (Fig. E), particularly in PAH patients (Fig. H). Ferroptosis scores exhibited a similar pattern (Fig. , F and I). In contrast, pyroptosis and necroptosis scores did not differ significantly among the four EC subpopulations (Supplementary Figures S2C and S2D, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C804), suggesting that arterial ECs represent the most active subpopulation of ferroptosis. Conversely, proliferation scores indicated that gCAPs were the most proliferative subpopulation (Fig. G), particularly in PAH patients (Fig. J). This pattern is consistent with the observed increased proportion of gCAPs and reduction in arterial ECs in PAH and suggests that ferroptosis and proliferation occur in distinct EC subpopulations.
 

## Pseudotime trajectory and cellular communication analysis for ECs in PAH patients
To further explore potential state transitions among EC subpopulations in PAH, pseudotime trajectory analysis was performed. ECs were organized along a trajectory and were divided into two different differentiation trajectories at branching point 2 (Fig. A, S2E). Arterial ECs were predominantly enriched at the beginning of the trajectory, whereas aCAPs mainly occupied intermediate states. Venous ECs and gCAPs were primarily located at the end of fate 1 and fate 2, respectively, suggesting a gradual progression from arterial ECs toward gCAPs states (Fig. B). Notably, the inflammatory genes IL6 and TNFRSF1A exhibited expression dynamics similar to GJA5 (marker gene of arterial ECs), whereas the proliferation-associated genes VEGFA and FGF showed trajectories concordant with GPIHBP1 (marker gene of gCAPs) (Fig. C). These expression patterns are consistent with a transition from a relatively pro-inflammatory program toward a more proliferative program along the inferred trajectory. Accordingly, we speculate that inflammatory mediators released during ferroptosis of arterial ECs may contribute to the proliferative activation of gCAPs.
 To evaluate interactions among ECs, ligand-receptor information derived from scRNA-seq data was analyzed. Compared with healthy controls, PAH samples exhibited increased overall interaction numbers and strength (Fig. , D and E). Moreover, IL6 signaling and TGF-β signaling (associated with SMAD7) between arterial ECs and gCAPs were enhanced in PAH compared with controls, suggesting that IL6 and TGF-β pathways may be involved in coordinating EC death and proliferative programs (Fig. , F and G).

## SMAD7 was downregulated in arterial ECs from PAH patients
To identify key genes regulating ferroptosis in arterial ECs, we summarized the top five upregulated and downregulated genes across the four endothelial subpopulations. Notably, SMAD7, a negative regulator of TGF-β signaling, was markedly downregulated in arterial ECs (Fig. A). To further assess protein expression levels, proteomic profiling of lung tissues from healthy controls and PAH patients was performed. The demographic and primary clinical characteristics were presented in Supplementary Table S3, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C805. SMAD7 protein abundance was likewise decreased in PAH patients compared with healthy controls (Fig. , B–D).
 To identify ferroptosis-related genes with potential causal effects on PAH, we conducted a multiomics SMR analysis. Both eQTL- and mQTL-based SMR consistently highlighted SMAD7 as a candidate, suggesting that its expression may be influenced by DNA methylation and is causally linked to PAH susceptibility, with significant colocalization (PPH4 > 0.5) (Supplementary Tables S4, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C806 and S5, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C807). Manhattan plots highlighted these significant signals (Supplementary Figures S3A and B, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C808). SMR effect plots and locus plots indicated a negative regulatory association between SMAD7 and ferroptosis in PAH, consistent with our single-cell analysis (Supplementary Figures S3C, D, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C808 and S4A, B, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C809).

## Inhibiting ferroptosis attenuated pulmonary vascular remodeling and enhanced right ventricular function in MCT-PAH rats
To validate the aforementioned analysis and evaluate the pathophysiological relevance of ferroptosis, a MCT-PAH rat model was established. Notably, treatment with ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1, 1 mg/kg) exerted therapeutic effects, as evidenced by reduced right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) and RVMI, together with attenuation of pulmonary arterial remodeling (Fig. , A–E). In parallel, the ferroptosis-related markers Gpx4 and Slc7a11 expression levels were decreased in lung tissues from MCT-PAH rats (Fig. , F–H). Immunofluorescence staining further showed diminished Gpx4 expression in pulmonary arterial ECs in MCT-PAH rats, which was partially restored following Fer-1 treatment (Fig. , I and J). These findings suggested that ferroptosis might be involved in the pulmonary arterial ECs of PAH rats.
 

## Smad7 deficiency promoted TGF-β–Smad2/3 signaling in pulmonary arterial ECs of MCT-PAH rats
Based on the single-cell transcriptomic, proteomic, and SMR analysis results, we hypothesized that ferroptosis in PAH may be associated with the downregulation of Smad7 in pulmonary arterial ECs. To further define the vascular localization of Smad7 in PAH, we assessed Smad7 expression in lung tissues by immunofluorescence. Smad7 expression was reduced in pulmonary arterial ECs from MCT-PAH rats (Fig. , A and B). Similar results were observed by immunoblotting (Fig. , C and D). In addition, hypoxic exposure of PAECs in vitro resulted in decreased Smad7 expression, as demonstrated by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. , E and F).
 Studies have shown that Smad7 functions as a negative regulator of TGF-β signaling by competitively interacting with Smad2/3 ( , ). Moreover, our single-cell transcriptomic analysis of cellular communication revealed a significant upregulation of TGF-β signaling in PAECs from PAH patients. Considering the essential function of TGF-β signaling in PAH, immunofluorescence staining was conducted on lung tissues from MCT-PAH rats. Compared with the control group, phosphorylated Smad3 (p-Smad3) was markedly increased in the pulmonary arterial ECs of MCT-PAH rats, whereas Smad7 overexpression significantly attenuated this elevation (Fig. , A and B). Moreover, in vitro experiments demonstrated that hypoxia-induced p-Smad3 upregulation in PAECs, and TGF-β1 stimulation increased p-Smad3 expression under normoxia conditions. Notably, Smad7 knockdown further enhanced TGF-β1-induced p-Smad3 expression (Fig. , C and D). The overall study design and experimental workflow are schematically illustrated in Supplementary Figure S5, http://links.lww.com/SHK/C847. Taken together, these results suggest that Smad7 inhibits TGF-β1 from activating downstream signaling by competitively blocking Smad2/3, thereby contributing to the regulation of PAH.
 

## Smad7 overexpression attenuated ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs and pulmonary vascular remodeling in MCT-PAH rats
Based on the scRNA-seq, proteomic, and SMR results, we hypothesized that reduced expression of Smad7 may play a pathophysiological role in PAH. Therefore, we further investigated the impact of Smad7 delivery on pulmonary vascular remodeling in MCT-PAH rats. AAV9 particles were administered via intratracheal instillation to achieve Smad7 overexpression in vivo. Quantitative polymerase chain reaction confirmed that AAV9-Smad7 significantly increased Smad7 expression in lung tissues from MCT-PAH rats (Supplementary Figure S2F, https://links.lww.com/SHK/C804). Compared with the MCT group, Smad7 overexpression attenuated the RVSP (Fig. , A and B), with a similar trend observed for RVMI (Fig. C). Moreover, AAV9-Smad7 markedly alleviated pulmonary vascular remodeling in MCT-induced PAH rats (Fig. , D and E).
 We next explored the effects of Smad7 restoration on ferroptosis in MCT-PAH rats. Compared with the MCT group, immunoblotting showed that the expression of the ferroptosis markers Gpx4 and Slc7a11 was increased in lung tissues from MCT-PAH rats after AAV9-Smad7 injection (Fig. , F–H). In parallel, transmission electron microscopy revealed that pulmonary arterial ECs in the MCT group exhibited mitochondrial alterations characterized by reduced cristae and mitochondrial swelling, whereas these changes were alleviated by AAV9-Smad7 (Fig. I). Immunofluorescence staining of lung tissue demonstrated that AAV9-Smad7 reduced the lipid peroxidation marker 4-HNE in pulmonary arterial ECs of PAH rats (Fig. , J and K). In a hypoxia-induced PAEC model in vitro, immunoblotting showed that Smad7 overexpression upregulated Gpx4 and Slc7a11 in PAECs (Fig. , L–N). Taken together, these results suggest that Smad7 may influence pulmonary vascular remodeling in PAH, at least in part, by modulating ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs.

## DISCUSSION
In the present study, the results suggest that downregulation of Smad7 may contribute to the activation of ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs by promoting TGF-β1–Smad2/3 signaling, thereby promoting pulmonary vascular remodeling in PAH. Through integrated multiomics analyses, including scRNA-seq, proteomics, and SMR, SMAD7 downregulation in PAECs from PAH patients was identified as a key regulatory event associated with ferroptosis. This mechanism may further promote the proliferation of gCAPs, which may partially explain the coexistence of endothelial cell death and proliferation observed in PAH. Furthermore, in vivo overexpression of Smad7 alleviated ferroptosis in PAECs and attenuated pulmonary vascular remodeling in MCT-PAH rats, with consistent effects observed in hypoxic PAEC models. Therefore, we speculated that Smad7 may serve as a potential regulator of ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs in PAH.
Previous studies have reported that 43 to 63 percent of PAH patients exhibit iron insufficiency, which is linked to decreased exercise ability and higher mortality rates, indicating that improper iron metabolism may play an important role in PAH pathogenesis ( – ). Furthermore, ferroptosis may promote PAH through metabolic and inflammatory mechanisms within the pulmonary vascular system ( ). However, the specific roles of ferroptosis-related genes in PAH remain poorly understood. In this study, scRNA-seq data from healthy controls and PAH patients were analyzed. The results demonstrated an increased proportion of ECs in PAH patients, accompanied by a marked tendency toward pro-inflammatory responses and cell death. This observation is consistent with previous studies that reported ferroptosis of pulmonary arterial ECs in a PAH rat model, accompanied by enhanced inflammatory responses ( , ). Therefore, ECs were subdivided into distinct subpopulations to further explore the potential effector cells of ferroptosis. Notably, among the four EC subpopulations, arterial ECs exhibited a greater propensity for ferroptosis, whereas gCAPs showed a stronger proliferative tendency. Previous studies have suggested that pathological conditions such as hypoxia, inflammation, mechanical stress, and oxidative stress induce abnormal endothelial apoptosis, which disrupts the physiological balance between endothelial apoptosis and proliferation, thereby leading to compensatory excessive proliferation of the remaining ECs ( , ). However, the present study proposes an alternative possibility that the simultaneous occurrence of cell death and proliferation in ECs in PAH may be related to different EC subpopulations. Pseudotime analysis showed that arterial ECs occupy an early stage of the differentiation trajectory and gradually transition toward gCAPs, accompanied by increased expression of IL6 and TNFRSF1A. Meanwhile, cell communication analysis indicated that in PAH patients, gCAPs and arterial ECs had elevated immune-inflammatory signals, particularly IL6 signaling. This suggests that, as an inflammatory mode of cell death, the inflammatory factors released from arterial ECs undergoing ferroptosis may further promote the proliferative transition of gCAPs. Therefore, in contrast to the classical model suggesting that EC apoptosis is followed by EC proliferation in PAH, our current findings suggest that these processes may occur concurrently in different EC subpopulations and are associated with ferroptosis.
In our study, integrated single-cell transcriptomics, proteomics, and SMR analyses consistently showed that SMAD7 was markedly downregulated in arterial ECs. SMR and colocalization analyses were used to leverage genetic variation to evaluate potential causal links between gene expression/DNA methylation and PAH risk. However, these approaches provide statistical evidence of association and are limited by the use of summary-level data, the assumption of a single causal variant, and the lack of cell-type-specific resolution. Therefore, integrating SMR with single-cell transcriptomic and proteomic data helps overcome these limitations by adding cell type specificity, enabling more robust prioritization of biologically relevant candidate regulators. Collectively, this multi-omics framework supports SMAD7 as a key endothelial regulator potentially involved in ferroptosis and pulmonary vascular remodeling in PAH.
Mechanistically, SMAD7 is a negative regulator of TGF-β signaling and has been reported to inhibit hepcidin production through a negative-feedback loop ( ). It has also been identified in The Cancer Genome Atlas as part of a predictive ferroptosis-related signature ( ). Additionally, we observed downregulation of Smad7 in both MCT-PAH rat pulmonary arterial ECs and hypoxia-induced PAECs, concomitant with ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs. Multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification analysis has also identified SMAD7 as a gene associated with mutations related to PAH ( ). Our experiments have shown that in PAECs, Smad7 can competitively bind Smad2/3, thereby inhibiting their phosphorylation. Moreover, we found that Smad7 overexpression significantly attenuated ferroptosis in pulmonary arterial ECs and pulmonary vascular remodeling in MCT-PAH rats. Therefore, these findings suggest that, as an inhibitory regulatory factor, Smad7 downregulation may increase the susceptibility of pulmonary arterial ECs to ferroptotic injury, thereby promoting endothelial dysfunction and pulmonary vascular remodeling in PAH. Nevertheless, our work provides an initial characterization of SMAD7 in the context of ferroptosis in PAH, and the downstream mechanisms remain to be defined. Future studies using endothelial-specific SMAD7 knockout models, together with systematic assessments of pulmonary vascular remodeling and hemodynamic changes, will be essential to further clarify the pathological significance of the TGF-β/SMAD7–ferroptosis axis in PAH.
Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size of PAH patients included in the single-cell transcriptomic analysis was relatively small. Second, the concurrent occurrence of ferroptosis and proliferation in different cell subpopulations serves primarily as a basis for hypothesis generation and requires further experimental validation. Finally, this study included only male rodents, which is a limitation given established sex-specific differences in PAH. Although this design reduces variability related to hormonal fluctuations, it limits the applicability of our findings to females.

## CONCLUSIONS
Through an integrated multiomics analysis combined with preliminary validation in animal and cellular models, this study provides further insight into the potential role of endothelial cell ferroptosis in PAH associated pulmonary vascular remodeling. Notably, our findings suggest that Smad7 may function as a negative regulator of endothelial ferroptosis by inhibiting the activation of TGF-β–Smad2/3 signaling, thereby participating in the modulation of ferroptosis in PAH. These results offer new mechanistic perspectives and identify potential therapeutic targets for ferroptosis-oriented interventions in PAH.
