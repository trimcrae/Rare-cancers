## Abstract
 To determine the role of inflammation-related proteins
in predicting
asthma severity and outcome, 92 inflammation-related proteins were
measured in the asthmatic serum using Olink analysis. Different bioinformatics
algorithms were developed to cross analyze with the single-cell or
transcriptome data sets from the Gene Expression Omnibus database
to explore the role of IL18R1 and related genes in asthma and idiopathic
pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Olink identified 52 differentially expressed
proteins in asthma. They were strongly linked to the cytokine–cytokine
receptor interaction, TNF, and NF-κB signaling pathway. Seven
proteins were found in both single-cell RNA and Olink analyses. Among
them, IL18R1 was predominantly expressed in mast cells, and the results
suggested enhanced communication between mast cells and CD 8+ T cells. IL18R1 was upregulated in serum and induced sputum and
bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of patients with uncontrolled or severe
asthma. IL18R1 was positively correlated with TNFSF1 and OSM and S100A12.
The diagnostic efficacy of these serum IL18R1-related molecules for
asthma ranged from 0.839 to 0.921. Moreover, high levels of IL18R1,
TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12 were significantly associated with shorter
survival times and worse lung function. IL18R1-related molecules may
serve as biomarkers for monitoring uncontrolled or severe asthma and
as prognostic markers for IPF.
Keywords: asthma, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, IL18R1, biomarker, prognosis

## Introduction
Asthma is a chronic respiratory inflammatory
disease that affects
more than 300 million people worldwide. Asthma symptoms are caused by airway inflammation, which triggers
airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR), mucus production, and airway remodeling. Up to 10% of adults and 2.5% of children have
severe asthma with persistent symptoms or frequent exacerbations despite
adequate treatment with high-dose inhaled glucocorticoids, long-acting
β2 agonists, or long-acting muscarinic antagonists. , , Severe asthma with repeated episodes
of allergen exposure and dysregulated mucosal surface inflammation
can lead to collagen deposition, increased numbers of myofibroblasts,
angiogenesis, and ultimately, subepithelial fibrosis. Mast cells (MCs) are the effector cells in the pathophysiology
of allergy. Emerging evidence indicates a role for IgE-independent
MC activation in the late-stage asthmatic response as well as in nonallergic
airway diseases including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD),
and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Tissue remodeling and fibrosis are major features of many pulmonary
diseases, including asthma and IPF. The
prognosis of patients with IPF is poor, with a 5 year survival rate
of less than 40%. , Therefore, it is important to
develop new biomarkers that can accurately predict disease outcome
(predictive biomarkers) and early markers of treatment responses (monitoring
biomarkers) for severe asthma and to determine whether these markers
can allow for early identification of patients with IPF with a poor
prognosis.
Serum inflammation-related proteins are associated
with asthma
development and progression. Elevated IL-6 levels have been reported
in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), serum, and induced sputum
of asthmatic patients and are associated with asthma severity, impaired
lung function, and airway remodeling response. IL-18 is a proinflammatory factor that promotes the production
of T helper (h)2 cytokines (e.g., IL-4, IL-5, IL-9, and IL-13) and
is closely associated with Th2 cell differentiation, IgE production,
and AHR formation. – Recent work identified an IL-18 association with
the pathogenesis of asthma, wherein increased IL-18 and IL-18R expression
was found in the serum and lung tissue of patients with asthma, , suggesting that IL-18 may participate in the development of asthma.
In addition, sustained stimulation by these inflammatory factors leads
to asthmatic airway remodeling, bronchial epithelial damage, and secretion
of growth factors by activated epithelial cells, including IL-13,
IL-1β, and TGF-β, all of which activate the underlying
mesenchymal cells, ultimately leading to subepithelial fibrosis, basement
membrane thickening, and smooth muscle hyperplasia. The recruitment of fibroblasts and release of excess extracellular
matrix (ECM) components, such as fibronectin and proteoglycans, subsequently
lead to pulmonary fibrosis. , Thus, inflammatory
activation plays an important role in the pathogenesis of asthma and
IPF. However, the correlation between inflammation-related proteins
and asthma and the prognostic value of these specific biomarkers remain
unclear.
In this study, Olink proteomics, which has shown excellent
reproducibility
and stability in the detection of proteins in plasma samples, was
used to explore new biomarkers for asthma, generate insights into
the pathogenesis of asthma, and guide asthma treatment. We used the
Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to assess the relationship
between these signature molecules, lung function, and prognosis in
patients with IPF.

## Materials and Methods

## Analysis of Inflammation-Related Biomarkers in Asthmatic Patients
Using Olink Multiplex Arrays
This study was conducted in
compliance with the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki and was approved
by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Putian
University (approval no. [2020]17). The inclusion criteria for poorly
controlled asthma patients (n = 24) refer to previous
studies. Participants without asthma
(n = 29) were included as controls. Approximately
2 mL of blood per patient was collected into serum separation tubes,
and the serum was then extracted by centrifugation at 1500 rpm for
10 min and frozen in an −80 °C refrigerator until assayed.
The serum samples were analyzed using Olink multiplex arrays according
to the manufacturer’s instructions, allowing analysis of 92
inflammation-associated proteins in a single test. Briefly, each inflammation-related
protein was recognized by antibodies and coupled with its specific
complementary oligonucleotide barcode, which was subsequently detected
and quantified using PCR. The final assay results are presented as
normalized protein expression values and log2-transformed.

## Functional Enrichment Analyses for Differentially Expressed
Proteins for the Olink Data
Differentially expressed proteins
in the Olink data were analyzed using the limma package with the following
criteria: p < 0.05. A heat map and volcano plot
were drawn using the ggplot package. , Functional
enrichment analyses, including gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia
of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses, were performed using
ggplot2. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks of differentially
expressed proteins were constructed and visualized using STRING and
Cytoscape (version 3.9.1). , 
The Spearman
correlation of the differentially expressed proteins was calculated,
and a heat map of the correlation was plotted using ggplot.

## Analysis of scRNA-seq Data
The single-cell RNA (scRNA)-seq
data of GSE164015 were analyzed using R package “Seurat”
(4.0.3). Cells with <200 genes, >2500 feature RNAs, or >20%
mitochondrial
genes were excluded. The “LogNormalize” method was used
to normalize gene expression. After normalization, 2000 highly variable
genes (HVGs) were obtained. Subsequently, significant principal components
were then identified using PCA, and the distribution of P-values was plotted using the JackStraw and ScoreJackStraw functions.
The FindClusters function was used to classify the cells into 26 principal
clusters with a resolution of 0.25. Based on cluster-specific cell
markers, nine clusters were identified and presented using uniform
manifold approximation and projection (UMAP), and irGSEA (https://github.com/chuiqin/irGSEA) was used to assess whether the gene sets were enriched in a particular
cell subpopulation. CellChat (https://htmlpreview.github.io/github.com/sqjin/CellChat/blob/master/tutorial/CellChat-vignette.html) was used to assess cellular communication between the cell subpopulations.

## Analysis of IL18R1 Expression in Patients with Asthma
Four data sets (GSE137268, GSE41863, GSE45111, and GSE76262) and
the clinical features of induced sputum samples were downloaded and
analyzed. To investigate the differential expression of IL18R1 between
asthma patients and controls across different types of asthma, we
used the limma function to analyze the data. Moreover, the ssGSEA
score was used to explore immune infiltration in the GSE76262 data
set samples, which was calculated according to the marker genes of
23 types of immune cells. The Spearman
correlation between IL18R1 and the MC score was also evaluated.

## IL18R1-Related Coexpression Genes and Differentially Expressed
Genes Analysis
Coexpression of genes correlated with IL18R1
expression in the GSE76262 microarray data of patients with severe
asthma was investigated. The Pearson correlation coefficient was calculated,
and a volcano plot of the coexpressed genes was generated using ggplot.
Patients with severe asthma from the GSE76262 data set were divided
into two groups (IL18R1low and IL18R1high) based
on the median value of IL18R1 mRNA. The limma package in R was used
to analyze and plot a volcano map of the DEGs between the IL18R1low and IL18R1high. IL18R1-related KEGG enrichment
analysis was performed using WebGestalt (http://www.webgestalt.org/) for gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). DrawVenn (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/) was used to explore the overlapping genes between coexpressed genes,
DEGs, and the 52 Olink inflammation-related circulating biomarkers.
The diagnostic efficiency of overlapping genes was evaluated according
to the area under the curve (AUC) of the Olink data set. The association
of IL18R1 with well-known asthma treatment-related genes (FKBP5 and
IL13RA1) was further analyzed using the GSE76262 data set.

## Prognostic and Lung Function Analysis
Kaplan–Meier
analysis and the log-rank test of L18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12
was used to compare survival among different groups using the R package
for Kaplan–Meier survival from the GSE70866 data set (BAL gene
expression profile from 212 IPF patients and 20 healthy donors). IPF
patients were divided into high- and low-expression groups according
to the optimal cutoff value of gene expression. The correlations between
L18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12 and lung function, including the predicted
FVC %, DLCO %, and FEV1%, in the GSE47460 data set (lung tissue gene
expression profile from 582 subjects including 254 interstitial lung
disease, 220 COPD, and 108 controls) were also calculated.

## Results

## Analysis of Olink Inflammation-Related Biomarkers in Asthma
Ninety-two inflammation-related proteins were evaluated using the
Olink analysis in asthmatic and nonasthmatic subjects. A total of
52 inflammation-related proteins were differentially expressed between
the asthma and control groups, of which 50 were upregulated and 2
were downregulated in the asthma group ( A). A heat map of these differentially expressed
proteins is shown in B, and a box plot comparing the expression levels of the 52
inflammation-related proteins between the asthma and control groups
is shown in C,D. Among them, IL-18 pathways, including IL-18, IL18R1, EN-RAGE
(S100A12), and OSM, and TNFSF14, were markedly upregulated in the
asthma group.
 

## Functional and Pathway Enrichment Analyses for Differentially
Expressed Inflammation-Related Biomarkers
To further explore
the potential function and pathway enrichment of differentially expressed
proteins, we performed GO and KEGG analyses . GO enrichment analysis
for biological processes (BP) indicated that these differential proteins
were associated with inflammatory response, cytokine-mediated signaling
pathway, and neutrophil chemotaxis ( A). KEGG pathway analysis showed that the
differential proteins were strongly linked to the cytokine–cytokine
receptor interaction, the TNF signaling pathway, and the NF-κB
signaling pathway ( B). Additionally, PPI networks were used to explore the interactions
among the differentially expressed inflammation-related biomarkers.
As shown in C, NF-κB signaling pathways, such as TNF and IL-18, had high
scores in the PPI network, suggesting that these molecules play important
roles in asthma. Critically, IL-18, an upstream activator of NF-κB,
and its receptor IL-18R1 are closely associated with severe asthma. Moreover, S100A12, HGF, OSM, TNFSF14, MCP-3,
and AXIN1 were positively correlated with IL18-R1 expression ( D), suggesting that
these molecules may be closely associated with severe asthma.
 

## Inflammation-Related Biomarkers in Single-Cell RNA Sequencing
Data from Asthma Patients
To explore the pathogenic mechanisms
of inflammatory molecules in asthma, an scRNA sequencing data set
(GSE164015) of bronchial cells from eight human BALF samples who had
asthma was analyzed for normalization, clustering, and high-variance
gene screening. The top 10 high-variance genes are identified in A. Nine distinct
cell clusters were assigned to cell lineage marker genes and visualized
using UMAP analysis ( B). A total of 1939 differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
from each cluster were obtained, and intersections were taken with
the Olink differentially expressed inflammation-related biomarkers.
Seven inflammation-related biomarkers, including IL18 and IL18R1 were
identified in both the scRNAs and the Olink data ( C). The expression of these
seven inflammation-related biomarkers in the scRNA clusters is shown
in D. Notably,
IL18 and IL18R1 were predominantly expressed in MCs, which are the
main effector cells in asthma development ( E,F). Moreover, we also found activation
of multiple signaling pathways in MCs, such as MYC-targets, PI3K-AKT-mTOR
signaling, and TNFA-signaling-via-NF-κB ( G). To explore the cell–cell interaction
network in the BALF of asthma patients, the CellChatDB package was
used to analyze cell–cell communication in different cell types.
We observed enhanced communication between MCs and CD 8+ T cells ( H).
 

## IL18R1 Expression in Patients with Asthma
Consistent
with our results, previous studies also demonstrated that IL18R1 is
upregulated in patients with asthma. However,
the role of IL18R1 in different asthma types is not well understood.
To explore the role of IL18R1 in asthma, four data sets were downloaded
from the GEO database. The mRNA level of IL18R1 was significantly
upregulated in the induced sputum of patients with asthma compared
to that in healthy controls ( A,G,J). Moreover, IL18R1 expression was slightly increased
in uncontrolled or moderate asthma ( B,F,K) and significantly increased in severe
asthma compared to healthy controls ( B,F,K). We also explored the relationship
between IL18R1 expression and airway inflammatory subtypes, which
correlated closely with the response to inhaled glucocorticoid therapy. We did not find any differences in IL18R1 expression
across the airway inflammatory infiltration subtypes, even after adjusting
for asthma severity ( C–E,H,I). The scRNA results showed that IL18R1 was
predominantly expressed in MCs. We found a significant increase in
MCs in asthmatic sputum and further confirmed a positive correlation
with the expression of IL18R1 ( L,M).
 

## Functional Enrichment Analysis of IL18R1-Related Genes in Asthma
To further investigate the mechanism of IL18R1 in severe asthma,
DEGs between the IL18R1high and IL18R1low groups
were compared in severe asthma. As shown in A, 1442 DEGs were identified (P < 0.05, |log2 FC| ≥ 0.5), of which 451 were upregulated
and 991 were downregulated in the IL18R1high group. To
further investigate the pathways altered in severe asthma between
the IL18R1high and IL18R1low groups, GSEA was
performed using WebGestalt (http://www.webgestalt.org/). Natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity,
the TNF signaling pathway, and the C-type lectin receptor signaling
pathway were activated in the gene sets, further suggesting that the
TNF signaling pathway involving IL18R1 plays an important role in
the pathogenesis of severe asthma ( B,C). Next, the coexpressed genes in conjunction
with IL18R1 in the GSE76262 data set were investigated. The results
show that 4108 coexpressed genes were highly correlated with IL18R1
expression in severe asthma (P < 0.05, |cor.|
≥ 0.3) ( D). We also compared the 1442 DEGs of IL18R1 and 4108 coexpressed
genes with the 52 Olink inflammation-related circulating biomarkers
by DrawVenn diagram, and we identified four overlapping genes associated
with the IL18R1 signaling pathway ( E). IL18R1 showed a strong positive correlation
with TNFSF1 and the OSM and a slightly positive correlation with S100A12,
which was strongly positively correlated with the OSM ( F). We also explored the diagnostic
value of these four biomarkers and IL18R1 in the serum of patients
with asthma. The results showed that IL18R1, IL18, TNFSF1, OSM, and
S100A12 levels were significantly higher in the asthma group, with
AUCs ranging from 0.839 to 0.921 ( G). The association of IL18R1 with some well-known
asthma treatment-related genes was further analyzed, and the results
show that IL18R1 was positively associated with FKBP5, the expression
of which was associated with high levels of corticosteroid use, and
IL13RA1, a lebrikizumab receptor that blocks IL-13 signaling ( H,I).
 

## Prognostic and Lung Function Effects of IL18R1 as a Characteristic
Asthma Molecule in IPF
A previous study indicated that asthmatic
airway remodeling, bronchial epithelial damage, and the secretion
of growth factors by activated epithelial cells ultimately lead to
IPF. However, prognostic and lung function
effects of IL18R1 signaling (IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12) as
a characteristic asthma biomarker in IPF are still unclear. K–M
survival analysis was performed for IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12
in the BALF of patients with IPF from the GSE70866 data set. Patients
with IPF were divided into high- and low-expression groups according
to the optimal cutoff value of gene expression. The results show that
patients with high expression of IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12
had significantly shorter survival times than those with low expression
( A–C).
Moreover, IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12 were significantly negatively
correlated with lung function indicators including the predicted FVC
%, DLCO %, and FEV1% in the lung tissue gene expression profiling
data set GSE47460 ( D).
 

## Discussion
The identification of clinically available
biomarkers, such as
serum markers, to provide an early indication of asthma control will
greatly facilitate the development of therapies for severe asthma,
reduce recurrent acute lung injury resulting from asthma exacerbations
leading to IPF, and may be important for the early identification
of patients with IPF who have poor prognosis. In this study, we found
that IL18R1 was predominantly expressed in MCs according to scRNA-seq
and was upregulated in serum and induced in the sputum of patients
with uncontrolled or severe asthma. Based on proteomic and transcriptome
analyses, it was not associated with airway inflammatory infiltration
subtypes. The reason maybe that IL18R1 was not enriched in eosinophils
and neutrophils. Moreover, we found that IL18R1 and its coexpressed
genes (IL18, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12) are not only of high diagnostic
value in asthma but are also closely associated with lung function
and poor prognosis in IPF.
Fifty-two inflammation-related proteins
were differentially expressed
in serum between the asthma and control groups using the Olink inflammation
panel assay. Several of these proteins were strongly associated with
asthma progression and were reported to be significantly altered in
serum. , Among them, IL-6, a biomarker of systemic
inflammation, was proved to be elevated in the serum and airways of
asthmatic patients. , The IL-6 family cytokines has
recently been found to be increased in the plasma of patients with
severe asthma associated with obesity and blood neutrophilia, possibly
originated from increased production by inflammatory macrophages in
the adipose tissue of obese patients. , OSM induced
by bacterial dysregulation triggers asthma-related symptoms, including
airway inflammation and mucus secretion. Blocking OSM with antibodies
reduces the symptoms associated with severe asthma in mice after exposure
to bacterial stimuli. OSM enhanced MC
growth through fibroblast-dependent pathway in mice. Our results indicated that the OSM had a role in T-cell-mediated
inflammatory processes in asthma and MCs were also involved. MMP10
is overexpressed in bronchial biopsies and serum from asthmatic subjects
with eosinophilic airway inflammation and is associated with fatal
asthma attacks in children. , , NF-κB signaling pathway is one central pathway involved in
airway remodeling processes in IPF. 
We explored the potential functional and pathway enrichment for
differentially expressed proteins and found that they were strongly
linked to the TNF and NF-κB signaling pathways, which are master
regulators of inflammatory responses associated with asthma progression. We further explored the pathogenic mechanisms
of these differential inflammation-related proteins in asthma using
scRNA sequencing and found that IL18R1 was predominantly expressed
in MCs, which are closely associated with asthma severity, suggesting that the IL18R1 signaling pathway
may be involved in the underlying pathophysiological processes of
asthma in MCs. Consistent with our results, IL-18 was found to increase
the number of IL-18R + MCs in the BALF of OVA-sensitized mice. IL-18/IL-18R1 was proved to promote circulating
fibrocyte differentiation. Moreover,
we found that IL18R1 was up-regulated in the induced sputum of patients
with uncontrolled or severe asthma. Previous studies have confirmed
that IL18R1 is highly expressed in the bronchial epithelium of patients
with severe asthma and that NF-κB and AP-1 are involved in IL18R1
receptor downstream signaling. Our results
further highlight the IL18R1 axis as a potential key inflammatory
pathway associated with severe asthma, which is significantly elevated
in the serum and sputum of patients with asthma and may serve as a
new biomarker. The functions and mechanisms of IL18R1 in severe asthma
were explored by using enrichment analyses. As expected, the current
GSEA analysis indicated that the function of IL18R1 was primarily
related to the activation of the TNF signaling pathway. The Venn diagram
further identified three of the Olink differential proteins (TNFSF1,
OSM, and S100A12) that are closely related to the IL18R1 signaling
pathway and that these molecules have high diagnostic efficacies for
asthma. S100A12 has been previously shown to cause degranulation of
mucosal and tissue MCs and amplify IgE-mediated responses that may
exacerbate allergic inflammation and asthma. How these molecules work together in the underlying pathophysiological
processes in asthma needs to be further explored.
Repeated airway
epithelial damage, chronic airway inflammation,
epithelial-mesenchymal transformation, and airway remodeling play
important roles in the pathology of asthma and IPF, suggesting similarities
between the two diseases. A previous study found that IL18R1 was expressed
in bronchoalveolar epithelial and alveolar macrophages from control
subjects and was strongly expressed in mesenchymal cells from patients
with IPF. S100A12 is mainly expressed
in monocytes and its expression is high in both the blood and BALF
of patients with IPF. , Additionally, S100A12 provokes
MC activation, indicating that these key asthma molecules play an
important role in the IPF progression. Here, Kaplan–Meier analysis
of IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12 in the BALF of patients with IPF
as characteristic asthma molecules for survival was performed using
the GSE70866 data set. Patients with high expression of IL18R1, TNFSF1,
OSM, and S100A12 had survival times significantly shorter than those
with low expression levels. Moreover, IL18R1, TNFSF1, OSM, and S100A12
were significantly negatively correlated with lung function, including
the predicted FVC %, DLCO %, and FEV1%, in the lung tissue GSE47460
data set. Thus, IL18R1-related molecules may be potential biomarkers
for predicting severe asthma and lung fibrosis.

## Conclusions
IL18R1 was predominantly expressed in MCs,
which suggested an enhanced
communication between MCs and CD 8+ T cells. IL18R1-related
molecules were upregulated in serum, induced sputum, and BALF of patients
with uncontrolled or severe asthma. These molecules exhibited high
diagnostic efficacy in asthma and were significantly associated with
shorter survival times and worse lung function.
