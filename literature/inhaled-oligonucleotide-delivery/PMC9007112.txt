## Abstract
 [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– inorganic-organic hybrid
nanoparticles (IOH-NPs) are novel saline antiviral nanocarriers with
foscarnet (FCN) as a drug anion. FCN as a pyrophosphate analogue serves
as a prototype of a viral DNA polymerase inhibitor. FCN is used for
the treatment of herpesvirus infections, including the drug-resistant
cytomegalovirus (CMV) and herpes simplex viruses, HSV-1 and HSV-2.
The novel [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs are characterized by aqueous synthesis, small size (20–30
nm), low material complexity, high biocompatibility, and high drug
load (up to 44 wt % FCN per nanoparticle). The antiviral activity
of the FCN-type IOH-NPs is probed for the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV).
Moreover, the uptake of FCN-type IOH-NPs into vesicles, cytoplasm,
and nuclei of nonphagocytic lung epithelial cells is evaluated. As
a result, a promising antiviral activity of the FCN-type IOH-NPs that
significantly outperforms freely dissolved FCN at the level of clinical
formulations is observed, encouraging a future use of FCN-type IOH-NPs
for the delivery of antivirals against respiratory viruses.
Keywords: antiviral, foscarnet, cytomegalovirus, nanocarrier, inorganic-organic hybrid nanoparticles

## Introduction
The application of nanocarriers for therapy and diagnostics started
in the late 1960s with the pioneering work by Speiser et al. , Since then, nanocarriers have been evaluated with regard to various
biomedical purposes, especially including cancer therapy, bacterial
treatment, and multimodal imaging. − After the first successful
attempts, considerable advances were made to transfer academic results
into clinical applications. Especially,
chemotherapeutic nanocarriers are among the first of still a small
number of nanocarrier formulations that have already received clinical
approval (e.g., non-PEGylated liposomal doxorubicin, PEGylated liposomal
doxorubicin, PEGylated liposomal irinotecan, albumin-bound paclitaxel). , The success of nanocarriers was fostered by a number of specific
advantages over conventional therapeutic approaches with freely dissolved
drugs. These include: (i) the reduction of undesired biomolecule interactions
on their way to the site of action causing side effects, (ii) the
different cellular uptake of nanoparticles and freely dissolved drugs,
which may result in higher drug concentrations, (iii) the various
options for targeting (e.g., functionalization with antibodies), or
(iv) the monitoring of nanocarriers via different imaging techniques
(e.g., optical imaging, magnetic resonance imaging, photoacoustic
imaging, radio-labeling). − 
Despite different nanocarrier-based therapy concepts and various
promising materials for tumor and bacterial treatment, a therapeutic
treatment of viral infections has rarely been addressed until now.
In contrast to tumor or bacterial cells, viruses are characterized
by a significantly smaller size (20–400 nm) and a host-based
replication mechanism, making their detection and treatment more challenging. , Most of the clinical antivirals inhibit virus replication inside
the infected cell by interacting with viral factors and enzymes, very
often the viral polymerase. This is typically achieved by the systemic
application of small antiviral molecules that are cell-permeable. − In principle, nanocarriers allow targeted intracellular delivery
of antiviral drugs after phagocytic/phenocytic cellular uptake, which
can result in significantly higher intracellular drug concentrations
than achievable with freely dissolved drug molecules. , Moreover, nanocarriers loaded with antivirals can be dissolved via
the normal cellular metabolism, so that high drug concentrations are
slowly released inside of the cells. Current antiviral nanocarrier
concepts include liposomal, lipid, polymeric, CNT-based, or dendrimer-based
delivery systems. − Among other limitations, the drug load of these systems is often
low with typically less than 5 wt % drug per nanocarrier.
Foscarnet
(FCN) is generally a good candidate to probe the efficacy
of novel nanocarrier concepts for the delivery of antiviral drugs.
Its mechanism of action is based on blocking the pyrophosphate-binding
sites of the viral DNA polymerase. Hence, FCN serves as an inhibitor
for DNA-chain elongation. FCN is clinically
used to treat infections with herpesviridae, including herpes simplex
viruses, HSV-1 and HSV-2, varicella-zoster, or cytomegaloviruses (CMV).
Aiming at nanocarriers, FCN has been only encapsulated in chitosan
or liposomal particles with sizes of 200–300 nm until now,
which did not result in a higher antiviral activity than an administration
of freely dissolved foscarnet. , As a novel concept
of antiviral nanocarriers, we present [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– inorganic-organic hybrid nanoparticles
(IOH-NPs) with an unprecedented foscarnet load of up to 44 wt-% per
nanoparticle for the first time. In addition to the high drug load,
the FCN-type IOH-NPs are characterized by the synthesis in water,
an uncomplex chemical composition, low toxicity, and good cellular
uptake. In vitro studies with the human cytomegalovirus
(HCMV) demonstrate that an IOH-mediated delivery of foscarnet considerably
improves the antiviral efficacy beyond its clinical formulation.

## Results and Discussion
2.1.

## Synthesis and the IOH-NP
Material Concept
Aiming at nanocarriers for drug delivery
and multimodal imaging,
we have developed saline inorganic-organic hybrid nanoparticles (IOH-NPs),
which consist of an inorganic cation and an organic anion. , The organic anion can be a fluorescent dye and/or a pharmaceutical
drug, functionalized with a phosphate, sulfonate, or carboxylate group.
Together with a suitable cation, this organic anion forms an insoluble
saline compound in water. Specific examples with fluorescent anions,
for instance, are [ZrO]2+[FMN]2–, [ZrO]2+[RRP]2–, or [ZrO]2+[DUT]2– (FMN: flavinmononucleotide, RRP: resorufin phosphate,
DUT: Dyomics-647 uridine triphosphate) with green, red, or infrared
emission, respectively. Examples for
IOH-NPs with drug anions, for instance, are [ZrO]2+[(FdUMP)0.995(DUT)0.005]2–, [ZrO]2+[(BMP)0.995(DUT)0.005]2–, or [ZrO]2+[(CLP)0.995(DUT)0.005]2– with the chemotherapeutic agent 5′-fluoro-2′-deoxyuridine-5′-monophosphate
(FdUMP), the glucocorticoid betamethasone phosphate (BMP), or the
antibiotic clindamycin phosphate as well as the previously mentioned
infrared emitting DUT. − Generally, IOH-NPs are characterized by an aqueous
synthesis, high dye/drug loads, and the adaptability of the material
concept.
In comparison to previous IOH-NPs, the realization
of IOH-NPs with antiviral drugs as pharmaceutical anions is more difficult
due to high costs, the often lipophilic properties (e.g., amantadine,
remdesivir, sofosbuvir), and/or the high-molecular weight (e.g., oligonucleotides)
of many antivirals. In addition to its general suitability as an antiviral
drug, foscarnet as an anionic drug with good water solubility and
low molecular weight seemed promising with regard to the IOH-NP concept
( ). FCN, however,
is a phosphonate, which—in contrast to phosphate-functionalized
drugs—often do not form any insoluble compound and nanoparticles
in water. With [ZrO]2+ as
an inorganic cation, which turned out to be the most suitable for
many IOH-NPs we have prepared earlier, − only OH-containing [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– IOH-NPs were obtained. Moreover, the formation of [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– turned
out to be highly sensitive to the pH to avoid dissolution at pH <
6 or the formation of [ZrO][OH]2 at pH > 8. For these
reasons,
here we also introduced Gd3+ as an inorganic cation to
prepare Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs, which show
good nanoparticle nucleation, high colloidal stability in water, and
with 44 wt-% an even higher FCN-load than [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– with 29 wt-%.
 In particular, [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs were prepared by water-based precipitation at room temperature
( ). Accordingly,
concentrated aqueous solutions of ZrOCl2 × 8H2O or GdCl3 × 6H2O were injected
with vigorous stirring into an aqueous solution of Na3(FCN).
Following the LaMer-Dinegar model of particle nucleation and particle
growth, a high supersaturation was induced
by the injection, which induces rapid particle nucleation, and thus,
the formation of small-sized nanoparticles. After purification, colorless
suspensions of [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– were obtained ( ).

2.2.

## Material Characterization
To characterize
the FCN-type IOH-NPs, first of all, particle size and colloidal properties
were addressed. According to dynamic light scattering (DLS), aqueous
suspensions of the as-prepared [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs exhibit mean hydrodynamic diameters of 47
± 7 and 59 ± 4 nm, respectively ( a and S1a). Scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) confirms the presence of spherical particles
with mean diameters of 18 ± 2 nm ([ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2–) and 32 ± 1
nm (Gd3+[FCN]3–) ( a–c and S1a–c). The latter values were calculated by the statistical evaluation
of 150 particles on SEM images. The larger particle diameter from
DLS reflects the hydrodynamic diameter and the presence of a rigid
layer of water molecules adsorbed on the particle surface. The immediate formation of uniform nanoparticles
and colloidally stable suspensions without the need of any additional
surface-active agent for size control and/or colloidal stabilization
is noteworthy and can be ascribed to an intrinsic charge stabilization.
This is evidenced by ζ-potential measurements, indicating negative
charging of −35 to −40 mV in water in the physiologically
most relevant pH range of 6.5–7.5 for [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– ( d and S1d). The as-prepared
IOH-NP suspensions do not show any sedimentation over a period of
2 weeks.
 To prove the chemical composition
of the [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs,
different analytical methods were
involved. First of all, X-ray diffraction (XRD) indicates the IOH-NPs
to be noncrystalline. Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy
shows evidence of the presence of foscarnet ( e and S1e). Accordingly,
spectra of [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– are well in agreement with Na3(FCN) as a reference and
show the characteristic FCN-type vibrations, including ν(C=O):
1800–1650, ν(P=O): 1415–1085, ν(P–O):
1100–1000, and ν(C–O): 1085–1050 cm–1. The observation of ν(O–H) at 3600–3000
cm–1 relates to the presence of water. Furthermore,
energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDXS) confirms the presence
of zirconium/gadolinium and phosphorus in the IOH-NPs (Figure S2). Finally, the chemical composition
of [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– was quantified by total
organics combustion via thermogravimetry (TG) and elemental analysis
(EA) (Figure S3). Here, the calculated
values are in agreement with the experimental observations and the
composition of the FCN-type IOH-NPs (see the Supporting Information (SI)).

2.3.

## Fluorescence Labeling
To enable the
FCN-type IOH-NPs for fluorescence-based monitoring, they were labeled
by a fluorescent dye. To this concern, the phosphate-functionalized
fluorescent dye Dyomics-647 uridine triphosphate (DUT) was added ( a). Due to its bright
emission, only a very low amount (about 0.01 mol %) of FCN needs to
be replaced by the dye anion. This can be expressed by the chemical
formula [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.39(DUT)0.01(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[(FCN)0.99(DUT)0.01]3–. The successful
incorporation of DUT into the FCN-type IOH-NPs is visible even with
the naked eye and indicated by the bluish color of the dye-modified
IOH-NPs although the dye concentration is low ( b,c and S4). Furthermore,
the characteristic absorption of DUT at 580–680 nm can be observed
in UV-vis spectra (Figure S5). Finally,
excitation and emission spectra of the dye-modified IOH-NPs show the
characteristic absorption at 550–650 nm (peaking at 647 nm)
and the deep red emission at 650–770 nm (peaking at 667 nm).
The fluorescence features of the FCN-type IOH-NPs are well in agreement
with the pure fluorescent dye ( d and S4).
 
2.4.

## Antiviral Activity
To verify the
applicability and efficiency of the FCN-type IOH-NPs as antiviral
drug carriers, a recombinant HCMV expressing mNeonGreen (HCMVIEr) was used to infect retinal pigment epithelial cells (RPE-1
cell line), as previously described. , Cells were
treated with [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs, and the viral spread was monitored by live-cell imaging
within 7 days after infection ( a). The gene for the mNeonGreen protein is expressed
with the control of the immediate-early HCMV promoter, enabling a
fluorescence-based identification of infected cells. Hence, increasing
areas of green fluorescence correspond to the spread of the virus
from cell to cell. An aqueous solution of molecular FCN (i.e., Na3(FCN) in H2O)—with a concentration as given
intravenously in the clinics (32 μM)—was used as a positive
control and compared with identical FCN concentrations provided by
the FCN-type IOH-NPs. Moreover, [ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– and Gd23+[AMP]23– IOH-NPs (AMP: adenosine monophosphate) were applied as drug-free
negative controls, which were expected not to have any influence on
the viral replication cycle ( a). After 7 days of cultivation,
all samples show a certain increase in the green fluorescence channel,
indicating virus expansion over the duration of the experiment. Thereafter, samples treated either with FCN-type
IOH-NPs in suspension or with freely dissolved FCN (positive control)
show a reduced HCMV spread ( a). Especially, the Gd3+[FCN]3–-IOH-NP-treated sample exhibits a very low green fluorescence signal.
In contrast, samples treated with the negative controls, i.e., the
drug-free [ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– and
Gd23+[AMP]23– IOH-NPs, show a significantly
stronger infection as indicated by the much more intense green emission
( a).
 The HCMV spread was quantified based on the green fluorescence
on the RPE-1 monolayer ( b,c). The effects on the viral growth with the FCN-type [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs were compared
with freely dissolved FCN (positive control) as well as with the drug-free
[ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– and Gd23+[AMP]23– IOH-NPs (negative controls). Solutions
of hydrogen phosphate (i.e., Na2(HPO4)) and
AMP (i.e., Na2(AMP)) were used as additional negative controls.
The concentrations of the IOH-NPs and the reference solutions were
adjusted to FCN concentrations of 10 μM ( b) and 100 μM ( c). As expected, the antiviral impact at
lower FCN concentrations was less expressed than at higher concentrations.
Nonetheless, even at the lowest tested concentration of 10 μM
FCN, where the antiviral effect of freely dissolved FCN is almost
negligible, FCN-type IOH-NPs still reduce the viral expansion by a
factor of 2 or more ( b), whereby the Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs
demonstrate a stronger antiviral effect than the [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– IOH-NPs
already at a low concentration. The advantage of the IOH-NPs becomes
even more pronounced over time and can be ascribed to a persistent
antiviral effect over several rounds of replication and a certain
delay in FCN release from the IOH-NPs until they are completely dissolved.
The higher activity of Gd3+[FCN]3– was
confirmed at the higher FCN concentration of 100 μM, although
the difference between the two IOH-NP species becomes less pronounced
than at 10 μM ( c). At 100 μM, the virus spread was almost completely
suppressed by both [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs over the whole period of examination. In accordance with
previously published results, − , IOH-NPs had only minimal effects on the cell viability even in the
presence of the viral infection. At 10 days postinfection and IOH-NP
treatment, cell death was below 5% for 100 μM [ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– IOH-NPs and below 2% in all
other conditions ( d). In conclusion, the IOH-NP-mediated delivery of FCN considerably
improves the antiviral efficacy beyond its clinical formulation.
To rationalize the antiviral activity of FCN and the results observed
for the IOH-NPs, the mechanism of action needs to be taken into account.
FCN is structurally similar to pyrophosphate. By replacing pyrophosphate,
FCN interferes with the viral DNA polymerase, blocks the replication
of viral genomes, and the production of the viral offspring. This requires high FCN concentrations inside
the cell and, foremost, in the nucleus, where the replication of viral
DNA takes place. Therefore, our results argue that IOH-NPs as drug
carriers support a FCN uptake into cells and maintain a high intracellular
drug concentration over a long time. Important factors, which can
support this effect are the difference between cellular uptake mechanisms
for nanoparticles and freely dissolved molecules as well as the slow
but continuous drug release from the FCN-type IOH-NPs inside the cells.
Moreover, it should be considered that each IOH-NP contains multiple
drug molecules, which ensures a quick achievement of high intracellular
FCN concentrations.
Nanocarriers can be utilized for transporting
drugs directly into
infected tissue, such as the lungs, e.g., via aerosolization or inhalation.
Here, the uptake and intracellular localization of the FCN-type IOH-NPs
into lung epithelial cells—the site of replication for many
respiratory viruses including SARS-CoV-2 or influenza A viruses—are
of specific interest. To locate the IOH-NPs inside the cells, we have
applied the fluorescence-labeled Gd3+[(FCN)0.99(DUT)0.01]3– IOH-NPs (see ) to Calu-3 lung cancer epithelial
cells and fixed the cells after 24 h. Staining the cell membrane and
the cell nuclei followed by confocal laser-scanning fluorescence microscopy
in Z-stacks allowed a three-dimensional localization of the IOH-NPs
( ). DUT-fluorescent
structures (white) corresponding to clusters of the FCN-type IOH-NPs
were observed at different intracellular compartments. In fact, three
distinct localizations were found: membrane-enveloped IOH-NPs at the
apical surface of cells ( a), cytoplasmic IOH-NPs ( b), and intranuclear IOH-NPs ( c). This suggests that the
FCN-type IOH-NPs and their cargo can readily enter the site of the
CMV DNA replication (nucleus) as well as the site of the virus assembly
(cytoplasm).

## Conclusions
Foscarnet-type
inorganic-organic hybrid nanoparticles (IOH-NPs)
with a composition of [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[FCN]3– are shown for the first time and represent a novel concept of nanocarriers
to transport antiviral agents. They are prepared via a straightforward
aqueous synthesis and featured by high drug loads (up to 44 wt %)
at low material complexity, which distinguishes FCN-type IOH-NPs from
other nanocarrier-based antiviral delivery systems. Furthermore, the
FCN-type IOH-NPs can be easily fluorescence-labeled using Dyomics-647
uridine triphosphate (DUT). Despite a low dye concentration (0.01
mol %), the resulting [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.39(DUT)0.01(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[(FCN)0.99(DUT)0.01]3– IOH-NPs
show intense deep red emission (580–680 nm).
The first in vitro studies on HCMV-infected cells
demonstrate a substantial increase of the antiviral efficiency for
the FCN-type IOH-NPs in comparison to freely dissolved FCN. Both were
applied with identical FCN concentrations at the level of clinical
formulations (10–100 μM). In addition to the generally
stronger therapeutic impact of the FCN-type IOH-NPs over freely dissolved
FCN, [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– and especially Gd3+[FCN]3– prove their ability to reduce viral populations even at very low
concentrations. In contrast, the antiviral impact of freely dissolved
FCN at such concentrations becomes almost negligible. These benefits
of the FCN-type IOH-NPs can allow a reduction of drug amounts needed
for therapy, and hence, a potential attenuation of side effects. Finally,
we demonstrate the uptake of FCN-type IOH-NPs at the site of the CMV
DNA replication (nucleus) as well as the site of the virus assembly
(cytoplasm) of nonphagocytic lung epithelial cells. The benefits of
the FCN-type IOH-NPs as well as the general flexibility of the IOH-NP
concept also with regard to other antiviral agents encourage a future
use for the delivery of antivirals against respiratory viruses.

## Experimental Section
4.1.

## Synthesis
4.1.1.

## Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs
Briefly, 0.5 mL of an aqueous solution
of GdCl3 × 6H2O (44.6 mg, 0.12 mmol, 99%,
Sigma-Aldrich,
Germany) was injected into 50 mL of a vigorously stirred solution
of Na3(FCN) (30.0 mg, 0.1 mmol, ≥98%, Alfa Aesar).
After 2 min, the IOH-NPs were separated from the suspension via centrifugation
(15 min, 25 000 r.p.m.) and washed twice with H2O (10 mL) by redispersion and centrifugation. Finally, 26.9 mg of
the as-prepared colorless Gd3+[FCN]3– IOH-NPs (85% yield) was dispersed in 10 mL of H2O.

4.1.2.

## [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– IOH-NPs
Briefly, 0.5 mL of
an aqueous solution of ZrOCl2 × 8H2O (51.6
mg, 0.16 mmol, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) was injected into 50 mL
of a vigorously stirred solution of Na3(FCN) (30.0 mg,
0.1 mmol, ≥98%, Alfa Aesar). Prior to the injection, the Na3(FCN) solution was adjusted to pH 7 by addition of 0.1 M NaOH.
After 2 min, the IOH-NPs were separated from the suspension via centrifugation
(15 min, 25 000 r.p.m.) and washed twice with H2O (10 mL) by redispersion and centrifugation. Finally, 24.8 mg of
the as-prepared colorless [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.4(OH)0.8]2– IOH-NPs (50% yield) was dispersed
in 10 mL of H2O.

4.1.3.

## Gd23+[AMP]23– IOH-NPs (Negative Reference) 
In total, 0.5 mL of an aqueous solution
of GdCl3 ×
6H2O (18.6 mg, 0.05 mmol, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany)
was injected into 50 mL of a vigorously stirred solution of Na2(AMP) (39.1 mg, 0.1 mmol, >99%, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany).
After
2 min, the IOH-NPs were separated from the suspension via centrifugation
(15 min, 25 000 r.p.m.) and washed twice with H2O (10 mL) by redispersion and centrifugation. Finally, 8.0 mg of
the as-prepared colorless Gd23+[AMP]23– IOH-NPs was dispersed in 10 mL of H2O.

4.1.4.

## [ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– IOH-NPs
(Negative Reference)
Briefly, 0.5
mL of an aqueous solution of ZrOCl2 × 8H2O (51.6 mg, 0.16 mmol, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) was injected
into 50 mL of a vigorously stirred solution of Na2(HPO4) (26.5 mg, 0.17 mmol, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany). After
2 min, the IOH-NPs were separated from the suspension via centrifugation
(15 min, 25 000 r.p.m.) and washed twice with H2O (10 mL) by redispersion and centrifugation. Finally, 24.8 mg of
the as-prepared colorless [ZrO]2+[HPO4]2– IOH-NPs was dispersed in 10 mL of H2O.

4.1.5.

## Fluorescence Labeling
To follow
the cellular uptake, the IOH-NPs were labeled with Dyomics-647 uridine
triphosphate (DUT, C46H55Li5N5O22P3S2, Dyomics, Germany)
as a fluorescent dye. To this concern, 25.0 nmol of DUT was added
to the abovementioned Na3(FCN) solution prior to the injection
of ZrOCl2 × 8H2O or GdCl3 ×
6H2O. As a result, [ZrO]2+[(FCN)0.39(DUT)0.01(OH)0.8]2– and Gd3+[(FCN)0.99(DUT)0.01]3– were obtained. Although the amount of the fluorescent dye is very
low, its presence is indicated by the bluish color of the IOH-NPs.

4.2.

## Biological Studies
4.2.1.

## Evaluation
of the Antiviral Efficacy
Retinal pigment epithelial cells
(RPE-1 cell line) were incubated
on a 96-well plate for 24 h and then infected with recombinant human
cytomegalovirus (HCMV). This HCMV had been engineered to encode a
green fluorescent protein (mNeonGreen) under control of an immediate
early promoter, hence enabling quantitative monitoring of the virus-spreading
process at any given point in time via live-cell fluorescence microscopy. Directly after infection, the cells were treated
with different concentrations of carefully redispersed IOH-NPs (ultrasonic
water bath 15 s, 15 s of vortexing) or the relevant references in
adjusted concentrations. Fluorescence-based analysis of the antiviral
impact was performed with an IncuCyte (Sartorius, Germany).

4.2.2.

## Imaging of the Cellular Uptake
Calu-3 (epithelial human
lung cancer) cells were seeded onto black
96-well plates with optical bottom (Thermo Fisher) at a density of
5 × 10–4 cells/mL. After 24 h, the IOH-NPs
were carefully redispersed and added to the growth medium at varying
concentrations. At varying time-points after treatment, the cells
were fixed with 4% formaldehyde in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS)
for 10 min at room temperature, followed by washing with phosphate-buffered
saline (PBS). Nuclei were labeled with DAPI (5 ng/mL). Cell membranes
were stained using the CytoPainter Cell Plasma Membrane Staining Kit
(Abcam, United Kingdom) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
Confocal three-dimensional images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM980
microscope in Airyscan super-resolution mode.

4.2.3.

## Cell Viability Assessment
Cells
from the evaluation of the antiviral efficacy were counterstained
with the Zombie-NIR dye (BioLegend, USA) according to the manufacturer’s
instructions and subsequently imaged and analyzed using an IncuCyte
S3 (Sartorius, Germany).
