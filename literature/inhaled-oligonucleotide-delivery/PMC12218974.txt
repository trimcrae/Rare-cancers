## Abstract
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide, characterized by high mortality rates. TRIM23, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, represents a potential molecular target for anticancer drug development. Additionally, Hexahydrocurcumin (HHC) exhibits notable anticancer activity as a natural product. Here, we aimed to investigate HHC’s anti-tumor role in HCC. Molecular docking assays and Cellular thermal shift assays were employed to predict HHC’s potential targeting of TRIM23. Immunofluorescence and western blot analyses were conducted to assess HHC’s inhibition of TRIM23 expression. Wound-healing, clone formation, and transwell assays were used to evaluate its anti-tumor efficacy. Quantitative proteome analysis and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) were employed to explore and verify HHC’s potential mechanism in HCC cells. Subcutaneous HCC tumor-bearing mice were used to further validate HHC’s function in vivo. Public databases confirmed TRIM23 as a potential biomarker of HCC, associated with poor prognosis. Molecular docking and Cellular thermal shift assays confirmed HHC as a direct inhibitor of TRIM23. Subsequent in vitro assays demonstrated HHC’s ability to impede HCC cell function. Quantitative proteome analysis revealed upregulation of MBNL1, a tumor suppressor, in HHC-treated HCC cells. Rescue analysis further demonstrated MBNL1 as a substrate of TRIM23, directly influencing the phenotypic outcomes of HHC-treated cells. Additionally, subcutaneous HCC tumor-bearing mice confirmed HHC’s efficacy in vivo. Our findings underscore TRIM23 as a promising therapeutic target for HCC treatment, with HHC emerging as an effective therapeutic agent by targeting the TRIM23/MBNL1 axis.

## Introduction
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a prevalent malignant tumor globally, ranking third in terms of mortality rates among cancer patients . Treatment modalities for HCC encompass a variety of approaches, including surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. Surgical resection remains the preferred treatment for early-stage HCC . However, due to the unique anatomical location and vital function of the liver, only a minority of patients are suitable candidates for surgical intervention , . For those ineligible for surgery or diagnosed with advanced-stage HCC, systemic treatments such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapy become the mainstay of treatment , . Nevertheless, challenges persist in HCC management, attributed to difficulties in early diagnosis and the considerable resistance exhibited by HCC patients towards conventional therapeutic modalities , . Consequently, exploring the underlying mechanisms of HCC pathogenesis and identifying novel therapeutic targets have emerged as crucial directions in current HCC research.
The TRIM (Tripartite Motif) family represents a group of proteins characterized by shared structural domains, playing pivotal roles in biological processes such as cellular signaling, immune response , , viral infection , and tumorigenesis . Consistent structural domains among TRIM proteins include a RING domain, a B-box domain, and a coiled-coil domain, with the RING domain conferring E3 ubiquitin ligase activity upon TRIM proteins . Through participation in tumor-related signaling pathways, TRIM proteins modulate key biological processes including cell proliferation, apoptosis, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance, thereby exerting influence on HCC initiation and progression . TRIM23, as a member of the TRIM family, has garnered increasing attention for its roles in cancer. Studies have demonstrated elevated expression of TRIM23 in various cancers, including HCC , colorectal cancer , , gastric cancer , and lung cancer . Its overexpression is closely associated with aggressive tumor growth, metastasis, and poor prognosis. Additionally, TRIM23 overexpression has been linked to resistance to chemotherapy in cancer treatment . Hence, investigations into TRIM23 not only contribute to a deeper understanding of HCC tumorigenesis but also offer novel avenues for developing therapeutic strategies targeting TRIM23.
In cancer therapy, small molecule drugs, natural compounds, and their derivatives have attracted significant attention as pivotal therapeutic agents – . Curcumin, a polyphenolic compound extracted from the rhizomes of turmeric, has shown therapeutic potential in various chronic diseases, especially cancer . Its anticancer properties are attributed to its interaction with multiple immune mediators within the tumor microenvironment, thereby exhibiting immunomodulatory capabilities . Hexahydrocurcumin (HHC), a major metabolite derived from curcumin extraction, shares similar phenolic groups with curcumin and demonstrates many analogous biological and pharmacological characteristics , . Previous studies have suggested that HHC, as a natural product, possesses remarkable anticancer activity . However, its therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms in HCC remain to be fully elucidated.
In this study, we investigated the expression and prognostic significance of TRIM23 in HCC, and identified HHC as a novel small molecule inhibitor of TRIM23. We characterized the anti-HCC activity and underlying mechanism of HHC both in vitro and in vivo. Our results strongly suggest that HHC represents a promising candidate for therapeutic intervention against HCC.

## Methods

## Cell lines and animals
The HCC cell lines (Huh-7, SMMC-7721, Hep3B, and HepG2) and normal liver cell line (L02) were obtained from the ATCC cell repository. All cells were cultured in 1640 medium supplemented with 1% penicillin-streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum under standard conditions of 5% CO2 and 37 °C. BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Shanghai BK Company and housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment with stable temperature and humidity. All animal experiments were conducted at the Zhejiang Chinese Medical University Animal Center in compliance with relevant regulations.

## Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
Tumor and adjacent non-tumorous tissue samples from 10 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients provided by Wenzhou TCM Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University were used for immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis. Tissue sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated through graded alcohols, and subjected to antigen retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at high temperature. Following blocking of endogenous peroxidase activity, sections were incubated with primary antibody against TRIM23 (Proteintech: TRIM23, cat no. 66837-1-Ig) at 4 °C overnight. After washing, sections were treated with a secondary antibody and visualized using a DAB substrate. Hematoxylin was used for counterstaining, and the slides were examined under a microscope for TRIM23 expression. Ethical approval and informed consent were obtained accordingly.

## Molecular docking
The small molecule structure of HHC (Compound CID: 5318039) was downloaded from PubChem and processed using the Molecular Operating Environment software. The protein structure of TRIM23 (ID: 5VZV) was downloaded from the RCSB PDB and processed using Pymol (version 4.6) software. Subsequently, molecular docking between HHC and TRIM23 was performed using AutoDockTools (version 1.5.7) according to software tutorials, selecting the binding conformation with the highest binding energy for analysis and visualization.

## Cellular thermal shift assays
HepG2 HCC cells were cultured overnight in 10 cm dishes. Subsequently, cells were treated with or without 50 µM of HHC at 37 °C for 2 h. Cells were collected and washed twice with PBS, then resuspended in PBS containing 1% protease inhibitor. The collected cells were heated in a PCR machine at 46–56 °C for 3 min, followed by cycles of 3 min at room temperature and 3 min in liquid nitrogen. After vortexing and centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and added to loading buffer, heated at 100 °C for 10 min to denature the proteins, and then subjected to western blot analysis.

## Western blotting
Briefly, cells were collected and washed twice with PBS, followed by lysis on ice using RIPA lysis buffer containing 1% protease inhibitor (Beyotime). Subsequently, the lysates were centrifuged, and the supernatants were collected for further processing. The protein samples were then loaded onto SDS-PAGE gels and subjected to electrophoresis, transfer, and blocking steps. The catalog number of the ladder used for the western blotting is 26,616 from ThermoFisher company. The membranes were incubated in primary antibodies (Proteintech: TRIM23, cat no. 12607-1-AP; MBNL1, cat no. 66837-1-Ig; GAPDH, cat no. 60004-1-Ig and Vinculin, cat no. 66305-1-Ig; Cell Signaling Technology, Histone H3, cat no.4499T) overnight at 4 °C. The following day, after incubation with secondary antibodies at room temperature for 2 h, membranes were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection reagents (BioSharp). Bio-Rad VersaDoc or ChemiDoc imaging systems were used to obtain the images, with Quantity One (version 4.6.7 from Bio-Rad) or Image Lab (version 5.2.1 from Bio-Rad) being used for quantification.

## Cell viability assay
5000 HepG2 HCC cells were seeded into a 96-well plate and treated with different concentrations of HHC (0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20 µM). After 24 h of treatment, the culture medium was replaced with medium containing 10% CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime), and the cells were further incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 2 h. Subsequently, the absorbance was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## Colony formation
800 HepG2 HCC cells were seeded into a 1.5 cm dish and treated with or without 5 µM HHC for 24 h, followed by continued incubation for 10 days. Subsequently, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and images were captured.

## Transwell assay
30,000 cells were seeded into Transwell chambers and cultured in serum-free medium with or without 5 µM HHC. The chambers were placed in a 24-well plate, with the lower chambers covered with 10% FBS medium. After 48 h of incubation, the cells in the Transwell chambers were fixed and stained with 4% paraformaldehyde and crystal violet. Following removal of the upper layer cells with a cotton swab, observations and images were captured under a microscope.

## Wound healing
80,000 cells were seeded into a wound scratch tool, allowed to adhere and grow to confluence, then the tool was removed. The cells were cultured in 1% FBS serum medium with or without 5 µM HHC, and observations and images were taken at 0, 24, and 48 h.

## Quantitative proteome analysis
HepG2 HCC cells were exposed to 5.0 µM HHC for 24 h. Subsequently, cellular samples were collected and forwarded to GeneChem Biotechnology Company (Shanghai, China) for quantitative proteome library construction and sequencing. Following established protocols from prior studies, subsequent steps including protein extraction, peptide dissociation, and machine operation were conducted. Proteins exhibiting a fold change > 1.2 and a p-value (Student’s t-test) < 0.05 were identified as differentially expressed proteins. These differentially expressed proteins underwent GO enrichment and KEGG enrichment analyses. Each experimental group was replicated three times for robustness and reliability. An online tool for visualization, WeiShengxin, plotted the results of KEGGen richness (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

## SiRNA transfection
We utilized the Lipofectamine 3000 reagent to transfect target-specific siRNA against MBNL1, prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The siRNA-Lipofectamine 3000 complex was added to the cell culture medium containing OPTI-MEM and incubated with cells for 24 h. After media replacement, the cells were further cultured with 5µM of HCC for an additional 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were harvested for subsequent experiments.

## Co-immunoprecipitation experiments
The HepG2 HCC cells were cultured in a 10 cm dish and subsequently harvested. The cells were lysed using lysis buffer containing 1% protease inhibitor. Following the instructions provided in the Co-IP kit (Epizyme), the subsequent steps were carried out. Briefly, a portion of the protein supernatant was collected as input, while the remaining protein supernatant was divided into two parts and incubated separately with IgG and MBNL1 antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Magnetic beads were then added and incubated, followed by collection using a magnetic rack, washing, and elution with loading buffer to denature the proteins.

## Subcutaneous HCC tumors in nude mice
Six-week-old male BALB/c nude mice were injected subcutaneously with 5 × 10^6 cells in the axilla. One week later, when the average volume of subcutaneous tumors reached 80 mm3, the mice were treated. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula Volume = (length × width^2) / 2. The mice were treated intraperitoneally with either saline, 2.5 mg/kg of HHC, 5 mg/kg of HHC, or 5 mg/kg of cisplatin, once daily. Tumor volume and body weight were measured weekly. After 28 days of drug treatment, the mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation, and subcutaneous tumors and peripheral blood were collected. The tumor mass was weighed, and IHC staining was performed on tumor tissues. ELISA experiments were conducted on peripheral blood to measure ALT, AST, and TILB levels.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using The SPSS (version 23.0) software. Comparisons across groups were performed by ANOVA or t-test. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. ∗P value < 0.05, ∗∗P value < 0.01 and ∗∗∗P value < 0.001.

## Result

## TRIM23 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in HCC
Using the TIMER2.0 database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/), it was observed that TRIM23 mRNA expression was significantly lower in tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues across various cancers (Fig.  A). However, utilizing liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) RNA-seq data from the TCGA database, we confirmed a significant upregulation of TRIM23 expression in tumor tissues compared to both paired and non-paired normal tissues (Fig.  B-C). Additionally, IHC results from 10 HCC patients obtained from Wenzhou TCM Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University demonstrated that TRIM23 expression was higher in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non-tumorous tissues (Supplementary Fig. 1). Moreover, survival analysis indicated that high TRIM23 expression was associated with a poorer prognosis in early-stage HCC, though not in late-stage HCC (Fig.  D and E). Additionally, analysis using the Timer2.0 database revealed a negative correlation between TRIM23 expression and CD8 + T cells as well as CD4 + T cells within the HCC tumor microenvironment, while a positive correlation was observed with cancer-associated fibroblasts and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (Fig.  F–I). Overall, these findings suggest that elevated TRIM23 expression in colorectal cancer is indicative of an unfavorable prognosis.
 

## HHC is identified as a small molecule TRIM24 inhibitor
In pursuit of potential small molecule inhibitors targeting TRIM23, we conducted a docking-based virtual screening of an internal natural product library, leveraging the crystal structure of TRIM23. This methodology yielded several promising chemical scaffolds as putative TRIM23 inhibitors, among which HHC emerged. Molecular docking analyses revealed that HHC established four hydrogen bonds with residues GLY-32, LEU-40, THR-54, and LYS-91, forming effective interactions with TRIM23 within distances ranging from 1.9 to 2.5 Å (illustrated by yellow dashed lines, Fig.  A–C). Subsequent cellular thermal shift assays demonstrated that while TRIM23 protein degradation increased with temperature in the control group, the presence of HHC decelerated this process (Fig.  E–F). Furthermore, western blotting and immunofluorescence analyses indicated a concentration-dependent downregulation of TRIM23 expression by HHC in HepG2 HCC cells (Fig.  G–I), implying its potential to selectively degrade TRIM23 in HCC cells.
 

## HHC exerts anticancer effects on HCC cells in vitro
To investigate the inhibitory effects of HHC on HCC cell viability, various concentrations of HHC were administered to four HCC cell lines (Huh-7, SMMC-7721, Hep3B, and HepG2) and one normal liver cell line (L02). Results revealed a significantly higher inhibitory effect of HHC on HCC cells compared to normal liver cells (IC50 = 3.221–5.730 µM vs. 12.94 µM), demonstrating a concentration-dependent trend (Fig.  A). Colony formation assays further confirmed a marked inhibition of HepG2 colony formation by HHC (Fig.  B-C). Moreover, wound healing experiments demonstrated that HHC effectively impeded HepG2 migration in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig.  D-E). Additionally, transwell assays indicated that HHC significantly suppressed the migration and invasion of HepG2 cells (Fig.  F-G). These findings suggest that HHC can effectively attenuate the phenotypic characteristics of HCC cells without eliciting adverse effects on normal liver cells.
 

## HHC regulates survival related pathways in HCC cells
To explore the molecular mechanisms underlying HHC, we conducted quantitative proteomic analysis on HepG2 HCC cells subjected to HHC treatment. A total of 1034 proteins were identified and annotated across all samples. Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated significant shifts in PC1 and PC2 between the HHC and control groups (Fig.  A). Following rigorous screening, 146 proteins were identified as differentially expressed (Fig.  B-C). Specifically, the HHC group exhibited upregulation of 54 proteins and downregulation of 92 proteins compared to the control group. Subsequent KEGG analysis revealed that the differentially expressed proteins post-HHC treatment were primarily associated with Fructose and mannose metabolism, the Pentose phosphate pathway, HIF-1 signaling pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, Glycolysis, Gluconeogenesis, and Necroptosis (Fig.  D). Furthermore, GO analysis indicated involvement of these proteins in processes such as Regulation of protein ubiquitination and Positive regulation of phosphoprotein phosphatase activity (Fig.  E). Collectively, these findings suggest that HHC may modulate survival-related pathways in HCC cells, including metabolism, necroptosis, ubiquitination, and phosphorylation.
 

## HHC may increase MBNL1 expression by targeting TRIM23
The TRIM family represents a classical group of E3 ubiquitin ligases. We postulate that HHC could potentially impede tumor growth by downregulating TRIM23, consequently leading to the stabilization of specific tumor suppressor factors. Analysis of proteomic data (Fig.  B) revealed a noteworthy elevation in MBNL1 levels subsequent to HHC treatment. Previous investigations have characterized MBNL1 as a tumor suppressive factor exerting control over RNA shearing functionality and driving extensive transcriptome alterations, thereby compromising angiogenesis and tumor growth – . Western blot analysis illustrated a significant reduction in TRIM23 expression and a concomitant increase in MBNL1 expression in HepG2 HCC cells upon HHC treatment (Fig.  A–C). To elucidate the mechanism underlying the upregulation of MBNL1, HepG2 HCC cells were co-treated with HHC and Cycloheximide (CHX) for 0, 6, and 12 h. Results demonstrated that MBNL1 protein degradation occurred under normal conditions (with typical TRIM23 levels), while the degradation trend of MBNL1 was impeded following HHC treatment (in the context of reduced TRIM23 levels) (Fig.  D-E). Furthermore, co-immunoprecipitation assays confirmed the interaction between TRIM23 and MBNL1 in HepG2 HCC cells (Fig.  F). In conclusion, we propose that HHC may enhance MBNL1 expression levels by suppressing its E3 ubiquitin ligase, TRIM23.
 

## HHC inhibits hepatoma carcinoma through TRIM23/MBNL1 axis
To further elucidate the role of MBNL1 in HCC, we employed siRNA targeting MBNL1 to investigate its impact on rescuing the anti-tumor effects exerted by HHC. As depicted in Fig.  A,B, treatment with HHC led to a significant decrease in TRIM23 expression and a concurrent increase in MBNL1 expression, whereas the administration of siRNA successfully abrogated the HHC-induced elevation of MBNL1 expression. Colony formation assays further revealed that siRNA-mediated knockdown of MBNL1 effectively restored the proliferation inhibition induced by HHC treatment (Fig.  C,D). Furthermore, wound healing assays demonstrated that downregulation of MBNL1 expression attenuated the inhibitory effect of HHC on the migration of HepG2 cells, even in the presence of reduced TRIM23 expression following HHC treatment (Fig.  E,F). Consistent findings were observed in transwell assays, further indicating that MBNL1 modulates the TRIM23-mediated effects of HHC treatment (Fig.  G–I). Collectively, these results highlight the pivotal role of the TRIM23/MBNL1 axis in mediating the anti-tumor effects of HHC in HCC.
 

## HHC suppresses hepatoma carcinoma in vivo
We evaluated the efficacy of HHC in a subcutaneous mouse model of HCC (Fig.  A). As depicted in Fig.  B–D, doses of 2.5 and 5.0 mg/kg/d of HHC demonstrated inhibitory effects on HepG2 subcutaneous tumor growth by 32.93% and 49.32%, respectively, while the inhibitory effect of cisplatin at a dose of 5 mg/kg/d was 51.86%. Furthermore, treatment with HHC at doses of 2.5 and 5.0 mg/kg/d did not induce significant alterations in average body weight, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), or total bilirubin (TBIL) levels in peripheral blood (Fig.  E–H). Conversely, treatment with cisplatin at a dose of 5 mg/kg/d led to a significant reduction in mouse body weight (Fig.  D). These results suggest that, within an effective anti-cancer range, HHC exhibits a superior ability to mitigate drug-induced toxicity compared to traditional chemotherapy agents.
 Additionally, immunohistochemistry results revealed that HHC treatment significantly downregulated the expression of TRIM23 while upregulating the expression of MBNL1 in subcutaneous tumor tissues (Fig.  I). These findings collectively indicate that HHC effectively suppresses HCC growth through the TRIM23/MBNL1 axis.

## Discussion
HCC is a malignant tumor with limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis . Therefore, investigating its pathogenesis involving various molecular and cellular regulatory mechanisms is crucial. Post-translational modifications of proteins, including glycosylation, acetylation, phosphorylation, and ubiquitination, play significant roles in the development of HCC – . Ubiquitination, mediated by ubiquitin ligases such as the TRIM family, marks specific protein targets with ubiquitin molecules, leading to their degradation or involvement in cellular signaling . The TRIM family, characterized by common structural domains, plays a pivotal role in HCC development[14]. Excessive expression of TRIM23 as an E3 ubiquitin ligase has been confirmed in various cancers, including HCC – . This study revealed that the elevated expression of TRIM23 in HCC acts as an E3 ubiquitin ligase targeting its substrate MBNL1, an anti-cancer factor, for ubiquitination and degradation. This finding provides important clues for further investigation into the pathogenesis of HCC.
Past studies have demonstrated the indispensable role of targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome system in cancer regulation . Consequently, there has been increasing research interest in small molecule inhibitors or natural products targeting various E3 ubiquitin ligases, such as the F-box family, β-transducin repeat-containing protein (β-TrCP), and S-phase kinase-associated protein 2 (Skp2) . Many natural products possess the ability to modulate the activity of E3 ubiquitin ligases, thereby impacting biological processes in tumor cells such as proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, and metastasis . To achieve better targeted degradation functionality, protein-targeted chimeras (ProTACs) have been developed to target various E3 ubiquitin ligases, resulting in improved pharmacokinetics and enhanced anti-tumor effects . In our study, we found that HHC, as a natural product, exhibits significant anti-HCC activity by inhibiting the protein expression of TRIM23 in HCC cells, thereby affecting its ubiquitination activity. Hence, natural products targeting E3 ubiquitin ligases like HHC hold promising prospects in the treatment of HCC, and we also propose the potential of HHC to be incorporated into ProTACs for targeted degradation of TRIM23.
Our study unveiled the pivotal role of TRIM23 in stabilizing MBNL1 protein, marking a significant discovery. Initially, research on MBNL1 focused on fibroblasts, neuronal tissues, etc., highlighting its crucial role in the ultimate differentiation of muscle cells and neurons . Moreover, in tumor cells, MBNL1, as an RNA-binding protein, participates in regulating RNA splicing and stability, exerting significant influence on processes like cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis . Hence, MBNL1 may serve as a potential anti-HCC target. In conclusion, elucidating the mechanisms of action of E3 ubiquitin ligase, TRIM23, and its substrate, MBNL1, in tumor development, holds crucial theoretical and clinical significance for HCC treatment.

## Conclusion
In this study, we investigated the role of TRIM23 in HCC and identified a novel TRIM23 inhibitor, HHC. HHC, which targets and degrades TRIM23, exhibits significant antitumor efficacy without causing substantial host toxicity. This effect is attributed to the induction of MBNL1 expression observed both in vitro and in vivo. In conclusion, our findings underscore the importance of the TRIM23/MBNL1 pathway in HCC treatment, and we propose HHC as a novel and promising therapeutic option for HCC.
