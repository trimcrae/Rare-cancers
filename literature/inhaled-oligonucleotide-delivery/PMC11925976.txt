However, the role of GDF15 in silicosis remains unclear. In this study, the serum level of GDF-15 was investigated in 46 patients with silicosis by ELISA and results showed it was higher than that of control patients. The effects of exogenous GDF15 on mRNA and miRNA expression profiles of MRC5 cells were analyzed by RNA sequencing. GDF15 activated human embryonic lung fibroblast MRC5 cells with upregulation of col1a and α-SMA. GDF15 reduced miR-338 expression and increased STAT1 expression in MRC5 cells. The results of the luciferase reporter assay and bioinformatics analysis indicated that STAT1 was a direct target of miR-338. miR-338 mimics down-regulated col1a and α-SMA expression induced by GDF15 with STAT1 overexpression, whereas miR-338 inhibitor up-regulated col1a and α-SMA expression induced by GDF15 with STAT1 knockdown. Those results indicated GDF15 activated MRC5 cells through the miR-338/STAT1 pathway and GDF-15 may play an important role in silicosis.

## Introduction
Silicosis, a common occupational disease, is caused by the sustained inhalation of silica dust (SiO2). According to a recent report, it is one of the most serious occupational diseases worldwide [ ]. The disease leads to anomalous pulmonary tissue repair and irreversible pulmonary failureis and has poor prognosis. Aberrant fibroblast activation is one of pathological characteristics of silicosis [ ]. However, the molecular mechanisms aberrant fibroblast activation are not fully understood.
Growth differentiation factor-15 (GDF15) is a cytokine belonging to the transforming growth factor beta family [ ]. In most tissues, different cell types express it at low levels under normal conditions [ ]. Evidence from recent studies indicates that GDF15 serves as a biomarker for human diseases such as diabetes [ ], cardiovascular disease [ ], renal failure [ ], and cancer [ ]. High levels of GDF15 were independently associated with higher mortality in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [ ]. The signaling pathways activated by GDF15 are complex and context-dependent,including GFRAL-RET, MAPK(mitogen-activated protein kinase) and PI3K /Akt (phosphoinositide 3-kinase)pathways [ ]. GDF-15 activates type 1 receptors and phosphorylates Smad2/3 and Smad1/5/8, which translocate to the nucleus in the form of heteromeric complexes with Smad4 [ ].
GDF15 has emerged as a significant player in the context of organ fibrosis. Elevated levels of GDF15 have been observed in the lungs of bleomycin-treated mice, correlating with the severity of fibrosis [ ]. GDF15 has been identified as a key player in the fibrotic remodeling of the heart, particularly following ischemic injury or radiation exposure [ ]. Blocking GDF15 with a neutralizing antibody resulted in a delay in primary hepatic stellate cell activation and remission of liver fibrosis induced by carbon tetrachloride or thioacetamide [ ]. However, the pathological role of GDF15 in silicosis remains unclear.
In the present paper, we measured the blood GDF15 levels in patients with silicosis and explored the potential role of GDF15 in silicosis to determine the probable molecular mechanism of action of GDF15 in lung fibroblast activation.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
Recombinant human GDF15 was purchased from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA). Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits for GDF15 were purchased from Abcam (Boston, MA, USA). A miRNA qPCR detection kit was obtained from GeneCopoeia (Rockville, MD, USA). A miRNeasy mini kit was procured from Qiagen (Dusseldorf, German). A revertaid H minus first strand cDNA synthesis kit and SYBR Green master mixture were purchased from Invitrogen (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Primers were provided by Sangon (Shanghai, China). Antibodies against STAT1, Col1a, α-SMA, and β-actin were obtained from Abcam. miR-338 mimics and inhibitors were synthesized by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). STAT1 siRNA and lentivirus vectors carrying the human STAT1 gene were purchased from GenePharma. Lipofectamine 2000 was purchased from Invitrogen. A cell counting kit-8(CCK-8) was procured from Beyotime (Haimen, China). Other common reagents were provided by Beijing Dingguo Biotechnology (Beijing, China).

## ELISA
For ELISA, we used samples from 46 male patients with silicosis and ten healthy controls. Participants were aged between 43 and 78 years at enrolment. The patients were clinically diagnosed with silicosis without other serious diseases, including cancer, myocardial infarction, and pulmonary embolism. Their lung function test results were evaluated by a study physician. The number of patients in phases 1 to 3 in this study was 15, 11, and 20, respectively. The experimental protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Hunan Prevention and Treatment Institute for Occupational Diseases(No: LS2021120801) and the ethics committee declares that written consent has been obtained from all patients and filed for record. Pyrogen-free EDTA collection tubes were used to collect peripheral venous blood samples and centrifuged at 2000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. Plasma was stored in − 80 °C freezers until further analyses (in 2021). The plasma GDF15 levels were detected using ELISA according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Each sample was tested three times.

## Cell culture and treatment
Human embryonic pulmonary fibroblast MRC5 cells, were cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The cells were treated with recombinant human GDF15, as previously described [ ]. MRC5 cells (at a primary density of 2 × 105/mL) were transfected with STAT1 siRNA or STAT1 cDNA. Fifty nanomolar miR-338 mimic or 100 nM miR-338 inhibitor was transfected into MRC5 cells using Lipofectamine 2000. Negative sequences were used at the same concentration as controls. After 6 h of culture post transfection, the cells were placed in fresh DMEM supplemented with antibiotics. Each experiment was performed in triplicate.

## Western blotting
As previously described [ ], cells were homogenized on ice and treated with an appropriate volume of protein lysis buffer. Tissues were centrifuged at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. A bicinchoninic acid reagent kit was used to measure the protein concentration. Discontinuous SDS-PAGE was performed using 20 µg of protein. Proteins were transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane using the semi-dry method. The membrane was blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin for 2 h and incubated overnight with rabbit anti-STAT1, anti-col1a, anti-α-SMA (1:1000), and anti-β-actin antibodies (1:5000) at 4 °C. Following this, the membrane was treated with a horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 h. The results were observed using the ECL reagent. The relative expression levels of STAT1, col1a, and α-SMA were normalized against β-actin. Each experiment was tested three times.

## RNA sequencing
The mRNA and miRNA expression levels in MRC5 cells treated (or not treated) with GDF15 were detected using Illumina HiSeq™(San Diego, CA, USA). TRIzol (Invitrogen, CA, USA) was used to dissolve the cells to extract RNA, and assays were performed by Sangon (Shanghai, China). The investment for SRNA library construction is 800ng/sample, and the investment for mRNA library construction is 1400ng/sample. The miRNA sequencing is a double ended sequencing in PE150 mode. The mRNA sequencing is single ended sequencing in SE75 mode.The raw data obtained from sequencing contains low-quality sequences with adapters. To ensure the quality of information analysis, the raw data was filtered and clean data was obtain based on the characteristics of miRNA and mRNA.Genes with normalized expression ratios of > 2.0 and < 0.5 were defined as upregulated and downregulated, respectively.ClusterProfiler was used for feature enrichment analysis and the top 10 GO are displayed in the result graph.

## RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR
As previously described [ ], total RNA from the cells was isolated and extracted using the TRIzol reagent. miRNA-specific stem-loop primer or oligo dT primers were used to synthesize cDNA using reverse transcriptase. The primers used for miR-338-5p were 5'-GGAACAATATCCTGGTGCTG-3' (forward) and 5'-TTTGGCACTAGCACATT-3' (reverse). The primers used for U6 were 5'-GCTTCGGCAGCACATATACTAAAAT-3' (forward) and 5'-CGCTTCACGAATTTGCGTGTCAT-3' (reverse). qRT-PCR was conducted on the ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). The relative expression level of miR-338-3p was normalized to that of small nuclear RNA U6.

## Cell proliferation assay
MRC5 cells were cultured at a primary density of 2,000 cells per well on a 96-well plate in triplicate. When the cells achieved approximately 70% confluence, they were transfected with different substances. After transfection, the cells were incubated for 24, 48, and 72 h with or without GDF15 stimulation. CCK-8 was used to determine cell proliferation according to the manufacturer’s manual. Each experiment was tested three times.

## Wound-healing scratch assay
MRC5 cells were cultured at a density of 50,000 cells/cm2 in 6-well plates, according to a previously described [ ]. When the cells formed a confluent monolayer, the surface of the cell layer was scratched with a 2 μL pipette tip, and the cells were then washed with PBS to remove cell debris. The scratch was visualized using microscopy and photographed, and the width of the wound was measured at 0, 24, and 48 h. Each experiment was tested three times.

## Luciferase reporter assay
As previously described [ ], the wild and mutant type 3’UTR fragments of human STAT1 were inserted downstream of the firefly luciferase coding region in the pmirGLO dual-luciferase miRNA target expression vector. Primer sequences for Wt-GLO-STAT1-3UTR-F (5–3’):gctagcagtatactgtatttttacctt.Primer sequences for Wt-GLO-STAT1 -3UTR-R (5–3’):tctagattagaaacaatattgttt. Primer sequences for mut-GLO-STAT1-3UTR- (5–3’):tctagattagaacaggtcatgttt. The vectors were co-transfected with miRNA or control mimics into MRC5 cells in a 24-well plate. Luciferase activities were measured at 48 h after transfection using a bioluminescence plate reader. Firefly luciferase activity was normalized using the following formula: Firefly luciferase activity/Renilla luciferase activity.Each experiment was tested three times.

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Comparisons between the two groups were performed using a Student’s t-test or unpaired t-test. Comparisons among three groups were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA). The data represent means ± S.D. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Increase in blood GDF15 level in patients with silicosis
Bioinformatics analysis indicated an increase in GDF15 expression in A549 cells treated with crystalline silica(Supplementary Data and Figure ).To study the role of GDF15 in silicosis, we measured the GDF15 level in the peripheral venous blood samples of patients with silicosis using ELISA. The GDF15 level in 46 patients with silicosis was 396.9 ± 150.6 pg/mL, whereas that in 10 control patients was 212.1 ± 13.66 pg/mL, which represented a statistically significant difference (P = 0.0003) (Fig.  a). 
The FEV1% is an important indicator of lung function. According to the standard used by Pertzov and Ozkaya [ , ], patients were divided into four groups: severe (FEV1 < 40%, n = 6), moderate (FEV1 between 40 and 60, n = 17), mild (FEV1 between 60 and 80, n = 18), and normal (FEV1 > 80%, n = 5). The GDF15 levels in the severe, moderate, mild and normal groups were 359.7 ± 151.9 pg/mL, 417.2 ± 137.2 pg/mL, 424.7 ± 170.4 pg/mL, and 273.6.2 ± 33.5 pg/mL, respectively. There was no statistically significant difference between the GDF15 levels in patients with different lung functions (R2 = 0.1034, P = 0.2002) (Fig.  b).
Based on the results of imaging examinations, the 46 patients with silicosis were divided into stages 1–3 in clinical practice; this included 15 patients in stage 1, 11 patients in stage 2, and 20 patients in stage 3. The GDF15 level were 371.7 ± 139.4 pg/mL, 433.3 ± 165.7 pg/mL, and 395.9 ± 153.9 pg/mL in stage1,2 and 3 patients, respectively. However, no statistically significant difference was observed among the GDF15 levels in patients with stage-1, -2, or -3 disease (R2 = 0.0236,P = 0.5979). The data indicated that the increased serum levels of GDF15 were not associated with the clinical stages (Fig.  c).

## GDF15 activates human embryonic lung fibroblast MRC5 cells
To investigate the effect of GDF15 on human embryonic lung fibroblast MRC5 cells, MRC5 cells were treated with GDF15 with dosage and time course. The expression of α-SMA and col1a was observed using western blotting. In different dosage experiments, the expression levels of α-SMA and col1a were highest at 100 ng/mL. When MRC5 cells were treated with 100 ng/mL of GDF15 for 24 h, the expression of col1a and α-SMA increased most significantly (Fig.  a and b).The CCK-8 assay revealed that GDF15 could induce MRC5 cell proliferation (Fig.  c). The wound-healing scratch assay showed that GDF15 increased MRC5 cell migration (Fig.  d). Thus, our results indicated that GDF15 induced MRC5 cell activation. 

## Effects of GDF15 on mRNA and miRNA expression in MRC5 cells
To study the regulation of GDF15 gene expression in human embryonic lung fibroblast MRC5 cells, the miRNA and mRNA expression profiles of MRC5 cells in the presence or absence of GDF15 were assessed using RNA sequencing.
The differentially expressed miRNAs were identified, which included nine upregulated genes and 18 downregulated genes (compared with those in the control group, genes showing a change in expression ratio greater than twofold or lesser than 0.5-fold change were identified; Fig.  a-c). The upregulated miRNAs included has-miR-6850, has-miR-219a, has-novel-229, and has-novel-230. The top five miRNAs with successively decreasing expression included has-miR-338, has-miR-1, has-miR-133a, has-miR-5002, and has-miR-196a. 
The differentially expressed mRNAs identified consisted of 230 upregulated genes and 238 downregulated genes (compared with those in the control group, genes showing a change in expression ratio greater than twofold or a lesser than 0.5-fold were identified, Fig.  d-f). The upregulated genes included PLD3, CCNA2, MYD88, MARK2, NLRP1, STAT1, and VMP1. The downregulated genes included ZNF219, OGDH, ADD1, SMARCD2, LIPA, FHL1, and ATP5F1.

## GDF15 reduced the expression of miR-338 in MRC5 cells
To explore the role of miR-338 in fibroblast activation induced by GDF15, we first detected the expression of miR-338 in MRC5 cells with or without GDF15 treatment using qRT-PCR. Our data showed that GDF15 significantly reduced miR-338 expression in MRC5 cells (P < 0.05, Fig.  a). Following this, miR-338 mimics or inhibitors was transfected into MRC5 cells. Our data showed that miR-338 mimics reduced the expression of col1a and α-SMA in MRC5 cells both with and without GFD15 treatment. However, the miR-338 inhibitor promoted col1a and α-SMA expression in MRC5 cells with and without GFD15 treatment (Fig.  b). CCK-8 assay data showed that miR-338 mimics inhibited MRC5 cell proliferation with and without GDF15 treatment. Meanwhile, the miR-338 inhibitor stimulated MRC5 cell proliferation with and without GDF15 treatment (Fig.  c). The results indicated that miR-338 played an important role in fibroblast activation induced by GDF15. 

## GDF15 increased the expression of STAT1 in MRC5 cells
To determine the role of STAT1 in GDF15-induced fibroblast activation, we first assessed the expression of STAT1 in MRC5 cells with GDF15 treatment using western blotting. Our data showed that GDF15 significantly increased STAT1 expression in MRC5 cells (P < 0.05, Fig.  a). Following this, STAT1 siRNA and cDNA were transfected into MRC5 cells. The data showed that STAT1 siRNA diminished the expression of col1a and α-SMA in MRC5 cells in the presence or absence of GDF15 treatment. However, STAT1 cDNA promoted the expression of col1a and α-SMA in MRC5 cells in the presence and absence of GDF15 treatment (Fig.  b). CCK-8 assay data showed that STAT1 siRNA inhibited MRC5 cell proliferation in the presence and absence of GDF15 treatment. Meanwhile, STAT1 cDNA stimulated MRC5 cell proliferation in the presence and absence of GDF15 treatment (Fig.  c). The data indicated that STAT1 played a significant role in GDF15-induced fibroblast activation. 

## GDF15 activates MRC5 cells through miR-338/STAT1 pathway
According to bioinformatics analysis, we could speculate that miR-338 could bind to the 3’UTR of the STAT1 mRNA. To investigate the role miR-338 in STAT1 expression, the normal or mutant binding site of miR-338 in 3’UTR of the STAT1 was cloned into the pmirGLO dual-luciferase miRNA target expression vector. The upregulation of miR-338 weakened the luciferase activity of the wild-type STAT1 3’UTR reporter gene, while it did not alter the activity of the mutant reporter gene (Fig.  a). Following this, the effect of miR-338 on STAT1 expression in MRC5 cells was detected using western blotting. Our data indicated that miR-338 mimics reduced STAT1 expression, whereas miR-338 inhibitors increased STAT1 expression (Fig.  b). The findings indicated that STAT1 might be a direct target of miR-338. 
MRC5 cells were co-transfected with miR-338 mimics and STAT1 cDNA. The western blotting results showed that miR-338 mimics reversed the expression of col1a and α-SMA induced by STAT1 upregulation (Fig.  c). When MRC5 cells were co-transfected with miR-338 inhibitors and STAT1 siRNA, the miR-338 inhibitors was found to reverse col1a and α-SMA expression induced by decreased STAT1 expression (Fig.  d). Thus, our data indicated that GDF15 might activate MRC5 cells via miR-338/STAT1.

## Discussion
GDF15 is widely distributed in mammalian tissues and plays important roles in different conditions [ – ]. Studies showed smoking induced GDF15 expression in human lung epithelial cells [ – ]. Recent studies have shown that GDF15 levels are associated with the severity of COVID-19 [ ]. However, the effect of GDF15 in silicosis has not been reported yet. In this study, we first measured the serum GDF15 level in patients with silicosis. Our results showed that the plasma GDF15 levels in patients with silicosis are higher than those in healthy individuals. Our data indicated that increased serum GDF15 levels were not related to clinical stages and FEV1% in patients with silicosis. We need more data to confirm the relationship between GDF15 expression and clinical parameters in future studies.
Kim et al. reported that GDF15 was a bonafide regulator for preventing the excessive proliferation and activation of fibroblasts in injured lungs [ ]. Evidence from many studies indicated that GDF15 could promote the development of pulmonary fibrosis. GDF15 was found to be a potential therapeutic target in pulmonary fibrosis and facilitated lung fibrosis by activating macrophages and fibroblasts [ , ]. Consistent with previous studies,our data showed that exogenous GDF15 increased α-SMA and col1a expression and induced the proliferation of MRC5 cells. To study the mechanisms of activation of fibroblasts by GDF15, we profiled the regulation of miRNA and mRNA expression by exogenous GDF15 in MRC5 cells. Then miR-338 and STAT1 attracted our great interest.
Reportedly, miR-338 is an anti-fibrotic miRNA that inhibits hepatic stellate cell activation and proliferation via the CDK4 signaling pathway [ ].CircHIPK3 regulated fibroblast proliferation by acting as a miR-338 sponge in bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis [ ]. miR-338 suppressed cardiac fibroblast activation, proliferation, and migration by directly targeting FGFR2(fibroblast growth factor receptor 2) in mice [ ]. In pulmonary fibrosis, miR-338 inhibited myofibroblast differentiation through the induction of PTEN(phosphatase and tensin homolog deleted on chromosome ten) [ ]. Earlier studies have shown that miR-338 can inhibit the progression of pulmonary fibrosis by suppressing the expression of LPA1 or EMT [ , ].Our results showed GDF15 significantly reduced miR-338 expression, and miR-338 participated in the GDF15-induced activation of MRC5 cells.These data indicate that miR-338 may play a vital role as an anti-fibrotic gene in organ fibrosis. However, the role of miR-338 in silicosis needs further elucidation.
Previous evidence indicates that STAT1 plays a critical role in the inflammatory response and tumorigenesis [ , ]. Indeed, STAT1 is also involved in organ fibrosis. STAT1 antisense oligodeoxynucleotides can ameliorate rat lung fibrosis [ ]. STAT1 inhibition suppressed the progression of liver fibrosis [ ]. STAT signaling plays a vital role in renal fibrosis, and STAT activation could induce EMT and contribute to renal damage [ , ]. In this study, we found that GDF15 increased STAT1 expression in MRC5 cells, and STAT1 participated in the activation of MRC5 cells by GDF15. Combined with bioinformatics analysis and RNA sequencing data, the results indicated that STAT1 might be a target of miR-338. Luciferase reporter assay and rescue experiment data confirmed this hypothesis.
In summary, our results indicate that GDF15 plays an important role in the pathogenesis of silicosis and GDF15 activates fibroblasts through the miR-338/STAT1 pathway in MRC5 cells. Therefore, GDF15 may be a promising target for silicosis treatment.However, our research needs to be further deepened.. Although we provide data on the increase of GDF15 in patient serum, animal experiments will provide more support to demonstrate the role of GDF15 in silicosis. Given the role of miR-338 in organ fibrosis, observing the expression of miR-338 in silicosis animal models will be beneficial for validating our results.
