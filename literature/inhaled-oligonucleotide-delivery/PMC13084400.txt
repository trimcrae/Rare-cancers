## Summary
Here, we present a protocol for gene knockdown in Trichoplax adhaerens using a silica nanoparticle-mediated RNA interference (RNAi) technique. We describe steps for preparing silica nanoparticles, assembling RNAi complexes, and delivering them to live specimens. This protocol enables reproducible suppression of target gene expression in T. adhaerens and achieves transcript knockdown efficiencies of approximately 50%–70%, as assessed by quantitative PCR.
For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Li et al.

## Before you begin

## Innovation
RNA interference (RNAi) is a widely used approach for investigating gene function through sequence-specific suppression of gene expression. However, experimental tools for functional genetic analysis in Trichoplax adhaerens remain limited. Here, we describe a silica nanoparticle–mediated RNAi method that enables reproducible gene knockdown in Trichoplax.
The protocol provides a straightforward and easy-to-follow workflow that includes the preparation of silica nanoparticles, the assembly of RNAi complexes, and their delivery to Trichoplax. By enabling the delivery of RNAi molecules to live specimens, the method supports functional studies of target genes in T. adhaerens. Using this approach, we achieve typical transcript suppression efficiencies of approximately 50–70%, as assessed by quantitative PCR. By organizing these steps into a single workflow, the protocol provides a practical method for RNAi experiments in Trichoplax.

## Synthesis of spiky silica nanoparticles

Timing: ∼18 h

1.Prepare the reaction mixture for SSN synthesis.a.Add 10 mL of distilled water, 40 mL of ethanol (EtOH), 1.56 mL of aqueous ammonia solution (NH3·H2O, 28–30%), and 0.225 mL of ethylenediamine (EDA, ≥99%) into a 250 mL round-bottom flask.
b.Stir the mixture continuously at 600 rpm with a magnetic stir bar in a round-bottom flask immersed in an oil bath with an integrated magnetic stirrer.
c.Maintain the temperature at 60°C.

Note: Ammonia solution and EDA are corrosive and volatile; perform the operation in a fume hood and avoid inhalation. Ethanol is flammable. Keep it away from ignition sources.

2.Following 5 min of stirring, add 1.74 mL of tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS, ≥99%) dropwise under continued stirring.

CRITICAL: Ensure the solution reaches and stabilizes at 60°C, then add TEOS to achieve controlled nucleation.

Note: TEOS has limited solubility in the ethanol-water mixture. Add TEOS quickly under stirring to prevent local over-concentration and non-uniform hydrolysis.

3.After an additional 5 min, under continued stirring, add 3-aminophenol (0.4125 g, 3.8 mmol) to the reaction mixture, followed by 0.9 mL of formaldehyde solution (37 wt%).
4.Allow the reaction to proceed at 60°C for 5 h under continuous magnetic stirring at 600 rpm in a fume hood.

CRITICAL: Maintain stable temperature and stirring speed. Shorter reaction times produce incomplete spikes, whereas reaction times longer than 6 h result in aggregation.

5.Using 10 mL conical polypropylene(PP) centrifuge tubes, collect the nanoparticles by centrifugation at 9,600 × g for 30 min at 20–25°C.a.Wash the precipitate three times with anhydrous ethanol and dry it in a vacuum drying oven at 60°C for 12 h.
b.Transfer the dried sample into a porcelain crucible and heat it in a muffle furnace under air atmosphere from 20–25°C to 600°C at a rate of 2°C/min.
c.Maintain the temperature for 5 h, then allow the sample to cool naturally to 20–25°C.

Note: Ethanol washings are flammable-dispose of properly. Calcination occurs at very high temperatures; handle the sample using heat-resistant gloves and tongs.

CRITICAL: Perform all operations involving dried silica nanoparticle powders—including opening the tube, transferring, scraping, or weighing—in a fume hood or ventilated enclosure. Handle powders gently to minimize aerosolization. Use wet wiping or HEPA-filtered vacuuming for cleanup, and avoid dry sweeping or the use of compressed air.

## APTES modification of SSNs

Timing: ∼36 h

6.Prepare the SSN suspension in anhydrous ethanol.a.Disperse 24 mg of SSNs in 3 mL of anhydrous ethanol.
b.Stir the suspension at 20–25°C under continuous magnetic stirring at 600 rpm for 24 h.

7.Functionalize SSNs with (3-aminopropyl) triethoxysilane (APTES).a.Add (3-aminopropyl) triethoxysilane (APTES) to the suspension.
b.Stir at 20–25°C under continuous magnetic stirring at 600 rpm for 24 h.

8.Purify the APTES-modified SSNs.a.Collect the APTES-modified SSNs by centrifugation in 10 mL conical polypropylene centrifuge tubes at 10,000 × g for 8 min.
b.Remove the supernatant, and wash the precipitate three times by dispersing in anhydrous ethanol followed by centrifugation.
c.Dry the product in a vacuum drying oven at 60°C for 12 h to obtain SSN-NH2.

CRITICAL: Wash the SSNs thoroughly to remove unreacted APTES; incomplete washing may result in surface passivation and nanoparticle aggregation.

Note: Store SSN–NH2 in a desiccator to prevent hydrolysis of surface amino groups.

## Characterization, reproducibility, and quality control of SSNs

Timing: ∼2 h

9.Redisperse SSNs or amine-functionalized SSNs (SSN–NH2) immediately prior to characterization to obtain a homogeneous suspension.a.Vortex the suspension or sonicate briefly for 1–2 min.
b.Perform the redispersion immediately before characterization to ensure a homogeneous suspension. Under proper dispersion conditions, no visible precipitation or large aggregates appear during subsequent measurements.

CRITICAL: Insufficient dispersion may lead to inaccurate dynamic light scattering (DLS) or zeta potential measurements.

10.For morphological characterization, deposit a small aliquot of the SSN suspension onto a conductive substrate, air-dried, and imaged using scanning electron microscopy (SEM).

Note: SSNs synthesized using this protocol typically display uniform spherical morphology with minimal aggregation and a primary particle diameter of approximately 200–250 nm.

11.Measure the hydrodynamic diameter and size distribution of SSNs by dynamic light scattering (DLS) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Note: Properly synthesized SSNs generally exhibit a hydrodynamic diameter within the range of 200–300 nm (typically ∼250 ± 50 nm), with a polydispersity index (PDI) ≤ 0.40, indicating acceptable dispersity.

12.Measure the zeta potential of the samples under identical buffer and temperature conditions before and after amine functionalization.

Note: Unmodified SSNs typically show a negative zeta potential (≤−10 mV), whereas SSN–NH2 exhibits a positive surface charge (≥+10 mV), reflecting a clear negative-to-positive charge reversal following amine functionalization.

## Design of siRNA

Timing: ∼1–2 h (sequence design and ordering only)

13.Design and obtain siRNA sequences.a.Design and synthesize siRNAs using GenePharma (Shanghai, China).Note: Each siRNA is 21 nucleotides long, comprising a 19-bp double-stranded region with two-nucleotide 3′ UU overhangs, ensuring target specificity.

b.Select siRNA target sequences based on the published Trichoplax adhaerens reference genome released in 2008. Scrambled siRNA (non-targeting) serves as a negative control.
c.Store synthesized siRNAs at −80°C in aliquots to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles in RNase-free water at a stock concentration of 20 μM.

## Design of shRNA

Timing: ∼1–3 h (sequence design and ordering only)

14.Design two to three target sequences (19–21 bp) for each gene following a protocol described in He et al, with minor modifications to the design procedure.

CRITICAL: Select target sites with GC content between 40% and 55%, and prioritize those with lower GC density at the 3′ terminus.

Note: A scrambled shRNA, which lacks any significant homology to the Trichoplax genome, serves as the negative control.

15.Design the full-length shRNA expression constructs consisting of the following ( ):a.5′-T7 promoter sequence (TAATACGACTCACTATA).
b.Sense strand of the target sequence.
c.Short hairpin loop (TTCAAGAGA).
d.Antisense strand of the target sequence.
e.3′-terminal TT transcription termination sequence.

16.Predict the secondary structure.a.Predict the secondary structure of the designed full-length shRNA using the structure prediction website (https://www.unafold.org/mfold/applications/rna-folding-form-v2.php).
b.Use the default parameters for structure prediction.
c.Retain only candidates that form a single, well-defined hairpin structure.
d.Discard sequences showing obvious misfolding.

17.Synthesize forward and reverse primers and anneal them to produce a double-stranded DNA transcription template for shRNA synthesis ( ).a.Prepare the primers as a stock solution of 50 μM.
b.Mix 5 μL of each primer in a PCR tube to a final volume of 10 μL.
c.Denature at 98°C for 5 min in a thermal cycler, then cool to 24°C at a rate of 5°C/s.

 

## shRNA synthesis

Timing: ∼5 h

18.Perform in vitro transcription to synthesize shRNA using T7 RNA polymerase. Prepare the reaction master mix as follows.

 

a.Incubate the reaction mixture at 37°C for 3 h. Verify synthesized shRNA by gel electrophoresis.Note: The theoretical length of the synthetic shRNA is approximately 50 bp. However, due to the hairpin structure of shRNA, the gel electrophoresis length is approximately 70–100 bp.

b.Incubate at 72°C for 10 min, then allow to cool naturally.
c.Add 1 μL DNase I and incubate at 37°C for 30 min to digest DNA templates. Re-verify the shRNA by gel electrophoresis.Note: Use RNase-free pipette tips throughout shRNA synthesis to prevent RNA degradation (see ).

19.Purify the synthesized shRNA.a.Remove RNA Clean Beads from 4°C and equilibrate at 20–25°C for 30 min. Vortex thoroughly before use.
b.Add 80 μL bead suspension and 200 μL isopropanol to the transcription reaction mixture. Mix thoroughly by pipetting at least 10 times.Note: Add an additional 200 μL isopropanol for transcripts <100 bp in length.

c.Incubate at 20–25°C for 5 min for RNA-bead binding.
d.Place PCR tube on magnetic stand for 5 min.
e.Carefully remove supernatant.
f.With the tube stable on magnetic stand, add 200 μL freshly prepared 80% ethanol (avoid disturbing beads). Incubate for 30 s at 20–25°C. Carefully remove supernatant. Repeat this wash once.
g.Air-dry beads with tube open for 5-10 min.
h.Remove tube from magnetic stand. Add 20-40 μL RNase-free H2O. Mix thoroughly by pipetting and incubate at 20–25°C for 5 min.
i.Return tube to magnetic stand for 5 min.
j.Carefully transfer supernatant to new nuclease-free tube.
k.Measure RNA concentration using a NanoPhotometer spectrophotometer and store all shRNA in aliquots at −80°C to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.Note: Both siRNA and shRNA are suitable for mediating gene knockdown using this protocol and show comparable knockdown efficiency. While siRNAs typically require commercial synthesis, shRNA can be readily generated in the laboratory, making them a more cost-effective option. In practice, individual shRNA can be tested initially, and, if necessary, multiple shRNA may be co-transfected with SSN reagents to enhance knockdown efficiency in Trichoplax.

## Sterilized seawater

•Measure the salinity using a salinity meter and adjust it to 30 PSU.
•Adjust the pH to 8.0–8.4 with sterile 1 M NaOH or HCl.

## Step-by-step method details

## Collection of Trichoplax adhaerens

Timing: ∼12–18 h

This step collects and prepares starved Trichoplax adhaerens for subsequent transfection. Starvation enhances uptake activity, increasing internalization of the SSN–RNAi complex and improving experimental reproducibility.
1.Place Trichoplax animals in 35 mm dishes containing fresh, sterilized seawater without algae for 12–18 h.
2.Seed 20–30 starved animals per well in a RNase-free 24-well plate, adjusting the volume to 200 μL with fresh sterilized seawater.
3.Allow animals to fully adhere to the bottom surface of the wells ( ).
 

## Preparation of the SSN transfection mixture

Timing: ∼30 min

This step prepares the SSN–RNAi complex by combining silica nanoparticles with siRNA or shRNA. Proper resuspension and incubation are critical for efficient delivery and knockdown.
4.Resuspend SSNs (10 μg/μL, stored at 4°C) by sonication or vortex mixing for 1–2 min.
Note: SSN may settle at the bottom of the tube. Vortex or sonicate thoroughly before use to ensure complete resuspension and accurate pipetting.
5.Retrieve shRNA or synthesized siRNA from the −80°C freezer and immediately place on ice to prevent degradation.
Note: Re-measuring the concentration of shRNA prior to transfection helps ensure accurate dosing and optimal knockdown efficiency (see and ).
6.In a RNase-free PCR tube, mix 1–2 μg of shRNA or siRNA with 5 μL SSNs.
Note: The maximum binding capacity is approximately 4 μg of shRNA or siRNA per 10 μL of SSNs. Mixing multiple shRNAs or siRNAs with SSNs enables simultaneous knockdown of multiple homologous genes (see ).
7.Mix thoroughly by pipetting and incubate at 20–25°C for 15–20 min ( ).
 

## Gene knockdown using SSN transfection

Timing: ∼2 days

This step delivers the SSN–RNAi complex into Trichoplax and monitors knockdown effects over two days.
8.Premix the SSNs transfection mixture with 5 μL of fresh, sterilized seawater.
Note: Premixing helps avoid abrupt osmotic changes upon addition to the culture and can help maintain the stability of shRNA/siRNA (see , and ).
9.Gently add the SSN–shRNA (or SSN–siRNA) mixture to the wells containing animals.
10.Gently swirl the 24-well plate to ensure uniform distribution of the SSN particles.
11.Examine transfected animals and replace the seawater.a.Check the transfected animals 24 h after treatment under a microscope to assess adhesion and morphology.
b.Carefully replace 50 μL of the supernatant with fresh, sterilized seawater to ensure that subsequent procedures proceed normally ( ).

Note: Exclude animals displaying abnormal morphology or impaired adhesion after adding the SSN-RNAi complex from downstream analysis (see ).
12.Collect transfected animals for further analysis ( ).a.At 48 h post-transfection, add 1 mL of fresh sterilized seawater to each well, then gently transfer the animals into a new 35-mm dish containing fresh sterilized seawater.
b.Add 200 μL of algae to the dish.
c.After 2 h of stabilization, proceed to downstream analysis.

Note: Animals in the negative control group receive the SSN-scrambled shRNA mixture under the same experimental conditions.

## Expected outcomes
Virus-mimetic SSNs have been demonstrated to be a highly efficient and biocompatible carrier for siRNA transfection. When prepared following the synthesis method described in this protocol, SSNs are expected to exhibit uniform nanoscale morphology and good dispersibility, as observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM). SEM imaging typically shows well-dispersed particles with uniform morphology and nanoscale dimensions ( A). Consistently, dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis is expected to show a narrow size distribution, while zeta potential measurements generally indicate a stable surface charge including a clear negative-to-positive shift after amine functionalization ( B and 7C).
 A successful RNAi experiment is expected to result in a significant reduction in the mRNA level of the target gene(s) compared with the control group. The extent of mRNA reduction depends primarily on the efficiency of RNAi and the intrinsic stability of the transcript. Here, we show that both SSN-mediated shRNA or siRNA knocking down can significantly decrease the expression of two ciliary genes, DNAAF3 and IFT88 ( A). , Further defects in ciliary motility or ciliogenesis can be observed in transfected animals ( B and 8C). 
 

## Quantification and statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9. Data are presented as the mean ± SD. Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For comparisons between two groups, p-values were determined using a two-tailed Student’s t test, with significance indicated as ∗∗∗ (p < 0.001) and ∗∗∗∗ (p < 0.0001). Data inclusion criteria were based on successful amplification or measurement, and any technical failures were excluded from analysis.

## Limitations
In this study, we established a gene knockdown method in Trichoplax adhaerens, a representative species of the phylum Placozoa. It is noteworthy that the knockdown efficiency typically reaches approximately 50–70% for most target genes, and achieving a stronger reduction in gene expression remains challenging. This limitation is likely attributable to the small cell size of Trichoplax (approximately 3–5 μm) and the generally low expression levels of many genes. Consequently, knockdown efficiency is variable and not uniform across different cell types. Alternatively, repeated dosing regimens or the use of alternative nanoparticle formulations with improved delivery properties may further enhance knockdown efficiency. Finally, because this approach results in only transient suppression of gene expression, the development of strategies that enable long-term or stable gene silencing will be necessary in future studies. Nevertheless, in most cases, this partial knockdown is sufficient to elicit detectable phenotypic changes.

## Troubleshooting

## Problem 1
RNA degradation during experimental handling (related to the ‘shRNA synthesis’ section and Step 6 and 8).

## Potential solution

•Seawater contains RNases that can degrade shRNA; therefore, premix SSNs and shRNA prior to use.
•Disinfect all work surfaces with ethanol before experiments, and maintain a clean working environment throughout.
•Wear RNase-free gloves, dedicated lab coats, and masks to minimize RNase contamination from users.
•Use certified RNase-free consumables and RNase-free water for all RNA-related steps.
•Keep RNA samples on ice to reduce RNase activity and prevent degradation.

## Problem 2
The qPCR results are inconsistent across biological replicates (related to Step 15).

## Potential solution

•Be cautious for RNA degradation to maintain consistent knockdown efficiency.
•Increase the number of experimental replicates.
•For samples with fewer than 20 individuals, use a micro-scale RNA extraction kit to obtain sufficient RNA yield and concentration.

## Problem 3
Low gene knockdown efficiency (related to Step 6 and 8).

## Potential solution

•Re-measure the concentrations and quality of shRNA prior to transfection to ensure accurate dosing and RNA integrity.
•If knockdown remains weak, perform a second round of transfection after the initial treatment to improve silencing efficiency.

## Problem 4
Incorrect water changing procedures during 24-hour seawater replacement may cause physical damage of animals and SSNs loss (related to Step 11).

## Potential solution
Gently dislodge animals adhering to walls to the bottom using a pipette. Wait 5 min, then perform partial seawater replacement: aspirate 50 μL of the supernatant only. Gently pipette to avoid removal of SSNs.

## Problem 5
Presence of redundant homologous genes (related to Step 6).

## Potential solution
Perform simultaneous knockdown of multiple homologous genes by mixing multiple shRNAs or siRNAs with SSNs. Verify the knockdown efficiency of each individual gene before proceeding to downstream analysis.
