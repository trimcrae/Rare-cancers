## Highlights
•RAMs played a pivotal role in resolving inflammation during LPS and/or E.coli-induced ARDS.
•RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis in an ALX/MAPK14/S100A8/A9-dependent manner.
•Plasma RvD1 and S100A8/A9 levels were negatively correlated, and associated with the outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS.

## Introduction
Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a pulmonary inflammatory process primarily caused by sepsis. The resolution of inflammation is an active process involving the endogenous biosynthesis of specialized pro-resolving mediators, including resolvin D1 (RvD1). Resident alveolar macrophages (RAMs) maintain pulmonary homeostasis and play a key role in the resolution phase. However, the role of RAMs in promoting the resolution of inflammation by RvD1 is unclear.

## Objectives
Here, we investigated the mechanisms of RvD1 on regulating RAMs to promote the resolution of ARDS.

## Methods
Mice were administered lipopolysaccharide and/or Escherichia coli via aerosol inhalation to establish a self-limited ARDS model. Then, RvD1 was administered at the peak inflammatory response. RAMs self-renewal was measured by flow cytometry, RAM phagocytosis was measured by two-photon fluorescence imaging. In addition, plasma was collected from intensive care unit patients on days 0–2, 3–5, and 6–9 to measure RvD1 and S100A8/A9 levels using triple quadrupole/linear ion trap mass spectrometry.

## Results
RAMs were found to play a pivotal role in resolving inflammation during ARDS, and RvD1 enhanced RAM proliferation and phagocytosis, which was abrogated by a lipoxin A4 receptor (ALX, RvD1 receptor) inhibitor. Both primary RAMs transfected with rS100A8/A9 and/or S100A8/A9 siRNA and S100A9-/- mice (also deficient in S100A8 function) showed higher turnover and phagocytic function, indicating that RvD1 exerted its effects on RAMs by inhibiting S100A8/A9 production in the resolution phase. RvD1 reduced S100A8/A9 and its upstream MAPK14 levels in vivo and in vitro. Finally, in the patients, RvD1 levels were lower, but S100A8/A9 levels were higher.

## Conclusions
We propose that RvD1 improved RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis via the ALX/MAPK14/S100A8/A9 signaling pathway. Plasma RvD1 and S100A8/A9 levels were negatively correlated, and associated with the outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS.

## Background
Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a pulmonary inflammatory process mainly caused by sepsis and is associated with high morbidity and mortality worldwide . It is characterized by extensive neutrophil infiltration in the lungs . Thus, the timely and effective removal of these cells is critical for minimizing damage to the surrounding tissue and alleviating inflammation. Recent studies showed that inflammatory resolution is an active process involving the endogenous biosynthesis of specialized pro-resolving mediators, such as resolvin D1 (RvD1) . RvD1 has been proven to interact with the lipoxin A4 receptor (ALX) to actively promote inflammatory resolution by enhancing the clearance of cellular debris, apoptotic polymorphonuclear leucocytes (PMNs), and microbes by macrophages , .
Two kinds of macrophages have been identified: resident alveolar macrophages (RAMs) and recruited macrophages . RAMs are established during embryogenesis and undergo rapid self-renewal when they are damaged , . They not only initiate local inflammatory processes but also maintain tissue homeostasis by surveillance of the local microenvironment and the phagocytosis of foreign microbes and dead cells , . However, the role of RAMs in promoting the resolution of inflammation by RvD1 is unclear.
S100A8 and S100A9 are members of the S100 family of calcium-binding proteins . S100A8 and S100A9 form non-covalent complexes, wherein S100A8 is the active component, and S100A9 exerts a regulatory function and protects S100A8 from degradation . S100A8/A9 modulates the polymerization of microtubules during the migration of phagocytes via the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway , .
We hypothesize that RAMs play a pivotal role in resolving inflammation during lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and Escherichia coli (E. coli)-induced ARDS, and their self-renewal and phagocytic function during the resolution phase are upregulated by RvD1 in an ALX/MAPK14/S100A8/A9-dependent manner. Our secondary hypothesis is that plasma RvD1 levels in sepsis-induced ARDS patients are negatively correlated with elevated plasma S100A8/A9 levels and are associated with the outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS.

## Materials and methods

## Reagents
Details regarding the reagents used are given in the .

## Establishment of mouse model of lung injury
Male C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Zhejiang Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., and S100A9-KO mice were obtained from Cyagen Biosciences. Details regarding the establishment of the mouse model of lung injury are given in the .

## Ethics statement
All experiments involving animals were conducted according to the ethical policies and procedures approved by the ethics committee of the Faculty of Pharmacy, Cairo University, Egypt (Approval no. MI-2364). Human trial was approved by the Second Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (Approval no. LCKY2020-36). All Patients signed consent forms.

## Pathological examination
Harvested lung lobes were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Details regarding histopathological analysis are given in the .

## Flow cytometry and cell sorting
BALF was obtained by lavaging the lungs with 500 µL of cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing EDTA (2 nM). Cells freshly isolated from BALF samples were diluted to 1 × 106 cells/100 µL and incubated with fluorochrome-labeled Ly6G, F4/80, CD11b, and CD11c antibodies. Details regarding flow cytometry and cell sorting are given in the .

## RNA-sequencing
Total RNA was isolated from the RAMs and enriched using the NEB Next® Poly(A) mRNA Magnetic Isolation Module to remove rRNA. Details regarding RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) are given in the .

## Transient transfection
Primary RAMs were transfected with negative control small interfering RNA (siRNA, siNeg) or siRNAs targeting S100A8/S100A9 according to the manufacturer's protocol (RiboBio Guangzhou, China). Details regarding transient transfection are given in the .

## Western blots
Protein was extracted from cells and/or tissues and separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide-gel electrophoresis, then transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. The blots were then incubated overnight with the primary antibodies and the appropriate secondary antibodies. Details regarding Western Blots are given in the .

## Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction analysis
Total RNA was extracted from alveolar macrophages using the Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen) and quantified using a NanoDrop 1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Details regarding the quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) assay are given in the .

## Phagocytosis assay
RAMs were seeded on 12-well Permanox slides (Nunc Lab-Tek Chamber Slide System; Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a density of 3 × 105 cells/well. Details regarding the reagents used in the phagocytosis assay are given in the .

## Efferocytosis assay
PMNs were obtained from BALF collected from the mice. RAMs were incubated with apoptotic neutrophils (1:4) for 90 mins after incubation with LPS/IFN-γ with or without RvD1 for 24 h. Details regarding the reagents used for the efferocytosis assay are given in the .

## Two-photon imaging
The mice were anesthetized with 1 % pentobarbital sodium and then injected intratracheally with FITC-Ly6G or PE-Siglec-F (0.5 µg/mouse) for 30 min to label neutrophils or RAMs, respectively. Details regarding the reagents used for two-photon imaging are given in the .

## Measurement of plasma RvD1
Plasma RvD1 levels were measured by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Briefly, RvD1-d5 was added to each sample as an internal standard, and ethyl acetate (ethyl acetate/sample, 4:1 vol/vol) was used to extract specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators (SPMs). Details regarding the reagents used to measure plasma RvD1 levels are given in the .

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism Software (version 9.1.2). Paired/unpaired Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test was used for pairwise and multiple comparisons, respectively. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results

## Established self-limited murine ARDS model and RAMs play pivotal roles in resolving LPS-induced ARDS
We established a reliable self-limited experimental model to facilitate investigations into the role of RAMs in the resolution of LPS-induced ARDS. Mice were administered a patented LPS (1 mg/kg) product ( ) via aerosol inhalation, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected from 0 h to 144 h ( A). Since the key histological event in inflammatory resolution is the loss of PMNs, the infiltration of Ly6G+ neutrophils was analyzed. The infiltration of neutrophils peaked at 72 h and decreased quickly, tending to reach a steady state in vivo ( B). The resolution interval (Ri) was used to define the time period wherein the number of neutrophils in BALF decreased from the peak level to 50 % (T50) , , . The maximum number of neutrophils (Ψmax) during the self-limiting response was 3.2 x 106 cells/murine exudate and was achieved at 72 h (Tmax), with an Ri of 48 h, suggesting that the self-limited murine model was successfully established ( B). 
Clodronate liposomes were used to deplete RAMs to determine the role of RAMs in the inflammatory process ( C). RAMs were defined as F4/80+ CD11blow CD11chigh and recruited macrophages were defined as F4/80+ CD11bhigh CD11clow ( ). The recruited macrophages peaked at 72 h, while the number of RAMs remained largely constant during the course of inflammation ( ). However, removal of the RAMs delayed the Ri by 50 % from 48 h to 72 h ( D), indicating that RAMs played a pivotal role in the resolution of LPS-induced ARDS.
We administered RvD1 at the peak inflammatory response time (72 h after LPS administration) to observe the effect of RvD1 on inflammatory resolution ( E). RvD1 inhibited neutrophils infiltration in a dose-dependent manner. A dose of 100 ng/mouse of RvD1 was used in the following experiments ( ). RvD1 reduced the Ri by 50 % from 48 h to 24 h ( E), suggesting that RvD1 promoted the resolution of inflammation in vivo. In addition, RvD1 significantly mitigated pulmonary pathological changes ( F) and decreased acute lung injury scores ( G) compared to untreated animals, suggesting that RvD1 exerted a protective effect in vivo. However, the beneficial effect of RvD1 on inhibiting neutrophils was abolished after removing RAMs ( H), indicating that RvD1-accelerated inflammatory resolution was dependent on RAMs.

## RvD1 increases the self-renewal of RAMs in LPS-induced ARDS
Because RvD1-promoted resolution depended on RAMs, the next question was how RvD1 regulates RAMs in vivo. We found that RvD1 increased the number of BALF RAMs during the resolution phase ( A). Ki67 is the main marker of proliferation . Thus, we stained cells with Ki67 and found that RvD1 increased the percentage of Ki67+ RAMs ( B). Two-photon fluorescence imaging, a powerful tool for monitoring live murine dynamics in vivo, was used in the present study to further confirm this phenomenon, ( ). RvD1 significantly increased the number of Siglec-F-labeled RAMs ( C), indicating that RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal. 

## RvD1 increases the phagocytic function of RAMs in LPS-induced ARDS
Given the central role of phagocytosis in inflammatory resolution, we next analyzed the effect of RvD1 on the phagocytic ability of RAMs. RvD1 enhanced the uptake of apoptotic neutrophils by RAMs during the resolution phase ( D). Two-photon fluorescence imaging showed that RvD1 enhanced the phagocytosis of Ly6G+ neutrophils ( C) and fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC-LPS) ( E). RvD1 significantly increased the expression of the CD206 and Arg1 ( F, 2G) and decreased CD86 and iNOS ( H, 2I) on RAMs. Taken together, these findings demonstrated that RvD1 improved the phagocytic function of RAMs in LPS-induced ARDS.

## RvD1 enhances the self-renewal and phagocytic function of RAMs in E. coli-induced ARDS
We established a reliable E. coli-induced self-limited model to further clarify the pro-resolution effect of RvD1. Mice were treated with E. coli (10−6) by aerosol inhalation and BALF was collected from 0 h to 120 h ( A). The infiltration of neutrophils peaked at 24 h and decreased quickly ( B), and the colony-forming units (CFUs) of E. coli in lung tissues peaked at 24 h ( C). RvD1 was administered at the peak of inflammation (24 h after E. coli administration) ( D). We found that RvD1 also reduced the Ri by 50 % from 48 h to 24 h ( E), suggesting that RvD1 promoted the resolution of inflammation in E. coli-induced ARDS. RvD1 also reduced the CFUs of E. coli in lung tissues ( F), enhanced the number of BALF RAMs ( G) and the percentage of Ki67+ RAMs ( H) in E. coli-induced ARDS, and promoted the uptake of FITC-E. coli by RAMs ( I), indicating that RvD1 improved the self-renewal and phagocytic function of RAMs in E. coli-induced ARDS.

## RvD1 downregulates S100A8/A9 expression on RAMs in vivo
We performed transcriptional profiling experiments to further characterize the mechanism by which RvD1 promoted the proliferation and phagocytosis of RAMs. RNA-seq analysis of RAMs revealed that 878 genes were significantly upregulated in LPS-treated mice compared to the control group. Twenty-six downregulated genes were observed in the LPS + RvD1 group versus the LPS group. There were seven common genes, including Ly6i, Ormi, S100A8, C1s1, Inhba, Glipr1, and GM43302 ( A-B). Principal component analysis of these differentially expressed genes (DEGs) showed that the LPS and LPS + RvD1 groups were distinct ( C). Gene ontology (GO) analysis of the upregulated genes showed significant enrichment in cell differentiation. In contrast, the downregulated genes were enriched for mitotic cell cycle phase transition ( D). 
RvD1 treatment significantly decreased the levels of S100A8, S100A9, INHBA, Ormi, Ly6i, and MAPK14 messenger RNAs (mRNAs) ( E). Since S100A8 is an alarm protein during inflammation , it preferentially forms a stable heterodimer with S100A9. As the S100A8 levels changed the most, we observed the effect on S100A8/A9 during the resolution phase. We isolated resident and recruited macrophages in BALF, and measured S100A8/A9 levels to identify the cellular sources of S100A8/A9 during the resolution phase of inflammation. We found that almost all of the S100A8/A9 was expressed on RAMs ( F). LPS-induced increases in S100A8/A9 expression on RAMs were abrogated by treatment with RvD1 ( G), indicating that RvD1 inhibited RAM-derived S100A8/A9 production during the resolution phase.

## S100A8/A9 inhibits RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis function in vitro
Cells were isolated from BALF ( A) and stimulated with different concentrations of LPS (100 ng/mL and 1 μg/mL) and/or interferon γ (IFN-γ, 10 ng/mL, 20 ng/mL, and 100 ng/mL) at various time points (6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h) to further confirm the effect of S100A8/A9 on RAMs. We found that 100 ng/mL LPS and 10 ng/mL IFN-γ significantly increased S100A8 and S100A9 mRNA expression for 24 h ( B-E). Treatment with 50 nM S100A8 siRNA and 50 nM S100A9 siRNA inhibited S100A8 and S100A9 mRNA ( F-H) and protein ( I-J) expression. As we expected, S100A8 siRNA and S100A9 siRNA upregulated the percentage of Ki67+ RAMs after LPS/IFN-γ stimulation ( A). In addition, the overexpression of S100A8 or S100A9 by plasmid transfection markedly reduced the percentage of Ki67+ cells ( B, K-L). S100A8 or S100A9 siRNA treatment reduced the G1 phase of RAMs after LPS/IFN-γ treatment ( C). 
Silenced S100A8/A9 cells were incubated with FITC-LPS/IFN-γ to determine whether S100A8/A9 also regulated the phagocytic uptake of LPS by RAMs in vitro. As shown in D-E, silencing either S100A8 or S100A9 enhanced the phagocytosis of FITC-LPS by RAMs. Knocking down S100A8/A9 in RAMs upregulated CD206 expression ( F).These data show that S100A8 and S100A9 are critical mediators of self-renewal and the phagocytic capacity of RAMs.

## RvD1-promoted RAM self-renewal is dependent on S100A8/A9 in vitro
Next, we asked whether RvD1 regulated RAM proliferation via S100A8A9 in vitro. We found that the LPS/IFN-γ-induced upregulation of S100A8 and S100A9 was abrogated by RvD1 ( G-H). CCK8 was used to observe cell proliferation, as shown in I. RvD1 enhanced RAM absorbance after incubation with LPS/IFN-γ. We further analyzed the role of RvD1 in cell cycle regulation. The G1 phase was increased after stimulation with LPS, while treatment with RvD1 obviously reduced the G1 phase ( J). Moreover, the silencing of S100A8 or S100A9 enhanced the proliferation rate of RAMs. However, RAM proliferation was unchanged after treatment with RvD1 ( K), indicating that RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal in an S100A8/A9-dependent manner. 

## RvD1 promotes RAM self-renewal and phagocytic function in a S100A8/A9 dependent manner in vivo
We established LPS-induced ARDS in S100A9-/- mice and monitored RAM proliferation and phagocytic function to further elucidate the role of S100A8/A9 in vivo. Since S100A9-/- mice do not express S100A9 protein ( ), the functional S100A8/A9 complex was absent in these mice. The absence of S100A9 significantly shortened the Ri from 48 to 24 h, suggesting that the deletion of S100A8/A9 enhanced the resolution of inflammation ( A). LPS-stimulated S100A9-/- mice had significantly more RAMs compared to their wild-type littermates after 48 h ( B), which coincided with the upregulation of Ki67 expression ( C). Two-photon fluorescence proved that the RAMs numbers and phagocytic activity of RAMs was significantly enhanced in S100A9-/- mice, but the neutrophils counts were less ( D). In the E. coli-induced ARDS model, the RAM count ( A), Ki67+ RAMs ( B), and phagocytosis of FITC-E. coli ( C) were also increased in S100A9-/- mice. A rescue experiment showed that RAM proliferation in S100A9-/- mice was eliminated ( F) after the administration of 10 µg of rS100A8 and/or 10 µg of rS100A9 ( E), and the phagocytic function of RAMs in S100A9-/- mice was also reduced ( G, H). These results indicated that RAM self-renewal and phagocytic function in vivo were dependent on S100A8/A9.
S100A9-/- mice were administered RvD1 at 48 h and BAFL was collected 72 h after LPS inhalation to further confirm the effect of RvD1 in vivo ( E). We found that the increased neutrophil count ( I), RAM proliferation ( J-K) and phagocytosis ( L-M) were unchanged after treatment with RvD1.
Thus, the effect of RvD1 on RAM self-renewal and phagocytic function during the resolution phase was dependent on the S100A8/A9 protein.

## RvD1 promotes RAM self-renewal and phagocytic function dependent on the MAPK14/S100A8/A9 signaling pathway
The p38 MAPK signaling pathway regulates S100A8/A9 during phagocyte migration . RNA-seq analysis showed that MAPK14 expression was reduced after RvD1 treatment, and we also found that RvD1 downregulated MAPK14 in the sorted RAMs ( E). We further verified the effect of MAPK14 in vivo and in vitro. In this study, MAPK14 levels were significantly reduced in RAMs after treatment with RvD1 ( A). Likewise, primary RAMs stimulated with RvD1 in vitro showed lower MAPK14 expression ( B). MAPK 14 siRNA reduced S100A8/A9 expression on RAMs ( C), and MAPK14 siRNA promoted RAM proliferation and phagocytosis after LPS intervention. However, the effects of RvD1 was abolished by MAPK14 siRNA ( D, E). Moreover, MAPK14 inhibitor, VX-702 reduced S100A8/A9 expression on RAMs ( F), and VX-702 promoted RAM proliferation and phagocytosis ( G, H) after LPS stimulation in vivo. Moreover, the effects of RvD1 was abolished by VX-702. Taken together, these results showed that MAPK14 upstream of S100A8/A9 was involved in RvD1-mediated resolution.

## The basic effect of RvD1 depends on its receptor ALX
ALX was identified as the RvD1 receptor . We found that the inhibitory effect of RvD1 on neutrophils could be partially blocked by the ALX inhibitor BOC-2 ( A). The improved effect of RvD1 on RAM numbers was also abrogated by BOC-2 treatment ( B). Moreover, the improved effect of RvD1 on RAM proliferation, phagocytosis, and the apoptosis of neutrophils and FITC-LPS were abolished by BOC-2 ( C-E), indicating that the basic effect of RvD1 on RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis was dependent on its ALX receptor.

## Plasma RvD1 and S100A8/A9 are associated with the clinical outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS patients
We measured RvD1 and S100A8/A9 levels in patient plasma to examine the clinical relevance of RvD1 and S100A8/A9 in sepsis-induced ARDS. We enrolled 35 intensive care unit (ICU) patients (including 17 survivors and 18 non-survivors) ( ) and eight healthy volunteers. Plasma samples were collected within 24 h of ICU admission (day 0) and then at continuous time points. Plasma RvD1 levels were measured using triple quadrupole/linear ion trap mass spectrometry ( A-B). RvD1 levels were significantly lower in patients compared to healthy controls at all of the examined time points ( C). RvD1 levels in the surviving patients were not different during those time points. However, RvD1 levels were significantly lower in non-survivors on days 6–9 compared to days 0–2. The RvD1 level of non-survivors was significantly lower than that of surviving patients on days 6–9 ( D). We also found that S100A8/A9 levels were markedly higher in patients compared to healthy controls at all of the examined time points ( E). The S100A8/A9 levels of the surviving patients were not different during those time points. S100A8/A9 levels in the non-survivors were higher compared to survivors at both days 3–5 and days 6–9 ( F). RvD1 levels were negatively correlated with S100A8/A9 levels ( G). These data suggest that RvD1 and S100A8/A9 were associated with the outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS. 

## Discussion
Here, we found that RAMs played a pivotal role in the resolution of LPS and/or E. coli-induced ARDS, and RvD1 enhanced the resolution of inflammation by promoting RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis in an ALX/MAPK14/S100A8/A9-dependent manner (graphical abstract). Plasma levels of RvD1 in sepsis-induced ARDS patients were negatively correlated with plasma S100A8/A9 levels, and were associated with the outcome of sepsis-induced ARDS.
ARDS is the result of excessive pulmonary inflammation, especially during sepsis development . Therefore, the resolution of pulmonary inflammation is of clinical significance. In our self-limited experimental model of ARDS, LPS and/or E. coli were sprayed into the pulmonary tract to establish a visible, uniform, and stable murine model. The inflammatory response entered the resolution phase 72 h after LPS inhalation and 24 h after E. coli inhalation and was characterized by a sharp decrease in inflammatory cell infiltration. A previous study showed that the depletion of RAMs immediately prior to pulmonary cryptococcal infection resulted in rapid clinical deterioration and the death of mice within 6 days post-infection with evidence of severe lung damage . Consistent with the previous study, we showed that the depletion of RAMs delayed resolution after LPS administration, indicating that these RAMs were critical in promoting the resolution of LPS-induced ARDS.
RvD1, a potent anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving mediator, selectively controls inflammatory responses in kidneys, lungs, and periodontal tissues . Previously, we demonstrated that RvD1 accelerated the resolution of inflammation by promoting the apoptosis of recruited macrophages and suppressed neutrophil infiltration by reducing CXCL2 expression and release from RAMs . Here, we observed that RvD1 shortened the Ri, promoted RAM self-renewal, and enhanced the clearance of neutrophils, LPS, or E. coli in the resolution phase, while the basic effect of RvD1 on neutrophils was abolished in RAM-deleted mice, suggesting that RvD1 accelerated the resolution of inflammation in a RAM-dependent manner.
The mechanisms by which RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal and phagocytic function in the resolution phase remain to be clarified. The transcriptional profiling experiment showed the greatest change in S100A8. S100A8 exists as a heterodimer with S100A9 and is a major component of the acute phase of inflammation . In the present study, S100A9-/- mice showed lower neutrophil infiltration in BALF, shortened Ri, and increased RAM counts during LPS-induced ARDS. We also found that S100A8/A9 was highly expressed on RAMs in inflamed lungs, consistent with previous reports , . S100A8/A9 has been reported to regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, and chemotaxis . Here, in vitro, RAM proliferation, particularly in the G1 phase, and phagocytosis were significantly increased after knocking down S100A8 or S100A9 in primary RAMs using specific siRNAs. In vivo, the proliferation and phagocytosis of RAMs were enhanced in S100A9-/- mice but attenuated after rS100A8 and rS100A9 treatment, consistent with a previous finding that S100A9 blockade impaired the efferocytosis of apoptotic cardiomyocytes . The results indicated that S100A8/A9 was a key regulator of RAM proliferation and phagocytosis during the resolution phase.
Accordingly, we further proved that RvD1 prevented LPS-induced increases in S100A8 and S100A9 expression in primary RAMs, enhanced the proliferation of cells, and accelerated S/G2 transition in vitro. However, RvD1 did not affect the proliferation or efferocytosis of S100A8 or S100A9-deficient RAMs. RvD1 had no effect on PMN and RAM numbers in S100A9-/- mice and did not change the function of RAMs in vivo. These data suggest that RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis during the resolution phase in an S100A8/A9 protein-dependent manner.
MAPK14, upstream of S100A8/A9, is ubiquitously expressed across different cell types . It was originally identified as a modulator of inflammatory responses and regulates the expression of proinflammatory cytokines in response to various stimuli , . MAPK14 also induces G2-M arrest and suppresses cell proliferation . A previous study showed that RvD1 attenuated MAPK-dependent cardiac injury during sepsis . Here, we found that RvD1 reduced LPS-induced elevations in MAPK14 in RAMs, and RvD1 abolished the MAPK14-mediated proliferation and phagocytosis of RAMs. Furthermore, MAPK14 siRNA and inhibitor reduced S100A8/A9 expression. Consistent with this, the activation of p38 in phagocytes resulted in S100A9 phosphorylation, whereas p38 blockade inhibited the phosphorylation of S100A9 . Therefore, we surmised that the effect of RvD1 on RAMs was dependent upon the MAPK14/S100A8/A9 signaling pathway.
RvD1 promotes phagocytosis and the clearance of human macrophages dependent on binding to its ALX receptor . Here, we demonstrated that the beneficial effects of RvD1 on RAMs were abrogated by an ALX antagonist (BOC-2), indicating that the effect of RvD1 on RAMs was through binding to ALX, consistent with a previous report that RvD1 interacted with ALX on phagocytes and played a role in resolving acute inflammation. The results suggest that the RvD1 response was ALX-dependent.
SPMs play a crucial role during both the onset and resolution of inflammation for the control of pulmonary disease , , . In humans, severe asthma was associated with lower levels of SPMs compared to healthy controls, as protectin D1 was decreased in patients with status asthmaticus compared to healthy controls . Another study demonstrated that the plasma levels of LXA4 were decreased in septic patients compared to normal patients, consistent with our study findings that RvD1 levels were significantly lower in patients. Moreover, we also found that RvD1 was decreased in non-survivors, consistent with a report that LXA4 levels were decreased in patients with severe asthma compared to those with moderate or nonsevere asthma, , suggesting that RvD1 levels correlated with the outcomes of sepsis-induced ARDS patients. In addition, elevated plasma S100A8/A9 levels were detected in patients in agreement with a study showing increased concentrations of S100A8/A9 in the peripheral blood of patients . Furthermore, S100A8/A9 levels in non-survivors were higher than those in survivors, consistent with a previous report that the endogenous inflammatory mediator S100A8/A9 was elevated in the brains of humans who died of sepsis, suggesting that significant elevations in S100A8/A9 are associated with high mortality in sepsis patients. Therefore, the negative correlation of RvD1 and S100A8/A9 levels further proved that RvD1 promoted the resolution of inflammation by reducing S100A8/A9 levels.
In conclusion, the present study demonstrated that i) RAMs played a pivotal role in resolving inflammation during LPS and/or E. coli-induced ARDS, ii) RvD1 promoted RAM self-renewal and phagocytosis during the resolution phase through the ALX/MAPK14/S100A8/A9 signaling pathway, and iii) plasma levels of RvD1 in sepsis-induced ARDS patients negatively correlated with plasma S100A8/A9 levels, and both were associated with ARDS outcomes.
