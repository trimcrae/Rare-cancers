## Abstract

## Background
Sevoflurane (Sev) is a commonly used volatile anesthetic that might suppress the process of breast cancer. Also, circular RNAs (circRNAs) have been reported to partake in the pathogenesis of breast cancer. Accordingly, this research was designed to investigate the mechanism of hsa_circ_0005962 on Sev‐mediated breast cancer development.

## Methods
Sev was applied to treat breast cancer cells. Cell proliferation ability, migration, invasion, and apoptosis were detected using Cell Counting Kit‐8 (CCK‐8), 5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine (EdU), Transwell, and flow cytometry assay. Proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), Matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP9), B‐cell lymphoma‐2 (Bcl‐2)‐associated X protein (Bax), and Epithelial stromal interaction 1 (EPSTI1) were assessed using western blot assay. circ_0000129, microRNA‐578 (miR‐578), and EPSTI1 levels were determined using real‐time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR). Using bioinformatics software (Circinteractome and Targetscan), the binding between miR‐578 and circ_0000129 or EPSTI1 were predicted, and proved using dual‐luciferase reporter and RNA pull‐down assay. The biological roles of circ_0000129 and Sevoflurane on tumor growth were analyzed using a xenograft tumor model in vivo.

## Results
Sevoflurane blocked tumor cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and promoted apoptosis. Circ_0000129 and EPSTI1 expression were increased, and miR‐578 was decreased in breast cancer cells. Also, they presented an opposite trend in Sev‐treated tumor cells. Circ_0000129 upregulation might abolish Sev‐mediated tumor progression in vitro. Mechanically, circ_0000129 can affect EPSTI1 expression by sponging miR‐578. Sev might inhibit tumor growth by regulating circ_0000129 in vivo.

## Conclusion
Circ_0000129 relieved Sev‐triggered suppression impacts on breast cancer development partly via the miR‐578/EPSTI1 axis, which provides a new mechanism for studying mediated therapy of breast cancer treatment.

Keywords: breast cancer, circ_0000129, EPSTI1, miR‐578, sevoflurane

Circ_0000129 expression was decreased in sevoflurane (Sev)‐treated breast cancer cells. Upregulation of circ_0000129 might overturn Sev‐induced tumor cell growth and metastasis suppression via regulating the miR‐578/EPSTI1 axis.

## INTRODUCTION
As the most frequently occurring malignancy in females, breast cancer is considered an increasing health problem in most countries, with approximately 2 088 849 newly diagnosed cases and 626 679 deaths reported in 2018. In recent years, this tumor arising in young women has attracted the extensive attention of many researchers. Although brilliant achievements have been made in treatment strategies, including surgical resection, adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, and targeted therapy, , tumor recurrence frequently occurs due to the metastasis and dissemination as a result of the release of tumor cells into the circulation. Accordingly, the exploration of effective therapies is highly desirable for the treatment of this malignant tumor. Of note, sevoflurane (Sev), which is a clinically commonly utilized inhaled anesthetic during surgery, has been confirmed to possess good anesthetic effects and antitumor activity in some types of cancers, including breast cancer. , Yet, little is known about the mechanisms underlying Sev in breast cancer treatment.
Over the last decade, some scholars have discovered that large proportions of mammalian genomic products are transcribed as non‐coding RNAs, which have been reported to partake in the control of various biological processes. , As a novel identified endogenous non‐coding RNA, circular RNAs (circRNAs) constitute single‐stranded closed‐loop RNA molecules lacking the 5′ end caps and 3′ end poly (A) tails produced by the back splicing events in general. , Unlike linear counterparts, the majority of circRNAs are relatively abundant, conserved, stable, and have tissue/cell‐specific expression patterns. , In fact, recent work has demonstrated that many circRNAs are inextricably correlated with Sev‐mediated malignant phenotypes in diverse tumors. For example, Gao et al. found that the deficiency of circ‐VIM and applying Sev exhibited synergistic effects in repression proliferation, migration, and invasion of esophageal cancer cells. Meanwhile, Xu et al. showed that enhanced hsa‐circ_121129 was able to reverse Sev‐mediated glioma malignant phenotype repression via regulating the miR‐761/TDIF2 axis. In terms of breast cancer, there have been no studies on the relationship between circRNAs and Sev on tumor biological characteristics in current research. Of interest, a recent article stated that a newly discovered circRNA, hsa_circ_0000129, was significantly upregulated in breast cancer and partook in the regulation of this tumor's malignant progression. Yet, whether circ_0000129 responds to Sev and is involved in this anesthetic‐mediated breast cancer progression are unknown.
Until now, the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) hypothesis is able to alter gene expression via competition for binding to mutual microRNA (miRNA) response elements, which has been thought of as the major regulation manner for circRNAs. , Also, this prevailing hypothesis has attracted growing attention as a new explanation for tumor development and progression. , Herein, our research first found that circ_0000129 was able to share binding sequences of miR‐578, according to the analysis of the online bioinformatics tool. Furthermore, in several studies, miR‐578 was frequently downregulated and responsible for various cancer development. , In another study, under‐expressed miR‐578 was able to constrain breast cancer development via reducing cell migration. Accordingly, whether the role of Sev on the malignant behavior phenotype of breast cancer is mediated by the circ_0000129‐miR‐578 regulatory network was further explored in our study.

## METHODS

## Cell culture and clinical samples
Under a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C, breast cancer cell lines (MCF‐7, CL‐0149; MDA‐MB‐231, CL‐0150, Procell) and normal human mammary epithelial cell line (MCF‐10A, ATCC CCL‐10317, ATCC) were, respectively, grown in Dulbecco's modified eagle medium (DMEM: Procell) and mammary epithelial cell basal medium (MEBM: ATCC), followed by the addition of 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS: Invitrogen). In addition, MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells in six‐well plates were introduced into an airtight chamber, followed by exposure to different doses of Sev (0, 1.7, 3.4, and 5.1%, Sigma‐Aldrich) for 6 h using an anesthetic vaporizer.
Meanwhile, samples of the breast cancer tissues and paired adjacent nontumor tissues (n = 31) were harvested from Zhongshan Hospital Xiamen University. The written informed consent was signed by all participants, and this research project was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital Xiamen University.

## Cell proliferation assays
For cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8) assay, after the transfection or Sev treatment, breast cancer cells in 96‐well plates were cultured overnight at 37°C, followed by mixing with CCK‐8 reagent (Peptide Institute) for 3 h. Meanwhile, bubbles were avoided in the wells, and cell viability at 450 nm was analyzed using a microplate reader. For 5‐ethynyl‐2′‐deoxyuridine (EdU) assay, transfected or treated 4 × 104 cells in six‐well plates were mixed with EdU (50 μM, RiboBio) for 2 h, followed by the addition of Apollo and DAPI. Finally, the samples were detected by a fluorescence microscope.

## Transwell assay
A 24‐transwell chamber (BD Biosciences) or Matrigel‐coated inserts in the transwell chamber were utilized to evaluate tumor cell migration and invasion ability. Generally, cells after transfection or treatment were incubated in the upper compartment. Lower counterparts were filled with the medium with 10% FBS. After being cultured for 24 h, cells on the lower surface were orderly mixed using paraformaldehyde and 0.1% crystal violet (Sigma‐Aldrich). Finally, the results were analyzed under a microscope (magnification×100).

## Cell apoptosis assay
After being resuspended in binding buffer, transfected or treated tumor cells were dual‐stained with annexin V‐FITC/PI (Sigma‐Aldrich) under a dark environment. Then, 15 min later, analysis of apoptosis cells was performed using a FACSCalibur (Becton Dickinson) flow cytometer.

## Western blot assay
Using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Beyotime), breast cancer cell lysates were prepared, followed by electrophoresis separation on a 10% SDS‐PAGE. Subsequently, the samples were transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore), which were then hybridized with primary antibodies: PCNA (1:1000, ab18197), matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP9, 1:1000, ab76003), B cell lymphoma‐2 (Bcl‐2)‐associated X protein (Bax, 1:1000, ab32503), epithelial stromal interaction 1 (EPSTI1, 1:1000, ab233036), and β‐actin (ab8227, 1:1000), and secondary antibody (ab205178, 1:10 000) from Abcam. Finally, blots were visualized by means of the ECL detection kit, and quantified using Image J software.

## Cell transfection
First, a lentiviral vector for stable circ_0000129 overexpression (circ_0000129) and empty vector (vector, a control) was obtained from GeneCopoeia, followed by package into pseudoviral particles. Then, stable cells were selected in the presence of puromycin (Sigma‐Aldrich). Meanwhile, 20 nM of oligonucleotides containing circ_0000129 or EPSTI1 small interference RNA (si‐circ_0000129 or si‐EPSTI1#1/2/3), miR‐578 mimic/inhibitor, and their respective controls (si‐NC, miR‐NC, and anti‐miR‐NC) from GenePharma were transfected into breast cancer cells for 48 h using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). Finally, the harvested tumor cells were treated with Sev at different doses.

## Real‐time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‐qPCR)
After being treated with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), the total RNA obtained from tumor cells was quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies), followed by synthesis cDNA using M‐MLV reverse transcriptase (Promega). Afterward, cDNA amplification was performed using the thermal cycler CFX6 system (Bio‐Rad) with miScript SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen). After normalization to β‐actin and U6, the target expressions were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The specific primer sequences are shown in Table  .
 

## Dual‐luciferase reporter assay
First, the binding was predicted using bioinformatics software, which was then verified using a dual‐luciferase reporter assay. In short, the wild‐type (WT) reporter plasmids (circ_0000129‐WT, EPSTI1‐3'UTR‐WT) and mutant (MUT) plasmids (circ_0000129‐MUT, EPSTI1‐3'UTR‐MUT) were constructed and collected from Geneseed. Subsequently, breast cancer cells were transfected with the plasmid and miR‐578 mimic or miR‐NC, followed by incubation for 48 h. Finally, the detection of luciferase activities was carried out using a dual‐luciferase reporter assay kit (Promega).

## 
RNA pulldown assay
Generally, breast cancer cells were transfected with biotinylated (Bio)‐miR‐NC or Bio‐miR‐578 (50 nM, Sangon Biotech), followed by sonicating treatment. After being incubated with magnetic beads (Sigma‐Aldrich) at 4°C overnight, the RNA complexes were extracted and analyzed using RT‐qPCR.

## Tumor xenograft assay
The animal experiment was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital Xiamen University. In short, 1 × 106 MCF‐7 cells stably transfected with vector or circ_0000129 were treated with 5.1% Sev or not for 6 h. After being subcutaneously injected into female BALB/C nude mice (Vital River Laboratory, Beijing, China) with six mice in each group, the 4‐week‐old mice were assigned to three groups (Control, Sev + vector, and Sev + circ_0000129) under a specific‐pathogen‐free environment. Tumor volume (length×width2 / 2) was assessed every week. Four weeks later, all mice were euthanized, and the resected tumors were weighed and analyzed further. Based on the previously described method, immunohistochemical staining was carried out. Tumor tissue sections were treated with antibody specific for Ki‐67 (ab16667, 1:200, Abcam). During the postoperative period, mice were checked daily to ensure they could eat, drink, eliminate, and ambulate normally. When evident physical deficits became apparent in the mice, such as ambulation reduction, disregarded observer, dyspnea or cachexia (loss of 15% of mice original bodyweight), mice were deemed to meet the criteria of euthanasia and were sacrificed to minimize suffering and distress.

## Statistical analysis
All data in this research were analyzed using GraphPad Prism7 (GraphPad Software) and are denoted as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The differences were deemed statistically significant at p < 0.05. Student's t‐test or one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey's tests was used to assess the significance of the variance.

## RESULTS

## Sev treatment repressed breast cancer cell malignant behavior in vitro
First, to investigate the biological role of Sev on breast cancer progression, tumor cells were exposed to various doses of Sev. As shown in Figure  , Sev treatment might hinder tumor cell proliferative ability in a dose‐dependent manner. Furthermore, transwell assay exhibited that the abilities of migration and invasion gradually declined in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells with the growing concentrations of Sev (Figure  ). In parallel, flow cytometry assay presented that Sev exposure concentration‐dependently might induce apoptosis rate of MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells (Figure  ). To further verify the effects of Sev on tumor progression, PCNA (a proliferation marker), MMP9 (migration/invasion marker), and Bax (proapoptosis marker) were checked using western blot assay. As shown in Figure  , the protein levels of PCNA and MMP9 were gradually decreased in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells treated with Sev, while Bax protein level was obviously increased. Overall, these results suggested that Sev could suppress breast cancer progression.
 

## Circ_0000129 expression was decreased in Sev‐treated breast cancer cells
Circ_0000129 was derived from the exons 6 and 7 of the TMOD4 gene on chr1: 151145974‐151149507, and the end of exon 6 and exon 7 was back‐spliced to form the circular structure. Meanwhile, to confirm the formation of circ_0000129, divergent primers were designed and amplified this circRNA in the templates (cDNA) and genomic DNA (gDNA) from tumor cells. RT‐qPCR assay suggested that circ_0000129 could only be detectable by divergent primers in cDNA but not in the gDNA group (Figure  ). In addition, an apparent upregulation of circ_0000129 in 31 breast cancer tissues and breast cancer cell lines (MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231) was observed compared with 31 normal tissues and MCF‐10A cells (Figure  ). Of interest, our data exhibited that the expression level of circ_0000129 was continuously reduced after Sev treatment in a concentration‐dependent manner (Figure  ). Together, these results indicated that Sev might regulate breast cancer progression by inhibiting circ_0000129.
 

## Upregulation of circ_0000129 might overturn Sev‐induced repression impact on breast cancer progression
Then, to explore whether circ_0000129 was involved in Sev‐mediated tumor cell malignant phenotypes, we conducted in vitro gain‐of‐function analyses. At first, the overexpression efficiency of circ_0000129 was proved in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells (Figure  ). Subsequently, CCK‐8 and EdU assay exhibited that the introduction of circ_0000129 might effectively mitigate the negative effect of Sev on tumor cell proliferative ability (Figure  ). Meanwhile, Sev exposure‐aroused reduction in migration and invasion was partly abrogated via circ_0000129 (Figure  ). In addition, the flow cytometry assay revealed that Sev treatment might significantly promote the apoptosis rate of MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells, which was partially impaired after the transfection of circ_0000129 (Figure  ). Similar to these results, PCNA, MMP9, and Bax protein levels in the Sev group were reversely changed by circ_0000129 upregulation (Figure  ). Altogether, these data implied that Sev treatment might repress breast cancer cell progression by regulating circ_0000129.
 

## Circ_0000129 could directly bind to miR‐578 in breast cancer cells
To identify the regulatory mechanism of circ_0000129 in the breast cancer process, the circ_0000129‐interacting miRNA was searched using Circinteractome. We found that circ_0000129 has some binding sequence with miR‐578 (Figure  ). Moreover, miR‐578 level was markedly reduced in tumor cell lines relative to MCF‐10A cells (Figure  ). On the contrary, miR‐578 level was dose‐dependently elevated by Sev in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells (Figure  ). To verify the binding, a dual‐luciferase reporter assay was performed and the results showed that the luciferase intensity of MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells was significantly declined after cotransfection of circ_0000129‐WT and miR‐578, while that had little change in the circ_0000129‐MUT and miR‐578 group (Figure  ). Also, RNA pulldown assay displayed that circ_0000129 expression of tumor cells was pulled‐down when using the Bio‐miR‐578 rather than Bio‐miR‐NC (Figure  ). In addition, miR‐578 expression was clearly decreased in breast cancer tissues and negatively associated with circ_0000129 level (Figure  ). Broadly, circ_0000129 acted as an efficient miRNA sponge for miR‐578.
 

## Circ_0000129 overexpression abolished the suppression role of Sev on breast cancer progression by targeting miR‐578
Subsequently, we further investigated the apparent relationship between circ_0000129 and miR‐578 on Sev‐mediated tumor development. Overexpression efficiency of miR‐578 in Sev‐treated tumor cells was presented in Figure  . Functionally, the upregulation of miR‐578 might notably weaken circ_0000129‐triggered increase in cell proliferative ability in Sev‐treated MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells (Figure  ). Simultaneously, the promoting role of migration and invasion caused by the forced expression of circ_0000129 in Sev‐treated tumor cells was effectively reversed via elevated miR‐578 (Figure  ). In addition, flow cytometry assay showed that the upregulation of circ_0000129 might remarkably hinder the apoptosis rate in Sev‐stimulated tumor cells, while the phenomenon was abolished after the cotransfection of miR‐578 mimic (Figure  ). Additionally, the alteration of PCNA, MMP9, and Bax protein levels under the same condition further verified the effects of circ_0000129 and miR‐578 on Sev‐mediated proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells (Figure  ). Collectively, the above results indicated that circ_0000129 might regulate Sev‐induced breast cancer cell behavioral phenotypes by sponging miR‐578.
 

## 
EPSTI1 was verified as a functional target of miR‐578
Then, we further analyzed the target mRNAs of miR‐578 using the TargetScan database. The results exhibited that EPSTI1‐3'UTR harbor putative binding sites of miR‐578 (Figure  ). Furthermore, we found that the protein level of EPSTI1 was apparently increased in breast cancer cell lines compared with MCF‐10A cells (Figure  ). Of note, western blot assay showed that EPSTI1 level was gradually reduced in Sev‐treated MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells in a dose‐dependent manner (Figure  ). Dual‐luciferase reporter assay displayed that miR‐578 overexpression decreased the luciferase activity in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells transfected with EPSTI1‐3'UTR‐WT rather than EPSTI1‐3'UTR‐MUT (Figure  ). Beyond that, EPSTI1 expression was significantly increased in breast cancer tissues and inversely correlated with miR‐578 level (Figure  ). In addition, the knockdown efficiency of anti‐miR‐578 and si‐circ_0000129 in tumor cells was confirmed (Figure  ). Next, western blot assay showed that the downregulation of miR‐578 might obviously enhance the protein level of EPSTI1 in tumor cells (Figure  ). Interestingly, our data displayed that the knockdown of miR‐578 might partly ameliorate the suppressive role of circ_0000129 silencing on EPSTI1 protein level in tumor cells (Figure  ), suggesting circ_0000129/miR‐578/EPSTI1 axis. Overall, these results indicated that miR‐578 might negatively regulate EPSTI1 expression by directly targeting EPSTI1 in breast cancer.
 

## Circ_0000129 might abrogate the repression of Sev on breast cancer cell malignant behavior by regulating EPSTI1

Additionally, to further verify the role of circ_0000129/miR‐578/EPSTI1 axis in regulating Sev‐mediated tumor cell behavioral phenotypes, we conducted rescue assays. First, EPSTI1 expression was apparently reduced in MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells transfected with si‐EPSTI1#1/2/3 relative to the si‐NC group (Supporting Information Figure  ). In particular, si‐EPSTI1#3 was the most significant. Therefore, si‐EPSTI1#3 (si‐EPSTI1) was utilized in subsequent studies. Then, RT‐qPCR results showed that the introduction of si‐EPSTI1 might obviously aggravate the inhibitory effect of Sev treatment on EPSTI1 level in breast cancer cells (Figure  ). Functionally, EPSTI1 absence might effectively reverse the promotion of circ_0000129 on cell proliferative ability in Sev‐treated tumor cells (Figure  ). Synchronously, the overexpression of circ_0000129 might facilitate the capacity of migration and invasion in Sev‐treated tumor cells, while these phenomena were effectively mitigated via EPSTI1 knockdown (Figure  ). Meanwhile, circ_0000129‐triggered decrease in apoptosis rate in Sev‐treated MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells was partly reversed through si‐EPSTI1 introduction (Figure  ). Western blot assay also presented similar results, in which EPSTI1 downregulation might abolish circ_0000129‐induced enhancement in PCNA and MMP9 and decline in Bax in treated Sev‐treated tumor cells (Figure  ). Overall, circ_0000129 might accelerate tumor progression by regulating EPSTI1 in Sev‐treated breast cancer cells.
 

## Circ_0000129 upregulation might relieve the anticancer effect of Sev in vivo
In addition, to verify the roles of Sev and circ_0000129 in breast cancer in vivo, a xenograft tumor mouse model was established. As shown in Figure  , Sev treatment might notably constrain tumor volume and weight versus the control group, whereas circ_0000129 upregulation partly abolished these effects. In addition, immunohistochemical staining displayed that Ki‐67 content was suppressed by Sev treatment, which was significantly overturned after the introduction of circ_0000129 (Figure  ). Also, further analysis presented that circ_0000129 and EPSTI1 expression was significantly reduced in the tumor tissues from the Sev + vector group, while their expression was improved in the Sev + circ_0000129 group (Figure  ). Additionally, the expression of miR‐578 in different groups was opposite to that of circ_0000129 (Figure  ). All in all, these data suggested that Sev could inhibit breast cancer cell growth in vivo by regulating circ_0000129.
 

## DISCUSSION
During the last decade, some researchers have discovered that anesthetic techniques or drugs were able to influence diverse tumor evolution. , , Of note, Sev is an inhalational hypnotic that is reported to be effective and safe across patient populations in clinical. Currently, increasing studies are focusing on the potential inhibitory role of Sev on a variety of human tumors through multiple mechanisms. , In terms of breast cancer, the present work suggested that Sev exposure was able to block tumor cell proliferation and metastasis in a dose‐dependent fashion, consistent with former studies. , In addition, breast cancer cells were induced with 5.1% Sev for the subsequent functional experiments in this study in vitro. Thus, depicting the detailed mechanism of Sev on tumor treatment could help understand its pharmacological effect.
In recent years, emerging evidence has revealed that Sev‐mediated tumor progression is closely associated with the regulatory mechanisms of non‐coding RNAs, such as miRNAs. , However, there has been no report about the functional role of circRNAs in Sev‐mediated breast cancer development. A number of studies have indicated that the majority of circRNAs have been widely applied as underlying tumor biomarkers because of their unique superiority of stability in structure, containing breast cancer. , It has been confirmed that dysregulated circRNAs are responsible for breast cancer progression in previous studies. , Coinciding with an earlier study, our data also unmasked a significant increase of circ_0000129 in tumor cells. Importantly, circ_0000129 displayed an opposite expression trend in Sev‐treated breast cancer cells for the first time. Beyond that, the current findings first suggested that the ectopic circ_0000129 was able to relieve Sev‐aroused malignant behavior suppression in this tumor in vitro. Synchronously, the present study explained that the repression of Sev on tumor cell growth might be reversed by elevated circ_0000129 in vivo assay. The above results implied that the emergence of circ_0000129 might provide novel light for the exploration of the Sev mechanism in breast cancer progression.
To date, large numbers of experiments have indicated that circRNAs are able to serve as natural miRNA sponges or RNA decoys, leading to the disinhibition of miRNA targets. , Moreover, the mechanism, a unifying function of circRNAs, is also applicable to breast cancer research. Hence, the miRNAs which bind to circ_0000129 were searched in this study. In this review, after binding was predicted using the bioinformatics tool, miR‐578 appeared as a latent target of circ_0000129, as proved by the dual‐luciferase reporter and RNA pulldown analysis. As reported by previous researchers, the abnormal expression of miR‐578 might take part in the modulation of malignant behaviors in various tumor cells, including breast cancer. , In agreement with these articles, the present study showed that miR‐578 content was reduced in these tumor cells. In addition, several miRNAs, such as miR‐203 and miR‐139‐5p, have been confirmed to partake in Sev‐mediated breast cancer cell proliferation. , Of note, we found for the first time that miR‐578 was expressed at a high level in Sev‐treated tumor cells. As expected, enhanced miR‐578 was able to abolish circ_0000129‐caused tumor progression promotion in Sev‐treated breast cancer cells. That was, circ_0000129 might regulate Sev‐aroused breast cancer progression via sponging miR‐578. As is widely recognized, miRNAs might regulate tumorigenesis through directly binding to 3'UTR of target genes. In this review, our data exhibited that EPSTI1, a stromal fibroblast‐induced gene in breast cancer, was a downstream target of miR‐578. What is more, some studies have reported that the knockdown of EPSTI1 might dwindle tumor cell migration and invasion, , implying that EPSTI1 acts as a carcinogenic factor in this tumor. Consistently, our data also confirmed that EPSTI1 expression was freakishly increased in these tumor cells. Interestingly, we noticed a significant decrease in EPSTI1 under Sev treatment. Therefore, EPSTI1 was chosen for in‐depth investigations. Notably, western blot analysis showed that the deficiency of circ_0000129 might diminish EPSTI1 expression, and the phenomenon was partly abolished via miR‐578 absence, indicating the circ_0000129/miR‐578/EPSTI1 axis in this tumor. Additionally, the current study displayed that enhanced circ_0000129 was able to attenuate Sev‐caused malignant inhibition of tumor via regulating EPSTI1 content, further supporting the role of Sev on tumor progression might be mediated by the regulatory mechanism.
In conclusion, the results of our study suggest that Sev might hinder breast cancer progression partly through regulating the circ_0000129/miR‐578/EPSTI1 network. This evidence not only provides a theoretical foundation for further studying the application of Sev in breast cancer surgery, but also lays the basis for researching circRNA‐directed breast cancer therapy in the future.
