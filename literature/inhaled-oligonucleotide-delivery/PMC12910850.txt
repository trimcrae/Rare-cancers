## Background
Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a progressive cardiopulmonary disorder marked by pathological vascular remodeling, predominantly driven by the hyperproliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs). Although bone morphogenetic protein 7 (BMP7) has been implicated in PASMCs dysregulation, the precise upstream mechanisms governing its expression in PH remain elusive.

## Methods
In vitro and in vivo models of hypoxia-induced PH were employed. The mRNA and protein levels of the different molecules were quantified using qRT-PCR and Western blot. The role of BMP7 in PASMCs proliferation, cell cycle progression and apoptosis were assessed using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assays, Western blotting, immunofluorescence, and flow cytometry. Moreover, the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms of BMP7 were elucidated using Chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative PCR (ChIP-qPCR) and quantitative methylation-specific PCR (qMSP). In vivo functional roles of BMP7 were validated using recombinant protein administration and AAV5-mediated gene knockdown in hypoxic mice, with evaluations of hemodynamics, echocardiography, and vascular remodeling.

## Results
Hypoxia significantly upregulated BMP7 expression in PASMCs and lung tissues of PH models. Exogenous BMP7 enhanced PASMCs proliferation, accelerated cell cycle progression and suppressed apoptosis. Conversely, BMP7 knockdown attenuated these pro-proliferative and anti-apoptotic responses. Mechanistically, DNMT1 was downregulated under hypoxia, and its genetic or pharmacological inhibition elevated BMP7 expression, whereas DNMT1 overexpression suppressed BMP7. ChIP-qPCR verified DNMT1 binding to the BMP7 promoter CpG island, and qMSP confirmed that DNMT1 loss reduced BMP7 promoter methylation. Consistent with a pathogenic role, BMP7 knockdown alleviated hypoxia-induced right ventricular hypertrophy and vascular remodeling, while BMP7 supplementation exacerbated these phenotypes.

## Conclusions
Our study unveils the DNMT1-BMP7 axis as a critical epigenetic pathway in PH. Hypoxia-induced DNMT1 downregulation derepresses BMP7 via promoter hypomethylation, driving PASMCs proliferation and pathological vascular remodeling. Targeting this axis may offer novel therapeutic strategies for PH.

## Background
Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a fatal vascular disorder characterized by progressive pulmonary arteriolar occlusion, leading to persistently increased pulmonary vascular resistance and eventual right ventricular failure, resulting in poor prognosis [ , ]. The pathophysiological mechanisms of PH are complex, involving vascular resistance, perivascular inflammatory cell infiltration and pulmonary arterial remodeling. Although various cell types contribute to the pathogenesis of PH, including endothelial cells (ECs), fibroblasts, and inflammatory cells, substantial evidence underscores the pivotal involvement of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) in the remodeling of pulmonary arteries and the advancement of PH [ ]. Dysfunction in PASMCs associated with PH is characterized by several pathological features, including increased cell proliferation, resistance to programmed cell death, phenotypic switching from contractile to synthetic, and metabolic disturbances [ ]. Despite progress in understanding the underlying mechanisms of PH and the development of targeted therapies, current drug therapies are largely symptomatic and none of the current treatment strategies cure this devastating disease [ ]. Therefore, identifying and characterizing new targets in the development and progression of PH is a pressing concern.
Bone morphogenetic protein 7 (BMP7), an essential component of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily, serves as a pleiotropic cytokine that exerts crucial control over skeletal formation, wound healing, and diverse biological processes under both normal and disease conditions. It significantly contributes to the processes of bone and cartilage formation and repair by facilitating the differentiation of stem cells into osteoblasts and chondrocytes, in addition to promoting the proliferation of these specific cell type [ , ]. BMP7 is also involved in a variety of biological processes, including kidney development [ ], eye formation [ ], and nervous system protection [ ]. Initial clinical investigations identified significantly elevated circulating BMP7 levels in patients with hereditary (HPAH) and idiopathic (IPAH) pulmonary artery hypertension compared to healthy controls. Moreover, these elevated concentrations positively correlated with mortality rates, suggesting that BMP7 could function as a prognostic biomarker in case of pulmonary artery hypertension (PAH) [ ]. To further explore these findings, we examined experimental models and observed consistent upregulation of BMP7 expression levels in lung tissues from monocrotaline (MCT)-induced PH animals. Mechanistically, BMP7 was found to enhance PASMCs proliferation through selective activation of the activin A receptor type 2 A (ACTR2A) signaling pathway [ ]. However, the effects of BMP7 on the progression of PH and the mechanisms regulating its expression remain unclear.
Epigenetics investigates heritable alterations in gene expression and phenotypic variations that occur independently of modifications in the underlying DNA sequence [ ]. The transgenerational transmission of epigenetic information primarily encompasses three major categories: (i) covalent DNA methylation, (ii) post-translational histone alterations, and (iii) regulatory networks governed by non-coding RNAs [ ]. DNA methylation represents an essential epigenetic modification characterized by the enzymatic transfer of methyl groups (-CH₃) to cytosine bases, specifically at the C5 position of the pyrimidine ring within CpG dinucleotides [ ]. In mammalian cells, this biochemical process predominantly generates 5-methylcytosine (5mC), which serves as a fundamental epigenetic mark regulating gene expression and chromatin organization. This epigenetic modification is catalyzed and maintained by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs), including DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b. As the predominant epigenetic hallmark of DNA, methylation exerts pleiotropic biological functions. It mediates transcriptional repression through promoter hypermethylation, wherein methylated DNA is recognized by specific transcription factors (TFs) or methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD) proteins, which recruit histone-modifying enzymes or chromatin-remodeling complexes to dynamically modulate chromatin architecture [ , ]. Although substantial evidence has established the association between PH and aberrant DNA methylation patterns, the precise mechanistic involvement of DNA methylation in PH pathogenesis remains to be fully elucidated.
To date, whether BMP7 expression is regulated by DNA methylation remains unclear. Our study demonstrates that upregulated BMP7 in hypoxic PH promotes PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression while inhibiting apoptosis, thereby exacerbating vascular remodeling and right ventricular dysfunction. Further investigations revealed decreased DNMT1 expression in PH models, which modulates PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression through BMP7 regulation. Chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative PCR (ChIP-qPCR) and quantitative Methylation-Specific PCR (qMSP) analyses showed that DNMT1 modulates BMP7 expression by directly binding its promoter and regulating CpG island methylation. Targeting BMP7 significantly ameliorates hypoxia-induced pulmonary vascular remodeling and right ventricular hypertrophy in PH. Collectively, these findings provide the first evidence of DNA methylation-dependent BMP7 regulation in PH, establishing a foundation for exploring its therapeutic potential.

## Materials and methods

## Animal models, functional studies, and hemodynamic and histological assessments
Male C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks, 20–25 g) were obtained from the accredited Laboratory Animal Center at Harbin Medical University. All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (revised 1996) and approved by the Institutional Animal Care Committee of Harbin Medical University (Ethical Approval No. KY2018-075).
In order to develop the PH model, mice were housed in normobaric hypoxia (10% FiO₂) for four weeks as previously described [ ]. For the gain-of-function study, one group of hypoxic mice received daily intraperitoneal injections of recombinant BMP7 protein (rBMP7, 500 µg/kg/day; R&D Systems, 5666-BP) dissolved in 10 mM HCl throughout the experimental period. For the loss-of-function study, another cohort of mice was administered an adeno-associated virus serotype 5 carrying short hairpin RNA targeting BMP7 (AAV5-shBMP7; the target sequence is provided in Supplementary Table , Hanheng Biotech, Shanghai) via intratracheal inhalation prior to hypoxic exposure, with AAV5 expressing a scramble shRNA serving as the negative control.
Following the 4-week exposure period, mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal administration of 2.5% tribromoethanol solution (0.1 mL/10 g; Sigma-Aldrich). Transthoracic echocardiography was performed to assess pulmonary hemodynamics, specifically by measuring the pulmonary artery velocity time integral (PAVTI) and the ratio of pulmonary artery acceleration time to ejection time (PAT/PET). The measurement of right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) was conducted utilizing a 1.2 F microtip pressure transducer (Scisense Inc), consistent with methodologies described in earlier studies [ ]. Euthanasia was performed via tribromoethanol overdose (400–500 mg/kg, IP) following AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2013 Edition). For histological assessment of pulmonary vascular remodeling, Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of lung sections were performed to evaluate pulmonary vascular remodeling. ImageJ-based morphometric analysis quantified the medial wall thickness of pulmonary arteries (50–100 μm external diameter) using the formula: (External Vascular Area – Lumen Area)/External Vascular Area × 100%.

## Cell culture
Primary human PASMCs (hPASMCs; Sciencell #3110) and murine PASMCs (mPASMCs; Pricella) were cultured in smooth muscle cell medium (SMCM; Sciencell #1101) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, where all cellular assays including proliferation measurements were conducted under these standard serum-supplemented conditions; for hypoxic exposure, cells were incubated in a specialized atmosphere comprising 3% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 92% N₂ for the indicated durations, while for pharmacological treatments, recombinant human BMP7 protein (5666-BP, R&D Systems) was administered at a concentration of 100 ng/mL for 48 h and the DNMT inhibitor 5-Azacytidine (5-Aza; ab142744, Abcam) was applied at 2 µM for 48 h, with all experiments using cells between passages 2–4 to ensure consistency.

## Western blot
Cellular and tissue proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer (Beyotime), with subsequent electrophoretic separation on 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gels. After transfer, the PVDF membranes were subjected to blocking with 5% non-fat dry milk for 2 h at ambient temperature prior to overnight incubation with primary antibodies (for details, see Supplementary Table ) at 4 °C. Following incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, protein signals were revealed by ECL substrate (Haigene Biotechnology) and digitally captured using a chemiluminescence detection apparatus.

## Immunofluorescence
Cell suspensions were plated on glass slips and chemically fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde at ambient temperature for 30 min, then treated with 0.3% Triton X-100 detergent for 15 min to achieve membrane permeabilization and blocked with 3% BSA at 37 °C for 1 h. After incubation with primary antibodies (4 °C, overnight) and PBS washes, samples were stained with fluorophore-conjugated secondaries (1:100) and DAPI (5 min, dark). Images were acquired using a Leica AF6000 live-cell imaging system.
Paraffin-embedded lung Sect. (5 μm) were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval in sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Prior to immunostaining, sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 and blocked with 3% BSA. Following overnight incubation with primary antibodies (4 °C; Supplementary Table ) and PBS washes, sections were incubated with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:100) and DAPI. Imaging was performed on a Leica AF6000 system.

## Cell counting kit 8 assay
Cells were seeded in 96-well plates and treated for 48 h. At 90% confluency, medium was replaced with CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime; 1:10 dilution in fresh medium). After 90 min incubation (37 °C), the absorbance was recorded at a wavelength of 450 nm utilizing a microplate reader.

## Flow cytometry
Cell cycle distribution was evaluated using a commercial Cell Cycle and Apoptosis Analysis Kit (Beyotime) per manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, the harvested cells were stained with propidium iodide (37 °C, 30 min) and analyzed by flow cytometry (CytoFLEX, Beckman Coulter) to quantify the distribution of cells across the distinct phases of the cell cycle.

## Quantitative real-time PCR
The transcript levels of BMP7 and DNMT1 were quantified by SYBR Green qPCR using the TOYOBO Master Mix (QPK-201) on a Tianlong thermocycler. ACTB was used as the reference gene for normalization, and the relative expression was calculated using the comparative CT (2^(–ΔΔCT)) method. The primer sequences used are listed in Supplementary Table .

## RNA interference and plasmid transfection
For optimal transfection efficiency, mPASMCs were seeded to reach 50–60% confluence at the time of transfection. Transfection was carried out employing Lipofectamine 2000 Reagent (Thermo Fisher, MA; Catalog #11668019) with either 50nM siRNA duplexes or 2 µg plasmid DNA, following the manufacturer’s standardized protocol. After 6 h of incubation with the transfection mixture, the medium was replaced with a complete medium and the sample was incubated for a further 48 h. The sequences of siRNAs targeting DNMT1 and BMP7 are provided in Supplementary Table . For overexpression studies, the mammalian expression vectors pCMV6-Dnmt1 (Catalog No. MR212057) and empty pCMV6-Entry plasmid were commercially obtained from Origene (Rockville, MD).

## Methylation-specific PCR
To assess BMP7 promoter methylation status across experimental conditions, we performed methylation-specific PCR (MSP) using a commercial kit (EM101, TIANGEN). Genomic DNA was isolated with a DNA extraction kit (DP304, TIANGEN) followed by bisulfite conversion using the Bisulfite Conversion Kit (DP215, TIANGEN). MSP was carried out with the following thermocycling conditions: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min; 35 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 20 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 20 s; followed by a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. The PCR products were separated by electrophoresis on 1% agarose gels and visualized using a digital gel documentation system. The primer sequences for the methylated and unmethylated BMP7 promoter regions were designed using the MethPrimer website (http://www.urogene.org/methprimer/) and are listed in Supplementary Table .

## Chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative PCR
The chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) procedure was conducted following the manufacturer’s instructions using the commercial ChIP assay kit (P2078, Beyotime Biotechnology). Approximately 5 million cultured cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde, with reaction quenching using 125 mM glycine. Cellular lysis was performed using SDS buffer containing PMSF protease inhibitor, after which the chromatin was fragmented by sonication to yield DNA segments between 200 and 1000 bp. For immunoprecipitation, we employed specific antibodies targeting DNMT1 (1.0 µg per sample, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) along with rabbit IgG as negative control. The interaction between DNMT1 and BMP7 promoter region was quantitatively assessed through real-time PCR analysis. Specific primers were designed to amplify CpG island 1 (CGI-1) and CpG island 2 (CGI-2) within the BMP7 promoter region. To serve as a negative control for the ChIP assay, an additional primer set was designed targeting a gene desert region, which is devoid of CpG islands and known regulatory elements. All primer sequences are provided in Supplementary Table .

## Quantitative methylation-specific PCR assay
Quantitative methylation-specific PCR (qMSP) was carried out on bisulfite-converted DNA according to the manufacturer’s protocol for the Methylamp MS-qPCR Fast Kit (Epigentek, Brooklyn, NY). Reactions utilized 20 ng of template DNA and primers designed by MethPrimer (for details, see Supplementary Table ). ACTB was amplified as a reference gene for normalization. Each experiment incorporated no-template controls and 100% methylated EpiTect Control DNA (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) as negative and positive controls, respectively. DNA methylation levels are presented as the percentage of methylated reference (PMR), which was calculated using the comparative CT method (2^(–ΔΔCT) × 100%).

## Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were conducted with GraphPad Prism 8.0. Data are presented as scatter plots overlaid with box plots (showing median and interquartile range). Due to small sample sizes (n < 10 per group) and non-normal distribution, non-parametric tests were employed: the Mann-Whitney U test for two-group comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. The false discovery rate (FDR) was controlled using the Benjamini, Krieger and Yekutieli method, and a P-value or FDR-adjusted P-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

## Results

## Hypoxia-induced BMP7 upregulation in pulmonary hypertension
To extend our previous findings of BMP7 elevation in PAH patients and MCT-induced PH models [ , ], we further investigated its expression pattern in hypoxia-induced PH, a major subtype associated with chronic lung diseases. Following 4 weeks of hypoxic exposure, PH development was confirmed through hemodynamic and histopathological assessments, including elevated RVSP (Fig. A and B) and characteristic pulmonary vascular remodeling visualized by H&E staining (Fig. C). Immunoblotting results demonstrated a marked elevation in BMP7 protein expression levels in PH lung specimens exposed to hypoxic conditions relative to normoxic control samples (Fig. D and E). Immunofluorescence studies demonstrated prominent BMP7 expression in α-SMA-positive cells (Fig. F), indicating substantial expression within the pulmonary vascular smooth muscle compartment. This cellular distribution pattern was further investigated through in vitro models using both mPASMCs and hPASMCs. Hypoxic exposure (24–72 h) induced time-dependent BMP7 upregulation in both cell types (Fig. G to J), with immunofluorescence confirming increased BMP7 expression in cytoplasmic and nuclear compartments of mPASMCs (Fig. K and L). These findings indicate that BMP7 expression is elevated in hypoxic PH, suggesting that increased BMP7 may contribute to PH progression.

 

## BMP7 promoted PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression while inhibiting apoptosis
To elucidate the functional consequences of BMP7 upregulation in PH pathogenesis, we systematically examined its mitogenic effects on PASMCs. The cultured mPASMCs were treated with recombinant mouse BMP7 protein (5666-BP, R&D Systems, Inc., Minneapolis, MN) at an optimal concentration of 100 ng/ml for 48 h. Subsequent CCK-8 assays revealed that exogenous recombinant BMP7 enhanced the cell viability of mPASMCs (Fig.  A). Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated that BMP7 upregulates the expression of proliferation markers PCNA (Fig.  B and C) and Ki67 (Fig.  D and E). To further investigate BMP7’s proliferative effects, we examined its impact on the cell cycle. Western blot revealed a marked increase in cyclin A2, cyclin B2, and cyclin D1 levels (Fig.  F and G), consistent with flow cytometry data showing an elevated proportion of cells in S + G2/M phase (Fig.  H and I). In addition, we evaluated the effect of BMP7 on apoptotic markers. Western blot analysis revealed that BMP7 promoted the expression of BCL2 while suppressing BAX and Caspase3 expression (Fig.  J and K).
To further validate the functional role of endogenous BMP7 in hypoxia-induced phenotypic alterations, we knocked down BMP7 expression in mPASMCs using specific siRNA under hypoxic conditions (Figure A and S1B). Silencing of BMP7 significantly attenuated the hypoxia-induced enhancement of cell viability, as determined by CCK-8 assay (Figure C). Consistently, the protein levels of the proliferation markers PCNA (Figure D and S1E) and Ki67 (Figure F and S1G) were markedly reduced upon BMP7 knockdown. Further cell cycle analysis demonstrated that depletion of BMP7 decreased the expression of key cell cycle regulators, including cyclin A2, cyclin B2 and cyclin D1 (Figures H and S1I). Moreover, ablation of BMP7 promoted cellular apoptosis, as evidenced by a significant decrease in the anti-apoptotic protein BCL2 and a concurrent increase in the pro-apoptotic proteins BAX and Cleaved Caspase3 (Figure J and S1K). These data collectively indicate that BMP7 promotes PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression while inhibiting apoptosis, thereby contributing to vascular remodeling in PH.

 

## DNMT1 downregulation promoted PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression via epigenetic derepression of BMP7 under hypoxia
Our previous studies demonstrated that BMP7 enhances PASMCs proliferation via selective activation of the activin A receptor type 2 A (ACTR2A) signaling pathway [ ]. However, the upstream regulatory mechanism of BMP7 expression in the PH model remains unclear. Given the established regulatory role of DNA methylation in gene expression, we examined its potential involvement in modulating BMP7 expression. Then we detected the expression of DNMT1, DNMT3a and DNMT3b, key enzymes that regulate DNA methylation, in hypoxia-treated mPASMCs and hPASMCs and found that the expression of DNMT1 decreased with the duration of hypoxia, whereas the expression of DNMT3b was elevated, but there was no significant change in DNMT3a (Fig. A to D). Consistent results were obtained in vivo (Fig. E and F). Subsequent qRT-PCR analysis demonstrated a significant reduction in DNMT1 mRNA expression in hypoxic lung tissues (Fig. G). Consistent with this, immunofluorescence staining confirmed the decrease in DNMT1 protein and revealed its co-localization with α-SMA (Fig. H).
Due to the established link between DNA methylation and gene expression, we investigated a potential association between elevated BMP7 in PH and reduced DNMT expression. Immunofluorescence confirmed decreased, nuclear-localized DNMT1 in hypoxic mPASMCs (Fig.  I and J), implicating it as a potential epigenetic regulator of BMP7. To validate DNMT1’s role in BMP7 regulation, we employed both pharmacological inhibition and genetic knockdown experiments in mPASMCs. Treatment of mPASMCs with the DNMT1 inhibitor 5-Azacytidine (5-Aza, 2µM; Abcam, ab142744) for 48 h significantly upregulated BMP7 expression, as demonstrated by qRT-PCR (Fig.  K) and Western blot analysis (Fig.  L and M). Then DNMT1 expression was silenced using siRNA, with knockdown efficiency confirmed by Western blot (Figure A and S2B). Consistent with these findings, siRNA-mediated DNMT1 knockdown enhanced BMP7 expression at both transcriptional (Fig.  N) and translational levels (Fig.  O and P). These data confirm that DNMT1 is downregulated in hypoxic PH and negatively regulates BMP7 expression.
We next investigated whether DNMT1 knockdown affects the proliferation and cell cycle progression of mPASMCs. CCK-8 assays revealed that DNMT1 depletion enhanced cell viability (Figure C). Consistent with this, both Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses showed an upregulation of the proliferation markers PCNA (Figure D and S2E) and Ki67(Figure F and S2G). Furthermore, Western blot analysis revealed that DNMT1 knockdown upregulated key cell cycle regulators, including cyclin A2, cyclin B2, and cyclin D1 (Figure H and S2I). This pro-proliferative effect was further corroborated by flow cytometry, which indicated a significant increase in the proportion of cells in the S and G2/M phases (Figure J and S2K). These findings collectively demonstrated that DNMT1 knockdown promoted proliferation and accelerated cell cycle progression in mPASMCs.

 

## DNMT1 regulated PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression through BMP7
While our previous studies had established that DNMT1 regulated the proliferation and cell cycle progression of PASMCs, it remained unclear whether it mediated these effects through BMP7. To address this, we constructed a DNMT1 overexpression vector and validated its efficiency in regulating BMP7 via Western blot (Fig.  A and B). Subsequent CCK-8 experiments showed that overexpression of DNMT1 inhibited hypoxia-induced PASMCs proliferation and that this effect could be reversed by recombinant BMP7 protein (rBMP7) (Fig.  C). Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated that DNMT1 overexpression suppressed hypoxia-induced PCNA (Fig.  D and E) and Ki67 (Fig.  F and G) expression, effects that were rescued by rBMP7 treatment. To investigate DNMT1-mediated cell cycle regulation through BMP7, we performed Western blot and flow cytometry analyses. DNMT1 overexpression significantly attenuated hypoxia-induced upregulation of cyclin A2, cyclin B2, and cyclin D1 (Fig.  H and I) and reduced S + G2/M phase cell populations (Fig.  J and K). Notably, these effects were reversed by rBMP7 treatment, demonstrating that DNMT1 regulates PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression through BMP7.
To further substantiate the specific role of BMP7 in DNMT1-mediated phenotypic effects, we employed a dual knockdown strategy in mPASMCs. As anticipated, CCK-8 assays revealed that the enhanced cell viability induced by DNMT1 knockdown was significantly attenuated upon concurrent BMP7 depletion (Figure A). Accordingly, the upregulation of proliferation markers PCNA (Figure B and S3C) and Ki67 (Figure D and S3E) following DNMT1 silencing was effectively reversed by BMP7 knockdown. Consistent results were obtained from cell cycle analysis, the increased expression of cyclin A2, cyclin B2, and cyclin D1 (Figure F and S3G) and the elevated proportion of cells in S + G2/M phases (Figure H and S3I) caused by DNMT1 deficiency were both abolished when BMP7 was co-silenced. These rescue experiments collectively demonstrate that the pro-proliferative and cell cycle-accelerating effects of DNMT1 knockdown are dependent on BMP7 upregulation.

 

## DNMT1 bound the BMP7 promoter and regulated its CpG methylation
Although DNMT1 was shown to regulate BMP7 expression, the underlying mechanism remained unclear. Using the MethPrimer database, we identified two CpG islands (CGIs) in the promoter region of BMP7: CGI-1 (NC_000068.8: c172,782,591 − 172,782,443, chromosome 2, GRCm39) and CGI-2 (NC_000068.8: c172,782,429 − 172,782,072, chromosome 2, GRCm39) (Fig.  A). Based on this finding, we investigated the role of DNMT1 in regulating the methylation levels of these CGIs. First, we assessed the DNA methylation status of the CGIs in the BMP7 promoter under hypoxic conditions using methylation-specific PCR (MSP). The results showed a significant reduction in the methylation level of the BMP7 promoter CGI in both hypoxia-treated PASMCs and mouse lung tissues (Fig.  B and C).
We then investigated whether DNMT1 regulates this DNA methylation by directly binding to the CGIs. Chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative PCR (ChIP-qPCR) analysis demonstrated specific binding of DNMT1 to CGI-1 of the BMP7 promoter under normoxic conditions (Fig.  D). In contrast, DNMT1 binding was not detected at the adjacent CGI-2 region, confirming the site-specificity of this interaction. Furthermore, hypoxia treatment significantly attenuated DNMT1 binding at CGI-1 compared to the normoxia group (Fig.  E). Treatment of mPASMCs with 5-Aza or siRNA-mediated knockdown of DNMT1 significantly reduced BMP7 promoter methylation, as determined by MSP (Fig.  F and G). In contrast, overexpression of DNMT1 increased promoter methylation (Fig.  H). Subsequent ChIP-qPCR analysis confirmed that DNMT1 overexpression significantly enhanced its binding to the CGI within the BMP7 promoter (Fig.  I). Furthermore, quantitative methylation-specific PCR (qMSP) revealed a significant reduction in BMP7 promoter methylation in hypoxia-treated mPASMCs (Fig.  J); a similar effect was observed upon DNMT1 knockdown (Fig.  K). Collectively, these findings indicate that DNMT1 directly binds to the BMP7 promoter and regulates its CGI methylation status.

 

## Targeting BMP7 ameliorated pulmonary vascular remodeling and right ventricular hypertrophy in pulmonary hypertension
Given the pro-proliferative effect of BMP7 on PASMCs in vitro, we next evaluated its contribution to PH pathogenesis in vivo. Based on the literature, a dose of 500 µg/kg/day was selected for rBMP7 administration. Exogenous rBMP7 (500 µg/kg/day) was injected intraperitoneally into hypoxic mice, and the results showed that rBMP7 significantly exacerbated RVSP (Figure A and S4B) and the right ventricular hypertrophy index (RV/LV + S) (Figure C) in hypoxic mice. Echocardiographic analysis demonstrated that rBMP7 further reduced both the PAVTI and the PAT/PET (Figure D to S4F) in hypoxia-exposed mice. Then HE staining confirmed that rBMP7 exacerbated hypoxia-induced pulmonary vascular remodeling (Figure G and S4H). Furthermore, analysis of the protein expression of PCNA (Figure I and S4J) and cell cycle-related proteins, including cyclin A2, cyclin B2 and cyclin D1 (Figure K and S4L) revealed that rBMP7 injection significantly increased the expression of these proliferation and cell cycle-related proteins. The above results revealed that BMP7 aggravates hypoxic pulmonary hypertension by stimulating PASMCs proliferation through cell cycle activation, resulting in worsened pulmonary vascular remodeling and right ventricular dysfunction.
To determine the therapeutic potential of BMP7 inhibition, we next performed a loss-of-function study in vivo. Mice were administered an adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5) carrying a short hairpin RNA targeting BMP7 (AAV5-shBMP7) to knock down its expression prior to hypoxic exposure. In stark contrast to the effects of rBMP7, knockdown of BMP7 significantly attenuated the hypoxia-induced increase in RVSP (Fig.  A and B) and reduced the right ventricular hypertrophy index (RV/LV + S) (Fig.  C). Echocardiographic parameters were also improved, as evidenced by increased PAVTI and PAT/PET (Fig.  D to F). Histological analysis of lung sections via HE staining confirmed that BMP7 knockdown markedly ameliorated hypoxia-induced pulmonary vascular remodeling (Fig.  G and H). At the molecular level, the protein levels of the proliferation marker PCNA (Fig.  I and J) and the key cell cycle regulators cyclin A2, cyclin B2, and cyclin D1 (Fig.  K and L) in lung tissues were significantly suppressed by AAV5-shBMP7 treatment. Collectively, these data demonstrate that targeted knockdown of BMP7 effectively alleviates the development of hypoxic pulmonary hypertension by suppressing PASMCs proliferation and cell cycle progression, thereby improving pulmonary hemodynamics and vascular remodeling.

 

## Discussion
Pulmonary hypertension (PH) represents a progressive disorder of the cardiopulmonary system, marked by abnormally high pressure within the pulmonary arteries and increased resistance in the pulmonary vasculature. The hallmark pathological features of PH encompass characteristic pulmonary vascular remodeling, including intimal proliferation, medial hypertrophy, adventitial thickening, and plexiform lesions [ ]. Importantly, aberrant PASMCs proliferation has been identified as a central pathological mechanism driving these structural alterations [ , ]. Despite recent advances, the mechanisms driving pathological PASMCs proliferation during PH progression have yet to be comprehensively defined. The primary novel discoveries presented in this study can be summarized in two key points (Fig. ). First, BMP7 expression is increased in a hypoxia-induced PH model and that exogenous recombinant BMP7 significantly promotes proliferation and cell cycle in PASMCs, exacerbating the progression of hypoxia-induced PH. Second, DNMT1 is reduced in the hypoxia-induced PH model and that DNMT1 regulates the expression of BMP7 by modulating the level of DNA methylation in its promoter region.

 

Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) represent a collection of structurally analogous and evolutionarily conserved proteins that are part of the TGF-β superfamily. Among these, BMP7 is a notable member that plays a crucial role in various physiological and pathological mechanisms, encompassing bone homeostasis, tissue regeneration, and oncogenesis [ – ]. Our study found increased expression of BMP7 in a model of hypoxia-induced PH, which appears to conflict with a previous report showing minimal endogenous BMP7 expression in hPASMCs under basal conditions [ ]. Interestingly, a recent study demonstrated that BMP7 expression in murine cardiomyocytes is subject to developmental silencing, showing a significant postnatal decline [ ]. This precedent leads us to hypothesize that the low basal BMP7 levels in hPASMCs might similarly reflect a developmentally silenced state, which can be reversed by pathological stimuli such as hypoxia, leading to its re-expression in PH. However, further studies are still needed to elucidate the changes in BMP7 expression in PASMCs during mammalian development. Additionally, another study indicated that the expression levels of BMP7 were diminished in pulmonary artery endothelial cells (PAECs) and lung tissue of rats subjected to hypoxic conditions [ ]. These discrepancies likely reflect cell type-specific responses to hypoxia, as BMP7 exerts pleiotropic effects across endothelial and smooth muscle lineages. Another explanation may be that BMP7 secreted by ECs acts in a paracrine manner on other cells, such as PASMCs, resulting in negative feedback regulation of their own BMP7 expression [ ].
The initiation of BMP signal transduction requires ligand binding to specific transmembrane serine/threonine kinase receptors. These receptors include three type II receptors: bone morphogenetic protein receptor type 2 (BMPR2), activin A receptor type 2 A (ACTR2A), and activin A receptor type 2B (ACTR2B). These type II receptors subsequently recruit and phosphorylate type I receptors from the activin receptor-like kinase (ALK) family, including ALK1, ALK2, ALK3, ALK5, and ALK6 [ , ]. Existing experimental evidence demonstrates that BMPR2 serves as an essential mediator of BMP-dependent growth suppression and phenotypic differentiation in PASMCs, for example, BMP4 and BMP7 significantly inhibited PASMCs proliferation and differentiation [ , ]. Notably, genetic ablation of BMPR2 resulted in complete abrogation of BMP-mediated growth suppression in pulmonary vascular cells, even at supra-physiological concentrations (200 ng/mL) of both BMP4 and BMP7 ligands [ ]. In this study, we showed that exogenous BMP7 (100 ng/ml, 48 h) promoted the proliferation and cell cycle progression of mPASMCs, which contrasts with previous studies. The divergent effects of BMP7 observed in different studies could potentially be influenced by factors such as the induction of pro-inflammatory signaling under specific conditions [ ], although this remains to be formally investigated. Consistent with the established role of BMPR2 in growth arrest and cellular differentiation, prior research has demonstrated that BMPR2 overexpression confers these functional responses to BMP7—a ligand that fails to elicit such effects in wild-type cells [ ]. These data indicate that BMP ligands normally signaling through alternative receptors, such as ACTR2A [ ], can acquire growth-inhibitory and pro-differentiation capabilities when mediated via the BMPR2 pathway. Notably, emerging biochemical evidence demonstrates that BMP7 exhibits significantly stronger binding affinity for ACTR2A compared to type I receptors, with approximately an order of magnitude difference in receptor binding kinetics [ ]. Therefore, it is reasonable to speculate that BMP7 may promote PASMCs proliferation by preferentially binding to ACTR2A, in contrast to BMPR2. Of course, further mechanistic investigations are warranted to elucidate the precise role of BMP7 in regulating PASMCs proliferation and phenotypic modulation.
Emerging evidence highlights the critical involvement of epigenetic dysregulation in PH pathogenesis. Hypoxia-induced PH models demonstrate global epigenetic alterations characterized by reduced genomic DNA methylation and diminished histone acetylation [ ]. These modifications potentially drive pathological vascular remodeling through aberrant PASMCs proliferation. Mechanistically, promoter hypomethylation of pro-proliferative genes coupled with downregulation of DNA methyltransferases appears to facilitate the phenotypic switching of vascular smooth muscle cells (SMCs), thereby promoting vascular remodeling in PH [ , ]. Our data consistently demonstrate downregulation of DNMT1 in hypoxic PASMCs and in the lungs of mice with chronic hypoxia-induced PH. This finding appears to contrast with recent reports showing increased DNMT1 expression in Sugen- and MCT-induced PH rat models [ , ]. This discrepancy underscores the context-dependent nature of epigenetic regulation in PH. We propose three explanations for these divergent findings. First, the pathogenic mechanisms differ substantially between models: Sugen and MCT models induce endothelial injury and inflammation that can upregulate DNMT1, whereas our chronic hypoxia model primarily drives vasoconstriction and remodeling, a context in which DNMT1 downregulation predominates. Second, species-specific epigenetic differences may contribute. Third, the temporal dynamics of DNMT1 expression remain uncharacterized across these models. Furthermore, our findings align with studies reporting decreased DNMT activity or expression in hypoxic settings [ ], supporting that our results represent a valid pathophysiological mechanism. Therefore, rather than being contradictory, these collective observations reveal the context-specific nature of DNMT1 in PH pathogenesis. Our work defines a novel pathway wherein hypoxia-induced DNMT1 loss leads to epigenetic derepression of BMP7, promoting PASMCs proliferation and vascular remodeling in the specific context of hypoxic PH.
Although our study provides valuable insights into the role of BMP7 in PH progression, it is important to acknowledge its limitations. While our study mechanistically establishes the DNMT1-BMP7 axis in experimental models, future validation in dedicated cohorts of PAH patients, with paired methylation and gene expression analyses of purified vascular cells, will be crucial to fully translate these findings to human pathology. Although we comprehensively validated BMP7 expression across human and animal models, we did not dynamically monitor BMP7 levels in patients’ blood to assess its correlation with disease severity—a promising direction for future clinical translation. Furthermore, our study not only defines a critical role for the DNMT1/BMP7 axis but also suggests several avenues for future research. These include elucidating the precise molecular interaction by identifying the exact CpG dinucleotides bound by DNMT1 within the BMP7 promoter and determining whether hypoxia-induced DNMT1 downregulation has broader, genome-wide epigenetic consequences beyond BMP7 regulation. Addressing these questions in future work will strengthen the theoretical foundation for targeting the DNMT1/BMP7 axis in the translational management of PH.

## Conclusions
In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that BMP7 is significantly upregulated in hypoxia-induced pulmonary hypertension and functions as a critical driver of disease progression by promoting PASMCs proliferation, accelerating cell cycle progression, and inhibiting apoptosis. Mechanistically, we have identified and validated a novel epigenetic pathway in which hypoxia-induced downregulation of DNMT1 leads to hypomethylation of the BMP7 promoter, thereby derepressing its expression. Collectively, these results establish the DNMT1/BMP7 axis as a key pathogenic mechanism in hypoxic PH and highlight its potential as a therapeutic target for intervention.
