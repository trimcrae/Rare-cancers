## Abstract
Allergic rhinitis (AR) is an allergic disease characterized by immunoglobulin E (IgE)‐mediated type I hypersensitivity disorder. In the current study, we illuminated the potential roles of microRNA‐141‐3p (miR‐141‐3p) in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)‐induced mucus production and the apoptosis in nasal epithelial cells (NECs). We demonstrated that miR‐141‐3p was significantly downregulated in nasal tissues from patients with AR and LPS‐treated NECs. Upregulation of miR‐141‐3p decreased the level of mucin 5AC (MUC5AC) in LPS‐treated NECs and induced NECs apoptosis. High Mobility Group Box 1 (HMGB1) was proved as a target of miR‐141‐3p and miR‐141‐3p negatively regulated its expression. In addition, we observed that HMGB1 was overexpressed in nasal mucosal tissues from patients with AR and LPS‐treated NECs. Finally, we proved that miR‐141‐3p decreased the level of MUAC5AC in LPS‐treated NECs through regulating HMGB1. In conclusion, miR‐141‐3p inhibited LPS‐induced MUAC5AC production and the apoptosis of LPS‐treated NECs by targeting HMGB1.
Keywords: allergic rhinitis, HMGB1, miR‐141‐3p, nasal epithelial cell

## INTRODUCTION
Allergic rhinitis (AR), which is a general allergic disorder, is a commonly chronic allergic disease among children. , AR is characterized by the symptoms of sneezing, nasal pruritus, and airflow obstruction, which are induced by immunoglobulin E (IgE)‐mediated type I hypersensitivity against inhaled allergens. The nasal epithelium is the first site of exposure to inhaled antigens. The nasal epithelial cells (NECs) play critical roles in the innate immune response to AR. , Hence, the investigation of the biology and function of NECs could be helpful for developing anti‐allergic treatments.
Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is a cell wall component of Gram‐negative bacteria and important for pro‐inflammatory mediators. LPS‐stimulated NECs is widely used as cell model to study the pathogenesis of AR. Reducing allergic airway responses via targeting LPS and its signaling pathway have been attracted increasing attentions. , LPS leads to the pathologic changes of allergic airway diseases, including eosinophil recruitment, subepithelial fibrosis, mucus cell metaplasia, and smooth muscle hypertrophy. , In LPS‐induced rhinitis model, neutrophil infiltration, nasal interleukin (IL)‐17, and interferon‐gamma (IFN‐γ) levels are increased in the nasal mucosa. In vitro, human primary nasal epithelial cell is cultured with LPS to construct AR model. In several references, LPS was used to stimulate primary nasal epithelial cell in the exploration of the pathogenesis or drug treatments in chronic inflammatory airway diseases, such as chronic_ rhinosinusitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases, and asthma. , , , , Since LPS is closely related with the pathogenesis of AR, LPS is used to stimulate nasal epithelial cell to construct AR model in our study.
Currently, a growing number of studies have found that microRNAs (miRNAs), a kind of endogenous, noncoding small RNAs, are involved in the development of allergic disease, including AR. For example, miRNA‐466a‐3p attenuates allergic nasal inflammation in mice through modulating GATA Binding Protein 3 (GATA3). MiRNA‐let‐7e modulates the progression of AR through regulating Janus Kinase 2/Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (JAK1‐SATA3) signaling pathway. In addition, miR‐375 prevents nasal mucosa cell from apoptosis and ameliorates AR by restraining JAK2‐STAT3 signaling pathway. Previous investigation also indicates that miR‐141‐3p can alleviate the chronic inflammatory pain (CIP) by downregulating the downstream target gene High Mobility Group Box 1 (HMGB1). MiRNA‐141 also ameliorates alcoholic hepatitis‐induced intestinal injury and intestinal endotoxemia partially via a Toll Like Receptor 4 (TLR4)‐dependent mechanism. Recently, the expression of miR‐143 has been proved to be dysregulated in AR and miR‐143 is involved in the development of AR. Previous investigation has proved that miR‐143 inhibits IL‐13‐induced inflammatory cytokine and mucus production in nasal epithelial cells from AR patients by targeting IL‐13 Ralpha1. Nevertheless, the potential role of miR‐141‐3p in AR still needs extensively explored.
In the present study, we investigated the functions of miR‐141‐3p in AR. We demonstrated that miR‐141‐3p negatively regulated the expression of HMGB1 and inhibited LPS‐induced mucus production in NECs.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS
2.1.

## Nasal mucosal tissues
Nasal mucosal tissues were collected from inferior turbinate sections in 36 patients with perennial AR. 36 normal nasal mucosa samples were collected from patients who received partial excision of the turbinate, uncinate process, and ethmoidal mucosa for surgical purposes, for example deviated nasal septum, or intracranial access. Patients received no systemic or topical corticosteroid therapy before this study. This study was approved by the Ethic Committee of the Second People's Hospital of Nantong. Informed consents were obtained from participants.

2.2.

## 
NECs culture
Inferior turbinate NECs were collected by nasal scraping under local anesthesia. Collected NECs were maintained in Bronchial Epithelial Growth Medium (BEGM) medium (Lonza, Walkersville, Maryland) under submerged conditions. NECs cells were treated with LPS (50 ng/mL) for 24 h. Cell supernatant were harvested for further experiments.

2.3.

## Ovalbumin (OVA) challenge protocol
BALB/C mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 6 in each group). In control group, mice were sensitized and challenged by saline alone. In AR (OVA) group, mice were injected intraperitoneal injection (i.p.) with 100 μL of sensitization fluid containing OVA (0.5 mg/mL) and Al(OH)3 (1:1) (20 mg/mL) three times per week. After 2 weeks, 50 μL of OVA solution (2 mg/mL) was given through pulverization inhalation for 30 min once a day for 1 week. Nasal mucosa tissues were collected and subjected for immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining assay. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second People's Hospital of Nantong.

2.4.

## Plasmid and siRNA transfection
MiRNA negative control (miR‐NC) and miR‐141‐3p mimics were obtained from RiboBio (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). SiRNA targeting HMGB1 (siHMGB1), siRNA control (siCon), pcDNA3.1‐HMGB1 plasmid (pc‐HMGB1) and pcDNA3.1 negative control (pc‐Vector) were obtained from Ambion (Carlsbad, California). Cell transfection was performed using Lipofactamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts).

2.5.

## Apoptosis assay
NECs cells were detached using trypsin. Resuspended NECs cells were stained with FITC‐conjugated Annexin V and PI (Bioworld Technology, Inc., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). After 15 min, the apoptosis of NECs was measured by flow cytometry.

2.6.

## Quantitative Real‐Time PCR (qPCR) assay
Total RNAs were isolated with Trizol (Thermo Fisher Scientific). cDNA was synthesized by using 1 μg of RNA and SuperScript III First‐Strand Synthesis System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). qPCR was carried out using a QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen, Germantown, Maryland) on a QuantStudio 3 Real‐Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The expression levels were calculated using the 2(−∆∆Ct) method. The primers used for PCR were as follows (sense and antisense, respectively): HMGB1: 5′‐GCTGACAAGGCTCGTTATGAA‐3′ and 5′‐CCTTTGATTTTGGGGCGGTA‐3′; MUC5AC: 5′‐CAGGACTCTCTGAAATCGTACCA‐3′ and 5′‐GAAGGCTCGTACCACAGGG‐3′; GAPDH: 5′‐AATGGACAACTGGTCGTGGAC‐3′ and 5′‐CCCTCCAGGGGATCTGTTTG‐3′; U6: 5’‐CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA‐3′ and 5′‐AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT‐3′; miR‐141‐3p: 5′‐CAUCUUCCAGUACAGUGUUGGA‐3′ and 5′‐CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACATATACT‐3′.

2.7.

## Immunoblotting assay
Proteins were extracted using a RAPI Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). 20 μg of proteins were loaded onto SDA‐PAGE and transferred to PVDF membrane. After blocking with 5% nonfat milk in Tris Buffered Saline Tween (TBST), PVDF membrane was incubated with HMGB1, MUAC5AC, or GAPDH antibody (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA) overnight following incubated with HRP‐conjugated secondary antibody (1:10 000, Bioworld Technology, Inc.) for 2 h. Bands were measured using ECL system (Millipore, Braunschweig).

2.8.

## Enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The level of MUC5AC in cell supernatant was measured using a commercial human MUC5AC ELISA kit (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) according to manufacturer's instructions.

2.9.

## Luciferase reporter gene assay
The fragment of HMGB1‐3′‐untranslated region (3′‐UTR) containing the wild‐type (wt) or mutant binding sites (mut) for miR‐141‐3p was inserted into pMIR‐Reporter (named as wt‐HMGB1 and mut‐HMGB1). NECs were cotransfected with miR‐141‐3p combination with HMGB1‐wt or HMGB1‐mut for 48 h. Luciferase activity in NECs was measured using Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Madison, Wisconsin).

2.10.

## Statistical analysis
All results were shown as mean ± SD. Data were calculated using GraphPad Prism 8. Student's t‐test or One‐way ANOVA followed by post hoc Tukey's test was utilized to analyze the difference. P value < .05 was considered as statistical difference.

## RESULTS
3.1.

## 
MiR‐141‐3p is downregulated in AR

To analyze the role of miR‐141‐3p in AR, we detected the expressions of miR‐141‐3p in 36 cases of nasal mucosal samples from patients with AR and normal nasal mucosa tissues. As demonstrated in Figure , miR‐141‐3p was significantly downregulated in patients with AR compared with that in normal nasal mucosa samples. We also measured the level of in the epithelia of nasal mucosa derived from AR mice. As showed in Figure , the level of miR‐141‐3p was markedly lower in AR mice compared to that in control mice. Finally, we observed that the expression level of miR‐141‐3p was also significantly decreased in LPS‐treated NECs compared with that in untreated NECs (Figure ). All these observations suggested that miR‐141‐3p was downregulated in AR.
 
3.2.

## 
MiR‐141‐3p restrains LPS‐induced MUC5AC expression in NECs

Lipopolysaccharide has been widely used to stimulate primary nasal epithelial cells in the exploration of the pathogenesis in chronic inflammatory airway diseases. , , , , Then, we determined the impact of miR‐141‐3p in AR using LPS‐treated NECs model. To increase the endogenous level of miR‐141‐3p, miR‐141‐3p mimics were transfetced into NECs. We observed that miR‐141‐3p was remarkably overregulated in miR‐141‐3p mimics transfected NECs compared with that in control (Figure ). Mucus overproduction is the common feature in nasal and paranasal sinus diseases, including rhinitis and sinusitis. Meanwhile, after treated with LPS, the MUC5AC protein in primary human NECs is significantly upregulated. In this study, the mRNA level of MUC5AC was increased in NECs treated with LPS. In contrast, the level of MUC5AC was reduced in miR‐141‐3p overexpressing NECs in the presence of LPS (Figure ). Consistently, the secretion of MUC5AC was increased in LPS‐treated NECs and miR‐141‐3p transfection markedly reduced the secretion of MUC5AC (Figure ). Several reports have confirmed that LPS treatment leads to cell injury through suppression of nasal epithelial cell proliferation and promotion of apoptosis in rhinitis model. , Finally, we found that LPS significantly induced the apoptosis of NECs, while miR‐141‐3p attenuated the effect of LPS in NECs apoptosis (Figure ). Altogether, our findings suggested that miR‐141‐3p suppressed LPS‐induced MUC5AC expression in NECs.
 
3.3.

## 
HMGB1 is regulated by MiR‐141‐3p
We explored the targets of miR‐141‐3p using online software Targetscan (http://www.targetscan.org/vert_71/) and miRTarBase (http://mirtarbase.mbc.nctu.edu.tw/php/index.php). We observed that miR‐141‐3p had conserved binding sites in the 3′‐UTR of HMGB1 (Figure ). To validate whether HMGB1 was a target of miR‐141‐3p, luciferase reporter gene assay was carried out. The result of luciferase assay indicated that miR‐141‐3p reduced the luciferase activity in NECs transfected with pMIR‐Reporter carrying wild‐type (wt) HMGB1 rather than mutant type (mut) HMGB1 (Figure ). To analyze whether the expression of HMGB1 was negatively modulated by miR‐141‐3p, NECs were transfected with miR‐141‐3p and we then measured the level of HMGB1. As shown in Figure , miR‐141‐3p significantly decreased the endogenous level of HMGB1 in NECs. Finally, we found that HMGB1 was significantly overexpressed in nasal mucosal samples from patients with AR (Figure ) and was upregulated in LPS‐treated NECs (Figure ). Finally, the immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining using nasal mucosa tissue from AR mice model implied that HMGB1 was overexpressed in OVA induced AR mice compared with that in the control group (Figure ). Hence, these findings proved that HMGB1 was negatively regulated by miR‐141‐3p.
 
3.4.

## 
HMGB1 silencing reduces the level of MUC5AC induced by LPSE

As HMGB1 a target of miR‐141‐3p, we investigated the roles of HMGB1 in AR using the LPS‐treated NECs model. Firstly, NECs were transfected with siRNA targeting HMGB1 (siHMGB1) and we observed that HMGB1 level was markedly reduced in NECs (Figure ). The downregulation of HMGB1 abolished the promoting effect of LPS on the expression and secretion of MU5AC in NECs (Figure ). In addition, LPS significantly induced the apoptosis of NECs, while downregulation of HMGB1 attenuated the effect of LPS (Figure ). Our data suggested that HMGB1 silencing inhibited the expression of MU5AC in LPS‐treated NECs.
 
3.5.

## 
MiR‐141‐3p mediates LPS‐induced the dysfunction of NECs by modulating HMGB1

We next analyzed whether the impact of miR‐141‐3p on LPS‐induced the NECs apoptosis and MUC5AC expression was depended on HMGB1. NECs were co‐transfected with miR‐141‐3p and pcDNA3.1‐HMGB1 plasmid (pc‐HMGB1). We observed that upregulation of HMGB1 rescued the expression of HMGB1, which was inhibited by miR‐141‐3p in LPS‐treated NECs (Figure ). We also found that pc‐HMGB1 transfection rescued the expression and secretion of MUC5AC, which were suppressed by miR‐141‐3p in LPS‐treated NECs (Figure ). Finally, pc‐HMGB1 rescued the effect of miR‐141‐3p on the apoptosis of NECs in the presence of LPS (Figure ). Therefore, our observations proved that miR‐141‐3p mediated LPS‐induced the dysfunction of NECs through targeting HMGB1 (Figure ).
 

4.

## DISCUSSION
Allergic rhinitis is one of the common noninfectious rhinitis. AR is still a therapeutic challenge and more effective pharmacological agents are needed. In this study, we proved that miR‐141‐3p was downregulated in nasal mucosal tissues from patients with AR whereas HMGB1 was significantly upregulated. We demonstrated that HMGB1 was a target of miR‐141‐3p and its level was modulated by miR‐141‐3p in NECs. In addition, we demonstrated the similar effect of miR‐141‐3p overexpression and HMGB1 silencing on LPS‐induced mucus production in primary NECs. Transfection of miR‐141‐3p or HMGB1 silencing inhibited the expression and secretion of MUC5AC in LPS‐treated NECs. More importantly, miR‐141‐3p negatively regulated the expression of MUC5AC induced by LPS was dependent on HMGB1. Altogether, we revealed the regulatory functions of miR‐141‐3p/HMGB1 in LPS‐treated NECs.
MiRNAs regulate the expressions of their target genes and participate into diverse pathological and physiological processes, including stress responses, signal transduction and cell differentiation. The precise functions of miRNAs have also been uncovered in various inflammatory disorders. For example, miR‐466a‐3p attenuates the allergic nasal inflammation in mice through modulating GATA3. MiRNA‐17‐5p aggravates LPS‐induced injury in NECs via regulating SMAD Family Member 7 (Smad7). In addition, miR‐375 inhibits the apoptosis of nasal mucosa cell and ameliorates AR through blocking JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway. Until now, there was no description about the precise role of miR‐141‐3p in NECs apoptosis and MUC5AC production induced by LPS. Interestingly, we observed that miR‐141‐3p was downregulated in both nasal mucosal samples from patients with AR and LPS‐stimulated NECs.
MUC5AC, which is a glycoprotein, belongs to the family of mucins. Mucus hypersecretion is a general characteristic of allergic respiratory disease. Hence, inhibiting mucus production is a potential approach to combating allergic disease. Our data indicated that LPS exposure raised MUC5AC production in NECs. We further demonstrated that miR‐141‐3p restrained LPS‐induced MUC5AC level, which suggesting a protective function of miR‐141‐3p in AR. Several investigations have described that LPS induces cell injury through inhibiting cell proliferation, promoting apoptosis and increasing the elicitation of inflammatory cytokines. , We then found that LPS significantly induced the apoptosis of NECs whereas overexpression of miR‐141‐3p attenuated the inducing effect of LPS on the apoptosis of NECs.
Our present study also showed that HMGB1 was a downstream target of miR‐141‐3p and its expression was regulated by miR‐141‐3p in NECs. Recently, increasing evidences indicate that HMGB1 serves as an inflammatory mediator initiator, and is overexpressed in the nasal secretions of patients with AR. For example, high mRNA level of HMGB1 is found in the nasal brushing samples in patients with AR. , We proved that HMGB1 was a directly target of miR‐141‐3p by using luciferase reporter gene experiment. Furthermore, the level of HMGB1 was negatively regulated by miR‐141‐3p. As expected, we found that HMGB1 was significantly overexpressed in AR patients and was upregulated in LPS‐treated NECs.
Next, we also verified the regulation of miR‐141‐3p in LPS‐induced MUC5AC production was depended on HMGB1. Furthermore, we identified that miR‐141‐3p positively regulated LPS‐induced MUC5AC production in NECs by targeting HMGB1. Hence, our observations implied that disrupting miR‐141‐3p/HMGB1 signaling axis might be used as a potential therapeutic target for AR.

5.

## CONCLUSION
We demonstrated that miR‐141‐3p was downregulated in nasal mucosal samples from patients with AR and LPS‐treated NECs. HMGB1 was a target of miR‐141‐3p and miR‐141‐3p regulated LPS‐induced mucus production in a HMGB1‐dependent manner.
