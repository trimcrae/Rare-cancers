## Background
This study was conducted to investigate the role and mechanism of LINC00662 in Sev-induced cognitive impairment by targeting let-7 g-5p.

## Methods
Mouse hippocampal HT22 cells were treated with sevoflurane, and cell viability was assessed by CCK-8. RT-qPCR was performed to detect the expression of LINC00662, let-7 g-5p, and LIMK2. Dual-luciferase and RIP assays verified their binding. The SPF male SD rats were grouped; hippocampal injection of si-LINC00662 or let-7 g-5p antagomir was combined with sevoflurane exposure, followed by MWM for cognitive evaluation. Apoptotic, and inflammatory markers were measured by RT-qPCR. The oxidative stress indicators were detected using corresponding kits.

## Results
Sevoflurane reduced cell viability, upregulated LINC00662 and LIMK2, and downregulated let-7 g-5p in a dose-dependent and time-dependent manner. LINC00662 directly bound to let-7 g-5p, and let-7 g-5p targeted LIMK2. Silencing LINC00662 reversed Sev-induced cell injury and inflammatory/oxidative stress responses, which were abolished by let-7 g-5p inhibition. In rats, silencing LINC00662 improved sevoflurane-induced cognitive deficits, whereas the let-7 g-5p antagomir worsened them.

## Conclusions
The LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis mediates Sev-induced neuronal injury and cognitive dysfunction by regulating neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis.

## Background
Sevoflurane (Sev) is a volatile inhalational anesthetic widely used in clinical practice [ , ]. Owing to its low lipid solubility and blood/gas partition coefficient (about 0.69), it is characterized by rapid induction and recovery, fast metabolism, and strong controllability [ ]. Sevoflurane has been safely applied as an anesthetic agent for more than 30 years [ ], and is particularly suitable for general anesthesia in adult and pediatric surgeries, anesthesia for short outpatient procedures, and the inhibition of myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury [ ]. However, sevoflurane may cause neuronal damage by inducing neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, leading to cognitive decline in rats in animal experiments [ ]. Moreover, sevoflurane is associated with postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) [ ], which is highly prevalent in elderly patients and may increase the risk of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [ ]. Although sevoflurane has significant advantages in clinical anesthesia, the specific mechanisms underlying its neurotoxicity remain incompletely understood. Further research is needed to optimize anesthetic protocols and balance anesthetic efficacy with neuro-safety.
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), a class of RNA molecules that do not encode proteins, participate in development, maintenance of homeostasis, and pathological processes of the nervous system through multilevel regulatory networks. The abnormal expression of these genes is closely associated with the occurrence and progression of various neurological diseases [ ]. As a typical neurodegenerative disease, AD studies have revealed that LINC00662 participates in the gene regulatory network of AD through a ceRNA mechanism, and its abnormal expression may promote the pathological process of AD by affecting ribosome protein-related pathways [ ]. Sev-induced neuroinflammation and Aβ deposition may overlap with the pathological process of AD. Although LINC00662 is a key molecule in the AD gene regulatory network, its role in sevoflurane neurotoxicity has not been investigated. LINC00662 is upregulated in microvascular endothelial cells (ECs) incubated with Aβ1 − 42, and knocking out LINC00662 reduces the permeability of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) in the AD microenvironment [ ]. LINC00662 is highly expressed in glioma tissues, and its high expression is significantly associated with shortened overall survival in patients. Inhibition of LINC00662 can significantly suppress the clonogenic ability of glioma cells [ ]. Ji et al. also reported that high expression of LINC00662 is significantly associated with the malignant phenotype of tumors. Knocking down LINC00662 can significantly inhibit the proliferation and invasion of glioma cells, reducing the migration and diffusion of tumor cells [ ]. However, the mechanism by which LINC00662 is involved in the regulation of cognitive function in Sev-induced neurotoxicity remains poorly understood.
The pathological mechanisms of sevoflurane-induced cognitive impairment overlap with those of AD, a neurodegenerative disorder closely associated with POCD [ ]. This overlap, characterized by neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and neuronal apoptosis, prompted us to focus on molecular targets known to regulate AD-related pathology. LINC00662 is upregulated in microvascular endothelial cells incubated with Aβ₁₋₄₂; silencing LINC00662 reduces the permeability across the blood-brain barrier in the AD microenvironment, highlighting its role in neurodegeneration-related vascular and neuronal damage [ ]. extensively involved in regulating cell differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis. They primarily exert tumor-suppressive effects by negatively regulating oncogenes such as RAS, MYC, and HMGA2, while also playing important roles in pathological processes associated with neurodegenerative diseases [ ]. Moverover, let-7 g-5p, which is associated with cognitive function, is downregulated. The let-7 family is one of the earliest discovered and evolutionarily highly conserved microRNA families. Initially identified as a regulator of developmental timing in Caenorhabditis elegans, let-7 has since been confirmed to act as a key post-transcriptional regulator in mammals, targeting a broad range of messenger RNAs through its conserved seed sequence. Members of this family are lated in patients with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), a prodromal stage of AD, and is correlated with abnormal brain glucose metabolism, a key pathological feature of AD [ , ]. Additionally, 27-hydroxycholesterol, a molecule that promotes Aβ accumulation, downregulates let-7 g-5p, further linking this miRNA to AD-like pathology [ ]. Targeted binding between the two was predicted by preliminary bioinformatics database analysis; therefore, we hypothesize that LINC00662 may be involved in this process by targeting let-7 g-5p.
No study has reported the role of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis in sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity. This is the first study to investigate the molecular mechanism by which this axis participates in cognitive impairment by regulating neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis, thereby providing a new perspective on the non-coding RNA regulatory network of POCD.

## Methods

## Cell culture and transfection
The mouse hippocampal neuronal cell line HT22 was cultured in DMEM. After the cells became adherent, they were placed in a tightly sealed chamber. The control group was exposed to air with 5% CO2, whereas the experimental groups were exposed to mixed gases containing 1%, 2%, and 4% sevoflurane and 5% CO2. The concentration of sevoflurane was monitored in real time using a Datex™ infrared analyzer during treatment to ensure precise control. A 500 mL sealed culture chamber for six-well plates was used. Experimental groups were exposed to mixed gases of 1%, 2%, and 4% sevoflurane, 5% CO2, and 95% air at 200 mL/min. After equilibration for 30 min, exposure began. After 6-hour of exposure, gas chromatography was performed to measure the amount of sevoflurane in the medium. The dissolved concentrations for the 1%, 2%, and 4% exposure groups were 12.3 ± 1.5 µmol/L, 25.1 ± 2.2 µmol/L, and 48.7 ± 3.8 µmol/L, validating effective and consistent dosing. After 6 h of incubation, cells were removed from the chamber and transferred to a conventional incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for further culture for 24 h before subsequent experiments.
Next, HT22 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were transfected with LINC00662 small interfering RNA (si-LINC00662), let-7 g-5p inhibitor, and their corresponding negative controls (si-NC, inhibitor NC) using the Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA).

## Animal grouping and exposure to Sev
This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Ethics Committee of Yancheng Third People’s Hospital (Approval Number: YCSY-2023-012). All animal experiments in this study were conducted strictly following the protocols stated in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health. SPF-grade male SD rats (n = 60; age: 18 months old) were purchased from Shanghai Jihui Laboratory Animal Care Co., Ltd (SCXK2022-0009) and met the SPF-grade standards. We obtained informed consent from the supplier, which confirmed that they agreed to provide these animals for this study as per relevant regulations. After seven days of adaptive feeding, the rats were randomly divided into 6 groups with 10 rats in each group. The specific groups were as follows: untreated group (control), sevoflurane treatment group, Sev + si-NC, Sev + si-LINC00662, Sev + si-LINC00662 + antagomir NC, and Sev + si-LINC00662 + let-7 g-5p antagomir. The transfection reagent was injected into the hippocampal region of the rats. Except for the rats in the control group, rats in all other groups were treated with 2.5% sevoflurane for 6 h daily for one consecutive week. A Datex-Ohmeda anesthesia machine (Model: Aestiva/5) was used to deliver a gas mixture containing 2.5% sevoflurane and 60% oxygen at a flow rate of 500 mL/min. The sevoflurane concentration was monitored in real time using a Datex infrared analyzer (accuracy: ±0.1%). A non-invasive pulse oximeter was used to monitor oxygen saturation (> 95%) and heart rate (300–400 bpm). The rectal temperature was maintained at 37 ± 0.5 °C using a thermostatic heating pad (checked every 30 min). The rats were grouped using a random number table (operated by an independent researcher). The experimenters who conducted the MWM test and analyzed the behavioral data were blinded to the group allocations. Predefined endpoints included oxygen saturation < 90% for five consecutive minutes, heart rate < 250 bpm for 10 consecutive minutes, convulsions, or respiratory depression. The exclusion criteria were as follow: (1) body weight loss > 10% during the adaptive feeding period; and (2) abnormal behaviors during MWM training (e.g., continuous wall-hugging swimming for > 5 min, which cannot reflect normal cognitive function). After excluding rats that met humane endpoints or were unfit for MWM testing, the effective sample size for each group was nine rats, and no additional deaths occurred among the remaining rats during the experiment. After the experiment, the rats were euthanized via intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (60 mg/kg), which rapidly induced deep anesthesia. The depth of anesthesia was confirmed by the absence of the toe pinch reflex and corneal reflex, which ensured that the rats were unconscious and free from pain. All rats were euthanized by cervical dislocation. This method is consistent with the guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals, minimizing animal suffering through humane treatment.

## Morris water maze (MWM) test
The MWM test was conducted to evaluate the learning and memorizing abilities of the rats. A circular pool (120 cm in diameter and 60 cm high) was filled with opaque water to conceal the platform (water temperature 22–24 °C). The pool was divided into four quadrants, and a 10-cm-diameter platform was placed in one quadrant, and submerged 2 cm below the water level. In the place navigation experiment, the rats were placed in the water from a semirandom quadrant, with researchers documenting the duration it took them to locate the platform, referred to as the escape latency. In the spatial probe experiment, the platform was removed, and the rats were given 90 s to swim freely. There was no significant difference in the average speed of rats in each group swimming for 90 s without a platform in MWM (Supplementary Fig. 1). The time spent in the target quadrant and the number of crossings over the original platform position were recorded. The raw behavioral traces of the rats are provided in Supplementary Fig. 2.

## RNA extraction and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
Total RNA was extracted from the hippocampal tissues of the rats and HT22 cells using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). LINC00662 and let-7 g-5p were reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript™ RT Kit (TaKaRa, Japan) and the TaqMan miRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Invitrogen, USA), respectively. The levels of LINC00662, let-7 g-5p, Bax, Bcl-2, IL-6, and TNF-α were then determined using the generated cDNA. GAPDH and U6 were used as internal references for quantitative analysis by the 2−ΔΔCT method.

## Dual-luciferase reporter assay
The wild-type or mutant LINC00662 sequences containing specific binding sites for let-7 g-5p were cloned and inserted into the pmirGLO vector to construct the recombinant plasmids LINC00662-WT and LINC00662-MUT, respectively. The above plasmids were co-transfected with let-7 g-5p mimics or mimic NC into HT22 cells. After 48 h of culture, luciferase activity was detected using a Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Beyotime, China).

## RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay
First, HT22 cells were lysed with a buffer and then incubated with magnetic beads coated with anti-Ago2 and anti-IgG antibodies. Total RNA was subsequently extracted from the HT22 cells, and the enrichment levels of LINC00662 and let-7 g-5p were determined by RT-qPCR.

## Detection of viability
The HT22 cells from each group were seeded in culture plates. After incubation for 0, 24, 48, or 72 h, Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent was added, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 1 h. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

## LDH, and SOD assays
After the experiment, HT22 cells and rat hippocampal tissues were collected, homogenized, and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant. Lactic dehydrogenase (LDH) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) levels were determined using an LDH detection kit and an SOD detection kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China), respectively.

## Western blotting analysis
First, HT22 cells from each experimental group and rat hippocampal tissues were collected, and RIPA lysis buffer was added for lysis at 4 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected, and the protein concentration was determined by the BCA method. A total of 5 µg of total protein was subjected to 12% SDS-PAGE for separation, and then electrotransferred to a PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk at room temperature for 2 h. The following primary antibodies were subsequently added: anti-Bax (1:1000, Abcam), anti-Bcl-2 (1:1000, Abcam), and anti-GAPDH (1:5000, CST) were added, followed by incubation at 4 °C overnight. The following day, an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (1:10000, Jackson ImmunoResearch) was added and the samples were incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Finally, visualization was performed using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Beyotime, China). The uncropped blots for all Western blotting experiments are provided in Supplementary Fig. 3.

## Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
Culture supernatants of HT22 cells and homogenate supernatants of rat hippocampal tissues were collected. The protein levels of IL-6 and TNF-α were determined via ELISA kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China).Statistical analysis.
The data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. The differences between any two groups were determined by conducting t-tests, whereas those among multiple groups were determined by conducting one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. For repeated-measures data, two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (group × training day) with Greenhouse-Geisser correction for sphericity was used, and effect sizes (η²) were reported. Sample size calculation: Using G*Power 3.1 software, with α of 0.05, β of 0.2, and effect size d of 1.2, the calculated minimum sample size per group was eight rats. To account for potential data exclusion, nine rats per group were included. For cell experiments, three independent biological replicates were used, which is consistent with the commonly used repetition standard in this field, and preliminary experiments confirmed that three independent biological replicates met the statistical test power. All results were considered to be statistically significant at P < 0.05.

## Results

## Sevoflurane inhibits cell viability, promotes LINC00662 expression, and suppresses let-7 g-5p expression
Sevoflurane significantly inhibited cell viability in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig.  A and B). Cell viability decreased with increasing sevoflurane concentrations (P < 0.05; Fig.  A). When 4% sevoflurane was used, prolonged treatment further reduced cell viability in a time-dependent manner (P < 0.05; Fig.  B). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that in HT22 cells treated with sevoflurane for 24 h, LINC00662 expression was upregulated, whereas let-7 g-5p expression was downregulated in a concentration-dependent manner (P < 0.01; Figs.  C-D).

 

## LINC00662 targets let-7 g-5p
By conducting bioinformatics analysis using the lncRNASNP2 database, we identified let-7 g-5p as a potential target miRNA of LINC00662, and predicted its specific binding sites (Fig.  A). Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that let-7 g-5p mimics significantly inhibited the luciferase activity of the LINC00662-WT vector (P < 0.0001), whereas no significant change was detected in the LINC00662-MUT vector (Fig.  B). RIP assays confirmed the interaction between LINC00662 and let-7 g-5p, as both were significantly enriched in the Anti-Ago2 group (P < 0.0001; Fig.  C).

 

## The role of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis in Sev-treated HT22 cells
The results RT-qPCR assays revealed that in Sev-treated HT22 cells, transfection with si-LINC00662 significantly decreased LINC00662 levels (P < 0.0001; Fig.  A) and increased let-7 g-5p levels. In contrast, the let-7 g-5p inhibitor significantly downregulated the expression of let-7 g-5p (P < 0.0001; Fig.  B). Sev-treated cells reduced cell viability, while silencing LINC00662 enhanced cell viability. The let-7 g-5p inhibitor abolished the protective effect of silencing LINC00662 on cell viability (P < 0.0001; Fig.  C). The results of the Western blotting analysis revealed that treatment of Sev upregulated Bax expression and downregulated Bcl-2 expression in HT22 cells. Transfection with si-LINC00662 reversed these effects, decreasing Bax and increasing Bcl-2 expression, which were counteracted by the let-7 g-5p inhibitor (P < 0.001; Fig.  D). Additionally, silencing LINC00662 reduced the levels of the proinflammatory factors IL-6 and TNF-α in Sev-treated cells, while the let-7 g-5p inhibitor significantly attenuated this inhibitory effect (P < 0.001; Fig.  E). The changes in LDH and SOD activities showed opposite trends to Bax and Bcl-2 (P < 0.05; Fig.  F).

 

## Effect of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis on cognitive impairment in Sev-treated rats
In the hippocampal tissues of Sev-treated rats, the expression of LINC00662 was upregulated, whereas the expression o let-7 g-5p was downregulated. Injection of si-LINC00662 reversed these effects, decreasing LINC00662 levels and increasing let-7 g-5p levels. In contrast, the let-7 g-5p inhibitor downregulated let-7 g-5p expression (P < 0.0001; Figs.  A-B). In the MWM test, Sev-treated rats presented greater escape latency, fewer platform crossings, and shorter time spent in the target quadrant. Transfection with si-LINC00662 significantly ameliorated these deficits, shortening escape latency and increasing platform crossings and target quadrant occupancy. These improvements disappeared when a let-7 g-5p antagomir was co-injected (P < 0.0001; Figs.  C-E).

 

## The impact of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis on Sev-induced apoptosis, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress
Western blot results demonstrated that silencing LINC00662 significantly suppressed Sev-induced upregulation of Bax and promoted Bcl-2 expression (P < 0.01, Fig.  A). Concurrently, LINC00662 silencing inhibited the concentrations of IL-6 and TNF-α induced by sevoflurane (P < 0.01, Fig.  B). Additionally, injection of si-LINC00662 in rats decreased LDH activity and increased SOD activity (P < 0.0001, Fig.  C). These protective effects were reversed by co-injection of a let-7 g-5p antagomir (P < 0.0001, Figs.  A-C).

 

## Let-7 g-5p directly targets LIMK2 and mediates Sev-induced neuronal injury
LIMK2 was identified as a potential downstream target gene of let-7 g-5p through miRDB, miRWalk, and TargetScan databases (Fig.  A), and complementary binding sites existed between the sequences of LIMK2 and let-7 g-5p (Fig.  B). Dual-luciferase reporter assay results showed that transfection of let-7 g-5p mimic significantly reduced the luciferase activity of LIMK2-WT, while no significant change was observed in the luciferase activity of LIMK2-MUT (P < 0.0001, Fig.  C). RIP assay further verified that both let-7 g-5p and LIMK2 were significantly enriched in the Anti-Ago2 group compared with the Anti-IgG group (P < 0.0001, Fig.  D), indicating that they are co-localized in the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). In addition, Sev treatment significantly upregulated the mRNA expression level of LIMK2 in HT22 cells, while transfection of let-7 g-5p mimic reversed this upregulation; however, co-transfection oe-LIMK2 restored the mRNA level of LIMK2 (P < 0.0001, Fig.  E). Other functional experiments further confirmed the regulatory role of the let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis: compared with the Sev group, the Sev + let-7 g-5p mimic group showed downregulated expression of the Bax and upregulated expression of Bcl-2 (P < 0.0001, Fig.  F); decreased concentrations of the pro-inflammatory factors IL-6 and TNF-α (P < 0.0001, Fig.  G); and reduced LDH activity as well as increased SOD activity (P < 0.0001, Fig.  H). In contrast, co-transfection with oe-LIMK2 abolished the protective effects of let-7 g-5p mimic, leading to aggravated neuronal injury, enhanced inflammatory response, and increased apoptotic tendency (P < 0.0001, Figs.  F-H).

 

## Discussion
POCD is a common anesthesia-induced postoperative complication that is associated with neurodegenerative diseases [ , ]. As a widely used inhalational anesthetic in clinical practice, sevoflurane has become the core drug of efficient anesthesia protocols because of its pharmacokinetic characteristics of rapid absorption and elimination [ ]. However, several studies have reported that it can induce POCD [ , ]. This study and previous studies confirmed that the neurotoxicity of sevoflurane cannot be ignored. It not only inhibits the viability of HT22 cells in a concentration- and time-dependent manner [ ], but also leads to cognitive dysfunction in rats by inducing neuroinflammation, oxidative stress and apoptosis [ ]. This was the first study to reveal the key regulatory role of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis in sevoflurane neurotoxicity, providing a new perspective for analyzing the mechanism underlying anesthesia-related nerve injury.
Multiple lncRNA/miRNA pathways are involved in regulating sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity. For example, Wen et al. (2025) reported that circHOMER1 can alleviate sevoflurane-induced hippocampal neuronal injury by targeting and negatively regulating miR-217. Its core mechanism of action involves the inhibition of miR-217-mediated silencing of downstream neuroprotective genes through a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) effect [ ]. Additionally, Chen et al. (2021) reported that P300 inhibitors can improve sevoflurane-induced cell apoptosis and cognitive impairment by regulating the activation of IL-17 A, indirectly suggesting that lncRNAs may participate in neurotoxic processes by modulating transcription factor-related pathways [ ]. Compared to the aforementioned pathways, the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis identified in this study has unique regulatory characteristics. The expression of LINC00662 is significantly upregulated in a concentration-dependent manner following sevoflurane exposure, and its expression pattern shows a high degree of synchronization with the downregulation of let-7 g-5p. In contrast, circHOMER1 was found to be downregulated after sevoflurane treatment in previous studies, indicating that different non-coding RNAs may exert either “pro-injury” or “anti-injury” bidirectional regulatory effects in sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity. As a key effector molecule in this pathway, let-7 g-5p directly targets LIMK2—a member of the LIM kinase family characterized by 1–2 terminal LIM motifs and an atypical C-terminal protein kinase domain, which is homologous to the neuronal LIM kinase associated with cognitive impairment in patients with Williams syndrome, implying its potential involvement in cognitive function regulation [ ]. In contrast, the downstream target genes of miR-217 in previous studies are more focused on signaling pathways related to neuronal survival (e.g., the PI3K/Akt pathway). Unlike the circHOMER1/miR-217 pathway, which exerts neuroprotective effects only at the cellular level, through dual verification in cellular and animal experiments, this study confirmed that the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis can simultaneously regulate sevoflurane-induced neuronal injury (e.g., decreased cell viability, and increased LDH release) and overall cognitive impairment (e.g., prolonged escape latency, and reduced time spent in the target quadrant in the MWM test). This suggests that the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis may play a core regulatory role in the “cellular injury-cognitive impairment” pathological cascade of sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity.
The mechanism identified in this study is complemented by other studies in important ways. Shen et al. [ ] reported that repeated sevoflurane exposure during the neonatal period can reduce the expression of the TET3 enzyme, induce DNA hypermethylation of the Shank2 gene (which subsequently inhibits its expression), and ultimately impair social recognition memory and spatial reference memory in male rats. This study determined the role of the “epigenetic modification-synaptic dysfunction” pathway. We found that the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis mediates injury by regulating Bax/Bcl-2, IL-6, TNF-α, LDH, and SOD. These findings suggest that sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity is driven by the synergistic action of multiple pathways, including “non-coding RNA regulation” and “DNA methylation,” suggesting that the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis is a key regulatory node. Our findings also indicate that future interventions should consider multi-pathway targeting. Another study [ ]confirmed that folic acid can reverse the sevoflurane-induced inhibition of myelination by promoting the differentiation of oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) and restoring the expression of myelin basic protein (MBP). Preoperative folic acid supplementation can reduce sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity, and its protective effect is associated with alleviating neuroinflammation and inhibiting demyelination. This finding aligns with the “silencing LINC00662 enhances SOD activity and reduces LDH release” observed in this study, both pointing to the core effect of “oxidative stress/inflammation inhibition.” Additionally, metabolic supplements such as folic acid may enhance their protective effects by regulating the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis (e.g., downregulating LINC00662, restoring let-7 g-5p expression, and thereby inhibiting LIMK2 upregulation). This bridges the clinical translation of the axis-mediated mechanism identified in this study. Future studies can verify the mediating role of this axis in the protective effect of folic acid, providing a basis for the development of combined intervention strategies involving “non-coding RNA targeting + metabolic supplementation”.
The pathological mechanisms of POCD and AD are overlapping and include neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and neuronal apoptosis—key pathways regulated by the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis identified in this study. The LINC00662/let-7 g-5p axis, which we identified as a regulator of these overlapping pathways, has also been implicated in AD-related pathology in previous studies, further supporting its potential as a common regulatory node. As a functional lncRNA discovered in recent years, LINC00662 is characteristically upregulated in various tumor tissues and drives carcinogenesis [ ]. In the field of neurological diseases, Yuan et al. confirmed that its levels are higher in glioma cells and cell lines, and it can promote apoptosis by regulating the proliferation and invasion of glioma cells [ ]. LINC00662 is upregulated in microvascular ECs incubated with Aβ1−42, and silencing LINC00662 reduces the permeability of the BBB in the AD microenvironment [ ]. We found that LINC00662 was upregulated in both sevoflurane-induced HT22 cells and rat hippocampal tissues. Moverover, silencing LINC00662 increased cell viability and reduced neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. The MWM experiment also confirmed that silencing LINC00662 improved the spatial memory ability of the rats.
Long non-coding RNAs can participate in the pathogenesis of neurological diseases by targeting miRNAs [ ]. Bioinformatics analysis using the lncRNASNP2 database identified let-7 g-5p as a target miRNA of LINC00662. Other studies have shown that aberrant expression of let-7 g-5p is involved in the progression of multiple neurodegenerative diseases: in patients with MCI, circulating blood let-7 g-5p levels are associated with overall dietary quality, suggesting that diet may participate in the pathological process of AD by regulating let-7 g-5p expression [ ]. Treatment with 27-hydroxycholesterol (27-OHC) downregulates let-7 g-5p expression, which may play a key role in 27-OHC-induced regulation of Aβ metabolism and thus participate in the pathological process of AD [ ]. Additionally, let-7 g-5p levels are decreased in the serum of MCI patients and are associated with a decreased in risk of MCI [ ]. In AD patients, plasma let-7 g-5p levels are significantly negatively correlated with glucose uptake in the brain (detected by FDG PET), indicating that elevated let-7 g-5p levels may be associated with reduced brain glucose metabolism, a typical pathological feature of AD [ ]. Our study revealed that let-7 g-5p expression decreased with increasing sevoflurane concentration. In sevoflurane-induced HT22 cells, the let-7 g-5p inhibitor abolished the protective effects of si-LINC00662, leading to a decrease in cell viability and an increase in neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis, which is consistent with previous findings [ ]. Meanwhile, the let-7 g-5p antagonist exacerbated cognitive dysfunction in rats. Notably, this study further identified LIMK2 as a direct downstream target of let-7 g-5p, completing the regulatory chain from LINC00662 to let-7 g-5p and ultimately to LIMK2, which mediates downstream pathological responses. This not only enriches the understanding of the non-coding RNA regulatory network in sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity but also links the previously reported roles of let-7 g-5p in neurodegenerative diseases to a specific downstream effector molecule [ ], providing a more precise molecular target for intervention.
A key limitation of this study lies in cross-species extrapolation. In vitro experiments were conducted using mouse hippocampal HT22 cells, whereas in vivo validation was performed in aged male SD rats. This cross-species design was based on two practical considerations: first, HT22 cells are a well-established and widely used cell line for studying hippocampal neuronal function and neurotoxicity, with abundant reference data for sevoflurane-related experiments [ , ]; second, aged SD rats are a classic animal model for POCD research, as their physiological characteristics (e.g., age-related decline in cognitive function and increased susceptibility to anesthesia-induced neurotoxicity) are more consistent with the clinical high-risk population of POCD (elderly patients) [ ]. However, potential differences in molecular regulatory networks between mice and rats cannot be excluded. For example, the expression pattern of LINC00662 or let-7 g-5p in response to sevoflurane exposure may differ between the two species, and the affinity of let-7 g-5p for downstream targets (e.g., TLR4, and CASP3) might differ at the sequence level. Additionally, the transcriptional response of inflammatory and apoptotic pathways to sevoflurane may be species-specific, which may have affected the translatability of our findings. Another closely related limitation is animal model selection bias. Besides the cross-species gap, this study included only aged male SD rats and did not involve female rats or young/middle-aged rats, further restricting the generalizability of the conclusions. Sex differences may modulate the regulatory mechanism of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis. For example, estrogen regulates neuroinflammatory pathways, and its absence in male models may lead to distinct responses to sevoflurane-induced neurotoxicity compared to those in females. Additionally, the physiological state of aged rats (with inherent cognitive decline) differs from that of young and middle-aged rats; thus, leaving the role of the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis in non-elderly individuals exposed to sevoflurane is not clear and needs to be determined.
To address these limitations, future studies must adopt targeted improvements. First, the LINC00662/let-7 g-5p/LIMK2 axis should be verified in a single-species model (e.g., in vivo experiments on rat primary hippocampal neurons combined with SD rats) to confirm the consistency of the regulatory mechanism across in vitro and in vivo settings. Second, supplementation models of female rats and rats of different age groups (three-month-old, 12-month-old) are needed to validate the sex-specific and age-specific effects of this axis in sevoflurane-induced cognitive impairment. Additionally, extending the research to non-human primate models or human-induced pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons may enhance the clinical translatability of the findings, as these models more closely mimic human neuronal physiology and the molecular basis of POCD.

## Conclusions
In tthis study, we established a novel regulatory paradigm for Sev-induced neurotoxicity: Sev exposure induces the upregulation of LINC00662 in hippocampal neurons. It acts as a “sponge” to adsorb let-7 g-5p, thereby downregulating it. The downregulation of let-7 g-5p relieves its inhibitory effect on downstream pro-injury gene LIMK2, resulting in LIMK2 upregulation, which ultimately drives neuroinflammatory cascades, oxidative stress damage, and neuronal apoptosis, which results in learning and memory dysfunction. This finding not only reveals a new regulatory mechanism involving non-coding RNA for anesthesia-related neurotoxicity but also provides potential molecular targets for the prevention and treatment of POCD.
