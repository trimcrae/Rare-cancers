## Background
Therapeutic hypothermia has a beneficial effect on cardiac function after acute myocardial infarction, but the exact mechanism is still unclear. Recent research has suggested that microRNAs participate in acute myocardial infarction to regulate cardiomyocyte survival. This study aimed to explore the ability of hypothermia‐regulated microRNA‐483‐3p (miR‐483‐3p) to inhibit hypoxia‐induced myocardial infarction.

## Methods and Results
Primary cardiomyocytes were cultured under hypoxia at 32 °C to mimic therapeutic hypothermia, and the differentially expressed microRNAs were determined by RNA sequencing. Therapeutic hypothermia recovered hypoxia‐induced increases in apoptosis, decreases in ATP levels, and decreases in miR‐483‐3p expression. Overexpression of miR‐483‐3p exhibited effects similar to those of therapeutic hypothermia on hypoxia in the treatment of cardiomyocytes to associate with maintaining the mitochondrial membrane potential, and cyclin‐dependent kinase 9 (Cdk9) was identified as a target gene with downregulated expression by miR‐483‐3p. Knockdown of Cdk9 also promoted cardiac survival, ATP production, and mitochondrial membrane potential stability under hypoxia. In vivo, the expression of miR‐483‐3p and Cdk9 was tested in the cardiac tissue of the mice with acute myocardial infarction, and the expression of miR‐483‐3p decreased and Cdk9 increased in the region of myocardial infarction. However, miR‐483‐3p was overexpressed with lentivirus, which suppressed apoptosis, infarct size (miR‐483‐3p, 22.00±4.04% versus negative control, 28.57±5.44%, P<0.05), and Cdk9 expression to improve cardiac contractility.

## Conclusions
MiR‐483‐3p antagonizes hypoxia, leading to cardiomyocyte injury by targeting Cdk9, which is a new mechanism of therapeutic hypothermia.

## 
MiR‐483‐3p Directly Targets Cdk9
MicroRNAs downregulate the expression of proteins by targeting the 3′UTR of genes for post‐transcriptional modification. To verify the target genes of miR‐483‐3p, we transfected cardiomyocytes with miR‐483‐3p, and differential protein expression was detected by mass spectrometry. Mass spectrometry showed that the expression of 58 proteins was decreased, and the expression of 44 proteins was increased (Figure  ). Based on the analysis by the miRanda, RNAhybrid, and TargetScan databases of the 3′UTR of genes related to miR‐483‐3p target sites, there were 1647 candidate genes that may bind with miR‐483‐3p (Figure  ). Compared with the potential miR‐483‐3p target genes and the proteins downregulated by miR‐483‐3p, the Venn analysis further narrowed 1647 to 4 candidate proteins, Cdk9, Rab10, U2af2 and Lypla2 (Figure  ). To further validate the expression of the 4 candidate proteins regulated by miR‐483‐3p, we transfected the miR‐483‐3p mimic and NC microRNA into cardiomyocytes to measure these protein levels. MiR‐483‐3p downregulated Cdk9, Rab10, U2af2, and Lypla2 expression; however, only Cdk9 expression was significantly downregulated (P<0.01, Figure  ).
 To determine whether Cdk9 is a miR‐483‐3p target gene, we synthesized wild‐type (3′UTR‐Cdk9 [wt]) and mutant (3′UTR‐Cdk9 [mut]) constructs of the Cdk9 3′UTR and cloned them into a plasmid (Figure  ). Cotransfection of miR‐483‐3p and the 3′UTR‐Cdk9 (wt) vectors significantly decreased luciferase activity (P<0.01), but there was no effect after cotransfection of miR‐15b with the 3′UTR‐Cdk9 (mut) or blank vector in HeLa cells (Figure  ). Cotransfection of NC with the blank control, 3′UTR‐Cdk9 construct (wt) or 3′UTR‐Cdk9 construct (mut) served as the NC (Figure  ). Thus, miR‐483‐3p directly targets the 3′UTR of the Cdk9 transcript.

## 
MiR‐483‐3p and Cdk9 Regulated ATP Levels and Mitochondrial Membrane Potential
Previous studies have shown that therapeutic hypothermia decreases ischemia‐induced mitochondrial injury, which maintains mitochondrial membrane integrity to maintain mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψ) and the levels of ATP. , In some previous studies demonstrated that oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos) complex containing 5 members played an important role in the mitochondrial membrane potential and function. Considering that Δψ and levels of ATP are closely related to therapeutic hypothermia, we transfected miR‐483‐3p and NC into cardiomyocytes with hypoxic treatment and found that miR‐483‐3p significantly promoted the aggregate/monomer JC‐1 ratio, indicating that miR‐483‐3p maintained mitochondrial membrane integrity (Figure  ). Cdk9 is a direct target gene of miR‐483‐3p, and its expression is downregulated by miR‐483‐3p. Thus, siRNA was used to specifically knock down the expression of Cdk9 (the knockdown efficiency is shown in Figure  ). We verified the impact of miR‐483‐3p and Cdk9 on ATP levels and OxPhos complex in cardiomyocytes. MiR‐483‐3p overexpression or Cdk9 knockdown significantly enhanced the ATP levels and OxPhos complex in cardiomyocytes compared with the NC in each group (P<0.05, Figure  and ). Moreover, under hypoxic treatment compared with the control treatment, knockdown of Cdk9 led to a significant decrease in apoptosis of ≈ 4‐fold and an increase in the JC‐1 ratio of ≈ 2‐fold (Figure  and ). Meanwhile, knockdown Cdk9 reduced expression of PPARG coactivator 1 alpha (Pgc‐1α), MYC proto‐oncogene, BHLH transcription factor (Myc), and transcription factor A, mitochondrial (Tfam), but only Myc was significantly decreased (Figure  ).
 

## 
MiR‐483‐3p Expression Decreased and Cdk9 Expression Increased in the Infarct Zone
Since miR‐483‐3p and Cdk9 participate in hypoxia‐induced cardiomyocyte injury in vitro, we further detected the concentrations of miR‐483‐3p and Cdk9 in the AMI region. In situ hybridization showed that the miR‐483‐3p levels declined on the first and third days but reversed on the seventh day after AMI compared with those in the sham group, and the miR‐483‐3p level tended to U‐type manner (Figure  and ). Similar to the in‐situ hybridization results, the TaqMan RT‐PCR results also showed that miR‐483‐3p expression was reduced on the first and third days but 7 days after AMI in a U‐type manner (Figure  ). After analysis of the α‐actinin (cardiomyocyte marker) expression in AMI, we found that the concentration of Cdk9 was significantly increased on the third day after AMI (P<0.01, Figure  and ).
 

## 
MiR‐483‐3p Reduced Apoptosis, Infarct Size, and Cdk9 Expression in an Animal Model of AMI

Based on the decreased expression of miR‐483‐3p in the AMI region, we overexpressed miR‐483‐3p in situ following infarction to observe apoptosis, infarct size and Cdk9 expression. After overexpression of miR‐483‐3p on the third day after AMI, the apoptosis ratio of the α‐actinin‐positive (cardiomyocyte marker) cells was significantly decreased by approximately 3‐fold compared with that of the NC group (P<0.01, Figure  and ). Moreover, the expression of Cdk9 in cardiomyocytes (α‐actinin‐positive cells) in the infarct region was reduced by miR‐483‐3p overexpression (Figure  and ). As shown by 2,3,5‐triphenyltetrazolium chloride analysis of infarct size/LV, overexpression of miR‐483‐3p also significantly decreased the AMI‐induced infarct size (P<0.05, Figure  and ). To further verify the relationship between Cdk9 and miR‐483‐3p, we constructed Cdk9 without 3′UTR region overexpression lentivirus (LV‐Cdk9) and injected LV‐Cdk9 or/and LV‐miR‐483‐3p into the precordial tissue surrounding the ligature to measure the infarction size and cardiac function. Compared with LV‐NC treatment, overexpression Cdk9 caused more severe infarction size and cardiac dysfunction, and antagonizing protective function of miR‐483‐3p in AMI (Figure  ).
 

## 
MiR‐483‐3p Improved Cardiac Function After AMI

Since short‐term cardiac dysfunction is an important adverse outcome of AMI, we assessed the cardiac function of the animal model of AMI with miR‐483‐3p overexpression by echocardiography. The echocardiographic images were used to analyze the LV ejection fraction, fractional shortening, cardiac output, LV end‐diastolic diameter, LV end‐systolic diameter, and stroke volume (Figure  ). For these indices, miR‐483‐3p overexpression caused LV ejection fraction, fractional shortening, and cardiac output to increase and LV end diastolic diameter, LV end‐systolic diameter, and stroke volume to decrease, but only LV ejection fraction, fractional shortening, LV end‐diastolic diameter, and LV end‐systolic diameter showed significant differences (P<0.05, Figure  through ). Thus, miR‐483‐3p suppressed MI‐induced cardiac dysfunction. For observing the long‐term effect of miR‐483‐3p and hypothermia on AMI, miR‐483‐3p overexpression and hypothermia were treated on the AMI mice for 4 weeks. And then, miR‐483‐3p overexpression or hypothermia reduced the infarction and fibrosis size of MI mice, moreover, these treatments promoted the cardiac contractility during 4 weeks. However, the miR‐483‐3p overexpression exhibited more beneficial effects on MI compared with hypothermia therapy (Figure  ).
 

## DISCUSSION
Therapeutic hypothermia is applied to relieve some acute diseases, and the mechanism of therapeutic hypothermia‐induced protective effects has been a subject of intense interest in recent years. Although multiple mechanisms are involved in the effects of therapeutic hypothermia, suppressing hypoxia‐induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis has been shown to be an important protective intervention in AMI. However, the regulatory mechanisms of microRNAs involved in mitochondrial membrane potential, ATP accumulation and apoptosis in AMI are not clear. In the present study, we found that therapeutic hypothermia inhibited hypoxia‐induced apoptosis and ATP depletion, and miR‐483‐3p, as the top significantly differentially expressed miRNA, was regulated by hypothermia. Furthermore, we found that miR‐483‐3p weakened apoptosis, preserved the mitochondrial membrane potential and promoted ATP levels in hypoxia‐treated cardiomyocytes by directly targeting Cdk9. In vivo, AMI downregulated miR‐483‐3p expression but upregulated Cdk9 expression in the infarct region; however, overexpression of miR‐483‐3p antagonized Cdk9 and relieved cardiac dysfunction by reducing apoptosis and restricting infarct size.
Some previous studies have demonstrated that AMI‐related ischemia caused cardiomyocyte oxygen depletion and the switch to anerobic glycolysis, which accelerated ATP exhaustion. During this process, the mitochondrial membrane integrity was disrupted, which showed that hypoxia inhibited electron transport to decrease the mitochondrial membrane potential, ultimately restricting ATP synthesis and causing apoptosis in cardiomyocytes. However, therapeutic hypothermia was shown to ameliorate mitochondrial permeability stabilization, reactive oxygen species production, and calcium channel homeostasis. , , In the present study, we found that hypoxia induced destabilization of ATP levels and mitochondrial membrane potential to cause apoptosis, but hypothermia (at 32 °C) ameliorated these effects to inhibit apoptosis in cardiomyocytes. Thus, therapeutic hypothermia exerted antiapoptotic effects by promoting mitochondrial function. Various mechanisms involving patterns of gene expression, including pretranscriptional and post‐transcriptional modifications, participated in therapeutic hypothermia. In our research, miR‐483‐3p, as the top differentially expressed miRNA, showed upregulated expression in response to hypothermia but downregulated expression in response to hypoxia in cardiomyocytes cultured in vitro, while miR‐483‐3p expression was decreased by MI in vivo. This result suggested that miR‐483‐3p and the target genes show a novel pattern of gene expression regulation that might have a potential protective effect on MI. Further studies demonstrated that overexpression of miR‐483‐3p significantly suppressed hypoxia‐induced apoptosis, decreases in the ATP level, increases in the OxPhos complex and changes in mitochondrial membrane potential in vitro. Regarding the effects of miR‐483‐3p on cell function, previous work showed that miR‐483‐3p was located in the second intron of IGF2 (insulin‐like growth factor 2), and miR‐483‐3p was involved in the insulin pathway and was regulated by glucose levels. These results indicated that miR‐483‐3p regulated physiological functions via metabolic processes, especially mitochondrial function. However, previous studies on the effect of miR‐483‐3p on apoptosis reported opposite results. Some previous studies reported that overexpression of miR‐483‐3p in H9C2, a cardiomyocyte‐like cell line, induced apoptosis by targeting IGF1 (insulin‐like growth factor 1), , but other studies showed that miR‐483‐3p targets IGF1 to inhibit apoptosis of fibroblast‐like synoviocytes and decreases natural killer cell cytotoxicity. In our research, we found that overexpression of miR‐483‐3p in primary cardiomyocytes did not cause apoptosis but suppressed hypoxia‐induced apoptosis. The reason for these different effects might be that miR‐483‐3p targets different genes to exert different functions. In the present study, Cdk9 was identified as a target gene of miR‐483‐3p that directly binds with miR‐483‐3p through the 3′UTR and is decreased by miR‐483‐3p in vitro. Moreover, knockdown of Cdk9 expression significantly reversed the hypoxia‐induced ATP‐mediated decrease in apoptosis and change in mitochondrial membrane potential, which was similar to the effect of miR‐483‐3p on cardiomyocytes. We further detected the function of miR‐483‐3p in an animal model of AMI, which showed that overexpression of miR‐483‐3p antagonized apoptosis and Cdk9 expression in cardiomyocytes and recovered cardiac function. Cdk9 has been reported to participate in cardiac hypertrophy, and pathological activation of Cdk9 leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptosis, and heart failure by inhibiting PGC‐1 (peroxisome proliferator‐activated receptor G coactivator‐1). Some other study showed that Cdk9 regulated the expression of Myc and Tfam to participate in apoptosis. , In our present study, knockdown Cdk9 decreased Pgc‐1α, Myc, and Tfam expression, which hinted that Cdk9 effect on hypoxia induced apoptosis of cardiomyocyte via these genes. Eventually, therapeutic hypothermia regulated mitochondrial function and ATP levels to affect hypoxia‐induced apoptosis via miR‐483‐3p directly targeting Cdk9 in cardiomyocytes.
Current protocols of therapeutic hypothermia in clinical practice are surface cooling, intravascular cooling, cold saline injection into coronary arteries, pericardial perfusion, and extracorporeal blood cooling. Moreover, therapeutic hypothermia decreased myocardial infarct size, remodeling and no‐reflow and improved myocardial function in some animal models. , However, some random cohort studies have failed to show the beneficial cardioprotective effect of therapeutic hypothermia after AMI, such as the Randomized Pilot Study of Mild Hypothermia in Cardiogenic Shock Complicating Myocardial Infarction Trial, Cooling as an adjunctive therapy to percutaneous intervention in patients with acute myocardial infarction and Intravascular Cooling Adjunctive to Percutaneous Coronary Intervention, because many patients in the hypothermia group could not accurately reach the target core body temperature, and severe hypothermia can result in body convulsions. Finding precise targets for alternative therapeutic hypothermia through another mechanism may compensate for the above shortcomings. In this research, we found that some miRNAs, including miR‐483‐3p, miR‐10b‐5p, miR‐19b, miR‐92a‐5p, miR‐708‐5p, and miR‐334d‐3p, were regulated by therapeutic hypothermia. The protective function of miR‐10‐5p and miR‐19b‐1 on AMI was identified in our previous studies, and miR‐483‐3p was detected and verified to be protective in this research. In some ex vivo studies of hypothermia, , hypothermia increased cardiac contractility, and our study showed similar results that miR‐483‐3p also significantly increased cardiac contractility. Moreover, hypothermia led to a decrease in stroke volume or cardiac output , in in vivo studies, but overexpression of miR‐483‐3p promoted stroke volume or cardiac output. Together, these data indicated that miR‐483‐3p partially simulated therapeutic hypothermia and improved some adverse effects of therapeutic hypothermia.

## Study Limitations
One shortcoming of this study is the mechanism of miR‐483‐3p targeting Cdk9 to regulate ATP levels, which will be studied in further research. Although we detected the effect of miR‐483‐3p on the mouse model of AMI, a large animal model is needed for verification, and some novel delivery methods should be further explored.

## CONCLUSIONS
In this study, therapeutic hypothermia inhibited hypoxia‐induced apoptosis and ATP level reduction and regulated miR‐483‐3p expression in cardiomyocytes. Overexpression of miR‐483‐3p exhibited a similar effect to therapeutic hypothermia on hypoxia‐treated cardiomyocytes by directly binding and decreasing Cdk9. In the mice with AMI, the expression of miR‐483‐3p decreased, but Cdk9 expression increased; however, overexpression of miR‐483‐3p in the region of MI restricted cardiomyocyte apoptosis and infarct size by inhibiting Cdk9 expression. Moreover, overexpression of miR‐483‐3p significantly ameliorated cardiac function in the mice with AMI. Collectively, hypothermia promoted the expression of miR‐483‐3p, inhibiting Cdk9 to antagonize hypoxia‐induced cardiomyocyte injury, which is a new mechanism of therapeutic hypothermia, and miR‐483‐3p is a potential therapeutic target miRNA for AMI.
