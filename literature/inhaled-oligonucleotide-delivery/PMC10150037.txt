Ribonucleic acid (RNA) therapeutics are an emerging type of medication. Extensive scientific research has been devoted to exploring the therapeutic potentials of diverse types of RNA therapeutics, such as small interfering RNA (siRNA), messenger RNA (mRNA) and RNA aptamer. The most recent successful example of RNA therapeutics is the COVID-19 vaccines enabled by mRNA nanotechnologies . Despite the enormous potential of RNA therapeutics in solving critical issues in treating various human diseases and elucidating underlying pathophysiological mechanisms, the instability of naked RNA limits its application and successfully delivering RNA with integrity remains a challenge. Therefore, the development and use of drug delivery technologies are crucial in overcoming these challenges and promoting the clinical translation of RNA therapeutics.
The emerging drug delivery technologies have led to remarkable progress in RNA encapsulation and overcoming biological barriers in the body . This will lead to a revolution for researchers and clinicians to explore their quests in diverse disease settings with RNA therapeutics and drug delivery nanotechnologies. The present theme issue aims to give an overview of the most exciting progress and the latest advances in RNA therapeutics and drug delivery, focusing on how these technologies will impact various medical applications. By guest-editing this special issue, we hope to enable some of the emerging investigators and prominent scientists to report their studies and share their thoughts.
This invited special issue covers four research articles and seven review articles covering hot topics in RNA therapeutics and delivery technologies on mRNA, siRNA and RNA aptamer, presenting significant advancement in the field. Zhang et al. described various types of RNA, including coding RNA and non-coding RNA, that can be used as therapeutics. They discussed RNA nanotechnologies and highlighted lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) as helpful tools for delivery. The recent progress of RNA therapeutic applications, focusing on cancer treatment, is also discussed in that review paper. Zhong et al. introduced different nanoparticle platforms, focusing on lipid, polymer, lipid-polymer hybrid, and protein/peptide-conjugates, that are widely used for delivering various mRNA modalities for cancer immunotherapy. Dong et al. developed a pH-responsive polymeric nanoplatform for phosphatase and tensin homologue (PTEN)-mRNA delivery. The up-regulated PTEN as a tumor suppressor restored the therapeutic effects of trastuzumab with inhibited tumor growth of HER2-positive trastuzumab-resistant breast tumors. Duan et al. reported a feasible coating strategy for developing mRNA vaccines. They utilized sodium alginate to encapsulate DOTAP liposome/mRNA complexes and explored its potential as a nanovaccine by delivering antigen-mRNA. Gao et al. introduced biological barriers for pulmonary siRNA delivery and discussed the three different routes of localized administration (inhalation, intranasal, and intratracheal) of siRNA-based therapeutics. They presented a comprehensive review of recent advancements in the non-viral delivery of siRNA for the treatment of pulmonary diseases including infection, inflammation, obstructive pulmonary disease, and cancer. RNA can also be decorated on display carriers to enhance the targeting ability. Uddin et al. discussed RNA nanotechnology for specific targeting and the application of RNA nanotechnology to display ligands, including chemical ligands, small peptides, or RNA aptamers, on the surface of exosomes as an emerging delivery platform for specific cancer targeting and drug delivery. Butowska et al. conjugated doxorubicin (DOX) to LNP and developed a co-delivery system of DOX and siRNA to knock down B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) and facilitate the delivery of DOX to cancer cells. They demonstrated that siBcl-2 LNP-DOX could effectively inhibit tumor growth in a mouse model of lymphoma and provided a platform for the co-delivery of siRNA and DOX for combination cancer therapy. Li et al. developed an endosomal pH-responsive nanoplatform to deliver siRNA of tubulin folding cofactor E (TBCE) and cisplatin prodrug for enhanced chemotherapy and reversing chemoresistance by knocking down TBCE. This codelivery strategy dramatically inhibits the growth of hepatocellular carcinoma. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are emerging biomarkers and therapeutic targets in cancer. Han et al. reviewed the mechanism and mode of action of lncRNAs and discussed the current delivery platforms of lncRNAs. They also summarized studies using lncRNA as a therapeutic target in cancer. Aptamers are a special type of single-stranded sequences of DNA or RNA. Aptamers have been widely studied as a targeting ligand to conjugate with other drugs as aptamer–drug conjugate (ApDC) with high targeting efficiency. He et al. reviewed the different types of ApDC and discussed the progress and challenges in the development of ApDC for cancer therapy. Zhao et al. introduced nucleic acid nanoassemblies and discussed the advantages of the progress of biomedical applications of RNA-based therapy and diagnostic strategies with DNA and RNA nanoassemblies. With the flexibility and deformability of nucleic acids and the development of nucleic acid nanotechnology, it is now able to construct nucleic acid with desired shape and structure to form nanoassemblies as novel therapeutics and diagnostic tools.
We expect this theme issue could help pave the way for advancing RNA therapeutics and delivery technologies, and provide valuable insights for readers in the converging field of RNA technology, gene therapy, pharmacotherapy, material science, nanotechnology, etc. On this occasion, I also would like to thank all contributing authors, peer reviewers, and journal editors for their efforts in this edition.
